## Problem

Standard RLHF reward learning via the Bradley-Terry-Luce model treats human preferences as noisy estimates of a common ground truth, but in AI alignment, individuals can have legitimate value differences. The paper asks whether the prevailing MLE-based aggregation satisfies basic axiomatic properties from social choice theory (Pareto optimality, pairwise majority consistency) when preferences are genuinely heterogeneous, and whether alternative rules with stronger guarantees exist.

## Input / Output

The method takes a profile of complete ordinal rankings over m candidates (each candidate represented by a distinct feature vector in R^d) and outputs a parameter vector θ* whose induced linear reward function r_θ(c) = ⟨θ, x_c⟩ defines a collective ranking. A critical constraint absent in classical social choice is that the output ranking must be *feasible*—i.e., it must be inducible by some non-degenerate parameter vector, meaning the collection of half-space intersections R^σ has nonempty interior. This feasibility constraint is what makes the problem fundamentally different from standard rank aggregation.

## Method

The paper introduces *linear social choice*, a new paradigm where aggregation rules must output only linearly inducible (feasible) rankings, and evaluates rules against adapted axioms. The key negative result (Theorem 3.1) shows that any loss-based rule using a nondecreasing weakly convex or strictly convex loss—including BTL's binary cross-entropy—fails both Pareto optimality and pairwise majority consistency. A stronger impossibility (Theorem 3.7) proves that *all* C1 rules (those depending only on pairwise majority relationships) fail PO in the linear setting, in stark contrast to classical social choice. To overcome this, the authors design LCPO (Leximax Copeland subject to PO), which sequentially selects candidates by Copeland score while restricting to rankings that respect PO dominance constraints, and prove it satisfies PO, PMC, majority consistency, and winner monotonicity. The rule is implementable in polynomial time via O(|C|²) linear programs checking feasibility of partial rankings.

## Results

The paper's main results are theoretical impossibility and existence theorems. Theorem 3.1 shows that any loss-based linear rank aggregation rule using a nondecreasing weakly convex or strictly convex loss function (including BTL/binary cross-entropy and hinge loss) fails both Pareto Optimality (PO) and Pairwise Majority Consistency (PMC). Theorem 3.7 establishes a stronger impossibility: all C1 linear rank aggregation rules (those depending only on majority relationships) fail PO. On the positive side, Theorem 4.3 proves that the proposed Leximax Copeland subject to PO (LCPO) rule simultaneously satisfies PO, PMC, majority consistency, and winner monotonicity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare methods empirically but evaluates them against axiomatic criteria. The standard BTL model (recovered as binary cross-entropy loss in the loss-based framework) fails both PO and PMC, as do all loss-based rules with convex nondecreasing losses (Theorem 3.1). A majority-based loss variant achieves PMC (Theorem 3.6) but still fails PO, and in fact no C1 rule can satisfy PO in the linear setting (Theorem 3.7). The proposed LCPO rule is the first linear rank aggregation rule shown to satisfy both PO and PMC simultaneously, along with two additional properties (majority consistency and winner monotonicity). In contrast, the related work of Siththaranjan et al. [25] shows BTL corresponds to Borda count under unrestricted reward functions, but Borda count cannot be realized as a linear rule and is impractical for RLHF.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on Noothigattu et al. (NeurIPS 2020), which analyzed axiomatic properties of reward functions learned as MLE estimators of random utility models, but departs by restricting to linear reward functions and introducing the constraint that the output must be induced by a single parameter vector. It also positions itself against a wave of concurrent work bridging social choice and RLHF (Conitzer et al., Dai & Fleisig, Mishra, Siththaranjan et al.), noting that while those papers conceptually support the axiomatic approach, none provide the technical impossibility results or constructive rules developed here. The key conceptual departure is the introduction of 'linear social choice' as a new paradigm: unlike classical social choice where all rankings are feasible outputs, the linearity constraint means many rankings cannot be realized, fundamentally altering which axioms can be simultaneously satisfied.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central negative result—that the BTL model (the de facto standard in RLHF) fails both Pareto Optimality and Pairwise Majority Consistency—provides a rigorous axiomatic critique of prevailing practice and motivates rethinking how reward functions are aggregated from heterogeneous human preferences. The stronger impossibility that all C1 (majority-dependent) linear rank aggregation rules fail PO means the linear constraint creates a genuine structural barrier absent in classical social choice, opening a rich space of open questions about which axiom combinations are achievable. The paper is purely theoretical with no empirical validation, and practical implementation of LCPO requires complete rankings or reliable majority estimates, which remains an open challenge for real-world RLHF pipelines where only sparse pairwise comparisons are available.
