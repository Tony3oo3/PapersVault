## Problem

Prompt tuning offers a practical advantage over adapter-based PEFT methods like LoRA by requiring no server-side storage of task-specific modules, yet it consistently underperforms them. The paper asks whether prompt tuning can be redesigned to match or exceed LoRA-level performance while preserving its server-free, client-computable soft-prompt mechanism.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241498/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text query or code snippet, encodes it with a small frozen encoder model (e.g., RoBERTa for NLU, CodeSage for code), and produces a soft prompt of m=10 virtual token vectors that is prepended to the input embeddings before being fed to the frozen foundation model. The FM then outputs a classification label, a completed method body, or a predicted program input/output. Because the soft prompt is computed entirely on the client side, the server processes the concatenated [soft-prompt | input] sequence identically regardless of task, incurring zero additional server-side computation or storage.

## Method

LoPA constructs the soft prompt as Z = Z_S ∘ g(Z_I), where Z_S is a task-specific component of m learnable vectors shared across all instances, and Z_I is an instance-specific component derived from the input via a low-rank decomposition Z_I = u × v^T with rank r ≪ min(d, m). Two separate MLP heads encode u ∈ R^{d×r} and v ∈ R^{m×r} from the encoder's output X', reducing parameter count by a factor of (r/d + r/m) compared to a full d×m encoding. The sigmoid gating function g performs a Hadamard product, creating a coupled learning dynamic where gradients to Z_S are modulated by σ(Z_I) and gradients to Z_I are scaled by Z_S and σ'(Z_I), enabling the two components to co-adapt in ways that the linear-sum composition of IDPG cannot capture. This non-linear factorization yields distinct input-dependent offset subspaces in the attention mechanism, giving the model greater flexibility to modulate deeper-layer attention patterns per instance while the shared Z_S acts as a regularizer against overfitting.

## Results

On the GLUE benchmark (RoBERTa-355M), LoPA achieves an average score of 90.53 with only 1.60M tunable parameters, within 0.8 points of full fine-tuning (91.35, 355M params) and 0.7 points of LoRA (91.26, 2.36M params). On code tasks across six foundation models (350M–8B), LoPA consistently outperforms zero-shot baselines by 5–15 points on CruxEval and matches or exceeds LoRA in 11 out of 24 total test cases. LoPA uses roughly two-thirds the parameters of LoRA while maintaining comparable accuracy, and requires no server-side adapter storage.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LoPA decisively outperforms standard prompt tuning (PT, Lester et al.) by 28.62 points on average across GLUE, and DePT (Shi et al.) by 25.39 points, confirming that instance-conditioned soft prompts are far more effective than fixed task-level prompts. Against S-IDPG (Wu et al.), which also generates per-instance prompts, LoPA gains 5.36 points on average, with particularly large margins in low-data settings (12.5 points on MRPC, 6.13 on RTE), attributed to the coupled gating between task and instance components. LoPA is within 0.7 points of LoRA (Hu et al.) on average GLUE score while using 760K fewer parameters, and actually outperforms LoRA in 11 of 24 total test cases across all tasks and models. On code tasks, LoPA matches or beats LoRA on CruxEval-I for all models except Llama-3 (gains of 2–4 points on DeepSeek-Coder-7B and Phi-3), while using roughly two-thirds the parameters. Compared to p-tuning-v2 (Liu et al.), LoPA improves by ~12 points on average, and versus prefix-tuning (Li et al.), LoPA achieves comparable average scores (90.53 vs. 89.78) with 16× fewer parameters (1.60M vs. 25.75M) and no server-side modification required.

## Relation to Prior Work

LoPA sits at the intersection of two prompt-tuning lineages: instance-agnostic soft prompting (prompt tuning [15], DePT [30]) that learns a single task-level prefix, and instance-dependent prompt generation (IDPG [38]) that encodes a per-sample prompt via an MLP. The key departure is that LoPA explicitly models both a shared task-specific component (Z_S) and a per-instance component (Z_I), combining them through a sigmoid gating function rather than a linear sum or concatenation, and applies a low-rank decomposition to Z_I to keep parameter count small. This positions LoPA as a middle ground between the under-parameterized fixed prompts of PT and the over-parameterized per-sample prompts of IDPG, while retaining the server-free deployment advantage of all input-prefix methods over adapter-based approaches like LoRA or prefix-tuning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that LoPA eliminates the need to store and route task-specific adapters on the FM server—users simply prepend a computed soft prompt to their query—making multi-tenant customization cheaper and more privacy-preserving. The paper's ablations show that the gating mechanism is essential (concatenation or max of Z_S and Z_I degrades performance by 2–7 points), and that the low-rank factorization of Z_I is what keeps parameter counts competitive with LoRA. However, the authors acknowledge that validation is limited to standard NLU and code benchmarks; whether LoPA generalizes to obscure, long-tail user tasks where LoRA or full fine-tuning may still be necessary remains an open question. The paper also raises an intriguing but unexplored interpretation of the soft prompt as a conditional auto-encoder latent code, which could open a new design space.
