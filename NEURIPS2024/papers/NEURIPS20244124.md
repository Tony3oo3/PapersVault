## Problem

Q-learning with linear function approximation is known to diverge under the deadly triad (off-policy, bootstrapping, function approximation), and existing convergence guarantees require impractical assumptions on the behavior policy or restricted transition structures. No single-timescale Q-learning algorithm with linear function approximation was known to converge under mild, verifiable conditions.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes i.i.d. state-action-reward-next-state tuples (s_k, a_k, r_{k+1}, s_{k+1}) sampled from a fixed behavior distribution d(s,a) > 0, along with a pre-specified feature matrix X ∈ R^{|S||A|×h} whose columns are orthogonal and non-negative. It outputs a parameter vector θ ∈ R^h approximating the Q-function, from which the greedy policy π(s) = argmax_a x(s,a)^T θ is extracted. The regularization weight η must exceed a computable threshold (condition 15) that depends on the feature matrix norms and the transition/visit distributions.

## Method

RegQ modifies the standard Q-learning update by subtracting an L2 regularization term ηθ_k, yielding θ_{k+1} = θ_k + α_k(x(s_k,a_k)δ_k − ηθ_k). The key insight is that this single modification makes the associated ODE a switched linear system whose subsystem matrices are either strictly negatively row-dominating or negative-definite, guaranteeing a common quadratic Lyapunov function and hence global asymptotic stability. Convergence is proven via the Borkar-Meyn ODE framework combined with upper and lower comparison systems (a switching-system technique from Lee and He 2020), establishing that θ_k → θ_η* almost surely, where θ_η* is the unique solution of the regularized projected Bellman equation. Unlike two-timescale baselines (CQL, Greedy-GQ), RegQ is single-timescale and requires no target network or projection step, and the regularization parameter can be set by simple feature scaling (Lemma 3.3) to satisfy the contraction condition γ‖Γ_η‖_∞ < 1.

## Results

The paper's primary experimental contribution is demonstrating convergence of RegQ in the Tsitsiklis-Van Roy θ→2θ and Baird Seven Star environments, where standard Q-learning with linear function approximation is known to diverge. RegQ achieves faster convergence than the two time-scale baselines (GGQ and CQL) in both counter-examples, as shown in the learning curves of Figure 2. In the Mountain Car environment (where Q-learning already converges), RegQ also performs competitively. Experiments with varying η ∈ {2⁻², 2⁻¹, 1, 2} and learning rates {0.01, 0.05} show that convergence rate generally improves as η increases, with no divergence observed across tested configurations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RegQ is compared against three baselines: Greedy-GQ (GGQ, Maei et al. 2010), Coupled Q-Learning (CQL, Carvalho et al. 2020), and Q-learning with target network (Qtarget, Zhang et al. 2021). Against GGQ and CQL, both of which are two time-scale algorithms, RegQ (single time-scale) achieves visibly faster convergence in both the θ→2θ and Baird Seven Star environments, as the paper explicitly states it 'performs faster than other two time-scale Q-learning algorithms.' Against Qtarget, RegQ avoids the slow convergence rate typically associated with target network updates (citing Kim et al. 2019), while also avoiding the projection step that makes Qtarget difficult to implement practically. All three baselines converge in the tested environments, but RegQ reaches the fixed point in fewer iterations. The paper does not report a single scalar metric (e.g., final loss or number of episodes to convergence threshold); comparisons are made via learning curves, making precise quantitative gaps difficult to extract.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Q-learning convergence literature by showing that a simple L2 regularization term added to the standard Q-learning update suffices to guarantee convergence under linear function approximation, avoiding the strong behavior-policy assumptions required by Melo et al. (2008) and Lee and He (2020). It is directly motivated by Bharadwaj Diddigi et al. (2020), who showed L2 regularization stabilizes off-policy TD-learning, and adapts that insight to the Q-learning setting. Unlike two-time-scale convergent variants (Carvalho et al. 2020, Maei et al. 2010) and target-network-based methods (Zhang et al. 2021, Chen et al. 2023), RegQ is single time-scale and avoids the target network entirely, while its convergence proof follows the switching-system + Borkar-Meyn ODE framework of Lee and He (2020) with new upper/lower comparison system constructions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that a one-line modification (adding ηθ to the Q-learning update) resolves the deadly triad divergence for linear function approximation without requiring target networks, two time-scales, or restrictive assumptions on the sampling distribution. The paper provides an error bound showing the regularization-induced bias vanishes as η→0 when the PBE solution exists, and is tight in both η→0 and η→∞ limits. Key open questions include whether the approach extends to nonlinear (deep) function approximation without target networks, and whether a bias-free convergent Q-learning variant can be designed. The scope is currently limited to linear function approximation with orthogonal features, which restricts immediate applicability to modern deep RL pipelines.
