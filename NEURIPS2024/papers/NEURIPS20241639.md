## Problem

Previous stereotype mitigation work in text-to-image diffusion models has focused exclusively on stereotypes engendered by individual objects (e.g., a 'politician' always appearing male), leaving unaddressed the more subtle association-engendered stereotypes that emerge only when multiple objects co-occur in a prompt. For instance, 'black people' and 'houses' separately produce no bias, but 'black people and a house' triggers a pronounced stereotype linking Black people to poorer housing. This paper is the first to formally define and tackle this multi-object association bias in T2I generation.

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard text prompt (including multi-object prompts like 'a photo of a black people and a house') and produces a stereotype-mitigated RGB image through the existing T2I diffusion pipeline. The sensitive constraints generated by the Sensitive Transformer are concatenated with the original prompt embedding to form an augmented input for the diffusion sampling process, so the T2I model itself is not retrained. The framework is applied to five mainstream pipelines (SD-1.5, SDXL, Lightning, Turbo, Cascade) at 512×512 or 1024×1024 resolution, and also generalizes to multi-model configurations involving LoRA and ControlNet.

## Method

MAS models stereotype mitigation as a probability distribution alignment problem: the goal is to shift the stereotyped sensitive-attribute distribution of generated images toward a stereotype-free target distribution. The framework has two core components. First, PIS CLIP extends the standard CLIP architecture from a two-way text–image mapping to a three-way prompt–image–stereotype mapping, trained with a symmetric cross-entropy loss over three paired similarity objectives to learn how prompts induce specific stereotypes in generated images. Second, a Sensitive Transformer uses the learned prompt-to-stereotype mapping as keys, the prompt embedding as query, and sensitive-constraint vectors as values in a scaled dot-product attention mechanism to produce prompt-specific sensitive constraints. These constraints are concatenated with the original prompt embedding and injected into the diffusion sampling loop, guiding the model to produce images whose sensitive-attribute distribution approximates the stereotype-free distribution without retraining the base T2I model. The authors also introduce the SDTV (Stereotype-Distribution-Total-Variation) metric, which uses maximum total variation distance across sensitive-attribute value pairs to quantify stereotype severity in a way that captures extreme biases that average-based metrics would dilute.

## Results

MAS achieves the lowest SDTV (lower = fewer stereotypes) across all four stereotype categories on SD-1.5: 0.17 (S-O&S-SA Gender), 0.21 (S-O&M-SA G.×R.), 0.17 (M-O&S-SA Gender), and 0.16 (M-O&M-SA). MAS is the only method evaluated that covers all four categories, including the two association-engendered stereotype types (M-O&S-SA and M-O&M-SA) where all other baselines report no results. Semantic preservation (CLIP-T2I) is maintained at 0.39, matching the original SD-1.5 baseline of 0.40. The approach generalizes across five T2I models (SD-1.5, SDXL, Lightning, Turbo, Cascade) and complex multi-model pipelines (R-SD + LoRA + ControlNet).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MAS outperforms all six baselines on the majority of SDTV metrics and is the only method that evaluates all four stereotype categories. On S-O&S-SA Gender, MAS (0.17) beats the strongest baseline Shen 2024 (0.22) by ~23%. On S-O&S-SA Race, MAS (0.21) substantially outperforms the best baseline Bansal 2022 (0.37) by ~43%. On S-O&M-SA G.×R., Bansal 2022 (0.19) edges out MAS (0.21) slightly, and on M-O&S-SA Race, Shen 2024 (0.19) is marginally better than MAS (0.20). However, MAS is the sole method that reports results on M-O&S-SA Region (0.20) and M-O&M-SA (0.16), the two association-engendered stereotype categories that all other baselines cannot address. Semantic preservation (CLIP-T2I) is maintained at 0.39, on par with the original model and most baselines, confirming that stereotype mitigation does not degrade prompt-image alignment.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the T2I stereotype mitigation line (Kim et al. 2023, Chuang et al. 2023, Shen et al. 2024, He et al. 2024) by identifying and addressing a gap those works leave open: stereotypes arising from the *association* of multiple objects rather than from a single object's sensitive attribute. Methodologically, it repurposes CLIP's contrastive framework into a three-way 'prompt–image–stereotype' mapping (PIS CLIP) and borrows the Transformer attention mechanism to generate prompt-specific sensitive constraints, framing the overall problem as probability distribution alignment in the spirit of He et al. 2024 but applied to joint multi-object distributions. The SDTV evaluation metric departs from the proportion-based metrics used by Saravanan et al. 2023 and Wang et al. 2023a by using total variation distance over attribute-value distributions, with a deliberate choice of maximum rather than mean to avoid underestimating extreme stereotypes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By being plug-and-play (no retraining of the base T2I model) and generalizing across five architectures and multi-model pipelines (LoRA, ControlNet), MAS offers a practical path for deploying stereotype mitigation in production T2I systems without the cost of fine-tuning. The SDTV metric provides a more principled, distribution-based evaluation that prior proportion-based metrics cannot capture for multi-object scenarios. Open questions remain: the approach targets a predefined set of sensitive attributes and object pairings, and the authors acknowledge that subtler, emergent stereotypes beyond the four defined categories are not addressed. The distribution-alignment formulation also suggests potential transfer to debiasing and detoxification in other generative modalities, which the authors flag as future work.
