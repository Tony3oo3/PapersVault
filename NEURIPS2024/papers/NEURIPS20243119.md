## Problem

Existing Automatic Prompt Optimization (APO) methods for text-to-image diffusion models are trained assuming a fixed model, making them impractical as new fine-tuned checkpoints (via LoRA, DreamBooth) emerge continuously. The paper formalizes Model-Generalized APO (MGAPO): train an APO pipeline on a limited set of known diffusion models so that optimized prompts generalize to previously unseen models without retraining, while preserving both semantic consistency and aesthetic quality.

## Input / Output

At inference, the method takes a natural language description (e.g., a multi-sentence caption generated by LLaVA) and the parameters of an unseen SD 1.5 fine-tuned checkpoint, and produces a 512×512 RGB image conditioned on an optimized prompt representation in CLIP text-encoder feature space. During training, each source-domain sample provides a triple of (natural language prompt, manually crafted keyword prompt, generated image) paired with a specific checkpoint; the ICL stage additionally retrieves 5 semantically similar prompt pairs from the dataset as demonstrations for GPT-3.5. The adapter output is a fixed-length token sequence (L×D) that replaces the standard CLIP text embedding as the cross-attention condition for the frozen denoiser.

## Method

AP-Adapter is a two-stage framework. Stage 1 uses in-context learning with GPT-3.5: Sentence-BERT retrieves the top-k most similar (natural, manual) prompt pairs as demonstrations, and GPT-3.5 rewrites the input natural language into a keyword prompt closer to manual prompts in CLIP space. Stage 2 introduces domain prototypes for model generalization: intermediate CLIP image-encoder features (18th layer of ViT-L/14) are processed through a DomainDrop-inspired channel-dropping mechanism guided by a domain discriminator, and the retained domain-specific channels are aligned to learnable prototypes via KL divergence. At inference, the Wasserstein distance between the keyword-prompt embedding and each source-domain prototype yields softmax-normalized weights, producing a weighted Prototype-Anchored Embedding that is concatenated with the prompt tokens and passed through a lightweight adapter (two transformer encoder layers + a zero-initialized linear layer). The adapter output is aligned to manual-prompt CLIP embeddings via MSE loss, while a diffusion denoising loss preserves generation fidelity; training alternates between updating prototypes (with alignment + discriminator losses) and updating the adapter (with alignment + denoising losses) to prevent interference.

## Results

On the target-domain (unseen model) evaluation, AP-Adapter achieves the best scores across all seven metrics: Color 0.477, Shape 0.422, Texture 0.452, BlipScore 0.332, Aesthetic Score 6.384, ImageReward 0.427, and HPS 0.218. The method narrows the gap to manual prompts (GT) substantially on image quality (Aesthetic 6.384 vs. 6.564; ImageReward 0.427 vs. 0.782) while matching or exceeding all APO baselines on semantic consistency. Ablation studies confirm that the domain-style distillation loss L_a contributes the largest single improvement (BlipScore 0.313→0.324, ImageReward 0.258→0.384), and that dropping domain-insensitive channels via the domain discriminator outperforms both random channel selection and no-prototype settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AP-Adapter outperforms all four APO baselines on every reported metric. Against the closest representation-optimization baseline, SUR-adapter, it gains +0.005 on Color, +0.009 on Shape, +0.003 on Texture, +0.007 on BlipScore, +0.375 on Aesthetic Score, +0.141 on ImageReward, and +0.020 on HPS. Against the text-optimization baselines (MagicPrompt, PromptPerfect, Promptist), the advantage is larger on semantic consistency (e.g., BlipScore 0.332 vs. 0.292–0.302) and ImageReward (0.427 vs. 0.066–0.124), while HPS is competitive (0.218 vs. 0.202–0.211). Notably, combining ICL-based keyword prompts with SUR-adapter (AKP+SUR-adapter) actually underperforms either component alone on several metrics, suggesting that the domain-prototype mechanism in AP-Adapter is not simply additive. The remaining gap to manual prompts is most pronounced on ImageReward (0.427 vs. 0.782) and BlipScore (0.332 vs. 0.400), indicating that fully matching human-crafted prompt quality on unseen models remains an open challenge.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends SUR-adapter's approach of aligning natural language prompt representations with manually designed prompts in CLIP feature space by introducing domain prototypes that encode model-specific information, enabling generalization to unseen diffusion models. The channel-dropping mechanism for extracting domain-specific features is directly adapted from DomainDrop, repurposing a domain generalization technique for the prompt optimization setting. The first-stage ICL-based prompt rewriting builds on in-context learning literature, using GPT-3.5 with similarity-retrieved demonstrations to convert natural language descriptions into keyword prompts. Conceptually, the paper's key departure is reframing APO as a domain generalization problem where each fine-tuned SD checkpoint constitutes a distinct domain, a formulation absent from prior APO work that assumes a fixed target model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As LoRA and DreamBooth make it trivial to create personalized SD checkpoints, the inability of APO methods to transfer across models becomes a practical bottleneck; this paper provides the first systematic treatment of that gap with a dedicated benchmark. However, the dataset is limited to single-character images from the CIVITAI community, and the number of domain prototypes must grow linearly with the number of source models, raising scalability concerns for the thousands of checkpoints that exist in practice. Open questions include whether parameter-level differences between models (rather than image-level domain signals) could be exploited more directly, and whether the approach extends to fundamentally different diffusion architectures rather than fine-tuned variants of SD 1.5.
