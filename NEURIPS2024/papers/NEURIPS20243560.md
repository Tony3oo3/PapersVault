## Problem

Prior work on privatizing low-switching online learning algorithms achieved regret with a T^{1/3}/ε dependence, implying a normalized regret of 1/(T^{2/3}ε) that deviates from the standard Tε scaling seen in most private optimization settings. It was unclear whether these transformations had fully exploited the potential of lazy algorithms, and whether better privacy-regret trade-offs were achievable through this approach.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243560/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The L2P transformation takes as input a lazy online learning algorithm whose measures {μ_t} satisfy a bounded δ₀-approximate max divergence (D_∞^{δ₀}(μ̄_{t+1}, μ̄_t) ≤ η) and a data-independent update rule, along with a batch size B and fake switching probability p. It produces a (ε,δ)-DP algorithm that outputs a prediction x_t at each round, where the algorithm maintains a parallel hidden sequence {y_t} (never revealed) used solely to normalize correlated sampling ratios. The key structural constraint is that the switching probability at round t depends only on the current loss ℓ_t rather than the full history, which is what enables the improved privacy composition.

## Method

The L2P transformation introduces two key techniques to convert lazy algorithms into private ones. First, it deploys a novel correlated sampling strategy that constructs a parallel sequence {y_t} (sampled independently of x_t) to normalize the ratio μ̄_t(x_{t-1})/μ̄_{t-1}(x_{t-1}) · μ̄_{t-1}(y_{t-1})/μ̄_t(y_{t-1}), ensuring the switching probability is a function of only the current loss ℓ_t rather than the entire history—this prevents the accumulation of privacy cost across rounds. Second, it provides a new regret analysis for batched lazy algorithms showing that grouping B rounds together adds only O(TB²η²) to regret, significantly improving over the prior O(B/η) bound from [AFKT23b], which allows larger batch sizes to reduce the number of switches and save privacy budget. Applied to multiplicative weights for DP-OPE and regularized multiplicative weights for DP-OCO, the transformation yields regret O(√(T log d) + T^{1/3} log d / ε^{2/3}) and O(√T + T^{1/3}√d / ε^{2/3}) respectively, improving the ε-dependence from 1/ε to 1/ε^{2/3} in the high-privacy regime. A matching lower bound for the natural family of low-switching private algorithms confirms these rates are optimal within that class.

## Results

For DP-OPE, the L2P transformation applied to multiplicative weights achieves regret $O(\sqrt{T\log d} + T^{1/3}\log d / \varepsilon^{2/3})$, improving the privacy-dependent term from $1/\varepsilon$ to $1/\varepsilon^{2/3}$ over prior work. For DP-OCO, applying L2P to regularized multiplicative weights yields regret $O(\sqrt{T} + T^{1/3}\sqrt{d}/\varepsilon^{2/3})$, eliminating the additional $T^{3/8}\sqrt{d}/\varepsilon^{3/4}$ term present in prior lazy-based methods. A matching lower bound of $\Omega(\sqrt{T} + T^{1/3}/\varepsilon^{2/3})$ is established for the natural family of low-switching private algorithms in the DP-OPE setting, confirming near-optimality of the upper bound up to logarithmic factors.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the DP-OPE baselines of Agarwal & Singh (ICML 2017) and Asi et al. (COLT 2023), which achieve regret with a $T^{1/3}\log d / \varepsilon$ privacy term, this paper improves the exponent on $\varepsilon$ from $-1$ to $-2/3$, yielding a strictly better rate in the high-privacy regime ($\varepsilon \ll 1$). Concretely, matching the non-private $\sqrt{T\log d}$ regret now requires only $\varepsilon \geq T^{-1/4}$ instead of $\varepsilon \geq T^{-1/6}$, and advanced composition allows solving $K \approx \sqrt{T}$ sequential DP-OPE instances with $\varepsilon=1$ versus $K \approx T^{1/3}$ for prior work. For DP-OCO, the paper outperforms both the DP-FTRL approach of Kairouz et al. (2021), which gives $d^{1/4}\sqrt{T}/\sqrt{\varepsilon}$, and the lazy regularized multiplicative weights of Agarwal et al. (2023), which gives $\sqrt{T} + T^{1/3}\sqrt{d}/\varepsilon + T^{3/8}\sqrt{d}/\varepsilon^{3/4}$, by achieving the cleaner $\sqrt{T} + T^{1/3}\sqrt{d}/\varepsilon^{2/3}$ bound. The lower bound in Section 4 shows that the DP-OPE rate is essentially tight for all low-switching private algorithms, indicating that fundamentally new techniques beyond limited switching are needed for further improvements.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the 2023 line of work connecting low-switching (lazy) online learning algorithms to differentially private online learning, initiated by Asi et al. [AFKT23b] for DP-OPE and extended by Agarwal et al. [AKST23a, AKST23b] for DP-OCO. The key departure is the L2P transformation, which unifies and improves upon these prior privatization strategies through two new techniques: a correlated-sampling switching rule that isolates privacy cost to a single round (via a parallel y-sequence), and a new batching analysis showing additive regret of TB²η² rather than the prior B/η. The transformation is general, applying to any lazy algorithm satisfying a mild divergence condition, and is instantiated with multiplicative weights for DP-OPE and regularized multiplicative weights for DP-OCO.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The improvement from 1/ε to 1/ε^(2/3) in the privacy-dependent regret term is practically significant in the high-privacy regime (ε≪1), reducing the privacy budget needed to match non-private regret from T^(-1/6) to T^(-1/4) for DP-OPE. The matching lower bound for the low-switching family establishes that these rates are optimal within this natural class, indicating that fundamentally new techniques beyond limited switching are needed for further improvements. Open questions include whether the lower bound extends to all private algorithms (not just low-switching ones), and whether DP-OCO can achieve the more standard normalized regret of d^(1/3)/(εT)^(2/3) rather than the current √d/(εT)^(2/3).
