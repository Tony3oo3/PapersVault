## Problem

Existing low-light enhancement methods face a dual limitation: Retinex-based frameworks primarily model global illumination degradation while neglecting local artifacts like noise and blur, and CNN/Transformer architectures struggle to capture long-range degradation due to limited receptive fields. State space models (SSMs) offer linear-complexity global modeling but, as demonstrated by MambaIR, lack the local invariance needed to preserve neighborhood structure in 2D visual data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240862/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a low-light RGB image (H×W×3) and produces an enhanced RGB image of the same resolution via a residual addition (N = F_out + L). During processing, the input is augmented with two illumination priors—the channel-wise maximum and mean maps—concatenated into a 5-channel tensor that guides both the state space scan and the selective kernel module. Training uses 128×128 random crops with rotation and flipping augmentation, and the final output is produced by a 3×3 convolution projecting back to 3 channels.

## Method

MambaLLIE is a U-shaped encoder-decoder whose core building block is the Global-then-Local State Space Block (GLSSB), which interleaves a Local-Enhanced State Space Module (LESSM) and an Implicit Retinex-aware Selective Kernel (IRSK) with layer normalization. LESSM augments the standard 2D selective scan with an illumination-prior-dependent local bias term e(L_p) added directly to the output, preserving neighborhood dependencies that fixed-direction scans would otherwise disrupt. IRSK constructs a sequence of depth-wise convolutions with growing kernel sizes (3×3, 3×5, 5×5) and uses sigmoid-activated illumination priors to produce spatially-varying selection weights, adaptively fusing multi-scale local features without explicit Retinex decomposition. The implicit Retinex design lets the network learn to segregate positive and negative illumination components within the selective kernel weights, avoiding the multi-branch constraints of explicit Retinex methods while still providing physical guidance.

## Results

MambaLLIE achieves state-of-the-art PSNR/SSIM across all five paired benchmark datasets: 22.95/0.847 on LOL-V2-real, 25.87/0.940 on LOL-V2-syn, 29.26/0.818 on SMID, 30.12/0.900 on SDSD-indoor, and 30.00/0.869 on SDSD-outdoor, with only 2.28M parameters and 20.85G FLOPS. On the ExDark object detection task, MambaLLIE achieves the best mean mAP of 0.768, and on the DARK FACE dataset it reaches 0.491 mAP for face detection. In the user study (70 participants), MambaLLIE scores highest on overall rating (4.243/5), local detail (4.129/5), and artifact/noise (4.100/5).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MambaLLIE outperforms the strongest Transformer-based baseline Retinexformer by an average of ~0.2 dB PSNR across all five datasets (e.g., +0.35 dB on SDSD-indoor, +0.20 dB on LOL-V2-syn) while using slightly more parameters (2.28M vs 1.61M). Against the closest SSM-based competitor MambaIR, MambaLLIE gains 1.70 dB on LOL-V2-real, 2.19 dB on SMID, and 1.15 dB on SDSD-indoor, with a notable efficiency advantage (20.85G vs 60.66G FLOPS). Compared to the earlier Transformer SNR-Net, MambaLLIE improves by ~1 dB PSNR on average across all datasets. Against traditional Retinex-based methods (RetinexNet, DeepUPE, KinD), MambaLLIE achieves gains exceeding 7 dB PSNR on all datasets. On downstream tasks, MambaLLIE achieves the best mean mAP (0.768) on ExDark object detection and the highest face detection mAP (0.491) on DARK FACE, surpassing Retinexformer (0.757/0.486) and MambaIR (0.751/0.482). In the no-reference perceptual evaluation (MUSIQ/NIMA) on five unpaired datasets, MambaLLIE leads or ties with Retinexformer and MambaIR in most comparisons, and outperforms the diffusion-based DiffLL consistently.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: Retinex-based low light enhancement (RetinexNet, KinD, Retinexformer), visual state space models (MambaIR, VMamba, Vim), and selective kernel mechanisms (LSKNet). It departs from explicit Retinex methods by replacing multi-branch illumination/reflectance decomposition with an implicit Retinex-aware selective kernel that uses max/mean illumination priors as conditioning signals. The core architectural departure from prior SSM-based vision models is the 'global-then-local' design: LESSM augments the 2D selective scan with a local bias term to preserve neighborhood structure, while IRSK applies spatially-varying depth-wise convolutions selected by illumination priors, addressing the known weakness of SSMs in capturing local 2D dependencies.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that state space models, with appropriate local-awareness modifications, can match or exceed Transformer-based methods for low light enhancement at comparable parameter budgets (2.28M vs. 1.61M for Retinexformer), offering a viable linear-complexity alternative for this task. The implicit Retinex framing avoids the architectural complexity of explicit decomposition while still leveraging physical priors, and the selective kernel behavior (learning complementary positive/negative illumination components) provides an interpretable mechanism. Limitations include the hand-crafted illumination prior design and reliance on PSNR/SSIM as primary metrics; the authors note future work on low light video enhancement as a natural extension.
