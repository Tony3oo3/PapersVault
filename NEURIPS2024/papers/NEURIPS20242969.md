## Problem

Existing OOD generalization methods (ERM, IRM-based) fail to align semantic feature prototypes across different environments, causing poor generalization when test-time spurious correlations differ from training. Additionally, most approaches require pre-partitioned environment labels that are unavailable in real-world settings. This paper identifies the misalignment of class prototypes across environments as the root cause and proposes using the geometric structure of neural collapse (simplex ETF) to enforce alignment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242969/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images of varying resolutions (28×28 for ColoredMNIST, 64×64 for ColoredCOCO/COCOPlaces, 224×224 for NICO) along with optional environment labels. It produces two outputs: a class prediction and a learned semantic mask that decomposes the input into invariant (semantic) and variable (spurious) components. The mask can operate at either the pixel level or the feature level, and the method supports both known and unknown environment settings, with the latter requiring automatic environment partitioning via local models.

## Method

NCFAL alternates between environment partitioning and mask learning, guided by a fixed simplex ETF classifier. When environment labels are unknown, local models are trained on spurious features (Ψ(x) = (1−m)⊙x) for each environment, and samples are assigned to the environment whose local model gives the highest logit at the true label position. For mask learning, the semantic features (Φ(x) = m⊙x) are projected and normalized, then pulled toward the corresponding vertex of a pre-fixed simplex ETF using a balanced cross-entropy loss with a learnable temperature β and class-frequency weighting γ to handle inter-environment class imbalance. Random noise is added to the mask during training to encourage exploration, followed by clipping to [0,1]. After convergence, the learned mask extracts semantic features that are fed into a standard CNN (4-layer CNN, ResNet8, or ResNet18 depending on dataset) for final predictive model training, achieving OOD accuracy gains of 5–15% over the best baselines across four datasets.

## Results

On the four OOD benchmark datasets, NCFAL achieves 66.4% OOD accuracy on ColoredMNIST, 58.0% on ColoredCOCO, 33.3% on COCOPlaces, and 85.4% on NICO (with known environments). Without environment labels, the method still achieves 66.9%, 56.9%, 36.7%, and 85.9% respectively, demonstrating that automatic environment partitioning can even outperform manual partitioning on COCOPlaces (36.7% vs. 33.3%). The method outperforms all 16 OOD baselines and ERM across every dataset, with the largest gains on ColoredMNIST (+6.1% over IRM) and COCOPlaces (+6.2% over IRM).

## Baselines & Comparisons

NCFAL outperforms all 16 OOD baselines and ERM on every dataset. On ColoredMNIST, it achieves 66.4%, surpassing the strongest baseline IRM (60.3%) by 6.1 points and ERM (51.5%) by 14.9 points. On ColoredCOCO, NCFAL reaches 58.0%, beating the best baseline CausIRL_CORAL (51.5%) by 6.5 points. On COCOPlaces, the gap is most pronounced: NCFAL achieves 33.3% versus IRM's 27.1% (+6.2 points) and ERM's 20.1% (+13.2 points). On the real-world NICO dataset, NCFAL attains 85.4%, exceeding the best baseline MMD (78.2%) by 7.2 points. Notably, the environment-free variant (Ours w/o env) matches or exceeds the known-environment variant on ColoredMNIST (66.9% vs. 66.4%), COCOPlaces (36.7% vs. 33.3%), and NICO (85.9% vs. 85.4%), suggesting that learned environment partitioning can be more effective than manual annotation, particularly on complex backgrounds.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the neural collapse phenomenon (Papyan et al., 2020) — previously studied as a training-time geometric property of balanced classifiers — as an explicit alignment target for out-of-distribution generalization. It departs from the dominant OOD regularization lineage (IRM, VREx, GroupDRO, SANDMask) by replacing gradient/variance-based invariance penalties with a fixed simplex ETF classifier that geometrically constrains semantic features to collapse to the same positions across environments. The automatic environment partitioning via local spurious-feature models extends prior work that assumed known environment labels, and the mask-learning objective (Eq. 5) with class-balanced weighting is a new loss formulation not present in prior OOD or neural-collapse literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a practical path for OOD generalization when environment labels are unavailable, which is the more realistic deployment scenario, and the F-norm analysis provides a concrete diagnostic for why ERM/IRM fail to align prototypes across environments. However, validation is limited to three synthetic/toy datasets and one real dataset with only two classes, leaving open whether the approach scales to high-dimensional, multi-class, or continuous-shift settings. The alternating environment-partitioning and mask-learning loop adds implementation complexity, and the pixel-level mask assumption may not transfer to tasks where semantic and spurious information are entangled at the feature level rather than the pixel level.
