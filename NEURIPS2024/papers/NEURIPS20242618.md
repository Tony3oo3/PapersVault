## Problem

DCCA-based multi-view representation learning methods exhibit a previously unexplored failure mode called model collapse, where downstream-task performance degrades sharply as training progresses. The paper identifies the root cause as the tendency of over-parameterized DNN weight matrices to become low-rank and redundant, a property that Linear CCA avoids because its single-layer weight matrices are constrained to be full-rank by the loss function. This collapse makes early stopping difficult and hinders the adoption of DCCA in large-scale applications.

## Input / Output

The method takes K views of the same object, each with potentially different feature dimensions (e.g., 1984-d HOG, 512-d GIST, 928-d SIFT for Caltech101; 1024-d visual + 300-d text for CUB; multiple 28×28 MNIST images for PolyMnist). Training is fully unsupervised, requiring only the multi-view data and a hyperparameter α weighting the noise regularization term. The output is a unified representation Z formed by concatenating the per-view network outputs, which is then used for downstream regression (Ridge Regression, R² metric) or classification (SVC, F1 score). The noise regularization additionally requires generating i.i.d. Gaussian white noise matrices matching each view's shape at every epoch.

## Method

NR-DCCA augments standard DCCA with a noise regularization (NR) loss that enforces the Correlation Invariant Property (CIP): the correlation between each view and random Gaussian noise must remain unchanged before and after the network transformation, i.e., ζ_k = |Corr(f_k(X_k), f_k(A_k)) − Corr(X_k, A_k)| is minimized. The key theoretical result (Theorem 1) proves that CIP holds if and only if the transformation matrix is full-rank, so the NR loss indirectly constrains DNN weight matrices against the low-rank collapse that degrades DCCA. A second theorem (Theorem 2) shows that full-rank weight matrices guarantee zero reconstruction loss and bounded denoising loss, linking the regularization to representation quality. The approach is pluggable: it requires no architectural changes, adds O(2K(MK)³) per-epoch cost for the extra correlation computation, and generalizes directly to DGCCA (as NR-DGCCA). The paper also introduces a synthetic data construction framework parameterized by a 'common rate' controlling the fraction of shared dimensions between views, enabling controlled evaluation of MVRL methods.

## Results

On real-world datasets, NR-DCCA achieves F1 scores of 0.993 on PolyMnist(5 views), 0.921 on CUB, and 0.625 on Caltech101, consistently outperforming all DCCA-based baselines. On CUB, NR-DCCA (0.921) surpasses DCCA (0.805) by 11.6 points and MVTCAE (0.900) by 2.1 points. On synthetic datasets, NR-DCCA maintains stable R2 scores of ~0.31–0.32 across all common rates (0%–100%), while DCCA degrades from 0.284 at epoch 100 to 0.106 at epoch 1200, demonstrating the model collapse that NR-DCCA prevents.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NR-DCCA wins decisively on CUB (0.921 F1), outperforming DCCA by 11.6 points, DCCAE by 7.1 points, DCCA_PRIVATE by 6.8 points, and even MVTCAE by 2.1 points. On Caltech101, NR-DCCA (0.625) beats DCCA (0.604), DGCCA (0.617), and DCCAE (0.605), while DCCA_PRIVATE and DGCCA_PRIVATE collapse to 0.480 and 0.476 respectively. On PolyMnist with 5 views, NR-DCCA (0.993) surpasses DCCA (0.938) by 5.5 points and DCCAE (0.987) by 0.6 points. The key differentiator is stability: DCCA-based methods show significant performance degradation over training epochs (model collapse), while NR-DCCA maintains consistent performance, as evidenced by the much lower variance in synthetic dataset results (±0.04–0.05 vs. ±0.04–0.06 for collapsed methods). The NR approach also generalizes to DGCCA, with NR-DGCCA achieving comparable gains (0.917 on CUB vs. 0.790 for DGCCA).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the DCCA lineage (Andrew et al. 2013, Benton et al. 2017) by identifying a previously unacknowledged failure mode—model collapse—and attributing it to the low-rank tendency of DNN weight matrices. The theoretical core draws on Linear CCA's full-rank guarantee (Wang et al. 2015) to define the Correlation Invariant Property (CIP), then repurposes noise regularization from supervised learning (Sietsma & Dow 1991, Bishop 1995) as a mechanism to enforce CIP in deep networks. The synthetic dataset construction framework with tunable common rates is a new evaluation tool for MVRL, complementing existing real-world benchmarks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Model collapse is a practical barrier to adopting DCCA-based methods in large-scale or long-training settings, and this paper provides both a diagnosis (low-rank weight matrices) and a minimal, theoretically justified fix (a single NR loss term). The CIP theorem gives a clean equivalence between full-rank weights and correlation invariance, offering a principled diagnostic tool beyond just performance monitoring. Open questions include whether the NR approach generalizes to contrastive learning or generative models, how it compares to orthogonality regularization or weight decay, and whether the square-matrix assumption in the theory limits applicability to architectures with mismatched input/output dimensions.
