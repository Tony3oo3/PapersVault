## Problem

Despite rapid progress in vision-language models, critical design choices—such as whether to use cross-attention or fully autoregressive architectures, which pre-trained backbones to pair, and how to handle image resolution—are often adopted without rigorous ablation. This makes it difficult for the community to identify which decisions genuinely improve performance versus those that are arbitrary, impeding grounded progress in the field.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242788/cropped_page7_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts RGB images at their native aspect ratio and variable resolution (no forced resizing to a fixed square), alongside a text prompt. Each image is encoded by SigLIP-SO400M and then pooled via a Perceiver resampler into 64 visual tokens (or 320 tokens when image splitting is applied at inference for OCR-heavy tasks). The output is a free-form text response generated autoregressively by the Mistral-7B language model, with no special structured output format required.

## Method

Idefics2 uses a fully autoregressive architecture where visual tokens (64 per image after Perceiver resampler pooling) are concatenated with text embeddings and fed jointly into the language model, rather than injecting image features via interleaved cross-attention layers. A key finding is that this architecture outperforms cross-attention when the pre-trained backbones are adapted via LoRA, whereas cross-attention wins only when backbones are frozen; LoRA also resolves training divergences that occur with full fine-tuning of the fully autoregressive setup. Pre-training is multi-stage: first at 384px resolution on OBELICS interleaved documents and image-text pairs, then at up to 980px with PDF documents added for OCR capability. During instruction fine-tuning on The Cauldron (50 vision-language datasets plus text-only instruction data), images are optionally split into 4 crops plus the original to trade inference compute for higher accuracy on text-reading benchmarks, and DoRA with NEFTune embedding noise is used to mitigate overfitting across multiple epochs.

## Results

Idefics2 (8B, 64 visual tokens per image) achieves 43.5 MMMU (val), 51.6 MathVista, 70.4 TextVQA, and 76.8 MMBench in zero-shot evaluation, outperforming all open VLMs in its size category. With image splitting (320 tokens), TextVQA improves to 73.0 while other benchmarks remain stable. The base model (Idefics2-base) scores 70.3 VQAv2, 57.9 TextVQA, 54.6 OKVQA, and 116.0 COCO CIDEr, surpassing Flamingo (9B) and MM1 (7B) on all four benchmarks while using only 64 tokens per image versus 144–720 for competitors.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against LLaVA-NeXT (13B, 2880 tokens/image), Idefics2 (8B, 64 tokens) wins on all four benchmarks: MMMU 43.5 vs 36.2, MathVista 51.6 vs 35.3, TextVQA 70.4 vs 67.1, and MMBench 76.8 vs 70.0, despite being 4× smaller and using 45× fewer visual tokens. Against DeepSeek-VL (7B, 576 tokens), Idefics2 leads on every metric, with the largest gap on MathVista (51.6 vs 36.1, +15.5 points). Against MM1-Chat (7B, 720 tokens), Idefics2 wins on MMMU (43.5 vs 37.0), MathVista (51.6 vs 35.9), and MMBench (76.8 vs 72.3) but trails slightly on TextVQA (70.4 vs 72.8); enabling image splitting closes this gap (73.0 vs 72.8). At the larger-model scale (Table 15), Idefics2 matches or exceeds MM1-Chat 30B on MathVista (51.6 vs 39.4) and MMBench (76.8 vs 75.1), and approaches Gemini 1.5 Pro on MathVista (51.6 vs 52.1), though it remains behind on MMMU and DocVQA.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself as a systematic empirical study filling a gap in the VLM literature: prior work has not properly ablated the choice between cross-attention (Flamingo, Idefics1) and fully autoregressive (LLaVA, BLIP-2) architectures under controlled conditions. It builds on the Perceiver resampler (Jaegle et al., 2021) as a trainable pooling mechanism and on NaViT-style aspect-ratio-preserving image processing (Dehghani et al., 2023), while leveraging the authors' own OBELICS dataset for pre-training. A key departure from prior work is the finding that LoRA-based training of pre-trained backbones reverses the architecture ranking—contradicting Karamcheti et al. (2024), who observed that unfreezing the visual backbone degrades performance—suggesting parameter-efficient fine-tuning is the critical differentiator.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides practitioners with clear, experimentally grounded guidance on architecture selection, token budget, and training stability for VLMs, reducing the trial-and-error that has characterized the field. The finding that 64 visual tokens (via Perceiver pooling) matches or exceeds performance of 576–2880-token configurations has direct implications for inference cost and deployment feasibility. Open questions remain around the lack of large well-trained open vision encoders, persistent hallucination issues, and the emerging vulnerability of VLMs to multimodal jailbreaking, all of which the authors flag as active research directions.
