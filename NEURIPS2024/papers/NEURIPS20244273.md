## Problem

Existing LLM uncertainty quantification methods are either prompt-wise rather than response-wise, consider only lexical rather than semantic information, require additional task-specific training, or are restricted to classification tasks. The state-of-the-art semantic entropy approach, while capturing semantic invariance, still assigns a single uncertainty score per prompt and uses only binary semantic equivalence, discarding the fine-grained semantic differences that could sharpen confidence estimation. This paper addresses these gaps with a response-wise, fine-grained, off-the-shelf confidence metric for free-form generation.

## Input / Output

Given a prompt x and a target response y*, the method samples M unique reference responses via diverse beam search from the same LLM and feeds each (target, reference) pair—concatenated with the prompt as shared context—into a pre-trained NLI classifier (DeBERTa-large-mnli) to obtain entailment, neutral, and contradiction probabilities. The output is a single scalar semantic density in [0,1], where 1 means all references are semantically equivalent to the target and 0 means all are contradictory, enabling practitioners to set a fixed confidence threshold. The method requires no training or fine-tuning of the LLM and integrates length-normalized sequence probabilities and temperature scaling directly into the density computation.

## Method

The core contribution adapts kernel density estimation to a prompt-specific semantic space where each response is represented by a contextual embedding of norm 1/2. Instead of relying on empirical frequencies (which would demand prohibitively many LLM inferences), the estimator uses the LLM's own explicit token-level sampling probabilities as weights, so each of the M reference responses needs to be sampled only once. Semantic distances are measured implicitly through an NLI model whose three-class output probabilities yield the expected squared distance between the target and each reference embedding. A dimension-invariant Epanechnikov kernel (with the gamma-function normalization removed) maps these expected distances to [0,1], guaranteeing the final semantic density is bounded and comparable across embedding dimensionalities. The resulting score is response-specific and captures graded semantic similarity rather than the binary equivalence used by prior semantic entropy methods.

## Results

Semantic density (SD) achieves the best AUROC in 26 out of 28 model–dataset combinations and the best AUPR in 27 out of 28, across seven SOTA LLMs (Llama-2-13B/70B, Llama-3-8B/70B, Mistral-7B, Mixtral-8x7B/8x22B) and four free-form QA benchmarks (CoQA, TriviaQA, SciQ, NQ). SD's AUROC ranges from 0.676 (Llama-2-70B on NQ) to 0.866 (Llama-3-8B and Mistral-7B on TriviaQA), with an average of approximately 0.77 across all settings. Paired t-tests confirm that SD's advantage over every baseline is statistically significant with p-values consistently below 10⁻⁶. On the summarization task (DUC-2004), SD also performs best in 6 of 7 model configurations, demonstrating generalizability beyond QA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SD outperforms semantic entropy (SE, Kuhn et al. 2023) in all 28 model–dataset combinations, with an average AUROC gap of roughly 0.15 (e.g., 0.783 vs. 0.633 on CoQA/Llama-2-13B; 0.866 vs. 0.662 on TriviaQA/Llama-3-8B). Against P(True) (Kadavath et al. 2022), SD wins in all 28 cases with a larger margin, as P(True) hovers near chance level (0.50–0.67) while SD stays above 0.67. The closest competitor is degree (Deg, Lin et al. 2023), which beats SD in only 2 of 28 cases (Llama-3-8B on CoQA: 0.795 vs. 0.738; Llama-2-70B on NQ: 0.691 vs. 0.676), both attributed to badly calibrated sequence likelihoods in the base LLM; in the remaining 26 cases SD leads by 0.01–0.13. SD also dominates length-normalized likelihood (NL, Murray & Chiang 2018) in all 28 cases (avg. gap ~0.09), length-normalized entropy (NE, Malinin & Gales 2021) in all 28 cases (avg. gap ~0.17), and predictive entropy (PE, Kadavath et al. 2022) in all 28 cases (avg. gap ~0.13). Finally, SD outperforms the Nature 2024 semantic entropy variant (Farquhar et al.) in every test case reported in Table A3, with gaps of 0.10–0.22 AUROC.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the semantic entropy framework (Kuhn et al., ICLR 2023) by replacing its binary semantic-equivalence grouping with a continuous, fine-grained semantic distance measured via NLI models, and by shifting from a prompt-wise entropy to a response-wise confidence score. The core estimator adapts classical kernel density estimation (Rosenblatt 1956; Parzen 1962) to the discrete LLM output space by substituting explicit token-level sampling probabilities for empirical frequencies, and employs a dimension-invariant Epanechnikov kernel to make the metric comparable across embedding dimensionalities. It departs from prior calibration and fine-tuning approaches (temperature scaling, LitCab, P(True)) by requiring no additional training, no task-specific labels, and no modification to the base LLM, positioning itself as an off-the-shelf post-hoc confidence measurement.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Semantic density offers a practical, training-free confidence indicator that works for free-form generation tasks and is response-specific, addressing two gaps that limit the deployment of LLMs in safety-critical domains (healthcare, finance). Key limitations include the need for access to internal token probabilities (unavailable in proprietary APIs) and the current focus on sentence-level responses rather than long-form outputs. Open questions include whether better sampling strategies for reference responses, improved contextual embedding models, or purpose-built kernel functions could further tighten the confidence estimate, and how the metric behaves under adversarial or out-of-distribution prompts.
