## Problem

3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) is uniquely vulnerable to the inherent noise in raw images: unlike NeRF's MLP which acts as a low-pass filter, 3DGS generates numerous thin, elongated Gaussians to overfit noise, severely degrading both reconstruction quality and real-time rendering speed—especially under limited training views. No prior method addresses how to jointly denoise and reconstruct HDR 3DGS from noisy raw images without requiring clean reference images.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243009/cropped_page1_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view noisy raw images (linear-domain, pre-ISP) along with calibrated per-camera noise parameters (system gain k, read noise σ_read, fixed pattern noise n_fp) and camera intrinsics/extrinsics. It outputs an HDR 3D Gaussian Splatting scene (Scaffold-GS representation) that renders novel views in real-time. The noise extractor is pretrained on the SID dataset (Sony DSLR, 14-bit) to avoid data leakage with the iPhone X RawNeRF evaluation set (12-bit), and lens distortion is corrected via a precomputed Newton-Raphson mapping before splatting.

## Method

The paper proposes a self-supervised dual-branch framework built on Scaffold-GS that jointly denoises and reconstructs HDR 3DGS. A CNN noise extractor F_n(·;Ω) predicts per-pixel noise from the input raw image (after subtracting fixed pattern noise), while the 3DGS branch is optimized toward pseudo noise-free targets (x̃ − n̂). The key innovation is the noise-robust reconstruction loss L_nrr, which combines three terms: (1) the RawNeRF scaling loss between the rendered distorted image and the pseudo-clean image, (2) a negative log-likelihood (NLL) loss that constrains the predicted noise to follow the physical Poisson-Gaussian heteroscedastic distribution N(0, σ_read² + x̂·k), and (3) a covariance loss that standardizes the predicted noise and penalizes inter-pixel spatial correlations, ensuring the extracted noise is statistically independent. This physically-grounded regularization prevents 3DGS from allocating Gaussians to fit noise, reducing both artifact count and Gaussian point count M, thereby improving both PSNR and FPS simultaneously.

## Results

On the RawNeRF dataset (full views), the proposed method achieves 59.49 dB Raw PSNR, 23.53 dB RGB PSNR, 0.535 SSIM, 0.499 LPIPS, and 80 FPS, outperforming all baselines across every metric. In limited-view settings (4–20 views), the method gains approximately 3 dB RGB PSNR over HDR Scaffold-GS and is roughly 4× faster in FPS at 4 views. On the LLFF dataset (3 training views, 1/4 resolution), the method reaches 43.42 dB Raw PSNR and 18.75 dB RGB PSNR at 216 FPS, surpassing FSGS by over 2 dB Raw PSNR and 3.3 dB RGB PSNR.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all eight baselines on every reported metric. Against the strongest two-stage pipeline (BM3D + 3DGS), it gains +1.09 dB Raw PSNR and +0.83 dB RGB PSNR while also improving SSIM and LPIPS. The self-supervised denoisers (Neighbor2Neighbor, LGBPN) and supervised denoisers (ELD, PMN, RViDeNet) all lose by 1–5.8 dB RGB PSNR, with the supervised methods suffering the largest gaps due to over-smoothing of high-frequency details. Compared to HDR Scaffold-GS (same raw input, standard RawNeRF loss), the method improves RGB PSNR by +0.84 dB and FPS by +7 in full views, and the gap widens to ~3 dB PSNR and ~4× FPS in the 4-view setting. On LLFF with only 3 views, the method beats FSGS by +2.2 dB Raw PSNR and +3.3 dB RGB PSNR at 1/4 resolution, confirming generalizability beyond the RawNeRF dataset.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Scaffold-GS as its 3D representation backbone and adopts the RawNeRF scaling loss for handling the wide dynamic range of raw images, but departs from both by introducing a joint noise-extraction and 3DGS optimization framework. Unlike two-stage pipelines (denoiser → 3DGS) or standard 3DGS trained with RawNeRF loss, the proposed noise-robust reconstruction loss (L_nrr) couples a physical Poisson-Gaussian noise model with a covariance decorrelation term, enabling self-supervised denoising without clean references. The paper also extends the analysis beyond prior raw-image denoising work (ELD, PMN, Neighbor2Neighbor, LGBPN) by incorporating a 3D geometric prior through the multi-view consistency of 3DGS, which those image-only methods lack.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that 3DGS is uniquely vulnerable to raw-image noise—producing elongated Gaussians that degrade both quality and FPS, unlike NeRF where the MLP acts as an implicit low-pass filter—reframes how practitioners should think about applying explicit 3D representations to noisy data. The practical impact is significant for real-time HDR rendering in low-light or high-contrast scenes with limited views, a common constraint in AR/VR and autonomous driving. However, the method still suffers from over-smoothing of high-frequency signals that statistically resemble noise, and the additional training cost (1.6h vs. 1.0h for Scaffold-GS) limits its appeal for very large-scale deployments. Open questions include whether the noise model can be made adaptive per-scene and whether the covariance loss can be made more discriminative for texture-rich regions.
