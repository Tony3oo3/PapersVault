## Problem

Existing monocular depth estimation methods predict depth per-pixel in the spatial domain, neglecting local inter-pixel correlations, and rely on a single forward pass that cannot progressively refine from global scene structure to local geometric details. This paper addresses both limitations by reformulating depth estimation as a progressive regression task in the discrete cosine transform (DCT) domain, where frequency components naturally separate coarse scene layout from fine local details.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242061/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image (e.g., 640×480 for NYU-Depth-V2, 1216×352 for KITTI) and produces a dense depth map at full resolution. Internally, the image is partitioned into non-overlapping 8×8 depth patches, and the network predicts DCT frequency coefficients at H/8 × W/8 resolution. The final spatial depth map is recovered via inverse 2D DCT, and intermediate depth estimates are available at each of the 9 progressive refinement steps.

## Method

DCDepth uses a Swin-Large encoder to extract multi-scale features, which are fused by a Pyramid Feature Fusion (PFF) module that employs a novel DCT-based downsampling strategy to minimize information loss during feature reduction. A CRF decoder upscales the fused features to 1/8 resolution, where a Progressive Prediction Head (PPH) operates. The PPH uses a GRU-based iterative mechanism: at each step it encodes the current spatial-domain depth estimate (via a spatial encoder) and the current frequency-domain coefficients (via a frequency encoder with cross-attention over valid components), then predicts a correction term ΔC that refines all previously estimated coefficients and adds new higher-frequency ones. Frequency components are grouped along subdiagonals and merged at high frequencies, reducing the full 64-coefficient prediction to 9 iterative steps. Training uses a scaled scale-invariant loss on intermediate depth maps plus a frequency sparsity regularizer and an edge-aware smoothness term.

## Results

On NYU-Depth-V2, DCDepth achieves the best Abs Rel of 0.085 and δ<1.25 of 0.940, with RMSE of 0.304, using 259M parameters (fewest among compared methods). On TOFDC, it attains Abs Rel 0.188 and RMSE 0.565, improving 19.7% and 8.7% over the prior best (VA-DepthNet). On KITTI (Eigen split), DCDepth reaches RMSE 2.044 and δ<1.25 0.977; on the KITTI official split it achieves the best iRMSE of 10.12 and SiLog of 9.60.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On NYU-Depth-V2, DCDepth outperforms the closest competitor VA-DepthNet [22] on Abs Rel (0.085 vs. 0.086) and δ<1.25 (0.940 vs. 0.937), while matching its RMSE (0.304) and log10 (0.037); it also beats IEBins [34] by 1.2% on Abs Rel and 0.4% on δ<1.25. On TOFDC, DCDepth achieves a substantial 19.7% reduction in Abs Rel (0.188 vs. 0.234) and 8.7% lower RMSE (0.565 vs. 0.619) over VA-DepthNet, though it trails by 0.1% on δ<1.25 (0.995 vs. 0.996). On KITTI Eigen split, DCDepth attains the best RMSE (2.044) but is marginally behind iDisc [27] on Abs Rel (0.051 vs. 0.050); on the KITTI official split it leads in iRMSE (10.12 vs. 10.62 for NDDepth [33]) and SiLog (9.60 vs. 9.62), while NDDepth edges it on Abs Rel (7.75 vs. 7.83). Overall, DCDepth uses 259M parameters—4.1% fewer than NeWCRFS [58]—while achieving the best or near-best results across all three datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm of pixel-wise spatial-domain depth estimation by reformulating the problem in the discrete cosine frequency domain, a concept borrowed from classical signal processing but never previously applied to monocular depth estimation. It builds on the standard Swin Transformer backbone and CRF-based decoder used by recent SOTA methods (NeWCRFS, VA-DepthNet, IEBins), but introduces two novel architectural components: a Progressive Prediction Head (PPH) that uses a GRU to iteratively predict DCT coefficients from low to high frequency, and a Pyramid Feature Fusion (PFF) module that employs DCT-based downsampling to reduce information loss. The progressive global-to-local prediction strategy is the paper's core conceptual departure, leveraging DCT's energy compaction property to structure the estimation hierarchy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The frequency-domain formulation offers a principled mechanism for modeling local inter-pixel correlations that spatial-domain methods handle implicitly, and the progressive low-to-high-frequency prediction provides an interpretable global-to-local estimation hierarchy. The DCT-based downsampling technique in PFF is a reusable architectural component that outperforms standard bilinear and PixelUnshuffle alternatives. A key limitation acknowledged by the authors is that sparse ground truth (as in KITTI) leads to inefficient supervision of frequency coefficients, making the method most effective with dense depth labels; developing better training strategies for sparse supervision remains an open question. The modest but consistent SOTA improvements across three diverse datasets (indoor, outdoor, dense, sparse) suggest the formulation is robust, though the 9-step iterative prediction adds inference latency compared to single-pass methods.
