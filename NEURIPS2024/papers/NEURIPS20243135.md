## Problem

Existing diffusion-based offline RL methods (guide-based like QGPO, select-based like IDQL) must query the value function on actions sampled from the diffusion behavior policy, which can be out-of-distribution, causing overestimation errors to be exploited during guidance or selection. Meanwhile, DICE methods provide an in-sample optimal stationary distribution ratio but rely on unimodal Gaussian policy extraction, limiting expressivity for multi-modal optimal policies. The paper asks: can we use DICE's in-sample ratio to directly transform a diffusion behavior policy into the optimal policy while avoiding OOD value queries entirely?

## Input / Output

The method takes a continuous state vector as input and produces a continuous action vector as output, trained entirely on offline (s, a, r, s') tuples from D4RL datasets. During evaluation, the diffusion model generates K candidate actions from the transformed optimal policy π*(a|s), and the DICE-derived critic Q*(s,a) selects the argmax among them. The key constraint is that all guidance learning uses only in-sample actions from the dataset (one sample per state), never requiring the diffusion model to generate actions during training.

## Method

Diffusion-DICE decomposes the optimal policy's score function into the behavior policy's score (from a pre-trained diffusion model) plus a guidance gradient ∇_{a_t} log E_{π^D(a_0|a_t,s)}[w*(s,a_0)], where w* is DICE's optimal stationary distribution ratio. The guidance term is learned via In-sample Guidance Learning (IGL), a convex objective min_θ E[w*(s,a)·e^{-g_θ(s,a_t,t)} + g_θ(s,a_t,t)] that requires only one dataset action per state, guaranteeing convergence to the exact log-expectation. A piecewise f-divergence (Pearson χ² for x≥1, reverse KL for 0≤x<1) ensures w* is strictly positive and numerically stable. At evaluation, the guide-then-select paradigm first generates a small number of in-support candidates from π*(a|s) using the learned score, then selects the best via Q*, minimizing error exploitation compared to guide-only or select-only baselines.

## Results

On the D4RL benchmark, Diffusion-DICE achieves state-of-the-art normalized scores in 13 out of 15 tasks where all baselines report results. Headline numbers include 60.0±0.6 on halfcheetah-medium (vs. 54.1 for QGPO and 51.1 for Diffusion-QL), 100.2±3.2 on hopper-medium, 91.3±3.1 on antmaze-medium-play, and 78.3±3.4 on kitchen-partial. The method also shows strong gains on the more challenging AntMaze and Kitchen tasks, where trajectory stitching and multi-modal policies are critical. All results are averaged over 5 random seeds with standard deviations reported.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Diffusion-DICE outperforms all four Gaussian-policy baselines (CQL, IQL, SQL, O-DICE) on nearly every task, with the largest gaps over O-DICE (e.g., +12.6 on halfcheetah-medium, +14.1 on antmaze-medium-play), confirming that the diffusion-based policy extraction captures multi-modality that a unimodal Gaussian cannot. Among diffusion-policy baselines, D-DICE beats the guide-based QGPO by 5.9 on halfcheetah-medium and 6.7 on antmaze-medium-play, and the select-based IDQL by 9.0 on halfcheetah-medium and 4.1 on antmaze-large-diverse. The largest single-task margin is +17.8 on kitchen-partial (78.3 vs. 60.5 for Diffusion-QL), where trajectory-level planning is most challenging. The only task where D-DICE does not achieve the best score is antmaze-umaze-diverse (82.0±8.4 vs. SfBC's 85.3±3.6), though the high variance suggests this may be within noise. The consistent advantage over both guide-only (QGPO) and select-only (IDQL, SfBC) methods validates the guide-then-select paradigm: in-sample guidance produces in-distribution candidates, reducing the number of selections needed and minimizing critic overestimation exploitation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reframes DICE-based offline RL (Sikchi et al. 2023; Mao et al. 2024/O-DICE) by showing that the optimal stationary distribution ratio induces a direct transformation from the behavior policy to the optimal policy, rather than merely a distribution-level correction. It then leverages diffusion models to perform this transformation, positioning itself between existing guide-only methods (QGPO) and select-only methods (IDQL, SfBC) by introducing a 'guide-then-select' paradigm where in-sample guidance learning (formulated as a convex optimization via a novel lemma) produces in-distribution candidates that are then refined by the DICE critic. The piecewise f-divergence combining Reverse KL and Pearson χ² properties is a practical addition to the DICE toolkit.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Diffusion-DICE provides a principled replacement for the Gaussian policy extraction bottleneck in DICE methods, enabling capture of multi-modal optimal policies that unimodal parameterizations cannot represent. The in-sample guidance learning guarantees that the guidance gradient is computed solely from dataset actions, avoiding the OOD error exploitation that plagues both contrastive guidance (QGPO) and large-candidate selection (IDQL). Key limitations include the computational cost of diffusion sampling at evaluation time and potential degradation in low-data regimes. Open questions include whether faster generative models (consistency models, idempotent generative networks) can replace diffusion models while preserving the score-function decomposition, and whether the guide-then-select paradigm extends to trajectory-level planning or multi-agent settings.
