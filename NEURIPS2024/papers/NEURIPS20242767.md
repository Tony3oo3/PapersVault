## Problem

Existing variational Bayesian domain adaptation methods, such as VDI, model global domain indices with a single Gaussian prior, which overlooks the inherent structural differences among diverse domains. This single-distribution assumption limits the expressiveness of the domain-index space and hinders effective adaptation when target domains exhibit varying degrees of domain shift. The paper asks whether a richer, multi-component prior over domain indices can yield more interpretable indices and better adaptation performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242767/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes data points paired with their discrete domain identity w, split into labeled source domains and unlabeled target domains; inputs range from 2D/6D tabular vectors (Circle, DG-15/60, TPT-48) to 224×224 RGB images (CompCars). Beyond standard class or regression predictions, GMDI outputs inferred global domain indices θ (shared per domain) and local domain indices u (per data point) in a latent 'domain-themes' space, along with domain-invariant data encodings z. Crucially, the ground-truth domain indices are unavailable during training and must be inferred as latent variables.

## Method

GMDI replaces VDI's single Gaussian prior on the global domain index θ with a dynamic Gaussian Mixture Model whose number of components is adaptively determined by a Chinese Restaurant Process (CRP) via a finite stick-breaking construction (upper bound K). In the generative process, each domain is assigned to a mixture component v through the CRP, and the local domain index u is then derived from the selected component θ^v to produce domain-invariant encodings z. Training maximizes a structured variational ELBO (with closed-form updates for the CRP stick-breaking variables β and component assignments v, and gradient-based updates for Gaussian variational posteriors of θ, u, and z) combined with an adversarial discriminator loss that enforces independence between θ and z. Theoretically, the paper proves GMDI's ELBO is strictly tighter than VDI's (since VDI is a special case with one component), yielding a more stringent lower bound on log-likelihood and a tighter upper bound on the overall objective.

## Results

On classification tasks, GMDI achieves 96.9% on Circle, 96.5% on DG-15, 99.3% on DG-60, and 44.4% on CompCars, outperforming the strongest baseline VDI by 2.6%, 1.8%, 3.4%, and 1.9% respectively. On the TPT-48 regression task, GMDI reduces average MSE to 2.087 (W→E) and 2.493 (N→S), representing 16% and 21% improvements over VDI (2.496 and 3.160). All other baselines (DANN, ADDA, CDANN, MDD, SENTRY) perform near or below random on the synthetic classification datasets, while GMDI consistently exceeds 96% accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GMDI outperforms all seven baselines across every dataset. The closest competitor is VDI (Xu et al., ICLR 2023), which uses a single Gaussian prior for domain indices; GMDI's Gaussian mixture prior yields consistent gains of 1.8–3.4% on classification and 16–21% MSE reduction on regression. D2V (Peng et al., ECCV 2020) is the second-strongest baseline on synthetic datasets (79.9–82.1%) but is not applicable to regression and trails GMDI by 14–17 points. Traditional adversarial methods (DANN, ADDA, CDANN, MDD, SENTRY) perform near random (50%) on the synthetic classification tasks and often worse than Source-only on TPT-48 regression, indicating severe negative transfer. On CompCars, all baselines cluster between 38.9% and 42.8%, while GMDI reaches 44.4%, a 5% improvement over Source-only. The ablation (GMDI w/o CRP) confirms that the adaptive component count from the Chinese Restaurant Process contributes an additional 1–3% over a fixed-component GMM.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Variational Domain Index (VDI) framework of Xu et al. (ICLR 2023), which first formalized the domain index as a latent variable inferred via variational Bayes for domain adaptation. The key departure is replacing VDI's single Gaussian prior on the global domain index with a dynamic Gaussian Mixture Model whose component count is adaptively determined by a Chinese Restaurant Process (CRP) with stick-breaking construction, drawing on Bayesian nonparametric clustering. The adversarial training component and the independence constraint between domain index and data encoding are inherited directly from VDI, while the mixture modeling introduces a 'domain-themes' space that groups similar domains into shared component distributions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled, theoretically grounded solution to the practical problem that a single Gaussian prior is too restrictive when target domains exhibit diverse and heterogeneous shifts. The more stringent ELBO (Theorem 3) gives a clean justification that the mixture prior strictly dominates the single-Gaussian prior in terms of bound tightness. However, the method still requires known domain identities (w) and cannot infer them as latent variables, limiting its applicability to settings where domain labels are available. The experimental evaluation, while thorough across five datasets, relies heavily on synthetic benchmarks (Circle, DG-15, DG-60) and relatively small real-world datasets, leaving open questions about scalability to large-scale, high-dimensional domain adaptation scenarios.
