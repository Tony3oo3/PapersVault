## Problem

In non-concave games—arising naturally in multi-agent deep learning, GANs, and MARL—classical equilibrium concepts fail: Nash equilibria may not exist, mixed/correlated equilibria have infinite support, and all are computationally intractable. While the Φ-equilibrium concept (Greenwald & Jafari, 2003) is guaranteed to exist for any set of strategy modifications even in non-concave games, its computational tractability remained completely open. This paper initiates the study of tractable Φ-equilibria in non-concave games, providing the first efficient uncoupled learning algorithms that converge to them.

## Input / Output

The method takes an n-player continuous game with convex compact strategy sets X_i ⊆ R^{d_i}, G-Lipschitz and L-smooth (but non-concave) utility functions, and gradient feedback ∇f^t(x^t) at each iterate. The output is the empirical distribution of joint strategy profiles played over T rounds, which constitutes an ε-approximate Φ-equilibrium. Crucially, the algorithm is uncoupled: each player independently runs its own online learning procedure without coordination, and the equilibrium emerges from the time-averaged play. For local deviations, the approximation guarantee is ε + δ²L/2, where δ bounds the maximum deviation size and L is the smoothness parameter.

## Method

For finite Φ, the paper designs a sampling-based algorithm that avoids the intractable fixed-point computation of Stoltz & Lugosi by constructing an approximate stationary distribution over a |Φ|-ary tree of depth h=√T, then sampling from it in O(√T) time per iteration; this yields O(√(T log|Φ|)) Φ-regret via a Hedge-based external regret minimizer over Φ. For infinite local Φ sets, a key reduction (Lemma 1) shows that no-Φ(δ)-regret against convex losses suffices to obtain an (ε + δ²L/2)-approximate Φ(δ)-equilibrium in non-concave games, since L-smoothness permits a linear approximation within δ-neighborhoods. For projection-based deviations, the paper proves that standard GD and Optimistic Gradient achieve near-optimal O(G√(δ(δ+D_X)T)) Φ_Proj(δ)-regret via a novel analysis that exploits projection geometry to bound non-telescoping terms, improving to O(T^{1/4}) in the game setting. For interpolation-based deviations, the problem reduces to standard external regret minimization, so GD with step size D_X/(G√T) suffices. All results hold in the first-order stationary regime ε = Ω(δ²L), where computing even a local Nash equilibrium is PPAD-hard.

## Results

For finite Φ, the paper achieves O(√(T log|Φ|)) Φ-regret with O(√T·|Φ|) per-iteration time (Theorem 2), yielding ε-approximate Φ-equilibrium in poly(1/ε, log|Φ|, log n) iterations. For infinite local Φ sets in the first-order stationary regime (ε = Ω(δ²L)), GD achieves O(G√(δ(δ+D_X)T)) Φ_Proj-regret (Theorem 3) and O(δG√T) Φ_Int-regret (Theorem 5), while OG achieves an improved O(T^{1/4}) individual Φ_Proj-regret in the game setting (Theorem 9). Lower bounds of Ω(δG√T) (adversarial, linear losses) and Ω(δ²LT) (non-convex, linear-span algorithms) confirm near-optimality of these guarantees.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical baselines; the 'comparison' is between solution concepts in non-concave games. The key finding is that all classical global solution concepts (Nash, MNE, CE, CCE) are either non-existent or NP-hard to compute in non-concave games, while local Nash equilibrium, though it exists, is PPAD-hard. In contrast, the paper's Φ-equilibrium concepts (finite, projection-based, convex-combination, and interpolation-based) are the only solution concepts that are simultaneously guaranteed to exist and admit efficient ε-approximation via simple decentralized learning dynamics (Hedge, GD, OG). The paper also shows that Φ_Proj-regret is provably incomparable to external regret (Examples 3–4), yet GD/OG still achieve near-optimal Φ_Proj-regret, which is a non-trivial result since no prior algorithm was known to minimize this regret even in the convex case.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Φ-equilibrium framework of Greenwald and Jafari [GJ03] and the no-Φ-regret algorithm of Stoltz and Lugosi [SL07], which guarantees existence of Φ-equilibria in non-concave games but requires an intractable fixed-point computation per iteration. The key departure is making this framework computationally efficient: for finite Φ, the authors replace the fixed-point step with a tree-based sampling procedure (inspired by [Zha+24] for concave games), and for infinite local Φ sets, they show that standard GD/OG algorithms—which were previously only known to minimize external regret—also achieve near-optimal Φ-regret. This positions the work at the intersection of online learning, game theory, and non-convex optimization, extending the well-studied concave-game no-regret literature to the non-concave regime where classical equilibrium concepts break down.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Non-concave games arise naturally in GAN training, multi-agent reinforcement learning, and deep multi-agent settings, yet classical equilibrium theory offers no tractable solution concepts there (Nash may not exist, local Nash is PPAD-hard, correlated equilibria have infinite support). This paper provides the first efficient algorithms for computing Φ-equilibria in this setting, showing that the first-order stationary regime (ε = Ω(δ²))—where local Nash equilibrium is provably intractable—becomes tractable once players' strategies are correlated. A key open question is whether the O(√T) adversarial Φ-regret bound for GD is tight (the lower bound is Ω(δG√T) vs. upper bound O(G√(δD_X)T)), and whether more classes of local Φ admit efficient regret minimization. The beam-search example in Appendix H shows the landscape is already rich, with some natural Φ sets where GD suffers linear regret.
