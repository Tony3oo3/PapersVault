## Problem

Existing theoretical analyses of transformer training for next-token prediction (NTP) provide only asymptotic convergence guarantees, leaving the finite-time (non-asymptotic) training dynamics uncharacterized. Additionally, whether a trained transformer can make non-trivial predictions on unseen data has not been formally established. This paper addresses both gaps by providing explicit convergence rates and a generalization guarantee for a one-layer transformer trained on NTP.

## Input / Output

The model takes a token sequence X = [x_1, ..., x_L] from a finite vocabulary V with orthogonal unit-norm embeddings (dimension d) and produces a probability distribution over V via a two-stage softmax: an inner softmax computes attention weights over the sequence tokens, and an outer softmax maps the attended representation to token probabilities. The training dataset D_0 consists of sentence-next-token pairs, and the loss is the average cross-entropy over all pairs. A key structural constraint is that the dataset must be 'realizable'—admitting a collocation (injective token-to-next-token map) and well-defined query-dependent partial orders with no confused tokens—so that the loss can be driven to zero.

## Method

The paper designs a two-stage normalized gradient descent (NGD) algorithm on a reparameterized one-layer transformer (self-attention with key-query matrix W_kq followed by a feed-forward layer W_ov). Stage 1 trains W_ov on the collocation to solve a hard-margin problem distinguishing each token's successor from all other tokens; Stage 2 fixes W_ov and trains W_kq on the full dataset to solve a hard-margin problem separating optimal from non-optimal tokens under each query-dependent partial order. The key technical contribution is proving that both layers converge in direction to their respective max-margin solutions at sub-linear rates (O(log t/t) for W_ov, O(log²t/t) for W_kq), while the cross-entropy loss converges linearly (exponentially in t). The generalization result shows the trained W_kq automatically extends the partial order by inserting non-comparable tokens between optimal and non-optimal tokens, enabling correct prediction on unseen sentences containing previously unobserved token-query combinations.

## Results

The paper establishes non-asymptotic convergence rates for a one-layer transformer trained via two-stage normalized gradient descent on next-token prediction. The feed-forward layer W_ov converges in direction to its max-margin solution at rate O(log t / t) (Proposition 1), while the key-query attention matrix W_kq converges in direction to its max-margin solution at rate O(log²t / t) (Theorem 1). The cross-entropy training loss converges linearly (exponentially) to its minimum value (Theorem 2), and the parameter norms grow linearly in t. The synthetic experiment (|V|=20, 900 iterations per stage) visually confirms all three convergence behaviors shown in Figure 2.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not compare against other training methods or baselines; it is a purely theoretical contribution that provides the first finite-time (non-asymptotic) convergence analysis for transformer training on next-token prediction. The closest prior works (Tian et al., 2023a; Li et al., 2024) established only asymptotic convergence for the same architecture and task, while Tarzanagh et al. (2023b,a) showed an equivalence to SVM for binary classification but without finite-time rates. The paper's contribution is therefore the gap-filling of explicit convergence rates (O(log t/t) and O(log²t/t) direction convergence, linear loss convergence) rather than a head-to-head method comparison. The single synthetic experiment validates the theory but does not benchmark against alternative optimizers or architectures.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on training dynamics of transformers for next-token prediction, building directly on Tian et al. (2023a) and Li et al. (2024), which established only asymptotic convergence for single-layer transformers trained on NTP. The proof technique draws heavily on the classical implicit bias framework for max-margin convergence (Soudry et al., 2018; Nacson et al., 2019; Ji and Telgarsky, 2021) and the SVM-equivalence perspective on attention (Tarzanagh et al., 2023b,a), but applies them in a new two-stage setting where the feed-forward and attention layers are trained sequentially. The key conceptual departure is the introduction of a partial-order-based structural framework (collocation + query-dependent partial orders) to characterize realizable NTP datasets, which had not been formalized in prior transformer theory.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first paper to provide finite-time (non-asymptotic) convergence guarantees for transformer training on next-token prediction, filling a notable gap left by prior asymptotic-only analyses. The generalization result (Theorem 3) that the trained transformer automatically extends the query-dependent partial order by inserting non-comparable tokens between optimal and non-optimal tokens offers a structural explanation for why transformers generalize to unseen sentence patterns. However, the analysis is confined to a one-layer transformer with orthonormal embeddings under a realizable (zero-loss) setting with no confused tokens, leaving open whether these results extend to multi-layer architectures, non-orthonormal vocabularies, or noisy/non-realizable data — all of which are necessary for practical relevance.
