## Problem

Grid cells in the entorhinal cortex support path integration through hexagonal lattice firing patterns, but recent experiments show these patterns distort near rewarded locations. While this distortion has been reported qualitatively, it remains unquantified: how do local reward signals propagate to global changes in grid cell firing fields, and what happens to the geometry and topology of the underlying toroidal neural manifolds?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243180/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The piRNN receives a 2D velocity vector (displacement per timestep) as recurrent input and produces a position estimate via linear readout of 1800 grid cell activities (organized into 150 modules) onto place cells. During saliency training, a Gaussian saliency kernel (σ* = 0.05L) centered at a reward location multiplicatively weights the position error loss, creating non-uniform spatial importance without altering the network architecture. The outputs of interest include the distorted grid cell firing fields (rate maps at 40×40 resolution), the toroidal neural manifold geometry, and the emergent heterogeneous unit types (diffused, banded, ring).

## Method

The paper introduces a two-phase training procedure: first, a standard piRNN is pretrained with a conformal isometry loss to learn grid cell representations; second, a saliency loss term (1+s(x))·L_error is added to prioritize accurate position decoding near a localized reward. The key architectural decision is freezing the place cell readout matrix Q during saliency training, which acts as implicit regularization preserving the toroidal topology of grid cell module manifolds. Theoretically, the paper models firing field deformations as diffeomorphisms Φ with energy-budget conservation, proving that the diffeomorphism group acts on both firing fields and neural manifolds while preserving barycenter and topology. Empirically, persistent homology on PCA-projected module activity confirms that toroidal topology (Betti numbers 1,2,1) survives saliency training only when readout is frozen, while the firing fields develop global distortions—diffused, banded, and ring units—despite the reward being spatially localized.

## Results

After pretraining, >70% of piRNN units are classified as grid cells based on gridness score, organized into 150 modules. Persistent homology analysis confirms toroidal topology (Betti numbers 1,2,1) is preserved after saliency training when the place cell readout is frozen, but destroyed when the readout is allowed to change. The reduction hypothesis (Conjecture 1) is empirically validated: modules with emergent diffused units show a measurable decrease in neural manifold size while retaining toroidal topology. Across 667 runs spanning varied saliency parameters (s₀ ∈ {1,10,100,1000}, σ* ∈ {0.05,0.1,0.15,0.2,0.5}), the paper consistently observes global distortions (ring, diffused, and band units) rather than the local attracted distortions predicted by prior rodent studies [15].

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not benchmark against competing piRNN architectures or spatial navigation methods; instead, its key comparisons are internal ablations. The most critical comparison is between frozen and trainable place cell readout during saliency training: freezing the readout preserves toroidal topology (Betti numbers 1,2,1) and enables continual learning, while allowing the readout to change destroys the toroidal structure entirely (Fig. 4A vs. 4C). A second comparison contrasts the piRNN's emergent distortions (global: diffused, ring, band units) against the locally-attracted distortions reported in rodent experiments by Boccara et al. [15]; the piRNNs do not reproduce the attracted pattern, challenging the conventional interpretation of reward-induced local magnification. Synthetic deformations (diffused, attracted, band units in Fig. 3) are compared to verify that global geometric changes (reduced manifold size for diffused units, increased curvature for attracted units) do not alter toroidal topology, with 17% band-unit substitution leaving the manifold largely unchanged. The paper also contrasts its saliency-trained piRNN against the original Xu et al. [19] pretraining, showing that the added saliency loss term (Eq. 7) is sufficient to induce the observed distortions without architectural changes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the piRNN path-integration framework of Xu et al. [19] and Gao et al. [30] by introducing a saliency-weighted loss term that models spatially localized rewards, and by applying the toroidal topology analysis of Gardner et al. [9] to study how reward information deforms grid cell neural manifolds. The theoretical contribution—a diffeomorphism group action relating firing field deformations to neural manifold geometry—is novel and departs from the purely local 'attracted units' interpretation of Boccara et al. [15]. The paper also leverages persistent homology (Vietoris-Rips filtrations) and dimensionality reduction (PCA, UMAP, MDS) as standard TDA tools, but applies them in the specific context of tracking topological invariants before and after reward training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a local reward produces global (not local) distortions in grid cell firing patterns challenges the prevailing interpretation from rodent experiments and suggests a duality between local reward structure and global spatial code deformation, analogous to Fourier duality. The practical insight that freezing place cell readout weights acts as implicit regularization preserving toroidal topology while permitting geometric adaptation offers a concrete mechanism for continual learning in spatial navigation circuits. Key open questions include whether attracted units (local magnification) can emerge under different reward geometries, whether the duality extends to non-Gaussian or multi-peak saliency distributions, and whether these computational predictions hold in biological entorhinal cortex recordings.
