## Problem

Existing cumulant-based methods for causal discovery in Poisson Branching Structural Causal Models (PB-SCM) fail to identify certain causal directions that are theoretically identifiable, such as collider structures among variables lacking a common descendant. This paper closes that gap by showing the Probability Generating Function (PGF) provides a strictly richer characterization of the causal structure, enabling identification of local patterns that higher-order cumulants cannot capture.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240372/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes i.i.d. samples from a d-dimensional count vector generated by PB-SCM and outputs the full causal DAG. In practice, the PGF is estimated empirically from the data, and the algorithm constructs 2×2 matrices from first and second partial derivatives of a local PGF (obtained by setting non-target variables to zero), then performs rank tests via eigenvalue analysis with bootstrap-based p-values. The overall computational complexity is O(d³), polynomial in the number of variables and pseudo-polynomial in the magnitude of observed counts.

## Method

The core contribution is a closed-form solution for the PGF of PB-SCM (Theorem 2), which decomposes into per-vertex terms T_{X_i}(1) that recursively enumerate all open/closed path combinations through the causal graph. Each monomial component in this expansion uniquely encodes a specific local subgraph structure—for instance, z_i z_j z_k² indicates two directed paths from X_i to X_k—enabling structural identification without requiring the full joint distribution. By introducing a local PGF (setting non-target variables to zero), the authors isolate specific local structures and derive three rank-test-based identifiability theorems: pairwise adjacency detection, triangular structure orientation, and unshielded collider detection. The practical algorithm first learns the skeleton via pairwise rank tests on matrix A, then orients edges by checking triangular (matrix B) and collider (matrix C) patterns, with a bootstrap-based eigenvalue test for statistical significance.

## Results

On synthetic 10-vertex PB-SCM graphs (default: in-degree 3.0, sample size 30,000), the proposed PGF-based method achieves F1 = 0.86 ± 0.03 and SHD = 6.40 ± 1.68, outperforming the closest baseline Cumulant (F1 = 0.80, SHD = 13.00) by ~6% in F1 and ~50% in SHD. Performance improves with denser structures (F1 rises from 0.74 at in-degree 2.0 to 0.89 at in-degree 3.5) and with larger sample sizes (F1 rises from 0.75 at n=5,000 to 0.87 at n=50,000). On real-world data, the method correctly identifies 7 out of 8 causal directions in the Football Events dataset and all 3 directions in the Shopping Mall dataset.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The PGF-based method consistently outperforms all four baselines across both sensitivity experiments. Against the most relevant baseline, Cumulant (Qiao et al. 2024b, same PB-SCM model family), the PGF method gains ~0.06 F1 and roughly halves the SHD (6.40 vs. 13.00 at in-degree 3.0), attributed to its ability to identify causal directions using low-order PGF components where the cumulant method requires high-order statistics. Against PC (Spirtes et al. 2000), the gap is larger (~0.32 F1 at in-degree 3.0), as PC can only identify up to the Markov equivalent class and struggles with the branching structure. GES (Chickering 2002) performs worst among the general-purpose methods (F1 = 0.41), degrading sharply with denser graphs (F1 drops to 0.37 at in-degree 3.5). OCD (Ni and Mallick 2022), designed for ordinal data, is least suitable for count data (F1 = 0.28), confirming that standard discrete-data methods are ill-suited for PB-SCM. In the 4-vertex case study, the PGF method correctly identifies the collider structure X₁→X₂←X₃ that Cumulant fails to recover, and in the 5-vertex case it identifies edge X₄→X₅ that Cumulant misses.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Qiao et al. (AAAI 2024), which introduced the Poisson Branching Structural Causal Model (PB-SCM) and a cumulant-based identifiability method for it. The key departure is replacing high-order cumulant analysis with Probability Generating Functions (PGF), which the authors show captures path information at all orders rather than only the highest order. This allows identification of local structures (e.g., colliders) that the cumulant-based approach provably misses. The paper also positions itself against general-purpose causal discovery methods (PC, GES, OCD) that are not designed for count data with inherent branching (binomial thinning) mechanisms.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper fills a concrete identifiability gap in PB-SCM by showing that PGF-based rank tests on local PGFs can orient edges (particularly collider structures) that cumulant-based methods cannot, yielding a more complete identifiability characterization (Theorem 7). The practical algorithm is efficient (pseudo-polynomial in variable magnitude) and validated on both synthetic and real-world count data (football events, e-commerce). However, the main limitation is that explicit PGF estimation does not scale well to large numbers of nodes, and the faithfulness/sufficiency assumptions restrict applicability. Open questions include developing sample-efficient PGF estimation and relaxing the faithfulness assumption.
