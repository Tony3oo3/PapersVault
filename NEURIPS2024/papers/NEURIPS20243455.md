## Problem

Video diffusion models learn strong spatial-temporal priors during generative training, but it remains unclear whether these priors transfer effectively to video recognition, and whether generation and recognition can be jointly optimized without sacrificing either capability. A fundamental obstacle is the mismatch between the two tasks' training and inference regimes: diffusion generation operates on corrupted inputs with a single conditioning frame, while recognition expects clean multi-frame inputs, making naive unification lead to poor optimization and a large training-inference gap.

## Input / Output

GenRec operates on 16-frame video clips at 256×256 resolution, encoded into latent space via a pretrained VAE. The key input detail is that instead of always conditioning on the first frame (as in SVD), the model receives a random subset of frames as visible condition while the remaining frames are masked out, with an average of only ~25% of original video information retained during training. For class-conditioned generation, a target class label is additionally injected via classifier guidance. Outputs are either a video class prediction (recognition) or a fully denoised video latent decoded back to pixel space (generation).

## Method

GenRec is built on Stable Video Diffusion and introduces a random-frame latent masking strategy: a random binary mask is applied to the clean latent z₀ to produce a masked latent z̄₀, which is channel-wise concatenated with the noisy latent z̃ᵢ and fed into a Spatial-Temporal UNet initialized from SVD. The UNet is decomposed into F_head (intermediate feature extractor) and F_tail (reconstruction), with an attentive-pooler + FC classification head attached to F_head for recognition. Training jointly minimizes an EDM-weighted L2 denoising loss and a cross-entropy classification loss (balanced by γ=10), with 10% condition dropout for classifier-free guidance. For class-conditioned generation, the recognition branch's score ∇log p(y|z) is injected into the EDM sampling loop as classifier guidance, sharpened by a scaling factor s>1, enabling category-directed video synthesis without a separate text encoder.

## Results

GenRec achieves 75.8% top-1 accuracy on SSV2 and 87.2% on K400 for video recognition, matching MVD-H on K400 and surpassing VideoMAE-L, OmniMAE-H, and MaskFeat-L. For class-conditioned image-to-video generation, GenRec achieves 46.5 FVD on SSV2 and 49.3 FVD on EK-100 (16 frames), substantially outperforming the next-best method SEER (112.9 and 271.4 FVD). In limited-frame recognition, GenRec retains 76.6% of full-video accuracy when only the front half is visible, outperforming TemPr (62.1%) and MVD† (74.1%) at the same observation ratio.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On video recognition without multimodal pretraining, GenRec (75.8% SSV2, 87.2% K400) matches MVD-H on K400 and surpasses VideoMAE-L (74.3/85.2), OmniMAE-H (75.5/85.4), and MaskFeat-L (75.0/86.4), though it trails MVD-H on SSV2 (77.3%) and Hiera-H on K400 (87.8%). Against multimodal-pretrained methods (InternVideo2, OmniVec-2), GenRec shows a clear gap (e.g., 75.8 vs. 86.1 on SSV2), which the authors attribute to the absence of large-scale cross-modal supervision. On class-conditioned image-to-video generation, GenRec dramatically outperforms all baselines: 46.5 FVD on SSV2 vs. SEER's 112.9 and VideoFusion's 163.2, and 49.3 on EK-100 vs. SEER's 271.4. In limited-frame recognition (Table 2), GenRec consistently beats both TemPr and MVD† across all observation ratios (e.g., 57.7% vs. 41.2% and 55.6% at ρ=0.5 on SSV2), and removing the generative loss (w/o G) causes a 2.3% drop at ρ=0.3, confirming the mutual benefit of joint training.

## Relation to Prior Work

GenRec builds directly on Stable Video Diffusion (SVD) as its backbone, inheriting its spatial-temporal priors and EDM-based training framework, and adapts classifier guidance from Dhariwal & Nichol for class-conditioned generation. It departs from prior work that either treats diffusion models as frozen feature extractors for understanding tasks or deconstructs them for classification while sacrificing generation capability. The key architectural departure is the random-frame latent masking strategy, which bridges the fundamentally different training paradigms of diffusion generation (corrupted inputs, single conditioning frame) and video recognition (clean multi-frame inputs) into a single joint optimization loop.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that a video diffusion model can simultaneously achieve competitive recognition accuracy (75.8% SSV2, 87.2% K400) and state-of-the-art class-conditioned generation (46.5 FVD) without either task degrading the other, suggesting that generative and discriminative objectives are mutually reinforcing rather than competing. The robustness to limited frames (76.6% accuracy retention at 50% visibility) has practical implications for bandwidth-constrained or partially observed video scenarios. Key limitations include the reliance on a large pretrained 2.1B-parameter model and the need for dataset-specific fine-tuning; open questions include whether the unified framework can be distilled into smaller models and whether it extends to longer videos or more complex multi-task settings.
