## Problem

Subgraph GNNs improve expressiveness over standard MPNNs by processing bags of subgraphs, but their O(n²) computational cost makes them impractical for large graphs. Existing approaches to reduce bag size—random sampling or learnable selection via discrete sampling—either yield suboptimal subgraph choices or require lengthy training protocols (1000–4000 epochs) and are limited to very small bag sizes. No prior method can flexibly and meaningfully select any desired number of subgraphs while maintaining strong performance.

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard undirected graph (adjacency matrix A ∈ ℝ^{n×n} and node feature matrix X ∈ ℝ^{n×d}) and produces a graph-level prediction (classification or regression). The key structural detail is that the graph is first coarsened via spectral clustering into T super-nodes (a user-controlled hyperparameter), yielding a product graph T(G)⊠G with T×n nodes. The node feature tensor X ∈ ℝ^{T×n×d} is updated through stacked layers and pooled via a two-stage sum-then-MLP procedure to produce the final graph representation. The bag size T can be set to any value from 1 up to n, enabling a continuous trade-off between efficiency and expressiveness.

## Method

CS-GNN replaces the full Cartesian product G⊠G (used by GNN-SSWL+) with T(G)⊠G, where T(G) is a coarsened graph obtained by partitioning nodes into T clusters via spectral clustering. This yields three distinct message-passing channels on the product graph: horizontal updates along the original graph's edges, vertical updates along the coarsened graph's edges, and symmetry-based updates derived from the S_n equivariance structure of the T×n×d node feature tensor. The symmetry-based updates are characterized by identifying all orbits of S_n on the index space P([n])×[n] and P([n])×[n]×P([n])×[n], then implementing a sparse subset of the resulting equivariant basis as edge features on a fully connected auxiliary graph. Four node marking strategies are proposed (simple, node+size, minimum distance, learned distance), with the learned distance function π_LD proven to be strictly more expressive than the others for certain coarsening functions. The framework is shown to be at least as expressive as standard MPNNs and strictly more expressive than both coarsened sum-graph baselines and node-based subgraph GNNs for appropriate choices of T.

## Results

On ZINC-12K, CS-GNN achieves 0.062±0.0007 MAE in the full-bag setting, outperforming Subgraphormer+PE (0.063±0.001) and GNN-SSWL+ (0.070±0.005). In the small-bag regime (T=4), it reaches 0.090±0.003 MAE, beating Mag-GNN (0.101) and random sampling (0.125) by a wide margin. On PEPTIDES-FUNC, CS-GNN achieves 0.6156 AP, surpassing GatedGCN+RWSE (0.6069) and all MPNN baselines, while on PEPTIDES-STRUCT it reaches 0.2539 MAE vs. 0.3357 for GatedGCN+RWSE. Across OGB datasets (MOLHIV, MOLBACE, MOLESOL), CS-GNN outperforms all efficient baselines for T∈{2,5} in 10 out of 12 dataset-bag-size combinations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against random subgraph sampling baselines, CS-GNN consistently wins across all bag sizes and all seven datasets, with MAE improvements of 0.019–0.035 on ZINC-12K. Against learned-selection methods (PL, Mag-GNN, OSAN), CS-GNN outperforms in 10 of 12 dataset-bag-size combinations on OGB and ZINC-12K, with the sole exception being MOLHIV at T=2 where PL (79.13) edges out CS-GNN (77.72). In the full-bag setting, CS-GNN achieves state-of-the-art on ZINC-12K (0.062 MAE vs. 0.063 for Subgraphormer+PE) but is slightly outperformed on OGB: Subgraphormer+PE leads on MOLBACE (84.35 vs. 80.71 ROC-AUC) while CS-GNN is competitive on MOLHIV (79.44 vs. 79.48) and MOLESOL (0.814 vs. 0.826 RMSE). On PEPTIDES, where full-bag methods are infeasible, CS-GNN surpasses all MPNN variants including GatedGCN+RWSE by 0.009 AP on PEPTIDES-FUNC and 0.082 MAE on PEPTIDES-STRUCT. The ablation (Table 3) confirms that symmetry-based updates contribute 0.005–0.034 MAE improvement across bag sizes, with the largest gain at T=2.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Subgraphormer framework [3], which established the equivalence between node-based Subgraph GNNs (specifically GNN-SSWL+ [38]) and message passing on the Cartesian product G□G. The key departure is replacing the self-product G□G with a coarsened product T(G)□G, where T is a spectral-clustering-based coarsening that maps nodes to super-nodes, thereby associating subgraphs with node clusters rather than individual nodes. This contrasts with prior efficient Subgraph GNNs (PL [5], MAG-GNN [20], OSAN [29]) that rely on discrete sampling or reinforcement learning to select small subsets of subgraphs, which complicates training and limits bag sizes. The paper further introduces a novel symmetry analysis of the resulting node feature tensor X(S,v) ∈ R^{2^n×n×d}, characterizing all affine equivariant layers under the S_n action on pairs (S,v) — a symmetry structure the authors note is 'yet unstudied in the context of machine learning.'

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides a principled, differentiable mechanism to control the number of subgraphs (via the hyperparameter T) without discrete sampling, directly addressing the O(n²) scalability bottleneck that has limited Subgraph GNNs to small molecules. The theoretical expressivity results (Propositions 4.2–4.4) clarify when coarsening-based subgraph GNNs strictly outperform both naive coarsening+MPNN baselines and full-bag node-based Subgraph GNNs. Open questions include whether alternative coarsening strategies (beyond spectral clustering) yield better performance, whether the full equivariant basis (only a sparse subset is used for efficiency) could further improve results, and whether the parameter-sharing scheme generalizes to higher-order subgraph GNNs. The main limitation is that achieving competitive performance still requires moderately large T, which can become a bottleneck for very large graphs.
