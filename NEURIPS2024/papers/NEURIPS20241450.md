## Problem

Existing one-stage dense object detectors (e.g., GFocal) produce excessive false positive bounding boxes on complex, noisy images because their non-adaptive anchor selection criterion prioritizes recall over precision, especially for small objects embedded in large ones. The paper addresses this by introducing an adaptive, uncertainty-guided mechanism that selectively searches only informative regions of the feature pyramid rather than evaluating all candidate anchors uniformly.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241450/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard RGB image, extracts multi-scale features via a pre-trained ResNet-50 backbone with a 3-layer FPN (P5–P3), and produces two outputs: (1) final bounding boxes for all detected objects, and (2) a three-level binary RL mask over FPN patches that zeros out confidence scores for unvisited regions, effectively filtering false positive anchors before the detection head. The RL mask is generated by running the trained agent through the FPN tree in a depth-first manner, recording which patches were visited.

## Method

AIRS formulates object search as a Markov Decision Process over the FPN hierarchy, where an RL agent performs top-down, depth-first traversal with a 5-dimensional action space (4 downward moves to child sub-patches + 1 upward move to the parent patch). The agent's Q-network is an evidential regression network outputting Normal-Inverse-Gaussian hyper-parameters, from which both the Q-value estimate and epistemic uncertainty are derived; the evidential Q-value (Q + λ·Var[μ]) dynamically balances exploitation of high-reward patches with exploration of uncertain ones, with λ annealed over training. The reward combines a quality score (ranked by DIoU-based positive anchor count within each layer) minus a depth- and epoch-dependent penalty, encouraging the agent to avoid costly deep searches in later training. States are encoded by a 3-layer MLP feature extractor followed by an RNN that accumulates temporal context, and the entire system is trained via off-policy Q-learning with a replay buffer. A theoretical convergence bound is established under Hölder smoothness of the Bellman operator, showing the gap to the optimal Q-function decays as O(n^{(α*−1)/2}) in sample size.

## Results

AIRS achieves 48.3 AP on MS COCO, 88.7 AP on Pascal VOC 2012, and 47.5 AP on Open Image V4, outperforming all two-stage (up to DINO-5scale at 47.9/87.1/46.4) and one-stage (up to GFocal at 45.0/86.5/45.8) baselines. The gains are most pronounced on small objects (AP^S: 32.1 vs 30.0 for DINO-5scale, 27.2 for GFocal on COCO) and on the constructed challenging subsets (AP^CH: 29.4 vs 29.0 for DINO-5scale, 25.4 for GFocal on COCO). Inference speed is competitive at 20.7 FPS with 32M parameters, comparable to GFocal (21.8 FPS) and FCOS (19.4 FPS).

## Baselines & Comparisons

AIRS outperforms the strongest two-stage baseline DINO-5scale by +0.4 AP on COCO, +1.6 on VOC, and +1.1 on Open Image V4, with the largest margins on small objects (+2.1 AP^S on COCO) and challenging subsets (+0.4 AP^CH on COCO, +4.4 on VOC, +0.8 on OIV4). Against its base one-stage detector GFocal, AIRS gains +3.3 AP on COCO, +2.2 on VOC, and +1.7 on OIV4, with particularly large improvements on small objects (+4.9 AP^S on COCO) and challenging subsets (+4.0 AP^CH on COCO, +3.0 on VOC, +2.7 on OIV4). Among other one-stage baselines, AIRS beats ATSS by +4.7 AP on COCO and +4.5 on VOC, and FCOS by +6.8 AP on COCO. Against RL-based detection baselines, AIRS achieves 88.7 mAP on VOC versus 73.1 for Tree-RL, 54.2 for PACNet, and 53.4 for ReinforceNet, demonstrating a substantial advantage in dense detection scenarios. Inference speed (20.7 FPS, 32M params) is slightly below GFocal (21.8 FPS) but competitive with all other one-stage methods, and the model generalizes consistently across Swin-T, EfficientNet, and ConvNeXt backbones (Table 2).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on GFocal's one-stage FPN-based detection framework and augments it with a reinforcement learning agent that performs hierarchical top-down search over the feature pyramid. The evidential Q-learning component adapts the Normal-Inverse-Gaussian distribution from evidential deep regression (Amini et al.) and the subjective-logic uncertainty decomposition from evidential deep learning (Sensoy et al.) to a regression Q-network, using epistemic uncertainty to drive exploration of unvisited patches. It departs from prior RL-based detectors (Bellver, Caicedo, Ba, Zhou) that either detect a fixed number of objects or operate without FPN integration, and from standard one-stage detectors that use a non-adaptive positive-anchor criterion favoring recall at the cost of precision.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical and persistent problem in dense detection—excessive false positives on complex scenes—by introducing an uncertainty-guided search that selectively prunes low-value patches, yielding ~4% gains on small-object and challenging-subset AP while maintaining competitive inference speed (32M params, ~21 FPS). The theoretical convergence guarantee for evidential Q-learning, while building on existing nonparametric regression results, provides a finite-sample bound that is tightened by the small action space (D=5) inherent to the hierarchical design. Open questions include whether the RL mask approach transfers to video or 3D detection, how it scales to even denser scenes (e.g., satellite imagery), and whether the added RL training complexity is justified over simpler adaptive thresholding strategies.
