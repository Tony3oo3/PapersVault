## Problem

Evaluating vision-language models on structure extraction tasks (e.g., converting a screenshot to code) is difficult because multiple valid structures can produce the same image, making reference-based comparison unreliable, and human evaluation is costly and slow. Existing VLM benchmarks either rely on expensive human judgment or are restricted to multiple-choice formats that cannot capture open-ended generation tasks like code synthesis.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243652/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each test instance is a screenshot (webpage at 1920×1080 native resolution, LaTeX diagram at 200 DPI, or cropped sheet-music section) paired with a zero-shot text prompt specifying the expected output format. The VLM produces structured code (LaTeX, HTML/CSS/JS as a JSON file list, or LilyPond), which is compiled by a task-specific renderer (TeX, Jekyll+Selenium, or LilyPond) into a rendered image. The final score is an image similarity value in [0,1] comparing the rendered image back to the original input, with compilation failures scored as zero.

## Method

Image2Struct introduces a round-trip evaluation pipeline that eliminates the need for ground-truth structures: an input image is fed to a VLM to produce code, the code is rendered into a new image, and the two images are compared automatically. The paper contributes two novel image similarity metrics—Cosine Similarity between Inception vectors (CIS), which uses only the penultimate-layer activations of Inception v3, and Block Earth Mover Similarity (EMS), which adapts Earth Mover's Distance by defining multidimensional signatures over pixel x-coordinates, y-coordinates, and values, then computing an optimal patch-level transport cost that penalizes spatial rearrangement. Data is continuously scraped from active online communities (GitHub Pages, arXiv, IMSLP) with a time window to minimize training-data leakage, and all 2,400 test instances across three domains are evaluated without human annotation. The benchmark is validated by showing that EMS and CIS correlate strongly (0.79 and 0.80) with Levenshtein edit distance to ground-truth structures where available.

## Results

Across 14 VLMs evaluated on 2,400 test instances spanning three domains (Webpages, LaTeX, Musical Scores), GPT-4o achieves the highest mean win rate of 0.923, with a rendering success rate of 0.980 and EMS of 0.710 on Webpages, 0.807/0.660 on LaTeX, and 0.491/0.340 on Musical Scores. The best single-domain EMS score is 0.724 (Claude 3.5 Sonnet on Webpages), while the average EMS across all models is only 0.370 (Webpages), 0.324 (LaTeX), and 0.069 (Music), indicating the benchmark is far from saturated. Closed-API models substantially outperform open-weight models, with the best open-weight model (LLaVA 1.6) achieving a mean win rate of only 0.385 versus 0.564 for the worst closed-API model (Palmyra Vision 003).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GPT-4o leads overall with a mean win rate of 0.923, outperforming Claude 3.5 Sonnet (0.821) and Gemini 1.5 Pro (0.769) across the aggregate ranking, though Claude 3.5 Sonnet edges it out on Webpages EMS (0.724 vs. 0.710) and Gemini 1.5 Pro matches it closely on LaTeX EMS (0.616 vs. 0.660). Claude 3 Opus achieves the highest LaTeX rendering success rate (0.836) and the best Music EMS (0.367), but its Webpages performance is notably weaker (RSR 0.655, EMS 0.378) compared to GPT-4o (0.980, 0.710). GPT-4 Vision performs competitively on Webpages (EMS 0.653) and LaTeX (EMS 0.598) but completely fails on Musical Scores (RSR 0.000) due to copyright-refusal behavior, whereas GPT-4o does not exhibit this refusal. Among open-weight models, LLaVA 1.6 (13B) is the strongest (mean win rate 0.385) but still trails the worst closed-API model (Palmyra Vision 003, 0.564) by a wide margin. Qwen-VL Chat and all IDEFICS variants perform near-zero on Webpages (RSR ≤ 0.031), suggesting they cannot generate valid HTML/CSS/JS, while Qwen-VL Chat achieves a reasonable LaTeX EMS of 0.525 despite its Webpages failure. Gemini 1.0 Pro Vision is the best model on Musical Scores (EMS 0.402, RSR 0.583), surpassing even GPT-4o (0.340, 0.491) in that domain.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Earth Mover's Distance framework (Rubner et al., 1998) by introducing a block-based, spatially-aware variant (EMS) that preserves positional information lost in classic EMD, and on LPIPS (Zhang et al., 2018) by simplifying it to a single-layer cosine similarity (CIS) for computational efficiency. Conceptually, the round-trip evaluation paradigm (image → structure → rendered image → similarity score) draws from backtranslation and cycle-consistency ideas in NLP and image-to-image translation, but is repurposed here as an evaluation methodology rather than a training signal. The paper positions itself against existing image-to-code benchmarks (Design2Code, Plot2Code, Soselia et al.) by offering a multi-domain, fully automatic, and renewable evaluation that avoids both human annotation and static test sets vulnerable to data leakage.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Image2Struct provides a practical, renewable benchmark that the VLM community can use to track progress on structure extraction without the cost of human evaluation or the risk of data contamination. The finding that even GPT-4o achieves only 0.724 EMS on the 'easiest' domain (Webpages) and 0.340 on Musical Scores indicates substantial headroom for improvement, while the domain-specific disparities (equations vs. plots, HTML vs. LilyPond) highlight that VLMs have uneven capabilities across structured formats. Open questions include whether round-trip supervision can be used for fine-tuning, whether evaluator VLMs can replace hand-crafted image metrics, and how performance changes under perturbations like noise or skew. The benchmark's renewable data pipeline also raises the question of longitudinal tracking as models improve.
