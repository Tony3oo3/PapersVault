## Problem

Existing transfer-based adversarial attacks assume the attacker can access the victim model's task and training data to build a closely aligned surrogate model. This paper tackles the harder and more practical scenario where the attacker only possesses the open-sourced SAM foundation model but has no knowledge of the downstream task, dataset, or fine-tuned parameters, yet still needs to generate effective adversarial examples that transfer to arbitrary SAM-based downstream models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242778/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a task-specific input image (e.g., a CT scan at 512×512 resolution), the open-sourced SAM ViT-H image encoder as the surrogate, and a pre-computed universal meta-initialization (UMI) perturbation learned offline on 20,000 natural images. It produces an adversarial perturbation δ_s bounded by ε=10 in the l∞ norm, which when added to the clean input yields an adversarial example that degrades segmentation performance on the unknown downstream model. The UMI is computed once offline (7 meta-iterations) and reused across all inputs, while the real-time attack requires only 10 gradient steps per image.

## Method

The method has two components. First, a Universal Meta-Initialization (UMI) algorithm uses Reptile meta-learning to jointly optimize a perturbation that is both a universal adversarial perturbation on the foundation model and a fast-adapting initialization for any downstream task, extracting the intrinsic vulnerability that persists after fine-tuning. Second, a Gradient Robust Adversarial Attack (GRAT) addresses a theoretically proven deviation: Proposition 1 shows that maximizing feature-embedding distance in the surrogate model produces a gradient direction that diverges from the optimal attack direction on the fine-tuned victim model due to the unknown parameter update Δφ_τ. To mitigate this, the gradient robust loss L_GR augments each layer's gradient with isotropic Gaussian noise, simulating the uncertainty from fine-tuning, so that the resulting adversarial perturbation remains effective despite the gradient mismatch. The full UMI-GRAT pipeline is plug-and-play and can be combined with existing attacks (MI-FGSM, PGN, BSR, ILPD) to further boost their transferability.

## Results

On Medical SAM (CT-Scans), UMI-GRAT reduces mDSC from 81.88 (clean) to 5.22 and increases mHD to 111.87, dramatically outperforming the best baseline BSR (mDSC 25.7, mHD 94.48). On Shadow-SAM (ISTD), UMI-GRAT achieves a BER of 12.46, more than doubling the best baseline (BSR at 5.20). On Camouflaged-SAM, UMI-GRAT achieves the best Sα and MAE across all three datasets (COD10K: Sα=0.360, MAE=0.248; CAMO: Sα=0.329, MAE=0.308; CHAME: Sα=0.332, MAE=0.293), with the gap over baselines being smaller since the data distribution closely resembles natural images.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UMI-GRAT decisively outperforms all five baselines on the two most challenging downstream tasks where the data distribution differs substantially from natural images. On Medical SAM, UMI-GRAT reduces mDSC to 5.22, a 4.9× improvement over the best baseline BSR (25.70) and 7.8× over MI-FGSM (40.83); on Shadow-SAM, it achieves BER 12.46 versus 5.20 for BSR (the strongest baseline). On Camouflaged-SAM, where data resembles natural images and all methods transfer well, UMI-GRAT still achieves the best Sα and MAE on all three datasets (COD10K, CAMO, CHAME), though the margin over ILPD and PGN is narrower (e.g., Sα 0.329 vs. 0.310 for ILPD on CAMO). The plug-and-play combinations (UMI-GRAT+BSR, UMI-GRAT+ILPD) further push mDSC below 4 on Medical SAM, confirming that UMI-GRAT composes additively with existing transfer-based strategies. Overall, the gradient robust loss (GR) contributes most when surrogate and victim distributions diverge (medical, shadow), while the universal meta-initialization (UMI) provides consistent gains across all settings, particularly when the victim model retains substantial information from the foundation model.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends transfer-based adversarial attacks to a more constrained and practical setting: attacking SAM's fine-tuned downstream models using only the open-sourced SAM as a surrogate, without knowledge of the downstream task or training data. It builds on Reptile (Nichol et al., 2018) for the meta-learning-based universal perturbation initialization and on MI-FGSM (Dong et al., 2018) as the base iterative attack, but departs from prior transfer attacks (DMI-FGSM, PGN, BSR, ILPD) that all assume the surrogate and victim models share the same task and data distribution. The gradient robust loss is a new theoretical contribution that formalizes the gradient deviation between surrogate and fine-tuned models via a chain-rule decomposition (Proposition 1), positioning the method as complementary to intermediate-level attacks rather than a replacement.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first systematic study of adversarial vulnerability in SAM-based downstream models under a realistic threat model where the attacker has no access to the private fine-tuning data, which is the actual deployment scenario for medical, shadow, and camouflage segmentation. The dramatic performance collapse (mDSC 81.88 → 5.22 on medical SAM) highlights a concrete security risk for organizations fine-tuning open-source foundation models on sensitive data. Key open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond SAM to other foundation models (e.g., CLIP, DINOv2), whether the gradient robust loss transfers to non-segmentation tasks, and what effective defenses exist against this class of attack — the paper explicitly acknowledges it provides no defense.
