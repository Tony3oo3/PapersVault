## Problem

Existing time-series variable selection algorithms return only a single Markov Boundary (MB) for a target, yet multiple minimal-size, equally predictive subsets can exist due to informational equivalence among variables. Finding only one solution is insufficient for knowledge discovery and can mislead practitioners, as other equally valid MBs may contain variables that were filtered out. No prior algorithm addressed the Multiple Time-Series Variable Selection (MTVS) problem of identifying all such minimal, optimally predictive subsets for multivariate time-series data.

## Input / Output

The method takes a multivariate time series of N variables with M regularly sampled observations, a designated target variable T, a maximum lag L, and three threshold parameters (α for forward termination, γ for backward elimination, δ for equivalence). Rather than enumerating all MBs explicitly (which can be exponential), the output is a compact representation: a reference MB plus a set of equivalence classes, where each class contains informationally equivalent variables that can be substituted to generate distinct MBs. Variables are further characterized as irreplaceable (in all MBs), replaceable (in some but not all), redundant, or irrelevant.

## Method

ChronoEpilogi operates in two variants: FBE (Forward-Backward-Equivalent) and FE (Forward-Equivalent, a greedy approximation with ~70% speedup). The forward phase greedily selects informative variables by maximizing Lag-Pearson correlation between candidate variables and ARDL model residuals, terminating when a likelihood ratio test confirms no further improvement. The backward phase (FBE only) iteratively removes redundant variables, and the equivalence phase tests whether any non-selected variable can substitute a selected one while preserving model performance. The key theoretical contribution is proving soundness and completeness under two mild assumptions—Composition (pairwise conditional independences imply joint conditional independence) and Interchangeability (any two MBs decompose into pairs of equivalent singletons)—which together guarantee that the algorithm finds all MBs without exhaustive enumeration. The FE variant accelerates equivalence discovery by testing residual-based proxies during the forward phase rather than fitting full replacement models, trading a small f1-score loss for substantial runtime reduction.

## Results

On the synthetic benchmark (270 MTS), ChronoEpilogi-FE achieves a causal f1-score of 0.94±0.19 with R²=0.373, matching FBE's predictive performance while running 70% faster (118s vs 375s). On five real datasets (Electricity, Solar, Traffic, PEMS-BAY, METR-LA), FE selects on average fewer than 4% of available TS variables (e.g., 11.7 out of 862 in Traffic, 4.1 out of 325 in PEMS-BAY), reducing variable count by 96% on average while conserving or improving R² over no-selection baselines. The Friedman-Nemenyi test confirms FE and GroupLasso are statistically indistinguishable in predictive performance, yet FE produces solutions 8× smaller than GL in Traffic, PEMS-BAY, and METR-LA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is GroupLasso (GL), the most scalable single-solution TVS method. On synthetic data, FE dramatically outperforms GL on causal f1-score (0.94 vs 0.57) while achieving comparable R² (0.373 vs 0.337) and similar runtime (118s vs 120s); the paper attributes GL's lower causal accuracy to its sensitivity to regularization tuning. On real datasets, the Friedman-Nemenyi post-hoc test finds no statistically significant difference between FE and GL in predictive performance (R²), confirming they are on par. However, FE produces substantially smaller solutions: in Traffic, PEMS-BAY, and METR-LA, FE selects 8× fewer variables than GL (e.g., 11.7 vs 79.1 in Traffic; 4.1 vs 37.7 in PEMS-BAY), while in Electricity and Solar GL produces slightly smaller sets. Both FE and GL outperform No Selection across all five datasets, with FE achieving the best R² on Electricity (0.940) and METR-LA (0.886). The key differentiator is that FE additionally returns multiple equivalent Markov boundaries (up to 7×10²⁴ in Traffic), enabling knowledge discovery that GL cannot provide.

## Relation to Prior Work

ChronoEpilogi extends the forward-backward Markov boundary discovery framework of KIAMB (Peña et al., 2007) and the multiple-solution greedy selection theory of Borboudakis & Tsamardinos (2021) from cross-sectional/cohort data to a time-series forecasting setting. The key conceptual departure is the introduction of two distributional assumptions—Composition and Interchangeability—that are weaker than faithfulness yet sufficient to guarantee soundness and completeness of a greedy algorithm for discovering all Markov boundaries in multivariate time series. Unlike Group Lasso, which returns a single sparse solution via regularization, ChronoEpilogi uses likelihood-ratio tests on ARDL model residuals to identify equivalence classes of informationally interchangeable variables, producing a compact Cartesian-product representation of the full solution space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for high-dimensional time-series forecasting: reducing input variables by 96% on average while conserving or improving predictive performance makes real-time deployment and model interpretation far more tractable. The finding that SHAP feature importance is nearly random (stability ≈ 0.05) across data splits when replaceable variables are present is a concrete warning for XAI practitioners who may draw misleading causal conclusions from correlated features. Key limitations include the stationarity assumption, reliance on linear ARDL models for residual computation, and evaluation on only five real datasets; relaxing stationarity and extending to non-linear residual models are identified as important future directions.
