## Problem

Existing multi-objective preference alignment methods (MORLHF, MODPO, SFT-based) are tightly coupled to policy model parameters, requiring expensive re-training for every new foundation model, and are statically aligned on a fixed set of pre-determined objectives with no mechanism to generalize to unseen ones. No prior method achieves both policy-agnostic alignment (working on any model, including closed-source LLMs) and zero-shot generalization to new alignment objectives.

## Input / Output

MetaAligner is a conditional seq2seq model that takes as input a query, the unaligned response generated by a frozen policy model, and a set of natural-language objective descriptions (e.g., 'Harmlessness: The response should avoid content that is offensive...'), all assembled into a prompting template. It outputs an edited/corrected response that better satisfies the specified objectives. Because objectives are specified purely as text in the prompt, the set of alignment targets can be freely recombined, shuffled, or extended with entirely new objective descriptions at inference time without any retraining.

## Method

MetaAligner follows a three-stage paradigm. First, a dynamic objectives reformulation algorithm reorganizes standard preference datasets by partitioning each response pair's objectives into sets where y1 wins, y2 wins, or both tie, then constructs contrastive and equal training subsets with randomly shuffled objective descriptions to enforce flexible objective conditioning. Second, a conditional weak-to-strong correction module (a standard LLM) is trained via SFT on top of a frozen policy model using a three-step curriculum: warming up on identical-response pairs, equal-preference alignment on tied pairs, and contrastive-preference alignment on contrastive pairs — the policy model's parameters are never updated, making the method policy-agnostic and applicable to closed-source models. Third, at inference the target objectives are specified solely through their text descriptions in the prompt, enabling zero-shot alignment to unseen objectives via in-context learning and theoretically unlimited simultaneous alignment targets. The key architectural insight is that by decoupling the correction module from the policy model and encoding objectives as prompt text rather than learned reward weights, the method achieves both plug-and-play deployment across arbitrary policy models and generalizable multi-objective alignment.

## Results

MetaAligner-13B achieves the highest average win rates of 82.67% on HH-RLHF and 61.75% on UltraFeedback when applied to LLaMA2-Chat-7B, outperforming all baselines. Across 10 policy models (ranging from 2B to 70B parameters, plus GPT-3.5-Turbo and Claude-3-Sonnet), MetaAligner-13B delivers up to +51.83% win-rate advantage on individual objectives (e.g., 'Instruction following' on UltraFeedback with LLaMA2-Chat-7B). The smallest model, MetaAligner-1.1B, saves up to 93.63% GPU training hours compared to MORLHF while maintaining competitive average performance (71.5% on HH-RLHF vs. 63.84% for MORLHF).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MetaAligner-13B decisively outperforms all four baselines on both datasets: it leads MORLHF by 18.83% (HH-RLHF) and 30.33% (UltraFeedback), MODPO by 13.67% and 27.65%, SFT (RiC) by 10.0% and 30.25%, and Aligner-7B by 8.0% and 18.52% in average win rates. MetaAligner-7B also surpasses all baselines (80.83% vs. 74.67% for Aligner-7B on HH-RLHF; 49.83% vs. 43.23% on UltraFeedback), while MetaAligner-1.1B matches or exceeds MORLHF/MODPO/SFT on HH-RLHF (71.5% vs. 63.84%/69.0%/72.67%) at only 6.4%–48.7% of their GPU training hours. On the larger LLaMA2-Chat-70B policy model, MetaAligner-7B beats Self-Refinement by 13.49% (HH-RLHF) and 8.84% (UltraFeedback) in average win rate at roughly 1/10 the inference cost. A notable weakness is that MetaAligner-1.1B underperforms on UltraFeedback (28.25% avg.) compared to all baselines, suggesting the smaller model struggles with the more complex single-turn queries in that dataset.

## Relation to Prior Work

MetaAligner extends the weak-to-strong correction paradigm of Aligner [12] from single-objective to multi-objective alignment, while departing fundamentally from prior multi-objective methods (MORLHF, MODPO, RiC) by decoupling alignment from policy model parameters. Where MORLHF relies on PPO-based RL and MODPO on DPO, both requiring full access to and training of the policy model, MetaAligner uses a lightweight seq2seq module trained via SFT that operates solely on policy model outputs. The dynamic objectives reformulation algorithm also distinguishes it from RiC-style methods that require explicit reward-to-text mappings, instead using preference vectors to construct objective-conditioned training pairs without scalar reward values.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The policy-agnostic design enables alignment of close-source models (GPT-3.5-Turbo, Claude-3) without parameter access and saves up to 93.63% GPU training hours, making multi-objective alignment practical for rapidly iterating foundation models. The zero-shot generalization to unseen objectives via in-context learning opens a new direction for expandable alignment, though the paper acknowledges limitations: only 6 unseen objectives were tested, the stacking architecture increases inference cost, and 'alignment tax' effects (e.g., aligning on Fairness degrading Readability) remain unresolved. Open questions include scalability to dozens of simultaneous objectives, domain-specific adaptation, and whether the generalization gap between supervised and in-context objectives can be closed.
