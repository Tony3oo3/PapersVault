## Problem

Existing semi-implicit variational inference (SIVI) methods parameterize the mixing distribution using pushforward (implicit) distributions, which makes the variational density intractable and prevents direct ELBO optimization. As a result, prior methods must resort to optimizing ELBO bounds, running expensive inner-loop MCMC chains, or solving minimax objectives. No prior SIVI method directly optimizes the ELBO while making no parametric assumption on the mixing distribution.

## Input / Output

The method takes a probabilistic model p(x,y), observed data y, a reparameterized neural network kernel k_θ (defined via a diffeomorphism φ and base distribution p_k), and regularization parameters (λ_r, λ_θ). It produces an approximate posterior q_{θ,r} where the mixing distribution r is represented as an empirical measure of M particles {Z_m} rather than a parametric family, and the variational parameters (θ, r) are jointly optimized. The kernel must satisfy a reparameterization assumption (φ(z,·) is a diffeomorphism for all z) to enable path-wise gradient estimation.

## Method

PVI formulates SIVI as minimization of a regularized free energy E_λ over the product space Θ × P(R^{dz}) equipped with Euclidean-Wasserstein geometry, where the θ-component follows standard Euclidean gradient descent and the r-component follows a Wasserstein-2 gradient flow. The key insight is identifying this gradient flow with a McKean-Vlasov SDE (the PVI flow) whose drift depends on the first variation of the free energy and whose diffusion is driven by the KL regularizer, enabling simulation without access to ∇_z log r_t. The algorithm discretizes in space via a particle approximation of r (M Dirac masses) and in time via Euler-Maruyama, with path-wise Monte Carlo gradient estimators (reparameterization trick) for the intractable score terms. Preconditioners (e.g., RMSProp-inspired) provide adaptive stepsizes for the θ and Z components. Theoretically, the paper establishes existence/uniqueness of the gradient flow solutions and propagation of chaos for the particle approximation via Γ-convergence analysis of a regularized functional.

## Results

On three toy density estimation problems (Banana, Multimodal, X-Shape), PVI achieves rejection rates at or near the nominal 0.05 level (0.05–0.06) and the lowest sliced Wasserstein distances (0.05–0.17), while UVI, SVI, and SM show substantially higher rejection rates (up to 0.65 for UVI on Multimodal) and larger Wasserstein distances. On Bayesian neural network regression, PVI attains the lowest test-set RMS error on all three datasets: 0.43 (Concrete), 0.87 (Protein), and 0.13 (Yacht), compared to 0.50/0.92/0.17–0.18 for UVI and SVI, and 0.92/1.02/0.98 for SM.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PVI outperforms all three SIVI baselines across every task. Against SM (score matching, Yu & Zhang 2023), PVI's advantage is the most dramatic: SM's rejection rates are 3–8× higher (e.g., 0.39 vs 0.06 on Banana) and its BNN RMS errors are roughly 2× worse (e.g., 0.92 vs 0.43 on Concrete). Against UVI (Titsias & Ruiz 2019), PVI wins on rejection rate for all three density problems and on RMS error for all three BNN datasets (by 0.05–0.07), though UVI achieves a slightly lower Wasserstein distance on Banana (0.11 vs 0.17). Against SVI (Yin & Zhou 2018), PVI is consistently better on both metrics; the gap is largest on the Multimodal problem where SVI's rejection rate is 0.13 versus PVI's 0.05. Overall, PVI is the only method that simultaneously achieves near-nominal rejection rates and the lowest Wasserstein distances on all density estimation problems, and the lowest RMS error on all BNN regression tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the semi-implicit variational inference (SIVI) framework of Yin and Zhou (2018) by replacing the standard pushforward/implicit parameterization of the mixing distribution with direct optimization over the full space P(R^{dz}) via particles. A key conceptual departure is Proposition 1, which shows that all existing SIVI parameterizations (SVI, UVI, SM) define the same variational family when using pushforward distributions, meaning the combined kernel-and-mixing parameterization of Yu and Zhang (2023) offers no additional expressivity. The gradient flow formulation draws on the Euclidean-Wasserstein geometry developed by Kuntz et al. (2023) for particle maximum likelihood, adapted here to the SIVI free energy functional, and the resulting McKean-Vlasov SDE connects to the broader literature on Wasserstein gradient flows in variational inference (Lambert et al., 2022).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By avoiding pushforward distributions for the mixing distribution, PVI can in principle express arbitrary mixing distributions (as M→∞), which is a fundamental expressivity advantage over all prior SIVI methods that are constrained by the neural network's ability to learn complex mappings. The theoretical contributions (existence/uniqueness of the gradient flow, propagation of chaos) provide rigorous justification for the particle approximation. However, the main limitation is that the theory only applies to a regularized variant (γ>0) while experiments use γ=0, leaving a gap between analysis and practice. An open question the authors highlight is kernel design: certain architectures (skip connections) are necessary for the kernel to effectively exploit an expressive mixing distribution, and systematic kernel design for PVI remains unresolved.
