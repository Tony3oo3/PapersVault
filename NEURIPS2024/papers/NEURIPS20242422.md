## Problem

Existing video motion control methods rely on training-based paradigms that require substantial compute and retraining for each new model architecture, and they treat motion encoding as a black box. A fundamental question remains unaddressed: how do video diffusion models actually encode cross-frame motion information within their intermediate features, and can this information be extracted and manipulated without any additional training?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242422/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on 512×512, 16-frame videos generated with 25 DDIM steps (≈3 min per sample on an RTX 3090). For motion control, it consumes a region mask and either a synthesized motion-direction signal (a piecewise-linear function fitted to motion-channel statistics) or a reference video from which MOFT is extracted via DDIM inversion. For point-drag manipulation, it additionally takes per-frame target point coordinates. The output is a video where motion within the masked region follows the specified direction or trajectory while content outside the mask is preserved via shared K&V and masked gradient clipping.

## Method

The core contribution is MOFT, a motion-aware feature extracted from intermediate U-Net blocks (after upper block 1) via two operations: (1) content correlation removal, which subtracts the frame-wise mean from each spatial position to isolate residual motion from shared semantic/appearance content, and (2) a motion channel filter that retains only the top ~4% of channels ranked by their PCA projection weights, which are shown to correlate strongly with panning direction. Motion control is achieved by optimizing noisy latents at each denoising step with a loss that aligns the extracted MOFT to a reference MOFT over the masked region, requiring no parameter updates. For point-drag, a compositional loss schedules MOFT guidance in early denoising steps (coarse motion) and DIFT guidance in later steps (precise point tracking), exploiting the observation that MOFT remains informative at high-noise timesteps where DIFT correspondence breaks down. The entire pipeline is training-free and generalizes across AnimateDiff, ModelScope, Stable Video Diffusion, ZeroScope, and Open-Sora without architecture-specific modifications.

## Results

On the motion feature design benchmark (270 prompt-motion direction pairs), MOFT (content correlation removal + motion channel filter) achieves a Motion Fidelity of 82.5 and Image Quality of 0.693, substantially outperforming the vanilla feature (31.1 / 0.512) and the SMM baseline (70.2 / 0.681). With synthesized guidance, MOFT reaches 84.0 Motion Fidelity while maintaining near-original image quality (0.693 vs. 0.697 unguided). In point-drag manipulation (40 image-motion pairs), MOFT-assisted DIFT reduces mean drag distance to 0.175, a 2.5× improvement over DIFT alone (0.437), though still trailing the training-based DragNUWA (0.075).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the SMM feature baseline [Yatim et al., 49], MOFT improves Motion Fidelity by 12.3 points (82.5 vs. 70.2) while also matching or exceeding image quality (0.693 vs. 0.681), confirming that the motion channel filter adds meaningful signal beyond simple frame-difference features. In point-drag manipulation, MOFT-assisted DIFT [Tang et al., 41] cuts the mean drag distance from 0.437 to 0.175, a 2.5× reduction, but still lags behind the training-based DragNUWA [Yin et al., 50] (0.075), indicating that learned trajectory supervision retains an advantage in absolute precision. In the user study (26 raters, 56 clips), the proposed method achieves the highest Naturalness score (3.49) among all three methods, surpassing both Gen-2 Motion Brush [Runway, 35] (2.90) and DragNUWA (2.08), while its Faithfulness (3.21) is competitive with Gen-2 MB (3.37) and clearly ahead of DragNUWA (2.50). Overall, the training-free MOFT pipeline matches or beats training-based methods on motion naturalness and is competitive on faithfulness, without requiring any model retraining.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the diffusion feature understanding line (DIFT for semantic correspondence, FreeControl for spatial control) to the temporal/motion dimension, showing that video diffusion features already encode interpretable motion information that can be isolated via content correlation removal and PCA-based channel filtering. It departs from the dominant training-based motion control paradigm (MotionCtrl, DragNUWA, VideoComposer) by demonstrating that no additional modules or retraining are needed—motion guidance is achieved purely through latent optimization against extracted MOFT features. The latent optimization framework itself is inherited from DragDiffusion and Space-time diffusion features, while the point-drag component composes MOFT with DIFT for a two-stage coarse-to-fine strategy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free, architecture-agnostic nature of MOFT means practitioners can apply motion control to any new video diffusion checkpoint without retraining, which is practically significant given the rapid pace of model releases. The interpretability angle (PCA separation, channel-level motion encoding) provides architectural insights that could inform future model design. Key limitations remain: the method cannot yet operate on real videos due to DDIM inversion artifacts, and precise motion scale control is unresolved. Open questions include whether better video inversion techniques will unlock real-video editing, and whether the motion channel structure is stable across model scales and training regimes.
