## Problem

Pre-trained vision-language models suffer from catastrophic forgetting when continually adapting to new downstream tasks, and existing anti-forgetting strategies (replay, knowledge distillation, weight consolidation) are entangled with the learning process, creating an inherent learning-forgetting trade-off. This paper identifies that the stability of a model's consecutive zero-shot predictions on unseen data is a reliable proxy for its anti-forgetting capability, and leverages this insight to decouple learning from forgetting entirely.

## Input / Output

The method consumes image-text triplets (one image, two texts with one concept altered) from both task-specific training data and a large unpaired, unlabeled wild dataset (12,358 images, 30,144 texts, 21,006 triplets). The BLIP-based model (ViT-B/16 image encoder + 12-layer BERT text encoder + cross-attention decoder + 2-layer MLP classifier) outputs a binary probability indicating whether the text precisely describes the image. Wild data images and texts are deliberately shuffled within training batches to ensure no pairing, and the wild set is entirely disjoint from all downstream task data.

## Method

ZAF introduces an EMA-LoRA architecture with two low-rank adapters: a standard LoRA adapter (A·B) updated on current task data via cross-entropy loss, and an EMA adapter (Ŵ) that accumulates historical knowledge through exponential moving average (α=0.85) at each epoch boundary. The key innovation is a zero-shot antidote loss L_ZS = ||P^t(T_wild, I_wild) − P̂^t(T_wild, I_wild)||₁, which regularizes the current model's predictions on wild data to match those of the previous EMA model, thereby stabilizing zero-shot predictions without touching task data. This decouples learning (CE loss on task data) from anti-forgetting (stability loss on wild data), avoiding the trade-off inherent in knowledge distillation approaches that operate on the same data. A PAC-Bayes theoretical analysis (Proposition 1) shows that generalization error bounds for old and future tasks share the same form, justifying why zero-shot stability on wild data reliably reflects anti-forgetting capability.

## Results

On the primary 'BLIP w/ NLVR' model, ZAF achieves FAA of 89.67%, 91.78%, and 88.74% on the 7 Task VG+VAW, 7 Task VG, and 5 Task VAW benchmarks respectively, with FFM (forgetting) of only 3.38%, 2.02%, and 2.67%. These results lead the strongest competitor ConStruct-VL by 3.70%, 4.82%, and 4.38% in FAA, while reducing forgetting by 3.56, 5.89, and 2.69 points in FFM. ZAF also achieves 10–30× training speedup over ConStruct-VL (e.g., 8.35h vs. 247.78h on 7 Task VG+VAW) with comparable model size (236M params). The zero-shot antidote, when plugged into existing methods, reduces FFM by an average of 6.37% across all benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ZAF outperforms all 10 baselines across every benchmark and metric. Against the strongest competitor ConStruct-VL [33] (which uses expensive adversarial pseudo-replay), ZAF leads by 3.70–4.82% in FAA and reduces FFM by 3.56–5.89 points, while training 11–30× faster. The parameter-isolation method Layered-LoRA [33] achieves 80.62% FAA on 7 Task VG+VAW (BLIP w/ NLVR) but suffers 13.92% forgetting, versus ZAF's 89.67% FAA and 3.38% FFM. Knowledge-distillation-based LwF [21] and weight-ensemble ZSCL [49] both show severe forgetting (FFM 23–31%), with ZSCL actually degrading below plain LoRA on the harder benchmarks. MoE-Adapters [45] and plain LoRA [10] lack anti-forgetting mechanisms and exhibit FFM above 23%. Among unimodal baselines (L2 [34], EWC [15], L2P [43], Dual Prompt [42]), the gap is even wider, with ZAF leading by 5.37% FAA over the best unimodal method (L2) on 7 Task VG+VAW, confirming that unimodal CL strategies are insufficient for multimodal reasoning tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on LoRA's low-rank adapter structure but introduces a temporal mixture strategy via Exponential Moving Average (EMA) on the adapters, departing from the spatial Mixture-of-Experts (MoE-Adapters) and task-specific parameter isolation (Layered-LoRA) used in prior continual VL frameworks. The key conceptual departure is the 'zero-shot antidote': rather than relying on replay, knowledge distillation on old data, or adversarial pseudo-replay (as in ConStruct-VL), it regularizes the model to maintain stable zero-shot predictions on unlabeled, unpaired wild data, effectively decoupling learning from forgetting. The PAC-Bayes theoretical analysis showing that old-task and future-task generalization errors share structurally identical upper bounds provides a formal justification for this connection, which prior CL work had not established.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play zero-shot antidote offers a privacy-preserving, replay-free mechanism that can be added to existing CL methods (demonstrated across LoRA, Layered-LoRA, MoE-Adapters, and ConStruct-VL), making it broadly applicable. The 10–30× training speedup over ConStruct-VL while achieving superior accuracy is practically significant for deployment. However, the approach is validated only on BLIP variants and SVLC reasoning tasks (not classification or generation), the wild data construction depends on ChatGPT4, and the method assumes a feasible base CL framework already exists. Open questions include whether the zero-shot stability signal generalizes to open-ended generation tasks or to settings where the pre-trained model has very weak zero-shot capability.
