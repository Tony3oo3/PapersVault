## Problem

Model-based RL algorithms suffer from an 'objective mismatch' where dynamics and reward models are trained with surrogate losses (e.g., MSE) rather than directly optimizing task performance, and prior differentiable trajectory optimization methods have only been demonstrated on low-dimensional state spaces with known dynamics. No existing approach combines differentiable trajectory optimization as a policy representation with deep model-based RL or scales it to high-dimensional sensory observations like images and point clouds on standard benchmarks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243473/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes high-dimensional sensory observations—RGB images (64×64 for DMC, multi-view for Robomimic) or 3D point clouds (512–1200 sampled points for MetaWorld/ManiSkill)—along with robot joint states, and produces continuous action sequences for robot end-effectors or locomotion joints. In the RL setting, actions are generated by solving a trajectory optimization problem in a learned latent space (dimension 50) using the Levenberg-Marquardt solver, initialized by a learned policy network. In the IL setting, a CVAE decoder performs the same trajectory optimization to reconstruct expert actions, with the planning horizon typically set to H=1.

## Method

DiffTORI replaces the non-differentiable MPPI planner in TD-MPC with a differentiable trajectory optimization layer (via the Theseus library), enabling backpropagation of a policy gradient loss through the optimization process to directly optimize the encoder, latent dynamics, reward, and Q-value networks for task performance rather than surrogate losses. The trajectory optimization problem is formulated in latent space with the dynamics constraints manually unrolled into the objective (since Theseus solves unconstrained nonlinear least-squares), and solved with Levenberg-Marquardt. For imitation learning, the method learns a cost function f_θ(z,a) and latent dynamics d_θ(z,a) end-to-end by backpropagating the imitation loss through the differentiable optimizer, with a CVAE wrapper to capture multimodal action distributions. A key practical design is action refinement: a pretrained base policy (e.g., Diffusion Policy or BC) provides the action initialization for the trajectory optimizer, which then iteratively refines it, consistently outperforming both the base policy and residual learning alternatives.

## Results

On 15 DeepMind Control Suite tasks (image observations), DiffTORI achieves the highest normalized return on 14/15 tasks, outperforming TD-MPC, SAC, and DrQ-v2 by a noticeable margin. On 22 MetaWorld tasks (point cloud inputs), DiffTORI+DP3 achieves 56.5% average success rate versus 48.8% for DP3 and 49.8% for Residual+DP3. On 5 Robomimic/Push-T tasks (image inputs), DiffTORI+Diffusion achieves 0.07 average failure rate, the lowest among all 8 compared methods. On 9 ManiSkill tasks (point cloud inputs), DiffTORI+BC achieves 0.42 average success rate versus 0.34 for BC and 0.36 for BC+residual.

## Baselines & Comparisons

In model-based RL (15 DMC tasks), DiffTORI outperforms TD-MPC (its base algorithm) by a large margin on 14/15 tasks, and also beats model-free baselines SAC and DrQ-v2, confirming that the policy gradient loss back-propagated through differentiable trajectory optimization is the key improvement over simply replacing MPPI with trajectory optimization. On MetaWorld (22 tasks), DiffTORI+DP3 improves average success rate by 7.7 percentage points over DP3 (56.5% vs 48.8%) and 6.7 points over Residual+DP3 (56.5% vs 49.8%), with the largest gains on harder tasks like Shelf Place (+14.3%) and Sweep Into (+15.0%). On Robomimic, DiffTORI+Diffusion achieves 0.07 average failure rate, beating Diffusion Policy (0.10), Residual+Diffusion (0.10), and dramatically outperforming IBC+Diffusion (0.78), showing that the EBM training objective actually degrades base actions while DiffTORI's differentiable trajectory optimization consistently refines them. On ManiSkill (9 tasks), DiffTORI+BC improves average success rate from 0.34 (BC) to 0.42, with the largest gain on PickCube (0.32 vs 0.19). Against Amos et al.'s differentiable MPC on pendulum swing-up, DiffTORI performs slightly worse without damping (14.60 vs 13.58 cost) but noticeably better with damping (10.64 vs 14.87), since Amos et al. assumes correct dynamics structure while DiffTORI learns it with neural networks.

## Relation to Prior Work

DiffTORI extends the line of work on differentiable trajectory optimization as an implicit policy (Amos et al. 2018, Jin et al. 2020), which previously only handled low-dimensional states with known or partially known dynamics. The key departure is combining differentiable trajectory optimization (via the Theseus library) with deep model-based RL (building on TD-MPC), where the encoder, latent dynamics, reward, and Q-function are all learned end-to-end by backpropagating a policy gradient loss through the optimization process. For imitation learning, it occupies a middle ground between explicit policies (BC, Diffusion Policy) and implicit policies (IBC), using test-time optimization with a learned cost function but with a more stable training procedure than EBM-based approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that the 'objective mismatch' problem in model-based RL—where surrogate losses for dynamics/reward prediction don't align with task performance—can be directly addressed by differentiating through the planning process, and that this approach scales to high-dimensional image and point cloud observations across 50 standard benchmark tasks. A clear limitation is computational cost: solving and backpropagating through trajectory optimization is ~5.6× slower per step than MPPI, though the authors note that GPU-accelerated solvers (e.g., BaSpaCho) and future algorithmic improvements may close this gap. Open questions include the relationship between the learned cost function and inverse RL, whether the approach generalizes to tasks with hard constraints, and whether the CVAE-based multimodal formulation can be simplified.
