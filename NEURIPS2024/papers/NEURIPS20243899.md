## Problem

Existing model merging methods either suffer from significant performance degradation relative to individual models or require tuning via validation data, training, or manually computed matrices. The paper identifies the root cause as a fundamental paradigm limitation: attempting to represent N task-specific models with a single merged weight vector is inherently sub-optimal, especially as the number of tasks grows or tasks become more challenging.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243899/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes N finetuned model weights (all derived from the same pretrained model) and the pretrained weights as input, computing task vectors as element-wise differences. It outputs a single unified task vector shared across all tasks, plus per-task 1-bit binary masks (same shape as the model but 32× smaller in storage) and a single scalar rescaler per task. At inference, the task-specific weights are reconstructed by applying the mask and rescaler to the unified task vector and adding the result to the pretrained weights, requiring no additional data or training at any stage.

## Method

EMR-MERGING decomposes model merging into three tuning-free steps operating on task vectors. First, ELECT constructs a unified task vector by determining a consensus sign per parameter (the sign with greater total magnitude across all task vectors) and then selecting the maximum absolute value among parameters matching that sign, thereby preserving the strongest shared signal while minimizing interference. Second, MASK generates a 1-bit binary mask per task that zeros out elements where the unified vector's sign disagrees with the task-specific vector's sign, aligning direction. Third, RESCALE computes a single scalar per task as the ratio of the task vector's total absolute magnitude to the masked unified vector's total absolute magnitude, aligning amplitude. Theoretical analysis proves that each step monotonically reduces the average L2 distance between the reconstructed task-specific weights and the original finetuned weights, and the masks and rescalers are shown to be applicable as post-processing to other task-vector-based merging methods (Task Arithmetic, Ties-Merging, AdaMerging++) as well.

## Results

On the primary benchmark of merging 8 ViT-B/32 models across vision tasks (SUN397, Cars, RESISC45, EuroSAT, SVHN, GTSRB, MNIST, DTD), EMR-MERGING achieves 88.7% average accuracy, approaching individual models (90.5%) and traditional MTL (88.9%), while the strongest baseline AdaMerging++ reaches only 81.1%. On the more challenging 30-task ViT-B/16 benchmark, EMR-MERGING attains 89.54% average accuracy versus 93.02% for individual models, whereas the best existing method (RegMean) drops to 60.25%. For language models, EMR-MERGING reduces the performance gap from individual models from 12% to 1.6% on GPT-2 (80.4% vs. 82.0% avg.) and outperforms all baselines on every GLUE task for RoBERTa.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EMR-MERGING outperforms all six baselines across every experimental setting. Weight Averaging [80] and Fisher Merging [46] suffer the most severe degradation (65.8% and 68.3% avg. on ViT-B/32 vs. 88.7% for EMR-MERGING), with Fisher Merging additionally requiring pre-computed Fisher information matrices. RegMean [33] (71.8%) and Task Arithmetic [30] (70.1%) also fall far short, with Task Arithmetic requiring manual tuning of the merging coefficient λ on validation data. Ties-Merging [84] (73.6%) improves over simple averaging but still lags EMR-MERGING by 15.1 points and also depends on hyper-parameter tuning. AdaMerging++ [85] (81.1%) is the closest competitor, yet it requires additional training to learn merging coefficients, whereas EMR-MERGING is entirely tuning-free. Notably, EMR-MERGING's masks and rescalers can be applied as a post-processing step to any task-vector-based method, boosting Task Arithmetic by 6.8%, Ties-Merging by 5.0%, and AdaMerging++ by 6.6% (Table 9), confirming their generality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant model-merging paradigm of producing a single unified weight vector (Eq. 1) and instead proposes a two-component output: a shared unified task vector plus lightweight per-task modulators (masks and rescalers). The Electing procedure inherits Ties-Merging's sign-election mechanism for resolving conflicts, while the rescaler draws on DARE's observation that amplitude correction after sparsification improves fidelity. However, the key conceptual shift is that the merged artifact is no longer a single model but a shared backbone with 1-bit masks and scalar rescalers per task, fundamentally changing what 'merging' produces. The method is positioned as a tuning-free alternative to Fisher-Merging, RegMean, Task Arithmetic, Ties-Merging, and AdaMerging, all of which require validation data, hyperparameter search, or additional training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is substantial: EMR-MERGING removes the data-availability and training-cost barriers that limit existing merging methods, making multi-task model consolidation feasible in privacy-sensitive or data-scarce settings. The demonstrated scaling to 30 models (where RegMean degrades ~25% while EMR-MERGING degrades ~3.5%) suggests the approach is viable for consolidating large model zoos. Key limitations include dependence on the pretrain-finetune paradigm (inapplicable to from-scratch models), slight additional storage for modulators, and the fact that the unified vector is still a single shared component that may not capture highly divergent task specializations. Open questions include generalization to architecturally heterogeneous models, integration with quantization, and whether the mask/rescaler post-processing can be further compressed or learned.
