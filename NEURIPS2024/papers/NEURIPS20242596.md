## Problem

Existing device placement methods for neural network computation graphs either follow a grouper-placer or encoder-placer architecture, fail to exploit the directed acyclic structure of computation graphs, and rely on large fine-grained graphs that slow convergence. No prior framework jointly learns node representations, graph partitions, and device assignments in an end-to-end fashion while operating on smaller, coarsened graphs.

## Input / Output

The method takes a computation graph (DAG) produced by the OpenVINO toolkit—already coarsened relative to TensorFlow/PyTorch graphs—and a list of available heterogeneous devices (e.g., CPU, iGPU, dGPU). Each node is initialized with a feature vector combining one-hot operation type, in/out-degree, fractal dimension, sinusoidal positional encoding of topological order, and padded output-shape tensor. The output is a per-node device assignment that minimizes end-to-end inference latency, measured on real hardware.

## Method

HSDAG bridges grouper-placer and encoder-placer paradigms by jointly learning node embeddings (via a GCN) and graph partitioning (via a Graph Parsing Network) in an end-to-end pipeline. The GPN computes an edge score matrix from node embeddings and applies a graph parsing algorithm to partition the graph into an unspecified, learnable number of groups, producing a coarsened graph and a node assignment matrix. A stochastic MLP policy then assigns each coarsened node to a device, and the full pipeline is trained with REINFORCE using the reciprocal of measured inference latency as reward. The framework further incorporates multifractal (fractal dimension) features, positional encodings, and node-specific output-shape features to capture multi-scale structural properties of the DAG, and iteratively refines embeddings by summing coarsened-node representations back into the original graph across RL episodes.

## Results

HSDAG achieves the best inference time across all three benchmarks: 0.0105 s on Inception-V3 (17.9% speedup over CPU-only), 0.00766 s on ResNet-50 (52.1% speedup), and 0.00267 s on BERT-base (58.2% speedup). The framework also has the lowest training runtime among compared methods (e.g., 2454 s vs. 2808 s for Placeto and 3706 s for the RNN-based approach on Inception-V3). Ablation studies confirm that removing output-shape or node-ID features halves the Inception-V3 speedup (from 17.9% to 8.59%), while removing graph structural features (fractal, in/out-degree) reduces it to 14.8%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Placeto [Addanki et al., NeurIPS 2019], HSDAG wins on all three benchmarks: 0.0105 s vs. 0.0116 s on Inception-V3, 0.00766 s vs. 0.00932 s on ResNet, and a dramatic 0.00267 s vs. 0.00651 s on BERT (where Placeto actually performs worse than CPU-only at −2.04% speedup). HSDAG also trains faster than Placeto (2454 s vs. 2808 s on Inception-V3; 2765 s vs. 4512 s on BERT). Against the RNN-based approach [Mirhoseini et al., ICML 2017], HSDAG achieves lower inference time on Inception-V3 (0.0105 s vs. 0.0128 s) and ResNet (0.00766 s vs. 0.00875 s), while the RNN-based method fails entirely on BERT with an out-of-memory error. HSDAG also outperforms the GPU-only baseline on Inception-V3 (17.9% vs. 6.25% speedup) and matches or exceeds it on ResNet and BERT, demonstrating that learned heterogeneous placement is strictly better than uniform GPU assignment.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges the two dominant device-placement architectures—grouper-placer (e.g., Mirhoseini et al. 2018) and encoder-placer (e.g., Placeto, Addanki et al. 2019)—by jointly learning node embeddings and graph partitions in an end-to-end fashion. It adopts the Graph Parsing Network (Song et al., ICLR 2024) as the mechanism for differentiable, learnable graph coarsening with an unspecified number of groups, replacing the fixed cluster counts and non-trainable partitioning heuristics used in prior grouper-placer methods. The co-location heuristic for initial graph condensing is taken directly from Mirhoseini et al. (ICML 2017), and the RL policy is trained with REINFORCE, consistent with the broader device-placement RL literature. A distinctive departure is the incorporation of multifractal (fractal dimension) features, positional encodings, and output-shape tensors into node representations, which prior device-placement frameworks did not exploit.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As foundation models grow in size and are deployed on increasingly heterogeneous hardware (CPU, GPU, NPU), automated device placement becomes a practical bottleneck; HSDAG's use of smaller OpenVINO-coarsened graphs and joint partition learning offers a more scalable and flexible pipeline than node-by-node or fixed-group approaches. However, the evaluation is limited to three models on a single Intel hardware configuration, the iGPU is excluded, and baselines were reimplemented from papers rather than using official code, which weakens the comparative evidence. Open questions include whether the framework generalizes to larger graphs (beyond ~1000 nodes), whether learned reward models can replace the costly real-hardware latency measurements, and how the approach scales to more than two device types.
