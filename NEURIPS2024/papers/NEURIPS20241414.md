## Problem

Existing video atmospheric turbulence mitigation (ATM) methods, whether supervised or self-supervised, effectively remove spatial distortions (blur, warping) but fail to eliminate the high-frequency temporal variations induced by turbulence, producing flickering and temporally incoherent restored videos. This gap persists because the stochastic, spatio-temporally varying nature of turbulence is not explicitly regularized in the temporal dimension of current restoration pipelines.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241414/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

ConVRT operates on short video clips (25 frames at 540×540) and is trained per-clip via test-time optimization rather than on a fixed dataset. It accepts either the raw turbulence-distorted video or, more commonly, a partially restored video produced by a base ATM method (DATUM, TMT, TurbNet, or VRT), and additionally queries a pre-trained MiDaS depth estimator for pixel-wise disparity used in the temporal consistency loss. The output is a temporally consistent restored video at the same resolution, with the canonical spatial feature map serving as a key-frame-like reference from which all other frames are derived via learned deformation offsets.

## Method

ConVRT represents a video with two decoupled implicit neural fields: a Temporal Deformation Field that combines a spatial feature map (H×W×Q₁) and a temporally downsampled feature map (T_res×Q₁, with T_res=5 ≪ T) via Hadamard product, then passes the result through a compact MLP to predict per-pixel (Δx, Δy) warping offsets; and a Spatial Content Field that queries a canonical spatial feature map (H×W×Q₂) at the warped coordinates and decodes RGB through a second MLP. The critical regularization is the reduced temporal resolution T_res, which forces neighboring frames to share temporal features, acting as a low-pass filter that suppresses turbulence-induced high-frequency temporal variations while preserving spatial detail. Training is self-supervised (no ground truth), minimizing a similarity loss (MSE + SSIM + LPIPS) against the base method's output plus a disparity-weighted L1 warp loss that penalizes large deformations in far (high-disparity) regions. The overall pipeline is hybrid: a supervised pre-trained ATM model provides the initial restoration, and ConVRT is then optimized per-clip to enforce temporal consistency on top.

## Results

On the HeatChamber dataset (the only one with ground truth for PSNR), ConVRT consistently reduces the average warp error E_warp by 4.4–24.1 points across base methods (e.g., TMT: 24.21→19.78; DATUM: 22.43→17.77; TurbNet: 41.48→17.36) while marginally improving per-frame PSNR (+0.15 to +0.93 dB) and SSIM (+0.01 to +0.05). On OTIS, the total-variation temporal metrics drop dramatically: Slice_tv decreases by ~87–98% (e.g., TMT 1365.77→368.40) and Flow_tv by ~87–93% (e.g., TMT 7334.87→963.53), confirming strong temporal smoothing. ConVRT also improves temporal consistency on BVI-CLEAR and TSR-WGAN, with Slice_tv reductions of 5–68% and Flow_tv reductions of 72–93% respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ConVRT is designed as a plug-and-play temporal consistency enhancer that post-processes outputs from any base ATM method. Against TMT (Zhang et al., TCI 2024), ConVRT reduces E_warp by 4.43 on HeatChamber and Slice_tv by 978 on OTIS, while slightly improving PSNR (+0.15 dB). Against DATUM (Zhang et al., CVPR 2024), the gains are similar: E_warp drops by 4.66 and Slice_tv by 873, with PSNR improving by +0.19 dB. Against the image-based TurbNet (Mao et al., ECCV 2022), ConVRT yields the largest temporal consistency gains (E_warp reduced by 24.12, Slice_tv by 2486) and the largest per-frame quality improvement (+0.93 dB PSNR), suggesting that image-level methods benefit most from temporal regularization. In qualitative comparisons on moving objects (Figure 9), both Li et al. (ICCV 2021) and Mao et al. (TCI 2020) fail to preserve motion, replacing moving parts with averaged background, whereas ConVRT maintains object integrity across frames. The one exception is TSR-WGAN Slice_tv, where TMT+ConVRT (135.70) is slightly worse than TMT alone (129.22), indicating that in some dynamic scenes the temporal regularization can marginally over-smooth.

## Relation to Prior Work

ConVRT repurposes the tensor-decomposition paradigm from implicit neural representations (TensorF, K-Planes) to the video atmospheric turbulence mitigation setting, where no prior work had explicitly decoupled spatial content from temporal deformation in a trainable video representation. It departs from both supervised video ATM methods (DATUM, TMT) that struggle with temporal consistency due to the stochastic nature of turbulence, and self-supervised methods (NeRT, Li et al.) that operate on static scenes and fail on moving objects. The hybrid training strategy—supervised pre-training on synthetic turbulence followed by self-supervised test-time optimization—bridges the gap between these two paradigms, positioning ConVRT as a plug-and-post refinement layer applicable to any existing ATM base method.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Temporal inconsistency in video ATM is a recognized but largely unaddressed failure mode of state-of-the-art methods, and ConVRT provides a clean, physics-motivated solution (constraining temporal representation capacity to exploit low-pass filtering) that consistently improves warp error across multiple base methods and real-world datasets. The plug-and-play design means practitioners can adopt it without retraining their base ATM models. Key open questions include scaling to longer video sequences and more complex motions (the method is currently optimized for ~25-frame clips at 540×540), improving computational efficiency (~10 min per clip), and whether the temporal consistency gains transfer to other spatio-temporal degradation tasks beyond turbulence.
