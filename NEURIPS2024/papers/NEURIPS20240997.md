## Problem

Social media platforms capture diverse adversarial attack samples from multiple attackers with different motives and styles, but existing graph adversarial training relies on single attack methods that cannot simulate this full spectrum. Behavior cloning fails on sequential attack trajectories due to compounding error, and standard IRL struggles to represent graph-structured data in the large action space of social networks while maintaining interpretability. The paper addresses how to reconstruct a generalized, interpretable attack policy from multi-source expert samples to generate additional adversarial data for robustness training.

## Input / Output

The method consumes N expert attack trajectories (state-action sequences) generated by four distinct graph adversarial attack methods (PageRank, GC-RWCS, PR-BCD, AdRumor-RL) on a social network graph, along with a surrogate feature extraction function f that maps state-action pairs to interpretable graph/node features. It outputs a mixture-of-experts learner policy π_L parameterized by K expert reward vectors and a gating network, which can generate new attack trajectories for data augmentation or adversarial training, plus a linear reward function whose coefficients provide feature-level post-hoc explanations of attack behavior. The hierarchical action space decomposes each edge-flip into three sub-decisions (source subgraph, destination subgraph, node pair), and negative samples are drawn via similarity-based sampling with a cosine threshold μ.

## Method

MoE-BiEntIRL augments maximum entropy IRL with a mixture-of-experts policy where K experts (each with its own linear reward vector θ_k) are selected by a gating network α_k(s), and parameters are learned via an EM algorithm that alternates between computing responsibilities γ_jkt and updating expert/gate parameters. To counter the deviation caused by imprecise surrogate feature representation in the large social graph action space, the method introduces precise sample guidance (forcing the learner to replicate expert actions at a set frequency during early episodes with maximum reward) and a bidirectional update mechanism that performs inverse gradient updates on reward parameters during guidance phases, synchronizing policy and reward learning. The attack policy is learned via hierarchical RL with LinUCB over three layers (source subgraph → destination subgraph → node pair), and the reconstructed policy generates additional adversarial samples used in a minimax adversarial training loop (min_σ max_ω L_D) to harden the target GCN rumor detector.

## Results

On Weibo with T=5, MoE-BiEntIRL achieves ΔL_A of 4.989 (PR-BCD) and 4.990 (AdRumor-RL), slightly exceeding the expert performance of 4.865 and 4.877 respectively. At T=20 on Weibo, the method reaches 19.876 and 19.936, again surpassing expert baselines (19.521, 19.854). On the more challenging Pheme dataset (T=5), MoE-BiEntIRL achieves 4.965 for AdRumor-RL reconstruction while EntIRL collapses to 0.018, demonstrating the method's robustness in difficult policy recovery scenarios. For defense, adversarial training with MoE-BiEntIRL-generated samples yields the best or second-best accuracy decline (−0.2731% PageRank, −0.2589% GC-RWCS) while maintaining the highest clean accuracy (72.07%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MoE-BiEntIRL is compared against two classical IRL baselines: Apprenticeship Learning (maximum-margin based) and EntIRL (maximum-entropy based). Against Apprenticeship Learning, MoE-BiEntIRL wins decisively across all settings — e.g., on Weibo T=5 for PR-BCD, it achieves 4.989 vs. 1.275, and on Pheme T=5 for AdRumor-RL, 4.965 vs. 3.387. Against EntIRL, the comparison is more nuanced: on Weibo, MoE-BiEntIRL matches or slightly exceeds EntIRL for high-cost attacks (4.989 vs. 4.650 for PR-BCD at T=5; 19.876 vs. 19.030 at T=20), but EntIRL outperforms it for simple low-cost attacks at T=20 (20.000 vs. 19.700 for GC-RWCS), which the authors attribute to Occam's Razor. The most striking gap appears on Pheme, where EntIRL essentially fails (ΔL_A ≈ 0.018 for AdRumor-RL) while MoE-BiEntIRL recovers 4.965, highlighting the advantage of the mixture-of-experts structure in handling multi-source attack trajectories.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Maximum Entropy IRL (EntIRL, Ziebart et al. 2008) by wrapping it in a Mixture-of-Experts framework to handle multi-source expert trajectories from diverse attackers, and builds directly on the hierarchical RL attack framework of AdRumor-RL (Lyu et al. 2023) for its feature design and three-level action decomposition (source subgraph → destination subgraph → node pair). It departs from standard adversarial training pipelines that design new attack methods to generate samples, instead framing the problem as policy reconstruction from already-captured adversarial trajectories, and from Behavior Cloning by adopting IRL to avoid compounding error in sequential graph perturbation. The precise sample guidance and bidirectional update mechanism are novel additions addressing the specific challenge of imprecise surrogate feature representation in large social graph action spaces.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical framing—reconstructing a generalized attack policy from platform-captured adversarial samples rather than engineering new attacks—offers social media operators a more realistic defense pipeline that also yields interpretable, feature-level explanations of attacker behavior. However, the evidence is limited to rumor detection on two datasets (Weibo, Pheme), and the Pheme results are substantially weaker than Weibo, raising questions about generalizability. The acknowledged limitations around noisy expert samples, the feasibility of grouping attacks by cost in real deployments, and distribution shift between expert data, negative samples, and the RL action space remain open challenges that will need addressing before broader adoption.
