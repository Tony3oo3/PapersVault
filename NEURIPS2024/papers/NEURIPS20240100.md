## Problem

Existing instruction-based image editing datasets suffer from three critical limitations: limited instruction diversity (human-annotated sets like MagicBrush are small, while LLM-generated sets like InstructPix2Pix lack variety), implicit biases from relying entirely on text-to-image models for both source and target images, and the absence of region-based editing annotations. These gaps prevent models from robustly interpreting diverse instructions and performing fine-grained, localized edits in real-world scenarios.

## Input / Output

The editing model takes a source RGB image (256×256 for training, 512×512 for generation), a natural-language editing instruction, and an optional binary region mask indicating where the edit should occur, and produces a single edited image. The dataset is structured as tuples ⟨I_s, I_t, T_e, I_m, T_s, T_t⟩ where source and target captions are retained for generation and evaluation purposes. For free-form editing samples, a blank mask is supplied to implicitly indicate the edit may affect the entire image.

## Method

UltraEdit is a ~4.1M-sample dataset produced through a three-stage automatic pipeline. Stage I uses LLMs conditioned on in-context human-written examples to generate diverse editing instructions and target captions from ~1.6M real image-caption pairs (COCO, NoCaps, Localized Narratives, etc.). Stage II generates free-form editing pairs by anchoring generation to real images: the source is produced via SDEdit-style noise perturbation on the anchor's latent, and the target via Prompt-to-Prompt control on the same latent, both using SDXL-Turbo (2–4 diffusion steps) to mitigate T2I model biases. Stage III adds ~108K region-based editing samples by automatically extracting edit regions with Recognize-Anything, GroundingDINO, and SAM, then generating targets through a modified inpainting pipeline that alternates between regular diffusion and masked inpainting (Eq. 1) to avoid boundary artifacts. The downstream editing model is a Stable Diffusion 1.5 U-Net augmented with four extra input channels for the region mask, fine-tuned on the dataset with a mixture of free-form and region-based samples.

## Results

On the MagicBrush benchmark (single-turn), the model trained on ULTRAEDIT with region input achieves L1=0.0575, L2=0.0172, CLIP-I=0.9307, and DINO=0.8982, setting new records among instruction-guided methods. On the challenging multi-turn setting, it further improves to L1=0.0745, CLIP-I=0.9045, DINO=0.8505. On Emu Edit Test, scaling ULTRAEDIT to 3M samples yields CLIPdir=0.1076 and CLIPout=0.2832, surpassing the Emu Edit baseline trained on 10M proprietary samples (CLIPdir=0.1066) while achieving lower L1 (0.0713 vs. 0.0895), indicating better content preservation alongside stronger instruction following.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against InstructPix2Pix (IP2P), ULTRAEDIT-trained models outperform on all four MagicBrush metrics in both single-turn and multi-turn settings, with the largest gap in DINO similarity (0.8982 vs. 0.7437 single-turn). Compared to IP2P fine-tuned on MagicBrush (which overfits to its own test set), ULTRAEDIT wins on L1, L2, and DINO in single-turn (0.0575 vs. 0.0625 L1; 0.8982 vs. 0.8987 DINO is a near-tie) and decisively on all metrics in multi-turn (0.0745 vs. 0.0964 L1; 0.9045 vs. 0.8924 CLIP-I). Against HIVE, the advantage is consistent across all settings. On Emu Edit Test, the 3M-scale ULTRAEDIT model surpasses the Emu Edit baseline (trained on 10M proprietary data) on CLIPdir (0.1076 vs. 0.1066) and L1 (0.0713 vs. 0.0895), while the 1M-scale model already beats Emu Edit on L1 (0.0515 vs. 0.0895). Global description-guided methods (GLIDE, Blended Diffusion) perform poorly on L1/L2 (e.g., GLIDE: L1=3.4973, L2=115.83 single-turn), confirming that instruction-guided editing with ULTRAEDIT is substantially more effective. The paper notes that IP2P w/ MagicBrush's strong single-turn CLIP-I (0.9332) is inflated by benchmark bias toward DALL-E 2–generated ground truths, as evidenced by its poor Emu Edit performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the instruction-based image editing line initiated by InstructPix2Pix and MagicBrush by addressing three specific gaps in their data construction: limited instruction diversity, implicit biases from purely T2I-generated images, and the absence of region-based editing data. It builds on Prompt-to-Prompt for source-to-target image generation, SDEdit-style noise perturbation for anchoring to real images, and Blended Diffusion/GLIDE-style inpainting for region-guided generation, but recombines these into a unified pipeline that uses real image anchors (from COCO, NoCaps, etc.) to counteract T2I model biases. The LLM-driven instruction generation with human-written in-context examples departs from InstructPix2Pix's purely GPT-3-generated instructions, and the automatic region extraction via Recognize-Anything + GroundingDINO + SAM is a novel addition not present in prior editing datasets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As the largest publicly released instruction-based image editing dataset (~4M samples, 750K unique instructions, 9+ editing types), UltraEdit provides a critical resource for the community that was previously limited to ~300K-sample datasets. The finding that real image anchors are necessary for scaling to work (without them, larger data volumes do not improve performance) is a practical insight that challenges the assumption that more T2I-generated data is always better. The demonstration that ~100K region-based samples significantly boost even free-form editing performance opens a path toward more precise, controllable editing. Open questions include whether the pipeline generalizes to higher-resolution or video editing, whether the automatic region annotations scale to more complex multi-object edits, and whether the benchmark-specific improvements (MagicBrush, Emu Edit) transfer to truly novel editing scenarios.
