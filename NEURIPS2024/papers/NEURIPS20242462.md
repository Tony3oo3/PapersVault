## Problem

Differentiable convex optimization layers enable end-to-end learning with implicit decision-making, but existing implicit differentiation methods require solving large linear systems involving the KKT matrix during the backward pass, which is computationally prohibitive for large-scale problems. Moreover, many approaches couple the forward and backward passes, restricting solver choices and preventing the use of efficient first-order methods. The paper addresses this by asking whether the backward pass can be reformulated into a simpler, decoupled problem that admits fast first-order solvers.

## Input / Output

In the forward pass, the layer receives convex optimization problem parameters y ∈ R^p (e.g., expected returns for portfolio optimization) and produces the optimal solution z* ∈ R^d. In the backward pass, it receives the upstream gradient vector ∂L/∂z* from the loss function and outputs parameter gradients ∂L/∂y ∈ R^p. A key structural detail is that the backward pass QP uses the active set identified during the forward pass to convert inequality constraints into equality constraints, reducing the problem dimension and ensuring the KKT matrix of the reformulated QP exactly matches the original differentiation system.

## Method

BPQP reformulates the backward pass—originally a linear system involving the full KKT matrix—into a simplified equality-constrained QP whose KKT conditions are algebraically identical to the original system (Theorem 1). This is achieved by replacing inequality constraints with the active-set equalities discovered in the forward pass, which eliminates the diagonal D(λ*)G coupling term and yields a standard QP structure. The decoupling means the forward and backward passes can use independent solvers; the default is OSQP (ADMM-based first-order method) for both, exploiting sparsity, solution polishing, and active-set identification. A small regularization (δ ≈ 10⁻⁶) is added to the KKT matrix to handle redundant constraints, with a one-step correction to recover the exact solution. The framework generalizes to QP, LP, and SOCP, and the 'General Gradients' extension shows that BPQP preserves the KKT residual norm at each iteration, enabling well-behaved gradients even when the forward solver has not fully converged.

## Results

On the real-world CSI 500 portfolio optimization task (500 variables), BPQP achieves a Sharpe ratio of 1.28 (±0.43) and annualized return of 17.67% (±6.11%), substantially outperforming the Two-Stage baseline (Sharpe 0.65, Ann.Ret. 9.28%) while training 2.75× faster than qpth/OptNet (7.7 vs. 21.2 min/epoch). On simulated large-scale problems, BPQP achieves up to 13.54×, 21.02×, and 1.67× speedup over existing differentiable layers on 100-dimension LP, QP, and SOCP respectively. In backward-pass accuracy (CosSim), BPQP achieves 0.992 (±0.09) on QP, the highest among all compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Two-Stage baseline, BPQP wins decisively on all portfolio decision metrics—Sharpe ratio improves from 0.65 to 1.28, annualized return from 9.28% to 17.67%, and regret drops from 0.0283 to 0.0129—while sacrificing some prediction accuracy (IC drops from 0.033 to 0.026, ICIR from 0.32 to 0.28), confirming that end-to-end decision-focused learning trades prediction quality for superior decisions. Against qpth/OptNet, BPQP matches on IC (both 0.026) but wins on Sharpe (1.28 vs. 1.25), annualized return (17.67% vs. 16.54%), regret (0.0129 vs. 0.0176), and is 2.75× faster in training (7.7 vs. 21.2 min/epoch); it loses on ICIR (0.28 vs. 0.38). Against CVXPY and JAXOpt, BPQP is the only method that can efficiently handle the 500-variable portfolio problem, as both fail at this scale (CVXPY requires slow conic reformulation; JAXOpt is infeasible). Against Alt-Diff, BPQP is faster and more robust, as Alt-Diff struggles to satisfy 500 inequality constraints within reasonable tolerance (588 min/epoch). Against the approximate solver DC3 (appendix), BPQP achieves far better decision quality (Sharpe 1.28 vs. −0.16) despite DC3 being faster in training (0.43 vs. 7.7 min/epoch), underscoring that gradient accuracy is critical for end-to-end decision performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

BPQP builds on the implicit differentiation paradigm for differentiable optimization layers established by OptNet (2017), CVXPY/DiffCP (2019), and JAXOpt (2021), all of which apply the Implicit Function Theorem to KKT conditions. Its key departure is a structural reformulation: by exploiting the active-set information obtained in the forward pass, the backward-pass linear system (Eq. 3) is shown to be equivalent to the KKT conditions of a simpler equality-constrained QP (Theorem 1). This decouples the forward and backward passes, allowing any first-order solver (e.g., OSQP/ADMM) to handle both, in contrast to OptNet's coupled primal-dual interior-point design or CVXPY's expensive conic reformulation. The approach also subsumes Alt-Diff's unrolling-based constraint decoupling by achieving similar separation through the active-set QP reformulation rather than iterative unrolling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 10–21× speedup over existing differentiable optimization layers makes end-to-end learning with convex optimization layers practical at scales (5000×2000) where prior methods fail entirely, opening the door to large-scale decision-focused learning in portfolio optimization, control, and signal processing. The decoupled design means future solver improvements (better ADMM variants, GPU acceleration) automatically transfer to BPQP without architectural changes. However, the framework is validated only on convex problems; the non-convex extension discussed in Section 6 lacks experimental evidence, and the reliance on accurate active-set identification from the forward pass could be a fragility point for ill-conditioned or degenerate problems. Open questions include whether the QP reformulation preserves accuracy under very loose forward-pass tolerances and how the method behaves when the active set changes frequently across training iterations.
