## Problem

No large-scale multimodal dataset exists for autonomous trucking, despite trucks presenting unique perception challenges absent from passenger-car datasets: dynamic trailer occlusions, elevated and differently mounted sensors, terminal environments with metal multipath reflections, and the need for robust long-range highway perception. All existing major AV datasets (KITTI, nuScenes, Waymo, Argoverse 2, ZOD, aiMotive) are recorded from passenger vehicles and do not capture these truck-specific conditions.

## Input / Output

The dataset provides synchronized multimodal sensor data from 4 RGB cameras (1928×1208, 10 Hz, undistorted to 1980×943 JPEG), 6 lidars (2× Hesai Pandar64 at 200 m + 4× Ouster OS0 at 35 m, 10 Hz), 6 Continental ARS 548 4D radars (~2600 points/sample, 20 Hz), 2 IMUs, and 1 RTK-GNSS (0.01 m accuracy), all in nuScenes format with unannotated intermediate sweeps. Annotations are manually labeled 3D bounding boxes (center, size, quaternion) for 27 classes with 15 attributes, unique tracking IDs, and 34 scene tags, reaching a 99.9th-percentile range of 226 m. The detection task evaluates 12 classes up to 150 m using nuScenes Detection Score (NDS) with distance-based mAP and five TP metrics.

## Method

The dataset is built through a four-step calibration pipeline (photogrammetric scan, plane-matching in a dedicated calibration hall, far-target angular correction, and application-level validation) achieving pixel-level camera-lidar alignment, with PTP-based sensor synchronization to <100 μs. Scene selection covers 747 scenes (~20 s each) from 25+ h of drives across 100 km², three seasons, and diverse weather; splits are determined by a 12-objective NSGA-II Pareto optimization balancing class/tag distribution similarity against temporal and spatial divergence. Baseline 3D detection uses PETR (transformer with FCOS3D backbone, 900 queries, Hungarian assignment) for camera, RadarGNN (5-layer MPNN on KNN graphs over fused 4D radar sweeps) for radar, and CenterPoint (SECOND voxel encoder + DCN CenterHead, 7 detection heads) for lidar, all trained supervised. Evaluation extends the standard nuScenes NDS protocol to 150 m range and adds animal and traffic sign as two extra detection classes beyond the original 10.

## Results

On the MAN TruckScenes test split (12 detection classes, 150 m evaluation range), the lidar-based CenterPoint baseline achieves the strongest performance with mAP 0.27 and NDS 0.41, followed by the radar-based RadarGNN (mAP 0.07, NDS 0.11) and the camera-based PETR (mAP 0.02, NDS 0.12). CenterPoint also leads on all error metrics: ATE 0.41 m, ASE 0.35, AOE 0.28 rad, AVE 2.73 m/s, and AAE 0.20. Performance degrades substantially with distance: CenterPoint's mAP drops from 0.48 (0–25 m) to 0.27 (0–150 m), and all three modalities show significant drops in tunnels, winter conditions, and terminal environments.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper establishes three single-modality baselines on the new MAN TruckScenes dataset rather than proposing a new method. CenterPoint (lidar) clearly outperforms both other modalities, achieving mAP 0.27 and NDS 0.41 versus RadarGNN's 0.07/0.11 and PETR's 0.02/0.12. RadarGNN shows the worst velocity estimation (AVE 8.00 m/s) due to using relative radial velocity instead of ego-motion-compensated measurements, and it fails entirely on non-metallic classes (pedestrian, bicycle, traffic cone, animal all at AP 0.00). PETR (camera) struggles most with minority classes (bus, other_vehicle, motorcycle, bicycle, animal all at AP 0.00) and accumulates false positives on drivable areas. All three methods show significant performance degradation in tunnels (CenterPoint drops to mAP 0.12/NDS 0.16), winter conditions (0.12/0.26), and at longer ranges, highlighting the need for more robust long-range perception in autonomous trucking.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the nuScenes data format, notation, and evaluation protocol (NDS metric), extending the multimodal perception dataset paradigm from passenger cars to heavy-duty trucks. It departs from prior work by introducing truck-specific sensor configurations (elevated lidar at 3.2 m, six 4D radar sensors with near-360° coverage, dual IMUs for chassis-cabin relative motion) and by applying NSGA-II multi-objective optimization to dataset split construction—a methodological contribution not seen in prior AV dataset papers. The baselines draw from the established 3D detection literature (PETR for camera, RadarGNN for radar, CenterPoint for lidar), positioning the dataset as a new benchmark rather than a new method.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MAN TruckScenes fills a critical gap: no prior large-scale multimodal dataset existed for autonomous trucking, yet trucks face fundamentally different perception challenges (trailer occlusions, terminal environments, 226 m annotation range, high-velocity highway traffic). The extremely low baseline results (camera mAP 0.02, lidar NDS 0.41) underscore that existing perception methods do not transfer to the trucking domain, opening substantial research space. Limitations include single-vehicle recording in Germany, manual annotation errors, and the absence of map data, while the 4D radar with 360° coverage and the NSGA-II split methodology offer reusable contributions beyond the dataset itself.
