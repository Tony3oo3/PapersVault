## Problem

The quadratic complexity of self-attention makes the pre-filling stage of long-context LLMs prohibitively slow—30 minutes for a 1M-token prompt on a single A100—while existing sparse attention methods either rely on fixed patterns that require retraining or introduce substantial estimation overhead that negates their speedup at long sequence lengths. The paper addresses the gap of efficiently exploiting the dynamic sparsity (up to 96.8%) observed in long-context attention matrices without any model modification or additional fine-tuning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241662/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a long text prompt (up to 1M tokens) and a pre-computed per-head sparse pattern assignment (determined offline in ~15 minutes on a single A100 using one validation sample). During inference, it dynamically constructs sparse indices from the input's Q and K vectors, then outputs generated text tokens with up to 10× reduced pre-filling latency. The approach is entirely training-free and plug-and-play: it requires no changes to the pre-training setup, position embeddings, or model architecture, and operates in bfloat16 on a single A100 (or scales to 8× A100 via tensor/context parallelism).

## Method

MInference identifies three recurring sparse attention patterns in long-context LLMs—A-shape (initial tokens + local windows), Vertical-Slash (dynamic vertical and diagonal lines), and Block-Sparse (spatially clustered blocks)—and assigns the optimal pattern to each attention head via an offline kernel-aware search that matches candidates to a target FLOPs budget and selects by attention-output recall. During inference, dynamic sparse indices are built with minimal overhead: Vertical-Slash heads matmul the last 64 query vectors against all keys to locate vertical/slash line positions, while Block-Sparse heads apply mean pooling on Q and K in 64-token blocks to estimate block-level attention weights. Three custom GPU kernels (built on Triton, FlashAttention, and the PIT dynamic sparse compiler) then execute the sparse attention computation, reducing 95% of attention FLOPs. The A-shape pattern requires no index-building overhead since its mask is static, while the dynamic patterns add only 5–20% overhead for index construction.

## Results

MInference achieves up to 10× pre-filling speedup for 1M-token prompts on a single A100 (reducing latency from 30 min to 3 min), with speedups of 1.8×, 4.1×, 6.8×, and 10× at 100K, 300K, 500K, and 1M tokens respectively. On InfiniteBench, MInference matches or slightly surpasses full attention (e.g., 38.8 vs. 38.2 average on LLaMA-3-8B-262K; 47.0 vs. 46.7 on GLM-4-9B-1M). On RULER, MInference achieves effective context windows of 32K and 64K for LLaMA-3-8B-262K and GLM-4-9B-1M respectively, outperforming full attention beyond 32K. On PG-19 (100K tokens), perplexity is only 0.2 higher than full attention, compared to +0.25 (Yi-9B) and +0.75 (LLaMA-3) for StreamingLLM.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MInference consistently outperforms all four baselines across models and tasks. Against StreamingLLM (Xiao et al., ICLR 2024), MInference achieves 38.8 vs. 23.8 average on LLaMA-3-8B-262K InfiniteBench, with the gap driven primarily by retrieval tasks (Retr.Num: 100.0 vs. 5.1; Retr.KV: 12.8 vs. 0.8) where StreamingLLM's fixed local-window approach fails. Against StreamingLLM w/ dilated (Longformer-style, Beltagy et al.) and w/ strided (Sparse Transformers-style, Child et al.), MInference leads by 14.6 and 25.5 points respectively on LLaMA-3, as both dilated and strided variants collapse on retrieval and multi-choice tasks. Against InfLLM (Xiao et al., NeurIPS 2024), MInference wins on all three tested models (38.8 vs. 34.8 on LLaMA-3, 37.7 vs. 31.9 on Yi-9B, 47.0 vs. 37.3 on GLM-4), with the largest gap on KV retrieval (12.8 vs. 1.2 on LLaMA-3). On RULER, MInference also surpasses InfLLM (87.0 vs. 62.9 average on LLaMA-3) and extends the effective context window from 4K (StreamingLLM) to 32K, matching or exceeding full attention's 16K.

## Relation to Prior Work

MInference builds on FlashAttention's IO-aware kernel design and PIT's permutation-invariant transformation for dynamic sparse computation, but departs fundamentally from prior sparse attention work in its treatment of the pre-filling stage for long-context LLMs. Static sparse patterns (Longformer, BigBird) require pre-training from scratch, while prior dynamic sparse methods (Liu et al. 2022, Ribar et al. 2024) rely on low-rank hidden-state projections that introduce substantial estimation overhead. MInference's key conceptual departure is identifying three spatially-structured patterns (A-shape, Vertical-Slash, Block-Sparse) that are characteristic of long-context attention, assigning them offline per head via a kernel-aware search, and then building dynamic indices online with minimal overhead (5–20%)—making it a training-free, plug-and-play accelerator rather than a new architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 10× pre-filling speedup at 1M tokens (30 min → 3 min on a single A100) with no accuracy loss is a major practical win for deploying long-context LLMs, and the training-free, model-agnostic design means it can be retrofitted onto existing checkpoints immediately. The method's compatibility with KV cache compression (SnapKV) and its portability across hardware (Triton-based kernels) further strengthen its practical value. Limitations include growing relative overhead at shorter contexts (<10K tokens) and performance degradation at higher sparsity rates. Open questions include whether the identified patterns generalize to multi-modal and encoder-decoder architectures (the paper provides preliminary evidence for T5 and LLaVA), and whether the offline pattern assignment remains stable across fine-tuning or continued pre-training.
