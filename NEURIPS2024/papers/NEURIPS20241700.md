## Problem

GCG, the standard method for constructing adversarial suffixes that jailbreak aligned LLMs, requires B=512 forward passes on the target model per iteration, making comprehensive safety studies prohibitively expensive. The paper asks whether a smaller draft model can pre-filter unpromising candidates before the costly target-model evaluation, and how to dynamically calibrate the filtering aggressiveness so that speedup does not degrade attack success rate.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241700/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a batch of B suffix candidates (each a single-token mutation of the current suffix), a small draft model (e.g., GPT-2 at 124M parameters), and a large target model (e.g., Llama2-7b-chat). It outputs the best-performing suffix by evaluating only a dynamically-sized filtered subset with the target model, where the filtered set size is (1−α)·B/R and α is the Spearman rank correlation between draft and target loss rankings on a small probe subset of k candidates (default k=B/16, R=8). The probe set is resampled from the current batch at every iteration, so the agreement score adapts to the local neighborhood of the optimization trajectory.

## Method

Probe sampling replaces the full B forward passes on the target model with a three-step loop: (i) compute losses on all B candidates with the cheap draft model and on a small probe subset with the target model in parallel; (ii) compute the probe agreement score α as the Spearman rank correlation between the two loss rankings, then set the filtered set size to (1−α)·B/R; (iii) evaluate only the top candidates by draft loss using the target model and select the best. The key insight is the locality property of GCG—since all candidates in a batch differ by only one token from the current suffix, draft-target agreement on a small probe subset reliably predicts agreement on the full batch, enabling adaptive filtering that is aggressive when models agree and conservative when they diverge. This adaptive mechanism outperforms any fixed filtered set size, and the stochasticity introduced by partial filtering actually improves ASR over vanilla GCG's deterministic greedy selection (e.g., 81.0 vs. 69.0 on Llama2-7b-chat). The same probe-agreement filtering generalizes to accelerate AutoPrompt (1.8×), APE (1.8–3.0×), and AutoDAN (2.3–2.5×) with minimal performance loss.

## Results

On AdvBench with Llama2-7b-Chat, Probe sampling alone achieves a 4.1× speedup (2.2s vs 8.9s per iteration) on Harmful Strings while improving ASR from 57.0 to 69.0, and a 3.5× speedup (2.6s vs 9.2s) on Harmful Behaviors with ASR improving from 69.0 to 81.0. Combined with simulated annealing, the speedup reaches 6.3× (Strings) and 5.6× (Behaviors) with ASR of 64.0 and 74.0 respectively. On Vicuna-7b-v1.3, Probe sampling achieves 2.4×/2.1× speedup alone and 3.6×/3.2× with annealing, while maintaining or improving ASR (e.g., 91.0 vs 88.0 on Strings). The method also accelerates AutoPrompt by 1.8×, APE by 1.8×–3.0×, and AutoDAN by 2.3×–2.5× with minimal performance degradation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GCG (Zou et al., 2023), the primary baseline, Probe sampling simultaneously achieves substantial speedup (3.5×–6.3×) and improved ASR on Llama2-7b-Chat (e.g., 81.0 vs 69.0 on Behaviors), which the authors attribute to beneficial randomness introduced by the draft-model filtering. Against AutoDAN (Liu et al., 2024), Probe sampling accelerates AutoDAN-GA by 2.3× (424.2s → 182.7s) and AutoDAN-HGA by 2.5× (237.9s → 95.3s) while maintaining or slightly improving ASR (55.9 vs 56.2; 62.1 vs 60.8). Against AutoPrompt (Shin et al., 2020), the method yields a 1.8× speedup on both SST-2 (228.4s → 127.2s) and SICK-E (42.7s → 23.6s) with only a 0.4–0.8 point accuracy drop. Against APE (Zhou et al., 2022), Probe sampling delivers 2.3× speedup on GSM8K, 1.8× on MMLU, and 3.0× on BBH, while accuracy is maintained or slightly improved (e.g., 22.4 vs 21.3 on GSM8K). In all four comparisons, Probe sampling wins on speed while matching or exceeding the baseline's task performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the draft-model intuition from speculative sampling (Chen et al., 2023; Leviathan et al., 2023) to the discrete token optimization setting of GCG (Zou et al., 2023), where the autoregressive assumption that makes speculative decoding work is broken because each suffix token replacement is independent. The key conceptual departure is the introduction of a probe-based agreement score (Spearman rank correlation on a small subset of candidates) that dynamically determines how much to trust the draft model, rather than using a fixed acceptance/rejection rule. The method is further generalized beyond GCG to other prompt optimization and jailbreak methods (AutoPrompt, APE, AutoDAN), positioning it as a general acceleration primitive for candidate-evaluation loops in discrete optimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

A 5.6× speedup for GCG is practically significant for LLM safety research, enabling more comprehensive exploration of adversarial vulnerabilities that was previously computationally prohibitive. The counterintuitive finding that probe sampling improves ASR over vanilla GCG (attributed to beneficial randomness) opens questions about whether controlled noise injection is a general principle for discrete optimization. Key limitations include reliance on open-source models (excluding proprietary LLMs) and degraded performance on large test sets. Open directions include extending the probe-agreement framework to multimodal settings and using small draft models at web scale to detect natural adversarial prompts.
