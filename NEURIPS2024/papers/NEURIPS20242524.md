## Problem

Standard neural networks output a single probability distribution and cannot express epistemic uncertainty, while Deep Ensembles—though widely used—lack theoretical justification and produce low-quality EU estimates. Existing credal-set approaches (e.g., imprecise BNNs) are computationally intractable, leaving a gap for a practical, principled method that represents predictions as convex sets of distributions to faithfully capture uncertainty about future data distributional shift.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242524/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes standard RGB images (224×224×3 for ResNet50/VGG16/ViT-Base experiments) and produces, per class, a lower and upper probability bound that jointly define a credal set (a convex set of valid probability vectors). From this credal set, the system derives a class label via maximin or maximax criteria, an epistemic uncertainty score (upper entropy minus lower entropy of the credal set), and a total uncertainty score (upper entropy). The ensemble aggregates M individual credal predictions by averaging their reachable probability intervals, which is provably guaranteed to yield a non-empty credal set.

## Method

CreNet modifies only the final classification layer of any backbone, doubling output nodes from C to 2C: one node per class for the interval midpoint (via an arbitrary activation) and one for the half-length (via Softplus to ensure non-negativity). An Interval SoftMax activation then maps these deterministic intervals to valid probability intervals that satisfy the credal-set convexity constraints (each lower ≤ upper, and the sum of lowers ≤ 1 ≤ sum of uppers). Training uses a composite loss: vanilla cross-entropy on the upper probability vector (encouraging optimistic, in-distribution predictions) plus a DRO-inspired component on the lower probability vector that selects the top-δ fraction of batch samples with highest lower-probability cross-entropy, simulating worst-case distributional shift. Credal Deep Ensembles (CreDEs) aggregate M independently trained CreNets by averaging their per-class reachable probability intervals, and EU is quantified as the difference between upper and lower Shannon entropies over the credal set, solved via constrained optimization (with a PIDR dimensionality-reduction trick for large class counts like ImageNet's 1000 classes).

## Results

On CIFAR-10 vs SVHN OOD detection, CreDEs-5 achieves 96.55% AUROC and 98.17% AUPRC, substantially outperforming DEs-5 (89.58% AUROC, 92.29% AUPRC). On the harder ImageNet vs ImageNet-O benchmark, CreDEs-5 reaches 67.82% AUROC vs 65.03% for DEs-5. On in-distribution samples, CreDEs-5 also achieves higher test accuracy (93.75% vs 93.32% on CIFAR-10; 79.54% vs 75.80% on CIFAR-100) and lower ECE (0.0092 vs 0.0131 on CIFAR-10) compared to DEs-5.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary baseline DEs-5 (Lakshminarayanan et al., 2017), CreDEs-5 wins on every reported metric: higher test accuracy (e.g., 93.75% vs 93.32% on CIFAR-10), lower ECE (0.0092 vs 0.0131), and substantially better OOD detection AUROC (96.55% vs 89.58% on CIFAR-10 vs SVHN; 67.82% vs 65.03% on ImageNet vs ImageNet-O). Against MCDropout (Gal & Ghahramani, 2016), CreDEs-5 achieves 79.14% AUROC vs 74.19% (0.1 dropout) and 61.66% (0.4 dropout) on CIFAR-10 vs SVHN EU-based OOD detection. Against BNN-R (Molchanov et al., 2017) and BNN-F (Wen et al., 2018), CreDEs-5 leads by 8.84 and 8.99 AUROC points respectively on the same benchmark. The paper also shows that even DEs trained with the same DRO loss (DEs*-5) are outperformed by CreDEs-5 on both accuracy (93.75% vs 91.53% on CIFAR-10) and OOD detection, confirming that the credal output structure—not just the DRO training signal—is responsible for the gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Deep Ensembles framework (Lakshminarayanan et al., 2017) by replacing each ensemble member's single probability output with a credal set (probability intervals per class), drawing on the Interval SoftMax activation from the authors' prior CreINNs work. The training strategy is the key conceptual departure: a DRO-inspired adversarial reweighting loss (borrowing the batch-level heuristic from Huang et al., 2022) is applied to the lower probability bound, giving the interval width a principled interpretation as epistemic uncertainty about train-test distributional divergence. This positions CreDEs between the computational simplicity of standard ensembles and the theoretical grounding of credal inference, while avoiding the OOD-data dependency of Dirichlet/evidential deep learning methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CreDEs offer a practical, low-overhead path to better epistemic uncertainty quantification (only doubling output nodes) with consistent gains in OOD detection across ResNet50, VGG16, and ViT-Base backbones on multiple benchmarks. The DRO-credal connection provides a more interpretable rationale for interval width than ad-hoc ensemble variance, and the PIDR algorithm makes credal entropy computation tractable for large class spaces. Open questions include formal statistical coverage guarantees (sketched via conformal learning in the appendix), extension to regression, and whether the method remains advantageous under strict memory constraints where even standard ensembles are impractical.
