## Problem

State-of-the-art manifold clustering methods, particularly deep-learning-based approaches achieving over 89% accuracy on CIFAR-10, lack any proof of theoretical correctness. This paper asks whether one can provide geometric conditions—based on manifold curvature, local sampling density, and inter-manifold separation—that guarantee a manifold-preserving representation is recovered from a convex optimization model, bridging the gap between empirical performance and theoretical understanding.

## Input / Output

The method takes high-dimensional data samples assumed to lie on a union of smooth nonlinear manifolds; in experiments, 32×32 CIFAR images are first mapped to feature vectors via a frozen CLIP ViT-L/14 encoder. For each query point y, the model produces a sparse coefficient vector c whose non-zero entries (when conditions are met) correspond only to points on the same manifold as y. These per-point coefficient vectors are assembled into a representation matrix, post-processed into a symmetric affinity matrix, and fed to spectral clustering to yield final cluster assignments.

## Method

The core model (WMC) is a convex program that finds a weighted ℓ₁-sparse self-expressive representation of y among all data points, where the weight matrix W has diagonal entries increasing with Euclidean distance to y, and the affine constraint 1ᵀc=1 is relaxed into a soft penalty term (1−1ᵀc)² with parameter η, equivalent to homogenizing the data. The key theoretical contribution is a curvature-based geometric condition (Lemma 3): if the manifold's reach exceeds the local sampling radius ζ, the curvature κ at y is sufficiently small relative to the inradius r^W of a convex body built from the d₁+1 nearest same-manifold neighbors, and the inter-manifold separation (captured by γ^W) is positive, then for λ in a derived interval (λˡ, λᵘ), every optimal solution is manifold-preserving and nonzero. Practically, each instance of (WMC) is solved via an active-set method, the resulting coefficient matrix is symmetrized and optionally normalized/thresholded to form an affinity matrix, and spectral clustering with k-means produces the final partition.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, L-WMC achieves 96.07% clustering accuracy (ACC) and 90.54% Normalized Mutual Information (NMI), while E-WMC achieves 94.34% ACC and 88.79% NMI. On CIFAR-20, both variants reach ~64% ACC, and on CIFAR-100 they reach ~69–70% ACC. These results are consistently better than subspace clustering baselines (EnSC, SSC, SMCE) but marginally below the state-of-the-art deep methods CPP (97.4% ACC on CIFAR-10) and TEMI (96.9% ACC on CIFAR-10), with the gap widening on CIFAR-100 (69.2% vs. 74.0% for CPP).

## Baselines & Comparisons

L-WMC and E-WMC consistently outperform all three non-fine-tuning baselines (SMCE, EnSC, SSC) on ACC across all three CIFAR datasets, with the largest margin on CIFAR-10 (96.07% vs. 86.86% for SMCE, 85.8% for EnSC, 85.4% for SSC). Against the fine-tuning deep methods, L-WMC trails CPP by 1.33% ACC on CIFAR-10 (96.07 vs. 97.4) and by 4.8% on CIFAR-100 (69.2 vs. 74.0), while E-WMC is nearly tied with CPP on CIFAR-20 ACC (64.1 vs. 64.2). On NMI, SMCE actually edges out both WMC variants on CIFAR-10 (90.66 vs. 90.54) and CIFAR-100 (77.17 vs. 76.49/76.63), suggesting the weighted ℓ₁ formulation captures some inter-sample structure that the unweighted NMI metric rewards. The key differentiator is that L-WMC/E-WMC are the first methods to provide geometric conditions (Lemma 1, Lemma 3) guaranteeing manifold-preserving solutions, whereas CPP and TEMI have no such theoretical correctness guarantee.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the self-expressiveness framework from subspace clustering to nonlinear manifold clustering by building directly on SMCE (Elhamifar & Vidal, NeurIPS 2011), replacing its hard affine constraint with a soft penalty to obtain a convex problem amenable to geometric analysis. The theoretical machinery draws on convex duality (weighted ℓ₁ norms, polar bodies, inradius) and differential geometry (manifold curvature, reach) to derive conditions under which solutions are manifold-preserving. In contrast to the large body of deep-learning-based manifold clustering methods (CPP, TEMI, etc.) that achieve high accuracy on CIFAR but lack correctness proofs, this work provides the first theoretical guarantees for a self-expressive nonlinear manifold clustering model, while remaining competitive empirically.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes the first geometric correctness conditions for nonlinear manifold clustering via self-expressiveness, linking solution quality to curvature, local sampling density, and inter-manifold separation—quantities that are interpretable but not directly computable from data without knowing the underlying manifold structure. A key open question the authors highlight is the absence of a principled strategy for choosing the hyperparameter λ to satisfy the theoretical conditions, which limits practical applicability of the guarantees. The empirical gap (~1–2% ACC) relative to deep SOTA methods like CPP suggests that theoretical grounding comes at a modest performance cost, and whether this gap can be closed while preserving guarantees remains an important direction.
