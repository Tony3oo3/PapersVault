## Problem

Despite the proliferation of synthetic time series generation methods (GANs, VAEs, simulation-based approaches), the field lacks a unified software framework and a consistent set of evaluation metrics, hindering both method comparison and practical adoption. This gap limits the applicability of synthetic time series to real-world scenarios such as data sharing under privacy constraints and data augmentation for training foundation models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20244097/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

TSGM accepts historical time series in three forms—raw, labeled (one label per series), or temporally labeled (labels indexed per timestep)—along with optional scalar or temporal conditioning variables, and for simulation-based generation, user-defined simulators with prior distributions over parameters. The framework outputs synthetic time series, a multi-dimensional quality assessment spanning seven metric categories (similarity/distance, predictive consistency, privacy, fairness, downstream effectiveness, diversity, and qualitative visualization), and augmented training sets. All operations run within the Keras ecosystem with multi-backend support (TensorFlow, PyTorch, JAX) and hardware acceleration on CPU, GPU, and TPU.

## Method

TSGM is a unified framework rather than a single model, integrating three generator families under one API: data-driven generators (GANs including TimeGAN, RCGAN, WaveGAN, and conditional/temporally-conditional variants; VAEs including TimeVAE and beta-VAE), simulation-based generators (user-defined parametric models with approximate Bayesian computation via rejection sampling for parameter estimation), and augmentation techniques (noise injection, slicing, warping, DTWBA). A key architectural component is the Architecture Zoo, a repository of tested CNN, RNN, and transformer-based architectures reusable as generators, discriminators, or downstream evaluators. The evaluation module implements seven metric types, including a privacy metric based on membership inference attack precision, a predictive consistency metric measuring rank agreement across model ensembles, and a downstream effectiveness metric comparing model performance with and without synthetic data. The framework supports conditional generation (scalar or temporal conditioning), integrates with Optuna for hyperparameter search, and provides a CLI for non-programmatic use, all built on Keras 3.0 for cross-backend extensibility.

## Results

On the ECG-200 dataset with a 1D-CNN classifier, TSGM's WindowWarp augmentation achieves the best accuracy of 0.91±0.01 and F1 of 0.91±0.01, outperforming the no-augmentation baseline (0.89±0.02) by 2 percentage points. In the generative model comparison (Demo 1), no single method dominates across all scenarios: for data sharing (consistency + privacy), different methods excel on different datasets, while for data augmentation (similarity + downstream effectiveness), the ranking shifts, highlighting the need for scenario-specific evaluation. Hardware benchmarks show GPU training reduces epoch time from ~26 min (CPU) to ~20 s, and TPU achieves ~16.5 s per two epochs after warmup.

## Baselines & Comparisons

In Demo 1, TSGM benchmarks TimeVAE [19], TimeGAN [78], and RCGAN [23] across three datasets (NASA C-MAPSS, sine-const, UCI Energy) for two use-case scenarios. The results (Fig. 4 heatmaps) show no universal winner: for data sharing (predictive consistency + privacy), method rankings vary by dataset, while for data augmentation (similarity + downstream effectiveness), different generators excel, underscoring the framework's motivation for unified multi-metric evaluation. In Demo 2, WindowWarp [61] achieves the highest accuracy (0.91) and F1 (0.91) on ECG-200, beating the baseline (0.89) and outperforming SliceAndShuffle [72] (0.89), MagnitudeWarp [38] (0.90), and DTWBA [24] (0.90). The framework-level comparison (Table 1) positions TSGM as the only tool supporting all generator types (NN, probabilistic, simulation-based), all evaluator categories (distance, predictive consistency, privacy, fairness, downstream effectiveness, diversity, qualitative), temporally conditioned generation, and classic augmentations simultaneously, whereas TimeSynth [48] covers only simulation-based generation, DeepEcho [82] and SynthCity [59] lack augmentation and temporal conditioning, and TSSurrogates [28] omits deep learning generators and most metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

TSGM unifies the fragmented landscape of synthetic time series generation by integrating data-driven methods (TimeGAN, RCGAN, WaveGAN, TimeVAE, beta-VAE) and simulation-based approaches (Lotka-Volterra, stochastic periodic simulators) under a single Keras-based framework. It departs from prior tools like TimeSynth, DeepEcho, and SynthCity by providing a substantially broader evaluation suite (similarity, predictive consistency, privacy, fairness, diversity, downstream effectiveness) and by bridging the previously separate data-driven and simulation-based communities. The framework also extends the GAN/VAE lineage with conditional and temporally conditional variants, and incorporates augmentation techniques (DTWBA, magnitude warping, window warping) that prior time series generation tools largely omitted.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TSGM addresses a practical gap: synthetic time series methods have not been as widely adopted as image/text generative models partly due to the absence of a unified, extensible platform with comprehensive quality assessment. The finding that no single generator dominates across both data-sharing and data-augmentation scenarios underscores the importance of scenario-specific evaluation, a point often overlooked in prior work. Open questions remain around irregularly sampled time series, generation of longer sequences, and the integration of state-of-the-art diffusion and transformer-based generators, which the authors acknowledge as future work. The framework's production validation and multi-backend support (TensorFlow, PyTorch, JAX) position it as a potential standard tool for the community.
