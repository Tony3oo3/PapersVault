## Problem

Existing NAS search spaces encode strong architectural priors (e.g., DARTS assumes convolutional cells), making it impossible to discover fundamentally different paradigms like transformers or MLP-Mixers. The paper addresses the need for a single, highly expressive search space that can represent diverse state-of-the-art architectures (ResNets, ViTs, MLP-Mixers) while still allowing human-designed architectures to be used as powerful priors to initialise search.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240061/cropped_page1_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a task dataset with train/val/test splits across diverse modalities (RGB images, text-as-images, audio spectrograms, chess board states, 1D signals) and optionally a set of seed architectures (ResNet18, WRN16-4, ViT, MLP-Mixer) to initialise the evolutionary population. It produces a neural network architecture encoded as a CFG derivation tree, which is instantiated into a trainable model operating in either Im mode (3D tensors of shape C×H×W) or Col mode (2D tensors of shape S×D). The search space is infinite in depth and width but guaranteed to terminate via a sub-critical branching rate constraint.

## Method

einspace defines its search space as a parameterised probabilistic context-free grammar (PCFG) over four groups of fundamental operations: Branching (one-to-many tensor cloning/splitting), Aggregation (many-to-one merging), Routing (one-to-one shape/order changes like im2col/col2im/permute), and Computation (one-to-one parameterised transforms like linear, norm, activation). Four module types (Sequential, Branching, Routing, Computation) are composed recursively via the grammar, with parameters tracking tensor shape, feature mode, and branching factor to guarantee validity. Convolutions are decomposed into im2col→linear→col2im within routing modules, while skip connections and multi-head attention emerge from branching+aggregation modules, enabling a single grammar to represent ConvNets, transformers, and MLP-only architectures. Probabilities on production rules (with p(M→C|M)=0.32) balance architecture complexity and ensure the CFG is consistent. Search uses regularised evolution where mutation samples a random node in the derivation tree, regenerates its subtree via the grammar, and validates the result for shape compatibility and resource constraints.

## Results

On the 8 Unseen NAS datasets, RE(RN18) (evolutionary search initialised with ResNet18) achieves an average accuracy of 78.17% (average rank 3.88), improving over the RN18 baseline (72.55%, rank 7.38) on every single task. The most dramatic gain is on the Language dataset, where RE(RN18) reaches 96.84% versus RN18's 92.16% (+5.76%). RE(Mix), seeded with a diverse mix of ResNet18, WRN16-4, ViT, and MLP-Mixer, achieves 77.56% average accuracy. On NASBench360, RE(WRN) matches the GAEA-on-DARTS average rank (2.35) and sets a new state-of-the-art on NinaPro (6.37 error vs. Expert's 8.73).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RE(RN18) beats the ResNet18 baseline on all 8 Unseen NAS tasks, with the largest margin on Language (+5.76%) and Gutenberg (+10.70%). Against the strongest NAS baselines (PC-DARTS at 80.30% and Bonsai-Net at 80.41% average), RE(RN18) (78.17%) trails slightly on average but wins on 4 of 8 individual datasets; the gap is largest on CIFARTile (60.65% vs. 92.28%), where the DARTS cell-based prior on convolutions is highly effective. Random search in einspace (59.76% avg) dramatically underperforms random search in the DARTS space (80.41%), confirming that expressive spaces require intelligent search strategies. On NASBench360, RE(WRN) matches GAEA-on-DARTS (both rank 2.35) and beats the task-specific Expert architecture on NinaPro (6.37 vs. 8.73 error), setting a new SOTA. Compared to the prior CFG-based space hNASBench201 under the same evolutionary search, einspace achieves better average performance, validating the added expressiveness. RE(Mix) seeded with diverse SOTA architectures (ResNet, WRN, ViT, MLP-Mixer) achieves 77.56% average, competitive with RE(RN18) while demonstrating the space's ability to unify heterogeneous architecture families.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the CFG-based search space construction pioneered by Schrodi et al. [47] (hNASBench201) in three key ways: it makes the grammar probabilistic to control architecture complexity, parameterises production rules to guarantee valid tensor shape propagation, and removes fixed hierarchical depth limits to allow unbounded derivations. It also departs from the ex nihilo philosophy of AutoML-Zero [41] by decomposing high-level operations (convolutions, attention) into fundamental routing, branching, aggregation, and computation primitives, while retaining the ability to seed search with existing SOTA architectures as priors. Unlike prior hybrid spaces (e.g., BossNAS [26]) that offer only direct choices between convolution and attention, einspace unifies ConvNets, Transformers, and MLP-only architectures in a single grammar.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that in a sufficiently expressive search space, the choice of search strategy matters (random search underperforms, unlike in narrow DARTS-style spaces) and that seeding evolutionary search with human-designed SOTA architectures yields consistent large improvements across diverse modalities. Key limitations include the absence of recurrent/state-space computation, ambiguity from multiple derivation trees for the same architecture, and the computational cost of the evolutionary search (2–4 days per dataset). Open questions include learning the PCFG probabilities directly, developing more efficient search strategies (crossover, hierarchical Bayesian optimisation), and resolving equivalence classes of derivation trees to reduce effective search space size.
