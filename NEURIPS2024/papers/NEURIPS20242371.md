## Problem

When the effective rank r_eff of the covariance matrix is large (up to d), standard streaming PCA via Oja's algorithm incurs an O(r_eff/n) sin² error that is inconsistent. For sparse PCA where v₁ is s-sparse, no known single-pass algorithm achieves the minimax O(s log(d)/n) error in O(d) space and O(nd) time without either requiring a strong initialization (|u₀ᵀv₁| = Ω(1)) or assuming a spiked covariance structure. This paper asks whether a simple thresholding step on Oja's output can close this gap for a general Σ.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes n i.i.d. σ-subgaussian data points X_i ∈ ℝᵈ in a single streaming pass, along with a cardinality parameter k ≥ s and a learning rate η = 3 log(n)/(n(λ₁−λ₂)). It outputs a sparse eigenvector estimate v̂ with sin² error O(σ*² · k log(d)/n) and a support set Ŝ ⊇ S with high probability. The data is split in half: the first half drives support recovery via Oja's algorithm, and the second half is used (optionally truncated to the recovered support) to compute the final eigenvector estimate. A SuccessBoost wrapper over ⌈30 log(1/δ)⌉ disjoint data buckets converts constant-probability guarantees into 1−δ high-probability ones.

## Method

The procedure is deceptively simple: initialize u₀ ~ N(0,I), run Oja's rank-one updates u_t ← (I + ηX_tX_tᵀ)u_{t−1} with renormalization, then threshold the unnormalized Oja vector by keeping the k largest-magnitude entries to recover the support S. The nontrivial contribution is the entrywise analysis of r_i = e_iᵀB_n u₀ (where B_n is the product of n random rank-one update matrices), which requires bounding the projection of a product of independent random matrices on a random vector. The authors derive tight bounds by solving a coupled 2×2 linear recursion for the v₁-aligned component α_n and the orthogonal-subspace component β_n, avoiding the loose Weyl-based bounds that fail when r_eff is large. A geometric aggregation (SuccessBoost) procedure then amplifies the constant-probability support recovery to arbitrary confidence. For the final eigenvector, the recovered support is used either to truncate the Oja output (Theorem 3.5) or to restrict the data to the support before running an optimal-step-size Oja variant (Theorem 3.7), yielding the minimax rate.

## Results

The paper's main result (Theorem 3.7) shows that a single-pass thresholded Oja algorithm achieves sin² error ≤ C'' · λ₁λ₂/(λ₁−λ₂)² · k log(d)/n with probability ≥ 1−d⁻¹⁰, matching the minimax lower bound O(σ*² s log(d)/n) up to constants, while operating in O(d) space and O(nd) time. Theorem 3.5 (vector truncation variant) gives sin² ≤ C'' (λ₁/(λ₁−λ₂))² · k log²(d)/n. Empirically (Figure 1a), the proposed algorithm outperforms all other O(nd)-time, O(d)-space baselines (JL09, YX15, WL16) on a spiked covariance model with r=2, n=d=1000, with the diagonal thresholding method [JL09] showing large error in the multi-spike setting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method is the only algorithm in Table 1 that simultaneously achieves O(d) space, O(nd) time, global convergence (no strong initialization), minimax-optimal O(s log(d)/n) sin² error, and applicability to a general covariance matrix Σ. The closest streaming competitors—Yang & Xu [YX15] and Wang & Lu [WL16]—match the O(d) space and O(nd) time budget but require a strong initialization |u₀ᵀv₁|=Ω(1) (local convergence only) and are restricted to the spiked model. Standard Oja's algorithm [JJK+16] shares the same space/time and global convergence but yields error O(r_eff/n), which degrades to O(d/n) when the effective rank is large, whereas the proposed thresholding recovers the sparsity-aware O(s log(d)/n) rate. Offline methods with minimax error (Ma [Ma13], Gataric [GWS20]) require O(d²) space and O(nd²) time, making them infeasible for streaming. The diagonal thresholding method [JL09] matches the space/time budget but provably fails for multi-spike models (Proposition 3.4), as confirmed empirically in Figure 1a where it shows large sin² error when r=2.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Oja's algorithm (1982) and its streaming PCA analysis (Jain et al., 2016) to the sparse PCA setting where the effective rank r_eff can be as large as d, a regime where all prior analyses of Oja's algorithm break down. It departs from existing streaming sparse PCA methods (Yang & Xu 2015, Wang & Lu 2016) that require strong initialization (|u₀ᵀv₁| = Ω(1)) and spiked covariance structure, by showing that a simple thresholding of the Oja vector with random initialization suffices for a general Σ. The core analytical novelty is an entrywise analysis of the unnormalized Oja vector as a product of independent random matrices projected on a random vector, solved via a system of coupled linear recursions — a technique entirely different from the matrix-norm-based analyses used in prior streaming PCA work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first single-pass algorithm achieving the minimax optimal O(s log d / n) sin² error for sparse PCA in O(d) space and O(nd) time without strong initialization or spiked-model assumptions, filling a gap identified in the literature. The entrywise deviation bounds (Lemmas 3.11, 3.12) and the system-of-recursions technique for bounding matrix products are of independent interest beyond sparse PCA. Key limitations include the requirement to know λ₁ − λ₂ for the learning rate, the signal strength condition in Assumption 2, and the restriction to the top eigenvector (extension to top-k is mentioned as future work). Open questions include whether the analysis extends to adversarial or non-subgaussian settings and whether adaptive learning rates can remove the eigengap knowledge requirement.
