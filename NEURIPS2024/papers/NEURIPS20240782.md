## Problem

Pretrained LLMs rely on dense multiplications that create severe memory, latency, and energy bottlenecks on edge and cloud hardware. While shift-and-add reparameterization can replace multiplications with cheaper hardware primitives, existing techniques (ShiftAddNet, ShiftAddViT) require training from scratch or extensive fine-tuning to recover accuracy—impractical for billion-parameter LLMs. This paper tackles the open problem of applying multiplication-free reparameterization to pretrained LLMs in a purely post-training manner without any additional training.

## Input / Output

The method consumes full-precision (FP16) weight matrices from a pretrained LLM along with a small calibration dataset for activation statistics. It produces, for each weight matrix, a set of binary matrices (each element ∈ {−1, +1}) paired with group-wise power-of-two (PoT) scaling factors, where each scaling factor is a single integer exponent P such that the factor equals sign(α)·2^P. At inference, the original matrix multiplication is replaced by bitwise shifts of FP16 activations (via integer addition on the exponent field), 8-bit LUT queries keyed by grouped binary weights, and additions of partial sums—eliminating all multiplications entirely. The group size is 8 columns × 1/8 rows (block-wise) or 1 column (column-wise), matching the BCQ LUT kernel granularity.

## Method

ShiftAddLLM reparameterizes each weight matrix W into q binary matrices {b_i} and PoT scaling factors {α_i} via alternating multi-bit BCQ (greedy initialization followed by least-squares/binary-search refinement), with all scaling factors quantized to additive powers of two during the alternating cycles. To mitigate accuracy loss, the paper introduces a multi-objective optimization that jointly minimizes weight quantization error and output activation error using column-wise (or block-wise) scaling factors: each scaling factor corresponds to a constant activation value, and subsequent columns' factors are updated after compensating for prior columns' quantization errors, unifying the weight-only and activation-only objectives. For efficiency, an automated mixed-bit allocation strategy assigns 2, 3, or 4 bits per layer by solving an integer program that minimizes a proxy importance score (F-norm × STD of the Optimal Brain Compression-style importance score) under a global average bit budget, allocating more bits to sensitive layers (e.g., Q/K projections, later blocks) and fewer to robust ones. The entire pipeline is post-training: no gradient updates or fine-tuning are performed on the LLM parameters.

## Results

On the OPT model family (125M–66B) evaluated on WikiText-2, ShiftAddLLM (Acc.) at 3-bit achieves an average perplexity of 10.39 on OPT-13B and 9.43 on OPT-66B, closely matching FP16 (10.13 and 9.34) while all baselines degrade substantially (e.g., OPTQ: 11.61/14.16, LUT-GEMM: 12.50/100.33, AWQ: 16.53/5622.00). At 2-bit, ShiftAddLLM maintains a PPL of 10.33 on OPT-66B versus 20.91 for the strongest baseline QuIP, an average reduction of 22.7 points. On LLaMA-2-70B, ShiftAddLLM achieves PPL 3.64 at 3-bit and 4.72 at 2-bit, outperforming all baselines. The method also delivers >80% memory savings (e.g., 23 GB vs. 122 GB at 3-bit for OPT-66B) and 87.2% energy reduction on an Eyeriss accelerator.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ShiftAddLLM consistently outperforms all six baselines across five LLM families. Against OPTQ, it achieves an average 3-bit PPL reduction of 5.63 on OPT and 1.82 on LLaMA, with the gap widening dramatically at 2-bit (e.g., OPT-66B: 10.33 vs. 20.91). Against LUT-GEMM, the advantage is even larger—38.47 average PPL reduction at 3-bit on OPT—and LUT-GEMM frequently collapses to PPL >100 at 2-bit while ShiftAddLLM remains functional. AWQ is the weakest baseline, often producing PPL values in the thousands or tens of thousands at both 3-bit and 2-bit, whereas ShiftAddLLM stays within 1–2 points of FP16. At 2-bit, QuIP is the strongest competitor, yet ShiftAddLLM still achieves a 22.74 average PPL reduction over it on OPT. In the appendix comparisons, ShiftAddLLM also beats OmniQuant (0.39 PPL reduction at 3-bit; OmniQuant fails entirely on OPT at 2-bit) and FlexRound (0.52 PPL reduction at 3-bit on LLaMA-2). On downstream tasks, ShiftAddLLM gains 13.37/13.19 average accuracy over OPTQ/LUT-GEMM at 3-bit on OPT-66B and 2.55/2.39 on LLaMA-2-70B, confirming that the perplexity advantage translates to task-level performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on binary-coding quantization (BCQ) as its quantization backbone, on additive power-of-two (PoT) quantization for representing scaling factors as shifts, and on DenseShift for implementing FP×PoT multiplication via a single integer addition. It departs from prior shift-and-add work (ShiftAddNet, ShiftAddViT, MatMul-free LM) by applying the concept to pretrained LLMs in a purely post-training manner without any fine-tuning or retraining, and from standard LLM quantization (OPTQ, LUT-GEMM, AWQ) by eliminating multiplications entirely rather than merely reducing their bit-width. The multi-objective optimization combining weight and activation objectives with column/block-wise scaling factors is a novel design that bridges the gap between these two lineages.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: 80%+ energy and memory reductions over FP16 LLMs, with the method remaining functional at 2-bit precision where all standard quantization baselines (OPTQ, LUT-GEMM, AWQ) collapse to unusable perplexity. A key limitation is that the highest-accuracy variant (column-wise scaling) lacks a fast CUDA kernel, so the latency-optimized variant (block-wise) trades some accuracy for speed, and the overall GPU speedup story depends on BCQ kernel support. Open questions include whether the approach extends to training-time use, whether it generalizes to MoE architectures beyond the single Mistral test, and whether the energy savings translate to real ASIC deployments beyond the Eyeriss-based estimation.
