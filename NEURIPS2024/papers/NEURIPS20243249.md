## Problem

Existing adversarial attack methods for pedestrian detectors (e.g., Adv-Tshirt, TCA) produce patterns that lose effectiveness at medium-to-long distances in the physical world, because they naively downscale patterns to simulate distant appearances, creating a widening appearance gap between simulated and real-world counterparts. Additionally, a conflict arises between the low-frequency patterns required at short versus long distances, hindering joint optimization across the full distance range.

## Input / Output

The optimization pipeline consumes short-distance pedestrian images (from INRIA, PennFudan, COCO, and online sources at ~4m scale), randomly selected background images, and a target detector model, while the DIC is conditioned on target distance, skylight RGB, and turbidity values sampled per batch. The output is a single adversarial pattern (200×133 pixels for patches, tiled for clothing) that must simultaneously evade detection across seven simulated distances (4m–40m) and generalize to physical-world testing at 3.5m–41m. The DIC itself requires only 15 learnable parameters and is trained on a small dataset of 45 printed-and-photographed image pairs.

## Method

FDA combines two key components: a physics-based Distant Image Converter (DIC) and Multi-Frequency Optimization (MFO). The DIC sequentially applies three modules—(1) an atmospheric perspective module using an exponential scattering model with learnable molecular density, wavelength, and empirical parameters; (2) a camera simulation module implemented as two channel-wise convolutions with learnable blurring kernels (circular for the anti-aliasing filter, square for the imaging chip) whose stride and kernel size scale with distance to preserve the physical blurring-to-image-height ratio; and (3) an effect filter module with eight learnable filters (brightness, saturation, sharpening, exposure, contrast, highlight/shadow, vibrance, color temperature). MFO resolves the short-vs-long distance optimization conflict via Multi-Scale Cropping (using smaller crop sizes at short distances to protect low-frequency structure) and Two-Stage Optimization (first optimizing low-frequency components for long distances, then high-frequency details for short distances while constraining low-frequency components via a Gaussian-blur L2 penalty). The full pipeline is trained with EOT over DIC parameters and randomized atmospheric conditions to ensure physical-world robustness.

## Results

The FDA patch achieves a 74% average attack success rate (ASR) across 7 distances (3.5–41 m) targeting YOLOv5, substantially outperforming Adv-Tshirt, NAP (19%), and T-SEA (42%). For clothing attacks, FDA clothing reaches 76% average ASR (front/back) and 61% (side view) versus 37% and 46% for TCA clothing, respectively. In black-box transfer, the ensemble-optimized FDA pattern achieves 75–87% average ASR across six unseen detectors (Mask R-CNN, FrRCNN, RetinaNet, YOLOv8, Deformable-DETR), while the single-model FDA pattern transfers best to Deformable-DETR (63%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FDA dominates all four baselines on the primary patch-attack metric (average ASR across 7 distances, YOLOv5 target): FDA reaches 74%, compared to T-SEA at 42%, NAP at 19%, and the Adv-Tshirt pipeline (which FDA's pipeline degenerates to without DIC+MFO) at roughly 22%. For clothing attacks, FDA clothing (76% front/back, 61% side) more than doubles TCA clothing (37% front/back, 46% side), a gap of 39 and 15 percentage points respectively. In the black-box transfer setting (Table 1), the single-model FDA pattern optimized for YOLOv5 transfers poorly to most targets (26–49%) except Deformable-DETR (63%), but integrating ensemble attack (optimizing against both Mask R-CNN variants) lifts all black-box ASRs above 75%, with RetinaNet-PVT reaching 87%. The DIC component alone contributes a 43-point ASR gain (22%→65%) over the naive Adv-Tshirt pipeline, and adding MFO further raises it to 74%, confirming both components are necessary for full-distance effectiveness.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the physical-world adversarial attack lineage (Adv-Patch, Adv-Tshirt, TCA) by identifying and addressing a previously overlooked failure mode: the appearance gap between naively downscaled digital simulations and their physical-world counterparts at medium-to-long distances. The optimization pipeline is explicitly built on Adv-Tshirt's framework, with toroidal cropping from TCA incorporated for clothing attacks, while the atmospheric perspective module adopts the turbidity-based scattering formulation from Morales et al. and He et al. The key conceptual departure is the diagnosis that naive downscaling (used by all prior methods) produces incorrect optimization targets, and the resolution via a 15-parameter physics-based Distant Image Converter rather than learned per-distance networks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that pedestrian detectors are far less robust at medium-to-long distances than previously assumed, with FDA patterns achieving 74% average ASR across 3.5–41 m against YOLOv5, and the finding generalizes to Deformable-DETR, Mask R-CNN, and RetinaNet. The frequency-conflict diagnosis (different low-frequency patterns needed at short vs. long distances) and the MFO resolution offer a reusable principle for any multi-scale adversarial optimization. Open questions include whether the DIC's 15-parameter physics model transfers to other sensing modalities (thermal, LiDAR), whether detectors can be trained to be robust to such full-distance patterns, and whether the approach extends beyond pedestrian detection to other object classes or tracking tasks.
