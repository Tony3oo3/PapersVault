## Problem

Stochastic GDA-type methods are widely used for nonconvex minimax problems in machine learning (GANs, DRO, fair classification), yet existing high-probability complexity guarantees only cover convex/concave minimax problems or restrictive non-monotone VI settings. No high-probability bounds existed for nonconvex minimax problems, even in the nonconvex/strongly concave case, leaving a significant theory-practice gap for the most common single-loop algorithms.

## Input / Output

The method requires a smooth minimax objective f(x,y) that is nonconvex in x and satisfies the Polyak-Lojasiewicz (PL) condition in y, with access to conditionally unbiased stochastic gradient estimates whose noise is sub-Gaussian (light-tailed). It outputs a randomly selected iterate (x̃, ỹ) from the sequence {0,...,T-1} that is an (ε, ε/√κ)-stationary point with probability at least 1−q̄, requiring O(ℓκ²δ²/ε⁴ + κ/ε²(ℓ + δ²log(1/q̄))) stochastic gradient calls. The algorithm operates in a purely stochastic streaming regime where mini-batching or parallel restarts are impractical.

## Method

The paper analyzes the smoothed alternating GDA (sm-AGDA) algorithm using a Lyapunov function V_t = f̂(x_t,y_t;z_t) + 2P(z_t) − 2Ψ(y_t;z_t) previously introduced for nonconvex-concave problems. The key technical contribution is a novel almost-sure descent property for this Lyapunov function (Theorem 7), which decomposes the per-iteration change into deterministic descent terms, martingale-difference cross terms, and noise-variance terms. A new concentration inequality (Theorem 9) is then developed specifically for this structure, handling both the state-dependent sub-Gaussian noise (via conditional moment-generating function bounds) and the additive noise term simultaneously. This yields high-probability bounds without restarts or parallel runs, with the failure probability q̄ appearing only in an additive O(ε⁻² log(1/q̄)) term rather than multiplying the dominant O(ε⁻⁴) term, making the high-probability overhead negligible relative to existing in-expectation rates.

## Results

The main theoretical contribution is the first high-probability complexity bound for sm-AGDA on NCPL problems: an (ε, ε/√κ)-stationary point is computed with probability at least 1−q̄ within O(ℓκ²δ²/ε⁴ + κ/ε²(ℓ + δ²log(1/q̄))) stochastic gradient calls (Corollary 14). The probability parameter q̄ appears only in the additive O(ε⁻² log(1/q̄)) term, making the overhead over the in-expectation bound negligible compared to the dominant ε⁻⁴ term. Numerically, on the synthetic NCPL game (d₁=d₂=30, T=10,000 iterations, 25 sample paths), the range statistic of the stationarity measure M_κ(t) diminishes to a value inversely proportional to T, consistent with the theoretical prediction. On DRO logistic regression (a9a, gisette, sido0), histograms over 200 runs show that sm-AGDA's stationarity metric distribution concentrates more tightly than both baselines at both 20 epochs and end-of-training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares sm-AGDA against two state-of-the-art baselines on distributionally robust nonconvex logistic regression: SAPD+ [72] (a two-loop accelerated stochastic primal-dual method) and SMDA/SMDAVR [31] (a variance-reduced mirror descent ascent method). On all three datasets (a9a, gisette, sido0), sm-AGDA's stationarity metric distribution concentrates more tightly than both baselines across 200 independent runs, at both the early phase (20 epochs) and end-of-training (250–550 epochs). The paper notes that sm-AGDA could accommodate larger stepsizes compared to the other algorithms, which likely contributed to its superior concentration behavior. Importantly, sm-AGDA is a single-loop method, whereas SAPD+ is two-loop, making sm-AGDA more practical in streaming/online settings where parallel runs or multiple passes are impractical. The theoretical advantage is that sm-AGDA is the only method in Table 1 with a high-probability bound that supports nonconvexity in the primal variable, while all other listed methods are restricted to convex/concave or monotone VI settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the sm-AGDA algorithm introduced by Zhang et al. [70] for nonconvex-concave minimax problems and the in-expectation complexity analysis of Yang et al. [66] for the broader nonconvex/PL (NCPL) setting. The key departure is in the type of guarantee: prior high-probability bounds for minimax problems were limited to convex/concave or monotone variational inequality settings, while this work provides the first such bounds for NCPL problems by deriving a novel almost-sure descent property for the Lyapunov function and a tailored concentration inequality (Theorem 9) that avoids the need for parallel restarts or Markov's inequality. The concentration result is positioned as potentially applicable to other first-order methods for nonconvex minimax problems beyond sm-AGDA.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

High-probability guarantees are essential for controlling tail risk in practical ML applications (e.g., GAN training, adversarial learning) where a single bad run can be costly, yet no such guarantees existed for nonconvex minimax problems before this work. The finding that the high-probability overhead is confined to the lower-order O(ε^{-2}) term rather than the dominant O(ε^{-4}) term is practically reassuring, suggesting that in-expectation rates are nearly tight even in the worst case. Limitations include the requirement of sub-Gaussian gradient noise (Assumption 3), which may not hold in all deep learning settings, and the fact that the DRO experiments involve a constrained dual domain where the theoretical guarantees do not directly apply. Open questions include extending the analysis to heavy-tailed noise, removing the PL-in-y assumption entirely, and whether the concentration inequality generalizes to two-loop or variance-reduced methods.
