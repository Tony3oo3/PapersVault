## Problem

In linear quadratic control with perturbations arising from multiple heterogeneous latent sources (e.g., wind, rain, or distinct power injections), black-box ML predictions of these latent variables are often unreliable. Existing approaches like the λ-confident policy use a single scalar trust parameter and provably cannot achieve an optimal consistency-robustness tradeoff: if the policy is (1+o(1))-consistent, it is at least ω(1)-robust. The paper asks whether disentangling predictions per latent component and learning a vectorized confidence parameter online can close this gap.

## Input / Output

At each time step t, the policy receives the observed state x_t ∈ ℝⁿ, a window of w disentangled ML predictions (s̃_{τ|t}(i) for i=1,…,k, τ=t,…,t+w−1) of k latent variables, and an estimated mixing parameter θ̃_t from an ICA procedure. It outputs a control action u_t ∈ ℝᵐ computed via an MPC-type formula that scales each predicted latent component by its corresponding confidence λ_t(i) ∈ [0,1], and updates the confidence vector λ_t online. The key structural detail is that λ_t is a k-dimensional vector (one trust level per latent source) rather than a scalar, and the predictions are consumed component-wise via Hadamard products before being passed through the mixing function f.

## Method

DISC (Disentangled Confidence) replaces the scalar trust parameter with a vector λ_t ∈ [0,1]^k and learns it online via FTRL (linear mixing) or FTPL (general mixing). The critical technical enabler is Lemma 1, which decouples the cumulative regret into a sum of per-step convex functions ζ_ℓ(λ), converting what would otherwise be a memory-dependent optimization into a standard memoryless online learning problem. The control action at each step is an MPC solution that blends the LQR feedback (−Kx_t) with a prediction-based correction term scaled by λ_t ∘ s̃_{τ|t}, so that components with high predicted error are automatically down-weighted. The resulting competitive ratio achieves a 'best-of-both-worlds' bound: the prediction-error term ε̄(i)/(Ω(T/w)+ε̄(i)) is negligible when predictions are accurate and bounded by 1 when they are not, yielding (1+o(1))-consistency and O(1)-robustness simultaneously with a sufficiently large prediction window.

## Results

The main theoretical contribution is a competitive ratio bound for DISC: CR(DISC) ≤ 1 + O(Σᵢ ε̄(i)/(Ω(T/w) + ε̄(i))) + O(w√(k/T)) + O(ρ_F^(2w) + η̄/T) for linear mixing (Theorem 4.2), and an analogous expected bound with O(wk²/√T) replacing the FTRL term for general mixing (Theorem 4.3). This establishes DISC as (1+o(1))-consistent and O(1)-robust with a sufficiently large prediction window w = ω(1), a first-of-its-kind best-of-both-worlds guarantee. Theorem 4.1 proves a fundamental impossibility: any fixed λ-CON policy that is (1+o(1))-consistent must be at least ω(1)-robust, showing the gap between static and online confidence learning can be arbitrarily large. Experimentally, DISC achieves the lowest cost ratio J(π)/J* across all tested scaling factors ν ∈ [0.25, 8] in drone navigation and the lowest average total cost with smallest variance in voltage control.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DISC is compared against three baselines: (1) LQR, which ignores all ML predictions and applies the standard state-feedback law u_t = -Kx_t; (2) MPC with untrusted predictions (λ = 1_k), which fully trusts the ML forecasts in the MPC optimization; and (3) the self-tuning policy from Li et al. [4] and Lin et al. [31], which uses a single scalar confidence parameter rather than a k-dimensional vector. In the drone navigation experiment (Figure 2), DISC consistently achieves the lowest cost ratio J(π)/J* across the full range of environmental predictability (scaling factor ν from 0.25 to 8), outperforming all three baselines. In the voltage control experiment (Figure 6), DISC achieves the lowest average total cost with the smallest variance over 5 random tests. The key advantage over the self-tuning policy [4] is that DISC's vectorized confidence parameter λ_t ∈ [0,1]^k allows per-component trust adjustment, which is critical when latent variables have heterogeneous predictability (e.g., predictable wind vs. unpredictable rainfall). The advantage over MPC (untrusted) is most pronounced when prediction errors are large, where fully trusting predictions degrades performance significantly.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the λ-confident policy from Li et al. [4] (2022), which introduced a scalar trust parameter for LQR with untrusted predictions, by vectorizing the confidence parameter to λ ∈ [0,1]^k and learning it online via FTRL or FTPL. The key technical departure is Lemma 1, which decouples the cumulative cost into memoryless per-step loss functions ζ_ℓ(λ), reducing the confidence-parameter selection to a standard online optimization problem—a connection the authors note was 'not previously discovered.' The disentanglement component draws on ICA (FastICA) and nonlinear ICA literature, but the paper's focus is on optimally utilizing the resulting (potentially inaccurate) latent variable predictions rather than on the disentanglement itself.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The component-wise error term ε̄(i)/(Ω(T/w)+ε̄(i)) provides a first-of-its-kind 'best-of-both-worlds' competitive ratio bound that gracefully degrades per latent component, which is practically important for systems where some disturbance sources are predictable (e.g., solar PV) while others are not (e.g., wind gusts). The impossibility result (Theorem 4.1) cleanly establishes why static confidence parameters are fundamentally insufficient, motivating the online learning approach. Limitations include the reliance on ICA identifiability assumptions for disentanglement, the linear system restriction, and the slower k²/√T convergence rate for general (non-convex) mixing. Open questions include extending to nonlinear dynamical systems and whether the best-of-both-worlds structure generalizes to other online decision-making problems beyond LQR.
