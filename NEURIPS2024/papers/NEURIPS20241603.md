## Problem

Vision-Language Models (VLMs) like CLIP are trained on massive image-caption pairs, but it remains unclear whether they memorize individual training images beyond what can be inferred from the caption or dataset-level correlations. Prior memorization studies either focus on generative models with decoders or on uni-modal representation models with simple foreground-background structure, leaving a gap for multi-modal representation learning where text and image modalities interact in complex ways and no direct decoder exists to probe retained information.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a target image-text pair from training set A, a disjoint public image set P (e.g., ImageNet or 50M filtered LAION images), and two VLMs (target f_A trained on A, reference f_B trained on disjoint B). It produces sample-level precision/recall/F-score gaps between the two models' object recovery, and population-level metrics (PPG, PRG, AUCG) that quantify the fraction of training data memorized. Object annotations for all images are obtained via the Detic open-vocabulary detector (21K classes), with a 0.5 confidence threshold (0.3 for Shutterstock), enabling multi-object comparison per image rather than single-label classification.

## Method

The VL-Déjà-Vu test exploits the insight that a memorized image-text pair causes the VLM's text embedding to retrieve public images that share more objects with the original training image than would be expected from the caption or dataset correlations alone. Concretely, for a target pair z, the method encodes z_txt with both f_A and f_B, retrieves k nearest public images from P in each model's embedding space, annotates all retrieved images with Detic, and measures the fraction of ground-truth objects in z_img that co-occur in the retrieved sets. The memorization signal is the gap between target and reference model performance, which cancels out dataset-level correlations since both models see the same public set. Population-level metrics (PPG, PRG, AUCG) aggregate binary comparisons across the training set, while sample-level metrics sort records by embedding distance or correct-prediction count to identify disproportionately memorized subsets. The paper further evaluates four mitigations—early stopping, temperature scaling, weight decay, and text token masking—finding that masking 30% of caption tokens reduces PPG from 9.1% to 3.4% with only a 1.1-point drop in ImageNet zero-shot accuracy.

## Results

On 10M filtered LAION training data (ViT-B-32, 200 epochs), the default OpenCLIP model achieves 25.2% ImageNet zero-shot accuracy while exhibiting significant déjà vu memorization: PPG = 9.1%, PRG = 9.2%, AUCG = 0.034. Memorization decreases with training set size (from 1M to 50M) but remains significant even at 50M samples. Text randomization with masking ratio 0.3 reduces PPG to 3.4% and AUCG to 0.013 while only dropping ImageNet accuracy to 24.1%; increasing masking to 0.5 further reduces PPG to 3.0% and AUCG to 0.007. At the sample level, precision/recall/F-score gaps between target and reference models exceed 0.3 for the most memorized top-10 records.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against external baseline methods; instead, it compares a target model (trained on the target image-text pair) against a reference model (trained on a disjoint set) to isolate memorization from spurious correlation. Among the four mitigation strategies evaluated, text randomization (masking) is the most effective: at mr=0.3 it reduces PPG by 63% (9.1%→3.4%) and AUCG by 62% (0.034→0.013) while sacrificing only 1.1% ImageNet accuracy. Weight decay tuning (0.1→0.3) also reduces memorization and surprisingly improves utility, but the reduction is less dramatic than text masking. Temperature scaling and early stopping reduce memorization but at a larger cost to model utility (e.g., T=25 drops accuracy to 21.7%). The paper also notes that text masking improves ARO benchmark accuracy (compositional reasoning), suggesting mitigated models are less prone to bag-of-words behavior.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the déjà vu memorization test from Meehan et al. (2023), which measured whether image-only SSL models retain foreground-object information beyond background correlations, to the multi-modal VLM setting. The key conceptual departure is that VLMs trained on rich image-caption pairs (e.g., LAION, Shutterstock) cannot be probed with a simple foreground-background split; instead, the authors design a kNN retrieval test that compares a target model (trained on the query pair) against a reference model (not trained on it) using a public image set and an object detector. The broader memorization literature on language models (Carlini et al.) and generative vision models (Somepalli et al., Carlini et al.) is positioned as uni-modal and decoder-dependent, whereas this work addresses the encoder-only, multi-modal case where information leakage is more subtle.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first systematic study of memorization in VLMs, showing that even at 50M training pairs—where zero-shot utility is competitive—significant object-level memorization persists and is concentrated in a non-uniform subset of samples. The practical finding that text masking (masking 30–50% of caption tokens) substantially reduces memorization with only a modest drop in ImageNet accuracy (and even improves ARO compositional-reasoning scores) gives practitioners a concrete, low-cost mitigation. A key open question is how to measure memorization for out-of-the-box models without access to a paired reference model and the full training set, which the authors explicitly leave for future work.
