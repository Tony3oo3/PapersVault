## Problem

Most existing time-series analysis (TSA) models rely solely on numerical series, overlooking exogenous textual signals such as concurrent events, policies, and reports that human experts naturally integrate. The absence of a comprehensive multimodal time-series dataset is due to three gaps in prior work: narrow domain coverage (limited to finance), coarse-grained modality alignment (text and numbers merely share a domain), and inherent data contamination (textual predictions leaking into test sets and outdated cutoffs exposed to LLM pretraining corpora).

## Input / Output

The multimodal TSF model takes a numerical lookback window X ∈ R^{l×d_in} and a textual series S ∈ R^{k×d_txt} where the text lookback length k is independent of the numerical lookback l, and the text feature dimension d_txt is inconsistent across samples (handled via pooling layers). Textual input is preprocessed into disentangled facts and predictions with binary start/end timestamps that align to numerical time steps at the appropriate granularity (e.g., a seasonal report maps to 12 weekly steps). The output is a forecast Y ∈ R^{h×d_out} over a horizon window h, evaluated by normalized MSE across four horizon lengths per dataset frequency.

## Method

Time-MMD is constructed through a three-stage pipeline: (1) numerical series from nine government-agency sources spanning agriculture, climate, economy, energy, environment, health (US and Africa), security, social good, and traffic; (2) textual series from manually selected report sources and weekly Google API searches, preprocessed by Llama3-70B to filter irrelevant content, disentangle facts from predictions (mitigating contamination), and generate summaries; (3) binary timestamps for fine-grained temporal alignment between text and numerical data. MM-TSFlib implements a model-agnostic multimodal integration framework where the numerical series is processed by a unimodal TSF backbone and the text by a frozen LLM (BERT, GPT-2, Llama-2/3) with a trainable MLP projection layer; the two outputs are combined via a learnable linear weighting mechanism in an end-to-end training loop, adding minimal trainable parameters while supporting over 20 TSF algorithms and 7 LLM backbones.

## Results

Across over 1,000 multimodal TSF experiments on Time-MMD, the multimodal versions outperformed their unimodal counterparts in 95% of cases, reducing mean squared error by an average of over 15% and up to 40% in domains with rich textual data (e.g., Health, Economy). The improvements are consistent across all 12 TSF backbones and robust to horizon window size, from short-term (6 steps) to long-term (336 steps) forecasting. SOTA unimodal models like iTransformer and PatchTST maintain leading performance in both unimodal and multimodal settings, validating the quality of Time-MMD's numerical data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's core comparison is between unimodal and multimodal versions of 12 TSF backbones (Transformer, Reformer, Informer, Autoformer, Crossformer, Non-stationary Transformer, FEDformer, iTransformer, PatchTST, DLinear, FiLM, Time-LLM). The multimodal extension via MM-TSFlib wins in 95% of all experiments, with the largest gains observed in text-rich domains: on Health (US), FEDformer drops from 1.32 to 0.98 MSE (26% reduction) and PatchTST from 1.36 to 0.99 (27% reduction) at horizon 12. On the Economy domain, improvements are even more dramatic, with Informer dropping from 1.59 to 0.28 (82% reduction) and Crossformer from 0.95 to 0.17 (82% reduction). The originally weaker Informer benefits most from multimodality, attributed to its long-range dependency modeling design. Notably, the choice of LLM backbone (BERT vs. GPT-2 vs. Llama-3-8B) shows no clear correlation with multimodal TSF performance, and Doc2Vec as a non-LLM text encoder also achieves meaningful improvements, though slightly worse than BERT.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by introducing the first multi-domain multimodal time series dataset (Time-MMD) and the first multimodal TSF library (MM-TSFlib), addressing three gaps in prior multimodal TS work: narrow domain coverage (prior datasets focused almost exclusively on financial/stock prediction), coarse-grained modality alignment, and inherent data contamination from predictions in text and LLM pretraining overlap. Conceptually, it departs from prior LLM-based TSA methods that incorporate only endogenous text (linguistic descriptions of statistical features) by introducing exogenous textual series—concurrent events, policy reports, and news—that provide genuinely new information beyond the numerical series. The multimodal integration framework itself is deliberately simple (frozen LLM + trainable projection layer + linear weighting), positioning it as a first-cut baseline rather than a methodological contribution, and it extends over 20 existing unimodal TSF backbones (Transformer, Informer, iTransformer, DLinear, etc.) to their multimodal counterparts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Time-MMD provides the first comprehensive, contamination-free multimodal TS benchmark spanning 9 diverse domains with fine-grained temporal alignment, enabling rigorous evaluation of multimodal TSA that was previously impossible. The consistent 15–40% MSE reduction across 1,000+ experiments and 12 TSF backbones validates that exogenous textual signals carry substantial predictive information, opening concrete research directions in multimodal imputation, anomaly detection, and foundation TS models. Key limitations include English-only text, a deliberately simple integration framework (only projection-layer fine-tuning), and the finding that LLM backbone choice has minimal impact—suggesting the current framework underutilizes LLM capacity. Open questions include more effective LLM fine-tuning strategies for TS, handling distributional shift in text, multilingual extension, and whether the gains persist under more realistic deployment settings.
