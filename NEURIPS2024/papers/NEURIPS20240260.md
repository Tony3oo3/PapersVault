## Problem

Integrating multiple heterogeneous property constraints (e.g., synthetic score on a 1–5 scale and gas permeability exceeding 10,000 Barrier units) into diffusion-based molecular generation remains unexplored. Existing approaches either collapse multi-conditions into a single objective—losing explicit property relations—or apply noise independently to atoms and bonds in the forward diffusion process, misaligning the noise distribution with the joint denoising step and degrading condition controllability.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a set of M conditions (numerical values such as SAS/SCS scores and log-scaled gas permeabilities, plus a categorical task label) together with the diffusion timestep, all encoded into a single D-dimensional condition vector. It outputs a molecular graph represented as one-hot atom-type node features and bond-type edge features (including a 'non-bond' type), with the full Graph DiT variant connecting all generated components rather than retaining only the largest connected component. Polymer outputs explicitly include polymerization points marked in the structure.

## Method

Graph DiT couples a condition encoder with a Transformer graph denoiser. The condition encoder maps numerical properties through a clustering-based scheme (learnable centroids with soft assignment via Linear–Softmax–Linear) and categorical properties via one-hot, summing all encodings into a single vector. The denoiser encodes noisy graph tokens (concatenated node and flattened edge features) into hidden states, then applies Transformer layers where standard LayerNorm is replaced by AdaLN: the condition vector produces per-layer scale γ and shift β (via two-layer MLPs with SiLU) that modulate each hidden representation. A key architectural departure is the graph-dependent noise model, which constructs a joint transition matrix Q_G from four sub-matrices (Q_V, Q_E, Q_EV, Q_VE) using Kronecker products and atom–bond co-occurrence statistics from training data, so that node and edge state transitions are coupled rather than independent. Training uses predictor-free (classifier-free) guidance: conditions are randomly dropped during training, and at inference the reverse step combines log-probabilities of unconditional and conditional denoising with a guidance scale s, directly estimating p_θ(G̃⁰|Gᵗ, C) without auxiliary predictor networks.

## Results

On the polymer multi-conditional generation task (Table 1), Graph DiT achieves the best average MAE of 0.9205 across four numerical conditions (Synth., O₂Perm, N₂Perm, CO₂Perm), a 17.86% improvement over the best baseline LSTM-HC (1.2098). It also attains the highest fragment-based Similarity (0.9600) and lowest Fréchet ChemNet Distance (6.6443) among all methods. On small molecule tasks (Table 2), Graph DiT achieves over 0.9 accuracy on categorical conditions (0.9135 BACE, 0.9417 BBBP, 0.9777 HIV), far surpassing the best baseline accuracy of ~0.67, and ranks 1st in condition controllability (Avg.Rank = 1.0) on BACE and BBBP.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Graph DiT outperforms all seven baselines on the primary polymer task (Table 1), achieving the lowest Avg.MAE (0.9205 vs. 1.2098 for LSTM-HC, the best baseline) and the best distribution learning metrics (Similarity 0.9600, Distance 6.6443). Among molecular optimization baselines, LSTM-HC is the strongest on condition control but still trails Graph DiT by 17.86% in average MAE; MARS and JTVAE-BO control synthetic scores well but fail on gas permeability (MAE >1.0 vs. Graph DiT's <0.9). Among diffusion baselines, DiGress and DiGress v2 produce diverse molecules (Diversity ~0.91) but collapse in distribution fitting (Similarity ~0.27) and have the worst condition control (Avg.MAE >2.0). GDSS and MOOD show competitive gas permeability MAE (~1.0–1.5) but near-zero Similarity (0.0000 and 0.0227), indicating they generate out-of-distribution structures. On small molecule tasks (Table 2), Graph DiT dominates on categorical condition accuracy (>0.9 vs. <0.67 for all baselines) and achieves Avg.Rank of 1.0 on BACE/BBBP, while LSTM-HC ranks 3rd–4th. The predictor-guided variants (DiGress v2, MOOD) do not substantially improve over their unconditional counterparts, confirming that the predictor-free AdaLN conditioning in Graph DiT is more effective for multi-conditional generation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the discrete graph diffusion framework of DiGress (Vignac et al., 2022) by replacing its independent atom/bond noise transitions with a graph-dependent noise model that captures joint node-edge distributions, and by swapping the DiT Transformer's standard normalization for condition-controlled AdaLN (Peebles & Xie, 2023). It also adapts classifier-free guidance (Ho & Salimans, 2022) into a predictor-free formulation that directly estimates clean-graph probabilities under multi-property conditions, departing from the predictor-based conditioning used in MOOD and DiGress v2. The clustering-based encoding for numerical conditions and the explicit treatment of mixed categorical/numerical condition sets represent a deliberate move beyond the single-condition or combined-objective formulations in prior molecular optimization work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Multi-conditional molecular generation is a practically critical bottleneck in drug and materials discovery, where designers must simultaneously satisfy synthesizability, target activity, and physical properties. The finding that predictor-free AdaLN conditioning outperforms explicit predictor guidance (MOOD, DiGress v2) challenges the prevailing assumption that additional property predictors improve controllability, and the graph-dependent noise model addresses a structural mismatch in how discrete diffusion corrupts graph data. Limitations include a trade-off between diversity and distribution fidelity, reliance on oracle-based evaluation (random forest proxies for true properties), and restriction to 2D graphs up to 50 atoms, leaving open questions about scaling to 3D conformations, larger polymers, and more complex condition interdependencies.
