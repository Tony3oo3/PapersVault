## Problem

Spectrograms and natural images occupy very different visual distributions, so playing a natural image as a spectrogram produces unnatural sounds, and existing 'spectrogram art' by musicians suffers a steep trade-off between image fidelity and audio quality. No prior method had demonstrated how to automatically synthesize a single 2D signal that is simultaneously a semantically meaningful image and a natural-sounding audio spectrogram, especially without paired image-sound training data.

## Input / Output

The method takes two free-form text prompts — one describing a desired visual scene (e.g., 'a painting of tigers, grayscale') and one describing a desired sound (e.g., 'tiger growling') — and produces a 256×1024 grayscale log-mel spectrogram corresponding to 10 seconds of audio. This spectrogram can be decoded into a waveform via a pretrained HiFi-GAN vocoder (or Griffin-Lim), or optionally colorized into an RGB image whose grayscale version remains the playable spectrogram. The latent representation is 4×32×128, a shape the image model was not explicitly trained on but still handles well.

## Method

The key insight is that Stable Diffusion v1.5 (image) and Auffusion (audio) share an identical latent space because Auffusion is finetuned from Stable Diffusion, enabling zero-shot composition without any paired data. At each DDIM denoising step, the method computes two classifier-free-guided noise estimates — one from the image model conditioned on the visual prompt and one from the audio model conditioned on the audio prompt — and combines them via a weighted average to form a multimodal noise estimate that steers the sample toward the intersection of both distributions (formulated as a product of experts, p_av(x) ∝ p_a(x)·p_v(x)). A warm-starting schedule lets one modality denoise alone for the first fraction of timesteps (e.g., audio-only for the first 10%) to establish high-level structure before both models jointly refine the sample. The entire pipeline is zero-shot: no fine-tuning, no paired image-sound data, and no architectural modifications to either pretrained model are required.

## Results

On 100 prompt pairs (10 image × 10 audio prompts from VGGSound), the proposed method achieves CLIP 28.2%, CLAP 33.5%, FID 226.46, and FAD 19.21, outperforming both baselines on all four metrics. The method approaches single-modality upper bounds: Stable Diffusion scores CLIP 34.5% (visual ceiling) and Auffusion scores CLAP 48.3% (audio ceiling). In human studies (N=100), the method achieves 77.4% average win-rate over SDS and 78.9% over Imprint on alignment, with 2–3× higher selection rates than baselines across audio quality, visual quality, and visual-audio sync.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two proposed baselines and two single-modality upper bounds. Against the SDS baseline (multimodal score distillation sampling using DeepFloyd IF + Auffusion), the proposed method wins on every metric: CLIP 28.2% vs. 25.4%, CLAP 33.5% vs. 23.4%, FID 226.46 vs. 273.03, and FAD 19.21 vs. 32.57; SDS also requires ~2 hours per sample versus 10 seconds. Against the Imprint baseline (subtracting a generated image from a generated spectrogram), the method again wins across the board: CLIP 28.2% vs. 27.2%, CLAP 33.5% vs. 32.3%, FID 226.46 vs. 244.84, and FAD 19.21 vs. 29.42. Relative to the single-modality benchmarks, the method achieves 82% of Stable Diffusion's CLIP score (28.2 vs. 34.5) and 69% of Auffusion's CLAP score (33.5 vs. 48.3), confirming it samples from the intersection of both distributions rather than collapsing to one modality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the compositional generation framework for diffusion models—where noise estimates from multiple models are summed to sample from a product of experts distribution (Liu et al., ECCV 2022; Du et al., NeurIPS 2020)—to the cross-modal setting, composing a text-to-image model (Stable Diffusion) with a text-to-spectrogram model (Auffusion) that share a latent space. It also draws on Factorized Diffusion (Geng et al., 2024) for the colorization step and connects to the audio steganography tradition (Aphex Twin, Venetian Snares) by automating what artists previously achieved manually. The key departure from prior compositional work is that the two models are trained on fundamentally different data distributions (natural images vs. log-mel spectrograms) rather than different conditional distributions within a single modality.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates a non-trivial overlap between the distributions of natural images and natural spectrograms, opening a new direction for multimodal art and generative modeling at the intersection of vision and audio. However, the method cannot simultaneously achieve high fidelity in both modalities, and its performance is bounded by the weaker audio diffusion model. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond the specific Stable Diffusion/Auffusion pair, whether higher-quality audio models would improve results, and whether the steganographic capability (hiding images in audio) raises meaningful safety concerns. The simplicity of the method (weighted averaging of noise estimates in a shared latent space) makes it an accessible template for future cross-modal composition experiments.
