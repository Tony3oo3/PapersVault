## Problem

Classical PDE solvers require fine mesh grids and small time steps for stability and convergence, incurring prohibitive computational cost, while pure deep learning approaches demand rich labeled data and lack interpretability and generalizability. Existing physics-encoded or hybrid numerical-NN methods either fail on coarse grids or use non-trainable numerical components that still require abundant training data. The paper addresses the gap of efficiently predicting spatiotemporal PDE dynamics on coarse grids with only 3–5 training trajectories while maintaining high accuracy and generalizability across initial conditions, force terms, and PDE parameters.

## Input / Output

The model consumes coarse solution states (velocity fields downsampled spatially by 4×–1024× and temporally by 1×–32× from high-resolution DNS, e.g., 25×25 for Burgers, 64×64 for NS), along with the external force term and Reynolds number as a 2D embedding feature map. It autoregressively produces the predicted coarse solution at the next time step via RK4 integration, and for the NS case additionally computes the pressure field on the fly via a spectral Poisson solver without requiring pressure labels. Training uses only 3–5 trajectories per PDE system with MSE loss on the downsampled ground truth.

## Method

P²C²Net combines two synergistic paths: a trainable PDE block that marches the coarse solution forward using a fourth-order Runge-Kutta integrator, and an FNO-based correction block that refines the solution on the fly to restore information lost at reduced resolution. The key architectural innovation is a learnable 5×5 symmetric convolution filter with only 7 free parameters, designed to satisfy sum rules of order (1,0) and achieve fourth-order accuracy for first-order spatial derivatives on coarse grids—making the PDE block trainable (unlike fixed numerical schemes in prior hybrid methods) while preserving interpretability. Boundary conditions are hard-encoded via periodic padding, and the Reynolds number is embedded as a trainable 2D feature map in both blocks to enable cross-Re generalization. The full model is trained end-to-end with a simple MSE loss in an autoregressive rollout setting, requiring no PDE residual regularization or auxiliary physics losses.

## Results

P²C²Net achieves state-of-the-art performance across all four PDE benchmarks with at least 50% improvement over the best baseline on every metric. On Burgers (25×25 grid), it reaches RMSE 0.0064 and HCT 1.4 s, outperforming the next-best PeRCNN (RMSE 0.0967, HCT 0.45 s) by 93.4% in RMSE and 211.7% in HCT. On the challenging Navier-Stokes case (64×64 grid, Re=1000), P²C²Net attains RMSE 0.3533 and HCT 7.20 s, beating UNet (RMSE 0.8224, HCT 3.96 s) by 57.0% in RMSE and 81.6% in HCT. The model requires only 3–5 training trajectories per dataset and operates on heavily downsampled grids (e.g., 2048²→64² for NS).

## Baselines & Comparisons

P²C²Net decisively outperforms all five baselines (FNO, UNet, DeepONet, PeRCNN, and LI) on every dataset and every metric. Against FNO, the closest pure neural-operator baseline, P²C²Net reduces RMSE by 93.4% on Burgers (0.0064 vs 0.0980) and by 65% on NS (0.3533 vs 1.0100); FNO diverges entirely (NaN) on the sparse Gray-Scott system. UNet is the strongest baseline on FN (RMSE 0.1730) and NS (RMSE 0.8224), yet P²C²Net still improves RMSE by 77.5% and 57.0% respectively, while extending HCT from 6.5 s to 10 s (FN) and from 3.96 s to 7.20 s (NS). PeRCNN, the closest physics-encoded competitor, is beaten by 91.5% in RMSE on GS (0.0135 vs 0.1586) and by 93.4% on Burgers. DeepONet and LI show the weakest performance, with LI diverging on NS (HCT only 0.60 s) and DeepONet failing to maintain stability beyond very short rollouts on most cases. The consistent >50% gain across all four systems confirms that the combination of a trainable PDE block with symmetric Conv filters and an FNO-based correction module provides a substantial advantage over both pure neural and hybrid numerical approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

P²C²Net sits at the intersection of physics-encoded learning (PeRCNN), hybrid numerical-neural solvers (Learned Interpolation), and neural operators (FNO). It departs from PeRCNN by making the PDE block trainable via a symmetric convolution filter that preserves finite-difference stencil properties while adapting to coarse grids, and from LI by rendering the numerical component learnable, thereby reducing the labeled-data requirement from hundreds of trajectories to 3–5. The architectural pattern of a white-box RK4-integrated PDE block coupled with a black-box FNO correction module is the paper's core contribution, unifying interpretability (the PDE block) with flexibility (the correction block) in a way neither prior family achieves alone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ability to simulate complex PDE dynamics (turbulent NS at Re=1000, reaction-diffusion patterns) on grids downsampled by up to 1024× spatially with only 3–5 training trajectories has direct practical value for scientific computing where high-resolution DNS is prohibitively expensive. The symmetric-filter design, grounded in sum-rule/vanishing-moment theory, offers a principled way to make derivative estimation trainable without sacrificing order of accuracy. Key limitations constrain broader adoption: the method is restricted to 2D regular grids with periodic boundaries, and the Poisson block is specific to incompressible NS. Extending to 3D, irregular meshes, and non-periodic boundary conditions are the natural next steps the authors identify.
