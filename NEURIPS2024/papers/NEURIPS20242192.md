## Problem

FlashAttention-2, despite eliminating intermediate global memory traffic, achieves only 35% utilization on the Hopper H100 GPU compared to 80–85% for optimized GEMM kernels, because it adheres to a simplified synchronous execution model and does not exploit Hopper's asynchronous Tensor Cores (WGMMA), Tensor Memory Accelerator (TMA), or FP8 low-precision hardware. The paper addresses how to redesign the attention algorithm to close this gap by leveraging asynchrony and low-precision in a way that respects the sequential dependencies between GEMM and softmax operations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242192/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The kernel consumes Q, K, V matrices of shape N×d (one head) in HBM, partitioned into query blocks of size B_r×d and key/value blocks of B_c×d, and produces the attention output O (N×d) and a logsumexp vector L (N) needed for the backward pass. For FP8 operation, Q and K must be contiguous in the head dimension while V must be contiguous in the sequence dimension (k-major layout for FP8 WGMMA), which the kernel handles via in-kernel SMEM transposition of V tiles. The algorithm is embarrassingly parallel across batch, number of heads, and query sequence length, with each CTA processing one query tile independently.

## Method

FlashAttention-3 introduces three complementary techniques for Hopper GPUs. First, a warp-specialized producer-consumer scheme with pingpong scheduling splits the CTA into producer warps (issuing TMA loads into a circular SMEM buffer) and consumer warps (executing WGMMA GEMMs and softmax), with synchronization barriers forcing the softmax of one warpgroup to overlap the GEMMs of another. Second, a 2-stage intra-warpgroup pipeline breaks the sequential dependency between GEMM and softmax by issuing the next iteration's QK^T WGMMA asynchronously before waiting on the current iteration's PV WGMMA, so softmax on S_next executes concurrently with the PV GEMM on P_cur. Third, for FP8, the algorithm addresses layout conformance (transforming FP32 accumulator register ownership to FP8 operand layout via byte_perm and shuffle instructions) and accuracy via block quantization (one scale per B_r×d or B_c×d block, fused with rotary embedding) and incoherent processing (multiplying Q and K by a random Hadamard-based orthogonal matrix before quantization to spread outliers), achieving 2.6× lower RMSE than per-tensor FP8 quantization.

## Results

FlashAttention-3 achieves 1.5–2.0× speedup over FlashAttention-2 in the BF16 forward pass on H100, reaching up to 840 TFLOPs/s (85% of theoretical peak), and 1.5–1.75× speedup in the backward pass. In FP8, it reaches 1.3 PFLOPs/s. For numerical accuracy, FP16 FlashAttention-3 matches FlashAttention-2 (RMSE 1.9e-4 vs. 1.9e-4, both 1.7× better than standard attention at 3.2e-4), while FP8 FlashAttention-3 with block quantization and incoherent processing achieves 2.6× lower RMSE (9.1e-3) than the per-tensor-quantized FP8 baseline (2.4e-2).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against FlashAttention-2, FlashAttention-3 wins decisively on speed: 1.5–2.0× faster in the BF16 forward pass and 1.5–1.75× faster in the backward pass across sequence lengths from 512 to 16k, with the gap widening at longer sequences. Against NVIDIA's cuDNN (a closed-source, H100-optimized FlashAttention-2 implementation), FlashAttention-3 in BF16 surpasses cuDNN for medium and long sequences (≥1k tokens), while FP8 FlashAttention-3 is roughly on par. Against a standard PyTorch attention implementation, FlashAttention-3 is up to 3–16× faster. On numerical accuracy, FP16 FlashAttention-3 ties FlashAttention-2 exactly (both 1.9e-4 RMSE) and both outperform standard attention (3.2e-4) by 1.7×; in FP8, FlashAttention-3 with block quantization and incoherent processing is 2.6× more accurate than the per-tensor-quantized baseline, with incoherent processing contributing the larger share of the error reduction.

## Relation to Prior Work

FlashAttention-3 extends the FlashAttention/FlashAttention-2 lineage (Dao et al. 2022, Dao 2023) by redesigning the algorithm around Hopper's asynchronous execution model: the synchronous tiling strategy of FA2 is replaced with warp-specialized producer-consumer pipelining (TMA for data movement, WGMMA for GEMM) and a 2-stage GEMM-softmax overlap that breaks sequential dependencies within the inner loop. For FP8, the paper adapts incoherent processing from QuIP/QuIP# (Chee et al. 2024, Tseng et al. 2024) and introduces block quantization to mitigate outlier-induced quantization error, while also solving the non-trivial register-layout conformance problem between FP32 WGMMA accumulators and FP8 operand requirements. Conceptually, the paper departs from FA2's simplified synchronous model to one that explicitly exploits hardware specialization (separate Tensor Core, TMA, and CUDA core units), a shift also seen in concurrent work like ThunderKittens and cuDNN 9 but taken further here with the pipelined softmax overlap.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Attention is the dominant computational bottleneck in LLM training and long-context inference, so a 1.5–2× speedup on the H100 (the current workhorse GPU) with 85% Tensor Core utilization has immediate and broad practical impact across the field. The FP8 path at 1.3 PFLOPs/s with only 2.6× higher error than BF16 (and 2.6× lower error than naive per-tensor FP8) opens a credible route to near-doubled throughput for training, though the paper explicitly flags that the effects of low-precision attention on large-scale training stability remain unvalidated. Open questions include whether the warp-specialization and pipelining techniques transfer cleanly to Blackwell (FP4) or other accelerators, how the approach performs for decode-phase inference (addressed separately via Flash-Decoding and GQA packing), and whether the 3-stage pipeline's underperformance (due to compiler reordering) can be resolved with future toolchain improvements.
