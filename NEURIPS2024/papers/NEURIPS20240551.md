## Problem

Robust average cost MDPs aim to minimize the worst-case per-step cost over an uncertainty set of transition kernels, but no policy-based algorithm with global convergence guarantees and finite iteration complexity existed for general uncertainty sets. Prior work either addressed only discounted cost settings, restricted to specific uncertainty sets (e.g., R-contamination or l1), or relied on value iteration methods without convergence rate characterizations. This paper fills that gap by providing the first policy optimization algorithm with provable global convergence for robust average cost MDPs under general (s,a)-rectangular uncertainty sets.

## Input / Output

The algorithm requires a model-based oracle that, given a policy π, returns the robust relative state-action value function Q_P^π (computed under the worst-case transition kernel). The uncertainty set is (s,a)-rectangular, defined by a divergence D (e.g., KL) between each state-action transition distribution and a nominal kernel P_0 with radius R. The output is a stationary randomized policy π* minimizing the worst-case average cost g_P^π, which is independent of the initial state under the ergodicity assumption. The algorithm operates on finite state and action spaces with direct policy parameterization (each state maps to a distribution over actions).

## Method

The paper derives the Fréchet sub-gradient of the robust average cost as ∇g_P^π(s,a) = d_P^π(s)·Q_P^π(s,a), leveraging the performance difference lemma for average cost MDPs and the Lipschitz continuity of the relative value function to handle the non-differentiability introduced by the max over transition kernels. Based on this sub-gradient, the robust policy mirror descent updates each state's policy via a Bregman divergence minimization weighted by the stationary distribution under the worst-case kernel, decoupling the per-state updates. The convergence analysis establishes a lower bound on the cost difference between two policies and proves the robust average cost satisfies the Polyak-Łojasiewicz condition, enabling a novel proof that bypasses the smoothness requirement unavailable in the robust setting. With increasing step size, the algorithm achieves linear convergence in the optimality gap; with constant step size η = 1/L_π, it reaches an ε-optimal policy in O(1/ε) iterations, matching the best known rate for non-robust policy mirror descent.

## Results

The paper's main theoretical contribution establishes that robust policy mirror descent achieves linear convergence in the optimality gap with increasing step size (Theorem 4.6) and converges to an ε-optimal policy with O(1/ε) iteration complexity under constant step size η = 1/L_π (Theorem 4.7). Empirically, on the Garnet(3,2) environment, the robust method converges faster than the non-robust baseline but both reach similar final robust average cost values, attributed to the small environment size. On the larger Garnet(5,2), Garnet(10,5), and Recycling Robot problems, the robust method consistently achieves lower mean robust average cost than the non-robust baseline across all 5 random seeds, with results reported as mean ± standard deviation over 2000-step training episodes (100 episodes for Garnet, 300 for the robot).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares its robust policy mirror descent against the non-robust policy mirror descent of Xiao [45] (JMLR 2022), the state-of-the-art convergence rate for policy mirror descent with constant step size in the non-robust setting. On the small Garnet(3,2) environment, both methods converge to approximately the same robust average cost, though the robust method reaches it faster; the authors attribute the tie to the limited state space making model mismatch less impactful. On the three larger environments—Garnet(5,2), Garnet(10,5), and the Recycling Robot problem—the robust method outperforms the non-robust baseline in mean robust average cost across all trials, demonstrating that the robustness benefit becomes more pronounced as the MDP complexity increases. The comparison uses identical hyperparameters (η=0.01, KL-divergence uncertainty set with R=0.1, uniform random initialization), isolating the effect of the robustness mechanism itself. Notably, the non-robust baseline does not account for the worst-case transition kernel, so its reported cost is evaluated under the same worst-case kernel, making the comparison a direct measure of robustness gain.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the robust policy mirror descent framework of Li et al. [21] from the discounted cost setting to the average cost setting, where the discount factor no longer provides a contraction and the objective becomes non-differentiable due to the max over the uncertainty set. The convergence analysis draws on the gradient-mapping and PL-condition techniques from Xiao [45] for non-robust policy mirror descent, but replaces the smoothness assumption (which fails for the robust objective) with a novel combination of a lower bound on the cost difference between policies and the first-order optimality condition of the mirror descent update. The paper also departs from prior robust average cost work [36, 22, 39, 40, 12], which relied on value iteration or restricted uncertainty sets, by providing the first policy-based algorithm with global convergence guarantees for general uncertainty sets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first policy-based algorithm with finite iteration complexity for robust average cost MDPs with general uncertainty sets, filling a gap where only value-iteration-based methods existed without clear stopping criteria or complexity bounds. The O(1/ε) convergence rate with constant step size matches the best non-robust counterpart, showing that robustness does not inherently degrade the convergence rate of policy optimization. The main limitation is the model-based setting (requiring an oracle for the robust Q-function); extending to model-free algorithms is identified as future work. The empirical validation is limited to small Garnet benchmarks and a single robot problem, leaving open questions about scalability to larger or continuous-state environments.
