## Problem

Training Concept Activation Vectors (CAVs) for interpretability requires large numbers of high-quality labeled images per concept, making CAVs impractical for arbitrary or numerous concepts. This data-scarcity bottleneck limits CAVs to a small predefined set of concepts and degrades their quality when training images are few, hindering both faithful concept representation and downstream model correction on large-scale datasets.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241249/cropped_page1_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a free-text concept description (e.g., 'a cat animal with orange stripes'), a pool of ~1000 probe images selected as the most and least activated by the VL model, and a frozen pre-trained target model backbone. It produces a single CAV vector in the target model's feature space (dimension D_f), whose activation values on any image approximate the VL model's concept presence score. For model correction, the trained LG-CAVs additionally produce per-sample reweighting coefficients that modulate the classification loss during fine-tuning of only the final linear layer.

## Method

LG-CAV trains a CAV by minimizing the MSE between its activation values (cosine similarity with target-model features) on probe images and the corresponding VL model's text-encoding activation values on the same images, thereby transferring concept knowledge across incompatible feature spaces without direct feature mapping. Three modules refine this: Gaussian Alignment (GA) rescales VL-model activations to match the target model's activation distribution via a linear transform between fitted Gaussians; Concept Ensemble (CE) averages text features from ~30 augmented prompts to enrich the concept representation; and Deviation Sample Reweighting (DSR) up-weights probe images whose target-model features show low variance in similarity to ground-truth positive images. For model correction, an Activation Sample Reweighting (ASR) module assigns higher training weights to class-k images that are more strongly activated by the LG-CAV of k's strongly-related concept, softening spurious correlations during final-layer fine-tuning without touching the backbone.

## Results

On the Broden dataset, LG-CAV (with all modules: GA+CE+DSR) achieves 77.45% concept accuracy on ResNet-18 and 79.07% on DenseNet-121, outperforming the best prior method OA-TCAV (72.62% and 73.90%) by 4.8 and 5.2 points respectively. For concept-to-class accuracy, LG-CAV reaches 24.58% on ResNet-18 and 27.72% on ViT-L, surpassing OA-TCAV (10.11% and 10.68%) by more than 2×. On full ImageNet model correction, LG-CAV improves top-1 accuracy by up to 0.68 points over the original model (e.g., 70.26% vs. 69.76% on ResNet-18, 81.38% vs. 81.07% on ViT-B), outperforming Concept_Distillation, Knowledge Distillation, and Label-free CBM across all nine backbones.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LG-CAV decisively outperforms all three CAV baselines on both concept accuracy and concept-to-class accuracy across all nine backbones. Against Original CAV [16], the gap is largest on ViT backbones (e.g., +5.17 points concept accuracy on ViT-B, +18.90 points concept-to-class accuracy on ViT-L), reflecting that the original method degrades most severely under data scarcity on transformer architectures. Text-to-Concept [23] also leverages CLIP but uses a single linear projection to map features, yielding only modest gains over Original CAV; LG-CAV's per-concept optimization with Gaussian alignment provides a 5–7 point advantage. OA-TCAV [40] uses adversarial training but still suffers from data scarcity, remaining 4–5 points below LG-CAV in concept accuracy. For model correction on full ImageNet, LG-CAV beats Concept_Distillation [11] (which directly aligns gradients with CAVs and actually hurts performance on ResNet-18: 69.46% vs. 70.26%), Knowledge Distillation [13] (69.93% vs. 70.26% on ResNet-18), and Label-free CBM [26] (71.95% on ResNet-50 only, N/A elsewhere). On CUB-200-2011, LG-CAV (77.31% on ResNet-18) surpasses PCBM [47] (58.80%), Trustworthy CBM [15] (68.30%), and Label-free CBM [26] (74.31%), while on ImageNet-40 it outperforms HiBug [4] (91.16% vs. 90.38% on ResNet-18).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the CAV framework (Kim et al., ICML 2018) by replacing the need for labeled concept images with language-guided supervision from a pre-trained vision-language model (CLIP). It is most closely related to Text-to-Concept (Moayeri et al., ICML 2023), which also leverages CLIP for CAV training but via a single linear projection shared across all concepts, whereas LG-CAV performs per-concept optimization through activation-value matching on probe images. The paper also departs from prior CAV work (OA-TCAV, Concept Gradient) that focuses solely on interpretation by introducing a model correction application using activation sample reweighting, a use case not explored in the CAV literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the need for large curated concept image sets, LG-CAV makes CAV-based interpretability practical for arbitrary concepts described in natural language, significantly lowering the barrier to entry for model auditing. The model correction application demonstrates that high-quality CAVs can improve classifier accuracy in an interpretable manner, though the gains are modest (0.5–0.9 points on ImageNet). Open questions include whether the Gaussian alignment assumption generalizes to non-Gaussian activation distributions, whether the method handles abstract or compositional concepts well, and whether the approach extends to non-visual modalities. The reliance on a frozen VL model also raises questions about how well the transferred concept knowledge captures domain-specific nuances absent from CLIP's pre-training data.
