## Problem

Existing cutting-plane methods for neural network verification, such as GCP-CROWN, rely on external MIP solvers to generate cuts, which fail to scale to large networks (14M–31M+ parameters). This paper addresses the gap by showing that the branch-and-bound search process itself contains rich structural information—verified subproblems reveal infeasible combinations of ReLU states—that can be exploited to generate effective, scalable cutting planes without any MIP solver.

## Input / Output

The method takes a ReLU neural network, an ℓ∞ perturbation ball (defined by a baseline input x₀ and radius ε), and a verification specification (e.g., robustness: the true-label logit must exceed all others). It outputs a verification verdict (UNSAT if the property holds for all inputs in the ball, Unknown otherwise) along with a lower bound on the verification objective. As a byproduct, it produces BaB inferred cutting planes—linear inequalities over relaxed ReLU indicator variables z∈[0,1]—which are globally valid across all subproblems and can be shared with MIP-based cuts from GCP-CROWN.

## Method

BICCOS generates cutting planes on the fly during branch-and-bound by encoding verified (UNSAT) subproblems as linear constraints that preclude the specific combination of active/inactive ReLU states along that path (Proposition 3.1). Because naive cuts are often redundant in the ongoing search (e.g., a neuron already split to active makes the cut trivially satisfied), the method applies a Neuron Elimination Heuristic: it computes per-neuron influence scores from bound improvements, retains neurons with positive Lagrange multipliers μ or τ, and drops a configurable percentile of low-influence neurons, then re-verifies to confirm the reduced constraint set still proves UNSAT, yielding a strictly stronger cut (Corollary 3.3). A multi-tree search presolving step explores multiple shallow BaB trees in parallel to discover additional cuts before the main search, with cuts shared across all trees. The bound propagation is extended (Theorem 3.2) to correctly handle fixed z variables from branching, and the search uses breadth-first ordering to generate more general cuts from shallower nodes earlier.

## Results

On the VNN-COMP 2024 benchmarks, BICCOS verifies 125/168 instances on cifar100-2024 and 140/157 on tinyimagenet-2024, outperforming β-CROWN (119 and 135) and GCP-CROWN with MIP cuts (119 and 134). On the SDP-FO benchmarks, BICCOS achieves 75.5% verified accuracy on MNIST CNN-A-Adv (vs. 71.0% for β-CROWN and 70.5% for GCP-CROWN) and 54.5% on CIFAR CNN-B-Adv (vs. 47.5% and 49.0%). Notably, GCP-CROWN with MIP cuts cannot scale to the large cifar100 and tinyimagenet networks (14.4–31.6M parameters), while BICCOS handles them without an MIP solver.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BICCOS outperforms all 11 baselines on the VNN-COMP benchmarks, achieving the highest number of verified instances across all four benchmark sets. Against the closest competitors, BICCOS verifies 1 more instance than GCP-CROWN on oval22 (26 vs. 25), 3 more on cifar100-2024 (125 vs. 119), and 5 more on tinyimagenet-2024 (140 vs. 134 for GCP-CROWN, 135 for β-CROWN). On the SDP-FO benchmarks, BICCOS surpasses β-CROWN by 4.5–7.0 percentage points and GCP-CROWN by 1.0–5.5 percentage points in verified accuracy across all seven models. GCP-CROWN with MIP cuts is the strongest baseline on smaller networks but fails to scale to cifar100 and tinyimagenet models due to MIP solver limitations, whereas BICCOS generates cuts without any MIP solver. Among non-CROWN baselines, VeriNet and MN-BaB are the closest on oval22 (17 and 19 verified) but are far behind BICCOS (26) and do not support the 2024 benchmarks. The ablation shows that Multi-Tree Search (MTS) is the most impactful component, boosting CIFAR CNN-B-Adv verified accuracy from 52.0% (base) to 54.5% (with MTS), while MIP cuts provide additional gains on smaller models where the MIP solver can still operate.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the β-CROWN bound propagation framework and the GCP-CROWN cutting-plane methodology for neural network verification. While GCP-CROWN relied on external MIP solvers to generate generic cuts (e.g., Gomory cuts) that fail to scale beyond small networks, BICCOS instead derives cuts directly from UNSAT subproblems discovered during the branch-and-bound search, eliminating the MIP dependency. The approach also departs from Venus/Venus2, which explored dependency-based constraints among neurons but did not extract them from verified BaB subproblems or apply the constraint-strengthening mechanism proposed here. The multi-tree search presolving step and the BFS-based tree traversal strategy represent further departures from standard single-tree DFS BaB verifiers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

BICCOS addresses a critical scalability bottleneck: MIP-based cutting planes cannot handle networks with 14–31 million parameters, which are common in modern benchmarks (CIFAR-100, Tiny-ImageNet). By generating cuts on-the-fly from the BaB tree itself, the method scales to these large architectures while maintaining soundness, and it won VNN-COMP 2024. Key limitations include the overhead of multi-tree search and constraint strengthening (2–20 seconds), which can hurt performance on very easy instances with short timeouts, and the heuristic nature of the neuron elimination step, which may not always find the optimal subset of constraints. Open questions include whether SMT-based conflict learning (DPLL/CDCL) can be systematically translated into BICCOS-style cuts, and whether the approach extends to non-ReLU activation functions.
