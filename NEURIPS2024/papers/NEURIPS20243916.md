## Problem

Existing two-stage multi-object 3D grounding methods like M3DRef-CLIP rely on a fixed maximum number of box proposals, hand-selected fixed camera poses for 2D rendering, and a fusion module that does not explicitly reason over spatial relationships among objects. These design choices lead to redundant or missed proposals, suboptimal 2D features for varying object sizes, and poor handling of spatially-referenced queries with distractors.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243916/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a 3D point cloud (point coordinates, normals, and per-point multi-view features, S ∈ R^{H×(3+3+128)}) and a natural language description. It produces a variable-size set of 3D bounding boxes whose cardinality is determined at inference time by a learned proposal probability threshold and a prediction threshold τ_pred, rather than being fixed a priori. The 2D auxiliary features are extracted by a frozen CLIP ViT-B/32 image encoder from rendered views of each proposal box.

## Method

D-LISA is a two-stage detection-and-selection pipeline built on the PointGroup detector backbone, introducing three modules. First, a dynamic box proposal module learns a per-candidate probability α_m = Sigmoid(Linear(s_m)) and applies NMS after thresholding, with a regularization loss L_dyn = Σα_m that penalizes the expected number of proposals, reducing the average from 62.4 to 30.5 boxes entering the fusion stage. Second, a dynamic multi-view renderer predicts scene-conditioned camera pose offsets via per-view MLPs conditioned on the average box size, added to predefined base poses, so that 2D renderings adapt to the scale of objects in each scene. Third, a language-informed spatial attention (LISA) block modifies standard self-attention by interpolating between the QK^T/√d term and an inverse-distance spatial matrix D using per-object spatial scores β_i = Sigmoid(Linear(g ⊕ f_i)), where g is the sentence feature and f_i is the object feature, enabling the model to dynamically weight spatial proximity reasoning based on the text query. Training combines PointGroup detection loss, a Hungarian-matched binary cross-entropy reference loss, a symmetric contrastive loss between object and word embeddings, and the dynamic proposal loss.

## Results

On the primary Multi3DRefer benchmark (multi-object 3D grounding), D-LISA achieves 51.2% F1@0.5 overall, a 12.8% absolute improvement over M3DRef-CLIP (38.4%) and 1.9% over M3DRef-CLIP w/NMS (49.3%). The largest gains appear in the Multiple Targets (MT) category (51.0 vs. 49.1) and Single Target with Distractors (ST w/D) (42.5 vs. 40.0). On single-object grounding, D-LISA sets a new state-of-the-art on ScanRefer with 46.9% Acc@0.5 (val) and 46.3% (test), and achieves 53.1% accuracy on Nr3D, outperforming all prior two-stage methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

D-LISA outperforms all 23 compared baselines across all three benchmarks. On Multi3DRefer, the closest competitor is M3DRef-CLIP w/NMS (49.3% F1@0.5), which D-LISA surpasses by 1.9 points; the original M3DRef-CLIP without NMS trails by 12.8 points (38.4%). Earlier two-stage methods like 3DVG-Trans (25.5%), D3Net (32.2%), and 3DJCG (26.6%) are substantially outperformed, with D-LISA gaining 19–26 points over them. On ScanRefer, D-LISA (46.9% val / 46.3% test Acc@0.5) edges out M3DRef-CLIP w/NMS (45.7% val) and M3DRef-CLIP (44.7% val / 45.5% test), and leads CORE-3DVG (43.8% val) and HAM (40.6% val / 40.1% test) by 3–6 points. On Nr3D, D-LISA (53.1%) beats M3DRef-CLIP (49.4%) by 3.7 points and HAM (48.2%) by 4.9 points, though it trails one-stage methods like 3DRP-Net (65.9%) and ViL3DRef (64.4%) that assume ground-truth proposals are provided.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on M3DRef-CLIP (Zhang et al., ICCV 2023), which introduced the Multi3DRefer benchmark and the two-stage detection-and-selection paradigm for multi-object 3D grounding. D-LISA departs from M3DRef-CLIP in three targeted ways: replacing the fixed maximum number of box proposals with a learned dynamic selection, substituting hand-selected fixed camera poses for scene-conditioned rendering viewpoints, and augmenting the standard transformer fusion with a language-informed spatial attention mechanism that explicitly balances visual attention weights against pairwise object distances. The spatial attention design also positions itself against prior relational-graph approaches (3DVG-Trans, ViL3DRef, CORE-3DVG) by using a simpler distance-only relation representation conditioned on language rather than hand-crafted spatial features.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Multi-object 3D grounding is a practically important capability for robotics and human-computer interaction, and the 12.8% absolute F1 improvement over M3DRef-CLIP (though largely attributable to adding NMS, with the three novel modules contributing ~2% over the NMS baseline) demonstrates that the two-stage pipeline still has meaningful headroom. The dynamic proposal and dynamic camera pose modules are general-purpose components that could be adopted in other two-stage 3D grounding or detection pipelines to reduce redundant computation. A key limitation acknowledged by the authors is that performance remains upper-bounded by the PointGroup detector quality, and the method sacrifices some accuracy on simpler single-object settings compared to the baseline. Open questions include whether the dynamic proposal mechanism can be extended to fully end-to-end training and whether the language-informed spatial attention generalizes to more complex relational queries beyond pairwise distances.
