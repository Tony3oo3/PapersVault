## Problem

Existing semantic entropy methods for LLM uncertainty quantification capture only binary equivalence relations between generated answers, treating all non-equivalent pairs as equally distant in semantic space. This fails to distinguish, for example, 'apple' from 'granny smith' versus 'apple' from 'house', leading to overestimated uncertainty when an LLM produces semantically similar but not strictly identical answers. The paper addresses this gap by incorporating a continuous distance metric in the semantic space of generations.

## Input / Output

Given a text query, the method samples N answers (N=10 in experiments) from a target LLM using top-K and nucleus sampling, then computes pairwise NLI predictions (entailment, neutral, contradiction) via DeBERTa-Large-MNLI to build a weighted semantic graph. The output is a single scalar uncertainty score — the von Neumann entropy of a unit-trace positive semidefinite kernel matrix constructed over the graph. The method operates in both white-box settings (using token likelihoods for cluster probabilities) and black-box settings (using empirical cluster frequencies), requiring no access to model internals beyond generated text.

## Method

KLE defines uncertainty as the von Neumann entropy (VNE) of a unit-trace positive semidefinite kernel over LLM generations or semantic clusters, where the kernel encodes fine-grained semantic similarity rather than binary equivalence. Concretely, NLI predictions form a weighted semantic graph, and graph kernels — heat kernels (e^{-tL}) or Matérn kernels ((2ν/κ²I + L)^{-ν}) derived from the graph Laplacian L — are computed and normalized to unit trace. The VNE reduces to the Shannon entropy of the kernel's eigenvalues, capturing how 'spread out' the semantic mass is across the answer space. Two variants are provided: KLE operates on individual texts, while KLE-c operates on semantic clusters for lower computational cost. The paper proves (Thm. 3.5) that KLE generalizes semantic entropy by showing a block-diagonal kernel recovers SE exactly, while non-block-diagonal kernels (e.g., heat kernels) capture additional similarity structure that SE cannot represent.

## Results

Across 60 experimental scenarios (12 LLMs × 5 datasets), KLE(K_HEAT) consistently achieves the best AUROC and AUARC scores. On the largest models, KLE(K_HEAT) reaches 0.87 AUROC on BioASQ and 0.84 on TriviaQA with Llama-2 70B Chat, and 0.92 AUROC on BioASQ with Falcon 40B Instruct, outperforming the strongest baseline P(True) (0.86 and 0.87 respectively). The method also works in black-box settings (no token-level probabilities required), and default hyperparameters selected via entropy convergence plots match validation-set-tuned performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

KLE(K_HEAT) outperforms all five baselines across the 60 experimental scenarios, with pairwise win rates significantly above chance (p < 0.05 by binomial test). Against Semantic Entropy (SE) and Discrete Semantic Entropy (DSE), KLE gains the most on TriviaQA (0.84 vs 0.77 AUROC for Llama-2 70B) and BioASQ (0.92 vs 0.85 for Falcon 40B), where fine-grained semantic similarity between non-equivalent answers matters most. Against P(True), the closest competitor, KLE(K_HEAT) wins on BioASQ (0.87 vs 0.86) and TriviaQA (0.84 vs 0.76) for Llama-2 70B, and on BioASQ (0.92 vs 0.87) and TriviaQA (0.80 vs 0.72) for Falcon 40B, though P(True) remains competitive on NQ and SQuAD. Token Predictive Entropy (PE) is consistently the weakest baseline, trailing KLE by 10–20 AUROC points on most datasets. Embedding Regression (ER) underperforms KLE on all datasets for both models, particularly on NQ (0.58 vs 0.78 AUROC for Llama-2 70B). Notably, KLE(K_HEAT) requires no token-level probabilities, making it applicable to black-box LLMs where P(True) and PE cannot operate.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends Semantic Entropy (SE) by Kuhn et al., which estimates uncertainty via hard equivalence clustering of LLM outputs. The key conceptual departure is replacing binary semantic equivalence with a continuous similarity measure encoded by positive semidefinite kernels, drawing on graph kernel theory (heat and Matérn kernels from PDE/SPDE literature) and quantum information theory (von Neumann entropy). The authors prove KLE is a strict generalization of SE (Thm. 3.5) and also relate to Lin et al.'s use of semantic graph Laplacians for black-box UQ, though KLE's kernel framework is more flexible and expressive.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By moving from discrete equivalence classes to a continuous semantic similarity space, KLE captures the nuance that 'Paris' and 'the capital of France' are closer in meaning than 'Paris' and 'Rome'—something SE fundamentally cannot express. The kernel framework is modular (supports combining multiple NLI models, different graph kernels, and cluster-level or text-level variants), making it a practical drop-in replacement for SE in both white-box and black-box settings. Open questions include whether embedding-based kernels could replace NLI-based graphs, how the method performs on longer-form generation or code, and whether the NLI model's own errors (≈0.8 agreement with humans) become a bottleneck at scale.
