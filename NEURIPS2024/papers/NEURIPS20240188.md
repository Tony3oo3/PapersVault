## Problem

NetVLAD-based VPR methods suffer from two coupled issues: (1) cluster centers learned on a training distribution become inappropriate under domain shift, causing significant performance drops at inference, and (2) the typical use of 64 clusters yields very high-dimensional global descriptors (e.g., 32768-dim) that are costly for real-time retrieval. The paper asks whether a VLAD-style aggregation can be made both domain-robust and compact by eliminating the cluster-center mechanism and drastically reducing the number of clusters.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a single RGB place image (224×224 in training, 322×322 at inference) and produces a fixed-length L2-normalized global descriptor via nearest-neighbor retrieval. With the default 4-cluster SuperVLAD on DINOv2-base (patch dim 768), the output is a 3072-dim vector; the 1-Cluster VLAD variant outputs a 768-dim vector, matching the dimensionality of GeM pooling or the ViT class token while outperforming both. The descriptor is obtained by intra-normalizing each cluster row, flattening the K×D matrix, and applying a final L2 normalization.

## Method

SuperVLAD replaces NetVLAD's residual aggregation (sum of x_i − c_k weighted by soft-assignment) with a direct weighted sum of the local patch features assigned to each cluster, thereby removing all cluster-center parameters and halving the trainable parameters of the aggregation layer. The soft assignment is retained via learnable projection vectors w_k and biases b_k (a 1×1 convolution + softmax), so the mechanism is a cluster-assignment rather than an attention mechanism. Only 4 real clusters are used (plus 1 ghost cluster that participates in assignment but is discarded from the output), yielding a compact 3072-dim descriptor without PCA or linear projection. A 1-Cluster VLAD variant (K=1, G=2) produces a 768-dim descriptor by assigning all VPR-relevant features to a single cluster while two ghost clusters absorb irrelevant content. An optional cluster-wise cross-image encoder (two stacked transformer blocks) models inter-image correlations within a batch to further boost robustness, and the full model is fine-tuned with multi-similarity loss on GSV-Cities, updating only the last four DINOv2 encoder layers.

## Results

SuperVLAD (DINOv2-base, 3072-dim descriptor) achieves the best Recall@1 on all four main VPR benchmarks: 95.0% on Pitts30k, 92.2% on MSLS-val, 91.0% on Nordland, and 93.2% on SPED. These results surpass the closest DINOv2-based competitors CricaVPR (4096-dim) and SALAD (8448-dim) while using a substantially more compact descriptor. The 1-Cluster VLAD variant (768-dim) also outperforms same-dimensional GeM pooling and class token baselines by 2.1–5.0% R@1 on MSLS-val.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SuperVLAD outperforms all ten baselines on all four main benchmark datasets. Against the closest DINOv2-based competitors, it beats CricaVPR (4096-dim) by 0.1% R@1 on Pitts30k, 2.2% on MSLS-val, 0.3% on Nordland, and 1.8% on SPED, while using a 25% smaller descriptor. It surpasses SALAD (8448-dim) by 2.5% R@1 on Pitts30k, ties on MSLS-val (92.2%), leads by 1.3% on Nordland, and by 1.1% on SPED, with a descriptor 64% smaller. Against the two-stage SelaVPR (DINOv2-large), SuperVLAD gains 2.2% R@1 on Pitts30k, 1.4% on MSLS-val, 3.7% on Nordland, and 3.7% on SPED. Over CNN-based methods (MixVPR, EigenPlaces, CosPlace, SFRS, NetVLAD), the margins are larger: e.g., +3.5% R@1 over MixVPR on Pitts30k and +22.8% over NetVLAD on Nordland. The only near-tie is MSLS-val R@1 where SALAD also reports 92.2%, but SuperVLAD still leads on R@5 (96.6 vs 96.4) and R@10 (97.4 vs 97.0).

## Relation to Prior Work

SuperVLAD directly extends the NetVLAD/VLAD lineage by removing the learned cluster centers that NetVLAD uses to compute residuals, replacing them with a direct weighted summation of local features assigned via soft assignment. It also incorporates the ghost cluster mechanism from GhostVLAD to enable a 1-Cluster VLAD variant, and optionally adopts the cross-image encoder from CricaVPR for cluster-wise batch interaction. Conceptually, it departs from SALAD's optimal-transport formulation (which still relies on clusters and iterative Sinkhorn computation) by maintaining NetVLAD's simpler soft-assignment while eliminating the cluster-center dependency that causes domain-shift degradation. The method is positioned within the broader trend of leveraging DINOv2 foundation models for VPR, but its contribution is specifically in the aggregation layer rather than the backbone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in producing a VPR descriptor that is simultaneously more compact (3072-dim vs. 8448 for SALAD, 4096 for CricaVPR), more parameter-efficient (0.0038M aggregator params vs. 1.4M for SALAD), and more robust to domain shift, without sacrificing accuracy. The 1-Cluster VLAD variant is particularly useful for applications requiring very low-dimensional features, matching GeM pooling's dimensionality while outperforming it by 2–5 R@1 points. Key limitations include the dependence on transformer-based backbones (SuperVLAD underperforms NetVLAD with CNN backbones at 64 clusters) and the cross-image encoder's requirement for batch size > 1 at inference, which restricts single-frame deployment. Open questions include whether the cluster-center-free formulation can be made effective with weaker backbones and whether the approach generalizes to indoor or other out-of-domain VPR settings where the relevant objects differ fundamentally.
