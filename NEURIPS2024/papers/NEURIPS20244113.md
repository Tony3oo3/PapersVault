## Problem

Existing generative image compositing methods operate purely at the 2D level, which causes them to fail on complex spatial relationships such as occlusion between the inserted object and background elements. Prior approaches either sacrifice object identity preservation for background harmony or vice versa, and none explicitly model the depth geometry that determines whether an object should appear in front of or behind scene elements. The paper argues that the root cause is the absence of 3D spatial reasoning in the compositing pipeline.

## Input / Output

The system takes a background RGB image, a reference object image, and a natural-language spatial instruction (e.g., 'Place the apple behind the burger') as input. The fine-tuned MLLM first outputs a 2.5D location — a normalized bounding box [x1, y1, x2, y2] and a single scalar depth value d ∈ [0,1] at the object's center point. The compositing model then produces a 512×512 RGB image where the reference object is inserted at the predicted location with correct occlusion, depth blur, and lighting harmonization. The method also supports alternative input modes: a user-specified mask for pose control, or a target image for identity transfer without pose change.

## Method

Bifröst is a two-stage pipeline. In stage 1, LLaVA (CLIP-ViT-Large + Vicuna) is fine-tuned with LoRA on a novel 2.5D counterfactual dataset built from MS COCO: objects are removed via SAM masking plus diffusion inpainting, and GPT-3 generates spatial-relation instructions, yielding 30,080 training pairs that teach the MLLM to predict bounding boxes and depth values from language. In stage 2, a Stable Diffusion 2.1 UNet is conditioned on three complementary signals: (a) DINOv2 ID tokens injected via cross-attention for identity preservation, (b) Sobel high-frequency detail maps (with mask-shape augmentation) concatenated to decoder features for fine-grained structure, and (c) DPT depth maps (scaled to the MLLM-predicted depth and fused into the background depth at the predicted bbox) concatenated alongside the detail maps for spatial/occlusion control. Training uses video datasets (YouTubeVOS, MOSE, VIPSeg) to capture pose and view variation, and classifier-free guidance (50% ID dropout, 30% depth/detail dropout) balances identity fidelity against scene harmonization at inference.

## Results

On the DreamBooth-based image compositing benchmark (900 object-scene combinations), Bifröst achieves a DINO-score of 77.75, CLIP-score of 89.72, and FID of 15.03, outperforming all four baselines across every metric. For the MLLM 2.5D location prediction task, the fine-tuned LLaVA achieves a BBox IoU of 0.8515 and BBox MSE of 0.0496, surpassing the LLaVA baseline (IoU 0.7567, MSE 0.0653) and being the only method to also predict depth (MSE 0.0658). In the user study, Bifröst with depth control scores 4.21/5 on 3D Awareness and 4.03/5 on Fidelity, substantially ahead of the next-best method (AnyDoor at 2.51 and 3.58 respectively).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Bifröst outperforms all four image compositing baselines on every metric. Against the strongest competitor AnyDoor (Chen et al., 2024), Bifröst gains +0.94 DINO-score, +0.99 CLIP-score, and −0.83 FID, with the gap widening substantially against older methods like Paint-by-Example (+5.52 DINO-score, +5.14 CLIP-score, −7.26 FID). The key qualitative advantage is 3D-aware occlusion handling: AnyDoor and earlier methods fail when the inserted object overlaps background objects, while Bifröst correctly renders depth ordering (e.g., an object behind a bowl). In the user study, Bifröst with depth control leads on Quality (3.96), Fidelity (4.03), and 3D Awareness (4.21), but trails Paint-by-Example on Diversity (3.07 vs. 3.87), which the authors attribute to depth constraints reducing pose variation. For the MLLM prediction sub-task, the fine-tuned model beats LLaVA baseline by 0.099 IoU and 0.016 MSE on bounding boxes, and is the only method capable of depth prediction (MiniGPTv2 and LLaVA do not support it). Overall, Bifröst wins on all primary metrics for image compositing and MLLM spatial prediction, with the sole trade-off being reduced diversity when depth control is active.

## Relation to Prior Work

Bifröst extends the line of diffusion-based object-level image compositing (Paint-by-Example, ObjectStitch, AnyDoor, IMPRINT) by introducing depth maps as an explicit 3D spatial condition, addressing the fundamental limitation that prior methods operate purely at the 2D level and cannot handle occlusion or complex depth relationships. It repurposes LLaVA (fine-tuned with LoRA on a novel counterfactual dataset built from MS COCO) as a 2.5D location predictor, and builds on AnyDoor's DINOv2-based ID extraction and ControlNet-style conditioning architecture for the compositing stage. The key conceptual departure is treating depth as a first-class generation condition rather than an implicit property, bridging the gap between 2D image editing and full 3D scene composition without explicit 3D reconstruction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By embedding depth into the compositing pipeline, Bifröst enables instruction-based image editing that respects occlusion and spatial ordering—capabilities absent from all prior 2D compositing methods—making it applicable to practical scenarios like e-commerce and professional editing where objects must be placed behind or in front of existing scene elements. The counterfactual dataset construction (inpainting objects out of COCO images) is a practical recipe for generating training data without new annotation, reducing the barrier for future work. However, the MLLM's OOD performance on unseen object categories remains a clear limitation, and the depth condition introduces a trade-off between spatial control and pose/view diversity that the authors acknowledge but leave unresolved. Extending the framework to 3D and video compositing is identified as the natural next step.
