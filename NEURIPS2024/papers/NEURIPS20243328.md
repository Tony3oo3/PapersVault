## Problem

Transformer-based Masked Point Modeling (MPM) suffers from quadratic complexity and large model sizes (e.g., 22.1M parameters for Point-MAE), making deployment on resource-constrained devices like robots and VR headsets impractical. Additionally, the Transformer decoder struggles to reconstruct masked patches because it treats all input tokens equally despite the large information-density gap between unmasked (informative) and randomly initialized masked (uninformative) tokens. The paper identifies that redundancy reduction—focusing computation on locally relevant patches rather than all pairwise interactions—is the key to both efficiency and performance in point cloud analysis.

## Input / Output

The model takes a raw 3D point cloud of L×3 coordinates, downsamples it to N central points via farthest point sampling, and forms KNN patches of K points each. During pre-training, a fraction (1−r) of patches are masked; the encoder processes only unmasked patches, and the decoder reconstructs the relative 3D coordinates of masked patches using Chamfer Distance loss. For downstream tasks, the same compact encoder (2.7M params, 1.3 GFLOPs) is fine-tuned to produce class labels (ScanObjectNN, ModelNet40), 3D bounding boxes via 3DETR (ScanNetV2), or per-point part labels (ShapeNetPart). The entire pre-training fits on a single 24GB RTX 3090 GPU.

## Method

LCM replaces the standard Transformer with two locally constrained components. The compact encoder stacks layers of a Local Aggregation Layer (LAL) and an FFN: the LAL uses static k-NN geometric constraints (computed once from central coordinates and shared across all layers) to gather k nearest-neighbor features, applies a Down MLP, local max pooling, and an Up MLP—requiring only two MLPs per layer and avoiding per-layer dynamic attention. The Mamba-based decoder replaces self-attention with Mamba's selective state space model (SSM) for linear-complexity sequence processing, but addresses SSM's strong dependence on input patch ordering by introducing a Local Constraints Feedforward Network (LCFFN) that implicitly exchanges information between geometrically adjacent patches based on their central coordinates. An information-theoretic analysis (Theorem 1) shows the Mamba decoder preserves at least as much mutual information between masked and unmasked patches as the Transformer decoder, because the Transformer's higher-order attention interactions are a function of the Mamba's linear output, forming a Markov chain that bounds the Transformer's mutual information from above.

## Results

On ScanObjectNN (the primary classification benchmark), Point-MAE w/LCM achieves 94.51% / 92.75% / 88.87% average accuracy on OBJ-BG / OBJ-ONLY / PB-T50-RS, improving over the Transformer-based Point-MAE by +1.84%, +0.67%, and +0.60% while reducing parameters from 22.1M to 2.7M (−88%) and FLOPs from 4.8G to 1.3G (−73%). On ScanNetV2 detection, Point-MAE w/LCM gains +5.2% AP₂₅ and +6.0% AP₅₀ over its Transformer counterpart. On ShapeNetPart segmentation, Point-MAE w/LCM reaches 85.1% mIoU_c, surpassing the Transformer-based Point-MAE (84.2%) by 0.9 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LCM consistently outperforms the Transformer backbone across all five MPM pre-training strategies it is integrated into (Point-BERT, MaskPoint, Point-MAE, Point-M2AE, ACT), with the largest gains on Point-MAE (+1.84% OBJ-BG) and ACT (+2.05% OBJ-BG). In the supervised-only setting, LCM (2.7M params) beats the 22.1M-param Transformer by 0.82%/0.15%/1.10% on the three ScanObjectNN variants, and also surpasses PointMamba (12.3M) and PointNet++ (1.5M). On ScanNetV2 detection, the improvement is even more pronounced: Point-BERT w/LCM gains +9.0% AP₅₀ over its Transformer version, and Point-MAE w/LCM gains +6.0% AP₅₀, attributed to local constraints filtering background noise in scene-level point clouds. On ModelNet40, Point-MAE w/LCM (94.2%, 2.7M params, 0.6G FLOPs) matches the best self-supervised result (PointGPT-B at 94.2% with 120.5M params) while being ~45× smaller. The only comparison where LCM does not clearly win is Point-M2AE w/LCM on PB-T50-RS (+0.32%), where the multi-scale architecture's additional FLOPs (6.7G) partially offset the efficiency gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself as a drop-in architecture replacement for the Transformer backbone in existing Masked Point Modeling (MPM) pre-training methods (Point-MAE, Point-BERT, MaskPoint, Point-M2AE, ACT). It draws on two distinct lineages: the local geometric aggregation ideas from PointNet++/DGCNN/PointMLP (replacing global self-attention with static k-NN neighborhood pooling in the encoder) and the Mamba/SSM literature (replacing self-attention with a selective state-space layer in the decoder). The key departure from prior work is the introduction of the LCFFN, which migrates the local-constraint idea into the Mamba decoder's feedforward path to eliminate SSM's sensitivity to input sequence ordering, and an information-theoretic argument showing the Mamba decoder preserves at least as much mutual information as the Transformer decoder for the asymmetric masked/unmasked token setup.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 88% parameter reduction and 73% FLOPs reduction while simultaneously improving accuracy across five independent pre-training strategies makes LCM a practically important building block for deploying point cloud pre-trained models on resource-constrained devices (robots, VR headsets). The redundancy-reduction framing and the overfitting analysis (Transformer overfits on small point cloud datasets like ScanObjectNN with ~15K samples) offer a useful conceptual lens for the field. Open questions remain around dynamic importance perception and long-range dependency modeling, which the authors explicitly defer; whether the local-constraint inductive bias generalizes to very large-scale outdoor scenes or multi-modal point cloud settings is untested.
