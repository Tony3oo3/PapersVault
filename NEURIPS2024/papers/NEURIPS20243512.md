## Problem

Predicting which molecular perturbation caused a given cellular morphology change (contrastive phenomolecular retrieval) is a core challenge in drug discovery, but prior methods like CLOOME achieve only ~7.5% top-1% retrieval accuracy on active molecules. The difficulty stems from three compounding issues: an order-of-magnitude smaller paired dataset than text-image domains with confounding batch effects, the presence of inactive molecules that create misannotated training pairs, and the need to generalize across variable molecular concentrations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243512/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 6-channel fluorescent microscopy images (2048×2048, block-mean downsampled to 256×256) representing cell-painting phenomic experiments, molecular structure graphs, and scalar concentration values (e.g., 0.01–25 µM). Images are embedded via the pretrained Phenom1 MAE (ViT-L/8) and molecules via a 1B-parameter MPNN (MolGPS) or RDKit/MACCS/Morgan fingerprints; phenomic embeddings from replicate experiments are averaged to marginalize batch effects. The output is a ranked list of (molecule, concentration) pairs scored by cosine similarity in a shared 512-d latent space, evaluated via top-1% recall on held-out images, unseen molecular scaffolds, or an out-of-distribution dataset (RXRX3).

## Method

MolPhenix is a 38.7M-parameter contrastive learner (6 ResNet blocks + linear projections) trained with a novel S2L (soft-weighted sigmoid locked) loss that unifies SigLIP's element-wise calibrated sigmoid with CWCL's inter-sample similarity weighting, where weights are computed from arctangent-of-L2 distances between Phenom1 embeddings to distinguish active from inactive perturbations. Three design guidelines drive performance: (1) a pretrained unimodal phenomics encoder (Phenom1) plus embedding averaging across replicates to mitigate batch effects and reduce paired-data requirements; (2) undersampling inactive molecules via p-value filtering on replicate consistency, combined with the S2L loss that treats the retrieval problem as a soft multi-label task rather than hard multi-class; (3) dual concentration encoding—implicit (treating each molecule-concentration pair as a distinct class in the S2L objective) and explicit (concatenating a one-hot or log-encoded concentration vector at the molecular encoder's input layer). This recipe yields 77.33% top-1% retrieval on active unseen molecules, an 8.1× improvement over the CLOOME baseline.

## Results

MolPhenix achieves 77.33% top-1% recall on active molecules in the zero-shot unseen images + unseen molecules setting (MolPhenix* with Phenom1 & MolGPS), representing an 8.1× improvement over the CLOOME baseline (7.87%). On the unseen dataset (RXRX3), MolPhenix* reaches 58.60% top-1% recall for active molecules versus 5.28% for CLOOME trained on raw images. In the held-out concentration setting, S2L loss outperforms CLOOME by up to 126%, and sigmoid concentration encoding generalizes best to unseen doses. Across all evaluation settings, MolPhenix consistently outperforms CLOOME by 1.26× when both use Phenom1 embeddings, confirming the contribution of the S2L loss and concentration encoding beyond the pre-trained encoder alone.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MolPhenix substantially outperforms CLOOME (Sanchez-Fernandez et al., Nature 2023), the prior state-of-the-art, across all evaluation settings: on the zero-shot unseen images + unseen molecules task for active molecules, MolPhenix* achieves 77.33% versus CLOOME's 7.87% (8.1× improvement), and on the RXRX3 unseen dataset, 58.60% versus 5.28%. When both methods use Phenom1 embeddings, MolPhenix still leads by 1.26× (63.87% vs. 50.57% on unseen images + molecules), isolating the contribution of S2L and concentration encoding. Among loss-function baselines (Table 2), S2L achieves 90.97% top-1% recall on unseen images (active), surpassing SigLIP (77.63%), CWCL (70.91%), CLOOME/InfoLOOB (35.72%), CLIP (33.73%), and Hopfield-CLIP (25.78%). CWCL and SigLIP, which incorporate inter-sample weighting and label-noise robustness respectively, both outperform the standard InfoNCE-based losses (CLIP, InfoLOOB, CLOOME) by 2×, validating the paper's design choices. On held-out concentrations (Table 4), S2L maintains its lead over all baselines, with the gap over CLOOME reaching 126%, while sigmoid concentration encoding generalizes better than one-hot to unseen doses.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on CLOOME (Sanchez-Fernandez et al., Nature 2023), the prior state-of-the-art for contrastive phenomolecular retrieval, and extends it by replacing raw image encoders with the pretrained Phenom1 masked autoencoder and a 1B-parameter MolGPS molecular GNN. The novel S2L loss is a principled combination of SigLIP's element-wise sigmoid formulation (robust to label noise) and CWCL's inter-sample similarity weighting (relaxing the negative-equidistant assumption), adapted to the molecular-phenomic setting where inactive perturbations create effective label noise. The paper also introduces explicit and implicit concentration encoding, a dimension largely absent from prior phenomolecular retrieval work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 8.1× improvement in zero-shot retrieval of active molecules (77.33% top-1% accuracy) opens a practical path toward virtual phenomics screening, where molecular impact on cells can be predicted in silico rather than through costly wet-lab experiments. Key limitations include reliance on a single cell line (ARPE-19) with a fixed genetic background, proprietary training data that limits reproducibility, and the absence of wet-lab validation of model predictions. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to genetic perturbations, multi-compound interventions, and diverse cell lines, and whether the concentration-encoding strategy transfers to continuous dose-response prediction.
