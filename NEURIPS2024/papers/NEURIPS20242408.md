## Problem

Pre-training of Multimodal LLMs is dominated by the computational cost of processing large numbers of visual tokens (144–576+) that far exceed the ~23 text tokens typical in image-text pairs, making training prohibitively expensive. Existing vision-language bridge modules maintain a fixed token count throughout both pre-training and fine-tuning, so reducing tokens for speed inevitably degrades final performance. The paper asks whether one can train with far fewer visual tokens during pre-training while still recovering full performance at fine-tuning time.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242408/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes RGB images at 224×224 (pre-training) or 448×448 (fine-tuning) alongside text prompts, producing text responses for captioning/VQA tasks or bounding-box coordinates for referring expression comprehension. A distinctive aspect is that the number of visual tokens fed to the language model is not fixed: it starts at 32–80 tokens during pre-training and can be scaled up to 528–1296 tokens during fine-tuning via the compound scaling strategy, without retraining the language model.

## Method

Chain-of-Sight is a vision-language bridge built from multi-scale visual resamplers: the CLIP-ViT-L/14 feature map is partitioned into non-overlapping windows of multiple sizes (e.g., 16×16 and 4×4), and Perceiver-style learnable queries within each window perform cross-attention only on their local windowed features, producing tokens at different spatial scales that are concatenated in a coarse-to-fine order. A post-pretrain compound token scaling strategy then increases the token count by jointly raising input resolution (224→448) and shrinking window sizes, enabling up to a 16× token expansion (e.g., 80→1296) without additional pre-training. New resampler parameters are initialized by inflating pre-trained weights, and new queries are initialized via nearest-neighbor interpolation. This design allows pre-training with ~90% fewer visual tokens (32 vs. 336), yielding a 3.7× wall-clock speedup (~73% reduction) while matching or surpassing the performance of models that use all tokens throughout training.

## Results

Chain-of-Sight (CoS-7B, 532M trainable params via LoRA) achieves 82.9 VQA, 64.0 GQA, 93.9 ScienceQA, 65.1 TextVQA, 85.9 POPE, 72.8 MMBench, and 68.9 SEED-Bench, while using only 80 visual tokens for pre-training (vs. 576 for LLaVA-1.5/VILA) and achieving a 3.7× pre-training speedup (73% wall-clock reduction). The larger CoS-8B (LLaMA3-8B, 540M trainable params) further improves to 84.3 VQA, 65.3 GQA, 95.7 ScienceQA, 76.6 MMBench, and 73.1 SEED-Bench. On referring expression comprehension, CoS-8B achieves 90.33 average across RefCOCO/RefCOCO+/RefCOCOg, surpassing all compared models including CogVLM-17B (90.25).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoS-7B (532M trainable params) substantially outperforms all 7B-scale baselines on most benchmarks: it beats LLaVA-1.5-7B by +4.4 on VQA (82.9 vs 78.5), +2.0 on GQA (64.0 vs 62.0), +27.1 on ScienceQA (93.9 vs 66.8), and +8.5 on MMBench (72.8 vs 64.3); it surpasses mPLUG-Owl2 by +3.5 on VQA and +8.3 on MMBench; and it leads Qwen-VL by +4.1 on VQA and +34.6 on MMBench. Against the larger CogVLM-17B, CoS-7B wins on ScienceQA (93.9 vs 91.2) but trails on TextVQA (65.1 vs 70.4), MMBench (72.8 vs 77.6), and SEED-Bench (68.9 vs 72.5). On referring expression comprehension (Table 6), CoS-7B achieves 87.68 average, beating Qwen-VL-7B (85.83), Ferret-7B (83.91), and MiniGPTv2 (84.29), while CoS-8B reaches 90.33, narrowly surpassing CogVLM-17B (90.25) and all other models. Notably, CoS uses only 80 pre-training visual tokens (vs. 576 for LLaVA-1.5/VILA/Mini-Gemini) and 532M–540M trainable parameters (vs. 7B–13B for full fine-tuning baselines), achieving a 3.7× pre-training speedup while matching or exceeding their performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

Chain-of-Sight builds on the Perceiver-style visual resampler (as used in BLIP-2, Qwen-VL) and the multi-level feature aggregation strategy from Cao et al. (CVPR 2024), but departs from the standard practice of maintaining a fixed visual token count across both pre-training and fine-tuning. Its core novelty is the multi-scale window partitioning of visual features—borrowing the classical FPN/multi-scale hierarchy idea from dense prediction—and the compound token scaling strategy that combines resolution and window-size manipulation to inflate tokens up to 16× post-pretraining. This positions the work as a bridge between the linear-projector family (LLaVA, which uses all patches) and the resampler family (BLIP-2, Qwen-VL, which use a fixed number of learnable queries), unifying both as special cases within its framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 73% pre-training speedup with no performance degradation is a practically significant efficiency gain for anyone training MLLMs, especially given that pre-training dominates total compute. The finding that 32 tokens suffice for pre-training while 336–1296 tokens are needed for fine-tuning challenges the implicit assumption that visual token count must be uniform across training stages. However, the use of LoRA throughout limits the generality of the results compared to full fine-tuning approaches, and the single visual backbone (CLIP-ViT-L/14) and moderate dataset scale (~65M pairs) leave open whether the speedup and performance parity hold at larger scales. Future work could explore whether the multi-scale resampler design transfers to video or higher-resolution settings, and whether full fine-tuning of the LLM would further close the gap with larger models.
