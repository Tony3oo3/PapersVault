## Problem

Current LLM evaluation platforms such as the HuggingFace open LLM leaderboard rely solely on accuracy, ignoring uncertainty — yet two models can achieve identical accuracy while exhibiting vastly different confidence levels. This paper addresses the gap by introducing a principled, statistically rigorous benchmarking framework that quantifies LLM uncertainty via conformal prediction across five NLP tasks and nine open-source model families.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240491/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each task instance is converted into a six-option multiple-choice question (A–D plus 'I don't know' and 'None of the above'), and the LLM is prompted with 1–5 in-context demonstrations depending on the task. The method extracts logits for each option letter from the language modeling head at the final token position, applies softmax to obtain per-option probabilities, and then produces a prediction set (a subset of the six options), its cardinality as the uncertainty score (Set Size), the empirical coverage rate, and standard accuracy. Results are averaged over two conformal score functions (LAC and APS) and three prompting strategies to reduce sensitivity to any single configuration.

## Method

The core mechanism is conformal prediction applied to LLM softmax scores: a calibration set (50% of data) is used to compute conformal scores under either the LAC function (1 − p_true) or the APS function (cumulative sum of ranked softmax scores up to the true label), from which a threshold q̂ is set at the ⌈(n+1)(1−α)⌉/n quantile with α=0.1. For each test instance, the prediction set contains all options whose conformal score does not exceed q̂, guaranteeing P(Y_t ∈ C(X_t)) ≥ 0.9 distribution-free. The paper benchmarks nine LLM series (Llama-2, Mistral, Qwen, Yi, DeepSeek, Falcon, Gemma, MPT, InternLM) across QA, reading comprehension, commonsense inference, dialogue response selection, and document summarization, revealing that higher accuracy does not imply lower uncertainty, larger models can be more uncertain than smaller ones, and instruction-finetuning tends to increase uncertainty. Conformal prediction is further validated against perplexity and entropy baselines on coverage rate and ECE, and extended to closed-source LLMs (via sampling-based probability estimation) and free-form generation (via perplexity-scored sampled answers).

## Results

Across 11 open-source LLMs (6B–14B) evaluated on five NLP tasks (QA, RC, CI, DRS, DS), Qwen-14B achieves the highest average accuracy (74.00%) and lowest average set size (2.17), while Falcon-7B and MPT-7B rank last on both metrics (24.84%/3.75 and 27.18%/3.57 respectively). Coverage rates consistently exceed 90% for most model-task pairs, validating the conformal prediction guarantee. A key finding is that accuracy and uncertainty rankings frequently diverge: e.g., InternLM-7B outperforms MPT-7B by 19.34 points in accuracy on DRS yet exhibits higher uncertainty (SS 3.63 vs. 3.59), and Qwen-7B surpasses Llama-2-7B by 9.61 points on QA while showing greater uncertainty (SS 3.26 vs. 3.20).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares conformal prediction (CP) against perplexity (PPL, derived from entropy) and maximal predicted probability (P_max) using InternLM-7B. On coverage rate, CP maintains ≥90% across all five tasks (avg. 90.96%), while PPL drops to 83.44% on QA and 86.80% on DRS (avg. 89.82%), because entropy is invariant to probability permutations and ignores accuracy. On Expected Calibration Error (ECE), CP achieves the lowest average (8.35%) versus Entropy (8.46%) and P_max (8.61%), with the largest gap on CI (3.16% vs. 3.45% vs. 3.75%) and DRS (12.11% vs. 12.40% vs. 12.45%). CP ties Entropy on QA (15.83%) and DS (9.30%) but edges out both on RC (1.33% vs. 1.32% vs. 1.41%). Overall, CP provides statistically rigorous, calibrated uncertainty estimates that are more robust to softmax temperature variations than entropy-based alternatives.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper applies conformal prediction — a distribution-free, model-agnostic uncertainty quantification framework established in statistics (Angelopoulos & Bates; Sadinle et al. for LAC; Romano et al. for APS) — to the LLM evaluation setting, a context where it had not previously been systematically deployed. It departs from dominant LLM leaderboards (HuggingFace Open LLM Leaderboard, OpenCompass, Chatbot Arena) that rely solely on accuracy, and from heuristic uncertainty measures (entropy, perplexity, sampling-based semantic entropy) by providing a statistically rigorous, calibration-based alternative. The paper also extends prior conformal prediction applications in NLP (POS tagging, paraphrase detection, fact verification) from small-scale classification to large-scale LLM benchmarking across multiple tasks and model families.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that accuracy and uncertainty are decoupled — with some high-accuracy models exhibiting *higher* uncertainty than low-accuracy counterparts — challenges the implicit assumption in current leaderboards that accuracy alone captures model competence. The observation that instruction finetuning systematically increases uncertainty raises practical concerns for deployed chat models. Open questions include whether the MCQA formulation adequately captures uncertainty in truly generative settings (the free-form TriviaQA extension shows coverage guarantees break down under sampling), whether the findings generalize beyond the 6B–14B scale range studied in the main experiments, and how to integrate uncertainty-aware evaluation into existing leaderboard infrastructure.
