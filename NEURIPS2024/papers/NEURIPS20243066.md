## Problem

Formal language theory analyses of transformer expressivity have been restricted to finite alphabets, where a token embedding function bounds the input vocabulary. This setting is inadequate for applications like time series forecasting, where transformers must handle arbitrary sequences of real-valued data without a priori limits on values. The paper initiates the study of what properties of numerical data sequences can be recognized by Unique Hard Attention Transformers (UHAT), asking how expressivity changes when the input alphabet is the infinite set Q^d rather than a finite set.

## Input / Output

The UHAT operates on sequences of d-tuples of rational numbers (Q^d)^n, optionally augmented with a positional encoding function p: N×N → R^s that prepends position-dependent vectors to each input element. A zero vector is appended as an end-of-input marker (or a unique (0,0) vector without positional encoding). The output is a binary acceptance decision determined by whether the inner product of a fixed acceptance vector with the first output position is positive. For circuit complexity analysis, each rational number is encoded as a binary string of its numerator and denominator, making the input a finite-alphabet word whose length is polynomial in the bit-size of the original data.

## Method

The paper establishes three main results through a three-step technical framework. First, it proves UHAT over data sequences lies in TC0 but not AC0 (Theorem 1): the UHAT is translated into a polynomial-sized Boolean combination of degree-2 polynomial inequalities with bounded alternations (Step I), real coefficients are replaced by rationals of polynomial size using convex geometry and Carathéodory's theorem to preserve acceptance on bounded-size inputs (Step II), and the resulting rational polynomial constraints are compiled into constant-depth threshold circuits (Step III). The lower bound uses TC0-completeness of integer multiplication under AC0 reductions. Second, it shows masked UHAT without positional encoding recognizes the non-regular language Double (sequences where each element exceeds twice its predecessor), going beyond symbolic automata and other infinite-alphabet regular models (Theorem 2). Third, it proves UHAT with positional encoding captures all languages definable in (LT)²L, an extension of LTL with unary numerical predicates and fixed-window arithmetic atoms, by constructing UHAT gadgets for normalization, next-position attention, and minimal-position selection (Theorem 3).

## Results

The paper establishes three main theoretical results. Theorem 1 proves that UHAT with positional encoding over data sequences is in TC⁰ but not in AC⁰, showing a strict increase in expressive power compared to the string-input setting where UHAT is contained in AC⁰. Theorem 2 demonstrates that masked UHAT without positional encoding can recognize the non-regular language Double = {(r₁,…,rₙ) ∈ ℚⁿ | 2rᵢ < rᵢ₊₁ for all i < n} over the infinite alphabet Σ = ℚ, going beyond all existing formal models over infinite alphabets (symbolic automata, register automata, etc.). Theorem 3 shows that every language definable in Locally Testable LTL ((LT)²L), which extends LTL with unary numerical predicates and local arithmetic tests, is accepted by UHAT with positional encoding, providing a concrete logical characterization of a subclass of UHAT languages over data sequences.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against empirical baselines, as it is a purely theoretical work. Instead, it contrasts the expressive power of UHAT over data sequences (Σ = ℚᵈ) with the well-known results for UHAT over finite alphabets. Over strings, UHAT without positional encodings captures only star-free (regular) languages and is contained in AC⁰; over data sequences, UHAT without positional encodings captures non-regular languages and is not contained in AC⁰ (it is TC⁰-hard under AC⁰-reductions via integer multiplication). The paper also shows that the Double language recognized by masked UHAT cannot be captured by symbolic automata, register automata, variable/parametric automata, or data automata variants, establishing a strict separation from all existing formal language models over infinite alphabets. On the positive side, (LT)²L provides a logical upper bound on UHAT expressiveness over data sequences, extending the LTL(Mon) characterization known for UHAT over finite alphabets [3].

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the formal language theoretic analysis of transformers (Hao et al. 2022; Angluin et al. 2023; Barcelo et al. 2024) from strings over finite alphabets to data sequences over the infinite alphabet Σ = Q^d. The circuit complexity connection (AC0/TC0) and the logical characterizations (LTL, star-free languages) from prior work on finite alphabets serve as the baseline, which the authors deliberately contrast against. The non-regularity result draws on the symbolic automata framework from D'Antoni & Veanes (2021) and Bojańczyk (2019) for formal language theory over infinite alphabets, while the TC0 upper bound leverages convex geometry (Carathéodory's theorem) to handle real-valued constants in transformer weights.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first systematic characterization of transformer expressiveness on numerical data sequences, directly relevant to time series forecasting and other applications where inputs are unbounded real/rational values rather than discrete tokens. The clean separation (AC0 → TC0, regular → non-regular) establishes that transformers on numerical data are fundamentally more powerful than on strings, with concrete implications: a UHAT for SQRTSUM would be a major complexity-theoretic breakthrough. Key open questions include a precise logical characterization of all UHAT languages over data sequences (the (LT)²L logic is a lower bound but not a full characterization), the expressiveness of other attention mechanisms (average hard, softmax) on data sequences, and whether bounded-precision variants preserve the TC0 upper bound.
