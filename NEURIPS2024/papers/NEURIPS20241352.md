## Problem

Existing methods that repurpose diffusion models for visual perception either add extra decoder heads (increasing cost and undermining generalization) or disrupt the original UNet structure, failing to uniformly adapt to all tasks. The fundamental question of how to design a fine-tuning framework that preserves both generalization ability and precise pixel-level prediction remains unaddressed. Few-shot semantic segmentation, which demands open-set generalization alongside high-quality segmentation, is an ideal testbed for this challenge, and no diffusion-based FSS model existed prior to this work.

## Input / Output

The method takes a support image, its binary mask, and a query image (all resized to 512×512 RGB), encodes them via the frozen VAE into latent variables, and produces a segmentation mask for the query image. The binary mask is converted to a white-foreground/black-background RGB image before VAE encoding, and the final output is obtained by averaging the three latent channels and applying a relative threshold of 0.25. Notably, the entire pipeline operates in latent space with no additional decoder head—the UNet directly outputs the mask latent in a single forward pass.

## Method

DiffewS initializes from Stable Diffusion 2.1 and introduces KV Fusion Self-Attention (FSA): within each UNet self-attention block, the query image's Q attends to concatenated K and V from both the query and support images, enabling cross-image interaction while fully reusing the original self-attention weights. The support mask is injected by channel-concatenating its VAE-encoded latent with the support image latent (with Marigold-style weight duplication to handle the dimension mismatch). Training uses a one-step image-to-mask (OI2M) objective where the UNet takes the query latent as input and directly predicts the mask latent via v-prediction, eliminating the multi-step denoising loop entirely. This minimal-disruption design—no extra decoder, no time-step conditioning, no architectural changes beyond the attention modification—allows the pre-trained diffusion prior to be fully leveraged, achieving SOTA in-context segmentation results with only 10k–30k training iterations.

## Results

Under the in-context setting, DiffewS achieves 71.3 mIoU (1-shot) and 72.2 mIoU (5-shot) on COCO-20i, 88.3 mIoU (1-shot) on PASCAL-5i, and 31.4 mIoU (1-shot) / 35.4 mIoU (5-shot) on the out-of-domain LVIS-92i. Under the strict few-shot setting, DiffewS attains 51.2 mIoU mean across four COCO-20i folds in 1-shot, marginally surpassing the previous SOTA DCAMA (50.9), and 56.0 mIoU in 5-shot. With the proposed training-time N-shot improvement (DiffewS-n), performance rises to 52.2 mIoU (1-shot) and 60.7 mIoU (5-shot), outperforming all baselines in both settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On COCO-20i (in-context), DiffewS (71.3) substantially outperforms the strongest generalist model SegGPT (56.1, +15.2) and the specialist model FPTrans (56.5, +14.8), as well as the SAM-based Matcher (52.7, +18.6) and PerSAM-F (23.5, +47.8). On PASCAL-5i, DiffewS (88.3) leads SegGPT (83.2, +5.1) and Matcher (67.9, +20.4). On the out-of-domain LVIS-92i, DiffewS (31.4) is slightly behind Matcher (33.0, −1.6) but far ahead of SegGPT (18.6) and PerSAM-F (18.4). Under the strict few-shot protocol (Table 2), DiffewS (51.2 mean 1-shot) edges out DCAMA (50.9) and HDMNet (50.0), while in 5-shot it trails DCAMA (56.0 vs. 58.3) until the training-time N-shot improvement is applied, at which point DiffewS-n (60.7) surpasses all baselines. Notably, DiffewS achieves these results with only 10k–30k training iterations versus the hundreds of epochs required by specialist baselines like HSNet and DCAMA.

## Relation to Prior Work

DiffewS builds directly on the Latent Diffusion Model architecture (Stable Diffusion 2.1), adopting Marigold's paradigm of repurposing a frozen VAE for latent-space supervision and its channel-mismatch handling for concatenated inputs. It departs from prior diffusion-for-perception work (ODISE, DVP, DatasetDM) that adds extra decoder heads, and from the concurrent in-context segmentation diffusion work [38] that disrupts the U-Net structure, by maximally preserving the original generative framework. The KV Fusion Self-Attention mechanism for query–support interaction is a novel design element not present in prior FSS methods (which typically use separate correlation or matching modules) nor in standard diffusion cross-attention (which is text-conditioned).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As the first diffusion-based model dedicated to few-shot semantic segmentation, DiffewS establishes a baseline and design template for a future diffusion-based generalist segmentation model, a direction the authors explicitly motivate. The large in-context performance gap over SegGPT (+15.2 mIoU on COCO-20i) suggests that diffusion priors encode richer visual structure than the ViT-based backbones used by prior generalist segmenters. However, the strict FSS margin over DCAMA is marginal (+0.3 mIoU), n-shot performance remains weaker than SOTA, and the absence of error bars and the reliance on a single training fold for ablations limit confidence. Open questions include whether the OI2M one-step finding generalizes to more complex dense prediction tasks and whether scaling training data can close the gap with SAM-based methods like Matcher.
