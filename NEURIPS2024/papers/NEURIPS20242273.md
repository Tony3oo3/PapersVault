## Problem

CNN-based anomaly detection methods capture local context but cannot model long-range dependencies, while transformer-based approaches (e.g., UniAD) achieve global modeling at the cost of quadratic computational complexity, limiting them to low-resolution feature maps. Mamba's selective state space models offer linear-complexity long-range modeling but had not been explored for multi-class unsupervised anomaly detection, leaving a gap between efficiency and global receptive field in this setting.

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images resized to 256×256 with no data augmentation and produces both an image-level anomaly score and a pixel-level anomaly map. During inference, the anomaly map is computed as the sum of cosine similarities between teacher (pre-trained ResNet34) and student (Mamba decoder) features across three spatial scales, while training minimizes the sum of MSE losses at those same scales. The model operates in a multi-class setting, handling all categories with a single unified model rather than per-class training.

## Method

MambaAD is a pyramid auto-encoder comprising a frozen pre-trained ResNet34 encoder, a Half-FPN bottleneck for multi-scale fusion, and a Mamba-based decoder (depth [3,4,6,3]) whose core building block is the Locality-Enhanced State Space (LSS) module. Each LSS module runs two parallel branches: a cascade of 2–3 Hybrid State Space (HSS) blocks for global modeling and two parallel depth-wise convolution paths (kernels k=5 and k=7, each flanked by 1×1 convolutions) for local feature capture, with outputs concatenated and fused via a 1×1 convolution plus residual connection. The HSS block encodes 2D feature maps into 1D sequences using a Hilbert-curve scanning strategy in eight directions (forward, reverse, width-height variants, and 90°-rotated variants), processes them through a selective SSM, and decodes back to 2D orientation, with the final output being the sum across all directions. The Hilbert scanning is specifically chosen because its space-filling property preserves spatial locality while enabling long-range SSM modeling, which aligns with the central concentration of features in industrial product images.

## Results

On MVTec-AD, MambaAD achieves 98.6/99.6/97.8 (image-level AU-ROC/AP/F1_max) and 97.7/56.3/59.2/93.1 (pixel-level AU-ROC/AP/F1_max/AU-PRO), reaching a comprehensive mAD of 86.0, surpassing the previous SoTA DiAD (84.0) by 2.0 points. On the more challenging VisA dataset, MambaAD attains mAD 78.7, outperforming DiAD (70.1) by 8.7 points. On the large-scale Real-IAD dataset (30 categories, 150K images), MambaAD achieves mAD 72.7 versus DiAD's 52.6, demonstrating strong scalability. The model uses only 25.7M parameters and 8.3G FLOPs, roughly 1/50th the cost of DiAD (1331.3M params, 451.5G FLOPs).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MambaAD outperforms all five baselines across all three primary datasets on the comprehensive mAD metric. Against the strongest multi-class baseline DiAD (diffusion-based, AAAI'24), MambaAD gains +2.0 mAD on MVTec-AD, +8.7 on VisA, and +20.1 on Real-IAD, with particularly large pixel-level AP improvements (e.g., +13.3 on VisA, +30.1 on Real-IAD). Compared to UniAD (transformer-based, NeurIPS'22), MambaAD improves mAD by +4.3 on MVTec-AD while using comparable parameters (25.7M vs. 24.5M) but 2.3× more FLOPs (8.3G vs. 3.6G). Against the CNN-based RD4AD (CVPR'22), MambaAD leads by +3.7 mAD on MVTec-AD and +4.1 on Real-IAD, while RD4AD uses 3.1× more parameters (80.6M). SimpleNet and DeSTSeg, designed for single-class settings, degrade severely in multi-class evaluation (e.g., SimpleNet drops to 42.3 mAD on Real-IAD), whereas MambaAD maintains consistent performance. In single-class evaluations (Appendix), MambaAD achieves near-optimal results, matching or exceeding PatchCore on most MVTec-AD categories, confirming its robustness across both multi-class and single-class protocols.

## Relation to Prior Work

MambaAD sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) reconstruction-based multi-class anomaly detection pioneered by UniAD, which established the pre-trained encoder + decoder + multi-scale feature comparison paradigm; (2) the Mamba/selective SSM family of architectures that offer linear-complexity long-range modeling as an alternative to quadratic-complexity transformers; and (3) visual SSM adaptations like Vmamba's cross-scan module, which the HSS block directly extends with five scanning methods and eight directions. The key departure is the LSS module, which fuses Mamba's global receptive field with parallel multi-kernel depth-wise convolutions for local context—something neither pure CNN (RD4AD) nor pure transformer (UniAD) decoders achieve. The Hilbert scanning strategy is a novel design choice motivated by the spatial distribution of industrial defects, distinguishing it from the simpler bidirectional sweeps used in prior visual Mamba work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As the first Mamba-based anomaly detection method, MambaAD opens a promising architectural direction that could reduce the computational bottleneck of transformer-based AD at scale, with the demonstrated ~50× parameter/FLOP reduction over DiAD while matching or exceeding accuracy being practically significant for deployment on production lines. The ablation showing that mixing multiple scanning methods degrades performance (rather than improving it) is a useful negative result that guides future SSM-for-vision design. However, the authors acknowledge the model is not yet efficient enough, and the accuracy gains over the previous SoTA (DiAD) are modest on MVTec-AD (+2.0 mAD) though larger on VisA (+8.7 mAD), leaving open whether Mamba's advantages persist on more diverse or higher-resolution benchmarks. A key open question is whether the Hilbert scanning advantage generalizes beyond centrally-placed industrial objects to more spatially heterogeneous anomaly patterns.
