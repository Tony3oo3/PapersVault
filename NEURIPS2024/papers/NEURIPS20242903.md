## Problem

Existing methods for compressing differentially private mechanisms either approximate the original output distribution (rejection sampling, importance sampling) or are restricted to additive-noise mechanisms (dithered quantization), and none simultaneously provide local DP guarantees with near-optimal communication. This paper asks whether one can exactly simulate an arbitrary local or central DP mechanism—preserving all statistical properties like unbiasedness and Gaussianity—while keeping the communication cost within a logarithmic factor of the information-theoretic lower bound and maintaining local differential privacy.

## Input / Output

The encoder receives a local data vector X, the conditional distribution P_{Z|X} of the target DP mechanism, a proposal distribution Q (absolutely continuous w.r.t. which P_{Z|X} is defined), and a shared random seed s. It outputs a single integer index K, which is prefix-free encoded (e.g., Elias delta code) and transmitted to the decoder. The decoder, holding the same seed s and the index K, reconstructs Z = Z_K by drawing the K-th sample from Q using a synchronized PRNG. The expected bit-length of K is bounded by I(X;Z) + O(log I(X;Z)), and the encoder additionally uses local randomness (a Poisson process) that is never transmitted, which is what provides the privacy guarantee.

## Method

PPR generalizes the Poisson functional representation by replacing the deterministic argmin selection with a stochastic one controlled by a parameter α > 1. Concretely, the encoder generates a rate-1 Poisson process (T_i), computes rescaled times T̃_i = T_i · (dP/dQ)(Z_i)^{-1}, and then draws K with probability proportional to T̃_k^{-α} / Σ_i T̃_i^{-α} using local randomness. This stochastic selection is the key privacy mechanism: it ensures the output Z = Z_K follows P_{Z|X} exactly (Proposition 4.2) while the mapping from X to the decoder's knowledge ((Z_i)_i, K) satisfies 2αε-DP (Theorem 4.5), with the multiplicative penalty 2α approaching 2 as α → 1. A tighter (ε,δ)-DP bound (Theorem 4.8) allows the ε-inflation to approach 1 at the cost of a small δ. The compression size E[log K] ≤ D(P‖Q) + O(1) is within a logarithmic gap of the mutual information lower bound I(X;Z), and an exact finite-time algorithm (Algorithm 1) is provided via a reparametrization that gives a stopping criterion for the argmin.

## Results

On distributed mean estimation (n=500 clients, d=1000, δ=10⁻⁶), PPR (α=2) consistently achieves lower MSE than CSGM across all ε∈[0.05,6] and compression sizes. At ε=1 with 50-bit compression, PPR achieves MSE 0.08173 vs. CSGM's 0.1231 (33.61% reduction); at ε=0.5 with 25-bit compression, PPR achieves MSE 0.3011 vs. CSGM's 0.3877 (22.33% reduction). The uncompressed Gaussian mechanism's MSE is matched by PPR at only 400 bits, whereas CSGM requires the full 1000 bits. The sliced PPR (d_chunk=50) retains O(d) running time (1.33 s at ε=0.05) while still outperforming CSGM at 400 bits.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole empirical baseline is the Coordinate Subsampled Gaussian Mechanism (CSGM) [Chen et al., NeurIPS 2024], an order-optimal scheme for DME under central DP that achieves compression via coordinate subsampling. PPR wins on MSE at every tested (ε, compression-size) pair: the gap ranges from ~22% (conservative privacy, ε=0.5, 25 bits) to ~34% (ε=1, 50 bits), which is notable since both methods are asymptotically near-optimal. Beyond MSE, PPR provides an additional local DP guarantee (2αε-local DP) that CSGM does not offer, making PPR strictly superior in the privacy-accuracy-communication trilemma. PPR also exactly preserves the Gaussian noise distribution, enabling precise central DP accounting, whereas CSGM's subsampling introduces non-Gaussian distortion. The sliced PPR variant (chunking d=1000 into 50-dim blocks) incurs a small MSE penalty but still outperforms CSGM at 400 bits while achieving O(d) running time.

## Relation to Prior Work

PPR directly generalizes the Poisson functional representation (PFR) of Li and El Gamal [61] and Li and Anantharam [60] by introducing a randomized encoder that stochastically selects the output index, thereby converting a deterministic channel-simulation tool into a privacy-preserving mechanism compressor. This departs from prior DP compression approaches in a key way: rejection sampling (Feldman and Talwar [31]) and importance/minimum random coding (Shah et al. [71], Triastcyn et al. [78]) only approximate the original mechanism's output distribution, while dithered quantization methods achieve exact simulation but are restricted to additive-noise mechanisms and require a trusted aggregator. PPR is the first construction that simultaneously achieves exact distribution recovery, local DP, and near-optimal compression for arbitrary (discrete or continuous) DP mechanisms.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The exact-simulation property has concrete downstream consequences: it preserves unbiasedness for DP-SGD, enables precise central-DP accounting for summable noise (e.g., Gaussian), and eliminates the need for generic privacy-amplification-by-shuffling. The main open limitation is running time, which is exponential in I(X;Z); the authors argue this is unavoidable for general mechanisms (citing an RP≠NP result) and that for reasonable local-DP mechanisms I(X;Z) is small, but the sliced-PPR workaround for high-dimensional vectors still incurs non-trivial per-client latency. A natural future direction is exploiting structure in P_{Z|X} (e.g., unimodality) to obtain polynomial-time exact compression, and extending the analysis to adaptive or multi-round settings where the 2α privacy inflation compounds.
