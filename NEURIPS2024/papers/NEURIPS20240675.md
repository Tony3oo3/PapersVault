## Problem

Prevailing spike-to-image reconstruction methods cascade three independent modules (spike embedding, motion estimation, intensity recovery) in a step-by-step fashion, which hinders the intrinsic collaboration of spatio-temporal complementary information and creates a 'chicken-and-egg' bottleneck between motion modeling and frame reconstruction. This sequential design also inflates model complexity and inference cost, limiting real-time deployment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240675/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a continuous binary spike stream S_{t1}^{3N} of spatio-temporal resolution H×W×3N (e.g., 1280×720×60 for SREDS training, 400×250 or 1000×1000 for real data), which is split into three non-overlapping sub-streams of length N=20 centered at timestamps t0, t1, t2. It outputs a single full-resolution intermediate intensity frame I_{t1} at the central timestamp, effectively performing video frame interpolation from asynchronous spike data.

## Method

STIR replaces the step-by-step cascade with a joint encoder-decoder that simultaneously performs warping-based inter-frame feature alignment and synthesis-based intra-frame feature filtering in a coarse-to-fine pyramid (L=5 levels). A hybrid spike embedding representation (HSER) combines multi-window TFP (explicit, interpretable) with a ResNet block (implicit, expressive) to produce initial feature maps. At the top-level pyramid, a symmetric interactive attention block uses multi-head cross-attention along channels to model bilateral correlations between the intermediate feature and both temporal contexts. At the bottom-level pyramid, G=3 groups of motion fields are estimated to compensate for local inaccuracies. Training uses a composite loss of L1 reconstruction, VGG perceptual, and multi-scale consistency terms, achieving 38.79 dB PSNR on SREDS with only 5.11M parameters and 0.42 TFLOPs.

## Results

On the synthetic SREDS dataset, STIR achieves 38.79 dB PSNR, 0.966 SSIM, and 0.0183 LPIPS, outperforming the strongest CNN-based baseline WGSE (37.44 dB PSNR) by 1.35 dB. On the real-captured dataset, STIR also leads with 5.394 NIQE and 15.854 BRISQUE. In terms of efficiency, STIR requires only 0.42 TFLOPs and 5.11M parameters, making it approximately 11× faster than Spk2ImgNet (9.17 TFLOPs) and 5× faster than WGSE (3.93 TFLOPs) while delivering superior reconstruction quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

STIR outperforms all eight baselines on every reported metric across both synthetic and real datasets. Against the strongest CNN-based spike-to-image methods, STIR surpasses WGSE [52] by 1.35 dB PSNR (38.79 vs. 37.44) and Spk2ImgNet [55] by 2.66 dB PSNR (38.79 vs. 36.13), while also reducing FLOPs by 5× and 11× respectively. The event-based baselines ET-Net [48] and HyperE2VID [10] (adapted via HSER) trail by 4.22 dB and 2.42 dB PSNR, respectively, reflecting the inherent difficulty of reconstructing absolute intensity from differential event signals. The SNN-based SSIR [57] is the most computationally efficient (0.24 TFLOPs, 0.38M params) but lags significantly in quality (32.61 dB PSNR). Traditional statistical methods (TFP, TFI, TFSTP) are far behind, with PSNR values below 26 dB, confirming the necessity of learned representations. Notably, STIR achieves the best quality with only 5.11M parameters and 0.42 TFLOPs, striking a favorable accuracy–efficiency trade-off that no single baseline matches.

## Relation to Prior Work

STIR departs from the prevailing step-by-step paradigm in spike-to-image reconstruction (spike embedding → motion estimation → intensity recovery, as in Spk2ImgNet and WGSE) by proposing a joint spatio-temporal interactive decoder that performs warping-based feature alignment and synthesis-based feature filtering simultaneously in a coarse-to-fine manner. The hybrid spike embedding representation (HSER) draws on the explicit TFP statistics from Zhu et al. (2019) and combines them with a lightweight ResNet-based implicit encoder, bridging the interpretability of traditional methods with the expressive power of learned representations. The symmetric interactive attention block and multi-motion field estimation are inspired by bilateral transformer designs in video frame interpolation (BiFormer, AMT) and adapted to the spiking camera setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The joint motion-intensity learning paradigm addresses a fundamental bottleneck in spiking camera reconstruction: the circular dependency between accurate motion modeling and high-quality frame recovery. The 11× speedup over Spk2ImgNet while achieving 1.35 dB higher PSNR makes real-time deployment on consumer-grade spiking cameras more feasible, which is critical for applications in autonomous driving and robotics. The demonstrated transferability of HSER to event-based architectures (e.g., HyperE2VID) suggests the embedding module could serve as a general-purpose front-end for neuromorphic vision tasks. A key open question remains the performance degradation in extremely low-light conditions, where fixed-length spike streams accumulate insufficient photons, a limitation shared across the field.
