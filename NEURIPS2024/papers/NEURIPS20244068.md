## Problem

Event-based 3D reconstruction has relied on NeRF-based methods that suffer from slow training/inference and limited scene-editing capabilities, or on non-learning optimization approaches that lack robustness. Meanwhile, 3D Gaussian Splatting offers fast rendering and explicit scene representation but has not been adapted to process asynchronous event streams, leaving a gap in efficient, robust event-based 3D reconstruction for challenging real-world conditions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20244068/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes asynchronous DVS event streams (sparse spatiotemporal points with polarity ±1 and microsecond timestamps) and known camera poses, rather than dense frames. It outputs an explicit 3D Gaussian scene representation (per-Gaussian position, covariance, opacity, and color) that supports real-time novel-view rendering via rasterization. The photovoltage contrast between two close-in-time poses serves as the supervision signal, with a learnable sensor threshold θ jointly optimized alongside the scene.

## Method

Event-3DGS bridges the modality gap between asynchronous events and 3DGS by comparing photovoltage contrast (log-intensity differences) rather than absolute intensity: the rendered contrast is computed as the log-difference of two 3DGS alpha-blended images at nearby poses, while the ground-truth contrast is extracted from events via a high-pass filter (first-order transfer function sτ/(1+sτ)) that suppresses noise inherent in pure integration. A novel event-based reconstruction loss introduces a tolerance parameter β (optimal at 0.5 for noisy real data) that penalizes only deviations exceeding the expected photovoltage uncertainty, avoiding over-constraining the optimization with noisy event-derived signals. The total loss combines this contrast loss with an intensity estimation loss (weighted by α, ramped from 0 to 0.9 after 8000 iterations for initialization), and all scene and sensor parameters are optimized end-to-end via backpropagation through the differentiable 3DGS rasterizer.

## Results

On the real-world Event-Camera dataset, Event-3DGS achieves an average PSNR of 13.167 dB, SSIM of 0.417, and LPIPS of 0.245, substantially outperforming E2VID (PSNR 6.643), E2VID+3DGS (PSNR 6.670), and PI-3DGS (PSNR 9.402). On the DeepVoxels synthetic dataset, Event-3DGS reaches an average PSNR of 21.762 dB and SSIM of 0.925, edging out PI-3DGS (PSNR 21.740) and significantly surpassing E2VID+3DGS (PSNR 19.535). Against the NeRF-based Ev-NeRF on real data, Event-3DGS improves PSNR from 11.879 to 14.381 and reduces LPIPS from 0.338 to 0.250. Training converges to high-quality results in approximately 5 minutes on an RTX 3080 Ti.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Event-3DGS decisively outperforms E2VID and E2VID+3DGS on the real-world dataset, improving average PSNR by approximately 6.5 dB (13.167 vs. 6.643/6.670) and SSIM by 0.10–0.13, demonstrating that naively converting events to frames before applying 3DGS is far inferior to directly processing event streams. Against PI-3DGS (the authors' own baseline using pure integration without high-pass filtering), Event-3DGS gains about 3.8 dB in PSNR and 0.13 in SSIM, confirming the critical role of the high-pass filter in suppressing real-world noise. On the synthetic DeepVoxels dataset, Event-3DGS and PI-3DGS are closely matched (PSNR 21.762 vs. 21.740), indicating that the filtering advantage is most pronounced under noisy real-world conditions. In the appendix comparison with the NeRF-based Ev-NeRF, Event-3DGS wins on PSNR (14.381 vs. 11.879) and LPIPS (0.250 vs. 0.338) but slightly trails on SSIM for the office_zigzag sequence (0.430 vs. 0.415), while leading on the other two sequences. Overall, Event-3DGS achieves the best or near-best results across all metrics and all datasets, with the largest margins appearing in challenging real-world scenarios involving fast motion and low light.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously separate research threads: event-based 3D reconstruction (dominated by NeRF-based methods like Ev-NeRF, E-NeRF, and EventNeRF) and 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS), which had been applied almost exclusively to RGB image/video data. The key conceptual departure is using photovoltage contrast (log-intensity differences) as the natural interface between asynchronous event streams and 3DGS's alpha-blending renderer, rather than first reconstructing full images via E2VID-style pipelines. The high-pass filter-based photovoltage estimation and the tolerance-based event loss are novel technical contributions that address the noise sensitivity of pure event integration in real-world settings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Event-3DGS establishes the first viable 3DGS pipeline for event data, offering ~5-minute training times versus hours for NeRF-based alternatives, and demonstrates robustness in extreme real-world conditions (fast motion, low light, high noise) where prior NeRF methods were primarily validated on simulated or ideal-condition data. However, the method is limited to static scenes (no 4DGS extension), uses a single learned threshold per scene rather than temporal adaptation, and the absolute reconstruction quality on real data (PSNR ~13 dB) remains modest. Open questions include extending to dynamic scenes, handling temporal threshold drift, and validating on color event cameras with Bayer-pattern demosaicing.
