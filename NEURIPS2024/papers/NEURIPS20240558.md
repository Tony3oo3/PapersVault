## Problem

Long-horizon manipulation tasks with general instructions implicitly encapsulate multiple sub-tasks, and existing approaches decompose them using either language planning (stepwise sub-instructions) or vision planning (subgoal images) in isolation. Text-only guidance can be ambiguous and lack spatial expressiveness, while vision-only planning without explicit reasoning is prone to semantic conflicts and incoherent image chains. The paper asks whether a framework can simultaneously perform both language and vision planning, emulating the human cognitive synergy between logical reasoning and visual imagining.

## Input / Output

The method takes a robot observation image (RGB frame from a tabletop simulator), a general task instruction that may be text-only (e.g., 'stack blocks as a pyramid') or multi-modal with interleaved object/goal images (e.g., 'Stack objects <img> in this order <img><img>'), and a prompt template. It produces stepwise sub-instructions (autoregressive text tokens), coherent subgoal images (generated by a conditional diffusion model at each planning step), and SE(2) robot actions predicted by a CLIPort-based low-level policy conditioned on both the sub-instruction and subgoal image simultaneously via a cross-attention fusion block.

## Method

PERIA integrates a 7B MLLM (Vicuna + ViT-B-32) with a Stable Diffusion-based image editing model through three training stages. First, a lightweight encoding-side alignment freezes the vision encoder and LLM while training only the projection layer W on captioning tasks (scene description, action recognition, short demonstration understanding) to ground visual features in the LLM's hidden space. Second, instruction tuning with LoRA equips the MLLM to decompose complex instructions into stepwise sub-instructions, while N special [IMG] tokens appended after the reasoning phase are predicted jointly; an output projection module R transforms these [IMG] hidden states plus word embeddings into visual guidance U for the diffusion model, providing richer conditioning than text-only prompts. Third, a consistency alignment loss feeds the generated subgoal image and current observation back into the MLLM for action-recognition captioning, penalizing disagreement with the groundtruth sub-instruction to enforce mutual coherence between the two planning modalities. The total training objective combines reasoning (CE), generation (denoising), and consistency (CE) losses, jointly updating the MLLM, diffusion UNet, and projection modules.

## Results

PERIA achieves the highest success rate across all nine task categories spanning three domains (Blocks&Bowls, Letters, VIMA-BENCH), with headline numbers of 63.9% (Stacking), 72.3% (Matching), 71.1% (Spell), 74.8% (Rearrange), and 89.3% (Constraint). The largest gains over the strongest baseline CoTDiffusion appear on VIMA-BENCH tasks (e.g., 74.8% vs. 51.2% on Rearrange, 89.3% vs. 76.1% on Constraint), where multi-modal instructions make language-only planning insufficient. On language planning accuracy, PERIA reaches 97.6% token accuracy and 0.98 semantic similarity, far exceeding EmbodiedGPT (65.9% / 0.68) and PAR (58.2% / 0.63).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PERIA outperforms all six baselines across every one of the nine task categories. Against the end-to-end baseline CLIPort, PERIA gains roughly 45–81 percentage points (e.g., 74.8% vs. 5.8% on VIMA Rearrange), confirming that hierarchical planning is essential for long-horizon tasks. Among language-planning baselines, PERIA surpasses LISA by ~37–61 points, PAR by ~31–58 points, and EmbodiedGPT by ~12–36 points, with the largest margins on VIMA-BENCH where interleaved image-text instructions stress the limits of text-only decomposition. Among vision-planning baselines, PERIA beats SuSIE (which only handles single-step instructions and is given oracle sub-instructions) by ~29–38 points and CoTDiffusion by ~13–24 points; the gap over CoTDiffusion is most pronounced on VIMA-BENCH Rearrange (74.8% vs. 51.2%), where the absence of explicit LLM reasoning in CoTDiffusion leads to semantic inconsistencies in generated subgoal image chains. The paper also reports that PERIA's FID for generated subgoal images (10.2–13.5) is lower than CoTDiffusion (13.1–17.6) and SuSIE+oracle (18.1–19.4), indicating higher visual fidelity in vision planning.

## Relation to Prior Work

PERIA sits at the intersection of two established lines of work in robotic manipulation: MLLM-based language planning (EmbodiedGPT, PAR, LISA) and diffusion-based vision planning (SuSIE, CoTDiffusion). It departs from both by jointly training an MLLM (initialized from LLaVA-style alignment) and a conditional diffusion model (initialized from InstructPix2Pix) in a single end-to-end pipeline, using [IMG] tokens extracted from the MLLM's hidden states as richer guidance for image generation than text-only prompts. The key architectural departure is the consistency alignment loss, which feeds generated subgoal images back through the MLLM as an action-recognition captioning task, explicitly enforcing mutual coherence between the two planning modalities—a mechanism absent from prior single-modality planners.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first systematic evidence that holistic language-and-vision planning outperforms either modality alone on long-horizon manipulation, with the gap widening as task horizon increases. The finding that the image-generation loss acts as a regularizer that substantially improves language planning accuracy (97.6% vs. 83.7% token accuracy without vision planning) suggests a broader principle: cross-modal supervision can reduce hallucination in MLLM reasoning. However, all results are in simulation (Ravens, VIMA), the joint MLLM+diffusion training is computationally expensive (~42 hours on 8 V100s), and real-world deployment remains untested—open questions include whether the consistency loss generalizes to noisier, higher-dimensional visual domains and whether lighter-weight joint training schemes can match the gains.
