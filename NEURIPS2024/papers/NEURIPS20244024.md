## Problem

Gradient-based prompt optimization methods for vision-language models (e.g., CoOp, CoCoOp) treat text prompts as continuous learnable vectors, producing prompts that are uninterpretable to humans and prone to severe overfitting on base classes, which degrades generalization to novel classes. No prior work had explored using large language models as black-box optimizers to generate and refine natural-language prompts for vision-language models, leaving a gap between the interpretability of handcrafted prompts and the performance of learned prompts.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20244024/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a small set of few-shot training images (1 or 16 per class) along with a structured Prompt Optimization Prompt containing task instructions, LMM-generated image descriptions, and an episodic memory of the top-20 previously generated prompts with their accuracy and loss scores. The output is a single human-readable text prompt string with a <CLASS> placeholder (e.g., 'Identify the unique visual features of the <CLASS> flower accurately'), which replaces the default CLIP template and is evaluated on base classes to compute the optimization signal. The entire process is parameter-free: no model weights are updated, and the LLM (GPT-3.5 Turbo or GPT-4o) is queried via API for 100 iterations, generating 5 candidate prompts per step.

## Method

IPO replaces gradient descent with an LLM-driven iterative optimization loop. At each step, the LLM receives a Prompt Optimization Prompt composed of three components: (1) instructions defining the optimization task and output format, (2) textual descriptions of training images generated by a large multimodal model (MiniCPM-V-2.0) to inject visual context, and (3) an episodic memory of the top-20 past prompts ranked by accuracy (always including the baseline 'a photo of <CLASS>'), which provides in-context feedback for refinement. The LLM generates 5 candidate prompts per iteration; each is scored by computing cross-entropy loss and accuracy on the base-class training set, and the best-performing prompts are retained in the memory for subsequent iterations. After 100 iterations, the highest-accuracy prompt is selected as the final output. This design ensures the generated prompts remain natural language (hence interpretable) while the iterative feedback mechanism drives performance improvements, and the LMM-generated image descriptions enable dataset-specific prompt tailoring that improves generalization to novel classes.

## Results

In the 1-shot setting averaged over 11 datasets, IPO achieves 77.00% novel-class accuracy and 74.29% harmonic mean (H), surpassing the original CLIP baseline (74.22% novel, 71.70% H) by +2.78% on novel classes. In the 16-shot setting, IPO reaches 80.51% novel accuracy and 80.21% H, outperforming the strongest gradient-based baseline CoPrompt (77.23% novel, 80.48% H) by +3.28% on novel classes. The method's key advantage is consistent novel-class generalization: on challenging fine-grained datasets like FGVCAircraft (36.32% vs. 4.87% for CoPrompt) and EuroSAT (82.13% vs. 51.60% for CoPrompt), IPO dramatically outperforms prefix-tuning methods that overfit on base classes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IPO outperforms all six gradient-based prompt-learning baselines (CoOp, CoCoOp, MaPLe, PromptSRC, CoPrompt) on novel-class accuracy in both 1-shot and 16-shot settings, with the largest margins on fine-grained and satellite datasets (e.g., FGVCAircraft: 36.32% vs. 4.87% for CoPrompt; EuroSAT: 82.13% vs. 51.60% for CoPrompt). Against the original CLIP zero-shot baseline, IPO gains +2.78% novel accuracy in 1-shot and +6.29% in 16-shot, confirming that the LLM-optimized prompts add meaningful signal beyond the default template. In 16-shot, IPO (80.21% H) edges out CoPrompt (80.48% H) on the harmonic mean but clearly wins on novel classes (80.51% vs. 77.23%), while trailing on base classes (79.92% vs. 84.00%), reflecting a deliberate trade-off toward generalization. IPO also surpasses the knowledge-bank method VPT+L2P (74.29% vs. 73.49% H in 1-shot) and the recent black-box adaptation method LFA (80.21% vs. 78.83% H in 16-shot), as well as PLOT (80.21% vs. 76.20% in 16-shot). The one area where IPO does not lead is base-class accuracy in 16-shot, where PromptSRC (84.26%) and CoPrompt (84.00%) remain stronger, consistent with IPO's design goal of reducing base-class overfitting rather than maximizing it.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the OPRO paradigm (LLMs as black-box optimizers for NLP tasks) to vision-language models for the first time, replacing gradient descent on continuous prompt vectors with iterative LLM-generated text prompts. It departs fundamentally from the dominant prompt-learning lineage (CoOp, CoCoOp, MaPLe, CoPrompt, PromptSRC), which treats prompts as uninterpretable learnable parameters, by producing human-readable prompts through an episodic-memory-augmented meta-prompt. The integration of a large multimodal model (MiniCPM-V) to generate image descriptions that condition the LLM's prompt generation is a novel cross-modal element not present in prior LLM-as-optimizer or meta-prompting work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The interpretability of generated prompts addresses a practical transparency gap in VLM deployment, enabling human oversight and debugging of prompt behavior that gradient-based methods cannot provide. The consistent reduction in base-to-novel overfitting across 11 datasets (e.g., +2.78% novel-class accuracy over CLIP in 1-shot) suggests the approach generalizes better than prefix-tuning methods that require substantial data. Key limitations include computational cost scaling with dataset size (~$4 per input at 50k tokens with GPT-4), dependence on proprietary LLM APIs, and the finding that all tested LMMs failed to directly perform the optimization task, leaving open whether fine-tuned LMMs could handle image-conditioned prompt optimization natively.
