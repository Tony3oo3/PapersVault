## Problem

Existing diffusion-based driving scene generators are trained on small-scale datasets like nuScenes, limiting them to narrow appearance and layout distributions and causing overfitting to the training set's visual patterns. Driving simulators offer rich layout controllability and safety-critical scenario generation but produce unrealistic images due to limited 3D assets and missing background details (the sim-to-real gap). No prior method effectively combines the appearance diversity of real-world data with the layout controllability of a simulator.

## Input / Output

SimGen takes a text prompt describing the desired scene appearance and spatial conditions rendered by the MetaDrive simulator (depth map and semantic segmentation at 256×448), optionally augmented with rendered RGB, instance segmentation maps, and top-down views. It produces a photorealistic driving scene image at 256×448 resolution, along with realistic depth and segmentation maps as intermediate outputs. The simulator conditions are consumed in two stages: first transformed into realistic equivalents, then used as control signals for the final image synthesis, so the model never directly conditions on raw simulator renders for the output image.

## Method

SimGen employs a two-stage cascade diffusion pipeline. The first stage (CondDiff) fine-tunes Stable Diffusion 2.1 to generate realistic depth and segmentation maps from text alone; at inference, it injects noise-added simulator conditions into an intermediate denoising step (SDEdit-style, t_s=0.5) so the model converts them into realistic conditions without any additional training on simulated data. The second stage (ImgDiff) uses a ControlNet-guided diffusion model with a unified adapter that maps variable multimodal conditions (realistic depth/segmentation, text features, and optional simulator extra conditions) into a fixed-length control vector via per-modality convolutional modules with valid masks that zero out background regions in simulator conditions to prevent conflicts. The adapter resolves both modal discrepancy (different condition availability across datasets) and condition disparity (missing backgrounds in simulator renders), enabling a single unified control interface. Training uses DIVA (147.5 hours of YouTube driving videos from 73 locations plus MetaDrive simulated data) and nuScenes, with classifier-free guidance and slice-noise augmentation to improve robustness.

## Results

On the nuScenes validation set, SimGen (trained on DIVA) achieves the best FID of 15.6 and the highest pixel diversity D_pix of 26.6, surpassing the previous best (DrivingDiffusion, FID 15.9, D_pix 20.1) by +6.5 in diversity. In terms of controllability, SimGen retains the closest alignment with ground-truth layouts, with only a −3.4 mIoU drop on vehicle segmentation and −6.0 AP drop on car detection relative to the oracle BEVFusion baseline. For data augmentation, blending SimGen-generated images with real nuScenes data lifts single-frame BEVFusion vehicle mIoU to 39.0 (+4.4 over the real-data-only baseline).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SimGen outperforms all five nuScenes-trained baselines on both FID and D_pix. The closest competitor, DrivingDiffusion, is beaten by 0.3 FID and 6.5 D_pix; MagicDrive trails by 1.0 FID and 6.9 D_pix; BEVGen and BEVControl are substantially worse (FID 25.5 and 24.9, respectively). On controllability (Table 4), SimGen achieves the smallest relative drops across all perception metrics (e.g., −3.4 mIoU on vehicle seg, −6.0 AP on car det), compared to MagicDrive (−5.1, −9.7) and BEVGen (−28.7, −22.3). For data augmentation (Table 5), SimGen yields the highest augmented-model scores on every metric (mIoU_Road 78.9, mIoU_Vehi 39.0, AP_Car 49.1, AP_Truck 23.6), edging out MagicDrive (77.4, 37.7, 48.0, 22.8) and BEVGen (71.9, 34.2, 47.3, 21.1). The ablation (Table 6) confirms each component contributes: the cascade pipeline reduces FID by 2.3, ExtraCond adds +1.3 AP_Car, and the Unified Adapter further improves FID by 0.8.

## Relation to Prior Work

SimGen builds on the latent diffusion framework of Stable Diffusion 2.1 and the conditional control mechanism of ControlNet, but departs from prior driving scene generators (BEVGen, MagicDrive, DrivingDiffusion) that are confined to small-scale datasets like nuScenes and suffer from overfitting. Its key architectural departure is a cascade diffusion pipeline: the first stage (CondDiff) uses SDEdit-style intermediate-time injection to transform simulated depth/segmentation into realistic conditions without requiring paired sim-to-real data, and the second stage (ImgDiff) employs a unified adapter inspired by UniControl to fuse multimodal conditions while masking conflicting background information. The DIVA dataset, blending 120 hours of YouTube driving footage with 27.5 hours of MetaDrive simulator output, is a new contribution that no prior driving generation work has attempted at this scale and geographic breadth.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in autonomous driving: the inability of prior generators to produce diverse, safety-critical, or geographically novel scenes because they overfit to limited training data. The DIVA dataset (147.5 hours, 73 cities, 22 countries) is a substantial open resource that could benefit the broader Sim2Real and driving perception community. However, SimGen remains single-view (limiting BEV perception applications), inherits slow diffusion inference, and its video generation is only preliminary. Open questions include whether the cascade approach generalizes to multi-view or 3D-consistent generation, whether faster samplers (distillation, consistency models) can preserve the controllability gains, and whether the unified adapter strategy scales to even more condition modalities.
