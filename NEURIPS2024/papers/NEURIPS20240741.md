## Problem

Denoising Diffusion Bridge Models (DDBMs) achieve strong performance on coupled-data tasks like image-to-image translation but require hundreds of network evaluations to produce decent-quality samples, severely limiting their practical deployment. This paper addresses the gap by extending the consistency model paradigm—previously developed for standard diffusion PF-ODEs—to the PF-ODEs of diffusion bridges, enabling few-step generation while preserving the informative non-Gaussian prior that makes bridges advantageous over standard diffusion models.

## Input / Output

The method takes a source image (e.g., edge map, depth map, or partially masked image) and a terminal endpoint image as conditioning inputs, and produces a generated target image or inpainted image at resolutions from 64×64 to 256×256. A key structural detail is that the bridge PF-ODE is singular at the terminal time T, so sampling must begin with a one-step posterior (stochastic) evaluation to obtain x_{T−γ} before the consistency function can be applied, making the minimum sampling cost two network function evaluations rather than one.

## Method

The paper introduces Consistency Diffusion Bridge Models (CDBMs) that learn a consistency function h_θ(x_t, t, y) → x_ε directly mapping any point on the bridge PF-ODE trajectory to the starting point. Two key technical enablers are proposed: (1) a unified notation for DDBM design spaces (noise schedule, prediction target, network precondition) that decouples empirical choices from theoretical premises, and (2) a first-order ODE solver derived via exponential integrators for the general-form bridge ODE, which naturally connects consistency distillation and consistency training for arbitrary noise schedules. On top of this, two training paradigms are defined: Consistency Bridge Distillation (CBD), which distills from a pre-trained DDBM score predictor using the ODE solver to generate target points, and Consistency Bridge Training (CBT), which uses an unbiased score estimator to eliminate the need for a separate pre-trained model. The approach achieves 4×–50× sampling speedup over base DDBMs while matching or exceeding their quality at equivalent computational budgets.

## Results

On Edges→Handbags (64×64), CBT at NFE=2 achieves FID 0.80, surpassing DDBM at NFE=100 (FID 0.89) and representing a ~50× sampling speedup over DDBM's NFE=118 baseline (FID 1.83). On DIODE-Outdoor (256×256), CBT at NFE=2 reaches FID 2.93, approaching DDBM at NFE=50 (FID 3.20) for a ~25× speedup. On ImageNet 256×256 inpainting, CBT at NFE=4 achieves FID 4.77, matching DDBM at NFE=10 (FID 4.81) for a ~4× speedup. CDBM also outperforms DDBM at the same NFE by large margins (e.g., NFE=2: FID 0.80 vs 6.70 on Edges→Handbags).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CDBM (both CBD and CBT) dramatically outperforms DDBM at the same NFE: at NFE=2 on Edges→Handbags, CBT achieves FID 0.80 versus DDBM's 6.70, and on DIODE-Outdoor, CBT reaches FID 2.93 versus DDBM's 73.08. At NFE=2, CBT also beats DDBM at NFE=100 (FID 0.80 vs 0.89 on Edges→Handbags; FID 2.93 vs 2.57 on DIODE-Outdoor), confirming the 4×–50× sampling acceleration claim. Against other bridge and diffusion-based methods, CDBM at NFE=2 substantially outperforms I²SB (FID 0.80 vs 7.43 on Edges→Handbags; 2.93 vs 9.34 on DIODE-Outdoor), SDEdit (26.5 / 31.14), Rectified Flow (25.3 / 77.18), and Pix2Pix (74.8 / 82.4) by wide margins. On ImageNet inpainting, CBT at NFE=4 (FID 4.77) matches DDBM at NFE=10 (FID 4.81) and slightly beats I²SB (FID 4.9) and Palette (FID 6.1), while CBD at NFE=2 (FID 5.65) already surpasses DDBM at NFE=2 (FID 17.17). Across all three datasets, CBT consistently outperforms CBD, confirming that fine-tuning a pre-trained DDBM into a consistency function is more effective than distillation from a separate score model.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the consistency model paradigm (Song et al., ICML 2023) from standard diffusion PF-ODEs to the PF-ODEs of denoising diffusion bridge models (DDBMs, Zhou et al. 2023), which are conditioned on a fixed terminal endpoint and exhibit a singularity at t=T. The key technical departure is a general-form first-order ODE solver derived via exponential integrators that works across arbitrary noise schedules (VP, VE, Brownian, I2SB, Bridge-TTS), removing the VE+Euler restriction of the original consistency model derivation. The fine-tuning paradigm (CBT) and sigmoid-style training schedule are adapted from Geng et al. (2024), while the unified design-space notation draws on the bridge matching literature to decouple empirical choices from theoretical premises.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CDBMs deliver a 4×–50× reduction in NFEs for bridge-based generation, making DDBMs practically deployable for image-to-image translation and inpainting where they previously required hundreds of evaluations. The unified framework means practitioners can drop CDBM onto any existing bridge formulation (I2SB, DDBM-VP, Bridge-TTS) without re-deriving solvers. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger/more complex datasets (the ImageNet inpainting speedup is only ~4×), how to mitigate the known numerical instability of consistency training at small Δt, and whether the singularity-skipping step (posterior sampling at T−γ) introduces quality degradation at very small γ.
