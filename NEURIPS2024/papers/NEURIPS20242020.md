## Problem

Existing EFCIL methods that represent classes as Gaussian distributions in latent space assume covariance matrices remain constant across incremental tasks, yet updating the feature extractor causes both means and covariances to drift. Additionally, dimensionality collapse during training makes early-task covariance matrices lower-rank than later-task ones, introducing numerical instabilities when inverting matrices for classification and producing a task-recency bias that no prior method addresses at the representation level.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-100, 64×64 for TinyImageNet, 224×224 for ImageNet-based and fine-grained datasets) and outputs a class label via Bayes classification over memorized multivariate Gaussian distributions. During training, only current-task data is available (no exemplars stored); at inference, the model is task-agnostic and classifies by computing Mahalanobis distances to per-class Gaussians in a bottleneck latent space of dimension S (64 for from-scratch, 32 for fine-grained). Each class is stored as a mean vector and full S×S covariance matrix, requiring S + S(S−1)/2 parameters per class.

## Method

AdaGauss trains a ResNet-18 feature extractor with three components: (1) projected distillation through a learnable 2-layer MLP projector that maps current features to match frozen previous-task features, yielding better eigenvalue distributions than standard logit or feature distillation; (2) an anti-collapse loss L_AC that penalizes the Cholesky decomposition diagonal of each minibatch's feature covariance to remain positive, directly preventing dimensionality collapse without artificially altering stored covariances; and (3) after each task, an auxiliary adapter network ψ trained on current-task data maps old latent-space features to the new space, through which N=10000 samples from each memorized Gaussian are passed to recompute both the mean and full covariance of every old class. The total training loss is L = L_CE + L_AC + λ·L_PKD, and inference uses Bayes classification (Mahalanobis distance) over the adapted Gaussian representations.

## Results

AdaGauss achieves state-of-the-art results across all EFCIL benchmarks. Training from scratch on ImageNetSubset (T=10), it reaches 51.1% A_last and 65.0% A_inc, improving over the previous best EFC by 3.7 and 5.1 points respectively. On CIFAR-100 (T=10), AdaGauss attains 46.1% A_last and 60.2% A_inc. With a pre-trained backbone on CUB-200 (T=10), it achieves 55.8% A_last and 66.2% A_inc, outperforming EFC by 4.8 points in A_last.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AdaGauss consistently outperforms all 10 baselines across every dataset and task split in the from-scratch setting. Against the strongest prior method EFC [24], it improves A_last by 3.7% (ImageNetSubset T=10) to 6.8% (ImageNetSubset T=20), and by 2.5% on CIFAR-100 T=10. The gap over older baselines is much larger: IL2A [51] trails by 23.4–25.1 points on ImageNetSubset, FeTrIL [31] by 14.9–16.0 points, and FeCAM [10] by 12.4–13.6 points. In the pre-trained fine-grained setting (Table 2), AdaGauss beats EFC by 4.8% A_last on CUB-200 (T=10) and 4.4% on FGVCAircraft (T=10). In the half-dataset setting (Table 8), AdaGauss is slightly behind FeCAM, ACIL, and DS-AL, which freeze the backbone after the first task, suggesting the advantage of covariance adaptation is most pronounced when the feature extractor must adapt across tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Gaussian-representation line of EFCIL methods, most directly building on EFC (Magistri et al., 2024), which adapts only the means of memorized class distributions, and FeCAM (Goswami et al., 2024), which provides the Bayes-classification framework over Gaussian class models. AdaGauss departs from both by (1) adapting both means and covariances via a learned adapter network after each task, and (2) introducing a Cholesky-based anti-collapse loss that prevents the feature extractor from producing degenerate, low-rank covariance matrices. The projected distillation through a learnable projector is also a first in EFCIL, drawing on representational-learning ideas from Kornblith et al. (2019) to improve the eigenvalue spectrum of features across tasks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clear diagnostic for a long-standing problem in EFCIL: task-recency bias originates at the representation level (dimensionality collapse making early-task covariance matrices lower-rank and numerically unstable when inverted), not merely at the linear-head level as previously assumed. This reframing opens a path for other EFCIL methods to address the bias without ad-hoc covariance shrinking. Limitations include the reliance on a bottleneck layer that may cap representational capacity, the fact that cross-entropy only enforces separability within the current task (old classes can overlap), and slightly weaker results in the half-dataset setting compared to methods that freeze the backbone. Open questions include whether contrastive losses could better maintain inter-class separability across tasks and whether the approach extends to very few samples per class without the bottleneck.
