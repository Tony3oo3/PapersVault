## Problem

Safety-aligned LLMs can be jail-broken by fine-tuning on user data mixed with even 5% harmful samples, and existing fine-tuning-stage defenses (e.g., Vlguard) incur substantial extra computation per fine-tuning request. The authors show that a natural Bi-State Optimization (BSO) baseline—alternating between alignment and fine-tuning data—suffers severe alignment degradation (up to 17.6% higher harmful score) when fewer steps are allocated to the alignment state, and identify 'excess drift' toward switching checkpoints as the root cause of this convergence instability.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained LLM (Llama2-7B, Mistral-7B, or Opt-3.7B) that has already undergone SFT-based safety alignment, a user fine-tuning dataset (default 5000 samples with 10% harmful data from BeaverTails mixed in), and the same 10000-sample BeaverTails alignment dataset used in the alignment stage. It produces a LoRA-adapted fine-tuned model (rank 8, DoubleLoRA) that simultaneously performs well on the user task and resists harmful outputs. The fine-tuning uses AdamW at learning rate 1e-5 with batch size 5, and the default step allocation is 100 alignment / 900 fine-tuning steps per cycle.

## Method

Lisa builds on Bi-State Optimization, which alternates K1 steps of gradient descent on the alignment loss f(w) and K2 steps on the fine-tuning loss h(w) over T cycles. The key insight is that under asymmetric step allocation, iterates drift excessively away from the switching checkpoint between states, causing the model to overfit one sub-loss and forget the other. Lisa remedies this by adding a proximal term (ρ/2)‖w − w_switch‖² to each state's sub-problem, where w_switch is the checkpoint from the other state, effectively making each state's optimization 'lazy' and constraining drift. Theoretically, under L-smoothness and KL-property assumptions, the authors prove that ρ > L is necessary for convergence, yielding finite-time convergence for θ=0, linear rate for θ∈(0,1/2], and sub-linear rate for θ∈(1/2,1). Empirically, Lisa reduces average harmful score by 6.54% over BSO and 7.07% over Vaccine-SFT while maintaining finetune accuracy within 0.43%.

## Results

On the default setting (SST2, n=5000, Llama2-7B), Lisa achieves an average harmful score of 39.58% and finetune accuracy of 94.75%, outperforming all baselines on harmful score. Compared to the strongest fine-tuning-stage baseline Vlguard (43.28% HS, 95.34% FA), Lisa reduces harmful score by 3.68% at a cost of only 0.59% finetune accuracy. Against the alignment-stage baseline Vaccine-SFT (46.86% HS, 94.47% FA), Lisa reduces harmful score by 7.07% while also improving finetune accuracy by 0.28%. The method generalizes across models (Opt-2.7B, Llama2-7B, Mistral-7B) and datasets (SST2, AGNEWS, GSM8K, AlpacaEval), with average harmful score reductions of 11.9% and 11.17% over Vlguard on model and dataset generalization tests respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Lisa outperforms all three baselines on harmful score across all harmful ratios. Against Vaccine-SFT (alignment-stage defense), Lisa achieves a 7.07% lower average harmful score (39.58 vs. 46.86) while also matching or exceeding its finetune accuracy (94.75 vs. 94.47), showing that a fine-tuning-stage defense can be more effective than strengthening the alignment stage alone. Against Vlguard (fine-tuning-stage defense that mixes alignment data into fine-tuning), Lisa reduces harmful score by 3.68% (39.58 vs. 43.28) at a negligible 0.59% finetune accuracy cost (94.75 vs. 95.34). Against EWC (continual-learning regularization), Lisa achieves a 3.36% lower harmful score (39.58 vs. 42.94) and a dramatically higher finetune accuracy (94.75 vs. 18.23), as EWC's fixed reference point severely degrades task performance. The paper's own BSO baseline (without proximal term) is 6.54% worse on harmful score than Lisa, confirming the proximal term is essential for controlling excess drift under asymmetric step allocation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself within the growing literature on defending LLMs against harmful fine-tuning attacks, departing from alignment-stage solutions (Vaccine) and data-mixing fine-tuning-stage solutions (Vlguard, ForgetFilter) by introducing Bi-State Optimization (BSO), which alternates between alignment and user fine-tuning datasets. The key conceptual departure is the discovery that BSO suffers from 'excess drift' under asymmetric step allocation, and the remedy—adding a proximal term to constrain drift toward switching checkpoints—borrows directly from proximal algorithms in federated learning (FedProx) and meta-learning (MAML). The convergence analysis under the KL property follows standard nonconvex optimization frameworks, but its application to this two-state alternating safety alignment problem is novel.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Fine-tuning-as-a-service is a real and growing business model where providers are liable for model outputs, making computationally affordable defenses against harmful fine-tuning practically urgent. Lisa's 8.3% overhead over vanilla SFT makes it deployable at scale, and the diagnosis of excess drift provides a clear mechanistic explanation for why naive alternating optimization fails under compute constraints. However, the paper acknowledges limitations: the approach has weak extension to RLHF-based alignment, and the proximal term introduces a trade-off where very large ρ essentially freezes the model at its initial aligned state, sacrificing fine-tuning accuracy. Open questions include whether the method generalizes to more sophisticated attacks (e.g., bi-directional anchoring) and to multi-turn or agentic settings.
