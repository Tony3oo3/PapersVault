## Problem

Existing novel view acoustic synthesis methods rely on NeRF-based implicit models that are computationally expensive and condition binaural audio only on the listener's line-of-sight view, ignoring the broader 3D scene geometry and material properties that govern sound propagation (e.g., reflections off walls the listener cannot see). No prior work learns a holistic, explicit 3D scene representation that jointly encodes geometry and implicit material characteristics as conditioning for spatial audio.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240910/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a mono audio signal emitted by a sound source at a known 3D position, a set of N training observations (each pairing a camera RGB image with a one-second binaural recording at a specific listener pose), and a target listener pose (3D position + head orientation) at inference. It outputs a binaural audio signal (left and right ear channels) at the novel pose; on the SoundSpaces dataset it instead predicts room impulse responses. The 3D scene is represented as a point cloud of Gaussian ellipsoids initialized from SfM, with each point carrying a learned audio-guidance parameter that encodes implicit material properties.

## Method

AV-GS decouples physical scene geometry from acoustic material properties via a two-stage pipeline: a 3k-iteration 3D-GS warm-up learns explicit Gaussian ellipsoids (position, quaternion, scale, opacity, spherical harmonics) for scene geometry, then a 40k-iteration stage optimizes per-point audio-guidance parameters α (initialized from SH and quaternion features) through an acoustic field network F. F maps each point's α together with its normalized direction vector to the listener and source into a per-point context, which is averaged over the k-nearest points to both entities to form a holistic scene-conditioning vector. An audio binauralizer B (adapted from AV-NeRF) fuses this context with the listener pose to generate frequency-domain mixture and difference masks applied to the mono STFT magnitude. A key differentiator is an audio-aware point densification strategy that samples new Gaussian points where per-point gradient contributions exceed a threshold, specifically populating textureless regions (walls, doors) that dominate sound absorption and diversion but are under-represented in standard 3D-GS.

## Results

On the RWAVS dataset (binaural audio synthesis), AV-GS achieves an overall MAG of 1.417 and ENV of 0.140, outperforming AV-NeRF (1.504 / 0.145) by 5.7% and 3.4% respectively, and NAF (2.283 / 0.157) by a large margin. On the SoundSpaces dataset (RIR generation), AV-GS achieves T60 of 2.23%, C50 of 0.53 dB, and EDT of 0.014 s, surpassing AV-NeRF (2.47% / 0.57 dB / 0.016 s). Inference time is 0.08 s per query versus 1.4 s for AV-NeRF (single view) and 7.6 s (four views), a ~17× speedup over the single-view baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AV-GS outperforms all three neural baselines across both datasets and all metrics. Against AV-NeRF [17] (the closest competitor, also using audio+visual cues), AV-GS improves overall MAG by 5.7% (1.417 vs 1.504) and ENV by 3.4% (0.140 vs 0.145) on RWAVS, and T60 by 9.7% (2.23% vs 2.47%) on SoundSpaces, while being ~17× faster at inference (0.08 s vs 1.4 s). Against NAF [22] (audio-only, implicit 2D grid), AV-GS reduces overall MAG from 2.283 to 1.417 (38% improvement) and ENV from 0.157 to 0.140. Against INRAS [35] (audio-only, implicit neural representation), the gap is even larger: MAG drops from 2.460 to 1.417 (42% improvement). The paper also notes that AV-NeRF degrades qualitatively in scenes with complex geometry or meaningless listener views, whereas AV-GS's holistic scene representation remains robust in those cases.

## Relation to Prior Work

AV-GS repurposes 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) [14]—originally designed for visual novel-view synthesis—as the geometric backbone for novel view acoustic synthesis (NVAS), a task previously addressed by implicit neural field methods (NAF, INRAS) and the vision-conditioned AV-NeRF [17]. The key conceptual departure is a decoupling strategy: physical geometry is learned in a warm-up 3DGS stage, while a separate audio-guidance parameter α per point (initialized from spherical harmonics and quaternion features) is optimized in a second stage to capture implicit material properties. The binauralizer architecture is adapted from AV-NeRF, but the conditioning shifts from a single listener-view image to a holistic, pose-specific scene context derived from the acoustic field network over nearby Gaussian points.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 17× inference speedup (0.08 s vs. 1.4 s) over AV-NeRF while simultaneously improving binaural quality makes real-time spatial audio rendering in AR/VR more feasible, and the audio-aware point densification (populating texture-less walls where sound absorption/diversion occurs) offers a transferable insight for adapting visual 3DGS to other physics-driven tasks. However, the method still learns a separate representation per scene, limiting generalizability, and performance degrades in larger multi-room environments—open questions include cross-scene transfer, room-level decomposition, and whether the decoupled geometry/material formulation scales to outdoor or unbounded scenes.
