## Problem

Existing pedestrian trajectory prediction methods universally assume that all observed coordinates are present, yet real-world deployments face sensor failures, occlusions, and limited fields of view that produce incomplete trajectories. No dedicated dataset, imputation benchmark, or systematic evaluation of how prediction models handle imputed coordinates has been available for this task.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243955/cropped_page5_idx9.webp)

## Input / Output

The input consists of 8-frame observed trajectories (3.2 s at 0.4 s intervals) with 0–4 (easy) or 4–7 (hard) coordinates randomly replaced by NaN, accompanied by a binary missing mask of the same length. The output is twofold: imputed (x, y) coordinates at the masked positions, and a 12-frame predicted future trajectory (4.8 s). Missing indices are drawn uniformly at random from the 8 observed frames, so any temporal position is equally likely to be absent, and the test set is expanded by creating 5 copies per trajectory with 0–4 (or 4–7) missing values to ensure balanced coverage.

## Method

TrajImpute is constructed by uniformly sampling m missing indices from the 8 observed frames of each trajectory in ETH, HOTEL, UNIV, ZARA1, and ZARA2, setting those coordinates to NaN, and recording a binary mask. The paper then benchmarks six imputation methods (SAITS, US-GAN, Transformer, TimesNet, BRITS, M-RNN) using MAE, MSE, RMSE, and MRE computed only at masked positions, finding SAITS consistently best. The best imputed trajectories are fed to six trajectory prediction models (GraphTern, SGCN-ET, EQMotion, TUTR, LBEBM-ET, GPGraph) and evaluated with ADE/FDE under clean, easy-impute, and hard-impute settings. A key finding is that all prediction models degrade substantially under hard imputation, with EQMotion showing the smallest decline, indicating that current architectures are not robust to imputed inputs and that purpose-built imputation-aware predictors are needed.

## Results

On the TrajImpute imputation benchmark, SAITS achieves the lowest MAE across 9 of 10 dataset-protocol combinations, with particularly strong results on UNIV-M Easy (MAE = 0.19) and ZARA2-M Easy (MAE = 0.13). M-RNN is consistently the worst imputer (MAE > 5.0 on every subset). For trajectory prediction on imputed data, EQMotion shows the smallest degradation under the hard protocol (ETH ADE/FDE: 0.47/0.63 vs. 0.40/0.61 clean), while TUTR and GraphTern degrade most severely (ETH ADE/FDE: 1.12/1.53 and 0.78/0.77, respectively).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among the six imputation baselines, SAITS [14] dominates, achieving the best MAE on 9 of 10 dataset-protocol splits (e.g., 0.50 on ETH-M Easy vs. 0.65 for US-GAN and 1.14 for TimesNet). TimesNet [16] is the sole method to beat SAITS, on ZARA1-M Hard (MAE 0.57 vs. 0.63). US-GAN [62] and BRITS [22] occupy the middle tier with comparable errors, while the vanilla Transformer [23] and M-RNN [9] are consistently the weakest (M-RNN MAE > 5.0 on every subset). For trajectory prediction, EQMotion [63] is the most robust to imputation error, maintaining ADE/FDE of 0.47/0.63 on ETH Hard-impute versus 0.40/0.61 clean. In contrast, TUTR [64] and GraphTern [6] suffer the largest drops (ETH Hard-impute ADE/FDE: 1.12/1.53 and 0.78/0.77), and on HOTEL Hard-impute TUTR degrades to 3.36/3.95 from a clean 0.11/0.18. SGCN-ET [61], LBEBM-ET [61], and GPGraph [65] show intermediate degradation, with GPGraph being the least affected among the non-EQMotion methods on ETH (0.92/0.93).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two well-established research threads: pedestrian trajectory prediction (ETH, HOTEL, UNIV, ZARA datasets) and multivariate time-series imputation (SAITS, BRITS, M-RNN, TimesNet, US-GAN). While a handful of prior works (GC-VRNN, INAM, NAOMI, GMAT) have addressed trajectory imputation, they rely on non-public datasets and lack systematic benchmarking. TrajImpute carves new ground by repurposing general-purpose imputation methods for the specific structure of pedestrian trajectories and providing the first public, unified benchmark that evaluates both imputation quality and downstream prediction performance under controlled missing-data protocols (easy: ≤4 missing frames; hard: 4–7 missing frames out of 8).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper exposes a significant practical gap: all six evaluated trajectory prediction models degrade substantially when fed imputed observations, with hard-imputation causing 5–15× ADE/FDE increases on some subsets (e.g., Hotel: GraphTern 0.14→1.68 ADE). This suggests that current prediction architectures are not robust to the imputation errors introduced by even the best general-purpose imputer (SAITS), pointing to a need for either trajectory-specific imputation models or prediction models natively designed to consume incomplete observations. Limitations include coverage of only five small 2D datasets, a two-stage pipeline (impute-then-predict) that may not be optimal, and the absence of end-to-end imputation-aware prediction baselines in the comparison.
