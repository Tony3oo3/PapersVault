## Problem

Diffusion-based imitation learning policies (e.g., Diffusion Policy) can generate diverse, multimodal actions but require tens to hundreds of recursive network evaluations per decision, making them impractical for real-time robot deployment. Behavioral Cloning is fast (single forward pass) but collapses to a single mode in states with multiple valid actions. No existing framework simultaneously achieves efficient inference and behavioral diversity without requiring an additional distillation or reflow training stage.

## Input / Output

The policy takes a state observation as conditioning input — 76×76 RGB images for manipulation tasks (RoboMimic, LIBERO) or low-dimensional state vectors (4–6 dimensional) for navigation (Maze2D) — and produces two outputs: a generated action vector and a scalar variance estimate. The variance estimate is computed by a lightweight 4-layer MLP (hidden dim 512) that reads bottleneck features from the main U-Net/MLP backbone, and it dynamically controls how many ODE integration steps are taken at inference. The action itself is obtained by simulating a state-conditioned ODE from a Gaussian noise sample z₀ ~ N(0,I) over t ∈ [0,1].

## Method

AdaFlow represents the policy as a state-conditioned rectified flow ODE (dz_t = v_θ(z_t, t|s) dt), trained with a standard least-squares velocity-matching objective. The key insight is that the conditional variance of the training loss, σ²(x,t|s) = var(a − x₀ | x_t = x, s), quantifies the straightness of the ODE trajectory: when the expert action is deterministic at a state, the trajectory is a straight line and a single Euler step is exact. A separate variance estimation network σ_φ is trained in a second stage via a Gaussian negative log-likelihood loss on the residuals of the frozen velocity network. At inference, an adaptive ODE solver sets the step size as ε_t = Clip(η/σ_φ(z_t,t|s), [ε_min, 1−t]), so low-variance (deterministic) states are resolved in one step while high-variance (multimodal) states use more steps. A theoretical result (Proposition 3.3) bounds the per-step 2-Wasserstein discretization error by ε_t² · E[σ²], and a global error bound (Proposition 3.5) shows the overall error scales as (N_ada/N_max)·ε_min, guaranteeing that the adaptive scheme never degrades below the fixed-step baseline.

## Results

On the RoboMimic benchmark, AdaFlow achieves an average NFE of only 1.17 while matching or exceeding Diffusion Policy at 100 NFE across all six manipulation tasks (e.g., 0.96 vs 0.97 on Can-mh, 0.96 vs 0.91 on Push-T). On LIBERO, AdaFlow reaches 0.91 average success rate at 1.27 NFE, outperforming Diffusion Policy's 0.88 at 20 NFE. On D4RL Maze2D, AdaFlow achieves 0.98/1.00/0.96/0.86 success rates at 1.56 NFE, surpassing BC (1.00/1.00/0.92/0.76) on the harder mazes while maintaining behavioral diversity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AdaFlow at 1.17 NFE matches or exceeds Diffusion Policy at 100 NFE on 8 of 10 RoboMimic sub-tasks (e.g., 0.96 vs 0.91 on Push-T, 0.80 vs 0.72 on Transport-mh), while Diffusion Policy at 1 NFE is essentially non-functional (≤0.04 success). Against Rectified Flow (which requires an expensive reflow/distillation stage to achieve 1-step generation), AdaFlow matches or slightly exceeds performance (e.g., 0.96 vs 0.92 on Square-mh, 0.80 vs 0.76 on Transport-mh) without any additional training phase. Compared to BC, AdaFlow matches on simple tasks (Lift, Can) but substantially outperforms on complex multi-modal tasks (Transport: 0.92/0.80 vs 0.76/0.62; ToolHang: 0.88 vs 0.67). IBC and BET are dramatically outperformed on most tasks (IBC drops to 0.00 on Can, Square, Transport; BET reaches only 0.38/0.21 on Transport). On LIBERO, AdaFlow (0.91 avg at 1.27 NFE) outperforms Diffusion Policy (0.88 avg at 20 NFE) and Rectified Flow (0.88 avg at 1 NFE with reflow), confirming consistent gains across benchmarks.

## Relation to Prior Work

AdaFlow builds directly on the Rectified Flow formulation (Liu et al., ICLR 2022), adopting its linear-interpolation ODE and velocity-field training objective as the policy backbone. It departs from Diffusion Policy (Chi et al., 2023) by replacing the multi-step DDPM/DDIM sampling with a flow-based ODE, and from Rectified Flow's reflow/distillation pipeline by instead exploiting the conditional variance of the training loss to drive an adaptive Euler step size at inference. The key conceptual departure is the identification of a previously unexploited link: the variance of the state-conditioned flow loss quantifies ODE trajectory curvature, enabling one-step generation for deterministic states without any additional training stage.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ~10× reduction in inference cost (average NFE ≈ 1.2–1.6 vs. 100 for Diffusion Policy) while matching or exceeding success rates makes flow-based policies practically viable for real-time robot control, a setting where latency is critical. The theoretical bound (Proposition 3.5) connecting the adaptive step count to global Wasserstein error provides a principled guarantee absent in heuristic adaptive solvers. Open questions include whether the variance-adaptive mechanism extends to online RL or multi-agent settings, whether the two-stage training (velocity then variance) remains optimal at scale, and whether the approach generalizes to continuous-time or stochastic flow formulations beyond rectified flow.
