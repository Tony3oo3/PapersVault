## Problem

LLMs struggle with reasoning because pre-training corpora of human-written text contain few high-quality reasoning samples, and prior synthetic logic corpora were designed without systematic grounding in symbolic logic theory. It remained unexplored whether such corpora could benefit state-of-the-art LLMs (70B+), and which sample design choices actually matter for maximizing reasoning gains.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242339/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives a set of natural-language facts (premises) containing arbitrary, unknown content drawn from a ~100k-word WordNet vocabulary, along with a hypothesis statement and up to 20 adversarial distractor formulas. It must generate the intermediate logical deduction steps that derive the hypothesis from the facts, followed by a final answer label (proved, disproved, or unknown). Training uses 100k samples (~0.1B tokens) for a single epoch, with loss computed only on the generated steps and label, not on the input facts.

## Method

The paper establishes four design principles grounded in first-order predicate logic: (1) assign arbitrary content to formula variables so LLMs learn to reason with unknown facts, (2) include adversarial distractors (e.g., a premise F∧G reduced to just F) to teach when conclusions cannot be derived, (3) construct multi-step deductions from axioms plus representative theorems (syllogism, contraposition, De Morgan's laws) with 1–8 steps, and (4) use nested linguistic templates yielding combinatorially diverse English expressions per logical formula. Based on these principles, the authors build FLD×2, a synthetic corpus of ~100k multi-step deduction samples generated via forward/backward random deduction followed by template-based English conversion. Training is supervised fine-tuning on LLaMA-3.1 (8B/70B) using the Recall Adam optimizer, which regularizes parameter updates to prevent the unknown facts in the corpus from displacing the model's pre-trained knowledge—a critical finding, as omitting this step causes performance to drop below the untrained baseline.

## Results

On LLaMA-3.1-70B, ALT on FLD×2 achieves the highest average score of 64.4 across 31 benchmarks, a +4.4-point gain over the untrained baseline (60.0). The largest individual gains are on logical reasoning benchmarks (up to +30 points, e.g., RobustLR from 49.6 to 81.6), coding (HumanEval from 32.3 to 42.6, +10.3 points), and MMLU-Pro (50.1 to 60.8, +10.7 points). On the 8B model, FLD×2 yields a +4.1-point average gain (47.9→52.0) with Logic improving from 42.8 to 52.2. The paper also reports up to +5 points on BBH (0-shot CoT: 82.1→83.6) and up to +6 points on NLI benchmarks (HELP: 45.8→51.3).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FLD×2 consistently outperforms all three baseline corpora (PARARULE-Plus, RuleTaker, FLD) on the 70B model. Against PARARULE-Plus (⊕ALT-PRP), FLD×2 gains +4.0 points on average, +8.4 on Logic, and +3.2 on Code, while PRP provides only marginal improvement over the untrained baseline. Against RuleTaker (⊕ALT-RT), FLD×2 leads by +1.7 on average, +4.7 on Logic (66.1 vs 61.4), and +1.6 on MMLU-Pro (60.8 vs 59.2), though RT is competitive on Math (62.1 vs 63.3). Against the closest competitor FLD (⊕ALT-FLD), FLD×2 edges ahead by +0.2 on average, +0.4 on Logic, +0.8 on NLI, and +0.9 on MMLU-Pro, while FLD slightly leads on Math (63.6 vs 63.3). Ablation experiments (Table 3) confirm that removing any single design principle (DP1–DP4) degrades performance, with the largest drop from removing adversarial distractors (DP2: Avg 52.0→50.6 on 8B). Critically, training without the Recall Adam optimizer causes severe degradation (e.g., 8B Avg drops from 52.0 to 43.5 with PRP), confirming that knowledge-forgetting prevention is essential for ALT to succeed.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the synthetic logic corpus line of work initiated by RuleTaker (Clark et al., 2021) and FLD (Morishita et al., 2023), which generated multi-step deductive reasoning samples to train language models. The key departure is a systematic, theory-grounded design: the authors derive four design principles from symbolic logic (completeness of first-order predicate logic, the arbitrariness of induction, the need for negative examples, and linguistic diversity) and instantiate them in FLD×2 with a ~100k vocabulary, adversarial distractors, ~50 deduction rules, and combinatorially diverse expressions. Unlike prior work that validated synthetic logic training only on small models (T5, RoBERTa), this paper demonstrates the approach on state-of-the-art LLaMA-3.1 (8B and 70B), and introduces Recall Adam to prevent catastrophic forgetting of pre-trained knowledge during fine-tuning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that even LLaMA-3.1-70B, pre-trained on 15+ trillion tokens, gains up to 30 points on logical reasoning and up to 10 points on math/coding from just 100k synthetic samples (~0.1B tokens), suggesting that targeted reasoning training is a highly sample-efficient complement to scale. The generalization to NLI and abductive reasoning—tasks not directly present in the training data—indicates that LLMs can integrate pre-trained commonsense knowledge with newly acquired logical reasoning, pointing toward a path to versatile AI. Limitations include coverage of only deductive reasoning in first-order predicate logic, and modest gains (≤2 points) on tasks requiring complex multi-step procedures or advanced domain knowledge, suggesting that ALT alone is insufficient for all reasoning challenges and that combining it with distillation or task-specific training may be necessary.
