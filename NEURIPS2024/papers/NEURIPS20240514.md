## Problem

Anomaly detectors are effective at flagging deviations but operate as black boxes, leaving users without actionable explanations of why an input is anomalous. While localization methods identify where the anomaly is, they fail to articulate what a non-anomalous version should look like, and no prior work provides a formal, domain-independent framework for specifying and evaluating the quality of such counterfactual repairs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240514/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Given an anomalous input (RGB image or multivariate time-series window of length 100) and a linearly decomposable anomaly detector, the method first binarizes feature-wise anomaly scores via a 90th-percentile threshold to identify the anomalous region ω(x). The output is a repaired sample x_fix evaluated against four formal properties—overall score reduction, similarity in non-anomalous regions, localized improvement in the anomalous region, and non-degradation elsewhere—rather than a single scalar metric, enabling per-region diagnostic of repair quality.

## Method

The core insight is that many practical anomaly detectors (reconstruction-based, normalizing-flow, language-model perplexity) satisfy linear decomposability, where the total score is a sum of per-feature scores plus a regularizer, enabling four domain-independent formal desiderata for counterfactual repairs. These properties are cast as differentiable loss functions that guide a diffusion model (DDPM for images, Diffusion-TS for time series) trained exclusively on non-anomalous data. At every denoising step, the iterate is nudged by the gradient of a weighted combination of the four property losses, while a masked in-filling operation replaces the non-anomalous region of each iterate with the corresponding noised version of the original input to enforce structural preservation. This property-guided diffusion outperforms unguided generation by 60–84% on median metric improvements across vision (MVTec-AD, VisA) and time-series (SWaT, HAI, WADI) benchmarks, with 99.26% of guided repairs classified as non-anomalous at 95% conformal confidence.

## Results

AR-Pro (property-guided diffusion) outperforms the unguided diffusion baseline across all four formal explainability metrics on both vision and time-series data. On VisA, the median improvements are +26.54% (M_s), +97.46% (M_d), +146.42% (M_ω), and +114.16% (M_ω̄); on MVTec-AD, +8.35%, +97.34%, +29.15%, and +154.76% respectively. For time-series, Llama-2 achieves median improvements of +46.36% (M_s), +20.77% (M_d), +110.32% (M_ω), and +91.23% (M_ω̄) across SWaT, WADI, and HAI. In terms of True Negative Rate (95% conformal threshold), guided repairs achieve a median TNR of 100% on both VisA and MVTec-AD, and 95.33% average on time-series datasets, versus a baseline average of only 2.67% on time-series.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's sole comparison is between AR-Pro (property-guided diffusion) and an unguided diffusion baseline (standard DDPM / Diffusion-TS without the property-based guidance term). AR-Pro wins on all four formal metrics across every dataset: the similarity metric M_d improves by ~97% on both vision datasets (reducing the ℓ² distance in non-anomalous regions from ~300–650 to ~8–10), and the localized improvement M_ω improves by 146% (VisA) and 29% (MVTec-AD). On time-series, Llama-2-based AR-Pro achieves +110% on M_ω and +91% on M_ω̄, while GPT-2-based AR-Pro achieves +178% on M_ω but slightly worsens M_d (−34%), likely due to the broader range of adjustments needed for smooth signal repairs. The True Negative Rate comparison is the most dramatic: on time-series, the baseline TNR is near zero (0.00–0.08) while guided repairs reach 0.86–1.00, confirming that unguided diffusion frequently fails to produce non-anomalous outputs. Overall, 99.26% of guided repairs across both domains are classified as non-anomalous at 95% confidence, versus far lower rates for the baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate threads—counterfactual explanations for anomaly detection (Sulem et al. [56], which is time-series-specific and uses a different property set) and anomaly repair via generative models [21,31,70]—under a single formal framework. The core algorithmic machinery is adapted from DDPM [24], gradient-based guidance from Dhariwal & Nichol [19], and masked in-filling from SDEdit [39], but the key conceptual departure is the 'linear decomposability' assumption on anomaly detectors, which enables domain-independent formal properties (P1–P4) for evaluating repair quality. Unlike prior counterfactual explanation work that relies on task-specific heuristics, AR-Pro grounds its desiderata in the algebraic structure of the detector's scoring function.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The formal, domain-independent properties provide a principled evaluation protocol for anomaly repairs that was previously absent, addressing a gap the authors identify in the explainability literature. The cross-domain validation (vision + time-series) with the same property set is encouraging, but the linear decomposability assumption, while common, excludes detectors with non-additive scoring (e.g., some transformer-based approaches). Open questions include whether the framework extends to non-linearly-decomposable detectors, how to reduce the significant inference-time overhead (nearly 2× for vision), and whether the properties remain meaningful when the anomaly detector itself is imperfect (as shown by the modest AUROC scores in Appendix B).
