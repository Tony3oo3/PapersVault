## Problem

Federated learning on resource-constrained edge devices faces prohibitive communication and computation costs, yet existing sparse FL methods either use unstructured pruning that is hard to exploit for computation savings, fix sparsity patterns without optimizing them, or still require transmitting large subsets of model parameters. No prior framework simultaneously optimizes sparse model structures while keeping both communication and computation overhead minimal.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242746/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each of N=100 clients holds a local non-iid image dataset (FMNIST 28×28, CIFAR-10/100 32×32) partitioned via Dirichlet distributions (α=0.2 or 0.1). The method outputs per-client class labels for image classification, but its distinctive outputs are the personalized sparse model structures (which filters/neurons are pruned) and the shared global thresholds that encode aggregated parameter importance. Crucially, only thresholds—at most 1% of total model parameters—are transmitted between server and clients, while all model parameters remain local and personalized.

## Method

SpaFL defines a trainable threshold for each filter or output neuron; if the mean magnitude of a filter's connected parameters falls below its threshold, all those parameters are pruned (structured sparsity via a unit-step mask). The key communication trick is that only these thresholds are exchanged with the server, not parameters, reducing uplink/downlink costs by roughly two orders of magnitude versus FedAvg. The threshold gradient, derived via the straight-through estimator, equals the negative element-wise product of the parameter gradient and parameter value, so thresholds naturally encode parameter importance; the difference between consecutive global thresholds Δτ(t) captures aggregated importance history that clients use to nudge their local parameters (Eq. 13) before local training. A sparsity regularizer R(t)=Σ exp(−τᵢ) progressively raises thresholds to enforce sparsity, while prematurely pruned filters are automatically recovered through joint optimization of τ and w. A generalization bound (Theorem 1) shows that lower average model density ρ̄ tightens the bound, providing theoretical justification that sparsity improves generalization.

## Results

SpaFL achieves the best accuracy on CIFAR-10 (69.75%) and CIFAR-100 (40.80%), outperforming the dense FedAvg baseline by 8.42% and 5.29% respectively, while using only 0.17% of FedAvg's communication and 12.0% of its computation. On FMNIST, SpaFL reaches 89.21% accuracy (nearly matching FedSpa's 89.30%) with 99.8% less communication. The achieved model densities are 35.36%, 30.57%, and 35.38% for FMNIST, CIFAR-10, and CIFAR-100, respectively, confirming that structured sparsity via trainable thresholds yields both efficiency and accuracy gains.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SpaFL outperforms all six baselines on CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100 accuracy while using the least communication and FLOPs. Against FedAvg (dense baseline), SpaFL gains +8.42% on CIFAR-10 and +5.29% on CIFAR-100 while consuming only 0.17% of its communication and 12.0% of its computation. Among structured-sparse baselines, SpaFL beats HeteroFL by +2.92% on CIFAR-10 and +2.98% on CIFAR-100, Fjord by +3.37% and +1.67%, and FedP3 by +2.21% and +3.07%, all while using 99.6–99.9% less communication. FedSpa achieves a marginally higher FMNIST accuracy (89.30% vs. 89.21%) but requires 298× more communication (55.26 Gbit vs. 0.19 Gbit) and 2.2× more FLOPs. FedPM, which only trains binary masks with frozen parameters, is the weakest baseline, trailing SpaFL by 26.48% on CIFAR-10 and 12.24% on CIFAR-100. The Local-only ablation (no threshold communication) confirms that sharing global thresholds is essential: it drops accuracy by 4.90% on CIFAR-10 and 7.03% on CIFAR-100 relative to full SpaFL.

## Relation to Prior Work

SpaFL builds directly on the trainable-threshold pruning mechanism of Dynamic Sparse Training (Liu et al., 2020), repurposing it within a federated learning context where only thresholds—not parameters or binary masks—are communicated between clients and the server. This departs from prior sparse-FL work in a key way: HeteroFL and Fjord fix sparsity patterns, FedPM communicates full binary masks (same downlink cost as FedAvg), and FedSpa/FedP3 still exchange subsets of model parameters. SpaFL's additional contribution is the parameter-importance extraction step (Eq. 13), which uses the difference between consecutive global thresholds to nudge local parameters in a direction informed by aggregated client importance, a mechanism not present in any of the cited baselines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 99.83% communication reduction relative to FedAvg while simultaneously improving accuracy is a practically significant efficiency win for resource-constrained edge deployments, and the generalization bound (Theorem 1) provides theoretical grounding that sparser models generalize better under this framework. However, the method cannot explicitly target a desired sparsity level (requiring multiple runs to tune α), the evaluation is limited to three small image-classification datasets (FMNIST, CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100) with at most ~11M parameters, and the convergence analysis is restricted to E=1 local epochs. Open questions include whether the threshold-sharing mechanism scales to larger models (e.g., ImageNet-scale), whether the XOR-based parameter update remains effective under extreme data heterogeneity, and how the approach interacts with differential privacy guarantees beyond the theoretical bound.
