## Problem

The k-sparse parity problem is a fundamental benchmark in computational complexity, with an established Statistical Query (SQ) lower bound of Ω(d^k) queries for learning. Prior gradient-based methods achieved sample complexity Õ(d^{k+1}) (Barak et al., 2022), leaving open whether SGD could match the SQ lower bound. This paper provides an affirmative answer, closing the gap to Õ(d^{k-1}) samples.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243590/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes binary input vectors x ∈ {-1,1}^d drawn uniformly, with labels y = Π_{j∈A} x_j encoding the parity of k specific coordinates (|A| = k, k ≤ O(√d)). The output is a trained two-layer network with m = 2^Θ(k) neurons that achieves a constant positive margin γm on the correlation loss, yielding test error at most ε. The second-layer weights a_r ∈ {±1} are fixed during training, and the network uses a polynomial activation σ(z) = z^k rather than standard ReLU.

## Method

The core architecture is a two-layer fully-connected network with polynomial activation σ(z) = z^k, binary initialization w_r^(0) ~ Unif({±1}^d), and a correlation loss ℓ(z) = 1 − z. Training uses sign SGD with a modified sign function featuring a dead-zone threshold ρ = 0.1k!, combined with weight decay λ = 1. The key mechanistic insight is that neurons split into 'good' (a_r = Π sign(w_{r,j}^{(0)})) and 'bad' classes: good neurons' feature coordinates remain fixed at their initial ±1 values while noise coordinates decay exponentially, whereas bad neurons' feature coordinates also decay. After T = O(k log d) iterations with batch size B = Õ(d^{k-1}), the trained network approximates (m/2^{k+1})·f(W*, x) where W* is a constructed 'good network' with margin k!·2^k, yielding total sample complexity Õ(d^{k-1}) that matches the SQ/CSQ lower bound up to logarithmic factors.

## Results

The paper achieves a sample complexity of Õ(d^{k-1}) for the k-sparse parity problem using a two-layer network with 2^Θ(k) neurons trained by sign SGD with batch size Õ(d^{k-1}) and O(k log d) iterations. This matches the Statistical Query (SQ) and Correlational SQ (CSQ) lower bound of Ω(d^{k-1}) samples (equivalently Ω(d^k) total queries) up to logarithmic factors. Empirically, the method achieves 99.69% test accuracy for k=2, 97.75% for k=3, and 96.89% for k=4 (averaged over 10 runs), confirming the theoretical guarantees.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's main improvement is over Barak et al. (2022), which achieved Õ(d^{k+1}) sample complexity with the same 2^Θ(k) width; this paper reduces the sample complexity by two orders of d to Õ(d^{k-1}), matching the SQ/CSQ lower bound. Compared to Edelman et al. (2024), which requires width (d/s)^k (polynomial in d) and sample complexity (s/k)^{k-1}·d²·log(d)/ε², this paper uses only constant-in-d width 2^Θ(k) and achieves strictly better sample complexity d^{k-1}·polylog(d) for k≥2. Against Suzuki et al. (2023), which achieves O(d) samples but requires exponential width e^d and e^d iterations (making it computationally infeasible), this paper uses only 2^Θ(k) width and O(k log d) iterations. For the special case k=2 (XOR), the result matches Glasgow (2023) in sample complexity (d·polylog(d)) but improves width from polylog(d) to O(1) and iterations from polylog(d) to log d. The method also matches the sample complexity of Abbe et al. (2023a) under Gaussian inputs, but does so for Boolean inputs with a fundamentally different training protocol (large-batch sign SGD over Õ(1) iterations vs. projected SGD over Õ(d^{k-1}) iterations).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Barak et al. (2022), who established that SGD with a two-layer ReLU network of width 2^Θ(k) can learn k-sparse parity with Õ(d^{k+1}) samples. The key methodological departure is replacing ReLU with a polynomial activation σ(z)=z^k and standard SGD with sign SGD (with a dead-zone threshold), which together create a mechanism where good neurons' feature coordinates are preserved while noise coordinates and bad neurons' coordinates decay exponentially. The result also subsumes Glasgow (2023) for the k=2 XOR case and aligns with the leap-k framework of Abbe et al. (2023a), though the analysis here is tailored to Boolean inputs rather than Gaussian.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first gradient-based method to match the Statistical Query lower bound (Ω(d^k) queries) for k-sparse parity, closing a gap of d^2 in sample complexity over prior SGD results. The conceptual insight—that sign SGD's gradient normalization combined with polynomial activation naturally separates signal from noise coordinates without explicit feature selection—may inform future work on other structured learning problems. Open questions include extending the analysis to non-standard coordinate systems, ReLU/sigmoid activations, and non-isotropic data distributions, all of which the authors identify as limitations.
