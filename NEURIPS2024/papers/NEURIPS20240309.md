## Problem

Machine learning benchmarks for robotics typically rely on oversimplified simulated environments that neglect critical real-world deployment factors such as safety constraints, sim-to-real gaps, limited data availability, real-time computation limits, and dynamic (non-quasi-static) interactions. This paper addresses that gap by introducing a structured, competitive benchmark on robot air hockey that explicitly evaluates the robustness, reliability, and safety of learning-based approaches under conditions that closely mirror real-world robotic deployment.

## Input / Output

The agent receives a 20-dimensional observation (puck x-y position, yaw, velocity; 7-DoF joint position and velocity) extended to 23 dimensions when an opponent mallet is present, and must output desired joint position/velocity commands at 50 Hz within a 1000 Hz simulation loop. Commands are interpolated via polynomial schemes (linear through quintic) to produce smooth trajectories, and the evaluation environment deliberately injects observation noise, tracking loss, model mismatch, and airflow disturbances to simulate the sim-to-real gap. Participants are restricted to one evaluation per day on the modified simulator, mimicking the cost and scarcity of real-world data collection.

## Method

The benchmark is organized as a three-stage competition: a qualifying stage with three sub-tasks (Hit, Defend, Prepare) each evaluated over 1000 episodes, a tournament stage with full 15-minute competitive games, and a real-world validation stage deploying top solutions on Kuka LBR IIWA 14 robots with a compliant end-effector and ADRC-based torque control. A central design element is the deployability score, which accumulates weighted penalty points for safety violations (end-effector table-height and boundary constraints, joint position/velocity limits, per-step computation time) and categorizes solutions into deployable, improvable, or non-deployable tiers. The retrospective analysis of 13+ submitted solutions reveals that hybrid approaches combining learning (RL, imitation learning, self-play) with robotics priors (model predictive control, kinematic constraints, physics-based inductive biases) consistently outperform purely data-driven methods, and that penalty-based safety training is brittle under out-of-distribution evaluation conditions.

## Results

In the qualifying stage, AiRLIHockey achieved the highest overall score of 73.8 (Hit: 54.9%, Defend: 84.5%, Prepare: 90.3%) with 327.5 penalty points, followed by Air-HocKIT at 66.2 (Hit: 52.2%, Defend: 79.0%, Prepare: 68.6%) with 341.0 penalty points. In the tournament stage, AiRLIHockey dominated with 33 total points (15 in Round 1, 18 in Round 2), scoring 270 goals against only 8 conceded across all matches. The two provided baselines scored 28.5 (planning-based) and 27.8 (ATACOM safe RL), with ATACOM achieving the lowest penalty points (33.0) among all teams, indicating superior safety compliance at the cost of task performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The planning-based baseline (Liu et al. 2021, IROS) achieves a qualifying score of 28.5 with 12.8% Hit, 25.4% Defend, and 66.3% Prepare success rates, but accumulates 352.5 penalty points, placing it in the 'Deployable' category. The ATACOM safe RL baseline (Liu et al. 2022, CoRL) scores 27.8 with 18.4% Hit, 36.1% Defend, and 30.1% Prepare, but stands out with only 33.0 penalty points—the lowest of all 13 teams—demonstrating that safety-constrained RL can achieve near-perfect constraint compliance. Both baselines are substantially outperformed by the top two teams: AiRLIHockey exceeds the planning baseline by 45.3 points overall and the ATACOM baseline by 46.0 points, while Air-HocKIT leads by 37.7 and 38.4 points respectively. Notably, the ATACOM baseline's superior safety (33.0 vs. 327.5–341.0 penalty points for the winners) highlights the performance-safety trade-off that the challenge is designed to expose.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the authors' prior work in robot air hockey control (Liu et al., IROS 2021 for planning-based baselines; Liu et al., CoRL 2022 for safe RL on constraint manifolds/ATACOM) to construct the first structured benchmark specifically targeting real-world deployment challenges in dynamic robot tasks. It departs from prior robot learning competitions (REAL, Learn to Move, TOTO, MyoSuite) by explicitly incorporating a sim-to-sim gap with hidden evaluation modifications, deployability scoring with safety constraints, and a real-world validation stage. The air hockey task itself has a long history in robotics (Bishop & Spong 1999; Namiki et al. 2013), but no prior benchmark had codified the interplay between performance, safety compliance, and computational constraints in a competitive multi-agent dynamic setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The challenge provides a concrete, reproducible testbed for evaluating whether learning-based methods can handle the full stack of real-world robotics challenges (safety, sim-to-real gap, limited data, real-time computation, observation noise) simultaneously, rather than in isolation. Key open questions identified include: making penalty-based safety robust to out-of-distribution situations, enabling RL to handle long-horizon competitive tasks without hand-crafted state machines, and combining data-driven learning with physical inductive biases (kinematics, MPC) for deployable solutions. Limitations include the assumption of perfect perception (Optitrack), a small participant pool (~13 teams), and the fact that real-world deployment required significant manual tuning, limiting the generalizability of the sim-to-real conclusions.
