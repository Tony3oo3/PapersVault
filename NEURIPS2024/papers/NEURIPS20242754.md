## Problem

Universal image embedding models that handle multiple fine-grained domains in a single network suffer from two key issues: conflicting visual cues across domains (e.g., color matters for food but not for car models) degrade joint classification training, and vastly different data distributions (class counts, long-tail skew) mean a fixed sampling schedule cannot adequately serve all domains. This leaves a significant performance gap between efficient universal solutions and expensive collections of per-domain specialist models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242754/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images and produces a 64-dimensional L2-normalized universal embedding per image, used for Euclidean nearest-neighbor retrieval. At test time, the index set pools images from all 8 domains (food, cars, products, clothing, natural world, artworks, landmarks, retail), so cross-domain false positives are possible. The backbone is a ViT-Base/16 whose [CLS] token is projected via a linear layer to the 64D embedding space, while per-domain teacher heads project to 256D spaces that are used only during training for distillation.

## Method

UDON introduces a shared-backbone multi-teacher online distillation framework: a single ViT backbone feeds both a universal student head (64D) and per-domain teacher heads (256D), all trained jointly in one pass. The student is trained with four losses — its own classification loss, the active domain teacher's classification loss, a relational distillation loss matching intra-batch cosine similarity matrices between student and teacher embeddings, and a logit distillation loss minimizing KL divergence between temperature-scaled softmax outputs. A dynamic sampling mechanism updates domain selection probabilities every 1000 steps proportional to each domain's current classification loss, so harder domains (large class counts, long-tail distributions like Met and GLDv2) receive more frequent training batches. Crucially, sharing the backbone between teachers and student outperforms distilling from independently trained specialist teachers while using roughly 4.5× fewer parameters and half the convergence steps.

## Results

On the UnED benchmark (8 fine-grained domains, 64-D embeddings), UDON with ImageNet21k pretraining achieves a mean Recall@1 of 65.3% and mean P@5 of 53.9%, improving over the previous state-of-the-art (USCSS) by +2.3% R@1 and +2.6% P@5. With CLIP pretraining, UDON reaches 67.7% R@1 and 56.9% P@5, a +1.8% R@1 gain over the best prior method. The largest per-domain improvements appear in Met (+5.2% R@1), GLDv2 (+10.3% R@1), and iNat (+1.8% R@1) under ImageNet21k pretraining, all characterized by large class counts and long-tail distributions. UDON narrows the gap to the non-realistic Specialist+Oracle upper bound to within 1–1.3% on average.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary baselines from [45] (USCRR, USCSS, UJCRR), UDON consistently wins on mean R@1 by 2.3% (IN21k) and 1.8% (CLIP), with the largest per-domain gains in Met (+5.2% R@1) and GLDv2 (+10.3% R@1) under IN21k pretraining. UDON with 64-D embeddings also outperforms all off-the-shelf 768-D models (SigLIP 62.0%, DINOv2 58.2%, CLIP 53.5% mean R@1), demonstrating that the distillation-based fine-tuning compensates for the much lower embedding dimensionality. Compared to the non-realistic Specialist+Oracle upper bound (70.4% R@1 with CLIP), UDON closes the gap to within 1.3% (67.7% R@1). In the distillation-variant comparison (Table 3), UDON's shared-backbone online distillation outperforms both the 8-separate-teachers setup (64.2% vs. 64.8% R@1) and the 1-separate-teacher setup (63.6% R@1) while using ~4.5× fewer parameters and ~2× fewer convergence steps. The MLP-projector baseline [37] with dynamic sampling (62.8% R@1) is also surpassed by UDON by 2.5% R@1, confirming that simply appending projectors does not replicate the benefit of domain-specific teacher distillation.

## Relation to Prior Work

UDON builds on the UnED universal embedding benchmark [45] and the knowledge distillation literature, but departs from prior multi-teacher distillation in two key ways. Unlike [15] and [10], which train separate backbones per teacher (costly to scale), UDON shares a single ViT backbone across all domain-specific teachers and the universal student, training them jointly in an online fashion. It also differs from [23]'s Dynamic Stop-and-Go sampling by computing domain sampling weights from training loss on the fly rather than requiring expensive validation-set feature extraction, and from [50] by ensuring teachers are not cross-updated since they specialize in disjoint visual domains.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

UDON narrows the performance gap between a single universal embedding and an oracle of per-domain specialist models to within 1–1.3%, while using roughly 4.5× fewer parameters than the 8-separate-teacher alternative. The dynamic sampler's ability to automatically allocate more training to long-tail, high-class-count domains (Met, GLDv2, iNat) without pre-training specialist models is a practical win for scaling to more domains. Limitations include a 20% throughput decrease from the added teacher heads and a small performance trade-off on simpler domains; open questions include whether the shared-backbone distillation advantage persists at much larger scale (ViT-Huge, 50+ domains) and whether the approach extends to video or multimodal universal embeddings.
