## Problem

Existing novel-view synthesis methods for urban areas either rely on volumetric rendering with non-interactive speeds or use rasterization-based 3D Gaussian Splatting that is limited to small-scale, homogeneous, static scenes. No prior approach simultaneously handles heterogeneous multi-sequence data with drastic appearance and geometry variations (weather, season, lighting), scales to tens of thousands of images across large dynamic areas, and renders at interactive frame rates.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242557/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes multiple heterogeneous RGB image sequences captured by a surround-view camera rig on a moving vehicle, along with calibrated intrinsics, ego-vehicle SE(3) poses, 3D bounding box tracks for dynamic objects, and LiDAR point clouds for initialization. Notably, timestamps are sequence-local (no global timeline mapping), and the method must reconcile sequences spanning different seasons, weather, and lighting. It outputs a plenoptic function f(P, K, t, s) that renders an RGB image and a depth map at arbitrary camera poses, sequence conditions, and time steps, achieving 20.4 FPS average at up to 1550×2048 resolution.

## Method

4DGF uses a hybrid representation: 3D Gaussians serve as a geometry scaffold but store no per-primitive appearance (reducing memory by >80%), while fixed-size neural fields (hash grids + tiny MLPs) regress per-Gaussian color, opacity attenuation for transient geometry, and non-rigid deformation offsets conditioned on sequence and object latent codes. A scene graph decomposes the scene into a root node, camera nodes, sequence nodes, and dynamic object nodes, with rigid SE(3) transformations on edges and learned latent vectors at nodes encoding local appearance and geometry. Non-rigid objects (pedestrians, cyclists) are modeled via a deformation head that predicts position offsets in canonical object-centric space, while global rigid motion is handled by the scene graph. Training jointly optimizes Gaussian parameters, neural field weights, latent codes, and camera/object pose residuals using an end-to-end differentiable rasterization pipeline with a modified adaptive density control that uses 2D screen size as a splitting criterion and scene-bounds-aware pruning to handle the extreme scale variation in urban scenes.

## Results

On the Argoverse 2 multi-sequence benchmark (1550×2048 resolution), 4DGF achieves 24.97 PSNR, 0.772 SSIM, and 0.447 LPIPS, outperforming the previous best (ML-NSG) by 3.8 dB in PSNR while rendering 293× faster (0.074 s vs. 21.7 s per frame). On KITTI (75% training views), 4DGF reaches 31.34 PSNR / 0.945 SSIM / 0.026 LPIPS, surpassing ML-NSG (28.38 PSNR) by nearly 3 dB. On Waymo Open (Dynamic-32 split), 4DGF achieves 29.04 PSNR with 0.025 s rendering time, compared to EmerNeRF's 27.62 PSNR at 18.9 s per frame.

## Baselines & Comparisons

4DGF outperforms all nine baselines across every dataset and metric. Against the closest competitor ML-NSG [17], 4DGF gains 3.8 dB PSNR on Argoverse 2 (24.97 vs. 21.15) and 2.96 dB on KITTI 75% (31.34 vs. 28.38), while rendering ~293× faster. SUDS [16] and NSG [15] trail by 4+ dB PSNR on Argoverse 2 and are 100–1000× slower. Among 3D-Gaussian-based concurrent work, StreetGaussians [73] achieves 25.79 PSNR on KITTI 75% versus 4DGF's 31.34, and NeuRAD [77] reaches 27.91 PSNR on the KITTI split from [77] versus 4DGF's 30.01. On Waymo Open, 4DGF surpasses EmerNeRF [76] by 1.4 dB PSNR (29.04 vs. 27.62) and is 756× faster (0.025 s vs. 18.9 s), while also maintaining equal or better quality in dynamic-only regions—a property none of the prior methods (D2NeRF [41], HyperNeRF [39], EmerNeRF [76]) achieve. MARS [83] is competitive on KITTI (24.23 PSNR at 75%) but falls well behind 4DGF (31.34) and does not support multi-sequence or heterogeneous data.

## Relation to Prior Work

4DGF sits at the intersection of two recent lines of work: 3D Gaussian Splatting (Kerbl et al., 2023), which provides the efficient tile-based rasterization and adaptive density control for geometry, and Multi-Level Neural Scene Graphs (Fischer et al., CVPR 2024), which provides the graph-based decomposition of dynamic objects with latent appearance codes. The key architectural departure from 3DGS is replacing per-primitive spherical harmonics with shared hash-grid neural fields for appearance, reducing memory by over 80% while enabling modeling of heterogeneous conditions (weather, season, lighting) and transient geometry. The departure from ML-NSG is the substitution of volumetric neural radiance fields with explicit 3D Gaussians for geometry, yielding a 200× rendering speedup while simultaneously improving reconstruction quality.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work makes interactive novel-view synthesis of large-scale, dynamic urban areas practical for the first time, opening the door to closed-loop robotic simulation, mixed-reality applications, and city-scale digital twins that were previously limited to slow volumetric methods. The ability to unify heterogeneous multi-sequence captures (different seasons, weather, lighting) into a single coherent 3D representation addresses a fundamental gap in urban reconstruction. Open questions include handling more complex camera models (equirectangular, LiDAR), modeling physical image formation effects (rolling shutter, motion blur), and whether the scene-graph decomposition can be made fully automatic without relying on 3D bounding box annotations.
