## Problem

Standard 3D CNNs treat all three spatial axes symmetrically, but anisotropic medical images (CT, MRI, OCT) have far higher information density in the axial-slice plane than along the temporal/through-plane axis. This asymmetry causes an 'axial-slice plane performance drop-off' where rich in-plane features are underutilized, and simply increasing channel depth to compensate inflates parameters and FLOPs without proportionally improving segmentation quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242769/cropped_page1_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 3D volumetric medical images as 5D tensors (B, C, D, H, W) and produces a 3D segmentation mask of identical spatial dimensions. A key implementation detail is that at uC stages 1–3, the 5D feature maps are rearranged into 4D tensors by stacking slices along the batch dimension to feed the 2D U-Net, then reshaped back to 5D after processing. Training uses 96×96×96 random crops with a Dice+CE loss, and the uC module is designed as a plug-and-play replacement for skip connections in any 3D segmentation backbone.

## Method

The U-shaped Connection (uC) replaces conventional skip connections with a simplified 2D U-Net that omits the initial and final convolution layers, retaining only downsampling (average pooling + two 2D conv–GN–ReLU blocks) and upsampling (transposed convolution + two 2D conv blocks) stages. In uC 3DU-Net, the 3D U-Net backbone applies uC at stages 1–3 (with 4, 3, and 2 down/up pairs respectively), while a Dual Feature Integration (DFi) module fuses the 2D slice-plane features from uC with the 3D spatial features from the decoder via a lightweight combination of concatenation, 1×1 convolutions, addition, and sigmoid-gated attention. The core insight is that for kernel size K, a 2D convolution requires only 1/K of the parameters of its 3D counterpart, so routing in-plane feature extraction through 2D convolutions dramatically improves the parameter-to-performance ratio while preserving volumetric context from the 3D encoder-decoder path.

## Results

On FLARE2021, uC 3DU-Net achieves a mean Dice of 83.2% with only 21.7M parameters and 286.4G FLOPs, surpassing the previous best 3D UX-Net (80.1% Dice, 53.0M params, 639.4G FLOPs) by 3.1% on the pancreas class. On OIMHS, it reaches 94.13% Dice and 89.48% mIoU, outperforming SASAN (93.53% Dice) and 3D UX-Net (92.90% Dice). On AbdomenCT-1K, uC 3DU-Net attains 93.25% Dice and 88.29% mIoU, exceeding all baselines including 3D U-Net (92.28% Dice) and SwinUNETR (92.36% Dice). On BTCV (13 classes), it achieves 82.74% Dice, marginally surpassing 3D UX-Net (82.31%) while using only 41% of its parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

uC 3DU-Net (21.7M params) outperforms all 12 baselines on FLARE2021 mean Dice (83.2%), with the largest margin over 3D UX-Net (+3.1% on pancreas) and nnU-Net (+4.0% mean). On OIMHS, it beats SASAN by 0.6% Dice (94.13 vs 93.53) and 3D UX-Net by 1.23% Dice (94.13 vs 92.90), while using only 41% of 3D UX-Net's parameters. On AbdomenCT-1K, it surpasses SwinUNETR by 0.89% Dice (93.25 vs 92.36) and 3D U-Net by 0.97% Dice (93.25 vs 92.28). On FeTA2021 (isotropic MRI), it ties 3D UX-Net at 84.5% mean Dice but with 59% fewer parameters. On BTCV, it edges out 3D UX-Net by 0.43% Dice (82.74 vs 82.31) with 59% fewer parameters. Against the two most recent baselines (UNETR++ and D-LKA Net from Table 14), uC 3DU-Net leads on all four datasets, with the largest gap on FeTA2021 (+3.6% Dice over D-LKA Net) and OIMHS (+2.5% Dice over D-LKA Net).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the 3D U-Net encoder-decoder architecture (Çiçek et al., 2016) and repurposes the 2D U-Net structure (Ronneberger et al., 2015) as a simplified skip-connection module (uC) within 3D segmentation networks. It departs from the standard skip-connection paradigm (concatenation or addition) and from prior skip-connection refinements (Attention U-Net, Dense U-Net, H-DenseUNet) by introducing a full 2D U-Net sub-network in place of a single connection path, motivated by the anisotropic information density inherent to CT, MRI, and OCT acquisition. The DFi fusion module is a lightweight alternative to the two 3×3 convolutions typically used after concatenation in U-Net decoders.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play uC module offers a practical, low-cost strategy for improving 3D medical segmentation on anisotropic data without retraining from scratch, and the finding that a 24-channel uC-augmented model can match or exceed a 48-channel model at ~30% of the parameters has direct implications for deployment on resource-constrained hardware. However, the performance gains over strong SOTA baselines are modest (0.4–1.2% Dice), the FLOPs actually increase relative to the base 3D U-Net (286G vs 135G), and the benefit on the isotropic FeTA dataset is noticeably smaller, suggesting the approach is most valuable for strongly anisotropic modalities. Open questions include whether uC generalizes to non-U-Net topologies (e.g., pure transformer decoders), whether the 2D slicing along the depth axis is always optimal, and whether the gains persist at larger model scales or with different normalization schemes.
