## Problem

Existing BEV map prediction methods are trained and evaluated on small-scale, manually-labeled autonomous vehicle datasets (NuScenes, KITTI-360-BEV, Argoverse) that cover limited geographies, use fixed sensor configurations, and lack diversity in camera models, terrain types, and capture scenarios. This constrains generalizability, making it difficult to achieve robust out-of-the-box map prediction in unseen locations. The paper asks how to construct a scalable, diverse dataset that enables generalizable BEV mapping across arbitrary environments.

## Input / Output

The model takes a monocular FPV image (resized and padded to 512×512), camera intrinsics (focal length), and camera pose/extrinsics (from IMU or Mapillary metadata) as input, and produces a gravity-aligned BEV semantic map at 0.5 m/pixel resolution over a 50 m × 50 m area with six static classes (Road, Parking, Sidewalk, Crossing, Building, Terrain). The BEV is rotated so the robot's forward direction points 'up' in the map plane, and the model is explicitly camera-intrinsics-agnostic, handling 17 different camera models and varying focal lengths without per-camera retraining.

## Method

The core contribution is the MIA data engine, which automatically curates ~1.2M FPV-BEV pairs from Mapillary (FPV images with SfM-rectified poses) and OpenStreetMap (vectorized road/sidewalk/building geometry) through two parallel pipelines: an FPV pipeline that filters by recency, camera model, pose discrepancy, and spatial sparsity before undistorting images, and a BEV pipeline that projects coordinates to UTM, retrieves OSM data, renders satellite-aligned semantic maps via an enhanced MapMachine pipeline (inferring missing sidewalks), and rasterizes into six-class masks. The Mapper model builds on OrienterNet's front-end for gravity alignment and 2D-to-3D lifting, replacing its ResNet encoder with DINOv2 ViT-B/14 with registers for improved generalizability. A linear layer estimates pixel-wise scores over 32 depth-normalized scale bins, which are mapped to metric depth using focal length, then projected via polar and Cartesian transforms to form a BEV feature map. A lightweight decoder head on the BEV features predicts the semantic map, trained with weighted Dice and binary cross-entropy loss over frustum pixels for 15 epochs on 4×H100 GPUs.

## Results

On the zero-shot MIA-OOD benchmark (held-out cities Houston and Pittsburgh), Mapper achieves 41.10 macro-mean IoU on Road & Sidewalk, outperforming TIIM (17.11) and SkyEye (17.14) by ~144%. On the standard NuScenes benchmark, Mapper's zero-shot Road & Sidewalk IoU of 45.97 approaches the fully-supervised TIIM in-domain score of 47.83, while exceeding zero-shot SkyEye (34.02) by 35%. On the extreme MIA-Rural and MIA-non-US splits, Mapper achieves 37.26 Avg R,S IoU versus 21.21 (TIIM) and 20.87 (SkyEye), confirming consistent generalization gains. Fine-tuning Mapper on just 10% of NuScenes data yields 27.12 mean IoU, surpassing TIIM trained from scratch on the same 10% (25.22).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Mapper (trained on MIA) is compared against two monocular BEV map prediction baselines: TIIM [32] (trained on NuScenes) and SkyEye [15] (trained on KITTI-360-BEV). On the zero-shot MIA-OOD benchmark, Mapper dominates both baselines by a wide margin, achieving 41.10 vs. 17.11 (TIIM) and 17.14 (SkyEye) on Avg. R,S IoU — a ~144% relative improvement. On NuScenes zero-shot, Mapper (45.97) slightly trails TIIM's in-domain score (47.83) but substantially exceeds zero-shot SkyEye (34.02) by 35%. On KITTI-360-BEV zero-shot, TIIM edges out Mapper on Avg. R,S (38.80 vs. 35.46), though the paper notes this is partly due to incomplete ground-truth labels in KITTI-360-BEV that inflate TIIM's road IoU. On the extreme MIA-Rural and MIA-non-US splits, Mapper consistently leads both baselines by ~16 points on Avg. R,S IoU (37.26 vs. 21.21/20.87). In the fine-tuning experiment, Mapper pre-trained on MIA then fine-tuned on 10% NuScenes data (27.12 mean IoU) outperforms TIIM trained from scratch on the same 10% subset (25.22), demonstrating MIA's value as a pre-training resource.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on OrienterNet's 2D-to-3D lifting architecture and gravity-alignment mechanism, repurposing a visual localization front-end for semantic BEV map prediction by adding a decoder head and swapping ResNet for DINOv2. It departs from the dominant BEV map prediction paradigm (TIIM, SkyEye) that relies on expensive, manually curated autonomous-vehicle datasets (NuScenes, KITTI-360-BEV, Waymo) by instead leveraging two world-scale crowd-sourced platforms—Mapillary for FPV images and OpenStreetMap for semantic maps—through a fully automated curation pipeline. The BEV rendering pipeline extends MapMachine with satellite-aligned rasterization and sidewalk inference, addressing limitations in both OrienterNet's map rendering and prior AV-based BEV generation from LiDAR splatting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MIA provides the first world-scale, automatically curated BEV map prediction dataset (1.2M pairs, 470 km², 17 camera models), enabling research on generalizability that was previously infeasible with small AV datasets. The finding that a simple camera-intrinsics-agnostic model pretrained on this data achieves zero-shot performance comparable to fully supervised baselines on road/sidewalk classes suggests data diversity may matter more than architectural complexity for map prediction. However, significant gaps remain: all models (including Mapper) fail on rural/highway scenarios, label noise from crowd-sourced data is higher than manual annotation, and dynamic classes are entirely absent. The open-source data engine positions MIA as a living, ever-growing benchmark that the community can extend to new geographies and scenarios.
