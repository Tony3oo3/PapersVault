## Problem

Rehearsal-based continual learning methods are the most effective at mitigating catastrophic forgetting, but their training efficiency is poorly addressed, especially for deployment on resource-constrained edge devices. Prior frequency-domain approaches either use DCT (losing spatial information needed for data augmentation) or introduce large numbers of cross-task learnable parameters that exacerbate forgetting. The paper asks whether frequency-domain representations can simultaneously reduce input size and improve classification accuracy in CL without adding task-specific parameters.

## Input / Output

The method accepts standard RGB images (32×32 for Split CIFAR-10 and Split Tiny-ImageNet; 224×224 for ImageNet-R) and outputs a class label. Unusually, the replay buffer stores DWT-encoded frequency-domain feature maps (H/2 × W/2 × 3 per merged component) rather than original images, and the classifier operates on a selectively masked subset (60%) of the feature extractor's output channels chosen per class based on inter-class frequency similarity.

## Method

CLFD comprises two plug-in components that modify only the input and output of a standard CNN feature extractor (ResNet-18). The Frequency Domain Feature Encoder (FFE) applies a single-level Haar DWT to the RGB input, then uses three 1×1 convolutions to merge the low-frequency (X_ll), high-frequency (X_lh, X_hl, X_hh), and all four sub-bands into three feature maps, halving spatial dimensions; FFE is frozen after the first task to avoid cross-task parameter drift. The Class-aware Frequency Domain Feature Selection (CFFS) computes cosine similarity on low-frequency components to identify the most and least similar classes, then applies a two-phase dropout schedule—Frequency Dropout (first 40% of epochs) that biases feature retention toward similar classes and away from dissimilar ones, followed by Semantic Dropout that reinforces the established selection—before masking to the top 60% of feature responses for classification. No cross-task learnable parameters are introduced, and the framework is orthogonal to the underlying rehearsal strategy (ER, DER++, ER-ACE, CLS-ER), enabling seamless integration.

## Results

On Split CIFAR-10 (Class-IL, buffer 125), CLFD-CLS-ER achieves 59.98% average accuracy, outperforming the SOTA baseline CLS-ER (53.57%) by 6.41% while reducing peak memory from 1016 MB to 401 MB (2.5×). On Split Tiny-ImageNet (Class-IL, buffer 125), CLFD-CLS-ER reaches 18.73% vs. CLS-ER's 16.35%. On the NVIDIA Jetson Orin NX edge device, CLFD achieves up to 2.6× training speedup and 3.0× peak memory reduction compared to the corresponding baseline methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CLFD consistently outperforms all four rehearsal-based baselines it integrates with. On S-CIFAR-10 (Class-IL, buffer 125), CLFD-ER-ACE (58.68%) surpasses ER-ACE (56.12%) by 2.56%, CLFD-DER++ (58.81%) beats DER++ (53.09%) by 5.72%, and CLFD-CLS-ER (59.98%) exceeds CLS-ER (53.57%) by 6.41%. On S-Tiny-ImageNet (buffer 125), CLFD-ER-ACE (15.88%) outperforms ER-ACE (11.09%) by 4.79%, and CLFD-CLS-ER (18.73%) beats CLS-ER (16.35%) by 2.38%. Against the sparse-training baseline SparCL-ER, CLFD-ER achieves higher Class-IL accuracy (45.56% vs. 43.74% at buffer 50) with fewer training FLOPs (2.8×10¹⁵ vs. 2.0×10¹⁵), and the combined CLFD+SparCL-ER reaches 55.15% with only 0.6×10¹⁵ FLOPs. All three regularization-based baselines (oEWC, SI, LwF) are far below the rehearsal-based methods, scoring below 20% on S-CIFAR-10 Class-IL, confirming that CLFD's gains are specific to the rehearsal paradigm. Across all comparisons, CLFD also reduces peak memory by 2.4–2.7× relative to the corresponding baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the frequency-domain learning literature (DCT-based acceleration [48,15] and DWT-based classification [29,47]) and the feature-selection mechanisms from Heterogeneous Dropout [1] and Semantic Dropout [39], but departs from both in key ways. Unlike prior DCT methods that destroy spatial information (preventing data augmentation) or DWT methods that introduce many cross-task learnable parameters (increasing forgetting risk), CLFD uses a frozen Haar-wavelet encoder plus a class-similarity-guided feature selection that adds no cross-task parameters. It also positions itself as orthogonal to sparse-training approaches like SparCL [46], which reduce FLOPs via weight masking rather than input-size reduction, and the two are shown to compose additively.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in making rehearsal-based continual learning viable on edge hardware: a 2.6× training speedup and 3× peak-memory reduction on a Jetson Orin NX, validated on real hardware, is a concrete step toward deploying CL in resource-constrained settings. The modular design (only input/output features of the feature extractor are modified) means CLFD can be dropped into any rehearsal-based method without architectural changes. Open questions include whether the gains persist at larger image resolutions (experiments use 32×32), whether the frozen-FFE assumption holds for very long task sequences, and how the framework performs under infinite-buffer or non-rehearsal settings, which the authors acknowledge as future work.
