## Problem

3D Gaussian Splatting and its geometry-focused variants (SuGaR, 2DGS, GOF) suffer from needle-like artifacts and inaccurate normals because Gaussians converge into highly anisotropic shapes with one dominant variance (effective rank ≈ 1). Existing flatness regularizations cannot distinguish between desirable disk-like Gaussians (erank ≈ 2) and degenerate needle-like ones (erank ≈ 1), since both exhibit near-zero variance along one axis. The paper identifies this structural failure mode and proposes a principled way to constrain Gaussian shapes during optimization.

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view RGB images (downsampled to 800×600 on DTU, 1600×1050 on Mip-NeRF360) with COLMAP-estimated camera poses, and produces a 3D Gaussian scene representation that supports real-time novel view rendering, TSDF-based mesh extraction, and normal map rendering. Crucially, it operates as a drop-in add-on module: the input pipeline, rendering pipeline, and output formats remain identical to the underlying 3DGS variant, with the only change being an additional differentiable loss term applied from iteration 7000 onward during training.

## Method

The paper introduces a differentiable effective rank regularization on the covariance matrices of 3D Gaussians, defined as L_erank = Σ_k λ_erank · max(−log(erank(G_k) − 1 + ε), 0) + s_3, which increases exponentially as a Gaussian's effective rank approaches 1, strongly penalizing needle-like shapes while permitting erank < 2 for representing thin structures. The effective rank is computed from the normalized squared scale parameters (s₁², s₂², s₃²) via Shannon entropy, making it a smooth, real-valued measure that jointly considers all three axes. A revised Adaptive Density Control (ADC) algorithm is also adopted, replacing the norm-of-summation splitting criterion with a summation-of-norms formulation, which is essential because disk-like Gaussians produce smaller per-pixel gradient signals that would otherwise prevent them from being split. The regularization is scheduled to activate at iteration 7000 following a coarse-to-fine paradigm, and the combination of both components yields approximately 10% fewer Gaussians than baseline 3DGS while improving both Chamfer distance and PSNR.

## Results

On the DTU dataset, adding the effective rank regularization ("+e") to 3DGS reduces mean Chamfer distance from 1.96 to 1.04 (a 47% improvement) while slightly increasing PSNR from 32.82 to 33.09. The same add-on improves SuGaR (CD 1.33→1.00), 2DGS (CD 0.80→0.77), and GOF (CD 0.74→0.66), with GOF+e achieving the best overall Chamfer distance of 0.66 and PSNR of 33.01. On Mip-NeRF360, 3DGS+e improves outdoor PSNR from 24.64 to 24.93 and indoor SSIM from 0.920 to 0.953, while also reducing storage from 734 MB to 646 MB.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper evaluates the effective rank regularization as a plug-in add-on against four 3DGS-based baselines: 3DGS, SuGaR, 2DGS, and GOF. The largest absolute gain is on 3DGS, where CD drops from 1.96 to 1.04 and PSNR rises from 32.82 to 33.09, confirming that the baseline suffers most from needle-like artifacts. SuGaR benefits substantially in geometry (CD 1.33→1.00) because its SDF-based regularization still permits non-planar Gaussians with erank > 2, which the effective rank term corrects. For 2DGS and GOF, which already incorporate well-designed geometry losses (depth distortion, ray-Gaussian intersection), the gains are smaller but consistent (CD 0.80→0.77 and 0.74→0.66 respectively), showing the regularization is complementary rather than redundant. On Mip-NeRF360, 3DGS+e outperforms 3DGS on all three metrics (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS) for both outdoor and indoor scenes, and also surpasses NeRF-based methods like Mip-NeRF360 (PSNR 24.47 outdoor) while being real-time. The method also requires ~10% fewer Gaussians than the 3DGS baseline, yielding a smaller storage footprint (98 MB vs. 113 MB on DTU) without any quality trade-off.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) and its geometry-focused variants (SuGaR, 2DGS, GOF), which all attempt to regularize Gaussians toward flatness for surface alignment. The key conceptual departure is the use of effective rank (Roy & Vetterli, 2007) as a differentiable shape descriptor that distinguishes disk-like Gaussians (erank≈2) from needle-like ones (erank≈1), a distinction that prior flatness-based regularizations conflate. The method also adopts the revised densification criterion from Bulò et al. and GOF, which is necessary because disk-like Gaussians produce weaker per-pixel gradient signals that the original ADC splitting rule fails to capture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The empirical finding that the majority of 'flat' Gaussians in 3DGS, SuGaR, and even 2DGS (which initializes at erank=2) actually collapse to needle-like shapes (erank≈1) provides a useful diagnostic for the 3DGS community and explains why flatness alone is insufficient for geometry. The effective rank regularization is a lightweight, differentiable add-on that improves Chamfer distance and reduces needle artifacts without degrading PSNR, making it broadly applicable to existing 3DGS pipelines. Open questions include whether combining erank regularization with structure-aware losses (depth distortion, normal consistency) yields further gains, and whether the fixed hyperparameter λ_erank=0.01 generalizes to scenes dominated by genuinely thin or elongated structures.
