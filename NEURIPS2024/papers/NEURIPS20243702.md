## Problem

Standard linear contextual bandit algorithms (e.g., LinUCB) implicitly incentivize strategic arms to misreport their contexts to maximize selection frequency, leading to linear regret when arms respond in Nash equilibrium. No existing algorithm simultaneously minimizes regret and aligns the arms' incentives to report truthfully, creating a fundamental gap between online learning and mechanism design in sequential decision-making with self-interested agents.

## Input / Output

Each arm i privately observes its true context x*_{t,i} ∈ R^d and reports a potentially gamed context x_{t,i} ∈ R^d; the learner only ever sees the reported contexts and receives rewards drawn from ⟨θ*, x*_{t,i_t}⟩ + η_t (based on true contexts, not reported ones). The mechanism outputs an arm selection per round and an elimination decision (grim trigger), with the key constraint that the learner never observes true contexts and must bound strategic regret uniformly over all Nash equilibria the algorithm induces. Contexts are bounded (‖x*_{t,i}‖₂ ≤ 1) and rewards are bounded in [0,1].

## Method

The paper proposes two mechanisms built on a grim trigger: GGTM (θ* known) eliminates an arm when its cumulative reported expected reward exceeds an optimistic confidence bound on its observed reward, and OptGTM (θ* unknown) generalizes this using per-arm least-squares estimators and confidence sets, eliminating an arm when the sum of pessimistic (LCB) expected rewards exceeds the optimistic (UCB) observed reward. The critical insight is that accurately estimating θ* is neither necessary nor feasible under strategic manipulation; instead, independent per-arm estimators prevent cross-arm collusion and the grim trigger uses the full sequence of past estimators (not the latest) to remain effective. Arm selection is optimistic (max UCB over active arms), and the mechanism is mutually destructive—elimination hurts both the arm and the learner—so the confidence width must be calibrated to make truthfulness an approximate NE. Theoretical guarantees show truthfulness is a Õ(√T)-NE under GGTM and a Õ(d√(KT))-NE under OptGTM, with strategic regret Õ(K²√(KT)) and Õ(dK²√(KT)) respectively under all NE.

## Results

The main theoretical results establish that OptGTM achieves Õ(d√(KT)) strategic regret when arms report truthfully (Theorem 5.1) and Õ(dK²√(KT)) regret under every Nash equilibrium (Theorem 5.2), while the simpler GGTM (with known θ*) achieves Õ(K²√(KT)) regret under every NE (Theorem 4.2). In contrast, incentive-unaware algorithms (greedy, LinUCB, RobustBandit) suffer linear regret Ω(T) on non-trivial instances (Proposition 3.3). In experiments with K=5 arms, d=5, T=10k rounds over 20 epochs of strategic adaptation, OptGTM maintains stable regret across all epochs while LinUCB degrades to near-uniform-sampling performance by the final epoch.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary experimental baseline is LinUCB [1,6], the standard linear contextual bandit algorithm. In epoch 0 (truthful, non-strategic arms), LinUCB outperforms OptGTM, as expected since OptGTM incurs additional regret from maintaining independent per-arm estimators (a √K overhead over the standard Õ(d√T) bound). However, as arms strategically adapt their reported contexts over 20 epochs using gradient ascent, LinUCB's regret grows dramatically and it nearly matches uniform random sampling by the final epoch, while OptGTM's regret remains stable. Theoretically, the incentive-unaware greedy algorithm and LinUCB both suffer Ω(T) linear regret under any Nash equilibrium (Proposition 3.3), whereas OptGTM guarantees Õ(dK²√(KT)) regret. The paper also notes that RobustBandit [8] for adversarial context corruptions similarly fails in the strategic setting. The key trade-off is that OptGTM's per-arm independence (necessary to prevent collusive manipulation) introduces the √K factor absent in standard non-strategic bandits.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of linear contextual bandits (LinUCB [1,6]) and mechanism design, adapting the grim trigger concept from repeated social dilemmas [28] to incentivize truthful context reporting by strategic arms. It departs from prior strategic bandit work [4,12] where arms privately receive rewards, and from adversarial corruption literature [3,20,39,45] which assumes a bounded corruption budget on rewards or contexts. The key conceptual departure is treating the problem as mechanism design under uncertainty rather than robustness to adversarial corruption, and showing that standard confidence-bound algorithms (LinUCB, RobustBandit) suffer linear regret when arms respond in Nash equilibrium rather than being bounded by a corruption budget.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes that incentive-unaware algorithms are fundamentally broken in strategic settings (linear regret), making mechanism design not optional but necessary for any learning algorithm deployed in environments with self-interested agents. The surprising finding that accurate estimation of θ* is unnecessary for regret minimization opens a new design space for incentive-aware learning. However, the K² dependence in the universal regret bound is conjectured suboptimal (optimal likely √K), and the assumption that arms never under-report their value limits generality. Broader implications for recommender systems and any platform where content creators game algorithms are significant, but the practical deployment challenges (e.g., variance-aware mechanisms, dropping the no-under-reporting assumption) remain open.
