## Problem

Biologically plausible recurrent cortical circuit models that implement divisive normalization (DN) dynamically, such as ORGaNICs, lack proven stability guarantees for arbitrary parameter choices and inputs, making them difficult to train via standard backpropagation-through-time without ad hoc regularization. This paper asks whether ORGaNICs are unconditionally stable and, if so, whether this intrinsic stability enables straightforward BPTT training comparable to LSTMs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240470/cropped_page6_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For the static task, a 28×28 grayscale MNIST image is presented as a constant input drive to the circuit, which evolves its n excitatory and n inhibitory membrane potentials to a fixed point; the steady-state firing rates of principal neurons feed a linear readout for 10-class digit prediction. For the sequential task, MNIST pixels are fed one per timestep in scanline order (sMNIST) or a random permutation (psMNIST), and the model predicts the digit after the full 784-pixel sequence. The circuit's hidden state consists of paired y (principal) and a (normalizing) membrane potentials per neuron, with firing rates computed as rectified power-law functions of these potentials.

## Method

The paper proves unconditional local asymptotic stability of arbitrary-dimensional ORGaNICs with identity recurrent weights (W_r = I) by applying Lyapunov's indirect method: the Jacobian at the analytically known normalization fixed point yields a quadratic eigenvalue problem equivalent to a system of coupled damped harmonic oscillators, and the damping matrix is shown to be a nonsingular M-matrix (hence Lyapunov diagonally stable) for all positive parameters and inputs. This connection also yields an explicit energy (Lyapunov) function revealing that the circuit minimizes a weighted combination of input-drive matching and divisive normalization. For arbitrary W_r, the authors prove stability of the 2D circuit and conjecture high-dimensional stability supported by 100% empirical success under bounded singular-value constraints. Training uses naive BPTT with no gradient clipping or scaling, exploiting the intrinsic stability to avoid exploding/vanishing gradients, and achieves 98.2% on static MNIST and 97.8% on sMNIST with 128 units, matching or exceeding LSTM baselines.

## Results

On static MNIST classification, ORGaNICs achieves 98.1–98.2% test accuracy, matching a 50-unit MLP (98.2%) and outperforming SSN by ~3 percentage points (94.9–95.2%). On sequential pixel-by-pixel MNIST (sMNIST), ORGaNICs with learned time constants reaches 97.7–97.8% accuracy with 64–128 hidden units, slightly exceeding LSTM (97.3%) while using fewer parameters (26k–100k vs. 68k). On the harder permuted sMNIST (psMNIST), ORGaNICs achieves 89.9–90.7%, trailing the best baselines (coRNN 96.6%, Lipschitz RNN 96.3%) by roughly 5–7 points. Notably, all ORGaNICs results are obtained via naive BPTT without gradient clipping or scaling, leveraging the circuit's intrinsic stability.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On static MNIST, ORGaNICs (98.1–98.2%) outperforms SSN (94.9–95.2%) by ~3 points and ties the MLP baseline (98.2%), demonstrating that a biologically plausible neurodynamical circuit can match a standard feedforward network. On sMNIST, ORGaNICs with learned time constants (97.7–97.8%) slightly edges out LSTM (97.3%) with fewer parameters, but falls short of coRNN (99.3%) and Lipschitz RNN (99.4%) by ~1.5–2 points. On psMNIST, ORGaNICs (89.9–90.7%) is the weakest among all compared methods, trailing AntisymmetricRNN (95.8%) by ~5 points and coRNN (96.6%) by ~6 points, suggesting the dynamic normalization mechanism is less effective when pixel order is randomized. The paper's key differentiator is not peak accuracy but trainability: ORGaNICs achieves these results with vanilla BPTT and no gradient clipping/scaling, whereas LSTM and other RNN baselines typically require such ad hoc stabilization. Fixed time constants in ORGaNICs (94.8% sMNIST, 84.8% psMNIST) show a significant performance drop, confirming that adaptive time constants are critical for handling long-term dependencies.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the ORGaNICs circuit model (Heeger & Mackey 2019; Heeger & Zemlianova 2020), which dynamically implements divisive normalization via recurrent gain modulation, and on the broader DN literature (Carandini & Heeger 1994; Heeger 1992) that established normalization as a canonical cortical computation. It departs from the RNN stability literature (Chang et al. 2019 antisymmetric RNN, Rusch et al. 2020 coRNN, Erichson et al. 2020 Lipschitz RNN) by showing that stability need not be imposed through structural constraints on the recurrent weight matrix (antisymmetry, unitary evolution, Lipschitz bounds) but instead emerges intrinsically from the divisive normalization nonlinearity itself. The paper also positions ORGaNICs as a biophysically plausible alternative to SSN (Rubin et al. 2015) and Wilson-Cowan models, which approximate DN only in restricted parameter regimes and lack unconditional stability guarantees.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unconditional stability proof for identity recurrent weights, combined with the connection to coupled damped harmonic oscillators and the derived energy function, provides a normative principle for what the circuit computes and enables training via naive BPTT without gradient clipping or scaling—addressing a long-standing barrier for biologically plausible neurodynamical models. However, the high-dimensional case with arbitrary recurrent weights remains a conjecture supported only by empirical evidence, and the parameter count grows quadratically with hidden state size due to the input gain modulator weight matrices. Open questions include whether stacked ORGaNICs layers with feedback connections maintain stability, whether the stability regime extends beyond the tested singular-value constraints on W_r, and whether the model scales to larger, more complex benchmarks beyond MNIST.
