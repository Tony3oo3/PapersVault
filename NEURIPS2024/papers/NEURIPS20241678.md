## Problem

Classifier-free guidance (CFG) in diffusion models simultaneously improves prompt alignment and image quality while reducing output variation, but these effects are entangled and cannot be controlled independently. The paper identifies that CFG's quality improvement actually stems from the accuracy gap between the conditional and unconditional denoisers rather than from the conditioning signal itself, and asks whether this quality effect can be isolated without the associated loss of diversity.

## Input / Output

At generation time, the method takes a noise sample and a conditioning signal (class label for ImageNet or text prompt for DeepFloyd IF) and produces a synthetic image at 64×64 or 512×512 resolution. Unusually, it requires two denoiser networks at inference: the main model and a degraded guiding model, both receiving the same conditioning input. The guiding model is a smaller-capacity and/or less-trained version of the main model, so the only additional input is the choice of which degraded checkpoint to use alongside the primary one.

## Method

Autoguidance replaces the unconditional denoiser D₀ in the standard CFG extrapolation (Equation 3) with a degraded version of the conditional model D₁ itself, trained on the same data and conditioning but with reduced capacity and/or fewer training iterations. The key insight is that under limited capacity, score matching over-emphasizes low-probability regions, so the weaker model's density is more spread out; the density ratio p₁/p₀ then produces an inward-pointing gradient that adaptively truncates samples toward the data manifold without the task-discrepancy problem of CFG. The two models must suffer from compatible degradations (same type of errors at different magnitudes), which is validated through controlled experiments with synthetic corruptions. In practice, the guiding model is simply a smaller EDM2 variant trained for 1/16th the iterations, and the guidance weight w is tuned via grid search alongside independent post-hoc EMA lengths for each model.

## Results

On ImageNet-512, autoguidance with EDM2-S improves FID from 2.56 (no guidance) to 1.34, and with EDM2-XXL to 1.25, setting new records. On ImageNet-64, autoguidance with EDM2-S achieves a record FID of 1.01 and FD_DINOv2 of 31.85. The method also works for unconditional generation, reducing FID from 11.67 to 3.86 for EDM2-S on ImageNet-512. FD_DINOv2 records are similarly improved, with EDM2-XXL lowering the 512×512 record from 29.16 to 24.18.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Autoguidance outperforms all compared baselines on every configuration. Against the unguided EDM2 baseline [24], it improves FID by 1.22 (512×512, EDM2-S: 2.56→1.34) and 0.57 (64×64: 1.58→1.01). Against classifier-free guidance [16], the gains are 0.89 FID (512×512, EDM2-S: 2.23→1.34) and 0.47 FID (64×64: 1.48→1.01). Against the concurrent guidance-interval method [27], autoguidance achieves 0.34 FID improvement on 512×512 (1.68→1.34) and 0.15 on 64×64 (where guidance interval is not directly listed but RIN [21] at 1.23 is the prior best, beaten by 0.22). The advantage is most dramatic in the unconditional setting, where autoguidance reduces FID from 11.67 to 3.86—a 3× improvement with no comparable baseline available.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reinterprets classifier-free guidance (CFG) [16] by identifying that its image-quality benefit actually stems from the quality gap between the conditional and unconditional denoisers, rather than from the class-likelihood boosting mechanism it was designed for. It builds on the EDM2 framework [24] for architecture, training, and post-hoc EMA, but departs from CFG's core design by replacing the unconditional guiding model with a degraded version of the same conditional model (reduced capacity and/or training), a procedure the authors call autoguidance. Conceptually, the method is also related to contrastive decoding in LLMs [29] and to recent noise-level-dependent guidance schedules [27, 54], but it addresses a different axis: disentangling quality improvement from prompt alignment rather than modulating guidance strength across noise levels.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Autoguidance sets new FID records on ImageNet (1.01 at 64×64, 1.25 at 512×512) while preserving sample diversity better than CFG, and uniquely extends guidance to unconditional models where CFG is inapplicable. The key open question is whether the method generalizes beyond the EDM2 family: the authors note that multi-stage training pipelines with changing data distributions may violate the assumption that both models suffer from compatible degradations, and no formal conditions guaranteeing beneficial guidance are yet established. The modest additional training cost (+3.6% to +11%) makes the technique practically attractive, but the need to train and maintain a separate guiding model adds deployment complexity that may limit adoption in large-scale text-to-image systems where early snapshots are unavailable.
