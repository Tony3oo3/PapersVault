## Problem

State-of-the-art Transformer decoders for semantic segmentation (e.g., Segmenter, MaskFormer) rely on empirical compositions of cross-attention, self-attention, and dot-product operations that lack theoretical justification. This absence of principled interpretation hinders targeted improvements such as identifying performance bottlenecks or understanding why certain architectural choices succeed. The paper asks: what underlying principle governs the design of these decoders, and can it yield a white-box alternative with interpretable components?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241576/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image, splits it into N non-overlapping patches, and encodes them via a ViT backbone into image embeddings Z ∈ R^{D×N} (zero-mean). The output is a per-patch segmentation mask M = Q^T Z ∈ R^{C×N}, where C is the number of predefined classes, effectively compressing D-dimensional embeddings into C-dimensional class scores. Experiments use 512×512 or 640×640 input resolutions on ADE20K, Cityscapes, and Pascal Context, with single- or multi-scale inference.

## Method

DEPICT derives its decoder by unrolling gradient steps of coding-rate objectives extended from PCA, yielding two fully attentional operators. First, a Multi-head Subspace Self-Attention (MSSA) operator refines image embeddings to construct an ideal principal subspace aligned with supervision, by maximizing the projected coding rate onto learned orthonormal bases P (grouped into H heads to reduce memory). Second, a Multi-head Subspace Cross-Attention (MSCA) operator extracts class embeddings Q as a low-rank approximation of the refined embeddings, constrained to lie within the principal subspace via projection onto P. Unlike the black-box Segmenter, DEPICT uses a single shared parameter matrix P (rather than four separate Q/K/V matrices), omits the FFN block entirely, and sets the number of class embeddings K equal to the number of classes C. The two variants—DEPICT-SA (self-attention only) and DEPICT-CA (self-attention followed by cross-attention)—consistently outperform Segmenter with 1/14 the parameters and 1/3 the FLOPs on ADE20K.

## Results

On ADE20K validation, DEPICT-SA with ViT-L at 640×640 achieves 52.9/54.3 mIoU (single/multi-scale), surpassing Segmenter (51.8/53.6) by 1.1/0.7 mIoU while using only 2M parameters vs. 28M and 17.8G FLOPs vs. 60.4G. Across all backbone sizes, DEPICT-SA consistently outperforms Segmenter: +1.2/+1.9 mIoU (ViT-T), +1.4/+0.8 mIoU (ViT-S), and +0.7/+0.7 mIoU (ViT-B). On the smaller Cityscapes and Pascal Context datasets, DEPICT-SA is marginally below Segmenter (78.8 vs. 79.1 mIoU on Cityscapes; 57.9 vs. 58.1 on Pascal Context), which the authors attribute to insufficient training data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Segmenter (the direct black-box counterpart), DEPICT-SA wins on every backbone size on ADE20K: +1.2/+1.9 mIoU (ViT-T), +1.4/+0.8 mIoU (ViT-S), +0.7/+0.7 mIoU (ViT-B), and +1.1/+0.7 mIoU (ViT-L at 640×640), while using 1/14 to 1/20 the parameters and roughly 1/3 to 1/4 the FLOPs. DEPICT-CA also beats Segmenter at ViT-T (+0.3/+0.6) and ViT-S (+0.5/+0.1) but is slightly behind at ViT-L (52.8 vs. 51.8 ss, 53.3 vs. 53.6 ms). Against MaskFormer (Swin-L, 640×640), DEPICT-SA trails by 1.2/1.3 mIoU, which the authors attribute to MaskFormer's finer segmentation granularity, pixel decoder, and hierarchical backbone rather than a fundamental decoder limitation. On the smaller Cityscapes (~3K images) and Pascal Context (~5K images) datasets, DEPICT-SA is marginally below Segmenter (−0.3/−0.3 and −0.2/−0.4 mIoU respectively), and the authors show via a data-scaling experiment (Table 3) that DEPICT's advantage emerges once sufficient training data (~16K+) is available.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the white-box transformer framework established by CRATE [42] and the coding-rate representation learning principles of MCR² [43] from the encoder/classifier side to the decoder side of Transformer-based semantic segmentation. While CRATE derived self-attention operators for ViT backbones and MCR² provided the theoretical foundation for discriminative representations, this work is the first to show that the empirical design of Transformer decoders (self-attention, cross-attention, dot-product) can be derived purely from PCA objectives expressed in terms of coding rate. It departs from the black-box Segmenter [35] and MaskFormer [8] by replacing their empirically motivated attention blocks with principled MSSA and MSCA operators, and from prior clustering-based segmentation interpretations [41, 17] by arguing that compression is a more fundamental lens than clustering.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first theoretical justification for why Transformer decoders outperform simple MLP classifiers in segmentation, grounding the self-attention, cross-attention, and dot-product operations in PCA and compression principles. This opens a principled path for identifying and addressing performance bottlenecks in segmentation decoders rather than relying on empirical trial-and-error. However, the practical gains are modest (≈1 mIoU over Segmenter on ADE20K), DEPICT underperforms on smaller datasets (Cityscapes, Pascal Context), and the method is limited to per-patch classification without pixel decoders or hierarchical backbones. Whether the compression-derived operators are compatible with the mask classification formulation, hierarchical encoders, and pixel-level refinement remains an open question that could determine the method's broader impact.
