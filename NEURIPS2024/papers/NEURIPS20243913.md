## Problem

Existing video diffusion models require massive training data and compute, and text prompts cannot prescribe fine-grained physical motion dynamics (e.g., fluid advection, rigid-body rotation, flocking). The paper asks whether a pretrained image diffusion model can be animated into a video generator with physically accurate, user-controllable motion without any additional training or reference video.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243913/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single starting image (real or SD-generated), a positive/negative text prompt, a time-varying optical flow sequence extracted from a physics simulation (Φ-flow for Navier-Stokes fluids, rigid-body kinematics, or Boids multi-agent via agentpy), and a per-frame spatial noise map η^f indicating where novel content should be synthesized. It outputs an autoregressive sequence of N RGB frames at the resolution of the underlying diffusion model (512×512 for SD v1.5, higher for SDXL), with O(1) space complexity since each frame depends only on the first and immediately preceding frame.

## Method

The core insight is that optical flow estimated in pixel space correlates strongly (cosine similarity ≈ 0.73) with flow in the SD noise latent space, enabling physics-derived flow to be applied directly to latents rather than pixels. At each autoregressive step, the previous frame is DDIM-inverted to timestep τ=400, the physics flow warps the resulting noisy latent, and the warped latent is denoised back to a clean frame using DDIM with classifier-free guidance. Two novel mechanisms ensure quality: Multiple Cross-Frame Attention (MCFA) replaces self-attention keys/values with those from both the first frame (global consistency) and the previous frame (local consistency), and Spatial-η enables pixel-wise switching between deterministic DDIM (for already-present content in motion) and stochastic DDPM (for regions where new content enters the scene, e.g., flow pointing outside image boundaries). The entire pipeline is zero-shot—no training, no reference video, no learned flow estimator—requiring only vanilla Stable Diffusion and a physics simulator.

## Results

MOTIONCRAFT achieves an average Frame Consistency of 0.9819±0.02 and Motion Consistency of 0.8655±0.12 across five physics-based video generation scenes, outperforming the T2V0 baseline (0.9658±0.01 and 0.6740±0.23 respectively). The most dramatic improvement is on the Satellite Scan scene, where Motion Consistency jumps from 0.2852 (T2V0) to 0.9219 (MOTIONCRAFT). MOTIONCRAFT wins on 4 out of 5 scenes for both metrics, with the sole exception being the Revolving Earth scene where T2V0 slightly edges ahead on both Frame Consistency (0.9812 vs. 0.9696) and Motion Consistency (0.7213 vs. 0.6783).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares MOTIONCRAFT against a single zero-shot baseline, Text2Video-Zero (T2V0), which applies a constant directional shift to the initial latent noise of Stable Diffusion and samples frames sequentially via DDPM. MOTIONCRAFT outperforms T2V0 on 4 out of 5 scenes for both Frame Consistency and Motion Consistency. The largest margin is on the Satellite Scan rigid-body scene, where T2V0's Motion Consistency collapses to 0.2852 while MOTIONCRAFT reaches 0.9219, reflecting T2V0's inability to handle non-trivial motion patterns beyond a simple shift. On the Revolving Earth scene, T2V0 slightly outperforms MOTIONCRAFT on both metrics (Frame Consistency 0.9812 vs. 0.9696; Motion Consistency 0.7213 vs. 0.6783), likely because the rotational motion is closer to the constant-shift assumption that T2V0 implicitly encodes. Overall, MOTIONCRAFT's advantage is most pronounced in scenarios requiring complex, non-uniform motion (fluid dynamics, multi-agent flocking), where T2V0's fixed-direction shift produces temporally inconsistent and artefact-ridden videos.

## Relation to Prior Work

MOTIONCRAFT builds on the Stable Diffusion latent diffusion framework and DDIM inversion/sampling, but departs from prior zero-shot video generators (notably Text2Video-Zero) by replacing their constant latent shift with physics-simulation-derived optical flow applied in the noise latent domain. The key conceptual departure is the empirical observation that optical flow is preserved across the VAE/UNet bottleneck (0.727 cosine correlation), enabling the diffusion prior to synthesize novel content in flow-undefined regions rather than merely displacing pixels. The method also generalizes T2V0's Cross-Frame Attention into Multiple Cross-Frame Attention (attending to both first and previous frames) and introduces Spatial-η, a per-pixel DDIM/DDPM switching mechanism, both of which are architectural modifications to the standard diffusion sampling loop.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that fine-grained, user-controllable motion dynamics (fluid, rigid-body, multi-agent) can be injected into video generation without any training, decoupling temporal evolution from content synthesis. This opens a path toward explainable and physically grounded video generation that text-prompt-only models cannot achieve. Key limitations include dependence on simulatable dynamics (e.g., dancer motion is infeasible), progressive color drift in later frames, and the open question of whether a closed feedback loop between the image generator and physics simulator could improve physical fidelity. The approach also raises the broader question of whether generative flow models conditioned on prompts and constrained by physics could replace hand-crafted simulations.
