## Problem

Existing radar-camera 3D object detection methods that incorporate temporal information simply concatenate BEV feature maps across frames without accounting for object motion, causing misalignment of dynamic objects and degraded detection accuracy. This paper addresses the gap by introducing a motion-aware temporal fusion strategy that explicitly estimates and compensates for object velocity before merging features across timestamps.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243448/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 6 synchronized camera images (256×704 or 512×1408) and 5 radar sensors whose point clouds are accumulated over 6 sweeps, processed through ResNet/ConvNeXt and PointPillars backbones respectively. It produces 3D bounding boxes with class, orientation, and velocity via a CenterPoint detection head operating on a 128×128 or 256×256 BEV feature map. Internally, the MFE module outputs per-pixel velocity vectors (vx, vy) and binary occupancy scores on the BEV grid, which drive the spatial shifting in the MGTF module.

## Method

CRT-Fusion comprises three modules: (1) Multi-View Fusion (MVF) enhances camera perspective features with radar BEV features via Radar-Camera Azimuth Attention (RCA), which groups radar features by matching azimuth angles to image columns and applies learned attention weights, before depth prediction and gated BEV fusion; (2) Motion Feature Estimator (MFE) uses two parallel convolutional heads to predict pixel-wise velocity and object occupancy for each BEV grid, supervised by IoU-thresholded ground-truth boxes; (3) Motion-Guided Temporal Fusion (MGTF) shifts each past BEV feature map by the predicted velocity × frame duration, concatenates the shifted map with the next timestamp's features, and masks with the occupancy score to filter irrelevant regions, iterating recurrently over N=6 frames. The key distinction from prior temporal fusion is operating at the feature level in the BEV domain with explicit motion compensation rather than at the object level, decoupling temporal fusion quality from the initial detector's accuracy. Training is two-phase (6 epochs without MGTF, then 18 epochs with the full pipeline) using a composite loss of detection, depth, segmentation, velocity MSE, and occupancy focal loss.

## Results

On the nuScenes validation set with a ResNet-50 backbone, CRT-Fusion achieves 57.2 NDS and 50.0 mAP without CBGS, and 59.7 NDS and 50.8 mAP with CBGS, outperforming the previous best radar-camera method RCBEVDet by +2.9% NDS and +5.5% mAP. With a ResNet-101 backbone, CRT-Fusion reaches 62.1 NDS and 55.4 mAP, surpassing CRN by +2.9% NDS and +2.9% mAP. On the nuScenes test set with ConvNeXt-B, CRT-Fusion achieves 64.9 NDS and 58.3 mAP (65.6 NDS, 58.9 mAP with TTA), setting a new state-of-the-art for radar-camera 3D object detection. The lightweight variant CRT-Fusion-light maintains 20.5 FPS while achieving 57.8 NDS and 48.8 mAP.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CRT-Fusion consistently outperforms all radar-camera fusion baselines across backbone configurations. Against CRN (the previous SOTA radar-camera method), CRT-Fusion gains +1.2% NDS and +1.0% mAP with R50 (no CBGS), and +2.9% NDS and +2.9% mAP with R101. Against RCBEVDet (the best method with CBGS), CRT-Fusion† achieves +2.9% NDS and +5.5% mAP with R50. On the nuScenes test set with ConvNeXt-B, CRT-Fusion surpasses RCBEVDet by +1.0% NDS and +3.3% mAP, and CRN† (with TTA) by +2.5% NDS and +0.8% mAP. Compared to camera-only methods, CRT-Fusion (R50) improves over BEVDepth by +12.2% NDS and +15.7% mAP, and over SOLOFusion† by +3.8% NDS and +7.3% mAP, demonstrating the substantial benefit of radar fusion. The lightweight variant CRT-Fusion-light matches or exceeds CRN's FPS (20.5 vs. 20.4) while delivering +1.8% NDS and −0.2% mAP, showing competitive efficiency.

## Relation to Prior Work

CRT-Fusion builds directly on BEVDepth's camera-only BEV pipeline and extends the radar-camera fusion line (CRN, RCBEVDet, CRAFT) by introducing two key departures: (1) a Radar-Camera Azimuth Attention (RCA) module that enhances camera perspective-view features with radar BEV features *before* depth prediction, rather than fusing at the BEV level as in CRN; and (2) a motion-aware temporal fusion strategy (MFE + MGTF) that explicitly compensates for object displacement across frames, in contrast to prior temporal methods that simply concatenate BEV feature maps without accounting for dynamics. The framework uses CenterPoint's detection head and PointPillars for radar encoding, positioning itself as a systematic upgrade to the BEV-based radar-camera paradigm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The motion-guided temporal fusion addresses a concrete weakness in prior radar-camera methods—degraded detection of dynamic objects—yielding consistent gains across velocity ranges and weather conditions, which is practically important for real-world autonomous driving deployment. A notable limitation is that the parallel temporal fusion structure causes computational cost to grow with the number of history frames, capping the usable temporal window; the authors identify recurrent fusion as a promising direction for scaling to longer horizons. The finding that MFE+MGTF actually degrades a camera-only model (Table 5) underscores that these modules are specifically suited to radar-camera settings where velocity estimation is reliable, an important caveat for practitioners considering transfer to other sensor configurations.
