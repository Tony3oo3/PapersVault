## Problem

Latent Consistency Models (LCMs) suffer from three fundamental design flaws: (1) their purely stochastic multi-step sampling causes inconsistent outputs across different inference-step settings with the same seed, (2) classifier-free guidance is restricted to a narrow 1–2 range and negative prompts are largely ignored due to the CFG-augmented ODE solver used during distillation, and (3) the standard L2/Huber loss provides insufficient fine-grained supervision for few-step (especially 1–3 step) generation, yielding markedly inferior samples. The paper asks whether the consistency design space can be generalized to resolve all three limitations simultaneously.

## Input / Output

The model operates in the VAE latent space of Stable Diffusion v1-5 (512×512) or SDXL (1024×1024), taking a text prompt, an optional negative prompt, and a random Gaussian noise vector as input, and producing a decoded RGB image or video as output. A key operational detail is that the number of inference steps (1–16) is decoupled from the number of sub-trajectories M used during training: the model is trained with M sub-trajectories (e.g., M=4) but can be sampled deterministically at any step count by selecting a subset of the M+1 edge timesteps, enabling multi-step refinement without retraining. For video generation, the same phased framework is applied following the AnimateLCM decoupled distillation design on WebVid-2M.

## Method

PCMs split the full PF-ODE trajectory into M sub-trajectories delimited by edge timesteps s₀=ε < s₁ < … < s_M=T, and enforce the self-consistency property independently on each sub-trajectory, so that all points within sub-trajectory m map to the same solution point x_{s_m}. The parameterization uses a DDIM-like closed form x_s = (α_s/α_t)x_t − α_s·ε̂_θ(x_t,t)·∫_{λ_t}^{λ_s} e^{−λ}dλ, but the learning target is the exact weighted integral of the teacher's epsilon predictions along the trajectory rather than a first-order score approximation, which is what distinguishes it from DDIM and enables stable few-step inference. Two additional mechanisms address the remaining LCM flaws: (a) the distillation ODE solver can optionally omit CFG (viable only because phasing reduces the distribution gap at each sub-trajectory boundary), which restores sensitivity to negative prompts and allows larger CFG values at inference; and (b) an adversarial consistency loss in latent space compares the student's predicted solution (noised to a random intermediate timestep) against the teacher's ODE-solved solution (noised identically), enforcing distributional alignment without the training-conflict that arises from a standard GAN loss when pre-training and distillation data distributions mismatch.

## Results

On COCO-30K with Stable Diffusion v1-5, PCM achieves FID-SD of 8.27 (1-step), 5.81 (4-step), and 4.70 (16-step), substantially outperforming LCM (53.43/6.66/7.56) and CTM (63.55/9.30/21.75) across all step settings. For 1-step generation, PCM reaches FID-SD 8.27 and CLIP Score 29.26, surpassing SD-Turbo (FID-SD 10.62) while achieving the highest consistency score (0.81 vs. 0.71). On Stable Diffusion XL, PCM attains FID-SD 4.92 at 16 steps, beating SDXL-Lightning (6.10) and SDXL-LCM (23.33). For video generation, PCM achieves CLIP Score 30.72 and Flow Magnitude 4.69 at 4 steps, outperforming AnimateLCM (30.62 / 2.40).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PCM decisively outperforms LCM and CTM across all step settings: at 16 steps on COCO-30K, PCM achieves FID-SD 4.70 vs. LCM's 7.56 and CTM's 21.75, with the gap widening as steps increase (CTM degrades to 139.29 at 16 steps on CC12M). Against SD-Turbo (adversarial diffusion distillation), PCM wins on FID-SD at 1-step (8.27 vs. 10.62) and dominates at 4+ steps (5.81 vs. 16.66 at 4 steps), while SD-Turbo retains a slight edge on CLIP Score at 1-step (31.21 vs. 29.26). InstaFlow (rectified-flow-based) is competitive only at 1-step (FID-SD 11.51) but degrades catastrophically at 2+ steps (69.79 at 2 steps), confirming it is not designed for multi-step sampling. On SDXL, PCM (FID-SD 4.92 at 16 steps) beats SDXL-Lightning (6.10) and SDXL-LCM (23.33), though SDXL-Turbo achieves a lower 1-step FID-SD (6.64) at 512×512 resolution. For video generation, PCM surpasses AnimateLCM on CLIP Score at 1-step (29.88 vs. 25.41) and Flow Magnitude at all steps (4.56/4.38/4.69 vs. 1.10/1.81/2.40), while matching or slightly exceeding it on CLIP Consistency (0.968 vs. 0.965 at 4 steps). TCD (trajectory consistency distillation) is only compared at 1-step and is clearly outperformed (FID-SD 68.04 vs. 8.27).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the consistency model framework (Song et al., 2023) and its latent variant (Luo et al., 2023) by phasing the PF-ODE trajectory into multiple sub-trajectories, each enforcing self-consistency independently, rather than mapping all points to a single solution. It explicitly departs from Consistency Trajectory Models (Kim et al., 2023), which learn O(N²) objectives, by restricting consistency to within-sub-trajectory pairs (O(N) objectives), and distinguishes itself from Multistep Consistency Models (Heek et al., 2024) through explicit boundary conditions, theoretical error bounds, and a fundamentally different parameterization (exact solution learning vs. first-order DDIM approximation). The adversarial consistency loss is theoretically shown to be compatible with consistency distillation (converging to zero when consistency is achieved), unlike standard GAN loss which provably fails under data distribution mismatch.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clear diagnostic for why LCMs suffer from limited CFG range and negative-prompt insensitivity (Theorem 4: double-CFG is equivalent to scaling by w·w'), and offers a practical fix (phasing enables CFG-free distillation). The substantial performance gap over LCMs (e.g., FID-SD 8.27 vs. 53.43 at 1-step on SD v1.5) and the extension to video generation make this practically impactful for few-step generation pipelines. However, the authors acknowledge that 1-step generation remains unstable with structural errors, and the core phasing idea has prior art in multistep consistency models, limiting the conceptual novelty. Open questions include whether the approach scales to even larger models (e.g., 10B+ parameter DiTs) and whether the adversarial loss can be replaced by lighter alternatives.
