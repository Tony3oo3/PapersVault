## Problem

Diffusion policies in offline RL are highly expressive but overfit to the fixed offline dataset, causing severe Q-value overestimation on out-of-distribution actions. Standard maximum-entropy regularization is intractable for diffusion-based policies because the log probability of the stochastic denoising sequence has no closed form. The paper tackles both gaps simultaneously: deriving a tractable entropy for a mean-reverting SDE policy and pairing it with pessimistic Q-ensemble estimation to prevent distributional shift.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a continuous environment state s_i and a pre-collected offline dataset D of (s, a, r, s') tuples, and produces a continuous action a_i generated by a reverse-time mean-reverting SDE conditioned on s_i. Action sampling requires only 5–10 diffusion steps (versus 30+ for the standard reverse SDE) thanks to a closed-form Gaussian posterior. The Q-value guiding policy improvement is the lower confidence bound (ensemble mean minus β·ensemble std) of 64 independently-trained Q-networks, not a single Q estimate.

## Method

The policy backbone is a mean-reverting SDE (Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process with σ_t² = 2θ_t) whose forward process drives actions to N(0,I) and whose reverse process generates actions from Gaussian noise conditioned on state. The key theoretical result is that the conditional posterior p(a^{t-1} | a^t, a^0) is Gaussian, which simultaneously yields an optimal 5-step sampling path and a tractable entropy: −log π(a_i⁰|s_i) = −log π(a_i¹|s_i) + C, where the constant is discarded. The training objective combines a noise-matching diffusion loss with a Q-guided policy-improvement term that uses the LCB of Q-ensembles (each member updated with its own independent Bellman target, following MSG) and an entropy-maximization term weighted by temperature α. The LCB coefficient β=4 and ensemble size M=64 control the degree of pessimism, while the entropy term encourages exploration of OOD actions without the overestimation that would result from a single Q-network.

## Results

On the D4RL AntMaze benchmark, the proposed method achieves an average normalized score of 86.7, outperforming the next-best method MSG (83.6) and Diffusion-QL (69.6) by 3.1 and 17.1 points respectively. On Gym tasks, the method reaches an average of 89.9, surpassing Diffusion-QL (88.0) and IDQL-A (82.1). On Kitchen tasks, it achieves 75.7 average, beating Diffusion-QL (69.0) and IQL+EDP (59.4). The method is evaluated over 5 random seeds with 100 evaluation trajectories per task.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method achieves the highest average score across all four D4RL domains. On AntMaze (the most challenging domain with sparse rewards), it outperforms MSG (83.6) by 3.1 points and Diffusion-QL (69.6) by 17.1 points, with the largest margin on Antmaze-large-play-v0 (81.2 vs. 72.6 for MSG and 46.4 for Diff-QL). On Gym tasks, it beats Diffusion-QL (88.0) by 1.9 points on average, with the clearest win on Halfcheetah-medium-replay-v2 (57.0 vs. 47.8). On Kitchen, it surpasses Diffusion-QL (69.0) by 6.7 points and IQL+EDP (59.4) by 16.3 points. On Adroit, it trails IQL+EDP (71.4) by 2.2 points on average, which the authors attribute to the fixed entropy temperature being suboptimal for precise-control tasks; with auto-tuned α, it reaches 79.8 on Pen-cloned-v1. Against conservative baselines (CQL, IQL, BCQ, BEAR), the method dominates across all domains, with particularly large gaps on AntMaze where BC and DT score 0.0 on medium/large mazes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Diffusion-QL [44], which uses diffusion models as expressive policies guided by Q-values in offline RL, by adding two components that were previously incompatible with diffusion-based policies: (1) a tractable entropy regularization derived from the mean-reverting (Ornstein-Uhlenbeck) SDE formulation, and (2) pessimistic value estimation via independent Q-ensembles (following MSG [11] and EDAC [1]). The SDE framework itself builds on IR-SDE [32] for image restoration, repurposing the mean-reverting process to model action distributions rather than pixel data. The entropy regularization connects to maximum-entropy RL (SAC [14,15]) but addresses the key obstacle that log-probabilities of diffusion policies were previously considered intractable.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The tractable entropy derivation for diffusion policies is a clean theoretical contribution that opens the door to maximum-entropy training for any score-based generative policy, not just this specific SDE. The 5-step optimal sampling (vs. 30+ for standard reverse SDE) is practically important for deployment on compute-constrained devices, though the authors acknowledge that real-time distillation remains an open challenge. The strong AntMaze results (86.7 vs. 69.6 for Diffusion-QL) demonstrate that combining exploration (entropy) with caution (LCB) is more effective than either alone for sparse-reward, suboptimal-trajectory settings. A limitation is that the method still requires iterative SDE sampling at inference time, and the fixed hyperparameters (α, β, M) that work well across D4RL tasks may not transfer to substantially different domains.
