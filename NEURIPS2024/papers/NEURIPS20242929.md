## Problem

Benchmark image datasets contain off-topic samples, near duplicates, and label errors that corrupt evaluation scores and hinder training, yet existing cleaning tools either rely on supervised features that inherit annotation bias, address only one issue type at a time, or lack dataset-specific context. This paper formalizes data cleaning as a ranking or scoring problem and asks whether a single, annotation-free pipeline can detect all three issue types simultaneously across diverse domains.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242929/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a labeled image classification dataset (RGB images resized to 224×224) and produces, for each of the three issue types, a score in [0,1] per sample or pair where lower values indicate higher likelihood of being problematic, along with an induced ranking. In human-in-the-loop mode the ranking guides a curator through a prioritized inspection list; in fully automatic mode, a logit-transformed score distribution is thresholded using a contamination-rate guess and significance level to flag issues without human review. No external annotations or pre-trained supervised models are required at inference time.

## Method

SELFCLEAN first trains a ViT-tiny encoder via self-supervised learning (DINO or SimCLR) directly on the target dataset, including its quality issues, and L2-normalizes the resulting embeddings onto a unit hypersphere so that cosine distance serves as the similarity metric. Off-topic samples are ranked by agglomerative single-linkage clustering in this space, where samples whose clusters merge late with larger groups are flagged as outliers. Near duplicates are identified by sorting all sample pairs in ascending order of pairwise cosine distance. Label errors are scored by the squared intra-/extra-class distance ratio, comparing each sample's distance to its nearest same-label neighbor against its nearest different-label neighbor. The key insight is that dataset-specific SSL representations capture the local geometric structure needed for all three distance-based indicators simultaneously, outperforming general-purpose supervised or self-supervised features, particularly for off-topic and near-duplicate detection on small or domain-specific datasets.

## Results

On synthetic 5% contamination across STL-10, VinDr-BodyPartXR, and DDI, SELFCLEAN with DINO pre-training achieves 98.4–100% AUROC and 55.1–100% AP for off-topic detection, 98.2–100% AUROC and 43.7–48.2% AP for near-duplicate detection, and 71.4–99.2% AUROC and 13.5–88.1% AP for label-error detection, outperforming all baselines. On natural contamination, SELFCLEAN nearly doubles the AP for label-error detection on ImageNet-1k (8.4% vs. 4.3%) and Food-101N (47.8% vs. 30.7%), and achieves 30.9% AP for near-duplicate detection on CelebA. Applying the fully automatic mode to 12 benchmarks reveals up to 16% near-duplicate prevalence (Fitzpatrick17k) and 5.4% unaccounted near duplicates in ISIC-2019, with cleaning the evaluation set significantly altering downstream F1 scores (e.g., −4.1 pt on Fitzpatrick17k kNN).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SELFCLEAN with DINO pre-training decisively outperforms all eight baselines across all three issue types. For off-topic detection, HBOS and ECOD (both operating on ImageNet features) achieve 66.9–95.7% AUROC but collapse to 1.9–2.2% AUROC on STL+BLUR, while SELFCLEAN (DINO) maintains 100% AUROC; the gap is largest on STL where dataset-specific representations capture context that general-purpose features miss. For near-duplicate detection, pHashing and SSIM are limited to pixel-level similarity and achieve <0.1–22.5% AP, whereas SELFCLEAN (DINO) reaches 43.7–50.8% AP by leveraging semantic proximity in the latent space. For label-error detection, Confident Learning is the strongest competitor (up to 79.0% AP on VDR+LBL), but SELFCLEAN (DINO) still leads with 88.1% AP on the same setting and 54.2% vs. 41.6% on STL+LBL; FastDup and NoiseRank trail further behind. On natural contamination, SELFCLEAN achieves roughly 2× the AP of the best competitor for label errors on both ImageNet-1k (8.4% vs. 4.3%) and Food-101N (47.8% vs. 30.7%), confirming the advantage generalizes beyond synthetic settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

SELFCLEAN repurposes self-supervised representation learning (DINO, SimCLR) for data auditing rather than downstream classification, combining dataset-specific SSL features with simple distance-based indicators (agglomerative clustering for outliers, pairwise distance for near duplicates, intra/extra-class ratio for label errors). It departs from prior data-cleaning tools (CleanLab, CleanVision, FastDup) that rely on supervised or handcrafted features by requiring no annotations during training, and from specialized outlier/near-duplicate/label-error methods by unifying all three under a single SSL embedding. The key conceptual shift is that the SSL model is trained on the contaminated dataset itself, so the latent space encodes the dataset's own context rather than a generic image prior.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that widely-used benchmarks (Fitzpatrick17k, CelebA, Food-101N) contain up to ~15% near duplicates that introduce data leaks and optimistically bias performance estimates, and that cleaning these sets measurably shifts downstream F1 scores. This has direct implications for how the field interprets benchmark results, especially as popular benchmarks saturate. Open questions include scaling near-duplicate detection to very large datasets, handling ambiguous or multi-label annotations, and establishing a standard evaluation protocol for data-cleaning methods. The authors also note that the automatic mode can introduce sampling bias, and that the tension between cleaning evaluation sets and avoiding optimistic bias has no clean resolution.
