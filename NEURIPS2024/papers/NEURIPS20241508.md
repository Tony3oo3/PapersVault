## Problem

Existing optimization-based methods that integrate diffusion models as priors for inverse problems (e.g., via ELBO or variational inference) primarily exploit the prior information encoded in the diffusion model while neglecting its inherent denoising capability. This paper addresses the gap by showing that observation noise can be absorbed into an auxiliary variable representing a noisy sample at an equivalent diffusion timestep, thereby allowing both the prior and the denoising mechanism to be leveraged simultaneously within a single optimization framework.

## Input / Output

The method takes a degraded observation y (e.g., a blurred, inpainted, or downsampled image; a mixed audio signal; or a Fourier magnitude spectrum), the corresponding observation operator A (linear or nonlinear), the noise level σ, and a pre-trained diffusion model (μ-predictor) as inputs, and produces a restored image (256×256 on ImageNet, 64×64 on CelebA/FFHQ), four separated instrument tracks (piano, bass, guitar, drums), or harmonically consistent generated tracks. No retraining of the diffusion model is required; the algorithm operates entirely in the forward/sampling direction of the pre-trained model, with the only tunable hyperparameters being step sizes η₁, η₂ and the equivalent noise level t_a determined by σ.

## Method

ProjDiff introduces an auxiliary variable x_{t_a} representing a noisy sample at an equivalent diffusion timestep (where ᾱ_{t_a} = 1/(1+σ²)), reformulating the inverse problem as a two-variable constrained optimization: maximize the joint log-prior log p_θ(x₀, x_{t_a}) subject to the linear constraint y' = [I, 0] x_{t_a}. A novel two-variable ELBO is derived (Proposition 1) that decomposes into a denoising matching term (for t ≤ t_a, pulling x₀ toward the μ-predictor's estimate) and a noisy prior term (for t > t_a, pulling x_{t_a} toward the diffusion manifold while respecting the observation). To avoid expensive Jacobian backpropagation through the μ-predictor, gradients are truncated (D_{x₀}μ_θ ≈ 0, D_{x_{t_a}}μ_θ ≈ 0), yielding simple closed-form stochastic gradients. The constrained variable x_{t_a} is updated via projection gradient descent (projecting back onto the observation subspace after each gradient step), while x₀ is updated by plain stochastic gradient descent, with time steps traversed in decreasing order from T to 1.

## Results

On noisy ImageNet restoration (σ=0.05, 100 NFEs), ProjDiff achieves 31.09 PSNR / 13.65 LPIPS / 13.69 FID on inpainting and 27.91 PSNR / 25.11 LPIPS / 32.27 FID on Gaussian deblurring, outperforming DDRM, DDNM+, DPS, and RED-diff. On the nonlinear phase retrieval task (FFHQ, 1000 NFEs), ProjDiff achieves 33.39 PSNR (noise-free) and 23.84 PSNR (σ=0.1), dramatically surpassing DPS (13.19/12.58) and RED-diff (14.19/12.36). On the SLACK2100 source separation task, ProjDiff achieves 19.23 average SI-SDRi, surpassing the previous SOTA Demucs+Gibbs (17.73) for the first time with a diffusion-based method.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DDRM and DDNM+ (both 100 NFEs), ProjDiff wins decisively on inpainting (31.09 vs ~30.00 PSNR; 13.65 vs ~17.1 LPIPS) and Gaussian deblurring (25.11 vs ~28.6 LPIPS; 32.27 vs ~46 FID), while roughly matching on super-resolution. Against DPS (1000 NFEs), ProjDiff wins on all three linear tasks despite using 10× fewer function evaluations. RED-diff [24] performs catastrophically on inpainting (9.85 PSNR) and is outperformed by ProjDiff on all tasks. On the nonlinear phase retrieval task, ProjDiff achieves 33.39 PSNR (noise-free) versus 13.19 (DPS) and 14.19 (RED-diff), a gap of ~20 dB, and similarly dominates traditional methods (ER 11.15, HIO 11.97, OSS 12.57). On source separation, ProjDiff (19.23 avg SI-SDRi) surpasses the previous SOTA Demucs+Gibbs (17.73) and RED-diff (17.72), and on partial generation it outperforms both MSDM and RED-diff across all 14 sub-tasks (e.g., BDG: 1.66 vs 2.19 vs 5.65 sub-FAD).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the optimization-based framework for diffusion priors in inverse problems established by Graikos et al. (NeurIPS 2022) and Mardani et al. (ICLR 2024), which both use the ELBO as a plug-and-play prior but handle observation noise only through a Gaussian likelihood term. The key conceptual departure is borrowing the 'equivalent noisy sample' idea from Cardoso et al. (ICLR 2024) and formalizing it as an auxiliary optimization variable, yielding a two-variable constrained objective that explicitly separates prior exploitation (noisy prior term) from denoising (denoising matching term). The DDIM reference distribution (Song et al., ICLR 2021) underpins the ELBO derivation, and gradient truncation through the μ-predictor makes the stochastic gradients computationally tractable without backpropagating through the network.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The two-variable formulation provides a principled way to simultaneously exploit both the data prior and the denoising capability of diffusion models, addressing a gap the authors identify in prior optimization-based methods. The practical impact is demonstrated by ProjDiff being the first diffusion-based algorithm to surpass Demucs in music source separation, and by large margins in phase retrieval. Open questions include extending the method to non-Gaussian noise (Poisson, multiplicative), handling highly complex nonlinear noisy observations where equivalent noise mapping is intractable, and developing adaptive step-size strategies to reduce manual tuning. The Restricted Encoding mechanism for weak-observation scenarios is a useful practical tool but its theoretical grounding remains heuristic.
