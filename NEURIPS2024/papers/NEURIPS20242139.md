## Problem

Existing LLM-based embodied planning methods (ZSP, SayCan, ProgPrompt, LLM-Planner) are designed for single, one-time instructions and lack a systematic mechanism to handle multiple simultaneous conditional instructions that must be continuously monitored in non-stationary environments. Without an efficient memory of the environment and a principled way to decide when to explore versus exploit, agents either re-collect environmental knowledge redundantly at every planning step or rely on stale observations that decay in validity over time.

## Input / Output

The method takes a set of M conditional natural-language instructions (e.g., 'If the TV is off, turn it on'), per-timestep environmental observations from 3D simulators (object positions, states, room adjacencies), and a Temporal Embodied Knowledge Graph (TEKG) of quadruples (source entity, relation, target entity, timestep) as context memory. At each timestep it outputs a single selected skill from a discrete action set (walk, grab, switch, put, open, close, etc.), per-query satisfaction probabilities with confidence levels, and a filtered set of executions to perform. The TEKG is updated incrementally via a contradiction-detection function that removes semantically conflicting quadruples (e.g., an object being both 'on' and 'off').

## Method

ExRAP decomposes each conditional instruction into a query (condition) and an execution (action) via an LLM instruction interpreter, then operates two coupled modules each timestep. First, a memory-augmented query evaluator retrieves relevant TEKG quadruples via sentence-embedding similarity (DPR + BM25) and uses an LLM to estimate P(query | memory), with an entropy-based temporal consistency constraint that discards responses whose entropy fails to increase relative to the previous step, thereby modeling information decay. Second, an exploration-integrated planner selects the next skill by maximizing a weighted sum of an exploitation value (LLM in-context scoring of skill effectiveness for pending executions, conditioned on retrieved demonstrations) and an exploration value (approximated mutual-information gain, computed as the reduction in query-response entropy weighted by the agent's graph-distance to query-relevant entities in the TEKG). This information-theoretic exploration term, adapted from DREAM's meta-RL framework, is the first to be integrated into LLM-based embodied task planning, enabling the agent to proactively visit regions that reduce uncertainty about unsatisfied conditions rather than only pursuing immediate task completion.

## Results

ExRAP achieves superior performance across all three environments (VirtualHome, ALFRED, CARLA) and all non-stationarity levels. On average, ExRAP gains 16.45% in Success Rate (SR) and reduces Pending Steps (PS) by 3.40 compared to the strongest baseline, LLM-Planner. The advantage widens with increasing non-stationarity: the SR gap over LLM-Planner grows from 15.35% (low) to 18.58% (high), and the PS gap expands from 2.96 to 3.73. In ALFRED under medium non-stationarity, ExRAP reaches 64.00% SR with 4.82 PS, versus 51.80% SR and 7.28 PS for LLM-Planner.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ExRAP consistently outperforms all four baselines across every environment and non-stationarity level on both SR and PS. Against the strongest baseline, LLM-Planner, ExRAP gains 16.45% SR on average (e.g., 55.14% vs. 39.89% in VirtualHome medium; 64.00% vs. 51.80% in ALFRED medium; 62.25% vs. 44.00% in CARLA medium) and reduces PS by 3.40 on average. SayCan and ProgPrompt trail LLM-Planner by 6–10% SR in most settings, with SayCan performing best in ALFRED (41.81% SR medium) but degrading sharply in CARLA (35.11% SR medium). ZSP is the weakest baseline across all environments, achieving only 20.06% SR in VirtualHome and 6.89% in CARLA under medium non-stationarity. Notably, ExRAP's advantage over LLM-Planner widens as non-stationarity increases (SR gap: 15.35% → 18.58%) and as instruction scale grows (SR gap: 18.49% → 22.04%), indicating the exploration-integrated planning and temporal consistency mechanisms become increasingly critical under dynamic, multi-task conditions.

## Relation to Prior Work

ExRAP sits at the intersection of three research threads: LLM-based embodied task planning (ZSP, SayCan, ProgPrompt, LLM-Planner), retrieval-augmented generation for grounding LLM reasoning in environmental state, and information-theoretic exploration from reinforcement learning (specifically DREAM's mutual-information-based exploration policy). The key departure from prior embodied planning work is the reframing of instruction following as a *continual*, multi-instruction, non-stationary problem rather than a single-shot directive, drawing an explicit analogy to continuous queries in database systems. The temporal embodied knowledge graph (TEKG) with entropy-based temporal consistency refinement is a novel memory mechanism not present in prior LLM-planner approaches, which typically rely on one-shot observation-to-plan pipelines without persistent, decaying environmental memory.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The continual instruction following formulation is more realistic for deployed home robots and autonomous driving agents that must simultaneously monitor multiple conditions and respond to environmental changes, yet prior LLM-based planners have largely ignored this setting. The finding that ExRAP maintains strong performance even with a 2B-parameter LLM (Gemma-2B) while baselines degrade sharply suggests that structured environmental memory and information-theoretic exploration can partially compensate for limited LLM capacity, which has practical implications for edge deployment. Open questions include the computational overhead of maintaining the TEKG and running iterative query evaluation at scale, whether the approach generalizes to truly open-world (non-simulated) environments, and how the method behaves when the number of simultaneous instructions grows beyond the 3–7 range tested here.
