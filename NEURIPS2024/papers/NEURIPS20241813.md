## Problem

Pairwise application of CLIP to more than two modalities captures only pairwise mutual information and fails to model higher-order conditional dependencies (e.g., I(a;b|c)). The paper demonstrates this failure with a controlled XOR experiment where the target is perfectly predictable from two modalities jointly, yet pairwise CLIP performs at random chance, revealing a fundamental gap in how existing contrastive objectives handle multimodal data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241813/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts any number of modalities per sample (e.g., image + text + audio, or ECG + blood labs + chest X-ray), each encoded by an independent, architecture-agnostic encoder into a shared 8192-dimensional embedding space. The scoring function is the multilinear inner product (MIP)—the coordinate-wise sum of element-wise products across all modality embeddings—replacing CLIP's pairwise dot product. For zero-shot retrieval, the element-wise product of any subset of representations serves as a sufficient statistic for ranking candidates of the remaining modality, and the full conditional probability p(y|x,z) can be computed directly from the MIP plus the marginal p(y).

## Method

Symile derives a multi-sample contrastive lower bound on total correlation (the KL divergence between the joint distribution and the product of marginals over all modalities), generalizing the InfoNCE/CLIP bound from pairwise mutual information. The loss maximizes this bound by contrasting positive tuples sampled from the joint distribution against negative tuples formed from the product of marginals, using the MIP as the scoring function. Each modality is treated as the anchor in turn, and the final loss averages the three (or M) directional terms. Theoretically, the optimal Symile representations form a sufficient statistic: the element-wise product of any subset of modality embeddings contains all information needed to predict the remaining modalities. For missing data, indicator dimensions are appended to encoder inputs so that incomplete samples remain out-of-support, allowing Symile to model dependencies among whichever modalities are observed.

## Results

On the Symile-M3 multilingual dataset (33M samples), Symile achieves zero-shot retrieval accuracies of 0.939, 0.919, and 0.882 on the 2-, 5-, and 10-language variants, respectively, while pairwise CLIP is bounded by 1/w and achieves only 0.473, 0.187, and 0.094. On the Symile-MIMIC clinical dataset (11,622 admissions), Symile reaches 0.435 ± 0.007 (SE) retrieval accuracy versus CLIP's 0.387 ± 0.003, a 12.5% relative improvement over random chance (0.1). Even with only 12.5% of training triples complete (each modality missing with probability 0.5), Symile retains 0.906 accuracy on Symile-M3-2, far exceeding CLIP's 0.473.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is pairwise CLIP, which the authors identify as the only architecture-agnostic contrastive approach for more than two modalities. Symile decisively outperforms CLIP on all three experimental settings. On Symile-M3, the gap is dramatic: Symile achieves 0.939 vs. CLIP's 0.473 on the 2-language variant, and the gap widens as the number of languages increases (0.919 vs. 0.187 at 5 languages; 0.882 vs. 0.094 at 10 languages), because CLIP's accuracy is fundamentally bounded by 1/w while Symile leverages joint information across all three modalities. On the clinical Symile-MIMIC dataset, Symile improves over CLIP by 12.5% relative (0.435 vs. 0.387), a more modest but statistically significant gain reflecting the more complex, real-world dependencies in medical data. Symile also maintains its advantage under missing data: with only 12.5% of training triples complete, Symile still reaches 0.906 on Symile-M3-2, well above CLIP's 0.473.

## Relation to Prior Work

Symile directly generalizes CLIP's contrastive framework from two modalities to an arbitrary number, replacing the dot-product scoring function with the multilinear inner product (MIP) and targeting total correlation instead of mutual information. The derivation of the total correlation lower bound adapts the variational technique from Barber & Agakov (2003), which underlies InfoNCE, to the multi-modality setting. The paper positions itself as the multi-modality analogue of CLIP's extension of SimCLR: just as CLIP moved contrastive learning from data augmentations to distinct modalities, Symile moves it from pairwise to joint information across any number of modalities. It departs from prior multimodal contrastive work (ImageBind, AudioCLIP, etc.) by showing that their pairwise CLIP objective is fundamentally incapable of capturing higher-order conditional dependencies, a limitation demonstrated through a simple XOR counterexample.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that pairwise CLIP performs at random chance on a problem where the target is perfectly predictable from all three modalities jointly is a clear demonstration that the dominant approach to multimodal representation learning is missing an entire class of dependencies. The sufficient-statistic guarantee (Theorem 3.3) provides a strong theoretical foundation for downstream use, and the released datasets (Symile-M3, Symile-MIMIC) offer concrete benchmarks for evaluating higher-order multimodal learning. Open questions include how the negative-sampling approximation scales beyond three modalities, whether the MIP-based loss can be adapted to memory-efficient sigmoid objectives (SigLIP), and how pre-trained Symile representations integrate into multimodal large language models. The moderate real-world gains (12.5% on MIMIC) suggest the practical benefit, while real, may be most pronounced in domains where higher-order interactions dominate over pairwise ones.
