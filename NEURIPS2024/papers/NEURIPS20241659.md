## Problem

PPO, the de-facto RL algorithm for fine-tuning generative models, requires multiple heuristics (value networks, clipping, KL regularization) that were designed for randomly initialized networks in continuous control, making it fragile, memory-intensive (four billion-parameter models in memory), and sensitive to implementation details. The paper asks what a minimalist RL algorithm would look like for the era of large generative models, where the policy is already well-initialized and the main challenge is stable, scalable policy optimization.

## Input / Output

At each iteration, REBEL consumes a prompt x, generates an on-policy completion y ~ π_t(·|x), and samples a paired completion y' from a base distribution μ (either an offline dataset or π_t itself), then queries a reward model to obtain r(x,y) and r(x,y'). The regression target is the scalar reward difference r(x,y) − r(x,y'), and the predictor is the log-policy-ratio difference (1/η)(ln π_θ(y|x)/π_t(y|x) − ln π_θ(y'|x)/π_t(y'|x)). The output is a sequence of fine-tuned policies; the final policy generates text or images. The base distribution μ can be hybrid (mixing offline and on-policy data), and in the preference setting the reward difference is replaced by a preference model's win-rate difference.

## Method

REBEL inverts the closed-form solution of the KL-regularized RL problem to express reward as a function of the policy, then eliminates the intractable partition function by regressing on paired samples (x, y, y') so that the partition function cancels in the difference. The resulting objective is a simple squared loss: the policy's log-ratio difference should predict the reward difference, solved by standard gradient descent (AdamW) at each iteration. This eliminates value networks, clipping heuristics, and Fisher Information Matrix inversion entirely. Theoretically, exact REBEL recovers Mirror Descent with KL divergence, and NPG emerges as a special case where the regression is solved by a single Gauss-Newton step; the regret bound O(√(1/T) + √(C·ε)) matches the strongest agnostic guarantees in the RL literature. The algorithm also extends cleanly to intransitive preferences via a Self-Play Preference Optimization reduction, using the preference model's win-rate against the current policy as an iteration-dependent reward.

## Results

On TL;DR summarization, REBEL achieves the highest GPT-4 winrate across all model sizes: 55.1% (1.4B) and 70.2% (2.8B), outperforming PPO (51.7% and 67.4%) and DPO (42.7% and 53.7%). On general chat with OpenChat-3.5, REBEL improves MT-Bench from 7.69 (base) to 8.06 and AlpacaEval 2.0 LC Win Rate from 12.2 to 17.3, surpassing the APA-trained Starling-LM-7B-alpha (7.43 and 14.7). When fine-tuning Llama-3-8B-Instruct, REBEL (1 iteration) reaches 48.3% AlpacaEval 2.0 LC Win Rate and 34.5% Arena Hard, beating DPO (44.9% and 34.0%). In image generation, REBEL converges faster than PPO in the early phase and reaches comparable asymptotic LAION aesthetic scores.

## Baselines & Comparisons

REBEL outperforms PPO on TL;DR winrate by 3.4 points (1.4B) and 2.8 points (2.8B) while using less peak memory and shorter runtime, since PPO requires maintaining both a policy and a value network. Against DPO, REBEL gains 12.4 and 16.5 winrate points at 1.4B and 2.8B respectively, and on Llama-3-8B AlpacaEval 2.0 REBEL (48.3%) beats DPO (44.9%) by 3.4 points, demonstrating the benefit of using fine-grained reward gaps over binary preference labels. REBEL also surpasses Iterative DPO by 7.9 and 7.1 winrate points on TL;DR at 1.4B and 2.8B. Compared to RLOO(k=4), which achieves similar winrates, REBEL requires only 2 generations per prompt versus 4, yielding lower memory usage and faster runtime. On general chat, REBEL outperforms APA (the method used to train Starling-LM-7B-alpha) on both MT-Bench (8.06 vs 7.43) and AlpacaEval 2.0 LC Win Rate (17.3 vs 14.7). In image generation, REBEL converges faster than the clipped PPO baseline in early training and reaches comparable asymptotic LAION aesthetic scores, while producing more diverse backgrounds.

## Relation to Prior Work

REBEL sits at the intersection of three lines of work: (1) the DPO reparameterization trick (Rafailov et al., 2023), which inverts the KL-regularized optimal policy to express reward in terms of the policy ratio; (2) the Mirror Descent / Natural Policy Gradient lineage (Kakade, 2001; Schulman et al., 2017), where REBEL is shown to recover exact MD under perfect regression and NPG as a one-step Gauss-Newton approximation; and (3) the SPO reduction for general preferences (Swamy et al., 2024), which REBEL instantiates with a regression-based base learner. The key departure from PPO is the elimination of both the value network and clipping heuristics, replacing them with a single squared-loss regression on relative rewards; the key departure from DPO is the use of fine-grained reward differences as continuous regression targets rather than binary win/loss labels.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

REBEL offers a practically simpler and theoretically stronger alternative to PPO for RLHF: it removes the need for a critic network and clipping, reduces memory from four models to two, and matches the strongest known agnostic regret bounds in the RL literature. Open questions include whether alternative loss functions (log loss, cross-entropy) yield tighter first-order or gap-dependent bounds, whether the coverage condition is truly necessary in the general (intransitive) preference setting, and how the method extends to full MDPs with resets. The modest but consistent empirical gains over PPO across summarization, chat, and image generation suggest the approach could become a practical default for generative model fine-tuning, though the margins are not large enough to be transformative on their own.
