## Problem

Discriminative inverse protein folding models suffer from error accumulation and cannot capture the one-to-many mapping from a single backbone to multiple plausible sequences. While GraDe-IF introduced discrete diffusion for this task, it relies on a uniform prior that discards the rich structural information available in the backbone, leaving open whether a structure-aware informative prior could improve sample quality and accelerate inference.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241260/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a protein backbone structure as input, represented as 3D Cartesian coordinates of backbone atoms (N, Cα, C, optionally O) for each of n residues, and produces a full-length amino acid sequence over the 20 standard residue types. The structure is first passed through a frozen PiFold encoder to yield a deterministic prior sequence, which then serves as the starting point of the Markov bridge refinement process rather than a uniform noise distribution.

## Method

Bridge-IF frames inverse folding as a Markov bridge between a structure-derived deterministic prior (from PiFold) and the native sequence, replacing the noise-to-data paradigm with a data-to-data process that requires only 25 timesteps versus 500 for GraDe-IF. A reparameterization trick introduces a latent Bernoulli variable to indicate whether each residue has been 'transformed,' reducing the variational lower bound to a re-weighted cross-entropy loss computed only over tokens not yet matching the ground truth. The bridge process is approximated by a pre-trained protein language model (ESM) modulated with structural and timestep conditions via two novel modules: AdaLN-Bias, which predicts small offsets on frozen layer-norm parameters from pooled structure and timestep embeddings, and a bottleneck cross-attention adapter that injects per-residue structural features while preserving pre-trained weights. All PLM parameters remain frozen during training, making the approach parameter-efficient.

## Results

On CATH v4.2 (All proteins), Bridge-IF with ESM-2 650M achieves a perplexity of 3.83 and a median recovery rate of 58.59%, surpassing the previous best iterative method KW-Design (3.90 / 57.38%) and the diffusion-based GraDe-IF (4.35 / 52.21%). On CATH v4.3, Bridge-IF (ESM-1b 650M) reaches 3.68 perplexity and 58.93% recovery, outperforming LM-Design (4.01 / 56.63%). On the PDB-struct foldability benchmark, Bridge-IF attains the highest TM-score of 0.81 (vs. 0.80 for native sequences and 0.73 for LM-Design) with 54.08% recovery, and on de novo design it achieves scTM 0.73 and designability 0.85 versus ProteinMPNN's 0.69 and 0.80.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Bridge-IF outperforms all autoregressive baselines (StructGNN, GraphTrans, GCA, GVP, AlphaDesign, ESM-IF) and the one-shot PiFold by a wide margin on both perplexity and recovery rate across CATH v4.2 and v4.3. Against the closest iterative PLM-based competitors, Bridge-IF (ESM-2 650M) beats KW-Design by 1.21 points in recovery (58.59% vs. 57.38%) and LM-Design by 2.84 points (58.49% vs. 55.65%) on CATH v4.2, while using the same pre-trained PLM weights. Compared to the only other diffusion-based method GraDe-IF, Bridge-IF improves recovery by 6.38 points (58.59% vs. 52.21%) while using only 25 diffusion steps versus GraDe-IF's 500. On the PDB-struct foldability benchmark, Bridge-IF achieves the best TM-score (0.81) and recovery (54.08%), surpassing LM-Design (0.73 / 51.23%) and ProteinMPNN (0.80 / 41.44%). In de novo design with Chroma-generated backbones, Bridge-IF exceeds ProteinMPNN in both scTM (0.73 vs. 0.69) and designability (0.85 vs. 0.80). On multi-chain PDB complexes, Bridge-IF (61.26% recovery) outperforms LM-Design with ESM-2 (59.81%) and ProteinMPNN (50.00%).

## Relation to Prior Work

Bridge-IF extends the Markov bridge framework from retrosynthesis (Retrobridge, Igashov et al. 2024) to inverse protein folding, replacing the standard data-to-noise diffusion paradigm with a data-to-data bridge between a structure-informed deterministic prior (from PiFold) and native sequences. It draws on DiT's adaptive layer normalization (Peebles & Xie 2023) to inject timestep and structural conditions into pre-trained protein language models, and borrows the reparameterization trick from discrete diffusion (Austin et al. 2021) to derive a simplified weighted cross-entropy loss. Conceptually, it departs from GraDe-IF's uniform prior by arguing that backbone structures provide far more information than random noise, and from iterative refinement methods (LM-Design, KW-Design) by casting the refinement process in a probabilistic Markov bridge framework rather than a heuristic correction loop.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a structure-informed prior combined with a Markov bridge formulation can match or exceed discriminative and standard diffusion baselines while using 20× fewer inference steps (25 vs. 500), suggesting that conditional generative problems benefit from informative priors rather than default noise distributions. The finding that probabilistic models (Bridge-IF, ProteinMPNN) outperform discriminative models on foldability (TM-score) despite comparable recovery rates highlights a gap between sequence-level metrics and structural validity that the field should address. Key limitations include the absence of wet-lab validation and reliance on a frozen PiFold prior, leaving open whether end-to-end training of the structure encoder within the bridge framework could further improve results. Future directions include scaling with AlphaFold2-predicted structures and applying Bridge-IF to functional protein engineering.
