## Problem

Test-time optimization methods for HMR (e.g., EFT) fine-tune a pretrained regression network on each test sample, but the pretrained model is not specifically designed to be a good starting point for such per-sample adaptation. Additionally, training-time objectives (using ground-truth 3D SMPLs) differ from test-time objectives (using only 2D reprojection loss), creating conflicting gradient directions that reduce the effectiveness of the meta-learning process.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240371/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image of a person and 2D joint detections (from OpenPose or RSN) as input, and outputs 3D SMPL parameters (pose θ ∈ R^{24×3}, shape β ∈ R^{10}) together with camera parameters π. The 2D joints are consumed as supervision signals during the iterative test-time optimization phase, and the quality of these joints directly affects final accuracy (GT joints yield the best results). At inference, the auxiliary network is frozen and used solely to generate pseudo GT meshes that guide the optimization of the main network.

## Method

The paper proposes a MAML-style meta-learning training procedure where, for each sample in a batch, a gradient step on the test-time loss is first performed (inner loop), followed by the standard training optimization over the full batch (outer loop), yielding meta-parameters that are inherently friendly to test-time adaptation. To resolve the objective mismatch between training (2D+3D losses with GT SMPLs) and testing (2D loss only), a dual-network architecture is introduced: a main regression network f_w and an auxiliary network f_u (same architecture, different parameters) are trained jointly, where f_u generates pseudo GT SMPL meshes that are incorporated into the test-time loss, making both inner and outer loop objectives share the same 2D+3D form. At test time, f_u is frozen to produce pseudo GT meshes that supervise up to 14 iterative gradient steps on f_w, starting from the meta-parameters, with early stopping when consecutive losses converge. The regression backbone can be HMR (ResNet-50) or CLIFF (HRNet-W48), and the method outperforms both regression-based and optimization-based SOTA on 3DPW and Human3.6M.

## Results

On 3DPW, the proposed method (OursCLIFF with HRNet-W48 and RSN 2D joints) achieves 62.4 MPJPE, 39.5 PA-MPJPE, and 78.1 PVE, outperforming the strongest regression baseline ProPose (68.3 MPJPE) and the strongest optimization baseline ReFit (65.8 MPJPE) by 5.9 and 3.4 mm respectively. On Human3.6M, it reaches 42.0 MPJPE and 29.1 PA-MPJPE, improving over CLIFF (47.1 MPJPE) by 5.1 mm. With OpenPose joints instead of RSN, the method still achieves 62.9 MPJPE on 3DPW, maintaining a clear lead over all compared approaches.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all 20 compared baselines on both 3DPW and Human3.6M. Against the regression backbone CLIFF [33], it reduces 3DPW MPJPE from 69.0 to 62.9 (−6.1 mm) and Human3.6M MPJPE from 47.1 to 43.9 (−3.2 mm). Among optimization-based methods, it beats ReFit [59] (65.8→62.9 MPJPE on 3DPW), PLIKS [51] (66.9→62.9), NIKI [31] (71.3→62.9), and EFT [22] (85.1→62.9), with the largest margin over EFT reflecting the benefit of meta-learned initialization over naive fine-tuning. On Human3.6M, it surpasses ProPose [13] (45.7→43.9 MPJPE) and ReFit [59] (48.4→43.9). In the OOD setting (trained on COCO, tested on Human3.6M), it achieves 83.8 MPJPE vs. EFTCLIFF's 85.5, and on LSP-Extended it reduces 2D reprojection loss from 8.3e-3 to 6.1e-3, confirming superior out-of-domain generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the test-time optimization paradigm introduced by EFT (Joo et al., 3DV 2021) by reframing it through the lens of meta-learning (MAML, Finn et al., ICML 2017), so that the regression network's parameters are explicitly trained to be good starting points for per-sample adaptation. It departs from Kim et al. (ICIP 2022), the first to apply meta-learning to HMR, in two key ways: (1) incorporating 3D SMPL supervision (ground-truth in the outer loop, pseudo-labels in the inner loop) rather than relying solely on 2D reprojection loss, and (2) introducing a dual-network architecture where an auxiliary network generates pseudo 3D meshes to unify the training and test-time optimization objectives. The regression backbone (HMR or CLIFF) is treated as a plug-in component, and the contribution is orthogonal to backbone choice.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap: standard pretrained regression models are not ideal starting points for test-time fine-tuning, and the meta-learning formulation provides a principled way to obtain parameters that are specifically amenable to per-sample adaptation. The dual-network pseudo-label mechanism is a clean solution to the train/test objective mismatch, and the finding that EFT degrades with more optimization steps while the proposed method consistently improves highlights the importance of 3D regularization during adaptation. Limitations include the dependency on external 2D joint detectors (OpenPose/RSN) at test time, slower inference (~1.1s for 14 iterations vs. ~0.04s for single-pass methods), and the fact that the approach is validated only on single-person, single-image HMR. Open questions include whether the meta-learning + pseudo-label strategy transfers to video HMR, multi-person settings, or other test-time adaptation tasks beyond HMR.
