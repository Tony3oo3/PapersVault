## Problem

Despite extensive adversarial attack research on face recognition and single-modality person re-identification, the security of visible-infrared person re-identification (VIReID) systems remains unexplored. The cross-modal nature of VIReID introduces unique challenges absent in single-modality settings: adversarial features must remain consistent across fundamentally different imaging mechanisms (visible texture vs. infrared thermal amplitude), and ranking disruption must account for both within-modality and cross-modality distance relationships.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20244291/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes visible and infrared pedestrian images (resized to 288×144) as input, applies a single universal adversarial perturbation independently to each modality, and produces adversarial pedestrian images that cause VIReID systems to output incorrect cross-modal rankings. The attack is evaluated on standard VIReID benchmarks (SYSU-MM01 with all-search and indoor-search protocols, RegDB with visible-to-thermal and thermal-to-visible protocols) using Rank-1, Rank-10, mAP, and mINP metrics, with the goal of driving these metrics near zero rather than merely degrading them.

## Method

The proposed attack operates on a dual-stream ResNet-50 backbone in two stages. First, a Frequency-Spatial Attention Module (FSAM) applies FFT to decompose features into amplitude and phase components, then applies a spatial attention mechanism (max/avg pooling + 7×7 convolution + sigmoid) to the phase component to emphasize spatially discriminative regions, and reconstructs features via IFFT; this module is inserted after both block 0 and block 4 of ResNet-50 to capture frequency-spatial information at multiple depths. Second, a shared module with an additional spatial attention layer produces final features. The key training signal is an Auxiliary Quadruple Adversarial Loss (AQAL) that jointly uses the FSAM-stage features (as auxiliary) and the final-stage features to form quadruplets: it minimizes same-modality same-identity distance, maximizes cross-modality same-identity distance, and enforces that intra-class distance remains smaller than inter-class distance, thereby amplifying modality gaps and disrupting cross-modal ranking while preserving within-modality ordering.

## Results

On SYSU-MM01 (All-search), the proposed attack reduces the AGW baseline's Rank-1 from 47.50% to 0.79% and mAP from 47.65% to 2.81%. On RegDB, the mAP drops from 66.37% (Visible-to-Thermal) and 65.90% (Thermal-to-Visible) to 0.85% and 0.89% respectively, nearly approaching zero. The method also generalizes across four additional VIReID systems (HCLoss, CAJ, MMN, DEEN), reducing their mAP by over 90% in all test modes (e.g., DEEN's All-search mAP falls from 71.80% to 3.55%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all six baselines by a substantial margin on every metric and dataset. On SYSU-MM01 All-search, it reduces Rank-1 to 0.79%, compared to DMR (9.20%), LTA (15.47%), UAP (17.59%), SMA (21.72%), PGD (26.60%), and FGSM (36.02%). On RegDB Visible-to-Thermal, the mAP drops to 0.85%, versus DMR (4.80%), LTA (10.86%), SMA (17.37%), UAP (24.42%), PGD (26.87%), and FGSM (44.39%). The gap is most pronounced against gradient-based methods (FGSM, PGD), which retain 25–45% mAP, while the proposed feature-level attack with FSAM and the auxiliary quadruple adversarial loss drives performance to near-zero. The method also demonstrates cross-system generality, degrading four additional VIReID architectures (HCLoss, CAJ, MMN, DEEN) by over 90% in mAP, confirming that the attack is not specific to the AGW backbone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of adversarial attacks on person re-identification (DMR, LTA, SMA) from single-modality visible ReID to the cross-modal visible-infrared setting, where the key additional challenge is aligning adversarial features across fundamentally different imaging mechanisms. It repurposes Universal Adversarial Perturbation (UAP) as the perturbation generator and introduces a Frequency-Spatial Attention Module that draws on FFT-based amplitude/phase decomposition (previously explored for modality-invariant feature learning in VIReID) combined with spatial attention, applied at two stages of a dual-stream ResNet-50. The Auxiliary Quadruple Adversarial Loss generalizes quadruplet-style metric learning to an adversarial objective that explicitly pulls same-modality-same-identity features together while pushing cross-modality-same-identity features apart, a formulation not present in prior single-modality ReID attacks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first systematic security evaluation of VIReID systems, demonstrating that all four tested state-of-the-art methods (HCLoss, CAJ, MMN, DEEN) are catastrophically vulnerable, with mAP dropping to near zero on RegDB. However, the paper is limited to digital attacks on two datasets and four systems with a single backbone, and proposes no defense mechanism, leaving the practical question of how to harden VIReID systems against such attacks entirely open. The near-complete ranking disruption also raises the question of whether the attack generalizes to physical-world deployments (e.g., adversarial patches or clothing modifications), which the authors acknowledge but do not test.
