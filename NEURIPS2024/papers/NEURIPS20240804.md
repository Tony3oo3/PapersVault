## Problem

Pre-trained vision-language models like CLIP underperform on new concepts because raw images and bare class names provide insufficient information: the model attends to irrelevant background elements while class names fail to capture the rich visual attributes learned during pre-training. Existing adaptation methods rely on post-training prompts that require labeled training data, limiting their applicability in truly zero-shot or resource-constrained settings. The paper asks whether input augmentation alone—without any prompt training—can close this gap while also handling the non-trivial challenges of intra-modal view importance and cross-modal semantic correlation.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a single RGB image (or sampled video frames) and a set of candidate class names, then internally expands each into N=50 augmented image views (via random resized cropping and flipping) and M=50 LLM-generated textual descriptions per class. The output is a softmax classification probability over the candidate classes, computed from the optimal transport cost between the weighted image-view distribution and each class's weighted description distribution. For few-shot transfer, a lightweight multimodal adapter is inserted into every transformer layer and trained on 1–16 labeled examples per class, with a distillation loss to prevent overfitting.

## Method

AWT is a three-stage, training-free pipeline. First, it augments the raw image with standard geometric transforms and generates diverse class descriptions via a two-step, dataset-aware LLM prompting strategy (first generating dataset-specific questions, then combining them with class names). Second, it assigns each augmented view an importance weight via an entropy-based mechanism: views that yield lower prediction entropy (higher confidence) receive greater mass, computed with a softmax over negative entropies with a temperature parameter (γ=1/2). Third, it formulates the image-to-class distance as a Kantorovich optimal transport problem where each view is a Dirac mass at its CLIP embedding position, the mass is the entropy-derived weight, and the transport cost is cosine distance; the solution is approximated via Sinkhorn's algorithm (ε=0.1). The final classification score for each class is the negative total transport cost, passed through a softmax. This OT formulation explicitly captures cross-modal correlations (e.g., a crop of a cat's eyes aligning with a description mentioning eyes) that simple embedding averaging would miss.

## Results

On zero-shot image classification across 14 datasets, AWT achieves 69.59% average top-1 accuracy without any additional training, outperforming the best training-based baseline (Self-TPT-v, 68.31%) by 1.28% and the best training-free baseline (SuS-X-SD, 67.54%) by 2.05%. On out-of-distribution generalization (ImageNet-A/V2/R/Sketch), AWT reaches 64.43% average, surpassing the previous best (TPT, 60.81%) by 3.62%. For zero-shot video action recognition, AWT sets new records: 85.4% on UCF101 (EP1), 56.1% on HMDB51 (EP1), and 76.1% on Kinetics-600, exceeding FROSTER by 0.4%, 1.6%, and 1.3% respectively. In few-shot settings (1–16 shots on 11 datasets), AWT surpasses the prior SOTA by 2.76%, 2.16%, 1.62%, 1.57%, and 1.75% average accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AWT is compared against 30 baselines spanning three categories: prompt learning (CoOp, CoCoOp, MaPLe, PLOT++, POMP, ProVP-Ref), test-time prompt tuning (TPT, DiffTPT, PromptAlign, Self-TPT-v), and augmentation-based methods (CuPL, VisDesc, WaffleCLIP, SuS-X-SD). On zero-shot image classification, AWT (69.59% avg) outperforms all 15 image baselines, with the largest margins over prompt-learning methods like PLOT++ (63.11%, +6.48%) and CoOp (63.42%, +6.17%), and a more modest but consistent lead over the strongest training-free competitor SuS-X-SD (67.54%, +2.05%) and the best training-based method Self-TPT-v (68.31%, +1.28%). On OOD generalization, AWT (64.43%) leads TPT (60.81%) by 3.62% and DiffTPT (60.65%) by 3.78%. For zero-shot video action recognition, AWT surpasses FROSTER (the prior SOTA) on all three datasets: UCF101 85.4% vs 85.0%, HMDB51 56.1% vs 54.5%, and Kinetics-600 76.1% vs 74.8%. In few-shot learning (16-shot, 11-dataset average), AWT (84.62%) beats ProVP-Ref (83.07%) by 1.55% and PromptSRC (82.87%) by 1.75%. Notably, AWT is the only method that requires no additional training yet outperforms all training-based approaches on the majority of datasets, though Self-TPT-v retains a slight edge on ImageNet-1k specifically (72.96% vs 71.32%).

## Relation to Prior Work

AWT carves new ground at the intersection of two previously separate lines of work: input augmentation via LLMs/diffusion models (CuPL, VisDesc, WaffleCLIP, SuS-X-SD) and optimal transport for VLM adaptation (PLOT, Wang et al.). While prior augmentation methods enhance only one modality and prior OT-based methods require trained prompts, AWT is the first to simultaneously augment both visual and textual inputs, dynamically weight them via prediction entropy, and use OT to capture cross-modal semantic correlations—all without any training. The paper explicitly positions itself as departing from PLOT and Wang et al. by 'eschewing the need for additional training resources, opting instead for an augmentation-based direction.'

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is substantial: AWT provides a training-free recipe that consistently outperforms all trained prompt-learning and test-time-tuning methods across 21 datasets and four task settings, making it immediately deployable in scenarios where labeled data or GPU training budgets are unavailable. The framework's versatility across VLMs (CLIP, ALIGN, SigLIP, EVA-CLIP), architectures (ResNet, ViT), and scales (ViT-B/32 to ViT-L/14) suggests broad applicability. Limitations include the computational cost of processing 50+ augmented views at inference, the dependency on LLM quality for description generation, and failure on very low-resolution images (CIFAR) where standard augmentations degrade quality. Open questions include whether the approach extends to dense prediction tasks (segmentation, detection) and whether the LLM dependency can be replaced by lighter-weight models without performance loss.
