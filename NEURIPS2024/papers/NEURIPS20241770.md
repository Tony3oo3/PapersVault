## Problem

Diffusion models have proven effective at learning realistic multimodal agent distributions, but applying them to closed-loop driving simulation faces three unresolved challenges: compounding distributional drift from iterative replanning, prohibitive inference cost (16 denoising steps per physical step), and limited controllability for generating targeted long-tail scenarios. No prior work jointly addresses scene initialization (generating or editing initial traffic layouts) and closed-loop rollout within a single unified generative framework.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a scene tensor x ∈ R^{A×T×D} (up to 128 agents, 91 timesteps at 10 Hz, 8 feature dimensions) along with global context (vectorized roadgraph polylines and traffic signals) encoded via a Perceiver IO encoder. All tasks—behavior prediction, scene generation, and controllable editing—are unified as inpainting: a binary mask specifies which (agent, timestep, feature) entries are given versus to-be-generated, and pre-filled values in the context tensor serve as hard constraints. The output is the full scene tensor with normalized position (x,y,z), heading, bounding box dimensions (l,h,w), and one-hot object type, which is inverse-normalized to recover physical units.

## Method

SceneDiffuser is a spatiotemporal diffusion model with a Perceiver IO global context encoder and a transformer denoiser backbone conditioned via Adaptive LayerNorm (AdaLN-Zero), trained end-to-end with a v-prediction loss on a mixture of uniform and monotonic noise schedules. The key innovation is Amortized Diffusion for rollout: instead of running 16 denoising steps at each of 80 physical timesteps (Full AR), the model performs a one-shot warm-up to initialize a future buffer, then applies exactly one denoising step per physical timestep while carrying over and refining prior predictions, reducing inference calls from 1280 to 96 (16×) while simultaneously reducing closed-loop error accumulation. Controllability is achieved through Generalized Hard Constraints (GHC), which iteratively apply a clipping operator (e.g., non-collision potential field minimization, range clamping, on-road enforcement) at every denoising step rather than only post-hoc, and through a few-shot prompted LLM that converts natural language scenario descriptions into structured inpainting constraint protos.

## Results

On the Waymo Open Sim Agents Challenge (WOSAC) test split, SceneDiffuser achieves a top open-loop composite metric of 0.736 (One-Shot, 0.125 Hz) and the best closed-loop composite metric of 0.703 among diffusion models (Amortized AR at 10 Hz). The Amortized AR rollout requires only 96 function evaluations per 80-step simulation (vs. 1280 for Full AR), a 16× reduction, while achieving a collision rate of 0.064 and offroad rate of 0.177. For scene generation, the M/1 model with hard constraints reaches a composite realism score of 0.558, approaching the logged oracle of 0.593.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SceneDiffuser (One-Shot) achieves the highest composite metric (0.736) in the WOSAC table, narrowly edging out Trajeglish (0.735) and MVTE (0.732), while also attaining the best MinADE (1.103) among all methods. In the closed-loop 10 Hz setting, Ours (Amortized) achieves a composite of 0.703 with the lowest collision rate (0.064) among 10 Hz methods, outperforming MVTE (0.732 composite but 0.090 collision) and Trajeglish (0.735 composite but 0.076 collision) on collision safety. Among diffusion-based methods specifically, SceneDiffuser dominates: VBD (0.720 composite) and SceneDM (0.628 composite) both operate at 0.125 Hz (effectively open-loop), while SceneDiffuser is the only diffusion model evaluated at a true 10 Hz closed-loop replan rate. The Amortized AR approach also requires 16× fewer model inference calls than Full AR (96 vs. 1280 FN evaluations per 80-step rollout), making it the most efficient closed-loop diffusion method reported. Against the non-diffusion baselines MTR+++ (2 Hz) and MVTE/Trajeglish (10 Hz), SceneDiffuser trades a small composite metric gap (0.703 vs. 0.732–0.735) for substantially lower collision rates and the ability to jointly model agent size and type.

## Relation to Prior Work

SceneDiffuser unifies two previously separate sub-problems in driving simulation—scene initialization (generation/editing) and closed-loop rollout—into a single spatiotemporal diffusion model trained end-to-end on logged scenes. Its core technical contribution, amortized diffusion, adapts the temporally-entangled diffusion idea from TEDi (human animation) to multi-agent driving simulation, but is the first to show it reduces closed-loop drift rather than merely cutting compute. The architecture draws on Perceiver IO for variable-length scene encoding, DPM++ for efficient sampling, and AdaLN conditioning, while the generalized hard constraints mechanism extends dynamic thresholding from image generation to structured trajectory data. Controllability via LLM few-shot prompting for constraint protos is a novel application layer on top of the inpainting-based diffusion framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 16× reduction in inference cost per rollout step makes diffusion-based closed-loop simulation practically viable at 10 Hz, addressing a key bottleneck that previously forced methods like VBD to fall back to open-loop evaluation. The finding that amortized (less frequent) replanning produces more realistic trajectories than full replanning challenges the common assumption that higher replan frequency is always better, and could influence how other iterative generative simulators are designed. Limitations include not exceeding non-diffusion autoregressive SOTA on WOSAC, reliance on logged validity masks rather than modeling them, and evaluation on a single dataset. Open questions include whether amortized diffusion generalizes to longer horizons, different agent counts, or other simulation domains, and whether the LLM-based constraint generation can scale beyond the 40% success rate reported.
