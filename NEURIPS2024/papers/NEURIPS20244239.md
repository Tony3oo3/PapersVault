## Problem

Despite LLMs showing growing capability in reasoning and planning, no benchmark exists for evaluating them in real-time strategic decision-making with long-term planning under time pressure. StarCraft II, with its asymmetric races, fog of war, and multi-layered strategy (economy, tech, military), is an ideal but previously inaccessible testbed because existing SC2 environments lack natural language interfaces for LLM agents.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20244239/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The agent receives structured text observations covering six categories (resources, units, buildings, in-process activities, enemy status, research progress) generated by the python-sc2 framework, along with a system prompt encoding race-specific strategy guidelines. The LLM outputs a structured analysis (situation overview, stage identification, strategy inference, suggestions) followed by K discrete macro actions (e.g., <BUILD GATEWAY>, <TRAIN PROBE>, <RESEARCH WARPGATERESEARCH>) extracted via regex from the CoT reasoning text. Micro-level actions (unit targeting, positioning) are handled by predefined rule-based scripts rather than the LLM, and the reward is binary {-1, 0, 1} for loss/draw/win.

## Method

The Chain of Summarization (CoS) method addresses the latency mismatch between fast-paced SC2 and slow LLM inference by batching K game frames before querying the model, analogous to hardware cache mechanisms and RL frame skipping. It operates in three stages: (1) Single-Frame Summarization compresses each raw observation into a condensed form via rule-based extraction or few-shot LLM summarization; (2) Multi-Frame Summarization aggregates K condensed frames into a single period summary σ, on which a full Chain-of-Thought reasoning pass is applied to produce a strategic analysis v; (3) an Action Extractor uses regex matching and similarity search to pull K executable actions from v and queue them for sequential execution. This design means the LLM is invoked once every K steps rather than every step, enabling real-time interaction (64–102 ms/step for fine-tuned 1.8B–7B models) while preserving strategic depth. The TextStarCraft II environment itself translates SC2's full game state into text via python-sc2, with the LLM controlling macro-strategy and rule-based scripts handling micro-strategy, supporting 10 difficulty levels and human-AI matches.

## Results

In TextStarCraft II against the built-in LV5 AI, GPT-4 Turbo achieves the highest win rate (12/20, 60%) with the lowest Population Block Ratio (PBR = 0.0337), followed by GPT-3.5 Turbo (11/20, 55%). Among fine-tuned open-source models, Qwen 7B (9/20) and Qwen 1.8B (8/20) outperform ChatGLM3 6B (3/20) and Llama2 7B (1/20). In human-AI matches, the fine-tuned Qwen 1.8B (running on 4 GB GPU) achieves a 5/10 record against a Gold-level player (MMR 2556), while losing 0/10 to Grandmaster and Pro players and winning 10/10 against a novice.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among closed-source LLMs evaluated with the full CoS pipeline, GPT-4 Turbo (12/20 wins) narrowly outperforms GPT-3.5 Turbo (11/20), while Gemini-Pro (5/20), GLM4 (4/20), and Claude 2.1 (3/20) trail significantly, indicating a substantial performance gap between top-tier and mid-tier commercial models. Among fine-tuned open-source models (using CoS without CoT due to computational constraints), Qwen 7B (9/20) and Qwen 1.8B (8/20) substantially outperform ChatGLM3 6B (3/20) and Llama2 7B (1/20), with the smaller Qwen 1.8B nearly matching the larger Qwen 7B. The paper also qualitatively contrasts the LLM agent with AlphaStar, showing that while AlphaStar excels at micromanagement, the LLM agent demonstrates superior strategic interpretability—proactively building defensive structures (e.g., Shield Batteries) and adapting unit compositions to counter enemy strategies, whereas AlphaStar repeatedly fails to transition unit compositions in response to opponent changes. In the data-quality ablation, fine-tuning on only the top-25% APU wins yields a 54/100 win rate versus 28/100 on the full dataset, confirming that high-quality training data is critical for fine-tuned model performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Chain-of-Thought prompting paradigm [27] into the domain of real-time strategy gaming by introducing Chain of Summarization (CoS), which compresses multi-frame observations and batches actions to bridge the latency gap between LLM inference and fast-paced gameplay. It carves new ground at the intersection of two well-developed lines of work: SC2 AI (AlphaStar, ROA-Star, Mini-AlphaStar) and LLM agent benchmarks (AGENTBENCH, Voyager, ReAct), explicitly noting that no prior benchmark evaluates LLMs in real-time strategic decision-making. The text-based environment (TextStarCraft II) repurposes the PySC2 framework to make SC2 accessible to language models, while rule-based micro-actions follow the OpenAI Five approach, deliberately separating macro-strategy (LLM-controlled) from micro-execution (scripted).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first dedicated benchmark for evaluating LLMs in real-time strategic decision-making, filling a gap the authors identify between general LLM agent benchmarks and the demands of fast-paced, multi-faceted strategy games. The finding that a 1.8B fine-tuned model can compete with Gold-level human players at 64ms/step latency is practically significant for deploying LLM agents on consumer hardware. Key limitations include the reliance on rule-based micro-actions, text-only observations (no visual input), and evaluation primarily against built-in AI rather than RL-trained opponents like AlphaStar. Open questions include whether CoS-style summarization generalizes to other real-time domains, how performance scales with model size beyond the tested range, and whether the interpretability advantage over RL agents translates into measurable strategic superiority against stronger opponents.
