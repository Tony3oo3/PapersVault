## Problem

Foundation models are lightly finetuned from heavily pretrained weights, which may reduce the ensemble diversity needed to observe agreement-on-the-line (AGL), a phenomenon that enables OOD performance estimation without labels. It is unclear whether AGL holds for finetuned FMs and, if not, what training choices can induce the necessary diversity. This paper systematically investigates which sources of randomness during finetuning reliably produce AGL across vision and language benchmarks.

## Input / Output

The method requires an ensemble of at least three finetuned foundation models (constructed via random linear head initialization and/or multiple base models), a labeled in-distribution validation set for computing ID accuracy and agreement, and unlabeled OOD samples for computing OOD agreement. No OOD labels are needed. The output is a point estimate of each ensemble member's OOD performance (accuracy, F1, or exact match) obtained by linearly transforming its ID performance using the slope and intercept of the agreement line, with a probit transform applied to improve linearity.

## Method

The paper demonstrates that among three diversity sources—random linear head initialization, data ordering, and data subsetting—only random head initialization reliably induces AGL in lightly finetuned FMs, while the other two produce agreement trends with slopes too close to the diagonal y=x line. For language tasks, ensembles spanning multiple base models (OPT, GPT2, Llama2, Alpaca, Vicuna) pretrained on different corpora also observe AGL without additional regularization. The estimation uses ALine-S (least-squares fit of the agreement line) or ALine-D (solving a linear system over all model pairs) to recover the slope and intercept, then maps each model's ID performance to an OOD estimate. The approach is validated across CLIP linear probing for image classification and full finetuning/LoRA for question-answering and text classification, outperforming confidence-based baselines (ATC, AC, DOC-Feat) by up to 20% MAPE.

## Results

Using AGL-based ALine-D with a diverse ensemble (random head initialization + multiple base models), the paper achieves MAPE as low as 0.55% on MNLI-Mismatched, 1.95% on CIFAR-10.1v6, and 3.61% on SQuAD-Shifts (Reddit/Amazon). Across all 16 evaluated OOD shifts with strong agreement correlation (R² > 0.95), ALine-D and ALine-S consistently outperform all confidence-based baselines, with the largest margin reaching ~20 percentage points on SQuAD-Shifts. The method works across vision (CIFAR-10C, ImageNetC, OfficeHome, FMoW) and language (SQuAD-Shifts, MNLI, SNLI) benchmarks, and across metrics (accuracy, F1, exact match).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares AGL-based ALine-S/D against four baselines: Naive Agreement (directly using pairwise agreement as OOD estimate), ATC (temperature-scaled confidence), AC (accuracy calibration), and DOC-Feat (document-feature-based confidence). ALine-D wins on 13 of 16 datasets, with the most dramatic margins on SQuAD-Shifts where it achieves 3.61% MAPE versus 19.06% (ATC) and 26.56% (Naive Agr) on Reddit, and 3.61% versus 24.35% (ATC) on Amazon. On vision tasks, ALine-D leads on CIFAR-10C (5.44% vs 6.90% ATC), CIFAR-100C (7.17% vs 8.18% ATC), and all OfficeHome splits (e.g., 14.64% vs 84.18% ATC on Product). The one notable exception is ImageNetV2 MatchFreq, where ATC achieves 3.02% MAPE versus ALine-D's 8.44%, and on MNLI-Mismatched where all methods perform well (0.41–12.00%). For failure cases with low agreement R² (≤0.95, e.g., Camelyon17-WILDS, iWildCam-WILDS, some CIFAR-10C corruptions), ATC generally outperforms ALine-D, confirming that AGL-based methods require strong linear correlation to be reliable.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Agreement-on-the-Line (AGL) framework of Baek et al. (2022), which was demonstrated for ensembles of neural networks trained from scratch over hundreds of epochs, to the regime of lightly finetuned foundation models (linear probing or a few epochs of full finetuning). It builds on the Accuracy-on-the-Line (ACL) phenomenon of Miller et al. (2021) and departs from prior work in two key ways: (1) it shows that AGL is not strictly a neural-network-specific phenomenon, as linear probes over CLIP features can exhibit AGL with random initialization, contradicting Baek et al.'s negative result on linear models over flattened CIFAR10; and (2) it identifies random head initialization as the critical diversity source, in contrast to the Generalization Disagreement Equality literature (Jiang et al., 2022) which found data subsetting to produce the most ID diversity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This provides a practical, label-free method for estimating OOD performance of foundation models across vision and language tasks, which is critical for safe deployment in settings where OOD labels are expensive or unavailable. The finding that random head initialization alone suffices to induce AGL is a simple, actionable recipe for practitioners constructing ensembles. However, the method has clear limitations: it fails when the agreement linear correlation is weak (e.g., Camelyon-WILDS, iWildCam-WILDS), it requires finetuning (zero-shot FMs do not exhibit AGL), and no theoretical guarantees are provided for why random initialization is the critical diversity source. Open questions include whether the approach extends to long-form generation tasks and whether the homogeneity of language model accuracy lines (all LLMs on the same line) reflects a genuine property of text pretraining or an artifact of the specific models studied.
