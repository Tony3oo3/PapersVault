## Problem

In non-generative ML settings (tabular regression, categorical prediction), detecting and correcting misalignment of a pre-trained model with user-desired properties (e.g., monotonicity, concavity) is difficult because single-sample outputs cannot be interpreted by humans, and enforcing alignment during training requires repeating costly training runs. Existing alignment methods like RLHF are limited to generative settings where humans can provide feedback on outputs. The paper also shows that simply scaling model size or training data cannot eliminate the need for alignment if the training data contains even small systematic biases.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pre-trained model f (e.g., XGBoost), a calibration dataset {(X_i, Y_i)} of size n, a property specification P encoded as a proximity-oblivious tester T, and a risk parameter α. It produces asymmetric prediction intervals F_λ̂(X) = [f(X) − λ̂⁻, f(X) + λ̂⁺] around each point prediction, where the two-sided parameter vector λ̂ = (λ̂⁺, λ̂⁻) is tuned via conformal risk control. The intervals are guaranteed to contain a function satisfying P on at least 1 − α fraction of the domain with high probability, and the asymmetry allows separate control of over- and under-prediction.

## Method

The core mechanism converts proximity-oblivious tester (POT) queries for a property P into binary loss functions L_i ∈ {0,1} that feed a multi-dimensional conformal risk control procedure. Specifically, a k-dimensional parameter vector λ ∈ R^k_{≥0} parameterizes the width of prediction intervals around f(X), and conformal risk control selects λ̂ as the minimal element satisfying (n/(n+1))R̂_n(λ) + B/(n+1) ≤ α, where B is the loss bound. The key theoretical result (Theorem 1) proves that Pr[F_λ̂ is ε-Faraway from P] ≤ α/ρ(ε), where ρ(ε) is the POT's detection probability, yielding a distribution-free alignment guarantee without retraining. When ρ(ε) ≤ α, the detection probability is amplified by running k independent POT calls and taking a logical OR, at the cost of reducing the effective calibration sample size to ⌊n/k⌋. The approach is demonstrated on monotonicity and concavity properties over UCI tabular datasets using XGBoost, and a stylized random-feature-model analysis shows that overparameterized models with biased training labels will always fail to satisfy P regardless of data size.

## Results

On the Combined Cycle Power Plant dataset (n=9,568), the conformal risk control method (Adjusted) achieves risk values of 0.10, 0.05, and 0.01 for target α = 0.1, 0.05, and 0.01 respectively, closely matching the theoretical guarantee. The Adjusted model outperforms the oracle Constrained model in MSE across all α levels (e.g., 10.47 vs. 16.21 at α=0.1), while the Unconstrained baseline has a risk of 0.75. The conformal intervals widen as α decreases (λ⁺ grows from 0.51 to 2.39, λ⁻ from 0.76 to 3.33), reflecting the trade-off between alignment tightness and interval width.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three configurations: the Unconstrained XGBoost model (no monotonicity constraint), the Adjusted model (conformal risk control post-processing, the paper's contribution), and the Constrained model (trained with monotonicity constraint on all pre-test data, serving as an oracle benchmark). The Adjusted model dramatically reduces the monotonicity violation risk from 0.75 (Unconstrained) to the target α level (0.10, 0.05, or 0.01), while the Constrained oracle achieves zero risk by construction. Notably, the Adjusted model achieves lower MSE than the Constrained oracle at every α level (e.g., 10.47 vs. 16.21 at α=0.1), meaning the conformal post-processing not only enforces the desired property but also preserves better predictive accuracy than retraining with the constraint. The Constrained model's higher MSE reflects the cost of enforcing monotonicity during training, whereas the conformal approach only widens prediction intervals without altering the underlying model. The risk values for the Adjusted model match the theoretical guarantee from Theorem 1 almost exactly, validating the conformal risk control framework.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously separate literatures: property testing (Goldreich, 2017; Ron, 2008) and conformal risk control (Angelopoulos et al., 2024). The key conceptual move is mapping proximity-oblivious tester (POT) queries for a shape property into loss functions that feed a conformal risk control procedure, yielding distribution-free guarantees that the resulting prediction interval contains a function close to the desired property. The multi-dimensional lambda extension of conformal risk control is noted by the authors as following directly from Angelopoulos et al. (2024), and the connection to Yadkori et al. (2024), who apply conformal risk control to LLM hallucination, is acknowledged. The paper departs from the dominant alignment literature (RLHF, reward modeling) by targeting non-generative, tabular regression models where human feedback on outputs is infeasible, and from prior shape-constraint work (monotonic networks, isotonic regression) by operating post-hoc on a frozen pre-trained model rather than during training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a practical, distribution-free mechanism to align pre-trained models with user-desired shape properties without retraining, which is valuable in safety-critical or fairness-sensitive deployments where retraining is costly or infeasible. The theoretical result that biased labels in training data prevent random feature models from satisfying a property P regardless of data size or parameter count challenges the common assumption that scale alone resolves alignment, though it is established in a stylized polynomial setting with specific degree conditions. Limitations include empirical validation on a single dataset (Combined Cycle Power Plant) in the main text, restriction to monotonicity and concavity properties, and conformal intervals that can be quite wide (e.g., λ⁻ up to 3.33 at α=0.01). Open questions include sample complexity of the POT-based loss, extension to multi-dimensional shape constraints, and application to policy functions in MDP/RL settings.
