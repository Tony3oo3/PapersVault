## Problem

Existing pre-trained time series forecasting models (TimesFM, Moirai, Chronos, Lag-Llama) require hundreds of millions to billions of parameters, demand extensive GPU resources, and largely ignore cross-channel correlations and exogenous signals that are critical in practical multivariate forecasting. This paper asks whether 'tiny' pre-trained models (starting from 1M parameters) can match or exceed the zero/few-shot forecasting accuracy of these large models while running on CPU-only machines.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242358/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a multivariate time series context window (length 512–1536 depending on variant) with c channels, which is per-instance normalized and split into non-overlapping patches. Optionally, known future values of exogenous channels are fed into a separate exogenous mixer block. The output is a forecast of length 96–720 for the target channels (c' ≤ c), produced by a linear head after the decoder. The backbone operates channel-independently (each channel processed separately), while the decoder and exogenous mixer handle cross-channel interactions during fine-tuning.

## Method

TTM builds on the TSMixer architecture (MLP-Mixer blocks interleaved with gated attention, avoiding quadratic self-attention) and introduces three key enhancements for multi-resolution pre-training with minimal capacity: (1) Adaptive Patching, where different backbone levels operate at different patch lengths (halving patches and doubling dimensions per level, analogous to Swin Transformer's hierarchical design); (2) Diverse Resolution Sampling, which augments high-resolution datasets via averaging or decimation to balance coverage across resolutions; and (3) Resolution Prefix Tuning, which prepends a learnable resolution-specific embedding to the first patch for explicit resolution conditioning. The model uses a multi-level design: a frozen channel-independent backbone pre-trained in univariate fashion with a direct forecasting MSE objective on ~1B samples from Monash and LibCity, plus a lightweight decoder (10–20% of backbone size) that is fine-tuned with channel mixing to capture cross-channel correlations, and an optional exogenous mixer that patches the forecast with overlapped windows (stride=1), substitutes true exogenous future values, and passes the result through a TSMixer block with channel mixing to learn lagged target-exogenous dependencies.

## Results

TTM (starting from 1M parameters) achieves zero-shot MSE of 0.394 on ETTH1, 0.345 on ETTH2, 0.386 on ETTM1, 0.281 on ETTM2, 0.237 on Weather, and 0.205 on Electricity (averaged across FL ∈ {96,192,336,720}), outperforming Moirai-S (14M) by 6%, Moirai-L (311M) by 4%, and TimesFM (200M) by 15%. In few-shot 5% fine-tuning, TTM_B surpasses GPT4TS (84M) by 15% and Time-LLM (7B) by 10%. Computationally, TTM_B requires only 4.7 ms GPU inference per batch and 0.01 s CPU time, compared to 1395 ms and 2340 s for Chronos-B (298× and 239K× faster respectively).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TTM consistently outperforms all major TS pre-trained baselines in zero-shot forecasting: TTM_B (1M) beats Moirai-S/B/L by 6%/1%/4% and TimesFM by 15%, while TTM_A (5M) improves further to 10%/4%/9% and 19% respectively. Against Chronos variants (8M–709M), TTM_B achieves 17–32% MSE reduction, and against Lag-Llama (3M) it improves by 40%. In few-shot 5% fine-tuning, TTM_B surpasses GPT4TS (84M) by 15% and Time-LLM (7B) by 10%, and also outperforms from-scratch architectures (PatchTST, TSMixer, TimeMixer, iTransformer, TimesNet, DLinear) by 15–40%. In full-shot head probing, TTM outperforms Moment (348M) by 3–4% and GPT4TS by 4–6%. For cross-channel and exogenous modeling on D2 datasets, TTM_Q-CM outperforms TSMixer-CM by 15–44% and GPT4TS by 18%, demonstrating the effectiveness of the exogenous mixer and decoder channel-mixing modules.

## Relation to Prior Work

TTM builds directly on the TSMixer architecture (Ekambaram et al., KDD 2023), an MLP-Mixer-based model that replaced quadratic self-attention with gated attention and mixing blocks for efficient TS forecasting. It departs from the dominant 'large pre-trained model' paradigm (TimesFM, Moirai, Chronos, Lag-Llama, Moment) that relies on hundreds of millions to billions of parameters, instead demonstrating that 1–5M parameter models can match or exceed them. Architecturally, TTM adapts the adaptive patching concept from Swin Transformers to the TS domain and borrows prefix-tuning from NLP for resolution conditioning, while its multi-level design (channel-independent pre-training followed by channel-mixing fine-tuning) is a novel decomposition not seen in prior TS foundation models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper challenges the prevailing assumption that TS foundation models must be large to generalize well, showing that resolution diversity in pre-training data (via DRS) matters more than raw data volume, and that a 1M-parameter model can outperform 200M–700M-parameter counterparts by 4–40% in zero-shot MSE. Practically, TTM's CPU-only inference (0.01s per batch vs. 2340s for Chronos) and 24–30 hour pre-training on 6 A100s dramatically lower the barrier to deploying pre-trained TS forecasters in resource-constrained industrial settings. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to probabilistic forecasting, longer horizons, or non-forecasting downstream tasks, and whether the exogenous-mixer design scales to very high-dimensional exogenous inputs.
