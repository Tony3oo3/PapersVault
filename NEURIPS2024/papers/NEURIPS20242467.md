## Problem

Interpretable deep learning methods like Concept Bottleneck Models (CBMs) cannot handle relational problems because they process only one entity at a time, while relational models like GNNs solve multi-entity tasks but offer no concept-based explanations. This paper bridges the gap by defining a unified framework where both concepts and tasks may depend on multiple entities, recovering standard CBMs and GNNs as special cases.

## Input / Output

The model consumes entity feature vectors (e.g., image embeddings or KGE vectors) alongside a relational structure specified as a set of templetized hyperedges over a first-order logic language, where each hyperedge defines a dependency from source ground atoms to a destination atom. Outputs include per-atom concept scores, a final task prediction obtained by max-aggregating predictions across all groundings of the bottleneck variables (corresponding to existential quantification), and—when using the R-DCR variant—learned first-order logic rules with universal and existential quantifiers that explain the prediction.

## Method

R-CBMs define a relational concept bottleneck as a positional directed hypergraph over the Herbrand base, where templetized hyperedges (containing variables) generate multiple ground hyperedges via variable substitution, and all instances of the same template share the same update and readout functions. The pipeline proceeds in three stages: an atom encoder maps entity features to concept embeddings, a message-passing loop over the dependency graph refines both embeddings and prediction scores at each step, and a max-aggregation operator combines predictions from all neighbourhoods of a destination atom. The key design choice is that the aggregation operator (max) gives the model a sound logical interpretation: the final prediction is true if the task predictor fires for at least one grounding of the extra variables, yielding explanations of the form ∀v̄ ∃ū φ(b(v̄,ū)) → p(v̄). Training is end-to-end SGD with cross-entropy loss, and the R-DCR variant replaces the black-box readout with a neural module that generates a symbolic logic rule executed on concept scores, providing fully interpretable predictions.

## Results

On the relational benchmarks, R-CBMs match or exceed relational black-box GNNs: R-CBM-Deep achieves 100% ROC-AUC on both RPS and Hanoi (vs. 100% and 98.77% for the GNN), 78.42% accuracy on Cora (vs. 76.66%), and 92.75% MRR on Countries S1 (vs. 91.56%). Standard CBMs fail catastrophically on relational tasks, scoring only ~55% ROC-AUC (near random) on RPS and Hanoi. On knowledge-graph embedding, R-CBM-Emb achieves 0.49 MRR on WN18RR and 0.35 MRR on FB15k-237, matching or exceeding the best KGE and logic-based baselines. R-DCR additionally generates interpretable first-order-logic rules while maintaining these performance levels.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Standard CBMs (CBM-Linear, CBM-Deep, DCR from Koh et al. 2020 and Barbiero et al. 2023) fail on relational tasks, scoring only 51–64% ROC-AUC on RPS/Hanoi (near the 50% random baseline), confirming their architectural inability to process multiple entities. R-CBMs dramatically outperform these non-relational baselines, with R-CBM-Deep reaching 100% on both synthetic benchmarks. Against the relational GNN black-box (Scarselli et al. 2008), R-CBMs match within ~1% on most datasets and exceed it on Cora (78.42% vs. 76.66%) and Countries S1 (92.75% vs. 91.56%), while R-DCR achieves 98.33% MRR on Countries S1. On KGE tasks, R-CBM-Emb outperforms DistMult (Yang et al. 2014) by 7 MRR points on WN18RR (0.49 vs. 0.42) and 11 points on FB15k-237 (0.35 vs. 0.24), and matches or exceeds logic-based methods RNNLogic (Qu et al. 2020) and RLogic (Cheng et al. 2022). R-DCR matches DeepStochLog (Winters et al. 2022), which is provided with ground-truth rules, while remaining fully end-to-end trained and generating its own interpretable rules.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate research threads—Concept Bottleneck Models (CBMs) for interpretable prediction and Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) for relational reasoning—into a single architectural family called R-CBMs, where both standard CBMs and message-passing GNNs emerge as special cases. The key conceptual move is the templetized hyperedge over an atom dependency graph, which generalizes the CBM bottleneck to handle multi-entity concepts and tasks, and extends GNN message-passing to simultaneously update both latent embeddings and interpretable prediction scores. The R-DCR variant further builds on Deep Concept Reasoners (Barbiero et al., 2023) to generate first-order-logic rules as explanations, connecting the framework to neuro-symbolic AI and inductive logic programming.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

R-CBMs close a genuine gap in the literature: prior interpretable models (CBMs) cannot process multiple entities, while prior relational models (GNNs, KGEs) lack concept-level explanations. The framework's generality—subsuming CBMs, GNNs, and DCRs as special cases—makes it a useful unifying lens for future work on interpretable relational learning. Key limitations acknowledged by the authors include scalability to very large domains (e.g., large knowledge graphs) and the need for manually specified relational templates, which restricts the search space; automatic template discovery and reduced variable-substitution heuristics are identified as natural next steps. The finding that interpretability (via R-DCR) does not materially sacrifice accuracy over black-box relational models is practically important for deployment in safety-critical relational settings.
