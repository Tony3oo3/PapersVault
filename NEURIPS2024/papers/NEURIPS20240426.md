## Problem

Existing LLM merging and mixture methods are typically validated on small, homogeneous model collections and assume all candidate models are beneficial to include. In real-world scenarios with a large, heterogeneous model zoo—spanning different architectures, initializations, and training paradigms—naive aggregation can cause severe performance degradation, and no comprehensive benchmark or systematic guideline exists for selecting and combining such diverse models.

## Input / Output

The method takes a collection of n pre-trained 7B LLMs (up to 16 in the main experiments) that may differ in architecture (Llama-2, CodeLlama, CrystalChat), initialization, and training recipe (fine-tuning vs. continuous pre-training), and produces a single aggregated LLM. The pipeline is training-free: no additional gradient updates are required for the core merging and mixture stages, though an optional MLP router variant uses one epoch of language-modeling fine-tuning on GPT4All. Evaluation spans commonsense reasoning (ARC, WinoGrande, MMLU), mathematics (GSM8K), and coding (MBPP, HumanEval).

## Method

Model-GLUE is a two-stage scaling recipe. First, it clusters the model zoo by pairwise cosine similarity of weights (threshold 0.95) to identify mergeable families, then applies selective merging within each cluster using either a greedy heuristic (iteratively adding the candidate that improves proxy-dataset accuracy, with coefficient search over {0.1,…,0.9}) or an evolutionary strategy (CMA-ES optimizing linear-interpolation coefficients). Second, it combines the merged cluster representatives with unmergeable domain-specific models via a model-level Mixture-of-Experts architecture, where each LLM serves as an expert and a linear router—initialized from task-specific prompt vectors—routes each input sample to the most appropriate expert using Top-K selection. Key empirical findings driving the design include: model-level mixture consistently outperforms FFN- and block-level mixture for heterogeneous experts; a simple linear router matches or beats an MLP router without training; and a hybrid variant that merges the bottom layers while applying FFN-level mixture to the rest further improves math and coding performance.

## Results

On the Which16 Llama-2-based model zoo, Model-GLUE achieves a 54.43% average across ARC, WinoGrande, MMLU, GSM8K, MBPP, and HumanEval, a 5.61% improvement over the best single model (MetaMath-Llemma-7B at 48.82%) without any additional training. Full Merging of all 12 mergeable models yields only 43.56%, and F-L-T Mixture of all 16 models yields 42.65%, both below the best single model, underscoring the necessity of selective aggregation. On a Mistral-based model zoo (Table 13), Model-GLUE reaches 60.18% average versus 57.69% for the best single model, confirming generalizability across model families.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among the six merging baselines evaluated (Linear/Model Soups, SLERP, Model Stock, Task Arithmetic, DARE, TIES), Linear interpolation is the most consistently competitive, achieving 43.59% average on Which12 under the evolutionary strategy, while TIES drops to 41.03% and Task Arithmetic to 40.27%. The critical finding is that all six methods fail catastrophically when merging dissimilar models: merging Llama-2-chat with CodeLlama causes TIES to collapse to 16.13% average (HumanEval at 0%) and DARE to 27.48%, whereas Model-GLUE's clustering-and-filtering pipeline avoids such harmful pairings entirely. On the Mistral model zoo, Model-GLUE (60.18%) outperforms DARE (58.82%), TIES (54.30%), and F-L-S mixture (59.48%), confirming that the selective merging + model-level mixture recipe generalizes beyond Llama-2. For homogeneous chat models (Which4-Chat), merging (41.54%) outperforms mixture (39.16%), while for domain-diverse models (Which4-Domain), mixture (43.95%) slightly edges out merging (43.86%), validating the paper's guideline to match aggregation strategy to model diversity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the model merging literature (Model Soup, Task Arithmetic, TIES, DARE, SLERP, Model Stock) and the Mixture-of-Experts paradigm (Mixtral, Switch Transformers), but departs from both by addressing a setting they largely ignore: a large, heterogeneous model zoo where not all models are mergeable or beneficial to include. Prior merging work assumed small, curated model collections (2–3 models) where all candidates are 'useful,' while prior model mixture work (BranchTrain-MiX, UltraFuser) was restricted to same-architecture, same-initialization experts. Model-GLUE introduces a selective pipeline—cosine-similarity clustering, heuristic/evolutionary filtering, and model-level mixture for unmergeable outliers—that systematically combines these two aggregation families for the first time at scale (up to 16 models).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first comprehensive, training-free recipe for scaling LLMs from a diverse open-source model zoo, yielding a 5.61% average improvement over the best single model without additional training—a practically significant result for practitioners who cannot afford to retrain. Key open questions remain: the work is confined to 7B Llama-2-based models (with a limited Mistral validation), the theoretical understanding of why selective merging helps is absent, and the integration of model stacking and multi-agent communication into the framework is left for future work. The finding that coarser-grained (model-level) mixture and simpler (linear) routers outperform their finer/more complex counterparts also raises questions about whether current MoE design intuitions transfer to the heterogeneous-expert setting.
