## Problem

Prior best-of-both-worlds algorithms for bandits with delayed feedback required prior knowledge of the maximal delay d_max and exhibited linear regret dependence on it, meaning a single delay of order T would make both stochastic and adversarial bounds linear in the time horizon. This paper addresses the gap by providing an algorithm that is robust to arbitrary excessive delays (up to order T) without any advance knowledge of d_max, replacing delay-magnitude-dependent terms with terms dependent on the number of outstanding observations at decision time.

## Input / Output

At each round t, the algorithm selects an arm from a set of K arms and incurs a loss in [0,1]^K, but the loss is only revealed after a variable delay d_t chosen by the environment. The algorithm maintains a running count of 'active' outstanding observations (those from non-skipped rounds that have not yet arrived) and adaptively skips rounds whose waiting time exceeds a data-driven threshold. The output is a probability distribution over arms at each round, with the theoretical guarantee being a best-of-both-worlds regret bound parameterized by the maximal number of outstanding observations σ̂_max and the number of skipped rounds S*, rather than by d_max or the total cumulative delay D.

## Method

The algorithm is a best-of-both-worlds modification of the FTRL algorithm with hybrid regularizer (negative Tsallis entropy + negative entropy with separate learning rates η_t^{-1}=√t and γ_t^{-1}=√(49D_t/log K)) from Zimmert and Seldin (2020), augmented with three key innovations. First, it introduces a novel implicit exploration scheme with biased loss estimators ℓ̂_{t,i} = ℓ_{t,i}·1(I_t=i)/max{x_{t,i}, λ_{t,t+d̂_t}} where λ_{s,t}=e^{-D_t/(D_t-D_s)}, which is the first implicit exploration schedule proven to work in a best-of-both-worlds setting. Second, it controls distribution drift (x_{t,i} ≤ 4·max(x_{s,i}, λ_{s,t})) using the implicit exploration terms combined with adaptive skipping of excessive delays, rather than damping the learning rate as in prior work. Third, it introduces a greedy rearrangement procedure (Algorithm 2) that reassigns colliding arrivals to future rounds to relate the drifted regret to the standard regret without requiring bounded delays, yielding an additive term of order σ_max rather than d_max.

## Results

The paper provides best-of-both-worlds regret bounds for multi-armed bandits with variable delays that are robust to excessive delay outliers. The adversarial regret bound is O(√(KT) + min_S{|S| + √(D_S̄ log K)} + S* + Kσ̂_max), and the stochastic regret bound is O(Σ_{i≠i*}(log T/Δ_i + σ̂_max/(Δ_i log K)) + Kσ̂_max + S*), where S* = O(min(d_max K^{1/3} log K, min_S{|S| + √(D_S̄ K^{2/3} log K)})). Crucially, the bounds depend on σ_max (maximal number of outstanding observations) rather than d_max (maximal delay), and a single delay of order T does not inflate the regret to linear in T, unlike prior best-of-both-worlds results.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Joulani et al. (2013), which provides only a stochastic bound, this paper achieves a best-of-both-worlds guarantee and replaces the σ_max·Δ_i term with σ̂_max/(Δ_i log K), which is tighter for small gaps. Against Zimmert and Seldin (2020), which is adversarial-only and minimax optimal, this paper matches the adversarial rate within a K^{1/3} factor on the delay-dependent term (the price of best-of-both-worlds) while adding a stochastic guarantee. The most direct comparison is with Masoudian et al. (2022), the prior best-of-both-worlds result: this paper eliminates the need for oracle knowledge of d_max, replaces additive d_max·K^{1/3} log K terms with Kσ̂_max + S* (where σ_max ≤ d_max and can be orders of magnitude smaller), and makes skipping genuinely beneficial by replacing √(D log K) with min_S(|S| + √(D_S̄ K^{2/3} log K)). Concretely, in the presence of a single delay of order T, Masoudian et al.'s bounds become O(T) (linear in horizon) while this paper's bounds remain sublinear, demonstrating the robustness improvement.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the FTRL-based adversarial algorithm of Zimmert and Seldin (2020), which parameterizes learning by running counts of outstanding observations rather than delay magnitudes, and on the best-of-both-worlds extension by Masoudian et al. (2022), which required prior knowledge of the maximal delay d_max and suffered linear regret dependence on it. The key departure is the introduction of implicit exploration (originally from Neu, 2015, for adversarial bandits) into the best-of-both-worlds setting for the first time, combined with the skipping technique of Thune et al. (2019) repurposed not merely as an optional add-on but as an indispensable mechanism to limit the time span over which distribution drift must be controlled. The hybrid Tsallis-entropy regularizer is inherited unchanged from Zimmert and Seldin, preserving the minimax-optimal adversarial bound while the stochastic analysis is the novel contribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes that best-of-both-worlds regret in delayed-feedback bandits is governed by the number of outstanding observations at decision time rather than the magnitude of delays, unifying a conceptual insight previously shown only in isolation for stochastic (Joulani et al., 2013) and adversarial (Zimmert and Seldin, 2020) regimes. Open questions include whether the K^{1/3} multiplicative factor on the delay-dependent adversarial term (the price for best-of-both-worlds guarantees) can be eliminated, whether the additional stochastic terms involving σ_max/(Δ_i log K) and S* are unavoidable, and whether the implicit exploration schedule generalizes to other best-of-both-worlds problems such as MDPs. The practical relevance is moderate: robustness to delay outliers (e.g., observations that never arrive) is important in real-world deployment, but the algorithm remains a theoretical construction without empirical validation.
