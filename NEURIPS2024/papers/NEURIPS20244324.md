## Problem

Generating egocentric (first-person) videos from sparse exocentric (third-person) videos for daily-life skilled human activities remains unsolved due to the large viewpoint gap between the two perspectives and the complexity of dynamic motions in real-world environments. Existing NeRF-based novel view synthesis methods require dozens to hundreds of views and per-scene optimization, while prior exo-to-ego translation work is limited to image-level output, simple desktop activities, or requires a specific first exocentric frame as input.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20244324/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes 4 exocentric videos (480×270, 8 frames at 7.5 fps) configured 360° around the scene, along with their camera poses and the target egocentric camera pose, and produces a corresponding egocentric video at 256×256 resolution. The 4 exocentric views are deliberately sparse and highly variable in their angular relationship to the egocentric viewpoint, making direct geometric correspondence difficult. The rendered egocentric features from the translation prior are sampled at 32×32 spatial resolution to match the latent space dimensions of the ego diffusion model.

## Method

Exo2Ego-V is a three-component video diffusion pipeline. First, a diffusion-based multi-view exocentric encoder (a trainable copy of the Ego UNet) extracts dense multi-scale appearance features from the 4 exocentric videos at t=0, with relative Exo2Ego camera pose embeddings injected via a 2-layer MLP; these features are concatenated with ego hidden states in self-attention layers so the ego latents can attend to exocentric content. Second, a lightweight PixelNeRF-based Exo2Ego view translation prior (6-layer MLP with ResNet34 image features) renders spatially aligned egocentric latent features that are channel-concatenated with the noisy ego latents as input to the diffusion UNet, providing coarse geometric guidance. Third, temporal attention layers (initialized from AnimateDiff) are inserted into the ego UNet to enforce cross-frame temporal consistency. Training proceeds in two stages: spatial appearance translation (jointly optimizing the exo encoder, translation prior, and ego UNet with reconstruction and noise-prediction losses), followed by temporal motion finetuning with all other modules frozen.

## Results

On the Ego-Exo4D dataset (5 categories: Cooking, Basketball, Covid Test, CPR, Bike), Exo2Ego-V achieves the best PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS across all categories under both unseen-action and unseen-take splits. The headline result is an average 35% improvement in LPIPS over the best baseline (SVD), e.g., Basketball unseen-action LPIPS drops from 0.533 (SVD) to 0.249 (Ours). On the H2O dataset, Exo2Ego-V achieves PSNR 18.60, SSIM 0.581, and LPIPS 0.189 versus SVD's 16.53, 0.468, and 0.271, confirming generalizability beyond Ego-Exo4D.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Exo2Ego-V is compared against three baselines: Stable Video Diffusion (SVD), Stable Diffusion (SD), and PixelNeRF. Exo2Ego-V outperforms all three baselines on every category and every metric (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS) under both unseen-action and unseen-take splits. The largest gains are over SVD, the strongest baseline: on Basketball (unseen action), LPIPS drops from 0.533 to 0.249 (a 53% reduction), and on Covid Test, LPIPS drops from 0.477 to 0.235 (a 51% reduction). PixelNeRF achieves competitive PSNR (e.g., 17.278 on Cooking vs. 17.367 for Ours) but produces very blurry outputs due to sparse-view reconstruction difficulty, resulting in much worse LPIPS (0.640 vs. 0.408 on Cooking). SD performs worst overall due to its 2D image-level generation lacking temporal consistency. On the H2O dataset, Exo2Ego-V also beats SVD by a clear margin (LPIPS 0.189 vs. 0.271), demonstrating cross-dataset generalizability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on latent diffusion models (Stable Diffusion's UNet architecture) as the generative backbone, AnimateDiff's temporal attention layers for cross-frame consistency, and PixelNeRF as the basis for a generalizable view translation prior. It departs from prior ego-exo translation work (P-GAN, STA-GAN, IDE, Exo2Ego) which was limited to simple activities, image-level output, or required specific camera setups, by formulating the problem as video generation from sparse 4-view exocentric inputs around complex daily-life scenes. The key architectural departure is the combination of a diffusion-based multi-view exocentric encoder (for dense appearance conditioning via self-attention) with a NeRF-based spatial alignment prior (for coarse egocentric feature guidance), rather than relying on either mechanism alone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to tackle exo-to-ego video generation for complex, real-world skilled activities (cooking, basketball, CPR, bike repair) using the Ego-Exo4D benchmark, opening a path toward generating egocentric training data from the much larger pool of exocentric video for robotics and AR applications. However, performance degrades substantially on unseen scenes, inference takes ~9 seconds for 8 frames, and the method is validated on only 5 activity categories. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to arbitrary activities, whether Gaussian Splatting could replace the PixelNeRF prior for efficiency, and whether real-time inference is achievable.
