## Problem

Sparse neural networks suffer from vanishing activations and gradients as sparsity increases, causing optimal hyperparameters (learning rate, initialization variance) to drift unpredictably away from their dense-model values. The standard practice of reusing dense-tuned HPs for sparse models is therefore suboptimal, and the cost of re-tuning at each sparsity level undermines the computational savings sparsity is meant to provide.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241066/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

SμPar takes a transformer model with a random unstructured static sparsity mask applied to hidden projection weights, along with a width multiplier m_d and density multiplier m_ρ relative to a base configuration, and base hyperparameters (σ_W,base, η_base) tuned on a small dense proxy model. It outputs corrected hidden-layer initialization variance σ²_W = σ²_base/(m_d·m_ρ) and Adam learning rate η = η_base/(m_d·m_ρ), while leaving embedding, output, bias, layer-norm, and attention-logit parameters unchanged from μP. The net effect is that activation norms, gradient norms, and weight-update magnitudes all remain Θ(1) in typical element size regardless of width or sparsity level.

## Method

SμPar extends the Feature Learning Desiderata (FLD) from μP by adding sparsity (density ρ) as a second scaling variable alongside width, requiring that the typical element size of forward activations Y, input gradients ∇_X L, and weight-update effects ΔY all remain Θ(1) with respect to both m_d and m_ρ. The derivation shows that for a random unstructured mask with density ρ, the forward-pass variance scales as d_in·ρ·σ²_W, the backward-pass variance as d_out·ρ·σ²_W, and the Adam weight-update effect as η·d_in·ρ·E[X·ΔW], leading to the unique corrections σ²_W = σ²_base/(m_d·m_ρ) and η = η_base/(m_d·m_ρ). Both the initialization and learning-rate corrections are necessary: ablations show that either alone fails to stabilize optimal HPs across sparsity. Because the corrections reduce to μP when ρ=1, SμPar inherits μTransfer—HPs tuned on a 39M dense proxy model transfer zero-shot to sparse models up to 610M parameters and 99.2% sparsity, yielding up to 11.9% relative loss improvement over standard parameterization at the highest sparsity tested.

## Results

On 610M-parameter GPT-like language models trained on 12.1B SlimPajama tokens, SμPar achieves up to 11.9% relative validation loss improvement over standard parameterization (SP) and 1.9% improvement over μP at 99.2% random unstructured sparsity. Across all sparsity levels tested, SμPar forms the Pareto frontier with an average loss gap of 0.8% better than SP and 2.1% better than μP. On downstream tasks, SμPar maintains 40%+ average accuracy at 99.2% sparsity, while SP drops to 34% (close to the 30% random baseline). SμPar also enables stable optimal hyperparameters (learning rate consistently near 2⁻⁶) across all sparsity levels, unlike SP and μP where optimal HPs drift unpredictably.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against μP (Yang et al., NeurIPS 2021), SμPar consistently outperforms across all sparsity levels, with the gap widening as sparsity increases: from negligible at 0% sparsity (where SμPar reduces to μP) to 1.9% better validation loss at 99.2% sparsity. The key advantage is HP stability: μP's optimal learning rate and initialization variance drift unpredictably with sparsity (even showing discontinuities), while SμPar maintains a consistent optimal LR near 2⁻⁶. Against SP (standard parameterization), SμPar achieves an average 0.8% loss improvement, growing to 11.9% at 99.2% sparsity, where SP models are nearly at random-chance level on downstream tasks. For dynamic sparse training methods RigL (Evci et al., ICML 2020) and GMP (Zhu et al., 2017), the paper finds that none of SP, μP, or SμPar achieve stable optimal learning rate transfer across sparsity levels; SμPar actually 'overcorrects' for these methods because magnitude-based pruning produces non-Gaussian weight distributions that violate SμPar's assumptions. This is identified as a limitation and direction for future work.

## Relation to Prior Work

SμPar generalizes maximal update parameterization (μP) [Yang et al., 2021; Yang & Hu, 2020] from the width-only setting to jointly control training dynamics across both width and sparsity. Prior sparse training work (Evci et al., 2022; Liu et al., 2018; Ramanujan et al., 2020; Tessera et al., 2021) identified individual signal-propagation failures—vanishing activations, degraded gradient flow, undersized weight updates—and proposed targeted fixes, but each addressed only a subset of the three operations in a training step. SμPar unifies these corrections under the Feature Learning Desiderata framework, showing that a single density-scaled initialization variance and learning rate simultaneously control forward activations, backward gradients, and weight-update effects. The paper also departs from the widespread practice of reusing dense-optimized hyperparameters for sparse models, demonstrating empirically that this practice systematically detunes training as sparsity increases.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SμPar dramatically reduces the cost of sparse training research by enabling hyperparameter transfer from small dense proxy models to large sparse models across arbitrary sparsity levels, removing the need to re-tune at each sparsity setting. The 11.9% relative loss improvement at 99.2% sparsity on 610M-parameter LLMs demonstrates that prior sparse training results based on reused dense HPs may be substantially underestimating achievable performance. Key limitations include inapplicability to dynamic sparsity methods (RigL, GMP) where non-Gaussian weight distributions cause SμPar to overcorrect, the need for per-optimizer derivation of scaling factors, and the assumption of random unstructured masks. An open question is whether a generalized parameterization can handle arbitrary sparse training algorithms and structured sparsity patterns while preserving the clean transfer properties.
