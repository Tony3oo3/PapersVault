## Problem

Sampling from non-log-concave distributions with high barriers between modes (metastability) remains difficult: existing diffusion-based samplers like RDMC and RSDMC require first-order gradient queries and still struggle with multimodality, while zeroth-order methods like rejection sampling suffer from curse of dimensionality. No prior sampler combines zeroth-order queries with denoising diffusion to handle general non-log-concave targets without isoperimetric assumptions.

## Input / Output

The method consumes only zeroth-order function evaluations of the potential V (no gradients), the global minimum value V* of V (approximated in practice via Newton's method), and the target dimension d. It outputs samples from p ∝ exp(−V) with non-asymptotic guarantees in both KL divergence and W2 distance. A notable practical feature is that the rejection sampling step (Algorithm 3) generating Monte Carlo score estimates can be fully parallelized, unlike sequential Langevin-based alternatives.

## Method

ZOD-MC is a two-level framework: an outer loop simulates the reverse Ornstein-Uhlenbeck (denoising) diffusion SDE via an exponential integrator, while an inner loop estimates the score function at each time step using a Monte Carlo estimator derived from Tweedie's formula. The key insight is that the conditional distribution p_{0|t}(·|x) appearing in Tweedie's formula has a structured potential (target V plus a quadratic term), enabling its sampling via rejection sampling with a Gaussian envelope centered at e^t·x and variance e^{2t}−1, where the global minimum V* provides the acceptance threshold. This eliminates the need for gradient queries entirely. The theoretical analysis decomposes the total error into initialization, discretization, and score-estimation components, yielding a non-asymptotic KL + W2 bound with inverse-polynomial dependence on accuracy ε but exponential dependence on dimension d, requiring no isoperimetric or smoothness assumptions beyond a quadratic growth condition on V.

## Results

At matched oracle complexity (total number of 0th and 1st order V queries), ZOD-MC achieves the lowest MMD and W2 errors among all compared methods on asymmetric, unbalanced 2D Gaussian mixtures. ZOD-MC is the most insensitive to increasing mode separation (barrier height), remaining the only method that successfully samples all modes when the separation parameter R is increased to 26. On the Muller-Brown potential, ZOD-MC is the only method that correctly samples all three modes, while all baselines get trapped at the origin mode. The theoretical zeroth-order query complexity is exp(tilde{O}(d)·O(log(ε^{-1}))), which is polynomial in inverse accuracy but exponential in dimension.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against RDMC and RSDMC (both first-order, diffusion-based), ZOD-MC achieves lower MMD and W2 at the same total oracle budget, and produces the best L2 score approximation across all time steps (Figure 4b). Against SLIPS, ZOD-MC is substantially more robust to mode separation, remaining the only method that samples all modes at R=26 while SLIPS and others get stuck at the origin. Against the proximal sampler, annealed importance sampling, sequential Monte Carlo, parallel tempering with MALA, and unadjusted Langevin, ZOD-MC is the only method that correctly recovers the full multimodal target in both the Gaussian mixture and Muller-Brown experiments. A key practical advantage is that ZOD-MC uses only zeroth-order queries (function evaluations of V), making it applicable to discontinuous or non-differentiable potentials where all first-order baselines (RDMC, RSDMC, SLIPS, Langevin) fundamentally fail. The main trade-off is dimensionality: ZOD-MC's exponential-in-d complexity is worse than RDMC's exp(O(log(d))·tilde{O}(ε^{-1})), though empirically the baselines also degrade with dimension.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the diffusion-based Monte Carlo sampling lineage (RDMC → RSDMC) by replacing first-order gradient queries with zeroth-order function evaluations and implementing the score-estimation oracle via rejection sampling rather than Langevin-based inner loops. The DDMC meta-algorithm generalizes the score-estimation step of RDMC/RSDMC to accept any Monte Carlo samples from the conditional distribution p_{0|t}(·|x), while ZOD-MC instantiates this oracle using the Restricted Gaussian Oracle (RGO) with a large noise parameter. Theoretically, the analysis builds on the exponential-integrator discretization of the reverse OU process and the stochastic localization framework, but removes all isoperimetric assumptions (LSI, log-concavity) that prior diffusion-based samplers required, at the cost of an exponential dimension dependence.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ZOD-MC is the first diffusion-based sampler that handles discontinuous potentials and requires no gradient access, making it applicable to problems where the potential is non-smooth or only available as a black-box function. Its demonstrated insensitivity to arbitrarily high inter-mode barriers addresses a key practical failure mode of Langevin-based and even prior diffusion-based samplers. However, the exponential dimension dependence (exp(Õ(d)·log(ε⁻¹))) limits its practical utility to low-dimensional settings (d ≲ 10), and the rejection-sampling oracle inherits the curse of dimensionality. Open questions include whether the dimension dependence can be improved, whether learned score approximations could replace rejection sampling at higher dimensions, and whether the zeroth-order advantage persists in higher-dimensional multimodal problems.
