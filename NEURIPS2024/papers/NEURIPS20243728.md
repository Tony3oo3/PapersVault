## Problem

Progressively deeper stacked networks (U-Net, Transformer) in diffusion models accumulate numerical propagation errors that degrade noisy prediction and hinder massively deep scalable training. The paper identifies that residual units inherently possess dynamic consistency with the reverse diffusion process, yet existing architectures lack a principled mechanism to exploit this property for stable deep training and to replace manually designed mean-variance schedulers.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243728/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts class labels (ImageNet), text prompts (JourneyDB, COCO), or no conditioning (unconditional video) and produces 256×256 images or 16-frame video clips at 256×256 per frame. All generation occurs in a VQ-VAE latent space with 8× spatial downsampling, and the gating parameters α, β are initialized to (1, 0) before full-parameter fine-tuning on top of pre-trained LDM or Latte backbones.

## Method

Neural-RDM introduces a minimum residual stacking unit (mrs-unit) defined as z_{i+1} = z_i + α·f(z_i) + β, where learnable gating weights α and β modulate each residual block's contribution. Three continuous-time ODEs underpin the framework: a Gating-Residual ODE that unifies flow-shaped (Transformer) and U-shaped (U-Net) stacking into a single dynamic description, a Denoising-Dynamics ODE that re-parameterizes the PF-ODE by replacing hand-crafted mean-variance schedulers (SMLD, DDPM) with the learned α, β, and a Residual-Sensitivity ODE derived via adjoint sensitivity analysis that proves the gating weights positively inhibit sensitivity decay as depth increases. Training uses full-parameter fine-tuning with an explicit sensitivity-correction loss (γ=0.35) to supervise α and β, enabling stable convergence at extreme depths without architectural redesign.

## Results

On ImageNet class-to-image (256×256), Neural-RDM-F achieves FID 2.12, sFID 3.75, and IS 295.32, setting new state-of-the-art among all compared methods. On JourneyDB text-to-image, Neural-RDM-U achieves FID 2.25, sFID 4.36, and IS 235.35. For video generation, Neural-RDM attains FVD 39.89 on SkyTimelapse, 91.22 on Taichi-HD, and 461.03 on UCF-101, with frame-level FID of 3.35 and IS of 85.97, outperforming the strongest baseline Latte-XL by 33.3% and 42.8% in FVD on SkyTimelapse and Taichi-HD respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Neural-RDM-F achieves the best class-to-image FID (2.12) on ImageNet, surpassing the strongest GAN baseline StyleGAN-XL (2.30), the Transformer-based DiT-XL/2-G (2.27), and Latte-XL (2.35), while also leading in sFID (3.75) and IS (295.32). For text-to-image on JourneyDB, Neural-RDM-U achieves the best FID (2.25) and sFID (4.36), outperforming LDM-4-G (FID 3.78), DiT-XL/2-G (FID 3.53), and Latte-XL (FID 2.74). In video generation, Neural-RDM dominates Latte-XL across all three FVD benchmarks: 39.89 vs. 59.82 on SkyTimelapse (33.3% improvement), 91.22 vs. 159.60 on Taichi-HD (42.8% improvement), and 461.03 vs. 477.97 on UCF-101, while also improving frame-level FID from 5.02 to 3.35 and IS from 68.53 to 85.97. All GAN-based video baselines (MoCoGAN, StyleGAN-V, MoStGAN-V, DIGAN) are substantially outperformed, with FVD gaps exceeding 100 points on SkyTimelapse. The only near-tie is UCF-101 class-to-video FVD where Neural-RDM (461.03) narrowly beats Latte-XL (477.97), confirming consistent but not always dramatic gains in the conditional video setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate families of deep generative architectures—U-shaped (U-Net/LDM) and flow-shaped (Transformer/DiT/Latte)—under a single continuous-time gating-residual ODE framework, treating the minimum residual stacking unit (mrs-unit) as the fundamental denoising building block. It builds directly on the PF-ODE formulation from Song et al. (ICLR 2020) by re-parameterizing the manually designed mean-variance scheduler (SMLD, DDPM) as learnable gating weights α and β, and on LDM and Latte as the practical backbones. The key departure from prior residual-network work (ResNet, ReZero, AdaLN) is the theoretical justification: the paper derives a Residual-Sensitivity ODE showing that vanilla residual stacking causes progressive sensitivity decay, and that the specific post-processing gating placement (z + α·f(z) + β) provides adaptive error correction that the other placements do not.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a central open question in generative modeling: whether diffusion models can be scaled to extreme depths (analogous to LLM scaling) without numerical instability, and provides a principled architectural answer. The unification of U-Net and Transformer under one ODE is conceptually clean and could guide future architecture design beyond the current two paradigms. However, the authors acknowledge that the gating mechanism only inhibits rather than eliminates sensitivity decay, and the performance gains over strong baselines (DiT-XL/2-G, Latte-XL) are solid but incremental (e.g., FID 2.12 vs. 2.27 on ImageNet). Whether this framework truly unlocks the kind of emergent capabilities seen in LLMs at much larger scale remains an open question, as experiments are limited to 256×256 resolution and moderate model sizes.
