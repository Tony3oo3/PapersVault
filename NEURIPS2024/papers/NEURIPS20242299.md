## Problem

Olfactory perception remains under-explored in machine learning due to the absence of large-scale datasets annotated with human olfactory labels and the lack of a single organizing principle for odor space. It is unknown whether general-purpose, self-supervised transformer models pre-trained on chemical structures encode representations that are meaningfully aligned with human olfactory experience, without any explicit olfactory supervision.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242299/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes SMILES strings of individual odorant molecules; for multicomponent odorants (MC-odorants), the 768-dim MoLFormer representation is computed as the arithmetic mean across all component molecules. Downstream, representations are reduced to 20 dimensions via PCA and z-scored before linear probes, or used directly for cosine-similarity computation in representational similarity analysis. Outputs span four evaluation axes: binary expert odor labels (138 descriptors), continuous perceptual ratings (15–23 descriptors on a bounded scale), pairwise human similarity scores, and 15 physicochemical descriptor values.

## Method

The paper uses MoLFormer, a transformer with linear attention and relative positional encodings pre-trained via masked-token prediction on PubChem and ZINC, to extract 768-dim odorant representations without any olfactory-specific fine-tuning. Alignment with human perception is assessed through three complementary protocols: (i) per-descriptor logistic regression on PCA-reduced representations for multi-label expert classification, (ii) Lasso-regularized linear regression for continuous perceptual ratings, and (iii) direct cosine-similarity computation between representation pairs correlated with human similarity ratings. Baselines include Open-POM (a supervised message-passing GNN trained end-to-end on the same GS-LF labels) and DAM (a hand-crafted 15-descriptor physicochemical model). A layer-wise analysis reveals that perceptual alignment increases with transformer depth while alignment with low-level physicochemical features decreases, mirroring the hierarchical abstraction observed in visual CNNs.

## Results

On the representational similarity analysis (RSA), MoLFormer achieves Pearson correlations of r=0.64 (p<0.0001) on the Snitz dataset and r=0.66 (p<0.0001) on the Ravia dataset, significantly outperforming the DAM baseline and matching Open-POM. On continuous perceptual rating prediction (Table 1), MoLFormer achieves CC=0.20±0.00 on Keller and CC=0.25±0.01 on Sagar, slightly below Open-POM (0.22 and 0.29) but above DAM on Keller (0.17). On the GS-LF expert-label classification task, MoLFormer achieves high micro-averaged ROC-AUC, outperforming DAM but below the supervised Open-POM. MoLFormer also predicts 13 out of 15 physicochemical descriptors as well as or better than Open-POM.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MoLFormer is compared against two baselines: Open-POM (a supervised message-passing GNN trained end-to-end on GS-LF) and DAM (a hand-crafted model using 15 physicochemical descriptors). On the RSA task (predicting pairwise human similarity ratings), MoLFormer significantly outperforms DAM and performs on par with Open-POM, achieving r=0.64–0.66. On continuous rating prediction, MoLFormer slightly underperforms Open-POM on both Keller (CC 0.20 vs. 0.22) and Sagar (CC 0.25 vs. 0.29), but notably outperforms DAM on Keller (CC 0.20 vs. 0.17) while underperforming DAM on Sagar (CC 0.25 vs. 0.35). On the GS-LF expert-label classification, MoLFormer outperforms DAM in ROC-AUC but falls short of Open-POM, which is expected since Open-POM is directly supervised on that dataset. Overall, MoLFormer—despite being trained solely via self-supervised masked-token prediction on general chemical structures—achieves alignment with human olfactory perception comparable to supervised models, particularly on similarity-based tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the representational alignment framework well-established in vision and language (e.g., comparing DNN activations to neural responses) to the olfactory domain, where prior work has been limited to supervised models: the Distance-Angle Model (Snitz et al.) using hand-engineered physicochemical descriptors, and the Principal Odor Map (Lee et al.), a supervised message-passing GNN trained on expert odor labels. The key departure is repurposing MoLFormer, a self-supervised transformer pre-trained via masked token prediction on PubChem and ZINC with no olfactory labels whatsoever, and probing its representations for alignment with human smell. The paper thus carves new ground by asking whether general chemical structure learning implicitly captures the features that drive human olfactory perception, rather than requiring task-specific olfactory supervision.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first empirical demonstration that a large-scale self-supervised molecular transformer, trained solely on chemical structures, produces representations linearly decodable into human olfactory judgments across multiple datasets and task types (expert labels, continuous ratings, pairwise similarity). The finding that alignment improves with layer depth while physicochemical descriptor predictability decreases mirrors the hierarchical organization seen in vision, suggesting a potential computational account of how chemical structure maps to perceptual experience. Key limitations include the absence of intensity/concentration modeling for mixtures, reliance on linear probes rather than full fine-tuning, and relatively small evaluation datasets. Open questions include whether this alignment extends to neural (fMRI) representations, which specific substructures drive the effect, and whether the observed hierarchy generalizes beyond the 768-dim MoLFormer architecture.
