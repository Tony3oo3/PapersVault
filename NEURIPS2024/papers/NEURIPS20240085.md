## Problem

Standard iterative solvers for graph diffusion equations (PPR, Katz, Heat kernel) require O(m) operations per iteration, making them prohibitively slow on large-scale graphs. Existing local solvers like APPR avoid this cost but are fundamentally sequential (Gauss-Seidel-style) and tailored to specific diffusion types, limiting their parallelizability on GPUs and generalizability across GDE families. The paper asks whether a unified local framework can localize arbitrary standard iterative solvers while preserving sublinear runtime guarantees.

## Input / Output

The method takes an undirected graph G(V,E), a source node s (or sparse source vector), a damping factor α ∈ (0,1), and a precision tolerance ε, and produces an approximate diffusion vector f̂ where the active set S_t evolves locally from {s} until all residuals fall below the threshold ‖D⁻¹r‖∞ ≤ αε (for PPR) or ‖D⁻¹r‖∞ ≤ ε (for Katz). The output is a full-length vector but with strong localization: the participation ratio p(f) is typically below 0.01, meaning most entries are negligible. The framework also supports dynamic graphs where the source matrix Q_t changes between snapshots, enabling incremental updates rather than recomputation from scratch.

## Method

The core contribution is a 'local diffusion process' framework that models iterative solving as a locally evolving set process: at each iteration t, only nodes in the active set S_t (those with residual above threshold) are updated, and the total cost is T·vol̄(S_T). Within this framework, three solvers are designed: (1) LocalSOR, a localized Successive Overrelaxation with tunable ω that subsumes APPR as the ω=1 special case and achieves fewer iterations via optimal ω*; (2) LocalGD, an embarrassingly parallel localized gradient descent that simultaneously updates all active nodes, making it GPU-friendly; and (3) LocalCH, a localized Chebyshev iteration for accelerated convergence when Q is symmetric. The key theoretical result is a sublinear runtime bound of min(vol̄(S_T)/(α·γ̄_T), 1/(αε)) where vol̄(S_T)/γ̄_T ≤ 1/ε, which is empirically tighter than the classical Θ(1/(αε)) bound for APPR. The framework reveals that existing local solvers are suboptimal special cases and provides a principled path to parallelizing them.

## Results

The proposed local solvers achieve speedup ratios of 100–1137× over their standard global counterparts in terms of operations for PPR computation across five graph datasets (Table 2). LocalGD delivers the highest speedups (157× on Citeseer, 1137× on ogbn-papers100M), while LocalSOR achieves 86–667× speedups. On GPU, LocalGD is the fastest method across a wide range of precision tolerances ε, and for dynamic GNN training (InstantGNN on ogbn-arxiv), the LocalSOR-based propagation reduces accumulated operations substantially compared to static APPR-based propagation, yielding roughly 2× faster training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares four standard iterative solvers (GS, SOR, GD, Chebyshev) against their proposed local counterparts (LocalGS, LocalSOR, LocalGD, LocalCH) on PPR, Katz, and Heat Kernel computation. All local methods consistently outperform their global versions by 2–3 orders of magnitude in operation count when ε is in the practical range (e.g., ε = 1/n or 1/m). LocalGD and LocalGS achieve the best overall speedups, while LocalSOR and LocalCH show greater relative gains at higher precision (smaller ε), as demonstrated on ogbn-arxiv where LocalSOR's advantage over LocalGS grows as ε decreases. Against the widely-used APPR algorithm [2], LocalSOR (with ω ≈ 1.19) reduces both iteration count and total operations on a toy graph (5 vs. 6 iterations; 28 vs. 42 operations) and achieves better additive error (0.215 vs. 0.292). For dynamic GNN training, the LocalSOR-based InstantGNN matches testing accuracy of the standard APPR-based propagation while significantly reducing training time and accumulated operations across 16 graph snapshots.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies the family of local push-based solvers (APPR for PPR, AKatz for Katz centrality, AHK for Heat kernel) under a single 'local diffusion process' framework modeled as a locally evolving set, drawing conceptual inspiration from the stochastic evolving-sets process of Morris and Peres. It reveals that APPR is a special case of local Gauss-Seidel with SOR (ω=1), and extends this by introducing a parallelizable local gradient descent (LocalGD) and a local Chebyshev method (LocalCH) that are provably sublinear and GPU-friendly. The framework also connects to the authors' concurrent work on locally evolving set processes [77], though applied here to a different class of equations (GDEs) with a focus on GNN training acceleration.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: up to 100× speedup over standard solvers on large graphs, GPU-friendly parallelism that existing sequential local methods (APPR) fundamentally lack, and a demonstrated 2× speedup for dynamic PPR-based GNN training. Key limitations include the loss of speedup at very high precision (small ε), unproven accelerated bounds for the overrelaxed (ω>1) and Chebyshev variants, and the framework currently covering only PPR and Katz with full proofs. Open questions include whether accelerated sublinear bounds exist without the monotonicity assumption, and whether the local message-passing strategy generalizes to broader GNN architectures beyond APPNP-style propagation.
