## Problem

Neural network interatomic potentials (NNIPs) have increasingly relied on physically-motivated constraints—particularly rotational equivariance enforced through costly tensor products of order L—to improve accuracy. The authors argue these constraints inhibit the model's ability to scale with compute and data, leading to performance plateaus, and ask how to design general-purpose NNIPs that exploit GPU efficiency and scale effectively rather than embedding handcrafted symmetries.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a radius-r molecular graph with three pre-computable attributes per node/edge: atomic number embeddings, radial basis expansion (RBF) of pairwise distances, and Bond-Orientational Order (BOO) features that encode neighborhood directionality in a rotationally invariant way. Outputs are per-atom forces (magnitude predicted from node readout features, direction from a vector-valued transformation of unit edge directions using edge readout features) and total system energy; for materials tasks, a stress tensor is also predicted. Notably, the force-direction head is deliberately non-equivariant by construction, yet the trained model recovers rotational equivariance from data.

## Method

EScAIP is a graph neural network whose core block applies multi-head self-attention at the neighbor level (sequence length = max number of neighbors M) rather than only at the node level, making it more expressive than standard graph attention. By operating on scalar, rotation/translation-invariant features (BOO instead of spherical harmonics), the architecture avoids expensive equivariant tensor products and can leverage highly optimized Triton attention kernels for GPU acceleration, yielding ~10× faster inference and ~5× less memory than comparable equivariant models. An ablation study on EquiformerV2 showed that scaling attention parameters (rather than increasing rotation order L) yields the largest performance gains at matched parameter counts, motivating the attention-centric design. The readout follows GemNet-OC with separate edge and node readout layers per block, and the force-direction output uses a vector-valued (not scalar) transformation of edge directions, which breaks strict equivariance but improves optimization and final accuracy.

## Results

On OC20 All+MD, EScAIP-Large (317M params) achieves 215 meV Energy MAE and 12.65 meV/Å Force MAE on the test set, outperforming EquiformerV2 (153M) by ~1.5 meV/Å in Force MAE. On OC22, EScAIP-Medium achieves 473 meV Energy MAE and 25.73 meV/Å Force MAE, beating EquiformerV2 (462 meV, 27.1 meV/Å). On Matbench-Discovery (MPTrj), EScAIP achieves the best F1 (0.781), MAE (38 eV/atom), and R² (0.792). In efficiency, EScAIP-Small runs at 107 samples/sec inference with 1.09 GB/sample memory, roughly 10× faster and 5× less memory than a comparable EquiformerV2 model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EScAIP achieves state-of-the-art Force MAE across all three OC20/OC22 splits, beating the strongest baseline EquiformerV2 by 1.1–1.5 meV/Å on OC20 All+MD (12.65 vs 13.80 test) and by 1.4 meV/Å on OC22 (25.73 vs 27.1 test). On OC20 2M, EScAIP-Medium (19.08 meV/Å) outperforms EquiformerV2 (20.46), eSCN (20.50), SCN (21.90), GemNet-OC (25.70), and GemNet-dT (29.50). On Matbench-Discovery, EScAIP (MAE 38, F1 0.781) surpasses EquiformerV2 (MAE 42, F1 0.77), ORB MPTrj (MAE 45), SevenNet (MAE 48), and MACE (MAE 57). On SPICE, EScAIP halves the Force MAE of MACE-OFF23 across all seven molecular categories (e.g., 5.86 vs 14.75 meV/Å on PubChem, 2.18 vs 6.62 on DES370K Dimers). On MD22, EScAIP outperforms sGDML, MACE, and VisNet-LSRM in both Energy and Force MAE, with the largest gap on the 370-atom double-walled nanotube (8.1 vs 12.0 meV/Å Force MAE vs MACE). Notably, EScAIP achieves these gains while being ~10× faster in inference and using ~5× less memory than comparable EquiformerV2 models.

## Relation to Prior Work

EScAIP departs from the dominant NNIP paradigm of embedding explicit rotational equivariance through computationally expensive tensor products (as in EquiformerV2, MACE, eSCN) and instead operates on rotation-invariant scalar features (atomic numbers, RBF distances, Bond-Orientational Order embeddings), learning equivariance implicitly from data. The architecture draws on GemNet-OC's dual readout design for edge and node features, but introduces a key novelty: multi-head self-attention applied at the neighbor level (sequence length = max neighbors) rather than only at the node level, implemented with optimized Triton kernels. The ablation study on EquiformerV2 provides the empirical motivation, showing that scaling attention parameters outperforms scaling rotation order L when parameter counts are controlled, and that an L=0 (fully invariant) model with BOO features can match L=6 performance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper challenges a core working assumption in the NNIP field—that explicit symmetry constraints are necessary for accurate force prediction—by demonstrating that a model without built-in equivariance can learn it to near-perfect accuracy (cosine similarity ≥0.999) while being 10x faster and using 5x less memory. This 'sweet lesson' framing has practical implications: it democratizes large-scale NNIP training by removing the computational bottleneck of equivariant tensor products, making state-of-the-art models accessible to a broader research community. Open questions include whether learned equivariance remains robust for truly out-of-distribution geometries or extreme conditions, whether the approach degrades in very small-data regimes where explicit constraints may still help, and whether the symmetry-breaking output head (vector-valued force direction) is a fundamental design choice or an artifact of the training setup.
