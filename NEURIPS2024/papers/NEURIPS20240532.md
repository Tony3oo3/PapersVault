## Problem

Self-attention in LLM inference scales quadratically with sequence length, making long-context generation expensive in both compute and memory bandwidth. Existing sparse attention methods either permanently evict tokens from the KV-cache (causing irreversible information loss) or impose fixed sparsity patterns, and none exploit the intrinsic low-dimensional structure of key vectors. This paper identifies that attention keys consistently occupy a significantly lower-dimensional subspace across diverse models and datasets, and leverages this to enable efficient, lossless dynamic token selection.

## Input / Output

At each autoregressive generation step, Loki receives the current token embedding, the accumulated KV-cache (keys stored in PCA-transformed form, values in original form), and a pre-computed D×D PCA projection matrix calibrated offline on a separate dataset (e.g., WikiText-103). It outputs the attention output vector for the current query, which feeds into the rest of the transformer layer. The KV-cache memory footprint is unchanged from vanilla attention; the savings come purely from reduced data movement and compute during the attention scoring step.

## Method

Loki projects keys and queries into a PCA-transformed space and uses only the top d principal components (d_f = 25–50% of full head dimension D) to compute approximate attention scores across all cached tokens, then selects the top-k most relevant tokens (k_f = 12.5–25% of the cache). For these selected tokens, it reverts to full dimensionality in the PCA space to compute exact attention scores and the final weighted sum of values. Two lemmas provide theoretical grounding: full-rank PCA transformation preserves attention scores exactly, and reduced-rank PCA provides a bounded approximation via Cauchy-Schwarz. The method requires no token deletion (all keys remain in cache), no fine-tuning, and no additional memory beyond the small PCA matrix. Optimized Triton kernels perform the three sparse matrix multiplications directly on KV-cache subsets without materializing dense temporaries, achieving up to 45% speedup over vanilla attention on Llama2-13B.

## Results

On perplexity (WikiText-2), Loki with k_f=0.25 and d_f=0.25 degrades by only 0.09–0.10 over full attention across Llama2-7B (5.20 vs 5.11), Llama2-13B (4.61 vs 4.57), Llama3-8B (5.66 vs 5.57), and Mistral-7B (4.92 vs 4.91), while H2O degrades by 0.17–0.19. The average degradation in downstream task accuracy across 6 benchmarks and 8 models is only 6.8%. Empirically, Loki achieves up to 45% speedup over vanilla attention for Llama2-13B (prompt length 3072, generation length 512), with a theoretical speedup of 2.6× under the k_f=0.25, d_f=0.25 configuration.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Loki is compared against two baselines: Exact-TopK (Gupta et al., 2021), which computes full-dimensionality attention scores and then selects the top-k keys, and H2O (Zhang et al., 2023), a token-eviction method that permanently removes low-importance tokens from the KV-cache. On perplexity, Loki consistently outperforms H2O across all four models (e.g., 5.20 vs 5.28 on Llama2-7B; 4.61 vs 4.70 on Llama2-13B), with the gap widening for larger models. Loki closely tracks Exact-TopK, which serves as the performance upper bound for any top-k selection method: the perplexity gap is ≤0.03 on Llama2-7B/13B and Mistral-7B, confirming that the low-dimensional ranking recovers the correct top-k tokens (Jaccard similarity ≈ 0.9). On short-context downstream tasks (Hellaswag, TriviaQA, Winogrande, ARC, GSM8K, MMLU), Loki matches full attention within ~1–2% for Llama2-7B/13B and Mistral-7B, while H2O shows larger drops (e.g., GSM8K drops from 13.87 to 7.96 for Loki vs. a larger drop for H2O on Llama2-7B). The key practical advantage is that Loki achieves these results with a 2.6× theoretical speedup and up to 45% empirical speedup, whereas Exact-TopK provides no speedup since it still computes full-dimensionality scores for all S keys.

## Relation to Prior Work

Loki sits at the intersection of sparse attention and low-rank approximation, but departs from both lineages in a specific way. Unlike token-eviction methods (H2O, Scissorhands, StreamingLLM) that permanently delete KV-cache entries, or fixed-pattern sparsity (Sparse-Transformers, Reformer), Loki dynamically selects top-k tokens at each step without any deletion, using approximate attention scores computed in a PCA-reduced key space. The implementation builds directly on SparQ's Triton kernel design for top-k attention, though the authors identify and fix two key inefficiencies (lack of n-dimension parallelism and inability to handle non-power-of-2 token counts). The paper also positions itself against InfiniGen, which uses a similar SVD-based low-rank approximation but focuses on CPU-GPU offloading rather than pure compute reduction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical 45% attention speedup with only ~7% average accuracy degradation across 8 models makes Loki a drop-in optimization for LLM inference pipelines, and the finding that PCA transforms generalize across calibration datasets removes a practical barrier to deployment. Key limitations remain: Loki does not reduce KV-cache memory (orthogonal to eviction methods), the top-k selection operation is a non-trivial bottleneck that could erode gains, and integration with production inference engines like FlashAttention or vLLM is left as future work. The catastrophic failure of the simpler PCAAttn variant (perplexity jumping from ~5 to 38+) highlights that the two-stage design (approximate ranking + exact scoring) is essential, not merely an optimization choice.
