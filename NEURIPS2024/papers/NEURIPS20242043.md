## Problem

LLM assistants are trained to state 'I am a language model,' but it remains unclear whether they genuinely know they are LLMs, recognize their deployment context, and reliably act on this self-knowledge. No large-scale, automatically scored benchmark existed to decompose and quantify situational awareness in LLMs into distinct, measurable abilities.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242043/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each task presents a text prompt (multiple-choice or free-form question/instruction) to a model, optionally prefixed with a situating system prompt that reminds the model it is an LLM. Responses are graded automatically via string matching or LLM-as-judge, yielding binary correct/incorrect per trial. The overall SAD score is the equal-weighted average across 7 task categories (16 tasks, 13,198 questions total), with per-category and per-task breakdowns also reported. Notably, correct answers for 5 of 16 tasks are model-specific (e.g., token counts depend on the model's tokenizer, identity questions depend on the model's name), so evaluating a new model requires supplying model metadata.

## Method

SAD decomposes situational awareness into three aspects—self-knowledge, inferences, and actions—operationalized through 7 task categories: FACTS (distinguishing self from humans/other LLMs), INFLUENCE (knowing what one can causally affect), INTROSPECT (token counting, self-prediction, rule-following), STAGES (classifying prompts as pretraining/finetuning/evaluation/deployment), SELF-RECOGNITION (identifying own generated text), ID-LEVERAGE (executing instructions conditional on self-identity), and ANTI-IMITATION (controlling output distributions to deviate from training-data patterns). The benchmark is designed so that inter-category correlations are low, confirming it captures distinct facets of situational awareness. The authors evaluate 16 LLMs (Llama-2, GPT-3/4, Claude families; base and chat variants) with and without situating prompts and Chain-of-Thought, and show that SAD performance is only partially predicted by MMLU, that chat finetuning improves SAD scores by ~4.5% on average, and that even the best model (Claude 3 Opus, 49.5%) falls well short of the 90.7% upper baseline.

## Results

Across 16 LLMs, all models score above random chance (27.4%) on the SAD benchmark, but even the best model (Claude 3 Opus) reaches only 49.5% overall, far below the 90.7% upper baseline. Chat-finetuned models outperform their base counterparts by +4.5% on average, and adding a situating prompt improves chat models by +3.2% and base models by +1.0%. Chain-of-Thought prompting yields an additional +5.2% average improvement. Performance varies substantially across the 7 task categories: even top models struggle with STAGES (~50.7% for Claude 3 Opus) and ANTI-IMITATION (~9.5%), while FACTS reaches 63.2%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares SAD performance against MMLU (general knowledge) and LMSYS Arena (human preference) to test whether situational awareness is an independent axis of capability. The correlation between SAD and MMLU is 0.76, and between SAD and LMSYS Arena is 0.87, indicating partial but not full overlap. Critically, models with nearly identical MMLU scores (~70%)—Llama-2-70b, GPT-3.5-Turbo, and Claude-Instant-1.2—show SAD scores spanning 31.6% to 42.7%, a 11-point gap that MMLU cannot explain. This demonstrates that SAD captures abilities (self-knowledge, introspection, anti-imitation) that are distinct from general knowledge and reasoning. Among the 7 SAD categories, FACTS correlates most strongly with MMLU (0.80), while ANTI-IMITATION shows a negative correlation (−0.20), further confirming that situational awareness is a multi-faceted construct not reducible to a single general-capability metric.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper operationalizes the conceptual definition of situational awareness for LLMs introduced by Berglund et al. (2023) and Ngo et al. (2023) into the first large-scale, multi-category behavioral benchmark. It incorporates two FACTS tasks adapted from Perez et al. (2022), who explored simple self-knowledge questions, but extends the scope dramatically with six entirely new task categories (INFLUENCE, INTROSPECT, STAGES, SELF-RECOGNITION, ID-LEVERAGE, ANTI-IMITATION) that test inference, action, and output-control dimensions absent from prior work. The paper also draws on the broader LLM evaluation benchmark tradition (MMLU, BigBench, TruthfulQA) for methodology but targets a qualitatively different capability axis.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SAD provides the field with a quantitative, decomposable measure of a capability that is central to both the benefits (autonomous planning) and risks (evaluation-aware deception, strategic behavior) of increasingly agentic LLMs. The finding that SAD scores are only partially predicted by MMLU—models with ~70% MMLU span 31–43% SAD—suggests situational awareness is a distinct axis of capability that current capability benchmarks miss. Key open questions include whether SAD performance predicts complex agentic behavior, whether the benchmark will saturate as models improve, and how it generalizes to multimodal or tool-using settings. The paper explicitly cautions against naively maximizing SAD scores given the associated safety risks.
