## Problem

Identifying the parameters of governing equations for collective behavior from time-evolving point clouds is inherently difficult because it traditionally requires tracking individual trajectories across observations, which becomes intractable as the number of entities grows. Prior topological approaches circumvent this by summarizing entire observation sequences into a single representation, but in doing so they discard the temporal dynamics that carry critical information about the underlying interaction laws. The paper asks whether one can instead learn a continuous latent dynamical model from per-time-point topological summaries and use it for parameter regression.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241049/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a sequence of 3D point clouds (M=200 points each) observed at possibly non-equidistant time points. Each point cloud is preprocessed via Vietoris-Rips persistent homology up to H₂, with resulting persistence diagrams vectorized using 20 exponential structure elements per diagram (yielding a 3.20-dimensional vector per homology dimension per time point); optionally, PointNet++ features are concatenated. The output is a small set of continuous parameters (2–4 values) specifying the governing equation (D'Orsogna, Vicsek, or volume exclusion model) that generated the observed dynamics.

## Method

The core architecture is a latent ODE in a variational Bayes framework: an mTAN attention-based encoder maps the sequence of vectorized persistence diagrams to an approximate posterior over an initial latent state z_{t₀} ∈ ℝ²⁰, a neural ODE integrates z forward in time, and a two-layer MLP decoder reconstructs the vectorized diagrams at observed time points. Training maximizes the ELBO (reconstruction + KL) augmented with an auxiliary MSE regression loss. A separate mTAN-based regression network attends over the latent path sampled at equidistant time points and linearly maps the summary to the governing-equation parameters. The modeling choice is theoretically justified by a stability chain (Eq. 3) bounding the distance between vectorized persistence diagrams by the point-set Wasserstein distance, ensuring that small temporal changes in the point clouds induce only small changes in the vectorized features, making a continuous latent ODE a sound generative model.

## Results

Neural Persistence Dynamics (joint variant v3) achieves the best parameter regression performance across all four collective-behavior datasets, with VE of 0.931 and SMAPE of 0.067 on dorsogna-1k, 0.689/0.088 on dorsogna-10k, 0.576/0.144 on vicsek-10k, and 0.871/0.081 on volex-10k. The PH-only variant (v1) is competitive with the joint variant on vicsek-10k and volex-10k (e.g., VE 0.869 vs. 0.871 on volex-10k), confirming that topological features alone carry most of the predictive signal. All results are averaged over 5 train/test splits and three observation-sampling fractions (20%, 50%, 80%), with the model trained using identical hyperparameters across all datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms both baselines—Path Signature Kernel (PSK) [23] and Crocker Stacks [57]—on every dataset and every metric. Against PSK, the gains are most pronounced on volex-10k (VE 0.871 vs. 0.509; SMAPE 0.081 vs. 0.190) and dorsogna-10k (VE 0.689 vs. 0.647), while on dorsogna-1k the gap is smaller but still significant (VE 0.931 vs. 0.828). Against Crocker Stacks, the advantage is even more dramatic: on volex-10k, Crocker Stacks essentially fails (VE 0.076, SMAPE 0.292), and on dorsogna-10k it achieves only VE 0.343 versus 0.689 for the proposed method. Notably, the PSK and Crocker Stacks baselines are evaluated under their best-case setting (all time points observed, with extensive hyperparameter tuning via cross-validation), whereas the proposed method is evaluated under a more challenging protocol that includes subsampled and non-equidistant observations, making the performance gap even more striking. The PH-only variant (v1) already surpasses both baselines on all four datasets, indicating that the topological representation itself is the primary driver of the improvement rather than the addition of PointNet++ features or the latent ODE dynamics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper combines three threads: (1) stable vectorization of persistence diagrams (Hofer et al., JMLR 2019), (2) latent ODEs for irregularly-sampled time series (Rubanova et al., NeurIPS 2019), and (3) attention-based encoders for temporal data (mTAN, Shukla & Marlin, ICLR 2021). It departs from prior topological approaches to collective behavior (crocker plots/stacks, path signature kernels) that collapse an entire observation sequence into a single summary, instead modeling the per-time-point dynamics of vectorized persistence diagrams in a continuous latent space. The stability chain (Eq. 3) linking point-set Wasserstein distance to vectorized diagram distance provides the theoretical justification for treating the sequence of vectorizations as a smooth trajectory amenable to ODE modeling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The approach substantially outperforms prior topological methods (PSK, crocker stacks) across four synthetic collective-behavior datasets, with particularly large gaps on the volume-exclusion model where crocker stacks achieve near-zero variance explained. The ablation revealing strong complementarity between PH and PointNet++ representations (VE 0.931 vs. 0.851/0.816 individually on dorsogna-1k) suggests that topological and geometric features capture genuinely different aspects of collective dynamics. Key limitations include the prohibitive cost of Vietoris-Rips PH (especially H₂) beyond ~200 points, the fixed-cardinality assumption in the stability argument, and evaluation exclusively on synthetic data; extending to Alpha/Witness complexes and variable-cardinality settings are natural next steps.
