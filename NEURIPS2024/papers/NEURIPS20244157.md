## Problem

Existing parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) methods such as LoRA inject low-rank additive matrices into pretrained weights but ignore the spectral structure (singular vectors) that encodes important geometric information about the pretrained model. This paper asks whether explicitly tuning the top singular vectors of pretrained weight matrices—rather than adding an independent low-rank perturbation—can improve fine-tuning quality, parameter efficiency, and multi-adapter fusion.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20244157/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pretrained weight matrix W ∈ R^{n×m}, computes its SVD W = USV^T, and then fine-tunes only the top-r columns of U and V (with r typically 4–32), producing a fine-tuned weight matrix that can be merged back into the original for zero-latency inference. For diffusion model experiments, the fine-tuned model additionally consumes text prompts and optional sketch/pose conditions (via T2I-Adapter) to generate 512×512 RGB images. For language model experiments, the fine-tuned model produces text completions evaluated on benchmarks like GSM8K and GLUE.

## Method

The paper proposes two spectral adaptation mechanisms that operate on the SVD of pretrained weights. Spectral Adapter^A additively updates the top-r singular vector columns: [U₁ + A_U·U₂] S [V₁ + A_V·V₂], where A_U and A_V are trainable matrices initialized at zero. Spectral Adapter^R orthogonally rotates the top-r columns via trainable orthogonal matrices R_U, R_V constructed through Cayley parameterization R = (I+Q)(I−Q)⁻¹, which exactly preserves the SVD structure and enables sequential fine-tuning without recomputation. Theoretically, Spectral Adapter^A achieves twice the rank capacity of LoRA for an equal trainable parameter budget (Lemma 3.1), and a subspace alignment analysis shows the top singular direction of pretrained weights identifies the ideal neuron subspace more robustly than random or bottom directions. For multi-adapter fusion, different concepts are assigned to non-overlapping column blocks of the singular vector matrices, naturally preventing identity loss and concept binding that plague naive LoRA merging.

## Results

On GLUE benchmarks with DeBERTaV3-base, Spectral Adapter^A achieves the highest average score of 88.03% with only 0.72% trainable parameters, outperforming AdaLoRA (87.10%, 1.07% params), DoRA (86.57%), LoRA (86.47%), and OFT (86.47%). On GSM8K with Mistral 7B, Spectral Adapter^A reaches 49.73±1.38 accuracy, surpassing LoRA (44.81±1.37) and DoRA (43.82±1.37) by a large margin while using the same 0.16% trainable parameters. For diffusion model fine-tuning, Spectral Adapter^R achieves recognizable custom concept generation with as few as 20k trainable parameters, compared to 200k+ for LoRA, OFT, and LiDB.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Spectral Adapter^A consistently outperforms all additive PEFT baselines: it beats LoRA by +1.56 avg on GLUE and +4.92 on GSM8K, DoRA by +1.46 on GLUE and +5.91 on GSM8K, and AdaLoRA by +0.93 on GLUE while using 32% fewer trainable parameters (0.72% vs 1.07%). Against OFT, Spectral^A gains +1.56 on GLUE average, with particularly large improvements on CoLA (+2.69) and RTE (+4.69). For multi-adapter fusion in diffusion models, Spectral^A outperforms Gradient Fusion, Orthogonal Adaptation, and FedAvg by preserving individual concept identity more faithfully when merging adapters for multiple objects or characters. On parameter efficiency for diffusion fine-tuning, Spectral^R dominates SVDiff (fixed ~100k budget), VeRA (requires >300k for reasonable quality), LiDB (competitive but coarser granularity), and OFT (requires >400k), achieving recognizable concept generation at just 20k parameters with finer-grained budget choices down to 1k.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the PEFT landscape by operating directly in the spectral decomposition of pretrained weights rather than in the original parameter space. Spectral Adapter^A is conceptually an additive method like LoRA but applied to the top singular vectors of the SVD, while Spectral Adapter^R is a rotational method analogous to OFT but restricted to the top-r spectral subspace. The paper departs from SVDiff, which tunes singular values (magnitudes) of flattened CNN weights, by instead tuning singular vectors (directions) in a layer-agnostic and rank-flexible manner. The theoretical rank-capacity analysis positions Spectral Adapter^A as having twice the expressiveness of LoRA under an equal parameter budget, and the connection to DoRA's magnitude-direction decomposition is made explicit for vector-form weights.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The spectral adapter offers a principled, drop-in upgrade to existing PEFT pipelines with negligible inference overhead, and the multi-adapter fusion application (distributing concepts across non-overlapping spectral columns) provides an elegant solution to a practical bottleneck in diffusion model customization. The parameter efficiency of Spectral Adapter^R—requiring only r×r trainable matrices versus n×r for LoRA—becomes increasingly attractive as model dimensions grow. Key open questions include whether the 'top spectral space' heuristic generalizes beyond the simplified two-layer theoretical setting, how SVD cost scales for frontier-scale models, and whether component-specific spectral tuning (e.g., attention vs. FFN layers) yields further gains.
