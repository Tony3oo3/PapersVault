## Problem

STE-based 2:4 sparse pre-training methods (SR-STE, STEP, Bi-Mask) rely on a discontinuous hard-thresholding pruning function, which causes three optimization pathologies: incorrect descending direction, inability to predict the amount of descent, and persistent sparse mask oscillation. These issues create a significant accuracy gap versus dense training and make N:M sparsity impractical for accelerating large-scale pre-training.

## Input / Output

The method is applied to the two linear layers in the feed-forward network of each transformer block in GPT-2 (124M/350M/774M), DeiT (tiny/small), and Transformer-base. Internally, a dense weight matrix is maintained and updated via STE gradients, while a 2:4-sparse projection of it is used for all forward and backward matrix multiplications. The sparse weights are the ones deployed at inference, so the output (next-token logits, class scores, or translation probabilities) is produced by a genuinely sparse network rather than a dense one.

## Method

S-STE replaces the discontinuous hard-thresholding with a continuous 2:4-specific soft-thresholding: within each 4-element block, the two largest-magnitude elements are retained after subtracting the second-largest magnitude (t = |t₂|), while the two smallest are zeroed, yielding a projection that is continuous everywhere yet always produces exactly 2 non-zeros per 4-element group. A per-tensor scaling factor β = wᵀS_soft(w) / ‖S_soft(w)‖² is computed once at initialization to minimize the MSE between the dense and sparse weights, avoiding the instability of dynamic rescaling. For backward-pass acceleration, minimum-variance unbiased estimation (MVUE) is applied only to the activation gradient ∇Zᵀ (not to S(W)ᵀ, which would accumulate errors), and the entire pipeline runs in FP8 (e3m4 forward, e5m2 backward) for an additional 2× GEMM speedup, yielding a theoretical 3× end-to-end acceleration over dense FP16 training.

## Results

On GPT-2 774M, S-STE achieves SQuAD EM 75.5 / F1 85.5 and GLUE average 78.6, surpassing the dense baseline (74.3/84.9, 76.2) and the strongest sparse baseline T-SR-STE+DF (74.3/84.6, 77.1) by +1.2 EM, +0.9 F1, and +1.5 GLUE points respectively. On Transformer-base (WMT 14 En-De), S-STE reaches 26.11 test BLEU, improving over SR-STE (25.84) and STEP (25.52) while remaining within 0.31 of the dense baseline (26.42). On DeiT-small (ImageNet-1K), S-STE attains 78.5% top-1 accuracy, outperforming Bi-Mask (77.6%) and SR-STE (75.7%) with only a 1.4-point gap to the dense model (79.9%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

S-STE consistently outperforms all four baselines across every task and model size tested. Against SR-STE [52] (the original hard-thresholding + regularization method), S-STE gains +0.27 test BLEU on Transformer-base, +2.8% top-1 on DeiT-small, and +1.5 GLUE / +1.2 SQuAD EM on GPT-2 774M. Against the stronger T-SR-STE+DF [20] (which adds dense fine-tuning in the last 1/6 of training), S-STE still leads by +1.5 GLUE and +1.2 EM on GPT-2 774M, and matches or exceeds it on 124M and 350M. Against Bi-Mask [51] on DeiT-small, S-STE improves top-1 accuracy by 0.9 points (78.5 vs. 77.6). Against STEP [28] on Transformer-base, S-STE gains +0.59 test BLEU (26.11 vs. 25.52). Notably, S-STE is the only sparse method that matches or exceeds the dense baseline on GPT-2 774M (78.6 vs. 76.2 GLUE), while all other sparse baselines remain below dense on at least one metric.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the SR-STE framework (Zhou et al., ICLR 2021) for N:M sparse pre-training, which maintains a dense weight vector and applies hard-thresholding each iteration. The key conceptual departure is identifying the *discontinuity* of the hard-thresholding function (rather than mere non-differentiability) as the root cause of optimization failure, and replacing it with a continuous soft-thresholding projection adapted from Vanderschueren & Vleeschouwer (2022). Unlike SR-STE, which patches the discontinuity with a regularization hyperparameter, or STEP/Bi-Mask, which add preconditioning or bidirectional masks, S-STE eliminates the discontinuity at the source, requiring no additional tuning parameters. It also incorporates MVUE (Chmiel et al., ICLR 2023) on activation gradients and FP8 quantization for practical acceleration.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

2:4 sparsity is natively supported on Nvidia Ampere and Hopper GPUs, yet has not been widely adopted for pre-training due to persistent accuracy gaps; S-STE narrows this gap substantially (e.g., 78.5% vs. 79.9% dense on ImageNet, 78.6 vs. 76.2 GLUE on GPT-2 774M) without introducing hyperparameters, making it a more practical drop-in replacement. However, the method is currently limited to FFN linear layers and does not address QKV projection layers, which would be needed for full-network sparsification. Additionally, the hardware acceleration story remains incomplete: cuSPARSElt on H100 achieves only ~1900 TFLOPS for 2:4-spMM versus 2000 TFLOPS for dense GEMM, negating the theoretical 2× speedup. Open questions include whether alternative smoother pruning functions could further reduce the residual accuracy gap, and whether the approach extends to attention mechanisms.
