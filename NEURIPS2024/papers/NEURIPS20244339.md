## Problem

Despite data curation being central to model performance, there is no large-scale, systematic benchmark for comparing different curation strategies (expert labeling, crowdsourcing, synthetic generation, embedding-based search) on their utility for image classification. Prior work like DataComp focused on filtering a fixed pool with a fixed CLIP architecture, leaving open how diverse curation strategies—spanning natural, synthetic, and embedding-retrieved data—compare under a unified evaluation protocol with both utility and analytic metrics.

## Input / Output

The benchmark takes as input a curated dataset (a 'shift') of RGB images paired with ImageNet-1K class labels, where the model architecture (ResNet-50), training protocol, and 1000-class label set are held fixed while only the data source and curation strategy vary. Outputs are two families of metrics: utility metrics (ImageNet-Val accuracy, average natural/synthetic OOD robustness across six shifts, average VTAB fine-tuning accuracy over 11 downstream tasks, and average DINO kNN SSL accuracy at four samples-per-class settings) and model-free analytic metrics (class coverage, long-tailedness and left-skewedness imbalance scores, CLIPScore/CLIP-IQA/Inception/CMMD quality scores, and Pearson correlations between per-class accuracy and class count).

## Method

The paper introduces SELECT, a benchmark that evaluates curation strategies along two axes—utility (requiring model training) and analytic metrics (model-free)—and ImageNet++, a dataset of five ImageNet-1K shifts each built with a distinct curation strategy: OI1000 (crowdsourced OpenImages), LA1000 img2img (CLIP image-embedding nearest-neighbor search over LAION-5B), LA1000 txt2img (CLIP text-embedding search), SD1000 img2img (Stable Diffusion inversion from ImageNet images), and SD1000 txt2img (Stable Diffusion from class-name prompts). Over 130 ResNet-50 models are trained under identical protocols (600 epochs, mixed precision, grid-searched learning rates, optional class-balanced weighted loss) and evaluated on the full metric suite. Key findings include: no reduced-cost strategy matches expert-labeled ImageNet on most utility metrics; embedding-based search (LA1000 img2img) is the strongest reduced-cost method despite being the smallest dataset; img2img consistently outperforms txt2img; and class imbalance (particularly long-tailedness) is the dominant factor degrading utility in crowdsourced and embedding-retrieved shifts, with a simple weighted-loss intervention recovering much of the lost robustness.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K validation, the expert-curated IN1000 baseline achieves 77.9% top-1 accuracy, substantially outperforming all reduced-cost curation strategies. The best reduced-cost method, LA1000(img2img) (embedding-based image-to-image search, 0.8M samples), reaches 59.6% base accuracy, 23.2% average natural robustness, and 41.1% average VTAB fine-tuning accuracy. Notably, OI1000 (crowdsourced, 1.2M) achieves the highest VTAB score (44.1%) despite only 42.3% base accuracy, while both Stable Diffusion shifts (SD1000) perform worst across nearly all metrics (23.7–25.2% base accuracy). The smallest dataset (LA1000 img2img, 0.8M) ranks second or third on every utility metric, demonstrating that dataset size is not a reliable predictor of curation quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares six curation strategies: Expert (IN1000), Crowdsourced (OI1000), Synthetic img2img (SD1000 img2img), Synthetic txt2img (SD1000 txt2img), Embedding img2img (LA1000 img2img), and Embedding txt2img (LA1000 txt2img). Expert curation (IN1000) dominates on base accuracy (77.9% vs. 59.6% for the best alternative) and all robustness metrics, confirming it remains the gold standard. Among reduced-cost methods, embedding-based image-to-image search (LA1000 img2img) consistently outperforms all others on base accuracy and robustness, despite being the smallest dataset at 0.8M samples. Surprisingly, the expensive crowdsourced OI1000 shift achieves the best VTAB fine-tuning score (44.1%) even surpassing IN1000 (41.2%), attributed to its diverse real-world imagery despite severe class imbalance (73.4% long-tailedness). Both Stable Diffusion shifts are the weakest performers across nearly all metrics, with SD1000(txt2img) scoring only 23.7% base accuracy. Across both LAION and SD families, img2img strategies outperform txt2img strategies on most metrics, reinforcing that conditioning on image embeddings rather than text prompts yields better curation quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the DataComp benchmark (NeurIPS 2023), which fixed model architecture and loss to compare data filtration strategies, by broadening the scope from filtering to the full space of data curation strategies including synthetic generation (Stable Diffusion), embedding-based search (CLIP img2img/txt2img), and crowdsourced labeling. It builds on prior ImageNet distribution shift work (LAIONet, synthetic ImageNet clones) but creates the largest superset of ImageNet-1K to date (ImageNet++) with five distinct shifts. The formalization of curation as a rational-choice utility function is a new conceptual framing, and the benchmark incorporates both utility metrics (accuracy, OOD robustness, VTAB fine-tuning, DINO-guided kNN) and analytic metrics (imbalance, quality scores, correlations) that prior work did not jointly report.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SELECT provides the first systematic, large-scale comparison of six curation strategies under identical training protocols (130+ models), giving practitioners concrete guidance on when reduced-cost methods are competitive and when they are not. The finding that class imbalance (long-tailedness), not dataset size or image quality, is the primary limiting factor for cost-efficient curation reframes where future effort should be directed. Key limitations include coverage of only one task (image classification), one label set (ImageNet-1K), and a coarse binary cost model; open questions include whether findings generalize to other domains, whether better quality metrics can predict curation utility, and whether label-noise correction can close the remaining gap to expert curation.
