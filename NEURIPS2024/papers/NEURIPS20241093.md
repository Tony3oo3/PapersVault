## Problem

Existing neuron description methods for CV models (MILAN, INVERT, CLIP-Dissect, FALCON) each define their own optimization criteria and evaluation metrics, making it impossible to perform unified, quantitative cross-comparisons of explanation quality. There is no general-purpose framework that can automatically assess whether a given textual explanation accurately captures the concept a neuron has learned to detect, without relying on ground-truth labels or human annotation.

## Input / Output

Given a target neuron f_i in any CV model, a textual explanation s (from any explanation method), and a control dataset of natural validation images, CoSy produces two scalar quality scores: AUC (non-parametric, 0–1, measuring separability of synthetic vs. control activations) and MAD (parametric, measuring mean activation difference normalized by control standard deviation). For multi-dimensional (convolutional) neurons, activations are aggregated via average pooling over spatial dimensions before scoring. The number of synthetic images m and the control set size n are tunable parameters, and the framework requires no ground-truth labels for the evaluated neuron.

## Method

CoSy is a three-step, architecture-agnostic evaluation pipeline that leverages text-to-image generative models to bridge the gap between textual explanations and visual neuron responses. Step 1: a text-to-image model (SDXL by default) synthesizes m images conditioned on the textual explanation s, producing a synthetic set X_1. Step 2: the target neuron's activations are collected on both the synthetic set X_1 and a control set X_0 of natural validation images, with spatial aggregation via average pooling for convolutional neurons. Step 3: the explanation is scored by comparing the two activation distributions using AUC (the probability that a random synthetic activation exceeds a random control activation) and MAD (the normalized mean difference). The key insight is that a faithful explanation should cause the neuron to respond more strongly to its own generated images than to unrelated control images, enabling quantitative, label-free evaluation. Sanity checks validate the framework by confirming high scores for ground-truth class explanations and near-random scores for random explanations across ResNet18, DenseNet161, GoogLeNet, and ViT-B/16.

## Results

Across six models on ImageNet and Places365, INVERT achieves the highest AUC scores in most settings (e.g., 0.96 on ResNet50/ImageNet, 0.94 on ResNet50/Places365), while CLIP-Dissect achieves the highest MAD scores for CNNs (e.g., 4.98 on ResNet50/ImageNet, 3.73 on ResNet50/Places365). MILAN consistently underperforms with AUC below 0.65 across all tasks, indicating near-random explanation quality. In the layer-wise analysis on ResNet18, all methods show declining AUC and MAD scores from the output layer down to layer 1, with MILAN and FALCON dropping to AUC ≈ 0.5 (random) in lower layers.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoSY evaluates four explanation methods rather than proposing a competing method, so the comparison reveals which existing methods produce the highest-quality textual descriptions. INVERT (Bykov et al., NeurIPS 2023) achieves the best AUC in 5 of 6 model-dataset settings (e.g., 0.96 on ResNet50/ImageNet vs. CLIP-Dissect's 0.95), though this is partly expected since INVERT optimizes AUC during explanation generation. CLIP-Dissect (Oikarinen & Weng, ICLR 2023) matches or exceeds INVERT on MAD for all CNNs (e.g., 4.98 vs. 4.62 on ResNet50/ImageNet) and achieves the highest AUC on DINO ViT-S/8 (0.95 vs. 0.73). MILAN (Hernandez et al., ICLR 2022) performs worst across all settings, with AUC ranging from 0.44 to 0.65—close to the 0.5 random baseline—attributed to its tendency to generate overly abstract explanations like 'white areas' or 'nothing.' FALCON (Kalibhat et al., ICML 2023), evaluated only in the layer-wise ResNet18 experiment, also shows AUC near 0.5 in lower layers, confirming that both MILAN and FALCON produce explanations that are essentially uninformative for describing neuron function.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by repurposing text-to-image generative models (SDXL, Stable Cascade) as an evaluation instrument for neuron interpretability, a use case entirely outside their original design purpose. It departs from the lineage of neuron description methods (NetDissect, CompExp, MILAN, FALCON, CLIP-Dissect, INVERT), each of which defined its own optimization objective (IoU, WPMI, AUC, CLIP score) and thus its own ad-hoc evaluation criterion, making cross-method comparison impossible. Conceptually, CoSY draws on the activation maximization tradition—testing whether a neuron discriminates concept-related from non-concept-related inputs—but automates the data collection step via generative synthesis rather than manual curation or optimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CoSY provides the first architecture-agnostic, quantitative benchmark for open-vocabulary neuron descriptions, enabling practitioners to make informed choices among explanation methods rather than relying on each method's self-reported metrics. The finding that two published methods (MILAN, FALCON) produce near-random explanations (AUC ≈ 0.5) when properly evaluated raises concerns about conclusions drawn from prior interpretability studies that relied on these methods. Key limitations include dependence on generative model quality for abstract concepts, restriction to CV, and the fact that AUC/MAD capture only one dimension of explanation quality (activation alignment) rather than semantic faithfulness or human plausibility. Open questions include extending the framework to NLP and healthcare, incorporating human-in-the-loop assessment, and evaluating LLM-generated neuron explanations.
