## Problem

Optimizing neural network wavefunctions in Quantum Variational Monte Carlo (QVMC) suffers from slow convergence, and while imaginary-time Schrödinger evolution provides a principled path to the ground state, existing discretization approaches either approximate updates in parametric space (limited by non-convexity) or project evolved states using quantum fidelity/Fubini-Study metrics with complex mathematical properties that do not account for finite step size. This paper asks whether one can discretize the evolution process itself in non-parametric function space and project back to the neural ansatz in a way that is both theoretically sound at finite time steps and computationally free of overhead.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes a molecular Hamiltonian (kinetic + potential terms under Born-Oppenheimer approximation), a real-valued neural wavefunction ansatz ψ_θ: R^{3N}→R, and a learning rate schedule {η_t}. At each iteration it draws MCMC samples from |ψ_θ|², computes local energies E_L(x), and updates parameters via a KFAC-preconditioned natural gradient where the per-sample coefficient is c^(i) = η_t E_L(x^(i)) − ½η_t² E_L²(x^(i)) rather than the standard η_t E_L(x^(i)). The output is the optimized wavefunction and its induced probability distribution, from which ground-state energies and physical observables are estimated over 20,000 MCMC evaluation steps.

## Method

Q²VMC discretizes the imaginary-time Schrödinger evolution as ψ^(n+1) = (1 − τE_L)ψ^(n)/(1 − τE₀), which provably converges to the ground state for any finite τ > 0 (unlike continuous-time methods requiring infinitesimal steps). The evolved distribution (1 − τE_L)²ψ_θ² is then projected back onto the parametric manifold by minimizing KL divergence within a trust region, yielding a natural-gradient update whose gradient direction is F^{-1}g where g involves the expectation of (1 − τE_L)²∇_θ log ψ_θ². Expanding this expression reveals that the update equals the standard QVMC natural gradient plus an additional quadratic term ½η²E_L²(x) (with τ = η/2), which costs nothing extra since E_L is already computed. The Fisher information preconditioning and the quadratic correction both emerge naturally from the KL projection geometry, and the method exactly recovers QVMC in the τ→0 limit, establishing consistency while delivering roughly 2× faster convergence at finite step sizes.

## Results

Q²VMC achieves lower ground state energies than both Psiformer and LapNet baselines across all six molecules (6–30 electrons), with improvements growing for larger systems. For example, on C₂H₆O (26 e⁻), Q²VMC+Psi reaches −155.04696(3) Ha versus Psiformer's −155.04656(7) Ha, and Q²VMC+Lap reaches −155.04619(4) Ha versus LapNet's −155.04563(6) Ha. The method also achieves approximately 2× faster convergence, reaching target energies in roughly half the iterations of standard QVMC, without additional computational overhead beyond computing the already-available local energy squared.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares Q²VMC against two state-of-the-art attention-based neural ansatzes: Psiformer (von Glehn et al., 2022) and LapNet (Li et al., 2023), each evaluated with and without the quadratic update modification. Q²VMC+Psi outperforms Psiformer on all six molecules, with the energy gap widening from ~0.00004 Ha on Li₂ to ~0.00046 Ha on C₂H₆O and ~0.00046 Ha on C₄H₆. Similarly, Q²VMC+Lap beats LapNet on all six systems, with the largest gains on C₂H₆O (~0.00056 Ha) and C₄H₆ (~0.00090 Ha). The improvements are most pronounced for larger molecules (≥26 electrons) where standard QVMC has not fully converged within the 200k-iteration budget, suggesting the quadratic term is particularly effective when the optimization landscape is still far from the ground state. The method also reduces the variance in batch energies, indicating more stable convergence. Ablation studies confirm that the gains cannot be replicated by simply increasing the QVMC learning rate, as larger learning rates either diverge or plateau below Q²VMC's performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the stochastic reconfiguration / natural gradient framework for QVMC (Sorella 2001, 2005) and the imaginary-time Schrödinger evolution perspective (McArdle 2019, Amara 1993), but departs from prior projection-based approaches (Wasserstein QMC [22], projected quantum evolution [33]) that rely on quantum fidelity or the Fubini-Study metric. The key conceptual departure is projecting the discretely evolved wavefunction onto the parametric manifold using KL divergence on the probability distribution |ψ|², which naturally yields a quadratic correction term to the standard QVMC update. Unlike continuous-time evolution methods that require infinitesimal steps, the discretized evolution here is provably insensitive to time step size, and the quadratic term adds no computational overhead since local energies are already computed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that a single-line code modification to existing QVMC pipelines (Psiformer, LapNet) yields roughly 2× faster convergence and consistently lower ground-state energies across six molecules up to 30 electrons, without hyperparameter retuning. However, the fundamental O(N⁴) scaling with electron count remains unaddressed, limiting applicability to larger systems, and the paper does not yet extend to excited states or relative energies—key applications in quantum chemistry. Open questions include determining the optimal imaginary time step, quantifying projection errors, and whether the speedup advantage persists or grows for larger, more strongly correlated systems.
