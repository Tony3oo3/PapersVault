## Problem

Model Predictive Shielding (MPS) guarantees provable safety in continuous, high-dimensional state spaces by overriding unsafe actions with a backup policy, but this backup policy is task-oblivious—it simply drives the agent to the nearest safe equilibrium (e.g., halting) rather than making task-specific progress. This conservatism inflates recovery regret, hinders the learned policy's exploration, and causes convergence to overly cautious behaviors that underperform. The paper asks whether safety can be maintained while simultaneously optimizing task objectives during the recovery phase.

## Input / Output

The method operates on continuous state vectors in R^n with continuous action spaces in R^m, requiring a deterministic dynamics model and a task-oblivious backup policy that defines the set of recoverable states. The MCTS planner takes the current state and the learned Q-function as inputs and outputs a finite-horizon sequence of recovery actions (length n+1) constrained to remain within recoverable states at every intermediate step. During training, the neural policy is updated via TD3 using a replay buffer that includes both normal transitions and penalty-labeled transitions for shield-triggering actions, enabling the policy to learn from the planner's recovery behavior.

## Method

DMPS replaces the static backup policy in MPS with a dynamic backup policy π*_backup realized by a local MCTS planner that searches over a finite horizon (n=5) for a sequence of actions maximizing a composite objective: the discounted sum of immediate rewards within the horizon plus the Q-function value at the terminal state, subject to the hard constraint that all intermediate states remain recoverable. This creates a synergistic loop: the planner leverages the neural policy's Q-function to account for long-term rewards beyond its short planning horizon, while the neural policy learns from the planner's task-aware recovery actions during training via a replay buffer with negative penalties for shield-triggering actions. The theoretical analysis proves that recovery regret is bounded by O(γ^n) as the number of MCTS iterations m→∞, meaning the planner's recovery actions approach optimality exponentially with horizon depth. Safety is guaranteed both during and after training because all planned actions are constrained to recoverable states, and the fallback to the task-oblivious backup policy (when planning fails) preserves the original MPS safety invariant.

## Results

Across 8 dynamic benchmarks, DMPS invokes the safety shield an average of 24.7 times per episode versus 124.1 for MPS (a 76% reduction), while achieving 29% higher episodic returns after convergence. On the most challenging benchmark (double-gates+, DI dynamics), DMPS achieves a return of 13.0 with 24.2 shield invocations, compared to MPS's -0.9 return with 239.8 invocations. All static benchmarks are solved with zero shield invocations by DMPS, and the SRL baselines (CPO, PPO-Lag, TD3) fail to achieve positive returns on any dynamic benchmark.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against MPS (the closest PSRL baseline), DMPS wins on both safety and performance in dynamic benchmarks: it triggers the shield 76% less frequently (24.7 vs. 124.1 average invocations/episode) and achieves 29% higher returns after convergence, with the largest gap on double-gates+ (DI) where DMPS scores 13.0 vs. MPS's -0.9. The only tie is single-gate (DI), where both achieve 11.6 return. Against REVEL, both DMPS and MPS substantially outperform it on static benchmarks (e.g., obstacle: DMPS 32.7 vs. REVEL -41.6), and REVEL is not applicable to dynamic benchmarks due to its white-box dynamics requirement. Against the SRL baselines (CPO, PPO-Lag, TD3), DMPS dominates on dynamic benchmarks where all three fail to achieve positive returns (ranging from -2.4 to -21.4) while DMPS maintains positive returns of 7.4–13.1; on static benchmarks, the SRL methods match or slightly exceed DMPS in return but with non-zero safety violations (e.g., CPO: 6.42 violations/episode on obstacle2 vs. DMPS's 0 shield invocations).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Model Predictive Shielding (MPS) [Bastani, 2021], which guarantees safety by delegating to a fixed, task-oblivious backup policy when the learned policy proposes unsafe actions. The key departure is replacing that static backup with a dynamic local planner (MCTS) that searches for task-optimal recovery actions over a finite horizon, using the learned Q-function to account for long-term rewards beyond the planning window. The approach also draws on the broader literature integrating planning with RL [Moerland et al., 2020; Schrittwieser et al., 2021] and on prior safe-RL shielding methods [Alshiekh et al., 2018; Anderson et al., 2020], but is the first to use dynamic planning specifically for optimal recovery within the MPS framework. The theoretical contribution—proving that recovery regret decays exponentially with planning horizon depth—has no direct analogue in prior MPS or shielding work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DMPS addresses a practical bottleneck in provably safe RL: conservative backup policies that stall learning by forcing the agent to halt rather than navigate around obstacles. The 76% reduction in shield invocations and 29% higher returns over MPS, combined with the clean exponential-regret bound, make this a meaningful step toward deploying safe RL in high-stakes domains like autonomous driving. Key limitations remain: the approach requires a deterministic world model, and planning overhead grows exponentially with horizon depth (though the Q-function term mitigates the need for very long horizons). Extending DMPS to stochastic dynamics and multi-agent settings, and reducing the computational cost of the planner for real-time deployment, are natural open questions.
