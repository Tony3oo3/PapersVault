## Problem

The optimal time complexity for decentralized stochastic asynchronous optimization—where n workers compute stochastic gradients at heterogeneous speeds and communicate over a directed multigraph with heterogeneous delays—was previously unknown. Existing methods (Minibatch SGD, SWIFT, Asynchronous SGD) either depend linearly on the slowest worker's computation time (making them vulnerable to stragglers) or express convergence in terms of spectral gaps of mixing matrices rather than physical time. This paper closes the gap by proving matching lower and upper bounds and introducing methods that achieve them.

## Input / Output

The method takes a smooth nonconvex objective f with an unbiased stochastic gradient oracle, upper bounds on per-worker computation times {h_i} and pairwise communication times {ρ_{i→j}} on a directed multigraph, and a starting point x⁰. It outputs an ε-stationary point (E[‖∇f(x̄)‖²] ≤ ε) with a worst-case time complexity guarantee expressed through the 'equilibrium time' t*, which encodes the interplay between the fastest subset of workers, the communication topology (via shortest-path distances τ_{i→j}), and the noise-to-signal ratio σ²/ε. Notably, the algorithm does not require knowledge of {h_i} or {τ_{i→j}} at runtime—it automatically adapts to the actual (potentially random and dynamic) speeds.

## Method

Fragile SGD (homogeneous case) designates a pivot worker j* and two spanning trees (one for broadcasting x^k outward, one for routing gradients inward). Each worker asynchronously computes stochastic gradients at the latest received point and atomically accumulates them, then forwards the accumulated vector along the inward spanning tree toward j*. The pivot aggregates gradients until a batch of size S = ⌈σ²/ε⌉ is collected, then performs a gradient step. Slow workers or those far from j* are automatically excluded because their contributions simply never arrive in time. Amelie SGD (heterogeneous case, where each worker i only accesses f_i) extends this by having workers accumulate local gradient averages and propagate scalar counters along the spanning tree; the pivot triggers an all-reduce once the harmonic mean of local batch sizes reaches S/n. The paper proves a lower bound via a 'level game' reduction (workers must flip geometric-distributed coins and share levels across the graph) showing no method can beat the equilibrium time t* up to a log n factor, and that Fragile/Amelie SGD match this bound.

## Results

The paper establishes that Fragile SGD achieves a nearly optimal time complexity of Θ(LΔ/ε · min_j t*(σ²/ε, [h_i], [τ_{i→j}+τ_{j→i}])) for the homogeneous nonconvex decentralized setting, matching the lower bound Ω(1/(log n+1) · LΔ/ε · min_j t*(σ²/ε, [h_i], [τ_{i→j}])) up to a logarithmic factor. For the heterogeneous setting, Amelie SGD achieves the optimal time complexity Θ(LΔ/ε · max{max_{i,j} τ_{i→j}, max_i h_i, σ²/(nε)·(1/n)Σh_i}), matching the corresponding lower bound up to a constant factor. Empirically, on MNIST with 100 workers in a 2D-Mesh (ρ=10s, slow communication), Fragile SGD is substantially faster than Minibatch SGD and achieves better test accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Fragile SGD provably dominates all four baselines in the homogeneous setting. Minibatch SGD and SWIFT both depend linearly on max_i h_i, making them arbitrarily slow when a single worker is a straggler (h_i→∞), whereas Fragile SGD automatically ignores slow workers via the min_k operator in its time complexity. Asynchronous SGD (Even et al., 2024) is shown to be suboptimal even in the special case τ_{i→j}=0 for all i,j, where Fragile SGD recovers the optimal Rennala SGD complexity. In the heterogeneous setting, Amelie SGD outperforms RelaySGD and Gradient Tracking (Vogels et al., 2021; Liu et al., 2024), which depend on max_i h_i in all regimes of σ²/ε, while Amelie SGD's stochastic term depends only on the average (1/n)Σh_i when σ²/ε is large. The paper also notes that Amelie SGD does not require the function-similarity assumption needed by Even et al. (2024) in the heterogeneous case.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on optimal time complexities for parallel stochastic optimization (Rennala SGD, Tyurin & Richtárik 2023) to the decentralized setting where communication costs are non-negligible. It departs from the dominant gossip-matrix framework (Koloskova et al. 2021, Shi et al. 2015) that characterizes convergence via spectral gaps of mixing matrices, instead formulating time complexity in terms of physical computation and communication times on a multigraph. The lower bound construction generalizes the 'level game' technique from Arjevani et al. (2022) and Tyurin & Richtárik (2023) to handle the min-over-workers structure introduced by asynchronous communication, while the upper-bound methods (Fragile SGD, Amelie SGD) draw on spanning-tree relay ideas from RelaySGD (Vogels et al. 2021) and Rennala SGD but with essential algorithmic changes for straggler-robustness and heterogeneous functions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to establish matching (up to log n) lower and upper bounds on time complexity for decentralized stochastic optimization with arbitrary heterogeneous computation and communication speeds, resolving a gap explicitly identified as open in the literature. The practical implication is that optimal decentralized training may require deliberately excluding slow or distant workers rather than using all available resources, and that the relevant performance metric is physical time rather than iteration count dependent on network topology. Limitations include the log n gap in the homogeneous case (attributed to technical difficulties in the lower bound analysis), the requirement of knowing time bounds for optimal pivot/tree selection in Corollary 1, and the fact that the heterogeneous bound (16) is pessimistic, depending on the slowest worker and network diameter. Open questions include closing the log n gap and developing adaptive strategies that estimate optimal pivots without prior knowledge of computation/communication times.
