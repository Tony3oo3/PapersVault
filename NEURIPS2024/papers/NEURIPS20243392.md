## Problem

Discrete diffusion models (D3PMs) for text generation require T neural network evaluations for T sampling steps, with no training-free acceleration method analogous to DDIM in continuous spaces. This paper addresses the under-explored gap of accelerating discrete diffusion sampling without retraining, by exploiting the unique structure of categorical noise in discrete-state diffusion.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243392/cropped_page9_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

For conditional generation (machine translation), the model takes a source sentence encoded via BPE subword tokens and produces a target-language translation; for unconditional generation, it takes a pure noise sample over the vocabulary and produces a text sequence. Each token is a one-hot vector over K categories (27 for text8, 256 for enwik8, or BPE vocabulary size for translation). The key structural detail is that the method pre-samples a transition time set T of cardinality |T| < T, so only |T| steps trigger neural network calls while the remaining steps simply copy the previous state.

## Method

The core mechanism replaces the time-varying noise w_t in the standard Markov forward process with a single fixed noise w, making the process non-Markov while provably preserving the marginal q(x_t|x_0) and conditional q(x_0|x_t). This introduces a per-token transition time τ (the unique step where the token flips from x_0 to noise), and the reverse process becomes fully deterministic given τ: x_{t-1} = 1(τ=t)·x_0 + 1(τ≠t)·x_t. The accelerated algorithm pre-samples the transition time set T = {τ_n} for all N tokens, then only invokes the neural network at steps t ∈ T, reducing NFE from T to |T| (which approaches O(1) as T→∞). The method is training-free, reusing the standard D3PM-trained predictor p_θ, and extends to a continuous-time variant (DNDM-C) that samples transition times from a continuous distribution on [0,1] for infinite-step generation.

## Results

On machine translation (absorbing diffusion, 1000 steps), DNDM-Absorb achieves BLEU 32.93 on IWSLT14 in 161.1s versus RDM-Absorb's 31.91 in 4197.4s (~26× faster with +1.0 BLEU). At 50 steps, DNDM is ~2.4× faster while matching or exceeding RDM's BLEU across all three translation datasets. For unconditional text generation, DNDM reduces perplexity from 1465.75 to 600.02 on text8 (5× faster) and from 801.78 to 556.78 on enwik8 (~13× faster) compared to vanilla multinomial diffusion sampling.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against RDM (Zheng et al., 2023), DNDM consistently wins on both speed and quality across all three machine translation datasets. At 1000 steps on IWSLT14 (absorbing diffusion), DNDM-Absorb achieves BLEU 32.93 in 161.1s versus RDM-Absorb's 31.91 in 4197.4s — a ~26× speedup with +1.0 BLEU. The speedup grows with step count: ~2.4× at 50 steps, ~26× at 1000 steps, and the infinite-step DNDM-C variant (174.6s) still outperforms RDM at 1000 steps. On unconditional text generation, DNDM outperforms vanilla multinomial diffusion (Hoogeboom et al., 2021b) on both perplexity and speed: 600.02 vs. 1465.75 perplexity on text8 (5× faster) and 556.78 vs. 801.78 on enwik8 (~13× faster). The top-k variant (DNDM-k) further improves BLEU by ~1–2 points over the base DNDM at negligible additional cost, and consistently outperforms RDM-k as well.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper draws a direct structural analogy between DDIM (Song et al., 2020a) in continuous spaces and a new non-Markov construction for discrete diffusion: by making the noise time-invariant (w instead of w_t), the forward process becomes non-Markov and naturally induces a 'transition time' for each token, from which a predetermined set of steps requiring neural network evaluation can be extracted. The training objective is inherited from the reparameterized loss of Zheng et al. (2023) (RDM), and the method accelerates both multinomial diffusion (Hoogeboom et al., 2021b) and absorbing diffusion (Austin et al., 2021) without any retraining. The paper explicitly positions DNDM as the discrete counterpart of DDIM (Table 1), while distinguishing itself from DDIM's discrete extension by achieving full de-randomization via the transition time set rather than residual stochasticity at each step.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free nature of DNDM means practitioners can immediately accelerate existing pretrained discrete diffusion models, achieving ~3× speedup at T=50 and ~30× at T=1000 while preserving or improving sample quality across machine translation and language modeling benchmarks. The infinite-step algorithm (DNDM-C) offers a principled bridge between discrete and continuous-time formulations for categorical data, though the paper acknowledges that infinite steps do not always outperform 1000 steps due to accumulated estimation error. Key open questions include whether the approach extends to autoregressive architectures, non-text modalities (audio, images), and whether the transition-time framework can be combined with other acceleration techniques (distillation, consistency models) for discrete diffusion.
