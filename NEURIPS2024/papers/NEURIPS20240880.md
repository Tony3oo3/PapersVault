## Problem

Existing neural implicit surface methods (NeuS, VolSDF, HF-NeuS) are limited to reconstructing opaque surfaces, while transparent-object reconstruction methods either handle only pure glass under strong assumptions (known geometry, homogeneous lighting) or neglect opaque objects entirely. No prior method can simultaneously reconstruct both thin transparent and opaque objects from multi-view images without modeling refraction or reflection.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240880/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes calibrated multi-view RGB images (100 views for synthetic Blender scenes; self-captured real-world scenes calibrated with ArUco boards) and produces a single 3D mesh containing both transparent and opaque surfaces. No masks are used during training. The output mesh is a two-layer structure: in regions where opacity α ≤ 0.5, the transparent front and back faces coincide at the non-negative local minimum; where α > 0.5, a single-layer opaque surface sits at the zero iso-surface.

## Method

The key theoretical contribution (Theorem 1) proves that NeuS's sigmoid-based density mapping is unbiased across the full opacity spectrum: for rendered opacity α ≤ 0.5, the maximum color weight aligns with a non-negative local minimum of the learned distance field, while for α > 0.5 it aligns with the zero iso-surface. This means the learned field is a 'mixed SDF and UDF' that cannot be extracted by standard marching cubes at a fixed iso-value. To handle this, the method takes the absolute value of the distance field, converting both surface types into local minima of a UDF, then applies DCUDF's two-stage optimization (300 + 100 epochs with VectorAdam) to project a marching-cubes envelope mesh onto these minima. Training itself uses the unmodified NeuS pipeline (color loss λ₁=1.0, Eikonal loss λ₂=0.1), requiring no architectural changes—only the extraction stage is novel.

## Results

On the 5-scene synthetic benchmark, α-NeuS achieves a mean Chamfer distance (CD) of 5.67×10⁻³, outperforming NeuS with zero iso-surface (23.41×10⁻³) and NeuS with 0.005 iso-surface (7.71×10⁻³). The method achieves 100% surface completeness (no holes) on all synthetic scenes, whereas NeuS with zero iso-surface leaves 20–40% of the ground-truth surface un-reconstructed. On the DTU opaque dataset (Appendix B.3), α-NeuS matches or slightly improves upon vanilla NeuS, confirming that the DCUDF-based extraction does not degrade performance on purely opaque scenes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against NeuS with zero iso-surface extraction, α-NeuS reduces mean CD from 23.41 to 5.67 (×4.1 improvement), primarily because the zero iso-surface completely misses transparent surfaces where the learned distance field has non-negative local minima, leaving 20–40% holes in the reconstructed mesh. Against NeuS with 0.005 iso-surface, α-NeuS still wins on mean CD (5.67 vs. 7.71) because the fixed 0.005 threshold does not correspond to the unbiased surface location, yielding sub-optimal one-way Chamfer distances. Against NeUDF (a UDF-learning baseline), α-NeuS produces sharper, more detailed reconstructions with lower Chamfer distances on synthetic scenes; NeUDF's UDF learning is described as volatile and over-smoothed, requiring additional regularizers that sacrifice fidelity. On the DTU opaque dataset, α-NeuS is essentially equivalent to vanilla NeuS (as expected for purely opaque scenes), with DCUDF extraction sometimes yielding slightly lower Chamfer distances than marching cubes due to more accurate vertex placement.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends NeuS (Wang et al., NeurIPS 2021) by proving its density-to-surface mapping is unbiased not only for opaque objects but across the full opacity spectrum from fully transparent to fully opaque, thereby completing NeuS's theoretical framework. For mesh extraction, it adapts DCUDF (Hou et al., ACM TOG 2023) by operating on the absolute value of the learned mixed SDF/UDF, enabling simultaneous extraction of non-negative local minima (transparent surfaces) and zero iso-surfaces (opaque surfaces). Prior transparent-object reconstruction methods (ReNeuS, NeuS-HSR, TransPIR, NeTO) either reconstruct only opaque objects behind glass, assume known material parameters, or focus exclusively on pure glass with refraction/reflection; none simultaneously recover both transparent and opaque geometry without such assumptions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The theoretical result that NeuS is unbiased for all opacities is a clean completion of a framework that had a gap, and the practical consequence—that taking the absolute distance field converts the mixed SDF/UDF into a pure UDF amenable to DCUDF extraction—is an elegant and reusable technique. However, the method is limited to thin transparent objects and cannot handle refraction or reflection, which are common in real glass; the benchmark (5 synthetic + 5 real scenes) is small and lacks error bars, limiting confidence in generalization. Open questions include whether the approach extends to thicker transparent objects, how it interacts with reflection/refraction-aware backbones like Ref-NeuS or ReNeuS, and whether the DCUDF extraction step can be made differentiable for end-to-end optimization.
