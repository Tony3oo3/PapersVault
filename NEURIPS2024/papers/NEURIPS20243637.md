## Problem

Conventional post-training quantization methods for large vision-language models sequentially search layer-wise rounding functions by minimizing per-layer activation discretization errors, ignoring the cross-layer dependency that causes quantization noise to accumulate and propagate across layers. This greedy strategy yields suboptimal rounding functions, leading to significant performance degradation especially at low bitwidths (e.g., W4A4), while directly solving the joint optimization over all layers is NP-hard due to the exponentially growing search space.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained LVLM (LLaVA-7B/13B, MoE-LLaVA-1.6B, or OpenFlamingo-3B) and a small calibration set of 64 vision-language pairs (batch size 8) to search rounding functions with frozen network parameters. It produces a uniformly quantized LVLM at W6A6 or W4A4 bitwidths, where text-modal activations use per-channel quantization and vision-modal activations use per-token quantization. The quantized model then accepts an RGB image plus a text question and generates a text response for multi-modal reasoning tasks such as visual question answering.

## Method

Q-VLM mines cross-layer dependency by observing a strong positive correlation (r=0.97) between activation entropy and the discretization error difference (DED) between layer-wise and joint rounding function search, and uses entropy as a low-cost proxy to partition the model into blocks where inter-layer dependency exceeds a threshold h₀. Within each block (max 3 layers), rounding functions are jointly optimized to minimize the block-output discretization error rather than per-layer error, achieving a trade-off between search cost and quantization accuracy. Additionally, the visual encoder is fine-tuned with a composite objective that minimizes Jacobian-weighted activation entropy (to weaken cross-layer dependency and enable finer block partitioning) plus discretization errors of both the visual encoder output and the full LVLM output, further reducing search cost while preserving quantization accuracy.

## Results

On ScienceQA, Q-VLM achieves 79.79% average accuracy under W4A4 for LLaVA-7B (vs. 89.81% full-precision), 80.78% for LLaVA-13B (vs. 90.00% FP), and 54.72% for MoE-LLaVA-1.6B (vs. 62.68% FP). Under W6A6, Q-VLM nearly matches full-precision: 89.34% (LLaVA-7B), 89.70% (LLaVA-13B), and 62.46% (MoE-LLaVA-1.6B). The method compresses memory by 2.78× and increases generation speed by 1.44× on the 13B LLaVA model, with W4A4 inference memory reduced to 9.6 GB (vs. 24.0 GB FP).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Q-VLM consistently outperforms both AWQ and QLoRA across all three model architectures (LLaVA-7B, LLaVA-13B, MoE-LLaVA-1.6B) and both bitwidth settings (W6A6, W4A4) on ScienceQA. Against QLoRA, the gains are +2.26% (LLaVA-7B W4A4: 79.79 vs. 77.53), +2.14% (LLaVA-13B W4A4: 80.78 vs. 78.64), and +1.48% (MoE-LLaVA-1.6B W4A4: 54.72 vs. 53.24). Against AWQ, the advantages are larger under 4-bit: +5.77% (LLaVA-7B), +2.87% (LLaVA-13B), and +1.74% (MoE-LLaVA-1.6B). In the appendix, Q-VLM also beats ZeroQuant-V2 by +1.71% on LLaVA-7B W4A4 (79.79 vs. 78.08) while reducing inference time from 7.3h to 6.1h. The advantage is most pronounced at W4A4, where cross-layer dependency and quantization errors are more impactful for lower-capacity networks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the post-training quantization (PTQ) lineage from large language models (GPTQ, AWQ, QLoRA, SmoothQuant, ZeroQuant) to large vision-language models (LVLMs), where the multi-modal architecture introduces a distinct challenge: cross-layer dependency of discretization errors across the visual encoder, projection layer, and language backbone. Unlike prior PTQ methods that sequentially search layer-wise rounding functions in a greedy manner, Q-VLM partitions the model into blocks guided by an entropy-based proxy for cross-layer dependency, and further optimizes the visual encoder to disentangle this dependency for finer-grained search. The approach builds on QLoRA's quantization initialization and AWQ's weight-channel protection, while departing from the sequential paradigm by jointly optimizing rounding functions within dependency-aware blocks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Q-VLM is the first method to achieve W4A4 quantization for multi-modal LVLMs, compressing a 13B LLaVA model by 2.78× in memory with 1.44× speedup and minimal accuracy loss, which is practically important for deploying LVLMs on resource-constrained devices. The entropy-based proxy for cross-layer dependency offers a low-cost alternative to explicit multi-forward-pass dependency evaluation, making block-wise quantization tractable. However, the authors acknowledge that performance degrades significantly at extremely low bitwidths, and the method's applicability to even larger foundation models or truly embedded deployment (cellphones, wearables) remains an open question. The visual encoder optimization, while effective, is specific to LLaVA-like architectures and its transferability to other multi-modal designs (e.g., cross-attention-based VLMs like OpenFlamingo) is only briefly explored.
