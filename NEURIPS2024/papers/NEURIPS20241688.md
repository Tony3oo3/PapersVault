## Problem

Training large reconstruction models (LRMs) for sparse-view 3D reconstruction has been bottlenecked by the scarcity, cost, and licensing concerns of real-world 3D datasets like Objaverse. This paper asks whether a purely synthesized, semantically-free procedural dataset can replace real 3D data for learning generic 3D reconstruction priors, and whether the resulting model can generalize to real objects.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241688/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes 4 or 8 sparse RGB views (512×512) with known camera poses, which are patchified via shared non-overlapping convolutions, flattened, and concatenated into a single token sequence for a pure self-attention transformer. The output is a set of 3D Gaussian Splatting parameters (position, covariance, opacity, color) that directly represent the reconstructed object and can be rendered or viewed interactively. Training supervision is purely RGB rendering loss with no explicit geometry or depth supervision.

## Method

The core contribution is Zeroverse, a 400K-object procedural dataset built by randomly composing 1–9 primitive shapes (cube, sphere, cylinder, cone, torus) with random affine transforms and textures, then applying three augmentations: height-field displacement for curved surfaces, boolean difference for concavity, and wireframe conversion for thin structures. The model architecture is GS-LRM, a pure transformer that maps concatenated multi-view patch tokens to Gaussian Splatting parameters, trained with RGB rendering loss on 64 A100 GPUs for 80K steps. The key insight is that sparse-view reconstruction relies primarily on local cross-view correspondence rather than global object semantics, so non-semantic synthetic data suffices. The paper further demonstrates that model-data co-design (augmentation mixing ratios, perceptual loss weight, Gaussian scale clipping) is critical for training stability, and that the resulting LRM-Zero generalizes to NeRF-based architectures, real-world benchmarks (GSO, ABO), and generative pipelines (Instant3D, One2345++).

## Results

On the 8-input-view setting at 512 resolution, LRM-Zero (trained entirely on synthetic Zeroverse data) achieves 31.62 PSNR / 0.960 SSIM / 0.039 LPIPS on GSO and 28.71 PSNR / 0.929 SSIM / 0.078 LPIPS on ABO, compared to GS-LRM trained on Objaverse at 33.23 / 0.971 / 0.031 and 30.92 / 0.944 / 0.067 respectively. The gap is approximately 1.1 PSNR on GSO and 1.9 PSNR on ABO, narrowing at 256 resolution (0.78 and 1.84 PSNR). On generalization to OpenIllumination, LRM-Zero actually outperforms GS-LRM (14.44 vs. 14.02 PSNR), and on Zeroverse-test it leads by 1.75 PSNR (28.23 vs. 26.48).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GS-LRM trained on Objaverse (the primary baseline), LRM-Zero trained purely on synthetic Zeroverse data is consistently behind on the standard GSO and ABO benchmarks: the gap is 1.61 PSNR on GSO and 2.21 PSNR on ABO at Res-512, narrowing to 1.12 PSNR and 1.84 PSNR at Res-256. The authors attribute this gap to LRM-Zero's inability to hallucinate invisible regions due to the lack of semantic priors in Zeroverse. However, on generalization to OpenIllumination (a realistic multi-illumination dataset), LRM-Zero actually surpasses GS-LRM by 0.42 PSNR (14.44 vs. 14.02), and on Zeroverse-test it leads by 1.75 PSNR. Against the NeRF-based LRM baseline (NeRF-LRM-Objv), NeRF-LRM-Zero is competitive on GSO (29.33 vs. 29.72 PSNR, a 0.39 gap) but trails more noticeably on ABO (28.96 vs. 30.79 PSNR, a 1.83 gap), suggesting the synthetic-data advantage is more pronounced for the Gaussian Splatting architecture than for NeRF.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the procedural synthesis pipeline from Xu et al. [100] (originally designed for relighting) and the GS-LRM transformer architecture [107] to train a feed-forward 3D reconstruction model entirely on non-semantic, procedurally generated shapes (Zeroverse). It departs from the dominant paradigm of training reconstruction models on large-scale real 3D datasets like Objaverse by demonstrating that local geometric and textural complexity, rather than global object semantics, may suffice for sparse-view reconstruction. The work also connects to the broader synthetic-data literature in CV (depth estimation, optical flow) but is the first to apply procedural synthesis at the scale needed for large reconstruction models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a proof-of-concept that 3D reconstruction can be learned without real-world semantic data, which has implications for data licensing, bias reduction, and the separation of generation and reconstruction in 3D pipelines. However, scalability remains unresolved (larger models underperform, and the optimal training recipe for synthetic data differs from real data), and the model still shows a measurable gap on invisible-region hallucination where semantic priors matter. Open questions include whether this approach extends to scene-level reconstruction, single-view settings, or tasks requiring stronger semantic understanding, and whether the performance gap can be closed with improved procedural complexity or larger models.
