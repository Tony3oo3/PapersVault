## Problem

In multilingual byte-level language models, non-Latin script languages (Indic, Cyrillic) are over-segmented because they require more bytes per character, leading to longer sequences, higher compute costs, and degraded downstream performance. Previous gradient-based tokenization methods like Dynamic Token Pooling apply a single uniform compression rate across all languages, which still fails to equalize segmentation granularity across scripts. The paper asks whether adaptive, script-specific compression can close this gap while maintaining modeling quality.

## Input / Output

The model consumes raw UTF-8 byte sequences with a unique script identifier prepended to the front, which routes the sequence to the appropriate language-script-specific boundary predictor. Internally, the boundary predictor outputs a binary segmentation (segment boundaries between byte positions), and the full model produces a next-byte probability distribution over the 256-byte vocabulary. The architecture operates at byte-level rather than character-level to avoid the large Unicode codepoint vocabulary problem, and sequences are processed up to a maximum length of 512 bytes.

## Method

MAGNET modifies the hourglass transformer architecture by replacing the single boundary predictor with multiple language-script-specific predictors, each trained with a distinct binomial prior β calibrated to that script's byte-to-word ratio (e.g., β=0.2 for Latin/5×, β=0.1 for Cyrillic/10×, β=0.05 for Indic/20×). The input sequence is tagged with its script and routed through the corresponding predictor, which uses a 2-layer MLP with Gumbel-sigmoid stochastic reparameterization to produce differentiable segment boundaries. Contiguous byte representations within each predicted segment are pooled by averaging, then passed through the middle transformer block, and an upsampling module with skip connections converts the pooled representations back to byte-level predictions. The training objective combines standard cross-entropy language modeling loss with a script-specific binomial regularizer that nudges the number of predicted segments toward the target compression rate for that script, enabling equitable segmentation granularity across languages within a single model.

## Results

On downstream tasks averaged across all languages, the best MAGNET configuration achieves 68.74 on XNLI, 85.41 on PAWS-X, 71.43 on SIB-200, and 44.61 on XQUAD, outperforming DTP 5× (68.16, 81.29, 69.17, 43.31) and DTP 10× (67.31, 75.99, 67.83, 35.83) while remaining competitive with the uncompressed byte-level model (68.68, 82.18, 71.05, 44.62). On dialectal tasks, MAGNET scores 87.25 on HaSCoSVa-2022 and 89.27 on ILI, matching or exceeding all baselines. In terms of segmentation equity, MAGNET (5,10,20)× reduces the average number of tokens for Indic languages by approximately 5× compared to DTP and subword tokenizers, closing the gap with Latin-script languages.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MAGNET consistently outperforms both DTP 5× and DTP 10× across all four in-language downstream tasks. Against DTP 5×, MAGNET gains +0.58 on XNLI, +4.12 on PAWS-X, +2.26 on SIB, and +1.30 on XQUAD; against DTP 10× the margins are even larger (e.g., +9.42 on PAWS-X, +8.78 on XQUAD). Compared to the uncompressed byte-level baseline, MAGNET matches or slightly exceeds performance on XNLI (+0.06), PAWS-X (+3.23), and SIB (+0.38), while being essentially tied on XQUAD (44.61 vs. 44.62), all while achieving 5×–20× sequence compression for non-Latin scripts. On dialectal tasks, MAGNET ties or slightly leads (ILI: 89.27 vs. 89.24 byte-level; HaSCoVa: 87.25 vs. 87.38 byte-level). The key differentiator is segmentation equity: MAGNET reduces Indic-language token counts by ~5× relative to DTP and BPE tokenizers, whereas DTP and subword tokenizers still produce pronounced over-segmentation for non-Latin scripts even with α-sampling.

## Relation to Prior Work

MAGNET builds directly on the hourglass transformer architecture and its gradient-based tokenization mechanism (Nawrot et al., 2022, 2023), which introduced a single boundary predictor trained via stochastic reparameterization to learn subword segments end-to-end. The key departure is replacing that single predictor with multiple language-script-specific predictors, each governed by a script-specific binomial prior calibrated to the script's byte-to-word ratio, thereby enforcing equitable segmentation granularity across scripts within one model. This positions MAGNET as a targeted fix for the over-segmentation disparity documented in prior multilingual tokenization analyses (Ahia et al., 2023; Petrov et al., 2023), while remaining within the tokenizer-free byte-level modeling paradigm initiated by ByT5 and extended by Dynamic Token Pooling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work addresses a concrete and under-addressed fairness gap: non-Latin scripts (Indic, Cyrillic) suffer 2–4× more bytes per character, and prior gradient-based tokenization methods apply a uniform compression rate that perpetuates this disparity. MAGNET's script-specific routing is a simple architectural modification with practical payoff—faster inference and lower compute for non-Latin languages—while maintaining competitive downstream accuracy. Open questions include whether the byte-to-word-ratio proxy generalizes to scripts without space-delimited words (CJK) or with discontinuous morphology (Semitic), whether the ~5% performance drop for Indic languages under high compression persists at scale, and whether the approach transfers to larger models where the relative benefit of compression may shift.
