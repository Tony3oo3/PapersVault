## Problem

Reconstructing high-fidelity 4D representations from single generated videos is hindered by non-rigidity, frame distortion, and unknown camera trajectories that break standard SfM pipelines. Existing dynamic reconstruction methods either degrade geometric accuracy under non-rigid warping or produce flickering textures, and none are designed to handle the soft background deformation and pose ambiguity inherent to AI-generated video.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20244172/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single monocular RGB video (T frames) produced by a text-to-video diffusion model (Vidu) as input, with no ground-truth camera poses or multi-view data. It outputs a 4D Gaussian surfel representation — a set of static 2D Gaussian primitives parameterized by center, rotation, scaling, opacity, and spherical-harmonic appearance — together with a time-varying non-rigid warping field (25 bones, each an SE(3) transform from a continuous MLP) and learned per-frame camera poses. The representation supports differentiable volume rendering of novel-view color, surface normals, and surfel features at any time step.

## Method

Vidu4D operates in two stages. First, a field initialization stage trains a neural SDF with the same bone-based warping structure to jointly estimate camera poses and a continuous warping field, using a cycle-consistency loss to bridge the distribution gap between SDF ray sampling and surfel surface sampling. Second, the Dynamic Gaussian Surfels (DGS) stage optimizes the surfel representation: each static Gaussian surfel is warped to time t via dual-quaternion blend skinning over 25 bones, where each bone's SE(3) transform is a NeRF-style MLP conditioned on local surfel coordinate u and time t, ensuring spatial-temporal continuity. Two key losses differentiate DGS from prior deformable Gaussian methods: (1) a warped-state normal consistency regularization that aligns surfel normals with finite-difference surface normals estimated in the warped state, preserving geometric integrity under non-rigid deformation; and (2) a dual-branch refinement that learns additional rotation (ΔR) and scaling (ΔS) corrections on the surfels in one branch while the other branch remains unrefined, both sharing the same centers and warping functions, which reduces texture flickering and recovers fine-grained appearance details.

## Results

On the average of 30 generated videos, Vidu4D achieves 27.30±2.66 PSNR, 0.9152±0.0602 SSIM, and 0.0877±0.0564 LPIPS, outperforming the strongest baseline SCGS (21.17±2.69 PSNR, 0.8547±0.0691 SSIM, 0.1504±0.0737 LPIPS) by approximately 6.1 PSNR. On individual test videos, Vidu4D scores 24.63 PSNR (Cat), 25.68 PSNR (Cheetah), and 28.58 PSNR (Dragon), consistently leading all baselines. Even when the field initialization component is added to SCGS (24.75 PSNR average), Vidu4D still maintains a ~2.5 PSNR advantage, confirming the contribution of the DGS architecture itself.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Vidu4D outperforms all four baselines across every metric and every test video. Against the NeRF-based methods (BANMo and D-NeRF), Vidu4D leads by over 10 PSNR on average (27.30 vs. 13.62 and 21.01), with substantially lower LPIPS (0.0877 vs. 0.3738 and 0.1522), reflecting far superior perceptual quality. Against the Gaussian splatting-based methods (Deformable-GS and SCGS), Vidu4D achieves ~6.1 PSNR improvement over SCGS (27.30 vs. 21.17) and ~14.1 PSNR over Deformable-GS (27.30 vs. 13.22) on the 30-video average. Notably, even when the paper's field initialization is grafted onto SCGS (yielding 24.75 PSNR), Vidu4D still wins by ~2.5 PSNR, isolating the DGS architecture's contribution. The paper also reports that SCGS and Deformable-GS overfit on the foggy dragon sequence, while Vidu4D maintains stable quality, suggesting better robustness to generated-video artifacts.

## Relation to Prior Work

Vidu4D extends the Gaussian Surfels (2D Gaussian Splatting) framework from static to dynamic scenes by introducing non-rigid warping via bone-based dual-quaternion blend skinning, building directly on the skinning formulation from BANMo and the surface-aligned primitive representation from 2DGS. It departs from prior dynamic Gaussian methods (Deformable-GS, 4DGS) that deform full 3D Gaussians with MLP or triplane fields, instead warping surface-aligned 2D surfels and introducing a warped-state normal regularization that accounts for the continuous deformation field. The field initialization stage, which uses a neural SDF with cycle-consistency to bootstrap camera poses and warping fields before DGS training, is a practical departure from prior monocular 4D methods that rely on SfM or assume known poses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By coupling a video generative model (Vidu) with a dedicated 4D reconstruction pipeline, Vidu4D demonstrates a viable text-to-4D generation pathway that preserves both appearance fidelity and geometric accuracy, opening a route toward controllable dynamic 3D content creation. Key limitations include the ~1-hour training time per scene on an A800 GPU, dependence on the quality and consistency of the input generated video, and scalability challenges for large scenes. Open questions include whether the bone-based skinning formulation generalizes to highly topologically-changing deformations, whether the dual-branch refinement can be made more efficient, and whether the pipeline can operate in real-time for interactive applications.
