## Problem

Bayesian inference over discrete parameter spaces (e.g., phylogenetic trees, DAGs, integer vectors) in a streaming setting is intractable with standard variational inference, which requires either large covariance matrices or mean-field approximations that lose expressivity. GFlowNets can sample from unnormalized discrete posteriors but were designed for static targets, with no mechanism to update the model as new data arrives without retraining from scratch. This paper is the first to enable GFlowNet training in dynamic, streaming environments.

## Input / Output

At each streaming step t+1, the method consumes only the newly observed data chunk D_{t+1} and the previously trained GFlowNet G_t (its forward policy, backward policy, and partition function estimate), without revisiting any past data D_1:t. The output is an updated GFlowNet G_{t+1} whose induced distribution p_T^{(t+1)} approximates the full posterior π_{t+1}(x) ∝ f(D_{t+1}|x)·π_t(x), from which posterior samples over the discrete parameter space (trees, sets, DAGs, or integer vectors) can be drawn. The discrete support can be arbitrarily large (e.g., all rooted binary trees on N labeled leaves), and the likelihood f(D_{t+1}|x) is evaluated only on the new chunk.

## Method

SB-GFlowNet replaces the intractable full-posterior evaluation with a streaming target f(D_{t+1}|x)·p_T^{(t)}(x), where p_T^{(t)} is the induced distribution of the previously trained GFlowNet, thereby avoiding storage of all past data. Two training schemes are proposed: (1) a Streaming Balance (SB) condition (Definition 1) derived by algebraically re-arranging the trajectory balance conditions of G_t and G_{t+1}, yielding a least-squares loss amenable to off-policy optimization; and (2) a KL streaming criterion (Definition 2) that minimizes D_KL[p_F^{(t+1)} || p_F^{(t)}·f(D_{t+1}|x)] on-policy, with low-variance RLOO gradient estimators. The SB loss requires learning a partition function Z_t at each step and excels for sparse targets, while the KL criterion avoids Z estimation and converges faster for dense targets. Theoretical analysis (Propositions 2–4) bounds how approximation errors at step t propagate to step t+1, showing that large data chunks make prior errors negligible.

## Results

On the online Bayesian phylogenetic inference task (Table 2), SB-GFlowNet achieves a TV distance of 0.00±0.04 (7 species), 0.00±0.01 (9 species), and 0.00±0.01 (11 species) to the true posterior, matching the standard GFlowNet trained from scratch on the full data. Crucially, SB-GFlowNet requires less than half the training time: 1279.68s vs. 2846.88s (7 species), 1714.49s vs. 3779.11s (9 species), and 2303.99s vs. 4821.74s (11 species), measured in seconds per 20k epochs. In the set generation toy task, the KL streaming criterion outperforms the SB (trajectory-balance) criterion for non-sparse targets (α=1.00: TV 0.13 vs. 0.21), while the SB criterion is more robust for sparse targets (α=0.50: TV 0.36 vs. 0.55).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's sole quantitative baseline is a standard GFlowNet (Bengio et al., JMLR 2023) trained from scratch to sample from the full posterior π(·|D₁:D₂) without any streaming update. SB-GFlowNet matches this baseline on approximation quality: the TV distance to the true posterior is statistically indistinguishable (0.00±0.04 vs. −0.02±0.04 for 7 species; 0.00±0.01 for both at 9 and 11 species). However, SB-GFlowNet wins decisively on computational efficiency, reducing training time by 55% (7 species), 55% (9 species), and 52% (11 species) by avoiding re-evaluation of the full likelihood over all previously seen data. The speedup is consistent across problem sizes, confirming that the streaming update scales favorably relative to batch retraining. No comparison is made against other streaming variational inference methods (e.g., SDA-Bayes), as SB-GFlowNet is the first method enabling VI over discrete combinatorial supports in a streaming setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends GFlowNets—originally designed for static target distributions—to the streaming setting by proposing a streaming balance condition (SB) derived from re-arranging the trajectory balance identity, and a KL-based streaming update criterion built on the GFlowNet–VI equivalence of Malkin et al. (2023). The posterior propagation principle ('today's posterior is tomorrow's prior') follows the Streaming, Distributed, Asynchronous (SDA) Bayes framework of Broderick et al. (2013), but is instantiated here for combinatorial/discrete support where prior VI methods required intractable continuous relaxations or scaled poorly with support size. The RLOO gradient estimator (Mnih & Rezende, 2016) is repurposed to provide low-variance gradients for the KL streaming criterion, and the DAG-GFlowNet architecture of Deleu et al. (2022) is adopted for the structure learning experiment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SB-GFlowNets are the first method enabling practical streaming variational inference over discrete parameter spaces, with theoretical guarantees bounding how approximation errors compound across updates and empirical speedups exceeding 2× over retraining from scratch. Key open questions include: (i) when accumulated errors from imperfect streaming updates necessitate retraining from an earlier checkpoint (catastrophic forgetting), (ii) how to handle changes in the generated object's size (e.g., a new species appearing during phylogenetic inference), and (iii) whether other GFlowNet training criteria (detailed balance, sub-trajectory balance) can be similarly adapted. The paper also notes that the expressiveness implied by different GFlowNet parameterizations remains unclear, a gap that limits principled model selection.
