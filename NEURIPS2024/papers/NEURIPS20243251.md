## Problem

Personalized federated learning in real-world deployments faces simultaneous challenges of limited local data, heterogeneous compute resources (forcing different model architectures across clients), statistical non-IID data, and privacy constraints. Existing Bayesian FL methods either assume homogeneous architectures, incur O(m³) posterior aggregation costs, or lack formal privacy guarantees, and no prior work jointly addresses all four challenges.

## Input / Output

Each client receives a local image dataset (MNIST 28×28 grayscale or CIFAR-10/100 32×32 RGB) with as few as 50 instances per class, while the server holds a small unlabeled alignment dataset (~2000 samples) from the same domain. Rather than exchanging weight vectors or posterior distributions, clients upload only their model outputs (logits) on the shared AD, and the server broadcasts a weighted aggregate of these outputs. Each client ultimately produces a personalized BNN with per-parameter Gaussian posteriors (mean and variance), yielding calibrated class probability distributions with uncertainty quantification.

## Method

FedBNN trains personalized Bayesian neural networks at each client via Bayes by Backprop (mean-field variational inference), but achieves cross-client collaboration entirely in the functional/output space rather than the weight space. In each global round, clients perform Monte Carlo forward passes on the shared unlabeled alignment dataset, upload their outputs to the server, and the server broadcasts a weighted aggregate. Each client then optimizes its BNN's prior parameters to match a corrected target (a convex combination of the global aggregate and its own local output, controlled by γ), effectively distilling peer knowledge into weight-space priors before fixing them and running local variational inference. This output-space mechanism naturally accommodates heterogeneous architectures (e.g., 2–3 conv-layer BNNs with ~50K parameters alongside VGG9-based BNNs with ~3M parameters) without requiring parameter alignment. A differentially private variant adds Gaussian noise to each client's AD outputs before upload, with a formal (ε,δ)-DP guarantee derived via zero-concentrated DP composition that is agnostic to the local learning algorithm.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 (non-IID, small data setting), FedBNN with homogeneous architectures achieves 70.6% test accuracy, outperforming the strongest non-Bayesian baseline FedAUX (60.0%) and the strongest Bayesian baseline pFedBayes (58.7%) by over 10 percentage points. Under heterogeneous compute (30% of clients with smaller models), FedBNN retains 68.2% accuracy on CIFAR-10 small, still exceeding all homogeneous baselines. The differentially private variant (ε≈9.98, δ=10⁻⁴) achieves 54.9% on CIFAR-10 small, substantially outperforming DP-FedAvg (35.2%) under comparable privacy guarantees. On MNIST small, FedBNN (Homo) reaches 94.9% vs. pFedBayes at 94.0%, and on CIFAR-100 small it achieves 49.65% vs. pFedGP at 47.06%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedBNN (Homo) outperforms all eight baselines across every dataset and data-size setting. On CIFAR-10 small (the most challenging regime), it beats FedAUX (60.0%) by 10.6 points, pFedBayes (58.7%) by 11.9 points, pFedME (48.5%) by 22.1 points, and FedAvg (43.1%) by 27.5 points. The gap narrows in the full-data setting where FedProx (79.2%) and pFedBayes (78.3%) approach FedBNN's 79.7%, but FedBNN still leads. Under heterogeneous compute (30% small-model clients), FedBNN (Hetero) at 68.2% on CIFAR-10 small still exceeds the best homogeneous baseline FedAUX (60.0%), demonstrating the benefit of allowing different model sizes. The DP variant (ε≈9.98) achieves 54.9% on CIFAR-10 small versus DP-FedAvg's 35.2%, a 19.7-point advantage under comparable privacy budgets. FOLA, the weakest Bayesian baseline, lags FedBNN by 27.3 points on CIFAR-10 small, and pFedGP trails by 25.0 points, highlighting the advantage of functional-space priors over weight-space aggregation.

## Relation to Prior Work

FedBNN extends the line of personalized Bayesian FL methods (pFedBayes, FOLA, pFedGP) by replacing their weight-space prior aggregation with a functional-space prior specification mechanism, drawing on centralized Bayesian deep learning results (Tran et al. 2022, Sun et al. 2019) that had not been adapted to federated settings. It also builds on knowledge distillation approaches for FL (FedAUX, FedMD) but departs from them by operating in the output space of Bayesian models rather than deterministic networks, and by using the distilled knowledge to set priors rather than as a direct training target. The DP guarantee follows the zCDP composition framework (Mironov 2017) applied to the Gaussian mechanism, but is notable for being agnostic to the client's internal learning algorithm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap in FL: real-world deployments involve clients with vastly different compute (phones, IoT sensors, edge servers), yet most Bayesian FL methods assume homogeneous architectures. The functional-space prior mechanism provides a principled way to transfer knowledge across models of different sizes without sharing weight distributions, reducing both communication cost (O(K) vs O(m) per round) and privacy risk. Key limitations include the requirement for an unlabeled alignment dataset at the server (though this is a mild assumption in cross-silo settings) and reliance on variational approximation rather than exact posterior inference. Open questions include scalability to transformer-scale models and whether the alignment dataset requirement can be relaxed further.
