## Problem

Quanta image sensors (SPAD arrays) produce extremely sparse 1-bit photon detection frames that are unusable without temporal binning, which destroys the nanosecond-scale temporal resolution that makes these sensors valuable. Existing self-supervised denoising methods like GAP assume Poisson photon statistics, but binary data follows a truncated Poisson (Bernoulli lattice) process, creating a complementary dependency between split training pairs that causes severe reconstruction artifacts when applied naively.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242805/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D binary volume (T×H×W) of raw SPAD frames where each pixel is 0 or 1, with extreme sparsity (e.g., <0.06 photons/pixel/frame in their simulation). It outputs a dense continuous-valued 3D volume at the same spatiotemporal resolution, predicting the underlying photon arrival probability at every pixel and frame. Inference operates on 48×512×512 patches at >150 fps on an RTX 4090, and the model can be applied to the raw data directly (single-shot) or via multi-shot averaging of Bernoulli-sampled sub-volumes for improved quality.

## Method

The key insight is that splitting a binary frame into input and target pairs creates a deterministic complementarity (a photon in the input guarantees absence in the target), unlike true Poisson splitting where multiple photons can occupy the same pixel. To address this, bit2bit introduces a masked cross-entropy loss that zeros out the loss at all locations where the input contains a 1, preventing the network from learning the trivial input→target=0 mapping. The network is a 3D ResUNet (depth 5, 32 initial features, group normalization with 8 groups, GeLU activations, pixel-shuffle upsampling) that optionally switches to 2D convolutions at a specified depth to control receptive field growth in the temporal dimension. Training is fully self-supervised: random 3D crops are split via Bernoulli sampling with a randomly drawn probability p ∈ [0, 1−10⁻⁶], and the masked loss is optimized with ADAMW over 150 epochs. The 3D formulation and stochastic p-sampling together leverage spatiotemporal structure and mitigate overfitting from the extreme sparsity of the data.

## Results

On simulated 1-bit quanta video data (0.059 photons/pixel/frame), bit2bit with masked loss and 3D processing achieves a mean PSNR of 33.93 (std 0.99) and SSIM of 0.959 (std 0.007), substantially outperforming all self-supervised baselines. With a 10-shot inference strategy (p∈[0.3,0.7]), the PSNR further improves to 34.35. The supervised upper bound (with ground truth) reaches PSNR 36.51, indicating bit2bit recovers ~93% of the achievable quality without any clean reference. Inference runs at >150 fps on an RTX 4090, processing a full 100k-frame SPAD dataset in under 30 minutes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

bit2bit (3D, masked) outperforms GAP (2D, masked) by 4.84 dB PSNR (33.93 vs 29.09) and 0.048 SSIM, demonstrating the benefit of 3D spatiotemporal processing over 2D frame-by-frame denoising. Against N2N with masking, bit2bit gains 5.10 dB PSNR (33.93 vs 28.83), and against N2V it gains 7.77 dB (33.93 vs 26.16), confirming that the Bernoulli-aware masked loss is critical for binary data where Poisson assumptions break down. Even N2N with two independent simulated samples (PSNR 32.15) is outperformed by bit2bit, and without the mask, N2N degrades to 23.14 dB with visible granular artifacts. The only method that surpasses bit2bit is the supervised baseline (36.51 dB), which requires unavailable ground truth. Qualitatively, bit2bit also substantially outperforms Quanta Burst Photography (QBP) on real SPAD data: QBP reconstructs a single image from 100k frames in ~30 min, while bit2bit reconstructs the full 100k-frame video at 150 fps, preserving sub-pixel temporal dynamics that QBP's binning-based approach inherently loses.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the self-supervised photon denoising framework of GAP (WACV 2024), which splits photon-counting images via binomial sampling to create N2N-style training pairs. The key departure is recognizing that binary SPAD data follows a Bernoulli lattice process (truncated Poisson) rather than a true Poisson distribution, which introduces a complementary dependency between input and target (input=1 forces target=0) that breaks the conditional independence assumption underlying GAP and N2N. The paper resolves this with a simple masking strategy that excludes input-positive pixels from the loss, and extends processing from 2D to 3D spatiotemporal volumes to exploit temporal structure. The N2N paired-training paradigm and the ResUNet architecture are carried forward, while the photon-splitting mechanism is adapted from binomial to Bernoulli sampling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This provides the first practical self-supervised solution for reconstructing high-temporal-resolution video from raw 1-bit SPAD data, a setting where ground truth is unavailable and temporal binning (the standard workaround) destroys the nanosecond-scale information that makes QIS valuable. The released dataset of real SPAD high-speed videos under diverse challenging conditions (strong/weak light, motion, ultra-fast events) fills a gap in the quanta imaging community. Key limitations include the requirement for per-dataset offline training, the assumption of a pure Poisson point process (incompatible with fixed-pattern noise or correlated detections), and the fact that quantitative evaluation relies on a single simulated video. The generalization to arbitrary n-dimensional binary point-process data and the potential for real-time inference on similar scenes are promising but unexplored directions.
