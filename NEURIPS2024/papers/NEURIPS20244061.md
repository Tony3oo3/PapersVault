## Problem

Deep RL agents suffer from plasticity loss when trained on sequences of tasks: after learning several tasks, the network becomes progressively harder to train on new ones. This is linked to parameters drifting into regions of parameter space with poor optimization properties—reduced weight matrix rank, correlated neuron directions, and degraded gradient propagation. The paper asks whether maintaining the favorable optimization structure of orthogonal initialization throughout training can preserve trainability across task changes.

## Input / Output

The agent receives a state vector from the environment (e.g., robot arm joint positions in MetaWorld, grid cell coordinates in Gridworld, or vehicle state in LunarLander) and outputs a continuous or discrete action along with a scalar value estimate. Tasks change at fixed intervals (e.g., every 1M steps in MetaWorld, 40K in Gridworld) by altering reward functions or context variables such as gravity, wind, or joint stiffness, without signaling the change to the agent. The network is a 2-hidden-layer MLP of width 64 with tanh activations by default.

## Method

The core contribution is adding a Parseval regularization term λ‖WWᵀ − sI‖²_F to the training objective for every weight matrix (except the final layer) in both the policy and value networks, encouraging rows of W to be mutually orthogonal with constant ℓ₂-norm √s. This preserves equal singular values and thus the dynamical isometry property of orthogonal initialization throughout training, keeping the input-output Jacobian well-conditioned. To relax the Lipschitz constraint that full orthogonality would impose, learnable diagonal layers (per-neuron scale plus bias) are inserted after each regularized layer, adding minimal parameters while restoring expressiveness. The regularization strength is scaled inversely with the square of network width to maintain consistent behavior across architectures. The approach is applied on top of RPO/PPO with standard orthogonal initialization and incurs only 1.8–11.4% additional runtime.

## Results

The paper evaluates Parseval regularization against four baselines across four continual RL benchmarks (Gridworld, CARL LunarLander, CARL DMCQuadruped, MetaWorld20-10) using performance profiles (1 − empirical CDF of per-task success rates). Parseval regularization yields the largest improvement over the RPO/PPO baseline on all four suites, with the most dramatic gains on Gridworld where the agent achieves non-zero success on nearly every task (vs. frequent 0% success for other methods). The computational overhead is modest at 1.8–11.4% additional runtime over the vanilla agent. Ablations show that regularizing angles between weight vectors contributes more to the benefit than regularizing row norms, though both components are necessary for full performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Parseval regularization outperforms all four baselines—Shrink-and-Perturb (SnP), Layer Norm, Regenerative Regularization (Regen), and Wasserstein Regen (W-Regen)—on every environment suite tested. The advantage is most pronounced on Gridworld, where Parseval enables the agent to make progress on almost every task while SnP, Layer Norm, Regen, and W-Regen frequently stall at 0% success rate (visible as a large mass near zero in their performance profiles). On MetaWorld20-10, Parseval shifts the entire success-rate distribution upward relative to all alternatives, with the gap being largest in the lower quantiles (i.e., the worst-performing tasks). On CARL LunarLander and DMCQuadruped, Parseval again leads, though the margins over the best alternative (typically Layer Norm or SnP) are smaller than on Gridworld. The paper notes that all baselines do improve over the unregularized RPO agent, confirming that the continual RL setting is challenging, but Parseval consistently achieves the best performance profile across all settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes Parseval regularization—originally proposed by Cisse et al. (ICML 2017) for adversarial robustness via Lipschitz constraints—as an optimization-preserving mechanism for continual RL. It sits at the intersection of two research threads: the orthogonal initialization / dynamical isometry literature (Saxe et al. 2013, Pennington et al. 2017) that showed well-conditioned weight matrices enable stable gradient propagation, and the plasticity loss literature (Lyle et al. 2023, Nikishin et al. 2022, Dohare et al. 2021) that identified trainability degradation in sequential task learning. Unlike prior plasticity-loss remedies that inject randomness (Shrink-and-Perturb, Continual Backprop) or reset weights, this work takes a purely architectural-regularization approach, maintaining the beneficial spectral structure of the initialization throughout training rather than periodically restoring it.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a simple, low-cost (1.8–11.4% runtime overhead) regularization that consistently outperforms more complex alternatives (Layer Norm, SnP, Regen) across four diverse benchmark suites, making it a practical drop-in improvement for continual RL practitioners. The diagnostic metrics it validates—stable rank, neuron weight correlation, and input-output Jacobian spread—offer a useful toolkit for monitoring and targeting trainability in future algorithms. Key limitations include the capacity constraint of near-orthogonal matrices (partially mitigated by diagonal layers), the complex and non-monotonic relationship between initialization rank/scale and performance, and evaluation limited to shallow (3-layer) networks, leaving open whether the benefits persist at greater depth where the Lipschitz constraint becomes more restrictive.
