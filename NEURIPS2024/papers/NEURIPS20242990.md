## Problem

Existing post-training quantization (PTQ) methods for hyper-scale Transformers face a fundamental accuracy-efficiency trade-off: block-wise reconstruction methods (e.g., BRECQ) capture cross-layer dependency within the attention module but require 20+ GPU hours even for small models, while fast learning-free schemes (e.g., OPTQ, Z-FOLD) ignore this dependency and rely on nearest rounding, leading to severe performance collapse at low bit-widths (INT2). The paper addresses how to achieve attention-aware quantization quality at layer-wise computational cost.

## Input / Output

The method consumes pre-trained Transformer weights (FP16) and 128 calibration segments of 2048 tokens sampled from C4, from which it pre-computes four statistics: E[XX^T], E[XA^TAX^T], E[K^TK], and E[Q^TQ]. It outputs per-layer integer weights (W_int) along with a shared scale s and zero-point z for each layer. Only weights are quantized; activations remain in full precision, and the pre-computed statistics are reused across all 2,000 optimization iterations, making the per-iteration cost independent of calibration set size.

## Method

aespa quantizes each attention projection (W_Q, W_K, W_V) separately in a divide-and-conquer fashion while targeting the attention output reconstruction error rather than individual layer outputs. For W_V, the loss reduces to tr(ΔW_V · E[XA^TAX^T] · ΔW_V^T), introducing a modified Hessian that encodes the attention map A. For W_Q and W_K, a first-order Taylor approximation of the softmax Jacobian followed by a Frobenius-norm upper bound yields losses of the form tr(E[K^TK] · ΔW_Q · E[XX^T] · ΔW_Q^T) and tr(E[Q^TQ] · ΔW_K · E[XX^T] · ΔW_K^T), respectively. These pre-computed, data-independent objectives allow each optimization iteration to be computed with a single matrix multiplication and element-wise product (O(d_h·d²) flops), regardless of calibration data volume. The pipeline first determines scale/zero-point via Z-FOLD with the proposed Hessian, then optimizes integer weights via AdaRound with the refined attention-wise losses, achieving ~10× fewer flops per iteration than joint block-wise methods while matching or exceeding their accuracy.

## Results

On INT2 quantization of LLaMA-7B (WikiText-2), aespa achieves PPL 11.94, substantially outperforming OmniQuant (18.18) and AffineQuant (18.83), while BRECQ runs out of memory. For INT3 on LLaMA-7B, aespa reaches PPL 6.579 vs. 6.716 (OmniQuant) and 6.712 (AffineQuant). On the larger LLaMA-30B at INT2, aespa achieves PPL 7.845 while OmniQuant reaches 10.15 and AffineQuant diverges (NaN). aespa also completes quantization ~10× faster than BRECQ in FLOPs (e.g., 0.24 vs. 6.7 GFLOPS per iteration for OPT-125M).

## Baselines & Comparisons

aespa uniformly outperforms all six baselines across every model and bit-width tested. Against BRECQ [18], aespa matches or beats it on OPT-125M/1.3B/2.7B (where BRECQ is the only method that doesn't OOM) while completing quantization in 5 minutes vs. ~2 GPU hours; BRECQ is infeasible (OOM) on all models ≥6.7B. Against OmniQuant [27] and AffineQuant [20], aespa wins on every entry: at INT2 on LLaMA-7B, aespa achieves PPL 11.94 vs. 18.18 (OmniQuant) and 18.83 (AffineQuant), and OmniQuant/AffineQuant produce NaN or PPL >10³ on several configurations where aespa remains stable. Against layer-wise methods OPTQ [7] and Z-FOLD [13] (Table 2, BLOOM models), aespa improves INT2 PPL by roughly 2× on small models (e.g., 44.91 vs. 65.45 for Z-FOLD on BLOOM-560M) and by a large margin on larger models. Against AdaRound [23] (Table 5, OPT-125M), aespa's attention-wise reconstruction target yields PPL 69.23 vs. 160.7 at INT2, confirming that cross-layer dependency modeling is the key accuracy driver.

## Relation to Prior Work

aespa occupies a middle ground between two families of PTQ methods: classic block-wise reconstruction (BRECQ) that jointly optimizes Q, K, V projections for attention output fidelity but is prohibitively slow for LLMs, and fast layer-wise schemes (OPTQ, Z-FOLD) that are efficient but ignore cross-layer dependency within the attention module. The paper's key conceptual move is to retain the attention-wise reconstruction target from BRECQ while quantizing each projection separately (divide-and-conquer), then derive simplified per-projection loss functions via Taylor approximation of softmax and upper-bound constructions that enable pre-computation-based loss evaluation. The implementation pipeline composes Z-FOLD for scale/zero-point initialization, OPTQ for initial weight updates, and AdaRound for iterative weight-rounding optimization, but replaces their standard layer-wise losses with the proposed attention-aware objectives and Hessians.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for deploying LLMs on resource-constrained hardware (mobile, TV, NPU), where quantization must be fast enough to run on every model update and accurate enough to preserve generation quality. The most important open limitation is that aespa targets only the attention module and weight-only quantization; extending to full Transformer block reconstruction (with nonlinear activations and normalization) or to weight-activation quantization for integer-only NPUs remains unresolved. The paper also notes that the method has not been validated on diffusion models, where time-step-dependent output distributions would require additional strategies. Whether the attention-aware Hessian E[XA^TAX^T] generalizes beyond the specific attention formulations tested (standard scaled dot-product) is an open question.
