## Problem

While online multitask representation learning in RL has received theoretical attention, there is no provable guarantee that pooling offline data from multiple related tasks yields a more accurate shared representation than learning each task in isolation. This gap is critical because in many practical domains (robotics, healthcare) online interaction with multiple task environments is infeasible, and the learned representation must also transfer to unseen downstream tasks.

## Input / Output

The upstream stage consumes a pooled offline dataset of (s, a, r, s') tuples from T source low-rank MDPs (dimension d, horizon H, action space cardinality K), each collected by a fixed behavior policy, and outputs a shared feature map φ̂, per-task transition kernels P̂^(t), and per-task policies. The downstream stage takes φ̂ as a frozen input and, depending on the setting, either interacts with a new MDP sharing φ* (reward-free or online) or receives a separate offline dataset for the new task (offline), producing an ε-optimal policy for an arbitrary or given reward function.

## Method

MORL pools all T source-task datasets and jointly estimates the shared embedding φ̂ and task-specific embeddings μ̂^(t) via a maximum likelihood estimation oracle over the product model class Φ×Ψ^T, then constructs per-task lower-confidence-bound penalty terms using the empirical covariance of φ̂ to perform pessimistic planning. The key theoretical insight is that the joint MLE yields a 1/T improvement in the task-average TV estimation error over independent learning, and the suboptimality bound scales with a multitask relative condition number C* (a partial-coverage quantity) rather than the global concentrability ratio. For downstream reward-free RL, the algorithm uses the frozen φ̂ with an optimistic exploration-driven reward to collect K_RFE trajectories, then plans with the revealed reward, achieving sample complexity O~(H⁴d³/ε²) — a O~(HdK) improvement over the best single-task low-rank result (RAFFLE) by eliminating the dependence on action-space cardinality K.

## Results

The main theoretical result (Theorem 4.4) shows that MORL achieves a sample complexity of Õ(H⁴d³/ε²) episodes for downstream reward-free RL in low-rank MDPs, improving the best prior bound (RAFFLE, Õ(H⁵d⁴K/ε²)) by a factor of Õ(HdK) and eliminating the dependence on action space cardinality K. For the upstream multitask task (Theorem 3.3), joint MLE-based learning requires only Õ(log|Φ|/(Tε²) + log|Ψ|/ε²) samples per task to achieve ε-accurate transition kernels, versus Õ(log|Φ|/ε² + log|Ψ|/ε²) for independent per-task learning, demonstrating a 1/T speedup in the representation-learning component. The downstream offline and online suboptimality gaps scale as O(κ_ρ^{-1/2}H²d/√N_off + H²d^{1/2}ξ_down) and Õ(H²d^{3/2}/√N_on + H²dξ_down) respectively, where ξ_down captures the upstream representation misspecification.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MORL outperforms all three single-task baselines on reward-free RL sample complexity in low-rank MDPs. Compared to RAFFLE (Cheng et al., 2023), the best prior single-task method, MORL improves the bound by a factor of Õ(HdK), reducing the H-dependence from H⁵ to H⁴, the d-dependence from d⁴ to d³, and completely eliminating the K-dependence. Against FLAMBE (Agarwal et al., 2020), the improvement is dramatic: from Õ(H²²d⁷K⁹/ε¹⁰) to Õ(H⁴d³/ε²), with a much milder dependence on ε (ε² vs. ε¹⁰). Compared to MOFFLE (Modi et al., 2024), MORL avoids the dependence on the reachability probability η and achieves a far tighter polynomial in H, d, and K. The key mechanism enabling these gains is that the shared representation learned from T upstream offline tasks removes the need for the downstream agent to simultaneously learn the representation and explore, which is what forces the K-dependence in single-task methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the joint MLE oracle from FLAMBE (Agarwal et al., 2020b) and the online multitask representation learning framework of REFUEL (Cheng et al., 2022) to the offline setting, where the agent has no environment interaction during upstream learning. The key algorithmic departure is replacing online exploration-driven data collection with a pooled offline MLE over all source tasks, combined with a pessimism-based policy optimization step (PEVI-style) that uses a relative condition number for partial coverage rather than global concentrability. For downstream reward-free RL, the paper builds on the optimistic learning procedures of Wang et al. (2020) and Zhang et al. (2021), modified to account for representation misspecification inherited from the upstream stage.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first theoretical treatment of offline multitask representation learning in RL, filling a gap where prior work (Cheng et al., 2022; Agarwal et al., 2023) only covered the online setting. The result that upstream offline representation learning can remove the action-space cardinality dependence K from reward-free RL sample complexity (contradicting the single-task lower bound of Cheng et al., 2023) is a notable theoretical insight. However, the analysis relies on strong structural assumptions (finite function classes, linear combination of upstream transition kernels for the downstream task, low-rank structure), and the paper provides no empirical validation, limiting immediate practical impact. Open questions include extending to general (infinite) function classes, relaxing the linear combination assumption, and studying whether the benefits persist in high-dimensional continuous-state settings.
