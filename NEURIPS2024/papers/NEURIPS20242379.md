## Problem

Blind face restoration methods can generate high-quality images from severely degraded inputs but often fail to preserve the subject's true facial identity. Existing reference-based approaches (e.g., DMDNet) rely on facial landmark detection to align reference features, which becomes unreliable when the LQ image is heavily corrupted. The paper addresses how to effectively integrate multiple unaligned reference images into a diffusion-based restoration pipeline without depending on landmark detection.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242379/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes one 512×512 low-quality face image (spatially aligned with the target) and a variable number of 512×512 high-quality reference face images (typically 5, but flexible at inference) that are NOT spatially aligned with the target due to differing poses, expressions, and lighting. The LQ image is encoded by a frozen VQGAN and channel-concatenated with the diffusion latent at every timestep, while reference images are processed once through the denoising U-net to produce cached key-value tokens. The output is a single 512×512 high-quality face image generated via 100 DDIM steps with classifier-free guidance (scale 1.5) towards the reference images.

## Method

ReF-LDM builds on a standard LDM with a frozen VQGAN autoencoder and a denoising U-net, adding two key components. First, the CacheKV mechanism extracts key-value tokens from each reference image's latent by passing it through the same U-net once at timestep t=0, then reuses these cached KVs at every self-attention layer across all T denoising timesteps—avoiding the redundant per-timestep network passes required by spatial-concatenation while providing better feature alignment than cross-attention or channel-concatenation. Second, a timestep-scaled identity loss multiplies the ArcFace cosine-distance identity loss by √(ᾱ_t), downweighting it at high-noise timesteps where the one-step decoded prediction is out-of-distribution for the face recognition model. Training proceeds in three stages: pre-training the VQGAN on 68,411 FFHQ images, pre-training an LQ-conditioned LDM, then fine-tuning on the 18,816-image FFHQ-Ref dataset with the combined LDM + timestep-scaled identity loss (λ=0.1).

## Results

On FFHQ-Ref-Severe, ReF-LDM achieves an identity similarity (IDS) of 0.676, more than doubling the best non-reference baseline CodeFormer (0.323) and substantially outperforming the reference-based DMDNet (0.185, which fails on 214/857 images due to landmark detection failures). On FFHQ-Ref-Moderate, ReF-LDM reaches IDS 0.840, surpassing DMDNet (0.810) and CodeFormer (0.760). On CelebA-Test-Ref, ReF-LDM attains IDS 0.779, beating DMDNet (0.752) and CodeFormer (0.660). ReF-LDM also achieves competitive FID scores (37.60 on Severe, 33.05 on Moderate) and the best fLPIPS on FFHQ-Ref-Moderate (0.088).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ReF-LDM decisively outperforms all three non-reference-based methods (CodeFormer, VQFR, DAEFR) on identity similarity across all three benchmarks, with the gap being most dramatic on FFHQ-Ref-Severe where IDS jumps from 0.323 (CodeFormer) to 0.676 (ReF-LDM). Against the only other reference-based method, DMDNet, ReF-LDM wins on IDS on all three benchmarks (0.676 vs. 0.185 on Severe, 0.840 vs. 0.810 on Moderate, 0.779 vs. 0.752 on CelebA-Test-Ref), while DMDNet suffers from landmark detection failures that cause it to produce no output for a substantial fraction of severely degraded images. On perceptual quality metrics (LPIPS, fLPIPS), ReF-LDM is competitive but slightly behind CodeFormer on some settings; the authors attribute this to background-region differences rather than face-region quality. On FID, ReF-LDM is competitive with the best baselines (37.60 on Severe vs. 34.40 for LDM; 33.05 on Moderate vs. 32.16 for LDM), indicating that the identity-preserving conditioning does not significantly degrade overall image realism.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the reference-based face restoration line (GFRNet, ASFNet, DMDNet) by replacing landmark-dependent feature extraction with a latent diffusion model that implicitly learns correspondences between the LQ image and multiple non-aligned reference images. The CacheKV mechanism draws on the self-attention structure of the LDM U-Net, repurposing it to cache reference-image keys/values in a single forward pass rather than re-processing them at every denoising timestep (as in MasaCtrl) or inflating the spatial dimension (as in spatial-concatenation). The timestep-scaled identity loss is a novel training objective that addresses the distribution mismatch between early-timestep diffusion predictions and the face-recognition embedding space, a problem not previously addressed in diffusion-based restoration.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The FFHQ-Ref dataset (20,405 images, 6,523 identities) is a practical resource that doubles the image count and sextuples the identity count of the prior CelebRef-HQ benchmark while offering better image quality and proper licensing, lowering the barrier for future reference-based restoration research. The CacheKV mechanism provides a general, efficient pattern for injecting non-spatially-aligned image conditions into diffusion models, potentially applicable beyond face restoration. Open questions include handling occluded faces, extreme poses, and illumination mismatches between references and targets, as well as whether the approach scales to more than a handful of references without prohibitive memory costs.
