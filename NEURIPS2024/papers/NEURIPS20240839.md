## Problem

Sequential recommender systems struggle with long-tail users (most users have fewer than 10 interactions) and long-tail items (most items have fewer than 30 interactions), leading to degraded recommendation quality for the majority of users and items. Existing remedies either suffer from a seesaw effect (improving tail items harms head items) or introduce noise by relying solely on collaborative signals, and prior LLM-based approaches either incur prohibitive inference costs or erode semantic structure during fine-tuning.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a user's time-ordered interaction sequence, pre-computed LLM semantic embeddings for items (derived from attribute/description prompts via text-embedding-ada-002) and for users (derived from interaction-history prompts), all cached offline so no LLM calls occur at serving time. It outputs a probability score for every candidate item via concatenation of dual-view user and item embeddings, from which a top-10 ranked list is produced. The semantic embedding layer is frozen throughout training, and the collaborative embedding layer is initialized from PCA-reduced LLM embeddings rather than randomly.

## Method

LLM-ESR introduces a dual-view modeling framework with a semantic branch (frozen LLM item embeddings passed through a two-layer adapter, then cross-attention, then a shared sequence encoder) and a collaborative branch (trainable item embeddings through the same cross-attention and shared encoder), fused at both the sequence level (cross-attention between the two item sequences) and the logit level (concatenation of dual-view user and item embeddings for scoring). To address long-tail users, a retrieval augmented self-distillation module retrieves N similar users via cosine similarity on LLM user embeddings, computes their dual-view representations as a teacher signal via mean pooling, and applies an MSE distillation loss to the target user's representation with gradients stopped on the teacher side. The total training objective combines a pairwise ranking loss with a weighted self-distillation loss (α=0.1), updating only the collaborative embeddings, adapter, cross-attention, and sequence encoder while keeping all LLM-derived embeddings frozen to preserve original semantic relations.

## Results

LLM-ESR consistently achieves the best performance across all three datasets (Yelp, Amazon Fashion, Amazon Beauty) and all three backbone SRS models (GRU4Rec, Bert4Rec, SASRec) on both H@10 and N@10 metrics. For example, on Yelp with SASRec, LLM-ESR reaches 0.6673 H@10 / 0.4208 N@10 overall, surpassing the strongest baseline LLMInit (0.6415 / 0.3997) by ~4% in H@10. The gains are especially pronounced for long-tail items: on Beauty/SASRec, tail-item H@10 jumps from 0.1714 (LLMInit) to 0.2257 (LLM-ESR), a 31% relative improvement. All improvements are statistically significant (two-sided t-test, p < 0.05).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LLM-ESR outperforms all eight baselines (CITIES, MELT, RLMRec, LLMInit, and the three backbone models) across every dataset, backbone, and user/item group, with all gains statistically significant (p < 0.05). LLMInit (which initializes item embeddings from LLMs but fine-tunes them) is consistently the strongest baseline, typically within 2–5% of LLM-ESR on overall H@10, but LLM-ESR pulls ahead most dramatically on tail items (e.g., +31% relative H@10 on Beauty/SASRec) and tail users. CITIES exhibits a severe seesaw problem: it sometimes improves tail-item scores but catastrophically degrades head-item and overall performance (e.g., dropping from 0.4388 to 0.2256 H@10 on Beauty/SASRec). MELT is competitive on Yelp/Bert4Rec (0.6206 H@10) but underperforms LLM-ESR by 6–10% on most other settings, reflecting its limitation to collaborative signals only. RLMRec, designed for collaborative filtering rather than sequential recommendation, consistently underperforms even the unmodified backbone models on several configurations. The framework's model-agnostic nature is confirmed by consistent improvements across GRU4Rec, Bert4Rec, and SASRec, whereas baselines like CITIES and MELT show backbone-dependent effectiveness.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 'LLMs enhancing RS' line of work, which avoids the high inference cost of using LLMs directly during recommendation. It departs from prior LLM-enhanced SRS methods (LLM2Bert4Rec, SAID) that use LLM embeddings as trainable initialization for item embedding layers, instead freezing the semantic embeddings and introducing a lightweight adapter for space transformation to preserve original semantic relations. The retrieval augmented self-distillation module draws on the self-distillation paradigm (Zhang et al., ICCV 2019) but repurposes it for sequential recommendation by retrieving semantically similar users via LLM-derived user embeddings as the teacher signal. The dual-view modeling with cross-attention fusion builds on the multi-view representation learning tradition in recommendation, combining it with the long-tail-specific motivation from CITIES and MELT.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LLM-ESR provides a practical, model-agnostic recipe for integrating LLM semantic knowledge into any sequential recommender without incurring LLM inference costs at serving time, making it deployable in latency-sensitive industrial settings. The consistent and substantial gains across three datasets and three backbone architectures, particularly for long-tail items and users, suggest that frozen LLM embeddings combined with collaborative signals is a robust pattern worth adopting. Open questions include whether more recent open-source LLM embedding models (e.g., LLM2Vec, GTE) yield further improvements, how the approach scales to very large catalogs, and whether the two hyperparameters (α, N) can be eliminated or auto-tuned. The limitation of relying on a single commercial API for embeddings also raises reproducibility and cost concerns for the broader community.
