## Problem

Endogenous distribution shifts in decentralized noncooperative games—where players' collective decisions alter the data distributions they depend on—have been largely overlooked in the performative prediction literature. Existing performative game studies either ignore coupled inequality constraints or assume full network observability, and no prior work provides an upper bound on the distance between the performative stable equilibrium (PSE) and Nash equilibrium (NE) in such games, a gap that is particularly difficult to close due to the absence of strong convexity on the joint cost function.

## Input / Output

Each player i receives stochastic samples ξ_i drawn from its decision-dependent distribution D_i(θ_i, θ_{-i}), its own strategy θ_i, and only neighbor information (strategies, estimates, and dual variables) over a connected, doubly stochastic communication graph A. Because player i's private cost J_i depends on all players' strategies but it can only observe neighbors, the algorithm maintains local estimates θ̂_{ih} of every other player's strategy, updated via weighted consensus. The output is the PSE point (joint equilibrium strategies), along with per-iteration primal updates θ_i^{t+1}, dual updates λ_i^{t+1}, and aggregate performance metrics: performative regret R_i(T) and constraint violation R_g(T).

## Method

The paper formulates a constrained performative game where each player minimizes its performative risk E_{ξ_i~D_i(θ)}[J_i(ξ_i;θ_i,θ_{-i})] subject to a coupled inequality constraint Σ_i g_i(θ_i) ≼ 0, with distribution sensitivity bounded via a Wasserstein-1 metric weighted by an influence graph P. For equilibrium analysis, the PSE is characterized as a fixed point of a repeated-risk-minimization mapping whose contraction coefficient scales as (1/μ)√(Σ_i L_i²ε_i² max_j p_ij), yielding linear convergence under a scalability-friendly condition; the NE existence follows from proving strong monotonicity of the performative gradient ∇PR(θ) = G_θ(θ) + H_θ(θ). The key theoretical contribution is the first PSE–NE distance bound, derived via strong duality and saddle-point inequalities, which depends on the Lipschitz constants G_i, sensitivity parameters ε_i, and self-influence weights p_ii. For computation, a decentralized stochastic primal-dual algorithm performs: (i) neighbor-to-neighbor exchange of strategies, estimates, and dual variables; (ii) consensus-based estimation of all other players' strategies; (iii) projected stochastic gradient descent on primal variables incorporating both the cost gradient and the constraint gradient weighted by the local dual variable; and (iv) projected gradient ascent on dual variables with a consensus term. Under a diminishing stepsize γ_t = τ₁^η(τ₂t+τ₁)^{-η} with η=1/2, both performative regret and constraint violation achieve O(T^{3/4}) convergence, matching the non-performative decentralized game rate while the performative effect only inflates the constant via the reduced monotonicity parameter μ̃ = μ − Σ_i L_iε_i max_j √p_ij.

## Results

The paper establishes that the proposed decentralized stochastic primal-dual algorithm achieves sublinear convergence with performative regret R_i(T) ≤ O(T^{3/4}) and constraint violation R_g(T) ≤ O(T^{3/4}), matching the convergence order of the non-performative case (Lu et al., 2020). The first upper bound on the PSE–NE distance is ‖θ^pse − θ^ne‖₂ ≤ (1/μ̃)√(Σᵢ Gᵢ²εᵢ²pᵢᵢ), where μ̃ = μ − Σᵢ Lᵢεᵢ maxⱼ√pᵢⱼ. In the networked Cournot game (5 firms, 3 markets), the normalized distance ‖θᵗ − θ^ne‖₂/‖θᵗ‖₂ stabilizes at values ≤ 10⁻¹, and total revenue at PSE closely matches that at NE across performative strengths ε ∈ {0.2, 0.4, 0.6}.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is primarily a theoretical paper, and no direct algorithmic baselines are benchmarked in the experiments. The numerical validation compares two internal scenarios: (1) the 'pse' approach (Algorithm 1 with partial information) versus the 'ne' approach (same procedure but with perfect knowledge of the decision-dependent distributions {Dᵢ(θ)}), showing that total revenue at PSE is very close to that at NE; and (2) three performative strengths (ε = 0.2, 0.4, 0.6), demonstrating that larger ε widens the normalized PSE–NE distance but the gap remains marginal (≤ 10⁻¹). Conceptually, the paper differentiates itself from Narang et al. (2023) and Wang et al. (2023) by handling coupled inequality constraints (requiring primal-dual + consensus) versus their unconstrained settings (requiring only local SGD), and by providing the first PSE–NE distance bound absent in both prior works.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of performative prediction (Perdomo et al., 2020) and decentralized noncooperative games with coupled constraints (Lu et al., 2020), combining both into a single framework. It departs from the closest prior performative-game works (Narang et al., 2023; Wang et al., 2023) in three key ways: it introduces coupled inequality constraints (requiring a primal-dual method rather than plain SGD), restricts communication to local neighbors rather than full network access, and allows each player's cost to depend on all other players' strategies rather than a local-plus-regularizer structure. The Wasserstein-1 sensitivity assumption on decision-dependent distributions is adopted directly from Wang et al. (2023), and the monotonicity assumption on the performative gradient component H follows Narang et al. (2023).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The first upper bound on the PSE–NE distance in performative games fills a notable gap: prior PP work could only bound the optimality gap under strong convexity of the joint cost, which is unavailable in noncooperative games. The result that performativity degrades only the convergence constant (not the order) relative to the non-performative case is practically reassuring for deployment in dynamic environments. Limitations include the requirement of strong monotonicity (μ > 0) on the stochastic gradient, the Wasserstein-1 sensitivity assumption, and a connected communication graph; what happens when the contraction condition (3) is violated—i.e., performativity is too strong—remains open. Broader implications for safety-critical multi-agent systems (autonomous vehicles, electricity markets) where both strategic competition and endogenous distribution shifts coexist are promising but unexplored.
