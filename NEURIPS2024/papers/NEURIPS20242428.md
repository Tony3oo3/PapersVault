## Problem

Existing image diffusion models lack fine-grained 3D pose control over individual objects in multi-object scenes. Prior 3D-aware generation methods either handle only single objects without backgrounds, rely on expensive paired 3D annotations, or cannot be applied to edit existing real-world images. The paper addresses this by asking whether a scalable, video-based training strategy can learn disentangled object appearance and 3D pose representations that plug into a pre-trained text-to-image diffusion model.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a source image (256×256) for appearance extraction via 2D bounding boxes, 3D bounding boxes (four projected corners with depth, forming a 12-D vector) for pose specification, and optionally a separate background image. At inference, users manipulate the 3D box coordinates to translate, rotate, or rescale objects, and can swap appearance tokens or background tokens across scenes for compositional generation. The output is a full 256×256 scene image where objects are rendered at the specified 3D poses with realistic lighting, shadows, and occlusion handling, generated via 50-step DDIM sampling with classifier-free guidance (scale 2.0).

## Method

A Neural Asset is a per-object tuple of an appearance token (K=4 D-dimensional vectors from RoIAlign on a jointly fine-tuned DINO ViT-B/8 feature map) and a pose token (1024-D embedding from a two-layer MLP on projected 3D box corners). The critical training insight is paired-frame supervision: appearance is extracted from a source video frame while pose is taken from a target frame, forcing the model to learn pose-invariant appearance and use the pose token for novel-view synthesis rather than pixel copying. These per-object tokens are serialized via channel-wise concatenation of appearance and pose, linear projection, and token-axis concatenation across objects, forming a drop-in replacement for text embeddings in Stable Diffusion v2.1. A separate background token (masked-image features plus relative camera pose) is appended to the sequence, enabling independent background control. The entire pipeline—DINO encoder, MLPs, and diffusion decoder—is fine-tuned end-to-end with Adam (batch 1536, 256 TPUv5 chips) using the standard denoising loss.

## Results

On multi-object editing, Neural Assets achieves PSNR of 22.03 (image-level) and 20.05 (object-level) on MOVi-E, 14.83/16.41 on Objectron, and 18.71/17.67 on Waymo Open, consistently outperforming both baselines by large margins. On single-object editing (OBJect), the method reaches 20.58 PSNR for translation, 18.52 for rotation, and 28.86 for removal (seen objects), surpassing 3DIT by 4–6 dB. Object identity preservation (DINO similarity) reaches 0.738 on MOVi-E and 0.790 on Objectron, compared to 0.336–0.555 for baselines, demonstrating substantially better appearance fidelity during 3D pose manipulation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two baselines: 3DIT [67], which fine-tunes Zero-1-to-3 with language-guided instructions for single-object 3D editing, and Chained, a pipeline combining SAM segmentation, Stable Diffusion inpainting, Zero-1-to-3 re-pose, and alpha blending. Neural Assets outperforms both baselines across all four datasets and all metrics. On MOVi-E, the gap is largest: 7.7 dB PSNR improvement over 3DIT at the object level (20.05 vs. 12.41) and a 0.402 DINO similarity advantage (0.738 vs. 0.336), reflecting 3DIT's inability to handle multiple objects or real-world backgrounds. On Objectron, the method gains 5.1 dB object-level PSNR over 3DIT (16.41 vs. 11.30) and 0.243 DINO similarity, while also achieving the best FID (0.55 vs. 1.99). On Waymo Open, the advantage is more moderate in image-level PSNR (18.71 vs. 18.28 Chained) but still substantial at the object level (17.67 vs. 16.20) and in DINO identity (0.653 vs. 0.596). The Chained baseline, while able to identify the correct object via SAM, produces unrealistic results due to missing lighting and shadow effects, whereas Neural Assets naturally harmonizes objects into new environments.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on Stable Diffusion v2.1 as the generative backbone and DINO ViT as the visual encoder, but departs from the text-token conditioning paradigm by replacing text embeddings with per-object 'Neural Asset' tokens that encode disentangled appearance and 3D pose. Conceptually, it extends the 3D asset workflow from computer graphics (select, place, animate) into the diffusion model setting, and generalizes prior single-object 3D editing (3DIT, Zero-1-to-3) and multi-object 2D spatial control (GLIGEN, InstanceDiffusion) into a unified multi-object 3D framework. The key methodological departure is the paired-frame training strategy, which forces appearance tokens to be pose-invariant by extracting them from a different video frame than the pose tokens, a design not present in prior object-centric or personalized generation work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Neural Assets provide a practical, scalable interface for 3D-aware multi-object scene editing that works on real-world video data without requiring explicit 3D geometry (NeRF, depth maps) or curated multi-view renderings. The compositional capabilities—object transfer, background swapping with automatic lighting adaptation—open a path toward neural-based scene simulators. Key open questions include: whether the approach extends to deformable or articulated objects, whether scalable 3D annotation pipelines (e.g., OmniNOCS) can remove the current dependency on hand-annotated 3D bounding boxes, and whether the symmetry-ambiguity and camera-object-motion-entanglement failure modes resolve with larger, more diverse training data.
