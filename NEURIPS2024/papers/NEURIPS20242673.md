## Problem

RNNs suffer from excessively long information propagation paths due to their recurrent structure, causing difficulty in capturing long-term dependencies, gradient vanishing/exploding, and inefficient sequential computation. While gated variants like LSTM and GRU improve information selection, they cannot overcome the fundamental O(L) propagation path limitation. This paper asks whether a new paradigm can replace RNNs by reducing the information propagation path to O(1) while retaining the advantages of sequential information modeling.

## Input / Output

The method takes a univariate historical time series of length L_h (e.g., 168 hourly steps) along with optional temporal external features (e.g., hour-of-day, day-of-week encodings) and produces a future prediction of length L_f (e.g., 1440 steps), specifically targeting the 'fewer inputs, longer outputs' regime. The 1D input is reshaped into a 2D tensor of shape R×P based on the natural period P of the series, and the output is generated by predicting R_f values per column and reshaping back to 1D. Experiments use five benchmark datasets (ECL, Traffic, ETTh1, ETTh2, Weather) with four task settings (168→168, 168→336, 168→720, 168→1440).

## Method

PGN replaces the RNN recurrent loop with a Historical Information Extraction (HIE) layer—a linear projection that aggregates all preceding time steps in parallel at each position—followed by a single sigmoid gate that simultaneously controls selection and fusion of historical versus current information, reducing the propagation path to O(1) while maintaining O(L) theoretical complexity with full parallelizability. TPGN builds on PGN by reshaping the 1D series into a 2D grid (R rows × P columns) based on the natural period, then processing two branches: a long-term branch that applies PGN along each column to capture periodic patterns while preserving local characteristics, and a short-term branch that uses two linear layers to compress rows into patches and then merge patches into a global representation. The two branch outputs are concatenated and passed through a final linear layer to produce the forecast. The overall TPGN complexity is O(√L), and the framework is general—PGN can be swapped for GRU, LSTM, MLP, or self-attention in the long-term branch.

## Results

TPGN achieves state-of-the-art performance across all 20 long-range forecasting tasks (5 datasets × 4 forecasting lengths), with an average MSE improvement of 12.35% and MAE improvement of 7.25% over the previous best methods. Per-dataset average MSE reductions are 17.31% (ECL), 9.38% (Traffic), 3.79% (ETTh1), 12.26% (ETTh2), and 19.09% (Weather). On the most challenging 168→1440 task, TPGN records MSE of 0.2484 (ECL), 0.1390 (Traffic), 0.1343 (ETTh1), 0.2514 (ETTh2), and 0.1786 (Weather), consistently outperforming all 13 baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TPGN outperforms all 13 baselines across every dataset and forecasting length. The closest competitor varies by domain: on ECL, WITRAN (RNN-based) is the runner-up (0.3255 vs. 0.2484 MSE at 168→1440, a 23.7% gap), while on Traffic, TimeMixer (MLP-based) is closest (0.1547 vs. 0.1390, an 11.2% gap). On ETTh1, MICN (CNN-based) is the nearest competitor (0.1444 vs. 0.1343, a 7.0% gap), and on ETTh2, iTransformer (Transformer-based) trails by 35.9% (0.3921 vs. 0.2514). The largest margins appear on Weather, where TPGN's 0.1786 MSE beats the next-best TimeMixer (0.3101) by 42.4%. Notably, TPGN's advantage grows with forecasting length: at 168→168 the gap over the best baseline is modest, but at 168→1440 the margin widens substantially, confirming TPGN's strength in long-range prediction from limited inputs.

## Relation to Prior Work

PGN departs from the recurrent structure that defines RNNs (LSTM, GRU) by replacing sequential hidden-state propagation with a linear Historical Information Extraction (HIE) layer that directly aggregates all prior time steps in O(1) path, combined with a single parallel gate. The TPGN framework repurposes the 2D temporal reshaping idea from WITRAN, TimesNet, and PDF for periodicity capture, and borrows the patch-aggregation mechanism from PatchTST for short-term information, but unifies them under a novel PGN backbone that had no direct predecessor. Conceptually, PGN sits between the O(1) path of attention-based Transformers and the O(L) path of RNNs, but achieves this through a simple linear-plus-gate design rather than quadratic attention or multi-head mechanisms.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a single-gate linear architecture can match or exceed multi-gate RNNs (GRU, LSTM) in the long-term branch, suggesting that the recurrent structure itself—not the gating—is the bottleneck for long-range dependency capture. However, the evaluation is restricted to single-variable forecasting (following WITRAN's protocol), and the authors explicitly acknowledge that variable-relationship modeling is left for future work, limiting immediate applicability to multivariate settings. The O(√L) complexity and parallelizability make TPGN attractive for deployment, but the 'RNN successor' framing may overstate practical impact given that the field has largely shifted toward Transformer and MLP-based methods. Open questions include whether PGN generalizes beyond time series (e.g., NLP, audio) and whether the 2D reshaping assumption (fixed natural period) degrades on aperiodic or multi-scale signals.
