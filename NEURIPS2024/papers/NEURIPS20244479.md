## Problem

Multimodal representation learning methods like CLIP require paired samples across modalities, but in scientific settings such as single-cell biology, measurement processes (RNA-seq, protein assays, microscopy) are destructive, making it impossible to collect multiple measurements from the same cell. Prior approaches that leverage perturbation labels, such as multimodal VAEs, include reconstruction objectives that capture modality-specific noise and degrade matching quality, leaving a gap in how to align unpaired observations across disparate measurement spaces using only observable data.

## Input / Output

The method takes two sets of unpaired observations from different modalities (e.g., 200-dim gene expression PCs and 134-dim protein measurements, or 1024-dim image embeddings and 128-dim scVI embeddings), each annotated with a shared experimental perturbation label t. It produces a soft matching matrix M of size n₁×n₂ computed separately within each perturbation group, where M_ij encodes the probability that sample i from modality 1 corresponds to sample j from modality 2. This matrix can be directly used for cross-modality prediction via barycentric projection or stochastic sampling, yielding predicted values in the target modality's original space (e.g., log-transformed gene expression counts).

## Method

The core insight is to treat the perturbation label t as a causal intervention on a shared latent state Z, then use Rubin's propensity score p(t|Z) as an identifiable, minimal-dimension proxy for Z that is computable from either modality alone. Practically, this requires only training two independent classifiers (one per modality) to predict t from the observed data; the predicted class probabilities serve as the estimated propensity scores, which live in a T-dimensional simplex. A cross-modality distance is defined as the Euclidean distance between logit-transformed propensity scores, and a soft matching matrix is constructed via entropic optimal transport (Sinkhorn algorithm) or shared nearest neighbours within each perturbation group. Crucially, no reconstruction or latent invariance objective is needed—the propensity score provably discards modality-specific noise U^(e) by construction, and Proposition 3.1 guarantees it captures all shared information between Z and t. For downstream cross-modality prediction, the matching matrix defines a conditional distribution over the target modality, and an unbiased gradient estimator using two independent samples per training example avoids the bias of single-sample Monte Carlo.

## Results

On the synthetic interventional image dataset, PS+OT achieves a latent MSE of 0.0316 and a trace metric of 18.329×10⁻³, outperforming VAE+OT (MSE 0.0324, trace 7.733×10⁻³) and SCOT (MSE 0.0354, trace 0.596×10⁻³). On CITE-seq data, PS+OT achieves a FOSCTTM of 0.3049 and trace of 0.1163, substantially beating VAE+OT (FOSCTTM 0.3953, trace 0.0814), SCOT (FOSCTTM 0.4596, trace 0.0200), and scGLUE (FOSCTTM 0.4412, trace 0.0362). On the downstream cross-modal prediction task, PS+OT with the unbiased loss achieves R²=0.233 on CITE-seq, surpassing even the ground-truth paired baseline (R²=0.224), and achieves the lowest KL divergence (43.554) on the out-of-distribution PerturbSeq/image prediction task.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the multimodal VAE of Yang et al. (2021), PS+OT consistently wins on all alignment metrics: on CITE-seq, FOSCTTM improves from 0.3953 to 0.3049 and trace from 0.0814 to 0.1163; on synthetic images, trace improves from 7.733 to 18.329×10⁻³ (a 2.4× gain). The paper attributes this to the VAE's reconstruction objective forcing the encoder to capture modality-specific noise (U^(e)), which is counterproductive for matching. Against SCOT (Demetci et al., 2022), which uses Gromov-Wasserstein distance on within-modality pairwise distances, PS+OT achieves a 1.5× lower FOSCTTM (0.3049 vs. 0.4596) and a 5.8× higher trace (0.1163 vs. 0.0200) on CITE-seq. Against scGLUE (Cao & Gao, 2022), which leverages gene-protein graph structure, PS+OT again dominates with FOSCTTM 0.3049 vs. 0.4412 (SNN) and 0.5309 (OT). Notably, on the downstream CITE-seq prediction task, PS+OT with the unbiased OT-projected loss (R²=0.233) slightly exceeds the ground-truth paired baseline (R²=0.224), suggesting the soft matching acts as an implicit regularizer against modality-specific variation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes Rubin's (1974) propensity score theory from causal inference to solve the unpaired multimodal alignment problem, treating experimental perturbations as 'treatments' and modality-specific observations as 'potential outcomes.' It departs from Yang et al. (2021), who use a multimodal VAE with perturbation classification as a regularizer, by showing that the propensity score classification objective alone—without reconstruction or latent invariance losses—is both theoretically sufficient and empirically superior for matching. For the matching step, it builds on optimal transport (Sinkhorn) and shared nearest neighbours, but applies them to the propensity score space rather than to learned latent representations as in SCOT (Demetci et al., 2022) or scGLUE (Cao and Gao, 2022).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a theoretically grounded, minimal-architecture solution to unpaired multimodal alignment that requires only per-modality classifiers, making it broadly applicable to any setting with perturbation labels. A notable open question is whether the observed improvement of soft OT matching over ground-truth pair training (on CITE-seq) reflects a general regularization benefit or is dataset-specific, since confidence intervals overlap. The method's dependence on strong assumptions (injectivity of measurement functions, independence of treatment from modality noise) and on the availability of perturbation labels limits its scope, and the paper acknowledges that matching is fundamentally impossible without shared signal. Future work could explore relaxing these assumptions and extending to more than two modalities.
