## Problem

Learning from multidomain (tensorial) data defined on multiple interacting graphs has received little attention, and existing product-graph GNNs rely on discrete polynomial filters that inherit over-smoothing, require expensive grid searches for filter-order tuning, and are typically restricted to two domains. The paper addresses this by introducing a principled continuous framework that jointly models interactions across an arbitrary number of factor graphs without these limitations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242863/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a D-dimensional tensor (e.g., traffic flow over 207 spatial nodes × 6 temporal steps at 5-minute resolution, or temperature over 32–109 stations × 10 hourly steps) together with domain-specific factor graphs: a spatial graph built from Gaussian kernels on pairwise node distances and a temporal graph as a simple path graph. It outputs a forecast tensor at multiple future horizons (15/30/60 minutes for traffic; 1–5 hours for weather), with the number of learnable parameters independent of graph size, ensuring scalability to larger factor graphs.

## Method

CITRUS is derived as the continuous solution to a Tensorial PDE on Graphs (TPDEG), where the heat-flow equation is defined mode-wise over P factor Laplacians. The key insight is that the exponential graph filter on the Cartesian product graph factorizes into a Kronecker product of per-factor heat kernels, so the full product-graph EVD is replaced by small subsets of eigenpairs from each (much smaller) factor graph, reducing spectral-decomposition complexity from O((∏N_p)³) to O(∑N_p²K_p). Each CITRUS block applies mode-p tensorial multiplications with e^{−t_l L_p} filters (where t_l is a learnable receptive field), followed by a linear channel-mixing matrix and a non-linear activation. The learnable receptive fields allow the network to adaptively control the graph neighborhood per layer and per factor, eliminating the need for discrete filter-order grid searches and mitigating over-smoothing, as formalized via a Dirichlet-energy bound whose convergence rate depends on the smallest spectral gap of the factor graphs.

## Results

On the PemsBay traffic dataset, CITRUS achieves MAE of 1.21 (H=3), 1.48 (H=6), and 1.78 (H=12), outperforming the closest PG-based competitor GTCNN (1.25, 1.65, 2.27) with the largest gap at the longest horizon. On MetrLA, CITRUS reaches MAE 3.44 and RMSE 6.85 at H=12, beating GTCNN (3.55, 7.35) and GRUGCN (3.62, 7.33). For weather forecasting on NOAA, CITRUS achieves rNMSE of 0.04 at H=1, dramatically outperforming the next-best method GRUGCN (0.15) and GTCNN (0.17). On Molene, CITRUS attains rNMSE 0.23 at H=1, marginally beating SGP (0.24) and substantially outperforming GTCNN (0.39).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CITRUS outperforms all 10 baselines on PemsBay across every horizon and metric, with the most dramatic gains at H=12 where it reduces MAE from 2.27 (GTCNN) to 1.78 and RMSE from 4.99 to 3.99. On MetrLA, CITRUS matches or beats GTCNN and GRUGCN at H=3 but pulls clearly ahead at H=6 and H=12 (e.g., MAE 3.44 vs. 3.55 for GTCNN and 3.62 for GRUGCN at H=12). For weather forecasting on NOAA, CITRUS achieves rNMSE 0.04 at H=1, a 4–10× improvement over the next-best methods (GRUGCN 0.15, GTCNN 0.17), and maintains the lead through H=5 (0.36 vs. 0.37 for GRUGCN). On the smaller Molene dataset, CITRUS edges out SGP (0.23 vs. 0.24 at H=1) and substantially beats GTCNN (0.39) and GRUGCN (0.49). The ablation study (Table 3) further confirms that the joint product-graph formulation of CITRUS outperforms sequential TTS/STT pipelines and their continuous (CTTS/CSTT) variants, with the gap widening at longer horizons (MAE 3.44 vs. 3.61–3.67 at H=12 on MetrLA).

## Relation to Prior Work

CITRUS extends the continuous GNN (CGNN) paradigm [20, 21], which solves heat PDEs on a single graph via exponential spectral filters, to the multi-domain setting by formulating a tensorial PDE on Cartesian product graphs (TPDEG). It directly addresses the limitations of GTCNN [10], the only prior GNN framework for product graphs, by replacing discrete polynomial filters (which require expensive grid search and restrict receptive fields) with continuous separable heat kernels that admit adaptive, learnable graph neighborhoods. The spectral decomposition of the product Laplacian into factor-graph EVDs [23, 24] provides the computational backbone, reducing complexity from cubic in the product size to cubic in individual factor sizes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The TPDEG framework offers a principled, generalizable route to multi-domain graph learning beyond the two-factor (space+time) setting that dominates prior work, with clean theoretical guarantees on stability under graph perturbations and over-smoothing governed by factor spectral gaps. However, the main experiments are limited to two factor graphs; the three-factor results in the appendix are on synthetic ER graphs only, and the over-smoothing upper bound is acknowledged as loose when the condition ln s − (2/P)·t̃·λ̃ > 0. Open questions include extending to Kronecker and Strong products, tightening the over-smoothing analysis, and validating the framework on genuinely multi-domain (beyond spatiotemporal) real-world data.
