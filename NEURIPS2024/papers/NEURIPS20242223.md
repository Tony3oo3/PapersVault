## Problem

Policies trained in a simulator often fail catastrophically when deployed in the real world due to dynamics mismatch. Existing offline domain calibration methods (e.g., DROPO, DROID) rely on evolutionary algorithms like CMA-ES to tune physics parameters, which suffer from poor sample efficiency when the number of interrelated parameters is large. The paper asks: can we calibrate a high-dimensional, non-differentiable source domain to match target dynamics using only a handful of offline trajectories, with gradient-based efficiency?

## Input / Output

The method consumes a static offline trajectory dataset (up to 2×10⁵ transitions) collected by an unknown behavior policy in the target domain, along with a manipulable MuJoCo simulator whose physics parameters (gravity, body masses, joint dampings) are known only within rough initial ranges. It produces calibrated point-estimate physics parameters for the source domain and a policy trained from scratch on the calibrated simulator, which is then deployed directly to the target domain without further interaction. The source domain is non-differentiable, so calibration operates on the parameter distribution rather than gradient descent through the simulator.

## Method

Madoc formulates domain calibration as a bandit RL objective derived from minimizing KL divergence between source and target trajectory distributions, decomposed into a behavior-cloning term, a bandit reward (log-likelihood ratio of target vs. source transitions), and a prior regularizer. Two binary classifiers trained on offline vs. simulated transitions serve as a reward model to estimate the otherwise intractable transition probability ratio. To handle large parameter spaces, the calibration is modeled as a cooperative MARL problem: a VAE encodes each parameter's identity into a latent space based on its effect on transitions, k-means clusters parameters with similar dynamics impact into groups, and each group is assigned a calibration agent. The agents are trained with DOP (off-policy multi-agent decomposed policy gradients) for value decomposition and SAC for stochastic actor updates, with the prior distribution set as an EMA target network for stability. After calibration, a fresh SAC policy is trained on the calibrated source domain and deployed to the target.

## Results

On the D4RL benchmark (12 locomotion tasks), Madoc achieves an average normalized return of 88.3, outperforming the strongest baselines H2O (74.6) and MOREC (71.2) by 13.7 and 17.1 points respectively. On the more challenging NeoRL benchmark (9 tasks with narrower data distributions), Madoc reaches 70.8 average normalized return, surpassing the next-best method OTED (48.4) by 22.4 points. The single-agent ablation Madoc-S scores 68.9 on D4RL and 57.2 on NeoRL, confirming that the multi-agent decomposition contributes 19.4 and 13.6 points respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Madoc outperforms all six baselines on average across both benchmarks. On D4RL, it beats the strongest hybrid offline-and-online method H2O (74.6) by 13.7 points and the state-of-the-art offline model-based method MOREC (71.2) by 17.1 points; the evolutionary-algorithm-based calibrators DROPO (52.4) and DROID (47.2) lag far behind, confirming their poor sample efficiency in high-dimensional parameter spaces. On NeoRL, where data distributions are narrower, the gap widens further: Madoc (70.8) surpasses OTED (48.4), CQL (43.7), and MOREC (41.1) by 22.4, 27.1, and 29.7 points respectively, while H2O and DR+BC collapse to 35.4 and 38.8. The single-agent variant Madoc-S (68.9 on D4RL, 57.2 on NeoRL) is consistently outperformed by the full multi-agent Madoc, validating the cooperative MARL formulation. Notably, MOREC wins on two Hopper tasks (105.0, 106.0) where its learned dynamics reward function excels, but its performance is highly inconsistent across other tasks (e.g., 6.1 on Ant-med-rep), whereas Madoc maintains strong performance across all 21 tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

Madoc builds on the offline domain calibration line of work (DROID, DROPO) that uses static target-domain data to adjust source-domain physics parameters, but departs from their reliance on evolutionary algorithms (CMA-ES) for black-box optimization. It also extends the single-agent bandit RL formulation of OTED by recasting calibration as a cooperative multi-agent problem, leveraging DOP's value decomposition for credit assignment among parameter groups. The reward model architecture follows the discriminator-based approach from DARC (Eysenbach et al., ICLR 2020), and the VAE-based automatic parameter grouping is a novel component that has no direct precedent in the domain calibration literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in sim-to-real transfer: when many physics parameters must be calibrated simultaneously, evolutionary and single-agent methods scale poorly, and the multi-agent formulation with VAE-based grouping offers a principled way to reduce the effective search space. The consistent outperformance across 21 locomotion tasks on two benchmarks (D4RL, NeoRL) with limited data (2×10⁵ transitions) makes this relevant for safety-critical deployment where target-domain interaction is infeasible. Key limitations include evaluation restricted to MuJoCo continuous-control tasks (no vision-based or high-dimensional state spaces), and the authors acknowledge that the classifier-based reward model may not generalize to high-dimensional visual observations, suggesting diffusion models as a future direction.
