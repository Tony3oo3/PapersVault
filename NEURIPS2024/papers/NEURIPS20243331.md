## Problem

Active geo-localization (AGL) requires an agent to find a target within a search area using only sequential partial aerial observations under a strict time budget. Prior work (e.g., AiRLoc) assumes the goal is always specified as an aerial image from the same scene, which is unrealistic: in search-and-rescue or disaster scenarios, goal descriptions typically arrive as ground-level photographs or natural language text from social media. No existing method handles this cross-modality gap while simultaneously learning efficient, history-aware navigation policies that generalize zero-shot to unseen environments and goal modalities.

## Input / Output

The agent receives a stream of 300×300-pixel aerial sub-images (one per grid cell visited on a 5×5 or 10×10 discretized search area) and a single goal description in one of three modalities: an aerial image patch, a ground-level photograph, or a natural language caption. The goal's true grid position is never revealed; the agent must infer it purely from visual similarity across its sequential observations. The output is a sequence of four discrete navigation actions (up, down, left, right) executed within a fixed exploration budget B (e.g., 10 steps for 5×5 grids), with success defined as occupying the goal cell before the budget expires.

## Method

GOMAA-Geo is a three-stage framework. First, a CLIP-based Multi-Modal Feature Extractor (CLIP-MMFE) trains an aerial image encoder via InfoNCE contrastive loss against CLIP's frozen image encoder using co-located ground-level/aerial image pairs, producing a unified embedding space where aerial images, ground-level images, and text all yield compatible goal representations. Second, a Goal-Aware Supervised Pretraining (GASP) stage trains a Falcon LLM on a sequence-modeling task: given the full observation-action history and the goal embedding, it predicts which actions would bring the agent closer to the goal (binary cross-entropy over the action set), learning a history-aware, goal-conditioned latent representation. Third, a PPO actor-critic planner (three-layer MLPs) is trained with the LLM frozen, using a dense reward that gives +1 for moving closer to the goal, −1 for moving away or revisiting a state, and +2 for reaching the goal. The key design choice is that the LLM handles long-range sequence modeling and modality-agnostic goal encoding, while the lightweight RL planner handles the actual decision-making, combining the strengths of both.

## Results

On the Masa dataset (5×5 grid, B=10), GOMAA-Geo achieves a success ratio (SR) of 0.4090–0.8034 across start-to-goal distances C=4–8, substantially outperforming all baselines. The paper reports SR improvements of 129.00% to 232.67% relative to the strongest baseline (DiT) across evaluation settings. In the zero-shot generalization setting on xBD-disaster (trained only on pre-disaster Masa data), GOMAA-Geo achieves SR of 0.4002–0.7391, with improvements of 221.15% to 346.83% over baselines. GOMAA-Geo also generalizes across goal modalities (text, ground-level image, aerial image) with comparable SR (~0.40–0.78) despite being trained exclusively on aerial-image goals.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GOMAA-Geo outperforms all three learned baselines across every distance setting. Against AiRLoc [24] (the closest prior AGL method, which assumes goals are always aerial images), GOMAA-Geo achieves roughly 2–3× higher SR (e.g., 0.8034 vs. 0.2415 at C=7 on Masa). Against Decision Transformer (DiT) [8], which is the strongest baseline, GOMAA-Geo still leads by a wide margin (e.g., 0.7168 vs. 0.3567 at C=6). Against the history-free PPO policy [33], the gap is even larger (e.g., 0.7854 vs. 0.2595 at C=8). In the zero-shot xBD-disaster setting, the advantage over DiT widens further (0.7391 vs. 0.3390 at C=7), confirming that GOMAA-Geo's cross-modality alignment and history-aware LLM representation generalize better than sequence-modeling or RL-only approaches under distribution shift.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the active geo-localization (AGL) task introduced by Pirinen et al. (2022), which assumed goals are always specified as aerial images, to a goal-modality-agnostic setting where goals can be described as text, ground-level images, or aerial images. The cross-modality alignment module builds directly on CLIP (Radford et al., 2021) and the ground-remote alignment strategy of Mall et al. (2023), which demonstrated that satellite images can be bridged to CLIP's shared embedding space via co-located ground-level imagery. The planning component combines a GASP-pretrained LLM (Falcon) for history-aware representation with a PPO actor-critic, departing from prior AGL work that relied solely on LSTM-based history encoding (AiRLoc) or offline sequence modeling (Decision Transformer).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important gap in search-and-rescue UAV operations: the goal description is rarely available in the same modality as the agent's observations, yet prior methods assumed modality match. The demonstrated zero-shot generalization from aerial-image-only training to text and ground-level image goals, as well as to post-disaster scenes, suggests a path toward deployable multi-modal SAR agents. However, the MM-GAG evaluation dataset is small (73 images across 11 countries), the state-action space is a discrete grid with known ego-pose, and the framework's dependence on a specific LLM architecture (Falcon outperforms Llama-2 and Mistral by large margins) raises open questions about robustness and scalability to continuous navigation. The finding that modality-invariant CLIP-aligned representations are nearly as effective as modality-specific satellite encoders (SatMAE) for the aerial-goal setting is a useful practical insight for practitioners choosing between specialized and general-purpose representation backbones.
