## Problem

Boltzmann Generators, which learn to sample from equilibrium molecular distributions via normalizing flows, have always required system-specific training on the target molecule, negating much of their potential speed-up over classical MD simulations. This paper introduces the first framework for Boltzmann Generators that are transferable across chemical space, enabling zero-shot sampling from the Boltzmann distribution of previously unseen molecules without retraining.

## Input / Output

The model takes 3D Cartesian coordinates of all atoms as input, augmented with per-atom embeddings encoding atom type (54 topology-defined classes), amino acid identity (20 classes), and amino acid position in the peptide sequence. It outputs independent 3D conformations drawn from an approximation of the target Boltzmann distribution, along with exact log-likelihoods that yield importance weights for reweighting to the unbiased target. A post-processing step validates bond-graph isomorphism, corrects atom ordering to the topology, and checks chirality before samples are accepted.

## Method

The transferable Boltzmann Generator (TBG) is a continuous normalizing flow whose time-dependent vector field is parametrized by an E(n)-equivariant graph neural network (EGNN), ensuring the push-forward distribution inherits the permutation and rotation symmetries of the molecular system. Training uses conditional flow matching with a linear interpolation path between a standard Gaussian prior and target samples, normalized per-peptide by atom count to handle variable system sizes within a batch. The key architectural insight enabling transferability is the 'TBG + full' embedding, which encodes amino acid identity and sequence position in addition to fine-grained atom types, allowing the model to distinguish topologically distinct molecules sharing the same atom composition. At inference, generated samples are validated via bond-graph isomorphism and chirality checks, then reweighted using the exact CNF log-likelihood to recover unbiased Boltzmann statistics. Ablation studies show that even 10× shorter training trajectories suffice for effective transfer, and that biasing training data toward rare states improves free-energy recovery at the cost of slightly lower ESS.

## Results

On the alanine dipeptide system, TBG+full achieves a negative log-likelihood of -127.06 and an effective sample size (ESS) of 6.03% under the classical force field, substantially outperforming the prior BG+backbone baseline (NLL -109.02, ESS 1.56%). For the transferable dipeptide (2AA) setting, TBG+full achieves a mean ESS of 15.29% (range 4.08–31.93%) and a 98% correct-configuration rate across 8 unseen test peptides, compared to 0.48% ESS and 13% correct configurations for the minimal TBG architecture. Even when trained on 10× shorter trajectories, TBG+full (smaller) retains a 6.13% mean ESS and 96% correct-configuration rate, demonstrating strong data efficiency.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against BG+backbone [23] (the prior equivariant flow-matching Boltzmann Generator for alanine dipeptide), TBG+full wins decisively on both metrics: for the classical force field, ESS improves from 1.56% to 6.03% (≈4×) and NLL from -109.02 to -127.06; for the semi-empirical GFN2-xTB force field, ESS doubles from 0.50% to 1.03% and NLL improves by ~17 nats. Against Timewarp [11] (a transferable model that learns large MD time steps combined with Metropolis–Hastings acceptance), TBG+full achieves a lower Wasserstein distance between the generated and MD Ramachandran plots at all three computational budgets tested (30,000 energy evaluations, 12 h, and 24 h wall-clock on an A-100 GPU), with the gap being largest at the 30,000-energy-evaluation budget. Additionally, unweighted TBG+full samples exhibit lower mean energies than Timewarp exploration-mode samples, indicating a closer approximation to the true Boltzmann distribution without requiring acceptance steps.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Boltzmann Generator framework (Noé et al., 2019) and its recent Cartesian-coordinate instantiation via equivariant flow matching (Klein et al., 2023) by introducing the first architecture that generalizes zero-shot to unseen molecules. The key departure from prior BGs is the inclusion of topology-aware embeddings (amino acid identity and sequence position) in an EGNN-based vector field, enabling transfer across chemical space without retraining. The closest prior work, Timewarp (Klein et al., 2023), also targets transferable Boltzmann sampling but generates correlated samples via learned large time steps with Metropolis-Hastings acceptance, whereas this paper generates independent samples via a continuous normalizing flow trained with flow matching.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The demonstration that a single model trained on 200 dipeptides can produce unbiased Boltzmann samples for 100 unseen dipeptides with effective sample sizes of 13–15% opens a path toward eliminating per-system retraining in molecular sampling, which is a major practical bottleneck. The data-efficiency finding—that trajectories as short as 5 ns (missing many metastable states) still yield a transferable model that recovers those states at inference—suggests the model learns generalizable structural priors rather than memorizing specific conformational basins. The primary open question is scaling: the authors note that moving to tetrapeptides or proteins likely requires more expressive equivariant architectures and substantially more compute, and that coarse-graining (needed for larger systems) would forfeit the explicit energy function required for reweighting, reducing the method to a Boltzmann Emulator.
