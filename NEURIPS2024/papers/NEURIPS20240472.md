## Problem

Existing theoretical analyses of rank collapse in transformers have largely assumed full bidirectional attention and either ignored LayerNorm or restricted value matrices to the identity. This paper asks two questions left open by prior work: (1) do practical attention masks (causal, sparse, sliding-window) alter the rank collapse dynamics, and (2) can LayerNorm, under more general value matrices, actually prevent tokens from collapsing to a rank-one subspace?

## Input / Output

The analysis takes token representations X ∈ R^{N×d}, an attention mask formalized as a directed graph G (with properties like quasi-strong connectivity, radius r, and center nodes), and layer-wise weight matrices W_Q, W_K, W_V. It produces explicit exponential convergence rate bounds for μ(X) (a Frobenius-norm measure of token heterogeneity) that depend on the graph radius r and a lower bound ε on attention weights, as well as a full characterization of which equilibrium ranks (from 1 to full) can coexist under LayerNorm dynamics. The self-attention is treated as a discrete-time dynamical system rather than the continuous-time ODE used in prior work, more closely matching the actual layer-by-layer architecture.

## Method

The paper develops a graph-theoretic framework for analyzing masked self-attention dynamics, representing the attention mask as a directed graph and exploiting reachability and radius properties to derive convergence rates. For pure attention, it shows that quasi-strongly connected masks (including causal and sliding-window patterns) guarantee exponential rank collapse at a rate governed by the graph radius r, with local attention provably slowing collapse. For LayerNorm, it first proves exponential collapse persists when value matrices are orthogonal (using infinite products of non-negative matrices and Hartfiel's ergodicity results), then constructs nontrivial counterexamples with non-orthogonal value matrices (e.g., upper-triangular with off-diagonal w > 1) where the dynamics simultaneously possess equilibria of every rank from 1 to full. The key mechanism enabling non-collapse is LayerNorm's scaling effect, which stabilizes a cancellation process that generates a repulsive component counteracting the attractive pull of the center node—something that would diverge without normalization.

## Results

The paper establishes three main theoretical results: (1) without LayerNorm, tokens under self-attention with any quasi-strongly connected mask (including causal and sliding-window) collapse exponentially to rank one at rate (1−ε^r)^(t/r), where r is the graph radius; (2) with LayerNorm and orthogonal value matrices, tokens still collapse exponentially to a common point on the unit sphere at rate (1−Nε^(2r))^(t/(2r)); (3) with LayerNorm and appropriately chosen value matrices, the dynamics can simultaneously possess equilibria of any rank from 1 to full, and a generic class of input sequences provably does not converge to rank one. Numerically, on 3000 Wikipedia 128-token samples, μ(X^(t)) converges to zero exponentially for pure SANs across all four mask types (complete, causal, sliding window, uni-directional sliding window), but adding LayerNorm alone prevents μ from converging to zero in randomly initialized models. Smaller temperature d_QK alleviates the initial collapse rate, with the effect more pronounced under global attention and in shallower layers.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against external methods but instead ablates architectural components within a controlled BERT-based experiment. Five model variants are compared: pure SAN, SAN+skip, SAN+LayerNorm, SAN+skip+LayerNorm, and full transformer, each under four attention masks (complete, causal, sliding window, uni-directional sliding window). Pure SANs show exponential rank collapse for all masks, with more local masks (sliding window, uni-directional) slowing the convergence rate. Adding LayerNorm alone (without skip connections) is sufficient to prevent μ from converging to zero in randomly initialized models, whereas skip connections alone make μ unstable in deeper layers. In pretrained models, LayerNorm works together with skip connections to stabilize representations. The causal mask (one center node) yields a looser collapse bound than the complete graph (N center nodes), confirming the theoretical prediction that fewer center nodes slow rank collapse. Token geometry experiments on BERT, GPT-2, T5, and ALBERT show all models maintain full rank (minimal singular values stay well above zero) while exhibiting anisotropy (tokens concentrate in a narrow region), consistent with the paper's full-rank equilibrium characterization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the rank collapse analysis initiated by Dong et al. (ICML 2021), which proved exponential collapse under full bidirectional attention, by generalizing to arbitrary attention masks (causal, sparse, sliding window) via a graph-theoretic framework based on quasi-strongly connected graphs. It also builds on Geshkovski et al. (2023), who provided a continuous-time analysis of self-attention with LayerNorm under the restrictive assumption of identity value matrices, by working in discrete time with general value matrices and showing that the LayerNorm dynamics can fundamentally alter long-term behavior. The key departure from prior work is the refutation of the hypothesis (stated in Dong et al.) that LayerNorm plays no role in rank collapse, demonstrated through explicit counterexamples where LayerNorm with appropriate value matrices prevents convergence to a rank-one subspace.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that LayerNorm can provably prevent complete rank collapse for a generic class of input sequences challenges a widely-held assumption in the transformer literature and suggests that the self-attention dynamics with LayerNorm constitute a far more expressive nonlinear system than previously recognized. The finding that local/sparse attention masks slow the collapse rate provides theoretical justification for the practical adoption of sliding-window and sparse attention in modern LLMs. Open questions remain about whether the rich equilibrium structure (any rank from 1 to full) is practically reachable by trained models, whether undirected masks admit similar non-collapsing equilibria, and how the coexistence of full rank with anisotropic token geometry (observed empirically in BERT, GPT-2, T5, ALBERT) aids downstream learning.
