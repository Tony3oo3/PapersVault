## Problem

Existing cryo-EM reconstruction methods cannot simultaneously resolve both compositional heterogeneity (mixtures of different protein species) and conformational heterogeneity (continuous structural motion) in a single ab initio pass. Sequential strategies fail because the extremely low signal-to-noise ratio prevents pre-clustering images by protein type, and unknown orientations make consensus poses unreliable for large motions. Neural-based methods like DRGN-AI and CryoDRGN2 handle conformational variability but lack the representational capacity to model multiple distinct protein structures within one dataset.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a set of N 2D cryo-EM projection images (typically 128×128 or 150×150 pixels) along with per-image CTF parameters, with no pre-estimated poses or class labels required. It outputs K neural-field-parameterized 3D density maps (one per compositional class), per-image pose estimates (rotation in SO(3) plus 2D in-plane translation), per-image d-dimensional conformational latent coordinates, and per-image K-dimensional class score vectors. The density maps are represented as continuous functions via residual MLPs with sinusoidal Fourier features rather than discrete voxel grids, and the number of classes K must be specified a priori.

## Method

Hydra parameterizes the space of accessible structures as a finite union of K low-dimensional manifolds, each implemented as a residual MLP (3 hidden layers of width 128, ReLU activations, Fourier features with 64 base frequencies) that maps a d-dimensional conformation vector to a 3D electron scattering potential. The key architectural insight is a likelihood-based loss: the negative log-likelihood of a Gaussian mixture model over the observed images, where the mixture components correspond to the K neural fields and the gating is handled by per-image score vectors optimized by SGD rather than a separate classifier. Optimization uses a hybrid strategy: hierarchical pose search (HPS) on a predefined SO(3)×R² grid (4,608 rotations × 49 translations via Hopf fibration and Healpix) is alternated with stochastic gradient descent on conformations, scores, and network weights for the first ~2 million images, after which poses are also optimized by SGD for efficiency. The autodecoding framework (no encoder) is deliberately used because encoders tend to memorize images under the extreme noise levels (SNR ~0.01) typical of cryo-EM.

## Results

On the tomotwin3 synthetic dataset (3,000 images, 3 protein species), Hydra (K=3) achieves perfect classification (ARI = 1.00) and per-image FSC of 0.394 (6id1) and 0.367 (4cr2), outperforming DRGN-AI (ARI 0.59, FSC 0.040) and CryoDRGN2 (ARI 0.36, FSC 0.070) while matching or exceeding CryoSPARC (ARI 1.00, FSC 0.367/0.338). On the ribosplike dataset (15,000 images, 3 species with conformational motion), Hydra (K=3) achieves 0.414 per-image FSC, 0.997 ARI, and 1.07° median pose error, surpassing all baselines on reconstruction quality and pose accuracy. On the experimental ryanodine receptor dataset (85,656 particles), Hydra (K=4) separates three protein complexes (RyR, p97, CIII) plus a junk class in a single ab initio run, whereas DRGN-AI (K=1) fails to recover non-RyR structures.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DRGN-AI (single neural field, K=1), Hydra wins decisively on all metrics: ARI improves from 0.59 to 1.00 on tomotwin3, per-image FSC jumps from 0.040 to 0.394 (6id1), and on ribosplike the pose error drops from 45.4° to 1.07° while FSC nearly doubles (0.207→0.414). Against CryoDRGN2, Hydra achieves perfect ARI (1.00 vs 0.36) on tomotwin3 and a 0.015 FSC advantage on ribosplike (0.414 vs 0.399), with a slightly better ARI (0.997 vs 0.986). Against CryoSPARC, the closest competitor, Hydra matches classification (ARI 1.00 on tomotwin3) but gains a consistent FSC edge (0.394 vs 0.367 on 6id1; 0.414 vs 0.344 on ribosplike) and a lower pose error (1.07° vs 1.58°). Notably, Hydra uses only 2 conformational dimensions versus 8 for DRGN-AI and CryoDRGN2, yet still outperforms them, demonstrating that the mixture-of-fields architecture is the key factor rather than latent space capacity.

## Relation to Prior Work

Hydra extends the neural field representation of DRGN-AI [26] by replacing the single implicit neural volume with a mixture of K residual MLPs, each specialized to one compositional state, and adapts DRGN-AI's two-step hierarchical pose search to the multi-map setting. Conceptually, the method is an instance of the implicitly-localized mixture-of-experts (MILE) framework from Jacobs et al. [19], where the gating mechanism is handled by an autodecoding likelihood rather than a separate classifier, motivated by the extremely low SNR of cryo-EM images. The key departure from prior neural-based methods (CryoDRGN, CryoDRGN2, DRGN-AI) is that they all rely on a single neural field and thus cannot represent fundamentally different protein shapes, while classical methods (RELION, CryoSPARC) handle discrete classes but not continuous conformational motion within each class.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Hydra broadens the scope of cryo-EM reconstruction to complex, unpurified samples (e.g., cell lysates, in situ cryo-ET) where multiple protein complexes with distinct conformations coexist, a setting that was previously intractable for neural-based methods. A key limitation is the need to specify the number of classes K a priori, which in practice requires expensive hyperparameter sweeps; the authors identify adaptive or hierarchical K selection as an important future direction. The method also currently enforces a shared latent dimension d across all classes, a constraint that could be relaxed with dictionary-based representations. Extending Hydra to subtomogram averaging for cryo-ET data is highlighted as a promising path toward structural biology discoveries in native cellular contexts.
