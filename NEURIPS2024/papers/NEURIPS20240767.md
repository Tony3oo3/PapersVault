## Problem

Existing PAC-Bayes bounds for unbounded losses depend on a free parameter λ that cannot be exactly optimized without resorting to approximate union-bound grids, and their bounded-CGF assumptions are uniform across all models, discarding model-specific concentration properties. This paper addresses both limitations by deriving a bound that permits exact λ optimization and introduces a model-dependent CGF framework that exploits per-model tail behavior.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240767/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a training set D of n i.i.d. samples, a data-independent prior π over a model class Θ ⊂ ℝ^d, and a non-negative loss function ℓ whose cumulant generating function Λ_θ(λ) is finite on [0, b). It produces a high-probability (1−δ) upper bound on the population Gibbs risk E_ρ[L(θ)] expressed as the empirical Gibbs risk plus an infimum over λ ∈ [0,b) of a complexity term involving KL(ρ|π), log(n/δ), and the posterior-averaged CGF E_ρ[Λ_θ(λ)]. A key output is the optimal posterior ρ*, which is not the standard Gibbs posterior but additionally penalizes models with large CGFs, yielding novel regularized objectives.

## Method

The core result (Theorem 7) extends the Cramér-Chernoff bound to the PAC-Bayesian setting by controlling the exponential moment of the Cramér transform of the generalization gap via a novel lemma (Lemma 6) that stochastically dominates nΛ_θ*(gen(θ,D)) by an Exp(1) random variable. The proof combines Donsker-Varadhan change of measure with Markov's inequality and the tail bound from Lemma 6, yielding a Pareto-distributed moment that integrates to n/(n−m), ultimately giving a log(n/δ) penalty that allows exact optimization of λ. Theorem 11 then generalizes the bounded-CGF assumption to a model-dependent function ψ(θ,λ), producing bounds where the posterior-averaged regularizer E_ρ[ψ(θ,λ)] directly shapes the optimal posterior. This framework instantiates to generalized sub-Gaussian losses (model-specific variance proxies), L2-norm regularization (via parameter Lipschitz assumptions), and input-gradient regularization (via log-Sobolev inequalities), each yielding a MAP estimate that corresponds to a known regularized objective with a theoretically justified trade-off parameter.

## Results

This is primarily a theoretical paper; its main contributions are novel PAC-Bayes bounds (Theorem 7, Theorem 11) rather than empirical benchmarks. The key theoretical result is that the free parameter λ in PAC-Bayes bounds for unbounded losses can be exactly optimized at a log(n) penalty, without resorting to union-bound grids. Empirically, on InceptionV3 trained on CIFAR-10, the paper verifies that the log-Sobolev assumption (Assumption 2) holds with C ≈ 0.005, and demonstrates that models with smaller L2-norms or input-gradient norms have substantially smaller CGFs (e.g., the L2-regularized model achieves 86.6% test accuracy with log-loss 0.49, versus 84.3% and 0.65 for the unregularized model).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not run head-to-head empirical comparisons against other methods; its comparisons are theoretical. Corollary 9 (bounded CGF with exact λ optimization) is shown to be tighter than the general bound of Rodríguez-Gálvez et al. (2024) when KL(ρ|π) ≥ √(6n/e)/π − 1, and recovers the Langford-Seeger bound (Corollary 8) for 0-1 loss. Compared to Hellström and Durisi (2021), which achieves exact λ optimization only for sub-Gaussian losses, Corollary 9 extends exact optimization to the general bounded-CGF case at the cost of a log(n) penalty. The model-dependent bounds (Theorem 11, Corollaries 13–16) go beyond prior work by introducing the CGF term E_ρ[ψ(θ,λ)] directly into the posterior optimization, yielding optimal posteriors that are not the standard Gibbs posterior and providing PAC-Bayesian certificates for L2 regularization and input-gradient regularization that were previously only justified heuristically.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Cramér-Chernoff large-deviation framework into the PAC-Bayesian setting, building on the bounded CGF assumption of Banerjee and Montufar (2021) and the oracle bounds of Alquier et al. (2016) and Germain et al. (2016). The key technical departure is a novel lemma (Lemma 6) that controls the survival function of the Cramér transform of the generalization gap via a Pareto-distributed random variable, enabling exact optimization of the free parameter λ without union-bound grids. The model-dependent CGF framework (Definition 10) generalizes the uniform bounded-CGF assumption and draws on Masegosa and Ortega (2023) for empirical evidence that individual models within a class have vastly different CGFs, as well as on log-Sobolev inequalities (Chafai, 2004) to connect input-gradient norms to generalization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The main practical impact is that Theorem 11 provides a unified PAC-Bayes framework from which L2 regularization, generalized sub-Gaussian bounds, and input-gradient regularization emerge as natural consequences of the optimal posterior, rather than being imposed through the prior. This gives theoretical certificates for regularization techniques that previously lacked PAC-Bayes justification, and the resulting optimal posteriors go beyond the standard Gibbs form. A key limitation is the implicit sub-exponential (light-tailed) assumption on losses, and the log n penalty for exact λ optimization, though the authors note this is virtually the same cost as grid-based union-bound approaches. Open questions include improving the logarithmic penalty beyond Lemma 6 and extending the framework to heavy-tailed losses or two-tailed bounds.
