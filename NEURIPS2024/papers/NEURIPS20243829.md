## Problem

LLMs exhibit a substantial gap (over 17%) between their vanilla question-answering accuracy and their ability to consistently apply a shared generic fact across multiple scenarios (abstract reasoning accuracy), whereas humans show only a small disparity. This reveals that current LLMs cannot flexibly leverage the generic facts stored in their parameters to guide reasoning across diverse contexts, raising the question of whether they are genuinely reasoning or merely pattern-matching.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243829/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the model receives a multiple-choice question with a few options and must produce a short explanation followed by the selected answer, without any generic fact provided as input. During training, each sample is presented in two paired forms: a K-example where the generic fact is explicitly given alongside the question, and an R-example where only the question is given, both targeting the same answer-and-explanation output. The AbsR dataset contains 18,020 training examples (9,010 questions) supported by 4,613 generic facts, with each fact backing 1–3 questions across varied scenarios.

## Method

MeanLearn is a supervised fine-tuning paradigm that trains an LLM on paired K-examples ⟨X, r, Y⟩ and R-examples ⟨X, Y⟩ guided by the same generic fact r, optimizing two conditional likelihoods simultaneously so the model implicitly internalizes the generic fact (Knowledge) and learns how to apply it across different question contexts (Reasoning). The AbsR dataset is constructed by sampling 4,613 high-confidence generic facts from GenericsKB and using GPT-4 to generate scenario-diverse multiple-choice questions with generic-fact-guided explanations for each fact. Training uses LoRA-based parameter-efficient fine-tuning for one epoch at a 5e-5 learning rate with AdamW, applied to Orca-2 (7B/13B), LLaMA-3 (8B), and Mistral (7B) base models. The key distinction from standard CoT or instruction tuning is that the generic fact is never provided at test time; the model must recall and apply it implicitly, mimicking how humans instinctively invoke general principles without explicit prompting.

## Results

On the 7B scale, MeanLearn achieves an average vanilla accuracy of 65.28% and AbsAcc of 53.39% across eight OOD benchmarks, outperforming all open-access baselines. On the 13B scale, MeanLearn reaches 64.23% vanilla accuracy and 50.51% AbsAcc, again leading all open-access competitors. Notably, the AbsR benchmark (the paper's own dataset) shows the largest gains: MeanLearn 7B scores 77.00% vanilla accuracy vs. Orca-2's 73.50%, and 64.58% AbsAcc vs. Orca-2's 60.42%. The 8B MeanLearn (built on LLaMA-3) achieves 67.12% vanilla accuracy and 55.66% AbsAcc, approaching the >20B PaLM-2 (65.77% / 54.99%) despite a 2.5× size gap.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MeanLearn consistently outperforms all open-access baselines. Against Orca-2 (its base model), MeanLearn 7B gains +3.44 points in average vanilla accuracy (65.28 vs. 61.84) and +4.53 in AbsAcc (53.39 vs. 48.86); the 13B variant gains +3.89 and +4.53 respectively. Against LLaMA-2, the margins are even larger: MeanLearn 7B exceeds LLaMA-2 7B by +22.59 vanilla and +24.16 AbsAcc. Against Vicuna and WizardLM, MeanLearn 7B leads by +8.80 and +34.00 in average vanilla accuracy, respectively. On the 8B scale, MeanLearn narrowly beats LLaMA-3 (+0.92 vanilla, +0.95 AbsAcc), which the authors attribute to LLaMA-3's dense pre-trained knowledge making it harder to inject new patterns. Against the limited-access GPT-3.5 (>20B), MeanLearn 7B still trails by 6.18 vanilla and 6.47 AbsAcc, but the 7B MeanLearn matches PaLM-2 (>20B) within 0.49 vanilla points. On Mistral-7B (Table 7), MeanLearn improves average vanilla accuracy from 70.47 to 73.50 and AbsAcc from 56.17 to 59.87, confirming cross-architecture generalizability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant chain-of-thought (CoT) paradigm for LLM reasoning, which focuses on step-by-step explanations for individual questions, by instead centering on the underlying generic fact that unifies multiple questions. It builds on the e-CARE causal reasoning dataset and GenericsKB knowledge base, and draws conceptual inspiration from implicit knowledge learning via hidden variables (Du et al., 2019), but repurposes these into a paired-example training paradigm (MeanLearn) that simultaneously teaches knowledge injection and reasoning application. Unlike Orca-2's supervised fine-tuning on reasoning traces—which the paper characterizes as rote learning that can erode factual knowledge—MeanLearn explicitly pairs generic-fact-conditioned examples with fact-free examples to induce implicit generalization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The quantified 17%+ gap between vanilla accuracy and AbsAcc across all tested LLMs (including GPT-3.5) provides concrete evidence that current models do not truly abstract generic principles, reframing the 'are LLMs reasoning or memorizing?' debate with a measurable criterion. The finding that Orca-2 13B knows fewer generic facts than its 7B counterpart (12.78% vs. 26.97%) challenges the assumption that scale monotonically improves knowledge retention. Open questions include whether AbsR's 18K examples suffice for robust generalization, whether the approach extends to complex multi-step generic facts (causal chains, logical rules), and whether the modest math improvements (Table 8) reflect genuine transfer or benchmark-specific effects.
