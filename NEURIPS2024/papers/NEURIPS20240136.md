## Problem

Offline multi-agent learning remains underexplored compared to the single-agent setting: Q-learning-based methods suffer from extrapolation errors, and sequence-modeling approaches (e.g., transformers) treat each agent independently without modeling inter-agent coordination. While diffusion models have recently shown strong distributional modeling for single-agent offline RL, applying them to multi-agent problems is non-trivial—using independent diffusion models per agent loses coordination, while concatenating all agents into one sequence breaks agent symmetry and scales poorly. MADIFF is the first diffusion-based framework that unifies decentralized policy, centralized control, and teammate modeling for offline multi-agent tasks.

## Input / Output

The diffusion model operates on observation trajectories (not state-action pairs), with a planning horizon H and optional history horizon C. In centralized mode, the input is the joint observation vector of all agents plus a global conditioning signal (e.g., return); in decentralized mode, each agent conditions only on its own local observation (or history) yet the model still generates the full joint trajectory for all agents, effectively performing teammate modeling. Actions are recovered from the generated next observation via a per-agent inverse dynamics model. Because inter-agent information is aggregated through attention rather than concatenation, the architecture is index-free: the input order of agents is arbitrary and does not affect outputs.

## Method

MADIFF uses a U-Net backbone per agent (1-D convolutions over the time dimension) with multi-head attention layers inserted before every decoder block. The attention is computed on the skip-connected encoder features across all agents, so each agent's denoising step dynamically fuses information from every other agent's encoded trajectory, enabling coordination at every diffusion step. Training minimizes a joint reverse-diffusion noise-prediction loss over the full multi-agent trajectory plus a per-agent inverse-dynamics loss, with classifier-free guidance (Bernoulli-dropped conditioning) to support return-conditioned generation. At inference, low-temperature sampling with classifier-free guidance steers generation toward high-return trajectories; in decentralized execution, agent i conditions on its own observation while the shared attention still lets it predict and react to all teammates' planned trajectories. The shared U-Net and attention parameters across agents keep the model compact and allow batching all agents' trajectories through a single network, making inference time nearly independent of the number of agents.

## Results

On offline MARL tasks (Table 1), MADIFF-D (decentralized) achieves the best or near-best performance across MPE, MA Mujoco, and SMAC. On MPE Spread Expert, MADIFF-C scores 116.7±3.0 vs. 114.9±2.6 for OMAR (best baseline), while MADIFF-D scores 95.0±5.3. On MA Mujoco 2halfcheetah Good, MADIFF-D reaches 8246±342, surpassing MA-TD3+BC (7025±439) by ~18%. On SMAC 3m Good, MADIFF-C achieves 19.9±0.1 win rate, edging out MA-CQL (19.6±0.3). For multi-agent trajectory prediction on the NBA dataset (Table 2), MADIFF-C reduces ADE from 15.15 to 7.92 and FDE from 24.91 to 14.06 at trajectory length 20, roughly halving the displacement errors of Baller2Vec++.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MADIFF-D outperforms all Q-learning-based baselines (MA-ICQ, MA-TD3+BC, MA-CQL, OMAR) on MPE tasks, with the largest gaps on mixed-quality datasets (e.g., Spread Medium: 64.9 vs. 47.9 for OMAR; Tag Medium: 77.2 vs. 66.7 for OMAR). On MA Mujoco, MADIFF-D surpasses MA-TD3+BC by 18% on 2halfcheetah Good (8246 vs. 7025) and by 13% on 2ant Good (2946 vs. 2922), while OMAR collapses to 1434 on 2halfcheetah Good. On SMAC, MADIFF-D is competitive with MA-CQL and MADT, achieving the best win rate on 3m Good (19.9 vs. 19.6 for MA-CQL) and 8m Good (19.8 vs. 19.6 for MA-ICQ), though the margins are small. For multi-agent trajectory prediction on the NBA dataset, MADIFF-C nearly halves ADE (7.92 vs. 15.15) and FDE (14.06 vs. 24.91) compared to Baller2Vec++ at trajectory length 20, though Baller2Vec++ achieves a lower minFDE20 (5.60 vs. 7.61) due to its higher sampling variance allowing best-of-20 selection. Overall, MADIFF-C (centralized) consistently outperforms MADIFF-D (decentralized), confirming the value of global information, while MADIFF-D still surpasses all decentralized baselines in most settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

MADIFF extends the diffusion-model-for-decision-making line (Janner et al. 2022, Ajay et al. 2023) from single-agent offline RL to the multi-agent setting, replacing the independent per-agent diffusion models or centralized value functions used in prior offline MARL work (MADT, OMAR, MA-ICQ) with a shared attention-based U-Net that performs inter-agent information exchange at every decoder layer during denoising. The CTDE paradigm is inherited from Q-learning-based multi-agent RL (QMIX, MADDPG), but here it is realized through a generative model rather than a value function, and the attention mechanism—borrowed from transformers—is repurposed to aggregate skip-connected encoder features across agents in an index-free manner. The inverse-dynamics + trajectory-diffusion paradigm and classifier-free guidance with low-temperature sampling are carried over directly from Ajay et al. (2023).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MADIFF is the first diffusion-based offline multi-agent framework, unifying decentralized policy, centralized controller, teammate modeling, and multi-agent trajectory prediction under a single architecture, which simplifies the methodological landscape for offline MAL. The finding that teammate modeling emerges as a free byproduct of joint trajectory generation—and that it measurably improves decentralized performance (Table 9)—suggests a new design principle for multi-agent generative models. Key open questions include scalability beyond ~8 agents (where per-agent trajectory inference becomes infeasible) and robustness in highly stochastic environments, where sequence-modeling methods remain vulnerable to chance high-reward trajectories. A potential direction is inferring latent teammate representations rather than full trajectories to address the scalability bottleneck.
