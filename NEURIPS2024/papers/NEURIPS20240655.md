## Problem

Existing long-term time series forecasting (LTSF) models treat discretely sampled data as sequences without recovering the continuous dynamical systems that generate them. While Koopman theory has been applied to model temporal dynamics, it assumes deterministic systems, which fails for real-world data exhibiting chaotic behavior (confirmed by positive Maximal Lyapunov Exponents across mainstream LTSF datasets). This paper asks: can chaos theory's concept of attractor invariance provide a principled framework for modeling and forecasting from discretely sampled chaotic dynamical systems?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240655/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes multivariate time series with input length 96 and produces forecasts at horizons of 96, 192, 336, and 720 steps. A channel-independent strategy is applied: each variable is independently embedded into a shared m-dimensional phase space via coordinate delay reconstruction (embedding dimension m and time delay τ determined by the CC mutual information method), then non-overlapping patches of length p are formed to yield a tensor of shape B×L×D where D=mp. The final output is projected back to the observation space via a learned observation function W_h, producing per-variable forecast sequences.

## Method

Attraos comprises three stages grounded in chaos theory. First, a non-parametric Phase Space Reconstruction (PSR) embeds each channel into an m-dimensional phase space using time-delay coordinates, recovering a topologically equivalent dynamical trajectory without learned parameters. Second, a Multi-Resolution Dynamic Memory Unit (MDMU) projects the trajectory onto orthogonal Legendre polynomial subspaces at hierarchically increasing window sizes (θ, 2θ, 4θ, …), using a diagonal A matrix (a rough approximation of the Hippo-LegT matrix) and time-varying B and Δ parameters; the Blelloch scan algorithm computes the linear recurrence in O(log L) time while implicitly integrating the multi-scale projection. Third, a frequency-enhanced local evolution strategy applies Fourier transformation to the polynomial-domain representations, selects M dominant low-frequency modes as attractor structures, and evolves them with a learned linear operator W_i ∈ ℂ^{N×N} before back-projecting to the original scale. This frequency-domain evolution amplifies attractor differences and suppresses noise, and the entire pipeline uses only ~0.2M parameters (one-twelfth of PatchTST).

## Results

Attraos achieves the best average MSE and MAE across all 7 mainstream LTSF datasets (input length 96, horizons {96,192,336,720}), with headline values of MSE 0.423/MAE 0.420 on ETTh1, MSE 0.246/MAE 0.271 on Weather, and MSE 0.187/MAE 0.157 on Crypto. On the chaotic datasets, Attraos leads with average MSE 0.847 on Lorenz96-3d and MSE 0.454 on Air Convection, outperforming the next-best Koopa by 7.8% and 2.2% respectively. Notably, Attraos achieves these results with only 0.2M parameters, roughly one-twelfth of PatchTST's 2.4M.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Attraos outperforms all seven baselines on every mainstream LTSF dataset in both MSE and MAE. The closest competitors are RWKV-TS and Koopa, which trail Attraos by roughly 0.02–0.03 MSE on most datasets (e.g., ETTh1: 0.454/0.450 vs. Attraos 0.423; Weather: 0.256/0.247 vs. 0.246). SSM-based models (Mamba4TS, S-Mamba) consistently outperform Transformer-based ones (PatchTST, iTransformer), while GPT-TS (a frozen pretrained LLM) performs only mid-pack, suggesting direct LLM application struggles with temporal dynamics. On the chaotic datasets, Attraos shows a wider margin: on Lorenz96-3d it beats Koopa (next best) by 7.8% MSE (0.847 vs. 0.919), and on Air Convection it edges out RWKV-TS by 2.2% (0.454 vs. 0.464). DLinear, a simple linear model, is surprisingly competitive on chaotic data (second-best on Air Convection), while deep Transformer models degrade most severely, highlighting their difficulty modeling chaotic dynamics. Attraos achieves all of this with only 0.2M parameters versus PatchTST's 2.4M (1/12th), making it the most parameter-efficient top performer.

## Relation to Prior Work

Attraos sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) State Space Models (Hippo, S4, Mamba) for efficient sequence modeling, which it extends with a multi-resolution polynomial projection (MDMU) and a Blelloch-scan-based implicit computation; (2) Koopman-theory-based forecasting (Koopa, Koopman Neural Forecaster), from which it deliberately departs by acknowledging the chaotic (non-deterministic) nature of real-world data evidenced by positive Lyapunov exponents, and instead leveraging attractor invariance; and (3) classical chaos theory tools (Takens' phase space reconstruction, Hopfield networks for attractor memory), which are repurposed as non-parametric embedding and evolution mechanisms within a modern deep learning architecture. The paper is the first to introduce a non-parametric PSR embedding into LTSF and to frame the forecasting problem explicitly through the lens of strange attractor dynamics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The chaos-theory framing provides a principled, physics-grounded alternative to purely data-driven LTSF models, and the positive Lyapunov exponent analysis across all mainstream datasets offers a concrete justification for why deterministic Koopman-based methods may be fundamentally limited. The 1/12 parameter efficiency relative to PatchTST is practically attractive for deployment. However, the empirical gains over strong SSM baselines (Mamba4TS, RWKV-TS) are modest and consistent rather than dramatic, and the non-parametric PSR step introduces sensitivity to the CC method for choosing embedding dimension and delay. Open questions include whether the attractor-invariance assumption holds under distribution shift, whether trainable PSR embeddings (mentioned as future work) can resolve the instability of the non-parametric approach, and whether the framework scales to truly high-dimensional chaotic systems beyond the 40-d Lorenz96 case study.
