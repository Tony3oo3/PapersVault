## Problem

Discrete diffusion models for language modeling have historically shown a significant log-likelihood gap compared to autoregressive (AR) models, with masked diffusion in particular being considered a weak baseline. This paper shows that the gap is largely due to suboptimal training recipes and unnecessarily complex objectives, rather than a fundamental limitation of the masked diffusion framework itself.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20244134/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a tokenized text sequence (context length 128 for LM1B, 1024 for OWT) or a DNA sequence (context length 1024) and independently masks each token with probability (1−α_t) at a randomly sampled time step t∈[0,1]. It produces either a full generated sequence via iterative unmasking (ancestral sampling) or an arbitrary-length sequence via semi-autoregressive (SAR) decoding, where the last L−L′ tokens of the previous block serve as a fixed prefix for the next generation round. The model is encoder-only (DiT or MosaicBERT), so generation is non-causal and all tokens are denoised in parallel at each step.

## Method

MDLM applies absorbing-state (masking) diffusion to discrete tokens, where the forward process independently replaces each token with [MASK] with probability (1−α_t). The key technical contribution is the SUBS parameterization of the reverse unmasking process, which enforces two structural properties—zero masking probabilities (the [MASK] logit is set to −∞) and carry-over unmasking (unmasked tokens are copied through unchanged). These properties enable a Rao-Blackwellized simplification of the D3PM ELBO: by analytically evaluating expectations that the SUBS constraints force to zero, the complex discrete-time diffusion loss reduces to a weighted average of standard masked cross-entropy (MLM) losses, and the continuous-time limit (T→∞) yields a clean integral objective. The architecture is a DiT-style encoder-only transformer with rotary positional embeddings and optional time-step conditioning, trained via standard gradient descent on the simplified objective. Efficient inference exploits the fact that unmasked tokens never change, allowing cached network evaluations to skip time steps where no new tokens unmask, and the SAR decoding scheme enables generation of arbitrary-length text by reusing the tail of the previous block as a prefix.

## Results

On LM1B, MDLM trained for 327B tokens achieves a perplexity upper bound of ≤23.00, within 14% of the AR Transformer baseline (20.86) and a 17% improvement over the previous best diffusion model SEDD (≤32.79 at 33B tokens). On OpenWebText (262B tokens), MDLM reaches ≤23.21 PPL versus 17.54 for AR. For semi-autoregressive generation of 2048-token sequences, MDLM achieves a generative PPL of 27.18 (vs. 35.43 for SSD-LM) while being ~25–30× faster (89.3 vs. 2473.9 sec/seq). On the HG38 DNA genome, MDLM fine-tuning of Caduceus achieves PPL ≤3.199, outperforming SEDD (≤3.216) and Plaid (≤3.240) while approaching the AR Mamba baseline (3.067).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MDLM decisively outperforms all prior diffusion baselines on LM1B: it improves over SEDD [33] by 17% (27.04 vs. 32.79 at 33B tokens), over DiffusionBert [26] by 2.4× (27.04 vs. 63.78), over D3PM [1] by 2.8× (27.04 vs. 76.90), and over BERT-Mouth [64] and Diffusion-LM [30] by >5×. Against AR baselines, MDLM at 327B tokens (23.00) is within 14% of the retrained AR Transformer (20.86) and slightly beats Transformer-XL [13] (23.5) but trails OmniNet [61] (21.5). In zero-shot evaluation (Table 3), MDLM surpasses AR on Lambada (47.52 vs. 51.28), Pubmed (41.89 vs. 49.01), and Arxiv (37.37 vs. 41.73), suggesting diffusion models may be more robust out-of-domain. For SAR generation, MDLM beats SSD-LM [24] by 23% in generative PPL (27.18 vs. 35.43) while being ~28× faster. On DNA, MDLM (≤3.199) edges out SEDD (≤3.216) and Plaid [23] (≤3.240) but remains slightly behind AR Mamba [21] (3.067) and HyenaDNA [39] (3.153). On GLUE, BERT+MDLM fine-tuning (82.06 avg) matches or slightly exceeds BERT pre-training (81.62) while far surpassing the AR baseline (74.88), confirming that diffusion fine-tuning preserves representation quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions masked (absorbing-state) diffusion as a strict, simplified subset of the general D3PM framework (Austin et al., 2021) and departs from the score-matching formulation of SEDD (Lou et al., 2023) by deriving a Rao-Blackwellized variational objective that reduces to a weighted average of masked language modeling losses. The SUBS parameterization (zero masking probabilities and carry-over unmasking) is the key technical departure, enabling both a tighter ELBO and a numerically stable implementation that avoids materializing full transition matrices. Conceptually, the paper bridges the gap between BERT-style encoder-only models and generative diffusion by showing that a principled masking-rate schedule endows BERT with generation capabilities, going beyond the ad-hoc Gibbs sampling approaches of prior BERT-as-generative-model work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is that BERT-style encoders can now be converted into generative models with a principled objective, and the semi-autoregressive sampler enables arbitrary-length text generation at 25-30× the speed of prior SAR diffusion methods. The finding that engineering choices (vocabulary size, DiT architecture, numerical stability) contribute more to performance than the theoretical formulation challenges the field's assumption that the discrete diffusion framework itself was the bottleneck. Open questions include whether the remaining 15-25% perplexity gap to AR models closes at scale, whether the simplified objective transfers to larger architectures, and whether the DNA sequence results generalize beyond the HG38 reference genome.
