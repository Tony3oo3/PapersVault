## Problem

Searching the large space of brain stimulation parameters (e.g., 4,560 double-electrode patterns from a 96-electrode array) to drive neural population activity toward specified states is intractable within a single experimental session, and neural recording instabilities make it difficult to merge stimulation-response samples across sessions. No prior framework combined cross-session data merging with a predictive model capable of generalizing to untested stimulation configurations under fast online computation constraints.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240760/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The CNN takes a 10×10 binary grid encoding the spatial positions of stimulated electrodes on the Utah array and outputs a t-dimensional vector (t=2 in this study) representing the predicted latent activity state in the aligned FA space. During closed-loop operation, the system consumes real-time 50 ms binned spike counts from usable electrodes, estimates the current latent state via the FA posterior, and outputs the next stimulation pattern (electrode selection) to apply on the following trial. The target state is a user-specified point in the 2D latent subspace selected from the 4D FA latent space.

## Method

MiSO integrates three components: (1) FA+Procrustes alignment that identifies a 4D latent space per session via factor analysis on non-stimulation trials and aligns all sessions to a reference space using a closed-form orthogonal Procrustes transform, enabling merging of stimulation-response samples across sessions; (2) a bagged CNN (two 3×3 convolutional layers with 32/64 channels, followed by two linear layers) trained on the merged samples to predict latent responses for all P possible stimulation patterns, leveraging spatial smoothness so that nearby electrodes induce similar responses; and (3) an epsilon-greedy closed-loop optimizer that, on each trial, applies the selected stimulation pattern, estimates the induced latent state in real time, updates the prediction for that pattern with a time-varying clipped learning rate (α_s = max(α_clip, 1/N(s))), and selects the next pattern by minimizing L1 distance to the target with probability 1−ε or sampling randomly with probability ε. The CNN is trained in two phases—random sampling followed by guided sampling targeting fringe regions of the response distribution—to maximize diversity of training data before closed-loop optimization begins.

## Results

In closed-loop experiments with a non-human primate, MiSO with double-electrode CNN (searching over 4,560 stimulation patterns) achieved significantly smaller L1 error to target states than both 'No uStim' and 'Random uStim' baselines (p < 0.05, one-tailed t-test, N=3 sessions), and outperformed MiSO restricted to 96 single-electrode patterns (p=0.079). In the single-electrode setting (96 patterns), MiSO with merged cross-session samples performed equivalently to a method using same-day observations (p=0.715, two-tailed t-test, N=5 sessions), demonstrating that cross-session merging enables effective closed-loop control from the outset. For offline prediction of untested stimulation patterns, the CNN outperformed both the GP and MLP models, with the advantage over GP being particularly pronounced for double-electrode patterns where the CNN's multiple convolutional filters better capture spatial structure.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares MiSO against four baselines in closed-loop settings: 'No uStim' (no stimulation), 'Random uStim' (random pattern selection), 'Single elec., same day observation' (same-day sample average initialization), and 'MiSO with single elec., CNN' (restricted to 96 single-electrode patterns). MiSO with double-electrode CNN outperformed all four baselines in L1 error to target (p < 0.05 vs. No uStim and Random uStim; p = 0.079 vs. single-electrode CNN), confirming that expanding the stimulation parameter space from 96 to 4,560 patterns enables reaching target states exclusively accessible via double-electrode stimulation. In the single-electrode setting, MiSO with merged cross-session samples matched same-day observation performance (p=0.715), validating the cross-session alignment approach. For the prediction model comparison (Fig. 3), the CNN achieved lower test MSE than both the GP and MLP when holding out stimulation patterns, with the CNN's advantage over GP being most pronounced for double-electrode patterns where the GP's single RBF kernel and lack of natural 2D input encoding limit its spatial generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the FA+Procrustes latent space alignment method from Degenhart et al. (2020), originally developed for stabilizing brain-computer interfaces across sessions, to the distinct problem of merging stimulation-response samples for closed-loop brain stimulation. Prior closed-loop stimulation work (Tafazoli et al. 2020, Laferriere et al. 2020, Saalmann et al. 2023) searched over stimulation parameter spaces but was limited by the number of samples collectable within a single session. MiSO departs from this lineage by introducing cross-session data merging as the key enabler for scaling to much larger parameter spaces (e.g., 4,560 double-electrode patterns), combined with a CNN that exploits spatial smoothness in the stimulation-response relationship to generalize to untested patterns.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MiSO demonstrates that latent space alignment can make closed-loop brain stimulation effective from the very first trial of a new session, removing the need for extensive within-session calibration and enabling practical deployment over parameter spaces that would otherwise require dozens of experimental sessions to sample. The equivalence between merged multi-session predictions and same-day observations (p=0.715) is a strong validation that cross-session alignment preserves stimulation-response structure. Open questions include whether the per-pattern online updates (which ignore spatial structure) can be replaced with model-based updates that propagate information across similar patterns, whether the approach scales to higher-dimensional target spaces (t>2) and more complex stimulation parameters (amplitude, frequency), and whether the framework generalizes beyond electrical microstimulation to other modalities like optogenetics.
