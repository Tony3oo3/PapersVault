## Problem

Existing MNL bandit algorithms for assortment optimization assume immediate feedback, but in real e-commerce and advertising settings, customer purchase decisions arrive with stochastic delays (hours to days). This paper is the first to address online learning of MNL choice parameters under delayed and potentially censored feedback, where the learner must make assortment decisions with biased, incomplete information.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240075/cropped_page8_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The algorithm receives a product catalog of N items with a display capacity K, and at each round observes censored feedback o_{i,s,t} that is a product of the true choice indicator and a censoring variable depending on whether the delay d_s has elapsed and whether it exceeds threshold μ. The output is a capacity-constrained assortment S_t to present each round, along with updated attraction parameter estimates that concentrate around the biased target ψ_μ v_i (the fraction of feedback actually observed) rather than the true v_i. In the non-thresholded (PA-DEMBA) variant, the seller additionally knows E[d_s] and all feedback is eventually incorporated.

## Method

DEMBA is an epoch-based UCB algorithm where epochs are delimited by immediate no-purchase decisions, and parameter updates occur only at epoch boundaries. The key technical insight is that the natural estimator v̂_{i,τ} is biased due to delay and censoring, so concentration is established around ψ_μ v_i rather than v_i, with confidence radii Δ_{i,τ} that incorporate both a Hoeffding term for the delay indicator and a Chernoff term for the choice count, plus a μ/|E_τ(i)| bias correction. The algorithm selects assortments by maximizing an optimistic revenue function using upper confidence bounds for product attractions and a lower confidence bound for the no-purchase option. PA-DEMBA extends this to the non-thresholded setting by replacing μ with E[d_s] and a Bernstein-type concentration term for the number of unobserved delayed feedbacks. Both algorithms achieve Õ(√(NT)) regret, matching a lower bound up to logarithmic factors.

## Results

The paper establishes a $\tilde{O}(\sqrt{NT})$ regret upper bound for DEMBA (thresholded delays) and PA-DEMBA (non-thresholded delays), with a matching lower bound of $\Omega(\sqrt{NT/\psi_\mu})$ up to logarithmic factors. In simulations with $N=10$, $K=4$, and 100 independent runs, DEMBA consistently outperforms explore-then-exploit (EXP) baselines across geometric and uniform delay distributions. When compared to MNL-Bandit (Agrawal et al., 2019), DEMBA matches its performance in the no-delay setting but maintains stable regret as delay increases ($\mathbb{E}[d_s]=50, 100$), whereas MNL-Bandit's regret deteriorates significantly.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares DEMBA against two baselines: (1) an explore-then-exploit (EXP) algorithm with three tuned exploration durations, and (2) MNL-Bandit from Agrawal et al. [2019]. Against EXP, DEMBA achieves lower cumulative regret in all tested settings (geometric and uniform delays, varying $\psi_\mu$ values), with the gap widening as $\psi_\mu$ increases, indicating better information utilization. Against MNL-Bandit, DEMBA performs nearly identically when there is no delay, confirming that the delay-handling machinery adds no overhead in the ideal case. However, as the expected delay grows from 0 to 50 to 100, MNL-Bandit's regret curve rises sharply while DEMBA's remains stable, demonstrating that MNL-Bandit fails to account for delayed feedback. PA-DEMBA similarly outperforms EXP baselines in the non-thresholded setting, confirming robustness when all delayed feedback is incorporated.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the MNL-Bandit framework of Agrawal et al. (2019), which uses epoch-based UCB for assortment selection under the Multinomial Logit model, by incorporating delayed and censored feedback. The delayed-feedback bandit literature (Joulani et al. 2013, Chapelle 2014, Vernade et al. 2020, Gael et al. 2020) addresses delays in classical MAB or linear bandit settings, but the authors argue these solutions do not transfer to the MNL/assortment setting because delayed feedback simultaneously affects both item value estimation and assortment composition. The paper thus occupies the intersection of two previously separate research threads: MNL-based assortment optimization and delayed-feedback online learning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first treatment of online assortment optimization under MNL with delayed feedback, providing both algorithms (DEMBA, PA-DEMBA) and matching Õ(√(NT)) upper and lower regret bounds, which establishes the fundamental difficulty of the problem. The practical relevance to e-commerce and online advertising—where conversion delays of hours to days are routine—is significant. Open questions include whether the gap between the upper bound (μ in log terms) and lower bound (ψ_μ in the denominator) can be closed, whether learning the delay distribution itself can improve the rate, and how the framework extends to multi-stage choice settings where preferences are revealed in multiple delayed stages.
