## Problem

Sequential networks for PDE simulation suffer from compounding rollout errors that grow with prediction horizon, making them unreliable for long-horizon autoregressive generation. Existing approaches either encode all coupled fields into a shared latent space—losing field-specific structure and introducing distortion—or lack explicit mechanisms for inter-field information exchange that captures the physical coupling inherent in systems like Navier-Stokes. This paper addresses both gaps by separating field encodings and introducing a dedicated cross-field communication module.

## Input / Output

The model consumes unstructured mesh field data (velocity components, pressure, or volume fraction) organized into field groups by physical dimension, along with scalar time-invariant parameters (e.g., Reynolds number). The mesh is partitioned into a fixed 8×8 grid of 64 patches, each padded to the maximum cell count (232 for cylinder flow, 156 for multiphase flow) and encoded into a compact token sequence. Output is an autoregressive sequence of latent tokens per field group, decoded back to the full mesh at each timestep, with generation conditioned on the initial state and time-invariant parameters throughout the rollout.

## Method

The architecture combines a ViT-like mesh autoencoder with a temporal transformer augmented by a State-Exchange Attention (SEA) module. The autoencoder divides the unstructured mesh into patches, applies an MLP projection followed by multi-head self-attention for spatial coherence, and produces transformer-compatible tokens. In the temporal model, each field group (e.g., {u,v} and {p}) is processed by its own decoder block with causal self-attention, RoPE positional encoding, and AdaLN conditioning. The SEA module then enables multidirectional cross-attention between field groups through a bottleneck (down-projection factor 2), allowing each field to query and attend to the others' representations. A Time-Invariant Parameter Injection (TIPI) module adds direct conditioning via MLP-projected parameters, while AdaLN provides indirect conditioning. Training uses L2 loss with teacher forcing, and inference proceeds autoregressively with causal masking.

## Results

On the 2D cylinder flow benchmark (Re=400), the full ViT-SEA Transformer achieves a time-averaged rollout error of 3.7×10⁻³, outperforming the strongest baseline PbGMR-GMUS Transformer-RealNVP (32.6×10⁻³) by 88% and GMR-GMUS Transformer (43.96×10⁻³) by 91%. On the multiphase flow case, the SEA module alone reduces the volume fraction (α) rollout error by 97% compared to the Field Fusion Encoder (FFE) Transformer and by 52% compared to a basic Transformer with no inter-field communication. The ViT mesh autoencoder also achieves competitive reconstruction error (1.7×10⁻³ for cylinder flow), matching PbGMR-GMUS (1.9×10⁻³) while substantially outperforming GMR-GMUS (14.3×10⁻³).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against three families of baselines on the cylinder flow benchmark. Against MGN and MGN-NI (Pfaff et al., 2020), which use graph-network encoders with simple MLP-based time stepping, the ViT-SEA Transformer achieves 99% and 98% average error reduction respectively, with the largest gains in the v-velocity component (2036→10.7 and 778→10.7). Against GMR-GMUS Transformer (Han et al., 2022), which pairs a graph mesh reducer with a temporal attention network, the proposed method reduces average rollout error from 43.96 to 3.7 (91% improvement), with particularly strong gains in pressure (38→0.3) and v-velocity (89→10.7). Against the strongest baseline, PbGMR-GMUS Transformer-RealNVP (Sun et al., 2023), which adds a RealNVP normalizing flow to the time-stepping transformer, the ViT-SEA Transformer still achieves an 88% average error reduction (32.6→3.7). On the multiphase flow case, the SEA module's advantage is most pronounced for the volume fraction field, where it reduces error by 97% over the FFE Transformer (which encodes all fields jointly) and 52% over a basic Transformer with no inter-field exchange, while also improving velocity components by 40–48.5%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the GMR-GMUS Transformer lineage (Han et al., 2022; Sun et al., 2023) by replacing the graph-based mesh encoder with a ViT-like patch autoencoder and, more critically, by decoupling the encoding of PDE state variables into separate latent spaces connected via a novel State-Exchange Attention (SEA) cross-attention module. The SEA design draws conceptual inspiration from spatio-temporal cross-attention in video generation models and from bottleneck mechanisms in expert-based architectures, but repurposes them to explicitly model the coupling between coupled PDE variables (e.g., velocity and pressure in Navier-Stokes). The temporal backbone adopts RoPE positional embeddings and AdaLN conditioning from recent autoregressive image/video generation work, while the TIPI module offers a lighter-weight alternative to attention-based parameter conditioning common in physics-domain models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 88–91% rollout error reduction over the current SOTA (PbGMR-GMUS Transformer-RealNVP) and the 97% reduction in volume fraction error for multiphase flow suggest that explicitly modeling inter-field coupling through cross-attention is a more effective strategy than relying on a shared embedding space to implicitly capture PDE physics. The information-theoretic analysis in Appendix E provides a principled justification: separate embeddings reduce distortion error, and the SEA channel addresses the coupling error that arises from missing mutual information between fields. Key open questions include how the architecture scales when the number of state variables grows (each requires a parallel transformer), whether the approach transfers to 3D or higher-dimensional PDEs, and how SEA interacts with probabilistic generative frameworks like diffusion models.
