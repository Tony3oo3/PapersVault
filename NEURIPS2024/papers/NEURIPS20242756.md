## Problem

While diffusion models have achieved strong results in decision-making, existing open-source libraries (Diffusers, Stable Diffusion) target computer vision and multimedia, and the few DM decision-making codebases that exist are tightly coupled to specific algorithms, making customized development and cross-algorithm comparison difficult. There is no unified, modularized library that decouples the core components of DM-based decision-making to support the diverse roles DMs play (planner, policy, data synthesizer).
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242756/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The library supports three operational modes: planners consume current states, history trajectories, and goal states to produce multi-step state-action trajectories (horizon 32–64); policies consume state vectors (5–60 dim) or multi-view RGB images (up to 4×84×84) to produce action chunks (1–8 steps); and data synthesizers consume state-action transitions to generate synthetic experience. A binary masking vector allows users to freeze arbitrary portions of trajectories during generation, converting the problem into an inpainting task where known segments are preserved while unknown segments are denoised.

## Method

CleanDiffuser decouples DM-based decision-making into three independent sub-modules — Diffusion Models (SDE/ODE parameterization + numerical solver), Network Architectures (8 options from MLP to 1D U-Net to Transformer), and Guided Sampling (classifier guidance and classifier-free guidance) — that are assembled into algorithm pipelines with roughly 10 lines of code. The diffusion model module internally separates the SDE/ODE from the solver, enabling cross-solver sampling (switching between DDPM, DDIM, DPM-Solver, DPM-Solver++, EDM, and Rectified Flow) and arbitrary sampling steps without retraining. Decision-making-specific features include a masking mechanism for inpainting-style trajectory generation, Diffusion-X sampling to reduce out-of-distribution outputs, a noise/data prediction switch, and warm-starting sampling for real-time inference. The library benchmarks 9 algorithms (3 planners, 5 policies, 1 data synthesizer) across 37 RL and IL environments, revealing that diffusion policies outperform planners, 5 sampling steps suffice for most tasks, and higher EMA rates stabilize training.

## Results

On the D4RL offline RL benchmark (15 tasks), DQL achieves the highest average score of 89.0, followed by IDQL (84.1), DD (83.2), and Diffuser (78.2), while the behavior cloning baseline scores only 51.9. In the Antmaze navigation tasks, diffusion policies dramatically outperform planners: DQL reaches 81.3 average success rate versus 13.3 for Diffuser and 3.0 for DD. On the offline IL benchmark (22 tasks), DiffusionPolicy with Chi_UNet1d achieves the best low-dimensional average of 0.90/0.96 (LAST/MAX success rate), outperforming ACT (0.79/0.89) and LSTM-GMM (0.66/0.84). The paper further shows that 5 sampling steps suffice for most tasks and that a higher EMA rate (0.9999 vs. 0.995) improves and stabilizes training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among the seven diffusion-based offline RL algorithms benchmarked, DQL (89.0 avg) clearly leads, outperforming IDQL (84.1), DD (83.2), and Diffuser (78.2); the gap is most dramatic on Antmaze where DQL scores 81.3 versus 13.3 for Diffuser and 3.0 for DD, confirming that diffusion planners are highly sensitive to guided sampling and prone to OOD trajectory generation. SynthER (69.4) and the BC baseline (51.9) trail significantly, with SynthER failing entirely on Kitchen and Antmaze (0.0). In offline IL, DiffusionPolicy with Chi_UNet1d achieves the best low-dimensional average (0.90/0.96), surpassing ACT (0.79/0.89), DiffusionPolicy with DiT1d (0.79/0.86), and LSTM-GMM (0.66/0.84); for image-based tasks, Chi_UNet1d (0.88/0.91) also edges out ACT (0.87/0.88) and DiT1d (0.78/0.83). DiffusionBC consistently underperforms DiffusionPolicy across architectures, with its Pearce_MLP variant scoring only 0.65/0.73 (low-dim) and 0.51/0.57 (image), suggesting that the Diffusion-X sampling steps add latency without proportional accuracy gains. Overall, the paper demonstrates that algorithmic design (e.g., Q-maximizing loss in DQL) matters more than model size or diffusion backbone choice for decision-making performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

CleanDiffuser builds on the foundational diffusion model literature (DDPM, DDIM, DPM-Solver, EDM, Rectified Flow) and the growing body of DM-based decision-making algorithms (Diffuser, Decision Diffuser, DiffusionPolicy, DiffusionBC, DQL, IDQL, EDP, SynthER, AdaptDiffuser), but departs from both existing DM libraries (Diffusers, Stable Diffusion) which target CV/multimedia and lack decision-making support, and from algorithm-specific codebases whose tightly coupled architectures hinder customized development. The paper's conceptual contribution is identifying three decoupled sub-modules—Diffusion Models, Network Architectures, and Guided Sampling Methods—as the essential building blocks for all DM-based decision-making paradigms (planner, policy, data synthesizer), and packaging them into a unified pipeline with decision-making-specific features like masking, cross-solver sampling, and Diffusion-X.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CleanDiffuser fills a critical infrastructure gap by providing the first dedicated, modularized DM library for decision-making, enabling reproducible comparison and rapid prototyping across 37 environments and 9 algorithms. The empirical analyses reveal several non-obvious design insights: diffusion planners show no superiority over diffusion policies (especially in Antmaze), 5 sampling steps often suffice where prior work used 100, model size has minimal impact relative to algorithm choice, and a 'sampling degradation' phenomenon where more steps can hurt performance remains unexplained. Open questions include the root cause of sampling degradation, the SDE-vs-ODE tradeoff in decision-making, optimal model sizing, and how to achieve real-time inference given the multi-step denoising bottleneck that limits practical deployment.
