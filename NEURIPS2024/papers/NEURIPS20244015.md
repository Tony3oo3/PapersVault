## Problem

Dynamic reconstruction in confocal NLOS imaging is severely limited by the dense raster-scanning acquisition, which makes practical frame rates extremely low. Existing under-scanning reconstruction methods treat each transient frame independently, overlooking the temporal consistency between adjacent frames that could compensate for unrecoverable regions. Additionally, no dedicated NLOS video dataset existed for training or evaluating dynamic reconstruction methods.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20244015/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes three consecutive under-scanning transient frames of size 16×16×512 (spatial grid × temporal bins) and produces a high-resolution 3D hidden albedo volume at 128×128 spatial resolution, along with a derived intensity image and depth map. An intermediate high-resolution transient frame (128×128×512) is also recovered, which serves both as a training supervision target and as input to the volume reconstruction stage. The 8× spatial upsampling is performed jointly with temporal upsampling in a single transient spreading module, reducing computational burden compared to full-resolution processing.

## Method

The core architecture introduces ST-Mamba blocks that handle the partial causality inherent in transient data: a unidirectional (causal) SSM along the temporal axis where photon arrival times are ordered, and a bidirectional SSM along the spatial axis where scanning points have no inherent ordering. A cross ST-Mamba mechanism then fuses features from adjacent frames into the reference frame using a shared gating factor derived from the reference input, replacing the query-key-value pattern of cross-attention with a simpler modulation-and-add operation. The recovered high-resolution transient is fed into a physics-based reconstruction module that applies a Fourier-domain inverse PSF filter (derived from the phasor-field virtual wave optics prior) to transform spatio-temporal features into 3D spatial volume features. Training uses a novel phasor field loss that convolves the predicted and ground-truth transients with a Gaussian-shaped illumination function before computing the L2 error, accentuating physically meaningful frequency components in low-SNR data.

## Results

On the synthetic test set (16×16 input → 128×128 output), the full multi-frame method (Ours) achieves the best performance across all four metrics: PSNR 24.46 dB, SSIM 84.08, RMSE 0.0880, and MAD 0.0397. The single-frame variant (Ours-S, without cross ST-Mamba) also outperforms all baselines, reaching PSNR 23.97 and SSIM 81.35, confirming the core ST-Mamba architecture's effectiveness. On real-world data captured at 4 FPS with a 16×16 scanning grid, the method qualitatively produces the clearest hidden-object reconstructions, including non-rigid motion (arm movements), while baselines suffer from noise, artifacts, or oversimplified geometry.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The closest competitor is USM [4], the only other deep method specifically designed for under-scanning NLOS; Ours surpasses it by 0.66 dB PSNR, 3.23 SSIM, and 0.0065 RMSE, with the gap widening on depth (MAD 0.0397 vs. 0.0432). Among general-purpose deep methods, I-K [31] and LFE [26] achieve PSNR ≈ 23.2 but require dense 128×128 input (interpolated from under-scanning), whereas Ours works directly from 16×16 input and still leads by ~1.2 dB. The iterative compressed-sensing method CSA [1], also designed for under-scanning, lags significantly (PSNR 20.70, RMSE 0.2647) due to its optimization-based nature and lack of temporal fusion. Traditional wave-based methods (LCT, RSD, FK) are far behind, with PSNR below 21 and RMSE above 0.39, confirming that learning-based approaches are essential for under-scanning reconstruction. On real-world data, all baselines degrade under low SNR conditions; CSA and USM lose generalization, while Ours maintains clear geometric detail and handles non-rigid motion robustly.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the Mamba selective state space model (Gu & Dao, 2024) and its bidirectional variant (Vision Mamba, Zhu et al., 2024) to the 3D NLOS transient imaging domain, where the temporal axis is causal but the spatial axis is not — a structural mismatch the authors resolve with a split causal/bidirectional SSM design. It extends the under-scanning NLOS reconstruction line (USM [4], CSA [1]) from single-frame to multi-frame dynamic settings by introducing a Cross ST-Mamba fusion mechanism, and incorporates the phasor-field physical prior (Liu et al., Nature 2019) both as a Fourier-domain inverse-PSF filter in the network and as a novel training loss. The work departs from prior deep NLOS methods (LFE, I-K, USM) that treat each transient frame independently, explicitly exploiting inter-frame temporal consistency as a first-class signal source.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Dynamic NLOS imaging at practical frame rates (here 4 FPS) remains an open problem, and this paper provides the first dedicated dataset (synthetic + real-world) and a principled architecture for it, lowering the barrier for future work in the area. The phasor-field loss is a useful inductive bias for low-SNR transient data that could transfer to other photon-counting imaging tasks. Key limitations include the reliance on a Fourier-based feature transformation that adds computational overhead, the confocal-only system design, and the modest multi-frame gain over the single-frame variant (~0.5 dB PSNR), suggesting that temporal consistency exploitation in NLOS is still far from saturated. Open questions include whether purely spatial-domain fusion (bypassing the temporal SSM) could match or exceed the current approach, and whether the method generalizes to non-confocal SPAD-array systems.
