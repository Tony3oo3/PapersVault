## Problem

Existing learning-based global routing methods (e.g., HubRouter) ensure connectivity but produce routing results with severe overflow because they inadequately account for routing resource availability during generation. This overflow makes the routes practically unusable in real chip design flows, where even a single overflow triggers time-consuming rip-up-and-reroute iterations. The paper addresses the gap of jointly optimizing wirelength and overflow in a single learning-based pass for large-scale nets.

## Input / Output

The network accepts two-channel grid maps at the variable original bounding-box scale of each net (not fixed to 64×64 as in HubRouter): a pin map indicating terminal locations and an overflow map computed via a logistic function of remaining edge resources. The output is a single-channel binary candidate point map identifying Steiner and corner point locations, which is then converted into a full overflow-avoiding rectilinear Steiner tree through a graph-based post-processing stage. Non-conflicting nets are batched into parallel routing tasks to improve throughput.

## Method

NeuralSteiner is a two-phase scheme. Phase 1 uses a modified ResNet-34 backbone (with max-pooling removed to preserve spatial detail) augmented with a recurrent crisscross attention (RCCA) module that aggregates features along both horizontal and vertical directions across the entire feature map, enabling the network to capture long-range pin and congestion correlations in large-scale nets. Training minimizes a composite loss of focal loss, dice loss, and a novel overflow loss that penalizes predicted points in high-congestion regions even when they are absent from the ground-truth label, encouraging the model to discover alternative low-overflow candidate locations. Phase 2 constructs a net augmented graph (NAG) by connecting all predicted candidate points and pins that share a horizontal or vertical coordinate with no intervening points, where edge weights balance Manhattan distance and accumulated overflow. A greedy minimum-spanning-tree algorithm then iteratively merges the two nearest connected components until all pins are linked, yielding an overflow-avoiding RST with guaranteed connectivity.

## Results

On the 8 large-scale ISPD07 chip designs, NeuralSteiner achieves an average 97.8% reduction in total overflow compared to HubRouter (GAN), reaching up to 99.8% on adaptec04_2d (97 vs. 45,230), while incurring only a 1.8% wirelength increase. On ISPD98 (ibm01–06), the average overflow reduction versus HubRouter (GAN) is 61.1%, with up to 95% on ibm05 (18 vs. 360). NeuralSteiner also runs faster than HubRouter (GAN) on ISPD07 (e.g., 347 s vs. 593 s on adaptec01_2d) thanks to its parallel routing task construction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NeuralSteiner dramatically outperforms the state-of-the-art learning-based method HubRouter (GAN) [8] on overflow: an average 97.8% reduction across 8 ISPD07 designs (e.g., 82 vs. 35,441 on adaptec01_2d) and 61.1% on ISPD98, while wirelength increases by at most 1.8% and generation time is shorter (347 s vs. 593 s on adaptec01_2d). Against traditional routers GeoSteiner [15] and FLUTE+ES [7], NeuralSteiner achieves orders-of-magnitude lower overflow (e.g., 82 vs. 35,945 and 32,518 on adaptec01_2d) at a small wirelength cost (~1–2%). BoxRouter [6] achieves zero overflow on ISPD98 but with substantially worse wirelength (e.g., 62,659 vs. 61,735 on ibm01) and longer runtimes. When integrated into CUGR [24] as a pre-routing step on ISPD18/19 benchmarks, NeuralSteiner reduces detailed-routing shorts by 4.4% and spaces by 19.1% on average, confirming its practical benefit in a full design flow.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the generative-model paradigm of HubRouter (VAE/DPM/GAN for hub-point generation) and DRL-based routing (REST, DQN) by reframing global routing as a supervised image-segmentation task: a ResNet-34 backbone augmented with recurrent crisscross attention predicts candidate Steiner/corner points, which are then connected via a greedy minimum-spanning-tree construction on a net augmented graph (NAG). The NAG-based post-processing replaces HubRouter's stripe-mask filtering and REST pin-hub connection, preserving a larger solution space for congestion avoidance. The training data is generated by the traditional CUGR router, and the overflow loss term (weighted 2×) is a novel addition that explicitly penalizes predicted points in congested regions, going beyond the focal + dice losses used in standard segmentation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The near-elimination of overflow (up to 99.8% reduction) while keeping wirelength loss under 1.8% addresses the single most critical practical barrier to deploying learning-based global routers in production chip design flows, where overflow triggers expensive rip-up-and-reroute iterations. The demonstrated generalization from small training nets (HPWL ≤ 128) to benchmarks with nets exceeding 16,000 pins, and the integration into CUGR reducing post-detailed-routing design-rule violations on ISPD18/19, suggest real industrial relevance. The main open limitation is the reliance on a heuristic greedy MST post-processing step, which the authors acknowledge as time-consuming; future work on end-to-end differentiable RST construction or continuous probabilistic point maps could close this gap.
