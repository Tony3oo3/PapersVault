## Problem

The state-of-the-art player-optimal stable regret bound for bandit learning in two-sided matching markets is O(K log T / Δ²), which is far from the known lower bound Ω(max{N log T / Δ², K log T / Δ}) when the number of arms K greatly exceeds the number of players N — a common scenario in labor markets and online advertising. This paper closes the gap by removing the dependence on K in the main regret order term.

## Input / Output

The algorithm operates on a one-to-one matching market with N players and K arms (N ≤ K), where players have unknown stochastic preferences (1-subgaussian rewards in (0,1]) and arms have known fixed preference rankings over players. The output is a player-optimal stable matching achieved over T rounds, with the regret measured as the cumulative gap between the optimal stable arm's reward and the actually received reward. The algorithm is presented in a centralized form but extends to a decentralized setting via phase-based communication with exponentially growing phase lengths.

## Method

The paper proposes AETGS (Adaptively Explore-then-Gale-Shapley) with elimination, which integrates the learning process directly into each Gale-Shapley proposal step rather than using a separate explore-then-commit phase. Players maintain UCB/LCB confidence intervals and round-robin explore their available arm set, but critically eliminate sub-optimal arms in real time (keeping at least N arms to prevent collisions) and immediately switch to exploitation once the best available arm is identified. If a higher-priority player occupies the focused arm, the player restarts exploration on the reduced set. This adaptive elimination ensures each sub-optimal arm is selected only O(log T / Δ_{i,m_i*,j}) times, matching the per-selection regret and yielding O(N² log T / Δ² + K log T / Δ) total regret. Additionally, the paper provides a refined inductive analysis of the centralized UCB algorithm under the α-condition, exploiting the sequential convergence structure to achieve O(N log T / Δ_N² + K log T / Δ) regret.

## Results

The main theoretical contribution is Theorem 4.1, which shows AETGS with elimination achieves a player-optimal stable regret of O(N² log T / Δ² + K log T / Δ), improving over the state-of-the-art O(K log T / Δ²) bound from Zhang et al. [31] and Kong and Li [16] by removing the dependence on K in the leading-order term. Under the α-condition, a refined analysis of the centralized UCB algorithm yields an even tighter O(N log T / Δ_N² + K log T / Δ) bound (Theorem 6.2). In experiments with N=3 players and K=10 arms over T=100k rounds, AETGS-E achieves the lowest maximum cumulative player-optimal stable regret and the lowest cumulative player-optimal instability among all baselines, with the Phased ETC baseline failing to converge within the displayed horizon.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares AETGS-E against three baselines that also guarantee player-optimal stable regret in general decentralized one-to-one markets: ETGS [16], ML-ETC [31], and Phased ETC [4]. In the N=3, K=10 experimental setting, AETGS-E achieves the lowest cumulative player-optimal stable regret and the lowest cumulative player-optimal instability, outperforming all three baselines. ETGS and ML-ETC both require fully exploring all K arms to estimate the complete preference ranking before committing, leading to higher regret; AETGS-E avoids this by adaptively eliminating sub-optimal arms and switching to exploitation as soon as the best available arm is identified. Phased ETC [4] suffers from a cold-start problem and has not converged to the stable matching within the 100k-round horizon. Theoretically, AETGS-E's O(N² log T / Δ² + K log T / Δ) bound strictly improves over ETGS and ML-ETC's O(K log T / Δ²) whenever N² < K (i.e., N < √K), which is the common regime in applications like labor markets and online advertising where the number of workers/slots far exceeds the number of employers/advertisers.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the ETC-type strategies of Zhang et al. (NeurIPS 2022) and Kong and Li (SODA 2023), which achieved O(K log T / Δ²) player-optimal stable regret by explicitly separating exploration (learning full preference rankings) from exploitation (running Gale-Shapley). The key departure is integrating the learning process directly into each GS step via an adaptive elimination mechanism, so players only explore until their optimal available arm is identified rather than estimating the complete top-N ranking. The α-condition analysis refines the centralized UCB of Liu et al. (AISTATS 2020) by exploiting the sequential convergence structure of the α-condition to remove K from the regret bound.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

In practical matching markets (labor, online advertising), the number of arms K (workers, publisher slots) vastly exceeds the number of players N (employers, advertisers), so removing K from the main regret term is a meaningful practical improvement that brings the upper bound close to the known lower bound Ω(max{N log T / Δ², K log T / Δ}). A key open question is whether the lower bound in general markets should depend on the paper's gap_4 (minimum gap among arms ranked up to σ_i(m_i*)+1) rather than the stricter gap_1 used in prior lower bounds, which the authors illustrate with a concrete counterexample. The α-condition result, while matching the lower bound in N, K, T dependence, uses a different Δ definition, leaving the exact optimality gap unresolved.
