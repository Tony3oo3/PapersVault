## Problem

Local-based neural surface representations improve geometric fidelity but at the cost of substantially more parameters, since each local patch requires its own latent code. The paper identifies a deeper issue: jointly recovering the spatial transformation (rotation/translation) and geometry of local patches is a non-convex optimization problem with spurious local minima, making learning difficult and representations less compact than they could be.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242091/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

During training, the method consumes point samples freely drawn in space (not restricted to the surface) paired with their ground-truth SDF values, organized per voxel in a 32×32×32 grid where only surface-intersecting voxels are active. At inference, the shared MLP is frozen and only per-voxel latent codes (125-dim) and coordinate frames are optimized for a single target shape over 800 iterations. The final output is a continuous SDF field from which a mesh is extracted via Marching Cubes at 128³ resolution.

## Method

CoFie introduces a Coordinate Field that assigns each valid voxel a learnable 6-DoF pose (quaternion rotation + translation), transforming query points from world coordinates into a locally aligned frame before the MLP decodes the SDF. This explicitly disentangles transformation from geometry, turning the non-convex joint fitting problem into a more tractable one; the coordinate frames are initialized via PCA on SDF derivatives to provide geometry-aware starting points. The MLP itself uses a quadratic layer as its final layer (in addition to 4 linear layers with 128 hidden channels) to capture the inherently quadratic structure of local SDFs that piecewise-linear ReLU networks cannot represent. Training jointly optimizes the shared MLP, all latent codes, and all coordinate fields across 1000 ShapeNet shapes; at test time the MLP is frozen and only the per-shape latent codes and coordinate fields are optimized, making CoFie a generalizable representation that works on unseen categories and real scans.

## Results

On novel instances of ShapeNet training categories (seen), CoFie achieves a mean chamfer distance of 2.05×10⁻⁴, reducing error by 48% relative to the best baseline DeepLS (3.91×10⁻⁴). On 10 unseen ShapeNet categories, CoFie achieves a mean chamfer distance of 3.18×10⁻⁴, a 56% reduction over DeepLS (7.27×10⁻⁴). On real Thingi10k scans, CoFie (1.87×10⁻⁴) approaches the shape-specific NGLOD (1.04×10⁻⁴) while using a single shared MLP, and outperforms DeepSDF (3.68×10⁻⁴) by nearly 2×.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoFie is compared against four main baselines: DeepSDF (global latent code), DeepLS (local-based, most direct comparison), NGLOD (shape-specific, per-shape MLP), and 3DS2VS (shape auto-encoding reference). Against DeepLS, the closest generalizable local-based method, CoFie wins on every category in both seen and unseen settings, with the gap widening from 48% (seen) to 56% (unseen), indicating stronger generalization. Against DeepSDF, CoFie reduces chamfer distance by roughly 3–4× across all settings. On real Thingi10k scans, CoFie (1.87) is within 1.8× of the shape-specific NGLOD (1.04), which trains a dedicated MLP per shape, while CoFie uses a single frozen shared MLP. The shape auto-encoding reference 3DS2VS performs worst on unseen categories (16.0 mean CD), confirming that the auto-decoding setting with local geometry awareness is critical for generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

CoFie extends the local-based neural implicit representation line (DeepSDF, DeepLS) by introducing a Coordinate Field that transforms each local voxel's geometry into an aligned coordinate frame before MLP decoding, rather than increasing local resolution to capture high-frequency details. The design is motivated by a theoretical analysis of quadratic surface patches showing that SDF fitting becomes non-convex when patches are unaligned, providing a principled justification for explicitly disentangling transformation from geometry. The coordinate field concept relates to LDIF's fixed per-primitive frames and MVP's oriented boxes, but CoFie's field is spatially varying, optimizable, and category-agnostic. The quadratic MLP layer departs from the standard ReLU-based piecewise-linear MLPs used in prior implicit representations, grounded in the observation that local SDFs have non-negligible quadratic components.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CoFie demonstrates that reducing spatial complexity via coordinate alignment is a more parameter-efficient strategy than increasing local resolution, achieving 48–56% lower chamfer distance at equal parameter budgets or comparable accuracy with 70% fewer parameters. The generalizable single-MLP design matching shape-specific overfitting methods (NGLOD) on real scans is practically significant for scalable 3D shape pipelines. Key limitations remain: the local-voxel formulation cannot perform shape completion (unlike DeepSDF's global priors), and fixed cell resolution breaks down on thin structures, pointing toward adaptive resolution as a natural next step. The theoretical non-convexity analysis of unaligned patches also opens questions about whether similar coordinate-alignment strategies could benefit other local-implicit or local-explicit representation families.
