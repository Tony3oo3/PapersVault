## Problem

In the multi-color secretary problem, candidates from different groups (e.g., universities) can only be compared within their own group, making inter-group ranking impossible. This model is overly pessimistic because in practice, inter-group comparisons can be obtained at a cost (e.g., standardized tests). The paper asks: given a fixed budget B for such costly comparisons, what is the optimal strategy to maximize the probability of selecting the best overall candidate?

## Input / Output

At each step t, the algorithm observes the candidate's group g_t and in-group rank r_t for free, and may optionally spend one unit of budget to determine whether the candidate is the best overall so far (R_t = 1). The algorithm must then immediately decide to stop (select) or skip (irrevocably reject). The success criterion is binary: the selected candidate must be the global maximum among all N candidates. The key structural constraint is that once a comparison is made at step t, the algorithm must follow with a stop or skip decision at that same step.

## Method

The paper introduces a family of Dynamic-Threshold (DT) algorithms where acceptance thresholds are indexed by group and remaining budget, changing dynamically as the budget is consumed. For the single-threshold case (all groups share one threshold), a recursive formula yields a closed-form asymptotic success probability that converges to 1/e at rate (K-1)^{B+1}/(B+1)!, showing extremely rapid improvement with budget. For two groups, static double-threshold algorithms are analyzed via integral recursions, and a closed-form lower bound is derived. The key technical contribution is a dynamic-programming characterization of the optimal memory-less algorithm, which reveals that for large N it coincides with a DT algorithm, enabling numerical computation of optimal group-specific thresholds that outperform the single-threshold approach when group proportions are imbalanced.

## Results

The paper establishes that the single-threshold algorithm with threshold ⌊N/e⌋ achieves an asymptotic success probability of at least (1/e)(1 − (K−1)^(B+1)/(B+1)!), converging to the 1/e upper bound extremely rapidly as budget B grows. For the two-group case, the static double-threshold algorithm with optimally chosen thresholds (α̃_B, β̃_B) achieves a success probability within min{1/(e(B+1)!), (4/e−1)λ(1−λ)} of 1/e, improving over the single-threshold algorithm when group proportions are imbalanced. Numerical experiments (N=500, 10^6 trials) confirm that the optimal memory-less algorithm A* is empirically indistinguishable from the DT algorithm with numerically computed optimal thresholds for large N, and that for B≥1 both thresholds become very close to 1/e.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against external baselines in a standard benchmark sense, as it is a theoretical study of a novel problem formulation. The key internal comparisons are: (1) the single-threshold algorithm (all groups share one threshold) versus the static double-threshold algorithm (different thresholds per group) — the latter yields substantial improvement when λ is close to 0 or 1, but matches the former when λ=0.5 due to symmetry; (2) the DT algorithm with numerically optimal thresholds versus the optimal memory-less algorithm A* computed via dynamic programming — Figure 7 confirms they are empirically equivalent for N=500 across λ∈{0.5, 0.7, 0.95} and B∈{0,1,2}; (3) all proposed algorithms versus the 1/e upper bound of the classical secretary problem — the gap closes rapidly with budget, with the single-threshold algorithm already near 1/e for B≥3 even with K=50 groups. The multi-color secretary problem of Correa et al. (2021a), which forbids inter-group comparisons entirely, serves as the pessimistic baseline that this paper's budgeted model strictly improves upon.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the multi-color secretary problem of Correa et al. (2021a), which assumed inter-group comparisons were entirely impossible, by introducing a budget B that allows costly cross-group comparisons. The classical 1/e secretary strategy (Dynkin, 1963) serves as the foundational baseline, and the paper's Dynamic-Threshold (DT) algorithm family generalizes it to the multi-group, budgeted setting. The analysis also draws on the broader literature on online algorithms with limited advice or costly hints, positioning the comparison budget as a form of constrained information acquisition. For the two-group case, the paper introduces a dynamic-programming-based optimal memory-less algorithm, a technique borrowed from optimal stopping theory but applied here to the novel budgeted multi-group setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper makes the multi-color secretary problem substantially more realistic by acknowledging that cross-group comparisons are possible but expensive, and shows that even a very small budget (B=1 or 2) recovers near-optimal 1/e success probability for moderate numbers of groups. A key open question is whether the optimal DT thresholds can be characterized analytically or computed efficiently for K > 2 groups, since the current results are limited to the two-group case. The finding that the optimal memory-less algorithm reduces to a DT algorithm for large N provides a structural bridge between the complex DP solution and the simpler threshold-based approach, but extending this equivalence to more groups remains unresolved.
