## Problem

Existing one-shot head avatar methods either rely on NeRF-based representations that cannot render in real time, or use 3D Gaussian splatting that requires per-identity training and cannot generalize to unseen subjects. The modeling of a generalizable, animatable 3D Gaussian head avatar reconstructable from a single image in a single forward pass remained unexplored.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241837/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single 512×512 source image and a driving image as input. The source image is processed by a frozen DINOv2 backbone to extract 296×296 local features and 768-dim global features, while camera pose and FLAME 3DMM parameters are tracked from both images. The output is an animatable 3D Gaussian avatar consisting of 175,232 Gaussian points (each carrying 32-dim features, opacity, scale, and rotation) that supports free-viewpoint rendering and expression control at 67 FPS on an A100 GPU with 2.5 GB VRAM.

## Method

The core contribution is a dual-lifting reconstruction branch that predicts forward and backward lifting distances from the image plane (rather than 3D positions directly), producing two sets of Gaussians that form an almost closed 3D head structure and eliminating the ambiguity inherent in single-plane lifting. An expression branch binds learnable 256-dim features to FLAME 3DMM vertices, concatenates them with global identity features, and passes them through a 6-layer MLP to predict expression Gaussian parameters whose positions are the deformed vertex positions. A StyleUNet-based neural renderer then decodes the 32-dim splatted features into RGB, refining the coarse result and resolving conflicts between the static reconstruction Gaussians and dynamic expression Gaussians. Training is supervised on 586K VFHQ frames using L1 and perceptual losses on both coarse and fine images, plus a novel lifting distance loss that constrains predicted lifting points to lie near tracked FLAME vertices, leveraging 3DMM geometry as a structural prior while still allowing the model to learn non-3DMM regions like hair and accessories.

## Results

On the VFHQ dataset, GAGAvatar achieves the best self-reenactment quality with PSNR 21.83, SSIM 0.818, and LPIPS 0.122, outperforming the closest competitor Portrait4D-v2 (PSNR 21.34, LPIPS 0.144). For expression and pose accuracy, it attains the lowest AED (0.111), APD (0.135), and AKD (3.349) among all methods. On HDTF, it similarly leads with PSNR 23.13 and AKD 2.985. Notably, GAGAvatar is the first method to achieve real-time reenactment at 67 FPS on an A100 GPU, compared to 19.82 FPS for the fastest baseline (StyleHeat).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GAGAvatar outperforms all 10 baselines on self-reenactment reconstruction quality (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS) and expression/pose accuracy (AED, APD, AKD) on both VFHQ and HDTF. The closest competitor, Portrait4D-v2, trails by 0.49 dB in PSNR and 0.022 in LPIPS on VFHQ, while GPAvatar (the authors' prior work) is surpassed by 0.79 dB in PSNR and 0.046 in LPIPS. On cross-identity reenactment, GAGAvatar achieves the best expression transfer (lowest AED and APD) but lags behind Real3DPortrait (0.663) and Portrait4D-v2 (0.656) on identity similarity (CSIM 0.633 on VFHQ), which the authors attribute to incomplete identity-expression decoupling in the FLAME 3DMM. The most striking advantage is speed: GAGAvatar runs at 67 FPS versus 19.82 FPS for StyleHeat (the fastest baseline) and 0.12 FPS for OTAvatar, making it the first real-time one-shot animatable head avatar method. Among NeRF-based methods (HideNeRF, GOHA, GPAvatar, Real3DPortrait, Portrait4D, Portrait4D-v2), GAGAvatar consistently leads on all self-reenactment metrics while being 3–500× faster at inference.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two prior lines of work: generalizable one-shot head avatar reconstruction (GOHA, GPAvatar, Real3DPortrait, Portrait4D) and real-time 3D Gaussian splatting rendering (Gaussian Head Avatar, TalkingGaussian). Prior NeRF-based methods achieve generalization but cannot render in real time, while prior 3D Gaussian methods achieve speed but require per-identity training. GAGAvatar bridges this gap by introducing a dual-lifting mechanism that predicts forward and backward Gaussian offsets from a single image plane (extracted via frozen DINOv2), combined with FLAME 3DMM vertex-bound Gaussians for expression control and a StyleUNet neural renderer for refinement — making it, to the authors' knowledge, the first generalizable 3D Gaussian head avatar with single-forward-pass reconstruction and real-time reenactment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 67 FPS real-time speed with generalization to unseen identities opens practical deployment scenarios (video conferencing, VR, interactive entertainment) that were previously infeasible with NeRF-based or per-identity 3D Gaussian methods. Key limitations remain: the 3DMM-based expression branch cannot control regions outside the FLAME mesh (hair, tongue), unseen areas from novel viewpoints lack detail, and cross-identity consistency is slightly degraded due to incomplete identity/expression decoupling in FLAME. Open questions include whether expression embeddings can be learned directly from images (bypassing 3DMM tracking), whether the approach extends to full-body avatars, and whether the dual-lifting formulation generalizes beyond head geometry.
