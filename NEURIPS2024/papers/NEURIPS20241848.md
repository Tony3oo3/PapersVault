## Problem

Generating a set of structurally diverse graphs that 'cover' the space of all possible graph structures has not been addressed in the literature, despite being essential for testing graph algorithms, evaluating neural approximations, and benchmarking GNNs. The paper also tackles the foundational sub-problem of defining diversity for a set of graphs, showing that widely used measures (average pairwise distance, Vendi Score, #Circles) fail to satisfy basic desiderata such as monotonicity and uniqueness simultaneously.

## Input / Output

The methods take as input a fixed number of nodes n (16 or 64), a target set size N (typically 100), a chosen graph distance (NetLSD-heat, NetLSD-wave, Graphlet Correlation Distance, or Portrait Divergence), and an initial pool of candidate graphs. They output a multiset of N undirected, simple graphs optimized to maximize an Energy-based diversity score (negative average of inverse pairwise distances to power γ=1). All algorithms operate under a fixed computational budget of generated graphs (3M for non-neural methods, 1M for the diffusion-based IGGM), and the diversity measure is decomposable so that adding one graph to a set only requires N−1 new distance computations.

## Method

The paper proposes an Energy diversity measure (−1/(N(N−1)) Σ 1/D(Gi,Gj)^γ) that provably satisfies both monotonicity and uniqueness, and then develops four complementary optimization strategies: (1) a Greedy selector that picks the most dissimilar graph from a large pre-generated pool at each step (with a theoretical guarantee of ≥2^γ× the optimal subset diversity), (2) a Genetic Algorithm using node-level crossover and Ipsen-Mikhailov-style mutation with a plateau-escape mechanism, (3) a Local Optimization loop performing single edge add/delete moves, and (4) an Iterative Graph Generative Modeling (IGGM) procedure that alternates between training a DiGress discrete denoising diffusion model on the current diverse set and greedily selecting a new diverse subset from its samples. The key insight is that standard generative ML is inapplicable because there is no target distribution to imitate—the objective is purely to maximize mutual dissimilarity—so the algorithms must be designed around the decomposable fitness structure f(G,S) rather than likelihood maximization. In practice, the algorithms are chained (e.g., Greedy→Genetic→LocalOpt) to combine broad exploration with fine-grained refinement.

## Results

The paper optimizes the Energy diversity measure (lower is better) across four graph distances (GCD, Portrait-div, NetLSD-heat, NetLSD-wave) on sets of 100 graphs with 16 nodes. The best non-neural pipeline (Greedy→Genetic→LocalOpt) achieves Energy scores of 0.135 (GCD), 1.245 (Portrait-div), 0.673 (NetLSD-heat), and 0.117 (NetLSD-wave). The neural IGGM method achieves the best GCD (0.120) and Portrait-div (1.213) scores, while matching the non-neural pipeline on NetLSD-heat (0.675). All proposed methods dramatically outperform baselines: e.g., NetLSD-heat Energy drops from 72.387 (ER-mix) to 0.673, a ~107× improvement.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two main baselines: ER-mix (Erdős-Rényi graphs with varying edge probability p) and GraphWorld (a degree-corrected stochastic block model benchmark). All proposed algorithms substantially outperform both baselines across all four graph distances. Against ER-mix, the best proposed method (IGGM) reduces GCD Energy from 0.281 to 0.120 (2.3×), Portrait-div from 43.057 to 1.213 (35×), NetLSD-heat from 72.387 to 0.675 (107×), and NetLSD-wave from 0.583 to 0.148 (3.9×). Against GraphWorld, improvements are also large: GCD from 0.466 to 0.120 (3.9×), Portrait-div from 3.917 to 1.213 (3.2×), NetLSD-heat from 5.108 to 0.675 (7.6×), and NetLSD-wave from 0.621 to 0.148 (4.2×). The Random Graph Generators baseline (a mixture of 7 graph models) also loses to all proposed methods, confirming that simply sampling from diverse random models is insufficient without explicit diversity optimization. The non-neural Greedy→Genetic→LocalOpt pipeline and the neural IGGM method are competitive with each other, with IGGM winning on GCD and Portrait-div while the non-neural pipeline wins on NetLSD-wave.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by formally defining the problem of generating a set of graphs that are maximally structurally diverse, which the authors state has not been addressed in prior literature. It repurposes existing graph distance measures (NetLSD, GCD, Portrait Divergence) and neural generative models (DiGress) for a fundamentally different objective—maximizing pairwise dissimilarity rather than matching a target distribution. The diversity measure (Energy) is motivated by physics-inspired repulsion and is positioned against prior diversity metrics from molecular generation (#Circles) and ML evaluation (Vendi Score), which the authors show fail to satisfy both monotonicity and uniqueness simultaneously.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled framework and practical algorithms for generating diverse graph test sets, which is essential for unbiased evaluation of graph algorithms and GNNs. The analysis revealing that different graph distances emphasize different structural aspects (NetLSD → sparsity, GCD → local structure, Portrait-div → global properties) offers useful guidance for practitioners choosing evaluation metrics. Key open questions include scalability to large graphs (n=64 already shows diminishing returns for local optimization), the unboundedness of the Energy measure, and the lack of practical downstream applications demonstrating that diverse graph sets actually improve algorithm evaluation.
