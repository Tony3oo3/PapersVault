## Problem

Optical neural networks (ONNs) suffer significant accuracy degradation when transitioning from ideal electronic simulation to physical implementation due to systematic errors such as laser intensity instability, CCD exposure mismatches, and unmeasurable environmental disturbances. Existing error compensation approaches either model only a subset of errors or rely on unconstrained DNNs with large optimization spaces, leading to slow convergence and suboptimal performance. This paper asks how to bridge the simulation-to-experiment gap by explicitly incorporating quantifiable physical information into the learning framework.

## Input / Output

Input images (28×28 grayscale for MNIST/Fashion MNIST, hand-drawn sketches for QuickDraw16) are loaded onto a digital micromirror device (DMD) as binary transmission functions and propagated through a 4f optical system with a spatial light modulator (SLM) encoding the trained phase distribution. The output is a class label determined by which of N equally-sized regions in the CCD-captured output field has the highest light intensity; since the CCD measures only intensity (not phase) and saturates at I_max, the effective output is a clipped intensity vector [I_1, ..., I_N] from which argmax yields the prediction.

## Method

The proposed physics-constrained ONN learning framework decomposes the total system error into quantifiable components (laser jitter Δf_jit, CCD bias Δf_dev) and unmeasurable residuals η, handling each with a targeted strategy. Laser instability is mitigated by a custom loss function (Eq. 4) that enforces a minimum intensity gap W_Gap between the top two classification regions, making the prediction robust to random intensity fluctuations. CCD exposure mismatch is eliminated by a calibration procedure that coarsely and finely adjusts exposure time to zero out the mean and variance of the simulation-experiment pixel difference. The remaining unmeasurable errors (crosstalk, misalignment, lens aberrations) are learned by a lightweight (~5,000-parameter) physics-prior-based error compensation DNN connected via residual links to the ideal 4f model, trained iteratively in a hardware-in-the-loop loop: pre-train the SLM phase → load onto hardware and capture the real output → train the DNN to map ideal output to real output → re-train the phase with the gap-aware loss → repeat until accuracy converges. By offloading the two dominant quantifiable errors to explicit physical constraints, the DNN's search space is dramatically narrowed, enabling rapid convergence and SOTA experimental accuracy (96.5% MNIST, 85.7% QuickDraw16, 77.5% Fashion MNIST).

## Results

On MNIST, the proposed physics-constrained ONN with quantifiable physical information achieves 96.5% experimental classification accuracy after optimization, up from 83.7% with direct deployment (no error compensation DNN) and 61.6% without any physical information. On Fashion MNIST, accuracy improves from 56.2% to 77.5%, and on QuickDraw16 from 63.0% to 85.7%. The error compensation network is lightweight with approximately 5,000 learnable parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DAT (Zheng et al., Nature Machine Intelligence 2023), the proposed method wins on MNIST by 4.1 percentage points (96.5% vs. 92.4%) and essentially ties on Fashion MNIST (77.5% vs. 77.3%). Against PAT (Wright et al., Nature 2022), the improvement on MNIST is substantial: 96.5% vs. 61.4% (a 35.1-point gain), reflecting the advantage of incorporating quantifiable physical priors over a purely data-driven error compensation DNN. Against Hybrid CNN (Chang et al., Scientific Reports 2018), the proposed method achieves 85.7% on QuickDraw16 versus 72.0%, a 13.7-point improvement. Notably, even without the error compensation DNN, the quantifiable physical information alone (CCD exposure adjustment + gap loss function) lifts MNIST accuracy from 61.6% to 83.7%, demonstrating that the physics-constrained design is the primary driver of performance rather than the DNN architecture.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the hybrid (hardware-in-the-loop) training paradigm for optical neural networks [24, 18] and the error-compensation DNN approach of Zheng et al. [25] and Wright et al. [37] by injecting quantifiable physical priors—laser intensity fluctuation bounds and CCD exposure calibration—directly into the compensation network, inspired by the physics-informed neural network (PINN) framework [26–29]. Rather than letting a generic DNN learn all simulation-to-experiment discrepancies from data alone, the authors decompose the error into two explicitly measurable components (handled by a gap-based loss function and a CCD exposure adjustment) and reserve the lightweight DNN (~5,000 parameters) for the remaining unmeasurable coupling and fixed errors. This positions the work as a targeted refinement of the error-compensation lineage, narrowing the optimization space through physical constraints rather than architectural complexity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The simulation-to-experiment gap has been a persistent bottleneck for deploying ONNs in practical, time-sensitive AI applications, and this paper demonstrates that explicitly accounting for just two quantifiable error sources can close most of that gap with a remarkably lightweight compensation network. However, the approach is currently validated only for image classification on a specific 4f-SLM-DMD setup, and the authors themselves note that the gap-based loss function may not transfer to other tasks such as image restoration or segmentation. Open questions include whether the physics-prior strategy generalizes to multi-layer or non-linear ONN architectures, whether it scales to higher-resolution or color datasets beyond the 28×28 grayscale regime, and how the method performs under varying environmental conditions (temperature, vibration) that alter the fixed-error landscape.
