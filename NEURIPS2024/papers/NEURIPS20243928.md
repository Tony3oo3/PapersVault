## Problem

In offline RL, datasets typically contain trajectories from multiple experts of varying quality, and existing OT-based methods (e.g., W-BRAC) use OT distance as a behavior-cloning regularization that forces the policy to mimic the entire dataset, including sub-optimal actions. This paper asks whether the offline RL problem itself can be recast as an optimal transport problem that selectively stitches the best actions from the dataset rather than cloning all of them.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243928/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a standard offline dataset of transitions (s, a, r, s') collected from one or more experts, with no requirement that the data be from a single optimal policy. It outputs a deterministic policy π: S→A that maps each state to only a *partial* subset of the expert action distribution—specifically the actions with the highest Q-values—rather than the full β(·|s). A scalar hyperparameter w ≥ 1 controls the selectivity: larger w restricts the policy to a smaller, higher-quality action subspace, while w=1 degenerates to full behavior cloning.

## Method

The core reformulation treats offline RL as a state-conditional partial (extremal) optimal transport problem where the Q-function is the transport cost and the policy is the transport map, with inequality constraints π⊲d(s) ≤ w·β(·|s) replacing the equality constraints of standard OT. This yields a maximin saddle-point objective: a neural potential f_ω is trained to vanish on outlier (sub-optimal) actions while being positive on good ones, and the policy π_θ is trained to minimize −Q(s,a) − f(s,a), which naturally steers it away from low-Q actions. Unlike prior OT-in-RL approaches, OT is not an auxiliary regularization with a coefficient α but constitutes the entire policy optimization, and no 1-Lipschitz constraint is imposed on f since the method does not compute Wasserstein-1 distance. The algorithm (PPL) alternates Bellman updates on Q, gradient ascent on f, and gradient descent on π using stochastic batches, and can be coupled with any overestimation-avoiding Q-learning method (CQL, ReBRAC, One-Step RL) as the cost function source.

## Results

On the D4RL Antmaze-v2 suite, PPL^R achieves a total normalized score of 520 across six environments, outperforming the next-best method ReBRAC (461.2) by +58.8 points, with the largest gains on large-play-v2 (+16.4) and large-diverse-v2 (+22.2). On MuJoCo (9 tasks), PPL^R reaches a total of 799.45, edging out ReBRAC (796.9) and substantially exceeding CQL (698.6) and IQL (692.6). On Android-v1 (12 tasks), PPL^R scores 711.44 versus ReBRAC's 703.2 and IQL's 588.5. All scores are averaged normalized returns over the final 10 evaluations and 5 random seeds.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CQL [32], PPL^R dominates across all three suites: Antmaze total 520 vs 283.5, MuJoCo 799.45 vs 698.6, and Android 711.44 vs 484.8, with the largest per-task gaps on large-play (+56.8) and large-diverse (+56.6) Antmaze environments. Against IQL [28], PPL^R wins on every Antmaze task (e.g., large-play 76.8 vs 39.6) and on MuJoCo/Android totals, though IQL remains competitive on individual MuJoCo medium-expert tasks. Against OTR+IQL [35], the previous best OT-based offline RL method, PPL^R achieves consistent improvements on all Antmaze datasets (total 520 vs 384) and MuJoCo (799.45 vs 685.6), confirming that treating OT as the full policy optimization objective outperforms using it as a reward-labeling or regularization component. Against TD3+BC [14], PPL^R wins on MuJoCo total (799.45 vs 659.2) and Android total (711.44 vs 600.5), though TD3+BC edges out PPL^R on a few individual MuJoCo tasks (e.g., Walker medium-replay 81.8 vs 78.66). Against ReBRAC [47], the closest competitor, PPL^R wins on Antmaze total (520 vs 461.2) and Android total (711.44 vs 703.2), and narrowly on MuJoCo total (799.45 vs 796.9), but loses on a handful of individual MuJoCo and Android tasks (e.g., Hopper medium 93.49 vs 102.0; Hammer Cloned 4.74 vs 6.7). Against OneStep-RL [6], PPL achieves higher scores on 7 of 9 MuJoCo tasks in the one-step setting (Table 4), with the largest gain on Hopper medium (80.4 vs 59.6) and Walker medium-replay (86.6 vs 49.5).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from prior OT-in-RL work (W-BRAC, OTR+IQL, Primal/Sinkhorn Imitation Learning) that treats optimal transport as a regularization term or pseudo-reward, instead reframing the entire policy optimization as a partial OT problem between state and action distributions. It builds directly on the extremal/partial OT formulation of Gazdieva et al. [17] (inequality mass constraints) and the neural maximin OT framework of Korotin et al. [27] (saddle-point optimization between a potential and a transport map), repurposing the Q-function as the transport cost and the policy as the transport map. The key conceptual shift is replacing the equality constraint (full behavior cloning) with an inequality constraint that allows the policy to map states only to the best subset of expert actions, formalizing 'stitching' as a partial transport problem.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled, theoretically grounded mechanism for policy stitching in offline RL with a clean improvement guarantee over the behavior policy, addressing a practical pain point when datasets contain mixed-quality expert demonstrations. The limitation that the unbalance parameter w is task-dependent (requiring per-environment tuning) is acknowledged and somewhat constrains practical adoption. The formulation opens a pathway for integrating other OT tools (regularized OT, general cost functionals, weak OT for stochastic policies) into offline RL, which the authors identify as future work. Open questions include whether the partial OT framing extends naturally to hierarchical or multi-agent settings and whether w can be learned adaptively rather than set manually.
