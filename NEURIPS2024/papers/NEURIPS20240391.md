## Problem

Lensless image reconstruction faces a fundamental tension between photorealism and consistency: traditional deconvolution methods preserve fidelity but produce blurry outputs, while generative restoration approaches inject rich details but can alter or hallucinate scene content. Additionally, most existing methods assume a shift-invariant PSF, which is physically inaccurate for lensless cameras where the PSF varies spatially across the field of view, causing degraded reconstruction quality at the periphery.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240391/cropped_page0_idx11.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single lensless camera measurement (a multiplexed diffraction pattern) as input and produces a full-resolution reconstructed scene image. For PhlatCam, the target is 384×384 pixels from a 1280×1480 sensor; for DiffuserCam, the target is 210×380 pixels from a 1080×1920 sensor (downsampled and cropped). The pipeline is inherently two-stage: the first stage outputs an intermediate low-frequency range-space image, and the second stage refines it into the final photorealistic reconstruction.

## Method

PhoCoLens decomposes the reconstruction problem via range-null space decomposition into two stages. Stage 1 (SVDeconv) uses a set of K×K learnable PSF kernels—initialized from a single calibrated PSF—to perform multi-kernel deconvolution in the Fourier domain, then fuses the K² intermediate results via inverse-distance-weighted interpolation and refines them with a U-Net, all trained with MSE + LPIPS loss to recover the range-space (low-frequency) content. Stage 2 (null-space diffusion) fine-tunes a frozen Stable Diffusion model using a StableSR-style conditioning encoder that injects the range-space reconstruction as multi-scale guidance; the training objective is modified to minimize ‖A(ε_θ(x_t, t, c) − ε)‖², ensuring the generated residual lies in the null space of the forward operator and thus preserving measurement consistency while the generative prior supplies realistic high-frequency details.

## Results

On PhlatCam, PhoCoLens achieves the best performance across all six metrics: PSNR 22.07, SSIM 0.601, LPIPS 0.215, ManIQA 0.357, ClipIQA 0.565, and MUSIQ 62.20, surpassing the next-best method (FlatNet-gen) by 1.54 dB in PSNR and 0.052 in SSIM while also leading on all visual quality metrics. On DiffuserCam, PhoCoLens achieves PSNR 24.12, SSIM 0.748, LPIPS 0.161, ManIQA 0.172, ClipIQA 0.339, and MUSIQ 32.84, winning on five of six metrics (UPDN edges it by 0.55 dB in PSNR but exhibits visible artifacts). The method achieves the best trade-off between data fidelity and photorealism, with the largest gains over generative baselines like FlatNet+DiffBIR in consistency (e.g., LPIPS 0.161 vs. 0.505 on DiffuserCam) and over traditional methods in visual quality (e.g., MUSIQ 32.84 vs. 19.43 for WienerDeconv).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PhoCoLens outperforms all traditional and learning-based baselines on PhlatCam across every metric, with the largest margins over WienerDeconv (PSNR 22.07 vs. 12.19) and ADMM (PSNR 22.07 vs. 13.45). Against the strongest learning-based baseline FlatNet-gen, PhoCoLens gains 1.54 dB PSNR and 0.052 SSIM while also improving all visual quality scores (e.g., MUSIQ 62.20 vs. 44.94). On DiffuserCam, UPDN achieves a marginally higher PSNR (24.67 vs. 24.12) but PhoCoLens leads on SSIM (0.748 vs. 0.747), LPIPS (0.161 vs. 0.256), and all three no-reference quality metrics, with the paper noting UPDN's outputs contain visible artifacts. The generative baseline FlatNet+DiffBIR achieves higher visual quality scores on DiffuserCam (MUSIQ 51.20 vs. 32.84) but at a severe fidelity cost (PSNR 18.97, LPIPS 0.505), and qualitatively distorts scene content (e.g., transforming a hand into clothes). DDNM+ similarly trades fidelity for visual quality (PSNR 18.36, LPIPS 0.516 on DiffuserCam). The spatially varying deconvolution component (SVDeconv) is validated in Table 2, where it outperforms MultiWienerNet by 2.52 dB PSNR on DiffuserCam range-space reconstruction (28.84 vs. 26.32), confirming the benefit of the proposed interpolation strategy over prior multi-kernel approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the lensless imaging reconstruction literature (Wiener deconvolution, FlatNet, Le-ADMM-U, UPDN) and the growing use of diffusion models for inverse problems (DDNM, DiffBIR, StableSR). Its key conceptual departure is a principled two-stage decomposition grounded in range-null space theory: a spatially varying deconvolution network (SVDeconv) that recovers the range-space (low-frequency, consistency-preserving) component using only a single calibrated PSF, followed by a conditional diffusion model that fills in the null-space (high-frequency, photorealistic) component. This contrasts with prior spatially varying deconvolution methods like MultiWienerNet, which require tedious multi-location PSF calibrations, and with generative restoration approaches like DiffBIR that improve visual quality at the cost of content fidelity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PhoCoLens addresses a fundamental tension in lensless imaging—achieving both photorealism and data consistency simultaneously—that prior methods could not resolve. The practical advantage of SVDeconv (single PSF calibration, automatic learning of spatial variations) makes the approach more deployable than existing multi-kernel methods. However, the two-stage pipeline combined with diffusion sampling time precludes real-time capture, and the diffusion model can still hallucinate high-frequency details in smooth scenes. Open questions include whether the diffusion stage can be accelerated for real-time photo/video and whether 3D spatially varying PSF modeling can extend the framework to depth-aware reconstruction.
