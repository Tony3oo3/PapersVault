## Problem

Intracranial sEEG, a less invasive alternative to ECoG, remains underexplored for speech decoding, and no well-annotated sEEG language dataset existed. Existing self-supervised brain-signal models either build channel-level tokens with spatial-temporal integration (which fails on region-specific cognitive tasks like speech decoding) or fuse all channels into brain-level tokens (which ignores the brain's desynchronization nature during tasks). This paper addresses both the data gap and the modeling gap by introducing a region-level token framework grounded in neuroscientific findings about vocal production.

## Input / Output

The model takes multi-channel sEEG signals (7–13 electrodes, 72–158 channels per subject) filtered to 0.5–200 Hz, resampled to 1000 Hz, bi-polar re-referenced, and z-score normalized. After region-specific channel selection, only ~10 channels from the ventral sensorimotor cortex (vSMC) and superior temporal gyrus (STG) are retained, reducing the input from ~110 channels. Signals are segmented into 100 ms non-overlapping patches (30 patches for 3-second downstream samples, 40 for 4-second pre-training samples), and the model outputs a 61-class Chinese word label via a classification head over the flattened Transformer output.

## Method

Du-IN uses a two-stage self-supervised pre-training pipeline. First, a Du-IN VQ-VAE discretizes sEEG patches into neural tokens: a spatial encoder (linear projection + three 1D depthwise convolution layers) fuses channels within each patch into a region-level embedding, an 8-layer Transformer encoder with layer-normalized attention models temporal dynamics, and a vector quantizer with a 2048×64 codex (using ℓ₂-normalized nearest-neighbor lookup) maps embeddings to discrete codes, with a Transformer decoder + transposed convolutions reconstructing the original signal. Second, a Du-IN MAE freezes the VQ-VAE codex and trains a fresh encoder to predict the discrete codex indices of ~50% masked patches (with symmetric masking for efficiency), learning contextual embeddings without reconstructing low-level signal details. The key architectural insight is that 1D depthwise convolution fuses channels within specific brain regions to form region-level tokens, preserving the specificity of brain computation that channel-level or whole-brain tokenization destroys, and the discrete codex target prevents the MAE from overfitting to fine-grained reconstruction.

## Results

On the 61-word Chinese speech classification task, Du-IN(mae) achieves 62.70% ± 4.69% top-1 accuracy, the best among all evaluated methods. The model uses only 4.38M parameters and requires just ~10 channels (from ~1 electrode in vSMC/STG regions) after region-specific channel selection, compared to 109.75 channels without selection (which yields only 30.12%). The two-stage pre-training (VQ-VAE + MAE) contributes a 6.41-point improvement over the randomly initialized Du-IN (56.29%), and the MAE pre-training alone outperforms VQ-VAE-only initialization by 4.46 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Du-IN(mae) outperforms all 9 baselines by a wide margin on the 61-word classification task. Among region-level token methods, it surpasses Neuro-BERT (49.51%) by 13.19 points, EEG-Conformer (45.82%) by 16.88 points, DeWave (32.43%) by 30.27 points, CNN-BiGRU (32.04%) by 30.66 points, and TS-TCC (24.85%) by 37.85 points. Among channel-level token foundation models, the gap is even more dramatic: Du-IN(mae) exceeds Brant (12.42% multi-subject) by 50.28 points, LaBraM-PopT (11.78%) by 50.92 points, LaBraM (11.53%) by 51.17 points, and BrainBERT (7.50% multi-subject) by 55.20 points. The paper attributes the poor performance of channel-level models to their inability to capture rapid brain dynamics (using 1-second patches) and their failure to encourage region-level embeddings, while region-level methods that use shared convolutional kernels across channels (e.g., EEG-Conformer) overlook the specificity of brain computation. Notably, even the randomly initialized Du-IN (56.29%) already outperforms all baselines, suggesting the architectural choice of 1D depthwise convolution for channel fusion within specific brain regions is the primary driver of performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in intracranial neural signal modeling, which builds channel-level tokens and applies spatial-temporal Transformers to recover inter-channel relationships (BrainBERT, Brant, LaBraM, PopT). Instead, Du-IN fuses channels within specific brain regions using 1D depthwise convolution to produce region-level tokens, then applies temporal-only Transformer modeling. The self-supervised pre-training combines VQ-VAE-style discrete codex tokenization (inspired by Van Den Oord et al.) with masked sEEG modeling (inspired by MAE and LaBraM's symmetric masking), creating a two-stage pipeline where the codex provides discrete supervision targets for the mask model. The architecture is explicitly grounded in neuroscientific findings about vocal production networks (vSMC, STG, dLMC), distinguishing it from modality-agnostic time-series approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that sEEG—a less invasive recording modality than ECoG—can support high-performance speech decoding, and that approximately one electrode in language-related regions suffices, validating prior neuroscience with a computational model. The stark failure of channel-level foundation models (6–12% accuracy) versus region-level models (56–63%) on this cognitive task challenges the assumption that spatial-temporal integration across all channels is necessary or beneficial for tasks localized to specific brain regions. Key limitations include the closed 61-word set, restriction to vocal production (not semantic processing), and a single language; open questions include whether sEEG can capture cross-region semantic information and whether the region-level tokenization strategy generalizes to other cognitive tasks or scales to larger electrode arrays.
