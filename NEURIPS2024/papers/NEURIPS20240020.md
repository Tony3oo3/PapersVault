## Problem

Existing video-language models compress dense video tokens via pooling or Q-Former, producing tokens with semantically entangled information that poorly aligns with the semantically disentangled word tokens LLMs are designed to process. This paper asks whether generating semantically decoupled, object-centric and event-centric video tokens can improve video-language modeling by better matching the representational structure of language.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240020/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a video sampled at 1 FPS (frames resized to 224×224) and a text question as input. The CLIP ViT-L/14 encoder produces 16×16×T dense tokens per video (e.g., 46,080 for a 3-minute clip), which the OE-Slots module compresses into exactly 192 semantically decoupled slots: 64 object-centric slots (8 sampled frames × 8 slots each) and 128 event-centric slots (16 downsampled spatial positions × 8 slots each). The frozen Vicuna-7B LLM then generates a free-form text answer conditioned on these 192 projected slots plus the text instruction.

## Method

The core contribution is the dual-branch Object-Event Slots (OE-Slots) module, inspired by SlowFast networks, which applies slot attention along complementary axes: the Object-Slots branch operates at low frame rate (8 frames) but full spatial resolution (16×16), using per-frame slot attention to extract object-centric slots; the Event-Slots branch operates at full temporal resolution (all T frames) but low spatial resolution (4×4 via stride-4 pooling), using per-spatial-position slot attention along the temporal axis to extract event-centric slots. Slot attention's normalization-over-slots mechanism introduces competition that promotes semantically decoupled representations, unlike Q-Former's cross-attention which produces entangled queries. Training follows a three-stage protocol: (1) self-supervised pre-training of slot attention modules via feature reconstruction (DINOSAUR-style), (2) separate single-branch instruction tuning, and (3) joint two-branch instruction tuning with the projection layer. Only the OE-Slots module and projection layers are trained (~27M parameters); both the CLIP encoder and Vicuna-7B LLM remain frozen, keeping total trainable cost low while achieving state-of-the-art video QA with just 192 tokens.

## Results

Slot-VLM achieves state-of-the-art average accuracy of 64.3% (Slot-VLM) and 65.0% (Slot-VLM†) on three open-ended video QA benchmarks (MSVD-QA, MSRVTT-QA, ActivityNet-QA), using only 192 video tokens — fewer than all compared methods. On MSRVTT-QA, Slot-VLM reaches 69.6% accuracy, surpassing the previous best (Video-LLaVA† at 59.2%) by over 10 points. On multi-choice benchmarks, Slot-VLM scores 55.8% on Egoschema (vs. 50.8% for LLoVi), 62.0% on NExT-QA (vs. 61.7% for VideoChat2), and 42.7% on STAR (vs. 41.8% for Flamingo-9B).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against methods using the same 100K video instruction pairs, Slot-VLM outperforms Video-ChatGPT by 10.0% (MSVD-QA), 20.3% (MSRVTT-QA), and 13.1% (ActivityNet-QA) in accuracy, and exceeds Video-LLaVA by 10.1%, 11.3%, and 7.6% respectively. Slot-VLM† (with 665K additional image-text pairs) achieves the best average accuracy of 65.0%, surpassing Video-LLaVA† (58.4%) by 6.6% and Chat-UniVi (55.1%, which uses 2M video pairs) by 9.9%. On MSRVTT-QA, Slot-VLM's 69.6% accuracy is a clear outlier, beating the next-best Video-LLaVA† (59.2%) by over 10 points. On multi-choice benchmarks, Slot-VLM leads Egoschema (55.8% vs. 50.8% for LLoVi) and NExT-QA (62.0% vs. 61.7% for VideoChat2), and is competitive on STAR (42.7% vs. 41.8% for Flamingo-9B). Notably, Slot-VLM uses only 192 video tokens, substantially fewer than VideoChat2 (1536), Video-ChatGPT (356), and Video-LLaMA (256), while achieving superior accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on slot attention (Locatello et al., 2020) and its real-world extension DINOSAUR (Seitzer et al., 2022), repurposing the mechanism from image object decomposition to video token generation for LLM alignment. It departs from the dominant Q-Former paradigm (BLIP-2, Video-LLaMA, VideoChat) and pooling-based compression (Video-ChatGPT) by arguing that video tokens should be semantically decoupled—mirroring how words in language are conceptually disentangled—rather than semantically entangled. The dual-branch design (spatial objects at low frame rate + temporal events at high frame rate) is explicitly inspired by SlowFast networks, but redirects the objective from refining grid-wise features to generating a compact set of interpretable slots for LLM input.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides evidence that semantically decoupled video tokens (192 total) can outperform both Q-Former and pooling-based approaches with substantially fewer tokens, offering a practical path to efficient video-language modeling. The conceptual framing—that LLMs benefit from 'word-like' disentangled visual tokens—opens a direction for future work on interpretable visual representations. Key limitations include imperfect object/event segmentation, reliance on a single CLIP encoder and Vicuna-7B LLM, and untested scalability to hour-long videos, where the fixed slot budget may be insufficient.
