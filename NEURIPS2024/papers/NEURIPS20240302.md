## Problem

Existing AVQA datasets generate questions from a small set of predefined templates (e.g., 33 templates in MUSIC-AVQA), yielding a limited vocabulary of only 93 words and no mechanism to measure performance on rare or out-of-distribution samples. Concurrently, state-of-the-art AVQA methods overlearn statistical regularities between critical question words and answers, making them brittle on rephrased or rare-answer questions. The paper addresses both gaps by introducing a robustness-focused benchmark and a debiasing training strategy.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a video sequence sampled at 1 fps and its audio track sampled at 16 kHz, partitioned into T non-overlapping 1-second visual-audio segment pairs, alongside a natural language question. The output is a discrete class label from a fixed vocabulary of 42 answer classes. The proposed MUSIC-AVQA-R dataset expands the test split from 9,129 to 211,572 rephrased questions (vocabulary size 465) and further splits them into head (frequent) and tail (rare) subsets per question type using a normalized Shannon-entropy threshold, enabling simultaneous in-distribution and out-of-distribution evaluation.

## Method

The architecture encodes audio via a frozen VGGish, video via a frozen ResNet-18, and question/multi-modal interactions via VisualBert, producing uni-modal and fused representations projected to a 42-way logit space. Three separate MLP bias learners, each taking only one modality's representation, capture uni-modal shortcut predictions. The MCCD strategy then applies two complementary losses: a discrepancy-enlargement term (joint inverse Euclidean distance) that pushes each uni-modal logit away from the multi-modal logit, and a cycle-guidance term (KL divergence along the question→audio→video→question cycle) that constrains uni-modal logits to be mutually similar, preventing any single modality from dominating. The bias learners are discarded at inference, and the strategy is plug-and-play, consistently improving 9–11 out of 13 baselines across both datasets.

## Results

On the standard MUSIC-AVQA test split, the proposed architecture achieves 72.20% overall accuracy, outperforming COCA (71.64%) and STG (71.52%) among methods using the same ResNet-18 visual encoder. On the newly introduced MUSIC-AVQA-R dataset (211,572 rephrased questions with head/tail distribution shift), the method attains 66.95% overall accuracy, a 9.32% improvement over STG+MCCD (57.63%) and 7.70% over LAVisH (59.25%). The MCCD strategy demonstrates plug-and-play efficacy, improving 11 out of 13 baselines on MUSIC-AVQA-R and 9 out of 13 on MUSIC-AVQA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among AVQA-specific baselines, the proposed method (66.95%) substantially outperforms LAVisH (59.25%), STG (52.80%), LAViT (47.40%), and AVSD (52.33%) on MUSIC-AVQA-R, with the largest gap over LAViT (9.55%). On the standard MUSIC-AVQA split, Ours (72.20%) beats COCA (71.64%) and STG (71.52%) but trails LAVisH (73.76%), which benefits from stronger ViT/Swin visual encoders rather than ResNet-18. Notably, single-modality baselines reveal surprising robustness: CONVLSTM (audio-only) achieves 55.20% and MCAN (vision-only) reaches 57.27% on MUSIC-AVQA-R, both exceeding several full AVQA methods, suggesting those methods over-rely on memorized cross-modal statistical regularities. The MCCD strategy consistently improves most baselines when added in plug-and-play fashion (e.g., STG: 52.80→57.63%, LAVisH: 59.25→66.95%), though a few methods like HME and PSAC show slight degradation, indicating the debiasing benefit is architecture-dependent. The 9.32% headline improvement is measured against STG+MCCD (57.63%), the strongest debiased AVQA baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the VQA robustness evaluation paradigm (GQA-OOD, adversarial VQA) to the audio-visual QA domain for the first time, constructing MUSIC-AVQA-R by rephrasing and distribution-shifting the test split of MUSIC-AVQA. On the method side, it builds on ensemble-learning debiasing approaches (RUBi, introspective distillation) but generalizes them from the two-modality (image+text) setting to three modalities (audio, video, text) via a cycle-guided KL-divergence constraint. COCA (Lao et al., AAAI 2023) is the only prior AVQA debiasing work, but it addresses only language bias; this paper explicitly targets all three uni-modal biases and their interplay.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first systematic evidence that SOTA AVQA methods (STG, LAVisH, LAViT) rely heavily on memorized statistical regularities rather than genuine multi-modal reasoning, as revealed by the large head-tail accuracy gap on MUSIC-AVQA-R. The plug-and-play MCCD strategy (improving 11/13 baselines) offers a practical debiasing tool for the community. Key limitations include the restricted 42-class single-word answer space inherited from MUSIC-AVQA, the music-only domain, and the absence of generative backbones, leaving open whether the findings and strategy transfer to open-ended or broader-domain AVQA.
