## Problem

Monotonic alpha-divergence minimization algorithms for variational inference were recently proposed with encouraging empirical results, but their convergence properties as the number of iterations grows were entirely undetermined. This paper fills that gap by establishing convergence guarantees and rates for both the exact and Monte-Carlo-based versions of the algorithm when the variational family is an exponential model.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243369/cropped_page8_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a target posterior density p(y) (known up to a normalizing constant) and a regular minimal exponential family q_η(y) = κ(y)exp[⟨η,S(y)⟩−A(η)] as input, and produces variational parameters η (equivalently mean parameters μ = ∂A(η)) that minimize the alpha-divergence D_α(q_η‖p). In the empirical setting, K i.i.d. samples are drawn from q_η at each iteration to compute Monte-Carlo estimators. For the VAE experiments, the input is RGB images (32×32 CIFAR-10, 128×128 CelebA) and the output is generated images scored by Fréchet Inception Distance.

## Method

The paper first proves that the exact monotonic algorithm converges geometrically to a strict local minimizer of the alpha-divergence, with asymptotic rate governed by the spectral radius ρ* = α·ρ(Cov_{q_η*}(S)^{−1} Cov_{φ̃^α}(S)). It then shows that the natural empirical version uses a biased ratio estimator that converges to the wrong fixed point, and proposes an unbiased Robbins-Monro recursion μ_{t+1} = μ_t + γ_t[Ê(μ_t) − μ_t·ℓ̂*α(μ_t)] that simultaneously serves as a natural gradient descent on the alpha-divergence objective. Almost sure convergence to a critical point and a law of the iterated logarithm are established under standard gain and moment conditions. The framework is extended to VAE training by showing the biased and unbiased algorithms correspond to gradient ascent on the Variational Rényi bound and a related linear bound respectively, with reparameterization-based gradient estimators for joint encoder-decoder updates.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, the best FID achieved by the biased VR approach is 81.2 (α=0.99) and by the unbiased UB approach is 85.8 (α=0.01), both substantially outperforming the IWAE baseline (122.2) and standard VAE baseline (121.1). On CelebA, the best FID scores are 234.3 (VR, α=0.7) and 231.1 (UB, α=0.7), again beating both the IWAE (243.8) and VAE (235.3) baselines. In toy Gaussian-mixture experiments, the paper's unbiased algorithm (UNB) and the monotonic maximization approach (MAX) exhibit the most stable convergence trajectories, while plain SGD on natural parameters (SGE) is the most sensitive to hyperparameter tuning and can diverge.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the IWAE baseline (α=0, Li & Turner 2016), both the VR and UB approaches achieve substantially lower FID on both datasets: on CIFAR-10 the best VR (81.2) and UB (85.8) scores beat IWAE (122.2) by 41 and 36 FID points respectively, while on CelebA the best scores (234.3 VR, 231.1 UB) beat IWAE (243.8) by 9–13 points. Against the standard VAE baseline (α=1, Kingma & Welling 2013), the gains are smaller but consistent: on CIFAR-10 the best VR (81.2) and UB (85.8) beat VAE (121.1) by 40 and 35 points, and on CelebA the best UB (231.1) beats VAE (235.3) by 4.2 points. In the toy experiments, the paper's UNB algorithm is at least as good as SGD on mean parameters (SGM), SGD on natural parameters (SGE), and natural gradient descent (NAT, Amari 1998) in terms of final alpha-divergence, while being less computationally expensive than SGM and NAT (which require inverting the Fisher information matrix at O(d³) cost) and more stable than SGE, which can diverge under unfavorable loss landscapes. The biased MAX approach converges fastest in early iterations but settles at slightly suboptimal parameters due to the normalization bias, eventually being surpassed by UNB after sufficient iterations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper provides the first rigorous asymptotic analysis of the monotonic alpha-divergence minimization algorithm introduced by Daudel et al. (JMLR 2023), which had previously been studied only empirically. The exact algorithm is analyzed through the lens of exponential family geometry, revealing a natural connection to Fisher information and steepest descent, while the empirical version is recast as a Robbins-Monro stochastic approximation. A key departure from prior work is the identification of a bias in the straightforward Monte-Carlo plug-in estimator (the ratio of two sample means) and the proposal of an unbiased alternative that preserves the monotonicity property of the exact algorithm. The VAE extension builds on the Rényi divergence variational inference framework of Li & Turner (NeurIPS 2016) and the reparameterization trick of Kingma & Welling (2013).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper closes a significant theoretical gap by establishing geometric convergence rates for the exact algorithm and almost-sure convergence plus a law of the iterated logarithm for the empirical version, giving practitioners confidence in the asymptotic behavior of alpha-divergence VI. The identification of the bias in the natural empirical estimator is practically important: it explains why the commonly used ratio-of-means update can converge to suboptimal parameters, and motivates the unbiased alternative. However, the analysis is restricted to exponential family variational models, and the VAE experiments on CIFAR-10 and CelebA show only modest FID improvements over standard VAE and IWAE baselines, with the optimal alpha varying inconsistently across datasets and approaches. Extending the theory to non-exponential families and scaling the VAE experiments to larger benchmarks remain open.
