## Problem

Learning tasks over symmetry groups (e.g., predicting element order or categorical invariants) typically rely on predefined group representations chosen from known representation theory, yet the choice of representation is critical and task-dependent. No prior method learns task-adaptive matrix representations of group elements while simultaneously respecting the algebraic structure (axioms and relations) of the group, limiting sample efficiency and generalization to unseen group elements.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241021/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes sequences of group generators encoded via a signed one-hot scheme (each generator maps to ±e_i, identity to the zero vector), where the sequence length is variable and the identity generator is included for padding. For the symmetric group task, the output is a discrete class label (element order); for the braid group task, the output is a 3-dimensional integer vector of Jordan-Holder multiplicities predicted via regression. Because the representation is built from generator-level matrices multiplied sequentially, the model naturally handles words of arbitrary length, enabling extrapolation to group elements longer than any seen in training.

## Method

MatrixNet is a sequence model whose core component is a 'matrix block' that maps each generator to an invertible n×n matrix via the matrix exponential of a linear (or odd nonlinear) transformation of the signed one-hot encoding, ensuring the identity maps to I and inverses map to matrix inverses by construction. Group element representations are formed by sequentially multiplying the generator matrices, and the resulting matrix is flattened and fed to a downstream MLP for the task. Group relations are enforced through an auxiliary Frobenius-norm loss that penalizes deviation of relation-word matrices from the identity, complementing the structural enforcement of group axioms. Three architectural variants are explored: a linear-network block (MatrixNet-LN), a tanh-nonlinear block (MatrixNet-NL), and a block-diagonal multi-channel block (MatrixNet-MC) inspired by irreducible representation decomposition. This design makes the model parameter-efficient (only generator representations are learned) and enables automatic generalization to longer or unseen group words without data augmentation.

## Results

On the S₁₀ order prediction task, MatrixNet-NL achieves 100% accuracy with a cross-entropy loss of 0.0003, substantially outperforming the LSTM baseline (77.3% accuracy) and the fixed-representation ablation (87% accuracy). On the B₃ Jordan-Holder multiplicity regression task, MatrixNet-LN and MatrixNet-NL achieve 99.9% accuracy with MSE of 0.001 and 0.00064 respectively, compared to 93.0% (MSE 0.075) for LSTM and 89.1% (MSE 0.132) for MLP. In the length extrapolation experiment, MatrixNet-LN and MatrixNet-NL maintain near-zero MSE as braid word length increases from 8 to 10, while baselines degrade significantly.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MatrixNet variants consistently outperform all sequence-model baselines (MLP, LSTM, Transformer) on both tasks. On the B₃ regression task, MatrixNet-LN and MatrixNet-NL achieve 99.9% accuracy with MSE below 0.001, beating the best baseline (LSTM at 93.0%, MSE 0.075) by roughly 7 percentage points in accuracy and an order of magnitude in MSE. On S₁₀ order prediction, MatrixNet-MC and MatrixNet-NL reach 100% accuracy while the LSTM achieves only 77.3% and the fixed-representation ablation (using standard permutation matrices) reaches 87%, demonstrating that learned representations are superior to predefined ones. The Transformer baseline performs worst on the braid task (42.3% accuracy), suggesting that general-purpose sequence architectures struggle with the algebraic structure of group elements. The relational error analysis (Table 4) confirms that the extrapolation advantage of MatrixNet-LN/NL stems from their near-zero group-relation violation (0.071 and 0.066 Frobenius norm) versus 13.63 for the base MatrixNet, showing that representation quality directly governs generalization to longer unseen words.

## Relation to Prior Work

MatrixNet departs from equivariant neural networks (Cohen & Welling, Kondor & Trivedi) by learning group representations rather than using fixed ones from known representation theory, and from symmetry-discovery methods (Park et al., Yang et al.) by starting with a known group and learning a linear matrix action rather than discovering unknown symmetries. The closest prior work is Hajij et al.'s algebraically-informed networks, which also learn group actions from a presentation, but MatrixNet restricts to linear (matrix) actions and adds a downstream task signal alongside the presentation. The matrix-exponential parameterization over signed one-hot generator encodings is a new architectural choice that structurally enforces the free-group homomorphism property, with a Frobenius-norm relation loss handling the remaining group-specific relations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that learned matrix representations outperform the 'obvious' fixed permutation representations for group-element prediction, and that low relational error directly enables length extrapolation without data augmentation — a practical advantage for infinite or combinatorially large groups. The braid group experiment connects to a genuinely open problem in representation theory (Jordan-Holder multiplicities under braid actions), positioning the method as a tool for mathematical discovery. Key limitations include the inexact enforcement of relations via auxiliary loss (rather than architectural constraint), restriction to finitely presented discrete groups, and evaluation only on small-scale groups (S₁₀, B₃), leaving open whether the approach scales to larger or continuous (Lie) groups.
