## Problem

Existing data parallelism strategies for LLM training (ZeRO-1/2/3, MiCS, FSDP) only offer binary partitioning choices—no partitioning or global partitioning—ignoring the intermediate option of intra-group partitioning. On mainstream heterogeneous GPU clusters where NVLink intra-node bandwidth (~2000 Gbps) far exceeds RDMA inter-node bandwidth (~90 Gbps), this limited trade-off space forces unnecessary inter-group communication during forward and backward passes, severely capping training throughput. The paper asks whether refined partitioning granularity and communication topology can close this gap.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an LLM model (e.g., LLaMA-7B/65B), a text training corpus (C4 from RedPajama), and the GPU cluster's topology parameters: number of groups (ng), GPUs per group (m), intra-group bandwidth (B'), inter-group bandwidth (B), total parameters (Ψ), trainable parameters (Ψ'), gradient accumulation steps (s), and per-micro-batch compute time (t_comp). It outputs trained model weights and, via the quantitative guideline, a recommended partitioning strategy (one of 14 PaRO-DP configurations) that minimizes predicted mini-batch time T. The guideline is a closed-form calculation over all 14 strategies, selecting the one with the smallest max(t_comm, t_comp).

## Method

PaRO-DP introduces a three-level partitioning scheme—No partitioning (N), Intra-group partitioning (I), and Global partitioning (G)—applied independently to parameters, gradients, and optimizer states, yielding 14 effective strategies (after eliminating 13 of 27 combinations via the insight that optimizer-state granularity must be ≥ parameter/gradient granularity). Strategies like P_IIG replace the global all-gather and reduce-scatter of ZeRO-3 with fast intra-group equivalents during every micro-batch's forward and backward passes, deferring only a single inter-group reduce-scatter and all-gather to the gradient-update step. PaRO-CC further accelerates the remaining global collective operations by decomposing the flat Ring topology into a two-level hierarchy (inner ring within each group, outer ring across groups), ensuring each communication step is purely intra- or inter-group rather than a mixed-speed bottleneck. A quantitative guideline computes t_comm for each strategy using closed-form bandwidth formulas and selects the partitioning that minimizes total mini-batch time, validated to match empirical throughput rankings across full-parameter, partial-parameter, and PEFT scenarios.

## Results

On LLaMA-65B partial-parameter training (Ψ′=Ψ/16) across 32 A100 GPUs, PaRO-IIG achieves 2.16 samples/sec versus ZeRO-3's 0.59 samples/sec — a 266% speedup — while using less peak memory (39.5 GB vs. 44.2 GB for MiCS). On LLaMA-7B full-parameter training, PaRO-NII reaches 4.65 samples/sec, outperforming ZeRO-2 (2.79) by 67% and ZeRO-3 (1.93) by 141%. PaRO-CC independently improves Megatron-TP end-to-end throughput by 17% (129→152 samples/sec) and reduces collective communication time by 25–40% compared to the traditional Ring topology.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PaRO decisively outperforms ZeRO-3 (GGG) across all tested scenarios: by 128% on LLaMA-7B full-parameter training (4.40 vs. 1.93 samples/sec for IIG), by 86% on LLaMA-65B full-parameter (0.39 vs. 0.21), and by 266% on LLaMA-65B partial-parameter (2.16 vs. 0.59). Against ZeRO-2 (NGG), PaRO-NII achieves a 67% throughput gain on 7B full-parameter training (4.65 vs. 2.79) and a 21% gain on 7B partial-parameter (14.46 vs. 11.94). PaRO-IIG matches or slightly exceeds MiCS (III) on 65B partial-parameter training (2.16 vs. 2.15 samples/sec) while using 4.75 GB less peak memory (39.48 vs. 44.23 GB). ZeRO++ and FSDP-hs are consistently outperformed: on 7B full-parameter training, PaRO-NII (4.65) beats ZeRO++ (2.43) by 91% and FSDP-hs (2.86) by 63%. In the PEFT scenario (Ψ′=3Ψ/1000, LLaMA-65B), PaRO-ING achieves 1.73 samples/sec versus ZeRO-3's 1.20 (44% improvement) and ZeRO++'s 1.28 (35% improvement).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the ZeRO family of model-state partitioning strategies by introducing a three-level partitioning granularity (No partitioning, Intra-group, Global) applied independently to parameters, gradients, and optimizer states, yielding 14 effective strategies versus ZeRO's three levels. It builds directly on the group-aware partitioning ideas in MiCS and ZeRO++ and the locality-aware sharding in FSDP-hybrid-shard, but departs from them by systematically enumerating all valid combinations and providing a quantitative selection guideline. The PaRO-CC contribution is orthogonal, rearranging the standard Ring topology into a hierarchical inner/outer ring to decouple intra- and inter-group communication steps, a technique applicable to any strategy requiring global collective operations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The systematic strategy set and quantitative guideline give practitioners a principled way to select the optimal DP configuration for their specific model size, trainable-parameter ratio, and cluster topology, rather than relying on heuristics. The first distributed training strategy tailored to PEFT scenarios (where Ψ′ ≪ Ψ) addresses a gap in the literature, and PaRO-CC's 17% end-to-end speedup in Megatron-TP demonstrates broad applicability beyond data parallelism. Key limitations include the focus on data parallelism alone (requiring integration with TP/PP for very large models) and the dependence on heterogeneous network topologies for the largest gains, leaving open how the framework performs on increasingly homogeneous interconnects.
