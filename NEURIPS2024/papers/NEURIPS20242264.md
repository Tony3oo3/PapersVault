## Problem

Error backpropagation, despite its ubiquity, is biologically implausible due to the weight transport problem, non-local credit assignment, and backward locking. Existing biologically plausible alternatives such as target propagation and feedback alignment either still depend on forward-pass activations (causing backward locking) or incur 3–6× computational overhead. This paper seeks a learning scheme that simultaneously resolves all three issues while maintaining competitive accuracy.

## Input / Output

For classification, the forward network consumes raw RGB images (28×28 for MNIST/FashionMNIST, 32×32 for CIFAR-10/100) while the feedback network independently consumes one-hot encoded target labels; the two networks operate in anti-parallel and never share activations. For the autoencoder task on STL-10, the forward path encodes images into a latent bottleneck and the feedback path decodes from the target reconstruction, with the layer-wise alignment loss replacing the standard reconstruction objective. The final output is a class logit vector (classification) or a reconstructed image (autoencoder).

## Method

CCL employs a dual-network architecture where a feedforward network F_fw maps inputs to outputs and a structurally mirrored feedback network F_bw maps targets back to the input space, each with its own independent weight matrices (U_l and V_l). Training minimizes a layer-wise MSE loss between the L2-normalized activations of corresponding forward and feedback layers, with a cross-entropy loss applied only at the output layer. A stop-gradient (detach) operation is applied at every layer boundary, ensuring each weight update depends only on local activations and thereby eliminating the backward-locking dependency between forward and feedback passes. The key insight, drawn from biological counter-current exchange, is that the bottom layers of each network retain more information (by the data processing inequality) and thus provide more informative guidance to the top layers of the other network, creating a reciprocal learning dynamic. Stabilization techniques include an auxiliary L2 orthogonality loss to prevent feature collapse, gradient centralization, and a flooding mechanism to halt updates when sample-wise error is small.

## Results

On MLP classification, CCL achieves 98.13% on MNIST, 88.58% on FashionMNIST, 52.73% on CIFAR-10, and 21.76% on CIFAR-100, matching or exceeding backpropagation (BP) on CIFAR-10 (52.73% vs. 50.03%) while remaining within ~1% on the other three datasets. On a 5-layer CNN, CCL reaches 82.94% on CIFAR-10 and 56.29% on CIFAR-100, surpassing BP on CIFAR-100 (56.29% vs. 51.92%) but trailing on CIFAR-10 (82.94% vs. 87.12%). For the STL-10 autoencoder task, CCL achieves a reconstruction MSE of 0.0086±0.0001 compared to BP's 0.0059±0.0001. Computationally, CCL requires only 2.55M FLOPs per sample on MNIST (6-layer MLP), comparable to BP's 2.39M and far below TP-family methods (18–126M).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CCL is the strongest biologically plausible method across all four MLP benchmarks, outperforming the closest competitor FW-DTP by 0.93% on MNIST, 0.80% on FashionMNIST, and 6.82% on CIFAR-10, while trailing it by only 0.67% on CIFAR-100. Against backpropagation, CCL is within 0.06% on MNIST and 1.00% on FashionMNIST, but actually exceeds BP by 2.70% on CIFAR-10 (52.73% vs. 50.03%) and trails by 0.79% on CIFAR-100. The gap over TP-family baselines (DRL, L-DRL) is substantial—5–10% on CIFAR-10/100—while CCL also surpasses FA and DFA by 2–6% on CIFAR-10. On the CNN setting, CCL reverses the MLP trend on CIFAR-100, beating BP by 4.37% (56.29% vs. 51.92%), though it lags BP by 4.18% on CIFAR-10. In the autoencoder task on STL-10, CCL's reconstruction MSE (0.0086) is ~46% higher than BP's (0.0059), indicating a quality gap in unsupervised reconstruction despite capturing general image structure.

## Relation to Prior Work

CCL synthesizes and extends ideas from the Target Propagation family (dual forward/feedback networks, layer-wise local targets) and Feedback Alignment (independent feedback weights) into a unified framework, but departs from both by introducing gradient detachment to fully decouple the forward and feedback passes, thereby addressing the backward-locking problem that plagues TP variants. The 'counter-current' framing draws an explicit analogy to biological exchange mechanisms (fish gills, renal systems), positioning the reciprocal information flow between the two networks as the core design principle rather than a mere implementation detail. Unlike TP methods that require additional feedback weight update loops per batch (3–6× FLOP overhead), CCL keeps computational cost on par with standard backpropagation by computing a single pairwise MSE loss across corresponding layers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CCL is notable for simultaneously addressing all three classical biological-plausibility objections (weight transport, non-local credit assignment, backward locking) while maintaining BP-level computational efficiency and matching BP accuracy on MNIST (98.13% vs 98.19%)—a combination no prior biologically plausible method achieved. The extension to autoencoder reconstruction on STL-10 is, to the authors' knowledge, the first application of a biologically plausible learning rule to unsupervised representation learning, opening a new application axis. Key open questions include the absence of theoretical convergence or stability guarantees, the reliance on training stabilizers (warmup, gradient centralization, flooding) that add practical complexity, and whether the approach scales to ImageNet-scale or transformer architectures; the CNN result on CIFAR-10 (82.94% vs 87.12% BP) also suggests a non-trivial accuracy gap in deeper convolutional settings.
