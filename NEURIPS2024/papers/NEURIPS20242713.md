## Problem

Entropy minimization is the dominant self-supervised loss for test-time adaptation, but it is prone to model collapse, overconfident predictions, and noisy parameter updates, especially under aggressive distribution shifts. Existing methods lack a statistically principled mechanism to detect when a shift has actually occurred and to control the magnitude of adaptation, risking unnecessary degradation on in-distribution data. This paper asks whether one can build a test-time adaptation scheme that provably does no harm when no shift is present while still adapting quickly and effectively when a shift is detected.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a fully online, batch-size-1 stream of unlabeled RGB test images, a pre-trained classifier (ViT-B/16 with layer norm or ResNet-50 with group norm), and a small holdout set of unlabeled source-domain images used only to pre-compute the empirical CDF of source entropies offline. At each step the method outputs an updated set of normalization-layer parameters (the only trainable weights) and a class prediction for the current test image. The holdout requirement is analogous to EATA's anti-forgetting reference set, and no test-time labels, augmentations, or access to the original training data are needed.

## Method

POEM couples two online components. First, a sequential drift-detection tool applies the probability integral transform to each test entropy using the pre-computed source CDF, then runs a betting martingale whose betting variable ε_j is learned online via a scale-free OGD (SF-OGD) step with a clip-aware loss, yielding an anytime regret bound of O(√t). Second, the adaptation mechanism exploits the fact that any valid betting function is a likelihood ratio: it integrates the betting function to obtain an estimated target CDF Q(u_j), applies the inverse source CDF to produce a pseudo-entropy target Z̃_j, and minimizes a squared matching loss (with high-entropy sample filtering and exponential weighting) between the actual test entropy and Z̃_j. Proposition 2 shows that under the log-optimal bet, this pseudo-entropy is exactly the optimal transport map from target to source entropies under the 1-D Wasserstein distance, giving the loss a principled distribution-matching interpretation. In the absence of shift, ε_j stays near zero so Z̃_j ≈ Z_j^t and the model is essentially untouched; under shift, the martingale grows rapidly and the matching loss drives the adapted model's entropy distribution back toward the source.

## Results

On ImageNet-C (severity level 5, averaged over 15 corruptions), POEM achieves 67.36% top-1 accuracy with a ViT model, outperforming the best baseline EATA (64.14%) by 3.22 percentage points. In the continual-shift setting, POEM adapts faster than all baselines, improving accuracy with as few as 100 examples per corruption segment. In the in-distribution setting, POEM maintains the original model's accuracy while keeping ECE and parameter deviation (‖ω‖²_F) near zero, whereas baseline methods unnecessarily increase ECE and modify parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

POEM outperforms all four entropy-minimization baselines (TENT, EATA, SAR, COTTA) on every corruption type for the ViT model in the single-shift setting, with a 3.22-point margin over the strongest baseline EATA. For ResNet50, POEM achieves higher accuracy on 9 out of 15 corruption types, with the advantage less pronounced due to the lower baseline accuracy of ResNet. In the continual-shift setting (Figure 2), POEM adapts faster than all baselines, reaching competitive accuracy with only 100 examples per corruption segment where baselines require more data. In the in-distribution experiment, POEM exhibits a 'no-harm' effect—minimal ECE change and near-zero parameter deviation—while TENT, EATA, SAR, and COTTA all increase ECE and unnecessarily modify model parameters. COTTA underperforms notably (55.34% ViT vs. 67.36% POEM), partly because its learning rate was tuned for the continual setting rather than single-shift.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant test-time adaptation paradigm of entropy minimization (TENT, EATA, SAR, COTTA) by proposing entropy *matching*—driving the distribution of test-time entropies to match the source distribution rather than simply minimizing entropy. The detection mechanism is borrowed from the testing-by-betting literature (Shafer & Vovk; Vovk's protected probabilistic regression), where martingales serve as sequential hypothesis tests, and the betting parameter is learned online via SF-OGD (Orabona & Pál). A key conceptual contribution is establishing that the pseudo-entropy values extracted from the betting function correspond to the optimal transport map between target and source entropy distributions under the Wasserstein metric, linking the statistical testing framework to distributional matching in a principled way.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 'no-harm' property—minimal parameter change and preserved calibration when no shift is present—addresses a well-known failure mode of entropy minimization methods that can degrade in-distribution performance or cause model collapse. The martingale-based detection provides a statistically grounded, anytime-valid signal for *when* to adapt, which is absent in competing methods. Limitations include the need for holdout source data to estimate the source CDF, sensitivity to the self-training learning rate, and potential failure under very aggressive shifts. Open questions include whether the framework extends to alternative self-supervised losses (Tsallis entropy, pseudo-label cross-entropy), whether data augmentations can be integrated, and whether the approach generalizes to non-i.i.d. or temporally correlated test streams.
