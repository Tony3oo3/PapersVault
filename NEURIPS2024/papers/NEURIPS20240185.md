## Problem

Video-language understanding lags behind image-language understanding despite abundant web video-text data, primarily because (1) videos introduce spatial-temporal complexity requiring large-scale training, and (2) web video-text supervision (subtitles, short descriptions) is noisy and insufficient for building temporal reasoning. Existing video-text pre-training models struggle on long-form benchmarks like EgoSchema, raising the question of whether LLMs can be aligned with the video modality without any real video training data.

## Input / Output

During pre-alignment, the model consumes sequences of 5–15 CLIP text features (one per textual frame, each derived by fusing a frame caption and object captions through the CLIP text encoder) rather than pixel data. At inference, it takes 10 uniformly sampled video frames encoded by the frozen CLIP-ViT-L image encoder, with each frame feature projected into the CLIP text feature space via a 2M-entry support memory before being fed to the LLM. Outputs include free-form video descriptions, open-ended QA answers, or a selected letter from multiple-choice options, all generated autoregressively by the LLM.

## Method

TOPA introduces a two-stage text-only pre-alignment that never touches real video pixels. First, Gemini Pro 1.0 generates 721K 'Tideos'—sequences of 5–15 textual frames each containing a scene caption and per-object captions—along with dense video descriptions and 3.5M QA pairs, forming the TextVid dataset. Second, each Tideo frame is encoded by the CLIP text encoder (frame caption and object captions are fused via average pooling), projected into the LLM embedding space through a linear layer, and processed by a frozen Llama2/Llama3 backbone augmented with a Llama-adapter; the model is trained with a unified autoregressive LM objective on three tasks (Tideo summarization, Tideo QA, and multi-choice Tideo QA at a 1:1:2 ratio). The key insight is that CLIP's contrastive pre-training aligns its image and text encoders in a shared space, so continuous CLIP text features from Tideos are structurally analogous to continuous CLIP image features from real videos. At zero-shot inference, a DeCap-style support-memory projection (Eq. 2) maps each real-video CLIP image feature into the text-feature manifold, closing the modality gap without any additional training. The pre-aligned model can then optionally be fine-tuned on downstream video QA datasets (NExT-QA, STAR, TVQA) where it directly consumes CLIP image features, yielding consistent gains over baselines without text-only pre-alignment.

## Results

On the challenging EgoSchema long-form video QA benchmark (zero-shot, no real video training), TOPA-Llama2-13B achieves 51.0% Top-1 accuracy, surpassing all previous video-text pre-training methods (best: MC-ViT-L at 44.4%) and image-MLLM adaptation approaches (best: IG-VLM-13B at 47.0%). On TVQA, TOPA-Llama2-13B reaches 50.2% zero-shot and 69.0% after finetuning; on NExT-QA it reaches 62.1% zero-shot and 75.1% finetuned. For zero-shot video captioning, TOPA-Llama2-13B achieves 33.4 CIDEr on MSR-VTT and 32.0 on VATEX, outperforming VideoCoCa (27.1/22.8) which was pre-trained on 144M video-text pairs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TOPA-Llama2-13B (51.0%) decisively outperforms all web video-text pre-training baselines: FrozenBiLM (26.9%), InternVideo (32.1%), LongViViT (33.3%), MC-ViT-L (44.4%), and InternVideo2s3-6B (41.1%), despite training on zero real videos. It also surpasses image-MLLM adaptation methods SEViLA (22.7%), MVU (37.6%), and IG-VLM-13B (47.0%), as well as LLM-based video agents using the same-scale LLM: Vamos-Llama2-13B (36.7%) and LangRepo-Mixtral-12B (41.2%). However, TOPA-Llama2-13B trails GPT-4-based agents (VideoAgent 54.1%, LifelongMemory 62.4%) and MoReVQA (51.7%), which leverage closed-source LLMs. On NExT-QA and STAR, TOPA underperforms SEViLA and IG-VLM (e.g., 62.1% vs. 63.6% on NExT-QA avg; 43.0% vs. 51.7% on STAR avg) because those methods inherit fine-grained visual understanding from LLaVA-based VLMs, whereas TOPA's CLIP text-feature training limits spatial detail. After supervised finetuning, TOPA-Llama2-13B reaches 75.1% on NExT-QA and 69.0% on TVQA, consistently outperforming its own no-pretraining baseline by +4.2% and +2.4% respectively, confirming that text-only pre-alignment provides a meaningful initialization benefit over direct finetuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

TOPA departs from the standard video-LLM alignment paradigm (pre-train on large-scale web video-text pairs, as in FrozenBiLM, InternVideo, LongViViT) by eliminating the need for any real video data during pre-alignment. It builds on two key prior ideas: CLIP's contrastively-aligned image-text feature space (Radford et al., 2021), which makes CLIP text features of sequential textual frames a valid proxy for CLIP image features of real video frames, and DeCap's (Li et al., 2023) support-memory projection to bridge the CLIP modality gap at inference time. The novel contribution is the conceptual insight that an LLM can be pre-aligned with the video modality using only LLM-generated 'textual videos' (Tideos), combined with the TextVid dataset as a scalable, high-quality supervision source that avoids the noisy web video-text pairs that limit prior approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TOPA offers a practical efficiency win: video-LLM pre-alignment can be done in 1–2 days on 4 A100 GPUs without collecting or processing any real video data, lowering the barrier to building video understanding models. The 51.0% EgoSchema result (zero-shot, no real video training) challenges the working assumption that large-scale real video pre-training is necessary for long-form video understanding. Key limitations remain: the CLIP modality gap degrades performance on fine-grained visual tasks (object localization, spatial relationships), and the approach is limited to keyframe-level understanding rather than high-fps temporal dynamics. Whether video instruction tuning on real data can close the fine-grained gap, and whether the text-only pre-alignment strategy generalizes to other modalities or longer contexts, are open questions.
