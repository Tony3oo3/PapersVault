## Problem

Denoising diffusion models are powerful generative tools but have seen little application to discriminative tasks where feature quality is critical. Naively appending a recursive denoising module to a backbone's final output improves discriminability but introduces significant extra inference latency, and the feature-space mismatch between successive backbone layers contradicts the same-space requirement of standard denoising layers.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241264/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images and operates on the intermediate feature maps produced at each layer of a pre-trained vision backbone (ViT, ResNet, Swin, V-Mamba). The output is a set of denoised feature vectors at the same spatial and channel dimensions as the original backbone features, ready for any downstream head (classification MLP, ReID metric, detection/segmentation decoder). Because the denoising parameters are fused into the existing embedding weights, the inference-time I/O is identical to the unmodified backbone with zero additional latency.

## Method

DenoiseRep reinterprets each of the N cascaded embedding layers in a backbone as one step of a recursive denoising process, so that progressive feature extraction from low-level to high-level is unified with step-by-step feature denoising. During training, the backbone is frozen and N independently initialized denoising layers are trained with a DDPM-style noise-prediction loss, where each layer i uses a fixed diffusion step t=i rather than a dynamic t∈[1,T]. At inference, a reparameterization fuses each denoising layer's weight W_D into the corresponding embedding weight via W′ = W − C₁(t)·W·W_D (Eq. 7), collapsing the dual-branch structure into a single branch with identical parameter count and zero extra FLOPs. The paper proves exact equivalence between the fused and unfused computations, and extends the derivation to multi-step denoising (Eq. 10) for stronger noise removal. The entire procedure is label-free (unsupervised) but can optionally incorporate a supervised loss with a trade-off parameter λ when labels are available.

## Results

On the primary benchmark (person ReID), TransReID-SSL (ViT-base) + DenoiseRep achieves 75.35% mAP on MSMT17, 93.26% mAP on Market-1501, 84.31% mAP on DukeMTMC-reID, and 88.08% mAP on CUHK-03, consistently outperforming the unmodified baseline by 0.35–0.61 mAP points. The method is computation-free: inference time remains 0.34 s (identical to baseline) versus 0.39 s (+15%) for a non-fused denoising variant. Generalization to other tasks includes +0.31% top-1 accuracy on ImageNet-1k (SwinV2-T: 82.13% vs 81.82%), +1.5 AP on COCO (Mask-RCNN/Swin-T: 44.3% vs 42.8%), and +1.2 B-IoU on ADE20K (FCN/ResNet-50: 29.9% vs 28.7%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DenoiseRep consistently improves every backbone it is applied to: TransReID-SSL (ViT-base) gains +0.35 mAP on MSMT17 (75.35 vs 75.00), +0.16 on Market-1501 (93.26 vs 93.10), +0.21 on DukeMTMC (84.31 vs 84.10), and +0.28 on CUHK-03 (88.08 vs 87.80). CLIP-REID + DenoiseRep surpasses the original CLIP-REID by +0.50 mAP on MSMT17 (76.30 vs 75.80) and +0.60 on DukeMTMC (83.70 vs 83.10), and also edges out TransReID-SSL (ViT-base) on MSMT17. On the smaller ViT-small backbone, improvements are larger (+1.03 mAP on MSMT17, +0.85 on Market-1501), supporting the authors' claim that weaker backbones benefit more from feature denoising. Across all 24 compared baselines in Table 3, DenoiseRep-augmented methods never underperform their unmodified counterparts, and the fused variant incurs zero additional inference latency (0.34 s vs 0.34 s baseline). On auxiliary tasks, DenoiseRep improves Mask-RCNN (Swin-T) on COCO by +1.5 AP (44.3% vs 42.8%), FCN (ResNet-50) on ADE20K by +1.2 B-IoU (29.9% vs 28.7%), and SwinV2-T on ImageNet-1k by +0.31% top-1 accuracy (82.13% vs 81.82%), confirming cross-task generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the DDPM denoising framework [21] from generative modeling to discriminative representation learning, departing from prior diffusion-based discriminative methods (DiffusionDet, DiffSeg) that were tightly coupled to specific task data structures (noise boxes, noise masks). The key conceptual move is reinterpreting each embedding layer in a pre-trained backbone as a denoising layer, so that the cascaded feature extraction pipeline is simultaneously a recursive denoising process. The parameter fusion technique—algebraically merging denoising layer weights into the corresponding embedding layer weights (Eq. 7)—is the architectural novelty that makes the approach computation-free at inference, distinguishing it from the straightforward 'append a denoising module' baseline shown in Fig. 1(b).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The computation-free, label-free plug-in property makes DenoiseRep attractive as a drop-in improvement for existing backbones across ReID, classification, detection, and segmentation without retraining the backbone or adding inference latency. However, the acknowledged limitation that parameter fusion restricts each layer to only one or two denoising steps caps the achievable denoising intensity, and the observed gains (≈0.5–1% mAP on ReID, ≈0.1–0.3% top-1 on ImageNet) are modest. An open question is whether multi-step denoising (Eq. 10) can be fused without inflating parameter count, and whether the approach yields larger gains on noisier or more challenging domains where feature quality is more degraded.
