## Problem

Vision-language foundation models like CLIP struggle to generalize when target domain data (e.g., satellite imagery in EuroSAT) differs substantially from web-scale pre-training data, especially under low-shot regimes. Although a wide range of expert models exist with complementary knowledge across modalities, tasks, and architectures, they remain 'isolated agents' with heterogeneous structures, and a unified mechanism to integrate their knowledge into CLIP-like models has not been explored.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243122/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the method takes a standard RGB image and a text prompt (class name template) and outputs a class label via a prediction score vector computed as cosine similarity between the prompted visual and textual features. During training, the pipeline additionally consumes intermediate features from 11 frozen heterogeneous agents (vision encoders, LLM-generated descriptions, T2I cross-attention maps, and I2T projection features), but all external agents are unloaded after distillation, so the inference graph is identical to vanilla CLIP with no model ensemble overhead.

## Method

TransAgent introduces a unified multi-source knowledge distillation framework with three modality-specific collaboration modules, each using a Mixture-of-Agents (MoA) gating mechanism to adaptively weight contributions from heterogeneous agents. Vision Agent Collaboration (VAC) extracts layer-wise intermediate features from four vision agents (MAE, DINO, SAM, ViTDet), gates them via an MLP over concatenated channel dimensions, and distills into CLIP's vision branch via L1 loss on prompted visual features. Language Agent Collaboration (LAC) queries GPT-3 and Vicuna for rich class descriptions, encodes them with BERT, gates the resulting text features, and distills into CLIP's text branch via L1 loss on the [EOS] token. Multi-modal Agent Collaboration (MAC) extracts per-class prediction score vectors from T2I agents (Stable Diffusion, PixArt-α) via cross-attention maps aggregated with LogSumExp pooling, and from I2T agents (BLIP-2, Shikra) via cosine similarity of projected visual and LLM textual features, then aligns CLIP's learned score vectors to the gated multi-modal scores via KL divergence. Only a small set of learnable visual and textual prompts (4 tokens each) are fine-tuned while all pre-trained models remain frozen, and the total loss combines cross-entropy with the three distillation terms weighted by hyperparameters λ₁=1, λ₂=25, λ₃=1.

## Results

On the base-to-novel generalization benchmark across 11 visual recognition datasets, TransAgent achieves an average harmonic mean (HM) of 81.27%, outperforming the previous best method PromptSRC (79.97%) by 1.30 points. The most dramatic gain is on EuroSAT (satellite imagery with large domain shift), where TransAgent reaches 89.89% HM versus CoOp's 68.69%—a ~21% absolute improvement. Under few-shot classification (1–16 shots), TransAgent consistently leads all baselines across all datasets, and in inference it requires only 2 min 35 s on ImageNet versus 16 min 14 s for CaFo while achieving higher accuracy (73.50% vs. 72.46%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TransAgent outperforms all seven compared baselines on the average HM across 11 datasets. Against the zero-shot CLIP baseline (71.70% HM), TransAgent gains +9.57 points, confirming that multi-source agent distillation substantially improves generalization. Compared to CoOp (71.66% HM), the improvement is ~10 points on average and reaches ~21 points on EuroSAT (89.89 vs. 68.69), the dataset with the largest domain shift. TransAgent also surpasses CoCoOp (75.83), MaPLe (78.55), RPO (77.78), and PromptSRC (79.97) by 5.44, 2.72, 3.49, and 1.30 HM points respectively, with the largest margins on fine-grained and domain-shifted datasets (FGVCAircraft, DTD, EuroSAT). Against CaFo, which uses a cascade of foundation models at inference, TransAgent achieves higher accuracy (73.50% vs. 72.46% on ImageNet) while reducing inference time from 16 min 14 s to 2 min 35 s, because all external agents are unloaded after distillation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the prompt-learning paradigm for CLIP adaptation (CoOp, CoCoOp, MaPLe, PromptSRC) by injecting external knowledge from 11 heterogeneous pre-trained models via multi-source distillation. It departs from the closest prior work, CaFo, which cascades foundation models at inference time, by instead distilling agent knowledge into CLIP's learnable prompts so all external models can be unloaded after training. The key conceptual departure from standard multi-teacher distillation is the use of latent feature vectors as a unified knowledge representation across architecturally diverse agents (ViT, UNet, DiT, LLM, MLLM), combined with a Mixture-of-Agents gating mechanism for adaptive per-domain weighting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a practical path to improving VLM generalization under domain shift without incurring inference-time model ensemble costs, which is critical for deployment. The ~20% gain on EuroSAT (satellite imagery) demonstrates that multi-source knowledge can address large distribution gaps that single-model prompt learning cannot. However, the authors acknowledge that distillation may introduce irrelevant domain knowledge and that few-shot training may not fully exploit data-hungry agents, leaving open whether directed or label-free knowledge transfer could further improve the approach.
