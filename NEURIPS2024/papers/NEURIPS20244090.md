## Problem

State-of-the-art molecular conformer generation methods either rely on large transformer-based diffusion models (MCF, 13M–242M parameters) that lack geometric inductive biases, or depend on cheminformatics tools like RDKiT to produce initial structures before diffusing over torsion angles. This paper asks whether a compact, equivariant flow-matching model operating directly on all-atom Cartesian coordinates can match or exceed these approaches in precision without complex internal geometry calculations or massive parameter counts.

## Input / Output

The model takes a 2D molecular graph as input, featurized with atomic numbers, bond features, and eight RDKit-derived atom attributes (chirality tag, degree, formal charge, hydrogen count, radical electrons, hybridization, aromaticity, ring membership). Edges are constructed from both a 10 Å radius graph and the molecular bond graph. The output is a set of 3D atomic coordinates (N×3) representing a low-energy conformer; during evaluation, 2K conformers are generated per molecule with K ground-truth references. A post-hoc chirality correction step flips the z-axis of any generated conformation whose oriented volume disagrees with the RDKit stereochemistry tag.

## Method

ET-Flow learns a time-dependent vector field v_θ(t, x) via conditional flow matching, transporting samples from a harmonic prior (where bonded atoms are placed at their equilibrium distance) to the target distribution of low-energy conformations. The architecture is a 20-layer equivariant transformer (8.3M parameters) based on TorchMD-NET with multi-head attention over invariant and equivariant features, followed by gated equivariant output blocks that produce the vector field. Three key design choices distinguish it from prior work: (1) a harmonic prior instead of a Gaussian, encoding the inductive bias that bonded atoms should be close; (2) Kabsch rotational alignment between prior and data samples before interpolation, shortening probability paths at negligible cost; and (3) stochastic sampling (noise injection) applied only during the final 20% of Euler integration steps to improve diversity without sacrificing accuracy. The model is O(3)-equivariant by default, with chirality handled via a simple post-hoc oriented-volume check rather than architectural modification, though an SO(3) variant using a cross-product term is also provided.

## Results

On GEOM-DRUGS (δ=0.75Å), ET-Flow (8.3M params) achieves a Precision Coverage of 74.38% and Precision AMR of 0.541Å, substantially outperforming all baselines including the largest MCF-L (242M params) which scores 66.8% and 0.618Å respectively. On Recall, ET-Flow matches MCF-S (79.53% vs 79.4% Coverage) while remaining competitive with the much larger MCF-B (84.0%) and MCF-L (84.7%). On GEOM-QM9 (δ=0.5Å), ET-Flow achieves the best Recall Coverage (96.47%) and Precision AMR (0.098Å) among all methods. For ensemble chemical properties, ET-Flow achieves the lowest median errors across energy (0.18 kcal/mol), dipole moment (0.18 D), HOMO-LUMO gap (0.35 kcal/mol), and minimum energy (0.02 kcal/mol).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ET-Flow decisively outperforms all four baseline families on precision metrics. Against MCF (Wang et al., 2024), the most recent state-of-the-art, ET-Flow achieves 74.38% Precision Coverage vs. 66.8% for MCF-L (242M params) and 57.4% for MCF-S (13M params), while using fewer parameters than even MCF-S. Against Torsional Diffusion (Jing et al., 2022), ET-Flow improves Precision Coverage by ~19 points (74.38% vs. 55.20%) and Precision AMR by 0.24Å (0.541 vs. 0.778). Against GeoMol (Ganea et al., 2021) and GeoDiff (Xu et al., 2022), the gains are even larger, with ET-Flow roughly doubling Precision Coverage over GeoMol (74.38% vs. 43.00%) and tripling it over GeoDiff (74.38% vs. 24.90%). On Recall, ET-Flow is competitive with MCF-S and Torsional Diffusion but trails the larger MCF-B and MCF-L by ~4–5 points in Coverage, which the authors attribute to the smaller model size. On ensemble chemical properties (Table 3), ET-Flow achieves the lowest median error across all four properties (E, μ, Δε, E_min), outperforming both Torsional Diffusion and MCF.

## Relation to Prior Work

ET-Flow sits at the intersection of three research threads: flow matching as a generative framework (Lipman et al., 2022; Albergo et al., 2023), equivariant transformer architectures for atomistic systems (TorchMD-NET, Thölke and De Fabritiis, 2022), and harmonic priors for molecular generation (Jing et al., 2023; Stark et al., 2023). It departs from the dominant diffusion-based paradigm (GeoDiff, Torsional Diffusion, MCF) by replacing SDE-based denoising with ODE-based vector field learning, and from prior equivariant conformer generators (which used EGNN or Tensor Field Networks) by adopting an equivariant transformer backbone. The Kabsch-alignment step for path straightening follows recent work on optimal transport in flow matching (Klein et al., 2024; Jing et al., 2024), while the stochastic sampling variant is adapted from Karras et al. (2022).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical takeaway is that a compact 8.3M-parameter equivariant model with flow matching and a harmonic prior can match or exceed the precision of much larger transformer-based models (MCF up to 242M parameters) while requiring only 5 inference steps to remain competitive, making high-throughput conformer generation substantially cheaper. Open questions remain around recall/diversity (where MCF-L still leads at high thresholds), the reliance on a post-hoc chirality correction rather than native SO(3) equivariance, and generalization to very large molecules (GEOM-XL), where ET-Flow trails MCF-B and MCF-L. The authors identify SDE-based sampling, model scaling, and native SO(3) equivariance as natural next steps.
