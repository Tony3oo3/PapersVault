## Problem

Existing 3D point cloud detectors follow a single-dataset training-and-testing paradigm, unable to generalize across domains due to large scale disparities (point cloud ranges differ by 10–20× between indoor and outdoor scenes) and conflicting label spaces. Multi-domain joint training is hindered by two forms of interference: data-level (differences in sparsity, object size, and range) and category-level (inconsistent category definitions across datasets). No prior 3D detector supports both indoor and outdoor point clouds simultaneously with a single set of parameters.

## Input / Output

The model takes raw 3D point clouds from heterogeneous sources (RGB-D cameras, reconstructed scans, 32/64-beam LiDAR) and voxelizes them at domain-appropriate resolutions (0.01 m indoor, 0.05 m outdoor), padding attributes to a unified 6-dim channel. It outputs oriented 3D bounding boxes with semantic category labels via an anchor-free center-point representation, predicting classification, regression, centerness, and 3D IoU directly from sparse voxel features without any sparse-to-dense conversion or pruning layers.

## Method

OneDet3D employs a fully sparse architecture (3D sparse-conv ResNet-50 + FPN backbone, anchor-free center-point head) that avoids fixed-size dense feature maps, making it inherently compatible with point clouds of vastly different scales. To combat data-level interference, it introduces domain-aware partitioning: scatter partitioning assigns domain-specific scaling/shifting parameters in normalization layers, and context partitioning learns domain-specific global context (indoor only), both routed by a lightweight domain classifier (sparse conv + GAP + softmax). To combat category-level interference, it uses language-guided classification where frozen CLIP text embeddings serve as class-specific fully-connected weights, multiplied with a shared 3D sparse-conv class-agnostic (foreground/background) branch to maintain gradient flow. Training uses soft focal loss with IoU_BEV as the soft target, 3D IoU loss for regression, and dataset-aware sampling to balance scale disparities across domains.

## Results

On the four jointly-trained datasets, OneDet3D (multi-dataset training) achieves 65.0 AP25 on SUN RGB-D, 70.9 AP25 on ScanNet, 92.8/84.2/82.3 APe/APm/APh on KITTI, and 81.0 AP on nuScenes, surpassing most single-dataset-trained detectors with only one set of parameters. In the multi-dataset training comparison (Table 3), OneDet3D dramatically outperforms all other methods: 65.0 vs. 8.3 (VoxelNeXt) on SUN RGB-D, 84.2 vs. 75.2 (Uni3D) on KITTI, and 80.9 vs. 76.7 (Uni3D) on nuScenes. Cross-domain evaluation on unseen S3DIS yields 53.5 AP25 (vs. 44.8 for FCAF3D trained on ScanNet), and on Waymo achieves 41.1 AP3D (vs. 9.6 for PV-RCNN/UVTR trained on nuScenes).

## Baselines & Comparisons

OneDet3D (multi-dataset training) outperforms all single-dataset baselines across all four evaluation datasets with a single parameter set: it beats FCAF3D by 1.2% AP25 on SUN RGB-D (65.0 vs. 63.8), matches or slightly exceeds PV-RCNN on KITTI (92.8 vs. 92.6 APe), and surpasses VoxelNeXt and UVTR on nuScenes (81.0 vs. 80.0/80.6 AP). In the critical multi-dataset training comparison (Table 3), OneDet3D achieves 65.0/70.9/84.2/80.9 AP on SUN/Scan/KIT/nuS, while the next-best Uni3D reaches only 9.7/5.6/75.2/76.7, and VoxelNeXt, FSDv2, and SAFDNet collapse to single-digit AP on indoor datasets. For cross-domain generalization, OneDet3D trained on all four datasets achieves 53.5 AP25 on unseen S3DIS (vs. 44.8 for FCAF3D trained on ScanNet) and 41.1 AP3D on unseen Waymo (vs. 9.6 for PV-RCNN/UVTR trained on nuScenes), demonstrating that multi-domain joint training substantially improves transfer to new domains. The method also outperforms open-vocabulary baselines: on SUN RGB-D it achieves 12.59 AP_novel vs. 6.65 for CoDA, and on ScanNet 15.52 vs. 6.02.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the fully sparse, anchor-free 3D detection architecture (in the VoxelNeXt/FCAF3D lineage) to the multi-domain joint training setting, which prior 3D detectors have not supported. It builds on CLIP's frozen text embeddings for a language-guided classification head and on Uni3DETR's soft focal loss with IoU targets and its observation that global context matters primarily in indoor scenes. The key departure from prior multi-dataset work (Uni3D, Swin3D++) is that those methods handle only indoor or only outdoor datasets, whereas OneDet3D is the first to jointly train across both with a single parameter set, using domain-aware partitioning in normalization and context layers to prevent data-level interference.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work addresses a fundamental limitation of 3D detection — the single-dataset training-and-testing paradigm — and demonstrates that a universal 3D detector spanning indoor and outdoor scenes is feasible, positioning 3D detection closer to the universality already achieved in 2D. The domain-aware partitioning and language-guided classification techniques are modular and could be adopted by other sparse 3D architectures. Limitations include the requirement for fully-annotated point clouds (no weakly- or self-supervised learning), and the approach has only been validated on six datasets; whether it scales to more diverse sensor modalities or 3D tasks (segmentation, tracking) remains open. The paper also notes potential negative social impacts from misuse of multi-source point cloud data.
