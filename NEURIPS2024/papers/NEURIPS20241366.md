## Problem

Existing self-supervised heterogeneous graph learning (SHGL) methods suffer from two key limitations: noisy inter-class connections in their message-passing graphs degrade representation quality, and cluster-level information produced by the implicit clustering process is never explicitly leveraged. Additionally, no prior work has formally connected SHGL to spectral clustering, leaving the theoretical foundations of these methods unclear.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241366/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a heterogeneous graph G = (V, E, X, T, R) with node features X ∈ R^{n×f}, node types T, and edge types R. It produces concatenated node representations [Z; Z̃] for downstream tasks (node classification, node clustering), an orthogonal cluster assignment matrix Y ∈ R^{n×c}, and a sparse rank-constrained affinity matrix S with exactly c connected components. The affinity matrix is computed only over k-nearest neighbors to maintain sparsity, and the cluster count c must be specified a priori.

## Method

SCHOOL first proves that optimizing any meta-path-based or adaptive-graph-based SHGL method is equivalent to spectral clustering with regularization under an orthogonality assumption (Theorem 2.2), and that this corresponds to a RatioCut into d partitions where d (representation dimension) far exceeds the number of classes c. To fix this, the method learns an adaptive affinity matrix S with a rank constraint (rank(L_S) = n−c) via a closed-form solution derived from KKT conditions, ensuring exactly c connected components and eliminating inter-class noise. To avoid the cubic cost of eigendecomposition, a projection head plus QR-based orthogonal layer replaces it, and a spectral loss L_sp fits the resulting cluster assignment Y to the eigenvectors of L_S. Dual consistency constraints are then applied: a node-level loss L_nc (Frobenius distance + log-sum-exp regularization) captures invariant information between projected node and heterogeneous representations, while a cluster-level loss L_cc aligns each node's projected representation with its cluster centroid to reduce intra-cluster variance. The overall objective J = L_sp + μL_nc + δL_cc is optimized end-to-end, and theoretical analysis (Theorem 2.6) shows the resulting representations achieve a lower model-complexity bound and higher generalization bound than prior SHGL with node-level constraints only.

## Results

On node classification across four heterogeneous graph datasets, SCHOOL achieves Macro-F1 of 92.7 (ACM), 93.0 (Yelp), 94.0 (DBLP), and 77.5 (Aminer), outperforming the best prior SHGL method HERO by an average of 1.1%. On node clustering, SCHOOL improves by an average of 3.1% over the best prior method HGMAE. On homogeneous graph datasets (Photo, Computers), SCHOOL also outperforms the best self-supervised baseline LRD on nearly all metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SCHOOL consistently outperforms all 22 baselines across all four heterogeneous graph datasets on both Macro-F1 and Micro-F1. The strongest prior SHGL method, HERO (ICLR 2024), is beaten by SCHOOL by 0.5–2.4 points per dataset (e.g., 77.5 vs. 75.1 Macro-F1 on Aminer, 94.0 vs. 93.8 on DBLP). Among meta-path-based SHGL methods, CPIM and HGCML are the closest competitors but trail SCHOOL by 1.3–2.4 points on Aminer and 0.5–1.1 points on DBLP. Semi-supervised methods (HAN, HGT) and traditional unsupervised methods (DeepWalk, Mp2vec) are substantially outperformed, with gaps exceeding 10 points on Aminer. On homogeneous graphs (Photo, Computers), SCHOOL surpasses the best self-supervised baseline LRD (NeurIPS 2024) and all other homogeneous graph methods including GCA, GRACE, and DSSL, confirming the method's generality beyond heterogeneous settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

SCHOOL builds directly on the adaptive-graph-based SHGL framework of HERO [30], extending it with a rank constraint on the learned affinity matrix to enforce exactly c connected components (matching the number of classes), and on SpectralNet [42]'s orthogonal layer to replace costly eigendecomposition with a learnable projection. The affinity matrix learning with closed-form solution follows the adaptive neighbor clustering lineage of CAN [34]. Conceptually, the paper's main departure is a unified theoretical reframing: it proves that all prior meta-path-based and adaptive-graph-based SHGL methods are equivalent to spectral clustering with regularization under orthogonality, and further connects them to the RatioCut graph-cut algorithm, revealing that prior methods partition representations into d (dimension) rather than c (number of classes) clusters.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The theoretical bridge between SHGL and spectral clustering/RatioCut provides a principled lens for diagnosing why prior methods underperform (noisy inter-class connections, wrong number of partitions) and motivates the rank constraint as a targeted fix. The dual consistency constraints (node-level + cluster-level) represent a natural but previously unexploited way to leverage the clustering structure that SHGL implicitly produces. However, the method assumes node features are available, leaving heterogeneous graphs with missing features unaddressed, and the empirical gains over the strongest baseline (HERO) are modest (~1.1% on classification), suggesting the theoretical insights may have broader applicability than the specific implementation demonstrates.
