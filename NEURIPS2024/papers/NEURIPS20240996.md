## Problem

While adversarial training is widely used for robustness, its fundamental statistical properties—particularly whether the resulting estimator achieves minimax optimality—had not been established in the high-dimensional sparse linear regression setting. Prior analyses were either asymptotic, relied on Gaussian design assumptions, or did not incorporate sparsity structure. This paper fills that gap by delivering the first non-asymptotic minimax-rate guarantee for the adversarial training estimator and extending the analysis to group-structured perturbations.

## Input / Output

The method takes a fixed, column-normalized design matrix X ∈ ℝ^{n×p} (with p > n), a response vector Y = Xβ* + ε with i.i.d. Gaussian noise, and a perturbation magnitude δ of order 1/√n. For group adversarial training, the p-dimensional input is partitioned into L non-overlapping groups with associated positive weights ω = (ω₁,…,ω_L), and the perturbation is constrained by the weighted (2,∞)-norm. The output is the estimated parameter vector β̂ (or β̃ for the group variant) together with a high-probability upper bound on the prediction error ‖X(β̂−β*)‖₂²/n.

## Method

The key insight is that the ℓ∞-adversarial training problem admits a convex dual formulation whose objective decomposes into a standard squared-loss term plus an implicit ℓ₁-regularization term (δ‖β‖₁·(2/n)Σ|Xᵢᵀβ−Yᵢ| + δ²‖β‖₁²), linking adversarial training to LASSO-type regularization. Under the restricted eigenvalue condition RE(s,3) and the condition 2‖Xᵀε‖∞/‖ε‖₁ ≤ δ, the prediction error is bounded by 3δ²s·max{9/γ²·(‖ε‖₁/n)², 164‖β*‖₂²}, yielding the minimax-optimal rate s log p / n up to constants. For group adversarial training under (2,∞)-perturbation, the dual introduces a group ℓ₂,₁-regularization term, and under the group restricted eigenvalue condition GRE(g,3), the error bound becomes (|G_J| + g log L)/n, which strictly improves over the classic rate when the true parameter exhibits group sparsity (i.e., |G_J|/s ≪ log p and g ≪ s). Numerical experiments using CVXPY on synthetic Gaussian linear models confirm the 1/n convergence slope and the empirical superiority of group adversarial training.

## Results

The main theoretical result (Corollary 2.6) establishes that the prediction error of the ℓ∞-adversarial-trained linear regression estimator is bounded by 192·(s log p / n)·R² with high probability, achieving the minimax-optimal rate s log p / n up to a constant factor. For group adversarial training under (2,∞)-perturbation (Corollary 3.7), the prediction error is bounded by 432·(|G_J| + g log L)/n·R², which is strictly better than the classic rate when |G_J|/s ≪ log p and g ≪ s. Numerical experiments on synthetic 500- and 600-dimensional models confirm the 1/n convergence rate (slope ≈ −1 on log-log plots) and show that group adversarial training yields lower prediction error and tighter coefficient shrinkage than classic adversarial training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is between classic ℓ∞-adversarial training and group (2,∞)-adversarial training. Theoretically, group adversarial training achieves a prediction error rate of (|G_J| + g log L)/n versus s log p / n for classic adversarial training, yielding a strict improvement when the ground-truth parameter has a good group structure (i.e., most nonzero components are captured in few groups). Empirically, in both Model 1 (500-dim, 8 nonzeros) and Model 2 (600-dim, 6 nonzeros with dummy-variable groups), group adversarial training produces lower prediction error curves and coefficient estimates closer to ground truth across all sample sizes tested (n = 50 to 550). The paper also positions its result against LASSO and related sparse estimators [3, 4, 11, 20], showing that adversarial training under ℓ∞-perturbation matches the minimax-optimal prediction error rate previously established only for explicit regularization methods, making it the first proof of minimax optimality for an adversarial training estimator.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the adversarial training analysis of Ribeiro et al. [21] (who established the dual formulation and a 1/√n rate) by incorporating sparsity and the restricted eigenvalue condition to achieve the faster s·log(p)/n minimax rate. The group adversarial training formulation is built on the Wasserstein distributionally robust optimization frameworks of Blanchet & Kang [5] and Chen & Paschalidis [7], while the proof techniques directly extend those used for LASSO, square-root LASSO, and group LASSO [3, 4, 11, 17]. Unlike prior asymptotic analyses [28] or Gaussian-design analyses [30], this work provides a non-asymptotic, design-agnostic (via RE condition) treatment under ℓ∞ perturbation, and is the first to establish minimax optimality for the adversarial training estimator.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The minimax optimality result elevates adversarial training from a purely robustness-oriented procedure to one with provable statistical efficiency guarantees, suggesting that the ℓ∞ perturbation's implicit ℓ1 regularization is not merely a side effect but a mechanism that recovers the optimal sparse estimation rate. The group extension shows that incorporating known group structure into the perturbation can strictly improve the prediction error bound, offering a principled way to exploit domain knowledge. Limitations include the restriction to linear regression, prediction error (rather than parameter estimation error), and a fixed design matrix; generalization to generalized linear models, random designs, and variable-selection consistency remain open.
