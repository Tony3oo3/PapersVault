## Problem

Next-token prediction models support variable-length generation and causal conditioning but cannot perform multi-step guidance and diverge on continuous data (e.g., video) past the training horizon. Full-sequence diffusion models enable guidance and stable continuous generation but are restricted to fixed-length, non-causal architectures, precluding variable-length sampling and causal uncertainty modeling. No existing framework unifies the flexibility of autoregressive models with the guidance and stability of diffusion models.

## Input / Output

The model consumes sequences of tokens—video frames (raw pixels), state-action-reward tuples for decision-making, or multivariate time series values—where each token is independently noised at a randomly sampled level during training. At sampling, a user-defined M×T scheduling matrix K specifies the noise level of every token at every denoising step, allowing the model to produce variable-length sequences (from a single next token to thousands of frames) with per-token uncertainty profiles. The output is a clean sequence of the same modality as the input, optionally steered by a guidance cost function applied to a lookahead window.

## Method

Diffusion Forcing trains a causal RNN (GRU recurrent state + U-Net denoiser) to predict the noise on each token given its own independently sampled noise level k_t and the latent state z_{t-1} summarizing all past tokens, using the standard ε-prediction diffusion loss (Eq. 3.1). The key insight is that noising is a form of partial masking: by training on all combinations of per-token noise levels, the model learns to denoise any mixture of clean and noisy tokens, unifying Bayesian filtering (k_t=0 posterior update) and prior dynamics (k_t=K) in a single architecture. At sampling, a 2D scheduling matrix K controls the denoising trajectory, enabling novel capabilities: zig-zag schedules that keep the far future more uncertain than the near future, Monte Carlo Guidance that averages guidance gradients over multiple stochastic future rollouts to maximize expected reward, and stable long-horizon autoregressive rollout by feeding slightly noisy (k>0) tokens back into the latent state instead of fully clean ones. The paper proves (Theorem 3.1) that the training objective optimizes a reweighted ELBO on the expected log-likelihood over all noise-level sequences, and that a fully expressive network can simultaneously optimize the likelihood of all subsequences.

## Results

On the D4RL maze planning benchmark (6 environments), Diffusion Forcing with Monte Carlo Guidance (MCG) achieves the highest average reward: 141.7 (single-task) and 146.2 (multi-task), outperforming Diffuser (119.5 / 129.4) and all offline RL baselines (IQL: 47.0 / 16.9; MPPI: 16.2 / 21.5). On the real-robot fruit-swapping task, DF achieves 80% success rate while Diffusion Policy fails entirely; under camera occlusion, DF retains 76% success versus 48% for a next-frame diffusion baseline. For video prediction on Minecraft and DMLab, DF stably rolls out sequences of 1000+ frames past the training horizon, whereas teacher-forcing and full-sequence diffusion baselines diverge within a few frames.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Diffuser [36] (full-sequence diffusion planner), DF wins on all 6 maze environments with MCG (e.g., 159.0 vs 123.0 on Maze2D-Large; 146.2 vs 129.4 multi-task average). Critically, when Diffuser's generated actions are executed directly (rather than via a hand-crafted PD controller), its performance collapses to 8.7 (single-task) and 20.6 (multi-task), revealing a causal inconsistency between generated states and actions that DF avoids by design. Against offline RL methods (IQL, CQL, MPPI), DF dominates by a wide margin (141.7 vs 47.0 for IQL on single-task average). On the robot fruit-swapping task, DF (80% success) outperforms Diffusion Policy [10], which fails due to lack of memory, and retains 76% success under camera occlusion versus 48% for a next-frame diffusion baseline. On time-series forecasting, DF is reported as competitive with prior diffusion [49] and transformer-based [50] methods, though specific numbers are deferred to the appendix.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate paradigms for sequence generative modeling: autoregressive next-token prediction (teacher forcing, as in language models) and full-sequence diffusion (as in Diffuser [36] and video diffusion [31]). The key departure is assigning independent, per-token noise levels rather than a uniform noise level across all tokens (full-sequence diffusion) or a single next-token prediction (autoregressive). The closest prior work is AR-Diffusion [65], which uses a causal architecture but imposes linearly dependent noise levels along the time axis; Diffusion Forcing instead samples noise levels independently per token, enabling arbitrary denoising schedules. The theoretical analysis draws on Bayesian filtering (HMMs) to frame the latent state dynamics, and the ELBO proof connects the training objective to variational inference over all subsequence likelihoods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper introduces a structurally new capability—Monte Carlo Guidance (MCG)—that is impossible for full-sequence diffusion models because causality plus variable per-token noise allows future-token gradients to guide past-token generation while respecting the causal structure. This has direct implications for planning and decision-making, where maximizing expected future reward (rather than a single trajectory's reward) is the natural objective. The video stabilization result (stable rollout past training horizon where autoregressive baselines diverge) addresses a well-known practical limitation. Key open questions include whether the approach scales to transformer architectures and internet-scale data, whether the RNN-based implementation is sufficient for high-resolution video, and how the method performs on more complex, high-dimensional decision-making tasks beyond 2D mazes.
