## Problem

Sharpness-aware minimization (SAM) improves generalization but requires two sequential gradient computations per update—one for the perturbation direction and one for the update—doubling training time relative to base optimizers. The central question is whether these two gradients can be computed in parallel without sacrificing convergence, since the perturbation gradient at the clean point is needed to define the perturbed point where the second gradient is evaluated. Naive alternatives (random perturbation, reusing the previous perturbed-point gradient) fail to converge even on simple convex quadratics, making principled parallelization non-trivial.

## Input / Output

SAMPa takes the current model parameters x_t, a mini-batch B_t, and hyperparameters (step size η_t, neighborhood radius ρ, interpolation ratio λ ∈ [0,1]) and produces updated parameters x_{t+1}. The key operational detail is that it requires two GPUs: one computes ∇f(ẋ_t, B_t) on the current batch while the other computes ∇f(y_{t+1}, B_{t+1}) on the next batch, after which the two gradients are combined as G_t = (1−λ)g̃_t + λg_{t+1}. The same-batch constraint that SAM enforces (both gradients on B_t) is relaxed here because the auxiliary gradient g_{t+1} is only used for the next iteration's perturbation, not the current update direction.

## Method

SAMPa breaks SAM's sequential dependency by replacing the perturbation gradient ∇f(x_t) with ∇f(y_t) from an auxiliary sequence defined by y_{t+1} = x_t − η_t∇f(y_t), which is itself an optimistic gradient descent step. This makes ∇f(ẋ_t) and ∇f(y_{t+1}) computable in parallel since neither depends on the other within the same iteration. The final update x_{t+1} = x_t − η_t[(1−λ)∇f(ẋ_t) + λ∇f(y_{t+1})] is a convex combination of the SAMPa correction and the OptGD correction, with λ=0 recovering pure SAMPa and λ=1 recovering the (failing) OptSAM. Convergence with fixed ρ is established via a novel Lyapunov potential V_t = f(x_t) + ½(1−η_tL)‖∇f(x_t)−∇f(y_t)‖² whose descent inequality telescopes, showing the auxiliary gradient discrepancy is controlled and vanishes under the initialization y_0 = x_0.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, SAMPa-0.2 achieves 95.99% average test accuracy across five architectures (DenseNet-121, ResNet-56, VGG19-BN, WRN-28-2, WRN-28-10), outperforming SAM (95.81%) and SGD (95.52%). On CIFAR-100, SAMPa-0.2 reaches 79.22% vs. SAM's 78.90%. On ImageNet-1K, SAMPa-0.2 achieves 77.44% Top-1 accuracy vs. SAM's 77.25%, and on GLUE, SAMPa-0.1 scores 78.0 vs. SAM's 76.6. In terms of efficiency, SAMPa-0.2 requires 10.94 s/epoch vs. SAM's 18.81 s/epoch on CIFAR-10 ResNet-56, a near 2× speedup with only 7.5% inter-GPU communication overhead.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SAMPa-0.2 consistently outperforms SAM across all evaluated tasks: +0.18% on CIFAR-10, +0.32% on CIFAR-100, +0.19% Top-1 on ImageNet-1K, and +1.4 on GLUE average score, while halving the per-iteration computational cost. Among efficient SAM variants on CIFAR-10 ResNet-56, SAMPa-0.2 (94.62%) beats LookSAM (91.42%), AE-SAM (94.46%), SAF (93.89%), MESA (94.23%), and ESAM (94.21%), while also achieving near-minimal wall-clock time (10.94 s/epoch vs. SAM's 18.81 s). SAF is marginally faster (10.09 s/epoch) but less accurate. When integrated with other SAM variants, SAMPa improves every one: mSAM 94.28→94.71, ASAM 94.84→94.95, SAM-ON 94.44→94.51, VaSSO 94.80→94.97, and BiSAM 94.49→95.13. On noisy-label CIFAR-10 (80% noise), SAMPa-0.2 achieves 49.92% vs. SAM's 48.01%, demonstrating superior robustness. The gradient penalization method (a structural analogue) matches SAMPa on clean data (94.57 vs. 94.62) but degrades under noise (48.26 vs. 49.92), highlighting SAMPa's advantage from using different batches for the two parallel gradients.

## Relation to Prior Work

SAMPa directly extends SAM (Foret et al., 2021) by introducing an auxiliary sequence y_t that breaks the sequential dependency between the two gradient computations, enabling full parallelization. It further integrates optimistic gradient descent (Popov, 1980; Daskalakis et al., 2017) through the SAMPa-λ formulation, which takes a convex combination of the parallelized SAM update and the optimistic GD step. The paper explicitly contrasts its approach with prior efficiency-oriented SAM variants (LookSAM, AE-SAM, SAF, MESA, ESAM) that reduce cost by skipping or approximating the ascent step, whereas SAMPa preserves both gradient computations but executes them concurrently. The convergence analysis departs from prior SAM theory by establishing a novel Lyapunov function that guarantees convergence with a fixed perturbation size, a setting where naive alternatives like RandSAM and OptSAM provably fail.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SAMPa offers a practical 2× speedup of SAM with consistent generalization improvements across vision, language, and noisy-label tasks, making SAM-based training more accessible without sacrificing (and in fact improving) quality. The convergence guarantee with fixed ρ via the Lyapunov function provides a principled design criterion for future SAM variants, which the authors argue is a promising methodology. Key limitations include the requirement for two GPUs (same total compute as SAM), the convergence proof being established only for λ=0, and the 7.5% communication overhead in current implementations. Open questions include extending the theoretical analysis to general λ, reducing per-GPU memory, and whether the parallelization strategy generalizes to other min-max or multi-step optimization schemes.
