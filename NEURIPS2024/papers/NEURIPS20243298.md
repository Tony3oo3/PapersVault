## Problem

Bilevel optimization has been extensively studied in the unconstrained Euclidean setting, but existing frameworks do not handle the case where both upper- and lower-level variables are constrained to Riemannian manifolds. Prior manifold-based bilevel works either reformulate the problem into a single-level KKT system (which scales poorly) or lack general-purpose algorithms with convergence guarantees. This paper fills the gap by providing a unified framework for Riemannian bilevel optimization with multiple hypergradient estimators, full convergence and complexity analyses, and extensions to stochastic and retraction-based settings.

## Input / Output

The method takes two Riemannian manifolds M_x and M_y (e.g., Stiefel, SPD, or doubly stochastic manifolds) together with jointly smooth bifunctions f and g on their product, where g is geodesic strongly convex in y. It produces an optimized upper-level point on M_x, an approximate lower-level solution on M_y, and a Riemannian hypergradient estimate at each iteration. In practice, inputs are matrices (SPD covariance matrices, orthonormal weight matrices, coupling matrices) or RGB images (for meta-learning and domain adaptation experiments), and the algorithm operates entirely in tangent spaces using exponential maps or retractions for updates.

## Method

The core contribution is a Riemannian hypergradient descent (RHGD) algorithm that derives the hypergradient of F(x) = f(x, y*(x)) via the implicit function theorem on manifolds, yielding the closed-form expression involving the Hessian inverse of g, cross-derivatives, and the Riemannian gradient of f. Four computationally efficient hypergradient estimation strategies are proposed—Hessian inverse, tangent-space conjugate gradient, truncated Neumann series, and automatic differentiation through the unrolled lower-level iterations—each with explicit error bounds that depend on the condition number κ_l and curvature constant ζ. The algorithm alternates S inner Riemannian gradient steps on y (solving the lower-level problem approximately) with a single outer hypergradient step on x, using exponential maps or general retractions for manifold updates. Convergence to an ε-stationary point is established with complexity O(κ_l^3 ε^{-1}) for the Hessian-inverse variant, matching Euclidean rates up to curvature-dependent factors, and the framework extends naturally to the stochastic setting with batch-size requirements of Θ(κ_l^2 ε^{-1}).

## Results

On the Caltech-Office unsupervised domain adaptation benchmark (12 source→target tasks), the proposed bilevel approach achieves the best or tied-best 1-NN classification accuracy on 11 out of 12 tasks, with notable margins such as 80.25% on A→D (vs. 75.80% for OT-SKH and 47.77% for OT-EMD) and 98.73% on W→C (vs. 87.26% and 96.82%). On MiniImageNet 5-way 5-shot meta learning, RHGD with Stiefel-constrained base parameters converges faster in test accuracy than the extrinsic PHGD baseline. On the ETH-80 deep hyper-representation task, the proposed method achieves strong validation accuracy with the 2-layer SPD network, with HINV and CG hypergradient estimators giving the best convergence.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two single-level optimal transport baselines on Caltech-Office: OT-EMD (Earth Mover's Distance) and OT-SKH (Sinkhorn distance). The proposed bilevel approach outperforms OT-SKH on 10 of 12 tasks (e.g., +4.45 pts on A→D, +3.86 pts on W→A, +11.47 pts on W→C) and ties on A→W (69.83%), while dominating OT-EMD by large margins across all tasks (average gap >20 pts). In the theoretical complexity comparison (Table 1), the proposed RHGD-HINV matches the Euclidean HGD-CG [40] gradient complexity for f (O(κ_l³ε⁻¹)) but incurs an additional curvature factor ζ in the g-gradient complexity (Õ(κ_l⁵ζε⁻¹) vs. Õ(κ_l⁴ε⁻¹)), reflecting the cost of geodesic strong convexity analysis. For stochastic bilevel optimization, RSHGD-HINV matches the G_f and JV_g complexities of stocBio [40, 11] while the G_g complexity carries the same curvature overhead. In the meta learning experiment, RHGD with intrinsic Riemannian updates converges faster than the extrinsic PHGD (projected gradient) baseline on MiniImageNet, demonstrating the benefit of native manifold optimization over projection-based approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the well-studied Euclidean bilevel optimization framework (Ji et al., ICML 2021; Ghadimi & Wang, 2018) to the Riemannian manifold setting, where both upper- and lower-level variables are constrained to manifolds. The hypergradient estimation strategies (conjugate gradient, truncated Neumann series, automatic differentiation, Hessian inverse) are direct analogues of their Euclidean counterparts, but the analysis must account for curvature effects via parallel transport, the exponential map, and a curvature constant ζ. The derivation of the Riemannian hypergradient relies on the implicit function theorem on manifolds (Han et al., TMLR 2023), and the framework subsumes Riemannian min-max optimization as a special case (g = −f). A contemporary work (Li & Ma, 2024) also proposes gradient-based algorithms for Riemannian bilevel optimization, but this paper provides broader hypergradient estimator analysis, retraction-based updates, and empirical validation across multiple ML applications.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work provides the first comprehensive framework with convergence guarantees for bilevel optimization where both levels live on Riemannian manifolds, filling a gap between the rich Euclidean bilevel optimization literature and the growing body of Riemannian optimization work. The practical demonstrations on SPD hyper-representation, Stiefel-constrained meta-learning, and doubly-stochastic-manifold domain adaptation show that the Riemannian formulation yields tangible improvements over projected Euclidean baselines. Open questions include relaxing the geodesic strong convexity assumption on the lower level, reducing the batch-size requirements in the stochastic setting, and further optimizing the dependence on the curvature constant ζ, which inflates complexity relative to the Euclidean case.
