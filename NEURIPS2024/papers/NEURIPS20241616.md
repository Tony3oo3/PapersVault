## Problem

Methodological research on learning from noisy human annotations requires realistic datasets, yet publicly available ones are limited to simple classification tasks, provide only hard class labels, and lack annotator metadata. The dopanim dataset fills this gap by offering a challenging doppelganger animal classification task where annotators have varying domain expertise, combined with soft likelihood annotations and rich annotator metadata for evaluating multi-annotator learning approaches.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241616/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The dataset comprises ~15,750 JPEG animal images from iNaturalist spanning 15 classes in four doppelganger groups (insects, hares/rabbits, squirrels, big cats), with ~10,500 images annotated by 20 humans yielding 52,000+ annotations. Each annotation consists of unnormalized likelihood scores across all 15 classes (set via sliders in LabelStudio), an annotation time, and a timestamp, rather than a single hard label. The ground truth labels come from iNaturalist's research-grade validation, and the dataset is split into balanced training (~10,484), validation (750), and test (4,500) sets with no observer overlap.

## Method

The paper's core contribution is the dataset itself plus a systematic benchmark of one-stage multi-annotator learning approaches across seven dataset variants that vary the number of annotations per instance (1, 2, variable, full) and annotator performance (random vs. worst-case selection). The benchmark uses a pre-trained DINOv2 ViT-S/14 backbone fine-tuned with an MLP head (128 hidden neurons, batch norm, dropout 0.5) under RAdam with cosine annealing over 50 epochs, evaluating eight approaches (cl, trace-reg, conal, union-net, geo-reg-w/f, madl, crowd-ar, annot-mix) against four baselines (ground truth, majority vote, soft majority vote, selection frequency). Three additional use cases explore leveraging human-estimated likelihoods as annotation weights, integrating annotator metadata (questionnaire responses, tutorial completion, annotation consistency) into training, and analyzing annotation time costs under uncertainty-sampling active learning.

## Results

On the full dopanim variant (5 annotations per instance, ~67% overall annotation accuracy), the best one-stage multi-annotator approach annot-mix achieves 82.2% test accuracy, outperforming the majority-vote baseline (77.8%) by 4.4 points and the ground-truth upper bound (87.1%) by 4.9 points. On the most challenging worst-1 variant (single worst annotation per instance, 22.4% accuracy), annot-mix reaches 34.7% accuracy versus 28.1% for majority vote, a 6.6-point gain. Across all seven variants, annot-mix and geo-reg-f consistently rank first and second in accuracy and Brier score, while majority-vote baselines remain competitive on top-label calibration error (TCE).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper benchmarks seven one-stage multi-annotator learning approaches against four lower baselines (majority vote, soft majority vote, selection-frequency, and a crowd-layer) and one upper baseline (ground-truth labels) across seven dopanim variants. annot-mix [18] (the authors' prior work) achieves the best accuracy on 6 of 7 variants, with geo-reg-f [20] a close second; on the full variant, annot-mix (82.2%) beats geo-reg-f (81.4%) by 0.8 points and the best lower baseline sf-base (78.9%) by 3.3 points. Instance-dependent approaches (madl [17], crowd-ar [4], annot-mix [18]) do not consistently outperform class-dependent ones (geo-reg-w/f [20], conal [7], union-net [61]), likely due to increased training complexity. Notably, the simple majority-vote baseline (mv-base) achieves competitive or even superior top-label calibration error (TCE) compared to most one-stage approaches, suggesting room for improvement in calibration. On the extreme worst-1 variant, annot-mix gains 6.6 points over majority vote (34.7% vs. 28.1%), while crowd-layer [1] and geo-reg-w [20] also show meaningful improvements over the naive baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground in the noisy-annotation dataset space by combining three underexplored dimensions: a genuinely difficult classification task (doppelganger animals requiring domain expertise), soft probabilistic labels capturing annotator uncertainty, and rich annotator metadata (questionnaires, tutorial history, annotation times). It departs from prior noisy-label datasets like cifar10n, cifar10h, and animal10n, which typically provide only hard labels from large crowds of anonymous workers with no metadata and relatively high overall accuracy (82–95%). The benchmark component positions the dataset within the established multi-annotator learning literature (learning from crowds), evaluating one-stage end-to-end approaches such as trace-reg, conal, geo-reg, madl, and annot-mix under a unified protocol with a DINOv2 ViT-S/14 backbone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dataset addresses a real scarcity in the field: publicly available benchmarks with realistic, low-accuracy human noise (~67%) that also support research beyond hard-label learning. The doppelganger framing ensures annotators face genuine ambiguity, making the noise structure more informative than the near-perfect annotations in many existing datasets. Key open questions include whether the findings scale to larger datasets and more classes, whether probabilistic labels are cost-effective given that uncertain images take longer to annotate (Fig. 10), and how these approaches perform on subjective tasks where no objective ground truth exists. The small scale (~15,750 images, 20 annotators) and single-backbone evaluation limit generalizability, but the released codebase enables straightforward extension.
