## Problem

Existing positional encoding schemes for Transformers are largely empirically motivated or tailored to specific tasks, lacking a unifying theoretical framework that connects the encoding to the underlying data structure. This makes theoretical evaluation difficult and prevents a principled generalization of encodings across different domain topologies such as sequences, trees, and grids.

## Input / Output

The method consumes structured inputs—token sequences, κ-ary branching trees, or 2D image grids (pixel patches)—and produces task-specific outputs (translated text, transformed sequences/trees, or class labels). The positional encoding is not a fixed additive signal but is derived from the algebraic specification of the input structure itself: sequences yield a single-generator subgroup, trees yield a κ-generator subgroup, and grids yield a direct sum of sequence interpretations, so the encoding adapts to the domain's topology rather than being imposed a priori.

## Method

The paper formalizes positions as elements of a free group (or direct sum of free groups) defined by the inductive structure of the domain, then constructs a homomorphic interpretation mapping each position to an orthogonal matrix in O(d). For sequences, this reduces to a single orthogonal generator W whose powers W^p encode relative offsets, which the paper proves equivalent to RoPE under a change of basis. For κ-ary trees, κ distinct orthogonal generators {W_1, …, W_κ} encode branching choices, and for 2D grids the encoding is a block-diagonal direct sum of two sequence encodings acting on independent subspaces. In practice, the orthogonal generators are parameterized by matrix-exponentiating skew-symmetric bases, and relative attention scores are computed by rotating queries and keys with their respective position matrices before the dot product, preserving the standard Transformer memory footprint while making the encoding trainable end-to-end.

## Results

On WMT14 EN→DE machine translation, APE (with RoPE-style initialization) achieves 23.93 BLEU, matching Rotary (tuned) at 23.92 and slightly trailing Rotary (frozen) at 24.03, while substantially outperforming Sinusoidal (14.57), Absolute (22.09), and Relative (23.15). On synthetic sequence tasks, APE achieves near-perfect perplexity (1.01) on REVERSE and REPEAT, compared to 3.92 and 1.85 for Sinusoidal. On tree transduction, APE (tree) achieves 1.01 PPL on ROTATE and 1.00 on C3, dramatically outperforming Tree-SQ (1.87 and 2.29 respectively) and reaching 2.24 PPL on TREE-OPS versus 19.18 for Tree-SQ. On CIFAR-10 image recognition, APE (grid) reaches 94.67% top-1 accuracy at epoch ≤300, surpassing Sinusoidal 2D (92.79%) and Absolute (92.68%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Sinusoidal encodings (Vaswani et al., 2017), APE wins decisively across all tasks: 23.93 vs. 14.57 BLEU on WMT14, and 1.01 vs. 3.92 PPL on sequence reversal. Against Absolute encodings (Gehring et al., 2017), APE gains +1.84 BLEU on WMT14 and reduces REVERSE perplexity from 4.62 to 1.01. Against Relative encodings (Shaw et al., 2018), APE improves WMT14 BLEU from 23.15 to 23.93 and cuts REVERSE PPL from 4.08 to 1.01. Against Rotary/RoPE (Su et al., 2023), APE is essentially tied on WMT14 (23.93 vs. 24.03 frozen, 23.92 tuned) and matches or slightly beats on synthetic tasks (1.01 vs. 1.09 PPL on REVERSE vs. frozen RoPE). Against Tree-SQ (Shiv & Quirk, 2019), APE (tree) achieves a dramatic 8.6× perplexity reduction on TREE-OPS (2.24 vs. 19.18) and a 2.5× reduction on ROTATE (1.05 vs. 2.60), confirming that algebraic tree encodings capture structural relationships that sequential or ad-hoc tree encodings miss. On CIFAR-10, APE (grid) outperforms Sinusoidal 2D (Wang & Liu, 2019) by +1.88% top-1 accuracy (94.67 vs. 92.79) and Absolute by +1.99% (94.67 vs. 92.68) at epoch ≤300.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper formalizes and extends the implicit group-theoretic structure underlying rotary positional encodings (RoPE, Su et al. 2023) and the earlier homomorphism-based framing hinted at by Wang et al. (2020). Where prior positional encoding schemes (Sinusoidal, Absolute, Relative, Tree-SQ) were largely empirically motivated or task-specific, APE provides a unified algebraic framework: positions are interpreted as elements of subgroups of the orthogonal group O(d), with the structure (sequence, k-ary tree, grid) determining the group's generators. The key departure is extending from the single-generator cyclic case (sequences, where APE reduces to a trainable RoPE) to multi-generator non-abelian groups (trees) and direct sums (grids), structures for which no principled encoding existed prior to this work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a principled, structure-aware alternative to ad hoc positional encodings that generalizes across sequences, trees, and grids without task-specific hyperparameter search, and the formal equivalence with RoPE provides a theoretical explanation for RoPE's empirical success. However, the experimental validation is limited to small synthetic tasks, one NMT benchmark, and CIFAR-10, leaving open whether the theoretical advantages persist at scale or on more complex downstream tasks. The authors acknowledge that the approach is restricted to simple inductive groups and that tree-structured encodings incur linear-time overhead, both of which constrain near-term practical adoption. A natural open question is whether the framework extends to more complex algebraic structures (e.g., non-abelian composite groups, graphs) and whether the orthogonal parameterization's optimization advantages over trigonometric ones hold in large-scale pretraining.
