## Problem

Existing control theory for linear dynamical systems assumes static state transition matrices, yet in real applications (stock markets, autonomous vehicles) the deployed policy itself alters the system dynamics. While performative prediction handles decision-dependent data distributions, it ignores temporal correlations between consecutive data samples. This paper introduces performative control for linear systems, where the policy-dependent dynamics create a sequence of correlated, policy-dependent state distributions over a finite horizon, and asks when a performatively stable control (PSC) solution exists and how to compute it.

## Input / Output

The method takes a linear system x_{t+1} = A_t x_t + B u_t + w_t where the transition matrix A_t = A + Δ_t has a policy-dependent perturbation Δ_t ~ D_t(M) governed by the disturbance-action policy matrix M (a convex set with bounded Frobenius norm ‖M‖_F ≤ M). The per-stage cost c_t(x,u) is μ-strongly convex and ζ-smooth. The output is the PSC fixed point M^PS satisfying M^PS = Φ(M^PS), where Φ maps a policy to the argmin of the expected total cost under the distributions induced by that policy, along with the associated control actions u_t = -Kx_t + M[w]_{t-1}^H.

## Method

The paper formulates the performative optimal control problem using disturbance-action policies (linear maps of past disturbances) and establishes that the expected total cost C_T(M; M') is strongly convex in M via the stochastic nature of the policy-dependent dynamics, even when M is not full column-rank. The key sufficient condition for PSC existence is a weighted sum ∑_t ε_t ∑_{i>t} ν_i < μ̃, where ε_t are Wasserstein-1 sensitivities of D_t(M) and ν_i capture state-norm growth, revealing a sensitivity propagation-and-aggregation structure. For almost surely stable dynamics, only the total sensitivity sum matters; for unstable dynamics, sensitivities must decay exponentially backward in time. The RSGD algorithm iterates over the full horizon to compute a stochastic gradient of the total cost, then performs a projected gradient step on M, achieving O(1/N) mean-square convergence to M^PS under the same sufficient condition.

## Results

The paper's main theoretical contribution is Theorem 1, which establishes that the proposed RSGD algorithm achieves an expected squared distance bound of O(1/N) to the performative stable control (PSC) solution under the sufficient condition (16) on the weighted sum of distributional sensitivities. Numerical validation on a stock market risk minimization problem (L=10 stocks, T=60 trading days, N=1000 iterations) confirms the O(1/N) convergence rate for the PS error ‖M_N − M^PS‖²_F in the almost surely strongly stable case across all sensitivity orderings (ascending, descending, random). In the almost surely unstable case, only the ascending sensitivity pattern (small early sensitivities) converges, while descending and random patterns diverge, matching the theoretical prediction of Proposition 2 requiring temporally backwards decaying sensitivities.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not compare against competing methods; it introduces a new theoretical framework (performative control) and validates its own theoretical predictions numerically. The numerical experiments serve as self-consistency checks: the observed convergence/divergence behavior of the RSGD iterates under different sensitivity orderings (ascending, descending, random) and system stability regimes (stable, unstable, general) aligns precisely with the sufficient conditions derived in Propositions 1–2 and Theorem 1. The closest related works are performative prediction [21], stateful performative prediction [3], and performative reinforcement learning [16, 24], but none of these address the continuous-state, policy-dependent linear dynamics with temporal correlations considered here, so no direct empirical comparison is made.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously separate research threads: performative prediction (where deployed models shift data distributions) and nonstochastic control (where disturbance-action policies handle general convex costs over linear systems). It extends the stateful performative prediction framework of Brown et al. [3] by making the distribution transition map itself policy-dependent, and adapts the disturbance-action policy structure from Hazan et al. [11] to a setting where the state transition matrix is no longer static. Unlike performative reinforcement learning [16, 24], which handles only tabular MDPs with finite state-action spaces, this work addresses continuous-state linear systems with infinite admissible policies, introducing temporal correlations in the decision-dependent data that are absent from all prior performative prediction formulations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes the first theoretical foundation for performative control, providing existence/uniqueness conditions and a convergent algorithm for a problem that arises naturally in finance, autonomous vehicles, and public policy where deployed decisions reshape the environment. A key structural insight is that system stability fundamentally changes the sensitivity requirements: stable systems tolerate arbitrary sensitivity ordering, while unstable systems demand temporally backwards-decaying sensitivities—a constraint with practical implications for when and how aggressively a controller can be deployed. Limitations include the restriction to linear dynamics, a single performative component (the transition matrix), and quadratic-like cost structures; extending to nonlinear systems, multiple performative components, or general non-convex costs remains open.
