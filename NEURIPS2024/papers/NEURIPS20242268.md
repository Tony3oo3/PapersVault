## Problem

While the co-learning dynamics of representations and value networks in DRL have received growing attention, the parameter-level evolution of policy networks during training has remained largely unexplored. This paper asks whether the policy learning path exhibits exploitable low-dimensional structure that could be leveraged to improve learning efficiency and stability in standard DRL algorithms.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a sliding-window FIFO buffer of historical policy parameter vectors (size 2000 for MuJoCo, 1000 for DMC) collected at fixed minibatch intervals, together with the current policy parameters. It stacks these vectors into a temporal parameter matrix of shape [window_size, ~10^5] and performs a full SVD on it. The output is a single set of modified policy parameters—trimmed to the top r_t SVD directions and boosted along the first two—that is loaded back into the agent's policy network, replacing the original parameters at sparse intervals (t_p ≥ 1000 steps).

## Method

The core tool is Temporal SVD applied to the policy learning path: stacking successive policy parameter vectors and decomposing the resulting matrix into U, Σ, V^T, where the columns of V^T define SVD directions in parameter space and the rows of U encode the temporal evolution along each direction. Empirical analysis reveals that singular value information is highly concentrated in the first few directions, that the first coordinate u_{*,1} evolves nearly monotonically with policy improvement, and that minor directions exhibit harmonic-wave-like oscillations (detours). PPTB exploits this structure in two steps: (1) Policy Path Trimming reconstructs the current policy using only the top r_t SVD directions (typically ≤ 64), zeroing out oscillatory minor-direction updates; and (2) Policy Path Boosting amplifies the first two coordinates by adding p_b·(u_{n,*} − u_{1,*}) to u_{n,*}, effectively extrapolating the monotonic improvement trend. The entire procedure is algorithm-agnostic, requiring only ~10 lines of code added to off-the-shelf DRL implementations (TD3, RAD, DoubleDQN), and the SVD computation adds less than 5% wall-clock overhead.

## Results

PPTB improves TD3's aggregate Score by 14.4% and AUC by 24.2% across four MuJoCo environments, with the largest gains in Walker2d (28.8% Score, 41.9% AUC) and Ant (22.8% Score, 34.0% AUC). For RAD on DMC, PPTB yields a 33.7% aggregate Score improvement at 100k timesteps (driven by an 86.9% gain in walker-walk) and a 54.8% AUC improvement at the same budget. PPTB also improves DoubleDQN on MinAtar, with Seaquest score increasing from 11.0 to 25.4 and SpaceInvader from 77.6 to 99.1.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against TD3, PPTB wins on all four MuJoCo environments for both Score and AUC, with the most dramatic gains in Walker2d (+28.8% Score, +41.9% AUC) and Ant (+22.8% Score, +34.0% AUC), while Hopper shows the smallest Score improvement (+0.77%) but still a meaningful AUC gain (+12.3%). Against RAD on DMC, PPTB dominates at the 100k timestep budget (aggregate Score +33.7%, AUC +54.8%), particularly in walker-walk where Score nearly doubles (210→349, +86.9%); at 500k timesteps the advantage narrows to +4.7% Score and +10.9% AUC as the base algorithm converges. Against DoubleDQN on MinAtar, PPTB improves all three tasks, with the largest relative gain in Seaquest (11.0→25.4, roughly 2.3×) and a solid improvement in SpaceInvader (77.6→99.1). Overall, PPTB consistently outperforms all three baselines across every environment and metric tested, with the largest relative improvements appearing in environments where the base algorithm struggles most (Walker2d, walker-walk, Seaquest).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of two research threads: (1) the growing body of work on DRL learning dynamics, particularly the co-learning dynamics between representations and value networks (Dabney et al. 2021, Kumar et al. 2021, Lyle et al. 2022, Nikishin et al. 2022) and related phenomena like Dormant Neurons (Sokar et al. 2023) and Policy Churn (Schaul et al. 2022), and (2) the observation that neural network training occurs in low-dimensional subspaces (Gur-Ari et al. 2018, Gressmann et al. 2020, Li et al. 2023). The key departure is applying Temporal SVD specifically to the *policy* parameter path (rather than value networks or generic SGD trajectories), revealing a clean structural split between monotonic major directions and oscillating minor directions. The proposed PPTB method repurposes this structure as a plug-in, drawing conceptual connections to network resetting for primacy bias (Nikishin et al. 2022) and momentum-based optimization, while being most closely related to the concurrent work on gradient subspaces in policy gradients (Schneider et al. 2024).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The empirical discovery that DRL policy paths concentrate in a very low-dimensional subspace with a sharp major/minor direction split provides a new diagnostic lens for understanding why DRL training is unstable and sample-inefficient. The practical impact is modest but real: a ~10-line modification yields consistent improvements (up to ~87% in some settings) across actor-critic and value-based algorithms without changing the underlying algorithm. However, the paper explicitly lacks theoretical justification for the observed phenomena, tests only two actor-critic algorithms (TD3, RAD) for the core analysis, uses fixed hyperparameters, and has not been evaluated in sparse-reward settings. Open questions include whether the low-dimensional structure persists for on-policy methods like PPO, whether adaptive (rather than fixed) trimming/boosting dimensions could further improve results, and whether the value-based results (DoubleDQN) generalize given the fundamentally different learning dynamics of TD bootstrapping versus policy gradients.
