## Problem

Aligning LLMs with human objectives typically requires expensive fine-tuning (RLHF, DPO, PPO) that is unstable and inflexible to new objectives, while existing test-time methods (prompting, guided decoding) leave the model unmodified and remain bounded by its original capabilities. Prior representation editing approaches apply a fixed perturbation vector throughout generation, ignoring the autoregressive, sequential nature of token production. This paper asks whether a principled, control-theoretic framework can dynamically steer LLM representations at each generation step to achieve alignment without any weight updates.

## Input / Output

At each autoregressive step, the method consumes the LLM's last-layer logits $o_t$ (a vector of dimension 4096 for the 7B/8B models tested) as input to a lightweight 2–3 layer value network, and produces a control signal vector $u_t$ of the same dimension that is added to the logits before the softmax sampling. The final output is the full aligned text response generated by the unmodified LLM under these per-step perturbations. The reward signal used to train the value function comes from a pre-trained reward model (e.g., LLaMA-7B-based on HH-RLHF, or UltraRM-13B on SHP) or from simple heuristics, and is only assigned at the EOS token, not on partial decodings.

## Method

RE-CONTROL formalizes an autoregressive LLM as a discrete-time stochastic dynamical system where the hidden state $s_t = \{h_t, o_t\}$ (key-value pairs plus logits) evolves via the transformer forward pass and the sampled token $y_t$ acts as the stochastic term. The key intervention is injecting a control signal $u_t$ into the state at every step, turning the uncontrolled system into a controlled one: $y_t \sim \text{Softmax}(W(o_t + u_t^o))$ and $h_{t+1}, o_{t+1} = f_{\text{LM}}(h_t + u_t^h, y_t)$. A value function $V_\phi$ is trained via one-step policy iteration on the zero-control policy, minimizing a Bellman-equation loss over sampled trajectories so that $V_\phi(s_t)$ predicts the expected terminal reward from state $s_t$. At test time, the control signal is obtained by $n$ steps of gradient ascent on $u_t$ with respect to $V_\phi(s_t + u_t)$, starting from $u_t = 0$; the small step size $\alpha$ and limited number of updates $n$ serve as implicit regularization that keeps the perturbation close to the original state, preventing reward hacking while preserving diversity and coherence. Because the value function is only a 2–3 layer MLP, each gradient step is extremely cheap, making the overall test-time overhead roughly 20× less than controlled decoding baselines.

## Results

On HH-RLHF with Vicuna-7B, RE-CONTROL achieves a GPT-4 win rate of 75.6% (80.3% with prompting), an average reward of 6.214, and inference time of 0.85 hours—outperforming all test-time baselines while being ~20× faster than controlled decoding. On Stanford SHP with Llama3-8B, RE-CONTROL reaches 71.0% win rate (77.0% with prompting) and average reward of −4.39, again leading all methods. The method also generalizes to the out-of-distribution HarmfulQA dataset, where RE-CONTROL + Prompting achieves the highest GPT-4 win rate on both Vicuna-7B and Falcon-7B.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RE-CONTROL outperforms static representation editing (Li et al., 2023) by 17.6% win rate on HH-RLHF (Vicuna-7B) and 24.6% on SHP (Llama3-8B), attributed to dynamic per-step control versus a fixed perturbation vector. Against controlled decoding (Khanov et al., 2024), RE-CONTROL achieves a higher win rate (75.6% vs. 72.3% on HH-RLHF) while running ~20× faster (0.85 h vs. 47.43 h), since CD requires repeated forward passes through the full reward model for each candidate token. Compared to CD prefix (Mudgal et al., 2023), RE-CONTROL wins on both win rate (75.6% vs. 74.6%) and average reward (6.214 vs. 6.105) on HH-RLHF, and the gap widens on SHP (71.0% vs. 64.0% for Llama3-8B). In the fine-tuning comparison (Figure 3), RE-CONTROL surpasses LoRA-based DPO (Rafailov et al., 2023) and PPO (Schulman et al., 2017) on all three metrics—diversity (0.824 vs. 0.807/0.815), coherence (0.579 vs. 0.567/0.559), and reward (6.214 vs. 6.048/6.089)—without any weight updates. Combining RE-CONTROL with prompting yields the largest gains, outperforming the strongest baseline (CD prefix + Prompting) by 7.6% win rate on HH-RLHF and 13.2% on SHP (Llama3-8B).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the representation engineering line of work (Zou et al. 2023; Li et al. 2023) by replacing the static, fixed-vector perturbation with a dynamic control signal derived from optimal control theory. The key conceptual departure is framing an autoregressive LLM as a discrete-time stochastic dynamical system and applying Bellman-equation-based value function training to the hidden state space, enabling gradient-based test-time optimization of the control signal. Unlike prior test-time alignment methods (prompting, controlled decoding) that operate on token probabilities or prompts, RE-CONTROL directly perturbs the representation space, and unlike fine-tuning methods (RLHF, DPO, PPO), it requires training only a 2–3 layer value network rather than updating the full model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RE-CONTROL offers a practical middle ground between expensive fine-tuning and limited test-time prompting: it matches LoRA-based DPO/PPO on alignment quality while training only a tiny value network, making rapid adaptation to new alignment objectives feasible without substantial compute. Open questions remain around multi-objective alignment (the paper uses a single reward model), whether more than one step of policy iteration would improve the value function, and whether perturbing attention key-value pairs rather than just the final-layer logits would yield further gains. The method's reliance on a pre-trained reward model and its sensitivity to the step-size/iteration-count hyperparameters (beyond which reward hacking degrades coherence to near zero) are practical limitations for deployment.
