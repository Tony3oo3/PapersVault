## Problem

Statistical models used to explain datasets—clustering, topic models, classification—learn high-dimensional parameters (cluster centers over neural embeddings, topic-word weight vectors, feature coefficients) that require significant human effort to interpret and often fail to capture coherent semantic categories. This paper asks whether model parameters can be made directly interpretable by representing them as natural language predicates, e.g., replacing an uninterpretable cluster center with the predicate 'discusses the U.S. Election.'
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242519/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a collection of text samples (or image captions) along with their neural embeddings (e.g., Instructor-XL), and uses two LLMs: one to compute binary denotations [φ](x) by checking whether predicate φ holds on sample x, and another to generate candidate predicates from sorted sample lists. The output is a set of K natural language predicates plus standard model parameters (one-hot cluster assignments, Brownian-motion weight trajectories, or logistic regression weight matrices), where the predicates themselves serve as the interpretable model parameters rather than post-hoc labels.

## Method

The paper introduces predicate-conditioned distributions p(x|φ⃗, w) ∝ exp(wᵀ[φ⃗](x)) as building blocks for clustering, time series, and classification models, where each predicate φ extracts a binary feature via LLM prompting. Since predicates are discrete strings and thus non-differentiable, the key optimization trick is a continuous relaxation: replace [φ_k](x) with the dot product φ̃_k · e_x between a unit-length continuous vector φ̃_k and the sample's normalized embedding e_x, making the loss differentiable and optimizable by gradient descent. After optimizing φ̃_k, a Discretize step prompts an LLM with samples sorted by their φ̃_k·e_x scores to generate candidate predicates, re-ranked by Pearson correlation with the continuous scores. The full algorithm alternates between optimizing weights (OptW), optimizing continuous predicates (OptRelaxedPhi), and discretizing, then iteratively refines by identifying the least useful predicate, re-optimizing only its continuous representation, and re-discretizing—making the procedure model-agnostic across all three statistical model families.

## Results

On the clustering benchmark across five datasets (AG-News, DBPedia, NYT, Bills, Wiki), the full method achieves an average F1/Surface of 0.64/0.57, outperforming the naive prompting baseline (0.27/0.45) and both ablations (No-Refine: 0.53/0.54; No-Relax: 0.56/0.54). On time series modeling, the method reaches 0.64/0.65 F1/Surface (time-averaged), and on multiclass classification it reaches 0.73/0.70. In Appendix D, the method's average F1 of 0.64 is close to K-means (0.65) while significantly outperforming LDA topic modeling (0.43), all while producing natural language explanations for each cluster.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GoalEx [53], a specialized integer-linear-programming method for explainable clustering, the proposed model-agnostic method matches or outperforms on 4 of 5 datasets (AG-News, NYT, Bills, Wiki) and loses only on DBPedia (0.68 vs. 0.75 F1), yielding a 0.02 average F1 improvement. Against LDA topic modeling, the method achieves a substantially higher average F1 (0.64 vs. 0.43) while producing directly interpretable natural language predicates rather than bag-of-words topic representations. Compared to K-means clustering (0.65 average F1), the method is within 0.01 F1 while additionally generating human-readable cluster descriptions. Both continuous relaxation and iterative refinement are validated as necessary: removing either component (No-Relax or No-Refine) degrades F1 by 0.08 and 0.11 respectively on average across all tasks, with statistically significant differences (p < 0.01 under paired t-tests).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies and extends two prior research threads: (1) Concept Bottleneck Models and GoalEx, which learn interpretable natural-language features for classification or clustering in isolation, and (2) LLM-prompting approaches that explain distributional differences or generate topic descriptions for specific tasks. The key departure is the introduction of predicate-conditioned distributions as a general building block, enabling a single model-agnostic optimization algorithm (continuous relaxation via embedding dot-products + iterative LLM discretization) to learn predicates across clustering, time series, and classification models. Unlike CBMs, which assume a fixed set of human-defined concepts and optimize for downstream accuracy, this work treats the predicates themselves as learnable parameters and evaluates recovery of ground-truth predicates.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a practical path to making statistical model parameters directly interpretable in natural language, applicable to both text and vision, and capable of explaining abstract concepts (e.g., math subareas, LLM capability differences) that n-gram or topic-model baselines produce only ambiguously. However, the method is currently slow due to heavy reliance on LLM API calls for denotation computation, produces sometimes redundant predicates, and lacks fine-grained control over predicate properties (e.g., ensuring subclusters are subsets of parents). Open questions include whether denotation computation can be distilled into a smaller local model, whether the approach scales to millions of samples, and whether the qualitative superiority over classical methods translates into measurable downstream utility in real-world deployments.
