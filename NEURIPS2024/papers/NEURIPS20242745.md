## Problem

Current egocentric video-language models rely on instance-level contrastive alignment between whole-video and whole-caption representations, which fails to capture fine-grained relationships between visual entities and linguistic elements (nouns for appearance, verbs for motion). This monolithic treatment also prevents interpretability: the model cannot explain which scene entities drive its predictions or provide visual grounding for text queries, unlike human compositional perception where distinct entities emerge from parts and interact contextually.

## Input / Output

The model consumes 4-frame video clips at 224×224 resolution (decomposed into T×K non-overlapping patches) and free-form text narrations processed by a 12-layer Transformer. Beyond the standard video and text embeddings, HENASY produces E dynamic scene entity tokens that evolve across frames, and per-frame saliency maps (obtained by composing similarity arrays from bootstrapping and entity grouping stages) that spatially localize each entity for visual grounding with both noun and verb-phrase queries.

## Method

HENASY is a dual-encoder architecture where the video branch composes a global TimeSFormer encoder with a hierarchical Local Entity Encoder and an Entity-Aware Decoder. The Local Entity Encoder operates in two stages: a bootstrapping stage of 6 cross-attention layers that aggregates patch features from corresponding global encoder layers into 64 initial group tokens, followed by an entity grouping stage of 3 DST blocks with 8 learnable group tokens that merge segments into E scene entities. The key mechanism is Temporal-Aware Grouping (TAG), which extends GroupViT's slot-based assignment to video by computing per-frame similarity matrices with Gumbel-Softmax and straight-through estimators, preserving the temporal dimension so entities can evolve freely across frames. Training employs four objectives: EgoNCE for video-narration alignment, Noun-Entity Contrastive Loss (NEC) using Hungarian matching between narration nouns and entity tokens, a novel Verb-Entities Contrastive Loss (VEC) that uses Llama-2-extracted verb phrases with a weighted many-to-one alignment strategy, and a projection loss that constrains predicted saliency maps to match detected bounding boxes via axis-projection Dice loss.

## Results

On zero-shot transfer, HENASY achieves 94.1% inter-video and 61.3% intra-video accuracy on EgoMCQ, 19.5% Top-1 and 38.2% Top-5 accuracy on EK100-CLS, and 35.9% Top-1 accuracy on EGTEA, all setting new state-of-the-art. As a feature extractor for temporal localization, HENASY reaches 11.5 R1@0.3 and 14.7 R5@0.5 on EgoNLQ, and 12.4 mAP on EgoMQ, outperforming all prior methods. On EK100-MIR retrieval, HENASY achieves 31.3 Avg mAP and 33.2 Avg nDCG, matching the best prior method (HelpingHands) while surpassing all others.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HENASY outperforms all five baselines across the majority of benchmarks. Against the closest competitor HelpingHands [4] (re-trained with the same TSF-B backbone for fairness), HENASY gains +0.9% inter-video and +2.5% intra-video on EgoMCQ, +3.1% Top-1 on EK100-CLS, and +0.6% Top-1 on EGTEA, while matching it exactly on EK100-MIR (31.3 vs 31.2 Avg mAP; 33.2 vs 33.2 Avg nDCG). Compared to LaViLa [5], HENASY improves EgoMCQ intra-video by +1.4%, EK100-CLS Top-1 by +3.1%, and EGTEA mean accuracy by +0.7%. Against EgoVLP [3] and EgoVLPv2 [6], the margins are larger (e.g., +3.5% EgoMCQ inter over EgoVLP; +4.6% EK100-MIR Avg nDCG over EgoVLPv2). In the feature-extraction setting (Table 2), HENASY surpasses HelpingHands by +0.3 R1@0.3 and +1.1 R5@0.3 on EgoNLQ, and by +0.8 mAP on EgoMQ, while also outperforming SlowFast [38] by a wide margin (e.g., 11.5 vs 5.5 R1@0.3 on EgoNLQ).

## Relation to Prior Work

HENASY extends the slot-based object discovery lineage (Slot Attention, GroupViT) from static images to dynamic video by introducing a Temporal-Aware Grouping (TAG) mechanism that preserves temporal coherence during token merging. It departs from standard egocentric VLMs (EgoVLP, LaViLa, HelpingHands) that rely solely on instance-level video-caption contrastive alignment, instead decomposing video into evolving scene entities and enforcing multi-grained alignment (video-narration, noun-entity, verb-entities). The bootstrapping stage, which couples the local entity encoder to early layers of a pre-trained TimeSFormer global encoder via cross-attention, is a novel architectural choice that leverages global context to initialize entity grouping without requiring external detectors or depth supervision.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

HENASY is the first egocentric VLM to provide strong interpretability through dynamic saliency maps that respond to both appearance (noun) and motion (verb) queries, which is critical for AR/virtual-assistant applications where users need to understand *why* a model made a decision. The 41% vs 22.7% mIoU gap over HelpingHands on visual grounding demonstrates that entity-level segmentation masks substantially outperform coarse bounding boxes for interpretability. However, the paper acknowledges it does not explicitly model interactions *between* entities (no dynamic scene graph), limits evaluation to ViT-based tasks, and does not address long-form video or generative settings — leaving open whether the compositional framework scales to more complex relational reasoning.
