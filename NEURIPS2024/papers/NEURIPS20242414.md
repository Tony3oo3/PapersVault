## Problem

Existing vision-language models are restricted to turn-based interaction, where the user must explicitly prompt the model at each step. Real-world situated tasks like live fitness coaching require the model to autonomously monitor a streaming video and proactively deliver timely corrective or affirmative feedback without any external prompting. No prior dataset or benchmark captures this asynchronous, interactive coaching scenario with fine-grained human actions, diverse mistakes, and temporally aligned corrective feedbacks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242414/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a live video stream in 64-frame windows (~4 seconds at 16 fps) with causal 3D convolutions, so it only sees past and present frames. Rather than producing a single text response per prompt, the model emits a sequence of action tokens: <next> requests the next frame window (staying silent), while <feedback> triggers generation of a short coaching utterance (average ~6–9 words). Feedbacks are temporally anchored to visually salient moments (mistake onset, correction, repetition counts), with an average silence period of ~5 seconds between successive feedbacks within an exercise segment.

## Method

STREAM-VLM couples a causal 3D CNN vision backbone (pre-trained on ImageNet, then fine-tuned on 460+ hours of QEVD-FIT-300K short clips for fine-grained fitness action recognition) with a LLaMA-2-7B language backbone via cross-attention adapters at multiple layers. Two special action tokens—<next> and <feedback>—are inserted into the LM's vocabulary, allowing the model to learn end-to-end when to observe versus when to speak, eliminating the need for fixed-interval prompting heuristics. Training proceeds in three stages: (1) 3D CNN pre-training on ImageNet followed by QEVD-FIT-300K; (2) alignment of the frozen vision backbone and LM on fitness questions and short-clip feedbacks, updating only the cross-attention adapter weights; (3) LoRA fine-tuning (dim=32) on long-range QEVD-FIT-COACH videos where feedback tokens are interleaved with <next> tokens at their ground-truth timestamps, simulating the interactive streaming setting. The 3D CNN and adapter weights remain frozen during stage 3, and training is limited to individual 30-second exercise segments.

## Results

On the QEVD-FIT-COACH benchmark, STREAM-VLM achieves the best performance across all metrics: METEOR 0.127, ROUGE-L 0.112, BERT score 0.863, LLM-Accuracy 2.45/5, and a temporal F-score of 0.56. The temporal F-score improvement (0.56 vs. 0.50 for all turn-based baselines) is the paper's headline finding, demonstrating that end-to-end streaming with action tokens enables the model to decide *when* to speak. Among zero-shot baselines (Table 3), LLaVA-NeXT performs best (METEOR 0.104, LLM-Acc. 2.27) but all zero-shot models produce repetitive, uninformative feedback lacking corrective specificity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

STREAM-VLM outperforms all compared baselines on every metric. Against the fine-tuned Video-ChatGPT (METEOR 0.108, LLM-Acc. 2.33) and LLaMA-VID (METEOR 0.106, LLM-Acc. 2.30), STREAM-VLM gains +0.019 METEOR and +0.12–0.15 LLM-Accuracy, while matching their BERT score of 0.863. The text-only Socratic-LLaMA-2-7B baseline (which uses 3D CNN activations as a textual prompt) scores 2.17 LLM-Accuracy, confirming that the 3D CNN's motion representations are critical but that a language model alone cannot match the end-to-end streaming approach. The most significant advantage is temporal: STREAM-VLM's T-F-Score of 0.56 exceeds the 0.50 of all turn-based models (which are forced to respond at fixed 5-second intervals), validating that the <next>/<feedback> action tokens enable learned timing. Among zero-shot baselines, LLaVA-NeXT (METEOR 0.104, LLM-Acc. 2.27) and LLaMA-VID (METEOR 0.100, LLM-Acc. 2.20) lead, but both produce generic, repetitive feedback without corrective specificity, as shown qualitatively in Fig. 4.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the 3D CNN fitness activity recognition architecture of Mercier et al. (2023) as its vision backbone and adopts the cross-attention fusion methodology from Flamingo (Alayrac et al., 2022) and Bhattacharyya et al. (2024) to bridge visual and language representations. It departs from prior interactive-assistant datasets (WTAG, HoloAssist) by introducing fine-grained human actions and diverse per-exercise mistakes, and from turn-based video-language models (Video-ChatGPT, LLaMA-VID, LLaVA-NeXT) by formulating feedback as a proactive, streaming decision rather than a prompted response. The special action tokens (<next>, <feedback>) represent a novel mechanism for end-to-end learning of when to speak, going beyond the fixed-interval prompting used to evaluate existing models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The QEVD benchmark (474+ hours, 1M+ QA pairs, 2,484 test feedbacks) is the first large-scale resource for evaluating asynchronous situated interaction in a real-world domain, and the results show that even fine-tuned SOTA video-language models achieve only ~2.3/5 LLM-accuracy, confirming the task is largely unsolved. The finding that a text-only Socratic model fed 3D CNN activations outperforms full vision-language models suggests that dedicated motion encoders matter more than general-purpose CLIP/ViT features for fine-grained human action understanding. Key open questions include extending beyond the fitness domain, training across multi-exercise transitions, incorporating speech input, and reducing reliance on large amounts of domain-specific aligned data.
