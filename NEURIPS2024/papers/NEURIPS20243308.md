## Problem

A* search's best-first expansion strategy provides limited exploration, causing it to become trapped in local-optimal branches when the guiding heuristic f(n) deviates substantially from the true cost f*(n). While two aspects of A* renaissance (learning-aided f estimation and path consistency) have been well studied, the third aspect—how to effectively select which open node to expand from the OPEN list—lacks deep investigation. This paper addresses that gap by asking whether a selective sampling process over OPEN can improve search efficiency when heuristics are imperfect.

## Input / Output

The method operates on a search tree where each node n carries an accumulated cost g(n) and a heuristic estimate h(n), combined into f(n) = g(n) + h(n). The key tunable inputs are the candidate set size K, the choice among three sampling strategies (uniform, clustering with N_c clusters and learning rate η, or UCT-like with exploration weight c_b), and a domain-specific heuristic network (e.g., a Morgan-fingerprint-based cost estimator for molecules or a circuit-graph-based value estimator for AIGs). Outputs are domain-specific solution paths: multi-step synthetic routes for retrosynthesis, 10-step AIG transformation sequences for logic synthesis, or move sequences for Sokoban, all constrained by a maximum expansion budget or wall-clock limit.

## Method

SeeA* replaces A*'s single node-selection step with a two-stage process: first, a selective sampling strategy constructs a dynamic candidate subset D ⊂ OPEN of size K; second, the node with the minimum f-value within D (rather than within all of OPEN) is expanded. Three sampling strategies are proposed—uniform random sampling, competitive-learning-based clustering that guarantees at least one node per cluster is sampled, and a UCT-like scoring E(n) = f(n) − c_b·√d_max/(1+d(n)) that penalizes deep nodes to encourage exploration. The theoretical analysis under a uniform (and Gaussian) prediction-error assumption proves that SeeA* expands the optimal node with higher probability than A* whenever the heuristic error σ is large relative to the gap between optimal and non-optimal path costs, with the advantage growing as the open-set size N_o increases. This makes SeeA* particularly effective in domains with large branching factors and imperfect heuristics, such as retrosynthetic planning and logic synthesis.

## Results

On the USPTO retrosynthetic planning benchmark (190 molecules), SeeA*(UCT) achieves the highest solved rate of 98.95% with the shortest average solution length (6.36 steps) and fewest node expansions (62.07), while also having the lowest wall-clock time (32.38s). On the MCNC logic synthesis benchmark (12 circuits), SeeA*(Cluster) achieves the best mean ADP reduction of 23.5%, surpassing the state-of-the-art ABC-RL (20.9%) by 2.6 percentage points. Across six additional molecular datasets (4719 molecules total), SeeA*(Cluster) attains the highest mean success rate of 63.56% and shortest mean solution length of 14.31 steps.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SeeA* outperforms all 14 baselines on the USPTO benchmark in solved rate, with SeeA*(UCT) at 98.95% versus the next-best LevinTS at 96.84% and A* search at 88.42%. Against the strongest exploration-based baseline LevinTS [38], SeeA*(UCT) improves solved rate by 2.11 points while reducing solution length from 7.45 to 6.36 steps; however, LevinTS uses fewer expansions (57.11 vs. 62.07). On MCNC logic synthesis, SeeA*(Cluster) leads all methods with 23.5% mean ADP reduction, beating ABC-RL [9] (20.9%), A* search (19.5%), and PHS [39] (15.9%); notably, PHS performs poorly on individual circuits (e.g., 5.2% on C6, 7.3% on C11) despite competitive results on others. The ε-Greedy baseline [52] achieves a reasonable 92.11% solved rate on USPTO but produces extremely long solutions (43.78 steps), confirming that unstructured exploration is inefficient. Weighted A* [18] (84.21%) and PHS [39] (87.37%) underperform even vanilla A* search (88.42%), consistent with the paper's theoretical claim that best-first variants relying heavily on inaccurate heuristics are particularly vulnerable. On Sokoban, SeeA* solves all 1000 test cases with shorter solution lengths than A*, WA*, LevinTS, PHS, and DeepCubeA [1], though the heuristic is more reliable in this domain.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the A* search lineage by targeting the third aspect of the Deep IA-search framework (Xu, 2018): how nodes are selected from the OPEN list. Prior exploration-enhanced A* variants modify the scoring function itself—WA* reweights h(n), LevinTS adds depth penalties, PHS incorporates a policy signal, and ε-Greedy randomly overrides the best-first choice. SeeA* instead introduces a structural change: a selective sampling step that constructs a candidate subset D from OPEN before the argmin is taken, decoupling *which nodes are considered* from *how they are scored*. The UCT-like sampling strategy borrows the exploration bonus from PUCT (AlphaZero) but repurposes it for candidate-set construction rather than tree-node selection, and the clustering strategy draws on competitive learning for online, incremental clustering without re-clustering overhead.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clean theoretical characterization (Theorem 4.3) of exactly when sampling-based exploration beats pure best-first search, tying the benefit to the prediction error σ and the open-set size N_o—this gives practitioners a principled criterion for when to deploy SeeA* versus vanilla A*. The consistent gains across three structurally different domains (molecular retrosynthesis, circuit optimization, Sokoban) strengthen the case that the method is domain-agnostic. However, the approach introduces three hyperparameters (K, N_c, c_b) that require per-domain tuning, and the theoretical analysis relies on a uniform-error assumption that may not hold for learned heuristics with structured biases. Open questions include whether more principled sampling strategies (e.g., learned or adaptive) can reduce the need for manual tuning, and whether the method extends naturally to multi-agent or stochastic settings.
