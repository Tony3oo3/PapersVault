## Problem

Standard weight decay applies a single, uniform penalty coefficient to all parameters, which is overly restrictive for some and insufficient for others. While layer-wise or scheduled weight decay has been explored, these approaches introduce additional task-sensitive hyperparameters. The paper asks whether individualized, dynamic regularization per parameter matrix can be achieved with minimal or no extra hyperparameters.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240286/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

CPR operates on individual parameter matrices (e.g., weight matrices in linear layers) of any neural network, excluding bias terms and normalization weights. It takes the standard training loop inputs (data batches, loss function, optimizer state) and produces updated parameters where each matrix's squared L2 norm is kept below a per-matrix upper bound κ^j. The only additional hyperparameter is the bound κ (or none with the Kappa-IP strategy), and the Lagrange multiplier update rate μ is fixed at 1.0 across all experiments.

## Method

CPR reformulates regularization as a constrained optimization problem: minimize the loss subject to R(θ^j) − κ^j ≤ 0 for each parameter matrix θ^j, where R is the squared L2 norm. This is solved via an adapted augmented Lagrangian method where each constraint has its own Lagrange multiplier λ^j that is updated every training step as λ^j ← (λ^j + μ·(R(θ^j) − κ^j))^+, and the constraint gradient is applied as a separate, decoupled update (analogous to how AdamW decouples weight decay from the loss gradient). Four strategies initialize the bound κ: a uniform value (Kappa-K), a scaled initial norm (Kappa-kI_0), a warm-start snapshot after s training steps (Kappa-WS), and the first inflection point of the regularization function's trajectory (Kappa-IP), the last being entirely hyperparameter-free. The method integrates with any gradient-based optimizer (Adam, SGD) with only 0.4–5.8% runtime overhead, and the per-matrix λ^j values adapt dynamically, effectively providing different regularization strengths that evolve over training.

## Results

On ImageNet pre-training with DeiT-Small (22M), AdamCPR with the hyperparameter-free Kappa-IP initialization achieves 79.84% top-1 accuracy, outperforming the best AdamW configuration (79.16% at weight decay 0.5). For GPT2s language modeling on OpenWebText, AdamCPR (Kappa-IP) reaches 17.94 perplexity versus 18.23 for the best AdamW setting, and requires only 2/3 of the training budget to match AdamW's final score. On medical image segmentation, CPR with SGD improves Dice scores over weight decay on all three benchmarks (e.g., BraTS: 76.65 vs. 76.22). Runtime overhead is minimal: 0.4%–5.8% for GPT2 models and negligible for smaller architectures.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CPR consistently outperforms AdamW (decoupled weight decay) [6] across all evaluated tasks: on ImageNet DeiT-Small, Kappa-IP gains +0.68% top-1 over the best AdamW; on GPT2s, Kappa-IP achieves 0.29 lower perplexity than the best AdamW. Against AdaDecay [13], CPR wins on both CIFAR-100 (Figure E.7/E.8) and GPT2s (17.94 vs. 18.24 PPL in Table H.1). AWD [14] is clearly outperformed on GPT2s (17.94 vs. 18.42 PPL) and CIFAR-100. AMOS [15] produces NaN perplexity on GPT2s and underperforms on CIFAR-100 (Figure E.8). Parameter rescaling [18] also trails CPR on CIFAR-100 across the tested range of rescaling factors. Overall, CPR is the top-performing method in every head-to-head comparison reported in the paper.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the augmented Lagrangian method from classical constrained optimization (Bertsekas [23], Nocedal & Wright [24]) to the deep learning regularization setting, repurposing a tool previously applied to multi-loss VAE/Transformer training (Rezende & Viola [20], Kohl et al. [21]) for individual parameter-matrix regularization. It departs from the weight-decay-scheduling line (Lewkowycz & Gur-Ari [8], Yun et al. [9], Caron et al. [10]) by replacing hand-designed schedules with a constraint that adapts per-parameter-matrix, and from gradient-norm-based adaptive methods (AdaDecay [13], AWD [14], AMOS [15]) by framing regularization as a feasibility problem rather than a penalty-scaling problem. The KKT analysis in Section 4.2 explicitly connects CPR to L2-regularized objectives, showing they share optimal solutions for appropriate κ, positioning CPR as a reparameterization of the same search with different hyperparameter semantics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CPR's hyperparameter-free variant (Kappa-IP) removes the weight-decay coefficient from the training recipe while matching or exceeding tuned AdamW across vision, language, and medical segmentation tasks, reducing the hyperparameter search burden for practitioners. The 33% training-budget savings on GPT2 pre-training is a concrete efficiency gain, and the observation that CPR controls L2 norms throughout training (rather than allowing intermediate over-adaptation) offers a mechanistic explanation for the improved generalization. Limitations include up to 6% runtime overhead at 1.1B parameters, diminished benefit when the training-to-parameter ratio is high, and the fact that Kappa-IP underperforms in short-warmup fine-tuning settings (e.g., LoRA on Mistral-7B). Open questions include whether the approach scales to trillion-parameter models and whether the inflection-point heuristic generalizes to non-convex, highly stochastic training regimes beyond those tested.
