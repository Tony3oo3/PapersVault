## Problem

Existing category-level articulated object pose estimation methods rely on part-centric modeling that ignores kinematic constraints, struggle with self-occlusion of smaller parts, and require intermediate representations (e.g., NOCS coordinates) followed by optimization-based pose recovery (Umeyama, RANSAC), making them slow and fragile. This paper addresses these gaps by formulating articulated pose estimation as a joint state prediction problem within an end-to-end framework that eliminates all post-processing optimization.

## Input / Output

The network takes a partial point cloud of an articulated object (sampled to 1,024 points, obtainable from RGB+depth via back-projection) and directly outputs per-part 6D poses, per-part 3D scales, part segmentation labels, and joint parameters/states in a single forward pass. Unlike prior methods that predict per-point canonical coordinates and then solve for pose via optimization, EfficientCAPER regresses the final poses directly, achieving ~50 FPS inference on a single GPU without any retrieval or iterative refinement step.

## Method

EfficientCAPER decomposes articulated pose estimation into two stages under a joint-centric modeling paradigm. Stage 1 estimates the free part's pose using an HS-Encoder (extending 3D-GC with hybrid-scope feature extraction) that feeds a decoupled rotation decoder (predicting two axis normals with confidence scores based on object symmetry) and a residual translation decoder. Stage 2 canonicalizes the point cloud via the estimated free-part pose, then a second HS-Encoder feeds four parallel decoder branches predicting joint states, joint parameters (axis direction and pivot), per-part scales, and segmentation labels; constrained-part poses are analytically recovered from joint states via the extended Rodrigues formula (revolute joints) or direct axis-aligned translation (prismatic joints). The key insight is that predicting a scalar joint state (angle or distance) is far easier for a network to learn than a full 6D pose or per-point canonical coordinates, and even a few visible points suffice for robust joint state regression under severe self-occlusion. Training uses a multi-task loss with a sign-penalty factor (α=20) on joint state errors to handle the difficulty of regressing values that can be positive or negative.

## Results

On the ArtImage dataset, EfficientCAPER achieves 3D IOU scores of 88–93% across all five categories (Laptop, Eyeglasses, Dishwasher, Scissors, Drawer), substantially outperforming baselines that range from 24–89%. Rotation errors are competitive or best in 4 of 5 categories (2.0°–5.1°), with the exception of Scissors where A-NCSH and ArtPERL achieve lower errors (2.0–2.9° vs. 5.0–5.1°). Inference time is 0.02 s per image, a 40–825× speedup over baselines (0.8–16.5 s). On the semi-synthetic ReArtMix dataset, EfficientCAPER reduces rotation error to 0.7–2.3° (vs. 3.0–6.3° for ReArtNet) and 3D IOU to 84–96% (vs. 24–52%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against A-NCSH [8], EfficientCAPER achieves substantially higher 3D IOU in every category (e.g., 88.2% vs. 56.7% on Laptop, 93.2% vs. 90.2% on Drawer) and lower rotation error in 4 of 5 categories, though it loses on Scissors rotation (5.0° vs. 2.0°). Against OMAD [14], EfficientCAPER dominates on all three pose metrics across all five categories, with the largest gaps in 3D IOU (e.g., 87.9% vs. 24.1% on Laptop part 2). Against ArtPERL [11], EfficientCAPER wins on 3D IOU in every category (by 14–47 percentage points) and on rotation in 4 of 5 categories, while translation errors are comparable. On the ReArtMix dataset, EfficientCAPER outperforms ReArtNet [13] on all metrics for all five categories, with rotation errors reduced by 2–4× (e.g., 0.7° vs. 3.1° on Drawer) and 3D IOU improved by 30–70 percentage points. On the 7-part RobotArm dataset, EfficientCAPER reduces per-part rotation error by 20–38% compared to A-NCSH (e.g., 14.6° vs. 23.5° on part 7), though translation error on the end effector (part 7) remains higher (0.304 m vs. 0.403 m for A-NCSH, but part 5 shows 0.126 m vs. 0.079 m). The most striking advantage is inference speed: 0.02 s per image versus 0.8–16.5 s for all baselines, enabling real-time operation at ~50 FPS.

## Relation to Prior Work

EfficientCAPER extends the category-level articulated object pose estimation line initiated by A-NCSH (Li et al., CVPR 2020) and OMAD (Xue et al., 2021), but departs from their part-centric, optimization-dependent paradigm by introducing a joint-centric pose modeling that directly regresses joint states as pose replacements. For the free-part stage, it adapts GPV-Pose's (Di et al., CVPR 2022) decoupled rotation with confidence-aware voting and FS-Net's (Chen et al., CVPR 2021) residual translation prediction, while replacing the 3D-GC backbone with the more expressive HS-Encoder from HS-Pose (Zheng et al., CVPR 2023). The key conceptual shift is treating articulation as a joint-state prediction problem rather than an independent per-part 6D regression or NOCS-coordinate recovery problem, thereby eliminating the need for Umeyama/RANSAC post-processing entirely.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The elimination of optimization-based post-processing yields a ~50 FPS inference speed (versus 0.9–16.5 s for prior methods), making real-time category-level articulated pose estimation practically viable for robotics and AR applications. The joint-centric formulation also provides a principled explanation for improved occlusion robustness: even a few visible points suffice to constrain a 1-DOF joint state, whereas part-centric methods need sufficient visible geometry for full 6D regression. However, the method assumes exactly one free part and only two joint types (revolute, prismatic), and its performance degrades on Scissors (5.0° rotation error vs. 2.0° for A-NCSH), suggesting the decoupled rotation strategy is not universally optimal. Open questions include generalization to cross-category settings, more complex kinematic chains, and whether the two-stage decomposition can be further unified.
