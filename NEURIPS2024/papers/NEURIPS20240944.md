## Problem

Existing weak-to-strong logit arithmetic methods transfer fine-tuned knowledge from a small model to a large model using a single, statically pre-adjusted weight (α) and a single expert model. This fails because the importance of fine-tuned knowledge varies across decoding steps and tasks, and a single expert cannot cover complex or unseen domains. The paper asks whether one can fine-tune a series of task-specific small models and transfer their knowledge to a much larger model without any additional gradient updates on the large model.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240944/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

At each decoding step, the method consumes the logits of a large pretrained model (LLaMA2-13B), the base logits of a small model (TinyLLaMA-1.1B or LLaMA2-7B), and the logits of one or more fine-tuned small-model experts, all sharing the same vocabulary. It produces a fused token probability distribution via softmax over the combined logits, from which the next token is sampled greedily. A key constraint is that all models must be from the same family (same vocabulary size), and the method requires only black-box logit access—no parameters or training data of the large model are needed.

## Method

The core mechanism replaces the static α in logit arithmetic with a per-step dynamic weight found by solving a KL-divergence-constrained optimization: the fused distribution P̃ should satisfy D_KL(P̃‖P) ≈ D_KL(Q_ft‖Q) and D_KL(P‖P̃) ≈ D_KL(Q‖Q_ft), where P is the large model's distribution and Q, Q_ft are the small model's base and fine-tuned distributions. In practice, α is searched over a bounded range (typically [0, 2] with step 0.1) to minimize the squared residual of these constraints at every token position. For multi-task settings, the method extends to T task-specific SLM experts and decomposes the intractable joint-distribution constraint into a sum of per-expert marginal KL constraints, effectively finding a centroid in Euclidean space of KL divergences. In multi-task inference, only one expert's α is optimized per step (cycling through experts) to avoid exponential search complexity, reducing cost from O(n^T·B·V) to O(n·T·B·V).

## Results

In single-task scenarios (Table 1), transferring knowledge from a 7B expert to the 13B model via dynamic logit fusion achieves an average score of 46.09, closing 96.4% of the gap to 13B full fine-tuning (47.79) and outperforming Proxy Tuning (44.43) by 3.8%. In multi-task scenarios (Table 2), the method achieves 39.42 average (seen + unseen), closing 86.3% of the gap to 13B multi-task tuning (45.68), and notably surpasses 13B multi-task tuning on the unseen MMLU benchmark (51.31 vs. 50.58). The method also outperforms the source 7B experts by 4.4% on average in single-task settings, demonstrating that the large model's inherent capabilities are leveraged during transfer.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Proxy Tuning (Liu et al., 2024), which uses a static α=1.0, the proposed dynamic method wins on every task in both single- and multi-task settings: 46.09 vs. 44.43 average (7B→13B single-task) and 39.42 vs. 34.89 (7B multi-task), with the largest gains on MMLU (57.15 vs. 56.24) and TriviaQA (57.11 vs. 52.81). Against 13B LoRA fine-tuning (Hu et al., 2021), the method is slightly below on average (46.09 vs. 48.60) but requires no gradient computation on the large model, only fine-tuning the 7B expert. Against Task Arithmetic (Ilharco et al., 2023), the paper shows in Figure 5b that combining dynamic logit fusion with task arithmetic yields the highest overall multi-task performance, improving 7.2% over task arithmetic alone and 1.5% over the proposed method alone, with the largest gains on unseen tasks. In multi-task settings, the method also outperforms 13B multi-task tuning on the unseen MMLU benchmark (51.31 vs. 50.58), demonstrating superior out-of-domain generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the logit-arithmetic framework for weak-to-strong knowledge transfer introduced by Mitchell et al. (ICLR 2024) and Liu et al. (Proxy Tuning, 2024), which transfer fine-tuned knowledge from a small model to a large model via a static behavioral-delta weight α. The key departure is replacing the single static α with a per-decoding-step dynamic optimization problem framed as a KL-divergence-constrained centroid search in Euclidean space, and generalizing from one expert to a set of task-specific small models whose contributions are adaptively weighted. The method also connects to the broader model-merging and task-arithmetic literature by showing that logit-level fusion can complement parameter-level merging without requiring access to the large model's weights.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that practitioners can fine-tune only small, cheap models and transfer their expertise to a much larger model at inference time without any gradient computation on the large model, closing 96.4% of the single-task and 86.3% of the multi-task gap to full fine-tuning. The ability to handle unseen tasks (MMLU) and to compose with in-context learning and task arithmetic broadens applicability. However, the method is constrained to models sharing the same vocabulary (tested only on the LLaMA family), incurs a ~2.5× inference slowdown, and the multi-task domain-specific results still lag behind direct multi-task fine-tuning, leaving open questions about scalability to heterogeneous architectures, larger expert pools, and discriminative settings.
