## Problem

Existing latent variable models for neural spiking data (e.g., LFADS, VAEs) extract interpretable low-dimensional representations but produce low-fidelity samples, while generative models (e.g., GANs) can synthesize realistic activity but lack an accessible latent space. Standard diffusion models cannot be directly applied to discrete spike counts, leaving a gap for a method that simultaneously infers smooth low-dimensional latents and generates realistic, conditionally controllable neural spiking data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243750/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes discrete spike-count matrices (n neurons × T time bins, e.g., 5 ms bins) with optional behavioral covariates such as reach angle (scalar), hand velocity traces (2-D time series), or trial-length masks. It produces two outputs: (1) continuous, time-aligned latent trajectories of dimension d ≪ n that capture shared population dynamics, and (2) generated discrete spike trains that can be sampled at arbitrary lengths (via S4 unrolling) and optionally conditioned on behavioral covariates. Trials of heterogeneous length (e.g., 2–10 s human speech recordings) are handled by zero-padding and masked loss computation.

## Method

LDNS is a two-stage latent diffusion framework. Stage 1 trains a deterministic regularized autoencoder with bidirectional S4 (structured state-space) layers to compress spiking data into smooth latents, minimizing Poisson NLL plus L2 and temporal-smoothness penalties, with coordinated dropout to prevent rate collapse. Stage 2 trains a DDPM denoiser (also built from S4 blocks) on the inferred latents, using ε-parameterization and a 1000-step linear noise schedule; S4's unrollable state-transition matrices enable generation at lengths far exceeding the training window. Conditioning on behavioral covariates is injected via adaptive instance normalization (for scalar/timestep signals like reach angle) or channel concatenation (for time-varying signals like velocity traces). A modular post-hoc autoregressive observation model with per-neuron spike-history couplings (T′=20 bins) is optionally fitted to capture single-neuron dynamics such as refractoriness, improving sample realism without altering the latent dynamics.

## Results

On the MC_Maze monkey reach dataset, LDNS with spike-history observation model (LDNS_sh) achieves the best performance across all four metrics: D_KL population spike count histogram of 0.0016, RMSE pairwise correlation of 0.0025, RMSE mean ISI of 0.024, and RMSE std ISI of 0.023. The Poisson-only LDNS variant is competitive with AutoLFADS on D_KL psch (0.0039 vs. 0.0040) and superior on RMSE std ISI (0.023 vs. 0.029). On the synthetic Lorenz dataset, LDNS accurately recovers latent attractor geometry, firing rates, and spiking statistics, and generalizes to 16× longer sequences without the instabilities observed in LFADS.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against AutoLFADS (the strongest VAE baseline), LDNS with Poisson observations ties on D_KL psch (0.0039 vs. 0.0040) and wins on RMSE std ISI (0.023 vs. 0.029) and RMSE mean ISI (0.037 vs. 0.039). With the spike-history observation model, LDNS_sh achieves a 56% reduction in D_KL psch over AutoLFADS_sh (0.0016 vs. 0.0036) and a 29% reduction in RMSE mean ISI (0.024 vs. 0.034), while matching on RMSE std ISI. Against TNDM, LDNS wins on all four metrics, with the largest gap on RMSE mean ISI (0.037 vs. 0.057 for Poisson; 0.024 vs. 0.057 for spike-history). Against pi-VAE, which lacks temporal modeling, LDNS dominates across all metrics (e.g., D_KL psch 0.0039 vs. 0.0063; RMSE mean ISI 0.037 vs. 0.064). The paper also notes that LDNS is significantly faster to train than AutoLFADS, which requires population-based hyperparameter tuning over a multi-GPU cluster for one day, whereas LDNS trains in under one hour on a single GPU.

## Relation to Prior Work

LDNS adapts the two-stage latent diffusion framework from image generation (Rombach et al., 2022) and the DDPM training procedure (Ho et al., 2020) to the discrete, variable-length domain of neural spiking data, using a deterministic regularized autoencoder (Ghosh et al., 2020) with S4 structured state-space layers for length generalization. It departs from prior neural LVMs (LFADS, pi-VAE, TNDM) that focus on latent inference but produce low-fidelity samples, and from GAN-based spike generators that lack an interpretable latent space, by unifying both capabilities. The modular spike-history autoregressive observation model extends the standard Poisson assumption used across most neural LVMs, borrowing from empirical spiking models (Macke et al., 2011) but applying it post-hoc to any rate-predicting model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LDNS enables closed-loop in silico experiments by generating realistic, conditionally controllable neural spiking data while simultaneously providing time-aligned low-dimensional latents for single-trial interpretation—a combination no prior method achieves. The variable-length generation capability (via S4) is particularly important for naturalistic behavioral paradigms where trial structure is absent. Key limitations include the need to manually select latent dimensionality and the propagation of autoencoder modeling errors into the diffusion stage. Open questions include whether the approach scales to thousands of neurons, whether latent dimensionality can be learned automatically, and whether the spike-history module generalizes to other observation model families.
