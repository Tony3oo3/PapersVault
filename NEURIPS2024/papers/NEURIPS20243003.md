## Problem

Traditional toxicity classifiers trained on single-annotator labels fail to capture the diversity of human perspectives, and standard ERM training exploits spurious correlations (e.g., associating phrases like 'I must remind you that' with non-toxicity), causing significant performance drops under distribution shift. No existing method simultaneously leverages crowdsourced multi-annotator labels and explicitly mitigates OOD risk from spurious features.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243003/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes text instances (questions or responses) each annotated by M workers (3 human annotators plus 3 LLMs: GPT-4, GPT-4 Turbo, Claude-2), producing a set of candidate labels per instance rather than a single ground-truth label. A small clean validation set with verified ground truth (1,000 samples) is also required for the outer-loop GroupDRO optimization. The model outputs a toxicity class prediction (15-class for questions, binary for responses) along with learned per-annotator soft-label weights computed by a separate RoBERTa-base network that maps input text to importance scores via softmax.

## Method

The paper proposes a bi-level optimization framework where the inner loop minimizes ERM cross-entropy loss on training data using soft labels (a weighted sum of one-hot annotator labels, with weights produced by a RoBERTa-base network conditioned on the input text), and the outer loop updates those soft-label weights to minimize a GroupDRO loss on a clean validation set, thereby reducing the classifier's reliance on spurious features. The connection between the two loops is established via a pseudo-update of the classifier parameters (one-step gradient descent), enabling gradient flow from the outer GroupDRO objective back to the soft-label weight network. The classifier itself is RoBERTa-large, while the weight estimator is RoBERTa-base, keeping the additional overhead to roughly 2× baseline training time. The authors prove monotonic decrease of the GroupDRO risk under a lower-bound assumption on the inner-product of gradients, and derive an O(1/√T) convergence rate for the soft-label weight gradient norm.

## Results

On the toxic question classification task (Q-A, all annotators), the proposed method achieves 78.41% average accuracy and 69.44% worst-group accuracy, outperforming the strongest baseline (Vanilla Soft Label at 74.81%/67.68%) by 3.6 and 1.8 points respectively. On the toxic response classification task (R-A), the method reaches 89.80% average accuracy and 77.82% worst-group accuracy, surpassing the next-best baseline (Vanilla Soft Label at 85.52%/62.57%) by 4.3 and 15.3 points. The method also outperforms GPT-4 Turbo on response classification and matches it on question classification, while significantly exceeding any individual human annotator.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed bi-level soft-label + GroupDRO method wins on both average and worst-group accuracy across all six baseline categories on both Q-A and R-A datasets. Among pre-training label identification methods, PM Voting (73.87%/65.78% on Q-A) and Snorkel (68.73%/47.47% on Q-A) are the strongest, but the proposed method exceeds them by 4.5 and 9.7 points in average accuracy respectively on Q-A, with an even larger gap in worst-group accuracy. The post-training Ensemble baseline (70.70%/56.57% on Q-A) is outperformed by 7.7 points in average accuracy. Online methods perform poorly: Average-label Learning (19.38%) and PRODEN (23.07%) on Q-A are far below the proposed method, likely due to their inability to handle the multi-class structure and annotation noise. The closest competitor is Vanilla Soft Label (74.81%/67.68% on Q-A; 85.52%/62.57% on R-A), which uses vote counts as weights without modeling annotator reliability; the proposed method improves worst-group accuracy by 15.3 points on R-A, demonstrating that learned soft-label weights combined with GroupDRO are critical for robustness against spurious features.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously separate research threads: learning from partial/crowdsourced labels (Snorkel, PRODEN, majority voting) and distributionally robust optimization (GroupDRO, CVaR DRO). It adopts the pseudo-update mechanism from MAML-style bilevel optimization to couple a soft-label weight estimator with a toxicity classifier, and uses GroupDRO as the outer-loop objective to penalize reliance on spurious features. Unlike prior partial-label methods that assume a fixed annotator quality or perform label identification in a single pass, this work jointly learns instance-dependent soft-label weights while simultaneously optimizing for worst-group OOD risk, a combination not previously explored in the toxicity classification literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The approach offers a practical path to robust toxicity moderation that leverages the growing availability of multi-source annotations (human + LLM) without requiring a single ground-truth label per instance, and it achieves worst-group accuracy substantially above all baselines (77.8% vs. ~65% on the response task). However, the method still requires a small clean validation set with group annotations for GroupDRO, and the ~2× compute overhead from the bilevel loop limits scalability. Open questions include extending the framework to multimodal toxicity, ensuring fairness across annotator demographics, and adapting the bilevel structure to noisy pairwise feedback in RLHF pipelines.
