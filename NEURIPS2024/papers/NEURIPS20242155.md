## Problem

Existing neural PDE solvers, including the SOTA Neur0Light, produce large errors (0.38 NMAE) when simulating real-world complicated photonic devices due to discrete high-contrast permittivity distributions, complex light-matter interactions (scattering, resonance), extreme sensitivity to local structural changes, non-uniform learning difficulty along the propagation path, and rich high-frequency content in the predicted fields. No single operator architecture had previously addressed all these challenges simultaneously with sufficient parameter efficiency.

## Input / Output

The model takes as input a multi-channel 2D field over an adaptively discretized domain (e.g., 80×384 for etched MMI, 128×144 for metaline) comprising the relative permittivity distribution, a masked light source field H_y^J, and two wave-prior channels (P_x, P_z) that encode wavelength-dependent propagation phase along each axis. The output is the complex-valued steady-state electromagnetic field over the same 2D grid, evaluated with normalized mean squared error (N-MSE) rather than MAE, since L2 distance is rotation-invariant in the complex plane while L1 is not.

## Method

The core contribution is a cross-axis factorized PACE operator that decomposes the full 2D Fourier integral kernel into horizontal and vertical 1D kernels applied sequentially, enabling explicit full-domain integration in a single layer (O(n log n)) rather than relying on multi-layer stacking to implicitly recover global coupling. Parameter efficiency is achieved through group-wise cross-axis integration (g=4 disjoint channel subgroups), reducing kernel parameters from κ_h×κ_v×C_o×C_i (FNO) to (κ_h+κ_v)×C_o×C_i/g. Two auxiliary components address specific challenges: a self-weighted path (linear→Sigmoid) provides instance-specific attention to regions of significant structural influence, and an explicit high-frequency projection unit (linear + nonlinear activation) amplifies high-frequency features before the integral. For extremely hard cases, a cascaded two-stage paradigm (PACE-I 12-layer → PACE-II 8-layer with larger Fourier modes) is trained jointly with cross-stage feature distillation, outperforming simply adding more layers at the same parameter budget.

## Results

On three real-world photonic device benchmarks (Etched MMI 3×3, Etched MMI 5×5, Metaline 3×3), the 12-layer PACE model achieves N-MSE test errors of 10.59, 11.91, and 2.82 (×10⁻²) respectively, using only 1.71M or 1.24M parameters. This represents a 73.85% average reduction in test error with 51.67% fewer parameters compared to all nine baselines, and a 39.03% error reduction with 17.70% fewer parameters versus the best baseline (16-layer Neur0Light). The two-stage PACE-I+PACE-II setup further reduces test error to 5.32 and 6.15 (×10⁻²) on Etched MMI 3×3 and 5×5, outperforming a 20-layer single model (7.04 and 7.88). PACE achieves 154–577× speedup over scipy-based FDFD and 11.8–12× over pardiso-based FDFD on a 20-core CPU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PACE outperforms all nine baselines across all three benchmarks. The closest competitor is Neur0Light [8] (16-layer, 2.11M params), the SOTA photonic neural operator; PACE reduces its test error by 39% on Etched MMI 3×3 (10.59 vs. 17.21), 32% on Etched MMI 5×5 (11.91 vs. 17.41), and 54% on Metaline 3×3 (2.82 vs. 6.09), while using 17–18% fewer parameters. Factorized FNO [29] is the next-best Fourier-based baseline (32.81 on MMI 3×3, 9.28 on Metaline), but PACE's cross-axis factorization provides substantially stronger full-domain modeling. Standard FNO-2d [16] (38.71 on MMI 3×3) and Tensorized FNO [13] (36.61) suffer from parameter bloat or aggressive decomposition that degrades accuracy on high-frequency optical fields. Convolutional baselines (UNet [17,4] at 65.32, Dil-ResNet [25] at 51.79 on MMI 3×3) and attention-based models [15] (69.85) fail to capture local structural sensitivity and long-range dependencies, with the attention model performing worst overall. U-NO [2] (42.86) and the latent spectral method [33] (55.16) also fall well short, confirming that the multi-scale, high-contrast nature of photonic PDEs demands the explicit cross-axis full-domain integration that PACE provides.

## Relation to Prior Work

PACE builds directly on Neur0Light (Gu et al., NeurIPS 2022), the SOTA physics-agnostic neural operator for parametric photonic device simulation, reusing its convolutional stem, wave-prior encoding, and even its operator in the first two blocks. The core architectural departure targets the factorized Fourier kernel lineage: where Neur0Light and Factorized FNO (Tran et al., ICLR 2023) decompose the 2-D kernel into independent 1-D Fourier transforms along each axis (requiring multiple stacked layers to recover full-domain integration), PACE proposes a cross-axis 2-D factorization that achieves explicit full-domain kernel integration in a single operator. The two-stage cascaded learning paradigm (rough-then-refine with cross-stage distillation) is a novel training strategy not present in prior neural-operator-for-PDE work, drawing an analogy to human progressive skill acquisition.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 39% error reduction over the best baseline (Neur0Light) with 17% fewer parameters, combined with 11.8–577× speedup over FDFD numerical solvers, makes high-fidelity photonic device simulation practically viable within iterative design loops. The cross-axis factorized kernel and two-stage cascaded learning are framed as generally applicable to multi-scale PDE problems beyond photonics, though this broader claim remains untested here. Open questions include extending to time-domain (FDTD) simulation, GPU-optimized FFT kernels for further speedup, and integrating PACE into closed-loop inverse design. The paper's focus on steady-state, 2-D, linear isotropic devices leaves 3-D and nonlinear photonic structures as significant unaddressed territory.
