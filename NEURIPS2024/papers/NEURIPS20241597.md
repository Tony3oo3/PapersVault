## Problem

Prior automated forecasting systems (e.g., Autocast) failed to approach the performance of competitive human forecaster crowds, and existing LMs perform near or below random guessing on forecasting questions without additional context. This paper asks whether a retrieval-augmented LM pipeline can close that gap and match or exceed the crowd aggregate on recent, post-knowledge-cutoff questions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241597/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes a binary forecasting question with its background description, resolution criteria, and key dates, plus a set of retrieved and summarized news articles bounded to a simulated retrieval date (chosen from a geometric schedule between the question's open and close dates). It produces a probability forecast in [0,1] accompanied by an explanatory reasoning trace. Six individual forecasts (3 from a prompted base GPT-4, 3 from a fine-tuned GPT-4 sampled at T=0.5) are aggregated via a trimmed mean into the final prediction, evaluated by Brier score against the platform's crowd aggregate.

## Method

The pipeline has three stages. Retrieval uses LM-generated search queries (via question decomposition into sub-questions), fetches articles from NewsCatcher and Google News, filters by GPT-3.5 relevance ratings (threshold ≥4 on a 1–6 scale), and summarizes the top 15 articles. Reasoning employs a structured scratchpad prompt that instructs the model to rephrase the question, argue for and against the outcome, weigh arguments by importance, and check for over/under-confidence against base rates. The key training contribution is a self-supervised fine-tuning procedure: the system generates 16 candidate reasonings per question across varied prompts and retrieval configurations, retains only those whose Brier score beats the crowd (with a 0.15 deviation cap and averaging with the crowd to prevent overconfidence), and fine-tunes GPT-4-0613 on the 6,000 most recent qualifying examples—teaching the model context-appropriate reasoning without prescriptive scratchpad instructions.

## Results

On the test set (914 binary questions published after June 2023), the full system achieves a Brier score of .179 and 71.5% accuracy, compared to the human crowd aggregate of .149 and 77.0%. The best individual LM baseline (GPT-4-1106-Preview, zero-shot) scores .208, so the system improves by .029 over the strongest single model. In a selective setting where the system only forecasts when the crowd is uncertain (0.3–0.7), at early retrieval dates, and with ≥5 relevant articles, the system beats the crowd (.240 vs .247 Brier score). Aggregating the system with the crowd (4× crowd weight) further improves the Brier score to .146.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The system (.179 Brier) outperforms every individual LM baseline: GPT-4-1106-Preview (.208), Claude-2.1 (.220), Gemini-Pro (.243), Mistral-8x7B-Instruct (.238), Llama-2-13B (.226), and GPT-3.5-Turbo-1106 (.274). However, the system still trails the human crowd aggregate (.149) by .030 Brier score on the full test set. The gap narrows substantially in the selective setting: when all three heuristics are met (crowd uncertain, early retrieval, ≥5 articles), the system beats the crowd by more than 1.5 standard errors (.240 vs .247). Ablations confirm that retrieval contributes ~.021 Brier improvement (no-IR: .206) and fine-tuning contributes ~.007 (no fine-tuning: .186), while fine-tuning GPT-3.5 instead of GPT-4 costs only .001 (.183 vs .182), showing the method is not solely dependent on the strongest base model.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LM-based forecasting line initiated by Zou et al. (2022, Autocast) and Yan et al. (2024, Autocast++), which showed that prior ML systems could not approach human crowd performance. It repurposes question decomposition (Min et al., 2019) for search query generation and scratchpad prompting (Nye et al., 2021) for structured forecasting reasoning, combining them with a self-supervised fine-tuning procedure inspired by STaR and ReST. Unlike prior retrieval-based forecasting work that predicted only binary resolutions, this system elicits calibrated probability forecasts with explanatory rationales and aggregates them via trimmed mean, positioning itself as the first end-to-end pipeline to near human crowd aggregate performance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the largest and most recent (2023–2024) forecasting dataset from five competitive platforms, a valuable resource for future work. The finding that a retrieval-augmented LM pipeline can approach (and in selective settings surpass) human crowd aggregates is practically significant for scalable, low-cost institutional forecasting. Key limitations include the persistent .03 Brier score gap on the full test set, the reliance on selective forecasting to beat the crowd, hedging behavior from safety training that degrades performance on high-confidence questions, and restriction to binary outcomes. Open questions include whether domain-adaptive training can close the gap in weak categories (e.g., Arts & Recreation, Environment & Energy) and whether the approach extends to multi-outcome or numerical forecasting.
