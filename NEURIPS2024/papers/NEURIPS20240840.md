## Problem

In average-reward MDPs, no existing algorithm simultaneously achieves minimax optimal regret Õ(√(sp(h*)SAT)), computational tractability, and no prior knowledge of the bias span sp(h*). Prior work either suffers from sub-optimal regret scaling with the diameter D rather than sp(h*), is intractable (e.g., EBF relies on an oracle for hard optimization), or requires knowing sp(h*) in advance. The paper asks whether all three requirements can be met at once.

## Input / Output

The algorithm PMEVI-DT takes as input a tabular MDP environment, an optional prior bias region H* ⊆ R^S (which can be set to R^S for a fully prior-less variant), a learning horizon T, and a confidence level δ. It also requires a system of confidence regions t ↦ M_t for the model, which can be instantiated with Weissman, empirical Bernstein, KL-likelihood, or trivial regions. The output is a sequence of policies played episodically via the doubling trick, with a high-probability regret guarantee of O(√(sp(h*)SAT·log(SAT/δ))) + O(sp(h*)·S^{5/2}·A^{3/2}·T^{9/20}·log²(SAT/δ)).

## Method

The core contribution is the PMEVI subroutine, which replaces the standard Extended Value Iteration (EVI) in any OFU-based algorithm. PMEVI combines two operations applied iteratively: (1) a β-mitigated extended Bellman operator L_t^β that caps the optimistic transition term p̃(s,a)·u by p̂_t(s,a)·u + β(s,a), where β is a variance-aware bound from an empirical Bernstein inequality applied to (p̂−p)h* but maximized over the bias confidence region H_t to remain independent of the unknown h*; and (2) a projection operator Γ_t onto H_t, the bias confidence region constructed from pairwise bias-difference estimates based on commute times between state pairs. The key insight is that the model confidence region M_t itself is largely irrelevant to achieving minimax optimality—what matters is inferring bias structure from data and using it to tighten the Bellman operator. The resulting projected mitigated operator 𝔏_t = Γ_t ∘ L_t^β preserves monotonicity, span non-expansiveness, and linearity, guaranteeing convergence and an optimistic gain 𝔤_t ≥ g*.

## Results

The main theoretical result (Theorem 5) establishes that PMEVI-DT achieves regret Õ(√(sp(h*)SAT)) with probability 1−26δ, matching the minimax lower bound Ω(√(sp(h*)SAT)) for the first time with a tractable algorithm. With UCRL2 confidence regions, the time complexity is O(DS³AT). In experiments on River-Swim (3-state and 5-state), PMEVI-DT with informative prior bias constraints (c up to 2) substantially reduces regret compared to UCRL2, but without prior knowledge (c=0), PMEVI-DT performs nearly identically to its EVI-based counterparts, indicating the bias confidence region is too loose in practice to yield empirical gains.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PMEVI-DT is the first algorithm to simultaneously achieve minimax optimal regret Õ(√(sp(h*)SAT)), polynomial-time tractability, and no prior knowledge of sp(h*). Compared to UCRL2 (DS√(AT)), PMEVI-DT replaces the diameter D with the (potentially much smaller) bias span sp(h*) and removes the extra factor of S. Compared to EBF [Zhang & Ji 2019], which also achieves the optimal bound, PMEVI-DT is tractable (O(DS³AT) time) whereas EBF requires an intractable oracle. Compared to SCAL and REGAL, PMEVI-DT does not require prior knowledge of sp(h*). In the River-Swim experiments, PMEVI-DT with informative priors (c=1.5 or 2) clearly outperforms UCRL2 in cumulative regret, but without priors (c=0) it matches EVI-based baselines, suggesting the theoretical advantage has not yet been realized empirically due to the bias confidence region being too wide in early learning phases.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the episodic optimism-in-face-of-uncertainty framework of UCRL2 (Auer et al. 2009) and its EVI subroutine, while incorporating the bias-difference estimation mechanism from EBF (Zhang and Ji 2019) and the bias-span-constrained exploration paradigm of SCAL (Fruit et al. 2018). The key conceptual departure is that the authors argue the choice of confidence region has 'little importance' for achieving minimax optimal regret; instead, the critical innovation is the PMEVI subroutine, which projects and mitigates the extended Bellman operator using inferred bias information. This shifts the design principle from 'tighten the confidence region' to 'constrain the Bellman operator with bias structure,' and PMEVI can be dropped into any existing EVI-based algorithm (UCRL2, UCRL2B, KL-UCRL) as a plug-in replacement.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PMEVI-DT is the first tractable algorithm achieving the minimax optimal regret bound \tilde{O}(\sqrt{\text{sp}(h^*)SAT}) for weakly communicating MDPs without requiring prior knowledge of the bias span, closing a gap that has persisted since Auer et al. 2009. The PMEVI subroutine is a reusable component that can immediately improve theoretical guarantees of multiple existing algorithms. However, the experimental results on river-swim reveal that without prior bias knowledge, PMEVI performs nearly identically to EVI, suggesting the bias confidence region is too loose in practice and that the theoretical advantage may not translate to practical gains. Open questions include whether bias estimation can be made more efficient, whether the EVI-based analysis itself can be tightened, and whether the gap between PAC sample complexity (which requires prior knowledge per recent impossibility results) and regret minimization (which does not) can be further exploited.
