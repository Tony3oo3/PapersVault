## Problem

Existing OOD detection methods rely on post-hoc scoring functions that implicitly or explicitly assume the learned feature space follows a specific distribution (e.g., Gaussian or Gibbs-Boltzmann), yet these assumptions frequently fail in practice, creating a mismatch between assumed and actual feature distributions. The paper tackles the under-explored question of whether one can deterministically shape the in-distribution feature distribution during pre-training of a discriminative model, thereby enabling assumption-free density-based OOD scoring.

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR benchmarks, 224×224 for ImageNet-100) and produces both a K-way class label and a continuous OOD/ID decision score derived from the learned density p_θ(x). Internally, the backbone (ResNet-18/34/50) maps inputs to ℓ2-normalized latent features on the unit hypersphere, and the scoring function evaluates a mixture of class-conditional von Mises–Fisher-like densities with per-class concentration parameters ε_k learned through prototype magnitudes. The normalization constant for each component is approximated online via a Poincaré asymptotic expansion of the modified Bessel function, avoiding the intractable integral over the latent space.

## Method

DRL explicitly enforces the latent feature space to conform to a pre-defined mixture distribution by parameterizing each class-conditional density as an exponential of inner products between ℓ2-normalized features and unnormalized class prototypes, with per-class concentration ε_k = ‖μ_k‖₂/τ. The training objective is a linear combination of a Bayes-based classification loss and an evidence lower bound (ELBO) that measures how well each sample fits the assumed distribution, preventing trivial solutions where features collapse or diverge. The optimization is formulated as a provably convergent EM algorithm: the E-step computes soft posterior labels via Bayes' theorem, and the M-step maximizes the ELBO with respect to all parameters. To handle the inconsistency of integrating EM into batch-based training, the authors rearrange sequential sampling so that half of each mini-batch overlaps with the previous iteration, providing on-the-fly soft labels from the current model. Unlike prior hyperspherical methods (CIDER, PALM) that fix prototype norms to a constant, DRL learns per-class concentration through prototype magnitudes and approximates the normalization constant online, preserving the flexibility of the mixture model.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 (ResNet-18), DRL achieves an average FPR95 of 11.58% and AUROC of 97.83% across five OOD test sets (SVHN, Places365, LSUN, iSUN, Textures), outperforming the strongest baseline PALM (14.96% FPR95, 97.57% AUROC) by 3.38% in FPR95. On CIFAR-100 (ResNet-34), DRL records 35.66% FPR95 and 90.13% AUROC, trailing PALM (28.02% FPR95) but surpassing CIDER (46.89%) and KNN+ (60.22%). On hard OOD detection (CIFAR-100 vs. semantically similar OOD sets), DRL achieves 60.88% FPR95 and 84.50% AUROC, beating PALM (64.40% FPR95) and all other baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the primary CIFAR-10 benchmark, DRL achieves the best average FPR95 (11.58%) and AUROC (97.83%), outperforming PALM (14.96% FPR95) by 3.38% and CIDER (12.95%) by 1.37%. DRL also surpasses the fine-tuning-based methods SSD+ (14.56%) and KNN+ (13.66%) by 2.98% and 2.08% respectively in average FPR95. On CIFAR-100, however, DRL (35.66% FPR95) underperforms PALM (28.02%) by 7.64%, though it still beats CIDER (46.89%) and KNN+ (60.22%). In the harder OOD setting (semantically similar OOD data), DRL reverses the CIFAR-100 gap and beats PALM (60.88% vs. 64.40% FPR95) and all other baselines. In the unsupervised setting, DRL (40.66% FPR95, 89.00% AUROC) outperforms PALM (43.37%, 88.07%) and CSI (53.55%, 86.11%), confirming robustness without ground-truth labels.

## Relation to Prior Work

DRL sits at the intersection of density-based OOD scoring (Energy, Mahalanobis/GEM) and hyperspherical representation learning for OOD detection (CIDER, PALM, CSI). It departs from PALM and CIDER in two key ways: it avoids ℓ₂-normalizing class prototypes (allowing per-class concentration via the vMF parameter εₖ) and replaces the fixed normalization constant with an online asymptotic approximation of the Bessel function. Theoretically, DRL reframes the training objective as a provably convergent EM algorithm with an ELBO, rather than relying solely on contrastive or classification losses, and introduces a sequential-sampling rearrangement to handle the inconsistency of EM within batch-based training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper answers a clean conceptual question—whether the ID feature distribution can be deterministically shaped during pre-training—by coupling distributional modeling with classification in a single EM loop, which removes the post-hoc assumption mismatch that plagues existing density-based scorers. However, the CIFAR-100 results (35.66% FPR95) underperform PALM (28.02%) by a wide margin, and the unsupervised setting shows only competitive rather than dominant performance, suggesting the approach's advantage is most pronounced in the 10-class regime. The authors acknowledge that only one density-function realization (Euclidean/vMF) is explored, leaving open whether richer or non-parametric kernels could close the gap on harder benchmarks.
