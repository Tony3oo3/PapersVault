## Problem

Classical CCA becomes uninterpretable in high-dimensional settings, and existing SCCA methods enforce sparsity only approximately via ℓ₁ penalties rather than exact ℓ₀ constraints. This paper addresses the gap by developing exact formulations and algorithms for SCCA with strict sparsity, establishing that the problem generalizes three NP-hard problems (sparse PCA, sparse SVD, sparse regression) and providing both polynomial-time solutions for low-rank cases and scalable exact/approximate algorithms for the general case.

## Input / Output

The method takes a block covariance matrix partitioned into B (n×n), C (m×m), and cross-covariance A (n×m), along with integer sparsity levels s₁ ≤ n and s₂ ≤ m. Unlike prior work, B and C are allowed to be singular (low-rank), which is typical in high-dimension low-sample-size genomic data. The output consists of sparse canonical loadings (x, y) with at most s₁ and s₂ nonzero components, the optimal correlation value v*, and the selected variable subsets S₁ ⊆ [n], S₂ ⊆ [m] that achieve this optimum.

## Method

The paper derives a combinatorial reformulation showing SCCA is equivalent to simultaneously selecting principal submatrices of B and C and a submatrix of A to maximize the largest singular value of √(B_S†) A_ST √(C_T†). For the low-rank case where s₁ ≥ rank(B) and s₂ ≥ rank(C), a polynomial-time O(n³+m³) exact algorithm exploits the null-space structure of B and C to reduce SCCA to standard CCA. For the general case, an equivalent mixed-integer SDP (MISDP) is derived, and a branch-and-cut algorithm is developed using closed-form cuts obtained from the Lagrangian dual of the inner SDP, with a separation oracle that evaluates the dual at each node. When A is rank-one, SCCA decomposes into two independent mixed-integer convex quadratic programs solvable via perspective formulations. Greedy and local search heuristics based on the combinatorial formulation provide fast approximation solutions that match optimal values on most tested instances.

## Results

The proposed local search algorithm achieves exact sparsity (S.x = s₁, S.y = s₂) across all test cases, while all three baselines violate the sparsity constraint. On correlation, the local search method outperforms the three baselines in 16 out of 22 testing cases, e.g., achieving Corr = 0.995 vs. 0.980 (Witten), 0.848 (Parkhomenko), and 0.985 (Chu) on the 1000×1000 breast cancer subset with s₁=s₂=10. For the low-rank breast cancer dataset (n=19,672, m=2,149, rank=89), Algorithm 1 solves SCCA to global optimality in 1 second when s₁≥r and s₂≥r̂. The branch-and-cut algorithm certifies optimality for full-rank instances up to 120×120, and the rank-one MICQP formulation extends solvability to 200×200.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares its local search algorithm against three established SCCA methods: Witten et al. [41] (penalized matrix decomposition), Parkhomenko et al. [37] (iterative penalized CCA), and Chu et al. [10] (linearized Bregman method). The local search method strictly enforces the ℓ₀ sparsity constraint (S.x = s₁, S.y = s₂ exactly), while all three baselines only approximate sparsity via convex penalties and consistently exceed the target sparsity (e.g., [41] produces S.x=166, S.y=401 when s₁=s₂=10 on the 1000×1000 case). On correlation, the local search method wins in 16 of 22 cases; it is most decisively ahead of [41] and [37] (e.g., 0.995 vs. 0.980 and 0.848 at n=1000), while [10] is competitive on correlation (0.985) but still violates sparsity (S.x=80, S.y=59). In terms of runtime, the local search method is faster than [10] and [37] on most instances (e.g., 11s vs. 60s and 58s at n=1000), though [41] is the fastest baseline (7.3s) at the cost of substantially lower correlation and much worse sparsity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the SCCA problem introduced by Parkhomenko et al. [36] and formalized by Witten et al. [41], but departs fundamentally from the dominant penalized-approach paradigm (l1, elastic net) by enforcing exact l0 sparsity through mixed-integer optimization. The combinatorial formulation (Theorem 1) reveals that SCCA requires simultaneous submatrix selection from three matrices (B, C, A), a structural distinction from sparse PCA and sparse SVD where only a single matrix A is partitioned. The MISDP reformulation and branch-and-cut algorithm with closed-form cuts extend the outer-approximation framework of Duran and Grossmann [15] to the semidefinite setting, while the rank-one decomposition into two MICQPs borrows perspective techniques from sparse regression [1, 43]. Unlike prior mixed-integer SCCA work [40], the paper does not require positive definiteness of B and C, accommodating the low-rank covariance matrices common in high-dimensional genomics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first paper to provide a complete exact-optimization toolkit for SCCA, bridging the gap between approximate penalized methods and certifiable optimality. The polynomial-time algorithm (O(n³+m³)) for low-rank cases is practically significant, solving a 19,672 × 2,149 breast cancer dataset in seconds, though it requires sparsity levels to exceed the covariance ranks. Key limitations remain: the branch-and-cut algorithm struggles beyond ~120×120 full-rank instances, and for small sparsity on large data only approximation algorithms are viable. Open questions include whether the combinatorial structure admits stronger approximation guarantees, how the framework extends to multiple canonical pairs (sketched in Appendix F), and whether the low-rank condition can be relaxed to cover more realistic high-dimensional settings.
