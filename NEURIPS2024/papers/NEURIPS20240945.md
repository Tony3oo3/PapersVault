## Problem

The standard déjà vu memorization test for representation learning models requires training two separate models on disjoint splits of the training data, making it infeasible to apply to large pre-trained open-source models that were trained on internet-scale datasets. This paper addresses the gap by showing that simpler reference models can approximate dataset-level correlations well enough to measure memorization in a single pre-trained model without any retraining.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240945/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

For image models, the method takes a background crop (foreground object removed) and a pre-trained SSL encoder (VICReg, Barlow Twins, DINO), producing a per-sample déjà vu score (the gap between KNN-based foreground prediction accuracy on the encoder's representation and the reference classifier's accuracy) and a population-level memorization rate. For VLMs, the input is a text caption paired with a pre-trained CLIP model, and the output is a population precision gap (PPG) and population recall gap (PRG) measuring how many objects the model recovers beyond what a GTE text-embedding reference model predicts from nearest-neighbor captions. The reference model is trained once per dataset and reused across all target models.

## Method

The core contribution is replacing the second SSL/VLM model in the two-model déjà vu test with a lightweight reference classifier that directly predicts the foreground label (or object set) from the background crop (or caption). For image models, two reference options are proposed: a ResNet50 trained end-to-end on background crops to predict the foreground class, and a Naive Bayes classifier operating on top-K object detections from Grounded-SAM. For VLMs, a pre-trained GTE text embedding model performs k-NN search over a public caption set, and objects are predicted from the images of the nearest-neighbor captions. The déjà vu score is computed as the difference between the target model's prediction accuracy (via KNN on its representations) and the reference model's accuracy, isolating memorization from dataset-level correlations. Validation on ImageNet and Shutterstock shows the one-model test yields aggregate memorization scores closely matching the two-model test, while sample-level agreement is moderate (~40%), reflecting different inductive biases between the reference classifiers and SSL-based KNN.

## Results

For image representation learning on ImageNet, the one-model test (ResNet50 classifier) identifies similar aggregate déjà vu memorization scores as the two-model test (KNN on SSL representations) for VICReg and Barlow Twins, while DINO shows substantially lower memorization in both. For VLMs trained on 40M Shutterstock pairs, the one-model test (GTE text embedding) achieves 0.07 PPG and 0.06 PRG for predicting top-10 objects with 10 nearest neighbors, compared to 0.06 PPG and 0.06 PRG for the two-model test. Pre-trained open-source models (e.g., YFCC15M CLIP) exhibit lower memorization (0.07 PPG/PRG) than models trained on smaller subsets, and pairwise sample-level agreement between reference models is only ~40% for image models and ~84% (Jaccard) for VLMs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the two-model test of Meehan et al. (NeurIPS 2023) for image SSL models, the proposed one-model test (ResNet50 or Naive Bayes classifier) produces closely matching aggregate déjà vu scores for VICReg and Barlow Twins, confirming that the simpler reference classifier captures dataset-level correlations comparably to a second SSL model. The one-model test slightly overestimates memorization at the sample level (only ~40% pairwise agreement between reference models), but ResNet50 agrees with the intersection of all three two-model tests on 86% of top-1 correlated examples. Against the two-model VLM test of Jayaraman et al. (2024), the one-model test using a GTE text embedding model achieves 0.07 PPG vs. 0.06 PPG and 0.06 PRG vs. 0.06 PRG for top-10 objects with 10 NNs, showing a small but consistent overestimation of memorization. The unimodal text-to-text setting (f_t2t) tracks the two-model test more closely (0.068 PPG) than the cross-modal text-to-image setting (f_t2i, 0.081 PPG). Overall, the one-model test is a practical and accurate approximation that enables memorization measurement of pre-trained open-source models where training a second model is infeasible.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the déjà vu memorization framework of Meehan et al. [2023] for self-supervised image models and Jayaraman et al. [2024] for vision-language models, both of which require training two separate models on disjoint data splits to separate true memorization from dataset-level correlations. The key departure is replacing the expensive second model with lightweight reference classifiers (ResNet50, Naive Bayes on object detections) or a pre-trained text embedding model (GTE), making the test applicable to pre-trained open-source models that were previously infeasible to evaluate. The paper also positions itself against Feldman's stability-based memorization, membership inference, and extraction attacks, arguing that its approach is more concrete, training-agnostic, and computationally efficient.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work enables, for the first time, principled memorization measurement of pre-trained open-source representation and vision-language models, which is critical for understanding the privacy implications of widely deployed models. The finding that OSS models memorize less than identically-architected models trained on smaller data subsets is practically reassuring but also raises open questions about how memorization scales with dataset size and whether the gap narrows at internet scale. Limitations include the approximation quality of the reference classifiers relative to the Bayes-optimal correlation, potential annotation noise from object detectors, and the fact that sample-level agreement between different reference methods is only ~40%, suggesting the measurement carries non-trivial uncertainty.
