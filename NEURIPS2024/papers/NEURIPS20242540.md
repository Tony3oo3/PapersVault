## Problem

Pathology vision-language foundation models (e.g., CONCH) extract generic features that contain task-irrelevant information such as background patterns and stain styles, which act as noise and degrade both accuracy and cross-site generalization for specific downstream tasks like cancer subtyping. No prior method provides a lightweight, supervision-free mechanism to adapt these generic features to task-specific needs while preserving the VLM's pretrained knowledge.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242540/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes as input a bag of patch features (512×512 px at 20× magnification) extracted by CONCH's frozen image encoder, along with pre-computed concept anchors derived from CONCH's text encoder using expert-designed prompts (e.g., 'an image of invasive ductal carcinoma'). It outputs enhanced task-specific patch features formed by concatenating the CIB-calibrated feature and the CFI interference vector, which are then fed into any standard MIL aggregator to produce a WSI-level class label. The entire CATE module is plug-and-play and adds only ~3 MB of parameters on top of the base MIL model.

## Method

CATE introduces two concept-driven modules that calibrate generic VLM features using task-specific concept anchors. The Concept-guided Information Bottleneck (CIB) module maximizes predictive information between calibrated features and concept anchors via an InfoNCE lower bound (PIM loss) while minimizing superfluous information by pushing a Gaussian variational distribution (parameterized by an MLP with reparameterization trick) toward a standard Gaussian prior (SIM loss), effectively discarding task-irrelevant details. The Concept-Feature Interference (CFI) module computes cosine similarities between each calibrated feature and all class-specific concept anchors, then maps the resulting similarity vector through a Self-Normalizing Network layer to produce a discriminative interference vector. The final enhanced feature is the concatenation of CIB and CFI outputs, and the overall training objective combines cross-entropy with weighted PIM and SIM losses, requiring no additional supervision beyond the standard WSI-level labels.

## Results

On TCGA-BRCA (N_IND=1), CATE integrated with ABMIL achieves an OOD-AUC of 0.951 (vs. 0.914 baseline, +4.05%) and IND-AUC of 0.998 (vs. 0.963, +3.63%). On TCGA-NSCLC (N_IND=2), CATE-MIL reaches an OOD-AUC of 0.945, surpassing the best baseline (DTFD-MIL at 0.903) by 4.2 percentage points. On TCGA-RCC (N_IND=3), CATE-MIL attains an OOD-AUC of 0.983, outperforming the strongest baseline (DSMIL at 0.977). CATE consistently improves OOD performance across all three cancer types and all tested MIL backbones, with the largest gains observed on BRCA where domain shift is most pronounced.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CATE is evaluated as a plug-in enhancement across five MIL backbones (ABMIL, CLAM, DSMIL, DTFD-MIL, TransMIL) and one feature re-embedding method (R²T-MIL). CATE+ABMIL achieves the highest OOD-AUC (0.951) on BRCA, outperforming R²T-MIL (0.901) by 5.0 points and the original ABMIL (0.914) by 4.1 points. CATE+CLAM shows the largest relative OOD-AUC gain (+4.85%) among all MIL backbones, reaching 0.951. On NSCLC (N_IND=2), CATE-MIL (0.945 OOD-AUC) beats DTFD-MIL (0.903), ABMIL (0.874), CLAM (0.875), DSMIL (0.839), TransMIL (0.790), and R²T-MIL (0.739) by 4.2, 7.1, 7.0, 10.6, 15.5, and 20.6 points respectively. On RCC (N_IND=3), CATE-MIL (0.983) edges out DSMIL (0.977) by 0.6 points and ABMIL (0.973) by 1.0 points. Notably, CATE-MIL shows slightly lower IND-AUC on NSCLC and RCC compared to some baselines, which the authors attribute to CATE's removal of site-specific patterns that inflate in-domain scores but do not reflect true discriminative ability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the pathology vision-language model CONCH [25] and the information bottleneck framework [1], combining them into a task-specific feature calibration paradigm for WSI analysis. It departs from prior feature re-embedding approaches (R2T-MIL [35], text-guided adaptation [41]) by leveraging the inherent image-text consistency of the VLM to generate concept anchors that guide an information-theoretic bottleneck, rather than requiring additional supervision or operating solely at the patch level. The method is positioned as a plug-and-play adapter for existing MIL architectures (ABMIL, CLAM, DSMIL, TransMIL, DTFD-MIL), distinguishing it from end-to-end retraining strategies.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CATE offers a lightweight, supervision-free mechanism to improve OOD generalization of pathology foundation models, which is critical given that different hospitals use different staining protocols and imaging pipelines. The consistent 3–5% OOD AUC gains across three cancer types and multiple MIL backbones make it practically attractive for clinical deployment where domain shift is the primary failure mode. Key limitations include dependence on expert-designed (or LLM-generated) prompts for concept anchors, and the method is currently validated only on classification tasks; extending it to survival prediction or biomarker estimation remains open. The finding that CATE degrades in-domain performance on NSCLC/RCC by removing site-specific shortcuts highlights a fundamental tension between generalization and in-distribution accuracy.
