## Problem

Existing decomposed linear dynamical systems (dLDS) for neural signal modeling suffer from two critical limitations: their cost-based inference (BPDN-DF) propagates noisy point estimates sequentially, producing inconsistent latent variable estimates on signals from the same generative process, and the dynamics model implicitly assumes a single fixed point at the origin, making it unable to represent systems with multiple fixed points or nonstationary drifts. These issues cause unstable inference, poor multi-step generalization, and failure to identify coherent structure in real neural recordings.

## Input / Output

The method consumes high-dimensional neural time-series (M-dimensional observations with M > N, e.g., 40-channel LFP spectrograms or 50-channel multi-channel LFP recordings) and produces a low-dimensional latent state trajectory (N-dimensional), sparse time-varying coefficients over K dynamic operators, and a time-varying offset term. The latent state is decomposed into a fast-dynamics component l_t and a slow-varying offset b_t (estimated via a moving average of window size S), while the dynamics matrix F_t at each time step is a sparse linear combination of K learned dynamic operators. All inference is unsupervised, requiring only the observed signal and hyperparameters (S, ξ, sparsity threshold η).

## Method

p-dLDS introduces two key innovations over dLDS. First, it replaces the sequential BPDN-DF point-estimate propagation with a probabilistic variational EM (vEM) procedure: a hierarchical coefficient transition density combines sparsity (Gaussian shrinkage toward zero with variance γ_{t,k}) and smoothness (Gaussian shrinkage toward the previous coefficient with variance σ_{t-1,k}²) in a single product, with inverse-gamma hyperpriors parameterized by the squared previous coefficient value (yielding a χ²-structured prior). The latent state posterior is computed efficiently via the Kalman smoother conditioned on the offset estimate, while coefficient posteriors are updated via SGD over the ELBO, initialized with SBL-DF. Second, a time-varying offset term b_t is added to the dynamics, with the latent state decomposed as x_t = l_t + b_t; Lemma 1 shows that without this decomposition the offset absorbs all dynamics (F_t → 0), so the additive split with b_t following a random walk and l_t following the decomposed linear dynamics prevents this degenerate solution. The offset is estimated as a moving average of the latent state, enabling the model to track multiple fixed points and nonstationary drifts while the dynamic operators capture local linear structure.

## Results

On the NASCAR synthetic system, p-dLDS achieves a dynamics MSE of 0.033×10⁻³ (vs. 0.0995 for SLDS, 0.1065 for rSLDS, and 123.19 for dLDS) and a 100-step R² of 0.450, while dLDS diverges entirely. On the Lorenz system, p-dLDS achieves a dynamics MSE of 0.141 (vs. 0.431 for SLDS, 0.304 for rSLDS, 1.123 for dLDS) and a 100-step R² of 0.418, whereas all baselines produce negative R² values or diverge. On the empirically-derived reaching task, p-dLDS reaches 42.31% top-1 and 70.51% top-3 reach-direction classification accuracy, outperforming SLDS (38.46%/57.69%) and dramatically exceeding rSLDS (12.82%/32.05%) and dLDS (10.25%/39.74%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

p-dLDS outperforms all three baselines (SLDS, rSLDS, dLDS) on every metric across all four evaluation settings. Against dLDS, the improvement is most dramatic: on NASCAR, p-dLDS reduces dynamics MSE from 123.19 to 0.033 (a ~3,700× reduction) and achieves a positive 100-step R² of 0.450 where dLDS diverges to −∞; on Lorenz, p-dLDS reduces dynamics MSE from 1.123 to 0.141 and again achieves positive multi-step R² where dLDS diverges. Against rSLDS, p-dLDS improves NASCAR dynamics MSE from 0.1065 to 0.033 and switch MSE from 13.17 to 7.34, and on the reaching task it achieves 42.31% top-1 accuracy versus rSLDS's 12.82% (a ~30-point gap) with dynamics MSE of 0.0567×10⁻² versus rSLDS's 292.41×10⁻². Against SLDS, p-dLDS consistently wins on dynamics MSE and multi-step R², and on the reaching task it achieves 42.31% top-1 accuracy versus SLDS's 38.46%. The only setting where baselines are competitive is the Lorenz switch MSE, where SLDS (0.0204) and rSLDS (0.0208) are close to p-dLDS (0.0137), though p-dLDS still leads.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the decomposed linear dynamical systems (dLDS) framework of Mudrik et al. (JMLR 2024), which approximates nonlinear neural dynamics as a sparse, time-varying mixture of linear regimes inferred via the deterministic BPDN-DF objective. The key departure is replacing this noise-sensitive point-estimate inference with a probabilistic variational EM procedure that marginalizes over temporal uncertainty, drawing on the hierarchical sparse Bayesian structure of SBL-DF (O'Shaughnessy et al., 2019). Additionally, the paper introduces a time-varying offset term to handle multi-fixed-point systems, a capability absent from both dLDS and the earlier discrete switching models (SLDS, rSLDS) that it also benchmarks against.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work addresses a practical fragility in computational neuroscience: deterministic inference for decomposed models accumulates errors over time and fails catastrophically on multi-step prediction (dLDS diverges to −∞ in 100-step R²), limiting scientific interpretability of learned latent dynamics. The probabilistic formulation and offset term make the model robust to dynamical noise and nonlinearity, and the clinical application to subcallosal cingulate cortex LFPs in treatment-resistant depression suggests potential for identifying interpretable neural correlates of emotional processing. Open questions include whether the offset window size can be learned automatically, whether the smoothness assumption in latent space is always appropriate, and whether the single-subject clinical findings generalize across patients.
