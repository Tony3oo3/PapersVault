## Problem

Existing text-to-3D and image-to-3D generation methods produce meshes with overly smooth surfaces or geometric details incorrectly baked into albedo maps, because high-resolution geometric texture data is largely absent from 2D/3D training datasets and humans cannot precisely describe fine surface geometry in natural language. This paper is the first to leverage high-resolution tactile sensing (GelSight) as an additional input modality to supply the missing geometric detail information for 3D generation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240939/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt or a single input image (real or SDXL-generated) plus one or more tactile normal patches captured by a GelSight Mini sensor (21×25 mm sensing area, 240×320 pixels, ~85 μm/pixel resolution). For multi-part generation, multiple tactile patches are paired with a text prompt specifying which object part receives which texture. The output is a 3D mesh (generated by Wonder3D or similar) with a refined albedo UV map and a high-fidelity tactile normal map, both stored in a multi-resolution hash-grid 3D texture field that can be rendered at arbitrary viewpoints.

## Method

The core contribution is a lightweight 3D texture field (multi-resolution hash grid) that jointly represents albedo and tangent-space tactile normals, optimized with a two-stage loss schedule: first 150 iterations of matching losses (visual L_VM against the base mesh albedo, tactile L_TM against an Image-Quilting-synthesized reference normal map), then 50 iterations of diffusion-guided refinement. The visual guidance loss L_VG uses a normal-conditioned ControlNet (Stable Diffusion backbone) to denoise rendered color images, ensuring the refined albedo stays consistent with the geometric normals; the tactile guidance loss L_TG uses a per-texture Texture DreamBooth (SD fine-tuned via LoRA on the tactile normal patches) to denoise rendered normal maps, pulling them toward the physical texture distribution. A key design choice is sampling distinct camera poses per modality—perspective orbiting views for visual supervision and close-range orthographic views along vertex normals for tactile supervision—to handle the large scale discrepancy between vision and touch. For multi-part objects, diffusion self-attention and cross-attention maps (following DiffSeg) segment rendered views into parts, which are integrated into a 3D label field via cross-entropy supervision, enabling region-specific texture assignment without manual annotation.

## Results

The paper evaluates via a human perceptual study on AMTurk with 1,000 paired responses, measuring user preference for texture appearance and geometric details. Tactile DreamFusion is preferred over DreamGaussian 85.43% of the time for texture and 92.85% for geometry, over Wonder3D 86.36% for texture and 88.07% for geometry, and over DreamCraft3D 61.54% for texture and 84.20% for geometry. The method achieves the largest margin on geometric details, confirming that tactile sensing primarily enhances fine-grained surface geometry rather than overall color appearance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DreamGaussian (3D Gaussian splatting with multi-step denoising refinement), Tactile DreamFusion wins decisively on both texture (85.43%) and geometry (92.85%), with the geometry gap being the largest in the paper — DreamGaussian's Gaussian primitives inherently smooth out fine surface details. Against Wonder3D (cross-domain diffusion for multi-view RGB+normal generation), the method wins on texture (86.36%) and geometry (88.07%); Wonder3D's geometry fusion tends to produce overly smooth surfaces lacking millimeter-scale bumps. Against DreamCraft3D (hierarchical neural field + DMTet with DreamBooth texture boosting), the texture comparison is closest at 61.54%, reflecting that DreamCraft3D's multi-view texture refinement produces reasonable color appearance, but the geometry gap (84.20%) reveals its inability to capture high-resolution tactile-scale details. Overall, the tactile modality provides the clearest advantage on geometric fidelity across all three baselines, while the visual-tactile alignment (enforced by ControlNet-conditioned visual guidance) gives a secondary but consistent edge on texture realism.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the DreamFusion-style score distillation paradigm by replacing single-step SDS with multi-step denoising and introducing a normal-conditioned ControlNet to enforce cross-modal alignment between visual and tactile textures. It builds on Wonder3D for base mesh generation and repurposes DreamBooth/LoRA fine-tuning (as 'TextureDreambooth') to capture the distribution of tactile normal maps, while the 3D texture field with multi-resolution hash encoding follows the representation choices in recent mesh-based 3D generation work. The key conceptual departure is treating high-resolution tactile sensing (GelSight) as a first-class input modality for 3D generation, a direction not explored in prior text-to-3D or image-to-3D literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that millimeter-scale geometric details from a physical tactile sensor can be transferred into generated 3D assets with coherent visual-tactile alignment, this work opens a new modality channel for 3D generation that purely computational 2D priors cannot replicate. The multi-part texture pipeline and the TouchTexture dataset (18 objects) provide a practical starting point, but the approach is constrained by the need for physical object access, the small dataset size, and dependence on the quality of the base mesh generator. Open questions include whether tactile data can be synthesized or estimated from images, how the method scales to hundreds of texture types, and whether the UV-seam artifacts can be eliminated.
