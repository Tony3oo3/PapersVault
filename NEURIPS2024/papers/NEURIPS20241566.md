## Problem

Existing steering vectors for LLMs (e.g., CAA) are extracted as activation differences on contrastive prompts, but the model's actual generation frequently diverges from the prompted direction, causing the vector to misrepresent the target behavior. This inconsistency is especially severe in alignment-critical scenarios such as jailbreaking, where the steered completion contradicts the appended answer across nearly all training samples, rendering the vector ineffective.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241566/cropped_page6_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

During training, the method consumes contrastive preference triplets (question, target-behavior response, opposite-behavior response) and optimizes a single steering vector of dimension equal to the model's hidden size (4096 for 7B, 5120 for 13B) at one chosen transformer layer. At inference, the vector is added to the residual-stream activations at every token position, scaled by a user-chosen multiplier that controls both direction (positive/negative) and intensity (magnitude), producing steered text generation without any weight modification.

## Method

BiPO formulates a DPO-style logistic loss over the log-likelihood ratio of target vs. opposite responses, but the learnable parameter is a single steering vector v injected into layer-L activations rather than model weights, so only one model is needed. A key innovation is the bi-directional objective: at each step a random sign d ∈ {−1, 1} is sampled, and the loss is evaluated with d·v applied, ensuring that both v and −v accurately encode their respective behaviors and widening the controllable steering range. The vector is optimized with AdamW (lr 5e-4, β=0.1) for a small number of epochs on a single A100, requiring only ~6×10⁻⁵ of the model's parameters. Because the vector is applied additively at a single layer, multiple behavior-specific vectors can be summed for synergistic multi-behavior steering, and the same vector transfers across architecturally identical models (e.g., Llama-2 → Vicuna) and LoRA-fine-tuned variants without re-optimization.

## Results

On the jailbreaking task (AdvBench), BiPO achieves a 73% Attack Success Rate for facilitating jailbreaks on Llama-2-7b-chat-hf, while both CAA and Freeform baselines achieve 0%. For defense against GCG adversarial suffixes, BiPO reduces ASR from 16% (initial) to 0%, compared to 16% for CAA and 3% for Freeform. On AI persona steering (GPT-4 1–4 scale), BiPO achieves a broader steering range: e.g., Power-seeking scores 1.08 at −2×v* to 2.88 at +2×v*, versus 1.25–2.66 for uni-directional optimization. MMLU accuracy remains essentially unchanged (±0.005) across all steering multipliers, confirming no utility degradation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BiPO decisively outperforms both baselines on the jailbreaking task: where CAA and Freeform both achieve 0% ASR for facilitating jailbreaks, BiPO reaches 73%; for defending against GCG attacks, BiPO drops ASR to 0% versus 16% (CAA) and 3% (Freeform). On AI persona steering, BiPO provides a wider controllable range (e.g., Power-seeking: 1.08–2.88 vs. baselines' narrower bands), and the paper attributes the baselines' failures to an inconsistency between the prompted choice and the model's actual completion. On TruthfulQA and hallucination tasks, both CAA and Freeform show minimal steering effect, while BiPO substantially improves truthfulness and can bidirectionally control hallucination. The baselines' core weakness—extracting vectors from activation differences at the appended-choice token—means the vector does not accurately encode the target generation behavior, a gap that BiPO's preference-optimization objective directly addresses.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of activation engineering (steering vectors à la CAA and Freeform) and preference optimization (DPO, SLiC). Prior steering-vector methods extract vectors from activation differences on contrastive prompts, implicitly assuming the model's generation follows the prompted direction; this paper identifies that assumption as broken, especially in alignment-sensitive scenarios, and instead repurposes the DPO loss to directly optimize a steering vector that modulates generation probability. The bi-directional training objective (randomly flipping the vector's sign during optimization) is a novel twist that enforces the vector to encode both the target and opposite behavior directions simultaneously.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method is extremely lightweight (a single ~4096-dim vector, <30 min training on one A100) yet achieves strong steering across personas, truthfulness, hallucination, and jailbreaking, with demonstrated transferability across architectures and LoRA-adapted models. The 73% jailbreak ASR (vs. 0% for baselines) and 0% defense ASR against GCG attacks highlight a practical safety concern: a tiny activation perturbation can fully bypass or restore alignment. Key limitations include the single-layer design, the need for hand-constructed preference pairs (hard for tasks like math), and evaluation limited to 7B-scale models; multi-layer steering and broader task coverage remain open.
