## Problem

Existing meta-learning methods rely on preselected prior pdfs (e.g., Gaussian) with fixed shapes, limiting their expressiveness when tasks have multimodal or asymmetric parameter distributions. This constraint becomes particularly detrimental in extremely data-scarce few-shot settings where the prior must carry most of the inductive signal. No prior method could learn a data-driven prior that dynamically adapts its form to optimally fit the collection of related tasks.

## Input / Output

The method consumes M-class × N-shot labeled support sets per task (84×84 RGB images) and unlabeled test images, along with the learned NCoV transformation parameters θ_f. Rather than directly producing model parameters, it outputs task-specific parameters φ_t = f(z_t; θ_f) obtained by transforming a latent vector z_t, which is then used to classify test samples. The prior pdf p_{Z'}(z') = ∫ p_Z(z)δ[z'−f(z)]dz has no closed form due to non-injectivity, so the method operates entirely in latent space during optimization.

## Method

MetaNCoV replaces the prespecified prior with a non-injective Sylvester change-of-variable (NCoV) transformation f(Z;θ_f) = Z + Aσ(BZ+c) that maps a known source distribution (e.g., standard Gaussian) to an arbitrary target prior over model parameters. Theorem 3.1 (Multivariate PIT) guarantees the existence of such a non-injective mapping to any target cdf, and Theorem 3.5 proves that a sufficiently wide one-layer Sylvester NCoV universally approximates the optimal transformation. The bilevel MetaNCoV algorithm (Algorithm 1) optimizes latent vectors z_t via K-step GD in the inner loop and updates θ_f via meta-gradients on validation loss in the outer loop, with the task-invariant initialization z_t^0 automatically derived as the MAP of the source distribution (e.g., z_t^0 = 0 for N(0,I)), eliminating the need to separately learn an initialization. The NCoV serves as a plug-in prior module compatible with different task-level optimizers (MAML, MetaSGD, Meta-Curvature), and its non-injectivity provides strictly greater expressiveness than injective normalizing flows, especially for distributions without full support.

## Results

On the 5-class miniImageNet benchmark with a 4-block CNN backbone, MetaSGD+MetaNCoV achieves 59.10% ± 1.52 (1-shot) and 71.48% ± 0.68 (5-shot) classification accuracy, outperforming all 10 baselines. The 1-shot gain is particularly pronounced: MetaNCoV improves over MAML (48.70%) by over 10 percentage points and over the strongest baseline MeTAL (52.63%) by ~6.5 points. On CUB-200-2011, MetaSGD+MetaNCoV reaches 69.94% (1-shot) and 80.54% (5-shot), surpassing the best prior method MattML (66.29% / 80.34%). In cross-domain transfer (trained on miniImageNet, tested on TieredImageNet, CUB, and Cars), MetaNCoV consistently leads all baselines, with the largest margins in the 1-shot regime (e.g., 61.50% vs. 58.56% for GAP on TieredImageNet).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MetaNCoV is evaluated as a plug-in prior against 19 baselines spanning NN-based (Meta-LSTM), optimization-based (MAML, MetaSGD, MC, Warp-MAML, MAML+L2F, MeTAL, Minimax-MAML), closed-form (R2D2), metric-learning (MatchingNet, ProtoNet, RelationNet, DN4), and cross-domain (ANIL, BOIL, SparseMAML+, GAP) methods. On miniImageNet (Table 1), MetaSGD+MetaNCoV (59.10% 1-shot, 71.48% 5-shot) beats every baseline, with the largest margins over MAML (+10.4 pts 1-shot) and MetaSGD (+8.6 pts 1-shot), confirming that the data-driven NCoV prior substantially outperforms prespecified Gaussian or degenerate priors. On CUB-200-2011 (Table 3), MetaNCoV surpasses the strongest meta-learning baseline MattML by ~3.6 pts in 1-shot (69.94% vs. 66.29%) while matching its 5-shot accuracy (80.54% vs. 80.34%), and dominates all metric-learning methods by 10+ points in 1-shot. In the cross-domain setting (Table 5), MetaNCoV leads GAP (the strongest cross-domain baseline) by 2.9 pts on TieredImageNet 1-shot, 3.1 pts on CUB 1-shot, and 3.2 pts on Cars 1-shot, demonstrating that the learned prior generalizes across domains without overfitting. The ablation (Table 4) further shows that the non-injective NCoV outperforms its injective NF counterpart by ~2.4 pts (1-shot), directly attributing the gain to the relaxed injectivity constraint.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the meta-learning framework of MAML and its optimization-based successors (MetaSGD, Meta-Curvature) by replacing their preselected prior pdfs (Gaussian, diagonal Gaussian, block-diagonal Gaussian) with a data-driven prior learned via a non-injective change-of-variable (NCoV) model. The NCoV architecture directly adopts the Sylvester normalizing flow structure but deliberately waives the injectivity constraint that is fundamental to conventional normalizing flows, enabling the model to approximate arbitrary target pdfs including those without full support. The theoretical foundation builds on the univariate probability integral transform, generalizing it to the multivariate case (Theorem 3.1) and proving universal approximation via sufficiently wide Sylvester NCoVs (Theorem 3.5). As a plug-in prior module, MetaNCoV is compatible with existing task-level optimizers (MAML, MetaSGD, MC) without requiring architectural changes to the backbone model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine limitation in meta-learning: preselected priors like Gaussians are inherently unimodal and symmetric, which may be poorly suited for tasks with multimodal or asymmetric parameter distributions. The empirical gains are most pronounced in the 1-shot regime (e.g., ~5% over the best baseline on miniImageNet), supporting the claim that expressive priors matter most when data is exceedingly scarce. Key limitations include the intractability of the induced pdf (no closed-form expression when f is non-injective), which precludes standard maximum-likelihood or ELBO-based training and necessitates latent-space optimization. Open questions flagged by the authors include bilevel convergence analysis, more general universal approximation theorems beyond Sylvester NCoVs, and whether the approach extends to domains requiring tractable density evaluation.
