## Problem

Fine-tuning diffusion models via RL for computational design (DNA/protein sequences, images) typically assumes access to online reward feedback or accurate reward functions, but scientific domains only provide static offline datasets with noisy measurements. In this offline setting, naively optimizing a learned reward model leads to overoptimization: the model exploits uncertain out-of-distribution regions, producing adversarial or invalid designs rather than genuinely high-quality ones. The paper addresses how to combine the extrapolation power of reward models with the validity constraints of pre-trained generative models without falling into this trap.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20244048/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an offline dataset of design-reward pairs (e.g., 200-nt DNA enhancer sequences with MPRA activity scores, 50-nt 5'UTR sequences with ribosomal load, or 255K AVA images with aesthetic scores) and a pre-trained diffusion model (Dirichlet diffusion for simplex-space sequences, Stable Diffusion v1.5 for 512×512 images). It produces a fine-tuned diffusion model whose generated samples achieve higher true rewards than any design in the offline data while remaining within the valid design space. For image generation, a text prompt conditions the pre-trained model, and an LLaVA-based check ensures generated images still match the prompt.

## Method

BRAID (douBly conseRvAitive fine-tuning diffusIon moDels) is a two-stage procedure. First, a conservative reward model r̂ − ĝ is trained on offline data, where ĝ is a calibrated uncertainty oracle (Gaussian process confidence interval or bootstrap ensemble minimum) that inflates penalties in regions poorly covered by offline data. Second, the pre-trained diffusion model is fine-tuned by solving a soft-entropy-regularized MDP planning problem: each denoising step is a policy, the terminal reward is the conservative reward r̂ − ĝ, and a KL penalty at every step anchors the fine-tuned policies to the pre-trained ones. Theorem 1 proves the optimal policy induces exactly the doubly conservative distribution exp((r̂−ĝ)/α)·p_pre / Z, and Theorem 2 provides a regret bound showing the method provably outperforms the best offline design when the reward model is well-specified. Training uses direct backpropagation through the full diffusion chain rather than policy gradients, making it computationally efficient and stable.

## Results

The paper evaluates on three domains: Enhancers (DNA, length 200), UTRs (RNA, length 50), and AVA (images). Across all three, BRAID (both Bonus and Bootstrap variants) consistently achieves the highest true reward r(x) among all methods, outperforming STRL, DDOM, and Offline Guidance. In the image generation task, STRL suffers severe overoptimization: by checkpoint 4, 389/400 generated images are invalid (do not match the prompt), dropping the mean LLaVA-adjusted score from 6.87 (ckpt-2) to 0.20, while BRAID maintains valid, high-reward generations. The paper reports results primarily as reward histograms (Figure 2) rather than a single numerical table.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BRAID outperforms all three baselines on every dataset. Against STRL (standard RL fine-tuning without conservatism), BRAID achieves higher true rewards on Enhancers, UTRs, and Images, because the conservative reward penalty (r̂ − ĝ) prevents the model from exploiting high-uncertainty OOD regions where the learned reward is unreliable. Against DDOM (Krishnamoorthy et al., 2023), which trains a diffusion model from scratch with classifier-free guidance conditioned on top-5% reward classes, BRAID wins because RL-based fine-tuning can extrapolate beyond the best offline reward, while conditioning-based methods are inherently capped at the conditioned value. Against Offline Guidance (Yuan et al., 2023), which applies classifier guidance on a pre-trained diffusion model, BRAID similarly outperforms for the same extrapolation reason. The gap between BRAID and STRL is most dramatic in image generation, where STRL's lack of conservatism leads to prompt-ignoring mode collapse (389/400 invalid at ckpt-4), whereas BRAID's KL penalty and uncertainty penalty keep generations within the valid design space while still improving aesthetic scores.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously separate research threads: RL-based fine-tuning of diffusion models (Black et al. 2023, Fan et al. 2023, Clark et al. 2023) and offline model-based optimization with conservative reward modeling (MOPO, CQL, MOReL). Prior diffusion fine-tuning work assumes online reward queries or known reward functions, while offline MBO methods lack the capacity to generate designs within complex valid spaces (e.g., natural images, foldable proteins). BRAID's key departure is the 'doubly conservative' formulation—combining an uncertainty-based penalty on the reward (from offline RL pessimism) with a KL penalty against a pretrained diffusion model (from generative modeling)—and framing the entire fine-tuning procedure as soft-entropy-regularized MDP planning, which yields a clean analytical characterization (Theorem 1) connecting the optimized policies to a closed-form target distribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically critical gap in scientific design (DNA/protein sequences, images) where experimental feedback is expensive and only static offline data is available, yet a pretrained generative model capturing the valid design space already exists. The regret guarantee (Theorem 2) provides the first formal justification that conservative fine-tuning can provably outperform the best offline design while avoiding invalid outputs, which is important for trust in AI-driven design pipelines. Limitations include the dependence on a pretrained model that accurately captures the valid space, the need for some online interaction for hyperparameter selection, and the fact that the coverage term in the regret bound can still be large when the comparator distribution diverges significantly from offline data. Open questions include how the approach behaves when the pretrained model only approximately captures the valid space, and whether the two-stage (train reward, then fine-tune) procedure can be made end-to-end.
