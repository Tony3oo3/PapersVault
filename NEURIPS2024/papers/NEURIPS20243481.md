## Problem

Standard dot-product self-attention computes attention weights using Euclidean distance, which is spherically invariant and treats all feature-space dimensions as equally important regardless of direction. This isotropic assumption causes the attention mechanism to act as a high-variance Nadaraya-Watson estimator, leading to representation collapse (hidden states increasingly composed of uninformative noise) and vulnerability to small input perturbations or contaminated samples.

## Input / Output

The method operates on standard transformer inputs—text token sequences for language modeling, patchified RGB images for vision tasks, and long-range sequences (up to 4K tokens) for LRA benchmarks—producing the same outputs as vanilla transformers (next-token logits, class scores, segmentation masks). The only additional computation is a diagonal matrix M derived from value-vector differences between adjacent layers, requiring no extra learnable parameters and negligible memory overhead beyond the standard Q, K, V projections.

## Method

Elliptical Attention replaces the standard dot product q_i^T k_j with q_i^T M k_j in the attention score, where M = diag(m_1, …, m_D) is a diagonal Mahalanobis matrix whose entries estimate the coordinate-wise variability of the underlying self-attention function. The estimator m_i is computed as the empirical mean of |v^{ℓ+1}(i) − v^ℓ(i)|/δ across adjacent layers, a parameter-free forward-Euler approximation of the directional derivative that requires no additional training. This transformation stretches the implicit neighborhood around each query from a hyper-sphere to a hyper-ellipsoid, upweighting keys in directions where the function varies most and downweighting (or ignoring) directions of low variability. Theoretically, this reduces the variance of the NW estimator without introducing bias, which the paper proves simultaneously tightens the Lipschitz error bound under input noise (robustness) and slows the layer-wise attenuation of representations toward a constant (resistance to collapse).

## Results

On ImageNet-1K under adversarial attacks (perturbation budget 1/255), DeiT-Elliptical achieves top-1 accuracy of 44.96% under PGD and 56.55% under SPSA, outperforming all baselines including RVT (43.39% PGD, 51.43% SPSA) and DeiT-MoM (43.78% PGD, 49.38% SPSA). On WikiText-103, Elliptical achieves the best clean perplexity (32.00) and second-best contaminated perplexity (52.59) under 2.5% Word Swap attack. On the LRA benchmark, Elliptical attains the highest average accuracy (59.4%) across five long-range tasks, and on ADE20K segmentation it improves mean IoU by 4.7% over DeiT (37.09% vs. 35.44%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DeiT-Elliptical outperforms the standard DeiT baseline on all three adversarial attacks (FGSM +2.03, PGD +3.12, SPSA +8.21 top-1) while maintaining comparable clean accuracy (72.36% vs. 72.23%). Against the strongest robust baselines, Elliptical beats RVT by 1.57 points on PGD and 5.12 points on SPSA, and beats DeiT-MoM by 1.18 points on PGD and 7.17 points on SPSA, though DeiT-MoM edges out Elliptical on FGSM (55.76 vs. 54.64). On WikiText-103, Elliptical achieves the best clean PPL (32.00) beating Transformer-SPKDE (32.18) and FourierFormer (32.85), and is nearly tied with Transformer-MoM on contaminated PPL (52.59 vs. 52.14). On LRA, Elliptical wins on 4 of 5 tasks (ListOps, Text, Image, Pathfinder) but trails Longformer on Retrieval (80.3 vs. 81.0). When combined with the SOTA robust FAN backbone, FAN-Elliptical further improves sequential Auto Attack accuracy from 33.29% to 34.54% and Square from 56.75% to 58.38%, demonstrating that Elliptical Attention is composable with existing robust methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Nadaraya-Watson regression interpretation of self-attention established by FourierFormer [54] and the robust KDE transformer line [23], which showed that dot-product attention is equivalent to NW regression with isotropic Gaussian kernels. The key departure is replacing the isotropic (spherically symmetric) kernel with a Mahalanobis metric whose diagonal entries are estimated from coordinate-wise variability of the underlying function, adapting the gradient-weighted neighborhood stretching idea from non-parametric regression (Kpotufe & Boularias [29, 30]) to the self-attention setting. Unlike prior Mahalanobis metric learning approaches that require backpropagation or density estimation, the proposed estimator is parameter-free, computed from value differences across adjacent layers, and requires negligible additional memory.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a theoretically grounded, parameter-free drop-in modification to any transformer that simultaneously addresses two previously treated-as-separate concerns—adversarial robustness and representation collapse—by unifying them under the geometry of the attention neighborhood. This is practically appealing because it requires no architectural changes, no additional learnable parameters, and negligible computational overhead, making it easy to adopt. A stated limitation is that the coordinate-wise variability estimator, based on inter-layer value differences, is prone to noise, and the authors identify more precise estimation methods with convergence guarantees as future work. The consistent improvements across scales (5.7M to 388M parameters) and modalities (text, image, segmentation) suggest the approach generalizes well, though the clean-data gains are modest (1–3%) compared to the more substantial robustness improvements.
