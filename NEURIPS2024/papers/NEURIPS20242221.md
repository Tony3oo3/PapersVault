## Problem

Existing human-centric referring expression comprehension (REC) benchmarks, derived by filtering general datasets like RefCOCO for person categories, contain only ~1,500 test images with extremely short annotations (3–9 words average) and limited vocabulary (~2,000–2,900 words). In the era of large multimodal models (LMMs) such as GPT-4V, these benchmarks are too small and too linguistically simple to meaningfully evaluate the full localization and language-comprehension capabilities of modern REC systems.

## Input / Output

Each test instance pairs an RGB image with a multi-sentence referring expression (averaging 93.2 words, ranging 15–241 words) that uniquely identifies one person in the scene. The model must output a bounding box (or a segmentation mask, which is converted to a tight bounding box for evaluation). Uniquely, every sentence within the referring expression carries a subject label from six categories—appearance, human-object interaction, location, action, celebrity, and OCR—enabling subject-specific evaluation protocols beyond a single aggregate accuracy score.

## Method

HC-RefLoCo is constructed through a multi-stage pipeline: (1) person instances are harvested from COCO 2017, Objects365, OpenImage v7, and LAION-5B (for celebrity images), yielding 13,452 images and 24,129 instances; (2) referring expressions are generated in three steps using GPT-4V—first an instance-level description from the cropped person, then a context-expanded description from the full image with the target circled in red, followed by manual review to correct hallucinations; (3) GPT-4 rewrites each expression to double the annotation count to 44,738, with further manual filtering for ambiguity. Each sentence is then manually assigned a subject label. The benchmark introduces three evaluation protocols: multi-threshold accuracy (Acc at IoU 0.5/0.75/0.9 plus mAcc), scale-aware accuracy (small/medium/large instances by √area), and per-subject mAcc, and is evaluated on 24 training-unconstrained models spanning bounding-box and mask-output LMMs.

## Results

On the HC-RefLoCo benchmark (13,452 images, 44,738 annotations), the best-performing model SPHINX-v2-1k achieves mAcc of 71.7% (Acc0.5=84.1%, Acc0.75=77.1%, Acc0.9=56.2%), while SPHINX-MoE-1k follows closely at mAcc=71.4%. Performance spans a wide range: ONE PEACE reaches mAcc=63.1%, Lenna 60.6%, and OFA-Large 58.1%, whereas GPT-4V achieves only mAcc=5.5% (Acc0.5=17.4%), highlighting the substantial challenge posed by long-context referring expressions. Mask-output models (e.g., GlaMM at mAcc=55.0%, PSALM at 51.1%) generally trail the top bounding-box-output models but outperform several smaller LMMs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The benchmark evaluates 24 models, revealing a clear performance hierarchy. SPHINX-v2-1k (mAcc=71.7%) and SPHINX-MoE-1k (71.4%) lead, outperforming the next tier—ONE PEACE (63.1%), SPHINX-1k (63.0%), and Lenna (60.6%)—by roughly 8–9 mAcc points. GPT-4V, despite its strong general multimodal capabilities, achieves only mAcc=5.5%, far below all specialized grounding models, indicating that long-context REC remains a significant challenge even for frontier LMMs. Among mask-output models, GlaMM (mAcc=55.0%) and PSALM (51.1%) are competitive with mid-tier bounding-box models like Qwen-VL (52.8%) and CogVLM (55.8%), but trail the top SPHINX variants by ~17 points. Per-subject analysis shows ONE PEACE excels in celebrity recognition (75.4% mAcc) while SPHINX-v2-1k leads in appearance (72.4%), HOI (73.0%), and OCR (72.3%), suggesting no single model dominates all linguistic sub-tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground in human-centric referring expression comprehension (REC) by constructing a purpose-built benchmark rather than filtering general-purpose datasets (RefCOCO, RefCOCO+, RefCOCOg) as prior HC-RefCOCO variants did. It repurposes the annotation-generation paradigm from GPT-4V-based captioning work [85] to produce long-context referring expressions (93.2 words average vs. 3–9 in prior benchmarks), and introduces sentence-level subject labels (appearance, HOI, location, action, celebrity, OCR) that have no precedent in existing REC benchmarks. The evaluation protocol extends standard IoU-based accuracy with scale-aware and subject-specific metrics, positioning the benchmark as a stress test for the 24 LMM-era grounding models (SPHINX, KOSMOS-2, Qwen-VL, LISA, etc.) that dominate the current REC landscape.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

HC-RefLoCo fills a clear gap: no dedicated human-centric REC benchmark existed that matched the scale and linguistic complexity demanded by modern LMMs, and the prior HC-RefCOCO variants (1,519 images, ~3-word annotations) are effectively saturated by current models. The benchmark's 44,738 annotations across 13,452 images, combined with its multi-protocol evaluation, will likely become a standard reference for tracking progress in human-centric grounding. Limitations include the restriction to six subject categories, potential residual annotation errors despite manual review, and the fact that celebrity recognition relies on a fixed set of 367 individuals. Open questions include whether the observed trade-off between annotation length and model performance (longer context helps strong-LLM models but hurts weaker ones) will persist as language encoders improve, and whether the benchmark can be extended to video or 3D human-centric settings.
