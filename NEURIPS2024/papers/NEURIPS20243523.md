## Problem

Directly applying image diffusion distillation to video diffusion models yields poor frame quality because public video datasets suffer from low resolution, motion blur, and watermarks, degrading both the teacher and the distilled student. This creates a fundamental tension: the distillation objective learns from low-quality video frames while any appearance-enhancement objective targets high-quality image data, and the training trajectories (low-quality) mismatch the inference trajectories (high-quality).
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243523/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a text prompt and produces a 16-frame video at 256×256 (ModelScopeT2V teacher) or 512×512 (AnimateDiff teacher) using as few as 1–8 sampling steps. Optionally, a separate high-quality image dataset (e.g., LAION-aes, anime-style, or 3D-cartoon-style images) is provided during training to steer frame appearance without requiring corresponding video data. The method is implemented as a LoRA adapter (rank 64) on top of a pre-trained video diffusion teacher, so inference requires only the lightweight adapter.

## Method

MCM builds on a video Latent Consistency Model (LCM) augmented with a pixel-space image discriminator, but resolves two key conflicts through two mechanisms. First, disentangled motion consistency distillation (MCD) replaces the standard consistency loss with one applied only to a learned motion representation (a two-layer MLP head on the LCM output), so the distillation objective enforces temporal consistency on motion while the adversarial loss independently shapes frame appearance toward the high-quality image distribution. Second, mixed trajectory distillation simulates the inference-time PF-ODE trajectory by running a single-step student generation from pure noise, re-noising the result, and mixing these generated high-quality trajectories with real low-quality video trajectories (weighted by λ_real=0.5) for the consistency loss, thereby closing the train–inference gap and preventing mode collapse that occurs when only generated trajectories are used.

## Results

On WebVid mini with ModelScopeT2V as teacher, MCM achieves FVD of 526 at 1 step, 450 at 2 steps, and 456 at 4 steps, substantially outperforming LCM (854/658/603) and DDIM (7231/2309/1229). With AnimateDiff as teacher, MCM reaches FVD of 1025 at 1 step and 827 at 4 steps, beating LCM (1242/1006) and AnimateLCM (1575/998). CLIPSIM scores are also competitive or best among all methods at 1–4 steps (e.g., 30.36 at 1 step with AnimateDiff vs. 27.32 for LCM). On the zero-shot MSRVTT benchmark, MCM similarly leads with FVD of 501 at 1 step (ModelScopeT2V) and 1197 at 1 step (AnimateDiff).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MCM outperforms all five baselines on both FVD and CLIPSIM at 1, 2, and 4 sampling steps across both teacher models. Against the training-free samplers, MCM reduces FVD by over 85% at 1 step with ModelScopeT2V (526 vs. 7231 for DDIM, 2030 for DPM++). Among trainable distillation methods, MCM beats LCM by 38% FVD at 1 step (526 vs. 854) and by 25% at 4 steps (456 vs. 603) with ModelScopeT2V; with AnimateDiff, MCM improves over LCM by 17% at 1 step (1025 vs. 1242) and 18% at 4 steps (827 vs. 1006). MCM also surpasses AnimateLCM (FVD@1: 526 vs. 1575 for AnimateDiff; 450 vs. 1333 at 2 steps) and AnimateDiff-Lightning (FVD@1: 1025 vs. 1288) consistently. The only metric where MCM does not lead is CLIPSIM@8 with ModelScopeT2V (29.58 vs. 30.73 for LCM), suggesting a slight trade-off in prompt alignment at higher step counts.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the consistency model (CM/LCM) framework for few-step diffusion sampling and the adversarial diffusion distillation (ADD) paradigm for frame quality, combining them into a single-stage video distillation pipeline. It departs from prior two-stage approaches (e.g., AnimateLCM's spatial-then-temporal training) by jointly learning motion and appearance in one end-to-end stage, and from standard LCM by restricting the consistency objective to a disentangled motion representation rather than the full latent. The mixed trajectory distillation idea—simulating inference-time ODE trajectories from the student's own single-step output to bridge the training-inference gap—has no direct precedent in the consistency distillation literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MCM addresses a practical bottleneck in video diffusion: public video datasets are inherently lower quality than image datasets, and this degrades both teacher and student models. The disentangled motion-appearance formulation offers a clean, reusable recipe for any setting where the temporal signal and spatial appearance come from different-quality sources. Limitations include sensitivity to training data distribution, the need for a separate image dataset for quality improvement, and the acknowledged deepfake risk. Open questions include whether the approach scales to longer/higher-resolution videos, whether the learnable motion head generalizes across architectures, and whether the mixed trajectory strategy can be extended to multi-step consistency distillation.
