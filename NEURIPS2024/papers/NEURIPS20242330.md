## Problem

Existing VLA models face a fundamental tension in open-world environments: text-only goal communication between a language model and a separate controller cannot adequately express complex, highly contextualized tasks, while emitting low-level control commands directly from a large VLA model is impractical for long-horizon tasks due to prohibitive context length and inference cost. The paper identifies that tokenizing behavior trajectories (actions) into a compact, semantically meaningful representation compatible with pretrained multimodal language models remains an unsolved problem, as raw actions are too long for sequence modeling and language-level action descriptions require excessive supervision.

## Input / Output

The model receives a task instruction (free-form text), a running memory summary (text), and a 128×128 pixel observation image, and produces a chain-of-thought reasoning trace followed by 5 discrete behavior tokens (drawn from an FSQ codebook of 15,360 codewords) that are fed to a separate causal policy decoder to generate per-frame motor actions. The model is forced to re-reason and emit new behavior tokens every 32 environment steps, creating a hierarchical loop where high-level reasoning operates at a much lower frequency than low-level control. The interaction data is structured as a fixed ordering of segments: instruction → memory → (observation → thought → behavior)*, with the first three segment types serving as a prefix and the thought/behavior segments predicted autoregressively.

## Method

OmniJARVIS introduces a self-supervised behavior tokenizer that replaces GROOT's continuous Gaussian latent with Finite Scalar Quantization (FSQ) at levels [8,8,8,6,5], producing 5 discrete tokens per 128-frame behavior trajectory; the encoder is a bidirectional transformer and the decoder is a causal transformer trained with an imitation-learning objective. These 35 FSQ-level tokens are appended to the vocabulary of a pretrained LLaVA-7B multimodal language model, and the full multimodal interaction sequence (vision tokens, language tokens, behavior tokens) is modeled with a prefix language modeling objective where instruction, memory, and observation form the prefix while thought and behavior tokens are predicted autoregressively. Training is two-stage: first the behavior tokenizer is trained self-supervisedly on the OpenAI Contractor Dataset, then the augmented MLM is SFT'd on ~1T tokens comprising 600k augmented trajectories (with LLM-synthesized instructions, memories, and thoughts) and 300k Minecraft QA conversations. The key architectural insight is that discrete, semantically grounded behavior tokens bridge the gap between the language model's reasoning/planning capability and the policy decoder's real-time motor control, enabling end-to-end inference without a separate language-conditioned controller.

## Results

On long-horizon programmatic tasks in Minecraft, OmniJARVIS achieves a 59% average success rate, substantially outperforming DEPS (43%), ReAct (23%), GPT zero-shot planner (15%), GROOT (2%), and STEVE-I (1%). On open-ended instruction following, OmniJARVIS attains the best FSD score of 886.2 (lower is better), beating DEPS (929.5) and Voyager (932.1). On embodied question answering, OmniJARVIS scores 8.40 (LLM-as-judge), surpassing GPT-3.5 (7.50) and LLaMA2-70B (2.50). On short-horizon atomic tasks, OmniJARVIS achieves competitive or best rewards (e.g., 25.8 on mining stones vs. 19.0 for GROOT).

## Baselines & Comparisons

OmniJARVIS decisively outperforms all baselines on programmatic tasks, achieving 59% average success rate versus 43% for DEPS (the strongest language-based planner), 23% for ReAct, 15% for GPT zero-shot, and near-zero for native-action methods STEVE-I (1%) and GROOT (2%). The gap is most pronounced on the Food group (44% vs. 12% for DEPS/ReAct), which the authors attribute to language-conditioned tokenizers requiring additional language supervision that was scarce during STEVE-I's training. On open-ended instruction following (FSD, lower is better), OmniJARVIS achieves 886.2, beating DEPS (929.5), Voyager (932.1), STEVE-I (972.7), and VPT (975.9). On embodied QA, OmniJARVIS (8.40) surpasses GPT-3.5 (7.50) and all open-source LLM baselines (Vicuna-7B: 2.34, LLaMA2-70B: 2.50). On atomic tasks, OmniJARVIS matches or exceeds GROOT on most sub-tasks (e.g., 25.8 vs. 19.0 on mining stones) while using language conditioning rather than video conditioning.

## Relation to Prior Work

OmniJARVIS carves a third path between the two dominant VLA architectures: (1) MLMs that emit textual goals to separate goal-conditioned controllers (DEPS, JARVIS-1, Voyager) and (2) MLMs that directly output low-level control commands (RT-2, LEO). It builds on GROOT's VAE-based behavior trajectory encoding but replaces the continuous Gaussian latent with discrete Finite Scalar Quantization (FSQ) codes, enabling the behavior tokens to be injected into a pretrained MLM's vocabulary (LLaVA-7B) for unified autoregressive modeling. The data pipeline repurposes OpenAI's Contractor Dataset and synthesizes instructions, memories, and thoughts via GPT-3.5, following the thought-cloning paradigm from Hu & Clune (2024).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a core tension in VLA models: large models needed for reasoning are too slow for real-time control, while small controllers lack planning ability. The hierarchical design (MLM reasons at ~3Hz, policy decoder acts at 20Hz) with self-supervised discrete tokens as the interface is a practical resolution that avoids the language bottleneck of goal-based systems. Open questions include whether the behavior tokenizer generalizes beyond Minecraft (the Montezuma's Revenge transfer is promising but limited), how language capabilities degrade after VLA fine-tuning, and whether the FSQ codebook can scale to more complex action spaces. The demonstrated scaling laws (log-linear loss decrease with data and model size) suggest the approach has headroom, but the current 0.59 average success rate on programmatic tasks still leaves substantial room for improvement on harder tasks (diamond group: 0.08).
