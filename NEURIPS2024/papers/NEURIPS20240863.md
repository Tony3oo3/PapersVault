## Problem

Current LM-based recommenders fine-tune language models with standard language modeling loss (next-token prediction), which is not designed for personalized ranking and ignores negative items entirely. This training paradigm fails to fully leverage user preference data and does not explicitly optimize the model to distinguish preferred items from dispreferred ones, hindering recommendation performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240863/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt x_u encoding the user's chronological interaction history (item titles) and a candidate item set C, and produces the title of the preferred item as a free-text response. During training, each prompt is paired with one positive item title and K randomly sampled negative item titles to form multi-negative preference data. Evaluation uses a re-ranking protocol where 20 non-interacted items are randomly drawn alongside the ground-truth item, and performance is measured by HitRatio@1 and a Valid Ratio indicating whether the model outputs a valid item title from the candidate set.

## Method

S-DPO extends DPO from the pairwise Bradley-Terry model to the Plackett-Luce model generalized from full rankings to partial rankings, yielding a softmax-structured loss over multiple negatives. The training pipeline has two stages: supervised fine-tuning to inject domain knowledge, followed by preference alignment where the S-DPO loss explicitly compares the preferred item against K dispreferred items using implicit reward ratios π_θ/π_ref. Theoretically, S-DPO bridges to the softmax loss over negative sampling in self-supervised recommendation, and gradient analysis reveals an inherent hard-negative mining mechanism where negatives with higher implicit rewards receive proportionally larger gradient updates. When K=1, S-DPO reduces exactly to standard DPO, making it a strict generalization. The approach also achieves lower computational complexity than naively applying DPO to K negatives, scaling as Θ((K+1)(C_M+1)S_t) versus Θ(2KC_M S_t).

## Results

S-DPO achieves HR@1 of 0.6609 (Goodreads), 0.6628 (LastFM), and 0.5263 (MovieLens), outperforming the strongest baseline LLaRA by 24.89%, 47.03%, and 11.10% respectively. The Valid Ratio remains above 0.989 across all three datasets, confirming that the preference alignment stage does not degrade the LM's instruction-following ability. Compared to traditional recommenders (GRU4Rec, Caser, SASRec), S-DPO yields relative improvements ranging from 36.31% to 196.82% in HR@1.

## Baselines & Comparisons

S-DPO outperforms all nine baselines on every dataset. Against the strongest LM-based baseline LLaRA (which integrates collaborative signals into SFT), S-DPO gains +0.1317 HR@1 on Goodreads (0.6609 vs. 0.5292), +0.2120 on LastFM (0.6628 vs. 0.4508), and +0.0526 on MovieLens (0.5263 vs. 0.4737). TALLRec, which relies solely on SFT with language modeling loss, is surpassed by S-DPO by +0.1626 (Goodreads), +0.2448 (LastFM), and +0.1368 (MovieLens). Traditional sequential recommenders (GRU4Rec, Caser, SASRec) all fall well below S-DPO, with the largest gap on LastFM where S-DPO achieves 0.6628 versus at most 0.2616. Untuned LLaMA2 and ChatRec perform poorly due to inadequate instruction-following (Valid Ratio as low as 0.34–0.44), while MoRec underperforms its traditional SASRec backbone because it leaves the LM's reasoning ability unused. The ablation in Figure 2a further confirms that DPO with a single negative (0.6342 on LastFM) is inferior to S-DPO with multiple negatives (0.6628), validating the benefit of the softmax ranking loss over pairwise preference optimization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends DPO (Rafailov et al., 2023) from its pairwise Bradley-Terry formulation to a multi-negative Plackett-Luce preference model, generalizing from full rankings to the partial rankings natural in recommendation. It follows the RLHF pipeline structure (SFT + preference alignment) established by Ouyang et al. (2022) but replaces the RL phase with a direct softmax-based loss. The key departure from prior LM-based recommenders (TALLRec, LLaRA, ChatRec) is that those methods optimize a language modeling (next-token) loss that ignores negative items entirely, whereas S-DPO explicitly incorporates multiple negatives and connects the resulting loss to the softmax/contrastive loss long used in traditional recommendation (Rendle, 2022; Wu et al., 2021).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

S-DPO provides the first principled ranking-aware training objective for LM-based recommenders, closing a gap where prior work adapted item representations or prompt formats but left the optimization misaligned with the ranking task. The theoretical bridge to softmax loss and the inherent hard-negative-mining property give practitioners a clear justification for why multiple negatives help, beyond empirical observation. Open questions include whether the benefits scale beyond 15 negatives (the paper's cap due to compute), whether the approach transfers to other preference-alignment domains beyond recommendation, and how to handle the growing computational cost of scoring many candidate item titles through a 7B LLM.
