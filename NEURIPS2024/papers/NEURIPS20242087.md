## Problem

Existing calibration error (CE) estimators either require labeled target data or are derived under the covariate shift assumption, leaving a gap for the label shift setting where the marginal label distribution p(Y) changes between source and target while p(X|Y) remains constant. In practice, target labels are often unavailable or prohibitively expensive (e.g., medical diagnostics during disease outbreaks), making it impossible to estimate or correct miscalibration on the shifted target domain. This paper addresses the open question of how to consistently estimate CE and perform calibration under label shift without any target labels.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a trained probabilistic classifier f: X → Δ^k, n labeled source samples {(x_i, y_i)}, and m unlabeled target samples {x_i}. It first estimates per-class importance weights ω_c = p_t(Y_c=1)/p_s(Y_c=1) using an unsupervised domain adaptation method (RLLS, ELSA, BBSL, or EM-BCTS), then computes a label-free CE estimate on the target data via a kernel-based ratio estimator. The final output is a single optimal temperature T* obtained by minimizing the estimated classwise CE over the target distribution, which rescales the model's logits to produce calibrated probability scores. The CE estimator values lie in [0, 2] and are consistent for any consistent kernel, with an error rate of O(n^{-1/2} + m^{-1/2}).

## Method

The core contribution is a novel, label-free, consistent L_p classwise calibration error estimator under label shift (Eq. 6). It estimates the conditional expectation E_{p_t}[Y_c | Z_c = z_c] by combining per-class importance weights (re-weighting the source label distribution to match the target) with a kernel-based ratio estimator that approximates p_t(Z_c | Y_c=1) using the source conditional p_s(Z_c | Y_c=1), which is valid under the label shift assumption that p(X|Y) is invariant. Proposition 3.1 proves pointwise consistency of this conditional expectation estimator, and the overall CE estimator inherits consistency for any consistent kernel (e.g., adaptive binning). LaSCal then uses this estimator as a differentiable loss: instead of minimizing cross-entropy as in standard temperature scaling, it minimizes the estimated classwise CE over the temperature parameter T (Eq. 7), yielding an accuracy-preserving post-hoc calibration that operates entirely on the unlabeled target distribution. The approach is modular, plugging into any post-hoc or trainable calibration framework and any importance weight estimator from the label shift adaptation literature.

## Results

On CIFAR-100-LT (IF=10), LaSCal achieves a macro-averaged L₂ classwise CE of 5.87, dramatically outperforming all baselines (next best: CPMC at 25.20, EM-BCTS at 26.43). On iWildCam, LaSCal achieves 14.71 CE, beating TransCal (14.67, essentially tied), HeadToTail (16.63), and TempScal (17.33). On Amazon, LaSCal reaches 3.38 CE, competitive with EM-BCTS (3.05) and ahead of HeadToTail (3.58) and CPMC (4.74). On CIFAR-10-LT, LaSCal (4.63) is slightly behind EM-BCTS (4.39) and CPMC (4.46) but ahead of all covariate-shift methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LaSCal's most dramatic advantage is on CIFAR-100-LT, where it achieves 5.87 macro CE versus 24–28 for all other methods (CPCS 25.13, CPMC 25.20, EM-BCTS 26.43, HeadToTail 27.40, TempScal 28.54, TransCal 56.37), a >4× improvement over the next best. On iWildCam, LaSCal (14.71) outperforms EM-BCTS (19.31), CPMC (19.38), CPCS (17.81), and HeadToTail (16.63), and is essentially tied with TransCal (14.67). On Amazon, LaSCal (3.38) slightly trails EM-BCTS (3.05) but beats HeadToTail (3.58), CPMC (4.74), and TempScal (6.10). On CIFAR-10-LT, LaSCal (4.63) is marginally behind EM-BCTS (4.39) and CPMC (4.46) but ahead of all covariate-shift baselines (CPCS 5.00, TransCal 6.53, HeadToTail 4.78, TempScal 4.88). Overall, LaSCal is the only method that simultaneously handles label shift without target labels while maintaining a consistent CE estimator, and it dominates in the high-class-count, low-data-per-class regime (CIFAR-100) where prior methods fail most severely.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two well-developed lines of work: calibration error estimation (Naeini et al., Guo et al., Vaicenavicius et al.) and label shift adaptation via importance weighting (BBSL, RLLS, ELSA, EM-BCTS). It departs from prior calibration-under-shift methods (CPCS, TransCal, HeadToTail), which are all derived under the covariate shift assumption p_s(X)≠p_t(X), by instead targeting the label shift assumption p_s(Y)≠p_t(Y) with p_s(X|Y)=p_t(X|Y). The key conceptual move is repurposing importance weights—previously used only to re-weight training for predictive accuracy—into a consistent kernel-based estimator of the conditional expectation E[Y_c|Z_c] on the target, thereby enabling label-free CE estimation and post-hoc calibration on the shifted domain.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first consistent, label-free CE estimator under label shift, filling a gap the authors explicitly identify as an open question. For safety-critical deployments (medical diagnostics, wildlife monitoring) where target labels are unavailable or prohibitively expensive, LaSCal provides a principled post-hoc calibration step that adapts to the target's class distribution without retraining. Key limitations include dependence on the quality of importance weight estimators (which degrade for underrepresented classes), a minimum data requirement (~4000 samples for reliable estimates), and scope restricted to label shift rather than covariate shift or their combination. Open questions include deriving consistent estimators under covariate shift, handling compound shifts more rigorously, and reducing the data requirement in severely label-scarce regimes.
