## Problem

Most GNNs rely on standard, non-graph-adaptive activation functions (e.g., ReLU), while existing graph-adaptive alternatives like GReLU and Max/Median filters are constrained by fixed blueprints (piecewise-linear or predefined operators) that limit their expressive flexibility. The paper asks whether a flexible, end-to-end learnable activation function can be designed that is simultaneously graph-adaptive, differentiable, bounded, and computationally efficient, yielding consistent gains across diverse graph tasks.

## Input / Output

DIGRAF consumes the intermediate node feature matrix H̄^(l) ∈ R^{N×C} (output of the l-th GNN layer) and the graph adjacency matrix A, and produces the activated node features H^(l) ∈ R^{N×C} applied element-wise. The transformation operates within a bounded domain Ω = [a, b] (typically symmetric around 0); values outside Ω pass through unchanged via an identity function. The per-graph parameter vector θ^(l) ∈ R^{N_P−1} is produced by an auxiliary GNN (GNN_ACT) followed by graph-level pooling, so the activation adapts to each input graph and feature pair.

## Method

DIGRAF parameterizes the activation as a 1D CPAB diffeomorphism: a continuous piecewise-affine velocity field v^θ is defined over a tessellation of Ω, and the activation is the flow map f^θ(x) = φ^θ(x, t=1) obtained by integrating v^θ over unit time. An additional GNN (GNN_ACT), sharing the backbone architecture of the main network but with its own weights, maps (H̄^(l), A) to the velocity-field parameters θ^(l) via a graph-level pooling operation, making the activation graph-adaptive and task-adaptive in an end-to-end fashion. Because the 1D CPAB case admits closed-form solutions for both the diffeomorphism and its gradient, computation is linear in the number of nodes and parallelizable. A Gaussian smoothness prior regularizes θ^(l) to maintain smooth velocity fields, and a Tanh squashing keeps parameters bounded in [−1, 1]. The resulting activation is provably differentiable, bounded within Ω, Lipschitz continuous, and permutation equivariant, while its diffeomorphic blueprint can represent a wide range of non-linearities (Sigmoid, Tanh, ELU, Softplus, etc.) without a fixed template.

## Results

On node classification, DIGRAF achieves 81.6% on BLOGCATALOG, 69.6% on FLICKR, 82.8% on CORA, and 79.3% on PUBMED, consistently outperforming all 12 baselines. On ZINC-12k regression, DIGRAF achieves an MAE of 0.1302, a ~18% relative improvement over the best baseline (Maxout at 0.1587). On OGB molecular tasks, DIGRAF reaches 80.28% ROC-AUC on MOLHIV (a 4.7% absolute gain over ReLU) and 80.37% on MOLBACE. Across 15 datasets spanning node classification, graph classification, and regression, DIGRAF is consistently in the top-1 or top-2 position.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DIGRAF outperforms all 12 baselines across every dataset and task. Against standard activations, DIGRAF gains 9.5 points over ReLU on BLOGCATALOG (81.6% vs. 72.1%) and 18.9 points over Sigmoid on FLICKR (69.6% vs. 18.3%). Among learnable activations, DIGRAF surpasses the strongest (Swish) by 5.6 points on FLICKR (69.6% vs. 55.7%) and 3.2 points on BLOGCATALOG (81.6% vs. 76.0%). Against graph-adaptive activations, DIGRAF beats GReLU (the best graph baseline) by 1.0 points on CORA (82.8% vs. 81.8%) and 4.3 points on FLICKR (69.6% vs. 54.4%), while outperforming Median by 11.3 points on FLICKR. On ZINC-12k, DIGRAF achieves MAE 0.1302 vs. 0.1587 for Maxout (best baseline), a ~18% relative improvement. DIGRAF also slightly outperforms its non-adaptive variant DIGRAF (W/O ADAP.) on all datasets, confirming the benefit of graph-adaptivity (e.g., 81.6% vs. 80.8% on BLOGCATALOG, 0.1302 vs. 0.1382 MAE on ZINC-12k).

## Relation to Prior Work

DIGRAF repurposes the CPAB diffeomorphism framework from Freifeld et al., originally designed for spatial grid deformation and alignment, as a blueprint for learning activation functions by shifting the transformation from horizontal (input-space) to vertical (output-space). It departs from prior graph-adaptive activations (GReLU's piecewise-linear structure, Max/Median filters) by replacing fixed functional templates with a flexible diffeomorphic mapping whose parameters are predicted by an auxiliary GNN. This positions DIGRAF at the intersection of learnable activation functions (PReLU, Maxout, Swish) and graph-adaptive mechanisms, adding input- and graph-dependence that neither family previously offered simultaneously.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DIGRAF provides a principled, theoretically grounded activation function for GNNs that guarantees differentiability, boundedness, permutation equivariance, and Lipschitz continuity by construction, while empirically delivering consistent gains across 15+ datasets and multiple GNN architectures. A key open question is whether restricting the activation to the diffeomorphism class (bijective, monotone) is truly optimal, since non-monotone or non-invertible non-linearities might be beneficial for certain tasks. The paper also leaves open whether the computational overhead of the auxiliary GNN_ACT is justified in resource-constrained settings, given that DIGRAF (W/O ADAP.) already captures most of the performance gain.
