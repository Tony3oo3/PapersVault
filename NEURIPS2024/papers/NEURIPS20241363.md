## Problem

Existing efficient personalization methods for text-to-image diffusion models reduce training steps or the number of updated parameters, but they still require backpropagation through the full diffusion U-Net, keeping all model weights in GPU memory. This makes on-device fine-tuning infeasible on resource-constrained hardware, where even inference alone can strain available memory. The paper identifies GPU memory I/O during the backward pass as the unaddressed bottleneck for subject-driven generation on-device.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a small set of user-specific images (3–15 per subject) and a text prompt containing a special identifier token (e.g., 'a S* backpack'), following the DreamBooth protocol. It produces a compact set of LoRA parameters (rank 128, ~24M parameters) that are transferred back into the original full U-Net for inference, requiring no additional memory beyond the small LoRA weights. The final output is a personalized image generated from a simple prompt, with the personalized LoRA serving as the portable artifact across inference sessions.

## Method

Hollowed Net exploits the observation that LoRA weight changes are near zero in the central blocks of a diffusion U-Net, so those layers contribute minimally to personalization. By leveraging the symmetric U-shape and skip-connections, the method temporarily removes a contiguous fraction of middle layers (39.2% in the main experiments) without needing projection layers or structural re-initialization. Training proceeds in two stages: first, a forward pass on the full pre-trained U-Net caches intermediate activations (200 samples suffice), and second, LoRA fine-tuning runs on the hollowed network using those cached activations as inputs to the upper blocks, avoiding the need to hold the full model in GPU memory. After fine-tuning, the learned LoRA weights are seamlessly transferred back to the corresponding layers of the original U-Net, enabling inference with only an 11% memory increase over standard inference and no architectural modification.

## Results

Hollowed Net achieves a DINO score of 0.660 (DreamBooth) and 0.603 (CustomConcept101), matching or marginally exceeding LoRA FT (r=128) at 0.658 and 0.603 respectively, while requiring only 3.88 GB peak GPU memory — just 11% above inference cost. In contrast, LoRA FT (r=128) requires 5.23 GB (+50% over inference) and Full FT requires 16.62 GB (+376%). On CLIP-I and CLIP-T, Hollowed Net scores 0.805/0.300 (DreamBooth) and 0.773/0.302 (CustomConcept101), essentially identical to LoRA FT (r=128). Total training FLOPs for Hollowed Net (2052T) are also lower than LoRA FT (2148T).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against LoRA FT (r=128), Hollowed Net matches or slightly exceeds subject fidelity (DINO 0.660 vs. 0.658 on DreamBooth; 0.603 vs. 0.603 on CustomConcept101) while reducing peak GPU memory by 26% (3.88 GB vs. 5.23 GB) and total training FLOPs by ~4.5% (2052T vs. 2148T). Against Full FT, Hollowed Net's DINO gap (0.660 vs. 0.663 on DreamBooth) falls within the standard deviation of Full FT across seeds, yet it uses 77% less GPU memory (3.88 GB vs. 16.62 GB). Against the layer-pruned BK-SDM baselines (Appendix A, Table 4), Hollowed Net achieves substantially higher DINO (0.660 vs. 0.629 for BK-SDM-Base and 0.602 for BK-SDM-Small) at comparable memory, demonstrating that temporary hollowing with pre-computed activations preserves personalization quality far better than permanent layer removal. Human evaluation (Table 2) confirms users perceive Hollowed Net and LoRA FT as statistically indistinguishable in both subject and text fidelity (49.3% ties on subject, 69.4% ties on text). LoRA FT at rank 1, despite fewer parameters, degrades DINO to 0.516/0.522 and cannot compete with Hollowed Net on either memory or quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on LoRA-based fine-tuning (Hu et al., 2021) and the DreamBooth personalization protocol (Ruiz et al., 2023), but departs from both dominant efficiency strategies in the literature: reducing training steps via auxiliary pretrained models (BLIP-Diffusion, SuTI) and reducing updateable parameters (LoRA, SVDiff). It also distinguishes itself from side-tuning approaches (LST, TinyTL) by avoiding the construction of a separate auxiliary network, and from LLM layer-pruning work (Gromov et al., Kim et al.) by only *temporarily* excluding middle U-Net layers during fine-tuning rather than permanently removing them. The key conceptual move is exploiting the U-Net's symmetric skip-connection topology to hollow out central layers without any projection or re-initialization, then transferring the learned LoRA weights back to the full model for inference.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Hollowed Net is the first method to address subject-driven generation specifically from a GPU memory I/O perspective, bringing fine-tuning memory down to within 11% of inference cost — a practical threshold for on-device deployment that prior parameter-efficient or step-reduction methods could not reach. The paper acknowledges a sensitivity to class-token granularity (e.g., 'poop emoji' under a coarse 'toy' token) and notes that the approach is orthogonal to quantization and other PEFT methods, leaving room for further memory reduction. Open questions include whether the two-stage pre-computation strategy generalizes to non-U-Net diffusion architectures (e.g., DiT/SDXL transformer backbones) and to LLM fine-tuning, which the authors flag as future work.
