## Problem

Training adaptive meta-RL agents in complex, open-ended simulators requires diverse training tasks, but existing approaches (domain randomization, procedural generation, unsupervised environment design) all assume well-behaved parameterizations where random sampling or mutations reliably produce meaningfully diverse levels. In realistic simulators with arbitrary or poorly-structured genotype spaces, this assumption fails, making hand-designing tasks prohibitively expensive and rendering UED methods inefficient at discovering useful diversity. The paper asks whether one can generate sufficiently diverse training tasks from such ill-parameterized simulators using only limited downstream knowledge.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242445/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

DIVA takes as input an unstructured environment generator E_U(θ) with no assumption that random genotypes produce diverse levels, a small set of downstream feature samples (e.g., 100 values per feature, as few as 5 in ablations) that characterize the target distribution without requiring access to the underlying levels, and a user-specified set of k diversity features with grid-archive resolution. It produces a QD archive of genotypes whose induced levels match the target feature distribution, from which training tasks are sampled during meta-training to yield a trained VariBAD adaptive policy. The key distinction from DR/PG is that only feature values (not simulator parameters) from the target domain are needed, a strictly weaker assumption.

## Method

DIVA performs two-stage quality diversity optimization (MAP-Elites for discrete genotypes, CMA-ES for continuous) over the environment parameter space to discover levels matching a target feature distribution estimated via KDE or parametric fitting from downstream samples. Stage 1 initializes the archive with bounds spanning both random E_U samples and the target region, then uses a gradually shrinking sample mask to guide the population toward the target; Stage 2 reinitializes the archive to target bounds and continues QD updates to densely populate the target region. During meta-training, levels are sampled from the archive using a discretized target density, and VariBAD is trained on the resulting task distribution. The core insight is that QD's archive-based search bypasses the need to evaluate agent performance on every generated level (unlike UED), while the feature-based archive provides fine-grained, interpretable control over diversity axes that can incorporate domain knowledge. A preliminary DIVA+ variant further integrates PLR⊥ learning-potential scoring to reweight the QD archive during meta-training.

## Results

DIVA outperforms all baselines (DR, PLR⊥, ACCEL) on all three evaluation domains—GRIDNAV, ALCHEMY, and RACING—in both final episode return and task success/completion rate, despite UED baselines (PLR⊥ and ACCEL) being granted 2.4×, 3.5×, and 4.0× more additional environment steps respectively. On the zero-shot F1 track transfer task, DIVA not only completes more tracks than baselines but also significantly outperforms the oracle agent (ODS) trained directly on the structured target distribution E_S. DIVA achieves these results using only a handful of downstream feature samples (as few as 5 in ablations) to define its QD archive, without requiring access to the underlying target levels.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DIVA is compared against four baselines: DR (domain randomization over E_U), PLR⊥ (replay-guided adversarial environment design), ACCEL (evolutionary curriculum with regret-based scoring), and ODS (oracle trained on the structured target distribution E_S). DIVA decisively outperforms both UED baselines (PLR⊥ and ACCEL) on all three domains in final episode return and success rate, even though these baselines receive 2.4×–4.0× more additional environment interaction steps for level evaluation. The key distinction is that PLR⊥ and ACCEL, despite generating roughly as many unique genotypes as DIVA (Figure 5d), fail to produce phenotypically diverse training levels because their performance-based scoring cannot navigate the ill-parameterized genotype space. On the F1 transfer evaluation, DIVA surpasses even ODS—the upper-bound oracle trained directly on E_S—suggesting that DIVA's QD-discovered levels (with fewer, sharper angles) provide more transferable diversity than the target distribution itself. The preliminary DIVA+ variant (combining DIVA's QD archive with PLR⊥'s learning-potential weighting) shows that the two approaches are complementary, recovering performance on a misspecified archive to match original DIVA results.

## Relation to Prior Work

DIVA sits at the intersection of quality diversity optimization (MAP-Elites, CMA-ES), meta-reinforcement learning (VariBAD), and unsupervised environment design (UED). It departs from UED methods like PLR⊥ and ACCEL, which assume that random sampling or mutation over the genotype space produces meaningfully diverse levels, by instead using QD to efficiently discover diverse regions even when the parameterization is ill-behaved. It also departs from domain randomization and procedural generation, which require carefully hand-designed parameterizations where random seeds directly produce meaningful diversity, by requiring only a small set of downstream feature samples rather than access to the underlying level parameters. The paper positions DIVA as the first instance of a new 'semi-supervised environment design' (SSED) paradigm that inherits the flexibility of UED and the supervised structure of DR/PG.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DIVA enables adaptive agent training in complex, open-ended simulators without requiring researchers to construct well-behaved parameterizations, lowering a significant practical barrier to applying meta-RL in realistic domains. The F1 transfer result—where DIVA outperforms an oracle trained directly on the target distribution—suggests that QD-discovered diversity can sometimes exceed what the target distribution itself provides, though the explanation remains somewhat speculative. Key open questions include automating the specification of feature axes and archive design (currently heuristic), extending to neural environment generators, and developing more principled integrations of UED and QD (as in the preliminary DIVA+ results). The approach's dependence on both feature samples and manually chosen feature dimensions limits its fully autonomous applicability.
