## Problem

PINNs empirically fail to converge when solving high-order or high-dimensional PDEs, yet no rigorous theoretical explanation existed for this pathology. The paper addresses the gap by proving that the gradient flow of PINNs converges to the global minimum with lower probability as the PDE order increases, and that this negative effect is amplified by dimensionality due to the differential operators embedded in the loss function.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240003/cropped_page8_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a kth-order linear PDE on a bounded domain Ω ⊂ R^d with Dirichlet/Neumann/Robin boundary conditions, a source function f, and fixed collocation points (n_o interior, n_b boundary). The network is a two-layer MLP of width m with ReLU^p activation (p ≥ k+1 required for well-defined derivatives), and the loss requires automatic differentiation of the network up to order k at every collocation point. The output is the approximate solution u(x) evaluated at arbitrary points, with convergence guaranteed only when m exceeds a bound that grows exponentially in both k and d.

## Method

The paper extends the gradient flow convergence analysis of Gao et al. from second-order PDEs to general kth-order linear PDEs with ReLU^p activation, proving that the required network width scales as C·(d+k choose d)^14 · p^(7k+4) · 2^(6p) · (log(md/δ))^(4p), revealing that the optimal activation power is p = k+1 and that both PDE order and dimension exponentially inflate the width requirement. To mitigate this, the paper introduces a variable splitting strategy that reformulates the kth-order PDE as a system of lower-order PDEs by introducing auxiliary networks for intermediate derivatives, reducing the maximum differential order in the loss from k to |ξ| (the largest gap between consecutive split levels). The key theoretical result shows that the finest splitting—separating all derivatives into auxiliary variables so the system consists only of first-order PDEs—yields the strongest convergence guarantee, relaxing the activation requirement to p ≥ 2 and dramatically reducing the width bound. Numerical experiments on bi-harmonic, Poisson, heat, elastic beam, and convection-diffusion equations confirm that VS-PINNs converge more reliably and with lower variance than vanilla PINNs, especially for higher-order problems.

## Results

The main theoretical result (Theorem 3.2) establishes that the gradient flow of PINNs with ReLU^p activation converges to the global minimum with high probability when the network width satisfies m > C · C(d+k,d)^14 · p^(7k+4) · 2^(6p) · (log(md/δ))^(4p), revealing an exponential dependence on both PDE order k and dimension d. For variable-splitting PINNs (Theorem 4.3), the bound improves to m > C · C(d+k,d)^6 · C(d+|ξ|,d)^8 · p^(7|ξ|+4) · 2^(6p) · (log(md/δ))^(4p), where |ξ| < k is the reduced derivative order. Experiments on the bi-harmonic, Poisson, heat, and elastic beam equations confirm that higher p and higher k require substantially wider networks (from 10^2 to 10^6) for convergence, and that VS-PINNs with finest splitting (|ξ|=1, p=2) train effectively with plain gradient descent where standard PINNs (p=5) fail.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is between standard PINNs and variable-splitting PINNs (VS-PINNs) on the same PDEs. On the second-order heat equation, VS-PINNs (with |ξ|=1, p=2) achieve faster and more stable loss convergence than standard PINNs (p=3), as shown in Figure 2(b). On the fourth-order elastic beam equation, standard PINNs requiring p≥5 fail to train properly with gradient descent, while VS-PINNs with finest splitting (|ξ|=1, p=2) converge effectively using the same GD optimizer. Among VS-PINN variants, the finest splitting (|ξ|=1) outperforms partial splitting (|ξ|=2, p=3) in both convergence speed and training stability (lower variance across random seeds). The paper also compares different activation powers p (3–10) on the heat equation, confirming that smaller p yields faster convergence, consistent with the theoretical prediction that the optimal p is k+1 (or |ξ|+1 for VS-PINNs).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the convergence analysis of Gao et al. (ICML 2023), who proved gradient descent convergence for two-layer PINNs restricted to second-order linear PDEs with ReLU³ activation, to general kth-order linear PDEs with ReLU^p activation, yielding tighter polylogarithmic bounds. It also builds on the variable splitting framework of Park et al. (2024), which established convergence guarantees for second-order PDEs, by generalizing the analysis to arbitrary order and showing that the finest splitting (reducing to first-order systems) is provably optimal. The theoretical machinery draws on the over-parameterized network convergence framework of Du et al. (2018), adapted to the PINN setting where the loss involves differential operators.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This paper provides the first rigorous theoretical explanation for the well-known empirical observation that PINNs struggle with high-order and high-dimensional PDEs, showing that the required network width grows exponentially in both the PDE order k and the dimension d, with the PDE order amplifying the dimensional sensitivity. The variable splitting strategy is validated as a principled remedy, with the finest splitting (first-order system) provably optimal for convergence. Key limitations include: only sufficient (not necessary) width conditions, restriction to two-layer MLPs and linear PDEs, continuous-time gradient flow rather than discrete gradient descent, and fixed collocation points rather than stochastic sampling. Open questions include extending the analysis to adaptive optimizers (Adam, L-BFGS), nonlinear PDEs, deeper architectures, and generalization error bounds.
