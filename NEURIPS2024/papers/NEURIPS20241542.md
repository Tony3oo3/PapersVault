## Problem

Polishing of Inertial Confinement Fusion (ICF) diamond-like carbon (HDC) capsule shells is a multi-day, multi-stage process where surface roughness must be monitored to determine optimal stopping points. Current practice requires halting the polisher, removing the shell, and performing time-consuming manual optical measurements with a scanning laser confocal microscope, making real-time process control infeasible. This paper introduces the first public dataset pairing raw vibration signals from the polishing machinery with corresponding surface roughness values, enabling automated, in-process roughness prediction.

## Input / Output

The input consists of raw accelerometer voltage-vs-time signals (Kistler K-Shear 8702B500, 10 kHz sampling rate) collected continuously during polishing, segmented into 6-minute windows from which 11 time-domain statistical features (kurtosis, skewness, variance, mean, peak/RMS acceleration, crest/shape/impulse/margin factors, entropy) are extracted. The primary regression output is deltaSa (change in surface roughness in nm), while a secondary classification output assigns each sample to its polishing-batch domain. Because manual Sa measurements are only available at stage boundaries (12–25 hr intervals), intermediate deltaSa labels are interpolated using an empirical linear-then-logarithmic decay model calibrated to observed polishing trends.

## Method

Two baseline regression approaches are built on an identical MLP (2 hidden layers × 100 neurons, MAE loss). The first directly maps the 11 vibration features to deltaSa. The second augments the same MLP with a gradient reversal layer (GRL) implementing the Unsupervised Domain Adaptation by Backpropagation method of Ganin & Lempitsky (2015), trained with a joint loss E = L_y + L_d where L_d is a binary cross-entropy domain-classification loss (source batches S173/S179/S211 vs. target S233 or S238) that forces the shared feature extractor to learn domain-invariant representations. A separate domain-detection task uses the same 11 features with standard classifiers (gradient boosting achieves 100% accuracy across five batches), confirming that polishing conditions induce measurable domain shifts. Domain adaptation reduces MAE from 0.11/0.205 nm (plain MLP) to 0.0674/0.0634 nm on S233/S238, and sequential adaptation (S233 → S238) further improves to 0.0618 nm, validating that the GRL-based mechanism effectively transfers knowledge across unseen polishing conditions.

## Results

For the primary deltaSa regression task, the plain MLP achieves a MAE of 0.11 nm on S233 and 0.205 nm on S238, while the same MLP augmented with Domain Adaptation (gradient reversal layer) reduces MAE to 0.0674 nm on S233 and 0.0634 nm on S238 — a roughly 39% and 69% improvement respectively. Sequential domain adaptation (adapting on S233 first, then S238) further improves S238 MAE to 0.0618 nm. For the secondary domain detection task, Gradient Boost achieves 100% accuracy in classifying five polishing batches into distinct domains, outperforming Decision Tree, Random Forest, and XGBoost (all at 99%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is between a plain MLP regressor and the same MLP augmented with Unsupervised Domain Adaptation via a gradient reversal layer (Ganin & Lempitsky, 2015). Domain Adaptation clearly wins on both test domains: it reduces MAE from 0.11 nm to 0.0674 nm on S233 (a 39% reduction) and from 0.205 nm to 0.0634 nm on S238 (a 69% reduction), confirming that domain shift between polishing batches is a significant source of prediction error. For the auxiliary domain detection task, Gradient Boost achieves perfect 100% accuracy, marginally outperforming Decision Tree, Random Forest, and XGBoost (all 99%), while Logistic Regression (49%) and SVM (48%) fail to separate the domains. No external baselines from prior literature are directly benchmarked; the comparisons are internal to the proposed pipeline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the well-established line of vibration-based surface roughness prediction in manufacturing (Hetherington 1999, Bukkapatnam 2008, Hanchate 2023, Botcha 2018) to the specific and previously unexplored domain of ICF high-density carbon capsule polishing. Methodologically, it builds directly on Ganin and Lempitsky's (2015) unsupervised domain adaptation via gradient reversal layer, applying it to a regression setting where both source and target domains shift due to different polishing conditions. The paper's primary contribution is not a new method but the first public release of raw accelerometer vibration data paired with surface roughness measurements for ICF shell polishing, filling a gap identified in prior work (Alexos 2023, Galla 2024) that used similar signals without public benchmarking.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This dataset is practically important for the multi-billion-dollar nuclear fusion industry because it enables real-time, automated surface roughness monitoring during the multi-day polishing process, replacing labor-intensive manual optical measurements. The domain adaptation result (reducing MAE from ~0.11–0.21 nm to ~0.06–0.07 nm) demonstrates that a simple MLP with a gradient reversal layer can generalize across unseen polishing conditions, though the evidence is limited to two test batches with a single run and no error bars. Key open questions include whether the 11 time-domain statistical features are sufficient or whether raw-signal deep learning (e.g., 1D CNNs, transformers) would improve accuracy, whether the interpolated Sa labels (assuming 64% linear decline in the first hour) introduce systematic bias, and how the approach scales as more polishing batches become available.
