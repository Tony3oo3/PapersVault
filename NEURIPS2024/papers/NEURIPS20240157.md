## Problem

Existing neural subset selection methods face a fundamental trade-off: pooling-based architectures (DeepSets, EquiVSet) scale to large inputs but lose complex inter-element interactions, while attention-based methods (Set Transformer, INSET) capture rich interactions but become infeasible when ground set sizes exceed memory capacity. No prior method simultaneously models complex subset-ground-set interactions and generalizes to arbitrarily large inputs through partitioning.

## Input / Output

The method takes a ground set V of n d-dimensional elements (text embeddings, image features, or molecular descriptors) and a supervision mask S indicating the target subset. V is randomly partitioned into m disjoint chunks processed independently, with each chunk assigned a learned slot embedding. The output is a scalar utility score F(S,V) used within an energy-based model to rank all 2^V subsets, from which the optimal subset S* is extracted. A key constraint is that the output must be invariant to both element permutations and the specific partitioning of V.

## Method

HORSE introduces a subset-based attention mechanism where m learned slot embeddings ζ attend to elements in V via dot-product attention, producing an m×n attention matrix. The critical innovation is a two-stage normalization: column-wise normalization nl(Â)_{i,j} = Â_{i,j}/Σ_i Â_{i,j} followed by row-wise rescaling M = diag(nl(Â)·1_n)^{-1}, which ensures the aggregated representation h(V) is identical regardless of how V is partitioned (the Identity Property). The final representation concatenates h(S) with g(h(S_1),...,h(S_m)) and passes through an MLP. Training follows the EquiVSet energy-based framework, maximizing the log-likelihood of optimal subsets under the constraint p_θ(S|V) ∝ F_θ(S;V). Theoretical proofs establish both permutation invariance (Theorem 4.1) and satisfaction of the Identity Property (Theorem 4.2) for any strictly positive activation σ.

## Results

HORSE is evaluated using Mean Jaccard Coefficient (MJC, in %) across four task families. On the Amazon Baby Registry product recommendation task (12 categories), HORSE achieves the best MJC in 11 of 12 categories, reaching 90.3 on Feeding, 87.6 on Bath, and 83.2 on Gear. On compound selection, HORSE achieves 43.2 on PDBBind-2 and 21.3 on BindingDB-2, surpassing the best baseline (INSET) by 6.1 and 1.5 points respectively. On set anomaly detection, HORSE attains 72.3 on Double MNIST and 74.3 on CIFAR-10, outperforming INSET by 2.6 and 3.1 points. In large-scale settings (|V| up to 1000), HORSE outperforms INSET and Set Transformer by up to 20% MJC.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HORSE is compared against five baselines: PGM (differentiable greedy maximization), DeepSet (pooling-based set encoding), Set Transformer (self-attention set encoding), EquiVSet (EBM-based subset selection without V information), and INSET (EBM with sum-pooled V context). HORSE wins in 11 of 12 product recommendation categories over INSET (the strongest baseline), with the largest margin on Media (+3.2) and Safety (+3.1); the sole near-tie is Diaper (88.0 vs 88.3). On compound selection, HORSE's advantage over INSET is most pronounced on the two-filter variants: PDBBind-2 (43.2 vs 37.1, +6.1) and BindingDB-2 (21.3 vs 19.8, +1.5), while single-filter gains are smaller (PDBBind: 94.1 vs 93.5). On set anomaly detection, HORSE leads INSET on all four datasets (e.g., Double MNIST 72.3 vs 69.7, CIFAR-10 74.3 vs 71.2). Set Transformer, while competitive on small sets, degrades significantly in large-scale settings (|V|≥600) where HORSE maintains consistent performance. EquiVSet and INSET both struggle on Safety, Carseats, and Furniture categories where they fail to surpass even PGM, whereas HORSE consistently outperforms all baselines in these challenging cases.

## Relation to Prior Work

HORSE extends the neural subset selection lineage from EquiVSet (Ou et al., 2022) and INSET (Xie et al., 2024) by replacing their simple sum-pooling aggregation of the ground set V with a subset-based attention mechanism that captures element-level interactions. The formal Identity Property, which requires the model to recover the source set V from partitioned encodings, is inspired by the mini-batch consistency framework of Willette et al. (2023) and the slot-based set encoding of Bruno et al. (2021). Unlike INSET, which concatenates a global V representation with S, HORSE partitions V into independently processable chunks and aggregates them through a normalization scheme that preserves permutation invariance, enabling scalable handling of large ground sets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Identity Property provides a clean formal criterion for evaluating whether a subset-selection model genuinely leverages the full ground set context, and the partitioning strategy offers a practical path to handling sets that exceed GPU memory. However, the large-scale experiments are limited to synthetic datasets (Two-Moons, Gaussian Mixture) with set sizes up to 1000, and the authors acknowledge that real distributed-environment validation remains future work. The ~20% improvement over INSET in large-scale settings is encouraging but would benefit from confirmation on real-world large-cardinality tasks (e.g., large-scale recommendation or drug screening with thousands of candidates).
