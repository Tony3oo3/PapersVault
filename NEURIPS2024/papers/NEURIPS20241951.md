## Problem

Existing automated jailbreaking methods for LLMs either require human supervision, white-box access to model weights, or produce prompts with no natural meaning that are easily detected by perplexity filters. The state-of-the-art black-box method, PAIR, achieves limited success rates (e.g., 78% on GPT-4o) while using a relatively high number of queries. This paper addresses the gap of achieving high jailbreak success rates with fewer queries using only black-box access and producing interpretable, natural-language prompts.

## Input / Output

The method takes a harmful information query (e.g., 'How to build a bomb?'), an attacker LLM (default: Vicuna-13B-v1.5), an evaluator LLM (default: GPT-4), and black-box query access to a target LLM. It iteratively refines candidate prompts over up to d=10 rounds, sending at most w=10 prompts per round to the target, and outputs a single jailbreak prompt that causes the target to produce the requested harmful information. The evaluator serves dual roles: scoring whether a response constitutes a successful jailbreak (Judge, 1–10 scale) and checking whether a candidate prompt is off-topic relative to the original query (Off-Topic, binary).

## Method

TAP (Tree of Attacks with Pruning) extends PAIR's single-chain iterative refinement into a tree search with two key additions: branching and pruning. At each iteration, the attacker LLM generates b=4 variations of every current prompt (branching), exploring diverse attack strategies in parallel. The evaluator then prunes off-topic prompts (Phase 1), the remaining prompts are sent to the target and scored (Attack and Assess), and only the top w=10 highest-scoring prompts are retained for the next round (Phase 2, Prune). This tree structure is repeated until a jailbreak is found or the maximum depth d=10 is reached. Ablations show branching is critical for success rate (removing it drops GPT-4-Turbo success from 84% to 48%) while pruning is critical for query efficiency (removing it nearly doubles queries from 22.5 to 55.4 without improving success). The combination of both mechanisms yields TAP's 94% success rate on GPT-4o with only 16.2 mean queries, versus PAIR's 78% with 40.3 queries.

## Results

On the AdvBench Subset (50 harmful prompts), TAP achieves jailbreak success rates of 94% on GPT4o, 90% on GPT4, 84% on GPT4-Turbo, and 98% on PaLM-2, using on average fewer than 30 queries per prompt (16.2–28.8 queries). This substantially outperforms the prior state-of-the-art black-box method PAIR, which achieves 78% on GPT4o, 60% on GPT4, and 44% on GPT4-Turbo while using 40–47 queries. TAP also maintains high success rates (80–96%) on LLMs protected by the Llama-Guard guardrail, and its generated prompts transfer to other models at rates comparable to PAIR.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against PAIR (the prior state-of-the-art black-box method), TAP achieves a significantly higher jailbreak success rate on nearly every target while also using fewer queries: e.g., 94% vs. 78% on GPT4o (16.2 vs. 40.3 queries), 90% vs. 60% on GPT4 (28.8 vs. 39.6 queries), and 84% vs. 44% on GPT4-Turbo (22.5 vs. 47.1 queries). The only exceptions are Llama-2-Chat-7B (where both methods perform poorly) and Claude-3 Opus (where TAP achieves 60% vs. 24% but uses more queries: 116.2 vs. 55.0). Against GCG (the state-of-the-art white-box method), TAP matches the 98% success rate on Vicuna-13B but uses only 11.8 queries versus GCG's 256K; on Llama-2-Chat-7B, GCG achieves 54% vs. TAP's 4%, but again at 256K queries. GCG cannot be evaluated on closed-source models (GPT family, PaLM-2, Gemini-Pro, Claude-3), whereas TAP operates purely in the black-box setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

TAP is a direct extension of PAIR (Chao et al. 2023), the prior state-of-the-art automated black-box jailbreaking method, which performs a single chain of iterative prompt refinement. The key conceptual addition is a tree structure: branching (the attacker generates multiple prompt variations per iteration) and pruning (an evaluator LLM filters off-topic and low-scoring candidates before querying the target). This tree-of-attacks framing is loosely inspired by Tree of Thoughts (Yao et al. 2023), though the authors note the analogy is partial since jailbreak generation does not decompose into sub-steps. TAP explicitly contrasts with white-box gradient-based methods (GCG, AutoDAN) that require model weights and produce non-interpretable prompts, and with template-based or manually discovered jailbreaks that lack automation or generalizability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TAP demonstrates that a simple architectural change (branching + pruning over a single refinement chain) yields large, consistent gains in both success rate and query efficiency across a wide range of state-of-the-art LLMs, including models protected by Llama-Guard guardrails. This has direct practical implications for AI safety: automated, scalable, black-box attacks lower the barrier for red-teaming and stress-testing alignment, while also raising the bar for defenses. Key limitations include reliance on an LLM judge with a 13% false-positive rate, evaluation on only two datasets of harmful-information prompts, and inability to track how closed-source target updates affect results. Open questions include whether TAP can generate genuinely novel jailbreaks (distinct from known patterns), whether it generalizes to non-harmful restricted content (bias, PII), and whether fine-tuned open-source evaluators can replace GPT-4 to reduce cost and dependency.
