## Problem

Conditioning function-valued (infinite-dimensional) stochastic processes on observations is an open problem when the underlying SDE is non-linear. Prior work either restricted to linear infinite-dimensional processes (e.g., Ornstein-Uhlenbeck) or discretised shapes first and then computed finite-dimensional bridges, losing the guarantee that the bridge remains well-defined as discretisation resolution increases. This paper fills the gap by deriving a rigorous infinite-dimensional conditioning framework for non-linear SDEs before any discretisation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240345/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an initial shape ξ₀ and a target shape V (both elements of a separable Hilbert space H, concretely L²(ℝ²,ℝ²) curves or point clouds), along with the SDE coefficients (A, F, B, Q) defining the stochastic flow of diffeomorphisms. It produces conditioned stochastic trajectories (bridges) between the two shapes and a learned score function parameterised over Fourier coefficients. A key design choice is that conditioning happens in the infinite-dimensional space first, and discretisation (via truncated Fourier basis or landmark points) is applied only afterwards, ensuring the bridge is well-defined in the limit of infinite resolution.

## Method

The core theoretical contribution is an infinite-dimensional Doob's h-transform: using infinite-dimensional Itô's lemma and Girsanov's theorem, the paper shows that conditioning a strong Markov solution of a non-linear Hilbert-space SDE on an endpoint event yields a new SDE with an additional drift term B(X)B(X)*∇log h(t,X), where h is a transition-operator-based function. Two conditioning regimes are provided: exact matching (h involves a Dirac delta on a set Γ, requiring twice-Fréchet-differentiable transition operators) and inexact matching (h involves a Gaussian kernel on the distance to V, requiring only twice-Fréchet-differentiable solutions). For sampling, the SDE is projected onto the first N Fourier basis elements (or N landmarks), reducing the problem to a finite-dimensional bridge; the intractable score ∇log h is then learned via the time-reversal score-matching algorithm of Heng et al. (2021), implemented with a U-net architecture using sinusoidal time embeddings and trained with the real denoising score matching loss.

## Results

For the Brownian motion validation (where a closed-form score is available), the learned score achieves RMSE of 5.09 with 8 Fourier bases, 6.66 with 16 bases, and 10.54 with 32 bases. Landmark discretisation gives RMSE of 7.95 (8 pts), 6.08 (16 pts), and 10.79 (32 pts), with training times ranging from ~96 s to ~949 s depending on dimensionality. The butterfly shape-bridging experiments are qualitative: 120 conditioned trajectories between two Papilio species show correct temporal interpolation (closer to the start shape at t=0.2, closer to the target at t=0.8), and a 40-specimen multi-species experiment produces plausible per-species trajectories from a Procrustes mean shape.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not benchmark against competing bridge-sampling methods; instead it validates the learned score against the analytically known score of Brownian motion, which serves as the ground-truth baseline. At 8 dimensions, Fourier discretisation (RMSE 5.09) outperforms landmarks (7.95), but at 16 dimensions the advantage reverses (6.66 vs. 6.08), and at 32 dimensions both approaches degrade similarly (~10.5–10.8). Training cost grows substantially with dimensionality for Fourier (105 s → 949 s) but remains relatively flat for landmarks (96 s → 183 s). The authors note that higher dimensions require more epochs to maintain accuracy, and that the U-net architecture they adopt (replacing the MLP from Heng et al. 2021) was necessary to scale beyond low dimensions. No direct comparison is made with the finite-dimensional bridge methods of Arnaudon et al. (2022) or the spectral diffusion approach of Phillips et al. (2022), which the authors position as prior work that only handles linear or pre-discretised settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Doob's h-transform from finite-dimensional SDEs (Rogers & Williams, 2000) to non-linear infinite-dimensional SDEs, filling a gap where prior infinite-dimensional work (Phillips et al., 2022) was restricted to linear Ornstein-Uhlenbeck processes. For the score-learning component, it adapts the time-reversal score-matching algorithm of Heng et al. (2021) to the discretised infinite-dimensional setting. In shape analysis, it departs from Arnaudon et al. (2022), who first discretise shapes and then compute finite-dimensional bridges, by instead conditioning in infinite dimensions and only then projecting onto a Fourier or landmark basis — a 'condition-then-discretise' strategy that the authors argue is more principled and well-defined in the limit.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result opens a previously unexplored theoretical space: conditioning non-linear function-valued stochastic processes without prior discretisation, which is a prerequisite for principled phylogenetic inference on shape data in evolutionary biology. Key limitations include the requirement for strong solutions and twice-Fréchet-differentiable transition operators (Assumption 3.3), which restricts the class of applicable SDEs, and the fact that only the time-reversal score is learned (not the forward bridge directly), so error compounds. Open questions include extending the framework to parameter estimation for phylogenetic trees, handling non-strong solutions, and scaling the score network to higher-dimensional discretisations.
