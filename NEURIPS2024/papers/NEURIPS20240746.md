## Problem

Existing HOI detectors that leverage large-scale visual-linguistic models like CLIP align only high-level text-image semantics, neglecting mid/low-level visual cues (spatial relations, shape, layout) and struggling with compositional generalization to novel interactions. Text-to-image diffusion models possess both the mid/low-level visual understanding and strong compositionality needed for HOI, but they emphasize instance-level generation rather than inter-object relations, making it unclear how to steer them toward relation-centric modeling and transfer their knowledge to detection.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image (resized to a maximum of 1333 pixels on the long side, shortest side between 480–800 px) and outputs human bounding boxes, object bounding boxes, and interaction class labels via a one-stage DETR-style decoder. Unusually, the visual backbone is a frozen Stable Diffusion denoising UNet that processes the VQGAN-encoded latent in a single noise-free forward pass (no iterative denoising), with learned relation embeddings injected as cross-attention conditioning to produce interaction-specific feature maps. During training, the method also generates synthetic HOI images with pseudo-annotations (bounding boxes derived from cross-attention probability maps) to augment the training set.

## Method

DIFFUSIONHOI introduces an inversion-based HOI modeling strategy that learns a single relation embedding v*_Rel per action via a disentanglement-based cycle-consistency objective: the HOI latent is decomposed by subtracting the object latent, the relation latent is reconstructed using v*_Rel as a text prompt, and the full HOI latent is recovered by adding the object latent back, supervised by an L2 consistency loss plus a relation-centric contrastive loss. These relation embeddings then serve dual roles: (1) as compositional text prompts to drive diffusion-model image generation of synthetic HOI samples with pseudo-annotations, enriching long-tailed and unseen interaction classes; and (2) as conditioning signals in a frozen diffusion UNet used as a feature extractor, where a single noise-free forward pass with relation-embedded cross-attention yields interaction-specific visual features for a DETR-style HOI decoder. An online update loss (L_Rel) aligns relation-query embeddings derived from visual features with those computed from decoded interaction-minus-object queries, enabling mutual boosting between HOI detection and relation embedding refinement throughout training.

## Results

On HICO-DET under the Default setup, DIFFUSIONHOI with a VQGAN backbone and Stable Diffusion achieves 38.12% mAP (Full), surpassing the previous best non-advanced-detector method RmLR (37.41%) by 0.71% and ViPLO (37.22%) by 0.90%. With a stronger ViT-L backbone and Stable unCLIP, performance rises to 42.54% mAP (Full), outperforming all prior methods by a substantial margin. On V-COCO, the method achieves 66.8/70.9 APS1role/APS2role (VQGAN) and 67.1/71.1 (ViT-L). Under zero-shot settings, DIFFUSIONHOI yields up to 6.43% mAP improvement on SWiG-HOI unseen categories and 6.53% on HICO-DET RF-UC unseen, demonstrating strong compositional generalization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DIFFUSIONHOI (VQGAN) outperforms all CLIP-based baselines on HICO-DET Default Full mAP: it beats RmLR (37.41%) by 0.71%, ViPLO (37.22%) by 0.90%, AGER (36.75%) by 1.37%, and CQL (35.36%) by 2.76%. The most notable gain is on Rare categories, where DIFFUSIONHOI achieves 38.93% versus 35.45% for ViPLO and 33.53% for AGER, indicating the relation-driven synthesis effectively alleviates the long-tail problem. On V-COCO, DIFFUSIONHOI leads all methods in both S1 (66.8) and S2 (70.9) roles. Under zero-shot evaluation, the method surpasses HOICLIP by 6.53% on RF-UC unseen (32.06 vs. 25.53) and by 8.23% on SWiG-HOI unseen (18.93 vs. 10.70 for CMD-SE), confirming superior compositional generalization. The only method reporting a higher Default Full score is PViC† (44.32%), which relies on a much stronger H-Deform-DETR object detector, making the comparison less direct.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends textual inversion (Gal et al., ICLR 2022) from object-centric to relation-centric modeling, using a disentanglement-based cycle-consistency objective to learn relation embeddings that capture human-action patterns rather than individual objects. It builds on the observation from Zhao et al. (ICCV 2023) and Xu et al. (CVPR 2023) that frozen diffusion models contain rich visual features for perception tasks, but departs from prior HOI methods (e.g., Gen-VLKT, HOICLIP) that rely on CLIP for knowledge transfer by instead leveraging diffusion models' generative understanding of mid/low-level visual concepts and their inherent compositionality. The HOI decoder architecture follows Gen-VLKT's query-based design, while the visual encoder is replaced with a single noise-free forward pass through a Stable Diffusion UNet.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to use a text-to-image diffusion model simultaneously as a feature extractor backbone and a data generator for HOI detection, addressing the long-standing gap where CLIP-based methods neglect spatial/structural cues and struggle with compositional generalization. The finding that relation embeddings learned via cycle-consistency substantially outperform plain textual descriptions for conditioning (Table 8 shows textual descriptions actually degrade performance) provides a concrete recipe for task-specific prompting of generative models. Limitations include inference speed (9.49 FPS without acceleration, comparable to two-stage methods) and the reliance on bounding box annotations for the disentanglement step. Open questions include whether the relation-inversion strategy generalizes to other relation-centric tasks (scene graph generation, video HOI) and whether the approach scales to open-vocabulary settings without pre-defined relation embeddings.
