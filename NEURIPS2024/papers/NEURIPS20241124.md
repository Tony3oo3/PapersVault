## Problem

Prior learning-augmented mechanism design for strategic facility location focused exclusively on deterministic mechanisms augmented with a single prediction of the optimal facility location. This paper addresses two open questions: whether randomized truthful mechanisms can achieve stronger robustness-consistency trade-offs than deterministic ones, and whether alternative (potentially more refined) prediction types—such as full agent locations or extreme-agent identities—can improve achievable guarantees beyond what a single optimal-location prediction provides.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241124/cropped_page9_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The mechanism receives n agents' reported locations in either 1D (a line) or 2D (Euclidean plane) plus an unreliable prediction, which may be the optimal facility location F*, the full vector of agent locations x̂, or the identities ê of the extreme agents (those at maximum distance from the optimal facility). It outputs a probability distribution over facility locations in the same metric space. The mechanism must be truthful in expectation, unanimous, anonymous, and scale-independent; predictions can be arbitrarily inaccurate, so the mechanism must maintain worst-case robustness regardless of prediction quality.

## Method

For the line, the paper proves a tight lower bound: no randomized truthful mechanism can be (1+δ)-consistent and better than (2−δ)-robust even with full agent-location predictions, by reducing to a restricted class of 'ONLYM' mechanisms (those that only place the facility at the midpoint when choosing an interior point) and deriving a contradiction from truthfulness constraints. The bound is achieved by randomizing between the LRM mechanism (1.5-robust) and the MinMaxP mechanism (1-consistent, 2-robust). For the plane, the paper introduces a new mechanism that, given predictions of extreme-agent IDs, runs the Centroid mechanism (returning the centroid with probability 1/2 and each extreme point with probability 1/2k) restricted to only the predicted extreme agents, achieving 1.67 consistency via Euler-line geometry (the centroid lies within R/3 of the circumcenter) while maintaining 2-robustness since all returned points fall inside the minimum enclosing circle. The paper also establishes a 1.118 lower bound for any randomized truthful mechanism in 2D and proves that no deterministic mechanism with full predictions can simultaneously achieve better than 2-consistency and 1+√2-robustness.

## Results

For the 1D (line) case, the paper proves a tight robustness-consistency trade-off: any randomized truthful mechanism that is (1+δ)-consistent (for δ∈[0,0.5]) cannot be better than (2−δ)-robust, even with full predictions of all agent locations. This bound is achieved by randomizing between the LRM mechanism (1.5-robust) and the MinMaxP mechanism (1-consistent, 2-robust). For the 2D (plane) case, the paper establishes a new lower bound of 1.118 for any randomized truthful mechanism, proves that no deterministic mechanism can simultaneously achieve better than 2-consistency and (1+√2)−robustness even with full predictions, and shows that no randomized mechanism can be 1-consistent and better than 2-robust. The main positive result is a mechanism using extreme-agent-ID predictions that achieves 1.67 consistency and 2 robustness in the plane, improving over the prior best of 2−1/n from Tang et al.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's main comparison is against the deterministic learning-augmented mechanism of Agrawal et al. [2022], which achieves 1-consistency and 2-robustness on the line (and 1-consistency and (1+√2)-robustness on the plane). The paper shows that on the line, this mechanism and the 1.5-robust randomized LRM mechanism of Procaccia and Tennenholtz [2013] are both Pareto optimal among all randomized mechanisms even when augmented with the strongest possible predictions (full agent locations), meaning no mechanism can improve upon either point on the robustness-consistency frontier. On the plane, the new mechanism using extreme-agent-ID predictions (Theorem 7) improves the consistency from 2−1/n (Tang et al.'s Centroid over all agents) to 1.67 while maintaining 2-robustness, by restricting the Centroid mechanism to only the predicted extreme agents and leveraging Euler line geometry. The paper also establishes that the 1.5 lower bound from the 1D case does not extend to 2D, providing a new 1.118 lower bound instead, and proves that the (1+√2)-robustness of Agrawal et al.'s deterministic mechanism is optimal even with full predictions (Theorem 4).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the learning-augmented mechanism design framework for strategic facility location introduced by Agrawal et al. (EC'22) and Xu and Lu (IJCAI'22), which were restricted to deterministic mechanisms augmented with a prediction of the optimal facility location. It builds on the foundational LRM randomized mechanism of Procaccia and Tennenholtz (2013) and the Centroid mechanism of Tang et al. (EC'20), repurposing them as building blocks for new learning-augmented randomized mechanisms. The key conceptual departure is exploring both the power of randomization and the impact of different prediction types (full agent locations vs. extreme agent IDs vs. optimal facility location), revealing that the strongest predictions do not always yield the best trade-offs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper fully characterizes the robustness-consistency Pareto frontier for randomized truthful mechanisms on the line, showing that even the strongest possible predictions cannot improve upon the trade-off between the deterministic learning-augmented mechanism (1-consistent, 2-robust) and the non-augmented randomized mechanism (1.5-robust). In two dimensions, it reveals that the 1.5 lower bound from the line does not extend, establishing a new 1.118 lower bound and showing that 1-consistency forces 2-robustness even with full predictions. The 1.67-consistency mechanism using extreme agent ID predictions and Euler line geometry is a novel positive result, but the gap between 1.118 and 2−1/n in 2D remains wide open, and many results lack tightness, leaving significant room for future work.
