## Problem

Entity alignment models require extensive, accurate seed alignments that are prohibitively expensive to obtain from cross-domain experts. While LLMs offer a path to automated annotation, two obstacles remain: the annotation space is O(|E||E'|) making exhaustive querying infeasible under a fixed budget, and LLMs produce noisy pseudo-labels that degrade alignment performance when used directly for training.

## Input / Output

The framework takes two knowledge graphs (each with entity names, relations, and relation triples) and a fixed LLM query budget B (default 0.1|E|). Before querying the LLM, candidate counterparts are filtered to the top-k=20 most similar entities via string edit distance, constraining the prompt to entity names plus relation triples to three randomly selected neighbors. The output is a trained GCN-based EA model (Dual-AMN) that produces ranked matching scores m_θ(e,e') for all cross-KG entity pairs, evaluated by Hit@1, Hit@10, and MRR in both directions.

## Method

LLM4EA is an iterative framework cycling through active selection, LLM annotation, label refinement, and EA training. Active selection computes relational uncertainty U_r (weighted by relation functionality F(r)) and neighbor uncertainty U_n (unweighted) for each source entity, then aggregates them via mean reciprocal rank to prioritize entities whose annotation most reduces uncertainty for themselves and their neighbors. The label refiner formulates pseudo-label quality as a combinatorial optimization minimizing structural incompatibility Φ(L) plus an L1 penalty for label removal, solved by a greedy algorithm that iteratively updates alignment and subrelation probabilities via functionality-based probabilistic reasoning (Eq. 5–6), adding labels exceeding threshold δ₀ and removing structurally incompatible ones. The refined labels train a Dual-AMN GCN model, whose high-confidence mutual top-1 predictions are injected back into the probabilistic reasoning system to update uncertainty estimates for the next active selection round. This closed-loop design enables the framework to be noise-adaptive: even with a weaker LLM (GPT-3.5), increasing the token budget by 2× matches GPT-4 performance at ~10× lower cost.

## Results

Using GPT-3.5 as the annotator with a budget of 0.1|E|, LLM4EA achieves MRR of 81.0% (EN-FR-15K), 92.6% (EN-DE-15K), 90.9% (D-W-15K), and 98.3% (D-Y-15K), outperforming the strongest baseline Dual-AMN by 19.4, 13.7, 19.0, and 6.9 percentage points respectively. With GPT-4, MRR improves to 86.0%, 95.3%, 92.9%, and 98.5%. Notably, increasing GPT-3.5's budget to 2× the default matches or exceeds GPT-4 performance at ~10× lower API cost, demonstrating the framework's noise-adaptive cost efficiency.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LLM4EA outperforms all six baselines across all four datasets. Against the strongest embedding-based baseline Dual-AMN, LLM4EA gains 22.3 points in Hit@1 on EN-FR-15K (74.2 vs 51.9) and 25.5 points on D-W-15K (87.5 vs 62.0), with smaller but consistent margins on EN-DE-15K (+18.6) and D-Y-15K (+11.9). RDGCN is the second-strongest baseline, trailing LLM4EA by 4.9–15.1 Hit@1 points depending on dataset. BootEA, which incorporates a label calibration step, is competitive on D-Y-15K (87.8 vs 97.7) but falls 21–29 points behind on the other three datasets. Translation-based methods (IMUSE, AlignE) and GCNAlign are substantially weaker, with IMUSE particularly struggling on D-W-15K (6.0 Hit@1) due to the lack of meaningful entity names in Wikidata. In the rule-based comparison (Table 7), LLM4EA achieves higher F1-scores than PARIS on all datasets (e.g., 86.2 vs 65.0 on EN-DE-15K), while lexical matching methods (Emb-Match, Str-Match) only remain competitive on D-Y-15K where many entities share identical names.

## Relation to Prior Work

LLM4EA departs from prior LLM-for-EA work (BERT-INT, SDEA, TEA, ChatEA), which all fine-tune a pretrained language model using clean seed alignments, by instead using LLMs in a zero-shot annotation role and treating the generated pseudo-labels as noisy training signals. The probabilistic reasoning core (functionality-based alignment probability propagation, Eqs. 5–6) is directly inherited from PARIS (Suchanek et al., 2012), but is repurposed here not for direct alignment inference but for filtering structurally incompatible labels. The active selection module adapts classical active learning to the noisy-label setting by combining relational and neighbor uncertainty metrics via rank aggregation, a design choice motivated by the absence of verifiable feedback on annotation quality.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework addresses a practical bottleneck in EA—expensive cross-domain human annotation—by showing that even noisy LLM pseudo-labels can be effectively leveraged through iterative refinement and budget-aware selection. The cost-efficiency finding (GPT-3.5 at 2× budget matches GPT-4 at 1× budget at ~10× lower API cost) offers a concrete, actionable strategy for practitioners constrained by LLM query budgets. Open questions include adaptive (non-uniform) budget allocation across iterations, extension to inductive or temporal KGs where the transductive base model must be retrained, and whether the label refiner's probabilistic reasoning scales to KGs with millions of entities.
