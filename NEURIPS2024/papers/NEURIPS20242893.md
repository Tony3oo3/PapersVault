## Problem

Existing red-teaming methods for text-to-image models focus on adversarial or malicious prompts, overlooking the more common scenario where benign users with entirely safe prompts unintentionally trigger unsafe image generation. Prior work (e.g., Adversarial Nibbler) identified this risk but relied on expensive, bias-prone crowdsourcing and human evaluation. The paper addresses the need for a fully automatic, scalable red-teaming framework that systematically discovers safe prompts causing harmful outputs across multiple toxic categories.

## Input / Output

The system takes a target toxic category (one of seven: hate, harassment, violence, self-harm, sexual, shocking, illegal activity), a set of 81 predefined keywords associated with that category, and a trivial initial prompt (e.g., 'a pic of a cat'). Over 50 iterative rounds, it produces a sequence of safe text prompts (each verified by four prompt safety detectors) and their corresponding generated images (verified by six image safety detectors). Each prompt is evaluated with 5 random seeds on the target T2I model at 512×512 resolution with guidance scale 7.5, yielding 255 prompts per model-category combination across 5 independent runs.

## Method

ART decomposes the red-teaming task into two specialized agents: a Guide Model (LLaVA-1.6-Mistral-7B fine-tuned with LoRA on a VLM dataset) that visually inspects generated images and outputs textual instructions for prompt modification, and a Writer Model (Llama-2-7B fine-tuned with LoRA on an LLM dataset) that rewrites prompts based on those instructions. This separation avoids requiring a single model to simultaneously understand images and craft text. Training data is built from a meta dataset of (safe prompt, unsafe image) pairs collected from Lexica and filtered by multiple detectors, then transformed into VLM instruction pairs and LLM prompt-rewriting pairs. The iterative loop—Writer generates prompt → T2I model generates image → Guide Model provides modification instructions → Writer rewrites—runs for 50 rounds, with Judge Models (4 prompt detectors + 6 image detectors) checking safety at each step. The use of LoRA adapters makes the framework extensible to new T2I models and emerging toxic categories without retraining from scratch.

## Results

ART achieves average success rates of 56.25%, 57.87%, and 63.31% (success ratio under all prompts) on Stable Diffusion 1.5, 2.1, and XL respectively, while maintaining a high safe-prompt ratio of 87.56% (SD 1.5), 87.56% (SD 2.1), and 87.56% (SD XL). The 'violence' category is the most vulnerable (78.06% success under safe prompts on SD 1.5), while 'harassment' is the hardest to trigger (58.70%). On the unseen FLUX.1-dev model, ART achieves up to 83.93% success (violence) with 87.84% safe-prompt ratio, outperforming Adversarial Nibbler's 61.42% overall success rate.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ART outperforms all three baselines on the primary metric of success ratio under all prompts for Stable Diffusion 1.5: 56.25% vs. Curiosity (44.31%), Groot (30.30%), and Naive (6.27%). Against Curiosity, ART also achieves a substantially higher safe-prompt ratio (87.56% vs. 90.59% — comparable) while supporting all seven toxic categories rather than only NSFW content. Against Groot, ART's advantage is most pronounced in overall efficiency: Groot's safe-prompt ratio drops to 43.72% on average (as low as 6.06% for 'hate' and 'sexual'), meaning most of its generated prompts are themselves toxic and would be filtered by real-world safeguards, whereas ART maintains ≥79.61% safe-prompt ratio across all categories. On the unseen FLUX.1-dev model, ART achieves 73.82–83.93% per-category success (safe prompts) versus Adversarial Nibbler's 61.42% overall, and a higher safe-prompt ratio (87.56% avg vs. 77.25%), demonstrating better generalizability to new models.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from prior T2I red-teaming work (FLIRT, Groot, MMA-Diffusion, Curiosity) that focuses on adversarial or explicitly harmful prompts, instead targeting the underexplored scenario where benign, safe prompts unintentionally trigger unsafe generations. It shares motivation with Adversarial Nibbler [36] but replaces costly, bias-prone crowdsourcing with an automatic LLM+VLM iterative loop, where a fine-tuned VLM (Guide Model) provides modification instructions and a fine-tuned LLM (Writer Model) rewrites prompts. The VLM-LLM decomposition is a key architectural choice: rather than asking a single model to both understand images and craft prompts, the task is split so each model handles a simpler subtask, reducing data and parameter requirements.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that popular open-source T2I models (SD 1.5, 2.1, XL) produce unsafe images at 56–63% success rates under purely safe prompts reveals a significant gap in current safeguards, and the counterintuitive result that the larger SDXL is *more* vulnerable suggests that scale alone does not improve safety. The three released datasets (MD, VD, LD) and the LoRA-based ART framework give the community reusable resources for systematic safety evaluation. Open questions include reducing cultural bias in image detectors, enabling the Guide Model to process multiple images per prompt, and extending the framework to emerging models like FLUX and commercial APIs where pre/post-processing filters are already deployed.
