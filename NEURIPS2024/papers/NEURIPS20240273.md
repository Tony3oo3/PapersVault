## Problem

Best-of-both-worlds algorithms for bandits with long-term constraints have relied exclusively on primal-dual methods, which require stringent assumptions (Slater's condition, knowledge of the Slater's parameter, or weakly-adaptive regret minimizers) and yield suboptimal polynomial dependence on the number of constraints m. This paper asks whether a simpler, UCB-based approach can match or improve upon these guarantees without such assumptions, particularly for general (non-knapsack) constraints where Slater's condition is far more restrictive.

## Input / Output

At each round the learner selects an action from K arms and observes a reward f_t(a_t) ∈ [0,1] and m cost values g_t(a_t) ∈ [-1,1]^m. The algorithm outputs a randomized strategy x_t ∈ Δ_K (sampled to produce a_t) and, crucially, a time-varying estimated feasible set X̂_t ⊆ Δ_K constructed as the intersection of m halfspaces defined by optimistic weighted-mean estimates of the costs. The regret minimizer operates on these moving sets, requiring no-regret only with respect to the intersection of all sets over the horizon, which is a weaker and more natural requirement than no-regret against a fixed set.

## Method

The algorithm has two components: (i) a modified EXP-IX regret minimizer that projects its strategy onto the current estimated feasible set via Bregman divergence, achieving Õ(√(KT)) regret against any strategy in the intersection of all moving sets; and (ii) an optimistic estimator of the feasible set built from weighted empirical means of past cost observations, where the weights are governed by adaptive learning rates η_t^(i)(a_t) = (1/n_t(a_t))(1 + Γ_t^(i)). The key insight is that the bonus term Γ_t^(i) — clipped to [0, 21√(Kt log(1/δ₂))] — is zero in stochastic settings (reducing to the empirical mean) but activates when cumulative violations grow large, effectively switching to an exponentially-weighted mean that tracks adversarial constraint changes. A UCB-style bonus b_t(a) = √(2 log(2/δ₂)/n_{t-1}(a)) ensures the true feasible set is contained in the estimate under stochastic constraints. This design yields Õ(√T) regret and violations in both settings, logarithmic dependence on m, and — uniquely — no requirement of Slater's condition in the stochastic case.

## Results

In the stochastic setting, Algorithm 1 achieves Õ(√(KT)) regret and Õ(√(KT log m)) constraint violations with high probability, without requiring Slater's condition (Theorem 7.1). In the adversarial setting, it achieves a competitive ratio of α = ρ/(1+ρ) with Õ(√(KT)) α-regret and Õ(√(KT log m)) violations (Theorem 7.2). A notable byproduct is a convergence-rate guarantee: the expected positive violation V_t^+ is bounded by Õ(√(Kt log m)), meaning the algorithm converges to strategies that are feasible in expectation (Theorem 7.3). The regret bound is independent of the number of constraints m, while violations depend only logarithmically on m.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is against primal-dual methods (Castiglioni et al. [16, 17, 11, 13, 10]), which are the only prior approaches providing best-of-both-worlds guarantees for bandits with general constraints. The proposed method improves on these in three key ways: (1) it achieves logarithmic dependence on the number of constraints m, whereas primal-dual methods exhibit polynomial dependence [17, 11, 13]; (2) it provides Õ(√T) regret in the stochastic setting without requiring Slater's condition, while [16, 28] need it to avoid O(T^{3/4}) regret; and (3) it avoids the strong requirement of weakly-adaptive primal and dual regret minimizers imposed by [17, 11, 13, 1]. Additionally, unlike prior UCB-based approaches for BwK [2, 7, 23], the algorithm guarantees no-regret even with adversarial rewards and stochastic constraints, and is the first UCB-like approach to achieve the optimal competitive ratio 1+1/ρ in the adversarial setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant primal-dual (LagrangeBwK) framework that has been the only approach providing best-of-both-worlds guarantees for bandits with long-term constraints, instead proposing an optimistic UCB-like estimation of constraints combined with a modified EXP-IX regret minimizer on moving strategy sets. The regret minimizer component is a direct modification of Neu's EXP-IX algorithm, while the constraint estimation leverages an OGD-equivalent weighted mean whose learning rates adaptively interpolate between the empirical mean (optimal for stochastic constraints) and the exponentially weighted mean (optimal for adversarial constraints). The paper positions itself as a simpler, more natural alternative that avoids the stringent assumptions of prior primal-dual methods—namely Slater's condition, knowledge of the Slater's parameter, and weakly-adaptive regret minimizers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first best-of-both-worlds algorithm for bandits with general constraints achieving logarithmic (rather than polynomial) dependence on the number of constraints, and the first to provide Õ(√T) stochastic regret without requiring Slater's condition. The conceptual insight that optimistic UCB estimation of constraints suffices for adversarial settings—when the field had assumed primal-dual machinery was necessary—opens a simpler analytical pathway for future constrained bandit problems. Open questions include whether the approach extends to contextual settings, whether the anytime violation bound can be tightened, and whether the adaptive learning rate design generalizes to non-linear constraint structures.
