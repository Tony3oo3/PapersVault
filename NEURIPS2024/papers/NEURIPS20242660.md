## Problem

Existing stability-based generalization bounds for meta-learning (e.g., Maurer 2005) require two separate stability conditions—one for single-task learning and one for the meta-level—yielding looser rates. Moreover, prior theoretical analyses largely assume convex and smooth losses, leaving the more practical regime of weakly convex, non-smooth losses (e.g., over-parameterized neural networks) without tight generalization guarantees. This paper asks whether a single, unified stability notion can yield tighter bounds and whether such bounds can be extended to weakly convex non-smooth objectives.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes a meta-sample of m tasks, each with n i.i.d. training examples, and at test time adapts to a new unseen task using n fresh examples. The bilevel structure means the meta-parameter is learned by minimizing an outer objective whose gradient depends on the solution of an inner proximal problem (RERM or K-step GD with ℓ₂ regularization toward the meta-parameter). The output is a meta-parameter w that, when combined with a test-task sample, yields a task-specific model u via the proximal update; the paper's primary deliverable is an explicit high-probability generalization bound of the form Õ(1/m + 1/n + 1/√(mn)) that is independent of the number of meta-learning iterations T and inner GD steps K.

## Method

The paper introduces uniform meta-stability, a single parameter β̄ that simultaneously captures sensitivity to replacing an entire task in the meta-sample and a single example in the test-task sample, unifying Maurer's two separate stability conditions. A novel concentration inequality (Theorem B.3) extending the Bousquet et al. (2020) partitioning technique to the two-level (task × sample) structure yields the generalization bound Õ(β̄ log(mn) log(1/δ) + √(log(1/δ)/(mn))). The authors instantiate this for a proximal meta-learning algorithm (Meta-Prox framework) with two task-specific solvers—RERM and GD—showing that the ℓ₂ proximal regularizer makes the inner problem strongly convex, so the output sensitivity is independent of iteration counts T and K. For weakly convex non-smooth losses, they require λ ≥ 2ρ and obtain a bound with an additional √(η/λ) term, using a proof technique analogous to Bassily et al. (2020) adapted to the bilevel structure. Extensions to stochastic (single-task-per-iteration) and adversarially robust variants are also provided.

## Results

The paper's main theoretical contribution is a generalization gap bound of Õ(β̄·log(mn)·log(1/δ) + M·√(log(1/δ)/(mn))) for uniformly meta-stable algorithms, where β̄ is the uniform meta-stability parameter. For convex, G-Lipschitz losses with RERM, β̄ = G²/(λm) + G²/(λn), yielding a total rate of Õ(1/m + 1/n + 1/√(mn)). For weakly convex, G-Lipschitz losses with GD, β̄ = G²√(η/λ) + G²/(λm) + G²/(λn). The empirical experiment on a synthetic sine-wave regression problem (two-layer tanh network, width 40) confirms that the generalization gap decreases at approximately O(1/m + 1/n + 1/√(mn)) as m and/or n increase.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's key comparison is against prior stability-based meta-learning bounds. Theorem 2.2 (building on Maurer 2005 and Bousquet et al. 2020) yields a rate of Õ(1/m + 1/n + 1/√m), which is strictly worse than the paper's Õ(1/m + 1/n + 1/√(mn)) for all m ≤ n². Against Guan et al. [2022], the paper studies a fundamentally different algorithm (proximal/meta-prox vs. support/query episodic training) and handles weakly convex non-smooth losses directly, whereas Guan et al. require the bilevel objective to be convex or smooth—an assumption shown to be violated in practice by Mishchenko et al. [2023]. Compared to Fallah et al. [2021], who propose a similar stability notion but for S/Q training with strongly convex losses, this paper handles the broader proximal framework and weakly convex losses. Against Denevi et al. [2019a] (online meta-learning with biased regularization), the paper's excess risk bound is tighter when n ≲ m (a common meta-learning regime), and does not require knowing the task variance Var_m a priori to set the regularization parameter λ.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies the two separate stability conditions from Maurer (2005) — uniform stability of single-task learning and uniform stability of meta-learning — into a single notion called uniform meta-stability, which yields a tighter generalization rate of Õ(1/m + 1/n + 1/√(mn)) compared to the prior Õ(1/m + 1/n + 1/√m). The algorithmic framework follows Zhou et al. (2019) Meta-Prox (proximal meta-learning), and the concentration technique extends Bousquet et al. (2020) to the two-level (task × sample) structure. The proof for weakly convex non-smooth losses adapts Bassily et al. (2020) to the bi-level optimization setting, while the adversarial robustness extension builds on the observation that the max-over-perturbations of a smooth loss is weakly convex. The paper explicitly departs from Guan et al. (2022), which studies episodic (support/query) training and assumes convex/smooth task-level losses, by directly analyzing the proximal framework with weakly convex non-smooth losses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified uniform meta-stability notion provides a cleaner and tighter theoretical lens for meta-learning generalization, and the first theoretical guarantees for adversarially robust meta-learning fill a notable gap (prior work was purely empirical). The excess risk analysis showing convergence to the approximation error σ² (average distance between task optima and the meta-parameter) gives practitioners a concrete quantity to target. Open questions highlighted include whether the bounds are tight (no lower bounds are given), how the theory connects to federated learning, and how to extend to privacy-preserving meta-learning. The empirical validation is limited to a 1D sine-wave regression toy problem, so practical relevance to deep meta-learning remains untested.
