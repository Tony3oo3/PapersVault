## Problem

Diffusion Transformers (DiTs) have universally adopted isotropic (plain stack) architectures, abandoning the U-Net structure whose inductive bias is theoretically beneficial for denoising. A naive combination of DiT blocks with a U-Net skeleton (DiT-UNet) yields only marginal gains, revealing that the U-Net's low-frequency-dominated backbone features contain redundancies that full-scale self-attention fails to exploit.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241647/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model operates on 32×32 latent representations (from the sd-vae-ft-ema VAE) corresponding to 256×256 or 512×512 RGB images. At inference, it takes random Gaussian noise in latent space and optionally a class label for conditional generation via classifier-free guidance (cfg=1.5). The U-Net processes the latent through 3 stages with 2× spatial downsampling at each encoder transition, so the bottleneck operates at 8×8 spatial resolution for 256×256 generation.

## Method

U-DiT embeds DiT blocks into a canonical 3-stage U-Net with skip connections, but its key innovation is downsampled self-attention: the QKV tuple is simultaneously converted into four 2× spatially downsampled features via pixel unshuffle, self-attention is computed independently within each downsampled feature, and outputs are merged back via pixel shuffle—saving 3/4 of self-attention FLOPs while preserving the full tensor size (no information loss). The downsampler uses a depthwise convolution with a residual shortcut plus pixel shuffle to avoid the cost of full convolutions. On top of this core mechanism, U-DiT incorporates cosine similarity attention, 2D rotary positional embeddings (RoPE2D), depthwise-convolution FFN, and re-parameterization, each contributing 2–5 FID improvement. The result is a model that outperforms DiT-XL/2 by ~10 FID at only 1/6 of its computation cost.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256, U-DiT-B (22.22 GFLOPs) achieves FID 16.64, outperforming DiT-XL/2 (118.68 GFLOPs, FID 20.05) with only 1/6 of the computation. U-DiT-L (85.00 GFLOPs) reaches FID 10.08, beating DiT-XL/2 by ~10 FIDs. With classifier-free guidance (cfg=1.5), U-DiT-L achieves FID 3.37, surpassing DiT-XL/2 (FID 6.24). At 512×512 resolution, U-DiT-B (106.7 GFLOPs) achieves FID 15.39 versus DiT-XL/2 (524.7 GFLOPs, FID 20.94).

## Baselines & Comparisons

U-DiTs consistently outperform all six baselines by large margins. Against isotropic DiTs, U-DiT-S beats DiT-S/2 by ~36 FIDs (31.51 vs 67.40) at nearly identical FLOPs, and U-DiT-B beats DiT-B/2 by ~26 FIDs (16.64 vs 42.84). The headline result is U-DiT-L (85 GFLOPs) surpassing DiT-XL/2 (118.7 GFLOPs) by ~10 FIDs (10.08 vs 20.05) while using only 1/6 the computation. Against U-ViT-XL (113 GFLOPs, FID 18.35), U-DiT-B (22.2 GFLOPs) achieves a lower FID of 16.64 with 5× fewer FLOPs. U-DiT-B also decisively beats PixArt-α-XL/2 (FID 24.75) and DiffiT-XL/2 (FID 36.86), both at ~118 GFLOPs, by 8 and 20 FIDs respectively. SiT and SiT-LLaMA (VisionLLaMA) are included in the FID-vs-FLOPs plot (Fig. 1) where U-DiTs dominate across the entire compute range.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the isotropic transformer convention established by DiT and its successors (DiffiT, FiT, VisionLLaMA), which all abandoned the U-Net architecture for latent-space diffusion. It re-introduces a canonical multi-stage U-Net structure around DiT blocks, but the key conceptual departure is the radical token downsampling of the full QKV tuple for self-attention, extending prior key-value-only downsampling (CMT, SRFormer, TODO) to a lossless, parallel formulation. The frequency-domain motivation draws on FreeU's observation that U-Net backbone features are low-frequency-dominated and on the theoretical analysis of U-Net downsampling as a noise filter in diffusion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper challenges the widely-held assumption (explicitly stated by DiT's authors) that U-Net inductive bias is 'not crucial' for latent diffusion transformers, showing that when properly leveraged via token downsampling, the U-Net structure yields dramatically better FID at a fraction of the compute. The practical impact is significant: a simple architectural modification (downsample QKV before attention, merge after) gives U-DiT-B a FID of 16.64 versus DiT-XL/2's 20.05 at 1/6 the FLOPs. Open questions include whether the approach transfers to text-to-image generation, video diffusion, and larger resolutions, and whether the gains persist at much longer training schedules (the paper trains to 1M steps vs. DiT's 7M).
