## Problem

In online bipartite matching, agents are partitioned into known classes and items arrive irrevocably, yet prior work has largely ignored whether fairness across classes can be guaranteed simultaneously with efficiency. The central open problem from Hosseini et al. (2022) asked whether any randomized algorithm for indivisible items can achieve a non-trivial class envy-freeness (CEF) approximation while also ensuring non-wastefulness or a utilitarian social welfare (USW) bound. This paper resolves that question affirmatively and further characterizes the fundamental trade-off between fairness and efficiency via a price-of-fairness result.

## Input / Output

The algorithm receives a bipartite graph G=(N,M,E) where agents N are partitioned into k known classes, and items in M arrive one-by-one in an adversarial order; upon each arrival, the incident edges to unsaturated agents are revealed and the algorithm must make an irrevocable assignment. The output is a matching that simultaneously satisfies 1/2-CEF, 1/2-CPROP, 1/2-USW, and non-wastefulness in expectation. A key structural constraint is that the class-level optimistic valuation V_i* is subadditive rather than additive, which fundamentally complicates the fairness analysis compared to standard fair-division settings.

## Method

The RANDOM algorithm performs a two-stage randomization: upon each item's arrival, it first selects a class uniformly at random from those containing at least one unsaturated agent who likes the item, then selects an agent uniformly at random within that class. The CEF proof introduces a novel technique of augmenting each class's matched set with 'dummy items' (copies of items that arrived when all class agents were already saturated), showing that the optimistic value of this augmented set is at most twice the actual matching size, and then proving via a probability-dominance lemma that the augmented set stochastically dominates any other class's allocation. The CPROP guarantee is established through a modified Equal-Filling-OCS analysis using independent per-agent rounding and an integration-by-parts argument over the agent saturation density. The paper complements these lower bounds with adversarial constructions yielding upper bounds of (e²−1)/(e²+1)≈0.761 for randomized indivisible CEF and 0.677 for deterministic divisible CEF, and proves a price-of-fairness theorem showing that any α-CEF guarantee forces USW to degrade to at most 1/(1+α).

## Results

The RANDOM algorithm simultaneously achieves 1/2-CEF, 1/2-CPROP, and 1/2-USW while maintaining non-wastefulness for indivisible online matching — the first such algorithm to resolve the open problem from Hosseini et al. (2022). The paper proves a matching upper bound showing no non-wasteful randomized algorithm can exceed (e²−1)/(e²+1) ≈ 0.761-CEF for indivisible items, and no deterministic algorithm can exceed 0.677-CEF for divisible items. Finally, the price-of-fairness result establishes that any α-CEF algorithm is limited to USW ≤ 1/(1+α) + ε, demonstrating an inverse proportionality between fairness and efficiency.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Compared to Hosseini et al. [21], the RANDOM algorithm's key advantage is achieving a 1/2-CEF guarantee *simultaneously* with non-wastefulness — a combination [21] explicitly left as an open problem. While [21] achieved a stronger 0.593-CPROP for indivisible matching via their Equal-Filling-OCS algorithm, that algorithm does not admit a CEF analysis, so the paper's simpler RANDOM algorithm trades a slightly weaker CPROP (1/2 vs. 0.593) for the first-ever CEF + non-wastefulness guarantee. For divisible matching, [21]'s 1−1/e CEF guarantee is improved upon by this paper's upper bound of 0.677, showing [21]'s algorithm is nearly tight. Relative to the classical Karp et al. [28] benchmark, the 1−1/e USW bound remains the gold standard for efficiency, but the paper shows that enforcing CEF fairness necessarily degrades USW: at the 0.761-CEF ceiling, USW drops below 0.568, well short of the 0.632 achievable without fairness constraints.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends the online class fair matching framework introduced by Hosseini et al. [21], which established the problem of allocating online-arriving items to agents partitioned into classes under fairness constraints (CEF, CPROP, USW) but left open whether a randomized non-wasteful algorithm could achieve any CEF guarantee. The impossibility constructions build on the upper triangular graph of Karp, Vazirani, and Vazirani [28] (the RANKING algorithm's tight 1−1/e bound), extending it to a two-class instance to derive CEF-specific upper bounds. The CPROP analysis modifies the Equal-Filling-OCS rounding technique from [21] with a simpler independent rounding scheme, trading a slightly weaker CPROP guarantee (1/2 vs. 0.593) for the ability to simultaneously prove CEF.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper resolves the central open problem from [21] by showing a simple randomized algorithm achieves 1/2-CEF, 1/2-CPROP, 1/2-USW, and non-wastefulness simultaneously, and introduces the 'price of fairness' for online matching, demonstrating an inverse proportionality between CEF approximation and achievable USW. The gap between the 1/2 algorithmic guarantee and the 0.761 upper bound for indivisible CEF remains open, as does the question of whether RANKING-style priority ordering can close it. The connection to Nash social welfare (showing CMNW implies CEF1 but not conversely) provides a useful bridge to the broader fair division literature, while the practical motivation—fair allocation in food banks, ad slots, and ridesharing—underscores the real-world relevance of these theoretical bounds.
