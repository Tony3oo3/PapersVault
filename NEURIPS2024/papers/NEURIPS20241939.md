## Problem

Existing text-to-3D generation methods guided by T2I or TC2I diffusion models produce anatomically inconsistent animals (extra/missing limbs, Janus-head artifacts) and cannot faithfully generate novel or imaginary creatures that are difficult to describe purely in text. No single parametric shape or pose prior exists for the full diversity of tetrapods, and prior controllable 3D generation methods rely on restrictive 2D conditions (depth, edges) that limit geometric freedom.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt describing the animal (e.g., 'a tiger', 'a dragon with three heads') and a 3D pose skeleton defined by 18 keypoints (eyes, nose, neck, 4×thighs/knees/paws, back, tail) connected by a bone sequence. The 3D pose can be auto-generated by a multi-agent LLM from a library of 16 reference animal poses, or manually created/edited in a web-based tool. The output is a 3D animal represented as an Instant-NGP NeRF rendering at 128×128×3 RGB, with an optional initial mesh generated from the skeleton using simple primitives (cylinders, cones, ellipses) for pre-training.

## Method

YOUDREAM introduces a TetraPose ControlNet trained on 13k curated 2D animal pose annotations (from AwA-pose and Animal Kingdom) to generate pose-conditioned images of tetrapods across mammals, reptiles, birds, and amphibians. For 3D generation, Score Distillation Sampling is adapted so that the 2D projection of the 3D pose for each randomly sampled camera view serves as the ControlNet condition, enforcing multi-view anatomical consistency without any 3D training data. A multi-agent LLM pipeline (Finder → Observer → Modifier, all using GPT-4o) automatically adapts a reference pose from a 16-animal library to produce the 3D skeleton for a novel animal. A key training strategy schedules the ControlNet scale with cosine decay (strong geometry early) while linearly increasing classifier-free guidance scale (strong appearance later), compensating for the ControlNet's lower diversity relative to Stable Diffusion. An RGB-domain loss and an initial shape pre-training stage (using depth-guided ControlNet on the skeleton-derived mesh) further stabilize geometry and reduce flickering.

## Results

In a user study with 32 participants evaluating 22 text-to-3D prompts (13 real animals, 9 unreal creatures), YOUDREAM receives 60–62% preference in both Naturalness and Text-Image Alignment categories, outperforming HiFA, Fantasia3D, 3DFuse, and MVDream. On CLIP similarity score (ViT-B/32, averaged over 9 views), YOUDREAM achieves 30.86 versus MVDream's 29.78. The multi-agent LLM pose generator achieves 91% user agreement that generated 3D poses correctly represent the requested animal (16 novel poses, 32 participants).

## Baselines & Comparisons

YOUDREAM outperforms all five main baselines (HiFA, Fantasia3D, 3DFuse, MVDream, and the additional T2I-guided methods Stable Dreamfusion, ProlificDreamer, LucidDreamer) in the user study, securing 60–62% preference in both Naturalness and Text-Image Alignment. Against MVDream specifically, YOUDREAM also wins on CLIP score (30.86 vs. 29.78), despite MVDream being a 3D-aware TC2I model trained on 3D objects while YOUDREAM uses no 3D training data. HiFA and Fantasia3D produce anatomically inconsistent animals (extra/missing limbs, Janus-head), while 3DFuse's reliance on image-to-point-cloud prediction leads to implausible geometry (e.g., elephant, T-Rex). For unreal creatures (e.g., six-legged lioness, three-headed dragon), MVDream fails to follow prompts faithfully (e.g., generating three legs instead of six for the Wampus cat), whereas YOUDREAM's pose control ensures correct anatomy. Against 3DFauna (a 3D animal reconstruction method), YOUDREAM produces higher-frequency detail and more faithful adherence to the input pose.

## Relation to Prior Work

YOUDREAM builds on the DreamFusion SDS framework and the ControlNet architecture, but departs from prior text-to-3D methods (HiFA, Fantasia3D, 3DFuse) that rely solely on text prompts or depth/edge cues, which the authors argue are insufficient for maintaining anatomical consistency in deformable objects. It also contrasts with 3D-aware diffusion models like MVDream, which learn multi-view consistency from 3D training data but suffer from limited diversity due to the scarcity of 3D datasets. The paper extends the human avatar generation paradigm (SMPL/SMPL-X pose priors) to the animal domain, where no single parametric model exists, by introducing a learned TetraPose ControlNet and a multi-agent LLM that adapts poses from a small 16-animal library rather than relying on a fixed parametric shape.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method addresses a practical gap: artists can specify anatomical structure (number of limbs, heads, tails, specific poses) that is inexpressible through text alone, enabling generation of novel creatures while maintaining geometric consistency. The multi-agent LLM pipeline reduces the human effort of pose creation to near-zero for common animals, making the tool accessible. Key limitations include restriction to four-limbed animals, a relatively low 128×128 NeRF resolution, and the need for a 3D pose input (even if automated). Open questions include scaling to more complex morphologies (insects, multi-limbed creatures), efficient animation from pose sequences, and whether the pose-control strategy generalizes beyond animals to other deformable categories.
