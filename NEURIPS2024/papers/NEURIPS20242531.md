## Problem

Multi-humanoid collaborative object transportation (e.g., two robots carrying a sofa) remains largely unaddressed because (1) motion capture data for multi-agent interactions is scarce and expensive, and (2) training multi-agent policies from scratch fails to converge due to the combinatorially expanded action space. Existing tracking-based approaches also cannot generalize to varying object sizes or arbitrary numbers of agents.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242531/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each agent observes a 300-dimensional vector comprising a 225-dim body state (root height/rotation/velocity, 12 joint positions/rotations/velocities in local frame) and a 75-dim goal feature (object position, rotation, bounding-box dynamics at the relevant end, target location/orientation, and bounding-box dimensions). The critical design choice is that in the multi-agent phase, each agent observes only the bounding box at *its own* end of the long object, making the object's dynamics a shared, physically-mediated communication channel. The policy outputs joint torques for a 28-DOF humanoid (12 actuated joints) at 30 Hz control frequency within a 60 Hz physics simulation.

## Method

CooHOI employs a two-phase curriculum: (1) a single humanoid learns object carrying via AMP (adversarial motion priors) with object bounding-box dynamics (vertices, rotation, velocity, angular velocity) embedded in the observation, plus stand-point and held-point rewards that bias the agent toward the shortest edge and geometric center of each end; (2) the single-agent policy is replicated to all agents and fine-tuned with MAPPO under a CTDE scheme with parameter sharing, where each agent observes only its end's bounding box. The key insight is that the rigid-body dynamics of the shared object act as an implicit communication channel: when one agent pushes or lifts, the bounding box at the other agent's end changes, and because the policy was trained to react to such dynamics changes, the teammate automatically adjusts without explicit messaging. Ablations confirm that removing any of the stand points, dynamics observation, reverse-walking skill, or single-agent initialization causes training to fail or deadlock.

## Results

In the single-agent box-carrying task, CooHOI achieves a 96.48% success rate with 6.9 cm precision over a 1–20 m distance range, outperforming InterPhys (94.3% success, 8.3 cm precision over 1–10 m). When weight information is included in the observation (CooHOI+WeightAug), the method matches InterPhys's success rate (93.98% vs. 94.3%) while nearly halving the precision error (4.8 cm vs. 8.3 cm) and doubling the transport distance. In the two-agent cooperative setting, CooHOI achieves an 89.54% success rate with 3.86 cm precision for carrying 15–40 kg objects over 2–20 m, whereas training a multi-agent policy from scratch yields 0% success. The framework also generalizes to diverse household objects (tables, armchairs, high stools, sofas) with success rates above 84%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two baselines. Against InterPhys [7] (the primary single-agent baseline), CooHOI+WeightAug matches the success rate (93.98% vs. 94.3%) while achieving substantially better precision (4.8 cm vs. 8.3 cm) and a wider transport distance range (1–20 m vs. 1–10 m); the plain CooHOI variant (without weight in observation) pushes success rate to 96.48% but narrows the weight range to 2–13 kg. Against the 'From Scratch' multi-agent baseline (training a two-agent MAPPO policy without the two-phase CooHOI curriculum), the comparison is stark: from-scratch training completely fails (0% success, NaN precision), while CooHOI's skill-transfer approach achieves 89.54% success with 3.86 cm precision. The ablation studies further confirm that removing any single component of the CooHOI framework (stand points, dynamic observation, reverse walking, or single-agent initialization) causes the two-agent policy to fail, underscoring that the two-phase design is essential rather than merely beneficial.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the AMP framework [25] for single-agent physics-based character control and the InterPhys [7] formulation for object manipulation, extending both into a multi-agent cooperative setting. It departs from prior multi-character interaction work (e.g., Zhang et al. [44]) that relied on tracking-based methods requiring expensive multi-agent motion capture data, instead proposing a two-phase paradigm: single-agent AMP skill learning followed by CTDE multi-agent RL (MAPPO [40]). The key conceptual departure is using manipulated object bounding-box dynamics as an implicit communication channel between agents, eliminating the need for explicit coordination signals or multi-agent reference motions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work addresses a practical gap in humanoid robotics—collaborative transport of large objects—without the prohibitive cost of multi-agent motion capture, making the approach more scalable to arbitrary numbers of agents and object types. The implicit communication mechanism (observing object dynamics changes caused by a teammate) is an elegant and generalizable coordination strategy that could inform broader multi-agent physical interaction research. However, limitations remain: the bounding-box representation constrains generalization to diverse object shapes, characters lack dexterous hands, and all results are simulation-only with no real-world validation, leaving open questions about transfer to physical humanoid robots.
