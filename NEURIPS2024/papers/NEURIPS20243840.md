## Problem

Reference-free diversity metrics for generative models, specifically VENDI and RKE scores, require eigendecomposition of an n×n kernel similarity matrix, incurring at least Ω(n²) and Ω(n^2.373) computational costs respectively. This quadratic or super-quadratic scaling makes accurate diversity estimation infeasible for the large sample sizes (hundreds of thousands) needed to capture the full variety of modern generative models, while reference-based alternatives (FID, Precision/Recall) are unavailable in prompt-based or novel-domain settings where no suitable reference dataset exists.

## Input / Output

The method consumes pre-computed embeddings of generated samples (e.g., DinoV2 768-d vectors for images, text-embedding-3-large for text, I3D 1024-d vectors for video), a Gaussian kernel bandwidth σ tuned per dataset, and an RFF dimension r. It outputs approximate VENDI_α and RKE diversity scores along with 2r proxy eigenvectors that assign each sample a likelihood score per identified mode, enabling interpretable cluster visualization. The stochastic implementation processes samples one at a time, accumulating a 2r×2r covariance matrix, so memory usage is independent of n and runtime grows only linearly with sample count.

## Method

FKEA replaces the n×n kernel matrix eigendecomposition with a random Fourier features (RFF) approximation: for a shift-invariant kernel k(x,x')=κ(x−x'), Bochner's theorem guarantees the Fourier transform κ̂ is a valid PDF, from which r frequency vectors ω₁,…,ω_r are drawn i.i.d. to construct a 2r-dimensional proxy feature map φ̃_r(x) = (1/√r)[cos(ω₁ᵀx), sin(ω₁ᵀx), …, cos(ω_rᵀx), sin(ω_rᵀx)]. The 2r×2r proxy covariance matrix C̃_{X,r} = (1/n)Σᵢ φ̃_r(xᵢ)φ̃_r(xᵢ)ᵀ shares its non-zero eigenvalues with the RFF proxy kernel matrix, and Theorem 2 proves that r = O(log n / ε²) suffices for an ε-bounded eigenspace approximation with probability ≥ 1−δ. The RKE score reduces to the Frobenius norm of C̃_{X,r} (no eigendecomposition needed), while general VENDI_α requires only a 2r×2r eigendecomposition. The proxy eigenvectors further yield interpretable mode score functions Û_i(x) that assign each sample a likelihood of belonging to the i-th identified cluster, connecting the entropy computation to unsupervised spectral clustering.

## Results

FKEA achieves a ~160× speedup over exact RKE at n=30k (25s vs. 4007s) and a ~200× speedup over exact VENDI (27s vs. 5488s), while scaling to n=250k samples where both exact baselines overflow memory at n=40k. On ImageNet with 50k samples, FKEA-VENDI-1 scores range from 7120.5 (BigGAN) to 8626.5 (DiT-XL-2), with the real dataset scoring 9176.9, and FKEA-RKE scores range from 492.4 (BigGAN) to 855.8 (DiT-XL-2) versus 996.7 for the dataset. The method's theoretical guarantee (Theorem 2) shows that only O(log n / ε²) Fourier features are needed for an ε-accurate eigenspace approximation, yielding O(n) linear complexity in sample size.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares FKEA against two exact baselines: the original RKE score [Jalali et al., NeurIPS 2023] and the VENDI score [Friedman & Dieng, TMLR 2023]. FKEA-RKE is approximately 31× faster at n=10k (7s vs. 217s), 83× faster at n=20k (16s vs. 1324s), and 160× faster at n=30k (25s vs. 4007s). FKEA-VENDI shows a similar trend: 26× faster at n=10k (11s vs. 286s) and 203× faster at n=30k (27s vs. 5488s). Critically, both exact baselines suffer memory overflow beyond n=30k–40k, while FKEA scales linearly to n=250k (238s for RKE, 267s for VENDI). The trade-off is that FKEA provides an approximation rather than an exact score, but Theorem 2 guarantees the approximation error is bounded by O(√(log n / r)), and empirical results (Figures 2, 3, 5) confirm the approximated scores track the exact scores closely and correctly reflect diversity changes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the VENDI score family (Friedman & Dieng, 2023) and the RKE score (Jalali et al., NeurIPS 2023), which compute Rényi entropy of kernel matrix eigenvalues as reference-free diversity metrics, and on the Random Fourier Features framework (Rahimi & Recht, 2007) for approximating shift-invariant kernels. The key departure is a reduction-theoretic lower bound (Theorem 1) showing that computing VENDI_α for α≠2 is at least as hard as matrix multiplication, combined with a constructive upper bound via RFFs that reduces the problem to O(n) time with O(log n/ε²) features. The interpretability angle—using FKEA's proxy eigenvectors to identify sample clusters—extends the kernel PCA / spectral clustering connection studied in prior work (Bengio et al., 2003) to the diversity-evaluation setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Reference-free diversity evaluation is essential for prompt-based generative models (e.g., Stable Diffusion XL) where no fixed reference dataset exists, yet exact VENDI/RKE computation becomes intractable beyond ~30k samples due to quadratic or super-quadratic complexity. FKEA's O(n) scaling to 250k+ samples with provable ε-accuracy makes these metrics practically usable for large-scale evaluation across image, text, and video modalities. Key limitations include restriction to shift-invariant kernels (excluding polynomial kernels) and dependence on embedding quality; open questions include sample complexity under high-dimensional feature maps and the role of different embedding spaces in shaping the identified clusters.
