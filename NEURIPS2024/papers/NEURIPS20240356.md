## Problem

Existing Referring 3D Segmentation methods rely on a two-stage pipeline (instance segmentation followed by language matching) that can miss the target object during pre-segmentation and requires both instance and semantic labels for training, making annotation time-consuming and labor-intensive. No prior work has shown that a single-stage approach supervised solely by binary object masks can achieve competitive or superior performance on this task.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240356/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D point cloud scene of N points with 6-dimensional RGBXYZ attributes (voxelized into regular voxels for sparse convolution) and a free-form natural language query sentence as input, and produces a binary per-point segmentation mask (0 or 1) identifying all points belonging to the described object. Unlike prior methods that require instance IDs and semantic category labels per point, LESS is trained exclusively with a single binary mask indicating the target object, reducing annotation effort by over 90% per sample.

## Method

LESS integrates a sparse 3D U-Net backbone with a Point-Word Cross-Modal Alignment (PWCA) module that applies cross-attention at each of the five encoder stages to fuse word-level text features into multi-scale point features, followed by a Tanh-gated residual connection. A Query Mask Predictor (QMP) with masked cross-attention decodes K=20 learnable queries into proposal masks, and a Query-Sentence Alignment (QSA) module computes softmax similarity between query embeddings and the sentence-level feature to produce a weighted combination of proposals as the final mask. Training combines three losses: standard BCE segmentation loss, an area regularization loss that minimizes the mean predicted probability across all points to suppress large-scale background, and a point-to-point contrastive loss that pulls L2-normalized features of positive (target) points toward their centroid while pushing them away from negative (background) points, enabling fine-grained separation of visually similar objects.

## Results

On the ScanRefer validation set, LESS with RoBERTa achieves 33.74% mIoU and 53.23% Acc@0.25, surpassing the best two-stage baseline (X-RefSeg with BERT) by 3.8% mIoU and 12.9% Acc@0.25 while using only binary mask supervision. The label effort drops from >20 minutes per sample (two-stage) to <2 minutes (LESS), saving over 90% annotation time. However, LESS trails on Acc@0.5 (29.88% vs. 33.77% for X-RefSeg BERT), which the authors attribute to the absence of instance-level prior knowledge in the single-stage pipeline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against TGNN (AAAI 2021), LESS with RoBERTa improves mIoU by 6.94% (33.74 vs. 26.10 with GRU) and Acc@0.25 by 18.23%, while also matching or exceeding TGNN-BERT on mIoU (33.74 vs. 27.80). Against X-RefSeg (AAAI 2024), the strongest two-stage baseline, LESS wins on mIoU (33.74 vs. 29.94, +3.8%) and Acc@0.25 (53.23 vs. 40.33, +12.9%) but loses on Acc@0.5 (29.88 vs. 33.77, −3.89%), a gap the authors explain by the lack of instance proposals that guarantee high-IoU matches. Against the concurrent 3D-STMN (AAAI 2024), trained from scratch for fair comparison, LESS outperforms by 11.34% mIoU, 18.27% Acc@0.25, and 12.65% Acc@0.5, demonstrating that the single-stage binary-mask approach is substantially more effective than the superpoint-text matching strategy when neither method benefits from pre-trained instance segmentation weights.

## Relation to Prior Work

LESS departs from the dominant two-stage paradigm in Referring 3D Segmentation (TGNN, X-RefSeg), which first performs instance segmentation with instance and semantic labels, then matches proposals to text queries. Instead, it repurposes the binary-mask supervision paradigm from referring image segmentation and the query-based mask decoding architecture from 3D instance segmentation (Mask3D, SuperPoint Transformer) into a single-stage pipeline. The Point-Word Cross-Modal Alignment module introduces a fine-grained cross-attention mechanism between word-level text features and multi-scale point features, while the Query-Sentence Alignment module adds a coarse-grained sentence-level matching step—combining granularities that prior 3D referring methods handled separately or not at all.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that binary mask supervision alone can surpass instance+semantic label supervision in mIoU on ScanRefer, LESS challenges the assumption that richer 3D annotations are necessary for competitive referring segmentation, potentially reducing annotation costs by over 90%. However, the persistent gap on Acc@0.5 and the acknowledged difficulty with ambiguous queries and visually similar objects suggest that binary supervision still lacks the structural inductive bias that instance labels provide. Open questions include whether this label-efficient paradigm generalizes beyond ScanRefer to larger or more cluttered scenes, and whether the area regularization and contrastive losses can be replaced by learned or self-supervised alternatives that scale to open-vocabulary settings.
