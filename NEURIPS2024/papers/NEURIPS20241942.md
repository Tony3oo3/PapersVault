## Problem

Large language models using RoPE positional encoding exhibit a persistent 'lost-in-the-middle' bias, where attention is disproportionately allocated to the beginning and end of long contexts while neglecting relevant information in the middle. Existing remedies either require fine-tuning, additional memory banks, multiple inference passes, or primarily target context-length extension rather than fixing the positional bias itself, leaving a gap for a zero-overhead, plug-and-play solution.

## Input / Output

The method operates on any pre-trained LLM that uses RoPE (e.g., Llama-2, Vicuna, StableBeluga) and takes a standard input text sequence as its only input. During the prefilling stage, it computes a per-head position-awareness score from the attention pattern of the most recent query, then assigns a fixed re-scaling ratio to each attention head for the remainder of inference. The output is standard generated text with no change in format, and the approach introduces no additional memory or compute overhead beyond the original forward pass.

## Method

Ms-PoE re-scales RoPE position indices by substituting position m with m/r, which compresses long-range positions into a shorter effective range and alleviates RoPE's long-term decay. Rather than applying a single uniform ratio, it assigns distinct scaling ratios per attention head based on a Position-Awareness Score (S_P) that measures the fraction of attention mass a head places on tokens exceeding a threshold (α=3× mean). Heads with high S_P (position-aware, reliably tracking relevant content across positions) receive ratios near 1 to preserve pre-trained behavior, while position-unaware heads receive larger ratios to counteract their bias toward beginning/end tokens. Ratios are linearly interpolated from R_min=1.2 to R_max=1.8 across heads sorted by S_P, determined once during prefilling and held fixed during decoding. This multi-scale design fuses short-range fidelity (preserving pre-training knowledge) with long-range sensitivity (recovering middle-context information) without any fine-tuning or architectural modification.

## Results

On the Zero-SCROLLS benchmark (7 tasks spanning summarization, QA, and information aggregation), Ms-PoE achieves an average accuracy gain of up to +3.8 over the original LLMs without any fine-tuning. The largest gains appear on Vicuna-7B (15.0 → 18.8 avg) and StableBeluga-7B (16.5 → 19.1 avg), with per-task improvements reaching +13.4 on Qasper and +7.2 on BookSumSort. On the MDQA and Key-Value Retrieval tasks, Ms-PoE reduces the gap between best and worst position accuracy by 2–4%, directly alleviating the 'lost-in-the-middle' effect. On LongBench-EN (13 tasks), Llama-2-7B-Chat improves from 35.82 to 38.02 average score.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares Ms-PoE against two positional-encoding modification baselines: Positional Interpolation (PI) [Chen et al., 2023] and Self-Extend [Jin et al., 2024], both of which apply a uniform scaling ratio across all attention heads. On MDQA (Vicuna-7B), Ms-PoE achieves 65.04% average accuracy, outperforming PI (62.40%) by +2.64 and Self-Extend (62.32%) by +2.72. On Key-Value Retrieval, the gap widens substantially: Ms-PoE reaches 79.96% versus PI's 67.60% (+12.36) and Self-Extend's 66.00% (+13.96), with the largest single-position gain of +43.72 at position 40. Ms-PoE also surpasses the attention-sorting approach [Peysakhovich & Lerer, 2023] in the scenario where the relevant document is already at the end of the input, where reordering-based methods cannot gain further improvement. The ablation (Table 3) confirms that the head-wise position-awareness scoring strategy is critical: random or sequential assignment of scaling ratios degrades performance below baseline, while the proposed position-awareness metric yields the best results across all positions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Positional Interpolation (PI) and Self-Extend, which rescale RoPE position indices to extend context windows, but repurposes the same mechanism to address the 'lost-in-the-middle' problem rather than length extrapolation. It departs from prior context-extension work by introducing a head-wise position-awareness score that assigns distinct scaling ratios (1.2–1.8) to different attention heads, drawing an analogy to multi-scale feature fusion in Inception-style vision models. Unlike attention sorting or Attention Buckets, which require reordering inputs or multiple forward passes, Ms-PoE is a single-pass, zero-overhead modification applied only to the positional encoding during the prefilling stage.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Ms-PoE offers a genuinely plug-and-play remedy for a well-documented and practically limiting failure mode of LLMs, requiring no fine-tuning, no extra memory, and no architectural changes—making it immediately deployable on any RoPE-based model. However, the evaluation is confined to RoPE-based models at 7B–13B scale, and the authors themselves note the limitation of not exploring ALiBi or other positional encodings. Open questions include whether the head-wise position-awareness property persists in larger or differently-architected models, whether the narrow 1.2–1.8 scaling range generalizes across tasks, and whether the approach composes with other context-extension techniques like YaRN or NTK-aware scaling.
