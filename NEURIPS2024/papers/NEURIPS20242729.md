## Problem

Existing symmetry discovery methods either assume generators belong to a predefined Lie group (restricting them to linear or affine transformations) or, when making no such assumption, only succeed on toy datasets far from real-world scale. Discovering continuous symmetries—including non-affine and approximate ones—from high-dimensional real-world data like CIFAR-10 or PDE solutions, without any prior knowledge of the symmetry structure, remains an open problem.

## Input / Output

The method consumes either 32×32 RGB images (CIFAR-10) or PDE solutions discretized on a 256×140 rectangular grid, along with a frozen pre-trained feature extractor (ResNet-18) or a PDE residual operator. It produces a set of N_sym learned infinitesimal generators, each an MLP mapping the spatial domain (or product space X×U for PDEs) to a vector field, from which one-parameter group transformations are generated by numerical ODE integration. For images, the transformation acts on the spatial domain via (ϑ_X(f))(x) = f(ϑ_X^{-1}(x)) with bilinear resampling; for PDEs, it acts jointly on (x, t, u) and requires WENO-based numerical differentiation to evaluate the PDE residual on the transformed, off-grid solution.

## Method

The core mechanism parameterizes each infinitesimal generator as an MLP h_θ: Z→R^n, whose flow ϑ_s^{h_θ} is computed by solving the autonomous ODE dϑ_s/ds = h_θ(ϑ_s) via a Neural ODE solver with adjoint backpropagation, thereby searching over the full infinite-dimensional space of smooth one-parameter groups rather than a finite-dimensional affine subspace. Training minimizes a task-specific differentiable validity score—cosine similarity of pre-trained features for images, or the sum of absolute PDE residuals for PDEs—averaged over random transformation scales s ~ Unif([−σ, σ]). Two regularizers prevent degenerate solutions: an orthonormality loss (with stop-gradient) that enforces mutual orthogonality of the learned vector fields so true symmetries occupy the first slots, and a Lipschitz loss that penalizes large vector-field variations between neighboring grid points to avoid boundary-only transformations. All loss terms are designed to be dimensionless (e.g., arccos for inner products, log for PDE residuals), enabling stable training with fixed hyperparameters across tasks.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, the learned symmetry augmentation achieves 94.9±0.1% test accuracy with ResNet-18, competitive with the known affine augmentation (95.1±0.2%) and default augmentation (95.1±0.1%), while no augmentation yields only 92.4±0.3%. For PDE solving with Fourier Neural Operators, the learned symmetry augmentation on KdV reduces test NMSE from 4.47 (no augmentation) to 1.12 with 2^5 training samples, approaching the ground-truth LPS result of 0.980. On cKdV with 2^5 data, combining the learned approximate symmetry (AS) with LPS achieves (3.70±0.15)×10⁻⁴ NMSE, outperforming LPS alone (5.90±0.17)×10⁻⁴ and no augmentation (7.90±1.21)×10⁻⁴.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is Brandstetter et al. [4], which uses known Lie Point Symmetries (LPS) for data augmentation of neural PDE solvers. On CIFAR-10, the learned symmetry augmentation (94.9%) is within 0.2% of the known affine augmentation (95.1%), demonstrating that the method can recover the correct affine generators from scratch without any prior knowledge of the symmetry group. On PDE tasks (KdV, KS), the learned symmetry augmentation consistently approaches but slightly trails the ground-truth LPS augmentation (e.g., KdV at 2^5 data: 1.12 vs. 0.980 NMSE), confirming the method recovers the correct symmetries. Notably, on cKdV the method discovers an approximate symmetry (time translation) that is not a true LPS; combining this AS with LPS outperforms LPS alone by ~37% in NMSE at 2^5 data (3.70×10⁻⁴ vs. 5.90×10⁻⁴), showing the method can find useful transformations invisible to classical symmetry analysis. The paper also positions itself against prior symmetry discovery methods (LieGAN, LieGG, L-conv, Forestano et al.) in Table 2, emphasizing that it is the first to learn affine symmetries from a real-world dataset (CIFAR-10) with no prior knowledge of the generators, whereas prior work was limited to toy datasets or required partial knowledge of the affine group.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on Neural ODEs [6] as the mechanism for parameterizing one-parameter groups, but departs fundamentally from prior symmetry discovery methods that either assume affine/linear generators (LieGAN, LieGG) or operate only on low-dimensional toy datasets (Liu & Tegmark, Forestano et al.). The key conceptual departure is the introduction of a task-specific differentiable validity score (cosine similarity of features for images, PDE residual for PDEs) that guides the search over the full space of continuous transformations, combined with orthonormality and Lipschitz regularizations to prevent trivial solutions. Unlike Gabel et al. [14], which learns PDE symmetries in a supervised setting, this method requires no prior knowledge of the symmetry group structure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to recover affine symmetries from a real-world dataset (CIFAR-10) by searching the entire space of continuous transformations without assuming a specific group, and it additionally discovers approximate symmetries in PDEs that classical Lie point symmetry analysis cannot identify. The finding that these approximate symmetries improve neural operator training, especially with limited data, suggests a broader principle: data-driven symmetry discovery can capture useful invariances invisible to algebraic methods. Open questions include scaling to higher-dimensional or non-periodic domains, handling non-local and conditional symmetries, and reducing the computational cost of ODE-based augmentation relative to closed-form transforms.
