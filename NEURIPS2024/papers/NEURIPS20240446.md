## Problem

Laplace approximations for Bayesian neural networks require a prior in weight space, but weight space is not interpretable, making it virtually impossible to specify informative priors beyond the default isotropic Gaussian, which exhibits known pathologies in deep networks. Existing function-space prior methods (e.g., functional variational inference) suffer from infinite KL divergence for most GP priors of interest, leaving no practical procedure to incorporate structured, interpretable prior knowledge (regularity, periodicity, length scale) into the Laplace approximation.

## Input / Output

The method takes a trained neural network at its MAP estimate w*, a GP prior specified by a mean function μ and covariance kernel Σ, and a set of context points C that define where function-space regularization is enforced. It produces a (potentially degenerate) Gaussian posterior over weights N(w*, S S^⊤) computed via a matrix-free low-rank factorization, from which predictive means, variances, and epistemic uncertainty estimates are derived. The context points play a dual role: a small i.i.d. batch is resampled at each training step to approximate the RKHS norm, while a larger fixed set (e.g., 25,000 Halton sequence points for images) is used once at the end to compute the posterior covariance.

## Method

FSP-LAPLACE addresses the non-existence of Lebesgue densities in infinite-dimensional function space by recasting the MAP estimate as a weak mode of the posterior measure under a GP prior, following Lambley's framework. This yields a training objective R_FSP combining the negative log-likelihood with an RKHS norm regularizer ‖f(·,w)−μ‖²_{H_Σ}, approximated via a minimum-norm interpolant at a small set of context points. After training, the network is linearized around w*, which converts the RKHS regularizer into a quadratic form (w−μ_{w*})^⊤ Σ_{w*}^† (w−μ_{w*}) over the weights, making the objective a valid unnormalized log-density. The Laplace Hessian Λ = Σ_{w*}^† − GNN is then computed entirely in a matrix-free manner: a fully-reorthogonalized Lanczos iteration with embedded LDL^⊤ factorization produces a rank-r approximation of Σ(C,C)^†, which is mapped through the Jacobian via backward-mode autodiff to yield a thin SVD of the prior precision, and a small r×r eigendecomposition gives the posterior covariance. A heuristic eigenvalue truncation ensures the posterior marginal variance never exceeds the prior marginal variance, preventing numerical blow-up.

## Results

On the Mauna Loa CO₂ forecasting task, FSP-LAPLACE achieves a mean squared error of 8.90 ± 1.15, substantially outperforming the linearized Laplace (28.26 ± 6.87), FVI (38.38 ± 2.10), and GP (35.66 ± 0.00). On MNIST, FSP-LAPLACE (RND context points) attains the best expected log-likelihood of −0.037 ± 0.001 and the lowest ECE of 0.002 ± 0.000, while matching the 98.9% accuracy of the Laplace baseline. On FashionMNIST, FSP-LAPLACE (KMNIST) achieves the highest OOD detection accuracy of 0.994 ± 0.001 and the best log-likelihood of −0.255 ± 0.002. On the ocean current modeling task, FSP-LAPLACE reduces MSE to 0.0169 ± 0.0001, roughly halving the error of the Laplace baseline (0.0320 ± 0.0016).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against FVI (Sun et al., 2019), FSP-LAPLACE consistently wins on expected log-likelihood, MSE, accuracy, ECE, and OOD detection accuracy across all evaluated tasks; for example, on Mauna Loa the MSE drops from 38.38 (FVI) to 8.90 (FSP-LAPLACE), and on MNIST the ECE drops from 0.073 to 0.002. Against the linearized Laplace (Immer et al., 2021), FSP-LAPLACE achieves substantially lower MSE on the Mauna Loa task (8.90 vs. 28.26) and higher OOD detection accuracy on FashionMNIST (0.994 vs. 0.801 with RND context points), while matching accuracy on MNIST (98.9%). Against the GP baseline (Rasmussen & Williams, 2006), FSP-LAPLACE achieves a 4× lower MSE on Mauna Loa (8.90 vs. 35.66) but the GP attains a better expected log-likelihood (−2124 vs. −6015), which the authors attribute to the neural network underestimating the likelihood scale. Against the Sparse GP (Hensman et al., 2013), FSP-LAPLACE dominates on log-likelihood, accuracy, ECE, and OOD accuracy for both MNIST and FashionMNIST, with the largest gap on FashionMNIST OOD accuracy (0.994 vs. 0.938). Overall, FSP-LAPLACE's advantage is most pronounced when informative prior knowledge is available (scientific tasks), while it remains competitive on black-box benchmarks where neural networks typically excel.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the linearized Laplace approximation framework (Immer et al.) by replacing the standard weight-space isotropic Gaussian prior with an interpretable Gaussian process prior in function space. The theoretical foundation draws on Lambley's weak mode framework to handle the non-existence of Lebesgue densities in infinite-dimensional function spaces, and the RKHS norm estimator follows Chen et al. The key departure from prior function-space prior work (Sun et al.'s functional VI) is the use of Laplace approximation instead of variational inference, which avoids the infinite KL divergence problem that plagues VI under most function-space priors of interest.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This fills a practical gap: for the first time, practitioners can encode structured inductive biases (periodicity, smoothness, length scale, physical constraints like Helmholtz decomposition) directly into a Laplace-approximated BNN without resorting to expensive cross-validation or type-II MLE for prior selection. The method's strength is most evident in scientific inference settings where kernel structure encodes domain knowledge, but it remains competitive on black-box tasks. A key open question the authors acknowledge is how to find good function-space priors in high-dimensional spaces (e.g., images) where explicit prior knowledge is difficult to articulate, limiting the method's advantage in those regimes.
