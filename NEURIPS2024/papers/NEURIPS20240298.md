## Problem

Single-entity skeleton backbones applied to multi-entity actions (e.g., two-person or hand-object interactions) suffer from inter-entity distribution discrepancies: different entities occupy distinct spatial regions in the world coordinate system, violating the i.i.d. assumption underlying common late-fusion practices. This bias leads to suboptimal backbone optimization and degraded recognition accuracy, yet no prior work has explicitly identified or addressed this distributional gap.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240298/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

CHASE takes a multi-entity skeleton sequence X ∈ R^{C×T×J×E} (C Cartesian coordinates per joint, T frames, J joints per entity, E entities) as input and produces a shifted version of the same tensor before feeding it to a single-entity backbone, which outputs an action class label. The method is agnostic to coordinate dimensionality (2D or 3D joints) and entity type (persons, hands, objects), and operates as a lightweight wrapper adding only ~1–2% parameters and ~2.5M FLOPs to the underlying backbone.

## Method

CHASE comprises three components: (1) an Implicit Convex Hull Constrained Adaptive Shift (ICHAS) that computes the shift vector p* = X·softmax(W), provably constraining the new origin to the interior of the skeleton convex hull while preserving inter-entity relative geometry; (2) a Coefficient Learning Block (CLB), a squeeze-and-excitation-style MLP that maps the input skeleton to the weight matrix W, making the shift sample-adaptive rather than fixed; and (3) a Mini-batch Pair-wise Maximum Mean Discrepancy (MPMMD) auxiliary loss that quantifies and minimizes pair-wise entity distribution gaps via RKHS embeddings with uniform mini-batch sampling over entity pairs. The total training objective is L = L_CLS + λ·L_mpmmd, and CHASE is applied as a normalization step immediately before any single-entity backbone (CTR-GCN, InfoGCN, STSA-Net, HD-GCN), requiring no architectural modification to the backbone itself.

## Results

CHASE consistently boosts single-entity backbones across six multi-entity action recognition datasets. On NTU Mutual 26 X-Sub, CTR-GCN+CHASE reaches 91.30% top-1 accuracy (vs. 89.32% vanilla), surpassing dedicated multi-entity methods like ISTA-Net (90.56%) and me-GCN (90.00%). The most dramatic gains appear on H2O (+9.37% for CTR-GCN, reaching 91.05%) and CAD (+9.16%, reaching 89.61%), where inter-entity distribution discrepancies are most severe. CHASE adds only ~26k trainable parameters (1–2% overhead) and ~2.50M FLOPs, making it a lightweight plug-in normalization step.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CHASE applied to CTR-GCN outperforms all dedicated multi-entity interaction models on NTU Mutual 26 X-Sub (91.30% vs. ISTA-Net 90.56%, me-GCN 90.00%, SkeleTR 87.80%, GDCN 85.80%) and X-Set (92.34% vs. ISTA-Net 91.72%). On H2O, CTR-GCN+CHASE (91.05%) surpasses ISTA-Net (89.09%) and H2OTR (90.90%), though EffHandEgoNet (91.32%) remains slightly ahead. On CAD, CTR-GCN+CHASE (89.61%) beats both ISTA-Net (87.16%) and AHNet-Large (89.32%), while on VD it outperforms Actor-Transformer (92.30%) and ISTA-Net (91.40%). Gains are most pronounced where inter-entity discrepancies are largest: +9.37% on H2O and +9.16% on CAD for CTR-GCN, versus only +0.21% on the extremely fine-grained ASB101 (1,380 classes). Across all four backbone architectures (CTR-GCN, InfoGCN, STSA-Net, HD-GCN), CHASE consistently improves over the vanilla counterpart, confirming its generality as a plug-in normalization step.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in multi-entity action recognition, where single-entity backbones (GCNs, transformers) are applied independently to each entity under an implicit i.i.d. assumption, and features are fused via averaging. Rather than designing new interaction architectures (as in ISTA-Net, GDCN, or SkeleTR), CHASE reframes the problem as a distribution-alignment issue: the world-coordinate origin introduces systematic inter-entity discrepancies that bias the shared backbone. The method draws on convex hull theory (Rockafellar) to constrain the adaptive shift, on MMD from kernel two-sample testing for the auxiliary loss, and on squeeze-and-excitation gating for lightweight coefficient parameterization, but the core contribution is the identification of inter-entity distribution discrepancy as a first-class problem and its resolution via a plug-in normalization step.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that a ~26K-parameter wrapper can close much of the gap between single-entity and purpose-built multi-entity backbones, CHASE challenges the assumption that complex interaction modeling is necessary for multi-entity recognition, offering a simpler and more modular alternative. The convex hull constraint is a theoretically grounded and practically critical design choice (its removal causes >60% accuracy collapse), suggesting that geometric plausibility constraints matter more than raw parameter capacity for this type of normalization. Open questions include whether the approach extends to non-skeleton modalities, whether it composes with human-centric foundation models, and whether the modest gains on very large label spaces (ASB101: +0.21%) indicate a ceiling for distribution-alignment alone.
