## Problem

Bisimulation metrics (from formal verification and RL) and optimal transport distances between Markov chains (from probability theory) have developed in isolation, with no known connection between them. Moreover, existing algorithms for computing these distances reduce the problem to dynamic programming where each Bellman update requires solving a full static optimal transport problem, making them computationally impractical. This paper shows the two notions are identical and derives a new formulation that enables efficient computation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20244268/cropped_page8_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes two finite Markov chains specified by their transition kernels P_X, P_Y, fixed initial states x_0, y_0, a ground cost function c: X×Y→R⁺, and a discount factor γ∈(0,1). It outputs an optimal transition coupling π_out (a joint distribution over next-state pairs for each current state pair) and the corresponding value function V^{π_out} giving the transport distance from every state pair. The optimization operates over occupancy couplings—a distribution over quadruples (xy, x'y') representing discounted visitation counts—yielding a finite LP with |X|²|Y|² variables and |X|²+|X||Y|+|Y|² linear constraints, rather than the infinite-dimensional space of full process laws.

## Method

The key reformulation replaces the infinite-dimensional coupling over sequence laws with a finite-dimensional LP over occupancy couplings, where the objective is linear and all constraints (flow conservation and transition coherence for each chain) are linear. Entropy regularization via conditional relative entropy is then introduced, transforming the LP into a smooth problem solvable by alternating Bregman projections (Mirror Sinkhorn) onto the two constraint sets B_X and B_Y. Each projection step is computed in closed form by solving Bellman-Sinkhorn fixed-point equations—contractive operators that generalize standard Bellman operators with a log-sum-exp structure—via a few steps of value iteration. The resulting algorithm, Sinkhorn Value Iteration (SVI), alternates between projecting onto X-constraints and Y-constraints while multiplicatively updating the transition coupling, and achieves an ε-accurate distance estimate in K ≥ 324‖c‖²∞ log|X||Y| / ((1−γ)⁵ε²) iterations. A simplified variant, Sinkhorn Policy Iteration (SPI), approximates the projection steps using the current coupling, trading a small theoretical cost for significant practical speedup.

## Results

The paper's experiments are primarily illustrative rather than a head-to-head benchmark. In the 4-rooms environment (γ=0.95), both SVI and SPI converge to the true transport distance at a rate essentially unaffected by the number of value-iteration steps m, with even m=1 remaining competitive. The theoretical guarantee (Theorem 2) establishes that K ≥ 324‖c‖²∞ log|X||Y| / ((1−γ)⁵ ε²) iterations suffice for an ε-accurate estimate. A qualitative experiment comparing a 9-state gridworld to a 4-rooms environment shows the computed distances capture the rotational symmetries of the second chain, confirming the metric's interpretability.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not include a formal head-to-head comparison table against prior methods in the main text. It notes that previous approaches for computing bisimulation metrics or OT distances between Markov chains (O'Connor et al. 2022, Brugère et al. 2024, Kemertas and Jepson 2022) use Sinkhorn's algorithm as a black-box subroutine within policy iteration or value iteration, making them difficult to analyze theoretically. The authors claim their SVI method is 'significantly more efficient than the best known methods' for computing bisimulation metrics, but the empirical evidence in the main text is limited to convergence plots for SVI and SPI across different m values, without direct timing or accuracy comparisons against these baselines. The full experimental suite (referenced in Appendix F) is not included in the main paper, so a definitive quantitative comparison is not available from the text provided.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously disconnected research threads: bisimulation metrics from theoretical computer science (Desharnais et al., van Breugel & Worrell, Ferns et al.) and optimal transport distances between stochastic processes (Moulos, O'Connor et al., Brugère et al.), showing they are exactly the same object when the ground cost is set to (1−γ)|r(x)−r(y)|. Algorithmically, it departs from prior DP-based approaches that required solving a full static OT problem at each Bellman update by introducing a finite-dimensional LP over 'occupancy couplings,' which enables a Sinkhorn-style method (SVI) built on Mirror Sinkhorn (Ballu & Berthet, 2023) and entropy-regularized MDP theory (Neu et al., 2017; Geist et al., 2019). The rounding step adapts Altschuler et al. (2017), and the overall framework is positioned as a bridge between computational OT and the bisimulation literature, which had previously relied on heuristics or expensive network-simplex-based solvers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The conceptual equivalence between bisimulation metrics and OT distances opens a two-way transfer of ideas: OT tools (entropy regularization, Sinkhorn iterations, stochastic optimization) become available to the bisimulation/RL-representation-learning community, while the rich fixed-point and modal-logic structure of bisimulation informs OT theory. The LP formulation over occupancy couplings is potentially differentiable with respect to transition kernels, which the authors flag as important for future representation-learning applications in RL. Key open questions include extending the convergence guarantees to finite m (imperfect projections), handling unknown transition kernels (learning from data), and scaling to larger state spaces; the current experiments are limited to small gridworld and 4-rooms environments.
