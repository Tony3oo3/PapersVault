## Problem

Existing LVLM benchmarks predominantly evaluate single-turn, single-image, short-form (multiple-choice or brief answer) scenarios, failing to capture the multi-turn, multi-image, long-context conversations that define real-world human-AI interaction. This gap leaves open-source LVLMs' deficiencies in sustained multi-image dialogue unmeasured and unaddressed, as evidenced by the large performance disparity (42.8% vs. 70.2%) between the best open-source and closed-source models on such tasks.

## Input / Output

Each MMDU sample interleaves 2–20 RGB images with up to 27 turns of conversation, reaching a maximum of 18k combined image+text tokens (average 8.2k). Images are referenced via positional tags (<image-1>, <image-2>, …) within the dialogue text, enabling arbitrary reordering and concatenation of dialogues. The model produces free-form, long-form text responses per turn, which are scored by GPT-4o across six dimensions (Creativity, Richness, Visual Perception, Logical Coherence, Answer Accuracy, Image Relationship Understanding) on a 0–10 scale with 2-point interval rubrics.

## Method

The benchmark is constructed by clustering open-source Wikipedia entries using sentence-transformer embeddings of entry tags and image captions (cosine similarity threshold τ=0.75), then prompting GPT-4o to generate multi-turn, multi-image Q&A pairs from each cluster, with two rounds of expert human review to eliminate hallucinations. For evaluation, GPT-4o acts as a judge comparing model outputs against reference answers across the six dimensions, achieving 97.5% Pearson correlation with human expert scoring. The companion MMDU-45k instruction-tuning dataset (45k conversations, 410k QA pairs, avg. 5k tokens, 2–5 images, 9 turns) is built via the same pipeline with 5% random human sampling for quality verification. Fine-tuning LLaVA-1.5 or InternLM-XC2 on MMDU-45k during the SFT stage (mixed with LLaVA-665K, lr=2e-4, 1 epoch) yields +5.0% and +14.5% on MMDU respectively, plus consistent gains on multi-image benchmarks (Mantis +6.9%, MMMU multi-pics +2.1%) and general benchmarks (MMStar +1.1%, MathVista +1.5%, ChartQA +1.2%).

## Results

On the MMDU benchmark (110 multi-turn, multi-image dialogues with up to 18k tokens and 20 images), GPT-4o achieves the highest average score of 70.2%, while the best open-source model (LLaVA 1.6-mistral) reaches only 42.8%, revealing a substantial closed-source vs. open-source gap. Fine-tuning with MMDU-45k yields a +14.5% improvement for InternLM-XC2 (35.6% → 50.1%) and +5.0% for LLaVA 1.5 (32.2% → 37.2%) on MMDU. Cross-benchmark gains from MMDU-45k SFT include +1.1% on MMStar, +1.5% on MathVista, and +1.2% on ChartQA for InternLM-XC2, with the largest multi-image gain being +6.9% on Mantis (sequence).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among closed-source models, GPT-4o leads all six dimensions (70.2% avg.), followed by GPT-4-turbo (66.3%), Claude3-Opus (62.6%), and Qwen-VL-Max (46.9%). Among open-source models, LLaVA 1.6-mistral is the strongest at 42.8%, ahead of Qwen-VL-7B (39.3%), InternVL-Chat-V1.5 (37.4%), and InternLM-XC2 (35.6%), while smaller models like Monkey (14.1%) and Idefics2 (25.4%) struggle significantly. The key finding is that fine-tuning with MMDU-45k narrows the gap: InternLM-XC2 jumps from 35.6% to 50.1% (+14.5%), surpassing all other open-source baselines and approaching Qwen-VL-Max (46.9%), while LLaVA 1.5 improves from 32.2% to 37.2% (+5.0%), surpassing LLaVA 1.5-13B (35.3%) and InternLM-XC2's original score. On multi-image benchmarks (Table 4), LLaVA 1.5+MMDU-45k outperforms the base model on all five tests, with the largest gain on Mantis (sequence) at +6.9% (37.8% → 44.7%), indicating that the dataset specifically addresses multi-image reasoning weaknesses. The paper attributes the persistent gap to GPT-4o (70.2% vs. 50.1% for the best fine-tuned open-source model) to the scarcity of multi-turn, multi-image instruction-tuning data in the open-source ecosystem.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by combining three previously separate evaluation axes—multi-turn conversation (ConvBench, max 3 turns), multi-image understanding (MMMU multi-pics, BLINK, max 4 images), and long-context processing—into a single unified benchmark, which the authors note no prior work achieves. The data construction pipeline repurposes Wikipedia clustering (via sentence-transformer embeddings) as a scalable source of topically coherent image sets, and the evaluation adopts the LLM-as-judge paradigm from NLP (MT-Bench, Chatbot Arena) and adapts it to open-ended multimodal scoring across six dimensions. On the training side, MMDU-45k extends the visual instruction-tuning lineage (LLaVA-Instruct-150K, LLaVA 1.5-665K, ShareGPT4V) by being the first open-source dataset to jointly cover multi-turn, multi-image, and long-context dialogues.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark exposes a 27-point gap between the best open-source LVLM (42.8%) and GPT-4o (70.2%) in realistic multi-turn multi-image settings, and the fine-tuning experiments suggest this gap is primarily a data-scarcity problem rather than an architectural one—a useful diagnostic for the open-source community. MMDU-45k provides a practical, immediately usable resource that improves not only MMDU but also transfer benchmarks (MMStar, MathVista, ChartQA, Mantis), making it broadly relevant. Key limitations include English-only coverage and a focus on general daily scenarios rather than specialized domains (math, science), leaving open questions about multilingual extension and domain-specific multi-image reasoning.
