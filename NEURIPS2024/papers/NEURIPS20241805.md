## Problem

Learning ε-optimal strategies in zero-sum imperfect information games with trajectory feedback suffers from high variance because existing methods apply importance sampling over entire sequences of actions, producing unstable loss estimates that are especially problematic in large games and when combined with function approximation. The paper asks whether optimal strategies can be learned without importance-sampling over the full action sequence, while retaining near-optimal sample complexity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241805/cropped_page8_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes single game trajectories observed under a fixed sampling policy (which is decoupled from the learning policy) along with the game tree structure (information sets, legal actions, and transition probabilities). It produces time-averaged behavioral policies for both players that constitute an ε-optimal profile. A key structural constraint is that importance sampling is applied only to the current action at each information set rather than to the full realization-plan probability, and the sampling policy must be fixed in advance for the entire learning horizon.

## Method

LocalOMD applies Online Mirror Descent locally at each information set using a dilated entropy regularizer with individually adaptive learning rates, and a generalized dual-stabilization technique (extending Fang et al. 2020) that handles time-varying regularization as long as the increments are convex. The critical mechanism is that the loss is divided by the importance-sampling weight through the learning rate, so the local loss at each information set only depends on the probability of the last action rather than the combined probability of the full realization plan, keeping estimates bounded by O(HA) instead of O(A_𝒳). The algorithm updates only along the observed trajectory per iteration (O(HA) time complexity), and with the balanced sampling policy and appropriately chosen rates, achieves a near-optimal Õ(H³(A_𝒳+B_𝒴)/ε²) sample complexity matching BalancedCFR but with a more generalizable per-iteration update.

## Results

LocalOMD is evaluated on three benchmark games (Kuhn poker with 12 actions, Leduc poker with 1092 actions, and Liars dice with 24570 actions) using the exploitability gap and the empirical variance of the estimated loss vectors as metrics. Theoretically, LocalOMD achieves a near-optimal sample complexity of Õ(H³(A_X + B_Y)/ε²) with the balanced sampling policy, matching the rate of BalancedCFR. Empirically, LocalOMD consistently outperforms BalancedCFR on exploitability across all three games and exhibits lower variance in loss estimation, while showing comparable or slightly mixed results against BalancedFTRL depending on the game.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against BalancedCFR (Bai et al., 2022), LocalOMD consistently achieves a lower exploitability gap across all three games (Kuhn poker, Leduc poker, Liars dice), primarily because BalancedCFR must update each depth with independent samples, requiring H times more episodes overall. LocalOMD also shows lower variance in its loss estimates compared to BalancedCFR, since the latter only estimates losses at each depth every H iterations. Against BalancedFTRL (Fiegel et al., 2023), the comparison is more nuanced: BalancedFTRL achieves better exploitability on Liars dice but worse performance on Leduc poker, while LocalOMD maintains more consistent performance across games. In terms of loss estimation variance, both fixed-sampling methods (LocalOMD and BalancedFTRL) achieve smaller variance than non-fixed approaches, with LocalOMD showing a more consistently balanced sampling profile.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: the dilated-entropy OMD framework for extensive-form games (Hoda et al., 2010; Kroer et al., 2015), the fixed-policy sampling framework from outcome-sampling MCCFR (Lanctot et al., 2009), and the dual-stabilization technique for time-varying regularization (Fang et al., 2020). It departs from prior OMD-based approaches (IXOMD, BalancedCFR) by decoupling the observation policy from the interaction policy, thereby eliminating importance sampling over action sequences and restricting it to only the current action. The local, per-information-set update structure also distinguishes it from global OMD methods, making it conceptually closer to CFR while retaining the theoretical machinery of mirror descent.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical contribution is variance reduction in loss estimation, which is critical when extending game-solving algorithms beyond the tabular setting to function approximation (e.g., neural networks), where high-variance importance-sampling weights cause unstable training. The near-optimal sample complexity O~(H³(A_X+B_Y)/ε²) matches BalancedCFR but with a more generalizable local update procedure. Open questions include whether the κ(μˢ) quantity admits a problem-dependent lower bound, whether the fixed-sampling framework extends to general-sum or multi-player settings, and whether on-policy (semi-bandit) variants can also avoid sequence-level importance sampling. The requirement to pre-specify a good sampling policy remains a practical limitation.
