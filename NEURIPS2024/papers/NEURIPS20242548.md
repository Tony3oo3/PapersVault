## Problem

Volumetric rendering methods like RawNeRF achieve HDR view synthesis from noisy RAW images but require ~130 GPU-hours of training and render at ~0.02 FPS, making real-time interaction impossible. While 3D Gaussian Splatting enables real-time rendering, directly applying it to nighttime RAW data fails because COLMAP produces sparse/inaccurate point clouds in low light, spherical harmonics cannot represent the unbounded RAW linear color space, and the resulting scene structure is too poor for downstream tasks like refocusing.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242548/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

LE3D takes multi-view noisy Bayer RAW images (4032×3024 DNG format, captured by iPhone X with short exposures) and COLMAP-estimated camera poses as input. It produces a 3D Gaussian Splatting scene representation that renders at ~103 FPS at 2K resolution (2016×1512), outputting HDR linear-color images (32-bit float, unbounded), weighted depth maps, and a weight distortion map (K=32 bins per pixel) that together enable real-time refocusing via MultiPlane Image synthesis, exposure variation, and swappable tone-mapping (global curve/color-temperature or local HDR+).

## Method

LE3D makes three key modifications to vanilla 3DGS. First, Cone Scatter Initialization defines a viewing frustum from the COLMAP camera poses (averaged viewing direction, max FOV, near/far planes from point cloud distances) and randomly scatters additional points within it to enrich distant-scene coverage that COLMAP misses in low light. Second, a tiny Color MLP (one 16-dim hidden layer, 19-dim input of 16-dim per-gaussian feature + 3-dim viewing direction, ReLU activation) with an exponential output activation replaces spherical harmonics to represent the unbounded RAW linear color space; each gaussian also carries a learnable color bias initialized to the log of its projected pixel average for stable early training. Third, two structural regularizations are added: a depth distortion loss (adapted from Mip-NeRF 360) that penalizes spread of blending weights along each ray, and a near-far regularization that minimizes the depth gap between the nearest and farthest M=5 gaussians on each ray weighted by their respective blending weights, jointly pruning floaters and concentrating gaussians on surfaces. Training uses RawNeRF's weighted L2 loss in the tone-curve domain plus these regularizations, with a total training time of ~1.5 GPU-hours on a single RTX 3090.

## Results

On the RawNeRF benchmark (3 test scenes, 2K resolution), LE3D achieves the best RAW PSNR of 61.08 dB and RAW SSIM of 0.9983, as well as the best sRGB PSNR of 24.70 dB. LE3D renders at 103 FPS (real-time) while requiring only 1.53 GPU×H of training time, compared to RawNeRF's 0.022 FPS and 129.54 GPU×H — a ~4,700× rendering speedup and ~85× training speedup. LE3D also supports replaceable tone-mapping (TM) for HDR rendering, which LDR-based methods (LDR-NeRF, LDR-3DGS) cannot do.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against RawNeRF (the primary volumetric-rendering competitor), LE3D wins on RAW PSNR (61.08 vs. 58.69 dB), RAW SSIM (0.9983 vs. 0.9969), and sRGB PSNR (24.70 vs. 24.08 dB), while slightly trailing on sRGB SSIM (0.6076 vs. 0.6100) and LPIPS (0.5071 vs. 0.4952) — a near-tie in perceptual quality. The critical advantage is speed: LE3D renders at 103 FPS versus RawNeRF's 0.022 FPS (~4,700× faster) and trains in 1.53 GPU×H versus 129.54 GPU×H (~85× faster). Against the RawGS baseline (vanilla 3DGS + RawNeRF's loss), LE3D improves RAW PSNR by 1.8 dB (61.08 vs. 59.28) and sRGB PSNR by 1.35 dB (24.70 vs. 23.35), at the cost of lower FPS (103 vs. 176) due to the Color MLP. Against LDR-based methods (LDR-NeRF, LDR-3DGS, HDR-3DGS), LE3D achieves substantially higher sRGB PSNR (24.70 vs. 20.04–20.33 dB) and SSIM (0.6076 vs. 0.5286–0.5541), confirming that training directly on RAW linear data preserves far more dynamic range information than post-processed LDR inputs.

## Relation to Prior Work

LE3D sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: RawNeRF's approach to HDR view synthesis from noisy RAW images (using weighted L2 loss and multi-exposure training) and 3DGS's differentiable rasterization for real-time rendering. The paper departs from vanilla 3DGS in three targeted ways: replacing spherical harmonics with a tiny Color MLP to handle the unbounded RAW linear color space, introducing Cone Scatter Initialization to compensate for COLMAP's failure in low-light scenes, and adding depth distortion plus a novel near-far regularization (inspired by Mip-NeRF 360's distortion loss) to improve scene structure. Conceptually, the paper treats 3DGS as a drop-in replacement for volumetric rendering in the RawNeRF pipeline, but identifies and resolves the specific incompatibilities (SH capacity, SfM quality, structural fidelity) that prevent a naive port.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By achieving real-time (100+ FPS) HDR view synthesis from noisy RAW images with only ~1.5 GPU hours of training, LE3D makes computational photography operations—tone mapping, exposure variation, refocusing, white balance adjustment—feasible as interactive 3D post-processing, a capability previously limited to offline volumetric rendering at ~0.02 FPS. The paper acknowledges that extremely dark scenes (very low SNR) remain a failure mode shared with RawNeRF, and that the sparse 3DGS representation still lags slightly behind dense NeRF sampling in noise robustness. Open questions include whether the Color MLP can be further compressed for mobile deployment, whether the approach generalizes to dynamic HDR scenes, and whether the near-far regularization transfers to other 3DGS applications beyond RAW-based reconstruction.
