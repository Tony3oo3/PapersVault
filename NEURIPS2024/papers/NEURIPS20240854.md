## Problem

Extending single-agent inverse soft-Q learning (IQ-Learn) to cooperative multi-agent settings requires simultaneously learning local value functions for decentralized execution and a joint value function for centralized training, while preserving IQ-Learn's key advantage of non-adversarial optimization. Existing multi-agent IL methods rely on adversarial training (e.g., GAIL-style), which introduces biased, high-variance gradient estimators and training instability. The paper addresses how to factorize the multi-agent IL objective under the CTDE paradigm while maintaining both policy consistency between local and global levels and convexity in the Q-function space.

## Input / Output

The method consumes per-agent local observations (o_i) and expert demonstration trajectories (D^E) as primary inputs, with the global state S additionally fed to hyper-networks that generate mixing network weights. A replay buffer of transitions collected by the current policy provides additional training signal for the value-function term. The output is a set of decentralized soft policies, where each agent's policy is the softmax of its local Q-values, enabling independent execution without access to other agents' observations at test time.

## Method

MIFQ factorizes the multi-agent inverse soft-Q objective using three network components: per-agent local Q-networks, two mixing networks (for Q and V) with non-negative weights and convex ELU activations, and hyper-networks that generate mixing weights from the global state. A key architectural choice is mixing the negative of local Q-values (Q^tot = −M_Q(−Q)), which simultaneously ensures the Individual-Global-Consistency (IGC) property—that the optimal joint policy equals the product of local softmax policies—and guarantees the training objective is concave in Q-space. This concavity allows the original max-min adversarial formulation to be reduced to a single non-adversarial concave maximization over Q, with the optimal policy recovered in closed form as the softmax of local Q-values. The practical loss incorporates a chi-squared regularizer for numerical stability and a replay-buffer-based approximation of the initial-state value term to avoid direct estimation.

## Results

On SMACv2, MIFQ (Soft) achieves winrates of 72.7% (Protoss 5v5), 77.3% (Protoss 10v10), 71.9% (Terran 5v5), and 72.7% (Terran 10v10), substantially outperforming the best baselines (MA-SQIL at 47.7% and IQ-VDN at 38.3% respectively). On Gold Miner, MIFQ (Soft) reaches 60.2%, 49.2%, and 39.8% winrates on easy/medium/hard, exceeding the strongest baseline (MA-SQIL) by 23.5, 20.9, and 18.9 percentage points. On MPE, MIFQ (Soft) is competitive with the best baselines (e.g., −20.5 vs. −18.3 for BC on reference; −26.3 vs. −29.5 for IQ-VDN on speaker), with the gap being smaller given MPE's deterministic and simpler nature.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MIFQ (Soft) decisively outperforms all three primary baselines across SMACv2 and Gold Miner. Against MA-SQIL (adapted SQIL [30]), MIFQ leads by 25.0 pp on Protoss 5v5 (72.7 vs. 47.7) and by 23.5 pp on Miner easy (60.2 vs. 36.7). Against MA-GAIL [36], the gap is even wider on harder tasks: 49.0 pp on Terran 10v10 (72.7 vs. 23.7) and 38.5 pp on Zerg 10v10 (59.4 vs. 20.9). Against MA-AIRL [42], MIFQ dominates on all SMACv2 maps (e.g., 77.3 vs. 19.8 on Protoss 10v10) and Miner (60.2 vs. 35.2 on easy). On MPE, MIFQ (Soft) is competitive but not uniformly best: it trails BC by 2.2 on reference (−20.5 vs. −18.3) and IQ-VDN by 1.6 on spread (−23.2 vs. −21.6), but clearly beats MA-SQIL, MA-AIRL, and MA-GAIL on all three MPE tasks. The deterministic variant MIFQ (Det) also beats all baselines but is consistently 5–10 pp below MIFQ (Soft), confirming the advantage of soft-policy learning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the single-agent inverse soft Q-learning framework (IQ-Learn, Garg et al. 2021) to cooperative multi-agent settings by integrating QMIX-style mixing and hyper-networks for centralized training with decentralized execution. It departs from prior multi-agent IL methods (MA-GAIL, MA-AIRL) that rely on adversarial training by showing the max-min objective can be converted to a concave maximization over the Q-space, provided mixing networks use non-negative weights and convex activations. The paper also generalizes the IGM principle (from QTRAN/QPLEX) to an IGC principle tailored to soft (max-entropy) policies, which is the natural policy form in inverse soft Q-learning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first evaluation of imitation learning algorithms on SMACv2, a procedurally generated and substantially harder multi-agent benchmark than prior SMACv1, establishing a new reference point for the field. The theoretical guarantee that concavity holds for any multi-layer feed-forward mixing network with non-negative weights and convex activations is a clean, general result that could inform future factorized learning designs. Key open questions remain: the method still falls short of expert performance (e.g., 72.7% vs. 86.7% on Protoss 5v5), struggles on the largest SMACv2 maps, and requires large amounts of clean expert demonstrations, limiting practical applicability in data-scarce settings.
