## Problem

Existing time series models are narrowly task-specific, requiring separate architectures and modules for forecasting, classification, anomaly detection, and imputation, and cannot handle heterogeneous data with varying numbers of variables and sequence lengths. Unifying both generative (forecasting, imputation) and predictive (classification, anomaly detection) tasks under a single shared-weight model that generalizes across domains remains an open challenge.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20244462/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a multivariate time series sample x ∈ R^{t×v} with arbitrary t and v, tokenized into non-overlapping patches along the time dimension, and appends learnable prompt tokens plus task tokens (GEN for generative tasks, CLS for classification). For forecasting, the GEN token is replicated f times to produce direct multi-step predictions of arbitrary length via unpatchification; for classification, the CLS token is matched to learned class embeddings by nearest-neighbor distance. The same network structure processes all tasks without architectural modification, and the model supports flexible forecast horizons (60–720+ steps) and variable counts (1–963 sensors) across 38 datasets.

## Method

UNITS introduces a unified token scheme with three token types—sample tokens (patched time series), prompt tokens (dataset/task context), and task tokens (GEN for generative, CLS for predictive)—that encode task specifications without post-hoc architectural changes. The backbone is a modified transformer with two-way self-attention over both time and variable dimensions, a DyLinear module that replaces the standard point-wise FFN with bilinearly interpolated weights to capture cross-token dependencies at varying sequence lengths, and a gating module that re-scales layer-wise features to reduce interference from multi-domain co-training. Pre-training uses a unified masked reconstruction objective that simultaneously trains the GEN tower (reconstructing masked sample tokens) and the CLS tower (reconstructing the full sample conditioned on the CLS embedding), enabling both generative and predictive capabilities in a single frozen model. Downstream adaptation is achieved via prompt learning (training only task-specific tokens on a frozen backbone) or full fine-tuning, supporting multi-task, few-shot, and zero-shot settings.

## Results

In single-task forecasting across 32 datasets, UNITS-ST achieves the best MSE on 28/32 and best MAE on 27/32, outperforming the previous best (iTransformer) by a clear margin (e.g., 0.377 vs 0.407 MSE on ETTm1, 0.163 vs 0.178 on ECL). On classification (10 datasets), UNITS-ST reaches 75.0% average accuracy vs. 73.6% for TimesNet; on anomaly detection (5 datasets), it achieves 89.21% average F1 vs. 85.26% for TimesNet. In the multi-task setting (38 tasks, one shared model), UNITS-SUP attains 0.439 average forecasting MSE and 81.6% classification accuracy, surpassing iTransformer (0.466 MSE) and TimesNet (80.9% accuracy) while using a single set of weights. Prompted UNITS-PMT further reduces forecasting MAE to 0.376, slightly beating the fully supervised model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UNITS-ST dominates iTransformer [67] on 28 of 32 forecasting datasets (MSE), 10 classification datasets (75.0% vs. 73.6% avg accuracy), and 5 anomaly detection datasets (89.21% vs. 85.26% avg F1), and wins all 16 imputation datasets. Against TimesNet [112], UNITS-ST wins on every visible forecasting dataset and leads by 1.4% in classification and 3.95% in anomaly detection F1. In the multi-task setting, UNITS-SUP (3.4M params) outperforms the 48× larger GPT4TS [129] (164.5M params) by 2.2% in forecasting MSE (0.439 vs. 0.449) while matching its classification accuracy (81.6% vs. 82.0%). UNITS also surpasses PatchTST [82], DLinear [119], FEDformer [128], and Autoformer [114] on all 9 representative forecasting datasets shown. In few-shot transfer (20% data), UNITS-FT achieves 65.2% classification accuracy and 0.481 forecasting MSE versus iTransformer-FT's 59.9% and 0.510, and in few-shot imputation (25% mask) UNITS-FT reduces MSE by 12.4% over iTransformer-FT (0.163 vs. 0.186). The one notable exception is Traffic forecasting, where iTransformer (0.428) slightly edges UNITS-ST (0.452), reflecting the challenge of very high-dimensional inputs (862 sensors).

## Relation to Prior Work

UNITS builds on the standard transformer architecture (Vaswani et al., 2017) but introduces a modified block with a dynamic linear operator (DyLinear) and a gating module to handle heterogeneous time series with varying variable counts and sequence lengths. It departs from the dominant paradigm of task-specific time series models (PatchTST, iTransformer, TimesNet) and LLM-reprogrammed approaches (GPT4TS, Time-LLM, TEMPO) by proposing a universal task tokenization scheme (GEN/CLS tokens) that unifies generative and predictive tasks within a single shared-weight architecture. The masked reconstruction pre-training strategy, which jointly optimizes both generative and predictive towers, extends prior self-supervised time series pre-training that typically targets only one task family.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

UNITS is the first model to demonstrate that a single compact (3.4M parameter) architecture can handle forecasting, classification, anomaly detection, and imputation simultaneously across 38 heterogeneous datasets, outperforming both task-specialized models and 48× larger LLM-based methods. The finding that prompt learning on a frozen pre-trained UNITS matches or slightly exceeds full fine-tuning suggests a practical pathway for rapid adaptation to new time series domains without retraining. Open questions include whether zero-shot generalization to truly novel task types is feasible, whether the approach scales to higher-dimensional or irregularly-sampled time series, and whether alternative backbones (Mamba, MLP-based) can further improve efficiency.
