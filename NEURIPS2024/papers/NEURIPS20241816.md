## Problem

LoRA achieves memory-efficient fine-tuning by restricting updates to a low-rank subspace, but this limits representation power and causes it to underperform full-parameter training, especially on large-scale datasets and memorization-heavy tasks. The paper identifies that LoRA's layerwise weight norms are heavily skewed—embedding and LM head layers dominate updates while intermediate layers receive minimal updates—yet no prior method exploits this asymmetry to match LoRA's memory footprint while retaining full-parameter expressiveness.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained decoder-only LLM (ranging from 120M to 70B parameters) and a supervised training dataset (e.g., 52K Alpaca-GPT4 instruction pairs, 1.5B-token OpenWebMath corpus, or domain-specific QA sets). Unlike LoRA, LISA does not introduce any additional adapter parameters; it directly modifies the full model weights, so the output is a standard full-parameter checkpoint with no architectural changes or merge step required. The only hyperparameters controlling the trade-off are the number of unfrozen intermediate layers γ and the sampling period K (how many optimizer steps elapse before re-sampling layers).

## Method

LISA (Layerwise Importance Sampled AdamW) is motivated by the observation that LoRA's per-layer weight norms are orders of magnitude larger in the embedding and LM head layers than in intermediate self-attention layers. The key idea is to replicate LoRA's effective per-layer learning-rate profile via importance sampling: instead of assigning different learning rates to each layer, LISA keeps the embedding and head layers permanently active and randomly unfreezes only γ intermediate layers per sampling period K, using AdamW on the active subset. The sampling probability for each intermediate layer is set proportional to its LoRA weight norm, which in practice reduces to uniform random selection since all intermediate layers share similar small norms. This yields the same expected parameter update as LoRA's implicit layerwise learning-rate schedule while operating in full parameter space, avoiding LoRA's low-rank bottleneck. Theoretically, LISA inherits O(1/√T) convergence guarantees for convex smooth objectives via randomized block-coordinate Adam analysis.

## Results

On MT-Bench, LISA achieves 4.94 on LLaMA-2-7B, 4.85 on Mistral-7B, and 6.72 on LLaMA-2-70B, consistently outperforming LoRA (4.45, 4.41, 6.10) and full-parameter training (4.75, 4.64, 6.25). On knowledge benchmarks, LISA reaches 46.21 MMLU (5-shot) and 26.06 AGIEval (3-shot) on LLaMA-2-7B, surpassing both LoRA and FT. In memory efficiency, LISA (E+H+2L) reduces peak GPU memory from 79 GB (LoRA rank 128) to 75 GB on LLaMA-2-70B, and enables LLaMA-2-7B fine-tuning on a single 24 GB RTX 4090. LISA also delivers ~2.9× speedup over full-parameter training and ~1.5× over LoRA per iteration.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against LoRA, LISA wins decisively on MT-Bench across all model sizes: +0.67 on TinyLlama (2.57 vs 1.90), +0.44 on Mistral-7B (4.85 vs 4.41), +0.49 on LLaMA-2-7B (4.94 vs 4.45), and +0.62 on LLaMA-2-70B (6.72 vs 6.10). LISA also surpasses LoRA on MMLU and AGIEval for all three moderate-scale models, and on GSM8K (61.1 vs 59.4) and PubMedQA (91.6 vs 90.8) for the 70B model. Against GaLore, LISA wins on MT-Bench for Mistral-7B (4.85 vs 4.36) and LLaMA-2-7B (4.94 vs 4.63) but trails slightly on TinyLlama (2.57 vs 2.61); on MMLU and AGIEval, LISA outperforms GaLore across all models. Notably, LISA also beats full-parameter training on MT-Bench for Mistral-7B and LLaMA-2-7B, suggesting an implicit regularization effect from layer freezing, while FT retains an edge on GSM8K for the 70B model (67.1 vs 61.1).

## Relation to Prior Work

LISA draws its core mechanism from importance sampling theory (Zhao & Zhang, ICML 2015), repurposing it from data-point sampling to layer-level sampling in LLM optimization. It departs from LoRA's paradigm of confining updates to low-rank adapter matrices by instead performing full-parameter updates on a randomly selected subset of layers, guided by the empirical observation that LoRA's weight norms are heavily skewed toward embedding and LM-head layers. While related to prior layer-freezing strategies (AutoFreeze, FreezeOut, SmartFRZ), LISA is distinguished by its simplicity, its derivation from LoRA's observed update pattern, and its compatibility with standard memory-reduction tooling like DeepSpeed without requiring auxiliary predictors or complex scheduling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LISA offers a practical, drop-in alternative to LoRA that matches or exceeds its performance on instruction-tuning benchmarks while using comparable or lower GPU memory, making 70B-scale fine-tuning feasible on a single 80GB GPU. The observation that restricting layer updates acts as an implicit regularizer (outperforming full-parameter training on MT-Bench) opens questions about the width-vs-depth trade-off in memorization versus reasoning. Key limitations remain: the forward pass still requires the full model in memory (addressable via QLoRA-style quantization), the uniform random sampling of intermediate layers is acknowledged as suboptimal, and the 'beats full parameter' advantage is selective—full-parameter training still leads on GSM8K for 70B models. Future work on data-aware or architecture-aware importance sampling could further close the gap.
