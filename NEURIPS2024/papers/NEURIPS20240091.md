## Problem

Existing time series diffusion models suffer from two critical limitations: they lack meaningful guidance during the denoising process (unlike image diffusion models that leverage text or label conditioning), and they struggle with the small size and class imbalance of real-world time series datasets, causing them to underperform on rare or complex prediction tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240091/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a multivariate historical time series of length 168 (e.g., 321 electricity clients, 21 weather indicators, or 12 ECG channels) and a pre-constructed retrieval database of time series windows of length l+h. It produces a probabilistic prediction over horizons of 96, 192, or 336 steps, evaluated with both point-estimate metrics (MSE, MAE) and the probabilistic CRPS score. The retrieval database is built from the training set using either the full training set (for unlabeled datasets) or a balanced subset across all categories (for imbalanced datasets like MIMIC-IV-ECG).

## Method

RATD combines an embedding-based retrieval stage with a reference-guided conditional diffusion model. A frozen pre-trained TCN encoder maps the historical series and database windows into a shared embedding space, from which the k nearest neighbors (k=3) are retrieved as references. The denoising network is built on DiffWave's 2D transformer architecture and introduces a novel Reference Modulated Attention (RMA) module placed at the front of each residual block, which fuses three feature streams—the noisy time series, side information (temporal + feature embeddings), and concatenated references—via 1D-CNN feature extraction followed by matrix dot-product fusion. The model predicts x_0 (the clean target) rather than the noise ε, which the ablation shows yields better performance because the reference-to-target relationship is more direct. Training uses the standard DDPM objective with the retrieved references injected at every diffusion step, allowing references to iteratively guide the entire reverse process.

## Results

RATD achieves the best MSE, MAE, and CRPS across all four standard benchmarks: Exchange (MSE 0.013, MAE 0.073, CRPS 0.339), Wind (MSE 0.784, MAE 0.579, CRPS 0.673), Electricity (MSE 0.151, MAE 0.246, CRPS 0.373), and Weather (MSE 0.281, MAE 0.293, CRPS 0.301). On the MIMIC-IV-ECG rare-disease subset (cases <2% of total), RATD achieves MSE 0.206 and CRPS 0.301, substantially outperforming iTransformer (MSE 0.423) and CSDI (MSE 0.499). The largest relative gains appear on Wind, where RATD reduces MSE by 26% over CSDI (0.784 vs 1.066) and 12% over TimeDiff (0.784 vs 0.896).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RATD outperforms all four diffusion-model baselines (CSDI, TimeDiff, mr-Diff, D3VAE) on every dataset and metric, with the largest margins on Wind (e.g., MSE 0.784 vs CSDI's 1.066, a 26% reduction) and Electricity (MSE 0.151 vs CSDI's 0.379, a 60% reduction). Against transformer-based methods, RATD beats iTransformer on all four datasets (e.g., Wind MSE 0.784 vs 0.932; Weather CRPS 0.301 vs 0.318) and dominates PatchTST, Informer, and Autoformer across the board. The closest competitor is NLinear on Electricity, which edges out RATD on MSE (0.147 vs 0.151) and MAE (0.239 vs 0.246), but RATD still leads on CRPS (0.373 vs 0.419), indicating better probabilistic calibration. On the MIMIC-IV-ECG rare-disease subset, RATD's advantage is most dramatic: MSE 0.206 vs iTransformer's 0.423 and CSDI's 0.499, confirming that retrieval-augmented guidance is particularly effective for underrepresented, complex patterns. Inference efficiency is competitive: RATD's non-autoregressive design matches or slightly beats TimeGrad, MG-TSD, and SSSD across prediction horizons despite the added retrieval step.

## Relation to Prior Work

RATD builds directly on the conditional diffusion framework of CSDI and the 2D transformer architecture of DiffWave, adapting the DDPM training objective to time series forecasting. Its key departure is the introduction of a retrieval-augmented mechanism—previously explored in NLP (REALM, RAG), image generation (Blattmann et al.), and a few time series works (MQ-ReTCNN, ReTime)—into the iterative denoising loop of a diffusion model, so that retrieved references can repeatedly modulate generation at every diffusion step. The novel Reference Modulated Attention (RMA) module fuses three feature streams (noisy input, side information, and concatenated references) via matrix dot products, distinguishing it from standard cross-attention or simple concatenation baselines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete and underappreciated weakness of time series diffusion models: the absence of a natural guidance signal analogous to text prompts in image diffusion. By retrieving relevant historical samples as soft guidance, RATD achieves roughly 2× lower MSE than the next-best method on rare ECG cases (<2% of the test set), suggesting retrieval can mitigate data-imbalance effects that plague standard training. Open questions include scalability to very high-dimensional multivariate series (the transformer backbone becomes expensive), the added ~10-hour retrieval preprocessing cost during training, and whether the approach extends to other generative time series tasks beyond forecasting (e.g., imputation, anomaly detection). The lack of error bars and single-seed evaluation limits confidence in the magnitude of reported gains.
