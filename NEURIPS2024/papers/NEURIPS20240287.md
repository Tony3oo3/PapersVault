## Problem

Surrogate gradient learning (SGL) enables gradient-based training of networks with non-differentiable activation functions (e.g., sign, spiking neurons) but lacks a rigorous theoretical foundation. The neural tangent kernel (NTK) framework has been highly successful in analyzing gradient descent for smooth networks, yet it is directly inapplicable to activation functions with jumps because the NTK diverges to a singular kernel in the infinite-width limit. The paper asks whether the NTK can be generalized to capture the learning dynamics of SGL and provide a principled characterization of what wide networks trained with surrogate derivatives actually learn.

## Input / Output

The method takes a multilayer perceptron with NTK parametrization (depth L, hidden widths n_l, activation function σ, surrogate derivative σ̃), randomly initialized weights θ, and a training dataset (X, Y). It produces the SG-NTK kernel I^(L)(x,x'), which is generally asymmetric because one factor uses the true Jacobian J_θ f and the other uses a quasi-Jacobian J^{σ,σ̃} where σ̃ replaces σ̇. From this kernel, the paper derives the GP mean m(x) = I(x,X) I(X,X)^{-1} Y (the kernel regression solution) and the GP covariance, which together characterize the distribution of the trained network function in the infinite-width limit.

## Method

The paper first shows that naively extending the NTK to the sign activation function (via erf_m approximation) yields a singular kernel with infinite diagonal, confirming that standard gradient descent is ill-posed for such networks. To fix this, it introduces quasi-Jacobian matrices J^{σ,σ̃}(x;θ) that replace the true activation derivative σ̇ with a surrogate derivative σ̃ in the recursive chain-rule formula, and defines the SG-NTK as the product of a true Jacobian and a quasi-Jacobian. The key theorems prove that this generalized kernel converges in probability to a deterministic, generally asymmetric analytic kernel I^(L) at initialization (Theorem 2.4) and remains constant during SGL training (Theorem 2.5), with explicit analytic expressions derived for the sign activation case by taking the m→∞ limit of erf_m. Numerical experiments on regression over the unit sphere confirm that the distribution of finite-width networks trained with SGL agrees with the GP predicted by the SG-NTK, even at moderate widths (n=100).

## Results

The paper's main numerical results validate three theoretical claims: (1) the analytic NTK for erf_m activation functions diverges at Δα=0 as m→∞, confirming the singularity of gradient descent for sign activations; (2) the empirical SG-NTK converges to the analytic SG-NTK in the infinite-width limit both at initialization and during training (Theorems 2.4 and 2.5), with the analytic SG-NTK remaining finite as m→∞; (3) 500 networks with sign activation and width n=500 trained with SGL (surrogate derivative σ̃=erf, t=30000 steps) show their mean closely matching the GP mean m(x)=I_sign(x,X)I_sign(X,X)^{-1}Y, with most individual networks falling within the ±2σ_GP confidence band. Agreement with the SG-NTK prediction is already visible at the moderate width n=100.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not benchmark against competing training methods but instead contrasts the classical NTK (gradient descent) with the proposed SG-NTK (surrogate gradient learning) on the same network architecture. The classical NTK for sign-like activations (erf_m with large m) produces a singular kernel with infinite diagonal entries, rendering kernel regression useless (it only interpolates training points and predicts zero elsewhere). In contrast, the SG-NTK with surrogate derivative σ̃=erf yields a finite, well-defined kernel that successfully predicts the learning dynamics of SGL-trained networks. Figure 3b further shows that the SG-NTK mean outperforms kernel regression with the GP covariance Σ_sign (dashed line in Figure 3a), which does not capture the SGL dynamics. The convergence of empirical SG-NTKs to their analytic counterparts (Figure 2, B.4) is comparable in quality to the classical NTK convergence (Figure 1, B.3), though convergence slows for larger m due to the steepness of erf_m.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the NTK framework of Jacot et al. (2018) and Lee et al. (2019) to surrogate gradient learning by introducing quasi-Jacobian matrices that replace the true activation derivative with a surrogate, breaking the symmetry of the standard NTK. The GP convergence machinery and width-function formalism are inherited from Matthews et al. (2018), while the SGL/STE learning rule itself comes from the spiking neural network and binary network literature (Hinton 2012, Bengio 2013, Zenke & Ganguli 2018). The approach is conceptually similar to Bordelon and Pehlevan's (2022) 'effective NTK' for modified learning rules, but here the modification is specifically the substitution of a non-existent derivative with a surrogate, which is what makes the problem non-trivial.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This provides the first rigorous infinite-width theoretical foundation for SGL, a method that has been widely used in practice for training SNNs and BNNs but lacked any principled justification. The key open question it addresses is whether SGL is well-defined in the limit where standard gradient descent provably breaks down (the NTK diverges to a singular kernel for sign activations). Limitations include the restriction to wide, randomly initialized networks and the fact that convergence during training is only proven for differentiable activations, with the sign-function case supported only by heuristic arguments and simulations. The work opens a path toward characterizing the function class that SGL learns and how it depends on the choice of surrogate derivative.
