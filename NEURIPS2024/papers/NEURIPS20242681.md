## Problem

ECG heartbeat morphology reconstruction from partial or corrupted observations (missing leads, baseline wander, electrode motion) is a central but fragmented challenge: existing methods require separate task-specific training for each problem, and no single pretrained generative model unifies denoising, lead reconstruction, and anomaly detection. Most prior generative ECG work targets rhythm (beat timing) rather than the morphological shape of individual heartbeats across multiple leads, which is where many pathologies manifest.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a partial observation matrix Y of size L̃×T̃ (a subset of the 9 modeled leads I, II, III, V1–V6 and/or a temporal sub-window of the 176-sample beat at 250 Hz), together with patient covariates (age, sex, RR interval) and, for artifact removal, a sparse Fourier-basis artifact model. It produces a full 9-lead × 176-sample heartbeat reconstruction; for anomaly detection it additionally yields a 1−R² score between the posterior mean and the observed non-conditioned segment; for QT prediction it generates T-waves across a sweep of RR values from which Fridericia coefficients are regressed. The augmented limb leads (aVL, aVR, aVF) are excluded since they are exact linear combinations of I, II, III.

## Method

BeatDiff is a lightweight (≈10⁶ parameter) variance-exploding DDM whose denoiser is a depth-2 1D UNet with the F-net decomposition from Dhariwal & Nichol, conditioned on patient features via a 2-layer MLP, temporal position via sinusoidal positional encoding, and noise level via Fourier positional encoding; it is trained by standard denoising loss on 214,460 healthy heartbeats from PhysioNet Challenge 2021. EM-BeatDiff then formulates every downstream task (denoising, missing-lead inpainting, QT prediction, anomaly detection) as a Bayesian linear inverse problem Y = A_θ X₀ Ā_θ + B_θ + D_θ ε D̄_θ and samples the posterior using MCGDiff: a Sequential Monte Carlo particle filter that guides the backward diffusion with closed-form Gaussian intermediate potentials, requiring no fine-tuning of the pretrained denoiser. Unknown parameters θ (noise variance, artifact Fourier coefficients) are estimated by Monte Carlo EM, where the E-step reuses the MCGDiff particles and the M-step performs proximal gradient steps on a sparse group LASSO penalty (for artifacts) or a simple L2 penalty, yielding a unified, task-agnostic inference pipeline.

## Results

EM-BeatDiff achieves dramatic improvements in artifact removal, reducing SSD from 4.37 to 0.14 for baseline wander and from 0.27 to 0.18 for electrode motion, while improving cosine similarity to 96.69% and 95.42% respectively. For missing lead reconstruction, EM-BeatDiff reduces SSD to 1.03 (smartwatch) and 1.10 (V1-6) versus 1.63 and 2.10 for EkGAN. In anomaly detection, EM-BeatDiff achieves AUC of 84.82 (MI), 93.06 (LAD), 79.02 (LAE), and 84.73 (LQT), outperforming AAE on all four conditions. BeatDiff itself is 400× faster and 900× smaller in memory than the SSDM baseline while achieving higher F1 (78% vs 76%) and AUC (79% vs 77%) in the sex classification balancing task.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SSDM [3], BeatDiff achieves higher F1 (78% vs 76%) and AUC (79% vs 77%) in the sex-classification balancing task while being 400× faster in inference (160 ms vs 75,000 ms) and 900× smaller in memory (42 Mb vs 39,000 Mb). Against WGAN [1], BeatDiff similarly outperforms on F1 and AUC with a modest model size increase (42 Mb vs 27 Mb). In artifact removal, EM-BeatDiff dramatically outperforms DeScoD [56]: SSD drops from 4.37 to 0.14 for baseline wander and from 0.27 to 0.18 for electrode motion, with cosine similarity improving from 95.20% to 96.69% and 92.73% to 95.42% respectively. For missing lead reconstruction, EM-BeatDiff beats EkGAN [41] on SSD (1.03 vs 1.63 for smartwatch; 1.10 vs 2.10 for V1-6) but loses on cosine similarity (86.02% vs 91.33% for smartwatch; 87.78% vs 93.42% for V1-6), suggesting EkGAN preserves global waveform shape better at the cost of larger pointwise errors. In anomaly detection, EM-BeatDiff outperforms AAE [76] on all four conditions, with the largest margin on LAD (+10.37 AUC) and LQT (+13.77 AUC), and a smaller but consistent gain on MI (+4.59) and LAE (+4.15).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on MCGDiff [13], which provides the SMC-based posterior sampling machinery for Bayesian inverse problems with diffusion priors, and on the variance-exploding diffusion framework of Song et al. [86] for the generative model itself. The denoiser architecture follows the F-net decomposition and UNet design of Dhariwal & Nichol [24], adapted to 1D ECG signals with patient-conditioning (age, sex, RR). The key conceptual departure from prior ECG generative work (e.g., SSDM [3], which generates 10-second signals) is the focus on single-beat morphology across 9 leads, combined with the MCEM framework [95] to estimate unknown observation-model parameters (noise level, artifact coefficients) without task-specific fine-tuning, thereby unifying denoising, missing-lead reconstruction, anomaly detection, and QT prediction under one pretrained prior.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in replacing multiple task-specific trained models (DeScoD for denoising, EkGAN for missing leads, AAE for anomaly detection) with a single lightweight pretrained diffusion model that requires no fine-tuning per task, which is particularly relevant for resource-constrained settings like smartwatch-based 12-lead reconstruction. The model's ability to recover the Fridericia QT–RR relationship without explicit encoding suggests it captures genuine physiological structure rather than mere statistical correlation. Key limitations include training exclusively on healthy ECGs, prototype-stage validation on a single dataset, and the acknowledged risk of hallucinated 'healthy counterparts' in anomaly detection that would require clinical study before deployment. Open questions include scaling to multi-beat and arrhythmic signals, conditioning on pathology labels, and validating on diverse patient populations.
