## Problem

Selecting the optimal subset of pre-training data to maximize downstream task performance is a combinatorial problem generally considered intractable. Existing loss-based methods like RHOLoss require sequential, online selection that is computationally expensive and not parallelizable, while other approaches (DSIR, DSDM) either lack a language model or rely on linear influence approximations. The paper asks whether a simpler, fully offline and parallelizable criterion can match or exceed these methods when targeting a distribution different from the training corpus.

## Input / Output

The method takes three text inputs: a large pre-training corpus (C4, ~175B tokens), a small downstream dataset (25M tokens from Books or 7.4M tokens from 8 QA task training splits), and a prior dataset (3.1B tokens sampled from C4). It produces a selected subset of sequences of fixed size n (e.g., 3.1B tokens) from the corpus, which is then used to train a target language model. A hyperparameter τ controls selection aggressiveness: from τn randomly sampled candidates, only the n sequences with the lowest conditional loss reduction score are retained, so larger τ means more aggressive filtering at the cost of more inference compute.

## Method

CoLoR-Filter derives its scoring criterion by formulating data selection as a Bayesian optimization over the posterior probability of downstream data, then applying an empirical Bayes prior (conditioning on a prior dataset) and a point-estimate approximation over model parameters. The resulting criterion scores each sequence by the difference in negative log-likelihood between a 'conditional' model (pre-trained on the prior, then fine-tuned on the downstream data) and a 'marginal' model (pre-trained on the prior only), selecting sequences where the conditional model assigns higher probability. Unlike RHO-down, this is a fully offline, parallelizable procedure requiring only inference of two fixed auxiliary models—no sequential training or conditioning on previously selected data. The paper shows that small 150M-parameter auxiliary models can select data that transfers effectively to a 1.2B-parameter target model, achieving 25× data efficiency on Books and 11× on downstream QA tasks relative to random selection.

## Results

Using 150m auxiliary models to select data from C4, CoLoR-Filter (τ=16) trains a 1.2b target model to match a 1.2b model trained on 25b random tokens with only ~1.5b filtered tokens for Books (25× less data) and ~3b tokens for the 8 downstream tasks (11× less data). On the 150m model comparison (Table 3), CoLoR-Filter achieves 49.1 average accuracy across 8 downstream tasks, outperforming Random 1× (46.3), DSIR (48.3), and Random 8× (47.1). For the 1.2b scale-generalization setting (Table 4), CoLoR-Filter at τ=16 (10b tokens) reaches 57.6 average accuracy versus 53.1 for Random trained on 25b tokens, a 4.5-point gain with 2.5× less training data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DSIR (importance resampling via n-gram features), CoLoR-Filter wins on average accuracy (49.1 vs. 48.3) and on most individual tasks, with particularly large margins on ARC-easy (+5.2), OpenBookQA (+4.2), and SciQ (+4.4), though DSIR edges ahead on BoolQ (+5.6) and Winogrande (+3.2). Against FineWeb-edu (a classifier-based educational content filter), CoLoR-Filter consistently outperforms across all τ values when training 680m models on 10b tokens from FineWeb, which the authors attribute to CoLoR-Filter's targeted selection for specific NLP tasks versus FineWeb-edu's general 'educational content' criterion. CoLoR-Filter also substantially outperforms the Random 8× baseline (49.1 vs. 47.1 avg), meaning it achieves better performance with 8× less data than random selection. The RHO-down variants (46.1 and 48.5) and Conditional Only (46.0) all underperform CoLoR-Filter, with the sequential/online nature of RHO-down introducing instability and the Conditional Only ablation lacking the marginal model's contrastive signal.

## Relation to Prior Work

CoLoR-Filter is a direct descendant of RHOLoss (Mindermann et al., 2022), sharing the same Bayesian data-selection objective but departing in three key ways: it targets out-of-distribution downstream tasks (rather than in-distribution hold-out data), introduces an empirical Bayes prior conditioned on a large pre-training corpus, and replaces the sequential/online selection with a fully parallelizable offline scoring procedure. The paper also positions itself relative to DSIR (importance resampling over n-gram features) and DSDM (TRAK datamodel-based scoring), noting that CoLoR-Filter operates directly in function space by comparing model losses rather than relying on feature-level importance weights or linear influence approximations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method's simplicity (just comparing losses of two small auxiliary models) combined with 25× data efficiency on Books and 11× on downstream tasks makes it a practical, low-overhead tool for targeted pre-training, and the demonstrated scale transfer from 150m to 1.2b models broadens its applicability. Open questions include whether the lack of an explicit diversity term limits performance in settings requiring broader coverage, why in-distribution selection fails entirely (Appendix E), and whether gains are task-dependent (BoolQ and Winogrande actually degrade). Extending the approach to fine-tuning, continual pre-training, code, or non-text modalities remains unexplored.
