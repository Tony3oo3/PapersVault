## Problem

Existing spatio-temporal datasets are constrained by limited spatial coverage (often single cities or regions), short temporal spans (months to a few years), and a lack of multimodal supplements beyond raw time series. These limitations impede model generalizability across regions and seasons, and prevent exploration of large language and vision models for spatio-temporal intelligence.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242120/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The pipeline ingests MSWX meteorological observations (10 variables at 3-hourly/0.1°) and MSWEP precipitation estimates, geographic metadata from Köppen climate classification, ETOPO2 elevation, C3S land cover, and world-geo-json country boundaries, and renders geo-images via GMT/PyGMT under Mercator projection. Outputs are organized as 6,480,000 raster grids (at 0.1°) with 9 spatial-temporal resolution variants (0.1°/0.5°/1° × 3-hourly/daily/monthly) totaling 6.82 trillion numerical records, plus per-grid meta-text files, LLaMA3-generated geographic descriptions, 6 geo-image types (geoid, free-air anomaly, magnetic anomaly, mask, relief, gravity gradient), and satellite remote sensing images at 1° and 0.5° resolutions.

## Method

The core contribution is a multi-source data curation pipeline that unifies three modalities over 45 years of global observations. For time series, MSWX precipitation is replaced with MSWEP's higher-quality gauge-satellite-reanalysis ensemble, then resampled to produce 9 resolution variants. For text, a spatial prompt engineering strategy feeds factual metadata (country, climate type, land vegetation, mean elevation) as auxiliary context to LLaMA3-8B, mitigating hallucinations and achieving ~70% attribute retention verified via BERT-based regression/classification probes. For images, PyGMT renders six geo-image categories per grid cell under Mercator projection, with polar regions excluded due to distortion. The empirical evaluation benchmarks 10+ models (TimesNet, STID, GWNet, STGCN, ConvLSTM, SatCLIP, UrbanVLP, etc.) on precipitation forecasting and spatial variable prediction, revealing that spatio-temporal architectures do not consistently outperform time-series-only baselines on non-stationary precipitation, motivating the multimodal design.

## Results

On the spatio-temporal precipitation forecasting task (30-day history → 7/15/30-day prediction), TimesNet achieves the best average MAE across all five countries (UK: 2.816, USA: 1.371, CN: 3.152, SA: 2.076, AUS: 1.835), winning 30 out of 30 country-horizon comparisons. Surprisingly, dedicated spatio-temporal models (STAEformer, STID, GWNet, STGCN) and the precipitation-specific ConvLSTM do not outperform pure time-series models, with STID ranking second (e.g., UK avg MAE 3.232 vs. TimesNet 2.816). The trivial historical-mean baseline (HI) performs worst (UK avg MAE 4.026). For spatial variable prediction, SatCLIP dramatically outperforms GeoCLIP and CSP (e.g., precipitation MSE 0.010 vs. 0.162 vs. 0.997), and UrbanVLP* leads vision-language prediction (e.g., CN precipitation R²=0.745 vs. UrbanCLIP 0.617 vs. CLIP 0.483).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The most striking finding is that TimesNet, a pure time-series model, achieves the best MAE in all 30 country-horizon combinations, outperforming every spatio-temporal model (STAEformer, STID, GWNet, STGCN) and the precipitation-specific ConvLSTM by 0.3–1.7 MAE points depending on region. STID and GWNet are the strongest spatio-temporal baselines (UK avg MAE ≈3.23), but still trail TimesNet by ~0.4 MAE; the authors attribute this gap to precipitation's non-stationarity and extreme fluctuations that obscure spatial patterns. The historical-mean baseline (HI) is consistently worst, confirming that temporal dependency matters. For the spatial prediction task, SatCLIP (satellite-pretrained) dominates GeoCLIP (street-view-pretrained) and CSP (task-specific) by 16×–100× in MSE, showing that satellite imagery encodes more relevant environmental semantics. In the vision-language setting, UrbanVLP* (without its street-view branch) outperforms UrbanCLIP and CLIP across all three countries and variables, with the largest gains in CN precipitation (R² 0.745 vs. 0.617 vs. 0.483), validating Terra's multimodal design.

## Relation to Prior Work

Terra carves new ground in the spatio-temporal dataset landscape by unifying three previously disconnected data streams—high-resolution meteorological time series (from GloH2O's MSWX/MSWEP products), geographic metadata (Köppen climate, ETOPO2 elevation, C3S land cover), and LLM-generated descriptive text (via LLaMA3 with spatial prompt engineering)—into a single multimodal resource. It explicitly positions itself against the ImageNet/Common Crawl analogy, arguing that spatio-temporal data mining has lacked a comparable foundational dataset to drive the field toward general intelligence. Unlike prior earth-science datasets (ClimateSet, ClimSim, SEVIR, Digital Typhoon) that are limited in spatial coverage, temporal span, granularity, or modality, Terra simultaneously achieves global 0.1°/3-hourly resolution over 45 years with text and image supplements, a combination no single prior dataset provides.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Terra provides the first dataset at a scale and modality breadth sufficient to support spatio-temporal foundation model research, directly addressing the gap the authors identify between current spatio-temporal data mining and the broader AGI trajectory. Key limitations include the absence of 0.1°-resolution text and image modalities (restricted to 0.5°/1°), the inherent obsolescence risk of LLM-generated text, and satellite image licensing instability. The empirical finding that spatio-temporal models fail to outperform univariate time-series models on precipitation forecasting raises an open question about whether spatial correlation is genuinely exploitable for non-stationary meteorological signals, or whether external multimodal context (which Terra enables) is necessary to unlock spatial structure. Future work will likely focus on building spatio-temporal foundation models pretrained on Terra and on determining which multimodal fusion strategies actually improve forecasting accuracy.
