## Problem

Existing fMRI foundation models like BrainLM rely on masked autoencoding to reconstruct raw BOLD time series, which is ill-suited to the inherently noisy, spatiotemporally sparse nature of fMRI data and yields suboptimal linear-probing representations. Additionally, no prior work has established a functional coordinate system for ROI positional encoding that respects brain functional parcellation, nor a masking strategy that accounts for the heterogeneous spatial and temporal structure of fMRI patches.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242731/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes ROI-parcellated resting-state fMRI (450 ROIs from Schaefer-400 cortical + Tian-Scale III subcortical atlases × 160 timesteps at ~2 s TR), with temporal resolution standardized across multi-band and single-band acquisition protocols via downsampling. During pretraining, the observation encoder produces patch-level latent representations from a single sampled observation block, and the predictor outputs predicted representations for target blocks in the same latent space. For downstream evaluation, the target encoder's output is average-pooled into a single global fMRI vector that feeds a linear head or fine-tuned MLP for age, sex, disease, and trait prediction.

## Method

Brain-JEPA replaces MAE-style pixel reconstruction with latent-space prediction: a ViT observation encoder processes a single observation block, and a narrower ViT predictor (with EMA-updated target encoder) predicts representations of target blocks, minimizing L2 distance in latent space. Two key innovations differentiate it from I-JEPA: (1) Brain Gradient Positioning derives a functional coordinate system via diffusion maps on a functional-connectivity affinity matrix, producing a gradient matrix G that encodes functional (not merely anatomical) relationships among ROIs, which is linearly projected and concatenated with sine/cosine temporal embeddings to form the full positional encoding; (2) Spatiotemporal Masking partitions the non-observation region into three non-overlapping zones—Cross-ROI (α), Cross-Time (β), and Double-Cross (γ)—and samples K target blocks from each, forcing the model to generalize across unseen ROIs, unseen timesteps, and both simultaneously, with an overlapped-sampling strategy that flexibly adjusts the observation-to-input ratio. This injects a strong inductive bias tailored to fMRI's spatiotemporal heterogeneity, yielding faster convergence and more abstract, transferable representations than random multi-block masking.

## Results

On HCP-Aging, Brain-JEPA (ViT-B, fine-tuned) achieves 0.298 MSE / 0.844 Pearson ρ for age prediction, 81.52% accuracy for sex classification, 0.897 MSE / 0.307 ρ for Neuroticism, and 0.972 MSE / 0.406 ρ for Flanker, outperforming BrainLM (MAE-based) on all four tasks. On ADNI, Brain-JEPA reaches 76.84% accuracy for NC/MCI classification and 71.00% for amyloid ±, and notably achieves 65.98% accuracy on the Asian MACC cohort (NC/MCI) despite being pretrained exclusively on Caucasian UK Biobank data. On the internal UKB held-out set, Brain-JEPA attains 0.501 MSE / 0.718 ρ for age and 88.17% accuracy for sex, surpassing BrainLM by 18.5% in MSE reduction and 1.7 pp in sex accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Brain-JEPA outperforms all eight baselines across the majority of tasks. Against the closest competitor BrainLM (MAE-based foundation model), Brain-JEPA wins on sex classification by a large margin (81.52% vs 74.39% ACC on HCP-Aging; 88.17% vs 86.47% on UKB), on Neuroticism (ρ 0.307 vs 0.231), on Flanker (ρ 0.406 vs 0.318), and on amyloid classification (71.00% vs 67.00% ACC on ADNI) and Asian NC/MCI (65.98% vs 61.65% ACC on MACC). Against task-specific deep learning models (BrainNetCNN, BrainGNN, BNT), Brain-JEPA leads by 9–11 pp in sex accuracy and 0.11–0.23 in age ρ on HCP-Aging. Among the additional baselines in the appendix, Brain-JEPA surpasses BrainMass (concurrent SSL work) on sex (81.52% vs 74.09% ACC) and amyloid (71.00% vs 68.00%), CSM on all HCP-Aging tasks, and SwiFT on sex (81.52% vs 73.48%) and Neuroticism (ρ 0.307 vs 0.096). The one near-tie is Flanker MSE on HCP-Aging (0.972 vs 0.971 for BrainLM), though Brain-JEPA still leads on the correlation metric (0.406 vs 0.318).

## Relation to Prior Work

Brain-JEPA adapts the I-JEPA latent-space prediction framework (Assran et al., CVPR 2023) to fMRI time series, departing from the MAE-based reconstruction approach used by BrainLM (Ortega Caro et al., ICLR 2023). Its two core innovations—Brain Gradient Positioning, which derives a functional coordinate system from diffusion-map eigenvectors of a functional connectivity graph (building on Coifman et al., PNAS 2005 and the gradient literature of Margulies et al.), and Spatiotemporal Masking, which partitions the input into Cross-ROI, Cross-Time, and Double-Cross regions—address two gaps the authors identify as neglected at the AI-neuroscience interface: the absence of a functionally meaningful positional encoding for ROIs and the lack of a masking strategy tailored to the heterogeneous spatiotemporal structure of fMRI patches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Brain-JEPA is the first JEPA-based brain dynamics foundation model and demonstrates that latent-space prediction outperforms pixel-level reconstruction for noisy, low-SNR fMRI signals, particularly in linear probing and cross-ethnic generalization (trained on Caucasian UKB, evaluated on Asian MACC). The functional coordinate system provides a principled, data-driven spatial encoding that aligns with known brain network architecture, offering a reusable tool for any transformer-based fMRI model. Key open questions include whether the approach scales to larger architectures (ViT-H and beyond), whether pretraining on more diverse multi-ethnic and multi-site cohorts further improves robustness, and how the framework extends to multi-modal integration (MEG, EEG, structural MRI).
