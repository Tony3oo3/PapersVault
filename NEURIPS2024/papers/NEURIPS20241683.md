## Problem

Existing video-language benchmarks are restricted to short clips (seconds to ~3 minutes), leaving a critical gap in evaluating whether multimodal models can genuinely comprehend and reason over hour-long video streams. The paper addresses this by asking: can current models synthesize information across multiple temporal segments in long-form egocentric video, or do they merely perform short-clip understanding with temporal localization?

## Input / Output

The benchmark takes 500 egocentric videos from Ego4D (20–120 min, avg 45.7 min) sampled at 0.5 fps at 512×384 resolution, paired with a five-way multiple-choice question and a task-agnostic instruction prompt. For Socratic models, the video is first decomposed into per-minute captions forming a 'world state history' before being fed to the answering LLM. Evaluation is performed in task/sub-task batches rather than per-question to limit information leakage, with accuracy reported per sub-task and in aggregate; predictive tasks additionally receive precise timestamps for targeted video trimming.

## Method

HourVideo introduces a novel task suite of 4 tasks and 18 sub-tasks (summarization, perception, visual reasoning, navigation) with handcrafted question prototypes explicitly designed to require identification and synthesis of information across multiple temporal segments. The dataset is built through a five-stage pipeline: (1) manual curation of 500 videos from 1,470 Ego4D candidates, (2) LLM-based MCQ generation using 25 task-specific prompts with Chain-of-Thought at temperature 0.1, (3) human-feedback refinement by 7 annotators (400+ hours) to fix noisy narrations and invalid options, (4) blind filtering that discards any MCQ correctly answered by GPT-4-turbo or GPT-4 without video access, and (5) expert refinement by 4 annotators (300+ hours) for precision and difficulty. Causal, counterfactual, spatial-layout, and navigation questions are generated entirely by human experts (658 MCQs, 5.1%). The resulting 12,976 questions are evaluated zero-shot against Blind LLMs, Socratic models, and native multimodal models, revealing that even Gemini 1.5 Pro (37.3%) falls far short of human experts (85.0%).

## Results

On HourVideo (12,976 five-way MCQs over 500 egocentric videos averaging 45.7 minutes), the best model, Gemini 1.5 Pro, achieves 37.3% average accuracy, still far below the 85.0% achieved by human experts. GPT-4 as a blind LLM scores 19.6% (essentially at the 20% random-chance level), while Socratic variants using GPT-4 (25.7%) and LLaVA-NeXT-34B-DPO (22.3%) show only marginal gains. The largest gap appears in navigation tasks, where Gemini 1.5 Pro reaches only 28.1% versus 86.7% for humans.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Gemini 1.5 Pro, the only native long-context multimodal model evaluated, outperforms all other baselines by a wide margin (37.3% vs. 25.7% for GPT-4 Socratic and 22.3% for LLaVA-NeXT-34B-DPO Socratic), confirming that end-to-end multimodal processing is more effective than caption-then-answer pipelines for hour-long videos. However, even Gemini 1.5 Pro trails human experts by a staggering 47.7 percentage points (37.3% vs. 85.0%), and its advantage over random chance (20%) is only 17.3 points. GPT-4 as a blind LLM (19.6%) performs at or below random chance, validating that the benchmark genuinely requires video content. The Socratic approach with GPT-4 captions (25.7%) modestly outperforms LLaVA-NeXT-34B-DPO captions (22.3%), particularly on summarization (41.0% vs. 34.6%), suggesting that stronger language models benefit more from textual video representations. Tarsier-7B, a SOTA short-form video model, scores 26.7%—comparable to Socratic LLMs—indicating that short-clip pretraining does not transfer well to hour-scale understanding.

## Relation to Prior Work

HourVideo carves new ground by combining two common benchmark-construction strategies—narrowly focused question prototypes (à la Kinetics, Something-Something) and a diverse multi-task suite (à la GLUE, MultiBench)—into a single benchmark specifically targeting hour-long egocentric video. It repurposes the Socratic models paradigm for evaluating models that cannot natively process long videos, and leverages Ego4D's narrations as a structured intermediate representation for LLM-based question generation. Unlike prior long-video benchmarks (EgoSchema at ~3 min, NExT-QA at ~40 s), HourVideo pushes the temporal horizon to 45.7 minutes on average and explicitly designs tasks that cannot be answered from a single short clip, thereby isolating genuine long-range comprehension from temporal localization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 85.0% (human) vs. 37.3% (Gemini 1.5 Pro) accuracy gap—nearly 50 percentage points—provides a clear, quantified target for the field and demonstrates that current multimodal models remain far from human-level long-form video understanding. The comprehensive 18-sub-task suite (summarization, perception, spatial/temporal/predictive/causal/counterfactual reasoning, navigation) offers fine-grained diagnostics that shorter benchmarks cannot. Key limitations include the absence of audio, restriction to egocentric Ego4D videos, and the inherent difficulty of MCQ evaluation for open-ended understanding; future work will need to address whether the gap narrows with longer native context windows, memory-augmented architectures, or multi-modal (audio, tactile) inputs.
