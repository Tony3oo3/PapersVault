## Problem

Current MLLMs handle visual recognition (e.g., grounding) and understanding (e.g., VQA) in a purely sequential token-by-token manner, which is inefficient for recognition since bounding box coordinates require ~25 text tokens per box and are inherently numerical data better suited to regression. Moreover, this sequential paradigm prevents recognition results from informing subsequent understanding, missing the cognitive hierarchy where low-level perception supports high-level cognition.

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB image (processed by a CLIP ViT encoder at 224×224 or 336×336 resolution) and a natural language text prompt as input. The output is a text response in which object positions are represented by single index tokens (e.g., '<d2>') that reference pre-detected bounding boxes from the parallel recognition stage, rather than generating coordinate values as text. In segmentation mode, the model additionally produces pixel-wise masks via a mask head on the DETR decoder.

## Method

Octopus inserts 64 learnable object queries into the LLM head alongside image and text tokens. The bottom 16 layers of a 32-layer Vicuna-7B LLM process these queries in parallel via attention, allowing them to absorb user-prompt semantics; a lightweight 4-layer DETR decoder then performs class-agnostic parallel recognition (detection, grounding, or segmentation depending on the prompt). The resulting object queries are relayed to the top 16 LLM layers, which perform sequential understanding and output index tokens to select the correct detection for each mentioned object, eliminating the need to generate coordinates token-by-token. Training proceeds in three stages: vision-language alignment, DETR pretraining on grounding datasets, and end-to-end fine-tuning, with a combined loss of Hungarian-matched DETR supervision (BCE + L1/GIoU) and standard next-token-prediction language modeling loss.

## Results

On Referring Expression Comprehension (REC), Octopus (7B, 224 res.) achieves 88.77/83.05/83.11 on RefCOCO/RefCOCO+/RefCOCOg val, outperforming the same-backbone sequential baseline Shikra (87.01/81.60/82.27) while being up to 5× faster (0.17s vs 0.89s per image on RefCOCO). On VQA, Octopus scores 78.5 (VQAv2), 56.0 (OKVQA), 62.29 (GQA), 45.6 (VizWiz), and 65.7 (SciQA), surpassing Shikra and InstructBLIP across all five benchmarks. On general MLLM benchmarks, Octopus attains 66.2 (MMBench), 63.9 (LLaVA-Bench), 58.6 (SEED), 32.1 (MM-Vet), and 84.8 (POPE), exceeding Shikra by +7.4 on MMBench and +0.9 on POPE.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Shikra [6] (same 7B backbone, same training data, purely sequential), Octopus wins on all eight REC splits (e.g., +1.76 on RefCOCO val, +3.47 on RefCOCO+ val at 224) while being 5× faster on RefCOCO and 4.6× faster on Flickr30k; on VQA, Octopus beats Shikra by +1.1 on VQAv2 and +8.8 on OKVQA; on MLLM benchmarks, Octopus leads Shikra by +7.4 on MMBench and +0.9 on POPE. Against MiniGPT-v2 [38], Octopus wins on RefCOCO (+0.71 val) and RefCOCO+ (+3.47 val) but trails slightly on RefCOCOg val (83.11 vs 84.19 at 224, closing to a tie at 336). Against InstructBLIP [4], Octopus gains +13.09 on GQA, +11.1 on VizWiz, and +5.2 on SciQA, and leads by +30.2 on MMBench (66.2 vs 36.0 for the 7B variant). Against LLaVA-1.5 [49] and Qwen-VL [50], Octopus matches or slightly exceeds on VQAv2 (78.5 vs 78.5/78.2) and leads on GQA (62.29 vs 62.0/57.5) and MMBench (66.2 vs 64.3/60.6), though Qwen-VL edges out on OKVQA (56.6 vs 56.0). Against specialist detectors (Grounding DINO [39], UniNext [40]) and specialist VQA models (GIT2 [51], PaLI [52]), Octopus as a generalist trails by 1.8–3.9 points on REC and 3.2–5.8 points on VQAv2, which is expected given the specialists use higher resolutions (512–640) and task-specific fine-tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

Octopus builds on the LLaVA-style MLLM architecture (CLIP ViT encoder + MLP connector + Vicuna-7B LLM head) and repurposes DETR's object query mechanism as a parallel recognition module embedded within the LLM's lower layers. It departs fundamentally from prior MLLMs (Shikra, MiniGPT-v2, VisionLLM) that handle both visual recognition and understanding through purely sequential token-by-token generation, instead introducing a hierarchical 'parallel recognition → sequential understanding' split where the bottom LLM layers + a 4-layer DETR decoder detect objects in parallel, and the top LLM layers perform sequential understanding by selecting from pre-detected boxes via lightweight index tokens rather than regenerating coordinates.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 5× inference speedup on grounding tasks (replacing ~25 coordinate tokens per box with a single index token) is a practical efficiency win for deployment scenarios involving dense object localization. More conceptually, the finding that parallel recognition in the bottom layers improves accuracy on VQA tasks where no boxes are even requested suggests that recognition results genuinely support high-level understanding, validating a brain-inspired cognitive hierarchy. However, the work is limited to a 7B LLM, a single visual encoder (CLIP ViT), and detection/segmentation as recognition tasks; scaling to larger models, more recognition modalities (pose, tracking), and open-ended instruction data remain open questions that could determine whether this architectural split generalizes.
