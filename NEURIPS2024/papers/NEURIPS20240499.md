## Problem

Purely neural approaches like LLMs fail to produce fully correct programs in unfamiliar domain-specific languages, while purely symbolic combinatorial search scales poorly to complex DSLs. A fundamental tension exists between the most efficient PBE search technique—bottom-up dynamic programming, which factorizes the search space by merging states with identical evaluation results—and powerful LLMs that require unlimited left-to-right context, making them incompatible with this search paradigm. The paper asks whether a single pre-trained LLM can guide program synthesis across diverse domains without domain-specific training data.

## Input / Output

Given a set of input-output examples (grid pairs for ARC, tensor pairs for TENSOR, string pairs for STRING) and a DSL context-free grammar, the method samples 100 completions from an LLM at temperature 1, which are then parsed into the grammar (or, in non-strict mode, lexically analyzed to extract operator frequencies from syntactically invalid outputs). The output is a whole program derivable from the grammar's start non-terminal that correctly transforms every input example to its corresponding output, verified by the DSL's evaluation function.

## Method

HYSYNTH approximates the LLM's conditional output distribution with a task-specific probabilistic context-free grammar (PCFG) learned via maximum likelihood estimation with Laplace smoothing from the LLM's sampled completions. The PCFG is converted to a weighted grammar (w(R) = ⌈−log p(R)⌉) that guides bottom-up enumerative search, assigning lower costs to components the LLM uses frequently and thereby pruning the search space by roughly 4× on the motivating example. The key insight is that while a context-free model is a poor approximation of the LLM's full joint distribution, it can reasonably approximate the LLM's conditional distribution over output programs for a given prompt, and its context-free nature makes it compatible with bottom-up search's state-merging efficiency. For the ARC domain, the synthesizer further employs a divide-and-conquer strategy that searches for transforms and filters separately, leveraging the rule-based DSL structure. In non-strict mode, syntactically invalid completions are retained and their operator frequencies extracted via lexical analysis, proving especially useful for custom DSLs where LLMs produce many ungrammatical outputs (e.g., only 37.5% valid for STRING).

## Results

HYSYNTH solves 58% of 299 PBE tasks overall, compared to 40% for unguided search and 6% for direct LLM sampling. In the TENSOR domain, LLM guidance enables HYSYNTH to solve 96% of the 69-task benchmark, while in ARC it solves 58 of 160 tasks. For the motivating ARC example, PCFG guidance reduces the search space from ~450K to ~220K programs (4× speedup), cutting solution time from 4.5 minutes to 1 minute. The approach is robust to sample size: varying from 10 to 100 LLM samples changes performance by at most 2 tasks in ARC and TENSOR, and 9 tasks in STRING.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HYSYNTH outperforms all three domain-specific baseline synthesizers: ARGA on ARC, PROBE on STRING, and TFCODER on TENSOR. The most dramatic gain is in TENSOR, where HYSYNTH solves 96% of tasks versus TFCODER's lower rate, and additionally eliminates the need for users to manually specify non-standard constants—a key usability limitation of TFCODER. On ARC, HYSYNTH's custom divide-and-conquer synthesizer already outperforms ARGA even without LLM guidance, and LLM guidance further increases the number of solved tasks. On STRING, HYSYNTH-STRING outperforms PROBE by initializing the search with LLM-derived PCFG weights rather than PROBE's uniform starting weights with on-the-fly updates. Direct LLM sampling (no search) performs very poorly across all domains (0–14 tasks), confirming that LLMs cannot reliably produce fully correct programs in unfamiliar DSLs, while the binary surrogate ablation (which excludes all components not seen in LLM samples) underperforms even unguided search on ARC and TENSOR, showing that probabilistic weighting is essential rather than mere component filtering.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the idea of using a tractable surrogate model to approximate an LLM's output distribution (originally an HMM for style-controlled generation, Zhang et al. 2023) to the program synthesis setting, replacing the HMM with a PCFG that is compatible with bottom-up enumerative search. It builds directly on existing bottom-up synthesizers (PROBE for strings, TFCODER for tensors) and the divide-and-conquer synthesis strategy of Alur et al. (2017) for the ARC domain, but departs from prior hybrid approaches that required domain-specific trained probabilistic models by instead deriving a task-specific PCFG from a single pre-trained LLM's sampled completions. The key conceptual contribution is resolving the tension between search efficiency (which demands context-free factorization) and model expressiveness (which demands long context) by accepting a lossy but compatible approximation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The most practically significant finding is in the TENSOR domain, where GPT-4o solves 0% of tasks directly yet its guidance enables HYSYNTH to solve 96%, demonstrating that an LLM's partial intuition about relevant operators is far more valuable than its ability to produce a complete correct program. The non-strict mode, which extracts signal from syntactically invalid completions, is a practical technique that reduces the effective cost of LLM sampling for low-resource DSLs. Key limitations include the need to implement a synthesizer per DSL and the dependence of guidance quality on LLM completion quality; the paper identifies more expressive context-dependent surrogate models as a natural future direction.
