## Problem

DDIM inversion, the standard mechanism for editing real images with text-guided diffusion models, accumulates prediction errors that degrade content preservation and edit fidelity. The paper identifies the root cause as a singularity at t=0 in the derivative dx_t/dt of traditional linear and cosine noise schedules, which makes the initial noise prediction unreliable and causes errors to compound throughout the inversion. Prior work has focused on correcting inversion errors downstream (e.g., path alignment), but no prior work has addressed the noise schedule itself as the source of the problem.

## Input / Output

The method takes a real source image (512×512×3 RGB) and a target text prompt describing the desired modification, and produces an edited image in the same format. It operates entirely in the latent space of a pretrained Stable Diffusion model (64×64×4) with 100 timesteps, an inversion guidance scale of 3.5, and a reverse guidance scale of 7.5. The Logistic Schedule is a drop-in replacement for the existing noise schedule—no retraining or architectural changes are required, and it is compatible with any existing inversion-based editing pipeline.

## Method

The paper introduces the Logistic Schedule, defined as α̅_t = 1/(1 + e^{−k(t−t₀)}) with k=0.015 and t₀=int(0.6T), which replaces the cumulative product α̅_t = ∏α_i used in linear and cosine schedules. The key theoretical insight is that treating DDIM inversion as solving a probability-flow ODE reveals a 0/0 singularity in dx_t/dt at t=0 for both linear and cosine schedules (Proposition 3.1), whereas the logistic schedule yields a finite, well-defined derivative at t=0 (approximately 1.486×10⁻³·ε − 1.318×10⁻³·x₀). This eliminates the initial prediction error that seeds accumulation throughout the inversion. Additionally, the logistic schedule produces a more gradual logSNR decline (steady information perturbation) and ensures the final inverted latent more closely resembles pure Gaussian noise (comprehensive pattern capture), both of which improve the quality of the noise space available for editing. The method is validated across eight editing tasks with four different editing methods (Edit Friendly DDPM, Pix2Pix Zero, StyleDiffusion, MasaCtrl) and three inversion techniques (Null-Text, NPI, Direct Inversion) on Stable Diffusion v1.5, v2.1, and SDXL.

## Results

Across four editing tasks (attribute editing, object switch, style/scene transfer, non-rigid editing) using ~1600 images, the Logistic Schedule consistently outperforms both linear and cosine schedules on all background preservation and structure metrics. Headline improvements include a 34.6% reduction in Structure Dist (17.37 vs. 26.57 for cosine) on attribute editing, a 38.2% reduction in MSE (49.47 vs. 80.01), and a 10.7% PSNR gain (24.78 vs. 22.38). CLIP visual similarity also improves by 0.8–2.5% across tasks, while CLIP textual similarity is competitive (within ~1% of the best baseline in 3 of 4 tasks).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares the proposed Logistic Schedule against two standard noise schedules: the scaled linear schedule from DDPM (Ho et al., NeurIPS 2020) and the cosine schedule from iDDPM (Nichol & Dhariwal, ICML 2021). The Logistic Schedule wins on all six preservation/structure metrics (Structure Dist, PSNR, LPIPS, MSE, SSIM, CLIP Visual) across all four editing tasks, with the largest gains over the linear baseline (e.g., 34–38% lower Structure Dist, 38–47% lower MSE). Against the cosine baseline, improvements are more moderate but still consistent (e.g., 29–34% lower Structure Dist, 17–26% lower LPIPS). The only metric where the Logistic Schedule does not achieve the best result is CLIP Textual similarity on Style/Scene Transferring (24.77 vs. 25.39 for linear), a 0.6% gap the authors attribute to the trade-off between content preservation and text alignment. Overall, the Logistic Schedule dominates on content preservation while remaining competitive on edit fidelity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the DDIM inversion framework (Song et al., ICLR 2021) and the probability flow ODE formulation, identifying a previously overlooked singularity at t=0 in standard noise schedules (linear, cosine) that destabilizes the inversion process. It departs from prior inversion-based editing methods (Null-Text Inversion, NPI, Direct Inversion) that correct errors on the reconstruction path by instead addressing the root cause—the noise schedule itself. The work also connects to the broader noise schedule design literature (iDDPM, Karras et al., Lin et al.) but repurposes it for a downstream editing task rather than model training, positioning itself as the first schedule designed specifically for real-image editing without retraining.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play nature (no retraining, compatible with multiple editing methods and diffusion backbones) makes this a low-friction improvement for practitioners already using DDIM-based inversion pipelines. The singularity analysis provides a clean theoretical explanation for why inversion errors accumulate, which could inform future schedule or sampler design. However, the gains are moderate (structure distance improves ~30-35% in some tasks, but text alignment occasionally decreases slightly), and the approach is limited to the VP-SDE/DDIM setting with Stable Diffusion variants. Open questions include whether the logistic form is truly optimal, whether the analysis extends to other SDE formulations (e.g., VE-SDE, flow matching), and whether the benefits persist at higher resolutions or with newer architectures like SDXL where content preservation already degrades.
