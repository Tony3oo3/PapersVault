## Problem

State-of-the-art deep learning weather forecasters such as Pangu-Weather and GraphCast rely on highly customized architectures (3D Earth-Specific Transformers, multi-mesh GNNs) with minimal ablation studies, making it unclear which components actually drive their performance. This paper asks whether a standard vision transformer backbone, paired with a carefully designed training recipe, can match or exceed these complex systems while being far more interpretable and computationally efficient.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242195/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a 17-channel gridded field (4 surface variables + 13 pressure-level variables) at 1.40625° resolution (128×256 grid) from 6-hourly ERA5 data, along with a scalar forecast interval δt ∈ {6, 12, 24} hours. It outputs the weather dynamics Δδt = Xδt − X0 (the difference between consecutive states), which is added back to the initial condition to recover the forecast. At inference, multiple rollouts using different interval combinations that sum to the target lead time are generated and averaged to produce the final forecast.

## Method

Stormer replaces the standard ViT patch embedding with a weather-specific embedding that first tokenizes each atmospheric variable independently, then aggregates across variables via a single-layer cross-attention with a learnable query, reducing sequence length by a factor of V while capturing non-linear inter-variable interactions. The time interval δt is injected into every transformer block through adaptive layer normalization (adaLN), where a 2-layer MLP regresses scale, shift, and gating parameters from the δt embedding, amplifying the conditioning signal relative to additive embeddings. Training uses a randomized dynamics forecasting objective that samples δt uniformly from {6, 12, 24} hours, combined with a pressure-weighted loss that up-weights near-surface variables and a latitude-weighting factor. A three-phase multi-step fine-tuning schedule (K = 1, 4, 8 rollout steps) mitigates error accumulation during iterative rollouts. At inference, the Best m in n strategy generates n heterogeneous interval combinations, validates them, and averages the m lowest-loss forecasts, providing an ensemble-like effect without training multiple networks.

## Results

On WeatherBench 2, Stormer achieves competitive latitude-weighted RMSE on 9 key atmospheric variables (T2m, U10, V10, MSLP, Z500, T850, Q700, U850, V850) at 1–14 day lead times. At 14-day forecasts, Stormer outperforms GraphCast by 10–20% across all 9 variables and is the only model that beats the climatology baseline at long lead times. Stormer gains approximately 1 day of forecast skill relative to climatology compared to the next-best deep learning model. Notably, Stormer achieves this while training on ~190× less data than Pangu-Weather and ~90× less than GraphCast, completing training in under 24 hours on 128 A100 GPUs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Pangu-Weather (3D Earth-Specific Transformer, 0.25°, hourly data), Stormer matches or exceeds accuracy at 1–5 day lead times and clearly outperforms it from day 6 onward, despite training on ~190× less data. Against GraphCast (GNN, 0.25°, 6-hourly, 37 pressure levels), Stormer lags slightly at short lead times (1–5 days) but surpasses it by a growing margin beyond day 6, achieving 10–20% lower RMSE at 14 days across all 9 evaluated variables. Against the operational IFS HRES numerical model, Stormer achieves lower RMSE for most variables except near-surface temperature (T2m) at initial lead times, and performs only slightly worse than the IFS ensemble mean (IFS ENS); it is the first model trained on 1.40625° data to surpass IFS HRES. The non-ensemble version of Stormer (using only 24-hour rollouts) still outperforms Pangu-Weather and remains competitive with GraphCast, confirming that the gains are not solely from the multi-interval averaging strategy.

## Relation to Prior Work

Stormer departs from the highly customized architectures of Pangu-Weather (3D Earth-Specific Transformer) and GraphCast (multi-mesh GNN) by showing that a standard Vision Transformer backbone, augmented with three targeted modifications, achieves competitive or superior performance. It inherits the weather-specific embedding (variable tokenization + cross-attention aggregation) and adaptive layer normalization from ClimaX, adopts the pressure-weighted loss from GraphCast, and the multi-step fine-tuning from FourCastNet, but introduces a novel randomized dynamics forecasting objective that unifies continuous and iterative forecasting paradigms. The key conceptual departure is demonstrating that architectural simplicity combined with a well-designed training recipe—rather than domain-specific architectural innovations—is sufficient for state-of-the-art weather forecasting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Stormer provides a significantly simpler and more computationally efficient framework for medium-range weather forecasting, training on ~190× less data than Pangu-Weather and ~90× less than GraphCast while outperforming both beyond 7 days, which lowers the barrier for future research and foundation-model development in weather and climate. Its favorable scaling properties with model size and training tokens suggest substantial headroom for further improvement at higher resolutions. Key limitations include operating at 1.40625° resolution (vs. 0.25° for competitors), preliminary probabilistic forecasting that is underdispersive without IC perturbations, and the need for multi-step fine-tuning to close short-lead-time gaps. Open questions include whether the framework scales gracefully to 0.25° data, how to better quantify uncertainty, and whether randomizing additional components (e.g., input variables) can further improve accuracy.
