## Problem

LLMs struggle with complex reasoning and planning, and while self-correction and self-learning have been proposed, it remains unclear whether LLMs can effectively critique their own outputs to drive meaningful improvement. The integration of MCTS with LLMs for self-improvement faces three unique challenges absent in domains like Go: data scarcity (no equivalent of self-play data), exponentially large token-level search spaces, and the subjective, nuanced nature of feedback in language tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241669/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an initial expert-generated prompt-response dataset (e.g., GSM8K or MATH math word problems with final answers) and a base policy model (Llama-2-70B or WizardMath-70B-V1.0). It synthesizes new prompts via a transformation function, runs η-MCTS to search for high-quality reasoning trajectories, and fine-tunes the policy on the top-scoring trajectories. The output is an improved policy model that generates step-by-step chain-of-thought reasoning with a final numerical answer, evaluated by exact match on the answer.

## Method

ALPHALLM establishes a self-improving loop with three components: (1) an imagination module that synthesizes new prompts from existing data to alleviate data scarcity; (2) η-MCTS, which formulates text generation as options over an MDP (each option is a variable-length token sequence terminated by a learned or rule-based function), reducing search depth compared to token-level or fixed sentence-level search; and (3) a trio of critic models — a value function (MLP on top of a smaller LLM), a process reward model (PRM) for per-option assessment, and an outcome reward model (ORM) for full-trajectory evaluation — whose weighted scores guide the search. η-MCTS further incorporates importance-based adaptive branching (branching factor proportional to node value deviation, bounded by depth-dependent constants), state merging via similarity predicates to eliminate redundant sub-trees, and fast rollouts using a smaller specialized LM (Abel-002-7B) for low-bias, low-variance terminal estimates. After MCTS, the highest-scoring trajectory per question is selected and used to fine-tune the policy via standard language modeling loss, completing one iteration of the self-improving loop.

## Results

ALPHALLM, built on Llama-2-70b, improves GSM8K accuracy from 57.8% (greedy) to 92.0% after two rounds of self-improvement with ηMCTS decoding, matching GPT-4's 92.0%. On MATH, ALPHALLM built on WizardMath-70B-V1.0 improves from 20.7% to 51.0%, surpassing GPT-4's 42.5%. With ηMCTS decoding alone (no self-improvement), the model already reaches 88.9% on GSM8K and 48.7% on MATH, demonstrating the search component's standalone value. The full pipeline (self-improvement + ηMCTS) achieves 92.0% and 51.0% respectively, performing comparably to GPT-4 while using only final-answer annotations (7.5k) for critic training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ALPHALLM matches GPT-4 exactly on GSM8K (92.0% vs 92.0%) and substantially outperforms it on MATH (51.0% vs 42.5%), while remaining slightly below GPT-4 with PAL (94.2% GSM8K, 51.8% MATH). Against the strongest proprietary baseline, Gemini 1.5 Pro, ALPHALLM is marginally below on GSM8K (92.0% vs 92.5%) but trails more noticeably on MATH (51.0% vs 58.5%). The most striking comparison is against the base open-source models: ALPHALLM lifts Llama-2-70b from 57.8% to 92.0% on GSM8K (+34.2 points) and WizardMath-70B from 20.7% to 51.0% on MATH (+30.3 points), all without additional human annotations beyond final answers. Compared to standard SFT on Llama-2-70b (69.3% GSM8K), ALPHALLM's self-improvement loop with synthetic prompts and ηMCTS trajectories yields a further +22.7 point gain, demonstrating that search-guided self-training is more effective than supervised fine-tuning on the same base model.

## Relation to Prior Work

ALPHALLM draws directly on the AlphaGo/AlphaZero paradigm of MCTS-guided self-play, adapting it to LLMs where the three pillars of AlphaGo's success (abundant self-play data, tractable search space, unambiguous feedback) all break down. It departs from Feng et al. (2023), the closest prior MCTS-for-LLM work, by introducing option-level search nodes (generalizing both token-level and sentence-level), importance-based adaptive branching, state merging, and a trio of critics (value function, PRM, ORM) rather than a single value model. On the self-improvement side, it differs from self-refine and self-play fine-tuning approaches by using MCTS-collected trajectories as the training signal, arguing that the quality gap between MCTS outputs and nucleus sampling is large enough to enable effective iterative fine-tuning without human annotations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates a concrete, reproducible pipeline where a 70B open-source model can close most of the gap to GPT-4 on mathematical reasoning using only final-answer annotations and MCTS-generated training data, suggesting a path toward reducing dependence on proprietary models for complex reasoning. However, the framework is validated only on math (where feedback is binary), the critic models are static across iterations, and the self-improvement loop is limited to 1–2 rounds, leaving open whether the approach generalizes to tasks with subjective or multi-dimensional feedback. A key open question is whether the large gap between greedy-decoding performance (73.7) and MCTS-decoding performance (92.0) on GSM8K indicates that the fine-tuning loop is underleveraging the search signal, or that the base model's capacity limits how much it can internalize from MCTS trajectories.
