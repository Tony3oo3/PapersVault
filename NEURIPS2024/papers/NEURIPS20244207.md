## Problem

Existing individualized regression methods for matrix-valued data rely on external prior knowledge of sample similarity (e.g., weighted networks or Laplacian regularization) to constrain sample-specific coefficients, information that is often unavailable in applications like personalized medicine and brain imaging. This paper asks whether model individualization can instead be derived entirely from the internal structure of the samples themselves, without any auxiliary similarity information.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20244207/cropped_page9_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes matrix-valued inputs (e.g., 48×48 brain MRI slices) that are reshaped into a p×d patch matrix via a block-reshaping operator, along with a scalar outcome (e.g., MMSE cognitive score). It produces a scalar prediction decomposed into a homogeneous (common) and heterogeneous (individualized) component, an individualized coefficient matrix C_i = C + A_i^T D for each sample, and a sample-specific internal relation map A_i that encodes pairwise patch dependencies. The block size (d₁, d₂) controls the granularity of the patch decomposition and is a user-specified hyperparameter.

## Method

The core contribution is modeling the individualized coefficient as C_i = C + A_i^T D, where the sample-specific factor A_i = g(X_i W X_i^T) captures internal patch-to-patch relations via a rotation-correlation-inspired mechanism, with W a learned d×d matrix and g a normalization function. This formulation is shown to be algebraically equivalent to a self-attention mechanism: W decomposes as W_Q W_K^T (composite query-key embedding), D as B W_V^T (projected value), and A_i serves as the attention map. Training proceeds via alternating minimization—updating (C, D) by a two-level ridge regression and updating W by OLS followed by Frobenius-norm normalization—initialized by the top left singular vector of a data-driven matrix. Under a 2-RIP condition on the transformed feature matrix, the paper proves geometric convergence of both W and D estimates, with contraction parameter κ₁κ₂ < 1.

## Results

On the ADNI brain MRI dataset (1059 subjects, predicting MMSE scores), AIR achieves a test RMSE of 3.145 (±0.019), outperforming all four baselines: LRMR (3.715), TRLasso (3.292), DKN (3.261), and ViT (3.282). In simulation studies with 28×28 images, AIR's advantage grows with the degree of individuation (DI): at DI=2.0 in Case 1, AIR achieves RMSE 10.599 versus 26.239 (LRMR), 27.007 (TRLasso), 11.741 (DKN), and 24.098 (ViT). AIR also recovers the true coefficient shapes accurately, and in the real data analysis identifies interpretable internal relations (e.g., corpus callosum–hippocampus connections) for individual subjects.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AIR outperforms all four baselines on both simulation and real data. Against LRMR [Zhou & Li, JRSS-B 2014], AIR reduces RMSE by ~15% on ADNI (3.145 vs 3.715) and by up to 60% in high-individuation simulations (10.599 vs 26.239 at DI=2.0). Against TRLasso [Zhou et al., JASA 2013], the gap is smaller on ADNI (3.145 vs 3.292) but widens substantially in simulation (10.599 vs 27.007). Against DKN [Feng & Yang, 2022], AIR leads by ~4% on ADNI (3.145 vs 3.261) and by ~10% in the hardest simulation setting. Against ViT [Dosovitskiy et al., ICLR 2021], AIR's advantage is most pronounced in simulation (10.599 vs 24.098 at DI=2.0) but more modest on ADNI (3.145 vs 3.282), likely because ViT requires large sample sizes to fully leverage its capacity. The paper notes that LRMR, TRLasso, and DKN tend to estimate the sum of homogeneous and heterogeneous coefficients, while ViT struggles with the limited sample size of 1059 subjects.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of individualized regression and self-attention. Prior individualized regression methods (varying coefficient models, FORMULA, Localized Lasso) all require external information about sample similarity to regularize sample-specific coefficients; here, the authors derive individualization entirely from internal patch-level relations within matrix-valued inputs, eliminating that dependency. The key conceptual move is showing that the resulting model is mathematically equivalent to a simplified Vision Transformer (linear attention with a regression head), thereby providing a statistical/individualized-modeling interpretation of the attention mechanism. The theoretical analysis builds on the RIP framework from matrix completion literature to establish geometric convergence of the alternating minimization algorithm for the non-convex problem.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a principled statistical lens on attention: rather than treating attention as a black-box reweighting, it frames the attention map as an optimal internal relation map that individualizes a linear regression, which could inform more interpretable and sample-efficient attention-based models. The removal of external similarity priors makes the approach applicable in settings (e.g., brain imaging) where patient-to-patient similarity is unknown. Limitations include the restriction to linear form, the requirement for matrix-valued inputs with meaningful patch structure, and the fact that the simplified ViT connection may limit approximation power for complex nonlinear patterns. Open questions include extending to nonlinear attention, handling 3D volumetric data natively, and whether the geometric convergence guarantees hold under weaker conditions than 2-RIP.
