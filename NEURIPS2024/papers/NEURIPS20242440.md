## Problem

Designing provably efficient randomized exploration algorithms for RL with multinomial logistic (MNL) transition models has remained open: existing MNL-MDP algorithms rely on UCB-based exploration with O(K) per-episode computation cost, and no frequentist regret analysis existed for non-linear model-based RL with randomized exploration without assuming stochastic optimism. Additionally, the problem-dependent constant κ (reflecting MNL non-linearity) appears in the leading regret term, which can be exponentially large in the worst case.

## Input / Output

The algorithms operate on episodic MDPs with inhomogeneous MNL transition kernels, where the transition probability P_h(s'|s,a) is parameterized by a d-dimensional transition core θ_h* through a known feature map φ(s,a,s')∈R^d. At each step the agent observes state s_h, selects an action a_h, and receives a known reward r(s_h,a_h)∈[0,1] plus next state s_{h+1}. The output is a greedy action at each step, an online estimate of the transition core updated via O(1) computation per episode (no storage of past samples), and a stochastically optimistic value function Q_h^k(s,a) constructed by injecting Gaussian noise scaled by the inverse Gram matrix. The maximum cardinality of reachable states U and the problem-dependent constant κ (from Assumption 4) govern the tightness of the regret bounds.

## Method

The paper proposes two randomized model-based RL algorithms. RRL-MNL estimates the transition core via an online Newton step (exploiting strong convexity of the MNL negative log-likelihood) and constructs a stochastically optimistic value function by sampling M=O(log H) i.i.d. Gaussian noise vectors ξ~N(0, σ_k² A_{k,h}^{-1}) and adding the maximum inner product with the dominant feature φ̂ (the reachable-state feature with largest A_{k,h}^{-1}-norm) as a randomized bonus. This yields Õ(κ^{-1} d^{3/2} H^{3/2} √T) regret. ORRL-MNL improves the κ-dependence by using online mirror descent with the Hessian of the per-round loss (local gradient information) as the Gram matrix, combined with a feature centralization technique that expresses the Hessian in terms of centralized features φ̄(s,a,s';θ)=φ(s,a,s')−E_{s''~P_θ}[φ(s,a,s'')]. The optimistic bonus then uses per-state optimistic sampling indices m(s')=argmax_m φ̄(s,a,s';θ̃)ᵀξ^{(m)} plus a deterministic confidence term, achieving Õ(d^{3/2} H^{3/2} √T + κ^{-1} d² H²) regret where κ^{-1} no longer multiplies the leading √T term. Both algorithms require only O(1) computation and storage per episode.

## Results

The paper establishes the first frequentist regret bounds for randomized RL algorithms with MNL transition models. RRL-MNL achieves Õ(κ⁻¹ d^{3/2} H^{3/2} √T) regret, while ORRL-MNL improves the dependence on κ to Õ(d^{3/2} H^{3/2} √T + κ⁻¹ d² H²), removing κ⁻¹ from the leading term. On the RiverSwim benchmark (|S|=4, H=12 and |S|=8, H=24), all three proposed algorithms (RRL-MNL, ORRL-MNL, UCRL-MNL+) outperform the state-of-the-art UCRL-MNL in episodic return, with ORRL-MNL and UCRL-MNL+ reaching optimal values fastest. Computationally, the proposed algorithms are at least 50× faster than UCRL-MNL over 1,000 episodes, with the gap widening as episodes progress due to constant-time per-episode cost versus UCRL-MNL's O(K) cost.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is UCRL-MNL [Hwang & Oh, AAAI 2023], the only prior provable algorithm for MNL-MDPs. All three proposed algorithms (RRL-MNL, ORRL-MNL, UCRL-MNL+) outperform UCRL-MNL on episodic return for both RiverSwim configurations (|S|=4, H=12 and |S|=8, H=24), with ORRL-MNL and UCRL-MNL+ reaching optimal values noticeably faster. In terms of regret, UCRL-MNL achieves Õ(κ⁻¹ d H^{3/2} √T), while ORRL-MNL improves this to Õ(d^{3/2} H^{3/2} √T + κ⁻¹ d² H²), removing the problem-dependent constant κ⁻¹ from the leading √T term at the cost of a larger d-dependence. Computationally, the proposed algorithms require O(1) time per episode (incremental Gram matrix updates) versus O(K) for UCRL-MNL (full MLE over all past samples), yielding at least a 50× speedup over 1,000 episodes that grows with K. The paper also notes that RRL-MNL's regret differs from UCRL-MNL by a factor of √d, consistent with the known gap between TS-based and UCB-based linear bandit bounds.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the MNL-MDP framework of Hwang and Oh [35] (which used a UCB-based algorithm with O(K) per-episode computation) by introducing the first randomized exploration algorithms for this setting. The online parameter estimation for RRL-MNL adapts the online Newton step from MNL contextual bandits [55], while ORRL-MNL borrows the online mirror descent update from Zhang and Sugiyama [76] and the feature centralization / self-concordant-like log-loss technique from Lee and Oh [50]—both originally developed for bandit problems. The optimistic sampling mechanism for ensuring stochastic optimism follows Agrawal and Jia [7], but the paper is the first to provide a frequentist regret analysis for a non-linear model-based RL algorithm with randomized exploration without assuming stochastic optimism, a gap left by Ishfaq et al. [37] who required this assumption for general function classes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes that randomized exploration is viable for non-linear parametric MDPs with provable frequentist guarantees and constant-time per-episode computation, removing a key practical bottleneck of prior MNL-MDP algorithms. The improved κ-dependence in ORRL-MNL (moving κ^{-1} from the leading √T term to a lower-order d²H² term) is a meaningful theoretical advance, though the worst-case κ^{-1} can still be exponentially large. Open questions include whether the κ-independence can be fully achieved in the leading term, whether the approach generalizes to other non-linear parametric transition models (e.g., softmax with different link functions), and whether the inhomogeneous setting can be further relaxed. Empirical validation is limited to a single small-scale RiverSwim variant, leaving open the question of scalability to larger or more complex environments.
