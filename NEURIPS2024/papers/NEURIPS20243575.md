## Problem

Prior motion-based video segmentation relies on instantaneous optical flow, which creates blind spots when objects are momentarily stationary or when multiple objects coincidentally move together. Long-term point trajectories offer complementary motion information, but their complex, non-parametric evolution over time makes them difficult to model with fixed basis functions, leaving a gap in how to use them as a supervisory signal for segmentation networks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243575/cropped_page4_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB frames at 192×352 resolution along with pre-computed optical flow (RAFT, forward/backward at temporal offsets 1–6) and sparse point trajectories (CoTracker, ~25k points per frame over a 41-frame context window with 11-frame temporal smoothing). The output is a soft segmentation mask M_t ∈ [0,1]^{HW×K} with K=4 components, which are merged into a binary foreground-background mask via normalised cut on appearance features for the binary VOS benchmark.

## Method

The core contribution is a trajectory loss L_t that exploits the self-expressive, low-rank structure of point trajectories: trajectories belonging to the same object should be explainable as a linear combination of a small number of basis trajectories, so the loss minimises singular values beyond rank r (r=5) of the masked trajectory matrix P_k. This is combined with a parametric optical flow loss L_f (quadratic embedding kernel with 12 parameters per segment) and a temporal smoothing loss L_τ that enforces mask consistency across frames via trajectory warping. The segmentation network (MaskFormer with DINO backbone) is trained end-to-end on a dataset of videos using only these motion-derived losses, requiring no manual annotations. The low-rank formulation is more numerically stable than full SVD reconstruction losses because gradients depend only on singular values rather than on U and V^T, which become ill-conditioned as training drives higher singular values toward zero.

## Results

On unsupervised video object segmentation, LRTL achieves Jaccard scores of 82.2 on DAVIS, 81.2 on SegTrackv2, and 79.6 on FBMS, outperforming all prior methods including multi-stage approaches (RCF: 80.9/76.7/69.9, LOCATE: 80.9/79.9/68.8) and single-stage methods (GWM: 79.5/78.9/78.4). On the synthetic MOVi-F benchmark for trajectory-based motion segmentation, LRTL achieves 46.07 ARI and 65.76 FG-ARI, substantially exceeding subspace clustering baselines (SSC: 11.12/39.21, LRR: 7.47/37.36) and K-Means (15.26/42.53). The method operates at 192×352 resolution without post-processing (e.g., CRF), yet surpasses methods using 480×848 resolution with multi-stage training and pseudo-labelling.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LRTL outperforms all 14 baselines across all three real-world benchmarks. Against the strongest multi-stage competitors RCF [37] and LOCATE [59], LRTL gains +1.3 J on DAVIS, +4.5 J on STv2, and +9.7 J on FBMS over RCF, and +1.3/+1.3/+10.8 J over LOCATE respectively. Against the closest single-stage method GWM [10] (which shares the same MaskFormer+DINO architecture and flow loss), LRTL improves by +2.7 J on DAVIS, +2.3 J on STv2, and +1.2 J on FBMS, demonstrating the added value of trajectory-based supervision. On the synthetic MOVi-F trajectory clustering task, LRTL (46.07 ARI) more than triples the performance of SSC (11.12) and LRR (7.47), and is 3× better than K-Means (15.26). Notably, LRTL achieves these results at a lower input resolution (192×352) without CRF post-processing, whereas RCF and LOCATE use 480×848 resolution with multi-stage pseudo-labelling and CRF refinement.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the optical-flow-based segmentation framework of GWM (Choudhury et al., BMVC 2022, same group) by adding a complementary trajectory-based loss that exploits long-term point tracking. Conceptually, it draws on subspace clustering's self-expressive property (SSC, LRR) but reformulates it as a differentiable singular-value penalty that can supervise a segmentation network end-to-end, avoiding the quadratic-memory affinity matrices and per-sequence spectral clustering of prior trajectory-based motion segmentation. The approach also builds on the recent generation of high-quality point trackers (CoTracker, TAPIR) trained on synthetic data, repurposing their sparse long-term motion estimates as a supervisory signal rather than a standalone segmentation input.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that spatially sparse but temporally long motion information (point trajectories) synergises with spatially dense but temporally short information (optical flow) for unsupervised video segmentation, achieving SOTA on DAVIS, SegTrackv2, and FBMS with a single end-to-end trained network. Open questions include better handling of occluded points (current trackers produce noisy estimates for invisible points), extending the framework to multiple non-rigid objects, and whether caching trajectories across frames could reduce redundant tracking. The loss formulation is architecture-agnostic and could potentially be applied to other motion-based grouping tasks beyond segmentation.
