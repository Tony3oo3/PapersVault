## Problem

Foundation models for time series forecasting are hindered by data scarcity, and existing LM-based approaches require either centralized cross-domain training (raising privacy and copyright concerns) or domain-specific fine-tuning that prevents knowledge sharing. No federated foundation model exists that can leverage pretrained language models for cross-domain time series forecasting while keeping raw data local to each domain owner.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242995/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes multivariate time series with domain-variant channel counts (7–321) and lookback windows (36 for ILI, 96 for others), and produces forecasts over future windows of 24–60 steps (ILI) or 96–720 steps (other datasets). Unusually, the raw time series is never shared; instead, each domain locally converts its series into patch tokens in the LM's embedding space, and only the parameters of the modality-alignment and prompt-adaption modules are communicated to the central server for aggregation.

## Method

TIME-FFM repurposes a frozen GPT-2 backbone (first 6 layers) for time series reasoning through three trainable components. First, a modality-alignment module applies channel-independent patching and a linear projection to convert each univariate series into a sequence of patch tokens in the LM's embedding space. Second, a prompt-adaption module uses a modified multi-head attention mechanism to score the correlation between a reduced set of text prototypes (projected from the LM's pretrained word embeddings) and the patch tokens, then selects the top-M most relevant prototypes as dynamically determined prompts—replacing hand-crafted domain instructions. Third, a personalized federated strategy learns a global encoder (modality alignment + prompt adaption) shared across all domains via FedAvg-style aggregation, while each domain retains its own prediction head that projects the LM's output representations to the forecast, balancing cross-domain generalization with domain-specific prediction.

## Results

On the main forecasting benchmark (averaged over 8 datasets, 4 prediction windows), TIME-FFM achieves an average MSE of 0.555 and MAE of 0.434, outperforming all federated baselines (FedIT: 0.910 MSE, FedAdapter^H: 1.015 MSE, FedAdapter^P: 1.009 MSE) and the centralized unified model UniTime (0.569 MSE). TIME-FFM secures the best result on all 8 datasets (1st Count = 8). In the 5% few-shot setting, TIME-FFM achieves 0.329 average MSE, a 20% reduction over UniTime (0.411), and in zero-shot forecasting it reduces MSE by 13.9% relative to the second-best method.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TIME-FFM decisively outperforms all three federated fine-tuning baselines (TY1): FedIT [31] (0.910 vs 0.555 avg MSE), FedAdapter^H [47] (1.015), and FedAdapter^P [48] (1.009), with a 39% MSE improvement over the best FL method. Against the centralized unified model UniTime [25], TIME-FFM achieves a marginal but consistent edge (0.555 vs 0.569 avg MSE), winning on 7 of 8 datasets. Among other centralized methods, TIME-FFM beats GPT4TS [23] (0.652), PatchTST [43] (0.690), TimesNet [4] (0.756), DLinear [49] (1.934), FEDformer [50], Autoformer [10], and Informer [9] on average. In the additional comparisons (Table 11), TIME-FFM outperforms iTransformer [52] (0.298 vs 0.302 avg MSE on 3 datasets) and N-BEATS [53] (0.298 vs 0.346), though iTransformer edges it on Weather specifically. Against the large-scale pretrained foundation models Moirai [32] and MOMENT [56] (Table 12), TIME-FFM is competitive but slightly behind on several datasets (e.g., Electricity: 0.216 vs 0.165 for MOMENT), which is notable given those models are pre-trained on large-scale time series archives while TIME-FFM operates under federated constraints without accessing raw cross-domain data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two research threads: (1) cross-modality adaptation of pretrained LMs for time series forecasting (Time-LLM, GPT4TS, UniTime), which typically operates in centralized settings, and (2) federated fine-tuning of LMs for NLP tasks (FedIT, FedAdapter, FedLoRA), which handles privacy but stays within the text modality. TIME-FFM departs from both by being the first to perform cross-modality LM adaptation under a federated paradigm, borrowing PatchTST's channel-independent patching for modality alignment and Time-LLM's text-prototype projection for prompt adaption, while introducing a personalized FL strategy (global encoder + domain-specific prediction heads) to handle cross-domain heterogeneity without raw data sharing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap: time series in finance, healthcare, and energy are often privacy-sensitive or license-restricted, making centralized foundation model training infeasible. The finding that a federated model can match or exceed a centralized unified model (UniTime) in zero-shot and few-shot settings challenges the assumption that full data access is necessary for strong generalization. Limitations include increased training time, evaluation on only 8 benchmark datasets with a single LM backbone (GPT2-6L), and the open question of whether patch embeddings and word embeddings share a deeper semantic correspondence that could enable more principled modality alignment.
