## Problem

Agent-based models require discrete origin-destination matrices (ODMs) to simulate individual agent trips, but existing approaches either run expensive ABM simulations or approximate the ODM with a continuous intensity matrix and apply ad-hoc discretisation. This continuous relaxation impedes conditioning on partially observed summary statistics, fails to explore the multimodal distribution over the discrete combinatorial support of constrained tables, and introduces discretisation errors that bias downstream ABM calibration.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243505/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes as input the observed log destination attraction vector y ∈ R^J, an I×J travel cost matrix C, a set of summary-statistic constraints C_T (row marginals, column marginals, total agent count, and/or fixed cell values), and the total number of agents A. It produces samples of the discrete I×J contingency table T with non-negative integer entries, the continuous trip intensity matrix Λ ∈ R_{>0}^{I×J}, and the two SIM utility parameters θ = (α, β). The discrete table T is the primary output for agent population synthesis, while Λ serves as the continuous mean-field surrogate for faster ABM emulation.

## Method

GENSIT couples a neural network ψ_NN that maps observed log-destination attraction y to SIM utility parameters θ with a forward Euler-Maruyama solver for the Harris-Wilson SDE, yielding a physics-driven, stochastic estimate of the continuous trip intensity Λ at each iteration. The continuous Λ is then mapped to the discrete table T via constrained sampling: when C_T contains at most one marginal (tractable case), closed-form Multinomial or Product-Multinomial draws are used; when both row and column marginals are fixed (intractable case), a Gibbs Markov Basis sampler operates on the fiber of Fisher's non-central multivariate hypergeometric distribution using 2×2 moves that preserve all summary statistics. Two sampling schemes are offered—Joint (a full Gibbs sampler where T information feeds back into the NN loss) and Disjoint (a collapsed Gibbs sampler that integrates out T)—allowing practitioners to trade off information propagation against convergence speed. The overall framework scales linearly in the number of origin-destination pairs IJ, which is at least I or J times faster than prior MCMC-based approaches, while jointly exploring both the continuous Λ and discrete T combinatorial spaces in a single optimisation-sampling loop.

## Results

On the Cambridge ODM (69×13, 33,704 agents), the Joint GENSIT scheme achieves the best reconstruction error (SRMSE) and ground truth coverage (CP) in T-space across all constraint regimes except the totally constrained case: e.g., SRMSE 0.55 and CP 89% for the doubly constrained ODM, versus 0.59/86% for SIT-MCMC and 1.22/20% for the Disjoint variant. On the larger Washington DC ODM (179×179, 200,029 agents), GENSIT (Joint) achieves SRMSE 2.37±0.08 and 99% CP 44%±1%, outperforming GMEL (SRMSE 2.43±0.15, CP 5%±1%) and SIM-NN (SRMSE 2.47±0.00, CP 24%±3%). The framework scales linearly in the number of origin-destination pairs IJ, achieving an O(J²) speed-up over MCMC-based methods like SIM-MCMC and SIT-MCMC.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GENSIT (Joint) outperforms SIT-MCMC [47] on SRMSE in all intractable (doubly constrained) Cambridge cases: 0.55 vs 0.59 (doubly), 0.43 vs 0.56 (doubly+10% cell), and 0.38 vs 0.51 (doubly+20% cell), while also achieving higher CP (89–94% vs 86–90%). GENSIT (Joint) dramatically outperforms SIM-NN [17] and SIM-MCMC [13] in the intractable regime, where those methods either cannot operate in T-space or produce much higher errors (e.g., SIM-NN SRMSE 0.43 in Λ-space for singly constrained vs GENSIT Joint 0.43 in T-space, but SIM-NN cannot produce discrete tables). On Washington DC, GENSIT (Joint) beats GMEL [27] by 2.5% in SRMSE (2.37 vs 2.43) and by 39 percentage points in CP (44% vs 5%), and beats SIM-NN by 4% in SRMSE (2.37 vs 2.47) and by 20 percentage points in CP (44% vs 24%). The only case where a baseline wins is the totally constrained Cambridge ODM, where SIT-MCMC's restricted θ support [0,2]² acts as implicit regularisation in the absence of rich constraint data, yielding SRMSE 0.70 vs GENSIT's 2.43.

## Relation to Prior Work

GENSIT unifies three previously separate research threads: (1) the Harris-Wilson SDE coupled with Spatial Interaction Models for continuous ODM intensities (Ellam et al. 2018; Gaskin et al. 2023), (2) neural network parameter calibration of SIMs that bypasses intractable Bayesian inference over the SDE (Gaskin et al. 2023, SIM-NN), and (3) Markov Basis / Gibbs sampling for discrete contingency tables under summary-statistic constraints (Diaconis & Sturmfels 1998; Zachos et al. 2024, SIT-MCMC). Prior work either restricted inference to the continuous Λ level (SIM-MCMC, SIM-NN, GMEL) or performed discrete T-space sampling via expensive MCMC with at least O(I) or O(J) overhead (SIT-MCMC). GENSIT departs by jointly optimising in Λ-space (via back-propagation through the Euler-Maruyama HW solver) and sampling in T-space (via closed-form or Gibbs Markov Basis) within a single linear-in-IJ loop, which no prior framework achieves.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The linear O(IJ) scaling makes discrete ODM reconstruction feasible at city-scale dimensions (e.g., 1000×1000) where MCMC-based approaches become prohibitive, directly enabling large-scale ABM population synthesis and policy scenario testing in transportation and epidemiology. The paper identifies an open problem in assimilating more complex constraint structures (beyond row/column marginals and cell fixes) and notes that the SIM's utility-function assumptions may not capture real agent decision-making, limiting generalisability. A notable limitation is that in the totally constrained regime, the simpler SIT-MCMC with its implicit θ∈[0,2]² regularisation still outperforms GENSIT, suggesting the neural approach's flexibility can be a liability without sufficient data. Broader applicability to contingency-table problems in neuroscience (brain connectivity) and epidemiology (contact matrices) is noted but not empirically demonstrated.
