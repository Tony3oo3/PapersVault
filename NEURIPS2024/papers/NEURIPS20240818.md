## Problem

Score Distillation Sampling (SDS) produces over-smoothed, over-saturated 3D shapes that fail to match the quality of the underlying 2D diffusion model, yet the root cause of this discrepancy has not been formally explained. The paper identifies that SDS's i.i.d. random noise resampling at each step introduces excessive variance relative to DDIM's conditionally-inferred noise, breaking the denoising trajectory and forcing practitioners to use extreme CFG values (≈100) that cause over-saturation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240818/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single text prompt as input and optimizes a 3D volumetric representation (InstantNGP) over 10k steps, rendering 512×512 views from random camera angles at each iteration. The frozen Stable Diffusion 2.1 model serves as the 2D guidance signal; no additional training data, multi-view supervision, or auxiliary networks are required. The output is a differentiable 3D shape that can be rendered from arbitrary viewpoints, with a typical generation taking ~2 hours on an NVIDIA A6000 GPU using 39.2 GB VRAM.

## Method

The paper reparametrizes the DDIM denoising ODE in terms of x_0(t) (the single-step denoised image), revealing that SDS is a high-variance special case of DDIM where the conditional noise term κ_y^t is replaced by i.i.d. Gaussian sampling. SDI closes this gap by approximating κ_y^t via prompt-conditioned DDIM inversion at each update step: the current 3D rendering is inverted through the diffusion ODE (using negative CFG γ_inv = −7.5) to recover a structured noise sample, which is then used in the standard SDS gradient update with positive CFG γ_fwd = 7.5. A linear annealing schedule on t (from 1 to 0.2) and a small entropy term on the inverted noise further stabilize generation. This single modification—replacing random noise with inverted noise—eliminates over-smoothing, preserves high-frequency detail, and allows standard CFG values without over-saturation, matching or surpassing state-of-the-art methods like ProlificDreamer and HiFA without additional networks or multi-stage pipelines.

## Results

On 43 text-to-3D prompts evaluated with 50 views each, SDI (10k steps) achieves the highest CLIP Score of 33.47±2.49, surpassing VSD/ProlificDreamer (33.31±2.39, 25k steps) and HIFA (32.80±2.35, 25k steps). SDI also attains the best CLIP IQA 'quality' score (82%) and matches HIFA's low divergence rate (4.7%) while running in 119 minutes with 39.2 GB VRAM—roughly half the time and memory of 25k-step baselines (235–334 min, 46–48 GB). Compared to the original SDS (29.81 CLIP Score, 18.6% divergence), SDI improves CLIP Score by ~3.7 points and reduces divergence by a factor of four.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SDI outperforms the original SDS (DreamFusion) on every metric: CLIP Score improves from 29.81 to 33.47, CLIP IQA quality from 76% to 82%, and divergence drops from 18.6% to 4.7%. Against SJC, SDI gains ~3 CLIP Score points and halves the divergence rate. Compared to VSD/ProlificDreamer (25k steps), SDI achieves a higher CLIP Score (33.47 vs. 33.31) and substantially better quality (82% vs. 77%) while using only 10k steps and ~40% less VRAM; VSD has a marginally better ImageReward (−1.17 vs. −1.18) but a much higher divergence rate (23.2% vs. 4.7%). SDI also beats ESD (32.79) and HIFA (32.80) on CLIP Score and quality, matching HIFA's divergence rate while running in roughly half the time. In the dedicated ISM (LucidDreamer) comparison (Table 2, 5k steps, ISM codebase), ISM has a slightly higher CLIP Score (28.60 vs. 28.47), but SDI achieves better quality (0.88 vs. 0.85) and ImageReward (−0.30 vs. −0.52). For the remaining baselines (NFSD, Magic3D, Fantasia3D, StableSD, ClassifierSD), the paper provides qualitative comparisons in Figures 7 and 28–30, where SDI consistently produces sharper, more detailed, and less over-saturated shapes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper re-derives Score Distillation Sampling (SDS) from DDIM via a change of variables to the single-step denoised image space, revealing that SDS is a high-variance reparameterization of DDIM that differs only in its noise sampling strategy (i.i.d. random vs. conditionally inferred). It positions Interval Score Matching (ISM) as a special case of the derived formulation and departs from prior heuristic-based improvements (ProlificDreamer's fine-tuned second model, HiFA's multi-technique pipeline) by offering a single principled modification: replacing random noise with prompt-conditioned DDIM inversion. The work builds directly on DreamFusion's SDS framework and DDIM's sampling/inversion machinery, unifying them under a common velocity-field interpretation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The theoretical unification of SDS and DDIM provides a clear explanation for why score distillation produces over-smoothed, over-saturated 3D shapes, and the proposed fix (conditional DDIM inversion with negative CFG) closes much of the quality gap to 2D sampling without additional networks or multi-stage training. However, 3D multi-view consistency remains a significant open problem: the method still suffers from content drift, the Janus problem, and hollow-face artifacts, and it inherits all biases and hallucinations of the underlying 2D diffusion model. Future work on view conditioning, depth/normal supervision, or video-based generation may be needed to fully resolve cross-view coherence.
