## Problem

Grammar-constrained decoding (GCD) guarantees that LLM outputs satisfy a given context-free grammar, but it distorts the LLM's learned distribution by treating all grammatically valid prefixes as equally likely (a binary approximation of future grammaticality). This bias can cause the model to produce grammatical yet low-likelihood outputs, effectively undermining the LLM's ability to follow prompts. The paper formalizes this gap as grammar-aligned decoding (GAD)—sampling from the LLM's distribution conditioned on the grammar—and shows that exact GAD is intractable, motivating an approximate solution.

## Input / Output

The method takes an autoregressive LLM (Mistral-7B), a context-free grammar specifying the allowed output structure, and a text prompt (e.g., a SyGuS specification or a sentence to parse). It produces a single grammar-constrained output string (a program term or a parenthesized parse tree) that is guaranteed to be in the grammar's language. Unlike standard GCD which produces one sample per call, ASAp is inherently iterative: it runs M sampling rounds, each round drawing one full sequence and updating internal bookkeeping before the next draw, with the final returned sample being the last one drawn.

## Method

ASAp (Adaptive Sampling with Approximate Expected Futures) augments standard GCD by maintaining, for every prefix encountered in past samples, an overapproximation c̃_S(w_{1:i}) of the Expected Future Grammaticality (EFG)—the probability that an unconstrained continuation of that prefix under the LLM lands in the grammar. The sampling conditional at each step is P(w_i | w_{1:i-1}) · c̃_S(w_{1:i}) normalized over all next tokens, replacing GCD's binary mask with a soft re-weighting. After each sampled sequence, the algorithm back-propagates from the last token to the first, setting c̃_S to 0 for tokens that provably lead to non-grammatical prefixes and recomputing parent EFG values via the recursive definition in Eq. 3. Theorem 1 proves c̃_S is always an upper bound on the true EFG, and Theorem 2 proves that as the number of iterations grows, c̃_S converges in probability to the exact EFG, yielding unbiased samples from the constrained LLM distribution. The algorithm is built directly on top of the Transformers-CFG incremental parser, requiring no changes to the LLM itself.

## Results

After 2,000 sampling iterations, ASAp achieves substantially lower sum-of-least-squares error in expectation alignment compared to GCD: 1.242 vs. 2.259 on SLIA, 0.802 vs. 1.852 on INV-BV4, and 0.159 vs. 0.109 on Constituency Parsing (CP). In terms of correctness on SyGuS benchmarks, ASAp produces correct solutions 38% more often than GCD in geomean across all tasks, though on SLIA benchmarks solvable by both methods, GCD still produces correct samples 73% more often. ASAp is the only method to solve the SLIA/initials_long-repeat benchmark (3 correct samples vs. 0 for GCD), and on the INV-BV benchmark find_inv_eq_bvlshr0_4bit, ASAp yields 635 correct samples versus only 228 for GCD.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is Grammar-Constrained Decoding (GCD) as implemented in Transformers-CFG [Geng et al., 2023]. ASAp wins decisively on distributional alignment: its KL divergence to the target GAD distribution converges to 0 over iterations while GCD's remains flat, and its empirical expectations track the true LLM distribution far more closely (e.g., 0.802 vs. 1.852 sum-of-squares error on INV-BV4). On correctness, the picture is mixed: ASAp outperforms GCD on most INV-BV benchmarks (e.g., 635 vs. 228 on find_inv_eq_bvlshr0_4bit; 84 vs. 36 on find_inv_bvugt_bvneg_4bit) and on the illustrative Ex. 3 scenario, but GCD still dominates on several SLIA benchmarks where the LLM's natural distribution already aligns well with the grammar (e.g., 1997 vs. 1960 on firstname; 1654 vs. 357 on dr-name). After QLoRA fine-tuning, ASAp's advantage is even more pronounced, reducing expectation error to 0.051 on INV-BV4 versus GCD's 0.677, confirming that the distributional bias of GCD persists even when the base model is task-tuned.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on grammar-constrained decoding (GCD) techniques such as Transformers-CFG, PICARD, and grammar prompting, which mask tokens that would lead to non-grammatical sequences during autoregressive generation. The key departure is conceptual: the authors show that all existing constrained decoding methods implicitly use a binary (0/1) approximation of the expected future grammaticality (EFG), which systematically distorts the LLM's learned distribution. ASAp is positioned as a drop-in refinement layered on top of any existing GCD implementation, replacing the binary mask with an iteratively refined over-approximation of EFG that provably converges to the exact conditional distribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a previously unrecognized and fundamental bias in all grammar-constrained decoding: outputs are grammatical but their likelihoods are not proportional to the LLM's own distribution, effectively undermining the model's learned capabilities (e.g., GCD drops a 90% prompt-following rate to 30%). The main open question is convergence speed—ASAp requires thousands of iterations and still fails to converge on some benchmarks, limiting its practical utility. Future work on targeted exploration (beam search bootstrapping, multi-token sampling) and extension beyond CFGs to general constraints are natural next steps that could make the theoretical framework practically deployable.
