## Problem

On-road object importance estimation lacks both a sufficiently large public dataset (only Ohn-Bar's 3,187-frame set is available) and a model that jointly considers the multiple top-down factors human drivers use—driver intention, semantic context, and traffic rule—alongside bottom-up visual features. Existing methods rely on either bottom-up saliency alone or a single type of top-down signal, leaving them unable to handle the dynamic and diverse conditions of real traffic.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a 16-frame RGB video clip (resized to 320×320) from the driver's perspective, corresponding optical flow images, a semantic segmentation map (produced by DeepLabv3), lane marking point coordinates (from CLRNet), and the ego-car's angular velocity at the first frame. Object bounding boxes are assumed known from an upstream detector. The output is a Softmax probability over all N detected objects at the final time step, indicating each object's importance relative to the others.

## Method

The architecture fuses a bottom-up pathway (dual ResNet18 backbones on RGB and optical flow, ROI pooling, LSTM for temporal features, and multi-head self-attention for spatial features) with a top-down pathway containing two modules. The DISG module multiplies a semantic feature (ResNet18 on the segmentation map) by a hand-crafted intention-guiding mask selected from three fixed patterns (left-turn, straight, right-turn) based on the ego-car's angular velocity, then refines object spatial features via multi-head cross-attention. The TRG module performs adaptive object-lane interaction: lane coordinates are cross-attended with object temporal features, and an MLP-based gating score adaptively scales the interaction to near-zero for objects weakly coupled to lanes (e.g., parked roadside cars), preventing spurious lane influence. The two top-down features are concatenated with the bottom-up feature through a final MLP and Softmax to yield per-object importance, trained with a combined BCE + Focal loss.

## Results

On the TOI dataset, the proposed model achieves 60% AP and 54% F1, outperforming the second-best method (Goal) by 20.0% in AP and 10.2% in F1. On the Ohn-Bar dataset, it reaches 64% AP and 53% F1, yielding a 23.1% AP improvement over Goal (52% AP). The model also achieves the highest accuracy (92% on TOI, 69% on Ohn-Bar) and the fastest inference speed (115 ms/clip) among all compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed model outperforms all seven baselines on both datasets across every reported metric. Against the strongest dedicated importance-estimation baseline, Goal (ICRA'19), it gains 10 AP points on TOI (60 vs. 50) and 12 AP points on Ohn-Bar (64 vs. 52), while also surpassing Goal's F1 by 5 points on TOI (54 vs. 49) and 27 points on Ohn-Bar (53 vs. 26). The bottom-up-only methods Zhang (ICRA'20) and Li (ICRA'22) perform poorly, with F1 scores of 0 on both datasets because they predict all objects as unimportant. Among the three salient-object-detection baselines (MENet, A2S, PGNet), PGNet is the strongest with 30 F1 on TOI and 27 F1 on Ohn-Bar, yet the proposed model still exceeds it by 24 and 26 F1 points respectively. Notably, the proposed model also runs faster (115 ms/clip) than all baselines except Ohn-Bar's original 2017 model (100 ms/clip), which itself achieves far lower accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the on-road object importance estimation task first formalized by Ohn-Bar et al. (2017), which relied on a small bottom-up spatio-temporal model, and subsequent single-fold top-down approaches (Goal/Gao 2019 using driver's goal, Li 2022 using ego-car trajectory). The key conceptual departure is the simultaneous integration of three distinct top-down guidance factors—driver intention, semantic context, and traffic rule—into a unified multi-pathway architecture, where the traffic rule component (modeled via an adaptive object-lane interaction mechanism) has no precedent in prior importance estimation work. Methodologically, the paper repurposes standard components (ResNet18, LSTM, multi-head cross-attention) but arranges them in a novel bottom-up/top-down fusion framework that no prior method has attempted at this level of multi-factor integration.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The TOI dataset (9,858 frames, 44,120 annotations) triples the scale of the only previously public benchmark, providing a much-needed foundation for training and evaluating more complex models in this underexplored area. The finding that traffic rule (via lane-object interaction) contributes ~30% AP improvement, and that naive object-lane interaction without adaptive weighting actually degrades performance, suggests that context-aware gating of auxiliary signals is critical rather than simply adding more features. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond three coarse intention types, how it performs with more diverse traffic rules (e.g., stop signs, right-of-way), and whether the reliance on external lane detection (CLRNet) and semantic segmentation (DeepLabv3) as fixed preprocessing limits robustness in degraded conditions.
