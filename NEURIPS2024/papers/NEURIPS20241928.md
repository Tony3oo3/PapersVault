## Problem

Existing jailbreak methods for text-to-image models rely on adversarial prompts that produce nonsensical, easily detectable text and can be quickly blocked by updated safety filters. This paper asks whether a simpler, harder-to-detect jailbreak is possible by exploiting the fact that safety filters cannot reject normal prompts and that image editing models lack safety mechanisms entirely.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241928/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single unsafe text prompt containing one or more sensitive words (e.g., 'pistol', 'gun') and produces a 512×512 RGB image that visually depicts the unsafe scene. The input prompt is first decomposed into a safe prompt (with the sensitive word replaced) for generation and a target text condition for inpainting. Evaluation is performed against commercial APIs (GPT-4, DALL-E 2) and open-source diffusion models with concept-removal defenses (SLD, ESD), using NudeNet and Q16 classifiers to verify the presence of unsafe content.

## Method

ColJailBreak is a three-stage pipeline that separates generation from editing to bypass safety filters. First, adaptive safe word substitution uses ChatGPT to build a pool of 10 shape-similar replacement words for each sensitive word, generating multiple candidate safe images via the target T2I model. Second, inpainting-driven injection uses CLIP Surgery to convert the substitution word into point prompts for SAM, producing a semantic segmentation mask around the substituted object, then applies Fooocus-Inpainting (guidance scale 7.5, 50 steps, strength 0.9999) with the original sensitive word as the text condition to replace that region with the unsafe object. Third, contrastive language-image-guided collaborative optimization scores each candidate unsafe image against the original unsafe prompt using CLIP ViT-B/32 cosine similarity, selecting the substitution that yields the most coherent and prompt-aligned result. The key insight is that the T2I model's safety filter only inspects the initial text prompt or the final image in isolation, while the intermediate inpainting step operates without any safety check, creating an exploitable gap.

## Results

On GPT-4 (external dataset), ColJailBreak achieves ASR of 58.82% (Violence), 65.71% (Harassment), 63.63% (Self-harm), and 72.00% (Nudity), with CLIP scores ranging from 0.2606 to 0.3500. On DALL-E 2 (external dataset), ASR reaches 58.82%, 62.86%, 63.63%, and 60.00% across the same categories. The most striking gap is on Nudity, where both baselines achieve 0% ASR while ColJailBreak reaches 60–72%, indicating that adversarial prompt methods completely fail to generate nudity content on these commercial models. On the authors' own dataset (w/Own), ColJailBreak further improves, reaching 80.00% ASR on Self-harm and 76.00% on Harassment for GPT-4.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ColJailBreak outperforms both baselines across nearly all categories and models. Against MMA-Diffusion [43], ColJailBreak gains 3–18 percentage points in ASR on GPT-4 (e.g., 65.71% vs. 51.42% on Harassment) and 8–11 points on DALL-E 2 (e.g., 62.86% vs. 51.42% on Harassment), while also achieving substantially higher CLIP scores (0.29–0.35 vs. 0.19–0.22), indicating better alignment between generated images and the original unsafe prompts. Against QF-Attack [49], the gap is even larger: on GPT-4, ColJailBreak exceeds QF-Attack by 15–36 ASR points (e.g., 65.71% vs. 30.00% on Harassment), and on DALL-E 2 by 11–32 points. The most decisive advantage is on Nudity, where both MMA-Diffusion and QF-Attack achieve 0% ASR on both commercial models, while ColJailBreak reaches 60–72%, demonstrating that adversarial prompt methods are entirely ineffective at bypassing nudity-specific safety filters. On the authors' curated dataset (w/Own), ColJailBreak further extends its lead, reaching 80.00% ASR on Self-harm (GPT-4) and 76.00% on Harassment (DALL-E 2), compared to 68.00% and 52.00% for MMA-Diffusion respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant line of T2I jailbreaking research (SneakyPrompt, MMA-Diffusion, QF-Attack, Ring-A-Bell), which focuses on crafting adversarial prompts to bypass text-based safety filters. Instead, it repurposes the well-studied image inpainting/editing literature (Inpainting Anything, Paint by Example, Fooocus-Inpainting) as the attack vehicle: first generating a safe image with a normal prompt, then editing a local region to inject unsafe content. The key conceptual departure is exploiting the asymmetry that generation models have safety filters while editing models do not, framing the two as a collaborative pipeline rather than treating them as independent systems.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper exposes a systemic architectural vulnerability: commercial T2I services protect the generation step but leave the editing step unguarded, creating a trivially exploitable gap. The practical implication is that any platform offering both generation and editing (or where users can chain a generated image into an external inpainting tool) is vulnerable without additional post-edit safety checks. Limitations include reduced efficiency and coherence for prompts with multiple sensitive words requiring sequential edits, and the evaluation is confined to two commercial models (GPT-4, DALL-E 2) with a relatively small dataset (100–105 prompts per category). An open question is whether adding safety filters to editing models would fully close this gap or merely shift the attack surface.
