## Problem

State-of-the-art dynamical systems reconstruction (DSR) methods rely on teacher forcing (TF) techniques like GTF to stabilize gradient propagation during training of recurrent latent models, but these require a (pseudo-)invertible decoder that maps the current latent state to the current observation. In neuroimaging, observations such as BOLD fMRI signals are convolved with the hemodynamic response function, so each measurement depends on a history of past latent states, rendering the decoder non-invertible and making existing TF-based DSR inapplicable to the most common neuroimaging modalities.

## Input / Output

The method takes preprocessed BOLD fMRI time series (e.g., 16 regions sampled at TR=1.4 s, yielding ~652 time points per subject in the LEMON dataset) along with nuisance regressors (movement, respiratory artifacts) as input. It produces a fully trained generative convSSM that runs autonomously to generate latent trajectories, from which dynamical system features—maximum Lyapunov exponents, attractor geometry (via state-space divergence D_stsp), and long-term temporal statistics (via power spectrum error D_PSE)—can be extracted. A key practical property is that the Wiener deconvolution of the HRF is computed once before training, so the per-epoch cost during SGD+GTF training depends only on the decoder matrix dimensions, not on the convolution filter length.

## Method

The convSSM combines a clipped shallow PLRNN (cshPLRNN) latent dynamics model with a convolutional observation model in which the HRF is convolved with the latent state before a linear regression to the observed BOLD signal. The central technical contribution is a new inversion algorithm for the non-invertible convolutional decoder: by exploiting the linearity of convolution and matrix multiplication, the authors separate the Wiener deconvolution step from the learnable regression matrices B and J, so that the deconvolved time series are computed once prior to training and the per-step control signal for GTF reduces to a simple pseudo-inverse multiplication (d_t = B⁺(x_t^deconv − J·r_t^deconv)). Training proceeds with standard SGD under Generalized Teacher Forcing, where the latent state at each step is interpolated between the model-generated state and this deconvolved control signal with a fixed mixing coefficient α. This design makes the per-epoch runtime approximately linear in latent dimension M, hidden dimension L, and observation dimension N, while being essentially independent of the HRF filter length (i.e., TR), enabling efficient scaling to large-scale multi-region fMRI reconstructions.

## Results

On the empirical LEMON fMRI dataset (51 subjects, 16 regions, T=652), convSSM achieves the best state space divergence (D_stsp = 2.73±1.09) and power spectrum error (D_PSE = 0.14±0.03) among all compared methods, with a 10-step prediction error of 1.78±0.38. On the Lorenz63 benchmark, convSSM significantly outperforms the standard SSM, with the performance gap widening as the hemodynamic convolution filter becomes more severe (e.g., at TR=0.2s, D_stsp drops from 2.97±0.75 for standard SSM to 0.29±0.47 for convSSM at σ=0.01). On the ALN simulation, convSSM significantly outperforms the standard SSM in latent space (rank-sum test Z=11.50, p≤.001), and DSR measures evaluated on short pseudo-empirical time series (T=500) strongly correlate with performance on long ground-truth test sets (T=5000). Additionally, convSSM reliably recovers the maximum Lyapunov exponent (λ_max) from short time series, and λ_max values differentiate between subjects (within-subject variance significantly lower than between-subject, T(50)=−11.53, p<.001).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the standard SSM (Hess et al., ICML 2023), convSSM achieves marginally better D_stsp (2.73 vs. 2.77) and D_PSE (0.14 vs. 0.15) on LEMON, but the advantage becomes dramatic on Lorenz63 under strong hemodynamic filtering: at TR=0.2s with σ=0.01, convSSM reduces D_stsp from 2.97 to 0.29 and D_PSE from 0.64 to 0.14, while the standard SSM converges in only 22/100 runs versus 92/100 for convSSM. Against MINDy (Singh et al., NeuroImage 2020), convSSM wins on all three metrics (D_stsp 2.73 vs. 6.79, D_PSE 0.14 vs. 0.27, 10-step PE 1.78 vs. 1.97). Against rSLDS (Linderman et al., NeurIPS 2020), convSSM achieves substantially lower D_stsp (2.73 vs. 15.51) and D_PSE (0.14 vs. 0.24), while matching on 10-step PE (1.78 vs. 1.78). Against LFADS (Pandarinath et al., Nature Methods 2018), convSSM wins on D_stsp (2.73 vs. 3.24) and D_PSE (0.14 vs. 0.43) and 10-step PE (1.78 vs. 2.45). The convSSM without GTF (α=0) degrades substantially (D_stsp 3.77, D_PSE 0.34), confirming that generalized teacher forcing is essential for the convolutional decoder setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the generalized teacher forcing (GTF) framework of Hess et al. (2023) for training state space models on chaotic dynamics to handle a critical practical limitation: non-invertible convolutional decoder models. Prior TF-based DSR methods (STF, GTF) require the observation model to be (pseudo-)invertible so that control signals can be projected into latent space, but real neuroimaging signals like BOLD are filtered versions of the underlying neural process, violating this assumption. The authors resolve this by exploiting the linearity of Wiener deconvolution to separate the deconvolution step from learnable parameters, enabling GTF to work with the hrf-based convolutional observation model originally formulated by Koppe et al. (2019). The latent dynamics are modeled with the clipped shallow PLRNN architecture from Durstewitz (2017) and its extensions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work removes a key barrier to deploying data-driven DSR on real fMRI data, where BOLD signals are inherently filtered and time series are short (~650 time points). The demonstration that established DSR performance measures evaluated on short pseudo-empirical series predict long-term dynamical properties (attractor geometry, Lyapunov exponents) is practically important for model selection in settings where ground truth is unavailable. The finding that fMRI resting-state signals predominantly exhibit chaotic dynamics with reliable inter-individual differences in λ_max opens avenues for using nonlinear DS features in clinical and cognitive assessment. Open questions remain around handling non-stationarity, combining data across subjects, and out-of-domain generalization of trained models.
