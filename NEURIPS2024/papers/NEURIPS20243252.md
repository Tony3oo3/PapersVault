## Problem

Exact probabilistic inference over integer-valued random variables is #P-hard, and existing neurosymbolic systems (ProbLog, Dice, DeepProbLog) resort to explicit enumeration of joint distributions, causing quadratic or worse scaling that limits them to toy problems. Additionally, the discrete nature of integers makes gradient-based learning difficult, preventing end-to-end training of neural components within probabilistic arithmetic expressions.

## Input / Output

The method consumes probability distributions over bounded integers, each represented as a vector of log-probabilities paired with an integer lower-bound offset, and composes them via linear arithmetic expressions (addition, negation, constant multiplication, integer division, modulo, and probabilistic branching). It outputs the full probability distribution of the resulting expression, exact expected values (including indicator expectations for comparisons), and, in the neurosymbolic setting, a Boolean validity label for MNIST addition or visual sudoku puzzles. All operations are closed: the output of any arithmetic operation is again a bounded probabilistic integer representable in the same tensor format.

## Method

The core insight is that the PMF of the sum of two random variables equals the convolution of their PMFs, which can be computed in O(N log N) via the fast Fourier transform rather than O(N²) via explicit joint construction. Distributions are represented as tensors of log-probabilities with an integer offset, and the 'fast log-conv-exp trick' achieves numerical stability by exploiting the linearity of the FFT to factor out max-log-probability scalars before exponentiating, avoiding underflow without per-iteration log-sum-exp calls. The framework PLIA_t formalizes all linear integer arithmetic operations (addition, negation, constant multiplication, integer division, modulo) as closed tensor manipulations, plus two exact inference primitives: expected value computation and probabilistic branching (if-then-else with probabilistic conditions). Because the entire pipeline is built from differentiable tensor operations in TensorFlow/PyTorch, gradient-based learning of neural classifiers (e.g., LeNet on MNIST digits) is obtained 'for free,' enabling end-to-end neurosymbolic training without approximation.

## Results

On exact probabilistic inference benchmarks, PLIA_t achieves a ~10^5 speedup over Dice (Cao et al., 2023) at domain size 2^15 (≈0.1 s CPU / ≈0.01 s GPU vs. ≈1000 s), and scales to domain size 2^24 within 100 s on CPU. On the probabilistic Luhn benchmark (sequence length 350), PLIA_t is ~10^2 faster than Dice. In neurosymbolic learning (Table 1), PLIA_t is the only method that completes MNIST addition at N=50 digits (43.0% accuracy, 11.98 min training) and visual sudoku at G=9 (63.0% accuracy, 6.39 min), while all baselines time out at these scales.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Dice (Cao et al., UAI 2023), the state-of-the-art exact inference method using binary decision diagrams, PLIA_t achieves a ~10^5 speedup at domain size 2^15 and a ~10^2 speedup on the probabilistic Luhn benchmark at sequence length 350, while also using less memory for larger problems. Against DeepProbLog (Manhaeve et al., NeurIPS 2018), PLIA_t matches or exceeds accuracy on all completed benchmarks (e.g., 95.88% vs. 94.20% on MNIST N=2) while being ~45× faster to train (1.97 min vs. 88.21 min); DPL times out on every problem beyond N=2. Against Scallop (Huang et al., NeurIPS 2021), PLIA_t achieves comparable or better accuracy (95.88% vs. 95.40% at N=2; 79.09% vs. T/O at N=15) with 11–9× shorter training times, and is the only method to complete N=50 and G=9. Against A-NeSI (van Krieken et al., NeurIPS 2023), PLIA_t wins on accuracy at N=15 (79.09% vs. 74.71%) and G=9 (63.00% vs. 61.50%), while training 91× faster at N=15 (7.85 min vs. 714.55 min) and 21× faster at G=9 (6.39 min vs. 134.86 min); A-NeSI slightly edges PLIA_t on MNIST N=4 (92.40% vs. 91.60%) and Sudoku G=4 (85.50% vs. 85.00%). Overall, PLIA_t is the only method that scales to the largest problem sizes in both benchmarks without timing out.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the convolution theorem and FFT from signal processing into the probabilistic programming and neurosymbolic AI setting, where prior work (ProbLog, Dice, Cao et al.) relied on explicit enumeration or knowledge compilation (BDDs) to compute distributions of sums of discrete random variables. The log-domain numerical stability trick is inspired by the log-einsum-exp trick for tractable probabilistic circuits, and the overall tensor-based formulation draws on standard deep learning library primitives (TensorFlow/PyTorch FFT, Einops). The key departure from prior neurosymbolic systems (DeepProbLog, Scallop, A-NeSI) is that differentiability and GPU parallelism come 'for free' from the tensor representation, rather than requiring custom gradient estimators or surrogate models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 10^5 inference speedup and 10^2 learning speedup make neurosymbolic combinatorial problems (e.g., 50-digit MNIST addition) tractable for the first time with exact inference, removing a major barrier to scaling beyond toy problems. The approach is hyperparameter-free and benefits automatically from ongoing hardware/FFT optimizations. However, the underlying problem remains #P-hard: domain sizes grow super-polynomially with expression depth, so the method still hits a wall for sufficiently complex expressions. The authors envision extending PLIA_t into a full neuroprobabilistic programming language, but the current scope is limited to linear integer arithmetic with a fixed set of operations.
