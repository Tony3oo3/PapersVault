## Problem

Existing multi-omic knowledge transfer methods (scJoint, scBridge, scNCL) assume abundant labeled scRNA-seq data to transfer cell types to scATAC-seq, but in practice annotating both modalities is prohibitively expensive. This paper tackles the underexplored and more realistic setting where both scRNA-seq and scATAC-seq data suffer from severe label scarcity, requiring simultaneous resolution of dual label scarcity and cross-omic heterogeneity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241292/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a small labeled scRNA-seq subset (1–10% of source data), a larger unlabeled scRNA-seq set, and an entirely unlabeled scATAC-seq set, all assumed to share the same closed-set of C cell types. A shared 2-layer MLP encoder (64-dim embeddings) and a 2-layer MLP classifier process both modalities; the output is per-cell softmax predictions over C cell types for scATAC-seq samples, plus aligned multi-omic embeddings. The OT-based pseudo-labeling operates on mini-batches of B unlabeled samples with a uniform class distribution constraint, and the cross-omic Mixup fuses B scRNA-seq hidden representations per scATAC-seq sample within a mini-batch.

## Method

DANCE addresses dual label scarcity through three coupled mechanisms. First, for source scRNA-seq data, it generates pseudo-labels via optimal transport: a Sinkhorn-regularized coupling matrix Q is solved under a uniform class-distribution constraint with cost matrix −log P, producing distribution-aware pseudo-labels that avoid the class-bias of argmax pseudo-labeling (Theorem 4.1 provides a CE-based quality guarantee). Second, for target scATAC-seq data, a divide-and-conquer strategy splits samples by confidence threshold τ=0.9: source-like samples receive consistency regularization (Gaussian perturbation + cross-entropy against pseudo-labels), while target-specific samples undergo progressive ambiguous-set learning where incorrect cell types are iteratively removed from a top-k candidate set with a decaying threshold μ, providing soft supervision (Theorem 4.2 shows soft labels dominate hard labels in estimation error under label noise). Third, cross-omic multi-sample Mixup computes class-wise similarity between scRNA-seq and scATAC-seq hidden representations using classifier weight vectors as prototypes, then fuses B scRNA-seq samples into a virtual sample via softmax-weighted averaging and aligns it to the corresponding scATAC-seq embedding via L2 loss—requiring neither pairwise data nor target labels.

## Results

DANCE achieves substantial improvements over all baselines across 11 multi-omic single-cell datasets at three label ratios (1%, 5%, 10%). Under the most challenging low-label setting (1%), DANCE reaches 50.23% on snRNA_10X_v3_A-ATAC, 75.72% on snRNA_10X_v3_A-snmC, 50.83% on snRNA_10X_v2-ATAC, and 60.12% on snRNA_10X_v2-snmC, compared to the best baselines (scBridge at 25.57%, scNCL at 27.16%, scBridge at 19.39%, and scNCL at 59.65% respectively). At the high-label setting (10%), DANCE reaches 80.12% on snRNA_10X_v3_A-snmC and 78.04% on snRNA_10X_v2-snmC, outperforming the strongest baseline scNCL (60.07% and 68.89%) by 20+ percentage points. The performance gap is largest under label scarcity, confirming DANCE's data efficiency.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DANCE outperforms all 11 baselines across every dataset and label ratio tested. The strongest competing methods are scNCL (neighborhood contrastive regularization, Bioinformatics 2023) and scBridge (heterogeneous integration, Nature Communications 2023), which are the only baselines that approach competitive performance on some datasets (e.g., scNCL reaches 68.89% on snRNA_10X_v2-snmC at high labels). Domain adaptation methods (DAN, CDAN, MCC) and the semi-supervised method FixMatch are consistently 20–50 percentage points behind DANCE, as they do not address the cross-omic heterogeneity gap. Multi-omic integration methods scJoint, scNCL, and scBridge all assume abundant labeled scRNA-seq data, so their performance degrades sharply under label scarcity (e.g., scBridge drops from 42.36% to 19.39% on snRNA_10X_v2-ATAC as labels decrease from high to low), whereas DANCE's performance remains relatively stable (54.79% → 50.83%), demonstrating its data efficiency. On the CITE-ASAP dataset (7 cell types), DANCE achieves 47.45% at high labels, outperforming the next-best method scNCL (73.39% on snRNA_SMARTer-snmC but only ~47% on CITE-ASAP). Additional baselines SLA, COT, Seurat, and Harmony (Table 11) are also consistently outperformed by 5–12 percentage points.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the semi-supervised learning paradigm (FixMatch-style confidence thresholding and consistency regularization) into the multi-omic single-cell domain, where prior methods like scJoint, scNCL, and scBridge all assume fully-labeled scRNA-seq source data. It departs from standard argmax pseudo-labeling by introducing an optimal transport (Sinkhorn) formulation that respects global class distributions, and from pairwise or centroid-based cross-modal alignment by proposing a label-free, sample-to-sample cross-omic multi-sample Mixup. The divide-and-conquer strategy for target data—splitting into source-like (consistency-regularized) and target-specific (ambiguous-set-learned) subsets—adapts the partial-label learning literature to the heterogeneous single-cell setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By addressing label scarcity in both source and target modalities simultaneously, DANCE directly reduces the annotation bottleneck that limits real-world single-cell studies, and the large empirical gains (often 20+ points over the best baseline under low-label settings) suggest the source-side label scarcity was a previously underappreciated bottleneck. The paper acknowledges limitations: it is restricted to closed-set transfer, static scenarios, and does not handle label noise or open-set cell types. Open questions include whether the OT-based pseudo-labeling and cross-omic Mixup generalize to dynamic applications (e.g., RNA velocity inference) and to settings with noisy or incomplete annotations.
