## Problem

ViTs poorly extrapolate to more patches at test time, preventing them from leveraging higher-resolution images without costly finetuning. The paper identifies position encoding as the primary cause: all existing methods (learnable embeddings, RoPE, ALiBi, Fourier features) require introducing new or modified position embeddings when the patch count changes, creating a distribution shift that disrupts the spatial algorithms attention heads have learned.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240621/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

LookHere is a drop-in replacement for position encoding in plain (attention-only, non-hierarchical) ViTs, consuming standard RGB images split into 16×16 non-overlapping patches. Models are trained at 224² px and tested at resolutions from 64² to 1024² px with no further training, producing class labels, patch-level segmentation maps via logit-lens projection onto the learned class embedding space, and interpretable attention maps. The precomputed LookHere matrices add only 0.016% FLOPs to a ViT-B/16 forward pass and introduce structured sparsity (up to 7/8 for 45° FOV) amenable to future kernel optimization.

## Method

LookHere replaces position embeddings with fixed 2D attention masks that assign each attention head a viewing direction and field of view (FOV), hard-blocking attention outside that FOV. Within a head's FOV, attention scores are penalized by the Euclidean distance between query and key patches, scaled by a head- and layer-specific slope function; the precomputed LookHere matrix is subtracted element-wise from the learned attention matrix before softmax, and no position embeddings are added to patch embeddings. Three variants are proposed—LH-180, LH-90, and LH-45—where for ViT-B's 12 heads, 8 are directed across the four cardinal and four intercardinal directions while 4 remain undirected. The key insight is that by hard-coding both directions and relative distances via masks, the model does not need to learn its own spatial algorithms that would break when new position embeddings are introduced during extrapolation, thereby limiting the distribution shift attention heads face when the grid size changes.

## Results

On ImageNet (trained and tested at 224²), LH-180 achieves 81.31% top-1 accuracy on Val (avg 80.01% over 8 runs), outperforming the strongest baseline 2D-RoPE (80.38% / 78.37%) by ~1% best and ~1.6% average. Against natural adversarial examples (ImageNet-A), LookHere variants improve top-1 by an average of 5.4% over 2D-RoPE (e.g., LH-180: 13.53% vs 13.03% best; 10.45% vs 8.84% avg). With extrapolation from 224² to 1024², LH-45 achieves 78.13% top-1 on Val versus 71.28% for 2D-RoPE — a 21.7% relative improvement — while all other baselines degrade more severely (e.g., 1D-learn drops to 55.67%, RPE-learn to 36.80%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LookHere (all three variants) outperforms all seven baselines on nearly every test condition at the training resolution. Against the strongest baseline, 2D-RoPE (CroCo v2), LH-180 gains +0.93% on Val and +1.36% on v2 (best runs), widening to +1.64% and +1.96% on average runs, indicating reduced hyperparameter sensitivity. On adversarial robustness (ImageNet-A), LookHere variants lead 2D-RoPE by ~0.5% best and ~1.6% average top-1, and by ~5.4% on average across all baselines. On the new ImageNet-HR benchmark, LH-180 achieves 89.86% vs 88.78% for 2D-RoPE. The gap widens dramatically under extrapolation: at 1024², LH-45 reaches 78.13% on Val while 2D-RoPE drops to 71.28% and 1D-learn to 55.67%, confirming that LookHere's directional masks limit the distribution shift faced by attention heads. The only exception where a baseline matches or slightly beats LookHere is the best 2D-RoPE run on ImageNet-R (32.45% vs 32.10% for LH-180), though LookHere still leads on average.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself within the ViT position encoding literature, which has recently been enriched by NLP-inspired methods like 2D-RoPE and 2D-ALiBi that inject positional information into attention layers. It departs from this lineage by replacing learned or fixed position embeddings with hand-designed 2D directional attention masks that assign each head a fixed field of view, drawing conceptual inspiration from 1D causal masks in autoregressive decoders. The work is motivationally aligned with FlexiViT and NaViT in improving the resolution flexibility of plain ViTs, but achieves this through a fundamentally different mechanism: hard-coding spatial structure rather than learning it. The distance-based bias within each FOV echoes ALiBi's linear penalty, but is combined with the novel directional masking to create a structured, interpretable inductive bias.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 21.7% extrapolation gap over 2D-RoPE at 1024² px, backed by 8-run hyperparameter sweeps across six test sets, makes this a practically relevant drop-in improvement for deploying ViTs at higher resolutions without finetuning. The introduction of ImageNet-HR (5k natively 1024² px images) addresses a real evaluation gap, and the finding that smaller objects benefit most from extrapolation has direct implications for fine-grained recognition. However, the method is limited to ViT-B/16 on ImageNet, relies on hand-designed masks that may not transfer to other architectures or tasks, and the structured sparsity (up to 7/8 for 45° FOV) that could yield significant speedups remains unexploited pending custom kernels. Whether the approach scales to larger models or extends to video and 3D point clouds remains open.
