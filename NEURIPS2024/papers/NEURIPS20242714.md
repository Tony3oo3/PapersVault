## Problem

While non-active exploration (i.i.d. random inputs) suffices for identifying linear dynamical systems, a counter-example based on piecewise-affine features shows it fails for general linearly parameterized nonlinear systems. The paper addresses the gap between bilinear systems (where non-active exploration works) and non-smooth systems (where it does not) by asking: under what smoothness conditions on the feature functions can non-active exploration still yield provable identification guarantees?

## Input / Output

The method consumes a single trajectory of states x_t and control inputs u_t (either i.i.d. or generated by a real-analytic policy plus i.i.d. perturbation), along with a known vector of real-analytic feature functions φ(x_t, u_t). It produces a point estimate θ̂_T via least-squares minimization and, separately, a convex uncertainty set Θ_T via set-membership estimation. The system must satisfy local input-to-state stability with bounded disturbances and inputs, and the feature functions must be linearly independent and real-analytic on R^{n_x+n_u}.

## Method

The core technical contribution is establishing the block-martingale-small-ball (BMSB) condition for general real-analytic feature functions, which is the key probabilistic persistent excitation requirement for non-asymptotic convergence. The proof leverages the fact that a non-trivial real-analytic function cannot vanish on a positive-measure set, so any linear combination of linearly independent analytic features is non-zero almost everywhere; combined with the Paley-Zygmund-Petrov inequality, this yields a uniform lower bound on the probability that the feature process avoids a small ball in any direction. This BMSB result is then plugged into the LSE meta-theorem of Simchowitz et al. (2018) to obtain a 1/√T convergence rate for the parameter estimate, and into the SME meta-theorem of Li et al. (2024) to obtain a 1/T diameter bound on the uncertainty set. The semi-continuity assumption on the noise distributions (no concentration on Lebesgue-null sets) is what connects the analytic zero-set property to a non-vanishing probability, and the results extend from open-loop i.i.d. inputs to closed-loop policies with i.i.d. perturbations.

## Results

The paper establishes that LSE achieves a non-asymptotic convergence rate of O(1/√T) for linearly parameterized nonlinear systems with real-analytic feature functions under i.i.d. non-active exploration inputs, matching the optimal rate for linear systems. For set-membership estimation (SME), the diameter of the uncertainty set converges at O(1/T) when the disturbance distribution satisfies a linear boundary-visiting condition (e.g., uniform or truncated-Gaussian). Numerical experiments on a 2-parameter pendulum and a 7-parameter quadrotor system confirm both rates: log-log plots show empirical LSE error scaling as O(1/√T) and SME diameter scaling as O(1/T), with the true parameters contained within the estimated uncertainty sets across all trajectory lengths tested.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not benchmark against competing estimation methods in a head-to-head tabular comparison; its contribution is theoretical rather than algorithmic. The key positioning is relative to two prior results: (1) the bilinear system result of Sattar et al. (2022), which showed BMSB holds for bilinear features under i.i.d. inputs, and (2) the piecewise-affine counter-example of Mania et al. (2022), which showed non-active exploration is fundamentally insufficient for non-smooth (e.g., ReLU) feature functions. This paper bridges the gap by proving that real-analytic feature functions (polynomials, trigonometric functions) are sufficient for BMSB, thereby identifying differentiability as the critical property separating systems where non-active exploration works from those where it does not. Numerically, the theoretical bounds from Theorem 2 (LSE) and Corollary 2 (SME) are validated against empirical performance on both the pendulum and quadrotor, with the empirical rates matching the predicted O(1/√T) and O(1/T) scalings respectively under both uniform and truncated-Gaussian noise distributions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the BMSB (block-martingale small-ball) framework from Simchowitz et al. (2018) for linear systems and Sattar et al. (2022) for bilinear systems to the much broader class of real-analytic feature functions, using the Paley-Zygmund Petrov inequality to establish probabilistic persistent excitation. It also leverages the SME meta-theorem from Li et al. (2024) to derive non-asymptotic diameter bounds for set-membership estimation in the nonlinear setting. Conceptually, the paper identifies real-analyticity as the precise smoothness threshold separating systems where non-active (i.i.d.) exploration suffices from the piecewise-affine counter-example of Mania et al. (2022) where it provably fails.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result provides the first non-asymptotic convergence guarantees for LSE and SME under non-active exploration for a broad and physically relevant class of nonlinear systems (polynomials, trigonometric functions), which covers most robotics, power systems, and mechanical applications. Key limitations include the non-constructive nature of the BMSB constants (s_φ, p_φ), the requirement of semi-continuous (non-atomic) noise distributions, and the reliance on local input-to-state stability. Open questions include deriving explicit dimension-dependent formulas for the BMSB constants, extending to discrete or adversarial disturbances, handling imperfect state observations, and analyzing the volume (rather than just diameter) of SME uncertainty sets.
