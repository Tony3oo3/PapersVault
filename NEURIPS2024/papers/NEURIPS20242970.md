## Problem

Vanilla pixel-level classifiers for semantic segmentation (a 1×1 convolution computing inner products between fixed prototypes and pixel features) suffer from two fundamental limitations: feature deviation in the semantic domain, where fixed prototypes trained on the training set become misaligned with test-image pixel features due to large intra-class variance, and information loss in the spatial domain, where the classifier ignores the structured spatial layout of objects, leading to blurred boundaries and poor small-target recognition.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image, extracts features via any backbone and decode head to produce a feature map S_f ∈ R^{H×W×D}, and outputs a pixel-level segmentation mask O_c ∈ R^{H×W×K} where K is the number of categories. SSA-Seg is designed as a drop-in replacement for the final 1×1 convolution classifier, adding only a depth-wise convolution and a few 1×1 convolutions, so it integrates with existing CNN or ViT backbones without modifying the backbone or segmentation head. A teacher classifier branch is active only during training and is removed at inference, so deployment cost is essentially unchanged.

## Method

SSA-Seg retains the original 1×1 convolution to produce a coarse mask M_c, which then guides two parallel adaptation branches. In the semantic branch (SEPA), the coarse mask is used to compute a per-class semantic domain center S_c by weighted averaging of pixel features, which is concatenated with the fixed prototype S and fused via a 1×1 convolution to yield an adaptive semantic prototype S_p that shifts toward the test image's feature distribution. In the spatial branch (SPPA), conditional positional encoding (CPVT) generates spatial features P_f, and a softmax over spatial dimensions of the coarse mask produces a spatial domain center P_c that captures the relative spatial distribution of each class; this is fused with a learned position basis P to form the spatial prototype P_p. Classification is then performed as O_c = (S_f ⊕ P_f) ⊗ (S_p ⊕ P_p)^T, jointly considering semantic and spatial similarity. To make the prototype offsets controllable, an online multi-domain distillation strategy introduces a teacher classifier that replaces the coarse mask with the ground-truth mask, and distills the student via three losses: a boundary-aware and category-aware response domain loss, a semantic domain loss that penalizes inter-class similarity mismatches, and a spatial domain loss that aligns spatial prototypes.

## Results

SSA-Seg consistently improves pixel-level segmentation baselines across three benchmarks with negligible overhead (≤0.1G FLOPs, ≤1ms latency). The headline gains are: SeaFormer-L reaches 45.36% mIoU on ADE20K (+3.00%), SegNeXt-T reaches 38.91% on COCO-Stuff-10K (+2.52%) and 52.58% on PASCAL-Context (+2.23%), and OCRNet-HRNet-W48 reaches 47.47% on ADE20K (+4.17%). These results establish state-of-the-art lightweight segmentation performance, with SeaFormer-L+SSA-Seg outperforming heavier mask-classification models like MaskFormer (44.5%) at a fraction of the compute.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against other adaptive classifiers (Table 2), SSA-Seg dominates: on OCRNet+HRNetV2-W48 it achieves 47.5% mIoU vs. CAC's 45.7% and GMMSeg's 44.8%; on SegNeXt-T it reaches 43.9% vs. CAC's 43.0%; and on SeaFormer-B it gains +2.5% while CAC yields only +0.1%. Against mask-classification models (Table 3), CGRSeg-L+SSA-Seg reaches 49.0% mIoU on ADE20K, surpassing Mask2Former (47.2%) with 3× fewer FLOPs (14.8G vs. 70.1G) and lower latency (42.6ms vs. 55.2ms). Among general pixel-level baselines in Table 1, the largest absolute gains appear on OCRNet (+4.17%) and UperNet-Swin-T (+3.42%), while even the strongest baseline (ViT-Adapter-L at 54.40%) still benefits by +0.99%. The paper also verifies that the improvements are not merely from added parameters: a control experiment (Baseline+Conv with matched model size) gains only +0.54% versus SSA-Seg's +3.00% on SeaFormer-L.

## Relation to Prior Work

SSA-Seg extends the standard 1×1 convolution pixel-level classifier inherited from FCN by making the fixed semantic prototypes adaptive per test image, building directly on the dynamic classifier idea of CAC (Tian et al., 2023) but adding explicit constraints via online multi-domain distillation to prevent uncontrolled prototype drift. It departs from prior prototype-based classifiers (ProtoSeg, GMMSeg, DNC) by introducing a spatial prototype adaptation branch that leverages CPVT conditional positional encodings to model object structure, a dimension entirely absent from earlier pixel-level classifiers. The online distillation strategy (response, semantic, and spatial domain losses) is novel in its use of ground-truth-guided teacher branches during training, distinguishing it from both offline distillation and CAC's unconstrained contextual adaptation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for real-time and mobile segmentation: SSA-Seg adds only ~0.1G FLOPs and <1ms latency while boosting lightweight models like SeaFormer-L and SegNeXt-T to state-of-the-art mIoU on ADE20K and COCO-Stuff-10K, making it a drop-in upgrade for deployment-constrained settings. A key limitation is that SSA-Seg is incompatible with mask-level classification paradigms (MaskFormer, Mask2Former, SegViT), restricting its applicability to the pixel-level classification family. An open question is whether the spatial prototype adaptation concept could be adapted to mask-level or query-based classifiers, and whether the ground-truth-guided online distillation could be replaced by self-supervised signals to reduce training complexity.
