## Problem

Existing pre-trained time-series models (e.g., MOMENT, UniTS) achieve strong generalization but remain black boxes with no human-understandable representations, while shapelet-based interpretable models are dataset-specific and fail to transfer across domains. This paper asks whether a single pre-trained model can produce both generalizable and interpretable tokens for time-series data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242928/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes univariate time series interpolated to a fixed length of 512, which are split into 64 non-overlapping patches of length 8. For each patch, it outputs an attribute tuple consisting of a discrete shape code (8-dimensional, drawn from a 512-entry codebook), a scalar offset, a scalar scale, a relative start position, and a relative duration. For downstream classification, two representation formats are available: the full token matrix (64 × 12) or a code histogram (a 512- or 64-dimensional frequency vector), both fed into a linear classifier to produce class labels.

## Method

VQShape uses a patch-based transformer encoder (8-layer, 512-dim embeddings) to map a univariate time series into 64 latent embeddings, each decoded via MLP heads into an attribute tuple (shape code, offset, scale, position, duration). The shape component is quantized against a learned codebook of 512 low-dimensional (8-dim) vectors using standard VQ-VAE nearest-neighbor lookup, creating a bottleneck that forces each code to encode only shape-level information. Training is self-supervised with four losses: full time-series reconstruction, subsequence shape reconstruction (comparing the decoded shape against the actual subsequence after normalization), a VQ loss augmented with entropy terms to prevent codebook collapse, and a diversity regularization that maps (position, length) pairs through a log-scaled polar transform to encourage the model to capture shape information at varied scales and locations. The key design insight is that by decomposing each subsequence into an abstracted shape plus continuous attributes (offset, scale, position, duration), the discrete codebook becomes a universal vocabulary of shapes that generalizes across domains, while the continuous attributes handle domain-specific variations in magnitude and timing.

## Results

On 29 UEA multivariate time-series classification datasets, VQShape achieves a mean accuracy of 72.3% and median accuracy of 81.0% using frozen pre-trained representations with a linear classifier, the highest mean accuracy among all 14 compared methods. VQShape wins top-1 on 6 datasets (tied with TS2Vec) and top-3 on 9 datasets. In the frozen pre-trained comparison (Table 2), VQShape pre-trained on only 9 datasets still achieves 72.3% mean accuracy, outperforming MOMENT (69.7%) and UniTS (55.9%) trained on the same 9 datasets, demonstrating cross-domain generalizability.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VQShape achieves the highest mean accuracy (72.3%) among all 14 methods, edging out T-Rep (71.9%), TS2Vec (71.2%), and TimesNet (71.0%), though none of these differences are statistically significant (Wilcoxon p > 0.5). VQShape significantly outperforms the other pre-trained models MOMENT (68.6%, p=0.000) and UniTS (62.9%, p=0.000), as well as all supervised and classical baselines. In the controlled frozen-representation comparison (Table 2), where all three pre-trained models are retrained on identical UEA data, VQShape achieves 72.3% mean accuracy vs. MOMENT's 69.7% and UniTS's 58.1%, winning top-1 on 16 of 29 datasets. Notably, VQShape pre-trained on only 9 datasets still reaches 72.3% mean accuracy (vs. MOMENT's 69.7% and UniTS's 55.9% on the same 9 datasets), indicating that its codebook of abstracted shapes generalizes to unseen domains. VQShape's key advantage over the statistically comparable black-box methods (TimesNet, T-Rep, TS2Vec) is interpretability: its token representations decompose each time series into named abstracted shapes with position, scale, and duration attributes, enabling rule-based explanations for classification decisions.

## Relation to Prior Work

VQShape sits at the intersection of three research threads: VQ-VAE-style discrete representation learning (van den Oord et al., 2017), patch-based transformer encoders for time series (PatchTST, MOMENT), and the classical shapelet literature (Ye & Keogh, 2011). The key departure from prior work is twofold: unlike TOTEM, which applies VQ-VAE to time series but yields opaque latent codes, VQShape explicitly decomposes each token into human-readable attributes (abstracted shape, offset, scale, position, length); and unlike shapelet-based methods, which learn dataset-specific, fixed-length subsequences, VQShape learns a universal, domain-agnostic codebook of abstracted shapes via self-supervised pre-training. The paper also draws on Concept Bottleneck Models from computer vision to motivate the code histogram representation as an interpretable, rule-like feature for classification.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The main practical contribution is a pre-trained model that produces interpretable time-series tokens without sacrificing classification accuracy relative to black-box foundation models like MOMENT and TS2Vec, offering a path toward transparent time-series analysis in safety-critical domains. However, the work is limited to classification on the UEA benchmark (29 datasets), and the authors note that certain data types (e.g., high-frequency sinusoidal signals in BasicMotions) can 'pollute' pre-training, suggesting that scaling to truly large-scale, diverse corpora will require careful data curation. Open questions include extending the interpretable token framework to forecasting, imputation, and anomaly detection, developing dynamic codebook sizing during training, and validating generalizability beyond the UEA archive to industrial or clinical time-series at scale.
