## Problem

Existing empirical evidence for public pretraining aiding differentially private training relies on in-distribution tasks (e.g., ImageNet→CIFAR-10), where zero-shot performance is already high and the need for private transfer is questionable. Tramèr et al. raised the concern that realistic private tasks—medical imaging, legal documents—involve extreme distribution shift where public features give near-random zero-shot accuracy, making it unclear whether public pretraining provides any benefit at all. This paper asks whether public representations can still improve private training sample complexity under such extreme concept shift.

## Input / Output

The method consumes two data streams: n₁ public samples from multiple diverse tasks (used only to estimate a shared low-rank subspace) and n₂ private samples from a single unseen task (used for the DP-protected regression). Empirically, inputs are RGB images (224×224 patches for PCam, satellite tiles for fMoW/RESISC45) processed by a frozen CLIP ViT-B/32 encoder producing 512-dimensional features; outputs are class labels (binary for PCam, 62-class for fMoW, 45-class for RESISC45). The private learner outputs a weight vector in the estimated k-dimensional subspace, which is then mapped back to the full d-dimensional space via the subspace basis.

## Method

The core algorithm is a two-phase procedure: (1) estimate a shared low-rank subspace B ∈ R^{d×k} from public data using the method-of-moments estimator from Tripuraneni et al., and (2) project private inputs into the estimated subspace and run DP-SGD for linear regression in k dimensions rather than d. The key theoretical insight is that the sample complexity of private learning scales with the subspace rank k (not the ambient dimension d), with an additive γ² bias term from subspace estimation error, even when the public and private tasks share no distributional similarity beyond the subspace. A matching lower bound via tracing attacks proves this rate is optimal among all two-stage algorithms that search within a fixed subspace estimate. Empirically, the paper validates this with CLIP ViT-B/32 features on three out-of-distribution datasets, showing linear probing with public features outperforms private training from scratch by up to 67% and consistently beats full finetuning under differential privacy.

## Results

Across three out-of-distribution datasets (PCam, fMoW, RESISC45), private linear probing on pretrained CLIP ViT-B/32 features improves accuracy by up to 67% over private training from scratch. Zero-shot CLIP achieves near-random accuracy on PCam (51.2%) and fMoW (1.64%), confirming extreme distribution shift, yet private linear probing recovers close to non-private performance (83.5% on PCam, 42.1% on fMoW, 91.7% on RESISC45). Linear probing consistently outperforms full finetuning under differential privacy, with a gap exceeding 50% on RESISC45 at all ε levels.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares four training paradigms under differential privacy: (1) private training from scratch, (2) private full finetuning of pretrained features, (3) private linear probing on frozen pretrained features, and (4) zero-shot CLIP (no private data). Private linear probing dominates private training from scratch on all three datasets, with the largest gap on fMoW where from-scratch training yields only ~10% accuracy at small ε while linear probing approaches the non-private 42.1%. Linear probing also consistently beats private full finetuning, by over 50% on RESISC45 at all ε values, corroborating theoretical results from Ke et al. [19] that linear probing is more sample-efficient under DP. Zero-shot CLIP is effectively useless on PCam (51.2%, binary) and fMoW (1.64%, 62-class), demonstrating that the distribution shift is too large for 'perfect privacy' without any private finetuning. The non-private reference (Table 1) shows that the same public features achieve 83.5% (PCam), 58.2% (fMoW), and 93.6% (RESISC45) with full finetuning, confirming that the private linear probing results are close to the non-private ceiling.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the nonprivate meta-learning framework of Tripuraneni et al. [28]—which studies transfer learning via a shared low-rank linear subspace estimated by method-of-moments—to the differentially private setting, where the subspace is estimated from public data and private linear regression is performed within it. It directly addresses concerns raised by Tramèr et al. [14] that prior empirical work on public pretraining for private learning [6–13] only evaluated in-distribution tasks (e.g., ImageNet→CIFAR-10), where zero-shot performance already makes transfer learning less relevant. The paper also builds on private linear regression bounds from Varshney et al. [57] and Cai et al. [58], and aligns with Ke et al. [19] in showing that linear probing outperforms full finetuning under DP, but departs from PILLAR [40] by requiring no in-distribution public data for subspace estimation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper resolves a key open question in the DP learning community: whether public pretraining provides meaningful benefits for genuinely sensitive tasks that are poorly represented in public data. The empirical finding of up to 67% accuracy improvement over private training from scratch under extreme concept shift (where zero-shot CLIP is at chance level) is practically significant for domains like medical imaging and remote sensing. The main limitation is that the theoretical model is restricted to linear regression with a shared low-rank subspace, which is a substantial simplification of the non-linear neural network representations used in practice; whether the sample complexity benefits generalize to deep non-linear features remains open. The finding that linear probing consistently outperforms full finetuning under DP also has practical implications for reducing compute requirements in private training pipelines.
