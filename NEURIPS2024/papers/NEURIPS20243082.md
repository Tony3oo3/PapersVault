## Problem

Offline visual RL suffers from two compounding difficulties: representation overfitting when extracting latent states from limited high-dimensional image data, and value overestimation that existing conservative methods address by overly penalizing out-of-distribution values, thereby suppressing exploration of potentially advantageous states. The paper asks whether accessible online simulators for related (not necessarily identical) tasks can serve as a 'test bed' to provide flexible, mildly conservative value constraints without the rigidity of purely offline approaches.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 64×64 RGB images as visual observations, a fixed offline trajectory dataset B^(T) for the target domain, and an interactable online simulator B^(S) for the source domain. Notably, the source and target domains may differ in action space dimensionality (e.g., 4D vs. 5D), reward scale, camera viewpoint, and physical dynamics, yet CoWorld handles these discrepancies through explicit alignment rather than requiring a shared representation. The output is a continuous control policy π_ψ that maps latent states to actions, trained entirely in the offline target domain but guided by the online source critic.

## Method

CoWorld follows an offline-online-offline transfer paradigm with three iterative stages. Stage A (state alignment) trains the target world model M_φ by minimizing a KL divergence between softmax-encoded observations from the source and target encoders, preventing the target encoder from overfitting limited offline data. Stage B (reward alignment) trains the source reward predictor on mixed data from both domains, relabeling target rewards as a convex combination of source predictions and true target rewards (controlled by factor k), so the source agent becomes 'target-informed.' Stage C (value constraint) introduces a min-max regularization term in the target critic loss: α·max(v_ξ(ẑ_t), sg(v_ξ'(ẑ_t))), which only penalizes the target critic when it overestimates relative to the source critic, leaving high-value estimates untouched to preserve exploration. The source and target maintain separate world models, actors, and critics with domain-specific parameters, and the source critic's gradient is stopped during the constraint to prevent mutual interference.

## Results

On Meta-World cross-task transfer (6 tasks), CoWorld (Best-Source) achieves an average return of 4241, outperforming the strongest baseline DV2 Finetune (3781) by 12.2% and Offline DV2 (2730) by 55.3%. On DeepMind Control Suite cross-dynamics transfer (6 tasks), CoWorld achieves an average return of 488, outperforming Offline DV2 by 169.6% and DrQ+BC by 37.5%. In the cross-environment Meta-World→RoboDesk setting with significant domain gaps (different robot arms, action spaces, reward scales, and camera views), CoWorld consistently outperforms both Offline DV2 and DV2 Finetune by large margins across all four RoboDesk tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoWorld outperforms all six compared baselines across all three benchmark suites. Against Offline DV2 (Lu et al., TMLR 2023), the closest model-based offline baseline, CoWorld achieves 55.3% higher average return on Meta-World (4241 vs. 2730) and 169.6% higher on DMC (488 vs. 181). Against DV2 Finetune (the strongest transfer baseline), CoWorld gains 12.2% on Meta-World and 17.0% on DMC, with the gap widening substantially in the cross-environment Meta-World→RoboDesk setting where DV2 Finetune degrades due to domain mismatch. Model-free baselines DrQ+BC, CQL, and CURL (Laskin et al., ICML 2020) all trail CoWorld by large margins (e.g., 626, 927, 641 vs. 4241 on Meta-World). LOMPO (Rafailov et al., PMLR 2021) achieves 1712 on Meta-World but suffers negative transfer when finetuned (792), while CoWorld's multi-source variant (4094) approaches best-source performance without manual task selection. Against the R3M foundation model (Nair et al., arXiv 2022), CoWorld achieves 3889 vs. 1609 (expert data) and 311 (medium-replay data) on Button Press Topdown, demonstrating that R3M's generalizable representations do not address the value overestimation problem inherent to offline RL.

## Relation to Prior Work

CoWorld builds directly on the DreamerV2 world model architecture (RSSM-based actor-critic) and extends it with a cross-domain transfer framework. It departs from the dominant offline-to-online transfer paradigm (pretrain offline, finetune online) by inverting the direction: an online source agent is trained first and then used as a 'test bed' to constrain the offline target policy. The min-max value constraint in Eq. (6) is a novel loss formulation that replaces the hard conservative penalties of CQL-style methods with a flexible, source-critic-guided regularization, while the state and reward alignment losses (Eqs. 3–5) address cross-domain discrepancies that prior finetuning approaches (DV2 Finetune, LOMPO Finetune) handle only implicitly.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper tackles two core bottlenecks in offline visual RL—representation overfitting and value overestimation—simultaneously, and does so by leveraging a practically available resource (existing RL simulators) rather than requiring new data collection. The consistent large margins across Meta-World, RoboDesk, and DMC, including cross-environment settings with different robots, action spaces, and reward scales, demonstrate robustness to domain gaps. The main limitation is increased training compute (~60% more wall-clock time than Offline DV2) and the requirement that a related online simulator be accessible, which may not hold in all real-world deployment scenarios. Open questions include whether the approach extends to truly novel tasks with no close simulator analogue, and whether the multi-source adaptive selection generalizes beyond the tested task families.
