## Problem

All existing color video snapshot compressive imaging (SCI) reconstruction algorithms are designed for the traditional RGGB Bayer pattern, yet mainstream smartphone cameras now use quad-Bayer CFA where each pixel is expanded into a 2×2 block of same-color sub-pixels. Applying Bayer-based methods to quad-Bayer measurements produces color distortion and ineffective demosaicing, and existing end-to-end approaches (transformer or CNN-transformer) demand prohibitive computational resources for mobile deployment. This paper introduces the first quad-Bayer-based video SCI reconstruction method and the first application of Mamba (state space models) to this task.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single 2D quad-Bayer patterned measurement Y ∈ R^(H×W) (a compressed snapshot encoding T sequential frames) and the corresponding coded-aperture masks M ∈ R^(H×W×T) as input, and outputs a full RGB color video X_out ∈ R^(H×W×3×T). Unlike traditional Bayer, the quad-Bayer spatial layout groups four same-color sub-pixels into 2×2 blocks, so the raw measurement is a single-channel image whose spatial structure encodes color information. Experiments use 512×512×3×32 middle-scale videos (compression ratio B=8) and 1080×1920×3×T large-scale videos, with training on DAVIS2017 at 480p resolution.

## Method

MambaSCI is a non-symmetric U-shaped encoder-decoder whose core building block is the Residual-Mamba-Block, which residually connects three modules with learnable scale parameters: (i) STMamba, which performs parallel forward-backward spatial scanning and forward temporal scanning via structured SSMs to capture long-range spatial-temporal dependencies at linear complexity; (ii) an Edge-Detail-Reconstruction (EDR) module combining linear projection with depthwise separable convolution for multi-scale local-global feature fusion that restores edge details lost to compression; and (iii) a Channel Attention (CA) module that compensates for the lack of inter-channel interaction in Mamba by weighting features based on learned channel importance. The encoder stacks three stages of Residual-Mamba-Block + Max-Pooling, the bottleneck concatenates additional Residual-Mamba-Blocks at fixed resolution, and the decoder uses residual convolutions with upsampling to reduce parameters. A final three-layer 3D convolution block (3×3×3, 3×3×3, 1×1×1) directly produces RGB output, bypassing explicit demosaicing. Three variants (Tiny/Small/Base with 8/10/16 channels) achieve 1.61–6.11M parameters and 165–557G FLOPS, substantially lower than transformer-based SOTA while matching or exceeding their PSNR.

## Results

On six mid-scale simulation color videos (512×512×3×32), MambaSCI-B achieves an average PSNR of 35.70 dB and SSIM of 0.959, outperforming the strongest E2E baseline STFormer-B (32.48 dB / 0.842) by 3.22 dB while using only 31% of the parameters (6.11M vs 19.49M) and 4.5% of the FLOPS (556.89G vs 12155.47G). The smaller MambaSCI-S variant (2.47M params, 247.53G FLOPS) reaches 34.53 dB / 0.950 SSIM, surpassing STFormer-S (27.06 dB) and EfficientSCI-S (26.86 dB) by over 7 dB. On four large-scale videos (1080×1920), MambaSCI-S achieves 36.31 dB on Football and 29.78 dB on Swinger, exceeding PnP-FastDVDnet by 1.55 dB and 1.18 dB respectively, while completing reconstruction of all four videos in 4.98 minutes versus 50.03 minutes for PnP-FastDVDnet.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MambaSCI outperforms all five baselines across every dataset and metric. Against the model-based GAP-TV [13], MambaSCI-B gains 7.17 dB average PSNR (35.70 vs 28.53), with GAP-TV exhibiting severe artifacts and a particularly poor 19.64 dB on Traffic. The PnP baselines PnP-FFDnet [14] (27.86 dB) and PnP-FastDVD [15] (31.35 dB) are both surpassed by even the smallest MambaSCI-T variant (32.13 dB), and the PnP methods show visible artifacts in visual comparisons. Among E2E methods, MambaSCI-B beats STFormer-B [17] by 3.22 dB PSNR and 0.117 SSIM while using only 31% of the parameters and 4.5% of the FLOPS; it also beats EfficientSCI-B [16] by 3.35 dB with 69% of the parameters and 9.8% of the FLOPS. At the small-model scale, MambaSCI-S (34.53 dB) surpasses STFormer-S (27.06 dB) and EfficientSCI-S (26.86 dB) by 7.47 and 7.67 dB respectively, indicating that the quad-Bayer-compatible Mamba-UNet design is substantially more effective than retraining existing Bayer-pattern E2E networks. On large-scale videos, MambaSCI-S leads PnP-FastDVDnet by 1.55 dB on Football and 1.18 dB on Swinger, while STFormer-S and EfficientSCI-S suffer severe color distortions and PSNR drops below 21 dB on several sequences.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the video snapshot compressive imaging (SCI) reconstruction task from the traditional Bayer CFA to the quad-Bayer pattern now standard in mainstream smartphone cameras, and introduces Mamba (a selective state space model) as the first SSM-based architecture for this task. Architecturally, it builds on the U-Net encoder-decoder framework and the STMamba module from ViVim for spatial-temporal scanning, but departs from prior E2E SCI methods (STFormer, EfficientSCI) that rely on transformer or CNN-transformer hybrids with quadratic complexity. The custom Residual-Mamba-Block, which fuses STMamba with an Edge-Detail-Reconstruction module and Channel Attention via learnable-scale residual connections, is a novel design not present in prior Mamba-for-vision or SCI literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work addresses a practical disconnect: quad-Bayer sensors are now ubiquitous in consumer smartphones, yet all existing color video SCI algorithms assume Bayer patterns, producing color distortion and failed demosaicing on real devices. The substantial efficiency gains (4.5% of STFormer-B's FLOPS at 3.2 dB higher PSNR) make MambaSCI a candidate for mobile deployment. Key limitations remain: evaluation is entirely on simulated data with no real quad-Bayer SCI measurements available, and the CA module's Conv3d operations inflate parameters, suggesting room for architectural simplification. Open questions include whether the linear-complexity advantage persists at even longer video sequences and whether the approach generalizes to other non-Bayer CFA layouts.
