## Problem

Attention mechanisms, State Space Models, and Recurrent Neural Networks are the three dominant token-mixing strategies in foundation models, yet they are developed and compared in isolation with no shared theoretical language. This paper addresses the absence of a principled framework that can express all three architecture classes in a common mathematical representation, making it impossible to rigorously identify which structural differences drive performance gaps (e.g., why SSMs outperform attention on long-range tasks).
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20244274/cropped_page6_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework takes the standard architectural parametrizations of attention (softmax, linear, normalized), SSMs (S6, SSD), and RNNs (qLSTM, RG-LRU) as symbolic inputs and produces a unified linear structured time-varying (LTV) recurrence with explicit state transition Λ_i, input B_i, and output C_i matrices of dimension N = nd. Empirically, the reformulated models consume token sequences from MQAR (up to L=512, 64 KV-pairs), LRA (up to 8k tokens), and WikiText-103 (context 1024), producing classification accuracy and perplexity scores. The hidden state dimension N is the key comparison axis: softmax attention requires N=∞, while finite-N models approximate it with increasing state expansion n.

## Method

The Dynamical Systems Framework (DSF) rewrites every architecture into a common LTV recurrence h_i = Λ_i h_{i-1} + B_i u_i, y_i = C_i h_i + D_i u_i, where Λ_i is a diagonal state transition matrix. For attention, the separable kernel decomposition ζ(q_i^T k_j) = φ(q_i)^T ψ(k_j) / η(q_i, k_i) yields a scalar Λ_i that cancels in the convolution kernel, revealing that softmax attention's exponential kernel is infinite-dimensional (Lemma 1). For S6, the discretization step Δ_i couples Λ_i and B_i in a way that does not cancel, creating input-dependent selectivity absent in attention. The framework proves that larger state dimension N always subsumes smaller N (Lemma 2), explaining why state expansion improves RNNs and SSMs. Empirically, the DSF identifies that replacing qLSTM's sigmoid forget gate with S6's reversed-sigmoid state transition (Eq. 23) improves performance, and that an exponential normalization η(u_i) = e^{W_η u_i} closes part of the gap between linear and softmax attention on WikiText-103.

## Results

On the LRA benchmark (70M params), S6 achieves 66.84% average accuracy, substantially outperforming softmax attention (55.96%), linear attention (53.52%), and the proposed normalized attention (58.08%). On WikiText-103 perplexity, softmax attention achieves the best score (13.15), followed by normalized attention (16.43) and linear attention (17.42). On the MQAR benchmark, linear attention converges to softmax attention's perfect accuracy as state expansion n increases, and the S6-inspired reversed-sigmoid state transition consistently outperforms the original qLSTM forget gate across all three task difficulties.

## Baselines & Comparisons

S6 (Mamba) [7] dominates on LRA with 66.84% average accuracy, beating softmax attention [2] by 10.9 points and linear attention [14] by 13.3 points; the gap is driven primarily by the Text (81.34% vs. 63.10%) and Image (65.08% vs. 34.22%) tasks. The proposed normalized attention (22) outperforms both linear attention (58.08% vs. 53.52%) and softmax attention (58.08% vs. 55.96%) on LRA, and closes 25% of the WikiText-103 perplexity gap between linear and softmax attention (16.43 vs. 17.42 vs. 13.15). SSD [8] outperforms S6 on MQAR, confirming that reducing the state-transition parameters from d to a single scalar does not degrade performance. The S6-inspired reversed-sigmoid state transition [7] consistently outperforms the original qLSTM [9] forget gate on all three MQAR task difficulties (L=64/128/256), demonstrating that architectural insights transfer across model classes. On WikiText-103, softmax attention retains a clear perplexity advantage (13.15) over all linear-complexity alternatives, indicating that the infinite-dimensional kernel of softmax (Lemma 1) still provides a modeling benefit that finite-state approximations have not fully recovered.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the S4→Mamba lineage of state space models (Gu et al.) and the kernelized/linear attention formulation of Katharopoulos et al., unifying both under a common linear time-varying (LTV) dynamical system representation. While prior works have established pairwise connections—Ali et al. linking Mamba to non-normalized softmax, Schlag et al. showing linear transformers as fast weight programmers, and Oren et al. relating transformers to multi-state RNNs—this paper is the first to place all three architecture families (softmax/linear attention, SSMs, RNNs) in a single parameterization (the DSF) enabling direct structural comparison. The framework departs from the computational-efficiency-driven parametrizations in the literature, deliberately rewriting models to expose mathematical structure (e.g., the role of normalization vs. discretization) rather than to be implemented directly.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The DSF provides a principled lens for architecture design: it shows that the performance gap between SSMs and attention on long-range tasks is largely attributable to the recurrent normalization (discretization) strategy rather than the fundamental recurrence structure, and that cross-architecture transfer (e.g., S6's state transition improving qLSTM) is a viable path to better RNNs. However, the empirical validation is limited to synthetic benchmarks (MQAR, LRA) and a 70M-parameter language model (WikiText-103), leaving open whether these insights hold at the scale of modern foundation models. The paper also opens a promising but unexplored direction: analyzing training dynamics as interacting dynamical systems via control-theoretic tools (Lyapunov stability), which could yield convergence guarantees for architecture-specific training.
