## Problem

Existing adversarial attacks on LLMs operate at the discrete token level (jailbreaks, prompt injection), a constraint driven by API-only access to closed-source models. As open-source models approach proprietary capability, the threat of attacks exploiting full weight and activation access—specifically continuous perturbations in the embedding space—remains largely unexplored. This paper investigates whether embedding-space attacks can bypass safety alignment, extract supposedly unlearned knowledge, and recover pretraining data more efficiently than discrete attacks or fine-tuning.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text instruction (tokenized and embedded into R^{n×D}), a short target response (e.g., 'Sure, the answer is'), and optimizes a continuous adversarial perturbation vector of fixed length (1, 5, or 20 tokens) appended to the instruction's embeddings. The model weights remain frozen throughout; only the embedding-space perturbation is updated, with no constraints placed on its magnitude. The output is the optimized perturbation, which when fed through the model produces harmful generations, reveals unlearned associations, or reproduces pretraining text.

## Method

The core mechanism is signed gradient descent on the continuous embedding vectors, minimizing cross-entropy between the model's generated output and a target response, with all adversarial tokens optimized simultaneously (in contrast to GCG's sequential token-by-token approach). Two variants are defined: individual attacks (one perturbation per instruction) and universal attacks (a single perturbation shared across all instructions, enabling generalization to unseen behaviors). A multi-layer variant additionally decodes intermediate hidden states from every layer via greedy decoding, revealing whether unlearned information persists at different network depths. The attack requires no training data, no perturbation budget, and achieves 100% C-ASR on training data in roughly 9 iterations on average—orders of magnitude faster than discrete attacks and substantially cheaper than QLoRa fine-tuning in both compute and memory.

## Results

Individual embedding space attacks achieve 100% cumulative attack success rate (C-ASR) on all seven tested models for safety alignment bypass, including circuit-breaker models where all prior attacks failed. Universal embedding attacks generalize to unseen harmful behaviors with 91.6% ASR (Llama2-7b-Chat), 97.5% (Mistral-7b-CB), and 96.25% (Llama3-8b-CB). On the Harry Potter unlearning benchmark, universal 1-token embedding attacks recover 30.9% C-ASR from the unlearned model (vs. 3.6% without attack and 34.5% for the original model), and on TOFU, embedding attacks raise cumulative ROUGE-1 from 0.28 to 0.54. For training data extraction on Llama3-8B, a universal embedding attack improves ROUGE-1 F1 from 0.11 to 0.22 on unseen Harry Potter paragraphs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against discrete attacks (GCG [4], AutoDAN [45], PAIR [5]), embedding space attacks achieve 100% C-ASR on training data for all models while being orders of magnitude faster in compute time; the only comparable discrete method is the adaptive attack [46], which also reaches high ASR but requires manually engineered per-model templates and could not be evaluated on circuit-breaker models. Against fine-tuning [47], universal embedding attacks reach 91.6% ASR after just 20 iterations versus fine-tuning's 88.8% after 50 iterations, while using only 25% of the memory and requiring no training data. On the unlearning task, embedding attacks dramatically outperform the logit-lens-based HPA (7.2%) and PDA (9.0%) baselines [28], with the universal 1-token attack achieving 30.9% C-ASR—nearly matching the original non-unlearned model's 34.5% accuracy. Embedding attacks are the only method shown to bypass circuit-breaker defenses [40], where all prior discrete and latent attacks achieved near-zero success. Additionally, embedding-attacked models can generate discrete jailbreaks achieving 43.6% ASR on unseen behaviors, whereas the unattacked model produces 0%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the GCG-style adversarial attack framework (Zou et al., 2023) by shifting optimization from discrete token space to the continuous embedding space, a move only feasible with full white-box access to open-source models. It departs from the dominant discrete-threat-model paradigm (GCG, AutoDAN, PAIR, adaptive attacks) and positions embedding-space attacks as a distinct, more powerful threat model for open-source LLMs. The unlearning-interrogation angle builds on logit-lens-based extraction methods (Patil et al., 2023) but replaces them with gradient-optimized adversarial perturbations, and the fine-tuning comparison draws on Qi et al. (2023) to show embedding attacks are cheaper and more effective at removing safety alignment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that embedding-space attacks bypass circuit-breaking defenses—which were specifically designed to be robust to adversarial inputs—raises a fundamental question about whether open-source LLMs can be made safe under full model access. The C-ASR metric and the demonstration that unlearned models retain recoverable knowledge (30.9% vs. 34.5% original accuracy) provide a rigorous interrogation tool for evaluating unlearning quality. Key limitations include the white-box requirement, testing primarily on 7B–8B models (one 70B test), the unconstrained perturbation magnitude, and the use of an affirmative-response objective that may conflate hallucination with successful extraction. Open questions include whether constrained or certified defenses can resist embedding-space attacks and whether the continuous-to-discrete transfer (43.6% ASR jailbreak generation) scales to proprietary models.
