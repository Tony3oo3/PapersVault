## Problem

Existing antibody sequence-structure co-design methods (MEAN, dyMEAN, DiffAb) are evaluated primarily on AAR and RMSD, which are easily gamed and ignore side-chain interactions critical for antigen binding. More fundamentally, these methods produce antibodies with high structural clashes and poor binding affinity because they are trained on limited real-world data and lack an explicit optimization signal for physical rationality and functionality. This paper reframes antibody design as an energy-based optimization problem and introduces a preference optimization framework to close the gap between generative quality and biophysical viability.

## Input / Output

The method conditions on the full antigen structure plus the antibody framework and non-target CDRs (128 nearest residues as context) and generates the CDR-H3 loop with three coupled outputs per residue: amino acid type (categorical over 20 residues), Cα coordinate (R³), and side-chain orientation (SO(3) rotation matrix). Side-chain atoms are subsequently packed by pyRosetta. The preference signal is computed at the residue level using Rosetta energy terms (total energy, non-repulsive interaction energy, and repulsive interaction energy between CDR side-chains/backbone and antigen), enabling fine-grained credit assignment that a whole-CDR reward cannot provide.

## Method

ABDPO first pre-trains a conditional SE(3)-equivariant diffusion model (same architecture as DiffAb) on SAbDab antigen-antibody complexes, then fine-tunes it via a direct energy-based preference optimization objective derived from DPO adapted to diffusion models. The key insight is residue-level credit assignment: since antibody energy decomposes additively over residues, the DPO loss is split per-residue so that each amino acid's likelihood is reweighted by its own estimated reward rather than the whole-CDR reward, yielding more targeted gradients. To prevent the model from trivially minimizing repulsion by pushing CDRs away from the antigen, the energy is decomposed into total, non-repulsive, and repulsive components, and gradient surgery (projecting conflicting gradients onto each other's normal planes) is applied to mitigate interference between these competing objectives. The final training loss (Eq. 8) replaces the intractable reverse-process log-likelihood ratio with KL divergences between the forward diffusion posterior and the model/reference denoisers, enabling efficient forward-process sampling during fine-tuning.

## Results

On the RAbD benchmark (55 complexes), ABDPO achieves a mean CDR E_total of 629.44 kcal/mol and CDR-Ag ΔG of 307.56 kcal/mol, dramatically outperforming the best baseline DiffAb (1729.51 and 1297.25 respectively). ABDPO is the only method to produce successful designs (N_success = 9 out of 55 complexes where at least one generated antibody has energies close to or lower than the natural antibody), while all baselines yield zero successes. The extended variant ABDPO+ (adding pLL and PHR preferences) achieves 5 successes with CDR E_total of 1106.48 and CDR-Ag ΔG of 637.62, while also improving AAR to 36.27% and reducing PHR to 44.21%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ABDPO decisively outperforms all four baselines on the two energy-based metrics that the paper argues are the most meaningful: CDR E_total (629.44 vs. 1729.51 for DiffAb, the closest competitor) and CDR-Ag ΔG (307.56 vs. 1041.43 for MEAN, the best baseline on this metric). On N_success, ABDPO achieves 9 successful designs while HERN, MEAN, dyMEAN, and DiffAb all achieve zero, indicating that no baseline produces even a single antibody with energies comparable to the natural one. ABDPO trades a slightly lower AAR (31.25% vs. 40.04% for dyMEAN) and marginally higher RMSD (1.98 vs. 1.69 for MEAN) for substantially better physical plausibility; the paper argues AAR is easily 'hacked' by generating high-frequency residues and RMSD ignores side chains critical for binding. ABDPO+ (with additional pLL and PHR preferences) recovers AAR to 36.27% and reduces PHR to 44.21% while still maintaining CDR E_total of 1106.48 and CDR-Ag ΔG of 637.62, both far below any baseline, demonstrating multi-objective compatibility.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the direct preference optimization (DPO) framework from LLM alignment (Rafailov et al., 2023) and its diffusion-model extension (Wallace et al., 2023) to the antibody design setting, using the DiffAb conditional diffusion architecture (Luo et al., 2022) as the base model. The key conceptual departure from prior antibody co-design methods (HERN, MEAN, dyMEAN, DiffAb) is reframing the objective from matching reference sequences/structures (AAR, RMSD) to minimizing biophysical energy, with a novel residue-level credit assignment that exploits the additivity of Rosetta energy terms. Gradient surgery (Yu et al., 2020) is repurposed to resolve conflicts between attraction and repulsion energy gradients during multi-objective fine-tuning, and the self-play data generation strategy addresses the scarcity of high-quality real-world antibody-antigen complexes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper exposes a significant gap in the antibody design literature: methods that achieve reasonable AAR and RMSD still produce antibodies with structural clashes and poor binding energies, suggesting the field's standard metrics were masking fundamental quality issues. The generic preference-optimization framework allows arbitrary energy or non-energy objectives to be combined, making it a flexible tool for the drug discovery pipeline. However, the practical impact is tempered by the small number of successful designs (9/55 complexes), the elevated hydrophobicity (PHR 69.67%) of generated sequences, and the inherent circularity of optimizing and evaluating on the same energy function. The unresolved question of how well in silico Rosetta energy correlates with wet-lab binding affinity remains a critical open problem for translating these computational designs into therapeutic candidates.
