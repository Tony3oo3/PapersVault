## Problem

Vision Transformers with local structures (region attention, convolutions) improve classification accuracy but remain vulnerable to adversarial perturbations and out-of-distribution inputs. Neural Cellular Automata possess strong robustness through recurrent local interactions and training stochasticity, but their standard Interaction stage incurs prohibitive computational cost in the high-dimensional token spaces typical of ViTs. The paper asks whether NCA can be made efficient enough to serve as lightweight, plug-and-play robustness-enhancing modules within existing ViT architectures.

## Input / Output

The method takes standard 224×224 RGB images and produces ImageNet class labels, operating within the standard supervised classification pipeline. Internally, AdaNCA modules consume and produce token maps of shape H×W×C (where C matches the ViT channel dimension) at specified inter-layer positions, with the recurrent evolution running for a small fixed number of steps (2–5) at test time. The stochastic mask is active only during training (dropout-like), so inference is fully deterministic and differentiable, avoiding obfuscated-gradient issues.

## Method

AdaNCA inserts lightweight NCA blocks between middle ViT layers, each performing recurrent residual updates via an Interaction stage followed by an MLP Update stage. The key architectural innovation is Dynamic Interaction: instead of concatenating outputs from M depth-wise convolutions (as in standard NCA), it computes a per-token weighted sum where scalar weights are produced by a small two-layer convolutional network conditioned on the token map, reducing channel dimensionality and enabling tokens to adapt their interaction strategy to local context. Multi-scale Dynamic Interaction extends this by adding dilation rates and a second weight network to aggregate across scales. For placement, the paper introduces the Set Cohesion Index κ (based on CKA layer similarities) to quantify network redundancy, then applies a dynamic programming algorithm to find the insertion positions that maximize the sum of κ across the resulting layer partitions. Training employs a dropout-like stochastic update (Bernoulli mask with activation compensation) and random recurrent step counts within a small range, both of which are ablated to confirm their contribution to robustness.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K, AdaNCA-enhanced ViTs consistently outperform their baselines across all eight robustness benchmarks with less than 3% additional parameters. The headline improvement is over 10% absolute accuracy gain under adversarial attacks (e.g., RVT-B: 21.9%→33.2% APGD-DLR; ConViT-B: 8.9%→20.1% CW). Clean accuracy also improves by 0.3–0.8 points across all four architectures (e.g., Swin-B: 83.4%→83.7%, ConViT-B: 82.4%→83.2%). On OOD benchmarks, AdaNCA reduces ImageNet-C mCE by 1.4–2.6 points and improves ImageNet-R/Sketch accuracy by 1.6–3.4 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the four direct baselines (RVT-B, FAN-B, Swin-B, ConViT-B), AdaNCA wins on every single metric across all eight robustness benchmarks and clean accuracy, with the largest adversarial gains on RVT-B (+11.3 pts APGD-DLR) and ConViT-B (+11.2 pts CW). Against the SOTA robustness method TAPADL [23], AdaNCA achieves substantially higher adversarial robustness (e.g., RVT-B-AdaNCA: 33.2% vs. 17.7% APGD-DLR; FAN-B-AdaNCA: 14.1% vs. 13.5% APGD-DLR) but slightly lower OOD performance on ImageNet-C (43.2 vs. 44.7 mCE for RVT; 44.7 vs. 43.7 for FAN), indicating a trade-off where AdaNCA prioritizes adversarial robustness. Enlarged baselines (Swin-B*, ConViT-B*) with more layers do not match AdaNCA's improvements, confirming gains are architectural rather than from extra parameters. In the direct comparison with ViTCA [62] (Appendix C.12), AdaNCA's Dynamic Interaction achieves higher clean accuracy (87.18% vs. 86.78%) with fewer parameters (27.94M vs. 28.48M) and comparable attack failure rate (22.35 vs. 19.47 at scale=1; 22.28 at scale=2). Notably, TAPADL actually degrades adversarial robustness relative to its own baselines in some settings (e.g., TAPADL-RVT: 27.6% PGD vs. RVT-B: 29.9%), whereas AdaNCA consistently improves both adversarial and OOD robustness simultaneously.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously separate lines of research: Vision Transformers with local inductive biases (Swin, ConViT, RVT, FAN) that improve classification accuracy but leave robustness largely unchanged, and Neural Cellular Automata (NCA) that model local interactions with strong generalization properties but have only been applied to small-scale tasks (MNIST, CIFAR10, texture synthesis). The key departure from prior NCA work is the introduction of Dynamic Interaction to replace channel-wise concatenation with a token-conditioned weighted sum, reducing computational overhead in high-dimensional ViT feature spaces. Unlike TAPADL, which modifies the ViT's attention mechanism and can actually degrade adversarial robustness, AdaNCA is inserted as a lightweight adaptor between existing layers without altering the backbone architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play nature of AdaNCA (<3% parameter increase, >10% absolute adversarial robustness gain) offers a practical path to hardening deployed ViTs without architectural redesign, and the Set Cohesion Index provides a principled, data-driven placement algorithm that practitioners can apply to new architectures. However, the method requires training from scratch (fine-tuning pre-trained models performs worse), is validated only on 224×224 ImageNet1K, and cannot generalize to unseen recurrent step counts, limiting immediate real-world adoption. Open questions include whether the redundancy-robustness correlation holds at larger scales (ImageNet22K, higher resolutions) and whether the NCA adaptor can be effectively integrated into already-converged ViTs without full retraining.
