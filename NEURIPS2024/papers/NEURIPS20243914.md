## Problem

Existing incremental learning methods focus exclusively on object classification while neglecting fine-grained states (e.g., color, material) attached to objects, and compositional learning methods do not consider the incremental paradigm where the number of composition classes grows over time. This paper introduces Compositional Incremental Learning (composition-IL), a new task where the model must continually learn new state-object compositions while primitive concepts (states and objects) can randomly recur across incremental sessions. The central challenge is the ambiguous composition boundary: models excessively prioritize object primitives over state primitives, rendering compositions that share the same object but differ in state indistinguishable.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image as input and simultaneously produces three predictions: a composition label (a state-object pair such as "red shirt"), a state label (e.g., "red"), and an object label (e.g., "shirt"). At inference, the final composition probability is augmented by adding a weighted sum of the state and object probabilities (p_final = p(c|x) + μ(p(s|x) + p(o|x))), leveraging primitive recognition to disambiguate compositions. Evaluation is conducted on two newly constructed benchmarks—Split-Clothing (35 compositions from 9 states × 8 objects, 5 tasks) and Split-UT-Zappos (80 compositions from 15 states × 12 objects, 5 or 10 tasks)—derived from Clothing16K and UT-Zappos50K.

## Method

CompILer is a rehearsal-free, prompt-based learner built on a frozen ViT-B/16 backbone that maintains three dedicated prompt pools (for states, objects, and compositions), each containing M prompts with associated learnable keys for query-key selection, regularized by a directional decoupled loss enforcing inter-pool discrepancy and intra-pool diversity. To address the inherent difficulty of state recognition, object-injected state prompting uses the fused object prompt as both key and value in a cross-attention operation to refine the query feature before selecting state prompts, so that object knowledge guides state disambiguation. Selected top-k prompts from each pool are fused via a learnable generalized-mean (GeM) pooling that adaptively interpolates between mean and max pooling to suppress irrelevant or contradictory information. Training optimizes a symmetric cross-entropy loss (forward CE plus reverse CE) for robustness to noisy labels, with the total loss balancing inter-pool, intra-pool, surrogate alignment, and classification objectives across all three label spaces.

## Results

On Split-Clothing (5 tasks), CompILer achieves 89.21% Avg Acc and 7.26% FTT, approaching the upper bound of 97.02% and outperforming the best prompt-based baseline Dual-Prompt (87.87% Avg Acc, 7.71% FTT) by 1.34 points in accuracy. On the more challenging Split-UT-Zappos, CompILer reaches 46.48% Avg Acc (5 tasks) and 34.43% Avg Acc (10 tasks), consistently leading all baselines by 0.7–3.2 points. Forgetting (FTT) is also minimized at 19.27% (5-task) and 28.69% (10-task), though Dual-Prompt and LGCL achieve slightly lower FTT on the 10-task setting by sacrificing plasticity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CompILer outperforms all seven baselines on Avg Acc across all three dataset settings. On Split-Clothing, it leads Dual-Prompt (87.87%) by 1.34 points and LGCL (87.32%) by 1.89 points, while the gap over prompt-free methods (EWC 47.89%, LwF 49.96%, iCaRL 68.65%) is substantial (20–41 points). On Split-UT-Zappos (5 tasks), CompILer (46.48%) surpasses CODA-Prompt (43.35%) by 3.13 points and L2P (42.20%) by 4.28 points. On the 10-task setting, the margin narrows to 0.87 points over LGCL (33.56%) and 1.42 points over Dual-Prompt (33.01%). For FTT, CompILer achieves the best forgetting on Split-Clothing (7.26%) and 5-task UT-Zappos (19.27%), but trails LGCL (24.37%) and Dual-Prompt (24.61%) on 10-task UT-Zappos, where those methods sacrifice plasticity for lower forgetting by dynamically growing task-specific parameters.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of prompt-based continual learning (L2P, Dual-Prompt, CODA-Prompt) and compositional zero-shot learning (CZSL), but departs from both in a key way: prior incremental learners treat each class as an atomic object label, while prior compositional learners focus on zero-shot generalization from seen to unseen compositions without an incremental stream. CompILer extends the single-pool prompt architecture of L2P into three dedicated pools (state, object, composition), borrows the directional decoupled loss from prompt-in-prompt learning to enforce inter-pool discrepancy and intra-pool diversity, and adapts generalized-mean pooling (originally for CNN retrieval) as a learnable prompt fusion operator. The object-injected state prompting mechanism is a novel cross-attention step that has no direct precedent in the prompt-based continual learning literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By formalizing composition-IL and releasing two tailored benchmarks (Split-Clothing, Split-UT-Zappos), the paper gives the community a concrete testbed for evaluating whether incremental learners can handle fine-grained state-object compositions rather than flat object classes. The low upper bound on Split-UT-Zappos (≈69%) due to long-tail distributions and invisible material states highlights that dataset construction for this task is itself an open problem. The paper also leaves open the question of whether the object→state asymmetry (object knowledge helps state prediction but not vice versa) generalizes beyond the two small-scale datasets, and whether the approach scales to scenarios with multiple concurrent states per object.
