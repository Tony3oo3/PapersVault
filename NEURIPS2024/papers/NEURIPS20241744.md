## Problem

Evaluating jailbreak attacks and defenses on LLMs suffers from three critical gaps: no standardized evaluation protocol, incomparable cost and success-rate computations across works, and widespread non-reproducibility due to withheld adversarial prompts, closed-source code, and reliance on evolving proprietary APIs. JailbreakBench addresses these by providing an open, community-driven benchmark that unifies attack and defense evaluation under a single threat model and scoring pipeline.

## Input / Output

The benchmark consumes adversarial prompts (jailbreak strings) paired with one of 100 harmful behavior goals from JBB-Behaviors, queries target LLMs (Vicuna-13B, Llama-2-7B, GPT-3.5, GPT-4) using greedy decoding with a fixed 150-token generation limit, and produces per-behavior safe/unsafe classifications from the Llama-3-70B judge. Outputs include aggregate attack success rate, average query and token counts per attack, refusal rates on 100 matched benign behaviors, and structured leaderboard entries with metadata (threat model, hyperparameters, evaluation date). Submissions are packaged as zipped artifact folders containing prompts, responses, and metadata, loadable via a single Python call.

## Method

JailbreakBench standardizes the full red-teaming pipeline around four pillars: (1) the JBB-Behaviors dataset of 100 harmful and 100 benign behaviors spanning ten OpenAI usage-policy categories, with 55% original and 45% sourced from AdvBench and TDC/HarmBench; (2) a rigorous judge selection protocol in which six candidate classifiers (rule-based, GPT-4, HarmBench Llama-2-13B, Llama Guard, Llama Guard 2, Llama-3-70B) are evaluated against 300 human-labeled prompt-response pairs, yielding Llama-3-70B with a custom system prompt as the chosen judge (90.7% agreement, 5.5% FNR); (3) an open repository of jailbreak artifacts from four baseline attacks (PAIR, GCG, JB-Chat AIM template, and Prompt+Random Search with self-transfer) that enables reproducible comparison without re-running expensive optimization; and (4) five implemented test-time defenses (SmoothLLM, Perplexity Filter, Erase-and-Check, Synonym Substitution, Remove Non-Dictionary) evaluated against transfer attacks. The framework enforces deterministic evaluation (temperature-0 sampling, fixed system prompts per model) and prohibits submissions from using the Llama-3-70B judge to prevent overfitting.

## Results

On the JBB-Behaviors dataset (100 harmful behaviors), Prompt with RS achieves the highest attack success rate across all target LLMs: 89% on Vicuna, 90% on Llama-2, 93% on GPT-3.5, and 78% on GPT-4, while also being the most query-efficient (2–3 queries on average for open-source and GPT-3.5 models). GCG achieves 80% ASR on Vicuna but drops to 3% on Llama-2 and 4% on GPT-4. Among defenses, Erase-and-Check is the most consistently effective, reducing Prompt with RS ASR to 24% (Vicuna), 25% (Llama-2), 8% (GPT-3.5), and 10% (GPT-4). For judge selection, Llama-3-70B achieves 90.7% agreement with human annotators (FPR 11.6%, FNR 5.5%), marginally outperforming GPT-4 (90.3% agreement) and substantially outperforming Llama Guard 2 (87.7%), HarmBench (78.3%), and the rule-based judge (56.0%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among the four attacks, Prompt with RS (Andriushchenko et al., 2024) dominates, achieving 89–93% ASR across all four target LLMs with only 2–3 queries on average for Vicuna and GPT-3.5, far outperforming GCG (80% Vicuna but 3–4% on Llama-2/GPT-4), PAIR (69–71% on Vicuna/GPT-3.5 but 0% on Llama-2), and JB-Chat (90% Vicuna only, 0% elsewhere). For defenses, Erase-and-Check (Kumar et al., 2023) is the most robust, reducing Prompt with RS ASR to 8–25% across models, while Perplexity Filter (Jain et al., 2023) is effective only against GCG (reducing it to 0–3%) and SmoothLLM (Robey et al., 2023) reduces GCG and PAIR but leaves Prompt with RS at 56% on GPT-4. In the judge comparison, Llama-3-70B (90.7% agreement, 5.5% FNR) is selected over GPT-4 (90.3% agreement, 9.1% FNR) for open-weight reproducibility; both substantially outperform Llama Guard 2 (87.7%), HarmBench's Llama-2-13B judge (78.3%, 26.8% FPR), Llama Guard (72.0%, 60.9% FNR), and the rule-based string-matching judge (56.0% agreement, 64.2% FPR). The benchmark also shows that transfer attacks from undefended models provide only a lower bound, as adaptive attacks could further increase ASR against specific defenses.

## Relation to Prior Work

JailbreakBench adapts the standardized adversarial robustness benchmarking paradigm of RobustBench (Croce et al., 2021) to the LLM jailbreaking domain, but departs from the closest prior benchmark, HarmBench (Mazeika et al., 2024), by focusing on adaptive attacks and test-time defenses rather than static prompt templates. The paper positions itself as a reproducibility-first alternative to existing evaluation efforts: unlike prior works that withhold adversarial prompts or rely on evolving proprietary APIs, JailbreakBench open-sources all jailbreak artifacts and standardizes the full evaluation pipeline (system prompts, decoding parameters, judge classifier). The judge selection builds on the LLM-as-a-judge paradigm (Zheng et al., 2023; Chao et al., 2023) but introduces a rigorous human-annotated comparison of six candidate classifiers, ultimately selecting Llama-3-70B over GPT-4 for open-weight reproducibility.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This benchmark addresses a critical gap in the LLM safety evaluation landscape: prior to its release, there was no clear standard of practice, costs and success rates were computed in incomparable ways, and many results were irreproducible. The open-sourcing of jailbreak artifacts is particularly impactful, as it enables adversarial training (Hubinger et al., 2024) and provides a stable comparison baseline that the community has already begun adopting (cited by Gemini 1.5 authors and multiple follow-up works within two months). Key limitations include the restriction to text-only inputs, the use of transfer (non-adaptive) attacks for defense evaluation, and the prohibition on system prompt modification, all of which constrain the threat model. Open questions include how to properly evaluate adaptive attacks against defenses at scale, whether the Llama-3-70B judge remains optimal as models evolve, and how to extend the benchmark to multimodal jailbreaking.
