## Problem

Naively injecting persistent homology (PH) features into GNN layers yields only marginal gains and poor interpretability, while dense graph pooling methods produce fully connected coarsened graphs that destroy all topological structure. The paper identifies that PH's filtration operation naturally aligns with graph pooling's hierarchical cut-off mechanism, but this structural correspondence has never been exploited to make pooling topology-aware.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240603/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an attributed graph (adjacency matrix A ∈ {0,1}^{n×n} and node feature matrix X ∈ R^{n×d}) and produces a coarsened graph whose edge weights are guaranteed in [0,1] after persistence injection, ultimately yielding a graph class label. TIP operates as a drop-in plug-in module appended after the standard coarsening step of existing dense pooling methods (DiffPool, MinCutPool, DMoNPool), replacing only the reconstruction loss L_r with the proposed topological loss L_topo while keeping all other architecture and hyperparameters unchanged. The coarsened graph is first resampled to a sparse binary matrix via Gumbel-softmax before PH is computed, ensuring the filtration operates on a meaningful topology rather than a dense one.

## Method

Topology-Invariant Pooling (TIP) injects PH into graph pooling at both feature and topology levels through three mechanisms: (1) Resampling converts the dense coarsened adjacency matrix into a sparse binary matrix using Gumbel-softmax, making it compatible with PH computation; (2) Persistence Injection computes 1-dimensional persistent homology using a learnable filtration (MLP + sigmoid on node features) and reweights edges via Hadamard product with persistence values (destruction minus creation time), encoding cycle significance directly into edge weights; (3) A topological loss L_topo vectorizes persistence diagrams using Perslay transformations (triangle, Gaussian, line point), computes mean and standard deviation vectors, and minimizes the squared L2 difference between original and coarsened graph statistics across all pooling layers. The key insight is that by enforcing topological invariance at the pooling level rather than merely appending PH features to GNN layers, message passing in the coarsened graph operates along persistent pooled topology, and the method is provably at least as expressive as 1-WL while being invariant under isomorphism.

## Results

On graph classification, DiffPool-TIP achieves the best overall accuracy across 8 benchmark datasets, reaching 83.75% on NCI1, 81.09% on NCI109, 65.05% on ENZYMES, and 77.75% on OGBG-MOLHIV. The most dramatic improvement is on ENZYMES, where DiffPool-TIP (65.05%) surpasses the base DiffPool (48.34%) by 16.7 points and outperforms the strongest topological baseline GSN (49.50%) by 15.6 points. On the topological similarity task (Wasserstein distance, lower is better), DiffPool-TIP reduces the distance from 234.57 (DiffPool) to 8.03 on the ring dataset, a ~29× improvement. On ZINC regression (MSE, lower is better), DiffPool-TIP achieves 0.28 vs. 0.34 for DiffPool.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TIP consistently outperforms all three base dense pooling methods (DiffPool, MinCutPool, DMoNPool) across nearly all datasets, with the largest gains on ENZYMES (+16.7 pts for DiffPool-TIP) and NCI1 (+6.1 pts). Against the GNN baselines (GCN, GIN, GraphSAGE), DiffPool-TIP wins on 7 of 8 datasets, with the closest margin on IMDB-BINARY where GSN (76.80%) narrowly edges out DiffPool-TIP (76.40%). The topological GNN TOGL [19] is competitive on OGBG-MOLHIV (77.21% vs. 77.75%) but is substantially outperformed on ENZYMES (46.09% vs. 65.05%), suggesting that feature-level PH integration is less effective than topology-level injection into pooling. GSN [4] is the strongest non-pooling baseline, matching DiffPool-TIP on NCI1 (83.50% vs. 83.75%) but losing on all other datasets. Sparse pooling methods (Graclus, TopK) are outperformed by TIP on most datasets, though Graclus is competitive on IMDB-MULTI (54.72% vs. 55.53%). On the topological similarity task, TIP reduces Wasserstein distance by 20–30× compared to dense pooling baselines (e.g., 8.03 vs. 234.57 on ring), while sparse methods (TopK, Graclus) achieve moderate improvements but still fall short of TIP.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm of integrating persistent homology into GNNs at the feature level (e.g., TOGL, GSN, Perslay-based reweighting) by instead injecting PH into the graph pooling layer itself, at both feature and topology levels. It builds on learnable filtration functions [16] for efficient PH computation, Perslay-style transformations [6] for vectorizing persistence diagrams, and Gumbel-softmax resampling [22] to convert dense coarsened adjacency matrices into sparse graphs amenable to PH. The key conceptual departure is the observation that PH filtration and GP coarsening are both hierarchical sparsification operations, enabling a natural alignment that prior feature-level PH-GNN works did not exploit. The topology-preserving loss (L_topo) replaces the standard reconstruction losses in DiffPool/MinCutPool/DMoNPool with a high-order statistical comparison of persistence diagrams, a design choice distinct from both the Wasserstein-distance approach and the cluster-structure objectives of existing pooling methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a fundamental flaw in dense graph pooling: coarsened graphs become nearly fully connected, destroying all meaningful topology and rendering subsequent message passing layers topology-blind. TIP addresses this as a drop-in plug-in, and the substantial improvements (e.g., 48.34→65.05 on ENZYMES with DiffPool) suggest that preserving persistent cycles during pooling is a critical but previously unaddressed factor in graph classification. However, the method's reliance on 1-dimensional (cycle) topology limits its applicability to tree-like graphs, and it cannot distinguish graphs by connected-component count alone—both acknowledged limitations that point to open questions about extending TIP to higher-dimensional or component-aware topological invariants. The theoretical result that self-loop-augmented 1-D PH is at least as expressive as 1-WL provides a clean justification but leaves open whether this expressivity advantage translates to consistent gains on all graph families.
