## Problem

Federated learning on resource-constrained edge devices demands both computation and communication efficiency, yet existing approaches either impose regularization on local training or require proxy datasets for server-side refinement. The paper asks whether a simpler paradigm—performing all local training in low precision while only the server aggregation remains high-precision—can match full-precision FL accuracy, and whether this can be proven to converge for non-iid data.

## Input / Output

Each client receives a quantized global model (block floating point, 6/8/32 bits) and trains on its local RGB image dataset (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100/CINIC-10, 28×28 for FashionMNIST) using Dirichlet-distributed non-iid splits. All intermediate quantities—activations, gradients, back-propagation signals, SGD momentum, and weights—are quantized to the chosen bit width on the client. The server outputs a high-precision global model (maintained via moving average) that classifies images; the quantization bit width is the primary hyperparameter controlling the accuracy-efficiency tradeoff.

## Method

The proposed paradigm has two modules. On the client side, block floating point quantization is applied to every numerical operation during local SGD (forward activations, backward signals, gradients, momentum, and weight updates), reducing both FLOPs and the size of uploaded models. On the server side, the received low-precision models are first combined via weighted averaging, then a high-precision moving average (coefficient λ) is maintained across rounds to recover a full-precision global model, inspired by Kolmogorov's law that noisy sample averages converge to the true expectation. Theoretically, the paper proves O(1/T) convergence for non-iid data under L-smoothness and μ-strong convexity, for both full and partial client participation, with the quantization gap δ appearing only in additive error terms. As a by-product, the restricted expressiveness of low-precision local models naturally regularizes against overfitting to small local datasets, reducing client drift and stabilizing aggregation under heterogeneous data.

## Results

On the four benchmark datasets (FashionMNIST, CIFAR-10, CINIC-10, CIFAR-100) with Dirichlet non-IID data (α=0.01, 0.04, 0.16), the proposed 8-bit low-precision local training with server-side moving average matches or exceeds full-precision (32-bit) FedAvg in most settings. For example, on CIFAR-10 with α=0.01, 8-bit w/ avg achieves 53.3% accuracy vs. 41.1% for standard 32-bit FedAvg (w/o avg), and on CIFAR-100 with α=0.04, it reaches 34.1% vs. 28.2%. The method also outperforms model-compression baselines (HeteroFL, Split-Mix) by 14–24% accuracy at comparable or lower communication cost (9.8 MB/round vs. 7.3 MB/round).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against standard FedAvg (32-bit, w/o avg), the proposed 8-bit w/ avg method consistently wins: on CIFAR-10 (α=0.01) it achieves 53.3% vs. 41.1% (+12.2 pp), on CINIC-10 (α=0.01) 43.3% vs. 35.4% (+7.9 pp), and on CIFAR-100 (α=0.04) 34.1% vs. 28.2% (+5.9 pp). When integrated with other FL methods (Table 2), 8-bit w/ avg improves over each method's own 32-bit w/o avg baseline—e.g., FedProx on CIFAR-10 (α=0.01): 51.4% vs. 45.1%; FedFTG on CIFAR-10 (α=0.01): 52.8% vs. 46.1%; ABAvg on CIFAR-10 (α=0.01): 51.1% vs. 44.5%. Against model-compression baselines on CIFAR-10 (α=0.01), 8-bit FedAvg (53.3% acc, 9.8 MB/round) substantially outperforms HeteroFL at 1/4–1/8 split (29.2% acc, 7.3 MB/round) and Split-Mix at 1/4–1/8 split (38.9% acc, 7.3 MB/round), while 6-bit FedAvg (49.4% acc, 7.3 MB/round) matches their communication budget with 10–20 pp higher accuracy. The method ties or slightly trails 32-bit w/ avg on a few settings (e.g., CIFAR-10 α=0.04: 57.4% vs. 58.6%), but the gap is within one standard deviation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on FedAvg and the broader line of efficient FL methods (HeteroFL, Split-Mix, FetchSGD) that reduce client-side computation or communication by compressing model parameters or gradients. Its key departure is to quantize the precision of all local training operations (weights, activations, gradients, momentum) rather than reducing model size, while preserving full-precision aggregation on the server via a moving average inspired by Kolmogorov's law. The approach is orthogonal to and composable with existing FL strategies like FedProx, ABAvg, FedGen, and FedFTG, which the paper demonstrates empirically.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for resource-constrained edge devices: quantizing local training simultaneously reduces both compute and communication costs without requiring architectural changes or proxy datasets on the server. The O(1/T) convergence guarantee under non-iid data provides theoretical grounding that low-precision FL is not merely a heuristic. However, experiments are limited to small 3-layer ConvNets and MLPs on image classification benchmarks (up to CIFAR100), leaving open whether the approach transfers to larger architectures (ResNet-50+, transformers), more aggressive quantization (4-bit or below), or settings with differential privacy constraints. The finding that quantization incidentally relieves local overfitting under data heterogeneity is an interesting but underexplored side effect that could inform future regularization strategies.
