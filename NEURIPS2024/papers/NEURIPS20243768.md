## Problem

Existing adaptive conformal prediction methods for dynamic environments rely on a single fixed learning model to construct prediction sets, yet the efficiency of those sets (size, regret) depends heavily on which model is used, and no single model consistently performs best across unknown distribution shifts. This paper addresses the gap by asking whether dynamically selecting among multiple candidate models 'on the fly' can yield more efficient prediction sets while preserving valid coverage guarantees.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243768/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a sequential stream of RGB images (e.g., 32×32 CIFAR-10C/100C, 64×64 TinyImageNet-C) along with a growing historical calibration set and M pre-trained classifiers (ResNet-50, ResNet-18, GoogLeNet, DenseNet-121, etc.) that are never retrained. At each time step it outputs a prediction set C(X_t) ⊆ {1,…,K} of candidate class labels constructed via a non-conformity score that combines softmax probabilities, rank-based terms, and a random tie-breaking variable, together with an adaptive miss coverage probability α_t that is updated after each true label is revealed. The prediction set is guaranteed to contain the true label with probability 1−α (target 0.9 in experiments).

## Method

SAMOCP introduces a two-level expert–model hierarchy. At the inner level, each expert maintains M parallel miss coverage probabilities (one per model), each updated via SF-OGD on a pinball loss, and selects which model's threshold to use through an exponential-weighted (weighted-majority) rule that down-weights models with higher recent loss. At the outer level, new experts are spawned at every time step with a lifetime determined by a dyadic formula λ(t) = g·max{2^n : t ≡ 0 mod 2^n}, and each expert is assigned a step size ε^n = min(ε, σ/√λ(n)) that shrinks for longer-lived experts; a second layer of exponential weights selects among all currently active experts. This design ensures that outdated experts expire automatically, preventing them from dragging down adaptation after sharp distribution shifts. The paper proves that SAMOCP achieves strongly adaptive regret over any arbitrary time interval of any width (Theorem 3) and bounded coverage error (Theorem 2), with dynamic regret scaling as Õ(T^{2/3} V^{1/3}) where V measures loss-function variation (Lemma 1).

## Results

On CIFAR-100C with gradual distribution shifts (target coverage 90%), SAMOCP achieves an average prediction set width of 5.43, average regret of 0.92×10⁻³, and single-width probability of 0.29, outperforming all single-model baselines on these efficiency metrics while maintaining 88.16% coverage. On CIFAR-10C with sudden shifts, SAMOCP reduces average width to 1.24 and single-width probability to 0.69, again the best among all methods. SAMOCP is also faster than SAOCP and SAOCP(MM) (34.87s vs. 40.74s and 47.80s per iteration) despite having the same maximum number of updates per time step.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SAMOCP is compared against FACI (Gibbs & Candès, 2022) and ScaleFreeOGD/SAOCP (Bhatnagar et al., 2023), the two most recent adaptive conformal prediction methods for dynamic environments. Against FACI, SAMOCP achieves a smaller average prediction set width (5.43 vs. 5.77 on CIFAR-100C; 1.24 vs. 1.30 on CIFAR-10C), lower average regret (0.92 vs. 1.18; 0.98 vs. 1.46), and higher single-width probability (0.29 vs. 0.28; 0.69 vs. 0.68), though FACI achieves slightly higher coverage (89.64% vs. 88.16%) and runs ~4× faster. Against ScaleFreeOGD and SAOCP, SAMOCP consistently produces narrower prediction sets (5.43 vs. 6.04 and 5.80) and lower regret (0.92 vs. 1.55 and 2.04), while SAOCP(MM) — a multi-model variant the authors constructed for fair comparison — suffers from significantly degraded coverage (85.89%) and much higher regret (4.42). The key trade-off is that SAMOCP's coverage (88.16–88.37%) falls slightly below the 90% target, whereas ScaleFreeOGD achieves near-perfect coverage (89.96–89.99%) at the cost of wider prediction sets and higher regret.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the strongly adaptive online conformal prediction framework of Bhatnagar et al. (2023, SAOCP) by replacing each single-model expert with a multi-model ensemble (MOCP) that dynamically selects among M candidate classifiers via weighted majority voting. The underlying adaptive miss-coverage mechanism inherits the pinball-loss-based SF-OGD update from Gibbs and Candès (2021) and Orabona and Pál (2018), while the expert lifetime schedule and strongly adaptive regret structure are taken directly from the SAOCP formulation. The key departure from prior work is the nested two-level selection: experts are chosen at the outer level (as in SAOCP), and within each expert, a model is chosen at the inner level based on recent performance, addressing the observation that no single model consistently dominates across all distribution shifts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled way to hedge model uncertainty in online conformal prediction without requiring prior knowledge of which model will be best under an unknown distribution shift, which is practically important for deployment settings where the data-generating process is non-stationary. The theoretical guarantees (strongly adaptive regret over arbitrary intervals, bounded coverage error) are meaningful, though the coverage in experiments consistently falls slightly below the 90% target (88–89%), and the method is slower than single-model baselines like ScaleFreeOGD. Open questions include whether the multi-model advantage persists when models are highly correlated, how the approach scales to very large model pools, and whether the deterministic aggregation of miss-coverage probabilities (used in practice) preserves the theoretical coverage guarantees.
