## Problem

Prompt-based continual learning methods achieve strong rehearsal-free performance but lack a theoretical explanation for why prompting is effective, hindering further principled design. Additionally, the paper identifies that prefix tuning—the dominant prompt-based mechanism—suffers from suboptimal sample efficiency, requiring an exponential number of data points for reasonable parameter estimation of the prompt experts.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243780/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-100, 224×224 for ImageNet-R and CUB-200) and outputs a class label from the union of all tasks seen so far. In the class-incremental setting, the model must infer both the task identity and the within-task class at test time without being told which task the image belongs to, using a frozen pre-trained ViT backbone with only small learnable prompt parameters (L_p × d) per task.

## Method

The paper reveals that each attention head in a ViT implicitly encodes a mixture-of-experts architecture with N shared linear experts (value vectors) and quadratic gating score functions (query-key dot products). Prefix tuning is then reframed as appending L new task-specific experts with linear gating to this pre-trained MoE. To fix the resulting suboptimal estimation rate of O(1/log^τ(n)), the authors propose NoRGa (Non-linear Residual Gates), which modifies only the prefix expert score functions by adding a non-linear activation σ(τ·s) plus a residual connection, introducing just two scalar parameters (α, τ) per head. Theoretically, under an algebraic independence condition between the expert and activation functions, NoRGa achieves parametric estimation rates of O_P(√(log(n)/n)) for both gating and expert parameters, reducing the required data from exponential to polynomial (≈ε⁻⁴). Empirically, NoRGa is plugged into the HiDe-Prompt framework and achieves state-of-the-art FA/CA across Split CIFAR-100, ImageNet-R, CUB-200, and 5-Datasets with multiple pre-training paradigms (Sup-21K, iBOT, DINO, MoCo).

## Results

NoRGa achieves state-of-the-art Final Average Accuracy (FA) and Cumulative Average Accuracy (CA) across all evaluated pre-trained models and datasets. On Split CIFAR-100 with Sup-21K weights, NoRGa reaches 94.48% FA and 95.83% CA, outperforming HiDe-Prompt (92.61% FA, 94.03% CA) by 1.87% and 1.80% respectively. On Split ImageNet-R with Sup-21K, NoRGa attains 79.52% CA versus HiDe-Prompt's 76.60%, a 2.92% gap. On the fine-grained Split CUB-200 benchmark, NoRGa leads HiDe-Prompt by 4.34% in FA (90.90% vs 86.56%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

NoRGa consistently outperforms all five prompt-based baselines across every pre-trained model and dataset. Against L2P (Wang, CVPR 2022), DualPrompt (Wang, ECCCV 2022), S-Prompt (Wang, NeurIPS 2022), and CODA-Prompt (Smith, CVPR 2023), NoRGa leads by 7–11% in FA on Split CIFAR-100 (Sup-21K), reflecting the substantial gap between earlier prompt-pool methods and the hierarchical HiDe-Prompt framework that NoRGa builds upon. The most direct comparison is against HiDe-Prompt (Wang, NeurIPS 2023), the immediate predecessor: NoRGa improves FA by 1.87% on Split CIFAR-100 (94.48% vs 92.61%) and by 2.46% on Split ImageNet-R with iBOT-21K (73.06% vs 70.83%), while maintaining comparable or lower Forgetting Measure. On the challenging fine-grained Split CUB-200 task, NoRGa's advantage widens to 4.34% FA over HiDe-Prompt (90.90% vs 86.56%), and on 5-Datasets with large inter-task differences it still edges the highest FA (94.16% vs 93.83%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the prompt-based continual learning framework of HiDe-Prompt [49] and the prefix tuning mechanism of Li & Liang [26], but introduces a fundamentally new theoretical lens: it shows that self-attention in Vision Transformers implicitly encodes a mixture-of-experts architecture with quadratic gating, and that prefix tuning is equivalent to injecting new task-specific experts into this MoE. This reframing departs from prior prompt-based CL work (L2P, DualPrompt, CODA-Prompt, S-Prompt), which treated prompts as opaque learnable parameters without a mechanistic explanation for their effectiveness. The proposed NoRGa modification is then motivated by a statistical argument (linear gating requires exponential data for parameter estimation) rather than by empirical trial-and-error, drawing on the MoE estimation literature (Voronoi-cell analysis, least-squares convergence rates).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The MoE-attention connection provides the first principled theoretical explanation for why prompt-based continual learning resists catastrophic forgetting, which could guide future prompt design beyond heuristic search. The statistical suboptimality result (exponential vs. polynomial sample complexity for linear vs. non-linear residual gating) is a clean theoretical justification for the architectural choice, though the practical gains over HiDe-Prompt are modest (1–2% FA). Open questions include whether more complex expert architectures (beyond the simple linear experts used here) or adaptively learned activation functions could yield larger improvements, and whether the MoE lens generalizes to other prompt injection strategies (e.g., prompt tuning on queries). The connection between attention and MoE may also inform broader questions about interpretability and capacity scaling in transformers.
