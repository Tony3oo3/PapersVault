## Problem

While image generation (IG) models have been widely leveraged to improve image understanding (IU) tasks, the reciprocal question—whether IU models can enhance IG—remains largely unexplored. In token-based IG frameworks, pixel reconstruction (e.g., VQGAN) has dominated tokenizer training, but the potential of feature reconstruction objectives, which distill knowledge from pretrained IU encoders, to improve generation quality is uncharted.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240976/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework operates on 256×256 RGB images, which the tokenizer encodes into sequences of 256 discrete tokens (a 16×16 grid at 16× downsampling). For class-conditional generation on ImageNet-1k, a class label conditions the autoregressive proposal network; for text-to-image on MS-COCO Captions, a text prompt serves as the condition. The decoder reconstructs 256×256 pixel images from the sampled code vectors, and the system is evaluated via FID, Inception Score, perplexity, and reconstruction FID.

## Method

The paper replaces the standard pixel-reconstruction training objective for VQ tokenizers with a feature-reconstruction objective (VQ-KD), where the tokenizer is trained to match the feature map of a frozen pretrained IU encoder (CLIP, DINO, MAE, or ViT) via cosine similarity on decoded code vectors. This distills semantic knowledge from the IU encoder into the discrete codebook, producing codes that carry clearer per-category semantics rather than ambiguous pixel-level information. The paper further proposes a simpler pipeline that clusters the frozen IU encoder's features to directly form a codebook, bypassing end-to-end tokenizer training entirely. Comprehensive experiments across AR and NAR proposal networks, multiple datasets, and tasks (class-conditional, unconditional, text-to-image) show that VQ-KD tokenizers with stronger IU capabilities consistently outperform VQGAN and FSQ, with VQ-KD_CLIP achieving 4.10 FID on ImageNet-1k using a 1.4B AR model.

## Results

On ImageNet-1k class-conditional generation, VQ-KD_CLIP achieves 11.78 FID_AR and 128.18 IS_AR with a GPT-2 Medium proposal network, outperforming VQGAN (24.11 FID_AR) and FSQ (40.17 FID_AR) by large margins. With a 1.4B-parameter AR proposal network, VQ-KD_CLIP reaches 4.10 FID on IN-1k at 256×256, surpassing all prior AR, NAR, and VQ-based diffusion baselines. On MS-COCO, VQ-KD_DINO achieves 17.55 FID_AR for unconditional generation and VQ-KD_CLIP achieves 11.17 FID_T2I for text-to-image, both substantially better than VQGAN (38.43 FID_AR, 24.11 FID_T2I) and FSQ (44.64 FID_AR, 23.36 FID_T2I).

## Baselines & Comparisons

VQ-KD tokenizers consistently outperform VQGAN [10] across all metrics: VQ-KD_CLIP achieves 11.78 FID_AR vs. VQGAN's 24.11, and 128.18 IS_AR vs. 39.52, while also reducing PPL from 116.75 to 53.73. FSQ [25] achieves 100% codebook usage but suffers from very high PPL (791.56) and worse FID (40.17 FID_AR) than VQ-KD_CLIP, indicating that high codebook usage alone does not guarantee good AR modelability. At the system level (Table 2), VQ-KD_CLIP with a 1.4B AR model achieves 4.10 FID, beating RQ-VAE [21] (8.71), ViT-VQGAN [47] (5.30), MoVQ [54] (8.78), MaskGIT [5] (6.18), LDM-8-G [33] (7.76), and CVQ-VAE [8] (6.87). Among VQ-KD variants, supervised teachers (CLIP, ViT) outperform unsupervised ones (DINO, MAE) on FID and IS, with VQ-KD_ViT achieving the best FID_NAR (8.45) and VQ-KD_CLIP the best IS_AR (128.18). The paper also shows that tokenizers with stronger IU capabilities require smaller proposal networks for competitive performance and are more robust to strong data augmentation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes VQ-KD from BEiT v2 [27], which was originally designed as a supervision signal for masked image modeling in image understanding, and repositions it as a tokenizer training objective for image generation. It departs from the dominant pixel-reconstruction paradigm (VQGAN [10], FSQ [25]) by arguing that distilling semantic knowledge from pretrained IU encoders (CLIP, DINO, MAE, ViT) into the tokenizer's codebook is more beneficial for downstream generation than faithfully reconstructing pixels. The paper also introduces a simpler clustering-based pipeline that directly converts frozen IU encoders into tokenizers, extending the idea that semantic structure in the codebook is the key driver of generation quality.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that IU-trained tokenizers substantially outperform pixel-reconstruction tokenizers (4.10 vs 15.78 FID at system level) challenges the long-standing assumption that pixel fidelity in the tokenizer is the primary determinant of IG quality, and opens a new direction of leveraging IU research for IG. Practically, the clustering pipeline offers a low-cost way to create strong tokenizers without end-to-end training. Limitations include potential degradation of fine visual details (problematic for editing tasks) and the fact that the decoder still relies on pixel-level reconstruction, so the benefit is specifically in the semantic organization of the codebook rather than eliminating the need for pixel modeling entirely. An open question is whether IU encoders can be co-designed with IG objectives to push performance further.
