## Problem

Existing discrete generative models either rely on stochastic Markov chain jumps that discard the continuous structure of categorical distributions, or operate on the probability simplex/logit space with ad hoc geometric assumptions (e.g., Euclidean interpolation, Dirichlet priors) that fail to capture the true intrinsic geometry of the statistical manifold. Additionally, likelihood in these models is typically only available through variational bounds rather than exact computation, and strong prior assumptions limit the complexity of patterns the models can learn.

## Input / Output

The method operates on the manifold of probability measures: each data point is a categorical distribution (a row of a matrix X ∈ [0,1]^{D×n} summing to 1), and the model learns a distribution over such measures. Prior noise is sampled uniformly from the (n−1)-simplex, and for conditional tasks (e.g., promoter design) transcription signals are concatenated as additional input to the vector field predictor. The output is a full categorical distribution from which discrete samples are drawn; exact NLL is computed via the Riemannian continuity equation, and for one-hot (boundary) targets a variational lower bound over a small neighborhood is used to obtain finite values.

## Method

SFM equips the statistical manifold of categorical distributions with a Riemannian structure via the Fisher information metric and learns a time-dependent vector field that follows geodesics (shortest paths) between noise and target distributions. A key practical innovation is a diffeomorphism π: μ_i → √μ_i that maps the simplex to the positive orthant of a unit sphere, where the geodesic distance and inner product are well-defined even at the boundary, resolving numerical instability. Training regresses a neural network vector field against the closed-form conditional vector field along spherical geodesics (using exponential/logarithm maps on S^{n−1}), optionally with optimal transport pairing based on the Fisher-Rao distance. The framework yields exact likelihood via the Riemannian divergence (estimated with Hutchinson's trace estimator) and admits an interpretation as following the steepest descent direction of the natural gradient, connecting flow matching to information geometry and second-order optimization.

## Results

On Binarized MNIST, SFM w/ OT achieves the best NLL of -1.687±0.020 and FID of 4.62, outperforming all diffusion and flow baselines. On Text8, SFM w/o OT achieves 1.386±0.033 BPC, the best among non-autoregressive flow/diffusion baselines (second only to autoregressive models like Transformer XL at 1.08). On the human promoter design task, SFM w/o OT achieves the lowest SP-MSE of 0.0258, beating DirichletFM (0.0269) and DDSM (0.0334). The Swiss roll toy example demonstrates that SFM captures complex manifold geometry that DirichletFM and DDSM fail to model due to their strong Dirichlet prior assumptions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SFM consistently outperforms all flow-matching and diffusion baselines on Binarized MNIST in FID (4.62 vs. 5.91 for LinearFM, 7.79 for DDSM, 34.42 for DFM, 67.36 for D3PM, and 77.35 for DirichletFM), with the notable exception that D3PM and DFM report discrete NLLs in parentheses that are not directly comparable to SFM's continuous NLL. On Text8, SFM (1.386 BPC) beats D3PM-absorb (1.47), BFN (1.41), MultiFlow (1.41), and Argmax Flow (1.80), but loses to SEDD-absorb (1.32) and autoregressive models (Discrete Flow 1.23, Transformer XL 1.08); the authors note that BPC is an unfavorable metric for SFM since it is not directly optimized during training. On promoter design, SFM w/o OT (0.0258 SP-MSE) beats DirichletFM (0.0269), DDSM (0.0334), D3PM-uniform (0.0375), and both Bit-Diffusion variants (0.0395, 0.0414). The comparison with LinearFM is particularly instructive: SFM's geometric advantage is clear on MNIST (NLL -1.687 vs. 0.622) and Text8 (BPC 1.386 vs. 1.651), confirming that following Fisher-Rao geodesics rather than Euclidean straight lines on the simplex yields substantially better generation. Optimal transport provides a modest but consistent improvement on MNIST (NLL -1.687 vs. -1.631) but is slightly suboptimal on the conditional promoter task, suggesting that distance-based pairing can discard useful conditional information.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the conditional flow matching (CFM) framework [38] and its Riemannian generalization [14] to the specific setting of the statistical manifold of categorical distributions, using the Fisher information metric from information geometry [51] to define the intrinsic Riemannian structure. It departs from prior discrete generative models in two key ways: (1) instead of Markov chain-based stochastic jumps (D3PM, SEDD, MultiFlow) or ad hoc geometric assumptions on the simplex/logit space (DirichletFM, DDSM, Argmax Flow), SFM follows true geodesics derived from the Fisher metric; (2) it introduces a diffeomorphism to the positive orthant of the unit sphere to resolve numerical instability at the boundary, enabling stable training and exact likelihood calculation via the continuity equation—something unavailable in Markov chain-based approaches that rely on variational bounds.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SFM provides the first principled geometric framework for discrete generation that requires no strong prior assumptions on the data distribution, demonstrated by its ability to capture complex non-Dirichlet geometry (Swiss roll) where DirichletFM and DDSM fail. The exact likelihood calculation (as opposed to variational lower bounds) is a practical advantage for model comparison and evaluation. However, the method assumes independence between classes (limiting applicability to ordinal data like CIFAR-10), and the iterative ODE-based sampling cannot modify input size, constraining it relative to autoregressive models. Open questions include extension to non-discrete generative tasks targeting probability distributions and whether the geometric advantage persists at larger scales or with more complex dependency structures.
