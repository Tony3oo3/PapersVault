## Problem

Classical algorithms for market equilibrium computation, such as proportional response (PR) dynamics, incur O(mn) cost per iteration, creating a scalability bottleneck for Internet-scale markets with tens of thousands of buyers and goods. It remains unclear whether further classical speedups beyond constant factors are possible. This paper provides the first quantum algorithm for market equilibrium computation that achieves sub-linear runtime in the product of buyers and goods.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240347/cropped_page8_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The algorithm takes quantum query access (via QRAM) to the budget vector B ∈ S^n and value matrix v ∈ M_{n×m}(I_+), along with a timestep limit T and error parameters ε_p, ε_ν. Rather than outputting the full O(mn) bid matrix, it produces quantum query access and ℓ1 sample access to the equilibrium bid values, with the solution encoded in QRAM-stored product vectors Π_p and Π_ν that allow on-the-fly reconstruction of individual bid entries by index. This output format is analogous to the state-preparation model used in quantum recommendation systems and linear system solvers.

## Method

The core contribution is a faulty proportional response (FPR) dynamics that tolerates multiplicative errors in price and utility estimates, paired with a quantum algorithm that uses quantum amplitude estimation (QAE) to compute ℓ1 norms (prices) and inner products (utilities) in O(√n/ε) and O(√m/ε) time respectively, instead of O(n) and O(m). The key architectural insight is that the full bid matrix is never stored; instead, cumulative product vectors Π_p^(t) and Π_ν^(t) are maintained in O(m+n) QRAM space, and individual bid entries are reconstructed on-the-fly via quantum arithmetic circuits in O(polylog(tmn)) time. The algorithm tracks the best iteration using a classical estimator of the EG objective and returns the corresponding product vectors, from which query and sample access to the equilibrium can be constructed. This yields a total runtime of Õ(T²√(mn·max(m,n))/ε²) with O(m+n) quantum memory, compared to Õ(mn/ε) classical runtime with O(mn) memory.

## Results

The quantum algorithm achieves a runtime of Õ(√(mn·max(m,n))/ε²) compared to Õ(mn/ε) for classical PR dynamics, providing a quadratic speedup in the smaller of the two dimensions (min(m,n)). Memory is reduced from O(mn) to O(m+n) QRAM space, though the algorithm requires 2× more iterations (2log(m)/ε vs. log(m)/ε) and has a worse additive-error dependence (1/ε² vs. 1/ε). In numerical simulations with n = m = 16384, the quantum algorithm converges significantly faster than PR dynamics when measured in total queries, and both outperform projected gradient descent in the mid-accuracy regime.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against classical PR dynamics (Wu & Zhang, STOC 2007), the quantum algorithm wins on per-iteration cost and memory: it reduces the per-iteration cost from O(mn) to Õ(√(mn·max(m,n))) via quantum norm and inner-product estimation, and reduces memory from O(mn) to O(m+n). However, it trades this for a 2× increase in iterations and a worse ε-dependence (1/ε² vs. 1/ε), so the net speedup is quadratic only in the smaller dimension. In the n = m = 16384 simulation, the quantum algorithm (green curve) converges to lower objective values than PR dynamics (blue curve) at the same query budget across both uniform and Gaussian value distributions. Against projected gradient descent (Gao & Kroer, NeurIPS 2020), both PR dynamics and the quantum algorithm outperform PGD in the mid-accuracy, low-iteration regime (Figure 1a), consistent with the empirical findings of Gao and Kroer that first-order methods with linear convergence only surpass PR-type dynamics at higher accuracy levels.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the proportional response (PR) dynamics of Wu and Zhang (2007) and Zhang (2011) as the base iterative method for Fisher market equilibrium, and on the Eisenberg-Gale and Shmyrev convex program formulations. The key conceptual departure is the introduction of the Faulty Proportional Response (FPR) dynamics, which tolerates multiplicative estimation errors in price and utility, thereby enabling the use of quantum amplitude estimation (QAE) subroutines for ℓ₁-norm and inner-product computation. The quantum algorithm also relies on QRAM for data access and positions itself against classical alternatives including projected gradient descent (Gao & Kroer, 2020) and interior-point methods, while drawing on the broader quantum optimization literature (quantum LP/SDP solvers, quantum recommendation systems) for its output model of quantum query and sample access.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first quantum algorithm to achieve sub-linear scaling in the product of buyers and goods for market equilibrium computation, providing a quadratic speedup in the smaller dimension. However, practical adoption is constrained by the need for QRAM and fault-tolerant QAE hardware, and the output is quantum query/sample access rather than the full bid matrix, which limits direct usability in classical downstream pipelines. Open questions include whether the FPR dynamics framework can be dequantized via sampling-based techniques, whether quasi-linear utility extensions preserve the speedup, and whether the low-rank structure of value matrices can be further exploited. The paper also notes that projected gradient descent, despite its superior classical convergence rate, may not admit comparable quantum speedups due to the lack of quantum advantages for simplex projections.
