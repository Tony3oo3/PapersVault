## Problem

Existing loop invariant generation methods—whether traditional provers, reinforcement learning approaches, or LLM-based systems—are restricted to numerical property verification and cannot handle programs involving complex data structures (linked lists, trees, hash tables) and memory manipulations. The paper demonstrates this gap by constructing a benchmark (LIG-MM) of 312 memory-manipulation programs on which even GPT-4 achieves less than 15% pass rate, showing that shape analysis and memory safety verification via loop invariants remains largely unsolved.

## Input / Output

The method takes a C program containing loops over pointer-based data structures, along with user-defined separation logic predicates (e.g., listrep, lseg, dlistrep) and the loop's precondition and condition. Symbolic execution first transforms these into a sequence of separation logic assertions (S0, S1, S2, …) by unrolling the loop body multiple times. The output is a loop invariant expressed as a disjunction of separation logic assertions, validated by an entailment solver that checks the three Hoare-logic properties (precondition implies invariant, invariant preserved by body, invariant implies postcondition).

## Method

LLM-SE fine-tunes a 350M-parameter CodeGen model via a self-supervised predicate-recovery task: data structure predicates are recursively split according to their inductive definitions into unfolded expressions, then augmented with noise/taboo terms and mixed via separating conjunction (*) and disjunction (||) to produce synthetic separation logic assertions; the LLM learns to recover the original predicate from these assertions. At inference time, an interactive loop feeds the symbolic-execution assertion sequence to the LLM, which proposes a separating conjunct; an entailment solver partitions assertions into those entailed by the conjunct (succ_I) and those not (fail_I), and the process recurses on each subset until all terms are covered. A SAT-based pick algorithm then selects a minimal equivalent set of assertions to form the final invariant. The framework extends to nested loops by recursively computing inner-loop invariants before symbolically executing the outer loop body.

## Results

On the LIG-MM benchmark (312 programs with memory manipulation), LLM-SE achieves a Pass Rate@1 of 38.78% and Pass Rate@8 of 42.95%, substantially outperforming all baselines. The closest competitors, SLING and GPT-4, achieve only 12.82% and 12.50% respectively at Pass Rate@1, while AutoSpec (designed for numerical programs) scores 0.00% across all attempts. The gap between LLM-SE and the best baseline is approximately 26 percentage points at Pass Rate@1.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LLM-SE decisively outperforms all three baselines on LIG-MM. Against SLING (the state-of-the-art separation logic invariant tool), LLM-SE achieves 38.78% vs. 12.82% at Pass Rate@1 and 42.95% vs. 21.79% at Pass Rate@8, roughly tripling the single-attempt success rate. Against GPT-4 (used with a prompt containing predicate definitions), LLM-SE leads by 26.28 points at Pass Rate@1 (38.78% vs. 12.50%) and by 25.27 points at Pass Rate@8 (42.95% vs. 17.68%). AutoSpec, an LLM-based method designed specifically for numerical property verification, completely fails on LIG-MM with 0.00% pass rate, confirming that numerical-program techniques do not transfer to memory-manipulation programs. The paper notes that baseline pass rates may be slightly inflated because invalid-format outputs from GPT-4 and SLING were manually re-checked for correctness.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of separation logic-based program verification and LLMs. Prior loop invariant generation work—whether traditional (SLING, LOOPINVGEN, llinva) or ML-based (CODE2INV, CLN2INV, AutoSpec)—has been restricted to numerical programs with scalar variables, neglecting shape analysis and memory safety. The paper repurposes the self-supervised learning paradigm (split-and-recover predicates) to address the labeled-data scarcity that plagues invariant generation, and combines a fine-tuned CodeGen model with symbolic execution in an interactive loop, departing from both the RL-on-target-programs approach of CODE2INV and the prompt-only approach of GPT-4-based baselines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The LIG-MM benchmark (312 programs from Linux Kernel, GlibC, LiteOS, Zephyr, SV-COMP, and course homework) fills a concrete gap: no prior benchmark tested loop invariant generation on programs with real data structures and multi-level pointers. The stark failure of GPT-4 (12.5%) and AutoSpec (0%) on this benchmark quantifies how far numerical-invariant methods are from handling memory manipulation. However, LLM-SE's 42.95% pass rate @8 remains far from practical deployment, and the authors acknowledge limitations in scalability, edge-case coverage, and computational overhead of symbolic execution. Open questions include whether larger LLMs or more sophisticated SE techniques can close the remaining gap, and whether the self-supervised predicate-recovery task generalizes to arbitrary user-defined data structures without manual predicate definitions.
