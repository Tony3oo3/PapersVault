## Problem

Despite transformers' empirical success, two fundamental questions remain largely unsolved: how to interpret task-relevant structure in learned weights, and which architectural features drive generalization. The interplay between attention paths—specific sequences of heads across layers—has been inaccessible to theory because prior analyses either use single-head or single-layer models where paths are undefined, or work in the infinite-width Gaussian process limit where hidden weights remain statistically independent after learning, erasing all path-path correlations.

## Input / Output

The network consumes a sequence of T tokens (each N₀-dimensional), either as noisy state representations in a synthetic Hidden Markov Chain task or as image patches with additive label and positional encodings in a one-shot in-context classification setup. A key architectural constraint is that attention at every layer is computed from the bare input x rather than the layer's processed output, and query/key weights are held fixed while only value, input-projection, and readout weights are learned. The theory produces the Bayesian mean predictor E[f*], its variance, and—centrally—an order parameter U of size H^L × H^L whose entries encode the learned overlap between effective weights along every pair of attention paths.

## Method

The paper develops a statistical mechanics theory for a deep multi-head self-attention network (L layers, H heads) that is linear in value weights, using the back-propagating kernel renormalization (BPKR) technique in the finite-width thermodynamic limit (N, P → ∞ with P/N = α > 0). The central result expresses the mean predictor as kernel regression where the kernel is a weighted sum over all H^{2L} path-path kernels C_{ππ'}, with weights given by an order parameter U determined by minimizing an action S that combines a Gaussian-prior entropy term and a data-dependent energy term. Beyond the GP limit (α > 0), the energy term drives task-relevant kernel combination: U develops non-uniform diagonal entries (suppressing adversarial paths, enhancing useful ones) and off-diagonal entries (enabling cross-path kernels that correlate effective weights across paths), both of which improve kernel-task alignment and generalization. The order parameter has a direct weight interpretation—U^{ππ'} = (1/N)⟨V^{eff,π}·V^{eff,π'ᵀ}⟩—allowing qualitative transfer to gradient-descent-trained networks, demonstrated via a head-pruning application that removes 25% of parameters with minimal accuracy loss on Omniglot and FashionMNIST.

## Results

On the synthetic Hidden Markov Chain task, the finite-width Bayesian network achieves ~94% classification accuracy (matching the best single 'good path'), substantially outperforming the GP limit which suffers from unsuppressed adversarial paths. On the one-shot image classification task (Omniglot training, 600 examples), the finite-width regime achieves 83.8% in-distribution accuracy on Omniglot and 86.6% out-of-distribution on FashionMNIST, both exceeding the GP limit. The theory-guided head pruning removes up to 25% of network parameters (2 of 8 heads) with only marginal in-distribution accuracy loss (83.8% → 80.2%), while out-of-distribution FashionMNIST accuracy actually improves (86.6% → 88.9%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is between the finite-width regime (α > 0) and the GP/infinite-width limit (α → 0), rather than against external methods. In the GP limit, the order parameter U reduces to a diagonal matrix with equal entries, discarding all cross-path kernels and weighting same-path kernels uniformly; this leads to degraded performance because adversarial paths are not suppressed. In the finite-width regime, the learned U suppresses adversarial paths and introduces strong off-diagonal correlations (e.g., anti-correlating the 'good' and 'denoising' paths), yielding improved kernel-task alignment and higher accuracy. The theory's predictions for the order parameter structure transfer qualitatively to a gradient-descent-trained model (trained on 528k examples with all parameters free), where the empirical U computed from a single weight realization preserves the block-diagonal structure and sign pattern. The pruning application demonstrates that the two lowest-scoring heads (25% of parameters) can be removed with minimal in-distribution loss and even improved OOD performance, consistent with prior observations that a few specialized heads carry most of the network's function.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the back-propagating kernel renormalization (BPKR) framework from deep linear networks [Li & Sompolinsky, 2021] and gated networks [Li & Sompolinsky, 2022] to a deep multi-head self-attention architecture, introducing the concept of attention paths as the natural unit of kernel combination. It departs from the infinite-width/GP limit characterization of transformers [Hron et al., 2020; Lavie et al., 2024], where hidden weights remain statistically independent and path interplay is lost, by working in the finite-width thermodynamic limit (P/N = α > 0) where a non-scalar order parameter captures task-relevant correlations between paths. Prior theoretical studies of transformers have been limited to single-head, single-layer, or constrained-weight settings that preclude a full characterization of multi-path interplay.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first exact statistical-mechanics theory showing that transformers' generalization advantage over the GP limit arises from a task-relevant kernel combination mechanism: the network learns to suppress adversarial paths, enhance useful ones, and create cross-path correlations for denoising. The order parameter's direct interpretation in terms of effective weight magnitudes and correlations (Eq. 13) enables practical applications like head pruning (25% size reduction with minimal loss), and the qualitative transfer to gradient-descent-trained networks broadens relevance. Key limitations include linearity in value weights, attention computed from the bare input rather than processed activations, and fixed query/key weights; extending the theory to full nonlinear transformers and characterizing query/key learning dynamics remain open challenges.
