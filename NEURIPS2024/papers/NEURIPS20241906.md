## Problem

Reinforcement learning agents are typically trained to improve over a single lifetime, and existing generational methods (iterated policy distillation, expert iteration) rely on hand-crafted imitation objectives rather than implicit accumulation. This paper asks whether a balance between social learning and independent discovery can produce emergent cultural accumulation in RL, where agents sustain generational improvement without any explicit imitation loss.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241906/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes partial environment observations (egocentric 3×3 grid cells for navigation, digit sequences, or city coordinates) together with observations of a previous-generation agent acting in the same shared environment, and produces discrete actions (digit predictions, navigation moves, or city selections). In the in-context setting, network weights are frozen and only the S5/GRU internal state carries accumulated information across generations; in the in-weights setting, each generation is randomly re-initialized but observes the prior generation's best agent during its training run. The visibility of the previous generation is linearly annealed from 1 to 0 across trials or training steps, guaranteeing that agents learn to act independently by the end of each episode or run.

## Method

The paper proposes two complementary algorithms for cultural accumulation in RL. In-context accumulation modifies the RL² meta-RL framework by training with a noisy oracle whose visibility is annealed across trials (p_obs: 1→0), so agents learn to both imitate and independently improve; at evaluation, the oracle is replaced by the best previous-generation agent whose internal state is reset between trials, and the best agent per generation is selected by final-trial reward. In-weights accumulation trains successive generations with random re-initialization, where each new generation observes the previous generation's best agent in the shared environment with visibility linearly annealed over the training run. Both approaches use standard PPO with S5 state-space memory and no explicit imitation loss—accumulation emerges purely from reward maximization. The critical design choice is balancing social learning against independent discovery: experiments show that too-accurate oracles cause over-reliance on imitation while too-noisy oracles prevent agents from acquiring useful social learning skills, with an intermediate noise level yielding the strongest accumulation.

## Results

Both in-context and in-weights cultural accumulation consistently outperform single-lifetime RL² baselines across all three environments (Memory Sequence, Goal Sequence, TSP). For in-weights accumulation, a single generation on Memory Sequence outperforms single-lifetime training equivalent to 5 complete generations, Goal Sequence accumulation exceeds a single agent trained for the duration of 4 accumulation steps, and TSP accumulation surpasses a 5-generation single-lifetime baseline after just 2 generations. In-context accumulation also surpasses RL² baselines on all tasks, with agents even exceeding the performance of the noisy oracles used during training when evaluated on new sequences. Combining in-weights accumulation with network resets compounds improvements, yielding higher returns than either method alone.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is RL² (Duan et al., 2016), trained as a single-lifetime agent on episodes equivalent to either one generation or all cumulative generations. The paper's cultural accumulation methods win against RL² on all three environments in both in-context and in-weights settings. For in-weights accumulation, the advantage is particularly stark: one generation of Memory Sequence accumulation matches five generations of continuous RL² training, and two generations of TSP accumulation match five generations of RL². The in-context setting also shows that the RL² baseline trained on longer episodes (matching total cumulative experience) fails to make meaningful learning progress on TSP, while the accumulating agents continue to improve. The paper further notes that in-weights accumulation combined with network resets (Nikishin et al., 2022) compounds gains beyond either technique independently, addressing the primacy bias that causes single-lifetime learners to converge prematurely.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the meta-RL framework of RL² (Duan et al., 2016) and the POMDP formulation of Ni et al. (2021) by modifying the training loop to include a noisy oracle whose visibility is annealed, enabling agents to learn both social learning and independent exploration. It builds on emergent social learning in multi-agent RL (Ndousse et al., 2021; Bhoopchand et al., 2023), but departs from prior generational methods like iterated policy distillation (Schmitt et al., 2018; Stooke et al., 2022) by achieving accumulation implicitly through observation of prior-generation behavior in shared environments rather than through explicit imitation losses or access to past policies. The key conceptual departure is chaining multiple generations of social learning (rather than a single expert-to-student transfer) and framing the result as cultural accumulation analogous to knowledge and skill transmission in biological populations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to demonstrate emergent cultural accumulation in RL under a general, domain-agnostic formulation, providing a concrete metric (multi-generation return exceeding single-lifetime return with equal budget) and two complementary mechanisms (in-context for knowledge, in-weights for skills). The finding that oracle accuracy follows an inverted-U relationship—where overly accurate oracles impede independent discovery—challenges the standard assumption in imitation learning that better demonstrations always help, and suggests a principled trade-off between social learning and exploration. Open questions include whether these mechanisms scale to more complex, open-ended tasks, whether the linear annealing of observation probability can be replaced by learned autocurricula, and how the approach behaves under heterogeneous or cooperative incentive structures. The paper also raises the broader question of whether self-improving generational loops in artificial agents warrant safety consideration.
