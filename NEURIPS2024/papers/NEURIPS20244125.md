## Problem

Semi-structured networks (SSNs) combine interpretable additive components with deep neural networks, but existing SSN architectures cannot be naively extended to functional data: identifiability of the additive part is unclear in the presence of a deep network, and standard functional regression implementations (additive models, boosting) scale poorly with the number of observations, predictors, and time points. This paper addresses both the identifiability and scalability gaps to enable interpretable, scalable function-on-function regression.

## Input / Output

The method consumes J functional predictor curves, each discretized on R equidistant time points (e.g., 101 gait-cycle samples from inertial sensors), and produces a single functional response curve discretized on Q time points (e.g., joint moment profiles over the same gait cycle). Both inputs and outputs are treated as elements of L² spaces, with integrals approximated via trapezoidal Riemann weights. In the biomechanical application, the 27 predictors are 3D joint angles, velocities, and accelerations from bilateral ankle, knee, and hip, while the 12 outcomes are corresponding joint moments.

## Method

The proposed semi-structured functional network (SSFNN) decomposes the prediction into an interpretable function-on-function regression (FFR) part λ⁺(t) = Σ_j ∫ w_j(s,t) x_j(s) ds, where each weight surface w_j is expanded in a tensor-product basis ψ(t)ᵀΘ_j φ_j(s), and a deep FNN part λ⁻(t) (e.g., InceptionTime), combined via μ(t) = τ(λ⁺(t) + λ⁻(t)). To resolve the identifiability problem—where the deep network can absorb all linear structure from λ⁺—the paper extends post-hoc orthogonalization (PHO) to functional data by rewriting λ⁺ as a linear combination of Kronecker-product basis vectors Ω_j(t)θ_j and projecting the deep network predictions onto the orthogonal complement of the interpretable basis. For scalability, the implementation avoids forming the full (n·Q)×(ΣK_j·U) design matrix by using array computations that multiply basis matrices individually with the weight matrix Θ, and by recycling shared basis evaluations across predictors that share the same time domain.

## Results

On the Fukuchi and Liew running biomechanics dataset (n=490, J=27 functional predictors), the semi-structured FNN (SSFNN) using all features achieves a median relative RMSE of 0.25 (MAD 0.04), outperforming the additive model (0.36), the deep-only FNN (0.31), and boosting (0.30). On the larger Carmago dataset (n=21,787, J=24), the semi-structured model achieves a mean relative MSE difference of 0.955 (SD 0.030), compared to 0.923 for the deep-only network and 0.872 for the structured-only FFR. In simulation, all methods (additive model, boosting, neural network) show comparable weight-surface estimation and prediction performance, with the neural network variant performing slightly better under low signal-to-noise ratio (SNR=0.1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the functional additive model of Scheipl et al. [53], the SSFNN achieves a substantially lower relative RMSE (0.25 vs. 0.36 on Fukuchi; the additive model cannot handle all 27 features and must use a selected subset). Against fdboost [5], the SSFNN with all features matches or slightly beats boosting (0.25 vs. 0.30 median rel. RMSE) while also providing an interpretable weight-surface component. Against the deep-only InceptionTime architecture from Liew et al. [32], the semi-structured model consistently outperforms the deep baseline: on Carmago the relative MSE difference improves from 0.923 to 0.955, and on Fukuchi the rel. RMSE drops from 0.31 to 0.25. On the air quality task, the non-linear FFR of Qi et al. [40] achieves a relative RMSE of 1.000 (i.e., near-perfect), and the SSFNN matches this (0.988–0.991) while the structured-only FNN slightly outperforms both on correlation (0.908 vs. 0.878), indicating the task is relatively easy and linear structure suffices.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the semi-structured network (SSN) framework of Rügamer et al. (2023) from scalar to functional data, combining a function-on-function regression (FFR) interpretable component with a deep functional neural network (FNN) component. The identifiability mechanism is a direct functional extension of post-hoc orthogonalization (PHO) from Rügamer (ICML 2023), while the interpretable part inherits the weight-surface formulation from the general FFR framework of Greven & Scheipl (2017). The deep component leverages established time-series architectures (InceptionTime) and the overall design parallels the wide-and-deep paradigm, but is the first to apply it in the function-on-function regression setting with basis-function encoders/decoders shared between the two branches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first interpretable semi-structured modeling approach for functional data, addressing a genuine gap where existing FFR methods lack expressivity and deep FNNs lack interpretability. The scalability improvements (array computation, basis recycling) make the method practical for large sensor datasets (e.g., 21,787 observations × 101 time points × 24 predictors) where classical FFR implementations become infeasible. Open questions include incorporating sparsity penalties for high-dimensional functional predictors, extending to nonlinear FFR components (which the authors note is challenging to interpret), and whether the PHO correction remains stable under distribution shift or very small sample sizes.
