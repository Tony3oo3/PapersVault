## Problem

Vision transformers achieve strong performance through softmax self-attention, but the O(N²d) quadratic complexity in the number of patches limits real-world deployment. Existing linear attention methods reduce this to O(Nd²) by decomposing the similarity function into kernel embeddings, yet they sacrifice accuracy and often require knowledge distillation or high-order attention residuals to close the performance gap, which severely inflates GPU memory during training.

## Input / Output

The model consumes standard RGB images (224×224 for ImageNet-1k, variable resolution for COCO and ADE20K) that are split into non-overlapping patches and linearly projected into query, key, and value embeddings. For image classification it outputs a 1000-way class label; for object detection (Mask R-CNN framework) it produces bounding boxes with class scores; and for semantic segmentation (UperNet framework) it yields pixel-level masks over 150 categories. An absolute positional embedding is added to the key matrix before the linear attention computation.

## Method

QT-ViT approximates the softmax similarity function exp(q·k/√d) with a second-order Taylor expansion, yielding (⟨φ(q),φ(k)⟩²+1)/2 where φ(x)=[x/d^{1/4}, 1]. The quadratic term is decomposed into separate kernel embeddings via the Kronecker product, giving a kernel φ̂(x)=[(1/√2)vec(φ(x)⊗φ(x)), 1/√2] that enables the standard linear-attention reassociation trick. To avoid the O(Nd³) cost of the full d²-dimensional Kronecker product, the paper proposes a compact approximation that retains only the self-multiplication terms {x_i²} (plus optional linear and constant terms with learnable scalars α, β, γ), reducing complexity to O(Nd²) while preserving most of the quadratic information. The resulting QT-ViT models are built on the EfficientViT architecture with a GELU(BN(v)) non-linear shortcut, and require no knowledge distillation or masked softmax outputs during training, unlike first-order Taylor or angular-kernel baselines.

## Results

On ImageNet-1k, the proposed QT-ViTs achieve state-of-the-art accuracy-efficiency trade-offs across all FLOPs ranges. QT-ViT-1 reaches 79.6% top-1 accuracy at 0.52G FLOPs, QT-ViT-4 achieves 84.7% top-1 at 5.26G FLOPs, and QT-ViT-6 attains 86.0% top-1 at 27.6G FLOPs. On COCO 2017 object detection, QT-ViT-3 achieves 42.6 mAP (vs. 42.3 for EfficientViT-B3), and on ADE20K semantic segmentation, QT-ViT-3 reaches 38.5 mIoU (vs. 38.0 for EfficientViT-B3).

## Baselines & Comparisons

QT-ViT consistently outperforms EfficientViT (the primary baseline architecture) across all six model sizes by 0.1–0.4% top-1 accuracy at roughly identical FLOPs and parameter counts. Against sparse-attention ViTs, QT-ViT-4 achieves 84.7% top-1 with 5.26G FLOPs, surpassing CSWin-B (84.2%, 15.0G) by 0.5% while using 64.9% fewer FLOPs, and beating Swin-T (81.3%, 4.5G) by 2.6%. Compared to CNN baselines, QT-ViT-3 exceeds ConvNeXt-T by 1.8% top-1 with 11.8% fewer FLOPs. Against other linear-attention methods, QT-ViT-1 (79.6%) outperforms ViTALiTy's first-order Taylor expansion (71.9%) by a large margin without requiring knowledge distillation or high-order residuals. In the kernel ablation (Table 2), the quadratic Taylor expansion kernel beats ReLU (EfficientViT) by 0.2%, cosine (Hydra) by 0.5%, mean (PolyNL) by 0.8%, and the angular kernel (Castling-ViT) by 0.5%, while approaching the full softmax baseline (79.8%) within just 0.2%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the linear attention line of work initiated by EfficientViT (ReLU kernel) and ViTALiTy (first-order Taylor expansion) by replacing the similarity function with a second-order Taylor expansion. The key technical departure is the use of the Kronecker product to decompose the quadratic term into separable kernel embeddings, followed by a compact approximation that retains only self-multiplication terms to reduce complexity from O(Nd³) to O(Nd²). Unlike ViTALiTy and Castling-ViT, which require knowledge distillation or high-order attention residuals to close the performance gap, QT-ViT achieves competitive accuracy without these memory-intensive training aids, while also outperforming other kernel choices (cosine, mean, sigmoid/softmax) in ablation studies.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

QT-ViT establishes a new Pareto-front in accuracy-speed trade-offs for efficient ViTs, consistently outperforming EfficientViT by 0.1–0.4% across six model sizes without additional training overhead. The practical significance lies in eliminating the need for knowledge distillation or masked softmax outputs during training, which reduces GPU memory and makes linear-attention ViTs more accessible for large-scale training. Open questions include whether the learnable trade-off parameters (α, β, γ) and the self-multiplication approximation generalize to other attention variants or modalities, and whether the modest gains over first-order expansion persist at substantially larger scales or with different positional encoding schemes.
