## Problem

Existing KG reasoning models are trained separately for each KG and cannot generalize to unseen entities, relations, or entirely new KGs due to unshared entity and relation vocabularies. While inductive methods handle new entities and fully-inductive methods handle new relations, they remain confined to a single KG. The paper asks whether a single pre-trained model can perform universal reasoning across arbitrary KGs using only a few in-context examples, without finetuning.

## Input / Output

The model takes a query fact (s, q, ?), the host KG, and M randomly sampled example facts for the query relation q. From these examples it constructs prompt graphs (subgraphs of 1-hop neighbors and k-hop paths around the example subject and object), which are tokenized via a unified scheme mapping entities to [dist-to-subject, dist-to-object] pairs and relations to a binary query-match flag. The output is a score for every entity in the KG, from which the missing entity is ranked; the model is pre-trained on source KGs and then frozen, so no target-KG-specific parameters are updated at inference time.

## Method

KG-ICL introduces a prompt graph centered on an example fact of the query relation, combining entity neighborhoods and relational paths to capture local context that global relation graphs miss. A unified tokenizer maps entities and relations across all KGs to a small set of predefined tokens based on shortest-path distances to the example subject/object and query-relation membership, making prompt graphs from different KGs comparable in 'the same language.' Two message passing networks are then employed: a prompt encoder with entity-centric and relation-centric aggregation (treating relations as special nodes updated from containing facts) that reads out relation representations as prompts, and a KG reasoner with hop-by-hop propagation that initializes relation embeddings from the prompt readout and the subject entity from the query relation representation, scoring all candidates after N layers. The model is pre-trained with multi-class log-loss over a mixture of source KGs and requires only ~89k parameters, converging in under six hours.

## Results

On 43 KGs across three reasoning settings, KG-ICL (pre-train only, no finetuning) achieves an average MRR of 0.442 and H@10 of 0.606, outperforming ULTRA pre-train (0.396 / 0.557) and supervised SOTA (0.351 / 0.493). The gains are most dramatic in the fully-inductive setting, where KG-ICL pre-train reaches MRR 0.439 versus 0.352 for ULTRA and 0.210 for supervised SOTA. With 5-epoch finetuning on the target dataset, KG-ICL further improves to an average MRR of 0.473 and H@10 of 0.638, setting the best result across all settings. The model uses only 89k parameters and requires just 5 example facts per query relation at inference time.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ULTRA [6] (the primary pre-training baseline), KG-ICL pre-train wins on all three settings: +4.1 MRR on inductive (0.554 vs 0.513), +8.7 MRR on fully-inductive (0.439 vs 0.352), and +1.7 MRR on transductive (0.346 vs 0.329); the finetuned version extends these margins further. Against InGram [5], the SOTA for fully-inductive reasoning, KG-ICL pre-train outperforms it on all 13 fully-inductive datasets (e.g., NL-0: 0.557 vs 0.309; WK-25: 0.423 vs 0.107), attributed to prompt graphs capturing local relation context better than InGram's global relation graph. Against supervised SOTA models (NBFNet [4], A*Net [21], NodePiece [19], RED-GNN [3], etc.), KG-ICL pre-train wins on 10/14 inductive, all 13/13 fully-inductive, and 7/16 transductive datasets; the transductive gap is smaller because supervised models directly optimize on test-set entities and relations. In the robustness analysis (Figure 4), KG-ICL maintains stable MRR across relation subgroups with varying training sample counts, whereas RED-GNN [3] shows a clear performance decline as training samples decrease, confirming the benefit of the unified tokenizer for cross-relation knowledge transfer.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends GraIL's [1] query-conditioned subgraph reasoning and entity labeling to relations via a unified tokenizer, and adapts the in-context learning paradigm from NLP (GPT-style few-shot prompting) to knowledge graph reasoning. It departs from ULTRA [6], the closest prior foundation model for KG reasoning, by replacing the 'pre-train then finetune' paradigm with 'pre-train then in-context learn'—requiring no target-dataset finetuning. It also contrasts with InGram [5], which uses a global relation graph to represent unseen relations; KG-ICL argues that local prompt graphs centered on example facts provide more discriminative, query-specific relation representations than global connectivity structures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

KG-ICL demonstrates that a 89k-parameter model pre-trained on three small KGs can achieve universal reasoning across 43 diverse KGs in transductive, inductive, and fully-inductive settings without any finetuning, offering a practical and efficient alternative to larger foundation models. The dramatic ablation (MRR dropping from 0.442 to 0.132 without prompt graphs) underscores that local contextual prompting is the critical mechanism for cross-KG knowledge transfer. Open questions include handling temporal or multi-relational facts, scaling to KGs with millions of relations, and extending in-context reasoning to downstream applications like recommender systems and question answering.
