## Problem

While heavy-tailed parameter distributions in SGD have been empirically observed and linked to generalization, prior theoretical work (Gurbuzbalaban et al., 2021) characterizes the tail index only implicitly through phase-transition equations. This paper asks whether one can derive fully explicit, quantitative upper and lower bounds on the tail index from a principled continuous-time approximation of SGD, and whether heavy tails can emerge even when local gradient noise is Gaussian.

## Input / Output

The analysis is conditional on a fixed design matrix A ∈ R^{n×d} and label vector b ∈ R^n, together with optimization hyperparameters (learning rate γ, batch size B, regularization δ). The output consists of explicit closed-form bounds η* and η_* on the asymptotic tail index that depend on the singular values of A, the hyperparameters, and the problem dimension r = rank(A). Unlike prior work assuming isotropic Gaussian data, the bounds are data-dependent through the singular value spectrum, and the tail index is lower-bounded by 1, excluding ultra-heavy-tailed regimes.

## Method

The paper analyzes homogenized SGD (hSGD), a diffusion approximation of SGD that uses the decoupling approximation C(x) ≈ (2/B)L(x)∇²L(x) for the gradient noise covariance. After a singular-value-based rescaling, hSGD becomes a system of coupled SDEs whose diffusion coefficients depend on the full state norm |Z_t|², in contrast to decoupled Pearson diffusions that depend only on the individual component. The key technical contribution is a convex stochastic order comparison (Theorem 3.1) showing the coupled hSGD dominates the decoupled Pearson diffusions in convex order, which immediately yields an explicit upper bound on the tail index from the known Pearson-type IV/V stationary distributions. A complementary lower bound is obtained via moment-stability conditions for SDEs (Li et al., 2019), requiring γ below a critical threshold. The paper further proves exponential convergence to the invariant distribution in 2-Wasserstein distance and validates all bounds numerically on synthetic and MNIST-based datasets, showing Student-t fits outperform the α-stable fits proposed in prior work.

## Results

The paper derives explicit upper and lower bounds on the asymptotic tail-index η of hSGD: η* = 1 + 2nB(λ₁²+δ)/(γλ₁⁴) (upper) and η* = 1 + 2nB(λ₁²+δ)/(γλ₁⁴) − Σᵢ₌₂ʳ λᵢ²/λ₁² (lower). In experiments on synthetic Gaussian data (n=2000, d=200), the empirical tail-index is sandwiched between η*=3.56 and η*=3.61. Kolmogorov-Smirnov tests confirm that a Student-t distribution fits the SGD parameter distribution (p-values 0.433–0.883, all > 0.05) while the α-stable distribution from Gurbuzbalaban et al. is rejected in all six settings (p-values 0.001–0.035, all < 0.05).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is against the α-stable distribution proposed by Gurbuzbalaban et al. (ICML 2021) as the surrogate for SGD parameter distributions. In all three datasets (synthetic Gaussian, raw MNIST pixels, and MNIST random features) and both model architectures (linear regression and a three-layer neural network), the Student-t distribution is accepted by the K-S test (p-values ranging from 0.208 to 0.883) while the α-stable distribution is consistently rejected (p-values from 0.001 to 0.035). The QQ-plots further show that the α-stable fit overestimates tail heaviness, particularly for the random feature model on real data, whereas the Student-t fit closely tracks the empirical ccdf including its tail. The paper also shows that the empirical ccdf is sandwiched between the theoretical upper and lower tail-index bounds (e.g., η* = 3.56 and η* = 3.61 for the synthetic dataset), validating the explicit quantitative bounds that Gurbuzbalaban et al. could only characterize implicitly through phase-transition analysis.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the homogenized SGD (hSGD) diffusion approximation introduced concurrently by Paquette et al. (2022) and Mori et al. (2022), and connects it to the well-studied class of Pearson diffusions (Forman and Sørensen, 2008) to obtain explicit tail-index bounds. It departs from Gurbuzbalaban et al. (2021), who analyzed SGD's heavy-tailed behavior directly through the stochastic recurrence and proposed α-stable distributions with only implicit tail-index characterization. The paper also challenges Simsekli et al. (2020), who argued that Brownian-motion-driven SDEs cannot accurately represent the observed heavy-tailed behavior of SGD, by showing that multiplicative (state-dependent) Gaussian noise in hSGD is sufficient to produce heavy tails.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The explicit, closed-form tail-index bounds (in terms of learning rate, batch size, regularization, and data matrix singular values) provide practitioners with a quantitative tool to predict and control the heaviness of SGD parameter distributions, which has been linked to generalization performance and escape from local minima. The finding that Student-t distributions fit empirical SGD iterates better than α-stable distributions (validated by KS tests across three datasets) offers a more tractable surrogate for downstream probabilistic modeling. However, the analysis is restricted to regularized linear regression with quadratic loss, and the lower bound on the tail-index is always above 1, so the framework cannot capture ultra-heavy tails; extending to non-linear models and tightening the hSGD-to-SGD gap remain open.
