## Problem

Gradient compression is widely used to reduce communication costs in distributed and federated learning, but all existing analyses assume with-replacement (SGD-style) sampling of stochastic gradients. Random Reshuffling (without-replacement sampling) is known to achieve faster convergence, yet no prior work has studied its interaction with gradient compression. This paper shows that naively combining the two (Q-RR) yields no improvement over compressed SGD because compression variance dominates the reshuffling variance, and then resolves this by compressing gradient differences instead of raw gradients.

## Input / Output

Each of M clients holds a local dataset of n samples (tabular features for logistic regression or 28×28 RGB images for CIFAR-10) and an initial model vector x₀ ∈ ℝᵈ. Clients apply an unbiased compression operator Q (e.g., Rand-k with ratio k/d ≈ 0.02–0.05) satisfying E[Q(x)] = x and E[‖Q(x)−x‖²] ≤ ω‖x‖² before communicating. The server aggregates compressed updates and broadcasts the new iterate; the final output is the trained parameter vector x_T after T communication rounds, evaluated by functional suboptimality or top-1 accuracy.

## Method

The paper introduces four algorithms in two pairs. Q-RR naively applies compression to each sampled gradient within a random reshuffling epoch, but its convergence rate contains an O(1/ε) term from compression variance that negates RR's O(1/√ε) advantage. DIANA-RR fixes this by maintaining n learnable shift-vectors per worker and compressing only the difference ∇f_m^{π_i}(x) − h_{t,m}^{π_i}, which eliminates the compression variance and recovers the superior O(1/√ε) rate. For the local-steps (federated) setting, Q-NASTYA similarly suffers from compression variance, while DIANA-NASTYA applies a single DIANA-style shift per client to the aggregated local gradient, again removing the O(1/ε) term. The key mechanism is that shifts track past gradients so the compressed residual has vanishing norm near the optimum, making the compression noise negligible in late-stage convergence.

## Results

The paper's primary results are communication complexity bounds (number of rounds to reach ε-accuracy) for distributed finite-sum optimization. The headline finding is that DIANA-RR achieves Õ(n(1+ω) + (1+ω/M)(L_max/μ̃) + σ_rad/√(εμ̃³)), eliminating the Õ(1/ε) term present in Q-RR, QSGD, and DIANA, making it superior for small ε. Similarly, DIANA-NASTYA achieves Õ(ω + (L_max/μ)(1+ω/M) + √(L_max/(εμ³))·√(ζ²+σ²/n)) for local-step methods. Empirically, on logistic regression (mushrooms, w8a, a9a) and ResNet-18/CIFAR-10, DIANA-RR consistently reaches the lowest functional sub-optimality, while Q-RR matches QSGD with no improvement from naive reshuffling+compression.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against QSGD, Q-RR provides no theoretical or empirical improvement: both have the same Õ(1/ε) dominating term, and experiments confirm identical convergence behavior on all datasets. DIANA-RR, however, strictly outperforms QSGD by eliminating the Õ(1/ε) term, replacing it with a Õ(1/√ε) term (analogous to how RR beats SGD). Against DIANA, DIANA-RR similarly removes the Õ(σ²/(Mμ²ε)) term, yielding a Õ(σ_rad/√(εμ̃³)) term that is superior for small ε; experiments on mushrooms, w8a, a9a, and ResNet-18/CIFAR-10 confirm DIANA-RR reaches lower sub-optimality than DIANA. Against FedCOM and FedPAQ (local-step baselines), Q-NASTYA shows comparable performance, while DIANA-NASTYA noticeably outperforms both across all logistic regression datasets. Against EF21-SGD (state-of-the-art for contractive compressors), DIANA-RR outperforms it in the strongly-convex setting with unbiased compressors like Rand-k. The paper also notes that FedCRR does not converge for large ω, while Q-NASTYA converges for any ω ≥ 0.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two well-studied but previously uncombined threads: distributed Random Reshuffling (FedRR, NASTYA) and gradient compression with variance reduction (DIANA, QSGD, FedPAQ, FedCOM). It builds directly on DIANA's control-variate mechanism for compressing gradient differences and on FedRR's without-replacement sampling framework, extending both to their joint setting. The key departure from prior work is the demonstration that naive composition (Q-RR) fails to inherit RR's convergence advantage under compression, necessitating DIANA-style shifts to recover the O(1/√ε) rate that RR provides over SGD's O(1/ε).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first theoretical analysis of distributed random reshuffling with gradient compression, closing a gap that practitioners have exploited empirically without understanding. The finding that compression variance negates RR's benefit unless explicitly controlled has direct implications for federated learning system design: simply adding RR to a compressed pipeline is insufficient. Limitations include theory restricted to strongly convex objectives (experiments on ResNet-18 are illustrative but not theoretically grounded), and DIANA-RR requires n shift-vectors per worker, a memory cost that DIANA-RR-1S partially addresses at some performance cost. Open questions include non-convex convergence guarantees and whether the n-shift requirement can be eliminated without rate degradation.
