## Problem

The Gromov-Wasserstein distance is a non-convex quadratic program that is NP-hard in general, and existing solvers (conditional gradient, entropic GW) either find only local optima or solve a different regularized objective. No prior tractable method provides a certificate of global optimality for the GW transportation plan, leaving practitioners without a principled way to verify whether a computed solution is truly optimal.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242230/cropped_page4_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes two discrete metric-measure spaces (C, α) and (D, β) with cost matrices of sizes m×m and n×n respectively, and produces a transportation plan π of size m×n together with a lower bound on the GW distance and an approximation ratio that certifies global optimality when it equals one. Unlike entropic GW, the formulation is valid for arbitrary cost tensors without requiring a decomposable loss structure, and the output is always a feasible transport plan in Π(α,β) with no rounding step needed. The SDP matrix variable P has dimension mn×mn, which limits practical applicability to moderate-sized instances (m, n up to ~20 with off-the-shelf solvers).

## Method

The paper proposes an SDP relaxation that starts from the standard Lasserre-style lifting of the GW quadratic program (introducing P = vec(π)vec(π)ᵀ and dropping the rank-one constraint) but shows this base relaxation is unbounded below. The key tightening step adds linear constraints that any rank-one P = vec(π)vec(π)ᵀ must satisfy: non-negativity P ≥ 0 and marginal consistency constraints P·vec(e_i 1_nᵀ) = α_i vec(π) and P·vec(1_m e_jᵀ) = β_j vec(π), which render the feasible region compact. The resulting (GW-SDP) is equivalent to the Lagrangian dual of the GW problem augmented with these redundant constraints, and strong duality holds by Slater's condition. When the optimal P has rank one, the duality gap vanishes and the approximation ratio in equation (4) equals one, yielding a polynomial-time certificate of global optimality. The approach is valid for all cost tensors and requires no rounding, distinguishing it from QAP relaxations that rely on orthogonality constraints invalid when m ≠ n.

## Results

On the 3D shape-matching dataset (72 objects, 7 classes), GW-SDP consistently returns the smallest GW distance across all five object-pair comparisons, e.g., 0.007416 for Elephant–Elephant versus 0.043879 (GW-CG) and 0.025688 (eGW-PPA). In the Gaussian matching experiment, the estimated approximation ratio (RHS of Eq. 4) is close to 1.0 in most instances, certifying global optimality of the SDP solution, while local solvers frequently return sub-optimal plans. Runtime scales as O((mn)³) due to the SDP matrix dimension: 0.24 s at n=6, 11.6 s at n=12, and 216 s at n=20, compared to sub-millisecond runtimes for GW-CG.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares GW-SDP against two baselines: GW-CG (conditional gradient / Frank-Wolfe solver from Vayer et al., 2019a, the default in PythonOT) and eGW-PPA (entropic GW with Sinkhorn projections from Peyré et al., 2016). GW-SDP wins decisively on all five shape-matching pairs, reducing the GW distance by 4–8× relative to GW-CG (e.g., 0.0074 vs 0.0439 for Elephant–Elephant) and by 2–3× relative to eGW-PPA. The closest margin is Cat–Cat, where GW-SDP (0.006549) edges out eGW-PPA (0.006757) by only ~3%, while GW-CG is 2.5× worse. In the Gaussian matching experiment, GW-SDP's approximation ratio is ≈1.0 in most runs, certifying global optimality, whereas GW-CG and eGW frequently return sub-optimal plans with higher objective values. The trade-off is computational: GW-SDP is orders of magnitude slower (216 s vs 0.0014 s at n=20), though the authors note that specialized solvers exploiting the low-rank structure of the optimal solution are a subject of ongoing work.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the standard SDP relaxation framework for quadratic programs (Bomze and de Klerk, 2002) and adapts it to the Gromov-Wasserstein distance, which is a non-convex QP. The key structural insight is borrowing linear coupling constraints (relating row/column sums of the quadratic variable P to the linear variable π) that were previously proposed for the Quadratic Assignment Problem (Zhao et al., 1998; Dym et al., 2017; Kezurer et al., 2015), while deliberately omitting the orthogonality constraints that are valid for permutation matrices in QAP but inapplicable to the general transport plans in GW. The paper further provides a clean Lagrangian duality interpretation, showing the relaxation is the dual of the GW problem augmented with redundant constraints, and proves strong duality via Slater's condition.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that the SDP relaxation frequently certifies global optimality of the GW transport plan (approximation ratio ≈ 1.0), especially when m = n, offering a polynomial-time algorithm with a proof of optimality for instances where the problem is otherwise NP-hard. However, the relaxation involves an mn × mn matrix variable, limiting applicability to moderate-sized instances with current off-the-shelf solvers; developing specialized solvers exploiting the low-rank structure of optimal solutions is identified as a critical open direction. A broader open question is whether the GW problem is genuinely intractable for all cost tensors or whether the structured tensors arising from actual metric spaces (satisfying triangle inequality) are inherently easier.
