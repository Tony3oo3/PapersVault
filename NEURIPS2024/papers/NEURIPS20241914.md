## Problem

State-of-the-art procedure learning methods rely on frame-to-frame mapping or assume monotonic alignment between video pairs, which breaks down when real-world sequences contain background frames, redundant frames, or non-monotonic action orderings. These methods either require extra processing steps (e.g., background removal) or additional modalities (gaze, depth, narration) to handle such irregularities, limiting scalability and generalization.

## Input / Output

The method consumes multiple unlabeled video clips of the same task (visual modality only), sampling 32 frames per video at 224×224 resolution with 2 stacked context frames along the temporal dimension. It produces 128-dimensional frame embeddings, per-frame key-step assignments via graph-cut segmentation, and a ranked list of key-step sequences derived from the frequency of observed orderings across videos. No text, gaze, depth, or optical flow inputs are required.

## Method

OPEL formulates cross-video frame alignment as an entropic optimal transport problem over a ResNet-50 encoder's Conv4c features (processed through 3D convolutions and projected to 128 dims), relaxing monotonicity assumptions by treating frames as samples from an unknown distribution. Two Laplace-distribution priors are injected into the transport matrix: an optimality prior centered on the OT-predicted best match and a temporal prior centered on the diagonal, combined with a dynamic weight φ that decays from 1.0 to 0.5 during training. A virtual frame row/column is appended to absorb background and redundant frames, while an inverse-difference-moment (IDM) regularizer and a KL-divergence constraint shape the transport plan. Training combines the regularized Sinkhorn OT loss with a C-IDM intra-video contrastive term and a novel inter-video contrastive loss that pulls OT-matched pairs together and pushes unmatched pairs apart, solved via 20 Sinkhorn-Knopp iterations. After embedding learning, frames are clustered into k key-steps using α-expansion graph-cut segmentation, and key-step ordering is determined by averaging normalized temporal positions within each cluster.

## Results

On the large-scale egocentric EgoProceL benchmark, OPEL achieves 22.4% (IoU) and 26.9% (F1) average improvement over the current SOTA (GPL-w/o BG). On third-person benchmarks (ProceL and CrossTask), OPEL obtains a 46.2% (F1) average enhancement over SOTA, reaching F1 scores of 34.9 and 35.1 respectively. OPEL also outperforms the multimodal STEPS model (which uses gaze and depth) on 4 out of 6 egocentric task groups while using only visual input.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OPEL outperforms all 13 baselines on the primary EgoProceL benchmark, with the closest competitor being GPL-w/o BG [2] (Bansal, WACV 2024), which OPEL surpasses by 4.8–18.5 F1 points across task groups. On third-person ProceL and CrossTask, OPEL (F1: 34.9, 35.1) dramatically exceeds the previous best STEPS [16] (F1: 24.9, 25.9) by ~10 points, and far surpasses earlier methods like Kukleva [32] (F1: 16.4, 15.3), Elhamifar [3] (F1: 14.0, 16.3), and Shen [47] (F1: 21.1, 21.0). Against the multimodal STEPS [16] on egocentric data, OPEL wins on CMU-MMAC (36.5 vs 28.3 F1), MECCANO (39.2 vs 36.4), and ProceL/CrossTask, but loses on EPIC-Tents (20.7 vs 42.2 F1) where STEPS benefits from gaze and depth inputs. In the loss-function comparison (Table 4), OPEL's OT-based loss outperforms TCC [8] (Dwibedi, CVPR 2019) by ~17 F1 points and LAV [41] (Haresh, CVPR 2021) by ~15 F1 points on CMU-MMAC. Against unsupervised action segmentation methods in Table 8, OPEL (F1: 34.9, 35.1) exceeds StepFormer [56] (F1: 21.9, 28.3) and Dvornik et al. [55] (F1: 25.3) by a wide margin, while also outperforming the narrated-video methods Alayrac [34] (F1: 5.5, 4.5) and Fried [37] (F1: —, 28.8 recall only).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the optimal transport (OT) framework for video alignment introduced by Liu et al. (CVPR 2022) — which was designed for supervised action segmentation — to the unsupervised procedure learning (PL) setting, where the goal is to identify key-steps and their ordering across multiple videos of the same task. The core OT machinery (Sinkhorn distance, Laplace-distribution priors, inverse-difference-moment regularization, virtual frame for background handling) is directly inherited from Liu et al., while the C-IDM contrastive loss is borrowed from Haresh et al. (CVPR 2021). The principal departure is the addition of a novel inter-video contrastive loss guided by the OT matrix and the application of graph-cut segmentation for key-step localization, positioning OPEL as a self-supervised PL method that requires only visual input without auxiliary modalities (gaze, depth, narration).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

OPEL demonstrates that a purely visual, OT-guided framework can match or exceed multimodal SOTA (e.g., STEPS which uses gaze and depth) on egocentric benchmarks while substantially outperforming prior PL methods on both ego- and exocentric datasets, suggesting that the OT formulation's ability to handle non-monotonicity and background frames without hand-crafted preprocessing is a meaningful practical advantage. A key limitation is the assumption that subjects use similar objects for identical key-steps, which may degrade performance in open-world settings with diverse tool usage. Open questions include whether the OT framework can incorporate richer semantic or contextual features to handle object variation, and whether the approach generalizes to tasks with highly variable step counts or to streaming/online settings.
