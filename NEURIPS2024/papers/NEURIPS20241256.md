## Problem

Existing diverse human motion prediction methods operate in an idealized, context-free setting that ignores the surrounding 3D environment, producing physically inconsistent predictions (object penetration, unrealistic interactions) when deployed in real-world scenes. This paper introduces the novel task of predicting diverse human motion within real-world 3D scenes, requiring the harmonization of stochastic human behavior with deterministic scene constraints such as collision avoidance and semantically appropriate human-object interactions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241256/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes L = 3 seconds of historical SMPL-X poses (global translation, orientation, and 32-dim body pose embedding per frame) together with a 3D scene point cloud (n_p × 6: XYZ coordinates plus RGB, ~500K vertices per scene from LiDAR or Vicon capture) and produces K diverse future motion sequences of ΔL = 5 seconds in the same SMPL-X format. The point cloud is voxelized into a 0.02 m height map for trajectory planning, and the ground is voxelized into 0.5 m patches as additional potential interaction targets when no object is reachable within the prediction horizon.

## Method

DiMoP3D comprises three stages that progressively convert scene context into a stochastic conditional factor θ = (planned trajectory, HOI end-pose, end timestamp). First, a UNet-like encoder-decoder on the point cloud performs instance segmentation and, via a cross-modal InterestNet that fuses scene features with observed motion, produces a per-point interest map from which a target object is sampled. Second, a modified A* planner (using a continuous height-based cost function and angular-velocity penalty for diverse, smooth paths) generates an obstacle-free trajectory, while a cVAE-based HOI-Estimator predicts the interactive end-pose given the target object's normalized point cloud. Third, a diffusion-based motion generator (predicting the clean sample directly rather than noise) denoises the future sequence while overwriting per-frame constraints—observed poses, planned trajectory translations, and the end-pose—at each step, and a MotionCLIP-based semantic alignment loss enforces consistency between the predicted motion and a natural-language description template matched to the target object class.

## Results

On GIMO, DiMoP3D achieves ADE 5.66, FDE 6.81, MMADE 6.57, MMFDE 7.44, and ACPD 0.98, outperforming the SoTA diverse HMP baseline BeLFusion (ADE 9.69, FDE 11.19, ACPD 13.73) by roughly 40% on accuracy and 14× on physical consistency. On CIRCLE, DiMoP3D further reduces ADE to 5.09 and ACPD to 0.87, again surpassing BeLFusion (ADE 7.91, ACPD 14.06) and even the deterministic scene-aware BiFU (ADE 5.80, ACPD 2.11). DiMoP3D also attains competitive diversity (APD 48.30 on GIMO, 42.24 on CIRCLE), exceeding BeLFusion while remaining below the non-scene-aware Dlow and SmoothDMP, which the authors attribute to their erratic, context-free predictions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the SoTA diverse HMP method BeLFusion [5], DiMoP3D wins decisively on all accuracy and physical-consistency metrics: ADE drops from 9.69 to 5.66 (−42%) and ACPD from 13.73 to 0.98 (−93%) on GIMO, with similar margins on CIRCLE (ADE 7.91→5.09, ACPD 14.06→0.87). DiMoP3D also surpasses the deterministic scene-aware BiFU [88] on ADE (5.66 vs 7.11) and ACPD (0.98 vs 3.73) on GIMO, showing that scene-aware diverse prediction can be both more accurate and more physically consistent than single-trajectory prediction. Compared to the non-scene-aware diverse methods Dlow [82] and SmoothDMP [72], DiMoP3D achieves substantially lower ADE/FDE/ACPD but reports lower APD (48.30 vs 55.12/68.80 on GIMO); the authors argue the higher APD of these baselines reflects erratic, scene-inconsistent predictions rather than meaningful diversity. In the cross-task comparison with scene-aware motion synthesis methods (Table 3), DiMoP3D outperforms SAMP [28], DN-Synt [68], and AffordMotion [70] on ADE, FDE, MMADE, MMFDE, and ACPD, while AffordMotion retains a slight edge on APD (52.54 vs 48.30) and FID (0.687 vs 0.769), which the authors attribute to AffordMotion's greater freedom in not conditioning on past motion.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: stochastic/diverse human motion prediction (DLow, SmoothDMP, BeLFusion) and scene-aware motion prediction/synthesis (BiFU/GIMO, SAMP, DN-Synt, AffordMotion). Prior diverse HMP methods operate in a context-free setting, ignoring 3D environment constraints, while scene-aware methods either produce single deterministic sequences or are synthesis-based rather than prediction-based. DiMoP3D departs from both by formulating diverse HMP explicitly within 3D scenes, using a structured decomposition (scene interpretation → trajectory/end-pose planning → diffusion-based generation) rather than naively concatenating scene features into a motion model. The diffusion backbone follows the clean-sample prediction paradigm of Human Motion Diffusion Model [63], and the semantic alignment leverages MotionCLIP for HOI consistency.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dramatic reduction in physical inconsistency (ACPD from ~14 to ~1) demonstrates that structured scene-motion integration is essential for realistic diverse prediction, a finding reinforced by the ablation showing that simply appending scene features to existing diverse HMP models yields only marginal gains. The task formulation—requiring simultaneous stochastic diversity and deterministic scene compliance—is practically important for robotics, autonomous driving, and VR where both plausible variation and physical safety matter. Key limitations include the fixed 5-second prediction horizon (with static padding or truncation for mismatched lengths) and reliance on per-scene point clouds, leaving open questions about variable-length prediction, end-to-end learning of the full pipeline, and generalization to outdoor or multi-agent scenes.
