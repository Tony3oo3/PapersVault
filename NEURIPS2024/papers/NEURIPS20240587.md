## Problem

Automated circuit discovery in Transformers is limited by two existing approaches: ACDC's greedy edge-ablation search is too slow to scale beyond a few hundred examples or billion-parameter models, while EAP's first-order linear approximations sacrifice faithfulness to full-model behavior. This paper reframes circuit discovery as a continuous optimization problem over edge masks, enabling gradient-based pruning that is both scalable and faithful.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240587/cropped_page1_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a frozen Transformer (e.g., GPT-2 Small or CodeLlama-13B), a dataset of clean–corrupted example pairs where the corrupted example differs in a task-critical feature, and a target sparsity fraction. It produces a binary edge mask over the model's computational graph (where each edge j→i is either present or absent), which defines a sparse circuit. Removed edges are not zeroed but replaced with interchange activations computed from the corrupted example, keeping the residual stream in-distribution. The final circuit is obtained by thresholding the continuous masks (typically around 0.5) after training.

## Method

Edge Pruning introduces trainable continuous masks z ∈ [0,1] over every edge in the Transformer's computational graph, modeled with the hard concrete distribution to enable differentiable L0 regularization. A key architectural modification replaces the standard residual stream with a disentangled residual stream that retains all previous layer activations, allowing each node to dynamically aggregate a different subset of inputs based on its incoming edge masks (Equation 3). Removed edges are filled with corrupted activations from interchange ablation rather than zeros, preserving in-distribution signal. Sparsity is enforced via a Lagrangian penalty on the edge masks, with a second set of node masks accelerating convergence to high sparsity. The full objective (KL divergence between full-model and circuit outputs plus the sparsity penalty) is optimized by SGD over the mask parameters while all model weights remain frozen, and the approach scales to 13B-parameter models using FSDP with full sharding and gradient checkpointing across 32 GPUs.

## Results

On GPT-2 Small, Edge Pruning finds circuits that are consistently more faithful (lower KL divergence) than ACDC and EAP on all four tasks, with the gap most pronounced on IOI and GT. On IOI, Edge Pruning achieves the same faithfulness as ACDC at 96.8% sparsity while using 2.65× fewer edges (98.8% sparsity). At 100K IOI examples, Edge Pruning achieves KL 0.20 in 3,042 s, outperforming EAP (KL 3.78, 12,260 s) and ACDC (which cannot scale). On CodeLlama-13B, Edge Pruning discovers circuits with 99.96–99.97% edge sparsity (1,041 and 1,464 edges out of 3.87M) that match full-model accuracy within 2–2.75%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ACDC (greedy edge-ablation search), Edge Pruning achieves substantially lower KL divergence on IOI and GT, especially at high sparsities where ACDC degrades below random (KL 0.69 on IOI). On IOI, Edge Pruning matches ACDC's faithfulness at 98.8% sparsity versus ACDC's 96.8%, using 2.65× fewer edges. ACDC's runtime scales linearly with the number of edges and it cannot complete a 100K-example run, whereas Edge Pruning finishes in ~3,000 s. Against EAP (first-order gradient approximation), Edge Pruning is more faithful on every task; the gap is largest on IOI (KL 0.25 vs 3.47 at 200 examples) and persists at 100K examples (KL 0.20 vs 3.78). EAP is faster at small dataset sizes (21 s vs 2,756 s at 200 examples), but Edge Pruning becomes the fastest method at 100K examples (3,042 s vs 12,260 s) while being far more accurate. On the simpler IOI-t1 and GP tasks, Edge Pruning is competitive with both baselines at low sparsities and pulls ahead at higher sparsities.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes gradient-based structured pruning (CoFi Pruning, Xia et al. 2022) for mechanistic interpretability rather than model compression, shifting the unit of pruning from nodes to edges and introducing a disentangled residual stream (Friedman et al. 2023) to make edge-level masking tractable. It departs from the two dominant automated circuit-discovery methods—ACDC's greedy per-edge ablation search (Conmy et al. 2023) and EAP's first-order linear approximation (Syed et al. 2023)—by formulating circuit discovery as a continuous optimization problem solved with L0 regularization via the hard concrete distribution (Louizos et al. 2018) and interchange ablation (Geiger et al. 2020) for counterfactual activations. The key conceptual move is treating the circuit-discovery objective (Equation 2) as directly optimizable rather than approximated, which enables capturing edge interactions that both greedy and linear methods miss.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Edge Pruning is the first automated circuit-discovery method to scale to multi-billion-parameter models (CodeLlama-13B, 100× larger than prior work), opening the door to mechanistic analysis of behaviors that only emerge at scale. The case study revealing substantial edge overlap (62.7%) between instruction-prompted and few-shot circuits, along with strong cross-evaluation performance, provides concrete evidence that LMs share core computational mechanisms across prompting paradigms. Key limitations remain: even at 99.96% sparsity, circuits retain over 1,000 edges whose full interpretation is non-trivial, and faithfulness to outputs does not guarantee the circuit captures the correct causal mechanism (interpretability illusion). Future work on automating the interpretation of these sparse-but-still-large circuits, and on distinguishing true mechanisms from redundant backups, is an open direction.
