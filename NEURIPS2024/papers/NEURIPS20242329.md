## Problem

In molecular multi-task learning, different properties are generated at different levels of theory due to computational cost: energy labels can be computed at DFT level, but equilibrium structures require repeated energy evaluations and are often generated at lower-accuracy semi-empirical levels. Standard multi-task learning cannot push structure prediction beyond the accuracy of its training data, and physically-related data (forces, off-equilibrium structures) from other tasks cannot be directly leveraged to improve a specific task without explicit physical constraints.

## Input / Output

The model takes a molecular graph (atom types and bonds) plus 3D atomic coordinates as input, with a diffusion time-step t embedded into the encoder. It produces two outputs: a scalar molecular energy (at t=0, unperturbed structure) and a denoised 3D equilibrium structure (equivariant w.r.t. input coordinates). The structure decoder re-receives the raw coordinates alongside encoder features to maintain rotational equivariance, and translational invariance is achieved by centering structures.

## Method

The paper introduces two physics-based consistency losses that connect the energy and structure decoders at the output end, complementing the shared encoder at the input end. The optimality consistency loss (large τ) enforces that the one-step denoised structure has lower energy than its perturbed version, using a stop-gradient on the energy model so only the structure decoder is updated. The score consistency loss (small τ) enforces that the structure model's score matches the negative energy gradient divided by k_B T (Boltzmann distribution), again updating only the structure decoder. A Proposition 1 proof shows that at exactly t=T the equivariant denoising target collapses to zero, motivating the use of a slightly smaller τ. Force labels on off-equilibrium structures are incorporated as a direct gradient supervision on the energy model, improving the learned energy landscape which then propagates to structure prediction through the consistency losses.

## Results

On the PCQM4Mv2 (PCQ) test set, consistency training reduces mean RMSD from 1.189 Å to 1.158 Å (denoising) and from 1.041 Å to 1.007 Å (DDIM) compared to multi-task learning alone. On QM9, the improvement is more pronounced: mean RMSD drops from 0.928 Å to 0.848 Å (denoising) and from 0.669 Å to 0.650 Å (DDIM). The advantage persists after fine-tuning on DFT-level structures (Table 3), and is further amplified when force data from SPICE or PM6 subsets are incorporated (Table 2), where consistency training achieves e.g. 1.113 Å vs. 1.199 Å mean RMSD on PCQ denoising with PM6 subset force data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's baseline is standard multi-task learning (shared Graphormer encoder with separate energy and structure decoders trained with task-specific losses). Consistency training, which adds optimality consistency (Eq. 10) and score consistency (Eq. 11) losses that only update the structure decoder, outperforms multi-task learning on every metric across both PCQ and QM9 test sets and both sampling methods (denoising and DDIM). The improvement is most visible on QM9 denoising mean RMSD (0.928 → 0.848 Å, ~8.6% reduction) and PCQ DDIM mean RMSD (1.041 → 1.007 Å, ~3.3% reduction). When force data are added (Table 2), multi-task learning does not uniformly benefit (e.g., PCQ denoising mean RMSD actually worsens from 1.189 to 1.199 with PM6 subset force), whereas consistency training consistently improves (1.113 Å), demonstrating a more direct information pathway. After fine-tuning on DFT-level structures (Table 3), the consistency-trained model still leads (e.g., QM9 DDIM min RMSD: 0.076 vs. 0.090 Å). Paired t-tests confirm significance (p < 0.05) for nearly all comparisons, with standard deviations around 0.003 Å.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the score-based/diffusion framework for molecular structure prediction (Song et al. 2021; Xu et al. 2022) and the denoising formulation (Kingma et al. 2021), but departs from prior work that connects energy to structure by introducing two distinct consistency losses derived from physical laws: an optimality loss (equilibrium structure minimizes energy) applied at large diffusion steps, and a score/Boltzmann consistency loss applied at small steps. Unlike Boltzmann generators (Noe et al. 2019) or Zheng et al. (2024) that assume access to an oracle energy function, this work explicitly targets the practical setting where the energy model is itself learned from data at a higher level of theory than the structure labels, and only the structure decoder is optimized by the consistency losses to avoid contaminating the energy model. The theoretical Proposition 1 (zero-map degeneracy of equivariant denoising at t=T) provides a principled justification for using a near-terminal but not exactly terminal time step.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine and common practical bottleneck in molecular ML: equilibrium structure data is orders of magnitude more expensive to generate than energy data, so large-scale datasets inevitably have structure labels at a lower level of theory than energy labels. The consistency losses provide a 'free lunch' mechanism to partially close this accuracy gap without requiring additional DFT-level structure data, and the framework generalizes naturally to other physically-connected molecular tasks (electronic structure, coarse-grained models). However, the demonstrated improvements are modest (RMSD reductions of ~0.03–0.08 Å), the scope is limited to energy–structure consistency on small molecules, and the force-data experiments use a mismatched DFT functional, leaving open whether the approach scales to larger systems or benefits more substantially with matched-level force data.
