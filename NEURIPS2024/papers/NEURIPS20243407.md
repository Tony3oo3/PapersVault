## Problem

Standard sparse autoencoders (SAEs) are trained to minimize mean squared reconstruction error of layer activations, which does not directly optimize for how important the learned features are to the network's actual performance. As a result, SAEs may waste capacity on features that reconstruct activations well but are functionally irrelevant, and may split features into overly specific variants. The paper asks how to train SAEs so that the features they learn are guaranteed to be functionally important for explaining the network's behavior.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243407/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes residual stream activations from a specific layer of a frozen Transformer (GPT2-small with 768-dim residual stream, or TinyStories-1M) and produces a sparse, overcomplete dictionary of 46,080 unit-norm feature directions (60× the residual stream dimension) along with a ReLU encoder that maps activations to sparse feature activations. The SAE output replaces the original layer activations, and the model is run forward from that point to produce output logits. Evaluation metrics include cross-entropy loss increase (performance explained), L0 (average features activated per datapoint), and the count of alive dictionary elements over the dataset.

## Method

The core contribution is replacing the standard MSE reconstruction loss with a KL divergence between the original model's output distribution and the distribution produced when SAE-reconstructed activations are passed through the remaining (frozen) network layers, combined with an L1 sparsity penalty on encoder activations. Two variants are introduced: SAE_e2e, which uses only KL divergence plus sparsity, and SAE_e2e+ds, which additionally adds MSE reconstruction losses at all downstream layers to prevent the SAE from exploiting alternative computational pathways. Because the model parameters are frozen and only the SAE is trained, the KL objective directly incentivizes learning features that preserve the model's output behavior, yielding a Pareto improvement: fewer features per datapoint (L0), fewer total alive features, and equal or better interpretability scores compared to standard locally-trained SAEs at matched performance-explained levels.

## Results

On GPT2-small layer 6, for a similar CE loss increase (~0.14), SAE_e2e requires L0 = 27.5 features per datapoint versus 69.4 for SAE_local — more than 2.5× fewer features to explain the same network performance. SAE_e2e+ds achieves an even lower CE loss increase (0.125) while using only 15k alive dictionary elements compared to 26k for SAE_local. Across all layers tested (2, 6, 10), for a given L0, both e2e variants achieve a CE loss increase less than 45% of SAE_local's, and automated-interpretability scores for SAE_e2e+ds are statistically indistinguishable from or slightly better than SAE_local.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is SAE_local, the standard sparse autoencoder trained with MSE reconstruction loss plus L1 sparsity (as in Bricken et al. 2023 and Cunningham et al. 2023). On the headline Pareto comparison, SAE_e2e and SAE_e2e+ds both dominate SAE_local: at matched CE loss increase, SAE_local requires over 2× the L0 (69.4 vs. 27.5 for SAE_e2e) and more than 1.5× the alive dictionary elements (26k vs. 22k), indicating it wastes capacity on functionally unimportant features. SAE_e2e+ds further reduces alive elements to 15k while achieving the lowest CE loss increase (0.125). However, SAE_e2e suffers from poor downstream reconstruction (activations take different computational pathways), whereas SAE_e2e+ds closely matches SAE_local's downstream MSE, making it the preferred variant for mechanistic interpretability. On the subject-verb agreement faithfulness task (Appendix I), results are inconclusive with no clear winner across SAE types, suggesting e2e training's advantage is most apparent on the full language-modeling distribution rather than narrow syntactic tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the SAE dictionary-learning framework for mechanistic interpretability established by Bricken et al. (2023) and Cunningham et al. (2023), retaining the same encoder-decoder architecture, unit-norm dictionary, and L1 sparsity penalty. The key departure is the training objective: rather than minimizing MSE reconstruction error at a single layer (SAE_local), the proposed SAE_e2e minimizes KL divergence between the original model's output distribution and the model with SAE activations inserted, while SAE_e2e+ds additionally penalizes downstream-layer reconstruction error. This is conceptually related to Tamkin et al. (2023), who trained sparse codebooks end-to-end, but differs critically in freezing the interpreted model's parameters rather than co-training them.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled, drop-in replacement for standard SAE training that directly optimizes for functional importance, yielding a clear Pareto improvement (fewer features per datapoint and fewer total features at equivalent performance explained) without sacrificing interpretability. However, results are limited to GPT2-small (124M) and TinyStories-1M (1M), and the subject-verb agreement faithfulness tests were inconclusive, leaving open whether the advantage persists at scale or on task-specific sub-distributions. The 2–3.5× training-time overhead and the observation that e2e SAEs take different computational pathways through the network (worse per-layer reconstruction) raise practical and conceptual questions about whether the learned features truly reflect the network's algorithm or merely an alternative route to the same output.
