## Problem

Particle-based Bayesian deep learning requires a similarity metric to compare networks, but naive L1/L2 distances lack permutation invariance and fail in high-dimensional spaces. While Centered Kernel Alignment (CKA) on feature Gram matrices addresses these issues, it has not been used as an optimization objective in Bayesian deep learning, and directly minimizing CKA suffers from vanishing gradients when two networks are already very similar, limiting its effectiveness as a repulsive training term.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20244390/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images (28×28 MNIST, 32×32 CIFAR, 64×64 TinyImageNet) alongside synthetically generated OOD examples (Perlin/simplex noise, random grids, heavily distorted inliers) that constitute 60–70% of the OOD batch. For hypernetwork variants, a Gaussian noise vector z ∈ ℝ^P is the sole input to the generator h(z) that produces full target-network weights. The output is a class prediction per ensemble member plus predictive entropy (or mutual information) used as the OOD score, with inlier/outlier separation measured by AUROC.

## Method

The core contribution is HE-CKA, which treats each ensemble member's centered and normalized feature Gram matrix as a point on the unit hypersphere and minimizes a Riesz s-kernel repulsive energy (arccos(CKA))^{-s} across all model pairs and layers, rather than directly minimizing pairwise CKA (cosine similarity). This yields non-vanishing gradients even when networks are nearly identical, producing a more uniform distribution of feature representations on the hypersphere. The HE-CKA term is added to the standard NLL with a Boltzmann-prior interpretation, using layer-wise weights w_l (increasing with depth) and smoothing constants ε_dist, ε_arc to prevent exploding gradients near CKA = ±1. For OOD detection, the Gram matrices are split into inlier and OOD subsets with separate repulsive strengths (γ_ID < γ_OOD), and an entropy-maximizing term (scaled by β) is applied to synthetic OOD points. The same diversity loss is applied to hypernetworks by backpropagating through M sampled target networks, preventing mode collapse in the generated weight distribution.

## Results

On the Dirty-MNIST vs Fashion-MNIST OOD benchmark (LeNet, 5 members), Ensemble+OOD HE-CKA achieves 99.996% AUROC (predictive entropy) and 99.742% AUROC (mutual information) while maintaining 84.09% in-distribution accuracy and 1.712% ECE. On CIFAR-10 vs SVHN with ResNet18 (5-member ensemble), OOD HE-CKA reaches 99.86% AUROC with 96.24% accuracy, surpassing a WideResNet-28-10+SN baseline (97.86% AUROC, 95.97% accuracy). On TinyImageNet, Ensemble+OOD HE-CKA achieves 99.31% AUROC on SVHN, 81.56%/87.64% on CIFAR-10/100, and 90.94% on DTD textures, substantially outperforming the plain ensemble (89.81%, 66.85%/67.33%, 68.96% respectively).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against plain deep ensembles (Lakshminarayanan et al., 2017), HE-CKA methods dramatically improve OOD AUROC: Ensemble+OOD HE-CKA reaches 99.996% PE-AUROC versus 86.10% for the standard ensemble, while matching or slightly exceeding in-distribution accuracy (84.09% vs 83.92%). Against SVGD+RBF (D' Angelo & Fortuin, 2021), HE-CKA wins on both AUROC PE (94.38% vs 91.00%) and ECE (2.85% vs 3.18%), and the OOD variant extends this gap to near-perfect separation. Against DDU (Mukhoti et al., 2023), Ensemble+OOD HE-CKA outperforms DDU's feature-density AUROC (99.996% vs 94.17%) on Dirty-MNIST; on CIFAR-10 vs SVHN, a ResNet18+OOD HE-CKA ensemble (99.86% AUROC, 96.24% accuracy) surpasses a WideResNet-28-10+SN (97.86% AUROC, 95.97% accuracy) while being faster at inference (9 ms vs 13 ms per sample). The hypernetwork variant with OOD HE-CKA also substantially outperforms the unregularized hypernetwork (98.07% vs 46.39% AUROC PE), demonstrating that the diversity term is critical to prevent posterior collapse.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper combines two previously independent ideas—Centered Kernel Alignment (CKA) for permutation-invariant network comparison (Kornblith et al., 2019) and Minimum Hyperspherical Energy (MHE) for uniform particle distribution (Liu et al., 2021)—into a new differentiable loss (HE-CKA) that addresses the vanishing-gradient problem of directly minimizing pairwise CKA. It departs from the closest prior work, D' Angelo & Fortuin (2021), which uses RBF kernels on weights or activations for repulsive SVGD terms, by instead operating on CKA-based feature Gram matrices that are invariant to channel permutations and isotropic scaling. The paper also extends the approach to Bayesian hypernetworks (Krueger et al., 2018; Ratzlaff & Fuxin, 2019) and introduces synthetic OOD examples as a practical mechanism for boosting feature diversity on out-of-distribution inputs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method offers a principled, permutation-invariant alternative to RBF-based repulsive terms for diversifying ensembles and hypernetworks, with strong empirical gains in OOD detection (e.g., 99.99% AUROC on Dirty-MNIST/Fashion-MNIST, ~99.4% on CIFAR-10/SVHN) while maintaining in-distribution accuracy. However, the approach introduces several hyperparameters (γ, β, layer weights w, smoothing terms ε_dist/ε_arc, Riesz parameter s) that require careful tuning per dataset and architecture, limiting plug-and-play adoption. Open questions include whether layer weighting and repulsive strength can be estimated automatically, how the method scales to very large ensembles or ImageNet-scale models, and whether the sensitivity of CKA-based kernels to batch size and particle count can be mitigated through normalized variants.
