## Problem

Fine-tuning LLMs with paraphrased data improves knowledge injection but incurs high recurring computational costs from external paraphrase generation and produces limited diversity at the discrete text level. Existing latent noise methods (NEFTune, FreeLB) perturb token embeddings with random or adversarial noise that ignores text semantics, failing to capture the structured variability that paraphrasing provides.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243802/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes knowledge documents D_K for fine-tuning and a separate training set D_train of sentences with LLM-generated paraphrases (as few as 50 sentences) to train the latent paraphrasers. It produces a fine-tuned LLM with injected knowledge and a set of small latent paraphraser modules (~250M parameters, 3.6% of a 7B LLM) that are trained once and transferable across domains. During fine-tuning, only the MLP layers of the transformer are updated, and the latent paraphrasers sample multiple noise vectors per token to compute an expected log-likelihood objective.

## Method

LaPael inserts a latent paraphraser before the MLP in the first few transformer layers, which generates input-dependent multiplicative noise: a linear projection maps each token's post-LayerNorm feature to a Gaussian mean, a reparameterized sample is passed through a 2-layer MLP with softplus to produce the noise vector, and a learnable binary mask (via concrete distribution) modulates per-token perturbation scale to protect key entities. The latent paraphraser is trained by minimizing the symmetric KL divergence between the empirical Gaussian distribution of layer outputs over K paraphrased sentences and the distribution over N stochastic samples from the original sentence with the paraphraser, using Monte Carlo estimation of mean and variance. An auxiliary mask loss enforces a target mask ratio and zeros out masks on named-entity tokens. During knowledge injection fine-tuning, the frozen latent paraphrasers sample N noise vectors per token, and the LLM is optimized on the expected negative log-likelihood over these samples, effectively performing latent-level data augmentation without any external paraphrase generation at inference time.

## Results

On synthetic-document QA benchmarks, LaPael (trained with 1k sentences) achieves F1 scores of 84.50 (SQuAD-syn), 91.33 (StreamingQA-syn), and 84.75 (ArchivalQA-syn), outperforming standard fine-tuning by 8.4, 3.0, and 8.6 F1 points respectively. LaPael also surpasses the noise-based baselines FreeLB (F1 79.67/88.56/77.67) and NEFTune (F1 77.91/87.77/75.81) across all three datasets. Notably, LaPael matches or exceeds fine-tuning with 10× paraphrased data (F1 80.51/92.32/81.24) on SQuAD and ArchivalQA without requiring any additional training data, and the method transfers across domains (e.g., SQuAD-trained paraphrasers improve NovelQA and MedMCQA by 2.1 and 5.9 F1 over fine-tuning).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LaPael consistently outperforms both noise-based baselines: FreeLB (adversarial noise on token embeddings) and NEFTune (random uniform noise on token embeddings) by 3.5–7.1 F1 points across all three synthetic-document benchmarks, confirming that learned, input-dependent latent perturbations are substantially more effective than fixed or adversarial noise. Against the data-augmentation baseline (fine-tuning with 10× paraphrased documents, marked †), LaPael wins on SQuAD-syn (84.50 vs. 80.51 F1) and ArchivalQA-syn (84.75 vs. 81.24 F1) while slightly trailing on StreamingQA-syn (91.33 vs. 92.32 F1), demonstrating that latent-level paraphrasing can match or exceed discrete data augmentation without the recurring cost of LLM-based paraphrase generation. In the cross-domain transfer setting (Table 4), SQuAD-trained latent paraphrasers improve NovelQA by 2.1 F1 and MedMCQA by 5.9 F1 over standard fine-tuning, and outperform both FreeLB and NEFTune on both target domains. In the RAG comparison on Events 2024-raw (Table 10), LaPael (F1 46.45) still trails RAG (F1 55.71) but narrows the gap from 16.1 to 9.3 F1 points relative to plain fine-tuning (F1 39.62), suggesting that latent perturbation partially closes the fine-tuning-vs-retrieval performance gap.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the learned latent perturbation paradigm from Lee et al. [25] (which perturbed word embeddings for OOD QA) to the knowledge injection setting, where the goal is to internalize new factual knowledge via fine-tuning. It departs from the data-level paraphrasing approach of Ovadia et al. [33] by replacing external LLM-based paraphrase generation with an input-dependent noise generator ('latent paraphraser') trained to match the latent distribution induced by paraphrases. Unlike NEFTune [16] and FreeLB [57], which apply random or adversarial noise without regard for text semantics, LaPael learns a structured, token-aware perturbation via KL-divergence matching between paraphrase-induced and noise-induced latent distributions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LaPael offers a practical solution to the recurring cost of paraphrase generation in iterative knowledge-update pipelines: once latent paraphrasers are trained (a one-time cost on ~50–1000 sentences), they can be reused across domains without further external model calls. The cross-domain transfer results and compatibility with LoRA suggest broad applicability. However, the method still trails RAG in absolute QA accuracy, worsens knowledge retention relative to standard fine-tuning, does not address the reversal curse, and has only been validated on 7B-scale models—leaving open whether the approach scales to larger architectures or continual pre-training regimes.
