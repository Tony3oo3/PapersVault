## Problem

Existing dynamic spatial structure methods for multivariate time series forecasting rely on a two-stage process (learning dynamic series representations, then generating graphs via pairwise interaction), which produces unconstrained output spaces and high-variance adjacency matrices sensitive to short time-window inputs. TPGNN partially addresses this with matrix polynomials but is restricted to matrix-power bases and uses timestamp-only coefficients that cannot adapt to current data. The paper seeks a spatial structure generation function with a well-constrained output space, higher expressiveness, and interpretable dynamics.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a multivariate time series window X_{t-H:t} ∈ R^{N×H×D} (N sensors, H history steps, D features) along with a timestamp encoding, and produces a multi-step forecast Ŷ_{t0:t0+F} via a linear head on the final block output. A secondary output is the coefficient vector α_t ∈ R^M at each time step, which records the convex weights over the M learned basis matrices and can be tracked over time to reveal when distinct spatial correlation patterns (e.g., day vs. night) dominate.

## Method

Sumba replaces the two-stage graph generation with a direct parameterization: the dynamic adjacency matrix A_t is a convex combination Σ_m α_{t,m} B_m where each basis matrix B_m = U Σ_m V^T shares a common pair of orthogonal matrices U, V (parameterized via the Cayley/Clay map on skew-symmetric matrices) and has a unique non-negative diagonal Σ_m. The coefficients α_t are computed by an attention-like matching between the flattened intermediate representation Z'_t (plus time encoding) and the singular-value vectors diag(Σ_m), so the basis is globally learned while the mixture adapts per time step. Temporal dependencies are captured by a channel-independent multi-scale TCN (kernels 1×2, 1×3, 1×6, 1×7), and spatial information is aggregated by graph convolution on A_t; L=3 such blocks are stacked. A low-rank approximation (rank K ≪ N) reduces the parameter count to O(NK + MK), and Theorem 3.1 guarantees the output space is bounded by Σ_m max(Σ_m), making the generated structures low-variance and the model easier to optimize.

## Results

Sumba achieves state-of-the-art forecasting performance across all six benchmark datasets (Electricity, Weather, PEMS, ETTh2, Traffic, Solar-Energy) at prediction horizons F=3, 6, 12, and 24. On PEMS (F=3), Sumba attains MAE 0.137, RMSE 0.204, and MAPE 1.060%, outperforming the best baseline ESG (MAE 0.142) by ~3.5%. On Solar-Energy (F=3), Sumba reaches MAE 0.046 and MAPE 0.541%, surpassing CrossFormer (MAE 0.047) and achieving up to 8.5% improvement over the best baseline on Weather. At longer horizons (F=12/24), Sumba yields improvements exceeding 20% in MAE and 25% in MAPE on Solar-Energy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Sumba outperforms all 15 baselines on PEMS and Solar-Energy at both F=3 and F=6, with the largest margins over TPGNN (the closest conceptual competitor using matrix polynomial basis): 8.1% MAE reduction on PEMS (0.137 vs 0.149) and 22% MAE reduction on Solar-Energy (0.046 vs 0.059). Among dynamic graph methods, Sumba beats ESG by 3.5% MAE on PEMS (0.137 vs 0.142) and CrossFormer by 2.1% MAE on Solar-Energy (0.046 vs 0.047). Static graph methods (MTGNN, MegaCRN) and pure temporal methods (ModernTCN, PatchTST) are consistently outperformed, confirming the benefit of explicit dynamic spatial structure modeling. At longer horizons (F=12, 24), Sumba maintains its lead, achieving over 20% MAE and 25% MAPE improvements on Solar-Energy. The only notable exception is at F=96 on Electricity, where Sumba (MAE 0.234) is slightly behind iTransformer (0.229) and Card (0.225), suggesting room for improvement in very long-term forecasting.

## Relation to Prior Work

Sumba departs from the dominant two-stage paradigm in dynamic graph-based forecasting (learn per-series dynamic representations, then compose spatial structures via attention or inner product) by directly parameterizing the spatial structure generation function as a convex combination of a learnable matrix basis. It extends TPGNN's matrix polynomial idea by replacing the restricted matrix-power basis with a general SVD-structured basis whose coefficients are computed adaptively from current window data rather than timestamps alone. The orthogonal parameterization via the Clay map (Lezcano Casado, 2019) and the shared U/V structure across basis matrices are borrowed from manifold optimization literature and repurposed here to enforce parameter efficiency and constrain the output space of the generated adjacency matrices.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The structured matrix basis offers a principled, low-variance alternative to the unbounded two-stage dynamic graph learning that plagues methods like iTransformer, Crossformer, and ESG, and the interpretability through tracking α coefficients over time provides a concrete window into how spatial correlations evolve (e.g., day/night patterns in Solar-Energy). However, the basis dimension M must be set empirically per dataset, and long-term forecasting (F=96) shows the model is less competitive than dedicated long-horizon methods like iTransformer and Card. Open questions include data-driven selection of M, integration with Transformer or state-space temporal backbones, and whether the shared-orthogonal-matrix constraint generalizes to non-stationary or highly heterogeneous series.
