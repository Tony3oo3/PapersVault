## Problem

Existing physical adversarial camouflage methods for vehicle detectors optimize 3D textures at the pixel level without any naturalness prior, producing conspicuous, attention-grabbing patterns that humans can easily identify. Moreover, none of these methods support user-controlled customization of the camouflage appearance, making it difficult to adapt the pattern to specific environments such as forests or deserts.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20244228/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a user-specified text prompt (e.g., 'colorful graffiti', 'zebra strips'), a 3D vehicle mesh (Audi E-Tron), a UV map mask, and camera pose parameters, and produces an adversarial UV-map texture image that, when rendered onto the mesh, yields a camouflaged vehicle image evading detection. The UV map is reordered so that the vehicle surface is as connected as possible in texture space, allowing the diffusion model to generate coherent natural patterns across the full vehicle body rather than fragmented patches.

## Method

CNCA leverages a pretrained Stable Diffusion v1.5 model to generate the adversarial UV-map texture, introducing an optimizable adversarial feature vector F_adv that is concatenated with the text prompt embedding to form the conditional input to the diffusion model. Gradients from the target detector (YOLOv3) are backpropagated through a differentiable rendering pipeline (PyTorch3D neural renderer plus a U-Net-based Environment Feature Renderer) to update F_adv, so the generated texture is simultaneously natural (guided by the text prompt) and adversarial. A PGD-style clipping strategy constrains F_adv to an ℓ_p norm ball of radius τ, providing a tunable knob to trade off naturalness against attack performance. The reordered UV map ensures the diffusion model's spatially coherent patterns map naturally onto the connected vehicle surface, further improving perceived naturalness.

## Results

In the digital world (Table 1), CNCA achieves a total AP@0.5 of 0.495 across six detectors, the lowest (best attack) among all methods, slightly outperforming ACTIVE (0.504) and DTA (0.541). In the physical world (Table 2), CNCA achieves the best overall AP@0.5 of 0.588, beating the next-best ACTIVE (0.627) and significantly outperforming DTA (0.756), FCA (0.763), and DAS (0.857). In subjective naturalness evaluation, CNCA scores 2.84/5, significantly higher than all adversarial baselines (range 1.84–2.11) while remaining well below the normal car (4.68), confirming a meaningful naturalness improvement over prior methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CNCA outperforms all four baselines in both digital and physical settings. Against the closest competitor ACTIVE, CNCA achieves a 0.009 lower total AP@0.5 in the digital world (0.495 vs. 0.504) and a 0.039 lower AP@0.5 in the physical world (0.588 vs. 0.627). The gap widens substantially against DTA (0.541 digital, 0.756 physical), FCA (0.650 digital, 0.763 physical), and DAS (0.723 digital, 0.857 physical), which suffer from limited coverage or inability to render environmental characteristics. In the physical world, CNCA achieves the best AP@0.5 against 4 out of 5 individual detectors (YOLOv3, YOLOX, SSD, CenterNet), with RetinaNet being the sole exception where ACTIVE (0.735) slightly edges out CNCA (0.780). On naturalness, CNCA (2.84/5) significantly surpasses all baselines (DAS 2.05, FCA 2.11, DTA 1.86, ACTIVE 1.84) with p-values < 0.004 in t-tests, while maintaining competitive attack performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the adversarial camouflage line of work (DAS, FCA, DTA, ACTIVE) by replacing direct pixel-level texture optimization with a pre-trained Stable Diffusion model as the texture generator. It inherits the differentiable rendering pipeline from DTA and ACTIVE (PyTorch3D + Environment Feature Renderer) but introduces a key architectural departure: an optimizable adversarial feature vector concatenated with text-prompt embeddings to steer the diffusion model's output toward adversarial patterns. The clipping strategy on this adversarial feature is a novel mechanism for controlling the naturalness–attack trade-off, which prior pixel-space methods could not express.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that generative models' natural-image priors can be harnessed for adversarial purposes, producing camouflage that is significantly more natural to human observers (2.84 vs. ~1.8–2.1 on a 5-point scale, with p < 0.001) while maintaining competitive detector evasion. This has direct implications for defense: if attacks can be made visually inconspicuous, detection-based countermeasures that rely on human oversight become less effective. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond vehicles, whether the 3D-mesh requirement can be relaxed, and whether automatic τ selection (e.g., via CLIP scores) can remove the manual tuning bottleneck.
