## Problem

Symbolic regression must search a discrete, exponential space of programmatic hypotheses, and state-of-the-art genetic algorithms rely on random mutation and crossover that ignore the structured background knowledge a human scientist would bring to the problem. No prior automated method induces and exploits a reusable library of abstract, interpretable concepts to bias the search, leaving a gap between the efficiency of pure evolutionary search and the knowledge-directed reasoning of human discovery.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241418/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

LASR takes a dataset of input-output pairs, a context-free grammar specifying available operators (e.g., +, *, sin, cos, exp, log, sqrt), and optionally a set of user-provided natural-language hints. It produces two artifacts: a symbolic equation (expression tree) that maximizes data likelihood while minimizing syntactic complexity, and an evolving library of natural-language concepts that summarize useful structural patterns. The LLM is queried zero-shot (no fine-tuning) and acts as a generative prior over both concepts and programs, while fitness is evaluated by direct execution of candidate equations on the data.

## Method

LASR extends PySR, a multi-population genetic algorithm, by injecting LLM-guided operations into its evolutionary loop with a mixture probability p. It alternates three phases: (i) hypothesis evolution, where standard mutation/crossover/initialization are stochastically replaced by LLM-guided variants that sample concepts from the library and prompt the LLM to generate or modify expression trees; (ii) concept abstraction, where a zero-shot LLM prompt summarizes Pareto-optimal (and worst-performing) hypotheses into a new natural-language concept added to the library; and (iii) concept evolution, where the LLM generates new concepts that logically follow from existing ones to maintain diversity. The overall objective is a hierarchical Bayesian MAP estimation over both the program π and the concept library C, with p(D|π) computed by execution and p(π|C)·p(C) approximated by LLM token prediction. This alternating maximization lets the concept library accumulate and refine semantic priors across iterations, regularizing the search toward simpler, physically meaningful equations.

## Results

On the Feynman Equations benchmark (100 physics equations), LASR achieves a 72/100 exact-match solve rate, substantially outperforming the strongest baseline PySR (59/100) and all other methods. On a synthetic dataset designed to eliminate data leakage concerns, LASR (Llama3-8B, p=0.1%) achieves a test-set R² of 0.913 versus PySR's 0.070. In a case study on BigBench, LASR discovers a 3-parameter scaling law (MSE 0.03598±0.00265) that matches the 5-parameter Chinchilla law (MSE 0.03610±0.00268) in predictive accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LASR outperforms all seven baselines on the Feynman Equations benchmark. The closest competitor is PySR (59/100), which is itself an ablation of LASR without LLM-guided operations and concept evolution; LASR's 13-equation advantage (72 vs. 59) is attributed to concept-directed search. Among deep-learning-based methods, uDSR (40/100) and AI Feynman (38/100) are the strongest, but both trail LASR by over 30 equations. Classical genetic programming methods (GPlearn at 20/100, AFP at 24/100, AFP-FE at 26/100, DSR at 23/100) are far behind, highlighting the importance of scalable search. On the synthetic (leakage-free) dataset, LASR's R² of 0.913 dwarfs PySR's 0.070, confirming that the gains are not due to memorization. In the BigBench scaling law experiment, LASR's discovered equation matches Chinchilla's MSE while using only 3 free parameters instead of 5.

## Relation to Prior Work

LASR extends PySR, a multi-population genetic algorithm for symbolic regression, by injecting LLM-guided mutation, crossover, and initialization operations that are conditioned on a dynamically learned library of natural-language concepts. Conceptually, it generalizes FunSearch (which conditions program generation on a static specification) by discovering and evolving the concept library in an open-ended loop alongside the hypotheses. The formulation also draws on the library-learning tradition (DreamCoder, LILo), but optimizes over natural-language descriptions of programs rather than executable library functions, and uses zero-shot LLM queries rather than amortized neural policies.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LASR demonstrates that even minimal LLM guidance (1% call probability with an 8B open-source model) can substantially outperform a strong evolutionary baseline, suggesting that LLMs' in-context reasoning provides a qualitatively different search bias rather than just more candidate generation. The interpretable concept library offers a unique diagnostic window into the search process, and the preliminary LLM scaling law discovery shows practical utility beyond benchmark equations. Key open questions include whether learned concepts are factually reliable (the paper acknowledges they may reflect model quirks), whether finetuning the LLM improves performance beyond zero-shot prompting, and whether the approach scales to higher-compute regimes or transfers across scientific domains.
