## Problem

Traditional mesh parameterization algorithms require well-behaved triangulations, manually specified cutting seams, and are restricted to disk-topology surfaces, making them impractical for the growing volume of user-generated 3D data with irregular geometries and complex topologies. Existing neural parameterization approaches are either local (multi-patch) or fixed-boundary, leaving global free-boundary parameterization as an unexplored problem setting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240093/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes an unstructured set of N 3D surface points (no connectivity or mesh structure required) with optional per-point normals, and produces per-point 2D UV coordinates, automatically learned cutting seams, and resulting texture mappings. Training operates in a per-model overfitting regime with 10,000 uniformly sampled points per iteration, and at inference any arbitrary surface-sampled point can be fed in to obtain its UV coordinate. The approach extends seamlessly to unoriented point clouds by simply removing the normal-consistency term from the loss.

## Method

FAM assembles four geometrically-interpretable MLP sub-networks—Deform-Net (adaptive UV grid deformation), Wrap-Net (2D-to-3D surface reconstruction), Cut-Net (learned surface cutting via offset displacement), and Unwrap-Net (3D-to-2D flattening)—into a bi-directional cycle mapping framework with shared parameters across both the 2D→3D→2D and 3D→2D→3D branches. Training is fully unsupervised, combining Chamfer distance for 3D reconstruction, L1 cycle-consistency losses in both domains, a non-overlap unwrapping penalty, and a conformal distortion regularizer computed from Jacobian eigenvalues via automatic differentiation. The key insight is that the wrapping direction provides implicit supervision for the unwrapping direction: by requiring the deformed UV grid to reconstruct the input surface and then cycle back consistently, the network learns both reasonable cutting seams and low-distortion UV coordinates without any ground-truth parameterization.

## Results

On 8 open-surface models, FAM achieves conformality metrics ranging from 0.037 (car-shell) to 0.166 (shirt), substantially outperforming SLIM on every model (e.g., 0.074 vs. 0.635 on human-face, 0.037 vs. 0.411 on car-shell). On 14 additional models with higher topological complexity (genus > 0, non-disk topologies), FAM produces conformality metrics between 0.062 and 0.311, a setting where SLIM is not applicable. Self-intersection rates are very low: 0.156% for open-surface models and 0.204% for higher-genus models. For unstructured point cloud parameterization, FAM reports conformality metrics of 0.037, 0.058, and 0.117 on three test models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SLIM (the state-of-the-art mesh parameterization method), FAM achieves dramatically lower conformality distortion on all 8 comparable open-surface models, with improvements of roughly 3×–8.5× (e.g., 0.074 vs. 0.635 on human-face, 0.037 vs. 0.411 on car-shell). Beyond these disk-topology models, FAM additionally handles 14 higher-genus and non-disk-topology surfaces where SLIM simply cannot be applied without manual cutting. Against FBCP-PC (a point-cloud-specific parameterization method), FAM reports higher conformality values on all three point cloud test models (e.g., 0.037 vs. 0.021 on cloth-pts, 0.117 vs. 0.023 on spiral-pts), but the authors note this comparison is unfair because FBCP-PC requires manually specifying ordered boundary point indices as additional input, whereas FAM is fully automated. In terms of self-intersection, both methods show very small proportions (FAM 0.156% vs. SLIM 0.133% on open surfaces), with FAM additionally handling higher-genus cases where SLIM is not applicable.

## Relation to Prior Work

FAM synthesizes ideas from several distinct lines of work into a new architecture: the 2D-grid-deformation paradigm of FoldingNet/AtlasNet, the fixed-boundary structurization of RegGeoNet/Flattening-Net, and the local multi-patch parameterization of DiffSR/Nuvo. However, it departs from all of these by targeting global (not multi-patch) free-boundary (not fixed-boundary) parameterization, operating purely on discrete points without mesh connectivity, and jointly learning cutting seams rather than requiring them as input. The bi-directional cycle mapping framework with parameter-shared MLP sub-networks (Deform-Net, Wrap-Net, Cut-Net, Unwrap-Net) is a novel architectural composition not present in any single prior method, and the Jacobian-based conformal regularization via automatic differentiation provides an explicit distortion constraint absent from the folding-style approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FAM demonstrates that a fully automated, unsupervised neural pipeline can handle the full UV unwrapping workflow—including cutting seam discovery and adaptive UV domain deformation—without manual intervention, which is a practical advance for the growing volume of user-generated and sensor-derived 3D data that lacks clean mesh topology. The dramatic conformality improvements over SLIM (e.g., 0.074 vs. 0.635 on human-face) suggest that jointly optimizing cuts and mapping in a learned framework can outperform the traditional two-stage pipeline even when the latter is given manually optimal cuts. Key limitations remain: the per-model overfitting paradigm precludes generalization across shapes, the method fails on highly complex multi-layer CAD geometries, and important practical properties (symmetry, seam visibility, seamless tiling) are unaddressed. Open questions include whether the framework can be adapted to amortized/generalizable learning and whether the cycle-mapping architecture scales to higher-genus or multi-component surfaces.
