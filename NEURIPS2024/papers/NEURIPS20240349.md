## Problem

Large language models frequently hallucinate, generating factually incorrect text with high confidence. Existing calibration and selective-prediction methods either require labeled uncertainty data, operate as post-hoc heuristics on model outputs, or depend on expensive multi-sample procedures. This paper asks whether a model can be taught during pretraining to explicitly signal its own uncertainty—without any external labels—by learning to emit a dedicated 'I don't know' token.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240349/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives an incomplete sentence (e.g., 'Paris is the capital of'), a rephrased question converted to sentence-completion form via GPT4 (for TriviaQA and PopQA), or a multiple-choice prompt, and produces either a factual text completion or the special [IDK] token. The [IDK] token is a single new vocabulary entry whose embedding is randomly initialized and learned jointly; uncertainty is expressed purely as probability mass assigned to this token in the next-token distribution, making it a soft, differentiable signal rather than a hard binary abstain decision.

## Method

IDK-tuning adds a new [IDK] token to the vocabulary and modifies the standard cross-entropy objective during continual pretraining. When the model's predicted probability on the gold token falls below the maximum token probability, a fraction λ of the target mass is shifted from the gold token to [IDK], where λ = Π × (1 − prob(gold)/max_prob) and Π (set to 0.5) caps the maximum shift. When the model is correct (λ = 0), the loss reduces to ordinary cross-entropy plus an anti-false-positive regularization term −log(1 − prob([IDK])) that penalizes residual [IDK] mass, preventing the model from over-abstaining. The entire procedure is self-supervised: no uncertainty labels or curated Q&A data are needed, and the uncertainty signal is derived entirely from the model's own prediction distribution over the pretraining corpus.

## Results

On Mistral-7B-v0.1, IDK-tuning achieves the highest precision across all five closed-book factual sentence completion benchmarks (LAMA Google-RE: 71.1, LAMA T-Rex: 88.5, LAMA SQuAD: 72.0, TriviaQA: 72.5, PopQA: 78.1), while incurring only modest recall drops (e.g., PopQA recall drops from 35.5 to 20.5). The method attains the best F1 on four of five datasets (51.7, 75.3, 54.9, 60.6), with PopQA F1 (32.5) slightly below the P(True) baseline (33.7). On the lm-eval-harness multiple-choice benchmarks, IDK-tuning improves F1 from 28.2 to 29.2, with precision rising from 28.2 to 48.8. Scaling analysis shows recall and F1 increase log-linearly with model size, while precision gains plateau; the two smallest Pythia models (70M, 160M) suffer training collapse.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IDK-tuning outperforms all three baselines on precision across every dataset, with the largest margin over the P(True) baseline (e.g., PopQA precision 78.1 vs. 40.3) and a consistent 1–4 point edge over Semantic Entropy (e.g., TriviaQA 72.5 vs. 68.5, LAMA SQuAD 72.0 vs. 70.2). On F1, IDK-tuning wins on four of five datasets, beating Semantic Entropy by 0.3–1.2 points; the sole exception is PopQA where P(True) edges ahead (33.7 vs. 32.5) due to its higher recall (29.0 vs. 20.5). The Confidence Threshold baseline, while competitive on LAMA T-Rex F1 (71.0), lags substantially on PopQA (31.2) and LAMA SQuAD (44.1), reflecting its inability to adapt per-instance uncertainty. Overall, IDK-tuning achieves the best precision–recall tradeoff, sacrificing roughly 10–15 recall points on PopQA and LAMA Google-RE while gaining 20–40 precision points, a tradeoff that no baseline matches simultaneously.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of model calibration, selective prediction, and hallucination mitigation in LLMs. It departs from prior calibration approaches (logit transformations, temperature scaling) and post-hoc uncertainty estimation methods (P(True) self-evaluation, semantic entropy sampling) by intervening directly in the pretraining objective: a special [IDK] token is added to the vocabulary and the standard cross-entropy loss is modified to shift probability mass toward [IDK] whenever the model is uncertain about its prediction. Unlike supervised approaches that fine-tune on labeled correct/incorrect statements or in-context learning methods that prompt the model to judge its own output, IDK-tuning requires no labeled data and operates as a drop-in replacement for the self-supervised language modeling loss during continued pretraining, making it scalable to web-crawled corpora.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method offers a principled, label-free mechanism for LLMs to explicitly signal uncertainty, which is critical for deploying reliable factual QA systems. However, the gains come with a precision-recall tradeoff: precision improves substantially (e.g., 48→71 on LAMA Google-RE) while recall drops modestly, and the method requires expensive continued pretraining that is impractical for most academic settings. Open questions include whether IDK-tuning can be applied during pretraining from scratch (where it might interact with knowledge acquisition), whether filtering to factual next-token predictions (e.g., named entities) would sharpen the signal, and why the method collapses for very small models (<410M parameters) due to numerical underflow of the initial [IDK] probability.
