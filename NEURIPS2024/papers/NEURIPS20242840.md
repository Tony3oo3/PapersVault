## Problem

Despite many state-of-the-art partial-label learning (PLL) methods incorporating diverse advanced techniques, they are highly complicated and differ significantly from one another, making it unclear which components are truly essential for effectiveness. The paper asks a fundamental question: what makes a PLL method effective, and can the success of PLL be distilled into minimal algorithm design principles?

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images (FMNIST 28×28, CIFAR-10/100 32×32, mini-ImageNet 84×84) paired with partial labels—sets of candidate labels where one fixed but unknown candidate is the true label. PLs are generated via a flipping strategy where each incorrect label is added with a specified probability (e.g., η=0.3 or 0.7 for FMNIST/CIFAR-10, η=0.05 or 0.1 for CIFAR-100), or instance-dependent probabilities derived from a pre-trained classifier's confidence. The output is a single class label from k classes, trained with standard backbones (LeNet-5, ResNet-18, ResNet-34).

## Method

The paper identifies mini-batch PL purification as the indispensable design principle: a progressive transition from uniform to one-hot pseudo-labels where weights are updated in a mini-batch-wise manner using the current model's confidence, rather than at epoch or one-step granularity. They distill this into SASM (single augmentations single model), a minimal working algorithm that uses zero-and-normalized outputs of the model on the same instance as its own optimization target, achieving SOTA-comparable accuracy without multiple forward passes or auxiliary components. They further prove (Lemma 3.1) that SASM is mathematically equivalent to minimizing the maximum likelihood loss ℓ_maxi, unifying pseudo-label manipulation with loss minimization. Building on this, they propose StreamPurify, an instance-dependent warm-up strategy that selectively transitions high-confidence samples into the purification phase while others continue with uniform targets, preventing harmful memorization of incorrect pseudo-labels and yielding consistent improvements across all tested SOTA methods.

## Results

The proposed minimal algorithm SASM achieves 93.83%/92.18% on FMNIST (η=0.3/0.7), 79.38%/78.19% on CIFAR-100 (η=0.05/0.1), and 55.45% on mini-ImageNet (instance-dependent), outperforming the full DPLL method (52.22% on mini-ImageNet) without requiring dual augmentations or additional loss terms. The dual-model variant SADM reaches 94.02%/92.36% on FMNIST and 80.11%/79.65% on CIFAR-100, surpassing PiCO (48.36% on mini-ImageNet) by over 6 points. StreamPurify, the proposed instance-dependent warm-up strategy, adds 0.02–1.82% accuracy improvement when applied to existing SOTA methods, with the largest gains on mini-ImageNet (e.g., +1.82% for SASM, +1.41% for DASM).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares its simplified methods (SASM, DASM, SADM, DADM, PRODEN+) against five SOTA PLL baselines: DPLL, PiCO, PaPi, CroSel, and PRODEN. SASM, the proposed minimal single-augmentation single-model algorithm, outperforms DPLL by 3.23 points on mini-ImageNet (55.45% vs. 52.22%) and by ~3 points on CIFAR-100 (79.38% vs. 76.81% at η=0.05), while matching or slightly exceeding DPLL on FMNIST. SADM surpasses PiCO by 6.28 points on mini-ImageNet (54.64% vs. 48.36%) and by 4.0 points on CIFAR-100 (80.11% vs. 76.11%), and also outperforms CroSel by 1.06 points on mini-ImageNet. However, PaPi remains the strongest baseline on mini-ImageNet (57.10%), exceeding SADM by 2.46 points, suggesting that its prototypical classifier component provides an additional advantage in the hardest setting. On CIFAR-100, CroSel achieves the highest accuracy (80.40%/80.06%), narrowly edging out SADM (80.11%/79.65%) and PaPi (80.10%/79.62%), indicating that sample selection in CroSel is particularly effective for this dataset. Overall, the paper's central finding is that mini-batch PL purification is the dominant factor: removing it (DASM-H, DASM-S, DADM-H, DADM-S) causes catastrophic drops (e.g., DADM-H falls to 35.72% on CIFAR-100 η=0.1), while the specific match architecture (DA vs. SA, DM vs. SM) contributes only marginal differences.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the mini-batch PL purification mechanism first introduced by PRODEN (Lv et al., ICML 2020) and the sample selection criterion from CroSel (Tian et al., CVPR 2024), but its primary contribution is analytical rather than methodological. It systematically dissects four SOTA PLL methods (DPLL, PiCO, PaPi, CroSel) to identify which components are truly essential, departing from the prevailing IBS/ABS taxonomy by showing that the boundary between identification-based and average-based strategies is far more fluid than the literature suggests. The paper also draws on semi-supervised learning techniques (consistency regularization, co-teaching, match-based pseudo-labeling) that have been borrowed into PLL, and on noisy-label learning (memorization effects, small-loss trick) to explain why progressive purification works.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By establishing mini-batch PL purification as the indispensable design principle and showing that additional techniques (data augmentation, contrastive learning, dual-model matching) yield only marginal gains on current benchmarks, the paper provides a clear roadmap for future PLL research and cautions against over-engineering. The StreamPurify strategy offers a practical, plug-and-play improvement compatible with all existing SOTA methods. Open questions include whether these findings hold beyond vision benchmarks, whether the marginal gains from auxiliary techniques become significant at larger scale or in more realistic (non-simulated) PL settings, and how to design augmentations that genuinely aid label disambiguation rather than merely improving representation quality.
