## Problem

Deep learning-based de novo peptide sequencing has advanced rapidly, but empirical results across papers are often incomparable due to inconsistent evaluation datasets, and existing metrics are limited to amino acid- or peptide-level precision and recall. No prior work has systematically evaluated model robustness to critical practical factors such as peptide length, noise peaks, and missing fragmentation, leaving researchers without guidance for model selection in specific scenarios.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243326/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each model receives a variable-length MS2 spectrum as a set of (m/z, intensity) 2-tuples along with the precursor charge (integer) and mass (float), and outputs a predicted peptide amino acid sequence. The benchmark evaluates predictions at three granularities—amino acid level (mass difference < 0.1 Da with prefix/suffix constraint ≤ 0.5 Da), peptide level (all residues must match), and PTM level—plus confidence scores (mean per-residue predictive probability), AUC of the precision-recall curve sorted by confidence, and computational efficiency (training/inference time per batch of 32, trainable parameter count). The three datasets span low-resolution (Seven-species, ~466 peaks avg.) to high-resolution (Nine-species, ~135 peaks avg.; HC-PT, ~184 peaks avg.) spectra with peptide lengths averaging 12.5–15.8 residues.

## Method

NovoBench is a unified evaluation framework rather than a new model; its core contribution is integrating six representative de novo sequencing methods—DeepNovo (CNN+LSTM), PointNovo (PointNet), CasaNovo (Transformer encoder-decoder), InstaNovo (Transformer with precursor encoder, pre-trained), AdaNovo (Transformer with mutual-information-based adaptive training), and π-HelixNovo (Transformer with complementary spectrum augmentation)—under identical training protocols (batch size 32, 30 epochs, A100 GPU) on three datasets with leave-one-species-out splits. Beyond standard metrics, the benchmark introduces PTM-level precision/recall, confidence-based AUC, and efficiency measurements, then systematically probes robustness by binning spectra along three axes: peptide length, Noise Signal Ratio (NSR, noise-to-signal peak ratio computed against a theoretical spectrum), and Missing Fragmentation Ratio (MFR, missing fragmentations divided by candidate cleavage sites). Key findings include that π-HelixNovo achieves the best overall performance across datasets and metrics, InstaNovo excels on high-resolution data after large-scale pre-training, all models degrade sharply with increasing MFR, and noise peaks have a smaller and non-monotonic effect (initial improvement followed by decline) compared to peptide length and missing fragmentation.

## Results

Across three datasets (Seven-species, Nine-species, HC-PT), π-HelixNovo achieves the best amino acid-level precision and recall on both Seven-species (0.481/0.472) and Nine-species (0.765/0.758), while InstaNovo leads on HC-PT (0.714/0.712). At the peptide level, InstaNovo attains the highest precision and AUC on Nine-species (0.532/0.495) and HC-PT (0.572/0.549), whereas π-HelixNovo is best on Seven-species (0.234/0.173). For PTM identification, InstaNovo achieves the highest recall on HC-PT (0.829) and π-HelixNovo leads on Nine-species (0.598). In terms of efficiency, DeepNovo and PointNovo are the fastest (inference ~0.04–0.25 s/batch) with the fewest parameters (4.8–8.6 M), while AdaNovo is the slowest (1.46–1.50 s/batch) with 66.3 M parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

π-HelixNovo (Yang 2024) dominates on the low-resolution Seven-species dataset, achieving the best amino acid-level precision (0.481) and peptide-level precision (0.234), and also leads on Nine-species amino acid-level metrics (0.765/0.758), outperforming PointNovo (0.740/0.671) and InstaNovo (0.736/0.703). InstaNovo (Eloff 2023), benefiting from large-scale pre-training, achieves the best peptide-level precision and AUC on both Nine-species (0.532/0.495) and HC-PT (0.572/0.549), surpassing AdaNovo (0.505/0.469) and π-HelixNovo (0.517/0.453) on Nine-species. PointNovo (Qiao 2021) is competitive on high-resolution data (HC-PT AA: 0.623/0.622, second-best) but collapses on the low-resolution Seven-species dataset (AA precision 0.196, peptide precision 0.022). DeepNovo (Tran 2017), despite being the earliest method, retains the highest amino acid-level precision on Seven-species (0.492) and the best PTM recall on that dataset (0.373), but is outperformed by all Transformer-based models on the other two datasets. CasaNovo (Yilmaz 2022) and AdaNovo (Xia 2024) show consistent but mid-pack performance across all datasets, with AdaNovo achieving the second-best peptide AUC on Nine-species (0.469) and the highest confidence scores among non-π-HelixNovo models (avg 0.824). Overall, no single model wins across all datasets and metrics, highlighting the importance of the unified benchmark for fair comparison.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper does not build on a single prior method but rather unifies and standardizes the evaluation of six existing de novo peptide sequencing models (DeepNovo, PointNovo, CasaNovo, InstaNovo, AdaNovo, π-HelixNovo) that span both traditional deep learning (CNN/LSTM/PointNet) and Transformer architectures. Prior work suffered from inconsistent evaluation datasets (different versions of the Nine-species dataset, different species splits) and limited metrics restricted to amino acid-level or peptide-level precision and recall. NovoBench carves new ground by introducing the first unified benchmark with diverse datasets, expanded metrics (PTM-level precision/recall, confidence scores, AUC, computational efficiency), and systematic robustness analysis under peptide length, noise peaks, and missing fragmentation ratio.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The lack of standardized evaluation has obscured true progress in de novo peptide sequencing, making cross-paper comparisons unreliable; NovoBench addresses this by providing a reproducible, unified framework. The finding that all models are highly sensitive to missing fragmentation ratio, and that noise peaks have a smaller-than-expected impact (with a counterintuitive initial performance improvement), provides practical guidance for model selection in specific experimental scenarios. Key limitations include the restriction to DDA data (no DIA support) and the absence of error bars across multiple random seeds in the main tables. Future work aims to build an automated end-to-end computational proteomics pipeline and extend to DIA-specific models.
