## Problem

Contextual multinomial logistic (MNL) bandits have a significant gap between upper and lower regret bounds, with no prior work establishing minimax optimality for either uniform or non-uniform rewards. The dependence of regret on the outside-option attraction parameter v₀ and the assortment size K was unclear, as existing lower bounds were derived under inconsistent assumptions (v₀=1 vs. v₀=K), and no computationally efficient algorithm achieved a regret that improves with K.

## Input / Output

At each round t, the algorithm receives d-dimensional feature vectors x_ti for all N items and their rewards r_ti ∈ [0,1], then selects an assortment S_t of at most K items. The output is the chosen assortment S_t and an updated parameter estimate w_{t+1} ∈ R^d. A key structural detail is that the algorithm uses a single shared parameter w* across all items (unlike multi-parameter models), with item-specific contexts, and the per-round computation is O(Kd³) for parameter updates plus O(N) for assortment selection—both independent of the round index t.

## Method

OFU-MNL+ combines online mirror descent for parameter estimation (replacing the intractable MLE used in prior work) with a UCB-based optimistic revenue. The parameter update uses a self-concordant-like Hessian G_t(w) and achieves O(Kd³) computation and O(d²) storage per round, independent of t. The key technical insight is that the MNL loss is 3√2-self-concordant-like (a K-independent constant), improving over the √6K bound in prior work, which yields a √K regret improvement. For uniform rewards, assortment selection reduces to picking the top-K items by optimistic utility α_ti = x_ti^T w_t + β_t(δ)||x_ti||_{H_t^{-1}} in O(N) time; for non-uniform rewards, a polynomial-time assortment optimization solver is used. A K-free elliptical potential lemma and a novel centralized-feature potential argument (for non-uniform rewards) complete the analysis, yielding minimax-optimal regret Õ(d√(T/K)) under uniform rewards (v₀=Θ(1)) and Õ(d√T) under non-uniform rewards, matching new lower bounds up to logarithmic factors.

## Results

Under uniform rewards with v₀=Θ(1), OFU-MNL+ achieves a minimax-optimal regret of Õ(d√(T/K)), matching the new lower bound Ω(d√(T/K)) up to logarithmic factors — an improvement over the previous best upper bound Õ(d√(KT)) by a factor of K. Under non-uniform rewards with v₀=Θ(1), the algorithm achieves Õ(d√T), matching the new lower bound Ω(d√T). Crucially, OFU-MNL+ requires only O(1) computation per round (O(Kd³) for parameter updates, O(N) for assortment selection), compared to O(t) for prior methods like UCB-MNL and TS-MNL.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares OFU-MNL+ against UCB-MNL [40] and TS-MNL [40] in empirical experiments over T=3000 rounds. OFU-MNL+ significantly outperforms both baselines in cumulative regret under both uniform and non-uniform reward settings (Figure 1), while maintaining a constant O(1) computation cost per round; in contrast, UCB-MNL and TS-MNL exhibit a linear O(t) dependence on the number of rounds. Theoretically, OFU-MNL+ improves upon the previous best upper bound of Perivier and Goyal [44] (Õ(dK√(κ'T)) ≈ Õ(d√(KT))) by a factor of K in the uniform reward setting, and is the first tractable algorithm to achieve minimax-optimal regret in adversarial contextual MNL bandits. The lower bound also improves upon Chen et al. [16] by a factor of √K, closing the gap between upper and lower bounds for the first time in this literature.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the online mirror descent parameter estimation framework of Zhang and Sugiyama [53] for constant-cost logistic bandit updates, and the polynomial-time optimistic revenue construction of Oh and Iyengar [41] for MNL assortment selection. It departs from prior contextual MNL bandit work (Chen et al. [16], Perivier and Goyal [44]) by explicitly incorporating the outside-option attraction parameter v₀ into both lower and upper bounds, and by proving that regret decreases with assortment size K under uniform rewards—a property no prior algorithm demonstrated. The key technical departure is a K-independent self-concordant-like property of the MNL loss (3√2 vs. √6K in prior work) and a K-free elliptical potential lemma, which together eliminate the harmful 1/κ = O(K²) dependence that plagued earlier bounds.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to close the gap between upper and lower regret bounds (up to logarithmic factors) in contextual MNL bandits, resolving a long-standing ambiguity about whether existing bounds were tight. The explicit characterization of how v₀ and K jointly affect regret clarifies why seemingly contradictory results in the literature (Ω(d√T/K) vs. Ω(√NT)) actually arise from different assumptions about the outside option. The constant-time algorithm OFU-MNL+ is practically significant for large-scale recommendation and retail applications where per-round computation must be bounded. Open questions include instance-dependent bounds under non-uniform rewards (where the mean-value-theorem argument breaks down) and whether the v₀ = Θ(K) regime admits further improvements.
