## Problem

In-context learning and supervised fine-tuning are the two dominant strategies for adapting LLMs to specific tasks, and they often yield comparable accuracy. However, it remains unknown whether these two paradigms structure the model's internal representation space in the same way. Prior comparisons focused on generalization performance and computational efficiency, leaving the geometry of hidden representations unexplored.

## Input / Output

The method consumes MMLU multiple-choice question prompts (with 0 or 5 few-shot examples prepended) and extracts the last-token hidden representation after the normalization layer at every transformer block across six open-source models (Llama2-7b/13b/70b, Llama3-8b/70b, Mistral-7b). For each layer it produces a suite of geometric descriptors: intrinsic dimension (via Gride), the number and composition of density peaks, the fraction of core cluster points, Adjusted Rand Index against both the 57 MMLU subjects and the four answer letters, and a hierarchical dendrogram encoding pairwise peak similarity through saddle-point densities.

## Method

The core technical contribution is applying the Advanced Density Peaks (ADP) algorithm directly on the high-dimensional data manifold to find probability modes without any explicit dimensionality reduction, combined with Gride for intrinsic dimension estimation. ADP identifies local density maxima, assigns points via iterative nearest-higher-density linking, estimates saddle-point densities between cluster borders, and uses a t-test on log-density differences to merge statistically unreliable peaks at a chosen confidence Z. The resulting peaks are hierarchically clustered using WPGMA linkage on a dissimilarity derived from saddle-point densities, yielding dendrograms that reveal semantic relationships among subjects. By tracking these quantities across all layers under three conditions (0-shot, 5-shot ICL, and LoRA fine-tuning), the paper uncovers a consistent two-phased structure: an intrinsic-dimension peak and sharp drop in cluster count around the middle of the network, with ICL producing subject-organized, hierarchically structured clusters in early layers while fine-tuning produces answer-letter-organized, sharper peaks in late layers.

## Results

Across Llama3-8b, Llama3-70b, and Mistral-7b, the paper finds a consistent two-phased geometric structure with a transition near layer 16–17, marked by an intrinsic dimension peak (from ~2.5 to 14–21 depending on setup) and a sharp drop in the number of density peaks. In early layers, 5-shot ICL produces clusters highly aligned with MMLU subjects (ARI = 0.82 for Llama3-8b, 0.72 for Llama3-70b, 0.63 for Mistral), with 75/77 clusters containing >80% tokens from a single subject. In late layers, fine-tuned models develop answer-organized clusters (ARI ≈ 0.25, 0.45, 0.2 respectively), where the dominant letter exceeds 90% of points in Llama3-8b and Mistral. Fine-tuning primarily modifies representations after the transition, with neighborhood-overlap similarity dropping from 0.5 to ~0.3 (Llama3-8b) and 0.6 to 0.4 (Llama3-70b).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is an analysis paper rather than a method paper, so the central comparison is between in-context learning (0-shot, 1-shot, 2-shot, 5-shot) and LoRA fine-tuning as two paradigms for adapting LLMs to the same MMLU task. Both paradigms reach comparable accuracy (e.g., Llama3-8b: 65.1% micro for 5-shot vs. 64.8% for fine-tuned), yet they produce markedly different internal geometries. ICL wins on early-layer semantic organization: 5-shot ARI with subjects reaches 0.82 (Llama3-8b) versus ~0.15–0.25 for 0-shot and fine-tuned, and the dendrogram structure mirrors subject similarity (STEM, medicine, humanities groupings). Fine-tuning wins on late-layer answer encoding: ARI with answer letters reaches ~0.25–0.45 in the second half of the network, while ICL stays below 0.05 in Llama3-8b and Mistral. In the largest model (Llama3-70b, >75% accuracy), the ICL landscape begins to resemble the fine-tuned one in late layers (ARI oscillating 0.35–0.40), suggesting convergence at scale. The geometric differences are robust to the ADP confidence parameter Z and replicate on a second dataset mixture (TheoremQA + Scibench + Stemez + RACE).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the density-peak clustering framework of d'Errico et al. (ADP) and the Gride intrinsic-dimension estimator to the problem of characterizing LLM hidden representations, moving beyond the linear-probing and distance/angle-based geometry analyses that have dominated prior work. It builds on Park et al.'s discovery of hierarchical concept geometry in the final hidden layer and Doimo et al.'s hierarchical nucleation analysis in CNNs, but applies these tools across all layers and in the specific context of comparing ICL versus SFT. While prior studies compared ICL and SFT on generalization, robustness, and efficiency, this is the first to analyze how the two paradigms differentially sculpt the probability landscape of internal representations, using the Adjusted Rand Index to link cluster structure to semantic subjects and answer labels.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that ICL and SFT achieve comparable accuracy through fundamentally different representational strategies—ICL organizing early layers by subject semantics while SFT sharpening late-layer answer clusters—has direct practical implications for parameter-efficient fine-tuning (e.g., allocating LoRA rank preferentially to later layers) and for interpretability efforts targeting specific network regions. Limitations include reliance on a single QA benchmark (MMLU), a fixed prompt structure, and density-estimation reliability at intrinsic dimensions up to ~22. Open questions include the mechanistic explanation for the mid-network transition, generalization to open-ended generation tasks, and whether the complementary ICL/SFT geometry persists under different fine-tuning protocols or model scales.
