## Problem

Decision-based black-box targeted attacks on image-to-text models remain unexplored: attackers who can only observe the model's output text (no gradients, parameters, or word probabilities) have no effective method to force a specific target caption. Existing gray-box attacks that do achieve targeted outputs suffer from semantic loss, where the model focuses on secondary visual details of a generated target image rather than the attacker's intended semantics, limiting their practical effectiveness.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243355/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a clean RGB image, an attacker-specified target semantics (e.g., a single word like 'photograph'), a surrogate visual model (ResNet-50) for computing Grad-CAM heatmaps, and a pre-trained CLIP text encoder for measuring text similarity. It produces an adversarial image whose perturbation is bounded by a mean per-pixel threshold ε (tested at 5, 10, 15, 25), such that the target image-to-text model outputs a caption semantically matching a target text the attacker constructs from the generated semantic dictionary. The attacker queries the target model only for its final output text, never accessing internal representations.

## Method

AAA is a three-stage evolutionary framework. In Ask, differential evolution perturbs the image within a small radius η to explore nearby feature-space regions; output texts of surviving individuals are parsed for nouns, adjectives, and verbs to build a target semantic dictionary of words that are both semantically relevant to the attacker's goal and close to the input image in the model's feature space. In Attend, Grad-CAM is computed via a surrogate model (ResNet-50) by mapping the target text to the nearest ImageNet category via CLIP text embeddings, producing an attention heatmap that shrinks the perturbation search space from a full hypersphere to a much smaller volume. In Attack, CurrentToBest Differential Evolution (CTB-DE) optimizes pixel perturbations only within the attention-guided region, using the CLIP text-encoder cosine distance between the model's output and the target text as the fitness function. This design avoids semantic loss because the target text is assembled from words already near the input image in feature space, rather than from a separately generated target image as in gray-box methods.

## Results

On Flickr30k with VIT-GPT2 at ε=25, AAA achieves METEOR 0.696, BLEU 0.658, CLIP 0.952, and SPICE 0.634, substantially outperforming the best gray-box baseline transfer+query (METEOR 0.433, BLEU 0.411, CLIP 0.832, SPICE 0.35). On Show-Attend-Tell at ε=25, AAA reaches METEOR 0.855, BLEU 0.799, CLIP 0.964, and SPICE 0.786, again surpassing the strongest gray-box method (METEOR 0.401, BLEU 0.355, CLIP 0.794, SPICE 0.311). At the tighter ε=15 setting, AAA still leads on both models (e.g., CLIP 0.851 on VIT-GPT2 vs. 0.824 for transfer+query gray-box). The black-box adaptations of the gray-box methods (transfer/black, transfer+query/black) perform nearly identically to clean samples, confirming their dependence on the target model's image encoder.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares AAA against two gray-box targeted attack methods: transfer [Lapid & Sipper, ECML-PKDD 2023] and transfer+query [Zhao et al., NeurIPS 2023], both of which require access to the target model's image encoder. AAA, operating purely in a decision-based black-box setting (only output text accessible), outperforms both gray-box baselines in their native gray-box settings across all four metrics on both VIT-GPT2 and Show-Attend-Tell. At ε=25 on VIT-GPT2, AAA's CLIP score of 0.952 exceeds transfer+query's 0.832 by 12 percentage points, and its METEOR of 0.696 exceeds 0.433 by 26 points. The advantage is even more pronounced on Show-Attend-Tell, where AAA's CLIP of 0.964 surpasses transfer+query's 0.794 by 17 points. When the gray-box methods are adapted to a true black-box setting (replacing the target encoder with CLIP's image encoder), their performance collapses to near clean-sample levels (e.g., CLIP 0.651 vs. 0.64 clean), confirming that their effectiveness is entirely dependent on encoder access. The paper attributes the gray-box methods' inferior targeted performance to semantic loss: the text-to-image model generates a target image with richer semantics than the target text, causing the image-to-text model to describe the image rather than reproduce the target text.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the CurrentToBest differential evolution strategy from JADE [39] as its core mutation operator and on Grad-CAM [37] for computing attention heatmaps, but repurposes both within a novel three-stage adversarial attack framework. It departs from the established line of white-box and gray-box attacks on image-to-text models (which rely on gradient access to the image encoder) by formulating the targeted attack as a large-scale combinatorial optimization problem solvable purely through evolutionary search over pixel perturbations. The Ask stage introduces a target semantic dictionary mechanism using WordNet-based similarity scoring, and the Attend stage leverages a surrogate model's attention map to shrink the search space—neither of which has a direct analogue in prior image-to-text attack literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first decision-based black-box targeted attack on image-to-text models, demonstrating that even with only output-text access, an attacker can force a model to produce a specific target caption. The finding that this black-box method outperforms gray-box methods (which have access to the image encoder) highlights a fundamental vulnerability: the text-to-image detour in gray-box attacks introduces semantic loss that caps their effectiveness. Key limitations include the high query count (each population member requires a model query per iteration) and slower convergence (~151 s vs. ~29–97 s for gradient-based gray-box methods), which a service provider could mitigate via rate-limiting. Open questions include whether state-of-the-art evolutionary algorithms can close the efficiency gap and whether the approach extends to autoregressive LLM-based captioners beyond GPT-2.
