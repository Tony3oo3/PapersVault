## Problem

Despite the rapid advancement of text-to-video diffusion models like Sora, no publicly available dataset exists that systematically studies the prompts these models depend on. Existing prompt-gallery datasets such as DiffusionDB target text-to-image generation and differ fundamentally in prompt semantics (static scene descriptions vs. dynamic temporal narratives), modality, and embedding infrastructure. This gap hinders research on text-to-video prompt engineering, large-scale model evaluation, fake video detection, and video copy detection.

## Input / Output

Each VidProM data point pairs a user-authored text-to-video prompt (up to 8192 tokens, far exceeding the 77-token CLIP limit used in DiffusionDB) with a 3072-dimensional embedding from OpenAI text-embedding-3-large, six NSFW probability scores (toxicity, obscenity, identity attack, insult, threat, sexual explicitness), a UUID, a timestamp, and four generated videos of varying lengths (3.0 s Pika, 1.6 s VideoCraft2, 2.0 s Text2Video-Zero, 2.0 s ModelScope). The auxiliary prompt-completion model accepts a short seed phrase (e.g., 'A cat sitting') and outputs three expanded, video-specific prompts enriched with dynamic actions, camera movements, and temporal cues.

## Method

The dataset is curated by scraping chat messages from 10 official Pika Discord channels (July 2023–February 2024) via DiscordChatExporter, extracting prompts with regular expressions, filtering out image-to-video and video-less entries, and deduplicating to yield 1,672,243 unique prompts. Prompts are embedded using OpenAI text-embedding-3-large (8192-token context) and scored for NSFW content across six dimensions using the Detoxify model. Videos are generated by distributing all prompts across 10 servers (8 Nvidia V100 GPUs each, ~50,631 GPU hours total) using four state-of-the-art diffusion models, producing 6.69 million videos totaling ~14.4 million seconds. A semantic de-duplication algorithm (cosine similarity threshold θ = 0.8, computed via Faiss on 8 A100 GPUs in ~0.6 hours) identifies 1,038,805 semantically unique prompts, forming the VidProS subset. For prompt completion, Mistral-7B-v0.1 is fine-tuned on VidProM as a causal language modeling task using DeepSpeed on 8 A100 GPUs in 2 hours.

## Results

VidProM contains 1,672,243 unique text-to-video prompts and 6.69 million generated videos (4×1,672,243) from four diffusion models, with 1,038,805 semantically unique prompts (VidProS). In the fake video detection benchmark (Appendix C), all eight state-of-the-art fake image detectors fail to generalize to video: the best method, Gram-Net (global texture statistics), achieves only 63.64% average accuracy and 69.08% average mAP, while diffusion-specific methods like DIRE (49.44% acc) and UnivFD (51.27% acc) perform near random. The dataset comparison with DiffusionDB shows VidProM has 40.6% more semantically unique prompts (1,038,805 vs. 739,010) and supports prompts up to 8,192 tokens versus CLIP's 77-token limit.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DiffusionDB, VidProM wins on semantic diversity (1,038,805 vs. 739,010 semantically unique prompts, +40.6%), embedding quality (text-embedding-3-large vs. OpenAI-CLIP), prompt length support (8,192 vs. 77 tokens), and collection duration (8 months vs. 1 month), but has fewer total media items (6.69M videos vs. ~14M images) due to the higher cost of video generation. In the fake video detection benchmark, Gram-Net [64] (global texture) is the clear winner at 63.64% average accuracy, outperforming FreDect [44] (60.98%) and all other methods by at least 2 points; however, even Gram-Net drops sharply from 84.19% on Pika to 50.46% on ModelScope, revealing poor cross-model generalization. Diffusion-specific detectors DIRE [47] (49.44%) and UnivFD [49] (51.27%) perform at or below random chance, indicating that features learned for image-level diffusion artifacts do not transfer to video frames. GAN-era methods CNNSpot [42] (50.41%) and LGrad [46] (51.69%) are similarly ineffective, confirming that the generative process shift from GANs to diffusion models invalidates prior detection cues. The overall conclusion is that no existing method reliably detects fake videos, motivating the need for dedicated video-level detection research enabled by VidProM.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the prompt-gallery dataset paradigm established by DiffusionDB (text-to-image) to the text-to-video modality, arguing that video prompts are semantically distinct (longer, more dynamic, time-aware) and require a dedicated resource. It departs from existing text-video datasets (WebVid-10M, Panda-70M, HDVILA-100M) that pair captions with real videos, instead collecting user-authored prompts paired with diffusion-generated videos from four models (Pika, VideoCraft2, Text2Video-Zero, ModelScope). For the prompt completion component, it fine-tunes Mistral-7B on the collected prompts, following the text-generation approach of prior text-to-image prompt generators but applied to the video domain for the first time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

VidProM fills a clear gap as the first large-scale text-to-video prompt dataset, enabling research directions the authors identify as previously unaddressed: text-to-video prompt engineering, efficient video generation via prompt retrieval, and video copy detection for diffusion models. The benchmarking of eight state-of-the-art fake image detectors on generated video frames (all near random accuracy) highlights a significant open problem in AI-generated content detection that the dataset is positioned to help solve. Key limitations include short video durations (1.6–3 seconds), reliance on four models of varying quality, and the NSFW content that slipped through moderation, though the authors provide six-dimensional NSFW scores to mitigate this. Future work with Sora-quality models and longer videos would substantially strengthen the dataset's utility.
