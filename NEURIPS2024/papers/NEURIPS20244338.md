## Problem

Existing adversarial perturbation methods for customized diffusion models (e.g., DreamBooth) are prompt-specific: they optimize perturbations against a fixed set of enumerated prompts and fail when an attacker uses an unseen prompt at inference. This paper addresses the gap by asking whether a single perturbation can be made robust to an arbitrary, unbounded set of attack prompts without pre-enumerating them.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20244338/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a target image (e.g., a person's photo or an artist's painting), a short reference text prompt (e.g., 'a photo of sks person'), and a fine-tuned Stable Diffusion 1.5 model (DreamBooth, LoRA, or Textual Invariant) as inputs. It produces a human-imperceptible adversarial perturbation δ bounded by a noise budget η (default 0.05, i.e., ~12.75/255 per pixel) that, when added to the original image, causes the diffusion model to generate low-quality, semantically incoherent outputs across diverse unseen prompts. The perturbation is computed in ~4–6 minutes on a single A800 GPU.

## Method

PAP models the distribution of all possible attack prompts as a Gaussian N(c_x, H⁻¹) in the text-embedding space via Laplace approximation, where c_x is the mode (the prompt embedding that best describes the image) and H⁻¹ is the covariance. Two estimators are derived: φ finds c_x by momentum-based iterative minimization of the diffusion loss starting from the reference prompt, and ψ estimates H⁻¹ as a scalar times the identity matrix using a second-order Taylor expansion of the loss around c_x (with a proven cosine-dissimilarity upper bound of 0.0909 under standard settings). The final perturbation is then obtained by maximizing the expected generation loss over the Gaussian prompt distribution via Monte Carlo sampling of prompts combined with PGD-style signed gradient ascent on the image. This distribution-aware objective, rather than a single-prompt objective, is what grants the perturbation its prompt-agnostic robustness.

## Results

PAP achieves the best defense performance across all six metrics on all three datasets. On Celeb-HQ, PAP attains FID=249.9, CLIP-I=0.5539, LPIPS=0.6730, LAION=5.280, BRISQUE=36.95, and CLIP=0.2543. On Wikiart (style protection), PAP reaches FID=448.3 and LPIPS=0.7782, outperforming the second-best method by at least 14.16% in FID and 12.71% in CLIP-I. The method requires only ~4–6 minutes per image set on an A800 GPU with a noise budget of 0.05.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PAP consistently outperforms all three prompt-specific baselines (AdvDM, Anti-DB, IAdvDM) on every metric across all three datasets. On Celeb-HQ, PAP exceeds the second-best Anti-DB by 13.55% in LPIPS (0.6730 vs. 0.5924) and reduces CLIP-I by 13.06% (0.5539 vs. 0.6371). On VGGFace2, PAP surpasses the runner-up by 6.35% in BRISQUE (35.02 vs. 32.93) and reduces CLIP by 6.83% (0.2577 vs. 0.2766). On Wikiart, PAP's FID of 448.3 exceeds the next-best AdvDM (392.7) by 14.16%, and its CLIP-I of 0.5641 is 12.71% lower than Anti-DB's 0.6462. The key differentiator is generalization: while baselines are trained on a single prompt and degrade sharply on unseen prompts, PAP's distribution-based approach maintains strong defense across all ten test prompts, which is the core motivation of the paper.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the adversarial perturbation line for customized diffusion models (AdvDM, Anti-DreamBooth, IAdvDM, Glaze, Photoguard) by shifting from a prompt-specific paradigm—where perturbations are optimized for enumerated, fixed prompts—to a prompt-agnostic formulation that models the attacker's prompt distribution via Laplace approximation and maximizes a disturbance expectation over that distribution. The core optimization loop draws directly from the AdvDM framework and momentum-based PGD variants (MIFGSM), while the distributional modeling introduces tools from Bayesian inference (Laplace approximation, Hessian estimation via Taylor expansion) that are novel to this defense setting. The key conceptual departure is treating the prompt as a random variable with a learnable distribution rather than a fixed input, which eliminates the brittleness of prior methods to unseen prompts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The prompt-agnostic framing addresses a critical practical gap: real-world attackers are not constrained to a known set of prompts, making prompt-specific defenses essentially useless in deployment. The method's generality across fine-tuning approaches (DreamBooth, LoRA, Textual Inversion) and datasets strengthens its practical relevance. However, open questions remain: the Laplace/Gaussian assumption on prompt distributions may break down for highly diverse or adversarially chosen prompt spaces, the Hessian simplification to a scalar-identity matrix (cosine dissimilarity ≤ 0.0909) is a significant approximation whose impact on tail behavior is unclear, and the method's degradation under DiffPure purification and JPEG compression (Table 6, Table 13) suggests it is not yet robust to standard image-processing pipelines that attackers could exploit.
