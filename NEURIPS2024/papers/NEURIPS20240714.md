## Problem

OOD detection methods trained with auxiliary outliers overfit to the limited diversity of those outliers, failing to generalize to unknown OOD data. The paper identifies this gap from a generalization-theory perspective—showing that the distribution shift between auxiliary outliers and true OOD data sets a hard floor on detection error—and asks how to provably enhance the effective diversity of a fixed, limited outlier set without costly data collection.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240714/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 32×32 RGB images (CIFAR-10/100) or 224×224 RGB images (ImageNet-200) as ID training data, plus an unlabeled auxiliary outlier set (ImageNet-RC or ImageNet-800). At test time it produces a continuous OOD detection score (energy, max-softmax, or K+1 logit depending on the chosen regularization) and a binary ID/OOD decision via a threshold calibrated to 95% ID recall. The mixed outliers generated during training are never seen at test time; only the trained classifier parameters θ are retained.

## Method

diverseMix is a score-adaptive mixup applied to auxiliary outliers during training. Instead of sampling the interpolation weight λ from a fixed Beta(α,α), it uses Beta(ŝ_i·α, ŝ_j·α) where ŝ_i = exp(S(x_i,θ)/T) / Σ_k exp(S(x_k,θ)/T) is a softmax-normalized OOD score of each outlier under the current model. Outliers that the model still scores highly (i.e., novel or under-learned) receive larger interpolation weights, steering the generated mixed samples toward regions of the input space the model has not yet encountered. This semantic-level interpolation (Assumption 1) guarantees the mixed outliers fall outside the original outlier manifold, formally expanding the outlier support from X_aux to GX_aux ⊃ X_aux. The theoretical contribution (Theorems 1–3) shows this expansion strictly reduces the distribution-shift term in the Rademacher-based generalization bound, providing a provable guarantee that diverseMix lowers the OOD detection error. The method is plug-and-play with any OOD regularization loss (Energy, OE, K+1) and requires no architectural changes.

## Results

On the CIFAR-10 benchmark, diverseMix achieves a FPR95 of 1.92% (AUROC 99.42%, AUPR 99.51%), outperforming the best baseline POEM (FPR95 2.54%) by a relative 24.4% reduction. On CIFAR-100, diverseMix reaches FPR95 8.51% (AUROC 98.24%), a 43.8% relative improvement over POEM (FPR95 15.14%). On the large-scale ImageNet-200 benchmark, diverseMix attains the best average AUROC of 89.36% and the best far-OOD AUROC of 91.35%, while maintaining 85.95% ID accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

diverseMix outperforms all 13 baselines on both CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100 FPR95. Among ID-only methods, it reduces FPR95 from 58.98% (MSP) and 26.55% (ODIN) to 1.92% on CIFAR-10, and from 80.30% (MSP) and 47.89% (Mahalanobis) to 8.51% on CIFAR-100. Among auxiliary-outlier methods, it beats the strongest competitor POEM by 0.62% FPR95 on CIFAR-10 (1.92 vs. 2.54) and by 6.63% on CIFAR-100 (8.51 vs. 15.14), while also surpassing NTOM (4.00/18.77), SOFL (5.41/19.32), and OE (9.66/19.54). MixOE and DivOE, which also use mixup-based strategies, underperform diverseMix substantially (14.54/27.71 and 11.41/18.91 FPR95 respectively), confirming that the score-adaptive interpolation is critical. On the ImageNet-200 benchmark, diverseMix achieves the best average AUROC (89.36%) and far-OOD AUROC (91.35%), surpassing OE (89.05%/90.51%) and DPN (87.89%/89.85%), while also maintaining competitive near-OOD performance (86.36% AUROC vs. OE's 86.86%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the mixup data augmentation technique (Zhang et al., ICLR 2018), repurposing it not for ID data regularization but for generating semantically novel auxiliary outliers in OOD detection. It departs from prior outlier-exposure methods (OE, Energy, POEM, DivOE) and mixup-based OOD methods (MixOE, OpenMix) by providing a Rademacher-complexity-based generalization bound that formally links auxiliary outlier diversity to the upper bound of OOD detection error, then proposing a score-adaptive interpolation strategy that dynamically weights mixup based on the model's current confidence in each outlier. The key conceptual departure from MixOE and OpenMix is that those methods mix ID and OOD samples to model the ID-to-OOD transition, whereas diverseMix mixes outliers among themselves to expand the semantic coverage of the outlier set.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The theoretical framework (Theorems 1–3) provides a clean, if somewhat idealized, explanation for why diverse auxiliary outliers improve OOD generalization, which could guide future method design beyond the specific diverseMix algorithm. The practical method is simple, plug-and-play, and compatible with multiple OOD regularization losses (Energy, OE, K+1), making it easy to adopt. However, the core Assumption 1 (that semantic interpolation produces points outside both parent semantic classes) is difficult to verify empirically and may not hold for all data distributions or architectures. Open questions include whether the score-adaptive mechanism provides meaningful gains over vanilla mixup at scale, whether the theoretical guarantees transfer to non-CNN backbones or non-image modalities, and whether the approach remains effective when the auxiliary outlier set is extremely small or semantically homogeneous.
