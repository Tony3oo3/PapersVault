## Problem

Existing citation prediction methods treat all citations as equivalent in a binary classification, ignoring that citations play vastly different roles ranging from foundational knowledge bases to superficial mentions. While LLMs could discern these logical relationships, two practical barriers remain: candidate sets in real databases contain millions of papers far exceeding LLM context windows, and directly prompting LLMs tends to produce surface-level textual similarity judgments rather than deeper logical reasoning about why papers are cited.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241526/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes only the title and abstract of the query paper and each candidate paper (no full text, no network features), with candidate sets scaled up to 100K papers. The retrieval module first narrows this to a small set (retrieval size r_q, optimally 7–8), and the LLM agentic module then re-ranks only the lower-ranked portion of that set, exempting the top (2t_q^1 − r_q) candidates from reranking to reduce token consumption. The final output is an ordered list of t_q^1 predicted core citations, evaluated via PREC@3/5 and NDCG@3/5.

## Method

HLM-Cite is a two-stage hybrid pipeline combining a fine-tuned embedding model with an LLM agentic workflow. The retrieval module uses GTE-base (110M parameters, 768-dim vectors) with a two-stage curriculum fine-tuning: Stage 1 performs binary classification (core citation vs. non-citation) with cross-entropy loss to establish easy discrimination, then Stage 2 introduces superficial citations as hard negatives and optimizes a NeuralNDCG ranking loss; the lower 7 BERT layers are frozen throughout. The ranking module deploys three LLM agents—Analyzer (extracts the logical reason each candidate is cited), Decider (produces a final ranking with explanations), and Guider (supplies a manually verified one-shot chain-of-thought example based on a Transformer-XL case)—all driven by GPT-3.5/4o with temperature 0. This architecture enables scaling to 100K candidates while achieving a 17.6% PREC@5 improvement over the strongest baseline (GTE-large-v1.5) across 19 scientific fields.

## Results

On the Microsoft Academic Graph dataset (19 scientific fields, 10K candidate sets), HLM-Cite with GPT-4o achieves an overall PREC@5 of 0.655 and NDCG@5 of 0.686, surpassing the strongest baseline (GTE-large-v1.5 at PREC@5 = 0.563, NDCG@5 = 0.606) by 17.6% in PREC@5. With GPT-3.5 as the agent LLM, the method still reaches PREC@5 = 0.644 and NDCG@5 = 0.677. The method scales to 100K candidate sets with up to 18.5% relative improvement over baselines, and the optimal retrieval size is 8 for natural science and 7 for social science.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HLM-Cite outperforms all 10 baseline categories across every metric and domain. Against the strongest embedding-based baselines, GTE-large-v1.5 (PREC@5 = 0.563) and SPECTER-2.0 (PREC@5 = 0.515), HLM-Cite with GPT-4o gains +0.092 and +0.140 in PREC@5 respectively. The scientific-domain-specific models (SciBERT at 0.046, SciNCL at 0.497, SciMult-MoE at 0.496, SPECTER at 0.460) all trail significantly, with SciBERT performing near-random. Network-aware pretraining methods (METAG at 0.090, SciPATTON at 0.371) also fall well short. General-purpose embedding models (BERT-base at 0.036, GTE-large at 0.557) confirm that domain-adapted retrieval plus LLM reasoning is essential. The 17.6% overall PREC@5 improvement is statistically significant (p < 0.001 via two-tailed t-test averaged across all fields), and the advantage grows with candidate set size, reaching 18.5% at 100K candidates.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the citation prediction literature (SPECTER, SciNCL, PATTON, SciMult) by introducing the concept of 'core citation,' reframing the task from binary cite/non-cite classification to a three-way distinction among core, superficial, and non-citations. Methodologically, it departs from prior single-model approaches by cascading a curriculum-finetuned GTE-base embedding model (for coarse retrieval from 100K candidates) with a multi-agent LLM workflow (Analyzer, Decider, Guider) for fine-grained ranking via chain-of-thought reasoning. The curriculum fine-tuning strategy—first learning to separate core from non-citations, then adding superficial citations as hard negatives with NeuralNDCG loss—is a deliberate adaptation of progressive training ideas to the scientific text domain.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in scalability: handling 100K candidate papers (thousands of times more than prior methods) makes text-based citation prediction viable for real-world research assistance across entire domains. The core citation concept, validated through 12M-paper statistics showing higher keyword overlap and more frequent in-text mentions, provides a principled way to distinguish foundational references from superficial ones, with implications for science-of-science studies on knowledge production. Key limitations include LLM hallucination (unfaithful analysis on specific samples) and dependence on proprietary GPT APIs for best performance, as open-source LLMs (Llama3, Mixtral) degrade substantially. Open questions include how to verify and constrain LLM agent outputs for reliability, and whether the workflow's gains persist as open-source models close the reasoning gap.
