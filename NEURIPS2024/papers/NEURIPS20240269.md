## Problem

Existing temporal knowledge graph reasoning methods face a fundamental trade-off: deep learning approaches lack interpretability and cannot be efficiently updated for evolving TKG data, while rule-based methods struggle to extract and adapt temporal logical rules to distribution shifts. Although LLMs show strong temporal reasoning, they operate as black boxes, suffer from hallucinations, and require resource-intensive fine-tuning to incorporate new knowledge. The paper addresses how to leverage LLMs for interpretable, dynamically-updating rule-based TKGR without any fine-tuning.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a temporal knowledge graph of quadruplets (e_s, r, e_o, t), a query of the form (e_s, r, ?, t_l), and a frozen LLM (ChatGPT) plus a pre-trained GNN (RE-GCN or TiRGN) as the graph-based reasoning component. It produces a ranked list of candidate entities scored by a weighted combination of rule-based confidence (incorporating temporal exponential decay) and GNN inner-product scores, with the weight α controlling the balance. The intermediate outputs include extracted temporal logical rules in the form r(e_s, e_o, t_l) ← ∧ r*(e_s, e_o, t_i), which are explicitly interpretable and updated across iterations.

## Method

LLM-DA is a four-stage pipeline that avoids LLM fine-tuning entirely. First, constrained Markovian random walks with temporal-order filtering and exponential-decay transition probabilities sample temporal logical rules from historical data. Second, a contextual relation selector uses Sentence-BERT cosine similarity to pick the top-k semantically relevant relations for each rule head, which are fed alongside sampled rules into ChatGPT to generate high-coverage general rules. Third, a dynamic adaptation strategy iteratively (5 iterations) identifies low-confidence rules on current data and prompts the LLM to rewrite them using rules freshly extracted from current data, ensuring rules track the evolving distribution. Fourth, candidate reasoning scores each entity by combining rule-based scores (rule confidence plus temporal decay of the supporting path) with GNN-based scores via a weighted sum (α=0.8–0.9), yielding the final ranked prediction.

## Results

On ICEWS14, LLM-DA(TiRGN) achieves MRR 0.471, Hit@1 0.369, Hit@3 0.526, and Hit@10 0.671, outperforming the strongest baseline TiRGN (MRR 0.441) by 3.0 MRR points. On ICEWS05-15, LLM-DA(TiRGN) reaches MRR 0.521, Hit@1 0.416, Hit@3 0.586, and hit@10 0.728, surpassing Mixtral-8x7B-CoH (MRR 0.497) by 2.4 MRR points. The method requires no fine-tuning of the LLM (ChatGPT is used as-is) and uses RE-GCN or TiRGN as the graph-based reasoning module.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LLM-DA(TiRGN) wins on all four metrics against every baseline on both ICEWS14 and ICEWS05-15. Against the strongest GNN baseline TiRGN, it gains +3.0 MRR on ICEWS14 (0.471 vs 0.441) and +2.6 MRR on ICEWS05-15 (0.521 vs 0.495). Against the strongest LLM-based baseline Mixtral-8x7B-CoH, it gains +3.2 MRR on ICEWS14 (0.471 vs 0.439) and +2.4 MRR on ICEWS05-15 (0.521 vs 0.497). The rule-based baseline TLogic (MRR 0.390/0.459) and the prompt-based PPT (MRR 0.384/0.389) are substantially outperformed, with PPT lagging by over 8 MRR points on ICEWS05-15. The chain-of-history LLM baselines (Llama-2-7b-CoH, Vicuna-7b-CoH) do not report MRR but their Hit@10 values (0.591–0.707) remain below LLM-DA's 0.671–0.728. GPT-NeoX, which also uses in-context learning without fine-tuning, is outperformed by LLM-DA by 3.7 MRR on ICEWS14 (0.471 vs 0.434 estimated from hit@10 0.565) and is unavailable on ICEWS05-15.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of rule-based TKGR (TLogic, TLmod, TILP) and LLM-based TKGR (GPT-NeoX, Mixtral-8x7B-CoH, PPT), departing from both in a key way: rather than using LLMs as direct predictors over historical events or learning rules via constrained random walks alone, LLM-DA uses LLMs as *rule generators* whose outputs are then dynamically adapted to current data. The constrained Markovian random walk for rule sampling borrows from TLogic's approach, while the dynamic adaptation strategy and contextual relation selector (Sentence-BERT cosine filtering) are novel components not present in prior rule-based or LLM-based TKGR methods. The graph-based reasoning module (RE-GCN or TiRGN) is repurposed as a complementary candidate generator rather than a standalone predictor.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses two practical pain points in LLM-based TKGR: interpretability (rules make the reasoning traceable) and distribution shift (dynamic rule updates avoid costly LLM fine-tuning). However, the gains over strong baselines like Mixtral-8x7B-CoH are modest (roughly 2–4 MRR points), and the method's reliance on manually crafted prompts, a fixed number of adaptation iterations, and API-based LLM calls limits its scalability and reproducibility. Open questions include whether automated prompt learning or query-conditioned rule generation could close the remaining gap, and whether the approach extends to TKGs with richer node semantics or larger relation vocabularies.
