## Problem

Standard denoising diffusion probabilistic models require hundreds to thousands of sequential denoising steps to produce high-quality images, making sampling computationally expensive. Additionally, unlike GANs, the latent space of DDPMs is not interpretable or smoothly interpolable, limiting their utility for controlled generation and editing tasks. UDPM addresses both limitations by generalizing the diffusion process to simultaneously degrade the signal in the spatial domain, drastically reducing the number of steps needed while yielding a compact, structured latent space.

## Input / Output

For unconditional generation, UDPM takes a small Gaussian noise vector (e.g., 8×8×3 for a 64×64 output with L=3 steps and γ=2) and produces a full-resolution RGB image. For class-conditional generation (CIFAR-10), a class label is additionally injected via a label-encoding block. The total FLOPs across all three reverse steps (operating on 8×8, 16×16, and 32×32 inputs) amount to only ~30% of a single 64×64 denoising step in a standard DDPM/EDM, and the model achieves 765 FPS on an RTX A6000 versus 255 FPS for one standard step.

## Method

UDPM augments the standard forward diffusion step with a spatial downsampling operator H (a blur filter followed by stride-γ subsampling), so each forward step both adds noise and reduces spatial resolution. The reverse process therefore performs a joint denoising-and-upsampling operation: a UNet f_θ predicts H^{l-1}x_0 from x_l (a super-resolution task at each step), and the analytic posterior q(x_{l-1}|x_l, x_0) is sampled using a covariance Σ_l that is applied efficiently via FFT. Training combines a weighted L2 loss, a VGG-based perceptual loss, and an adversarial loss (with step-dependent weights) to produce sharp outputs, since the per-step super-resolution objective alone yields blurry results. The network uses a depth-to-space layer to upsample by γ, and the entire pipeline requires only L=3 steps with manually tuned noise schedules (six scalar hyperparameters total), achieving FID 6.86 on CIFAR-10 with less than one standard diffusion step of compute.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, UDPM achieves an FID of 6.86 using only 3 diffusion steps whose total computational cost is equivalent to 0.3 of a single standard denoising step (13.35 GFLOPs vs. 40.62 GFLOPs for one DDPM/EDM step). On FFHQ and AFHQv2 (64×64), UDPM achieves FID scores of 7.41 and 7.10 respectively, also with 3 steps. Sampling speed reaches 765 FPS on an NVIDIA RTX A6000, compared to 255 FPS for a single denoising diffusion step with a similar network.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UDPM (FID 6.86, <1 step equivalent) outperforms all single-step methods: TDPM (FID 8.91) and Consistency Models (FID 8.70), and all 5-step methods including DPM-Solver++ (FID 288.99 at 5 steps), GENIE (11.20), DEIS (15.37), and GGDM (13.77). It also beats DDIM (93.51 at 5 steps) by a wide margin. The only method that achieves a lower FID on CIFAR-10 is DDGAN (FID 4.08 at 2 steps), which incorporates a dedicated discriminator network. EDM achieves the best absolute FID (1.79) but requires 35 steps; at 5 steps its FID degrades to 35.54, far worse than UDPM's 6.86. On FFHQ and AFHQv2, UDPM (FID 7.41 and 7.10) is substantially better than EDM at 5 steps (FID 344.76 and 266.02), though EDM at 79 steps remains superior (FID 2.39 and 1.96). The key advantage of UDPM is achieving competitive quality at a fraction of the computational cost of any baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

UDPM generalizes the standard DDPM/EDM forward process by augmenting the noise-adding step with a spatial downsampling operator (blur + subsampling), so that the reverse process simultaneously denoises and upsamples. This departs from cold diffusion [4] (which replaces noise with arbitrary transforms but lacks an explicit reverse process), soft diffusion [8] (which blurs before noise but forfeits analytical access to the posterior), and wavelet-score approaches [12,17] (which learn high-frequency coefficients rather than performing explicit upsampling). The training objective borrows the perceptual and adversarial losses from super-resolution literature [38,23], and the UNet backbone follows the NCSN/EDM architecture [37,18], while the discriminator follows DDGAN [41].

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical contribution is a ~30× reduction in sampling cost relative to standard DDPM (3 UDPM steps ≈ 0.3 of one diffusion step) while retaining a GAN-like interpretable and interpolable latent space — a property that pure diffusion models lack. However, evaluation is confined to 32×32 and 64×64 images on a single GPU, leaving open whether the approach scales to high-resolution or large-scale datasets (e.g., ImageNet, 256×256+). The interpretability, while promising, is still qualitatively weaker than StyleGAN's latent space, and the paper itself flags this as a limitation. Future work on editing operations and larger-scale validation would be needed to confirm the broader utility of the spatially-structured latent space.
