## Problem

For computationally intensive, non-differentiable models (e.g., those involving numerical ODE integration or particle filters), importance-weighted forward-KL variational inference (IWFVI) must generate a fresh set of samples at every gradient step, making each update as expensive as a full model evaluation. This paper asks whether model evaluations can be reused across multiple gradient steps without sacrificing posterior approximation quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20244402/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a point-wise evaluable (but not necessarily differentiable) joint density p(y,z), observations y, and initial variational parameters φ₀. It produces optimized variational posterior parameters φ minimizing the forward KL upper bound, from which approximate posterior samples can be drawn. A key operational detail is that each batch of N samples is evaluated against the model exactly once and then reused across multiple optimizer steps until the trust region is violated, so the total number of model evaluations is decoupled from the number of gradient steps.

## Method

VISA replaces the per-step resampling of IWFVI with a sequence of sample-average approximations (SAAs). At any point, a fixed set of N samples {z^(i)} is drawn from a frozen proposal q_φ̃, and the deterministic surrogate objective L̂_F(φ; φ̃) = Σ ŵ_φ̃^(i) log p(y,z^(i))/q_φ(z^(i)) is optimized with a standard gradient-based optimizer (e.g., Adam). The SAA is refreshed only when the effective sample size (ESS) of the ratio v(z) = q_φ(z)/q_φ̃(z) over the fixed sample set drops below a user-chosen threshold α ∈ [0,1], which serves as a trust-region criterion motivated by the χ²-divergence between the proposal and the current variational distribution. This design means that for small learning rates, many gradient steps can be taken per model evaluation, substantially reducing total compute while the ESS-based refresh mechanism prevents the optimizer from overfitting to a stale set of high-weight samples.

## Results

On 128-dim diagonal and 32-dim dense Gaussian targets, VISA with N=10 samples converges substantially faster than IWFVI and BBVI-SF at conservative learning rates (0.001–0.01), reaching comparable symmetric KL-divergence with fewer model evaluations. At lr=0.05, VISA becomes unstable and fails to converge reliably, while IWFVI still converges. On the Lotka-Volterra ODE model (N=100 samples), VISA achieves a forward KL-divergence comparable to IWFVI (convergence threshold −712.7 nats) while requiring significantly fewer model evaluations at smaller step sizes (0.001, 0.005). On the Pickover Attractor (N=10 samples, 500-particle filter), VISA converges more stably and with fewer batch evaluations than IWFVI.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against IWFVI (Le et al., 2018), the primary baseline, VISA wins on computational efficiency at conservative learning rates: it reaches comparable forward KL-divergence with significantly fewer model evaluations on all three tasks (Gaussians, Lotka-Volterra, Pickover Attractor). However, VISA loses at higher learning rates (0.05), where it becomes unstable and fails to converge while IWFVI still succeeds, and VISA is more susceptible to bias when the ESS threshold α is set too low (e.g., α=0.3 leads to underestimation of posterior variance). Against BBVI-RP (Kingma & Welling, 2014), VISA is strictly dominated in convergence speed since BBVI-RP exploits model differentiability with a single sample per gradient step, but BBVI-RP is inapplicable to non-differentiable models. Against BBVI-SF (Ranganath et al., 2014), VISA clearly wins at lower learning rates, converging faster with the same N=10 samples, while both methods fail at the highest learning rate (0.05). Overall, VISA's advantage is most pronounced when the model is expensive to evaluate and a conservative step size is used, making it a drop-in replacement for IWFVI that reuses model evaluations across multiple gradient steps.

## Relation to Prior Work

VISA extends importance-weighted forward-KL variational inference (IWFVI, from the reweighted wake-sleep lineage) by replacing the fresh-sample-at-each-step protocol with a sequence of sample-average approximations, each valid within an ESS-defined trust region. This adapts the SAA framework recently applied to reparameterized reverse-KL VI (Giordano et al., 2024; Burroni et al., 2024) to the forward-KL setting, where samples are drawn from the *parameterized* variational distribution rather than a fixed parameter-free base, and the model need not be differentiable. The key conceptual departure from prior SAA-VI work is the trust-region mechanism: rather than fixing samples for the entire optimization, VISA refreshes them whenever the variational parameters drift too far from the proposal, balancing sample reuse against approximation bias.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

For practitioners working with expensive, non-differentiable simulation-based models (ODE solvers, particle filters, stochastic programs), VISA offers a practical drop-in replacement for IWFVI that can reduce model evaluations by a significant factor at conservative learning rates. However, the method is more susceptible to bias and posterior-variance underestimation than IWFVI, particularly at low ESS thresholds, and is not well-suited to high-dimensional latent spaces. Open questions include whether second-order optimization can be stabilized in the forward-KL SAA setting (L-BFGS experiments failed) and whether adaptive trust-region scheduling could mitigate the bias-variance tradeoff without sacrificing the computational savings.
