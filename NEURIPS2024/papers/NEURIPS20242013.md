## Problem

Rectified flows learn smooth ODE trajectories that are less susceptible to truncation error than diffusion models, but prior work required at least two Reflow iterations (plus an optional distillation stage) to achieve competitive one-step generation, making training computationally expensive and still lagging behind distillation methods like consistency models in the low-NFE regime. The paper asks whether a single Reflow iteration can suffice and, if so, what training techniques are needed to close the performance gap.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242013/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes standard Gaussian noise z ~ N(0, I) (optionally conditioned on a class label for ImageNet) and integrates the learned velocity field v_θ from t=1 to t=0 to produce a synthetic RGB image at 32×32 (CIFAR-10) or 64×64 (AFHQ, FFHQ, ImageNet). Because the model is a neural ODE, it also supports inversion from a real image back to noise by integrating the ODE in reverse, enabling applications like interpolation and image-to-image translation with as few as 6 total NFEs (4 for inversion + 2 for generation).

## Method

The paper's central insight is that under realistic data distributions, the linear interpolation trajectories of 1-rectified flow-generated pairs rarely intersect, implying the optimal 2-rectified flow is already nearly straight and a single Reflow iteration suffices. This observation unlocks two key training improvements: (1) a U-shaped timestep distribution p_t(u) ∝ exp(4u) + exp(−4u) that concentrates training on the challenging endpoints (t≈0 and t≈1) where the model must directly predict data from noise or vice versa, and (2) an LPIPS-Huber premetric loss that combines a Pseudo-Huber term with a perceptual LPIPS term weighted by (1−t), which is valid because the near-zero lower bound of the training loss means non-L2 losses do not shift the optimum. The 1-rectified flow is initialized from a pre-trained EDM model via a time/scale conversion (Proposition 1), and an optional fine-tuning stage incorporates real data–synthetic noise pairs collected by inverting the 1-rectified flow ODE. Together these techniques reduce the one-step FID on CIFAR-10 from 12.21 (vanilla 2-rectified flow) to 3.07, outperforming consistency distillation and progressive distillation in the 1–2 NFE regime.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, 2-rectified flow++ achieves FID 3.07 at 1 NFE and 2.40 at 2 NFE, reducing the FID of the previous 2-rectified flow (12.21) by 75% in the one-step setting. On ImageNet 64×64, the method achieves FID 3.07 (1 NFE) and 2.40 (2 NFE), outperforming all distillation-based baselines (CD, PD, DFNO, TRACT, BOOT) in the one-step regime and closing the gap with iCT (4.02 vs. 3.07 at 1 NFE). Ablations on AFHQ and FFHQ confirm consistent gains: the U-shaped timestep distribution improves FID by 23–28%, and the LPIPS-Huber loss provides an additional 26–50% improvement over the Huber-only variant.

## Baselines & Comparisons

2-rectified flow++ (FID 3.07 at 1 NFE) outperforms all distillation-based methods on CIFAR-10: CD (3.55), PD (9.12), DFNO (3.78), TRACT (3.78), and DMD (3.77). It also surpasses the previous 2-rectified flow (12.21) by 9.14 FID points (75% reduction) and beats 2-rectified flow + distillation (4.85) without requiring an extra distillation stage. However, it still trails the best consistency models: iCT (2.83) and iCT-deep (2.51) at 1 NFE, and CTM+GAN (1.98) which uses an additional GAN objective. On ImageNet 64×64, the method (FID 3.07 at 1 NFE) beats CD (6.20), PD (15.39), DFNO (7.83), TRACT (7.43), and BOOT (16.3), and narrows the gap to iCT (4.02) to within ~1 FID point, despite using half the batch size and no real data during training. At 2 NFE, 2-rectified flow++ (2.40) matches iCT (2.46) on CIFAR-10 and outperforms iCT (3.20) on ImageNet.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Rectified Flow framework and Reflow algorithm of Liu et al. (2022), which iteratively straightens ODE trajectories by retraining on synthetic data-noise pairs. The key conceptual departure is the argument that a single Reflow iteration suffices to produce nearly straight trajectories, based on the observation that linear interpolation paths of 1-rectified flow rarely intersect in realistic settings—contradicting the prior empirical consensus that at least two Reflow rounds (plus optional distillation) are needed. The paper also leverages EDM (Karras et al., 2022) for initialization via a time/scale conversion derived from Pokle et al. (2023), and introduces a U-shaped timestep distribution and LPIPS-Huber premetric as training improvements that are enabled by the near-zero lower bound of the 2-rectified flow loss.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By reducing the Reflow pipeline from multiple expensive training stages to a single stage, this work makes rectified flows substantially more practical and competitive with knowledge distillation methods (CD, PD) in the 1–2 NFE regime, while retaining the qualitative advantages of neural ODEs (inversion, likelihood evaluation, flexible NFE). The conceptual insight about trajectory intersections being rare is a useful reframing that shifts the research focus from stacking Reflow rounds to improving single-round training. Limitations include that the method still trails iCT in FID, training is ~15% slower due to LPIPS, and the single-Reflow argument is supported by empirical evidence (norm/autocorrelation analysis) rather than a formal proof; whether the approach scales to higher-resolution or video generation remains open.
