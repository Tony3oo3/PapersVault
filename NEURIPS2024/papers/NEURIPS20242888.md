## Problem

Traditional inner-product-based convolution in 3D point cloud networks suffers from explicit feature loss when input patches are nearly orthogonal to the kernel, causing the dot product to vanish. The paper investigates whether ℓp-norm-based convolution can serve as a more robust and efficient alternative, and identifies which specific norm (p=1,2,∞) is most suitable for 3D point cloud tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242888/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 3D point clouds where each point is represented by XYZ coordinates (9-dimensional vectors including RGB and normalized room location for S3DIS), processed through sliding windows of size m×n. Outputs are per-point part segmentation labels (ShapeNet), per-point semantic class labels across 13 categories (S3DIS), or complete point clouds for garment pose estimation (GarmentNets). The ℓ1-norm convolution computes the negative Manhattan distance between each sampled patch and the kernel, replacing the standard dot product, which means the underlying arithmetic is addition rather than multiplication.

## Method

The paper replaces the inner-product convolution in PointNet/PointNet++ with an ℓ1-norm (Manhattan distance) based convolution, proving universal approximation for ℓp-norm nets and showing via variance analysis that ℓp-norm convolutions are more robust to Gaussian noise than inner-product ones. Since ℓ∞ discards all but the maximum element and ℓ2 reduces to a linear transform of standard convolution, ℓ1 is identified as the most promising norm. To overcome the vanishing gradient and signSGD misalignment inherent in ℓ1-norm training, the authors propose a Mixed Gradient Strategy (MGS) that dynamically blends signSGD and ℓ2-like gradients via a decaying coefficient λ^k, and a Dynamic Learning Rate Controller (DLC) that constrains the learning rate between two time-varying bounds. A regret-based convergence guarantee of O(√T*) is established for the combined optimizer (OMD), and experiments on ShapeNet, S3DIS, and GarmentNets show competitive accuracy with ~61% energy reduction due to the replacement of multiplications with additions.

## Results

On ShapeNet parts segmentation, ℓ₁-PointNet++ achieves 86.5% mean mIoU, slightly surpassing PointNet++ (86.2%), while ℓ₁-PointNet reaches 83.3% vs. PointNet's 83.7%. On S3DIS semantic segmentation, ℓ₁-PointNet++ attains 53.9% mean IoU and 82.9% overall accuracy, matching or exceeding PointNet++ (53.5% / 83.0%), while reducing energy consumption by ~61% (1.328 μJ vs. 3.395 μJ). On GarmentNets pose estimation, ℓ₁-GarmentNets achieves lower Chamfer distance than the original on 4 out of 6 garment categories (e.g., Dress: 1.83 vs. 1.94 cm; Pants: 0.99 vs. 1.03 cm).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against PointNet [51], ℓ₁-PointNet achieves nearly identical performance: 83.3% vs. 83.7% mean mIoU on ShapeNet and 47.6% vs. 47.7% mean IoU on S3DIS, while cutting energy from 7.981 μJ to 3.471 μJ (56% reduction). Against PointNet++ [28], ℓ₁-PointNet++ slightly outperforms the original on both ShapeNet (86.5% vs. 86.2% mean mIoU) and S3DIS (53.9% vs. 53.5% mean IoU), with a 61% energy reduction (1.328 μJ vs. 3.395 μJ). Against GarmentNets [44], ℓ₁-GarmentNets achieves lower Chamfer distance on Dress (1.83 vs. 1.94 cm), Skirt (1.91 vs. 2.00 cm), Top (1.26 vs. 1.30 cm), Pants (0.99 vs. 1.03 cm), and Shirt (1.62 vs. 1.70 cm), but is slightly worse on Jumpsuit (1.56 vs. 1.45 cm). Overall, the ℓ₁-norm variant matches or marginally exceeds the inner-product baselines across all three task types while delivering substantial energy savings, validating the paper's core claim that addition-based convolution is a viable and more efficient alternative to multiplication-based convolution.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the standard inner-product (multiplication-based) convolution used in 3D point cloud backbones like PointNet and PointNet++, replacing it with an ℓp-norm distance as the similarity measure between kernel and input patches. It sits alongside other alternative convolution variants (depthwise separable, dilated, deformable) but takes a fundamentally different axis of modification: changing the algebraic operation (addition via ℓ1-norm vs. multiplication via inner product) rather than the sampling pattern or kernel structure. The theoretical grounding draws on universal approximation theory for neural networks and online convex optimization (regret minimization), while the practical optimization strategies (Mixed Gradient Strategy, Dynamic Learning Rate Controller) are novel contributions tailored to the specific training pathologies of ℓ1-norm nets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper's main practical contribution is demonstrating that ℓ1-norm convolution can match inner-product convolution performance on 3D segmentation and pose estimation tasks while reducing energy consumption by ~61% and lowering instruction latency, which is relevant for real-time and edge-deployed 3D perception. However, the gains are bounded: inference speed is actually marginally slower due to the absence of CUDA/cuDNN kernels for Manhattan distance, and the experiments cover only three datasets with a single base architecture family (PointNet/PointNet++). Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to other 3D architectures (e.g., sparse convolutions, transformers), whether the energy savings survive at scale, and whether the custom optimization strategies are necessary or whether simpler alternatives (e.g., straight-through estimators) could suffice.
