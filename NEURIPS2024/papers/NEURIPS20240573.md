## Problem

Existing data pruning methods are designed for train-from-scratch settings and perform poorly when applied with pretrained molecular models, often falling below even random pruning. The paper identifies this as a source-free data pruning problem: selecting informative downstream samples for finetuning while remaining agnostic to the inaccessible pretraining data, a scenario that is especially challenging due to distribution shift between large-scale pretraining corpora and diverse downstream molecular tasks.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pretrained molecular encoder (e.g., GIN pretrained via GraphMAE on PCQM4Mv2, or SchNet/PaiNN pretrained via GeoSSL) and a target-domain dataset of 2D molecular graphs or 3D geometries. At each training epoch, it dynamically selects a coreset by ranking all samples on the absolute loss discrepancy between an online model and an EMA reference model, retaining the top-K samples (where K is determined by the pruning ratio). Unlike static pruning, the selection set changes every epoch, and the framework is plug-and-play across backbone architectures and pretraining strategies.

## Method

MolPeg maintains two encoders during finetuning: an online model updated by standard gradient descent and a reference model updated via exponential moving average (EMA) with a pace coefficient β, so the online model tracks the target domain while the reference model retains source-domain knowledge. The scoring function is the absolute loss discrepancy |L(x, θ_t) − L(x, ξ_t)| between the two models on each sample, which simultaneously captures easy (representative) and hard (challenging) samples. A Taylor expansion under slow parameter updates shows this discrepancy approximates the dot product between the per-sample gradient and a weighted sum of historical gradients (the 'EMA gradient'), giving a gradient-projection interpretation: samples whose gradients align with or oppose the EMA direction are retained, while those orthogonal to it are discarded. The selection is performed dynamically at each epoch rather than once before training, and the framework requires no access to the pretraining data, making it directly applicable to off-the-shelf pretrained molecular models.

## Results

On the HIV dataset (ROC-AUC), MolPeg achieves 86.0% at 40% pruning, surpassing the full-dataset baseline of 85.1%, and maintains 84.8% even at 80% pruning (vs. 85.1% full). On the larger PCBA dataset (AP), MolPeg reaches 26.4% at 60% pruning and 26.8% at 40% pruning, both exceeding the full-dataset score of 26.3%. On QM9 (MAE, lower is better), MolPeg achieves 14.3 meV on U0 and 1.62 meV on ZPVE at 20% pruning, outperforming InfoBatch (15.6/1.64) and random pruning (15.7/1.65). On MUV (ROC-AUC), MolPeg reaches 81.2% at 40% pruning vs. 80.2% full dataset.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MolPeg consistently outperforms all 13 baselines across HIV, PCBA, QM9, and MUV at every pruning ratio. Among static methods, the strongest competitors are Glister, GraNd-20, and EL2N-20, which trail MolPeg by 0.5–1.5% ROC-AUC on HIV at 40% pruning (e.g., Glister 84.9 vs. MolPeg 86.0) and by 1.0–2.0% AP on PCBA at 60% pruning (e.g., EL2N 22.5 vs. MolPeg 26.4). Weaker static methods like Herding (76.9 at 60% HIV) and K-Means (78.5) show substantially larger gaps. Among dynamic methods, InfoBatch is the closest competitor, matching MolPeg on PCBA at 20% pruning (27.0 vs. 27.0) but trailing by 0.5–1.0% on HIV at 40–60% pruning (85.4/84.9 vs. 86.0/85.5) and by 0.9–1.0% AP on PCBA at 60–80% pruning (25.5/22.8 vs. 26.4/23.9). UCB and ε-greedy are consistently 1–2% behind MolPeg on HIV and 2–3% behind on PCBA. The key differentiator is that MolPeg is the only method that surpasses full-dataset training at moderate pruning ratios (40–60%), while all baselines remain at or below the full-dataset baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves out a new problem formulation—source-free data pruning—by combining two previously separate threads: dynamic data pruning (InfoBatch, UCB, ε-greedy) and the pretrain-finetune paradigm in molecular machine learning. It departs from prior cross-domain DP work (Zhang et al., Xie et al.) that prunes *pretraining* data to match downstream distributions, instead pruning *downstream* data while treating the pretrained model as a frozen proxy for source-domain knowledge. The scoring mechanism (absolute loss discrepancy between an online and EMA reference model) is a novel repurposing of the easy-vs-hard sample debate (Sorscher et al.) into a cross-domain context, with a Taylor-expansion-based theoretical link to GraNd gradient-norm scoring and momentum-style optimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that moderate pruning (40–60%) can *exceed* full-dataset fine-tuning performance challenges the default assumption that more downstream data is always better in transfer learning, suggesting that distribution-shift-misaligned samples actively harm generalization. Practically, MolPeg is plug-and-play with any pretrained molecular model and requires no source-data access or retraining, making it immediately deployable. However, the performance margins over full-dataset training are modest (≤1% ROC-AUC), the method is validated only on molecular tasks, and the loss-discrepancy metric is acknowledged as a simple first attempt—extending to LLMs, vision, or NLP and designing richer cross-domain informativeness measures remain open.
