## Problem

Existing few-shot object detection (FSOD) benchmarks enforce artificial base/novel class partitions that are violated by web-scale pre-training of modern VLMs, and zero-shot VLMs like GroundingDINO already outperform state-of-the-art FSOD methods by 15+ AP on COCO. Beyond this protocol mismatch, VLMs suffer from a concept alignment problem: their web-scale training leads them to define target classes differently than a specific application requires (e.g., predicting the full truck rather than just the cab as annotated in nuImages), and no principled benchmark exists to evaluate how well VLMs can be aligned to such target concepts using a few multi-modal examples.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240617/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark takes an RGB test image, a text prompt (class name, synonyms, or generated descriptions), and K few-shot visual examples per target class as input, producing bounding boxes with class labels. The K-shot examples are multi-modal, combining both textual descriptions and visual crops that mimic the instructions given to human annotators. Evaluation is performed on nuImages' 18 classes grouped by frequency (many/medium/few), with K-shot splits constructed by randomly selecting images until exactly K annotations per class are collected, averaged over three random splits.

## Method

The paper proposes the Foundational FSOD protocol, which replaces the conventional base-class pre-training stage with web-scale VLM pre-training and evaluates fine-tuning on K multi-modal (text + visual) examples per target class. The key empirical contribution is a systematic comparison of seven alignment strategies: prompt engineering with synonyms, standard fine-tuning, Federated Fine-Tuning (which exploits the observation that FSOD datasets are federated mini-datasets by generating pseudo-negatives from zero-shot predictions), language prompt tuning (fine-tuning only text embeddings), visual prompting, multi-modal prompting via MQ-GLIP's Gated Class Scalable Perceiver that fuses visual and textual cues via class-wise cross-attention, and iterative GPT-4o prompting to generate class descriptions. The best open-source baseline (MQ-GLIP multi-modal prompting) achieves 21.42 mAP without any gradient-based fine-tuning, while the CVPR 2024 competition winner reaches 45.35 mAP by combining pseudo-label generation with fine-tuning.

## Results

On COCO, zero-shot GroundingDINO achieves 48.3 AP, outperforming the best FSOD method DiGeo (33.1 AP) by 16.2 AP without any fine-tuning. On the proposed nuImages Foundational FSOD benchmark (10-shot), MQ-GLIP with multi-modal prompting achieves 21.42 mAP as the best open-source baseline, compared to 17.01 mAP for GLIP (text-only) and 14.40 mAP for Detic. The CVPR 2024 competition winner (PHP_hhh) reaches 45.35 mAP, surpassing the best baseline by 23.3 mAP. Federated Fine-Tuning improves Detic from 16.09 to 17.24 mAP, and adding GPT-4o-generated text descriptions to MQ-GLIP's prompts yields a further 0.6 mAP gain (21.42 → 22.03 mAP).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On COCO, zero-shot GroundingDINO (48.3 AP) decisively outperforms all conventional FSOD methods, beating DiGeo (33.1 AP) by 16.2 AP and Retentive R-CNN (32.9 AP) by 15.4 AP, despite never being trained on COCO. On the nuImages Foundational FSOD benchmark, MQ-GLIP with multi-modal prompting (21.42 mAP) surpasses GLIP with language prompt tuning (19.41 mAP), Detic with federated fine-tuning (17.24 mAP), and GroundingDINO zero-shot (12.05 mAP), demonstrating that combining visual and textual few-shot examples yields the strongest alignment. Multi-modal prompting (21.42) clearly outperforms its uni-modal counterparts: MQ-GLIP-Text (17.01) and MQ-GLIP-Image (14.07), confirming the benefit of joint text-visual cues. Federated Fine-Tuning improves over standard fine-tuning for Detic by 1.15 mAP (16.09 → 17.24), and adding prompt engineering further gains 0.47 mAP. GPT-4o zero-shot classification achieves only 9.95 mAP, but using it to generate text descriptions for MQ-GLIP's prompts improves performance by 0.6 mAP (21.42 → 22.03). The CVPR 2024 competition winner (PHP_hhh, 45.35 mAP) dramatically exceeds all open-source baselines by 23.3 mAP, with NJUST KMG (32.56 mAP) and zjyd_cxy_vision (31.57 mAP) also substantially outperforming the MQ-GLIP baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the conventional few-shot object detection (FSOD) paradigm, which partitions datasets into disjoint base and novel classes and pre-trains on base classes only. It argues that this protocol is artificial in the era of web-scale vision-language models, where pre-training data inevitably overlaps with target concepts (e.g., ImageNet contains 'sports car' while COCO-FSOD treats 'car' as novel). Rather than proposing a new detector architecture, the paper repurposes existing VLMs (GroundingDINO, GLIP, Detic, MQ-GLIP) and existing adaptation strategies (prompt tuning, federated fine-tuning, in-context prompting) under a new 'Foundational FSOD' protocol that allows web-scale pre-training and multi-modal K-shot concept alignment, drawing an explicit analogy to how human annotators learn target concepts from few-shot text-and-image instructions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes that zero-shot VLMs already outperform the entire FSOD literature on COCO (48.3 vs. 33.1 AP), fundamentally challenging the premise of existing few-shot benchmarks and redirecting the field toward concept alignment rather than generalization from disjoint base classes. The 23.3 mAP gap between the paper's best baseline (MQ-GLIP at 21.4 mAP) and the CVPR 2024 competition winner (45.35 mAP) signals substantial headroom for future methods, particularly for rare classes where all current VLMs struggle (few-class AP remains below 20). Open questions include how to fairly benchmark when foundation models may have seen test data, whether in-domain pre-training (e.g., other autonomous driving datasets) should be permitted, and how to address the persistent long-tail performance gap for rare categories like strollers and debris.
