## Problem

Existing federated multi-view clustering (FedMVC) methods assume homogeneous clients—either all single-view or all multi-view—yet real-world deployments (e.g., hospitals with CT/X-ray/EHR vs. remote clinics with a single modality) involve heterogeneous hybrid views where single-view and multi-view clients coexist. This heterogeneity creates a client gap (single-view clients obtain biased partitions) and a view gap (views differ in quality and complementarity), which prior methods cannot jointly address.

## Input / Output

The method consumes unlabeled samples distributed across M multi-view clients (each holding V aligned views, e.g., image + text) and S single-view clients (each holding one view type), with no raw data ever leaving a client. It produces (V+1) heterogeneous global models—one per view type plus one multi-view model—and final cluster assignments obtained by applying K-means on the common-semantics features H extracted by the corresponding global model. The encoder-decoder backbone uses a fixed architecture (Input–Fc500–Fc500–Fc200–Fc20) with 20-dimensional latent features, and all models share a common output dimension d=20.

## Method

FMCSC bridges the client and view gaps through three coupled mechanisms. First, cross-client consensus pre-training has the earliest-finished multi-view client distribute its autoencoder parameters to all other clients, aligning unsupervised local models and preventing the feature-space confusion that naive aggregation would cause. Second, local-synergistic contrastive learning operates at two levels: multi-view clients use feature-level contrastive loss to pull the concatenated common semantics H toward each view-specific feature h^v, while single-view clients use model-level contrastive loss to align their local model output with the more generalized global model output; additionally, multi-view clients explicitly optimize single-view global models (Eq. 6) so that even view-specific global models acquire multi-view-informed semantics. Third, global-specific weighting aggregation on the server aggregates same-architecture models using weights derived from mutual information bounds (Theorem 1), so that higher-quality models (those with greater H–H^v or H–H^g mutual information) receive larger aggregation weights, yielding (V+1) heterogeneous global models. Theoretical analysis (Theorem 2) provides generalization bounds showing that increasing samples per client and the number of participating clients tightens the bound, while the view-distribution divergence term quantifies the residual view gap.

## Results

Under the standard M/S=1:1 setting, FMCSC achieves the best clustering performance across all four datasets. On MNIST-USPS, it reaches 92.9% ACC, 84.2% NMI, and 85.0% ARI, outperforming the strongest centralized baseline ProImp (87.3% ACC) by ~5.6 points. On Multi-Fashion, FMCSC attains 90.4% ACC and 82.8% NMI, surpassing the best baseline DSIMVC (77.7% ACC) by over 12 points. Even in the challenging NUSWIDE setting with 5 views, FMCSC leads with 52.7% ACC versus 51.5% for the next-best method JPLTD.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FMCSC outperforms all 9 baselines across every dataset and metric at the standard M/S=1:1 ratio. Against the strongest centralized method ProImp, FMCSC gains +5.6% ACC on MNIST-USPS (92.9 vs. 87.3) and +12.7% ACC on Multi-Fashion (90.4 vs. 69.0), while on BDGP the margin is smaller (+1.5% ACC over ProImp's 90.4). The two federated baselines are substantially outperformed: FedDMVC drops to 69.9% ACC on MNIST-USPS and 66.6% on Multi-Fashion, and FCUIF reaches only 72.8% and 68.4% respectively, indicating that existing FedMVC methods struggle with heterogeneous hybrid views. On NUSWIDE, where all methods perform poorly due to 5 heterogeneous views, FMCSC still leads JPLTD (51.5% ACC) and DSIMVC (50.6% ACC) by 1–2 points. Notably, FMCSC maintains its advantage even when single-view clients outnumber multi-view clients (M/S=1:2), achieving 90.1% ACC on MNIST-USPS versus 84.8% for ProImp, demonstrating robustness to increasing client heterogeneity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends federated multi-view clustering (FedMVC) from the isomorphic-client assumption (all clients are either single-view or multi-view, as in FedDMVC [8] and FCUIF [36]) to a heterogeneous hybrid setting where both types coexist. The local-synergistic contrastive learning draws on the mutual-information lower bound of contrastive losses established by Zhong et al. [51] and van den Oord et al. [32], while the generalization bound in Theorem 2 adapts the clustering-specific analysis of Tang et al. [37] and Li & Liu [23] to the federated, multi-client setting. The global-specific weighting aggregation is a novel architectural choice that produces multiple heterogeneous global models (one per view type plus one multi-view model) rather than a single unified model, departing from standard FedAvg-style aggregation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The heterogeneous hybrid view scenario is well-motivated by real-world deployments (e.g., hospitals with varying imaging equipment, smartphones with different sensor suites), and the paper provides the first systematic treatment with theoretical guarantees. However, the method's effectiveness is uneven: while it achieves strong results on MNIST-USPS, BDGP, and Multi-Fashion, performance on NUSWIDE (5 views, higher-dimensional features) drops substantially (56% ACC), suggesting the approach may struggle with more complex or higher-dimensional view spaces. The privacy analysis is limited to a brief differential-privacy experiment, and the paper acknowledges future work on downstream tasks (medical, financial) but does not validate at scale. Open questions include whether the mutual-information-based weighting generalizes beyond the tested datasets and how the method behaves with truly non-IID sample distributions across clients.
