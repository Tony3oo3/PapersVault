## Problem

Softmax Loss (SL), the dominant loss in modern recommender systems, suffers from two fundamental limitations rooted in its use of the exponential function: (1) it is a loose surrogate for ranking metrics like DCG because exp(·) diverges substantially from the Heaviside step function at large score gaps, and (2) it is highly sensitive to false negative noise because the exponential gradient assigns disproportionately large weights to hard negative instances, allowing noisy samples to dominate training.

## Input / Output

The method operates on standard collaborative filtering data: a user-item interaction matrix where each observed (u,i) pair is a positive instance. User and item embeddings are mapped to d-dimensional vectors, and the preference score is computed as cosine similarity scaled by 1/2 (absorbed into the temperature τ). The loss is applied to pairwise score gaps d_uij = f(u,j) − f(u,i) between each positive item i and all negative items j, with 1000 negatives sampled per positive during training. The output is a ranked list of items per user, evaluated via NDCG@20 and Recall@20.

## Method

PSL replaces the exponential function in SL with a flexible surrogate activation σ(·) that better approximates the Heaviside step function, yielding the loss L_PSL(u) = Σ_{i∈P_u} log(Σ_{j∈I} σ(d_uij)^{1/τ}). Three specific activations are advocated: σ_tanh = tanh(d_uij)+1, σ_atan = arctan(d_uij)+1, and σ_relu = ReLU(d_uij+1), all satisfying δ(d_uij) ≤ σ(d_uij) ≤ exp(d_uij) on [−1,1]. The temperature is deliberately placed outside the activation (σ^{1/τ} rather than σ(·/τ)), which is critical: it enables a tighter DCG surrogate bound (Lemma 4.1), controls the gradient weight distribution to mitigate false-negative sensitivity, and—most importantly—establishes that optimizing PSL is exactly equivalent to performing KL-divergence Distributionally Robust Optimization over the BPR pairwise loss (Theorem 4.2), where τ acts as the Lagrange multiplier controlling the perturbation radius. This DRO interpretation provides a principled explanation for PSL's superior OOD robustness and noise resistance, and the entire modification requires only swapping one function in the SL implementation.

## Results

Under the IID setting, PSL-relu achieves the best NDCG@20 across all three backbones, improving over SL by 1.40% (Amazon-Book/MF), 2.31% (Amazon-Electronic/MF), 1.72% (Amazon-Movie/MF), and 1.42% (Gowalla/MF). Under the OOD setting with popularity shift, improvements widen to 2.02%–5.02% NDCG@20 over SL, with the largest gain on Amazon-Electronic (5.02%). Under the Noise setting with 50% false-negative ratio, PSL retains over 10% relative NDCG advantage over SL, while DRO-enhanced baselines like AdvInfoNCE degrade similarly to or worse than SL.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PSL consistently outperforms all five baselines across datasets and backbones. Against SL (the primary SOTA baseline), PSL-relu gains 1.4–2.3% NDCG@20 in IID and 2.0–5.0% in OOD, with the gap widening under distribution shift as predicted by the DRO theory. Against BPR, PSL improves NDCG@20 by roughly 2–4× (e.g., 0.1227 vs 0.0453 on Amazon-Book/MF), confirming that DRO-enhanced pairwise optimization substantially narrows the gap to DCG. Against AdvInfoNCE, PSL wins on every dataset; notably AdvInfoNCE sometimes degrades below SL (e.g., 0.1172 vs 0.1210 NDCG on Amazon-Book/MF), consistent with the paper's argument that its double-exponential activation loosens the DCG surrogate. Against BSL, PSL shows a small but consistent edge (e.g., 0.1227 vs 0.1212 on Amazon-Book/MF), while BSL's improvements over SL are marginal (0.0–0.5%). Against LLPAUC, PSL dominates by a wide margin (e.g., 0.1227 vs 0.0811 NDCG on Amazon-Book/MF), reflecting LLPAUC's focus on Recall rather than top-ranked DCG.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reformulates Softmax Loss (SL) from a pairwise perspective, identifying the exponential function as the root cause of two limitations: a loose surrogate for DCG and excessive sensitivity to false negatives. It then replaces exp(·) with bounded activations (tanh, atan, relu) to define Pairwise Softmax Loss (PSL), establishing that PSL is equivalent to applying KL-divergence DRO over the classical BPR pairwise loss. This positions PSL as a unifying framework that subsumes SL (exp activation), AdvInfoNCE (double-exp activation), and BPR (pre-DRO base), while departing from prior DRO-enhanced SL variants (AdvInfoNCE, BSL) by arguing that DRO over a *better* base loss (BPR with bounded activations) is preferable to DRO over SL's inherently loose exponential surrogate.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The clean theoretical unification—showing that a simple activation swap in SL yields a DRO-empowered BPR that is simultaneously a tighter DCG surrogate and more noise-robust—gives practitioners a principled, drop-in replacement for SL with minimal code changes. The counterintuitive finding that DRO-enhanced losses like AdvInfoNCE can be *more* noise-sensitive than plain SL challenges the common assumption that DRO universally improves robustness. The main open limitation is computational efficiency: like SL, PSL requires sampling ~1000 negatives per positive per iteration, and the paper does not address how to reduce this cost. Whether the 1–5% NDCG gains persist in industrial-scale settings with different negative sampling strategies remains to be seen.
