## Problem

Existing certified approximate unlearning methods based on noisy gradient descent (e.g., Delete-to-Descent, Langevin Unlearning) are restricted to full-batch settings, and extending their sensitivity-based or Langevin-dynamics analyses to mini-batch noisy SGD with tight guarantees is non-trivial. This paper establishes the first approximate unlearning guarantee for projected noisy stochastic gradient descent (PNSGD) under convexity, showing that mini-batch unlearning can achieve the same privacy-utility trade-off as full-batch while using a fraction of the gradient computations.

## Input / Output

The method takes a model at (or near) the stationary distribution of PNSGD training on dataset D, a fixed cyclic mini-batch sequence B, and an unlearning request specifying which data point to remove (replacing it to form adjacent dataset D'). It outputs the last iterate after K unlearning epochs of PNSGD on D', accompanied by a certified (α, ε)-Rényi Unlearning guarantee. The objective must be L-smooth, M-Lipschitz, and m-strongly convex over a bounded convex set C_R of radius R, with step size η ≤ 1/L; experiments use binary logistic regression with ℓ₂ regularization on MNIST (grayscale) and CIFAR-10 (RGB, via ResNet18 embeddings).

## Method

The key technical contribution is a tight W_∞ distance tracking analysis (Lemma 3.3) between adjacent PNSGD learning processes, yielding an initial distance bound Z_B ≈ O(ηM/b) instead of the vacuous O(R) projection-diameter bound used in prior DP analyses. This W_∞ bound is then converted to a Rényi divergence privacy loss via the privacy amplification by iteration (PABI) framework of Altschuler and Talwar, giving ε = O(Z_B² c^{2Kn/b}) for K unlearning epochs with contraction rate c = 1 − ηm. For sequential unlearning, the paper exploits the triangle inequality of W_∞ (a true metric) rather than the weak triangle inequality of Rényi divergence, so the privacy loss grows linearly rather than exponentially in the number of requests. The approach also supports batch unlearning (multiple points removed at once) and an improved bound under randomized mini-batch sequences via joint convexity of KL divergence.

## Results

For unlearning 100 data points under (1, 1/n)-unlearning, PNSGD requires roughly 10% of the unlearning epochs of D2D (full-batch, b=n) and 2% of LU (mini-batch, b=128), while all methods achieve similar test accuracy (~0.9 on MNIST, ~0.98 on CIFAR-10). Under the stricter (0.01, 1/n)-unlearning constraint for 100 sequential requests, PNSGD needs at most 1 unlearning epoch per request at b=32 and at most 5 at b=512. The paper reports that under the same privacy constraint, PNSGD achieves similar utility while using only 2% (mini-batch) and 10% (full-batch) of the gradient computations of state-of-the-art baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares PNSGD against two state-of-the-art full-batch gradient-based unlearning methods: Delete-to-Descent (D2D, Neel et al. 2021) and Langevin Unlearning (LU, Chien et al. 2024). For unlearning a single data point with K=1 epoch, PNSGD outperforms D2D in both utility and complexity (D2D requires a non-private internal state and K=1 or 5 epochs to achieve any guarantee). Against LU, mini-batch PNSGD either outperforms or matches it on utility under the same privacy constraint, though LU's Langevin-dynamics-based analysis yields a slightly tighter per-request privacy bound in the full-batch case. The decisive advantage emerges with multiple (100) sequential unlearning requests: PNSGD requires only ~10% of D2D's and ~2% of LU's unlearning epochs (for b=n and b=128 respectively) while maintaining comparable accuracy. This large gap is attributed to the paper's W-infinity tracking analysis, which uses the standard triangle inequality (linear growth in the number of requests) versus LU's weak triangle inequality on Rényi divergence (exponential growth in the number of requests).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the privacy amplification by iteration (PABI) framework of Feldman et al. [20] and the PNSGD convergence analysis of Altschuler and Talwar [15,16], repurposing their contractive noisy iteration (CNI) machinery to establish the first certified approximate unlearning guarantee for mini-batch PNSGD under convexity. It departs from the Langevin-dynamics-based analysis used by Langevin Unlearning (LU) [11] and the sensitivity-based analysis of Delete-to-Descent (D2D) [9] by instead tracking the infinite Wasserstein distance (W∞) between adjacent (un)learning processes, which yields non-vacuous bounds where the naive projection-diameter bound of [15] would be vacuous. The key methodological departure is that W∞ is a true metric, enabling standard triangle inequality for sequential unlearning (linear privacy growth) where the Rényi-divergence-based weak triangle inequality in [11] forces exponential growth.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first non-vacuous certified unlearning guarantee for mini-batch noisy SGD, showing that unlearning can be done with only 2–10% of the gradient computations required by state-of-the-art full-batch baselines while maintaining comparable utility under the same privacy constraint. The characterization of a privacy-utility-complexity trade-off governed by mini-batch size b is a new practical insight for practitioners choosing hyperparameters. The main limitation is the (strong) convexity assumption inherited from [15,16], and extending the analysis to non-convex objectives (e.g., via Langevin dynamics as in [11]) remains open, though the authors conjecture such bounds would be looser. The framework also naturally extends to sequential and batch unlearning, which is increasingly relevant for real-world GDPR compliance scenarios with frequent data removal requests.
