## Problem

Standard convergence analyses for gradient descent rely on a global L-smoothness constant (the worst-case Lipschitz constant of the gradient), which is often pessimistic and fails to predict actual optimization speed. Since GD iterates depend only on local properties of the objective along the optimization path, the paper asks whether convergence bounds can be derived that depend solely on the conditioning of f along the trajectory rather than on global worst-case constants.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240473/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a differentiable convex function f, gradient evaluations at consecutive iterates, and an initial point x_0. It produces sub-optimality bounds parameterized by directional smoothness constants M(x_k, x_{k+1}) computed from gradient values at consecutive iterates, along with adapted step-size sequences (strongly adapted, Polyak, or normalized). Unusually, the bounds are path-dependent: they tighten or loosen based on the actual trajectory taken, and the Polyak step-size achieves the tightest possible rate without any explicit computation of the directional smoothness.

## Method

The paper introduces three constructive directional smoothness functions M(x,y) — point-wise D, path-wise A, and optimal H — that measure gradient variation along the chord between consecutive iterates rather than globally. The key mechanism is a localized descent lemma: f(x_{k+1}) ≤ f(x_k) − (η_k − η_k²M(x_k,x_{k+1})/2)‖∇f(x_k)‖², which yields sub-optimality bounds strictly tighter than L-smoothness whenever M(x_k,x_{k+1}) < L. For quadratic objectives, the strongly adapted step-size η_k = 1/M(x_{k+1},x_k) admits closed-form solutions that recover the Rayleigh quotient and Cauchy step-size. For general convex functions, the paper proves that the Polyak step-size and normalized GD adapt to any directional smoothness function without explicit knowledge of it, achieving the same path-dependent O(1/k) rate as strongly adapted step-sizes, and extends the analysis to Nesterov acceleration via estimating sequences.

## Results

On logistic regression problems from the UCI repository (mammographic, ionosphere, horse-colic, ozone), the paper's directional-smoothness-based convergence bounds are an order of magnitude tighter than classical L-smoothness guarantees, particularly on ionosphere. The Polyak step-size achieves the best empirical convergence on every dataset except ozone, where GD with strongly adapted step-sizes (η_k = 1/D_k) solves the problem to high accuracy in just a few iterations. On a synthetic quadratic with Hessian skew (L=1000), strongly adapted step-sizes are significantly larger than 2/L early in training and converge to the edge-of-stability value of 2/L, yielding substantially faster convergence than fixed η_k = 1/L.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares five step-size strategies on logistic regression: standard GD with η_k = 1/L, GD with strongly adapted step-sizes (η_k = 1/D_k), the Polyak step-size, normalized GD, and AdGD (Malitsky and Mishchenko, 2020). The Polyak step-size outperforms all other methods on horse-colic and two other datasets, while strongly adapted step-sizes (η_k = 1/D_k) win decisively on ozone, solving it to high accuracy in just a few iterations. All adaptive step-size methods (strongly adapted, Polyak, normalized GD) consistently outperform the fixed η_k = 1/L baseline. Compared specifically to AdGD, the Polyak step-size and strongly adapted step-sizes match or exceed its performance, with the key advantage being that the Polyak step-size adapts to any directional smoothness function without requiring a local smoothness oracle, whereas AdGD relies on a smoothed version of the previous iteration's point-wise directional smoothness.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper generalizes global L-smoothness into a family of constructive directional smoothness functions (point-wise D, path-wise A, optimal H) that measure gradient variation along the optimization path rather than over the entire domain. It extends the local-smoothness line of work by Malitsky and Mishchenko (2020) and non-uniform smoothness (Mei et al., 2021) by providing explicit, evaluable smoothness constants without requiring boundedness assumptions, local smoothness oracles, or algorithm modifications. The acceleration analysis builds directly on Nesterov's estimating sequences framework (2018), while the exponential search component adapts Carmon and Hinder (2022). A key departure is showing that the Polyak step-size implicitly adapts to any directional smoothness function, unifying it with the strongly adapted step-size theory.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The path-dependent convergence bounds offer a more faithful theoretical lens on why gradient descent converges faster than L-smoothness predicts, and the Polyak step-size result provides a principled explanation for its empirical superiority without requiring knowledge of the smoothness constant. For quadratics, the framework recovers the Cauchy and Dai-Yang step-sizes with new non-asymptotic guarantees, connecting classical heuristics to the directional smoothness theory. Limitations include the restriction to convex (and differentiable) objectives, the impracticality of computing strongly adapted step-sizes for general functions, and experiments limited to small-scale logistic regression. Open questions include extending the framework to non-convex and stochastic settings, and whether the path-dependent rates can guide practical step-size schedules in deep learning.
