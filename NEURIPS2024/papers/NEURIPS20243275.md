## Problem

Existing disentangled representation learning (DRL) methods rely on the unrealistic assumption that semantic factors are statistically independent, yet real-world attributes are pervasively correlated (e.g., baldness correlates with gender, bangs with hair color). Prior structured DRL approaches that do model interrelations either require supervision or represent relations as binary and unidirectional, limiting their practicality. This paper addresses the gap by proposing a fully unsupervised framework that simultaneously disentangles factors and discovers bidirectional, weighted interrelations among them.

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images (64×64 in main experiments, 256×256 for high-definition results) as input, which are first pre-processed via 68-point facial landmark detection to crop and normalize the region of interest. It produces a 6-dimensional disentangled latent variable z, a relation-aware latent variable z_rel = A^T z fed to the decoder, a reconstructed image, and a bidirectional weighted DisGraph (an n×n adjacency matrix where nodes are attributes, edges are interrelations, and weights are Somers' D impact scores in [0,1]). The MLLM branch additionally consumes the pre-processed image alongside natural-language prompts to score each attribute on a 0–5 ordinal scale.

## Method

GEM integrates two complementary branches through a bidirectional weighted DisGraph: a β-VAE encoder extracts disentangled factors as initial graph nodes, while GPT-4o (used as a relation predictor) scores attributes and discovers interrelations, with Somers' D computing asymmetric bidirectional impact scores for each attribute pair. A GNN-based graph learner (E_gnn) then dynamically refines the DisGraph via structure bootstrapping and multi-view graph contrastive learning, converting the sketched adjacency matrix into node embeddings whose average forms z_rel for the decoder. Because the decoder input z_rel no longer follows the standard Gaussian prior, the β-VAE loss is reformulated and optimized with an adversarial training strategy (cross-entropy on a discriminator D(x,z) = log(p_θ(x,z)/(βq_φ(x,z)))), yielding a total loss that balances adversarial reconstruction, disentanglement, and graph-aware terms. The key insight is that MLLM commonsense reasoning provides a reliable, supervision-free inductive bias for interrelation discovery, while the bidirectional weighted graph captures the asymmetric, graded nature of real-world attribute interactions that binary causal models cannot represent.

## Results

On CelebA, GEM achieves FID 46.0±0.1 and KID 48.3×10⁻³±0.2, outperforming all DRL baselines. On LSUN-horse, GEM reaches FID 101.0±1.1 and KID 65.5×10⁻³±1.7, and on LSUN-bedroom, FID 125.4±1.2 and KID 76.1×10⁻³±1.1. In disentanglement metrics (Appendix Table 3), GEM attains MIG 0.53 and IRS 0.60, surpassing β-VAE (MIG 0.49, IRS 0.55) and vanilla VAE (MIG 0.19, IRS 0.35). Computationally, GEM (Full) uses 49.6M parameters and 3.2 GFLOPs, comparable to FactorVAE (55.9M, 3.8 GFLOPs) and DEAR (53.4M, 3.5 GFLOPs).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GEM outperforms all five DRL baselines on both FID and KID across all three datasets. Against the closest DRL competitor, vanilla VAE [14], GEM improves CelebA FID from 53.3 to 46.0 (−13.7%) and KID from 51.4 to 48.3. The supervised causal method DEAR [29] is the strongest baseline on LSUN-horse (FID 136.4) and LSUN-bedroom (FID 177.6), yet GEM still leads by 35.4 and 52.2 FID points respectively. Among unsupervised VAE variants, β-VAE [15] and β-TCVAE [16] suffer the largest degradation (FID >136 on CelebA), while FactorVAE [43] is intermediate (FID 134.5); GEM surpasses all of them by 80+ FID points on CelebA. In the appendix comparison against generation-focused models (Table 4), GEM (FID 46.05 at 64×64) trails DDPM (8.56) and Stable Diffusion (7.72), which the authors attribute to the inherent disentanglement–reconstruction trade-off rather than a methodological deficiency.

## Relation to Prior Work

GEM extends the β-VAE family of disentangled representation learning (β-VAE, β-TCVAE, FactorVAE) by departing from the flat independence assumption that underpins all standard DRL. It also builds on structured DRL lines—Hierarchical DRL and Causal DRL (CausalVAE, DEAR)—but explicitly rejects their reliance on supervision and their binary, unidirectional relation modeling. The key conceptual departure is the integration of a multimodal large language model (GPT-4o) as an unsupervised relation predictor, combined with a bidirectional weighted graph (DisGraph) refined by a GNN, which has no direct precedent in the DRL literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a recognized fundamental limitation of unsupervised DRL—the unrealistic independence assumption—by showing that MLLM commonsense reasoning can supply the missing inductive bias for relation discovery without labels. This opens a path toward interpretable, generalizable disentanglement on complex real-world data. However, significant open questions remain: the reconstruction-quality trade-off with the β-VAE penalty is acknowledged but unresolved, the framework is not designed for 3D data, and the entire relation-discovery pipeline hinges on the reliability of a single proprietary MLLM (GPT-4o), raising questions about robustness, cost, and reproducibility. Integration with modern generative backbones (diffusion, autoregressive) is identified as the most promising next step.
