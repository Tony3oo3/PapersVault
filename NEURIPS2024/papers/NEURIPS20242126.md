## Problem

Existing theoretical work on in-context learning in transformers has largely been confined to simplified architectures (linear attention, single-layer models) and simple tasks (linear regression), leaving it unclear how the full suite of transformer building blocks—relative positional embedding, multi-head attention, feed-forward networks, and normalization—jointly enable ICL on tasks with latent causal structure. This paper asks whether and how a two-attention-layer transformer trained by gradient flow on n-gram Markov chain data converges to a model that performs ICL, and what role each architectural component plays.

## Input / Output

The model TF(M,H,d,D) takes as input a sequence of L one-hot token vectors (each in R^d) drawn from a random n-gram Markov chain, with an additional zero vector appended at position L+1 to serve as the query position. It outputs a probability distribution over the d-element vocabulary for the (L+1)-th token. The architecture is parameterized by a window size M for relative positional embedding, H attention heads in the first layer, vocabulary size d, and a maximum polynomial degree D controlling FFN complexity; training minimizes the population cross-entropy loss over sequences sampled from a mixture of Markov transition kernels.

## Method

The paper proves that three-stage gradient flow on the cross-entropy loss converges to a limiting model implementing a Generalized Induction Head (GIH) mechanism. In Stage I, the FFN (which realizes a low-degree polynomial kernel over head outputs) learns to select an information-optimal subset S* of heads via a modified χ²-mutual information criterion, with the ratio c_{S*}/c_S growing exponentially. In Stage II, relative positional embedding weights in the first attention layer are trained so that each head in S* becomes a one-hot copier of a specific parent token within the length-M window. In Stage III, the scalar weight a in the second attention layer grows (first exponentially, then as 1/a) until the softmax attention becomes an exponential kernel classifier that aggregates tokens whose partial histories on S* match those at the output position. The key architectural insight is that RPE provides the positional structure for copying, the FFN with normalization acts as a feature selector over head outputs, and the second-layer softmax attention serves as the kernel-based classifier—each component is necessary and their convergence rates are strictly separated (exponential, polynomial, logarithmic).

## Results

The paper proves that gradient flow on a two-attention-layer transformer trained on n-gram Markov chain data converges through three distinct stages: (i) the FFN learns the information-optimal subset S* at an exponential rate, (ii) the first attention layer's RPE weights concentrate each head on a single parent token at a polynomial rate, and (iii) the second attention layer's scalar weight a grows logarithmically to implement an exponential kernel classifier. The limiting model implements a Generalized Induction Head (GIH) mechanism with an approximation error of O(L^{-1/8}) relative to the ideal GIH estimator. Simulation experiments (M=H=3, d=3, D=2, L=100, pa={-1,-2}) validate all three phases, and generalization tests show decreasing cross-entropy loss as sequence length increases from 10 to 1000, with robustness to prior distribution shifts (Dirichlet α ∈ {0.05, 0.1, 0.2}).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical paper without direct method-to-method benchmark comparisons; the primary validation is between theoretical predictions and simulation experiments. The key comparison is with Nichani et al. (2024), which analyzes the n=1 (single-parent) case: the present paper recovers their results as a special case and extends the analysis to general n-gram Markov chains with multiple parents. The simplified disentangled transformer (2.5) is validated against a full transformer with query/key/value projection matrices (W_Q, W_K, W_V): the full model converges to the same behavior, with W_Q and W_K approaching zero and W_V approaching a scaled identity, confirming the simplification is faithful. The three-stage training paradigm (training FFN, RPE, and second-attention weights sequentially) is shown to produce the same dynamics as simultaneous full-model training, as verified in the experiments of §B.2.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the induction head mechanism (Elhage et al., 2021) and the training dynamics analysis of Nichani et al. (2024) from first-order Markov chains (single parent) to general n-gram Markov chains with multiple parents. It builds on the disentangled transformer architecture of Friedman et al. (2024) to make the full two-layer transformer amenable to theoretical analysis, incorporating relative positional embedding, multi-head softmax attention, and a feed-forward network with normalization. The key departure from prior work is the introduction of a 'generalized induction head' (GIH) estimator that uses a modified χ²-mutual information criterion to select an informative subset of parents, rather than assuming a single parent as in Nichani et al. (2024).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first theoretical result showing how all major building blocks of a transformer (RPE as copier, FFN with normalization as selector, second attention as exponential kernel classifier) contribute congruously to in-context learning, providing a mechanistic account that was previously missing. The identification of three training phases with strictly separated convergence rates (exponential, polynomial, logarithmic) offers a clean theoretical explanation for why these stages naturally emerge during simultaneous training. Open questions include extending the analysis to standard (non-polynomial) FFN layers, understanding training dynamics beyond a single induction head loop, and generalizing to more complex data structures beyond Markov chains. Limitations include the simplified normalization scheme and the reliance on a three-stage training paradigm for the main theorem, though experiments confirm simultaneous training converges similarly.
