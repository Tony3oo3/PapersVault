## Problem

Conditional generative modeling for likelihood-free inference requires learning a transport map from a source distribution to a target conditional distribution, but existing COT methods either rely on adversarial GAN training or require simulating from the model during training. Moreover, no principled dynamic (geometric) formulation of conditional optimal transport existed in general separable Hilbert spaces, limiting applicability to infinite-dimensional Bayesian inverse problems.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242967/cropped_page6_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an observation y (finite-dimensional vector or infinite-dimensional function, e.g., a noisy pressure field) and a source sample u_0 drawn from a tractable distribution (e.g., Gaussian noise or a Matérn prior), and produces posterior samples u ~ ν(u|y) by integrating a learned ODE. The source and target measures must share the same Y-marginal μ; when this does not hold, a preprocessing map T_Y aligns them. For the Darcy flow problem, both y and u are functions on a 2D domain, handled via a Fourier Neural Operator to support arbitrary discretizations.

## Method

The paper proves that the conditional Wasserstein space P_p^μ(Y×U) is a geodesic space and that absolutely continuous curves therein are generated by triangular vector fields (zero velocity in the Y-component), yielding a conditional Benamou-Brenier theorem. Building on this, COT-FM couples source and target samples via a conditional optimal transport plan computed with a cost c_ε = |y_1−y_0|² + ε|u_1−u_0|² that penalizes Y-dimension mass transfer, then learns a triangular vector field v^θ(t,y,u) via standard flow matching (MSE loss on conditional vector fields, no simulation required). At inference, given a fixed y, one samples u_0 ~ η(u|y) and integrates the ODE ∂_t(y,u_t) = v^θ(t,y,u_t) to obtain a sample from ν(u|y). The framework is agnostic to the source distribution and applies in infinite-dimensional settings, demonstrated on a Darcy flow PDE inverse problem using a Fourier Neural Operator backbone.

## Results

On four 2D synthetic datasets (Checkerboard, Moons, Circles, Swissroll), COT-FM achieves the lowest W₂ and MMD distances to the ground-truth joint distribution across all tasks, e.g. W₂ = 4.69±1.00 on Checkerboard vs. 6.27±0.81 (PCP-Map) and 8.20±0.49 (COT-Flow). On the Lotka-Volterra inverse problem, COT-FM attains W₂ = 4.02±0.06 to MCMC samples, outperforming all baselines. On the infinite-dimensional Darcy Flow PDE problem, COT-FM achieves MSE = 5.40±0.08 to the true permeability field, beating WaMGAN (6.55±0.07) and FFM (7.30±0.07), while matching FFM on CRPS (15.56 vs. 15.47).

## Baselines & Comparisons

COT-FM outperforms all four baselines on every metric across all three experimental settings. Against PCP-Map (Wang et al., 2023), COT-FM reduces W₂ by 25–65% on the 2D datasets (e.g. 4.69 vs. 6.27 on Checkerboard) and by 20% on Lotka-Volterra (4.02 vs. 5.04); PCP-Map relies on input-convex networks, limiting its applicability to high-dimensional or function-space problems. Against COT-Flow (Wang et al., 2023), COT-FM is substantially better, with the gap most pronounced on Moons (W₂ 6.50 vs. 18.49) where COT-Flow's adversarial training struggles. The plain flow-matching ablation (FM, Lipman et al., 2022) without COT coupling is consistently the weakest baseline, confirming that the conditional optimal transport coupling is the key ingredient. On the infinite-dimensional Darcy Flow problem, COT-FM beats WaMGAN (Hosseini et al., 2023) on MSE (5.40 vs. 6.55) and CRPS (15.56 vs. 18.75), and beats the function-space flow-matching ablation FFM (Kerrigan et al., 2023) on MSE (5.40 vs. 7.30) while matching it on CRPS (15.56 vs. 15.47).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of flow matching (Lipman et al., 2022), the Benamou-Brenier dynamic formulation of optimal transport (Benamou & Brenier, 2000), and the conditional optimal transport (COT) framework with triangular maps (Hosseini et al., 2023; Baptista et al., 2020). Prior COT learning methods (PCP-Map, COT-Flow, WaMGAN) rely on adversarial training or simulation during training, while prior flow matching approaches for conditional generation do not enforce the COT coupling structure. The paper's key theoretical contribution is proving that the conditional Wasserstein space P_p^μ(Y×U) is a geodesic space and establishing a conditional Benamou-Brenier theorem, thereby providing the missing dynamical/geometric foundation that connects static COT maps to flow-based generative modeling in a principled, simulation-free manner.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The conditional Benamou-Brenier theorem and the geodesic structure of the conditional Wasserstein space provide a rigorous theoretical backbone for conditional flow-based generative models, filling a gap between static COT theory and dynamic flow matching. Practically, COT-FM offers a simulation-free alternative to GAN-based COT methods and extends naturally to infinite-dimensional function spaces, making it applicable to Bayesian inverse problems (e.g., Darcy flow PDE) where prior methods were limited to finite dimensions. Key limitations include the computational cost of computing the full COT plan (necessitating minibatch approximations whose relationship to the true plan is not characterized) and the additional overhead over standard flow matching. An open question is whether the minibatch COT approximation degrades gracefully or can be made provably consistent at scale.
