## Problem

The sample complexity of distributionally robust MDPs (RMDPs) has remained largely undetermined beyond a few specific divergences (TV, χ², KL, Wasserstein), with minimax-optimal bounds achieved only for TV distance. This paper asks two open questions: whether robust RL can be more sample-efficient than standard RL under general smooth L_p norms, and whether s-rectangular RMDPs (with stochastic optimal policies) require more samples than sa-rectangular RMDPs (with deterministic optimal policies).

## Input / Output

The algorithm assumes access to a generative model (simulator) that produces N i.i.d. next-state samples for each state-action pair under the nominal transition kernel P⁰, yielding a total of NSA samples. The uncertainty set is parameterized by a general smooth L_p norm (twice continuously differentiable, weighted, and rescaled) with radius σ, and for s-rectangularity the radius is rescaled as σ̃ = σ‖1_A‖ to ensure a fair comparison with the sa-rectangular case. The output is an ε-optimal robust policy satisfying V^{⋆,σ}(s) − V^{π̂,σ}(s) ≤ ε for all states, along with near-minimax-optimal sample complexity upper and lower bounds that depend on the geometric constant C_g = 1/min_s‖e_s‖ of the chosen norm.

## Method

The core technical contribution is a new dual optimization formulation for the robust Bellman operator under arbitrary smooth L_p norms (Lemmas 3–4), which introduces the dual span semi-norm sp(·)_* as the central quantity governing the worst-case value. A key insight is that the robust value function's dynamic range, measured by this span semi-norm, shrinks as the uncertainty radius σ grows (Lemmas 5–6), enabling a concentration argument that yields sample complexity O~(SA/((1−γ)² max{1−γ, C_g σ} ε²)) for sa-rectangularity — matching a new minimax lower bound and improving prior L_p results by a factor of 1/(1−γ) when σ ≳ 1−γ. For s-rectangularity, the dual form introduces an additional ‖π_s‖_* factor reflecting policy stochasticity, and the paper provides the first lower bound (via L_∞ norm) confirming tightness. The analysis shows that robust RL is never harder than standard RL in sample complexity and that s-rectangularity is at least as easy as sa-rectangularity, with instance-dependent bounds that tighten further when the policy is uniform or deterministic.

## Results

For sa-rectangular RMDPs with general smooth L_p norms, the paper establishes a near-minimax-optimal sample complexity of Õ(SA / ((1−γ)² max{1−γ, C_g σ} ε²)), with a matching lower bound confirming tightness across almost the full range of uncertainty levels. For s-rectangular RMDPs, the upper bound improves to Õ(SA / ((1−γ)² max{1−γ, C_g min_s‖π_s‖_* σ̃} ε²)), and the paper provides the first lower bound for s-rectangular RMDPs (using L_∞ norm) at Õ(SA / ((1−γ)² max{1−γ, σ̃} ε²)). A key finding is that robust RL is never harder than standard RL (which requires Õ(SA / ((1−γ)³ ε²))), and can be easier by a factor of σ/(1−γ) when the uncertainty level σ ≳ 1−γ.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Compared to Yang et al. (2022) and Panaganti & Kalathil (2022), which only handle TV distance with S²-dependent bounds, this paper achieves SA-dependent bounds for the full class of general smooth L_p norms under both sa- and s-rectangularity. Against Shi et al. (2023), which achieved the state-of-the-art minimax-optimal bound SA/((1−γ)² max{1−γ,σ} ε²) exclusively for TV (L_1), this paper matches that rate while extending it to all general L_p norms (p≥2) and weighted/rescaled variants. The most significant improvement is over Clavier et al. (2023): in the regime σ ≳ 1−γ, the prior bound of Õ(SA/((1−γ)⁴ε²)) is tightened to Õ(SA/(σ(1−γ)²ε²)), yielding a factor of 1/(1−γ) improvement that grows with the discount factor. For s-rectangular RMDPs, this paper provides the first lower bound (using L_∞), confirming that the upper bound is tight and that s-rectangular RMDPs are no harder than sa-rectangular ones in terms of sample complexity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the sample complexity analysis of distributionally robust MDPs from specific divergences (TV, KL, χ²) studied by Yang et al. [2022], Panaganti & Kalathil [2022], and Shi et al. [2023] to the broader class of general smooth Lp norms. It builds directly on Clavier et al. [2023], which first addressed Lp norms but with weaker bounds, by deriving new dual optimization formulations and concentration lemmas that control the infinite span seminorm of the value function. The technical foundation draws on the dual-form approach of Iyengar [2005] and the generative-model analysis framework of Agarwal et al. [2020], adapted here to the robust setting with norm-specific geometric constants.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical implication is that distributionally robust RL is never more sample-inefficient than standard RL and can be strictly easier when uncertainty is large, which provides a strong theoretical justification for adopting robustness without fearing a data-efficiency penalty. The first lower bounds for s-rectangular RMDPs and the demonstration that s-rectangular is not harder than sa-rectangular resolve open questions about the relative difficulty of these formulations. Open questions include extending results to f-divergences (where the value-range-shrinking phenomenon is unverified), finite-horizon settings, and linear MDPs, which the authors identify as natural next steps.
