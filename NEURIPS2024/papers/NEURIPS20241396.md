## Problem

In offline model-based optimization, surrogate models trained on limited offline data produce unreliable predictions for out-of-distribution inputs encountered along optimization trajectories, yielding candidate designs that score poorly under the true oracle. Existing regularization approaches either require hand-tuning a static penalty hyperparameter or directly modifying the surrogate model during optimization, and none provide a task-agnostic mechanism that dynamically adapts the in-distribution constraint to the optimization trajectory in real time.

## Input / Output

The method takes an offline dataset of input-objective pairs, a neural-network surrogate objective f_θ trained on that dataset, and a VAE latent space prior p_VAE(z) as input, and operates entirely in the continuous VAE latent space Z rather than the original (often discrete) design space. It outputs top-k design candidates ranked by the Lagrangian-regularized objective value, along with a dynamically computed regularization strength α at each acquisition step. The source critic is retrained every 4 acquisition steps on newly sampled trajectory points, making the optimization objective time-varying and causally dependent on past observations.

## Method

The core contribution is adaptive source critic regularization (aSCR), which formulates offline MBO as a constrained optimization problem where a K-Lipschitz source critic (a WGAN-inspired fully connected net with weight clamping) penalizes generated samples that are out-of-distribution relative to the offline dataset, measured via the Wasserstein-1 distance. The key insight is that the Lagrange multiplier λ controlling the constraint strength can be computed dynamically at each step by solving the dual problem approximately: Monte Carlo samples are drawn from the VAE prior p_VAE(z), and for each of 200 discretized α values, the algorithm finds the latent point minimizing the combined gradient norm of the surrogate and critic, then selects the α maximizing the dual function. This avoids hand-tuning and adapts the regularization to the current optimization trajectory. The framework is optimizer-agnostic and is instantiated with Bayesian optimization (GABO, using qEI acquisition with a GP posterior) and gradient ascent (GAGA), with the source critic retrained every 4 acquisition steps to track the evolving distribution of proposed designs.

## Results

Across eight offline MBO tasks spanning molecular design, protein design, DNA design, robotics, and clinical medicine, GABO (GABO with aSCR) achieves the best average rank of 3.8 on the constrained top-1 oracle evaluation (next best: COM at 5.5) and 3.0 on the relaxed top-128 evaluation (next best: BootGen at 4.6). GABO proposes top-1 designs that outperform the best design in the offline dataset on 6 of 8 tasks, more than any other method. The aSCR regularization also improves its parent optimizers: GABO (rank 3.8) substantially outperforms vanilla BO (rank 8.8) and TuRBO (rank 9.0), while GAGA (rank 7.4) outperforms plain gradient ascent (rank 9.0).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GABO (aSCR + BO-qEI) achieves the best average rank of 3.8 on the top-1 oracle evaluation, outperforming the closest competitor COM (rank 5.5) and all other baselines including BONET (5.7), BootGen (6.2), Anneal (6.8), and its own parent BO (8.8) and TuRBO (9.0). On the relaxed top-128 evaluation, GABO's advantage widens to rank 3.0 versus BootGen (4.6) and BONET (5.6). GABO wins on 3 of 8 tasks outright (Branin, LogP, UTR) and is second-best on 2 more (GFP, ChEMBL) for k=1; for k=128 it wins on 4 tasks (Branin, LogP, UTR, ChEMBL). The closest competitor COM (Trabucco et al., 2021) uses a static hand-tuned regularization and wins on D'Kitty (0.90 vs 0.71) and LogP (30.8 vs 21.3) for k=1, but GABO's adaptive λ avoids COM's catastrophic failures on Branin (-3099 vs -2.6) and Warfarin. GAGA (aSCR + gradient ascent, rank 7.4) also improves over plain gradient ascent (rank 9.0), confirming that aSCR is optimizer-agnostic, though it trails GABO due to the inherent exploration limitations of first-order methods in high-dimensional latent spaces.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the WGAN source critic (Arjovsky et al., 2017) not for generation but as a dynamic regularization mechanism for offline optimization, and extends the Lagrangian formulation of COM (Trabucco et al., 2021) by replacing its hand-tuned static regularization weight with an adaptively computed Lagrange multiplier via a Monte Carlo approximation of the dual problem. The parent optimizer is TuRBO's quasi-expected improvement (Eriksson et al., 2019), which the authors augment with the source critic penalty rather than replacing. The key conceptual departure from prior offline MBO work (BONET, DDOM, RoMA, BDI, ExPT, ROMO) is that aSCR is explicitly task- and optimizer-agnostic, operating as a plug-in constraint rather than a bespoke generative or trajectory-learning architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in offline MBO—surrogate overestimation on out-of-distribution inputs—by constraining optimization trajectories to in-distribution regions, which is critical for high-stakes applications (drug dosing, protein design) where oracle queries are expensive or dangerous. A key limitation is the requirement for a VAE latent space prior to make the Lagrange multiplier computation tractable, restricting applicability to tasks amenable to such priors. The introduction of the Warfarin conditional MBO task fills a gap in the benchmarking landscape, and the finding that dynamic source critic retraining yields a 67.4% improvement in top-1 performance (but 0% in top-128) for GABO highlights an open question about when adversarial regularization is most beneficial. Future work could extend aSCR to arbitrary configuration spaces and more sophisticated parent optimizers beyond BO-qEI and gradient ascent.
