## Problem

Gradient descent for overparameterized non-convex low-rank matrix sensing suffers from two compounding issues: it is slowed by saddle points and overparameterization degrades its convergence from linear to sub-linear rates. Existing preconditioned methods (ScaledGD, PrecGD) mitigate the condition-number dependence but remain trapped in saddle regions due to their damping parameter. The paper asks whether a second-order method with gradient-descent-level per-iteration cost can achieve global Q-linear convergence from random initialization, independent of the condition number and unimpeded by saddle points.

## Input / Output

The method consumes m linear measurements b = A(M) of an unknown rank-r matrix M ∈ R^{n×n}, where the sensing operator A(·) must satisfy the rank-(r+1) Restricted Isometry Property with a sufficiently small constant δ. The optimization variable is a single matrix X ∈ R^{2n×d} with d > r (overparameterized), initialized as random Gaussian with variance σ ≤ c₀‖Σ‖₂/n. The output is the factorization X = [U; V] and the recovered matrix M̂ = PXXᵀQ, where P and Q are fixed projection matrices that unify symmetric (U=V) and asymmetric (U≠V) sensing into one formulation.

## Method

The AGN method applies the Gauss-Newton framework to the nonlinear least-squares reformulation ψ(X) = ½‖A(PXXᵀQ) − b‖₂², but replaces the intractable full GN step (whose Jacobian is singular when d > r) with a Gauss-Seidel decomposition: it alternately updates the U-block and V-block of X, each via a closed-form least-squares solve using the Moore-Penrose pseudo-inverse under the RIP condition. This avoids the Levenberg-Marquardt damping λ that slows ScaledGD/PrecGD near saddle points. The key theoretical result is global Q-linear convergence ψ(X_{t+1}) − ψ* ≤ c_q[ψ(X_t) − ψ*] from random Gaussian initialization, with convergence factor c_q independent of the condition number κ(M). Saddle-point analysis further shows that while GD and PrecGD achieve only o(ε) objective decrease near saddles, AGN achieves a Θ(1) decrease, explaining its empirically faster escape from saddle regions.

## Results

AGN achieves a global Q-linear convergence rate with iteration complexity log(1/ε) from random initialization, independent of the condition number κ of the target matrix M. In contrast, GD requires κ¹¹ log(κ²/n) + κ¹⁰ log(κ⁶/ε) iterations and ScaledGD(λ) requires log κ · log(κn) + log(1/ε) iterations. In wall-clock time on a 100×100 matrix with κ=10, AGN completes in 27.24 s versus 87.12 s for PrecGD, 55.87 s for ScaledGD(λ), and >500 s for GD; on 500×500 with κ=50, AGN takes 632 s versus 1922 s (PrecGD), 1259 s (ScaledGD), and >5000 s (GD).

## Baselines & Comparisons

AGN dominates all three baselines on both iteration complexity and wall-clock time. Compared to GD [20], AGN is orders of magnitude faster (27 s vs >500 s on 100×100) because GD is severely slowed by saddle points and its convergence rate degrades with κ. Compared to PrecGD [15], AGN is roughly 3× faster (27 s vs 87 s on 100×100, κ=10) and, critically, AGN's convergence rate is independent of κ while PrecGD's final rate is κ-independent but it still stalls in saddle regions due to the damping parameter λ. Compared to ScaledGD(λ) [21], AGN is about 2× faster (27 s vs 56 s on 100×100, κ=10) and avoids the log κ · log(κn) overhead that ScaledGD incurs when escaping saddle regions. The paper further shows that PrecGD and ScaledGD(λ) are special cases of the Levenberg-Marquardt method within the Gauss-Newton framework, explaining why the damping factor λ that ensures monotonic descent simultaneously impedes escape from saddle points.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies symmetric and asymmetric low-rank matrix sensing under a single Burer-Monteiro-style factorization and applies a Gauss-Seidel decomposition of the Gauss-Newton subproblem, departing from the Levenberg-Marquardt damping used by ScaledGD(λ) and PrecGD. It explicitly shows that ScaledGD(λ) and PrecGD are special cases of the Levenberg-Marquardt method within the Gauss-Newton framework, thereby placing preconditioned gradient descent and second-order methods on a common theoretical footing. The saddle-point analysis extends prior landscape results (Ye & Du 2021, Xiong et al. 2023) by quantifying why first-order and preconditioned methods stall near saddle points while the proposed AGN does not, and it generalizes local Gauss-Newton convergence results (Liu et al. 2015, Zilber & Nadler 2022) to a global Q-linear guarantee from random initialization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The global Q-linear convergence with a rate independent of the condition number of M addresses a key bottleneck for ill-conditioned matrix sensing that plagues gradient descent and its preconditioned variants. The observation that symmetric parameterization breaks the descent property in the overparameterized regime while asymmetric parameterization restores it offers a practical design principle for structured non-convex factorization problems. Limitations include the reliance on a small RIP constant, the saddle-point analysis being restricted to rank-1 ground truth, and the method's applicability being tied to the low-rank structure—extending these results to larger RIP constants, higher-rank saddle analysis, and deep network optimization remain open.
