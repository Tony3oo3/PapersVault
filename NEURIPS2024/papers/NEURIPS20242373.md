## Problem

While UCB-based methods dominate the theoretical landscape for cooperative multi-agent reinforcement learning, they suffer from poor practical performance, expensive confidence-set computation, and limited generalizability beyond linear structures. Randomized exploration strategies (Thompson Sampling, perturbed-history exploration, Langevin Monte Carlo) have shown strong empirical and theoretical results in single-agent RL and bandits, but no prior work provides provably efficient randomized exploration for cooperative MARL with communication complexity guarantees.

## Input / Output

Each of M agents in parallel MDPs receives its own state trajectory and reward signal, and all agents share the same state and action spaces. Agents communicate with a central server under a determinant-based synchronization condition (3.3), uploading local transition tuples (s, a, s') or, in the linear setting, compressed d×d covariance matrices and d-dimensional vectors. The output at each step is a greedy action per agent derived from the max-over-samples Q-function estimate, with the full learned policy parameterized by the LSVI regression weights.

## Method

The paper proposes a unified framework (Algorithm 1) where M agents run LSVI in parallel and synchronize data with a server, combined with two Thompson-Sampling-type exploration strategies. CoopTS-PHE adds i.i.d. Gaussian noise to rewards and the regularizer in the LSVI loss, then solves N independent least-squares problems and takes the max over the N resulting Q-functions; CoopTS-LMC instead performs noisy gradient descent (Langevin dynamics) for J_k steps per episode to approximate the posterior. Both strategies use a multi-sampling trick (N independent parameter copies) and avoid the expensive Cholesky decomposition required by exact linear TS, making them directly applicable to deep function classes. Under linear MDPs, both achieve a Õ(d^{3/2}H²√(MK)) regret bound with Õ(dHM²) communication complexity, matching the best known UCB-based cooperative MARL results while being the first theoretical guarantees for randomized exploration in this setting.

## Results

Theoretically, both CoopTS-PHE and CoopTS-LMC achieve a cumulative regret of $\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}(d^{3/2}H^2\sqrt{MK})$ with communication complexity $\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}(dHM^2)$ under linear parallel MDPs, matching the best single-agent randomized RL regret bounds (M=1) and the best cooperative MARL communication complexity. In the misspecified setting, CoopTS-PHE tolerates model error $\zeta=\mathcal{O}(\sqrt{d/MK})$ while CoopTS-LMC requires $\zeta=\mathcal{O}(\sqrt{1/MK})$, indicating PHE has a $\sqrt{d}$-factor higher tolerance. Empirically, on N-chain (N=25, m=3 agents), PHE and LMC outperform all DQN-based baselines; on Super Mario Bros (m=4, heterogeneous environments), both strategies surpass vanilla DQN, Double DQN, Bootstrapped DQN, and Noisy-Net; on BuildingEnv (Tampa, hot-humid), PHE and LMC achieve higher mean returns than all baselines including random actions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

In the theoretical comparison (Table 1), CoopTS-PHE and CoopTS-LMC match the regret of Coop-LSVI [24] ($\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(d^{3/2}H^2\sqrt{MK})$) while reducing communication complexity from $dHM^3$ to $dHM^2$, matching Asyn-LSVI [56]. Unlike all prior multi-agent baselines (Coop-LSVI, Asyn-LSVI), both proposed methods support randomized exploration and generalize to deep RL. In the single-agent limit (M=1), the regret matches LSVI-PHE [32] and LMC-LSVI [33], and is comparable to LSVI-UCB [36] and OPT-RLSVI [88], though LSVI-ASE [34] achieves a tighter $\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(dH^2\sqrt{K})$ bound. Empirically, on N-chain with m=3 agents, PHE and LMC clearly outperform vanilla DQN, Double DQN, Bootstrapped DQN, and Noisy-Net in average episode return, with PHE showing less fluctuation than LMC. On Super Mario Bros with heterogeneous environments (m=4), LMC shows a more pronounced advantage over PHE, while both still outperform all DQN baselines. On BuildingEnv (Tampa), PHE and LMC achieve higher mean returns than all baselines including random actions, as shown by violin plot distributions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously separate research threads: randomized exploration in single-agent RL (PHE from Ishfaq et al. 2021, LMC from Ishfaq et al. 2024) and cooperative MARL in parallel MDPs (Coop-LSVI from Dubey & Pentland 2021, Asyn-LSVI from Min et al. 2023). Prior cooperative MARL work relied exclusively on UCB-based exploration, which the authors argue is computationally expensive and performs poorly in practice, while prior randomized exploration work was confined to single-agent settings. The paper's unified framework extends both PHE and LMC to the multi-agent parallel MDP setting with a determinant-based synchronization mechanism, and shows that in the linear case these noisy-gradient/noisy-reward strategies are equivalent to (or converge to) standard Thompson Sampling without requiring expensive matrix square-root computations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first provable regret guarantee for randomized exploration in cooperative MARL, closing a gap between the strong theoretical results of UCB-based cooperative MARL and the superior practical performance of TS-type methods. The matching communication complexity with the asynchronous Asyn-LSVI algorithm (despite being synchronous) and the misspecification analysis showing PHE tolerates √d more model error than LMC provide concrete guidance for practitioners choosing between the two strategies. Open questions include extending to fully decentralized or federated settings without a central server, reducing communication costs in general (nonlinear) function classes, and understanding whether the PHE-vs-LMC tolerance gap persists in deeper network architectures.
