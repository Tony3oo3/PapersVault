## Problem

Classical active learning assumes the learner must acquire knowledge across the entire domain, but real-world tasks require predicting at specific targets (e.g., a test set, unsafe parameter regions) that may lie outside the region where data can be collected. While MacKay (1992) proposed selecting samples to minimize posterior uncertainty about such targets, no theoretical convergence guarantees existed for this 'transductive' setting. This paper provides the first uniform convergence rates showing that uncertainty-minimizing decision rules converge to the smallest attainable uncertainty from the accessible sample space.

## Input / Output

The method takes two subsets of the domain: a target space A (where predictions are needed) and a sample space S (where observations can be made), along with a GP kernel or neural-network embedding that defines similarity between points. In active fine-tuning, A is the unlabeled test distribution and S is the labeled training pool; in safe Bayesian optimization, A_n is the set of potential maximizers and S_n is the pessimistic safe set, both evolving each round. The output is a sequence of selected data points from S (or batches thereof) that minimize posterior uncertainty about f_A, yielding function predictions with bounded epistemic uncertainty and, in applications, fine-tuned models or safe optimal parameters.

## Method

The core decision rule selects each new sample x_n ∈ S to minimize the posterior entropy (ITL) or total variance (VTL) of the prediction targets f_A, which is equivalent to maximizing conditional information gain I(f_A; y_x | D_{n-1}). Under a submodularity assumption on the information-gain or variance-reduction function, greedy selection achieves a (1−1/e) approximation to the optimal non-adaptive design. The key theoretical result (Theorem 3.2) bounds the marginal posterior variance at any target x ∈ A by the irreducible uncertainty η²_S(x) (the variance given complete knowledge of f on S) plus a reducible term O(γ_{A,S}(n)/√n) that vanishes as n grows. For neural-network fine-tuning, the GP is instantiated via a linear model in a learned embedding space (NTK, last-layer, or loss-gradient embeddings), making the kernel k(x,x') = φ_θ(x)ᵀΣφ_θ(x'), and batch selection is performed greedily to ensure both relevance to A and diversity within each batch. For safe Bayesian optimization, the target and sample spaces are updated adaptively each round, and the same convergence machinery guarantees eventual optimality within the largest reachable safe set.

## Results

In active fine-tuning experiments (Figure 4), ITL, VTL, and CTL consistently and significantly outperform all baselines on both MNIST (batch size 1) and CIFAR-100 (batch size 10), with ITL retrieving samples from the target distribution P_A at more than twice the rate of COSINESIMILARITY. In safe Bayesian optimization (Figure 5), ITL and VTL achieve lower cumulative regret than SAFEOPT, ORACLE SAFEOPT, and ISE across 1D, 2D synthetic tasks and a real quadcopter controller tuning experiment, with VTL showing a slight edge over ITL due to its focus on marginal variances that drive safe-set expansion. In GP experiments (Figure 3), ITL and VTL reduce the entropy and total variance of the target space faster than uncertainty sampling (UNSA) and random sampling, with the advantage growing as the target space shrinks relative to the domain.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against uncertainty sampling (UNSA, Lewis & Catlett 1994), ITL and VTL clearly win in GP experiments (Figure 3), as UNSA samples points with high prior uncertainty that are not informative about the target space A; ITL and VTL also outperform 'local' UNSA constrained to A∩S. In active fine-tuning (Figure 4), diversity-based BADGE (Ash et al. 2020) is more competitive than RANDOM but still loses to ITL/VTL because it does not explicitly target relevance to P_A. Relevance-based COSINESIMILARITY has an initial advantage over RANDOM but quickly falls behind for batch sizes >1 due to diminishing informativeness, while ITL/VTL maintain their lead by balancing both relevance and diversity. The heuristic INFORMATIONDENSITY (Settles & Craven 2008) also loses to ITL/VTL. In safe BO (Figure 5), SAFEOPT (Berkenkamp et al. 2021) is undirected and expands in all directions including known-suboptimal ones, making ITL/VTL's directed exploration more sample-efficient; ISE (Bottero et al. 2022) is purely expansionist without an exploration trade-off, and ITL/VTL outperform it on the 2D task. EIC (Gardner et al. 2014) is noted to violate safety constraints, whereas ITL and VTL never do.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the classical inductive active learning framework (where target space, sample space, and domain all coincide) to the 'transductive' setting where prediction targets A and accessible sample space S can differ. The decision rules ITL and VTL were previously proposed empirically by MacKay (1992) and subsequent works (Seo et al. 2000, Yu et al. 2006, Bogunovic et al. 2016) without theoretical guarantees; this paper provides the first convergence rates for these rules in the transductive setting, building on submodular maximization theory (Nemhauser et al. 1978) and GP information capacity bounds. The batch selection strategy draws on adaptive submodular optimization (Chen & Krause 2013), and the safe BO application generalizes prior safe exploration methods (Berkenkamp et al. 2021) by framing the exploration-expansion dilemma as a directed transductive learning problem.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The introduction of 'irreducible uncertainty' η²_S(x) — the fundamental limit on what can be learned about points outside S from observations within S — provides a clean theoretical lens for understanding when directed sampling helps and when it cannot. The practical demonstrations on active fine-tuning of EfficientNet-B0 and safe quadcopter controller tuning show that jointly optimizing for relevance and diversity (as ITL/VTL do) substantially outperforms methods that optimize for only one. Open questions include how the task complexity α_{A,S}(n) (which can exceed 1 in directed settings) affects convergence in practice, whether the GP/linearized-embedding assumptions hold for modern deep architectures, and whether the observed scaling trend (growing advantage with larger data pools) persists at frontier model scale.
