## Problem

Policy Mirror Descent (PMD) methods guarantee convergence to the global optimum of the RL objective, but they require exact action-value functions that are inaccessible in standard RL settings. Existing inexact PMD analyses demand uniform-norm approximation error bounds that are typically only achievable with a perfect simulator queried on arbitrary state-action pairs, and require re-sampling the environment for every policy iterate. The paper asks whether PMD can be deployed in RL with efficient, closed-form action-value estimators that still preserve global convergence guarantees.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a fixed dataset of state-action-transition-reward tuples (x_i, a_i, x'_i, r_i) to learn the world model once, after which all PMD iterations proceed via n×n matrix operations without further environment interaction. At inference, given a state x, the policy is evaluated as π_T(x) = SOFTMAX(η H_x C_T), where H_x is the row vector of kernel evaluations k(x, x_i) and C_T is the accumulated weight matrix from the PMD loop. The action space is assumed finite (|A| < ∞), while the state space can be infinite (e.g., a compact subset of R^d), with the policy represented implicitly through kernel features in a Sobolev space.

## Method

POWR learns the MDP transition operator T and reward function r as Conditional Mean Embeddings (CMEs) between Sobolev spaces F = H⊗H and G = R^{|A|}⊗H, using a regularized least-squares estimator T_n = S_n^* K_λ^{-1} Z_n. The key insight is an operatorial characterization of the action-value function as q_π = (Id − γTP_π)^{-1} r, which, combined with a kernel trick, yields a closed-form estimator q̂_π = S_n^*(Id − γBM_π)^{-1} b requiring only an n×n linear solve per PMD step. The policy update uses KL-divergence mirror descent (equivalent to Natural Policy Gradient), expressed recursively as a softmax over cumulative estimated action-values. The paper proves that Sobolev spaces of smoothness s > d/2 guarantee (G,F)-compatibility of all PMD iterates, and under a strong source condition on the sampling distribution, the algorithm converges to the global optimum at rate O(1/T + δ²n^{-α}) with high probability.

## Results

POWR is evaluated on three Gym environments (FrozenLake-v1, Taxi-v3, MountainCar-v0) by reporting average cumulative reward versus number of environment interactions (timesteps) across 7 independent runs. The paper reports that POWR outperforms all baselines (A2C, DQN, TRPO, PPO) by a significant margin in sample efficiency, i.e., the reward achieved after a given number of timesteps. On Taxi-v3, POWR avoids converging to a local optimum, matching only DQN in this regard. No specific numerical reward values are tabulated; results are presented as learning curves in Figure 1 with shaded min/max regions across runs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

POWR is compared against four established RL baselines: A2C [41], DQN [4], TRPO [7], and PPO [6], on FrozenLake-v1, Taxi-v3, and MountainCar-v0. POWR outperforms all four methods in sample efficiency (reward per timestep) across all three environments. On Taxi-v3 specifically, POWR and DQN both avoid the local optimum that traps A2C, TRPO, and PPO. The main drawback of POWR is reduced stability during early training compared to the baselines, which the authors attribute to a sub-optimal interplay between exploration (world model learning) and exploitation (PMD steps). The comparison is qualitative (learning curves) rather than a single final-metric table, so exact numerical gaps are not stated in the text.

## Relation to Prior Work

POWR sits at the intersection of two research threads: Policy Mirror Descent (PMD) methods, which guarantee global convergence for policy optimization under full information [10, 11, 12], and Conditional Mean Embedding (CME) world models, which learn transition operators in RKHS without requiring a simulator [14, 15, 22]. The paper departs from traditional world-model approaches (e.g., Ha & Schmidhuber [16], Dreamer [19]) that learn a latent simulator and incur both model and sampling error, instead using CMEs to estimate expectations directly and incurring only model error. It also extends the inexact PMD convergence analysis of [12]—which required uniform-norm bounds on approximation error typically guaranteed only by a perfect simulator—to a setting where the error is controlled by CME learning rates, and generalizes PMD from tabular to infinite state spaces via Sobolev-space compatibility.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical implication is that POWR avoids the need to re-sample the MDP for every PMD iterate, replacing an expensive per-policy estimation loop with a single world-model learning phase followed by closed-form matrix operations. Theoretically, the convergence rate of O(log T/T) under decreasing approximation error suggests that alternating exploration (world-model refinement) and exploitation (PMD steps) could yield near-optimal rates without requiring a perfect model upfront. However, empirical validation is limited to three simple Gym environments (FrozenLake, Taxi, MountainCar) with acknowledged training instability, and the method has not been tested at scale or in continuous-action settings. Open questions include extending to infinite action spaces, scaling via Nyström or reduced-rank approximations, and whether the alternating strategy actually improves the exploration-exploitation trade-off in practice.
