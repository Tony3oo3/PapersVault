## Problem

Existing model-switching strategies for LLMs rely on separately trained routing models, require access to model weights or fine-tuning capabilities, and demand large amounts of task-specific data. This paper addresses how to efficiently route queries between black-box LLM APIs of varying sizes and costs to optimize the cost-performance tradeoff, using only a small number of training examples (as few as 50) and no architectural modifications.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20244163/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes a context passage (story, dialogue history, or research paper, up to 3500 tokens) and a question as input, along with access to N black-box LLM APIs ordered by parameter count and cost. A small training set of <context, question, ground-truth, LM_answer> triplets (as few as 50) is used to calibrate the router. The output is a final answer produced by whichever LM the router selects, with the routing decision made after a self-verification step that yields a confidence probability. The verification is performed by the same small model that generated the answer, using a generic 4-shot entailment prompt shared across all tasks.

## Method

AutoMix operates in three steps: (1) a small LM generates an initial answer, (2) the same small LM performs self-verification by framing answer correctness as a textual entailment task (sampling k times at high temperature and computing the fraction of 'correct' verdicts), and (3) a POMDP-based router decides whether to accept the answer or escalate to a larger LM. The POMDP treats the true performance of each LM on the query as a hidden state, with noisy verification probabilities as observations; observation probabilities are estimated via Gaussian kernel density estimation over the continuous state space. The reward function R = P − λ·C balances performance against cost, and the policy is solved offline using the AdaOps solver with particle filtering, reducing inference to a simple lookup table. A key advantage is that the POMDP can identify unsolvable queries (where no model would succeed) and avoid routing them to expensive LMs, and it generalizes to N-model settings by sequentially updating its belief with each verifier's output.

## Results

AutoMix-POMDP (AutoMix+P) achieves consistently positive ΔIBC across all five datasets and three SLMs, with the largest gains on DIPLOMAT (up to 156.8% with MISTRAL-7B) and COQA (up to 86.5% with LLAMA2-13B). The method reduces computational cost by over 50% for comparable performance relative to always using the LLM. With as few as 50 training examples, AutoMix maintains 15% ΔIBC and beats baselines by more than 20% in absolute terms. The POMDP router is the only method yielding positive gains across all 15 dataset-model configurations tested.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AutoMix is compared against two state-of-the-art model-routing baselines: FrugalGPT (Chen et al., 2023), which uses a finetuned DistillBert router with a 0-cost verifier, and HybridLLM (Ding et al., 2024), which uses a trained DeBERTa router that directly selects between SLM and LLM. AutoMix+POMDP outperforms both baselines in 14 out of 15 dataset-model configurations, with the sole near-tie being QASPER with LLAMA2-13B (8.5 vs. FrugalGPT's 9.4). The margins are substantial: on DIPLOMAT with MISTRAL-7B, AutoMix+P achieves 156.8% ΔIBC versus 67.1% for HybridLLM and 16.8% for FrugalGPT. Notably, both baselines require domain-specific training data and incur no verification cost at inference, yet AutoMix—using only a generic 4-shot prompt and as few as 50 training examples—consistently surpasses them. In low-data regimes (≤50 samples), AutoMix's advantage over FrugalGPT widens to over 20% in absolute ΔIBC, highlighting its practical advantage when task-specific data is scarce.

## Relation to Prior Work

AutoMix departs from prior model-routing work (FrugalGPT, HybridLLM) that trains dedicated classifier routers (DistilBERT, DeBERTa) by instead combining few-shot self-verification framed as textual entailment with a POMDP-based meta-router, requiring only black-box API access and as few as 50 labeled examples. It also distinguishes itself from self-verification literature (Weng et al., Jiang et al.) by using verification not for output correction but as a noisy observation signal for a decision-theoretic routing agent. The POMDP formulation repurposes a classical sequential-decision framework (previously applied to robotics, crowdsourcing, navigation) to the novel setting of LLM cost-quality routing, where the key insight is that noisy verifier outputs are naturally modeled as partial observations of an underlying difficulty state.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AutoMix offers a practical, low-overhead method to reduce LLM API costs by over 50% while maintaining comparable performance, which is directly relevant as organizations increasingly deploy multiple model tiers. The ability to identify unsolvable queries and avoid routing them to expensive models, combined with the POMDP's graceful handling of noisy self-verification, addresses real deployment challenges that trained-router baselines handle poorly. Key limitations include restriction to context-grounded reasoning tasks (not factual QA or commonsense reasoning) and dependence on the quality of few-shot verification, which the paper acknowledges can be uninformative in some settings. Open questions include extension to open-ended generation where performance is subjective, and whether the approach generalizes to non-contextual tasks as open-source models improve.
