## Problem

Deep neural networks achieve state-of-the-art accuracy on tabular data but remain opaque black boxes, while existing interpretable-by-design models (linear models, decision trees, NAM, TabNet) sacrifice predictive performance. The paper addresses the open question of whether a single architecture can be simultaneously deep enough to capture complex feature interactions and linear enough to provide per-instance explanations without a separate surrogate or post-hoc explainer.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240998/cropped_page3_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a standard M-dimensional tabular feature vector (numerical features standardized, categorical features one-hot encoded or natively handled) and a categorical target label during training. A single forward pass through the hypernetwork produces both the class probability (via softmax over generated logits) and the instance-specific linear weight matrix w(x_n; θ) ∈ R^{C×(M+1)} that constitutes the explanation. Feature attribution scores are derived directly as the element-wise product w(x_n;θ)_m · x_{n,m}, requiring no additional model queries or sampling.

## Method

IMN is a deep MLP hypernetwork (TabResNet backbone with 2 residual blocks, 128 units, GELU activations) that maps each input x_n to the full parameter set of a linear model in the original feature space. For multi-class classification, the generated weights define per-class logits z_c = Σ_m w_{c,m}·x_{n,m} + w_{c,0} followed by softmax; for regression, a single linear combination is produced. Training is purely supervised end-to-end: the hypernetwork parameters θ are optimized to minimize the task loss plus an L1 sparsity penalty on the generated weights, so the network learns to produce accurate local linear models shared across all data points rather than fitting a separate linear model per instance. The resulting architecture is 'mesomorphic'—deep in its parameter-generating pathway yet locally linear in its prediction function—and feature attribution is read off directly from the generated coefficients without any additional optimization or sampling procedure.

## Results

On the 35-dataset AutoML benchmark, IMN achieves a comparable average AUROC rank to TabResNet and CatBoost (no statistically significant difference, p>0.05), while significantly outperforming TabNet and NAM. On the XAI-Bench explainability evaluation, IMN ties with Kernel SHAP at 7 wins across 15 metric-dataset combinations (faithfulness, infidelity, monotonicity, Shapley correlation), and strongly outperforms TabNet (0 wins). Inference time is 0.025s per instance for IMN versus 1.60s for TabNet (64× faster) and 17.7–565s for SHAP(TabResNet).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against white-box interpretable models (Decision Trees, Logistic Regression), IMN achieves a statistically significant improvement in AUROC across the AutoML benchmark, confirming that the hypernetwork-based approach captures non-linear interactions that linear/tree models miss. Compared to the black-box TabResNet backbone (same architecture, no hypernetwork), IMN shows no statistically significant accuracy difference, demonstrating that explainability comes at essentially zero cost. CatBoost is also statistically indistinguishable from IMN in accuracy, while Random Forest is significantly worse. Among interpretable deep-learning baselines, IMN significantly outperforms both TabNet (on accuracy and all interpretability metrics) and NAM (on accuracy), with TabNet additionally being 64× slower at inference. On the XAI-Bench explainability evaluation, IMN ties with Kernel SHAP (7 wins each out of 15) and outperforms LIME, MAPLE, L2X, BreakDown, and SHAP on most metrics; it dominates TabNet (0 wins) across all five interpretability measures. DANet and HyperTab (Appendix B) are also outperformed by IMN under default hyperparameters, consistent with the main findings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the hypernetwork paradigm (Ha et al., 2017), where a neural network generates parameters of another network, and on TabResNet (Kadra et al., 2021) as the backbone architecture. It departs from prior interpretable-by-design methods (TabNet, NAM, DANet) that sacrifice accuracy for interpretability, and from post-hoc local explainers (LIME, SHAP) that require separate surrogate training per instance. The closest prior work is Alvarez-Melis & Jaakkola (2018), which also trains hypernetworks for local models, but IMN generates interpretable linear models directly in the original feature space via end-to-end supervised optimization rather than learning prototype instances through an encoder.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical contribution is that explainability comes at essentially zero additional cost: IMN matches its black-box TabResNet counterpart in accuracy while providing competitive interpretability on XAI-Bench, with 64× faster inference than TabNet and up to 11,400× faster than SHAP. This challenges the assumed trade-off between model depth/accuracy and interpretability for tabular data. The main limitation is that per-instance models are restricted to linear form, and the authors suggest extending the hypernetwork to generate decision trees or local SVMs. An open question is whether the mesomorphic principle generalizes beyond tabular data to other modalities, though a preliminary CIFAR-10 experiment in the appendix hints at this possibility.
