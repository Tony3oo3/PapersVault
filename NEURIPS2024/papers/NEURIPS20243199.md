## Problem

Existing multi-subject personalization methods for text-to-image diffusion models (e.g., DreamBooth, Cut-Mix, Textual Inversion) suffer from identity mixing when generating images containing multiple user-defined subjects simultaneously, where attributes from different subjects bleed into each other. This problem is especially severe for semantically similar subjects that share visual attributes like color or texture, and prior attempts to fix it via spatial conditioning (bounding boxes, ControlNet, cross-attention manipulation) either produce stitching artifacts or prove ineffective on recent architectures like SDXL.

## Input / Output

The method takes a small set of reference images (typically 5–10 per subject) for 2–5 user-defined subjects along with a free-form text prompt describing a scene. Reference images are automatically segmented using OWLv2 (open-vocabulary detection) and SAM (segmentation) in a preprocessing step. The output is a single 1024×1024 RGB image (SDXL resolution) containing all personalized subjects in the prompted scene without identity mixing. The method is model-agnostic, demonstrated on both U-Net-based (SDXL, SD v1.5) and DiT-based (FLUX) backbones.

## Method

MuDI decouples subject identities through two mechanisms built on SAM-segmented subjects. During training, a data augmentation called Seg-Mix randomly composes the segmented subjects (with random positions, scales, and permitted overlap) onto a blank canvas, paired with a minimal prompt ('A photo of [V1] and [V2], simple background'), which strips identity-irrelevant background information and prevents attribute leakage between subjects; the model is then fine-tuned via the DreamBooth objective with LoRA (rank 32). During inference, instead of sampling from pure Gaussian noise, the denoising process is initialized with a mean-shifted latent created by VAE-encoding a randomly composed image of the segmented subjects, scaled by a coefficient γ, which provides coarse layout and identity hints that prevent subject missing and dominance. The approach is further aided by descriptive class nouns (e.g., 'Weimaraner' instead of 'dog') automatically generated by GPT-4V to sharpen identity separation for similar subjects, and the authors introduce a new Detect-and-Compare (D&C) metric that measures multi-subject fidelity by comparing a similarity matrix between detected and reference subjects against a ground-truth reference-reference matrix, directly penalizing identity mixing.

## Results

On the new 8-pair multi-subject dataset evaluated with SDXL, MuDI achieves a D&C-DS score of 0.637 and D&C-DINO of 0.610, substantially outperforming the strongest baseline Cut-Mix (0.432 / 0.460) by roughly 47% in relative terms. MuDI also leads on text fidelity with ImageReward 0.770 and CLIP score 0.263, compared to DreamBooth's 0.579 and 0.255. In human evaluation, MuDI achieves twice the success rate for preventing identity mixing over Cut-Mix and is preferred over 70% of the time in side-by-side comparisons against the strongest baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MuDI outperforms all six baselines on both multi-subject fidelity (D&C scores) and text fidelity (ImageReward, CLIP score). Against the closest multi-subject baseline Cut-Mix [15], MuDI improves D&C-DS from 0.432 to 0.637 (+47% relative) and ImageReward from −0.287 to 0.770, while also eliminating the stitching artifacts that plague Cut-Mix. DreamBooth [42] achieves 0.371 D&C-DS and 0.579 ImageReward; adding region control [14] actually degrades both metrics (0.340 D&C-DS, 0.349 ImageReward), confirming that cross-attention-based spatial conditioning is ineffective for SDXL. Textual Inversion [13] performs worst overall (0.116 D&C-DS, −0.149 ImageReward), failing to preserve subject details. In the appendix comparison with Stable Diffusion v2, MuDI (0.692 D&C-DS) also beats Cones2 [25] (0.408) and Mix-of-Show [14] (0.367), while matching Mix-of-Show with ControlNet (0.688) at 1.1× inference speed versus 1.5×. Custom Diffusion [21] scores 0.353 D&C-DS with SDXL, further below MuDI.

## Relation to Prior Work

MuDI builds on the DreamBooth fine-tuning paradigm for single-subject personalization and extends it to multiple subjects, departing from prior multi-subject approaches that rely on spatial conditioning (ControlNet, region control via cross-attention maps) or Cut-Mix-style augmentation. The key conceptual departure is using a foundation segmentation model (SAM) as a tool for identity decoupling—both as a data augmentation mechanism (Seg-Mix) that removes identity-irrelevant background information, and as an inference-time initialization that provides layout hints without requiring auxiliary inputs like bounding boxes or sketches. The paper also introduces the Detect-and-Compare (D&C) metric and a dedicated evaluation dataset, filling gaps left by prior work that evaluated multi-subject fidelity using single-subject metrics (CLIP-I, DreamSim) extended naively.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MuDI provides a practical, model-agnostic solution to identity mixing that works across architectures (U-Net-based SDXL and DiT-based FLUX) without requiring spatial conditioning or cross-attention manipulation, making it broadly applicable as diffusion model architectures evolve. The D&C metric and the 8-pair dataset of identity-mixing-prone subjects offer the community a more appropriate evaluation framework for multi-subject personalization. Open questions include how the approach scales beyond 5 subjects (success rates drop notably for highly similar subjects), whether the fine-tuning requirement can be eliminated for zero-shot multi-subject composition, and whether the initialization strategy generalizes to video generation or 3D personalization.
