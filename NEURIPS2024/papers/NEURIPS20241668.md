## Problem

LLMs lack the specialized domain knowledge needed to serve as practical crop science assistants, and existing agriculture-related evaluation datasets (CCA, Embrapa, Agri Exam) are limited in both quantity and geographic coverage. No instruction tuning dataset or comprehensive benchmark existed specifically for the crop science domain, leaving a gap between general-purpose LLM capabilities and the professional demands of grain practitioners.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241668/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes 290 professional books, 62K research papers (English and Chinese), corporate reports, and structured XLS files describing 5,990 rice species, converting them via text extraction tools into TXT/XLS intermediates before LLM-based dialogue generation. The CROP dataset outputs 210,038 single-turn and 1,871 multi-turn bilingual dialogues spanning five task types (closed-book Q&A, open-book Q&A, NER, event extraction, summarization) for rice and corn. The CROP benchmark outputs 5,045 multiple-choice questions with Roman-numeral answer options, each labeled with a difficulty tier (easy/moderate/difficult) determined by GPT-4 and GPT-3.5 performance, covering 22 countries across six continents.

## Method

The core contribution is a task-oriented, LLM-human integrated data generation pipeline. For single-turn dialogues, GPT-4 is prompted with a four-component structure (role description, reference text, task-specific guidelines, one-shot example) following the Persona-Task-Context-Format rule, generating Q&As from unstructured text or converting structured XLS data via LLM-generated templates. For multi-turn dialogues, the authors adapt the CAMEL multi-agent framework with a domain-specific twist: two LLM agents (a crop expert assistant and a farmer user) engage in goal-directed dialogue, with each turn conditioned on RAG-retrieved context to ground responses in domain literature. Quality is enforced through a two-stage filtering process: first, an LLM grader performs pairwise ranking in groups of four (retaining top-2 across two random permutations via intersection), then 115 human domain experts remove ~5% of single-turn and ~7% of multi-turn samples. For fine-tuning experiments, open-source 7B–8B models (LLaMA3, Qwen1.5, InternLM2) are trained with LoRA (rank 8, alpha 16, lr 5e-5) on the combined COIG-CQIA + CROP dataset using LLaMA-Factory with DeepSpeed ZeRO-2, yielding an average 29% accuracy improvement on the CROP benchmark.

## Results

On the CROP benchmark (5,045 MCQs across three difficulty levels), Claude-3 achieves the highest overall accuracy among commercial LLMs at 0.900, while GPT-3.5 lags significantly at 0.328. Fine-tuning open-source LLMs with the CROP dataset yields an average overall accuracy improvement of ~29% over base models: LLaMA3-8B improves from 0.348 to 0.752 (+0.404), and InternLM2-7B from 0.368 to 0.748 (+0.380). Even after CROP fine-tuning, open-source models (best: 0.768 for InternLM2+CQIA+CROP) remain below the top commercial models (Claude-3: 0.900), and all models struggle substantially on the 'difficult' subset (ranging from 0.000 for GPT-4 to 0.458 for Claude-3).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among commercial LLMs, Claude-3 (0.900) outperforms GPT-4 (0.856) and Qwen-max (0.866) on overall accuracy, and dominates on the difficult subset (0.458 vs. 0.000 for GPT-4 and 0.301 for Qwen), revealing that GPT-4's perfect scores on easy/moderate questions mask a complete failure on hard questions. GPT-3.5 (0.328) is the weakest commercial model, answering only easy questions correctly. For open-source models, CROP fine-tuning produces the largest gains for LLaMA3-8B (+0.404 overall) and InternLM2-7B (+0.380), while Qwen1.5-7B sees only a marginal +0.030 improvement, suggesting it already had stronger baseline crop-science knowledge. After CROP fine-tuning, the best open-source model (InternLM2+CQIA+CROP at 0.768) still trails Claude-3 by 13.2 percentage points, indicating a remaining gap between open-source and top commercial LLMs in domain expertise. The CROP dataset's contribution is most pronounced on the moderate difficulty level, where all fine-tuned open-source models gain 0.38–0.44 in accuracy over their base versions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the CAMEL multi-agent dialogue framework [38] with RAG augmentation to generate domain-specific multi-turn dialogues for crop science, and follows the LLM-centric data generation pipeline paradigm (GPT-4 as generator, human-in-the-loop filtering) established in recent instruction tuning dataset construction work. It extends the general instruction tuning paradigm exemplified by COIG-CQIA into the crop science domain, while its benchmark surpasses prior agriculture-related question databases (CCA, Embrapa, Agri Exam) in scale, bilingual coverage, and geographic breadth. The task design framework draws on UltraChat's scope taxonomy (Information Access, Information Transformation) and integrates dual guidance from researchers and domain experts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CROP provides the first large-scale instruction tuning resource and a comprehensive bilingual benchmark for crop science, filling a gap that limits LLMs' practical utility for agricultural practitioners. Key limitations include coverage restricted to rice and corn (excluding wheat, tubers, legumes), text-only modality, and experiments limited to 7–8B parameter models, leaving open whether the dataset's benefits transfer to 70B+ models. The benchmark's difficulty labeling is circular (defined by GPT-4's own performance), which constrains its interpretability for tracking progress on the hardest questions. Future work should extend to additional crop families, incorporate multimodal data (images, charts), and validate on larger model scales.
