## Problem

State space models (SSMs) have achieved strong results in sequence modeling with linear-time complexity, but they are designed for independent sequences and lack a principled mechanism to incorporate evolving graph topology. The paper asks whether the foundational discretized linear dynamical system (LDS) methodology underlying SSMs can be extended to temporal graphs, where the graph structure itself changes over time and most structural mutations occur between observed snapshots.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20244033/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a sequence of L discrete-time graph snapshots, each consisting of a graph topology G_l (with fixed node set V and time-varying edge set) and a node feature matrix X_l ∈ R^{N_V×d}. The output is a per-node class label prediction at each snapshot. A critical practical constraint is that observation timestamps are interleaved with structural mutation times, so the majority of edge insertions/deletions between consecutive snapshots remain unobserved, which the method must handle without access to intermediate graph states.

## Method

The paper derives a piecewise linear-time-invariant (LTI) state space model by solving a novel Laplacian-regularized online approximation objective (GHIPPO), which extends the HIPPO memory compression framework to graph-typed inputs by adding a graph Laplacian smoothness penalty that couples node memories through topology. Because the graph structure is piecewise-constant with unobserved mutation times, standard ODE discretization fails; the paper addresses this with a mixed discretization strategy that approximates the unobserved intermediate states via (i) a learnable 1-layer GNN for inter-node diffusion and (ii) a Mix module (CONV1D or gated interpolation) for intra-node mixing of consecutive snapshot features. The resulting recurrence U_l = U_{l-1} e^{Δ_l A} + Δ_l X̂_l B^⊤ is instantiated in three variants—GRAPHSSM-S4 (SISO), GRAPHSSM-S5 (MIMO), and GRAPHSSM-S6 (input-selective Δ, B, C)—stacked K-deep with residual connections and nonlinear activations, yielding linear-time training and inference over the snapshot sequence.

## Results

GRAPHSSM-S4 achieves state-of-the-art node classification performance across all six temporal graph benchmarks. On the four small-scale datasets, it attains 85.26% Micro-F1 on DBLP-3, 93.52% on Brain, 49.21% on Reddit, and 76.80% on DBLP-10, with the largest margin (≈5 points) on Reddit where topology matters most. On the large-scale arXiv and Tmall datasets, GRAPHSSM reaches 70.67% and 66.29% Micro-F1 respectively, while several strong baselines (STAR, tNodeEmbed, ROLAND) run out of memory. The paper reports an average 14% Micro-F1 improvement over SOTA baselines on Reddit and DBLP-10 combined.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GRAPHSSM outperforms all 11 baselines on every dataset. Against the strongest temporal GNN baselines, the margins are: +1.05% Micro-F1 over ROLAND on DBLP-3, +1.17% over tNodeEmbed on Brain, +4.99% over ROLAND on Reddit, and +1.74% over SpikeNet on DBLP-10. Static embedding methods (DeepWalk, Node2Vec) and continuous-time methods (HTNE, M²DNE, DynamicTriad) are consistently 10–30 points behind, with HTNE and M²DNE performing worse than static baselines on Brain (22–24% vs 51–53%), confirming that continuous-time methods are poorly suited to discrete-time snapshots. On the large-scale arXiv and Tmall datasets, GRAPHSSM beats the best non-OOM baselines (ROLAND at 68.27% and SpikeNet at 66.10%) by 2.4% and 0.2% Micro-F1 respectively, while STAR, tNodeEmbed, and ROLAND (on Tmall) fail entirely due to out-of-memory errors. The S4 variant is the best-performing SSM architecture; S5 (fewer parameters) degrades by 1–5 points, and S6 (selective state space) underperforms S4 on Reddit and DBLP-10, suggesting the selective mechanism is ill-suited to graph sequences.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the HIPPO framework (Gu et al., 2020) from independent sequence inputs to graph-structured inputs by introducing a Laplacian-regularized online approximation objective (GHIPPO), yielding a piecewise LTI dynamical system whose discretization is complicated by unobserved graph mutations. The resulting GRAPHSSM architecture instantiates S4, S5, and S6 (Mamba) as graph-informed variants, with a learnable GNN approximating the diffusion step and a mixing module handling the gap between observed snapshots. It departs from prior temporal GNN approaches (STAR, EvolveGCN, ROLAND) that combine sequence models with static GNNs in an ad-hoc manner, instead deriving the recurrent structure from a principled memory-compression objective. The work is conceptually adjacent to Graph Mamba (Behrouz & Hashemi, 2024) but differs in its first-principles derivation and explicit treatment of the unobserved-mutation discretization problem.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The first-principles derivation provides a theoretical justification for why SSMs are a natural fit for temporal graphs (optimal memory compression of both features and topology), and the linear-time complexity makes GRAPHSSM practical for long-sequence benchmarks (arXiv, Tmall) where RNN-based and attention-based baselines either underperform or run out of memory. The explicit identification of the unobserved graph mutation problem and the mixed discretization strategy address a gap that prior SSM-on-graphs work has not formally treated. Key limitations include confinement to discrete-time temporal graphs (continuous-time extension is non-trivial), the mixing module being a heuristic approximation rather than an exact solution, and the finding that the selective mechanism (S6/Mamba) underperforms on graph sequences, suggesting that input-dependent state modulation may not transfer well to structured data.
