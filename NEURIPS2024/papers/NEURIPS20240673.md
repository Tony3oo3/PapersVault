## Problem

Existing link prediction methods rely on conventional supervised learning with static train/test splits, making them unable to adapt to dynamically evolving graphs or to balance exploitation of known links against exploration of uncertain ones. This paper reformulates link prediction as sequential decision-making under contextual bandits, where each prediction is one round of interaction with binary feedback, and introduces the first algorithm to fuse contextual bandits with PageRank for collaborative exploitation and exploration.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240673/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At each round t, the method receives an evolving undirected graph G_{t-1}, a serving node v_t, and a set of k candidate nodes with d-dimensional context vectors. It outputs a single selected target node (the argmax of the propagated score vector), and the graph is updated by adding an edge if the binary reward is 1. For node classification, the problem is transformed by adding k supernodes representing classes, so the output becomes a class label via the selected supernode.

## Method

PRB maintains two neural networks: an exploitation network f1 that maps node contexts to estimated rewards, and an exploration network f2 that takes the gradient of f1 with respect to its parameters as input and predicts the potential gain (residual reward). The combined score h_t = f1 + f2 for each candidate node serves as the teleportation vector in a PageRank equation v_t = αP_t v_t + (1−α)h_t, where P_t is the transition matrix of the current graph and α is a damping factor. This propagates exploitation-exploration scores through graph connectivity, so that nodes well-connected to high-scoring candidates receive boosted scores. Theoretical analysis via Neural Tangent Kernel shows the cumulative pseudo-regret grows sub-linearly as Õ(√(d̃kT))·√(max(d̃, S²)), with the bound interpolating between pure bandit (α=0) and pure graph-optimization (α=1) regimes.

## Results

In online link prediction (10,000 rounds), PRB achieves the lowest cumulative regret across all four datasets: 1555 (MovieLens), 1455 (AmazonFashion), 1929 (Facebook), and 3236 (GrQc), outperforming the best bandit baseline EE-Net by 5.1%, 14.3%, 15.2%, and 5.3% respectively. In offline link prediction, PRB attains the highest Hits@k on all six benchmarks, with a minimum improvement of 0.68% (Collab, 67.29 vs. 66.61) and a maximum of 4.2% (DDI, 88.31 vs. 84.11) over the strongest GNN baseline NCNC. On online node classification, PRB reduces cumulative regret by 3.0% (Cora), 1.2% (Citeseer), and 3.5% (Pubmed) compared to the best bandit baseline NeuralTS.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PRB consistently outperforms all 14 baselines across both online and offline settings. Against bandit-based methods (EE-Net, Neural Greedy, NeuralUCB, NeuralTS), PRB achieves the lowest cumulative regret on every online dataset, with the largest margin over EE-Net on AmazonFashion (1455 vs. 1698, a 14.3% reduction). Against heuristic link-prediction baselines (CN, AA, RA), PRB's advantage is overwhelming—e.g., 92.33 vs. 33.92 Hits@100 on Cora—confirming that learned exploitation-exploration far exceeds static structural scores. Against GNN-based methods, PRB surpasses the strongest competitors BUDDY (88.00) and NCNC (89.65) on Cora by 4.33 and 2.68 points respectively, and extends this lead to DDI (88.31 vs. 84.11 for NCNC, a 4.2% gain). NBFnet fails with out-of-memory errors on Collab and PPA, while PRB completes all datasets. In the appendix comparison, PRB also beats EvePPR (the PageRank-only baseline) and EE-Net (the bandit-only baseline), validating that the fusion of both components is necessary for optimal performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

PRB fuses two previously separate research threads: the exploitation-exploration neural network framework from EE-Net (Ban et al., ICLR 2022) and efficient personalized PageRank for evolving graphs (Li et al., WWW 2023). The theoretical analysis extends the NTK-based regret bounds from neural contextual bandits (Zhou et al., ICML 2020) to a new reward formulation that jointly encodes node contexts and graph connectivity. Conceptually, the paper departs from the dominant supervised-learning paradigm for link prediction (GNNs like BUDDY, NCNC, SEAL) by reframing the problem as sequential decision-making, and from pure bandit approaches by injecting graph structure through PageRank propagation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PRB provides the first principled fusion of exploitation-exploration with graph connectivity for link prediction, with a sub-linear regret guarantee that existing graph-based methods lack. The finding that an online-designed method outperforms SOTA supervised GNNs in the standard offline setting suggests that structured exploration can break performance bottlenecks that purely supervised approaches hit. However, the paper acknowledges it only explores PageRank as the propagation mechanism (not other random walks or GNN message passing), and evaluation is limited to link prediction and node classification, leaving open whether the fusion generalizes to subgraph matching, clustering, or other graph tasks.
