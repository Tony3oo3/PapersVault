## Problem

Existing prototype-based interpretable classifiers rely on CNN backbones with spatially rigid prototypes that cannot accommodate geometric variations of objects, while the few ViT-based prototype models (e.g., ProtoPFormer) fail to project learned prototypes to actual training examples, leaving their 'cases' without well-defined visual representations. Deformable ProtoPNet addresses geometric variation but uses deformable convolutions incompatible with ViT's discrete token outputs and produces incoherent prototypes. The paper asks how to build a ViT-based prototype model that is both inherently interpretable and capable of learning spatially deformed, semantically coherent prototypes with an adaptive number of parts.

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB image of shape H×W×C, splits it into N patches of size L×L×C, and encodes them via a pretrained ViT backbone (DeiT or CaiT) into latent feature tokens computed as the difference between each patch token and the class token (a focal-similarity design). The output is a class label via softmax over summed prototype similarity scores, plus pixel-space visual explanations: each prototype is projected to its closest training image patch, so the bounding boxes shown in the reasoning process correspond exactly to the latent tokens the model compared against, without requiring up-sampling.

## Method

ProtoViT replaces the rigid rectangular prototype of ProtoPNet with a set of K sub-prototypes matched to non-overlapping latent tokens via a greedy matching algorithm constrained by an adjacency mask (radius r) that enforces geometric contiguity. An adaptive slots mechanism uses learnable sigmoid indicators (temperature τ=100) to prune sub-prototypes that are semantically incoherent, giving each prototype a dynamic number of parts; a coherence loss (Eq. 4) penalizes the most dissimilar pair of included sub-prototypes within each prototype. Training proceeds in four stages: (1) joint SGD on encoder and prototypes with modified cluster/separation losses plus coherence and orthogonality losses, (2) slots pruning by optimizing only the slot indicators against coherence and cross-entropy loss, (3) projection of each prototype to its closest training patch (ensuring inherent interpretability), and (4) convex optimization of the final evidence layer with an L1 sparsity penalty on negative cross-class weights. The class-token subtraction in the feature encoding (Eq. 1) is shown via ablation to improve both accuracy (~1%) and class-specific saliency of prototypes.

## Results

On CUB 200-2011, ProtoViT achieves 85.37% top-1 accuracy with DeiT-Small and 85.82% with CaiT-XXS, outperforming all prior prototype-based methods including CNN-backbone models (best CNN: TesNet at 81.5%). On Stanford Cars, ProtoViT reaches 91.84% (DeiT-Small) and 92.40% (CaiT-XXS). On the Location Misalignment Benchmark, ProtoViT (DeiT-Small) achieves the lowest Percentage Change in Ranking (PRC = 1.28%) and Percentage Change in Activation (PAC = 2.92%), indicating superior spatial alignment of prototype explanations compared to CNN-based baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ProtoViT consistently outperforms all ViT-backbone baselines (ProtoPFormer, ViT-Net) across every backbone size on both CUB and Cars, with the largest gap over ProtoPFormer being +0.52% on CUB (DeiT-Small) and +1.36% on Cars (DeiT-Small). Against the strongest CNN-backbone prototype model, TesNet [46], ProtoViT (CaiT-XXS) surpasses it by +4.32% on CUB (85.82% vs. 81.5%) while achieving comparable Cars accuracy (92.40% vs. 92.6%). On the Location Misalignment Benchmark, ProtoViT achieves the lowest PRC (1.28%) and PAC (2.92%), substantially better than ProtoPNet (PRC 13.5%, PAC 23.7%) and ProtoTree (PRC 13.5%, PAC 23.7%), though TesNet has a slightly lower PLC (16.0% vs. 21.68%). In user studies, ProtoViT received a mean understanding score of 3.85/4 versus 2.68 for ProtoPNet and 1.86 for Deformable ProtoPNet, with a statistically significant improvement (p < 10⁻²⁰) in total clarity score over ProtoPNet.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the prototype-based image classification lineage initiated by ProtoPNet (rigid CNN prototypes) and Deformable ProtoPNet (deformed but CNN-based prototypes) by integrating Vision Transformer backbones while preserving inherent interpretability through prototype projection to real training patches. It departs from prior ViT-prototype attempts like ProtoPFormer and ViT-Net, which the authors argue fail to provide inherently interpretable explanations because they do not project learned prototypes to visually grounded examples. The greedy matching algorithm with adjacency masking and the adaptive slots mechanism are novel architectural components that replace the deformable convolution layers used in Deformable ProtoPNet, which are incompatible with ViT's discrete token representations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ProtoViT is the first prototype-based model to simultaneously offer ViT backbones, spatially deformed prototypes, coherent feature representations, adaptive prototype sizes, and inherent interpretability, filling a gap identified in Table 1 of the paper. The finding that a ViT-based prototype model matches or outperforms CNN-based models on the Location Misalignment Benchmark is notable, suggesting that attention-based global mixing does not necessarily degrade spatial interpretability. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger datasets (ImageNet-scale), whether textual justifications can be added via vision-language models, and whether the location misalignment issue (present but mitigated) can be fully resolved.
