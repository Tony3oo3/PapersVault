## Problem

Existing RL with general utility (covering risk-sensitive objectives, exploration, constrained RL, etc.) assumes a fixed environment, while existing robust RL handles only linear utility. This paper asks whether one can train a robust policy for general utility under worst-case environmental perturbation and obtain provable convergence guarantees for the resulting nonconvex-nonconcave minimax problem.

## Input / Output

The method takes a finite MDP (state space S, action space A, discount factor γ), a general utility function f over the occupancy measure Δ^{S×A}, a convex compact ambiguity set Ξ for the transition kernel parameter ξ (e.g., s-rectangular L₁ or L∞ neighborhoods around a nominal kernel), and an initial state distribution ρ. It outputs robust policy parameters θ ∈ Θ minimizing the worst-case utility max_{ξ∈Ξ} f(λ_{θ,ξ}), accompanied by either an ε-stationary point guarantee (Theorem 2) or a global O(1/K) convergence rate to the optimal robust policy (Theorem 3) on polyhedral ambiguity sets.

## Method

The paper formulates robust RL with general utility as min_θ max_ξ f(λ_{θ,ξ}) and designs a two-phase projected stochastic gradient descent-ascent algorithm: Phase 1 performs inner-loop policy descent and outer-loop environment ascent on the original objective, converging to a stationary point of an envelope function; Phase 2 adds a quadratic correction term −L_{ξ,ξ}‖ξ−ξ̃‖² to recover a near-stationary point of the original f, with convergence proved via a novel projected gradient dominance property (Proposition 4) that handles constrained policy spaces without bias. For s-rectangular polyhedral ambiguity sets, a second algorithm exploits the fact that argmax_ξ f(λ_{θ,ξ}) is always attained at a vertex of Ξ (Proposition 6, proved via a novel local invertibility property of the occupancy measure in ξ), then finds a descent direction by solving a convex optimization over near-optimal vertices, yielding O(1/K) global convergence to the optimal robust policy.

## Results

The paper's main quantitative results are theoretical convergence guarantees. Theorem 2 establishes that Algorithm 1 (two-phase stochastic GDA) requires O(log²[(1−γ)⁻¹ε⁻¹] / ((1−γ)²⁵ε¹⁰)) stochastic samples to reach an ε-stationary point of the robust general-utility objective. Theorem 3 proves that Algorithm 2 achieves a global convergence rate of O(1/K) on s-rectangular polyhedral ambiguity sets (including L¹ and L∞), matching the state-of-the-art rate for robust RL policy gradient methods. A single simulation (10 states, 5 actions, entropy utility, L² ambiguity set with radius 0.01) in Appendix A confirms the projected gradient norm decays to a small value, consistent with Theorem 2.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical baseline comparisons; the `compares_to` list is empty. The contribution is framed as a unifying framework that subsumes existing methods (robust RL, constrained RL, entropy-regularized RL, pure exploration) as special cases rather than competing against them. The paper positions its gradient dominance property (Proposition 4) as strictly stronger than the analogous result in Barakat et al. [6], requiring fewer assumptions (no positive-definite Fisher information matrix), introducing no bias term, and extending to constrained parameterized policies. The O(1/K) global convergence rate on polyhedral ambiguity sets matches the rate achieved by Wang et al. [42] for robust RL with linear utility, but extends it to the much harder nonconvex-nonconcave setting with general convex utility. The key technical novelty over prior work is the local invertibility property of the occupancy measure with respect to the transition kernel (Proposition 5), which has no counterpart in the existing robust RL literature.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate research threads: RL with general (non-linear) utility over occupancy measures (Zhang et al. 2020, Barakat et al. 2023) and robust RL under transition kernel uncertainty (Nilim & El Ghaoui 2005, Iyengar 2005). Prior robust RL work restricts to linear utility, while prior general-utility RL assumes a fixed environment; this paper is the first to study their intersection as a nonconvex-nonconcave minimax problem. The algorithmic design draws on the local invertibility assumption from Zhang et al. 2021 and Barakat et al. 2023, the s-rectangular polyhedral ambiguity set structure from Wang et al. 2023 and Wiesemann et al. 2013, and the GDA framework for nonconvex-concave minimax from Lin et al. 2020.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first convergence guarantees for robust RL with general convex utility, filling a gap between two well-studied but previously disconnected problem classes. The two-phase algorithm design—where the first phase converges to a stationary point of a different envelope function, necessitating a corrected second phase—is a non-trivial technical contribution. However, the practical impact is currently limited: the sample complexity is O(ε^{-10}), the global convergence algorithm requires vertex enumeration over the ambiguity set, and the empirical validation is a single small-scale simulation. Open questions include extending to large or continuous state-action spaces, reducing vertex enumeration via structural properties, and handling non-convex utility functions.
