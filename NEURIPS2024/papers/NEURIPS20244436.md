## Problem

Lanczos-FA is the most widely used algorithm for approximating f(A)b in practice, yet its best-known theoretical bound (Fact 3) is a loose uniform polynomial approximation guarantee that fails to explain its near-optimal convergence observed empirically. Prior near-optimality results existed only for the special case f(x)=1/x (where Lanczos-FA equals Conjugate Gradient) and a few other isolated functions. This paper provides the first near-instance-optimality guarantee for Lanczos-FA on a broad class of rational functions, closing the theory-practice gap for functions like the matrix square root and exponential that are well-approximated by rationals.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes a symmetric matrix A exclusively through matrix-vector products (no eigendecomposition is formed), a scalar function f: R→R, a starting vector b ∈ R^d, and an iteration count k. It outputs a vector approximation to f(A)b computed as Q f(T) Q^T b, where Q is the orthonormal Krylov basis and T is the tridiagonal Lanczos matrix. The theoretical output is an error bound of the form ‖f(A)b − lan_k(f;A,b)‖₂ ≤ C · min_{deg(p)<ck} ‖f(A)b − p(A)b‖₂, where C depends on the rational function's denominator degree and the condition numbers of shifted matrices, and c is a degree-reduction factor approaching 1 as k grows.

## Method

The core result (Theorem 4) proves near-instance-optimality for degree-(p,q) rational functions r(x)=n(x)/m(x) with real poles outside the eigenvalue interval. The proof constructs a telescoping sum decomposition of the Lanczos-FA error: err_k(r) is expressed as a sum over j=1..q of terms involving the A_j-norm optimal approximation error for the partial rational function r_j(x)=n(x)/m_{1,j}(x), weighted by products of (T−z_i I)^{-1} operators. Each term is bounded by converting the A_j-norm optimality (which follows from the CG equivalence for 1/x) to a 2-norm bound via condition number factors, then relating polynomial approximation of r_j to that of the full r by a degree shift of (q−j). The resulting prefactor is C = q·κ(A_1)·…·κ(A_q) with degree reduction c = 1−(q−1)/k. For non-rational functions well-approximated by rationals (Lemma 5), the bound adds a uniform approximation error term ‖f−r‖_I. Theorems 6 and 7 extend to x^{±1/2} by bounding the Lanczos-FA error for the square root via the 1/x error (using Cauchy integral formula bounds from [10]) and then leveraging the fact that squaring a degree-k/2 polynomial approximation to x^{−1/2} yields a degree-k approximation to x^{−1}.

## Results

The main theoretical result (Theorem 4) proves that Lanczos-FA satisfies near-instance-optimality for rational functions r(x) = n(x)/m(x) of degree (p,q) with real poles outside the spectrum, with approximation factor C = q·κ(A₁)·κ(A₂)···κ(A_q) and iteration scaling c = 1−(q−1)/k. For the matrix inverse square root (Theorem 6), the bound is C = 3κ(A)/(√(πk)) with c = 1/2; for the matrix square root (Theorem 7), C = 3κ(A)²/k^(3/2) with c = 1/2. Experiments confirm that the observed optimality ratio (Lanczos-FA error / Krylov-optimal error) scales at least as Ω(√(qκ(A))) for A^(−q)b, and that the Theorem 4 bound is tighter than the standard Fact 3 bound for moderate-to-large k on rational approximations to exp(−x/10) and log(x).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the specialized sign-function methods of Jin & Sidford [44] ("rational" and "slanczos"), Lanczos-FA far outperforms both on all test problems from [44] (Figure 5), despite those methods having better theoretical guarantees; Lanczos-FA converges to comparable accuracy in a fraction of the matrix-vector products. Against Lanczos-OR [11], which is exactly optimal for rational functions in a weighted norm, the theoretical bound is actually tighter (√κ(r(A)) vs. q·κ(A₁)···κ(A_q)), but in practice Lanczos-OR's 2-norm optimality ratio grows as Ω(κ(A)^(q/2)) while Lanczos-FA's grows only as Ω(√(qκ(A))) (Figure 8 vs. Figure 3), making Lanczos-FA substantially better in the Euclidean norm for high-degree rational functions. Against the rational-approximation + CG pipeline of Pleiss et al. [59], Lanczos-FA applied directly to the original function f(x) (e.g., x^(−0.4)) converges at essentially the same rate as applying Lanczos-FA to a high-degree rational approximant (Figure 4), suggesting the direct approach is both simpler and avoids the fixed approximation error of a finite-degree rational. Overall, Lanczos-FA matches or beats the Krylov-optimal iterate (computed by direct least-squares) on all tested spectra and functions, with the error ratio never exceeding a small constant multiple of optimal.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the known exact A-norm optimality of Lanczos-FA for f(x)=1/x (where it reduces to Conjugate Gradient) to a broad class of rational functions with real poles outside the spectrum, establishing the first near-instance-optimality guarantee for Lanczos-FA beyond the linear-system case. It builds on the error bounds of Chen et al. (2022) for relating Lanczos-FA errors across different functions, and on the Lanczos-OR method of Chen et al. (2023) which achieves tighter guarantees but for a different algorithm. The paper departs from the standard Fact 3 bound (uniform polynomial approximation over the eigenvalue interval) by providing instance-dependent guarantees that account for the specific spectrum and starting vector, and it introduces a formal 'near instance optimality' framework (Definition 2) that unifies and generalizes prior special-case results for the matrix exponential and indefinite linear systems.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result provides a long-sought theoretical explanation for why the simplest, most general Krylov method (Lanczos-FA) routinely outperforms purpose-built algorithms for specific matrix functions by orders of magnitude in practice. The approximation factor depends on the rational function's denominator degree and the condition number but not on the iteration count k, making the guarantee increasingly tight as k grows. Key open questions include extending the analysis to rational functions with complex conjugate poles, Stieltjes/Markov functions (infinite-degree rational limits), tightening the exponential-in-q prefactor (empirically the ratio scales closer to √(qκ)), and establishing robustness under finite-precision arithmetic. For practitioners in ML, statistics, and data science who rely on matrix function approximation (Gaussian process regression, Hessian spectral analysis, Bayesian inference), this validates Lanczos-FA as a safe default choice.
