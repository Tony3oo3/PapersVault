## Problem

Text-to-image diffusion models such as Stable Diffusion produce incorrect attribute binding in complex multi-object prompts (e.g., generating a 'yellow banana' for 'blue banana'), yet prior work has not identified the specific mechanism in the CLIP text encoder responsible. This paper pinpoints two root causes: an attribute bias where objects inherently favor certain attributes in the text embedding space, and a contextual issue in padding embeddings where the causal attention mechanism causes different concepts to become entangled, leading to color leakage and object sticking.

## Input / Output

The method takes a natural language text prompt and produces a photorealistic RGB image through the standard Stable Diffusion pipeline. It uses Stanza's dependency parser to extract attribute-object pairs, then modifies only the object word embeddings within the CLIP text encoding before passing them to the U-Net. The entire manipulation is confined to the textual embedding space with no additional inputs, architectural changes, or training, adding approximately 2.9% runtime and 6.5% memory overhead over vanilla Stable Diffusion.

## Method

Magnet introduces positive and negative binding vectors applied to each object's word embedding to attract its target attribute and repel unrelated attributes, analogous to a magnet pulling and pushing. The vectors are estimated by comparing the object's word embedding across decontextualized prompts (with target attribute, with other attributes, and unconditional), using an unconditional concept as a pivot to avoid manual semantic contrast. An adaptive strength mechanism scales the manipulation based on the cosine similarity between the first [EOT] and last padding embeddings, applying stronger correction to unnatural concepts that suffer greater attribute bias. A neighbor strategy improves estimation accuracy by averaging binding vectors computed from K semantically similar objects rather than relying on the target object alone. The modified object embeddings are then fed to the standard U-Net denoising pipeline without any changes to the diffusion architecture or cross-attention layers.

## Results

On the CC-500 fine-grained benchmark, Magnet achieves 74.0% attribute alignment (manual), outperforming Stable Diffusion (59.1%), Structure Diffusion (63.9%), and Attend-and-Excite (66.2%). Magnet also improves object existence to 68.6% (vs. 65.8% for SD) and GroundingDINO detection to 76.5% (vs. 71.5% for SD), while adding only 2.9% runtime overhead and 6.5% memory compared to vanilla SD. On the coarser ABC-6K human evaluation, Magnet scores highest in image quality (26.57) and attribute disentanglement (27.14). FID on MS-COCO captions improves from 19.04 to 18.92 (SD V1.4) and from 19.76 to 19.20 (SD V2.1), confirming no quality degradation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Magnet decisively outperforms all three baselines on attribute alignment (74.0% vs. 59.1% SD, 63.9% Structure Diffusion, 66.2% Attend-and-Excite), which is the paper's primary concern. Against Structure Diffusion (the other training-free method), Magnet wins on detection (+4.4%), object existence (+4.6%), and attribute alignment (+10.1%) while being faster (6.81s vs. 7.94s). Against Attend-and-Excite (the optimization-based method), Magnet trades object existence (68.6% vs. 84.6%) and detection (76.5% vs. 84.3%) for a large gain in attribute alignment (+7.8%) and dramatically lower cost (2.9% vs. 102.4% runtime overhead; 6.5% vs. 155.7% memory overhead). On the coarse-grained human evaluation (Table 1), Magnet leads in image quality and attribute disentanglement on both ABC-6K and CC-500, though the 'no winner' category remains high (28–39%), reflecting the difficulty of these prompts. Overall, Magnet is positioned as the best efficiency–quality trade-off: it matches or exceeds training-free baselines on all metrics and approaches the optimization-based method on attribute binding at a fraction of the computational cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on Stable Diffusion's CLIP-based text encoding pipeline and departs from prior attribute-binding approaches (Structure Diffusion, Attend-and-Excite) that operate in the cross-attention or latent space. Its key conceptual departure is identifying the padding embeddings (repeated [EOT] tokens) as the locus of concept entanglement, and proposing to fix binding by manipulating only the object word embeddings in the textual space. The analysis also draws on VLM literature on compositional understanding and bags-of-words behavior to explain why the [EOT] embedding produces an inaccurate, entangled representation that propagates into padding tokens.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The mechanistic diagnosis of padding-embedding entanglement as the root cause of color leakage and object sticking provides a concrete, testable explanation for a long-standing T2I failure mode, and the training-free binding-vector fix is practically attractive (2.9% runtime, 6.5% memory overhead). However, Magnet still struggles with strongly attribute-biased objects (e.g., 'broccoli'), positional relations, and missing objects, and its adaptation to non-CLIP encoders (T5) is left as future work. An open question is whether the padding-embedding context issue persists in next-generation diffusion models that use longer or differently structured text conditioning.
