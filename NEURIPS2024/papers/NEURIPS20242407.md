## Problem

Context-based offline meta-RL (COMRL) has produced several effective algorithms for task representation learning (FOCAL, CORRO, CSRO), but these were developed independently without a unifying theoretical lens, leaving unclear why earlier methods are vulnerable to context shift from OOD behavior policies at test time. The paper identifies that the absence of a common information-theoretic objective prevents principled algorithm design and obscures the incremental robustness gains across the FOCAL→CORRO→CSRO lineage.

## Input / Output

The method consumes trajectory segments of length n (sequences of (s, a, r, s') tuples) from offline datasets as context, which are encoded into a fixed-dimensional latent task representation z. At test time, a single trajectory from the new task serves as the few-shot context, and the universal policy π_θ(a|s, z) produces actions conditioned on both the current state and the inferred task representation. The framework is model-agnostic, supporting both MLP-based actor-critic and Decision Transformer backbones where z is embedded as a prompt token in the sequence.

## Method

The central contribution is Theorem 2.3, which proves that FOCAL, CORRO, and CSRO objectives correspond to the upper bound I(Z;X), lower bound I(Z;X_t|X_b), and their convex interpolation of the mutual information I(Z;M), respectively. By decomposing I(Z;X) into primary causality (task-related transitions and rewards) and lesser causality (behavior-policy-induced state-actions), the framework explains why maximizing the upper bound captures spurious correlations while the lower bound is more robust to context shift. Two novel instantiations are proposed: UNICORN-SUP directly optimizes I(Z;M) via cross-entropy task classification with a concentration bound on finite-sample estimation error, and UNICORN-SS combines a FOCAL contrastive loss with a generative reconstruction loss in a convex combination, providing a tighter approximation of I(Z;M) without requiring explicit task labels.

## Results

On the six MuJoCo/MetaWorld benchmarks, UNICORN-SS achieves the best or near-best average return across both IID and OOD settings. On HalfCheetah-Dir, UNICORN-SS reaches 1307±26 (IID) and 1296±24 (OOD), substantially outperforming CSRO (1180±228 / 458±253) and FOCAL (1186±272 / 861±253). On Walker-Param, UNICORN-SS achieves 419±44 (IID) and 407±46 (OOD) versus CSRO's 310±58 / 279±65, demonstrating a clear advantage under OOD behavior policies. UNICORN-SS also shows the most stable training curves, avoiding the late-training performance collapse observed in FOCAL and CORRO on HalfCheetah-Dir and Walker-Param.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UNICORN-SS outperforms all context-based baselines (FOCAL, CORRO, CSRO) on most benchmarks, with the largest margins on OOD settings: on HalfCheetah-Dir OOD, UNICORN-SS (1296) exceeds FOCAL (861) by ~50% and CSRO (458) by ~183%. CSRO is the closest competitor, matching or slightly exceeding UNICORN on Ant-Dir IID (276 vs. 267) and Reach OOD (2801 vs. 2604), but UNICORN-SS dominates on Walker-Param (407 vs. 279 OOD) and HalfCheetah-Dir OOD. CORRO fails catastrophically on narrow-distribution datasets (e.g., −4±10 on Ant-Dir expert), while UNICORN-SS maintains 279±10. Gradient-based MACAW and transformer-based Prompt-DT are significantly weaker, with Prompt-DT collapsing to near-zero returns on Ant-Dir OOD (0±0) and HalfCheetah-Dir OOD (−25±9). The label-free variant UNICORN-SS-0 also outperforms the generative baseline BOReL on Ant-Dir (220±16 IID, 200±9 OOD vs. 206±18, 187±10).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies three successive context-based offline meta-RL algorithms—FOCAL (2021, distance metric learning), CORRO (2022, InfoNCE contrastive loss), and CSRO (2023, FOCAL + CLUB regularization)—under a single information-theoretic framework, proving they all optimize different bounds (upper, lower, and convex interpolation) of the mutual information I(Z; M) between the task variable and its latent representation. The causal decomposition of the context into behavior-related (s,a) and task-related (s',r) components provides the mechanism explaining why each successive method improved OOD robustness: by progressively trading off spurious correlation with the behavior policy against causal correlation with the MDP dynamics. The two new instantiations (supervised cross-entropy and self-supervised reconstruction + contrastive) depart from prior work by directly targeting I(Z;M) rather than its bounds, and by demonstrating model-agnostic applicability to both MLP and Decision Transformer backbones.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The theoretical unification provides a principled design space for future COMRL algorithms—any method optimizing a tighter bound or better approximation of I(Z;M) is guaranteed to improve task representation quality—shifting the field from ad-hoc loss engineering to information-theoretic optimization. The framework's model-agnostic nature and the demonstrated compatibility with Decision Transformers position it as a potential offline pre-training paradigm for RL foundation models, though this remains speculative at the current scale (≤40 tasks, ~450k transitions). Key open questions include: whether the decoupling assumption between representation learning and policy optimization holds at scale, how the framework extends to online settings where the static M and X assumptions break, and whether the supervised variant's concentration bound (Theorem 2.4) becomes tight enough with larger task sets to close the gap with the self-supervised variant.
