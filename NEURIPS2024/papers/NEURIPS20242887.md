## Problem

Prior coreset constructions for logistic regression have size depending on a data complexity measure μ_y(X), but it was unclear whether this dependency is fundamental to any data structure approximating logistic loss or merely an artifact of the mergeable coreset framework. Additionally, μ_y(X) was conjectured to be computationally hard to evaluate, limiting its practical utility in guiding coreset design. This paper addresses both gaps by proving general space lower bounds and providing an exact polynomial-time algorithm for μ_y(X).

## Input / Output

The theoretical results take a data matrix X ∈ R^{n×d} with binary labels y ∈ {-1,1}^n and a parameter vector β ∈ R^d, and produce space complexity lower bounds (in bits) for any data structure achieving ε-relative error approximation to the logistic loss. The computational algorithm takes the same (X, y) pair and outputs the exact value of μ_y(X) by solving a linear program with 2n variables and 4n constraints, where the constraint matrix involves the orthogonal projection onto Range(D_y X). Entries of X are assumed to be specifiable in O(log nd) bits.

## Method

The lower bound proofs proceed by a two-step reduction: first, a relative error approximation to logistic loss can simulate a relative error approximation to ReLU loss (via scaling β by a large constant t and exploiting the asymptotic equivalence of (1/t)log(1+e^{tr}) and max{0,r}); second, a ReLU loss approximation yields an ℓ₁-subspace embedding, whose space complexity is lower bounded via the INDEX problem in communication complexity. This yields Ω̃(d/ε²) space in the constant-μ regime and Ω̃(d·μ_y(X)) space for constant ε. For computing μ_y(X), the paper formulates it as a linear program by decomposing z = D_y Xβ into positive and negative parts (z_+, z_-), constraining z to lie in Range(D_y X) via an orthogonal projection, and minimizing 1^T(z_+ − z_-) subject to ‖z‖₁ ≤ C — a pure LP with 2n variables and 4n constraints that refutes the prior hardness conjecture.

## Results

The paper's primary contributions are theoretical: it proves a Õ(d/ε²) space lower bound for any data structure approximating logistic loss to ε-relative error when μ_y(X) = O(1) (Theorem 1), and a Õ(d·μ_y(X)) lower bound for constant ε (Theorem 3). Experimentally, the paper's exact LP formulation (Theorem 4) computes μ_y(X) precisely, while the sketching-based approximation of Munteanu et al. [10] yields upper and lower bounds that can be very loose. On the KDDCup dataset (100,654 points, 33 features), the exact computation on a 16,384-point subsample is used as a proxy for the full dataset, and the sketching-based bounds are shown to be substantially wider than the true value.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares its exact linear-programming computation of μ_y(X) (Theorem 4) against the sketching-based upper and lower bounds proposed by Munteanu et al. [10] (NeurIPS 2018). On both synthetic Gaussian data (n=10,000, d=100) and the KDDCup dataset, the exact LP values on sketched matrices closely track the true μ_y(X) of the full data, while the Munteanu et al. bounds — which guarantee μ_y(X) ∈ [t, t·O(d log d)] with probability 1−δ — can be very loose, with the upper bound potentially orders of magnitude above the true value. The paper notes that the upper bound is conservative even in the best case since the guarantees of Munteanu et al. hold only up to a constant factor. This demonstrates that prior experimental evaluations of coreset constructions, which relied on these loose estimates, may have overestimated the complexity measure and thus the required coreset size.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the coreset and sketching literature for logistic regression initiated by Munteanu et al. [10] and advanced by Mai et al. [7] and Munteanu and Omlor [9], which established upper bounds on coreset size depending on the classification complexity measure μ_y(X). The lower bound technique builds on the INDEX-problem reduction framework from Li et al. [6] for ℓ₁-subspace embeddings, adapted here through a novel reduction from logistic loss to ReLU loss (Lemma 2.1). It departs from Woodruff and Yasuda [17], who proved a similar μ_y(X) lower bound but only for mergeable coresets, by showing the dependency is fundamental to any data structure providing relative-error approximation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The results establish that existing coreset constructions are essentially optimal in the constant-complexity regime and that the μ_y(X) factor cannot be eliminated by any compression scheme, providing a clear ceiling for future work. The LP formulation for computing μ_y(X) exactly (refuting a prior conjecture of hardness) is a practical tool that enables more informed coreset design and empirical evaluation. Open questions include closing the remaining d-factor gap between upper and lower bounds, extending Theorem 1 to the 'for-each' guarantee, and understanding whether matrix sparsification can beat coreset-based compression by a d factor.
