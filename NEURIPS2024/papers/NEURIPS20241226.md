## Problem

Existing generative models for crystal structures either require brute-force unconditional sampling or a pre-specified chemical formula at inference time, making it difficult for domain experts to explore novel compositions from high-level descriptions. No end-to-end system exists that accepts natural language instructions (e.g., 'a stable chalcogenide with atom ratio 1:1:2 not in ICSD') and produces physically viable crystal structures, largely because no paired language-to-structure dataset exists and the task spans discrete symbolic knowledge to continuous 3D coordinates.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241226/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes a free-form natural language instruction describing desired crystal properties (composition constraints, space group, stability, novelty) and produces a full crystal structure encoded as an A×4 tensor (x, y, z fractional coordinates plus continuous atom number for each atom) together with a 2×3 lattice parameter vector. Intermediate outputs include ranked chemical formulae and space groups generated by the LLM, and the final structure is selected from W×L candidates via a GNN-predicted formation energy score, with validity verified by pymatgen parsing and SMACT charge-balance checks.

## Method

GenMS factorizes the language-to-structure generator as π = π_hi ∘ π_lo, where π_hi is a retrieval-augmented LLM that proposes candidate chemical formulae and space groups from the language instruction, and π_lo is a diffusion model operating on a novel compact crystal representation (A×4 continuous tensor with order-invariant self-attention and U-Net-style skip connections, avoiding the sparsity overhead of voxel or graph encodings). A pretrained GNN (from Merchant et al., Nature 2023) serves as the low-level heuristic R_lo, predicting formation energy to rank sampled structures. The full pipeline is formulated as a multi-objective optimization (Eq. 1) solved by a best-first tree search (Algorithm 1) that branches over H formulae and L structures per formula, with high-level pruning via regex/uniqueness/compliance checkers and LLM-based ranking. The compact representation improves DFT convergence from 62% (UniMat) to 93%, and best-of-N sampling with N=10 yields lower-energy structures for over 80% of compositions.

## Results

GenMS achieves 100% CIF validity and 95–100% structural validity across perovskite, pyrochlore, and spinel families, with match rates of 93%, 86%, and 89% respectively—far exceeding the best LLM prompting baseline (36% match rate for perovskite at 25-shot). DFT-verified formation energies are −1.32 eV/atom (perovskite), −2.56 eV/atom (pyrochlore), and −1.77 eV/atom (spinel), substantially lower than any prompting baseline. Uniqueness reaches 90% for perovskite but drops to 44–49% for pyrochlore and spinel, indicating room for improvement in novel composition discovery.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against UniMat [6] (periodic-table-shaped tensor diffusion), GenMS's compact crystal representation achieves a DFT convergence rate of 0.93 versus 0.62 and a lower mean formation energy of −0.49 ± 0.03 eV/atom versus −0.40 ± 0.06 eV/atom, a 50% improvement in convergence. Against CrystalLM [9] (autoregressive LLM fine-tuned to generate CIF files), GenMS's diffusion model attains a 93% success rate versus 86–87% for small/large LLMs, and a 48% match rate to unseen holdout structures versus 26–37%. In the end-to-end language-to-structure evaluation (Table 1), GenMS dramatically outperforms all LLM CIF-prompting baselines: structural validity jumps from 4–66% (best prompting) to 95–100%, match rate from 0–36% to 86–93%, and DFT formation energy is consistently lower. The only metric where GenMS does not clearly dominate is uniqueness for pyrochlore and spinel (44–49%), where 25-shot prompting reaches 45–51%, suggesting the LLM's compositional creativity is competitive even without the diffusion pipeline.

## Relation to Prior Work

GenMS draws on the hierarchical generation paradigm from text-to-image and text-to-video models (latent diffusion, cascaded diffusion), adapting the high-level LLM → low-level diffusion pipeline to the crystal structure domain. For the structure generation component, it builds on diffusion-based crystal generators (UniMat, CDVAE, MatterGen) but departs from their reliance on formula conditioning or brute-force unconditional sampling by introducing a compact A×4 tensor representation that repurposes the 3D U-Net architecture. The property-prediction GNN is taken directly from Merchant et al. (Nature 2023), and the overall search-and-select framework borrows from hierarchical planning in robotics and autonomous driving, where LLMs propose high-level plans verified by low-level simulators.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GenMS establishes the first viable end-to-end pipeline from free-form natural language to physically plausible crystal structures, opening a natural-language interface for materials discovery that could complement existing databases. Key open questions remain: the framework struggles with complex structures (Mxenes, Kagome lattices), synthesizability is not assessed, and effectiveness for target-property design (e.g., specific band gaps or ionic conductivities) is unverified. The compact crystal representation's 50% improvement in DFT convergence over UniMat is a practical contribution likely to be adopted independently of the full GenMS pipeline.
