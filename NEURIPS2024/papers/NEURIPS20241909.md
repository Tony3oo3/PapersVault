## Problem

Existing multi-modal fusion methods on SSMs either aggregate all modalities into a single feature stream (losing intra-modal propagation) or rely on alignment supervision that is difficult to obtain. Mamba's hardware-aware parallelism design, which relies on precomputing a global convolution kernel from a single state chain, makes it non-trivial to introduce inter-modal state coupling without sacrificing the linear-time parallel computation that gives Mamba its efficiency advantage over Transformers.

## Input / Output

The model consumes pre-extracted sequential features from multiple modalities simultaneously: for sentiment analysis, 768-dim BERT word embeddings, 74-dim COVAREP acoustic frames, and 35-dim facial action unit vectors; for movie classification, image and text description features; for cancer subtype prediction, mRNA, DNA methylation, and miRNA expression vectors. All modalities are projected to a shared state dimension (dstate=64) before coupling. The model outputs a scalar sentiment score (regression) or a discrete class label (classification), with the final layer applying pooling over the coupled state sequences.

## Method

Coupled Mamba maintains M independent state chains (one per modality) but couples them at each time step: the state transition for modality m is computed as h_t^m = S_m · Σ_m h_{t-1}^m + B̄_m x_t^m, where S_m is a modality-specific state transition matrix applied to the summed hidden states of all modalities. This summation-before-transition design (inspired by Coupled Hidden Markov Models) avoids the parameter explosion of full pairwise transition matrices while still enabling inter-modal information flow. To preserve Mamba's parallelism, the authors derive a closed-form global convolution kernel K̄ = (CP^0, CP^1, …, CP^t) where P = Σ_m S_m, reducing the coupled recurrence to a single convolution over the aggregated input U_t = Σ_m B̄_m x_t^m. Each layer contains M Coupled Mamba blocks with selective scanning (input-dependent Δ, B, C), SiLU-gated residual connections, and a 1D convolution for local context, stacked over 3 layers with hidden dimension 128 and expansion factor 2.

## Results

On CMU-MOSEI (the primary benchmark), Coupled Mamba achieves MAE 0.547, Corr 0.756, Acc-2 85.6%, and F1-Score 85.5% under the unaligned setting, outperforming the strongest baselines MISA (F1 85.3%) and TETFN (F1 85.2%). On the Chinese datasets, it gains 0.9% F1-Score over TETFN on CH-SIMS (81.3% vs. 80.2%) and 2.3% over MFN on CH-SIMSV2 (83.5% vs. 81.2%). Cross-domain results on BRCA (MacroF1 85.4% vs. 83.9% for MM-Dynamics) and MM-IMDB (MicroF1 62.41% vs. 60.35% for DynMM) confirm generalizability, while inference is 49% faster and GPU memory usage drops by 83.7% compared to Transformer-based fusion.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Coupled Mamba outperforms all 30 baselines across every evaluated dataset. On CMU-MOSEI, it surpasses the strongest unaligned baselines TETFN (F1 85.2%) and IMDer3 (F1 85.1%) by 0.3–0.4% F1-Score, and also beats the aligned MISA (F1 85.3%) and MAG-BERT (F1 85.1%) despite operating without alignment supervision. On CH-SIMS, it leads TETFN by 1.1% F1-Score (81.3% vs. 80.2%) and 0.6% Acc-2, while on CH-SIMSV2 it improves over MFN by 2.3% F1-Score (83.5% vs. 81.2%) and 2.3% Acc-2. In cross-domain settings, Coupled Mamba beats MM-Dynamics on BRCA by 0.6% MacroF1 (85.4% vs. 83.9%) and DynMM on MM-IMDB by 2.06% MicroF1 (62.41% vs. 60.35%). The ablation against cross-attention fusion (Table 8) shows a 1.0% F1-Score gap (85.5% vs. 84.5%), confirming that the coupled state-space mechanism is the key driver rather than generic attention-based fusion.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Mamba's selective state space model to the multi-modal setting by coupling the hidden state chains of different modalities, drawing a direct conceptual parallel to Coupled Hidden Markov Models (CHMM) where multiple Markov chains interact at each time step. It departs from the dominant multi-modal fusion paradigm of aggregating modality-specific features into a single stream (early/mid fusion) or exchanging information via cross-attention, instead preserving independent intra-modal state propagation while introducing inter-modal state dependencies. The key technical contribution is deriving a global convolution kernel for the coupled system that preserves Mamba's hardware-aware parallelism, which is non-trivial once multi-modal state information is introduced into the selective scanning recurrence.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine gap: multi-modal fusion on state space models was largely unexplored, and the naive aggregation approach loses intra-modal dynamics. The 49% inference speedup and 83.7% GPU memory reduction over Transformer-based fusion are practically significant for deployment, though these gains are largely inherited from the SSM backbone rather than the coupling mechanism itself. Limitations include the requirement that all modalities share the same state dimension (addressed only via projection layers), validation primarily on sentiment analysis and a few classification benchmarks, and modest accuracy gains (0.4–2.3% F1) over strong baselines. Open questions include whether the coupling scheme scales gracefully to more than three modalities, whether it benefits from longer sequences where SSMs have their clearest advantage, and whether the shared-summation approximation (Eq. 6) loses information compared to the full per-pair transition formulation (Eq. 5) in more complex multi-modal settings.
