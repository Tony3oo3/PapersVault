## Problem

The construction of self-attention in transformers relies on heuristics and empirical design, lacking a principled theoretical framework that explains what the mechanism actually computes. This gap leaves the vulnerability of attention to data corruption and adversarial perturbation unaddressed at a structural level, since no existing theory identifies the underlying geometric operation that attention performs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243214/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on sequences of N feature vectors X ∈ R^{N×D_x} (image patches for ViT, token embeddings for language models), which are linearly projected into query, key, and value matrices. In RPC-Attention, the key matrix K ∈ R^{N×D} serves as the corrupted measurement matrix in a Principal Component Pursuit problem, and the attention output H is set to the recovered low-rank matrix L from the PAP algorithm rather than the standard softmax(QK^T/√D)V product. The final model outputs class labels (ImageNet-1K), segmentation masks (ADE20K), or next-token predictions (WikiText-103) as in standard supervised settings.

## Method

The paper derives softmax self-attention from kernel PCA by showing that attention outputs h_i are projections of query vectors q_i onto D_v principal component axes of the key matrix K in an infinite-dimensional feature space induced by the softmax kernel k(x,y)=exp(x^T y/√D). It further proves that the learned value matrix V captures the eigenvectors of the centered Gram matrix of the key vectors, validated empirically on trained ViT-tiny models. Building on this, RPC-Attention replaces the singular value thresholding step in the PCP ADMM solver with a softmax attention operator (justified by the known low-rank/over-smoothing property of attention outputs), yielding the Principal Attention Pursuit (PAP) algorithm that iteratively separates the key matrix into a clean low-rank component and a sparse corruption component. The resulting robust attention is applied either only in the first layer (4–6 iterations) or across all layers (2 iterations) to balance robustness gains against computational cost.

## Results

On clean ImageNet-1K, RPC-SymViT (6 PAP iterations in layer 1) achieves 71.49% top-1 accuracy, improving over the SymViT baseline (70.44%) by over 1 point. On robustness benchmarks, RPC-SymViT gains +1.05% on ImageNet-R (30.03% vs. 28.98%), +0.82% on ImageNet-A (7.33% vs. 6.51%), reduces mean corruption error on ImageNet-C from 74.75 to 73.03, and improves AUPR on ImageNet-O from 17.43 to 20.29. On WikiText-103, RPC-Symlm reduces validation perplexity from 38.37 to 37.38 and test perplexity from 36.36 to 35.26. On ADE20K segmentation, RPC-SymViT improves mean accuracy from 44.27% to 45.27% (clean) and from 14.85% to 16.44% (corrupted).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares RPC-Attention against two baselines: SymViT (a ViT-tiny with standard symmetric softmax attention) for vision tasks and Symlm (a standard transformer language model with symmetric attention) for language modeling. RPC-SymViT consistently outperforms SymViT across all robustness benchmarks: +1.05% top-1 on clean ImageNet-1K, +1.05% on ImageNet-R, +0.82% on ImageNet-A, −1.72 mCE on ImageNet-C, and +2.86 AUPR on ImageNet-O. Under adversarial attacks, the gains are even more pronounced: RPC-SymViT (2iter/all-layer) improves FGSM top-1 from 23.38% to 29.20% (+5.82%), SPSA from 47.94% to 51.01% (+3.07%), and PGD from 4.98% to 6.12% (+1.14%). On WikiText-103, RPC-Symlm reduces validation perplexity by 0.99 (38.37→37.38) and test perplexity by 1.10 (36.36→35.26), with similar gains under word-swap attack (42.90→41.83 valid PPL). On ADE20K segmentation, RPC-SymViT improves mean accuracy by 0.94–1.59 points and mean IoU by 0.24–1.13 points over SymViT on both clean and corrupted data. The paper also validates that the Scaled Attention variant (a reparameterization from Remark 3) outperforms standard asymmetric softmax attention, reaching 73.02% vs. 72.18% top-1 on ImageNet-1K, further supporting the kernel PCA interpretation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves a new theoretical lineage by deriving softmax self-attention from kernel PCA, showing that attention outputs are projections of query vectors onto principal component axes of the key matrix in an infinite-dimensional feature space. While prior work has interpreted attention through kernel smoothers [81], ODE/PDE dynamics [50, 51], probabilistic GMMs [52, 23], and asymmetric kernel SVD [15], none had revealed the exact eigenvector structure of the value matrix V. The practical robustness component builds directly on Principal Component Pursuit (PCP) [11] and its ADMM solver [41, 91], replacing the singular value thresholding step with a softmax attention operator to exploit the known low-rank structure of attention outputs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The kernel PCA derivation provides the first principled, non-heuristic explanation for why the value matrix in self-attention takes its particular form, and the empirical validation that trained V converges to eigenvectors of the centered Gram matrix of keys is a concrete, testable prediction of the theory. RPC-Attention offers a drop-in robustness improvement with modest computational overhead, though the iterative PAP algorithm remains a practical limitation that the authors mitigate by restricting it to the first layer. Key open questions include a formal convergence proof for multi-layer multi-head transformers under SGD, extending the framework to explain cross-attention and multi-layer interactions, and whether the robustness gains scale to larger models and more diverse tasks.
