## Problem

Scaling vision resolution in online video LLMs is prohibitively expensive: processing every frame with dense spatial tokens causes quadratic growth in computation and memory over long streaming videos. Existing token-reduction strategies (pruning, merging, Q-Former) either discard visual information or are incompatible with the causal, frame-by-frame processing required for real-time streaming, leaving a gap between spatial understanding quality and computational feasibility.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243487/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes streaming video at 2 FPS, where each frame is encoded by SigLIP-L/16 into 10 tokens (1 CLS + 9 average-pooled spatial tokens) and projected via a 2-layer MLP before interleaving with text tokens in a causal sequence. Outputs are temporally aligned text responses (narrations, action forecasts, keystep labels, task summaries) generated at the current frame, with an explicit EOS mechanism that keeps the model silent when no response is warranted. Because the model processes frames causally, it must retain the full KV cache of all prior vision and language tokens, making memory a first-order constraint.

## Method

VideoLLM-MoD introduces a Mixture-of-Depths mechanism for vision tokens: a lightweight LayerExpert (a single linear projection) scores each vision token's importance within its frame, and only the top-k tokens (keep ratio r=0.2 by default) proceed through self-attention and FFN in that layer, while the remaining 80% bypass via residual connection. LayerExpert is inserted every other decoder layer (interleaved strategy) of a Llama-3-8B backbone fine-tuned with LoRA (rank 128, scale 256), and the model is trained with a combined language-modeling loss and a streaming EOS loss that enforces silence on non-responsive frames. The key insight is that skipping computation for redundant vision tokens in mid-to-deep layers preserves the full token sequence in context (unlike token dropping or merging), so the model retains complete visual history while reducing FLOPs to ~0.6× the full-computation baseline and KV-cache memory to support 1.7× longer context at inference.

## Results

On the Ego4D Narration Stream benchmark, VideoLLM-MoD achieves LM-PPL 2.41, TimeDiff 2.04, Fluency 45.2%, and LM-Correctness 48.9% using only 30.74T FLOPs, matching the Full-computation baseline (48.29T FLOPs) while reducing training FLOPs by ~42% and training time from 24hrs to 14hrs (~1.7× speedup). On the EgoExo4D Fine-grained Keystep Recognition benchmark, VideoLLM-MoD achieves 44.85% accuracy (val v1), substantially outperforming VideoLLM-online (40.53%) and VI Encoder (40.23%). On COIN, the method reaches 63.4% step recognition and 92.8% task recognition, surpassing both VideoLLM-online and the Full-computation baseline. On Video-MME, VideoLLM-MoD achieves 49.2% overall accuracy with only 22hrs training, outperforming VideoLLaMA2 (47.9%, 65hrs) and Chat-UniVi (40.6%, 53hrs).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Full-computation baseline (same architecture without LayerExpert), VideoLLM-MoD achieves nearly identical performance (Fluency 45.2% vs 45.3%, LM-Correctness 48.9% vs 49.0%) while reducing FLOPs from 48.29T to 30.74T (~42% savings) and training time from 24hrs to 14hrs. Compared to VideoLLM-online [9], which uses only 1 CLS token per frame, VideoLLM-MoD with 10 tokens/frame (1+3×3) significantly outperforms on EgoExo4D keystep recognition (44.85% vs 40.53%) and COIN step recognition (63.4% vs 62.5%), confirming that expanded spatial resolution benefits fine-grained understanding. Against the naive efficiency baselines EarlyExit and LayerSkip, VideoLLM-MoD substantially outperforms (Fluency 45.2% vs 41.3%/42.0%), demonstrating that selective per-token routing is critical versus uniform layer skipping. On general video QA benchmarks, VideoLLM-MoD surpasses LLaMA-VID [20] by large margins (MSVD-QA: 78.5% vs 69.7%; MSRVTT-QA: 65.3% vs 57.7%; ActivityNet-QA: 53.4% vs 47.4%) while using only 5.8 TFLOPs vs 9.8 TFLOPs, though it trails slightly on image benchmarks (GQA: 62.8 vs 64.3). On Ego4D LTA, VideoLLM-MoD (Action ED@20: 0.884) is competitive with but slightly below Palm (0.886) and AntGPT (0.877), which leverage EgoVLP pretrained features and cascading enhancement methods rather than end-to-end training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends VideoLLM-online's streaming video LLM framework by addressing its key limitation: the use of a single CLS token per frame, which restricts spatial understanding. The core mechanism is adapted from Mixture-of-Depths (MoD) for language tokens [Raposo et al., 2024], but repurposed to selectively skip computation for redundant *vision* tokens in specific transformer layers via a learnable LayerExpert router. Unlike prior efficiency approaches that reduce the number of vision tokens (pruning, merging, Q-Former compression) or add cross-attention overhead (CogAgent, LLaMA-VID), VideoLLM-MoD preserves the full token sequence in context while reducing per-token FLOPs, maintaining causal context integrity essential for streaming.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for real-time video applications (AR glasses, autonomous driving, always-on assistants) where quadratic scaling of vision tokens with video duration is prohibitive. The finding that selective layer-skipping can *improve* rather than merely maintain performance suggests sparsity acts as an implicit regularizer, an insight that may generalize to other multimodal settings. However, the paper is limited to egocentric and instructional video domains, and the performance margins over the full-computation baseline are small (≤0.1–0.2% on most metrics), leaving open whether the 'free lunch' property holds at larger scales or in exo-centric settings. The approach also assumes a fixed keep ratio and insertion pattern, and does not explore adaptive per-frame or per-query routing.
