## Problem

Existing fairness toolkits (AIF360, Fairlearn) are limited to tabular data, enforce fairness on training data where overfitting makes error-rate metrics trivially satisfied, and do not allow joint optimization of a user-specified performance objective alongside fairness constraints. This paper addresses these gaps by providing a single, highly customizable post-processing method that works across tabular, NLP, and computer vision domains while enforcing fairness on held-out validation data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242987/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes confidence scores from any pretrained classifier (sklearn, XGBoost, PyTorch, BERT, ResNet-50), a labeled validation set, and group membership labels that may be inferred via an auxiliary classifier when unavailable at test time. For deep networks, it takes outputs from a two-headed model (task classifier + group predictor) and produces a merged single-head fair network with identical architecture to the original. The output includes per-group decision thresholds, fair binary predictions, and a Pareto frontier of objective-vs-fairness trade-offs that can be visualized and queried.

## Method

OxonFair performs per-group thresholding via an efficient grid search that exploits cumulative sums of positives and negatives sorted by classifier score within each group, reducing complexity from O(nT^k) to O(T^k + n log n) where T is the number of thresholds and k the number of groups. It jointly optimizes a user-specified performance objective (accuracy, F1, balanced accuracy, custom utility) subject to a fairness constraint (any measure expressible as a function of per-group TP, FP, FN, TN) on validation data, selecting the best point on the resulting Pareto frontier. For deep networks, a two-headed architecture (shared backbone with separate linear heads for classification and group prediction) is trained, optimal thresholds are found on validation data, and the heads are merged into a single linear layer via the identity f(x) − t·g(x) = (w_f − t·w_g)·B(x) + (b_f − t·b_g), yielding a deployable fair model with no inference-time group information required.

## Results

On CelebA (gender-independent attributes), OxonFair DEO achieves 92.75% accuracy and 3.21% DEO, outperforming all fair baselines on DEO (next best: FairMixup at 3.58%) while remaining within 0.32% of the unconstrained ERM multitask accuracy (93.07%). On the Adult tabular dataset, OxonFair matches Fairlearn's test accuracy (81% for Decision Tree) while reducing DEO from 8.8% to 1.1%. On the Multilingual Twitter hate speech dataset (gender), OxonFair*F1 achieves 47.5% F1 and 5.59% DEO, improving F1 over the BERT baseline (40.8%) while reducing DEO from 21.4% to 5.59%. On Jigsaw (religion, 3 groups), OxonFair*F1 achieves 0.84% DEO versus 7.33% for the baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Fairlearn's reductions method on tabular data, OxonFair achieves comparable accuracy (81% vs 81% for Decision Tree on Adult) while reducing test DEO from 8.8% to 1.1%, primarily because OxonFair enforces fairness on held-out validation data rather than overfitting training data. On CelebA, OxonFair DEO (3.21% DEO) outperforms all in-processing fair methods including Domain Independent (7.78%), Regularized FNNC (3.77%), and FairMixup (3.58%), while maintaining accuracy within 0.32% of the unconstrained ERM multitask baseline. On NLP (Twitter gender), OxonFair*F1 improves F1 over the BERT baseline (47.5% vs 40.8%) while reducing DEO from 21.4% to 5.59%, outperforming CDA (16.0%), DP regularization (17.9%), EO regularization (13.2%), Dropout (13.8%), and Rebalance (19.1%) on DEO. For minimax fairness on Adult, OxonFair achieves 80.5% min-group accuracy on test versus 77.9% for the Abernethy et al. [67] minimax training method, while maintaining 84.6% overall accuracy. Against Fairret [56] on tabular data, OxonFair shows superior accuracy/fairness trade-offs, attributed to Fairret's relaxed constraints and train-test mismatch. Against Cruz & Hardt [57] (specialist equalized odds solver), OxonFair can be extended to match their randomized-threshold approach while additionally supporting deterministic thresholds and inferred group membership, which [57] does not.

## Relation to Prior Work

OxonFair extends the classical per-group thresholding approach (Kamiran et al. 2013; Feldman et al. 2015; Hardt et al. 2016) by making it maximally expressive: it can optimize any measure decomposable into TP/FP/FN/TN, jointly with a user-specified performance objective, rather than being locked to a fixed fairness criterion. It builds directly on Lohaus et al. (NeurIPS 2022), who showed that two neural network heads (classifier + group predictor) can be merged into a single fair network, but generalizes that result from demographic parity alone to all supported fairness measures. A key conceptual departure from existing toolkits (AIF360, Fairlearn) is enforcing fairness constraints on held-out validation data rather than training data, motivated by Zietlow et al. (2022) showing that overfitting in high-capacity models renders training-set fairness constraints vacuous.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first fairness toolkit that supports NLP and computer vision alongside tabular data, filling a gap where AIF360 and Fairlearn simply do not operate. The validation-data enforcement and joint objective optimization address a practical failure mode (overfitting) that silently breaks existing methods, and the expressiveness (14+ fairness definitions, arbitrary objectives, levelling-up, minimax, utility) gives domain experts and policymakers direct control over which harms to address. Limitations include exponential complexity in the number of groups, known suboptimality for equalized odds, and dependence on externally assigned group labels that may not reflect self-identification. Open questions include whether the approach scales beyond a handful of groups, how to handle individual fairness, and how to address the fundamental data-scarcity problem for underrepresented groups that no post-processing method can fix.
