## Problem

NeRF-based implicit methods achieve high-quality surface reconstruction but require extensive training and rendering time, making them impractical for large-scale open scenes. Conversely, 3D Gaussian Splatting enables real-time rendering but represents scenes as discrete Gaussian primitives, leading to excessive memory consumption and rough, fragmented surfaces in sparse regions. The paper addresses the gap of achieving continuous, high-fidelity surface reconstruction with the rendering speed of 3DGS without the memory overhead of explicit Gaussian management.

## Input / Output

The method takes multi-view RGB images and a downsampled SfM point cloud as input, where the point cloud is voxelized into a sparse grid (voxel size 0.01) that seeds up to 10 Gaussian primitives per voxel. Outputs include novel view rendered images, a continuous SDF field extracted via a logistic mapping from the opacity CDF, and a 3D mesh generated through Marching Cubes or Marching Tetrahedra. Depth maps and normal maps are also produced for visualization. Notably, the SDF is defined per-ray (line-of-sight-based) rather than as a global 3D field, which is a deliberate trade-off to maintain compatibility with the 3DGS rasterization pipeline.

## Method

GVKF replaces the discrete opacity values in standard 3DGS alpha blending with 1D Gaussian kernel functions derived from each 3D Gaussian via Ray-Gaussian Intersection, producing a continuous opacity density field ρ(t) along each ray through kernel regression. Gaussian primitives are managed by a sparse voxel grid that dynamically subdivides (octree, max depth 3) based on accumulated gradient thresholds, and their attributes (position, opacity, rotation, scale, color) are decoded from a shared 32-dim feature vector via four MLPs rather than stored as explicit tensors. The rendering equation is modified so that each Gaussian contributes a kernel K_i(t−t_i) with a piecewise form (Gaussian decay before the intersection point, constant 1 after), preserving the alpha-blending structure while enabling continuous scene representation. For surface extraction, the paper proves that the peak of the opacity PDF does not coincide with the true surface, derives a transcendental equation to locate the surface offset u_0, and then maps the opacity CDF Φ(t) to SDF D(t) via an inverse logistic function with a smoothness parameter μ.

## Results

On the Waymo Open Scene dataset, GVKF achieves the highest PSNR of 30.24 dB for novel view synthesis, surpassing 2DGS (28.51), 3DGS (27.99), and StreetSurf (27.12), while attaining a Chamfer Distance of 1.57 (second only to StreetSurf's 1.02 and NeuS's 0.76). GVKF uses only 30 MB of storage and 14 GB GPU memory, a 7–18× reduction over competing 3DGS-based methods (228–540 MB storage, 22–33 GB GPU). On Tanks and Temples, GVKF achieves a mean F1 of 0.36, outperforming all explicit baselines (2DGS: 0.30, SuGaR: 0.19, 3DGS: 0.09) and approaching implicit methods (NeuS: 0.38, Neuralangelo: 0.50). On Mip-NeRF 360 outdoor scenes, GVKF leads all methods in PSNR (25.47), SSIM (0.757), and LPIPS (0.240).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GVKF outperforms all explicit 3DGS-based methods (3DGS, SuGaR, 2DGS) on both PSNR and Chamfer Distance on Waymo, with a 1.7 dB PSNR gain over 2DGS (30.24 vs. 28.51) and a 2.0× improvement in C-D over 3DGS (1.57 vs. 3.57). Against implicit NeRF-based methods, GVKF achieves comparable geometric accuracy (C-D 1.57 vs. StreetSurf 1.02, NeuS 0.76) while delivering 32 FPS real-time rendering versus ~0.1 FPS for all NeRF-based baselines, and uses 5–18× less storage. On Tanks and Temples, GVKF (mean F1 0.36) beats all explicit methods—2DGS (0.30), SuGaR (0.19), 3DGS (0.09)—and is within 0.02 of NeuS (0.38) and Geo-NeuS (0.35), though it trails Neuralangelo (0.50) which benefits from a global SDF fitting approach. On Mip-NeRF 360 outdoor scenes, GVKF leads every NVS metric over 2DGS (PSNR 25.47 vs. 24.33), 3DGS (24.24), and Mip-NeRF 360 (24.47). Compared to the concurrent GOF method, GVKF achieves higher PSNR (25.47 vs. 24.53) with 10× less storage (68 MB vs. 649 MB), though GOF attains slightly better F1 scores on Tanks and Temples (0.46 vs. 0.36) due to its iterative isosurface extraction.

## Relation to Prior Work

GVKF sits at the intersection of two lines of work: 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS, 2DGS, SuGaR) for fast explicit rendering, and NeRF-based implicit surface reconstruction (NeuS, StreetSurf, Neuralangelo) for high-quality geometry. The key conceptual departure is a mathematical reframing: the paper shows that 3DGS alpha blending is equivalent to evaluating 'collapsed' (zero-width) kernel functions, and by replacing them with proper Gaussian kernel functions via ray-Gaussian intersection, one recovers a continuous opacity density field without resorting to dense volume rendering. The voxel-based implicit management of Gaussians (MLP-decoded from sparse octree cells) draws directly from Scaffold-GS and Octree-GS, but is repurposed here to enable the kernel regression interpretation rather than merely organizing splats for rendering.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 87% storage reduction (30 MB vs. 230 MB for 3DGS on Waymo) and 40% GPU memory savings make large-scale open-scene reconstruction feasible on consumer hardware, which is practically significant for autonomous driving and VR pipelines. The theoretical finding that the CDF peak is systematically offset from the true surface (u₀ < 0) and the derived logistic SDF mapping provide a principled alternative to ad-hoc opacity-to-SDF conversions used in prior 3DGS-based surface methods. However, the method's local line-of-sight SDF fitting limits it in sparse-view regions, and it still trails GOF in geometric F1 scores, leaving open whether the voxel-implicit formulation can match fully explicit methods in reconstruction fidelity while retaining its efficiency advantages.
