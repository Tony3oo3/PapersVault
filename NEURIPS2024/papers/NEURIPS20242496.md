## Problem

Accelerating pretrained diffusion models to few-step generators remains challenging: existing reflow-based methods like InstaFlow require expensive synthetic dataset construction that introduces distribution shift, adversarial distillation methods (SDXL-Lightning, UFOGen) suffer from training instability and mode collapse, and consistency distillation (LCM-LoRA) produces images with fewer details and noticeable domain shift. The key unresolved question is how to straighten flow trajectories using real training data with online target synthesis, while preserving compatibility with the rich ecosystem of pretrained diffusion model workflows.

## Input / Output

During training, PeRFlow consumes real images from LAION-5B-Aesthetics (512×512 for SD-v1.5, 768×768 for SD-v2.1, 1024×1024 for SDXL, 16×384×384 video clips for AnimateDiff) along with text captions, and an ODE solver (DDIM, 8 steps per window) that computes endpoints online. At inference, the accelerated model takes a text prompt and random Gaussian noise to produce images or video clips in as few as 4 steps. Additionally, the trained student model yields a weight delta ΔW = θ − φ that can be directly added to any pipeline (ControlNet, IP-Adapter, Wonder3D) pretrained on the original diffusion model, enabling few-step generation without retraining those plugins.

## Method

PeRFlow divides the full sampling trajectory [1,0] into K time windows and applies the reflow operation independently within each window, training a velocity field to match the linear interpolation between the window's start and end states. Starting points z_{t_k} are constructed by adding noise to real training images according to the marginal distribution, and endpoints z_{t_{k-1}} are solved online via a DDIM ODE solver over the shortened interval—avoiding both synthetic dataset storage and the accumulated numerical error of full-trajectory integration. A dedicated ε-prediction parameterization (two variants: one derived from the diffusion process definition, one from the DDIM update rule) bridges the gap between pretrained diffusion models and the flow-based student, enabling direct weight initialization from the teacher for fast convergence. The method supports two CFG modes (CFG-sync preserves sampling diversity; CFG-fixed trades diversity for fewer structural failures), and the resulting ΔW weights serve as a universal plug-and-play accelerator compatible with arbitrary pretrained SD workflows.

## Results

On SD-v1.5, PeRFlow achieves FID 8.60 (4-step) and 8.52 (8-step) on LAION-5B-Aesthetics, substantially outperforming InstaFlow (14.32/10.98) and LCM-LoRA (15.28/19.21). On SDXL, PeRFlow reaches FID 13.30 (4-step) and 13.06 (8-step) on LAION-5B, beating SDXL-Lightning (15.47/14.37) and LCM-LoRA (13.66/13.31). Notably, PeRFlow's FID improves slightly with more inference steps, whereas LCM-LoRA's FID unexpectedly degrades (e.g., 15.28→19.21 on LAION-5B for SD-v1.5). PeRFlow also shows the smallest domain shift to the original pretrained models, with FID 8.28 (4-step) against SD-v1.5 reference vs. 16.67 for InstaFlow and 15.63 for LCM-LoRA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PeRFlow decisively outperforms InstaFlow on all SD-v1.5 metrics, achieving FID 8.60 vs. 14.32 (4-step, LAION-5B) and 8.28 vs. 16.67 (4-step, SD-v1.5 reference), a gap of roughly 6–8 FID points. Against LCM-LoRA, PeRFlow wins by a wide margin on SD-v1.5 (8.60 vs. 15.28 on LAION-5B 4-step) and by a smaller but consistent margin on SDXL (13.30 vs. 13.66 on LAION-5B 4-step); LCM-LoRA also suffers from FID degradation at higher step counts, while PeRFlow's FID remains stable or improves. On SDXL, PeRFlow beats SDXL-Lightning across all three reference distributions (e.g., 13.30 vs. 15.47 on LAION-5B 4-step, 9.28 vs. 11.41 on SDXL reference 4-step). In terms of domain shift, PeRFlow preserves the original model's style and diversity far better than both LCM-LoRA and InstaFlow, as confirmed by lower FID against the original model's own outputs and qualitative comparisons in Figures 5–6. The CLIP score (Appendix Table 2) further confirms that PeRFlow's 4-step SDXL generation (0.337) matches the 25-step original SDXL, while Lightning (0.330) and LCM-LoRA (0.327) fall slightly short.

## Relation to Prior Work

PeRFlow extends the rectified flow / reflow framework (Liu et al., 2022; InstaFlow, ICLR 2023) by replacing the single full-trajectory reflow with a piecewise, divide-and-conquer strategy that operates on shorter time windows using real training data. It departs from adversarial distillation methods (SDXL-Lightning, UFOGen) and consistency distillation (LCM) by relying purely on supervised flow-matching, and from InstaFlow by eliminating the need for a pre-generated synthetic dataset. The dedicated ε-prediction-to-velocity parameterization, which inherits the DDIM update rule as initialization, is a novel bridge between the pretrained diffusion model's training objective and the flow-matching student.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: PeRFlow delivers 4-step generation on SD 1.5, SD 2.1, SDXL, and AnimateDiff with FID values competitive with or better than adversarial and consistency-distillation baselines, while preserving the original model's style and diversity. The ΔW plug-and-play property—where the weight delta can accelerate ControlNet, IP-Adapter, and Wonder3D pipelines without retraining—opens a path to cheaply accelerating the entire Stable Diffusion ecosystem. Key limitations include the current 4-step minimum (no 1–2 step inference) and the reliance on online ODE solving during training, which still incurs per-iteration compute cost. Future work on multi-stage training and eliminating target synthesis entirely could push toward single-step generation.
