## Problem

End-to-end transformer-based MOT trackers suffer from negative interference when trained on heterogeneous scenarios, where the model learns conflicting scene-specific parameters, and they generalize poorly to new domains without expensive fine-tuning. This paper asks whether decomposing adaptation into attribute-specific, independently trained modules can eliminate negative transfer while enabling zero-shot domain generalization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20244250/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The tracker consumes RGB video frames along with five discrete scene attributes (lighting: good/bad; viewpoint: high/medium/low; occupancy: low/medium/high; location: indoor/outdoor; camera motion: static/moving) that are provided by a domain expert or inferred automatically. These attributes route to specific PEFT modules which are composed in parameter space before a single forward pass, so the inference pipeline is identical to the base MOTRv2 tracker with no additional latency. Output is per-frame tracked bounding boxes with persistent object IDs, evaluated via HOTA, IDF1, MOTA, DetA, and AssA.

## Method

PASTA modularizes a frozen MOTRv2 (Deformable DETR + ResNet) backbone by attaching 12 lightweight PEFT modules—LoRA (r=16) on every linear layer of the encoder-decoder and channel-wise scale & shift on every convolutional layer of the backbone—one per discrete attribute value. Each module is trained independently (randomly sampling one attribute per iteration) so gradient updates never cross attribute boundaries, preventing negative interference. At inference, a Domain Expert selects one module per attribute; the selected modules' task vectors (parameter displacements from the base weights) are aggregated via weighted averaging (Eq. 1), and for zero-shot transfer a soft routing scheme (ρ=0.8) blends the selected module with all other modules of the same attribute to enrich the representation. The scale & shift operations are re-parameterized into the preceding convolution, so the composed model incurs zero additional inference cost, and the total trainable parameter count drops from 42M to 15M.

## Results

On MOTSynth (in-domain), PASTA achieves HOTA 53.0, IDF1 57.6, MOTA 62.0, and AssA 50.4 with only 15M trainable parameters, outperforming the monolithic MOTRv2-MS baseline (42M params) by +0.6 HOTA and +1.4 AssA. In zero-shot transfer to MOT17, PASTA (ρ=0.8) reaches HOTA 64.0 and IDF1 74.9, improving over MOTRv2-MS by +1.4 HOTA and +1.9 IDF1. On PersonPath22 zero-shot, PASTA achieves MOTA 50.0 and IDF1 53.8, surpassing MOTRv2-MS by +1.7 MOTA and +0.7 IDF1 while also reducing false positives from 28,483 to 18,038.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PASTA outperforms its direct monolithic baseline MOTRv2-MS on all association and tracking metrics (HOTA +0.6, IDF1 +1.0, AssA +1.4) while using 64% fewer trainable parameters (15M vs. 42M). Against tracking-by-detection methods, PASTA surpasses ByteTrack by +7.3 HOTA and +8.5 AssA, and OC-Sort by +6.1 HOTA and +4.8 AssA, though ByteTrack retains a slight MOTA edge (61.8 vs. 62.0 is actually a PASTA win by 0.2). TrackFormer is substantially outperformed across all metrics (e.g., +11.7 HOTA), confirming the advantage of the MOTRv2 backbone over the earlier TrackFormer architecture. In zero-shot MOT17 transfer, PASTA (ρ=0.8) beats MOTRv2-MS by +1.4 HOTA and +1.9 IDF1, and in zero-shot PersonPath22 it gains +1.7 MOTA and +0.7 IDF1 while halving false positives. However, PASTA still trails fully-trained ByteTrack in zero-shot settings (e.g., 64.0 vs. 67.9 HOTA on MOT17), which the authors attribute to the inherent robustness of parameter-free association strategies in TbD pipelines under domain shift.

## Relation to Prior Work

PASTA builds directly on MOTRv2 (Zhang et al., CVPR 2023) as its base query-based tracker, applying LoRA to the Deformable DETR encoder-decoder and channel-wise scale-and-shift to the ResNet backbone as its two PEFT mechanisms. The key conceptual departure is from monolithic fine-tuning: rather than updating all parameters jointly across heterogeneous scenarios, PASTA trains attribute-specific expert modules independently and composes them via task-vector arithmetic (Ilharco et al., 2022), drawing on the Modular Deep Learning framework (Pfeiffer et al., 2023). This positions the work at the intersection of PEFT, model merging, and domain adaptation for tracking, distinguishing it from prior domain-adaptation efforts like GHOST and DARTH that target tracking-by-detection pipelines and require test-time adaptation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical deployment bottleneck: end-to-end query-based trackers overfit to training domains and degrade sharply under domain shift, while tracking-by-detection methods remain more robust but lack end-to-end differentiability. PASTA's modular design lets surveillance operators swap lightweight (~5 MB) attribute modules without retraining, and the zero-shot synth-to-real results (+1.4 HOTA on MOT17, +1.7 MOTA on PersonPath22 over the monolithic baseline) suggest a viable path toward configurable, domain-adaptive tracking. However, the reliance on a manually annotated expert router limits scalability, the tested attribute set is narrow (five pedestrian-scene attributes), and the absolute gains are modest, leaving open whether the approach generalizes to richer attribute spaces, non-pedestrian tracking, or fully automatic routing.
