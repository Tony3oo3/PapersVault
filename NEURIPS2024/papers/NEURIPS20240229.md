## Problem

Autoregressive graph generators are efficient but sensitive to node/edge ordering, producing non-exchangeable probabilities, while diffusion-based generators are permutation-invariant but require thousands of denoising steps and auxiliary structural features to achieve competitive quality. No prior method combines the data efficiency of autoregression with the permutation invariance of diffusion for graph generation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240229/cropped_page4_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, PARD starts from a pure categorical noise graph (uniform random node and edge labels) and iteratively denoises it block-by-block to produce a complete labeled graph. The graph size is not fixed in advance; instead, a separate block-size predictor determines how many nodes each successive block adds, with generation stopping when the predicted block size is zero. Node and edge labels are categorical (one-hot), and 'no edge' is treated as an additional edge label type.

## Method

PARD decomposes the joint graph probability into a product of block-wise conditional probabilities using a unique, permutation-equivariant structural partial order (Algorithm 1) that ranks nodes by multi-hop weighted degree while preserving connectivity. Each block's conditional distribution is modeled by a shared discrete denoising diffusion with a permutation-equivariant network, which the paper proves is necessary because no equivariant network—regardless of expressivity—can perform general graph transformations when structurally equivalent nodes/edges exist (Theorem 3.2). The equivariant backbone is a hybrid higher-order graph transformer that interleaves GRIT attention (O(n²) memory) with PPGN edge updates (3-WL expressivity) at reduced edge representation size. A GPT-style causal masking scheme, extended to PPGN's matrix-matrix products via a derived safe product formula (Eq. 10), enables parallel training of all K_B blocks with shared representations, yielding >10× speedup and scaling to 1.9M-graph datasets like MOSES.

## Results

On QM9, PARD achieves 97.5% Validity and 86.1% Molecule Stability without any auxiliary features, matching DiGress with specialized features (79.8% Mol. Stability) and approaching the dataset optimum (87.0%). On ZINC250K, PARD achieves the best FCD (1.98) and Uniqueness (99.99%) with only 4.1M parameters, compared to DiGress's 23.06 FCD and 81.23% Uniqueness at 18.43M parameters. On the 1.9M-molecule MOSES benchmark, PARD achieves 1.00 FCD and 0.56 SNN, outperforming DiGress (1.19 FCD, 0.52 SNN). On five generic graph datasets, PARD achieves the best or near-best MMD across Degree, Clustering, and Orbit metrics, with particularly large margins on CAVEMAN (0.002 Degree MMD vs 0.019 for DiGress) and CORA (0.0003 Degree MMD vs 0.044 for DiGress).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PARD consistently outperforms DiGress [44], the strongest prior diffusion baseline, across all evaluated settings: on ZINC250K, PARD reduces FCD from 23.06 to 1.98 and improves Uniqueness from 81.23% to 99.99% while using 4.5× fewer parameters (4.1M vs 18.43M); on MOSES, PARD achieves lower FCD (1.00 vs 1.19) and higher SNN (0.56 vs 0.52); on generic graphs, PARD achieves the best MMD on 13 out of 15 metrics. Against SwinGNN-L [46], PARD matches FCD on ZINC250K (1.98 vs 1.99) but substantially improves Validity (95.23% vs 90.68%) and Uniqueness (99.99% vs 99.73%). PARD also clearly surpasses GraphArm [25] on generic graphs (e.g., CAVEMAN Degree MMD 0.002 vs 0.039) and GDSS [24] across all datasets. On QM9, PARD without any extra features (97.5% Validity, 86.1% Mol. Stability) matches or exceeds DiGress with specialized structural features (95.4% Validity, 79.8% Mol. Stability), demonstrating that the autoregressive decomposition eliminates the need for symmetry-breaking auxiliary features. The only metric where PARD trails is Novelty on MOSES (78.2% vs 95.0% for DiGress), though PARD leads on all other MOSES metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

PARD sits at the intersection of two dominant paradigms in graph generation: autoregressive models (GraphRNN, GRAN) that decompose joint distributions into sequential conditionals but are permutation-sensitive, and discrete denoising diffusion models (DiGress, D3PM) that are permutation-invariant but require thousands of steps and auxiliary features. The key conceptual departure is the identification of a unique, permutation-equivariant structural partial order that makes the autoregressive sequence itself permutation-invariant, combined with a theoretical impossibility result showing that no equivariant network can perform general graph transformation without symmetry breaking—thereby motivating the use of local diffusion within each autoregressive block. Architecturally, it builds on PPGN for 3-WL expressivity and GRIT for transformer-based node processing, with a novel causal masking scheme enabling parallel training across all blocks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PARD provides the first principled framework that unifies the data efficiency of autoregression with the permutation invariance of diffusion, eliminating the need for hand-crafted structural features (eigenvectors, cycle counts) that DiGress relies on. The impossibility theorem for equivariant graph transformation is a foundational result that clarifies a long-standing practical difficulty in equivariant generation and motivates the annealing/diffusion mechanism. Open questions include whether caching mechanisms can further reduce inference cost, the theoretical characterization of when graph transformation succeeds under noise, and the potential for combining PARD's parallel autoregressive mechanism with language models for language-graph generative pretraining. A noted limitation is that the causal masking for parallel training may reduce expressiveness on symmetry-rich datasets like GRID.
