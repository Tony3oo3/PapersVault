## Problem

Standard UV-based texturing of meshes suffers from inherent artefacts—seams, distortions, wasted UV-space, vertex duplication, and varying resolution—that are particularly acute with automatic UV-unwrapping. Most state-of-the-art texture generation methods operate in UV-space and thus inherit all these drawbacks. The paper asks whether textures can be generated directly on the curved surface of 3D objects, bypassing UV-mapping entirely.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20244065/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D triangle mesh (vertices and faces, pre-filtered to under 60,000 vertices) as geometric input and Gaussian noise as the diffusion starting point. During training, points are sampled on the mesh surface via Poisson Disk Sampling at a target of ~5,000 points, and their colours are queried from existing UV-textures or base colours. The output is an unstructured coloured point-cloud (P points with RGB albedo values) that is resolution-agnostic and does not need to coincide with mesh vertices; it is rendered via three-nearest-neighbour Euclidean interpolation in a Mitsuba3 path-tracer, producing relightable albedo textures without baked lighting.

## Method

UV3-TeD is a denoising diffusion probabilistic model whose U-Net-shaped architecture operates exclusively on the mesh surface using novel Attention-enhanced Heat Diffusion Blocks. Each block combines three DiffusionNet-style operators (spectral heat diffusion, spatial gradient features, and a per-point MLP) with a diffused farthest-sampled attention layer that first heat-diffuses features across the surface, computes multi-headed self-attention on 250 farthest-point samples, then diffuses the result back to all points—enabling long-range consistency without full-resolution attention. A mixed robust Laplacian (convex combination of the robust mesh LBO and a point-cloud LBO with weight ρ=0.05) ensures heat diffuses geodesically while still bridging topological errors and disconnected components. An online sampling strategy recycles precomputed eigendecompositions by interpolating eigenvectors to PDS points and approximating the mass matrix from the PDS radius, avoiding costly recomputation. Shape conditioning is provided via scale-invariant heat kernel signatures (local intrinsic coordinates) and slope-adjusted normalised eigenvalues (global class descriptors), both embedded by MLPs and injected into the attention layers.

## Results

On ShapeNet chairs, UV3-TeD achieves a FID of 54.20 and KID of 42.17×10⁻⁴, outperforming Point-UV Diffusion (FID 63.35, KID 83.19×10⁻⁴ in UV mode; FID 65.09, KID 126.18×10⁻⁴ in point-cloud mode) on both quality metrics. UV3-TeD also generates significantly more diverse textures, scoring 0.21 LPIPS versus 0.08–0.09 for Point-UV Diffusion. Against a DiffusionNet DDPM baseline (same architecture without shape conditioning or attention), UV3-TeD reduces FID from 116.58 to 54.20 and KID from 468.09×10⁻⁴ to 42.17×10⁻⁴, while maintaining comparable diversity (0.21 vs. 0.24 LPIPS).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is against Point-UV Diffusion [58], the closest non-UV-based method that also generates coloured point-clouds. UV3-TeD outperforms Point-UV Diffusion on both FID (54.20 vs. 63.35 in UV mode, 65.09 in point-cloud mode) and KID (42.17 vs. 83.19/126.18), indicating substantially better sample quality. UV3-TeD also produces markedly more diverse textures (LPIPS 0.21 vs. 0.08–0.09), suggesting the farthest-sampled attention and shape conditioning enable richer variation without semantic segmentation. The second baseline is a DiffusionNet DDPM ablation that shares the U-Net architecture but lacks shape conditioning, farthest-sampled attention, and the mixed Laplacian; UV3-TeD reduces FID by more than half (116.58 → 54.20) and KID by over 90% (468.09 → 42.17), confirming that the proposed attention and conditioning are critical. The paper notes that UV-based methods benefit from mature image-generation tooling and larger training data, so the comparison is framed as a paradigm shift rather than a direct quality contest.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on DiffusionNet [48] for its heat-diffusion-based surface operators, but departs from the dominant UV-mapping paradigm that underpins most texture generation methods (Point-UV Diffusion [58], Texturify [49], TexFusion [7]). The key conceptual shift is representing textures as unstructured point-clouds constrained to the mesh surface rather than as UV images, eliminating seams, distortions, and wasted UV-space by construction. The novel diffused farthest-sampled attention layer extends DiffusionNet blocks by enabling global geodesic communication, while the mixed Laplacian operator fuses mesh and point-cloud connectivity to handle topological defects that pure mesh-based operators cannot.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work validates a surface-native texture representation that avoids the well-known artefacts of UV-mapping, opening a path toward seam-free, resolution-agnostic texture generation that could reduce memory footprints and simplify artist workflows. However, current results are limited to low-frequency albedo textures at ~5000 points, and the paper acknowledges that high-frequency detail requires thousands of epochs and that UV-based pipelines still benefit from the maturity of image generation. Open questions include whether this representation can scale to high-resolution textures, support full BRDF generation, and compete with the rapidly advancing UV-space diffusion methods once they incorporate similar surface-aware operators.
