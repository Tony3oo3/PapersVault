## Problem

Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) systems must feed entire retrieved documents into the LLM, inflating context length by over 10× and increasing inference cost. Existing context compression methods either require storing per-token LLM activations (impractical for millions of documents) or achieve modest compression rates, and none exploit the fact that dense retrieval already distills document content into a single high-dimensional embedding.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243475/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text query and a single pre-computed document embedding (e.g., 4096-dim from SFR) as input, and produces a generated text answer. The document embedding is projected into the LLM's token embedding space via a two-layer MLP, yielding exactly one additional token [X] in the input sequence regardless of the original document length (~175 tokens on average), achieving a ~178× compression ratio. Both the retriever and the LLM remain frozen; only the projector is trained.

## Method

xRAG redefines the dense retrieval embedding as a 'retrieval modality' feature and fuses it into the LLM's representation space through a lightweight modality bridge (a two-layer MLP accounting for <0.1% of LLM parameters). Training proceeds in two stages: (1) Paraphrase Pretraining, where the LLM learns to reconstruct the full document text conditioned on its projected embedding, aligning the two modalities; and (2) Context-aware Instruction Tuning, which combines standard language modeling on the projected context with a self-distillation objective that minimizes KL divergence between the xRAG output distribution and the full-document RAG output distribution. This self-distillation is critical for robustness, teaching xRAG to fall back on internal LLM knowledge when the retrieved document is noisy or misleading. The design preserves plug-and-play RAG by reusing offline-constructed document embeddings and requiring no LLM parameter updates.

## Results

On six knowledge-intensive tasks, xRAG compresses a ~175-token retrieved document into a single token while achieving an average score of 42.46 (Mistral-7b) and 46.91 (Mixtral-8x7b), compared to 43.63 and 46.22 for the uncompressed RAG upper bound. Notably, xRAG with Mixtral-8x7b actually exceeds the full RAG baseline (46.91 vs. 46.22), while with Mistral-7b it retains 83% of the RAG improvement (16.2% vs. 19.4% relative gain over no-retrieval). In terms of efficiency, xRAG reduces total FLOPs by a factor of 3.53 and CUDA time by 1.64× compared to standard RAG, with the only trainable component (a two-layer MLP projector) accounting for less than 0.1% of LLM parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against LLMLingua (the primary plug-and-play compression baseline), xRAG wins decisively on every task and both model backbones: with Mistral-7b, xRAG scores 42.46 average vs. LLMLingua† at 38.01 and LLMLingua‡ at 37.48, a gap of 4.5–5.0 points despite xRAG using 1 token versus ~61–99 tokens. With Mixtral-8x7b, the gap widens further: xRAG reaches 46.91 while LLMLingua† drops below the no-retrieval baseline (42.32 vs. 42.76), showing that surface-form compression actively harms the larger model. Against TF-IDF (a discrete 1-token compression lower bound), xRAG leads by 5.0 points (Mistral-7b) and 3.5 points (Mixtral-8x7b), confirming that the modality-fusion approach captures far more information from the embedding than keyword-based selection. xRAG also approaches or exceeds the full RAG upper bound: it retains 83% of RAG's improvement with Mistral-7b (16.2% vs. 19.4%) and surpasses RAG with Mixtral-8x7b (9.7% vs. 8.0%), likely because the compressed representation is more robust to noisy or misleading retrieved documents.

## Relation to Prior Work

xRAG repurposes a component of dense retrieval systems—the document embedding computed by a sentence encoder—as a 'retrieval modality feature' to be fused into a frozen LLM via a lightweight projector, drawing a direct analogy to how vision-language models (CLIP, BLIP-2) integrate image features through a modality bridge. This departs from prior context compression work (Gist tokens, ICAE, AutoCompressor, LLMLingua, RECOMP) in two key ways: it avoids storing LLM activations per compressed token and avoids any surface-form text compression, instead leveraging the embedding that already exists for retrieval. The two-stage training (paraphrase pretraining + context-aware instruction tuning with self-distillation) is inspired by multimodal alignment strategies and self-distillation literature, but applied here to align a retrieval embedding with the LLM's internal representation space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

xRAG demonstrates that a single projected retrieval token can recover most of the RAG performance gain (16.2% vs 19.4% for Mistral-7b; 9.7% vs 8.0% for Mixtral-8x7b) while cutting FLOPs by 3.53×, with only 0.07–0.46% of LLM parameters trainable and full reuse of offline-constructed embeddings—making it genuinely plug-and-play. Key limitations remain: a substantial gap on multi-hop and fact-checking reasoning tasks (HotpotQA, FactKG), restriction to top-1 single-vector retrieval, and no handling of sparse or multi-vector retrieval. Open directions include scaling to multi-document settings (where xRAG's per-document cost is one token vs. ~175 for RAG), integrating reasoning-specific training data, and combining with ColBERT-style multi-vector retrieval.
