## Problem

Existing deep learning models for exposure correction have not deeply integrated Retinex theory into their architecture, and most treat reflectance alone as the final output, ignoring the illumination component. Additionally, the trade-off between restoration quality and computational efficiency remains under-explored, particularly for multi-exposure correction where under-exposed and over-exposed images follow opposite optimization flows toward a single normal-exposed target.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241692/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single under-exposed or over-exposed RGB image (256×256 crops during training) and produces a correctly exposed RGB image as the final output. Internally, a lightweight Retinex estimator first decomposes the input into a modulated reflectance R′ and a modulated illumination L′ via element-wise multiplication with learned inverse maps, which are then independently refined by two Mamba-based restoration branches before being recombined via Hadamard product. The channel-mean matrix of the input is also fed to the Retinex estimator as an auxiliary signal.

## Method

ECMamba is a dual-branch Mamba-based network that fully embeds Retinex theory: a Retinex estimator E maps the degraded input into two intermediary spaces (R′ and L′) that are significantly closer to the target distribution than the raw input, enabling subsequent fine-grained restoration by two Exposure Correction Mamba Modules (ECMM) with a two-scale U-Net structure. The core operator is a novel Retinex-SS2D layer that replaces self-attention with a Feature-Aware 2D Selective Scanning (FA-SS2D) mechanism: Deformable Convolution guided by Retinex features produces an activation response map, and tokens are then sequenced by activation frequency (most important first) rather than by fixed spatial direction, before being processed by the standard S6 selective state-space recurrence. Training is one-stage and supervised, combining L1, SSIM, and VGG perceptual losses on the final output with self-supervised L1 constraints that enforce L̄⊙L_out≈1 and R̄⊙R_out≈1, plus an auxiliary L1 loss on R_out to regularize the ill-posed decomposition.

## Results

On the ME dataset, ECMamba achieves 23.76 dB average PSNR and 0.879 SSIM, outperforming the second-best method (LACT) by 0.19 dB and 0.007 SSIM. On the SICE dataset, the improvement is more pronounced: 22.05 dB average PSNR and 0.736 SSIM, surpassing the runner-up (FECNet+ERL) by 0.83 dB and 0.051 SSIM. For under-exposure correction, ECMamba reaches 27.69 dB on LOLv1, 29.24 dB on LOLv2-real, and 29.94 dB on LOLv2-synthetic, exceeding the best baseline (LLFlow-SKF) by ~1.10 dB average PSNR while using only 1.75M parameters (4.4% of LLFlow-SKF's 39.91M).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ECMamba outperforms all 12 baselines across both multi-exposure (ME, SICE) and under-exposure (LOL) benchmarks. On the ME dataset, the closest competitor is LACT [4] (23.57 dB avg PSNR), which ECMamba surpasses by 0.19 dB; FECNet+ERL [21] (23.15 dB) and FECNet [20] (23.12 dB) trail by ~0.6 dB. On SICE, the gap widens substantially: ECMamba leads FECNet+ERL [21] by 0.83 dB and Retiformer [6] by 0.87 dB in average PSNR, with a particularly large SSIM margin (0.736 vs. 0.678 for FECNet+ERL). For under-exposure correction, ECMamba beats the strongest baseline LLFlow-SKF [38] by 0.89 dB on LOLv1, 1.05 dB on LOLv2-real, and 1.08 dB on LOLv2-synthetic, while using only 1.75M parameters versus LLFlow-SKF's 39.91M (a 23× reduction). Older methods such as ZeroDCE [16], RUAS [24], URetinexNet [37], and KinD [44] are outperformed by 8–12 dB, reflecting their inability to handle over-exposed inputs. The efficiency advantage is notable: ECMamba matches or exceeds Retiformer [6] (1.61M params) in quality while remaining far lighter than flow-based methods like LLFlow [34] (37.68M) and LLFlow-SKF [38] (39.91M).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Mamba selective state-space model (S6) for efficient long-range dependency modeling and on Retinex theory for physically-motivated image decomposition into reflectance and illumination. It departs from prior multi-exposure correction methods (FECNet, LACT, ECLNet) that rely on exposure normalization or regularization tricks, and from prior Retinex-based networks (URetinexNet, Retiformer) that treat reflectance alone as the final output. The key conceptual departure is reframing the 2D-to-1D scanning problem for state-space models as a 'feature-sensitive' rather than 'direction-sensitive' issue, leading to the FA-SS2D mechanism that orders tokens by activation frequency derived from deformable convolution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ECMamba achieves SOTA multi-exposure and under-exposure correction with only 1.75M parameters (4.4% of LLFlow-SKF's 39.91M), making it practically relevant for resource-constrained deployment. The dual-branch Retinex framework addresses a fundamental gap: prior methods struggle with multi-exposure correction because the mapping flows for under- and over-exposure are opposite, and the intermediary space construction (R', L') significantly reduces the optimization distance. The authors acknowledge that extreme exposure cases remain challenging due to irreversible information loss, and propose integrating generative priors with Mamba as a future direction.
