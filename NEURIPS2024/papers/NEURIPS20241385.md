## Problem

Federated Semi-Supervised Learning (FSSL) in the labels-at-server setting suffers a large performance gap compared to centralized SSL, especially when only tens of labeled samples are available at the server. This gap is driven by confirmation bias, which is amplified in FSSL by multiple local training epochs and the physical separation of labeled (server) and unlabeled (client) data that prevents joint optimization. Existing FSSL methods rely on fixed pseudo-labeling thresholds and uniform aggregation weights, failing to adapt to heterogeneous client learning progress.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241385/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes unlabeled data distributed across 100 clients (32×32 RGB images for CIFAR-10/100/SVHN/Fashion-MNIST, or text for AGNews) and a very small labeled set at the server (as few as 10 samples for CIFAR-10, 100 for CIFAR-100, 40 for SVHN). Clients produce no labels; pseudo-labels are generated by the global model before each local training phase. The output is a single global classifier (WideResNet28×2/×8 for images, BERT-base-uncased with a linear head for text) that maps inputs to class probabilities, evaluated by top-1 accuracy under both balanced IID and Dirichlet non-IID (α=0.1, 0.3) data splits.

## Method

(FL)² combines three mechanisms to combat confirmation bias in FSSL. First, client-specific adaptive thresholding (CAT) replaces the fixed pseudo-labeling threshold with a per-client, per-class threshold computed as the mean confidence of the global model on each client's local data, scaled by a MaxNorm of class-wise average probabilities—yielding a low threshold early in training that gradually tightens. Second, sharpness-aware consistency regularization (SACR) applies ASAM-style adversarial weight perturbation to a high-confidence subset (fixed threshold τ_f=0.95) and minimizes the KL divergence between the original and perturbed model outputs on strongly augmented inputs, rather than the standard SAM objective of minimizing loss at perturbed weights; this avoids generalizing incorrectly pseudo-labeled samples. Third, learning status-aware aggregation (LSAA) assigns each client an aggregation weight proportional to (1 − τ_t^k), so clients with lower confidence (harder data) contribute more strongly to the global model update. The server additionally fine-tunes the aggregated model with its small labeled set after each round, following SemiFL's alternate-training strategy.

## Results

On the primary benchmarks, (FL)² achieves 94.6% accuracy on SVHN (250 labels, IID), 81.5% on CIFAR-10 (40 labels, IID), and 28.1% on CIFAR-100 (400 labels, IID), consistently outperforming all FSSL baselines. The most dramatic gains appear in label-scarce settings: 38.9% on CIFAR-10 with only 10 labels (IID) versus 10.0% for SemiFL, and 92.6% on SVHN with 250 labels under non-IID-0.3 versus 42.7% for SemiFL. The method also extends to text classification, achieving 87.0% on AGNews with 20 labels (IID) versus 47.4% for SemiFL.

## Baselines & Comparisons

(FL)² outperforms SemiFL [8] in 17 out of 18 settings in Table 1, with the sole exception being Non-IID-0.3/CIFAR-100/100-labels where SemiFL edges ahead by 0.3% (12.4% vs 12.1%). The margins over SemiFL are largest in label-scarce and non-IID regimes: +22.2% on CIFAR-10/10-labels/IID, +21.9% on SVHN/40-labels/IID, and +20.3% on SVHN/250-labels/Non-IID-0.3. Against FedMatch [7] and FedCon [9], (FL)² wins in every setting, with gaps of 10–25% depending on dataset and label budget. Compared to the centralized SSL reference FreeMatch [19] (Fig. 1), (FL)² substantially narrows the SSL–FSSL gap, particularly as labeled data shrinks from hundreds to tens of samples, though a residual gap remains in the most extreme low-label regime.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Federated Semi-Supervised Learning (FSSL) in the labels-at-server setting, building directly on SemiFL's alternate training and pseudo-label generation strategy, FreeMatch's adaptive thresholding for centralized SSL, and Sharpness-Aware Minimization (SAM/ASAM) for generalization. The key conceptual departure is the finding that standard SAM is counterproductive in FSSL: it indiscriminately generalizes both correct and incorrect pseudo-labels, and the standard SAM objective (minimizing loss at perturbed weights) fails to transfer. The paper replaces this with a sharpness-aware consistency regularization that measures output divergence between original and adversarially perturbed models, applied only to high-confidence samples, combined with client-specific adaptive thresholds and learning-status-weighted aggregation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper targets a practically important gap: real-world FL deployments (medical imaging, sensor networks) often have only tens of labeled samples at the server, where existing FSSL methods collapse to near-chance accuracy. By bridging the SSL–FSSL performance gap in these extreme label-scarce regimes, (FL)² makes federated semi-supervised learning more deployable. However, the approach adds computational overhead on clients (extra inference for perturbed models, more pseudo-labels generated), and the analysis remains purely empirical—no theoretical characterization of how generalizing incorrect pseudo-labels degrades performance is provided. A key open question is whether the sharpness-aware consistency formulation generalizes beyond image classification to other modalities or to the labels-at-clients FSSL setting.
