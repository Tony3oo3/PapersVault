## Problem

Fourier Neural Operators (FNOs) parameterize the Fourier-space kernel independently for each frequency mode, causing parameter counts to grow exponentially with PDE dimensionality. The standard workaround—truncating frequencies above a threshold—limits high-frequency representation and introduces non-trivial hyperparameter tuning. This paper asks whether the kernel can be specified with a fixed number of parameters regardless of how many frequency modes are retained.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243650/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes discretized input functions a: D → R^{d_a} on bounded domains D ⊂ R^d, supporting regular grids (e.g., 64×64, 128×128), structured meshes (e.g., 221×51 for Airfoil), and point clouds (e.g., 972 points for Elasticity), and produces discretized solution fields u: D → R^{d_u} at the same spatial resolution. Unlike standard FNOs that discard modes above a cutoff, AM-FNO processes all frequency modes present in the training data, and the zero-shot super-resolution experiments show it generalizes from 32×32 training to 64×64 evaluation without retraining.

## Method

AM-FNO replaces the per-mode kernel parameters R(k) with neural networks that map frequency vectors to complex-valued kernel entries: R_{p,q}(k) = NN_re(k) + i·NN_im(k), so a fixed-size network handles arbitrarily many modes. Two implementations are provided: (1) two-layer Kolmogorov-Arnold Networks with B-spline activations, and (2) MLPs whose inputs are first embedded via Chebyshev orthogonal basis functions to counteract spectral bias. For high-dimensional PDEs, a dimensional factorization trick constructs the full kernel as a product of per-dimension 1D kernels, keeping parameter count linear in dimension. The operator layer adopts a residual-connection architecture (from F-FNO) rather than FNO's pointwise linear map, and the amortized parameterization inherently regularizes against overfitting to high-frequency noise.

## Results

On seven PDE benchmarks (Darcy, NS-2D, Pipe, Airfoil, Elasticity, CFD-1D, CFD-2D), AM-FNO (MLP) achieves the lowest l2 relative error across all settings, with headline values of 4.21e-3 (Darcy), 8.51e-2 (NS-2D), 5.64e-3 (Airfoil), and 2.16e-3 (CFD-2D). AM-FNO (KAN) achieves 4.28e-3, 1.08e-1, 6.06e-3, and 2.70e-3 respectively. The paper reports an average 31% (MLP) and 22% (KAN) reduction in l2 relative error over the strongest baseline, with the largest gains on high-frequency-heavy CFD benchmarks (up to 52% on CFD-2D). In zero-shot super-resolution (trained at 32×32, tested at 64×64 on NS-2D), AM-FNO (MLP) achieves 8.73e-2, outperforming FNO trained at the same 64×64 resolution (1.28e-1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

AM-FNO (MLP) outperforms all seven baselines on every benchmark where both are evaluated, with the largest margins on high-frequency-heavy CFD tasks: 40% lower error than U-FNO on CFD-1D (1.47e-2 vs 2.44e-2) and 52% lower on CFD-2D (2.16e-3 vs 4.52e-3). Against the strongest baseline LSM, AM-FNO (MLP) reduces error by 40% on Darcy (4.21e-3 vs 7.01e-3), 12% on Airfoil (5.64e-3 vs 6.39e-3), and 10% on Elasticity (2.03e-2 vs 2.25e-2). U-FNO is the closest competitor on low-frequency benchmarks (NS-2D: 1.22e-1 vs 8.51e-2 for AM-FNO MLP), while F-FNO and Geo-FNO are consistently 2–5× worse on irregular-geometry tasks (Pipe, Airfoil, Elasticity). AFNO, which uses a shared MLP over all frequency modes, is the weakest baseline on most benchmarks (e.g., 6.72e-2 on CFD-2D vs 2.16e-3 for AM-FNO MLP), confirming that uniform frequency treatment severely limits expressiveness. OFormer is competitive only on Darcy and NS-2D but does not support irregular geometries or CFD benchmarks. Notably, AM-FNO (MLP) also beats FNO trained at the target 64×64 resolution in zero-shot super-resolution (8.73e-2 vs 1.28e-1), demonstrating superior data efficiency.

## Relation to Prior Work

AM-FNO directly extends FNO (Li et al., 2020) by replacing its point-by-point parameterization of Fourier-space kernel values with neural network amortization, thereby accommodating all frequency modes with a fixed parameter budget. It addresses the uniform-frequency limitation of AFNO (Guibas et al., 2021) by using input-dependent NN transformations rather than a shared MLP, and borrows the dimensional factorization trick and residual operator-layer architecture from F-FNO (Tran et al., 2023). The KAN variant (Liu et al., 2024) represents the first application of Kolmogorov-Arnold Networks to operator learning for PDEs, while the MLP variant introduces Chebyshev orthogonal embedding as a remedy for spectral bias.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The amortized parameterization resolves the core FNO trade-off between parameter explosion (from retaining all frequency modes) and information loss (from truncation), while providing inherent regularization against high-frequency noise. The zero-shot super-resolution results—where AM-FNO trained at 32×32 outperforms FNO trained at 64×64—suggest substantial data-efficiency gains for high-resolution PDE solving. Limitations include evaluation only on idealized benchmarks (no real-world irregular geometries beyond structured meshes) and the acknowledged difficulty of scaling to extremely high-dimensional PDEs where FFT cost dominates. Open questions include whether the orthogonal embedding strategy generalizes to 3D+ problems and whether KAN's interpretability advantage translates to practical benefits in operator learning.
