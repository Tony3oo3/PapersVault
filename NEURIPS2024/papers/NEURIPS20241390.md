## Problem

Meta-RL research is bottlenecked by the lack of publicly available, computationally affordable environments with sufficient task diversity. The most capable meta-RL benchmark (XLand) is closed-source and requires five weeks of training on large clusters, forcing most recent adaptive-agent work to fall back on trivially simple environments like bandits or basic navigation. This paper addresses the gap by providing an open-source, JAX-accelerated grid-world suite that matches XLand's task complexity while running millions of steps per second on a single GPU.

## Input / Output

The agent receives observations as 2D arrays of tile IDs (15 types) and color IDs (14 types) representing a partial field of view, not raw images—though an optional wrapper renders them as 224×224 RGB frames. Actions are six discrete choices (move_forward, turn_left, turn_right, pick_up, put_down, toggle), with the constraint that the agent can hold only one object at a time. Each episode is defined by a ruleset: a set of production rules (up to 18) and a root goal, all hidden from the agent, with object positions randomized on every reset. The environment outputs a TimeStep containing observation, reward, step_type (FIRST/MID/LAST), discount, and a full State encoding the grid, agent position, rule/goal array encodings, and a PRNG key.

## Method

The core technical contribution is a JAX-native environment where production rules and goals are encoded as fixed-length integer arrays (rule/goal ID + padded arguments) rather than Python objects, enabling them to change between resets while remaining compatible with jax.jit and jax.vmap. Tasks are procedurally generated as binary trees: the root is a goal, internal nodes are production rules whose output objects feed the parent's inputs, and leaves define the initial objects placed on the grid; distractor rules and objects introduce dead ends to prevent brute-forcing. Four pre-sampled benchmarks (trivial, small, medium, high) contain up to 3 million unique rulesets with increasing tree depth and rule count. Baselines use recurrent PPO (RL²) with a single-layer GRU (1024 hidden dim) under the Anakin architecture, with the entire training loop jit-compiled and parallelized across devices via jax.pmap, reaching ~1M steps/s on a single A100 and ~6M on eight.

## Results

XLand-MiniGrid achieves near log-log linear scaling of simulation throughput with the number of parallel environments, reaching tens of millions of steps per second on multiple A100 GPUs, with single-device saturation around 2^13 environments. Training throughput for the recurrent PPO/RL² baseline saturates near 1M steps per second on a single A100 and scales to approximately 6M steps per second across multiple devices. The RL² baseline's 20th percentile return remains far from optimal across all four benchmarks (trivial-1m through high-1m), and even training for one trillion transitions on high-1m (72 hours on 8×A100 at ~3.85M SPS) does not close the gap. Generalization to held-out goal types shows a large performance drop on every benchmark, including the minimal-diversity trivial set.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not compare against external meta-RL methods; its purpose is to provide a scalable environment and validate that the proposed benchmarks are challenging. The sole baseline is an RL² agent (recurrent PPO with a GRU, 1024 hidden dim, 1 layer) trained on 13×13 four-room grids. On the trivial-1m benchmark the 20th percentile return plateaus well below optimal, and performance degrades monotonically as benchmark complexity increases from trivial to high. At the extreme scale of 1T transitions on high-1m, the 20th percentile still lags significantly behind the mean, indicating the task distribution remains unsolved. In the generalization experiment (excluding goal types with IDs other than 1, 3, 4 from training), the 20th percentile on unseen tasks drops sharply on all benchmarks, confirming that even the simplest task distribution poses a non-trivial generalization challenge. The authors note that training the full Ada agent (Team et al., 2023) for a comparable duration would be infeasible, positioning XLand-MiniGrid as a democratizing alternative.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper combines the task-generation paradigm of XLand (Team et al., 2023) — production rules and goals organized as trees — with the visual grid-world interface of MiniGrid (Chevalier-Boisvert et al., 2023), reimplementing both from scratch in JAX for hardware-accelerated execution. It positions itself against the broader JAX-based environment ecosystem (Jumanji, Gymnax, Brax, PGX) by specifically targeting meta-RL needs: stateless environments with swappable rulesets, pre-sampled task distributions, and multi-device parallelism via jax.vmap/pmap. The key departure from prior work is that it makes a non-trivial, open-ended meta-RL environment (comparable in spirit to XLand or Alchemy) accessible on consumer hardware, whereas prior complex meta-RL environments were either closed-source (XLand) or too slow for rapid iteration (Alchemy at 30 FPS).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper fills a concrete gap: before this work, researchers studying adaptive agents were forced to use either simple environments (bandits, 2D navigation) or closed-source/slow ones, limiting the ability to study scaling and generalization properties of meta-RL algorithms. The validation that even one trillion transitions with a simple RL² baseline leaves the high-1m benchmark largely unsolved, and that generalization gaps persist even on the minimal-diversity trivial benchmark, establishes a clear research frontier. Open questions include whether more sophisticated architectures (SSMs, Transformers) can close the generalization gap, whether the benchmarks are sufficiently hard, and how to extend to multi-agent settings and procedurally generated mazes — all noted as future work by the authors.
