## Problem

Fine-grained visual classification (FGVC) requires distinguishing closely related sub-classes with subtle visual differences, but FGVC datasets are small and traditional augmentation methods introduce limited semantic diversity. Recent generative augmentation methods that use real images as Img2Img guidance face a fundamental trade-off: maintaining class fidelity constrains the diversity of generated samples, and this limitation is particularly acute in full-dataset FGVC training, which remains under-explored compared to few-shot settings.

## Input / Output

For each real training image, the pipeline extracts a Canny edge map and pairs it with a GPT-4-generated prompt (from a pool of 100 per meta-class) and a subject reference image drawn from the same sub-class but a different source image. The generation model (ControlNet + BLIP-diffusion) produces M=2 synthetic images at 512×512 resolution per real image, which are then filtered via CLIP semantic relevance and top-k predictive confidence before being used to replace real samples during training with probability α (default 0.4, 0.1 for CUB).

## Method

SaSPA conditions diffusion-based generation on two abstract representations rather than the source image itself: (1) Canny edge maps provide structural conditioning via ControlNet, preserving object shape and layout while freeing surface-level details, and (2) BLIP-diffusion provides subject-level conditioning using a reference image from the same sub-class, ensuring correct texture, color, and fine-grained class characteristics. This dual conditioning decouples structural and semantic fidelity from any single real image, enabling the model to introduce novel backgrounds, lighting, and compositional variations while maintaining sub-class accuracy. GPT-4 generates diverse prompts per meta-class to further expand scene variety, and a two-stage filtering pipeline (CLIP semantic filtering for meta-class relevance, top-k confidence filtering with a dataset-trained model for sub-class correctness) removes low-quality samples. The method requires no fine-tuning of the generation model, distinguishing it from approaches like Diff-Mix that rely on Textual Inversion and DreamBooth per sub-class.

## Results

On full-dataset FGVC training, SaSPA achieves the highest test accuracy across all five benchmark datasets: 86.6% on Aircraft, 76.2% on CompCars, 93.8% on Stanford Cars, 83.2% on CUB-200-2011, and 71.9% on DTD. These results outperform the best traditional augmentation (CAL-Aug or RandAug+CutMix) by 0.7–3.6 points and the best generative baselines (Real Guidance, ALIA) by 1.1–3.6 points. In the concurrent diff-mix comparison at higher resolutions (448×448 ResNet50, 384×384 ViT-B/16), SaSPA+CutMix leads on Aircraft (90.79%) and Cars (95.34%) but trails diff-mix on CUB (87.14% vs. 87.56% with ResNet50).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SaSPA consistently outperforms all traditional augmentation baselines (CAL-Aug, RandAug, CutMix, and their combinations) across all five datasets, with the largest margin on CompCars (+3.6 pts over RandAug+CutMix) and the smallest on CUB (+0.7 pts over CAL-Aug). Among generative baselines, Real Guidance (He et al., ICLR 2023) and ALIA (Dunlap et al., NeurIPS 2023) both fail to deliver consistent improvements and sometimes degrade performance (e.g., ALIA drops to 83.1% on Aircraft vs. 84.9% for CAL-Aug). Against the concurrent diff-mix method (Wang et al., CVPR 2024), which requires per-class fine-tuning via Textual Inversion and DreamBooth, SaSPA wins on Aircraft and Cars across both ResNet50 and ViT-B/16 architectures without any fine-tuning, but underperforms on CUB with ResNet50 (86.92% vs. 87.16%) and both architectures with ViT-B/16, where CutMix alone is competitive. The paper also shows that SaSPA's advantage is most pronounced in few-shot settings (4–16 shots) and in mitigating contextual bias (OOD accuracy of 41.5% vs. 25.1% for ALIA on the Airbus-vs-Boeing split), where the diversity of generated samples provides the greatest benefit.

## Relation to Prior Work

SaSPA builds on the diffusion-model-based augmentation line initiated by Real Guidance (He et al., ICLR 2023) and ALIA (Dunlap et al., NeurIPS 2023), both of which use SDEdit/Img2Img with real images as guidance to preserve class fidelity. The key departure is replacing direct image conditioning with two abstract representations—Canny edge maps (via ControlNet) and subject embeddings (via BLIP-diffusion)—which decouples structural preservation from surface-level mimicry, thereby breaking the fidelity-diversity trade-off inherent in Img2Img approaches. Unlike the concurrent diff-mix (Wang et al., CVPR 2024), which fine-tunes the generator per sub-class via Textual Inversion and DreamBooth, SaSPA operates zero-shot without any fine-tuning, trading some per-class specialization for simplicity and lower compute cost.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first systematic evaluation of generative augmentation for full-dataset FGVC training (as opposed to the more common few-shot or contextual-bias settings), and its finding that higher FID (i.e., augmentations that look *less* like the real data) correlates with better downstream accuracy challenges the implicit assumption that augmentation should closely mimic the source distribution. The observed relationship between real-data fraction and optimal synthetic-data ratio (α) offers a practical guideline for practitioners. Open questions include whether the method extends to non-rigid objects at higher resolutions (CUB underperforms at 448×448), whether temporal consistency can be maintained for video-based FGVC, and whether the LLM-dependent prompt generation degrades for obscure meta-classes.
