## Problem

Event cameras produce temporal data governed by biological neural principles (spiking, membrane potential dynamics), yet existing deep learning models process this data with generic architectures (CNNs, MLPs) that ignore the underlying temporal structure. The paper asks whether explicitly encoding the Leaky Integrate-and-Fire membrane potential as a learnable parametric function within the network architecture can outperform task-specific modeling approaches on event-based vision tasks.

## Input / Output

Each PPLN node takes a k-dimensional spatial feature vector x and a normalized timestamp t ∈ [0,1], and outputs a scalar membrane potential evaluated at t. In practice, the full network consumes task-specific inputs: a blurry image concatenated with event histograms (deblurring, outputting 14 sharp frames), 10 conventional frames plus 10× event slices (steering, outputting angles at 10 timestamps), or event histograms from two cameras (pose, outputting 2D joint heatmaps at 10 timestamps that are projected to 3D). The timestamp t is the key distinguishing input: it is not a channel or spatial dimension but a continuous query variable that selects where on the predicted piecewise linear curve to evaluate.

## Method

A PPLN node predicts n piecewise linear segments (slopes m, intercepts b, and interval sizes s via softmax) from the input x using small trainable matrices, then evaluates the resulting membrane potential function at the query timestamp t. Two operators ensure trainability: a temperature-based smoothing that blends adjacent segments so gradients with respect to segment endpoints are non-zero, and an integral normalization that subtracts the temporal mean and adds a learned offset, making gradients with respect to all slopes and intercepts dense rather than sparse one-hot vectors. The architecture supports convolution by restricting the coefficient-prediction weights to local receptive fields, enabling drop-in replacement of standard conv/MLP layers within U-Net, FCN, and other backbones. A convergence theorem (Theorem 3.1) guarantees that under uniform sampling and sufficient temperature, the learned coefficients recover the true piecewise linear function up to O(max noise) error. All experiments use n=3 segments, and the PPLN is trained end-to-end with standard optimizers (Adam or RMSProp) and L1/L2 losses.

## Results

On the HQF motion deblurring benchmark, PPLN achieves MSE 0.152, PSNR 24.284 dB, and SSIM 0.728, outperforming the prior state-of-the-art DeblurSR by 5.6% in MSE, 0.372 dB in PSNR, and 4.9% in SSIM. On DDD20 steering prediction, PPLN reduces overall RMSE from 4.55° (Hu) to 3.15° (a 30.8% improvement) and increases EVA from 0.845 to 0.926. On DHP19 human pose estimation, PPLN achieves 3D MPJPE of 73.05 mm, a 11.1% improvement over the Calabrese baseline (82.17 mm).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PPLN outperforms all seven compared baselines across all three tasks. In motion deblurring, PPLN beats the closest competitor DeblurSR (Song et al., 2024) by 5.6% in MSE and 0.372 dB in PSNR, while the gap over earlier methods is much larger: E-CIR (Song et al., 2022) is 26.6% worse in MSE, eSL-Net++ (Pan et al., 2020) is 60.5% worse, and eSL-Net (Wang et al., 2020) is 197% worse. In steering prediction, PPLN improves over Hu et al. (2020) by 30.8% in overall RMSE (3.15° vs. 4.55°) and 9.6% in EVA; even when the baseline is modified to match PPLN's input/output dimensions (HuMod), PPLN still wins by 5.7% RMSE. In human pose estimation, PPLN reduces 3D MPJPE from 82.17 mm (Calabrese et al., 2019) to 73.05 mm (11.1% improvement), and outperforms the modified baseline (CalabreseMod, 130.24 mm) by 44.0%. Notably, PPLN is a generic architecture without task-specific inductive biases, whereas baselines like eSL-Net use dictionary learning, E-CIR uses per-pixel polynomial approximation, and DeblurSR uses implicit neural representation.

## Relation to Prior Work

PPLNs sit at the intersection of two recent trends: bio-inspired temporal modeling (Leaky Integrate-and-Fire neurons, Spiking Neural Networks) and learnable parametric function networks (Kolmogorov-Arnold Networks, Piecewise Linear Units). The key departure from SNNs is that PPLNs operate with real-valued membrane potentials on GPU rather than binary spikes on neuromorphic hardware, positioning them as a drop-in replacement for standard temporal inference layers. Unlike KANs, which use input-independent B-splines, PPLNs predict input-dependent piecewise linear coefficients at inference time, enabling the network to adapt its temporal function shape to each input. The architecture is applied by replacing convolutional and MLP layers in existing event-based vision pipelines (U-Net for deblurring, FCN for pose estimation, CNN+linear for steering).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a generic bio-inspired temporal architecture can outperform task-specific modeling (dictionary learning, per-pixel polynomials, implicit neural representations) across multiple event-based vision tasks, suggesting that architectural alignment with the data-generating process (neural activity) is more important than task-specific inductive biases. The integral normalization operator is a practical contribution that addresses a fundamental gradient sparsity problem in piecewise linear temporal models, with a convergence theorem providing theoretical grounding. Open questions include whether the fixed segment count n can be made dynamic (the authors plan recurrent prediction), whether more accurate membrane potential modeling (refractory periods, nonlinear decay) would further improve performance, and whether the approach generalizes beyond event-based vision to other temporal domains. The hyperparameter n currently requires manual tuning without theoretical guidance.
