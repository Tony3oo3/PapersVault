## Problem

Sum-based aggregators in MPGNNs have provable separation guarantees but underperform in practice compared to more complex aggregation schemes. The paper identifies the root cause: sum-based aggregators cannot 'mix' features belonging to distinct neighbors, as the cross-derivative between any two neighbor inputs is exactly zero, preventing the capture of joint topological or feature relationships that many downstream tasks require.

## Input / Output

SSMA is a plug-and-play aggregation module that takes a set of n neighbor feature vectors (each d-dimensional) and produces a single d-dimensional compressed representation. Internally, it encodes the neighbors as a 2D discrete signal of size (n+1)×(n(d−1)+1), yielding a representation of O(n²d) dimensions before compression. For scalability on dense neighborhoods, it optionally reduces the effective neighborhood to κ neighbors via random sampling or GAT-style attention slots before performing the convolution.

## Method

SSMA encodes each neighbor's d-dimensional feature vector as a univariate polynomial in z, then constructs a generalized DeepSets polynomial p(t,z) = ∏ᵢ(t − Enc(Xᵢ)) in two variables. The coefficients of this polynomial are computed by sequentially applying 2D circular convolution across the neighbor axis, which is implemented efficiently via 2D FFT, pointwise multiplication in the frequency domain, and 2D IFFT. This convolutional structure inherently produces higher-order cross-terms between distinct neighbors, achieving a provable neighbor-mixing property that sum-based aggregators lack. The resulting 2D coefficient matrix is compressed back to d dimensions through a low-rank linear layer (two consecutive linear layers with a bottleneck), and geometric-mean normalization of the frequency-domain product stabilizes optimization. The construction provides a polynomial-size O(n²d) separating invariant for vector multisets, resolving an open question about efficiently generalizing the DeepSets polynomial to multidimensional features.

## Results

SSMA integrated into standard MPGNN architectures achieves consistent improvements across all benchmarks. On the TU datasets, the largest relative gains appear on IMDB-B (17.7% improvement, e.g., GAT+SSMA reaches 74.5% vs. GAT's 51.0%) and ZINC (20.4% MAE reduction, e.g., GIN+SSMA achieves 0.222 vs. GIN's 0.252). On OGBN-Products, the most challenging dense-neighborhood setting, SSMA yields up to 23.9% relative improvement (e.g., GraphGPS+SSMA reaches 67.62% vs. GraphGPS's 48.89%). On LRGB Peptides-func, SSMA improves AP by 3.02% (e.g., GCN+SSMA: 63.3 vs. GCN: 61.1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SSMA is evaluated as a plug-and-play replacement for the aggregation module in seven MPGNN architectures (GCN, GAT, GATv2, GIN, GraphGPS, PNA, ESAN), all of which use sum-based aggregation by default. Across every architecture and every benchmark, SSMA strictly outperforms the corresponding sum-aggregation baseline, with the largest gains on featureless topological data (IMDB-B: +17.7% relative) and molecular regression (ZINC: +20.4% relative MAE reduction). Against the variance-preserving aggregation (VPA) [39], SSMA wins on all nine TU datasets tested (e.g., GAT+SSMA: 78.6% on IMDB-B vs. GAT+VPA: 71.1%; GIN+SSMA: 87.7% on MUTAG vs. GIN+VPA: 86.7%). Against the generalised f-mean aggregation [25], which parameterizes a space encompassing sum, mean, min, and max, SSMA again dominates across all architectures and datasets (e.g., GIN+SSMA: 90.5% on MUTAG vs. GIN+f-mean: 70.9%; GraphGPS+SSMA: 86.1% on MUTAG vs. GraphGPS+f-mean: 55.7%). On the dense-neighborhood OGBN-Products benchmark, SSMA's attentional neighbor selection proves critical: GraphGPS+SSMA reaches 67.62% accuracy versus 48.89% for vanilla GraphGPS, a 23.9% relative improvement. The ESAN+SSMA combination achieves the strongest absolute results (96.3% on MUTAG, 80.6% on IMDB-B, 0.096 MAE on ZINC), confirming that SSMA composes well with subgraph-based architectures.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the DeepSets framework (Zaheer et al., 2017), which established sum-based aggregation as a provably separating multiset representation, and on subsequent work tightening the representation size bounds (Dym & Gortler, Amir et al.). The key conceptual departure is identifying 'neighbor-mixing'—the ability to capture joint interactions between distinct neighbors—as a property that sum-based aggregators fundamentally lack (their cross-neighbor second derivative is zero), despite having full separation guarantees. SSMA reframes the DeepSets polynomial through the lens of 2D circular convolution, using ring-theoretic tools (UFDs, Gauss's lemma) to prove that sequentially convolving encoded neighbor features yields a separating invariant for vector multisets with O(n²d) size, thereby answering an open question about efficiently generalizing the DeepSets polynomial to vector features.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a concrete theoretical explanation for a long-standing practical gap: why sum-based aggregators with provable separation underperform more complex aggregators in practice. SSMA's plug-and-play design means it can be dropped into any existing MPGNN (GCN, GAT, GIN, GraphGPS, PNA, ESAN) without architectural changes, and the consistent gains across TU, OGB, LRGB, and ZINC benchmarks suggest broad applicability. Key limitations include the O(n²d) representation size scaling quadratically with neighborhood size (mitigated by neighbor selection), the need for explicit normalization to stabilize the product-based convolution, and the fact that bi-Lipschitz stability remains unattainable for any differentiable invariant representation. Open questions include whether the quadratic scaling can be reduced and whether the convolutional mixing can be made more stable without ad-hoc normalization.
