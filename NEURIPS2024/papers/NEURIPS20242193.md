## Problem

Existing generative models (DDPMs, GANs) can learn rich visual representations, but extracting segmentation from them requires additional supervision, fine-tuning, or post-hoc analysis of intermediate features. No prior method learns meaningful spatial partitions purely as a byproduct of training a generative model from scratch with only a denoising objective, without any mask priors, extra loss terms, or labeled data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242193/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

During training, the model consumes noised RGB images (x_t at 64×64 or 128×128) and predicts the added Gaussian noise. For real-image segmentation, a clean image is noised to a fixed level (t=30) and passed through a single forward pass, yielding K soft region masks (typically K=3) as the segmentation output. For generation, pure Gaussian noise is iteratively denoised over T=1000 steps, producing both a synthetic image and its corresponding region masks simultaneously. On ImageNet, a class label is additionally injected as a conditioning signal.

## Method

The paper restructures the standard DDPM U-Net into a factorized architecture with three key components: an Encoder (first half of U-Net), a shared Middle Block, and a Mask Generator (decoder-style subnetwork) that produces K soft region masks via softmax. The critical design is parallel decoding: K weight-shared Decoder branches each receive the shared latent h_mid and a masked view of encoder features (h_enc ⊙ m_k), denoising only their assigned region, with outputs combined as ε̂ = Σ ε̂_k ⊙ m_k. The entire system is trained end-to-end with the standard DDPM noise-prediction loss alone—no mask regularization, no segmentation labels, no auxiliary objectives. Weight sharing across decoder branches is essential (ablations show separate decoders or concatenation-based conditioning degrade both segmentation quality and FID). The structured bottleneck forces the network to discover interpretable spatial partitions because the only way to reduce denoising error under the parallel-decoding constraint is to assign coherent regions to each branch.

## Results

On unsupervised segmentation, the method achieves 90.1% Acc / 79.7 IOU / 87.2 DICE on Flower and 89.6% Acc / 56.1 IOU / 69.4 DICE on CUB, outperforming all prior unsupervised baselines. On CelebAMask-HQ it reaches 87.9% Acc and 80.3 mIOU (vs. 95.7/90.2 for a supervised U-Net), and on ImageNet 80.7% Acc / 67.7 mIOU. For image generation, the factorized architecture consistently improves FID over standard DDPM across all datasets (e.g., CUB-128: 14.01→10.28; ImageNet-64: 7.02→6.54) while simultaneously producing masks with higher pixel accuracy (e.g., ImageNet-64: 71.2→84.1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On Flower, the method (90.1 Acc / 79.7 IOU / 87.2 DICE) outperforms GrabCut (82.0/69.2/79.1), ReDO (87.9/76.4/–), IEM (88.3/76.8/84.6), and IEM+SegNet (89.6/78.9/86.0) on all three metrics. On CUB, it similarly beats GrabCut (72.3/36.0/48.7), PerturbGAN (–/38.0/–), ReDO (84.5/42.6/–), IEM (88.6/52.2/66.0), and IEM+SegNet (89.3/55.1/68.7), with the largest margin over PerturbGAN in IOU (56.1 vs. 38.0). Against DatasetDDPM-unsup (K-means on pre-trained DDPM features), the method gains substantial margins: +9.4 Acc on CelebA (87.9 vs. 78.5) and +6.6 Acc on ImageNet (80.7 vs. 74.1), while remaining within ~8 Acc of a fully supervised U-Net. For generation quality, the factorized architecture improves FID over the standard DDPM U-Net by 2.5–3.7 points across all seven dataset/resolution configurations, and simultaneously produces masks with 6–13 points higher pixel accuracy than the two-stage DDPM+DatasetDDPM-unsup pipeline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper restructures the standard DDPM U-Net [27] into a factorized architecture with a mask generator and parallel weight-shared per-region decoders, trained end-to-end with only the denoising objective. It departs from prior diffusion-based representation learning (DatasetDDPM [2], DiffAE [48], PADE [70]) which extract features post-hoc from pre-trained models, and from generative segmentation methods (PerturbGAN [4], ReDO [9]) that require predefined object classes or auxiliary loss terms. The key conceptual departure is using the architecture itself as a constraint—rather than adding regularization or fine-tuning—so that interpretable region partitions emerge as a necessary byproduct of learning to denoise.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates a general principle: carefully designed architectural bottlenecks can force a generative model to learn interpretable representations without any task-specific supervision, potentially applicable beyond segmentation. However, the current scope is limited to 2–3 class partitions on relatively simple scenes (faces, flowers, birds), and the hierarchical extension remains a preliminary proof of concept on a toy 3D-shape dataset. Open questions include whether this factorization paradigm scales to complex multi-object scenes with many semantic regions, whether it generalizes to other modalities, and whether the generation-quality improvement from parallel per-region decoding persists at larger model scales.
