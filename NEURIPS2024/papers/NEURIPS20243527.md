## Problem

Despite significant progress in federated learning of large language models (FedLLM), no realistic benchmark exists for evaluating FL methods in real-world scenarios. Previous works rely on artificially partitioning centralized datasets, which fails to capture natural properties of cross-user data such as diverse languages, varying data quality and quantity, heterogeneous sequence lengths, and individual user preferences. This makes fair comparison and practical evaluation of FedLLM methods extremely difficult.

## Input / Output

The benchmark operates on text instruction-response pairs for instruction tuning and instruction-preferred/dispreferred response triples for preference alignment. Datasets are naturally split by real user IDs (ranging from 38 to 747 clients), with each client contributing a variable number of samples (e.g., 13±21 to 527±477 per client). Evaluation produces scores across 6 metrics: four open-ended (MT-Bench, Vicuna, AdvBench safety rate, and Ref-GPT4 in-domain scoring) and two close-ended (MMLU knowledge, HumanEval code generation), ensuring both task-specific and general capability assessment.

## Method

FedLLM-Bench introduces four naturally user-split datasets covering two post-training tasks: three for federated instruction tuning (Fed-Aya for multilingual collaboration across 8 languages, Fed-ChatbotIT for single-turn chat, Fed-WildChat for multi-turn chat) and one for federated preference alignment (Fed-ChatbotPA). The benchmark integrates 8 FL baseline methods (FedAvg, FedProx, SCAFFOLD, FedAvgM, FedYogi, FedAdagrad, FedAdam, and local training) using LoRA-based parameter-efficient fine-tuning on Llama-2-7B (IT) and Alpaca-7B (PA) with 8-bit quantization. Key empirical findings include: FL consistently outperforms local training, FedAvg emerges as a robust default for multi-turn settings, and collaboration among similar-language clients (FedSimLang) can outperform full multilingual collaboration on specific languages. The benchmark also provides the first demonstration of user-level differential privacy in FedLLM, showing that (1, 1e-4)-DP FedAvg retains comparable performance to non-private training.

## Results

Across all four FedLLM-Bench datasets, federated learning methods consistently outperform local training without collaboration. On Fed-WildChat (single-turn), FedAvg achieves 4.81 MT-Bench-1, 7.99 Vicuna, and 5.88 Ref-GPT4 versus local training's 4.15, 7.03, and 4.50; on multi-turn, FedAvg reaches 3.99 MT-Bench and 5.86 Ref-GPT4 vs. local's 3.27 and 4.68. On the multilingual Fed-Aya dataset, FedAvg and FedProx lead with average Ref-GPT4 scores of 4.90 and 4.92 (vs. best local at 4.66), though no single FL method dominates all 8 languages. On Fed-ChatbotPA (preference alignment), FedAvgM achieves the highest average open-ended score of 5.79 and FedAvg/SCAFFOLD lead on safety (AdvBench 16.2/16.0 vs. local 11.0).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper benchmarks 7 FL methods against local training (no collaboration) as the baseline. FedAvg [7] emerges as the most robust method, achieving the best or near-best scores across nearly all metrics on Fed-WildChat (both single- and multi-turn) and Fed-Aya, and is competitive on Fed-ChatbotIT and Fed-ChatbotPA. FedProx [26] and SCAFFOLD [27] (client-side methods) are consistently competitive but do not surpass FedAvg on the multi-turn setting, suggesting that pre-trained initialization and LoRA reduce the benefit of gradient correction. Among server-side adaptive methods from [32], FedAdagrad leads on Fed-ChatbotIT (5.48 avg open-ended) and FedAdam leads on unfiltered Fed-WildChat (6.32 avg) and Mistral-7B experiments (7.55 avg), while FedAvgM [31] is best on Fed-ChatbotPA (5.79 avg). Notably, no single FL method dominates across all datasets and metrics, and all FL methods preserve closed-ended capabilities (MMLU, HumanEval) comparable to local training, confirming that collaboration does not compromise pre-trained knowledge.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by providing the first realistic benchmark for federated LLM post-training, departing from prior FedLLM works (OpenFedLLM, FederatedScope-LLM, FedML-LLM) that all relied on artificially partitioned centralized datasets (split by task type, coding language, or web domain). Conceptually, it extends the lineage of realistic FL benchmarks like LEAF, FLAIR, and FLamby—originally designed for image and text classification—to the LLM post-training setting, using naturally user-split data from public sources (Aya, WildChat, Chatbot-Arena) rather than synthetic heterogeneity. The paper repurposes standard FL optimization methods (FedAvg, FedProx, SCAFFOLD, adaptive server-side optimizers) as baselines within a unified evaluation protocol, rather than proposing a new training algorithm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FedLLM-Bench addresses a critical infrastructure gap: without realistic, naturally-split datasets, the FedLLM community cannot fairly compare methods or validate claims about handling real-world heterogeneity (language, quality, quantity, length, preference). The exploratory findings—particularly that simple FedAvg is remarkably robust in multi-turn settings and that cross-language collaboration yields mixed per-language benefits—open concrete research directions on language personalization and collaboration structure design. Key limitations include testing only 7B-scale models, absence of error bars due to compute constraints, and incomplete coverage of safety alignment, leaving open whether conclusions generalize to larger models or more adversarial settings.
