## Problem

Model editing has seen significant progress for large language models, but effective strategies for editing vision Transformers (ViTs) in computer vision remain largely untapped. The core challenge is correcting predictive errors in pre-trained ViTs—particularly those arising from subpopulation shifts—without costly retraining, while simultaneously balancing generalization to neighboring failures and locality to avoid unintended effects on unrelated examples.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20244271/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At training time, the hypernetwork is meta-trained on CutMix-augmented pairs (a 224×224 natural image from ImageNet-1k paired with its CutMix version, where a 48×48 to 128×128 patch from an irrelevant image is overlaid), requiring no real editing data. At test time, a single editing sample (image + ground-truth label) is fed through the frozen ViT to extract [cls]-token features, which the hypernetwork maps to a structured binary mask of dimension N_m × 6 (covering six FC layers across the 8th–10th FFNs). The mask is then applied via Hadamard product during a few steps of gradient descent to produce the updated ViT parameters.

## Method

The paper proposes a locate-then-edit framework that prioritizes learning *where* to edit over *how* to edit. A hypernetwork (five attention blocks mirroring the base ViT architecture, with six learnable tokens corresponding to the six target FC layers) is meta-trained via bi-level optimization: the inner loop performs T=5 steps of gradient descent on masked parameters to minimize a KL-divergence reliability loss, while the outer loop refines the hypernetwork to produce sparser, more targeted masks. To avoid needing real editing samples, the hypernetwork is trained solely on CutMix pseudo-samples where the optimization objective aligns the CutMix sample's prediction distribution to the original image's. Binarization is approximated by a fixed sigmoid (k=10) during training for gradient flow, and replaced by a hard threshold ρ at test time, which directly controls the generalization–locality trade-off. A decoupling trick introduces an auxiliary variable to separate the hypernetwork's gradient pathway from the inner-loop computation, reducing memory and compute costs.

## Results

On the proposed editing benchmark (18 groups of subpopulation-shifted images), the proposed method achieves a near-100% success rate (SR) for single-example editing, consistent with most competing methods. The key differentiator is the GR-LR Pareto front: the proposed method achieves the best trade-off between generalization rate and locality rate across all sparsity levels (0.25–0.95, corresponding to 12.4%–0.83% of total model parameters), without relying on previously trained data to enforce locality. Ablations confirm that the hypernetwork-identified binary masks substantially outperform random masks at sparsity below 1.65% of parameters, and that mask IoU is high within the same failure group but low across groups, validating parameter specificity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method dominates the GR-LR Pareto front over all eight baselines on both ViT-B/16 and ViT-S/16. T-Patcher [29] and ROME [38] maintain locality by explicitly using previously seen data, but T-Patcher shows high generalization variability across editing samples and ROME (designed for autoregressive LLMs) generalizes poorly to ViTs. LoRA [28] preserves locality through low-rank updates but fails to generalize. KnowledgeEditor (KE) [9] and MEND [40] exhibit low locality on MAD-searched natural images and poor generalization to AI-generated images. FT-ℓ2 [39], KN [8], and SPT [23] all show that increasing parameter sparsity improves locality at the expense of generalization, but none match the Pareto front of the proposed method, which attributes its advantage to the meta-learned where-to-edit localization rather than sparsity alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the model editing paradigm from NLP (ROME, MEMIT, MEND, KnowledgeEditor) to Vision Transformers, where prior work was limited to CNN-based classifiers (Santurkar et al.) or GAN rewriting (Bau et al.). It departs from the dominant NLP editing strategy of prioritizing 'how-to-edit' (hypernetworks generating parameter updates) by instead meta-learning a hypernetwork to solve 'where-to-edit' — generating sparse binary masks over FFN parameters via a MAML-style bi-level optimization. The pseudo-sample generation via CutMix is a notable departure from prior editing methods that require curated editing datasets, and the structured tuning over intermediate FFN dimensions (rather than full parameter updates) reduces the search space while preserving editability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first systematic model editing method for ViTs and introduces a dedicated benchmark with subpopulation shifts (natural underrepresented images via MAD competition, and AI-generated images via Textural Inversion/PUG), filling a gap where CV model editing had no standardized evaluation. The finding that a simple CutMix-based pseudo-sample strategy rivals the much more expensive PGD adversarial attack for training the localization hypernetwork is practically significant for scalability. Open questions include why CutMix suffices for simulating real-world distribution shifts, whether the approach extends to other architectures (Swin, CNNs) and tasks (dense prediction, multimodal LLMs), and how to handle batch editing without prohibitive compute.
