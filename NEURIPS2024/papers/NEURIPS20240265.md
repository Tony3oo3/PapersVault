## Problem

Existing federated learning algorithms under non-i.i.d. client participation either require strong assumptions (e.g., all clients participate within a bounded window), fail to achieve linear speedup with reduced communication, or are limited to convex objectives. No prior method simultaneously provides reduced communication cost, linear speedup in the number of clients, and resilience to data heterogeneity for general non-convex optimization under periodic participation patterns such as cyclic availability.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes local stochastic gradient samples from N clients whose availability follows a periodic pattern (cyclic groups rotating each round, or regularized windows of P rounds), along with per-round participation weights q_r^i normalized to sum to one. It produces an ε-stationary point of the global non-convex objective f(x) = (1/N)Σf_i(x) after R communication rounds, with each client performing I local SGD steps per round. Experiments validate on image classification (Fashion-MNIST with logistic regression, CIFAR-10 with a two-layer CNN) using N=250 clients partitioned into K̄ groups with S clients sampled per round.

## Method

Amplified SCAFFOLD combines two mechanisms: (1) amplified updates, where weighted local model deltas are accumulated over a window of P rounds and applied as a single scaled global update every P rounds, and (2) long-range control variates, where SCAFFOLD-style correction terms are computed from gradients averaged across entire P-round windows rather than per-round, ensuring equal client representation in expectation. The key analytical contribution is a fine-grained treatment that takes expectation jointly over both client participation and stochastic gradient noise (rather than conditioning on participation as in prior work), enabling exploitation of the unbiased-participation property to reduce communication. Controlling control variate errors under non-i.i.d. participation requires bounding a nested non-uniform average of non-uniform averages of error terms, handled via mild regularity conditions on the participation pattern. For cyclic participation, this yields O(ε^{-2}) communication complexity to find an ε-stationary point, improving over the O(κ²ε^{-4}) of prior work while remaining independent of the data heterogeneity parameter κ.

## Results

For cyclic participation with N clients, S participating per round, and K̄ groups, Amplified SCAFFOLD achieves O(LK̄(N/S)ε⁻²) communication rounds to find an ε-stationary point, improving over the prior best O(κ²ε⁻⁴) of Amplified FedAvg. On the synthetic objective, Amplified SCAFFOLD requires 800 communication rounds to reach objective value 0.2, versus 1900 for SCAFFOLD and 4800 for both FedAvg and Amplified FedAvg. On Fashion-MNIST (N=250, S=10, K̄=5, s=5%), Amplified SCAFFOLD is robust to extreme data heterogeneity, with test accuracy dropping only from 84.6% (s=100%) to 84.45% (s=2.5%), while all baselines suffer larger degradation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Amplified SCAFFOLD outperforms all baselines in every experimental setting (synthetic, Fashion-MNIST, CIFAR-10). On the synthetic objective, it requires 800 rounds to reach objective 0.2, compared to 1900 for SCAFFOLD and 4800 for both FedAvg and Amplified FedAvg. SCAFFOLD, despite lacking theoretical guarantees under non-i.i.d. participation, is the strongest baseline and is competitive on Fashion-MNIST, though still significantly slower than Amplified SCAFFOLD on the synthetic objective and CIFAR-10. Amplified FedAvg [39], the only prior method with theoretical guarantees for non-i.i.d. participation, performs comparably to plain FedAvg in practice (contrary to its original paper's claims, which used pretraining), and suffers from data heterogeneity. In the ablation study, Amplified SCAFFOLD maintains near-constant test accuracy (84.6%→84.45%) as data heterogeneity increases from s=100% to s=2.5%, while FedAvg, FedProx, and Amplified FedAvg all degrade noticeably. Additional baselines (FedAdam, FedYogi, FedAvg-M) in Appendix E also underperform Amplified SCAFFOLD under periodic participation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper combines two previously separate ideas: amplified updates from Amplified FedAvg [39] (which handles arbitrary client participation but incurs ε⁻⁴ communication cost and κ² heterogeneity dependence) and SCAFFOLD-style control variates [17] (which eliminate heterogeneity but require i.i.d. participation). The key departure is a tighter convergence analysis that takes expectation over both stochastic gradient noise and client participation simultaneously, rather than conditioning on participation as in [39], which enables the ε⁻² communication bound. The paper also extends the analysis of control variate errors to non-i.i.d. participation via a recursion over P-round windows, handling a nested non-uniform average that has no analogue in the i.i.d. setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first algorithm to simultaneously achieve reduced communication (ε⁻²), linear speedup, and heterogeneity resilience under periodic (non-i.i.d.) client participation for general non-convex objectives, reducing the prior best cyclic-participation bound from O(κ²ε⁻⁴) to O(ε⁻²). A notable limitation is that the framework requires well-defined participation windows with equal expected participation across clients, which may not hold when devices freely join or leave during training. The experimental finding that Amplified FedAvg performs comparably to plain FedAvg when training from scratch (contradicting its original paper's fine-tuning-based evaluation) raises questions about the practical utility of amplification without variance reduction.
