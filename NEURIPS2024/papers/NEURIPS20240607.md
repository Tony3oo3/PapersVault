## Problem

Existing AI benchmarks for scientific reasoning are predominantly knowledge-intensive, text-only, or limited to one or two disciplines, making them insufficient for evaluating the complex, interdisciplinary cognitive reasoning required for scientific discovery. No prior benchmark simultaneously covers Olympic-level difficulty across seven disciplines, supports interleaved multimodal inputs, provides fine-grained process-level evaluation of reasoning steps, and includes systematic data leakage detection.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240607/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark consumes 11,163 bilingual (English/Chinese) problems from 62 Olympic competitions, presented in three modalities: text-only, interleaved text-image sequences (4,960 problems with 7,571 images across 5 types), and image-caption (where InternVL-Chat-V1.5 generates textual descriptions replacing images). Problems span 13 answer types ranging from single-choice to code generation, with average problem length of 244.8 tokens and average solution length of 417.1 tokens. Outputs include answer-level accuracy (via rule-based matching with SymPy for algebraic equivalence and unbiased pass@1 with n=5 for code), process-level scores (per-step 0/1 correctness averaged across steps), and fine-grained scores across 8 logical and 5 visual reasoning ability categories.

## Method

OlympicArena is constructed by collecting PDFs from 62 competitions, converting them to markdown via Mathpix, and annotating ~30 students through a custom Streamlit interface with embedded rule-based validation (e.g., flagging variables in numerical answers, missing equals signs in equations). GPT-4V serves as an annotator for three difficulty tiers (knowledge recall, concept application, cognitive reasoning) and for tagging each problem with relevant logical reasoning abilities (deductive, inductive, abductive, analogical, cause-and-effect, critical thinking, decompositional, quantitative) and visual reasoning abilities (pattern recognition, spatial, diagrammatic, symbol interpretation, comparative visualization). Evaluation combines rule-based matching (SymPy for expressions/equations, interval/set/tuple comparison, pass@k for code) with GPT-4V model-based judging for open-ended 'others' answers (~5% of problems, validated at ~80% human agreement). Process-level evaluation reformats both gold and model solutions into structured steps via GPT-4, then GPT-4V scores each step 0 or 1 against the reference, achieving 83% inter-annotator agreement with human judgments. Data leakage is detected using the N-gram Prediction Accuracy metric (5-gram, multiple starting points, edit distance and ROUGE-L thresholds at 75%) applied to base and chat model variants.

## Results

On OlympicArena (11,163 problems across 7 disciplines and 62 competitions), GPT-4o achieves the highest overall accuracy at 39.97% in the multimodal setting, 38.17% in text-only, and 38.50% with image captions. Mathematics (28.67%) and Physics (29.71%) are the most challenging subjects for GPT-4o, while Biology (52.18%) and Geography (56.23%) are relatively easier. Open-source LMMs such as LLaVA-NeXT-34B (17.38%), InternVL-Chat-V1.5 (16.63%), and Qwen-VL-Chat (6.90%) struggle to reach 20% overall accuracy. Process-level evaluation reveals that GPT-4o scores 62.3% on step correctness, substantially higher than its answer-level accuracy, indicating untapped reasoning potential.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GPT-4o dominates across all three settings (multimodal, image-caption, text-only), achieving 39.97%, 38.50%, and 38.17% overall accuracy respectively, outperforming GPT-4V (32.76% LMM) by ~7.2 points and GPT-4 (32.37% LLM) by ~7.6 points. Among open-source models, Qwen1.5-32B-Chat (23.69% LLM) and LLaVA-NeXT-34B (17.38% LMM) lead, but all remain below 25% overall, trailing GPT-4o by over 15 points. Claude3 Sonnet shows minimal gain from adding vision (23.02% → 23.13%), while Gemini Pro Vision (20.97%) and Qwen-VL-Max (20.65%) hover around 21%. Notably, most LMMs do not outperform their text-only counterparts on multimodal problems, with GPT-4o being the primary exception (39.97% vs. 38.17%), suggesting that current LMMs still struggle to effectively leverage interleaved visual information for complex cognitive reasoning. In the CS (code generation) sub-task, even GPT-4o achieves only 9.00% pass@1, while several open-source models score 0%, highlighting a severe gap in algorithmic reasoning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of multidisciplinary AI benchmarking (MMLU, C-Eval, GPQA, SciBench) by shifting the focus from knowledge-intensive and concept-application problems to Olympic-level cognitive reasoning across seven disciplines. It departs from the concurrent OlympiadBench [17] by covering all seven scientific fields (not just math and physics), adding 13 answer types, bilingual support, and—critically—process-level evaluation that prior benchmarks lack. The fine-grained annotation of 8 logical and 5 visual reasoning abilities builds on cognitive science taxonomies [16, 43] but applies them systematically to a large-scale benchmark for the first time, and the N-gram-based data leakage detection [54] is integrated directly into the benchmark design rather than treated as an afterthought.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark provides a rigorous, leakage-validated yardstick for tracking progress toward superintelligent AI in scientific reasoning, with the finding that even GPT-4o plateaus at ~40% overall accuracy underscoring a substantial gap. The process-level evaluation reveals that models often execute intermediate reasoning steps correctly despite producing wrong final answers, suggesting significant untapped potential and pointing toward targeted improvements in long-chain reasoning reliability. A key open question is why LMMs fail to leverage interleaved visual information for complex reasoning—many show no improvement (or even degradation) over their text-only counterparts—raising fundamental questions about how visual and textual modalities are integrated in current architectures. The benchmark also opens the door to studying whether training strategies (e.g., process supervision, RL on intermediate steps) can close the gap between step-level competence and end-to-end correctness.
