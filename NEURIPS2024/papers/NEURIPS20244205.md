## Problem

Tractable counterfactual estimation in general settings—where exogenous variables can be discrete or continuous, and expressions involve arbitrary finite numbers of hypothetical worlds—remains challenging even when the SCM is fully specified. Existing approaches either require restrictive structural assumptions (back door, front door, instrumental variables), rely on rejection sampling that lacks scalability (e.g., NCM), or are limited to fully observed endogenous variables. This paper addresses the gap by providing a general-purpose, efficient importance sampling framework that does not depend on specific SCM structure.

## Input / Output

The method requires a partially specified SCM where the exogenous distribution P_U is sampleable and computable, and the causal mechanisms F_x are evaluable as black boxes (e.g., neural networks in proxy SCMs). Counterfactual conditions y_* encode a set of 4-tuples ⟨Y, y, X, x⟩ specifying observed variables, their values, intervened variables, and their values across potentially multiple submodels. The output is an unbiased estimate of counterfactual probabilities P(Y_* ∈ Y_*), which can be further used to estimate counterfactual densities (via small cubes δ_l(y_*)) and counterfactual effects (ATE, ETT, NDE, CtfDE). The method handles both discrete and continuous exogenous spaces, with the continuous case using probability density functions and the discrete case using probability mass functions.

## Method

The key insight is to replace a single proposal distribution Q_U with a conditional proposal Q_{U|y_*} and derive a shared variance upper bound (Theorem 1) across all counterfactual events, which reduces to minimizing an expected log-likelihood objective (Eq. 8) amenable to gradient-based optimization. This transforms the variance minimization problem into a conditional distribution learning problem: a neural network (MLP encoder h with attention-based aggregator g) maps counterfactual conditions y_* to parameters θ_{y_*} of the proposal distribution, which can be instantiated as GMM, MAF, NICE, or SOSPF. The authors further inject counterfactual Markov boundaries—computed via d-separation on augmented graphs—as weight masks into the encoder, ensuring each exogenous variable's parameters depend only on its relevant counterfactual information. At inference, a multiple importance sampling estimator (Eq. 9) averages over sampled conditions y_* for additional robustness, and the entire pipeline requires only one training run to support estimation of any finite collection of counterfactual probability terms in an L3 expression.

## Results

On the main comparison (Table 1), EXOM[MAF] achieves ESP of 0.581 and FR of 0.005 on SIMPSON-NLIN (|s|=1), compared to 0.008/0.971 for Rejection Sampling, 0.037/0.809 for CEIS, and 0.008/0.961 for NIS. As the number of submodels increases to |s|=3 and |s|=5, all three baselines completely fail on continuous SCMs (ESP=0.000, FR=1.000), while EXOM[MAF] maintains ESP of 0.606–0.698 and FR of 0.031–0.075 on SIMPSON-NLIN. On the discrete FAIRNESS-XW SCM, EXOM[MAF] still dominates with FR of 0.007–0.070 versus 0.065–0.727 for baselines. On proxy SCMs (Table 2), EXOM[MAF] achieves FR of 0.01 on SIMPSON-NLIN (|s|=1) versus 0.90 for RS, and counterfactual effect biases of ~0.01 on FAIRNESS, matching RS's accuracy while being far more efficient in high-dimensional settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EXOM[MAF] decisively outperforms all three baselines (Rejection Sampling, CEIS, and NIS) across every SCM type and submodel count. On the continuous SIMPSON-NLIN SCM with |s|=1, EXOM[MAF] achieves an ESP of 0.581, a 73× improvement over RS (0.008) and a 16× improvement over CEIS (0.037), while reducing FR from 0.971 to 0.005. The gap widens dramatically as |s| increases: for |s|≥3 on both SIMPSON-NLIN and NAPKIN, all three baselines completely fail (ESP=0.000, FR=1.000), whereas EXOM[MAF] retains ESP above 0.39 and FR below 0.18. On the discrete FAIRNESS-XW SCM, EXOM[MAF] still leads with FR of 0.007–0.070 versus 0.065–0.727 for the best baseline, though the absolute gap is smaller due to the lower inherent sampling difficulty in discrete spaces. EXOM[GMM] also outperforms baselines but is consistently weaker than EXOM[MAF], confirming that the richer representational capacity of masked autoregressive flows benefits the conditional proposal learning objective.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the importance sampling literature (Geyer et al. 2019 on cross-entropy-based IS; Müller et al. 2019 on neural IS) by replacing the standard 'one-off' proposal distribution with a conditional proposal Q_{U|Y_*}, transforming variance minimization into a conditional distribution learning problem solvable by normalizing flows or GMMs. It further departs from prior counterfactual estimation work (which is typically restricted to specific identification strategies like back-door or front-door) by providing a general-purpose estimator applicable to arbitrary finite collections of hypothetical worlds. The injection of counterfactual Markov boundaries as structural masks into the conditioning network draws on Markov boundary literature from causal discovery, but applies it in a novel way to exogenous-variable density modeling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Tractable counterfactual estimation in general settings has long been considered intractable (NP-hard for marginal inference on SCMs), and existing neural proxy SCM approaches either require full observation of endogenous variables or rely on rejection sampling that lacks scalability. EXOM provides a principled, reusable proposal-learning framework that bridges this gap, with empirical validation across three structurally distinct SCM families and two identifiable proxy SCMs (CausalNF, NCM). Key limitations include the requirement of a partially specified SCM, the finite-submodel assumption, and the boundedness condition on importance weights that may fail for infinite-support events. Open questions include whether the Markov boundary injection generalizes to non-recursive or cyclic SCMs, and how the method scales to very high-dimensional exogenous spaces.
