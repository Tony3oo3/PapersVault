## Problem

Existing visual pretraining methods (contrastive and captioning-based) learn holistic image representations but largely overlook region-specific spatial details. Prior region-aware approaches (GLIP, RegionCLIP) require complex architectures with RPN/FPN for RoI generation and are computationally expensive for multi-object matching. The paper asks whether natural language can serve as a simple, flexible interface to incorporate location-aware tasks into generative visual pretraining without such architectural overhead.

## Input / Output

The model takes a 224×224 RGB image and a task-specific text prefix ("Cap:", "ARef:", or "GCap:") as input, and autoregressively generates a text sequence containing captions and/or bounding box coordinates. Bounding boxes are represented as up to 500 integral coordinate values directly converted to strings and tokenized with the same 32K SentencePiece vocabulary, avoiding special location tokens. For the RES extension, 16 VQ-VAE segmentation tokens (from a 128-entry codebook) are appended after the box to specify mask shape within the predicted region.

## Method

LocCa uses a standard ViT-L/14 encoder (~600M total params) paired with a 12-layer transformer decoder, trained from scratch with a single autoregressive generative loss across three tasks distinguished by text prefixes. The two location-aware tasks—Automatic Referring Expression (AREF: predict caption then box) and Grounded Captioning (GCAP: predict box then caption)—are formulated as sequential prediction over the same token space, with losses applied to both the textual and coordinate portions. Pseudo-annotations are generated by OWL-ViT on 1B WebLI image/alt-text pairs, with one box-caption and one box-category pair randomly sampled per image. The key design choice is that the decoder handles all tasks uniformly via task prefixes, enabling flexible inference (e.g., conditioning on a caption to predict only its location) while keeping inference cost identical to a standard captioner. Parallel prediction (50% of examples) is retained from CapPa to strengthen visual representations.

## Results

On RefCOCO/+/g referring expression comprehension, LocCa_L achieves 91.94/86.47/87.46 Acc@0.5 (val) and LocCa_G reaches 92.99/89.12/89.24, establishing new state-of-the-art across all splits. On holistic tasks, LocCa maintains competitive performance: 84.5% ImageNet-1k accuracy, 127.1 CIDEr on COCO captioning, 72.8 on VQAv2, and 61.8 on GQA. When plugged into PaLI-3, LocCa_L outperforms the default SigLIP_L backbone on all six vision-language tasks (e.g., 49.2 vs 41.1 on TextVQA, 50.9 vs 46.2 on ST-VQA). On ADE20K semantic segmentation, LocCa achieves 51.81 mIoU, surpassing the ImageNet-pretrained Seg ViT-L baseline (50.71) by ~1 point.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On RefCOCO REC, LocCa_L (91.94 val) outperforms OFA_L (90.05), UNINEXT_L (91.43), and PixelLLM (89.8), while LocCa_G (92.99 val) surpasses even the larger OFA_H (92.04), UNINEXT_H (92.64), and ONE-PEACE_1.5B (92.58). Against 13B-parameter LLM-based models Shikra (87.83) and Ferret (89.48), LocCa_L (0.6B) leads by 4.1 and 2.5 points respectively on RefCOCO val. On the clean evaluation setup (no val/test image leakage), LocCa_G achieves 91.20/86.89/87.34 vs RefTR's 85.65/77.55/79.25, a margin of 5.6/9.3/8.1 points. On holistic tasks (Table 3), LocCa matches or slightly exceeds CapPa on classification (84.5 vs 84.4 ImageNet) and outperforms on VQAv2 (72.8 vs 70.9) and GQA (61.8 vs 58.3), while being slightly behind on OCR-VQA (64.5 vs 65.6). In the PaLI-3 integration (Table 4), LocCa_L beats SigLIP_L on all six tasks (e.g., 49.2 vs 41.1 TextVQA, 50.9 vs 46.2 ST-VQA), and LocCa_G consistently outperforms SigLIP_G. On ADE20K segmentation, LocCa (51.81 mIoU) surpasses Seg ViT-L (50.71) and CapPa (49.92) by ~2 points.

## Relation to Prior Work

LocCa extends the image captioning pretraining paradigm of Tschannen et al. (2023), which demonstrated that autoregressive captioning alone yields visual representations competitive with contrastive pretraining. It departs from region-based contrastive methods (GLIP, GLIPv2, RegionCLIP) that require complex RoI generation architectures (RPN, FPN) and from multi-task VLMs (OFA, Unified-IO, Florence-2, PixelLLM) that rely on pretrained backbones, by training a simple encoder-decoder from scratch with location-aware proxy tasks (automatic referring expression and grounded captioning) integrated via task prefixes. The key conceptual departure is using natural language as a unified interface for both holistic and location-aware pretraining objectives, rather than treating localization as a separate architectural component.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LocCa demonstrates that location-aware pretraining can be achieved with a simple autoregressive loss and no pretrained components, outperforming both contrastive (SigLIP) and complex multi-task architectures on localization tasks while preserving holistic understanding. The finding that the LocCa vision encoder outperforms SigLIP when plugged into PaLI-3 suggests that generative pretraining with location cues may be a more effective recipe for building VLM backbones than contrastive pretraining alone. Open questions include whether the approach extends to pixel-level tasks (segmentation), how it scales with more objects per image during pretraining, and whether the zero-shot multi-object detection capability can be made more reliable through better decoding strategies.
