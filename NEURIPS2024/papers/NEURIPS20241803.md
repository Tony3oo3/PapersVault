## Problem

Content-agnostic watermarking methods, including prominent diffusion-based approaches like Tree-Ring, claim strong robustness against distortions and regeneration attacks but have not been evaluated against steganalysis-based attacks. This paper reveals that such methods embed fixed, content-independent patterns that can be exposed by simply averaging multiple watermarked images, enabling watermark removal and forgery with minimal perceptual distortion.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241803/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a collection of n watermarked images and either paired clean images (greybox) or unpaired clean images from public datasets like ImageNet (blackbox), plus a single target image on which to perform removal or forgery. Images are resized to 256×256 or 512×512 depending on the watermarking method; audio is preprocessed to 16 kHz mono with only the first two seconds retained. The output is an extracted watermark pattern δ̂_w (a spatial-domain image or time-domain audio signal), which is then subtracted from or added to the target image to produce a watermark-removed or forged image.

## Method

Under the assumption that content-agnostic watermarks add a fixed perturbation δ_w independent of image content (x_w = x_∅ + δ_w), the adversary approximates δ_w by averaging n watermarked images and n clean images, causing random content to cancel while the shared watermark pattern persists. The extracted pattern is then subtracted (removal) or added (forgery) to a target image. For Tree-Ring specifically, the steganalysis incorporates DDIM inversion steps from the detection pipeline before averaging, yielding patterns in the Fourier domain that closely match the injected ring pattern. The approach requires no access to the watermarking algorithm, no training, and no optimization—only simple pixel-wise averaging—making it a lightweight blackbox attack. The paper further shows that mixing multiple watermark keys in the adversary's collection degrades removal efficacy, suggesting multi-key assignment as a partial mitigation.

## Results

The steganalysis-based removal reduces Tree-Ring's detection AUC from 1.00 to 0.24 (blackbox) and 0.14 (greybox) at n=5000, while maintaining PSNR above 15.4 dB. Across all five content-agnostic image methods, detection/decoding performance drops to near-chance levels (e.g., RoSteALS bit accuracy 0.244, DwtDctSvd 0.572, RingID 0.130), whereas all five content-adaptive methods retain >0.90 bit accuracy. For audio, AudioSeal's detection accuracy drops from 1.0 to ~0.75, and WavMark's bit accuracy paradoxically improves to 1.0 upon pattern subtraction, revealing systematic bias. Watermark forgery on Tree-Ring achieves 0.00 detection accuracy at 1% FPR, meaning forged watermarks are indistinguishable from authentic ones.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper attacks 12 image and 2 audio watermarking methods, revealing a clear split: all content-agnostic methods (Tree-Ring, RingID, RAWatermark, DwtDctSvd, RoSteALS, Gaussian Shading) are effectively defeated by simple averaging steganalysis, with detection/decoding metrics dropping to 0.13–0.57, while all content-adaptive methods (Stable Signature, WmAdapter, RivaGAN, SSL, HiDDeN) remain robust with bit accuracies above 0.90. Tree-Ring, previously shown to resist VAE compression and regeneration attacks, is the most striking case: its AUC collapses from 1.0 to 0.14 (greybox) with only ~0.2 dB PSNR loss, and the extracted pattern enables perfect forgery (0.00 detection at 1% FPR). Among content-agnostic methods, RoSteALS and RingID are most vulnerable (bit accuracy ~0.24 and 0.13 respectively), while Gaussian Shading is the least vulnerable due to its large-magnitude pattern causing visible artefacts upon subtraction. For audio, AudioSeal (content-agnostic) drops from 1.0 to ~0.75 detection accuracy, while WavMark's counterintuitive improvement to 1.0 bit accuracy upon subtraction exposes its systematic bias and watermark locations. The multi-key mixing experiment shows that combining 3 different Tree-Ring keys raises AUC from <0.2 to >0.7, partially mitigating but not eliminating the vulnerability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes steganalysis — traditionally a tool for detecting hidden messages in media — as a systematic attack vector against digital watermarking, carving a new threat model distinct from prior distortion-based attacks (compression, noise, blurring) and regeneration attacks (diffusion/VAE re-synthesis). It introduces a clean conceptual taxonomy of content-agnostic versus content-adaptive watermarking that organizes the field and identifies which class is vulnerable. The Tree-Ring case study is the first blackbox attack on that method, building on its own DDIM-inversion detection pipeline to extract the injected ring pattern from the Fourier domain, revealing that the 'semantic' watermark is in fact a propagated low-level content-agnostic ripple.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a training-free averaging operation can neutralize watermarks that were explicitly designed and benchmarked to resist strong distortions and regeneration attacks fundamentally reframes the robustness evaluation landscape for watermarking. The paper's security guidelines — mandating steganalysis evaluation alongside distortion robustness and encouraging content-adaptive designs — are likely to influence how future watermarking methods are proposed and audited. Open questions include whether more sophisticated steganalysis (multi-domain, learned) could also compromise content-adaptive methods, whether the multi-key mitigation holds under adaptive adversaries, and whether the approach generalizes to video, 3D, and other modalities the authors mention but do not test.
