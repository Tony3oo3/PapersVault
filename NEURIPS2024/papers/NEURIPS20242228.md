## Problem

Existing methods for generating adversarial prompts against LLMs suffer from a steep trade-off between diversity and attack success rate: they either restrict themselves to a single predefined attack strategy, require extensive human annotation, or collapse to a narrow set of similar prompts. This limits their utility both as diagnostic tools for model vulnerabilities and as sources of diverse synthetic data for safety fine-tuning.

## Input / Output

The method takes a target LLM, a set of K predefined feature dimensions (categorical, e.g., Risk Category and Attack Style, or numerical, e.g., prompt length), a Mutator LLM, and a Judge LLM as inputs. It produces a K-dimensional archive (a discrete grid) where each cell stores the highest-fitness adversarial prompt for a specific combination of feature categories, along with the target's response to that prompt and a fitness score. The archive size is determined by the product of category counts across all K dimensions (e.g., 10×10 = 100 cells for two 10-category features), and the output is a diverse set of prompts covering every feature combination rather than a single optimal prompt.

## Method

RAINBOW TEAMING casts adversarial prompt generation as a quality-diversity search problem built on MAP-Elites, maintaining a K-dimensional archive where each axis corresponds to a diversity feature. At each iteration, it samples a parent prompt from the archive and a target descriptor z′ (biased toward low-fitness cells), then applies K directed mutations via a Mutator LLM—one per feature dimension—to produce a candidate prompt with descriptor z′. A BLEU-based similarity filter discards candidates too similar to their parent, preserving linguistic diversity. Archive updates use a preference-based Judge LLM that performs pairwise comparisons (with position swapping) between the candidate's target response and the incumbent's response, rather than a fixed-scale score, which avoids reward hacking and enables continued open-ended improvement. The key insight is that previously discovered prompts serve as stepping stones: effective attacks in one feature region can be mutated into effective attacks in another, accelerating coverage of the full feature space.

## Results

RAINBOW TEAMING achieves an attack success rate (ASR) exceeding 90% across all tested LLMs after 2000 iterations: ~92% on Llama 2-chat 7B and Llama 3-Instruct 8B, and 98% on Mistral 7B and Vicuna 7B (GPT-4 evaluated). On JailbreakBench, RAINBOW TEAMING discovers 66 successful jailbreaks across 41 harmful behaviours (Llama Guard, best-of-4), substantially outperforming PAIR with the same mutator LLM (14/11). Fine-tuning Llama 2-chat 7B on RAINBOW TEAMING-generated synthetic data reduces ASR from 92%/95% to 0.3%/0.7% (GPT-4/Llama Guard) while preserving GSM8K (0.224→0.219) and MMLU (0.412→0.405) scores.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary external baseline is PAIR [8], a black-box iterative jailbreaking method. On JailbreakBench, RAINBOW TEAMING discovers 66 successful jailbreaks across 41 behaviours (Llama Guard), compared to only 14/11 for PAIR using the same Llama 2 70B mutator LLM — a roughly 4.7× improvement in total jailbreaks and 3.7× in behaviours covered. RAINBOW TEAMING also achieves 8/7 on the original JailbreakBench classifier versus 1/1 for PAIR with the RT mutator. In terms of diversity, RAINBOW TEAMING's Self-BLEU is 0.51 versus 0.74 for PAIR, indicating substantially more linguistically diverse prompts. Internally, RAINBOW TEAMING also outperforms two ablation baselines (No Stepping Stones and Same Cell Mutations) on ASR against Llama 2-chat 7B, confirming the value of cross-category stepping stones and directed mutations.

## Relation to Prior Work

RAINBOW TEAMING builds directly on MAP-Elites (Mouret & Clune, 2015), a quality-diversity evolutionary search algorithm, and on the use of LLMs as mutation operators over language (Lehman et al., 2022). It departs from prior black-box adversarial prompt generation methods (PAIR, TAP, AutoDAN) that target a single harmful behaviour or a single attack strategy, by explicitly optimising for both effectiveness and diversity across multiple risk categories and attack styles simultaneously. The paper also positions itself within the broader open-endedness literature, framing adversarial prompt discovery as an open-ended search problem rather than a fixed-objective optimisation, and contrasts with score-based evaluators by adopting a preference-based (pairwise comparison) judge to avoid reward hacking.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The demonstration that heavily safety-aligned models (Llama 2-chat, Llama 3-Instruct) still yield >90% attack success rates under diverse, automated red-teaming underscores that current safety alignment is far from robust. The finding that just 1500 RAINBOW TEAMING-generated prompts, used for supervised fine-tuning, reduce ASR from 92% to 0.3% while preserving GSM8K/MMLU capabilities offers a concrete, low-cost pathway to improve LLM safety without large human-annotated datasets. Open questions include whether the pre-defined feature grid can be discovered automatically, how the method scales to multimodal or vision-language models, and whether iterative attack-finetune loops converge to a stable robustness ceiling. The computational cost (14,000 batched inference calls per run) remains a practical barrier to widespread adoption.
