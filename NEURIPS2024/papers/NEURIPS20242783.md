## Problem

Traditional text embeddings capture overall semantic information, but for instruction data the critical signal is the task category (typically a verb-noun phrase) rather than specific content or knowledge. No existing benchmark or embedding method is designed to prioritize task identification over sentence-level semantics, leaving instruction-related tasks like data selection and demonstration retrieval without a suitable representation space.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242783/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single-turn instruction string and a prompt template (the PIE prompt, which frames the task as 'the essence of an instruction is its task intention') as input. For Llama2-based models, the output embedding is the mean of last-token hidden states across the final two layers; for BERT, it is the [CLS] token from the last layer. The resulting dense vector is used for cosine-similarity-based clustering, pairwise intention-similarity scoring, and retrieval in downstream tasks.

## Method

The paper introduces instruction embedding, a representation that encodes task category rather than general semantics, and builds the IEB benchmark (47k instructions across 1,353 verb-noun task categories) by syntactically parsing instructions with the Berkeley Neural Parser to extract verb-noun pairs, supplemented by GPT-4-generated complex samples and WordNet-based category merging. The PIE method uses a prompt combining chain-of-thought and knowledge-enhancement cues to steer LLMs toward extracting task intention, and can be used learning-free or as a starting point for supervised contrastive fine-tuning. During fine-tuning, positive pairs are drawn from the same task category, while hard negatives are constructed by sampling instructions that share either the same verb or the same noun but differ in the other, forcing the model to discriminate fine-grained task distinctions rather than relying on surface word overlap.

## Results

On the IEB benchmark, the supervised fine-tuned PIE-prompt Llama2 achieves the best instruction clustering ARI of 0.9125 and IIS-Spearman of 0.8450, while PIE-prompt BERT reaches ARI 0.8974, CP 0.9453, and Silhouette 0.5180. Without fine-tuning, PIE-prompt already substantially improves over no-prompt baselines (e.g., Vicuna goes from ARI 0.1198 to 0.5305; Llama2 from 0.1813 to 0.4814). In downstream tasks, instruction embedding outperforms text embedding for data selection (e.g., Mistral: 7.92 vs. 2.98 on AlpacaEval with 10 samples) and demonstration retrieval across all four evaluated LLMs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Compared to SimCSE (Wiki, unsupervised contrastive learning), the supervised PIE-prompt fine-tuned BERT improves ARI from 0.4741 to 0.8974 and Silhouette from 0.1225 to 0.5180, while IIS-Spearman rises from 0.7460 to 0.8446. Against PromptBERT (Wiki semantic-prompt), the gap is even larger: ARI jumps from 0.3371 to 0.8974 for BERT, showing that task-focused prompting plus supervised contrastive learning far outperforms generic semantic embedding fine-tuning. The TART models (Asai et al., 2023), designed for task-aware retrieval, achieve ARI 0.4984 (dual-contriever-msmarco) and 0.2850 (full-flan-t5-xl), both well below the proposed method's 0.8974–0.9125; the authors attribute this to TART encoding query semantic information alongside task, which dilutes task-specific focus. Notably, even without any fine-tuning, PIE-prompt Vicuna (ARI 0.5305) already surpasses the unsupervised Wiki BERT baseline (ARI 0.4741), demonstrating the value of the prompt design alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the contrastive learning framework of SimCSE (Gao et al., 2021) and the prompt-guided embedding approach of PromptBERT (Jiang et al., 2022), but reframes the embedding objective for instruction data. The key conceptual departure comes from LIMA (Zhou et al., 2023), which showed that instruction fine-tuning teaches interaction style rather than new knowledge, and from Wu et al. (2023b), which demonstrated that instruction tuning highlights verb-noun task words. Rather than treating instructions as generic text where semantic similarity is the goal, the paper argues that the essential information to encode is the task category (typically a verb-noun phrase), positioning instruction embedding as a specialized subset of text embedding with a different optimization target. The paper also distinguishes itself from TART (Asai et al., 2023) and task embedding (Vu et al., 2020) by operating at the phrase-level task category rather than full semantic content or dataset-level task representations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The IEB benchmark (47k samples, 1353 task categories) fills a gap by providing a dedicated evaluation resource for instruction-specific embeddings, which prior text embedding benchmarks (STS-based) were not designed to assess. The demonstrated superiority of task-focused embeddings over semantic embeddings across four downstream tasks (data selection, demonstration retrieval, benchmark compression, task correlation analysis) provides practical guidance for the instruction tuning community. However, the benchmark is limited to single-turn, English instructions and does not handle multi-step or multi-turn instructions, and the 93% manual validation accuracy leaves room for label noise. Open questions include whether the verb-noun task categorization generalizes to non-English or more complex compositional instructions, and whether instruction embeddings can be integrated into retrieval-augmented generation pipelines without losing the semantic information needed for content matching.
