## Problem

Text-to-image diffusion models like Stable Diffusion suffer from poor prompt compliance on complex scenes (low multi-object recall, inability to render text, inconsistent spatial constraints), and existing remedies are inadequate: inference-based prompt compliance (IBPC) methods scale linearly with object count and become prohibitively slow, while visually conditioned DMs (ControlNet, T2I-Adapter) require users to manually produce or extract condition maps (segmentation, depth, pose), which is tedious and error-prone. No prior framework jointly models images and their conditioning variables in a modular, editable, and efficiently samplable way.

## Input / Output

The FG-DM takes a text prompt (encoded by CLIP) and optionally a subset of visual condition maps (segmentation, depth, normal, sketch, or pose) as input, and jointly produces the final RGB image together with all intermediate condition maps in a single forward pass. Condition maps are synthesized at quarter resolution (e.g., 64×64 latent) to enable fast filtering, while the final image is generated at full resolution (256×256 or 256×512). Discrete outputs like segmentation labels are recovered from the SD autoencoder's 3-channel RGB output via a color-to-label hashmap with thresholding, and the factor graph structure allows any subset of factors to be skipped at inference via classifier-free guidance.

## Method

The FG-DM decomposes the joint distribution P(x, {y^i}) into a chain of conditional factors P(x|{y^i})·P(y^1|y^2,...,y^K)·...·P(y^K), where each factor is implemented by a small T2I-Adapter branch (four feature-extraction blocks with timestep-residual connections) grafted onto a frozen pre-trained Stable Diffusion U-Net, sharing the VAE encoder-decoder across all factors. A key training innovation is an attention distillation loss that minimizes KL divergence between the cross- and self-attention maps of the frozen SD teacher and each adapted student factor, grounding spatial-textual binding in the pretrained model's semantics and improving condition fidelity by 5.6 FID points for segmentation. For prompt compliance, the framework enables sampling-based prompt compliance (SBPC): N segmentation maps are synthesized at low resolution and few timesteps (10 DDIM steps), object recall is computed directly from the masks, and only the highest-recall mask is passed to the image factor—achieving 75% recall at 4× lower latency than sampling N full images with SD. The model is trained with classifier-free guidance (20% null-condition dropout) so that any subset of factors can be activated at inference, and the overall loss combines per-factor noise-prediction MSE with the weighted attention distillation term.

## Results

On COCO text-to-image synthesis, FG-DM with segmentation conditioning achieves FID 19.6 (vs. SD's 20.5) and with normal map conditioning achieves FID 19.3, while the sketch condition yields the highest CLIP score (30.3). For prompt compliance on ADE20K (8.5 objects/prompt), FG-DM with N=10 seeds and 10 DDIM steps reaches 75.1% average object recall — matching SD with N=10 — but at 4× lower inference time (5.75s vs. 23s). A user study with 10 human evaluators confirms FG-DM is preferred over SD+CEM for image quality (61.4%) and prompt adherence (57.8%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Stable Diffusion v1.4, FG-DM (Seg) improves FID from 20.5 to 19.6 and FG-DM (Normal) to 19.3, while also achieving higher precision (0.56/0.59 vs. 0.50). For object recall on ADE20K, FG-DM with a single seed (N=1) achieves 67.8% recall versus SD's 59.8% (+8 points); with N=10 both reach 75.1%, but FG-DM is 4× faster (5.75s vs. 23s) because it only needs to synthesize low-resolution segmentation maps for filtering. Against Attend-and-Excite (A-E), FG-DM (N=1) outperforms by 4.2 recall points (67.8% vs. 63.6%) while being 7.5× faster (4.81s vs. 36s), since A-E's per-object optimization scales linearly with scene complexity. Against 4M-XL, FG-DM (Seg) achieves FID 19.6 versus 39.1 — nearly a 2× improvement — despite 4M-XL being a larger model trained from scratch. Against SegFormer-B5 for segmentation mask generation, FG-DM produces superior masks (FID 86.1 vs. 112.6, Precision 0.72 vs. 0.61) at comparable throughput (6.4 img/s). In the user study, FG-DM is preferred over the SD+CEM pipeline for both image quality (61.4% vs. 35.5%) and condition quality (63.1% vs. 33.5%), confirming that jointly synthesized conditions are more coherent than those extracted by separate off-the-shelf models.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on Stable Diffusion's latent diffusion architecture, T2I-Adapter's conditioning mechanism, and ControlNet's image synthesis factor, but departs fundamentally from the conditional-distribution paradigm P(x|{y^i}) used by all prior VC-DMs. Instead of a single joint denoising U-Net (as in SemanticGAN, GCDP, HyperHuman, or 4M) or a monolithic autoregressive model, FG-DM decomposes the joint P(x,{y^i}) into a modular factor graph of independently trained conditional DMs, each implemented by a small adapter on a frozen SD backbone. The attention distillation loss, which uses SD's cross-attention maps as a teacher signal for condition-synthesis factors, is a novel training objective not present in prior joint or conditional diffusion models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The FG-DM framework offers a practical path to controllable generation without manual condition specification: sampling-based prompt compliance (SBPC) achieves 15% higher object recall than single-seed SD at 4× lower cost, and the modular factor graph enables fine-grained editing, data augmentation for downstream segmentation/depth models, and continual learning by adding new condition factors without retraining. Key limitations include runtime growth with more than two factors, the need for user effort in fine-grained spatial edits, and the fact that pixel-level inversion/editing remains less robust than mask-level editing. Open directions include sharing attention maps across factors to reduce runtime, automating condition edits via LLMs, and extending the framework to 3D and video generation.
