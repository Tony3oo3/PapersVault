## Problem

Fine-tuning text-to-image diffusion models on a few reference images (e.g., DreamBooth) causes catastrophic forgetting of pretrained knowledge, leading to poor text-image alignment and limited compositional generation. Existing remedies like prior preservation loss trade subject fidelity for prompt fidelity without improving the Pareto frontier, and the problem worsens with fewer reference images. The paper seeks a principled, data-free regularization that retains pretrained capabilities while learning new concepts.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243280/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt and a small set of 4–6 reference images (for subject) or style reference images as input, and produces personalized images at 1024×1024 resolution via Stable Diffusion XL. Fine-tuning operates on LoRA adapters (rank 32 for subject, rank 64 for style) plus optional learned textual embeddings, requiring only a single A100 40GB GPU. At inference, a consistency guidance scale (ω_con) lets the user dial the tradeoff between subject/style fidelity and prompt fidelity, and separately fine-tuned subject and style LoRAs can be arithmetically merged to produce 'my subject in my style' images.

## Method

DCO introduces a log-sigmoid training loss that penalizes the difference between the fine-tuned model's noise-prediction error and the frozen pretrained model's noise-prediction error at each timestep, effectively bounding the KL divergence between the two models' latent distributions. The paper proves this loss is equivalent to optimizing an implicit consistency reward (measuring agreement between generated images and the reference set) derived from a constrained policy optimization objective, analogous to DPO's implicit reward but applied to diffusion personalization. At inference, consistency guidance sampling decomposes the predicted noise into pretrained, text-guided, and consistency-guided components, giving a tunable knob for the fidelity–alignment tradeoff. Because DCO minimizes model shift relative to the pretrained weights, independently fine-tuned subject and style LoRAs can be combined via simple arithmetic addition without the interference that plagues regular DreamBooth fine-tuning, outperforming post-optimization methods like ZipLoRA.

## Results

On the 'my subject in my style' merging task (Table 1), DCO Merge achieves subject similarity 0.462, style similarity 0.651, and image-text similarity 0.773, outperforming DB Merge (0.386 / 0.672 / 0.430) and DB ZipLoRA (0.406 / 0.662 / 0.729) on subject and text scores while remaining competitive on style. On subject personalization (Fig. 5a), DCO positions on a strictly superior Pareto frontier between DINOv2 image similarity and SigLIP image-text similarity compared to both DreamBooth and DreamBooth with prior preservation loss. In a user study (Table 2), DCO is preferred over DB+p.p. for subject fidelity 81.9% of the time and over DB for prompt fidelity 72.7% of the time.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DreamBooth (DB), DCO wins on image-text alignment (prompt fidelity) while maintaining or improving subject fidelity, placing it on a strictly superior Pareto frontier in both subject and style personalization (Fig. 5a, 5b). Against DreamBooth with prior preservation loss (DB+p.p.), DCO achieves significantly higher subject fidelity (81.9% user preference win rate) at comparable prompt fidelity, whereas DB+p.p. shifts the operating point toward lower subject consistency. Against ZipLoRA (the post-optimization merging method applied to DB fine-tuned LoRAs), DCO Merge with simple arithmetic merging outperforms on subject similarity (0.462 vs. 0.406) and image-text similarity (0.773 vs. 0.729) while being only marginally lower on style similarity (0.651 vs. 0.662), demonstrating that DCO's training objective inherently produces more composable LoRAs without requiring any post-hoc optimization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on DreamBooth's fine-tuning paradigm for subject personalization and the implicit reward formulation from DPO (Rafailov et al.) and its diffusion adaptation (Wallace et al.), but departs from both by deriving the training objective from a constrained policy optimization framework (relative entropy policy search). Where DreamBooth addresses catastrophic forgetting with an auxiliary prior dataset (prior preservation loss), DCO replaces this with a log-sigmoid loss that directly regularizes the KL divergence between fine-tuned and pretrained models on the reference data alone, requiring no additional data. The practical loss (Eq. 11) is as simple as the standard ε-prediction loss plus one extra forward pass through the frozen pretrained model, making it a drop-in replacement for any existing DreamBooth/LoRA pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DCO provides a principled, data-free alternative to prior preservation loss that pushes the Pareto frontier between subject fidelity and prompt fidelity strictly outward, and the finding that arithmetically merged DCO LoRAs outperform ZipLoRA (which explicitly optimizes per-layer merge coefficients) suggests that training-time regularization can substitute for inference-time post-optimization in model composition. Limitations include instability in multi-subject composition for semantically similar concepts and a 1.3×/2× computational overhead in training/inference. Open questions include whether the cosine-similarity-based interference metric used by prior merging work is the right diagnostic, and whether the approach extends to more complex multi-concept scenes or larger model families.
