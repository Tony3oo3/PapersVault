## Problem

Existing diffusion-based tracking methods (DiffusionTrack, DiffMOT) perform denoising in 2D coordinate space, restricting them to bounding-box-only representations and failing to exploit the conditional generation mechanism of latent diffusion models. Moreover, standard diffusion requires an extensive and unnecessary mapping to a Gaussian noise domain for what is fundamentally a deterministic frame-to-frame transition. This paper asks whether tracking can be reformulated as a visual-domain diffusion process that replaces full denoising with a simpler, faster interpolation mechanism.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a consecutive video frame pair (I_t, I_{t+1}) encoded into latent space by a frozen VAE, along with a single indicator representation for the target in frame t (a point, structured point set for pose, bounding box, segmentation mask, or free-text prompt). The indicator is converted into a conditioning vector τ via a task-specific extractor (Gaussian kernel, pooling, or word embedding) and injected through cross-attention at every U-Net layer. The output is the target's location in frame t+1 in the same representation type, extracted by element-wise multiplying averaged self-attention and cross-attention maps over all N layers and T×0.8 diffusion steps, then applying a type-specific mapping (argmax for points, thresholding for segments, min/max for boxes).

## Method

DINTR replaces the standard diffusion denoising objective with a deterministic latent-space interpolation: starting from the current frame's clean latent z_0^t, the model iteratively refines an estimate of the next frame's latent z_0^{t+1} over T steps by adding a learned offset (z_{k-1}^{t+1} − z_k^t) at each step, guided by the target indicator via cross-attention. This offset-based operator (Eqn. 9) is provably equivalent to linear blending but is accumulative and stable, avoiding the instability of single-anchor formulations when α_k approaches 0 or 1. The U-Net is fine-tuned in-place from pre-trained LDM or ADM weights for 500 steps with a simple L2 loss between the predicted and ground-truth next-frame latents, operating online and auto-regressively. Final object localization is obtained by a 'reversed conditional process' that aggregates spatial attention maps across all layers and timesteps, requiring no dedicated prediction head. This yields a 2× speedup over the reconstruction-based variant while achieving SOTA or competitive results across seven benchmarks spanning five indicator types.

## Results

On TAP-Vid point tracking, DINTR achieves the best AJ on Kinetics (57.8), Kubric (85.5), and DAVIS (62.3), with the highest OA on Kinetics (89.4) and DAVIS (88.9). On PoseTrack21, DINTR scores 82.5 mAP, 64.9 MOTA, 71.5 IDF1, and 55.5 HOTA. For multi-object tracking on MOT17, DINTR achieves 78.0 MOTA, 77.6 IDF1, and 63.5 HOTA; on GroOT (referring tracking), it reaches 68.9 MOTA and 57.5 HOTA. On segment tracking, DINTR attains 75.4 J&F on DAVIS and 67.4 sMOTSA on MOTS, the best among all compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against TAPIR (the strongest point-tracking baseline), DINTR wins on Kinetics AJ (57.8 vs 57.1), DAVIS AJ (62.3 vs 59.8), and DAVIS OA (88.9 vs 87.6), but trails on RGB-Stacking AJ (65.2 vs 66.2) and Kubric OA (95.2 vs 95.8). Against the diffusion-based MOT baselines, DINTR outperforms DiffusionTrack on MOT17 across all metrics (MOTA 78.0 vs 77.9, IDF1 77.6 vs 73.8, HOTA 63.5 vs 60.8) but falls slightly short of DiffMOT on MOTA (78.0 vs 79.8) and IDF1 (77.6 vs 79.3). On referring tracking (GroOT), DINTR clearly surpasses MENDER (MOTA 68.9 vs 65.5, HOTA 57.5 vs 53.2) and MDETR+TrackFormer (MOTA 68.9 vs 62.6). For segment tracking on MOTS, DINTR achieves the best sMOTSA (67.4) over UNICORN (65.3), PointTrackV2 (62.3), and TrackFormer (54.9). On pose tracking, DINTR (82.5 mAP) is marginally below DiffPose (83.0 mAP) but uniquely reports full tracking metrics (MOTA 64.9, IDF1 71.5, HOTA 55.5) that DiffPose does not provide. On LaSOT SOT, DINTR ties UNICORN on Precision (0.74) and wins on Success (0.70 vs 0.68), while for text-based referring it outperforms AdaSwitcher (0.60 vs 0.55 Precision) and GTI (0.60 vs 0.47).

## Relation to Prior Work

DINTR builds on Latent Diffusion Models (LDM) and ADM as its base architecture, but departs fundamentally from prior diffusion-based tracking methods (DiffusionTrack, DiffMOT) that operate in 2D coordinate space by formulating tracking entirely in the visual latent domain. The key conceptual departure is replacing the standard Gaussian denoising process with a deterministic interpolation operator drawn from classical image processing (iterative alpha-blending), which the authors prove equivalent to the denoising schedule but more stable and 2× faster. This positions DINTR as a bridge between generative diffusion mechanics and classical temporal interpolation, while also unifying five indication types (point, pose, box, segment, text) through the conditional cross-attention mechanism that prior coordinate-space diffusion methods could not support.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unification of five indication representations under a single data- and process-unified framework addresses a long-standing fragmentation in tracking where each representation required a specialized model. The finding that a simple offset-based interpolation operator can replace the full multi-step Gaussian denoising while maintaining (or improving) tracking accuracy challenges the assumption that the full diffusion trajectory is necessary for temporal correspondence modeling. However, practical adoption is limited by inference speed (~3.2s/frame at T=50), a minor performance gap to motion-model-based methods like DiffMOT on MOT17, and the reliance on external detectors/segmenters for initialization. Open questions include whether the interpolation formulation can be accelerated to real-time, extended to open-vocabulary tracking, and integrated with geometric or camera-motion constraints.
