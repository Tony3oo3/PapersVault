## Problem

Existing methods for distilling 2D foundation model knowledge into 3D networks suffer from two critical issues: KNN-based point tokenization groups points from different SAM regions into the same 3D token, causing representational conflicts during region-level distillation, and the inherent long-tail distribution in 3D datasets causes the model to be dominated by gradients from over-represented samples. This paper addresses both gaps to enable effective 2D-to-3D knowledge transfer for downstream tasks like object detection and semantic segmentation.

## Input / Output

The method consumes paired 3D point clouds and RGB images from ScanNet (78,000 frames sampled every 25 frames), with SAM-generated segmentation masks computed offline to define region boundaries. The 3D point cloud is projected onto the 2D image plane to establish point-to-region correspondences, and DINOv2 ViT-B extracts 2D features that are interpolated back to original image resolution. The output is a pre-trained plain transformer encoder that, after fine-tuning with task-specific decoders, produces 3D object bounding boxes (evaluated via AP25/AP50) or per-point semantic segmentation labels (evaluated via mIoU/mAcc).

## Method

The framework operates in two stages. In Stage 1, SAM-guided tokenization replaces KNN by assigning each 3D point to a token based on its SAM region membership in the projected 2D image, ensuring a one-to-one correspondence between 3D tokens and 2D region features and eliminating cross-region conflicts; a group-balanced re-weighting strategy then upweights tail groups (identified via K-means clustering of DINOv2 features) in the L1 distillation loss. In Stage 2, the Stage 1 model is frozen as a teacher, and a student model receives masked point tokens (60% masking ratio) and must predict both the teacher's instance-level global embedding (via MSE on pooled features) and the token-wise local embeddings of masked positions, learning contextualized representations from partial 3D input. The plain transformer backbone uses PointNet for per-token feature extraction, and the entire pipeline is trained with AdamW (lr=0.001, weight decay=0.05, batch size 64) on 4 A100 GPUs with cosine scheduling and 10-epoch warmup.

## Results

On ScanNetV2 3D object detection with the GroupFree3D backbone, the proposed method achieves AP25=72.3 and AP50=55.7, surpassing Bridge3D (69.1/51.9) by +3.2 and +3.8. With the 3DETR backbone on ScanNetV2, it reaches AP25=68.2 and AP50=48.4, improving over Bridge3D (65.3/44.2) by +2.9 and +4.2. For 3D semantic segmentation, the method attains mIoU=71.8 on S3DIS and mIoU=75.4 on ScanNet, outperforming Bridge3D by +1.6 and +1.5 mIoU respectively, while using only 2D foundation models (DINOv2) rather than the 2D+text modality combination employed by Bridge3D.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method consistently outperforms Bridge3D [10], the closest prior SOTA, across all detection and segmentation benchmarks: +2.9/+4.2 AP25/AP50 on ScanNetV2 (3DETR), +3.2/+3.8 on ScanNetV2 (GroupFree3D), +1.7/+2.4 on SUN RGB-D (3DETR), and +1.6/+1.5 mIoU on S3DIS/ScanNet for segmentation. Against earlier pre-training methods, the gains are even larger: over Point-MAE [37] by +4.8/+8.6 AP25/AP50 on ScanNetV2 (3DETR), over PiMAE [7] by +5.2/+8.2, and over DepthContrast [55] by +6.2/+5.5. On SOTA detectors, applying the pre-training to CAGroup3D [47] yields +1.4/+1.1 AP25/AP50, while VDETR [45] benefits more substantially with +2.2/+2.9, confirming the generality of the SAM-guided tokenization. In the cross-backbone comparison (Table 6), the method surpasses CLIP2Scene [8] by +6.2 AP25, Seal [32] by +5.5, and PPT [48] by +5.4 on ScanNet detection, while also leading in segmentation mIoU by +6.2 over PPT. The ablation (Table 3) confirms SAM-guided tokenization contributes the largest single gain (+1.1 AP25 over the next-best component combination), followed by masked token prediction (+1.5) and group-balanced re-weighting (+1.1).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper is a direct extension of Bridge3D (same first author, NeurIPS 2024), which first demonstrated region-level knowledge distillation from 2D foundation models (SAM, CLIP) into 3D plain transformers. The key conceptual departure is replacing KNN-based point tokenization with SAM-guided tokenization, ensuring a one-to-one correspondence between 3D tokens and 2D mask regions to eliminate the information conflicts that arise when KNN groups points from different SAM regions into the same token. The two-stage masked token prediction framework is inspired by VideoPrism's latent-space reconstruction for cross-modality distillation, and the group-balanced re-weighting strategy adapts class-balanced loss ideas (Cui et al., Yu et al.) to the label-free pre-training setting via K-means pseudo-labeling of foundation-model features.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The SAM-guided tokenization addresses a practical misalignment problem that limits the effectiveness of 2D-to-3D knowledge distillation, and the ablation confirms it is the single most impactful component. However, the approach is validated only on indoor datasets (ScanNet, S3DIS, SUN RGB-D) and relies on high-quality SAM masks, raising open questions about generalization to outdoor or large-scale 3D scenes where mask quality may degrade. The moderate but consistent gains over Bridge3D (+2.9–4.2 AP on detection, +1.5–1.6 mIoU on segmentation) suggest the tokenization alignment and long-tail re-weighting are meaningful but not transformative, and the field still lacks a large-scale 3D foundation model that would obviate the need for 2D-to-3D distillation altogether.
