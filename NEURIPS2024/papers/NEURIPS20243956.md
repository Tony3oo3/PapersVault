## Problem

Existing data-driven trajectory generation methods (attention networks, GANs, diffusion models) replicate real-world mobility only at the distribution level, lacking semantic understanding of why individuals visit specific places. This limits their ability to simulate novel or unforeseen scenarios (e.g., a pandemic) where the data distribution shifts significantly. The paper asks how LLM agents can be aligned with semantically rich activity data to produce interpretable, motivation-aware personal mobility trajectories.

## Input / Output

The framework consumes anonymized check-in trajectories at 10-minute resolution (location name, category, GPS coordinates, timestamp) from Twitter/Foursquare, along with 10 predefined candidate personas and a target date; an optional scenario context prompt (e.g., pandemic instructions) can be appended. Output is a structured JSON containing a 'plan' field (ordered list of POI-at-time pairs for the full day) and a 'reason' field (natural-language justification), plus intermediate artifacts: a natural-language activity pattern description and a one-sentence daily motivation summary. Locations are anonymized to category+ID with coordinates converted to integer IDs before prompting.

## Method

LLMob is a two-phase LLM agent framework. Phase 1 (self-consistent pattern identification) generates C=10 candidate activity patterns by prompting GPT-3.5 with different personas and preprocessed trajectory statistics (commuting distance, typical start/end times, frequent locations), then selects the best pattern via a scoring function that asks the LLM to rate how likely the target person versus other residents would follow each candidate pattern. Phase 2 (motivation-driven generation) retrieves a daily motivation through one of two retrieval-augmented strategies: (a) evolving-based, where the LLM summarizes motivations from the past k≤7 days and infers the target date's motivation, or (b) learning-based, where a small fully-connected network trained with InfoNCE contrastive loss approximates date-to-date similarity to retrieve the most analogous historical day, whose activities are then summarized into a motivation. The final trajectory is generated by instructing the LLM agent to act as the identified persona, following the pattern and motivation, constrained to the person's most frequently visited locations.

## Results

On the Tokyo personal activity trajectory dataset (100 users, 10-min intervals), LLMob-E and LLMob-L achieve the best Step Interval (SI) and Daily Activity Routine Distribution (DARD) JSD scores across all three evaluation settings (normal→normal, abnormal→abnormal, abnormal→normal). In the standard normal→normal setting, LLMob-E attains SI=0.046 and DARD=0.125, roughly halving the DARD JSD of the best baseline (DiffTraj at 0.318). LLMob is runner-up on Spatial-Temporal Visits Distribution (STVD) and does not lead on Step Distance (SD), where DeepMove (0.008) and DiffTraj (0.008 in abnormal→abnormal) are strongest. On the Osaka dataset, LLMob-E achieves DARD=0.121 and STVD=0.380, substantially outperforming DiffTraj (DARD=0.406, STVD=0.691) and TrajGAIL (DARD=0.525, STVD=0.483).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LLMob decisively outperforms all seven baselines on DARD (activity routine distribution), achieving JSD values of 0.117–0.136 versus 0.218–0.693 for baselines, a gap of roughly 2–5×. On SI (temporal step intervals), LLMob-E leads with 0.043–0.056, beating the next-best TrajGAIL (0.058–0.060) and DiffTraj (0.142–0.251) by a wide margin. On STVD (spatial-temporal visit distribution), TrajGAIL remains the strongest baseline (0.428–0.523), with LLMob as runner-up (0.531–0.615); DiffTraj is competitive on STVD in the normal→normal setting (0.692) but degrades in cross-domain settings. On SD (step distance), DeepMove (0.008–0.013) and DiffTraj (0.008 in abnormal→abnormal) are best, while LLMob (0.049–0.064) is mid-pack. MM, LSTM, STAN, and ActSTD are consistently outperformed by LLMob on all four metrics across all three settings. The paper also shows that LLMob's advantage is robust across LLM backends (GPT-3.5, GPT-4o-mini, Llama 3-8B) and generalizes to the Osaka dataset, where LLMob-E achieves DARD=0.121 vs. DiffTraj's 0.406.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of two established research threads: data-driven personal mobility generation (Markov models, attention-based predictors like DeepMove/STAN, adversarial generators like TrajGAIL/ActSTD, and diffusion models like DiffTraj) and LLM agent frameworks for social simulation (Park et al.'s generative agents, economic and epidemic modeling with LLMs). Rather than building on a single prior method, it repurposes the LLM agent paradigm—action, memory, planning—from social science simulation and applies it to trajectory generation for the first time on real-world activity data. The key conceptual departure from prior mobility generators is the shift from purely numerical/structured inputs (GPS coordinates, POI indices) to semantically rich activity descriptions, leveraging the LLM's ability to reason about motivations and habitual patterns in natural language.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that an LLM agent framework can generate interpretable, semantically grounded daily activity trajectories from real-world data, with a substantial advantage on the activity-routine distribution metric (DARD) over all data-driven baselines. The pandemic simulation experiment shows practical utility for urban policy analysis, where conditioning the agent on external context produces realistic behavioral shifts. Key limitations include the absence of inter-agent interactions, reliance on a single primary dataset (Tokyo), and evaluation primarily with GPT-3.5 rather than frontier models. Open questions include whether the semantic advantage scales to multi-agent urban simulations, whether it generalizes across cities and cultures, and how computational cost constrains deployment at city scale.
