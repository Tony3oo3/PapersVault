## Problem

Adaptive optimizers like AdaGrad and Adam are widely used to reduce hyperparameter tuning, but their performance degrades significantly when data features are poorly scaled or ill-conditioned. This paper asks whether one can design an adaptive method that is provably scale-invariant—i.e., produces identical loss trajectories regardless of how the input features are rescaled—while retaining the best-known O(log T / √T) convergence rate for smooth non-convex objectives.

## Input / Output

KATE operates on an L-smooth, generally non-convex objective f(w) in either expectation or finite-sum form, consuming one unbiased stochastic gradient estimate g_t per iteration along with a per-coordinate hyperparameter vector η ∈ ℝ₊ᵈ and a base step size β. It outputs a sequence of parameter iterates w_t whose minimum expected gradient norm satisfies min_{t≤T} E[‖∇f(w_t)‖] = O(√(log T) / T^{1/4}), and for GLMs with zero initialization, the functional values and classification accuracies are provably independent of any diagonal rescaling of the feature matrix.

## Method

KATE modifies AdaGrad by replacing the square-root denominator √(Σ g_τ²) with the un-squared sum b_t² = Σ g_τ², which makes the per-coordinate step size scale-invariant for generalized linear models. To prevent the step size from decaying too rapidly (which would break convergence), KATE introduces an increasing numerator sequence m_t² = η·b_t² + Σ_{τ≤t} g_τ²/b_τ², yielding the per-coordinate step ν_t[k] = β·m_t[k]/b_t²[k]. The key theoretical results are: (i) Proposition 2.1 proves that for GLMs with w₀ = 0, the iterates satisfy ŵ_t = V·ŵ_t^V and f(ŵ_t) = f^V(ŵ_t^V) under any diagonal scaling V, and (ii) Theorem 3.4 establishes O(√(log T)/T^{1/4}) convergence under bounded-variance noise, matching AdaGrad and Adam. The step sizes are provably monotonically decreasing (Lemma 2.2), which is the critical property enabling the telescoping convergence analysis.

## Results

KATE achieves an O(log T / √T) convergence rate for smooth non-convex stochastic optimization (Theorem 3.4), matching the best-known rates for AdaGrad and Adam, while additionally providing a scale-invariance guarantee for Generalized Linear Models (Proposition 2.1). In the deterministic setting, KATE achieves the optimal O(1/T) rate (Theorem 3.3). Empirically, KATE consistently outperforms AdaGrad across all tested tasks (logistic regression, CIFAR-10 image classification, BERT fine-tuning on Emotions) and matches or surpasses Adam, with the advantage being most pronounced under poor data scaling (e.g., Δ = 10⁻⁸ in logistic regression, where KATE reaches loss 10⁻³ after 10⁴ iterations while other methods fail even after 10⁵).

## Baselines & Comparisons

KATE is compared against AdaGrad, AdaGradNorm, Adam, and SGD variants (SGD-decay, SGD-constant) across logistic regression, image classification (CIFAR-10 with ResNet-18), and text classification (BERT fine-tuning on Emotions). Against AdaGrad, KATE consistently wins: on logistic regression with poorly scaled data (Δ = 10⁻⁸), KATE achieves a functional value of 10⁻³ after 10⁴ iterations while AdaGrad fails to reach this even after 10⁵ iterations; on real datasets (heart, australian, splice), KATE achieves lower loss and higher accuracy than AdaGrad across all 5,000 iterations. Against Adam, KATE matches or slightly surpasses performance on CIFAR-10 (outperforming Adam with η ∈ {0, 0.001, 0.1}) and performs comparably on BERT fine-tuning, while clearly outperforming AdaGrad in both settings. Against SGD variants, KATE is robust to the initialization parameter Δ across a wide range (10⁻⁸ to 10⁸), whereas SGD-decay and SGD-constant diverge when Δ is small. The key differentiator is scale-invariance: KATE's trajectories on scaled and unscaled data are identical (verified in Appendix C), while AdaGrad's performance degrades significantly under poor scaling.

## Relation to Prior Work

KATE is a direct modification of AdaGrad (Duchi et al., 2011) that removes the square root from the per-coordinate stepsize denominator and introduces an increasing numerator sequence m_t to prevent the stepsize from decaying too rapidly. The scale-invariance property connects to Orabona et al. (2015), who proposed a scale-invariant AdaGrad for online convex optimization of GLMs, but KATE achieves the same property without requiring explicit feature vectors in the update rule, making it applicable to general stochastic optimization. The convergence analysis follows the proof techniques of Ward et al. (2020) and Defossez et al. (2020) for AdaGrad/Adam, while the design motivation draws from the broader family of adaptive methods including D-Adaptation, Prodigy, and Polyak stepsizes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Scale-invariance is a practically important property: it means KATE's convergence speed is independent of how the input features are scaled, eliminating a common source of poor performance and reducing the need for data preprocessing or careful hyperparameter tuning. The O(log T / √T) stochastic convergence rate matches the best known for AdaGrad and Adam, and the O(1/T) deterministic rate is optimal. However, the scale-invariance guarantee is proven only for GLMs, and the parameter-free version of the deterministic result (removing the need to know L) is left as future work. Open questions include whether the scale-invariance property extends to non-GLM objectives and whether the increasing numerator m_t can be further refined to improve the stochastic rate beyond the current O(log^{1/2} T / T^{1/4}) for the gradient norm metric.
