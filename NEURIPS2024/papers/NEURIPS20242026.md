## Problem

Existing identifiability guarantees for causal representation learning rely on single-node hard interventions that isolate one latent variable from its causal parents, an assumption that is unrealistic when the latent variables themselves are unknown. This paper asks what the best-achievable identification guarantee is when only data from general (soft) environments are available, and identifies a fundamental surrounded-node ambiguity (SNA) that persists even in the fully linear case with single-node soft interventions.

## Input / Output

The method takes observations x ∈ R^n (n ≥ d) from K ≥ d environments, where each environment generates data via a linear non-Gaussian causal model z = A_k z + Ω_k^{1/2} ε with a shared linear mixing matrix G of full column rank. It outputs the unmixing matrix H = G† and the causal DAG, identified up to the SNA equivalence relation: the graph is recovered exactly (up to node permutation), but each latent variable may be a linear mixture of the ground-truth variables in its surrounding set. The environments must satisfy a node-level non-degeneracy condition requiring the weight vectors at each node to span the full parent space.

## Method

LiNGCReL operates in two stages: first, linear ICA recovers the composite matrices M_k = B_k H for each environment (where B_k = Ω_k^{-1/2}(I − A_k)), and second, an effect-cancellation scheme via orthogonal projections determines the causal structure. The key mechanism is tracking the 'remaining degree of freedom' (RDF): by projecting the row space of M_k for a candidate node onto the orthogonal complement of already-identified ancestors' row spaces and monitoring dimension drops as nodes are added, the algorithm identifies both a valid topological ordering and the exact parent set of each node. The unmixing matrix H is then recovered as intersections of appropriately chosen row spaces of the M_k matrices. Theoretically, the paper proves that O(d) diverse environments suffice for identification up to SNA (Theorem 1), that SNA is an unavoidable intrinsic ambiguity (Theorem 3), and that Ω(d²) single-node soft interventions would be needed to achieve the same guarantee (Theorem 2).

## Results

On synthetic linear non-Gaussian causal models with d ∈ {5, 8, 10} nodes and K = d environments, LiNGCReL recovers the ground-truth causal graph exactly for approximately 80% of sampled models, with a median average SNA error below 1%. For nodes with an empty surrounding set (sur_G(i) = ∅), the SNA error equals the true error and both are small (e.g., 2.8e-3 to 3.4e-3 in the d=5 example), confirming exact recovery up to permutation and sign. For nodes with non-empty surrounding sets, the true error is substantially larger than the SNA error (e.g., 0.99 vs. 1.1e-2 for node 4), validating that the algorithm recovers the model up to the intrinsic surrounded-node ambiguity predicted by Theorem 1.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not include empirical comparisons against other causal representation learning algorithms; it is primarily a theoretical contribution that introduces the first identifiability guarantee for linear CRL from fully general (non-single-node) environments. The key theoretical comparison is with prior work on single-node interventions: Theorem 2 shows that Θ(d²) single-node soft interventions are necessary in the worst case to achieve identification up to SNA, whereas Theorem 1 shows that only O(d) diverse general environments suffice, highlighting a concrete advantage of learning from heterogeneous environments over targeted interventions. The paper also contrasts its SNA-based identifiability with the stronger full-identification guarantees available under hard interventions, proving via Theorem 3 that SNA is an unavoidable barrier even with single-node soft interventions. Empirically, the experiments serve as validation of the theoretical guarantees rather than a benchmark against competing methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the causal representation learning (CRL) literature by moving beyond the standard assumption of single-node hard interventions to the more realistic setting of general environments. It builds on LiNGAM [38] for linear causal graph discovery, adapting its topological-ordering and parent-identification scheme to the setting where observations are unknown linear mixtures of latent variables, and adopts the surrounded-node ambiguity (SNA) framework from Varici et al. [46] as its identifiability target. The key departure from prior CRL work [36, 49, 47] is that no structural assumptions on the intervention type are made—environments can be arbitrary, with no requirement that they correspond to single-node interventions or share any similarity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes that SNA is a fundamental, unavoidable barrier in CRL from general environments, even in the fully linear case with single-node soft interventions, which challenges the intuition that soft interventions can approximate hard interventions arbitrarily well. The result that O(d) diverse environments suffice while Ω(d²) single-node interventions are necessary highlights a practical advantage of collecting data from heterogeneous settings over carefully designed experiments. Open questions include extending the theory to nonlinear mixing functions and causal models, understanding the concrete form of interventions in real-world applications (e.g., single-cell genomics where interventions can reverse edge directions), and developing better finite-sample guarantees for the LiNGCReL algorithm.
