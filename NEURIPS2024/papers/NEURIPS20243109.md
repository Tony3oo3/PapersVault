## Problem

Existing probing studies for world representations in LLMs produce conflicting conclusions because they probe for a complete world state without distinguishing the functional role of different state variables. The paper argues that the necessary level of abstraction varies by task: some require full world dynamics for future-state prediction, while others only need goal-oriented information for task completion. Without systematically controlling for abstraction level, positive or negative probing results cannot be correctly interpreted.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243109/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The LLM receives a textual planning prompt comprising a task instruction, initial and target container-object configurations, and previously executed actions, and generates a sequence of symbolic actions (move, grab, put). For probing, the last hidden state h_t^(m) from the 6th-to-last layer is extracted at each decoding step, paired with the ground-truth world state s_t, and fed to two-layer neural probes that classify individual predicates (e.g., store, agentLoc, nextObj, subgoal). The output is a normalized recovery rate per predicate, grouped by the coarsest abstraction level it belongs to, enabling comparison across raw, world-irrelevant, Q*-irrelevant, and π*-irrelevant abstractions.

## Method

The paper formalizes world state abstraction using RL theory, defining three nested levels: world-irrelevant (preserves transition dynamics and reward), Q*-irrelevant (preserves optimal action values), and π*-irrelevant (preserves only the optimal policy). To instantiate this, they design REPLACE, a text-based planning task with a modular predicate structure (store, held, agentLoc, boxName) that allows analytical derivation of distinct abstract state spaces at each level, ensuring S_φw ≠ S_φQ ≠ S_φπ. LLMs (Llama2, Mistral, Llama3, Phi3, Pythia) are prompted via ICL or fine-tuned with LoRA on the GRIPPER and COOK datasets, and their decoding representations are probed with two-layer classifiers trained per predicate. The key finding is that reasonably performing LLMs predominantly encode goal-oriented abstractions (Q*- and π*-irrelevant) while suppressing raw state and world-irrelevant information, and that both fine-tuning and advanced pre-training selectively strengthen this goal-oriented encoding without improving general world-dynamics recovery.

## Results

On the REPLACE planning task, fine-tuned LLMs (SFT) achieve 88–92% success rate and 61–87% optimal rate on GRIPPER, compared to near-random 2–3% success under in-context learning (ICL). Probing experiments reveal that goal-oriented abstractions (Q*-irrelevant and π*-irrelevant) are recovered at 70–95% recovery rate from SFT model representations, while raw state and world-irrelevant abstractions (e.g., store, boxName) are suppressed to 5–20% recovery rate. Phi3-17b ICL shows intermediate behavior with lower but still significant goal-oriented abstraction recovery (e.g., nextObjDirect at 89.4% vs. 75.7% for Phi3-3.8b), while the train-from-scratch Transformer baseline recovers almost no abstraction above chance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares four model configurations: ICL (in-context learning with 2 demonstrations), SFT (LoRA fine-tuning on 10k samples), a train-from-scratch 6-layer Transformer, and Phi3-17b ICL as a strong pre-trained baseline. SFT dramatically outperforms ICL on both planning (88–92% vs. 2–3% success) and probing of goal-oriented abstractions (e.g., nextObjDirect: 87–92% SFT vs. 13–46% ICL). The train-from-scratch Transformer serves as a lower bound, recovering almost no abstraction above random (all predicates < 1% recovery rate except agentLoc at 93%, which is trivially encoded in the input). Scaling pre-trained models (Pythia-70m → Phi3-3.8b → Phi3-17b) progressively improves goal-oriented abstraction recovery (e.g., nextObjDirect: 15% → 25% → 89%) but does not improve recovery of world-irrelevant predicates like store (stays at 6–11% across all scales). The counterfactual COLORGRIPPER experiment confirms that predicates become recoverable only when they enter the goal-oriented abstraction, validating that the encoding is task-driven rather than a general world model.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on probing world-state information from LLM hidden representations (Li et al. 2021, Li et al. 2022, Kim & Schuster 2023) by reframing the probing target from the raw world state to a hierarchy of state abstractions derived from RL theory (Li et al. 2006, Abel 2022). Prior probing studies implicitly assume that recovering the complete state is both necessary and the right question, but this paper argues that the relevant abstraction level depends on the task, and that conflating levels leads to contradictory conclusions across studies. The REPLACE task draws on the gripper problem (McDermott 2000) and entity tracking (Kim & Schuster 2023) but is specifically designed so that the state spaces at each abstraction level are analytically distinct, enabling clean separation of what is actually encoded.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that well-performing LLMs (whether via fine-tuning or advanced pre-training) preferentially maintain goal-oriented abstractions (Q*-irrelevant, π*-irrelevant) while discarding world dynamics (store, boxName) during decoding provides a principled reconciliation of conflicting prior results: positive probing of state variables in game settings reflects goal-oriented abstraction, while negative results on entity tracking reflect the absence of a more general world model. This reframing has practical implications for interpreting LLM capabilities and for designing future probing experiments. Key open questions include whether these findings generalize beyond synthetic planning tasks to open-ended NLP, whether the LLMs actually *use* the probed abstractions for decision-making (vs. merely encoding them), and how training dynamics (e.g., RL fine-tuning vs. SFT) might shift the balance toward more general world representations.
