## Problem

Existing 3D scene editing methods (NVS, 3D generation, NeRF/3DGS editing) are constrained to synthetic categories, require explicit camera poses, and struggle to generalize to in-the-wild scenes with natural illumination and shadows. The paper reformulates 3D editing as a multi-view 2D inpainting task, arguing that partially inpainting masked regions in existing views is far simpler than synthesizing entire novel views from scratch, and that unmasked contextual cues (lighting, shadows, camera motion) can be activated by foundational T2I models without pose conditions.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an ordered sequence of N+1 views (resized to 256×256) where the first view is a clean, unmasked reference produced by any 2D editing tool, and subsequent views carry masks covering the target object region. Additional inputs include a text prompt (CLIP-encoded) and RAFT optical flow between consecutive frames, all masked to prevent leakage. The output is a set of multi-view inpainted images with cross-view consistency; these can be further fed into Dust3R or MVSFormer++ for dense point clouds and 3D Gaussian Splatting reconstruction without per-scene optimization.

## Method

MVInpainter is built on a frozen SD1.5-inpainting backbone augmented with three key components: (1) AnimateDiff's LoRA and temporal transformer blocks provide video motion priors for structural consistency across views; (2) a Reference Key&Value (Ref-KV) mechanism spatially concatenates the noise-free reference view's key and value features into the target views' self-attention, injecting appearance guidance without the memory cost of full multi-frame attention; (3) a slot-attention flow grouping module uses K=4 learnable query vectors to aggregate dense RAFT optical flow into a single high-level motion embedding, which is concatenated with the CLIP text token to provide pose-free camera-motion control. Two variants are trained: MVInpainter-O on object-centric data (CO3D, MVImgNet) with SAM-tracking object masks and InternLM captions, and MVInpainter-F on forward-facing data (Real10K, Scannet++, DL3DV) with prompt-tuning tokens for scene-level inpainting. At inference, a heuristic masking adaption strategy warps the 3D bounding-box convex hull across views using RANSAC-based perspective transformation on a ground plane, enabling object insertion without any pose information.

## Results

On the object-centric NVS task (CO3D+MVImgNet), MVInpainter achieves PSNR 20.25, LPIPS 0.185, FID 17.56, KID 0.154, and CLIP 0.8182, outperforming all baselines by a wide margin. On the zero-shot Omni3D test set, it maintains strong performance with PSNR 19.19, LPIPS 0.153, FID 16.40, and CLIP 0.7667. For forward-facing scene inpainting (Scannet++/Real10K/DL3DV), MVInpainter achieves the best LPIPS (0.173), FID (15.58), and KID (0.252), while for object removal on SPInNeRF it attains the best LPIPS (0.036), FID (7.66), and DINO-A (0.8972).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MVInpainter decisively outperforms all three object-centric baselines: ZeroNVS [64] (PSNR 12.44, FID 41.90), Nerfiller [80] (PSNR 18.29, FID 36.64), and LeftRefill [7] (PSNR 17.74, FID 38.06) on CO3D+MVImgNet, with the largest gap in FID (17.56 vs. 36–42) and CLIP identity score (0.8182 vs. 0.60–0.66). For forward-facing object removal on SPInNeRF, MVInpainter achieves the best LPIPS (0.036) and FID (7.66), beating ProPainter [102] (LPIPS 0.047, FID 12.25) and LaMa [70] (LPIPS 0.054, FID 15.26), though ProPainter edges ahead on PSNR (31.72 vs. 28.87). In scene-level inpainting, MVInpainter dominates on FID (15.58 vs. 37–62) and KID (0.252 vs. 0.899–2.642) over LaMa, MAT, SD-inpainting, LeftRefill, and ProPainter. Against the NeRF-editing baseline SPIn-NeRF [51], MVInpainter wins on PSNR (28.87 vs. 25.82), LPIPS (0.036 vs. 0.084), and FID (7.66 vs. 38.13) while being slightly lower on DINO-L consistency (0.5937 vs. 0.6350). In inference time, MVInpainter (11.5s for 24 views) is comparable to AnimateDiff (10.1s) but substantially faster than Nerfiller (32.4s) and LeftRefill (33.0s).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reformulates 3D scene editing as multi-view 2D inpainting, departing from the dominant paradigms of novel view synthesis (which generates entire views from scratch) and 3D generation (which relies on synthetic objects and SDS optimization). It builds directly on the SD1.5-inpainting backbone, AnimateDiff's temporal transformers for motion priors, and slot attention for pose-free flow grouping, but the key conceptual move is leveraging unmasked regions to implicitly carry illumination, shadow, and camera-motion cues that 3D generation trained on synthetic data cannot capture. Unlike prior NeRF/3DGS editing methods that require accurate camera poses and costly test-time optimization, MVInpainter operates end-to-end without any pose conditions, and unlike video inpainting methods that handle small viewpoint changes, it targets the larger viewpoint shifts needed for 3D scene editing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work provides a practical, pose-free pipeline for in-the-wild 3D scene editing that avoids the synthetic-data bias of 3D generation and the pose dependency of NeRF/3DGS editing, making it applicable to real-world scenarios like short video editing where poses are unavailable. The clean separation into MVInpainter-O (object-centric) and MVInpainter-F (forward-facing) with a shared frozen SD backbone is architecturally efficient and flexible. Key limitations include failure on 360° scene inpainting where backgrounds are entirely unseen in the reference view, and the frozen SD backbone constrains capacity for large viewpoint changes. Open questions include whether scaling to more powerful attention mechanisms and larger training data can close the gap on extreme viewpoint changes, and whether the inpainting formulation generalizes to dynamic scenes.
