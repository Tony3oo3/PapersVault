## Problem

In federated learning, an honest-but-curious server receives gradient updates from clients and can potentially invert them to recover private data. Prior gradient inversion attacks achieved exact reconstruction only for batch size b=1, while larger batches (b>1) were assumed to permit only approximate recovery. This paper challenges that assumption, showing for the first time that exact reconstruction of entire batches with b>1 is theoretically and practically achievable.

## Input / Output

SPEAR operates on a single fully connected linear layer followed by a ReLU activation, consuming the weight gradient ∂L/∂W (m×n), the bias gradient ∂L/∂b (m×1), and the known network parameters W and b. It outputs the full input batch X (n×b) up to column permutation, with the constraint that b ≤ min(m, n). The method requires no knowledge of the data distribution, labels, or image structure, and works on both image inputs (up to 720×720 ImageNet) and tabular data.

## Method

SPEAR exploits two structural properties of gradients in fully connected ReLU networks: (1) the weight gradient ∂L/∂W = ∂L/∂Z · X^T has rank at most b, enabling a low-rank SVD decomposition into L·R, and (2) ReLU induces ~50% sparsity in the activation gradient ∂L/∂Z. The core challenge is recovering the unknown disaggregation matrix Q (where X^T = Q^{-1}R and ∂L/∂Z = LQ). SPEAR samples random (b−1)×b submatrices of L, computes their kernel directions, and filters out incorrect proposals by checking whether L·q̄' produces a sparse vector (matching the expected sparsity of ∂L/∂Z columns). A two-stage greedy selection then picks the b correct directions: first a dictionary-learning-inspired greedy rank-increasing selection, then iterative swaps optimizing a sparsity matching score λ that measures consistency between reconstructed pre-activations Z' and the ReLU-induced zero pattern. Column scales are recovered in closed form from the bias gradient via a left inverse of L.

## Results

SPEAR achieves exact reconstruction (PSNR > 100) across all tested datasets at batch size b=20, with PSNR of 99.1 on MNIST, 106.6 on CIFAR-10, 110.7 on Tiny-ImageNet, and 125.4–125.6 on ImageNet (224×224 and 720×720), all in under 3 minutes per batch. On ImageNet 256×256, SPEAR reaches a PSNR of 124.2 in 2.0 minutes, compared to 19.6 for Geiping et al. (18 min) and 38.0 for CI-Net with Sigmoid (1.6 hrs). On the Adult tabular dataset (b=16), SPEAR achieves 100% discrete accuracy and a continuous MAE of 20.4, versus 97% and 4922.7 for Tableak, while being 6× faster.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SPEAR dominates all four baselines on every metric tested. Against Geiping et al. [1] (the prior state-of-the-art for ReLU networks), SPEAR improves PSNR from 19.6 to 124.2 (exact vs. approximate reconstruction) while being 9× faster (2 min vs. 18 min). Against CI-Net [12], SPEAR outperforms both its Sigmoid variant (PSNR 124.2 vs. 38.0) and ReLU variant (124.2 vs. 15.6) by a wide margin, and is 100× faster than CI-Net. In the tabular domain, SPEAR beats Tableak [8] on both discrete accuracy (100% vs. 97%) and continuous MAE (20.4 vs. 4922.7) on the Adult dataset while running 6× faster. In the CNN feature-inversion setting (VGG16), SPEAR+FI achieves lower LPIPS (0.362 vs. 0.388) and higher feature cosine similarity (0.984 vs. 0.939) than CPA [9]+FI+Geiping et al., confirming that exact feature recovery translates to better image reconstruction.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the gradient inversion literature in federated learning by combining two previously separate insights: the low-rank structure of weight gradients (exploited approximately by Kariyappa et al.'s Cocktail Party Attack) and ReLU-induced sparsity in activation gradients. Prior work achieved exact reconstruction only for batch size b=1 (Phong et al., Zhu & Blaschko) and relied on optimization-based approximate methods for b>1 (Geiping et al., Yin et al., Vero et al.), operating under the assumption that exact batch recovery was infeasible in the honest-but-curious setting. SPEAR departs from this by framing the problem through a disaggregation matrix recovered via sparsely-used dictionary learning (Spielman et al.), but in a fundamentally different regime: rather than finding the sparsest approximate solution, it identifies the unique exact solution whose sparsity pattern is consistent with the ReLU activation mask.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This result fundamentally revises the threat model for federated learning on ReLU networks: what was assumed to be a safe regime (b>1, honest-but-curious) is now shown to be vulnerable to exact recovery, with practical implications for any deployment relying on small-to-moderate batch sizes. The exponential scaling with batch size suggests that large effective batch sizes via secure aggregation may be the primary practical defense, but the demonstrated robustness to DP-SGD noise (PSNR >28 even when noise magnitude matches gradient magnitude) raises concerns that standard privacy budgets may be insufficient. Open questions include extending the method to convolutional and transformer architectures, making b>100 practical without the hybrid optimization-based approach, and understanding the interaction with more aggressive privacy mechanisms.
