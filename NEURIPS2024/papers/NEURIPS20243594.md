## Problem

Existing KV cache eviction methods for long-context LLM inference permanently discard tokens based on historical or current attention scores, assuming token importance is static. However, the paper observes that token importance is dynamic: tokens initially deemed unimportant can regain critical importance hundreds or thousands of decoding steps later, meaning permanent eviction silently degrades accuracy on long-range dependency tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243594/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on KV cache organized into fixed-size pages (default 32 tokens per page). Each filled page's keys and values are asynchronously copied to CPU memory as a backup, while a compact digest (a bounding-sphere center+radius or bounding-cuboid max/min vector pair, ~2d floats vs. 32×d for the full page) is retained on GPU. At each decoding step, the current query vector is compared against all page digests to produce importance scores, which drive eviction of low-ranked GPU pages and recall of high-ranked evicted pages from CPU before attention is computed over the selected top-k pages.

## Method

ARKVALE is a training-free, page-based KV cache manager that decouples eviction from permanent loss. When a KV page fills, its full keys and values are asynchronously offloaded to CPU memory, and its keys are summarized into a bounding-volume digest (either a bounding-sphere defined by center c and radius r, or a bounding-cuboid defined by element-wise max and min vectors). Before each attention computation, the importance of every page—whether currently resident on GPU or evicted to CPU—is estimated by approximating the maximum dot product between the query and the page's keys using the bounding-volume geometry (e.g., q·c + r|q| for spheres, or Σᵢ max(qᵢ·bᵢ^max, qᵢ·bᵢ^min) for cuboids). Pages are then ranked by these scores; the top-k pages (k governed by a hyperparameter, default 40) are selected for attention, any evicted top-k pages are recalled from CPU backup, and the lowest-ranked resident pages are evicted to make room. The cuboid-mean variant achieves ~95% top-1 recall accuracy in importance estimation, enabling ARKVALE to maintain >95% passkey-retrieval accuracy and near-full-model LongBench scores under 2k–4k cache budgets while delivering up to 2.2× latency and 4.6× throughput speedups.

## Results

On the passkey retrieval task, ARKVALE maintains 95–100% accuracy across all context lengths (10k, 20k, 30k) and cache budgets (512–4096), while all baselines drop to 0–40%. On LongBench (6 datasets), ARKVALE nearly matches the full-model (Origin) performance at cache budgets ≥ 2048, whereas StreamingLLM, H2O, and TOVA show noticeable accuracy degradation below 2048. In terms of efficiency, ARKVALE achieves up to 2.2× (1.7× average) decoding latency speedup and up to 4.6× (3.5× average) batching throughput improvement over the full-KV-cache baseline under a 40 GB memory limit.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ARKVALE decisively outperforms all three baselines on the passkey retrieval task, where StreamingLLM, H2O, and TOVA achieve at most 40% accuracy (and often 0–5%) due to their irreversible eviction of tokens that may later become critical. On LongBench, ARKVALE matches the full-model (Origin) performance at cache budgets of 2048 or higher across all six datasets, while StreamingLLM, H2O, and TOVA exhibit noticeable accuracy drops below 2048 and even at 4096. Among the baselines, TOVA is marginally stronger than H2O and StreamingLLM on passkey retrieval (e.g., 20% vs. 15% at 10k/2048), but all three remain far below ARKVALE's 95–100%. The gap widens with increasing context length and decreasing cache budget, confirming that the dynamism of token importance (which ARKVALE exploits via page recall) is the key differentiator. In throughput experiments, ARKVALE's reduced per-sample KV cache memory enables larger batch sizes, yielding up to 4.6× throughput over the full-cache baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

ARKVALE builds on the paged KV cache management paradigm introduced by vLLM's PagedAttention, extending it from a static memory allocator to a dynamic eviction-and-recall system. It departs from prior KV cache eviction methods (StreamingLLM, H2O, TOVA, Scissorhands) that rely on the persistence assumption—once a token is evicted, it is permanently discarded—by introducing the observation that token importance is dynamic and can shift across decoding steps. The bounding-volume summarization technique (bounding spheres and AABBs) is borrowed from computer graphics collision detection and repurposed here as a lightweight digest for estimating page importance without materializing full keys. Unlike sparse-attention methods (Quest, ALISA, IceFormer) that require the full KV cache to reside in GPU memory, ARKVALE explicitly controls cache size by offloading pages to CPU memory, making it complementary rather than competing with those approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dynamism observation—that tokens initially deemed unimportant can regain critical importance hundreds or thousands of steps later—challenges the persistence hypothesis underpinning most prior eviction strategies and suggests that irreversible eviction is fundamentally lossy for long-context tasks. Practically, the 2.2× latency and 4.6× throughput gains under a 2k–4k cache budget make long-context serving significantly more accessible on commodity hardware. Key limitations include the CPU memory overhead of storing page backups (which may spill to disk under memory pressure) and the prefill-phase backup latency that cannot be fully overlapped. Open questions include whether the bounding-volume digest generalizes to multi-head or multi-layer joint importance estimation, and whether the approach scales to the 128k–2M token contexts that newer models now support.
