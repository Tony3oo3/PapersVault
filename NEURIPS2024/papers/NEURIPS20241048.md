## Problem

Non-autoregressive Transformers (NATs) for direct speech-to-speech translation suffer from incoherent and repetitive generations due to the multi-modality problem, where complex acoustic and linguistic variations in speech create a difficult target distribution for parallel decoding. Existing normalization strategies either require transcription data to build text-to-unit models or rely on manually designed perturbation functions, limiting their generality and ease of use.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241048/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes a source speech waveform as input, encodes it via a Conformer encoder, and produces target speech units from a 1000-cluster K-means vocabulary (derived from 768-dim mHuBERT features). These units are then converted to target waveforms by an off-the-shelf HifiGAN-based unit-vocoder. The entire pipeline is textless: no intermediate transcription or translation is used, and the normalization step (DIFFNORM) operates purely on latent speech representations without requiring any linguistic annotations.

## Method

DIFFNORM normalizes target speech units via a two-stage self-supervised pipeline: a VAE compresses 768-dim mHuBERT features into 128-dim latents (regularized with a Gaussian KL constraint), and a modified DiT-based diffusion model is trained with a multitask objective combining noise estimation, reconstruction, and NLL losses to denoise the latents. At data-preparation time, synthetic noise is injected at a chosen timestep T and the diffusion model reverses the process to produce normalized units that obscure non-crucial acoustic details while preserving semantic content. Separately, classifier-free guidance is adapted to the CMLM decoder: during training, source encoder representations are randomly replaced with a learnable null vector (p=0.15), and during iterative parallel decoding, both conditional and unconditional token distributions are computed and mixed via a guidance weight ω to push generation away from unconditioned (incoherent) outputs. This combination of distribution simplification (DIFFNORM) and model regularization (CG) yields +7 ASR-BLEU for En-Es and +2 for En-Fr over prior NAT baselines while maintaining 14× and 5× inference speedups respectively.

## Results

The best system (CMLM+DIFFNORM+CG) achieves 19.49 ASR-BLEU on En-Es and 17.54 ASR-BLEU on En-Fr, representing approximately +7 BLEU over the prior non-autoregressive CMLM+BiP baseline (12.62 En-Es, 16.97 En-Fr) and +2 BLEU over CMLM alone. The system maintains 5.34× inference speedup (4651 units/sec) over the autoregressive Transformer baseline (870 units/sec), and the paper reports up to 14× speedup for En-Es when comparing against the autoregressive Conformer at higher decoding iterations. All results are evaluated on the CVSS benchmark with 15 decoding iterations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the autoregressive Transformer [31] (10.07 En-Es, 15.28 En-Fr), the proposed CMLM+DIFFNORM+CG wins by ~9.4 BLEU on En-Es and ~2.3 BLEU on En-Fr while running 5.34× faster. Against Norm Transformer [32] (12.98 En-Es, 15.93 En-Fr), the improvement is ~6.5 BLEU (En-Es) and ~1.6 BLEU (En-Fr). Against the strongest prior non-autoregressive system CMLM+BiP [21] (12.62 En-Es, 16.97 En-Fr), the gain is ~6.9 BLEU on En-Es and ~0.6 BLEU on En-Fr. The Conformer autoregressive model (13.75 En-Es, 17.07 En-Fr) is also outperformed on En-Es (by ~5.7 BLEU) while matching on En-Fr (17.54 vs 17.07). All non-autoregressive variants share the same 4651 units/sec throughput, so the quality gains come without any speed penalty.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of non-autoregressive speech-to-speech translation (building on CMLM from Ghazvininejad et al. and the S2UT formulation from Lee et al. and Huang et al.) and diffusion-based denoising (DDPM, DiT). The key departure from prior normalization strategies is that DIFFNORM replaces transcription-dependent CTC fine-tuning (Lee et al., 2022b) and manually designed bilateral perturbation (Huang et al., 2023) with a self-supervised denoising diffusion objective over latent speech representations. Additionally, the paper is the first to adapt classifier-free guidance—a technique well-established in image generation—to regularize non-autoregressive speech-to-unit models, repurposing a generative-modeling trick as a training-time robustness mechanism for a sequence transduction task.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The combination of diffusion-based normalization and classifier-free guidance yields a +7 ASR-BLEU gain for En-Es while preserving the 14× inference speedup of non-autoregressive decoding, making high-quality direct S2ST practically deployable. A key open question is the preprocessing cost: DIFFNORM requires running the diffusion model over all training samples, which the authors acknowledge could become prohibitive at scale beyond the ~200K–800K pairs in CVSS. Whether the approach generalizes beyond the two language pairs and one benchmark tested here, and whether the normalization step can be made online or amortized, remain important directions for follow-up work.
