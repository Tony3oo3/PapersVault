## Problem

CLIP and similar vision-language models excel at recognizing what objects are present in an image but struggle with pixel-level tasks that additionally require understanding where objects are located. Existing open-vocabulary semantic segmentation methods either require expensive pixel-level semantic labels or rely on image-level captions that provide 'what' but not 'where', leading to weak spatial supervision. This paper addresses the gap by leveraging unlabeled masks from vision foundation models (SAM, DINO) as purely spatial supervision, without any semantic labels whatsoever.

## Input / Output

During training, the method consumes RGB images (640×640 for ConvNeXt, 320×320 for ViT) paired with unlabeled masks generated by SAM (from SA-1B) or DINO (via k-means with k=16), with no class names or captions. At inference, it takes an RGB image and free-form text prompts (using a 7-prompt ensemble) and produces a dense image-text similarity map at the CLIP feature resolution (20×20), which is upsampled to 80×80 by a lightweight decoder to yield per-pixel class scores. The output is a soft segmentation map over an unbounded vocabulary of text-defined classes, with no post-processing applied.

## Method

PixelCLIP fine-tunes the CLIP image encoder by mask-pooling its feature map with unlabeled masks to obtain per-mask features, then applies an online clustering algorithm (Sinkhorn-Knopp with entropy regularization) to group these mask features into k global semantic clusters. Each cluster is represented by a learnable class prompt fed into the frozen CLIP text encoder, so the prediction is reformulated as cosine similarity between image features and class text features rather than individual mask features. The clustering is global across the entire training set (not per-image), which forces the learnable prompts to capture general semantic concepts despite the absence of any class names. A momentum encoder (γ=0.999) extracts the mask-pooled features to stabilize joint optimization of the image encoder and class prompts, preventing catastrophic forgetting of CLIP's pre-trained knowledge. Training uses per-pixel binary cross-entropy loss over the clustered ground-truth masks, with the key insight that global semantic clustering is essential—without it, the framework collapses as CLIP is forced to discriminate semantically identical over-segmented regions.

## Results

PixelCLIP achieves an average of +16.2 mIoU improvement over CLIP across five open-vocabulary semantic segmentation benchmarks (COCO-Stuff, ADE-20K, PASCAL-Context, CityScapes, PASCAL VOC). With the default ConvNeXt-B backbone and SAM masks, PixelCLIP reaches 21.4 mIoU on COCO-Stuff, 20.3 on ADE-150, 35.4 on PASCAL-Context, 34.8 on CityScapes, and 67.2 on VOC. The ViT-B/16 variant with SAM masks achieves 23.6, 18.7, 37.9, 27.2, and 85.9 mIoU respectively, surpassing caption-supervised methods like TCL and SegCLIP on all benchmarks while using only unlabeled masks from SA-1B (0.5M images).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PixelCLIP (ViT-B/16, SAM) surpasses TCL [7] on all five benchmarks (e.g., 23.6 vs 19.6 on COCO-Stuff, 85.9 vs 77.5 on VOC) despite using no semantic labels, while TCL requires millions of image-caption pairs. It also outperforms SegCLIP [9] on all reported benchmarks (23.6 vs 26.5* on COCO-Stuff where SegCLIP saw training images, 37.9 vs 24.7 on Context, 85.9 vs 52.6 on VOC). Against SAM-CLIP [10], which leverages 40M image-level semantic labels plus SA-1B, PixelCLIP is competitive (18.7 vs 17.1 on ADE-150, 37.9 vs 29.2 on Context, 85.9 vs 60.6 on VOC) while using only unlabeled masks. Compared to the no-label baseline ZeroSeg [28], PixelCLIP achieves substantial gains (23.6 vs 20.2 on COCO-Stuff, 37.9 vs 20.4 on Context, 85.9 vs 40.8 on VOC). Over the OpenCLIP ConvNeXt-B baseline, PixelCLIP delivers the largest absolute improvements, e.g., +17.7 mIoU on PASCAL-Context (35.4 vs 16.5) and +31.2 on VOC (67.2 vs 34.8). In zero-shot mask classification (Table 2), replacing OpenCLIP with PixelCLIP inside ZegFormer [1] and FC-CLIP [24] yields consistent gains of +2.4 to +19.7 mIoU, confirming the method's plug-and-play applicability.

## Relation to Prior Work

PixelCLIP sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) fine-tuning CLIP for dense prediction (CAT-Seg, ZegCLIP, OWL-ViT), which previously required pixel-level semantic labels; (2) weakly-supervised open-vocabulary segmentation using image-level captions (TCL, SegCLIP, SAM-CLIP), which the authors argue provides only 'what' without 'where'; and (3) self-supervised online clustering (DINOv2's Sinkhorn-Knopp scheme) and prompt learning (CoOp/CoCoOp), which are repurposed here to define implicit semantic categories from unlabeled masks. The key departure is that no semantic labels of any kind—neither pixel-level nor image-level—are used; instead, spatial supervision from SAM/DINO masks combined with globally learned class prompts substitutes for explicit class names, a formulation the authors explicitly contrast with all prior open-vocabulary segmentation work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: PixelCLIP can be dropped into existing CLIP-based segmentation frameworks (ZegFormer, FC-CLIP) by simply replacing the CLIP weights, yielding immediate gains without architectural changes. The conceptual finding that spatial supervision alone (where objects are) can outperform weak semantic supervision (what objects are, via captions) challenges the assumption that some form of class-name signal is necessary for dense vision-language alignment. Open questions include whether better per-mask feature extraction (beyond simple mask pooling) could close the remaining gap to pixel-label methods, whether the learned class prompts can be made more interpretable or controllable, and whether the approach generalizes to panoptic or instance-level tasks. The paper acknowledges that noisy early-stage mask features from the untrained mask decoder may limit performance, and that the implicit class space is difficult to audit.
