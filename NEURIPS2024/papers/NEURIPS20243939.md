## Problem

State-of-the-art equivariant message-passing models for interatomic potentials rely on spherical tensors whose Clebsch-Gordan tensor products are computationally demanding (O(L⁵)) and require complicated numerical coefficients. Existing Cartesian tensor alternatives (e.g., TensorNet, CACE) avoid these costs but lack flexibility in their message-passing mechanisms, restricting them to invariant-filter convolutions and limiting the range of architectures and expressive power. This paper asks whether higher-rank irreducible Cartesian tensors can provide both the computational efficiency of Cartesian representations and the architectural flexibility of spherical ones.

## Input / Output

The model takes an atomic configuration as a graph where nodes carry atomic numbers and 3D Cartesian coordinates, with edges defined by a cutoff distance. Radial distances are embedded via Bessel functions multiplied by a smooth polynomial cutoff, while angular information is encoded as irreducible Cartesian tensors of the unit interatomic vectors up to rank l_max. The network outputs per-atom energy contributions (summed to total energy) and atomic forces computed analytically as negative gradients of the total energy with respect to coordinates, enabling direct use in molecular dynamics simulations.

## Method

The core contribution is a formalism for constructing irreducible (symmetric, traceless) Cartesian tensors of arbitrary rank l from unit vectors (Eq. 1) and defining their irreducible tensor product (Eqs. 2–3) for both even and odd parity cases, yielding output tensors of rank l₃ ∈ {|l₁−l₂|, …, l₁+l₂}. These operations are integrated into an equivariant MPNN that extends the MACE architecture to the Cartesian basis: two-body features are built via equivariant convolutions using irreducible Cartesian tensor filters, and (ν+1)-body features are constructed through iterative ν-fold irreducible Cartesian tensor products. The authors prove equivariance under O(3) and preservation of the traceless property for all message-passing layers. Three product-basis variants are explored—full (all ν-fold products), symmetric (exploiting commutativity of the two-fold product), and latent-space (reducing feature channels during product computation)—to trade off expressiveness against the O(9^L · L!/(2^{L/2}(L/2)!)) per-product cost, which is favorable over the O(L⁵) Clebsch-Gordan cost for the practically relevant range L ≤ 4.

## Results

On the 3BPA dataset, ICTP_full achieves 29.80±0.92 meV energy RMSE and 62.82±1.23 meV/Å force RMSE at 1200 K, outperforming MACE (31.15±1.58 and 65.22±3.52) and NequIP (38.52±1.63 and 76.18±1.11). On Ta-V-Cr-W multicomponent alloys, ICTP_sym achieves 1.38±0.09 meV/atom energy error and 0.028±0.001 eV/Å force error, compared to MACE's 2.19±0.31 meV/atom and 0.029±0.001 eV/Å. The latent-space variant ICTP_sym+lt reduces inference time to 3.51 ms per structure (vs. 4.66 ms for MACE) while maintaining comparable accuracy. On rMD17 with only 50 training configurations, ICTP_sym outperforms both MACE and NequIP for most of the ten molecules.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against MACE (the primary spherical-tensor baseline), ICTP_full slightly outperforms on 3BPA energy and force RMSEs at all temperatures (e.g., 29.80 vs. 31.15 meV at 1200 K) but is ~1.4× slower in inference due to the larger pre-factor K in the Cartesian product basis; ICTP_sym+lt recovers this gap (3.51 ms vs. 4.66 ms) at a small accuracy cost. On Ta-V-Cr-W, ICTP_sym clearly beats MACE in energy prediction (1.38 vs. 2.19 meV/atom) while matching on forces. Against NequIP, ICTP variants are consistently superior on 3BPA (e.g., 29.80 vs. 38.52 meV at 1200 K) and on rMD17 with 50 training configurations. Against TensorNet (the prior Cartesian baseline using rank-2 irreducible tensors), ICTP_sym matches or exceeds performance on rMD17 (e.g., Aspirin E-MAE 2.27 vs. 2.4) while supporting higher-rank tensors and many-body features that TensorNet cannot express. Against CACE (Cartesian ACE), ICTP is substantially more accurate on 3BPA (2.70 vs. 6.3 meV at 300 K). On acetylacetone, ICTP_full and MACE perform nearly identically (0.75±0.04 vs. 0.75±0.05 meV E-RMSE at 300 K), but ICTP uses fewer parameters (2.77M vs. 2.80M).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the MACE architecture (Batatia et al., NeurIPS 2022) from spherical tensors to higher-rank irreducible Cartesian tensors, drawing on the mathematical framework of Lehman & Parke (1989) for constructing symmetric, traceless tensors and their products. It departs from prior Cartesian approaches (TensorNet, CACE) that were restricted to invariant filters and reducible tensors, by introducing an irreducible Cartesian tensor product that enables flexible equivariant convolutions and many-body feature construction analogous to the Clebsch-Gordan product in the spherical basis. The work also contrasts with Gaunt-coefficient-based spherical products, which reduce complexity but exclude odd tensor products, limiting architectural expressiveness.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

For the practically relevant range of tensor ranks (L ≤ 4), irreducible Cartesian tensors offer lower per-operation computational and memory cost than spherical counterparts, while the proven equivariance and traceless properties guarantee correctness. However, the pre-factor K from the ν-fold Cartesian product basis currently makes end-to-end inference slower than MACE, and the architecture follows MACE's restriction to even tensor products; further modifications are needed to fully exploit the Cartesian advantage. Open questions include reducing the product-basis pre-factor, incorporating odd tensor products, and whether the approach scales favorably to higher ranks or larger systems where spherical methods regain their asymptotic edge.
