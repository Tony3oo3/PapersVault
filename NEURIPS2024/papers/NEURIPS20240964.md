## Problem

Diffusion models achieve state-of-the-art image super-resolution but require substantial memory and computation per denoising step, making them impractical for edge devices. Existing binarization methods, designed for classification or single-step SR networks, fail on diffusion models because the UNet's dimension changes break identity shortcuts and skip-connection fusion, and the multi-step iterative process causes activation distributions to shift dramatically across timesteps.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240964/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a low-resolution RGB image (bicubically downsampled from HR) and a noise image at the target HR resolution, which are concatenated along the channel dimension as input to the UNet noise estimator, along with a sinusoidal timestep embedding. It outputs the predicted noise ε_t at each of 50 DDIM sampling steps, iteratively denoising to produce the final high-resolution RGB image at ×2 or ×4 scale. The noise estimation network operates on 64-channel features with 4 encoder/decoder levels.

## Method

BI-DiffSR is a binarized convolutional UNet for the noise estimation network of a conditional diffusion model. Three structural modules address UNet-specific binarization challenges: CP-Down/CP-Up confine resolution changes to dedicated modules (using Pixel-UnShuffle/Pixel-Shuffle) so that ResBlocks maintain consistent input/output dimensions, enabling identity shortcuts for full-precision information transfer; CS-Fusion splits encoder and decoder skip-connection features by odd/even channels, re-pairs them to balance activation value ranges, then fuses via two binarized convolutions and addition. For the activation challenge, TaR and TaA apply K=5 pairs of learnable biases and RPReLU functions (inspired by mixture-of-experts), selecting the appropriate pair based on the current timestep group to adapt the binarized module's input shift and output nonlinearity to the varying activation distributions. The binarized convolutions use XNOR and bit-counting with mean-absolute-value weight scaling, trained end-to-end with L1 loss and the straight-through estimator.

## Results

On Set5 (×2), BI-DiffSR achieves 35.68 dB PSNR and 0.0277 LPIPS, surpassing the best prior binarization method BBCU (34.31 dB / 0.0393 LPIPS) by 1.37 dB and 0.0116 LPIPS. On Urban100 and Manga109 (×2), BI-DiffSR gains 0.92 dB and 1.11 dB PSNR over BBCU, respectively. Compared to the full-precision SR3 model (36.69 dB on Set5), BI-DiffSR achieves comparable perceptual quality (93.6% of SR3's LPIPS on Manga109) while using only 8.3% parameters (4.58M vs. 55.41M) and 20.8% OPs (36.67G vs. 176.41G).

## Baselines & Comparisons

BI-DiffSR outperforms all six binarization baselines across all four datasets at both ×2 and ×4 scales. The two weakest baselines, BNN and DoReFa, fail catastrophically on diffusion models (PSNR drops to 12–16 dB, far below bicubic), confirming that naive binarization is incompatible with the iterative denoising process. XNOR and IRNet achieve moderate results (~32 dB on Set5) but still trail BI-DiffSR by over 3 dB. ReActNet and BBCU are the strongest prior methods, with BBCU achieving 34.31 dB on Set5 (×2); BI-DiffSR surpasses BBCU by 1.37 dB on Set5, 1.34 dB on B100, 0.92 dB on Urban100, and 1.11 dB on Manga109. Against the full-precision SR3 model, BI-DiffSR narrows the PSNR gap to ~1 dB on Set5 while matching or exceeding SR3's LPIPS on Manga109 (0.0172 vs. 0.0161), all with 8.3% of the parameters and 20.8% of the FLOPs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two research lines: diffusion-model-based super-resolution (SR3, DDNM) and extreme model compression via 1-bit binarization (BNN, XNOR-Net, IRNet, ReActNet, BBCU). It departs from prior binarization work, which targets classification or single-step restoration networks, by identifying that the UNet backbone and multi-step iterative denoising of diffusion models create two distinct failure modes—dimension mismatch breaking identity shortcuts and timestep-varying activation distributions—that existing binarization methods do not address. The architectural solutions (CP-Down/Up, CS-Fusion) are novel to this setting, while the timestep-aware activation design borrows the mixture-of-experts idea of selecting among multiple parameter sets, repurposed here to handle the discrete timestep axis rather than input-dependent routing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

BI-DiffSR demonstrates that the most aggressive form of quantization (1-bit) is viable for diffusion-based SR, reducing parameters to 8.3% and FLOPs to 20.8% of full-precision SR3 while retaining ~94% of its LPIPS score on Manga109. This opens a path toward deploying generative SR on resource-constrained edge devices (mobile, IoT), a practical gap that faster-sampling methods (DDIM, DPM-Solver) alone do not close. Open questions include whether the architectural principles (dimension-consistent scaling, channel-shuffle fusion, timestep-grouped activation) transfer to other diffusion-based tasks (inpainting, deblurring, text-to-image), whether they hold under real-world (non-bicubic) degradation, and whether the fixed K=5 timestep grouping is optimal or should be learned.
