## Problem

Existing spatio-temporal imputation methods rely on iterative message-passing across spatial and temporal dimensions, which causes error accumulation and information loss when data is highly sparse (up to 95% missing) and the graph is large-scale. The paper provides a PAC-learnability analysis showing that iterative models' sample complexity grows with both the number of iterations K and the structural sparsity τ of the spatio-temporal graph, making them fundamentally limited for real-world scenarios where entire sensor nodes are missing (spatial missingness) rather than just individual time points.

## Input / Output

The method takes a sparse spatio-temporal tensor X̃ ∈ R^{N×T×F_x} (with NaN entries at missing ST points), a fixed graph G=(V,E,A), and static node attributes as input. It produces fully imputed spatio-temporal representations that feed into task-specific decoders for imputation (MAE on missing entries), congestion classification (weighted cross-entropy), or travel time prediction (MAE). A key distinction from prior work is that spatial missingness—where entire nodes/devices are absent for all time steps—is treated as a first-class problem, not merely point-level dropout, and the method must generalize from as few as 10% of available sensors to the full city-scale graph.

## Method

OPCR operates in two stages. In the first stage (one-step propagation), a GNN encodes static node attributes into spatial embeddings, and a sparse self-attention mechanism propagates values from observed nodes directly to all nodes in a single step; similarly, a temporal self-attention with positional encoding propagates from observed time steps to all time steps per node. This avoids the multi-iteration error accumulation of GNN-based baselines, and the paper proves its PAC-learnability bound depends only on data sparsity ρ, not on graph structure or iteration count. In the second stage (confidence-based refinement), each ST point receives a confidence score β computed from the fraction of its spatial and temporal attention mass that falls on observed versus missing data. These confidences serve as propagation weights in a shallow (typically 2-layer) spatio-temporal message-passing step that refines the combined spatial+temporal imputation, correcting biases introduced by the independent single-dimension reconstructions.

## Results

On the imputation task at 95% missing rate, OPCR achieves the lowest MAE across nearly all datasets and settings. On spatially missing traffic data, OPCR reduces MAE to 2.27 on PEMS-BAY (vs. 4.93 for SPIN-H, a 26% improvement) and 3.20 on METR-LA (vs. 4.51 for CSDI, a 37% improvement). On large-scale PV-US, OPCR reaches 1.73 (point missing) and 2.01 (spatial missing), outperforming the best baseline SPIN-H (1.94 and 2.63) by 11% and 24% respectively. On the T4C22 traffic prediction task, OPCR also achieves the best congestion classification (e.g., 0.8114 on LONDON) and travel time prediction (e.g., 74.44 MAE on LONDON) among all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OPCR outperforms all 11 baselines on the majority of dataset-setting combinations. The strongest competitor is SPIN-H, which leads on small-scale point-missing data (e.g., 1.84 on PEMS-BAY) but degrades sharply under spatial missing (4.93 on PEMS-BAY) due to error accumulation in iterative propagation; OPCR narrows this gap to 2.27. CSDI is the best baseline on METR-LA spatial missing (4.51) but OPCR improves to 3.20 (37% reduction). PriSTI achieves competitive results on traffic spatial missing (3.05 on PEMS-BAY) yet is highly unstable on large-scale PV-US (11.75 vs. OPCR's 2.01). Temporal-only baselines (BRITS, SAITS) fail catastrophically on large-scale datasets (BRITS reaches 30.48 MAE on PV-US), while graph-only baselines (FP, PCFI) and tabular methods (Mean, MF, GRAPE) remain in the 4–12 MAE range, confirming that neither spatial nor temporal information alone suffices. On the T4C22 prediction task, OPCR achieves the best congestion classification (0.8114 LONDON, 0.8444 MADRID, 0.8519 Melbourne) and travel time prediction (74.44, 55.39, 31.54 MAE), beating SPIN-H by 2–3 points in classification and 3–4 MAE in prediction.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant iterative message-passing paradigm in spatio-temporal imputation (SPIN, GRIN, IGNNK) by providing a PAC-learnability analysis that formally shows iterative methods are constrained by both data sparsity and graph structural sparsity, requiring more training samples as iterations increase. Rather than building on a single prior architecture, OPCR repurposes sparse attention mechanisms (from Transformers) and GNNs to perform a single global propagation step, bypassing the multi-hop iterative process entirely. The confidence-based refinement stage draws conceptually from PCFI's propagation confidence idea but applies it to spatio-temporal interactions rather than static graph features.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The PAC-learnability bound provides a rigorous theoretical explanation for why iterative spatio-temporal imputation degrades on large-scale sparse data, moving the discussion beyond empirical observation. Practically, the one-step approach enables efficient imputation on city-scale graphs (50k+ nodes) where iterative methods become computationally prohibitive, lowering deployment barriers for intelligent transportation systems. Key limitations include the assumption of random (MAR) masking, the still-iterative (though shallow) refinement stage, and evaluation limited to traffic and energy domains. Open questions include extending the theory to non-random missing patterns and exploring unsupervised downstream tasks.
