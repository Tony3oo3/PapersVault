## Problem

3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) is discrete and unstructured, preventing standard generative frameworks designed for structured data (voxels, triplanes, images) from directly generating Gaussian primitives. Existing work either voxelizes Gaussians into grids—introducing information loss and capping the number of Gaussians by voxel resolution—or relies on slow per-scene optimization. The paper asks: can we represent 3DGS in a continuous, generatable form that preserves fidelity while allowing arbitrary Gaussian counts?

## Input / Output

The model accepts flexible conditions—text prompts (encoded by frozen CLIP), RGB images (CLIP), partial 3DGS (modified PointNet), or 3D point clouds (PointNet)—or operates unconditionally. It outputs a set of N 3D Gaussian primitives, each with center, rotation, scale, opacity, and color, where N is user-specified (e.g., 50K–350K) and not constrained by any grid resolution. The generation pipeline first produces a single latent vector, then extracts Gaussians via octree-guided sampling and a short optimization step, yielding renderable 3DGS at 1024×1024 resolution.

## Method

DiffGS represents 3DGS through three disentangled continuous functions: a Gaussian Probability Function (GauPF) that models the likelihood of each 3D location being a Gaussian center, a Gaussian Color Function (GauCF), and a Gaussian Transform Function (GauTF) that predict appearance and geometric attributes from a known center. These functions are implemented as a triplane decoder followed by small MLP predictors, and are compressed into a single latent vector by a Gaussian VAE (PointNet-based encoder + triplane decoder). A latent diffusion model (DALLE-2 architecture) is trained in this latent space to generate new 3DGS shapes, with cross-attention conditioning for controllable generation. To recover discrete Gaussians from the continuous functions, an octree-guided extraction algorithm progressively subdivides 3D space to locate high-probability regions, samples proxy centers, and refines them via gradient optimization against GauPF—enabling arbitrary Gaussian counts without voxelization.

## Results

On unconditional 3DGS generation over ShapeNet, DiffGS achieves FID-50K of 47.03 and KID-50K of 3.436‰ on Airplane, and FID-50K of 35.28 and KID-50K of 2.148‰ on Chair, outperforming all baselines. For text-to-3D generation, DiffGS attains a CLIP score of 33.42, surpassing DreamGaussian (29.08), LGM (32.52), and Shap-E (32.76). In terms of efficiency, DiffGS uses only 127.4M parameters with 9.5s inference time, compared to 929.9M/99.7s for DiffTF and 258.7M/197s for DreamGaussian.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiffGS outperforms the GAN-based GET3D [16] on Chair unconditional generation with FID 35.28 vs. 59.51 and KID 2.148 vs. 2.414, while GET3D does not report Airplane results. Against the diffusion-based DiffTF [4], DiffGS achieves substantially lower FID (47.03 vs. 110.8 on Airplane; 35.28 vs. 93.02 on Chair) and KID (3.436 vs. 9.173; 2.148 vs. 6.708), indicating markedly sharper and more diverse generations. For text-to-3D, DiffGS (CLIP 33.42) beats DreamGaussian [59] (29.08) by 4.34 points, LGM [58] (32.52) by 0.9 points, and Shap-E [27] (32.76) by 0.66 points. In image-to-3D visual comparisons (Fig. 7), DiffGS produces more detailed and accurate renderings than Splatter Image [57], TriplaneGaussian [86], LGM [58], and DreamGaussian [59]. For unconditional car generation (Fig. 10), DiffGS visually surpasses both the GAN-based EG3D [5] and the NeRF-based SSD-NeRF [8] in geometry and appearance fidelity. In efficiency (Table 6), DiffGS is the most parameter-efficient (127.4M) among all compared methods and matches GET3D (5.0s) and LGM (10.5s) in inference speed while being far faster than DiffTF (99.7s) and DreamGaussian (197s).

## Relation to Prior Work

DiffGS departs from concurrent voxelized 3DGS generation approaches (GaussianCube, GVGEN) that transport Gaussians into structural grids, instead representing the discrete and unstructured 3DGS with three continuous neural functions (GauPF, GauCF, GauTF) that model geometry, color, and transforms independently. The generative pipeline follows the established VAE + Latent Diffusion Model schema (analogous to Stable Diffusion's architecture) but applied to the latent space of these Gaussian Splatting Functions, with a triplane decoder and DALLE-2-style U-Net. The octree-guided extraction algorithm draws a direct analogy to Marching Cubes for SDFs, but adapted to sample and optimize Gaussian centers from a probability field rather than extract an isosurface.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DiffGS offers a scalable path to 3DGS generation without the resolution ceiling and information loss inherent in voxelization, enabling arbitrary Gaussian counts and native 3DGS output directly usable in real-time rendering pipelines. The multi-modal conditioning (text, image, partial 3DGS, point clouds) positions it as a flexible backbone for downstream 3D content creation. However, experiments are confined to ShapeNet (airplane, chair, car) and DeepFashion3D, the acknowledged tendency toward overly creative color schemes limits real-world applicability, and fair comparisons with Objaverse-trained methods remain open. Scaling to diverse object categories, dynamic scenes, and larger-scale datasets are natural next steps.
