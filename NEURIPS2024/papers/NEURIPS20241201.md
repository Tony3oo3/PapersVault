## Problem

When bidders use no-regret learning algorithms in repeated auctions with persistent valuations, the runner-up bidder fails to converge to truthful bidding, causing second-price auctions with reserves (Myerson's optimal mechanism for rational bidders) to generate strictly less revenue. The paper asks whether truthful auction mechanisms exist that both promote convergence to truthful bidding among learning agents and achieve strong revenue guarantees, and how randomization can be leveraged to achieve this.

## Input / Output

The setting involves two bidders with persistent valuations drawn i.i.d. from a regular distribution over the discrete set B_Δ = {0, 1/Δ, …, 1}, who use mean-based no-regret learning algorithms (e.g., MWU) to bid over T rounds with full feedback. The auctioneer must commit to auction rules (allocation and payment functions) before the interaction begins, oblivious to the bid history, and the output is characterized by revenue guarantees and auctioneer regret bounds measured against the revenue of SPA with truthful bids. A key constraint is that bidders never bid above their true valuation, and the auctioneer's decisions cannot adapt to observed bids to maintain truthfulness.

## Method

The paper introduces three complementary technical contributions. First, it characterizes convergence in general deterministic truthful auctions, showing a sharp phase transition: the runner-up converges to truthful bidding only if its learning rate is strictly faster (by a factor of ω(1)) than the winner's, using an epoch-based analysis that tracks cumulative utility differences across O(1/η_T) windows. Second, it defines strictly-IC auctions (where truthful bidding is strictly dominant) and provides a black-box transformation that mixes any IC auction with a strictly-IC auction at a vanishing rate q_T = o(1), guaranteeing truthful convergence while keeping allocation and payment rules o(1)-close to the original. Third, in the non-asymptotic prior-free regime, it shows that a constant auction policy incurs Θ̃(T^{3/4}) auctioneer regret (tight), while a two-phase schedule—using a strictly-IC auction (e.g., the Staircase Auction with γ_A = Θ(1/Δ²)) for T₀ rounds followed by SPA—achieves the optimal Θ̃(√T) regret, separating exploration and exploitation in a way that contrasts with standard bandit settings.

## Results

The paper establishes that in repeated auctions with persistent valuations and mean-based no-regret learners (e.g., MWU), the runner-up bidder in any deterministic truthful auction converges to bidding strictly below its true value when its learning rate is not strictly faster than the winner's, yielding a revenue gap of at least a constant c > 0 compared to the rational-bidder benchmark (Theorem 3.1). In the non-asymptotic regime, the auctioneer regret for any fixed truthful auction is tightly bounded as Õ(T^{3/4}) (Propositions 5.1–5.2), while allowing a single pre-committed switch from a strictly-IC auction to SPA reduces regret to Õ(√T) (Theorem 5.3), which is near-optimal (Proposition 5.4). Asymptotically, a randomized strictly-IC auction achieves revenue within o(1) of Myerson's optimal auction (Corollary 4.5), in stark contrast to SPA with reserve which is strictly suboptimal.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is between SPA with reserve (the Myerson-optimal mechanism for rational bidders) and randomized truthful auctions in the learning-agent setting. The paper proves that SPA with reserve is strictly suboptimal: the runner-up bidder's expected bid is strictly below its valuation, causing a constant revenue loss that persists as T → ∞ (Theorem 3.1). In contrast, a randomized strictly-IC auction (constructed via a black-box transformation, Theorem 4.4) recovers the full Myerson-optimal revenue up to o(1). For the finite-horizon comparison, a constant auction policy (using the same mechanism for all T rounds) incurs Õ(T^{3/4}) auctioneer regret, while a two-phase schedule (strictly-IC auction for T₀ rounds, then SPA) achieves Õ(√T) regret — an Õ(T^{1/4}) improvement. This √T bound is shown to be near-tight even when the auctioneer can choose a different auction every round (Proposition 5.4). The paper also contrasts its setting with Feng et al. (2021), where i.i.d. redrawn valuations provide inherent randomness that naturally drives convergence to truthful bidding, whereas persistent valuations require external randomization in the mechanism to achieve the same effect.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the model of Kolumbus and Nisan (2022a), who showed that the runner-up bidder in a second-price auction with persistent valuations and no-regret learners fails to converge to truthful bidding, by generalizing this failure to all deterministic truthful auctions and revealing that the learning-rate ratio between bidders creates a sharp phase transition in convergence behavior. It builds on the mean-based no-regret framework of Braverman et al. (2018) and departs from the i.i.d.-valuation setting of Feng et al. (2021), where inherent randomness in redrawn valuations naturally drives convergence. The paper also contrasts with Cai et al. (2023), who exploit learning behavior to extract welfare via non-truthful auctions, whereas here the focus is on truthful mechanisms and revenue maximization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central finding—that randomized truthful auctions can strictly outperform Myerson's SPA-with-reserve when bidders are no-regret learners—fundamentally challenges the classical mechanism design paradigm and suggests that auction platforms (e.g., online ad exchanges) should incorporate randomization to incentivize truthful learning. The non-asymptotic results (Θ̃(T^{3/4}) vs. Θ̃(√T) regret) provide actionable guidance: a two-phase schedule (strictly-IC auction followed by SPA) is optimal, which is counterintuitive compared to standard exploration-exploitation intermixing. Open questions include extending to multiple bidders, partial feedback, and non-persistent valuations, as well as understanding whether the meta-game (agents choosing what value to report to their bidding algorithm) remains truthful under richer auction classes.
