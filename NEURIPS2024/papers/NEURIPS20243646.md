## Problem

LLMs frequently generate chain-of-thought rationales containing mistakes and contradictions, making it difficult to trust their final predictions. While CoT prompting improves answer accuracy, the paper identifies that it simultaneously introduces inconsistencies between the model's internal representations in middle layers and those in final layers, undermining the reliability of the reasoning process. No existing calibration method can assess the reliability of individual reasoning paths without additional training or human annotations.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a standard text prompt (context + query in a true-or-false QA format) and, during inference, extracts hidden states at the answer token from every intermediate layer of a decoder-only Transformer. It produces two outputs: an internal consistency score (a value in [0,1] representing the fraction of layers whose logit-lens-decoded prediction matches the final answer) and a calibrated final answer selected by up-weighting sampled reasoning paths by their consistency scores. The approach requires no training and operates entirely at inference time on off-the-shelf models.

## Method

The core mechanism uses the logit lens to decode latent predictions from each intermediate layer's hidden state at the answer token, then defines internal consistency as the fraction of layers whose decoded prediction agrees with the final stated answer (Equation 2). This consistency score is integrated into self-consistency decoding: multiple reasoning paths are sampled, and the final answer is selected by summing internal consistency scores across paths for each candidate answer, effectively up-weighting paths with robust cross-layer agreement. A key preprocessing step balances the miscalibrated latent predictions at each layer using a median-threshold procedure to avoid systematic bias toward one class. The paper further introduces layer-weighted aggregation (SC+IC(tune) and SC+IC(transfer)), where a learned weight vector over layers replaces uniform averaging, and demonstrates that weights tuned on one dataset transfer well to others. Analysis of attention and FFN components reveals that middle layers attend strongly to query and reasoning steps while later FFN layers dominate final outputs, explaining the mechanistic origin of the observed internal inconsistency.

## Results

SC+IC achieves mean calibrated accuracy of 62.4% (CoT) and 71.0% (L2M) on Llama-2-7B, outperforming SC (61.8%/69.8%) and SC+Δ (62.2%/70.3%). With layer-weighted aggregation (SC+IC(tune)), gains increase further to 63.8%/73.4% on Llama-2-7B and 81.1%/84.4% on Mixtral-8×7B. Across all four models, SC+IC consistently outperforms both baselines with mean improvements of 1.8%–4.9% in calibrated accuracy, with the largest gains on symbolic (CoinFlip) and logical reasoning (PrOntoQA, ProofWriter) tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares SC+IC against two baselines: standard Self-Consistency (SC; Wang et al., 2022) and SC+Δ (Wang and Zhou, 2024), which uses a logit-based confidence measure Δ_k = p(ŷ¹|x) − p(ŷ²|x). SC+IC wins on the mean across all four models for both CoT and L2M prompting, with the largest margin over SC on Mistral-7B (76.1% vs. 74.6% CoT, +1.5 pp) and Llama-2-13B (70.6% vs. 68.9%, +1.7 pp). Against SC+Δ, SC+IC also leads on mean CoT for every model (e.g., 76.1% vs. 74.9% on Mistral-7B; 80.6% vs. 80.1% on Mixtral-8×7B), though the gap narrows for larger models. On individual dataset cells, SC+IC occasionally ties or slightly trails SC+Δ (e.g., PrOntoQA CoT on Llama-2-7B: 49.0 vs. 50.2), but the consistent mean advantage and the larger gains from layer-weighted variants (SC+IC(tune)/transfer) confirm the overall superiority of the internal-consistency weighting strategy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the logit lens / tuned lens line of work (nostalgebraist, 2020; Belrose et al., 2023) from interpretability diagnostics to a practical calibration mechanism for chain-of-thought reasoning. It builds directly on the self-consistency ensemble framework (Wang et al., 2022) by replacing majority voting with internal-consistency-weighted aggregation, and adopts the latent-prediction balancing procedure from Halawi et al. (2023). Unlike prior calibration approaches that operate on final-layer logits or require trained classifiers, the paper's internal consistency metric is training-free and draws on intermediate-layer representations, positioning it at the intersection of mechanistic interpretability and inference-time calibration.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that CoT prompting *decreases* internal consistency (middle-layer vs. final-layer agreement) while improving accuracy is a useful diagnostic: it suggests verbalized rationales can introduce noise that the model's later layers partially override, offering a mechanistic explanation for unfaithful reasoning. The layer-weighted aggregation showing transferable importance patterns across tasks hints at a general structure in how Transformers consolidate predictions, though the current scope is limited to binary True/False tasks and decoder-only models. Open questions include extending the approach to multi-class or open-ended generation, encoder-decoder architectures, and more complex prompting strategies (tree-of-thoughts, program-aided reasoning), where the binary-answer assumption no longer holds.
