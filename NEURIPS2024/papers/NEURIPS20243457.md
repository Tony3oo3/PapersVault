## Problem

No dataset combining 3D crystal structures with experimental superconducting transition temperatures (Tc) existed, and over 50% of structures in major databases like ICSD are disordered, yet existing GNN-based crystal property predictors were designed almost exclusively for ordered structures. This paper addresses both gaps by introducing a structure-to-Tc dataset and methods that natively handle substitutional and positional disorder.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243457/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For prediction, the input is a periodic crystal graph where each site carries a lattice matrix, Cartesian coordinates, atomic species features, and occupancy weights (w_i) that encode disordered sites—substitutional disorder (multiple species at one site) and positional disorder (partial occupancy at a single site). The model outputs a scalar Tc in Kelvin. For generation, DiffCSP-SC takes a target Tc percentile as guidance and produces a full crystal structure (lattice matrix, fractional coordinates, and atomic species), with generation restricted to ordered crystals (w_i=1) to reduce the search space.

## Method

SODNet is an SE(3)-equivariant graph attention network that represents disordered sites via occupancy-weighted linear combinations of k-hot atomic embeddings for nodes and occupancy-scaled RBF×spherical-harmonics features for edges. Its equivariant attention splits messages into type-0 scalars (processed with leaky ReLU + softmax to yield attention weights β_ij) and type-L vectors (processed with an equivariant gate activation followed by tensor-product with spherical harmonics to yield messages v_ij), then aggregates β_ij·v_ij over neighbors to predict Tc. DiffCSP-SC extends the DiffCSP diffusion framework by replacing the EGNN backbone with a transformer that uses Fourier-transformed relative fractional coordinates (ensuring periodic translation invariance) and L^T L (ensuring O(3)-equivariance) in its attention keys and values, and incorporates SODNet as a property-guidance model during the reverse diffusion process. Both models are pre-trained on ~1.14M crystals from Materials Project, OQMD, Matgen, and ICSD before fine-tuning on the 1,578-entry SuperCon3D dataset.

## Results

On the SuperCon3D dataset, SODNet achieves a MAE of 0.505 log(K) and R² of 0.748 when trained and tested on all disorder types (O/SD/PD/SPD), outperforming the next-best deep learning method Matformer (MAE 0.748, R² 0.570) by 17.6% in MAE and 4.4% in R². For the inverse generation task, DiffCSP-SC with pre-training achieves SR10=0.37, SR30=0.37, and SR50=0.50, surpassing DiffCSP (0.30/0.30/0.45), SyMat (0.28/0.28/0.35), and CDVAE (0.25/0.25/0.30). On real-world validation with 7 recently discovered superconductors not in training data, SODNet predicts Tc with relative errors below 21% for 6 out of 7 materials.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SODNet outperforms all deep learning baselines on the Tc prediction task. Against Matformer (the strongest deep learning baseline), SODNet reduces MAE by 17.6% (0.505 vs 0.748) and improves R² by 4.4% (0.748 vs 0.570) on the full disorder setting. Against MEGNet, which is the only other method handling disordered structures, SODNet achieves a 41.7% MAE reduction and 66.1% R² improvement on PD-disordered data (0.505 vs 0.889 MAE; 0.748 vs 0.431 R²). Among the remaining GNN baselines (SchNet, CGCNN, DimeNet++, SphereNet, ALIGNN), SODNet leads by 0.25–0.39 in MAE and 0.28–0.35 in R². On the generative task, DiffCSP-SC with pre-training achieves SR50=0.50, beating DiffCSP (0.45), SyMat (0.35), and CDVAE (0.30); without pre-training, all generative models perform poorly (SR50 ≤ 0.10), highlighting the critical role of large-scale pre-training. Notably, the traditional SVM-geo method achieves a higher R² (0.827) than SODNet on ordered-only data, but SODNet's advantage emerges when disordered structures are included, where SVM-geo cannot operate.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the equivariant GNN line of work for crystal property prediction (CGCNN, MEGNet, ALIGNN, Matformer) by introducing a unified graph representation that handles both substitutional and positional disorder, going beyond MEGNet's substitutional-only treatment. For structure generation, it builds directly on DiffCSP's joint lattice-and-fractional-coordinate diffusion framework, replacing its EGNN message passing with a transformer-based attention mechanism and adding superconductivity-guided optimization. The key conceptual departure is the construction of SuperCon3D, the first dataset pairing experimental Tc values with 3D crystal structures (including disordered sites), filling a gap where prior datasets (SuperCon, S2S, 3DSC) offered only compositions, binary labels, or computationally derived Tc.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The SuperCon3D dataset and the SODNet/DiffCSP-SC pipeline provide the first end-to-end data-driven workflow for superconductor discovery that explicitly accounts for the >50% of ICSD structures that are disordered, a gap that has limited prior GNN-based approaches. However, the dataset's small size (1,578 entries), elemental skewness toward Cu and O, and the 67% prediction error on extreme-pressure Ti highlight that data-driven Tc prediction remains far from reliable for novel compositions. Open questions include whether the disordered-structure representation generalizes to interstitial disorder (absent here), whether the generated candidates will survive wet-lab validation, and whether the iterative dataset-enrichment pipeline can overcome the current data bottleneck.
