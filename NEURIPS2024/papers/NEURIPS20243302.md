## Problem

In vision models, gradient updates to first-layer weights are directly proportional to input pixel magnitudes, causing high-contrast or extreme-brightness images to dominate learning while low-contrast images contribute minimally. This stands in contrast to language models, where embedding layers are trained directly via the gradient of the pre-activation. The paper asks whether one can make first-layer pre-activations in vision models trainable with the same dynamics as embedding layers, without modifying the architecture or preprocessing.

## Input / Output

TrAct operates on standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR, 224×224 for ImageNet) that are conventionally standardized to mean 0 and std 1, and produces class labels via the unchanged downstream network. For convolutional models, the input is unfolded into patches so that the first layer is expressed as a matrix multiplication; for vision transformers, the patch embedding projection is the target layer. The method modifies only the backward pass of this single first layer, leaving all forward computations, downstream layers, and inference completely unchanged.

## Method

TrAct conceptually performs a gradient descent step on the first-layer activations z to produce a target z*, then solves a ridge-regression sub-problem to find the weight update ΔW that minimizes ‖z* − (W+ΔW)x‖² subject to a small-update constraint. This yields the closed-form update W ← W − η·∇_z L(z)·xᵀ·(xxᵀ/b + λI_n)⁻¹, where n is the input dimensionality (e.g., 27 for a 3×3 RGB conv kernel, 768 for a 16×16 patch embedding) and λ is a single regularization hyperparameter (default 0.1). The correction term (xxᵀ/b + λI_n)⁻¹ effectively whitens the input-dependent gradient, decoupling the magnitude of weight updates from pixel values. Implementation requires only a custom backward function for the first layer (a single matrix inversion of size n×n), making it trivially integrable into existing training loops with PyTorch or JAX, and compatible with any optimizer (SGD, Adam, etc.) by treating the TrAct update as the gradient fed to the optimizer.

## Results

On CIFAR-100 (36 architectures, 200 epochs), TrAct improves average top-1 accuracy by 0.49% (75.90% → 76.39%) and top-5 by 0.23% (93.19% → 93.42%), outperforming the baseline on 33/36 and 34/36 models respectively. On ImageNet, ResNet-50 trained with TrAct for 60 epochs matches the 90-epoch baseline (75.68% vs 75.70% top-1), yielding a 1.5× speedup. For DeiT-III ViT-S on ImageNet, TrAct achieves 82.18% top-1 at 800 epochs vs 81.97% baseline, and the runtime overhead is only 0.08–0.25% for large models. Overall, TrAct accelerates training by 1.25×–4× depending on model size.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the DeiT-III training recipe [5] (Touvron, ECCV 2022), TrAct consistently improves ImageNet ViT results without changing any hyperparameters: ViT-S at 400 epochs reaches 81.50% top-1 vs 81.23% (reproduced baseline), and at 800 epochs reaches 82.18% vs 81.97%; ViT-B at 400 epochs reaches 83.58% vs 83.34%. Against Dual PatchNorm [9] (Kumar, TMLR 2023) in the CIFAR-10 ViT ablation, TrAct outperforms it in most training lengths; DualPatchNorm is comparable to simple input patch normalization and only insignificantly beats TrAct at 200 epochs, while at 800 epochs both normalization-based methods fail to improve over the vanilla baseline whereas TrAct still gains accuracy. In the broader CIFAR-100 comparison across 36 architectures, TrAct beats the vanilla training baseline (same hyperparameters, SGD with momentum) on 31/36 models for both top-1 and top-5 simultaneously, with the overall best single-model result also achieved by TrAct.

## Relation to Prior Work

TrAct draws on the ridge regression closed-form solution (Hoerl & Kennard, 1970) to derive a corrective term for first-layer gradient updates, and is conceptually inspired by second-order optimization methods (ISAAC, K-FAC, Newton Losses) that modify gradient computation. It departs from prior normalization-based approaches (batch norm, layer norm, DualPatchNorm) by targeting the training dynamics directly rather than modifying inputs or architecture. The paper's core conceptual framing—bridging the gap between embedding-layer training in language models and linear/conv-layer training in vision models—is novel, positioning TrAct as a gradient-level intervention rather than a data- or architecture-level one.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TrAct offers a drop-in, single-hyperparameter modification to first-layer backpropagation that consistently speeds up training by 1.25×–4× across 50+ experimental settings spanning CNNs and ViTs, with negligible inference impact and no architecture changes. Its practical appeal is high: integration is a one-line backward-pass replacement, and the default λ=0.1 works robustly across all tested configurations. Limitations include applicability only to the first layer (frozen first layers, as in some detection pipelines, require unfreezing), and the method has not been explored for deeper layers where the input-proportionality argument is less direct. Open questions include whether the approach extends to generative models, video, or multi-modal settings where first-layer inputs have different statistical structure.
