## Problem

First-order methods for bilevel optimization have been developed only for unconstrained problems, leaving the important constrained setting underexplored. Existing approaches for linearly constrained bilevel programs either require Hessian computations (preventing fully first-order operation) or impose strong regularity assumptions directly on the hyperobjective F that are impossible to verify in practice. This paper asks whether fully first-order algorithms with finite-time stationarity guarantees can be designed for bilevel programs with linear constraints, using assumptions solely on the constituent functions f and g.

## Input / Output

The algorithms consume only zeroth- and first-order oracle access to the upper-level objective f and lower-level objective g, along with the linear constraint matrices A, B, b and an initial point x₀. For the equality-constrained setting, x is restricted to a convex compact set X and the output is an ε-stationary point of F (gradient norm ≤ ε). For the inequality-constrained setting, x is unconstrained and the output is a (δ,ε)-Goldstein stationary point, meaning a convex combination of subgradients within a δ-ball has norm at most ε. The inequality setting with dimension-free rates additionally requires oracle access to the optimal dual variable λ* of the lower-level problem.

## Method

For linear equality constraints, the key insight is a finite-difference hypergradient proxy: instead of differentiating through y*(x), the algorithm solves a perturbed lower-level problem (minimizing g + δf over the same linear constraint) and approximates (dy*/dx)ᵀ∇_y f by the difference of KKT gradients divided by δ, yielding O(δ) error without any Hessian computation. Projected gradient descent on the resulting smooth F then achieves Õ(ε⁻²) oracle calls. For linear inequality constraints, where F can be nonsmooth and nonconvex, the paper develops two new algorithms (Algorithms 2 and 3) that combine clipped momentum updates with the online-to-nonconvex framework of Cutkosky, Mehta, and Orabona to handle inexact oracles, achieving Goldstein stationarity in Õ(dδ⁻¹ε⁻³) calls. The dimension-free Õ(δ⁻¹ε⁻⁴) rate for inequalities is obtained by reformulating the problem via a penalty function to construct an inexact gradient oracle for F, which is then fed into Algorithm 3.

## Results

The paper establishes three main theoretical complexity guarantees: (1) for linear equality constraints, Õ(ε⁻²) gradient oracle calls to reach ε-stationarity of the hyperobjective F; (2) for linear inequality constraints, Õ(dδ⁻¹ε⁻³) oracle calls to reach (δ,ε)-Goldstein stationarity; and (3) for linear inequality constraints with oracle access to the optimal dual variable, a dimension-free rate of Õ(δ⁻¹ε⁻⁴) oracle calls. Numerically, on a toy bilevel problem with d_x=100, d_y=200, and n_const=40, the proposed fully first-order method (F2CBA) converges to the same optimal solution at the same rate as cvxpyLayer, while achieving significantly lower per-iteration computation cost, especially as d_y scales from 100 to 1000.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares its fully first-order constrained bilevel algorithm (F2CBA, Algorithm 3 + Algorithm 4) against cvxpyLayer [Agrawal et al., 2019], a differentiable convex optimization layer that requires second-order derivatives (Hessian-vector products). On the toy bilevel problem (Problem 6.1) with d_x=100, d_y=200, and n_const=40, both methods converge to the same optimal solution at the same rate, confirming that the inexact first-order gradients (with α=0.1) do not degrade the final solution quality. However, F2CBA significantly outperforms cvxpyLayer in per-iteration computation cost, with the gap widening as the lower-level dimension d_y increases from 100 to 1000, since cvxpyLayer's Hessian-based backpropagation scales poorly with d_y. Varying the gradient inexactness α (Figure 2b) shows that larger α slows convergence but both methods still reach the same optimum, validating the theoretical (δ,ε)-stationarity guarantee. The comparison is limited to a single synthetic problem and one baseline, so the practical advantage over other constrained bilevel methods (e.g., Khanduri et al. [37], Yao et al. [38], Lu and Mei [39]) is established only theoretically.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the fully first-order bilevel optimization framework of Liu et al. [28] and Kwon et al. [40] (unconstrained) to the linearly constrained setting, where prior work (Khanduri et al. [37]) required Hessian computations and unverifiable regularity assumptions on the hyperobjective F. For the inequality-constrained case, the paper departs from Yao et al. [38] and Lu and Mei [39], which used KKT stationarity as a proxy, by providing guarantees directly on the hyperobjective F via Goldstein stationarity. The inexact gradient oracle for the inequality case is constructed through a novel penalty-method reformulation, while the nonsmooth nonconvex optimization subroutines (Algorithms 2 and 3) leverage the online-to-nonconvex framework of Cutkosky, Mehta, and Orabona [47] to handle inexact oracles—a non-trivial extension of their exact-gradient results.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first fully first-order method with finite-time hypergradient stationarity guarantees for linearly constrained bilevel optimization, removing both the Hessian requirement and the unverifiable assumptions on F that limited prior work. Two important limitations remain: the dimension-free rate for inequality constraints (Theorem 1.4) requires exact access to the optimal dual variable λ*, which is not available in a first-order fashion, and the Õ(δ⁻¹ε⁻⁴) rate may not be tight (the best known nonsmooth nonconvex rate is O(δ⁻¹ε⁻³)). Extending the results to general convex constraints and removing the dual-access assumption are identified as key open directions.
