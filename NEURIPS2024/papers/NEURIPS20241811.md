## Problem

Materials science knowledge is scattered across over 10 million papers, and existing structured databases (Materials Project, OQMD, NOMAD) rely heavily on computational predictions (DFT, MD) that often diverge from experimental observations. Prior materials knowledge graphs (MatKG, MatKG2, MMKG) either require extensive expert annotation, use multi-step NER/RE pipelines that lose source traceability, or predict relationships between nodes rather than extracting them from real text, making them difficult to update and less credible. This paper addresses the need for an experimentally grounded, traceable, and efficiently updatable materials knowledge graph.

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes 150,000 peer-reviewed abstracts from Web of Science (energy materials: batteries, solar cells, catalysts) stored as JSON 'DOI–text' pairs, with a small training set of 75 expert-annotated abstracts and a 2,000-entity binary classification set for formula/name disambiguation. The output is the MKG containing 162,605 nodes and 731,772 directed edges organized under a fixed ontology (Name, Formula, Acronym, Structure/Phase, Application, Property, Descriptor, Domain, Synthesis, Characterization, DOI), where every triple is linked to its source DOI for provenance. The graph is stored in Neo4j (supporting subgraph matching queries) and exported in RDF and CSV formats, and the graph-completion module additionally outputs ranked material–application link predictions.

## Method

The core contribution is an end-to-end fine-tuned LLM (Darwin 7B, selected over LLaMA/LLaMA2) that performs NER and RE simultaneously in a single pass, preserving the source DOI for every extracted triple—unlike MatKG2's multi-step pipeline that discards provenance. A three-stage entity-resolution pipeline then cleans the output: ER-NF/A uses ChemDataExtractor and mat2vec embeddings to disambiguate Name/Formula/Acronym, ER-N/F applies a fine-tuned binary classifier (2,000 labeled entities) to fix formula-vs-name confusion, and ER-ED clusters remaining labels via DBSCAN into an expert-curated dictionary of ~600 terms. The system iteratively retrains the LLM on high-quality inference outputs across ten batches of 15,000 abstracts. For graph completion, the paper proposes a weighted Jaccard similarity J_mat(A,B) = Σ wᵢ·|Aᵢ∩Bᵢ|/|Aᵢ∪Bᵢ| across material attributes, combined with TransE embeddings, to predict novel material–application links validated against subsequent years' publications (48.5% of top-200 predictions from 2014 data confirmed within nine years).

## Results

The fine-tuned Darwin model with entity resolution (Darwin(ER)) achieves F1 scores of 0.9296 (NER), 0.8827 (RE), and 0.8913 (ER) on the 60-abstract evaluation set, substantially outperforming all baselines. The resulting MKG contains 162,605 nodes and 731,772 edges extracted from 150,000 peer-reviewed abstracts. For graph completion (link prediction), the network-based similarity method validates 48.5% of top-200 material–application predictions within 9 years (using 2014 data), outperforming both direct Jaccard similarity and TransE-based prediction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three baselines: LLaMA 7b, LLaMA2 7b, and TransE. On the NER/RE/ER extraction tasks, Darwin(ER) decisively outperforms both LLaMA variants: it achieves 0.9296 F1 on NER versus 0.7541 (LLaMA2 7b) and 0.6158 (LLaMA 7b), and 0.8827 F1 on RE versus 0.6557 and 0.5355 respectively. The ER task shows the largest relative gap, with Darwin(ER) reaching 0.8913 F1 compared to 0.4557 (LLaMA2 7b) and 0.3722 (LLaMA 7b), confirming that the dedicated entity-resolution pipeline (ChemDataExtractor + mat2vec + expert dictionary) is critical. For link prediction in graph completion, the proposed network-based similarity (enhanced Jaccard) consistently outperforms TransE across all validation windows, with the grey line in Figure 6(a) tracking well above the green TransE line, indicating that structural graph reasoning better captures material–application relationships than embedding-based translation models.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of materials knowledge graph construction (MMKG, MatKG, MatKG2, MOF-KG) by replacing multi-step NER+RE pipelines with a single fine-tuned domain-specific LLM (Darwin) that handles both entity recognition and relation extraction end-to-end while preserving DOI-level provenance for every triple. It departs from prior computational databases (Materials Project, OQMD, NOMAD) by grounding the graph exclusively in experimental literature rather than DFT/MD simulations. The entity resolution stage integrates ChemDataExtractor, mat2vec embeddings, and DBSCAN clustering with an expert-curated dictionary, and the graph completion leverages an enhanced weighted Jaccard similarity alongside TransE embeddings for link prediction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MKG (162,605 nodes, 731,772 edges from 150,000 energy-materials abstracts) is the first large-scale, experimentally grounded, fully traceable materials knowledge graph, filling a gap left by computational-only databases. The 48.5% nine-year validation rate for material-application link predictions demonstrates practical predictive utility for accelerating materials discovery. Key limitations include reliance on abstracts only (omitting experimental methods and data), focus on energy materials, and entity loss from strict normalization. Open questions include whether the pipeline scales to full-text extraction, whether the ontology generalizes beyond energy materials, and whether time-aware graph embeddings can capture evolving material-application relationships.
