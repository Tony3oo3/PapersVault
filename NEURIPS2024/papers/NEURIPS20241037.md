## Problem

Mamba-based diffusion models process 2D image tokens in a fixed scanning order, introducing an inductive bias that limits their ability to capture both local pixel dependencies and long-range frequency relationships. Prior scanning strategies (bidirectional, cross-scanning, zigzag-8) address only the spatial ordering problem and fail to exploit frequency-domain structure, which is critical for high-fidelity image synthesis. This paper tackles the gap by integrating wavelet-based frequency scanning with spatial scanning in a unified Mamba diffusion architecture.

## Input / Output

The model operates entirely in the latent space of a pre-trained VAE encoder, receiving Gaussian noise at a compressed resolution (e.g., 32×32 latent for 256×256 images, 64×64 for 512×512) and optionally a class-embedding condition. Generation uses a flow-matching ODE with the adaptive 'dopri5' solver, requiring 61–82 NFEs depending on resolution, and produces a decoded RGB image. The architecture is evaluated at 256×256 and 512×512 resolutions on CelebA-HQ, LSUN Church, and ImageNet-1k.

## Method

DiMSUM's core DiM block combines two parallel Mamba pathways: a spatial Mamba using sweep-4 scanning over patchified latent tokens, and a Wavelet Mamba that first applies a two-level Haar DWT to decompose features into LL/LH/HL/HH subbands, then performs window scanning (analogous to a sliding CNN kernel) within each subband to capture local frequency structure. The two outputs are fused via a query-swapped cross-attention fusion layer (CAFL), where spatial queries attend to wavelet keys/values and vice versa, enabling dynamic spatial-frequency synergy without naive concatenation. Every four DiM blocks, a globally weight-shared DiT-style transformer block is inserted to provide order-invariant global context mixing, complementing Mamba's sequential inductive bias. Conditioning is injected by initializing Mamba's first hidden state with a linear projection of the class embedding (or a learnable token for unconditional generation), and the entire network is trained with a flow-matching velocity objective using a generalized VP interpolation schedule.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256 class-conditional generation, DiMSUM achieves FID 2.11 with guidance (8.61 unconditional) and Recall 0.59, using only 460M parameters and 510 epochs—surpassing DiT-XL/2-G (FID 2.27, 675M params, 1.4K epochs) and SiT-XL/2-G (FID 2.15, 675M params). On CelebA-HQ 256×256, DiMSUM reaches FID 4.62 and Recall 0.52 in just 225 epochs, outperforming Zigma (FID 7.66, 400 epochs) and LFM-8 (FID 5.26, 500 epochs). On LSUN Church 256×256, it achieves FID 3.76 and Recall 0.56, beating DIFFUSSM (FID 3.94) and approaching StyleGAN2 (FID 3.86) while achieving substantially higher recall (0.56 vs 0.36).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiMSUM outperforms all transformer-based diffusion baselines on ImageNet 256: it achieves FID 2.11 (with guidance) versus DiT-XL/2-G (2.27) and SiT-XL/2-G (2.15), while using ~30% fewer parameters (460M vs 675M) and roughly 2.7× fewer training epochs (510 vs 1.4K). Against the SSM-based DIFFUSSM-XL-G, DiMSUM improves FID from 2.28 to 2.11 with fewer parameters (460M vs 673M) and fewer total iterations. On CelebA-HQ 256, DiMSUM's FID of 4.62 substantially beats Zigma (7.66), LFM-8 (5.26), and LDM-4 (5.11), while also achieving the highest Recall (0.52) and converging in only 225 epochs versus 400–600 for baselines. On LSUN Church, DiMSUM (FID 3.76, Recall 0.56) surpasses DIFFUSSM (3.94) and LDM (4.02), and while slightly behind StyleGAN2 in FID (3.86), it achieves dramatically higher recall (0.56 vs 0.36), indicating far greater sample diversity. Inference speed is also competitive: DiMSUM-L/2 completes a 256×256 sample in 2.20s versus DiT-L/2's 3.80s, and at 512×512 the gap widens (2.86s vs 4.78s) with DiMSUM requiring fewer GFLOPs (337 vs 361).

## Relation to Prior Work

DiMSUM sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) Mamba-based diffusion models (Zigma, DIFFUSSM) that process image tokens via spatial scanning orders, (2) frequency-aware generative models (WaveDiff, FreqMamba) that decompose images into wavelet subbands for faster convergence, and (3) hybrid Mamba-Transformer architectures (Jamba, Zamba) that interleave attention blocks to compensate for SSMs' weaker in-context learning. The key departure is that prior Mamba diffusion models are confined to spatial-domain scanning, while prior wavelet-based generators do not integrate frequency features into a state-space sequence model. DiMSUM unifies these by applying window scanning over multi-level Haar wavelet subbands and fusing the resulting frequency features with spatial Mamba outputs via a query-swapped cross-attention layer, a combination not previously explored in the diffusion setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a 460M-parameter Mamba-based diffusion model can match or exceed 675M-parameter DiT-XL/2 on ImageNet while requiring roughly 30% fewer training iterations, suggesting that linear-complexity sequence models are a viable backbone for high-quality image synthesis at scale. The spatial-frequency fusion technique and the finding that sweep-4 scanning outperforms more elaborate orders (sweep-8, zigzag-8) offer practical guidance for future Mamba-based vision architectures. Open questions include whether the approach extends to text-to-image generation at higher resolutions, whether a multiscale or hierarchical variant could further improve quality, and whether the manually defined scanning orders can be learned or eliminated entirely.
