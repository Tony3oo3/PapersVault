## Problem

Discrepancy distance is the central quantity governing out-of-distribution generalization in domain adaptation, but computing or even testing it is intractable in general, and no prior work provided polynomial-time algorithms for learning with distribution shift. The earlier TDS learning framework of Klivans et al. (2024) relied on L2-sandwiching approximators, a restrictive condition that excluded important classes such as constant-depth circuits and polynomial threshold functions, and yielded only constant-factor error guarantees. This paper asks whether a more natural L1-sandwiching condition suffices and whether localized versions of discrepancy testing can yield efficient, near-optimal, and universal TDS learners.

## Input / Output

The algorithm operates in two decoupled phases: the training phase consumes labeled examples from the training distribution D and outputs a candidate hypothesis f_hat, while the testing phase consumes only unlabeled examples from the test marginal D' together with f_hat and returns either a rejection or an acceptance with a guaranteed test-error bound. A key design constraint is universality: the tester must accept for all test marginals in a broad class D (e.g., distributions with bounded fourth moments and anti-concentration), not merely for D' = D. For halfspace intersections, the testing phase runs in fully polynomial time in d, k, and 1/ε, making it practical for resource-limited users of pre-trained models.

## Method

The core technical contribution is a suite of efficient localized discrepancy testers, each tailored to a different notion of neighborhood around the candidate hypothesis f_hat. For classes with low-degree L1-sandwiching polynomials (AC0 circuits, degree-2 PTFs), the tester estimates Chow parameters E[f_hat(x)·x^α] under both marginals and rejects if they diverge, certifying low discrepancy over the entire concept class C as the neighborhood. For low-dimensional convex sets, the training phase recovers the relevant subspace, and the tester checks concentration of the test marginal on a cylindrical grid over that subspace, exploiting boundary smoothness to bound disagreement. For halfspace intersections, a geometric lemma showing that balanced convex sets are locally balanced implies that two low-disagreement hypotheses can only differ far from the origin or near the boundary, so the tester simply checks that the test distribution does not place excessive mass near the boundary of f_hat. The L1-sandwiching result (Theorem 3.1) resolves the main open question from prior TDS work, yielding exponential runtime improvements for AC0 circuits and the first TDS learning results for degree-2 PTFs, with error guarantees matching the information-theoretic optimum λ rather than O(λ).

## Results

The paper provides the first TDS learning algorithms for degree-2 polynomial threshold functions (runtime d^{Õ(1/ε^9)}) and achieves an exponential improvement for constant-depth AC⁰ circuits (d^{O(log(s/ε))^{O(t)}} vs. the prior d^{√s · O(log(s/ε))^{O(t)}}). The error guarantee improves from O(λ) + ε (prior work) to the near-optimal λ + opt_train + O(ε). For balanced intersections of k halfspaces, the paper gives the first TDS learner with fully polynomial test time poly(d, k, 1/ε), improving over the prior (k/ε)^{O(k²)} test-time complexity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Compared to [KSV24b] (Klivans et al., COLT 2024), which used L₂-sandwiching, this paper's L₁-sandwiching approach yields an exponential runtime improvement for AC⁰ circuits (removing the √s factor) and extends coverage from formulas to general circuits, while also delivering the first TDS learning results for degree-2 PTFs. The error guarantee tightens from O(λ) + ε to λ + opt_train + O(ε), matching the information-theoretic lower bound. Compared to [KSV24a] (Klivans et al., COLT 2024) for halfspace intersections, the overall runtime improves from d^{O(log(k/ε))} + poly(d)exp(O(k⁶/ε⁸)) to poly(d)exp(O(k⁵/ε)), and the test-time complexity drops from (k/ε)^{O(k²)} to fully polynomial poly(d, k, 1/ε). Additionally, the new tester is universal (accepts a wide class of heavy-tailed and multimodal test distributions), a property the prior algorithm lacked.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Testable Learning with Distribution Shift (TDS) framework introduced by the same authors in [KSV24b], which showed that L2-sandwiching polynomial approximators imply efficient TDS learning. The key conceptual departure is replacing the restrictive L2-sandwiching requirement with the more natural and well-studied L1-sandwiching notion, and unifying all prior TDS results under a single localized discrepancy testing framework. The localized discrepancy definition generalizes the notion from [ZLWJ20] and connects TDS learning to testable agnostic learning [RV23, GKK23], while the separation of training and testing phases builds on the structural insights from [KSV24a] on halfspace intersections.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first provably efficient TDS learning algorithms for constant-depth circuits (AC0) and degree-2 polynomial threshold functions, with exponential runtime improvements over prior work, and introduces universal TDS learners that certify correctness even for heavy-tailed and multimodal test distributions where no efficient agnostic learning algorithms are known. The NP-hardness result for global discrepancy testing (Appendix F) sharpens the understanding of why localization is essential. Open questions include whether the balancing assumption can be lifted, whether training-time access to test marginals could yield stronger guarantees, and whether the Gaussian/uniform training marginal assumptions can be relaxed to general distributions.
