## Problem

Score Distillation Sampling (SDS), the standard technique for extracting visual guidance from diffusion priors in text-to-3D generation, paradoxically degrades reconstruction quality in sparse-view 3D reconstruction as input views increase — a phenomenon the authors term the 'SDS dilemma.' The root cause is mode deviation: under sparse views, the rendered image distribution is inherently suboptimal, and SDS drives it toward the nearest (potentially wrong) mode of the diffusion prior rather than the target mode consistent with the given views, because the low information entropy of sparse views provides insufficient constraint to disambiguate modes.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 3 sparse RGB views (downsampled 8× on LLFF, 4× on DTU), known camera poses, and a single BLIP-generated text prompt. It produces a 3D Gaussian Splatting scene (initialized via SfM from the 3 views) that renders novel views in real time. Notably, the diffusion guidance uses Stable Diffusion Inpainting v1-5 rather than standard Stable Diffusion, so that the inpainting model shares the same VAE latent space as the text-conditioned diffusion model, enabling the rectified distribution to operate in a compatible feature domain. Diffusion guidance is active only during iterations 2K–9.5K of a 10K-iteration training schedule.

## Method

IPSM (Inline Prior Guided Score Matching) rectifies the rendered image distribution by inverse-warping seen views to pseudo unseen viewpoints using rendered depth and relative camera poses, producing a masked warped image that serves as an inline geometry-consistency prior. This rectified distribution acts as an intermediate state that decomposes the original SDS objective into two sub-objectives: (1) pulling the rendered distribution toward the rectified distribution (controlling mode-seeking direction), and (2) pulling the rectified distribution toward the diffusion prior (providing visual guidance), thereby suppressing mode deviation without any fine-tuning or pre-training. The full IPSM-Gaussian pipeline builds on 3DGS with Gaussian unpooling (following FSGS), adds Pearson-correlation depth regularization against monocular depth estimates to stabilize the inverse warping, and adds a masked L1 geometry consistency loss at the pixel level. The total training loss combines standard L1/SSIM supervision on input views, the two-term IPSM loss, depth regularization, and geometry consistency, with the diffusion guidance weighted at λ_IPSM=2.0 and the rectification sub-objective weighted at η_r=0.1.

## Results

On the LLFF dataset (3 sparse views), IPSM-Gaussian achieves SSIM 0.702, LPIPS 0.207, PSNR 20.44, and AVGE 0.101, outperforming the best 3DGS-based baseline FSGS (AVGE 0.108) by 6.48% and DNGaussian† (AVGE 0.109) by 7.34%. On the DTU dataset, it achieves SSIM 0.856, LPIPS 0.121, PSNR 19.99, and AVGE 0.077, improving AVGE by 23.76% over FSGS and 21.43% over FreeNeRF. The method also demonstrates superior training stability, with standard deviations roughly 3–11× lower than vanilla SDS across independent runs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IPSM-Gaussian outperforms all 3DGS-based baselines on both datasets. On LLFF, it improves AVGE by 6.48% over FSGS (0.108→0.101) and 7.34% over SfM-initialized DNGaussian† (0.109→0.101), while also achieving the best SSIM (0.702 vs. 0.687) and LPIPS (0.207 vs. 0.228). On DTU, the gains are more pronounced: AVGE improves by 23.76% over FSGS (0.101→0.077) and 21.43% over FreeNeRF (0.098→0.077), with PSNR reaching 19.99 vs. FSGS's 17.69. Among NeRF-based methods, IPSM-Gaussian surpasses SparseNeRF by 12.5% in SSIM and FreeNeRF by 32.79% in LPIPS on LLFF. Direct comparison with vanilla SDS (Table 3) shows that SDS(CFG=100) degrades all metrics (e.g., LPIPS worsens by 44.49%), while IPSM(CFG=7.5) improves LPIPS by 9.84% and AVGE by 9.60% over the base 3DGS, confirming that the mode-deviation problem is resolved by the inline-prior rectification.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on 3D Gaussian Splatting as the 3D representation backbone and Score Distillation Sampling (SDS) from DreamFusion as the diffusion guidance mechanism, but fundamentally repositions SDS for sparse-view reconstruction rather than text-to-3D generation. It departs from prior sparse-view methods (RegNeRF, FreeNeRF, DNGaussian) that rely on semantic or depth priors by showing that diffusion visual priors can be effectively extracted without the expensive fine-tuning (Deceptive-NeRF) or encoder pre-training (ReconFusion) that previous diffusion-based approaches require. The key conceptual departure is the identification of 'mode deviation' as the root cause of SDS failure under sparse views, and the introduction of 'inline priors' from inter-viewpoint pose relationships to rectify the rendered image distribution before score matching.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clear theoretical diagnosis (mode deviation) for why SDS, a widely-used technique in text-to-3D, is counterproductive in sparse-view reconstruction, which explains a practical puzzle many practitioners have encountered. The IPSM solution is attractive because it requires no fine-tuning or pre-training, making diffusion-based guidance accessible with standard Stable Diffusion Inpainting models. However, the limitation that the rectified distribution must share the same VAE feature space as the diffusion prior restricts model choice, and the authors acknowledge that replacing the inpainting model with a fine-tuned diffusion prior (à la VSD) could improve scalability at the cost of additional compute. Open questions include whether the inline prior mechanism generalizes to extreme view extrapolation (where the paper shows current methods, including theirs, still struggle) and whether 3D diffusion priors with sufficient training data could eventually subsume this approach.
