## Problem

Existing vision-language connectors in MLLMs (e.g., Q-Former, Perceiver Resampler) rely on randomly initialized, image-invariant learnable queries to aggregate visual information, which requires hundreds of millions of training samples to become effective and can cause significant information loss for uncommon inputs. The paper identifies that pre-trained Vision Transformers naturally develop image-specific 'visual anchors'—tokens that the [CLS] token disproportionately attends to—which could serve as superior, content-aware information aggregators without the need for extensive training data.

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB image at 336×336 resolution (processed by CLIP ViT-L/14 into 577 visual tokens) and a text instruction or question. The Anchor Former compresses the 577 visual tokens down to 145 or 257 tokens before feeding them to the LLM (Vicuna-7B or 13B), which generates a free-form text response. The anchor selection leverages the attention map of the [CLS] token from the second-to-last ViT layer, requiring no additional forward pass beyond the standard vision encoder computation.

## Method

AcFormer consists of three components: (1) an Anchor Selector that applies a progressive search algorithm over the [CLS] token's attention map, distributing token selection across attention heads (T_N/H tokens per head) and skipping duplicates to yield image-specific visual anchors; (2) an Information Aggregation Module—a 6-layer bidirectional transformer encoder (hidden dim 512, 8 heads) that performs cross-attention with the selected anchors as queries and the full 577 visual tokens as keys/values, followed by feedforward layers; and (3) a linear projection into the LLM's embedding space. The key distinction from Perceiver Resampler or Q-Former is that the queries are not fixed learnable parameters but are dynamically selected per image based on the ViT's own attention structure, providing content-aware aggregation with strong prior knowledge from pretraining. This reduces visual tokens by roughly two-thirds (577→145) while matching or exceeding LLaVA-1.5 accuracy across nine benchmarks.

## Results

With only 145 visual tokens (vs. 577 for LLaVA-1.5), AcFormer achieves 68.4 MMB, 1846.1 MME, and 86.1 POPE with a 7B LLM, outperforming LLaVA-1.5 (64.3 MMB, 1794.6 MME, 85.9 POPE) while running at 2.23× pre-training speed. On general VQA tasks with 257 tokens, AcFormer matches or exceeds LLaVA-1.5 on TextVQA (58.2 vs. 58.2), VizWiz (52.8 vs. 50.0), and SQA-Img (69.4 vs. 66.8), with only marginal drops on GQA (61.2 vs. 62.0) and VQAv2 (78.4 vs. 78.5). The 13B variant further extends these gains, reaching 69.2 MMB and 1870.0 MME at 2.30× speed.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary baseline LLaVA-1.5 (577 tokens), AcFormer (145 tokens) wins on POPE (+0.2), MME (+51.5), and MMB (+4.1) while trailing by only 0.2 on MM-Vet, all at 2.23× speed. Compared to Qwen-VL-Chat (256 tokens, 448 res), AcFormer achieves higher MMB (68.4 vs. 60.6) and comparable MME (1846.1 vs. 1845.3) with fewer tokens and lower resolution. Against Perceiver Resampler (PR) and C-Abstractor in ablation studies (both at 145 tokens), AcFormer leads by 3.0–5.9 points on TextVQA and 1.6–3.0 points on MMB, confirming that image-specific visual anchors outperform both fixed learnable queries and spatial convolution. Older 32-token methods (MiniGPT-4, InstructBLIP) are substantially outperformed across all metrics (e.g., MMB 68.4 vs. 24.3/36.0). On general VQA, AcFormer (257 tokens) matches LLaVA-1.5 on TextVQA and VQAv2, surpasses it on VizWiz (+2.8) and SQA-Img (+2.6), but trails slightly on GQA (−0.8) and VQAv2 (−0.1). The data-scaling experiment (60M pre-training pairs) confirms AcFormer's advantage over PR persists at scale, gaining +1.7 on TextVQA and +2.7 on VQAv2.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself within the vision-language connector design space for MLLMs, building directly on the Perceiver Resampler framework from Flamingo and the LLaVA-1.5 training pipeline. The key departure is from the standard practice of using randomly initialized, image-agnostic learnable queries (as in Q-Former and Perceiver Resampler) for information aggregation: instead, the authors identify image-specific 'visual anchors' from the pre-trained ViT's attention maps and use those as the queries. This reframes the connector design problem from 'learn what to attend to' to 'discover what the vision encoder already attends to,' leveraging the ViT's own inductive structure rather than imposing an external aggregation mechanism.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is a ~2.2× pre-training speedup with 145 visual tokens (vs. 577 in LLaVA-1.5) while matching or slightly exceeding baseline accuracy on most benchmarks, making it relevant for anyone seeking efficient MLLM deployment. The empirical observation that ViT attention maps reveal consistent, image-specific aggregation points (69.38% overlap with feature-map activations) is an interesting but under-theorized finding; the paper acknowledges the lack of theoretical explanation for why these anchors emerge. Open questions include whether the anchor phenomenon generalizes beyond CLIP-family encoders, whether it persists at higher resolutions or with different ViT architectures, and whether the modest accuracy gains on fine-grained tasks (TextVQA) can be further improved without sacrificing the token reduction.
