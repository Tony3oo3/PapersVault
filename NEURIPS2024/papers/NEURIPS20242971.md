## Problem

The spatio-temporal relations between measurable impacts of extreme events (e.g., agricultural droughts) and their precursor drivers in climate data remain poorly understood, particularly because drivers can occur weeks earlier and at different locations than the extreme event, with inhomogeneous spatial responses. No prior work has addressed the end-to-end identification of spatio-temporal drivers from multivariate climate data using only annotations of the extreme event impacts, without any labels for the drivers themselves.

## Input / Output

The model consumes multivariate weekly climate reanalysis data shaped as V×C×T×Lat×Lon, where C=2 (mean and standard deviation per week), T=6–8 sequential weeks, and V=6 physical variables (2m temperature, cloud cover, precipitation, soil moisture, albedo, relative humidity). It produces two outputs: a binary spatio-temporal driver mask Q∈{0,1}^{V×T×Lat×Lon} flagging anomalous regions per variable, and an extreme-event probability map E∈R^{Lat×Lon} at the target time step. Critically, the extreme prediction is computed exclusively from the binary driver masks rather than from continuous features, enforcing that only spatio-temporally relevant anomalies inform the prediction.

## Method

The architecture comprises three jointly trained components: (1) independent per-variable feature extractors using a Video Swin Transformer (or Mamba) backbone to prevent cross-variable information leakage, (2) a Lookup-Free Quantization (LFQ) layer that binarizes each variable's features into a two-code representation (driver vs. non-driver) via a sign-based threshold, and (3) a multi-head 3D CNN classifier (V+1 heads) that predicts extreme events from the binary driver masks. The key insight is that by restricting the classifier's input to binary quantized codes, the network is forced to identify only the spatio-temporal configurations of anomalies that are genuinely correlated with extremes. Training uses three loss terms: a weighted cross-entropy for extreme prediction across all V+1 heads, a quantization loss (commitment + per-code entropy + batch diversity) to stabilize the binary codes, and a driver loss that penalizes non-zero driver assignments in spatio-temporal regions where no extreme event occurred at any time step.

## Results

On the synthetic CERRA benchmark, the proposed method (Video Swin Transformer backbone) achieves a driver-detection F1-score of 80.44 and IoU of 67.28 on the test set, outperforming the best baseline (ARNet, F1=73.68) by ~7 points. The Mamba backbone variant achieves slightly higher validation F1 (83.45) and IoU (71.60). On the synthetic NOAA benchmark, the method reaches F1=82.55 (test), again leading all baselines by a wide margin (best baseline: DeepMIL at F1=72.02). On real-world ERA5-Land and CERRA reanalysis data, the model successfully predicts extreme agricultural droughts from identified drivers, and removing the quantization step (i.e., no driver detection) improves extreme detection F1 by only ~1–2%, confirming that the identified drivers carry nearly all predictive information.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all nine baselines across every metric (F1, IoU, OA) on all three synthetic benchmarks. On the primary synthetic CERRA test set, the method achieves F1=80.44, beating the strongest baseline ARNet (MIL) by ~6.8 F1 points and SimpleNet (one-class) by ~7.0 points. The MIL-based baselines (DeepMIL, ARNet) are the closest competitors, while one-class methods (OCSVM, IF) and reconstruction-based methods (STEALNet, UniAD) lag significantly due to false positives from uncorrelated variables. Integrated Gradients (explainable AI) performs worst among structured baselines (F1≈23–30), confirming that direct gradient attribution fails to isolate spatio-temporal drivers. On the harder synthetic artificial dataset (higher anomaly ratio, red noise, quadratic coupling), the gap narrows slightly but the method still leads all baselines. The ablation in Table 2 further shows that the LFQ quantization loss and the driver loss are both essential—removing either drops F1 from 82.78 to below 32.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes lookup-free quantization (LFQ) and vector quantization (VQ) mechanisms—originally designed for discrete representation learning in generative models—as a binary bottleneck that forces a spatio-temporal network to identify only driver anomalies correlated with extreme events, rather than all anomalies. It departs from standard anomaly detection (one-class, reconstruction-based, MIL) by requiring the identified anomalies to be predictive of a specific downstream extreme, and from post-hoc interpretability methods (integrated gradients) by embedding driver identification directly into the architecture. The Video Swin Transformer backbone and 3D CNN classifier are standard components; the novelty lies in the quantization-based driver identification layer and the multi-head training strategy that prevents the network from relying on only a subset of variables.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper introduces a well-motivated new task (identifying spatio-temporal drivers of extremes) along with three synthetic benchmarks that enable quantitative evaluation where ground-truth driver labels do not exist in real climate data. Key limitations include the binary (not scored) nature of driver identification, the limited temporal window that cannot capture long-range teleconnections (e.g., El Niño), and the fact that the model identifies correlations rather than causal relationships. Open questions include scaling to a large number of climate variables, providing confidence scores for drivers, and validating whether the identified spatio-temporal patterns hold under climate change scenarios where historical relationships may shift.
