## Problem

No standardized benchmark existed for comparing models that predict single-neuron responses in mouse primary visual cortex to dynamic video stimuli. Existing efforts either focused on static images, covered very few neurons, or lacked withheld test sets for fair model comparison. This paper establishes the SENSORIUM 2023 competition to fill this gap with a large-scale dataset (78,853 neurons, 10 mice) and a fair evaluation protocol with both in-domain and out-of-distribution tracks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243776/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Models consume grayscale video frames at 30 fps (clips of ~8–11 seconds) concatenated along the channel dimension with four behavioral variables (locomotion speed, pupil size, horizontal and vertical pupil center position, all resampled to 30 Hz), and produce predicted single-neuron calcium imaging responses upsampled to 30 Hz. The dataset spans 78,853 neurons across 10 mice with ~1200 minutes of stimuli total, split into training, validation, live test, and final test sets. Evaluation uses two metrics—single-trial correlation and correlation-to-average—computed independently per neuron and then averaged, with the first 50 frames excluded as a burn-in period for history-dependent models.

## Method

The competition established a two-track benchmark (in-domain natural movies and out-of-distribution parametric stimuli) with a live leaderboard for iterative feedback and a withheld final test set for fair ranking, accompanied by three baseline models (GRU, factorized 3D convolution, and their 14-model ensemble). Three winning architectures emerged: DwiseNeuro (3D factorized inverted-residual convolutions with squeeze-and-excitation, DropPath, and a 'cortex' module enabling all-to-all interactions after spatial global average pooling), Dynamic-V1FM (3D Swin Transformer core with a Hierarchical Gaussian Readout fusing multi-resolution features), and ViV1T (factorized spatiotemporal transformer with FlashAttention-2 separating spatial and temporal attention). A key empirical finding is that all three winners incorporated a mechanism for long-range all-to-all interactions—either through transformer self-attention or the cortex module—suggesting that local convolutional inductive biases alone are insufficient for modeling mouse V1. The winning model improved over the ensemble baseline by 48% on the main track and 70% on the OOD track, and classic computer-vision techniques (skip connections, squeeze-and-excitation, CutMix augmentation) proved transferable to neural predictive modeling.

## Results

The winning model DwiseNeuro achieved a single-trial correlation (ρ_st) of 0.291 on the main track (natural movies) and 0.221 on the bonus track (OOD stimuli), surpassing the ensemble baseline (0.197 and 0.129) by 48% and 71%, respectively. On the correlation-to-average metric (ρ_ta), DwiseNeuro reached 0.542 (main) and 0.429 (bonus), compared to 0.371 and 0.241 for the ensemble baseline. The competition received 163 submissions from 22 teams, with the top three models (DwiseNeuro, Dynamic-V1FM, ViV1T) all substantially outperforming the strongest baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

All three winning models substantially outperformed the three provided baselines on both tracks. DwiseNeuro (1st place) beat the ensemble baseline by 0.094 in single-trial correlation on the main track (0.291 vs. 0.197) and by 0.092 on the bonus track (0.221 vs. 0.129), representing relative improvements of ~48% and ~71%. Dynamic-V1FM (2nd) and ViV1T (3rd) also exceeded the ensemble baseline on both tracks, with Dynamic-V1FM achieving 0.265/0.183 and ViV1T achieving 0.243/0.178 (main/bonus ρ_st). The gap between the winner and the runner-up was larger on the bonus track (0.221 vs. 0.183) than on the main track (0.291 vs. 0.265), suggesting DwiseNeuro's factorized 3D convolution architecture with a 'cortex' module generalizes better to unseen stimulus types. The GRU baseline, representing the prior state of the art, was outperformed by the ensemble baseline by ~86% on the main track, and by the winning model by ~174%, highlighting the dramatic progress enabled by the competition.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the SENSORIUM 2022 competition (Willeke et al., 2022) from static to dynamic (video) stimuli, filling a gap the authors explicitly identify: no public benchmark existed for predicting single-neuron responses to spatio-temporal stimuli in mouse V1. The baseline architectures build directly on the 3D factorized convolution core from Vystrčilova et al. (2024) and Hoefling et al. (2022), combined with the Gaussian readout from Lurz et al. (2021), which has become a standard component in the field. The winning models depart from this CNN-centric lineage by introducing transformer-based cores (Video Swin Transformer, factorized ViT) and a novel 'cortex' module with all-to-all nonlinear processing, drawing on computer vision techniques (squeeze-and-excitation, DropPath, CutMix) not previously applied to neural predictive modeling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first standardized benchmark with a held-out test set for dynamic-stimulus neural response prediction in mouse V1, providing 78,853 neurons across 10 mice with ~120 minutes of stimuli per recording. The observation that all three winning architectures incorporate all-to-all interaction mechanisms (transformers or the cortex module) challenges the assumption that CNNs, originally modeled after V1, provide the best inductive bias for V1, opening questions about whether long-range lateral connections or global behavioral signals drive this advantage. Key open questions include extending to chromatic stimuli, active behaviors, and beyond V1, as well as whether the architectural changes preserve biologically interpretable properties. A practical limitation is the compute barrier: winning models require 40–48 GB of GPU memory, excluding many researchers from free platforms like Colab.
