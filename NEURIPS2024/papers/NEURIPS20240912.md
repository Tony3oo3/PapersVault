## Problem

Sparse Mixture of Experts (SMoE) models suffer from unstable training dynamics and poor robustness to distribution shifts and data contamination, limiting their deployment in large-scale tasks. While SMoE efficiently scales parameter count by activating only a subset of experts per input, the underlying layer dynamics lack the stabilizing mechanisms that standard optimization enjoys, making the models fragile under adversarial perturbations and out-of-distribution inputs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240912/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For language modeling, the method consumes text token sequences from WikiText-103 (word-level, ~103M training words) and produces next-word predictions evaluated by perplexity on clean and word-swap-attacked validation/test sets. For vision, it takes 224×224 RGB images from ImageNet-1K (1.28M training, 50K validation) and outputs 1000-class labels, with robustness evaluated on ImageNet-R (artistic renditions), ImageNet-A (adversarial examples), and ImageNet-C (15 corruption types at 5 severity levels). A key implementation detail is that the momentum vector p_t is carried forward across layers within a single forward pass, so each layer acts as one iteration of the accelerated descent, with p_0 initialized to zero.

## Method

The paper establishes a formal analogy between SMoE layer dynamics and stochastic gradient descent on a multi-objective optimization problem: each expert optimizes one objective, the router's softmax weights approximate the optimal convex combination α* from MGDA, and the SMoE output plays the role of the gradient. By substituting the standard residual connection x_{t+1} = x_t + f(x_t) with a heavy-ball momentum update p_t = -f(x_t) + μp_{t-1}, x_{t+1} = x_t + γp_t, they derive MomentumSMoE, which provably expands the stable eigenvalue range from γσ(n) ∈ (0,2) to γσ(n) ∈ (0, 2+2μ), nearly doubling the convergence margin. The framework generalizes to AdamSMoE (incorporating AdamW's element-wise adaptive scaling and decoupled weight decay) and Robust MomentumSMoE (adding a feedback signal y_t = x_t + α(x_t - x_{t-1}) from the Robust Momentum Method to steer toward robust solutions). The modification requires only a few lines of code change to the residual connection, with negligible additional computation, and is demonstrated to be universally applicable across Switch Transformer, GLaM, V-MoE, and Soft MoE architectures.

## Results

On WikiText-103 language modeling, AdamSMoE-medium achieves 31.59 valid PPL and 33.25 test PPL, outperforming the SMoE-medium baseline (33.76/35.55) by over 2 PPL. Under word-swap attack, AdamSMoE-medium reduces valid PPL from 42.24 to 39.27. On ImageNet-1K, Momentum-Soft MoE reaches 75.47% top-1 accuracy versus 73.52% for Soft MoE, and Robust MomentumV-MoE reduces ImageNet-C mCE from 67.14 to 64.92 while improving IN-R top-1 from 36.10% to 37.57%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Switch Transformer (SMoE) baseline [18], both MomentumSMoE and AdamSMoE consistently reduce perplexity across all model sizes: AdamSMoE-medium improves valid PPL by 2.17 (31.59 vs. 33.76) and test PPL by 2.30 (33.25 vs. 35.55) on clean data, with even larger gains under attack (2.97 valid PPL). Against GLaM [16], AdamGLaM-medium achieves 32.99 valid PPL versus 36.37 for the baseline, a 3.38 PPL improvement, confirming the method transfers across different MoE architectures. On ImageNet-1K, Robust MomentumV-MoE [61] outperforms V-MoE on all robustness benchmarks: IN-R top-1 improves from 36.10% to 37.57%, IN-A from 5.25% to 6.37%, and IN-C mCE drops from 67.14 to 64.92, while clean IN-1K accuracy remains comparable (73.20% vs. 73.16%). Finally, Momentum-Soft MoE surpasses the Soft MoE baseline [56] by 1.95% top-1 accuracy (75.47% vs. 73.52%) on ImageNet-1K, demonstrating that the momentum framework generalizes beyond sparse to soft MoE models.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the SMoE architecture lineage (Switch Transformer, GLaM, V-MoE, Soft MoE) by introducing a novel theoretical lens: it reframes the SMoE forward pass as a stochastic gradient descent step on a multi-objective optimization problem, where each expert optimizes one objective and the router learns the optimal convex combination (à la MGDA). This GD analogy then naturally motivates integrating heavy-ball momentum, AdamW, and Robust Momentum directly into the SMoE layer dynamics, departing from prior SMoE work that focused on routing strategies and load balancing losses. It also extends the line of momentum-in-DNN work (MomentumRNN, Momentum Transformer, momentum in ResNet) by embedding momentum into the forward-pass computation of the expert layer rather than into the training optimizer or residual connections.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled, theoretically grounded framework for understanding and improving SMoE dynamics, with the practical advantage that the modification requires only a few lines of code and negligible computational overhead. Its universality across SMoE, MoE, GLaM, V-MoE, and Soft MoE architectures makes it a broadly applicable design principle. Key limitations include the finding that momentum is ineffective or harmful for very shallow models (3 layers), and the absence of a theoretical explanation for the observed robustness gains. Open questions include developing a fixed-point iteration analysis of MomentumSMoE and extending the framework to handle the instability observed when applying AdamSMoE to vision models like V-MoE.
