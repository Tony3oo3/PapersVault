## Problem

Linear attention and its gated variants enable efficient recurrent inference but still underperform softmax Transformers on in-context recall-intensive tasks, and training from scratch on trillions of tokens remains prohibitively expensive. The T2R paradigm (finetuning pretrained Transformers to RNNs) reduces training cost to 1–3% of from-scratch training, but linear attention's different kernel from softmax causes significant performance degradation during finetuning. The paper revisits ABC, which retains softmax and uses bounded memory slots, but identifies that ABC lacks a forgetting mechanism and introduces an unwarranted bias toward early tokens.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a text token sequence (processed via the Mistral tokenizer) and produces next-token predictions, following a standard Llama-style decoder architecture. During inference, the model operates as a recurrent network maintaining a bounded hidden state of size 128Ld per model (64 memory slots × 4 heads × 2 for keys and values × d dimensions), which is substantially smaller than RetNet (512Ld) or GLA (256Ld), yielding faster inference. Training uses a chunkwise parallel algorithm with a fixed chunk size, and the model is trained on 100B SlimPajama tokens at 1.3B and 2.7B parameter scales, or finetuned from Mistral-7B with 20B–100B additional tokens.

## Method

GSA reformulates ABC as a two-pass Gated Linear Attention (GLA) linked by a softmax operation: the first GLA pass computes context-aware slot scores using queries, keys, and the complement of the forget gate (1−α) as values; the second GLA pass uses the softmax of those scores as queries to read from value memories gated by α. The per-slot gated update rule (K̃_t = Diag(α_t)·K̃_{t−1} + (1−α_t)⊗k_t) simultaneously enables selective forgetting of stale information and introduces a recency inductive bias, resolving ABC's two key limitations. Because GSA decomposes into two GLA passes, it inherits GLA's hardware-efficient chunkwise training algorithm (implemented in the FLA Triton library), achieving training throughput comparable to GLA while its smaller state size (128Ld vs. 256Ld or 512Ld) yields faster inference. The architecture uses 4 heads with 64 slots each, keeping total per-layer parameters at approximately 4d², matching a standard softmax attention layer.

## Results

On 1.3B and 2.7B language modeling (100B SlimPajama tokens), GSA achieves 51.7 and 55.3 average zero-shot accuracy respectively, matching HGRN2 (51.9/55.4) while using the same 128×Ld state size, and outperforming GLA (50.4/53.5) and RetNet (50.6/52.9) which require 256×Ld and 512×Ld states. On recall-intensive tasks (FDA, SWDE, SQuAD, NQ, TriviaQA, DROP), GSA achieves 31.8 (1.3B) and 36.5 (2.7B) average, substantially outperforming all other subquadratic baselines (GLA: 31.4/34.3; HGRN2: 25.9/29.5; RetNet: 28.4/31.0; Mamba: 27.7/30.5). In the T2R finetuning setting, Mistral-7B finetuned to GSA reaches 56.9 average (100B tokens), surpassing RWKV6-7B (54.1) and Mamba-7B (55.7) trained from scratch on >1T tokens, and outperforming Mistral-to-GLA (52.5) and Mistral-to-RetNet (47.1) at 20B tokens.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GSA matches HGRN2 on commonsense reasoning (51.7 vs 51.9 at 1.3B; 55.3 vs 55.4 at 2.7B) with the same 128×Ld state size, while outperforming GLA by 1.3–1.8 points and RetNet by 1.1–2.4 points in average accuracy. On recall-intensive tasks, GSA's advantage is most pronounced: at 2.7B it achieves 36.5 average vs GLA (34.3), RetNet (31.0), Mamba (30.5), and HGRN2 (29.5), a 2.2–6.6 point margin over all subquadratic baselines. In the T2R setting, Mistral-to-GSA (53.9 at 20B tokens) outperforms Mistral-to-GLA (52.5) and Mistral-to-RetNet (47.1) by 1.4 and 6.8 points respectively, and at 100B tokens (56.9) surpasses SUPRA (54.2) and even RWKV6-7B (54.1) and Mamba-7B (55.7) trained from scratch on >1T tokens. GSA also outperforms Xfmr++ (Llama) on recall tasks at 2.7B (36.5 vs 41.7 for Xfmr++), though the Transformer still leads by 5.2 points, confirming the residual recall gap. On long-context tasks (16K input), GSA consistently leads all subquadratic models (e.g., 19.2 on NarrativeQA vs GLA 17.2, RetNet 0.0) and approaches Mistral's original performance (25.1).

## Relation to Prior Work

GSA fuses two previously separate ideas: the bounded-memory-slot formulation of ABC (Peng et al., ACL 2022), which retains a softmax readout over a fixed number of key-value slots, and the data-dependent gating mechanism of GLA (Yang et al., ICML 2024), which enables hardware-efficient chunkwise training. The key reformulation is showing that ABC is equivalent to two-pass linear attention linked by softmax, and that adding GLA-style gating yields a two-pass GLA that inherits FLA's efficient training kernel. This positions GSA as a gated, slot-based variant of linear attention that deliberately preserves the softmax operator—a design choice that distinguishes it from RetNet, GLA, HGRN2, and Mamba, all of which replace softmax with kernel or decay-based alternatives.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The most practically significant finding is in the T2R (Transformer-to-RNN) finetuning setting: Mistral-7B finetuned to GSA on only 100B tokens outperforms RWKV6-7B and Mamba-7B trained from scratch on over 1T tokens, suggesting that retaining softmax during the finetuning transition is critical and that the full cost of from-scratch training may be unnecessary for competitive recurrent models. On recall-intensive tasks, GSA substantially outperforms other linear models at the same state size, partially closing the recall-throughput gap that has been a fundamental limitation of constant-memory recurrent architectures. Open questions include whether hybrid GSA-attention models can address the remaining copy-oriented task failures (Phonebook Lookup, Needle-In-Haystack), whether a two-pass DeltaNet variant would further improve retrieval, and whether these results hold at the 7B+ scale with trillions of training tokens.
