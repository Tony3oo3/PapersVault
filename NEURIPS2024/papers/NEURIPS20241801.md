## Problem

Learning surrogate models for population dynamics of physical systems that depend on physics parameters is challenging because existing diffusion- and flow-based methods condition on time and parameter, requiring a separate (expensive) inference path for each (t, μ) pair, while action-matching approaches that learn dynamics over time suffer from training instability when the time integral in the loss is estimated via Monte Carlo. This paper addresses both gaps by formulating a parametric variational problem over time and physics parameters and introducing higher-order quadrature to stabilize training.

## Input / Output

The method consumes sample trajectories {X^i_{t_j, μ_k}} from high-fidelity simulations (e.g., particle-in-cell Vlasov-Poisson solvers, Euler-Maruyama SDE integrators) at discrete time points and physics parameters, along with initial-condition samples from ρ_{0,μ}. At inference, it produces full sample trajectories by integrating the SDE dX̂ = ∇s_{t,μ}(X̂)dt + εdW_t from initial conditions, where the SDE time coincides with the physics time, yielding O(K) cost for a trajectory of length K rather than O(Kτ) for conditioning-based methods. The learned gradient field ∇s_{t,μ} implicitly represents the population density ρ_{t,μ} at all times simultaneously.

## Method

HOAM formulates a variational problem (extending Benamou-Brenier optimal transport and action matching) to learn a parameter- and time-dependent scalar field s_{t,μ} whose gradient ∇s_{t,μ} minimizes kinetic energy subject to the continuity equation, with an optional entropic regularization term (ε²/2)Δs that yields a Fokker-Planck equation for sampling. The field is parametrized by a neural network with CoLoRA (continuous low-rank adaptation) layers, where a small hyper-network maps (t, μ) to scalar modulation weights that modulate low-rank updates of the main network, ensuring smoothness in both time and parameter. The critical technical contribution is the empirical loss estimation: the one-dimensional time integral is computed with composite Simpson's or Gauss-Legendre quadrature (requiring data at specific nodes, with interpolation if needed), while the high-dimensional spatial and parameter integrals use Monte Carlo mini-batching. This hybrid quadrature-Monte Carlo scheme dramatically reduces variance in the loss estimate, preventing the training divergences observed with pure Monte Carlo time integration (original action matching).

## Results

HOAM-S achieves the lowest relative error in electric energy (e.e.) on the two-stream instability (0.078) and bump-on-tail (0.427), while HOAM-G achieves the best e.e. on the 6D strong Landau problem (0.447). On the 9D chaotic system, HOAM-S attains the lowest Sinkhorn divergence (0.214) compared to CFM (0.259), NCSM (0.869), and AM (80.1). Inference runtimes for HOAM are 6–7 seconds across all problems, versus 139–161 s for CFM and 1,133–4,531 s for NCSM, representing 1–2 orders of magnitude speedup. AM fails entirely on the 6D strong Landau problem (NaN) and produces a Sinkhorn divergence of 80.1 on the 9D chaos problem.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HOAM-S outperforms all baselines on the two-stream instability, achieving an e.e. of 0.078 versus 0.245 (NCSM), 0.275 (AM), and 1.44 (CFM), while matching their 6 s runtime. On the 9D chaotic system, HOAM-S achieves the best Sinkhorn divergence (0.214) compared to CFM (0.259), NCSM (0.869), and AM (80.1), with AM failing to converge. On the 6D strong Landau problem, HOAM-G achieves the best e.e. (0.447) versus CFM (0.629) and NCSM (4.06), while AM produces NaN (complete failure). The key runtime advantage is that HOAM requires only 6–7 s per inference, compared to 139–161 s for CFM and 1,133–4,531 s for NCSM, because HOAM generates an entire trajectory in one SDE integration pass rather than solving a separate sampling problem at each time step. NCSM is the most expensive baseline by a wide margin (up to 4,531 s on strong Landau) due to its annealed Langevin sampling requiring multiple steps per time point. CFM is faster than NCSM but still 20× slower than HOAM and produces substantially worse accuracy on the Vlasov problems.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the action-matching loss of Neklyudov et al. (ICML 2023), which learns stochastic population dynamics from samples via a Benamou-Brenier-style variational problem, by introducing physics-parameter dependence and a higher-order quadrature scheme for the time integral. The parameter dependence is handled through CoLoRA weight-modulation layers (Berman & Peherstorfer, ICML 2024), while the key technical departure is replacing Monte Carlo estimation of the time integral with composite Simpson's or Gauss-Legendre quadrature. This positions the work between optimal transport theory (Benamou-Brenier, 2000) and modern generative modeling (diffusion/flow-based methods), but deliberately avoids the per-time-step conditioning that makes NCSM and CFM expensive for trajectory generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The O(K) inference scaling of HOAM versus O(Kτ) or O(Kτσ) for conditioned diffusion/flow methods makes it particularly attractive for parametric model reduction where many trajectories across many parameter values are needed. The finding that higher-order quadrature is not merely beneficial but essential for training stability (AM diverges without it, especially in higher dimensions) provides a clear, actionable insight for anyone working with action-matching-type losses. Limitations include the requirement for sufficiently dense time samples (problematic in computational biology) and the restriction to kinetic-energy or entropic energy functionals; exploring other Lagrangians on the Wasserstein manifold remains open.
