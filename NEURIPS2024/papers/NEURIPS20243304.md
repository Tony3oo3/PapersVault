## Problem

Existing debiasing estimators for MNAR data (IPS, DR) are unbiased but suffer from unbounded variance and generalization bounds when propensity scores approach zero, making them unstable in industrial settings. The paper proves that no regularizer can fix this limitation and that unbiasedness inherently forces unbounded variance, revealing that estimator design is fundamentally a quantitative joint optimization of bias and variance rather than a simple trade-off.

## Input / Output

The framework consumes a user-item interaction matrix with missing entries, binary observation indicators o_{u,i}, estimated propensity scores p̂_{u,i} ∈ (0,1), and base model predictions from matrix factorization. Unusually, the method produces a per-pair optimal parameter α_{u,i} ∈ [0,1] computed in closed form from the propensity and a global weight ratio w₂/w₁, rather than a single global hyperparameter. The output is a debiased prediction estimate with provably bounded variance and generalization bound for every user-item pair, regardless of how small the propensity becomes.

## Method

The paper introduces D-IPS and D-DR dynamic estimators that replace the fixed propensity denominator p̂_{u,i} with f^{α_{u,i}}(p̂_{u,i}), where f is a monotonically increasing function satisfying f(0)=0, f(1)=1, and f(p̂)≥p̂, and α_{u,i} is a per-pair optimizable exponent. When α=1 the estimator reduces to standard IPS/DR (unbiased, unbounded variance); when α=0 it reduces to naive/EIB (biased, bounded variance). The optimal α_{u,i} is derived analytically by minimizing the weighted objective w₁·h_B + w₂·h_V, where h_B and h_V are closed-form functions of the propensity and α that determine bias and variance respectively. The paper further proves that adopting this optimal α guarantees bounded variance and generalization bounds for all propensities, and provides explicit generalization bound expressions decomposed into bias and variance terms. Four concrete f(·) functions (identity, sin, log, tanh) are provided, with the log variant performing best empirically across COAT, Yahoo! R3, and KuaiRec datasets.

## Results

On COAT, D-DR achieves the best AUC of 0.7804 (±0.0023), outperforming the strongest DR baseline DR-V2 (0.7749) by 3.53% and the strongest IPS baseline IPS-V2 (0.7737) by 3.16%. On Yahoo! R3, D-DR reaches AUC 0.6999 (±0.0026), beating Stable MRDR (0.6929) by 1.98%. On KuaiRec, D-IPS achieves AUC 0.7947 (±0.0005), surpassing IPS-V2 (0.7787) by 8.65%, though SNIPS (0.8015) retains a slight edge. Across all datasets, the dynamic estimators consistently reduce standard deviation by 30–70% relative to their static counterparts (e.g., D-IPS std 0.0015 vs. IPS std 0.0058 on COAT).

## Baselines & Comparisons

D-DR (the proposed dynamic DR estimator) achieves the best AUC on COAT (0.7804) and Yahoo! R3 (0.6999), beating DR-V2 by 3.53% and 1.98% respectively, and outperforming Stable MRDR (0.6929) on Yahoo! R3 by 1.0%. D-IPS surpasses IPS-V2 by 3.16% (COAT), 2.16% (Yahoo! R3), and 8.65% (KuaiRec) in AUC, and also reduces the standard deviation dramatically (e.g., 0.0015 vs. 0.0024 on COAT). On KuaiRec, SNIPS (0.8015) still edges out D-DR (0.7956) in AUC, and TMRDR-CL (0.8047) leads in NDCG@50, indicating that self-normalized and targeted methods retain advantages on this particular dataset. The dynamic variants consistently reduce variance: D-IPS has 74% lower std than IPS on COAT (0.0015 vs. 0.0058), and D-DR has 50% lower std than DR on Yahoo! R3 (0.0026 vs. 0.0013 for DR, though DR already has low std there). The naive estimator, while having the lowest variance, is consistently outperformed by all debiased methods in AUC, confirming the bias-variance trade-off the paper formalizes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on IPS (Schnabel et al., ICML 2016) and DR (Wang et al., ICML 2019) as its foundational estimators, parameterizing them with a per-pair exponent α and a designed function f(·) to create D-IPS and D-DR. It departs from the broader debiasing literature (SNIPS, MRDR, Stable DR, TDR-CL, IPS-V2/DR-V2) by proving that regularization fundamentally cannot bound variance (Theorem 3.3) and that unbiasedness necessarily implies unbounded variance (Theorem 3.4), thereby reframing the design goal from 'eliminate bias' or 'reduce variance' to a quantitative per-pair joint optimization of both. The matrix factorization base model and the MNAR setting follow the standard setup from prior recommendation debiasing work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The theoretical results formalize a limitation that practitioners have informally recognized (the 1/p blowup) but had not seen proven in a general form, providing a clear justification for why static estimators and regularizers are insufficient. The dynamic per-pair α mechanism is a practical, lightweight modification that achieves bounded variance with modest but consistent AUC/NDCG gains (2–8%) across three datasets. Open questions remain around how to select the weight ratio w₂/w₁ and the function f(·) in practice without oracle propensities, and whether the framework extends to multi-task or sequential settings. The paper's scope is limited to recommendation with matrix factorization, and the theoretical guarantees assume accurate propensity estimation.
