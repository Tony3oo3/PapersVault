## Problem

Most time-series Transformer architectures require separate training and deployment for each forecasting horizon, and existing foundation models (TimesFM, MOIRAI, DAM) either suffer from autoregressive error propagation, cannot capture abrupt changes, or lack a guaranteed horizon-invariance property. This paper identifies and systematically addresses the underexplored challenge of building a single Transformer that produces robust, consistent forecasts across arbitrary inference horizons without per-horizon retraining.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243785/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a univariate time series context window (default lookback of 96 timesteps) concatenated with zero-valued placeholders for the target horizon, then segments the combined sequence into non-overlapping multi-scale patches of sizes {8, 16, 32}. It produces all future values simultaneously in a non-autoregressive manner, meaning the forecast for any specific future position is invariant to how many additional placeholder positions are appended beyond it. The model is trained once with a fixed maximum horizon (H_max = 720) and a reweighted loss, yet can be queried at any inference horizon including unseen ones (e.g., 1024).

## Method

ElasTST is a non-autoregressive Transformer encoder that combines four key designs: (1) structured self-attention masks that set attention weights to zero for any key position consisting solely of placeholders, preventing information exchange between placeholder tokens and thereby guaranteeing horizon-invariance; (2) Tunable RoPE (TRoPE), which reparameterizes the standard RoPE base frequency into learnable period coefficients P_j distributed over [1, 1000] (rather than the NLP default [1, 10000]) and optimizes them jointly with model weights to capture dataset-specific periodicities; (3) a multi-scale patch assembly where patches of sizes 8, 16, and 32 are each encoded by dedicated MLPs, processed sequentially through a shared Transformer backbone, and their decoded outputs are averaged for the final forecast; and (4) a horizon reweighting training loss that approximates the expectation of randomly sampling horizons from [1, T_max] via a harmonic-series weight ω(τ) ≈ (1/T_max)(ln T_max − ln τ), eliminating the need for multi-horizon data sampling.

## Results

ElasTST is trained once with a maximum horizon of 720 and a horizon-reweighting loss, then evaluated across all horizons (96, 192, 336, 720) without per-horizon retraining. On NMAE, ElasTST achieves the best or near-best results on 6 of 8 datasets across all four horizons, with particularly large margins on Traffic (e.g., 0.195 vs. 0.246 for iTransformer at horizon 96), ETTh2 (0.158 vs. 0.177 for iTransformer at 96), and Weather (0.086 vs. 0.239 for Autoformer at 96). On the two datasets where it is not the best at the shortest horizon (ETTh1: 0.342 vs. iTransformer's 0.321; Exchange: 0.026 vs. PatchTST's 0.023), the gap narrows or reverses at longer horizons, where ElasTST's single-model advantage becomes decisive (e.g., ETTh1 at 720: 0.376 vs. iTransformer's 0.408).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against per-horizon-tuned baselines, ElasTST (trained once) achieves the best NMAE on 6 of 8 datasets at most horizons. The largest margins appear on Traffic (up to 0.051 NMAE better than iTransformer at horizon 96), ETTh2 (up to 0.049 better than iTransformer at 720), and Weather (up to 0.146 better than Autoformer at 96). ElasTST is slightly outperformed at the shortest horizon on ETTh1 (0.342 vs. iTransformer's 0.321) and Exchange (0.026 vs. PatchTST's 0.023), but reverses the lead at longer horizons where the single-model design advantage compounds. Against foundation models (TimesFM, MOIRAI) evaluated in Figure 2, ElasTST shows markedly more stable performance across unseen horizons (e.g., 1024), whereas TimesFM suffers from autoregressive error propagation and MOIRAI degrades on challenging datasets like Weather and Electricity despite its zero-shot capability. The ablation confirms that structured attention masks, tunable RoPE, and multi-patch assembly each contribute meaningfully, with tunable RoPE being most critical for extrapolation beyond the training horizon.

## Relation to Prior Work

ElasTST builds on two main lineages: (1) the non-autoregressive placeholder-based input scheme from MOIRAI and diffusion Transformers, which ElasTST extends with structured self-attention masks to guarantee a horizon-invariance property that MOIRAI lacks; and (2) RoPE from the NLP domain, which ElasTST adapts by redefining period coefficients as tunable parameters with a narrower range (P_max=1000 vs. 10000) better suited to time-series periodicities. It also extends PatchTST's patching concept into a multi-scale assembly within a shared backbone, and introduces a horizon reweighting loss that theoretically approximates random multi-horizon sampling with a single fixed training horizon. The paper positions itself against both per-horizon-tuned architectures (iTransformer, PatchTST, Autoformer) and zero-shot foundation models (TimesFM, MOIRAI), arguing that neither adequately addresses robustness across inference horizons from a single trained checkpoint.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical deployment gap—maintaining separate model checkpoints per horizon is operationally burdensome—and provides a clean architectural guarantee (horizon-invariance via structured masks) that prior work had not formalized. Key limitations include the absence of a pre-training phase, restriction to univariate forecasting, and evaluation on only 8 standard benchmark datasets, leaving open whether the design choices (especially tunable RoPE and multi-patch assembly) transfer to multivariate or industrial-scale settings. The horizon reweighting scheme is model-agnostic and could be adopted by other architectures, but the paper acknowledges it is not optimal for all datasets (e.g., Exchange performs worse with the longest training horizon). Future work on pre-training protocols and broader dataset coverage would be needed to establish ElasTST as a foundation-model backbone.
