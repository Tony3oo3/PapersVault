## Problem

Streaming algorithms for approximating the top eigenvector of A^T A have either required i.i.d. sampling assumptions or, for worst-case inputs in arbitrary order, a large spectral gap R = Ω(log n · log d) (Price and Xun, FOCS 2024). This paper closes the gap for the intermediate random-order model: worst-case inputs with rows presented in a uniformly random order, where the required gap drops to R = Ω(1) and the space cost is parameterized by the number of heavy rows h rather than the full dimension d.

## Input / Output

The algorithm receives n row vectors a_1, …, a_n ∈ R^d in a single pass, presented in a uniformly random order, and must use O(h·d·polylog(d)) bits of space where h is the number of rows with norm at least ||A||_F / √(d·polylog(d)). It outputs a unit vector v̂ satisfying ⟨v̂, v_1⟩² ≥ 1 − O(1/√R) with probability ≥ 4/5, where v_1 is the top eigenvector of A^T A and R = σ_1(A)²/σ_2(A)² is the spectral gap. The algorithm also maintains a Gaussian sketch (G·A) to select among O(log(nd)) candidate vectors generated under different guesses of ||A||_2².

## Method

The core algorithm is a single-pass block power method: the random-order stream is partitioned into t = Θ(log d) consecutive non-overlapping blocks, and within each block, row-norm sampling (via Magdon-Ismail's non-commutative Bernstein bound) yields a submatrix B_j whose quadratic form B_j^T B_j approximates A^T A in spectral norm. The algorithm then computes v′ = (B_t^T B_t)···(B_1^T B_1)g for a random Gaussian starting vector g in one pass, and the analysis relies on the Wang and Xi (1997) singular-value inequality for matrix products to show that v′/||v′||₂ aligns with v_1 when the gap R is constant. Heavy rows (norm ≥ ||A||_F/√(d·polylog(d))) are stored explicitly and handled in a separate case analysis: if they dominate the spectral norm, their top eigenvector is computed directly; otherwise the block power method is run on the remaining light rows after row-norm rescaling equalizes their norms. The paper also improves Price and Xun's gap requirement from Ω(log n · log d) to Ω(log² d) for arbitrary-order streams and Ω(log d) for random-order streams via the same row-norm subsampling technique.

## Results

The paper proves that for random-order streams with gap R = σ₁(A)²/σ₂(A)² = Ω(1), a streaming algorithm using O(h·d·polylog(d)) bits of space outputs a unit vector v̂ with ⟨v̂, v₁⟩² ≥ 1 − O(1/√R) with probability ≥ 4/5, where h is the number of heavy rows. A matching lower bound shows that Ω(h·d/R) bits of space are necessary to achieve correlation 1 − 1/(CR²). The paper also improves the gap requirement of Price and Xun (FOCS 2024) from R = Ω(log n · log d) to R = Ω(log² d) for arbitrary-order streams and R = Ω(log d) for random-order streams, and constructs a hard instance with R = O(log d / log log d) where Oja's algorithm with any fixed learning rate fails to achieve constant correlation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is against Price and Xun (FOCS 2024), who study the same eigenvector approximation problem for arbitrary-order streams. Their algorithm requires a gap of R = Ω(log n · log d) and uses O(d · polylog(d)) bits of space; this paper reduces that requirement to R = Ω(log² d) for arbitrary-order streams (by applying row-norm subsampling before running their Oja's algorithm variant) and further to R = Ω(log d) for random-order streams. The paper's main algorithm goes further, achieving R = Ω(1) for random-order streams at the cost of O(h · d · polylog(d)) space (parameterized by the number of heavy rows h). On the lower bound side, Price and Xun show Ω(d²/R³) bits are needed for arbitrary-order streams; this paper shows Ω(h·d/R) bits are needed for random-order streams, which is near-tight for their algorithm. The paper also demonstrates that Oja's algorithm (the core of Price and Xun's method) fundamentally fails at gaps as large as R = O(log d / log log d), even on randomly ordered streams, motivating the need for the block power method approach.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions the random-order streaming model as a natural middle ground between the i.i.d. sampling setting (where Oja's algorithm works) and the fully adversarial arbitrary-order setting (where Price and Xun, FOCS 2024, require R=Ω(log n·log d)). The algorithm is a variant of the block power method inspired by Gu (2015), but adapted to single-pass streaming by leveraging row-norm sampling from Magdon-Ismail (2010) to construct non-overlapping approximate quadratic forms. The key technical departure is the use of the Wang and Xi (1997) singular value inequality to analyze the product of approximate quadratic forms, which is what enables the constant-gap guarantee. The paper also directly improves Price and Xun's gap requirements to Ω(log²d) for arbitrary streams and Ω(log d) for random-order streams via subsampling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The constant-gap (R=Ω(1)) guarantee for random-order streams is a meaningful theoretical advance, closing the gap between what is achievable under i.i.d. assumptions and the logarithmic-gap barrier for adversarial streams. The lower bound showing that Ω(hd/R) bits are necessary establishes that the heavy-row parameterization in the algorithm is essentially tight, ruling out simpler uniform-space solutions. Open questions include whether the gap for arbitrary-order streams can be further reduced below Ω(log²d), whether the block power method approach extends to multi-dimensional PCA (top-k eigenvectors), and whether the random-order assumption can be relaxed to weaker distributional guarantees on the stream ordering.
