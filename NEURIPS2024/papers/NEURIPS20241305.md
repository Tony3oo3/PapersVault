## Problem

Pre-trained text-to-image diffusion models are limited to their native resolution (e.g., 1024² for SDXL), and existing methods for scaling to higher resolutions either require expensive multi-GPU hardware, introduce structural duplications and artifacts, or demand additional training. This paper asks whether a single consumer GPU can generate gigapixel-scale images from a pre-trained foundation model without any fine-tuning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241305/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single text prompt as input and produces an RGB image at an arbitrary target resolution mH × nW (m, n ≥ 1), where H×W is the base model's native size (1024² for SDXL). All resolutions up to 32768² require only 8.4 GB of GPU memory on a single RTX 3090, since denoising operates on fixed 128² latent patches rather than the full latent space. The framework supports both direct two-step generation (base → target) and multi-step cascaded generation through intermediate resolutions.

## Method

Pixelsmith first generates a base image with SDXL, upsamples it in pixel space via Lanczos interpolation, and re-encodes it into the VAE latent space to serve as structural guidance. During the higher-resolution denoising process, random 128² patches are selected and denoised one at a time (tracking per-pixel denoising state to avoid redundant work), keeping memory constant regardless of output resolution. A tunable 'Slider' parameter controls the timestep at which the process transitions from guided denoising (using the upsampled image) to free generation, balancing structural fidelity against fine-detail synthesis. The guidance mechanism operates in Fourier space: phases of the guidance and current latent patches are averaged while the amplitude is taken from the current patch, preserving structural coherence without over-constraining detail. A chess-like mask alternates between generated and guidance values across the patch grid to suppress prompt-duplication artifacts, and overlapping patch boundaries are averaged to eliminate seam artifacts.

## Results

On LAION-5B (1,000 prompt-image pairs), Pixelsmith achieves the best FID (63.116 at 2048², 63.686 at 4096²), KID (0.002 at both), IS (24.242 at 2048², 19.741 at 4096²), and CLIP score (33.429 at 2048², 32.369 at 4096²) among all compared methods. Inference time is 130 s at 2048² and 549 s at 4096² on a single RTX 3090 (8.4 GB VRAM), substantially faster than DemoFusion (219 s / 1632 s) and AccDiffusion (231 s / 1710 s). FouriScale runs out of memory at 4096² on the same GPU, while Pixelsmith handles all tested resolutions within the same 8.4 GB budget.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Pixelsmith outperforms all six competing methods on FID, KID, IS, and CLIP at both 2048² and 4096². Against the closest FID competitor DemoFusion (64.422 / 66.186), Pixelsmith improves by ~1.3 FID points at 2048² and ~2.5 at 4096², while also being roughly 1.7× faster (130 s vs 219 s; 549 s vs 1632 s). AccDiffusion is the second-best on FID (65.728 / 67.084) but is slower (231 s / 1710 s) and lags on IS and CLIP. ScaleCrafter is the fastest at 2048² (80 s) but trails Pixelsmith by ~14 FID points and ~5 IS points; at 4096² it is 2.3× slower (1257 s) with a 41-point FID gap. HiDiffusion is fast (50 s / 255 s) but produces noticeably worse quality (FID 74.773 / 97.614). FouriScale cannot run at 4096² on an RTX 3090 due to out-of-memory, whereas Pixelsmith completes in 549 s within 8.4 GB. The base SDXL model, when naively upscaled, degrades severely (FID 111.452 at 2048², 195.117 at 4096²), confirming the need for the proposed guidance and patch-denoising pipeline.

## Relation to Prior Work

Pixelsmith adapts the random patch denoising strategy from DiffInfinite (Aversa et al., 2024), originally developed for histopathology mask-image synthesis, to the text-to-image setting, where a global prompt conditions the entire latent space rather than per-patch segmentation masks. It sits within the broader family of training-free higher-resolution diffusion methods (MultiDiffusion, ScaleCrafter, DemoFusion, HiDiffusion, FouriScale, AccDiffusion) but departs from them by introducing an FFT-based phase-averaging guidance mechanism (the Slider) and a chess-like masking scheme to suppress the structural duplications and long-range artifacts that plague existing patch-based approaches. Unlike cascade methods that require passing through fixed intermediate resolutions, Pixelsmith can jump directly from the base 1024² resolution to any target resolution in a single generative step while remaining at a fixed 8.4 GB memory footprint.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is substantial: generating gigapixel-scale images on a single consumer GPU (RTX 3090) with no fine-tuning opens the door to applications in satellite imagery, medical imaging, and digital art that previously demanded multi-GPU clusters or expensive super-resolution pipelines. However, the Slider requires manual per-image tuning, and the paper acknowledges that at very high resolutions (e.g., 32768²), suppressing artifacts forces the Slider toward high values that suppress fine-detail generation, creating a fundamental quality trade-off. The field still lacks appropriate quantitative metrics for evaluating ultra-high-resolution outputs, and whether automated Slider selection or learned guidance could resolve the structure-versus-detail tension remains an open question.
