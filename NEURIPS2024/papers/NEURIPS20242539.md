## Problem

Process supervision for LLM verifiers—evaluating each intermediate reasoning step—outperforms outcome supervision but requires either expensive human annotations or computationally intensive Monte Carlo Tree Search sampling to generate step-level labels. This paper asks whether process annotations can be produced automatically from an outcome-supervised model's own confidence signals, without ground truth answers or additional sampling.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242539/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The verifier receives a question q concatenated with a prefix of reasoning steps S_i^(1:t) from one of n candidate solutions (typically 5) and outputs a scalar confidence score approximating the probability of reaching a correct final answer from that step onward. For final response selection, the product of all step-level scores is used as the aggregation function to rank candidates. The auto-generated process labels are binary (1 = correct, 0 = incorrect) under a 'first error location' scheme where the first step exceeding a confidence-drop threshold θ and all subsequent steps are labeled 0.

## Method

AUTOPSV first trains an outcome-supervised verifier (OSV) with MSE loss on final-answer correctness, which by Theorem 1 learns to assign each intermediate step a score approximating p(correct answer | step prefix, question). The key mechanism is computing the relative confidence change Δ_conf^t = (f(S^(1:t+1)) − f(S^(1:t))) / f(S^(1:t)) between consecutive steps; a drop below threshold θ (set to −0.5) flags that step as the first error. These automatically generated binary process labels—requiring no ground truth and no MCTS sampling—are then used to fine-tune the OSV into an enhanced OSV+PSV model via a process-supervision MSE loss. This two-stage pipeline (outcome-supervised pretraining → confidence-change-based relabeling → process-supervised fine-tuning) reduces annotation cost by roughly an order of magnitude compared to MCTS while also enabling the use of unlabeled data, since no gold answers are needed for the relabeling step.

## Results

On GSM8K, the process-supervised enhanced verifier (OSV+PSV) achieves 61.41% (Mistral-Instruct), 76.04% (Mixtral-Instruct), and 85.15% (Qwen-72B) accuracy, consistently outperforming both Self-Consistency (50.03%, 69.06%, 81.27%) and the outcome-supervised-only verifier (OSV: 61.18%, 74.91%, 84.91%). On the harder MATH benchmark, OSV+PSV reaches 5.30%, 16.92%, and 39.36% respectively, surpassing Self-Consistency by 3.66, 6.26, and 0.74 points. On commonsense reasoning (HellaSwag, Winogrande, ANLI), OSV+PSV leads or matches OSV across most generator configurations, with the largest gains on ANLI (e.g., 71.4% vs 69.1% for Qwen-72B).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Self-Consistency [8], OSV+PSV wins on GSM8K for all three generators (e.g., 76.04% vs 69.06% for Mixtral-Instruct, a +6.98 pt gain) and on MATH for Mistral-Instruct and Mixtral-Instruct, though it trails SC by 0.74 pt on MATH with Qwen-72B. Against the MCTS-based process labeling from Math-Shepherd [17] and the model-induced process supervision framework [18], AUTOPSV is competitive: it outperforms MCTS on GSM8K for Mistral-Instruct (55.32% vs 54.13%) and Qwen-72B (82.83% vs 82.17%), while MCTS edges ahead on Mixtral-Instruct GSM8K (72.36% vs 72.12%) and MATH for Mistral-Instruct (3.3% vs 3.24%). A critical practical advantage is that AUTOPSV requires only ~127–273 tokens per question for annotation versus ~2,808–21,626 tokens for MCTS (Table 9), and uniquely leverages unlabeled data (e.g., +1.63 pt on GSM8K for Mixtral-Instruct when adding WizardLM problems), which MCTS cannot exploit.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of outcome-supervised verifiers (OSV; Yu et al., 2023) and process-supervised verifiers (PSV; Uesato et al., 2022; Lightman et al., 2023), which have traditionally been treated as separate training paradigms requiring different annotation strategies. The key departure is repurposing the OSV's per-step confidence scores as a signal for automatic process labeling via relative confidence change (Δ_conf), replacing the expensive MCTS-based annotation of Wang et al. (2023, 2024) or human step-level annotation. Conceptually, the paper bridges the gap by showing that a model trained only on final-answer correctness can still produce meaningful step-level error signals, eliminating the need for the additional sampling that MCTS requires.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in the ~20× reduction in annotation tokens versus MCTS and the ability to leverage unlabeled data (e.g., WizardLM-generated problems) for process supervision, which neither MCTS nor standard OSV can exploit. However, the performance gains over OSV alone are modest (typically 1–2 points on GSM8K), and the method's effectiveness is tightly coupled to the quality of the underlying OSV. Open questions include whether the automatically generated PSV can be used for step-wise PPO to improve generators directly (flagged as future work), and whether the confidence-change signal generalizes beyond arithmetic calculation errors to more subtle logical or conceptual mistakes in reasoning.
