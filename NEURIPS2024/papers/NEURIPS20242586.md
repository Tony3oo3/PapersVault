## Problem

LLMs suffer a sharp decline in inference ability when input length exceeds their maximum training length, yet the underlying mechanism of this extrapolation failure remained unexplained. Prior work showed that removing position encoding (NoPE) can help, but no theoretical account existed for why NoPE still fails beyond its effective window, nor for why standard PE methods (RoPE, ALiBi) also degrade. Existing weave-PE plug-ins like ReRoPE and Leaky-ReRoPE offer practical fixes but incur quadratic memory and inference costs, limiting their scalability.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained LLM (using RoPE or ALiBi) and an arbitrary-length input token sequence, requiring no fine-tuning or architectural modification. The input is dynamically segmented into sub-sequences via a DynamicSplit function whose chunk widths depend on both the input length and the model's max training length (typically F=100, L=512, with intermediate chunks sized to fit within the training window). During prefill, each chunk is processed sequentially with cached key-value pairs; during decoding, Stair PE is applied to the current token against all cached KV pairs to generate next tokens one at a time.

## Method

Mesa-Extrapolation combines two key innovations: (1) Stair PE, a weave position encoding that maps relative positions beyond a threshold N to a stair-like function I = N + ⌈(t−i−N)/E⌉, providing finer-grained extrapolated positions than ReRoPE's flat clamping while reusing previously trained positions for stability; and (2) a chunk-based triangular attention matrix that splits the full attention computation into sequential chunks, reducing memory from O(n²) to O((2+√2)n). Regular PE is applied to all chunks except the last, where Stair PE rearranges relative positions after concatenating all prior KV pairs. The paper provides the first theoretical analysis (Theorems 3.1–3.3, Corollary 4.1) proving that hidden state values fall below a threshold H when input exceeds the effective window M for both NoPE and PE, but remain above H under weave PE with N ≪ M, establishing a formal mechanism for extrapolation success.

## Results

On the GovReport summarization task (LLaMA2-7B-Chat), Mesa-Extrapolation achieves ROUGE-1/2/L of 41.0/20.4/37.5 at 4k input length, maintaining competitive scores (e.g., 30.2/10.1/26.6) even at 11k, while Origin and Streaming-LLM collapse to near-zero beyond 4k. On passkey retrieval, Mesa-Extrapolation sustains stable accuracy well beyond the 4k max training length, outperforming Dynamic-NTK (which degrades after ~12k) and matching ReRoPE/Leaky-ReRoPE. In terms of efficiency, Mesa-Extrapolation achieves linear memory usage O((2+√2)n) and the fastest decoding speed, compared to 2×O(n²) memory for ReRoPE/Leaky-ReRoPE and O(n²) for Origin/Dynamic-NTK.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Mesa-Extrapolation clearly outperforms Streaming-LLM and Origin, both of which fail entirely beyond the 4k max training length (ROUGE drops to 0.0). Against Dynamic-NTK, Mesa-Extrapolation is slightly behind on ROUGE within the 11k range (e.g., 30.2 vs. 35.4 ROUGE-1 at 11k), but Dynamic-NTK's effective extrapolation range is limited to ~12k and it still requires quadratic O(n²) memory. Compared to ReRoPE and Leaky-ReRoPE, Mesa-Extrapolation achieves comparable ROUGE scores (within ~2-5 points) while reducing memory from 2×O(n²) to O((2+√2)n) and achieving the fastest decoding speed. On LongBench, Mesa-Extrapolation outperforms LM-Infinite on most tasks (e.g., 42.76 vs. 30.55 on Multi-Document QA at 8k+) and matches or exceeds Dynamic-NTK on Single-Document QA. The key differentiator is efficiency: Mesa-Extrapolation is the only method combining competitive quality with linear memory and the fastest inference, making it a practical plug-in for resource-constrained deployments.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper formalizes and extends the 'weave PE' paradigm introduced by ReRoPE and Leaky-ReRoPE (Su, 2023b), providing the first theoretical justification for why such position-weaving schemes enable extrapolation beyond the training window. It builds on the constructive Transformer analysis of Kazemnejad et al. (2023) and Lindner et al. (2023) to prove that hidden state values cross a threshold at the effective window boundary, explaining both NoPE and PE failure modes. The practical contribution—Mesa-Extrapolation—combines a novel Stair PE weave function with a chunk-based triangular attention matrix, departing from prior weave PE methods (which require O(n²) memory due to double attention computation) by achieving O(n) memory through sequential chunk processing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The theoretical framework connecting hidden state thresholds to extrapolation failure provides a principled lens for diagnosing and designing length-extrapolation methods, moving the field beyond empirical trial-and-error. Practically, Mesa-Extrapolation offers a zero-cost plug-in that reduces memory from quadratic to linear, making long-context inference feasible on constrained hardware. Key limitations include unvalidated performance at very long lengths (beyond ~60k), instability on models with approximate ALiBi (MPT), and a mid-length performance dip (7k–11k) attributed to the fixed extrapolation width E. Open questions include whether fine-tuning on top of Mesa-Extrapolation can close the remaining gap to Dynamic-NTK, and whether adaptive E selection can eliminate the mid-length degradation.
