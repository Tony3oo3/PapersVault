## Problem

While LLMs exhibit surprising robustness to low-bit quantization, the underlying cause and the mechanism by which residual performance degradation arises remain unexplained. This paper reveals that parameter heterogeneity—a tiny subset of 'cherry' parameters carrying 50–300× greater sensitivity to perturbation than the remaining 99%—is the root cause, and that existing mixed-precision strategies lack a principled, high-discriminability criterion for identifying which parameters to preserve.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pre-trained LLM's full-precision weight matrices and a calibration dataset (C4 for base models, ShareGPT for chat models, ~50k/20k samples of 2048 tokens). It outputs a mixed-precision model in which, for each parameter matrix, the top 1/256 columns by average Hessian impact are retained in FP16 (with their column indices stored as INT16), while all remaining parameters are quantized to 2-, 3-, or 4-bit symmetric MinMax integers with per-group scaling factors. The cherry-parameter selection is data-independent, validated by >60% overlap across different calibration samples and datasets.

## Method

CherryQ first computes each parameter's impact as the diagonal of the Hessian (approximated via the Fisher Information Matrix, E[g_i²]), then selects the top 1/256 columns per matrix as cherry parameters based on this metric, which the paper shows achieves the highest heterogeneity score (ratio of mean top-1% to max bottom-99%) compared to weight-magnitude or activation-based criteria. The quantization is then performed via a quantization-aware training loop that jointly optimizes both parameter groups end-to-end: cherry parameters are updated with standard gradient descent in FP16, while normal parameters are updated through the Straight-Through Estimator in their low-precision representation, allowing the two groups to co-adapt rather than being frozen sequentially as in prior PTQ approaches. For extreme 2-bit quantization, CherryQ additionally integrates AWQ's per-column scaling trick (Q′(w) = Q(w·s)/s) to further reduce output distortion, with the scale searched per layer before training begins.

## Results

On 3-bit quantization of LLaMA2-7B (group size 128), CherryQ achieves perplexity of 7.39 (C4) and 5.93 (WikiText-2), compared to FP16 baselines of 6.97 and 5.47, outperforming all other methods (QAT: 9.25/6.90, GPTQ: 8.28/6.74, AWQ: 7.84/6.24, OmniQuant: 7.75/6.03). In the extreme 2-bit setting, CherryQ achieves 9.55/8.34 perplexity on LLaMA2-7B, dramatically outperforming OmniQuant (15.02/11.06) and GPTQ (33.70/36.77), while AWQ diverges entirely (>10^5). On the Vicuna-bench pairwise evaluation, 3-bit CherryQ Vicuna-1.5 achieves a win-tie-lose ratio competitive with the FP16 model, indicating near-lossless quantization at 3 bits.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CherryQ consistently outperforms all five baselines (QAT, GPTQ, AWQ, OmniQuant, SqueezeLLM) on perplexity across both 7B and 13B LLaMA2 models at 3-bit and 4-bit settings. The margin is most pronounced in the 2-bit regime: CherryQ achieves 9.55 C4 perplexity on LLaMA2-7B versus 15.02 for OmniQuant, 33.70 for GPTQ, and >10^5 for AWQ, representing a 36–72% reduction over the best prior method. On downstream tasks (HellaSwag, Winogrande, ARC, TruthfulQA, GSM8K, MMLU), CherryQ achieves the highest average score (48.7 vs. 46.9 for QAT and 46.8 for GPTQ at 3-bit 7B-g64), with the largest gap on GSM8K (10.4 vs. 7.3/8.1). For chat models, 3-bit CherryQ Vicuna-1.5 achieves a win-tie-lose ratio against FP16 that is competitive or slightly favorable, whereas QAT and GPTQ show clear degradation. The impact-based cherry parameter selection criterion also outperforms weight-based and activation-based selection, confirming the heterogeneity-driven design choice.

## Relation to Prior Work

CherryQ extends the quantization-aware training framework of LLM-QAT by introducing a mixed-precision optimization loop that simultaneously updates high-precision 'cherry' parameters via standard gradient descent and low-precision normal parameters via the Straight-Through Estimator. It also incorporates AWQ's column-scaling trick for extreme 2-bit quantization. Conceptually, the paper departs from prior outlier-focused quantization work (which identifies sensitive parameters via weight magnitude or activation statistics) by arguing that the Hessian/Fisher-based impact metric provides the highest heterogeneity score and thus the most discriminative criterion for cherry parameter selection, a claim validated by ablation experiments.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The parameter heterogeneity finding provides a unifying explanation for both LLMs' surprising robustness to low-bit quantization and the effectiveness of mixed-precision strategies, reframing a practical heuristic as a structural property of trained models. Practically, CherryQ's 3-bit Vicuna-1.5 matching its 16-bit counterpart on Vicuna-bench and its strong 2-bit results (perplexity ~8 vs. >10 for OmniQuant) make it a compelling option for memory-constrained deployment. Open questions include whether the impact-based identification generalizes to architectures beyond Transformer LLMs, how computational cost scales to 70B+ models, and whether the 1/256 cherry ratio is truly universal or architecture-dependent.
