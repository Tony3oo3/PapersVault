## Problem

In cooperative MARL value factorization, the mixing network suffers from a growing proportion of dormant (inactive) neurons during training, which reduces its expressive power and degrades learning. Existing parameter perturbing methods from single-agent RL (ReDo, ReSet) fail in MARL because they disrupt learned cooperative knowledge, and the previously undiscovered correlation between dormant neurons and a small set of over-active neurons (with disproportionately large activation scores) has not been addressed.

## Input / Output

ReBorn operates on the mixing network weights of any value factorization algorithm, using local agent observations and action-observation histories as standard MARL inputs. It periodically computes normalized activation scores (NAS) for each neuron in the mixing network to identify dormant neurons (NAS ≤ 0.1) and over-active neurons (NAS ≥ 3), then produces rebalanced mixing network weights while leaving agent network parameters untouched (identity mapping). The rebalanced weights feed back into the standard value factorization pipeline to produce the joint action-value function Q_tot and individual agent actions.

## Method

ReBorn periodically (every T=200K steps) detects dormant and over-active neurons in the mixing network and transfers weights from each over-active neuron to M (2–5) randomly selected dormant neurons in the same layer: input weights are scaled by β_i ∈ [0.5, 1.5], output weights by (1/β_i)·α_i where α_i are softmax-normalized samples from a Gaussian (ensuring Σα_i = 1), and biases by β_i; unselected dormant neurons receive Xavier reinitialization. The key theoretical contribution is the Knowledge Invariant (KI) principle, which requires that learned action preferences (i.e., the ordering of joint actions) remain unchanged after parameter perturbation. The paper proves ReBorn satisfies KI for QMIX, QPLEX, DMIX, and RMIX by leveraging their monotonicity properties, while proving ReDo and ReSet violate KI and can consequently break the IGM principle. This weight-sharing mechanism preserves cooperative knowledge while redistributing activation mass away from over-active neurons, effectively 'reborn' dormant neurons without forgetting.

## Results

ReBorn consistently improves test win rates across QMIX, QPLEX, DMIX, and RMIX on SMAC scenarios (3s5z_vs_3s6z, MMM2, 27m_vs_30m), SMACv2, and Predator-prey environments, while simultaneously reducing the dormant neuron ratio in mixing networks. The improvements are most pronounced in harder, larger-agent scenarios (e.g., 27m_vs_30m with 27 agents), where the dormant ratio is highest. ReBorn also reduces the over-active NAS sum ratio (the fraction of total activation score concentrated in over-active neurons), indicating a more balanced network activation profile. All experiments use 5 random seeds with 95% confidence intervals under the PyMARL framework.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ReBorn outperforms all four parameter-perturbing baselines (ReDo, ReSet, SR, MARR) on win rate, dormant ratio, and over-active NAS ratio across MMM2, 27m_vs_30m, and Predator-prey large (Figure 6). Specifically, ReDo and ReSet reduce dormant ratios but fail to address over-active neurons and violate the KI principle, leading to forgetting of learned cooperative knowledge; SR and MARR (reset replay methods) also underperform ReBorn. Against the base algorithms without ReBorn, adding ReBorn improves QMIX, QPLEX, DMIX, and RMIX win rates across all tested environments (Figure 5). In the KI ablation (Figure 7), applying ReBorn only to the mixing network (satisfying KI) outperforms applying it to the entire network (violating KI), with the latter causing performance drops in QMIX and QPLEX. Among KI-satisfying variants (Figure 8), ReBorn's weight-sharing strategy outperforms ReDo-style re-initialization, ReSet-style last-layer reset, Reverse ReDo (resetting over-active neurons), and pruning of dormant neurons.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the dormant neuron phenomenon identified in single-agent RL (Sokar et al., ICML 2023) to the MARL value factorization setting, showing it primarily manifests in mixing networks and worsens with agent count. It departs from prior parameter-perturbation methods (ReDo, ReSet) by introducing the Knowledge Invariant (KI) principle, which formalizes the requirement that learned cooperative action preferences must be preserved after weight perturbation—a constraint absent from single-agent RL. The discovery of over-active neurons as a correlated, previously unreported phenomenon provides a mechanistic explanation for why naive re-initialization fails in MARL, and ReBorn's weight-sharing approach is positioned as orthogonal to existing value factorization methods (QMIX, QPLEX, DMIX, RMIX).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The KI principle offers a theoretically grounded criterion for when parameter perturbation is safe in cooperative MARL, and its violation can break the IGM principle—a foundational requirement for value factorization. Practically, ReBorn is a lightweight, drop-in mechanism that improves multiple popular algorithms across SMAC, SMACv2, and predator-prey without architectural changes. Open questions include whether the over-active/dormant neuron correlation generalizes beyond discrete-action environments, whether adaptive thresholds and recycling schedules can be learned, and whether the approach extends to policy-based or continuous-action MARL. The fixed hyperparameters (α=0.1, β=3, T=0.2M) and reliance on normalized activation scores alone for neuron identification are acknowledged limitations.
