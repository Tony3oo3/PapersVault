## Problem

Machine learning approaches for molecular structure elucidation have been restricted to single spectroscopic modalities (e.g., only NMR or only IR) because no publicly available dataset contains spectra from multiple techniques for the same set of molecules. This gap prevents the development of models that integrate complementary information across modalities, as human chemists routinely do when combining NMR, IR, and MS data to determine a molecular structure.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243995/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For structure elucidation, the model receives a structured text representation of one or more spectra (IR as 400 binned intensity tokens spanning 400–4000 cm⁻¹; ¹H-NMR as peak position/multiplet-type/integration tokens; ¹³C-NMR as peak position tokens; MS/MS as m/z–intensity pairs at multiple collision energies) plus the molecular formula, and outputs a canonical SMILES string. For the reverse spectra-prediction task, the input is a SMILES string and the output is the corresponding spectrum in the same structured format. For functional group prediction, raw spectrum vectors (IR: 1800-dim, NMR: 10000-dim, HSQC: 512×512 matrix) or structured text are mapped to multilabel functional group assignments across 35 SMARTS-defined groups.

## Method

The primary contribution is a dataset of 790k molecules (5–35 heavy atoms, sourced from USPTO reactions) with six simulated spectral modalities: ¹H-NMR, ¹³C-NMR, and HSQC-NMR via MestReNova; IR via 500 ns molecular dynamics (GAFF force field in LAMMPS, dipole-moment-based spectrum extraction); and positive/negative MS/MS via three tools (CFM-ID 4.0, SCARF, ICEBERG) at 10/20/40 eV. For benchmarking, a vanilla 4-layer encoder-decoder transformer (512 hidden dim, 8 heads, OpenNMT-py) is trained for 250k steps with spectra converted to structured text tokens and the molecular formula as a prior. The paper also evaluates a 1D-CNN (Jung et al.) and XGBoost on functional group classification, using 5-fold cross-validation. Key findings show that combining ¹H- and ¹³C-NMR yields the best structure elucidation (73.4% Top-1), while IR and MS/MS alone perform substantially worse, and the simulated spectra show higher similarity to their experimental counterparts than to unrelated spectra.

## Results

For structure elucidation (the primary task), the best single-modality result is 1H-NMR achieving 64.99% Top-1 accuracy, while combining 1H-NMR and 13C-NMR reaches 73.38% Top-1 and 89.98% Top-10 accuracy. IR spectra perform worst at 9.97% Top-1, and MS/MS results vary widely by simulator (CFM-ID positive: 23.53% Top-1; SCARF: 1.92% Top-1). For functional group prediction, the transformer achieves the highest F1 scores on 6 of 7 modalities (e.g., 0.935 on 1H-NMR, 0.913 on 13C-NMR), with the 1D-CNN slightly outperforming on IR (0.895 vs. 0.881).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper benchmarks three model architectures (Transformer, 1D-CNN from Jung et al. [41], and XGBoost) on the functional group prediction task across seven spectral modalities. The Transformer clearly dominates on MS/MS and NMR modalities: on CFM-ID positive MS/MS it reaches F1 0.897 vs. 0.725 (XGBoost) and 0.645 (1D-CNN), and on 1H-NMR it achieves 0.935 vs. 0.797 and 0.839 respectively. The 1D-CNN from Jung et al. is the only baseline that beats the Transformer, and only marginally, on IR spectra (F1 0.895 vs. 0.881), which the authors attribute to IR spectra being non-sparse and thus well-suited to convolutional processing. XGBoost consistently trails both deep learning models on NMR and MS/MS but remains competitive on IR (0.834). On SCARF-generated MS/MS, all three methods cluster closely (0.771, 0.763, 0.737), suggesting the lower-quality SCARF spectra limit all architectures similarly.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the single-modality structure elucidation line of work by Alberts et al. [14, 17], which demonstrated that transformer models can predict molecular structures from IR or NMR spectra individually. The key departure is the introduction of the first comprehensive multimodal spectroscopic dataset (IR, ¹H-NMR, ¹³C-NMR, HSQC-NMR, and MS/MS in both ion modes) for 790k molecules, explicitly motivated by the observation that human chemists integrate multiple spectral modalities while prior ML work has been confined to single-modality tasks. The dataset is constructed by simulating spectra for molecules extracted from the USPTO reaction dataset, using MestReNova for NMR, LAMMPS molecular dynamics for IR, and CFM-ID/SCARF/ICEBERG for MS/MS, thereby unifying previously fragmented and often private spectral resources into a single open benchmark.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This dataset addresses a critical gap: prior structure elucidation works relied on non-public or small experimental datasets, hindering transparent benchmarking and multimodal model development. By providing 790k molecules with six spectral modalities and standardized single-modality benchmarks, it enables the field to move toward the multimodal integration that mirrors expert chemist workflows. Key limitations include the entirely simulated nature of the data (creating a distribution shift to experimental spectra, as evidenced by the 0% zero-shot MS/MS accuracy for CFM-ID on experimental data) and the USPTO bias toward industrially relevant molecules. Open questions include how to optimally fuse modalities (early vs. medium vs. late fusion), handling mixtures, and whether fine-tuning on experimental spectra can close the sim-to-real gap.
