## Problem

Publicly available 3D CFD training data for automotive aerodynamics ML is scarce, and existing datasets like AhmedML use mesh resolutions (~20M cells) well below the industry standard of >100M cells for scale-resolving simulations, limiting the ability to test ML method scaling. Additionally, the Ahmed body's long flat roof decouples front and rear aerodynamics, making it less representative of modern road vehicles than the Windsor body, which also has richer experimental validation data including tomographic 3D PIV.

## Input / Output

The dataset provides 355 geometry variants parameterized by 7 design variables (nose/windshield height ratio, fastback length ratio, side taper, ground clearance, etc.) sampled via a Halton quasi-random sequence. For each variant, outputs include STL surface meshes (89k nodes), high-resolution VTP surface meshes (4.4M nodes) with pressure coefficient, skin-friction coefficient, and y+, time-averaged volume flow fields in VTU format (pressure, velocity, Reynolds stresses, TKE), force/moment coefficient CSVs (both fixed and case-dependent reference areas), and PNG slice images. The ML evaluation consumes the low-resolution STL mesh directly or integrates wall-shear stress and pressure from the VTP mesh to predict drag and lift coefficients.

## Method

The dataset is generated using the Volcano ScaLES GPU-native solver, which solves compressible Navier-Stokes equations with a nominally 4th-order finite difference discretization, an immersed-boundary method with an equilibrium wall-model (probing at 1.5Δ from walls), and the Vreman constant-coefficient subgrid-scale closure, integrated with SSP 3rd-order Runge-Kutta time stepping. Simulations run on ~275M cell Cartesian octree meshes (selected via a 3-level grid refinement study) on 8×Nvidia A100 GPUs, taking ~28 hours per case. For the ML evaluation, a modified MeshGraphNets GNN is trained in a supervised fashion with a 60/20/20 split, achieving MSE < 0.00028 for drag and < 0.0175 for lift, with inference at 0.15 s per geometry on x8 Nvidia L40s GPUs.

## Results

The CFD validation shows the G2 grid (275M cells) predicts a drag coefficient of 0.34, closely matching the experimental value of 0.33 (G3 at 468M cells gives 0.33). The preliminary GNN evaluation (modified MeshGraphNets) achieves an MSE below 0.00028 for drag coefficient and below 0.0175 for lift coefficient on a 60/20/20 train/validation/test split, with inference time of 0.15 seconds per geometry on 8× Nvidia L40s GPUs. Each of the 355 CFD simulations uses >280M cells and takes approximately 28 hours on 8× A100 GPUs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not compare its ML approach against other surrogate models or baselines; the GNN evaluation is explicitly described as preliminary and illustrative rather than a benchmark. The primary quantitative comparison is the CFD validation against experimental data: the G2 grid (chosen as the dataset baseline) predicts C_d = 0.34 versus the experimental 0.33, a ~3% overprediction, while the finer G3 grid matches experiment exactly at 0.33. The authors position WindsorML alongside the concurrently released AhmedML and DrivAerML datasets as a complementary suite for ML development, but no head-to-head ML method comparisons are presented. The paper explicitly invites the community to use the dataset for more thorough evaluation of different ML methodologies, acknowledging that the included GNN results are not intended as a state-of-the-art benchmark.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the AhmedML dataset (Ashton et al., 2024) — a 500-variant Ahmed body dataset using hybrid RANS-LES at ~20M cells — to the more road-car-representative Windsor body geometry with substantially higher-fidelity wall-modeled LES at 280M+ cells, addressing the fidelity gap that limited AhmedML's relevance to industry-scale simulations. The ML evaluation builds on MeshGraphNets (Pfaff et al., 2021) as a preliminary demonstration of how the dataset can be consumed. The work is positioned within a concurrent three-dataset ecosystem (AhmedML, WindsorML, DrivAerML) designed to provide consistent, openly licensed training data across increasing geometric complexity, and the Windsor body itself has been a standard benchmark in the AutoCFD prediction workshops.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first large-scale, high-fidelity CFD dataset for the Windsor body released under a permissive CC-BY-SA license, directly addressing the critical bottleneck of publicly available 3D training data for ML surrogate models in automotive aerodynamics. Key limitations include the absence of boundary-condition variation (fixed Re, fixed yaw), the lack of time-resolved outputs (only time-averaged fields), and the geometric simplification relative to real vehicles. Open questions include whether ML methods that perform well at 20M-cell scale (AhmedML) remain efficient and accurate at the 280M+ cell scale introduced here, and whether the 355-geometry sample size is sufficient for generalization to unseen design regions.
