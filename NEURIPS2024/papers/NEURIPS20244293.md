## Problem

Existing interpretability methods for vision models (attribution maps, prototype analysis) highlight pixels or features but require expert interpretation, while natural-language explanation approaches depend on labor-intensive manual annotation for training. No prior method can produce human-readable, structured explanations for vision models trained solely on pixel data without additional supervision or predefined rationales.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20244293/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a test image and a pre-trained vision model (CNN or ViT) as input, along with an LLM (ChatGPT/GPT-4) as an external knowledge provider and a text-to-image API (Bing/Stable Diffusion) for generating support images. The output is a tree-structured explanation where the root is the predicted class and internal/leaf nodes are visual attributes organized hierarchically from general to specific; the explanation is the merged root-to-leaf path of the top-k nearest nodes in the parse tree. Notably, the explanation is generated entirely post-hoc without any retraining of the vision model, and the support images used to build the tree are never used in model training.

## Method

LVX constructs a per-category attribute tree by prompting an LLM with in-context templates to generate hierarchical visual attributes (Concepts, Substances, Attributes, Environments), then retrieves support images via a text-to-image API and maps them into the vision model's embedding space to form a visual embedding tree. The tree is iteratively refined over five rounds: training samples are routed through the tree via point-to-set distance, rarely-visited nodes are pruned, and frequently-visited nodes are expanded by querying the LLM for finer-grained attributes. At test time, the sample's embedding is routed through the tree by nearest-neighbor search, and the traversed path yields a sample-specific rationale. As a secondary contribution, the generated trees serve as pseudo-labels in a hierarchical multi-label contrastive loss (HiMulCon) to fine-tune the vision model, improving both accuracy and explanation faithfulness without using the support set in training.

## Results

On the calibration task, LVX-calibrated ResNet-18 achieves 95.14% top-1 accuracy on CIFAR-10, 77.33% on CIFAR-100, and 70.28% on ImageNet, outperforming all neural-tree baselines. For interpretability, LVX achieves the lowest Model-induced Sample-Concept Distance (MSCD) across all architectures (e.g., −1.186 for ViT-S on ImageNet vs. −0.730 for TrDec), indicating the highest faithfulness to the model's internal logic. On plausibility, LVX consistently yields higher MCS and Tree Kernel scores than TrDec and SubTree baselines across 12 networks on CIFAR-10, 11 on CIFAR-100, and 8 on ImageNet. In a user study with 37 participants, LVX explanations were selected as most plausible 57.66% of the time, compared to 22.88% for TrDec.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LVX outperforms all three named baselines across every reported metric. Against TrDec [79] (tree-topology RNN decoder), LVX achieves lower MSCD (e.g., −1.186 vs. −0.730 for ViT-S on ImageNet) and higher stability under Gaussian noise and Cutout perturbations (MCS 62.1 vs. 56.2 at σ=0.1 on RN-18); TrDec also fails to generate valid attributes in deeper tree layers. Against NBDT [77], LVX-calibrated ResNet-18 gains +0.32% on CIFAR-10 (95.14% vs. 94.82%), +0.24% on CIFAR-100 (77.33% vs. 77.09%), and a substantial +5.01% on ImageNet (70.28% vs. 65.27%). Against DNDF [38], LVX leads by +0.82% on CIFAR-10 and +10.15% on CIFAR-100, though DNDF was not evaluated on ImageNet. LVX also surpasses the simpler SubTree and Constant baselines on plausibility (MCS/TK) and faithfulness (MSCD), with the refinement stage contributing an additional +2.5–3.0 MCS points over the unrefined LLM-generated tree.

## Relation to Prior Work

LVX sits at the intersection of prototype-based explainable models (TALIP, ProtoPNet, NPT) and LLM-assisted explanation, but departs from both in key ways. Unlike prototype methods that assign no semantic meaning to their reference points, LVX uses an LLM to label each prototype node with a human-readable visual attribute, and unlike prior LLM-explanation work that targets vision-language models, LVX explains pure pixel-trained vision models by bridging the text-image gap through a text-to-image API. The tree refinement mechanism (pruning/growing nodes based on training-set traversal) is conceptually related to neural decision tree adaptation but operates post-hoc without modifying the network, distinguishing it from NBDT and DNDF which replace the classification head.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The introduction of H-CIFAR10/100 and H-ImageNet as the first hierarchical-attribute benchmarks for vision model explanation fills a notable gap, and the finding that explanation-based calibration improves OOD accuracy by 4–7 points (ImageNet-A/Sketch) suggests interpretability and robustness are not in tension. However, the method's reliance on a text-to-image API, CLIP-based filtering, and iterative LLM queries makes it computationally expensive and sensitive to the quality of generated/retrieved support images; the paper acknowledges false positives and OOD concerns in the support set. Open questions include whether the approach scales to open-vocabulary or few-shot settings, how it handles fine-grained categories where LLM-generated attributes may be unreliable, and whether the calibration benefit persists when the explanation tree is imperfect.
