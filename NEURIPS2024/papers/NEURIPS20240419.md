## Problem

Despite SAM's widespread practical success in improving deep network generalization, no prior work established its fundamental convergence properties (gradient norm → 0, function values → optimal, iterates → stationary point) under the standard constant stepsize used in practice. Existing analyses either showed convergence only to a neighborhood of the optimum (Dai et al., 2023) or to averaged gradient bounds that do not imply pointwise convergence (Si and Yun, 2023). The only prior fundamental convergence result was for the unnormalized variant USAM, leaving the normalized SAM and its efficient variants (RSAM, VaSSO, F-SAM) without such guarantees.

## Input / Output

The method takes a C¹-smooth objective function f: ℝⁿ → ℝ, an initial point, a diminishing stepsize sequence {t_k} (e.g., t_k = 1/k or cosine-decay), and perturbation radii {ρ_k} that are bounded or decrease at an arbitrarily slow rate (e.g., ρ_k = C/k^0.001, which is numerically indistinguishable from a constant over practical iteration counts). The output is a sequence of iterates with provable convergence: in the nonconvex case, ∇f(x^k) → 0 and f(x^k) → f(x̄) for any accumulation point x̄; under the KL property, the full iterate sequence converges to a stationary point. The analysis covers both convex and nonconvex settings, with the convex case additionally requiring only bounded level sets for full convergence.

## Method

The paper first demonstrates that SAM with constant stepsize and constant perturbation radius fails to converge even on strongly convex quadratics (it oscillates around the solution), motivating the use of diminishing stepsizes. For the nonconvex case, the key technical insight is to recast SAM and all its normalized variants (RSAM, VaSSO, F-SAM) as a new Inexact Gradient Descent (IGD) method with absolute error ‖g^k − ∇f(x^k)‖ ≤ ε_k, where ε_k = Lρ_k. This absolute-error IGD framework had not been previously analyzed because the function value sequence need not be monotone, disrupting standard descent-based convergence proofs; the authors adapt nonmonotone analysis techniques from random reshuffling literature (Li et al., 2023) combined with a novel three-sequences lemma to handle this. For USAM and unnormalized variants, a separate IGD-with-relative-error analysis under the L-descent condition (weaker than Lipschitz gradient) yields convergence with explicit rates under the KL property. The universality of the framework means all convergence results transfer immediately to any method whose update can be written as x^{k+1} = x^k − t_k ∇f(x^k + ρ_k d^k/‖d^k‖).

## Results

The paper's primary contribution is theoretical: it establishes fundamental convergence properties (gradient convergence to zero, function value convergence, and iterate convergence under KL) for SAM with diminishing stepsizes in both convex and nonconvex settings, and for USAM under the weaker L-descent condition. Empirically, on CIFAR-100 with WRN28-10, SAM with Diminish 3 stepsize (η₁/m log m) achieves 79.70% test accuracy, outperforming the constant stepsize (74.63%) by over 5 points. On CIFAR-10, Diminish 3 achieves 94.75% (ResNet18) and 95.88% (WRN28-10), consistently matching or exceeding the constant stepsize baseline across all three architectures.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical convergence analysis paper, so the empirical comparisons are between stepsize schedules for the same SAM method rather than between different optimization algorithms. The constant stepsize (the standard practical choice in Foret et al. 2021) serves as the baseline. Diminish 3 (η₁/m log m) consistently outperforms the constant stepsize on CIFAR-100 across all architectures, with the largest gap on WRN28-10 (79.70% vs. 74.63%, a +5.07 point improvement) and ResNet18 (75.65% vs. 71.77%, +3.88 points). On CIFAR-10, the gains are smaller but still positive for Diminish 3 on ResNet18 (+0.65) and WRN28-10 (+0.55), though ResNet34 shows a slight decrease (94.47 vs. 94.38). Without momentum (Table 5), Diminish 2 becomes the best schedule on CIFAR-100 WRN28-10 (74.23% vs. 71.11% constant), while Diminish 1 degrades significantly, suggesting the interaction between stepsize decay rate and momentum matters in practice.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the inexact gradient descent (IGD) framework of Khanh et al. [2023b] from relative-error to absolute-error settings, a shift necessitated by the structure of SAM's normalized perturbation step. It refines the USAM convergence analysis of Andriushchenko and Flammarion [2022] under weaker assumptions (L-descent condition vs. Lipschitz gradient) and adapts the nonmonotone function-value sequence analysis of Li et al. [2023] (originally for random reshuffling) to handle the non-monotonicity introduced by SAM's backward perturbation. The paper positions itself as the first to establish fundamental convergence properties (gradient convergence, function value convergence, iterate convergence) for normalized SAM, noting that prior works (Dai et al. 2023, Si and Yun 2023) only achieved convergence to neighborhoods rather than exact optima.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This fills a critical theoretical gap: SAM is one of the most widely used optimization methods in deep learning, yet its fundamental convergence properties were previously unestablished for the normalized variant. The universal IGD-with-absolute-error framework immediately covers multiple practical variants (RSAM, VaSSO, F-SAM), providing a single analytical lens for the growing family of sharpness-aware methods. Key limitations include the restriction to deterministic settings (no stochastic or random reshuffling analysis), the absence of momentum coupling, and the theoretical requirement of diminishing perturbation radii (though practically near-constant radii suffice). Open questions include extending the analysis to stochastic SAM, understanding the interplay with momentum, and whether the KL-based iterate convergence results can be sharpened for the specific structure of neural network loss landscapes.
