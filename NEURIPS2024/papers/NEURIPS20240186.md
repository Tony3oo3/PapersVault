## Problem

Traditional multi-label learning assumes a closed label set, but real-world environments are open and dynamic where unknown labels emerge during testing. The multi-label setting makes this especially challenging because unknown labels almost always co-occur with known labels on the same instance, making it difficult to separate instances containing unknown labels from those with only known labels—a problem that single-label open set recognition methods cannot address directly.

## Input / Output

The method takes a multi-label training set of feature vectors paired with label indicator vectors, where 50% of labels are designated as known and the remainder as unknown; only instances without unknown labels (60% of the data) are used for training. For each unseen test instance, SLAN outputs two things: a predicted set of relevant known labels (via a kernel-based multi-label classifier) and a binary recognition decision (known/unknown) produced by ensemble majority voting across per-label sub-labeling discrepancy scores against a threshold ρ_k calibrated to a user-specified percentile τ of training instances.

## Method

SLAN (Sub-Labeling informAion reconstructionN) enriches labeling information in per-label sub-spaces by leveraging the manifold structure of the feature space. A weighted directed graph is first constructed via global sparse reconstruction (L1-regularized, solved by ADMM), encoding how each training instance can be linearly reconstructed from others. For each label l_k, the method generates enriched sub-labeling vectors f_i^k in the (q−1)-dimensional sub-space Y\{l_k} that preserve the feature-space manifold structure while being differentiated from the holistic supervision vector z_i; the gap between f_i^k and the projected z_i (via a projection matrix P_k that removes the k-th row) serves as a per-label unknownness signal. A unified objective (Eq. 6) jointly optimizes the sub-labeling enrichment, the open-set recognizer, and a kernel-based multi-label classifier through alternating optimization, so that the classifier is regularized by the recognizer and vice versa. At test time, the recognizer flags an instance as 'unknown' if the majority of per-label discrepancy scores exceed their respective thresholds, while the classifier independently predicts the relevant known label set.

## Results

SLAN achieves superior or comparable performance against all multi-label baselines in 91.7% of cases across five evaluation metrics (ranking loss, one-error, coverage, average precision, macro-AUC) on nine benchmark datasets. On the F-measure for open-set recognition, SLAN outperforms anomaly detection baselines in 83.3% of cases, e.g., achieving 0.684 F-measure on bibtex (#label=5) versus 0.567 (OC-SVM), 0.564 (iForest), and 0.478 (MUENLFOREST). The win/tie/loss counts against MUENLPLR are 195/0/0 across all multi-label metrics, indicating consistent dominance. Performance degrades gracefully as the number of unknown labels increases, with SLAN maintaining the largest margin over baselines at higher #label settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against multi-label classifiers, SLAN dominates MUENLPLR in all 195 comparisons (0 ties, 0 losses) across all five metrics and all datasets, with ranking loss gaps of 0.05–0.28 (e.g., 0.114 vs 0.396 on bibtex). Against LIFT, SLAN wins 154 of 195 comparisons; the notable exceptions are on corel5k where LIFT's label-specific clustering features give it a slight edge at low #label. Against SENCE, SLAN wins 107 but ties 68 and loses 20, reflecting SENCE's competitive clustering-ensemble approach on moderate-density datasets. Against LIMIC, SLAN wins 165 of 195 with no losses. Against anomaly detection baselines, SLAN achieves 26/4/0 wins/ties/losses over both OC-SVM and iForest on F-measure, and 23/7/0 over MUENLFOREST; the few ties occur on education and arts where the label density is low and the open-set signal is weak. The paper attributes MUENLFOREST's inferiority to its reliance on MUENLPLR predictive values that are trained without open-space risk awareness.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two well-studied paradigms: multi-label learning (MLL) and open set recognition (OSR). It builds directly on the feature-induced labeling information enrichment framework (Zhang et al., AAAI 2018), which transfers structural information from feature space to label space via manifold assumptions, but extends this enrichment to per-label sub-spaces to create a discriminative signal for unknown-label detection. The key conceptual departure is from prior OSR work (Scheirer et al., Bendale & Boult, EVT-based methods), all of which assume a single ground-truth label per instance; the multi-label co-occurrence of known and unknown labels on the same instance breaks these assumptions and necessitates the sub-labeling differentiation strategy. The paper also distinguishes itself from streaming multi-label learning (SMLL), where new labels emerge during training, by restricting unknown labels to the testing phase only.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MLOSR formalizes a practically important but previously undefined problem: how to both classify known labels and flag unknown labels when an instance carries multiple labels simultaneously. The jointly-optimized recognizer-classifier framework offers a practical recipe, but the approach enriches #labels+1 sub-labeling vectors, limiting scalability to extreme multi-label settings, and operates entirely within a traditional kernel/linear MLL schema rather than deep representations. Open questions include whether the sub-labeling differentiation principle transfers to deep architectures, how to design evaluation metrics specific to MLOSR (beyond repurposing F-measure), and whether the manifold-based structural assumption holds under the distributional shifts typical of truly open environments.
