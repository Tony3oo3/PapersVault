## Problem

Standard gradient descent and proximal gradient methods require knowledge of a global Lipschitz smoothness constant L to set a valid stepsize, which is often unavailable, overly conservative when curvature varies, or nonexistent for functions that are only locally smooth. The prior adaptive method AdGD [MM20] removed this requirement but could not accommodate the standard 1/L stepsize and was restricted to unconstrained problems. This paper refines the AdGD analysis to permit larger steps and extends the framework to the composite (proximal) case min F(x) = f(x) + g(x).

## Input / Output

The algorithm requires only an initial point x⁰, gradient evaluations of the smooth part f, the proximal operator of the nonsmooth part g, and a single initial stepsize α₀ chosen so that α₀L₁ ∈ [1/√2, 2] (a one-time linesearch). No additional function or gradient evaluations are needed beyond those of the base method. The output is a sequence of iterates converging to a minimizer of F with the rate min_{i∈[k]}(F(xⁱ)−F*) ≤ R²/(2∑αᵢ), yielding O(1/k) when α₀ satisfies the initialization condition.

## Method

The key technical insight is a decomposition of ‖x^{k+1}−x^k‖² that, combined with monotonicity of ∇f (Lemma 1), avoids the Cauchy-Schwarz bound used in [MM20] and replaces the stepsize constraint α_k L_k ≤ 1/2 with the weaker α_k²L_k² − α_k²/(2α_{k-1}²) ≤ 1/2, thereby admitting the standard 1/L step. The stepsize at each iteration is set as the minimum of a growth-rate cap √(2/3+θ_{k-1})·α_{k-1} and the local-smoothness cap α_{k-1}/√([2α_{k-1}²L_k²−1]₊), where L_k is the observed gradient-difference ratio. Extending to the proximal case (Algorithm 3) is nontrivial: the prox-mapping introduces a nonlinear update and the analysis must track a composite Lyapunov energy involving both f and g, with a novel monotonicity lemma (Lemma 13) controlling the subgradient of g across iterations. Convergence is established under only local Lipschitzness of ∇f, matching the assumptions needed for the continuous gradient flow, and the proof for the proximal case handles the possibility that α_k → ∞.

## Results

The paper establishes an O(1/k) convergence rate for AdProxGD: min_{i∈[k]}(F(xⁱ) − F*) ≤ R²/(2∑ᵢ αᵢ), which under the initial stepsize condition α₀L₁ ∈ [1/√2, 2] simplifies to LR²/(√2·k), where L is the local Lipschitz constant over the bounded trajectory. A key theoretical advance is that convergence is guaranteed under only local Lipschitzness of ∇f (not global), matching the assumptions needed for gradient flow. Empirically, across five test problems (maximum likelihood estimation, low-rank matrix completion, piecewise-linear curve, nonnegative matrix factorization, and dual entropy maximization), AdProxGD converges faster than all nine Armijo linesearch variants in the majority of scenarios, with the advantage most pronounced in the matrix completion and NMF settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is ProxGD with Armijo's linesearch, tested with nine (s, r) parameter pairs: (1.2, 0.5), (1.5, 0.8), (1.1, 0.5), (1.2, 0.9), (1.1, 0.9), (1.5, 0.5), (1.2, 0.8), (1.1, 0.8), and (1.5, 0.9). In the maximum likelihood estimation problem (Figure 1), AdProxGD outperforms all linesearch variants in both tested scenarios (n=100 and n=50). For low-rank matrix completion (Figure 2), AdProxGD is consistently faster than every linesearch version in both n=100 and n=200 settings. The piecewise-linear curve problem (Figure 3) shows the same pattern. In nonnegative matrix factorization (Figure 4), AdProxGD again wins in both configurations. The one partial exception is the dual entropy maximization problem (Figure 5), where in the second scenario (m=100, n=500) one linesearch variant is roughly comparable, though AdProxGD still leads in the first scenario. The authors note that the choice of (s, r) for linesearch matters substantially, and that linesearch can become numerically fragile near the solution due to very small values in the Armijo condition.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends AdGD (Malitsky & Mishchenko, ICML 2020), which introduced adaptive gradient descent using observed gradient differences to estimate local smoothness without requiring a global Lipschitz constant. The current work refines the stepsize analysis (replacing a Cauchy-Schwarz-based bound with a monotonicity-based argument that permits larger steps) and extends the method from unconstrained minimization to the composite proximal setting F = f + g. It also positions itself against Armijo linesearch (the standard robust alternative), Adagrad-type methods (which have diminishing steps and thus lack true adaptivity), and Barzilai-Borwein heuristics (which lack convergence guarantees). A concurrent extension by Latafat et al. [Lat+23] also addressed the proximal case, but the present paper uses a different and simpler proof with a novel Lyapunov energy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is that users of first-order methods no longer need to know or estimate the Lipschitz constant of the gradient, and the method converges under only local Lipschitzness — the same assumption needed for gradient flow existence — rather than global Lipschitzness. This is particularly relevant for objectives like x⁴ or logistic loss where curvature varies dramatically across the domain. Open questions include: establishing an O(1/k) rate for the last-iterate gradient norm (the lack-of-descent property makes this hard), extending the analysis to nonconvex settings (where the authors report encouraging but unproven empirical behavior), and whether the stepsize sum can be bounded closer to k/L with a readable proof. The method's advantage over linesearch is that it incurs zero additional function evaluations per iteration.
