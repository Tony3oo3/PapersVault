## Problem

Threshold-based auto-labeling (TBAL) relies on confidence scores to decide which unlabeled points to auto-label, but standard choices like softmax outputs and post-hoc calibration methods (temperature scaling, histogram binning) are misaligned with the TBAL objective of maximizing coverage under a bounded error constraint. These methods produce overconfident scores with large overlap between correct and incorrect predictions, resulting in substantially suboptimal coverage. No principled framework existed for learning confidence functions specifically optimized for the auto-labeling task.

## Input / Output

Colander takes as input a trained classifier h (any architecture), the concatenated representations from its last two layers (logits z^(1) ∈ R^k and pre-logits z^(2) ∈ R^{d2}), and a randomly split validation set D_val divided into D_cal (fraction ν) for learning the confidence function and D_th for threshold estimation. It outputs a learned 2-layer confidence function ĝ_i mapping R^{k+d2} → Δ^k, per-class auto-labeling thresholds t̂_i ∈ T^k, and the resulting auto-labeled subset of the unlabeled pool. The confidence function is retrained each TBAL iteration as the base model changes.

## Method

Colander formulates the auto-labeling objective as an optimization problem (P1) over the joint space of confidence functions g ∈ G and thresholds t ∈ T^k, maximizing population coverage subject to a bounded auto-labeling error constraint. To make this tractable, it replaces population quantities with empirical estimates on D_cal (P2), then introduces sigmoid surrogates σ(α, g(x)[ŷ] − t[ŷ]) for the 0-1 threshold indicators to obtain a differentiable surrogate objective (P3) solved jointly for g and t via Adam. The confidence function is a 2-layer network g(x) = softmax(W₂ tanh(W₁ z(x;h))) applied to concatenated last-layer representations, deliberately kept small since the base classifier already performs heavy representation learning. After solving (P3), reliable per-class thresholds are estimated on the held-out D_th using a confidence-interval-corrected error criterion (ê_y + C₁·ζ(ê_y) ≤ ε_a), ensuring the error constraint is not violated by the relaxed optimization.

## Results

Colander achieves up to 60% improvement in auto-labeling coverage over all baselines while maintaining auto-labeling error below the 5% tolerance. On CIFAR-10 with vanilla training, Colander reaches 78.5% coverage at 3.0% error, compared to Softmax's 14.0% coverage at 4.8% error and Temperature Scaling's 23.2% at 7.3% error. On 20 Newsgroups, Colander achieves 80.6% coverage at 2.5% error versus Softmax's 48.2% at 6.0% error. On Tiny-ImageNet (200 classes), Colander attains 59.2% coverage at 1.4% error while Softmax only reaches 32.6% at 11.1% error (violating the tolerance).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Colander decisively outperforms all six post-hoc calibration baselines (Softmax, Temperature Scaling, Dirichlet, Scaling-Binning, Top-Label Histogram Binning, and Adaptive Temperature Scaling) on every dataset, achieving 2–6× higher coverage while simultaneously reducing error. The calibration methods (TS, Dirichlet, SB, Top-HB, AdaTS) improve coverage over raw Softmax but consistently violate the 5% error tolerance (e.g., TS reaches 7.3% error on CIFAR-10, 9.7% on 20 Newsgroups, and 16.3% on Tiny-ImageNet), whereas Colander stays well below 5% in all settings. Among train-time methods, CRL and FMFP—designed to enforce ordinal ranking of confidence scores—show no meaningful improvement over vanilla training in the TBAL setting, likely because training error reaches zero quickly with limited data, leaving no signal for the ranking loss to exploit. Squentropy provides a modest boost over vanilla (e.g., 36.2% vs. 32.6% coverage on Tiny-ImageNet with Softmax), and Colander combined with Squentropy achieves the best overall results (66.5% coverage at 0.6% error on Tiny-ImageNet). The gains from Colander are consistent across all four train-time methods, confirming its independence from the specific training procedure.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the threshold-based auto-labeling (TBAL) framework established by Vishwakarma et al. (NeurIPS 2023) and Qiu et al. (ICLR 2023), which use model confidence scores above a threshold to auto-label data. It departs from the standard practice of repurposing calibration methods (temperature scaling, histogram binning, Dirichlet calibration) or ordinal-ranking train-time methods (CRL, FMFP) for the confidence function, arguing that these are misaligned with the TBAL objective of maximizing coverage under an error constraint. The key conceptual contribution is formulating the choice of confidence function as a dedicated optimization problem over a function class, solved via a sigmoid-relaxed surrogate, yielding the Colander method—a small post-hoc neural network trained specifically for TBAL rather than for probability calibration.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The up-to-60% coverage improvement over all calibration and ordinal-ranking baselines while maintaining sub-5% error has direct practical impact on labeling cost in industry TBAL systems like Amazon SageMaker Ground Truth. A notable finding is that ordinal-ranking methods (CRL, FMFP), designed to separate correct from incorrect predictions, fail to help TBAL because training error saturates quickly with limited data, leaving no signal for the ranking loss. The main limitation is the continued dependence on validation data for learning the confidence function, and an open question is whether this requirement can be eliminated or reduced. The method's simplicity (a 2-layer network, post-hoc, compatible with any train-time procedure) makes it easy to adopt in existing TBAL pipelines.
