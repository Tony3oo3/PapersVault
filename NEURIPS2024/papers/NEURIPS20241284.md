## Problem

Existing sparsely annotated object detection (SAOD) methods rely on fixed confidence thresholds to filter pseudo-labels, which fails for aerial scenes where large objects (e.g., planes) naturally exhibit high predicted probabilities while small objects (e.g., vehicles) have low ones. This probability imbalance makes a single threshold either too permissive for small objects or too restrictive for large ones, degrading pseudo-label quality and detector performance.

## Input / Output

The method takes high-resolution aerial RGB images (e.g., DOTA at 1024×1024, HRSC2016 at 2048×2048) where only 1–10% of instances per class are annotated with class labels and oriented/horizontal bounding boxes, alongside a large pool of fully unlabeled images. It produces oriented bounding boxes (OBB) or horizontal bounding boxes (HBB) with class labels for all 15 DOTA categories or the single ship class in HRSC2016. The sparsely annotated set also seeds class-wise prototypes (K=10 per class, 1024-d or 256-d features) that persist throughout training to guide pseudo-label exploration.

## Method

PECL formulates pseudo-label selection as a sequential conformal decision-making problem solved by a reinforcement-learning agent in a closed loop with detector training. The conformal pseudo-label explorer (a 3-layer MLP) takes an exploratory characteristic—concatenating the conformal confidence level (a class-conditional non-conformity score computed against K learned prototypes), the predicted probability, and the maximum cosine similarity to class prototypes—with contextual features from already-selected pseudo-labels and sparse annotations, then outputs a binary select/reject action. A multi-clue selection evaluator (Q-network) scores each state-action pair using an instant reward derived from the change in information entropy and confidence margin of the same-class instance set, and an experience replay pool stabilizes policy updates. The detector is progressively updated each epoch using both the sparse ground-truth annotations and the adaptively selected pseudo-labels, while prototypes evolve via momentum updates with a contrastive-plus-regularization loss, creating a mutually reinforcing loop that adapts selection sensitivity to each category's inherent probability distribution.

## Results

On the DOTA dataset at the 5% label rate (OBB task), ReDet w/PECL achieves 67.06% mAP, surpassing the best sparse-annotated baseline (Region-based, 65.71%) by 1.35% and the best semi-supervised method (Unbiased Teacher, 64.74%) by 2.32%. At the more challenging 1% label rate, PECL yields gains of 10.25%, 7.55%, and 15.62% over S²A-Net, OR-CNN, and ReDet baselines respectively. On HRSC2016 at 10% label rate, ReDet w/PECL reaches 87.29% OBB mAP, a 7.63% improvement over the ReDet baseline. The method also generalizes to the HBB task, where ReDet w/PECL at 10% label rate (69.95%) approaches the fully supervised ReDet (77.47%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PECL outperforms all nine compared baselines across both datasets and label rates. Against the strongest semi-supervised method, Unbiased Teacher [14] (64.74%), ReDet w/PECL leads by 2.32% mAP at 5% label rate on DOTA. Among sparse-annotated methods, PECL surpasses Region-based [19] (65.71%) by 1.35%, Co-mining [26] (65.35%) by 1.71%, and BRL [35] (65.04%) by 2.02%. With S²A-Net as the backbone, PECL (57.42%) beats SOOD [7] (53.74%) by 3.68% and Calibrated Teacher [25] (55.81%) by 1.61%. The gains are most pronounced at low label rates: at 1% on DOTA, ReDet w/PECL improves over the ReDet baseline by 15.62% mAP, and on HRSC2016 at 10%, OR-CNN w/PECL jumps from 55.70% to 85.50% OBB mAP (+29.80%), indicating PECL's particular strength in extremely sparse annotation regimes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of sparsely annotated object detection (SAOD) and conformal prediction, departing from the dominant fixed-threshold pseudo-label filtering strategy used by prior SAOD methods (Co-mining, Calibrated Teacher, Region-based). It repurposes conformal prediction's non-conformity scores and confidence levels (Shafer & Vovk, 2008) not as a standalone calibration tool but as a feature within a reinforcement learning decision-making loop, where a policy network adaptively selects pseudo-labels per candidate. The RL formulation draws on actor-critic and Q-learning paradigms previously applied to other CV tasks (tracking, video summarization, medical segmentation), but the specific combination of conformal uncertainty quantification with experience-replay-based policy optimization for pseudo-label selection in aerial scenes is novel.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work addresses a practical bottleneck in aerial intelligence systems where full instance-level annotation is prohibitively expensive, and the adaptive pseudo-label selection directly tackles the class-imbalance problem unique to aerial scenes (large planes vs. small vehicles). The finding that gains are substantially larger for dense small objects (33–46% mAP) than for sparse large objects (7–9%) validates the contextual-reasoning hypothesis and suggests the method's value scales with scene density. Limitations include restricted applicability to dense scenes, increased training time from the RL loop, and a notable performance drop at 100% label rate (75.18% vs. 76.25% baseline), indicating pseudo-label noise can harm fully supervised settings. Open questions include whether the conformal-RL framework generalizes to non-aerial domains and whether the RL overhead can be reduced without sacrificing adaptivity.
