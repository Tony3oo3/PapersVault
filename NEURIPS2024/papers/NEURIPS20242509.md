## Problem

Existing Concept Bottleneck Models (CBMs) that use LLMs and Vision-Language Models to automate concept annotation suffer from two critical flaws: predicted concepts frequently mismatch the input image (unfaithful interpretation), and the concept layer encodes unintended information such that even randomly initialized concepts achieve comparable accuracy to well-designed ones. The paper provides the first rigorous theoretical analysis of this information leakage and introduces a unified evaluation metric to control it.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242509/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images processed by a frozen backbone (CLIP-RN50, ResNet-18, or ResNet-50) producing d-dimensional embeddings, and outputs a class label, a one-hot concept vector over fine-grained visual concepts, and a top-5 interpretable decision explanation. Concept annotations are generated by Grounding DINO as bounding boxes with confidence scores (threshold T=0.15), and the final prediction layer is constrained to have a target Number of Effective Concepts (NEC), typically NEC=5, so each class decision is attributed to at most ~5 concepts on average.

## Method

VLG-CBM is a three-stage pipeline: (1) LLMs generate candidate visual concepts per class, then Grounding DINO (an open-vocabulary object detector) grounds these concepts to spatial bounding boxes in training images, automatically filtering non-visual concepts and producing a concept-labeled dataset with crop-to-concept augmentation; (2) the Concept Bottleneck Layer is trained with binary cross-entropy on the grounded labels; (3) a sparse linear final layer is trained with elastic-net regularization via GLM-SAGA, with the regularization path tuned to enforce a target NEC. The key differentiator is using vision-grounded object detection to ensure concept faithfulness (avoiding the CLIP modality gap), and the theoretical result (Theorem 4.1) proving that a random CBL with k≥d concepts can exactly replicate any linear classifier, which motivates the NEC metric for controlling information leakage and enabling fair cross-method comparison.

## Results

On the CLIP-RN50 backbone, VLG-CBM achieves 88.55% ANEC-5 on CIFAR-10, 65.73% on CIFAR-100, 59.74% on ImageNet, and 60.38% on CUB, outperforming all baselines by at least 4.27% and up to 51.09% on ANEC-5. On ANEC-avg, VLG-CBM leads by at least 0.45% and up to 29.78% across the five benchmarks. With non-CLIP backbones (ResNet-18/50), VLG-CBM reaches 75.79% ANEC-5 on CUB and 73.15% on ImageNet, surpassing LF-CBM by 22.28% and 12.85% respectively. A random CBL baseline achieves only 67.55% ANEC-5 on CIFAR-10, confirming that NEC-controlled evaluation effectively suppresses information leakage.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VLG-CBM outperforms LF-CBM [15] on every dataset and metric: by 4.50% on CIFAR-10 ANEC-5, 9.21% on CIFAR-100, 6.86% on ImageNet, and 29.03% on CUB. Against LaBo [27], VLG-CBM leads by 9.86% on CIFAR-10, 20.91% on CIFAR-100, 35.47% on ImageNet, and 18.41% on CUB (all ANEC-5). LM4CV [25] performs worst among all baselines, falling below even the random CBL on ANEC-5 for CIFAR-100 (14.64% vs. 29.52%), ImageNet (3.77% vs. 18.04%), and CUB (3.63% vs. 25.37%), which the authors attribute to its dense final layer becoming inefficient under NEC constraints. On non-CLIP backbones (Table 3), VLG-CBM beats LF-CBM by 22.28% on CUB and 12.85% on ImageNet (ANEC-5), while also matching or exceeding LF-CBM on ANEC-avg. The random CBL baseline confirms the information leakage concern: at high NEC it approaches trained model accuracy, but at NEC=5 its accuracy drops sharply, validating the NEC-controlled evaluation protocol.

## Relation to Prior Work

VLG-CBM extends the Concept Bottleneck Model framework (Koh et al., ICML 2020) and the label-free CBM line of work (LF-CBM, Oikarinen et al., ICLR 2023) by replacing VLM-based concept alignment (e.g., CLIP) with spatially grounded annotations from an open-vocabulary object detector (Grounding DINO). It departs from prior scalable CBMs (LF-CBM, LM4CV, LaBo) in two key ways: (1) concepts are filtered and localized via detection bounding boxes rather than relying on modality-gap-prone VLM embeddings, and (2) a sparse elastic-net final layer trained with GLM-SAGA replaces dense or uncontrolled sparsity. The paper also introduces the first formal theoretical analysis of information leakage in CBMs (Theorem 4.1), going beyond the empirical observations of prior work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The theoretical result that a random CBL with k ≥ d concepts can perfectly approximate any linear classifier provides a rigorous foundation for why accuracy alone is an unreliable CBM evaluation metric, and motivates the NEC metric as a practical control knob for information leakage and interpretability. The method's dependence on large pretrained detectors (Grounding DINO) mirrors the CLIP dependency of prior CBMs, so it does not eliminate the need for large models but shifts the bottleneck. Open questions include whether segmentation-level vision guidance could further improve faithfulness, and whether the NEC-controlled evaluation framework will be adopted as a standard for comparing CBMs across the community.
