## Problem

The KL divergence loss is one of the most widely used objectives in deep learning (adversarial training, knowledge distillation, semi-supervised learning), yet its gradient optimization mechanism has not been rigorously analyzed. Two practical issues remain unaddressed: the asymmetric gradient flow that silently disables the wMSE component when one model's logits are detached (as in knowledge distillation), and the reliance on sample-wise prediction scores for weighting, which misleads optimization on hard or misclassified examples.

## Input / Output

The loss operates on pairs of logit vectors o_m, o_n ∈ R^C produced by two (or one) CNN models given input images x_m, x_n. In adversarial training, x_m is a clean image and x_n is its PGD-perturbed counterpart fed to the same network; in knowledge distillation, the same image passes through a frozen teacher and a trainable student. The output is a scalar loss combining a weighted MSE term over pairwise logit differences and a cross-entropy term with soft labels. A running class-wise average probability vector s̄_y ∈ R^C is maintained per class during training to compute the global weights, adding negligible overhead (a C×C matrix update per batch).

## Method

The paper first proves (Theorem 1) that the KL divergence loss is gradient-equivalent to a Decoupled KL (DKL) loss comprising a weighted MSE (wMSE) over pairwise logit differences and a cross-entropy with soft labels, making the two components independently analyzable. Building on this decomposition, the authors derive the Improved KL (IKL) loss with two modifications: (1) breaking the asymmetric optimization by removing the stop-gradient on Δn in the wMSE term, so that the wMSE component always contributes gradients to the trainable model even when the reference model is frozen; and (2) replacing the sample-wise weight matrix w_m^{j,k} = s_m^j · s_m^k with a class-wise global weight w̄_y^{j,k} = s̄_y^j · s̄_y^k, where s̄_y is the mean probability vector over all training samples of class y, thereby regularizing against misclassification-induced bias. The final IKL loss (Eq. 10) is a drop-in replacement for KL in any pipeline, requiring only the maintenance of per-class running averages, and achieves state-of-the-art adversarial robustness on RobustBench and competitive knowledge distillation accuracy on CIFAR-10/100 and ImageNet.

## Results

On CIFAR-100 with basic augmentation (ε=8/255), IKL-AT (WRN-34-10) achieves 65.76% clean accuracy and 31.91% Auto-Attack robustness, setting a new state-of-the-art on RobustBench. With AutoAug, robustness further improves to 32.53%, and with 50M generated images (WRN-28-10), the model reaches 73.85% clean and 39.18% AA. On ImageNet knowledge distillation, IKL-KD achieves 71.91% top-1 (ResNet34→ResNet18) and 72.84% (ResNet50→MobileNet), surpassing all prior logits-based and feature-based methods while saving 38–52% training time compared to ReviewKD.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On CIFAR-100 adversarial training with basic augmentation, IKL-AT outperforms AWP by 3.05% AA (31.91 vs 28.86), LAS-AT by 1.14% (31.91 vs 30.77), and ACAT by 1.68% (31.91 vs 30.23), while matching ACAT's clean accuracy (65.76 vs 65.75). With 50M generated images, IKL-AT surpasses DM-AT by 1.27% clean and 0.35% AA (73.85/39.18 vs 72.58/38.83). On CIFAR-10, IKL-AT beats AWP by 0.92% AA (57.09 vs 56.17) and Rice et al. by 3.67% (57.09 vs 53.42). For ImageNet knowledge distillation, IKL-KD outperforms DKD by 0.21% (71.91 vs 71.70) and 0.79% (72.84 vs 72.05), ReviewKD by 0.30% and 0.28%, and feature-based methods OFD/CRD by 1.10–1.67% across both teacher-student pairs. On imbalanced ImageNet-LT, IKL-KD improves over KL-KD by 0.89% overall (49.29 vs 48.31) with ResNet-50 student, and in semi-supervised learning, replacing the consistency loss with IKL boosts Mean-Teacher from 67.38% to 70.05% on CIFAR-100.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on TRADES (Zhang et al., ICML 2019) for adversarial training and DKD (Zhao et al., CVPR 2022) for knowledge distillation, both of which use KL divergence as a core loss component without analyzing its internal gradient structure. The key conceptual departure is a mathematical proof that KL divergence loss is gradient-equivalent to a composite of a weighted MSE (capturing pairwise logit differences) and a cross-entropy with soft labels — a decomposition no prior work had established. From this decomposition, the authors identify and fix two structural issues: asymmetric gradient flow that silently disables the wMSE component when the teacher is detached (as in KD), and sample-wise prediction bias in the wMSE weighting that destabilizes training on hard examples.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The DKL decomposition provides a principled lens for understanding and modifying KL-based losses across many applications (adversarial training, KD, semi-supervised learning, incremental learning), potentially guiding future loss design beyond the specific IKL formulation. The SOTA on RobustBench (32.53% AA on CIFAR-100 without generated data) is achieved with modest margins (~0.7%) but is notable for also saving 33% computation versus prior best. Limitations include the requirement to maintain and update class-wise global statistics during training, evaluation restricted to image classification, and the fact that the class-wise information is most beneficial in later training stages. The authors explicitly flag OOD robustness and incremental learning as unexplored extensions.
