## Problem

FHE-based private training is 1–3 orders of magnitude slower than plaintext training, and while data pruning could reduce the number of samples processed, existing plaintext pruning methods (EL2N, forgetting score, entropy) rely on costly non-linear FHE operations (square root, O(N²) sorting) that negate or exceed the speedup. Additionally, sample-wise pruning leaves ciphertexts largely sparse because multiple samples are SIMD-packed into a single ciphertext, so the actual number of ciphertexts processed in each training step barely decreases.

## Input / Output

The client encrypts the dataset using CKKS with N=2^16 (32,768 slots per ciphertext) and multiplicative depth L=12, then sends the ciphertexts to the server. The server performs all training homomorphically and never decrypts; the only client-side decryption is of the importance scores (2 ciphertexts for CIFAR-10, ~4 MB) to compute the pruning threshold, after which a binary mask is sent back to the server. The final output is the trained model with encrypted weights, which the client decrypts locally.

## Method

HEPrune introduces three crypto-oriented optimizations layered on top of an FHE training pipeline. First, HEFS replaces the EL2N l2-norm (requiring ~10 Newton iterations for HE.Sqrt) with an l1-norm computed as HE.Max(Y−P, P−Y), reducing the score circuit to two homomorphic subtractions and one comparison. Second, Client-Aided Masking (CAM) offloads the O(N²) homomorphic sorting to the client, which decrypts the scores, runs QuickSelect in O(N) (~15 ms), and returns a binary mask, adding only ~4 MB of communication. Third, Ciphertext-Wise Pruning (CWP) packs surviving samples from sparse ciphertexts into empty slots of other ciphertexts via rotation and addition, eliminating the sparse ciphertexts that would otherwise still be processed in every forward/backward pass. Together these techniques achieve a 16× end-to-end speedup with ≤0.6% accuracy drop at 10% data retention.

## Results

HEPrune achieves a ~6.6× end-to-end training speedup over HETAL across all five datasets (e.g., 44.76h vs. 293.3h on CIFAR-10, 41.89h vs. 276.75h on MNIST) while maintaining accuracy within 0.26–0.73% of the full-data baseline. On the SNIPS audio dataset, HEPrune actually exceeds HETAL's accuracy (95.14% vs. 95.00%). Under the most aggressive pruning (1% of data), the paper reports up to 16× speedup with only a 0.6% accuracy drop on CIFAR-10, and 60.8× speedup with a 5.26% drop when training an MLP from scratch on MNIST.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is HETAL [Lee et al., ICML 2023], the most efficient full-data FHE private training framework at the time. HEPrune decisively wins on runtime across all five datasets, reducing training time by 6.5–6.6× (e.g., 44.76h vs. 293.3h on CIFAR-10; 17.36h vs. 113.7h on SNIPS). On accuracy, HEPrune trades a small amount of performance: it drops 0.26% on CIFAR-10 (96.31% vs. 96.57%), 0.73% on MNIST (95.54% vs. 96.27%), 0.25% on Face Mask Detection (95.21% vs. 95.46%), and 0.20% on DermaMNIST (75.86% vs. 76.06%). Notably, on SNIPS, HEPrune slightly outperforms HETAL (95.14% vs. 95.00%), suggesting that pruning noisy samples can occasionally improve generalization. The paper also shows that a naive application of plaintext pruning scores (e.g., EL2N with FHE sorting) actually *slows down* training to 488.91h on CIFAR-10 (0.6× speedup), underscoring that the crypto-oriented optimizations (HEFS, CAM, CWP) are essential for the method to be beneficial.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the HETAL private training framework (Lee et al., ICML 2023) by incorporating encrypted data pruning, and adapts the EL2N importance score (Paul et al., NeurIPS 2021) to the FHE setting. It departs from prior plaintext data pruning methods (forgetting score, entropy score, GraNd) by showing they incur prohibitive FHE overhead due to non-linear operations, and introduces three crypto-oriented optimizations: an HE-friendly score (HEFS) replacing EL2N, client-aided masking to avoid homomorphic sorting, and ciphertext-wise pruning to eliminate sparse ciphertexts. The key conceptual departure is recognizing that sample-wise pruning in FHE does not reduce ciphertext count, a problem absent in plaintext settings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FHE-based private training is 1–3 orders of magnitude slower than plaintext training, making it impractical for many applications; a 16× speedup with <1% accuracy loss meaningfully narrows this gap. The ciphertext-wise pruning insight is broadly applicable to any FHE training pipeline using SIMD packing, not just data pruning. However, the method is currently validated only on simple MLPs and small-to-medium datasets (≤70K samples), leaving open whether it scales to CNNs, Transformers, and large-scale benchmarks like ImageNet. Extending to deeper architectures and larger datasets, and reducing the reliance on client involvement (CAM), are the most pressing open questions.
