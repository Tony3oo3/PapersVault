## Problem

Masked prediction is a versatile self-supervised pretraining paradigm for offline RL, but it remains unclear how to design and arrange masking schemes to balance the learning of skills at different levels of complexity. Token-wise random masking, widely adopted from NLP, fails to capture long-term dependencies in decision-making data due to its low information density and interleaved state-action modality structure, causing the model to rely on local correlations rather than global reasoning.

## Input / Output

The model consumes state-action trajectories (context length 64, hidden dimension 256) from offline datasets of 12M steps collected from TD3 replay buffers across Walker, Quadruped, and Jaco environments. For zero-shot skill prompting, it takes a short 8-timestep state-action prompt and autoregressively generates 120 timesteps of actions; for goal-conditioned planning, it takes a start state interleaved with goal states at 20–80 timesteps ahead and predicts the connecting actions; for offline RL, it outputs a policy via actor-critic heads finetuned on top of the pretrained encoder with a causal attention mask.

## Method

CurrMask replaces token-wise masking with block-wise masking, where consecutive state-action tokens form semantically meaningful skill entities, forcing the model to reason over global dependencies rather than local correlations. A masking pool of 100 schemes (5 mask ratios × 20 block sizes) provides diverse prediction difficulties. An automated curriculum based on the EXP3 non-stochastic multi-armed bandit algorithm dynamically selects which masking scheme to train on at each evaluation interval, using the decrease in target reconstruction loss (rescaled via historical percentiles) as a reward signal. This balances exploitation of effective schemes with exploration of new ones, allowing the model to progressively acquire skills from simple local behaviors to complex long-horizon reasoning without requiring downstream task information during pretraining.

## Results

On zero-shot skill prompting across 9 MuJoCo tasks (Walker, Quadruped, Jaco), CurrMask achieves an average reward of 75.0, outperforming the strongest baseline Mixed-prog (68.2) by 10% and MaskDP (56.7) by 32%. On goal-conditioned planning (lower L2 distance is better), CurrMask achieves an average distance of 8.60, marginally beating MaskDP (8.71) and Mixed (8.73), while significantly outperforming Goal-GPT (12.61). In offline RL finetuning, CurrMask consistently yields faster convergence and higher final returns than all masked-prediction and autoregressive baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against MaskDP (Liu et al., 2022), which uses token-wise random masking, CurrMask achieves a 32% improvement in average skill prompting reward (75.0 vs. 56.7) and a 1.3% improvement in goal-conditioned planning distance (8.60 vs. 8.71), with the largest gains on Walker Walk (+37%) and Walker Run (+33%). Against MTM (Wu et al., 2023), which uses random autoregressive masking, CurrMask leads by 19% on skill prompting (75.0 vs. 63.0) and by 19% on planning distance (8.60 vs. 10.59). Against the autoregressive GPT baseline (Brown et al., 2020), CurrMask outperforms by 15% on skill prompting (75.0 vs. 65.3) and by 32% on planning distance (8.60 vs. 12.61), indicating that bidirectional masked prediction with curriculum scheduling is substantially more effective than unidirectional sequence modeling for versatile skill acquisition. Notably, CurrMask wins 7 of 9 skill prompting tasks and 8 of 9 planning tasks, while the block-wise baselines (Mixed, Mixed-prog) only match or slightly exceed MaskDP on planning, confirming that the curriculum scheduling—not merely block-wise masking—is the key driver of CurrMask's advantage.

## Relation to Prior Work

CurrMask extends the masked prediction framework for RL pretraining established by MaskDP (Liu et al., 2022) by replacing its random token-wise masking with block-wise masking borrowed from SpanBERT and BEiT, motivated by the low information density and interleaved state-action structure of decision-making data. The key departure is the introduction of an automated curriculum over masking schemes, formulated as a non-stochastic multi-armed bandit problem (EXP3) that adaptively schedules masking difficulty based on reconstruction loss decrease, building on the automated curriculum learning framework of Graves et al. (2017). Unlike prior work that uses fixed, random, or manually staged masking schedules (MaskDP, MTM, UniMask, SMART), CurrMask treats the masking scheme selection as an online optimization problem, dynamically balancing exploration of new masking configurations against exploitation of those yielding the greatest learning progress.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled answer to the underexplored question of how to design and sequence masking schemes for RL pretraining, showing that both the structure (block-wise vs. token-wise) and the temporal ordering (curriculum) of masking matter for downstream versatility. The finding that a manually designed progressive curriculum (Mixed-prog) does not consistently outperform random masking, while the adaptive bandit-based curriculum does, highlights the value of data-driven scheduling over heuristics. However, the method is validated only on low-dimensional MuJoCo control tasks (walker, quadruped, Jaco arm), and the authors acknowledge that computational overhead from the bandit mechanism and the potential sensitivity to environment structure remain open questions, particularly for scaling to image-based or high-dimensional RL settings.
