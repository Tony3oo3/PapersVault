## Problem

Existing zero-shot TTS systems produce high-fidelity speech for formal reading but fail to capture the spontaneous dynamics of natural conversation—overlapping speech, interjections, laughter, and seamless turn-taking. Prior dialogue generation methods either lack zero-shot voice cloning, generate speakers sequentially without overlap, or rely on speaker IDs rather than reference audio, making them unsuitable for realistic multi-talker, multi-round dialogue synthesis.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243182/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes a chronologically ordered dialogue transcription annotated with [spkchange] and [laughter] tokens, plus a short reference audio prompt for each target speaker, and produces a single mono-channel waveform in which multiple speakers may overlap naturally. Unlike conventional pipelines that synthesize each utterance independently and concatenate, CoVoMix outputs a single mixed mel-spectrogram (intermediate) that encodes concurrent speech from all talkers, which is then decoded to waveform by a HiFi-GAN vocoder. The transcription format requires no explicit overlap labels or phoneme-level alignment; speaker turns are demarcated solely by the [spkchange] token.

## Method

CoVoMix is a three-stage pipeline: (1) a multi-stream text-to-semantic encoder-decoder transformer that maps dialogue text (tokenized by BERT) into parallel HuBERT semantic token streams—one per speaker—by splitting the decoder's final linear layer into two halves along the feature dimension, enabling joint modeling of inter-speaker context; (2) a flow-matching transformer encoder that generates a single mixed mel-spectrogram conditioned on the multi-stream semantic embeddings and multi-speaker acoustic prompts, trained with a Conditional Flow Matching objective and classifier-free guidance (p_uncond=0.3, α=0.7); (3) a standard HiFi-GAN vocoder for waveform synthesis. The key architectural insight is generating one mixed spectrogram rather than per-speaker channels, which naturally produces overlapping speech, realistic turn-taking pauses, and paralinguistic behaviors like laughter without explicit alignment or duration modeling. Training is fully supervised on the Fisher 2k-hour telephone conversation dataset, with the T2S model trained via cross-entropy and the acoustic model via CFM loss, both from scratch on 8×V100 GPUs.

## Results

On the Fisher monologue test set, CoVoMix achieves WER 8.95, MCD 6.04, NISQA 3.01, and CMOS +0.83, outperforming the phoneme-based baseline (WER 15.85, MCD 9.45, CMOS −1.60) and CoVoSingle (WER 9.99, CMOS 0.00). On the dialogue test set, CoVoMix achieves MCD 6.82, NISQA 2.87, CMOS +0.81, and SMOS +0.60, significantly surpassing CoVoSingle (CMOS 0.00, SMOS 0.00) in perceived naturalness and speaker similarity. Notably, both proposed systems exceed the ground-truth NISQA score (3.01 vs. 3.03 monologue; 2.87 vs. 2.73 dialogue), indicating the flow-matching acoustic model produces cleaner audio than the original 8 kHz telephone recordings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares CoVoMix against two internal baselines: a phoneme-based flow-matching model (similar to VoiceBox) and CoVoSingle (the same pipeline restricted to monologue data with utterance-level concatenation for dialogue). Against the phoneme-based baseline, both CoVoSingle and CoVoMix achieve substantially lower WER (9.99/8.95 vs. 15.85) and MCD (6.15/6.04 vs. 9.45) on monologue, with CMOS improving from −1.60 to 0.00/+0.83, confirming that semantic token sequences eliminate the need for error-prone forced alignment. Against CoVoSingle, CoVoMix wins decisively on subjective dialogue metrics (CMOS +0.81 vs. 0.00; SMOS +0.60 vs. 0.00, both p<0.01), demonstrating that joint multi-stream generation produces more natural turn-taking and consistent speaker identity. However, CoVoMix's dialogue WER (19.84) is higher than CoVoSingle's (11.77) because overlapping speech segments cause transcription–audio mismatches in the chronologically sorted reference, a known limitation of ASR-based evaluation on mixed speech.

## Relation to Prior Work

CoVoMix builds directly on the conditional flow-matching framework of Lipman et al. (2022) and the VoiceBox paradigm (Le et al., 2023), adapting the flow-matching acoustic model to accept multi-stream semantic token inputs and produce a single mixed mel-spectrogram rather than a single-speaker one. It departs from prior dialogue generation work (dGSLM, CHATS, SoundStorm) in two key ways: it operates in a zero-shot voice-cloning setting (rather than relying on speaker IDs or fixed training speakers), and it generates a single-channel mixed waveform with overlapping speech rather than separate per-speaker channels or strictly sequential utterances. The use of HuBERT discrete semantic tokens as an intermediate representation, combined with a multi-stream autoregressive text-to-semantic decoder, distinguishes it from phoneme-based baselines and avoids the forced-alignment fragility that plagues conventional TTS pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first system to demonstrate zero-shot, multi-talker, single-channel conversational speech generation with natural turn-taking, overlapping speech, and spontaneous behaviors like laughter, filling a clear gap between high-quality monologue TTS and realistic multi-speaker dialogue. The comprehensive dialogue-specific evaluation metrics (turn-taking statistics, paralinguistic behavior detection, cross-utterance speaker consistency) provide a reusable framework for the community. Key limitations include the 8 kHz Fisher dataset (telephone quality), the autoregressive text-to-semantic model's tendency to omit or duplicate words, and the restriction to two speakers; scaling to more talkers, higher-fidelity data, and non-autoregressive semantic prediction remain open.
