## Problem

Existing robust GNN defenses (SoftMedian, TWIRLS, ElasticGNN) exhibit closely aligned robustness under adaptive attacks yet all suffer catastrophic performance degradation as attack budgets increase, eventually underperforming graph-agnostic MLPs. The paper identifies that these methods share a common ℓ₁-based graph smoothing principle whose inherent estimation bias accumulates with each adversarial edge, explaining the progressive failure. No prior work had unified these defenses under a single robust estimation framework or addressed the bias mechanism.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a graph adjacency matrix A ∈ {0,1}^{n×n} and a node feature matrix F ∈ ℝ^{n×d}, and produces robust node representations that feed into a classification head for node labels. The architecture follows a decoupled GNN design (MLP encoder + 10 aggregation layers, similar to APPNP), where each aggregation layer computes per-edge weights W_ij based on the ℓ₂ distance between degree-normalized node features, then performs a weighted neighborhood aggregation with a skip connection to the initial features. The key unusual aspect is that edge weights are computed dynamically at every layer from the current feature state, enabling progressive pruning of suspicious edges as the network deepens.

## Method

The paper proposes RUNG (Robust Unbiased Graph aggregation), built on two key components. First, a Robust and Unbiased Graph signal Estimator (RUGE) that replaces the ℓ₁ edge-difference penalty with the Minimax Concave Penalty (MCP) ρ_γ(y), which approximates ℓ₁ for small differences but saturates to a constant for large differences, thereby suppressing smoothing across adversarial edges without the constant shrinkage bias of ℓ₁. Second, a Quasi-Newton IRLS (QN-IRLS) algorithm that solves the resulting non-smooth, non-convex objective by constructing a quadratic upper bound at each iteration and using a diagonal approximate Hessian Q̂ = 2(diag(q)+λI) to compute a stepsize-free Newton-like update, with a guaranteed descent theorem. Unfolding each QN-IRLS iteration as one GNN layer yields the RUNG aggregation: F^(k+1) = (diag(q^(k))+λI)^{-1}((W^(k)⊙Ã)F^(k)+λF^(0)), where W_ij^(k) = max(0, 1/y_ij^(k) − 1/γ) automatically zeros out edges whose node feature difference exceeds the threshold γ, effectively pruning malicious edges. The method covers GCN, APPNP, and ElasticGNN as special cases by varying the penalty ρ.

## Results

On Cora ML under adaptive local attacks (the strongest evaluation setting), RUNG achieves 70.7% accuracy at 100% perturbation budget and maintains 69.3% even at 200% budget, substantially outperforming all baselines. The next-best method, TWIRLS-T, drops to 54.7% at 100% budget, while ℓ1-based methods like SoftMedian (46.7%) and TWIRLS (36.0%) degrade catastrophically. On the global attack setting (Table 2), RUNG reaches 65.1% at 40% budget versus 60.8% for TWIRLS-T and 58.7% for TWIRLS. On the large-scale Ogbn-Arxiv dataset, RUNG achieves 61.2% at 10% budget compared to 50.1% for SoftMedian and 42.2% for APPNP.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RUNG dominates all 16 baselines across every attack budget on both Cora ML and Citeseer. At the critical 100% local attack budget on Cora ML, RUNG (70.7%) outperforms the strongest competing defense TWIRLS-T (54.7%) by 16 points, SoftMedian (46.7%) by 24 points, HANG (39.5%) by 31 points, and GARNET (42.7%) by 28 points. The ℓ1-based baselines (SoftMedian, TWIRLS, RUNG-ℓ1) show closely aligned performance, confirming the paper's unified ℓ1-based graph smoothing analysis, but all suffer catastrophic degradation beyond 100% budget due to accumulated estimation bias. RUNG's MCP-based penalty eliminates this bias, maintaining near-constant accuracy (69.3%) from 150% to 200% budget where TWIRLS-T drops to 40.7% and SoftMedian to 8.7%. Notably, RUNG-ℓ1 (the ℓ1 variant) performs comparably to TWIRLS-T, isolating the MCP penalty as the source of RUNG's superior robustness. On the large-scale Ogbn-Arxiv dataset, RUNG (61.2% at 10% budget) outperforms SoftMedian (50.1%) and APPNP (42.2%) by 11 and 19 points respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies three architecturally distinct robust GNNs—SoftMedian, TWIRLS, and ElasticGNN—under a single ℓ₁-based graph signal smoothing framework, revealing they share the same underlying estimation principle despite different optimization solvers (soft median approximation, IRLS, and PAPC respectively). It then departs from this lineage by identifying the well-known ℓ₁ estimation bias (from high-dimensional statistics) as the mechanism causing catastrophic performance degradation under large attack budgets, and imports the MCP penalty from variable selection literature to construct an unbiased robust estimator. The resulting QN-IRLS algorithm extends classical IRLS with a diagonal quasi-Newton Hessian approximation, and the overall architecture follows the decoupled GNN design of APPNP.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified ℓ₁-based view provides a principled diagnostic for why a family of seemingly diverse robust GNNs exhibit aligned performance and shared failure modes, giving the field a clearer taxonomy of defenses. The bias analysis opens a concrete direction for future robust GNN design: any ℓ₁-based smoothing will accumulate bias with attack budget, so unbiased penalties (MCP, SCAD) or alternative mechanisms are needed. Limitations include evaluation primarily on homophilic citation graphs, the observation that RUNG's advantage is most pronounced at large budgets (where all methods struggle), and the acknowledged need for further efficiency improvements in the QN-IRLS solver.
