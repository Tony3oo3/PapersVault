## Problem

Selling a single item to n sequential buyers via posted pricing is a practical alternative to optimal auctions, but the tight sample complexity for learning near-optimal prices was unknown across all four settings (independent vs. correlated distributions, welfare vs. revenue maximization). Prior work gave Õ(n/ε²) upper bounds, leaving open whether the linear dependency on n is truly necessary or an artifact of the analysis.

## Input / Output

The learning algorithm receives T IID samples of the full value vector V ∈ [0,1]ⁿ drawn from an unknown distribution D, along with error parameter ε ∈ (0,1) and failure probability δ ∈ (0,1). It outputs a price vector π = (π₁,…,πₙ) fixed before the first buyer arrives, achieving ε-additive approximation to the optimal policy with probability at least 1−δ. For correlated distributions, the policy may be restricted to a change-point class Π_S where prices can only change at a specified subset S ⊆ {2,…,n}, reducing the number of free price parameters to |S|+1.

## Method

For welfare maximization on product distributions, the key insight is to analyze a martingale that accumulates the per-buyer errors introduced by replacing true distributions with empirical ones in the backward-induction dynamic program; because errors for different buyers balance, Freedman's inequality yields an O(1/ε² · log²(1/δ)) sample bound independent of n. For revenue maximization, a matching Ω(n/ε²) lower bound is established via a Hellinger-distance argument: each buyer's distribution is chosen from a High/Low pair that shifts the optimal price by O(ε/n), and only O(1/n) of the samples carry distinguishing information. For correlated distributions, the upper bound is obtained by bounding the pseudo-dimension of the restricted policy class Π_S: the 'good sets' {ρ : π_ρ(v) ≥ z} are shown to be expressible as unions of O(|S|+1) axis-aligned halfspaces, and the piecewise-decomposability framework of Balcan et al. (STOC 2021) then gives O((|S|+1) log(|S|+1)) pseudo-dimension, translating to the stated sample complexity via standard uniform-convergence results. The learning algorithm in the correlated setting is simply sample average approximation (SAA), computable in O(Tn(Tn)^{1+|S|}) time.

## Results

The paper establishes tight (up to log factors) sample complexity bounds for posted pricing across four settings. For product distributions, welfare maximization requires only O(1/ε² · log²(1/δ)) samples — independent of the number of buyers n — while revenue maximization requires Ω(n/ε²) samples, confirming a separation between the two objectives. For correlated distributions, the paper shows Ω(n/ε²) samples are necessary even for welfare, and when restricting to policies with |S| change-points, the sample complexity is O(((1+|S|)log(1+|S|) + log(1/δ))/ε²) with a matching Ω((1+|S|)/ε²) lower bound.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's main comparison is against prior sample complexity results rather than empirical baselines. Guo et al. [GHTZ21] previously showed Õ(n/ε²) samples suffice for both welfare and revenue maximization on product distributions; this paper improves the welfare bound to O(1/ε² · log²(1/δ)), removing the linear dependence on n entirely, while proving the n-dependence is unavoidable for revenue via an Ω(n/ε²) lower bound. For correlated distributions, Balcan et al. [BDD+21] gave a Õ(n/ε²) upper bound via generalization guarantees; this paper matches it with a lower bound for welfare and refines the upper bound to O((|S|log|S|)/ε²) when the policy is restricted to |S| change-points, with a matching lower bound. The key methodological contribution is the use of Freedman's martingale inequality to avoid a poly(n) loss in the welfare analysis, and the application of pseudo-dimension bounds via piecewise decomposability for the correlated setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the sample complexity analysis of posted pricing initiated by Kleinberg and Leighton (2003) for a single buyer and generalized by Guo et al. (2021) to n buyers, who established O(n/ε²) upper bounds for both welfare and revenue objectives under product distributions. The key methodological departure is the use of a martingale-based argument with Freedman's inequality to show that welfare maximization under product distributions actually requires only O(1/ε²) samples, independent of n, by exploiting cancellation of errors across the dynamic program. For correlated distributions, the paper applies the pseudo-dimension and piecewise-decomposability framework of Balcan et al. (STOC 2021) to bound the complexity of the policy class with restricted change-points, yielding sample complexity depending only on the number of change-points rather than n.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper resolves a natural open question by providing matching upper and lower bounds (up to logarithmic factors) across all four settings (product/correlated × welfare/revenue), revealing a clean separation: welfare is n-independent for product distributions but n-dependent for correlated ones, while revenue is always n-dependent. This separation has practical implications for mechanism design, suggesting that learning near-optimal prices for welfare (prophet inequality) is fundamentally easier than for revenue when buyers are independent. Open questions include whether more efficient algorithms exist for the correlated case (the SAA algorithm has runtime O(Tn(Tn)^{1+|S|})), and whether the gap between the O(k log k) upper and Ω(k) lower bounds for change-point policies can be closed.
