## Problem

Existing approaches to blackbox function estimation sit at two extremes: experimental design estimates the entire function (prohibitively expensive), while Bayesian optimization targets only maximizers/minimizers (lacking information about intermediate regions). Level set estimation offers a middle ground but requires a threshold that can yield undesirably large or small sets without domain knowledge. This paper formalizes the active set ordering problem, which estimates exactly k inputs with the highest function values using pairwise comparisons, avoiding both the cost of full estimation and the threshold sensitivity of level sets.

## Input / Output

The method operates on a finite discretized input domain of n points in R^d and a top-k parameter k, sequentially selecting one input per iteration to obtain a noisy evaluation y(x) = f(x) + ε(x) with known noise variance σ_n². The output is the predicted top-k set S_μ(k) — exactly k inputs ranked by GP posterior mean — along with a cumulative regret guarantee over the sequence of predictions. Unlike level set estimation, the output set size is fixed at k regardless of function values, and the prediction is made purely from the GP posterior mean rather than from confidence intervals or acquisition functions.

## Method

The Mean Prediction (MP) algorithm predicts the top-k set using only the GP posterior mean (Lemma 3.5 shows the ordering that minimizes worst-case regret equals the mean-based ordering), then selects the next sampling input by identifying the boundary pair (x̄_t, x̄'_t) between S_μ(k) and its complement that maximizes the upper confidence bound of pairwise regret, and sampling from one of four candidate inputs derived from that pair (argmax of upper bound, argmin of lower bound, argmax of interval length, or argmin of interval length among the first two). A novel regret definition for pairwise orderings is introduced, and the cumulative regret is bounded by O(√(Tβ_T γ_T)) with probability at least 1−δ, matching the GP-UCB rate. When k=1, the framework recovers GP-UCB exactly, providing a new theoretical justification for using the GP posterior mean maximizer as the predicted optimum and revealing that GP-UCB is not the only sampling strategy achieving sublinear regret.

## Results

The paper evaluates the Mean Prediction (MP) algorithm using the proposed set-ordering regret $r_{\pi_{\mu_t}(S_{\mu_t}(k), S_{\mu_t}^c(k))}$ across 7 datasets (4 synthetic, 3 real-world environmental). MP with any of the four sampling strategies (∇, △, ∨, ∧) consistently converges to lower regret than both the Var (uncertainty sampling) and Rand (random) baselines for estimating the top-5 set and multiple top-k sets {S(1), S(10), S(20)}. The theoretical cumulative regret bound is $\mathcal{O}(\sqrt{T\beta_T\gamma_T})$, matching GP-UCB's bound for the SE kernel ($\mathcal{O}^*(\sqrt{T(\log T)^{2d}})$). For Bayesian optimization (k=1), MP with $\mathbf{x}_t = \bar{\mathbf{x}}_t \nabla \bar{\mathbf{x}}_t'$ is equivalent to GP-UCB and performs comparably to it, though EI and MES achieve lower regret in some experiments (e.g., NO₃ and Phosphorus datasets).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Var (uncertainty sampling, $\mathbf{x}_t = \arg\max_x \sigma_t^2(x)$), MP wins on all 7 datasets for both single top-k and multiple top-k set estimation, converging to noticeably lower regret because MP concentrates its sampling budget on the boundary of the top-k set rather than uniformly across the domain. Against Rand (random sampling), MP also dominates across all experiments. For the Bayesian optimization comparison (k=1, Figure 4), MP with $\bar{\mathbf{x}}_t \nabla \bar{\mathbf{x}}_t'$ is algorithmically identical to GP-UCB [Srinivas et al., ICML 2010], so they achieve identical regret curves by construction. Against PI [Kushner, 1964] and EI [Močkus, 1982], MP/GP-UCB performs comparably on most datasets, but EI outperforms both on the NO₃ and Phosphorus datasets. Against MES [Wang & Jegelka, ICML 2017], MES also outperforms MP/GP-UCB in some experiments (Figs. 4b, 4c), consistent with MES being a more sophisticated acquisition function. The paper explicitly notes its aim is not to demonstrate state-of-the-art BO performance but to show MP is comparable to GP-UCB while providing a unified framework that generalizes to top-k and multiple-set estimation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself between two well-studied extremes: Bayesian experimental design (estimating the entire function globally) and Bayesian optimization (finding only maximizers/minimizers). It draws on the GP-UCB framework of Srinivas et al. (2010) and level set estimation literature, but introduces a new problem formulation—active set ordering—where the goal is to identify top-k inputs via pairwise comparison regret rather than threshold-based level sets. The key conceptual departure is showing that GP-UCB emerges as a special case (k=1) of a more general ordering-based framework, and that the GP posterior mean (not the UCB) is the theoretically justified predictor for the maximizer, a justification absent from prior entropy-search analyses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unification of BO, level set estimation, and top-k identification under a single ordering-based framework provides a principled middle ground for applications where estimating the full function is too expensive but finding only the global optimum is insufficient (e.g., environmental monitoring requiring top-k locations). The theoretical contribution—showing that multiple sampling strategies beyond UCB achieve sublinear regret for the ordering problem—opens the door to alternative acquisition functions. Limitations include the finite input domain assumption, the reliance on GP model correctness, and the fact that the regret bound (O*(√(T(log T)^{2d}))) is looser than the known lower bound (Ω(√(T(log T)^{d/2}))) for the SE kernel, leaving a gap that future work could tighten.
