## Problem

Existing fine-tuning pipelines for Visual Foundation Models in Visual Place Recognition rely on first-order probing (linear probing, GeM pooling) that cannot capture second-order statistics, and the dominant second-order aggregator NetVLAD requires a costly, dataset-dependent offline initialization of semantic centroids that introduces inductive bias and limits generalization. Simply removing centroids (as SALAD does) degrades aggregation performance, leaving a gap between the theoretical benefit of second-order features and their practical deployment in VPR.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 224×224 RGB images (query and database) processed by a frozen DINOv2 ViT backbone into 16×16 patch-level local descriptors. These descriptors are projected through two parallel two-layer MLP branches (F_C to 128-dim, F_P to 64-dim with softmax) and multiplied element-wise to produce an 8192-dimensional global descriptor vector. Retrieval is performed via Euclidean distance between query and database descriptors, with Recall@K as the evaluation metric.

## Method

EMVP reinterprets NetVLAD as bilinear pooling and shows that applying Constant Normalization (softmax or ℓ2-norm) to the assignment probability matrix makes the semantic-centroid term a dataset-level constant that can be dropped, yielding Centroid-Free Probing: G ≈ F_C(X)ᵀ F_P(X), a bilinear product of two learned linear projections that implicitly encodes centroid priors without explicit initialization. A Dynamic Power Normalization (DPN) module replaces the fixed exponent in classical Matrix Power Normalization with a per-image learnable scalar computed from the bilinear feature, enabling adaptive preservation of discriminative region information. DPN is deployed in two roles: DPN_C post-processes the CFP output, and DPN_R is inserted into the last 4 frozen ViT blocks (sequentially or in parallel) to recalibrate intermediate features toward background regions critical for place recognition. The full EMVP pipeline trains only ~0.05M parameters (vs. 27.1M for full fine-tuning SALAD) on GSV-Cities, achieving 93.9% R@1 on MSLS Val and 96.5% on Pitts250k-test.

## Results

EMVP-L achieves 93.9% R@1 on MSLS Validation, 96.5% R@1 on Pittsburgh250k-test, 94.6% R@1 on SPED, and 78.4% R@1 on Nordland, setting new state-of-the-art among single-stage VPR methods. Notably, EMVP-L outperforms the full fine-tuning method SALAD by 1.7% R@1 on MSLS while using only 0.06M trainable parameters versus SALAD's 27.1M (a 99.8% reduction). Compared to the prior SOTA PEFT method PSRP, EMVP saves 64.3% of trainable parameters (0.05M vs. 0.14M) while achieving higher accuracy across all four benchmark datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EMVP-L outperforms all single-stage baselines across all four benchmark datasets. Against the closest competitor SALAD (full fine-tuning on DINOv2), EMVP-L gains +1.7% R@1 on MSLS (93.9 vs. 92.2), +1.4% on Pitts250k (96.5 vs. 95.1), +2.5% on SPED (94.6 vs. 92.1), and +2.4% on Nordland (78.4 vs. 76.0), while using 99.8% fewer trainable parameters. Against MixVPR (the prior best single-stage method without VFM), EMVP-L improves R@1 by +5.9% on MSLS, +1.9% on Pitts250k, and +9.4% on SPED. In the two-stage comparison (Table 1b), EMVP-L as a single-stage method surpasses SelaVPR with re-ranking by +3.1% R@1 on MSLS (93.9 vs. 90.8) and +1.2% on Pitts30k (94.0 vs. 92.8), demonstrating that the VFM-based single-stage pipeline eliminates the need for a costly re-ranking stage. Among PEFT methods, the sequential DPN_R variant (0.05M params) outperforms PSRP (0.14M params) by +0.5% R@1 on MSLS and +0.4% on Pitts250k, confirming that the proposed DPN module is both more parameter-efficient and more effective than prior second-order probing approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reinterprets the classic NetVLAD aggregation (Arandjelovic et al., 2016) through the lens of bilinear pooling (Lin et al., 2015), showing that semantic centroids can be eliminated via a Constant Normalization constraint, yielding the Centroid-Free Probing (CFP) stage. It extends the Matrix Power Normalization (MPN) post-processing (Lin et al., 2017) into a learnable, context-adaptive Dynamic Power Normalization (DPN) module. While SALAD (Izquierdo & Civera, 2024) also removes explicit centroids when fine-tuning DINOv2, EMVP provides the theoretical justification for this simplification and further introduces a PEFT pipeline combining DPN-based recalibration with CFP, distinguishing itself from single-branch second-order methods like Moment Probing (Gao et al., 2023) by exploiting cross-branch bilinear interactions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for deploying VPR on resource-constrained mobile robots: EMVP achieves state-of-the-art accuracy (93.9% R@1 on MSLS) with only 0.05M trainable parameters versus 27.1M for full fine-tuning, reducing communication and storage overhead. The removal of costly offline k-means centroid initialization also eliminates a practical bottleneck and a source of inductive bias that limited generalization across environments. Open questions include whether the CFP+DPN pipeline transfers to other VFMs (CLIP, SAM) with different pre-training objectives, and whether the fixed choice of recalibrating the last 4 Transformer blocks can be made adaptive. The paper acknowledges that bad weather and ambiguous scenes remain challenging, and that the approach has only been validated with DINOv2.
