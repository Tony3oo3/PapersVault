## Problem

Existing intent learning methods for sequential recommendation rely on an alternating EM framework where clustering (E-step) and representation learning (M-step) are optimized separately. This creates two critical issues: clustering must run on the full dataset, causing out-of-memory problems at industrial scale, and the decoupled optimization leads to sub-optimal performance and implementation complexity.

## Input / Output

The method consumes user behavior sequences (item IDs truncated or left-padded to a fixed length T=50) and an item vocabulary, encoding them via a 2-head Transformer with latent dimension d'=64. Outputs include a next-item prediction score over the item set and a set of k learnable cluster centers (k=256 or 512) that represent latent user intents; each user's aggregated behavior embedding (dimension d=T·d'=3200) is assigned to the nearest intent center for downstream group-based recommendation.

## Method

ELCRec replaces the EM framework with an end-to-end learnable clustering module (ELCM) that initializes k cluster centers as trainable neural parameters and optimizes them jointly with the encoder via a two-term loss: an intent-decoupling term that pushes cluster centers apart on the unit sphere, and an intent-behavior alignment term that pulls each mini-batch behavior embedding toward all cluster centers (avoiding confirmation bias from nearest-center assignment). A second component, intent-assisted contrastive learning (ICL), fuses the nearest cluster center into augmented behavior views (via concatenation or shift) and adds an intent-level contrastive loss between behavior embeddings and cluster centers, using the learned intents as self-supervision signals. The full model is trained with a single multi-task objective combining next-item prediction, ICL (weight 0.1), and clustering loss (weight α), enabling mini-batch optimization that scales linearly with batch size rather than total user count.

## Results

ELCRec achieves the best or near-best performance across all four public benchmarks (Sports, Beauty, Toys, Yelp) on 15 out of 16 metrics. The most notable improvement is NDCG@5 on Beauty, where ELCRec reaches 0.0355, outperforming the runner-up ICLRec (0.0326) by 8.90%. On Sports, ELCRec achieves HR@5 of 0.0286 (8.75% over ICLRec) and NDCG@5 of 0.0185 (6.94% over ICLRec). The only non-significant result is HR@5 on Toys (0.0585 vs. ICLRec's 0.0586, p=0.122), where the two methods are essentially tied.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ELCRec outperforms all 12 baselines on 15 of 16 metric-dataset combinations, with the sole exception being HR@5 on Toys where it is essentially tied with ICLRec (0.0585 vs. 0.0586, p=0.122). The closest competitor is ICLRec [18], the most recent intent-learning method using the EM framework; ELCRec beats it by 6.87–8.90% on HR@5 and NDCG@5 for Sports and Beauty, and by 1.14–2.74% on Yelp. Among self-supervised sequential methods, CL4SRec [108] and S3-Rec [129] are the strongest non-intent baselines, but ELCRec surpasses them by 6–13% on NDCG@5 across datasets. Conventional sequential models (SASRec [39], BERT4Rec [94], GRU4Rec [34], Caser [96]) and the non-sequential BPR-MF [88] are consistently outperformed by large margins (e.g., ELCRec achieves 0.0355 NDCG@5 on Beauty vs. 0.0241 for SASRec). The intent-learning method IORec [49] is also consistently beaten, with ELCRec improving NDCG@5 by 64% on Toys (0.0403 vs. 0.0197). On the additional ML-1M and MIND-small datasets, ELCRec further outperforms ICLRec by 11.83% and 4.33% on NDCG@5 respectively, confirming cross-domain generalizability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repositions intent learning for sequential recommendation by replacing the dominant EM (alternating clustering + representation learning) paradigm with a unified end-to-end framework. Conceptually, it draws from three threads: (1) intent learning methods like ICLRec and IORec that discover latent user intents via clustering, (2) contrastive self-supervised learning for sequential recommendation (S3-Rec, CL4SRec), and (3) differentiable/online clustering where cluster assignments are learned jointly with representations. The key architectural departure is initializing cluster centers as learnable neural parameters and using them simultaneously as clustering targets and self-supervision signals for contrastive learning, eliminating the need for a separate clustering pass over the full dataset.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in making intent learning scalable to industrial-scale systems (validated on 130M page views at Alipay) by reducing clustering from a full-data O(|U|kd) operation to a mini-batch O(bkd) one, while also simplifying deployment by removing alternating optimization. However, several open questions remain: the cluster number k must be pre-specified, the update rate of learnable cluster centers is difficult to control (risking drift or instability), and the inconsistent ablation on Toys (where adding ICL to B+ELCM degrades performance) suggests the interaction between the two modules is not yet fully understood. Broader applicability beyond e-commerce and live-streaming domains also remains to be established.
