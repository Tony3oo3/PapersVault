## Problem

Model selection for Gaussian processes scales as O(n³) in time and O(n²) in memory, making it intractable for large datasets. While scalable approximations like SVGP and SGPR reduce this cost, they introduce approximation errors that bias hyperparameter selection—SVGP, for instance, overestimates observation noise and produces oversmoothed posteriors. Computation-aware GPs (CaGP) provably avoid overconfidence by guaranteeing posterior variance is always at least the exact GP variance, but previously lacked both a model selection procedure and linear-time inference, leaving them impractical for large-scale use.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a tabular training set (X ∈ R^{n×d}, y ∈ R^n) and produces posterior predictive mean and variance at test points, optimized kernel hyperparameters (output scale, per-dimension lengthscales, observation noise), and a learned sparse action matrix S ∈ R^{n×i}. The actions have a block-diagonal structure with i blocks of size k = n/i, so the total number of trainable action parameters equals n, keeping the optimization problem tractable. The method is limited to GP regression with a conjugate Gaussian likelihood and a Matérn(3/2) kernel.

## Method

The paper introduces two key technical contributions. First, a novel ELBO training loss (Eq. 11) that penalizes the full data y rather than only the i-dimensional projected data, avoiding the poor generalization of a naive projected-data NLL; the intractable terms cancel between the evidence and KL components, yielding an objective with the same O(ni) cost as CaGP's predictive distribution. Second, a sparse block-diagonal structure on the action matrix S (Eq. 14) that reduces posterior inference and model selection to linear time O(ni·max(i,k)) in the number of training points while remaining amenable to GPU acceleration. The actions are optimized end-to-end alongside kernel hyperparameters θ via joint gradient descent, so the data projection adapts to the current hyperparameter setting rather than being fixed a priori. Theoretically, the CaGP posterior variance is guaranteed to be monotonically decreasing in the number of actions and always bounded below by the exact GP variance, ensuring the method never becomes overconfident regardless of the computational budget.

## Results

On the largest dataset (Power, n=1.8M), CaGP-Opt achieves a test NLL of -2.103 and RMSE of 0.030, matching SVGP (-2.104, 0.029) while using only i=512 actions versus SVGP's m=1024 inducing points. On the 391K-point Road dataset, CaGP-Opt dramatically outperforms SVGP with NLL -0.291 vs. 0.149 and RMSE 0.159 vs. 0.277. On Protein (41K points), CaGP-CG achieves the best NLL (0.820) among all approximate methods, beating SVGP (0.851) by 3.6%. Across all six UCI benchmarks, CaGP-Opt consistently ranks first or second in NLL and RMSE, with wall-clock runtimes comparable to or faster than SVGP.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SVGP (the primary state-of-the-art baseline), CaGP-Opt wins decisively on Road (NLL -0.291 vs. 0.149; RMSE 0.159 vs. 0.277) and matches it on Power (NLL -2.103 vs. -2.104) while using half the rank (i=512 vs. m=1024). On Protein, CaGP-CG achieves the best NLL (0.820) among all approximate methods, outperforming SVGP (0.851) by 3.6%, though CGGP achieves the best RMSE (0.517 vs. 0.542). Against SGPR, CaGP-Opt wins on Parkinsons (NLL -3.449 vs. -3.245) and KEGG (NLL -0.694 vs. -0.712 is a narrow loss, but CaGP-Opt is faster at 22m vs. 9m), while SGPR (LBFGS) retains the edge on Bike (NLL -3.017 vs. -2.438). Against CGGP, CaGP-CG consistently improves NLL (e.g., -2.936 vs. -2.663 on Parkinsons; 0.820 vs. 0.853 on Protein) at lower computational cost, since CaGP-CG recovers CGGP's posterior mean but uses a different, more informative training objective. The paper also shows that SVGP systematically overestimates observation noise and lengthscales, leading to oversmoothing, whereas CaGP-Opt interprets more data as signal, explaining its superior RMSE.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the computation-aware Gaussian process (CaGP) framework of Wenger et al. (NeurIPS 2022), which provided theoretical guarantees that approximate posterior variance never underestimates the exact GP variance, by solving two open problems: linear-time inference and model selection. The key methodological departure from prior scalable GP methods (SVGP, SGPR, CGGP) is the use of a sparse block-structured action matrix optimized end-to-end with hyperparameters, combined with a novel ELBO training loss that penalizes the full data rather than just the projected data. The work sits at the intersection of probabilistic numerics (quantifying approximation error as computational uncertainty) and variational GP inference, but unlike SVGP/SGPR, it provably avoids the overconfidence pathology that biases hyperparameter selection toward oversmoothing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work makes GP model selection practical at scale (1.8M points in hours on a single GPU) while preserving calibrated uncertainty quantification, which is critical for downstream decision-making tasks like Bayesian optimization. The finding that a method with guaranteed conservative (never underconfident) variance outperforms SVGP in both NLL and coverage challenges the implicit assumption that tighter (potentially overconfident) posteriors are preferable. Key limitations include restriction to conjugate Gaussian likelihoods (no classification), inability to use stochastic minibatching (requiring more GPU memory than SVGP for very large datasets), and the need to tune the number of actions. Future work on non-conjugate models and task-aware action selection could broaden applicability significantly.
