## Problem

Existing parameter-efficient finetuning (PEFT) methods face three underaddressed challenges beyond parameter count: (1) serving heterogeneous requests (each needing a different adapter) in a single batch incurs substantial batch matrix multiplication overhead, (2) LLM interpretability remains difficult at billion-parameter scale, and (3) composing adapters trained for different tasks into a single multitasking model is non-trivial. The paper asks whether a single, extremely lightweight adaptation mechanism can simultaneously address all three.

## Input / Output

RoAd operates on the output vectors of linear layers within a frozen LLM (RoBERTa, LLaMA, or LLaVA's LLM backbone), rotating pairs of adjacent dimensions in the hidden representation. For text tasks it accepts standard text prompts and produces task predictions (classification, correlation) or generated completions; for the multimodal LLaVA experiment it accepts image + text prompts. A key operational detail is that during batched inference with heterogeneous adapters, RoAd replaces batch matrix multiplication with element-wise multiplication of two saved vectors (cosine and sine components), making per-request adapter switching nearly free.

## Method

Pilot studies show that finetuning primarily alters the angular (directional) component of representations rather than their magnitude, motivating a rotation-based adaptation. RoAd applies a block-diagonal 2D rotation matrix R to the output h of each linear layer, where each 2×2 block R_i = [[cos θ_i, −sin θ_i],[sin θ_i, cos θ_i]] rotates dimensions 2i−1 and 2i; a relaxation variant adds per-block scaling factors α_i to also adjust magnitude. Three variants (RoAd₁, RoAd₂, RoAd₄) trade off parameter count (d₂, 2d₂, 4d₂ trainable parameters per layer) by sharing or unsharing θ and α across the four entries of each block. Because R is sparse and structured, the rotation z = Rh can be rewritten as z = R¹⊗h + R²⊗ĥ (element-wise products with a rearranged h), eliminating BMM during batched serving. RoAd is also expressible as a distributed interchange intervention (DII), enabling orthogonal subspace composition: different halves of R can be trained on different tasks and combined to yield emergent multitasking behavior without further adaptation.

## Results

On the GLUE benchmark with RoBERTa-large, RoAd₁(fc1) achieves 88.8 average score with only 0.03% trainable parameters, matching or exceeding full finetuning (88.6) and outperforming all other PEFT methods. On eight commonsense reasoning tasks with LLaMA-13B, RoAd₂ achieves 83.8 average accuracy with 0.03% parameters, surpassing LoReFT (83.3) and LoRA (80.5). On four arithmetic reasoning tasks with LLaMA-13B, RoAd₄ reaches 52.3 average accuracy, outperforming LoRA (51.1) and LoReFT (49.6). On AlpacaEval2.0 with LLaMA2-7B, RoAd₁ achieves a 62.64% win rate with just 0.02% trainable parameters, beating LoRA (61.55% at 0.83% params).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RoAd consistently outperforms all 11 baselines on the GLUE benchmark: RoAd₁ matches full finetuning (85.6 vs 85.6 on RoBERTa-base; 88.7 vs 88.6 on RoBERTa-large) while using <0.1% parameters, beating Adapter (85.0/88.0), LoRA (84.7/88.1), BOFT (82.8), OFT (82.3), BitFit (82.3), (IA)³ (84.2), RED (84.3), and LoReFT (84.2/88.2). On commonsense reasoning with LLaMA-13B, RoAd₂ (83.8) and RoAd₄ (83.7) surpass LoReFT (83.3), DoRA (81.5), LoRA (80.5), and AdapterP (81.5), with a particularly large margin over Prefix-tuning (68.4). On arithmetic reasoning with LLaMA-13B, all RoAd variants (51.9–52.3) beat LoRA (51.1), LoReFT (49.6), and AdapterP (50.2), though on LLaMA-7B LoRA (46.9) still edges out RoAd₂ (46.2). On AlpacaEval2.0, RoAd₁ (62.64% win rate) outperforms LoRA (61.55%) and LoReFT (60.21%) with 40× fewer parameters than LoRA. On LLaVA visual instruction tuning, RoAd₁+LoRA matches LoRA's 68.5 average with only 1.19% vs 4.61% trainable parameters, demonstrating strong scalability when combined.

## Relation to Prior Work

RoAd is explicitly positioned as a special case of OFT (Qiu et al., NeurIPS 2023) with block size w=2, inheriting the orthogonal-finetuning lineage but eliminating the need for Cayley parameterization and matrix inversions by exploiting the inherent orthogonality of 2D rotation matrices. The rotation mechanism draws directly from RoPE (Su et al., 2024), repurposing its 2D rotation structure with trainable angles and no positional encoding. On the interpretability side, RoAd integrates into the Distributed Interchange Intervention (DII) framework (Geiger et al., 2024), joining a growing family of representation-editing PEFT methods (RED, LoReFT) that can be analyzed through causal intervention. The key departure from OFT/BOFT is the reformulation of the rotation as element-wise multiplication, which sidesteps the batched matrix multiplication bottleneck that limits heterogeneous adapter serving.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for deployment: achieving 2× throughput over LoRA for heterogeneous batch serving with <0.1% trainable parameters addresses a real bottleneck in multi-tenant LLM serving. The pilot study's finding that finetuning predominantly alters angular components rather than magnitudes provides a clean mechanistic explanation for why rotation-based adaptation works, potentially guiding future PEFT design. However, the scalability limitation (bounded number of θ_i and α_i parameters) means RoAd cannot serve as a universal PEFT replacement; the paper acknowledges this by combining RoAd with LoRA for knowledge-intensive tasks like visual instruction tuning. Open questions include whether the angular-dominance finding holds for very large models or non-English tasks, and whether the composability demonstrated qualitatively (German/English subspace merging) generalizes to more complex multi-task combinations.
