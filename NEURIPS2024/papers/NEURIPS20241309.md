## Problem

Given a dataset labeled only with relative distance comparisons (e.g., 'x is closer to y than to z'), what is the smallest dimension d of an ℓ_p-space in which the data can be faithfully represented? Prior work either assumed access to all Θ(n³) pairwise comparisons or provided only lower bounds for the dense ℓ_2 case, leaving the general relationship between the number of constraints m, the number of points n, and the required embedding dimension d completely unresolved.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241309/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes either (a) m triplet comparisons (x_i, y_i^+, z_i^-) asserting that y_i is closer to anchor x_i than negative z_i, or (b) n points each with an ordered list of k nearest neighbors. The output is a concrete embedding F: V → R^d in a specified ℓ_p-space that preserves the ordinal distance relationships, along with tight theoretical bounds on the minimum required d. Notably, the input contains no actual distance values—only relative orderings—and the embedding must satisfy strict inequalities (not approximate ones) for all given constraints.

## Method

The central insight is that the embedding dimension is governed by the arboricity r of the constraint graph (the minimum number of forests partitioning its edges), not by the raw number of edges m. For ℓ_2, the embedding is split into two 2r-dimensional parts: one encodes pairwise inner products by solving linear systems in an arboricity ordering (where each vertex has at most 2r-1 preceding neighbors), and the other equalizes norms via a proper graph coloring onto orthogonal basis vectors. For general ℓ_p, a Neighbor-Collection Coloring (NCC) scheme with K = Θ(r log n) random colorings encodes distances bit-by-bit in binary, yielding O(r² log³ n) dimensions. For full k-NN preservation (not just ordering), a random design-based component using (α,R)-designs separates neighbors from non-neighbors with high probability, giving the final O(k¹⁰ log¹⁰ n) bound. For ℓ_∞, a hybrid approach combines distance-2 edge coloring on low-degree vertices with a Fréchet-style embedding for high-degree vertices, achieving O(m^{2/3}) dimensions.

## Results

The paper establishes tight theoretical bounds on the minimum embedding dimension for contrastive learning and k-NN. For contrastive learning in ℓ₂, the dimension is Θ(√m) (tight); in ℓ_∞ it is O(m^{2/3}) sufficient with Õ(√m) necessary; and in ℓ_p (p≥1) it is O(m) sufficient with Õ(√m) necessary. For k-NN ordering in ℓ₂, the dimension is Θ(k) (tight); in ℓ_p it is Õ(k²) sufficient with Õ(k) necessary. Empirically on CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100, when d ≥ √m the fraction of satisfied triplets is ≥99%, while for m=10⁵–10⁶ with d < √m accuracy drops sharply to ~93%, and accuracy plateaus around d ≈ √m ≈ 316 for m=10⁵.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is primarily a theoretical paper, so comparisons are against prior theoretical bounds rather than competing methods. The closest prior work is Chatziafratis & Indyk (SOSA 2024), which established an Ω(√m) lower bound for ℓ₂ contrastive embeddings under dense-instance assumptions (m = Θ(n³)) and an Ω(k) lower bound for k-NN ordering. This paper provides matching upper bounds (O(√m) for ℓ₂ contrastive, O(k) for ℓ₂ k-NN ordering) without density assumptions, closing the gap. The empirical experiments on CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100 validate the theory: training a ResNet-18 with triplet loss achieves ≥99% triplet satisfaction when d ≥ √m, and accuracy plateaus at d ≈ √m, confirming the tightness of the theoretical bound. No direct method-to-method comparison is performed, as the contribution is establishing the first complete set of upper and lower bounds for these embedding dimension questions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the ordinal (non-metric) embedding literature, which previously assumed access to all Θ(n⁴) pairwise distance comparisons, to the more practical setting where only m specific constraints are given. It builds directly on [CI24], which provided Ω(n) lower bounds for dense contrastive instances and Ω(k) lower bounds for k-NN ordering in ℓ₂, by delivering matching upper bounds and extending to all ℓ_p norms. The key technical novelty is the use of graph arboricity (from Nash-Williams' forest decomposition) as the structural parameter governing embedding dimension, complementing [AAE+24]'s sample-complexity analysis which assumed a fixed dimension. For the k-NN construction, the paper repurposes (α,R)-designs from [DKS12] and the Fréchet embedding for the ℓ_∞ case.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to give tight (or near-tight) bounds on the minimum embedding dimension for contrastive learning and k-NN preservation, directly informing the choice of embedding layer size in deep learning architectures. The arboricity-based analysis is more fine-grained than edge-count bounds and captures dataset structure, though the authors note it may not capture all relevant properties for sharper data-dependent bounds. Open questions include improving the k-NN upper bound (reducing the k¹⁰ and log n dependence), extending to approximate settings where only a fraction of constraints need to be satisfied, and exploring bi-criteria algorithms. The experiments on CIFAR-10/100 with ResNet-18 validate the √m threshold empirically, showing accuracy plateaus at d ≈ √m.
