## Problem

Adversarial low-rank MDPs with unknown transitions had no provably efficient algorithms when combined with bandit feedback, and the only prior full-information result (Zhao et al., 2024) achieved a suboptimal T^{5/6} regret. The paper addresses both gaps: improving the full-information regret to T^{2/3} and initiating the study of the bandit-feedback setting, where the learner only observes losses at visited state-action pairs and must simultaneously learn the transition representation.

## Input / Output

The algorithms consume state observations x_h, a finite action space A, a feature class Φ (and optionally a second class Υ in the model-based setting), and either full-information or bandit loss feedback over T episodes of horizon H. A key structural constraint is that under bandit feedback, the loss must satisfy a linear representation assumption (ℓ_h(x,a) = φ_h(x,a)ᵀ g_h) to avoid regret scaling polynomially with the number of states; without this, a lower bound of Ω(√(|X||A|T)) applies. Outputs include per-episode policies π^t, estimated transitions P̂ (model-based) or policy covers Ψ (model-free), and Q-function estimates Q̂ used for policy optimization.

## Method

The core insight is a two-phase separation of representation learning from policy optimization, which avoids the exploration-exploitation conflict in prior simultaneous approaches. For full-information feedback, Algorithm 1 first runs reward-free exploration (Cheng et al., 2023) to estimate the transition, then applies exponential weights over policies using the estimated Q-function as the loss, achieving T^{2/3} regret. For bandit feedback, the key technical contribution is a novel off-policy loss estimator that exploits the low-rank structure: the occupancy d_h^π(x) decomposes as φ_{h-1}^*(π)ᵀ μ_h^*(x), enabling an unbiased value estimator of the form φ_{h-1}(π)ᵀ(Σ_{h-1})^{-1}φ_{h-1}(π^t) · (importance-sampled loss), where Σ is a covariance matrix over policy features. Model-free variants (Algorithms 3–5) replace the transition estimate with a VoX policy cover and epoch-based commitment to mixtures of exploratory and exploitation policies; the adaptive-adversary variant (Algorithm 4) further uses spanner policies and a known loss feature to handle history-dependent losses.

## Results

For full-information feedback with unknown transitions, Algorithm 1 achieves Õ(H³(d²+|A|)T^{2/3}) regret, improving the prior best bound of O(T^{5/6}) by Zhao et al. (2024). For bandit feedback with linear losses, the paper provides both computationally inefficient algorithms (Algorithm 2, Algorithm 5) achieving Õ(d²H³|A|(d²+|A|)T^{2/3}) regret, and oracle-efficient model-free algorithms (Algorithm 3, Algorithm 4) achieving poly(A,d,H)·T^{4/5} regret. A lower bound of Ω(√(|X||A|T)) is shown for bandit feedback with unstructured losses, demonstrating that the linear loss assumption is necessary to avoid polynomial dependence on the number of states.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole prior work in adversarial low-rank MDPs is Zhao et al. (2024), which achieves O(T^{5/6}) regret under full-information feedback with unknown transitions. Algorithm 1 in this paper improves that bound to O(T^{2/3}) by separating representation learning (via reward-free exploration) from policy optimization into two distinct phases, rather than performing them simultaneously as in Zhao et al. (2024). More significantly, this paper is the first to address the bandit feedback setting for adversarial low-rank MDPs, a problem entirely unaddressed by Zhao et al. (2024). The T^{4/5} regret of the oracle-efficient model-free algorithms (Algorithms 3 and 4) actually outperforms Zhao et al.'s T^{5/6} bound despite operating under the strictly harder bandit feedback regime. The paper also establishes that without linear structure on the loss, the regret necessarily scales as Ω(√(|X||A|T)), in contrast to the full-information case where the lower bound is only Ω(√(|A|T)) independent of |X|.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of adversarial MDP learning and low-rank MDP representation learning. It directly extends Zhao et al. (2024), who studied adversarial low-rank MDPs with full-information feedback and unknown transitions, by (i) improving their T^{5/6} regret to T^{2/3} via a two-phase design that separates representation learning from policy optimization, and (ii) initiating the study of the bandit-feedback variant, which had not been addressed for low-rank MDPs. The model-free algorithms build on VoX (Mhammedi et al., 2023) for policy-cover exploration and RepLearn (Modi et al., 2024) for representation learning, while the model-based algorithms leverage the reward-free exploration of Cheng et al. (2023) and the off-policy evaluation ideas from Liu et al. (2023) for adversarial linear MDPs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first provably sublinear-regret result for the combination of feature learning, bandit feedback, and adversarial losses in low-rank MDPs—a setting that mirrors practical RL where all three elements co-occur. A key conceptual contribution is the lower bound showing that without linear structure on the loss, regret must scale polynomially with the number of states under bandit feedback, in contrast to the full-information case; this clarifies when low-rank structure actually helps. The optimal √T regret remains out of reach due to the inherent two-phase (explore-then-exploit) design, and the authors identify on-the-fly representation learning as the key direction for closing this gap. The algorithms are also computationally inefficient in several variants, limiting immediate practical adoption.
