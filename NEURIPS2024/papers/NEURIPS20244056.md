## Problem

Existing 3D pose estimation methods parameterize rotations in the spatial domain (Euler angles, quaternions, axis-angle), which introduces discontinuities, singularities, and gimbal lock. These spatial parameterizations are fundamentally incompatible with SO(3)-equivariant spherical CNNs, which operate in the frequency domain for computational efficiency, creating a mismatch between the network's internal representation and the output space. The paper asks whether 3D rotations can be regressed directly in the frequency domain as Wigner-D coefficients, aligning the output representation with the equivariant architecture.

## Input / Output

The method takes a 224×224 RGB image and produces a 455-dimensional vector of Wigner-D matrix coefficients (truncated at frequency level L=6, where M=Σ(2l+1)²=455). At inference, this frequency-domain vector is converted to a 3×3 rotation matrix by computing similarities against a 2.36M-point SO(3) HEALPix grid (1.875° precision) and taking the argmax of the resulting non-parametric distribution. Unlike prior equivariant methods that output discretized probability distributions over SO(3) bins, this method outputs a continuous regression target, with discretization only at inference time.

## Method

The architecture consists of four stages: (1) a pre-trained ResNet extracts 2048-dim features reduced to 512 channels via 1×1 convolution; (2) a spherical mapper orthographically projects the 2D feature map onto an S² HEALPix hemisphere (recursion level 2, 20 sample points); (3) a spherical FFT converts the spatial signal to spherical harmonic coefficients; and (4) an S²-equivariant convolution with global support followed by an SO(3)-equivariant group convolution with local support (handling rotations up to 22.5°) produces the final Wigner-D coefficients. The key insight is that by regressing Wigner-D coefficients directly, the output naturally lives in the same frequency domain as the spherical CNN's internal operations, eliminating the need for spatial-domain conversion during training. Training uses a frequency-domain MSE loss with per-frequency-level weights w_l to normalize across harmonic degrees, chosen over cosine, geodesic, or L1 losses because it preserves both direction and magnitude of frequency components. The SO(3)-equivariance of the network ensures that rotating the input image produces a predictably rotated output, improving data efficiency and generalization to unseen rotations.

## Results

On ModelNet10-SO(3), the proposed Wigner-D harmonics predictor with ResNet-101 achieves 77.3% Acc@15°, 78.0% Acc@30°, and 11.9° median rotation error, outperforming all baselines. On PASCAL3D+, the method reaches 89.2% Acc@30° and 8.6° median error, setting a new state of the art. In few-shot experiments (3–100 training views per CAD model), the method consistently achieves the highest accuracy and lowest error across all view counts, demonstrating superior data efficiency from SO(3)-equivariance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest equivariant baseline, Liu et al. (Uni) [44], our ResNet-50 model is marginally below on Acc@15° (0.759 vs. 0.760) and median error (15.1° vs. 14.6°), but our ResNet-101 model surpasses it on all three metrics (0.773 vs. 0.744 Acc@15°, 0.780 vs. 0.774 Acc@30°, 11.9° vs. 12.2° median error). Compared to the Laplace-distribution method of Yin et al. [75], our ResNet-50 improves Acc@15° by 1.7 points (0.759 vs. 0.742) and our ResNet-101 improves median error by 0.8° (11.9° vs. 12.7°). Against the closest equivariant non-parametric baseline Klee et al. (I2S) [35], our method gains 3.1 points on Acc@15° (0.759 vs. 0.728) and reduces median error by 0.6° (15.1° vs. 15.7°) with ResNet-50. On PASCAL3D+, our method (89.2% Acc@30°, 8.6° median) outperforms the previous best Klee et al. [35] (87.2%, 9.8°) by 2.0 points in accuracy and 1.2° in error, and beats Howell et al. [28] (9.2°) by 0.6° in median error. In the few-shot setting, our method maintains a consistent 2–5 point advantage in Acc@30° over I2S [35] and RotLaplace [75] at every training-view count from 3 to 100, confirming that SO(3)-equivariance provides a structural data-efficiency benefit rather than merely a large-data advantage.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the equivariant pose estimation line initiated by Klee et al. (I2S, ICLR 2023) and Howell et al. (NeurIPS 2023), which both use spherical CNNs to project image features onto S² and predict non-parametric distributions over SO(3) via discretized HEALPix grids. The key departure is replacing the spatial-domain or discretized output with direct regression of Wigner-D matrix coefficients in the frequency domain, aligning the network's output representation with the natural operating space of spherical CNNs. This eliminates the need for cross-entropy or distributional losses over discretized rotations and instead uses a simple frequency-domain MSE loss, while retaining the SO(3)-equivariant architecture from Cohen et al.'s Spherical CNNs (ICLR 2018).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The frequency-domain Wigner-D formulation provides a principled resolution to the long-standing tension between spatial rotation parameterizations (which suffer from discontinuities, gimbal lock, and double-cover issues) and the frequency-domain operations of equivariant spherical networks. The demonstrated data efficiency (consistent superiority across 3–100 training views) validates that equivariance plus representation alignment yields meaningful sample savings. A notable limitation is that for symmetric objects (SYMSOL), the regression loss alone produces overly sharp distributions that underperform distributional baselines, requiring joint training with cross-entropy to recover multi-modal pose uncertainty. Open questions include whether frequency-domain rotation representations generalize beyond single-object pose to multi-object or 6-DoF settings, and whether the computational cost of spherical convolutions at high bandlimits limits practical deployment.
