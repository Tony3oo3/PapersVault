## Problem

The Maximum Mutual Information (MMI) criterion, the standard objective for rationale extraction in rationalization, is vulnerable to spurious features that are statistically correlated with the label but not causally responsible for it. Existing penalty-based remedies (invariance, intervention, sufficiency/necessity) still distinguish spurious features from plain noise in the loss landscape, requiring delicate hyperparameter tuning and leaving the extractor susceptible to local optima. The paper asks whether MMI can be replaced entirely by a criterion that treats spurious features as equivalent to plain noise, eliminating the need for auxiliary regularizers.

## Input / Output

The method takes a token-level text sequence X (max length 256) and a binary task label Y. The extractor produces a binary mask M via gumbel-softmax sampling, yielding a rationale candidate Z (a sparse subset of tokens at a pre-defined sparsity of ~10%, 20%, or 30%) and its complement X−Z. The predictor consumes both X and X−Z to produce two class distributions, which are then compared via KL divergence to drive rationale selection. The final outputs are the extracted rationale (token subset) and the predicted class label.

## Method

The core insight is a reversed perspective: instead of maximizing I(Y;Z) over the selected rationale, the paper maximizes the Remaining Discrepancy (MRD), defined as D_KL(P(Y|X) ‖ P(Y|X−Z)), over the unselected remainder. Theoretically, d-separation guarantees that removing either plain noise or spurious features leaves P(Y|X−Z) unchanged relative to P(Y|X), while removing causal features causes a significant shift—so the MRD objective naturally assigns equal loss to noise and spurious selections, guiding the extractor solely toward causal features without any penalty term. In practice, both conditional distributions are approximated by a shared predictor: P(Ŷ_X|X) is trained to approximate P(Y|X) and P(Ŷ_{−Z}|X−Z) to approximate P(Y|X−Z) via cross-entropy minimization. The extractor is then updated to maximize the KL divergence between these two approximated distributions, augmented with standard sparsity and coherence constraints, while the predictor is updated to minimize both cross-entropies in an alternating fashion.

## Results

On the six standard rationalization benchmarks (Beer-Appearance, Beer-Aroma, Beer-Palate, Hotel-Location, Hotel-Service, Hotel-Cleanliness), MRD achieves the highest F1 score (overlap with human-annotated rationales) across nearly all sparsity levels (10%, 20%, 30%). The headline improvement is a 10.4% F1 gain over the strongest baseline MCD (63.5% vs. 53.1% on Beer-Palate at S≈10%). With a BERT encoder, MRD improves F1 by 15.6% on Beer-Appearance (54.6% vs. 39.0% for CR) and 6.0% on Beer-Aroma (55.1% vs. 49.1%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MRD consistently outperforms all six baselines across the 18 dataset-sparsity settings. Against the strongest baseline MCD (Liu et al., 2023b), MRD achieves up to 10.4% higher F1 (63.5% vs. 53.1% on Beer-Palate at S≈10%) and wins in the majority of settings, with the gap most pronounced at lower sparsity levels where spurious correlations are hardest to avoid. Compared to the penalty-based Inter_RAT (Yue et al., 2023), MRD improves F1 by more than 10% in 14 out of 18 settings and by more than 20% in 2 settings, confirming the paper's argument that penalty terms struggle to coordinate with MMI. INVRAT (Chang et al., 2020) performs poorly across the board (e.g., F1 of 36.2 on Beer-Appearance at S≈10%), consistent with the known limitations of IRM-based environment partitioning. NIR (Storek et al., 2023) is competitive at higher sparsity but lags MRD by 1–4 F1 points at S≈10%. In the BERT-based comparison (Table 4), MRD surpasses CR (Zhang et al., 2023) by 15.6% F1 on Beer-Appearance and 6.0% on Beer-Aroma, and also outperforms VIB and A2R.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the rationalization framework initiated by RNP (Lei et al., 2016) and builds directly on the d-separation-based causal analysis from MCD (Liu et al., 2023b), borrowing its probabilistic graphical model and the technique of approximating true distributions with a predictor's output. However, it departs fundamentally from all prior penalty-based approaches (INVRAT, Inter_RAT, CR) that attempt to fix MMI by adding supplementary regularizers, and from MCD itself, which revises MMI to minimum conditional dependence but still cannot equate spurious features with noise. The key conceptual shift is reversing the optimization perspective: instead of asking what the selected rationale Z should contain (maximizing I(Y;Z)), MRD asks what the remaining input X-Z should look like, exploiting the d-separation property that removing non-causal features leaves P(Y|X-Z) unchanged.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper challenges a foundational assumption in the rationalization literature—that the MMI criterion is necessary for rationale extraction—by showing it can be entirely replaced with a criterion that inherently neutralizes spurious correlations without any penalty tuning. This simplifies the loss landscape and removes the delicate hyperparameter coordination between MMI and penalty terms that plagues existing methods. Limitations include the restriction to text classification (no LLMs, vision, or graph data yet), and the practical gains, while consistent, are moderate (up to 10.4% F1 over MCD, with smaller margins at higher sparsity). Open questions include whether MRD extends to the LLM fine-tuning setting the authors mention, and whether the shared-predictor approximation introduces biases in more complex architectures.
