## Problem

Online sample selection methods that control the false selection rate (FSR) treat each decision independently and cannot capture pairwise relationships among selected samples, such as diversity or similarity constraints. Existing online multiple testing frameworks (e.g., α-investing, LOND, SAFFRON) address only individual error-rate control and suffer from the 'alpha-death' issue where the procedure halts prematurely. This paper asks how to simultaneously enforce both individual constraints (e.g., FSR, expected cost) and interactive constraints (e.g., expected similarity among correctly selected samples) in a sequential, real-time selection setting with statistical guarantees.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a historical labeled dataset D (split into a training set for fitting a predictive model μ̂ and a calibration set for kernel density estimation of the lFDR), a stream of unlabeled covariate vectors X_t arriving sequentially, a user-specified target region A for the response, cost functions G_0 and G_1, a pairwise similarity function g (e.g., weighted RBF kernel), and target levels α and K. At each time step it outputs a binary decision δ_t ∈ {0,1}, and the final output is the selection set {X_i : δ_i = 1} up to a user-chosen stopping time T (e.g., selecting m samples or exhausting a budget). The calibration set size n_cal governs the estimation accuracy of the lFDR, and the method remains effective even with n_cal as small as 200.

## Method

The II-COS procedure operates in two phases: an offline estimation phase where a predictive model μ̂ is fit on the training set and the local FDR L_t = Pr(θ_t=0 | W_t) is estimated via kernel density estimation on the calibration set, and an online decision phase where each arriving sample is selected only if both the individual constraint (cumulative expected cost V_t/R_t ≤ α) and the interactive constraint (cumulative expected similarity TS_t/NS_t ≤ K) are satisfied. The key insight is that the lFDR provides a valid per-sample uncertainty measure that, when accumulated sequentially, exactly controls the individual constraint in the oracle case, while the pairwise similarity terms weighted by (1−L_i)(1−L_j) yield a tractable interactive constraint. The data-driven version replaces true L_t with kernel-density-based estimates, and the paper proves that both constraints are controlled asymptotically as the calibration size grows, with explicit finite-sample bounds depending on the Hölder smoothness of the underlying densities. The framework is model-agnostic (works with random forests, SVMs, neural networks) and reduces to the SAST rule when K = +∞, making it a strict generalization of prior online FDR control methods.

## Results

On the Candidate dataset (target FSR α=0.2, ES K=1×10⁻³), II-COS achieves FSR=0.19 and ES=0.98×10⁻³, the only method satisfying both constraints simultaneously, while SAST (ES=8.73×10⁻³) and CP (ES=10.34×10⁻³) violate the ES target by ~9×. On the Income dataset (target FSR α=0.2, ES K=6×10⁻³), II-COS achieves FSR=0.16 and ES=5.56×10⁻³, whereas CP inflates FSR to 0.42 and SAST's ES reaches 30.90×10⁻³ (5× over target). On synthetic classification data, II-COS and SAST both maintain FSR near the nominal 0.1 level, but only II-COS keeps ES under K=0.045; conformal p-value methods (LOND, SAFFRON, ADDIS) suffer from alpha-death and select far fewer than the target m=100 samples.

## Baselines & Comparisons

II-COS is the only method that simultaneously controls both the individual constraint (FSR) and the interactive constraint (ES) on both real datasets. SAST [17] matches II-COS on FSR (0.19 vs 0.19 on Candidate) but fails dramatically on ES (8.73×10⁻³ vs target 1×10⁻³, a ~9× violation), because it ignores pairwise diversity entirely. The offline conformal p-value method CP [7] achieves a slightly lower FSR on Candidate (0.16) but inflates FSR to 0.42 on Income (more than double the target) and also violates ES. The three online FDR methods—LOND [18], SAFFRON [30], and ADDIS [40]—all suffer from the alpha-death problem: with n_cal=4000 they still select far fewer than m=100 samples (e.g., LOND selects only ~44 on average at n_cal=2000), making them impractical for the stated selection task. On synthetic data, LOND and SAFFRON yield slightly conservative FSR values while ADDIS inflates FSR above the nominal level, but none of them address the interactive constraint at all.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate research threads—predictive inference (lFDR, conformal p-values) and online multiple testing (α-investing, SAST, LOND, SAFFRON, ADDIS)—into a single sequential selection framework. It explicitly builds on SAST (Gang et al. 2023), which the authors show is a special case of their II-COS when the interactive constraint is set to infinity, and on the lFDR oracle model (Efron et al. 2001) for quantifying per-sample selection uncertainty. The key conceptual departure from prior online selection work is the introduction of interactive (pairwise) constraints—such as expected similarity among selected samples—alongside the standard individual constraint (FSR/generalized cost), a combination that had not been addressed in the online setting. Offline selection methods (Jin & Candès 2023, Wu et al. 2023) handle similar goals but lack the sequential, anytime-valid guarantees that II-COS provides.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first unified framework for online sample selection that simultaneously controls both individual cost (e.g., FSR) and interactive diversity constraints with asymptotic guarantees, addressing a practical gap in recruitment, recommendation, and precision marketing where selecting diverse yet reliable candidates is essential. The near-oracle efficiency (stopping time within ~1% of the oracle) and robustness to small calibration sets (n_cal ≈ 200) make the method practically deployable. Open questions include extending interactive constraints beyond pairwise binary functions (e.g., Gini index), incorporating post-decision feedback, and handling non-stationary covariate distributions more generally. The alpha-death vulnerability of conformal-p-value-based baselines highlights a broader limitation of p-value-centric online testing that may motivate further methodological work.
