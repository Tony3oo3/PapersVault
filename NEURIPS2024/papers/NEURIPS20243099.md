## Problem

Repeated fair division between two players with private valuations was first studied by Aumann and Maschler (1995) in a highly restricted simultaneous setting with specific valuations, leaving the general case largely unexplored. This paper provides the first substantial analysis of both the sequential (cut-and-choose) and simultaneous versions with arbitrary bounded value densities, addressing two central questions: how much can one player exploit a nearly myopic opponent, and whether equitable utility profiles are achievable in the limit.

## Input / Output

Each player holds a private bounded value density function v_i: [0,1] → [δ, Δ] (normalized to integrate to 1), and the game runs for T rounds where Alice selects a cut point a_t ∈ [0,1] and Bob selects L or R. In the sequential setting Bob observes a_t before choosing; in the simultaneous setting he does not. The key quantitative outputs are Stackelberg regret bounds (e.g., O(T^{(α+1)/2} log T) when Bob has O(T^α) regret), the guarantee that both players' average payoffs converge to 1/2, and the O(1/√T) convergence rate of fictitious play to the equitable profile.

## Method

The paper develops three complementary techniques. First, Alice exploits a nearly myopic Bob via a discretized binary search: she partitions the current interval into 6 equal-value sub-intervals, cuts at each interior point for η = ⌈√f(T)·T⌉ rounds to read Bob's majority response, and recurses on a 3/6-width sub-interval, yielding Stackelberg regret O(√(T·f(T))·log T). Second, to enforce equitable payoffs, Alice's strategy adapts Blackwell approachability to an uncountable type space by constructing a countably infinite set of representative Bob valuations and defining a countably-infinite-dimensional inner-product space where the target set S is the region where all representatives have payoff ≤ 1/2; each round she selects a cut that moves the empirical payoff vector toward S. Third, fictitious play is analyzed by tracking two scalar quantities α_t (Bob's L/R imbalance) and β_t (cumulative Bob-value asymmetry of Alice's cuts), which form a counterclockwise spiral in α-β space whose radius ρ_t = |α_t| + |β_t| grows as O(√T), directly yielding the O(1/√T) convergence rate.

## Results

The paper establishes four main theoretical results. First, if Bob has regret O(T^α) in the sequential setting, Alice's Stackelberg regret is O(T^((α+1)/2) log T) when she knows α (sharp), and O(T/log T) when she does not. Second, both players can enforce the equitable utility profile (1/2, 1/2): Alice guarantees her average payoff ≥ 1/2 − Θ(1/√T) while capping Bob at ≤ 1/2 + Θ(1/ln T), and Bob achieves symmetric guarantees in the sequential setting. Third, in the simultaneous setting Bob's guarantee holds only in expectation. Fourth, fictitious play converges to (1/2, 1/2) at rate O(1/√T), matching Karlin's conjectured rate for this class of games.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical baselines or datasets. The closest prior work is Aumann and Maschler (1995), who analyzed the simultaneous setting for a restricted two-type Bob (uniform Alice, Bob with/without a cherry) using Blackwell approachability. The present paper extends this in three key ways: (1) arbitrary bounded value densities for both players rather than a fixed two-type model; (2) a new sequential setting where Bob observes Alice's cut before choosing; and (3) explicit convergence rates (O(1/√T) for fictitious play, O(1/√T) and O(1/ln T) for the equitable payoff guarantees). The paper also positions its exploitation results (Theorem 1) relative to concurrent work on exploiting no-regret learners (Deng et al. 2019; Haghtalab et al. 2022), noting that those techniques do not apply here because the setting involves continuous action spaces and explicit regret bounds rather than discounted payoffs. For fictitious play, the O(1/√T) rate matches the conjecture of Karlin (1959) and the results of Abernethy et al. (2021) for diagonal payoff matrices, but applies to a degenerate 2×∞ game where Bob has non-unique best responses, a case not covered by prior convergence results.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the repeated cake cutting model of Aumann and Maschler (1995), which considered only the simultaneous setting with two discrete Bob types and a restricted Alice (uniform density, two cut points). The authors generalize to arbitrary bounded value densities, introduce the sequential (cut-and-choose) variant, and provide the first full characterization of exploitation, equitable payoffs, and fictitious play convergence in this setting. The exploitation analysis draws on the broader literature on exploiting no-regret learners (Deng et al. 2019; Haghtalab et al. 2022), while the equitable payoff guarantees leverage Blackwell approachability (1956) adapted to an infinite-dimensional space of Bob types.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes that repeated cut-and-choose, despite its intuitive fairness, has a fundamental strategic vulnerability: a nearly myopic chooser can be systematically exploited via binary search, yet equitable outcomes remain enforceable by both players. This resolves a long-standing open question from Aumann and Maschler and provides the first convergence rate (O(1/√T)) for fictitious play in a continuous-action fair division game. Open questions include extending to richer feedback models (turn-based cutting, multiple pieces), settings with both goods and bads, and understanding how randomized learning rules like exponential weights affect the achievable payoff profiles.
