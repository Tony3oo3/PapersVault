## Problem

In programmatic weak supervision, models are trained using noisy weak labels without ground truth, but standard evaluation metrics (accuracy, precision, recall, F1) cannot be computed without access to true labels. Prior work showed that weakly supervised classifiers often implicitly rely on a small set of high-quality labels for validation, undermining the purpose of weak supervision. This paper addresses the gap by providing a principled way to bound these metrics without any ground truth labels.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a dataset of unlabeled features with weak labels {(X_i, Z_i)}, a fitted label model P_{Y|Z} (e.g., from Snorkel or an oracle), and a fixed classifier h. It produces lower and upper Fréchet bounds on any metric expressible as E[g(X,Y,Z)]—specifically accuracy, precision, recall, and F1—along with asymptotic confidence intervals. A key practical detail is that the optimization dimension depends only on |Y|×|Z| (the label and weak-label cardinalities), not on the feature dimension d, making it scalable to high-dimensional text or image features.

## Method

The core idea is to frame metric estimation as a partial identification problem: given only the marginals P_{X,Z} and P_{Y,Z}, the joint P_{X,Y,Z} is not identifiable, but Fréchet bounds on E[g(X,Y,Z)] are. The paper derives a dual formulation (via optimal transport duality) that converts the infinite-dimensional Fréchet bound into a finite-dimensional convex program over a matrix a ∈ R^{|Y|×|Z|}. To handle the non-smooth max/min operators in the dual, the authors apply log-sum-exp smoothing (soft min/max), yielding a smooth convex objective solvable with L-BFGS; the sum-to-zero constraint is enforced via a quadratic penalty. Theoretical contributions include proving asymptotic normality of the bound estimators (enabling confidence intervals) and bounding the error introduced by label model misspecification in total variation distance. The approach is validated on 15 Wrench benchmark datasets and a hate speech dataset, showing that even Snorkel's default label model (without ground truth) produces bounds close to oracle bounds.

## Results

On the Wrench benchmark datasets, the Fréchet bounds-based model selection achieves test accuracy of 0.77–0.81 on agnews, imdb, and yelp, and F1 scores of 0.76 and 0.96 on tennis and commercial, respectively. Notably, on tennis (F1) and commercial (F1), the Fréchet bounds approach outperforms a labeled validation set of size n=100 by 5 and 5 percentage points (0.76 vs. 0.71, 0.96 vs. 0.91). The bounds are valid (contain the true test metric) across all 14 Wrench datasets and the Hate Speech dataset, with the Snorkel label model producing bounds comparable to an oracle that uses true labels to estimate P_{Y|Z}.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is the Snorkel label model approach, which selects models by maximizing the expected accuracy under the fitted P_{Y|Z} (equivalent to assuming X ⊥ Y | Z). The Fréchet bounds-based selection (using lower bound, upper bound, or their average) matches the label model on agnews, imdb, and yelp (all within 0.01), and slightly outperforms it on tennis F1 (0.76 vs. 0.75). More strikingly, when uncertainty is low (tennis, commercial), the Fréchet bounds approach beats a labeled validation set of 100 samples by 5 percentage points on F1, demonstrating that no ground truth labels are needed for competitive model selection. On yelp, the labeled baseline (0.82) marginally edges out all Fréchet-based methods (0.81), suggesting that when the label model is well-specified and uncertainty is moderate, a small labeled set can still provide a slight advantage. Overall, the Fréchet bounds method is most beneficial when the conditional entropy H(Y|Z) or H(X|Z) is small, yielding tight bounds that enable reliable model selection without any labeled data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the Fréchet bounds framework from Ruschendorf and Bartl et al. (originally developed in financial risk and optimal transport) to the problem of evaluating weakly supervised classifiers, where the joint distribution P_{X,Y,Z} is only partially identified through its marginals P_{X,Z} and P_{Y,Z}. The dual formulation is derived from the optimal transport duality of Beiglbock and Schachermayer, and the non-smooth max/min operators are replaced with log-sum-exp smoothings from An et al. to enable efficient convex optimization. Conceptually, the paper departs from the adversarial learning / distributionally robust optimization literature (Mazzetto et al., Arachie & Huang) by focusing on post-hoc evaluation rather than worst-case learning, and it directly addresses the gap identified by Zhu et al. [66] that weak supervision's practical utility is undermined by the need for ground truth labels during validation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work provides the first principled method to bound accuracy, precision, recall, and F1 score of weakly supervised classifiers without any ground truth labels, removing a fundamental bottleneck that has limited the practical adoption of PWS. The empirical finding that Snorkel's default label model yields bounds comparable to an oracle (true-label) estimate, and that Fréchet-bound-based model selection can outperform a 100-sample labeled validation set on certain datasets, is practically significant. Key limitations include the requirement that Y and Z be finite (restricting to classification), potential wide bounds when weak labels are uninformative, and sensitivity to label model misspecification (though Theorem 5.1 provides a linear error bound in TV distance). Open questions include extending to continuous label spaces, end-to-end weak supervision methods, and applications to statistical matching.
