## Problem

Test-time adaptation (TTA) has never been demonstrated on microcontroller units (MCUs) due to three compounding barriers: full-network backpropagation causes memory exhaustion, normalization layers required by most TTA methods are unsupported on MCU hardware, and effective adaptation typically demands large batch sizes that exceed the single-sample memory budget of constrained devices. This paper is the first to enable TTA on an MCU (STM32H747, 512 KB SRAM) while maintaining competitive accuracy.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a single unlabeled test sample (32×32 RGB image or audio waveform) at a time with batch size 1, which is the practical constraint on MCUs. It produces a class prediction and, simultaneously, updates only the lightweight early-exit heads (a WS layer plus one linear layer) on-device via entropy minimization, while all backbone submodules remain frozen. The entire pipeline—including the backpropagation engine—must fit within 512 KB SRAM and 1 MB Flash on the target MCU.

## Method

TinyTTA partitions a pre-trained CNN into K submodules grouped by similar activation memory footprint (self-ensemble), attaches a WS-normalized linear classifier (early-exit head) to each, and trains them jointly offline with a cross-entropy loss plus an L1 alignment loss to the full model. At test time, each sample cascades through submodules sequentially; the entropy of the current exit's prediction is compared against a per-submodule threshold, and the sample exits as soon as confidence is sufficient, so easy samples (minor distribution shift) traverse fewer layers while hard samples (severe shift) may pass through the entire network. Only the exit head at the chosen exit is updated via entropy minimization, eliminating backpropagation through the frozen backbone and thus removing activation memory. Weight Standardization replaces BN/GN in the exit classifiers, providing batch-agnostic normalization computed solely from weights—no batch statistics are accumulated, making the method robust at batch size 1 and compatible with MCU hardware that lacks normalization-layer support. The TinyTTA Engine, built on TensorFlow Lite Micro, implements floating-point backpropagation for standard DNN operators with a layer-wise update strategy and dynamic heap allocation to keep the on-device memory overhead to ~40 KB SRAM and ~85 KB Flash.

## Results

On MCUNet with batch size 1, TinyTTA achieves 64.3% accuracy on CIFAR-10-C, 51.6% on CIFAR-100-C, 58.1% on OfficeHome, and 63.6% on PACS, outperforming the second-best method (EcoTTA) by up to 57.6% relative improvement. In terms of efficiency, TinyTTA requires only 5.65 MB of memory on CIFAR-10-C (6× less than CoTTA's 33.88 MB), achieves 0.22 s/sample latency (12% faster than EATA), and consumes 0.44 J per image (65% less than CoTTA's 12.50 J). On the STM32H747 MCU (512 KB SRAM), TinyTTA is the only method that runs TTA within the memory constraint, achieving 64.3% accuracy with 123 KB SRAM and 50.7 ms latency.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TinyTTA outperforms all six baselines across accuracy, memory, latency, and energy. Against TENT (Wang, ICLR 2021), TinyTTA uses 2.3× less memory than TENT-Modulating and 4.2× less than TENT-Fine-tuning, while halving latency (0.22 s vs. 0.51 s) and energy (0.44 J vs. 1.02 J). Against CoTTA/EcoTTA (Song, CVPR 2023), TinyTTA achieves dramatically higher accuracy on MCUNet (64.3% vs. 13.1% on CIFAR-10-C) with 6× less memory and 28× lower latency (0.22 s vs. 6.25 s). Against EATA (Niu, ICML 2022), TinyTTA reduces latency by 12% and energy by 12% while achieving higher accuracy. Against TinyEngine with TENT (Lin, NeurIPS 2022, Appendix C.4), TinyTTA significantly outperforms across all four models and four datasets due to dynamic early-exit adaptation without recompilation. Against TTT (Sun, ICML 2020, Appendix J), TinyTTA boosts MCUNet accuracy from 16% to 64% and EfficientNet from 18% to 68% on CIFAR-10-C while also reducing memory usage. Against bias-only fine-tuning from TinyTL (Cai, NeurIPS 2020, Appendix I), TinyTTA's early-exit branch updates provide substantially more stable and accurate adaptation under distribution shifts.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the test-time adaptation (TTA) literature—dominated by normalization-layer-based methods (TENT, TTN, NOTE) and full-model fine-tuning approaches (CoTTA, TTT)—by repurposing two ideas from other contexts: early-exit inference (from BranchyNet and Shallow-Deep Networks) and weight standardization (from Qiao et al.) to address the specific constraints of MCU deployment. It departs from prior TTA work in three key ways: (1) replacing backpropagation through the full network with updates only to lightweight early-exit heads, (2) substituting batch-dependent normalization layers with batch-agnostic WS, and (3) introducing a self-ensemble partitioning strategy that groups layers by activation memory similarity. The TinyTTA Engine further extends TensorFlow Lite Micro, which previously supported only forward-pass inference, by adding on-device backpropagation for a subset of operators.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TinyTTA is the first framework to demonstrate that TTA is feasible on a 512 KB MCU (STM32H747), opening a path toward adaptive AI in extremely resource-constrained IoT, healthcare, and robotics settings where models must continuously adjust to sensor drift and environmental changes without cloud connectivity. The practical impact is significant but bounded: the method requires an offline self-ensemble retraining step, has only been validated at batch size 1 on a single MCU architecture, and the large accuracy margins over baselines partly reflect the catastrophic failure of normalization-based TTA at batch size 1 rather than a fundamental algorithmic advantage. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to larger batch sizes, other MCU families, and non-image modalities (the audio experiment in Appendix H is a promising but preliminary step).
