## Problem

Despite the established equivalence between GFlowNet training and hierarchical variational inference, prior work (Malkin et al., 2023) found that directly minimizing divergence measures (KL, Rényi-α) as learning objectives underperformed the standard trajectory balance (TB) loss. This paper identifies the root cause as the high variance of REINFORCE-based gradient estimators for divergence objectives, which was masked in prior evaluations by sparse target distributions and biased baselines, and shows that proper variance reduction makes divergence-based training competitive or superior.

## Input / Output

The method takes a target distribution specified by an unnormalized reward function r over terminal states and a state space structured as a measurable pointed DAG (with forward/backward transition kernels), and produces samples from the target distribution over compositional objects. The forward policy p_F is parameterized by task-specific neural networks (MLPs for sequences and continuous distributions, GINs for phylogenetic trees, deep sets for set generation) and is trained to match the target; the backward policy p_B is typically fixed. Evaluation uses L1 distance for discrete targets and Jensen-Shannon divergence for continuous targets, plus top-K reward and number-of-modes metrics.

## Method

The paper replaces the standard TB/DB log-squared loss with f-divergence minimization (forward KL, reverse KL, Rényi-α, Tsallis-α) as the training objective, extending the theoretical equivalence between GFlowNet training and HVI from discrete to arbitrary topological spaces (Proposition 1). The key technical contribution is a collection of control variates for the REINFORCE gradient estimator: (1) using ∇θ log p_F(τ) as a zero-mean control variate with a batch-based linearly-approximated optimal baseline (Equation 6), and (2) the REINFORCE leave-one-out estimator (Equation 7) for the score-function term, which is computable via a single vector-Jacobian product in autodiff frameworks. These variance-reduction techniques reduce gradient variance by orders of magnitude (Figure 2), and the resulting divergence-based training objectives frequently accelerate convergence and achieve better final accuracy than TB loss across seven generative tasks spanning discrete and continuous domains.

## Results

The primary metric is Jensen-Shannon divergence (lower is better) between the learned and target distributions. On set generation, all divergence-based objectives (Rev. KL, For. KL, Rényi-α, Tsallis-α) achieve 0.03±0.00, halving the TB loss's 0.07±0.00. On autoregressive sequence generation, reverse KL reaches 0.16±0.06 versus TB's 0.28±0.06 (a 43% reduction), and on mixture of Gaussians, forward KL achieves 0.09±0.10 versus TB's 0.31±0.08 (a 3.4× improvement). On Bayesian phylogenetic inference, all methods are statistically indistinguishable (~0.21–0.22). Control variates reduce gradient variance by orders of magnitude (Figure 2) and visibly accelerate convergence (Figure 6).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is the Trajectory Balance (TB) loss from Malkin et al. (NeurIPS 2022), which minimizes the log-squared difference between forward and backward policies. Divergence-based objectives win decisively on set generation (0.03 vs. 0.07 JS divergence, a 2× improvement) and on mixture of Gaussians (forward KL: 0.09 vs. 0.31, a 3.4× improvement). Reverse KL also substantially outperforms TB on autoregressive sequence generation (0.16 vs. 0.28). On Bayesian phylogenetic inference, all methods tie within noise (≈0.21–0.22). The one clear loss is on hypergrid navigation, where the extremely sparse reward landscape favors TB's off-policy exploration; the authors note that a sufficiently negative α in Rényi-α can partially recover coverage but do not fully close the gap. Overall, the paper's central claim—that divergence minimization with control variates is a practical and often superior alternative to TB loss—is supported across 6 of 7 benchmark tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the GFlowNet–hierarchical variational inference (HVI) equivalence established by Malkin et al. [56] from finitely supported distributions to arbitrary measurable topological spaces, building on the continuous GFlowNet framework of Lahlou et al. [48]. Its central methodological departure from [56] is the argument that the previously observed inferiority of divergence-based objectives (KL, Rényi-α, Tsallis-α) relative to trajectory balance (TB) loss was an artifact of high-variance REINFORCE gradient estimators rather than a fundamental limitation. To address this, the paper adapts control variate techniques from the black-box VI literature—specifically the leave-one-out estimator of Shi et al. [85] and Salimans & Knowles [82]—to the GFlowNet setting, yielding provably variance-reduced, unbiased gradient estimators for all divergence-based objectives.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that standard f-divergence minimization is a practical and often superior training objective for GFlowNets when paired with appropriate variance reduction, this work reopens the entire variational inference literature as a source of algorithmic ideas for GFlowNet training (e.g., χ-divergence losses, MCMC-hybrid objectives, importance-weighted autoencoder-style training). The theoretical extension to arbitrary topological spaces also broadens the formal guarantees beyond the discrete setting. Key limitations include evaluation on relatively small-scale problems, a fixed α=0.5 without systematic sensitivity analysis, and the continued underperformance of divergence-based objectives on extremely sparse targets (hypergrid), leaving open questions about optimal α selection, scaling to NLP and drug-discovery domains, and compatibility with off-policy techniques like replay buffers or local search.
