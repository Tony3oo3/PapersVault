## Problem

Differentially private mean estimators in high dimensions suffer from a curse of dimensionality, requiring Ω(√d) samples even when the data is highly anisotropic and concentrated on a small number of principal components. This paper asks whether dimension-independent sample complexity is achievable under (ε,δ)-DP when the covariance is far from isotropic, and establishes near-optimal upper and lower bounds for both known and unknown covariance settings.

## Input / Output

The algorithm takes n i.i.d. samples from a subgaussian distribution in R^d with unknown mean μ, along with privacy parameters (ε, δ), target error α, and (in the known-covariance case) the covariance matrix Σ. It outputs a mean estimate μ̂ ∈ R^d satisfying ||μ̂ − μ||₂ ≤ α with probability 0.99 while being (ε,δ)-differentially private. The sample complexity depends on tr(Σ) and tr(Σ^{1/2}) rather than d directly, so for anisotropic distributions with k dominant singular values the complexity is Õ(k/α² + k/(αε)), independent of d.

## Method

For known covariance, the key innovation is replacing the d-coordinate-wise outlier filtering (which costs Ω(√d) samples) with the FriendlyCore/BasicFilter procedure, which removes outliers using only Õ(1/ε) samples via a rescaled distance predicate ||Σ^{-1/4}(x−y)||₂ ≤ λ. The remaining data's empirical mean is then released with anisotropic Gaussian noise of covariance tr(Σ^{1/2})Σ^{1/2}/(ε²n²), distributing the privacy budget unevenly across coordinates proportional to their variance. For unknown covariance, the algorithm splits the data into two halves: one half is used to privately identify the top k ≈ ε²n² variances via the sparse vector technique and estimate their individual values, while the other half feeds the known-covariance estimator on those coordinates plus a spherical-noise estimator on the remainder. A Hölder's inequality argument bounds the bottom-coordinate error in terms of ‖σ‖₁/k, yielding a d^{1/4} dimension dependence. The matching lower bound applies the fingerprinting technique to a carefully selected bucket of almost-isotropic coordinates whose size scales with tr(Σ^{1/2}).

## Results

For known covariance, the paper achieves a dimension-independent sample complexity of $n = \tilde{O}(\text{tr}(\Sigma)/\alpha^2 + \text{tr}(\Sigma^{1/2})/(\alpha\varepsilon) + \log(1/\delta)/\varepsilon)$, which is optimal up to polylogarithmic factors (lower bound: $n = \Omega(\text{tr}(\Sigma^{1/2})/(\alpha\varepsilon\log^2 d))$). For unknown covariance, the dimension dependence improves from $\sqrt{d}$ (prior best) to $d^{1/4}$, with sample complexity $n = \tilde{O}(\text{tr}(\Sigma)/\alpha^2 + \sum_i \Sigma_{ii}^{1/2}/(\alpha\varepsilon) + d^{1/4}\sqrt{\sum_i \Sigma_{ii}^{1/2}}/(\sqrt{\alpha}\,\varepsilon))$. In the illustrative anisotropic example where $k$ singular values are 1 and the rest are $1/d$, the known-covariance bound reduces to $n = \tilde{O}(k/\alpha^2 + k/(\alpha\varepsilon))$, fully independent of $d$.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is against the folklore estimator and Aumüller et al. [8], both of which require $\Omega(\sqrt{d}/\varepsilon)$ samples due to performing $d$ independent coordinate-wise filtering steps. This paper eliminates that $\sqrt{d}$ term entirely in the known-covariance case by replacing coordinate-wise filtering with the FriendlyCore (BasicFilter) procedure [63], which uses only $\tilde{O}(1/\varepsilon)$ samples regardless of dimension. For unknown covariance, prior approaches either require $\Theta(d^{3/2})$ samples for full private covariance estimation [39,40] or $\Omega(\sqrt{d}/\varepsilon)$ for diagonal variance estimation [41]; this paper reduces the dimension dependence to $d^{1/4}$ by combining the Sparse Vector technique [23] to identify top-$k$ variances with the known-covariance estimator on those coordinates and spherical noise on the rest. The lower bound (Theorem 1.2) confirms the known-covariance upper bound is optimal up to $\log^2 d$ factors, via a fingerprinting argument [39] applied to a carefully chosen near-isotropic coordinate subset. The paper also notes that pure DP is fundamentally limited to $\Omega(d/\varepsilon)$ by the packing argument [32,18], motivating the focus on $(\varepsilon,\delta)$-DP throughout.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the FriendlyCore/BasicFilter framework of Tsfadia et al. [63] for dimension-independent private aggregation, the univariate DP confidence interval techniques of Karwa and Vadhan [41] that underpin the folklore estimator, and the variance-aware noise scheduling of Aumüller et al. [8] for diagonal covariance. The key departure is replacing the d independent coordinate-wise outlier-removal steps (which forced Ω(√d) sample complexity in all prior work) with a single FriendlyCore filtering pass requiring only Õ(1/ε) samples, thereby breaking the dimension barrier for anisotropic distributions. For the unknown-covariance case, the paper introduces a sparse-vector-technique-based identification of top-k variances, combining the known-covariance estimator with a trace-only spherical-noise estimator for the remaining coordinates.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result establishes that the Ω(√d) sample complexity barrier for (ε,δ)-DP mean estimation is not fundamental but rather an artifact of prior algorithmic design, and that dimension-free estimation is achievable (and optimal up to log factors) when the covariance is anisotropic. This has direct implications for high-dimensional genomic, financial, and sensor data where signals concentrate on few principal components. The open question of optimal sample complexity under unknown covariance (currently d^{1/4}) and whether the log(1/δ) dependence can be decoupled from the tr(Σ^{1/2})/(αε) term remain important directions. The theoretical contribution is clean but the practical impact is primarily for DP-theory practitioners rather than mainstream ML systems.
