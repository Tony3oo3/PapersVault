## Problem

Stochastic extragradient (SEG) methods have had limited success on unconstrained convex-concave (monotone) finite-sum minimax problems: existing variants including SEG with uniform sampling, random reshuffling, and flip-flop shuffling can all diverge in this setting. The paper asks whether a carefully designed shuffling-based SEG can achieve convergence guarantees in the monotone setting and provably faster rates in the strongly monotone setting, without requiring increasing batch sizes, bounded domains, or component-wise convex-concavity.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a finite-sum minimax objective f(x,y) = (1/n)Σᵢ fᵢ(x,y) with L-smooth components and M-Lipschitz Hessian saddle gradients, an initial iterate z₀, and a decaying stepsize schedule βₖ = O(k^{-1/3} log k) with αₖ = βₖ/2. It operates in epochs of 2n iterations (two passes over all components), and the output is the sequence of epoch-boundary iterates z₀ᵏ, with convergence measured by minₖ E‖Fz₀ᵏ‖² in the monotone case or E‖z₀ᴷ − z*‖² in the strongly monotone case. No batch size increase or per-component monotonicity is required.

## Method

SEG-FFA combines three minimal modifications to vanilla same-sample SEG: (i) flip-flop shuffling, where a random permutation is used for the first n iterations and reversed for the next n, ensuring all pairwise Jacobian terms DFⱼ(z)Fᵢ(z) appear in the epoch update; (ii) the standard two-step SEG update (extrapolation with step αₖ, then update with step βₖ) using the same component for both steps; and (iii) an anchoring step z₀ᵏ⁺¹ = (z₀ᵏ + z₂ₙᵏ)/2 that takes a convex combination of the epoch's initial and final iterates. The key design principle is second-order Taylor matching: the epoch-wise update of SEG-FFA deterministically matches the deterministic extragradient update up to O(η²) terms, leaving only an O(η³) approximation error. This matching is shown to be necessary for convergence in the monotone setting (SEG-RR has O(η²) error and diverges; SEG-FF without anchoring matches EG+ only with divergent parameters η₂ = 2η₁). The result is a convergence rate of Õ((log K)²/K^{1/3}) in the monotone case and Õ(1/nK⁴) in the strongly monotone case, the latter provably faster than the Ω(1/nK³) lower bound for SGDA-RR and SEG-RR.

## Results

In the convex-concave (C-C) setting, SEG-FFA achieves a convergence rate of $\tilde{O}(1/K^{1/3})$ on the squared gradient norm, while SEG-US, SEG-RR, and SEG-FF all diverge (lower bound $\Omega(1)$). In the strongly-convex-strongly-concave (SC-SC) setting, SEG-FFA achieves $\tilde{O}(1/nK^4)$ on the last-iterate distance, which is provably faster than the $\Omega(1/nK^3)$ lower bounds established for both SGDA-RR and SEG-RR. The key design principle is second-order Taylor expansion matching to the deterministic EG method, yielding an $\mathcal{O}(\eta^3)$ approximation error per epoch.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SEG-FFA decisively outperforms all shuffling-based SEG baselines in the C-C setting: while SEG-US, SEG-RR, and SEG-FF all diverge (proven via an explicit counterexample in Theorem 4.1), SEG-FFA converges at $\tilde{O}(1/K^{1/3})$. In the SC-SC setting, SEG-FFA achieves $\tilde{O}(1/nK^4)$, which is a provable improvement over the $\Omega(1/nK^3)$ lower bounds the paper establishes for both SGDA-RR [15] and SEG-RR [18] (Theorem 5.6), representing a full order-of-magnitude gap in the epoch dependence. The paper also identifies a flaw in the claimed C-C convergence of SEG-RR by Emmanouilidis et al. [18], showing their Jensen's inequality argument is invalid. Experimentally, on randomly generated quadratic minimax problems, SEG-FFA is the only method that converges in the monotone case, while in the strongly monotone case it achieves the smallest gradient norm among all compared methods (SEG-US, SEG-RR, SEG-FF, SGDA-US, SGDA-RR).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of stochastic extragradient methods for minimax problems, shuffling-based sampling from finite-sum optimization, and the Taylor expansion matching principle. It builds directly on Korpelevich's extragradient (EG) as the reference deterministic method, Rajput et al.'s flip-flop shuffling scheme, and the Krasnosel'skii-Mann iteration for the anchoring step. The key departure from prior work is the demonstration that shuffling alone (random reshuffling or flip-flop) cannot fix SEG's divergence in monotone settings, and that a simple anchoring step is the critical missing ingredient to achieve second-order Taylor matching to EG. The design principle unifies these components: the method must match EG's update up to O(η²) terms to converge, which flip-flop sampling enables but anchoring is required to make compatible with convergent stepsize choices.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This resolves a long-standing gap in stochastic minimax optimization: SEG, the stochastic analog of the well-known superior EG method, had never been shown to converge in general unconstrained convex-concave settings without restrictive assumptions (increasing batch size, bounded domain, per-component convexity, or uniform variance bounds). The provable K⁴ rate gap over K³ for RR-based methods in strongly monotone settings provides a concrete theoretical advantage. Open questions include extending the second-order matching framework to nonconvex-nonconcave problems, whether matching can be achieved without anchoring, and relaxing the Lipschitz Hessian (M-smoothness) assumption. The practical impact is currently limited by the quadratic experimental setting and the specific structural assumptions required.
