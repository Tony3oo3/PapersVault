## Problem

Current RAG pipelines face a trade-off: using too few retrieved contexts (small k) compromises recall, while using too many introduces noise that degrades generation quality. Existing solutions rely on separate expert ranking models (e.g., BERT, T5) that lack the zero-shot generalization of LLMs, and no prior work has shown that a single LLM can be instruction-tuned to handle both context ranking and answer generation effectively within a unified RAG framework.

## Input / Output

At inference, the system takes a question and top-N retrieved passages (N=100 for 8B, N=30 for 70B) from a dense retriever (Dragon by default). The LLM first scores each passage's relevance by generating a single 'True' or 'False' token, using the token probability as the relevance score to select the top-k (k=5) passages. These k passages are then concatenated with the question to produce the final answer, which is a short phrase for OpenQA, a True/False label for FEVER, or a longer span for conversational QA.

## Method

RankRAG uses a two-stage instruction-tuning procedure on Llama3 (8B/70B). Stage I performs standard SFT on 128K instruction-following examples. Stage II is the key contribution: it unifies five data types—context-rich QA, retrieval-augmented QA (with BM25 hard negatives), single-passage context ranking (MS MARCO), multi-passage retrieval-augmented ranking, and conversational ranking—into a standardized (x, c, y) format where the question is x, the passage(s) are c, and the output y is either an answer span or a relevance label. At inference, the retrieve-rerank-generate pipeline uses the LLM's 'True' token probability as a cross-encoder-style relevance score to rerank top-N passages down to top-k before generation. Remarkably, only ~50K ranking pairs (~10% of MS MARCO) in the training blend suffice to outperform dedicated rankers (RankBERT, monoT5, BGE-Reranker) trained on 10× more data, because the unified format lets ranking and generation capabilities mutually reinforce each other.

## Results

On nine general-domain knowledge-intensive benchmarks (NQ, TriviaQA, PopQA, HotpotQA, 2WikimQA, FEVER, Doc2Dial, TopiOCQA, INSCIT), Llama3-RankRAG-8B achieves an average score of 52.6, outperforming Llama3-ChatQA-1.5-8B (49.6) by 3.0 points and GPT-4-0613 (42.0) by 10.6 points. Llama3-RankRAG-70B reaches 56.1 average, surpassing Llama3-ChatQA-1.5-70B (53.6) and Llama3-Instruct-70B (47.1). On the five biomedical Mirage benchmarks, RankRAG-70B attains 78.06 average without any biomedical fine-tuning, reaching over 98% of GPT-4's 79.97, while RankRAG-8B (63.95) surpasses the domain-specific Meditron-70B (60.18) by 3.8 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RankRAG-8B outperforms the strongest open-source RAG baseline Llama3-ChatQA-1.5-8B by 3.0 average points across all nine tasks, with particularly large gains on long-tail PopQA (+5.0 EM) and multi-hop 2WikimQA (+4.6 EM), where retrieval quality is most critical. Against GPT-4-0613, RankRAG-8B leads by 10.6 average points, and notably GPT-4's RAG performance actually degrades (e.g., HotpotQA drops from 34.5 to 27.6 EM) when retrieved passages lack relevant information. RankRAG-70B extends these advantages, beating Llama3-ChatQA-1.5-70B by 2.5 average and Llama3-Instruct-70B by 9.0 average. In the ranking ablation (Table 6), RankRAG-8B trained on only ~50k ranking pairs surpasses dedicated rankers trained on 10× more data (e.g., RankBERT 110M, monoT5 3B, RankLLAMA 8B all use ~503k pairs) on most tasks, and even outperforms BGE-Rerank-v2-m3 trained on 1.6M pairs. On biomedical benchmarks, RankRAG-70B (78.06 avg) reaches 97.6% of GPT-4 (79.97) without any domain-specific fine-tuning, while RankRAG-8B (63.95) beats the medical-specialized Meditron-70B (60.18) by 3.8 points.

## Relation to Prior Work

RankRAG sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: instruction-tuned RAG models (ChatQA-1.5, RA-DIT, InstructRetro) that enhance LLMs for context-grounded generation, and cross-encoder rerankers (RankBERT, monoT5, BGE-Reranker, RankLLAMA) that refine retrieved passages before generation. The key departure is unifying both capabilities in a single LLM via a shared (x, c, y) instruction format, treating relevance judgment as a QA task (True/False or passage-index identification) rather than a separate scoring model. This repurposes the LLM's existing instruction-following and reading-comprehension abilities for ranking, avoiding the need for a dedicated moderate-sized reranker while retaining the LLM's zero-shot generalization advantage.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a single instruction-tuned LLM replaces the two-model retrieve-rerank-generate pipeline, simplifying deployment and improving domain transfer (biomedical results without domain-specific fine-tuning). The data-efficiency finding—that ~50k ranking pairs mixed into a diverse instruction-tuning blend outperform dedicated rankers trained on 500k+ pairs—challenges the assumption that ranking quality requires large-scale specialized supervision. Open questions include integration with multi-round/iterative RAG pipelines, the latency cost of sample-wise LLM reranking at scale, and whether the unified format generalizes to non-English or multimodal retrieval settings.
