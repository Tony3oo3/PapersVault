## Problem

Multimodal Large Language Models are trained to output natural language tokens, but embodied agents require actions in continuous or discrete spaces, creating a fundamental representational gap. While prior work has proposed various architectures to bridge this gap (MLP heads, uniform discretization, semantic text mapping), no systematic comparison exists across action space types, embodiments, and environments. This paper asks: what principles and strategies are necessary to effectively close the gap between MLLM output spaces and embodied agent action spaces?

## Input / Output

The policy consumes RGB visual observations (200×200 for CALVIN, Meta-World, and BabyAI; 336×336 for Habitat Pick and Language Rearrangement), a natural language task instruction, and optionally agent proprioception (joint angles and gripper state). Visual tokens are downsampled from ~556 to 4 via a Perceiver layer to allow longer observation histories. The output is a sequence of m action tokens autoregressively generated by the MLLM, which are decoded into a single environment action—either a continuous vector (4–10 DoF for manipulation) or a discrete skill selection (6 actions in BabyAI, 70 high-level skills in Language Rearrangement).

## Method

The paper introduces a unified Action Space Adapter (ASA) framework with three components—an adapter head, adapter embedding, and adapter decoder—that re-parameterizes a frozen LLaVA-1.5-7B MLLM to output actions. For continuous action spaces, the key contribution is Residual Vector Quantization (RVQ) tokenization: multiple learned codebooks (default: 2 codebooks of 512 codes each) residually model the action, yielding K^M representable actions with manageable per-codebook sizes and achieving 10× lower reconstruction MSE than single-codebook VQ. For discrete action spaces, the best-performing adapter (SemLang) maps each action to a semantically related sequence of tokens already in the MLLM vocabulary (e.g., 'pick apple' → tokens [5839, 26163]), reusing the pretrained language head with zero additional parameters. The study empirically compares seven ASAs (Pred, Uniform, VQ, RVQ, SemLang, Lang, and a non-semantic composite) across five environments and 114 tasks, training with LoRA (~100M LLM parameters + ~40M ASA parameters) via supervised fine-tuning from expert demonstrations or PPO reinforcement learning, and shows that RVQ's gains are unique to MLLMs rather than attributable to the tokenization decoder alone.

## Results

For continuous action spaces, RVQ (Residual Vector Quantization) achieves the best success rate across all three environments: 72% on CALVIN, 84% on Meta-World, and 29% on Habitat Pick, outperforming direct regression (Pred) by 4%, 23%, and 10% respectively. For discrete action spaces, SemLang (semantic language tokenization) achieves 51% on Language Rearrangement and 40% on BabyAI, outperforming categorical prediction (Pred) by 9% and 8%. The RVQ tokenization also enables sample-efficient RL, with SemLang converging in 20M training steps versus 70M for Pred on Language Rearrangement.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RVQ consistently outperforms all other continuous-action ASAs: it beats Pred by 12% average absolute difference, VQ by 18%, and Uniform by 27% across CALVIN, Meta-World, and Habitat Pick. The advantage of RVQ over VQ is attributed to superior action reconstruction (MSE 0.005 vs 0.05 on Meta-World), with RVQ's residual codebooks capturing finer action details critical for precise control tasks like pressing and picking. For discrete actions, SemLang outperforms Pred by 9% on Language Rearrangement and 8% on BabyAI, while non-semantic Lang performs worst (14% and 11% below SemLang respectively), confirming that semantic alignment with the MLLM's native vocabulary is key. Against prior work, RVQ sets a new state-of-the-art on Meta-World ML-45 (84% vs 79% for DualMind) and SemLang achieves SOTA on Language Rearrangement (51% vs 42% for LLaRP), though RVQ underperforms RoboFlamingo on CALVIN (72% vs 82%) due to different MLLM and sensor inputs, and SemLang underperforms GFlan on BabyAI (40% vs 55%) because GFlan uses ground-truth language state descriptions rather than RGB images. Notably, RVQ's gains are unique to MLLMs: on non-LLM policies trained from scratch, RVQ actually hurts performance (−7% on CALVIN, −15% on Meta-World), indicating the MLLM leverages its pre-trained token-sequence knowledge to benefit from the learned action tokenization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies a fragmented line of work on adapting MLLMs for embodied action (RT-2's uniform discretization, RoboFlamingo/LLaRP's MLP regression, GFlan's language scoring, PaLM-E's semantic tokens) under a single 'Action Space Adapter' framework, enabling systematic comparison. The best-performing continuous-action tokenization (RVQ) is directly borrowed from autoregressive image generation (Lee et al., CVPR 2022) and the VQ codebook from VQ-VAE (Van Den Oord et al., NeurIPS 2017), repurposed for action spaces. Unlike prior work that typically studies one adapter in one environment, this paper compares seven ASAs across five embodied environments and 114 tasks, isolating which design principles matter for continuous versus discrete action spaces.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides actionable design guidance for grounding MLLMs in embodied tasks: learned residual tokenization for continuous actions and semantic language alignment for discrete actions. A notable finding is that RVQ's gains are specific to MLLMs (helping LLaVA by up to 23% while hurting a from-scratch transformer by 15%), suggesting the benefit comes from the MLLM's pre-trained token-sequencing knowledge rather than purely from action representation quality. Key limitations include evaluation on a single MLLM (LLaVA-1.5), a single LoRA training regime, and the requirement for offline demonstrations to train the VQ codebook. Open questions include whether these findings transfer to other base MLLMs, full fine-tuning, and off-policy or offline RL settings, and whether the current performance levels (e.g., 40% on BabyAI) are sufficient for real-robot deployment.
