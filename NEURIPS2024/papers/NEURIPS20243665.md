## Problem

While extragradient and optimistic gradient methods have achieved tight O(T^{-1/2}) last-iterate convergence for constrained monotone variational inequalities (MVIs), Frank-Wolfe algorithms—a fundamentally different class of projection-free methods—had no convergence guarantees for general MVI problems. Moreover, no algorithm had a provable last-iterate convergence guarantee for constrained stochastic MVI problems without strong curvature assumptions on the operator or feasible set.

## Input / Output

The algorithm takes a monotone operator F: X → R^d on a convex compact set X, a strongly convex regularizer f(·) whose gradient diverges at the boundary of X (e.g., negative entropy on a simplex), and a tunable smoothing parameter τ. In the stochastic variant, it additionally receives noisy estimates F(x_t) + z_t with bounded conditional variance. The output is the last iterate x_t, which converges to an MVI solution in expectation; in the zero-sum game instantiation, this corresponds to Nash equilibrium mixed strategies with a bounded Nash gap.

## Method

The paper proposes a generalized Frank-Wolfe algorithm that replaces the hard linear minimization oracle with a smoothed subproblem s_t = argmin_{s∈X}{sᵀF(x_t) + τf(s)}, where the strongly convex regularizer ensures the FW direction s(x) is Lipschitz continuous in x (Lemma 4.1). Convergence is established via a Lyapunov argument on the regularized gap V(x) = max_s{(x−s)ᵀF(x) − τf(s)}, yielding O(T^{-1/2}) last-iterate convergence with diminishing stepsizes. For the stochastic setting, a two-timescale structure is introduced: a fast-timescale estimator y_t recursively averages noisy operator evaluations to reduce variance, while the slow-timescale FW update uses y_t in place of F(x_t), achieving O(T^{-1/6}) last-iterate convergence. The smoothing parameter τ is chosen as O(T^{-1/2}) to balance the regularization bias against the convergence rate.

## Results

The paper establishes an O(T^{-1/2}) last-iterate convergence rate for the generalized Frank-Wolfe algorithm in deterministic constrained monotone variational inequality (MVI) problems (Theorem 4.1), matching the tight rate recently proved for extragradient and optimistic gradient methods [15]. For the stochastic MVI setting, a two-timescale variant achieves O(T^{-1/6}) last-iterate convergence (Theorem 5.1), which is the first such guarantee for constrained stochastic MVI problems without curvature assumptions. As a corollary, smoothed fictitious play in finite zero-sum games achieves a Õ(T^{-1/2}) Nash gap convergence rate (Theorem 3.1), with iteration complexity Õ(|A¹||A²|/ε²) to reach ε-accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is the extragradient method [15], which also achieves a tight O(T^{-1/2}) last-iterate convergence rate for constrained MVI problems. Both methods match in convergence rate, but the proof techniques differ: the extragradient analysis in [15] relies on computer-aided proofs (sum-of-squares programming), while the generalized FW analysis uses a natural Lyapunov argument on a regularized gap function. In numerical experiments (Rock-Paper-Scissors, Burglar-Policeman, and a 100×100 random matrix game), both algorithms show similar performance, though extragradient appears more stable and asymptotically superior, likely due to the softmax bias in generalized FW that depends on the regularization parameter τ. In the stochastic setting, no existing algorithm (extragradient, optimistic gradient, or Halpern iteration) has a provable last-iterate convergence guarantee for constrained stochastic MVI problems, so the paper only verifies its own algorithm's convergence empirically, confirming it converges (though not to zero due to constant stepsizes and noise).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions generalized Frank-Wolfe (smoothed conditional gradient) as an orthogonal alternative to the dominant gradient-based methods (extragradient, optimistic gradient) for solving constrained monotone variational inequalities. It draws a direct lineage from the classic smoothed fictitious play algorithm in game theory (Fudenberg & Levine, 1995) and the generalized conditional gradient framework (Bach, 2012; Bredies et al., 2009), showing that smoothed FP is a special case of generalized FW for zero-sum games. For the stochastic variant, it adapts the two-timescale variance-reduction structure from stochastic FW in convex optimization (Mokhtari et al., 2018) to the MVI setting, where the non-gradient structure of F(·) introduces additional analytical challenges.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first last-iterate convergence guarantee for a constrained stochastic MVI algorithm without curvature assumptions on the operator or feasible set, filling a notable gap where prior stochastic methods either required strong monotonicity/coercivity or only offered averaged-iterate bounds. The O(T^{-1/2}) deterministic rate matches extragradient and optimistic gradient, but the proof uses a natural Lyapunov argument rather than the computer-aided sum-of-squares or performance-estimation techniques required by prior work. Open questions include whether the O(T^{-1/2}) rate is tight for FW-type algorithms, what happens in the unregularized (τ=0) regime where vanilla FW directions become non-smooth, and whether the O(T^{-1/6}) stochastic rate can be improved. The connection to smoothed FP also yields the first finite-time convergence bound for that canonical game-theoretic algorithm.
