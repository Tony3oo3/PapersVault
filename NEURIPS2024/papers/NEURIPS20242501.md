## Problem

When only a vanishingly small fraction of nodes (n_Q = o(n)) in a target network have observed edges, standard network estimation cannot achieve o(1) error because unobserved nodes have no incident edges to inform their latent positions. This paper asks whether a fully observed source network P sharing latent structure with target Q can enable vanishing estimation error, and formalizes the precise source-target relationship needed via a relative ranking condition on 2-hop graph distances.

## Input / Output

The method takes a full n×n source adjacency matrix A_P, an n_Q×n_Q target subgraph A_Q restricted to a uniformly random node subset S of size n_Q = o(n), and produces a full n×n edge probability estimate Q̂. The source-target relationship is not pointwise closeness of edge probabilities but a relative condition: nodes ranked close in P's graph distance must also rank close in Q's, allowing pairs where one network is much denser than the other. The algorithm is nonparametric, requiring only Hölder smoothness of the underlying edge probability functions.

## Method

Algorithm 1 computes the graph distance d_P(i,j) = ||(e_i − e_j)^T P²(I − e_i e_i^T − e_j e_j^T)||²₂ for all node pairs, then for each target pair (i,j) estimates Q̂_ij by averaging A_Q entries over the bottom h-quantile neighborhoods of i and j under P's graph distance, with bandwidth h = c√(log n_Q / n_Q). This nonparametric approach requires no parametric form on f_P or f_Q and achieves error O((d/2)^((β∧1)/2) (log n / n_Q)^((β∧1)/(2d))) under the rankings assumption. For the structured SBM case, Algorithm 2 uses spectral clustering (Chen et al. 2014) to recover community assignments in both networks, learns a projection mapping from P-communities to Q-communities via the observed nodes in S, and estimates inter-community edge probabilities from A_Q, achieving the minimax rate k_Q²/n_Q². The paper proves matching minimax lower bounds via Fano's inequality with Gilbert-Varshamov codes, establishing that the SBM rate is information-theoretically optimal.

## Results

On simulated networks (n=200, n_Q=50, 50 trials, MSE scaled by 1e2), Algorithm 1 achieves the best transfer performance in 2 of 3 settings: 0.7473±0.0648 on Noisy-MMSB and 0.5744±0.1086 on R^10-Latent, both beating the Oracle baseline with p=0.1 (0.9556 and 0.7715 respectively) despite using only ~6% of the edge observations the Oracle sees. On Smooth Graphons, Algorithm 1 (1.7656±0.7494) is outperformed by Oracle(p=0.1) (0.5016±0.0562). Algorithm 1 consistently outperforms Algorithm 2 across all three simulated settings, with the gap largest on Smooth Graphons (1.77 vs 4.50). On real-world metabolic networks, Algorithm 1 is competitive with Oracle(p=0.1) when a well-matched source organism is chosen.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two main baselines. Against the Oracle baseline (Universal Singular Value Thresholding from Chatterjee 2015 applied to a noisy full-graph observation), Algorithm 1 wins on Noisy-MMSB (0.747 vs 0.956) and R^10-Latent (0.574 vs 0.772) at p=0.1, but loses on Smooth Graphons (1.766 vs 0.502); it beats Oracle(p=0.3) and Oracle(p=0.5) in all three settings. Notably, Algorithms 1 and 2 use only ~6% unbiased edge observations from Q, while Oracle(p=0.1) observes ~90% in expectation, making the wins on MMSB and Latent models particularly striking. Against the Levin et al. (2022) baseline (adapted for the transfer setting), both Algorithm 1 and Algorithm 2 outperform it, though both remain worse than the non-transfer Oracle(p=0.0) as expected. On real-world email networks, Algorithm 2 achieves performance comparable to Algorithm 1 and Oracle(p∈{0.01, 0.05}), suggesting the email data is well-approximated by SBMs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the transfer learning paradigm from classification and regression (Cai and Wei, 2021; Cai and Pu, 2024) to network estimation in latent variable models, where edge correlations make the problem fundamentally different. The core algorithmic mechanism repurposes the graph distance from Mao et al. (2021) — originally designed for network-assisted covariate estimation — as a ranking tool to identify which observed target nodes are informative for estimating unobserved edges. For the SBM special case, the minimax lower bound proof adapts Fano's inequality with Gilbert-Varshamov codes in a coupled source-target construction, while the upper bound relies on exact recovery guarantees from Chen et al. (2014) for spectral clustering. The rankings assumption (Definition 1.3) is a novel relative condition on quantile orderings of graph distances, deliberately more flexible than pointwise closeness of f_P and f_Q.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first formal treatment of transfer learning for network estimation where only an o(1) fraction of target nodes are observed, showing that vanishing error is achievable under a structural similarity condition between source and target. The minimax optimality result for SBMs provides a clean benchmark, and the nonparametric Algorithm 1 avoids parametric assumptions on the latent functions. Key open questions include extending to sparser networks (edge density Ω(log n/n) vs. the current Ω(1/√n) requirement), handling multiple source networks, and understanding the dimension dependence in minimax lower bounds for general network estimation. The empirical results on metabolic networks and email networks demonstrate practical relevance for biological and social network inference where full observation is infeasible.
