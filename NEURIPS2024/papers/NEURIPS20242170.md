## Problem

State-of-the-art 3D GANs rely on ray casting-based volume rendering, which requires O(H×W×D) point sampling per view and limits training to lower resolutions or forces architectural compromises that degrade quality. 3D Gaussian Splatting offers ~100× faster rasterization-based rendering, but its standard training pipeline depends on scene-specific SfM initialization and non-differentiable densification, making it incompatible with the single-generator adversarial framework. A naive Gaussian generator in a GAN setting diverges or produces elongated-Gaussian artifacts because nothing constrains the positions or scales of the synthesized splats.

## Input / Output

The generator consumes a latent code z (mapped to a style code w via a mapping network) and a camera pose θ, and produces a hierarchical set of 3D Gaussians — 87K at 256×256 and 349K at 512×512 — organized across L=5 or L=6 levels with an upsampling ratio r=4 per level. These Gaussians are rendered to a 2D RGB image via a tile-based rasterizer with depth-sorted α-blending. A separate, lower-capacity background generator produces 10K Gaussians constrained to a sphere of radius 3, while foreground Gaussians reside in the [-1,1]³ cube.

## Method

GSGAN introduces a hierarchical Gaussian representation in which each finer-level Gaussian is parameterized relative to its coarser-level parent: position is expressed in the parent's local coordinate frame (locality constraint), scale monotonically decreases via a negative residual plus a resolution-dependent constant Δs, and opacity/quaternion/color use additive residuals. To decouple regularization from rendering, the generator also synthesizes a parallel set of anchor Gaussians that only feed into the densify operation, preventing near-zero-variance scales from over-constraining child positions. The generator itself is a stack of transformer blocks (self-attention at coarse levels, local KNN attention at fine levels) conditioned by AdaIN and adaptive layerscale, with a toGaus output head that applies tanh to local positions and −softplus to scale differences. Training combines non-saturating adversarial loss with R1 regularization, a pose-contrastive objective linking image embeddings to camera parameters, and center/KNN losses on the coarsest-level anchor positions to keep the scene centered and compact.

## Results

On FFHQ-512, GSGAN achieves a FID of 5.60, slightly behind the state-of-the-art Mimic3D (5.37) but substantially ahead of GRAM-HD (10.4) and EpiGRAF (9.92). On AFHQ-Cat-512, GSGAN achieves a FID of 3.79, outperforming Mimic3D (4.29) and approaching the non-3D-consistent EG3D baseline (2.72). The headline advantage is rendering speed: GSGAN renders at 3.0 ms per frame at 512×512, which is ~134× faster than Mimic3D (402.1 ms) and ~66× faster than GRAM-HD (197.9 ms), while maintaining strict 3D consistency. Training cost is also reduced: 28 RTX A6000 days versus 64 A100 days for Mimic3D on FFHQ-512.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest 3D-consistent baseline Mimic3D, GSGAN trades a small FID gap on FFHQ-512 (5.60 vs 5.37) for a 134× rendering speedup (3.0 ms vs 402.1 ms) and a lower FID on AFHQ-Cat-512 (3.79 vs 4.29). Compared to GRAM-HD, GSGAN improves FID by 4.8 points on FFHQ-256 (6.59 vs 10.4) and 3.9 points on AFHQ-Cat-512 (3.79 vs 7.67), while rendering 66× faster. Against EpiGRAF and VoxGRAF, GSGAN achieves substantially lower FID (5.60 vs 9.92 on FFHQ-512; 3.43 vs 6.93 on AFHQ-Cat-256). On 3D consistency (NeuS2 reconstruction), GSGAN outperforms GRAM-HD by 7.2 dB PSNR on FFHQ-256 (41.69 vs 34.45) and 5.7 dB on FFHQ-512 (37.85 vs 32.15), while matching Mimic3D within 0.01 SSIM on FFHQ-256 (0.9883 vs 0.9927). The non-3D-consistent EG3D baseline still holds a FID advantage (4.70 vs 5.60 on FFHQ-512), but GSGAN renders 5× faster than EG3D's 15.5 ms and provides true multi-view 3D consistency that EG3D lacks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper transplants 3D Gaussian Splatting (3D-GS) from the reconstruction and text-to-3D literature into the 3D GAN framework, replacing the ray-casting volume rendering used by EG3D, GRAM, EpiGRAF, VoxGRAF, and Mimic3D with a tile-based rasterizer. The key departure is that 3D-GS traditionally requires SfM initialization and heuristic densification, neither of which is available in a single-generator adversarial setting; the authors address this gap with a hierarchical Gaussian representation where finer-level Gaussians are parameterized by coarser-level counterparts via locality-constrained positions and monotonically decreasing scales. The anchor Gaussian mechanism—decoupling the regularization role from the rendering role—has no direct precedent in prior 3D-GS or 3D GAN work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ~100× rendering speedup (3 ms vs. 402 ms at 512×512) removes the primary computational bottleneck of 3D GANs, making high-resolution 3D-consistent generation far more tractable and opening the door to applications (interactive editing, large-scale 3D content creation) that were previously impractical. The hierarchical regularization design is a general principle—constraining a set of free parameters through a coarse-to-fine dependency chain—that could transfer to other Gaussian-based or point-based generative models. Key limitations remain: the fixed Gaussian count precludes scene-adaptive complexity, the scale hyperparameter Δs requires manual tuning per resolution, and evaluation is confined to face and cat datasets, leaving open whether the approach generalizes to arbitrary object categories or unbounded scenes.
