## Problem

While numerous deep learning methods have been developed for general protein binding site prediction, their applicability to antibody-specific epitope prediction remains largely unexplored, and existing benchmark datasets are too small (e.g., 582 complexes in Bepipred-3.0) and lack epitope diversity to properly evaluate generalizability. Current methods achieve a ceiling MCC of only 0.06, and no consistent evaluation pipeline exists that accounts for the structural specificity of antibody-antigen interactions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240373/cropped_page4_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pair of disjoint molecular graphs: an antibody graph built from CDR residues of both heavy and light chains, and an antigen graph built from surface residues (those with non-zero solvent-accessible surface area). Edges in both graphs connect residues whose non-hydrogen atoms are within 4.5 Å, and node features come from protein language model embeddings (AntiBERTy for antibody, ESM2-650M for antigen). The model produces two outputs: binary node-level labels identifying epitope residues on the antigen, and binary edge-level labels for the bipartite residue-residue contact graph between antibody and antigen.

## Method

WALLE processes the antibody and antigen graphs through separate GNN pathways (GCN, GAT, or GraphSAGE with 2 layers), each projecting PLM embeddings from their native dimension to 128 then to 64, before a decoder (inner product or linear layer with dropout) predicts bipartite edge probabilities. Node-level epitope labels are derived by summing predicted probabilities of all edges incident to each antigen node and applying a learned threshold. Training minimizes a weighted binary cross-entropy loss on bipartite edges plus an L1 regularizer penalizing deviation from the mean number of positive contacts (~43) observed in the training set. The key design insight is that combining sequential context from protein language models with geometric neighborhood information from graph convolutions yields 3–10× MCC improvement over baselines that use only one modality, as confirmed by ablation studies removing either the GNN layers or the PLM embeddings.

## Results

On the AsEP dataset (1,723 antibody-antigen complexes), WALLE achieves an MCC of 0.305 and F1 of 0.357 on the standard epitope-to-antigen-surface-ratio split, and MCC 0.152 / F1 0.204 on the harder epitope-group split. These represent 3–10× improvements in MCC over the best baseline (MaSIF-site at MCC 0.037 on the standard split). On the bipartite link prediction task, WALLE reaches MCC 0.131 (standard split) and 0.063 (epitope-group split), establishing the first baseline for this finer-grained formulation. AlphaFold2-Multimer, evaluated on a 76-complex subset excluded from its training data, achieves MCC 0.262 but requires ~1.66 hours per prediction versus seconds for WALLE.

## Baselines & Comparisons

WALLE outperforms all four main baselines across every metric on both dataset splits. On the standard split, WALLE's MCC of 0.305 is roughly 8× that of MaSIF-site (0.037), 10× that of EpiPred (0.029), 11× that of ESMFold (0.028), and ~19× that of ESMBind (0.016). The gap narrows on the more challenging epitope-group split, where WALLE (MCC 0.152) still leads MaSIF-site (0.046) by ~3.3×, but EpiPred drops to a negative MCC (−0.006) and ESMBind is near chance (0.002). AlphaFold2-Multimer, tested on a separate 76-complex subset, achieves MCC 0.262—competitive with WALLE's 0.305 but at a cost of 1.66 hours per prediction versus seconds. Bepipred3.0, a sequence-only method, scores MCC 0.110–0.140 across confidence thresholds on AsEP, well below WALLE, confirming that structural and antibody-specific information is essential for conformational epitope prediction.

## Relation to Prior Work

WALLE builds directly on the graph-based antibody-antigen interaction prediction framework established by PECAN and EPMP, which treat antibody and antigen as separate graphs processed through distinct GNN pathways. The key departure is the replacement of manually engineered PSSM node embeddings with pretrained protein language model embeddings (AntiBERTy for antibodies, ESM2 for antigens), and the reformulation of the prediction target from node classification to bipartite link prediction between the two graphs. This positions WALLE at the intersection of two previously separate lines of work: structure-based GNN methods (PECAN, EPMP, MaSIF-site) and sequence-based protein language models (ESMBind, ESMFold), which prior methods had not combined.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AsEP provides the largest antibody-antigen complex dataset with epitope-group clustering, addressing a critical gap that has limited method development and fair comparison in this area. The benchmark reveals that general protein binding-site predictors perform poorly on antibody-specific epitope prediction, and that combining sequential (PLM) and structural (GNN) features is essential — a design guideline for future methods. However, the significant performance drop on unseen epitopes (MCC 0.305 → 0.152) highlights that generalization remains an open problem, likely requiring inductive GNNs, larger datasets, or pretraining strategies with active forgetting. The bipartite link prediction formulation also opens a path toward interpretable, residue-level interaction attribution that node classification alone cannot provide.
