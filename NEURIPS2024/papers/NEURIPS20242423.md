## Problem

Learning constrained MDPs (CMDPs) with large or infinite state spaces under qπ-realizability—a setting where value functions of all policies are linearly representable but the transition model need not be—remains largely unexplored. Prior sample-efficient CMDP algorithms are confined to tabular or linear-MDP settings, and the local-access model (which allows restarting only from previously visited states) has not been leveraged for constrained problems. This paper provides the first polynomial sample complexity guarantee for CMDP learning in the qπ-realizable local-access setting.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes a local-access simulator (queryable only from previously encountered states), a known feature map φ: S×A → R^d, the initial state s₀, reward and constraint functions r,c: S×A → [0,1], and a target accuracy ε. It outputs a non-stationary mixture policy π̄_K that randomly selects one of K policies and follows it for the entire episode, ensuring strictly positive probability on every action at every state. Unlike standard RL outputs, the mixture policy is reconstructed from stored least-squares weights at each core-set extension rather than being stored explicitly across the full state-action space.

## Method

Confident-NPG-CMDP frames the CMDP as a saddle-point problem and alternates between natural policy gradient updates (softmax over estimated primal action-values Q^p = Q^r + λQ^c) and mirror-descent updates on the Lagrange multiplier λ. Data collection is decoupled from policy updates: the algorithm gathers on-policy rollouts only every m iterations (where m is chosen to bound the per-trajectory importance sampling ratio by 1+ρ₀), and between collection phases it reuses stored trajectories via off-policy least-squares estimation. A core-set mechanism incrementally discovers informative state-action pairs, and the policy is updated only for newly covered states, preserving the accuracy guarantee of earlier estimates for previously covered states. This design reduces the sample complexity from Õ(ε⁻⁴) (naive application of CAPI-QPI-Plan) to Õ(poly(d)ε⁻³) while handling both relaxed and strict feasibility, and tolerates a misspecification error ω with only an additive Õ(ω) degradation.

## Results

The paper provides theoretical sample complexity guarantees rather than empirical benchmarks. For relaxed feasibility, Confident-NPG-CMDP achieves a suboptimality of Õ(√d(1−γ)⁻²ζ⁻¹ω) + ε and constraint violation of the same order, using Õ(ε⁻³ζ⁻³d²(1−γ)⁻¹¹) queries to the local-access simulator. For strict feasibility (zero constraint violation), the sample complexity increases to Õ(ε⁻³ζ⁻⁶(1−γ)⁻¹⁴d²) queries. The paper claims this is the first result achieving polynomial sample complexity for CMDP learning under q_π-realizability, improving upon the unconstrained MDP bound of Õ(d/(1−γ)⁴ε²) from CAPI-QPI-Plan by incorporating the dual variable with only an additional ε⁻¹ factor rather than ε⁻².

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical experiments or head-to-head baseline comparisons. The paper positions its contribution relative to prior theoretical results: (1) Tabular CMDP algorithms (Vaswani et al., 2022) achieve Õ(SA/((1−γ)³ε²)) for relaxed and Õ(SA/((1−γ)⁵ζ²ε²)) for strict feasibility, but scale polynomially in state-space cardinality S; (2) Linear MDP CMDP results (Miryoosefi and Jin, 2022) achieve Õ(d³H⁶/ε²) but require the stronger linear transition model assumption; (3) The unconstrained q_π-realizable MDP result (Weisz et al., 2022, CAPI-QPI-Plan) achieves Õ(d/(1−γ)⁴ε²) but cannot handle constraints. Confident-NPG-CMDP is the first to extend polynomial sample complexity to the constrained setting under q_π-realizability, with the key algorithmic innovation being the use of natural policy gradient (softmax) rather than greedy updates to control the importance sampling ratio and avoid an extra ε⁻² factor from dual variable management.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the CAPI-QPI-Plan framework (Weisz et al., 2022) from unconstrained MDPs to the constrained setting by introducing a primal-dual structure with natural policy gradient updates and mirror descent on the dual variable. Prior CMDP sample-complexity results were confined to tabular or linear-MDP settings, where the transition model is assumed linearly representable; here the authors work under the more general q_π-realizability assumption, which only requires value functions to be linearly representable. The key algorithmic departure from CAPI-QPI-Plan is the use of a softmax (natural policy gradient) policy parameterization rather than greedy updates, which enables controlled off-policy importance sampling and avoids an extra ε^{-2} factor in the sample complexity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first result achieving polynomial sample complexity for CMDP under q_π-realizability, closing a significant gap between the well-understood linear-MDP CMDP literature and the more general (but harder) q_π-realizable setting. The algorithm's ability to handle both relaxed and strict feasibility, as well as model misspecification, makes it broadly applicable to safety-critical RL problems where the true value function may not be exactly linear. Open questions include whether the ε^{-3} dependence is tight, whether the local-access assumption can be relaxed to full online access, and whether the computational cost of maintaining multiple core sets and reconstructing mixture policies is practical at scale.
