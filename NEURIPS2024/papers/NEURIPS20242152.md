## Problem

Large pretrained multimodal models assume modality-complete inputs, yet real-world deployments frequently encounter missing modalities due to privacy constraints or collection difficulty, causing significant performance degradation. Existing prompt-based approaches like MMP insert independent prompts at each layer without modeling correlations between prompts across layers, between prompts and input features, or the complementary semantics of different modalities.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242152/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 224×224 RGB images (patchified at 16×16 by ViT-B/16) and text sequences (max 77 tokens via CLIP tokenizer), substituting zero-filled tensors for any missing modality. The missing-modality type (complete, image-only, or text-only) selects which of three prompt sets is applied. Output is a class label produced by a fully-connected layer over the concatenated task-related tokens from both the image and text encoders.

## Method

DCP designs three complementary prompt types prepended to input and intermediate features of a frozen CLIP backbone. (1) Correlated prompts are generated layer-by-layer via bottleneck MLPs that observe the preceding layer's prompts from both modalities (cross-modal concatenation), enabling hierarchical and cross-modal information flow. (2) Dynamic prompts are computed per-sample from input features through a self-attention module, adapting instructions to each input's characteristics. (3) Modal-common prompts encode shared semantics across modalities and are projected into each modality's space via MLPs, encouraging each encoder to focus on its unique features. Only the prompts (~4M parameters, 2.4% of the 151M model) and the final FC layer are trained, keeping the entire backbone frozen for parameter-efficient adaptation.

## Results

On the default 70% missing-rate setting (validation set), DCP achieves 54.24 F1-Macro on MM-IMDb (missing-both), 82.38 top-1 accuracy on UPMC Food-101 (missing-both), and 63.78 AUROC on Hateful Memes (missing-both). The method introduces only 4.0M extra parameters (2.4% of the 151M CLIP backbone) while consistently outperforming all baselines by 1–3 points across every dataset and missing scenario. Performance degrades gracefully from 0% to 100% missing rate, with the largest drops occurring in the missing-text case on MM-IMDb and Food-101.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DCP consistently outperforms all four baselines (CoOp, MMP, MaPLe, DePT) across all three datasets and all missing-modality cases at every tested missing rate (50%, 70%, 90%). The largest margin is over MMP, the most directly comparable method: DCP exceeds MMP by ~2.9 F1-Macro on MM-IMDb (54.24 vs 51.34 at 70% missing-both), ~2.8 accuracy on Food-101 (82.38 vs 79.56), and ~1.2 AUROC on Hateful Memes (63.78 vs 62.56). DePT is the strongest competing baseline, trailing DCP by 1.1–2.5 points depending on dataset and scenario. CoOp, which only prepends prompts at the input level, is the weakest baseline, lagging DCP by 3–5 points. Notably, on Hateful Memes the missing-image case causes the largest performance drop (unlike MM-IMDb and Food-101 where missing-text is more damaging), reflecting the dataset's emphasis on visual content.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends MMP (Lee et al., CVPR 2023), which first introduced missing-aware prompts to handle modality-incomplete inputs in frozen multimodal backbones. While MMP inserts independent, fixed prompts at each layer, DCP departs by introducing three structured prompt types: correlated prompts that propagate information across layers and modalities via bottleneck MLPs, dynamic prompts generated from input features via self-attention, and modal-common prompts that disentangle shared from modality-specific semantics. The design draws on the broader prompt-learning lineage (CoOp, CoCoOp, MaPLe, DePT) for vision-language models, but repurposes these ideas specifically for the missing-modality setting where the backbone must remain frozen and only a small set of learnable tokens is updated.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Missing modalities are a persistent real-world constraint (privacy, sensor failure, collection difficulty), and DCP offers a parameter-efficient solution (only ~4M extra parameters, 2.4% of the CLIP backbone) that consistently outperforms prior prompt-based and reconstruction-based methods across three datasets and multiple missing rates. The generalizability across backbones (CLIP, CoCa, ViLT) and the finding that models trained on missing-both cases transfer robustly to any single-modality-missing test scenario are practically useful. However, the work is limited to two-stream, two-modality settings, reports no error bars, and has not been validated on generative or retrieval tasks, leaving open whether the correlation design scales to more complex multimodal pipelines.
