## Problem

While LLMs can generate generic summaries that rival expert-written ones, it remains unclear whether these summaries align with the diverse, implicit preferences of ordinary readers. No publicly available dataset captures personalized summaries annotated by the general public with user profiles and source attribution, making it impossible to study how individual reading focus diverges from machine-generated or journalist-written summaries.

## Input / Output

The dataset consists of 441 Norwegian news articles (from 10 categories) each paired with multiple user-annotated personalized summaries (≥50 words) with explicit source sentences highlighted from the article, user demographic profiles, QA comprehension pairs, and GPT-4-generated generic summaries with sources. For the empirical study, the LLM receives a Norwegian news article plus 2–10 historical user annotations as in-context examples, optionally augmented with pre-extracted entities (via GPT-4o NER), plot components (event storyline, cause, result), or article position distributions. The model outputs a personalized summary intended to match the target user's interests.

## Method

PersonalSum is constructed through a three-stage iterative pipeline: (1) GPT-4 generates generic summaries revised by three Norwegian students who also highlight source sentences and create QA pairs; (2) Amazon Mechanical Turk workers (39 distinct annotators) write personalized summaries with sources, filtered by QA comprehension checks, minimum length, and time thresholds; (3) GPT-3.5-Turbo evaluates coherence, consistency, and relevance (score < 0.8 triggers human review), achieving 98% accuracy on high-relevance items. The empirical study benchmarks four LLMs (GPT-3.5-Turbo, Gemini-1.0-pro, NorwAI-Mixtral-8x7B-instruct, Llama3-Instruct) under few-shot in-context learning with systematically varied prompt configurations (Generic, Direct, Entity, Plot, Position, and combinations) using ROUGE, BERTScore, and a NorBERT-based entailment metric. A targeted Topic-centric PersonalSum subset (72 articles with ≥3 overlapping entities per HIT) isolates entity-driven personalization. Key findings: 5-shot prompting outperforms 2- and 10-shot, plot and article structure contribute beyond entities alone, and excessive user history introduces noise that degrades generation quality.

## Results

On Topic-centric PersonalSum with 5-shot prompting, GPT-3.5 Turbo achieves the highest ROUGE-1 of 40.22 (Plot prompting) and ROUGE-2 of 18.71 (All factors), compared to 37.61 and 16.95 for generic summaries respectively. All personalized prompting strategies outperform generic summaries across all three models on this dataset, confirming the personalization signal. On the main PersonalSum dataset (2-shot), improvements are marginal (e.g., GPT-3.5 Turbo ROUGE-1: 37.90 generic vs. 38.43 all-factors), and BERTScore (≈75) fails to differentiate between prompting strategies. The paper finds that 5-shot prompting yields the best results, while 10-shot performs worse than 2-shot due to noise from excessive user history.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against external summarization baselines; instead, it benchmarks different in-context learning prompting strategies (Generic, Direct, Entity, Plot, Position, and their combinations) across three LLMs. On Topic-centric PersonalSum, all personalized strategies consistently outperform the Generic baseline: GPT-3.5 Turbo gains +2.6 ROUGE-1 points with Plot prompting (40.22 vs. 37.61), and Gemini 1.0 Pro gains +2.17 with Entity+Plot (38.04 vs. 35.87). On the main PersonalSum dataset, however, improvements over Generic are marginal (≤0.5 ROUGE-1 points), suggesting that random article assignment dilutes the personalization signal. Among individual factors, Plot prompting is most effective for GPT-3.5 Turbo, while Entity+Plot is best for Gemini, and Plot+Position dominates for NorwAI-Mixtral. BERTScore remains nearly flat across all strategies (≈74–76), indicating it cannot capture the focus-level differences that ROUGE metrics partially reflect. Human evaluation (Fleiss' κ ≥ 0.71) confirms that personalized prompts produce summaries more aligned with user interests, with the main failure modes being excessive detail and divergent topic focus.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of personalized summarization and LLM evaluation. While prior work on personalized summarization has focused on product reviews (Amazon Reviews) or headline generation (PENS), and controllable summarization datasets (DUC, QMSum, MACSUM) use explicit control attributes, no existing resource captures ordinary users' implicit reading preferences over full news articles with source-level provenance. The paper repurposes the few-shot in-context learning paradigm for LLMs as a lightweight evaluation framework, using GPT-4o for NER and plot extraction to operationalize three personalization signals (entities, plot, position) without any fine-tuning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PersonalSum is the first dataset to pair user profiles, multi-annotator personalized summaries with source sentences, and machine-generated generic summaries for the same articles, enabling direct comparison of public readers' focus versus LLM defaults. The finding that entities/topics are only one of several key personalization factors (alongside plot and article structure) challenges the common assumption that named entities alone suffice for personalization. However, the dataset is small (1,816 summaries), Norwegian-only, and limited to few-shot prompting, leaving open whether these findings generalize to larger-scale or fine-tuning settings, and whether the implicit personalization signals can be captured at scale without manual annotation.
