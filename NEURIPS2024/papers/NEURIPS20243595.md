## Problem

AI methods for discovering formulas of mathematical constants have been ineffective because such formulas must be exact to infinite precision—there is no meaningful 'distance' between a candidate formula and a target constant, making standard optimization and search strategies inapplicable. This paper addresses the absence of a usable metric by showing that the convergence dynamics of a formula's rational approximant sequence (rather than its numerical limit or defining coefficients) encode rich structural information that can guide automated discovery.

## Input / Output

The method consumes polynomial continued fraction (PCF) definitions—pairs of integer-coefficient polynomials a(n), b(n) up to degree 2 with coefficients in [-5,5]—and produces a set of dynamical metrics per formula (irrationality measure δ, convergence-rate coefficients η,γ,β, and denominator-growth coefficients η',γ',β'), which are then used for unsupervised HDBSCAN clustering. Labels are assigned post-hoc via a pipeline that matches cluster members against 126 known anchor formulas and validates novel candidates using PSLQ against a library of mathematical constants. The final output includes 441 novel formula hypotheses, infinite formula families (e.g., for π and ln(2)), and a complete classification of PCF convergence behavior.

## Method

The core insight is that the trajectory of a PCF's converging sequence p_n/q_n—specifically the asymptotic scaling of the approximation error ε(n) ~ n!^η · e^{γn} · n^β and the reduced denominator q̃_n ~ n!^{η'} · e^{γ'n} · n^{β'}—provides a far more discriminative representation than the polynomial coefficients or the limit value itself (confirmed by Davies-Bouldin Index: 0.13–1.11 for dynamical metrics vs. 67.23 for the limit). To compute the irrationality measure δ without knowing the limit, the authors introduce the Blind-δ algorithm, which replaces |L − p_n/q_n| with |p_n/q_n − p_m/q_m| for m=2n, preserving the same asymptotic convergence rate. A companion δ-Predictor formula (Eq. 5) estimates δ from the eigenvalue ratio of the recurrence matrix, extending a prior result to all converging PCFs. Clustering is performed with HDBSCAN on the selected dynamical metrics, followed by a systematic labeling loop: known anchor formulas seed cluster identities, PSLQ validates or refutes constant assignments, and unresolved clusters are recursively split or flagged for manual investigation. The approach generalizes to higher-degree PCFs (up to degree 3/4) as a zero-shot classifier, correctly identifying novel formulas outside the training distribution.

## Results

The methodology was applied to 1,543,926 converging polynomial continued fractions (PCFs) with degree ≤ 2 and coefficients in [-5,5]. Using 126 known anchor formulas, the automated clustering and labeling pipeline discovered 441 novel mathematical formula hypotheses, with the most productive constants being e² (178 new conjectures from 28 anchors), π (116 from 39), e (80 from 44), and √17 (55 from 1). The Davies-Bouldin Index (lower is better) confirms that dynamical metrics dramatically outperform the numerical limit for clustering: the factorial coefficient η′ of q̃ₙ achieves 0.13, the exponential coefficient γ′ achieves 0.51, and the irrationality measure δ achieves 1.11, versus 67.23 for the limit value L. The approach was validated on a higher-degree test set (Aₙ up to 3rd degree, Bₙ up to 4th degree, coefficients in [-2,2]), where novel formulas were correctly identified and labeled despite being of mathematical forms unseen during training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not compare against external baseline methods in the traditional ML sense; the `compares_to` set is empty. The primary internal comparison is between different metric choices for clustering, quantified via the Davies-Bouldin Index. The dynamical metrics (η′ = 0.13, γ′ = 0.51, δ = 1.11) are orders of magnitude better than the conventional limit value L (67.23), confirming that convergence dynamics, not numerical values, carry the relevant structure for grouping formulas. The factorial coefficient η′ of the reduced denominator q̃ₙ is the single strongest metric, consistent with prior work by Elimelech et al. (2023) identifying η′≈0 as a strong indicator of mathematical constant formulas. The paper also contrasts its approach with prior exhaustive-search methods (Ramanujan Machine, distributed computation by Elimelech et al.) by demonstrating that the dynamical-metric clustering reduces the PSLQ search space by approximately 5,000×, making identification of constant links computationally feasible on the full dataset.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on polynomial continued fraction (PCF) analysis by generalizing the δ-Predictor formula from Ben David et al. (2021), which was restricted to balanced PCFs (deg(B)=2·deg(A)), to any converging PCF. It builds directly on Elimelech et al. (2023), adopting their finding that η'≈0 signals mathematical constant formulas and their Conservative Matrix Fields framework for generalizing discovered patterns into infinite families. The key conceptual departure from prior formula-discovery efforts (Ramanujan Machine, exhaustive search, ATP-based conjecture generation) is the introduction of convergence-dynamics metrics as the organizing principle for formula similarity, rather than relying on numerical proximity or polynomial coefficient space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first automated clustering of mathematical formulas, revealing that formulas sharing dynamical convergence properties tend to relate to the same constant — a structural insight that enables systematic discovery rather than brute-force search. The Blind-δ algorithm removes a long-standing practical barrier (needing to know the limit to compute irrationality measure), and the discovered infinite families for π and ln(2) are concrete mathematical contributions awaiting proof. Key open questions include: formal proofs for the novel formulas (Eq. 8, 9), the unexplained 'band structures' in the γ vs. γ' space (Fig. 2c), and whether the methodology scales to non-polynomial continued fractions or entirely different formula classes. The approach is currently limited to degree ≤ 2 with small coefficients, and all novel formulas remain numerically validated conjectures.
