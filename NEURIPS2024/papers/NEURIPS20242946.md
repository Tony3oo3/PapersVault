## Problem

Linear ensemble sampling is an empirically popular randomized exploration method, but its theoretical regret guarantees have lagged behind other randomized algorithms. Prior work required an ensemble size linear in T (Qin et al.) or achieved a suboptimal O(d^(5/2)√T) frequentist regret (Janz et al.), falling short of the O(d^(3/2)√T) bound attained by Thompson Sampling and Perturbed-History Exploration. This paper closes that gap by proving a matching O(d^(3/2)√T) regret with only a logarithmic ensemble size.

## Input / Output

At each of T time steps, the agent observes a finite arm set X ⊂ R^d with ‖x‖₂ ≤ 1, selects an arm, and receives a scalar reward Y_t = X_t^⊤θ* + η_t where η_t is conditionally σ-subGaussian. The algorithm maintains m parallel ridge-regression estimators, each perturbed by an initial Gaussian vector W^j ~ N(0, λβ_T²I_d) and per-step reward perturbations Z_t^j ~ N(0, β_T²). The output is the selected arm X_t = argmax_{x∈X} x^⊤θ_{t-1}^{j_t} where j_t is sampled uniformly from [m], and the theoretical guarantee is a high-probability regret bound independent of both K and m.

## Method

The core technical contribution is a general regret analysis framework (Theorem 2) that reduces the problem to verifying two conditions on any estimator: a concentration bound on ‖θ_t − θ*‖_{V_{t-1}} and a lower bound p on the conditional probability of selecting an optimistic arm. The key insight for ensemble sampling is that optimism only requires anti-concentration of the perturbation vector in a single direction U_{t-1} (determined by the history of selected arms), rather than in all d directions as prior analyses required. This decouples the dependency between the arm sequence and the perturbation, enabling a union bound over T^K permutation-equivalence classes of arm sequences instead of K^T sequences, which yields the logarithmic ensemble size requirement m = Ω(K log T). As a byproduct, the paper proves that LinPHE is exactly equivalent to linear ensemble sampling with m = T and round-robin model selection, immediately transferring the O(d^(3/2)√T) regret bound to LinPHE with no dependence on K.

## Results

The paper proves that linear ensemble sampling with ensemble size m = Ω(K log T) achieves a frequentist regret bound of Õ(d^(3/2)√T) (Theorem 1), matching the state-of-the-art for randomized linear bandit algorithms like Thompson Sampling. As a byproduct, the paper shows LinPHE is a special case of linear ensemble sampling with m = T, yielding a new Õ(d^(3/2)√T) regret bound for LinPHE that is independent of the number of arms K (Corollary 1). The regret bound does not depend on K or m even logarithmically, and the algorithm avoids the symmetrized perturbations used in prior work.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Compared to Janz et al. [10], this paper improves the frequentist regret bound from Õ(d^(5/2)√T) to Õ(d^(3/2)√T) — a factor of d improvement — while maintaining a logarithmic ensemble size and simplifying the algorithm by removing Rademacher symmetrization. Against Qin et al. [21], the improvement is both in the regret notion (frequentist vs. Bayesian) and in ensemble size (Ω(K log T) vs. Ω(KT)), though Qin et al.'s Bayesian bound has a different dependence on d and K. Compared to Lu and Van Roy [16], whose frequentist analysis is noted as invalid, this paper provides the first valid frequentist regret guarantee for linear ensemble sampling. For LinPHE, the paper's Corollary 1 gives Õ(d^(3/2)√T) independent of K, improving upon Kveton et al. [12]'s Õ(d√T log K) bound by removing the dependence on the number of arms entirely, at the cost of a √d factor in d.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two lines of work on randomized exploration in linear bandits: ensemble sampling (Lu and Van Roy, 2017; Qin et al., 2022; Janz et al., 2023) and perturbed-history exploration (Kveton et al., 2020). It builds directly on the analysis framework of Abeille and Lazaric (2017) for linear Thompson sampling, generalizing it into a concise, unified regret bound (Theorem 2) that applies to both perturbation-based and optimism-based algorithms. The key departure from Janz et al. (2023) is both algorithmic (removing symmetrized Rademacher perturbations) and analytical (exploiting a single anti-concentration direction rather than all directions), which yields the tighter O(d^(3/2)√T) bound. The paper also establishes, for the first time, that LinPHE is a special case of linear ensemble sampling when the ensemble size equals T, unifying two previously separate algorithmic families.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By matching the state-of-the-art O(d^(3/2)√T) regret of Thompson sampling with a logarithmic ensemble size, this result removes the last theoretical barrier to adopting ensemble sampling as a principled exploration strategy in linear bandits, and by extension in more complex settings where posterior computation is intractable. The LinPHE–ensemble sampling equivalence is a genuinely useful structural insight that allows cross-pollination of analysis techniques between the two families. Open questions include extending the results to non-linear reward functions, infinite arm sets, and general contextual settings, as well as understanding whether the O(d^(3/2)√T) bound is minimax-optimal for ensemble sampling (the authors conjecture it is, citing a negative result for LinTS). The general framework (Theorem 2) may also find independent use in analyzing other randomized or optimistic bandit algorithms.
