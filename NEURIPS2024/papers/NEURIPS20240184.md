## Problem

The manifold hypothesis—that high-dimensional data lies on or near a low-dimensional manifold—is widely assumed in machine learning, yet its impact on the computational complexity of learning neural networks remains largely unexplored. Prior hardness results for feedforward networks were established under i.i.d. Gaussian or uniform Boolean inputs, leaving open which geometric assumptions on the data manifold, if any, render learning efficiently solvable versus fundamentally hard.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240184/cropped_page4_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The theoretical framework takes inputs supported on smooth d-dimensional submanifolds of [0,1]^n with bounded reach (a global curvature measure) and target functions from the class of constant-depth ReLU networks with O(poly(n)) width and weight magnitude. In the learnable regime, the output is a polynomial-time PAC learning guarantee; in the hard regime, the output is an exponential lower bound Ω(τ² · 2^{n^Ω(1)}) on the number of statistical queries required. Empirically, the paper also estimates intrinsic dimensions of MNIST, KMNIST, and FMNIST (28×28 grayscale) using score vectors from a trained diffusion model.

## Method

The paper establishes a dichotomy based on manifold geometry. For the learnable regime, it shows that any sequence of manifolds admitting an ε-net with polynomially many samples (e.g., manifolds with bounded positive Ricci curvature, which via Bishop-Gromov have bounded diameter) guarantees learnability through a Lipschitz interpolation argument: the network's Lipschitz constant bounds the error between nearby points, so a polynomial-size net suffices. For the hard regime, it constructs 1-dimensional space-filling curves whose paths follow a Gray code, wrapping around 2^{n^Ω(1)} quadrants of the Boolean hypercube while maintaining reach O(n^α) for α < 0.5. On these manifolds, inputs are approximately Boolean with high probability, allowing a direct reduction from the SQ hardness of learning parity functions to the manifold setting. The tightness boundary is at reach ω(√n), beyond which volume is polynomial and the manifold becomes efficiently sampleable.

## Results

The paper's main contributions are theoretical: (1) learnability of constant-depth ReLU networks with polynomial sample and runtime complexity over efficiently sampleable manifolds (e.g., bounded Ricci curvature), and (2) exponential SQ hardness (Ω(τ² · 2^{n^{Ω(1)}}) queries) for single-hidden-layer ReLU networks over manifolds with reach O(n^α) for α < 0.5. Empirically, training succeeds on a d=10 hypersphere (learnable regime) with fixed 1000 samples across all ambient dimensions n, while learning fails on a 1D space-filling curve with reach R=0.5 when ambient dimension is large. Intrinsic dimension estimates via diffusion model score vectors reveal significant heterogeneity across image classes (e.g., FMNIST ranges from 125 to 596 across ten classes).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical paper without direct method-to-method comparisons; the 'compares_to' set is empty. The central empirical comparison is between two geometric regimes: inputs drawn from a d=10 hypersphere (learnable, bounded positive curvature per Proposition 3.5) versus inputs from a 1D space-filling curve with reach R=0.5 (hard per Theorem 3.6). In the learnable regime, the trained network achieves low test error with only 1000 samples for all ambient dimensions, even when target functions are drawn from the class proven hard under i.i.d. Gaussian inputs [36]. In the hard regime, learning only succeeds at small ambient dimensions and degrades as n grows, confirming the exponential SQ lower bound. The intrinsic dimension study (Table 1) is not a method comparison but a geometric characterization; it corroborates prior findings [77, 87] that real data manifolds exhibit heterogeneous intrinsic dimensions, placing them in an intermediate regime where neither the learnable nor hard guarantees directly apply.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the established line of SQ and cryptographic hardness results for neural network learning (under i.i.d. Gaussian or Boolean inputs, as in [36, 26, 48, 32]) to the geometric setting of data supported on low-dimensional manifolds. The learnability result draws directly from the manifold reconstruction literature [9, 43, 42], where efficiently sampleable manifolds (those coverable with polynomially many samples) are the standard assumption, and shows via a simple Lipschitz interpolation argument that this condition suffices for PAC learnability of ReLU networks. The key conceptual departure is identifying the reach (a global curvature bound) as the critical geometric parameter: when reach is O(n^α) for α < 0.5, space-filling curves following Gray codes can encode exponentially many Boolean quadrants, reducing the problem to known parity hardness, while reach = ω(√n) guarantees polynomial volume and hence learnability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes that the manifold hypothesis—widely assumed in ML to explain why high-dimensional data is tractable—does not by itself render neural networks learnable; bounding curvature alone is insufficient without volume constraints. The clean threshold at reach = O(√n) separating the hard and learnable regimes is a useful theoretical benchmark, but the authors note that real-world data likely occupies an intermediate regime with heterogeneous intrinsic dimension and curvature (as their preliminary FMNIST/KMNIST experiments suggest), where provable learnability remains open. This motivates future work on characterizing and exploiting heterogeneous geometric structure, and suggests that practical learnability may depend on properties beyond global smoothness assumptions.
