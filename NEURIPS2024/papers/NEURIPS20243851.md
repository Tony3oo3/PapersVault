key_contribution: A two-step boundary-conditional forward process for continuous diffusion models that (1) computes the exact stopping time where the deterministic ODE trajectory crosses the discrete decoding boundary and (2) rescales the forward and reverse trajectories to condition noisy sampling on that boundary, achieving state-of-the-art among continuous diffusion language models (up to 7.8% BLEU improvement) and a new SOTA for categorical image generation on CIFAR-10 (FID 10.99).
limitations: The method is designed exclusively for continuous diffusion models and does not apply to discrete-state-space diffusion processes. Image generation is only evaluated on CIFAR-10, leaving open whether the approach scales to larger or higher-resolution image datasets.

surprise_factor: The conceptual reframing—that the learned probability contours of a continuous diffusion model systematically deviate from the true discrete boundary, and that conditioning on that boundary via trajectory rescaling closes the gap—is clean and well-motivated, but not deeply surprising to anyone familiar with embedding-space geometry. ## Problem

Continuous diffusion models represent discrete samples (words, pixels) as single points in continuous space, but in reality each discrete token corresponds to an entire region (the set of all continuous vectors that decode to that token). The learned probability density contours deviate from the true discrete boundary, causing a mismatch between the model's learned distribution and the actual decoding geometry. This paper identifies the absence of discrete boundary guidance as a key reason for this discrepancy.

## Input / Output

For language tasks, the model takes a source sentence (translation) or source document (summarization) as conditioning input and produces a generated text sequence via deterministic 20-step sampling from Gaussian noise in the embedding space. For image generation, it takes pure Gaussian noise and produces a 32×32×3 CIFAR-10 image using 1000 diffusion steps. Discrete data is mapped into continuous space via token embeddings (language) or binary coding / pixel embeddings (images), and a confidence factor r∈[0,1] controls how strongly the discrete boundary conditions the trajectory (r=0 recovers standard diffusion).

## Method

The core contribution is a two-step boundary-conditional forward process built on the flow matching / ODE framework. First, boundary estimation computes the exact stopping time t₀ and position x_{t₀} where the deterministic forward trajectory crosses the discrete boundary (defined as the locus where the decoding likelihood for the target token equals that of the nearest competing token), using closed-form solutions for variance-preserving, variance-exploding, and optimal-transport coefficient functions. Second, trajectory rescaling re-anchors the forward path so that the noisy point at time t is sampled from a distribution conditioned on the boundary point, effectively compressing the trajectory from x_{t₀}→noise rather than x₀→noise. A confidence factor r linearly interpolates between the original and rescaled timesteps. Training uses an x₀-prediction MSE objective (an upper bound on the vector-field loss) plus a rounding cross-entropy term for language, and the reverse process is a deterministic DDIM-style sampler with an asynchronous trajectory-alteration step that tracks the evolving pseudo-target and rescaled noise.

## Results

On language modeling, the proposed boundary conditional diffusion model achieves 33.42 BLEU on IWSLT14 DE-EN, 26.69 on WMT14 EN-DE, 33.15 on WMT16 EN-RO, and 36.44 ROUGE-1 on GIGAWORD, establishing new state-of-the-art among continuous diffusion language models. With a simple reranking step, the model surpasses the autoregressive Transformer baseline on IWSLT14 DE-EN (35.02 vs. 34.31 BLEU) and WMT16 EN-RO (34.33 vs. 34.05). On CIFAR-10 image generation, the method achieves FID 3.86 with binary coding (comparable to DDIM's 4.04) and FID 10.99 with trainable pixel embeddings, setting a new state-of-the-art for categorical image generation (previous best: SDDM-VQ at 12.23).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all continuous diffusion language model baselines across all four tasks: it beats Difformer by up to 3.07 BLEU (WMT16 EN-RO: 33.15 vs. 30.08) and Dinoiser by up to 1.66 BLEU (WMT16 EN-RO: 33.15 vs. 31.49), and improves over DiffuSeq by up to 73.6% relative on WMT14 EN-DE (26.69 vs. 15.37). Against discrete diffusion baselines, it surpasses D3PM by 5.81 BLEU on IWSLT14 and SEDD by 1.55 BLEU on the same task. Compared to the autoregressive Transformer, the model trails by 0.89–1.32 BLEU without reranking but, with a 7-length beam reranking step, exceeds the Transformer on IWSLT14 DE-EN (35.02 vs. 34.31) and WMT16 EN-RO (34.33 vs. 34.05). For image generation on CIFAR-10, the method achieves FID 3.86 with binary coding (slightly worse than the original Bit Diffusion's 3.48 but better than DDIM's 4.04), FID 9.15 with fixed embeddings (vs. 12.96 for reproduced Bit Diffusion), and FID 10.99 with trainable embeddings, which is a new state-of-the-art for categorical pixels, beating SDDM-VQ (12.23) and D3PM-VQ (16.47) by 1.24 and 5.48 FID respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of continuous diffusion models applied to discrete data (DiffuSeq, SeqDiffuSeq, Difformer, Dinoiser for language; Bit Diffusion for images) by identifying a fundamental mismatch: prior work treats each discrete sample as a single point in continuous space, whereas it actually corresponds to a region (the set of all continuous vectors that decode to the same discrete token). The authors formalize this via a two-step forward process—boundary estimation followed by trajectory rescaling—built on the flow matching / ODE framework of Lipman et al. (2023) to make the boundary crossing deterministic and tractable. The Dirac-delta-based conditional forward process and the rescaled reverse process (a DDIM-like deterministic sampler) are the key technical departures from standard DDPM/DDIM pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework is general and plug-and-play: it can be layered on top of existing continuous diffusion architectures (transformers for text, U-Net for images) without changing the model backbone, which makes it practically attractive for practitioners already invested in diffusion-based generation. However, the method introduces a hand-tuned confidence factor r that trades off boundary fidelity against training stability, and the deterministic-trajectory requirement limits compatibility with stochastic samplers (Gaussian sampling degrades FID substantially). Open questions include whether the boundary estimation generalizes to hierarchical or compositional discrete structures (e.g., variable-length sequences, multi-modal tokens), whether r can be learned end-to-end, and whether the approach scales to larger image datasets beyond CIFAR-10.
