## Problem

Data-driven equation discovery methods such as SINDy and genetic programming search over large, unconstrained function spaces and are unaware of fundamental physical symmetries (e.g., rotational invariance, scaling), leading them to discover overly complex or physically inconsistent equations, especially under noise. This paper asks whether the Lie point symmetries of an ODE can be exploited to compress the equation search space and improve both the accuracy and parsimony of the recovered governing equations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242084/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes discrete-time trajectories x_{0:T} of an autonomous ODE sampled at a fixed interval Δt (with optional Gaussian noise and GP smoothing), plus a symmetry group G acting on the phase space X — either specified a priori as a known Lie group or learned from data via LaLiGAN. The output is a symbolic ODE ẋ = h(x) expressed as a linear combination of basis functions (for SINDy) or a tree of operations (for GP), with coefficients constrained or regularized to satisfy the symmetry. For high-dimensional observations (e.g., 100×100 reaction-diffusion grids), equations are discovered in a learned 2D latent space rather than the raw input space.

## Method

The key theoretical result (Proposition 3.2) establishes that a time-independent symmetry of an autonomous ODE is equivalent to equivariance of its flow map, yielding four infinitesimal/finite constraint equations (Theorem 3.3). For linear symmetries, the paper solves the constraint L_v W = W M_Θ(J_Θ(·)L_v(·)) explicitly as a linear system on vec(W), reducing the parameter space from d×p to r free parameters via SVD of the concatenated constraint matrix (EquivSINDy-c). For general or learned nonlinear symmetries where the constraint cannot be solved in closed form, a relative symmetry loss L_sym based on the Jacobian-vector product J_{f_τ}(x)v(x) − v(f_τ(x)) is added as a regularization term to the base equation-discovery objective (EquivSINDy-r / EquivGP-r). When the symmetry is unknown, LaLiGAN is first trained adversarially on trajectory pairs to learn a latent linear group action, whose infinitesimal generator is then extracted via automatic differentiation and fed into either the constraint solver or the regularization loss.

## Results

On the Damped Oscillator (20% noise), EquivSINDy-c achieves 90% joint success probability in recovering both equations, compared to 15% for SINDy and 0% for GP, D-CODE, and WSINDy. On the Growth System (5% noise), EquivSINDy-c achieves 100% joint success versus 3% for SINDy. Under the harder unknown-symmetry setting with symmetry regularization, EquivSINDy-r reaches 36% joint success on Lotka-Volterra (99% noise) versus 24% for SINDy, and EquivGP-r reaches 80% joint success on the Glycolytic Oscillator (20% noise) versus 14% for SINDy and 0% for GP and D-CODE.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EquivSINDy-c (linear symmetry constraint) dominates all sparse regression and GP baselines on the Damped Oscillator and Growth systems, achieving 90–100% joint success where SINDy reaches only 3–15% and GP/D-CODE/WSINDy reach 0%. On the harder unknown-symmetry systems, EquivSINDy-r outperforms SINDy (36% vs 24% joint success on L-V; 28% vs 14% on Glycolytic) and WSINDy (36% vs 6%; 28% vs 4%), while GP and D-CODE achieve 0% joint success on both. EquivGP-r (symmetry-regularized genetic programming) achieves 80% joint success on the Glycolytic Oscillator, far exceeding GP (0%) and D-CODE (0%). On the high-dimensional reaction-diffusion system, the equivariant SINDy Autoencoder produces stable linear latent equations with lower prediction error than both SINDy Autoencoder (which diverges) and LaLiGAN+SINDy (which discovers inconsistent equation forms). Against the Neural-ODE-based TRS-ODEN on the Lotka-Volterra system, the symmetry-regularized discovered equations achieve lower long-term prediction error without requiring prior knowledge of time-reversal symmetry or energy conservation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) data-driven equation discovery via sparse regression (SINDy, Weak SINDy, SINDy Autoencoder) and genetic programming (PySR, D-CODE); (2) Lie group symmetry theory applied to differential equations; and (3) equivariance-based inductive biases in machine learning (equivariant networks, LaLiGAN for symmetry discovery). While prior work has exploited specific symmetries in narrow equation-discovery settings (e.g., reflections in POD coefficients, spatial rotations in fluid dynamics), this paper provides a general theoretical criterion—ODE symmetry is equivalent to flow-map equivariance—and derives two practical mechanisms (explicit linear constraint solving and symmetry regularization) that are agnostic to the specific equation discovery algorithm. The use of LaLiGAN to learn unknown symmetries before equation discovery is a novel pipeline composition, though each component is individually established.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The symmetry-informed approach offers a principled way to compress the equation search space, directly addressing the combinatorial explosion that plagues symbolic regression. The demonstrated improvements (e.g., 15%→90% success on the damped oscillator, 3%→100% on the growth system) are substantial and suggest that even modest symmetry knowledge can dramatically improve discovery reliability under noise. Key limitations include restriction to time-independent symmetries of autonomous ODEs, the requirement that the function library be closed under the symmetry action for the constraint approach, and the dependency on accurate symmetry learning when symmetries are unknown. Open questions include extension to time-dependent and non-autonomous systems, PDEs, and whether the regularization approach can handle higher-dimensional or partially observed systems where symmetry learning becomes more challenging.
