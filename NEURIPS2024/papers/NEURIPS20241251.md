## Problem

Existing generalizable 3D Gaussian Splatting methods (e.g., pixelSplat, MVSplat) depend on epipolar line sampling or plane-sweep cost volumes to establish cross-view correspondences, but these geometric priors become unreliable in non-overlapping and occluded regions of complex real-world scenes, producing artifacts and inaccurate geometry. This paper asks whether robust 3D perception for novel view synthesis can be achieved without any explicit epipolar constraints, using data-driven 3D priors instead.

## Input / Output

The model takes N sparse reference RGB images at 256×256 resolution along with their camera intrinsics and pose matrices, and in a single feed-forward pass predicts N×H×W per-pixel 3D Gaussian primitives (position, covariance, opacity, and first-order spherical harmonics). These primitives are merged across all views and rendered via rasterization-based volume splatting to produce novel view images. Intermediate per-view depth maps are also generated and refined during the alignment process, serving as a byproduct for geometry evaluation.

## Method

eFreeSplat replaces epipolar line sampling with a CroCo v2–pretrained ViT-B encoder and cross-attention decoder that perform self-supervised cross-view completion, yielding multiview feature maps that encode spatial correspondences without any geometric inductive bias. A 2D cross-view U-Net then predicts per-pixel coarse depths and Gaussian features from these feature maps. The key Iterative Cross-view Gaussians Alignment (ICGA) module runs for 2 iterations: it warps each view's features onto the other view's image plane via a homographic matrix derived from the current coarse depth, computes L2 and cosine similarity between warped and target features, and uses these similarity signals to update both the depth and feature maps, progressively harmonizing depth scales across views. Finally, aligned depths are unprojected to 3D Gaussian centers, a second U-Net predicts the remaining Gaussian parameters (scaling, rotation, opacity, SH coefficients), and all views' primitives are merged for rasterization-based rendering. The entire pipeline is trained end-to-end with MSE + LPIPS loss on RealEstate10K and ACID, using the CroCo v2 pretrained weights as initialization for the backbone.

## Results

On RealEstate10K, eFreeSplat achieves 26.45 dB PSNR, 0.865 SSIM, and 0.126 LPIPS, outperforming the prior SOTA MVSplat (26.39 dB PSNR, 0.869 SSIM, 0.128 LPIPS) by 0.06 dB in PSNR and 0.002 in LPIPS. On ACID, eFreeSplat reaches 28.30 dB PSNR, 0.851 SSIM, and 0.140 LPIPS, surpassing MVSplat by 0.05 dB in PSNR and 0.004 in SSIM. Inference time is 0.061 s per scene on a single RTX-4090, comparable to MVSplat (0.046 s) and pixelSplat (0.100 s). In low-overlap settings (overlap ≤ 0.5), eFreeSplat's advantage over MVSplat widens to 0.82 dB PSNR.

## Baselines & Comparisons

eFreeSplat outperforms all four baselines on LPIPS (perceptual quality) across both datasets, achieving 0.126 on RealEstate10K and 0.140 on ACID. Against the closest competitor MVSplat [9], eFreeSplat gains 0.06 dB PSNR on RealEstate10K and 0.05 dB on ACID, while also improving SSIM on ACID (0.851 vs. 0.843); MVSplat retains a marginal SSIM edge on RealEstate10K (0.869 vs. 0.865). Compared to pixelSplat [6], eFreeSplat leads by 0.56 dB PSNR on RealEstate10K and 0.16 dB on ACID. The two light-field baselines, Du et al. [12] and GPNR [49], trail by 1.6–2.3 dB PSNR and are also substantially slower (1.6 s and 13.2 s inference vs. 0.06 s). The gap widens notably in low-overlap scenarios: at overlap 0.5, eFreeSplat achieves 25.46 dB PSNR versus 24.64 for MVSplat and 24.96 for pixelSplat, confirming the advantage of epipolar-free perception in non-overlapping regions.

## Relation to Prior Work

eFreeSplat builds on 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) as its rendering and representation backbone, and on CroCo v2's self-supervised cross-view completion pre-training as its feature extraction engine. It departs from the dominant epipolar-prior paradigm in generalizable NVS (pixelSplat, MVSplat, GPNR) by replacing epipolar line sampling and plane-sweep stereo with data-driven 3D priors learned from large-scale cross-view completion. The Iterative Cross-view Gaussians Alignment (ICGA) module is a novel mechanism not present in prior 3DGS-based GNVS methods, addressing depth scale inconsistency without explicit geometric constraints. The paper also positions itself against purely geometry-free approaches (SRT, GS-LRM) by arguing that CroCo's cross-view pre-training provides targeted 3D inductive biases without the computational overhead of large Transformer decoders.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that epipolar priors, long considered essential for cross-view geometric reasoning in GNVS, can be replaced by learned 3D priors from cross-view completion pre-training, with particular gains in non-overlapping and occluded regions where epipolar lines are unreliable. However, the overall SOTA improvement is modest (~0.05–0.06 dB PSNR), and the method remains data-hungry and sensitive to distribution mismatch between pre-training and target datasets (e.g., failure on very close viewpoints). Open questions include whether fine-tuning the CroCo backbone on target-domain data can close the distribution gap, whether the approach generalizes to more than two reference views, and whether per-pixel Gaussian mapping can be augmented with scene-completion priors for occluded regions.
