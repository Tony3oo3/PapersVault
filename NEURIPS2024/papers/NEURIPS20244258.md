## Problem

Vanilla transformers produce promising but non-smooth (zigzag) imputations for sparse, noisy functional data, and standard remedies such as L2 parameter penalties, curvature penalties, or post-hoc smoothing fail to guarantee differentiability. The paper asks whether a transformer can be modified to inherently produce smooth imputations while maintaining or improving prediction accuracy over state-of-the-art methods like PACE.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20244258/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For each subject, the input is a set of irregularly-spaced noisy observations {(t_ij, y_ij)}_{j=1}^{n_i} with n_i ranging from 3 to 30, padded to a common length n_max and augmented with sinusoidal positional encoding to form a (1+p)×n_max matrix. The output is a smooth imputed curve evaluated on a fixed grid of M=101 equally-spaced points over [0,1], guaranteed to be continuously differentiable (C^1) as M→∞. The model is trained end-to-end with MSE loss on observed points plus a pinball loss at the 10th and 90th quantiles for uncertainty quantification.

## Method

SAND (Self-AtteNtion on Derivative) is a lightweight module stacked after a GeLU-activated transformer that operates in two steps: a Diff operator (self-attention without softmax, allowing negative attention weights to approximate f'(a)≈(f(b)−f(a))/(b−a)) learns the derivative of the coarse imputation, and an Intg operator (cumulative summation) reconstructs a smooth curve by numerically integrating that derivative. Removing softmax is the critical design choice, since derivative approximation requires both positive and negative weights, unlike standard attention which constrains weights to be non-negative. The number of hidden nodes h_d is set via the eigenvalues of the empirical covariance of imputations (Theorem 2), drawing an explicit connection to FPCA dimension reduction where h_d plays the role of the number of principal components needed to explain 1−ε fraction of variance. The full pipeline is trained end-to-end with no separate smoothing step, and Theorem 1 proves the output is C^1 on [0,1] as M→∞.

## Results

On simulated functional data (SNR=4, 40 basis functions), SAND achieves the lowest MSE across all sparsity levels: 146.5 (n_i=30), 410.9 (n_i=8–12), and 758.1 (n_i=3–5), outperforming the best baseline PACE by 23%, 9%, and 5% respectively. SAND also yields the lowest total variation (TV) in every setting, e.g., 164.6 vs. PACE's 187.1 at n_i=30, confirming its smoothness advantage. On the UK electricity dataset (n_i=30), SAND reduces MSE to 10.0 and TV to 15.7, beating PACE (12.8 / 19.0) and the vanilla GeLU transformer GT (10.7 / 20.6). On the Framingham Heart Study, SAND achieves MSE 0.019, a 75% reduction over PACE (0.076) and all other baselines except GT.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SAND outperforms all six statistical and deep-learning baselines on both MSE and TV across every sparsity level in the simulation study. Against PACE (the strongest traditional FDA method), SAND reduces MSE by 23% at n_i=30 (146.5 vs. 189.9) and by 5% at n_i=3–5 (758.1 vs. 795.5), while also achieving lower TV in all three settings. MICE and GAIN degrade severely under sparse observation (MSE >1400 at n_i=3–5), whereas SAND remains competitive. Among transformer variants, SAND beats the best penalized transformer (GT2T, MSE 160.6 at n_i=30) by 9% and the extra-attention variant ATT (185.1) by 21%, while also producing substantially smoother outputs (TV 164.6 vs. 171.9 and 220.0). On the Framingham Heart Study, SAND's MSE of 0.019 is 75% lower than PACE (0.076), FACE (0.075), and CNP (0.087), though PACE and CNP achieve slightly lower TV (0.04 vs. 0.09), suggesting they undersmooth relative to SAND.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the transformer architecture (Vaswani et al., 2017) to the task of imputing sparse and noisy functional data, departing from the classical FDA toolkit (PACE, FPCA, kernel smoothing) that relies on linear dimension reduction and from earlier deep-learning imputation methods (neural processes, GAIN) that model conditional distributions over functions. The key architectural departure is the SAND module, which repurposes the self-attention mechanism by removing the softmax (to allow signed weights for derivative approximation) and appending a cumulative-sum integration step, thereby enforcing C¹ smoothness end-to-end. Theoretically, the paper bridges the deep-learning and classical-statistics literatures by showing (Corollary 1) that SAND with a fixed number of hidden nodes is equivalent to a rank-constrained projection, mirroring the FPCA dimension-reduction perspective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SAND provides a clean, end-to-end differentiable imputation pipeline that simultaneously achieves lower prediction error and provably smoother outputs than both statistical baselines (PACE, FPCA) and deep-learning baselines (CNP, GAIN), which is practically important for downstream tasks like trajectory prediction in longitudinal studies. The theoretical guarantee of continuous differentiability (Theorem 1) and the explicit error-bound-to-hidden-dimension relationship (Theorem 2) give practitioners a principled way to set model capacity. Open questions include whether the approach extends to multi-dimensional or multi-output functional data, whether the M→∞ discretization assumption in Theorem 1 holds in practice for very short curves, and whether the two-stage design (GT followed by SAND) can be unified into a single architecture without the intermediate noisy imputation.
