## Problem

Safety-aligned LLMs remain vulnerable to harmful fine-tuning attacks (HFAs) where an attacker with access to model weights can fine-tune them to produce harmful content, effectively reversing safety training. Existing defences either assume the defender retains control over the fine-tuning process or only provide superficial protection that can be undone by stronger attacks. This paper seeks a defence that works even when the attacker has full weight access and the defender cannot intervene.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pretrained LLM (e.g., llama2-7b-chat) along with paired harmful and harmless Q&A samples where the same question/prompt appears in both sets (harmful answer vs. safe refusal), requiring a mask to exclude shared tokens from the noising objective. It produces immunized LLM weights that resist in-distribution HFAs while preserving general capability and trainability on harmless tasks. The defence is applied layer-wise to post-MLP, pre-residual intermediate activations across all transformer layers, using multi-kernel MMD with a Gaussian kernel as a differentiable proxy for KL divergence to estimate the distributional distance between harmful representations and Gaussian noise.

## Method

RepNoise is a three-component loss that reduces mutual information between harmful inputs and their intermediate representations across all layers. The adversarial loss performs gradient ascent on harmful samples to increase the transition probability barrier (derived from Achille et al.'s loss-landscape dynamics), while a stability loss on harmless samples prevents capability degradation. The key innovation is the noise loss, which pushes harmful representations toward Gaussian noise via MMD, directly minimizing I(X;Z) rather than just I(Z;Y) — addressing the failure mode where gradient ascent alone leaves representation structure intact and recoverable. The theoretical grounding (Theorem 1) shows that minimizing I(X;Z) minimizes the transition probability to harmful parameters by simultaneously increasing both the static potential and reachability terms. The defence's effectiveness depends critically on its 'depth': ablations show that freezing early layers eliminates protection entirely, confirming that information must be removed uniformly across all layers rather than concentrated in later ones.

## Results

On the harmful QA task (BeaverTails), RepNoise maintains harmfulness classifier scores of 0.08–0.13 across all attack settings (learning rates 3×10⁻⁵ to 8×10⁻⁵, 1k–10k samples), compared to the base model's 0.47–0.74. On toxic content generation (DecodingTrust/RTP), RepNoise achieves toxicity scores of 0.00–0.07 post-attack versus the base model's 0.40–0.74. Stability is preserved: RepNoise scores within 0.01–0.02 of the base model on TruthfulQA (0.37 vs 0.38), MMLU (0.45 vs 0.46), HellaSwag (0.57 vs 0.58), and ARC-easy (0.72 vs 0.74). The defence generalizes to unseen harm categories, keeping post-attack scores at 0.00–0.14 across five withheld harm types.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RepNoise substantially outperforms both formal baselines across all attack strengths. Security Vectors [70] provides some resistance at low learning rates (0.07–0.08 at 3×10⁻⁵) but degrades rapidly to 0.52–0.66 at 8×10⁻⁵, whereas RepNoise stays at 0.11–0.12. Vaccine [32] (both ρ=1 and ρ=10) offers essentially no protection, with post-attack scores of 0.28–0.76 that are comparable to or worse than the undefended base model. Among the ablation-style comparisons, gradient ascent and adversarial loss provide partial resistance only at the weakest attack (3×10⁻⁵ @1k: 0.38 and 0.26 respectively) but fail at stronger settings (reaching 0.74–0.77), confirming the paper's mechanistic finding that these methods leave harmful representation structure intact. Additional safety training (SFT on refusals) provides zero resistance (0.74–0.76 post-attack). RepNoise is the only method that maintains harmfulness below 0.14 across all six attack configurations, with a Mann-Whitney U-test p<0.001 confirming statistical significance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the information bottleneck framework (Tishby & Zaslavsky) and the transition probability model for learning task transfer (Achille et al.) to derive a principled loss function for removing harmful information from LLM representations. It departs from prior HFA defences (Security Vectors, Vaccine, Lazy Safety Alignment) that assume the defender retains control over the fine-tuning process, instead targeting the harder setting where the defender has no post-release intervention. The method also contrasts with mechanistic findings that safety alignment merely routes around rather than eliminates harmful representations, positioning RepNoise as an information-removal approach rather than a suppression approach.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first defence that satisfies all four immunization conditions (resistance, stability, generalization, trainability) in the no-defender-control setting, and its mechanistic analysis revealing that effective defence requires 'depth'—uniform information removal across all layers rather than surface-level changes in later layers—offers a useful design principle for future safety methods. However, significant limitations remain: the defence is defeated at higher learning rates (≥3×10⁻⁴), fails on out-of-distribution attacks (HEX-PHI), and requires paired safe/unsafe training data. Open questions include whether the depth principle generalizes to other modalities, whether the in-distribution restriction can be relaxed, and whether harmless fine-tuning can gradually erode the defence over time.
