## Problem

Existing unsupervised homography estimation methods (UDHN, biHomE, CAU) assume image pairs share the same modality or differ only in minor lighting, and they fail catastrophically on multimodal pairs (e.g., satellite vs. map, RGB vs. NIR). A naive fix—adding a modality-mapping encoder and training everything jointly—collapses into a trivial solution where the encoder outputs constants and the registration network predicts the identity matrix. The paper tackles how to simultaneously close both the geometry gap and the modality gap without ground-truth labels and without this collapse.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241959/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pair of unaligned images from different modalities (a moving image I^A and a fixed image I^B, typically 128×128 or 192×192 pixels) and outputs a 3×3 homography matrix H^AB that warps I^A to align with I^B. The framework is registration-network-agnostic: it can be plugged into any homography estimator (DHN, RAFT, IHN, RHWF) by replacing the supervised loss at each iterative step with the proposed unsupervised losses, requiring no ground-truth homography or pre-aligned pairs during training.

## Method

AltO employs a two-phase alternating optimization loop analogous to EM. In the Geometry Learning (GL) phase, the encoder and projector are frozen while the registration network is trained with a proposed Geometry Barlow Twins (GBT) loss that extends Barlow Twins to 2-D feature maps by treating spatial positions as the batch dimension, thereby enforcing local feature similarity at corresponding regions after warping. In the Modality-Agnostic Representation Learning (MARL) phase, the registration network is frozen while a shared ResNet-34 encoder (stem + stages 1–2) and projector (stage 3 + global average pooling) are trained with the standard Barlow Twins loss to map both modalities into a common feature space. Alternating between these phases prevents the trivial-solution collapse that occurs when all modules are trained simultaneously. The GBT loss operates on 32×32 spatial feature maps with λ=0.005, and the framework is applied at every iterative step of recurrent registration networks (RAFT, IHN, RHWF), replacing the ground-truth supervision at each step.

## Results

On the three multimodal benchmarks (Google Map, Google Earth, Deep NIR), AltO with IHN-1 achieves MACE of 3.06, 1.82, and 3.11 respectively, approaching the supervised IHN-1 performance of 0.92, 1.60, and 2.11. All existing unsupervised methods (UDHN, CAU, biHomE) fail catastrophically with MACE values exceeding 18 on most datasets, indicating they cannot learn meaningful homography on multimodal pairs. AltO with RAFT achieves 3.10, 3.24, and 3.60 MACE, and with RHWF-1 achieves 3.49, 1.84, and 3.22, demonstrating consistent performance across diverse registration architectures.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AltO dramatically outperforms all three existing unsupervised baselines. UDHN (28.58/18.71/24.97 MACE) fails on Google Map and Deep NIR because its pixel-level comparison is highly sensitive to modality differences; it only partially works on Google Earth where intensity distributions are similar. CAU (24.00/23.77/24.90) and biHomE (24.08/23.55/26.37) also fail across all datasets, as they are designed for minor lighting variations rather than true multimodal gaps. In contrast, AltO with IHN-1 achieves 3.06/1.82/3.11 MACE, a 7–14× improvement over the best unsupervised baseline. Compared to supervised counterparts using the same registration network, AltO shows a modest gap (e.g., 3.06 vs. 0.92 MACE for IHN-1 on Google Map), which the authors note is negligible in practical applications. The framework's architecture-agnostic nature is confirmed by consistent gains across CNN (DHN), CNN+RNN (RAFT), iterative (IHN-1), and Transformer (RHWF-1) backbones.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the unsupervised homography estimation line (UDHN, biHomE, CAU) to the multimodal setting by introducing an EM-like alternating optimization that decouples geometry alignment from modality mapping. It repurposes Barlow Twins from self-supervised representation learning, extending it to a 2D spatial variant (Geometry Barlow Twins) for the geometry gap, while using the standard form for the modality gap. The framework is deliberately architecture-agnostic, plugging into existing registration networks (DHN, RAFT, IHN, RHWF) rather than proposing a new one, and it departs from biHomE's reliance on ImageNet pre-training by learning a modality-agnostic encoder from scratch.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AltO provides a practical, drop-in replacement for ground-truth supervision in multimodal homography estimation, closing most of the gap to supervised methods across three datasets and four network architectures. The alternating optimization as a solution to the trivial-solution collapse is a clean, generalizable recipe that could transfer to other paired-image tasks with domain gaps. Open questions include whether a single-phase formulation can avoid the trivial solution without alternating, whether transformer-based encoders/projectors improve the modality-agnostic space, and whether the approach extends to larger displacements or non-planar deformations beyond homography.
