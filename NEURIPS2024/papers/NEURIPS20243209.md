## Problem

Cross-domain 3D semantic segmentation encompasses both domain adaptation (DA3SS) and domain generalization (DG3SS), yet existing methods address these tasks in isolation and primarily target synthetic-to-real or specific scene-layout shifts. No universal framework exists that simultaneously enhances encoder generalizability across diverse datasets while leveraging the powerful open-vocabulary recognition of 2D Visual Foundation Models (VFMs) for 3D point-cloud understanding.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243209/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes paired front-camera RGB images, LiDAR point clouds, and a 1-channel sparse depth map derived from LiDAR via perspective projection (repeated to 3 channels to match RGB). The 2D branch processes images through a frozen ViT encoder (CLIP or SAM, ViT-B or ViT-L) with SemanticFPN decoder, while the 3D branch uses SparseConvNet with 5 cm voxel size. Outputs are 3D point-wise semantic labels (primary) and 2D pixel-wise semantic labels (auxiliary), with a cross-modal ensemble 'xM' averaging predicted probabilities from both branches for final evaluation.

## Method

UniDSeg introduces a Learnable-parameter-inspired Mechanism into frozen VFM encoders, alternating two lightweight layer-wise modules: Modal Transitional Prompting (MTP) injects 3D-to-2D transitional prompts constructed from sparse depth embeddings and low-frequency-perturbed image embeddings via a shared MLP into the prompt space before each ViT layer, while Learnable Spatial Tunability (LST) applies low-rank learnable tokens (rank 32) in the query space after each layer to perform instance-level attention matching between prompted patch embeddings and tokens. Only ~1–2% of backbone parameters (1.82M for ViT-B, 4.70M for ViT-L) are trainable, preserving pre-existing target awareness from large-scale pre-training. Training uses cross-modal KL-divergence mutual learning between 2D and 3D auxiliary classifiers on source (and target, for DA) domains, with the overall loss combining point-wise segmentation supervision and weighted cross-modal alignment.

## Results

UniDSeg achieves state-of-the-art ensemble (xM) mIoU across all four cross-domain 3D semantic segmentation scenarios. On the most challenging A2D2/sKITTI setting, it reaches 57.5% mIoU for domain adaptation and 54.4% for domain generalization, surpassing the previous best (VFMSeg at 50.0% DA, MM2D3D at 43.6% DG). On vKITTI/sKITTI, it achieves 62.0% (DA) and 60.0% (DG) mIoU. The method requires only 1.5–2.1% trainable parameters of the VFM backbone (e.g., 1.82M out of 86.9M for CLIP:ViT-B) while outperforming full fine-tuning by 0.5–2.7% mIoU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the cross-modal baseline xMUDA [19], UniDSeg improves xM mIoU by 3.5–13.8% on DA3SS and 3.7–21.0% on DG3SS across the four scenarios, with the largest gains on vKITTI/sKITTI (synthetic-to-real) and A2D2/sKITTI (different sensor setups). Compared to the most recent VFM-based method VFMSeg [51], UniDSeg achieves a substantial 7.5% mIoU improvement on A2D2/sKITTI DA (57.5 vs. 50.0) and a 1.0% gain on vKITTI/sKITTI DA (62.0 vs. 61.0). Against MM2D3D [6], which uses a parallel depth encoder, UniDSeg outperforms it on all DG3SS scenarios (e.g., 54.4 vs. 43.6 on A2D2/sKITTI) and on 3 of 4 DA3SS scenarios, losing only on nuScenes:Day/Night DA (71.2 vs. 72.1). For the fully-supervised SemanticKITTI task, UniDSeg (65.6% mIoU) surpasses 2DPASS [52] (64.7%) and Cylinder3D [62] (65.9% is close), while in the source-free setting (Table 10) it outperforms SUMMIT [41] by 5.1% on USA/Sing. and 11.8% on A2D2/sKITTI. The ablation (Table 3) confirms both MTP and LST contribute independently: MTP alone adds 1.3–1.8% mIoU over frozen VFM, LST alone adds 2.1–2.4%, and their combination yields the peak performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the cross-modal mutual learning framework of xMUDA (Jaritz et al., TPAMI 2023) by replacing its generic 2D backbone with a frozen Visual Foundation Model (CLIP/SAM ViT) augmented by two lightweight learnable modules: Modal Transitional Prompting (MTP), which injects 3D-to-2D transitional knowledge via sparse depth prompts, and Learnable Spatial Tunability (LST), which uses low-rank decomposed tokens (inspired by LoRA) in the query space. Conceptually, it departs from prior prompt-tuning work (Jia et al., ECCV 2022) by grounding prompts in physically meaningful 3D geometry rather than generic pixel perturbations, and from full fine-tuning approaches by preserving the pre-trained VFM's open-vocabulary recognition while adding only ~1–2% trainable parameters. The low-frequency amplitude perturbation borrows from FDA (Yang et al., CVPR 2020) to regularize style invariance during training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in demonstrating that a single frozen VFM encoder with minimal learnable adapters can simultaneously handle both domain adaptation and domain generalization for 3D semantic segmentation, eliminating the need for task-specific architectural redesigns. The finding that DG performance (without any target-domain data) approaches or matches DA performance (with target data) on several benchmarks suggests VFM priors carry substantial domain-agnostic knowledge. A notable limitation is that pseudo-label re-training fails to improve 2D predictions, indicating the limited learnable parameters cannot absorb additional target-domain supervision—a tension between parameter efficiency and adaptability that remains open. Future work could explore scaling the learnable capacity or combining VFM priors with stronger target-domain self-training signals.
