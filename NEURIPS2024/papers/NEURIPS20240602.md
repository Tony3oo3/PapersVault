## Problem

Computing spectral projectors on invariant subspaces of Hermitian definite generalized eigenvalue problems (GEPs) with provable forward-error guarantees in nearly matrix multiplication time was an open problem. Prior work achieved backward-error diagonalization in Õ(n^ω) time but required spectral simplicity assumptions and unknown eigenvector condition numbers, and no end-to-end forward-error bound existed for arbitrary invariant subspaces. Additionally, the Cholesky factorization—a natural tool for exploiting the Hermitian structure of definite GEPs—lacked a stable fast-arithmetic analysis for general dense matrices.

## Input / Output

The algorithm takes a Hermitian definite pencil (H, S) of size n with ‖H‖, ‖S⁻¹‖ ≤ 1, a gap index k ∈ [n−1], and an accuracy parameter ε ∈ (0,1), and returns a matrix Π̃_k satisfying ‖Π̃_k − Π_k‖₂ ≤ ε with probability at least 1−1/n, where Π_k is the true spectral projector onto the invariant subspace of the k smallest (or largest) generalized eigenvalues. The bit complexity is O(n^{ω+η} polylog(n, ε⁻¹, κ(S), gap_k⁻¹)), and the algorithm internally generates only Õ(n) standard normal random numbers. For PCA applications, a subsequent deflation step converts the projector into a k-column orthonormal basis C̃_k satisfying ‖X − XC̃_kC̃_kᵀ‖ ≤ (1+ε)‖X − XC_kC_kᵀ‖.

## Method

The method proceeds in three stages. First, a novel logarithmically-stable O(T_MM(n)) analysis of the Cholesky factorization (the first for general dense matrices in floating point) enables an accurate reduction of the GEP HC = SCΛ to a regular Hermitian eigenproblem L*HL(L⁻¹C) = (L⁻¹C)Λ. Second, a new eigenvalue-counting algorithm approximates the k-th spectral gap and midpoint without any diagonalization: it iteratively halves intervals using COUNT queries (how many eigenvalues fall below a threshold), with a random Gaussian perturbation ensuring eigenvalue separation via the Wegner density-of-states estimate, requiring only Õ(n) random bits. Third, once the gap and midpoint are known, the matrix sign function sgn(μ̃_k − S⁻¹H) is approximated via Newton iteration to produce the spectral projector, followed by deflation to extract an orthonormal basis. This counting-based gap estimation is provably faster by a factor of O(log n) compared to diagonalization-based approaches, since it avoids computing the full eigendecomposition.

## Results

The main result (Theorem 1.1) shows that the spectral projector on the k-smallest-eigenvalue invariant subspace of a Hermitian definite GEP can be computed with forward error ≤ ε in O(n^{ω+η} polylog(n, ε^{-1}, κ(S), gap_k^{-1})) bit operations with probability ≥ 1−1/n, where ω ≲ 2.372 is the matrix multiplication exponent. For the spectral gap computation specifically (Theorem 3.1), the counting-query algorithm requires O(T_MM(n) log(1/(δε·gap_k)) log(1/(ε·gap_k))) arithmetic operations, improving over the diagonalization-based approach by a factor of O(log n) in both the general polylog regime and the constant-parameter regime. For classical PCA (Theorem 4.1), the first matrix-multiplication-type bit complexity upper bound is established, and a similar bound is obtained for Block-Krylov PCA (Theorem 4.2).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is between its novel eigenvalue-counting-query approach (Theorem 3.1) and a diagonalization-based baseline (Proposition B.2, built on the EIG algorithm of Banks et al. [15]). In the regime where ε, gap_k, κ^{-1}(S) ∈ Ω(poly(1/n)), the counting algorithm requires O(T_MM(n) log²(n)) arithmetic operations versus O(T_MM(n) log³(n)) for diagonalization, yielding an O(log n) speedup. In the extreme case where all parameters are Θ(1), the counting approach needs only O(T_MM(n) log(n)) operations, matching or beating even a single call to the full EIG diagonalization routine (which costs O(T_MM(n) log²(n))). The authors conclude that, based on currently available algorithms, explicit diagonalization is redundant for computing spectral gaps and invariant subspaces. No empirical benchmarks are provided; all comparisons are in terms of provable bit-complexity upper bounds in the floating-point model.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the nearly-matrix-multiplication-time framework of Banks et al. [15] (sign function, pseudospectral shattering, diagonalization) and Demmel–Dumitriu–Holtz [39,40] (stable fast matrix multiplication and inversion), extending their backward-error diagonalization guarantees to a full forward-error analysis for spectral projectors of Hermitian definite GEPs. The key departure is a counting-query strategy for spectral gaps that replaces the full diagonalization required by [15,41], leveraging a Wegner-type smoothed analysis with only Õ(n) random bits rather than the Õ(n²) needed for pseudospectral shattering. A foundational new ingredient is the first provable O(n^{ω+η}) floating-point stability analysis for Cholesky factorization, closing a gap left by prior results that covered LU but not Cholesky in finite precision.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first end-to-end forward-error guarantee for invariant subspaces of definite GEPs in nearly matrix multiplication time, breaking the Õ(n³) barrier of classic eigensolvers and yielding new bit-complexity upper bounds for classical PCA, Block-Krylov PCA, and DFT density-matrix computation. The counting-query approach demonstrates that explicit diagonalization is redundant for gap estimation, saving a factor of O(log n) over the best prior diagonalization-based method. Open problems include reducing the bit-precision bottleneck inherited from the SGN algorithm, extending the analysis to sparse/banded matrices common in DFT, and adapting the Block-Krylov framework to distributed or streaming PCA settings.
