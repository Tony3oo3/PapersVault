## Problem

QAOA's required circuit depth for satisfactory performance is problem-specific and often exceeds the coherence limits of current quantum devices, yet the optimal mixer Hamiltonian depends jointly on the specific problem structure and the available depth p. Existing mixer design approaches (e.g., ADAPT-QAOA, multi-angle QAOA) require deep domain expertise, incur high per-instance computational costs, and lack generalizability across different tasks and circuit configurations. The paper addresses this by providing a convergence theory linking parameter grouping to effective dimension, and by building a learning framework that dynamically generates problem- and depth-tailored mixer Hamiltonians.

## Input / Output

The method takes a combinatorial optimization problem G (e.g., a weighted graph for Max-Cut or a 1D spin chain for TFIM) and a target circuit depth p as input. The problem is encoded as a directed acyclic graph G_C where two-qubit gates (Z_i Z_j) are internal nodes and qubits are input/output nodes, while p is encoded via sinusoidal positional embedding. The output is a generalized mixer Hamiltonian H_M = Σ_j β_j Σ_{i∈G_j} P_i, specifying per-qubit operator types (X or Y) and a parameter grouping partition {G_j}, which is then plugged into a standard QAOA solver to produce the approximate solution bitstring.

## Method

MG-Net employs an estimator-generator architecture trained in two stages. In Stage 1 (supervised), a cost estimator—comprising GNN branches for the problem graph and mixer graph plus a depth branch, fused by an MLP—learns to predict the achievable minimum QAOA cost from labeled (problem, mixer, depth, cost) tuples. In Stage 2 (unsupervised), with the estimator frozen, a mixer generator (two GNN branches feeding separate MLPs) learns to output the operator type per qubit (node classification) and the parameter-sharing structure (link prediction via Gumbel-Softmax) that minimizes the estimator's predicted cost. The theoretical foundation (Theorem 3.1) proves via representation theory that d_eff(FG) = d_eff(PG) ≤ d_eff(NG), establishing that parameter grouping reduces effective dimension and accelerates convergence, while the optimal grouping is both problem- and depth-dependent—motivating the dynamic generation approach. The generalized mixer Hamiltonian extends beyond Pauli-X to include Pauli-Y operators, and the parameter grouping is modeled as a binary edge-prediction task that avoids pre-specifying the number of groups.

## Results

On weighted Max-Cut, MG-Net achieves approximation ratios of 0.99 (6 qubits), 0.95 (16 qubits), and 0.96 (64 qubits), consistently outperforming all classical and quantum baselines. The advantage is most dramatic at 64 qubits, where MG-Net reaches 0.96 versus 0.91 for the Goemans-Williamson algorithm, 0.79 for Greedy, 0.19 for standard QAOA, and 0.0 for ma-QAOA. The cost estimator also demonstrates strong predictive accuracy, with estimated costs closely tracking actual QAOA performance across circuit depths and parameter grouping strategies.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MG-Net outperforms all five baselines across every problem size tested. Against the classical Goemans-Williamson (GW) algorithm, MG-Net gains +0.05 at 6 qubits (0.99 vs. 0.94), +0.02 at 16 qubits (0.95 vs. 0.93), and a substantial +0.05 at 64 qubits (0.96 vs. 0.91). Standard QAOA (fully grouped) degrades severely with scale, dropping to 0.35 at 16 qubits and 0.19 at 64 qubits, while MG-Net maintains 0.95 and 0.96 respectively. Multi-angle QAOA (ma-QAOA), which uses independent parameters per qubit, achieves 0.98 at 6 qubits but collapses to 0.0 at 64 qubits, whereas MG-Net's dynamic parameter grouping avoids this failure. ADAPT-QAOA is the weakest quantum baseline (0.75 at 6 qubits, 0.58 at 16 qubits) and is not evaluated at 64 qubits due to prohibitive measurement costs. The Greedy algorithm, while competitive at small scale (0.89 at 6 qubits), falls to 0.79 at 64 qubits, well below MG-Net's 0.96.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the QAOA framework (Farhi et al., 2014) by treating the mixer Hamiltonian H_M as a learnable, problem- and depth-dependent design variable rather than a fixed component. It builds directly on the parameter-grouping and spatial-symmetry analysis of Sauvage et al. (2022) and the over-parameterization convergence theory of You et al. (2022) and Wang et al. (2023), formalizing how effective dimension d_eff governs convergence across fully-grouped, partially-grouped, and non-grouped ansatz designs. Unlike prior QAOA variants (ma-QAOA, ADAPT-QAOA, DC-QAOA) that either fix H_M, search it per-problem with domain expertise, or lack depth-awareness, MG-Net introduces a unified GNN-based estimator-generator that jointly conditions on problem structure and circuit depth p, and unlike quantum architecture search methods (QAS, DQAS, QuantumDARTS) that must re-search for each new problem, MG-Net generalizes across a problem class from a limited training set.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a central practical bottleneck for QAOA on near-term and early fault-tolerant devices: the required circuit depth is problem-specific and often exceeds hardware limits, and the optimal mixer Hamiltonian shifts with depth. The theoretical result (Theorem 3.1) provides a principled, representation-theoretic explanation for why parameter grouping interacts non-trivially with depth, and the empirical observation that ma-QAOA degrades beyond p≈42 validates the over-parameterization threshold. Key limitations include the need for a labeled dataset to train the cost estimator (requiring repeated QAOA runs), restriction to early fault-tolerant (noiseless) settings, and current scope limited to QAOA rather than general VQAs. Open questions include extending MG-Net to noisy intermediate-scale devices, other variational algorithms, and reducing the training data requirement.
