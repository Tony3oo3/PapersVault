## Problem

Data-driven weather forecasting models are black-box systems trained on fixed lead times from datasets with a fixed temporal resolution (e.g., 1 hour), learning data-pair mappings rather than fine-grained physical evolution. This prevents them from generalizing to finer temporal scales (e.g., 30-minute forecasts) that fall below the dataset's inherent time resolution, creating a gap between medium-range forecasting and nowcasting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240734/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a global weather state of 9 atmospheric variables (surface wind, temperature, precipitation, and upper-air geopotential, humidity, wind, temperature) across 13 pressure levels at 128×256 spatial resolution. It produces a future weather state at an arbitrary lead time from 30 minutes to 5 days, where the lead time is injected as a learnable Fourier-embedded condition into the decoder. Notably, the model is trained only on hourly data pairs yet can natively output 30-minute forecasts without any frame interpolation, validated against independent NASA 30-minute satellite observations.

## Method

WeatherGFT stacks 24 HybridBlocks, each containing three PDE kernels (simulating 300 s of physical evolution via five atmospheric PDEs for u, v, z, q, T, with differentials computed by convolution and integrals by matrix multiplication) and one parallel Swin Attention Block, fused by a learnable router initialized at 0.5:0.5. The PDE kernel performs the major temporal evolution while the Attention Block adaptively corrects accumulated errors, with the router weight shifting toward AI as lead time increases. A lead-time-conditional Swin Transformer decoder, conditioned on a Fourier-embedded lead time, generates forecasts from the output of any HybridBlock. Multi-lead-time training uses outputs from the 4th, 12th, and 24th blocks (1 h, 3 h, 6 h) during training, enabling the 2nd block to produce a 30-minute forecast at inference—a scale with no training labels—by leveraging the physical evolution learned in the PDE kernels rather than pure data interpolation.

## Results

On the generalized 30-minute precipitation nowcast (a lead time with no training data), WeatherGFT achieves CSI@0.5 = 0.28 and CSI@1.5 = 0.17, outperforming all interpolation-based baselines (best CSI@1.5 = 0.13 from Keisler+UPR). At the trained 120-minute lead time, WeatherGFT reaches CSI@0.5 = 0.54 and CSI@1.5 = 0.40, surpassing the strongest baseline ClimODE+Flavr (0.47 / 0.34). For medium-range forecasting, WeatherGFT's z500 RMSE at 6 h is 21.78, beating the physical model ECMWF-IFS, and its t2m RMSE at 1 h (0.49) is the best among all compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

WeatherGFT outperforms all three data-driven baselines (FourCastNet, Keisler, ClimODE) on CSI@1.5 across every lead time, with the largest margins at the generalized 30-min (0.17 vs. 0.13 best) and 120-min (0.40 vs. 0.34 best) settings. ClimODE is the closest competitor, particularly at 120-min where it reaches CSI@0.5 = 0.47 vs. WeatherGFT's 0.54, but still trails by 7 points. FourCastNet and Keisler are broadly comparable to each other and consistently rank below ClimODE; their frame-interpolated 30-min outputs suffer from blurring that degrades extreme-value detection. On medium-range forecasting (Figure 4), WeatherGFT's z500 RMSE at 6 h (21.78) beats the physical model ECMWF-IFS, and its t2m/u10 surface variable predictions are the best among all methods tested. Notably, WeatherGFT produces 30-min and 90-min forecasts natively without any post-hoc interpolation, whereas all baselines require an additional frame-interpolation model (Flavr or UPR) to reach those temporal scales.

## Relation to Prior Work

WeatherGFT departs from the dominant black-box paradigm of data-driven weather forecasting (FourCastNet, GraphCast, FengWu) by embedding explicit PDE kernels as forward-pass components within the network architecture, rather than treating physics as a loss term (PINNs, DeepPhysiNet) or a general Euler kernel (ClimODE, PIHC-MoE). It also builds on the lead time conditional decoder concept from Nguyen et al. [43] and Andrychowicz et al. [1], and adapts learnable Fourier embeddings from Salimans & Ho [48] for temporal conditioning. The key architectural novelty is the HybridBlock with a learnable router that adaptively fuses PDE-based physical evolution with neural network bias correction, combined with a multi-lead time training strategy that has no direct precedent in prior weather forecasting literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work demonstrates for the first time that a model trained on hourly data can produce 30-minute forecasts without interpolation, bridging the long-standing gap between nowcasting and medium-range forecasting within a single unified framework. The router weight analysis provides rare interpretability into the physics-AI division of labor, confirming that PDEs drive evolution while neural networks adaptively correct accumulated errors. However, significant limitations remain: only five atmospheric PDEs are modeled (far from a complete dynamical core), experiments are restricted to 128×256 resolution, and 15-minute predictions cannot be validated due to lack of ground truth. Open questions include whether the approach scales to operational resolutions (721×1440), whether additional physical laws (radiation, microphysics, boundary layer) can be incorporated, and whether the sub-resolution generalization capability transfers to other physical simulation domains.
