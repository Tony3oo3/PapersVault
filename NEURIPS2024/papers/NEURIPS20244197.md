## Problem

Existing model-agnostic watermarking methods for AI-generated images face a fundamental tradeoff: encoder-decoder approaches (e.g., RivaGAN) offer strong detection but require expensive feed-forward passes that preclude real-time on-the-fly deployment, while frequency-domain methods (e.g., DwtDctSvd) are fast but vulnerable to adversarial watermark-removal attacks. Moreover, prior theoretical guarantees on false positive rates rely on IID Bernoulli assumptions about extracted binary codes that do not hold empirically, and no existing method provides distribution-free, provable FPR guarantees under adversarial perturbations.

## Input / Output

The method takes AI-generated RGB images in [0,1]^{C×W×H} format (e.g., 512×512 from Stable Diffusion) and produces a watermarked image of identical dimensions by applying a single forward FFT, two element-wise additions, and an inverse FFT followed by clipping to [0,1]. For detection, a potentially modified image (subject to rotations, crops, JPEG compression, Gaussian noise, or adversarial VAE/diffusion-model attacks) is fed into a ResNet-18 classifier that outputs a scalar score in [0,1], which is compared against a conformally calibrated threshold to yield a binary watermarked/unwatermarked decision. The entire watermark injection pipeline runs in ~5 ms per image on CPU, enabling batch processing of hundreds of images in under a second.

## Method

RAW embeds two learnable watermarks simultaneously: a frequency-domain watermark added in the FFT space (with a circular mask of radius 150) and a spatial-domain watermark added directly to pixel values, both scaled by visibility parameters c₁ and c₂. These watermark parameters are jointly trained with a ResNet-18 verification classifier using binary cross-entropy loss over a two-view contrastive augmentation scheme; the watermark is optimized with SignSGD (for data-level optimization stability) while the classifier uses standard SGD. For provable guarantees, the inference stage applies randomized smoothing to the trained classifier—computing Φ⁻¹(𝔼_{Z~N(0,σ²I)}[V_θ(X+Z)])—which yields a σ⁻¹-Lipschitz robust verifier, and then sets the detection threshold via the empirical CDF of smoothed scores on the watermarked training set with a DKW finite-sample correction. This combination of conformal prediction and randomized smoothing provides a distribution-free upper bound on the false positive rate that holds even when test images are adversarially perturbed within an ℓ₂-ball of radius γ, without any distributional assumptions on the data.

## Results

On MS-COCO, RAW achieves an adversarial AUROC (Ad-ROC) of 0.92 and a benign AUROC (N-ROC) of 0.98, with a watermark encoding speed of 0.0051 s/image (CPU), approximately 30× faster than frequency-based methods and 200× faster than encoder-decoder methods. On DiffusionDB, RAW achieves Ad-ROC of 0.90 and N-ROC of 0.98 with an encoding speed of 0.0078 s/image. Image quality is preserved with FID of 24.75 (MS-COCO) and 23.48 (DiffusionDB), and CLIP scores of 0.360 and 0.425 respectively, comparable to RivaGAN.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RAW substantially outperforms DwtDct and DwtDctSvd on adversarial robustness: on MS-COCO, RAW achieves Ad-ROC of 0.92 versus 0.54 (DwtDct) and 0.75 (DwtDctSvd), while also being 9–24× faster in encoding speed. Against RivaGan, RAW improves Ad-ROC from 0.81 to 0.92 (MS-COCO) and 0.82 to 0.90 (DiffusionDB) while reducing encoding time from 1.16 s to 0.0051 s (~200× speedup), with comparable FID and CLIP scores. StegaStamp achieves a marginally higher Ad-ROC (0.93 vs. 0.92 on MS-COCO) but at a severe image-quality cost: its FID degrades to 42.31 (vs. 24.75 for RAW) and CLIP drops to 0.291 (vs. 0.360), indicating that StegaStamp's robustness comes from injecting heavy noise that visibly distorts the image. On benign (unmanipulated) detection, all methods including RAW achieve N-ROC of 0.98–0.99, making this metric less discriminative; the key differentiator is performance under the nine adversarial manipulations and attacks evaluated in Table 3.

## Relation to Prior Work

RAW departs from the dominant encoder-decoder paradigm (RivaGAN, StegaStamp, HiDDeN) that embeds fixed binary codes in latent spaces, instead learning watermarks directly in both the frequency and spatial domains of the original image and jointly training them with a lightweight classifier (ResNet-18). The theoretical guarantees draw on conformal prediction (Vovk; Lei & Wasserman) for distribution-free FPR control and on randomized smoothing (Salman et al.; Levine et al.) to extend those guarantees to adversarially perturbed inputs, building on the authors' own prior backdoor-detection framework (Xian et al., NeurIPS 2023) for the proof structure. Unlike model-specific diffusion watermarks (Tree-Ring, Stable Signature) that require access to the generative process, RAW is model-agnostic and injectable post-generation, positioning it alongside classical frequency-domain methods (DwtDct, DwtDctSvd) but with learned, data-adaptive watermark patterns.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The combination of plug-and-play, on-the-fly deployment (30–200× faster injection than competing methods) with distribution-free, certified FPR guarantees under bounded adversarial perturbations addresses a gap that no prior watermarking method fills, as summarized in Table 1. However, the certified adversarial radius (γ = 0.001) is extremely small, limiting the practical scope of the theoretical guarantee to near-imperceptible perturbations rather than the stronger attacks evaluated empirically. Open questions include efficient fine-tuning between watermark-classifier pairs for multi-user deployments, the maximum number of concurrent watermarks in a single training session, and whether wider certified radii are achievable with better smoothing strategies. The IID assumption on test data relative to the watermarked training set also constrains real-world applicability where distribution shift is common.
