## Problem

Existing motion forecasting methods process each driving scene independently, ignoring the temporal continuity and contextual relationships between successive scenes that are inherent to real-world autonomous driving. This isolated treatment makes predictions suboptimal and the resulting systems inefficient for continuous deployment, where the ego-vehicle's motion forecasting must operate as a streaming process over time.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242483/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes vectorized agent trajectories (position, heading, velocity, acceleration over historical frames) and HD map lane segments, all normalized to the ego-agent's local coordinate system, plus accumulated scene context features and a memory bank of previously predicted trajectories from earlier time steps. A data reorganization strategy splits each original scene into 3 overlapping sub-scenes (3s history, 6s prediction each) to create continuous sequences. The output is 6 predicted future trajectory modes with associated probability scores, progressively refined across the sequence of sub-scenes.

## Method

RealMotion introduces two sequential streams operating at the scene level within an encoder-decoder framework. The scene context stream aligns historical scene features to the current coordinate system via Motion-aware Layer Normalization (MLN) encoding position, heading, and timestamp differences, then uses map-map and agent-scene cross-attention to progressively accumulate temporal context. The agent trajectory stream maintains a FIFO memory bank of n previously predicted trajectories, aligns them via rotation to the current frame, and refines current predictions using a lightweight Transformer decoder where Trajectory Embedding (a single-layer MLP on flattened trajectories) replaces positional encoding to measure spatial similarity between memory and current trajectories. The encoder uses PointNet-like pooling for map segments and stacked neighborhood attention for agents, followed by a Transformer encoder; the decoder is a simple MLP producing trajectories and mode probabilities. Training is supervised end-to-end with smooth-L1 regression loss, cross-entropy classification loss, and an auxiliary refinement loss on trajectory offsets, with gradients backpropagated across all sub-scene segments.

## Results

On the Argoverse 2 test set, RealMotion achieves state-of-the-art performance with minFDE1 of 3.93 and minADE1 of 1.59, improving 8.60% and 5.91% over the previous best (QCNet: 4.30, 1.69). It also leads on minFDE6 (1.24 vs. 1.29) and MR6 (0.15 vs. 0.16), while slightly trailing QCNet on minADE6 (0.66 vs. 0.65) and b-minFDE6 (1.89 vs. 1.91). On the multi-agent Argoverse 2 test set, RealMotion achieves the best avgMinFDE6 (1.32) and avgMinADE6 (0.62), outperforming Gnet by 9.6% and 10.1% respectively. On Argoverse 1, RealMotion attains the best minADE6 (0.61) but trails HPNet on minFDE6 (0.91 vs. 0.87).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RealMotion decisively outperforms the previous state-of-the-art QCNet [51] on the single-mode metrics (minFDE1: 3.93 vs. 4.30, minADE1: 1.59 vs. 1.69) and on minFDE6 (1.24 vs. 1.29) and MR6 (0.15 vs. 0.16), though it marginally trails QCNet on minADE6 (0.66 vs. 0.65) and b-minFDE6 (1.89 vs. 1.91). Against all other baselines in Table 1 (HDGT, THOMAS, GoRela, HPTR, Forecast-MAE, GANet, Gnet, ProphNet, etc.), RealMotion achieves the best minFDE1 and minADE1 by a wide margin (e.g., 27% lower minFDE1 than HDGT). On the multi-agent Argoverse 2 test set, RealMotion beats Gnet [11] on every metric (avgMinFDE6: 1.32 vs. 1.46, avgMinADE6: 0.62 vs. 0.69) and Forecast-MAE [5] (avgMinFDE6: 1.32 vs. 1.55). On Argoverse 1, RealMotion achieves the best minADE6 (0.61) among all listed methods but is slightly behind HPNet [35] on minFDE6 (0.91 vs. 0.87), suggesting the continuous-driving advantage is most pronounced on the longer-horizon Argoverse 2 setting. The ablation (Table 4) confirms that the data reorganization alone yields ~2.3% minFDE1 improvement, while the two streams together add a further ~6.8% over the independent variant RealMotion-I.

## Relation to Prior Work

RealMotion builds on the encoder-decoder architecture of Forecast-MAE and Motion Transformer, the vectorized scene representation from VectorNet, and Motion-aware Layer Normalization for cross-frame feature alignment. Its key departure from prior work is the shift from treating each driving scene as an independent one-shot prediction to a sequential, streaming formulation where a scene context stream accumulates historical environment understanding and an agent trajectory stream refines predictions using a memory bank of past forecasts. The data reorganization strategy—splitting trajectories into overlapping segments to create continuous sub-scene sequences—is a practical bridge between existing independent-scene benchmarks and the continuous setting the architecture assumes, and the authors demonstrate its generality by plugging it into QCNet.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper closes a notable gap between how motion forecasting is benchmarked (independent scenes) and how it operates in deployed autonomous driving systems (repeated, sequential inference), and the 8.6% minFDE1 improvement over QCNet with fewer parameters (2.9M vs 7.7M) and competitive 20 ms online latency makes the continuous framing practically attractive. However, the approach requires a sufficient number of historical frames for serialization, limiting applicability to datasets like Waymo that provide only 10 frames, and the authors acknowledge that existing benchmarks still underrepresent the rich temporal context available in real driving. A key open question is how to integrate this streaming framework into a full end-to-end autonomous driving stack where perception, planning, and control all operate continuously.
