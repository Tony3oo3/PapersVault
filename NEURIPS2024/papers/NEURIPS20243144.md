## Problem

Existing NeRF optimization does not explicitly model the distribution of training camera poses or the resulting triangulation quality, despite this being a well-known determinant of reconstruction accuracy in classical vision. This gap is especially critical in sparse, unconstrained view settings where limited input views make the system highly sensitive to noise and camera placement. ProvNeRF addresses this by modeling, for every 3D point, the locations from which it is likely visible (its provenance) as a learnable stochastic field.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243144/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained NeRF (e.g., SCADE or DDP) along with sparse training images and their camera poses, and performs post-hoc optimization to learn a per-point provenance stochastic field. Provenance samples are represented as distance-direction tuples (t, d) ∈ R₊ × D³, where t encodes the distance to the observing camera and d encodes the viewing direction (with length modulated by transmittance to handle occlusion). The learned field enables two downstream outputs: improved novel view synthesis via a transmittance-matching regularizer, and per-point triangulation uncertainty maps computed from the angular spread of sampled provenances.

## Method

ProvNeRF extends Implicit Maximum Likelihood Estimation (IMLE) to functional space (fIMLE) to model a stochastic field over R³, where each point x has a potentially multimodal distribution over provenance locations. The latent stochastic field Z is a random linear transformation of x concatenated with x itself, and a neural network H_θ maps each latent function sample to a provenance function sample via composition. The intractable fIMLE objective (involving L² integral norms between functions) is reformulated via calculus of variations into an efficient pointwise loss that compares empirical provenance samples against model predictions at randomly sampled scene points. The total loss jointly optimizes the NeRF's opacity/color fields and the provenance field, and for novel view synthesis an additional hinge loss (L_ProvNVS) enforces that transmittance along training rays matches visibility predicted by sampled provenance directions, effectively removing floaters without extra priors.

## Results

On the sparse, unconstrained novel view synthesis task, ProvNeRF achieves 21.73 PSNR / 0.733 SSIM / 0.291 LPIPS on Scannet and 20.36 PSNR / 0.663 SSIM / 0.349 LPIPS on Tanks and Temples, outperforming all baselines. For triangulation uncertainty estimation (measured by NLL), ProvNeRF achieves −3.05 average NLL on Scannet and −11.0 on Matterport3D, substantially outperforming Bayes' Rays (5.47 / 5.49) and CF-NeRF (660 / 507). The method also extends to 3D Gaussian Splatting, where it produces uncertainty maps with better correlation to depth errors than FishRF.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ProvNeRF outperforms all four NVS baselines on both datasets. On Scannet, it beats SCADE (21.54 PSNR) by 0.19 dB and DäRF (21.28 PSNR) by 0.45 dB, while also achieving the best LPIPS (0.291 vs. 0.292 for SCADE). On Tanks and Temples, the margin over SCADE is 0.23 dB (20.36 vs. 20.13) and over DäRF is 0.69 dB (20.36 vs. 19.67). The improvement over SCADE is attributed to the provenance-based regularizer (L_ProvNVS) that removes additional floaters without requiring extra priors. For uncertainty estimation, ProvNeRF achieves dramatically lower NLL than Bayes' Rays (−3.05 vs. 5.47 on Scannet) and CF-NeRF (−3.05 vs. 660), because it computes a fundamentally grounded triangulation uncertainty from classical multiview geometry rather than approximating the posterior. In the 3DGS extension, ProvNeRF's uncertainty maps show stronger correlation with depth errors than FishRF, with substantially better NLL on three Scannet scenes.

## Relation to Prior Work

ProvNeRF extends Implicit Maximum Likelihood Estimation (IMLE) from Li & Malik, originally designed for finite-dimensional distributions, to the infinite-dimensional setting of stochastic fields over R³, which they term fIMLE. It builds on the NeRF representation and its volume-rendering pipeline, but departs from the standard practice of adding deterministic auxiliary fields (semantics, normals, CLIP embeddings) by instead modeling a per-point probabilistic distribution — the provenance — that captures where each 3D point is likely visible from. This positions the work at the intersection of classical multiview geometry (triangulation quality, camera baseline effects) and modern implicit neural representations, filling a gap the authors identify: no prior NeRF work explicitly models the spatial distribution of observing cameras during optimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The provenance stochastic field provides a principled, geometry-grounded mechanism for quantifying triangulation uncertainty that is more interpretable than the entangled epistemic uncertainties produced by ensemble or variational NeRF methods. The post-hoc applicability to any pre-trained NeRF or 3DGS model makes it a potentially reusable component, and the preliminary 3DGS extension suggests broader representational generality. However, the ~8-hour post-hoc optimization cost limits real-time use, the NVS gains over strong baselines like SCADE are modest (~0.2 dB PSNR), and the hyperparameters for integrating provenance into downstream tasks are manually tuned per application, leaving open the question of adaptive, task-agnostic integration.
