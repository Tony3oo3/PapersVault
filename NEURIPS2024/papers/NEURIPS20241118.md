## Problem

Unsupervised outdoor point cloud registration lacks reliable optimization objectives: existing pseudo-label-based methods rely solely on low-level geometric cues, producing noisy labels due to incomplete scene perception, while indoor-focused methods depend on RGB-D data and differentiable rendering that are infeasible for large-scale LiDAR. The paper identifies that high-level feature representations and low-level geometric cues are complementary for inlier discovery and outlier rejection, but no prior unsupervised method integrates both.

## Input / Output

The method consumes two partially overlapping LiDAR point clouds (P ∈ R^{m×3}, Q ∈ R^{n×3}) acquired at distances of 5–50 m, where low overlap and density variation are the primary challenges. It outputs a rigid transformation T = {R, t} with R ∈ SO(3) and t ∈ R³. Internally, the teacher produces pseudo-labels consisting of dense correspondences C, sparse correspondences Ĉ (from downsampled views), and feature-space positive/negative anchors A₊, A₋ that summarize inlier and outlier feature distributions respectively.

## Method

INTEGER uses a two-stage teacher-student framework: the teacher is initialized on synthetic pairs (cropped fragments with periodic sampling to simulate LiDAR irregularity), then adapted per mini-batch via a two-pass Feature-Geometry Coherence Mining (FGCM) module. In the first pass, the teacher self-adapts using InfoNCE on hard samples (correspondences rejected by spatial compatibility but close to positive anchors in feature space); in the second pass, Feature-Geometry Clustering iteratively expands the correspondence set by selecting top-k candidates whose combined Euclidean/cosine similarity to the positive anchor exceeds that to the negative anchor, then filters with SC²-PCR spatial compatibility. The student is trained with Anchor-Based Contrastive Learning (ABCont), which injects the teacher's anchors as universal inlier/outlier representatives into an InfoNCE loss, making contrastive learning robust to pseudo-label noise, and with a Mixed-Density Student (MDS) that applies ABCont to both full-resolution and sparsely-downsampled views to enforce density-invariant features. Progressive training gradually extends the distance range of training pairs.

## Results

On KITTI, INTEGER achieves a mean Registration Recall (mRR) of 84.0%, outperforming the best unsupervised baseline EYOC (83.2%) and RIENet (50.7%), while remaining competitive with supervised methods like FCGF+C (84.6%) and Predator (87.9%). On nuScenes, INTEGER reaches 63.1% mRR, surpassing all supervised baselines (FCGF+C: 59.3%, Predator: 51.0%) and unsupervised EYOC (61.7%). In the generalizability setting (KITTI→nuScenes), INTEGER achieves 62.6% mRR, exceeding both supervised FCGF+C (60.3%) and unsupervised EYOC (55.3%). In the most challenging distant scenario (d∈[40,50)), INTEGER achieves 54.2% RR on KITTI and 30.2% on KITTI→nuScenes, substantially outperforming EYOC (52.3% and 19.7% respectively).

## Baselines & Comparisons

INTEGER outperforms the closest unsupervised competitor EYOC [Liu et al., CVPR 2024] across all three evaluation settings: KITTI (mRR 84.0 vs 83.2), nuScenes (63.1 vs 61.7), and KITTI→nuScenes generalizability (62.6 vs 55.3), with the largest gap in distant scenarios (RR@[40,50): 54.2 vs 52.3 on KITTI, 30.2 vs 19.7 on KITTI→nuScenes). RIENet [Shen et al., AAAI 2022] is substantially weaker, achieving only 50.7% mRR on KITTI and 47.1% on nuScenes, particularly struggling in low-overlap and low-resolution settings. Against supervised baselines, INTEGER is competitive on KITTI (84.0 vs Predator's 87.9 and FCGF+C's 84.6) but notably surpasses all supervised methods on nuScenes (63.1 vs FCGF+C's 59.3 and Predator's 51.0) and in the cross-dataset generalizability test (62.6 vs FCGF+C's 60.3 and FCGF's 60.1). The paper also shows INTEGER is agnostic to the choice of pose estimator within FGCM (SC²-PCR, FastMAC, MAC, PointDSC all yield similar tIR@1st Epoch scores of 79–81%), and is adaptable to different registration backbones (FCGF and Predator), though performance degrades with more complex architectures like Predator.

## Relation to Prior Work

INTEGER builds directly on the teacher-student pseudo-label framework of EYOC [12] and the neighborhood-consensus approach of RIENet [13], but departs from both by integrating high-level feature-space anchors alongside low-level geometric cues for pseudo-label mining. The anchor-based contrastive learning (ABCont) draws on proxy-based contrastive learning from COIN [41] and the mixed-density strategy from HINTED [48], repurposing them for registration-specific density invariance. The synthetic teacher initialization follows PointContrast [40] and periodic sampling from [39], while the spatial compatibility filtering leverages SC²-PCR [21]. Conceptually, the paper's key departure is the observation that inlier/outlier correspondences cluster around latent anchors in feature space, enabling a feature-geometry coherence signal that purely geometric methods (EYOC, RIENet) cannot exploit.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

INTEGER is the first unsupervised registration method to jointly exploit feature-space structure and geometric compatibility for pseudo-label mining, closing a gap where prior methods relied on geometric cues alone and produced noisy optimization objectives. The cross-dataset generalizability result (62.6 mRR on KITTI→nuScenes, surpassing supervised FCGF at 60.1) is practically significant for real-world deployment where retraining on new LiDAR hardware is costly. Key limitations remain: performance is bounded by teacher quality (especially in distant scenarios), and the iterative FGCM module adds computational overhead. Open questions include whether more robust teacher initialization strategies or non-iterative pseudo-label mining could further close the gap to supervised methods.
