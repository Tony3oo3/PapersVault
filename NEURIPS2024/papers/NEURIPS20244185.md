## Problem

Phylogenetic inference requires jointly optimizing discrete tree topology and continuous branch lengths, but existing variational inference methods either depend on pre-generated topologies (limiting search space) or optimize these two components separately. Additionally, prior methods require input sequences to be aligned to equal lengths and rely on explicit evolutionary models, overlooking rich sequence features that a pre-trained language model could capture.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20244185/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes raw, unaligned DNA sequences of variable length as input (no padding or equal-length constraint required) and produces a full phylogenetic tree comprising both a discrete unrooted binary topology and continuous non-negative branch lengths for each edge. Sequences are processed by DNABERT2 into fixed-dimensional genomic embeddings (768-d), which serve as the sole input to the tree construction and optimization pipeline, eliminating the need for explicit evolutionary substitution models.

## Method

PhyloGen frames phylogenetic inference as a conditionally constrained tree structure generation problem with three modules: (1) DNABERT2 extracts genomic embeddings from raw sequences; (2) an MLP maps embeddings to latent variables z*, from which a distance matrix (using XOR-based nucleotide mismatch) feeds the Neighbor-Joining algorithm to produce an initial topology; (3) a TreeEncoder/TreeDecoder pair refines the topology via variational inference while a DGCNN-based branch length module (using linear-time dual-pass traversal for node features and reparameterized exponential sampling) jointly optimizes branch lengths. A novel scoring function (MLP-based) is added to the multi-sample ELBO objective to stabilize gradient descent, and topological invariance is enforced through distance-based edge features in the DGCNN that are invariant to translational rotations in latent space.

## Results

PhyloGen achieves the highest Marginal Log Likelihood (MLL) and ELBO across all eight benchmark datasets (DS1–DS8, 27–64 species). On MLL, PhyloGen improves over the strongest Structure Generation baseline (ARTree) by 198 points on DS1 (−6910.02 vs. −7108.41) and by up to ~394 points on DS6 (−6330.21 vs. −6724.37). On ELBO, PhyloGen leads SBN by 104 points on DS1 (−7005.98 vs. −7110.24) and by 404 points on DS6 (−6324.9 vs. −6728.65). The method also demonstrates superior topological diversity (Diversity Index 0.89 vs. MrBayes 0.87, GeoPhy 0.36) and faster convergence than all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PhyloGen outperforms all seven baselines on MLL across every dataset, with the largest margins over GeoPhy (e.g., −6330.21 vs. −6735.14 on DS6, a gap of ~405 points) and PhyloGFN (−6330.21 vs. −6730.68 on DS6). Among Structure Generation methods, ARTree is the closest competitor, tracking MrBayes/SBN closely but still trailing PhyloGen by 198–394 MLL points depending on dataset. On ELBO (Table 2), PhyloGen similarly leads all baselines, with the most pronounced advantage on DS6 (−6324.9 vs. SBN −6728.65, a 404-point gap) and DS4 (−13113.03 vs. SBN −13331.83). VBPI and VBPI-GNN, which rely on pre-generated topologies from MCMC, achieve MLL values nearly identical to MrBayes/SBN but are not directly comparable since their search space is restricted. PhyloGen also demonstrates superior topological diversity (Diversity Index 0.89, Top 95% Frequency 149) compared to MrBayes (0.87, 42) and GeoPhy (0.36, 11), and its bipartition frequency distributions closely match MrBayes on DS1–DS3, confirming consistency with the gold standard.

## Relation to Prior Work

PhyloGen carves new ground at the intersection of pre-trained genomic language models and variational phylogenetic inference. Prior VI-based methods (VBPI, PhyloGFN, ARTree, GeoPhy) either require pre-generated topologies, aligned equal-length sequences, or optimize topology and branch lengths separately; PhyloGen departs from all three by using DNABERT2 embeddings to jointly optimize both via a reparameterized variational framework with DGCNN-based branch length learning. The framing of phylogenetic inference as a 'conditionally constrained tree structure generation problem' borrows the graph structure generation (GSG) paradigm from the GNN literature, while the Neighbor-Joining initialization and topological invariance constraint are adapted from classical phylogenetics. The scoring function for stabilizing ELBO gradients is a practical addition not seen in prior phylogenetic VI work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By removing the alignment requirement and evolutionary model dependency, PhyloGen lowers the practical barrier to phylogenetic inference and opens the door to applying pre-trained sequence models (currently limited to DNA) to protein and single-cell data. The consistent MLL/ELBO improvements over the MCMC gold standard (MrBayes) across all eight datasets, combined with higher topological diversity than MrBayes itself, suggest that language-model priors capture evolutionary signal that traditional likelihood-based methods miss. Open questions include whether the approach scales to thousands of taxa (NJ initialization is O(n²)), whether the exponential branch-length prior is sufficient for deep divergences, and whether the gains persist on noisier or more heterogeneous genomic data.
