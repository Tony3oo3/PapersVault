## Problem

Differentiable structure learning reformulates DAG discovery as continuous optimization in the graph space, but it has lacked a feasible way to incorporate partial order constraints—a common form of prior knowledge in real-world causal discovery (e.g., gene activation sequences, treatment protocols). The core difficulty is a hypothesis-space misalignment: partial orders are naturally defined over total orderings, while differentiable structure learning optimizes over weighted adjacency matrices, making direct constraint transfer non-trivial.

## Input / Output

The method takes observational data D ∈ R^{m×d} (tabular samples from a structural equation model) and a set of partial order constraints O = {(i,j)} specifying that variable X_i precedes X_j. It outputs a DAG structure as a weighted adjacency matrix W ∈ R^{d×d}, from which edges are extracted by thresholding |W_{i,j}| > γ. The module is plug-and-play: it replaces the standard acyclicity constraint h(W)=0 with an augmented version h'(W,O)=0 and can be dropped into any backbone solver (NOTEARS, NOTEARS-MLP, DAGMA) without altering the optimization procedure.

## Method

The key insight is that partial order constraints in the ordering space are equivalent to path-prohibition constraints in the graph space, but naively enforcing these prohibitions requires C(m,2) terms for a sequential ordering of length m, making it impractical. Instead, the paper computes the transitive reduction O⁻ of the partial orders, decomposes it into maximal paths, and for each maximal path o constructs an augmented adjacency matrix A(W,o) = W + τW_o − W∘W_o that injects the path's edges into W. The augmented acyclicity constraint h'(W,O) = Σ_{o∈P(O⁻)} h(A(W,o)) = 0 then simultaneously enforces both acyclicity and all partial order constraints. Theorem 1 proves this constraint is both necessary and sufficient: a graph is a DAG satisfying O if and only if h'(W,O)=0. Crucially, a sequential ordering of m variables contributes only one factor of h regardless of m, reducing the constraint complexity from quadratic to linear in chain length.

## Results

On the Sachs real-world dataset (11 nodes, 17 edges), PPO-1-11 (single chain of 11 nodes) with only 50 samples achieves SHD=12 and F1=0.57, matching or exceeding NOTEARS without prior on the full 853 samples (SHD=12, F1=0.39) — a 90% reduction in required data. On synthetic ER and SF graphs (d=20–50), PPO consistently reduces SHD and increases F1 over all three backbones (NOTEARS, NOTEARS-MLP, DAGMA), with gains growing as the ordering chain size increases from 50% to 100% of nodes. For multi-chained orderings, improvements are more pronounced with more chains, though runtime initially increases before decreasing as denser orderings reduce the number of maximal paths.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper evaluates its plug-and-play PPO module on three backbone algorithms: NOTEARS (linear, Zheng et al. 2018), NOTEARS-MLP (nonlinear, Zheng et al. 2020), and DAGMA (log-det acyclicity, Bello et al. 2022). On the Sachs dataset, PPO-1-11 consistently outperforms NOTEARS across all sample sizes, with the most dramatic gain at s=50 where F1 improves from 0.30 to 0.57 and FDR drops from 0.76 to 0.27. On synthetic benchmarks (ER-4, SF-4, d=20–50), PPO-NOTEARS achieves lower SHD and higher F1 than NOTEARS in nearly all settings, with the advantage widening as the ordering chain covers more nodes (e.g., PPO-NOTEARS-1-100 vs. NOTEARS on ER-4 Gaussian, d=50). PPO-DAGMA and PPO-NOTEARS-MLP show similar qualitative improvements over their respective backbones, though some degradation occurs on SF-4 graphs with NOTEARS-MLP and DAGMA. The method also outperforms the naive path-prohibition formulation (Equation 8) in runtime by orders of magnitude (e.g., 41.9s vs. 1475.3s at d=50, full chain) while achieving comparable F1 scores.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the differentiable structure learning framework initiated by NOTEARS (Zheng et al., 2018), which reformulated DAG structure learning as continuous optimization via an acyclicity constraint h(W)=0. While prior work has incorporated simple priors like edge constraints or strict time-series orderings (as in DBN learning), the integration of general partial order constraints into the continuous optimization setting had not been addressed due to a fundamental misalignment: partial orders are defined in the ordering space, while differentiable structure learning operates in the graph space. The paper bridges this gap by converting partial orders into equivalent path prohibition constraints and then efficiently implementing them through an augmented acyclicity term built from the transitive reduction's maximal paths, rather than the combinatorially expensive per-pair path prohibitions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first method to enable differentiable structure learning algorithms (NOTEARS, DAGMA, NOTEARS-MLP) to leverage partial order priors, which are common in real-world causal discovery (gene activation sequences, treatment protocols, seasonal patterns). The demonstrated 90% sample reduction on the Sachs dataset highlights practical value for resource-constrained experimental settings. Key limitations include the inability to strictly satisfy the augmented acyclicity constraint during augmented Lagrangian optimization (potentially violating some order constraints in output) and degraded efficiency for complex partial order structures with many maximal paths. Open questions include developing more refined optimization techniques that guarantee constraint satisfaction and more efficient gradient computation for multi-chain orderings.
