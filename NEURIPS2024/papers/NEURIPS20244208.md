## Problem

Existing urban change segmentation datasets rely on coarse land cover classification (typically 6–11 classes) and provide only bi-temporal snapshots, which cannot capture the fine-grained land use semantics driven by human activities or the rapid pace of urban change. This limits the ability to understand how human activities are distributed across urban landscapes and their environmental impacts, constraining the development of proper deep learning models for urban monitoring.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20244208/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each of the 62,752 patches contains two bi-temporal Google Earth RGB images (512×512 px, 0.2–0.5 m GSD) and 25 monthly Sentinel-1/2 composite images (128×128 px, 10 m, 14 bands) spanning August 2018–August 2020. The Sentinel patches are intentionally larger than and center-surround the high-resolution patches rather than being strictly aligned, providing broader spatial context. The model produces three outputs: 17-class pixel-wise segmentation maps for both time points, a binary change detection map, and a semantic change detection map, all supervised by pixel-wise annotations covering over 30 billion pixels.

## Method

FUSU-Net is a unified two-branch architecture that jointly performs segmentation and change detection by exploiting multi-temporal Sentinel observations. The first branch uses U-TAE with temporal attention to encode the 25-month Sentinel time-series into a compact temporal feature map; the second branch uses an ImageNet-pretrained HRNet backbone with separate ASPP heads to produce bi-temporal segmentation, followed by a subtraction and Spatial Pyramid Pooling head for binary change detection. A fusion module aligns the time-series features to the bi-temporal features via center-cropping, 1×1 convolution with upsampling, and a dilated-convolution bottleneck, then adds them element-wise. Training optimizes four losses simultaneously—multi-class cross-entropy for T1, T2, and time-series segmentation, plus a BCE change loss weighted 2×—so that the temporal branch is directly supervised by the T1 ground truth, providing an auxiliary signal that guides the high-resolution segmentation and change detection.

## Results

On the FUSU dataset, FUSU-Net achieves 50.10% mIoU on 17-class semantic segmentation, outperforming the strongest baseline HRNet (46.88%) by 3.22 percentage points. For binary change detection, FUSU-Net reaches 79.80% IoU, surpassing the next-best method DMINet (72.59%) by 7.21%. For semantic change detection, FUSU-Net attains 41.09% mIoU, leading ICIFNet (36.17%) by 4.92%. The ablation in Table 7 confirms that incorporating all 25 time-series Sentinel images yields the best performance across all three tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FUSU-Net outperforms all seven segmentation baselines on mIoU, with the largest margin over U-TAE (50.10% vs. 37.63%) and Fast-SCNN (50.10% vs. 35.56%), and a 2.36-point lead over the closest competitor Deeplab-v3 (47.74%). The advantage is most pronounced on semantically difficult classes: commercial land (36.69% vs. 33.21% for Deeplab-v3) and traffic land (74.79% vs. 70.83% for FCN). In binary change detection, FUSU-Net leads DMINet by 7.21% IoU (79.80% vs. 72.59%) and A2Net by 10.58% (79.80% vs. 69.22%), with the gap widening to 31.89% over BIT (47.91%). For semantic change detection, FUSU-Net surpasses ICIFNet (41.09% vs. 36.17% mIoU) and ChangeFormer (41.09% vs. 32.17%), while the weakest baseline SSCD-1 trails by 25.80 points. Cross-dataset experiments (Table 4) reveal a dramatic mIoU drop (from ~50% to ~10%) when training on one city and testing on the other, confirming severe domain shift between Jiaxing and Xi'an that no baseline adequately resolves.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of urban change detection datasets and time-series remote sensing. Prior urban change segmentation datasets (SECOND, Hi-UCD, LoveDA, DynamicEarthNet) focus on coarse-grained land cover classification (6–11 classes) with limited temporal coverage, while time-series segmentation work (U-TAE, ViTS) operates on single-snapshot or single-sensor settings. FUSU departs by introducing the first fine-grained land use (17-class) change segmentation dataset and a data-expanding paradigm that augments bi-temporal high-resolution images with 25 monthly Sentinel-1/2 observations, then unifies these heterogeneous inputs in a single architecture (FUSU-Net) that jointly performs segmentation and change detection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dataset addresses a genuine gap: land use semantics (driven by human activity) are fundamentally different from land cover (driven by physical surface), and no prior dataset provided pixel-wise land use change annotations at this scale (30B pixels, 847 km²). The dramatic cross-domain failure of all six unsupervised domain adaptation methods (~10 mIoU vs. ~50 same-domain) reveals that the Xi'an–Jiaxing gap is far harder than typical domain shift benchmarks, pointing to an open problem in remote sensing domain adaptation. Key limitations include the restriction to five Chinese districts, the acknowledged inadequacy of simple Sentinel-1/2 concatenation (SAR actually hurts performance), and the need for multi-modal data (economic, population) to truly capture land use semantics.
