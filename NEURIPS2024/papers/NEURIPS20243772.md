## Problem

Existing scientific QA datasets are limited in scale (typically under 5K questions) and focus exclusively on textual content such as abstracts and paragraphs, overlooking the critical information embedded in figures and tables. No large-scale benchmark exists for evaluating multimodal systems on their ability to interpret complex scientific figures, tables, and their interplay with surrounding paper text.

## Input / Output

The system receives all figures and tables (with captions) from a single scientific paper alongside a free-form question; for the full-paper variant, the entire paper text is also included. Closed-source models process all images simultaneously at full resolution, while open-source models receive images one-by-one in a multi-turn setup at 224px. The output is a free-form natural language answer; in the CoT variant, the model additionally outputs the identified relevant figures before generating the answer.

## Method

SPIQA is constructed by prompting Gemini 1.5 Pro to generate question-answer-rationale triplets for each figure or table, conditioned on the figure image, its caption, and the first passage referencing it in the paper, yielding 270K QAs from 25,859 papers across 19 CS conferences. Three task formulations are defined: direct QA over figures/tables only, direct QA over the full paper, and a two-stage CoT QA where the model first retrieves relevant figures before answering. For evaluation, the paper introduces L3Score, which computes a continuous similarity score by taking the softmax over the log-probabilities of 'yes' and 'no' tokens from GPT-4o when asked whether a candidate answer is semantically equivalent to the ground truth, eliminating the scale-sensitivity of prior LLM-as-judge metrics. Two open-source models (InstructBLIP, LLaVA 1.5) are fine-tuned with LoRA (rank 32) on the training split to demonstrate the dataset's utility for building specialized scientific QA systems.

## Results

On the SPIQA benchmark, GPT-4o achieves the highest L3Score of 64.00 on test-A and 46.68 on test-C, while Claude-3 (Opus) leads on test-B with 49.54 L3Score. Among open-source models, InternLM-XC performs best with 18.28 L3Score on test-A, still trailing GPT-4o by over 45 points. Fine-tuning InstructBLIP-7B and LLaVA-1.5-7B on the SPIQA training set yields average L3Score improvements of ~28 and ~26 points respectively across the three test splits, bringing them close to Gemini Pro Vision's zero-shot performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GPT-4o outperforms all other closed-source models on test-A (64.00 vs. 61.26 for Claude-3) and test-C (46.68 vs. 43.88 for Claude-3) in L3Score, while Claude-3 (Opus) edges out GPT-4o on test-B (49.54 vs. 46.22). Among the Gemini family, Gemini 1.5 Flash and Pro substantially surpass the earlier Gemini Pro Vision by 10–17 L3Score points on test-A, with Gemini 1.5 Pro also leading on test-B (43.27 vs. 31.84). All open-source models (SPHINX-v2, InstructBLIP, LLaVA, XGen-MM, InternLM-XC, CogVLM) score below 19 L3Score in zero-shot, with InternLM-XC (18.28) and CogVLM (16.89) being the strongest, yet they remain 45+ points behind GPT-4o. Fine-tuning InstructBLIP-7B on SPIQA closes most of this gap, reaching 43.90 L3Score on test-A (nearly matching Gemini Pro Vision's 43.85), while fine-tuned LLaVA-1.5-7B reaches 45.45. The paper also shows that CoT QA and full-paper input further boost GPT-4o to 66.09 L3Score on test-A, and that captions are critical—omitting them drops GPT-4o by ~2 L3Score points.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of two established but disjoint research threads: text-only scientific QA (QASPER, QASA, PubMedQA) and standalone figure/table QA (ChartQA, PlotQA, MathVista). Prior scientific QA datasets are limited to a few thousand human-annotated questions focused on abstracts or main text, while figure/table QA benchmarks treat visual elements in isolation without surrounding paper context. SPIQA unifies these by requiring joint reasoning over figures, tables, captions, and full paper text at scale (270K QAs), using MLLMs (Gemini 1.5 Pro) for automatic question generation rather than expensive human annotation. The proposed L3Score metric extends the LLM-as-judge paradigm (LAVE, LIMA, Prometheus-Vision) by replacing discrete rating scales with log-likelihood-based scoring, and the CoT QA task adapts chain-of-thought retrieval to the multimodal document setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SPIQA provides the first large-scale benchmark for evaluating MLLMs on the practically critical task of understanding scientific papers holistically, revealing that even state-of-the-art closed models (GPT-4o, Claude-3) achieve only ~47-64 L3Score, far from the ~68 human baseline. The finding that fine-tuning 7B open-source models (InstructBLIP, LLaVA 1.5) on SPIQA's 262K training QAs closes most of the gap to a powerful closed model like Gemini Pro Vision suggests the dataset is valuable not just for evaluation but as training data for building specialized scientific QA systems. Key limitations include restriction to computer science papers, a single human evaluator for the human baseline, and reliance on a closed model (GPT-4o) for L3Score computation. Extending to other scientific domains and developing open-source L3Score alternatives are natural next steps.
