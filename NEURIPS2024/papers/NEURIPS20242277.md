## Problem

The theory of large stepsize gradient descent for nonlinear networks was largely underdeveloped: existing results on edge-of-stability dynamics, margin maximization, and accelerated convergence were restricted to linear predictors. This paper addresses that gap by providing the first implicit bias and optimization efficiency analysis for large stepsize GD training non-homogeneous two-layer networks under logistic loss, covering commonly used activations like GELU, Softplus, and SiLU that violate exact homogeneity.

## Input / Output

The analysis takes a binary classification dataset with bounded feature vectors (‖x_i‖ ≤ 1), a two-layer network in mean-field scaling with a continuously differentiable activation satisfying a near-homogeneity condition (bounded error κ between f(w;x) and ⟨∇f(w;x), w⟩), and a large constant stepsize. It produces three guarantees: a phase transition time bound showing GD enters the stable phase in O(η) steps under linear separability, a Θ(1/t) risk convergence rate with Θ(log t) parameter norm growth in the stable phase, and an O(1/T²) accelerated risk bound when the stepsize is optimally chosen. The results hold for any network width m, going beyond both NTK and mean-field regimes.

## Method

The core technical contribution is constructing a hierarchy of auxiliary margin functions (γ^a, γ^b, γ^c) that serve as smooth approximators of the normalized margin, specifically designed to absorb errors from both large stepsize updates and predictor non-homogeneity. The key insight is that the modified margin γ^c, which incorporates a second-order correction term depending on the stepsize, smoothness constant β, and homogeneity error κ, is provably monotonically increasing in the stable phase—establishing an implicit bias toward margin maximization for non-homogeneous predictors for the first time. For the EoS phase, they track the gradient potential to show the average risk decreases despite local oscillations, guaranteeing finite-time entry into the stable phase under linear separability and a bounded-away-from-zero activation derivative. By choosing a stepsize that balances the EoS duration against the stable-phase convergence rate, they achieve an O(1/T²) risk bound, extending the acceleration known for linear predictors to two-layer networks. The proof extends Lyu and Li's homogeneous margin analysis by introducing the auxiliary functions to handle the non-homogeneity error and large-stepsize corrections, with the convexity of a carefully designed function Φ playing a central role in proving margin monotonicity.

## Results

The paper establishes that large stepsize GD on two-layer networks with near-homogeneous activations enters a stable phase after at most τ = O(η) steps (Theorem 4.1), after which the empirical risk decreases monotonically at rate L(w_t) ≤ 2/(α²γ²η(t−s)). By choosing an optimally large stepsize η = α²γ²T / (256(1+4κ)c₂), GD achieves an accelerated risk bound of O(1/T²) (Corollary 4.2), compared to the Ω(1/t) lower bound for GD that never enters the EoS phase (Theorem 4.3). Empirically, on a 6,000-sample CIFAR-10 subset (airplane vs. automobile), large stepsize GD (η̃=0.2) achieves higher test accuracy than small stepsize GD (η̃=0.005), and the normalized margin increases nearly monotonically in the stable phase for both settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is between large stepsize GD (which undergoes an EoS phase with oscillating risk) and small stepsize GD (which monotonically decreases risk). Theoretically, large stepsize GD achieves O(1/T²) empirical risk with an optimally chosen stepsize, while monotonic GD is lower-bounded at Ω(1/t) (Theorem 4.3), yielding a strict acceleration. Empirically on CIFAR-10, large stepsize GD (η̃=0.2) reaches lower empirical risk and higher test accuracy than small stepsize GD (η̃=0.005) after the stable phase begins. The paper extends the EoS-phase and acceleration results of Wu et al. [2024] from linear predictors to two-layer networks with non-homogeneous activations (GELU, Softplus, SiLU, Huberized ReLU), and extends the margin-improvement implicit bias results of Lyu and Li [2020] and Ji and Telgarsky [2020] from homogeneous predictors with infinitesimal stepsizes to non-homogeneous predictors with arbitrarily large stepsizes. The normalized margin improvement (Theorem 2.2) holds under only a bounded homogeneity error κ, which is weaker than the exact homogeneity or quasi-homogeneity assumptions in prior work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first theoretical characterization of large stepsize GD for non-homogeneous neural networks, closing a gap between the small-stepsize/homogeneous theory and the large-stepsize/nonlinear practice that dominates modern training. The provable O(1/t²) acceleration over O(1/t) for suitably chosen large stepsizes gives a theoretical justification for the empirically observed benefit of aggressive learning rates. Key limitations remain: the EoS-phase analysis requires linear separability and a derivative bounded away from zero (excluding ReLU), the results are limited to two-layer networks, and the stable-phase entry condition depends on the risk reaching a stepsize-dependent threshold that may not be reachable for bounded predictors (e.g., sigmoid). Extending to deeper architectures, relaxing separability, and handling non-smooth activations are natural open questions.
