## Problem

Spiking neural networks suffer from limited expressiveness because their binary spike feature maps constrain the output feature representation, leading to significant accuracy degradation compared to full-precision ANNs. While increasing timesteps can partially mitigate this, it inflates inference cost. The paper asks whether enhancing the output feature representation (the feature vector before the classifier) — a known lever for accuracy in ANNs — can similarly improve SNNs, and how to do so effectively.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241637/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For static image datasets (CIFAR-10/100 at 32×32, ImageNet at 224×224), raw RGB images are fed directly into the SNN, with the first convolutional layer encoding them into spikes. For the neuromorphic CIFAR10-DVS dataset (48×48), native 0/1 spike sequences are used as input. The network processes information over T timesteps (typically 1–10) using LIF neurons internally, but the final activation layer outputs a full-precision feature vector (via ReLU) that is averaged over timesteps and passed to a fully connected classifier to produce a class label.

## Method

The paper proposes two complementary mechanisms to enhance the SNN's output feature. First, the L_AF loss feeds the SNN's output feature through the pre-trained ANN's classifier to produce P_s, then computes a KL-divergence between SoftMax(P_s) and SoftMax(P_a) (the ANN's own output on the same input), effectively using the ANN's classifier as a task-aware alignment target rather than directly matching feature vectors. This design is motivated by ANN-SNN conversion results showing that a homogeneous ANN's weights already encode useful structure. Second, RepAct replaces the last LIF neuron layer with a ReLU activation, converting the discrete binary output feature into a full-precision vector at negligible extra cost. Training uses spatial-temporal backpropagation with surrogate gradients and threshold-dependent batch normalization, optimizing a total loss of cross-entropy plus λ·L_AF (λ=0.1 for CIFAR, λ=1 for ImageNet).

## Results

On CIFAR-10, the proposed EnOF-SNN with ResNet19 achieves 96.19%±0.10 top-1 accuracy at only 2 timesteps, surpassing the previous best (RecDis-SNN at 95.55% with 6 timesteps) by 0.64% while using 3× fewer timesteps. On CIFAR-100, ResNet19 reaches 82.43%±0.09 at 2 timesteps, outperforming TET (74.72% at 6 timesteps) by 7.71%. On ImageNet, ResNet34 achieves 67.40%±0.10 at 4 timesteps, and on the neuromorphic CIFAR10-DVS dataset, ResNet20 reaches 80.50%±0.20 at 10 timesteps, setting new state-of-the-art on both.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On CIFAR-10, EnOF-SNN (ResNet19, 2 timesteps) achieves 96.19%, outperforming RecDis-SNN (95.55% at 6 timesteps) by 0.64%, TET (94.50% at 6 timesteps) by 1.69%, and STBP-tdBN (93.16% at 6 timesteps) by 3.03%, all while using 3× fewer timesteps. On CIFAR-100, the gap widens dramatically: ResNet19 at 2 timesteps (82.43%) beats TET (74.72% at 6 timesteps) by 7.71% and TEBN (76.13% at 4 timesteps) by 6.02%. On ImageNet, the method is competitive but slightly trails GLIF (67.52% vs. 67.40% at 4 timesteps) and Real Spike (67.69% at 4 timesteps), though it outperforms SEW ResNet (67.04%) and RecDis-SNN (67.33% at 6 timesteps). On the neuromorphic CIFAR10-DVS dataset, EnOF-SNN clearly dominates, with ResNet20 reaching 80.50% at 10 timesteps versus Ternary Spike (78.70%) and Real Spike (78.00%) at the same timestep count. Overall, the method consistently wins or ties on static datasets with fewer timesteps and achieves the best results on the neuromorphic benchmark.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of two established SNN research threads: ANN-to-SNN weight conversion (RMP-SNN, Dspike) and knowledge distillation for SNNs (KDSNN, RecDis-SNN). Rather than directly aligning SNN and ANN feature vectors (standard KD) or copying weights wholesale (conversion), it uses the pre-trained ANN's classifier as a shared evaluation function, feeding both SNN and ANN features through it and minimizing KL-divergence on the resulting outputs. This design is explicitly motivated by the empirical observation that homogeneous ANN-to-SNN weight transfer works well, suggesting the classifier encodes task-relevant structure that direct feature matching misses. The second component, RepAct (replacing the final LIF layer with ReLU), is a minimal architectural departure from standard SNN designs that all prior methods in the comparison tables retain.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical value lies in achieving competitive accuracy with very few timesteps (1–4) across CIFAR-10/100, ImageNet, and neuromorphic data, which directly addresses the energy-efficiency motivation of SNNs. However, the method requires a fully pre-trained ANN as a teacher, adding a non-trivial training cost, and the RepAct modification means the final layer no longer operates in the spike domain, partially undermining the energy savings that motivate SNNs. Open questions include whether the classifier-bridge idea generalizes to non-ResNet/VGG SNN architectures (e.g., SpikFormer), whether the pre-trained ANN can be replaced by a lighter proxy, and whether the approach extends to non-classification tasks like detection or segmentation where the 'output feature' structure differs.
