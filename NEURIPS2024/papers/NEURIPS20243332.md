## Problem

Existing data-driven fluid dynamics models assume training and test data share the same distribution, but real-world applications face two critical distribution shifts: parameter-based shifts (e.g., unseen PDE coefficients or pressures) and temporal distribution shifts during long-term auto-regressive rollout. No prior work connects prompt learning with dynamical system modeling to simultaneously address both types of shifts in spatio-temporal forecasting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243332/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes historical spatio-temporal observations from N irregularly located sensors over T₀ time steps (each sensor producing d_o observation channels) along with physical system parameters ξ (e.g., viscosity coefficient ν, diffusion coefficient D) that define the environment. It outputs per-sensor future observations for T prediction steps. The irregular sensor layout is handled by reconstructing observations onto regular grids via attention-based frame reconstruction before feeding them into the forecasting backbone.

## Method

PURE learns time-evolving prompt embeddings through three coupled modules: (1) Multi-view Context Exploration initializes prompts by fusing attention-based spatio-temporal reconstruction with FFT-enhanced frequency-domain processing of both observations and parameter embeddings; (2) a continuous Graph ODE with an attention mechanism that uses interpolated observation sequences as keys and prompt embeddings as queries to capture temporal evolution of prompts, addressing temporal distribution shifts that static prompts cannot handle; (3) the time-evolving prompts are concatenated with observation embeddings from a basic FNO + ViT-convolution forecasting backbone, while a Jensen-Shannon mutual information minimization objective (adversarially trained) decouples prompts from observations to enforce invariance. Theoretical analysis (Theorems 3.1–3.2) proves that neglecting observations in the ODE causes exponentially growing prediction error under distribution shift, while the attention-augmented formulation bounds the error to O(ε·e^{ct}), guaranteeing approximation of the true dynamics with arbitrary precision.

## Results

PURE achieves the best MSE across all five benchmarks in both in-distribution and out-of-distribution settings. On Prometheus, PURE reaches 0.0323 (w/o OOD) and 0.0328 (w/ OOD), improving 6.10% and 8.63% over the second-best DGODE. The largest OOD gains appear on Navier-Stokes (26.07% over DGODE) and ERA5 (28.77% over DGODE), where PURE records MSEs of 0.0763 and 0.0401 respectively. On Spherical-SWE, PURE achieves 0.0022 in-distribution (matching DGODE) and 0.0024 OOD (16.67% better than DGODE).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PURE outperforms all 13 baselines on every benchmark in both in-distribution and OOD settings. The closest competitor is DGODE [80], which PURE beats by 6–29% in OOD MSE across datasets; the gap is smallest on Spherical-SWE in-distribution (0.0022 vs 0.0022, a tie) but widens to 16.67% in OOD. Among visual backbones, ResNet and ViT are the strongest (MSE ~0.063–0.067 on Prometheus), yet PURE still improves by 49–50%. Neural operator baselines (FNO, UNO, CNO, NMO) show severe OOD degradation on ERA5 (MSE 0.52–0.98), whereas PURE maintains 0.0401. CGODE is particularly weak on 3D Reaction-Diffusion (MSE 0.8371) and Spherical-SWE (0.0873), where PURE achieves 0.0119 and 0.0022 respectively. In the extended OOD comparison (Table 9), PURE also surpasses dedicated OOD methods LEADS (0.0374/0.0403), CODA (0.0353/0.0372), and NUWA (0.0359/0.0398) on Prometheus, with the largest margin on ERA5 OOD (0.0401 vs 0.4233 for LEADS).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes prompt learning—a technique well-established in NLP and vision for adapting pre-trained models—into the fluid dynamics domain, where it has not previously been applied. It builds directly on the Coupled Graph ODE framework [26] by augmenting the ODE dynamics with attention-weighted interpolated observations (Eqn. 8), and leverages the Fourier Neural Operator [45] for frequency-domain context mining. The closest prior work is DGODE/Prometheus [80], which addresses parameter-based OOD shifts in fluid dynamics via disentangled representations; PURE extends this by additionally modeling temporal distribution shifts during auto-regressive rollout through time-evolving prompts, and by introducing mutual information minimization to enforce invariance of observation embeddings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper tackles a practically important gap: existing fluid dynamics models degrade sharply under both parameter changes (e.g., different viscosity coefficients) and temporal drift during long rollouts, yet most prior work addresses only the former. The modular prompt-evolution design can be plugged into any base forecasting model (demonstrated with FNO, NMO, DGODE, ResNet), making it a potentially reusable component for the broader spatio-temporal forecasting community. Limitations include the IID assumption, high computational cost of the graph ODE solver, and evaluation restricted to five benchmarks; open questions include whether the approach scales to higher-dimensional or multi-physics systems and whether the mutual information objective remains effective under extreme distributional shifts.
