## Problem

Existing multi-task learning frameworks for EHR data rely on human experts to manually select which diseases to train jointly and to hand-craft model architectures, with no prior work jointly automating both task grouping and architecture search. This limits scalability and introduces the risk of task interference from poorly chosen groupings, while the optimal architecture may differ across task groups.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20244102/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes EHR time series (sequences of length L with 76-dimensional features from MIMIC-IV ICU stays) and a set of N binary disease-prediction tasks. It produces a configuration of up to B task groups, each paired with a searched DAG-based encoder architecture, and ultimately per-task disease predictions. The joint search space is the Cartesian product of all 2^N − 1 task combinations and all DAG architectures over a fixed set of operations {Identity, Zero, FFN, RNN, Attention}, making exhaustive evaluation infeasible.

## Method

AutoDP employs a surrogate model to learn the mapping from (task combination, architecture) pairs to per-task multi-task gains, avoiding exhaustive MTL training. The surrogate uses a graph encoder (D-VAE-style sequential aggregation over DAG nodes) for architectures and a self-attention encoder over task embeddings for task combinations, fused by a 2-layer MLP to predict gains. A progressive sampling strategy with UCB acquisition (balancing exploitation and exploration) iteratively selects which (C, A) pairs to evaluate with ground-truth MTL training, updating the surrogate each round. Finally, a greedy search algorithm (random mutation of one task or one operation per step, accepting only if overall gain improves) derives the near-optimal configuration from the trained surrogate over the huge search space.

## Results

On MIMIC-IV with 25 ICU-stay prediction tasks, AutoDP^(μ+σ) achieves the highest averaged per-task gain over single-task LSTM backbone: +2.80% ROC and +13.43% AVP at Task@25, +2.55% ROC and +8.81% AVP at Task@10, and +1.69% ROC and +7.74% AVP at Task@5. These gains are statistically significant over the strongest baseline (MTG+DARTS) with p-values ranging from 0.027 to 0.045. The method also shows no negative transfer, with all individual tasks achieving positive AVP gains, and some tasks exceeding 20% improvement.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AutoDP^(μ+σ) outperforms all six baselines across every task setting and both metrics. Against the hand-crafted EHR encoders, LSTM yields the weakest gains (+0.09% ROC at Task@5), while Transformer, Retain, and Adacare provide moderate improvements; DARTS as a single shared model is the strongest hand-crafted/NAS baseline at +1.28% ROC and +5.01% AVP (Task@5). The multi-task grouping method MTG-Net [13], when paired with DARTS, is the closest competitor, reaching +1.47% ROC and +6.41% AVP (Task@5) and +2.41% ROC and +11.76% AVP (Task@25). AutoDP^(μ+σ) surpasses MTG+DARTS by +0.22% ROC and +1.33% AVP at Task@5, and by +0.39% ROC and +1.67% AVP at Task@25, with all differences statistically significant (p < 0.05). The ablation further confirms that the joint optimization of task grouping and architecture search is essential: replacing either progressive sampling or greedy search with random alternatives drops AVP by 1–2 points, and using disease-category-based grouping (GPT-4-assigned) instead of the searched grouping reduces Task@25 AVP from +13.43% to +8.61%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three established lines of work: multi-task learning for EHR (Suo et al., Razavian et al., Harutyunyan et al.), automated task grouping (Standley et al., Fifty et al., Song et al./MTG-Net), and multi-task neural architecture search (Ahn et al., Sun et al./AdaShare, Gao et al./MTL-NAS). The key departure is that all prior approaches treat task grouping and architecture design as separate, sequentially solved problems—MTG-Net assumes a fixed architecture across groups, while MTL-NAS assumes a fixed task set. AutoDP unifies both into a single joint search space and introduces a surrogate model (graph encoder + self-attention) with progressive UCB-based sampling to navigate this space efficiently, a formulation not present in either the MTL or NAS literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By automating both task grouping and architecture selection, AutoDP removes the two most labor-intensive and error-prone human decisions in MTL framework design for EHR, making it more scalable as the number of target diseases grows. The finding that data-driven task grouping substantially outperforms medically-motivated (GPT-4-classified) grouping challenges the assumption that clinical taxonomy is a reliable proxy for synergistic task structure. Open questions remain: the framework assumes homogeneous input data across tasks, relies on a single dataset (MIMIC-IV), uses approximate greedy search, and incurs non-trivial compute (~200 GPU-hours for 25 tasks), limiting immediate deployment in resource-constrained clinical settings.
