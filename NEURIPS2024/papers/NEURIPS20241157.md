## Problem

Spiking Neural Networks (SNNs) offer energy-efficient, event-driven computation ideal for wearable sEMG gesture recognition, but their sparse binary representations make standard attention mechanisms and domain adaptation infeasible, limiting accuracy. Additionally, sEMG signals are highly sensitive to distribution shifts such as forearm posture changes, causing severe accuracy degradation (down to ~30%) when a model trained on one posture is deployed on another, and existing source-free domain adaptation methods cannot operate on spike-based features.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241157/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes 8-channel raw sEMG signals sampled at 2000 Hz, which are first processed via Root Mean Square (RMS) with a 100 ms window and 0.5 ms step to produce fixed-length feature vectors, then encoded into binary spike trains by a ConvLIF layer. The output is a 10-class gesture label determined by the neuron with the highest membrane potential in a modified LIF classifier, alongside the full membrane potential sequence (across T time steps) which is stored in a memory list for downstream source-free domain adaptation. The dataset spans 15 subjects, 10 daily-life gestures, and 3 forearm postures (horizontal-on-surface, elevated-diagonal, horizontal-in-air).

## Method

SpGesture comprises two core components: (1) JASNN, an SNN backbone where a ConvLIF encoder and multi-channel ConvLIF extractor produce spike features that are refined by a Spiking Jaccard Attention (SJA) module computing attention weights via element-wise min/max (intersection/union) operations directly on binary spike sequences—avoiding dot-product sparsity collapse and softmax overhead while remaining deployable on neuromorphic hardware; and (2) SSFDA, a source-free domain adaptation procedure that stores membrane potentials (augmented with scaled Gaussian noise) in a memory list, retrieves k-nearest neighbors via Pearson correlation, and generates pseudo-labels through a probabilistic mechanism (mode of neighbor labels with probability 1−p, random selection with probability p) to prevent overfitting. Training uses the SuperSpike surrogate gradient with rate coding, and the SSFDA loss combines a Smooth NLL consistency term (aligning predictions with k-nearest neighbors) and a KL-divergence regularization term (pushing the average predicted class distribution toward uniformity), enabling adaptation on unlabeled target data without access to source data or labels.

## Results

On the SpGesture sEMG Gesture Dataset (10 gestures × 3 forearm postures, 15 subjects), JASNN achieves 89.26% ± 0.31% top-1 accuracy, the highest among all DNN and SNN baselines. The best DNN baseline (Informer) reaches 88.32%, and the best SNN baseline (LSNN) reaches 86.24%, meaning JASNN outperforms both by ~1% and ~3% respectively. Adding Spiking Jaccard Attention to the LSNN backbone alone lifts accuracy from 86.24% to 88.10%, and the SSFDA module provides an additional 4.5% accuracy gain under distribution shift. Inference latency on CPU is below 100 ms, and SJA is 36.37× faster than traditional attention mechanisms.

## Baselines & Comparisons

JASNN (89.26%) outperforms all 14 baselines on top-1 accuracy. Among DNN baselines, it beats the strongest, Informer (88.32%), by 0.94%, and TCN+Attention (87.10%) by 2.16%. Among SNN baselines, it surpasses LSNN (86.24%) by 3.02%, SCNN (84.30%) by 4.96%, and SIB+SNN (77.84%) by 11.42%. The SJA mechanism is shown to be transferable: plugging it into the LSNN backbone raises accuracy from 86.24% to 88.10% (+1.86%), confirming its generality beyond the proposed JASNN architecture. In the SSFDA ablation (Table 2), the proposed Probabilistic Label Generation (PLG) improves accuracy by 4.10% over the JASNN baseline, outperforming the Pseudo-Label method (+1.87%) and Neighborhood-guided Pseudo-Labels (+2.33%). In the attention ablation, raw dot-product attention on spikes collapses to 11.31% accuracy due to sparsity, while MA-SNN (converting spikes to continuous values) reaches 85.67%; SJA achieves 87.44% while preserving spike-level computation compatible with neuromorphic hardware.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: SNN-based gesture recognition (building on LSNN, SCNN, and SIB+SNN), attention mechanisms for SNNs (departing from MA-SNN's approach of converting spikes to continuous values for dot-product attention), and source-free domain adaptation (extending pseudo-labeling and neighborhood-based methods from DNNs into the SNN setting). The key conceptual departure is replacing the standard dot-product/softmax attention with a Jaccard-similarity-based mechanism that operates directly on binary spike trains using only min/max and addition operations, preserving compatibility with neuromorphic hardware. The SSFDA component is the first to leverage membrane potential curves as a memory list for k-NN-based pseudo-label generation in an SNN, adapting ideas from Yang et al.'s neighborhood-structure SFDA to the spiking domain.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in demonstrating that SNNs can match DNN-level accuracy (89.26% vs. 88.32% for Informer) on sEMG gesture recognition while maintaining sub-100ms CPU latency and hardware-friendly operations, making them viable for real-time wearable deployment. The new multi-posture dataset addresses a recognized gap where prior sEMG datasets used a single forearm posture, masking real-world distribution shifts. However, the evaluation is limited to one SNN backbone (SCNN-based), a single type of distribution shift (forearm posture), and no actual neuromorphic chip validation; generalization to other SNN architectures, other shift sources (electrode movement, inter-subject variability), and true energy measurements on spiking hardware remain open.
