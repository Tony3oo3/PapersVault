## Problem

Text-to-image personalization methods face a fundamental trade-off: Textual Inversion overfits the learned concept's embedding, causing generated images to ignore other prompt tokens, while DreamBooth under-learns the embedding alignment, causing the model to overlook the personalized concept entirely. The paper attributes both failures to incorrect learning of the embedding alignment for the new concept, and asks how to properly learn this alignment without overfitting or underfitting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241259/cropped_page0_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a small set of reference images (typically 3–5) depicting a target concept and a fixed training prompt of the form 'a photo of a [V] [super-category]', where [V] is a rare token and [super-category] is the object's category. It produces a fine-tuned Stable Diffusion V2.1 model that, given any novel text prompt containing [V], generates a 512×512 personalized image preserving the concept's identity while respecting all other prompt elements (scene, style, color, etc.). Training completes in approximately 20 minutes on a single A100 GPU (or ~6 minutes with a fast variant).

## Method

AttnDreamBooth decomposes personalization into three sequential training stages that each target a distinct aspect of the concept: (1) a short Textual Inversion-style phase (60 steps, lr 10⁻³) that learns the embedding alignment while deliberately stopping early to avoid overfitting, yielding a coarse attention map; (2) fine-tuning of all cross-attention layers in the U-Net (100 steps, lr 2×10⁻⁵) to refine the attention map, with the text encoder and textual embedding frozen; (3) full U-Net fine-tuning (500 steps, lr 2×10⁻⁶) to capture subject identity, again with the text encoder frozen. A cross-attention map regularization term is applied throughout all three stages, penalizing the squared difference in mean and variance of attention map values between the [V] token and its [super-category] token across all 16 cross-attention layers, thereby encouraging the new concept to attend to the same spatial region as its category in a self-supervised manner.

## Results

On 22 concepts evaluated with 24 diverse prompts (background change, environment interaction, color change, artistic style), AttnDreamBooth achieves 0.7257 identity preservation and 0.2532 text alignment (CLIP cosine similarity). The fast version (6 min training vs. 20 min) achieves 0.7268 identity and 0.2536 text alignment, matching or slightly exceeding the full model. In a user study with 700 responses from 35 participants, AttnDreamBooth is preferred over all four quantitative baselines, with the strongest margin against ViCo (83.4% preference).

## Baselines & Comparisons

AttnDreamBooth achieves a balanced trade-off that no single baseline matches: ViCo attains the highest identity preservation (0.7507) but the lowest text alignment (0.2106), reflecting its tendency to overfit the concept; TI+DB scores the best text alignment (0.2578) but the second-worst identity (0.7017), consistent with its tendency to neglect the learned concept. Against OFT, AttnDreamBooth ties on identity (0.7257) and leads on text alignment (0.2532 vs. 0.2445). Against NeTI, it wins on both metrics (0.7257 vs. 0.6901 identity; 0.2532 vs. 0.2522 text). In qualitative comparisons (Appendix Figures 10–11), the fast version of AttnDreamBooth outperforms DreamMatcher and FreeCustom on complex prompts, and the full method surpasses SuTI and Instruct-Imagen on both identity preservation and text alignment, particularly when prompts combine style, scene, and appearance changes simultaneously.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the two dominant text-to-image personalization paradigms—Textual Inversion (optimizing a textual embedding) and DreamBooth (fine-tuning the U-Net)—and diagnoses a shared failure mode: incorrect learning of the embedding alignment for the new concept. It departs from prior multi-stage approaches (PTI, MagiCapture, Break-A-Scene) by explicitly decomposing the personalization process into three functionally distinct stages (embedding alignment, attention map refinement, subject identity) rather than simply chaining TI then DB. The cross-attention map regularization term, which constrains the mean and variance of attention maps between the concept token and its super-category, draws inspiration from Perfusion's key-locking strategy and Custom Diffusion's attention analysis but operates in a self-supervised, training-time manner.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a persistent and practically important trade-off in text-to-image personalization: methods that preserve identity well (e.g., ViCo) sacrifice text alignment, and vice versa. AttnDreamBooth's three-stage decomposition with attention regularization offers a practical (~20 min, or ~6 min fast) recipe that is competitive on both metrics simultaneously and notably improves qualitative results on complex, multi-element prompts. Open questions include whether the fixed training steps generalize across diverse concepts (the authors acknowledge this limitation), whether the approach extends naturally to multi-concept composition, and whether the cross-attention regularization can be made adaptive per concept.
