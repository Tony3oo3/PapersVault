## Problem

Existing scaling rules for deep neural networks—Standard Parameterization, Maximal Update Parameterization (μP), and spectral scaling conditions—were derived under the assumption that architectures are representable as Tensor Programs (NE⊗ORT programs). This paper shows that structured state-space models (SSMs) like Mamba and S4 violate this assumption due to their HiPPO-structured transition matrices, causing all three scaling rules to produce vanishing or exploding signals in the infinite-width limit. The central question is: what is the correct scaling that enables non-trivial feature learning in SSMs as width approaches infinity?

## Input / Output

The analysis takes a structured SSM layer (Mamba S6 or S4) with two width parameters—N_x (latent state dimension) and N_u (output/input channel dimension)—and produces a complete parameterization: initialization variances σ_B and σ_C for the input/output projection matrices, and per-parameter learning rate scalings η_a, η_B, η_C. Unusually, the correct scalings depend on the ratio N_x/N_u and involve Riemann zeta functions (ζ(2), ζ(4)) arising from the polynomial decay of the HiPPO-structured matrix A^{-1}, rather than the simple 1/√N_u factors seen in standard architectures. The results are derived in the sequential limit N_u→∞ then N_x→∞, with the proportional limit (N_x/N_u held constant) deferred to future work.

## Method

The paper performs a detailed forward and backward signal propagation analysis through a Mamba S6 layer, tracking the asymptotic scaling of hidden states, outputs, and their gradient updates as widths grow. It first proves (Proposition 2.2) that SSMs are not representable as NE⊗ORT programs because the HiPPO-structured matrix A produces non-identically-distributed coordinates in the hidden states, breaking the core i.i.d. assumption of Tensor Programs. It then shows (Proposition 2.3) that spectral scaling conditions, while sufficient for feature learning in TP-representable architectures, fail for SSMs because the spectral norm of the discretized B matrix does not correctly predict the output norm due to the anisotropic decay of A^{-1}. By applying the Kolmogorov Strong Law of Large Numbers and Lindenberg-Feller CLT to the sums arising in the ZOH-discretized recurrence, the paper derives the unique correct scaling: σ_B ∈ Θ(√(N_x/N_u)), σ_C ∈ Θ(1/√(N_x·N_u)), η_B ∈ Θ(N_x/√N_u), η_C ∈ Θ(1/(N_x·√N_u)), and η_a ∈ Θ(N_u), which it calls μP-SSM. Empirically, this scaling yields stable training, monotonic performance improvement with width, and transferability of optimal learning rates from small to large models on wikitext-103.

## Results

The paper derives the unique scaling conditions for feature learning in SSMs: σ_B ∈ Θ(√(N_x/N_u)), σ_C ∈ Θ(√(1/(N_x·N_u))), η_a ∈ Θ(N_u), η_B ∈ Θ(N_x/√N_u), and η_C ∈ Θ(1/(N_x·√N_u)). Empirically, on wikitext-103 with Mamba (3 SSM blocks, N_u = 16·N_x), μP-SSM achieves substantially lower test loss than Standard Parameterization across all widths, and shows greater stability at large learning rates compared to μP (heuristic). Notably, μP-SSM exhibits stable learning-rate transfer from small to large model widths and monotonically improving performance with scale, whereas standard scaling shows non-monotonic behavior where performance drops after a certain width.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three parameterizations: Standard Parameterization (SP), μP (heuristic) from Yang et al. (2022), and the proposed μP-SSM. Under SP, both latent states and outputs explode at initialization, and their updates also explode, leading to complete instability. Under μP (heuristic) with ZOH discretization, latent states vanish with width while outputs retain correct scaling; with Euler discretization, the opposite occurs (latent states correct, outputs explode). μP-SSM is the only parameterization that achieves correct scaling for both latent states and outputs simultaneously under both discretization schemes, and also yields correct scaling for all updates. On wikitext-103, μP-SSM achieves lower test loss than both SP and μP (heuristic) across all tested widths, with the advantage over μP (heuristic) being most pronounced at large learning rates where μP (heuristic) becomes unstable. Additionally, μP-SSM is the only scheme that exhibits stable hyperparameter (learning rate) transfer from small to large widths and monotonically improving performance with scale.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Tensor Programs (TP) and Maximal Update Parameterization (μP) framework—developed for MLPs, transformers, and CNNs—to structured state space models (SSMs) like S4 and Mamba. The critical departure is a proof that SSMs are fundamentally not representable as NE⊗ORT programs due to the HiPPO-structured transition matrix A, which breaks the i.i.d. coordinate assumption underlying the TP Master Theorems. Consequently, the paper derives a new scaling (μP-SSM) through direct forward/backward signal propagation analysis rather than through the TP machinery, and shows that spectral scaling conditions (Yang et al., 2023a), which suffice for feature learning in TP-representable architectures, are insufficient for SSMs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As Mamba and other SSMs gain traction as transformer alternatives, knowing the correct infinite-width scaling is essential for stable training at scale and for transferring hyperparameters from small to large models. The paper's analysis is limited to the sequential limit (N_u → ∞ then N_x → ∞) rather than the proportional limit, and the empirical validation is restricted to small-scale wikitext experiments with 3 SSM blocks. A key open question is whether the Tensor Programs framework can be generalized (e.g., by relaxing i.i.d. assumptions to Lindenberg/Kolmogorov conditions) to formally cover SSMs, which the authors identify as a substantial but necessary future direction.
