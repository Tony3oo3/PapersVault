## Problem

Current adversarial robustness evaluation aggregates local robustness over a fixed test set, which may be biased and unrepresentative of the true global data distribution, and most attack-independent methods are restricted to white-box access and suffer from high computational cost. This paper addresses three gaps: the lack of a principled global (distribution-level) robustness metric, the white-box limitation of existing certified methods, and the computational inefficiency of iterative attack-based or interval-bound evaluation.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a K-way classifier (only its output logits are needed, enabling black-box use), an off-the-shelf conditional GAN or diffusion model, and random latent vectors z ~ N(0, I). It produces a single scalar GREAT Score per model — the mean certified L2-norm perturbation lower bound over the generative model's sampling distribution — which supports model ranking and per-sample certified perturbation estimates. Because evaluation uses only synthetically generated images and a single forward pass per sample, no real data or gradient access is required, making it suitable for privacy-sensitive remote auditing of online APIs.

## Method

GREAT Score defines a local robustness score g(G(z)) = √(π/2) · max{f_c(G(z)) − max_{k≠c} f_k(G(z)), 0} for each generated sample, where c is the ground-truth class. The key theoretical insight is applying Stein's Lemma to the Gaussian latent space of the generative model, which yields a closed-form global Lipschitz constant √(2/π) in L2-norm, eliminating the need for expensive local Lipschitz estimation. This transforms the local certificate from [59] into a certified lower bound on the minimal adversarial perturbation, and the global score is simply the expectation of g over z ~ N(0, I). A concentration inequality (Theorem 2) provides a probabilistic guarantee that the sample mean over n ≥ 32e·log(2/δ)/ε² generated samples is ε-close to the true mean with probability 1−δ. Computationally, each sample requires only one forward pass through the classifier, yielding 800–2000× speedup over Auto-Attack while achieving Spearman rank correlation of 0.90 with RobustBench on CIFAR-10 after calibration.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, calibrated GREAT Score achieves a Spearman's rank correlation of 0.8971 with RobustBench rankings (vs. 0.7296 for Auto-Attack on generated samples), while uncalibrated GREAT Score reaches 0.6618. On ImageNet, GREAT Score achieves a 0.9 correlation with RobustBench, outperforming Auto-Attack's 0.872. GREAT Score is 800–2000× faster per sample than Auto-Attack (e.g., 0.038 s vs. 60.872 s for Rebuffi_extra). The local GREAT Score is verified as a certified lower bound of CW attack distortion across all 17 CIFAR-10 models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CW Attack [7], GREAT Score is verified as a certified lower bound: for every one of the 17 CIFAR-10 models, the per-sample local GREAT Score is ≤ the CW attack distortion (Figure 1), and globally the mean GREAT Score (e.g., 0.507 for Rebuffi_extra) is below the mean CW distortion (1.859). Against Auto-Attack [10], calibrated GREAT Score achieves a higher Spearman's rank correlation with RobustBench (0.8971 vs. 0.7296 on CIFAR-10; 0.9 vs. 0.872 on ImageNet) while being 800–2000× faster per sample. Against the RobustBench benchmark [11] itself, GREAT Score reproduces the model ranking with strong correlation without requiring any adversarial attack, confirming it as a complementary attack-independent metric. Against Square Attack [1] on the DEEPFACE API, higher GREAT Score consistently corresponds to higher robust accuracy (e.g., 0.979 GREAT Score ↔ 84.40% RA for Old vs. 0.774 ↔ 72.60% for Young), validating the score's predictive power in a black-box setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the CLEVER framework [59], which derived a closed-form certified local radius via the maximum local Lipschitz constant, by shifting the focus from per-sample local robustness to a distribution-level (global) robustness metric. The key theoretical departure is the use of Stein's Lemma to obtain a closed-form global Lipschitz constant over a Gaussian sampling distribution, eliminating the need to estimate local Lipschitz constants per sample. Generative models (GANs, diffusion models) serve as proxies for the unknown true data distribution, a role not previously exploited in certified robustness evaluation. The paper positions itself against the dominant attack-based evaluation paradigm (Auto-Attack on RobustBench) by offering an attack-independent, black-box-compatible alternative.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GREAT Score provides a practical 800–2000× speedup over iterative attack-based evaluation while maintaining high rank correlation with RobustBench, making large-scale model auditing feasible on modest hardware. Its black-box applicability and use of synthetic data enable privacy-sensitive remote auditing, as demonstrated on online facial recognition APIs. Key limitations include restriction to L2-norm perturbations (extending to other norms would require generalizing Stein's Lemma) and dependence on the quality of the chosen generative model as a distributional proxy. Open questions include whether the framework can be extended to multi-modal or non-image data, and how to formally quantify the approximation error introduced by the generative model relative to the true data distribution.
