## Problem

Existing controllable T2I diffusion methods face a fundamental trade-off: training-based approaches (ControlNet, IP-Adapter) require paired data and auxiliary module training, while training-free guidance-based methods (FreeControl, Diffusion Self-Guidance) incur 2–20× longer sampling steps, high GPU memory from backpropagation, and fragile per-image hyperparameter tuning. No prior method simultaneously provides disentangled structure and appearance control that is both training-free and guidance-free, limiting flexibility and scalability to arbitrary condition modalities and larger models.

## Input / Output

Ctrl-X takes three inputs: a structure image of arbitrary modality (natural photos, depth maps, point clouds, 3D meshes, segmentation masks, normal maps, bounding boxes, pose skeletons, etc.), an appearance image (a natural image providing style/color/texture), and a text prompt describing the desired output. It produces a 1024×1024 generated image (SDXL v1.0) that inherits the spatial layout of the structure image and the visual appearance of the appearance image. In prompt-driven conditional generation mode, the appearance image is jointly generated from the text prompt rather than user-supplied, and the method extends directly to text-to-video models (AnimateDiff, LaVie) producing temporally consistent videos.

## Method

Ctrl-X's key insight is that diffusion U-Net feature maps from early denoising steps already encode sufficient spatial structure and high-level appearance to enable control without any guidance or backpropagation. For structure control, it obtains the noised structure latent via the forward diffusion process (avoiding inversion and its associated appearance leakage), then directly injects convolution features and self-attention maps from the structure latent into the corresponding output U-Net layers during early time steps (t ≤ τ^s). For appearance transfer, it computes a cross-image attention map between the output and appearance latents (Q from output, K from appearance, with spatial normalization to isolate structural correspondence), then uses this attention to compute spatially-weighted mean and standard deviation maps of the appearance features, which are applied as a spatially-aware normalization (generalizing AdaIN) to the output features before self-attention proceeds normally. The two controls operate on disjoint layer sets and time schedules, enabling independent tuning of structure fidelity versus appearance alignment, and the entire pipeline is plug-and-play with no additional parameters or optimization.

## Results

On the 256-pair structure-appearance control dataset, Ctrl-X achieves DINO-I scores of 0.686 (natural), 0.698 (ControlNet-supported), and 0.676 (new conditions), the best appearance transfer among all training-free methods and competitive with training-based baselines. Structure preservation (DINO Self-similarity) is 0.057/0.121/0.109 across the three condition types, matching or approaching training-based methods like ControlNet+IP-Adapter (0.068/0.136/0.139). Inference takes 10.91 s with 11.51 GiB peak GPU memory on an H100, making it 35× faster than FreeControl (378.89 s, 44.34 GiB) while remaining slightly slower than training-based ControlNet+IP-Adapter (6.21 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against training-based baselines, Ctrl-X matches or exceeds ControlNet+IP-Adapter on DINO-I (0.686 vs 0.656 natural; 0.698 vs 0.686 ControlNet-supported) while achieving comparable structure preservation (Self-sim 0.057 vs 0.068 natural). T2I-Adapter+IP-Adapter is outperformed on both metrics across all condition types. Uni-ControlNet shows the weakest appearance transfer (DINO-I 0.506–0.574) due to compressing appearance into prompt tokens. Splicing ViT Features achieves the best Self-sim and DINO-I scores but requires per-image training (1557 s inference) and produces blurry outputs with appearance leakage. Among training-free baselines, Ctrl-X decisively outperforms FreeControl on DINO-I (0.686 vs 0.572 natural) and Cross-Image Attention on both metrics (Self-sim 0.057 vs 0.145; DINO-I 0.686 vs 0.651 natural), while being 35× faster than FreeControl and 4× faster than Cross-Image Attention. In the prompt-driven conditional generation setting (Table 5), Ctrl-X achieves the best CLIP score (0.322/0.326) among training-free methods, outperforming FreeControl (0.340/0.339) only slightly but with substantially lower LPIPS (less appearance leakage: 0.635/0.590 vs 0.557/0.492), and surpasses SDEdit, Prompt-to-Prompt, Plug-and-Play, and InfEdit on prompt alignment.

## Relation to Prior Work

Ctrl-X carves out a new middle ground between two established lines of controllable diffusion work: training-based methods (ControlNet, T2I-Adapter, IP-Adapter) that require paired data to train auxiliary modules, and training-free guidance-based methods (FreeControl, Diffusion Self-Guidance) that optimize latents via backpropagation over the diffusion network. Its key conceptual departure is reformulating controllable generation as a feed-forward 'cut-and-paste' of two independent operations—direct feature/attention injection for structure and spatially-aware AdaIN-style normalization for appearance—eliminating both training and guidance entirely. The spatially-aware appearance transfer repurposes the cross-image attention correspondence insight from Cross-Image Attention and the feature-statistics transfer idea from AdaAttN, but applies them within the diffusion U-Net's self-attention layers rather than in pixel space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 35× inference speedup over guidance-based methods while maintaining competitive structure and appearance fidelity makes fine-grained controllable generation practical for interactive and video applications, and the plug-and-play design extends naturally to T2V models (AnimateDiff, LaVie). The ability to handle arbitrary in-the-wild structure conditions (3D meshes, point clouds, sketches) without paired training data addresses a real gap that training-based methods cannot fill. Key limitations include degraded appearance transfer for small subjects due to low-resolution feature maps, and a fundamental trade-off between structure adherence and appearance fidelity that the control schedules only partially resolve. Open questions include whether the approach degrades gracefully on much larger or architecturally different diffusion models, and whether the forward-diffusion structure signal remains sufficient as models evolve.
