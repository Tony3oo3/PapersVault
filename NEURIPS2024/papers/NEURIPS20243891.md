## Problem

Classifier-free guidance (CFG) is universally applied with a constant weight across all sampling steps in diffusion models, yet the authors demonstrate that this uniform application is suboptimal: guidance at high noise levels drastically reduces sample diversity and causes mode drops, while at low noise levels it is largely unnecessary. No prior work had quantified these effects on distribution metrics (FID, FD_DINOv2) or proposed a simple, training-free remedy that preserves the benefits of guidance while eliminating its harms.

## Input / Output

The method operates on any pretrained diffusion model (EDM2, DiT, Stable Diffusion XL) without retraining, requiring only two additional scalar hyperparameters—σ_lo and σ_hi—that define the noise-level interval where guidance is active. These values must be snapped to the discrete step boundaries of the chosen sampler (e.g., 32-step Heun or 250-step iDDPM), so the effective precision is limited by the number of sampling steps. The output is a single generated image at the model's native resolution (512×512 for ImageNet experiments, 1024×1024 for SD-XL), with the same conditioning interface (text prompt or class label) as the underlying model.

## Method

The core modification replaces the constant guidance weight w in the CFG ODE (Equation 4) with a piecewise constant function w(σ) that equals w only within the interval (σ_lo, σ_hi] and equals 1 (i.e., no guidance) everywhere else. This is a few-line change to the sampling loop: at each step, the unconditional denoiser is simply skipped if the current noise level falls outside the interval, reducing the number of unconditional forward passes and thus inference cost. The key insight, illustrated with a 1D toy example, is that guidance at high noise levels causes sample trajectories to drift far from the smoothed data distribution—effectively repelling them via the unconditional component—leading to mode drops and reduced diversity, while at low noise levels the conditional and unconditional trajectories have already converged so guidance adds nothing. The optimal interval is found by a grid search over w, σ_lo, and σ_hi (or a sequential 1-D search exploiting the weak sensitivity to σ_lo), and the resulting parameters are robust to changes in the number of sampling steps. Applied to EDM2-XXL on ImageNet-512, this simple change improves FID from 1.81 to 1.40 and FD_DINOv2 from 33.09 to 29.16, with consistent gains across architectures and datasets.

## Results

On ImageNet-512, limiting classifier-free guidance to a specific noise-level interval improves FID from 1.81 to 1.40 with EDM2-XXL (the new state-of-the-art) and from 2.23 to 1.68 with EDM2-S. FD_DINOv2 also improves significantly, from 33.09 to 29.16 (EDM2-XXL) and from 52.32 to 46.25 (EDM2-S). The same trend holds for the DiT-XL/2 diffusion transformer, where FID drops from 3.04 to 2.40. The method also reduces sampling cost since guidance (requiring an extra unconditional model evaluation) is disabled outside the interval, yielding over 20% speed-up in the Stable Diffusion XL setting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is standard classifier-free guidance (CFG) [18], where a constant guidance weight is applied at every sampling step. The proposed guidance-interval method wins decisively on both FID and FD_DINOv2 across all three model architectures tested: EDM2-S (FID 1.68 vs. 2.23), EDM2-XXL (FID 1.40 vs. 1.81), and DiT-XL/2 (FID 2.40 vs. 3.04). The improvements are consistent in magnitude (~0.4–0.6 FID points) and are accompanied by lower FD_DINOv2 scores, indicating better distributional fidelity. Notably, the guidance interval method also permits higher guidance weights (e.g., w=2.0–2.9 vs. 1.2–1.4 for CFG on EDM2-XXL) without the over-truncation and mode-dropping artifacts that plague standard CFG at elevated weights. The method additionally reduces inference cost by skipping unconditional model evaluations outside the interval, providing a free speed-up of over 20% in the SD-XL setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly modifies the classifier-free guidance (CFG) ODE of Ho and Salimans [18] by replacing the constant guidance weight with a piecewise-constant function active only within a noise-level interval, formulated within the EDM ODE framework of Karras et al. [22]. It departs from prior noise-level-dependent guidance approaches (Muse, Masked DiT v2, Sadat et al.'s dynamic CFG) by showing that a simple binary on/off at an interval outperforms continuous or annealed weighting schemes. The qualitative observation that guidance is harmful at high noise levels had been noted anecdotally in the Stable Diffusion community, but this paper provides the first systematic quantitative validation across architectures and datasets, and explicitly contrasts with Sadat et al.'s finding that dynamic CFG degrades results.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The technique is a few lines of code that simultaneously improves generation quality (FID 1.81→1.40 on ImageNet-512) and reduces inference cost by skipping unconditional model evaluations, making it highly likely to be adopted as a standard sampler parameter in all CFG-based diffusion models. Open questions include whether the optimal interval can be derived analytically from the ODE rather than found by grid search, and how non-idealities in trained denoisers interact with the guidance interval. The finding that smooth weighting functions do not improve over binary inclusion, and that guidance harms accumulate cumulatively over consecutive steps, suggests the underlying mechanism is not yet fully understood.
