## Problem

Thorax disease classification on chest X-rays is severely limited by ubiquitous noise and background/non-disease areas that degrade feature quality, yet no principled method exists to selectively suppress these adverse effects during neural network training. Existing approaches rely on architectural choices (CNNs vs. ViTs) or self-supervised pre-training strategies but lack a mechanism to filter irrelevant high-rank feature components that encode noise and background information.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 224×224 chest X-ray images processed by pre-trained backbones (ViT-S/16, ViT-B/16, ResNet-50, DenseNet-121) and produces multi-label binary disease predictions (e.g., 14 classes on NIH-ChestX-ray14, 5 on CheXpert, 3 on COVIDx) via a sigmoid-activated linear head. The low-rank regularization operates on the d-dimensional feature vectors extracted from the layer preceding the final classifier, with the rank threshold T set as a fraction γ of min(n,d) determined by cross-validation. A secondary output is disease localization bounding boxes derived from Grad-CAM heatmaps.

## Method

LRFL is a three-stage pipeline: (1) MAE self-supervised pre-training on ImageNet and 0.5M chest X-rays, (2) standard cross-entropy fine-tuning for 75 epochs, and (3) low-rank feature learning where the backbone is frozen and only the linear classifier is trained with an added truncated nuclear norm (TNN) regularization term. The key insight is the Low Frequency Property: the top-r eigenvectors of the kernel Gram matrix capture ~96% of the label signal, so penalizing the sum of singular values beyond rank T (‖F‖_T) forces the classifier to rely on low-rank features while discarding noise and background encoded in high-rank components. To make TNN optimizable by standard SGD, the authors introduce a separable approximation using left/right eigenvectors (U̅, V̅) computed periodically from the feature matrix, yielding a per-sample loss amenable to minibatch gradient descent. A sharp generalization bound (Theorem 3.1) shows the bound tightens as ‖F‖_T decreases, providing theoretical justification. Optionally, synthetic images generated by a conditional Diffusion Transformer (DiT) are added to the training set, with the number selected via cross-validation to avoid noise injection.

## Results

On CheXpert, ViT-B-LR achieves 89.8% mAUC (vs. 89.3% for ViT-B) and ViT-S-LR achieves 89.6% mAUC (vs. 89.2% for ViT-S). On COVIDx, ViT-B-LR reaches 97.0% top-1 accuracy (vs. 95.3% for ViT-B) and ViT-S-LR reaches 96.8% (vs. 95.2% for ViT-S). On NIH ChestX-ray14, ViT-B-LR achieves 83.4% mAUC (vs. 83.0% for ViT-B), setting a new state-of-the-art. With diffusion-model-generated synthetic data, ViT-B-LR further improves to 90.4% mAUC on CheXpert and 97.5% accuracy on COVIDx.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LRFL consistently outperforms its corresponding base models across all three datasets: on CheXpert, ViT-B-LR improves mAUC by 0.5% (89.8% vs. 89.3%) and ViT-S-LR by 0.4% (89.6% vs. 89.2%); on COVIDx, ViT-B-LR improves top-1 accuracy by 1.7% (97.0% vs. 95.3%) and ViT-S-LR by 1.6% (96.8% vs. 95.2%); on NIH ChestX-ray14, ViT-B-LR improves mAUC by 0.4% (83.4% vs. 83.0%). Against the strongest CNN-based baselines, LRFL models dominate: ViT-B-LR (83.4%) surpasses the best CNN (Kim et al. [104], DN121 at 82.2%) by 1.2% on NIH ChestX-ray14, and ViT-B-LR (89.8%) exceeds the best CNN (Pham et al. [9], DN121 at 89.4%) by 0.4% on CheXpert. On COVIDx, ViT-B-LR (97.0%) outperforms the best CNN (DN121 [2] at 93.5%) by 3.5% and the previous SOTA (ViT-B [2] at 95.3%) by 1.7%. The paper also shows that standard fine-tuning tricks (mix-up, label smoothing, EMA) either fail to improve or degrade performance on chest X-rays, whereas LRFL provides consistent gains (e.g., ViT-B: 83.00% → 83.40% with LRFL vs. 82.37% with mix-up). With diffusion-model synthetic data augmentation, ViT-B-LR further extends its lead to 90.4% mAUC on CheXpert and 97.5% accuracy on COVIDx, surpassing all prior methods by 1.0–2.2%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the MAE pre-training pipeline for thorax disease classification established by Xiao et al. (WACV 2023), adopting the same pre-training setup and fine-tuning protocol as its primary baseline. The core methodological contribution extends truncated nuclear norm (TNN) regularization—originally developed for tensor completion and matrix recovery (Hu et al., 2013)—into the training of neural networks by introducing a novel separable approximation that makes the TNN term compatible with standard SGD. The theoretical analysis draws on local Rademacher complexity theory (Bartlett et al., 2005) to derive a sharp generalization bound for linear networks with low-rank features, connecting the empirical Low Frequency Property observed in medical imaging to a principled regularization objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The LRFL method offers a universally applicable, architecture-agnostic regularization that addresses a persistent practical challenge in radiographic image analysis: the adverse influence of noise and non-disease background regions on classification. The demonstrated reduction in kernel complexity and the stronger performance gains in low-data regimes (1.05% at 5% data vs. ~0.1% at 50%) are particularly relevant given the scarcity of high-quality medical annotations. Open questions include whether the separable TNN approximation remains tight as the feature rank T grows, whether the approach transfers to other imaging modalities (CT, MRI), and whether the periodic SVD updates in Algorithm 1 can be further accelerated for very large feature matrices. The finding that standard augmentation tricks (mix-up, label smoothing, EMA) degrade performance on chest X-rays also suggests that medical imaging may require fundamentally different regularization strategies than natural image classification.
