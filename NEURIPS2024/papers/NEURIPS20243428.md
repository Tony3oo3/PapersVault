## Problem

YOLO detectors rely on non-maximum suppression (NMS) for post-processing, which prevents true end-to-end deployment and adds inference latency, while their architectural components suffer from unexamined computational redundancy and constrained model capability. No prior work has simultaneously addressed both the NMS dependency and the holistic efficiency-accuracy trade-off across all YOLO components (head, downsampling, blocks, attention).

## Input / Output

The model takes a standard RGB image as input and directly outputs bounding boxes with class labels in a single forward pass, requiring no NMS or any other post-processing step. Latency is measured end-to-end on a T4 GPU with TensorRT FP16, and the model is trained and evaluated on the COCO dataset. The one-to-one inference head produces exactly one prediction per anchor point, eliminating the redundant predictions that necessitate NMS in conventional YOLOs.

## Method

YOLOv10 introduces two complementary strategies. First, consistent dual assignments enable NMS-free training: a one-to-many head provides rich supervision during training while a one-to-one head is used at inference, with a consistent matching metric (α_o2o = r·α_o2m, β_o2o = r·β_o2m) that provably minimizes the 1-Wasserstein supervision gap between the two branches. Second, a holistic efficiency-accuracy driven design replaces the standard classification head with depthwise separable convolutions, decouples spatial downsampling from channel expansion (pointwise then depthwise instead of a single stride-2 3×3 conv), and applies a compact inverted block (CIB) selectively to stages identified as redundant via intrinsic rank analysis. For accuracy, large-kernel (7×7) depthwise convolutions with reparameterization are added to CIB in deep stages of small models, and a partial self-attention module splits channels in half and applies MHSA+FFN to only one half, injecting global modeling at minimal cost.

## Results

On COCO val, YOLOv10 achieves state-of-the-art end-to-end latency-accuracy trade-offs across all model scales. YOLOv10-S reaches 46.3 AP at 2.49 ms latency (7.2M params, 21.6G FLOPs), while YOLOv10-X achieves 54.4 AP at 10.70 ms (29.5M params, 160.4G FLOPs). Compared to the YOLOv8 baseline, YOLOv10 gains 0.3–1.4 AP while reducing parameters by 28–57% and latency by 37–70%. The NMS-free design eliminates post-processing overhead, yielding end-to-end latencies of 1.84 ms (N) to 10.70 ms (X) on T4 GPU with TensorRT FP16.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against YOLOv8 (the direct baseline), YOLOv10 achieves 1.2–1.4 AP gains on N/S variants while cutting parameters by 28–36% and latency by 65–70%; on L/X, it gains 0.3/0.5 AP with 1.8×/2.3× fewer parameters. Versus RT-DETR, YOLOv10-S is 1.8× faster (2.49 ms vs. 4.58 ms) at nearly identical AP (46.3 vs. 46.5), and YOLOv10-X is 1.3× faster (10.70 ms vs. 13.71 ms) while matching AP (54.4 vs. 54.3). Compared to YOLOv9-C, YOLOv10-B delivers the same 52.5 AP with 46% lower latency (5.74 ms vs. 10.57 ms) and 25% fewer parameters. Against YOLOv6-3.0, YOLOv10-N/S outperform by 1.5/2.0 AP with 51%/61% fewer parameters. Gold-YOLO-L is surpassed by YOLOv10-L by 1.4 AP with 68% fewer parameters and 32% lower latency. YOLO-MS is outperformed by YOLOv10-B/M with 46%/62% latency reduction at equal or better AP. Among lightweight detectors, YOLOv10-N beats DEYO-tiny by 0.9 AP at lower latency, and YOLOv10-S surpasses DAMO-YOLO-S by 0.3 AP with 56% fewer parameters. YOLOv7-X is outperformed by YOLOv10-B by 0.4 AP with 73% lower latency (5.74 ms vs. 21.45 ms).

## Relation to Prior Work

YOLOv10 builds directly on YOLOv8 as its architectural baseline and extends the broader YOLO lineage (YOLOv4–v9) by addressing two persistent limitations: the reliance on NMS post-processing and unexamined computational redundancy across components. For NMS-free training, it departs from prior one-to-one matching approaches (OneNet, DeFCN, FCOS_pss) that sacrifice supervision quality, instead introducing a dual-branch design where a one-to-many head provides rich training signal while a one-to-one head handles inference, unified by a theoretically-derived consistent matching metric. The holistic efficiency-accuracy design strategy (compact inverted blocks, partial self-attention, rank-guided block allocation, decoupled downsampling) represents a systematic, component-by-component audit that contrasts with the ad-hoc architectural additions seen in prior YOLO variants, while the PSA module draws on attention head redundancy findings and LeViT-style dimension reduction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

YOLOv10 establishes a new state-of-the-art for real-time end-to-end detection, achieving 46% lower latency than YOLOv9-C at matched AP and 1.8× faster inference than RT-DETR-R18 at similar accuracy, making it a strong default choice for deployment-sensitive applications. The consistent matching metric provides a general, hyperparameter-free principle for harmonizing dual-branch supervision that could transfer to other detector families. A key open question is the residual performance gap (up to 1.0 AP for small models) between NMS-free and NMS-based training, which the authors attribute to limited feature discriminability in smaller models; closing this gap and exploring pretraining on larger datasets like Objects365 are identified as future directions.
