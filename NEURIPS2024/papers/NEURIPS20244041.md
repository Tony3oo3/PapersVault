## Problem

Prior work on group actions in neural networks almost exclusively models group actions on the data space, overlooking scenarios where the group acts on unobserved latent factors (e.g., occluded digits, 3D object geometry behind 2D projections, or contrast changes applied only to a subregion of an image). This paper addresses the gap by proposing to learn group actions directly on latent representations, enabling the model to handle cases where the group action is not a valid transformation of the observed data.

## Input / Output

The model consumes pairs of images (x₁, x₂) from the same orbit along with the known group element g such that x₂ = g·x₁. The encoder maps each image to a latent vector z = [z_v; z_i] split into group-varying and group-invariant components; the group action is applied only to z_v, and the decoder renders the transformed image. For 2D/3D rotation groups, the varying latent is reshaped into a k×n matrix and the rotation matrix is applied row-wise; for the affine contrast group, a simple affine transform is applied to z_v. The output is a single rendered image at the target group-action pose, with no test-time optimization required.

## Method

The core mechanism is an autoencoder in which the group action α: G × Z → Z is applied to the latent representation before decoding, rather than being baked into the data-space architecture. The latent space is decomposed into a direct product Z_v × Z_i, where the group acts nontrivially only on Z_v, leaving Z_i fixed. Training minimizes a symmetric reconstruction loss L(x₂, D(g·z₁)) + L(x₁, D(g⁻¹·z₂)), optionally augmented with LPIPS perceptual loss for rendered 3D scenes. To counteract detail loss from spatial mixing during global group actions, the decoder incorporates U-Net-style skip connections with cross-attention modules (encoder features as keys/values, decoder features as queries). Theoretically, the paper proves (Propositions 4.2–4.3) that any decoder consistent with the latent group action induces a valid group action on the decoder's image space, making the proposed framework a strict superset of prior data-space group-action methods.

## Results

The proposed latent group action model achieves the best PSNR and SSIM across all five datasets. On the rotated and blocked MNIST dataset (where the group acts on a latent factor, not the full image), it reaches 23.55 PSNR and 1.000 SSIM, substantially outperforming Winter et al. (14.05 PSNR, 0.586 SSIM) and Hwang et al. (10.19 PSNR, 0.990 SSIM). On the plane-in-the-sky 3D rendering task, it achieves 25.24 PSNR and 0.112 LPIPS, nearly halving the LPIPS of the best baseline (Dupont et al., 0.239) and dramatically outperforming Sajjadi et al. (0.280 LPIPS, 0.489 SSIM).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all four baselines on every dataset and metric. Against Winter et al. [32] (group-invariant/equivariant representations with steerable CNNs), the gap is most dramatic on the rotated-and-blocked MNIST: 23.55 vs. 14.05 PSNR, because Winter's method is designed for data-space group actions and cannot handle the occluding block that is invariant to rotation. Against Hwang et al. [13] (Maganet, which models the group as a Euclidean vector space under addition), the method wins by 10.78 PSNR on rotated MNIST and 13.36 PSNR on blocked MNIST, since SO(2) is not a Euclidean space and the diffeomorphism constraint limits Hwang's model. On the 3D rendering tasks, the method beats Dupont et al. [9] (equivariant neural rendering) by 2.0 PSNR and 0.041 LPIPS on NMR, and by 1.0 PSNR and 0.127 LPIPS on plane-in-the-sky, where Dupont's rigid latent-coordinate rotation is too inflexible for a fixed background. Against Sajjadi et al. [25] (Scene Representation Transformer), the method wins on plane-in-the-sky by 1.7 PSNR and 0.332 SSIM, as Sajjadi's transformer lacks any group structure and fails to capture full 3D rotations from a single view.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm of learning group actions on the data space (as in G-CNNs, steerable CNNs, and equivariant diffusion models) by instead learning group actions on latent representations within an autoencoder framework. It generalizes prior latent-group-action approaches (Park et al., Wang et al.) that relied on contrastive losses or specific formulations, and contrasts with Hwang et al.'s Maganet (which restricts groups to Euclidean vector spaces via diffeomorphic encoders/decoders) and Winter et al. (which requires group-specific equivariant layers). The key conceptual departure is that the group need not act on the data space at all, enabling scenarios like occlusion or partial-object rotation that are inexpressible under data-space group actions. The authors prove their formulation is a strict superset of data-space group action methods via a consistency condition on the decoder.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and addresses a genuine gap: many real-world symmetry scenarios (occluded digits, rotating objects against fixed backgrounds, contrast changes on sub-regions) involve groups acting on latent factors rather than the observed data, and no prior method handles this without architectural constraints. The practical impact is that practitioners can use any expressive encoder/decoder (no group-specific layers) and still model group structure, potentially benefiting from the full ecosystem of modern deep learning architectures. The main limitation is the requirement for ground-truth group action labels during training, which the authors acknowledge and leave semi-supervised/unsupervised extensions as future work. Open questions include whether the approach scales to high-dimensional continuous groups beyond SO(2)/SO(3) and whether the consistency condition (Proposition 4.3) can be enforced more tightly in practice.
