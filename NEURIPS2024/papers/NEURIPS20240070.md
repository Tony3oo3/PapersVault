## Problem

Controlling a simulated humanoid with dexterous hands to grasp diverse objects and follow arbitrary trajectories remains unsolved. Prior methods either use disembodied hands (sacrificing physical realism and body-hand coordination) or restrict themselves to single object sequences, vertical lifts, or pre-recorded trajectories. The combination of high-DoF bipedal balance, stable multi-finger grasps across thousands of object shapes, and free-form trajectory following has not been achieved in a single scalable policy.

## Input / Output

At test time, the method requires only the object mesh (encoded as a 512-d Basis Point Set latent code), a random initial object pose, and a desired 6-DoF object trajectory (position, orientation, linear and angular velocity) sampled 20 frames ahead at 15 Hz. No paired full-body motion, pre-grasp poses, or reference body kinematics are needed at inference. The output is a sequence of PD-controller targets for all 51 actuated joints (153 DoF total) of an SMPL-X humanoid, producing physically simulated full-body grasping and transport motion in Isaac Gym at 60 Hz simulation / 30 Hz policy rate.

## Method

Omnigrasp uses a two-stage hierarchical RL framework. First, PULSE-X is trained by distilling a dexterous motion imitator (PHC-X, trained on a constructed dexterous AMASS dataset pairing body motion with hand motion from GRAB/Re:InterHand) into a 48-d variational latent space via online DAgger-style distillation, yielding a frozen decoder and prior that map latent codes to joint actuation. Second, the Omnigrasp policy (a GRU with 512-d hidden state) is trained with PPO to output residual latent codes added to the prior's mean, which are decoded by PULSE-X's frozen decoder into PD targets. The key insight is that this pretrained motion representation serves as a strong human-like action prior, enabling effective RL exploration in a 48-d space rather than the 153-d joint space, and allowing simple state/reward designs without any paired body-object motion data. A stepwise pre-grasp reward (approach → precise hand imitation → object trajectory tracking) guides the policy using synthetic pre-grasps from GrabNet, while a 3D trajectory generator and hard-negative mining over >1200 objects provide training diversity without MoCap trajectories.

## Results

On the GRAB cross-object test set (140 sequences, 5 unseen objects), Omnigrasp trained with generated trajectories achieves 100% grasp success rate and 94.1% trajectory success rate, with a position error of 30.2 mm and TTR of 99.6%. On the cross-subject test set (92 sequences, 44 objects), it reaches 98.9% grasp success and 90.5% trajectory success. On the OakInk dataset (1700 objects), the method scales to 1272/1330 training objects with 93.7% grasp success and 86.2% trajectory success, and generalizes to unseen objects at 94.3% grasp success. On OMOMO (7 large objects), it achieves 100% success on both grasping and trajectory following.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Omnigrasp substantially outperforms all baselines on trajectory following, which is the hardest metric. Against PPO-10B (raw RL without PULSE-X, ~10B samples), Omnigrasp improves Succ_traj from 55.9% to 94.1% and reduces acceleration error from 21.0 to 5.4 mm/frame², demonstrating the critical role of the motion latent space. Against PHC (direct motion imitation with ground-truth kinematic poses), Omnigrasp achieves 100% vs. 3.6% grasp success, showing that the imitator's ~30 mm tracking error is too large for precise grasping. Against AMP (adversarial motion prior), Omnigrasp more than doubles Succ_traj (94.1% vs. 46.6%) and improves Succ_grasp (100% vs. 90.4%). Against the prior SOTA Braun et al. [6], Omnigrasp improves Succ_grasp from 79% to 100% and TTR from 85% to 99.6%, while also reporting Succ_traj (94.1%) which Braun et al. do not evaluate. The only metric where a baseline is competitive is E_rot, where PPO-10B achieves 0.4 rad vs. Omnigrasp's 0.93 rad, attributed to the gap between randomly generated rotations and MoCap object rotations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends PULSE, a universal humanoid motion representation trained on large-scale AMASS data, to the dexterous setting (PULSE-X) by augmenting training data with articulated finger motion and distilling a motion imitator (PHC-X) into a 48-dim latent action space. It departs from prior dexterous manipulation work (D-Grasp, UniDexGrasp, PGDM) that relies on disembodied hands controlled by non-physical forces, and from full-body interaction methods (Braun et al., PhysHOI) that train one policy per object or require paired MoCap data. The key conceptual departure is using a single universal motion prior as the action space for a hierarchical RL policy, eliminating the need for task-specific adversarial priors (AMP, PMP) or per-object policies, and replacing ground-truth object trajectories with a synthetic 3D trajectory generator extended from PACER.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Omnigrasp demonstrates that a pretrained universal motion representation can enable a single policy to grasp over 1200 diverse objects and follow arbitrary 3D trajectories with a full-body dexterous humanoid, a scale and generality not previously achieved in simulation. The cross-dataset result—training only on 50 GRAB objects yet successfully grasping 1000+ unseen OakInk objects—suggests the learned motor skills transfer broadly, which is encouraging for humanoid robotics deployment. However, the method remains simulation-only, rotation tracking error is still notable (~0.9 rad), in-hand manipulation is unsupported, and the limited baseline set (only three comparators, one of which is a weak PPO-10B) makes it difficult to fully calibrate the magnitude of improvement. Open questions include whether the universal motion prior approach scales to more complex manipulation (reorientation, articulation) and how the system transfers to real hardware.
