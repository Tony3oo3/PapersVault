## Problem

Latent diffusion-based video models currently rely on 2D image VAEs with uniform frame sampling for temporal compression, which ignores inter-frame motion and produces low-FPS, visually unsmooth videos. While 3D video VAEs exist (e.g., Open-Sora-Plan), they are trained independently and create a latent space gap with pretrained image/video models, requiring enormous compute to bridge even when using those models as initialization. No commonly used continuous 3D video VAE exists that is both spatio-temporally compressive and directly compatible with existing pretrained diffusion models like Stable Diffusion.

## Input / Output

The model accepts either a video clip of (T+1)×H×W×3 RGB frames or a single H×W×3 image (which is processed with temporal padding when T=0), and produces continuous latent tokens of shape (t+1)×h×w×c with 4× temporal and 8× spatial compression. A key design choice is that the same network handles both modalities seamlessly, and temporal tiling (with one-frame overlap between blocks) enables encoding/decoding of arbitrary video lengths and resolutions within a fixed memory budget (e.g., 17×576×576 per block at inference).

## Method

CV-VAE inflates the Stable Diffusion 2D VAE into a 3D VAE by replacing 2D convolutions with 3D convolutions (initialized by copying 2D weights and zero-padding the temporal dimension), but uses a hybrid 2D+3D architecture where half the convolutions in each ResBlock remain 2D, reducing parameters and FLOPs by ~30% with negligible quality loss. The core novelty is a latent space regularization loss: the video encoder's latent output is decoded by the frozen 2D image decoder, and the reconstruction is compared against a mapped version of the input video (using random frame sampling as the mapping function), forcing the 3D VAE's latent distribution to stay aligned with the 2D VAE's. The full training objective combines standard VAE losses (L1 reconstruction, adversarial, KL) with this decoder-based regularization (λ₁=1, λ₂=0), trained on mixed image and video data for 200K steps on 16 A100s. This compatibility means pretrained models like SVD or VideoCrafter2 can swap in CV-VAE with only ~12K parameters of fine-tuning to generate 4× more frames with smoother motion.

## Results

On COCO-Val, CV-VAE (2D+3D) achieves PSNR 27.6, SSIM 0.805, and LPIPS 0.136, outperforming VAE-SD2.1 (PSNR 26.6, SSIM 0.773) and VAE-OSP (PSNR 27.0, SSIM 0.791) while providing 4× temporal compression. On Webvid-Val, CV-VAE reaches PSNR 28.5, SSIM 0.817, and LPIPS 0.143, surpassing VAE-OSP (PSNR 26.7, SSIM 0.781) by a wide margin. The full 3D variant achieves marginally better scores (PSNR 27.7/28.6) at the cost of ~47% more parameters (100M+156M vs. 68M+114M). When integrated into SVD without finetuning, CV-VAE in image mode preserves generation quality (FVD 419 vs. 402), while in video mode with output-layer finetuning it generates 97-frame videos with PIC 0.858 on UCF101 versus 0.791 for the original 25-frame SVD.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against VAE-SD2.1 [28], CV-VAE (2D+3D) wins on COCO across all three metrics (PSNR +1.0, SSIM +0.032, LPIPS −0.009) and on Webvid SSIM/LPIPS, though it trails slightly on Webvid PSNR (28.5 vs. 28.9); critically, CV-VAE provides 4× temporal compression versus 1×. Against VAE-OSP [1], the closest 4×-compression competitor, CV-VAE wins on every metric on both datasets (e.g., Webvid PSNR 28.5 vs. 26.7, LPIPS 0.143 vs. 0.166) while using fewer parameters (68M+114M vs. 94M+135M). Against VQGAN [12] and TATS [14], CV-VAE dominates by large margins (e.g., COCO LPIPS 0.136 vs. 0.186 and 0.287). In the SVD integration test (Table 3), CV-VAE in image mode (frozen) matches SVD quality (FVD 419 vs. 402), and in video mode with output-layer finetuning it improves PIC from 0.791 to 0.858 on UCF101 while generating 4× more frames. Against RIFE [18] as a frame interpolator, CV-VAE wins on KVD (2.26 vs. 3.12) and PIC (0.734 vs. 0.721) but loses on FVD (295 vs. 253). For text-to-video with VideoCrafter2 [7], fine-tuned CV-VAE in video mode achieves the best CLIP Frame Consistency (0.301 vs. 0.292) and Textual Alignment (0.987 vs. 0.960). In the appendix comparison with VAE-SD3 [13], CVVAE-SD3 wins on image reconstruction (PSNR 33.6 vs. 32.6) but loses on video (PSNR 32.0 vs. 33.4) due to the higher temporal compression ratio.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Stable Diffusion image VAE (LDM) into a 3D video VAE by inflating 2D convolutions to 3D convolutions, following the model-inflation paradigm used in prior discrete video VAEs like 3D-VQVAE and TATS. However, it departs from all prior video VAEs (VQGAN, TATS, MAGVIT, Open-Sora-Plan) by introducing a latent space regularization loss that explicitly constrains the 3D VAE's latent distribution to match the 2D image VAE's, ensuring drop-in compatibility with pretrained diffusion models. The key conceptual departure is that prior work either used discrete tokens (VQ-VAE framework) or trained continuous 3D VAEs independently without considering alignment to existing pretrained latent spaces, which the paper shows causes a critical latent space gap that is prohibitively expensive to bridge.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: existing video diffusion models (SVD, VideoCrafter2) can generate 4× more frames with smoother motion by simply swapping in CV-VAE and fine-tuning ~12K parameters, avoiding the massive compute cost of retraining in a new latent space. The latent space regularization technique is simple and broadly applicable to any scenario where a new VAE must remain compatible with a pretrained generative model. A key limitation is that the latent channel dimension is locked to the image VAE's (4 channels for SD2.1), which caps reconstruction quality; the appendix shows that with SD3's 16-channel VAE, video reconstruction still lags behind the image-only baseline, suggesting the temporal compression cost grows with higher fidelity requirements. An open question is whether this compatibility approach generalizes to transformer-based video VAEs or to models not initialized from SD.
