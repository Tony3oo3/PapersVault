## Problem

Transformer-based architectures are highly vulnerable to adversarial attacks where small input modifications can easily fool the model, yet existing defenses either require expensive retraining and fine-tuning or rely on task-specific domain knowledge, limiting their applicability to large language models and cross-domain generalization. The paper seeks a universal, training-free strategy that can be applied to any pretrained transformer to enhance its robustness without architectural redesign.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20244369/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

ProAttention operates as a drop-in replacement for the standard attention layer, consuming the attention weight matrix A and value matrix V (both N×D) and producing a robust output matrix Z of identical dimensions. It is modality-agnostic: the same 4-line PyTorch module processes text token sequences, image patches, or graph node features identically, with the only tunable hyperparameters being the number of Newton-IRLS iterations K (default 3) and the penalty threshold (γ for MCP, δ for Huber). The output format is unchanged from the vanilla transformer—class logits, generated tokens, or node embeddings—so no downstream modifications are required.

## Method

The paper reformulates the vanilla self-attention mechanism as a weighted least-squares (WLS) token estimator, revealing that adversarial tokens act as outliers whose quadratic residuals dominate the output representation. To counter this, it replaces the ℓ₂ penalty with robust alternatives (MCP, Huber, ℓ₁) and derives a Newton-IRLS algorithm with a closed-form reweighted-sum iteration and a rigorous loss-descent guarantee (Theorem 3.2). Concretely, at each of K iterations the algorithm computes pairwise distances between the current estimate and all value vectors, derives per-token weights that down-weight or zero-out large residuals, normalizes these weights against the original attention scores, and updates the estimate via a single matrix multiplication. The entire procedure is packaged as a ProAttention module (4 core lines of PyTorch code) that can be inserted into any pretrained transformer—BERT, ViT, GAT, LLaMA—without additional training or fine-tuning, adding only (1+2K)·N·N·D operations versus 2·N·N·D for vanilla attention.

## Results

On AGNEWS topic classification under TextFooler, ProTransformer (MCP) improves AUA by 19.5% (BERT: 19.7→39.2%), 28.3% (ALBERT: 20.6→48.9%), 16.1% (DistilBERT: 13.2→29.3%), and 11.4% (RoBERTa: 13.0→24.4%) over vanilla backbones without any fine-tuning. In vision, Pro-ViT outperforms the second-best model (ConViT) by 35.64%, 46.28%, and 33.14% under PGD at budgets 1/255, 4/255, and 8/255 on CIFAR-10. In graph learning, Pro-GAT achieves 66.5% node classification accuracy at 40% perturbation budget on Cora-ML, surpassing the backbone GAT (56.7%) and all other baselines. For LLMs, Pro-Vicuna reduces jailbreak ASR to 1.8% (δ=0.1), comparable to SmoothLLM's randomized smoothing defense.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Pro-BERT(MCP) without fine-tuning (AUA 39.2% under TextFooler) already matches or exceeds FreeLB (38.0%), PGD-Adv (36.8%), MixADA (35.6%), and TA-VAT (36.2%), all of which require adversarial training. When combined with adversarial training, Pro-BERT(MCP)+AT achieves the best AUA of 56.8% under TextFooler, outperforming BERT+AT (42.1%) by 14.7 points. In vision (Table 3), Pro-ViT dominates all competing vision transformers (DeiT, ConViT, BEiT, Swin, ViT) under PGD at every budget, e.g., 77.39% vs. ConViT's 41.75% at 1/255. In graph learning (Table 4), Pro-GAT outperforms GCN, GNNGuard, RGCN, GRAND, ProGNN, Jaccard-GCN, and SoftMedian across all perturbation budgets on Cora-ML, achieving 66.5% at 40% budget vs. SoftMedian's 58.1% and GAT's 56.7%. For LLM jailbreak defense, Pro-Vicuna(Huber) with δ=0.1 reduces ASR to 1.8%, matching SmoothLLM's randomized smoothing (which requires multiple inferences) while being a single-pass plug-and-play layer.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes classical robust statistics tools (MCP, Huber, L1 penalties, IRLS) into the transformer attention context by establishing a new theoretical interpretation of self-attention as a weighted least-squares token estimator. It departs from prior empirical defenses (adversarial training, data augmentation) and certifiable defenses that require substantial training or inference overhead, and from architecture-specific robustness designs (RobuTrans, CrisisBERT, TableFormer) that rely on domain knowledge. The closest prior work is Han et al.'s robust kernel density estimation for self-attention, but ProAttention avoids the excess computation and limited improvement of that approach by using a closed-form Newton-IRLS iteration that converges in 2–3 steps. The key conceptual departure is the plug-and-play paradigm: no retraining or fine-tuning is required, making it orthogonal to and combinable with existing defenses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play nature makes ProTransformer immediately deployable on any pretrained transformer without the prohibitive cost of adversarial retraining, which is especially valuable for large language models where retraining is impractical. The demonstrated generality across language, vision, and graph domains with a single 4-line code change suggests a broadly applicable robustness primitive. However, the paper acknowledges that the full potential of ProAttention may only be realized through end-to-end training or fine-tuning, which remains unexplored. Open questions include whether the computational overhead (1–2× inference time) is acceptable in latency-sensitive LLM serving, and whether the robustness gains persist under adaptive attacks that specifically target the reweighting mechanism.
