## Problem

Spiking neural networks lack an effective, hardware-friendly positional encoding mechanism for sequential tasks. Existing SNN PE approaches (e.g., Spikformer's relative PE) produce non-unique spike representations across positions and generate non-binary values when added to input spikes, violating the spike-form constraint essential for neuromorphic hardware. The paper asks whether a biologically grounded, spike-native PE can be designed that ensures positional uniqueness while remaining compatible with LIF neuron circuits.

## Input / Output

The method consumes binary spike matrices of shape T×B×L×D (time steps × batch × sequence length × feature dimension) and produces a same-shaped spike matrix with positional information injected. For time series and text, the sequence length L is natural; for images, patches must be linearized into a pseudo-sequence (e.g., 160 patches for CIFAR10-DVS), making the method incompatible with SNNs that lack an explicit sequence-length axis. The CPG-PE module itself is parameter-free and outputs a 2N-dimensional spike vector per position, which is concatenated with the input before a linear projection restores the original feature dimension D.

## Method

The key theoretical insight is that the standard sinusoidal PE in Transformers is a particular solution to the membrane potential dynamics of a two-neuron CPG with mutual inhibition/excitation (coupled oscillators with constant intrinsic drive). CPG-PE instantiates N pairs of such CPG neurons, each pair producing spike trains via Heaviside-stepped sinusoids with geometrically spaced frequencies (η·t/τ^(i/N)), yielding 2N binary channels that uniquely encode each position. The implementation concatenates the original spike matrix X with the CPG-PE output X′ along the feature axis, then applies a linear layer, batch normalization, and a spike neuron layer to project back to dimension D—avoiding the non-binary addition problem of prior SNN PE methods. The authors further prove that a single CPG-PE neuron can be physically realized with just two LIF neurons (an Emitter and a Resetter) configured with specific input currents, confirming full compatibility with existing neuromorphic chip designs without additional circuitry.

## Results

On time-series forecasting, Spikformer with CPG-PE achieves an average R² of 0.744 across Metr-LA, PEMS-Bay, Solar, and Electricity, outperforming Spikformer without PE (0.719) and with the original RPE (0.733). On text classification, Spikformer with CPG-PE reaches 79.41% average accuracy across 6 benchmarks (e.g., 82.42% on MR, 82.90% on SST-2), surpassing the no-PE baseline (77.25%) and Float-PE (78.44%). On ImageNet, CPG-PE achieves 71.17% top-1 accuracy with 15.66M parameters, beating both Spikformer w/o PE (69.46%) and Spikformer w/RPE (70.24%) while using fewer parameters than RPE (16.81M).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the SNN baselines from [32] (SpikeTCN w/o PE and SpikeRNN w/o PE), CPG-PE consistently improves R² by 0.01–0.02 and reduces RSE by 0.01–0.03 across all four time-series datasets, narrowing the gap to their ANN counterparts (TCN, RNN). Compared to Spikformer's original relative PE (RPE) from [4], CPG-PE outperforms on every benchmark: time-series (avg R² 0.744 vs. 0.733), text classification (79.41% vs. not directly reported but Float-PE at 78.44% is the closest non-spike alternative), and image classification (83.38% avg vs. 83.12% avg; 94.82% vs. 94.64% on CIFAR-10). On ImageNet, CPG-PE beats RPE by 0.93% accuracy (71.17% vs. 70.24%) while using 1.15M fewer parameters. Against the ANN references (Transformer [15], TCN [38], fine-tuned BERT [39], ViT [23]), SNNs with CPG-PE still trail by a margin (e.g., 79.41% vs. 84.33% avg on text; 71.17% vs. not directly comparable on ImageNet), but the gap is substantially smaller than without PE. The Random-PE ablation (77.23% avg) confirms that the structured CPG spike patterns, not merely added parameters, drive the improvement.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Spikformer architecture (Zhou et al., ICLR 2023) and the authors' own prior work on SNN time-series forecasting (Lv et al., ICML 2024), but its core conceptual contribution is a novel bridge between neuroscience and deep learning: it proves that the sinusoidal positional encoding in Transformers (Vaswani et al., 2017) is a particular solution of the membrane potential dynamics of a specific two-neuron central pattern generator. This departs from prior SNN positional encoding approaches (Spikformer's convolutional RPE, Spike-driven Transformer's similar design), which the authors argue are better characterized as spike-element-wise residual connections rather than true PE modules, and from ANN PE methods (HiPPO, trigonometric encodings) that are incompatible with spike-form computation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The CPG-PE provides the first parameter-free, spike-form, hardware-compatible positional encoding for SNNs, with the practical advantage of being implementable using only standard LIF neuron circuits on neuromorphic chips. The theoretical connection to CPGs also opens a speculative but intriguing line of inquiry into whether biological CPGs serve a positional-encoding-like function in sensory processing. Key limitations include the requirement for a sequence-length dimension (preventing direct application to non-sequential SNNs like SEW-ResNet), modest gains on image data where patches lack inherent order, and the fact that CPG-PE is absolute rather than relative, leaving the design of spike-form relative PE for SNNs as an open problem.
