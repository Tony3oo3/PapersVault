## Problem

Standard bilevel optimization methods (AID, ITD) for machine learning require strong convexity of the inner objective with respect to model parameters, an assumption that fails when over-parameterized neural networks are used, leading to multiple inner solutions and ambiguous, ill-conditioned implicit gradients. The paper identifies that many bilevel ML problems (instrumental regression, model-based RL) have a natural functional structure where the inner objective is strongly convex in the prediction function itself, but this functional perspective has not been exploited for algorithm design or theory.

## Input / Output

The method consumes two datasets (inner samples from distribution P and outer samples from Q), a parametric model family τ: Θ→H (typically a neural network) for the prediction function, and an optional separate model ν for the adjoint function. A key structural detail is that the adjoint objective requires a Hessian-vector product with respect to the prediction output v ∈ R^{d_v} (typically small dimension), not with respect to network parameters, which eliminates the need for second-order derivatives through the network and reduces memory costs. The output is the optimized outer parameter ω along with the learned prediction and adjoint functions, from which the total gradient ∇F(ω) is computed as a sum of an explicit term and an implicit term involving the adjoint.

## Method

FuncID adopts a 'differentiate implicitly, then parameterize' strategy: it first derives an unambiguous implicit gradient in an abstract Hilbert space H using a functional implicit differentiation theorem (based on Hadamard differentiability, weaker than continuous Fréchet differentiability) and the adjoint sensitivity method, yielding a well-conditioned quadratic adjoint objective L_adj in the same function space. Both the prediction function h_ω* and adjoint function a_ω* are then approximated by neural networks trained with standard stochastic gradient descent on empirical objectives, with optional regularization. Unlike AID/ITD which parameterize the inner solution and solve an ill-conditioned linear system in parameter space, FuncID computes the Hessian with respect to the small-dimensional output v, avoiding instabilities from over-parameterization. Convergence to stationary points is established via biased SGD analysis, with error terms controlled by inner and adjoint sub-optimality gaps that can be reduced by increasing network capacity.

## Results

On the dsprites IV regression task (5K training samples), FuncID achieves the lowest test MSE among all methods, outperforming the state-of-the-art DFIV with a statistically significant margin (p-value = 0.003, one-sided paired t-test) and also attaining the lowest inner-loss values, indicating more reliable convergence. On the CartPole model-based RL benchmark, FuncID reaches the maximum evaluation reward of 5 in the well-specified setting (32 hidden units), surpassing OMD which plateaus at reward 4, while matching MLE. In the misspecified setting (3 hidden units), FuncID performs comparably to OMD and significantly outperforms MLE, demonstrating robustness to model capacity limitations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DFIV (Xu et al., 2021a), the prior state-of-the-art for deep IV regression, FuncID achieves lower out-of-sample MSE on the 5K-sample dsprites experiment (p = 0.003) and comparable performance at 10K samples. Against AID and ITD, FuncID substantially outperforms both on the IV regression task, with the paper attributing the gap to the parametric approach's failure to exploit the functional structure and its reliance on ill-conditioned Hessian systems. Against the penalty-based methods of Shen and Chen (2023), FuncID beats the gradient penalty (GD penal.) and is comparable to or slightly better than the value function penalty (Val. penal.), though the latter exhibits higher variance with occasional poor outliers. In the CartPole RL task, FuncID outperforms OMD (Nikishin et al., 2022) in the well-specified setting (reward 5 vs. 4) and is comparable in the misspecified setting, while both bilevel methods significantly outperform the standard MLE baseline (Sutton, 1991) under model misspecification. FuncID also converges faster than MLE and yields consistently better prediction error than OMD in the RL experiments.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the standard parametric bilevel optimization framework (AID, ITD) by reformulating the inner problem over a Hilbert function space rather than a finite-dimensional parameter space, adopting a 'differentiate implicitly, then parameterize' strategy. It builds on the adjoint sensitivity method from optimal control (Pontryagin) and the implicit function theorem, but extends both to infinite-dimensional settings using the weaker notion of Hadamard differentiability instead of continuous Fréchet differentiability. The closest prior work is restricted to kernel methods (Rosset, Kunapuli et al.) or abstract algorithms limited to finite Hilbert spaces (Suonpera and Valkonen), making this the first functional bilevel framework compatible with deep network function approximation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The functional perspective resolves the ambiguity problem that arises when over-parameterized networks yield multiple inner-level solutions, by making strong convexity a mild assumption on the prediction function rather than the (high-dimensional) network parameters. This yields a stable algorithm with reduced memory and time costs compared to AID, since Hessian-vector products are computed with respect to the small output dimension rather than network parameters. Open questions include quantifying inner/adjoint sub-optimality errors more tightly, extending to other function spaces (RKHS, Sobolev), and handling non-smooth objectives. The framework is currently validated on two application domains (IV regression, model-based RL), leaving broader empirical validation as future work.
