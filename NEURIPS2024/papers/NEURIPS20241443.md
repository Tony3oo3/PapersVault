## Problem

Most existing methods for stochastic compositional optimization (SCO) require smoothness of both the outer and inner functions, excluding important applications involving nonsmooth objectives (e.g., deep networks with ReLU, risk-averse portfolio management). For the nonsmooth case, prior work either provides only asymptotic convergence or relies on restrictive additional assumptions such as weak-convexity and relative smoothness. This paper addresses the nonconvex nonsmooth SCO problem where both f and g are merely Lipschitz continuous, using gradient-free (zeroth-order) methods with non-asymptotic convergence guarantees.

## Input / Output

The algorithms consume only zeroth-order (function-value) evaluations of the stochastic components F(·;ξ) and G(·;ζ) at perturbed points x ± δw, with no access to gradients or Jacobians. Because the inner function g(x) = E_ζ[G(x;ζ)] is intractable, the method replaces exact g-evaluations with mini-batch averages over b_g samples. The output is a (δ,ε)-Goldstein stationary point of the composite objective Φ(x) = f(g(x)), where δ is the smoothing radius (set to 0.1 in experiments) and ε is the stationarity tolerance measured via the Goldstein δ-subdifferential rather than the intractable Clarke subdifferential.

## Method

GFCOM combines randomized smoothing with a two-point zeroth-order gradient estimator: it samples a random direction w, evaluates the outer function F at mini-batch estimates of g(x ± δw), and forms a finite-difference gradient of the smoothed surrogate Φ_δ, whose gradient provably lies in the Goldstein δ-subdifferential. The key practical challenge—that g(x) is an intractable expectation—is handled by replacing g(x ± δw) with b_g-sample averages y_{t,j} and z_{t,j}. GFCOM+ further applies SPIDER-style recursive variance reduction (v_t = q_t − q_{t−1} + v_{t−1}) on the outer function only, improving the ε-dependence from ε^{-6} to ε^{-5}; the inner function is deliberately left un-reduced because the perturbation noise δ(w_t − w_{t−1}) does not vanish asymptotically. For the convex nonsmooth case, a two-phase warm-start strategy (WS-GFCOM) first drives sub-optimality below a threshold ρ using GFCOM, then refines to a Goldstein stationary point, yielding an improved d^{3}δ^{-2.4}ε^{-4} + d^{3.5}δ^{-2}ε^{-5} complexity.

## Results

The paper establishes non-asymptotic stochastic zeroth-order oracle complexity bounds for finding (δ,ε)-Goldstein stationary points of nonsmooth compositional objectives. GFCOM achieves O(d^{3.5}δ^{-3}ε^{-6}) complexity, while the variance-reduced GFCOM+ improves this to O(d^{3.5}δ^{-3}ε^{-5}). For the convex nonsmooth case, the warm-started WS-GFCOM+ further reduces the leading term to O(d^{3}δ^{-2.4}ε^{-4} + d^{3.5}δ^{-2}ε^{-5}). Empirically, GFCOM+ converges substantially faster than both GFCOM and the Kiefer-Wolfowitz baseline across all six portfolio management datasets and three RL state sizes (n=400, 600, 800).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares GFCOM and GFCOM+ against a Kiefer-Wolfowitz style zeroth-order baseline (the zeroth-order extension of SCGD from Wang et al. [2, 40]). On all six portfolio management datasets (Asia Pacific ex Japan OP, Europe OP, Global ex US OP, Global OP, Japan OP, North America OP), GFCOM+ converges much faster than both GFCOM and the KW baseline, while GFCOM and the KW baseline show nearly identical performance (their curves overlap in Figure 1). On the reinforcement learning Bellman residual minimization task with state sizes n=400, 600, and 800, GFCOM+ again significantly outperforms both the GFCOM and KW baseline methods. The variance reduction in GFCOM+ (using the SPIDER-style recursive estimator) is the key factor driving the empirical speedup, consistent with the improved theoretical complexity from ε^{-6} to ε^{-5}.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two research threads: (1) stochastic compositional optimization (SCO), where prior work (Wang et al. 2017, Hu et al. 2019, Yuan et al. 2019) assumes smoothness of both the outer and inner functions, and (2) zeroth-order nonsmooth nonconvex optimization, where recent work (Zhang et al. 2020, Lin et al. 2022, Chen et al. 2023) establishes finite-time complexity for finding Goldstein stationary points but does not handle composition structure. The paper bridges these by applying randomized smoothing and variance-reduction-based zeroth-order estimators to the compositional setting, where the inner function value is itself an expectation that must be estimated. The warm-start extension for the convex case draws on the optimal bandit/zero-order convex optimization result of Shamir (2017).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first non-asymptotic zeroth-order complexity guarantees for nonsmooth SCO, a setting that arises naturally in risk management, RL (Bellman residual minimization with robust losses), and meta-learning where smoothness assumptions fail. A notable limitation is that variance reduction is applied only to the outer function; the authors explain (Section 4.3) that extending it to the inner estimator is incompatible with randomized smoothing because the perturbation noise does not vanish asymptotically. Open questions include whether lower bounds match the upper bounds and whether the d^{3.5} dimension dependence can be improved, as well as whether the warm-start strategy generalizes beyond the convex case.
