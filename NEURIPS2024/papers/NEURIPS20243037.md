## Problem

Traditional Batch Normalization, designed for discrete networks without a temporal dimension, fundamentally breaks when applied to Neural ODEs because adaptive step-size solvers produce variable time discretizations that prevent reliable accumulation of population statistics across batches. Prior work observed that BN degrades Neural ODE performance but left the underlying cause unexplained. This paper identifies the temporal mismatch as the root cause and proposes a continuous-time BN variant that resolves it.

## Input / Output

For image classification, the model takes standard RGB images (32×32 CIFAR-10/100, 64×64 Tiny-ImageNet, 28×28 MNIST) and outputs class logits via a single linear layer after the Neural ODE. For physical system modeling, it consumes 17-dimensional robot state vectors and autoregressively predicts the next state; for circuit modeling, it takes 56 features (16 transistor widths + 40 historical current values) and predicts 40 future current values. A key operational detail is that the ODE solver's adaptive discretization means the number of internal time steps N varies per batch, so the normalization layer must handle arbitrary, non-aligned time grids at every forward pass.

## Method

TA-BN predefines M+1 evenly-spaced time grids over [0,T] and attaches a pair of population statistics (μ*, σ*²) and learnable parameters (γ*, α*) to each grid point. During training, normalization uses mini-batch statistics as usual, but γ and α are obtained by linearly interpolating from the grid to the actual ODE time point t_j; simultaneously, the observed mini-batch statistics are interpolated back onto the grid and folded into the running population estimates via a momentum update. During inference, both the parameters and the population statistics are interpolated from the grid to whatever time points the solver happens to visit, eliminating the need for exact grid alignment. The interpolation is piecewise linear between the two nearest grid neighbors, which the authors show approximates any continuous function arbitrarily well as M→∞ while remaining computationally cheap. This time-aware design lets TA-BN stabilize deep Neural ODE backbones (up to 18-layer U-Net) that otherwise diverge, and achieves 91.1% CIFAR-10 accuracy with 2.2M parameters in an unmixed single-ODE-plus-linear architecture.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with 2.2M parameters, TA-BN achieves 91.0% top-1 accuracy, substantially outperforming Mini-batch BN (82.2%), Pop-TI BN (54.8%), and no BN (51.7%), making it the first unmixed Neural ODE architecture to approach MobileNetV2-level parameter efficiency (~94% at ~2M params). On SVHN, TA-BN reaches 95.8% accuracy vs. 90.6% for Mini-batch BN. For physical system modeling, TA-BN achieves the lowest MAE on both Walker2d-v2 (0.946) and HalfCheetah-v2 (1.28), beating the prior best baseline [42] (1.02 and 1.46 respectively).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TA-BN decisively outperforms all BN variants on CIFAR-10: 91.0% vs. 82.2% (Mini-batch BN), 54.8% (Pop-TI BN), and 51.7% (no BN), with the gap widening on harder datasets (CIFAR-100: 66.4% vs. 49.2% for Mini-batch BN). Against prior Neural ODE methods, TA-BN surpasses Aug-NODE [7] by 30.4 points on CIFAR-10 (91.0% vs. 60.6%) and STEER [12] by 28.9 points (91.0% vs. 62.1%), while using comparable or fewer parameters. When integrated into existing variants, TA-BN boosts SONODE [34] from 30.2% to 85.3% and HBNODE [41] from 85.1% to 86.7% on CIFAR-10, confirming broad compatibility. On physical system modeling, TA-BN achieves the lowest MAE on both Walker2d-v2 (0.946) and HalfCheetah-v2 (1.28), improving over the forward-invariance baseline [42] (1.02 and 1.46) by 7% and 12% respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of Neural ODEs (Chen et al., 2018) and Batch Normalization (Ioffe & Szegedy, 2015), addressing a gap identified by prior empirical studies (Gusak et al., 2020; Oganesyan et al., 2020) that observed BN degrading Neural ODE performance without explaining why. While temporal BN variants exist for RNNs and spiking networks, the paper argues that Neural ODEs' adaptive step-size solvers create a fundamentally different challenge: discretized time grids differ across batches, making standard population-statistic accumulation infeasible. TA-BN departs from both the mini-batch and time-independent population approaches by associating learnable parameters and running statistics with a fixed set of regular time grids and performing temporal interpolation at arbitrary solver time points.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TA-BN enables scaling Neural ODE backbones beyond ~10 layers without numerical instability, and achieves 91.1% CIFAR-10 accuracy with 2.2M parameters in an unmixed architecture—the first to approach MobileNetV2-level parameter efficiency. The diagnosis that Pop-TI BN (the default in PyTorch/TorchDiffeq) produces meaningless test-time predictions is practically important for anyone who has attempted BN in Neural ODEs. Open questions include whether more sophisticated interpolation strategies (beyond linear) can reduce the runtime overhead, and whether TA-BN's benefits persist at ImageNet scale or in generative/reversible Neural ODE settings.
