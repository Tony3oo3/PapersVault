## Problem

Text-to-image diffusion models frequently fail to faithfully align generated images with text prompts, which the authors decompose into two distinct causes: concept ignorance (the model omits concepts mentioned in the prompt entirely) and concept mismapping (the model activates a concept but maps it to the wrong spatial region, especially for attributes). Existing remedies either require hand-crafted heuristic designs per misalignment type or rely on LLM-based prompt splitting that still suffers from local attribute binding errors, leaving no universal end-to-end solution.

## Input / Output

At inference, the method takes a single text prompt (up to 77 tokens, matching the CLIP text encoder limit of the base Stable Diffusion models) and produces a 512×512 or 1024×1024 RGB image with no additional overhead beyond the standard diffusion sampling pipeline. During training, the method additionally consumes ground-truth image-text pairs from COCO for the mixed latent strategy, segmentation masks from Grounded-SAM for the attribute concentration loss, and log-likelihood scores from a frozen BLIP image-to-text model as the alignment supervision signal.

## Method

CoMat is an end-to-end LoRA fine-tuning strategy for the UNet of a pre-trained latent diffusion model that introduces two complementary supervision mechanisms. The Concept Activation Module computes the negative log-likelihood of the prompt under a frozen image-to-text model (BLIP) given the generated image, creating a differentiable signal that forces the diffusion model to revisit and attend to previously ignored text tokens; this is stabilized by a fidelity preservation adversarial loss (using a UNet discriminator trained on generated latents from the original model) and a mixed latent strategy that jointly denoises pure-noise latents and noisy ground-truth image latents. The Attribute Concentration Module parses the prompt with spaCy to extract entity-attribute tuples, segments each entity's region with Grounded-SAM, and applies region-level and pixel-level positive/negative attention losses that encourage attribute tokens to concentrate within their entity's mask while suppressing activation outside it. Gradients flow through only 5 of 50 denoising steps (following the deep reward supervision technique), and the total training objective combines the image-to-text loss, both attribute concentration losses, and the adversarial loss with a balancing coefficient λ.

## Results

On T2I-CompBench, CoMat-SDXL achieves the best scores across attribute binding (Color 0.7827, Shape 0.5329, Texture 0.6468), spatial relationship (0.2428), and complex composition (0.3680), outperforming the SDXL baseline by up to +0.1948 on Color binding. On TIFA, CoMat-SDXL reaches 87.5 (vs. 85.9 for SDXL), and on DPG-Bench it scores 77.13 (vs. 74.65 for SDXL), both state-of-the-art among open-source models. The FID-10K score of 15.43 (with fidelity preservation and mixed latent) actually improves over the SD1.5 baseline's 16.69, confirming that alignment gains do not come at the cost of image quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoMat-SDXL outperforms its SDXL baseline on all six T2I-CompBench sub-categories, with the largest gain on Color binding (+0.1948) and Texture (+0.1169), and also leads on TIFA (+1.6) and DPG-Bench (+2.48). Against the strongest external baselines, CoMat-SDXL surpasses PixArt-α on Color (0.7827 vs. 0.6690), Shape (0.5329 vs. 0.4927), Spatial (0.2428 vs. 0.2064), and Complex (0.3680 vs. 0.3433), while PixArt-α retains a marginal edge on Texture (0.6477 vs. 0.6468) and Non-Spatial (0.3197 vs. 0.3187). CoMat-SDXL also beats Playground-v2 on all six sub-categories and on both TIFA (87.5 vs. 86.2) and DPG-Bench (77.13 vs. 74.54). The attention-based baselines (Attend-and-Excite, TokenCompose) and planning-based methods (StructureDiffusion, Composable Diffusion) are substantially outperformed, with CoMat-SDXL leading by 0.14–0.38 on individual sub-categories. Notably, CoMat-SD1.5 (the lighter model) already surpasses PixArt-α on TIFA (85.8 vs. 82.9) and DPG-Bench (73.32 vs. 71.11), demonstrating that the fine-tuning strategy is model-agnostic.

## Relation to Prior Work

CoMat builds on the latent diffusion model framework (Stable Diffusion/SDXL) and the gradient-propagation technique from Deep Reward Supervisions [73] to enable end-to-end fine-tuning through the full denoising process. It departs from the dominant attention-modification approaches (Attend-and-Excite, TokenCompose) that require heuristic, task-specific designs for each misalignment type, and from planning-based methods that decompose prompts via LLMs but still suffer from local attribute errors. The closest prior work is Fang et al. [18], which also leverages an image captioning model, but CoMat directly optimizes the prompt's log-likelihood under the generated image rather than comparing generated captions to the prompt, and adds a fidelity preservation module (UNet discriminator on generated latents) plus a mixed latent strategy to prevent reward hacking.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CoMat offers a universal, end-to-end fine-tuning recipe that addresses multiple misalignment types (object existence, attribute binding, spatial/non-spatial relationships, complex compositions) without task-specific heuristics and without any extra inference cost, making it practically attractive for practitioners fine-tuning diffusion models. The finding that a 469M-parameter BLIP outperforms a 7.2B LLaVA as the supervisory image-to-text model—because sensitivity to attribute/relation/quantity perturbations matters more than captioning fluency—challenges the assumption that larger MLLMs are automatically better for this role. Open questions include whether the approach extends to video generation, how to handle same-name objects in attribute concentration, and whether MLLMs with richer reasoning can provide finer-grained guidance than captioning log-likelihood.
