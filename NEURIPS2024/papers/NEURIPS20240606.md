## Problem

Diffusion models achieve high-fidelity generation but require prohibitive computational resources for training, especially at high resolutions, limiting large-scale training to a few well-resourced organizations. While distillation has accelerated inference, the training cost of the teacher diffusion model remains the dominant bottleneck. PaGoDA addresses this by reordering the pipeline so that super-resolution occurs *after* distillation, enabling the expensive diffusion pretraining to happen at a drastically downsampled resolution (e.g., 8× downsampling yields a 64× reduction in training compute).

## Input / Output

The method takes a Gaussian prior sample z (and optionally a class label or CLIP text embedding) as input and produces a single-step RGB image at resolutions from 64×64 up to 512×512. Notably, PaGoDA operates in pixel space rather than latent space, eliminating the VAE decoding step that adds latency to latent-distilled models like LCM. For text-to-image, the generator is conditioned on CLIP text embeddings and a CFG scale ω, with the discriminator also receiving ω as an auxiliary condition. The DDIM inversion of real data produces the latent codes used during training, and the final generator maps these codes directly to pixel-space images in one forward pass.

## Method

PaGoDA is a three-stage pipeline: (1) pretrain a diffusion model on downsampled data (e.g., 64×64 for a 512×512 target), (2) distill the teacher into a one-step generator using a novel DDIM-inversion-based reconstruction loss that maps inverted latents back to real data (data-to-noise distillation), combined with an adversarial loss to cover the full prior manifold and avoid the prior-hole problem, and (3) progressively grow the generator by adding ResNet upscaler blocks while freezing most parameters, training only the new high-resolution layers with reconstruction and adversarial losses in the target space. The key architectural insight is that the reconstruction loss preserves high-frequency signal from real data across resolution jumps, preventing object misalignment, while the adversarial loss (using a StyleGAN-XL discriminator) captures fine details. Theorem 3.1 proves that the optimal student under this combined objective exactly matches the data distribution, unlike conventional noise-to-data distillation. For text-to-image, the paper introduces a classifier-free guided adversarial loss with a learned CFG weight estimator to handle the intractable sampling from the guided distribution.

## Results

PaGoDA achieves state-of-the-art FID on ImageNet with a single NFE across all resolutions: 1.21 at 64×64, 1.48 at 128×128, 1.56 at 256×256, and 1.80 at 512×512, outperforming both multi-step diffusion models (e.g., simple Diffusion at 3.54 FID at 512×512 with 250 NFE) and one-step GANs (StyleGAN-XL at 2.41 FID at 512×512). Its Inception Score (251.31 at 512×512) is competitive with CFG-augmented diffusion models, and its diversity recall (0.58–0.63) exceeds StyleGAN-XL (0.52–0.55). For text-to-image on COCO, PaGoDA achieves FID 20.4 with 1 NFE in 0.05s, matching the quality of its DeepFloyd-IF teacher (FID 22.3, 27 NFE) while being ~60× faster.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PaGoDA (1 NFE) outperforms all compared baselines on FID at every ImageNet resolution. Against the closest one-step competitor StyleGAN-XL (also 1 NFE), PaGoDA improves FID from 1.51→1.21 at 64×64, 1.81→1.48 at 128×128, 2.30→1.56 at 256×256, and 2.41→1.80 at 512×512, while also achieving higher diversity recall (0.63 vs. 0.52 at 64×64). Against multi-step diffusion models, the gains are larger: simple Diffusion (250 NFE) reports 3.54 FID at 512×512 vs. PaGoDA's 1.80, and DiT-XL (250 NFE) reports 12.03 FID at 512×512. PaGoDA also slightly edges out EDM2-XXL (63 NFE, FID 1.91) at 512×512. For text-to-image, PaGoDA (FID 20.4, 0.05s) is competitive with the best one-step latent distillation methods (ADD: FID 19.7, 0.09s; Scott: FID 21.9, 0.09s) while being roughly 2× faster, and substantially outperforms LCM (FID 30.4) and InstaFlow (FID 23.4). On the larger FID-30K benchmark, PaGoDA (10.23) trails GigaGAN (9.09) but beats DMD (11.49) and all other one-step methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

PaGoDA reorders the standard diffusion distillation pipeline by inserting a progressive super-resolution stage *after* one-step distillation rather than treating distillation as the terminal step. Its core mechanism—DDIM inversion to create data-latent pairs for a reconstruction (data-to-noise) loss—departs from the conventional noise-to-data distillation objective used in CTM, DMD, and ADD, and the progressive upscaling strategy borrows directly from Progressive Growing GAN. The adversarial component leverages StyleGAN-XL's discriminator architecture, while the diffusion teacher follows EDM's discrete-time scheduling, and the text-to-image extension introduces a novel classifier-free guided adversarial loss that has no direct precedent in prior GAN or distillation literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 64× reduction in diffusion pretraining cost (from 8×8 downsampling) is a practically significant efficiency win that could democratize large-scale diffusion training beyond well-resourced labs, and the pipeline is explicitly composable with Latent Diffusion Models for further compression. The theoretical guarantees (Theorem 3.1: optimal student matches p_data under the reconstruction loss; Theorem 3.2: training stability) provide a clean mathematical justification for why the data-to-noise formulation is fundamentally superior to standard distillation. Open questions include whether the approach transfers cleanly to LDM latent spaces (the paper leaves this as future work), how it scales to 1024×1024 and beyond, and whether the T2I results—limited by the inability to train Stage 1 on LAION due to content concerns—would improve with full three-stage training.
