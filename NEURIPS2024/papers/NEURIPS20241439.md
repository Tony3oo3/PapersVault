## Problem

Existing biologically plausible learning models use the top-down (TD) stream solely to propagate feedback signals for synaptic updates, ignoring its well-established second role in the visual cortex: directing attention toward tasks and locations of interest. This paper addresses the open challenge of integrating both learning and visual guidance within a single TD pathway, producing the first biologically motivated model where the TD stream simultaneously carries error signals for learning and instruction signals that guide bottom-up processing.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241439/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes RGB images (28×28 MNIST/Fashion-MNIST, 32×32 CIFAR-10, 28×28 Multi-MNIST overlaid digit pairs, 112×112 CelebA face crops) and, in the guided setting, a one-hot task vector specifying which sub-task to perform. In unguided mode it outputs a single class label; in guided mode it outputs a task-conditioned prediction (e.g., left vs. right digit class, or a binary attribute value for each of 40 CelebA attributes). Notably, a single shared linear decoder serves all tasks, unlike baselines that require a separate prediction head per task.

## Method

The architecture pairs a feedforward BU network with a symmetric TD network (reversed connectivity, transposed weight shapes) linked by lateral connections. A novel GaLU activation gates each BU neuron by the sign of its TD counter-neuron, so a task instruction propagated through the TD stream selectively activates a sparse sub-network that the BU stream then uses for computation. Learning uses a 'Counter-Hebb' rule that updates each synapse by the product of its pre-synaptic activity and the post-synaptic *counter* neuron's activity (rather than the post-synaptic neuron itself), applied identically to both BU and TD weights. The guided algorithm runs three passes per sample: TD (instruction → sub-network selection), BU (image → prediction via GaLU-gated ReLU), and TD (error → gradient-like signal via GaLU with bias-blocking). Under symmetric weights, ReLU in BU, GaLU in TD, and bias-blocking, the CH update is provably equivalent to exact backpropagation; under asymmetric initialization it approximates BP as training progresses.

## Results

In unguided image classification, CH with symmetric initialization (CH Sym Init) matches backpropagation: 98.34% on MNIST, 89.99% on Fashion-MNIST, and 55.54% on CIFAR-10, versus BP's 98.33%, 89.94%, and 55.47% respectively. In guided multi-task learning, CH Sym Init achieves 94.20% average task accuracy on Multi-MNIST and 89.69% on CelebA, competitive with state-of-the-art non-biological MTL methods (94.76–94.87% and 90.90–90.99% respectively). The asymmetric initialization variant (CH Asym Init) shows a larger gap, reaching 98.17% on MNIST and 88.92% on Multi-MNIST, but still outperforms most other biologically plausible methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

In the unguided setting (Table 1), CH Sym Init is essentially equivalent to BP across all three benchmarks (e.g., 55.54% vs. 55.47% on CIFAR-10), confirming the theoretical equivalence. CH Asym Init (54.28% on CIFAR-10) still outperforms all other biological methods including CIM (51.86%), FA (52.37%), and EP (49.28%). In the guided setting (Table 2), CH Sym Init (94.20% Multi-MNIST, 89.69% CelebA) trails the best MTL baselines by roughly 0.5–1.3 percentage points, but notably does so without dropout, regularization, early stopping, or task-specific decoders — all of which the baselines employ. The gap widens substantially for CH Asym Init (88.92% Multi-MNIST, 79.25% CelebA), highlighting the sensitivity to weight initialization. On CelebA specifically, CH Sym Init (89.69%) is within ~1.2% of the top methods (90.90–90.99%) despite using a single shared decoder for all 40 tasks, whereas baselines require separate decoders per task. The paper also shows that in the 'weak symmetric' regime (noisy updates on symmetric initialization), performance remains nearly identical to exact BP (71.98% vs. 72.06% on CIFAR-10 with ResNet-18), while Feedback Alignment collapses to near-chance (23.11%), demonstrating superior scalability of CH under realistic biological noise.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the lineage of biologically plausible BU-TD learning models (Feedback Alignment, Target Propagation, Predictive Coding, Equilibrium Propagation) by introducing a 'Counter-Hebb' synaptic update rule derived from classical Hebbian learning, where the post-synaptic signal is replaced by its counter-neuron in the opposite stream. The key conceptual departure from all prior biological learning models is the integration of the TD stream's dual cortical role: propagating error signals for learning AND directing visual attention via task-specific sub-network selection, using the GaLU gating mechanism for lateral BU-TD connectivity. The paper also draws an explicit parallel to Vision-Language Models, where a language/instruction stream guides a visual stream, positioning the biological model as a principled analog to this emerging AI architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a recognized gap in biologically plausible learning: the TD stream's role in ongoing visual guidance has been largely neglected in favor of its use purely for error propagation. The exact equivalence to backpropagation under symmetric weights, combined with the demonstration that a single TD network can simultaneously perform learning and task-specific attention guidance without additional parameters, provides a clean architectural principle. However, performance degrades noticeably with asymmetric weights on larger models (CelebA drops from ~90% to ~79%), and the need for signed (positive/negative) neural signals remains an unresolved biological plausibility issue. The connection to VLMs and the sparse sub-network analysis (~80% of the network unused at inference) suggest practical directions for compact multi-task architectures, though the current results are competitive rather than state-of-the-art on guided benchmarks.
