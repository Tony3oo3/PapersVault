## Problem

Existing LiDAR NeRF methods require precomputed poses, yet point cloud registration methods (ICP, GeoTrans, SGHR) suffer from error accumulation and local optima at low sampling frequencies (e.g., 2 Hz). Meanwhile, pose-free NeRF approaches like BARF overlook the explicit geometric structure in point clouds and overfit individual sparse frames, failing to propagate sufficient gradients for effective pose correction. GeoNLF addresses both gaps by coupling geometric registration priors with global neural reconstruction in a unified pose-free framework.

## Input / Output

The method takes a sequence of sparsely sampled LiDAR point clouds (36 frames at 2 Hz from NuScenes 32-beam keyframes, or 24 frames sampled every 5th from KITTI-360's 10 Hz stream) with deliberately perturbed poses (20° rotation, 3 m translation noise) as input. Point clouds are projected into range images for pixel-wise supervision while also being used directly as 3D geometric constraints. The output is twofold: novel LiDAR point cloud views synthesized via differentiable volume rendering (depth, intensity, ray-drop probability), and globally aligned SE(3) poses for all frames in the sequence, evaluated with ATE/RPE and CD/F-score metrics.

## Method

GeoNLF alternates between two optimization stages: (1) bundle-adjusting neural LiDAR fields using a Hybrid Planar-Grid positional encoding (multi-scale planar + hash grids from LiDAR4D) with coarse-to-fine activation, and (2) a graph-based pure geometric optimizer (Geo-optimizer) that constructs a multi-view graph connecting each frame to its n temporally preceding neighbors and minimizes a Graph-based Robust Chamfer Distance (G-RCD) with temperature-scheduled soft weighting to suppress non-overlapping regions. A selective-reweighting strategy reduces the learning rate of the neural fields for the top-k highest-loss (outlier) frames while keeping pose learning rates unchanged, directing gradients toward pose correction rather than radiance-field overfitting. Geometric constraints beyond the 2D range map include a 3D Chamfer distance and a normal-vector loss computed directly between synthesized and ground-truth point clouds. Poses are parameterized in SE(3) with decoupled rotation (via exponential map) and translation updates to avoid coupling-induced optimization artifacts.

## Results

On NuScenes, GeoNLF achieves a Chamfer Distance of 0.2408 and F-score of 0.8647 for point cloud NVS, outperforming the second-best HASH-LN (CD 0.9691, F-score 0.8011) by 4× in CD and 7.9% in F-score. On KITTI-360, GeoNLF reaches CD 0.2363 and F-score 0.9178, a 13.1% F-score improvement over the runner-up. For pose estimation, GeoNLF attains ATE of 0.228 m (NuScenes) and 0.170 m (KITTI-360), with RPE_t of 7.06 cm and 5.45 cm respectively, substantially outperforming all registration and pose-free NeRF baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GeoNLF dominates all seven baselines on both datasets. Against the pose-free NeRF baselines BARF-LN and HASH-LN, GeoNLF reduces CD by 5.3× and 4.0× on NuScenes (0.2408 vs 1.2695 and 0.9691) and by 13× and 11× on KITTI-360, while also improving F-score by 10.6% and 7.9% (NuScenes) and 48.8% and 51.1% (KITTI-360). The GeoTrans-assisted NeRF baseline, which uses pre-registered poses, is the strongest competitor on KITTI-360 (CD 0.5753, F-score 0.8116) but still trails GeoNLF by 2.4× in CD and 13.1% in F-score; on NuScenes GeoTrans fails catastrophically (CD 4.1587, F-score 0.2993) due to inaccurate pairwise registration. For pose estimation, GeoNLF achieves ATE of 0.228 m on NuScenes and 0.170 m on KITTI-360, beating the best registration method GeoTrans (0.892 m / 0.246 m) by 3.9× and 1.4×, and outperforming multi-view methods SGHR (9.557 m / 2.539 m) and MICP (2.519 m / 1.483 m) by orders of magnitude. Learning-based registration HRegNet shows severe generalization failure (ATE 7.815 m on NuScenes, 7.423 m on KITTI-360), confirming the paper's observation that data-driven methods struggle with distribution shift in pose noise settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

GeoNLF extends the bundle-adjusting NeRF paradigm (BARF, HASH) from the image domain to LiDAR point cloud novel view synthesis, building on LiDAR-NeRF's differentiable range-image rendering pipeline and LiDAR4D's Hybrid Planar-Grid encoding for large-scale scenes. The key departure is the recognition that photometric bundle adjustment fails for textureless, sparse LiDAR data, motivating a hybrid framework that alternates between neural reconstruction and a graph-based pure geometric optimizer (inspired by ICP's Chamfer Distance objective). This contrasts with prior pose-free NeRFs that rely solely on rendering loss, and with prior PCR methods (ICP, GeoTrans, SGHR) that operate pairwise or multi-view without global neural consistency.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap in autonomous driving simulation: pose-free LiDAR NVS for large-scale outdoor scenes at low sampling frequency (2Hz), where both registration and reconstruction must be solved jointly. The selective-reweighting strategy and 3D geometric constraints offer reusable techniques for any sparse-view neural reconstruction task. However, the method is limited to static scenes and relies on temporal sequence priors, leaving dynamic outdoor LiDAR NVS and single-frame pose estimation as open problems. Generalization beyond the two tested datasets (NuScenes, KITTI-360) and to higher-frequency or more diverse sensor configurations remains unverified.
