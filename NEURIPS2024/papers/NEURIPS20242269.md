## Problem

Linear complexity models (LinFormer, SSM, LinRNN) have been proposed to replace quadratic softmax attention, but the optimal design of these models remains an open question. No unified theoretical framework exists to determine which components are necessary for a linear model to functionally approximate softmax attention, and existing models each fail to satisfy all the required conditions simultaneously, resulting in suboptimal performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242269/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For language modeling, the model consumes autoregressive text sequences from the Pile dataset and produces next-token predictions with O(1) per-token inference cost via a fixed-size hidden state (d_k' × d_v' matrix per head) rather than a growing KV cache. For image classification, it processes RGB images through a MetaLA Transformer encoder and outputs class labels. The hidden state size is determined by the query dimension d_k = d/2 and value dimension d_v = d, split across H heads, giving each head a d_k'/d_v' state matrix.

## Method

The paper first unifies LinFormer, SSM, and LinRNN into a general linear attention form parameterized by functions f_q, f_k, f_v, f_α, f_g and a hidden state update S_t = diag(α_t) S_{t-1} + k_t^T v_t. It then defines four necessary conditions (C0–C3) for optimal linear approximation to softmax attention and proves via Propositions A2.1–A2.3 that only Query Q and dynamic decay Λ_t are required: fixed decay leads to unbounded parameters, absence of Q prevents channel selection, and K is redundant because (1−α_t) can substitute its role in balancing historical memory against new input. MetaLA instantiates this by replacing k_t with (1−α_t) in the state update, using a full-rank W_α (rather than GLA's low-rank factorization) to produce dynamic decay, and adding two auxiliary enhancements: self-augmentation (a learnable w_aug term added at output to boost a token's attention to itself without altering the hidden state) and a short convolution before the MetaLA layer to strengthen local interactions.

## Results

On SuperGLUE (1.4B params, 300B tokens), MetaLA_b achieves 49.22 average, outperforming the Transformer-based Pythia (44.14) by 5.08 points and all linear baselines. On Commonsense Reasoning at the same scale, MetaLA_b reaches 49.99 average, slightly edging out Pythia (49.85). On ImageNet-1k, MetaLA achieves 75.33% top-1 accuracy (6M params) and 80.14% (23M params), surpassing HGRN (74.40/80.09), Mamba (73.39/79.60), and DeiT (72.20/79.90). On the Long Range Arena benchmark, MetaLA scores 86.67 average, competitive with SOTA linear models like S5 (87.46) and Mega (88.21) while substantially outperforming Mamba (66.59) and Transformer (47.81).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MetaLA consistently outperforms Mamba [16] across language modeling benchmarks (SuperGLUE: 45.81 vs 42.83 at 100B tokens; ImageNet: 75.33% vs 73.39%) and by a large margin on LRA (86.67 vs 66.59), though Mamba slightly edges MetaLA_a on Commonsense Reasoning at 100B tokens (47.37 vs 47.01). Against GLA [15], MetaLA wins on every benchmark: SuperGLUE (45.81 vs 43.95), Commonsense (47.01 vs 46.95), and ImageNet (75.33% vs 72.47%). Compared to HGRN [20], MetaLA achieves higher SuperGLUE scores (45.81 vs 45.60) and ImageNet accuracy (75.33% vs 74.40%), but trails slightly on LRA (86.67 vs 86.91). MetaLA surpasses RetNet [14] on both SuperGLUE (45.81 vs 45.20) and Commonsense (47.01 vs 46.86) at 100B tokens, attributed to its use of dynamic decay versus RetNet's fixed decay. Against the Transformer-based Pythia [44], MetaLA_b at 300B tokens achieves substantially higher SuperGLUE (49.22 vs 44.14) and comparable Commonsense (49.99 vs 49.85) scores. On LRA, MetaLA (86.67) is competitive with S5 (87.46) and Mega (88.21) but falls slightly short, while decisively beating S4 (86.09), DSS (81.88), LRU (86.30), and the vanilla Transformer (47.81). On the MQAR associative recall task, MetaLA outperforms three-parameter-group models (Mamba, GLA, Based [43]) and fixed-decay models (RWKV-4 [17]), validating the theoretical claim that Q + dynamic decay is the optimal minimal parameterization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies three previously distinct families of linear-complexity sequence models—LinFormer (kernel-based linear attention), SSM (state-space models like Mamba), and LinRNN (gated linear recurrences like RWKV/HGRN)—into a single 'linear attention' parameterization with Query, Key, Value, and decay terms. It then departs from prior work by providing a theoretical analysis showing that the optimal linear approximation to softmax attention requires only Query and dynamic decay, making the Key matrix redundant. This contrasts with most existing models (Mamba, GLA, RetNet, RWKV) that either retain a Key matrix, use fixed decay, or lack a Query selection mechanism. The self-augmentation technique is adapted from TransNormer, and the short convolution is motivated by Mamba and Griffin.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled theoretical framework (four necessary conditions C0–C3) for evaluating and designing linear attention models, which should guide future architecture choices beyond ad-hoc engineering. The finding that no existing model satisfies all conditions simultaneously, and that removing the Key matrix while retaining dynamic decay is both theoretically optimal and empirically beneficial, offers a clear design recipe. Open questions remain: whether functional approximation to softmax attention imposes a fundamental capacity ceiling on linear models (the authors note linear models sometimes outperform softmax attention, which is paradoxical), and how to further improve recall ability of limited hidden states. The practical impact is moderate—MetaLA is competitive but not dramatically superior to Mamba or GLA at matched scale.
