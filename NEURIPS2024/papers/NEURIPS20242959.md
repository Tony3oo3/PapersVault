## Problem

Existing object-centric learning methods (e.g., Slot Attention) demonstrate strong empirical object-binding but lack theoretical identifiability guarantees for the learned slot representations. Prior identifiability results (Brady et al.) require restrictive additive decoders and computationally prohibitive second-order optimization to enforce compositional contrast, limiting scalability to high-dimensional images. This paper asks: can slot identifiability be guaranteed by imposing structure on the latent space rather than the mixing function?

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images of size H×W×C, encodes them to a downsampled latent spatial representation z ∈ R^{N×d} (with N < HW), and produces K stochastic object slots, each represented as a full Gaussian distribution (mean μ_k, diagonal variance σ_k², mixing coefficient π_k) rather than a single point vector. The aggregate posterior q(z) over the entire dataset is a non-degenerate global GMM with MK components, which can be tractably sampled from for scene composition. Reconstruction is performed by a decoder (additive, convolutional, or transformer) operating on the sampled slots.

## Method

Probabilistic Slot Attention (PSA) replaces the deterministic slot update in standard Slot Attention with a per-datapoint Gaussian Mixture Model fit via a bespoke EM algorithm: at each attention iteration, agreement between keys and slots is measured by normalized Gaussian probability densities (rather than softmax dot-products), and slot parameters (means, variances, mixing coefficients) are updated in closed form. After T iterations, each slot is a full Gaussian distribution, and the aggregate posterior q(z) is computed by marginalizing over the dataset, yielding a non-degenerate global GMM with MK components. The key identifiability insight is that permutation equivariance of the PSA function causes concatenated slots to form a K!-component GMM in R^{Kd}, which—combined with a piecewise-affine weakly-injective decoder (e.g., LeakyReLU MLPs or transformers)—guarantees slot identifiability up to affine transformation and permutation, extending Kivva et al.'s GMM identifiability result to the object-centric setting. This avoids additive decoders entirely, and ARD-style pruning of inactive slots via mixing coefficients provides automatic estimation of the number of objects per scene.

## Results

On the CLEVR dataset, PSA-ProJ achieves the highest slot identifiability score (SMCC) of 0.66±0.06 and R² of 0.62±0.08, outperforming standard Slot Attention (SA) which scores 0.56±0.02 SMCC and 0.55±0.05 R². On OBJECTS-ROOM, PSA-ProJ reaches 0.71±0.00 SMCC and 0.62±0.02 R², while PSA (with additive decoder) achieves the best R² of 0.64±0.02. On the synthetic 2D identifiability experiment, PSA recovers the latent GMM structure across runs with SMCC of 0.93±0.04, providing direct visual evidence of the theoretical identifiability guarantee. On Pascal VOC2012, PSA MLP (w/ DINO) with slot-attention masks achieves MBOi of 0.405 and MBOc of 0.436, competitive with the DINOSAUR MLP baseline (0.395, 0.409).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PSA-ProJ (the full model with key-value projections) consistently outperforms standard Slot Attention (SA) on both CLEVR and OBJECTS-ROOM: SMCC improves from 0.56 to 0.66 on CLEVR and from 0.66 to 0.71 on OBJECTS-ROOM, with R² also improving (0.55→0.62 on CLEVR). PSA with an additive decoder matches SA's SMCC on OBJECTS-ROOM (0.66) but substantially improves R² (0.64 vs. 0.54), suggesting better slot-to-object alignment. The non-additive variant PSA-NoA matches SA on CLEVR SMCC (0.56) but lags on R² (0.42 vs. 0.55), indicating that the additive decoder inductive bias still helps reconstruction quality even when identifiability is already guaranteed by the latent GMM structure. MONET and the plain autoencoder (AE) are clearly inferior, with SMCC scores 0.1–0.3 lower than SA. On Pascal VOC2012, PSA MLP (w/ DINO) with slot-attention masks achieves MBOc of 0.436, exceeding the DINOSAUR MLP baseline (0.409) and SLATE (0.324), confirming that the probabilistic slot attention module scales to real-world data while remaining competitive with state-of-the-art object-centric methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Slot Attention (Locatello et al., 2020) by replacing its deterministic slot updates with a probabilistic EM procedure that fits a per-datapoint Gaussian Mixture Model over slot representations, and builds on the identifiability theory of Kivva et al. (2022) for deep generative models with GMM priors. It departs from the dominant identifiability strategy in object-centric learning (Brady et al., 2023; Lachapelle et al., 2024), which restricts the mixing function to additive decoders and requires explicitly minimizing a computationally expensive compositional contrast. Instead, this work shifts the identifiability burden to the latent space by imposing a mixture prior structure, enabling identifiability guarantees with general piecewise-affine decoders (e.g., standard MLPs with LeakyReLU) that are more expressive and scalable than additive decoders.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result provides the first identifiability guarantee for object-centric slot representations that does not require additive decoders or second-order optimization, opening the door to using transformer and other non-additive decoders with theoretical correctness guarantees. Key limitations include the weak injectivity assumption on the decoder (which may fail for highly non-injective architectures), the assumption of permutation invariance that technically breaks invertibility, and the lack of treatment for occluded or overlapping objects. Open questions include whether the identifiability guarantees can be extended to handle permutation invariance explicitly, whether the GMM prior can be replaced with richer latent structures, and how the approach behaves under model misspecification in real-world settings.
