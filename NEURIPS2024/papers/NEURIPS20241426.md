## Problem

3D Gaussian Splatting has excelled in RGB view synthesis and surface reconstruction, but its application to volumetric tasks like X-ray CT remains unexplored. The paper identifies a previously unknown integration bias in the standard 3DGS formulation: when splatting a 3D Gaussian onto the 2D image plane, a covariance-related scaling factor is omitted, causing density values queried from different views to be inconsistent and preventing accurate 3D volume retrieval. No prior 3DGS-based method could directly reconstruct a CT volume from sparse projections.

## Input / Output

The method consumes N sparse-view X-ray projections (75, 50, or 25 views) in logarithmic space (i.e., line integrals of attenuation per the Beer-Lambert law) at 512×512 (synthetic) or 560×560 (real-world) resolution, along with cone-beam scanner geometry parameters (source-detector distance, source-object distance, rotation angles, detector physical size). It outputs a discrete 3D density volume of size 256×256×256 representing the isotropic attenuation coefficient field. The method is self-supervised: no external training data or ground-truth volumes are required during optimization, only the measured projections and photometric losses.

## Method

R²-Gaussian represents the scanned object as a set of radiative Gaussian kernels, each a local density field parameterized by central density ρ, position p, and covariance Σ (decomposed into rotation and scale), with no view-dependent color since X-ray attenuation is isotropic. The key theoretical contribution is a rectified X-ray rasterizer: by analytically integrating each 3D Gaussian along the ray direction in ray space, the authors show the 2D projection is a simple sum of 2D Gaussians whose central density must be scaled by μ_i = (2π|Σ̃_i|/|Σ̂_i|)^{1/2}—a factor standard 3DGS omits, causing view-inconsistent density retrieval. A CUDA-based differentiable voxelizer extracts 3D volumes by summing kernel contributions per voxel within 8×8×8 tiles, enabling both final volume output and per-iteration 3D total-variation regularization. Training uses FDK-analytical initialization (sub-second), photometric L1 + D-SSIM losses on rendered projections, TV regularization on randomly sampled sub-volumes, and a modified adaptive densification strategy that halves densities upon cloning/splitting to stabilize optimization.

## Results

On the synthetic dataset (75-view), R²-Gaussian achieves 38.88 dB PSNR and 0.959 SSIM at 30k iterations (8m21s), outperforming the best NeRF-based method SAX-NeRF (38.07 dB, 13h5m) by 0.81 dB while being ~100× faster. On the real-world FIPS dataset (75-view), it reaches 39.40 dB PSNR and 0.875 SSIM in 14m16s, surpassing NAF (38.58 dB, 51m28s) by 0.82 dB. Notably, even at just 10k iterations (~3 minutes), the method already exceeds all baselines in PSNR, and its 50-view results match or exceed the 75-view results of competing methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

R²-Gaussian outperforms all six baselines in PSNR across every sparse-view setting on both synthetic and real-world data. Against the strongest NeRF-based competitor SAX-NeRF, it gains 0.81 dB PSNR on synthetic (38.88 vs. 38.07) and 4.47 dB on real-world (39.40 vs. 34.93) at 75 views, while being roughly 100× faster (8 min vs. 13 h). Compared to the most efficient NeRF method NAF, R²-Gaussian improves PSNR by 0.82 dB on real-world 75-view data and is 3.7× faster (14 min vs. 51 min). Against traditional iterative methods, R²-Gaussian at 10k iterations (~3 min) already surpasses SART (36.06 dB) and ASD-POCS (36.64 dB) on synthetic 75-view by 2.23 and 1.65 dB respectively, while matching their speed. The method also demonstrates strong robustness to view reduction: its 50-view synthetic PSNR (37.98 dB) exceeds the 75-view PSNR of NAF (37.84 dB) and IntraTomo (35.42 dB), indicating superior interpolation from fewer projections.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) from its native RGB rendering domain to X-ray tomographic reconstruction, departing from the alpha-blending composition model with a Beer-Lambert-law-based summation of 2D Gaussians. It builds on the NeRF-based tomography line (NAF, IntraTomo, SAX-NeRF) and traditional iterative/analytical CT algorithms (FDK, SART, ASD-POCS), but replaces their volume-rendering or iterative optimization with differentiable Gaussian rasterization and voxelization. Concurrent works (X-Gaussian, DDGS-CT) use 3DGS only as a projection-augmentation tool feeding traditional reconstruction; this paper is the first to use 3DGS as a direct volumetric representation with a rectified projection that eliminates a previously unknown integration bias.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The discovery of the integration bias—a covariance-related scaling factor that standard 3DGS omits when projecting 3D Gaussians to 2D—challenges a foundational assumption in the 3DGS community and may affect other tasks beyond tomography (MRI, surface reconstruction). Practically, achieving SOTA-quality CT reconstruction in under 4 minutes (vs. 30+ minutes for NeRF methods) makes real-time or near-real-time medical diagnosis and industrial inspection feasible. Open questions include whether the bias generalizes to other 3DGS applications, how to handle calibration errors and anisotropic scattering effects, and whether the method's suboptimal extrapolation in limited-angle CT can be addressed.
