## Problem

Existing multimodal video benchmarks are overwhelmingly human-centric, with animal data comprising only ~1% of evaluation content (e.g., MVBench), creating an agent bias that obscures model performance in real-world ecological and conservation contexts. This gap limits both the practical applicability of these models to animal welfare tasks and our understanding of their robustness to the high intra-class variability inherent in diverse animal species.

## Input / Output

Each evaluation instance pairs an animal video clip (16 uniformly sampled frames resized to 224×224, duration constrained to 3–35 seconds, averaging 14.61 s) with a four-option multiple-choice question. The model outputs a single selected option letter, and accuracy is computed per task across 41,839 QA entries spanning 7 animal categories and 819 species. For robustness testing, a subset of 100 samples per task is additionally evaluated on diffusion-edited videos simulating snow, frost, distance, and direction variations.

## Method

The benchmark is constructed via an automated pipeline that filters data from six source datasets (MammalNet, Animal Kingdom, LoTE-Animal, TGIF-QA, MSRVTT-QA, NExT-QA) according to task-specific rules, then uses ChatGPT to generate question templates and applies frequency-balanced option design (e.g., mixing head and tail actions for action tasks, cross-category and same-category species for object recognition) to produce multiple-choice QA pairs. Thirteen tasks are defined: nine common perception/cognition tasks (object existence/recognition, action recognition/sequence/prediction/localization, object/action counting, reasoning) and four conservation-specific tasks (predator-prey monitoring, social interaction analysis, breeding behavior monitoring, stress/pain detection). For realistic scenario simulation, the authors employ Stable Diffusion inpainting to perform outpainting beyond original frame boundaries when simulating shooting distance and direction changes, selecting a guide frame via minimum-max-spectrum-value criterion and blending outpainted regions with original frames through guided diffusion steps at multiple noise levels to minimize edge artifacts.

## Results

On Animal-Bench (41,839 QA pairs across 13 tasks), VideoChat2 achieves the highest overall accuracy of 53.66%, well above the random baseline of 27.89%. However, even the best model struggles with temporal tasks: Action Localization (AL) peaks at only 25.45% (Valley) and Action Counting (AC) at 29.16% (VideoChat2), both near random chance. Among the four special conservation tasks, Predator-Prey Behavior Monitoring (52.44%) and Social Interaction Analysis (52.42%) show the highest accuracy, while Breeding Behavior Monitoring (47.69%) and Stress/Pain Detection (54.20%) are moderate. In robustness testing, VideoChat2 shows the smallest accuracy drop (3.70%) under simulated real-world perturbations, while Video-LLaMA is most sensitive (8.72% drop), with shooting parameter changes causing larger degradation than weather changes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VideoChat2 (Li et al., 2023) dominates 11 out of 13 tasks, achieving 53.66% overall accuracy and excelling particularly on Object Recognition (86.75%), Reasoning (68.23%), and Action Recognition (66.27%). Video-LLaMA (Zhang et al., 2023) ranks second at 39.54% overall, with notable strength on Object Recognition (60.23%) and Reasoning (35.08%), but is the most sensitive to real-world perturbations (8.72% accuracy drop). VideoChat (Li et al., 2023) and Chat-UniVi (Jin et al., 2023) are competitive mid-tier models at 37.87% and 37.64% respectively, with Chat-UniVi leading on Object Counting (31.61%) and Reasoning (36.46%) among non-VideoChat2 models. Video-LLaVA (Lin et al., 2023), Video-ChatGPT (Maaz et al., 2023), mPLUG-Owl (Ye et al., 2023), and Valley (Luo et al., 2023) trail at 34.54%, 32.08%, 31.83%, and 31.58% respectively. Notably, all models perform near or below random on Action Localization (21–25%) and Action Counting (22–29%), revealing a critical gap in temporal modeling. In robustness testing, VideoChat2 is most stable (3.70% drop) while Video-LLaMA degrades most (8.72%), and all models are more sensitive to shooting parameter changes (distance, direction) than to weather changes (snow, frost).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of two existing lines of work: comprehensive multimodal video benchmarks (MVBench, Video-Bench, Video-MME) and animal-specific video datasets (MammalNet, Animal Kingdom, LoTE-Animal). While prior comprehensive benchmarks are overwhelmingly human-centric (animal data ~1% in MVBench) and animal-specific datasets cover only narrow classification tasks, Animal-Bench unifies 13 tasks spanning both common perception/cognition and conservation-specific behaviors (predator-prey, breeding, stress detection) across 819 species. For robustness evaluation, it extends editing-based benchmarking (LANCE, ImageNet-E, occlusion studies) by applying diffusion-model outpainting to simulate realistic animal filming conditions—weather changes and shooting parameter shifts—rather than the counterfactual or camera-only perturbations used previously.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Animal-Bench addresses a clear agent-bias gap in the MLLM evaluation landscape and provides a practical tool for the growing intersection of AI and wildlife conservation (species counting, stress detection, behavior monitoring). Key open questions remain: whether the near-random performance on temporal tasks (action localization, action counting) reflects a fundamental limitation of current frame-sampling architectures or simply insufficient training data with animals as agents; whether the observed category bias (mammals/birds >> amphibians/reptiles) will persist as training data diversifies; and whether the diffusion-based editing faithfully captures the distributional shifts of real field recordings. The finding that dedicated temporal modeling modules provide limited gains over stronger video encoders challenges a common architectural assumption and may redirect design priorities.
