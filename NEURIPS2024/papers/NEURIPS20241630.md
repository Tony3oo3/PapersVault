## Problem

While Scaffold-GS reduces spatial redundancy among individual 3D Gaussians by grouping them under shared anchor features, significant spatial redundancy persists among the anchors themselves, as evidenced by high cosine similarity between neighboring anchors. Existing 3DGS compression methods encode each Gaussian or anchor independently, ignoring the spatial dependencies that could be exploited for more efficient coding. This paper introduces the first autoregressive context model at the anchor level to eliminate this remaining redundancy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241630/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a trained Scaffold-GS model consisting of N anchor points, each carrying a 50-dimensional context feature, a 3D position, a 3D scaling vector, and k learnable 3D offsets, and compresses them into a bitstream via entropy coding. At inference, the decoded anchor features are fed into the same Scaffold-GS decoding pipeline (an MLP that predicts per-Gaussian color, rotation, scaling, and opacity conditioned on camera position and direction) to render novel views via tile-based rasterization. The compressed file achieves ~15× size reduction over Scaffold-GS and ~100× over vanilla 3DGS while matching or exceeding rendering fidelity.

## Method

ContextGS partitions anchors into K=3 hierarchical levels using a bottom-up voxel quantization strategy where each coarser level selects one representative anchor per coarser voxel, with a target ratio τ=0.2 between adjacent levels determined by binary search. Anchors are encoded autoregressively from coarsest to finest level: already-decoded anchors at level k+1 provide their features and scaling as spatial context to an MLP that predicts the mean, variance, and quantization step for the Gaussian distribution of each anchor's feature at level k. A low-dimensional quantized hyperprior (dimension 50//h_c) is additionally attached to every anchor and modeled with a fully factorized density to provide a global prior, especially critical for the coarsest level which has no decoded neighbors. Decoded anchors from coarser levels are directly forwarded into the finest level (anchor forward), avoiding redundant storage and enabling the final rendering to use all anchors. Training jointly optimizes the Scaffold-GS rendering loss (SSIM + L1), a masking loss on Gaussian offsets, and the total entropy coding loss across all levels and the hyperprior.

## Results

ContextGS achieves an average 15× size reduction over its Scaffold-GS backbone (e.g., 14.00 MB vs. 183.0 MB on BungeeNeRF) and ~100× over vanilla 3DGS, while simultaneously improving PSNR by 0.28 dB on BungeeNeRF (26.90 vs. 26.62) and 0.20 dB on Mip-NeRF360 (27.62 vs. 27.50). The high-rate variant further boosts fidelity, reaching 27.15 dB PSNR on BungeeNeRF and 30.39 dB on DeepBlending at 21.80 MB and 6.60 MB respectively. Across all four datasets, ContextGS (low-rate) outperforms the closest competitor HAC in both PSNR and file size.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ContextGS (low-rate) outperforms the closest SOTA competitor HAC [5] on all four datasets in both PSNR and file size: e.g., on BungeeNeRF it achieves 26.90 dB PSNR at 14.00 MB versus HAC's 26.48 dB at 18.49 MB, and on Mip-NeRF360 it reaches 27.62 dB at 12.68 MB versus 27.53 dB at 15.26 MB. Compared to its Scaffold-GS backbone, ContextGS improves PSNR by 0.11–0.28 dB across datasets while reducing size by 13–15× (e.g., 14.00 MB vs. 183.0 MB on BungeeNeRF). Against vanilla 3DGS, the compression ratio reaches ~100× (e.g., 14.00 MB vs. 1616 MB on BungeeNeRF) with a PSNR gain of 2.03 dB. The high-rate variant further narrows the gap to uncompressed quality, matching or exceeding 3DGS PSNR on BungeeNeRF (27.15 vs. 24.87) and DeepBlending (30.39 vs. 29.42) at only 21.80 MB and 6.60 MB respectively. All other baselines (EAGLES, LightGaussian, Compact3DGS, Compressed3D, Morgenstern et al., Navaneet et al.) are dominated by ContextGS in both fidelity and size across every dataset.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Scaffold-GS (CVPR 2024), which introduced anchor points to capture shared features of nearby 3D Gaussians within voxels, and on 3DGS (SIGGRAPH 2023) as the underlying scene representation. The key departure from prior 3DGS compression work (HAC, Compact3DGS, Compressed3D, LightGaussian) is the introduction of an autoregressive context model at the anchor level, inspired by conditional probability models in learned image compression (e.g., PixelCNN, Ballé et al. 2018). While HAC uses a hash-grid prior and other methods compress Gaussians independently, ContextGS exploits spatial redundancy among anchors by encoding them hierarchically from coarse to fine levels, with a quantized hyperprior (borrowed from learned image compression) handling the coarsest level where no already-decoded anchors exist.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 15× compression over Scaffold-GS and 100× over vanilla 3DGS while maintaining or improving rendering quality makes 3DGS models significantly more practical for storage, transmission, and deployment on resource-constrained devices. The autoregressive anchor-level context model is a natural but previously unexplored direction for 3DGS compression, and the finding that entropy constraints act as a beneficial regularizer (improving PSNR over the uncompressed backbone) suggests broader applicability of compression-aware training. Open questions include scalability to very large multi-scale scenes, whether the fixed 3-level hierarchy is optimal for all scene types, and whether the encoding/decoding latency (tens of seconds) can be reduced for interactive applications.
