## Problem

Gaussian graphical models estimated from real-world data can encode discriminatory biases in their conditional dependence structures, yet no formal definition of fairness exists for graphical models, and the effect of biased observations on GGM estimation is largely unexplored. Existing fairness notions for graph-based machine learning target downstream tasks or unweighted topology, leaving the estimation of fair statistical relationships from biased data unaddressed.

## Input / Output

The method takes n zero-mean Gaussian observations collected in a data matrix X ∈ R^{n×p} (from which the sample covariance Σ̂ is computed) and a binary group indicator matrix Z ∈ {0,1}^{p×g} encoding each node's sensitive-attribute membership. It produces a sparse, positive semi-definite precision matrix Θ* with bounded spectral norm (‖Θ‖₂² ≤ α), whose off-diagonal entries define the weighted, signed edges of the estimated graphical model. The εI regularization term permits rank-deficient solutions, allowing disconnected graph structures.

## Method

Fair GLASSO augments the standard graphical lasso objective with two convex bias regularizers: H(Θ), which penalizes discrepancies between average within-group and across-group edge weights for every group pair, and H_node(Θ), a stronger node-wise variant requiring each individual node to be balanced across all groups. The resulting penalized maximum likelihood problem is solved via an accelerated proximal gradient method (FISTA) with a projection step onto the feasible set of PSD matrices with bounded spectral norm, yielding O(p³) per-iteration complexity and an O(1/k²) convergence rate. Theoretically, the paper establishes a fairness-accuracy tradeoff: the Frobenius error bound includes a term scaling with the fourth root of the true model's bias H(Θ₀), but if the fairness penalty weight μ₂ is sufficiently small (proportional to p² log p / (g²n√H(Θ₀))), the standard graphical lasso error rate is recovered without degradation.

## Results

On four real-world networks, FGL (Fair GLASSO with group-wise bias penalty H) achieves the lowest estimation error on School (0.1417 vs. GL's 0.2661), Co-authorship (0.1896 vs. 0.1995), and Friendship (0.0505 vs. 0.6477), while also reducing bias. With strong bias mitigation (μ₂=10⁶), FGL simultaneously achieves near-GL accuracy and dramatically lower bias (e.g., 0.0308 vs. 0.3111 on School; 0.1899 vs. 12.61 on Co-authorship). On MovieLens, FGL with μ₂=10⁶ is the only method to achieve both low error (0.0248) and low bias (0.6106) simultaneously. On synthetic Karate Club data, FGL is the only approach that simultaneously decreases both bias and error as sample size grows.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against traditional Graphical Lasso (GL, Friedman et al. 2008), FGL and NFGL achieve substantially lower error on School (0.1417 vs. 0.2661), Co-authorship (0.1896 vs. 0.1995), and Friendship (0.0505 vs. 0.6477), while GL retains a slight edge on MovieLens error (0.0223 vs. 0.0253). However, FGL with μ₂=10⁶ matches GL's MovieLens accuracy (0.0248) while reducing bias from 0.9529 to 0.6106. Against FST (Navarro et al. 2024, spectral-template inference with group-wise bias penalty), FGL/NFGL dominate on both error and bias across all four datasets—for example, on Co-authorship FGL achieves error 0.1896 vs. FST's 0.4188 at μ₂=1, and bias 0.1899 vs. FST's 129.54 at μ₂=10⁶. Against NFST (Kose et al. 2023, node-wise bias penalty via spectral templates), FGL/NFGL similarly outperform on every dataset and metric; on School, NFGL (μ₂=1) achieves error 0.1417 vs. NFST's 0.4386. The paper also shows that its squared-summand bias metrics H and H_node improve both fairness and accuracy over the L1-based metrics from [15, 18] (FGL-L1, NFGL-L1), confirming the design choice of squaring summands in the penalty.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the graphical lasso framework (Friedman et al. 2008; Rothman et al. 2008) by incorporating fairness regularizers derived from demographic parity, adapting the celebrated sparse precision matrix estimator to produce unbiased statistical dependencies across sensitive groups. It builds directly on the authors' prior work on fair network topology inference via spectral templates (Navarro et al. 2024) and fairness-aware graph filter design (Kose et al. 2023), but departs from those by operating on the precision matrix entries (conditional dependencies with signs and magnitudes) rather than unweighted graph topology. The optimization leverages FISTA (Beck & Teboulle 2009) for the resulting convex problem, and the theoretical error bounds extend Rothman et al.'s graphical lasso convergence rate by adding a term that quantifies the cost of bias in the true precision matrix.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GGMs underpin high-stakes applications in neuroscience, genomics, and finance, yet biases in graph data—both in connectivity density and in correlation signs within groups—have been largely unaddressed in statistical estimation. The paper's key practical insight is that when the true graph is fair, the fairness penalty acts as an informative structural prior that simultaneously improves accuracy and fairness, a finding validated across synthetic and real-world data. Limitations include the Gaussian assumption, the equal-group-size requirement (AS4), and the restriction to demographic parity (equalized odds cannot be computed for the true precision matrix). Open questions include extending to non-Gaussian distributions, heterogeneous group sizes, and scalability beyond p≈1000 nodes where the O(p³) per-iteration cost becomes prohibitive.
