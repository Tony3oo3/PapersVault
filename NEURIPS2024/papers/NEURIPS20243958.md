## Problem

Model-based RL enables transfer to arbitrary rewards via planning, but autoregressive rollouts suffer from compounding error that limits performance on long-horizon tasks. Successor features avoid this by directly modeling long-term outcomes, but they are inherently tied to a specific policy, making it difficult to extract optimal behaviors for new downstream tasks. The paper asks: can we model the full distribution of long-term outcomes in a dataset (without committing to any single policy) to enable zero-shot policy optimization for arbitrary reward functions?

## Input / Output

The method takes an offline transition dataset {(s, a, s')} collected by a behavior policy and, at test time, a small set of state-reward pairs (10,000 transitions) from which reward weights w are inferred via linear least-squares regression on 128-dimensional random Fourier features. The output is a single optimal action at the current state, produced in two stages: first selecting the highest-valued successor feature ψ* under the constraint p(ψ|s) ≥ ε, then decoding it through the readout policy. No test-time policy optimization or environment interaction is required beyond the initial reward regression.

## Method

DiSPOs jointly learn two diffusion-model components: an outcome model p(ψ|s) that captures the distribution of all discounted feature sums (successor features) reachable from state s under the behavior policy, trained via a distributional Bellman update; and a readout policy π(a|s,ψ) that maps a desired outcome to the action realizing it, trained by maximum likelihood. Both are parameterized as conditional DDIMs with FiLM conditioning to handle the multimodality of outcomes and actions. At inference, policy optimization reduces to guided diffusion sampling: the linear reward weights w_r are added as a guidance term to the score function ∇_ψ log p(ψ|s), and stochastic gradient Langevin dynamics yield the optimal ψ* in a single denoising pass. Under full data coverage in deterministic MDPs, the method provably recovers the optimal policy, and the suboptimality gap scales as O(λ(τ+ε)/(1−γ)) under partial coverage.

## Results

On D4RL Antmaze, DiSPOs achieve average episodic returns of 631±67 (medium-diverse) and 359±59 (large-diverse), substantially outperforming successor feature methods (USFA: 394, FB: 294, RaMP: 266) and model-based RL (MOPO: 236, COMBO: 418). On Franka Kitchen, DiSPOs complete 43–46 tasks versus 0–11 for all baselines, demonstrating near-complete task resolution where others fail. On Hopper, DiSPOs achieve 800±0 on stand and 832±22 on jump, though COMBO (982±157) and MOPO (596±211) outperform on forward and backward respectively. DiSPOs also solve non-goal-conditioned preference tasks (139–142 return) where goal-conditioned RL (GC-IQL) drops to 72–83.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiSPOs decisively outperform all successor feature baselines (USFA, FB, RaMP) across Antmaze and Kitchen, with gaps of 100–250 return on medium/large Antmaze and near-total task completion on Kitchen where USFA and RaMP score 0. Against model-based RL, DiSPOs win on all Antmaze variants (e.g., 631 vs. 418 for COMBO on medium-diverse) and Kitchen, but lose to COMBO on Hopper forward (566 vs. 982) and to MOPO on Hopper backward (367 vs. 596), suggesting model rollouts remain competitive for short-horizon locomotion. DiSPOs tie with MOPO on Hopper stand (both 800) and beat it on jump (832 vs. 753). Against goal-conditioned RL (GC-IQL), DiSPOs win on all medium/large Antmaze environments (631 vs. 403, 359 vs. 226) and solve preference-based path tasks (139–142) where GC-IQL cannot disambiguate between paths (72–83). On trajectory stitching (Roboverse), DiSPOs achieve 40–66% success while RaMP and Decision Transformer both score 0, confirming the dynamic-programming-based stitching advantage.

## Relation to Prior Work

DiSPOs extends the successor feature framework (Barreto et al., 2017) by replacing the single-policy successor feature with a *distribution* over all outcomes covered in the dataset, trained via a distributional Bellman update (Bellemare et al., 2017). This decouples the model from any particular policy, enabling zero-shot policy optimization for arbitrary rewards without the compounding error that plagues autoregressive model-based RL. The practical instantiation uses conditional diffusion models (DDIM) for both the outcome distribution and the readout policy, and the paper shows that the constrained outcome-selection problem (Eq. 3) reduces to guided diffusion sampling (Dhariwal & Nichol, 2021), providing a clean theoretical bridge between planning and generative model inference.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DiSPOs offers a principled alternative to both model-based RL (which suffers compounding error) and standard successor features (which are policy-dependent), providing a new class of world models that support zero-shot transfer without test-time policy optimization. The theoretical guarantees (Theorem 5.3, 5.5) and the trajectory stitching capability are practically important for data-efficient offline RL. Key limitations include the requirement that rewards be linearly expressible in the chosen features, the restriction to deterministic MDPs in theory (though experiments use noisy data), and sensitivity to dataset coverage/skewness. Open questions include extending to stochastic MDPs, learning expressive features that go beyond random Fourier features, and adapting to online reward inference.
