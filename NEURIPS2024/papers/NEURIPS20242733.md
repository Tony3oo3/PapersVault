## Problem

Deep RL policies in continuous control tasks can produce chaotic closed-loop dynamics where arbitrarily small state perturbations (from sensor noise or adversarial attacks) cause exponentially diverging state and reward trajectories, making long-term behaviour unpredictable and worst-case performance unguaranteeable. While prior work identified chaos in the policy optimisation landscape, no study had quantified the stability of fully trained policies under state perturbation using Lyapunov Exponents or proposed a training-time mechanism to reduce this chaos.

## Input / Output

The agent receives a state vector from deterministic DeepMind Control Suite environments (4-dimensional for Pointmass and Cartpole, 18-dimensional for Walker and Cheetah) and outputs a continuous control action. During training, the MLE regularisation additionally consumes L=3 parallel stochastic trajectory predictions from the RSSM over a 15-step imagination horizon, computing the variance of predicted states and hidden representations across these trajectories as a proxy for local state divergence. Evaluation is performed under Gaussian observation noise (σ ∈ [0,1]) added to each state observation to test robustness.

## Method

The paper proposes Maximal Lyapunov Exponent (MLE) regularisation for Dreamer V3, adding a variance-based penalty to the standard REINFORCE + entropy policy loss. At each training step, the RSSM generates L=3 parallel stochastic trajectory predictions over a time horizon T from the same initial state; the sum of Var(S_t) and Var(H_t) across these trajectories at each timestep forms the regularisation term (Equation 5), which is added to the Dreamer V3 loss (Equation 6). The key insight is that variance across stochastic rollouts from an identical initial state estimates the local state divergence rate (i.e., the MLE), so minimising this term incentivises the policy to produce stable, non-chaotic state dynamics rather than solely optimising expected return. The paper further validates the chaos diagnosis using the Benettin et al. Gram-Schmidt orthonormalisation method to compute full Lyapunov Exponent spectra over 1000 steps with 20 initial states, confirming positive MLE and negative SLE (chaotic regime) for all deep RL policies in high-dimensional environments.

## Results

MLE regularisation reduces the Maximal Lyapunov Exponent across most environments while maintaining or improving reward: Walker Stand MLE drops from 0.1688 to 0.0654, Walker Walk from 0.1614 to 0.1405, and Walker Run from 0.1345 to 0.1106, with Pointmass achieving a negative MLE (−0.0275) indicating stable dynamics. Reward is preserved or improved in 5 of 7 environments (e.g., Cartpole Swingup: 781.4→866.4, Walker Run: 646.3→698.4). Under Gaussian observation noise (σ up to 1.0), MLE-regularised Dreamer V3 significantly outperforms unregularised Dreamer V3 in 4 of 6 environments, confirming improved robustness to sensor perturbations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is unregularised Dreamer V3 (DR3). MLE regularisation consistently reduces the MLE in 6 of 7 environments (most dramatically in Walker Stand, where MLE drops by 61%), while maintaining or improving reward in 5 of 7 environments; the exceptions are Cheetah Run (reward drops from 737.7 to 675.2) and Cartpole Swingup (MLE slightly increases from 0.0149 to 0.0235). SAC and TD3 are evaluated as additional baselines for chaos analysis: both produce stable (negative MLE) dynamics in simple low-dimensional environments (Pointmass, Cartpole Balance, Cartpole Swingup) but chaotic dynamics in complex high-dimensional environments (Walker, Cheetah), similar to DR3. Under Gaussian observation noise, MLE-regularised Dreamer V3 significantly outperforms unregularised DR3 in Cartpole Balance, Cartpole Swingup, Walker Walk, and Walker Run, while matching DR3 in Walker Stand and Cheetah Run. SAC and TD3 are not directly compared to MLE DR3 in a unified table, as the paper's contribution is specifically a modification of the Dreamer V3 architecture.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Dreamer V3's Recurrent State Space Model architecture, adding a Maximal Lyapunov Exponent regularisation term to its policy loss. Conceptually, it extends the line of work connecting chaos theory to RL (Rahn et al. on fractal return landscapes, Wang et al. on Hölder continuity of objectives, Parmas et al. on gradient explosion in model-based RL) by shifting the focus from policy-parameter perturbations during training to state perturbations in fully trained policies. The authors explicitly position their use of the full Lyapunov Exponent spectrum (via the Benettin et al. orthonormalisation method) as the first application to measure chaos in trained DNN policies for continuous control, distinguishing their empirical stability analysis from prior theoretical characterisations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that near-perfect-scoring policies in simple environments (e.g., Cartpole Balance under Dreamer V3) can still be fundamentally chaotic challenges the implicit assumption that high average reward implies robust, predictable behaviour — a critical concern for real-world deployment where sensor noise is unavoidable. The MLE regularisation provides a principled, architecture-agnostic mechanism to reduce state divergence, though its practical impact is modest: MLE remains positive in most environments and noisy-environment improvements are limited to four of six tasks. Open questions include whether this approach generalises to stochastic or partially observable environments, whether it can be applied to other model-based RL architectures beyond Dreamer V3, and whether the RSSM's own approximation error undermines the divergence estimate in high-dimensional or long-horizon tasks.
