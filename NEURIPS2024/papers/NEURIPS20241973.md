## Problem

The minimax regret for multi-armed bandits with expert advice (BwE) remained unresolved, with a gap between the best upper bound O(√(KT log₊(N/K))) and the previous lower bound Ω(√(KT log N / log K)). Additionally, contextual linear bandits (CLB) under stochastic-context and adversarial-loss settings lacked a tight regret characterization, with the best known upper bound being O(d√(T log T)). This paper closes both gaps by establishing matching lower bounds.

## Input / Output

For BwE, the player is given K arms and N experts; critically, the player must commit to an expert before observing all expert advice in each round, then pulls the arm recommended by the chosen expert and receives a loss in [0,1]. For CLB, the player observes a context X_t drawn from a finite set of size S, has access to a feature mapping φ: X×[K]→R^d, and selects an arm I_t∈[K] to receive a loss in [-1,1] with expectation ⟨θ_t, φ(X_t, I_t)⟩ where θ_t is adversarial. The output is a minimax regret rate expressed as a function of the problem parameters (K, N, T for BwE; d, K, S, T for CLB).

## Method

For the BwE lower bound, the paper reduces the problem to best-expert identification (BEI) and constructs an instance with m=⌊(K-1)/2⌋ independent two-arm sub-problems, each advised by N′=⌊(N-1)/m⌋ experts, then uses KL divergence and Pinsker's inequality to show any (ε,δ)-PAC BEI algorithm requires Ω(m·log N′/ε²) rounds, yielding Ω(√(KT log₊(N/K))). For the CLB upper bound, the paper develops an FTRL algorithm with Tsallis entropy regularization ψ_α(w)=(1/α)Σ(w(i)−w(i)^α) for α∈(0,1), which generalizes EXP2's Shannon entropy (the α→1 limit), combined with a novel context-dependent learning rate η(x) that adapts to the observed context. The key analytical step bounds the expected squared loss estimator via a Hölder's inequality argument over the top-d entries of the joint context-arm probability distribution, yielding O(√(dT log₊(KS/d))). The matching lower bound is constructed by taking S independent copies of the Dani et al. [2008] linear bandit instance with orthogonal feature spaces across contexts, giving Ω(√(dT log₊(K min{1,S/d}))).

## Results

For multi-armed bandits with expert advice (BwE), the paper establishes a minimax regret lower bound of Ω(√(KT log₊(N/K))), matching Kale's (2014) upper bound and resolving an open question posed by Seldin and Lugosi (2016). For linear bandits, the paper improves the regret upper bound from O(√(dT log K)) to O(√(dT log₊(K/d))), which is tight in the special cases K=d and K=2^d. For contextual linear bandits (CLB), the paper achieves a tight regret bound of Θ(√(dT log₊(K min{1, S/d}))), improving upon the previous O(d√(T log T)) bound by Liu et al. (2024) in the stochastic-context, adversarial-loss setting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

For BwE, the paper's lower bound Ω(√(KT log₊(N/K))) strictly improves upon the previous best lower bound Ω(√(KT log N / log K)) by Seldin and Lugosi (2016), closing the gap with Kale's (2014) upper bound and establishing the exact minimax rate. The EXP4 bound O(√(KT log N)) from Auer et al. (2002) is shown to be suboptimal by a factor of √(log K) when N=K. For linear bandits, the paper's O(√(dT log₊(K/d))) upper bound improves upon Bubeck et al.'s (2012) O(√(dT log K)) by replacing log K with log₊(K/d), and matches the Dani et al. (2008) lower bound Ω(√(d²T)) in the K=2^d regime. For contextual linear bandits, the paper's O(√(dT log₊(K min{1, S/d}))) upper bound improves upon Liu et al.'s (2024) O(d√(T log T)) bound in the stochastic-context setting, and the matching lower bound confirms tightness under the condition K ≤ 2^d ≤ K^S.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two well-studied lines in online learning: multi-armed bandits with expert advice (BwE) and (contextual) linear bandits. For BwE, it builds on the lower-bound construction technique from Chen et al. [2024] on interpolating full-information and bandit problems, adapting their independent-copy structure to close the gap left by Seldin and Lugosi's [2016] conjectured bound. For the algorithmic side, it generalizes the EXP2/FTRL framework of Bubeck et al. [2012] by replacing Shannon entropy with a Tsallis entropy regularizer (parameterized by α ∈ (0,1)), a technique previously applied to standard bandits [Audibert and Bubeck, 2009] and BwE [Kale, 2014], and further introduces context-dependent learning rates for the contextual setting. The key Bregman divergence inequality for the Tsallis regularizer is inherited from Ito et al. [2024].

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper resolves a concrete open question posed by Seldin and Lugosi [2016] by establishing the exact minimax regret Θ(√(KT log_+(N/K))) for BwE, and provides the first matching upper and lower bounds for contextual linear bandits under the stochastic-context/adversarial-loss model. The Tsallis-entropy FTRL with context-dependent learning rates is a reusable analytical tool that may extend to other structured bandit problems (sleeping bandits, graph bandits). Remaining open problems include: extending the BwE lower bound to the 'classical' setting where the player observes all expert advice before acting, determining tight minimax bounds for linear bandits at intermediate K values (between d and 2^d), and relaxing the finite-context and bounded-probability assumptions (Assumption 1) for broader practical applicability.
