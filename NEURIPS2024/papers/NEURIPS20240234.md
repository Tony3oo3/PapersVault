## Problem

Geometric deep learning architectures for equivariance require known group structure and meticulously handcrafted designs, yet many real-world transformations—camera motion in image space, non-rigid shape deformations, homographies—lack identifiable group structure or are non-linear and non-compact. This paper asks whether one can learn a latent space in which such complex, unknown world-space transformations manifest as simple, tractable isometries, without assuming any group structure or prior knowledge of the transformation.

## Input / Output

The method consumes pairs (or triplets) of observations related by an unknown world-space transformation T: homography-warped MNIST digits (40×40), conformally-augmented 3D shapes rasterized to 96×192 spherical grids, or adjacent video frames center-cropped to 144×144. The autoencoder produces spatially-structured latent encodings (functions over a domain N with d channels), and the key intermediate output is the isometric functional map τ_Ω—a k×k orthogonal, block-diagonal matrix in the learned operator eigenbasis—that relates the two encodings. Downstream, τ_Ω or the projected encodings feed into a VN-MLP for classification or a standard MLP for SE(3) pose regression.

## Method

NIso is an architecture-agnostic autoencoder that jointly learns an encoder/decoder, a diagonal mass matrix M, and a PSD operator Ω (parameterized by its eigenfunctions Φ and eigenvalues Λ) in the latent space. For T-related observation pairs, the functional map τ between their encodings is constrained to be an isometry: it must preserve the M-inner product and commute with Ω, which in the eigenbasis reduces to τ_Ω being orthogonal and Λ-commutative (i.e., block-diagonal with block sizes set by eigenvalue multiplicity). τ_Ω is recovered via a differentiable Procrustes projection of a fuzzy eigenvalue-masked cross-correlation, enabling gradient flow to Ω's parameters. Training minimizes a composite loss combining latent equivariance, reconstruction of T-related observations, and a multiplicity loss (norm of the graph Laplacian of the eigenvalue mask) that promotes distinct eigenvalues and thus a diagonal-as-possible, sparse τ_Ω. An optional spectral dropout layer encourages physically meaningful low-to-high frequency ordering of the learned spectrum.

## Results

On homography-perturbed MNIST, NIso with triplet pre-training achieves 97.38% accuracy, approaching the handcrafted LieDecomp baseline (98.30%) and surpassing homConv (95.71%), while the pairwise regime (reflecting real-world settings) still reaches 92.52%. On conformal SHREC '11 shape classification, NIso achieves 90.26% accuracy, outperforming the SoTA Möbius Convolutions handcrafted network (86.5%). On CO3Dv2 camera pose estimation, NIso achieves the lowest mean ATE at all non-zero frame skips, reaching 0.081 at skip 9 versus 0.104 (NFT), 0.110 (DINOv2), 0.110 (BeIT), and 0.109 (ViT/DUSt3R), demonstrating that isometric latent maps encode geometrically informative transformation parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NIso consistently outperforms the Neural Fourier Transform (NFT) [37] across all three tasks: on homNIST (92.52% vs. 41.93% pairwise; 97.38% vs. 67.15% triplet), on SHREC '11 (90.26% vs. 83.24%), and on CO3Dv2 pose estimation at all non-zero frame skips (e.g., 0.071 vs. 0.104 ATE at skip 9). Against the handcrafted homography-equivariant network homConv [6] (95.71%), NIso in the pairwise regime falls slightly short (92.52%) but the triplet variant (97.38%) surpasses it; against LieDecomp [45] (98.30%), the triplet NIso is within 1%. On conformal shape classification, NIso (90.26%) clearly beats the SoTA Möbius Convolutions [7] (86.5%), suggesting the learned isometric latent space handles inexact conformal symmetries better than the exact Möbius group architecture. For pose estimation, NIso outperforms both strong representation learners DINOv2 [47] and BeIT [48] (0.071 vs. 0.110 ATE at skip 9) and the DUSt3R-style ViT transformer baseline [51] (0.071 vs. 0.109), with the gap widening at larger frame skips, indicating the isometric maps capture scale-invariant geometric structure that raw feature tokens do not.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the functional maps framework of Ovsjanikov et al. (2012), which represents transformations between shapes as linear operators on function spaces, and repurposes it as a regularization mechanism for autoencoder latent spaces. The closest prior work is the Neural Fourier Transform (NFT, Koyama et al., ICLR 2023), which learns latent spaces where known group actions manifest as finite-dimensional irreducible representations. NIso departs from NFT by replacing group IRs with isometric functional maps that commute with a learned operator, thereby removing the requirement that world-space transformations form a compact group — a critical distinction for handling non-compact symmetries like homographies and non-rigid deformations. The paper also positions itself against the broader geometric deep learning literature (equivariant CNNs, spherical CNNs, Möbius convolutions) by offering an architecture-agnostic alternative that avoids handcrafting group-specific network layers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key contribution is a principled, architecture-agnostic recipe for self-supervised equivariant representation learning that requires no knowledge of the underlying symmetry group, making it applicable to the many real-world transformations (camera motion, non-rigid deformation, conformal mapping) that lack tractable group structure. The demonstration that SE(3) camera poses can be regressed directly from the isometric maps between latents is a compelling signal that the learned geometry is genuinely informative rather than merely a convenient coordinate change. Limitations include the inability to handle partiality/occlusion between input pairs and the inability to transfer learned operators across domains with different connectivity, both of which are significant for real-world deployment. Open questions include whether the approach scales to high-resolution images and large 3D scenes, and whether the isometric structure can be exploited for accelerated inference via DiffusionNet-style spectral operations.
