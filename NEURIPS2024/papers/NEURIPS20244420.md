## Problem

Most LLM compression techniques focus exclusively on weight optimization (pruning, quantization, distillation) while neglecting the exploration of optimal architectures. Traditional neural architecture search methods are infeasible for LLMs due to their enormous parameter counts and the prohibitive cost of training randomly initialized architectures to convergence. This paper addresses the unexplored problem of finding efficient LLM subnets in a training-free manner that preserves the original model's capabilities while reducing memory and accelerating inference.

## Input / Output

The method consumes the full weight matrices of a pre-trained LLM (LLaMA, OPT, or Vicuna families), 128 calibration samples from WikiText2, and a target global inheriting ratio (e.g., 50%–90%). It produces a structurally compressed subnet defined by binary selection masks S_attn and S_mlp per transformer block, where specific rows of W_Q, W_K, W_V, W_U, W_G and columns of W_O, W_D are retained. The output is a small-dense model with reduced internal dimensions (e.g., head dimension m < M, MLP hidden size p < P) that can be directly deployed for faster inference, with reformed weights that compensate for the omitted parameters.

## Method

The framework operates in three training-free stages. First, an importance-aware initialization computes WoodFisher-style scores per row and column of each weight matrix to select an initial subnet at the target inheriting ratio, with shared masks across layers within the same module (self-attention or MLP) to maintain consistent hidden sizes. Second, an evolutionary search refines the architecture over 50 generations using a mask mutation operator that identifies specific channel indices (not merely channel counts) with a similarity constraint to preserve structural continuity, combined with crossover and depth mutation; candidates are evaluated by perplexity on a small calibration set. Third, an ADMM-based reformation algorithm solves a constrained least-squares problem to adjust inherited column weights using the omitted weights as compensation, converging in ~30 iterations with only 128 calibration samples. The key distinction from prior structured pruning is the global, layer-wise variable inheriting ratio and the mask-level search that explores which specific channels to retain rather than uniformly removing them.

## Results

On LLaMA-7B at 80% inheriting ratio, the proposed method achieves a WikiText2 perplexity of 6.89 and PTB perplexity of 36.06, with an average zero-shot accuracy of 59.71% across seven reasoning datasets. On LLaMA-13B at the same ratio, it reaches 5.90 WikiText2 PPL and 62.10% average accuracy. At more aggressive compression (60% inheriting ratio on LLaMA-7B), the method achieves 10.21 WikiText2 PPL, substantially outperforming LLM-Pruner (38.27) and SliceGPT (279.52), while FLAP reaches 12.34. The method also extends to LLaMA-30B and 65B, consistently outperforming FLAP across all inheriting ratios from 90% down to 50%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against three SOTA structured pruning baselines: LLM-Pruner, SliceGPT, and FLAP. The proposed method wins on all metrics (WikiText2 PPL, PTB PPL, and average zero-shot accuracy) across both LLaMA-7B and LLaMA-13B at 90% and 80% inheriting ratios. The gap is most dramatic against SliceGPT, which suffers severe PTB perplexity degradation (e.g., 143.89 vs. 36.06 for Ours on LLaMA-7B at 80%), and against LLM-Pruner at lower ratios (e.g., 38.27 vs. 10.21 WikiText2 PPL at 60% on LLaMA-7B). Against FLAP, the closest competitor, the method achieves consistent but smaller margins: 0.51 PPL improvement on WikiText2 and 1.13% accuracy gain on LLaMA-7B at 80%, and 0.13 PPL and 1.54% accuracy on LLaMA-13B at 80%. On the OPT family (where only SliceGPT is available as a baseline), the method also outperforms SliceGPT across all model sizes and ratios. For the largest models (LLaMA-30B, 65B), only FLAP is comparable, and the method consistently achieves lower perplexity at every inheriting ratio tested.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of neural architecture search (NAS) and LLM compression, explicitly noting that 'the realm of architecture search for LLMs remains unexplored.' It builds on SparseGPT/WoodFisher for importance-score-based initialization and on ADMM for weight reformation, but departs from both structured pruning (LLM-Pruner, SliceGPT, FLAP) and one-shot NAS for transformers (AutoFormer, NASViT) by treating a pretrained LLM as a frozen supernet and performing evolutionary search over structural subnets without any gradient-based training. The mask mutation mechanism, which identifies specific channel indices rather than just channel counts, is a key methodological departure from prior NAS formulations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free nature makes this approach immediately deployable on existing LLMs without retraining, which is practically significant for organizations that cannot afford to retrain 7B–65B parameter models. The consistent superiority across model families (LLaMA, OPT, Vicuna), sizes (125M–65B), and compression ratios (50%–90%) suggests broad applicability. However, the search cost scales with model size (5 hours for 7B on a single A100), the search space excludes head count and embedding/output layers, and the method is limited to row/column structural subnets rather than arbitrary sparsity patterns. Open questions include whether the approach extends to mixture-of-experts architectures, newer model families, or can be combined with quantization for further compression.
