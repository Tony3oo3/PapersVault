## Problem

Prior adaptive depth networks lack a general principle explaining which layers can be safely skipped and require exhaustive self-distillation across all target sub-networks, leading to long training times and conflicting optimization objectives. No formal analysis existed for why individual residual blocks can or cannot be dropped, making depth adaptation hard to generalize across architectures.

## Input / Output

The method takes standard RGB images (224×224 for ImageNet, variable resolution for COCO) plus a user-specified boolean skip vector of length N_r (one entry per residual stage) that instantly selects a sub-network without retraining. For classification it outputs a 1000-way softmax over ImageNet classes; for detection and segmentation it outputs bounding boxes and pixel-level masks via standard detector heads (Faster-RCNN, Mask-RCNN, RetinaNet) built on the adaptive backbone. The skip vector is the key control knob: e.g., (FFFF) runs the full super-net while (TTTT) runs the minimal base-net, and all 2^N_r intermediate combinations are valid at test time.

## Method

Each residual stage is split into a mandatory sub-path (F_base) that learns hierarchical features and a skippable sub-path (F_skippable) trained to only refine those features while preserving their distribution. Training uses a simplified self-distillation strategy where only the super-net (all sub-paths active) acts as teacher and the base-net (all skippable sub-paths skipped) acts as student, minimizing KL divergence between their intermediate features and final outputs—avoiding the exhaustive per-sub-network training of prior work. Skip-aware batch/layer normalization (two sets per mandatory sub-path, switched at inference) handles the internal covariate shift introduced by different skip configurations. A Taylor-expansion analysis shows that the KL constraint forces skippable residual functions to behave like small-magnitude gradient-descent steps on the loss, formally explaining why skipping them causes minimal accuracy loss. At test time, the 2^N_r sub-networks are selected combinatorially by choosing which skippable sub-paths to bypass, yielding a Pareto frontier that outperforms individually trained networks of the same depth.

## Results

On ImageNet, the adaptive depth networks consistently outperform their individual counterparts at both the super-net and base-net levels: ResNet50-ADN achieves 77.6% top-1 (vs. 76.7% for ResNet50) at full depth and 76.1% (vs. 75.0% for ResNet50-Base) at minimum depth. For vision transformers, ViT-b/16-ADN reaches 81.4% (vs. 81.1%) and 80.6% (vs. 78.7%), while Swin-T-ADN reaches 81.6% (vs. 81.5%) and 78.0% (vs. 77.4%). On MS COCO, ResNet50-ADN as a backbone improves Faster-RCNN Box AP from 36.4 to 37.8 and Mask-RCNN Box AP from 37.2 to 38.3 over individual ResNet50.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The adaptive depth networks outperform all individual network baselines at both full and minimum depth: ResNet50-ADN beats ResNet50 by 0.9% (77.6 vs. 76.7) and ResNet50-Base by 1.1% (76.1 vs. 75.0); ViT-b/16-ADN beats ViT-b/16-Base by a large 1.9% margin (80.6 vs. 78.7); Swin-T-ADN beats Swin-T-Base by 0.6% (78.0 vs. 77.4). Against static pruning methods, ResNet50-ADN(TTTT) matches GReg-1 (76.1% at 2.6G vs. 76.1% at 2.8G) and outperforms DepGraph (76.1% vs. 75.8%) and DR-ResNet50 (76.1% vs. 75.3%) at comparable FLOPs. For vision transformers, Swin-T-ADN(TTTT) at 2.3G FLOPs outperforms Ada-DeiT-S (78.0% vs. 77.3%) but trails X-Pruner (78.0% vs. 80.7%) and SPViT-Swin-Ti (78.0% vs. 80.1%), though those operate at higher FLOPs (3.2G and 3.3G respectively). On COCO, ResNet50-ADN as a backbone improves over individual ResNet50 across all three detectors (Faster-RCNN, Mask-RCNN, RetinaNet) by 1.0–1.4 Box AP points, confirming generalization beyond classification.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the observation (Greff et al., ICLR 2016; Jastrzebski et al., ICLR 2018) that residual blocks perform iterative feature refinement, and formalizes this into an architectural principle: split each residual stage into a mandatory sub-path for hierarchical learning and a skippable sub-path for refinement. It departs from prior adaptive networks (AlphaNet, Slimmable Networks, MSDNet) that exhaustively train all target sub-networks via random-sampling self-distillation, instead training only the super-net and base-net to optimize sub-paths, then connecting them combinatorially at test time. The skip-aware BN design reduces the N sets of switchable normalizations required by width-adaptive networks to just two sets, simplifying training substantially.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical, generalizable recipe for adaptive depth networks applicable to both CNNs and transformers with minimal training overhead, addressing a key barrier (long, complex training) that has limited adoption of adaptive networks. The finding that sub-networks from a single model outperform individually trained counterparts suggests a regularization benefit from shared-parameter self-distillation that goes beyond simple knowledge transfer. A stated limitation is the narrower FLOPs adaptation range compared to width scaling, and the authors identify combining depth adaptation with width and resolution adaptation as future work. Open questions include whether the approach extends to non-hierarchical architectures or tasks where intermediate feature extraction is infeasible.
