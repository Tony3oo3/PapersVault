## Problem

Neural operators are widely used for their discretization invariance, yet it was unknown whether bijective neural operators (diffeomorphisms on Hilbert spaces) can always be continuously approximated by finite-dimensional diffeomorphisms. This paper establishes a fundamental no-go theorem showing that, in general, no continuous discretization scheme exists for arbitrary diffeomorphisms, and then characterizes precisely when continuous discretization is possible.

## Input / Output

The input is a bijective neural operator F: X → X on an infinite-dimensional Hilbert space X, structured as a composition of generalized layers F(x) = x + T₂G(T₁x) with compact linear operators T₁, T₂ and a C¹ nonlinear map G. The output is a family of finite-dimensional diffeomorphisms F_V: V → V indexed by finite-dimensional subspaces V ⊂ X, such that F_V converges to F uniformly on bounded sets as V → X, with the additional requirement that the mapping F ↦ (F_V) is continuous. For bilipschitz operators, the output further includes a decomposition into strongly monotone residual finite-rank layers plus a single reflection, each locally invertible via iteration.

## Method

The paper frames discretization as a functor from a category of Hilbert-space diffeomorphisms to a category of finite-dimensional approximation sequences, then proves a no-go theorem using topological degree theory: a continuous path of infinite-dimensional diffeomorphisms connecting orientation-preserving to orientation-reversing maps (possible in infinite dimensions) forces any finite-dimensional discretization to jump between disconnected components, violating continuity. The workaround is strong monotonicity: the paper proves that the linear discretization F_V = P_V F|_V preserves strong monotonicity and yields a continuous approximation functor, and that bilipschitz neural operator layers can always be decomposed (on bounded sets) into a composition of strongly monotone neural operator layers plus a single reflection operator. Finally, a universality result shows that bilipschitz layers are approximated by residual finite-rank neural operators (identity plus compact finite-rank corrections) with ReCU activations, where each block is strongly monotone and locally invertible, and the inverse is itself a limit of neural operators.

## Results

The paper establishes a no-go theorem (Theorem 2) proving that no continuous approximation functor exists mapping general C¹-diffeomorphisms on Hilbert spaces to finite-dimensional diffeomorphisms, even allowing nonlinear discretization. The positive counterpart (Theorem 3) shows that strongly monotone neural operator layers do admit continuous discretization via the linear projection functor A_lin. Theorem 4 provides a constructive decomposition: any bilipschitz neural operator layer on a ball can be written as a composition of J = O(ε⁻²) strongly monotone layers plus a single reflection, enabling continuous discretization. Section 4 gives a quantitative error bound: discretization via feed-forward ReLU networks requires at most C(d)·log₂((1+r)/ε_V) layers and C(d)·ε_V^d·log₂((1+r)/ε_V) non-zero weights to achieve ε_V-accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical baselines or dataset comparisons. The central 'comparison' is between general diffeomorphisms (which provably cannot be continuously discretized, Theorem 2) and structured subclasses that can. Strongly monotone diffeomorphisms (Theorem 3) and bilipschitz neural operators (Theorems 4–5) are shown to sidestep the topological obstruction by ensuring the Jacobian determinant never changes sign in finite-dimensional approximations. The paper positions its framework relative to prior work on discretization invariance in inverse problems [37, 48, 51] and FEM locking phenomena [4], showing that the no-go result has a concrete PDE analogue (Appendix A.2) where FEM matrices lose invertibility along a continuous path of invertible infinite-dimensional operators. The residual neural operator construction (Theorem 5, Corollary 1) is shown to be a universal approximator for bilipschitz layers, connecting to the Fourier Neural Operator [38, 39] when the basis is Fourier.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the neural operator framework of Kovachki et al. [30] by introducing a generalized layer definition with skip connections and studying the fundamental limits of their discretization. It builds directly on the authors' prior work on bijective neural operators [14], shifting focus from existence of bijective maps to the question of whether such maps admit continuous finite-dimensional approximations. The category-theoretic framing connects to recent work on categorical deep learning [17], while the discretization-invariance perspective draws on the inverse problems literature [37, 48, 51] where analogous obstructions (e.g., Besov priors) were identified. The no-go theorem leverages classical results on the connectedness of the orthogonal group in infinite dimensions [32, 45] to establish a topological obstruction that has no finite-dimensional analogue.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The no-go theorem reveals a fundamental topological obstruction to discretizing general diffeomorphic neural operators, challenging the implicit assumption that discretization invariance is always well-behaved. The positive results—showing that strongly monotone and bilipschitz neural operators can be continuously discretized via decomposition into strongly monotone layers—provide a constructive path forward and a rigorous platform for practical discretization. Open questions include whether the results extend to non-separable Hilbert spaces, whether the no-go obstruction manifests in specific architectures (e.g., FNOs), and how the depth requirements (J = O(ε⁻²) layers) scale in practice. The framework also invites quantitative stability analysis of discretization functors beyond the simple example given.
