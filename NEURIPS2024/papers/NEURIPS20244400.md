## Problem

The Segment Anything Model (SAM) achieves remarkable promptable segmentation but depends on 11 million human-annotated images requiring over 20 minutes of labeling each, preventing it from following the scaling laws that benefit LLMs. Previous unsupervised segmentation methods (CutLER, SOHES) either produce only instance/semantic-level masks without hierarchical structure or capture limited granularity ranges, leaving a significant performance gap of over 10% AR behind supervised SAM. The paper asks whether fine-grained, multi-granular segmentation masks comparable to SA-1B can be generated entirely without human supervision.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20244400/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For pseudo-mask generation, raw RGB images are resized to 1024×1024 and processed by a DINO-pretrained ViT-B/8; the pipeline produces on average 334 pseudo masks per image across 6 hierarchical granularity levels. For whole-image inference, the trained model (Mask2Former with ResNet-50 backbone, 23M params) takes a single RGB image and outputs class-agnostic segmentation masks at all granularity levels simultaneously. For promptable inference, the model (Semantic-SAM with Swin-Tiny backbone, 25M params) takes an RGB image plus a single point prompt and returns 6 masks representing different granularity levels, with the user selecting the appropriate one by confidence score.

## Method

UnSAM's core contribution is a divide-and-conquer pipeline that generates hierarchical pseudo masks without supervision. The divide stage applies CutLER's MaskCut (Normalized Cuts on DINO ViT key-feature cosine similarity) to extract semantic and instance-level masks, filtering by a confidence threshold τ=0.3. The conquer stage then crops each mask region to 256×256, extracts patch features from a DINO ViT-B/8 encoder, and performs iterative agglomerative merging at six decreasing cosine-similarity thresholds (0.6 down to 0.1), where at each step the two most similar adjacent patches from different clusters are merged and their features updated by size-weighted averaging, producing a nested hierarchy H={S₀,...,S₆}. These pseudo masks supervise a Mask2Former (whole-image) or Semantic-SAM (promptable) model, followed by a self-training round that merges high-confidence predictions (score > 0.7) back into the training set. The UnSAM+ variant further merges these unsupervised masks with SA-1B ground truth (keeping unsupervised masks whose max IoU with any GT mask ≤ 0.02), enabling a lightly semi-supervised model that surpasses fully-supervised SAM by 6.7% AR and 3.9% AP on SA-1B.

## Results

Using only 1% of SA-1B (0.1M images) with a ResNet-50 backbone (23M params), UnSAM achieves an average AR of 39.2% across seven datasets, surpassing the previous unsupervised SOTA (SOHES, 30.1%) by 11.0% and approaching the fully-supervised SAM (42.1% with 11M images and ViT-B/8). On part-segmentation benchmarks, the gains are especially large: UnSAM exceeds SOHES by 16.7% on PartImageNet and 12.6% on PACO. The lightly semi-supervised UnSAM+ (combining 1% SA-1B ground truth with unsupervised pseudo masks) further outperforms SAM by 6.7% AR and 3.9% AP on SA-1B, and by 17.7% AR on PartImageNet.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the supervised SAM (ViT-B/8, 11M images), UnSAM (RN-50, 0.1M images) narrows the gap to just ~3% average AR (39.2 vs 42.1), and actually surpasses SAM on PartImageNet (51.6 vs 28.3) and PACO (27.5 vs 18.1) by 24.4% and 11.6% respectively, revealing entities that human annotators missed. Against the previous unsupervised SOTA SOHES (ViT-B/8, 0.2M images), UnSAM wins on every dataset by a wide margin (+11.0% average AR), despite using a 4× smaller backbone and half the training data. CutLER (RN-50, 1.3M images) is outperformed by UnSAM by 17.4% average AR, with the largest gap on PartImageNet (51.6 vs 28.7). FreeSOLO (RN-101, 1.3M images) is far behind at 7.3% average AR, underscoring the advantage of the divide-and-conquer hierarchical pseudo-mask generation over single-level clustering. The lightly semi-supervised UnSAM+ (1% SA-1B GT + unsupervised masks) beats SAM on 6 of 7 datasets, with the most dramatic gains on PartImageNet (+17.7% AR) and PACO (+14.2% AR), confirming that unsupervised pseudo masks complement human annotations rather than merely replicate them.

## Relation to Prior Work

UnSAM builds directly on CutLER's Normalized Cuts-based MaskCut for the top-down 'divide' stage and on SOHES's iterative merging for the bottom-up 'conquer' stage, but unifies them into a single hierarchical pseudo-mask generation pipeline that produces far more masks per image across a broader range of granularities than either method alone. For model training, it repurposes Mask2Former (whole-image) and Semantic-SAM (promptable) as off-the-shelf decoders trained on the generated pseudo-labels, following the self-training recipe established by CutLER. The key departure from prior unsupervised segmentation work is the explicit divide-then-conquer structure that captures part-level entities nested within instance-level masks, and the UnSAM+ variant, which is novel in demonstrating that unsupervised pseudo-masks can be merged with a small fraction of SA-1B ground truth to outperform fully supervised SAM.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses the fundamental scalability bottleneck of human annotation in segmentation: by showing that a divide-and-conquer pseudo-label pipeline plus self-training can rival SAM with only 1% of SA-1B and a 23M-parameter backbone, it opens a path toward applying segmentation to truly massive unlabeled image collections. The UnSAM+ result—that adding unsupervised masks to just 1% of SA-1B exceeds 100% of SA-1B's supervised performance—suggests that annotation bias (systematic omission of small parts like ears, tails, and fine details) is a more limiting factor than annotation quantity. Open questions include whether the approach scales to video or 3D, whether the iterative merging step can be made more robust to repetitive textures (a noted failure mode), and whether the pseudo-label quality gap versus human annotations narrows with larger backbones or more training data.
