## Problem

State-of-the-art generative model inversion attacks (MIAs) constrain the inversion optimization to a fixed distributional prior learned from a public auxiliary dataset. The paper identifies that the inherent distribution gap between this prior and the unknown private training data distribution leads to a low probability of sampling actual private data during inversion, fundamentally limiting attack performance. The key research question is how to mitigate this distribution discrepancy when only query access to the target model is available.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241051/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes query access to a well-trained target classifier, a pre-trained GAN (StyleGAN for 224×224 high-resolution tasks, or GANs trained from scratch on public data for 64×64 low-resolution tasks), a target identity/class, and a public auxiliary dataset with no identity overlap with the private set. It produces reconstructed face images in the GAN's output space that approximate the actual private training samples. The inversion is performed entirely in the GAN's latent space, and the generator is iteratively fine-tuned between inversion rounds, making the prior dynamic rather than fixed.

## Method

PPDG-MI introduces an iterative three-step loop: (1) run a round of generative MI to produce pseudo-private samples, (2) select high-quality pseudo-private samples by ranking them on expected prediction scores under random image transformations, and (3) fine-tune the GAN generator to increase the density of the prior around these high-quality samples. For high-resolution StyleGAN-based attacks, the paper provides three tuning variants: point-wise tuning (locating a high-density neighbor via LPIPS + discriminator logit, then pulling it toward the pseudo-private sample), and two batch-wise variants using MMD or conditional transport (CT) in LPIPS feature space to align the generated batch with the pseudo-private batch. For low-resolution attacks where the adversary trains the GAN from scratch, a simpler vanilla strategy fine-tunes both G and D with the original GAN objective on the union of public data and high-quality pseudo-private samples. The core insight is that enhancing the prior's density around pseudo-private data indirectly raises the density around the actual private data, increasing the probability of recovering it in subsequent inversion rounds.

## Results

In the high-resolution PPA setting (224×224), PPDG-MMD achieves the largest gains, improving top-1 attack accuracy by +7.73% on CelebA/ResNet-18 (80.80→88.53), +7.28% on CelebA/DenseNet-121 (76.74→84.02), and +8.47% on CelebA/ResNeSt-50 (64.52→72.99), while also reducing KNN distance by 0.05–0.06. In the low-resolution setting (64×64), PPDG-vanilla boosts LOM(GMI) top-1 accuracy from 64.54% to 76.89% (+12.35%) on CelebA/VGG16. Against SOTA defenses, PPDG-MMD recovers 58.55% top-1 accuracy under BiDO-HSIC (vs. 52.50% baseline) and PPDG-CT reaches 16.25% under NegLS (vs. 11.35% baseline).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PPDG-MI consistently outperforms all nine baselines. Against PPA (Struppek et al., ICML 2022), the primary high-resolution baseline, PPDG-MMD improves top-1 accuracy by 3.8–8.5% and reduces KNN distance by 0.02–0.06 across all target models and both CelebA/FaceScrub datasets. In the low-resolution white-box setting, PPDG-vanilla beats GMI (Zhang et al., CVPR 2020) by +5.07% Acc@1, KEDMI (Chen et al., ICCV 2021) by +3.36%, and LOM (Nguyen et al., CVPR 2023) by +12.35% (LOM-GMI) on CelebA/VGG16. Against PLG-MI (Yuan et al., AAAI 2023), PPDG-vanilla gains +1.11% Acc@1 on VGG16 and +2.25% on face.evoLVe. In the black-box setting, PPDG-vanilla dramatically improves RLB-MI (Han et al., CVPR 2023) from 38.50% to 61.60% top-1 accuracy (+23.10%), and in the label-only setting, it boosts BREP-MI (Kahla et al., CVPR 2022) from 66.40% to 71.10% (+4.70%). Against SOTA defenses BiDO-HSIC (Peng et al., KDD 2022) and NegLS (Struppek et al., ICLR 2024), PPDG-MI variants recover 4–6% additional top-1 accuracy over the unprotected baseline, demonstrating that the density-enhancement strategy partially overcomes these privacy protections.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the generative model inversion (MI) framework initiated by Zhang et al. (GMI, CVPR 2020) and refined by PPA (Struppek et al., ICML 2022), which constrain inversion optimization to a GAN's latent space learned from a public auxiliary dataset. The key departure is identifying that all SOTA generative MIAs share a fundamental limitation: they use a fixed prior throughout inversion, creating an irreducible distribution gap between the prior and the unknown private data distribution. PPDG-MI introduces an iterative loop where the generator is dynamically fine-tuned using pseudo-private samples recovered during the attack, drawing on distribution discrepancy measures (MMD, conditional transport) from the broader two-sample testing literature to guide the tuning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that the fixed-prior assumption in generative MIAs is suboptimal and providing a practical iterative tuning framework, this work opens a new research direction for dynamic-prior model inversion and raises the bar for privacy defenses that must now account for adaptive attack pipelines. The finding that direct distribution alignment catastrophically destroys the StyleGAN manifold on high-dimensional data (Acc@1 drops from 84% to 18%) is an important negative result that motivates the nuanced point-wise/batch-wise tuning strategies. Limitations include evaluation primarily on face recognition tasks, added computational overhead from fine-tuning rounds, and the question of whether the approach generalizes to non-image modalities or settings where the GAN prior is already well-aligned with the private data.
