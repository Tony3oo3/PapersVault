## Problem

Text-to-Multi-view-Video (T2MVid) generation via diffusion models remains entirely unexplored. Two fundamental barriers exist: no large-scale captioned multi-view video datasets are available (unlike the billion-scale 2D image or million-scale 2D video corpora), and the four-dimensional nature of multi-view video (viewpoint × time × 2D space) makes modeling from scratch intractable. The paper asks whether pre-trained multi-view image and 2D video diffusion model layers can be jointly combined and reused to build a T2MVid model with only a small training set.

## Input / Output

The model takes a text prompt and a sequence of camera poses (24 views with uniformly distributed azimuth angles) as input and produces a set of synchronized multi-view videos of a single dynamic 3D object. During training, 4 mutually orthogonal views are randomly sampled per batch at 256×256 resolution with 16 frames extracted at stride 3, yielding a 6D feature tensor (batch × views × frames × channels × height × width). Inference uses 50 DDIM steps with CFG weight 7.5 on a single A100 GPU.

## Method

Vivid-ZOO factors T2MVid generation into a multi-view spatial module (reusing frozen MVDream V1.5 weights with adapted multi-view self-attention that reshapes features across all views at each frame to enforce geometric consistency) and a multi-view temporal module (reusing frozen AnimateDiff V2.0 2D temporal layers inflated via rearrange to handle per-view temporal coherence). The key architectural contribution is a pair of lightweight MLP alignment layers: 3D-2D alignment layers (zero-initialized, inserted via residual connections before 2D temporal attention) project multi-view spatial features into the latent space of the pre-trained 2D temporal layers, and 2D-3D alignment layers (after 2D temporal attention) project features back to the multi-view spatial feature space. This design bridges the domain gap between synthetic multi-view training data and real-world 2D video data without fine-tuning the frozen layers. Only the alignment layers are trained (100K steps, AdamW at lr 1e-4, batch 16 on 8 A100s), reducing training cost to ~2 days versus 3–5 days for the base models.

## Results

Vivid-ZOO achieves an FVD of 1634.28±45.24, substantially outperforming the MVDream+IP-AnimateDiff baseline (2038.66±44.36) by approximately 20%. On CLIP text-alignment score, Vivid-ZOO scores 32.24±0.78, slightly below the baseline's 32.71±0.67. In the human preference study evaluating overall quality (text alignment, temporal coherence, and multi-view consistency), Vivid-ZOO is preferred 72% of the time versus only 28% for the baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against a single composite baseline, MVDream+IP-AnimateDiff, which combines the pre-trained multi-view image diffusion model MVDream [80] with the 2D video diffusion model AnimateDiff [23] (via IP-Adapter) in a two-stage pipeline. Vivid-ZOO wins decisively on FVD (1634.28 vs. 2038.66), indicating significantly better video quality and temporal coherence, and on overall human preference (72% vs. 28%), reflecting superior multi-view consistency. However, the baseline achieves a marginally higher CLIP score (32.71 vs. 32.24), suggesting slightly better text alignment in isolation. The paper attributes the baseline's weaknesses to noticeable 3D inconsistency among views (e.g., mismatched appearances and motions across viewpoints) and smaller, less vivid motions, while Vivid-ZOO's unified single-stage diffusion model preserves both spatial 3D consistency and temporal coherence simultaneously.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper is the first to formulate Text-to-Multi-view-Video (T2MVid) generation as a diffusion modeling problem, building on two distinct lineages: multi-view image diffusion (MVDream, SPAD) for spatial 3D consistency and 2D video diffusion (AnimateDiff, SVD) for temporal coherence. Rather than training a unified model from scratch on scarce multi-view video data, it factors the problem into viewpoint-space and time components and reuses pre-trained layers from both model families, bridging their domain gap (synthetic multi-view vs. real 2D video) with lightweight 3D-2D and 2D-3D alignment MLPs inspired by ControlNet's residual injection. This departs from the standard fine-tuning or LoRA-based adaptation strategies used in 2D video generation, which the paper shows to be insufficient for the multi-view setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As the first T2MVid diffusion model, this work opens a new research direction at the intersection of 3D-consistent generation and video synthesis, with direct applications to AR/VR, 4D reconstruction, and interactive content creation. The factorization-plus-alignment recipe offers a practical template for combining pre-trained diffusion models from different domains with minimal training cost (~2 days on 8 A100s vs. 8+ days for the base models). Key limitations include reliance on synthetic 4D objects from Objaverse, point-light rendering assumptions, 4 views at 256×256 resolution, and a single two-stage baseline for comparison; scaling to real-world multi-view video data, more views, higher resolutions, and complex multi-object scenes remain open.
