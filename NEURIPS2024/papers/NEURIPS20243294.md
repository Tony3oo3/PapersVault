## Problem

Existing video multi-modal sentiment analysis focuses on the sentiment of speakers within the video, neglecting the induced sentiment of viewers watching it. Inferring viewer sentiment from comments on micro-videos is challenging because comments are indirect responses to video content rather than descriptions, creating a semantic gap that makes text-only analysis unreliable and requiring precise grounding of relevant video segments for each comment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243294/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single text comment (encoded by RoBERTa into token-level features) and pre-extracted temporal visual features of the associated micro video (from I3D, R(2+1)D, or VideoMAEv2) as input. It produces two independent classification outputs: a 3-class opinion label (positive/negative/neutral) and an 8-class emotion label following the Plutchik wheel (joy, disgust, surprise, sadness, trust, fear, anger, anticipation). Notably, a single video is paired with up to 20 comments that may express different sentiments, and the video input is a sequence of frame-level feature vectors rather than raw pixels.

## Method

VC-CSA addresses the video-comment grounding challenge through three modules. First, Multi-scale Temporal Representation stacks 1D CNNs (kernel 3, stride 1) to produce a hierarchy of video features at progressively coarser temporal granularities. Second, Consensus Semantic Learning introduces trainable Consensus Tokens in a transformer encoder block where direct video-text attention is masked, forcing semantic alignment to flow exclusively through the tokens and bridging the gap between comment language and visual content. Third, Golden Feature Grounding performs two-step temporal grounding: multi-head attention scores the video timeline using consensus tokens as queries, then an LSTM memory module filters temporal redundancies to produce sparse grounding weights that extract the 'golden' video features most relevant to the comment. Finally, a multi-view attention fusion lets each comment token attend to the multi-scale golden features, which are concatenated with text features, passed through self-attention and max pooling, and classified via two softmax heads trained with cross-entropy loss.

## Results

On the CSMV dataset, VC-CSA with VideoMAEv2 features achieves the best overall performance: 74.56% Micro F1 and 68.90% Macro F1 for opinion classification, and 63.67% Micro F1 and 56.18% Macro F1 for emotion classification. With I3D features, VC-CSA scores 73.52% Micro F1 (opinion) and 62.99% Micro F1 (emotion), still outperforming all baselines. Text-only models (BERT, RoBERTa) lag significantly, with opinion Micro F1 around 56–57% and emotion Micro F1 around 43–47%, underscoring the critical role of video content. On a small YouTube test set (21 videos, 138 comments), VC-CSA(I3D) achieves 71.73% opinion Micro F1 and 61.59% emotion Micro F1, suggesting cross-platform generalizability.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VC-CSA outperforms all seven baselines across every video encoder and both classification tasks. Against the strongest multimodal baseline MISA (VideoMAEv2), VC-CSA gains +1.56 points in opinion Micro F1 (74.56 vs. 73.00) and +3.98 points in emotion Micro F1 (63.67 vs. 59.69). The gap widens substantially for emotion recognition: VC-CSA(I3D) leads MISA(I3D) by 5.57 points in emotion Micro F1 (62.99 vs. 57.42), indicating that the video-content-aware grounding modules are particularly beneficial for fine-grained emotion inference. Text-only models (BERT, RoBERTa) trail the best multimodal baselines by roughly 16–17 points in opinion Micro F1 and 15–16 points in emotion Micro F1, confirming that video content is indispensable for this task. Among the traditional multimodal methods, MISA consistently ranks highest, while TBJE, SELF-MM, MMIM, and CubeMLP cluster in a narrower 64–66% opinion Micro F1 range, suggesting that generic cross-modal fusion strategies struggle with the comment-to-video grounding challenge unique to MSA-CRVI.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves out a new task formulation by shifting the focus of multi-modal sentiment analysis from the speaker's expressed emotion (as in MOUD, CMU-MOSEI, IEMOCAP, MELD) to the viewer's induced sentiment conveyed through textual comments about a video. It also departs from prior induced-emotion research (DEAP, COGNIMUSE, LIRIS-ACCEDE) that relies on expensive physiological signals (EEG, facial video) by instead using freely available comment text paired with pre-trained video features (I3D, R(2+1)D, VideoMAEv2). Architecturally, the VC-CSA method repurposes standard components—multi-scale 1D CNNs, a masked transformer with learnable consensus tokens, and an LSTM-based temporal weighting—into a pipeline specifically designed to bridge the semantic gap between a comment and the relevant video segment, rather than fusing modalities that share the same semantic content as in prior MSA work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The CSMV dataset (107K comments, 8.2K videos, 68.83 hours) is the largest of its kind and opens a practical avenue for studying public sentiment toward video content in advertising, social media analytics, and public opinion monitoring. However, the current formulation excludes audio features entirely, which is a significant limitation for a video task; the authors acknowledge this and plan to release audio features in future work. The cross-platform generalization evidence is very thin (21 YouTube videos, 138 comments), leaving open whether the grounding mechanisms transfer beyond TikTok's short-form, hashtag-organized content. A key open question is whether the multi-scale temporal grounding and consensus-token design generalize to longer-form video or to settings where comments reference very specific sub-second moments.
