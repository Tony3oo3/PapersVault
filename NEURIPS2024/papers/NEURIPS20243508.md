## Problem

Existing cloth simulators operate on discrete surface representations (meshes, particle systems) with a fixed spatial resolution, leading to resolution-dependent results where slightly different meshing produces inconsistent folds and wrinkles, memory that grows linearly with vertex count, and difficulty integrating the solver into differentiable neural pipelines. This paper asks whether a continuous, coordinate-based neural representation can overcome these fundamental limitations while still obeying the physics of thin-shell cloth deformation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243508/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a thin shell in a reference configuration (either an analytical parametric surface or a mesh, which is first fitted by a separate GELU-activated MLP to obtain continuous parametric access), material parameters (linear isotropic or non-linear anisotropic Clyde woven-fabric model), boundary conditions (Dirichlet point/shape constraints and periodic seam constraints), and external force fields. It produces a Neural Deformation Field (NDF) — a SIREN-activated MLP whose weights encode the equilibrium displacement — that can be queried at arbitrary spatial resolutions to extract the deformed surface as a 3D mesh, with no re-training required for resolution changes.

## Method

The core contribution is a Neural Deformation Field (NDF): a 5-layer, 512-unit MLP with periodic sine (SIREN) activations that maps curvilinear coordinates (ξ¹, ξ²) to a 3D displacement u(ξ), supervised by minimising the total potential energy functional of the non-linear Kirchhoff-Love thin shell theory. Membrane and bending strains are computed from the NDF via automatic differentiation (requiring C²-continuous activations), and the loss integrates the hyperelastic strain energy minus external work over the parametric domain using stratified sampling; for anisotropic materials, thickness integration uses Simpson's 3-point rule. Boundary conditions are embedded as hard constraints rather than soft loss terms: Dirichlet conditions multiply the network output by a Gaussian distance function, while periodic seam conditions remap the network input to (cos ξ, sin ξ) to enforce Fourier-series periodicity. This yields a memory-constant, resolution-independent, and discretisation-consistent quasistatic cloth simulator that can be edited at test time via material conditioning or parameter fine-tuning.

## Results

On the Belytschko obstacle course, NeuralClothSim matches analytical reference solutions with high precision: 0.487 (vs. 0.487 analytical) for the square plate, 0.3018 (vs. 0.3024) for the Scordelis-Lo roof, 1.81e-5 (vs. 1.825e-5) for the rigid-end pinched cylinder, and 4.58e-4 (vs. 4.52e-4) for the free-end pinched cylinder. The method also demonstrates resolution-independent consistency: simulations extracted from the NDF at different mesh discretisations (e.g., 400 vs. 10k vertices) produce identical folds and wrinkles, unlike FEM-based baselines. Memory usage remains constant (network weights) regardless of spatial resolution, whereas FEM methods grow linearly with vertex count.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Guo et al. [23] (deep collocation for Kirchhoff plates), NeuralClothSim dramatically outperforms on the square plate: 0.487 (matching the analytical solution exactly) versus Guo's 2.566, an error of over 5×. Against Bastek et al. [5] (PINNs for shell structures), NeuralClothSim is more accurate on the Scordelis-Lo roof (0.3018 vs. 0.297, both close to the analytical 0.3024) and uniquely solves the pinched cylinder benchmarks that Bastek et al. do not address; qualitatively, Bastek et al. fails to produce wrinkles in sleeve twisting due to linear strain and GELU activations, while NeuralClothSim captures the correct high-frequency fold patterns. Against FEM-based differentiable simulators DiffARCSim [38] and DiffCloth [36], NeuralClothSim wins on simulation consistency: at different initial mesh discretisations, FEM methods produce visibly different folds and wrinkles, whereas the NDF yields identical equilibrium states regardless of resolution. On memory efficiency, NeuralClothSim stores a fixed-size network (~constant memory) while DiffARCSim and DiffCloth memory grows linearly with vertex count, making the neural approach substantially more compact for high-resolution queries.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of neural fields (SIREN-style coordinate-based MLPs) and physics-informed neural networks, applying both to a domain—cloth simulation—where prior neural approaches (Bertiche et al.) were restricted to garment-on-body scenarios and prior PINN shell work (Bastek & Kochmann) handled only linear small-strain regimes. It departs from the dominant discrete FEM-based cloth simulators (DiffARCSim, DiffCloth, P-Cloth) by replacing mesh representations with a continuous neural deformation field supervised by the full non-linear Kirchhoff-Love shell energy, and adapts the hard-constraint boundary encoding of Lu et al. to enforce Dirichlet and periodic conditions directly in the network architecture. The non-linear anisotropic material model of Clyde et al. is integrated as a drop-in energy density, keeping the formulation orthogonal to constitutive modelling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The constant-memory, resolution-independent querying of the NDF addresses a fundamental scalability limitation of mesh-based simulators and enables downstream applications (rendering, inverse problems, simulation editing) that require adaptive sampling without re-meshing. However, the method is strictly quasistatic, lacks collision and friction handling, and requires per-scenario training (10–30 min), limiting immediate practical adoption. Open questions include whether the approach extends to dynamic (inertial) simulation, how it scales to complex multi-body contact scenes, and whether the per-scenario training cost can be amortised across a family of garments or materials.
