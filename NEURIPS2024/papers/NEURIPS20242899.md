## Problem

Neural networks are increasingly deployed in safety-critical computer systems (databases, OSes, networked systems), yet existing NN-verification benchmarks focus on vision or control tasks and overlook the unique verification challenges of system applications—such as massive specification entry counts, monotonicity, probabilistic, and temporal properties, and hierarchical model structures. No benchmark suite exists to systematically expose these gaps and evaluate whether current verifiers can handle NN4Sys workloads.

## Input / Output

Each benchmark case pairs a trained neural network (exported as ONNX, ranging from ~0.6K to 25M parameters) with a set of safety specifications written in VNN-LIB format. Specifications are generated from templates with configurable random seeds and entry counts, and fall into four categories: reachability (input-output bounding), monotonicity (order-preserving outputs), probabilistic (bounded false-positive/negative rates), and temporal (eventual behavior over k steps). The verifier produces a pass/fail verdict per specification entry along with wall-clock runtime; the benchmark suite reports average runtime over 10 generated cases per configuration.

## Method

NN4SysBench comprises six NN4Sys applications (learned index, learned cardinality estimation, learned bloom filter, learned congestion control, learned adaptive bitrate, learned scheduler) each available in a vanilla version (original architectures) and a compatible version (modified to use only operator sets supported by current verifiers). To make unsupported specification types tractable, the paper introduces three approximation mechanisms: a dual-model architecture that duplicates a network side-by-side to express monotonicity as a reachability constraint on the output difference; a chain-model that concatenates k copies of the network to reduce k-step temporal properties to a single-pass reachability check; and a unit-counting scheme that partitions the input space into 10K equal cells to approximate probabilistic guarantees. The learned index benchmark replaces the hierarchical RMI structure with a single network trained via Ouroboros-style techniques on a 150K-key lognormal dataset. The suite is evaluated with two state-of-the-art verifiers (αβ-CROWN and Marabou), revealing that no single verifier dominates across all benchmarks and that verification difficulty scales with model size, specification type, and safety of the property.

## Results

NN4SysBench demonstrates that verification difficulty spans orders of magnitude across its six benchmarks (Figure 4, log-scale runtime). All compatible benchmarks are verifiable by at least one of the two evaluated verifiers (αβ-CROWN or Marabou), confirming the benchmark's practical utility. However, neither verifier can handle all benchmarks: αβ-CROWN fails on chain-models due to unsupported scatter operators, while Marabou fails on benchmarks using Add(const), Matmul(var), and slice operators. Difficulty analysis (Figure 5) shows that monotonicity specifications are harder than reachability specifications, and safe specifications are harder to verify than unsafe ones for the same model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper evaluates two state-of-the-art verifiers on all NN4SysBench benchmarks: αβ-CROWN (bound-propagation + branch-and-bound, GPU-accelerated) and Marabou (SMT-based, CPU-only). The two verifiers exhibit complementary strengths: Marabou solves small networks very efficiently, while αβ-CROWN handles larger networks that Marabou cannot address within the 180-second timeout. However, both have operator-level gaps—αβ-CROWN cannot verify chain-models (LearnedSched_chain, AdaptBitrate_chain) due to missing scatter support, and Marabou cannot verify benchmarks requiring Add(const), Matmul(var), or slice operators. No single verifier dominates across all benchmarks, which the authors argue validates NN4SysBench's coverage of diverse verification difficulties. The comparison is noted as not entirely apple-to-apple since αβ-CROWN uses GPUs while Marabou does not.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the NN-verification benchmarking tradition (ACAS XU, MNIST, CIFAR-10, Carvana UNet) by introducing a benchmark suite specifically for neural networks deployed in computer systems (NN4Sys), a domain where prior benchmarks have been largely absent. It adopts the chain-model technique from Eliyahu et al. (SIGCOMM'21) and Wu et al. (OOPSLA'22) to approximate temporal specifications, and borrows training approaches from Ouroboros (MLSys'23) for the learned index benchmark. The key departure from prior work is the identification of five NN4Sys-specific verification challenges—large specification entry counts, monotonicity, probabilistic, temporal, and hierarchical-model properties—that existing verifiers lack targeted support for, and the construction of a 'compatible' version that maps these challenges onto currently verifiable architectures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

NN4Sys applications (learned indexes, schedulers, congestion controllers) are increasingly proposed for safety-critical infrastructure, yet no prior benchmark suite systematically exposed the verification gaps they create. The benchmark's three-year track record in VNN-COMP validates its utility, and the finding that even the compatible version hits unsupported operators (scatter, variable MatMul, slice) in both αβ-CROWN and Marabou underscores that current verifiers are not yet ready for real NN4Sys. Open questions include designing high-quality compound specifications (e.g., combining monotonicity with error-bounded reachability to reduce 150K entries), extending to more applications (I/O latency, memory allocation), and developing verifier support for the vanilla architectures. The paper explicitly notes its specifications are 'better-to-obey' rather than 'have-to-obey,' leaving the design of provably necessary safety properties for NN4Sys as an open problem.
