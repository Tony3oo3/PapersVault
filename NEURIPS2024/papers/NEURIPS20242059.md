## Problem

Large vision-language models (LVLMs) cannot be frequently retrained due to prohibitive computational costs, leaving them ignorant of up-to-date knowledge (e.g., events occurring after their release date). While commercial LVLMs like GPT-4V offer internet-augmented generation (IAG), the specific retrieval and filtering mechanics remain undisclosed. This paper addresses the gap by proposing the first open-source, plug-and-play framework that equips arbitrary LVLMs with up-to-date knowledge at inference time without any fine-tuning of the LVLM itself.

## Input / Output

The framework takes an RGB image and a natural-language multiple-choice question (four candidate answers plus a 'No Correct Answers' option) as input, and produces a single option letter as output. Internally, the search engine returns websites whose content is segmented every third sentence; the hierarchical filtering model selects N websites and M content segments, which are stitched into a compact context prompt. The framework is model-agnostic: any LVLM can be plugged in as the final answerer, and the filtering model operates independently of the LVLM's architecture or context-length limits.

## Method

SearchLVLMs consists of four stages: (1) a query generator that extracts text queries via LLM prompting and image queries via Bing Visual Search; (2) a search engine that retrieves websites and scrapes their full content into sentence-level segments; (3) a hierarchical filtering model comprising a website filter and a content filter, both trained via instruction tuning with LoRA on LLaVA-1.5 or Qwen-VL backbones, where score regression is converted to a 6-class classification problem (scores 0.0–1.0 in 0.2 steps); and (4) an augmented generation step that prompts the target LVLM with the filtered context. Training labels are generated by a multi-model voting mechanism: five LVLMs answer each VQA sample using a candidate segment, and the fraction answering correctly becomes the pseudo-score. A diversity selection step (CLIP features + k-means clustering) prevents the LVLM from receiving redundant, repetitive context that would cause it to parrot inputs instead of answering. The entire framework requires no fine-tuning of the target LVLM, making it truly plug-and-play across 15 tested models.

## Results

On the UDK-VQA test set (1,000 samples), the full SearchLVLMs framework achieves 92.9% overall accuracy with InternVL-1.5 and 90.2% with LLaVA-1.6, outperforming GPT-4o's self-contained IAG (57.8%) by ~33% and GPT-4V's IAG (64.5%) by ~26%. The framework also improves GPT-4o from 37.2% (Raw) to 91.8% and Gemini 1.5 Pro from 9.1% (Raw) to 83.3%. On generalization datasets (GQA, InfoSeek, A-OKVQA), the framework provides modest but consistent gains (e.g., LLaVA-1.6: 37.86→41.25 on InfoSeek, 75.53→76.22 on A-OKVQA), confirming it does not degrade general VQA ability.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The framework decisively outperforms all compared baselines on UDK-VQA. Against GPT-4o's self-contained IAG (57.8%), LLaVA-1.6 + SearchLVLMs (90.2%) gains ~32 points, and InternVL-1.5 + SearchLVLMs (92.9%) gains ~35 points. Against GPT-4V's IAG (64.5%), the framework's GPT-4V variant (87.2%) improves by 22.7 points. The retrieval-based baselines (Gen. [51] at 31.3%, Cham. [13] at 31.3%, and C→F [47] at 62.3% for LLaVA-1.6) are all substantially outperformed, with C→F being the closest competitor but still trailing by ~28 points. Gemini 1.5 Pro with full long-context input (LC, 76.4%) is also surpassed by the framework's shorter-context approach (83.3%), demonstrating that hierarchical filtering is more effective than simply feeding all retrieved content. On generalization datasets (GQA, InfoSeek, A-OKVQA), the framework provides small but positive gains over Raw baselines (e.g., +3.4 on InfoSeek for LLaVA-1.6), while CFR [50] and Omni-SMoLA [49] are only reported on GQA and A-OKVQA respectively and are not directly comparable on UDK-VQA.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends retrieval-augmented generation from text-only LLMs (Komeili et al., Lazaridou et al.) to the vision-language setting, departing from prior IAG work that fed raw website snippets directly into models. It also diverges from vision-language RAG methods (REVEAL, Re-ViLM, RA-CM3) that retrieve from static, pre-built image-text memories rather than performing live internet search at inference. The hierarchical filtering model trained via instruction tuning and pseudo-labeling is the key methodological departure: prior approaches either used similarity-based segment selection or overwhelmed LVLMs with unfiltered long contexts, whereas here a two-stage website-then-content filter with CLIP-based diversity selection produces compact, targeted prompts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework addresses a fundamental and unsolvable-by-retraining limitation of all LVLMs (knowledge cutoff) in a plug-and-play manner across 15 models without fine-tuning, making it immediately practical. Key limitations include dependence on search-engine quality, pseudo-labeling rather than human annotation, and measurable performance degradation when entity distribution shifts (UDK-VQA-20240905 shows smaller gains). Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to open-ended VQA beyond multiple-choice, to non-English queries, and how the full pipeline's latency and cost scale in production settings.
