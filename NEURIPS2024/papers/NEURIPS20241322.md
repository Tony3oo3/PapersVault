## Problem

Existing learning-based garment animation methods require large-scale garment-wise datasets (hundreds of samples across diverse topologies, materials, and human shapes) that are expensive and time-consuming to collect, and forcing models to match observed garment dynamics directly may hinder generalization to unseen cases. The paper identifies that constitutive relations (stress-strain behaviors) are topology-independent and shared across garments of the same material, yet no prior method learns these relations directly from a single piece of cloth without relying on analytical physics models or differentiable simulators.

## Input / Output

For EUNet training, the input is a sequence of 30-frame trajectories of a square cloth (484 vertices, 1.4k edges) pinned at two top corners and deforming under gravity, with 800 training and 200 test sequences per material (silk, leather, denim, cotton). EUNet consumes edge-wise features—length differences Δl, relative vertex-normal angles Δθ encoded as [cos θ, sin θ], and a 5-dimensional material attribute vector—and outputs per-edge potential and dissipation energy units. For garment animation, the system takes a garment template mesh, a human body mesh sequence, and external forces, and produces animated garment vertex positions via energy optimization, with EUNet serving as the material prior.

## Method

The paper proposes a disentangled scheme: (1) EUNet, a 4-block MLP (dim 128, 2 FC layers per block), learns constitutive laws as edge-wise energy units from cloth trajectories without any analytical physics model or differentiable simulator; (2) the pre-trained EUNet is embedded as an energy term in an incremental-potential (backward-Euler) optimization to animate diverse garments. EUNet decomposes total energy into a potential branch φ_p (conditioned on Δl, Δθ, material attributes) and a dissipation branch φ_d (conditioned on edge velocity, scaled by adjacent face area), both summed over edges to yield garment-level energy. Training uses a supervised loss matching the energy change between adjacent frames (derived from kinetic + gravitational energy conservation) plus a vertex-wise contrastive loss that penalizes configurations where random vertex disturbances fail to increase total energy, reducing ambiguity from degenerate energy minima. At animation time, EUNet's predicted energy is combined with inertia, gravity, collision, and external-force losses in a neural simulator (MGN-S or MGN-H), requiring no garment-level training data.

## Results

On Cloth3D (80-frame sequences), MGN-H+EUNet achieves the best overall Euclidean error of 66.39±39.36 mm, outperforming HOOD (84.85±29.93 mm), LayersNet (92.72±60.93 mm), and MGN (170.34±170.92 mm). MGN-S+EUNet attains the lowest collision rate at 0.12%, compared to 0.46% for HOOD and 14.61–17.72% for supervised baselines. The EUNet-constrained simulators also show substantially lower standard deviations than garment-wise supervised methods, indicating more stable long-term rollouts.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the supervised MGN baseline (Pfaff et al., ICLR 2021), both EUNet variants reduce overall Euclidean error by more than 2× (66.39 vs. 170.34 mm for MGN-H+EUNet) and cut collision rates from 17.72% to 0.44%. Compared to LayersNet (Shao et al., 2023), MGN-H+EUNet improves overall error from 92.72 to 66.39 mm while reducing collisions from 14.61% to 0.44%. The strongest comparison is against HOOD (Grigorev et al., CVPR 2023), which uses a hierarchical GNN with analytical physics priors: MGN-H+EUNet achieves 66.39 mm overall error versus HOOD's 84.85 mm (a ~22% reduction), with comparable collision rates (0.44% vs. 0.46%). Notably, MGN-S+EUNet achieves the lowest collision rate of all methods (0.12%) but shows higher variance on the Dress category (156.16±87.03 mm), suggesting the simpler MGN-S architecture struggles more with loose garments. The paper also includes MGN-S+PHYS (analytical StVK + bending model) as an additional reference, which EUNet outperforms on overall error (66.39 vs. 106.63 mm), demonstrating that learned constitutive relations from a single cloth piece surpass hand-crafted physics models even when the latter are parameter-fitted to the target garments.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in learning-based garment animation, which either trains supervised models on large-scale garment datasets (MGN, LayersNet) or embeds analytical physics models like StVK elasticity and Bridson bending as priors (SNUG, HOOD). Instead, it introduces a disentangled scheme that first learns constitutive relations from a single piece of cloth via a novel Energy Unit Network (EUNet), then applies those learned energy functions as constraints within the energy-optimization framework established by SNUG and HOOD. The simulator backbones (MeshGraphNet and the hierarchical GNN from HOOD) are reused unchanged; the novelty lies entirely in replacing analytical material models with a data-driven, topology-independent energy predictor trained on cloth trajectories.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical implication is dramatic data reduction: animating diverse garments requires only a few hundred cloth trajectories rather than tens of thousands of garment frames, making the pipeline accessible when garment-level data is expensive or unavailable. However, the approach is currently validated on only four materials and one dataset (Cloth3D), and the MGN-S+EUNet variant actually degrades on dresses (156 mm vs. 92 mm for HOOD), suggesting the edge-wise energy decomposition may struggle with highly deformable topologies. Open questions include whether EUNet generalizes to anisotropic or multi-layered fabrics, whether the lack of self-collision in training data limits real-world applicability, and whether the contrastive loss can be replaced by more principled thermodynamic constraints.
