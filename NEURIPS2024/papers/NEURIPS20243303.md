## Problem

Despite parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) methods like visual prompt tuning (VPT) leveraging pre-trained ViT knowledge, long-tailed visual recognition still exhibits poor generalization on tail classes due to sharp local minima. Existing sharpness-aware minimization (SAM) approaches that flatten loss landscapes suffer from two critical limitations: the perturbation direction is dominated by head-class gradients, and the method requires two sequential gradient computations that double training time.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243303/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-100-LT, 224×224 for iNaturalist/Places/ImageNet-LT) and outputs a single class label from a long-tailed distribution. Only the prompt tokens (n_p × D) and the linear classification header are trainable, while the ViT-B/16 backbone (pre-trained on ImageNet-21K) remains frozen. A merge-prompt strategy concatenates the last-layer prompt with the class token before feeding into the classifier, so the output logits incorporate both learned prompt information and the original class representation.

## Method

GNM-PT replaces SAM's gradient-direction perturbation with random Gaussian perturbations drawn from N(0, σ²), minimizing the mean loss over a parameter neighborhood rather than the maximum. This sample-independent perturbation avoids head-class gradient dominance and requires only a single forward-backward pass per step, cutting computational overhead by ~44% compared to SAM. Theoretically, the Gaussian neighborhood loss provides a tighter PAC-Bayesian upper bound on the true distribution loss than SAM's max-based formulation. The full pipeline uses VPT-deep (prompts at every ViT block) with GCL loss, a two-stage training schedule (robust training followed by DRW classifier re-balancing), and the merge-prompt mechanism to fuse prompt information into the final classification token.

## Results

GNM-PT achieves state-of-the-art top-1 classification accuracy of 90.3% on CIFAR100-LT (IR=100), 76.5% on iNaturalist 2018, 50.1% on Places-LT, and 80.4% on ImageNet-LT. On CIFAR100-LT, it outperforms the second-best method LPT by 1.3%, 1.2%, 1.2%, and 0.8% at imbalance ratios of 200, 100, 50, and 10 respectively. Computationally, GNM adds less than 2 seconds per epoch overhead (40.16s vs. 39.78s baseline), whereas SAM more than doubles training time (72.51s), representing a 44.6% reduction in execution time compared to SAM.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GNM-PT consistently outperforms all visual-only MHSA-based baselines: on CIFAR100-LT it beats LPT (89.1% at IR=100) by 1.2% and VPT (81.0%) by 9.3%; on iNat it surpasses LPT (76.1%) and LiVT (76.1%) by 0.4%, approaching VL-LTR (76.83%) which relies on image-text pairs; on Places-LT it exceeds LPT (49.75%) by 0.35% and matches VL-LTR (50.13%); on ImageNet-LT it leads VPT (77.2%) by 3.2% and GML (78.0%) by 2.4%. Against SAM-based methods, GNM-PT achieves comparable or better accuracy with roughly half the computation time (41.87s vs. 74.36s per epoch for GCL+DRW). Unlike CCSAM and ImbSAM, which primarily flatten the loss landscape for head or tail classes respectively, GNM improves all classes uniformly (e.g., +1.82% head, +0.09% med, +1.82% tail on CIFAR100-LT IR=100 with GCL+DRW). The method also generalizes beyond VPT, showing gains when applied to AdapterFormer and ResNet backbones, though improvements are more modest for architectures with fewer learnable parameters.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of parameter-efficient fine-tuning (specifically Visual Prompt Tuning, VPT) and sharpness-aware optimization for long-tailed recognition. It directly modifies the SAM framework of Foret et al. by replacing the max-loss neighborhood objective with a mean-loss Gaussian neighborhood objective, and by substituting the gradient-direction perturbation with a sample-independent random perturbation. The theoretical analysis builds on the PAC-Bayesian generalization bounds used in SAM and ImbSAM, but shows the Gaussian mean formulation yields a tighter upper bound. The paper also draws on the long-tailed recognition literature (GCL, DRW, LPT) for the training pipeline, but its core contribution is the GNM optimizer rather than a new data or model architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GNM-PT offers a practically useful drop-in optimizer that matches or slightly exceeds SAM-based methods while halving the per-step computational cost, which is meaningful for PEFT workflows where training budgets are constrained. The conceptual insight that a sample-independent random perturbation outperforms the theoretically optimal gradient-direction perturbation for long-tailed data is a clean result with broader implications for how sharpness-aware methods should be adapted to imbalanced settings. However, the paper acknowledges that classifier re-balancing (DRW) still sacrifices head-class accuracy for tail-class gains, and the overall improvements over the strongest baseline (LPT) are modest (≤1.3%), leaving open the question of whether a unified optimization strategy can simultaneously improve feature representation and classifier calibration without this trade-off.
