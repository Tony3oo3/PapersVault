## Problem

Behavioral cloning in offline imitation learning degrades when the expert demonstration dataset is collected from an arbitrary state distribution rather than the expert's stationary distribution, a covariate shift that standard BC and distribution-matching objectives cannot handle. Existing distributionally robust approaches like DR-BC constrain the uncertainty set only by a TV-distance radius, ignoring the structural constraint that valid state distributions must satisfy the Bellman flow equation, leading to overly pessimistic or ineffective robustness.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an offline dataset of (s, a, s') triplets where states are drawn from an arbitrary distribution d_D (not necessarily the expert's stationary distribution), along with known MDP transition dynamics T and initial state distribution ρ_0. It outputs a deterministic imitator policy trained via a two-step procedure: first computing a stationary-distribution-correction weight function w* from an initial BC policy, then training the final policy with weighted BC. The weight function is parameterized as a function over state-action pairs, and the policy is represented with linear RBF features (toy domain) or tanh-normal networks (D4RL).

## Method

DrilDICE formulates a minimax objective that maximizes the policy loss over an uncertainty set of state distributions constrained by both the Bellman flow equation and an f-divergence regularization to the data distribution d_D, yielding a tighter upper bound on the true expert-distribution loss than unconstrained DRO. By reparameterizing the inner maximization in DICE style (substituting w = d/d_D and introducing a dual variable ν for the Bellman constraint), the paper derives a closed-form solution w* = max(0, (f')⁻¹(e_{π,ν}/α)) for the weight function, reducing the problem to alternating optimization over ν and π. To make the approach practical with TV-distance-like behavior, the authors introduce a soft-TV f-divergence based on the log-cosh function, whose derivative is invertible (via tanh⁻¹), enabling the closed-form weight solution while preserving the discriminative weighting properties of TV-distance. The resulting algorithm alternates between updating the dual variable ν (via gradient descent on the DICE-style objective) and updating the policy π (via weighted BC with the computed w*), following the same optimization structure as OptiDICE but with the Bellman-flow-constrained worst-case weighting.

## Results

On D4RL continuous control tasks (hopper, walker2d, halfcheetah) with rebalanced expert datasets (Scenario 1), DrilDICE outperforms all baselines in 14 out of 15 problem settings, achieving normalized scores up to 77.6 (walker2d, p(D1)=0.9) versus 30.4 for BC and 46.0 for DR-BC. In the time-dependent collection scenario (Scenario 2), DrilDICE achieves the highest mean score in 11 out of 12 settings, e.g., 84.3 on walker2d (a,b)=(1,5) versus 61.5 for BC. On the Four Rooms toy domain, DrilDICE attains the best normalized score across all 8 covariate shift scenarios (e.g., 95.04 on Room 1 vs. 90.84 for BC) and substantially improves worst-25% performance (e.g., 80.16 vs. 63.36 for BC on Room 1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DrilDICE consistently outperforms all four baselines across the three covariate shift scenarios. Compared to BC (standard behavioral cloning), DrilDICE achieves substantially higher normalized scores in nearly all settings, with the largest gaps on walker2d (e.g., 73.7 vs. 22.9 at p(D1)=0.5 in Scenario 1). Against DR-BC (distributionally robust BC without Bellman flow constraints), DrilDICE wins in all 15 Scenario 1 settings, confirming that the Bellman flow constraint is critical for tightening the uncertainty set. DemoDICE (distribution matching) and OptiDICE-BC (best-case weighting) both fail to outperform plain BC in more than half of the problem settings, demonstrating that distribution matching and best-case weighting are not robust to the state-distribution shifts studied here. AW-BC (adversarial weighting over all distributions) is overly pessimistic and also underperforms BC in most settings. The only setting where DrilDICE does not achieve the top score is halfcheetah at p(D1)=0.1 in Scenario 1, where AW-BC (53.2) narrowly edges out DrilDICE (52.5).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the DICE family of offline policy optimization methods (OptiDICE, DualDICE) by embedding them within a distributionally robust optimization framework constrained by Bellman flow equations. It departs from prior DRO-based imitation learning (DR-BC, adversarial weighted BC) by restricting the uncertainty set to distributions that are valid stationary distributions of some policy, yielding a tighter upper bound on the true BC loss. The introduction of soft-TV distance as a practical f-divergence with a closed-form inverse derivative is a technical contribution that enables the DICE-style alternating optimization to work with a TV-like robustness radius.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important gap: real-world expert demonstrations are often collected from non-stationary state distributions (biased sensors, incomplete trajectories, cost-constrained collection), and standard BC or distribution-matching methods degrade significantly under such shifts. The consistent finding that Bellman flow constraints are essential (DrilDICE outperforms DR-BC across all 15+ settings) provides a clear design principle for future robust offline IL methods. Open questions include extending the approach to handle transition shifts and noisy demonstrations simultaneously, and validating on real-world (non-simulated) data collection scenarios.
