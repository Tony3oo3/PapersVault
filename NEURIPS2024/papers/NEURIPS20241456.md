## Problem

Residue-level protein active site identification remains underexplored because precise per-residue annotations are scarce, limiting the performance of protein language models that are primarily trained on sequence data. While informative textual descriptions of proteins are widely available from databases like Swiss-Prot, they have not been effectively leveraged to complement sequence-based models for this fine-grained task.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241456/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

During training, the model consumes paired protein amino acid sequences (truncated to 512 residues) and 17-attribute textual descriptions (e.g., Function, Organism, Taxonomic Lineage) reconstructed into full sentences via manual prompting. At inference, only the protein sequence is provided; the missing text modality is generated on-the-fly by the Prot2Text agent model. The output is a per-residue binary label (active site or not) produced by an MLP head with a 0.5 sigmoid threshold.

## Method

MMSite is a two-stage 'First Align, Then Fuse' framework built on frozen PLM (ESM-1b) and BLM (PubMedBERT-abs) encoders. A Multi-Attribute Cross-Attention (MACross) module processes the 17 text attributes hierarchically: the Function attribute serves as key/value while the remaining 16 attributes query it via cross-attention, capturing inter-attribute relationships. In the Align stage, a Shared Transformer Encoder maps both modalities, and a cross-modal soft-label alignment loss (KL divergence between intra-modal similarity distributions and inter-modal cosine similarities) pulls the text feature space toward the sequence space without hard negative pairs. In the Fuse stage, a multi-head cross-attention uses the sequence as query and text as key/value, then concatenates the fused features with the original sequence features before a prediction head; only these Stage-3 modules are trainable.

## Results

On the ProTAD dataset (10% clustering threshold), MMSite achieves Fmax 0.8250, AUPRC 0.8909, MCC 0.8319, OS 0.8549, and FPR 0.1689, outperforming all 21 baselines across every metric. The best sequence-only baseline (S-PLM) reaches Fmax 0.7262, and the best sequence+structure baseline (PST t33_so) reaches Fmax 0.6708, both substantially below MMSite. MMSite also surpasses the best sequence+text baseline (ProtST w/o retrain, ESM-2) by a large margin (Fmax 0.8250 vs. 0.5483). On newly discovered proteins (temporal evaluation), MMSite maintains strong performance with Fmax 0.8432 and FPR 0.1420.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MMSite (ESM-1b + PubMedBERT-abs) outperforms all 21 baselines on all five metrics at the 10% clustering threshold. Among sequence-only PLMs, S-PLM is the strongest baseline (Fmax 0.7262), but MMSite still leads by +0.099 Fmax and −0.077 FPR. Structure-based methods (MIF, PST) underperform sequence-only methods; PST t33_so (Fmax 0.6708) is the best structure-inclusive baseline but trails MMSite by +0.154 Fmax. The closest multi-modal competitor, ProtST w/o retrain with ESM-2 (Fmax 0.5483), is surpassed by a wide margin of +0.277 Fmax, suggesting that ProtST's limited textual utilization and reduced data volume hinder its performance. In Appendix D.1, MMSite also dominates classical methods (Random Forest Fmax 0.4339, SVM 0.4270, DISCERN 0.2870) and the newer SaProt PLM (Fmax 0.7181). The advantage is most pronounced at lower clustering thresholds (10–30%), where data is scarcer, indicating that the textual modality provides complementary information that sequence-only models cannot recover from limited training data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of protein language models (ESM, ProtTrans), multi-modal biological learning (ProtST, Prot2Text, BioT5), and cross-modal alignment techniques (BLIP's 'Align before Fuse', soft-label alignment from image-text retrieval). It departs from prior PLM work that targets sequence-level properties by focusing on residue-level active site prediction, and from structure-based methods (MIF, PST, DeepSurf) by requiring only sequence and text at inference. The MACross module for multi-attribute text processing and the 'First Align, Then Fuse' two-stage strategy are the primary architectural contributions, repurposing contrastive alignment ideas from vision-language pretraining to the protein domain.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ProTAD dataset (570K+ protein-text pairs with 17 attributes) is a valuable resource for multi-modal protein research, and the demonstration that textual descriptions can substantially boost residue-level prediction (≈0.105 average metric improvement over sequence-only) validates the multi-modal approach for protein function. However, the near-perfect performance at 90% clustering threshold raises data leakage concerns, and the method's reliance on Swiss-Prot annotations limits applicability to well-characterized proteins. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to proteins without any textual annotations beyond what Prot2Text can generate, and whether the alignment strategy transfers to other residue-level tasks (e.g., binding site prediction, post-translational modification sites).
