## Problem

Existing Iterative Diffusion Optimization (IDO) techniques for stochastic optimal control, such as the adjoint (relative entropy) loss, have highly non-convex functional landscapes that lead to unstable training and higher final error. The paper asks whether an analogous least-squares matching loss—similar to the conditional score matching loss in diffusion models—can be constructed for the stochastic control setting to obtain convex landscapes, lower gradient variance, and more stable optimization.

## Input / Output

The method consumes the state cost f(x,t), terminal cost g(x), invertible diffusion coefficient σ(t), base drift b(x,t), and noise level λ, and produces a feedback control u(x,t) parameterized by a neural network. Unusually, the method jointly optimizes a family of reparameterization matrices M_t (parameterized by a second neural network) that appear inside the matching vector field; these matrices are optimized solely to reduce the variance of the gradient estimator. Trajectories are simulated via Euler-Maruyama and detached from the computational graph, so no backpropagation through the SDE is required.

## Method

SOCM defines a least-squares loss where the control u is regressed onto a random matching vector field w(t, v, X^v, B, M_t) weighted by an importance factor α. The key technical ingredient is the path-wise reparameterization trick (Prop. 1), which expresses the gradient of a conditional expectation with respect to the initial condition of a stochastic process by introducing arbitrary differentiable matrix-valued functions M_t (with M_t(t)=Id) and applying Girsanov's theorem, shifting the dependence on the initial value into the stochastic integrands. A bias-variance decomposition (Prop. 2) shows the bias term equals the cross-entropy loss (so the landscape in u is unchanged) while the variance term depends only on M, motivating joint optimization of M to reduce gradient variance. Training (Algorithm 2) alternates Adam updates on the control parameters θ and matrix parameters ω, with trajectories detached from the graph so that gradients flow only through the explicit dependence of the loss on u and M.

## Results

On the QUADRATIC ORNSTEIN UHLENBECK (EASY and HARD) settings, SOCM achieves the lowest L² control error, improving over all existing IDO methods by approximately 10×. On LINEAR ORNSTEIN UHLENBECK, SOCM's error is roughly 5× smaller than any competing method. On the challenging DOUBLE WELL problem (1024 modes in the terminal cost), SOCM achieves the third-smallest error, slightly behind the variance loss and adjoint method. In the two-mode Gaussian mixture sampling experiment, SOCM slightly outperforms the Adjoint method at dimensions d=2–64, while at d=128 all methods degrade (Adjoint: 0.146±0.001, SOCM: 7.49±0.01, Cross-Entropy: 12.61±0.02).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SOCM is compared against five existing IDO losses: the Adjoint (relative entropy) loss, Cross-entropy loss, Variance loss, Log-variance loss, and Moment loss. On three of four control benchmarks (Quadratic OU Easy, Quadratic OU Hard, Linear OU), SOCM decisively outperforms all baselines by 5–10× in L² control error, with the Adjoint method being the strongest competitor. On Double Well, SOCM ranks third—slightly behind the Variance loss and Adjoint method—but the Adjoint method exhibits training instabilities that persist even at small learning rates, making SOCM more reliable in practice. In the Gaussian mixture sampling experiment, SOCM marginally beats the Adjoint method at d≤64, while Cross-entropy (which shares the same importance weight α as SOCM) performs substantially worse at all dimensions, confirming that SOCM's reparameterization matrices provide meaningful variance reduction beyond what importance weighting alone achieves. The two SOCM ablations (fixed identity matrices, and SOCM-Adjoint using the adjoint method instead of the path-wise reparameterization trick) both underperform full SOCM, validating the necessity of both the learned reparameterization matrices and the path-wise reparameterization trick.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions stochastic optimal control as the continuous-time analog of training continuous normalizing flows, and imports the least-squares 'matching vector field' philosophy from diffusion models and flow matching into the IDO framework introduced by Nusken and Richter [59]. The key departure from prior IDO losses (adjoint/relative-entropy, cross-entropy, variance, moment) is the introduction of a path-wise reparameterization trick that expresses the gradient of a conditional expectation with respect to the process initial value as an expectation over the same process, parameterized by a family of reparameterization matrices M_t that are jointly optimized to reduce variance. The bias-variance decomposition (Prop. 2) cleanly shows that SOCM's bias term recovers the cross-entropy loss, positioning SOCM as a variance-reduction wrapper around an existing objective rather than a fundamentally different landscape.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The path-wise reparameterization trick is a genuinely novel gradient-estimation technique for functionals of random processes that the authors argue is of independent interest and potentially applicable to diffusion model training. However, the practical impact is currently limited: the importance weight α's variance explodes when costs are large or dimension is high (d=128 already fails), and all experiments are on low-dimensional (d≤2) toy problems with known ground-truth controls. The paper is best viewed as a foundational theoretical contribution that opens a path toward bringing the stability benefits of score-matching-style losses to stochastic control, but the high-dimensional barrier remains the central open question.
