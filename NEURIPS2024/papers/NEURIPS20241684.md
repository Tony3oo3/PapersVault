## Problem

Worldwide image geolocalization suffers from two fundamental limitations: conventional visual representations cannot capture subtle location-aware semantics (distant places share similar visual features), and the heterogeneous geographical distribution of training data causes inconsistent retrieval quality across query types. Existing RAG-based methods compound this by using a fixed number of retrieved references, making generation brittle for images at data-sparse or visually ambiguous locations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241684/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB query image as input and produces a single GPS coordinate pair (latitude, longitude) as output. Internally, the query image is encoded by a frozen ViT-L/14 vision encoder and passed to GPT4V at 'low' resolution for candidate generation. The final coordinate is selected from a candidate pool of size K×N+S (where K=4 RAG prompts each generate N candidates, plus S retrieved image coordinates), with the selection performed in a learned multi-modality embedding space rather than by the LMM itself.

## Method

G3 is a three-stage RAG framework. First, Geo-alignment trains two lightweight feed-forward heads on a frozen ViT-L/14 encoder via symmetric contrastive loss to align image embeddings with both GPS coordinates (projected via Mercator projection and encoded with multi-scale random Fourier features) and textual geographical descriptions (obtained via Nominatim reverse geocoding at city/county/state/country/continent levels), producing location-aware image representations for retrieval. Second, Geo-diversification constructs K=4 RAG prompts with varying numbers of reference coordinates (0, 5, 10, 15 positive/negative pairs) to generate diverse GPS candidates via GPT4V, adapting to heterogeneous query types that benefit from different reference counts. Third, Geo-verification scores all candidates (retrieved and generated) by computing cosine similarity between the query's image-to-GPS embedding and each candidate's GPS embedding, selecting the highest-scoring coordinate as the final prediction.

## Results

On IM2GPS3K, G3 achieves 16.65% (1km), 40.94% (25km), 55.56% (200km), 71.24% (750km), and 84.68% (2500km) threshold accuracy, outperforming the second-best method (Img2Loc) by 8.5%, 2.8%, and 3.3% at the 1km, 25km, and 200km thresholds respectively. On YFCC4K, G3 achieves 23.99% (1km), 35.89% (25km), 46.98% (200km), 64.26% (750km), and 78.15% (2500km), with improvements of 21.3%, 16.9%, 13.5%, 3.3%, and 0.6% over the second-best method across all thresholds. G3 achieves the best or near-best performance across all metrics on both datasets, with the only exception being PIGEON's slightly higher 750km (72.4 vs. 71.24) and 2500km (85.3 vs. 84.68) scores on IM2GPS3K.

## Baselines & Comparisons

G3 outperforms all nine baselines on nearly every metric across both datasets. Against the closest competitor Img2Loc (the prior RAG-based SOTA), G3 gains +1.31 points at 1km and +5.21 points at 1km on IM2GPS3K and YFCC4K respectively, with consistent 2–5 point margins at intermediate thresholds. GeoCLIP, the strongest retrieval-based baseline, is surpassed by G3 by 2.54 points at 1km on IM2GPS3K and a substantial 14.4 points at 1km on YFCC4K, where GeoCLIP's fixed GPS gallery becomes a bottleneck. PIGEON, the strongest classification-based method, is beaten by G3 on all YFCC4K metrics (e.g., +13.59 at 1km) and on IM2GPS3K at 1km (+5.35) and 25km (+4.24), though PIGEON edges out G3 slightly at 750km (72.4 vs. 71.24) and 2500km (85.3 vs. 84.68) on IM2GPS3K. The classification-based baselines (PlaNet, CPlaNet, ISNs, Translocator, GeoDecoder) and the kNN baseline are all substantially outperformed, with G3 achieving 3–9 points higher than the best classification method (GeoDecoder) at the 1km threshold on IM2GPS3K.

## Relation to Prior Work

G3 extends two recent lines of work in worldwide geolocalization: GeoCLIP's CLIP-inspired image-to-GPS alignment (which used Equal Earth Projection and random Fourier features for coordinate encoding) and Img2Loc's retrieval-augmented generation pipeline that feeds retrieved coordinates into LMM prompts. G3 departs from GeoCLIP by jointly aligning images with both GPS coordinates and textual geographical descriptions (city, country, etc.) using Mercator projection, and from Img2Loc by replacing its single fixed-reference RAG prompt with a prompt-ensembling strategy (Geo-diversification) and a multi-modality verification step (Geo-verification) that selects among retrieved and generated candidates. The framework also introduces the MP16-Pro dataset, which augments the original MP16 geotagged images with multi-level Nominatim textual descriptions to support the three-way alignment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Worldwide geolocalization at coordinate-level precision has direct applications in crime tracking, navigation, and content moderation, yet prior methods struggled with visually similar but geographically distant scenes and with the long-tail distribution of image data across the globe. G3's modular design (alignment → diversification → verification) offers a practical recipe that practitioners can adapt, and the released MP16-Pro dataset (4.72M images with textual geo-descriptions) lowers the barrier for future multi-modality geolocalization research. Key limitations remain: the method's efficiency is constrained by the large candidate pool from Geo-diversification and the concatenated three-vector storage, and performance depends on access to a proprietary LMM (GPT4V). Open questions include whether fine-tuning open LMMs can reduce the number of required candidates, and whether quantization (e.g., Product Quantization) can alleviate the storage and retrieval latency overhead.
