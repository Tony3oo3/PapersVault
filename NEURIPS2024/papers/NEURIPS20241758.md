## Problem

When adapting CLIP to video recognition by adding temporal parameters (e.g., N-layer Transformers), a clear trade-off emerges: more specialized parameters improve close-set accuracy but catastrophically degrade zero-shot generalization on unseen categories. No existing method achieves strong performance on both settings simultaneously with a single unified model, leaving the generalization/specialization tension of additional parameters largely unaddressed.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241758/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a video clip of T sparsely sampled frames (8 or 16), each resized to 224×224, along with a text label rendered through 28 prompt templates. Each frame is independently encoded by the frozen or fine-tuned CLIP visual encoder into a D-dimensional embedding; the temporal module then operates on the sequence of frame tokens. The output is a single video embedding z = e + ρ·t (spatial + modulated temporal), compared via cosine similarity against the text embedding for classification. The temporal module is architecturally separated from the CLIP encoder, which enables the test-time temporal feature modulation that scales the temporal contribution by a semantic-association coefficient ρ.

## Method

MoTE replaces the FFN in each temporal Transformer layer with N (default 4) experts initialized differently, so each expert follows a distinct optimization trajectory and captures a different data-bias view. During training, a multinomial routing policy (P(α_i=1) ∝ exp(i)) assigns each batch to one expert, giving higher-indexed experts more data and thus varying degrees of specialization; at inference, all experts are collapsed into a single FFN via weight averaging, preserving the original parameter count. Weight Merging Regularization (WMR) constructs a flat region in weight space by sampling a temperature τ from a discrete set {±2ⁿ·β}∪{∞}, merging experts with soft weights, and optimizing the merged model with the task-specific cross-entropy loss—this explicitly reconciles specialization (task loss) with generalization (flatness) in one unified model. A plug-and-play Temporal Feature Modulation module at test time scales the temporal feature by ρ = exp(−(1−M(y_t y_fᵀ))/γ), where M is sequential max-then-average pooling over the similarity between K nearest text features of fine-tuning and test categories, reducing overfitting on unseen categories.

## Results

MoTE achieves a Trade-off score of 80.1 (ViT-L/14, 4×3 views) and 80.5 (ViT-L/14, 16 frames), defined as the harmonic mean of K400 Top-1 and the harmonic mean of zero-shot accuracies on UCF-101, HMDB-51, and K600. On zero-shot evaluation, MoTE (ViT-L/14) sets new state-of-the-art on UCF-101 (88.7%) and HMDB-51 (61.4%) with only 8 frames, while achieving 78.4% on K600. For close-set K400, MoTE reaches 86.8% Top-1 (ViT-L/14) and 83.0% (ViT-B/16), competitive with the strongest baselines. In few-shot settings, MoTE consistently outperforms MAXI on HMDB-51 (e.g., 61.3% vs. 58.0% at K=2) and UCF-101 (88.8% vs. 86.8% at K=2).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MoTE (ViT-L/14) achieves the best Trade-off score of 80.1, surpassing Open-VCLIP (78.5), DiST (76.4), and Text4Vis (75.3) by 1.6, 3.7, and 4.8 points respectively, while maintaining competitive close-set accuracy (86.8% vs. 86.9% for DiST). On zero-shot UCF-101 and HMDB-51, MoTE (ViT-L/14) sets new SOTA at 88.7% and 61.4%, outperforming OTI (88.1%, 59.3%) and Open-VCLIP (87.6%, 59.0%). However, on K600 zero-shot, MoTE (78.4%) underperforms Open-VCLIP (81.1%) and MAXI (71.5% with ViT-B/16), which the authors attribute to the complexity of K600's 220 unseen categories. In few-shot evaluation, MoTE consistently beats MAXI on HMDB-51 (e.g., +3.3 at K=2) and UCF-101 (e.g., +2.0 at K=2), despite MAXI using K400 fine-tuning weights as initialization. Against ViFi-CLIP, which uses different hyperparameters for close-set and zero-shot and more input frames, MoTE still achieves a better balanced Trade-off (74.7 vs. 72.9 with ViT-B/16).

## Relation to Prior Work

MoTE extends the CLIP-to-video transfer paradigm (Text4Vis, X-CLIP, ViFi-CLIP) by replacing the standard FFN in each temporal Transformer layer with a mixture of temporal experts, repurposing the Mixture-of-Experts architecture—originally designed for scaling pre-trained models—into the transfer learning setting. The stochastic routing mechanism draws from Zuo et al. (ICLR 2021), but the multinomial distribution with index-dependent activation probabilities is a novel twist that deliberately assigns different data volumes to each expert. The Weight Merging Regularization introduces a new conceptual bridge between loss-landscape flatness (Keskar et al., Li et al.) and the expert-merging process, while Temporal Feature Modulation provides a lightweight test-time adaptation strategy absent from prior VLM-to-video methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a fundamental and under-explored tension in VLM adaptation: additional specialized parameters improve close-set performance but degrade zero-shot generalization, and no prior method achieves both simultaneously in a single unified model. The repurposing of MoE for transfer learning (rather than pre-training) and the flatness-based merging regularizer offer a reusable conceptual toolkit that could extend to other parameter forms (adapters, prompts) and downstream tasks beyond video recognition. Key limitations include the text space being restricted to category names (limiting semantic richness) and evaluation confined to video action recognition; the authors note that extending to generative text models and other parameter forms remains open.
