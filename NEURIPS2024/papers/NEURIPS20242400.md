## Problem

Large-vocabulary object detectors (LVDs) such as Detic learn strong objectness features that localize objects accurately across domains, but they fail to classify those objects when applied to downstream data with different distributions and vocabularies. While CLIP possesses rich open-vocabulary classification knowledge, no prior work has explicitly extracted and transferred CLIP's implicit knowledge graph structure to improve LVD adaptation, leaving a gap between CLIP's semantic relational knowledge and the pseudo-label quality needed for unsupervised domain adaptation.

## Input / Output

The method consumes unlabeled downstream domain images (shorter side resized to 640 px), a pretrained Detic detector (CenterNet2 + Swin-B trained on LVIS/ImageNet-21K), frozen CLIP image and text encoders, and the WordNet lexical database. It produces calibrated detection pseudo labels—bounding boxes with class probability vectors refined by both language and vision knowledge graphs—which are then used to train a student detector via standard detection losses. The knowledge graph nodes are not raw category names but domain-generalizable prompts constructed from WordNet synset definitions and their hyponym sets, and edges are computed as CLIP feature distances rather than hand-crafted semantic relations.

## Method

KGD operates in two consecutive stages. In KG Extraction, a Language Knowledge Graph (LKG) is built by encoding WordNet-derived prompts (synset definitions + hyponyms) with CLIP's text encoder as nodes and their feature distances as edges, while a Vision Knowledge Graph (VKG) is initialized from CLIP text embeddings and dynamically updated each iteration using visual centroids from current pseudo labels with manifold smoothing. In KG Encapsulation, a two-layer GCN over the LKG reasons about object classification conditioned on neighboring text nodes, and the VKG calibrates predictions via cosine-similarity-weighted softmax over its nodes; both calibrated probabilities are multiplied with the teacher detector's original predictions to produce refined pseudo labels. The overall training follows a Mean Teacher framework where the student is trained on these graph-calibrated pseudo labels and the teacher is updated via EMA, with the GCN weights trained by a cross-entropy loss over LKG nodes.

## Results

KGD achieves 53.6 AP50 on Cityscapes and 40.3 AP50 on Vistas, improving over the Detic baseline (46.5 / 35.0) by +7.1 and +5.3 respectively. Averaged across all 11 downstream detection datasets, KGD reaches 44.44 AP50, outperforming the best source-free domain adaptation baseline (IRG-SFDA† at 42.12) by 2.32 points. On the artistic illustration datasets (Clipart1k, Watercolor2k, Comic2k), KGD gains of +4.1 to +4.4 AP50 over the strongest SOTA, and on intelligent surveillance (VisDrone) it improves by +2.8 AP50 over the best competitor.

## Baselines & Comparisons

KGD outperforms all 18 compared baselines across every dataset. Against the Detic baseline, KGD gains +7.1 AP50 on Cityscapes and +5.3 on Vistas. Among source-free domain adaptation methods, the strongest competitor is IRG-SFDA† (51.7 on Cityscapes), which KGD surpasses by 1.9 AP50; other SOTA methods (SHOT†, HCL†, SFOD†, PETS†, TPDS†) all fall in the 50.3–50.8 range on Cityscapes, trailing KGD by 2.8–3.3 points. Against CLIP knowledge distillation methods (VILD, RegionKD, OADP combined with Mean Teacher), KGD leads by 3.0–3.4 AP50 on Cityscapes, attributed to its explicit knowledge-graph structure capturing inter-class semantic relations rather than mere feature-space alignment. Against prior knowledge-graph-based detection methods (KGE, Context Matters), KGD improves by 4.2–4.7 AP50 on Cityscapes, benefiting from CLIP-derived graphs versus hand-crafted ones. KGD also generalizes to open-vocabulary detectors, improving GLIP (+7.4), VILD (+7.6), RegionKD (+5.8), UniDet (+2.1), and RegionCLIP (+5.3) on Cityscapes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Detic large-vocabulary detector (which uses CLIP text embeddings as its classifier) and the Mean Teacher self-training framework, but departs from both prior domain adaptation methods for detection (SHOT, SFOD, HCL, IRG-SFDA, PETS, TPDS) and prior CLIP knowledge distillation methods (VILD, RegionKD, OADP). Where prior DA methods struggle with LVDs due to vocabulary and distribution discrepancies, and where prior CLIP distillation methods merely align feature spaces, KGD instead extracts explicit knowledge graphs from CLIP's implicit parameters—using WordNet hierarchy for language KG and dynamic manifold smoothing for vision KG—and encapsulates them via GCN-based reasoning. The authors claim this is the first work to study distilling CLIP knowledge graphs specifically for the object detection task.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete and practical gap: LVDs like Detic can localize objects well across domains but fail at classification, and the KG distillation approach provides a principled mechanism to transfer CLIP's inter-class semantic structure rather than just per-category features. The consistent ~7 AP50 gains across 11 diverse datasets (autonomous driving, surveillance, artistic illustration) suggest broad applicability. However, the approach is tied to Detic's architecture and pre-training on LVIS/ImageNet-21K, raising open questions about generalization to truly open-world categories absent from those sources, and the baselines (source-free DA methods not designed for LVDs) may understate the competitive landscape. The authors note future work could integrate Multi-Modal LLMs for more comprehensive knowledge transfer.
