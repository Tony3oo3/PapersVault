## Problem

Existing human pose estimation models trained on daily-activity datasets perform poorly on pedestrian pre-collision poses in traffic scenes, where poses are specific, rapidly changing, and often involve occluded lower limbs. No dedicated dataset or estimation framework existed for this task, making it difficult to reconstruct accurate pre-collision poses for accident analysis and active pedestrian safety systems.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240784/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The ITP stage takes a single pedestrian image crop (pre-selected bounding box from a dashcam frame) and outputs a 15-keypoint 2D pose as a heatmap. The PTM stage takes a temporal sequence of T=16 frames of 2D poses (15 JHMDB keypoints converted to 17 Human3.6M keypoints by computing pelvis, spine, and nose as midpoints) and outputs SMPL pose parameters θ ∈ R^{T×24×6} (6D rotation), shape parameters β ∈ R^{10}, 3D keypoints for 17 joints, and a 4-class pose label (Normal, Run, Avoid, Collision).

## Method

PPSENet is a two-stage top-down pipeline: a ResNet50-based ITP network estimates 2D pose heatmaps from image crops, and a PTM network lifts the 2D pose sequence to a 3D SMPL mesh. The PTM encoder is a Dual-stream Spatio-temporal Transformer (DSTformer) that runs spatial and temporal multi-head self-attention in two parallel branches and fuses them with adaptive softmax weights, initialized from MotionBERT pre-trained on large-scale motion datasets. The decoder uses iterative regression (3 iterations) to progressively refine pose and shape parameters, and an additional classification head predicts the pose category. A novel pose class loss (cross-entropy over four collision-behavior categories) is added to the standard SMPL and motion losses, exploiting the fact that pre-collision poses fall into distinct behavioral clusters to improve regression accuracy on this small dataset.

## Results

On the PVCP testset with detected 2D pose input, PPSENet achieves the best Procrustes-aligned metrics: PA-MPVE of 144.19 mm and PA-MPJPE_14j of 114.50 mm, outperforming all baselines. For the 17-joint 3D-only representation, PPSENet reaches MPJPE_17j of 198.16 mm and PA-MPJPE_17j of 105.86 mm, substantially better than MotionBERT (222.70 / 123.33 mm). The one-stage method PARE achieves the lowest raw MPVE (226.98 mm) and MPJPE_14j (191.97 mm), but this is partly because PVCP lacks global 3D scene layout, making Procrustes-aligned metrics the more meaningful comparison where PPSENet leads.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PPSENet outperforms all four baselines on the Procrustes-aligned metrics (PA-MPVE and PA-MPJPE), which remove global position and scale errors that are unavoidable given PVCP's lack of 3D scene layout. Against the closest two-stage competitor MotionBERT (fine-tuned on PVCP), PPSENet reduces PA-MPVE by 19.4 mm (144.19 vs. 163.58) and PA-MPJPE_17j by 17.5 mm (105.86 vs. 123.33), with the gap driven by the iterative regression decoder and pose class loss. The one-stage PARE method achieves the lowest raw MPVE (226.98 mm) and MPJPE_14j (191.97 mm), benefiting from its occlusion-aware design for lower-limb occlusion in collision scenes, but PPSENet still surpasses it on PA-MPVE (144.19 vs. 155.72) and PA-MPJPE_14j (114.50 vs. 119.85). VIBE, the weakest baseline, shows MPVE over 849 mm, indicating that generic video pose models fail severely on pre-collision poses. For single-class pre-collision poses (Run, Avoid, Collision), PPSENet's advantage over MotionBERT is even more pronounced, e.g., PA-MPVE of 133.45 mm for Run vs. 149.99 mm for MotionBERT.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 2D-to-3D pose lifting paradigm (as in SPIN, Pose2Mesh, and MotionBERT) to the specific domain of pedestrian pre-collision poses captured from dashcam videos. The PTM stage directly adopts MotionBERT's DSTformer encoder and iterative regression decoder as its architectural backbone, while the ITP stage follows the classic ResNet-50 + heatmap regression pipeline. The key departures from prior work are the introduction of a pose-classification loss (Normal/Run/Avoid/Collision) as an auxiliary supervision signal and the construction of the first dedicated dataset (PVCP) with SMPL mesh annotations for collision scenarios, filling a gap left by general-purpose pose datasets (MSCOCO, Human3.6M, PW3D) that lack both the domain specificity and the temporal continuity of pre-collision sequences.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The PVCP dataset addresses a genuine gap in pedestrian safety research: prior collision simulation work relied on fixed gait templates or labor-intensive manual angle measurement, and no open dataset provided 2D/3D/SMPL annotations for pre-collision poses from the vehicle perspective. However, the dataset is small (~20K poses, 209 videos), lacks camera intrinsics and vehicle speed, and the two-stage pipeline propagates 2D estimation errors into the 3D mesh, limiting absolute accuracy (MPVE ~258 mm with detected 2D input). Open questions include whether one-stage methods (e.g., PARE) can be adapted to this domain without the error propagation of 2D lifting, whether additional modalities (LiDAR, radar) could close the gap, and whether the pose-class taxonomy generalizes beyond the four categories defined here.
