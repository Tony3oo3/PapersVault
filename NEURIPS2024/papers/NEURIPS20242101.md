## Problem

Whether a complex search-based planning algorithm (Stockfish 16) can be approximated by a feed-forward transformer trained purely via supervised learning, without any explicit search at test time. The paper asks how far amortized planning can be pushed with scale alone, and whether the resulting 'searchless' policy can match engines that rely on Monte Carlo tree search or alpha-beta pruning during inference.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242101/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a fixed-length 77-token FEN string (flattened run-length encoding of the board, side to move, castling rights, en passant target, half-move clock, full-move counter) plus a 2-token UCI action index (from 1968 possible moves) for action-value prediction, or just the 77-token state for state-value/behavioral cloning. Outputs are log-softmax categorical distributions over 128 value bins (for value prediction) or 1968 action indices (for behavioral cloning). The FEN representation is technically non-Markovian since it lacks full move history, requiring external workarounds for rules like threefold repetition.

## Method

A decoder-only transformer (up to 270M parameters: 16 layers, 8 heads, 1024 embedding dim, SwiGLU, post-norm, no causal masking, learned positional encodings) is trained via supervised learning on ChessBench — 10M Lichess games yielding 15.3B Stockfish-16-annotated action-values. Win percentages are discretized into K=128 bins and the model is trained with an HL-Gauss smoothed cross-entropy loss. The resulting policy evaluates the predictor over all legal actions in the current state and selects the argmax expected action-value, performing no search. Action-value prediction (trained on ~30× more data points than state-value or behavioral cloning) consistently outperforms the alternatives, and scaling both model size (9M→270M) and dataset size (10K→10M games) monotonically improves generalization to novel boards, achieving grandmaster-level play (Lichess blitz Elo 2895) without test-time search.

## Results

The 270M-parameter transformer trained via supervised learning on ChessBench (10M games, 15.3B action-values) achieves a tournament Elo of 2299 (±15) and a Lichess blitz Elo of 2895 against human opponents (grandmaster level), without using any explicit search at test time. On 10K Lichess puzzles, it reaches 95.4% accuracy, nearly matching AlphaZero with 400 MCTS simulations (95.6%) and approaching Leela Chess Zero's value network alone (95.9%). Scaling from 9M to 270M parameters consistently improves all metrics (puzzle accuracy from 88.9% to 95.4%, tournament Elo from 2025 to 2299), confirming that both model and dataset scale are critical for strong chess performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the searchless baselines, the 270M transformer (2299 tournament Elo) essentially matches Leela Chess Zero's policy network alone (2292) and trails its value network alone (2418) by ~120 Elo, while substantially outperforming AlphaZero's policy net (1777) and value net (1992) by 300–500 Elo. On puzzle accuracy, the 270M model (95.4%) is within 0.5% of AlphaZero with full MCTS (95.6%) and 0.5% below LCZero's value net alone (95.9%), despite using no search and only FEN input (vs. full PGN history for all baselines). Against GPT-3.5-turbo-instruct, the transformer dominates on every metric (2299 vs. 1755 Lichess Elo vs. bots; 95.4% vs. 66.5% puzzle accuracy). However, the gap to the oracle Stockfish 16 (2711 tournament Elo, 99.8% puzzles) and to LCZero with 400 MCTS simulations (2858, 99.6%) remains substantial (~400 Elo, ~4–5% puzzles), indicating that perfect distillation of search-based value estimation into a feed-forward transformer is not yet achievable at this scale.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in strong chess engines (AlphaZero, Leela Chess Zero) that combines reinforcement learning via self-play with Monte Carlo tree search at test time. Instead, it repurposes the modern decoder-only transformer architecture (Llama-style) for a planning task, training it purely via supervised learning to distill Stockfish 16's search-based value estimation into a feed-forward model. The conceptual lineage runs from classical search-and-evaluation engines through the AlphaZero paradigm to this paper's 'searchless' distillation approach, while the introduction of ChessBench (10M games, 15B annotated data points) provides a new large-scale benchmark that prior chess AI work lacked. The paper also connects to the broader emerging literature on transformers as general algorithm approximators, positioning chess as a testbed where memorization is provably futile.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The demonstration that a 270M-parameter transformer trained solely via supervised learning can achieve grandmaster-level play (2895 Lichess blitz Elo) without any explicit search at test time is a concrete step toward understanding the limits of amortized planning with feed-forward networks. However, the persistent performance gap to Stockfish 16 (2935 Elo) and LCZero with MCTS (2858 Elo) raises the open question of whether further scaling alone can close it or whether architectural innovations (e.g., iterative computation across layers, domain-specific adaptations as in ChessFormer) are necessary. ChessBench, with its 15B action-value annotations and open-source release, provides a rigorous and reproducible benchmark for future work on distilling complex algorithms into neural networks. The finding that the gap cannot be fully closed by scale alone suggests that the 'transformer as universal algorithm approximator' narrative has non-trivial boundaries that warrant further investigation.
