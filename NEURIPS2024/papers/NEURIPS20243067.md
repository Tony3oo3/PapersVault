## Problem

Image relighting has traditionally relied on inverse graphics—recovering explicit geometry and surface properties, then re-rendering with a new light—which is error-prone, limited to the chosen physical model, and struggles with complex indoor interreflections. This paper asks whether a purely data-driven approach, with no explicit physical model of intrinsics or extrinsics, can achieve state-of-the-art relighting on real scenes, and whether useful intrinsic properties (like albedo) can emerge naturally from the relighting training objective alone.

## Input / Output

The model takes two 256×256 RGB images: a source scene image (the target to be relit) and a reference image from a potentially different scene that encodes the desired lighting. During training, pairs are drawn from the same scene under two different fixed lightings (MIT multi-illumination dataset, 1,015 scenes × 25 lightings); at inference, the reference can come from any scene. The primary output is the relighted source image; a secondary output is an albedo map obtained for free by zeroing the lighting contribution (α=0) at decode time, requiring no albedo supervision or CG training data.

## Method

The architecture is a U-Net autoencoder whose encoder produces multi-resolution spatial intrinsic feature maps S_{s,i}^l (L2-normalized per channel) and a 16-dimensional extrinsic vector L_s^l. The key structural innovation is *constrained scaling*: the extrinsic vector is injected into the decoder via F ⊙ (1 + α·tanh(MLP(L))) with α = 5×10⁻³, creating a severe information bottleneck that forces the lighting signal to be spatially pooled and prevents intrinsic details of the reference scene from leaking into the output. Training combines a pixel-wise relighting loss (L2 + SSIM + gradient), an *intrinsicness* loss that penalizes differences in S across lighting conditions for the same scene (regularized with a coding-rate-reduction term to prevent feature collapse), and a uniformity regularizer on L. Albedo emerges as a byproduct: setting α=0 at inference disables the lighting channel entirely, yielding a lighting-invariant intrinsic map that the paper shows is competitive with supervised albedo methods on the IIW benchmark.

## Results

On the MIT multi-illumination dataset, the proposed method achieves RMSE 0.297 and SSIM 0.473 on raw output, and RMSE 0.222 and SSIM 0.571 after color correction, outperforming all baselines on color-corrected metrics. For zero-shot albedo estimation on the IIW dataset (with no albedo training data), the method achieves a WHDR of 15.81 (with flattening and tuned threshold), surpassing the best supervised baseline Intrinsic Diffusion (17.10) and BBA (17.04). The method also generalizes zero-shot to out-of-distribution IIW and StyleGAN images without fine-tuning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SA-AE [22], the proposed method wins on color-corrected metrics (RMSE 0.222 vs. 0.232; SSIM 0.571 vs. 0.559) despite requiring no light-index supervision, while SA-AE with ground-truth light labels slightly edges ahead on raw RMSE (0.288 vs. 0.297). The unsupervised variant of SA-AE degrades substantially (RMSE 0.443) because its extrinsic representation leaks intrinsic scene information without the constrained-scaling bottleneck. Against S3Net [51], the proposed method wins decisively on all metrics (RMSE 0.297 vs. 0.499–0.512; SSIM 0.473 vs. 0.331–0.336), as S3Net's concatenation of source and reference images hinders generalization to cross-scene references. For albedo estimation on IIW, the method (WHDR 15.81 with flattening and tuned δ) outperforms Intrinsic Diffusion [29] (17.10), InverseRender [52] (21.40), and BBA [16] (17.04), despite never being trained on albedo maps or CG data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant inverse-graphics paradigm for relighting (recover explicit geometry and material parameters, then re-render) by representing both scene intrinsics and illumination as purely latent variables with no physical interpretation. It builds on the intrinsic-image literature (albedo, shading decomposition) and the image-based relighting line (SA-AE, S3Net) but removes the need for explicit lighting labels, depth maps, or parametric illumination models. The constrained-scaling bottleneck and coding-rate regularization draw on self-supervised representation learning (maximal coding rate reduction) to enforce lighting invariance of the intrinsic code, a mechanism not previously applied to relighting. The paper also connects to recent work showing that generative models (StyleGAN) encode intrinsic properties, but here intrinsics emerge from a discriminative relighting task rather than from a generative objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central finding—that albedo-like maps and luminaire awareness emerge as unsupervised byproducts of relighting training—challenges the assumption that recovering intrinsic scene properties requires explicit physical models or dedicated supervision. This opens a path toward intrinsic decomposition without the large CG datasets or manual annotations that current SOTA methods depend on. Key limitations include the requirement for paired multi-illumination data (resource-intensive to capture), poor handling of saturated LDR pixels, and the fact that intrinsics remain latent rather than explicitly interpretable (no depth or normal maps). Open questions include whether explicit intrinsics (depth, normals, roughness) can be reliably probed from the latent space, and whether the approach can be extended to fully unsupervised training on in-the-wild image collections.
