## Problem

Existing 3D-annotated datasets are limited to a small number of rigid categories (5–12) or narrow domains such as autonomous driving and indoor furniture, preventing the development of unified models that infer both 2D and 3D information for arbitrary rigid objects in natural images. Moreover, previous datasets lack cross-category 3D alignment of canonical poses, which is essential for models to exploit shared semantic parts across categories and generalize to unseen ones.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243045/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The dataset provides real RGB images from ImageNet21k with per-object 2D bounding boxes, 6D pose annotations (3D location parameterized as 2D center plus distance, and 3D viewpoint as azimuth, elevation, and in-plane rotation relative to a category canonical pose), scene density labels, four-level visual quality labels, and GPT-4v-generated natural-language captions with a special <pose6d> token referencing each object's 3D annotation. For the probing task, inputs are cropped and resized images centered on the annotated object, and outputs are three linearly-probed viewpoint classifications; for pose estimation tasks, outputs are continuous 6D pose predictions evaluated via rotation-matrix angle error.

## Method

ImageNet3D is constructed by selecting 200 rigid categories from ImageNet21k, obtaining 2D boxes via Grounding DINO with human refinement, collecting representative CAD models from Objaverse, and manually aligning canonical poses across all categories using three rules: shared semantic parts (e.g., wheels, spouts), similar shapes, and common-knowledge front directions. Thirty trained annotators label 6D poses, scene density, and visual quality through a custom web tool, and GPT-4v generates interleaved captions by receiving 2D/3D annotations and object names as textual context. For evaluation, the paper introduces two new tasks—linear probing of frozen foundation-model features on 3D viewpoint classification, and open-vocabulary pose estimation where models trained on 63 categories must generalize to 137 unseen ones—alongside standard joint classification and category-level pose estimation using classification-based backbones (ResNet50, SwinTransformer-T), 3D compositional models (NOVUM, NMM-Sphere with a shared sphere mesh and part-contrastive learning), and an LLaVA-pose variant.

## Results

On the joint image classification and category-level pose estimation task (3D-aware accuracy at π/6 threshold), NMM-Sphere achieves the best average of 57.4%, followed by NOVUM (56.2%), SwinTrans-T-General (53.2%), ResNet50-General (50.9%), and LLaVA-pose (49.1%). For linear probing of object-level 3D awareness, DINO v2 leads with 56.3% pose accuracy at π/6, substantially outperforming MAE (46.6%), DINO (42.0%), MiDaS (40.5%), CLIP (39.7%), and DeiT III (36.6%). On open-vocabulary pose estimation, models trained only on 63 common categories generalize to 137 novel categories with limited success: ResNet50-General reaches 37.1% vs. 53.6% when trained directly on novel categories, revealing a significant generalization gap.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among visual foundation models probed for 3D awareness, DINO v2 (56.3%) outperforms all others by a wide margin, beating MAE (46.6%) by ~10 points and DeiT III (36.6%) by ~20 points, confirming that self-supervised pretraining on large-scale data (LVD-142M) yields the strongest implicit 3D viewpoint discrimination. For the joint classification+pose task, the 3D compositional model NMM-Sphere (57.4%) surpasses the classification-based SwinTrans-T-General (53.2%) by 4.2 points and ResNet50-General (50.9%) by 6.5 points, while also outperforming the prior compositional model NOVUM (56.2%) by 1.2 points. LLaVA-pose (49.1%) underperforms all other baselines, suggesting that current MLLM architectures struggle with precise 3D pose regression. On open-vocabulary pose estimation, all category-agnostic models (ResNet50-General 37.1%, SwinTrans-T-General 35.8%, NMM-Sphere 29.5%) fall substantially short of the oracle trained on novel categories (53.6%), with NMM-Sphere showing the weakest generalization despite its strong in-distribution performance, indicating that contrastive part features do not transfer as effectively to unseen categories as classification-based features.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends ObjectNet3D (Xiang et al., ECCV 2016) from 100 to 200 rigid categories and 57K to 86K annotated instances, while introducing two key design elements absent from all prior 3D-annotated datasets: cross-category 3D alignment of canonical poses and natural language captions interleaved with 6D pose tokens. The caption generation pipeline builds on the GPT-assisted approach from Visual Instruction Tuning (Liu et al., NeurIPS 2023), and the NMM-Sphere baseline extends the 3D compositional render-and-compare framework of NOVUM (Jesslen et al., 2023) with a unified sphere mesh and category-agnostic contrastive features. Conceptually, the paper departs from the specialist-model paradigm of prior category-level pose estimation (which typically covers 5–20 categories) by explicitly targeting a single unified model across 200 categories, and introduces open-vocabulary pose estimation as a new evaluation axis.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ImageNet3D is likely to become the standard benchmark for general-purpose object-level 3D understanding, filling a critical gap where no prior dataset covered more than 100 rigid categories with high-quality 6D annotations. The cross-category 3D alignment design is a non-trivial contribution: the ablation showing NMM-Sphere collapsing from 57.4% to 10.6% without alignment demonstrates that this is not a cosmetic improvement but a structural requirement for multi-category 3D learning. Open questions remain: how to close the 16-point gap between open-vocabulary and in-distribution pose estimation, whether the object-centric nature of ImageNet21k images limits transfer to multi-object scenes, and how to effectively integrate these 3D models with LLMs for embodied reasoning. The finding that DINO v2 leads in 3D awareness but with much smaller inter-model gaps than previously reported for low-level 3D tasks suggests object-level 3D understanding may be a harder axis to probe.
