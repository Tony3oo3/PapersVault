## Problem

Existing black-box jailbreaking attacks rely on either stochastic search (genetic algorithms that randomly select mutators) or in-context learning (which has limited capacity for sequential refinement decisions), both of which are inefficient in the vast prompt-structure search space. This paper asks whether a DRL agent can replace random mutator selection with a learned, guided search policy that is more effective and less random than genetic methods.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240842/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At each time step, the DRL agent receives the XLM-RoBERTa hidden representation of the current jailbreaking prompt (the harmful question embedded in a prompt structure) as its state, and outputs a categorical distribution over five predefined mutators (rephrase, crossover, generate_similar, shorten, expand) executed by a helper LLM (GPT-3.5-turbo). The target LLM's response to the updated prompt is scored by cosine similarity against a reference answer from an unaligned model, producing a dense reward in [0,1]. The final output is a refined jailbreaking prompt structure that elicits an on-topic harmful response from the target LLM, verified at test time by GPT-4 judgment.

## Method

RLbreaker formulates jailbreaking as a Markov Decision Process where a lightweight MLP agent learns a policy to select which of five LLM-executed mutators to apply at each step, replacing the random mutator selection of genetic algorithms with a learned, guided search. The state is the XLM-RoBERTa embedding of the current prompt (deliberately excluding the target LLM's response to keep dimensionality low), and the reward is the cosine similarity between the target LLM's response and a pre-computed reference answer from an unaligned model, which measures answer relevance rather than mere harmfulness. Training uses a customized PPO objective where the value network is removed and the discounted return is used directly as the advantage estimate, reducing training randomness. The agent is trained for up to T=5 steps per episode with early termination when reward exceeds τ=0.7, and at test time the trained policy refines prompt structures from the training set against unseen questions, with GPT-4 serving as the success oracle.

## Results

On the AdvBench dataset (520 harmful questions), RLbreaker achieves the highest GPT-Judge score across all three primary target models: 0.5250 on Llama2-70b-chat, 1.0000 on Mixtral-8x7B-Instruct, and 0.3688 on GPT-3.5-turbo (Full set). On the harder Max50 subset (50 most toxic questions), RLbreaker scores 0.4000, 1.0000, and 0.3200 respectively, substantially outperforming the best genetic-based baseline (GPTFUZZER: 0.0400, 0.2600, 0.0800). RLbreaker also achieves the highest cosine similarity (Sim.) on Llama2-70b-chat (0.7964) and GPT-3.5-turbo (0.7341), though GPTFUZZER edges it slightly on Mixtral Sim. (0.7859 vs. 0.7480). Per-question generation time ranges from 3.0 s (Vicuna-7b) to 18.5 s (GPT-3.5-turbo), comparable to or faster than most baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RLbreaker decisively outperforms both in-context learning baselines (PAIR and Cipher) on GPT-Judge across all three models, with the gap being most dramatic on Llama2-70b-chat (0.5250 vs. 0.0094 for PAIR and 0.1094 for Cipher). Against the genetic-method baselines, RLbreaker achieves 3.5× higher GPT-Judge on Llama2-70b-chat (0.5250 vs. 0.1500 for GPTFUZZER and 0.1468 for AutoDAN) and 3.6× higher on GPT-3.5-turbo (0.3688 vs. 0.1031 for GPTFUZZER). On Mixtral-8x7B-Instruct, RLbreaker reaches a perfect 1.0000 GPT-Judge, surpassing AutoDAN (0.6750) and GPTFUZZER (0.5688) by large margins, though GPTFUZZER slightly edges RLbreaker on the Sim. metric (0.7859 vs. 0.7480). The white-box GCG attack, despite gradient access, remains the weakest baseline on GPT-Judge (0.0656–0.1188), confirming that token-level adversarial search is ineffective against large models. On the hardest Max50 subset, RLbreaker's advantage over all baselines widens further, e.g., 0.4000 vs. 0.0400 (GPTFUZZER) on Llama2-70b-chat.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reframes black-box LLM jailbreaking as a guided search problem, departing from the dominant genetic-algorithm paradigm (GPTFUZZER, AutoDAN) that randomly selects mutators without strategic guidance. It borrows the five prompt-structure mutators from GPTFUZZER as its action space but replaces random selection with a DRL agent trained via a customized PPO algorithm (with the value network removed). The reward function—cosine similarity between the target LLM's response and a reference answer from an unaligned model—addresses a gap in prior work where keyword matching or harmfulness detectors fail to measure whether the response actually answers the specific question.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that DRL-guided search substantially outperforms stochastic (genetic) search for jailbreaking, particularly on the hardest questions (Max50) and against perplexity-based defenses where baselines are nearly neutralized. The transferability finding—that policies trained against stronger-aligned models transfer better to weaker ones—suggests a practical red-teaming workflow. Open questions include reducing false negatives in the cosine-similarity reward, extending to multi-modal and multi-turn settings, and whether the DRL framework generalizes beyond the five mutators to more diverse attack operators.
