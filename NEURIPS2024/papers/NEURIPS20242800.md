## Problem

Deep RL policies are brittle to small environmental variations (e.g., different lane counts, weather, flow density), and the two standard remedies—training N independent models or one multi-task model—suffer from high compute cost and negative transfer, respectively. The paper tackles the Sequential Source Task Selection (SSTS) problem: given a training budget K < N, how to strategically choose which K source tasks to train on so that zero-shot transfer to all N target tasks maximizes expected generalization performance. To the authors' knowledge, this is the first work to explicitly model generalization performance as a first-class object in contextual RL.

## Input / Output

The method takes a continuous, bounded context space X (e.g., road length, inflow rate, pole mass), a training budget K, and access to train and evaluate policies on any context-MDP M_x. It produces a set of K trained policies π_{1:K} and, for each target task x' ∈ X, the best-performing policy selected via zero-shot transfer (i.e., max over k of J(π_k, x')). The selection is sequential and online: each new source task is chosen based on feedback from previously trained policies, and the context space is assumed one-dimensional with smooth performance variation. The method is agnostic to the underlying RL algorithm (DQN, PPO, A2C) and operates on state-vector observations.

## Method

MBTL layers a Bayesian optimization loop on top of any single-task RL algorithm. It models generalization performance in two parts: (1) a Gaussian Process regression over the source training performance J(π_x, x) with an RBF kernel, and (2) a linear generalization gap ΔJ(π_x, x') ≈ θ|x − x'| (Assumption 1). At each step k, the next source task is selected by maximizing a UCB-based acquisition function that combines the GP posterior mean, uncertainty (scaled by β_k), the predicted generalization gap, and the current best per-task performance, all clipped at zero and averaged over target tasks. Theoretically, MBTL achieves sublinear cumulative regret O(√(K·β_K·γ_K)), and tighter bounds (e.g., O(√(C·β_K·γ_K·log K))) when the effective search space shrinks as previously trained policies cover more of the context space. Empirically, MBTL matches or exceeds independent and multi-task baselines with up to 43× fewer training samples across traffic and continuous-control CMDPs.

## Results

On traffic CMDPs (K=15 source tasks), MBTL achieves an average normalized performance of 0.7641, outperforming Independent training (0.6778) and Multi-task RL (0.6017), while closely approaching the Sequential Oracle (0.7844). On standard continuous control CMDPs (Cartpole, Pendulum, BipedalWalker, HalfCheetah with N=100 contexts), MBTL reaches 0.8950 average performance versus 0.8093 for Independent and 0.6957 for Multi-task, with the Oracle at 0.9255. MBTL matches or exceeds baseline performance with up to 43× fewer training samples (e.g., BipedalWalker-Friction requires only 2 models vs. 100 for Independent), and rapidly approaches Oracle-level performance within approximately 10 transfer steps.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MBTL is compared against two canonical baselines (Independent training of N separate models, and Multi-task RL with a single context-conditioned policy) and three heuristic multi-policy baselines (Random, Equidistant, and Greedy source task selection), plus a Sequential Oracle upper bound. MBTL consistently outperforms both Independent and Multi-task baselines across all 10 traffic CMDP tasks and 12 continuous control tasks, with the largest gaps on Eco-Driving (e.g., 0.4985 vs. 0.3850 for Independent on Green Phase) and HalfCheetah (0.9214 vs. 0.6679 for Independent on Gravity). Among the multi-policy heuristics, MBTL is competitive with or slightly exceeds Random, Equidistant, and Greedy on most tasks, though Equidistant edges out MBTL on a few traffic tasks (e.g., Eco-Driving Inflow: 0.5513 vs. 0.5172). The key advantage of MBTL is sample efficiency: it reaches baseline-level performance with as few as 1–5 trained models versus 40–100 for Independent training (up to 43× fewer samples), and approaches the Oracle within ~10 transfer steps. The Sequential Oracle, which has access to all policies' generalization performance, remains the upper bound, with MBTL typically within 1–3% of Oracle average performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Bayesian optimization framework of Srinivas et al. [41] for GP-based sequential decision-making, adapting it to the novel problem of source task selection in contextual MDPs. It departs from standard CRL approaches (which assume fixed task distributions) and from multi-task RL (which trains a single universal policy) by introducing an intermediate 'multi-policy' paradigm where K < N models are strategically trained. The explicit modeling of generalization performance—combining GP regression for training performance with a linear generalization gap model—is positioned as the first such formulation for contextual RL, extending prior source task selection work [25, 28, 33] that did not explicitly model the generalization landscape.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MBTL provides a principled, theoretically grounded framework (sublinear regret guarantees) for the practically critical question of which tasks to train on when generalization across a continuum of contexts is desired. The up to 43x sample efficiency gain over independent and multi-task baselines is compelling, though these baselines are relatively weak. A key limitation is the restriction to single-dimensional context variation with explicit similarity, leaving high-dimensional context spaces and formal task similarity metrics as open problems. The insensitivity to the underlying RL algorithm and acquisition function choice suggests the framework is robust and could be layered atop future RL advances.
