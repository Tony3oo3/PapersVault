## Problem

State-of-the-art two-stage last-layer retraining methods for worst-group accuracy (WGA), such as RAD and SELF, suffer catastrophic performance degradation under symmetric label noise because their error-set construction selects noisy majority points over true minority examples. Existing noise-robust alternatives like END require full model retraining and underperform at low noise levels, leaving a gap for a lightweight, domain-agnostic correction that preserves the efficiency of last-layer retraining while achieving robustness.

## Input / Output

The method consumes frozen embeddings from a pre-trained base model (assumed trained on clean but imbalanced data) along with their noisy class labels, operating entirely in the latent embedding space rather than on raw pixels or text. It outputs a set of corrected class labels after kNN label spreading, which are then fed into a two-stage LLR procedure to produce a retrained last-layer classifier. The approach requires no domain annotations during training, only access to the base model's embedding function and a small clean validation set for hyperparameter selection.

## Method

The core contribution is a modular kNN label spreading preprocessing step applied in the base model's latent embedding space. Because clean embeddings exhibit near-linear separability (neural collapse), a uniform majority vote over k nearest neighbors corrects most symmetric label noise in a single round. The number of neighbors k is chosen to scale with the noise level p, following the excess risk bound of Gao et al. (2018), which predicts k should grow as O((R* + p/(1-2p))²). After T rounds of spreading (T=1 in practice), the cleaned labels are passed unchanged to existing two-stage LLR methods—yielding kNN-RAD or kNN-SELF—so the approach is a drop-in addition with minimal computational overhead. The method is domain-agnostic: it never uses group or domain labels during training, relying solely on the geometric structure of the embedding space to identify and correct mislabeled points.

## Results

On Waterbirds, kNN-RAD achieves 90.92% worst-group accuracy (WGA) at 0% label noise and maintains 86.90% at 30% noise, while vanilla RAD collapses to 19.52% and SELF to 0.66% at the same noise level. On CelebA, kNN-RAD sustains 79.5% WGA at 30% noise versus 0% for RAD, and outperforms the full-retraining method END (81.8%) at every noise level despite only updating the last layer. On CivilComments, kNN-RAD matches oracle domain-aware baselines (GUW*: 79.39%, GDS*: 81.21%) at 30% noise with 78.56% WGA, without requiring domain annotations. The method requires only a single round of kNN label spreading as a drop-in preprocessing step before standard two-stage LLR corrections.

## Baselines & Comparisons

kNN-RAD dramatically outperforms vanilla RAD [22] at all non-zero noise levels: on Waterbirds, RAD collapses from 91.23% to 19.52% WGA at 30% noise while kNN-RAD retains 86.90%; on CelebA, RAD drops to 0% at 20% noise while kNN-RAD holds 82.29%. Similarly, kNN-SELF [11] rescues SELF from catastrophic failure (SELF drops to 0.66% on Waterbirds at 30% noise vs. kNN-SELF's 82.44%). Against END [16], the only other method designed for label-noise robustness, kNN-RAD wins at every noise level on both Waterbirds (86.90% vs. 81.8% at 30%) and CelebA (79.5% vs. 77.8% at 30%), despite END performing full model retraining while kNN-RAD updates only the last layer. Notably, kNN-RAD also matches or exceeds oracle domain-aware baselines: on Waterbirds at 30% noise it surpasses both GUW* (84.78%) and GDS* (78.09%), and on CivilComments it is within 1–3% of GUW* and GDS* across all noise levels, all without requiring domain annotations during training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two-stage last-layer retraining for worst-group accuracy (RAD, SELF) and kNN label propagation, connecting them via the observation that pretrained model embeddings are near-linearly separable (neural collapse), making label spreading an effective denoising step. It departs from Oh et al.'s END, which modifies the error-set construction to be noise-robust at the cost of clean-data performance, and from robust-loss approaches (α-loss, loss correction), which the paper demonstrates are insufficient because the error set itself becomes dominated by noisy majority points. The contribution is explicitly modular: the kNN preprocessing is a drop-in that can be combined with any existing or future two-stage LLR method.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper documents a catastrophic failure mode (RAD dropping to 0% WGA at 20% noise on CelebA) that practitioners deploying two-stage fairness corrections with real-world noisy labels would encounter, and provides a minimal-overhead fix that restores SOTA performance without domain annotations. Key limitations include the assumption of clean embeddings (though partially relaxed experimentally), the hyperparameter sensitivity of k, and the absence of formal fairness guarantees. An open question is whether coupled label-cleaning and error-set-selection procedures could further reduce the variance observed in kNN-SELF, and whether the approach extends to asymmetric or class-dependent noise patterns.
