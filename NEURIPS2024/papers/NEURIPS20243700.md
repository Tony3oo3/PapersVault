## Problem

While numerous algorithms have been developed for learning under instance-dependent label noise, there has been relatively little theory characterizing the fundamental limits of this learning paradigm, particularly in the multi-class setting. This paper asks: what are the minimax-optimal rates for excess risk under multi-class, instance-dependent label noise, and can the simplest possible strategy—ignoring the noise entirely—achieve near-optimal performance?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243700/cropped_page4_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images (CIFAR-10/100 at 32×32, MNIST at 28×28) paired with potentially corrupted labels, and outputs a single class prediction. In practice, the pipeline first maps each image to a fixed-dimensional feature vector via a frozen self-supervised or transfer-learned backbone (e.g., DINOv2, ResNet-50), then fits a logistic regression on those features using the noisy labels directly—no data augmentation, no noise-aware loss, and no hyperparameters beyond standard cross-validation of the linear head.

## Method

The paper introduces Relative Signal Strength (RSS), a pointwise measure quantifying how much label certainty in the noisy posterior transfers to the clean posterior, and uses it to cast label-noise learning as domain adaptation under posterior drift. Leveraging RSS, the authors establish nearly matching minimax upper and lower bounds on excess risk, proving that Noise-Ignorant ERM (NI-ERM)—which minimizes empirical 0/1 loss on noisy labels as if they were clean—is (nearly) minimax optimal. They further derive precise linear-algebraic conditions on the noise transition matrix (e.g., diagonal dominance for zero-Bayes-risk distributions) that guarantee noise immunity, meaning the Bayes classifier is unchanged by the noise. For practical deployment, the authors propose FE+NI-ERM: a two-step procedure that first extracts features with any label-free method (self-supervised contrastive learning, ImageNet pretraining, or DINOv2) and then trains a linear classifier on those features using noisy labels in a noise-ignorant manner, achieving state-of-the-art accuracy on CIFAR-10N and CIFAR-100N benchmarks.

## Results

On CIFAR-10N, FE+NI-ERM (DINOv2 features + logistic regression) achieves 98.69–98.80% test accuracy across the Aggre, Rand1, Rand2, and Rand3 noise types, and 95.71% on the most challenging 'Worst' category. On CIFAR-100N, it reaches 83.17% accuracy, substantially outperforming all baselines. All results are deterministic (±0.00) since the method uses sklearn logistic regression with cross-validation and no data augmentation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FE+NI-ERM outperforms all four baselines on 5 of 6 noise categories. On CIFAR-10N, it beats ProMix by ~1.0–1.4 points on Aggre, Rand1, Rand2, and Rand3 (e.g., 98.80% vs 97.39% on Rand1), and beats ILL, PLS, and DivideMix by 2–6 points across all four categories. The sole exception is CIFAR-10N 'Worst,' where ProMix (96.34%) edges out FE+NI-ERM (95.71%) by 0.63 points. On CIFAR-100N, the advantage is dramatic: FE+NI-ERM achieves 83.17% versus 73.79% for ProMix, 73.25% for PLS, 71.13% for DivideMix, and 68.07% for ILL — a margin of 9.4–15.1 points. Notably, FE+NI-ERM requires no data augmentation and no hand-tuned hyperparameters beyond standard cross-validation, whereas the competing methods rely on complex training pipelines with hard-coded hyperparameters.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the binary-classification analysis of Cannings et al. (2020) and Cai and Wei (2021) to the multi-class, instance-dependent label noise setting by introducing Relative Signal Strength (RSS), a pointwise measure that relaxes the prior assumption that clean and noisy Bayes classifiers must agree. It also builds on the posterior drift / domain adaptation framing of Scott (2019) and the minimax analysis of Im and Grigas (2023), but provides a more refined characterization via RSS and establishes nearly matching upper and lower bounds. The practical component draws on the self-supervised feature extraction paradigm (DINOv2, contrastive ResNet) and the observation that simple models are inherently robust to label noise, connecting to prior work on noise-immunity under symmetric noise (Ghosh and Kumar, 2017; Menon et al., 2018).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central theoretical result—that Noise Ignorant ERM is nearly minimax optimal under instance-dependent label noise—challenges the prevailing assumption that sophisticated noise-correction or robust-training procedures are necessary, and provides a clean, unified lens (RSS) for understanding when and why learning from noisy labels succeeds or fails. Practically, the 'feature extraction + linear NI-ERM' recipe is strikingly simple (no data augmentation, no noise-specific hyperparameters) yet achieves state-of-the-art on CIFAR-N, suggesting that much of the complexity in prior noisy-label methods may be unnecessary when good self-supervised features are available. Key limitations include the restriction to linear classifiers on top of frozen features, evaluation limited to CIFAR-10/100, and the open question of whether the RSS framework and NI-ERM optimality extend to end-to-end deep network training or other modalities.
