## Problem

Existing multivariate probabilistic forecasting models (e.g., GPVar) assume temporal independence of errors, yet real-world residuals exhibit substantial autocorrelation and cross-lag correlation due to omitted covariates and model misspecification. Prior work on correlated errors was either restricted to univariate models or deterministic settings, leaving a gap for multivariate probabilistic forecasting with Gaussian-distributed errors.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241719/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes P steps of historical multivariate time series (N variables) plus time-dependent covariates (hour of day, day of week, series identifier) and produces multivariate Gaussian parameters (mean vector, low-rank-plus-diagonal covariance) at each autoregressive step. During multistep-ahead prediction, it generates calibrated samples by iteratively conditioning the next-step error distribution on observed residuals from the preceding D−1 steps, leveraging the learned cross-covariance structure. The training batch is restructured into D consecutive one-step-ahead prediction slices (each of length P+1) to jointly model cross-correlation over a temporal window, rather than evaluating likelihood independently at each step.

## Method

The paper introduces a plug-and-play GLS loss that captures error cross-correlation over D steps by organizing D one-step-ahead prediction slices into a single batch and modeling their joint covariance. Cross-covariance is parameterized through a dynamic D×D correlation matrix C_t applied to low-dimensional latent variables r_t (dimension R ≪ N) via a Kronecker structure C_t ⊗ I_R, where C_t is a dynamic weighted sum of squared-exponential kernel matrices with varying lengthscales plus an identity component, with weights generated from the hidden state through a small neural network. The resulting batch covariance Σ_t^bat = L_t^bat(C_t ⊗ I_R)(L_t^bat)^T + diag(d_t^bat) is inverted and its determinant computed in O(D³R³) rather than O(D³B³) using the Sherman-Morrison-Woodbury identity and matrix determinant lemma. During prediction, the learned correlation structure calibrates the predictive distribution at each rolling step by computing the conditional Gaussian of the next error given observed residuals from prior steps, reducing residual cross-correlation and improving both point accuracy and uncertainty quantification.

## Results

On the CRPS_sum metric across 9 benchmark datasets, the proposed method achieves an average relative improvement of 13.79% for GPVar and 6.91% for the Transformer base model compared to their respective baselines without correlated error modeling. The method consistently outperforms the naive VAR and GARCH statistical baselines on most datasets, with the largest gains observed on m4_hourly (GPVar: 0.0358 vs. 0.0613) and wikipedia (Transformer: 0.0500 vs. 0.0841). Additional metrics (Energy Score, RRMSE, quantile losses) confirm consistent improvements, with the Energy Score improving by 5.58% (GPVar) and 3.21% (Transformer) on average.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two naive statistical baselines (VAR(1) and DCC-GARCH(1,1)) and, more importantly, against the same deep base models (GPVar and Transformer) trained without the proposed correlated-error loss. The proposed method (w/) outperforms its own base model (w/o) on 7 out of 9 datasets for GPVar and 7 out of 9 for Transformer, yielding average relative CRPS_sum improvements of 13.79% and 6.91% respectively. Both deep models with the method substantially outperform VAR and GARCH on nearly all datasets where those baselines are applicable (e.g., on m4_hourly, GPVar w/ achieves 0.0358 vs. VAR's 0.2352 and GARCH's 0.2758). The exceptions where the method does not improve are exchange_rate for GPVar (0.0117 vs. 0.0068) and electricity for Transformer (0.0638 vs. 0.0494), suggesting the benefit depends on the degree of residual autocorrelation present in the data. The Energy Score (a multivariate metric) shows the most consistent gains, improving by 5.58% (GPVar) and 3.21% (Transformer) on average, confirming that the method captures cross-series dependencies effectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the univariate GLS-loss approach of Zheng et al. [9] to the multivariate setting, where error cross-correlation involves both autocovariance and cross-lag covariance across N time series. It builds on GPVar's [3] low-rank-plus-diagonal contemporaneous covariance parameterization and autoregressive Gaussian likelihood framework, adding a temporal dimension via a Kronecker-structured dynamic correlation matrix C_t ⊗ I_R over low-dimensional latent processes. The key departure from prior work is the use of the Sherman-Morrison-Woodbury identity to make the DB×DB likelihood computation tractable by reducing it to a DR×DR matrix inversion, where R ≪ B. The paper positions itself as the first to explicitly model error cross-covariance in multivariate probabilistic (as opposed to deterministic) time series forecasting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method provides a practical, plug-and-play correction for a well-known but often-ignored assumption (temporal independence of errors) in autoregressive probabilistic forecasters, with modest parameter overhead and no architectural changes required. However, the Kronecker structure C_t ⊗ I_R forces all latent temporal processes to share an identical autocorrelation pattern, which the authors acknowledge causes the model to learn the 'mildest' correlation among time series in a batch—a significant limitation for heterogeneous multivariate systems. Open questions include whether more flexible coregionalization (e.g., LMC with per-process C_r,t matrices) or fully learnable Toeplitz/AR(p) parameterizations of C_t would close the gap, and whether the Gaussian error assumption can be relaxed via copula transformations without losing the marginalization properties essential to the calibration step.
