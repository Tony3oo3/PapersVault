## Problem

Future motion prediction in dynamic scenes remains challenging because traditional video diffusion models are conditioned on single RGB frames or text prompts, which lack the microsecond-level temporal granularity needed to capture subtle motion dynamics. Event cameras record intensity changes asynchronously at high temporal resolution, but no prior work has integrated this high-frequency motion signal into a generative diffusion framework for motion simulation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243349/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a sequence of event frames formatted as voxel grids (B×H×W with B=3 time bins, 20ms intervals) as conditioning input, where at inference multiple high-temporal-resolution event frames (s>1) are aggregated as a test-time prompt rather than a single frame. The output is a future event sequence representing predicted object motion in event form. Events are normalized using maximum-value normalization across the sequence to avoid flickering, and the model operates at 128×128 resolution after cropping from source data (256×256 for VisEvent, 512–720 crops for EventVOT).

## Method

The approach fine-tunes a pre-trained Stable Video Diffusion U-Net on event sequences using a standard diffusion denoising loss, with both spatial and temporal attention layers jointly fine-tuned and RGB-trained CLIP features injected as conditioning (event-trained CLIP underperforms). At inference, a high-temporal-resolution guided sampling strategy replaces the first s entries of the random noise tensor with noised event prompt frames during the initial τ denoising steps, injecting motion priors into the reverse diffusion trajectory. A reinforcement learning alignment stage then refines the model using PPO, treating the multi-step denoising as a Markov chain and optimizing a reward composed of FVD and SSIM, with per-batch standard deviation normalization to prevent the model from collapsing toward static scenes. The full pipeline is trained in a distributed setup with 4 GPUs for trajectory generation and 4 GPUs for RL updates.

## Results

On the VisEvent and EventVOT datasets, E-Motion achieves the best FVD (1055.25), MSE (0.0170), SSIM (0.7998), LPIPS (0.3123), and mIoU (0.302) among all compared methods in the event modality. In downstream object tracking, the method reaches an upper-bound ratio R_upper of 0.891, substantially exceeding TAU (0.669), SimVP (0.640), and PredRNN (0.496), approaching the ground-truth event ceiling of 1.000. The method also generalizes to the BS-ERGB dataset captured by a different event camera, maintaining strong prediction quality under extreme lighting and occlusion.

## Baselines & Comparisons

E-Motion outperforms all event-modality baselines on FVD, MSE, SSIM, LPIPS, and mIoU. Against the strongest event baseline TAU [50], it reduces FVD from 1218.03 to 1055.25 (−13.4%) and improves mIoU from 0.228 to 0.302 (+32.5%), though SimVP [13] retains a marginal aIoU advantage (0.532 vs. 0.522). Compared to the video-modality SVD baseline [1], E-Motion achieves a 6.0% lower FVD (1055 vs. 1123) and a substantially higher SSIM (0.800 vs. 0.645), demonstrating that event-sequence conditioning transfers motion knowledge more effectively than RGB-frame conditioning. In downstream tracking (Table 2), E-Motion reaches R_upper = 0.891, a 33% improvement over TAU (0.669) and 39% over SimVP (0.640), approaching the ground-truth event upper bound of 1.000. PredRNNv2 [59] and PhydNet [17] are consistently the weakest baselines across all metrics, with PhydNet's FVD (1602.84) being 52% worse than E-Motion's.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously separate research threads: event-based vision (which provides high-temporal-resolution motion signals) and video diffusion models (which provide strong generative priors from large-scale RGB pre-training). It builds directly on Stable Video Diffusion as the backbone, adapting it to accept voxelized event sequences as conditioning inputs, and departs from prior event-prediction work (SimVP, TAU, PredRNN) by replacing recurrent or attention-based predictors with a diffusion generative process. The reinforcement learning alignment step draws on recent work applying PPO to diffusion models (Zhang et al., 2024) but is the first to use it specifically for stabilizing event-sequence generation trajectories.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper opens a promising direction for leveraging event cameras' microsecond-level temporal resolution within the powerful generative framework of diffusion models, with demonstrated downstream utility for tracking, segmentation, and flow estimation. However, the method's 1521M parameters and 694 GFLOPs (roughly 25–65× more than competing predictors) raise serious questions about real-time deployability in the autonomous driving and robotics settings the authors target. Key open questions include whether the RL alignment can be distilled into a lighter model, whether the texture-free nature of event data fundamentally caps semantic fidelity, and whether the approach generalizes beyond the two datasets tested.
