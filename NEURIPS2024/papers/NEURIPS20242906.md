## Problem

Contrastive learning's theoretical foundations remain poorly understood, and standard methods like InfoNCE treat alignment as a single-step pairwise matching problem that struggles with noisy views, data quality variations, and domain shifts. The paper asks whether reframing contrastive learning as a distributional alignment problem via optimal transport can provide both a unifying theoretical lens and practical tools for more flexible, robust representation learning.

## Input / Output

The method consumes two independently augmented views of each image in a batch (e.g., 32×32 CIFAR or 224×224 ImageNet crops) and produces a B×B transport plan (coupling matrix) between the normalized embeddings of the two view sets. During training, the encoder and projector are updated to minimize the divergence between this computed transport plan and a user-specified target alignment plan; for downstream evaluation, a linear probe is trained on frozen embeddings to output class labels. The target plan can be customized beyond the identity matrix to encode domain or class block structures.

## Method

GCA formulates contrastive learning as a bi-level optimization: the inner problem computes a transport plan P_θ by iteratively applying Bregman (KL) projections onto row and column marginal constraint sets (i.e., Sinkhorn iterations), while the outer problem minimizes a divergence d_M between P_θ and a target plan P_tgt. The key insight is that InfoNCE, RINCE, and BYOL are all recoverable as special cases of GCA by choosing specific divergences, constraint sets, and iteration counts (Table 1). The novel GCA-UOT variant further relaxes the hard marginal constraints into soft KL penalties with tunable weights λ₁, λ₂, yielding an unbalanced transport formulation that adaptively down-weights noisy positive alignments. The target plan P_tgt can be structured as a block-diagonal matrix incorporating domain or class priors, enabling the framework to simultaneously encode multiple similarity levels in the latent space.

## Results

GCA-UOT achieves the best linear-probe accuracy across all four standard datasets: 92.61% on CIFAR-10, 71.45% on CIFAR-100, 91.96% on SVHN, and 74.09% on ImageNet-100. Under extreme augmentations and corruption (CIFAR-10C), GCA-UOT's advantage widens substantially, reaching 89.61% on CIFAR-10C and 77.60% on CIFAR-10C (Ex), outperforming the next-best method (RINCE/GCA-RINCE) by 1.0–2.8 points. The GCA iterative variants (GCA-INCE, GCA-RINCE) consistently improve over their single-step counterparts by 0.1–1.5 points in standard settings and 0.2–1.2 points in noisy settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GCA-UOT outperforms all baselines on every dataset and setting. Against INCE (the most direct single-step contrastive baseline), GCA-UOT gains +0.60 to +2.79 points, with the largest margin on CIFAR-10C (Ex) where the gap reaches +2.76 points. Compared to RINCE, which is already designed for noisy views, GCA-UOT still leads by +1.56 to +2.79 points, confirming that the unbalanced OT relaxation provides additional robustness beyond RINCE's parameter-based rebalancing. Against SimCLR and BYOL, GCA-UOT achieves +0.45 to +8.20 points, with the most dramatic gap on CIFAR-10C (Ex) where BYOL drops to 69.40% while GCA-UOT maintains 77.60%. The IOT and IOT-uni methods from [51], which also use an OT perspective, are substantially outperformed by GCA-UOT (by 1.6–20.9 points), particularly under corruption where IOT collapses to 67.36% on CIFAR-10C versus GCA-UOT's 89.61%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the inverse optimal transport (IOT) connection to contrastive learning established by Shi et al. [51], extending it into a unified Generalized Contrastive Alignment (GCA) framework that subsumes InfoNCE, RINCE, and BYOL as single-step special cases of a bi-level proximal-point objective. The key departure is the introduction of iterative Bregman projection steps (yielding GCA-INCE and GCA-RINCE) and an unbalanced OT relaxation (GCA-UOT) that softens the hard marginal constraints, drawing on the UOT literature [11, 44]. The framework also repurposes the target transport plan as a flexible interface for encoding domain-specific or hierarchical alignment constraints, going beyond the identity-matching assumption of standard CL.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The GCA framework provides a principled, OT-based lens for understanding and modifying contrastive losses, giving practitioners a systematic way to trade off alignment strictness, robustness to noisy views, and domain-awareness through hyperparameters (ε, λ₁, λ₂, α, β). However, empirical gains over strong baselines are modest (typically 0.5–1.5% on standard augmentations, smaller under noise), and experiments are confined to CIFAR-scale and ImageNet-100 datasets with ResNet backbones, leaving open whether the iterative/UOT advantages persist at full ImageNet scale or in multi-modal and non-image settings. The paper itself flags future directions in graphs, time series, and multi-modal alignment, and notes that pure uniformity maximization risks shortcut features that GCA-UOT partially mitigates.
