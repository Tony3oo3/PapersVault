## Problem

Existing text-conditioned motion diffusion models are designed for single characters and cannot generate multi-person interactions, while prior multi-person interaction methods (e.g., InterGen) require training on multi-person datasets with a fixed number of characters and cannot generalize to arbitrary group sizes. A core technical obstacle is that mainstream motion models use relative representations where global joint positions are only recoverable by integrating velocities, making precise spatial control of individual joints infeasible with standard inpainting or root-only trajectory approaches.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt per person, joint-joint contact pairs specifying which joints of which individuals should be in contact or separated at specific frame ranges with a target distance, and a spatial control signal c ∈ R^{N×J×3} in global space. It outputs multi-person 3D motion sequences in the relative representation (N×D, e.g., 263-dim for HumanML3D) that can be converted to global coordinates via forward kinematics. The contact pairs can be automatically generated by GPT-4 from a multi-person interaction description, and the resulting motions can serve as reference trajectories for physics-based character simulators.

## Method

InterControl builds on a frozen MDM diffusion model and adds two complementary modules: (1) a Motion ControlNet, a trainable copy of MDM connected via zero-initialized linear layers (following the ControlNet paradigm), which ingests enriched spatial conditions including relative joint-to-target distances, root-to-target distances, and body orientation normals to produce coherent motions adapted to sparse global constraints; and (2) an IK Guidance module that, at each denoising step, runs L-BFGS optimization on the posterior mean μ_t to precisely align global joint positions with desired spatial targets, supporting both attraction (contact) and repulsion (avoidance) via ReLU-based distance losses. For multi-person interactions, the L-BFGS optimizer jointly minimizes a multi-person loss over both individuals' posterior means within a single optimization loop, enabling simultaneous control of any joint of any person at any time. The entire system is trained only on single-person data (HumanML3D) with random joint/frame masks, and zero-shot generalizes to arbitrary group sizes by treating each person's motion independently while coupling them through shared spatial conditions.

## Results

On HumanML3D, InterControl achieves the best FID (0.159) among all spatially controllable methods for root joint control, with trajectory error of 0.0132 (50 cm threshold) and location error of 0.0004, substantially outperforming GMD (0.0987/0.0356) and OmniControl (0.0387/0.0096). In zero-shot two-person interaction generation, InterControl reduces average spatial error to 0.0084 m compared to PriorMDM's 0.6723 m, and a user study with 134 participants shows 81.2% preference for InterControl over PriorMDM. For random single-joint control, InterControl achieves FID 0.178 versus OmniControl's 0.310, demonstrating robustness to joint selection.

## Baselines & Comparisons

InterControl outperforms all spatially controllable baselines on FID: 0.159 vs. OmniControl (0.218), GMD (0.276), and PriorMDM (0.498) for root control. On spatial accuracy metrics, InterControl achieves trajectory error of 0.0132 and location error of 0.0004, roughly 3× better than OmniControl (0.0387/0.0096) and 7× better than GMD (0.0987/0.0356). OmniControl slightly edges InterControl on R-precision (0.687 vs. 0.671) and foot-skating ratio (0.0547 vs. 0.0729) for root control, and on average error for random-one-joint (0.0404 vs. 0.0741 m), but InterControl is more robust across joint variants (FID gap of 0.021 between root and random-3 vs. OmniControl's 0.092 gap between root and random-1). In the zero-shot interaction setting, InterControl reduces average spatial error by ~80× compared to PriorMDM (0.0084 m vs. 0.6723 m) and wins the user study 81.2% to 18.8%. Methods without spatial control capability (T2M-GPT, MotionGPT, MLD, PhysDiff) are not directly comparable for interaction generation despite some achieving lower FID on pure text-to-motion tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on MDM (a text-conditioned motion diffusion model) and adapts the ControlNet architecture from image generation to the motion domain, creating a Motion ControlNet that processes global spatial conditions while keeping the original MDM frozen. It departs from prior controllable motion methods (PriorMDM's inpainting, GMD's root-only trajectory control) by combining the ControlNet with an inverse-kinematics guidance step using L-BFGS, achieving precise per-joint spatial alignment that neither approach could accomplish. The concurrent work OmniControl shares the ControlNet + guidance design but targets single-person control; InterControl extends this to multi-person interaction generation by repurposing joint-joint distance constraints as the interaction specification, with GPT-4 serving as an off-the-shelf planner to convert natural-language interaction descriptions into structured contact pairs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ability to generate multi-person interactions from a model trained exclusively on single-person data removes a significant data bottleneck in the field, since multi-person motion capture datasets are scarce and typically lack text annotations. The zero-shot generalization to arbitrary group sizes is practically important for animation, games, and VR pipelines where fixed-size interaction models are infeasible. Key limitations remain: the interaction vocabulary is restricted to spatial relations (distances and orientations), motion quality is bounded by the single-person base model, and the plausibility of generated interactions depends entirely on the LLM's ability to produce sensible contact plans. Open questions include whether this framework can handle more complex interaction semantics (e.g., force-based contacts, object-mediated interactions) and whether the LLM planner's reliability scales to longer or more intricate multi-agent scenarios.
