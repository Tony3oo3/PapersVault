## Problem

Existing PTM-based continual learning methods face a dual limitation: directly applying parameter-efficient tuning in the first session overwrites the general knowledge inherent in pre-trained models, while freezing all parameters in subsequent sessions prevents the model from absorbing novel concepts not seen initially. No prior replay-free framework simultaneously preserves PTM generalizability and maintains plasticity for incremental classes with constant computational cost.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243611/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images processed through a frozen ViT-B/16 backbone augmented with lightweight PET modules (Adapter, SSF, or VPT), operating in a replay-free class-incremental setting where only the current session's data is accessible. At inference, predictions are produced via random projections of features combined with second-order statistics (Gram matrix) and imprinted classification weights, yielding logits over all classes seen so far. The framework also handles domain-incremental settings where data distributions shift significantly across sessions while class labels remain fixed.

## Method

SAFE introduces a complementary slow-fast architecture inspired by complementary learning systems theory. The slow learner is trained in the first session using a cross-correlation matrix between PTM and PET-applied features, maximizing diagonal elements (alignment with PTM statistics) and minimizing off-diagonal elements (reducing redundancy), then frozen with imprinted weights for subsequent sessions. The fast learner, updated in each incremental session, is guided by the slow learner through a cosine feature-alignment loss on a hypersphere and a bidirectional cross-classification loss that feeds fast features into slow classification weights and vice versa, preventing semantic drift without storing exemplars. At inference, an entropy-based aggregation strategy dynamically weights the two learners' logits, assigning higher confidence to whichever learner is more certain for a given sample, thereby exploiting the slow learner's stability on old classes and the fast learner's adaptability on recent ones.

## Results

SAFE achieves the best final accuracy across all six class-incremental benchmarks, with an average of 84.6% over CIFAR-100, ImageNet-R, ImageNet-A, CUB-200, Omnibenchmark, and VTAB. The most notable gain is on ImageNet-A, where SAFE reaches 66.6%, surpassing the second-best replay-free method RanPAC (61.8%) by 4.4%. SAFE also outperforms replay-based methods SLCA (79.2% avg) and SSIAT without storing any data distributions, and improves the average accuracy over the previous best replay-free approach by 2.1%. On the domain-incremental DomainNet benchmark, SAFE achieves 67.82% final accuracy, outperforming RanPAC (66.6%) by 1.2%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SAFE consistently outperforms all nine baselines across every dataset. Against the strongest replay-free baseline RanPAC [23], SAFE gains +0.6 on CIFAR-100, +2.9 on ImageNet-R, +4.8 on ImageNet-A, +0.8 on CUB-200, +1.0 on Omnibenchmark, and +2.4 on VTAB, for a +2.1% average improvement. Against replay-based methods SLCA [49] and SSIAT [35], which store data distributions for rehearsal, SAFE still achieves higher final accuracy on all six datasets (e.g., 66.6% vs. 59.8% on IN-A vs. SLCA) while being entirely replay-free. Prompt-based methods L2P [42], DualPrompt [41], and CODAPrompt [33] trail SAFE by 8–24 points on ImageNet-A, highlighting the limitation of prompt-only adaptation for hard OOD classes. ADaM [52] and EASE [54] are the closest replay-free competitors but remain 4–12 points behind SAFE on ImageNet-A and 1.4–5.5 points on average. On DomainNet (domain-incremental), SAFE (67.82%) beats RanPAC (66.6%) by 1.2% and ADaM (50.3%) by 17.5%, confirming robustness under significant distribution shift.

## Relation to Prior Work

SAFE is explicitly built upon RanPAC [23], reusing its random-projection inference mechanism (Eqs. 9–10) and PET-based training protocol, while introducing a slow-fast dual-learner architecture inspired by complementary learning systems theory [19]. It departs from the prevailing PTM-based CL paradigm (L2P, DualPrompt, ADaM, RanPAC) that applies PET only in the first session and then freezes all parameters, arguing that this both overwrites PTM knowledge and eliminates plasticity for later sessions. The key conceptual departure is treating the first-session adaptation as a knowledge-transfer problem (via a cross-correlation matrix between PTM and PET-applied features) rather than a simple fine-tuning step, and introducing a continuously updated fast learner guided by the frozen slow learner in subsequent sessions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SAFE offers a clean, replay-free, constant-complexity framework that simultaneously addresses the two main failure modes of PTM-based CL—loss of generalizability and lack of plasticity—without requiring stored exemplars, expanding prompt pools, or linearly growing model complexity. The 4.4% gain on ImageNet-A and consistent improvements across seven benchmarks make it a strong practical baseline for PTM-based continual learning. However, the method inherits RanPAC's dependence on a strong pre-trained backbone, introduces three balancing hyper-parameters whose optimal values may shift across domains, and keeps the slow learner permanently frozen after session one; the authors note that periodic slow-learner updates could be a promising but unexplored direction.
