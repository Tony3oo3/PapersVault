## Problem

Directly fine-tuning CLIP on fashion data causes the image encoder to attend to visually dominant regions (e.g., dark areas of clothing) while ignoring small but critical details like logos, composition, and brand that are essential for distinguishing visually similar fashion items. Existing fashion VLMs address this with complex BERT-based architectures and multiple fashion-specific objectives, sacrificing CLIP's simplicity and ease of extension to downstream tasks. The paper asks whether CLIP's minimal two-encoder architecture and single contrastive objective can be preserved while explicitly learning fine-grained, entity-aware visual representations.

## Input / Output

The model takes a fashion product image (split into 144 ViT-B-32 patches) and a text string that concatenates the product description with tag entities (Brand, Sub-category, Season, Composition) as a single input to the unchanged CLIP text encoder. It produces one global image embedding and one global text embedding for standard contrastive retrieval, plus C×S=8 selection tokens that are average-pooled per entity into 4 entity-specific embeddings used for the region contrastive loss. Unlike EI-CLIP, tag entities are not separately encoded at inference, making the model practical when entities are embedded within free-text descriptions.

## Method

E² inserts 8 randomly initialized selection tokens (2 per tag entity) and lightweight fusion blocks into the CLIP ViT-B-32 image encoder, adding only 1.9% parameters. In each fusion block, selection tokens act as queries in a hard-attention mechanism (Gumbel-softmax with straight-through estimator) to explicitly select one most relevant image patch token and update themselves via residual averaging, iteratively accumulating entity-specific visual information across multiple stages. After the final fusion stage, all image patch tokens are dropped so that only the global token and selection tokens enter the last transformer layer, forcing the global embedding to concentrate on entity-relevant details. Training uses two contrastive losses: the standard global image-text contrastive loss and a region contrastive loss that aligns each entity's pooled selection token embedding with the corresponding tag entity's text embedding, making selection tokens learn to attend to brand logos, fabric textures, seasonal cues, and category-defining features.

## Results

On FashionGen (full evaluation), E² achieves SumR=497.9 and MeanR@1=63.7, outperforming the strongest baseline FILIP-FT (SumR=470.2, MeanR@1=55.7) by 27.7 SumR points and 8.0 MeanR@1 points. On the larger and more challenging AmazonFashion dataset (1.3M pairs), E² achieves SumR=112.2 versus CLIP-FT's 99.5 and FILIP-FT's 100.0. For zero-shot image captioning, replacing CLIP with E² in DeCap improves BLEU@4 from 16.88 to 21.35, and in CapDec from 14.23 to 19.13.

## Baselines & Comparisons

E² outperforms all 18 compared baselines across every evaluation setting. On FashionGen full evaluation, E² (SumR=497.9) surpasses the closest competitors FILIP-FT (470.2) and CLIP-FT (468.2) by 27.7 and 29.7 SumR points respectively, with the largest gap over BERT-based models like FashionViL (406.5) and FashionSAP (451.8). On the more challenging AmazonFashion dataset, E² (SumR=112.2) leads CLIP-FT (99.5) and FILIP-FT (100.0) by 12.7 and 12.2 points. For zero-shot captioning, E²-enhanced DeCap (B@4=21.35) and CapDec (B@4=19.13) improve over their CLIP-based counterparts by 4.47 and 4.90 BLEU@4 points. On Fashion-IQ zero-shot text-guided retrieval, E²-enhanced CIReVL (avg R@10=31.73) beats CIReVL (28.29), SEARLE (22.89), and PALAVRA (19.76). Against EI-CLIP, which improves the text encoder rather than the image encoder, E² (SumR=497.9) still leads by 9.0 points under the same tag-entity-concatenation setting (Table 9).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on CLIP's two-encoder contrastive architecture, departing from the dominant BERT-based fashion VLMs (FashionSAP, FAME-ViL, FashionViL) that rely on multi-task objectives (MLM, ITM, ITC, MIM) and complex architectures. Among CLIP-based fashion adaptations, it differs from FashionCLIP (direct fine-tuning) and EI-CLIP (entity-aware text encoders) by targeting the visual encoder specifically: it inserts lightweight selection tokens and fusion blocks into the ViT layers and supervises them with a region contrastive loss aligned to tag entities. The Gumbel-softmax hard-attention mechanism for token selection draws from discrete representation learning, but its application to fashion-specific entity grounding within a frozen-structure ViT is novel.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a minimal architectural intervention (1.9% additional parameters, contrastive loss only) can close the domain gap between general-purpose CLIP and fashion-specific retrieval, offering a practical recipe for domain-adapting CLIP without multi-task complexity. The new AmazonFashion dataset (1.3M pairs, 38K brands) is a significant practical contribution, being 44× larger than FashionGen with more realistic short descriptions. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond fashion to other fine-grained domains (e.g., medical imaging, industrial inspection), and whether the dependence on structured tag entities limits applicability to unstructured product descriptions.
