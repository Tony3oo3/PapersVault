## Problem

Transformer-based LLMs suffer severe performance degradation when inputs exceed their trained context window, yet existing extension methods (RoPE base modification, ALiBi, NoPE temperature scaling) operate on positional encodings or attention scores without explaining the underlying mechanism. This paper identifies the missing link: how positional information is implicitly encoded in hidden states, how it forms across layers, and why out-of-distribution positional vectors beyond the context window cause the collapse of attention sinks and long-term decay.

## Input / Output

The analysis pipeline takes token sequences (up to 8192 tokens) and pre-computed positional vectors obtained by averaging hidden states over 32K RedPajama samples via mean-based decomposition. The positional vector replacement method consumes the hidden state at a single early layer (4th for TL-NoPE) and the pre-computed positional vectors, producing a modified hidden state with interpolated positional components. The attention window extension method takes standard query/key/value tensors and produces attention scores with a scaled window size and a temperature-adjusted logit, requiring no modification to model weights.

## Method

The paper first disentangles each hidden state into a positional vector and a semantic vector via mean-based decomposition, revealing that after the first layer only initial tokens (≤4) form distinct positional vectors that subsequently serve as anchors shaping positional information in all later tokens through attention. Beyond the context window, these positional vectors become out-of-distribution, disrupting attention sinks and long-term decay and corrupting output logits. Based on this, two training-free extension methods are proposed: (1) Positional Vector Replacement, which linearly interpolates the positional vectors within the context window by a ratio r, scales them by a factor α, and substitutes them into the hidden state at a single critical early layer (keeping the first four tokens unchanged) for NoPE models; and (2) Attention Window Extension, which scales the window size by r and multiplies attention logits by a temperature factor λ for window-attention NoPE models, effectively controlling how positional information propagates across windows and layers.

## Results

On PG-19 language modeling (context window C=2048), without any interpolation the PPL explodes beyond 2K (e.g., >10³ for TL-NoPE and TL-RoPE). The proposed Positional Vector Replacement (r=2, α=1.1) achieves PPL of 13.54 at 2K and 15.58 at 4K on TL-NoPE, outperforming the Attention Scaling baseline (17.03/17.05) at 4K. For window-attention models, Attention Window Extension (r=4, λ=1.2) reduces TL-Window PPL from 660.51 to 29.30 at 8K tokens, a >20× improvement over the unmodified model. At 8K, Positional Vector Replacement (r=5, α=1.3) yields PPL 73.79, slightly worse than Attention Scaling (λ=1.3) at 46.59, which the authors attribute to decreasing effective interpolation ratio across layers.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two baselines: Dynamic NTK [11] for RoPE-based models and Attention Scaling [20] for NoPE models. On TL-NoPE, Positional Vector Replacement (r=2, α=1.1) beats Attention Scaling (λ=1.2) at 4K (PPL 15.58 vs. 17.05), and the r=5 variant matches Attention Scaling (λ=1.3) at 6K (49.79 vs. 51.50) but underperforms at 8K (73.79 vs. 46.59). For TL-Window, Attention Window Extension (r=4, λ=1.2) achieves PPL 29.30 at 8K, a dramatic improvement over the unmodified model (660.51) and substantially better than Attention Scaling's >10³ failure at 8K on the full-attention TL-NoPE variant. Dynamic NTK remains the strongest baseline for TL-RoPE, achieving PPL 28.58 at 8K, but the paper does not propose a RoPE-specific method. Overall, the proposed methods are competitive with or slightly better than baselines at moderate extension factors (2×–3×) but show degradation at 4× extension, which the authors attribute to imperfect positional vector interpolation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the mean-based decomposition framework of Song & Zhong [21] to disentangle positional vectors from hidden states, and extends the implicit positional information findings of Haviv et al. [19] by tracing how positional vectors form across layers and positions. It departs from prior context window extension work (NTK scaling, positional interpolation, ALiBi) that operates on explicit positional encodings or attention scores, instead providing a mechanistic interpretation through the lens of positional vectors in hidden states. The attention window extension method adapts the attention scaling approach of Wang et al. [20] to the window attention setting, while the positional vector replacement method is a novel direct manipulation of decomposed positional vectors in NoPE models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a unifying interpretability framework showing that OOD positional vectors are the root cause of performance degradation beyond the context window, and that both length extrapolation and context window extension work by preserving or interpolating positional vector consistency. However, the practical impact is limited: the proposed training-free methods only apply to NoPE and window attention architectures, not the dominant RoPE + full attention paradigm used by most production LLMs. The findings are validated primarily on 1.1B TinyLlama models, and the authors acknowledge that broader validation on larger, mainstream models remains open. A key open question is whether the 'anchor token' mechanism and positional vector interpolation dynamics observed at small scale persist in larger models with more layers and parameters.
