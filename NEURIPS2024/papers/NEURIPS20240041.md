## Problem

Generating multi-view consistent 360° panoramic images from text descriptions and camera poses remains unsolved: existing single-view panorama methods cannot support scene exploration, perspective multi-view methods lack true 360° coverage, and directly extending perspective epipolar attention to equirectangular projection fails due to fundamentally different camera geometry. Additionally, no large-scale panoramic video-text dataset with camera poses existed to train such a system.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240041/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes free-form text descriptions and a set of N camera pose matrices (rotation + translation) as input, and simultaneously produces N equirectangular projection (ERP) panoramas at 256×512 resolution (multi-view) or 512×1024 (single-view). Unlike prior single-view panorama generators that only support 3DoF rotation, the multi-view output enables 6DoF roaming and can directly feed 360° Gaussian Splatting or 360° NeRF pipelines for novel view synthesis.

## Method

DiffPano is a two-stage framework built on Stable Diffusion. First, a single-view text-to-panorama model is obtained by LoRA fine-tuning the frozen SD UNet on a custom panoramic video-text dataset (rendered from HM3D via Habitat Simulator, captioned with BLIP-2 + Llama-2), with data augmentation that concatenates the right edge to the left edge to enforce ERP left-right continuity. Second, the single-view model is extended to multi-view generation via a spherical epipolar-aware attention module: the authors derive the epipolar line equation in ERP space (Eq. 14), and for each pixel in a target view, uniformly sample S points along the camera ray, reproject them onto K nearest reference views, and perform cross-attention with Plücker-coordinate and harmonic-depth positional encodings. Training is split into two stages—first with minimal camera displacement to strengthen the epipolar constraint, then with larger displacements to improve text conditioning and scalability—allowing the model to generate consistent panoramas across arbitrary unseen camera trajectories.

## Results

For single-view panorama generation, Pano-SD achieves a CLIP Score of 28.98 (best among all methods), an FID of 48.52 (comparable to PanFusion's 47.62 and far better than Text2Light's 76.50), and an inference time of 5.1 s — roughly 5× faster than PanFusion (27.6 s) and 10× faster than Text2Light (50.4 s). For multi-view panorama generation, a user study with ~50 raters scores DiffPano highest on all three criteria: image quality (4.0), image-text consistency (4.2), and multi-view consistency (4.3), outperforming both MVDream and PanFusion.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Text2Light [9], Pano-SD substantially reduces FID (48.52 vs. 76.50) and improves CLIP Score (28.98 vs. 27.48) while being ~10× faster at inference. Against PanFusion [70], Pano-SD trades a slightly higher FID (48.52 vs. 47.62) and lower IS (3.30 vs. 4.49) for a better CLIP Score (28.98 vs. 28.73) and a 5.4× speedup (5.1 s vs. 27.6 s), making it far more practical as a backbone for multi-view generation. For the multi-view task, DiffPano outperforms the adapted MVDream [43] baseline on all three user-study metrics (4.0/4.2/4.3 vs. 3.6/3.7/3.8), with the largest gap in multi-view consistency (+0.5). DiffPano also beats PanFusion [70] on multi-view consistency (4.3 vs. 3.0), though PanFusion edges it slightly on image-text consistency (4.0 vs. 4.2 is actually DiffPano winning). In the perspective-image comparison (Table 5), Pano-SD achieves the lowest FID (164.75) among the three methods tested, confirming that the LoRA-finetuned panorama model retains strong perspective-image quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Stable Diffusion + LoRA fine-tuning paradigm (used in single-view panorama generation by PanFusion and Text2Light) to the multi-view setting, and adapts the epipolar attention mechanism from EpiDiff/LFN (designed for perspective images) to the equirectangular projection domain by deriving a spherical epipolar line formula. It departs from multi-view perspective generation methods like MVDream, which rely on 3D self-attention over flattened feature maps and rotation-only camera embeddings, by instead using ray-based cross-attention with full 6DoF pose conditioning. The conceptual core is the geometric translation of epipolar geometry from the planar (perspective) to the spherical (panoramic) case, combined with a two-stage curriculum that first enforces consistency under small camera displacements before introducing content-changing translations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first formulation of text-to-multi-view panorama generation, opening a path toward 6DoF immersive scene exploration from text alone, with direct applications to VR roaming, interior design preview, and 360° Gaussian Splatting/NeRF inputs. The large-scale panoramic video-text dataset (millions of keyframes with poses and captions) is a practical resource that was previously absent. Key limitations include hallucination as the number of generated frames grows, evaluation restricted to indoor HM3D scenes, and the absence of a true multi-view panorama baseline (the MVDream adaptation is a proxy). Future work on video diffusion models for longer panoramic sequences and outdoor scenes would strengthen the contribution.
