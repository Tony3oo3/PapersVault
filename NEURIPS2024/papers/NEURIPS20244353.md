## Problem

Existing LLM-based trading agents (FINGPT, FINMEM, FINAGENT) rely on short-term sentiment-driven risk preferences that fail to control long-term risk exposure, are limited to single-asset trading, and overload a single agent's context window with multi-source information. Multi-agent alternatives like STOCKAGENT incur high peer-to-peer communication costs and lack a clear optimization objective. The paper addresses how to synthesize multi-source financial information through structured agent collaboration while enabling timely, experience-based refinement of investment beliefs across training episodes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20244353/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system consumes five modalities of financial data—daily stock prices (tabular), daily news (text), SEC 10-K/10-Q filings (text), earnings call audio (processed via Whisper API), and Zacks equity research (text)—each routed to a dedicated analyst agent based on information timeliness (annual filings persist longest, daily news shortest). For single-stock trading, the output is a discrete buy/sell/hold decision (where 'sell' permits short positions) plus reasoning text; for portfolio management, it additionally produces daily-rebalanced portfolio weights solved via a mean-variance convex optimization constrained by the directional decisions. The system also emits conceptualized investment beliefs that are selectively back-propagated from the manager to relevant analysts rather than broadcast to all agents.

## Method

FINCON organizes seven specialized analyst agents (three textual, one audio, one data-analysis, one stock-selection) under a single manager agent in a synthesized hierarchy that mimics an investment firm, reducing redundant peer-to-peer communication. A dual-level risk-control component governs learning: within-episode, a CVaR monitor (average of the worst 1% of daily PnLs) triggers immediate risk-averse behavior when the value drops; over-episode, a Conceptual Verbal Reinforcement (CVRF) mechanism compares consecutive training episodes, distills conceptualized investment insights from winning vs. losing trajectories, and updates the manager's prompts via textual gradient descent where the 'learning rate' is the overlapping percentage of trading decisions between episodes. The entire system is formalized as an infinite-horizon POMDP whose policies are parameterized by textual prompts rather than numerical weights, and the manager's episodic memory stores PnL series and updated beliefs that persist across episodes. Only four training episodes are needed for convergence, and belief updates are selectively propagated to relevant analysts rather than all agents, minimizing communication overhead.

## Results

In single-stock trading across 8 assets, FINCON achieves the highest Cumulative Return (CR) and Sharpe Ratio (SR) in most cases, e.g., 82.87% CR / 1.97 SR on TSLA, 69.24% CR / 2.37 SR on NFLX, and 57.05% CR / 0.83 SR on COIN (a recently IPO'd stock where DRL methods fail to converge). In portfolio management, FINCON achieves 113.84% CR and 3.27 SR on Portfolio 1 (TSLA, MSFT, PFE) and 32.92% CR and 1.37 SR on Portfolio 2 (AMZN, GM, LLY), with MDD of 16.16% and 21.50% respectively. Ablation studies confirm that removing the within-episode CVaR risk control drops GOOG CR from 25.08% to −1.46%, and removing over-episode belief updates drops Portfolio 1 CR from 113.84% to 28.43%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FINCON dominates all 10 baselines on both tasks. In single-stock trading, it outperforms the best LLM-based agent (FINMEM: 34.62% CR on TSLA) by ~48 percentage points and the best DRL agent (DQN: 27.02% CR on MSFT) by ~4.6 points, while also achieving lower MDD in most cases. DRL agents (A2C, PPO, DQN) struggle severely on recently IPO'd stocks like COIN (no results reported) and NIO (A2C: −91.91% CR), whereas FINCON handles them robustly. In portfolio management, FINCON's 113.84% CR on Portfolio 1 exceeds Markowitz MV (12.64%) by ~101 points and FINRL-A2C (19.46%) by ~94 points; on Portfolio 2 it beats the Equal-Weighted ETF (15.06%) by ~18 points. The only metric where a baseline matches or edges FINCON is MDD on Portfolio 2, where FINRL-A2C (15.79%) and Equal-Weighted ETF (14.66%) show slightly lower drawdown than FINCON (21.50%), though FINCON still leads decisively on CR and SR.

## Relation to Prior Work

FINCON synthesizes the hierarchical multi-agent design from generative agents (Park et al.) and cognitive architectures for language agents (Sumers et al.) with the verbal reinforcement paradigm from Reflexion (Shinn et al.) and textual gradient descent (Tang et al.), but departs from prior financial LLM agents (FINGPT, FINMEM, FINAGENT) in two key ways: it replaces peer-to-peer agent discussion with a streamlined manager-analyst hierarchy to reduce communication overhead, and it introduces a dual-level risk-control mechanism (within-episode CVaR monitoring plus over-episode conceptual verbal reinforcement) that prior systems lacked. The over-episode learning rate is derived from the overlapping percentage of trading decisions between consecutive episodes rather than prompt editing distance, and the framework extends the LLM trading task from single-asset to multi-asset portfolio management via mean-variance optimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FINCON is the first LLM multi-agent system to address both single-stock trading and portfolio management, filling a gap where prior financial LLM agents were limited to single-asset tasks. The dual-level risk control is practically significant: the ablation shows that removing within-episode CVaR monitoring causes catastrophic performance degradation (e.g., NIO CR drops from +17.5% to −52.9%), suggesting that real-time risk awareness is essential for LLM-based trading in volatile markets. However, the evaluation is limited to 8 stocks and two 3-stock portfolios with a single backbone model (GPT-4-Turbo), and the portfolio management baselines (Markowitz MV, FINRL-A2C, Equal-Weighted ETF) are relatively weak since no competing LLM-based portfolio agents exist. The authors acknowledge hallucination issues in multi-asset settings and identify scaling to larger portfolios (tens of assets) as a critical open challenge given LLM context-length constraints.
