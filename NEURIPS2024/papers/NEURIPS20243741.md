## Problem

Standard conformal prediction guarantees valid coverage only under i.i.d. exchangeability, which is violated when training data is corrupted (e.g., missing features, noisy labels) while test data is clean. Weighted conformal prediction (WCP) handles the resulting covariate shift but requires the test-time privileged information Z^test to compute likelihood-ratio weights, which is unavailable in the target setting. This paper asks: can one construct provably valid prediction sets under corruption-induced distribution shift without access to Z^test at inference time?

## Input / Output

The method consumes corrupted training samples (X_i^obs, Y_i^obs, Z_i, M_i) where Z_i (privileged information) and M_i (corruption indicator) are training-only, a pre-trained base model f̂, and a clean test feature X^test. It outputs a single prediction set C^PCP(X^test) ⊆ Y with a guaranteed marginal coverage of at least 1−α. A key structural detail is that the calibration threshold Q^PCP is a fixed scalar computed entirely from calibration data, independent of the unknown Z^test, unlike WCP whose threshold is a function of Z^test. For scarce data, the LOO-PCP variant avoids data splitting by using leave-one-out refits, at the cost of a 2α coverage guarantee.

## Method

PCP reformulates WCP by eliminating the dependence on Z^test through a two-level quantile construction. First, each calibration point i is treated as a pseudo-test point, and a WCP threshold Q_i is computed using the remaining uncorrupted calibration scores with normalized weights p_j^i that incorporate w_i as the 'test' weight. Second, the final threshold Q^PCP is the (1−β)-th empirical quantile of {Q_i} ∪ {∞}. The key theoretical insight (Lemma 1) is that the WCP quantile Q(ω) is non-decreasing in the test weight ω, so Q^PCP can be rewritten as Q(w̃_{n+1}) where w̃_{n+1} is the (1−β)-quantile of the calibration weights. Since the weights {w_i} are exchangeable, P(w_{n+1} ≤ w̃_{n+1}) ≥ 1−β, and combining this with the WCP coverage guarantee at level 1−α+β yields the final 1−α coverage via a union bound. The LOO-PCP variant extends this to the jackknife+ framework, using leave-one-out models and the JAW weighted construction to avoid data splitting while maintaining a 1−2α guarantee.

## Results

Across three experimental settings (causal inference on IHDP, missing response on six tabular datasets, and noisy labels on CIFAR-10N), PCP consistently achieves the target 90% marginal coverage rate while producing prediction sets of comparable size to the infeasible oracle WCP that has access to the unknown test privileged information Z^test. Naive conformal prediction (Naive CP) and naive weighted conformal prediction (Naive WCP) both undercover, falling below the 90% target, while the Two-Staged baseline is overly conservative, reaching approximately 95% coverage. On CIFAR-10N (17.23% noisy labels), PCP achieves coverage slightly above 90% with set sizes matching the infeasible WCP, demonstrating robustness to estimated (rather than true) corruption weights.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PCP outperforms Naive CP (Vovk et al., 2005) and Naive WCP/JAW (Tibshirani et al., 2019; Prinster et al., 2022) on all three experimental settings: both naive baselines fail to achieve the 90% coverage target because they do not correctly account for the distribution shift induced by corruption. PCP matches the Infeasible WCP (oracle JAW with access to Z^test) in both coverage rate and prediction set size, confirming that the proposed method loses negligible statistical efficiency by not requiring the test-time privileged information. The Two-Staged baseline (a conformal prediction approach that first predicts Z^test then applies WCP) is valid but overly conservative, producing intervals roughly 5 percentage points wider than PCP (achieving ~95% vs. 90% coverage). For the LOO-PCP variant on the small IHDP dataset (n=747), the method achieves the 1−2α coverage guarantee (80% target) with interval widths comparable to the infeasible JAW, while naive jackknife+ undercovers.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the weighted conformal prediction (WCP) framework of Tibshirani et al. (2019) to a setting where the covariate shift is mediated by privileged information (PI) that is unavailable at test time. While WCP requires the test-time PI to compute likelihood-ratio weights, PCP replaces this with a conservative quantile over calibration thresholds, eliminating the need for Z^test. The approach also builds on the jackknife+ and JAW methods for scarce-data settings, and connects to the privileged information learning literature (Vapnik & Vashist, 2009), though in a fundamentally different direction: prior PI work leverages PI to improve prediction accuracy, whereas PCP uses PI solely to ensure statistical validity of prediction sets under corrupted training data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first calibration scheme with a provable coverage guarantee for the practically important setting where training data is corrupted (noisy labels, missing features/responses) and the corruption mechanism is explained by PI that vanishes at inference. The conditional independence assumption (X(0),Y(0)) ⊥ M | Z relaxes the strong ignorability assumption from causal inference by allowing confounders to be absent at test time, broadening applicability. Key open questions include: (i) the theoretical effect of estimated (rather than true) corruption probabilities on coverage, and (ii) whether the conditional independence assumption can be further relaxed beyond the ε-bounded density difference result in Appendix A.5. The method is most impactful for high-stakes domains (medical diagnosis, causal inference) where data corruption is systematic and PI is naturally available during data collection but not at deployment.
