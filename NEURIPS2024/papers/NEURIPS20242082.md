## Problem

Existing inference procedures for high-dimensional single index models (SIMs) rely on sub-Gaussian assumptions on the error term, causing severe efficiency loss under heavy-tailed distributions or outliers. Moreover, prior group inference methods for SIMs do not establish asymptotic honesty, meaning their validity depends on the zero and nonzero coefficients being well-separated—a condition rarely met in practice. This paper addresses both gaps simultaneously by developing robust, honest group and simultaneous inference without any moment condition on the error.

## Input / Output

The method takes a sample of n observations with p-dimensional covariates X and a scalar response Y, where p can grow exponentially in n. It produces two types of outputs: (1) a group test decision for a prespecified subset G of predictors (reject or accept H_{0,G}), and (2) an FDR-controlled set of significant individual predictors across all p dimensions. A key design choice is that the response is transformed via its empirical distribution function F_n(Y_i) before any regression step, so the procedure only requires sub-Gaussian covariates and imposes no moment or tail conditions on the error or response.

## Method

Under the linearity condition on predictors, the SIM is recast into a pseudo-linear model h(Y) = β_h^T X + e by applying the response-distribution transformation h(Y) = F(Y), which guarantees the transformed error is bounded regardless of the original error's tail. An orthogonalization-based test statistic T̃_{nj} is then constructed for each coefficient, making it locally insensitive to high-dimensional nuisance parameters (γ_j, θ_j) estimated via penalized least squares with LASSO, SCAD, or MCP penalties. The group test uses the max-norm M_{n,G} = max_{j∈G} T̃_{nj}², whose null distribution converges to a Gumbel extreme-value law, yielding an honest test that does not require signal separation. A Gaussian multiplier bootstrap based on symmetrized U-statistic kernels provides an alternative critical value with faster finite-sample accuracy. For simultaneous inference, a BH-type threshold procedure controls FDR asymptotically under moderate dimensionality (p = O(n^c)).

## Results

Under the nonlinear model Y=exp(X^T β+ε) with normal errors and no outliers, the proposed method achieves empirical power 1.000 for both small group G₂ and large group G₄, compared to 0.785 and 0.430 for the ST baseline. Under Cauchy errors (t(1)) or with 10% outliers, ST's power collapses to 0.040–0.080 while the proposed method maintains power 1.000 across all group sizes and model types. For FDR control at level 0.2, the proposed procedure achieves FDR 0.19–0.21 with power 1.000 in all settings, whereas BH-Vₙ and BH-Wₙ lose power to 0.011–0.066 under Cauchy errors.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ST (Zhang & Cheng, JASA 2017), the proposed method wins decisively on power under all challenging conditions: under the nonlinear model with normal errors, ST achieves only 0.785 (G₂) and 0.430 (G₄) power versus 1.000 for the proposed method; with outliers or Cauchy errors, ST's power drops to 0.040–0.115 (essentially the nominal level) while the proposed method retains power 1.000. Both methods control type I error near the nominal 0.05 level under clean data. Against BH-Vₙ (Eftekhari et al., JMLR 2021, debiased LASSO), the proposed method dominates on both FDR and power: under Cauchy errors with sparsity 8, BH-Vₙ has FDR 0.460 and power 0.014, while the proposed method achieves FDR 0.212 and power 1.000. Against BH-Wₙ (Ning & Liu, Annals of Statistics 2017, decorrelated score), the proposed method achieves substantially higher power under heavy-tailed errors (1.000 vs. 0.022–0.066) while both control FDR below 0.2, though BH-Wₙ is more conservative (FDR 0.087–0.142). On the riboflavin real dataset, the proposed FDR procedure identifies 10 significant genes at level 0.1, overlapping with genes found by prior methods (YXLD_at, YCKE_at, YXLE_at, XHLA_at) while additionally detecting 6 more.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the rank-LASSO framework of Rejchel and Bogdan [43] to accommodate both convex and non-convex penalties (SCAD, MCP) with error bounds for local optima, and embeds it within the orthogonalization-based inference paradigm of Ning and Liu [40]. The key conceptual departure is the use of the empirical CDF as a response transformation, which leverages the linearity condition result of Li and Duan [32] to recast the general single index model into a pseudo-linear model, thereby avoiding the sub-Gaussian error assumption that underpins existing SIM inference methods (Eftekhari et al. [17], Wu et al. [53]). Group inference for high-dimensional linear models was established by Zhang and Cheng [59], Dezeure et al. [16], and Guo et al. [23], but none addressed the SIM setting or provided asymptotic honesty; this paper fills that gap while adding robustness to heavy-tailed errors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for applications where responses exhibit outliers or heavy-tailed distributions (common in genomics, finance, and clinical data), as existing high-dimensional SIM inference procedures lose efficiency or fail entirely under such conditions. The paper provides a clean theoretical recipe—distribution transformation plus orthogonalization—that requires no moment conditions on the error and no tuning of a robustification parameter, making it straightforward to implement. Open questions include extending the framework beyond the linearity condition (which restricts predictor distributions), handling even link functions where κ_h = 0 (sparse phase retrieval), and providing quantitative robustness bounds. The FDR control procedure for simultaneous individual testing in the SIM setting is also a new contribution with direct applications to gene pathway analysis.
