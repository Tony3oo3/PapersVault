## Problem

Current WSI survival prediction methods either ignore spatial context (MIL-based approaches) or model patches as a homogeneous graph, failing to account for the fact that different tissue types (tumor, stroma, immune infiltration, necrosis) contribute unequally to prognosis. Moreover, GCN-like architectures assume node homophily, which is violated by the inherent heterogeneity of intratumoral tissue, and no prior method systematically incorporates clinical prior knowledge about which tissue categories are prognostically relevant into the graph learning process.

## Input / Output

Each WSI is segmented into non-overlapping 256×256 patches at 20× magnification, encoded by the UNI foundation model into 1024-dimensional vectors, and classified into one of five prognostic tissue categories (tumor, stroma, immune infiltration, necrosis, others) by a fine-tuned UNI classifier. A heterogeneous graph is constructed with k=4 spatial nearest-neighbor edges per node. The model outputs a single scalar survival risk score per slide (trained with Cox partial likelihood against clinical survival time) and produces per-prototype attention heatmaps over patches for interpretability.

## Method

ProtoSurv decouples the heterogeneous graph into two complementary views: a Structure View that applies L=4 layers of GCN message passing and concatenates all intermediate layer outputs to capture multi-hop neighborhood context, and a Histology View that learns K=8 prototypes per tissue category by averaging category node features, shifting them with learnable parameters, and refining via cross-attention over all global nodes (not just same-category nodes) to capture intra-category subtypes. A Prior Guided Fusion module then uses cross-attention with the prototypes as queries and the Structure View features as keys/values to select prognostically relevant regions across multiple spatial scales, followed by average pooling to produce the slide-level representation. Training combines Cox regression loss with a compatibility loss (encouraging prototypes to align with their category's node features) and an orthogonality loss (enforcing distinctness among prototypes within the same category), with hyperparameters α=0.01 and β=0.1.

## Results

ProtoSurv achieves the best C-index on three of five TCGA cancer datasets: BRCA (0.720±0.040), LGG (0.774±0.063), and LUAD (0.658±0.046). It is second-best on PAAD (0.669±0.049, behind PANTHER's 0.673) and COAD (0.692±0.045, behind TransMIL's 0.695). Across all five datasets, ProtoSurv outperforms the strongest graph-based baselines (HEAT, HGSurvNet) and all classic MIL methods, with the largest margin on LGG where it exceeds the next-best method (PANTHER, 0.748) by 0.026 C-index.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ProtoSurv outperforms all nine baselines on BRCA, LGG, and LUAD, with the most notable margins over HGSurvNet (+0.019 on BRCA, +0.028 on LGG) and HEAT (+0.026 on LGG, +0.016 on LUAD). On COAD, TransMIL (0.695) narrowly edges out ProtoSurv (0.692), while on PAAD the unsupervised PANTHER (0.673) slightly leads ProtoSurv (0.669), likely due to PAAD's small sample size (208 cases) favoring PANTHER's minimal parameter count. Among the graph-based methods, ProtoSurv consistently surpasses both the homogeneous GCN approaches (Patch-GCN, DeepGraphConv) and the heterogeneous/hypergraph methods (HEAT, HGSurvNet) across all five datasets. The classic attention-based MIL baselines (WSISA, ABMIL, DeepAttnMISL) are uniformly outperformed by 0.05–0.15 C-index, confirming the benefit of explicit spatial graph modeling over bag-level attention aggregation.

## Relation to Prior Work

ProtoSurv extends the heterogeneous graph line of work initiated by HEAT (Chan et al., CVPR 2023) from classification/staging to survival prediction, while drawing its core architectural inspiration from ProtoGNN (Dong et al., 2022) for handling non-homophilous graphs. It departs from homogeneous GNN-based WSI methods (Patch-GCN, DeepGraphConv) by explicitly modeling tissue-type heterogeneity and incorporating pathological prior knowledge about prognostic-relevant tissue categories. The prototype learning mechanism, adapted from Snell et al.'s prototypical networks, is repurposed here to capture intra-category subtypes (e.g., stroma subtypes) rather than few-shot class discrimination, and the cross-attention fusion between Structure View and Histology View is a novel combination not present in either parent work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that explicitly encoding tissue heterogeneity with domain-informed categories consistently outperforms homogeneous graph approaches across five cancer types, validating the hypothesis that GCN-like homophily assumptions are detrimental for WSI survival prediction. A key open question is the dependency on a trained tissue classifier; while ablations show robustness to classifier quality (even K-means works), the authors acknowledge that obtaining reliable pseudo-labels without additional annotation remains a barrier to broader deployment. The multi-prototype design and its interpretability (attention heatmaps revealing which prototypes capture global vs. cross-category interactions) offer a template for integrating clinical priors into other WSI tasks beyond survival.
