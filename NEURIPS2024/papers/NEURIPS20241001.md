## Problem

When a pretrained LLM is fine-tuned into a multimodal LLM, it catastrophically forgets its text-only instruction-following abilities, with performance drops of up to 13 points on benchmarks like RACE-High. The paper identifies this forgetting as stemming from cross-layer attention shifts—excessive focus on visual tokens disrupts the attention patterns on text following the image—and shows that existing remedies (replaying large text-only datasets or switching between separate LLM/MLLM deployments) incur prohibitive computational or memory costs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241001/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts a mixed sequence of textual tokens and visual feature tokens (produced by a SigLIP or CLIP encoder plus a linear projector), where the visual tokens can be inserted at any position within the text. During inference, text-only prompts deactivate the visual learner branch entirely, while multimodal prompts activate both visual and textual learners. The output is a standard autoregressive text response, with loss computed only on textual tokens (visual token positions are masked from the next-token prediction objective).

## Method

WINGS introduces parallel Low-Rank Residual Attention (LoRRA) modules—'wings'—at every Transformer layer's attention block, with one visual learner and one textual learner operating alongside the main attention. Each LoRRA computes cross-attention between the current hidden state (as query) and the initial visual or textual features (as key/value), using low-rank factorized projections (W_a·W_b) with an identity residual (1+W) so that outputs are zero at initialization. A token-wise soft router, implemented as a single-layer MLP over the main branch's attention weights, dynamically blends the two learners' outputs per token. Training proceeds in two stages: first, only the projector and visual learners are fine-tuned on image-text pairs to align visual features while minimizing disruption to the main branch; then textual learners are added and both are co-trained with the attention-weight-based router. The paper also introduces the MLLM-LAWS metric (Pearson correlation of layer-level attention proportions on pre- vs. post-image text) to quantify attention shift, validated across 100+ MLLMs, and constructs an Interleaved Image-Text (IIT) benchmark for multi-turn mixed-modality evaluation.

## Results

WINGS (base, 7B) achieves 60.5 on MMLU and 69.8 on CMMLU, surpassing the strongest open-source 7B MLLM DeepSeek-VL (53.9/64.0) by 6.6 and 5.8 points respectively on text-only benchmarks. On multimodal tasks, WINGS scores 39.9 on MMMU-Val, 70.5 on MMBench-EN, and 45.8 on HallusionBench, leading all open-source 7B models. WINGS_pro further improves to 82.8/76.3 on RACE-M/H and 83.1 on ScienceQA, achieving state-of-the-art on 13 out of 18 evaluated datasets. The 1.8B variant (WINGS_1.8B) remains competitive with larger efficient models like MiniCPM-V_2.4B on both text-only and multimodal tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

WINGS decisively outperforms LLaVA_v1.5 (the same-architecture baseline) on nearly all text-only benchmarks: MMLU 60.5 vs 51.1 (+9.4), CMMLU 69.8 vs 38.3 (+31.5), and RACE-High 69.6 vs 53.2 (+16.4), while also leading on MMMU (39.9 vs 35.7) and HallusionBench (45.8 vs 29.7). Against DeepSeek-VL, the strongest open-source 7B competitor, WINGS wins on MMLU (+6.6), CMMLU (+5.8), RACE-M/H (+3.6/+5.8), and HallusionBench (+9.6), but DeepSeek-VL retains an edge on MMBench-EN (72.7 vs 70.5) and ScienceQA (80.6 vs 78.8) for the base model; WINGS_pro closes this gap (73.1/83.1). WINGS also surpasses Monkey on text-only (MMLU 60.5 vs 52.8, CMMLU 69.8 vs 66.9) and HallusionBench (45.8 vs 42.1), while Monkey leads slightly on MMMU-Val (40.3 vs 39.9). In the efficient 1.8–2.4B category, WINGS_1.8B outperforms DeepSeek-VL_1.3B on MMLU (44.9 vs 31.7) and CMMLU (50.9 vs 38.2), and is competitive with MiniCPM-V_2.4B on most metrics. Against private APIs (GPT-4V, Gemini pro vision), WINGS_pro narrows the gap substantially on text-only (MMLU 61.3 vs 79.3/85.9) and is within 1–2 points on HallusionBench (47.3 vs 46.5/45.2), though a clear gap remains on reasoning-heavy benchmarks like HellaSwag and GSM8K.

## Relation to Prior Work

WINGS extends the LLaVA two-stage training pipeline (visual alignment followed by instruction tuning) by inserting parallel Low-Rank Residual Attention (LoRRA) modules at every transformer layer, drawing directly on LoRA's low-rank parameterization but applying it to the full attention mechanism rather than individual linear projections. It departs from prior anti-forgetting strategies—replaying large text-only corpora (DeepSeek-VL, MM1) or switching between separate LLM/MLLM deployments—by instead diagnosing the problem through a new cross-layer attention metric (MLLM-LAWS) and compensating with lightweight modality-specific learners and a token-wise router. Conceptually, the parallel learners resemble Mixture-of-Experts at the attention level, but with at least three orders of magnitude fewer parameters, positioning WINGS as a targeted, architecture-level fix rather than a data-level or deployment-level workaround.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Text-only forgetting is a practical bottleneck for deploying MLLMs in real-world conversational settings where users interleave text-only and visual queries; WINGS offers a lightweight, plug-in solution that preserves (and sometimes improves) text-only performance without requiring large text-only replay datasets. The MLLM-LAWS diagnostic provides a new interpretability lens for understanding how attention redistribution across layers correlates with capability degradation, which could inform future MLLM training and debugging. Open questions include whether the attention-shift diagnosis generalizes to non-encoder-based or monolithic multimodal architectures, whether the approach extends to additional modalities (video, audio), and whether the residual need for some text-only replay data can be fully eliminated. The IIT benchmark also fills a gap in evaluating multi-turn mixed-modality conversations that prior single-turn benchmarks miss.
