## Problem

Controlling LLM generation to satisfy strict logical constraints at inference time remains intractable because the required marginal probability p_lm(α | x_t, x_<t) demands summing over exponentially many future token sequences. Prior tractable approaches like GeLaTo guarantee constraint satisfaction but only handle keyword-inclusion, while classifier-based methods (FUDGE, NADO) offer no guarantee and require retraining per constraint. The paper asks whether tractable, guaranteed constraint satisfaction can be generalized to arbitrary logical constraints expressible as deterministic finite automata, and whether the HMM approximation remains faithful at the scale of modern 7B+ LLMs.

## Input / Output

At inference time, Ctrl-G consumes a frozen LLM (e.g., TULU2-7B or GPT2-large), a pre-distilled HMM with up to 32,768 hidden states (~2B parameters), and a DFA encoding the desired constraint (e.g., keyphrase inclusion, word-count bounds, or ordered keyphrase sequences for text insertion). The output is a token sequence sampled autoregressively from the product of the LLM's next-token distribution and the HMM's constraint-satisfaction probability. Notably, for text insertion the suffix is never fed to the LLM; instead it is encoded entirely within the DFA, making the HMM solely responsible for steering generation toward coherence with the suffix.

## Method

Ctrl-G is a three-step neuro-symbolic pipeline: (1) distill an HMM from the LLM via EM on sampled sequences, (2) construct a DFA for the constraint using standard algorithms (KMP for single phrases, Aho-Corasick for inflection variants, and compositional operations like intersection and concatenation for compound constraints), and (3) at each autoregressive step, compute p_hmm(α | x_t, x_<t) by a forward recurrence over the joint HMM-hidden-state × DFA-state space, then sample the next token from the product of LLM and HMM probabilities. The key theoretical contribution is showing that this marginalization is tractable in O(nmh²) time (n = sequence length, m = DFA edges, h = HMM states) by exploiting the Markov properties of both the HMM and the DFA, with a one-time O(mh|Σ|) precomputation of edge-emission sums. This generalizes GeLaTo from keyword-inclusion to arbitrary DFAs while guaranteeing 100% constraint satisfaction, and the HMM need only be distilled once per LLM regardless of how many different constraints are later applied.

## Results

On the CommonGen benchmark (GPT2-large), Ctrl-G achieves 100% constraint satisfaction with a test BLEU-4 of 34.4 and ROUGE-L of 46.4, outperforming GeLaTo (34.1/45.9) and all other baselines. On the interactive text editing task (TULU2-7B + 2B HMM), Ctrl-G achieves 100% constraint satisfaction across all settings, yielding an overall satisfaction rate of 92% (continuation) and 79% (insertion), compared to GPT4's 56% and 24% respectively. On text infilling, Ctrl-G outperforms the fully supervised ILM baseline by up to +3.4 BLEU-4 at 40% masking ratio. As a proof-of-concept on GSM, Ctrl-G improves TULU2-7B's majority-vote accuracy from 24.9% to 28.3% (+3.4%) by enforcing keyphrase-inclusion constraints during chain-of-thought reasoning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On CommonGen, Ctrl-G (BLEU-4 34.4, ROUGE-L 46.4, 100% constraint) outperforms FUDGE (24.6, 40.4, 47%), A*esque (28.2, 43.4, 98.8%), NADO (88.8% constraint), and GeLaTo (34.1, 45.9, 100%) on all automatic metrics while matching GeLaTo's perfect constraint satisfaction; it is also ~30x faster than GeLaTo (4.6s vs 143s per example with 5 concepts). On text infilling, Ctrl-G beats the fully supervised ILM baseline on BLEU-4 at all masking ratios above 13%, with the gap growing from +0.2 to +3.4 as masking increases from 13% to 40%. On interactive text editing, Ctrl-G achieves 100% constraint satisfaction in every setting, dramatically outperforming GPT4 (57% continuation, 36% insertion) and GPT3.5 (43%, 29%) on success rate; in terms of overall satisfaction (constraint met + quality > 3), Ctrl-G scores 92%/79% vs GPT4's 56%/24%. However, GPT4 retains an edge on raw generation quality for continuation (4.40 vs 4.08 average), while Ctrl-G matches or exceeds GPT4 on insertion quality (3.67 vs 3.44). On GSM, Ctrl-G's keyphrase-enforced reasoning yields a 3.4% absolute accuracy gain over unconstrained majority voting.

## Relation to Prior Work

Ctrl-G directly generalizes GeLaTo (Zhang et al., ICML 2023), which distilled an HMM to approximate an LLM and used it to guarantee keyword-inclusion constraints during generation. The key conceptual leap is extending from a single constraint type (keyword inclusion) to arbitrary logical constraints expressible as deterministic finite automata, enabling composition via intersection, union, complement, and concatenation. This positions Ctrl-G in contrast to both classifier-based approaches (FUDGE, NADO, GeDi) that approximate the intractable marginal without guarantees, and pure logical-reasoning approaches (guidance, SGLang) that only discard unsatisfiable tokens without estimating how likely each token is to lead to constraint satisfaction. The probabilistic-reasoning framing—computing p_hmm(α|x_t, x_<t) via a joint HMM-DFA forward-backward recurrence—is the paper's core methodological contribution over all prior lines of work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Ctrl-G offers a practically attractive combination: guaranteed constraint satisfaction, no retraining for new constraints (only the DFA changes), and demonstrated scaling to 7B-parameter LLMs where GeLaTo was limited to ~0.1B models. The proof-of-concept on GSM suggests a broader paradigm of using logical constraints to approximate soft control (e.g., enforcing use of all problem numbers in chain-of-thought), opening a path toward constraint-guided reasoning. However, the framework is limited to constraints expressible as DFAs over bounded-length sequences, and the HMM approximation quality for very large or highly stochastic LLMs remains an open question. The GPT4 comparison, while striking, uses a metric (overall satisfaction rate) that inherently advantages guaranteed-satisfaction methods, so the true quality gap in unconstrained generation is smaller than the headline numbers suggest.
