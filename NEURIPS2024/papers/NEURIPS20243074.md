## Problem

Personalized image generation requires preserving a user-specified subject's identity while following a text prompt, but existing methods either demand per-subject finetuning or costly pre-training on large domain-specific datasets (e.g., 50M face images), limiting flexibility across different subject types. Classifier guidance offers a training-free alternative, but its original formulation requires a noise-aware classifier trained on noised inputs, making it incompatible with the off-the-shelf image discriminators that are readily available. This paper shows that for rectified flow, this limitation can be resolved with a simple fixed-point reformulation that only needs clean-image discriminators.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single reference image (a face photo, animal photo, or object photo) and a free-form text prompt as input, and produces a 512×512 RGB image that preserves the reference subject's identity while following the prompt. It operates entirely at test time on a frozen piecewise rectified flow (finetuned from Stable Diffusion 1.5 with 4 time windows), requiring no training or finetuning of the generator. For face tasks, an SCRFD detector and ArcFace model extract identity features; for general subjects, OWL-ViT localizes the object and DINOv2 extracts visual features, with an optional ℓ₁ loss for regularly shaped objects.

## Method

The key insight is that rectified flow's straightness property allows the intermediate classifier guidance ∇_{z_t} log p(c|z_t) to be bypassed by substituting t=1, reducing the problem to a fixed-point equation ẑ₁ = z₁ + s·[∇_{z₀}z₁]∇_{ẑ₁} log p(c|ẑ₁) that only involves the clean-image endpoint. To ensure convergence, the target trajectory is anchored to a reference trajectory, yielding a Lipschitz constant of s·L₁·L₂ that can be made <1 by choosing a sufficiently small guidance scale s (Proposition 2). In practice, the method extends to piecewise rectified flow by reinitializing the reference trajectory at each segment boundary and using a straight-through estimator for cross-segment Jacobians, solved via N iterations of fixed-point updates (Algorithm 1). The classifier signal is computed as cosine similarity between features extracted by an off-the-shelf discriminator (ArcFace or DINOv2) from the generated image and the reference image, enabling plug-and-play personalization across faces, animals, objects, and multi-subject compositions without any training.

## Results

On CelebA-HQ face-centric personalization, RectifID (100 iterations, SD 1.5) achieves the highest identity similarity of 0.5930 among all compared methods, surpassing the training-based InstantID (0.5806, trained on 60M face images) while requiring no training data. With SD 2.1 as the base model, RectifID (20 iterations) achieves the best prompt consistency of 0.3151, outperforming SDXL-based methods. The method is training-free and runs in 9 seconds (20 iterations) or 46 seconds (100 iterations) on an A800 GPU, compared to 6331 seconds for Textual Inversion and 623 seconds for DreamBooth.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RectifID (100 iter, SD 1.5) achieves the best identity score (0.5930), beating the strongest training-based baseline InstantID (0.5806, trained on 60M images) by 0.0124 while being entirely training-free. Against IP-Adapter (10M training), RectifID (20 iter, SD 1.5) wins on both identity (0.4860 vs. 0.4778) and prompt consistency (0.2995 vs. 0.2627). RectifID (20 iter, SD 2.1) leads on prompt consistency (0.3151) over PhotoMaker (0.3079) and InstantID (0.3071), both of which require extensive pre-training. All tuning-based baselines (Textual Inversion, DreamBooth, NeTI, Celeb Basis, Cross Initialization) are substantially outperformed on identity by RectifID, with gaps of 0.11–0.39. For subject-driven and multi-subject generation (qualitative comparisons on DreamBooth subset), RectifID is compared against Emu2, Textual Inversion, DreamBooth, BLIP-Diffusion, FastComposer, and Cones 2; the paper reports that RectifID achieves competitive or superior identity and prompt consistency, with Cones 2 (SD 2.1-based) being the closest competitor on multi-subject tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: classifier guidance for diffusion models (Dhariwal & Nichol, 2021), which traditionally requires a noise-aware classifier, and rectified flow (Liu et al., 2023), whose straightness property enables a clean fixed-point reformulation of the guidance problem. The key departure is showing that the noise-aware classifier dependency can be eliminated by exploiting trajectory straightness, reducing the problem to a fixed-point equation on the clean endpoint, and then anchoring the target trajectory to a reference for convergence. For implementation, it builds on PeRFlow's piecewise-linear approximation (Yan et al., 2024) rather than assuming a perfectly straight flow. This positions the work as a training-free alternative to both fine-tuning-based personalization (Textual Inversion, DreamBooth) and pre-training-based methods (IP-Adapter, InstantID, PhotoMaker) that require large domain-specific datasets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that a training-free method achieves identity similarity (0.5930) surpassing InstantID (0.5806), which was trained on 60M face images, while remaining flexible across faces, live subjects, objects, and multi-subject composition by simply swapping the off-the-shelf discriminator. The convergence guarantee for the anchored fixed-point iteration (Proposition 2) provides theoretical grounding absent in prior gradient-descent-based guidance approaches. However, the theoretical guarantee is limited to ideal rectified flow, the method struggles with irregularly shaped objects (e.g., plush toys), and inference time (46s for 100 iterations) remains substantially higher than training-based baselines (2–6s), leaving open the question of whether more sophisticated numerical solvers can close this gap. The lack of pre-trained discriminators for many domains also limits immediate applicability beyond faces and common subjects.
