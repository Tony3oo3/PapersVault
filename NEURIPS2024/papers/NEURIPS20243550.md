## Problem

Existing cooperative multi-agent reinforcement learning assumes either full control over all agents (CMARL) or control over exactly one agent (AHT), but real-world scenarios—such as autonomous vehicles from different companies sharing a road—require controlling a variable subset of agents while cooperating with uncontrolled teammates of unknown types. This paper formalizes N-agent ad hoc teamwork (NAHT), where N controlled agents must coordinate with M−N uncontrolled agents, and proves via a matrix-game counterexample that even an optimal AHT policy is suboptimal in the N=2 NAHT setting.

## Input / Output

Each controlled agent consumes only its own local observation and action history (no global state access), which the recurrent encoder compresses into a fixed-dimension team embedding vector. The policy network outputs a discrete action distribution per controlled agent, conditioned jointly on the current local observation and the team embedding. A key asymmetry in data usage: the value (critic) network is trained on transitions from both controlled and uncontrolled agents, while the policy network is trained exclusively on controlled-agent data, since the policy update is sensitive to off-policy contamination.

## Method

POAM combines an encoder-decoder agent-modeling module with an independent actor-critic policy (based on IPPO) under full parameter sharing. The encoder maps each agent's local history to a team embedding vector; two decoders are trained to predict all other agents' observations (MSE loss) and action distributions (negative log-likelihood under a Categorical), forcing the embedding to capture teammate behavior. The policy and critic networks both condition on this embedding, enabling the controlled agents to adapt their actions to the inferred characteristics of encountered teammates. The critic is additionally trained on uncontrolled-agent transitions—treating them as a form of exploration data—which the paper shows yields significant sample-efficiency gains over purely on-policy training. The independent-learning framework (rather than centralized training) is chosen specifically to handle the varying number of agents without architectural changes.

## Results

POAM is evaluated on MPE (mpe-pp) and four SMAC tasks (5v6, 8v9, 10v11, 3s5z) using M-N cross-play scores (mean return over all N from 1 to M-1). POAM achieves the highest asymptotic return on 3 of 5 tasks (mpe-pp, 5v6, 3s5z) and superior sample efficiency over IPPO-NAHT across all tasks. In out-of-distribution generalization, POAM significantly outperforms IPPO-NAHT on 3 of 5 tasks when paired with unseen teammate seeds. On the cross-play mean xp score (average M-N return against 5 MARL baselines), POAM-AHT achieves 9.118 on mpe-pp and 19.139 on 3s5z, substantially exceeding the best naive MARL baseline (QMIX: 4.350 and 13.798 respectively).

## Baselines & Comparisons

POAM consistently outperforms all naive MARL baselines (VDN, QMIX, IQL, IPPO, MAPPO) in the NAHT setting, with the largest gaps on mpe-pp (POAM-AHT: 9.118 vs. best MARL QMIX: 4.350) and 3s5z (19.139 vs. 13.798). Against IPPO-NAHT (POAM without agent modeling), POAM achieves higher asymptotic returns on 3 of 5 tasks and better sample efficiency on all tasks, while IPPO-NAHT converges to similar returns on 8v9 and 10v11 given sufficient training. POAM-AHT (AHT-trained policy duplicated across agents) is beaten by POAM on all tasks with N>1, confirming the theoretical argument that AHT policies are suboptimal for multi-agent control. Among the naive MARL baselines, QMIX is the strongest, but it still lags behind POAM-AHT on mpe-pp and 3s5z, and is outperformed by POAM on the majority of tasks. The agent modeling module provides the key differentiator: it enables POAM to adapt to diverse teammate behaviors within an episode, yielding both faster convergence and better OOD generalization compared to IPPO-NAHT.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper formalizes N-agent ad hoc teamwork (NAHT) as a generalization of both cooperative MARL (all agents controlled) and ad hoc teamwork (single agent controlled), filling the gap where a variable subset of agents is controlled while the rest are uncontrolled. The proposed POAM algorithm builds on IPPO/MAPPO (Yu et al., 2022) as the base policy gradient method and extends the agent-modeling paradigm from Papoudakis et al. (2021) and Rahman et al. (2021)—which characterize single-agent teammates as learned embedding vectors—to the multi-agent control setting. A key conceptual departure is the theoretical demonstration (Section 4) that an optimal AHT policy is provably suboptimal in the N=2 NAHT setting, justifying the need for a dedicated multi-agent formulation rather than naively replicating single-agent AHT policies.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

NAHT opens a broader class of realistic cooperative scenarios—such as autonomous vehicles from different companies sharing a road, or heterogeneous robot fleets in warehouses—that neither CMARL nor AHT can optimally address. The agent modeling component's contribution to out-of-distribution generalization is practically significant, as real deployments will inevitably encounter teammates not seen during training. However, the current scope is limited to homogeneous, non-communicating agents with full parameter sharing, and the on-policy actor cannot exploit uncontrolled agent data for policy updates; extending to heterogeneous roles, inter-agent communication, and off-policy methods are natural next steps that the authors identify as open.
