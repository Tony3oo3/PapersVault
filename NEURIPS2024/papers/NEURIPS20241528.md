## Problem

Existing Mendelian randomization methods assume a known one-directional causal relationship and rely on expert knowledge or strong assumptions (majority rule, plurality rule) to select valid instruments. In real-world bi-directional settings (e.g., obesity ↔ vitamin D), some genetic variants are invalid due to horizontal pleiotropy, and the causal direction itself is unknown, making it impossible to consistently estimate both causal effects from purely observational data without prior knowledge of which IVs are valid or which direction they belong to.

## Input / Output

The method consumes one-sample individual-level data: a matrix of g measured genetic variants (SNPs coded 0/1/2), two continuous phenotypes X and Y, a significance level α for the Pearson correlation test, and a parameter W capping the maximum IV set size to control combinatorial search. It outputs two partitioned valid IV sets (one assigned to X→Y, one to Y→X) along with two TSLS-based causal effect estimates β̂_{X→Y} and β̂_{Y→X}. Unlike two-sample MR methods, all associations are computed within the same cohort, and the algorithm requires no pre-specified number of valid IVs or causal direction.

## Method

The core insight is a pseudo-residual constraint: for a valid IV set G, the residual Y − X·TSLS(X,Y,G\{G_j}) is uncorrelated with every G_j ∈ G, whereas invalid sets generically violate this (Propositions 2–3). The PReBiM algorithm exploits this via a cluster-fusion-like search: it initializes with the pair of IVs minimizing the sum of pseudo-residual correlations, incrementally expands each cluster by adding the variant with the smallest correlation that passes a Fisher-transformed Pearson test, and merges or discards clusters until two valid sets are found. Direction assignment uses a variance-ratio criterion (|corr(G_j,Y)/corr(G_j,X)| < 1 implies G_j belongs to X→Y) under a natural variance inequality assumption. Final causal effects are then estimated by standard TSLS on the direction-specific valid IV sets, and the paper proves unbiasedness in the large-sample limit under three mild assumptions (≥2 valid IVs per direction, generic parameter values, and a variance ordering condition).

## Results

In the bi-directional MR setting, PReBiM achieves the highest Correct-Selecting Rate (CSR) and lowest Mean Squared Error (MSE) across all sample sizes (2k, 5k, 10k) and IV configurations S(2,2,6), S(3,3,8), S(4,4,10). At n=10k, PReBiM reaches CSR of 0.93–0.95 and MSE as low as 0.011–0.044, compared to the next-best method IV-TETRAD (CSR 0.23–0.71, MSE 0.58–0.81). On real-world data, PReBiM identifies FTO and FAIM2 as valid IVs for obesity→vitamin D (effect −1.15) and DHCR7 and CYP24A1 for the reverse direction (effect −0.05), consistent with prior findings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PReBiM decisively outperforms all four baselines on both CSR and MSE in the bi-directional MR setting. IV-TETRAD [Silva & Shimizu, 2017] is the closest competitor, reaching CSR up to 0.71 in S(4,4,10) but still trailing PReBiM (0.93) by over 20 percentage points, with MSE roughly 15–20× higher (0.635 vs. 0.027). sisVIVE [Kang et al., 2016] and TSHT [Guo et al., 2018] perform poorly, with CSR below 0.41 and 0.12 respectively, because they rely on majority/plurality rules that are violated when only 2–4 of 6–10 IVs are valid. NAIVE regression and MR-Egger [Bowden et al., 2015] do not perform IV selection (no CSR output) and produce consistently biased estimates (MSE 0.30–0.80). In the one-directional setting (Figure 5), PReBiM is comparable to IV-TETRAD and superior to sisVIVE and TSHT, with the only exception being S(2,0,6) where IV-TETRAD benefits from a pre-specified IV count that PReBiM does not have.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the bi-directional MR framework of Li and Ye (2022) from a two-sample setting with a strict noise-distribution assumption to a one-sample setting, and generalizes the one-directional IV identification theory of Silva and Shimizu (2017, IV-TETRAD) to handle simultaneous causal effects in both directions. The key conceptual departure is the introduction of a 'pseudo-residual' construct whose correlation with candidate IVs provides a testable constraint for distinguishing valid from invalid IV sets in the bi-directional model—something that prior one-directional methods (sisVIVE, TSHT, MR-Egger) cannot address because they presuppose a known causal direction. The cluster-fusion algorithm for discovering valid IV sets is a practical instantiation of the identification theory, drawing on the Two-Valid-IVs assumption shared with IV-TETRAD but applying it in a fundamentally different (cyclic) causal structure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Bi-directional causal relationships (obesity↔vitamin D, BMI↔T2D) are pervasive in biomedical and social-science applications, yet most MR methods assume a known one-directional causal structure, making them inapplicable or biased in these settings. The necessary-and-sufficient identification conditions (Theorem 1) provide a clean theoretical foundation that clarifies exactly when and why bi-directional MR is estimable from observational data, which is valuable for method developers. Key limitations include the assumption that genetic variants are mutually independent (a strong restriction in practice due to linkage disequilibrium), the linearity of the structural equations, and the requirement of at least two valid IVs per direction. Open questions include extending the framework to dependent genetic variants, nonlinear models, and settings where confounding exists among the instruments themselves.
