## Problem

Real-world time series contain multi-scale periodic components (e.g., yearly, weekly, daily) with clear inclusion and overlap relationships, yet existing methods either model temporal dependencies directly on scattered 1D points or, as in TimesNet, flatten periodicity into 2D tensors without explicitly representing the hierarchical inclusion structure between periods of different scales. This limits the ability to capture how shorter periods nest within longer ones and how same-level periods relate to each other.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240415/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a multivariate time series of length L and C channels, processed channel-independently. For reconstruction tasks (forecasting, imputation, anomaly detection), the input is first normalized and decomposed into seasonal and trend parts via moving average; only the seasonal part enters the Periodic Pyramid, while the trend is added back after prediction and the result is de-normalized. For classification, decomposition is skipped since the trend is a discriminative feature. The look-back window is typically 512 (64 for Exchange), and outputs range from forecasted/imputed sequences of variable target length to class labels or per-point anomaly scores.

## Method

Peri-midFormer constructs a Periodic Pyramid by applying FFT to the seasonal component, selecting the top-k dominant frequencies, and partitioning the series into periodic components at k levels (longest period at the top, shortest at the bottom), where each level's components explicitly encode inclusion relationships with adjacent levels. The Periodic Pyramid Attention Mechanism (PPAM) then computes self-attention restricted to each component's parent (overlapping component above), all same-level siblings, and child (overlapping components below), rather than full attention across all components, thereby capturing inter-level and intra-level periodic dependencies with reduced complexity. For reconstruction tasks, Periodic Feature Flows Aggregation collects top-to-bottom branches through the pyramid, projects each flow to the target output length, and averages across flows to produce the final seasonal prediction. The architecture is trained end-to-end with task-specific losses (MSE for forecasting/imputation/anomaly detection, cross-entropy for classification) using the Adam optimizer.

## Results

On long-term forecasting (averaged over prediction lengths 96/192/336/720), Peri-midFormer achieves an average MSE of 0.308 and MAE of 0.337, tying with Time-LLM (MSE 0.308) and outperforming GPT4TS (MSE 0.324), TimesNet (MSE 0.422), and Pyraformer (MSE 1.147). On short-term M4 forecasting, it achieves SMAPE 11.833, MASE 1.584, and OWA 0.850, beating Time-LLM and GPT4TS. For classification on 10 UEA datasets, Peri-midFormer reaches 76.5% average accuracy, surpassing TSLANet (76.0%) and GPT4TS (74.0%). On anomaly detection, it achieves 85.40% average F1, second only to GPT4TS (86.72%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Peri-midFormer achieves the best or near-best average MSE (0.308) on long-term forecasting, tying with Time-LLM but at far lower computational cost (244G vs 12.69T train FLOPs on Electricity); it clearly outperforms GPT4TS (0.324), TSLANet (0.320), PatchTST (0.331), TimesNet (0.422), and Pyraformer (1.147). On short-term M4 forecasting, Peri-midFormer (SMAPE 11.833, OWA 0.850) beats Time-LLM (11.991, 0.859), GPT4TS (11.991, 0.861), and N-BEATS (11.851, 0.855). For classification, Peri-midFormer (76.5%) surpasses TSLANet (76.0%), GPT4TS (74.0%), TimesNet (73.6%), and all other Transformer-based baselines. On imputation, Peri-midFormer (avg MSE 0.047) edges out GPT4TS (0.048) and TimesNet (0.050), though it trails GPT4TS on individual ETT datasets. On anomaly detection, Peri-midFormer (85.40% F1) is second to GPT4TS (86.72%) but beats TimesNet (85.24%), PatchTST (82.79%), and all other baselines; the authors attribute the gap to the binary-event nature of anomaly data limiting periodicity exploitation.

## Relation to Prior Work

Peri-midFormer synthesizes three distinct lineages: Pyraformer's pyramidal attention structure (but inverted from bottom-up convolutional downsampling to top-down periodic decomposition), TimesNet's FFT-based identification of dominant periods (but replacing 2D CNN feature extraction with structured attention), and Autoformer's seasonal-trend decomposition (as a preprocessing step to isolate periodic content). The key conceptual departure is treating the pyramid not as a hierarchy of downsampled representations but as a hierarchy of periodic components with explicit inclusion/overlap relationships, enabling a structured attention mechanism (PPAM) that only attends between components that are structurally related (parent, sibling, or child). This contrasts with Pyraformer's simple two-fold attention and TimesNet's independent 2D CNN processing of each period.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The periodic pyramid provides an interpretable, explicit representation of multi-scale periodic structure that is implicit in 1D time series, and the single architecture achieving competitive results across five diverse tasks (forecasting, imputation, classification, anomaly detection) with modest compute is practically valuable. However, the paper's own analysis reveals a clear limitation: performance degrades on datasets lacking strong periodicity (Exchange, Yearly, anomaly detection), suggesting the method's ceiling is bounded by the periodic content in the data. An open question is whether the FFT-based period selection can be made adaptive or learned, and whether the approach can be extended to handle non-stationary or aperiodic signals without the current decomposition workaround.
