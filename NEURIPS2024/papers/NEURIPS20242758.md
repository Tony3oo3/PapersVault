## Problem

Existing creator competition models (e.g., C³) capture how creators strategically select topics or quality but ignore the dimension of content creation volume, which is equally critical for platform sustainability. This paper asks: how does a recommendation algorithm's exploration strength (its matching precision) affect creators' equilibrium production frequency, and can we find the optimal per-user exploration level that balances short-term user satisfaction against long-term content supply?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242758/cropped_page8_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a game instance G(n, m, M, {c_i}, β) where M ∈ R^{n×m} is the user-creator relevance matrix (entries in [0,1]), each c_i is a convex cost function characterizing creator i's production cost, λ is a scalar welfare weight, and β ∈ R_{>0}^m are per-user exploration parameters. It outputs the optimal personalized exploration vector β*, the unique PNE creation frequencies x*(β*) ∈ R_{≥0}^n, and the resulting social welfare W_λ. The PNE is computed at each optimization step via multi-agent mirror descent (Algorithm 2), and the gradient computation uses a user-subset sketch of size ⌈δm⌉ to reduce matrix inversion cost from O(n³) to O(n²δm + n(δm)² + (δm)³).

## Method

The paper introduces the Cournot Content Creation Competition (C⁴) game, where creators with fixed niches strategically choose production frequency x_i to maximize expected traffic under a Personalized Probabilistic Matching (PPM) mechanism that allocates user j's traffic proportionally to exp(β_j w_ij). The key theoretical result (Theorem 2) proves that for homogeneous users, user satisfaction U is strictly increasing in β while total content volume V is strictly decreasing in β, establishing a fundamental trade-off; the proof uses the implicit function theorem to differentiate the PNE w.r.t. β and the Sherman-Morrison formula to handle the rank-one matrix structure. To find the optimal β maximizing W_λ = U + λV, the paper formulates a bi-level optimization problem and solves it with projected gradient descent, where the gradient is computed via an explicit Jacobian formula (Proposition 1) that avoids full matrix inversion by exploiting the diagonal-plus-rank-one structure of ∂F/∂x. The PNE at each step is solved by multi-agent mirror descent with exact gradients, leveraging the strict monotonicity of C⁴ games to guarantee last-iterate convergence.

## Results

Algorithm 1 achieves a welfare improvement of over 20% in W_λ = U + 0.5V compared to the baseline of exact matching (β=100 for all users), in both the synthetic clustered environment and the MovieLens-1m environment. The empirical results confirm Theorem 2: increasing β monotonically raises total user satisfaction U while decreasing total content creation volume V, with the drop in V becoming more pronounced at higher β. Personalized per-user β consistently yields slightly better welfare than a single homogeneous β across all users, and the optimal β_j is inversely correlated with user selectivity—less selective users (higher average relevance, lower variance) receive smaller β_j (more exploration).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against external recommendation algorithms; instead, it benchmarks its proposed Algorithm 1 (approximated gradient descent for optimal personalized β) against a natural baseline of near-deterministic matching (β=100 for all users, representing a purely relevance-driven policy with minimal exploration). Algorithm 1 outperforms this baseline by over 20% in the hybrid welfare metric W_λ in both the synthetic and MovieLens-1m environments. A secondary comparison is between personalized per-user β_j and a single homogeneous β shared across all users: the personalized variant consistently achieves slightly higher welfare, confirming that user heterogeneity in preference selectivity matters for optimal exploration allocation. The paper also reports results under a more creator-weighted welfare (λ=0.1 vs. λ=0.5), showing that the qualitative pattern—prioritizing accuracy for selective users and exploration for open users—remains consistent across different trade-off weights.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Content Creator Competition (C³) framework [52, 54, 55], which models creators strategically selecting topics or quality to compete for platform-allocated traffic, by introducing production frequency as an additional strategic dimension. The resulting C⁴ model draws its competitive structure from Cournot competition in economics (firms choosing output quantities) and its traffic allocation mechanism from the Tullock contest [48], extended to accommodate heterogeneous convex cost functions. For solving the equilibrium, the paper leverages multi-agent mirror descent [7], whose convergence guarantees in strictly monotone games make the PNE both predictable and computationally accessible.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper reveals a counterintuitive but well-supported trade-off: increasing recommendation accuracy (reducing exploration) improves short-term user satisfaction while simultaneously reducing total content creation volume, threatening long-term platform sustainability. The proposed per-user exploration optimization (Algorithm 1) offers a practical pre-deployment audit tool for UGC platforms. Key limitations include the theoretical guarantee (Theorem 2) being proven only for a single homogeneous user (m=1), with generalization to heterogeneous populations demonstrated empirically rather than analytically, and the assumption that creators maintain fixed niches rather than dynamically shifting topics or quality.
