## Problem

Sequential updating MARL methods (e.g., HAPPO, A2PO) naturally diversify agents by updating them one-by-one, but the exploration strategy in this paradigm remains unexplored. Existing multi-agent exploration techniques rely on parameter sharing for homogeneous agents and degrade in heterogeneous scenarios, failing to exploit the key advantage of sequential updating: access to preceding agents' updated policies. No prior method provides a unified exploration mechanism that works for both heterogeneous sequential and homogeneous simultaneous updating.

## Input / Output

Each agent i at episode k receives the state observation s, the updated policy of the preceding agent (π̄_k^{i-1}) from the same episode, and its own policy from the previous episode (π̄_{k-1}^{i}). The method produces optimized per-agent policies that are both more exploratory (divergent from their own history) and more heterogeneous (divergent from the preceding agent). The divergence estimator consumes paired trajectories from both the current and fixed policies, computing Gram matrices over states and actions with a Gaussian kernel of bandwidth σ, so the input effectively requires maintaining one extra episode of buffer data beyond standard sequential methods.

## Method

MADPO introduces Mutual Policy Divergence Maximization (Mutual PDM), a mutual intrinsic reward combining intra-agent divergence (current policy vs. its own previous-episode policy, encouraging exploration) and inter-agent divergence (current policy vs. the preceding agent's updated policy, promoting heterogeneity), weighted by a coefficient λ. To measure policy discrepancy, the paper replaces the standard KL divergence with the conditional Cauchy-Schwarz (CS) divergence, which is provably lower-bounded by −log n (Proposition 2) for stability and whose maximization implicitly maximizes the 2nd-order Rényi entropy of the target policy (Proposition 1), providing an entropy-guided exploration incentive absent in KL. The CS divergence is estimated empirically via kernel density estimation with Gaussian kernels over state-action trajectories. The full objective augments HAPPO's extrinsic clipped PPO reward with the two CS-divergence terms, and the algorithm maintains one additional episode of buffer data to compute the inter-agent divergence from the preceding agent's updated policy.

## Results

MADPO is evaluated on 10 MA-Mujoco scenarios (Ant, HalfCheetah, Walker2d, Humanoid with up to 17 agents) and 10 Bi-DexHands bimanual manipulation tasks, using 5 random seeds with 95% confidence intervals. The paper reports that MADPO consistently outperforms all baselines in both benchmarks, with the advantage becoming more pronounced as the number of agents increases in MA-Mujoco. Statistical significance is assessed via the interquartile mean (IQM) sample efficiency test from rliable, where MADPO achieves higher aggregate IQM rewards across all 20 tasks compared to HAPPO, HATRPO, A2PO, and MAPPO. Wall-time overhead is modest: MADPO adds roughly 5–15 minutes per 10M training steps relative to the fastest baselines (e.g., 2h13m vs. 1h49m for MAPPO on Walker2d-v2-6x1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MADPO is compared against four baselines: MAPPO (simultaneous, with parameter sharing), HAPPO and HATRPO (sequential, from Kuba et al. 2022), and A2PO (sequential, from Wang et al. 2023). MADPO outperforms all four baselines across both MA-Mujoco and Bi-DexHands in terms of best episodic reward and IQM aggregate sample efficiency. The gap is most pronounced in high-agent-count MA-Mujoco tasks (e.g., Humanoid-v2-17x1) and in the more challenging Bi-DexHands bimanual tasks, where HAPPO and other sequential methods are reported to get trapped in local optima due to insufficient exploration. In the ablation (Fig. 5), replacing the conditional CS divergence with KL-divergence or plain policy entropy leads to consistent performance degradation, confirming that the mutual PDM framework with CS divergence is the key driver of the gains. Wall-time cost of MADPO is comparable to baselines (within ~10–20% overhead), so the performance improvement does not come at a prohibitive computational price.

## Relation to Prior Work

MADPO extends the sequential updating MARL lineage initiated by Bertsekas (2021) and developed by HAPPO (Kuba et al., 2022) and A2PO (Wang et al., 2023), which use preceding-agent information for advantage estimation but leave exploration unaddressed. The key departure is repurposing the conditional Cauchy-Schwarz divergence (Yu et al., 2023, originally for time-series and sequential decision making) as a policy-divergence measure that simultaneously provides Rényi-entropy-guided exploration incentives and a deterministic lower bound for stability. Unlike prior divergence-regularized RL methods that operate in simultaneous updating or single-agent settings, MADPO is the first to exploit the sequential structure to define both intra-agent (temporal) and inter-agent (spatial) divergence as a joint intrinsic reward.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper fills a concrete gap: sequential MARL methods had no principled exploration mechanism that leverages the unique preceding-agent information available in the one-by-one update scheme. The CS divergence's dual property (entropy maximization + boundedness) is a clean theoretical contribution that explains why it outperforms KL divergence empirically. However, the method's memory requirement grows with agent count (storing prior-episode minibatches), limiting scalability to very large agent populations, and the evaluation is confined to two simulation benchmarks with up to 17 agents. Open questions include whether the mutual PDM framework transfers to off-policy or partially observable settings, and whether the random-order advantage observed in ablations generalizes beyond the tested topologies.
