## Problem

Deep RL networks exhibit 'churn'—unintended output changes on states outside the current training batch after each update—but how churn arises and compounds across the value-policy iteration cycle remained uncharacterized. Prior work (Schaul et al., 2022) observed policy churn in value-based methods but did not formalize the interplay between value and policy churn or its long-term bias on parameter updates. This paper identifies a 'chain effect' where value and policy churns mutually amplify through deviations in action gradients and policy values, derailing learning across value-based, policy-gradient, and actor-critic settings.

## Input / Output

CHAIN is a plug-in regularization that operates on the same state-vector inputs and produces the same actions or Q-values as the base algorithm (DoubleDQN, PPO, TD3, SAC, IQL, AWAC). The key additional requirement is a second batch B_ref sampled independently from the replay buffer at each update, used solely to compute the churn-reduction loss against the previous network version. The method adds no architectural changes; it only augments the training objective with a few lines of code, effectively doubling the per-update batch size.

## Method

The paper first formalizes value churn C_Q and policy churn C_π via Neural Tangent Kernel (NTK) analysis, showing they are governed by the network's kernel structure and the TD error or action gradient of the training batch. It then proves a chain effect: parameter updates cause churn (U.I), churn induces deviations in action gradients and policy values (U.II), and these deviations bias subsequent parameter updates (U.III), forming a compounding cycle. To break this cycle, CHAIN adds two regularization losses—L_QC (mean squared change in Q-values on B_ref between consecutive updates) for value networks and L_PC (MSE or KL policy distance on B_ref) for policy networks—combined with the standard RL objective via coefficients λ_Q and λ_π. An automatic adjustment scheme keeps the regularization term at a fixed relative scale β to the original loss, eliminating manual tuning. The method is evaluated across 20 online tasks (MinAtar, MuJoCo, DMC) and 8 offline datasets (D4RL Antmaze, Adroit), showing consistent churn reduction and performance gains, and also improves scaling of PPO networks up to 8× wider or deeper.

## Results

On D4RL Antmaze, CHAIN IQL (PCR) improves over IQL on 4 of 6 tasks, with the largest gain on AM-umaze-diverse-v2 (62.50 vs. 54.25) and AM-medium-play-v2 (72.50 vs. 65.75). On D4RL Adroit, CHAIN AWAC (PCR) boosts pen-human-v1 from 81.12 to 99.72 and pen-cloned-v1 from 89.56 to 95.49. In the scaling setting, CHAIN PPO (8× wider) achieves 5592.94 on Ant (10M) versus 4235.63 for vanilla PPO (8× wider), and 5246.15 on Humanoid versus 3198.01, demonstrating that churn reduction substantially mitigates the performance degradation from network scaling.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CHAIN DDQN consistently outperforms DoubleDQN on MinAtar in both sample efficiency and final episode return, with the most pronounced gains on Asterix and Seaquest (Figure 4). CHAIN PPO beats vanilla PPO in most MuJoCo and DMC tasks with higher sample efficiency and final return (Figure 5), and the improvement is consistent across different λ_π choices. For actor-critic methods, CHAIN-PCR (policy churn reduction) provides the largest gains, particularly on Ant-v4 for both TD3 and SAC, while CHAIN-VCR yields smaller but positive improvements; the authors attribute this asymmetry to the target network already partially capping value churn. In offline RL, CHAIN IQL (PCR) improves over IQL on 4 of 6 Antmaze tasks (e.g., +8.25 on AM-umaze-diverse-v2), and CHAIN AWAC (PCR) improves pen-human-v1 by +18.6 points over AWAC. In the scaling setting, CHAIN PPO outperforms scaled PPO across nearly all width and depth ratios, with the gap widening as scale increases (e.g., +1357 on Ant at 8× wider), confirming that uncontrolled churn is a key bottleneck for scaling DRL agents.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Schaul et al. [2022], who identified the 'policy churn' phenomenon in DoubleDQN (where ~10% of greedy actions flip after a single batch update), by generalizing the analysis to a unified Generalized Policy Iteration framework that captures both value churn and policy churn across all DRL paradigms. The key conceptual advance is the 'chain effect': a compounding cycle where parameter updates cause churn, churn deviates action gradients and policy values, and those deviations bias subsequent updates, creating a self-reinforcing loop. The proposed CHAIN regularization connects to the broader literature on interference, catastrophic forgetting, and plasticity loss, with the NTK-based analysis (Eq. 2) providing a mechanistic link between network kernel properties and churn magnitude.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The chain effect provides a unifying explanation for several previously disconnected DRL pathologies (greedy action instability, implicit trust-region violation in PPO, dual bias in actor-critic), and the CHAIN method offers a minimal, plug-in remedy that improves sample efficiency and final performance across online/offline, value-based/policy-based settings with only a few lines of code. The connection between uncontrolled churn and DRL scaling failure is a practically important finding, though the authors note CHAIN achieves only sub-linear scaling gains. Open questions include: whether the chain effect can be characterized under concrete theoretical assumptions, how to automatically select the best combination of VCR/PCR/DCR for actor-critic methods, and whether some degree of churn is actually beneficial for generalization.
