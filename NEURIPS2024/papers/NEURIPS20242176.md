## Problem

3D point cloud recognition models suffer significant performance degradation under real-world corruptions (noise, density changes, geometric transformations), yet existing data augmentation methods operate exclusively in the spatial domain where low information density and heavy redundancy make it difficult to identify which specific information is corrupted. This paper asks whether analyzing corruptions in the frequency domain can reveal actionable structure for improving corruption robustness.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242176/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D point cloud of n points (each with xyz coordinates) and outputs a class label. During training, the point cloud is first converted to a weighted k-nearest-neighbor graph (k=30, edge weights w_ij = exp(-d_ij²)), then transformed to the frequency domain via graph Fourier transform. Adversarial perturbations are generated in the frequency domain (high-frequency band: first 100 of 1024 frequencies; low-frequency band: remaining 924) with ℓ_p-norm constraints of 0.3 and 0.5 respectively, then mapped back to spatial domain via inverse GFT before being fed to the classifier.

## Method

Frequency Adversarial Training (FAT) improves corruption robustness by training with adversarial examples generated in the frequency domain. The key insight is that a model's sensitivity to high/low frequency bands—measured by the GFT spectrum of its Jacobian matrix—is negatively correlated with its robustness to high/low frequency corruptions. FAT simultaneously generates high-frequency adversarial examples (via PGD, altering fine details) and low-frequency adversarial examples (via AOF, altering rough shapes), and trains using the AdvProp framework with three separate batch normalization statistics for clean, high-frequency adversarial, and low-frequency adversarial samples to prevent mutual interference. A theoretical guarantee (Theorem 1) shows that frequency-domain adversarial robustness yields a bounded OOD generalization error, with the bound tightening as the perturbation radius δ increases and the loss sensitivity ε decreases.

## Results

On ModelNet-C, FAT achieves the lowest mean corruption error (mCE) across all four architectures: 0.825 (DGCNN), 1.237 (PointNet), 0.907 (PCT), and 0.850 (GDANet), while maintaining clean overall accuracy (OA) within 0.1–1.0% of vanilla training. When combined with WOLFMix data augmentation, GDANet+FAT reaches a new state-of-the-art mCE of 0.537 on ModelNet-C and 0.555 on ModelNet40-C (with ER_cor of 0.147), surpassing the previous best of 0.635 mCE by PCT+CutMix-R. FAT also generalizes to the real-world ScanObjectNN-C benchmark and the modern PointNeXt architecture, consistently outperforming vanilla training, adversarial training, and DUP Defense.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FAT consistently outperforms DUP Defense [93] on mCE across all four architectures (e.g., 0.825 vs. 0.905 for DGCNN, 1.237 vs. 1.246 for PointNet), while DUP Defense suffers a larger clean OA drop (e.g., 0.906 vs. 0.925 for DGCNN). Against standard adversarial training, FAT achieves lower mCE in every setting (e.g., 0.825 vs. 0.926 for DGCNN, 0.907 vs. 0.976 for PCT). When combined with spatial-domain data augmentations, FAT provides consistent gains: RSMix+FAT reduces mCE from 1.276 to 1.084 (PointNet) and from 0.680 to 0.617 (GDANet); PointWOLF+FAT reduces mCE from 1.311 to 0.993 (PointNet); WOLFMix+FAT reduces mCE from 0.601 to 0.537 (GDANet). On ModelNet40-C, GDANet+WOLFMix+FAT achieves 0.555 mCE, beating the previous SOTA of PCT+CutMix-R [90] (0.635 mCE) by 0.080. On ScanObjectNN-C, FAT+AdaptPoint [62] improves mCE from 1.256 to 1.196 (PointNet) and from 0.783 to 0.761 (PointNeXt), confirming that FAT is complementary to existing sample-adaptive augmentation strategies.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 2D image frequency-domain robustness analysis (Yin et al., 2019) to 3D point clouds by introducing the graph Fourier transform (GFT) as the natural frequency-domain tool for irregular point cloud data. It departs from the dominant spatial-domain data augmentation paradigm (PointMixup, PointCutMix, RSMix, PointWOLF, WOLFMix) by instead performing adversarial training in the frequency domain, generating separate high- and low-frequency perturbations. The training procedure builds directly on the AdvProp framework (Xie et al., 2020) with three separate BN streams, and leverages PGD and AOF for perturbation generation. A novel GFT-based Jacobian sensitivity metric is introduced to diagnose which frequency bands a model is vulnerable to, linking model internals to corruption robustness.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a theoretical OOD generalization bound (Theorem 1) connecting frequency-domain adversarial robustness to out-of-distribution performance, giving practitioners a principled justification for frequency-domain adversarial training. The demonstrated complementarity between FAT and spatial-domain augmentation (e.g., WOLFMix) suggests that combining frequency- and spatial-domain regularization is a productive direction for robust 3D perception. Limitations include a small but consistent clean-accuracy drop (0.1–1.0%) and the reliance on a fixed k-NN graph and frequency split (λ=100) that may not be optimal across all point cloud distributions. Open questions include whether the approach extends to segmentation/detection tasks, whether adaptive frequency splits or learned graph structures could further improve results, and whether the GFT-based sensitivity metric can guide architecture design.
