## Problem

Existing logit-based methods for unsupervised accuracy estimation (e.g., nuclear norm of the softmax matrix) suffer from overconfidence under poorly-calibrated scenarios, leading to biased and unreliable estimates especially under natural distribution shifts. The paper asks two questions: what explains the correlation between logits and generalization performance, and how to mitigate the overconfidence that degrades logit-based estimators when the model is miscalibrated.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241321/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

MANO takes a pre-trained classifier (ResNet, WRN, ViT, or ConvNeXt) and an unlabeled test set of RGB images, requiring no access to training data or test labels (source-free, label-free regime). It produces a single scalar score S(f, D_test) ∈ [0,1] computed as the entry-wise L_p norm (p=4) of the N×K normalized prediction matrix, which is designed to be linearly correlated with true OOD accuracy. The score is scale-invariant to dataset size N and number of classes K, and is computationally cheaper than nuclear-norm methods since it avoids SVD.

## Method

MANO operates in two steps: (1) a data-dependent normalization called softrun that maps raw logits to the probability simplex Δ_K, and (2) aggregation via the L_p norm of the resulting prediction matrix. Softrun selects between a 2nd-order Taylor polynomial (1 + q + q²/2) and the full exponential (softmax) based on a dataset-level calibration criterion Φ(D_test) — the negative average log-softmax, proportional to KL divergence from the uniform distribution. When Φ ≤ η (model uncertain/poorly calibrated), Taylor truncation limits error accumulation; when Φ > η (model confident), full softmax preserves logit information. Theoretically, the MANO score is shown to equal a constant minus a scaled average Tsallis p-entropy of the normalized predictions (Theorem 3.3), establishing a direct link between the score and model uncertainty: higher entropy (more uncertainty) yields a lower score, which corresponds to lower true accuracy.

## Results

MANO achieves near-perfect estimation under synthetic shift (average R² = 0.995 on CIFAR-10, 0.977 on CIFAR-100, 0.962 on TinyImageNet, 0.988 on ImageNet) and subpopulation shift (R² ≥ 0.977 across all four BREEDS datasets). Under the most challenging natural shift, MANO achieves average R² of 0.891 (PACS), 0.854 (Office-Home), 0.899 (DomainNet), and 0.977 (RR1-WILDS), with ρ consistently above 0.91. These results are obtained across ResNet18, ResNet50, and WRN-50-2, and extend to ViT and ConvNeXt architectures.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MANO outperforms all 11 baselines across all 12 benchmarks and three shift types. The most dramatic gains appear under natural shift, where the previous state-of-the-art Nuclear norm (Deng et al., 2023) drops to R² = 0.609 on PACS and 0.750 on Office-Home, while MANO reaches 0.891 and 0.854 respectively—a relative improvement of ~46% and ~14%. Under synthetic shift, MANO achieves R² > 0.96 on all four datasets, marginally ahead of Nuclear (0.939–0.972) and clearly ahead of simpler baselines like ConfScore (0.840–0.981) and Entropy (0.916–0.981). Under subpopulation shift, MANO reaches R² ≥ 0.977 with ρ ≥ 0.982, while Nuclear achieves 0.947–0.985 and Dispersion 0.839–0.949. The paper further shows that equipping Nuclear with the proposed softrun normalization (instead of softmax) improves its R² from 0.692 to 0.826 on poorly-calibrated Office-Home, confirming that the normalization strategy is the key differentiator. Methods requiring retraining (Rotation, AgreeScore) or access to training data (Fréchet) are consistently weaker under natural shift, with Fréchet dropping to R² < 0.1 on PACS and DomainNet.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the logit-based unsupervised accuracy estimation line (ConfScore, Nuclear norm, ATC, Entropy) by providing a geometric interpretation of why logits correlate with generalization: they encode distances to decision boundaries under the low-density separation assumption borrowed from semi-supervised learning. It departs from prior methods primarily in its normalization strategy—showing that the standard softmax accumulates prediction errors under poor calibration and proposing softrun, a calibration-adaptive normalization that switches between Taylor approximation and exact exponential. The L_p norm aggregation is a simpler, SVD-free alternative to the nuclear norm used by Deng et al. (2023), and the theoretical link to Tsallis entropy provides a formal uncertainty interpretation absent in prior logit-based estimators.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MANO offers a practical, training-free accuracy estimator that is computationally cheaper than nuclear-norm methods and robust across synthetic, natural, and subpopulation shifts, making it attractive for real-world model monitoring where labels are unavailable. The softrun normalization is a transferable technique that demonstrably improves other logit-based methods (e.g., boosting Nuclear's R² from 0.692 to 0.826 on poorly-calibrated Office-Home), suggesting broader utility beyond this specific estimator. Key limitations include the fixed hyperparameter η for the calibration threshold and the assumption that the LDS condition holds; future work on automatic, hyperparameter-free normalization selection and on settings where the LDS assumption breaks down (e.g., adversarial or semantic shifts) remain open.
