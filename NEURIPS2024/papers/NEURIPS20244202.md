## Problem

Existing open-vocabulary scene graph generation methods rely on scene-agnostic text classifiers (category names or part-level prompts) that remain fixed across all contexts. These classifiers struggle to model the high visual variance of relations (e.g., 'on' looks very different for 'dog on bed' vs. 'people on road') and can produce misleading descriptions irrelevant to the presented image, since LLMs generate them without considering specific scene content.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image (resized to dimensions in [600, 1000]) along with pre-detected object bounding boxes and their category labels, and outputs a relation classification score for each subject-object pair. The visual features are extracted by cropping each object's bounding box and passing it through CLIP's frozen ViT-B/32 visual encoder, while text features come from CLIP's text encoder processing 42 scene-specific descriptions (21 raw-opposite pairs). The final prediction is the relation category with the highest self-normalized similarity score.

## Method

SDSGG replaces fixed text classifiers with scene-specific descriptions (SSDs) generated via multi-persona collaboration, where GPT-3.5 plays distinct expert roles (biologist, physicist, engineer) to produce diverse, comprehensive scene descriptions from multiple viewpoints. Each SSD is paired with an LLM-generated opposite description, and the self-normalized similarity (difference between cosine similarities of the image to the original and opposite descriptions) adaptively reweights each classifier so that contextually irrelevant descriptions contribute near-zero signal. On the visual side, a Mutual Visual Adapter (MVA) uses cross-attention to project CLIP's subject and object patch features into a low-dimensional interaction-aware space, capturing the complicated interplay between the two entities, with a directional marker (text embeddings of 'subject'/'object') to preserve geometric relation orientation. Training employs a contrastive loss that pulls self-normalized similarities toward association targets (C_r^n ∈ {1, 0, -1}) while a margin term regularizes MVA outputs toward original CLIP predictions to prevent overfitting on the base split.

## Results

On the VG semantic split (24 predicate categories), SDSGG achieves 21.5/29.3/34.9 R@20/50/100 and 16.8/22.7/28.4 mR@20/50/100, surpassing the strongest baseline RECODE★ by 10.9/11.0/9.9 points on R@K. On the VG base/novel split, SDSGG reaches 26.5/31.6 R@50/100 (base) and 25.4/29.6 R@50/100 (novel), outperforming Epic by 3.9/4.4 and 18.0/19.9 points respectively. On GQA, SDSGG achieves 43.9/49.5 R@50/100 on the base split and 37.4/42.9 R@50/100 on the novel split, substantially exceeding the CLS baseline (6.4/7.9 and 28.3/32.1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CLS (CLIP zero-shot with category-level prompts) [4], SDSGG wins by a large margin on all splits: 16.6–27.7% R@K on VG base, 5.2–7.3% R@K on VG novel, and 29.2–41.6% R@K on GQA base. Against Epic [3] (entangled cross-modal prompt with contrastive learning), SDSGG outperforms on both VG base (26.5 vs. 22.6 R@50) and novel (25.4 vs. 7.4 R@50), with the novel-split gap of 18.0% R@50 highlighting Epic's overfitting to the training split. Against RECODE and its variants [12] (part-level prompts with composite visual cues) on the VG semantic split, SDSGG exceeds the best variant RECODE★ by 10.9/11.0/9.9 points on R@20/50/100 and 6.1/4.0/0.6 points on mR@20/50/100, without relying on additional data augmentation or filtering strategies. The consistent and substantial margins across all three baselines and both datasets demonstrate that scene-specific descriptions with self-normalized similarity and the mutual visual adapter provide a clear advantage over fixed, scene-agnostic text classifiers.

## Relation to Prior Work

SDSGG extends the CLIP-based zero-shot pipeline for open-vocabulary scene graph generation by replacing the fixed, scene-agnostic text classifiers (category names or part-level prompts used by Epic and RECODE) with scene-specific descriptions generated through LLM multi-persona collaboration. It builds directly on Menon & Vondrick's (ICLR 2023) idea of using LLM-generated descriptions as VLM text classifiers, but departs from that line by introducing a self-normalized similarity mechanism (pairing each description with its opposite) and a mutual visual adapter with cross-attention to model subject-object interplay, rather than treating all generated descriptions as equally valid affirmative classifiers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a concrete and previously under-examined limitation of OVSGG: fixed text classifiers cannot adapt to the high variance in visual relations across contexts, and some LLM-generated descriptions may be irrelevant or misleading for a given image. The self-normalized similarity mechanism and multi-persona description generation offer a practical, training-light recipe that practitioners can adopt. However, the method is limited to predicate classification (objects are given), relies on GPT-3.5 for description generation, and the multi-persona design is acknowledged as an early attempt with open questions about role selection and description completeness. Extending the framework to full scene graph generation (including object detection) and to other relation detection tasks remains future work.
