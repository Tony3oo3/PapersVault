## Problem

Existing BEV map layout estimation methods rely on constructing dense BEV features, which are inaccurate in occluded or low-visibility regions, producing incoherent maps with numerous artifacts. The key unresolved question is how to align perspective-view (PV) image features with generative models that encode map prior knowledge, so that corrupted or invisible areas can be filled in coherently rather than hallucinated from noisy dense features.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242251/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-camera surround-view images (6 cameras at 256×704 for nuScenes, 7 cameras at 600×960 for Argoverse) along with camera-LiDAR calibration, or a single monocular image (600×800) for the monocular variant. It first produces a sparse 25×25 grid of BEV token indices (each drawn from a 256-entry codebook of 128-dim vectors), which are then decoded into a 200×200 pixel BEV semantic map at 0.5 m/pixel (surround-view) or 0.25 m/pixel (monocular) with binary segmentation across 11 layout classes.

## Method

VQ-Map is a two-stage pipeline. Stage 1 trains a VQ-VAE-like model that patchifies groundtruth BEV maps (8×8 patches), embeds them via three 3×3 convolutions, quantizes against a learned codebook (K=256, D=128) using L2-normalized nearest-neighbor search, and reconstructs the map through a 5-layer residual decoder, yielding a codebook embedding that encodes high-level BEV semantics as prior knowledge. Stage 2 trains a token decoder (8 deformable cross-attention layers, 512-dim, SwinT-tiny + FPN backbone) that uses learnable 3D reference points projected into image space to query sparse backbone features and predict BEV tokens via focal-loss classification, entirely bypassing dense BEV feature construction. At inference, predicted tokens are mapped through the frozen codebook embedding and decoded into the final BEV map, with the codebook serving as an off-the-shelf bridge between PV perception and BEV generation.

## Results

On the surround-view BEV map layout estimation task on nuScenes, VQ-Map achieves a new state-of-the-art 62.2 mIoU, surpassing the previous best DDP (59.4) by 2.8 mIoU and BEVFusion (56.6) by 5.6 mIoU. For the monocular task, VQ-Map reaches 47.6 mIoU on nuScenes (vs. GitNet's 45.2) and 73.4 IoU on Argoverse (vs. TaDe's 68.3), setting new records in both settings. Notably, the Argoverse results use the off-the-shelf codebook embedding and decoder trained on nuScenes, demonstrating strong cross-dataset transferability.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VQ-Map outperforms all 15 compared baselines across both surround-view and monocular settings. In the surround-view nuScenes comparison (Table 1), VQ-Map leads DDP [19] (59.4) by 2.8 mIoU, MetaBEV [35] (59.3) by 2.9, X-Align [34] (58.0) by 4.2, and BEVFusion [1] (56.6) by 5.6, with the largest per-class gain being +10.3 on Stop Line over BEVFusion. Among generative-model-based competitors, MapPrior [17] (56.7) and DDP [19] (59.4) are the closest, but both rely on dense BEV features or diffusion decoders that VQ-Map avoids. In the monocular setting (Table 2), VQ-Map beats GitNet [42] (45.2 mIoU on nuScenes, 67.1 on Argoverse) by 2.4 and 6.3 respectively, and TaDe [16] (45.0, 68.3) by 2.6 and 5.1. Earlier baselines such as LSS [3] (44.4), OFT [36] (42.1), CVT [37] (40.2), VED [40] (25.2), VPN [41] (31.9), PON [39] (34.5), and DiffBEV [20] (44.1) are all surpassed by wide margins (13–22 mIoU). The paper also reports that VQ-Map's tiny variant (44.2M params, 86.8G MACs) already matches DDP's 59.4 mIoU while using roughly 7× fewer MACs, highlighting a favorable accuracy–efficiency tradeoff.

## Relation to Prior Work

VQ-Map departs from the dominant BEV map estimation paradigm (LSS, BEVFusion, MetaBEV) that constructs dense BEV features via depth-based lifting or cross-view attention, and instead repurposes the VQ-VAE discrete representation framework to encode groundtruth BEV maps into sparse tokens with a learned codebook. It also differentiates itself from prior generative BEV approaches (MapPrior, DDP, DiffBEV) that still require accurate dense BEV features for PV-BEV alignment, by using the codebook indices themselves as classification targets for a deformable-attention token decoder. The token decoder architecture draws directly from Deformable DETR's sparse query mechanism, while the generation decoder follows VQ-GAN's residual-block design, making the overall pipeline a fusion of discrete representation learning and sparse transformer-based perception.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The codebook embedding functions as a reusable, off-the-shelf bridge between perspective and bird's-eye-view spaces, demonstrated by zero-shot transfer from nuScenes to Argoverse without retraining the generative stage. The ablation showing that sparse-feature token classification (focal loss) consistently outperforms dense-feature latent regression (MSE) for PV-BEV alignment offers a practical design principle for future BEV perception pipelines. Key limitations include poor handling of position-sensitive small-area semantics (e.g., stop lines) due to patch-level tokenization, and the two-stage training overhead. Open directions the authors identify include token-based multi-task modeling for autonomous driving and integration with large language models for richer scene understanding.
