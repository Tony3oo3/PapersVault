## Problem

Existing VLM benchmarks rely on fixed, curated question sets and reference-based metrics that fail to capture how diverse human users actually interact with vision-language models in open-ended, multi-round conversations. This creates a significant gap between benchmark rankings and real-world user preferences, making it difficult to track which models genuinely perform best in practical multimodal scenarios.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241527/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Users upload arbitrary RGB images and type free-form text prompts, engaging in multi-round chats where two randomly sampled VLMs respond simultaneously; users then cast a 4-way vote (left, right, tie, both bad) with optional written justification. The system produces two complementary outputs: (1) a live Elo leaderboard computed via Bradley-Terry maximum likelihood over all pairwise votes, and (2) a fast-evaluation score on WV-BENCH where GPT-4o judges each model's answer against a fixed Claude-3-Sonnet reference using a 5-level scale (Much Better / Better / Tie / Worse / Much Worse), yielding a single scalar score with 95% confidence interval per model.

## Method

WILDVISION-ARENA is a crowdsourced, chatbot-style platform hosting 20+ VLMs (GPT-4o, GPT-4V, Gemini-Pro, Reka-Flash, LLaVA-NEXT, etc.) where users conduct side-by-side multi-turn image chats and vote; ratings are estimated via the Bradley-Terry model with tie votes duplicated and split to handle the binary logistic framework. From 20,000+ in-the-wild conversations and 8,000+ votes, the authors curate WILDVISION-BENCH by applying NSFW filtering, image deduplication, and diversity sampling to select 500 high-quality samples. For automatic evaluation, they adapt the AlpacaEval paradigm to the visual domain: GPT-4o acts as judge, first generating its own answer, then comparing each candidate VLM's response against the Claude-3-Sonnet reference on a 5-level scale. This reference-model-based pairwise scoring achieves a 0.94 Spearman correlation with the arena's human Elo ratings, substantially outperforming fixed benchmarks (MMVet, MMMU, MMStar, MMBench) in alignment with human preferences, demonstrating that in-the-wild data with a strong reference model captures real-world capability gaps that static benchmarks miss.

## Results

WILDVISION-BENCH achieves a Spearman correlation of 0.94 with WILDVISION-ARENA Elo ratings (human preferences), significantly outperforming other benchmarks like MMVet, MMMU, and MMStar. On the 500-sample WV-BENCH, GPT-4o scores 89.41 (80.6% win rate vs. Claude-3-Sonnet), GPT-4V scores 80.01 (71.8%), and Reka-Flash scores 64.79 (58.8%). Among open-source models, LLaVA-NEXT-34B leads with a score of 51.91 (49.2% win rate), surpassing Gemini-1.5-Flash and Claude-3-Sonnet on the arena leaderboard. The platform collected 8,076 votes over 102 days across 20+ models, with an average of 1.34 rounds per conversation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is the Spearman correlation between each benchmark's model rankings and the WILDVISION-ARENA Elo (human preference) rankings. WILDVISION-BENCH achieves the highest correlation at 0.94, substantially outperforming MMVet (the runner-up), MMMU, and MMStar. MMBench-series (CCBench, MMBench EN, MMBench CN) focuses on fine-grained perception and reasoning via multiple-choice questions, while HallusionBench targets hallucination detection and MathVista targets mathematical reasoning in visual contexts — all show lower alignment with real-world human preferences than WV-BENCH. The paper also validates GPT-4V as a local evaluator, finding it has high agreement with humans on left/right votes (binary F1 = 0.78) but lower agreement on tie/bad votes (4-way F1 = 0.42). Among the 22 evaluated VLMs, GPT-4o dominates with an 89.41 score, while open-source LLaVA-NEXT-34B (51.91) is the strongest open model, surpassing proprietary Gemini-1.5-Flash and Claude-3-Sonnet on the arena leaderboard.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends the Chatbot Arena paradigm [12]—originally designed for text-only LLMs—to the vision-language domain, adapting the Elo rating system and Bradley-Terry statistical estimation to multimodal pairwise comparisons. For automatic evaluation, it repurposes the AlpacaEval [44] reference-model comparison framework (using GPT-4o as judge against Claude-3-Sonnet) to the visual context, filling a gap the authors identify between fixed, reference-based VLM benchmarks (MMVet, MMMU, MMStar, MMBench) and real-world human preferences. The key conceptual departure is the 'in-the-wild' data curation: rather than constructing a static test set, WV-BENCH is sampled from 20K+ actual user submissions, making it a dynamic, contamination-resistant evaluation that no prior VLM benchmark attempts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work provides the first large-scale, human-preference-aligned evaluation infrastructure for VLMs, analogous to Chatbot Arena's role for text LLMs, and the 0.94 Spearman correlation between the 500-sample WV-BENCH and the full arena Elo demonstrates that fast automatic evaluation can faithfully proxy crowd-sourced human judgment. The 20K+ interaction dataset reveals concrete failure modes (spatial reasoning, subtle contextual cues, expert domain knowledge, hallucination under provocation) that static benchmarks systematically miss, giving researchers actionable targets. Open questions include the platform's scalability, the bias toward English-speaking and research-interested users, and whether the hidden-set rotation adequately prevents contamination as models are updated. The significant Elo gap between the best open-source model (LLaVA-NEXT-34B at 1059) and GPT-4o (1235) underscores that the open-source VLM ecosystem still lags substantially behind proprietary systems in real-world generalization.
