## Problem

Large Multimodal Models possess broad but generic visual knowledge (e.g., recognizing 'a dog') and cannot handle personalized, user-specific subjects (e.g., recognizing 'my dog named <bo>'). This paper introduces the novel task of personalizing LMMs so they can recognize and converse about a specific subject given only a handful of unlabeled images, without degrading the model's pre-trained general capabilities.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241294/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

During training, the method consumes a small set of unlabeled images (typically 5) of a single subject plus ~200 negative images (100 hard negatives retrieved via CLIP similarity from LAION, 100 easy random negatives). At inference, the model accepts either an RGB image paired with a text question referencing the subject by its identifier token (e.g., '<sks>'), or a text-only question with no image. Outputs are free-form text responses covering three modes: binary recognition (Yes/No), visual question answering about the subject's location or actions in a new image, and text-only descriptions of the subject's intrinsic visual attributes.

## Method

Yo'LLaVA freezes all pre-trained LLaVA-1.5-13B weights (vision encoder, projector, language model) and introduces only a new identifier token <sks> plus k=16 latent soft-prompt tokens, training just these k+1 input embeddings and the corresponding output classifier row. The subject is represented as the learnable prompt '<sks> is <token_1>...<token_k>', which the model learns to embed fine-grained visual attributes into. Training uses two data streams: (1) recognition QA with positive and hard-negative images (mined via CLIP retrieval from LAION) to teach discriminative recognition, and (2) conversation QA about intrinsic attributes where the reference image is deliberately omitted during training, forcing the model to distill visual knowledge into the soft prompts rather than reading it from the input image. This design achieves 92.4% weighted recognition accuracy and 88.3% text-only QA accuracy with only 16 tokens, outperforming GPT-4V with 5k image tokens while retaining near-identical performance on general benchmarks (POPE, MMBench, LLaVA-Wild).

## Results

On the Yo'LLaVA Personalized Concept Dataset (40 subjects), Yo'LLaVA achieves 0.924 weighted recognition accuracy using only 16 learnable tokens, outperforming LLaVA with a 16-token text prompt (0.819) and GPT-4V with a 1k-token image prompt (0.901). For visual question answering, Yo'LLaVA scores 0.929, and for text-only QA (no reference image), it reaches 0.883, surpassing all text-prompting baselines. Against the concurrent MyVLM system, Yo'LLaVA improves weighted recognition accuracy from 93.8% to 96.4% and recall from 96.0% to 100.0%, without relying on any external face/object recognizer.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against vanilla LLaVA, Yo'LLaVA wins decisively on every metric: weighted recognition jumps from 0.500 to 0.924, visual QA from 0.899 to 0.929, and text-only QA from 0.659 to 0.883. Compared to LLaVA with a 16-token human-written text prompt, Yo'LLaVA improves weighted recognition by ~10 points (0.924 vs. 0.819) and text-only QA by ~8 points (0.883 vs. 0.803), while GPT-4V with a 16-token text prompt slightly edges out Yo'LLaVA on visual QA (0.932 vs. 0.929). Against GPT-4V with image prompting, Yo'LLaVA matches the 5k-token setting on weighted recognition (0.924 vs. 0.925) using only 16 tokens (~1/300th the token budget), and substantially outperforms the 1k-token image prompt (0.924 vs. 0.901); however, GPT-4V with 5 reference images still leads on text-only QA (0.987 vs. 0.883) since all intrinsic details are directly visible in the provided images. Against the concurrent MyVLM system, Yo'LLaVA achieves higher weighted recognition (96.4% vs. 93.8%) and perfect recall (100.0% vs. 96.0%) while eliminating the need for external face/object recognizers and training captions.

## Relation to Prior Work

Yo'LLaVA builds directly on LLaVA-1.5-13B as a frozen backbone, drawing on the soft prompt tuning lineage (prefix-tuning, ToolkenGPT, visual prompt tuning) to represent a personalized subject as a small set of learnable latent tokens plus an identifier token. It departs from text-based prompting approaches (which the authors show are lossy for fine-grained visual details) and from concurrent work MyVLM, which relies on external face/object recognizers, by keeping the entire personalization within the LMM itself. Hard negative mining from the metric learning literature is repurposed to prevent over-generalization when only a handful of positive images are available.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes personalization of LMMs for user-specific visual concepts as a distinct task and provides a lightweight, forgetting-free recipe (16 tokens, single-GPU training) that enables recognition, visual QA, and text-only conversation about a subject. Practical impact is clear for applications like health tracking, education, and entertainment where generic LMM knowledge is insufficient. Key limitations include imperfect fine-grained recognition (especially small faces), inherited hallucinations, and a small 40-subject evaluation set that limits generalizability claims. Open questions include scaling to many simultaneous subjects, integrating structured user metadata, and whether the learned tokens can be composed or transferred across related concepts.
