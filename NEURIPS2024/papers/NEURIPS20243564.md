## Problem

Despite the proliferation of task-agnostic decoupling techniques for privacy-preserving ML, no standardized benchmark exists to systematically compare their privacy-utility trade-offs, particularly in the context of synthetic data generation. Existing studies evaluate decoupling methods in isolation with inconsistent protocols, making cross-method comparison difficult and hindering progress in the field.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243564/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The pipeline takes 128×128 face images from FairFace or UTKFace along with structured text prompts (e.g., 'A {Age} year-old {Race} {Gender}') as conditioning. It produces synthetic face images where the sensitive attribute (race) is anonymized while non-sensitive attributes (age, gender) are preserved, plus a suite of evaluation metrics: FID scores for image quality, and ResNet18 classification accuracies for both privacy leakage (race) and utility (age, gender) on held-out test splits. The framework defines four structured data portions (Tr, V, G, Test) with multi-label stratification to ensure fair evaluation.

## Method

The framework fine-tunes Stable Diffusion v1-5 with LoRA adapters (rank 4, ~4.5% of total parameters) inserted at the output of every cross-attention block in the conditional UNet backbone. During training, LoRA features are adaptively average-pooled across spatial dimensions and fed to a proxy adversary; the min-max objective simultaneously minimizes the diffusion MSE loss (utility) and maximizes the adversary's classification loss (privacy). Three decoupling strategies are integrated: cross-entropy (CE) with a fully connected adversary head, CE+Mask with a Filter Generating Network that produces binary channel masks to selectively zero out sensitive information, and Metric Learning that swaps positive/negative pairs in a triplet margin loss to collapse the sensitive-attribute subspace. Generation uses an image-to-image pipeline (strength 0.75, guidance 7.5) conditioned on the original image and attribute prompt, with LoRA weights fully fused into the base model. Evaluation trains separate ResNet18 classifiers on the synthetic G split to measure privacy (race accuracy, ideally ~20% for 5 classes) and utility (age/gender accuracy), complemented by a Pretrained (PT) protocol that runs inference on real images to isolate decoupling from generation artifacts.

## Results

On the primary Setup A (FairFace, same-dataset train/test), CE+Mask achieves the strongest privacy with a Pretrained (PT) race-classification accuracy of 14.18%—below the 20% random-guess baseline for 5 race classes—while retaining utility close to the real-data baseline (Age 34.44% vs. 36.60%, Gender 68.77% vs. 72.94%). CE achieves PT privacy of 19.27% with comparable utility (Age 33.81%, Gender 70.47%). A key finding is that PT evaluation alone overestimates privacy: the CLS-based privacy metric (trained on generated images) remains at 37–46% across all methods, revealing information leakage during the diffusion generation process. Adding the V split (V+G) increases classifier training data, widening the privacy gap between methods and the baseline (e.g., CE+Mask CLS drops from 37.07% to 38.22% while baseline rises to 55.86%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper benchmarks three decoupling methods—CE and CE+Mask (adapted from DISCO, Singh et al. 2020) and Metric Learning (adapted from Chopra et al. 2022)—against a real-data baseline. CE+Mask consistently achieves the best PT privacy across all setups (14.18% on FairFace, 7.67% on UTKFace, 13.69% on cross-dataset), often below the 20% random-guess level for 5 race classes, while CE trails at 19–26% PT accuracy. On utility, CE+Mask and CE are closely matched, with CE+Mask slightly ahead in the V+G setting (Age 35.30% vs. 34.44% on FairFace). Metric Learning, which lacks a PT evaluation, shows competitive CLS privacy (37.49% in V+G FairFace) but suffers a larger utility drop on Age (28.72% vs. 35.30% for CE+Mask). The paper's central empirical finding is that no single method dominates: CE+Mask wins on privacy, Metric Learning occasionally wins on gender utility, and the gap between PT and CLS privacy metrics (10–25 points) highlights that generation-time leakage is a critical, previously unmeasured factor in decoupling evaluation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper integrates three established adversarial decoupling techniques—cross-entropy decoupling, FGN-based channel masking (from DISCO [47]), and metric-learning-based obfuscation (from Chopra et al. [11])—into a Latent Diffusion Model (Stable Diffusion v1.5) fine-tuned via LoRA adapters, a combination none of the original decoupling studies had explored. The core task of separating sensitive from non-sensitive attributes in a representation is well-studied in the adversarial representation learning literature, but the paper's contribution is wrapping these methods in a unified synthetic-image-generation pipeline with standardized data splits, evaluation classifiers, and FID-based quality metrics. It positions itself as the missing systematic benchmark: prior decoupling papers each evaluate their own method on their own protocol, making cross-method comparison infeasible.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals a practically important gap: pretrained (PT) privacy metrics can report near-random accuracy (~20% for 5 race classes) while a classifier trained on the generated data still achieves substantially higher accuracy, indicating that the non-LoRA pretrained layers in the diffusion model leak sensitive information during the generation step. This cautions against relying on a single privacy metric and highlights that the generation pipeline itself is an attack surface. Limitations include the small LoRA parameter budget (~4.5%) that cannot fully shift the generation distribution (FID scores of 17–37), the absence of formal privacy guarantees, and evaluation on only two face datasets. Open questions the authors flag include optimal LoRA layer placement for the privacy-utility trade-off, integration of stronger anonymization (e.g., GDPR-compliant methods), and adversarial attack/defense studies.
