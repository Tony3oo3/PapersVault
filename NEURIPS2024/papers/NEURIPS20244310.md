## Problem

Existing part-level 3D datasets are severely limited in object category coverage (ShapeNetPart: 16, PartNet: 24, 3DCoMPaT: 42 categories) and lack the combination of fine-grained part annotations, material properties, and compositional structure needed for rich 3D understanding. No prior resource simultaneously provides hundreds of object categories, over 1,000 part classes, and 293 material classes with rendered multi-view imagery and exact text-3D alignment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20244310/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For classification and segmentation, models consume 2048-point XYZ point clouds (or 2048-point RGB clouds for material segmentation) and output per-point part masks (fine or coarse), material labels (13 coarse classes), or shape class labels. For the Compositional Shape Retrieval task, the input is a template caption describing up to 6 parts with material and GPT-4-generated color descriptions (constrained to 77 OpenCLIP tokens) matched against 8,196-point colored point cloud embeddings, producing a ranked list of candidate shapes. Each shape in the dataset also ships 8 rendered views with corresponding depth, part, and material maps.

## Method

The primary contribution is the 3DCoMPaT200 dataset: 19k artist-designed CAD shapes across 200 categories, annotated with 1,031 fine-grained and 118 coarse-grained part labels, 293 fine-grained and 13 coarse-grained material classes, and up to 1,000 material-shape compositions per shape. For benchmarking, the paper evaluates six point-cloud architectures (PointNet++, PCT, CurveNet, PointBert, PointTransformer V2/V3) under supervised training on shape classification, part/material segmentation (with and without a shape-class prior embedding), and Grounded Compositional Recognition metrics. The novel Compositional Shape Retrieval task trains ULIP, Uni3D, and OpenShape using contrastive learning over 10 compositions per shape, where template captions are auto-generated from part-material metadata augmented with GPT-4 color descriptions; retrieval performance is measured at 1, 3, and 6 parts to quantify how compositional detail improves shape identification.

## Results

On the 3DCoMPaT200 dataset, PointTransformer V3 achieves the highest shape classification accuracy of 88.48% (instance) and 89.79% (class-average), while PCT leads in fine-grained part segmentation with 81.83% mIoU (with shape prior) and material segmentation with 98.88% mIoU. In the Grounded Compositional Recognition (GCR) benchmark, PointTransformer V3 attains the best Grounded-Value-All scores of 42.80% (fine-grained) and 72.91% (coarse-grained). For the novel Compositional Shape Retrieval task, Uni3D achieves the highest R@1 of 59.4% with 6-part descriptions, with all models showing substantial improvement from 1-part (23–27% R@1) to 6-part (55–59% R@1) configurations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PointTransformer V3 dominates the GCR benchmark, outperforming PointBert (the second-best) by 12.2 points in fine-grained Grounded-Value-All (42.80% vs. 29.21%) and by 6.1 points in coarse-grained (72.91% vs. 66.80%). PCT is the strongest in part segmentation mIoU (81.83% fine-grained with prior) and material segmentation (98.88% mIoU), surpassing PointTransformer V3 (61.37% and 98.54% respectively) in these specific tasks. PointNet++ is competitive in shape classification (88.32% vs. 88.48% for PTv3) and achieves the second-best coarse-grained GCR Grounded-Value-All (58.49%), but trails in fine-grained settings. CurveNet consistently underperforms, particularly in material segmentation (74.26% mIoU fine-grained vs. 98.88% for PCT) and coarse-grained GCR (27.32% Grounded-Value-All). For the novel Compositional Shape Retrieval task, Uni3D leads with 59.4% R@1 (6 parts), edging out OpenShape (56.62%) and ULIP (54.8%), though all three show a clear scaling trend with increasing part descriptions (e.g., ULIP improves from 23.04% to 54.8% R@1 going from 1 to 6 parts).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper is a direct scale-up of the 3DCoMPaT (ECCV 2022) and 3DCoMPaT++ (arXiv 2023) datasets, expanding object categories from 42 to 200 and shapes from ~7.2K to 19K while retaining the same part-material annotation pipeline and GCR evaluation metrics. For the retrieval experiments, it builds on the text-3D alignment paradigm established by ULIP, Uni3D, and OpenShape, but departs from prior work by generating captions from structured compositional metadata (part names, material styles, GPT-4-derived color descriptions) rather than from generic image-caption models like BLIP-2. The key conceptual departure is the introduction of Compositional Shape Retrieval, which conditions retrieval on a variable number of part descriptions (1, 3, or 6) to probe compositional understanding, a formulation absent from prior text-3D retrieval benchmarks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dataset addresses a concrete gap: prior part-level 3D datasets (ShapeNetPart: 16 categories, PartNet: 24, 3DCoMPaT: 42) offered very limited category coverage, constraining the generalizability of learned part and material representations. By providing 200 categories, 1,031 fine-grained parts, and 293 materials with 304M rendered images, 3DCoMPaT200 enables more realistic compositional 3D understanding and could serve as a foundation for compositional 3D generation (text-to-3D, image-to-3D). However, the long-tail category distribution, the absence of error bars across random seeds, and the reliance on a single composition for most experiments limit the robustness of the empirical claims. Open questions include whether the compositional retrieval gains observed with more parts transfer to open-vocabulary or zero-shot settings, and whether the dataset's CAD-based geometry generalizes to scanned or real-world 3D data.
