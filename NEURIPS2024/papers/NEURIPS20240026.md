## Problem

Modern deep policy gradient methods (SAC, PPO, TD3) rely on large replay buffers, target networks, and batch updates, making them infeasible on resource-constrained robots and edge devices. When forced into incremental learning—processing only the most recent sample without any buffer or batch—they fail catastrophically. No robust incremental deep policy gradient method existed that could learn effectively with deep neural networks in real time.

## Input / Output

The agent receives a continuous state vector (joint angles, velocities, sensor readings) or, for the vision-based Visual Reacher task, three stacked 84×84×3 RGB images concatenated with proprioception. It outputs a continuous action in [-1,1] per dimension via a squashed normal policy (tanh of a Gaussian sample). Critically, the method consumes exactly one transition per update—no stored experience is ever reused—and the observation is normalized online via Welford's running mean/variance before entering the network.

## Method

AVG (Action Value Gradient) is an incremental, on-policy actor-critic algorithm that uses the reparameterization gradient estimator extended with entropy-augmented value functions, updating both a single Q-network and a squashed-normal actor on every single transition. It eliminates replay buffers, target networks, and double Q-learning entirely, replacing them with three stabilization techniques: (1) observation normalization via Welford's online algorithm, (2) penultimate normalization that projects the penultimate-layer features onto the unit sphere (no mean subtraction, unlike layer norm), and (3) temporal-difference error scaling by an online estimate of σ_δ derived from running statistics of rewards, discount factors, and cumulative returns. The actor update backpropagates through the sampled action into the critic (pathwise gradient), while the critic is updated with the scaled TD error. The paper also provides the first convergence analysis for model-free methods using the reparameterization gradient estimator, extending Xiong et al. (2022) from deterministic to general reparameterized policies.

## Results

AVG is the only incremental (buffer-size-1) method that learns effectively across all tested benchmarks, achieving final performance comparable to full SAC (1M replay buffer) on Gymnasium tasks (Ant, Hopper, HalfCheetah, Humanoid, Walker2d) and surpassing SAC on the sparse-reward Dog benchmarks (stand, walk, trot) from DeepMind Control Suite. On real robots, AVG outperforms both SAC-1 and SAC-100 on UR-Reacher-2, and on Create-Mover (Jetson Nano 4GB), AVG requires only ~37 ms per learning update versus ~67 ms for SAC-1, well within the 150 ms action cycle. All other incremental baselines (IAC, SAC-1, TD3-1) fail catastrophically without the proposed normalization and scaling techniques.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against full SAC (1M replay buffer, the standard reference), AVG matches performance on the five Gymnasium continuous-control tasks and surpasses SAC on the three sparse-reward Dog tasks from DeepMind Control Suite, where SAC itself requires non-default layer normalization to perform well. Against PPO and TD3 with reduced replay buffers (Fig. 1), both methods fail catastrophically as buffer size shrinks toward 1, while AVG (which uses no buffer at all) continues to learn. Against the incremental one-step actor-critic (IAC, Sutton & Barto 2018), AVG clearly outperforms the unmodified version, which fails on all deep-network tasks; however, when IAC is augmented with the same observation normalization, penultimate normalization, and TD-error scaling (IAC+), it performs comparably to AVG, suggesting the stabilization techniques are broadly applicable. Against incremental SAC-1 and TD3-1 (buffer and minibatch size set to 1), both fail on all environments; adding normalization and scaling rescues IAC but only partially helps SAC-1 (succeeding on 2 of 5 Gymnasium tasks) and does not help TD3-1 at all. On the two real-robot tasks, AVG is the only method that learns a useful policy under the imposed memory and compute constraints, while SAC-1 and SAC-100 both fail on UR-Reacher-2 and SAC-1 fails on Create-Mover.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the reparameterization gradient (RG) estimator from batch off-policy methods (SAC, TD3) into the incremental on-policy setting, building directly on the reparameterization policy gradient theorem of Lan et al. (2022) and the entropy-regularized actor-critic structure of SAC. It departs from the standard deep RL stabilization recipe—replay buffers, target networks, and batch updates—by showing that observation normalization, penultimate normalization, and TD error scaling can substitute for these components in the incremental regime. The convergence analysis in Appendix I extends Xiong et al. (2022) from deterministic to reparameterized policies, providing the first convergence guarantee for model-free methods using the RG estimator.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AVG demonstrates for the first time that effective deep RL is possible on physical robots using only incremental updates and minimal memory, opening a path toward lifelong on-device learning for resource-constrained platforms like edge robots and Mars rovers. Key limitations include low sample efficiency relative to batch methods, sensitivity to hyperparameters, and restriction to continuous action spaces. Open questions include whether eligibility traces can improve sample efficiency, whether the approach extends to discrete actions, and whether the normalization/scaling recipe generalizes to other incremental algorithms beyond AVG.
