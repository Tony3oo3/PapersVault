## Problem

While prior work has shown that SSL encoders memorize individual training data points and linked this memorization to downstream generalization, it remains unknown where inside the encoder this memorization is localized. Existing localization methods are confined to supervised learning or the language domain, are coarse-grained (per-layer only), or require label information that is unavailable in SSL.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pre-trained SSL encoder (ResNet or ViT), a subset of training images, a reference encoder trained on the same dataset minus one data point, and the training augmentation set. All scores are computed in a single forward pass without gradient computation or labels. For UnitMem, unit activations are averaged across all spatial elements of convolutional feature maps (or across all 65/197 patches in ViTs) and further averaged over 10 random augmentations per data point, yielding a bounded [0,1] score per unit and per layer.

## Method

The paper proposes two complementary metrics. LayerMem normalizes the SSLMem leave-one-out alignment score to [0,1] and averages it over a subset of training points at each layer's output, with a ΔLayerMem variant that subtracts the previous layer's score to isolate each layer's incremental contribution. UnitMem adapts the class-selectivity index from supervised learning by making it label-agnostic, computing it over individual training data points rather than classes, and averaging over the training augmentation set; it measures the ratio of a unit's maximum mean activation (across all data points) to the mean activation over all remaining points, yielding a per-unit memorization score. Both metrics are architecture-agnostic (applicable to CNNs and ViTs), require no downstream task or labels, and are verified by deliberately inserting memorization into specific units and confirming correct detection, as well as by showing that pruning the most memorizing units causes the largest downstream accuracy drop.

## Results

LayerMem scores in a ResNet9 (SimCLR, CIFAR-10) increase from 0.091 at layer 1 to 0.311 at the final residual connection, with ΔLayerMem confirming non-monotonic per-layer contributions (peak ΔLM = 0.034 at layer 6). In ViT-Tiny (MAE, CIFAR-10), fully-connected layers consistently memorize more than attention layers within each block (e.g., block 12: FC LayerMem 0.303 vs. Attention 0.281). Fine-tuning the most memorizing layers (identified via ΔLayerMem) on STL-10 yields 57.9% accuracy, outperforming fine-tuning the last layer (52.7%) and the no-fine-tuning baseline (48.6%). Pruning the top-10% UnitMem units per layer drops CIFAR-10 linear probing to 53.04%, versus 58.09% for random pruning and 62.58% for pruning the least memorizing units.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not benchmark against competing memorization-localization methods but instead validates its metrics against standard practices. For fine-tuning, localizing and fine-tuning the most memorizing layers (via ΔLayerMem) outperforms the standard practice of fine-tuning only the last layer by 5.2 percentage points on STL-10 (57.9% vs. 52.7%). For pruning, removing the most memorizing units (top UnitMem) causes a significantly larger accuracy drop than random pruning (53.04% vs. 58.09% on CIFAR-10 at 10% pruning), confirming that UnitMem correctly identifies functionally important units. Comparing SSL vs. SL memorization patterns, SSL units consistently exhibit higher UnitMem (data-point memorization) than ClassMem across all layers, while SL units show the opposite trend in deeper layers, where class memorization dominates. Across SSL frameworks (SimCLR, DINO, MAE, SimSiam), memorization patterns are remarkably similar for the same architecture and dataset, with LayerMem and UnitMem scores differing by less than 0.01 between frameworks at corresponding layers.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends memorization localization from supervised learning and language transformers to SSL vision encoders. It builds directly on SSLMem (Wang et al., 2024) for quantifying per-data-point memorization without labels, and adapts the class selectivity metric (Morcos et al., 2018) into UnitMem by making it label-agnostic, per-data-point, and augmentation-aware. Prior localization work in SL (Baldock et al., 2021; Stephenson et al., 2020; Maini et al., 2023) either required labels or was confined to per-layer granularity, while language-domain localization (Meng et al., 2022; Zhu et al., 2020) relied on discrete tokens and deletion benchmarks inapplicable to SSL. The key departure is that both LayerMem and UnitMem operate in a single forward pass without gradients or labels, making them practical for large pretrained encoders.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that SSL encoders memorize individual data points across all layers (rather than classes in deeper layers as in SL) reframes how we think about what SSL representations encode and has direct practical implications: fine-tuning the most memorizing layers (identified by LayerMem) outperforms standard last-layer fine-tuning, and pruning least-memorizing units (identified by UnitMem) preserves more downstream accuracy than random pruning. Limitations include reliance on a reference encoder (g) trained without one data point for LayerMem, and the fact that UnitMem identifies which unit memorizes a point but not the causal mechanism. Open questions include whether these localization metrics transfer to generative SSL models (e.g., diffusion) and whether memorization-informed pruning can be made more aggressive without performance loss.
