## Problem

Existing research on Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning in LLMs relies on qualitative assessments of model limitations, leaving two critical gaps: there is no quantitative metric to measure the upper-bound of CoT capability, and there is no principled guidance for optimizing CoT performance. This paper addresses both by introducing a formal Reasoning Boundary Framework that quantifies where a model's reasoning degrades and prescribes how to push that boundary outward.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241739/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework takes a text reasoning prompt, a target LLM, and task-specific difficulty parameters (e.g., number of reasoning steps, maximum multiplication value, number of knowledge entities per hop) as input. It produces a scalar reasoning boundary value determined via binary search on accuracy at predefined thresholds (90% and 10%), a combined RB estimate computed as a weighted harmonic mean of sub-task boundaries with task-specific scaling factors N_i and offsets b_i, a three-way category label (CFRB/PFRB/CIRB), and an optimized MARP prompt that constrains per-step operations and planning depth. The BIGGSM benchmark (610 samples, 1–16 reasoning steps, computation range 6–3e5) is constructed specifically to span the full RB spectrum for measurement.

## Method

The core mechanism defines a Reasoning Boundary B(t|m) as the supremum difficulty d at which model accuracy drops below a threshold K1, then proves via Taylor expansion of the difficulty function that the combined boundary for n sub-tasks follows a weighted harmonic mean: B(t1,...,tn|m) ≈ 1/Σ(Ni/(Bi − bi)). This combination law is empirically validated across arithmetic, mathematical reasoning, multi-hop QA, and multilingual tasks. For optimization, the paper identifies two levers: (1) RB promotion, where Tool Usage drives the calculation boundary to infinity (B(c)→∞) and PoT further improves the planning boundary B*(p)>B(p) by using code's clearer logic; and (2) reasoning path optimization via MARP prompting, which caps per-step operations (e.g., ≤5 basic ops, multiplication <1.5e5) and maximizes computation per step to reduce global planning steps, achieving 80.55% on BIGGSM with PoT+MARP versus 57.00% for vanilla CoT. The framework is validated across 27 models and 5 tasks, with a scaling law showing RB increases with parameter count and data quality.

## Results

On BIGGSM with GPT-3.5-Turbo, the proposed MARP prompting achieves state-of-the-art performance: PoT+MARP reaches 80.55% accuracy (vs. 78.25% for PoT alone) while also reducing input tokens from 657 to 576 and output tokens from 78 to 76. CoT+MARP improves over vanilla CoT by 7.37 points (64.37% vs. 57.00%) with fewer input tokens (614 vs. 780). The framework is validated across 27 models and 5 tasks (arithmetic, mathematical reasoning, multi-hop QA, multilingual math), confirming the existence of reasoning boundaries and the weighted harmonic mean combination law.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PoT+MARP (80.55%) outperforms all four baselines on BIGGSM: it beats CoT (Wei et al., 57.00%) by 23.55 points, Tool Usage (Paranjape et al., 71.64%) by 8.91 points, PoT (Chen et al., 78.25%) by 2.30 points, and Complex-CoT (Fu et al., 59.78%) by 20.77 points. CoT+MARP (64.37%) also surpasses vanilla CoT by 7.37 points and Complex-CoT by 4.59 points, while using fewer input tokens than both. Notably, PoT+MARP achieves the highest accuracy while simultaneously consuming the fewest input tokens (576) and output tokens (76) among all methods, demonstrating that boundary-aware prompting is both more accurate and more efficient. The paper further explains *why* PoT outperforms Tool Usage: PoT improves the planning boundary B(p) beyond what Tool Usage achieves, since code provides clearer logical structure than natural language.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on a line of qualitative boundary analyses for CoT reasoning (Madaan et al. 2023, Wang et al. 2023a, Bi et al. 2024, Feng et al. 2024) that identified CoT limitations but lacked formal quantification. It departs from this lineage by introducing the Reasoning Boundary (RB) as a measurable upper-bound and a weighted-harmonic-mean combination law that unifies multiple sub-capabilities into a single predictive formula. The optimization side draws on PoT (Chen et al. 2023), Tool Usage (Paranjape et al. 2023), Self-Consistency (Wang et al. 2022), and Complex-CoT (Fu et al. 2023), reinterpreting their effectiveness through the RB lens and proposing MARP as a boundary-aware prompting strategy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides the first quantitative metric for CoT upper-bounds and a practical combination law that can guide which sub-capability to target for improvement, potentially reducing the trial-and-error in CoT strategy selection. The finding that all open-source models exhibit CFRB = 0 (complete infeasibility at the 90%-accuracy threshold) while closed-source models do not highlights a concrete, measurable gap in open-model reasoning. Limitations include the reliance on a Taylor-expansion approximation for the combination law (no rigorous error bounds), the small BIGGSM benchmark (610 samples), and the absence of causal analysis among basic RBs, leaving open whether the harmonic-mean structure holds under correlated sub-capabilities or in dynamic, multi-turn settings.
