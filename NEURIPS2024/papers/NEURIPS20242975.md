## Problem

Training-free loss-guided diffusion methods (DPS, DSG, LGD-MC) alternate a single diffusion step with a single constraint gradient step per timestep, which is suboptimal for challenging constraints and degrades sharply under reduced neural function evaluation budgets. There is no principled mechanism to determine how many gradient steps are safe to take at each diffusion level before the proxy constraint objective diverges from the true posterior.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pretrained unconditional diffusion model and a differentiable constraint loss L(x̂₀, y) specified at inference time. For image tasks, the input is a 256×256 degraded observation (4× bicubic downsampled, 128×128 box-masked, or 61×61 Gaussian-blurred) and the output is a full 256×256 restored image. For motion tasks, the diffusion model operates on local joint rotations while constraints are defined in global Cartesian space (root/hand/foot trajectories, obstacle spheres, or inequality conditions on joint heights and angular momentum), producing complete 3D motion sequences.

## Method

Trust Sampling reformulates each diffusion timestep as an independent constrained optimization: minimize the proxy loss L(x̂₀(x′), y) subject to x′ remaining on the state manifold at level t. Rather than a single gradient step, it takes up to J_t = g_trust(t) normalized gradient steps per diffusion level, where the trust schedule (typically a stochastic linear function g_trust(t) = m·t + c) limits iterations more aggressively at high-noise early timesteps where the proxy is less reliable. A second termination criterion monitors the predicted noise magnitude ‖ε_θ(x′, t)‖ and halts optimization if it exceeds a threshold ε_max calibrated from unconstrained base-model samples, preventing the iterate from leaving the learned state manifold. The outer loop follows standard DDIM sampling with a fixed step size w ∈ {0.5, 1.0}, and inequality constraints are handled by clamping violations to zero before composing the mean-squared loss.

## Results

On FFHQ 256×256 (1000 images), Trust Sampling achieves FID of 16.99 for 4× super-resolution, 15.28 for box inpainting, and 21.19 for Gaussian deblurring, outperforming all baselines by 4–12 FID points. LPIPS scores are 0.156 (SR), 0.141 (inpaint), and 0.176 (deblur), again the best or near-best across tasks. On ImageNet 256×256 (100 images), the same pattern holds with FID of 55.24 (SR), 99.87 (inpaint), and 69.49 (deblur). For 3D motion, Trust Sampling achieves the best diversity (21.5 root tracking, 20.4 hand/foot tracking) while maintaining competitive FID (561.6, 694.1) and low constraint violation (0.026 m, 0.038 m).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Trust Sampling substantially outperforms DPS [11] on FID across all three image tasks (e.g., 16.99 vs. 29.48 for SR, a 42% reduction) and on LPIPS (0.156 vs. 0.212 for SR). Against DPS+DSG [58], Trust Sampling wins on FID for SR (16.99 vs. 27.06) and deblurring (21.19 vs. 24.06), and on LPIPS for SR (0.156 vs. 0.193) and deblurring (0.176 vs. 0.194), though DPS+DSG edges out slightly on inpainting LPIPS (0.137 vs. 0.141). LGD-MC [47] performs closest to DPS and is consistently outperformed by Trust Sampling on both FID and LPIPS for SR and deblurring, with the gap widening at n=100 for deblurring (21.19 vs. 27.23 FID). For motion generation, DPS achieves the lowest FID (542.8) but violates constraints by 0.13 m; DSG reduces constraint violation to 0.022 m but degrades FID to 715.1 and diversity to 25.0; Trust Sampling strikes the best balance with FID 561.6, diversity 21.5 (closest to GT's 17.3), and constraint violation 0.026 m.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the training-free loss-guided diffusion paradigm initiated by DPS (Chung et al.) and refined by DSG and LGD-MC, which alternate a single diffusion step with a single constraint gradient step at each timestep. Trust Sampling departs from this fixed alternation by reframing each diffusion level as an independent constrained optimization problem, permitting multiple gradient steps on a proxy constraint function before terminating. The termination is governed by two principled criteria: a trust schedule tied to the variance of the posterior at each diffusion level (derived via a Jensen's inequality bound on the proxy-true objective gap), and an early-stop based on the predicted noise magnitude as a proxy for state-manifold membership. This optimization-centric view subsumes the single-step methods as a special case (J=1) and provides a more flexible algorithmic design space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Trust Sampling offers a practical, training-free recipe that substantially improves constraint satisfaction and generation fidelity over existing guided diffusion methods across both image restoration (super-resolution, inpainting, deblurring) and 3D human motion generation, with the added benefit of reduced NFEs via manifold-boundary early termination. However, the method still relies on manually tuned heuristic parameters (step size w, ε_max, schedule slope/offset) that must be set per base model and task, and the theoretical analysis of the proxy gap is restricted to the scalar case. Open questions include whether more sophisticated optimization techniques (line search, higher-order methods) can further close the gap, whether parameter selection can be automated, and whether the modest improvements observed on motion tasks (where DPS still wins on FID) indicate a domain-specific limitation of the trust-schedule approach.
