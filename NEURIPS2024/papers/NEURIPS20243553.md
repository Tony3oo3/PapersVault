## Problem

Existing coded computing schemes for distributed computation are grounded in algebraic coding theory (e.g., Reed-Solomon, Lagrange codes), which restricts them to specific computation functions like polynomials and matrix multiplication, operates over finite fields causing numerical instability with real-valued data, and imposes rigid recovery thresholds below which decoding fails entirely. These properties are fundamentally misaligned with the approximate, real-valued, and flexible nature of machine learning inference and training workloads, leaving a significant gap between coded computing and general distributed computing.

## Input / Output

The framework takes K raw data points {x_k} ∈ R^d and an arbitrary computing function f: R^d → R^m (e.g., a trained neural network) as input, and produces approximate evaluations f̂(x_k) ≈ f(x_k) for all k. The master node encodes the K points into N coded data points (each a smooth combination of all K raw points) sent to N workers; the decoder then recovers the K outputs from the |F| ≥ N−S responses of non-straggler workers. The entire scheme is non-parametric, requiring only two smoothing hyperparameters (λ_enc, λ_dec) selected via cross-validation, and uses fixed Chebyshev interpolation points for both encoding and decoding layers.

## Method

LeTCC replaces the algebraic coding foundation with a learning-theoretic one by defining an end-to-end mean squared error loss and decomposing it (via AM-GM) into a decoder generalization error term and an encoder training error term. The decoder is designed as a second-order smoothing spline (Kernel Ridge Regression) in the RKHS of second-order Sobolev functions, yielding an explicit closed-form solution via B-spline basis functions. The encoder is then derived by applying the representer theorem to the resulting regularized objective, which couples the encoder's Sobolev norm as a penalty. The paper proves convergence rates of O(S³N⁻³) in the noiseless setting and O(S^{8/5}N^{-3/5}) in the noisy setting, both strictly faster than the state-of-the-art BACC scheme, and validates the framework on LeNet5, RepVGG, and ViT inference tasks across varying straggler counts.

## Results

LeTCC achieves lower RMSE and higher Relative Accuracy than the state-of-the-art BACC across all three model architectures. Specifically, LeTCC reduces RMSE by 15% (LeNet5 on MNIST: 2.18 vs. 2.55), 17% (RepVGG on CIFAR-10: 2.04 vs. 2.44), and 9% (ViT on ImageNet-1K: 0.62 vs. 0.68), while improving RelAcc by 2%, 5%, and 4% respectively. Theoretically, the framework achieves convergence rates of O(S³N⁻³) in the noiseless setting and O(S^{8/5}N^{-3/5}) in the noisy setting, both faster than BACC's upper bound.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is BACC (Jahani-Nezhad & Maddah-Ali, IEEE TPAMI 2022), the state-of-the-art model-agnostic coded computing scheme using Berrut rational interpolation. LeTCC consistently outperforms BACC on both RMSE and RelAcc across all three architectures (LeNet5/MNIST, RepVGG/CIFAR-10, ViT/ImageNet-1K) and across nearly all straggler counts in both low-redundancy (small N/K) and high-redundancy (large N/K) regimes, with the gap widening in the more practical low-redundancy setting. The second comparison is with Lagrange coded computing (Yu et al., AISTATS 2019), included in Appendix D for polynomial computation, where LeTCC also demonstrates superior recovery accuracy. The paper attributes BACC's inferior performance to high-frequency noise in its coded samples, which degrades the approximation quality of the original prediction.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper re-grounds coded computing in learning theory rather than algebraic coding theory. Prior work (Lagrange coded computing [3], BACC [29]) inherits the three-layer encode-compute-decode architecture from Reed-Solomon-style error correction, restricting computations to polynomials or matrix multiplication and operating over finite fields. Here, the encoder and decoder are instead designed as second-order smoothing splines in the RKHS of Sobolev functions, following the kernel ridge regression framework of Wahba [32], which removes the polynomial restriction and yields a numerically stable, model-agnostic scheme for arbitrary computing functions including deep neural networks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The learning-theoretic foundation removes the recovery-threshold failure mode of algebraic codes and provides explicit convergence rates (O(S³N⁻³) noiseless, O(S^{8/5}N^{-3/5}) noisy) that provably beat the state-of-the-art BACC bound. However, the framework currently addresses only straggler mitigation; Byzantine robustness and privacy remain open extensions the authors flag for future work. The encoder optimization involves a non-linear objective that, while solvable under mild assumptions via smoothing splines, may not scale trivially to very high-dimensional inputs or extremely large worker counts, and the theoretical guarantees are stated for one-dimensional functions with multi-dimensional generalization deferred to an appendix.
