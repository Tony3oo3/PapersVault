## Problem

Verifying infinite-time safety of neural network-based cyber-physical systems (NNCSs) remains intractable: open-loop NNV ignores physical dynamics, closed-loop NNV is limited to bounded time horizons, and barrier-certificate approaches have not scaled beyond simple linear controllers. A further barrier is that the verification queries arising from hybrid-system reasoning contain nonlinear polynomial arithmetic with arbitrary propositional structure, while efficient NN verification tools only support linear, normalized constraints.

## Input / Output

The method takes a proven dL contract (a nondeterministic control envelope with an inductive invariant), a piece-wise linear (ReLU) neural network, and variable ranges as input. It produces either a dL proof of infinite-time safety for the NNCS or an exhaustive enumeration of unsafe counterexample regions in the input space. The intermediate verification query is a nonlinear, non-normalized open-loop NNV formula over polynomial real arithmetic with arbitrary logical structure, which is the key departure from standard VNNLIB-format queries.

## Method

VerSAILLE formalizes the NN as a nondeterministic mirror hybrid program in dL and uses ModelPlex to synthesize a controller monitor formula, reducing the infinite-time safety question to an open-loop NNV query (the NN must satisfy the monitor within the envelope's inductive invariant). Mosaic then lifts off-the-shelf linear open-loop NNV tools to handle the resulting polynomial, non-normalized queries through three mechanisms: (1) LINEARIZE, which uses OVERT-based piece-wise linear over/under-approximations to soundly bound nonlinear atoms without replacing them; (2) MOSAIC, a generalization of DPLL(T) that partitions the input space into 'azulejos' (linear polytopes) so that reachability-based tools explore each input region exactly once, avoiding the exponential blowup of naive expansion; and (3) a completeness-preserving filter that generalizes each concrete counterexample to a locally affine region and checks it via SMT solving over at most I free variables, eliminating spurious counterexamples from the linearization.

## Results

On the ACAS X vertical collision avoidance benchmark (6 hidden layers, 45 neurons each, 9 advisories), N3V verifies infinite-time safety for the DNC and DND advisories in 0.35h and 0.28h respectively, while exhaustively enumerating counterexample regions for all six other advisories (e.g., 49,428 CE regions for DES1500 in 5.45h, 5,259 for SDES2500 in 3.66h). For the Adaptive Cruise Control running example, verification or exhaustive counterexample enumeration completes in 47–300 seconds depending on NN size. On the ACAS case study, MOSAIC generates only 19k normalized queries versus up to 39 trillion propositionally feasible queries from naive DNNV-style expansion, and the largest verified NNs contain 270 ReLUs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DNNV's expansion-based normalization [87], MOSAIC achieves a dramatic reduction in query count: for the ACAS case study, naive DNNV-style rewriting may produce up to 39 trillion propositionally feasible queries, while MOSAIC generates only 19k, because it deduplicates reachability computations by grouping output constraints under shared input regions. Against Genin et al.'s tailored ACAS approach [42], N3V provides a fully automated, general-purpose pipeline applicable to any dL-expressible CPS (not just ACAS X), and produces exhaustive counterexample region characterizations rather than simplified control-output verifications. The paper also contrasts with closed-loop NNV tools (e.g., VeriSig, ReachNN), noting that their approximation errors prevent infinite-time guarantees over the full controllable state space, whereas N3V's open-loop + dL approach avoids analyzing plant dynamics entirely. SMT-based approaches (e.g., dReal, Z3) lack the approximation issues but cannot scale to the 270-Neuron NNs analyzed here; N3V's SMT filtering is restricted to small input-dimension subproblems (≤I variables) after the NN is reduced to an affine map, making it tractable.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on three distinct research threads: (1) differential dynamic logic (dL) and KeYmaera X for proving infinite-time safety of abstract control envelopes in hybrid systems, (2) ModelPlex for synthesizing controller monitor formulas that link dL contracts to concrete implementations, and (3) open-loop NNV tools (nnenum, Marabou) that verify linear input-output properties of piece-wise linear NNs. The key departure is the 'nondeterministic mirror' mechanism that formally connects a concrete NN to a dL model, reducing infinite-time NNCS safety to an open-loop NNV query, and the Mosaic framework that lifts linear NNV tools to polynomial constraints with arbitrary logical structure—something no prior NNV tool supported. This positions the work as a fourth strategy alongside open-loop NNV (ignores dynamics), closed-loop NNV (time-bounded), and barrier certificates (limited scalability), rather than an improvement to any single one.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first approach that provides provable infinite-time safety for a concrete NNCS by reusing established control-theory proofs, which is critical for safety-critical deployments (e.g., adaptive cruise control must be safe regardless of trip length). The ability to exhaustively characterize unsafe regions (49,428 counterexample regions for one ACAS advisory) enables targeted retraining or fallback controller design, going well beyond point-wise counterexamples from prior work. Limitations include current restriction to ReLU NNs, scalability ceiling around 270 ReLUs for global properties, and the practical bottleneck of limited availability of NNCS benchmarks that are safe w.r.t. infinite-time horizons. Future work on extending to other activation functions, non-polynomial specifications, and applying Mosaic to neural barrier certificate verification could broaden applicability significantly.
