## Problem

GAN-based scene text editing methods suffer from limited generalization due to constrained model capacity and the 'bucket effect' of divide-and-conquer decomposition, while diffusion-based methods produce undesired style deviations and frequent typos because of weak correlation between text prompts and glyph structures. The paper identifies insufficient prior guidance on both text style and glyph structure as the primary factor impeding faithful editing, and notes the absence of a real-world paired-image benchmark for comprehensive evaluation.

## Input / Output

The method takes a cropped source text image (resized to 256×256, perspective-corrected from a full-size scene photo) and a target text prompt (max 24 characters) as input, and produces an edited text image where the target text is rendered in the source's style (font, color, spatial transformation, stereoscopic effect) while preserving background texture. Unlike inpainting-based competitors that operate on full-size images with masked regions, TextCtrl performs synthesis on the cropped text region and perspective-transforms the result back, enabling editing at arbitrary text scales without quality degradation in unmasked areas.

## Method

TextCtrl decomposes STE into a conditional diffusion generator guided by two explicit priors: (1) a character-level transformer encoder T pre-trained with CLIP loss against a frozen scene text recognizer, using font-variance augmentation across 730 fonts to build a robust glyph structure representation C_struct that replaces key-value in the U-Net's cross-attention modules; and (2) a ViT style encoder S pre-trained via multi-task disentanglement (color transfer via AdaIN, font transfer via Pyramid Pooling, text removal, and segmentation) to extract fine-grained style features C_style injected through skip-connections and the middle block in a ControlNet-style manner. During inference, a Glyph-adaptive Mutual Self-Attention (GaMuSa) mechanism runs a parallel DDIM-inversion reconstruction branch alongside the editing branch, integrating Key-Value features between branches at each denoising step with an adaptive intensity λ computed as the cosine similarity between the intermediate latent (decoded by a text recognizer) and the target text embedding, thereby injecting source-image style prior without additional tuning. Training combines standard denoising loss with a construction loss (perceptual + Gram-matrix style + MSE) and an OCR cross-entropy linguistic loss for end-to-end supervision.

## Results

On the proposed ScenePair benchmark, TextCtrl achieves 84.67% word accuracy (ACC) and 0.936 normalized edit distance (NED) for paired target text, surpassing the best diffusion-based baseline TextDiffuser (51.48% ACC, 0.719 NED) by over 33 percentage points in accuracy. On randomly chosen target text, TextCtrl reaches 66.95% ACC versus 33.99% for TextDiffuser. For style fidelity on ScenePair (cropped text image), TextCtrl attains the best SSIM (37.56×10⁻²), PSNR (14.99), MSE (4.47×10⁻²), and FID (43.78) among all compared methods. On TamperScene, TextCtrl achieves 74.17% ACC and 0.909 NED, slightly below MOSTEL's 76.79% ACC but exceeding it in NED.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TextCtrl decisively outperforms all five baselines on the ScenePair benchmark for both paired and random target text, with the largest gap over TextDiffuser (51.48% → 84.67% ACC, +33.2 pp) and AnyText (51.12% → 84.67% ACC, +33.6 pp). Against GAN-based methods SRNet and MOSTEL, the accuracy improvement is even more pronounced (17.84% and 37.69% respectively), reflecting the limited generalization of GAN architectures. On TamperScene, TextCtrl slightly trails MOSTEL in ACC (74.17% vs. 76.79%) but surpasses it in NED (0.909 vs. 0.858), which the authors attribute to ambiguous low-resolution images in TamperScene that hinder style disentanglement. For style fidelity (Table 1), TextCtrl leads all methods on every metric at both cropped-text and full-image levels, with the most notable margin in FID (43.78 vs. 51.79 for AnyText) and SSIM (37.56 vs. 30.73 for AnyText). The inpainting-based diffusion methods (DiffSTE, TextDiffuser, AnyText) show degraded full-image quality due to artifacts in unmasked regions, whereas TextCtrl's synthesis-based approach avoids this issue.

## Relation to Prior Work

TextCtrl builds on the diffusion-based scene text editing line (DiffSTE, TextDiffuser, AnyText) but departs from their reliance on implicit style cues from unmasked regions or coarse-grained image concatenation. It explicitly decomposes the editing prerequisite into two pre-trained encoders—a character-level glyph structure encoder aligned via CLIP loss with font-variance augmentation, and a multi-task style disentanglement encoder (color, font, removal, segmentation)—rather than treating style as a monolithic signal. The inference-time Glyph-adaptive Mutual Self-Attention mechanism extends MasaCtrl's cross-branch KV substitution with a glyph-similarity-gated integration, and the ScenePair benchmark fills a gap left by prior evaluations that relied on synthetic data or lacked paired real-world images.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ScenePair dataset (1,280 real-world image pairs with full-size context) is the first to enable simultaneous style-fidelity and rendering-accuracy evaluation on real images, addressing a recognized gap in the field. The finding that explicit multi-task style pre-training outperforms ControlNet (a widely adopted implicit control technique) by a meaningful margin on both quality metrics and parameter efficiency (118M vs. 332M) suggests that task-specific disentanglement can be more effective than general-purpose conditional injection for text-centric editing. Open questions remain around arbitrary-shape text (curved, circular) where geometric prior guidance is still insufficient, and the authors acknowledge that current pixel/feature-level metrics are sub-optimal for assessing text style coherency, leaving room for better evaluation paradigms.
