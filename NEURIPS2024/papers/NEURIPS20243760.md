## Problem

Parameter-efficient prompt tuning of large 3D foundation models (e.g., ULIP, ULIP-2) boosts downstream recognition accuracy but causes a severe drop in 3D domain generalization, with unseen-class performance falling well below zero-shot baselines. Prior 3D domain generalization work relied on small models and small datasets, and existing prompt learning methods for large 3D models entirely ignored generalization. Additionally, existing 3DDG benchmarks cover only ~10 shared classes and lack evaluation on unseen categories, corrupted data, or few-shot regimes.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a 3D point cloud (N×3 points projected into u patches plus a class token) and text descriptions (tokenized sequences with start/end flags and a class token) as dual-branch inputs. Learnable prompts of length 2 are inserted into the first 9 Transformer layers of both the point cloud and text encoders, yielding only 16,896 trainable parameters against a frozen 82.3M-parameter backbone. The output is a class probability distribution over C categories computed via temperature-scaled cosine similarity between the prompt-augmented point cloud and text features, enabling open-vocabulary recognition where the category set is defined at inference time by the text descriptions.

## Method

Point-PRC imposes three explicit regulation constraints on the prompt learning trajectory to preserve the general knowledge in pre-trained 3D foundation models. (1) Mutual Agreement Constraint (MAC) minimizes L1 distance between frozen and prompt-augmented features in both modalities plus KL divergence between their predicted class distributions, anchoring the learning trajectory to task-agnostic knowledge. (2) Text Diversity Constraint (TDC) aggregates multiple text descriptions per class—generated by LLMs (GPT-3.5, GPT-4, PointLLM) or manual templates—via average pooling to prevent the model from overfitting to a single description. (3) Model Ensemble Constraint (MEC) applies a Gaussian-weighted interpolation of prompt parameters across training epochs, assigning low weight to early (randomly initialized) and late (overfitting-prone) checkpoints while emphasizing mid-training parameters. The total loss is the sum of cross-entropy and the weighted regulation loss (α·L_p + β·L_t + γ·L_D), and the framework is plug-and-play across ULIP, ULIP-2, PointCLIP, and PointCLIP V2 without modifying the backbone.

## Results

On the base-to-new class generalization benchmark (averaged over 5 datasets), ULIP-2 with the proposed regulation framework (Point-PRC) achieves 79.48% harmonic mean (HM) of base and new class accuracy, improving over vanilla ULIP-2 prompt tuning (72.57%) by +6.91% and over ULIP+RC (70.64%) by +8.84%. The framework also boosts task-specific base class accuracy from 77.91% to 83.18% for ULIP-2, demonstrating that generalization and recognition performance improve simultaneously. On cross-dataset OOD generalization, ULIP-2+RC reaches 51.19% average accuracy across five target domains (+6.20% over ULIP-2), and on ModelNet-C corruption robustness it achieves 76.82% average (+4.78% over ULIP-2).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against four large 3D foundation models (P-CLIP, P-CLIP2, ULIP, ULIP-2) with standard prompt tuning, plus two prior 3D domain generalization methods (MetaSets, PDG) and one recent OOD method (I-OODG) on the Sim-to-Real and PointDA benchmarks. Point-PRC consistently outperforms all baselines: ULIP-2+RC achieves 79.48% HM vs. 72.57% for ULIP-2, 50.10% for P-CLIP2, and 35.80% for P-CLIP on the base-to-new benchmark. On Sim-to-Real, ULIP-2+RC (65.02% avg) surpasses the prior best PDG-D (59.2%) by +5.82% while using only 16-shot prompt tuning versus full training. On PointDA, ULIP-2+RC (74.74% avg) leads I-OODG (67.8%) by +6.94%. The framework also improves robustness to data corruption (ULIP-2+RC: 76.82% vs. 72.04% for ULIP-2 on ModelNet-C) and few-shot generalization (e.g., +5.39% at 1-shot for ULIP-2), confirming gains across all evaluation dimensions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two research threads: parameter-efficient prompt learning for large 3D foundation models (PPT, IDPT, Point-PEFT, DAPT) and 3D domain generalization (PointDAN, MetaSets, PDG). Prior prompt learning works for 3D focus exclusively on boosting downstream task accuracy and ignore generalization, while prior 3D DG methods operate on small encoders (PointNet, DGCNN) and small datasets. Point-PRC departs from both by being the first to impose explicit regulation constraints (mutual agreement, text diversity, model ensemble) on prompt tuning of large multi-modal 3D models (ULIP, ULIP-2, PointCLIP series) to preserve the general knowledge acquired during pre-training, and by introducing three new evaluation benchmarks (base-to-new, cross-dataset OOD/corruption, few-shot) that go beyond the limited shared-class settings of PointDA and Sim-to-Real.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes that lightweight prompt tuning, while improving task-specific accuracy, systematically degrades the domain generalization of large 3D models—a failure mode unaddressed in prior prompt learning literature. The three new benchmarks (particularly base-to-new and OOD generalization to Omni3D's 216 classes) fill a critical evaluation gap and will likely become standard for future 3D DG research. However, the framework is validated only on object-level classification; extending it to scene-level tasks (3D semantic segmentation, object detection) remains open, as does developing more sophisticated text-fusion strategies beyond simple average pooling of LLM-generated descriptions.
