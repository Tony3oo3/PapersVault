## Problem

Existing 3D MLLMs lack general object referencing and grounding capabilities: scene-level embeddings cannot efficiently address individual objects, and location-token approaches underperform on 3D grounding benchmarks due to the severe scarcity of scene-language data (tens of thousands of instances versus the million-level data available for 2D). This paper tackles how to enable precise, unambiguous object-level interaction with 3D scenes in a language model without requiring large-scale 3D scene-language pre-training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243619/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a 3D point cloud (or RGB video) that is first decomposed into ~100 object proposals by Mask3D (or ~48 by DEVA for video). Each object's point cloud is projected onto multi-view images to produce 2D masks, and the resulting per-object 3D (Uni3D) and 2D (DINOv2) features are concatenated with a learnable identifier token to form the scene embedding sequence fed into Vicuna-7B. Outputs are free-form natural language that may embed object identifiers (e.g., <OBJ013>) to ground specific objects, unifying grounding, dense captioning, and VQA into a single QA response format.

## Method

The core architectural insight is representing the 3D scene as an explicit sequence of object-level embeddings rather than a single scene-level vector. Each object proposal receives a learnable identifier token added to the LLM vocabulary, a 3D feature from Uni3D, and a 2D feature from DINOv2 (extracted from multi-view masked projections), all projected into the LLM embedding space via three-layer MLPs. These three tokens per object are concatenated in a fixed order to form the scene embedding, which the LLM processes alongside the user's text query. All downstream tasks—single/multi-object grounding, dense captioning, and VQA—are reformulated as single-turn QA pairs with no task-specific heads, and the model is trained in a single stage (no separate alignment phase) using LoRA (rank 16) on Vicuna-7B for 2 epochs (3200 steps) with a cross-entropy loss on the assistant response tokens only.

## Results

Chat-Scene achieves 50.2% Acc@0.5 on ScanRefer, 52.4% F1@0.5 on Multi3DRefer, 77.1% CIDEr@0.5 on Scan2Cap, 87.7 CIDEr on ScanQA, and 54.6% EM on SQA3D, setting new state-of-the-art across all five 3D scene-language benchmarks without task-specific fine-tuning. The headline improvements are +3.7% Acc@0.5 on ScanRefer over ConcreteNet, +14.0% F1@0.5 on Multi3DRefer over M3DRef-CLIP, +8.7% CIDEr@0.5 on Scan2Cap over Vote2Cap-DETR++, and +7.7 CIDEr on ScanQA over LEO/Scene-LLM. All results are obtained with a single joint training stage (2 epochs, ~8 hours on 4×A100) using a unified question-answering format with no additional task heads.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Chat-Scene outperforms all 30 compared baselines across every benchmark it is evaluated on. On ScanRefer, it surpasses the best expert model ConcreteNet (46.5 Acc@0.5) by 3.7 points and the best LLM-based model 3D-LLM (30.3 Acc@0.25, no Acc@0.5 reported) by a wide margin. On Multi3DRefer, the 14.0-point F1@0.5 improvement over M3DRef-CLIP (38.4) is the largest gap in the paper, highlighting the benefit of object identifiers for multi-object grounding. On Scan2Cap, Chat-Scene leads Vote2Cap-DETR++ (67.6 C@0.5) by 8.7 points, and on ScanQA it exceeds both LEO and Scene-LLM (80.0 CIDEr) by 7.7 points. On SQA3D, the margin is smaller (54.6 vs. 54.2 EM for Scene-LLM), suggesting that VQA without explicit object referencing benefits less from the identifier mechanism. Notably, Chat-Scene is the only method that reports competitive scores on all five benchmarks simultaneously, whereas expert models typically specialize in one or two tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant 3D MLLM paradigm (3D-LLM, Chat-3D, LL3DA, LEO) that encodes entire scenes into hidden embeddings via Q-Former or direct projection, instead decomposing scenes into object proposals and representing them as explicit object-level embeddings. The object identifier mechanism draws conceptual inspiration from 2D grounding work (e.g., Ferret's refer-and-ground tokens) but adapts it to the 3D setting with learnable tokens that replace the data-hungry location tokens of 3D-LLM. Rather than training scene encoders from scratch, the method repurposes well-trained 2D (DINOv2) and 3D (Uni3D) object-centric encoders, sidestepping the severe scene-language data scarcity that plagues 3D pre-training. The unified QA formulation without task-specific heads extends the instruction-tuning philosophy of LLM-based 3D models but adds the grounding capability that prior LLM-based methods (LL3DA, LEO, Scene-LLM) lack.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The object-identifier approach offers a practical recipe for 3D MLLMs to handle grounding and referencing without requiring the million-scale scene-language data that 2D grounding models need, making it a potentially widely adopted design pattern for 3D scene understanding. The significant margins (14% on Multi3DRefer, 8.7% on Scan2Cap) suggest that prior methods were bottlenecked by their scene-level representation rather than by the LLM itself. Key limitations remain: reliance on frozen pre-trained detectors/encoders, poor handling of structural elements (walls, floors), and continued struggles with counting and fine-grained spatial relations. Open questions include whether end-to-end joint training with the detector can close the gap to the upper bound, and whether the approach generalizes beyond indoor ScanNet scenes to outdoor or more diverse environments.
