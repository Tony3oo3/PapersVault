## Problem

Purely neural grounding and segmentation models (e.g., Grounded SAM, LISA, GLIP) fail to interpret deictic prompts — context-dependent descriptions that identify objects solely by their spatial and relational context (e.g., 'the object on the boat and holding an umbrella') rather than by explicit names or properties. These models lack the compositional reasoning needed to resolve multi-relation descriptions over complex scenes, often segmenting the wrong object (e.g., the boat instead of the person on it).

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and a deictic text prompt that describes the target object exclusively through its relations to other scene entities (e.g., 'an object that has a handle and that is on a bench'), without naming the target. It produces segmentation masks via SAM applied to cropped bounding-box regions identified by the reasoning pipeline, along with per-object confidence scores. The scene graph (from Visual Genome or a learned SGG) provides the relational facts over which logical inference is performed, and the prompts are synthetically generated from 19 predefined spatial/relational predicates.

## Method

DeiSAM is a modular neuro-symbolic pipeline that translates deictic prompts into first-order logic rules via an LLM (GPT-3.5-turbo with few-shot and chain-of-thought prompting), parses the image into a scene graph, bridges terminology mismatches through a semantic unifier (embedding-based synonym matching), and performs differentiable forward chaining (NEUMANN) on a bipartite reasoning graph using soft-logic operations (product for conjunction, log-sum-exp for disjunction) to infer target objects. The rule format is deliberately restricted to two variables (X, Y) to ensure linear rather than exponential scaling of reasoning cost with prompt complexity. Because the forward reasoner is fully differentiable, gradients can be back-propagated through the entire pipeline, enabling end-to-end training of components such as weighted mixtures of scene graph generators. The identified target bounding boxes are then fed to SAM for final segmentation.

## Results

On the newly introduced DeiVG benchmark, DeiSAM achieves mAP of 65.14%, 85.40%, and 87.83% on DeiVG₁, DeiVG₂, and DeiVG₃ respectively, substantially outperforming the strongest neural baseline LISA (14.90%, 56.03%, 75.79%). On the abstract reasoning segmentation task DeiCLEVR, DeiSAM reaches 99.29% and 99.57% mAP for delete and sort operations, while the best neural baseline (GroundedSAM) scores only 7.6% and 15.39%. On DeiRefCOCO+ (deictic variant of RefCOCO+), DeiSAM achieves 71.56/79.51/66.43 mAP on val/testA/testB, compared to 44.92/47.60/43.23 for LISA and 30.06/31.75/28.12 for GroundedSAM.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DeiSAM outperforms all six neural baselines by large margins across every evaluated dataset. The strongest baseline, LISA (Lai et al., 2023), achieves 56.03% mAP on DeiVG₂ versus DeiSAM's 85.40%, a gap of nearly 30 points; on the more abstract DeiRefCOCO+ benchmark this gap widens to over 32 points (47.60% vs. 79.51% on testA). GroundedSAM (Ren et al., 2024) and GroundingDINO-SAM (Liu et al., 2023) struggle with deictic prompts that omit explicit object names, scoring only 31.75% and 32.33% mAP on DeiVG₂/testA respectively, while DeiSAM maintains 85.40% and 79.51%. GLIP-SAM (Li et al., 2022) and OFA-SAM (Wang et al., 2022) are essentially non-functional on multi-relation prompts (0.03% and 9.01% on DeiVG₂), and SEEM (Zou et al., 2023) scores below 8% across all splits. On the synthetic DeiCLEVR task, DeiSAM's near-perfect scores (99.29%/99.57%) contrast starkly with GroundedSAM (7.6%/15.39%) and LISA (12.88%/11.15%), confirming that purely neural pipelines cannot handle abstract list-operation reasoning.

## Relation to Prior Work

DeiSAM extends the differentiable forward reasoning framework of NEUMANN (Shindo et al., 2024) and α-ILP (Shindo et al., 2023) from synthetic visual reasoning tasks to natural-image segmentation, introducing LLMs as logic rule generators and a semantic unification module to bridge vocabulary gaps between prompts and scene graphs. It departs from purely neural grounded segmentation pipelines (Grounded SAM, LISA, GLIP-SAM) by explicitly encoding the reasoning process as a differentiable logic program over scene graph facts, rather than relying on end-to-end learned representations. The modular design also connects to the broader neuro-symbolic rule learning literature (Evans & Grefenstette, 2018; Minervini et al., 2020) and LLM-as-program-generator work (Suris et al., 2023; Ishay et al., 2023), but is the first to apply this paradigm to deictic object segmentation in natural images.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that explicit logical reasoning over scene graphs yields dramatically better performance than neural approaches on prompts requiring multi-relation composition without explicit object names, and that the entire pipeline can be trained end-to-end via differentiable reasoning. Key limitations include the dependence on scene graph quality (Visual Genome annotations are noisy and incomplete), the semantic unification step achieving only ~72% accuracy at best, and the ~10s runtime dominated by LLM API calls. Open questions include whether better SGGs or LLM-based unification can close the remaining gap, whether the approach scales to open-vocabulary scenes without curated scene graphs, and whether the differentiable training signal can improve SGGs in a truly end-to-end fashion beyond the toy weighted-mixture experiment.
