## Problem

The effectiveness of in-context learning in Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) is highly sensitive to how in-context demonstrations (ICDs) are selected and ordered, yet statistical-based evaluation methods from NLP (perplexity, entropy) are inapplicable to VL because LVLMs consume continuous image patches rather than discrete tokens. This paper asks whether effective VL ICD sequences contain inherent temporal statistical patterns that a small model can learn to automatically compose new ICD sequences for test queries.

## Input / Output

Given a query (an image for captioning, or an image paired with a question for VQA) and a supporting set of ~10^5 training examples, Lever-LM autoregressively generates an ordered K-shot ICD sequence by selecting examples one at a time. The generated ICD sequence is prepended to the query and fed to a frozen 9B LVLM (OpenFlamingo or IDEFICS) for standard ICL decoding. Notably, the Lever-LM's vocabulary consists of entire (image, text) pairs from the supporting set rather than individual words, and it can extrapolate to longer sequences (up to 8-shot) even when trained only on 2-shot configurations.

## Method

The core insight is that configuring an ICD sequence mirrors human sentence composition: each ICD should be chosen conditionally based on previously selected ones, and effective sequences may contain learnable temporal statistical patterns. Lever-LM is a two-layer Transformer (~67M parameters) whose vocabulary tokens are the examples from the supporting set, with each token embedded as the sum of CLIP image and text embeddings plus a randomly initialized learnable vector. A training dataset is constructed by using a frozen LVLM to greedily select, for each anchor sample, the ICD that maximizes the LVLM's prediction confidence on the ground-truth label, with beam search to retain diverse high-quality sequences. Lever-LM is then trained with standard cross-entropy loss to predict the next ICD token given the query embedding and previously selected ICDs. At inference, given a novel query, Lever-LM autoregressively generates K ICDs one by one, simultaneously handling both selection and ordering in a single unified process rather than the separate retrieval-then-reordering pipeline used by prior methods.

## Results

Lever-LM (a 67M-parameter Transformer) consistently outperforms all similarity-based retrieval baselines on both Image Captioning (CIDEr) and Visual Question Answering (accuracy) across two 9B LVLMs (OpenFlamingo and IDEFICS). On OpenFlamingo, Lever-LM achieves 92.45 CIDEr (IC) and 51.31% accuracy (VQA) in Avg:1~8, surpassing the best baseline SIIR by 2.54 and 1.62 points respectively. On IDEFICS, the gains are larger: 100.38 CIDEr (IC, +3.02 over RS) and 54.44% accuracy (VQA, +0.52 over SIIR). Notably, Lever-LM trained on only 2-shot ICDs extrapolates to 8-shot configurations while maintaining top performance, and its inference time (0.328s) is comparable to SIIR (0.317s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against similarity-based retrieval methods from two prior VL-ICL studies: Yang et al. [24] (source of SIIR and SITR for image captioning) and Li et al. [25] (source of SIIR, SITR, and STTR for VQA). Lever-LM wins across all four model-task combinations. Against Yang et al.'s SIIR on OpenFlamingo IC, Lever-LM gains +2.54 CIDEr (92.45 vs. 89.91); on IDEFICS IC the margin widens to +12.19 (100.38 vs. 88.19), as SIIR suffers from short-cut inference with IDEFICS. Against Li et al.'s STTR on OpenFlamingo VQA, Lever-LM leads by +3.33 accuracy (51.31 vs. 47.98), and on IDEFICS VQA the gap over STTR is +4.69 (54.44 vs. 49.75). The paper also notes that heuristic baselines exhibit unstable performance across LVLMs (e.g., SIIR beats RS on OpenFlamingo IC but loses badly on IDEFICS IC), whereas Lever-LM maintains consistent superiority, suggesting it captures stable statistical patterns in ICD configurations rather than relying on surface-level similarity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the NLP literature on in-context demonstration (ICD) selection and ordering to the vision-language domain, where prior statistical methods (perplexity, entropy-based scoring) are inapplicable due to continuous image patches in modern LVLMs. It departs from the standard two-stage paradigm of independently retrieving and reordering demonstrations by reframing ICD configuration as a sequential generation problem analogous to sentence composition, where each demonstration is chosen conditionally on previously selected ones. The method repurposes the language model architecture with a non-standard vocabulary of supporting-set examples, drawing on the observation that effective ICD sequences may exhibit temporal statistical patterns, while building on the similarity-based retrieval baselines from [24, 25] that previously dominated VL ICD configuration.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a 67M-parameter model can effectively configure demonstrations for 9B-parameter LVLMs, offering a computationally cheap 'lever' for improving ICL without modifying the large model. The length extrapolation capability (trained on 2-shot, generalizing to 8-shot) and the discovery of a fixed 'golden' ICD sequence that works across queries are practically useful for reducing per-query configuration overhead. However, the dataset construction strategy is acknowledged as suboptimal (4-shot D_M underperforms 2-shot), the evaluation is limited to two LVLMs and two tasks, and the improvements over simple similarity-based baselines, while consistent, are moderate. Open questions include how to construct higher-quality training data for longer ICD sequences and how to obtain more stable golden sequences that generalize across tasks.
