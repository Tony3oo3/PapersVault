## Problem

Sequential multi-task linear bandits with shared low-rank representations have only been analyzed under a strong task diversity assumption requiring that any large subset of task parameters well-spans the underlying m-dimensional subspace. This assumption is hard to verify in practice and may fail when tasks arrive adversarially or in clusters. The paper provides the first nontrivial meta-regret bound for this setting without any task diversity assumption, improving over the trivial independent single-task baseline of Õ(Nd√τ) when N is large and m ≪ d.

## Input / Output

The algorithm receives N sequential d-dimensional linear bandit tasks, each with horizon τ, where the unknown reward parameters θ_n ∈ R^d are guaranteed to lie in a fixed m-dimensional subspace (Assumption 1). The action set is a well-conditioned ellipsoid A = {x : x^T M^{-1} x ≤ 1}, and task parameter norms are bounded in [θ_min, θ_max]. The learner must know N, m, and τ in advance (though N can be relaxed via a doubling trick). At each time step it outputs an arm selection A_{n,t} ∈ A, and internally maintains a distribution over an ε-cover of the Grassmannian of m-dimensional subspaces to guide exploration.

## Method

BOSS (Bandit Online Subspace Selection) uses a bi-level architecture. At the lower level, each task is solved by either Algorithm 1 (meta-exploration: full-dimensional PEGE-style exploration along canonical basis vectors, yielding an unbiased θ̂_n estimate) or Algorithm 2 (meta-exploitation: subspace-restricted exploration along the columns of a chosen basis B̂_n, yielding lower regret when B̂_n approximates the true subspace). At the upper level, an Exponential Weight Algorithm (EWA/Hedge) runs over an ε-cover of the Grassmannian to select which subspace B̂_n to use for exploitation, with a random meta-exploration probability p providing the feedback signal. The key technical device is an importance-weighted surrogate cost C̄_n(B) = C̃_n(B)·Z_n/p that converts the partial-observation problem (cost is only observed in exploration rounds) into a standard online learning problem, enabling EWA to achieve a logarithmic regret over the expert set. The resulting meta-regret is Õ(Nm√τ + N^{2/3}τ^{2/3}dm^{1/3} + Nd² + τmd), where the second term is the sublinear overhead for learning the shared representation.

## Results

The BOSS algorithm achieves a meta-regret of $\tilde{O}(Nm\sqrt{\tau} + N^{2/3}\tau^{2/3}dm^{1/3} + Nd^2 + \tau md)$ for sequential multi-task linear bandits without any task diversity assumption, improving upon the individual single-task baseline of $\tilde{O}(Nd\sqrt{\tau})$ when $N \gg m\sqrt{\tau}$ and $\tau \gg d^2$. Empirically, on synthetic environments with $(N,\tau,d,m)=(4000,500,10,3)$ and an adversarial subspace schedule (new dimensions revealed only at tasks 1, 2501, 3501), BOSS and BOSS-no-oracle both outperform independent PEGE and SeqRepL (Qin et al. 2022) in cumulative regret. The gap between BOSS-no-oracle and BOSS is explained by the finite expert set not covering the true subspace $B$.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the independent PEGE baseline (which solves each task in isolation with $\tilde{O}(Nd\sqrt{\tau})$ regret), BOSS achieves a strictly better guarantee whenever $N$ is sufficiently large relative to $m\sqrt{\tau}$, since the leading term $Nm\sqrt{\tau}$ replaces $Nd\sqrt{\tau}$ and the overhead $N^{2/3}\tau^{2/3}dm^{1/3}$ grows sublinearly in $N$. Against SeqRepL (Qin et al. 2022), which requires the task diversity assumption, BOSS wins in the adversarial no-diversity setting (Figure 1a) where SeqRepL's deterministic exploration schedule is exploited by the environment; however, SeqRepL actually achieves a tighter subspace estimate $\|\hat{B}_{n,\perp}^\top B_n\|_F$ (Figure 1b), and BOSS's regret advantage comes from its superior $\hat{\theta}_n$ estimation (Figure 1c). In the regime where task diversity is satisfied (Figure 3, Appendix H.2), SeqRepL and BOSS perform almost identically, confirming that BOSS does not sacrifice performance when the stronger assumption holds. The theoretical gap between BOSS's upper bound and the known lower bound $\Omega(Nm\sqrt{\tau}+d\sqrt{m\tau N})$ is larger than for Qin et al. [2022], which the authors attribute to the absence of the task diversity assumption.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on three pillars: PEGE (Rusmevichientong & Tsitsiklis, 2010) as the full-dimensional meta-exploration base algorithm, the subspace-restricted exploration procedure from Yang et al. (2020) for meta-exploitation, and the EWA/Hedge algorithm (Freund & Schapire, 1997) for online subspace selection at the upper level. The key departure is from Qin et al. (2022), which achieves near-optimal regret in the sequential setting but requires a 'task diversity' assumption (that any large subset of task parameters well-spans the shared subspace). By removing this assumption, the paper reframes the problem as a bandit online subspace selection game, where the learner must balance meta-exploration (learning the subspace) against meta-exploitation (using a subspace estimate to reduce per-task regret).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first nontrivial regret bound for sequential multi-task linear bandits without the task diversity assumption, a setting that is arguably more realistic since task diversity is hard to verify and may fail in practice. The bound improves over the individual single-task baseline (Ñd√τ) when N is large and m ≪ d, but a gap remains relative to the known lower bound, and 'burn-in' terms (Nd², τmd) can dominate in less favorable parameter regimes. Open questions include closing the gap to lower bounds, extending to general or time-varying action spaces, removing the need to know m a priori, and addressing the computational infeasibility of the exponentially large expert set required by the EWA-based subspace selection.
