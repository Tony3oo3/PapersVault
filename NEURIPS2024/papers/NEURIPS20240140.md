## Problem

Existing adversarially robust conformal prediction methods rely on randomised smoothing, restricting them to ℓ₂-norm bounded perturbations and classification tasks, while producing overly conservative (often trivial) prediction sets. No prior framework extends robust conformal prediction to regression tasks or to arbitrary ℓₚ-norm bounded attacks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240140/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained neural network (CNN for image classification, quantile regressor for regression), a calibration set, a test input (32×32 or 64×64 RGB image, or a state vector from an RL environment), and an adversarial budget ε with a chosen norm (ℓ₁, ℓ₂, or ℓ∞). For classification it outputs a prediction set of class labels; for regression it outputs a prediction interval [lower, upper] on the cumulative reward. The guarantee holds uniformly over all perturbations δ with ‖δ‖ₚ ≤ ε, not just the specific adversarial example applied.

## Method

VRCP replaces the probabilistic randomised smoothing used by prior work with deterministic neural network verification (CROWN or α-CROWN via auto_LiRPA) to compute output bounds of the predictor over the entire ε-ball B_ε(x). Two variants are provided: VRCP-I computes a lower bound on the test score at inference time (best-case score), inflating the prediction set per test point, while VRCP-C computes upper bounds on calibration scores at calibration time (worst-case threshold), allowing standard scores at inference. For classification the score S(x,y)=1−f(x)_y is bounded by verifying f(x) coordinate-wise; for regression the CQR score is bounded by verifying the lower and upper quantile regressors. This verification-based mechanism is norm-agnostic (supporting any ℓₚ the verifier handles) and task-agnostic (classification and regression), and it avoids the Monte-Carlo sampling, holdout-set dependence, and score-transform heuristics of RSCP+.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 (ε=0.03, α=0.1), VRCP-I achieves 98.6% marginal coverage with an average prediction set size of 4.45, while VRCP-C achieves 99.5% coverage with size 5.02. On CIFAR-100 (ε=0.02), VRCP-I reaches 97.1% coverage with set size 22.53, and on Tiny-ImageNet (ε=0.02), VRCP-I achieves 95.8% coverage with set size 72.49. In all settings, both VRCP variants exceed the nominal 90% coverage target while producing substantially tighter prediction sets than the SotA randomised-smoothing baselines, which either produce trivial sets (RSCP+) or exhibit high variance (RSCP+ PTT).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against vanilla CP (Vovk et al., 2005), both VRCP variants recover the nominal 90% coverage guarantee that vanilla CP violates under adversarial perturbations (vanilla drops to 87.8% on CIFAR-10, 89.0% on CIFAR-100, and 88.6% on Tiny-ImageNet), at the cost of modestly larger prediction sets (e.g., 4.45 vs. 1.72 on CIFAR-10). Against RSCP+ (Yan et al., 2023), VRCP-I produces dramatically more informative sets: on CIFAR-10 the average size is 4.45 versus RSCP+'s trivial 10.0 (all classes), on CIFAR-100 it is 22.53 versus 100.0, and on Tiny-ImageNet it is 72.49 versus 200.0. Compared to RSCP+ with PTT, VRCP-I achieves lower average set sizes (4.45 vs. 8.36 on CIFAR-10; 22.53 vs. 26.38 on CIFAR-100; 72.49 vs. 90.64 on Tiny-ImageNet) with far tighter confidence intervals (±0.011 vs. ±0.780 on CIFAR-10), indicating minimal sample dependence. VRCP-C is slightly less efficient than VRCP-I but avoids per-test-point verification overhead, making it preferable when the attack budget ε is fixed. In regression (PettingZoo MPE), VRCP-I and VRCP-C both maintain coverage above 90% across all ε values tested, while vanilla CP drops to 74.5% coverage at ε=0.04 in the Adversary environment.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends inductive conformal prediction (Vovk et al., 2005) and conformalized quantile regression (Romano et al., 2019) by replacing the probabilistic randomised-smoothing mechanism used in prior adversarially robust CP methods (RSCP, RSCP+) with deterministic neural network verification (CROWN, α-CROWN). The key conceptual departure is shifting from a Monte-Carlo smoothing framework—which is inherently limited to ℓ2 norms and classification—to a verifier-agnostic approach that supports arbitrary ℓp norms and regression. The authors position VRCP as the first work to combine NN verification algorithms with CP, explicitly contrasting with the smoothing-based lineage of Gendler et al. (2021) and Yan et al. (2023).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

VRCP provides a principled, non-probabilistic alternative to randomised smoothing for robust uncertainty quantification, with the practical advantage of verifier-agnosticism: any future improvement in NN verification tools directly translates into tighter prediction sets. The significant efficiency gap over RSCP+ (e.g., average set size 4.45 vs. 10.0 on CIFAR10, where RSCP+ is trivial) makes the method practically useful where prior approaches produced uninformative sets. However, scalability remains a key open question: for large networks, incomplete verifiers yield bounds too loose to be useful, and complete verifiers are computationally infeasible, limiting immediate deployment to small-to-medium architectures. The paper also leaves open whether adversarial training at the model level could further tighten bounds and improve set efficiency.
