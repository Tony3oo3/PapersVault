## Problem

Deep equilibrium models achieve strong parameter efficiency but incur high inference cost because approximating the fixed point requires many forward passes of the DEQ layer. Existing acceleration strategies such as fixed-point reuse and global early stopping control termination uniformly across all hidden-state dimensions, ignoring the fact that different dimensions converge at substantially different speeds. This paper identifies and exploits this heterogeneous convergence to skip computation on already-stabilized dimensions.

## Input / Output

For the implicit neural representation task, the network takes 2D pixel coordinates (x, y) as input and predicts the corresponding RGB or greyscale value, operating on 512×512 images. For optical flow estimation, the input is a pair of RGB image frames and the output is a dense per-pixel displacement field. In both settings the method operates internally on the hidden-state activations (512-dim vectors for INR, 256-channel feature maps for the CNN-based flow estimator) during the fixed-point iteration loop, rather than modifying the external input/output interface.

## Method

DeltaDEQ reformulates each linear operation in the DEQ forward pass (dense matrix-vector product or convolution) by decomposing the current hidden state into a cached previous result plus a sparse delta: z^i = z^{i-1} + Δz^i, where Δz^i is thresholded element-wise to zero out changes below τ. Only the sparse delta is passed through the expensive linear operator, and the result is accumulated into a running cache, reducing FLOPs proportionally to the delta sparsity. The method is instantiated for both RNN-type layers (skipping zero columns in the weight matrix) and convolutional layers (skipping zero patches in the feature map), and is combined with Krasnoselskii-Mann fixed-point iteration, fixed-point reuse across timesteps, and global early stopping. Because the backward pass via the implicit function theorem is independent of the forward-pass iteration count, the delta rule introduces no additional training cost, and the approach is orthogonal to existing DEQ acceleration techniques.

## Results

On optical flow estimation, DeltaDEQ with early stopping (τ=0.005) achieves 73% FLOPs reduction on Sintel (520G→141G per pair) and 76% on KITTI (621G→152G) while maintaining comparable accuracy (AEPE 3.90 vs. 3.73 for DEQ on Sintel; F1-all 13.75% vs. 13.45% on KITTI). With fine-tuning (DeltaDEQ-ft, τ=0.005), FLOPs drop further to 127G/144G (84%/77% reduction) while slightly improving accuracy (AEPE 3.86, F1-all 13.64%). On implicit neural representation, DeltaDEQ-Fourier achieves ~84% FLOPs reduction (3.2T→0.5T) with only a 1 dB PSNR drop (32.32→31.30). Even at τ=0 (no thresholding), the inherent ReLU sparsity yields >50% delta activation sparsity, confirming the heterogeneous convergence phenomenon.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the direct DEQ baseline (DEQ-RAFT with Picard iteration [4]), DeltaDEQ matches or slightly degrades accuracy (AEPE 3.90 vs. 3.73 on Sintel; 3.86 vs. 3.73 on KITTI for DeltaDEQ-ft) while reducing FLOPs by 73–84%, making it the most practically relevant comparison. Against non-DEQ optical flow methods (PWC-Net, VCN, GMA, RAFT), DeltaDEQ achieves substantially better accuracy (AEPE ~3.9 vs. 4.7–10.4 on KITTI) at comparable or lower FLOPs than RAFT (141G vs. 5.04G per pair — note RAFT's FLOPs are per single forward pass while DEQ methods iterate). For the INR task, DeltaDEQ-Fourier matches the DEQ-Fourier baseline [32] within ~1 dB PSNR (31.30 vs. 32.32) at 84% fewer FLOPs, and DeltaDEQ-Siren similarly matches DEQ-Siren (29.64 vs. 29.94 PSNR) with 16–32% training FLOPs reduction. The delta rule is orthogonal to fixed-point reuse and early stopping, so gains compound with these existing acceleration techniques rather than competing with them.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the delta activation sparsity line of work (Neil et al. 2017; Parger et al. 2022/2023) from exploiting temporal correlation between consecutive inputs to exploiting the convergence dynamics of fixed-point iterations themselves. The key conceptual departure is identifying 'heterogeneous convergence'—that different dimensions of DEQ hidden states stabilize at markedly different rates—so that the delta rule generates sparsity even for static inputs without any inter-frame similarity assumption. The method is applied within the DEQ framework (Bai et al. 2019) and its instantiations for optical flow (DEQ-RAFT, Bai et al. 2022) and implicit neural representation ((implicit)², Huang et al. 2021), and is orthogonal to existing DEQ acceleration techniques like fixed-point reuse and global early stopping.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 73–84% FLOPs reductions with minimal accuracy loss make DEQ-based models substantially more deployable, and the method's independence from temporal correlation broadens its applicability to static inputs and any iterative fixed-point solver. A notable secondary finding is that simple Picard or Krasnoselskii-Mann iterations match or outperform Broyden/Anderson root-solving for the forward pass, simplifying the inference pipeline. The main open limitation is that theoretical FLOPs savings for sparse convolution require specialized hardware or libraries (e.g., SpConv) to translate into wall-clock speedups, and the approach has not yet been validated on transformer-based DEQ or diffusion-model settings despite the authors noting its potential there.
