## Problem

Current MLLMs are fundamentally designed for single-image understanding, and their visual encoding strategies—whether continuous ViT sequences or discrete token sequences—do not transfer well to multi-image reasoning. Continuous sequences become prohibitively long across multiple images, while discrete sequences alone lose fine-grained spatial details, leaving a gap in how models integrate and reason over information spanning several images simultaneously.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243235/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts K RGB images (each 336×336, encoded by ViT-L with 14×14 patches yielding 567 continuous tokens, and by SEED yielding 32 discrete tokens) alongside a text prompt. After the patch-reduction step retains only 25% of continuous tokens, each image contributes 176 visual tokens (144 continuous + 32 discrete) to the LLM context. The model produces free-form text responses for VQA, multi-image reasoning, captioning, and video comprehension tasks.

## Method

MaVEn encodes each image in two parallel streams: SEED discrete visual tokens (coarse-grained semantic abstractions) and ViT continuous patch tokens (fine-grained spatial details). The discrete tokens are merged into the LLM's vocabulary as a unified multimodal vocabulary, while the continuous tokens are projected via an MLP. A key mechanism is a patch selector MLP that scores each continuous patch against the global semantic state (EOS hidden state) of the discrete token sequence, retaining only the top-25% most semantically relevant patches to reduce sequence length. Training proceeds in four stages: (1) patch selector on 1M Grounding SAM pseudo-labels, (2) embedding layer adaptation to the expanded vocabulary via cross-modal autoregressive generation, (3) visual projector alignment to the unified embedding space, and (4) full instruction fine-tuning with all parameters unfrozen except the visual encoder and patch selector.

## Results

On the DEMON Benchmark (multi-image reasoning), MaVEn achieves the highest scores in 5 out of 7 tasks, including Multi-Image Reasoning (54.38 vs. 50.28 for VPG-C), Visual Relation Inference (30.24 vs. 25.90), and Knowledge Grounded QA (51.53 vs. 48.60). On SEED-Bench, MaVEn attains an average score of 60.89 (vs. 58.6 for LLaVA-1.5), with a notable 12-point improvement over Otter in video understanding (42.11 vs. 30.4). On single-image benchmarks, MaVEn outperforms LLaVA-1.5 across all VQA datasets (e.g., VQAv2: 79.1 vs. 78.5; GQA: 62.5 vs. 62.0) and achieves the best MME score (1530.10) and MMBench score (65.2) among 7B-parameter models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MaVEn (7.2B) consistently outperforms LLaVA-1.5 (7.2B) across all VQA benchmarks (VQAv2: 79.1 vs. 78.5; GQA: 62.5 vs. 62.0; TextVQA: 59.8 vs. 58.2) and multi-modal benchmarks (MME: 1530.10 vs. 1510.70; MMBench: 65.2 vs. 64.3), with only a marginal 0.1-point loss on MM-Vet (30.4 vs. 30.5). Against the larger Qwen-VL-Chat (9.6B), MaVEn wins on VQAv2 (+0.9), GQA (+5.0), VizWizQA (+11.6), MME (+42.5), and MMBench (+4.6), but trails on TextVQA (59.8 vs. 61.5) and SciQA (67.3 vs. 68.2). On the multi-image DEMON Benchmark, MaVEn surpasses the strongest multi-image baseline VPG-C on 5 of 7 tasks (e.g., Multi-Image Reasoning: 54.38 vs. 50.28; Visual Relation Inference: 30.24 vs. 25.90) but lags on Multi-Modal Dialogue (34.63 vs. 37.50) and Visual Story Telling (21.53 vs. 25.20). On SEED-Bench, MaVEn achieves the best overall average (60.89) and video understanding score (42.11), exceeding LLaVA-1.5 by 2.3 points overall and 4.8 points on video, while InstructBLIP (8.2B) trails by 7.5 points in average score. The ablation (Table 4) confirms that the hybrid discrete+continuous encoding yields the best results (SEED-Bench 60.89, DEMON 39.51, MMBench 65.21) compared to discrete-only (43.2/24.19/34.5) or continuous-only (58.6/30.66/64.3) variants.

## Relation to Prior Work

MaVEn sits at the intersection of two prior lines of work: discrete visual tokenization for LLMs (SEED, Ge et al. 2023) and the continuous ViT + MLP projector paradigm popularized by LLaVA (Liu et al. 2023). Rather than adopting one encoding strategy, the paper fuses both into a single unified multimodal vocabulary, departing from the data-centric multi-image approaches (OpenFlamingo, Otter, MMC4) that attempt to solve multi-image reasoning purely through interleaved training data. The dynamic patch-reduction mechanism, guided by discrete token semantics via a learned selector, is a novel architectural component not present in either parent framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine gap: most MLLMs are architecturally designed for single-image input, and the attention-map analysis (Figure 6) reveals that in multi-image settings the LLM largely ignores continuous visual tokens without discrete anchors. The hybrid encoding plus reduction mechanism offers a practical recipe that other MLLM builders could adopt to extend multi-image capability without retraining from scratch. However, the approach adds substantial complexity (a 1.3B SEED tokenizer, an expanded embedding table, a separately trained patch selector), and the single-image gains over LLaVA-1.5 are modest (e.g., 79.1 vs 78.5 on VQAv2), raising questions about whether the overhead is justified outside multi-image scenarios. Open questions include scalability to many more images, whether the pseudo-label-based patch selector generalizes beyond caption-aligned semantics, and whether the discrete tokenizer can be replaced by a lighter alternative.
