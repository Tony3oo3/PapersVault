## Problem

Existing SNN robustness methods rely on simplified direct encoding (repeating the image T times) and a fixed membrane potential leak factor, both of which are oversimplified simulations of biological neurons and lack theoretical grounding for generalizability. While the biological nervous system maintains reliability in complex noise environments through selective visual attention and non-fixed membrane potential leaks, these mechanisms have not been properly modeled in SNNs to defend against diverse adversarial and natural noise.

## Input / Output

The method takes standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 64×64 for Tiny-ImageNet) and produces a class label via an L-layer SNN with LIF neurons. Unusually, the input image is first transformed into the frequency domain via DFT and masked with a time-varying box window before being encoded into a T-step temporal spike train, so the network never sees the raw spatial image directly. Adversarial perturbations are applied in the image domain before the FE module, and the DFT/IDFT/masking operations are differentiable, allowing end-to-end training.

## Method

FEEL-SNN introduces two biologically inspired mechanisms grounded in a unified theoretical robustness framework (Theorem 1) that decomposes SNN robustness into perturbation, weight, and surrogate-gradient terms. Frequency Encoding (FE) transforms the input via DFT and applies a box-window mask whose radius r decreases across time steps (e.g., r=[16,14,12,10] for T=4), progressively suppressing high-frequency noise first while preserving low-frequency image content, mimicking selective visual attention. Evolutionary Leak Factor (EL) makes the LIF leak factor λ a per-neuron, per-time-step learnable parameter optimized via backpropagation with surrogate gradients, allowing different neurons to adapt their membrane potential decay to the frequency content at each step, mimicking non-fixed biological membrane leaks. Together, FE attenuates input perturbation ε(t) and EL optimizes the leak factor λ in the critical ① term of the robustness bound, and both can be combined with existing defenses (adversarial training, Lipschitz regularization, stochastic gating) for further gains.

## Results

On CIFAR-100 with VGG11 (T=8, ε=8/255), FEEL-SNN improves PGD robustness accuracy from 0.16% (vanilla) to 2.04%, and when combined with the strongest SOTA baseline RAT+StoG, pushes PGD accuracy from 15.43% to 18.07% while maintaining clean accuracy (69.97% vs. 69.12%). On CIFAR-10 with VGG11 (T=4, ε=4/255), FEEL nearly doubles PGD accuracy from 15.59% to 30.27% and CW accuracy from 6.92% to 12.39%. Across all datasets (CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, Tiny-ImageNet) and architectures (VGG11, WideResNet16, ResNet19), FE and FEEL consistently improve robustness under FGSM, PGD, BIM, and CW attacks while preserving or slightly improving clean accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares FEEL-SNN against three SOTA robust SNN methods: adversarial training (AT) [12], regularized adversarial training (RAT) [8], and stochastic gating (StoG) [10]. FEEL consistently outperforms each baseline: AT+FEEL improves PGD accuracy from 8.63% to 11.07% (+2.44 pp) and CW from 16.54% to 21.78% (+5.24 pp); RAT+FEEL improves PGD from 8.87% to 12.36% (+3.49 pp) and CW from 20.79% to 25.52% (+4.73 pp); StoG+FEEL improves PGD from 0.26% to 0.55% and CW from 19.87% to 24.79% (+4.92 pp). The largest gains appear when FEEL is stacked with the strongest combination: RAT+StoG+FEEL achieves 18.07% PGD and 50.56% CW accuracy, surpassing RAT+StoG by +2.64 pp and +18.48 pp respectively. Clean accuracy is largely preserved or slightly improved across all combinations (e.g., RAT+StoG+FEEL: 69.97% vs. 69.12%), confirming that the frequency encoding and evolutionary leak factor do not sacrifice original performance for robustness.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the prevailing SNN robustness literature, which has relied on experimental analysis of structural parameters (firing thresholds, time windows [11, 9]) or fixed leak factors [32, 3] without theoretical grounding. It introduces a unified theoretical framework (Theorem 1) derived via BPTT that decomposes SNN robustness into three terms—perturbation/leak, weights, and surrogate gradients—thereby providing the first principled justification for why encoding and leak factor matter. Conceptually, it repurposes two biological mechanisms (selective visual attention and non-fixed membrane potential leak) into concrete SNN design choices: a DFT-based frequency encoding that progressively suppresses high-to-low frequencies across time steps, and a per-neuron, per-time-step trainable leak factor, both of which replace the simplified direct-encoding and fixed-λ assumptions used by prior SNN robustness work [10, 8, 17].

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The theoretical framework gives the SNN robustness community a principled target (minimizing the perturbation×leak product in the BPTT gradient) rather than ad-hoc hyperparameter tuning, and the FE/EL modules are lightweight, differentiable, and composable with existing defenses (AT, RAT, StoG), making them easy to adopt. A key open question is generalization to event-based (DVS) data, which the authors explicitly flag as future work, and to larger-scale benchmarks beyond CIFAR/Tiny-ImageNet. The biological motivation also positions the work as a bridge between computational neuroscience and practical SNN deployment, potentially informing how neuromorphic hardware should handle noisy sensory inputs.
