## Problem

Existing multimodal LLMs require joint-modal pretraining and extensive modal-specific tuning to support multiple input types, meaning that adding a new modality demands re-accessing all historical data and repeating the full training pipeline. This creates a significant computational bottleneck that prevents MLLMs from being incrementally expanded to new modalities in a resource-efficient manner.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241577/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system accepts a modality-specific sample (RGB image, video clip, audio waveform, depth map, or point cloud) paired with a text instruction, where each modality is first encoded by a dedicated frozen encoder (EVA-CLIP-ViT-G/14 for image/video/depth, BEATs for audio, ULIP-2 for 3D) before being aligned to the LLM via a shared Q-Former with modality-specific learnable queries. The output is free-form text: a short answer for QA tasks, a caption for description tasks, or a class label for classification. Critically, training at each modality stage uses only uni-modal data—no joint-modal or cross-modal paired data is ever required.

## Method

PathWeave builds on X-InstructBLIP's frozen Q-Former and introduces an Adapter-in-Adapter (AnA) framework with three components. First, LoRA-based uni-modal adapters are inserted in parallel into Q-Former linear layers and trained on the current modality's data, then frozen to preserve that modality's knowledge. Second, cross-modal adapters are formed by inserting lightweight in-adapters (single linear layers matching the LoRA rank) into the previously frozen uni-modal adapters, allowing the ongoing modality to extract and leverage historical knowledge without reactivating those frozen parameters. Third, an MoE-based gating module computes softmax weights over all adapter paths (cross-modal and uni-modal) to adaptively modulate each path's contribution, preventing interference between modalities with large distribution gaps. The entire LLM, base Q-Former, and all encoders remain frozen throughout; only the adapters (0.8–2.4M parameters) are trained sequentially in the order image → video → audio → depth → 3D, reducing parameter training burden by 98.73% compared to joint-modal approaches.

## Results

On the proposed MCL benchmark, PathWeave achieves zero forgetting (F_m = 0.00) across all four modality transitions (Image→Video→Audio→Depth→3D) while maintaining strong transfer learning scores: T₂ = 56.63 (Video→Audio), T₃ = 83.35 (Audio→Depth), and T₄ = 73.45 (Depth→3D). In terms of parameter efficiency, PathWeave trains only 0.8–2.4M parameters (vs. 189.91M+ for X-InstructBLIP and 7B+ for OneLLM), reducing parameter training burden by 98.73% while using 13.1G GPU memory and 0.23s/it. On the average in-domain transfer learning metric, PathWeave scores 63.74, surpassing the next-best CL method (EProj, 33.91) by 29.83 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest CL baseline EProj [28], PathWeave matches its zero-forgetting guarantee (F_m = 0.00 across all stages) but dramatically outperforms it on transfer learning: T₂ = 56.63 vs. 17.67 (+38.96), T₃ = 83.35 vs. 70.75 (+12.60), and T₄ = 73.45 vs. 7.75 (+65.70), showing EProj's scalability collapses on modalities with large distribution gaps from images. WiSE-FT [54] and L2 Reg&WE [25] achieve low forgetting (F ≤ 13.18) but sacrifice transfer learning severely (T₂ = 15.70 and 7.33 respectively), underperforming PathWeave by over 38 points on Audio and 66 points on 3D. Continual-FT achieves the highest raw T scores (e.g., T₄ = 149.9) but suffers catastrophic forgetting (F₃ = 68.19, F₄ = 65.34), confirming the plasticity–stability trade-off. Compared to full MLLM baselines (X-InstructBLIP [20], OneLLM [21], X-LLM [22], ChatBridge [23]), PathWeave uses 98.73% fewer trainable parameters and 23.2M vs. 1007M+ training samples, while remaining competitive on QA tasks (e.g., MSVD QA 48.2 vs. 51.7 for X-InstructBLIP; Clotho Caps 28.6 vs. 29.4). The ablation confirms the In-Adapter contributes +1.1 points and MoE gating contributes +4.0 points on average transfer learning over a plain incremental adapter baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of multimodal LLMs and continual learning, building directly on X-InstructBLIP's unified Q-Former architecture as its base interface and on LoRA as the fundamental adapter unit. It departs from prior multimodal MLLMs (X-LLM, ChatBridge, OneLLM) that require joint-modal pretraining or finetuning by reframing modality expansion as a continual learning problem where each new modality is learned sequentially on uni-modal data alone. The Adapter-in-Adapter (AnA) design—combining frozen uni-modal adapters with trainable cross-modal in-adapters and an MoE gating module—is a novel architectural contribution that has no direct precedent in either the MLLM or CL literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 98.73% reduction in trainable parameters while maintaining near-parity with joint-modal MLLMs makes incremental modality expansion practically accessible, and the MCL benchmark fills a gap in evaluating whether models truly retain prior modalities after sequential expansion. However, the approach is limited to five modalities and cannot perform cross-modal joint reasoning (e.g., reasoning over both video and audio simultaneously), which the authors acknowledge as a key limitation. Open questions include whether the in-adapter knowledge transfer mechanism generalizes to substantially more modalities or to modalities with very different representational structures, and whether the zero-forgetting guarantee (achieved architecturally via freezing) holds under distribution shift or domain adaptation.
