## Problem

In algorithm-agnostic goodness-of-fit testing, the standard predictiveness-comparison test statistic degenerates under the null hypothesis of equal model quality: its influence function vanishes, preventing convergence to a non-degenerate distribution and invalidating asymptotic normality. Existing remedies—additional data splitting (Williamson et al., Dai et al.) or data perturbation (Rinaldo et al.)—either sacrifice effective sample size and power or require heuristic tuning of the perturbation magnitude. The paper seeks a principled mechanism that restores valid size control while recovering the lost power from data reuse.

## Input / Output

The method consumes n observed (Y, X) pairs, a fixed number of cross-fitting folds K, and a slider parameter τ ∈ [0,1) that governs the proportion of overlap between two testing-data splits within each fold. It outputs a one-sided p-value for H₀: ψ_S = 0 via the normalized statistic T_τ = {n/(2−τ)}^{1/2} ψ_{n,S} / ν_{n,S,τ}, where the variance estimator ν_{n,S,τ}² is a plug-in quantity built from empirical Gâteaux derivatives of the predictiveness criterion. A practical default for τ is τ₀ = (n − 2n₀)/(n − n₀) with n₀ = 50, truncated at 0.9 to guard against near-degeneracy.

## Method

Zipper modifies the standard K-fold cross-fitting procedure by introducing a controlled overlap between the two testing-data splits used to evaluate the full and restricted models. Within each fold D_k, the data is divided into two sets D_{k,A} and D_{k,B} of equal size that share an overlapping region D_{k,o} of proportion τ; the full model's predictiveness is assessed on D_{k,A} while the restricted model's is assessed on D_{k,B}. Under H₀ the influence functions coincide (φ = φ_S), so the overlapping terms cancel in the linear expansion, leaving a two-sample mean comparison over the non-overlapping parts of size (1−τ)n/(2−τ) each—this is the source of non-degeneracy. The asymptotic variance under H₀ simplifies to (1−τ)(σ² + σ_S²), which is consistently estimated by a plug-in variance of the empirical Gâteaux derivatives. Under H₁, the power function G_{S,n,α}(τ) is monotonically increasing in τ whenever Cov{φ(Z), φ_S(Z)} ≥ 0 (which holds for squared-loss variable importance), so larger τ recovers more of the data's information while the non-overlapping portion still guarantees a valid normal limit.

## Results

In the variable importance assessment (n=500, α=5%), Zipper maintains correct empirical size across all settings (3.7%–5.6%), while WGSC-2 inflates to 16.7%–19.9% in high-dimensional settings (p=1000) and DSP-Pert fails badly (35.0%–38.6%). In the model specification test, Zipper achieves 96.9%–100% power under the alternative, compared to only 31.2%–81.4% for WGSC-3 and 34.9%–79.3% for DSP-Split, while maintaining valid size (~4.2%–4.3%) under the null. The power advantage over the vanilla splitting baselines (τ=0) is attributed to the overlap mechanism and the variance estimation scheme.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against WGSC-3 (Williamson et al. [5], Algorithm 3) and DSP-Split (Dai et al. [23]), which both correspond to the non-overlapping τ=0 case, Zipper achieves substantially higher power while maintaining comparable size control. In the model specification test, Zipper reaches 96.9% power (Scenario ii, p=5) versus 31.2% for WGSC-3 and 34.9% for DSP-Split, and 100% (Scenario iii) versus 81.4% and 79.3% respectively. Against WGSC-2 (Williamson et al. [5], Algorithm 2, equivalent to τ=1), Zipper wins decisively on size: WGSC-2 is overly conservative in low dimensions (0.1%–0.6% size) and severely anti-conservative in high dimensions (16.7%–19.9%), while Zipper stays near the nominal 5%. Against DSP-Pert (Dai et al. [23], data perturbation), Zipper provides reliable size control where DSP-Pert fails in high-dimensional settings (35%–38.6% empirical size vs. Zipper's 4.3%–5.6%), and the paper further shows analytically that Zipper dominates the perturbation method in power for any τ∈(0,1) under a natural parameter correspondence.

## Relation to Prior Work

The Zipper device is a direct generalization of the additional-splitting strategy proposed by Williamson et al. (2023) and Dai et al. (2024) for handling degeneracy in algorithm-agnostic variable importance testing. While those methods fully separate the two testing data splits (corresponding to τ=0), and the fully-overlapping variant (τ=1) is known to reintroduce degeneracy, Zipper introduces a continuous slider parameter τ∈[0,1) that controls the proportion of overlap between the two splits. This positions the paper as a principled interpolation between the non-overlapping (valid but low-power) and fully-overlapping (high-power but degenerate) extremes, building on the cross-fitting and influence-function framework established by Williamson et al. and the data perturbation approach of Rinaldo et al. and Dai et al.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The degeneracy problem is a fundamental obstacle in algorithm-agnostic inference: without addressing it, standard cross-fitting-based tests for variable importance, model specification, and model selection fail to have valid size under the null. Zipper provides a simple, theoretically grounded fix that requires no external randomization (unlike data perturbation) and no heuristic tuning of noise levels, while delivering substantial power gains (e.g., 47%→100% in some settings). Open questions include data-adaptive selection of τ, extension to rank-based or other two-sample test statistics for heavy-tailed data, and incorporation into large-scale multiple-testing procedures with error rate control, all of which the authors flag as future work.
