## Problem

Existing gradient-based attacks for tabular data (e.g., CPGD) fail to generate valid adversarial examples that satisfy complex feature constraints such as categorical types, immutability, and inter-feature relations, while the only search-based alternative (MOEVA) is computationally expensive and requires extensive hyperparameter tuning. This leaves a critical gap in evaluating the adversarial robustness of deep tabular models deployed in critical domains like finance, healthcare, and cybersecurity.

## Input / Output

The attack takes a clean tabular example x with its true label y, a target classifier h, a formal constraint set Ω expressed in a grammar supporting AND/OR, comparisons, and arithmetic over features, and an L2 perturbation budget ε. It produces an untargeted adversarial example x+δ that is misclassified by h, satisfies all constraints in Ω, and respects the perturbation bound. Effectiveness is measured as robust accuracy (fraction of valid adversarial examples successfully misclassified), while efficiency is measured in wall-clock execution time.

## Method

CAPGD extends CPGD with four mechanisms: an adaptive step-size that halves based on optimization trend (loss-increase ratio ρ=0.75) rather than a fixed decay schedule, momentum (α=0.75) to smooth gradient updates, dual initialization from both the original example and a random point in the L2-ball to avoid local optima, and an improved repair operator that enforces equality constraints at each iteration. CAA then combines CAPGD with MOEVA in a sequential ensemble: CAPGD runs first to cheaply capture 'easy' adversarial examples, and MOEVA runs only on the remaining examples that CAPGD failed to attack. This design exploits the complementarity between the two attack families—CAPGD generates 477 unique examples and MOEVA 953—maximizing the total adversarial set while reducing computation by up to 5× compared to running MOEVA alone.

## Results

CAA (the proposed ensemble of CAPGD + MOEVA) achieves the lowest robust accuracy in 17 out of 20 dataset/architecture settings, with accuracy drops of up to 96.1 percentage points compared to CAPGD alone, 84.3 points vs BF*, and 21.7 points vs MOEVA. On the most vulnerable settings (LCLD/RLN, CTU/TabNet), CAA reduces robust accuracy to 0.0%. In terms of efficiency, CAA is up to 5× faster than MOEVA and up to 418× faster than BF*, while CAPGD alone is 75× faster than MOEVA. The only setting where CAA is not best is LCLD/STG, where BF* achieves a marginally lower robust accuracy (53.0% vs 53.6%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CPGD (Simonetto et al., IJCAI 2022), CAPGD dramatically outperforms: on URL/TabTransformer, CPGD achieves 91.9% robust accuracy while CAPGD drops it to 10.9% (an 81-point improvement). CAPGD also subsumes all successful examples from CPGD (132 examples) and LowProFool (3 examples), generating 6,597 additional adversarial examples that neither baseline can produce. Against MOEVA (also from Simonetto et al.), CAA achieves lower robust accuracy in 17/20 settings (e.g., 8.9% vs 18.2% on URL/TabTr., 0.0% vs 0.8% on LCLD/RLN) while being up to 5× faster (e.g., 10s vs 50s on LCLD/RLN). Against BF* (Kulynych et al., 2018), CAA wins in 19/20 settings and is up to 418× faster (e.g., 25s vs 10,472s on WIDS/TabNet); the sole exception is LCLD/STG where BF* edges out CAA by 0.6 percentage points (53.0% vs 53.6%) but at 3.4× the computational cost. Against LowProFool (Ballet et al., 2019), CAPGD is overwhelmingly superior—LPF fails to reduce robust accuracy below 90.9% in any setting, while CAPGD achieves values as low as 0.2%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the constrained adversarial attack framework of Simonetto et al. (IJCAI 2022), which introduced CPGD (a gradient-based attack with constraint penalties) and MOEVA (a multi-objective evolutionary search attack) for tabular data with feature relationship constraints. The key departure is diagnosing why CPGD underperforms—fixed step schedules, lack of momentum, no inter-iteration constraint repair, and single initialization—and addressing each with adaptive mechanisms to produce CAPGD. The paper then positions CAA as a practical ensemble that exploits the observed complementarity between gradient (fast, covers 'easy' examples) and search (slow, covers 'hard' examples) attacks, building on the BF* and MOEVA search-based lineage while dropping BF* due to its computational cost.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Tabular deep learning models are increasingly deployed in critical domains (credit scoring, ICU triage, botnet detection), yet their adversarial robustness has been evaluated with attacks that either ignore feature constraints or are computationally prohibitive. CAA provides a single, parameter-light baseline that the authors argue should become the minimal evaluation test for any new tabular defense or robust architecture. Open questions remain: gradient methods still fail entirely on high-constraint datasets like CTU (360 constraints), adversarial training helps only for some architecture-dataset pairs, and the interaction between constraint complexity and attack success is not yet well understood. The paper also notes that incoherent constraint definitions can produce invalid examples at large perturbation budgets, a practical concern for real-world deployment.
