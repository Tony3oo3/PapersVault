## Problem

Safety-aligned LLMs (e.g., Llama-2-Chat, Llama-3-Instruct) lose their safety guardrails when fine-tuned with LoRA, even on purely benign datasets. Existing defenses like SafeInstr and BEA require injecting additional safety or backdoor samples into the training data, making them data-dependent and less practical. The paper asks whether safety can be preserved post-hoc without any extra training data or retraining.

## Input / Output

The method consumes three weight tensors: the LoRA update matrices (ΔW = AB^T) produced by standard fine-tuning, the base (unaligned) model weights, and the aligned (chat/instruct) model weights. It outputs a modified set of LoRA weights where selected layers have been projected onto the safety-aligned subspace, yielding a fine-tuned model that retains both downstream utility and safety alignment. The alignment matrix is stored on disk and loaded layer-by-layer onto GPU during projection, so no additional GPU memory is required beyond the base model.

## Method

Safe LoRA constructs a per-layer alignment matrix V = W_aligned − W_unaligned, then builds a projection operator C = VV^T / ‖V‖_F (a faster approximation avoiding the costly (V^T V)^{-1} inverse). After standard LoRA fine-tuning, for each layer it computes the Frobenius-norm cosine similarity between the original LoRA update ΔW and its projection CΔW. Layers whose similarity falls below a threshold τ (equivalently, the top-K lowest-similarity layers) have their updates replaced by the projected version, while the rest are left unchanged. This selective projection keeps the fine-tuned weights close to the safety-aligned subspace without sacrificing utility, and the entire procedure is training-free and data-free—requiring only the publicly available base and chat checkpoints.

## Results

On the PureBad dataset (purely malicious fine-tuning data) with Llama-2-7B-Chat, Safe LoRA reduces the harmfulness score from 4.66 (native LoRA) to 1.055 while achieving the highest MT-Bench utility score of 6.34, matching the original un-fine-tuned model. On the Dialog Summary dataset (mixed benign + 100 harmful samples), Safe LoRA lowers the harmfulness score from 2.63 to 1.297 while retaining a Rouge-1 F1 of 49.79% (vs. 50.66% for native LoRA). On the benign Alpaca dataset, Safe LoRA reduces harmfulness from 2.25 to 1.09 with an MT-Bench score of 5.62, outperforming both SafeInstr (2.04) and BEA (2.56) on safety.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against native LoRA, Safe LoRA dramatically outperforms on safety across all datasets: on PureBad (Llama-2), harmfulness drops from 4.66 to 1.055 while utility actually improves from 4.54 to 6.34 MT-Bench. Compared to SafeInstr, Safe LoRA achieves a marginally lower harmfulness score (1.055 vs. 1.064 on PureBad/Llama-2) but a substantially higher utility score (6.34 vs. 5.74), and crucially requires no additional training data. Against BEA, Safe LoRA wins on both harmfulness (1.055 vs. 1.203) and utility (6.34 vs. 5.87) on PureBad/Llama-2, and on the benign Alpaca dataset Safe LoRA achieves a harmfulness of 1.09 versus BEA's 2.56. Against Vaccine (Appendix A.6), Safe LoRA dominates on both metrics: on PureBad it achieves 1.055 harmfulness and 6.34 MT-Bench versus Vaccine's 3.282 and 2.812 (single LoRA) or 3.861 and 0.99 (double LoRA). The one scenario where Safe LoRA is not the clear winner is Llama-3-8B-Instruct on PureBad, where BEA achieves higher utility (6.89 vs. 5.05) and SafeInstr achieves lower ASR (3.64% vs. 6.36%), though Safe LoRA still attains the lowest harmfulness score (1.10).

## Relation to Prior Work

Safe LoRA is explicitly positioned as a one-liner patch to the original LoRA (Hu et al., ICLR 2022), extending it with a post-hoc projection step onto a safety-aligned subspace. The alignment matrix (W_aligned − W_unaligned) draws on the model-arithmetic and task-vector literature (Ilharco et al., 2022; Wortsman et al., 2022), where weight differences between checkpoints encode interpretable directions. Unlike prior defenses against fine-tuning-induced safety degradation—SafeInstr (Bianchi et al., 2024) and BEA (Wang et al., 2024)—which require injecting additional safety or backdoor samples into the training set, Safe LoRA is both training-free and data-free, requiring only the publicly available base and chat/instruct checkpoints.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is high: a single projection operation, applied to a small fraction of LoRA layers (~11% for Llama-2, ~35% for Llama-3), restores safety to near-original levels without any retraining or extra data, making it immediately deployable by practitioners who fine-tune open-weight LLMs. The finding that the number of projected layers correlates with a model's inherent alignment strength offers a useful diagnostic. However, the authors acknowledge that the method's transparency could enable adaptive attacks, and generalization to multimodal models (e.g., text-to-image) remains an open question. Whether the alignment subspace captured by a single base-to-chat weight difference is sufficient for more complex or multi-stage alignment pipelines is also unresolved.
