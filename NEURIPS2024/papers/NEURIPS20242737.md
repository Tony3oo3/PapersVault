## Problem

Speculative decoding accelerates LLM inference by having a small draft model propose tokens that a larger target model verifies in parallel, but the drafting step itself remains a bottleneck: the draft model still performs slow autoregressive generation, and all draft tokens are allocated equal computational time despite later tokens having exponentially lower acceptance probability. This paper addresses both inefficiencies to push speculative decoding speedups beyond the typical 2–3× range.

## Input / Output

The algorithm takes a text prompt, a target LLM, an ordered list of draft models (neural models of decreasing size), and a statistical language model (Max-Gram) as the base of the cascade. It produces generated text tokens whose output distribution is identical to autoregressive sampling from the target model alone. A hyperparameter matrix K_nn (upper-triangular) controls how many tokens each cascade level generates per step, and a lenience parameter l relaxes acceptance only between draft models while the target model always reviews with l=1 to guarantee distributional correctness.

## Method

CS Drafting combines two orthogonal cascades. The Vertical Cascade recursively applies speculative decoding downward: each neural draft model reviews the generation of a smaller model rather than generating autoregressively, cascading until reaching a statistical language model (Max-Gram) with negligible cost; lenience is applied only at draft-model review stages to preserve the target model's output distribution. The Horizontal Cascade allocates the largest draft model to the earliest (highest-acceptance-probability) tokens and progressively smaller models to later tokens, reducing wasted compute on tokens unlikely to be accepted. Max-Gram (MaG) serves as the base statistical model, greedily matching maximal n-grams between the input prefix and the generation, falling back to a Wikipedia bigram distribution when no match exists. Theoretical analysis via probability generating functions proves that the vertical cascade strictly improves expected walltime improvement factor (EWIF) when the base model's cost is negligible, and the horizontal cascade's EWIF formula shows the first token's acceptance probability dominates the speedup, justifying the size-graded allocation.

## Results

On FLAN-T5 (encoder-decoder), CS Drafting with three models (BASE, SMALL, MaG) achieves a standardized walltime improvement (SWI) of 3.88× on GSM8K and 4.88× on MMLU, outperforming the best single-model speculative decoding baseline (3.38× and 4.12× respectively). This translates to an additional speedup of up to 44% over speculative decoding on GSM8K and up to 81% on MMLU. On the decoder-only Vicuna-7B model, CS Drafting reaches 56.72 tokens/s on GSM8K and 55.60 tokens/s on MMLU, and when combined with tree attention, further improves to 63.81 and 63.37 tokens/s, surpassing Medusa (61.87 and 56.19 tokens/s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CS Drafting consistently outperforms standard speculative decoding (Leviathan et al., ICML 2023) across all tested configurations: on GSM8K, the best CS Drafting setup (BASE, SMALL, MaG) achieves 3.88× SWI versus 3.38× for the best speculative decoding (BASE), a 14.8% relative improvement; on MMLU, the gap widens to 4.88× vs. 4.12× (18.4% relative improvement, or up to 81% additional speedup in the PW metric). On the decoder-only Vicuna-7B model, CS Drafting (56.72 tokens/s on GSM8K) beats speculative decoding (44.31 tokens/s) by ~28%, but falls short of Medusa (61.87 tokens/s) in raw walltime. However, when CS Drafting is combined with tree attention, it surpasses Medusa on both GSM8K (63.81 vs. 61.87 tokens/s) and MMLU (63.37 vs. 56.19 tokens/s), demonstrating that the cascade approach is composable with other acceleration techniques. Notably, CS Drafting with a single neural model plus MaG already exceeds the best single-model speculative decoding by up to 70% on MMLU, with no additional deployment overhead beyond the negligible-cost bigram model.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the speculative decoding framework of Leviathan et al. (ICML 2023) by introducing two orthogonal cascades: a vertical cascade that recursively applies speculative decoding down to a statistical language model (eliminating autoregressive generation from all neural draft models), and a horizontal cascade that assigns progressively smaller models to later, lower-value draft tokens. The vertical cascade is conceptually related to Spector & Re's staged speculative decoding, but the authors differentiate their approach by making it fully recursive and incorporating lenience between draft models. The Max-Gram statistical drafting component and the formal generating-function analysis of EWIF for cascaded systems are novel contributions not present in prior speculative decoding work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LLM inference latency is a critical bottleneck for serving at scale, and CS Drafting offers a lossless (distribution-preserving) speedup of up to 81% over standard speculative decoding without requiring fine-tuning or architectural changes. The finding that larger draft models become preferable when assisted by a statistical model (reversing the vanilla speculative decoding preference for smaller drafts) suggests that the optimal draft model size is context-dependent and may shift as the drafting pipeline evolves. Open questions include how the approach scales to more than three model tiers, whether the hyperparameter sensitivity (particularly K_nn) holds across diverse hardware, and how CS Drafting interacts with emerging serving optimizations like continuous batching and quantization.
