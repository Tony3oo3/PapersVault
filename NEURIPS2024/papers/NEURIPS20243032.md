## Problem

Existing financial LLM benchmarks (PIXIU, FinanceBench, BizBench) cover only a narrow set of NLP-centric tasks and lack evaluation of complex financial capabilities such as forecasting, risk management, decision-making, and stock trading. No prior benchmark provides a holistic, multi-aspect evaluation of LLMs across the full spectrum of financial applications, leaving their true capabilities and limitations in the domain largely unexplored.

## Input / Output

FinBen consumes heterogeneous financial inputs including unstructured text (news, SEC filings, earnings transcripts, regulatory documents), tabular records (credit scores, fraud features, insurance claims), and time-series data (OHLCV stock prices over 497 trading days, tweets). Outputs span structured labels (entity tags, sentiment classes, causal spans), free-form text (summaries, long-form regulatory answers), and structured trading decisions in JSON format specifying buy/sell/hold with supporting memory indices. The stock trading task uniquely requires the model to synthesize short-term news, mid-term filings, and long-term price history into a single decision, while risk management tasks present highly imbalanced tabular inputs where minority classes (fraud, distress) constitute only a small fraction of samples.

## Method

FinBen organizes 42 datasets into 24 tasks under a novel eight-aspect taxonomy (IE, TA, QA, TG, RM, FO, DM, SP), drawing from three sources: reformulated open-source datasets converted to instruction-response pairs, existing benchmark datasets (e.g., PIXIU), and two newly constructed datasets. The FinTrade dataset integrates historical OHLCV prices, SEC 10-K/10-Q filings, and daily news for 10 stocks over one year to enable agent-based stock trading evaluation using the FinMem LLM agent with layered memory (short-term, mid-term, reflection). The Regulations dataset contains 254 expert-curated QA pairs on EU OTC derivatives (EMIR) for long-form regulatory question answering. Evaluation is conducted in zero-shot and few-shot settings across 21 LLMs (GPT-4, Gemini, LLaMA2/3, FinMA, FinGPT, etc.) with metrics tailored per task (F1, EM Accuracy, ROUGE, BERTScore, Sharpe Ratio, Maximum Drawdown, MCC), and is the first benchmark to incorporate both agent-based and RAG-based evaluation strategies for financial LLMs.

## Results

On the novel stock trading task (FinTrade), GPT-4 achieves the highest Sharpe Ratio of 1.51±1.08 and cumulative return of 28.19%±25.27%, substantially outperforming Gemini (SR 1.03, CR 14.95%), LLaMA2-70B (SR 0.52, CR 4.02%), and the Buy&Hold baseline (SR 0.02, CR −4.00%). On forecasting (stock movement prediction), all LLMs perform near random: the best accuracy is ~57% (LLaMA3.1-70B on CIKM18) with MCC values between −0.03 and 0.06, confirming a fundamental limitation. In information extraction, GPT-4 dominates (NER EntityF1 0.83, FINER-ORD 0.77), while in text generation Gemini leads on EDTSum. Across all 42 datasets, closed-source models (GPT-4, Gemini) consistently outperform open-source alternatives, with LLaMA2-70B being the strongest open-source model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GPT-4 is the overall strongest model, leading in information extraction (NER EntityF1 0.83 vs. Gemini 0.61, LLaMA2-70B 0.04), question answering (TATQA EM 0.44 for LLaMA3.1-70B vs. GPT-4's superior performance), and stock trading (SR 1.51 vs. Gemini 1.03). Gemini outperforms GPT-4 specifically on text generation (EDTSum) and shows competitive forecasting results. Among open-source models, LLaMA2-70B and LLaMA3.1-70B are the strongest, with LLaMA3.1-70B achieving the best SC causal classification F1 (0.87) and TATQA EM (0.44), but all open-source models trail closed-source by large margins on complex tasks. Instruction-tuned financial LLMs (FinMA-7B, FinGPT) show modest gains over their base LLaMA-7B on sentiment analysis but remain far below GPT-4 on IE and QA. Traditional RL methods (DQN, PPO, A2C) are significantly outperformed by GPT-4 on stock trading (SR −0.17 to 0.01 vs. 1.51). Smaller models (<70B) largely fail on decision-making tasks, producing static 'hold' strategies due to limited context and instruction-following ability.

## Relation to Prior Work

FinBen extends the line of financial LLM evaluation benchmarks (PIXIU, FinanceBench, BizBench, CFBenchmark) that were limited to a handful of NLP-centric tasks such as sentiment analysis, NER, and QA. It departs from these by introducing a broader taxonomy of eight aspects—adding forecasting, risk management, decision-making (stock trading), and bilingual evaluation—and by incorporating agent-based and RAG-based evaluation strategies not present in any prior financial benchmark. The stock trading evaluation specifically builds on the FinMem agent framework (Yu et al., 2023, 2024), repurposing a trading agent architecture as an evaluation harness rather than a standalone contribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As the first comprehensive open-source benchmark covering 42 datasets and 24 financial tasks, FinBen provides the field with a standardized, reproducible evaluation protocol that has already been adopted for the first financial LLM shared task at IJCAI-2024. Key open questions remain: LLMs still perform near-random on stock movement forecasting, smaller models (<70B) cannot reliably follow trading instructions, and cross-lingual transfer (e.g., Chinese-tuned models degrading on English tasks) is unresolved. The benchmark's current limitations—American market data, English-centric content, and compute constraints capping evaluation at 70B models—leave significant room for expansion into global markets, multimodal inputs, and larger model families.
