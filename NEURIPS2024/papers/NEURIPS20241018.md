## Problem

Weakly supervised point cloud semantic segmentation suffers from inadequate supervision signals due to sparse annotations, and existing constraints (contrastive learning, self-training, similarity metrics) rely on heuristic assumptions that ignore the inherent distribution of network embeddings. The paper asks two fundamental questions: how to mathematically characterize the semantic segmentation feature space under weak supervision, and how to intensify this intrinsic distribution during learning to provide reliable auxiliary constraints.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241018/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D point cloud with a small subset of point-level semantic labels (label rates ranging from 10% down to 0.001%) and produces per-point semantic predictions via the weakly supervised learning branch, supplemented by per-point posterior probability distributions over all categories from the moVMF that offer a Bayesian interpretation of each prediction. Network features are L2-normalized onto the unit hypersphere before entering the distribution alignment branch, and at inference only the weakly supervised branch is activated to maintain consistency with fully supervised deployment.

## Method

DGNet comprises a weakly supervised learning branch (using truncated cross-entropy loss to prevent overfitting on sparse labels) and a distribution alignment branch that constrains the embedding distribution to a mixture of von Mises-Fisher distributions (moVMF) on the unit hypersphere. The key mechanism is a Nested Expectation-Maximum Algorithm: the E-step alternately updates moVMF parameters (category weights α and mean directions u) and soft cluster assignments via the soft-moVMF algorithm, while the M-step backpropagates a vMF loss (derived from maximum likelihood estimation) plus a discriminative loss (encouraging orthogonal category prototypes) to refine network parameters. A consistency loss in cross-entropy form bridges the two branches by aligning segmentation class probabilities with moVMF posterior probabilities. The choice of moVMF with cosine similarity over alternatives (GMM with Euclidean norm, category prototypes) is justified by the radial nature of the segment head, insensitivity to the curse of dimensionality, and superior intra/inter-category fitting capability.

## Results

On S3DIS Area 5, DGNet with PointNeXt backbone achieves 67.8% mIoU at 0.1% label rate (vs. 65.0% baseline) and 62.4% mIoU at 0.01% label rate (vs. 58.4% baseline), representing improvements of +2.8 and +4.0 points respectively. With the weaker DeepGCN backbone, the gains are even more dramatic: 58.4% vs. 43.9% at 0.1% and 52.8% vs. 35.9% at 0.01%. On ScanNetV2, DGNet(PointNeXt) reaches 67.4% mIoU at 1% label rate, surpassing the prior SOTA AADNet (66.8%), and 62.9% at the 20-points-per-scene setting. On SemanticKITTI, DGNet(RandLA-Net) achieves 51.8% and 41.2% mIoU at 0.1% and 0.01% label rates, outperforming SQN by 1.0 and 2.1 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DGNet consistently outperforms all compared weakly supervised baselines across all three datasets. On S3DIS at 0.1% label rate, DGNet(PointNeXt) at 67.8% mIoU surpasses AADNet (67.2%), CPCM (66.3%), and SQN (64.1%); at 0.01%, DGNet(PointNeXt) at 62.4% beats AADNet (60.8%) and SQN (45.3%). The improvement is most pronounced with the weaker DeepGCN backbone, where DGNet lifts mIoU from 43.9% to 58.4% at 0.1% and from 35.9% to 52.8% at 0.01%, indicating the distribution guidance is especially valuable when the backbone struggles with sparse supervision. On ScanNetV2, DGNet(PointNeXt) edges out AADNet by 0.6 points at 1% (67.4 vs. 66.8) and by 0.4 points at 20pts (62.9 vs. 62.5), while also surpassing GaIA (65.2%) and DCL (59.6%) at 1%. On SemanticKITTI, DGNet(RandLA-Net) outperforms SQN by 1.0 points at 0.1% (51.8 vs. 50.8) and by 2.1 points at 0.01% (41.2 vs. 39.1), and also beats RPSC (50.9%) and CPCM (34.7%). The paper also demonstrates that DGNet achieves over 97% of fully supervised performance (67.8% vs. 69.2% for PointNeXt) with only 0.1% labeled points on S3DIS.

## Relation to Prior Work

DGNet departs from the four dominant weakly supervised point cloud segmentation paradigms (perturbation consistency, contrastive learning, self-training, and similarity metric) by reframing the problem as one of characterizing and enforcing the intrinsic distribution of network embeddings. Conceptually closest to the similarity metric family, DGNet instead describes the feature space holistically via a mixture of von Mises-Fisher distributions (moVMF) on the unit hypersphere, integrating the soft-moVMF EM algorithm [3] as a distribution alignment branch. The truncated cross-entropy loss [60] is borrowed to mitigate overfitting on sparse labels, and the overall design echoes the clustering-as-pseudo-label philosophy of DeepClustering [6] and DINOVMF [13], but applied specifically to weakly supervised 3D segmentation rather than unsupervised pretraining or face verification.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DGNet provides a mathematically grounded, plug-and-play mechanism for weakly supervised point cloud segmentation that yields consistent 2–4 mIoU gains over strong baselines at extreme label rates (0.01%–0.001%), and the moVMF posterior probabilities offer an interpretable probabilistic explanation for per-point predictions. The systematic ablation comparing distance metrics and distribution models (Table 4) gives practitioners clear guidance on feature-space design choices. However, the authors acknowledge that the distribution exploration is preliminary: more sophisticated distributions (e.g., [12]) could further constrain features but risk overfitting under sparse annotations, leaving open how to balance expressive feature descriptions with the limited supervision signal. The approach's reliance on a fixed concentration parameter κ and a memory bank for missing-class initialization also constrains its applicability to highly imbalanced or open-vocabulary settings.
