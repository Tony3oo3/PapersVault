## Problem

Spikformer-like SNN architectures rely on spike-spike matrix multiplication in their self-attention module, which produces significant output errors on event-driven asynchronous neuromorphic hardware due to imprecise spike arrival timing. On synchronous hardware, reducing time steps to lower energy consumption degrades SNN performance. The paper addresses both gaps by designing a transformer-like SNN architecture that uses only asynchronous-compatible operations while maintaining competitive accuracy at a single time step.

## Input / Output

The network accepts RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR, 224×224 for ImageNet) or DVS event streams (128×128 downsampled to 48×48 for CIFAR10-DVS), which are converted into patch token sequences via the IPSPS module (64 patches of D-dim vectors for CIFAR, 196 patches for ImageNet). The output is a class label produced by a classification head (adaptive average pooling + fully connected layer) that performs floating-point operations and must be offloaded to CPU. For fully event-driven deployment, all bias terms are removed and batch normalization layers are absorbed into weights through a two-phase training pipeline.

## Method

STMixer replaces the Spikformer SSA module with a Spiking Token Mixing (STM) module that applies a single trainable N×N weight matrix to the value spike matrix, eliminating the spike-spike matrix multiplication that breaks under asynchronous timing. Multi-head STM partitions the value matrix into H groups along the channel dimension, assigning each group its own W_STM for finer-grained token mixing. The IPSPS module adds a shallow convolutional residual path (contributing 1/8 of output channels) to the standard SPS, preserving input image information that would otherwise be lost through five successive spiking conv layers. Training employs a modified Surrogate Module Learning (SML) approach where the surrogate encoder uses the STMixer architecture instead of large-kernel convolutions, reducing computational overhead while mitigating gradient error accumulation. The architecture is validated through a one-shot NAS over 5^6 encoder substructures and benchmarked on both synchronous (T=1) and fully event-driven asynchronous scenarios.

## Results

On ImageNet, STMixer-8-768-16 achieves 76.68% top-1 accuracy with only T=1 time step and 4.45 mJ estimated energy, surpassing Spikingformer-8-768 (75.85%, T=4, 13.68 mJ) by 0.83% while using 3× fewer time steps. On CIFAR-100, STMixer-4-384-32 reaches 81.87% (T=4) and 80.00% (T=1), outperforming Spikingformer (79.09%) and SML (79.49%). On CIFAR-10, STMixer achieves 96.01% (T=4), matching SML (96.04%) while using fewer parameters (8.29M vs 11.22M). On neuromorphic datasets, STMixer-2-512-8 achieves 82.67% on CIFAR10-DVS and 97.19% on DVS128 Gesture (both T=10), setting new state-of-the-art.

## Baselines & Comparisons

STMixer outperforms all 12 baselines on at least one dataset. On CIFAR-100, STMixer (81.87%, T=4) beats Spikingformer (79.09%) by 2.78%, SML (79.49%) by 2.38%, Spikformer (77.86%) by 4.01%, and SpikeDHS (76.25%) by 5.62%. On ImageNet, STMixer-8-768-16 (76.68%, T=1, 4.45 mJ) surpasses Spikingformer-8-768 (75.85%, T=4, 13.68 mJ) by 0.83% with 3× lower energy, and Spikformer-10-512 (73.68%) by 3.00%. On CIFAR-10, STMixer (96.01%) essentially ties SML (96.04%) while using 25% fewer parameters, and beats Spikingformer (95.61%) and Spikformer (95.19%). On neuromorphic datasets, STMixer (82.67% CIFAR10-DVS, 97.19% DVS128) outperforms Spikingformer (79.9%, 96.2%), Spikformer (78.6%, 95.8%), PLIF (74.8%, 97.6%), TA-SNN (72.0%, 98.6%), and LIAF-Net (70.4%, 97.6%). The older CNN-based baselines (tdBN, TET, AutoSNN, SNASNet, SEW-ResNet) are all surpassed by 2–13% across datasets, confirming the advantage of the transformer-like SNN architecture.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Spikformer/Spikingformer line of transformer-based SNNs, adopting their overall encoder framework (patch splitting, stacked encoders, classification head) and membrane-potential residual connections. The key departure is replacing the Spiking Self-Attention (SSA) module—which requires spike-spike matrix multiplication—with a Spiking Token Mixing (STM) module that uses a single learnable N×N weight matrix applied to the value matrix, drawing on the metaformer insight (MLP-Mixer, ResMLP) that linear token mixing suffices for competitive vision performance. The authors also introduce IPSPS to mitigate information loss in the patch-splitting stage and adapt their prior Surrogate Module Learning (SML) method by swapping its convolutional encoder for the lighter STMixer encoder.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that STMixer is the first Spikformer-like architecture composed entirely of operations (conv, FC, residual) supported by event-driven asynchronous neuromorphic chips such as Speck and Loihi, removing the architectural barrier that has prevented state-of-the-art SNNs from being deployed on the lowest-power hardware. The finding that T=1 suffices for competitive ImageNet accuracy (76.68% vs. 75.85% for Spikingformer at T=4) with roughly 3× lower estimated energy suggests a path to SNNs that are genuinely more energy-efficient than ANNs on synchronous hardware. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger models and more complex tasks, whether real asynchronous hardware (beyond simulation) can realize the predicted energy savings, and whether the single-matrix token mixing generalizes beyond vision to other modalities.
