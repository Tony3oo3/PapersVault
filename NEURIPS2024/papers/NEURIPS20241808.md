## Problem

Existing multimodal LLMs for audio require parameter updates (adaptor training, LoRA, or full fine-tuning) to align audio and text modalities, typically focus only on understanding tasks without supporting audio generation, and demand large-scale audio-text alignment data. This paper asks whether a frozen LLM can handle diverse audio understanding and generation tasks in a few-shot in-context learning style with zero parameter updates, by treating compressed audio as a 'foreign language' the LLM can learn from a handful of demonstrations.

## Input / Output

The system accepts raw 16 kHz audio signals and a text prompt containing a task instruction plus K-shot input-output demonstrations. The audio is first compressed by LLM-Codec into a discrete token sequence of ~57 tokens per second, where each token is a word or sub-word drawn directly from the frozen LLAMA 2 vocabulary (not a separate audio codebook). For understanding tasks the LLM outputs a text class label; for generation tasks (e.g., text-to-speech, speech denoising) it outputs a new discrete token sequence that is decoded back into a 16 kHz audio waveform. The unusual constraint is that the LLM's context window limits the number of demonstrations that can be provided, and all audio tokens must be valid entries in the LLM's existing vocabulary.

## Method

The core contribution is LLM-Codec, a VQ-VAE-based audio codec that quantizes audio into the token space of a frozen LLM. It employs a three-layer multi-scale residual VQ strategy with different down-sampling rates per layer (k₁=4, k₂=2, k₃=1): the first layer encodes semantic information at low token rate, the second encodes coarse-grained acoustic detail, and the third captures residual acoustic information at full frame rate, yielding 57 tokens per second of audio after 480× encoder downsampling. Codebooks are initialized from the LLAMA 2 7B vocabulary (a 3,248-entry Oxford 5000 Words subset for layer 1; the full 32,000-entry LLAMA codebook for layers 2–3) and kept frozen throughout training, forcing the codec to map audio into the LLM's existing representational space. Two auxiliary losses stabilize and shape training: a semantic loss (L1 distance to a T5-base global representation of the audio content) guides the first VQ layer toward semantic encoding, and a consistency loss (L1 distance to Whisper encoder frame-level features) prevents collapse of the second layer given the frozen codebooks and aggressive compression. The codec is trained with a GAN objective using six multi-scale Mel-spectrogram discriminators plus time-domain and STFT reconstruction losses, on 2k hours of speech (MLS) and sound (AudioCaps) data. At inference, the frozen LLAMA 2 7B model receives the instruction, demonstrations, and query audio token sequence as a single prompt and performs in-context learning to produce either a text label or a new audio token sequence.

## Results

For audio reconstruction, LLM-Codec achieves PESQ 2.55 and STOI 0.82 while compressing 1-second audio into only 57 tokens (vs. 225 for Encodec and 150 for DAC). On 2-way speech emotion classification (ESD), the semantic-layer LLM-Codec reaches 53–59% accuracy with task induction, outperforming BLSP (29–50%). On 2-way sound event classification (ESC-50), LLM-Codec (semantic layer) achieves 57–73% accuracy versus BLSP's 15–54%. For simple text-to-speech on FSDD, the system achieves 70% content accuracy and 2.92 DNSMOS quality, and for speech denoising it reaches PESQ 2.17 / STOI 0.57 without any task-specific training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Encodec [9] and DAC [25] on reconstruction quality, LLM-Codec achieves higher PESQ (2.55 vs. 2.18 and 1.76) and STOI (0.82 vs. 0.79 and 0.78) while using substantially fewer tokens per second (57 vs. 225 and 150). Against BLSP [40] on audio understanding, LLM-Codec (semantic layer) consistently outperforms BLSP across speech emotion classification (53–59% vs. 29–50%) and 2-way sound event classification (57–73% vs. 15–54%), with the gap widening when task induction is provided; notably, BLSP degrades sharply under repeated demonstrations while LLM-Codec improves. On the Dynamic-SUPERB benchmark [16], LLM-Codec surpasses ImageBind-LLM, Whisper-LLM, and ASR-ChatGPT on 3 of 4 tasks (e.g., 50% vs. 28% on Bird Sound Detection, 55% vs. 58% on Chord Classification), though it lags ASR-ChatGPT on Language Identification (25% vs. 96%), likely due to English-only training data. For speech denoising, LLM-Codec (PESQ 2.17, STOI 0.57) underperforms the specialized diffusion model SGMSE+ [33] (PESQ 3.53, STOI 0.79), confirming that few-shot in-context learning still trails task-specific models. On text-to-audio generation (Table 10), LLM-Codec (FAD 17.16, KL 3.05) outperforms AudioGen [24] (FAD 20.4) but trails Tango (FAD 12.7) and AudioLDM (FAD 15.6).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm of multimodal LLMs (WavLLM, SALMONN, Qwen-audio, SpeechGPT) that pair a separate audio encoder with a trainable adaptor or LoRA fine-tuning to align modalities. Instead, LLM-Codec quantizes audio directly into the frozen LLM's vocabulary space using a multi-scale residual VQ initialized with LLAMA 2's codebook, treating compressed audio as a 'foreign language' the LLM can learn from few demonstrations. The multi-scale RVQ design (semantic → coarse acoustic → residual acoustic at different granularities) extends prior factorized codecs like FACodec and SpeechTokenizer, while the semantic loss (T5-guided) and consistency loss (Whisper-guided) are novel training objectives. The in-context learning framing builds on GPT-3-style few-shot prompting and audio-specific ICL work (SALM, ICL-GSLM, Dynamic-SUPERB), but is the first to apply it to both audio understanding and generation with a fully frozen LLM.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates a viable path to universal audio task solving without any LLM parameter updates, which could dramatically reduce the cost of deploying audio capabilities across diverse tasks. However, performance remains well below specialized models (e.g., 53–60% on 2-way classification vs. near-ceiling for dedicated classifiers; 2.17 PESQ vs. 3.53 for SGMSE+ in denoising), and the context-length constraint limits the number of demonstrations. Open questions include whether scaling to larger LLMs or fine-tuning with LLM-Codec on text-audio pairs closes the gap, whether the lexical representation of audio has a principled theoretical grounding, and whether the approach generalizes beyond English-centric training data. The open-sourced LLM-Codec provides a useful building block for the broader multi-modal LLM community.
