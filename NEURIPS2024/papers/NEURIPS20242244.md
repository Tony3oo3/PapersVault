## Problem

Existing coarse-to-fine point cloud registration methods produce sparse and geometrically inconsistent coarse correspondences because global cross-attention attends to similar yet irrelevant regions. This forces reliance on computationally expensive optimal transport for fine matching and RANSAC-based hypothesis-and-selection for outlier rejection, making these methods neither efficient nor scalable for real-time applications such as LiDAR odometry.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242244/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes two partially overlapped 3D point clouds (outdoor LiDAR at 0.3 m voxel resolution or indoor RGBD at 2.5 cm voxel resolution) and outputs a rigid transformation (rotation in SO(3) and translation in R³) estimated via the weighted Kabsch algorithm. Internally it produces semi-dense coarse correspondences at 1/4 resolution, sparse keypoint correspondences (typically ~1000 keypoints, <250 correspondences), and dense virtual correspondences at 1/2 resolution, all used hierarchically for pose estimation.

## Method

CAST employs a KPConv feature pyramid backbone followed by a coarse matching module built from alternating consistency-aware self-attention and spot-guided cross-attention blocks with multi-scale feature fusion between 1/4 and 1/8 resolution features. The spot-guided cross-attention restricts each query node's attention to a 'spot'—the union of neighborhoods of its confident neighbors' correspondences—selected via a consistency-aware matching confidence criterion (product of matching score and normalized generalized degree in a geometric compatibility graph), thereby avoiding interference from irrelevant regions. The consistency-aware self-attention performs two-stage sparse sampling (by generalized degree, then matching score) from the compatibility graph to attend only to salient, geometrically consistent nodes. For fine matching, a lightweight sparse-to-dense pipeline detects keypoints via an attentive keypoint detector, predicts virtual keypoint-to-patch correspondences with single-head local attention, filters outliers through three compatibility graph embedding layers (replacing RANSAC), and refines the pose with dense local attention within a fixed radius—all without optimal transport.

## Results

On KITTI odometry, CAST achieves a relative translation error (RTE) of 2.5 cm and 100% registration recall (RR), a 60.3% RTE improvement over the prior state-of-the-art DiffusionPCR (6.3 cm). On the more challenging nuScenes benchmark, CAST attains the lowest RTE (0.12 m) and RRE (0.20°) while maintaining 99.9% RR. On indoor 3DMatch, CAST reaches 95.2% RR at 1000 points with the fastest runtime (0.182 s), and on the low-overlap 3DLoMatch it achieves 75.1% RR. In the indoor registration comparison (Table 9), CAST obtains 96.48% RR, 5.64 cm RTE, and 1.71° RRE on 3DMatch, outperforming all graph-based and spectral baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CAST decisively outperforms all descriptor-based methods (3DFeat-Net, FCGF, D3Feat, SpinNet, Predator) and all coarse-to-fine correspondence methods (CoFiNet, GeoTransformer, OIF-Net, PEAL, DiffusionPCR) on KITTI RTE, reducing it from 6.3–8.2 cm to 2.5 cm, while achieving perfect 100% RR versus 99.8% for most competitors. On nuScenes, CAST beats the strongest baseline HRegNet (0.18 m / 0.45°) by 33% in RTE and 56% in RRE, and substantially outperforms traditional methods like ICP (0.15 m / 0.21° but only 36.8% RR) and RANSAC (0.21 m / 0.74°, 60.9% RR). On 3DMatch, CAST's 95.2% RR surpasses DiffusionPCR (94.5%) and PEAL (94.1%) while running 10× faster (0.182 s vs ~1.9–2.1 s); on 3DLoMatch, CAST (75.1%) trails the iterative methods PEAL (78.8%) and DiffusionPCR (79.2%) but remains competitive with non-iterative baselines and is the fastest. In the indoor registration comparison (Table 9), CAST dominates all graph/spectral baselines (MAC, FastMAC, SC2-PCR, PointDSC, TEASER++) on both RR and pose error. The only consistent weakness is RRE on KITTI (0.27° vs 0.23° for several methods), which the authors attribute to numerical artifacts in SVD-based pose estimation rather than true rotational inaccuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

CAST extends the coarse-to-fine matching paradigm established by CoFiNet, GeoTransformer, and OIF-Net, but departs from their reliance on global cross-attention and optimal transport fine matching. It adapts the spot-guided cross-attention concept from ASTR (2D image matching) to 3D point clouds with a novel consistency-aware confidence criterion combining feature similarity and geometric compatibility, and borrows the compatibility graph embedding from PointDSC for outlier rejection. The key conceptual shift is from sparse, loose coarse correspondences requiring expensive optimal transport to semi-dense, geometrically consistent correspondences that enable a lightweight sparse-to-dense fine matching via local attention.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 60% reduction in translation error on KITTI (2.5cm vs. 6.3cm) combined with the lowest runtime (0.182s vs. ~2s for iterative methods) makes CAST practically relevant for real-time odometry and SLAM, where existing coarse-to-fine methods are too slow. The fine matching module's ability to deploy independently without coarse matching opens a path to lightweight registration pipelines with pose priors. However, performance degrades in very low-overlap scenarios (3DLoMatch), where the sparsity of keypoints limits inlier ratio, and the authors acknowledge that dense feature matching or better outlier rejection for coarse correspondences remains an open direction.
