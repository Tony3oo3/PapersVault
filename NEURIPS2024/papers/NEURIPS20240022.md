## Problem

While spectral clustering enjoys well-established consistency guarantees for community detection in stochastic block models, modern embedding methods such as node2vec and DeepWalk—despite their strong empirical performance—lack theoretical guarantees for what information their learned representations carry. This paper asks whether node2vec embeddings, when used as features for k-means clustering, can provably recover the true community structure of nodes in (degree-corrected) stochastic block models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240022/cropped_page8_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an undirected network adjacency matrix generated from a (degree-corrected) stochastic block model as input and produces d-dimensional node embedding vectors as intermediate output, which are then clustered via k-means to yield community assignments. The embedding dimension d must satisfy d ≥ rank(M*), where M* is a κ×κ matrix determined by the SBM parameters and node2vec hyperparameters; in practice d=64 is used. The theoretical guarantees hold under specific sparsity regimes: ρ_n ≫ log(n)/n for DeepWalk on SBMs, ρ_n = n^{-α} for general node2vec, and ρ_n ≫ log(n)/n with bounded degree heterogeneity for DCSBMs.

## Method

The core result shows that the node2vec loss function concentrates uniformly around a population-level surrogate whose global minimizer has the form M* = Π_C M̃* Π_C^T, where M̃* is a κ×κ matrix depending only on community labels. The proof proceeds by first showing that positive and negative sampling probabilities concentrate around functions of the latent community variables (Theorems S1–S3), then establishing uniform convergence of the empirical loss to its expectation over the adjacency matrix via chaining bounds (Theorem S5), and finally characterizing the unique minimizer of the population loss. This yields that any minimizer of the node2vec loss produces embeddings that converge (up to rotation) to community-dependent vectors at rate O_p((max{log n, d}/(nρ_n))^{1/2}), which directly implies weak consistency of k-means clustering with misclassification rate O_p(δ^{-2} r_n). The analysis covers both unconstrained (U≠V) and constrained (U=V) embedding regimes, with the constrained case requiring p̃ > q̃ for non-trivial recovery.

## Results

The paper's primary contribution is theoretical: it proves that k-means clustering on node2vec embeddings yields weakly consistent community recovery (misclassification rate → 0 in probability) under SBM and DCSBM models, with convergence rate O_p((max{log n, d}/(nρ_n))^{1/2}) in the dense regime. Empirically, on the Political Blog network (1,490 nodes, 2 communities), node2vec + k-means achieves an average NMI of 0.75 across embedding dimensions and unigram parameters, while spectral clustering fails to recover the communities due to degree heterogeneity. On the Email-Eu-Core network (986 nodes, 42 departments), node2vec + k-means substantially outperforms spectral clustering in NMI across all tested embedding dimensions (d = 16–256) and unigram parameters (α = −1 to 1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline compared is spectral clustering (using the normalized graph Laplacian), as introduced by Ng et al. [37]. On the Political Blog network, node2vec + k-means achieves an average NMI of 0.75, while spectral clustering is described as unable to recover the communities at all, regardless of which graph Laplacian variant is used — a failure attributed to degree heterogeneity in the network. On the Email-Eu-Core network, node2vec + k-means again shows substantially higher NMI than spectral clustering across all parameter settings tested. The paper also notes that in simulated SBM/DC-SBM experiments, node2vec's empirical convergence rate appears super-linear for dense networks but sub-linear for relatively sparse ones, suggesting it may be suboptimal compared to spectral methods in the sparse regime (citing Zhang [50]). Overall, node2vec + k-means wins decisively on both real networks, with the advantage most pronounced where degree heterogeneity is high.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper provides the first asymptotic distributional analysis of node2vec/DeepWalk embeddings under (degree-corrected) stochastic block models, filling a gap left by the well-developed theory for spectral clustering [30, 37]. It builds directly on the node2vec [16] and DeepWalk [38] random-walk embedding framework, and positions itself against concurrent work by Zhang and Tang [52] (which studies best-rank-d approximations of the minimizer under p=q=1) and Davison [10, 11] (which study constrained embeddings and subsampling). The key departure is that this paper gives guarantees for the actual minimizers of the node2vec cross-entropy loss at embedding dimension d = Ω(κ), rather than for Frobenius-norm approximations, and extends to general node2vec hyperparameters p, q beyond the DeepWalk special case.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result gives practitioners a principled justification for the widely-used node2vec + k-means pipeline, showing it achieves weakly consistent community recovery under standard SBM/DCSBM assumptions. However, the guarantees are limited to weak consistency (not strong), the DCSBM theory requires the unigram parameter α=1, and the constrained U=V regime can be uninformative when within-community edges do not dominate. Open questions include extending to strong consistency (potentially by combining with [52]), handling more general sparsity regimes, and providing guarantees for newer embedding methods like GraphSAGE [17] and Deep Graph Infomax [47]. The empirical finding that node2vec outperforms spectral clustering on real networks with degree heterogeneity (e.g., political blogs) while spectral clustering fails entirely is practically useful but not deeply surprising given the DCSBM motivation.
