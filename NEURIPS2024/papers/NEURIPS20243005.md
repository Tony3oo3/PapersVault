## Problem

On-device model adaptation is blocked by backpropagation's need to store intermediate activations, which is prohibitive on edge devices with limited memory. While forward gradients (zeroth-order optimization) eliminate this storage requirement, their feasibility in the fixed-point precision used by actual edge hardware (NPUs, DSPs, MCUs) remains unexplored. This paper investigates whether quantized forward gradients can achieve competitive accuracy while operating entirely within the arithmetic constraints of existing inference accelerators.

## Input / Output

The method consumes pre-trained model weights in 16-bit fixed-point format along with local data (RGB images at 28×28 to 84×84, or audio clips of 0.3s–30s) and a small perturbation scale ε (typically 1e-3). All computation—weight perturbation, two forward passes, sign-based gradient estimation, and weight update—is performed in fixed-point arithmetic (16-bit weights, 8-bit perturbation/gradients), requiring only a 32-bit accumulator and multiply-shift operations available on standard inference hardware. The output is the adapted model in the same 16-bit quantized format, with no float operations needed at any stage.

## Method

The proposed QZO-FF algorithm replaces backpropagation with sign-m-SPSA: for each of m random perturbation directions z, it performs two forward passes (with +εz and −εz perturbed weights), takes only the sign of the loss difference, and accumulates the signed perturbation direction as the gradient estimate. This sign-based design is inherently quantization-friendly, constraining gradient values to the same range as the perturbation for static quantization. All operations are formulated in the fixed-point domain: the perturbation is quantized using the weight's scaling factor Δ_w, re-quantization after addition uses a multiply-shift approximation, and the learning rate is absorbed into a pre-computed re-quantization constant. Four enhancements further close the accuracy gap: momentum-guided sampling (blending zero-centered and momentum-centered Gaussian perturbations), sharpness-aware perturbation (an extra gradient-ascent step before the main update), sparse update (pruning 90% of trainable parameters), and kernel-wise normalization. A critical finding is that 16-bit weight precision is necessary—8-bit weights fail to converge because the small ε perturbation is clipped by the coarse quantization step—while 8-bit suffices for perturbation and gradient storage.

## Results

On vision few-shot learning (5-way 5-shot), forward gradient (FF) learning achieves comparable accuracy (within 5%) to backpropagation (BP) on 26 out of 30 tasks across ResNet-12, ResNet-18, and ViT-tiny backbones. The largest gap is −11.08% (ResNet-12 FT on Cifar100_fs), while FF sometimes matches or exceeds BP (e.g., ViT-tiny FT on CUB: 93.58% vs 93.08%). On audio few-shot learning (5-way 1-shot), FF achieves comparable or better performance to BP on 11 out of 16 tasks, with the largest positive gap being +11.66% (AST FT on ESC-50 SimpleShot). Quantized FF (16w8a) incurs minimal additional degradation (≤1%) over fp16 FF, and reduces scratch memory by 2.8× (31.64→11.43 MB for ViT-tiny FT) or to just 0.40 MB with sparse update.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is between forward gradient (FF) learning and standard backpropagation (BP), with zero-shot as a lower-bound reference. FF generally trails BP by 1–5% on most vision tasks but narrows the gap substantially with stronger backbones: ViT-tiny FF,LP matches or beats BP,LP on 3 of 5 datasets (e.g., +0.06% on Cifar100_fs, +0.62% on miniImageNet), while ResNet-12 FF,FT lags BP,FT by up to 11.08% on Cifar100_fs. On audio, FF can exceed BP by as much as +11.66% (AST FT on ESC-50 SimpleShot) but also falls short by up to −11.39% (CRNN FT on ESC-50 ProtoNet), reflecting the extreme difficulty of 5-way 1-shot. Quantized FF (16w8a) adds at most ~1% degradation over fp16 FF, confirming that fixed-point computation is viable. In cross-domain adaptation (VWW), quantized FF with LP reaches 87.30% vs BP's 87.93% (0.63% gap), and in OOD adaptation (Cifar10-C), the worst FF-vs-BP gap is 6.98% (hard corruption, LP 3-layer decoder), while with 90% sparsity FF still stays within ~1% of full FF.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the forward gradient / zeroth-order optimization lineage (Baydin et al. 2022, Liu et al. 2020, Spall 1992) by formulating the entire training pipeline—weight perturbation, gradient estimation, and weight update—in the fixed-point quantized space, yielding the QZO-FF estimator. It departs from MeZO (Malladi et al. 2023), which demonstrated forward-gradient fine-tuning of LLMs in float precision, by targeting the fixed-point inference engines (NPUs, DSPs, MCUs) that dominate edge hardware and by proposing sign-m-SPSA to suppress the high-variance noise inherent in two-forward-call gradient estimation. The algorithmic enhancements (momentum-guided sampling, sharpness-aware perturbation, sparse update, kernel-wise normalization) are positioned as practical bridges to close the remaining accuracy gap versus backpropagation in the quantized regime.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first comprehensive empirical evidence that quantized forward gradients (16w8a) can adapt diverse architectures (ResNet, ViT, CRNN, AST; 100K–80M params) across vision and audio benchmarks with only a same-as-inference memory footprint, making on-device fine-tuning feasible on hardware that lacks backpropagation support. Key limitations remain: 8-bit weight quantization fails to converge (16-bit is mandatory), a good pre-trained initialization is critical (training from scratch is not guaranteed), and the accuracy gap widens on harder OOD corruptions and smaller backbones. Open questions include whether ultra-low-bit (4-bit, 8-bit) forward gradient learning is achievable with new quantization schemes, and whether the approach extends beyond classification to generative or multi-modal tasks.
