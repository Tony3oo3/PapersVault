## Problem

Entity alignment across knowledge graphs of different scales produces dangling entities (nodes with no counterpart), yet their labels are far more expensive to acquire than matchable pairs. Prior methods either require a portion of labeled dangling entities, rely on side information like entity names, or assume one-to-one correspondence. This paper tackles the stricter and more realistic setting where neither labeled dangling entities nor side information are available.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242506/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes two KGs (source and target) plus a small set of pre-aligned anchor pairs (30% of matchable entities for training) as input, with no labeled dangling entities or side information. It produces three outputs: (1) a binary matchable/dangling classification for every node, (2) an estimated proportion π_p of matchable entities that gates whether the two KGs are even alignable, and (3) aligned entity pairs selected as mutually nearest neighbors in the learned embedding space. The pipeline is two-phase: dangling detection first, then alignment only on predicted matchable entities.

## Method

The framework Lambda combines a GNN encoder (KEESA) with two losses. KEESA uses an adaptive dangling indicator (a learnable scalar r_ej passed through tanh) to selectively down-weight dangling neighbors during aggregation, a relation projection attention that projects relation embeddings onto entities with an orthogonal regularizer, Householder transformations for intra-graph representation, and a proxy matching attention layer for cross-graph alignment, all fused via a gating mechanism. The spectral contrastive learning loss L_info is derived from the InfoNCE-to-spectral-clustering equivalence and employs a custom soft-margin alignment loss H(·); its inherent asymmetry (dangling entities appear only as negatives, matchable only as positives) naturally separates the two classes in embedding space. For dangling detection, iPULE is an iterative positive-unlabeled learning algorithm that derives an unbiased risk estimator without assuming p_u(x)=p(x), proves a tighter uniform deviation bound than the Non-negative Risk Estimator, and guarantees convergence via equivalence to the EM algorithm. A warm-up phase jointly optimizes L_info and L_pu, after which the algorithm alternates an E-step (estimating π_p^u from classifier outputs) and an M-step (updating model parameters) until convergence.

## Results

On GA16K (dangling-unaware setting), Lambda achieves Hits@1 = 67.59, outperforming the strongest baseline Dual-AMN (64.49) by ~3 points, while remaining competitive on Hits@10 (80.33 vs. 80.55) and Hits@50 (84.35 vs. 84.67). On DBP2.0 in the consolidated setting (dangling-aware, no labeled dangling entities), Lambda achieves the best F1 across all six language directions for both dangling detection (e.g., ZH-EN F1 = 0.836 vs. MTransE+BR 0.767) and entity alignment (e.g., ZH-EN F1 = 0.344 vs. MTransE+BR 0.335), despite not using any of the 30% labeled dangling entities that baselines exploit. The method's recall advantage is substantial (e.g., ZH-EN alignment recall 0.447 vs. 0.362 for MTransE+BR), though precision is slightly lower in some directions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against dangling-aware baselines on DBP2.0 (consolidated setting), Lambda outperforms MTransE+BR (the strongest baseline) on F1 in all six language directions for entity alignment, with the largest gains on FR-EN (F1 0.271 vs. 0.233) and EN-FR (0.251 vs. 0.222), while AliNet variants trail by a wider margin (e.g., ZH-EN F1 0.238 for AliNet+BR vs. 0.344 for Lambda). For dangling detection, Lambda achieves F1 = 0.836 on ZH-EN versus 0.767 for MTransE+BR and 0.643 for AliNet+BR, with the advantage driven primarily by higher recall (0.925 vs. 0.728). On GA16K (dangling-unaware setting), Lambda's Hits@1 of 67.59 beats Dual-AMN (64.49) and MRAEA (63.97), though Dual-AMN edges ahead slightly on Hits@10 (80.55 vs. 80.33) and Hits@50 (84.67 vs. 84.35). TransEdge performs catastrophically (Hits@1 = 0.03) due to error propagation from dangling entities corrupting its bootstrapping. The paper notes that Lambda's slightly lower precision in some directions stems from dangling entities residing at the decision boundary, as visualized in the embedding space, but the overall F1 advantage is consistent and the method requires no labeled dangling entities—unlike all baselines which use 30%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the entity alignment (EA) literature on dangling entities by removing two assumptions that prior methods rely on: labeled dangling entities (required by Sun et al. [33] and MHP [19]) and side information such as entity names (required by UED [24] and SoTead [23]). Methodologically, it builds on the Proxy Matching Attention Layer from Dual-AMN [25] for cross-graph representation, derives its spectral contrastive loss from the InfoNCE-to-spectral-clustering equivalence established by Tan et al. [40], and improves upon the Non-negative Risk Estimator of Kiryo et al. [13] by removing the p_u(x)=p(x) constraint and proving a tighter uniform deviation bound. The KEESA encoder's adaptive dangling indicator and relation projection attention are novel architectural components designed to prevent 'pollution' of matchable entity embeddings during neighborhood aggregation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a realistic and underexplored setting where dangling entity labels are prohibitively expensive to obtain (e.g., KG plagiarism detection, cross-scale KG fusion), and provides the first framework with theoretical guarantees (unbiasedness, tighter deviation bound, EM-style convergence) for the associated PU learning problem. A practical byproduct is that the estimated matchable proportion π_p can serve as a pre-screening signal to avoid wasting computation on unalignable KG pairs. Limitations include slightly inferior precision compared to MTransE w/ BR (attributed to dangling entities residing at the decision boundary) and the reliance on a preference condition (Δ_U ≈ Δ_P) for the convergence proof that may not always hold in practice. Open questions include whether the approach scales to very large KGs beyond ~200K entities and whether the iterative prior estimation remains stable under highly imbalanced positive proportions.
