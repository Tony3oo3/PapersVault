## Problem

Proportional fairness guarantees (the core and fully justified representation) for clustering had only been studied in the centroid setting, where each agent's loss is its distance to a cluster center. In many applications—clustered federated learning, document clustering, social network segmentation—there are no cluster centers; agents simply prefer to be near other agents in their cluster. This paper initiates the study of proportional fairness in this non-centroid setting, asking whether compelling guarantees exist and whether algorithms known to work for centroid clustering transfer.

## Input / Output

The method takes a set of n agents, a distance metric d satisfying the triangle inequality, and a target number of clusters k. Agent losses are defined by one of three classes: arbitrary (any non-negative function of the cluster), average (mean distance to co-cluster members), or maximum (distance to the farthest co-cluster member). The primary output is a k-clustering partition; the auditing variant (AuditFJR) additionally takes a given clustering C and returns a multiplicative estimate θ of its FJR approximation ratio, guaranteed to lie within a constant factor of the true value.

## Method

The core algorithm is GREEDYCOHESIVECLUSTERING, a parametric greedy framework that iteratively calls a subroutine A to find a 'cohesive' cluster of at least ⌈n/k⌉ agents and removes them, repeating until fewer than ⌈n/k⌉ agents remain. The efficient instantiation GREEDYCAPTURE uses SMALLESTAGENTBALL, which finds the smallest agent-centered ball capturing ⌈n/k⌉ agents—differing from the centroid version by growing balls centered at agents (not arbitrary locations) and stopping once the threshold is met. The key theoretical insight is that SMALLESTAGENTBALL is a 4-approximation (average loss) or 2-approximation (maximum loss) for the MOST COHESIVE CLUSTER problem, and via a general reduction (Theorem 4), plugging any λ-approximation into GREEDYCOHESIVECLUSTERING yields a λ-FJR clustering. This gives exact FJR existence for arbitrary losses (using an exhaustive subroutine) and constant-factor FJR in polynomial time for structured losses. A separate AuditFJR algorithm reuses the same cohesive-cluster subroutine to estimate the FJR violation of any given clustering up to a constant factor.

## Results

Theoretically, GREEDYCAPTURE achieves a (2·⌈n/k⌉−3)-core for average loss and a 2-core for maximum loss in O(kn) time, while providing 4-FJR and 2-FJR respectively in polynomial time. An inefficient algorithm (GREEDYCOHESIVECLUSTERING with an exact subroutine) achieves exact FJR (α=1) even under arbitrary loss functions. Empirically, on Census Income, Diabetes, and Iris datasets, GREEDYCAPTURE's FJR approximation stays very close to 1 across all values of k, while its accuracy (average within-cluster distance, k-means, and k-medoids objectives) is less than twice that of k-means++ and k-medoids.

|---|---|---|---|
| Arbitrary | ∞ | ∞ | 1 |
| Average | O(n/k) (polytime) | 1.3 | 1 (4 in polytime) |
| Maximum | 2 (polytime) | 1 | 1 (2 in polytime) |

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares GREEDYCAPTURE against k-means++ and k-medoids on three real datasets (Census Income, Diabetes, Iris). Across all four fairness metrics (core and FJR for both average and maximum loss), GREEDYCAPTURE is significantly fairer than both baselines, consistently across different values of k. Notably, the FJR approximation of GREEDYCAPTURE empirically stays very close to 1, far better than its worst-case bounds of 4 (average) and 2 (maximum). The fairness advantage comes at only a modest accuracy cost: all three traditional clustering objectives (average within-cluster distance, k-means, k-medoids) achieved by GREEDYCAPTURE are less than twice those of k-means++ and k-medoids. This contrasts with the centroid clustering experiments of Chen et al. [1], where k-means++ outperformed GREEDYCAPTURE on core approximation for Iris and Diabetes; in the non-centroid setting, GREEDYCAPTURE significantly outperforms k-means++.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the proportional fairness framework for clustering from the centroid setting (Chen et al., ICML 2019; Aziz et al., 2023) to non-centroid clustering, where agent loss depends on co-clustered agents rather than a cluster center. The main algorithm, GREEDYCAPTURE, is a direct adaptation of the centroid-clustering algorithm from Chen et al., with two key modifications: balls are centered at agents rather than feasible center locations, and balls stop growing once they capture ⌈n/k⌉ agents. The FJR guarantee is adapted from Peters et al. (NeurIPS 2021) via the Greedy Cohesive Rule, and the paper also draws connections to the hedonic games literature on coalition formation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper reveals a fundamental structural difference between centroid and non-centroid clustering: the core can be empty for average loss (with a lower bound of ~1.366), and the same GREEDYCAPTURE algorithm that achieves constant core approximation in the centroid setting degrades to O(n/k) here. FJR, while a slight relaxation, is always satisfiable even under arbitrary losses, making it the more practical guarantee. Key open questions include whether the core is always non-empty for maximum loss, whether a constant core approximation exists for average loss, and whether a single clustering can simultaneously satisfy FJR for both loss functions with a better ratio than 4/2.
