## Problem

Deep learning models require massive training datasets and operate as black boxes with no explicit decision criteria, making it impossible to anticipate performance under domain shift or to reconstruct the model from a small subset of data. Unlike SVMs, which encode their decision boundary in a small set of support vectors recoverable via the KKT condition, deep networks offer no analogous mechanism. This paper asks whether a principled generalization of the KKT condition can identify 'support vectors' within trained deep networks, enabling both interpretability and data-efficient reconstruction.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a frozen pretrained classifier (ConvNet for CIFAR-10/100/SVHN at 32×32, ResNet-50 for ImageNet at 224×224), a target class label (hard one-hot or soft mixture y = (1−δ)y_a + δy_b), and an initial image drawn from Gaussian noise or a real sample. It outputs a small set of generated Deep Support Vectors (one or more images per class) that lie on the data manifold and encode the decision boundary, a distilled dataset for few-shot retraining, or edited images whose class has been shifted via latent guidance. Critically, the classifier parameters θ* are never updated; only the input image x and per-sample Lagrange multipliers λ are optimized, requiring one forward pass and two backward passes per iteration.

## Method

The core contribution is the DeepKKT condition, a four-part generalization of the classical SVM KKT conditions to nonlinear, multi-class deep networks: (i) primal feasibility (the frozen model correctly classifies x), (ii) dual feasibility (λ_i ≥ 0), (iii) stationarity (θ* = −Σ λ_i ∇_θ L(Φ(x_i;θ*), y_i)), and (iv) a manifold constraint ensuring generated samples lie on the data distribution. The key mechanistic shift is replacing the weight vector θ with the input x as the optimization variable, so the stationarity condition becomes a loss L_stat = D(θ*, −Σ λ_i ∇_θ L) that is minimized over x and λ rather than over network parameters. To keep generated images on the data manifold, the total loss augments L_stat with a primal cross-entropy term for misclassified candidates, total-variation and L1-norm image priors, and an augmentation-invariance term that enforces the KKT conditions hold under random crops, flips, and noise. The resulting optimization (Algorithm 1) iteratively refines N×C candidate images and their multipliers, discarding any candidate whose λ drops below zero, until convergence—yielding DSVs that visually encode decision criteria, outperform gradient-matching baselines in few-shot distillation, and, when driven by soft labels, function as a lightweight latent generative model analogous to score-based diffusion.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 few-shot dataset distillation, DSVs achieve 22.69% top-1 accuracy with 1 image/class and 1 training sample (0.02% ratio), outperforming DC (16.48%), DSA (15.41%), and DM (13.03%). At 50 images/class with 50 training samples (1% ratio), DSVs reach 53.56% accuracy versus 41.22% (DC), 41.29% (DSA), and 48.93% (DM). Uniquely, DSVs also support zero-shot distillation (no training data access), achieving 21.68%, 30.35%, and 39.35% accuracy at 1, 10, and 50 images/class respectively, a setting infeasible for all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares DSVs against three dataset distillation baselines: DC (gradient matching, Zhao et al. 2020), DSA (differentiable siamese augmentation, Zhao et al. 2021), and DM (distribution matching, Zhao et al. 2022). In every directly comparable setting, DSVs win decisively: at 1 img/cls with 1 shot, DSVs gain +6.21 pp over DC and +9.66 pp over DM; at 10 img/cls with 10 shots, DSVs lead by +12.84 pp over DC and +8.13 pp over DM; at 50 img/cls with 50 shots, DSVs surpass the strongest baseline DM by +4.63 pp. Critically, DSVs are the only method capable of zero-shot distillation (0 training samples), where baselines are entirely inapplicable because they require access to the full training set for gradient or distribution matching. The baselines also require Hessian computation and model snapshots from all training stages, conditions DSVs do not need, making DSVs the only method applicable in practical edge-device or privacy-sensitive settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of model inversion work by Haim et al. (NeurIPS 2022) and Yu & Wang (NeurIPS 2023), which exploited the KKT stationarity condition to reconstruct or generate data from trained classifiers, but were limited to binary classification, low-dimensional datasets (MNIST, CIFAR-10), and SVM-like architectures. The DeepKKT condition generalizes this to multi-class, high-dimensional deep networks (ResNet-50 on ImageNet) by adding a manifold constraint and adapting the primal feasibility to cross-entropy loss. The theoretical justification for the stationarity condition draws on the implicit bias analysis of Soudry et al. (ICLR 2018), connecting the DeepKKT formulation to the gradient dynamics of overparameterized networks. Unlike DeepSVM approaches that modify architectures to incorporate SVM components, this work operates entirely on frozen pretrained models without architectural changes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a compelling conceptual bridge between SVM interpretability and deep learning, providing a mechanism for global (rather than local) model explanations and enabling dataset distillation without access to training data—addressing both privacy and communication bottlenecks in federated/edge settings. The finding that a ResNet-50 classifier can function as a lightweight latent generative model (1/20th the size of SDXL) for interpolation and editing is practically significant, though the generative quality evidence remains primarily qualitative. A key limitation acknowledged by the authors is that the equivalence between DeepKKT and true SVM support vectors is intuitive rather than rigorously proven, leaving the theoretical foundation as an open problem. The model inversion capability also raises dual-use concerns for privacy, which the authors partially address by noting DSVs represent generalized boundaries rather than specific training samples.
