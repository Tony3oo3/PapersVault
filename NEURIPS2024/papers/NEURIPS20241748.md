## Problem

Transformer-based time series forecasters, despite state-of-the-art performance, remain vulnerable to high-frequency signals, computationally expensive, and unable to fully exploit the entire frequency spectrum. A simple synthetic experiment shows that even iTransformer fails to accurately forecast a signal composed of just three frequency components, revealing a fundamental bottleneck in how attention mechanisms process spectral information. The paper asks whether frequency filters from classical signal processing can directly replace MLP and Transformer blocks for time series forecasting.

## Input / Output

The model takes a multivariate time series tensor of shape N×L (N variables, lookback window L) and produces predictions of shape N×τ for τ future steps. Before filtering, the input undergoes reversible instance normalization (RevIN) along the time dimension to handle non-stationarity, and the output is denormalized after the feed-forward network. The filter bandwidth is set equal to the lookback window length L, and the model has only two hyperparameters: this bandwidth and the FFN hidden size, making it extremely lightweight compared to Transformer baselines.

## Method

FilterNet replaces the standard MLP or Transformer temporal modeling block with a frequency filter block operating in the Fourier domain. Two learnable filter types are proposed: (i) PaiFilter, which applies a randomly initialized, channel-shared complex-valued kernel via element-wise multiplication in the frequency domain (equivalent to circular convolution in time), and (ii) TexFilter, which generates a data-dependent filter through a small neural network that embeds the Fourier-transformed input and applies learnable complex-valued multiplications with an activation. The full pipeline is: RevIN → FFT → frequency filter (PaiFilter or TexFilter) → IFFT → FFN → InverseNorm. The key insight is that this filtering operation can approximately surrogate both linear mappings (as in DLinear) and attention mappings (as in iTransformer), while naturally handling high-frequency noise and utilizing the full spectrum, achieving O(log L) complexity via FFT.

## Results

On the standard L=96 setting, FilterNet (PaiFilter) achieves the best MSE across all eight benchmarks: 0.318 on ETTm1, 0.174 on ETTm2, 0.375 on ETTh1, 0.292 on ETTh2, 0.176 on Electricity, 0.083 on Exchange, 0.395 on Traffic, and 0.164 on Weather (τ=96). The average improvement over all 12 baselines is statistically significant at 95% confidence. The model's computational complexity is O(log L), and it matches DLinear in training time while delivering substantially lower MSE (e.g., 0.176 vs. 0.200 on Electricity at τ=96).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FilterNet (PaiFilter) outperforms all 12 baselines on the majority of dataset–horizon combinations. Against the strongest Transformer baselines, PaiFilter beats iTransformer by 3–10% MSE on most datasets (e.g., 0.318 vs. 0.334 on ETTm1-96; 0.395 vs. 0.462 on Traffic-96), though iTransformer edges ahead on Electricity-96 (0.148 vs. 0.176) and ETTh2-720 (0.427 vs. 0.430). PatchTST is consistently outperformed, with gaps widening at longer horizons (e.g., 0.456 vs. 0.454 on ETTm1-720 is close, but 0.467 vs. 0.626 on Traffic-720 is a 25% gap). Among MLP-based baselines, DLinear is the closest competitor but still trails PaiFilter on 6 of 8 datasets at τ=96; RLinear shows similar performance to DLinear. FITS, which uses a low-pass filter, is uniformly outperformed, supporting the paper's claim that an all-pass frequency filter is more effective than a low-pass one. TexFilter (the data-dependent variant) further improves over PaiFilter on large-scale datasets like Electricity (0.147 vs. 0.176 at τ=96) and Exchange (0.091 vs. 0.083 at τ=96), while PaiFilter remains superior on smaller datasets like ETTh1 (0.375 vs. 0.382 at τ=96). Older Transformer baselines (Informer, Autoformer, Pyraformer, FEDformer) and TCN-based methods (MICN, TimesNet) are substantially outperformed, with MSE gaps often exceeding 20%.

## Relation to Prior Work

FilterNet carves new ground by repurposing classical signal-processing frequency filters as the sole architectural primitive for time series forecasting, rather than embedding frequency components into existing Transformer or MLP backbones as done by FEDformer, FITS, or FreTS. Conceptually, it unifies two dominant paradigms—MLP-based linear mappings (DLinear, RLinear) and Transformer-based attention (iTransformer, PatchTST)—as special cases of learnable frequency filtering: the plain shaping filter (PaiFilter) surrogates static linear weights, while the contextual shaping filter (TexFilter) surrogates data-dependent attention scores. The paper explicitly departs from prior frequency-domain work by using all-pass filters as the core mechanism rather than low-pass filtering or FFT-based attention modifications, and by wrapping filters in instance normalization (RevIN) to handle non-stationarity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a compelling conceptual simplification: a single learnable complex-valued frequency kernel plus an FFN can match or exceed architectures with attention, multi-scale convolutions, or decomposition blocks, at O(log L) complexity. This suggests that much of the architectural complexity in current SOTA forecasters may be unnecessary, and that the key inductive bias for time series is frequency selectivity rather than sequential modeling. Open questions include whether the channel-independence assumption (shared filters across variables) limits performance on strongly coupled multivariate systems, whether the all-pass filter advantage generalizes to non-forecasting tasks (classification, anomaly detection), and whether the approach scales to very long sequences (thousands of timesteps) where the FFT cost becomes non-trivial. The paper is primarily empirical with no theoretical guarantees on filter expressiveness or convergence.
