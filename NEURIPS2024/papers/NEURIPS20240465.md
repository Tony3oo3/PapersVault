## Problem

Existing AI alignment methods (RLHF, DPO) are trained on static snapshots of contemporary human preferences, making them susceptible to reinforcing current moral blindspots and biases at societal scale — a phenomenon the paper calls value lock-in. No prior alignment framework incorporates the temporal dimension of evolving human values, and no experimental infrastructure exists to study how AI systems might learn and implement the mechanics of moral progress rather than merely reflecting the status quo.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240465/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework consumes 38GB of historical text spanning 1221–2022 AD and 18 historical LLMs (Llama3-8B/70B continued-pretrained per century) that serve as human proxy models. At each of 9 time steps (one per century), the AI agent issues natural-language requests and receives responses from the human proxy; these interactions produce ±1 preference annotations on matched response pairs. The output is an updated model policy plus a 19-dimensional value embedding φ(θ) computed from 5104 questions across four categories (basic morality, social morality, values, views), with benchmark scores expressed as sums of cosine similarities between the agent's and target's value embeddings.

## Method

ProgressGym formalizes progress alignment as a POMDP where the state space is the parameter space of historical LLMs (human proxies), actions are natural-language requests, and observations are proxy responses, with a 19-dimensional value embedding φ providing an interpretable projection of model parameters. Three codified challenges instantiate different transition and utility functions: PG-Follow (deterministic state trajectory, accuracy utility), PG-Predict (stochastic transition, progress-weighted utility favoring future values), and PG-Coevolve (bidirectional influence where AI outputs finetune the human proxy, measuring net progress induced). As baseline algorithms, lifelong methods apply a classical aligner (RLHF or DPO) at each time step in either iterative or independent mode, while extrapolative methods first compute M-th-order backward-difference extrapolations of preference annotations (analytically equivalent to polynomial extrapolation on the reward/loss function) before passing the projected future preferences to the aligner. The morality evaluation pipeline computes marginal action and inclination likelihoods across multiple question templates to build the 19-dimensional embedding, drawing questions from Moral Choice, MFQ, and IWF frameworks expanded via LLM-generated scenarios.

## Results

Across the three ProgressGym challenges (full scores: PG-Follow=8, PG-Predict=45, PG-Coevolve=45), DPO consistently outperforms RLHF as the classical alignment backbone, winning 13 out of 15 head-to-head comparisons. The best PG-Follow score is 7.034/8 (Lifelong-Iterative + DPO), the best PG-Predict score is 31.683/45 (Lifelong-Iterative + DPO), and the best PG-Coevolve score is 38.959/45 (Extrapolative_{2,2}-Independent + DPO). Notably, second-order extrapolation (Extrapolative_{2,2}) outperforms lifelong following in PG-Follow under RLHF (6.753 vs. 4.275), while first-order extrapolation (Extrapolative_{1,1}) is consistently the weakest variant across all settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against external prior-work baselines; instead, it evaluates its own proposed algorithm families (Lifelong vs. Extrapolative_{1,1} vs. Extrapolative_{2,2}) under two classical alignment backbones (RLHF and DPO). DPO dominates RLHF in 13 of 15 direct comparisons, supporting the hypothesis that DPO's lack of a reward model avoids initialization-induced biases. Within the RLHF column, Extrapolative_{2,2}-Independent achieves the best PG-Follow score (6.753 vs. Lifelong-Independent's 4.275), suggesting that second-order predictive extrapolation adds value when the underlying alignment method is less stable. Under DPO, Lifelong-Iterative edges out all extrapolative variants on PG-Follow (7.034) and PG-Predict (31.683), indicating that DPO's inherent stability reduces the benefit of explicit extrapolation. First-order extrapolation (Extrapolative_{1,1}) is uniformly the weakest configuration, hinting that moral progress dynamics are not well captured by simple linear trends. Several algorithm-challenge pairs (marked N/A or blank) failed due to the alignment process breaking the model's instruction-following capability, a limitation the authors flag as an open robustness challenge.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the AI alignment literature by introducing a temporal dimension that prior methods (RLHF, DPO, and their variants) explicitly lack. It builds directly on RLHF and DPO as classical alignment building blocks (Γ_classical), wrapping them in lifelong and extrapolative pipelines, and draws its evaluation questions from the Moral Choice framework, MFQ, and IWF questionnaire. Conceptually, it bridges the AI alignment literature with the moral philosophy and cultural evolution literature on human moral progress (Buchanan & Powell, Singer), repurposing the notion of societal moral improvement as a technical target for alignment algorithms. The POMDP formulation with evolving value states is a deliberate generalization of the standard static-preference alignment setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ProgressGym provides the first experimental infrastructure for studying temporal alignment, with 38GB of historical text spanning 9 centuries and 18 historical LLMs, making it a potentially important resource for the alignment community. However, the authors acknowledge significant limitations: limited cultural diversity in the dataset, human proxy models with only partial fidelity to historical value trends, and explicitly exploratory benchmark results. Key open questions include whether extrapolative methods can capture genuinely novel moral concepts (as opposed to quantitative shifts along existing dimensions), how the framework scales to real-world multi-stakeholder settings, and whether the value-neutral approach to moral progress is sufficient or whether directional assumptions are unavoidable.
