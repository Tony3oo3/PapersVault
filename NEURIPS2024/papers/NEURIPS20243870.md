## Problem

Vision-based model-free RL agents struggle to generalize to test environments with dynamically changing backgrounds, as demonstrated by significant performance drops on the 'Video Hard' benchmark of DMControl-GB. The paper identifies two root causes of this degradation: imbalanced saliency, where the agent biases salient features toward the most recent frames in a stacked input, and observational overfitting, where the agent latches onto background regions correlated with task-object motion rather than the object itself.

## Input / Output

The agent consumes 3 consecutive RGB frames of size 3×84×84 (with random shift up to ±4 pixels as weak augmentation) and outputs a continuous control action via a SAC policy. A key architectural detail is that the 3 frames are not concatenated at the pixel level before encoding; instead, each frame passes through a 3-layer shallow encoder independently, and the resulting feature maps are stacked along the channel dimension before entering an 8-layer deeper encoder. The critic loss is computed on a 50/50 mixture of clean (random-shift-only) and SRO-augmented states, while the actor always sees clean images.

## Method

SimGRL introduces two complementary regularizations on top of the SVEA data-mixing framework. First, a feature-level frame stack splits the original 11-layer CNN encoder into a 3-layer image encoder f¹ (16 channels) that processes each frame independently and an 8-layer feature encoder f² (32 channels) that encodes the channel-concatenated feature maps, forcing the critic to learn spatially salient features per frame and eliminating the imbalanced saliency caused by joint pixel-level encoding. Second, a shifted random overlay (SRO) augmentation samples a single enlarged natural image from the Places dataset, crops n patches with regular spatial offsets (dh, dw ~ unif{−l, l}, l=6) to simulate moving backgrounds across consecutive frames, and interpolates them at α=0.5 with the original frames; this injects task-irrelevant dynamics that train the agent to ignore background motion and focus on the task object, directly countering observational overfitting. No auxiliary losses, additional networks, or extra training stages are required, and the method achieves 1.55× higher throughput than the prior SOTA (SGQN) while improving average Video Hard performance by 15%.

## Results

On the DMControl-GB Video Hard benchmark (the most challenging setting with fully dynamic distracting backgrounds), SimGRL achieves state-of-the-art performance on all 6 tasks, with an average 15% improvement over the previous SOTA (SGQN). The largest gains are on 'Cartpole, Swingup' (+34% over SGQN, 727 vs. 544) and 'Cheetah, Run' (+79% over SGQN, 301 vs. 157). On Video Easy, SimGRL is SOTA on 4/6 tasks and competitive on the remaining two. SimGRL also runs 1.55× faster than SGQN (9.54 vs. 6.16 FPS), requiring 44% less wall-clock time to reach the same training budget.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SimGRL outperforms the previous SOTA SGQN (NeurIPS 2022) on all 6 Video Hard tasks, with the largest margins on 'Cartpole, Swingup' (727 vs. 544, +34%) and 'Cheetah, Run' (301 vs. 157, +79%), while also being 1.55× faster in throughput. Against CG2A (ICCV 2023) and EAR (ICCV 2023), SimGRL leads by 5–12% on Video Hard, with CG2A being the closest competitor on 'Walker, Walk' (773 vs. 739 for SGQN, 773 vs. 383 for CG2A). The SVEA baseline (NeurIPS 2021), which SimGRL builds upon, shows dramatic degradation on Video Hard (e.g., 393 on Cartpole vs. SimGRL's 727), confirming that the two identified pitfalls (imbalanced saliency and observational overfitting) are the primary bottleneck. SimGRL also surpasses model-based robust RL methods (DRIBO, RePo) in zero-shot test performance on Video Hard while using a simpler model-free architecture without auxiliary representation learning stages. On the DistractingCS benchmark, SimGRL is the only method achieving practical performance on 'Ball in Cup, Catch' and 'Finger, Spin' at low-to-moderate intensities. On robotic manipulation, SimGRL matches or exceeds CG2A (which uses both random convolution and overlay) using only the shifted random overlay augmentation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the SVEA framework (Hansen et al., NeurIPS 2021), which introduced a data-mixing strategy to stabilize Q-learning under strong augmentation, and on SODA's (Hansen & Wang, ICRA 2021) random overlay augmentation for injecting background diversity. It departs from the broader visual RL generalization literature (DrQ, RAD, SGQN, TLDA, EAR, CG2A) by diagnosing two specific failure modes—imbalanced saliency from image-level frame stacking and observational overfitting to correlated backgrounds—through gradient-based attribution mask analysis, then addressing them with a minimal architectural split of the encoder and a shifted variant of random overlay. Unlike model-based robust RL approaches (DBC, TIA, DRIBO, RePo) that learn explicit invariant representations, SimGRL operates purely within the model-free SAC setting using only implicit regularization, requiring no auxiliary losses or additional networks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of the standard image-level frame stack as a source of imbalanced saliency challenges a near-universal practice in visual RL, and the TID metrics provide a reusable quantitative tool for diagnosing saliency quality in trained agents. The plug-and-play nature of the two modifications (encoder split + shifted overlay) makes them easy to adopt across existing pipelines, and the 1.55× throughput advantage over SGQN addresses a practical efficiency concern. However, the paper does not explicitly discuss limitations; performance on 'Cheetah, Run' in Video Easy still lags behind SVEA, and generalization to extremely distorted environments (high-intensity DistractingCS) remains unresolved. Open questions include whether the feature-level frame stack generalizes to transformer-based encoders and whether the TID metrics transfer to non-DMControl domains.
