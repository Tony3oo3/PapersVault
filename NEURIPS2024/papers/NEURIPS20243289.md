## Problem

Video diffusion models like Stable Video Diffusion require 25+ iterative denoising steps, making them impractical for real-time applications. While consistency distillation can reduce this to 4–8 steps, no prior work has achieved single-step video generation with competitive quality. This paper bridges that gap by extending latent adversarial distillation—previously demonstrated only for image diffusion—to the video domain.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243289/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a single conditioning image (the first frame) at 1024×576 resolution and produces a 14-frame video clip at 7 FPS in one forward pass. Internally, the image is encoded to a 4-channel latent representation via a frame encoder, and the generator operates entirely in this latent space. Training videos are fixed at 768×448 resolution, while inference upscales to 1024×576.

## Method

SF-V fine-tunes the pre-trained SVD UNet as a single-step generator via latent adversarial training, where the discriminator reuses the frozen UNet encoder as its backbone and adds four spatial heads and four temporal heads at each downsampling block. The spatial heads treat each frame independently (reshaping temporal and batch axes together), while the temporal heads merge spatial dimensions into the batch axis to enforce motion consistency and prevent the generator from collapsing all frames to the conditioning image. Training uses a hinge adversarial loss with R1 gradient penalty plus a Pseudo-Huber reconstruction loss, with discriminator inputs noised via a discretized lognormal distribution (T_d=1000) for stability. The generator samples noise levels from a 4-step schedule (T_g=4), and at inference a single forward pass at the highest noise level directly yields the clean latent, achieving ~23× speedup over 25-step SVD with FVD comparable to 16-step sampling.

## Results

On UCF-101 (14-frame videos at 1024×576, 7 FPS), SF-V achieves an FVD of 180.9 with a single denoising step (0.30 s latency on one A100 GPU). This is comparable to SVD with 16 sampling steps (FVD 194.4, 6.89 s), yielding approximately a 23× speedup in the denoising process. Against the best existing few-step video methods, SF-V outperforms AnimateLCM at 8 steps (FVD 281.0, 1.85 s) by a wide margin, representing a >6× speedup with better quality. For single-step generation, SF-V's FVD of 180.9 dramatically surpasses UFOGen (1917.2) and LADD (1893.8), both of which were originally designed for image diffusion.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SF-V is compared against four baselines, all evaluated on the same SVD backbone and UCF-101 protocol. Against SVD (the base model), SF-V at 1 step (FVD 180.9) matches SVD at 16 steps (FVD 194.4) while being ~23× faster in the denoising phase. Against AnimateLCM (consistency distillation), SF-V at 1 step outperforms AnimateLCM at 8 steps (FVD 281.0) and AnimateLCM* at 8 steps (FVD 321.1), achieving a >6× speedup with lower FVD. Against UFOGen and LADD (adversarial distillation methods originally for image diffusion), SF-V achieves a 10× lower FVD (180.9 vs. 1917.2 and 1893.8) at the same 1-step latency of 0.30 s, demonstrating that the spatial-temporal discriminator heads and video-specific training are critical for transferring adversarial distillation to the video domain. The paper notes that simply re-using the original LADD/UFOGen discriminators causes out-of-memory issues on video models, so their re-implementations use the proposed spatial-temporal discriminator.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends LADD's latent adversarial diffusion distillation framework from the image domain to video generation, using Stable Video Diffusion (SVD) as the base model. While consistency-based approaches (AnimateLCM, VideoLCM) reduce sampling to 4 steps, they still struggle at 1 step; prior adversarial distillation methods (UFOGen, LADD) achieve 1-step image generation but have not been scaled to video. The key architectural departure is the introduction of separate spatial and temporal discriminator heads on a frozen UNet encoder backbone, which the authors show is critical to prevent the generator from collapsing to the conditioning image and to enforce motion consistency.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Achieving 1-step video generation with FVD comparable to 16-step SVD (180.9 vs. 194.4) represents a ~23× speedup that makes real-time video synthesis and editing practically feasible, a significant step toward democratizing video generation. The spatial-temporal discriminator design is a clean, reusable architectural contribution that could transfer to other video diffusion models. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to longer sequences, higher resolutions, or text-to-video settings, and whether the remaining bottleneck (VAE encode/decode) can be similarly accelerated. The authors note that complex-motion conditioning images still produce artifacts inherited from the base SVD model.
