## Problem

Spatial reasoning—a fundamental component of human cognition involving navigation, relationship understanding, and object counting—remains largely under-explored in evaluations of LLMs and VLMs. While these models achieve strong performance on many reasoning tasks, no prior benchmark systematically isolates how input modality (text-only, vision-only, or both) affects spatial reasoning, leaving it unclear whether visual inputs genuinely help or hinder model performance on spatial tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242399/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each problem in SpatialEval is presented in three interchangeable input formats: TQA (a self-contained textual description, e.g., pairwise spatial relations or ASCII maze grids), VQA (an image alone, e.g., a rendered map or colored-block maze), and VTQA (both the image and its full textual description, making the information redundant across modalities). All questions are four-option multiple-choice, and the same question set is used across all three formats to enable controlled modality comparisons. For Spatial-Real, the textual descriptions are dense captions averaging over 1,000 words per image from the DCI dataset.

## Method

SpatialEval introduces four configurable synthetic tasks (Spatial-Map, Maze-Nav, Spatial-Grid) plus one real-image task (Spatial-Real) designed to avoid data leakage, support controlled experiments, and scale in sample size. The core experimental design is a systematic modality ablation: the same VLM is evaluated under VQA, VTQA, and TQA conditions, and the image is further replaced with Gaussian noise or an unrelated random image to test whether models actually attend to visual content. By pairing each VLM with its LLM backbone under identical text-only inputs, the paper isolates the contribution of the visual encoder from the language model. The key finding is that VLMs frequently underperform their LLM counterparts on spatial tasks, that removing or corrupting the image can improve accuracy, and that textual descriptions consistently outperform visual inputs—revealing that current VLM architectures treat vision as a lossy translation into language space rather than a first-class reasoning modality.

## Results

On the SpatialEval benchmark (4-choice multiple choice, random guessing = 25%), spatial reasoning proves surprisingly difficult: most open-source VLMs score at or below random on Vision-only (VQA) inputs across Spatial-Map, Maze-Nav, and Spatial-Grid. Among open-source LLMs, LLaMA-3-8B achieves the best text-only accuracy at 71.9% on Spatial-Grid, followed by Mistral-7B at 62.1%. Among proprietary models, GPT-4o reaches 98.9% accuracy on Spatial-Grid with Vision-text (VTQA) input and 98.6% with Text-only, while GPT-4V improves by 25.6% on Spatial-Grid when switching from Vision-only (66.8%) to Vision-text (92.4%). A striking finding is that removing the image entirely (TQA vs. VTQA) often *improves* VLM performance, e.g., LLaVA-1.6-34B gains 20.1% on Spatial-Grid when the image is absent.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper evaluates 11+ model families across three input modalities. GPT-4o dominates proprietary models, achieving 98.9% on Spatial-Grid (VTQA) and 98.6% (TQA), far exceeding GPT-4V (92.4%/92.3%) and Claude 3 Opus (88.8%/95.7%). Among open-source LLMs, LLaMA-3-8B (71.9%) and Mistral-7B (62.1%) lead on Spatial-Grid text-only, while Vicuna-13B-1.5 (39%) and Nous-Hermes-2-Yi-34B (32%) trail on Spatial-Map. A critical finding is that VLMs with the same language backbone consistently underperform their LLM counterparts on Vision-only inputs: LLaVA-1.6-Mistral-7B scores 47.1% vs. Mistral-7B's 62.1% on Spatial-Grid (15% drop), and InstructBLIP-Vicuna-13B scores 17% vs. Vicuna-13B-1.5's 39% on Spatial-Map. Conversely, VLMs often *outperform* their LLM backbones on text-only inputs (e.g., LLaVA-1.6-Vicuna-13B vs. Vicuna-13B-1.5), suggesting multimodal pre-training strengthens the language backbone. On the real-image Spatial-Real task, the modality gap widens dramatically (30% between VTQA and VQA vs. 7% on synthetic tasks), with LLaVA-1.6-Vicuna-13B reaching 83.2% (VTQA) versus 44.5% (VQA).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by introducing a controlled multi-modal evaluation framework (TQA, VQA, VTQA) for spatial reasoning, where the same question is answerable from text alone, image alone, or both. It departs from prior VQA benchmarks (Visual Genome, GQA, BLINK) that treat images as mandatory and text as optional, and from earlier spatial-reasoning evaluations (Yamada et al. on navigation, Fu et al. on science/games, Zhang et al. on math diagrams) that did not systematically isolate the contribution of each modality. The Spatial-Real task repurposes the DCI dataset's dense captions to extend evaluation to natural images, while the three synthetic tasks (Spatial-Map, Maze-Nav, Spatial-Grid) are designed from scratch with configurability and data-leakage avoidance as guiding principles.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that VLMs perform worse with visual input than without it on spatial tasks challenges a core assumption in the multimodal learning community and points to a fundamental architectural limitation: current VLMs 'translate' vision into language space rather than reasoning jointly across modalities. The paper identifies a clear path forward—architectures that treat vision as a first-class information source—but does not propose new training strategies or adaptation methods, leaving the 'how' as an open question. Limitations include the reliance on synthetic tasks for most experiments (though Spatial-Real partially addresses this), the absence of theoretical analysis, and the fact that the benchmark evaluates only multiple-choice accuracy rather than open-ended spatial reasoning.
