## Problem

Existing AUC maximization methods for imbalanced binary classification assume identical training and test distributions and require labeled negative data. In practice, positive data (attacks, diseases, anomalies) shifts over time while negative data remains stable, and labeled negative data is often unavailable. No prior method can maximize the test-distribution AUC using only positive and unlabeled training data plus unlabeled test data under this positive distribution shift.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241137/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes positive labeled instances and unlabeled instances from the training distribution, unlabeled instances from the test distribution, and the training positive class-prior π_tr (which can be estimated from the PU data if unavailable). It produces a differentiable score function s: R^D → R for ranking instances, applicable to any architecture (linear, kernel, feed-forward, or CNN). Notably, the test class-prior π_te is not required, and no labeled negative data is needed at any stage.

## Method

The core contribution is a theoretical derivation showing that, under the positive distribution shift assumption (p_tr^n = p_te^n), the smoothed AUC on the test distribution can be rewritten using only the positive and marginal training densities and the marginal test density. By expressing p_te^p via p_te and p_te^n, applying the symmetric-function identity σ(z)+σ(−z)=1 to eliminate a constant term, and substituting p_te^n = p_tr^n expressed through p_tr and p_tr^p, the authors obtain a simple pairwise loss: −E_{x~p_te, x̄~p_tr}[f(x,x̄)] + π_tr·E_{x~p_te, x^p~p_tr^p}[f(x,x^p)], where f is any symmetric function (e.g., sigmoid). This loss is minimized via stochastic gradient descent with mini-batches sampled from the positive training set and the combined unlabeled training/test sets, requiring no knowledge of the test class-prior and no labeled negative examples.

## Results

The proposed method achieves the best or statistically comparable test AUC in 11 out of 12 dataset–class-prior combinations across MNIST, Fashion-MNIST, SVHN, and CIFAR-10. The most dramatic gains appear on SVHN (AUC 0.554–0.736 vs. ~0.50–0.52 for all baselines) and CIFAR-10 at higher test class-priors (0.825 at π_te=0.2, 0.874 at π_te=0.3 vs. 0.74–0.75 for puAUC/PULA). On the simpler MNIST and Fashion-MNIST datasets, the method is competitive with PURR (which uses the true test class-prior) and PULA, with the gap narrowing as π_te increases. The method also outperforms all baselines on two tabular datasets (HReadmission: 0.747 vs. best baseline 0.695; Hypertension: 0.630 vs. 0.643 for PURR).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against nnPU [25], the proposed method achieves substantially higher AUC on SVHN (0.554–0.736 vs. 0.501) and CIFAR-10 (0.727–0.874 vs. 0.451–0.467), while on MNIST and Fashion-MNIST nnPU remains competitive at low π_te but degrades as the shift intensifies. Against puAUC [58], Ours wins on SVHN by a wide margin (up to +0.22 AUC) and on CIFAR-10 at π_te ≥ 0.2 (0.825 vs. 0.741), though puAUC edges ahead on CIFAR-10 at π_te=0.1 (0.749 vs. 0.727) and on Fashion-MNIST at low π_te. Against PULA [21], the pattern is similar: Ours dominates on SVHN and CIFAR-10 at higher π_te, while PULA retains an advantage on Fashion-MNIST at π_te=0.1–0.2 (0.954–0.955 vs. 0.787–0.891). Against PURR [16], the closest competitor designed for the same positive distribution shift, Ours wins on SVHN (0.554–0.736 vs. 0.511–0.522) and CIFAR-10 at π_te=0.2–0.3 (0.825–0.874 vs. 0.536–0.710), but PURR leads on MNIST across all π_te values (0.815–0.914 vs. 0.723–0.902) and is comparable on Fashion-MNIST at π_te=0.3 (0.961 vs. 0.960); notably, PURR requires the true test class-prior π_te, which the proposed method does not.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three established lines of work: AUC maximization for imbalanced classification, positive-unlabeled (PU) learning, and domain adaptation under positive distribution shift (Hammoudeh & Lowd, NeurIPS 2020). It departs from prior AUC maximization methods that assume identical train/test distributions and require labeled negative data, and from PURR which handles positive shift but minimizes classification risk (inappropriate for imbalanced data) and requires the test class-prior. The key theoretical contribution leverages the symmetric-loss property (σ(z)+σ(−z)=K, Charoenphakdee et al., ICML 2019) and the PU-based AUC rewriting technique (Sakai et al., 2018) to derive a loss that maximizes test-distribution AUC using only positive and unlabeled training data plus unlabeled test data, without needing the test class-prior.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method addresses a highly practical scenario—imbalanced classification where the positive class evolves over time (intrusion detection, medical diagnosis, visual inspection) while the negative class remains stable—and does so with minimal labeling requirements (only positive labels in training). The clean theoretical derivation and simple loss form make it easy to adopt with any differentiable classifier. A key limitation is that the method is guaranteed only under positive distribution shift; if the negative-conditional density also changes, performance is not preserved, and the authors note that detecting the shift type from data remains an open problem. The finding that the non-negative loss correction (standard in PU learning) is ineffective here, while a tighter bound using the test class-prior helps, suggests the AUC's pairwise structure creates different optimization dynamics than instance-wise losses.
