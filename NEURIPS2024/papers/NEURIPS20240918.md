## Problem

Existing QR code customization methods—whether based on image transfer, style transfer, or generative models—cannot simultaneously preserve face identity, maintain aesthetic quality, and ensure scannability. Style-transfer approaches produce unnatural facial textures, image-transfer methods introduce visible artifacts, and generative-model-based techniques struggle to exclude QR patterns from the facial region without making the code unscannable. The core difficulty is that face identity, aesthetic background, and QR pattern impose three inherently conflicting control signals on the same image.

## Input / Output

The pipeline takes a reference face image f (real, StyleGAN2-generated, or cartoon), a version-5 QR code m (37×37 modules), and free-form text prompts c describing the desired background style, and produces a 1024×1024 RGB QR code image that is scannable by standard decoders while preserving the input face identity. The entire process is training-free, requiring only a pre-trained SDXL model, an InstantID network, and a QR Controller, with the final latent optimization running for 300 iterations (~120 s on an NVIDIA 4090).

## Method

Face2QR is a three-stage training-free pipeline built on SDXL. Stage 1 (IDQR) generates an initial image using two parallel control networks—InstantID for face identity and a QR Controller for luminance pattern—but this yields >43% module error due to conflicts between face and QR signals in the center region. Stage 2 (IDRS) resolves this by binarizing the initial image, fixing the face and marker modules (located via InsightFace), and reshuffling the remaining QR modules to preserve decodability, then regenerating the image guided by the resulting fine-grained halftone blueprint, halving the error rate. Stage 3 (IDSE) strengthens finder/alignment patterns and refines the image in VQ-VAE latent space using a spatially dynamic code loss that applies a smaller Gaussian kernel variance (σ_f=1.5) and lower weight (w_f=1.0) in the face region versus a larger variance (σ_b=3.0) and higher weight (w_b=15.0) in the background, combined with an L2-Wasserstein aesthetic content loss on VGG-19 features, achieving scannability without distorting facial features.

## Results

On the quantitative comparison (Table 6), Face2QR achieves a face feature distance d of 0.51 and an AesBench aesthetic score of 90.1, outperforming both ArtCoder (d=0.50, B_a=62.0) and Text2QR (d=0.43, B_a=87.5). In practical scanning tests across three decoders (Scanner, TikTok, WeChat) at three physical sizes (3cm, 5cm, 7cm) and two angles (45°, 90°), Face2QR achieves an average success rate exceeding 94%, with most configurations reaching 100%. The user study with 30 participants evaluating 150 QR images confirms that Face2QR is preferred over competing methods in both face ID preservation and aesthetic quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Face2QR outperforms ArtCoder [36] on both metrics: face feature distance d (0.51 vs. 0.50) and AesBench aesthetic score (90.1 vs. 62.0), with the aesthetic gap being particularly large since ArtCoder's neural style transfer tends to distort facial textures. Against Text2QR [43], the closest competitor, Face2QR achieves a notably higher face feature distance (0.51 vs. 0.43), reflecting its dedicated InstantID-based face control, while also edging out on aesthetics (90.1 vs. 87.5). In scannability tests (Table 5), Face2QR and Text2QR perform comparably, both achieving near-100% success rates across most decoder/size/angle combinations, with Face2QR showing a slight edge at 3cm size with the Scanner app (100% vs. 96% at 45°). QArt [6] and Halftone QR [5] are compared only qualitatively in Table 1, where Face2QR produces more harmonious face-background integration without the artifacts visible in those methods. For identity preservation specifically, Face2QR matches the baseline InstantID [40] pipeline (Table 3), confirming that the QR code generation process does not degrade face fidelity beyond what the underlying identity model achieves.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper composes three existing building blocks—Stable Diffusion XL as the generative backbone, InstantID as a face-identity control network, and the QR Controller from Text2QR—into a three-stage pipeline that addresses a gap none of them fill individually: simultaneous face-identity preservation, aesthetic customization, and QR scannability. The key architectural departure is the IDRS module, which exploits the redundancy in QR error-correction encoding to reshuffle modules away from the facial region, thereby resolving the inherent spatial conflict between face features and QR patterns that prior generative QR methods (notably Text2QR) could not handle. The IDSE stage further departs from Text2QR's uniform latent optimization by introducing a spatially dynamic code loss with region-specific Gaussian kernel variances and loss weights, a design motivated by the need to protect facial texture while still enforcing decodability in the background.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that the three-way trade-off between face identity, aesthetics, and scannability can be managed in a training-free pipeline, which is practically important for social-media and marketing applications where users expect personalized, scannable QR codes. However, the approach remains constrained by the underlying generative model's failure modes (e.g., inconsistent outputs, prompt–image conflicts) and requires ~120 s of GPU optimization per image, limiting real-time deployment. Open questions include whether the QR-reshuffling strategy generalizes to higher QR versions with denser module grids, whether the spatially adaptive loss transfers to other region-sensitive generation tasks beyond QR codes, and how the pipeline behaves under adversarial or low-quality input faces.
