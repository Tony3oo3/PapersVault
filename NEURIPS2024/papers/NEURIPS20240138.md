## Problem

Identifying which training images most influence a specific generated image from a text-to-image model is critical for understanding model behavior, yet the gold-standard counterfactual test (retrain from scratch without candidate images and verify the output changes) is computationally infeasible at scale. Existing approximations—closed-form influence functions and off-the-shelf feature matching—either underperform or fail to capture fine-grained visual attributes like pose and object count, leaving a gap between tractable methods and accurate counterfactual prediction.

## Input / Output

Given a synthesized image query (with its conditioning text prompt), a pretrained latent diffusion model, and the full training dataset, the method produces a scalar influence score for every training image, computed as the increase in that image's DDPM loss after unlearning the query. Losses are evaluated with strided timesteps (stride 50–100) to keep computation tractable, and because models are trained with horizontal flips, each training image is scored on both its original and flipped version (taking the max). The ranked scores yield a top-K set of influential images for counterfactual leave-K-out evaluation.

## Method

The key insight is to reverse the unlearning direction: rather than unlearning each of N training images separately, the method unlearns the single synthesized image once and then measures which training images are most 'forgotten' (i.e., show the largest loss increase). Unlearning is performed via a Newton update θ ← θ + (α/N) F⁻¹ ∇L(ẑ, θ), where F is a diagonal Fisher information matrix estimated over the training set, which regularizes the update to prevent catastrophic forgetting of unrelated concepts. Only the key and value projection matrices (W^k, W^v) in cross-attention layers are updated, a small subset that the ablations show outperforms full-weight or broader attention updates. The attribution score τ(ẑ, z) = L(z, θ_{-ẑ}) − L(z, θ₀) is then computed for every training image z, requiring a single unlearning optimization per query rather than N separate retraining or unlearning steps. This formulation connects to influence functions in the limit of a single infinitesimal step but uses larger, explicit loss evaluations for greater accuracy.

## Results

On the MSCOCO leave-K-out counterfactual evaluation (110 synthesized images), the proposed unlearning method achieves the highest loss change ΔL(ẑ,θ) across all K values: 5.6×10⁻³ (K=500), 6.7×10⁻³ (K=1000), and 9.8×10⁻³ (K=4000), outperforming the best influence-function baseline D-TRAK (5.4/6.6/9.6×10⁻³). On generation deviation measured by CLIP similarity (lower is better), the method achieves 7.3/7.0/6.4×10⁻¹ at K=500/1000/4000, matching or beating all baselines. On MSE deviation (higher is better), D-TRAK leads (5.9/6.4/7.8×10⁻²) while the proposed method scores 5.1/5.7/6.1×10⁻². On the Customized Model Benchmark (400 queries, 100K LAION retrieval pool), the method significantly outperforms feature-matching baselines on artist-style models while matching them on object-centric models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed unlearning method achieves the best loss change (ΔL) across all K values, beating the strongest influence-function baseline D-TRAK by 0.2–0.2×10⁻³ at K=500–1000 and matching it at K=4000 (9.8 vs 9.6). On CLIP-based generation deviation, the method ties D-TRAK at K=500 (7.3) and K=4000 (6.4) and wins at K=1000 (7.0 vs 7.1), while DINO achieves the best CLIP score at K=4000 (6.3). However, D-TRAK clearly outperforms the proposed method on MSE deviation (5.9/6.4/7.8 vs 5.1/5.7/6.1 at K=500/1000/4000), which the authors acknowledge. Feature-matching baselines (DINO, DINOv2, CLIP) and text matching perform substantially worse on ΔL, with DINO being the strongest feature-based method (8.1 at K=4000 vs 9.8 for Ours). DataInf performs near-random, attributed to its matrix-inverse approximation being unsuitable for from-scratch trained models. On the Customized Model Benchmark, the method significantly outperforms CLIP(AbC) and DINO(AbC) on artist-style models in both Recall@10 and mAP, while performing on par on object-centric models; D-TRAK is second-best on artist-style but worse than the proposed method on object-centric models.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes machine unlearning (EWC/Fisher-regularized weight updates from Guo et al. and Tanno et al.) for data attribution, reversing the direction: instead of unlearning individual training images, it unlearns the synthesized output and measures which training images are forgotten. It positions influence functions (TRAK, D-TRAK, JourneyTRAK) as a special case of their unlearning formulation (single infinitesimal Newton step with linearized loss), and departs from prior attribution-by-customization work (Wang et al. 2023) by operating on general large-scale models rather than few-shot fine-tuned ones. The counterfactual evaluation protocol (leave-K-out retraining from scratch) extends the seed-consistency evaluation introduced by Georgiev et al. into a more rigorous gold-standard setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first rigorous counterfactual validation of data attribution for text-to-image models at scale (retraining from scratch on MSCOCO), establishing a benchmark that prior influence-function and feature-matching methods could not match. Key limitations include the computational bottleneck of evaluating training-set loss after unlearning, the inability to capture group interactions among influential images, and the restriction to whole-image attribution rather than per-aspect (style, structure, segment). Open questions include whether the unlearning-based approach scales to billion-parameter models like Stable Diffusion XL, and whether the Fisher-regularized Newton update can be made more efficient for large training sets.
