## Problem

Bayesian Optimization requires specifying a GP kernel length scale that defines the smoothness of the function class, but maximum likelihood estimation on limited observed data can misspecify this in unexplored regions. The only prior algorithm with theoretical guarantees, A-GP-UCB, progressively decreases the length scale without a stopping mechanism, causing over-exploration and a regret bound polynomially (g(T)^d) worse than an oracle that knows the optimal length scale.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242740/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The algorithm takes a black-box function on a compact domain X ⊂ R^d, an initial length scale upper bound θ₀ (typically from MLE on a few initial points), and a user-specified growth function g(t) controlling the pace of introducing shorter length scales. At each iteration it outputs a query point x_t selected via GP-UCB under the currently chosen length scale, while internally maintaining a growing set of candidate length scales and per-candidate query histories. The theoretical guarantee is a cumulative regret bound O(d ln g(T)) times the oracle bound, and the extension LNB-GP-UCB additionally takes an initial RKHS norm B₀ and a second growth function b(t) for the norm dimension.

## Method

LB-GP-UCB extends regret-balancing with elimination from multi-armed bandit model selection to handle infinitely many base learners by generating candidate length scales geometrically via q(i) = θ₀ e^{-i/d} and introducing new shorter candidates at a pace dictated by g(t). At each step it selects the length scale whose suspected GP-UCB regret bound is smallest given its own query count, then queries the point maximizing the corresponding UCB. Crucially, unlike A-GP-UCB which permanently commits to a single decreasing length scale, LB-GP-UCB retains all previously introduced longer scales while adding shorter ones, and eliminates underperforming candidates via a rejection rule comparing observed function values against confidence-adjusted bounds. This yields a cumulative regret bound only O(d ln g(T)) away from the oracle, compared to O(g(T)^d) for A-GP-UCB, and the extension LNB-GP-UCB handles an unknown RKHS norm simultaneously with an additional O(ln b(T)) factor.

## Results

The headline theoretical result is that LB-GP-UCB achieves a suboptimality gap of O(d ln g(T)) relative to an oracle optimiser, compared to O(g(T)^d) for A-GP-UCB (Table 1). Empirically, LB-GP-UCB outperforms all baselines on cumulative and best regret across all four benchmarks (Berkenkamp, Michalewicz, CrossedBarrel, AGNP), with the gap over A-GP-UCB most pronounced on CrossedBarrel where A-GP-UCB underperforms significantly. Running times are comparable to A-GP-UCB and MLE (e.g., ~48–49 s on material design tasks), while MCMC is 5–20× slower (e.g., 471 s vs. 48 s on CrossedBarrel).

**Table 1: Comparison of optimality (suboptimality gap R_T / R*(T)) for A-GP-UCB and LB-GP-UCB under fixed growth functions.**

| Algorithm | Unknown θ | Unknown θ and B |
|---|---|---|
| A-GP-UCB [6] | O(g(T)^d) | O(b(T) g(T)^d) |
| LB-GP-UCB / LNB-GP-UCB (ours) | **O(d ln g(T))** | **O(d ln g(T) ln b(T))** |

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against A-GP-UCB [6], LB-GP-UCB wins on cumulative regret across all four benchmarks: on the Berkenkamp toy problem both find the global optimum but A-GP-UCB's regret grows faster due to over-exploration; on Michalewicz, LB-GP-UCB selects length scales closer to the estimated optimal θ̂*, yielding lower cumulative and best regret; on CrossedBarrel, A-GP-UCB struggles and underperforms substantially in cumulative regret while LB-GP-UCB performs well on both metrics; on AGNP, A-GP-UCB finds low-regret solutions quickly but LB-GP-UCB matches or exceeds it on cumulative regret. Against the MCMC baseline [13], LB-GP-UCB dominates on both regret and runtime: MCMC gets stuck at suboptimal solutions on the needle-in-a-haystack material design problems (CrossedBarrel, AGNP) and is 5–20× slower per run. Against MLE, LB-GP-UCB avoids the local-optimum trap visible in Figure 1 (where MLE over-estimates the length scale and persistently selects a suboptimal x=1), and the ablation study (Figure 4) shows LB-GP-UCB is far more robust to the choice of growth function g(t) than A-GP-UCB, which is highly sensitive to g(t) and decreases its length scale beyond θ̂*.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the regret-balancing aggregation framework of Pacchiano et al. (2020) from a finite set of base algorithms to an infinite, dynamically expanding set, enabling it to handle the continuous length-scale hyperparameter in GP-UCB. It directly addresses the limitations of A-GP-UCB (Berkenkamp et al., 2019), the only prior algorithm with theoretical guarantees for BO with unknown hyperparameters, by replacing its irreversible length-scale decrease with a mechanism that retains longer scales while introducing shorter ones. The base GP-UCB algorithm and its regret bounds (Chowdhury & Gopalan, 2017) remain the building block, with the key conceptual shift being from sequential search over hyperparameters to parallel aggregation with elimination.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The logarithmic improvement in the suboptimality gap (from O(g(T)^d) to O(d ln g(T))) is practically significant because it allows much more aggressive growth functions without incurring the polynomial penalty that A-GP-UCB suffers, making the algorithm far more robust to the user-specified growth function. However, the work is limited to isotropic kernels, assumes known noise magnitude, and relies on standard GPs that do not scale to high dimensions or large datasets. Open questions include extending to non-isotropic length scales (which requires new MIG bounds), handling unknown noise, and adapting to sparse or high-dimensional GP formulations.
