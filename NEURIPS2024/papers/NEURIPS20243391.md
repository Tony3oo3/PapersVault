## Problem

Existing generative methods for biological sequences predominantly generate entirely new sequences from scratch, risking significant deviation from naturally occurring sequences and compromising safety and predictability. Despite the critical importance of editing existing sequences to enhance specific biological properties while minimizing alterations, there has been a notable scarcity of generative-modeling approaches specifically designed for this editing task. This paper addresses that gap by proposing a GFlowNet-based editing algorithm that modifies only identified sub-optimal positions in a seed sequence.

## Input / Output

The method takes a biological seed sequence (DNA of length 8 or 200, or protein of length 12–60), a pre-trained GFlowNet flow function F_θ, and three hyperparameters: δ (sub-optimality threshold), λ (regularization toward the original token), and σ (Gaussian noise variance controlling exploration). It produces one or more edited sequences of the same length as the input, with the number of edits controllable via δ and λ. The stochastic policy enables generating a diverse set of edited sequences per input, and the method can also be applied to combine a long and short sequence to produce a shorter output.

## Method

GFNSeqEditor operates autoregressively, processing the seed sequence token by token. At each position t, a sub-optimal-position identifier D(·) compares the normalized flow F_θ(x̂_{:t-1}+x_t) against the maximum over all available actions, flagging the position for editing if the ratio falls below δ times the max ratio plus Gaussian noise ν~N(0,σ²). When a position is flagged, a new token is sampled from a mixture policy π(a|x̂_{:t-1}) = (1−λ)·F_θ(x̂_{:t-1}+a)/ΣF_θ(x̂_{:t-1}+a′) + λ·1_{a=x_t}, which trades off property improvement against similarity to the original sequence. The GFlowNet flow function is trained offline via the trajectory-balance objective with one round of active learning, amortizing the search cost over the learning process so that editing at inference time requires no evaluation of unseen sequences. The paper further derives theoretical lower and upper bounds on the expected reward, property improvement, and Levenshtein distance of edited sequences as functions of δ, λ, and σ, establishing a principled trade-off between edit count and property gain.

## Results

On the TFbinding dataset (DNA, length 8), GFNSeqEditor achieves a property improvement (PI) of 0.14 with 24.27% edit percentage and diversity of 3.84, yielding the best GMDPI of 0.73. On AMP (protein, length 12–60), it reaches PI=0.33, diversity=14.34, and GMDPI=2.17 at ~34.5% edits. On CRE (DNA, length 200), GFNSeqEditor achieves PI=9.90, diversity=40.41, and GMDPI=20.00 at ~21.9% edits, substantially outperforming all baselines on the combined GMDPI metric across all three datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GFNSeqEditor outperforms all six baselines on the combined GMDPI metric (geometric mean of PI and diversity) across all three datasets, with the largest margin on CRE where it achieves GMDPI=20.00 versus the next-best GFlowNet-E at 12.53. On property improvement, GFNSeqEditor leads on every dataset: 0.14 vs. 0.12 (DE) on TFbinding, 0.33 vs. 0.28 (GFlowNet-E) on AMP, and 9.90 vs. 4.24 (GFlowNet-E) on CRE. The closest competitor is GFlowNet-E (adapted GFlowNet-AL that edits only the tail of the sequence), which is consistently second on PI but trails GFNSeqEditor by 27% on TFbinding, 15% on AMP, and 57% on CRE. LaMBO achieves the highest raw diversity on AMP (15.61) and CRE (62.95), but its PI is far lower, resulting in a much worse GMDPI. Directed Evolution and Ledidi, which rely on local search with proxy models, are outperformed by a wide margin on both PI and GMDPI. Seq2Seq, a deterministic transformer-based editor, produces only one edit per input (zero diversity) and requires a much higher edit percentage (42–78%) to achieve modest PI gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the GFlowNet framework (Bengio et al. 2021, 2023) from its established role in de novo sequence generation (Jain et al. 2022, GFlowNet-AL) toward a fundamentally different use: leveraging a pre-trained flow function to identify sub-optimal positions within a seed sequence and guide stochastic edits at those positions. It departs from prior sequence-editing methods—directed evolution (Sinai et al. 2020), Ledidi (Schreiber et al. 2020), LaMBO (Stanton et al. 2022), and MOGFN-AL (Jain et al. 2023)—which rely on local search or iterative proxy-model evaluation, by amortizing the search cost into the flow function's training and using global flow information to make position-level editing decisions. The theoretical analysis providing hyperparameter-controllable bounds on reward, property improvement, and edit count is a novel contribution not present in prior editing work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GFNSeqEditor addresses a practical gap in synthetic biology: the need to modify existing sequences (e.g., regulatory elements, therapeutic peptides) while preserving similarity to known-safe sequences, rather than generating entirely novel sequences that may trigger immune responses or fall outside model training distributions. The hyperparameter-controllable edit budget and theoretical guarantees make the method more operationally reliable than proxy-dependent iterative methods. Key limitations include dependence on a well-trained GFlowNet (requiring quality offline data and active-learning rounds), evaluation on relatively short sequences (length 8–200), and the fact that the flow function's quality is the primary bottleneck. Open questions include scalability to longer genomic sequences, applicability to multi-objective editing, and whether the sub-optimal-position identification criterion generalizes beyond the autoregressive GFlowNet construction.
