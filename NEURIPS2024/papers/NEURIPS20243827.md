## Problem

Sparse Gaussian process surrogates in Bayesian optimization are designed for global regression, producing overly smooth posteriors that degrade acquisition function optimization, especially in high-dimensional or heterogeneous landscapes. Existing strategies to focus sparse GPs on promising regions have only been validated on low-dimensional problems with large online budgets, leaving open whether they can handle large offline datasets or problems with hundreds of variables.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243827/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a black-box objective function, an initial offline dataset (ranging from 2,000 to 10,000 points in experiments), axis-aligned search space bounds (unit hypercube [0,1]^d), and a fixed number of inducing variables (50–200). At each BO iteration it outputs a batch of B candidate points (B=4–100 depending on the task) along with the focalized GP posterior over the current search region. The search region is an axis-aligned hypercube whose side lengths are halved at each hierarchical depth, and the final batch is sampled via a Softmax over acquisition values from all depths, blending global and local candidates.

## Method

The core contribution is a focalized ELBO (L2) that replaces the standard SVGP data-likelihood term with a weighted version where each training point is weighted by its maximum kernel value to the current search region, plus a regularization term that penalizes the ratio of total weight to in-region point count, preventing the model from collapsing to a large-lengthscale global fit. This maintains O(m³) complexity and introduces no new hyperparameters. On top of this, FOCALBO performs hierarchical acquisition optimization: starting from the full input space, it trains a fresh focalized GP for each progressively smaller search region (halving side lengths at each depth), optimizes the acquisition function within each region, and samples the final batch via Softmax over all proposed candidates. The optimization depth H is automatically adjusted—decreased when the best point comes from a shallower (more global) level, increased otherwise—balancing exploration and exploitation without manual tuning.

## Results

On synthetic functions (Shekel, Michalewicz, GP-sampled) with 2000 offline points and 500 online budget (batch size 10), FOCALBO significantly improves all acquisition functions (TS, EI, PI) over SVGP and consistently achieves top-tier performance. On robot morphology design (10,000 offline points, 128 online evaluations, batch size 4), FOCALBO with TuRBO is the first GP-based method to reach top-tier performance reported by prior offline MBO works, while all sparse GP baselines struggle to gain from the initial data. On the 585-dimensional musculoskeletal system control task (2000 offline points, 3000 online budget, batch size 100), FOCALBO achieves higher maximum reward and faster convergence than all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FOCALBO consistently outperforms SVGP [25] across all tasks and acquisition functions; on Michalewicz (where a large fraction of the input space is flat), SVGP increases noise estimation to maintain stationary predictions while focalized GP focuses on the local search region and successfully optimizes. FOCALBO also beats WOGP [10] on synthetic functions, though WOGP was only evaluated there due to its extremely slow training on larger datasets. Against Vecchia GP [12], FOCALBO achieves superior optimization performance on both synthetic functions and the high-dimensional robot morphology and musculoskeletal control tasks. In direct comparison with TuRBO [46] (Appendix B.4), FOCALBO outperforms TuRBO on both robot morphology design and musculoskeletal system control with smaller computational cost, attributed to TuRBO's inability to quickly adapt when the online evaluation budget is small. Notably, FOCALBO and TuRBO are complementary: combining FOCALBO with TuRBO yields the best overall performance on robot morphology design, demonstrating that the hierarchical focalized strategy and trust-region local optimization address different bottlenecks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the variational sparse GP framework (Titsias 2009; Hensman et al. 2013) by introducing a focalized ELBO that reweights the data likelihood term based on kernel proximity to a search region, shifting the modeling objective from global regression fidelity to local prediction accuracy. It departs from prior sparse-GP-for-BO work (WOGP, IMP-DPP, Vecchia GP) that either selects inducing points to approximate high-performing regions or uses sequential point selection, by instead training the GP with a region-aware loss and performing hierarchical acquisition optimization over progressively shrinking sub-regions. The hierarchical search strategy is conceptually related to TuRBO's trust-region approach but differs in using a single shared sparse GP with data sharing across regions rather than independent exact GPs per trust region, and in adaptively adjusting the search depth based on optimization performance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FOCALBO is the first sparse GP-based BO method demonstrated to handle 585-dimensional optimization with large offline datasets (2000+ points) and large online budgets (3000 evaluations), a regime where prior sparse GP BO methods were untested. The focalized loss is a minimal modification (weighted likelihood + a simple regularization term) that requires no additional hyperparameters and maintains the same O(m³) complexity as standard SVGP, making it easy to adopt. Open questions include the lack of rigorous regret bounds for the focalized ELBO, the heuristic nature of the depth-adaptation mechanism, and whether the approach generalizes beyond the specific high-dimensional control tasks tested. The paper also notes that whole-body musculoskeletal control and large-scale parameter tuning remain future directions.
