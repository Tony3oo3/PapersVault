## Problem

Existing 4D Gaussian Splatting methods are validated primarily on controlled multi-view or slow-motion monocular setups, and they overfit severely when applied to casually recorded handheld monocular videos with fast-moving objects. Standard regularization techniques (diffusion priors, depth smoothness) mitigate overfitting but uniformly degrade training-image reconstruction quality, creating an undesirable trade-off. Additionally, Structure from Motion fails to place Gaussian primitives in fast-moving dynamic regions, leaving those areas uninitialized and causing excessive primitive proliferation during training.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a casually recorded monocular video (single handheld camera) along with per-frame depth maps and scene flow estimated by a monocular depth/flow model [71], and optical flow from RAFT. It produces a 4D Gaussian scene representation (deformed via a Hexplane grid at resolution 64×64×64×150) that renders photorealistic novel views at arbitrary camera poses and time steps at ~75 FPS on a single RTX A5000. A DreamBooth-fine-tuned Stable Diffusion model, conditioned on BLIP-generated text prompts from training frames, is used as an auxiliary regularizer during training but is not part of the inference pipeline.

## Method

The core contribution is an uncertainty-aware regularization framework that selectively applies priors only where the scene is under-observed. Each Gaussian primitive's uncertainty is quantified by aggregating its α-blending contribution across all training pixels (Eq. 7–8), then a 2D uncertainty map is rendered for unseen views via α-blending (Eq. 9). Two regularization losses are weighted by this map: a diffusion-based loss (L_UA-diff) that compares rendered novel views against DDIM-refined versions from a DreamBooth-fine-tuned Stable Diffusion model, and a depth-smoothing loss (L_UA-TV) that applies total variation to the rendered depth map only in high-uncertainty regions. A dynamic region densification module identifies fast-moving pixels via scene flow and initializes additional Gaussian primitives at those locations using inverse depth projection, addressing the SfM initialization gap. The full training objective combines reconstruction, Hexplane grid smoothness, depth/flow data-driven losses, and the two uncertainty-aware regularization terms, with regularization activated from iteration 20,000 of 40,000 total.

## Results

On the DyCheck dataset (casually recorded monocular videos), UA-4DGS achieves the best performance among all 4D Gaussian Splatting methods, reaching mPSNR 15.25, mSSIM 0.488, and mLPIPS 0.390, outperforming 4DGS [61] (mPSNR 14.14) by 1.11 dB in mPSNR. However, MLP-based methods (T-NeRF, NSFF, Nerfies) still lead with mPSNR 16.45–16.96, though at <1 FPS versus 75 FPS for UA-4DGS. On the LLFF few-shot static scene benchmark (3 views), UA-3DGS achieves PSNR 20.89, SSIM 0.716, and LPIPS 0.217, surpassing FSGS [72] (PSNR 20.43) and all MLP-based baselines (best: SparseNeRF at PSNR 19.86).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among the three 4D Gaussian Splatting baselines, UA-4DGS consistently wins on all metrics: it improves mPSNR by 1.11 dB over 4DGS [61] (15.25 vs. 14.14), by 1.78 dB over Zhan et al. [31] (15.25 vs. 13.47), and by 2.27 dB over D-3DGS [68] (15.25 vs. 12.98), while also achieving the best mLPIPS (0.390 vs. 0.430, 0.448, 0.470). Against MLP-based methods (T-NeRF, NSFF, Nerfies, HyperNeRF), UA-4DGS still lags by 1.2–1.7 dB in mPSNR, confirming that Gaussian Splatting remains more prone to overfitting in the challenging in-the-wild monocular setting. On the LLFF few-shot static benchmark, UA-3DGS outperforms FSGS [72] by 0.46 dB in PSNR (20.89 vs. 20.43) and 0.034 in SSIM, and beats all MLP-based few-shot methods (SparseNeRF 19.86, FreeNeRF 19.63, RegNeRF 19.08) by at least 1 dB in PSNR. The paper notes that the gap to MLP methods on DyCheck is likely due to Gaussian Splatting's local optimization being more susceptible to overfitting with limited multi-view information, a gap that the uncertainty-aware regularization narrows but does not fully close.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends 4D Gaussian Splatting (4DGS [61], built on 3DGS [23]) to the challenging setting of casually recorded monocular videos, where prior 4DGS methods have been limited to controlled multi-view or slow-motion monocular setups. The key conceptual departure is an uncertainty-aware regularization that selectively applies diffusion (Stable Diffusion + DreamBooth) and depth-smoothness priors only to regions with low observation support, rather than uniformly regularizing all pixels as in prior sparse-reconstruction work (RegNeRF, FreeNeRF, DiffusionNeRF). It also introduces dynamic region densification to address the initialization failure of SfM in fast-moving areas, complementing the Hexplane-based deformation inherited from 4DGS.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first systematic treatment of 4DGS on in-the-wild handheld monocular video, a setting critical for practical AR/VR applications but previously unaddressed by Gaussian Splatting methods. The uncertainty-aware regularization principle—applying priors only where data is insufficient—is generalizable beyond 4DGS (demonstrated on static few-shot scenes via FSGS) and could inform broader sparse-reconstruction pipelines. However, performance still lags behind MLP-based methods (e.g., T-NeRF) on the same dataset, and the regularization operates at the 2D image level rather than the Gaussian primitive level, leaving open questions about whether primitive-level uncertainty and temporal consistency in densification could close the remaining gap.
