## Problem

Existing driving world models suffer from three coupled limitations: they are trained on narrow, geographically limited datasets and thus fail to generalize to unseen environments; they operate at low frame rates and resolutions, losing critical structural and dynamic details; and they support only a single action modality (e.g., steering angle), making them incompatible with the diverse action formats produced by prevalent planning algorithms. No prior work simultaneously addresses generalization, high-fidelity prediction, and versatile multi-modal action controllability in a single driving world model.

## Input / Output

Vista takes up to three consecutive condition frames (injected via latent replacement to encode position, velocity, and acceleration priors) plus an optional action condition—command (4-class categorical), goal point (2D normalized coordinate), trajectory (flattened 2D displacement sequence in meters), or angle & speed (normalized to [-1,1] and km/h respectively)—all encoded as 128-channel Fourier embeddings fed through cross-attention. It outputs a 25-frame driving video at 10 Hz and 576×1024 resolution, extendable to 15-second rollouts via autoregressive prediction with 3-frame overlap. Additionally, given a condition frame and action, Vista produces a scalar reward score in [0,1] computed as the exponential of negative averaged conditional variance over M=5 denoising rounds of 10 steps each.

## Method

Vista is initialized from Stable Video Diffusion (SVD, 2.5B parameters) and trained in two phases. Phase 1 introduces two novel losses on top of the standard diffusion objective: a dynamics enhancement loss that adaptively re-weights the per-frame MSE by the squared frame-difference discrepancy between prediction and ground truth (highlighting moving regions), and a structure preservation loss that penalizes high-frequency feature mismatch via 2D Fourier high-pass filtering to retain edges and lane markings. Condition frames are injected by replacing noisy latents with clean encoded latents (latent replacement) rather than channel concatenation, with separate timestep embeddings for condition vs. prediction frames. Phase 2 freezes the pretrained UNet and adds LoRA adapters (rank 16) to all attention layers plus new zero-initialized projection layers for action conditioning via cross-attention; training proceeds at 320×576 for 120K iterations then fine-tunes at 576×1024 for 10K iterations, using a collaborative strategy that mixes unlabeled OpenDV-YouTube (actions zeroed) with labeled nuScenes, and enforces action independence by activating only one action mode per sample. A triangular classifier-free guidance schedule (s_min=1.0, s_max=2.5) prevents saturation drift during long-horizon autoregressive rollouts.

## Results

On the nuScenes validation set, Vista achieves FID 6.9 and FVD 89.4, surpassing the best prior driving world model (GenAD) by 55% in FID (15.4→6.9) and 27% in FVD (122.7→89.4, where Drive-WM is the best FVD baseline). In human evaluation across four datasets (OpenDV-YouTube-val, nuScenes, Waymo, CODA), Vista is preferred over the strongest general-purpose video generators (SVD, DynamiCrafter, I2VGen-XL) in over 70% of comparisons on both visual quality and motion rationality. For action controllability, Vista reduces average trajectory difference from 3.785 m (action-free) to 0.832 m (with angle & speed + 3 dynamic priors) on nuScenes, and from 3.646 m to 1.140 m (with trajectory + 3 priors) on the unseen Waymo dataset.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Vista dominates all five driving-specific world models on both FID and FVD. Against the closest competitor GenAD (CVPR 2024), Vista improves FID from 15.4 to 6.9 (55% reduction) and FVD from 184.0 to 89.4 (51% reduction); against Drive-WM, the best FVD baseline, Vista cuts FVD from 122.7 to 89.4 (27% reduction). The gap over earlier methods is even larger: DriveGAN (FID 73.4, FVD 502.3), DriveDreamer (FID 52.6, FVD 452.0), and WoVoGen (FID 27.6, FVD 417.7) are all outperformed by 2–10×. In the human evaluation against three general-purpose video generators (SVD, DynamiCrafter, I2VGen-XL), Vista is preferred in over 70% of pairwise comparisons on both visual quality and motion rationality, confirming that its driving-specific training yields more physically plausible futures than web-scale video models. On the unseen Waymo dataset, Vista's action controllability (trajectory condition) reduces FVD from 311.8 (action-free) to 268.9, demonstrating zero-shot generalization of control that none of the prior driving world models have validated.

## Relation to Prior Work

Vista builds directly on Stable Video Diffusion (SVD) as its base latent diffusion architecture, adapting it from a general-purpose image-to-video generator into a dedicated driving world model. It departs from prior driving world models (DriveGAN, DriveDreamer, WoVoGen, Drive-WM, GenAD) that are confined to low resolutions (≤256×448), low frame rates (≤2 Hz), and single-modality action controls, by introducing two targeted losses (dynamics enhancement and structure preservation), a latent replacement mechanism for multi-frame dynamic priors, and a LoRA-based unified conditioning interface for multi-modal actions. The collaborative training strategy on OpenDV-YouTube and nuScenes, along with the action independence constraint, distinguishes Vista from GenAD, which fully fine-tunes on a single dataset and requires manual labeling of the large-scale corpus.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Vista establishes a new state-of-the-art for driving world models with a substantial margin (55% FID, 27% FVD improvement), and demonstrates for the first time that a driving world model can serve as a generalizable reward function using its own prediction uncertainty rather than external detectors. The practical impact is significant: the multi-modal action controllability (command, goal point, trajectory, angle/speed) that generalizes zero-shot to unseen datasets like Waymo makes it directly compatible with prevalent planning algorithms. Key open questions remain around computational efficiency for real-time deployment, quality degradation in very long-horizon rollouts, and whether the approach transfers to surround-view or scalable transformer architectures.
