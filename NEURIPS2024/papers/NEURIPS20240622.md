## Problem

Directly applying centralized adaptive minimax methods (e.g., TiAda) to distributed settings causes non-convergence because locally computed adaptive stepsizes become inconsistent across nodes, introducing a persistent steady-state error. No existing fully distributed adaptive method achieves near-optimal convergence for nonconvex-strongly-concave minimax problems without requiring knowledge of problem-dependent parameters such as smoothness or strong-concavity constants.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240622/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each node i holds a private local loss f_i(x,y) computed from its own data distribution D_i (e.g., a subset of MNIST or CIFAR-10 classes), and the nodes communicate over a fixed graph with a doubly-stochastic weight matrix W. Beyond the standard decision variables x_{i,k} and y_{i,k}, each node transmits two additional scalar quantities—the accumulated gradient norms m_{i,k}^x and m_{i,k}^y—to its neighbors at every iteration. The algorithm outputs the average primal variable x̄_K that is an ε-stationary point of the global minimax objective, with a guaranteed rate of Õ(ε^{-(4+δ)}) for arbitrarily small δ > 0.

## Method

D-AdaST augments the standard distributed GDA update with a lightweight stepsize tracking protocol: each node maintains running sums of squared gradient norms for both primal and dual variables, and these sums are averaged across the network using the same doubly-stochastic matrix W that governs consensus on the decision variables. The primal stepsize is scaled by v_{i,k}^{-α} where v_{i,k} = max{m_{i,k}^x, m_{i,k}^y}, while the dual stepsize is scaled by u_{i,k}^{-β} where u_{i,k} = m_{i,k}^x, with 0 < β < α < 1 enforcing time-scale separation between the two updates. The tracking protocol ensures that the per-node stepsize inconsistencies ζ_v² and ζ_u² vanish asymptotically, eliminating the steady-state error that causes vanilla distributed adaptive methods to diverge. Theoretically, the paper characterizes explicit transient times for time-scale separation and network quasi-independence, and proves a near-optimal Õ(ε^{-(4+δ)}) sample complexity matching the centralized TiAda rate, while also constructing counterexamples showing that without tracking, D-TiAda provably fails to converge.

## Results

Theoretically, D-AdaST achieves a near-optimal convergence rate of Õ(ε^-(4+δ)) for any arbitrarily small δ > 0 to find an ε-stationary point for distributed NC-SC minimax problems, matching the centralized TiAda rate and approaching the known lower bound of Ω(ε^-4). In contrast, D-TiAda (without stepsize tracking) provably suffers a constant steady-state error term Õ((ζ_v² + κ²ζ_u²)C²) that prevents exact convergence. Experimentally, D-AdaST outperforms D-AdaGrad, D-TiAda, and D-NeAda across synthetic quadratic objectives (n=50,100 nodes), robust CNN training on MNIST (n=20,40 nodes over ring/exponential/dense graphs), and Wasserstein GAN training on CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100 (n=10 nodes), with D-AdaST achieving higher inception scores and faster convergence of ‖∇_x f(x,y)‖².

## Baselines & Comparisons

D-AdaST consistently outperforms all three baselines across every experimental setting. Against D-TiAda (the direct distributed extension of centralized TiAda), D-AdaST eliminates the stepsize inconsistency that causes D-TiAda to fail to converge entirely (Theorem 1 shows D-TiAda can remain stationary indefinitely, and Figure 1(c) shows ζ_v² never converges for D-TiAda while it decays to zero for D-AdaST). Against D-AdaGrad, D-AdaST achieves faster convergence of the primal gradient norm on both synthetic functions and robust CNN training, particularly when the initial stepsize ratio γ_x/γ_y is favorable (e.g., 0.2). Against D-NeAda (a double-loop method requiring 15 inner-loop iterations per outer step for fair comparison), D-AdaST achieves lower training loss on MNIST robust CNN across ring (ρ_W=0.97), exponential (ρ_W=0.67), and dense (ρ_W=0.55) graphs, and higher inception scores on CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100 GANs. Notably, D-AdaST's performance is relatively insensitive to graph connectivity (quasi-independence of network), while baselines degrade more on sparse topologies like the ring graph.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the centralized TiAda algorithm (Li et al., 2023), which achieves parameter-agnostic time-scale separation for nonconvex-strongly-concave minimax via asymmetric exponential decay rates on primal and dual stepsizes, to the fully distributed setting. The key departure is the discovery that naively applying TiAda's per-node adaptive rule (yielding D-TiAda) provably fails to converge due to stepsize inconsistency across heterogeneous nodes, and the introduction of a lightweight stepsize tracking protocol (two extra scalar variables per node) that restores consistency. This positions D-AdaST between the fixed-stepsize distributed minimax literature (Local SGDA, decentralized SGDA) and the centralized adaptive minimax line (TiAda, NeAda), filling the gap of parameter-agnostic adaptive methods in distributed settings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

D-AdaST is the first fully distributed adaptive minimax method achieving near-optimal O~(ε^-(4+δ)) convergence without requiring knowledge of smoothness, strong-concavity, or network parameters, which is practically significant for distributed training of GANs and robust models where such parameters are intractable to estimate. The counterexample proving non-convergence of the natural distributed extension of TiAda is a useful negative result that cautions against porting centralized adaptive methods to distributed settings without coordination. Open questions include whether the stepsize tracking overhead (two scalars per iteration) remains negligible at scale, whether the approach extends to non-strongly-concave or nonconvex-nonconcave settings, and whether the near-optimal rate can be tightened to exactly O(ε^-4) as in the centralized lower bound.
