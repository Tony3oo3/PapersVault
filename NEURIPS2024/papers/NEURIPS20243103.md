## Problem

Real-world image dehazing is hindered by two intertwined challenges: existing deep methods fail to accurately model the complex distribution of real haze (causing color distortion and detail blurring), and the scarcity of paired real-world data forces reliance on synthetic data that introduces a domain gap. Prior unfolding approaches like PDN do not exploit the complementary information between the dehazed image and transmission map, leading to overfitting, and no principled pseudo-labeling strategy existed for real-world dehazing.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single hazy RGB image (H×W×3) and outputs a dehazed RGB image (H×W×3) together with a per-pixel transmission map (H×W×1). The global atmospheric light is not explicitly predicted; instead it is absorbed into a simplified scattering equation where an all-ones matrix replaces the atmospheric light term, shifting the optimization focus to scene and haze characterization. Training uses synthetic paired data (RIDCP500, 500 images with estimated depth) for pre-training and 4,807 real hazy images from the RESIDE URHI subset for pseudo-label fine-tuning, with evaluation on the RTTS subset (~4,000 images) and Fattal's 31-image set.

## Method

CORUN is a 4-stage deep unfolding network that alternates Transmission and Scene Gradient Descent Modules (TGDM/SGDM) with Cooperative Proximal Mapping Modules (T-CPMM/S-CPMM) at each stage, implementing dual proximal gradient descent on a cooperative energy function that jointly optimizes the transmission map and the clear image under a simplified atmospheric scattering model. Learnable step-size parameters (λ_k, μ_k) at each stage allow adaptive control of updates, while a global coherence loss enforces physical consistency only at the output, preventing overfitting of intermediate layers. Colabator is a plug-and-play iterative mean-teacher framework that generates pseudo-labels for real-world fine-tuning: a teacher network (EMA-updated from the student) dehazes non-augmented inputs, a composite quality assessor (DA-CLIP haze-density score × MUSIQ quality score) produces a spatial trust weight map, and an optimal label pool retains only the best-ever pseudo-label per image, updating it only when both density and quality scores improve. The student is trained on strongly augmented inputs supervised by the weighted pseudo-labels, with the overall fine-tuning loss combining contrastive perceptual loss, global coherence loss, and a CLIP-based density loss.

## Results

On the RTTS dataset, CORUN+ achieves the best performance across all three non-reference metrics: FADE 0.824, BRISQUE 11.956, and NIMA 5.342. Compared to the second-best method RIDCP (FADE 0.944, BRISQUE 17.293, NIMA 4.965), CORUN+ improves FADE by 12.7%, BRISQUE by 30.8%, and NIMA by 7.6%. In a user study with 21 evaluators, CORUN+ scores 7.76/10 on RTTS and 8.04/10 on Fattal's dataset, outperforming RIDCP (7.14/7.28) and DGUN (6.04/6.33). For downstream object detection (YOLOv3 mAP on RTTS), CORUN+ achieves 0.57 mean AP, surpassing RIDCP (0.56) and DGUN (0.55).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CORUN+ outperforms all 8 baselines on every metric (FADE, BRISQUE, NIMA) on the RTTS dataset. The closest competitor is RIDCP [7], which achieves the second-best BRISQUE (17.293) and NIMA (4.965) but is surpassed by CORUN+ by 30.8% and 7.6% respectively on those metrics. PDN [11], the first deep unfolding network for dehazing, shows the best FADE among baselines (0.876) but suffers from detail blurring due to not exploiting complementary information between the dehazed image and transmission map. DGUN [10] produces aesthetically pleasing results (NIMA 4.653) but loses significant local detail, while D4 [26] causes overall brightness reduction and detail loss in darker areas. The paper also demonstrates that Colabator is plug-and-play: when applied to DGUN, it improves FADE from 1.111 to 0.857 and BRISQUE from 25.085 to 20.731, confirming its generalizability beyond CORUN.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the deep unfolding network lineage for dehazing initiated by PDN (ECCV 2018) and the general proximal gradient descent framework of DGUN (CVPR 2022). CORUN departs from PDN's single-path unfolding by introducing a dual-path cooperative architecture that jointly optimizes transmission and scene features via paired T&SGDM and T&S-CPMM modules, while Colabator introduces the first iterative mean-teacher pseudo-labeling framework specifically tailored to real-world dehazing, combining DA-CLIP haze-density scoring and MUSIQ quality assessment with an optimal label pool to filter unreliable pseudo-labels. The work positions itself against GAN-based unpaired methods (D4, DAD, CDD-GAN) and synthetic-data-only approaches, arguing that coherence-controlled pseudo-labeling avoids the artifacts and domain gaps inherent in those paradigms.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play Colabator framework is practically significant: it improves diverse architectures (DGUN, C2PNet, FFA-Net, GDN) without architectural modification, offering a general recipe for bridging the synthetic-to-real gap in dehazing. However, the method still struggles with extreme haze where scene information is irrecoverably lost, and it does not address co-occurring degradations like blur or low-light, limiting its applicability to complex real-world scenes. Open questions include whether generative priors or multi-modal inputs (depth, event cameras) can recover details in the densest haze regimes, and whether the CLIP-based quality gating generalizes beyond the specific text prompt ('hazy') used here.
