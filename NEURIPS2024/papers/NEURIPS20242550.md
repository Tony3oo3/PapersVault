## Problem

Existing concept-unlearning methods for text-to-image diffusion models operate at the prompt level, making them trivially bypassed by adversarial prompt attacks such as Ring-A-Bell, SneakyPrompt, and Concept Inversion. Moving to image-based unlearning is a natural fix, but naively pushing the model away from unsafe images introduces ambiguity: the model cannot distinguish which visual features (e.g., nudity) should be forgotten from unrelated features (e.g., the person, the background) that must be preserved, leading to catastrophic degradation of general generation quality.

## Input / Output

At training time, DUO consumes paired RGB images (256 pairs per concept) synthesized via SDEdit, where each pair shares all visual attributes except the target unsafe concept; the model is fine-tuned as a LoRA adapter (rank 32) on Stable Diffusion 1.4 or SD3, with cross-attention layers frozen to decouple unlearning from text conditioning. At inference time, the unlearned LoRA is loaded onto the base diffusion model, which still accepts a standard text prompt and produces a 512×512 (SD1.4) or 1024×1024 (SD3) image, but the internal visual features associated with the unlearned concept have been removed regardless of what the prompt requests.

## Method

DUO formulates concept unlearning as a Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) problem over paired images: SDEdit is used to generate safe counterparts of unsafe images by adding noise at t=0.75T and denoising with negative guidance on the unsafe concept, ensuring the only difference between the pair is the target feature. The Diffusion-DPO loss (derived via the ELBO and Jensen's inequality) is then applied so the model's denoising score improves on the safe image while degrading on the unsafe one, effectively performing gradient descent on preferred samples and gradient ascent on dispreferred ones. A novel output-preserving regularization term ‖ε_φ(x_T) − ε_θ(x_T)‖² is added at the fully-noised timestep t=T to anchor the model's prior distribution without interfering with the removal of unsafe visual features at lower noise levels. The entire procedure is parameter-efficient: only LoRA weights are updated, and for complex concepts like violence the unlearning is decomposed into four sub-concepts (blood, suffering, gun, horror) trained separately and merged.

## Results

On the nudity unlearning task, DUO (β=250) achieves a FID of 13.59 and CLIP score of 30.84 on MS COCO 30k, matching the original SD1.4v (FID 13.52, CS 30.95) while achieving ~90% Defense Success Rate (DSR) against all three red teaming methods (Ring-A-Bell, SneakyPrompt, Concept Inversion). For violence unlearning, DUO (β=1000) achieves a FID of 13.37 and CLIP score of 30.78, again comparable to the original model. The Pareto curves (Figures 4, 7) show DUO consistently dominates baselines in the DSR vs. prior-preservation trade-off, with a sharp bend indicating diminishing returns beyond β=500 for black-box attacks and β=250 for white-box attacks.

| SPM | 14.05 | 30.84 |

## Baselines & Comparisons

All three baselines (ESD, UCE, SPM) are prompt-based unlearning methods that are easily circumvented by adversarial prompt attacks (Ring-A-Bell, SneakyPrompt) and white-box attacks (Concept Inversion), whereas DUO achieves ~90% DSR against all of them by directly removing visual features rather than relying on text conditioning. On prior preservation, DUO (β=250) matches SPM (FID 13.59 vs. 14.05) and UCE (FID 13.59 vs. 13.95) on nudity, and DUO (β=1000) even slightly outperforms SPM on violence (FID 13.37 vs. 13.53). The one notable trade-off is that DUO (β=500) shows a significant CLIP score drop (19.88 vs. ~30.8 for baselines) on nudity, though this is resolved at β=250 where CLIP recovers to 30.84. In the visually-similar-concept ablation (Table 3), DUO (black-box setting) achieves the lowest LPIPS to the original model for both 'man' (0.14) and 'ketchup' (0.23) generation, outperforming SPM (0.15, 0.20) and substantially outperforming ESD (0.58, 0.69), confirming that image-based unlearning with paired data preserves unrelated features more faithfully than prompt-based methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant line of prompt-based concept unlearning (ESD, CA, SPM, UCE, Forget-me-not) by shifting the unlearning target from text-conditioned behavior to internal visual features, making the defense independent of the input prompt. Methodologically, it repurposes Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) and its diffusion-model extension (Diffusion-DPO) for the unlearning task, using SDEdit to synthesize paired safe/unsafe images that resolve the ambiguity of which visual features to erase. The closest concurrent work, SafeGen, also uses image pairs but relies on supervised learning with blurred targets, whereas DUO's preference-optimization formulation provides more selective guidance without direct training on degraded images.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By removing visual features rather than text associations, DUO addresses a fundamental vulnerability in current T2I safety mechanisms: adversarial prompt attacks that trivially bypass prompt-based unlearning. The practical impact is significant for both open-source and closed-source deployment, as the defense persists even when model weights are accessible. Key limitations remain: concepts sharing visual features with the target (e.g., 'blood' vs. 'ketchup') may be partially affected, and the paper acknowledges that new adversarial attacks could still emerge. Open questions include scaling the approach to more abstract or compositional concepts and whether the SDEdit-based data curation generalizes beyond nudity and violence.
