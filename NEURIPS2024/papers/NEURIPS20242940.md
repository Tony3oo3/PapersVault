## Problem

Existing dense (occupancy) and sparse (trajectory-set) representations for multi-agent driving behaviors suffer from occlusion, modality collapse, and exponential combinatorial complexity, leading to unstable collective behavioral patterns when prediction and planning are jointly optimized. No prior work formulates an explicit topological supervision that captures consensual future interactions among all scene agents in a way that simultaneously stabilizes both prediction and planning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242940/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes historical agent kinematic states (x, y, v_x, v_y, θ) over 2 s at 10 Hz (planning) or 1 s (prediction), HD map polylines segmented into 256 (planning) or 768 (prediction) pieces of length 20, and only the ego vehicle's current state to avoid closed-loop training gap. Outputs include per-agent multimodal future trajectories as Gaussian mixtures (M=6 modes for planning, M=64 anchored for prediction), an N_a × N_a binary edge-topology matrix encoding pairwise braid intertwines, and a branched ego plan consisting of a short-term safe segment (t_b=3 s) followed by M_J=6 long-term branches conditioned on joint predictions of the top-K_M=4 interactive agents.

## Method

BeTop derives from braid theory: each agent's future trajectory is mapped into a shared local coordinate frame, and pairwise segment intersections (intertwine indicators) define an asymmetric edge topology that distinguishes assertive from passive interactions. BeTopNet implements a synergistic Transformer decoder with dual streams per layer—a TopoDecoder that iteratively refines the edge-topology query and a TransDecoder that decodes trajectories using topology-guided local attention, which restricts cross-attention keys to the top-K=32 agents ranked by the current topology estimate. Training combines imitation objectives (NLL on GMM components selected by winner-take-all, BCE on topology edges) with an imitative contingency planning loss that minimizes a worst-case short-term repulsive-potential cost plus probability-weighted branched long-term costs, all learned end-to-end. The topology acts as an explicit interaction prior that replaces implicit attention or multi-step game-theoretic rollouts, yielding stable joint behavioral patterns at O(N_I·N_a) cost versus O((N_a−1)^{N_a}) for full braid inference.

## Results

On nuPlan planning, BeTopNet achieves the top average planning score of 0.766 on Test14-Hard (+7.9% over PlanTF's 0.725) and 0.878 on Test14-Random (+3.7% over PlanTF's 0.847), with particularly strong closed-loop non-reactive scores of 0.771 and 0.902 respectively. On the proposed Test14-Inter interactive benchmark, BeTopNet attains 0.894 PDMScore (+3.8% over PlanTF's 0.871). On WOMD motion prediction, BeTopNet achieves 0.4566 mAP for marginal prediction (+2.7% over EDA) and 0.2412 mAP for joint prediction (+4.1% over MTR++), ranking 1st on both WOMD leaderboards at submission.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BeTopNet outperforms all learning-based planners on nuPlan: it beats PlanTF [73] by +7.9% average score on Test14-Hard and +3.7% on Test14-Random, with the largest gap in closed-loop non-reactive simulation (+6.2% Hard, +4.3% Random). Against rule-based PDM-Closed [69], BeTopNet gains +15.8% and +18.4% CLS-NR on Hard and Random respectively, while also surpassing the hybrid PDM-Hybrid [69] by +4.7% average on Hard. On WOMD marginal prediction, BeTopNet edges out EDA [53] by +2.7% mAP and +3.4% Soft mAP without using extra LiDAR data or pretrained models (unlike MGTR [85]), and surpasses MTR++ [25] by +6.0% mAP. On WOMD joint prediction, BeTopNet leads MTR++ [25] by +4.1% mAP and AMP [32] by +5.1% mAP, while achieving a large +25.4% mAP margin over the game-theoretic GameFormer [8]. The method also improves upon PlanTF [73] by +3.8% PDMScore on the proposed interactive Test14-Inter benchmark, with +5.5% driving progress and +2.9% driving compliance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the transformer-based motion prediction architecture of MTR [22] and the iterative decoding paradigm of DAB-DETR [78], but introduces a fundamentally new supervision signal: Behavioral Topology (BeTop), derived from braid theory [46], which explicitly labels consensual multi-agent future interactions as a topological graph. It departs from prior implicit interaction modeling (GNNs, attention-based relation learning) by providing an explicit, geometry-invariant topological prior that bridges dense occupancy representations and sparse trajectory predictions. The contingency planning paradigm from [79] is adapted with a repulsive potential field cost from GameFormer [8], and the topology-guided local attention mechanism selectively queries only top-K interactive agents rather than the full scene.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

BeTop provides the first explicit topological supervision for multi-agent behavioral patterns in autonomous driving, offering a principled way to stabilize joint prediction and planning under interaction uncertainty. The Test14-Inter benchmark addresses a real gap in evaluating interactive corner cases where simple motion models fail but human experts succeed. Key limitations include the one-step (non-recursive) topology formulation, 2D-only formulation, and disconnection from perception modules; the authors identify multi-step recursive BeTop, 3D extension, and end-to-end perception integration as natural next steps. The computational complexity of O(N_I · N_a) for topology computation (vs. O((N_a-1)^{N_a}) for full braid inference) makes it practical, but scalability to very large agent counts remains to be tested.
