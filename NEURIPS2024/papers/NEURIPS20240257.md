## Problem

Deep-learning-based Model-X knockoff methods struggle to simultaneously satisfy the swap property (for FDR control) and minimize reconstructability (for selection power) at the sample level, because enforcing the swap property pushes the generated knockoff toward the original data, creating high collinearity. This trade-off is exacerbated in small-sample and non-Gaussian settings, where existing methods either lose FDR control or suffer from near-zero power.

## Input / Output

The method takes an n×p data matrix X and a uniformly distributed random vector Z of the same dimension as input to the Knockoff Transformer, which outputs a knockoff matrix X̃ of shape n×p. After post-training perturbation with a row-permuted version of X, the final knockoff is combined with X into an n×2p design matrix; ridge regression (or DeepPINK for the IBD case study) yields knockoff statistics w_j = |β̂_j| − |β̂_{j+p}|, from which features are selected via the standard Model-X threshold at a target FDR level q (typically 0.1).

## Method

DeepDRK trains a Vision-Transformer-based Knockoff Transformer (8 heads, 8 layers, 512 hidden dim) adversarially against K=2 Gumbel-softmax swappers that generate random swap subsets B. The swap loss uses sliced Wasserstein distance between (X, X̃) and its swapped version, augmented with a risk-extrapolation (REx) term to equalize SWD across swappers and a cosine-similarity penalty to prevent swapper mode collapse. A dependency regularization loss based on sliced Wasserstein correlation (SWC) between X and X̃ is added to reduce reconstructability, though the authors observe it competes with the swap loss during training. To resolve this, a post-training dependency regularized perturbation (DRP) blends the trained knockoff with a row-permuted copy of X at weight α_n (set to 0.5), which provably vanishes asymptotically (Proposition 3.2) while empirically boosting power without sacrificing FDR control.

## Results

On the Gaussian mixture synthetic benchmark (p=100), DeepDRK is the only method that maintains FDR near the 0.1 nominal threshold across all sample sizes and data distributions. At n=200, DeepDRK achieves mean FDR of 0.116 with mean power of 0.791, while all four baselines exceed FDR 0.39 (ranging up to 0.772) despite achieving near-perfect power (0.97–1.00). At n=2000, DeepDRK reaches FDR 0.081 with power 0.973, matching KnockoffGAN's power (0.973) while achieving lower FDR (0.081 vs. 0.118). On the semi-synthetic scRNA-seq and IBD metabolomics datasets, DeepDRK consistently delivers the highest power among all FDR-controlled methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DeepDRK decisively outperforms DDLK, Deep Knockoff, and sRMMD on FDR control: at n=200, these three baselines exhibit mean FDR of 0.72–0.77 (roughly 7× the 0.1 target), while DeepDRK achieves 0.116; at n=2000, the gap narrows slightly but remains stark (0.63–0.73 vs. 0.081). The price DeepDRK pays is lower power at small sample sizes (0.791 at n=200 vs. 0.97–1.00 for the high-FDR baselines), though this gap closes at n=2000 where DeepDRK matches KnockoffGAN's power (0.973) while achieving lower FDR (0.081 vs. 0.118). KnockoffGAN is the only other baseline that approaches FDR control, but it still exceeds the 0.1 threshold at both sample sizes and shows higher variance in FDR (std 0.110 at n=200). On the semi-synthetic scRNA-seq 'Tanh' case, KnockoffGAN and sRMMD drop to near-zero power while DDLK's FDR remains uncontrolled; DeepDRK maintains the best power among all FDR-controlled methods. On the IBD metabolomics case study (real response, no ground truth), DeepDRK identifies 19 of 23 literature-supported metabolites, the highest recall among all five methods, while sRMMD identifies only 5 of 5 (lowest FDR but severe under-selection).

## Relation to Prior Work

DeepDRK extends the Model-X knockoff framework (Candès et al., 2018) from parametric/Gaussian settings to arbitrary distributions by replacing GAN- or autoencoder-based knockoff generators with a transformer-based architecture trained under a multi-source adversarial attack formulation. It builds directly on DDLK's (Sudarshan et al., 2020) single neural-network swapper by generalizing to K independent swappers with a risk-extrapolation (REx) term and a diversity regularizer, and on Spector & Janson's (2022) reconstructability minimization by substituting the Gaussian-specific conditional-variance objective with a distribution-free sliced Wasserstein correlation (SWC) loss. The post-training dependency regularized perturbation (DRP) is a novel contribution not present in any prior knockoff method.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and formalizes a fundamental tension in deep knockoff training: the swap loss (controlling Type I error / FDR) and the dependency regularization loss (controlling Type II error / power) compete and cannot both be minimized simultaneously at the sample level, analogous to the classical Type I–Type II tradeoff. This reframing, combined with the DRP post-training step, provides a principled way to decouple the two objectives and recover power without sacrificing FDR guarantees. Open questions remain: the theoretical characterization of how SWC minimization and DRP jointly affect the swap property and selection power is acknowledged as unresolved, and the method's behavior under very high dimensionality (p >> n) or highly non-stationary data is not explored.
