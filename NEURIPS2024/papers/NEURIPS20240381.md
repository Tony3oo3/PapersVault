## Problem

Speculative decoding accelerates LLM inference by drafting tokens with a small model and verifying them in parallel with the target model, but training a separate draft model is costly and impractical. Existing self-drafting methods (Medusa, REST, Lookahead) either achieve unsatisfactory token acceptance rates or incur high drafting latency, and fixed-step drafting wastes computation on difficult tokens while missing opportunities on easy ones. The paper tackles the joint problem of reducing drafting cost and dynamically adapting draft length to token difficulty without any external drafter.

## Input / Output

At inference, the method consumes a text prompt (token sequence) and the frozen weights of a target LLM (e.g., Vicuna-7B/13B). The shallow sub-network (first ℓ layers, ℓ=2 for 7B, ℓ=3 for 13B) processes each token sequentially during drafting, while the remaining layers (ℓ+1 through L) process all drafted tokens' hidden states in a single parallel forward pass during verification. The output is a sequence of generated tokens that is distributionally identical to standard autoregressive decoding (lossless), with the number of tokens per verification step ranging from 1 to γ+1 depending on acceptance.

## Method

Kangaroo introduces a double early-exiting mechanism for self-speculative decoding. The first early exit constructs the self-drafting model from the target LLM's first ℓ layers plus a lightweight adapter (one multi-head attention layer and two LayerNorm layers, ~67M parameters) and the shared frozen LM Head, trained via knowledge distillation from the full model to bridge the representation gap between shallow and deep layers. The second early exit is a token-level dynamic stopping rule: during drafting, the process halts as soon as the self-drafting model's top-1 confidence for the current token drops below a threshold η, avoiding wasted computation on uncertain tokens. This stopping criterion generalizes to tree decoding, where both tree depth and per-level node selection (Top-K children) are determined dynamically by confidence scores rather than a fixed static tree. Verification requires only the remaining layers ℓ+1 through L applied in parallel to the concatenated early-exited hidden states, making the per-token verification cost independent of the number of drafted tokens.

## Results

On Spec-Bench with Vicuna-7B, Kangaroo (with dynamic tree verification) achieves an average walltime speedup of 1.72×, peaking at 2.04× on the Math subtask and 1.93× on MT-Bench. For Vicuna-13B, the average speedup is 1.65× with a peak of 1.87× on Math. Notably, Kangaroo uses only 67M additional parameters (one multi-head attention layer + two LayerNorm layers), which is 88.7% fewer than Medusa's 591M heads, while still outperforming Medusa on average speedup at both model sizes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Kangaroo outperforms all five baselines on average speedup at both 7B and 13B model sizes. Against Medusa (the strongest self-drafting baseline), Kangaroo achieves 1.72× vs 1.63× (7B) and 1.65× vs 1.24× (13B) average speedup, while using only 67M parameters versus Medusa's 591M (88.7% fewer). Against SpS (which uses a separately trained 68M draft model), Kangaroo wins by 0.16× (7B) and 0.11× (13B) on average, and the gap widens substantially on Math (2.04× vs 1.51× for 7B). Against REST, Kangaroo leads by 0.29× (7B) and 0.42× (13B) on average, with the largest margin on Math (2.04× vs 1.23× for 7B). Lookahead decoding is the weakest baseline, with Kangaroo surpassing it by 0.43× (7B) and 0.61× (13B) on average; on 13B, Lookahead actually degrades below 1× on three subtasks. In the appendix (Table 4, Llama2-13B-Chat), Kangaroo w/o Tree also outperforms Draft & Verify by 0.33× on average speedup (1.45× vs 1.12×).

## Relation to Prior Work

Kangaroo sits at the intersection of speculative decoding (Leviathan et al., 2023) and early-exiting literature, repurposing the latter as a mechanism for constructing a self-drafting model rather than for standalone inference acceleration. It departs from Medusa's approach of training independent FFN heads on the penultimate layer by instead reusing the first few transformer layers of the target LLM plus its frozen LM Head, bridging the representation gap with a minimal adapter (one multi-head attention + two LayerNorms). The token-level dynamic early stopping extends the fixed-step drafting paradigm common to all prior speculative decoding methods, making both tree depth and node selection adaptive to per-token confidence.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is substantial: a 67M-parameter adapter outperforms Medusa's 591M-parameter heads in walltime speedup across six diverse subtasks and two model scales, making self-speculative decoding far more accessible for deployment. The double early-exiting formulation (layer-level for the draft model, token-level for dynamic stopping) provides a clean, generalizable design principle. However, the paper acknowledges that the representation gap between shallow and deep layers may persist on complex tasks, and the method's effectiveness is tied to the specific target architecture. Open questions include whether the adapter can be further compressed, how the approach generalizes to non-Vicuna/LLaMA architectures, and whether the dynamic tree construction can be made hardware-aware for heterogeneous accelerators.
