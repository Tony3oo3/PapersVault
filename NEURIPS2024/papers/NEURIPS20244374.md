## Problem

Existing conditional diffusion guidance methods for graph generation (classifier-based and classifier-free) treat structural and property information uniformly, lacking the hierarchical, asymmetric co-evolution needed to capture intricate dependencies between graph structure and its associated properties. This inadequacy leads to suboptimal representations, particularly in tasks requiring precise control over multiple graph properties simultaneously.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20244374/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts one or more scalar conditioning values (e.g., quantum properties like Cv or μ for QM9, composite docking/QED/SA scores for ZINC250K, or graph metrics like density and clustering for network graphs) and produces a full graph structure: for 3D molecules this includes node coordinates (N×3), node features (N×d1), and edge information (N×N×d2); for 2D graphs it produces node features (N×F) and a weighted adjacency matrix (N×N). The number of stem processes scales with the number of conditioned properties, and the conditioning context is injected via adaptive layer normalization or feature concatenation depending on the architecture.

## Method

Twigs defines a trunk SDE over the graph structure y_s and k stem SDEs over dependent property variables y_i, with the joint distribution factorizing as p(y_s)·∏p(y_i|y_s). The key mechanism, loop guidance, orchestrates information flow during reverse sampling: the structure is first denoised by the trunk score s_θ, the denoised structure then conditions each stem score s_φ_i to denoise its property, and the updated property scores are summed back into the trunk score to further refine the structure—forming a closed guidance loop at each timestep. Training minimizes a denoising score matching objective over the combined score s_θ + Σs_φ_i, with the architecture instantiated as either a Diffusion Graph Transformer (for 3D molecules) or a GCN with graph multi-head attention over higher-order adjacency matrices (for 2D graphs). The reverse SDE is derived from the Kolmogorov forward/backward equations under the conditional independence factorization, yielding a principled SDE-based framework that generalizes classifier-free and classifier-based guidance as special cases.

## Results

On QM9 single-property conditional generation, Twigs achieves the lowest MAE across all six quantum properties (e.g., 0.559 for Cv, 0.627 for μ, 1.36 for α, 323 for Δε, 225 for εHOMO, 244 for εLUMO), outperforming the strongest baseline JODO by 3–5%. On ZINC250K molecule optimization, Twigs attains the best novel top-5% docking score on 4 of 5 targets (e.g., 10.542 for 5ht1b vs. MOOD's 10.487) and the highest novel hit ratio on 4 of 5 targets (e.g., 16.366% for 5ht1b vs. MOOD's 11.873%). On generic network graphs (Community-small and Enzymes), Twigs achieves the lowest MAE on all eight property-dataset combinations, with margins of 1–15% over the next-best method (MOOD-4).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Twigs consistently outperforms all 12 baselines across every evaluated task. Against the strongest diffusion-based competitors on QM9 (JODO, EEGSDE, EDM, TEDMol, GeoLDM, EquiFM), Twigs reduces MAE by 3–5% over JODO (the prior best) and by 10–50% over earlier methods like EDM and EEGSDE. On ZINC250K molecule optimization, Twigs surpasses MOOD (the best score-based baseline) on 4 of 5 targets for both docking score and novel hit ratio, with the largest gap on 5ht1b (16.37% vs. 11.87% hit ratio) and fa7 (0.90% vs. 0.43%); it is second only to MOOD on braf. Against reinforcement-learning baselines (REINVENT, MORLD) and VAE-based HierVAE, Twigs achieves substantially higher novel hit ratios (e.g., 3.73% vs. 0.48% for REINVENT on parp1). On generic network graphs (Community-small, Enzymes), Twigs beats GDSS, GDSS-T, Digress, and both MOOD variants on all eight property-dataset combinations, with the largest margin over GDSS on assortativity (15.2 vs. 19.6 MAE on Community-small). The only case where Twigs is not the absolute best is braf on ZINC250K, where MOOD edges it out by a small margin (10.421 vs. 10.343 docking score; 2.207% vs. 1.933% hit ratio).

## Relation to Prior Work

Twigs extends score-based diffusion models for graph generation (Song et al. 2021; GDSS by Jo et al. 2022) by introducing an asymmetric hierarchical structure: a trunk process for graph structure and multiple stem processes for properties, connected via a novel 'loop guidance' mechanism. This departs from both classifier-based guidance (MOOD, EEGSDE) and classifier-free guidance (JODO, EDM), which treat structure and properties within a single uniform diffusion flow, and from symmetric multi-flow approaches (GDSS, EDGE) that assign equivalent roles to nodes and edges. The SDE-based formulation and the factorization of the joint score into trunk and stem contributions provide a principled mathematical framework that unifies and generalizes prior guidance schemes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Twigs offers a more expressive and controllable framework for conditional graph generation by explicitly modeling the dynamic interplay between structure and properties through co-evolving diffusion processes, rather than relying on post-hoc guidance signals. The consistent improvements across QM9, ZINC250K, and network graph datasets suggest the hierarchical decomposition captures dependencies that flat guidance schemes miss. Key limitations include the conditional independence assumption among properties given structure (which the authors show works practically but may not always hold), modest computational overhead from additional stem processes, and the current restriction to graph-structured data. Whether the loop guidance principle transfers to non-graph domains (images, text, audio) remains an open and potentially impactful question.
