## Problem

Temporal modeling in time series anomaly detection either assumes feature independence or mixes variables of diverse physical nature, omitting or diluting the spatial associations that characterize normal behavior in multivariate systems. The paper identifies that anomalies systematically dissolve pre-existing inter-feature associations (Spatial Association Reduction, SAR), but existing temporal detectors lack the spatial information needed to exploit this phenomenon for robust detection and diagnosis.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241532/cropped_page1_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a multivariate time series window X ∈ R^(2W×N) (default 2W=100, N features) which is temporally split into two equal halves for parallel processing. It produces a scalar anomaly score per timestamp combining normalized data-space and association-space reconstruction errors, plus a per-feature anomaly score (data reconstruction error) used to rank and identify the anomalous feature set for diagnosis. The spatial dimension is processed before the temporal dimension (transposed windows), making the architecture spatially aware by design.

## Method

SARAD comprises two modules: an encoder-only Transformer that spatially reconstructs the input via MHSA on transposed subseries (learning pairwise inter-feature association mappings A_h^L), and a 2-layer MLP that autoencodes the aggregated spatial progression in the association space. The key mechanism is subseries division: the window is split in half, each half is encoded independently, and the non-negative backward difference ReLU(A_1 − A_2) of their final-layer association mappings captures the reduction-only change (SAR). Progression is aggregated via column sums across all H heads in the final layer to form S ∈ R^(H×N), which the MLP reconstructs. Training is end-to-end with joint loss L = ‖X̂−X‖² + λ‖Ŝ−S‖² and gradient stopping on S to prevent association representation collapse. The joint detection criterion s = (r−μ_r)/σ_r + (p−μ_p)/σ_p combines data-space and association-space errors, complementing each other: data errors capture magnitude deviations while progression errors are sensitive to association dissolution even when raw values remain close to normal.

## Results

SARAD achieves state-of-the-art anomaly detection on three of four benchmarks. On SMD, it reaches 79.97% AUC-ROC and 79.67% VUS-ROC, outperforming the next-best MAD-GAN (64.35% / 64.16%) by over 15 points. On HAI, it attains 96.87% AUC-ROC and 96.17% VUS-ROC, surpassing DiffAD (86.96% / 86.25%) by ~10 points. On PSM and SWaT, SARAD performs on par with the best baselines (GDN and DIF respectively), trailing by only 1.36% and 0.36% on VUS-ROC while leading on AUC-PR.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SARAD decisively outperforms all baselines on SMD (+15.51% VUS-ROC over MAD-GAN, +13.60% over GDN) and HAI (+9.92% VUS-ROC over DiffAD, +12.14% over GDN), demonstrating the value of spatial association modeling on systems with rich inter-feature dependencies. On PSM, GDN narrowly edges SARAD on VUS-ROC (63.13 vs 61.77) and AUC-ROC (63.51 vs 61.87), while SARAD leads on AUC-PR (41.06 vs 40.66) and VUS-PR (41.01 vs 40.53), indicating a near-tie. On SWaT, DIF slightly surpasses SARAD on AUC-ROC (89.38 vs 88.29) and VUS-ROC (87.88 vs 87.52), but SARAD wins on VUS-PR (70.68 vs 70.54) and substantially on AUC-PR (72.90 vs 73.19 is actually DIF winning; SARAD's AUC-PR is 72.90 vs DIF's 73.19). Temporal-modeling baselines (TranAD, AT, ATF-UAD, DCdetector) consistently underperform, with AT scoring as low as 47.41% AUC-ROC on HAI, confirming the paper's argument that temporal-only modeling is insufficient for spatial anomaly detection. GDN, despite its explicit graph-based spatial modeling, is limited by its single-timestamp prediction target and cannot match SARAD's range-wise detection capability.

## Relation to Prior Work

SARAD repurposes the Transformer architecture from its dominant role in temporal modeling (TranAD, AT, ATF-UAD, DCdetector) to explicitly model spatial inter-feature associations, following the spatial-modeling insight of iTransformer (Liu et al., 2024) and Crossformer but redirecting it toward anomaly detection rather than forecasting. It departs from graph-based spatial detectors like GDN, which learn a static feature graph but score anomalies via single-timestamp prediction errors, by instead tracking how pairwise associations *change over time* during an anomaly. The subseries-division mechanism and the autoencoding-in-association-space criterion are novel architectural choices that have no direct precedent in prior anomaly detection literature, though the underlying observation—that anomalies break correlations—echoes the dynamic watermarking defense literature in cyber-physical security.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of Spatial Association Reduction (SAR) as a systematic, column-wise phenomenon provides a new detection signal that is complementary to and independent of data-space reconstruction errors, addressing a known weakness of temporal detectors at anomaly onset. The approach is non-intrusive and applicable to any supervisory system, making it broadly deployable. Key limitations include quadratic time complexity in the number of features (restricting scalability to very large sensor networks), evaluation on only four datasets, and incomplete diagnosis labels on SWaT and HAI that cap achievable diagnosis scores. Open questions include whether hierarchical or clustered variants can scale SARAD to thousands of features, and whether the SAR signal generalizes to domains outside industrial control (e.g., healthcare, finance).
