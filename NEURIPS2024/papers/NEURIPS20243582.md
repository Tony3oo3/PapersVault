## Problem

Existing FL anomaly-detection and robust-aggregation methods rely on aggregate metrics (loss, accuracy) or parameter/gradient similarity, which cannot track, visualise, or explain how individual clients' predictive performance and decision-making processes evolve over training. This limits human trust and control over FL dynamics and makes it difficult to distinguish malicious clients from legitimately underperforming ones in non-IID settings.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243582/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes each client's model parameters θ^(k)(t) evaluated on a small server-owned validation set (as few as 250 samples), plus a counterfactual generator co-trained with the predictor. For image data (small-MNIST, small-CIFAR-10), counterfactuals are generated at a 1,000-dim ResNet-18 embedding level rather than pixel space to conceal sensitive information. Outputs are 2D coordinates in two planes (EBP via PCA on per-sample error vectors; CBP via t-SNE on pairwise sliced-Wasserstein distances between clients' counterfactual distributions), which are then converted into scalar behavioural scores s^(k)(t) used as aggregation weights.

## Method

FBPs model FL as a dynamical system where each client's behaviour is governed by local training forces and periodic server-aggregation corrections (Eq. 4), and empirically track this via two complementary 2D planes: the Error Behavioural Plane maps per-sample prediction errors through PCA to reveal where clients differ in predictive performance, while the Counterfactual Behavioural Plane maps pairwise Wasserstein distances between clients' counterfactual distributions through t-SNE to reveal differences in decision boundaries. The counterfactual generator (CFGen) is a two-VAE latent-variable model that explicitly conditions the counterfactual latent z' on target labels, trained end-to-end with the predictor so that FL aggregation naturally personalises it per client. Federated Behavioural Shields (FBS) then computes a multiplicative score combining an error score (1 − min(‖e^(k)‖,1), distance from the EBP origin) and a counterfactual score (inverse mean distance to all other clients in the CBP), smoothed by a moving average over L rounds, and uses these scores as weights in the aggregation step to down-weight or exclude malicious clients without requiring prior knowledge of the attack.

## Results

Federated Behavioural Shields (FBSs) outperforms all state-of-the-art robust aggregation baselines (Krum, Median, Trimmed-mean, RFA) across five attack types (No attack, Crafted-noise, Inverted-gradient, Label-flipping, Inverted-loss) on Breast Cancer, Diabetes, and small-MNIST datasets. The method achieves up to 10 pp improvement over Median under Label-flipping attack and up to 16 pp improvement over baselines in the no-attack setting on small-MNIST. On Breast Cancer (non-IID), FBSs achieves 95.8% mean accuracy across all attack conditions, compared to 97.0% in the IID setting. Additionally, FBSs surpasses standard FedAvg in the absence of attackers on both Breast Cancer and small-MNIST, demonstrating that behavioural-based weighting is superior to sample-count-based aggregation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FBSs consistently outperforms Krum [12], Median [11], Trimmed-mean [11], and RFA [42] across most attack-dataset combinations, with the sole exception being Inverted-gradient on small-MNIST where Trimmed-mean performs slightly better. The largest margin is over Median, the most robust baseline, reaching 10 pp under Label-flipping and 16 pp in the no-attack setting on small-MNIST. FBSs also surpasses standard FedAvg [20] in the absence of attackers on Breast Cancer and small-MNIST, indicating that behavioural weighting (combining error and counterfactual scores) is inherently superior to uniform sample-count aggregation. The paper notes that predictive performance alone (error score) is insufficient in non-IID settings, as honest clients may show low performance and be misclassified; the counterfactual score provides complementary decision-making information that is critical for robust detection. Overall, the combined FBSs (error + counterfactual) generally outperforms either component alone, though counterfactual-only scoring occasionally matches or exceeds the full method under no-attack conditions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes two established ideas from different subfields—behavioural/semantic spaces from evolutionary computation (Mouret & Clune; Zhang et al.) and counterfactual explanations from XAI (Wachter et al.; Dominici et al.'s CFGen; VCNet)—and combines them into a dual-plane framework specifically for federated learning. It departs from prior FL robustness work (Krum, Median, Trimmed-mean, RFA) that relies on parameter-space or gradient-space distances by instead scoring clients on predictive-error diversity and decision-boundary similarity, and from prior FL visualisation tools (VADAF, HetVis) that use single-metric or Grad-CAM-based descriptors. The dynamical-systems framing (Eq. 4) formalises client behaviour evolution in a way not previously attempted in the FL literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The FBS aggregation mechanism offers a practical, attack-agnostic defense that outperforms standard robust aggregation baselines by up to 16 pp, and the dual-plane visualisation gives FL operators an interpretable window into client dynamics without accessing raw data. Key limitations include the dependence on a server-side validation set (mitigated but not eliminated by the counterfactual plane) and the additional communication/compute overhead of co-training a counterfactual generator. Open questions include scalability to thousands of clients, integration with differential privacy or homomorphic encryption, and whether the behavioural-plane framework can be extended to clustered or hierarchical FL settings.
