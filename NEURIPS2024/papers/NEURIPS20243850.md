## Problem

Differentiable logic gate networks (LGNs) offer extremely efficient inference by operating directly on hardware-native logic gates, but prior work was limited to randomly connected architectures that could not capture spatial structure in images (capping at 62% on CIFAR-10), could not scale beyond 6 layers due to vanishing gradients from washed-out activations, and required prohibitive training times. The paper asks how to bring the inductive biases of convolution, deeper network training, and computational efficiency to differentiable LGNs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243850/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Inputs are binary-encoded images: smaller CIFAR-10 models (S, M) use 2-bit precision via 3 thresholds, while larger models (B, L, G) use 5-bit precision with fixed, non-learned edge and curvature detector kernels that convert pixels to binary encodings; MNIST uses 1-bit. Outputs are class labels produced by a group-sum over per-class output neurons, divided by a learned temperature τ to form logits for softmax cross-entropy. All reported accuracies are from discretized (hard) LGNs where each node's gate is fixed to its highest-probability choice.

## Method

The core contribution is the logic gate tree convolution: each kernel is a complete binary tree of depth d (typically 3) with learnable logic gates at internal nodes, whose leaves are randomly sampled from the kernel's receptive field, and whose parameters are shared across all spatial placements. Two supporting components enable scaling: (1) logical OR pooling via the maximum t-conorm (max) replaces max-pooling, which is faster to compute and backpropagate; and (2) residual initializations set each gate's softmax distribution to ~90% probability on the feedforward AND gate (z₃=5) instead of a random Gaussian, preventing vanishing gradients and enabling training of 23-layer networks. The LogicTreeNet architecture stacks 4 such convolutional blocks with 2×2 or-pooling (reducing spatial size by 4× per block), followed by 3 randomly connected layers and a group-sum head, trained with AdamW and optionally a neural network teacher on class scores. Fused CUDA kernels compute entire tree-plus-pooling operations in a single pass, reducing memory accesses by 68% and enabling training speeds up to 200× faster per gate than prior LGN implementations.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, LogicTreeNet-G achieves 86.29% test accuracy using only 61M logic gates, matching the accuracy of XNOR-Net (86.28%) while requiring 29× fewer gates. On MNIST, LogicTreeNet-M reaches 99.23% accuracy with 566K gates and 5ns FPGA inference time, outperforming all BNN baselines in both accuracy and speed. On FPGA hardware, LogicTreeNet-B (CIFAR-10) achieves 24ns per image versus 45.6μs for FINN CNV, a 1900× speedup at comparable accuracy (80.17% vs 80.10%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LogicTreeNet-G (86.29%, 61M gates) matches XNOR-Net's accuracy (86.28%) while using only 3.4% of its gate count (1780M), a 29× reduction. LogicTreeNet-B (80.17%, 16M gates) slightly exceeds FINN CNV (80.10%, 901M gates) in accuracy while requiring 56× fewer gates and running 1900× faster on FPGA (24ns vs 45.6μs). LogicTreeNet-L (84.99%, 28.9M gates) surpasses LUTNet (84.95%, 1290M gates) in accuracy with 44.6× fewer gates. On MNIST, LogicTreeNet-M (99.23%, 566K gates, 5ns) outperforms FINN CNV (98.40%, 5.28M gates, 641ns) in both accuracy and speed (160× faster), and beats LowBitNN (99.2%, 152μs) in accuracy while being 30000× faster. The original DiffLogic Net [7] is substantially outperformed: the largest prior model (62.14%, 5.12M gates) is surpassed by LogicTreeNet-M (71.01%, 3.08M gates) with fewer gates and 8.9% higher accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Petersen et al.'s (NeurIPS 2022) differentiable logic gate networks, which learned networks of binary logic gates via probabilistic relaxation but were limited to random connectivity and 6 layers of depth. The key departure is introducing convolutional structure through logic gate tree kernels, logical OR pooling (via maximum t-conorm), and residual initializations, enabling spatial pattern capture and training of much deeper networks. It positions itself as fundamentally different from BNNs and quantized networks, which optimize a matmul-based abstraction and then translate to logic, whereas LGNs optimize the logic gates directly at the hardware level.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 29× reduction in logic gate count (61M vs. 1780M for comparable CIFAR-10 accuracy) and ~1900× FPGA inference speedup (24ns vs. 45.6μs) make this a compelling option for embedded and real-time applications where chip area and latency are critical. Open questions include whether the approach scales to ImageNet-level tasks, whether it generalizes to continuous-output tasks like object detection or segmentation, and whether custom ASIC implementations can close the remaining gap to ~500M FPS (currently bottlenecked by data transfer to FPGA). The paper also notes that the comparison to BNNs is somewhat asymmetric since BNNs carry the inductive bias and computational overhead of matrix multiplication.
