## Problem

Survival prediction models are typically evaluated for discrimination and marginal calibration, but real-world clinical and operational decisions require conditional calibration—correctly calibrated predictions within arbitrary feature-defined subgroups (e.g., elderly males, low-salary employees). Prior conformal post-processing methods for survival (notably CSD) rely on Kaplan-Meier sampling, which becomes inaccurate under high censoring rates or when the KM curve terminates at high survival probabilities, and none of these methods provide conditional calibration guarantees.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242736/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

CiPOT is a model-agnostic post-processing step that consumes the ISD predictions of any trained survival model (AFT, DeepSurv, DeepHit, CoxTime, etc.) on a held-out conformal set, along with a user-specified set of percentile levels P and a repetition parameter R. For censored subjects, it draws R pseudo-uniform conformity scores from U[0, Ŝ(c_i|x_i)] rather than requiring the unobserved event time. The output is a vertically rescaled ISD curve for a new instance, guaranteed to be monotone decreasing and to preserve the relative ordering of survival probabilities at every time point (hence preserving AUROC and Antolini's C^td).

## Method

CiPOT defines the conformity score as the Individual survival Probability at Observed Time (iPOT): Ŝ_M(e_i|x_i) for uncensored subjects, and a pseudo-uniform draw from U[0, Ŝ_M(c_i|x_i)] for censored subjects, leveraging the fact that the survival probability at the true event time is uniform under the probability integral transform. The conformal adjustment then vertically shifts each ISD curve so that the empirical percentile of the iPOT scores maps to the target percentile, effectively rescaling the curve in probability space rather than time space (as CSD does). This vertical scaling inherently preserves monotonicity and the relative ordering of survival probabilities at any fixed time, guaranteeing AUROC and C^td invariance. The paper proves asymptotic marginal calibration under exchangeability and conditional independent censoring, and asymptotic conditional calibration under the additional assumption that the base model is a consistent survival estimator with a differentiable, bounded-derivative inverse. A new worst-slab calibration metric (Cal_ws) is introduced to evaluate conditional calibration by adversarially searching for the least-calibrated feature slab on an exploration set.

## Results

Across 104 method comparisons (15 datasets × 7 baselines), CiPOT achieves near-optimal marginal calibration in 95/104 cases (91%) versus baselines and 68/104 (65%) versus CSD, while maintaining discriminative C-index in 82/104 cases (79% ties + wins). For conditional calibration (Cal_ws, evaluated on 69 comparisons with larger datasets), CiPOT wins 64/69 (93%) against baselines and 51/69 (74%) against CSD. CiPOT also improves IBS in 63/104 and MAE-PO in 54/104 comparisons, and reduces space complexity from O(N·|P|·R) (CSD) to O(N·R).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CSD (Qi et al., ICML 2024), the closest prior conformal method, CiPOT wins on marginal calibration (68 vs. 36) and conditional calibration (51 vs. 18), but loses on C-index (26 vs. 11) because vertical scaling can reverse median-time ordering, whereas CSD's horizontal shifting preserves it. CiPOT also preserves AUROC and Antolini's time-dependent C-index (proven theoretically), which CSD does not guarantee. Against the 7 survival baselines (AFT, GB, DeepSurv, N-MTLR, DeepHit, CoxTime, CQRNN), CiPOT dominates on calibration metrics (95/104 Cal_margin wins, 64/69 Cal_ws wins) while matching C-index in 75/104 cases. The main exception is DeepHit, which is heavily miscalibrated (predicts S=0 at the last time point), where CSD's horizontal shift handles the tail better than CiPOT's vertical scaling. KM serves as an empirical lower bound for marginal calibration (red dashed lines in Figure 3), and CiPOT approaches or matches this bound in most settings. CiPOT is also computationally more efficient than CSD, with O(N·R) vs. O(N·|P|·R) space complexity.

## Relation to Prior Work

CiPOT is a direct refinement of CSD (Qi et al., ICML 2024), replacing its horizontal KM-sampling adjustment with a vertical scaling based on the Individual survival Probability at Observed Time (iPOT) as a conformity score. This design choice, rooted in the probability integral transform and the distribution calibration definition of Haider et al. (JMLR 2020), eliminates CSD's dependence on Kaplan-Meier estimation and its homoskedasticity assumption, while inheriting the split conformal prediction framework from Papadopoulos et al. (2002) and Romano et al. (2019). The key conceptual departure is that CiPOT operates in probability space rather than time space, which inherently preserves monotonicity and AUROC-based discrimination metrics that CSD's horizontal shifts can violate.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Conditional calibration is critical for individual-level clinical decisions and fairness (equalized calibration across protected groups), yet no prior method addressed it in the censored survival setting. CiPOT provides the first asymptotic conditional calibration guarantee for survival analysis, along with a practical worst-slab metric (Cal_ws) for evaluation. Key limitations include the reliance on a consistent base estimator for the conditional guarantee (a standard but strong assumption), and weaker performance than CSD when the base model is severely miscalibrated (e.g., DeepHit's forced-zero tail). Open questions include whether finite-sample conditional guarantees are achievable under weaker assumptions, and how the method behaves when the base model violates the unbounded-range assumption.
