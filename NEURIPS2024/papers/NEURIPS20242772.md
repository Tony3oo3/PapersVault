## Problem

Existing long-context extrapolation methods for RoPE-based LLMs rely on the out-of-distribution (OOD) rotation angle theory, which predicts that smaller RoPE base values should improve context length. However, the paper shows this leads to only superficial long-context capability: the model maintains low perplexity but loses the ability to retrieve information from long contexts. The paper asks what actually bounds the true context length a model can process, and why OOD theory is insufficient to explain long-context behavior.

## Input / Output

The theoretical component takes a target context length L and RoPE dimension d as input, and computes a numerical lower bound on the RoPE base value (base_L) such that B_{m,θ} ≥ 0 for all relative distances m up to L. The empirical component fine-tunes or pre-trains transformer LLMs (Llama2-7B, Baichuan2-7B, a 2B model) on text sequences at various context lengths (4k to 32k) with different RoPE base values, producing perplexity on PG19 and retrieval accuracy on Long-eval, Needle-in-a-Haystack, and RULER benchmarks. The key output is not a single score but a constraint: the base must exceed a context-length-dependent threshold for genuine long-context retrieval to be possible.

## Method

The paper derives a novel 'long-term decay' property of RoPE: the quantity B_{m,θ} = Σ_{i=0}^{d/2-1} cos(mθ_i), which measures the model's ability to attend more to similar tokens than random tokens, decays as relative distance m increases (Theorem 1). For true context length L, the requirement B_{m,θ} ≥ 0 for all m ∈ [0, L] yields an absolute lower bound on the RoPE base (Eq. 11), computed numerically since no closed form exists. The paper validates this bound empirically by fine-tuning Llama2-7B and Baichuan2-7B at 32k context with varying bases (showing Long-eval accuracy drops sharply below the theoretical bound while perplexity remains flat), and by pre-training a 2B model from scratch with base=100 (showing effective context length is limited to ~500 tokens despite 4k training length). A controlled experiment with two OOD-free methods that differ only in B_{m,θ} behavior demonstrates that OOD avoidance alone does not guarantee long-context retrieval capability.

## Results

The paper derives an absolute lower bound for RoPE's base parameter as a function of target context length: e.g., 4.3×10³ for 1k, 2.7×10⁴ for 4k, 6.4×10⁵ for 32k, and 5.1×10⁸ for 1M tokens. Empirically, fine-tuning Llama2-7B on 32k context shows that perplexity on PG19 remains low across all tested bases (500 to 1e9), but Long-eval retrieval accuracy drops sharply below the theoretical bound of ~6.4×10⁵. On the RULER benchmark at 32k context, the average score is 7.40 for base=500, 14.19 for base=1e4, 61.08 for base=2e5, and peaks at 67.67 for base=5e6, confirming that bases below the derived lower bound yield only superficial long-context capability.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not compare against external methods but instead compares different RoPE base values (500, 1e4, 2e5, 6e5, 9e5, 5e6, 1e9) as the controlled variable. The key finding is that the theoretically derived lower bound (6.4×10⁵ for 32k) closely matches the empirical threshold: bases at or above ~6e5 achieve RULER averages of 65–68 at 32k context, while bases below it (500, 1e4, 2e5) score only 7–61. In the OOD-free comparison (Table 3), Method 1 (uniform θ scaling with base=5e6) achieves Long-eval scores of 0.33/0.27 at 15k/30k, while Method 2 (hybrid high/low-frequency scaling) scores 0.40/0.00, despite both avoiding OOD rotation angles—demonstrating that OOD avoidance alone is insufficient and the B_{m,θ}≥0 condition is the binding constraint. A 2B model pre-trained from scratch with base=100 could only retrieve from ~500 tokens despite 4k training context, and increasing the base to 1e6 during fine-tuning recovered substantially more effective context length.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on RoPE (Su et al.) and the family of long-context extension methods (PI, NTK-aware, YaRN, Dynamical-NTK) that are motivated by the out-of-distribution (OOD) rotation angle theory. It also engages critically with the recent proposal by Liu et al. to use a much smaller RoPE base (e.g., 500) to avoid OOD, showing that this approach yields only superficial long-context capability. The key conceptual departure is the introduction of a new theoretical property—long-term decay of the model's ability to attend more to similar tokens than random tokens—which is orthogonal to the OOD perspective and reveals an absolute lower bound on RoPE's base that OOD theory cannot capture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that OOD theory is insufficient to explain long-context capability challenges a dominant framework in the field and provides practitioners with a concrete, actionable lower bound table for RoPE's base at each target context length. The 'superficial capability' phenomenon—where perplexity remains low but retrieval fails—has direct implications for how long-context models should be evaluated and for pre-training decisions (a too-small base wastes training data). Open questions include whether an upper bound on RoPE's base exists, how the multi-layer stacking effect relaxes the single-layer bound, and whether the derived bound generalizes to non-standard RoPE variants or other position embedding schemes.
