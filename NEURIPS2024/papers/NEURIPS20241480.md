## Problem

Existing structural conditions on neural network loss landscapes—such as the Polyak-Lojasiewicz (PL) inequality, the Aiming condition, and quasar-convexity—either require impractical levels of over-parametrization (width scaling as Ω̃(n^{3/2})) or fundamentally exclude saddle points and local minima, which are known to exist in finite-width networks. This paper asks whether a weaker, more permissive function class can characterize realistic loss landscapes while still yielding convergence guarantees for gradient-based optimizers.

## Input / Output

The method operates on a standard ERM problem f(x) = (1/n)Σ f_i(x) with L-smooth individual losses and a finite interpolation error σ_int². Empirically, verification requires two training runs with the same seed: the first produces a final iterate x^K used as a proxy for Proj(x^k, S), and the second measures the quantity T(x_k) = ⟨∇f_{i_k}(x^k), x^k − x^K⟩ − α(f_{i_k}(x^k) − f_{i_k}(x^K)) − β f_{i_k}(x^k) (assuming f_i* = 0) across all iterations. The output is a feasible region of (α, β) pairs (with α ≥ β + 0.1) that satisfy the condition along the trajectory, plus theoretical convergence bounds of the form O(K^{-1/2}) + O(βσ_int²) for SGD.

## Method

The paper introduces the α-β-condition: for every x and every sample i, there exists x_p ∈ Proj(x, S) such that ⟨∇f_i(x), x − x_p⟩ ≥ α(f_i(x) − f_i(x_p)) − β(f_i(x) − f_i*), with α > β > 0. The key structural insight is that the β(f_i(x) − f_i*) term on the right-hand side absorbs the non-convexity (saddle points, local minima) that PL and Aiming cannot handle, while the α term preserves the 'aiming toward the minimizer set' property. The condition is shown to hold for 2-layer ReLU networks with logistic loss, matrix factorization, and several synthetic examples with saddle points or local minima, all without requiring over-parametrization. Under this condition, the paper proves O(K^{-1/2}) convergence for SGD (with a non-vanishing O(βσ_int²) neighborhood), and analogous guarantees for SPS_max and NGN, where the neighborhood size shrinks with model width, depth, and batch size. Empirical validation spans MLP, CNN, ResNet, LSTM, GNN, U-Net, DLRM, and Transformer architectures on ten datasets, confirming that feasible (α, β) pairs exist along actual training trajectories and that β decreases as models become more over-parameterized.

## Results

The paper proves that SGD converges at rate O(K^{-1/2}) under the α-β-condition (Theorem 1), with a non-vanishing error term O(βσ_int²) that shrinks as models become more over-parameterized. Empirically, the α-β-condition is verified across MLP (Fashion-MNIST), CNN (CIFAR-10), ResNet (CIFAR-100), LSTM (PTB, Wikitext-2), Transformer (SlimPajama, 70M/160M Pythia), GNN (OGBG), U-Net (FastMRI), and DLRM (Criteo 1TB). In contrast, the PL condition yields constants μ of order 10^{-8}–10^{-7} for LSTM training (implying impractically slow theoretical convergence), and the Aiming condition is violated (negative gradient-to-minimizer angle) during early training of LSTM models on both PTB and Wikitext-2.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is against three existing structural conditions: QCvx, Aiming, and PL. The α-β-condition strictly generalizes QCvx (reducing to it when β=0 and S is a singleton) and is incomparable with Aiming and PL in general. Empirically, Aiming is violated for a 3-layer LSTM on both PTB and Wikitext-2 (the gradient-to-minimizer angle becomes negative during early training), and PL yields μ ≈ 10^{-8}–10^{-7}, implying convergence rates orders of magnitude slower than observed. For the half-space learning problem (which has known saddle points), both Aiming and PL fail while α-β holds. Theoretically, the α-β-condition is verified for matrix factorization (which has saddle points) and 2-layer ReLU networks with logistic loss on any bounded set, without the sub-quadratic over-parametrization (Ω̃(n^{3/2})) required by prior PL/Aiming results. The convergence guarantees under α-β are weaker than under PL (sub-linear O(K^{-1/2}) vs. linear), but the condition is empirically satisfied in settings where PL and Aiming are not.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself against the family of convex-like structural assumptions (PL, Aiming, QCvx, KL) used to prove convergence of gradient-based optimizers, arguing that these conditions require impractical over-parametrization (sub-quadratic in dataset size) and exclude saddle points and local minima. The α-β-condition is introduced as a strictly more general framework: it recovers QCvx as a special case (β=0, singleton S) while accommodating non-convex features. For convergence analysis, the paper extends the SGD/SPS_max/NGN literature by deriving guarantees under this weaker condition, building on the smoothness and functional-dissimilarity assumptions standard in stochastic optimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a theoretically grounded and empirically validated condition that characterizes the loss landscape of practical (non-over-parametrized) neural networks, filling a gap between the idealized infinite-width theory and the messy finite-width reality. The non-vanishing βσ_int² term in the convergence rate honestly reflects that optimizers converge to a neighborhood rather than the exact minimum, aligning with empirical observations in language model training. Key open questions include: whether momentum-based methods (SGD with momentum, Adam) can be analyzed under this condition, whether tighter theoretical bounds on α and β can be derived for specific architectures, and how the condition behaves under architectural changes (depth, width) in a principled theoretical framework rather than empirical observation.
