## Problem

Previous rigorous proofs of the Strong Lottery Ticket Hypothesis established that random overparameterized networks contain subnetworks approximating target networks, but provided no guarantee on the sparsity of those subnetworks — winning tickets had Θ(m) parameters, essentially matching the original network size. This paper asks: given a desired sparsity level α (density γ = 1−α), how large must the overparameterized network be to guarantee the existence of a γm-parameter winning ticket that ε-approximates a target network with m_t parameters?

## Input / Output

The method takes a randomly initialized 2ℓ-layer feed-forward (or G-equivariant) network with weights drawn from Uniform[−1,1] and a family of target ReLU networks with spectral-norm-bounded weight matrices. It produces a pruned subnetwork of controlled density γ = ργ′ (where ρ captures layer-width imbalance) that ε-approximates every target in the family simultaneously, along with an explicit bound on the required overparameterization m/m_t as a function of γ and ε. The key structural constraint is that each pruned layer retains exactly k = γ′n* active edges per block, yielding a uniform sparsity pattern across layers.

## Method

The core contribution is the Random Fixed-Size Subset Sum (RFSS) Problem, which asks how large a sample of n i.i.d. sum-bounded random variables must be so that any target z ∈ [−√k, √k] can be ε-approximated by the sum of exactly k elements (rather than up to k, as in the classical RSS). The proof uses the second moment method generalized to arbitrary fixed subset size k, decomposing the second moment into three overlap regimes (full, partial, and small overlap) and bounding each via Chebyshev's inequality on the hypergeometric intersection distribution and the sum-bounded density condition. This yields the essentially tight requirement n ≥ c·log²(k/ε)/H₂(k/n), where H₂ is binary entropy. By replacing the unbounded RSS corollary from Lueker [17] with this fixed-size variant at each pruning step of the Pensia et al. [24] framework, the authors obtain Theorem 3 (DNNs) and Theorem 4 (equivariant networks), which interpolate between the dense-ticket regime of Pensia et al. (γ = constant) and the sparse-ticket regime of Malach et al. (γ = Θ(ε²/m_t²)), and a matching lower bound (Theorem 5) confirms the overparameterization requirement is essentially tight.

## Results

The paper's main quantitative contribution is Theorem 3 (SSLTH for DNNs), which shows that a random 2ℓ-layer network with overparameterization n_i* = c_amp · log²(2ℓd_{i-1}d_i γ'n_i*/ε) / H₂(γ') can be pruned to a subnetwork of density γ = ργ' that ε-approximates any target network. In the sparse-ticket regime (γm = Θ(m_t)), the required overparameterization is Θ(m_t²/ε²) with winning-ticket density γ = ε/m_t. In the dense-ticket regime (γm = Θ(m)), the result recovers Pensia et al.'s O(log(m_t²/ε²)) overparameterization up to a logarithmic factor. Theorem 5 provides a matching lower bound, establishing that the overparameterization dependence on sparsity is essentially tight.

**Comparison of overparameterization and sparsity guarantees across SLTH results (for target networks with m_t parameters, all layers of width d).**

| Result | Overparameterization (m/m_t) | Winning-ticket density γ | Sparsity α = 1−γ |
|---|---|---|---|
| Malach et al. [20] | Õ(m_t²/ε²) | Õ(ε²/m_t²) | 1 − Õ(ε²/m_t²) |
| Pensia et al. [24] | O(log(m_t²/ε²)) | Θ(1) (i.e. γm = Θ(m)) | 1 − Θ(1) |
| **This work (Theorem 3)** | c_amp · log²(2ℓd²γ/ε) / H₂(γ) | γ (free parameter) | 1 − γ |
| This work, sparse regime (γ = ε/m_t) | Θ(m_t²/ε²) | ε/m_t | 1 − ε/m_t |
| This work, dense regime (γ = const) | O(log²(1/ε)) | Θ(1) | 1 − Θ(1) |

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares its sparsity-aware SLTH bounds against two prior extremes. Against Malach et al. [20], which proved SLTH with overparameterization Õ(m_t²/ε²) but only guaranteed a winning ticket of the same size as the target (density γ = Õ(ε²/m_t²)), this paper recovers the same overparameterization while achieving a strictly better density of γ = ε/m_t (a polynomial improvement in m_t). Against Pensia et al. [24], which achieved the near-optimal O(log(m_t²/ε²)) overparameterization but only for dense tickets (γm = Θ(m), i.e., the ticket is roughly as large as the original network), this paper recovers the same overparameterization up to a logarithmic factor in the dense regime, and crucially extends the analysis to arbitrary sparsity levels via the binary entropy term H₂(γ). Theorem 5 establishes a lower bound of (d²/2)·log₂(k/ε)/H₂(k/n) on the required number of parameters n, which matches the upper bound of Theorem 3 up to constant factors, confirming that the sparsity-overparameterization tradeoff is essentially tight.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of rigorous SLTH proofs initiated by Malach et al. [20] and Pensia et al. [24], which relied on the Random Subset Sum (RSS) problem to show that overparameterized random networks contain subnetworks approximating target networks without training. The key departure is the introduction of the Random Fixed-Size Subset Sum (RFSS) problem, which constrains the subset size to exactly k, enabling control over the sparsity of the resulting winning ticket. This bridges the gap between the two prior extremes: Malach et al.'s result where the ticket size matches the target network (high sparsity, polynomial overparameterization) and Pensia et al.'s result where the ticket is nearly as large as the full network (low sparsity, logarithmic overparameterization). The proof technique generalizes Lueker's second-moment method to arbitrary fixed subset sizes, and the SLTH applications follow the pruning framework of Pensia et al. and Ferbach et al. for equivariant networks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work provides the first sparsity-aware SLTH guarantees, answering a natural question that had remained open: how much overparameterization is needed as a function of the desired sparsity level. The essentially tight characterization via binary entropy H₂(k/n) gives a clean interpolation formula, and the matching lower bound (Theorem 5) confirms optimality up to constants. However, the paper only proves existence of sparse winning tickets and offers no algorithm to find them, which remains a major open problem. Future directions include extending RFSS to the multidimensional case (enabling structured pruning guarantees) and improving the success probability from constant to 1−ε to remove a logarithmic factor, as well as developing efficient training-by-pruning methods that can actually locate these sparse tickets.
