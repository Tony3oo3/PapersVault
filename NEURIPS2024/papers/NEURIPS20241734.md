## Problem

Large-scale outdoor 3D datasets with fine-grained semantic annotations remain scarce: existing options are either small-scale with limited label vocabularies (e.g., SemanticKITTI with 25 classes) or large-scale but lacking dense multi-view imagery and detailed per-point semantics. This paper introduces USCILab3D, a 10M-image, 1.4M-point-cloud outdoor dataset collected over 12 months across a 229-acre campus, annotated with 267 semantic categories to bridge the gap between scale and annotation richness.

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes synchronized multi-view RGB images (5× Intel Realsense D455, 1280×720 at 15 FPS, 90°×65° FOV) and 32-beam 360° Velodyne HDL-32E LiDAR scans (10 Hz), along with calibrated camera intrinsics/extrinsics obtained via a 1 m checkerboard and Line-and-Plane Correspondence. Outputs are organized into 'sectors' of 1250 images and ~167 scans, yielding per-point semantic labels (267 classes, 12 categories) stored in JSON, pixel-wise Grounded-SAM masks, LeGO-LOAM and COLMAP SfM poses, and merged sector-level point clouds with per-point color and label assignments.

## Method

The annotation pipeline is entirely foundation-model-driven with no manual labeling. GPT-4 is queried every ~225 frames (≈15 s of robot travel) across all five camera views to enumerate visible semantic classes, producing 4,162 raw labels that are then clustered by GPT-4 into 267 unique labels across 12 categories. These labels serve as text prompts to Grounded-SAM, which generates pixel-wise segmentation masks; the authors verify robustness by showing that adding irrelevant prompts increases incorrect pixel labels by less than 1%. The 2D masks are projected onto 3D LiDAR points using calibrated camera extrinsics and interpolated LeGO-LOAM poses, with DBSCAN clustering applied per label to reject outlier projections caused by moving objects or calibration drift. LeGO-LOAM also produces merged sector-level point clouds, while COLMAP SfM provides complementary pose estimates for generative-model applications such as 3D Gaussian Splatting.

## Results

On the Novel View Synthesis benchmark using 3D Gaussian Splatting (7000 iterations, 7/8 train, 1/8 test), USCILab3D achieves PSNR 26.02, SSIM 0.86, and LPIPS 0.20 at 1280×720 resolution. These results surpass ETH3D (PSNR 21.25, SSIM 0.83, LPIPS 0.27), Tanks&Temples (PSNR 21.20, SSIM 0.77, LPIPS 0.28), and Mip-NeRF360 (PSNR 25.19, SSIM 0.75, LPIPS 0.25) on all three metrics. Deep Blending achieves a slightly higher PSNR (27.01) and SSIM (0.87) but with a substantially worse LPIPS (0.32) and only 2.6K images. The paper also reports semantic segmentation, panoptic segmentation, and semantic scene completion results in the supplemental material.

## Baselines & Comparisons

USCILab3D outperforms ETH3D by +4.77 dB PSNR, +0.03 SSIM, and −0.07 LPIPS, and beats Tanks&Temples by +4.82 dB PSNR, +0.09 SSIM, and −0.08 LPIPS, confirming that the large-scale, densely-overlapping multi-view captures yield substantially better reconstruction quality. Against Mip-NeRF360, USCILab3D leads by +0.83 dB PSNR, +0.11 SSIM, and −0.05 LPIPS, a more modest but consistent margin. The only dataset where USCILab3D does not dominate is Deep Blending, which achieves a higher PSNR (27.01 vs. 26.02) and SSIM (0.87 vs. 0.86), though Deep Blending's LPIPS is notably worse (0.32 vs. 0.20) and it contains only 2.6K images, making it a much smaller and less diverse benchmark. Overall, USCILab3D is the only dataset in the comparison that provides large-scale outdoor scenes with dense multi-view overlap, making it suitable for a wider range of downstream applications such as simulators.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the annotation pipeline of GPT-4 (for open-vocabulary label generation and clustering) and Grounded-SAM (for pixel-wise segmentation), combined with LeGO-LOAM and COLMAP for 3D trajectory reconstruction and pose estimation. It departs from prior outdoor semantic datasets like SemanticKITTI (25 categories) and nuScenes (31 categories) by leveraging foundation models to produce 267 fine-grained semantic labels, and from indoor datasets (ScanNet, Matterport3D) by providing large-scale outdoor multi-view coverage. The authors position USCILab3D as filling the gap between smaller-scale detailed semantic datasets and larger-scale but undetailed ones, and claim it is the first pipeline to annotate 3D point clouds entirely via image and text foundation models without manual intervention.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dataset's scale (10M images, 1.4M point clouds, ~6TB) and 12-month collection window capturing seasonal and weather variation make it a valuable resource for long-term 3D perception, novel view synthesis, and visual navigation research. However, all annotations remain machine-generated and unvalidated by human annotators, raising questions about label reliability at the 267-category granularity. The geographic scope is limited to a single 229-acre campus, and the authors acknowledge that merging LiDAR and COLMAP poses for unified temporal indexing is still an open problem. The foundation-model annotation pipeline itself is a methodological contribution that could be extended to other domains, but its error characteristics at scale remain unquantified.
