## Problem

Species range maps are essential for conservation and ecology, but the 'long tail' of species with few or no observations makes reliable range estimation difficult. Existing deep SDM approaches improve with more training data but have not effectively leveraged the rich habitat and range descriptions available in free-form text (e.g., Wikipedia) to enable zero-shot or few-shot range prediction for previously unseen species.

## Input / Output

The model takes geographic coordinates (lat, lon) as spatial input and free-form text descriptions (Wikipedia sections or short LLM-generated summaries of habitat/range) as species input, optionally augmented with environmental covariates. At inference, a single forward pass of the frozen GritLM encodes the text into a 4096-dim embedding, which a 3-layer FC network maps to a 256-dim species vector; the dot product with a 256-dim location embedding (from an MLP on coordinates) through a sigmoid yields the per-location presence probability, which is thresholded to produce a global range map. For few-shot use, the text-derived species vector serves as an L2 regularization target for logistic regression on position features.

## Method

LE-SINR extends the SINR implicit neural representation framework with a dual-branch species encoder: a set of learnable species tokens for observed species and a text-based encoder (frozen GritLM + 3-layer FC network) that maps Wikipedia text to the same 256-dim embedding space. Training uses a modified AN-full presence-only loss where negatives are sampled from other species at the same location and from random locations, approximated by selecting M=192 negatives per batch to keep computation tractable. The key insight is that aligning text descriptions with spatial locations through millions of species observations causes the location encoder to internalize rich geographic concepts (biomes, climate zones, countries, topography) from language, enabling zero-shot range estimation for novel species from text alone. For few-shot estimation, logistic regression on frozen position features is regularized toward the text-derived species embedding, reducing the number of required observations by up to 10× on S&T species.

## Results

On the zero-shot range estimation task (no observations for the target species), LE-SINR with range text and environmental features achieves a MAP of 0.53 on IUCN and 0.64 on eBird S&T, substantially outperforming the Model Mean baseline (0.09/0.35) and Constant Prediction baseline (0.01/0.22). With habitat text only, the model still reaches 0.32/0.52 MAP. When explicit species tokens are available (i.e., the species was seen during training), LE-SINR matches or slightly exceeds the Oracle SINR upper bound (0.69 vs. 0.67 IUCN; 0.78 vs. 0.77 S&T), confirming no performance degradation on observed species.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is the Oracle SINR model [Cole et al., ICML 2023], which is trained with observations from the evaluation species and serves as an upper bound. LE-SINR's zero-shot range-text predictions (0.53 IUCN / 0.64 S&T with +Env) close much of the gap to Oracle SINR (0.67 / 0.77) without any observations for the target species, and the paper reports that zero-shot range text is equivalent to roughly 10 observations in the few-shot logistic-regression setting on S&T. When species tokens are available, LE-SINR matches or slightly exceeds Oracle SINR (0.69 vs. 0.67 IUCN; 0.78 vs. 0.77 S&T), showing that the added text branch does not hurt supervised performance. Against the trivial baselines (Constant Prediction at 0.01/0.22 and Model Mean at 0.09/0.35), LE-SINR zero-shot results represent a 5–6× improvement in MAP. In the few-shot regime, incorporating the text-derived prior via L2 regularization toward the text embedding reduces the number of observations needed by a factor of 3× on IUCN and 10× on S&T to match the performance of a model trained on that many observations without the text prior.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the SINR framework (Cole et al., ICML 2023), which learns implicit neural representations of geographic space from presence-only observations, by adding a text-based species encoder branch that maps Wikipedia descriptions into the same embedding space. It departs from LD-SDM (Sastry et al., 2023), which also combines LLMs with SDMs but relies on rigid taxonomic hierarchy strings rather than free-form habitat and range descriptions, limiting generalization to unseen species. The approach also contrasts with GeoLLM (Manvi et al., ICLR 2024), which extracts geospatial knowledge from LLMs but requires a forward pass per location, whereas LE-SINR needs only one LLM pass per species text query, making it computationally efficient for dense global range map generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses the critical 'long tail' problem in species range mapping, where millions of species lack sufficient observations for reliable range estimation. The zero-shot capability—generating a plausible global range map from a few sentences of habitat or range description with no observations—is a genuinely new tool for ecologists working with poorly documented species. However, the approach inherits geographic biases from both Wikipedia and iNaturalist (skewed toward North America and Western Europe), relies on LLM-generated summaries that may propagate errors, and has not been compared against meta-learning or other few-shot paradigms. Open questions include whether the method generalizes to regions underrepresented in both text and observation data, and whether the precision is sufficient for high-stakes conservation or policy decisions.
