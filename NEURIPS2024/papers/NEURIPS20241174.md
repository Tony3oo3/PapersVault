## Problem

Large-scale face recognition datasets contain rich topological structure information, but existing FR models fail to preserve this structure in their latent embeddings, limiting generalization. The paper identifies that directly aligning input and latent space topology via persistent homology causes a 'structure collapse' phenomenon (discrepancy drops to zero during training but re-emerges at evaluation), and asks how to effectively encode and preserve this structure information without overfitting.

## Input / Output

The model takes 112×112 RGB face images and produces a latent embedding vector (dimension l) and a K-class classification probability vector. During training, a fraction (ξ=0.2) of each mini-batch is randomly perturbed with one of four augmentations (Random Erasing, GaussianBlur, Grayscale, ColorJitter) before passing through the network; the perturbed latent features are then topologically aligned with the original input space. At inference, no augmentation is applied and the model outputs the same embedding as a standard ArcFace model.

## Method

TopoFR introduces two complementary strategies on top of a standard ResNet + ArcFace pipeline. First, PTSA (Perturbation-guided Topological Structure Alignment) applies random structure perturbation to a subset of training samples to increase latent space diversity, then aligns the topological structure of the original input space with the perturbed latent space using persistent homology; instead of expensive bottleneck or Wasserstein distances, it retrieves persistence diagram values by subsetting pairwise distance matrices with persistence pairing indices for efficient, outlier-robust comparison. Second, SDE (Structure Damage Estimation) models prediction entropy with a Gaussian-Uniform Mixture fitted via EM to estimate per-sample uncertainty, then combines this with a Focal-loss term to compute a Structure Damage Score that adaptively reweights the ArcFace classification loss, directing the model to prioritize hard samples that damage latent space topology. The total objective is L_cls + α·L_sa, where α balances classification and structure alignment.

## Results

On the IJB-C benchmark (TAR@FAR=1e-4), R200 TopoFR trained on Glint360K achieves 97.84%, surpassing R200 AdaFace (97.33%) and TransFace-L (97.61%). On IJB-B (TAR@FAR=1e-4), the same model reaches 96.56%, outperforming AdaFace (96.12%). On the MFR-Ongoing challenge, R200 TopoFR achieves 93.96% on Mask and 92.85% on East Asian subsets, beating UniFace by +1.53% and +2.84% respectively, and ranks second on the academic leaderboard. On the saturated LFW/CFP-FP/AgeDB-30 benchmarks, TopoFR still yields small but consistent gains (e.g., 99.45% CFP-FP vs. 99.24% for AdaFace on Glint360K).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TopoFR consistently outperforms AdaFace across all backbone sizes and training datasets, with the largest margins on IJB-C: +0.22% (R50/MS1MV2), +0.06% (R100/MS1MV2), +0.15% (R200/MS1MV2), and +0.51% (R200/Glint360K). Against the ViT-based TransFace, TopoFR achieves higher IJB-C accuracy at every backbone level (e.g., R100 TopoFR 97.60% vs. TransFace-B 97.45% on Glint360K; R200 TopoFR 97.84% vs. TransFace-L 97.61%), demonstrating that the topological structure alignment strategy is effective even with a CNN backbone. On the MFR-Ongoing challenge, R200 TopoFR surpasses both UniFace and Partial FC on all reported MFR sub-benchmarks, with particularly large gains on the East Asian subset (+2.84% over UniFace, +3.33% over Partial FC) and the Mask subset (+1.53% over UniFace, +2.09% over Partial FC). On the saturated LFW/CFP-FP/AgeDB-30 benchmarks, improvements over the strongest baselines are modest (≤0.3%), consistent with the near-ceiling performance of existing methods. The SDE hard-sample mining strategy alone (Table 7) outperforms Focal Loss, MV-Softmax, AT_k, and EHSM by 0.38–0.60% on IJB-C (1e-4), confirming that the GUM-based uncertainty estimation provides a more effective difficulty signal than prior re-weighting schemes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the ArcFace training pipeline as its backbone and loss foundation, and adapts the Gaussian-Uniform Mixture (GUM) model from robust regression literature for hard sample scoring. Conceptually, it departs from the dominant FR paradigm of margin-based losses and deeper architectures by introducing topological data analysis—specifically persistent homology—into face recognition for the first time. The key methodological departure is the PTSA strategy, which avoids the structure collapse that arises from naive PH alignment by perturbing the latent space before alignment, and the SDE strategy, which combines GUM-based uncertainty estimation with Focal-loss-style reweighting to mine hard samples in a structure-aware manner.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to systematically study and exploit the topological structure of large-scale face datasets for improving FR generalization, and the discovery that direct PH alignment causes structure collapse (a form of overfitting in topological space) is a useful cautionary finding for anyone applying TDA to representation learning. The practical impact is demonstrated by consistent SOTA results across multiple benchmarks and a 2nd-place finish on the MFR-Ongoing leaderboard. Open questions include whether the approach can handle noisy labels (which SDE may inadvertently amplify), whether higher-dimensional homology beyond H₀ provides additional gains, and whether the 1.16× training overhead is acceptable in production settings.
