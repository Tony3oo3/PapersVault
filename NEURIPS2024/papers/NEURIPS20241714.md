## Problem

Existing complex logical query answering (CLQA) methods learn entity and relation embeddings bound to a specific knowledge graph vocabulary, making them transductive and unable to generalize to new graphs. Even the few inductive approaches that handle new entities still require a fixed relation vocabulary, leaving the hardest inductive (e,r) setting—where both entities and relations at inference time are entirely unseen—untackled by any prior method.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241714/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes an incomplete knowledge graph (a set of entities, relation types, and observed triples) and a first-order logic query expressed as a composition of relation projections and logical operators (∧, ∨, ¬). It produces a scalar score in [0,1] for every entity in the graph, interpreted as the probability that the entity satisfies the query's target variable, from which a ranked answer set is extracted. No external node or edge features are required; the model relies solely on the graph's relational structure, and the same pre-trained weights operate on graphs of arbitrary size and vocabulary without per-graph fine-tuning.

## Method

ULTRAQUERY decomposes query execution into two vocabulary-independent components: an inductive relation projection operator and non-parametric logical operators. The projection operator adapts the ULTRA inductive KG completion model, which builds relation representations dynamically from a meta-graph of four structural meta-relations (head-to-tail, tail-to-head, head-to-head, tail-to-tail) that are independent of any specific relation vocabulary; a labeling-trick GNN first encodes the meta-graph to produce conditional relation embeddings, then a second GNN over the entity graph scores each candidate entity. Logical operators (conjunction, disjunction, negation) are implemented as product fuzzy logic operations (element-wise multiplication, x+y−xy, and 1−x) over the [0,1] score vectors, requiring no learned parameters. A key challenge is the multi-source propagation issue: the pre-trained projection operator was trained on single-source 1p link prediction, but multi-hop queries initialize multiple nodes with non-zero scores at intermediate hops. ULTRAQUERY addresses this via short fine-tuning (10k steps) on complex queries from a single dataset (FB15k237), trading a small amount of 1p accuracy for substantially better performance on multi-hop and negation queries.

## Results

Averaged across 23 datasets, a single ULTRAQUERY model (177k parameters, trained only on FB15k237) achieves 0.31 MRR on EPFO queries and 0.178 MRR on negation queries, outperforming the best per-dataset baselines by 50% (EPFO) and 70% (negation) in relative MRR. On the 11 fully inductive (e,r) WikiTopics-CLQA datasets, ULTRAQUERY achieves 0.280 average EPFO MRR versus 0.016 for the heuristic baseline. On the hardest inductive (e) datasets (FB 550%, 300%, 217%), ULTRAQUERY outperforms the trainable GNN-QE baseline by 68–100% in EPFO MRR (e.g., 0.373 vs 0.222 on FB 550%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against QTO (30M parameters, trained per-dataset) on transductive benchmarks, ULTRAQUERY (177k params) loses on FB15k237 (0.242 vs 0.335 EPFO MRR) and NELL995 (0.226 vs 0.329), but wins on FB15k (0.764 vs 0.740 EPFO, 0.567 vs 0.492 Neg), attributed to QTO's brute-force materialization of the full V×V×R score tensor. Against GNN-QE (2M params, trained per-dataset) on inductive (e) datasets, ULTRAQUERY wins decisively on larger inference graphs (FB 550%: 0.373 vs 0.222; FB 300%: 0.359 vs 0.291; FB 217%: 0.375 vs 0.346 EPFO MRR) but underperforms on smaller graphs (FB 106%: 0.226 vs 0.373; FB 113%: 0.240 vs 0.352), where GNN-QE's graph-specific relation embeddings provide an advantage. Against the non-parametric edge-type heuristic on the 11 inductive (e,r) WikiTopics-CLQA datasets, ULTRAQUERY achieves 10–80× higher MRR (e.g., 0.569 vs 0.007 on Location, 0.317 vs 0.019 on Health), confirming that no existing trainable method can handle this fully inductive setting. Overall, ULTRAQUERY sets a new state of the art on 15 of 23 datasets while using 100–170× fewer parameters than the baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

ULTRAQUERY builds directly on ULTRA [15], an inductive KG completion model that represents relations via a meta-graph of four vocabulary-independent interaction types (head-to-tail, tail-to-head, head-to-head, tail-to-tail), and on labeling-trick GNNs [37] that break node symmetries without entity embeddings. It departs from the dominant CLQA paradigm (GQE, BetaE, QTO, GNN-QE) where both projection and logical operators are parameterized by graph-specific entity/relation embeddings, replacing them with a single inductive GNN-based projection operator and non-parametric product-logic fuzzy operators. The key conceptual leap is combining these two vocabulary-independent components into a unified zero-shot query answering framework, and identifying the multi-source propagation distribution shift between single-source link prediction pre-training and multi-hop query execution as a critical failure mode.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that a single small (177k parameter) model can answer complex logical queries on arbitrary KGs with entirely new entity and relation vocabularies, eliminating the need to train a dedicated model per graph. The multi-source propagation finding is practically important: it explains why naively reusing a pre-trained link predictor for multi-hop queries fails and motivates either short fine-tuning or score thresholding. Open questions include whether the approach scales to very large KGs (the authors suggest A*Net or AdaProp integration), whether it extends to non-tree query patterns, temporal queries, and queries without anchor nodes, and whether the weaker ROC AUC (easy vs. hard answer separation) can be improved without sacrificing zero-shot generality.
