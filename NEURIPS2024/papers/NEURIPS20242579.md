## Problem

The mechanisms underlying rich feature learning in finite-width networks remain poorly understood, with most theoretical insights derived from the opposing lazy (NTK) regime. While prior work identified that overall scale and the relative scale between layers influence learning dynamics, the precise role of the directionality of unbalanced initializations—whether earlier or later layers learn faster—and how this interacts with overall scale to determine the learning regime was not characterized. This paper asks: how exactly do layer-specific initialization variances and learning rates determine the transition between lazy and rich learning, and what inductive biases arise in each regime?

## Input / Output

The analysis takes a two-layer network (linear or piecewise linear) with parameters initialized at a given overall scale τ and relative scale δ = η_w a² − η_a‖w‖², trained on a dataset via continuous gradient flow with layer-specific learning rates. It produces exact closed-form solutions for parameter and function-space trajectories, a classification of the learning regime (lazy, rich, or delayed rich), the full NTK evolution over time, and a δ-dependent inductive bias potential that characterizes which interpolating solution the dynamics converge to in the overparameterized setting.

## Method

The core technique derives exact solutions to the gradient flow ODEs by exploiting the conserved quantity δ = η_w a² − η_a‖w‖², which constrains parameter trajectories to hyperboloids whose geometry (one-sheeted, double cone, or two-sheeted) is determined by the sign and magnitude of δ. By introducing hyperbolic coordinates μ = a‖w‖ and spherical coordinates φ = cos(angle between w and β*), the coupled nonlinear system reduces to a Bernoulli equation for μ and a Riccati equation for φ, both solvable in closed form. The key insight is that δ's directionality creates fundamentally different dynamics: upstream (δ > 0) causes fast alignment of w with minimal kernel movement, balanced (δ = 0) produces the classic two-phase rich learning (alignment then fitting), and downstream (δ < 0) yields a delayed rich regime where the NTK is initially static. The analysis extends to wide and deep linear networks via Kronecker-sum preconditioning matrices and to piecewise linear (ReLU) networks, where upstream initializations uniquely accelerate rich learning by rapidly changing activation patterns without large parameter movement—a mechanism absent in the linear setting.

## Results

The paper derives exact gradient flow solutions for a two-layer single-neuron linear network, showing that the conserved quantity δ = η_w a² − η_a‖w‖² fully determines the learning regime: upstream (δ ≫ 0) yields lazy dynamics with minimal kernel movement, balanced (δ = 0) yields rich dynamics with distinct alignment and fitting phases, and downstream (δ ≪ 0) yields a delayed rich regime. In nonlinear (ReLU) networks, the key finding is that upstream initializations (faster learning in earlier layers) accelerate rich feature learning, in contrast to linear networks where only balanced initializations produce rapid rich learning. Empirically, upstream initialization (achieved by dividing the first layer's initialization by α ≫ 1) reduces the time to grokking in modular arithmetic, decreases sample complexity on the Random Hierarchy Model, and promotes edge-detector alignment in CNN early layers, while driving NTK evolution through activation pattern changes rather than large parameter shifts.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical paper without a standard benchmark comparison table; its contributions are analytical characterizations and empirical validation rather than method-vs-method competition. The findings extend and correct prior work in several ways: (1) Unlike Azulay et al. [9], who showed that unbalanced initializations in two-layer linear networks tend toward lazy dynamics, this paper reveals that the *direction* of imbalance matters—upstream vs. downstream initializations produce fundamentally different trajectories, and in nonlinear networks upstream initializations actually *accelerate* rich learning. (2) Building on Chizat et al. [8], who showed overall scale τ determines lazy vs. rich, this paper adds the relative scale δ as a second independent axis, producing a two-dimensional phase portrait (Fig. 1b) where rapid rich learning occurs only at small τ *and* large positive δ. (3) The infinite-width phase diagram of Luo et al. [17] is recovered as a limiting case, with the conserved quantity δ replacing the relative initialization scale β_a/β_W. (4) The inductive bias theorem (Theorem 3.1) extends Azulay et al.'s result to allow δ < 0, revealing a tradeoff between minimum-norm solutions and preserving initialization direction that is absent in the balanced case.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the exact-solution methodology for linear network dynamics pioneered by Saxe et al. [10] and the time-warped mirror flow analysis of Azulay et al. [9] to a minimal two-layer single-neuron model that captures the full lazy-to-rich transition. It builds on Chizat et al.'s [8] observation that overall scale determines the regime by introducing a second axis—the relative scale's directionality (upstream vs. downstream)—and showing that this directionality, previously overlooked, fundamentally alters the learning trajectory. The analysis is then generalized to wide/deep linear networks and two-layer piecewise-linear networks, connecting to the infinite-width phase diagrams of Luo et al. [17] and the quantization literature on ReLU networks [49–55].

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides one of the few analytically tractable models for the lazy-to-rich transition in finite-width networks, yielding exact solutions that reveal how conserved quantities constrain trajectory geometry and determine inductive bias. The key practical implication—that upstream initializations (faster learning in earlier layers) accelerate rich feature learning in nonlinear networks—offers a concrete, testable lever for improving generalization, reducing sample complexity, and accelerating grokking. Major limitations include the inability to extend the exact analysis to deeper nonlinear networks and the omission of SGD's stochastic effects, which break the conservation laws central to the theory. Open questions include how the distribution of per-neuron conserved quantities at finite width governs feature learning and whether the upstream-initialization principle generalizes across architectures and tasks.
