## Problem

Current AI models for action recognition struggle to generalize from natural RGB videos to biological motion perception (BMP) stimuli such as point-light displays, where only minimal motion information is available. While humans recognize actions from such cues without any training, existing models perform near chance levels, and no systematic large-scale benchmark covering multiple BMP properties exists for evaluating both human and AI performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241701/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes only patch-level optical flows computed from frozen DINO ViT feature maps — not raw RGB pixels or pixel-level optical flows. Videos are downsampled to 32 frames at 224×224 resolution, and flows are estimated at the granularity of feature-map patches (N = H×W) across all T frame pairs, producing a dense flow tensor of shape T×N×2×(T−1). The output is a single action class label from 10 predefined classes (pick up, throw, sit down, stand up, kick, jump up, point, nod/bow, falling down, arm circles).

## Method

MP computes patch-level optical flows by matching DINO feature patches across all frame pairs via normalized pairwise similarities, yielding dense flow sequences relative to every reference frame. Two parallel pathways process these flows: (1) Flow Snapshot Neurons — K=6 learnable slots that use competitive slot attention to bind with and distill prototypical motion patterns from the dense flow sequences, regularized by a contrastive walk loss for slot diversity; and (2) Motion Invariant Neurons — which project each flow into four directional components (+x, −x, +y, −y) and aggregate magnitudes over time to produce features invariant to temporal ordering. Multi-scale fusion combines flow snapshot activations computed at strides 1, 2, 4, and 8 with motion invariant features through self-attention blocks and sinusoidal time embeddings, followed by a fully connected classifier. The overall loss combines cross-entropy for action prediction at multiple levels with the contrastive walk loss (weighted α=10), and the model is trained end-to-end on natural RGB videos with a frozen DINO backbone.

## Results

On the BMP benchmark (62,656 stimuli, 24 conditions), MP achieves 69.00% top-1 accuracy on J-6P Joint videos and 49.68% on SP-8P-1LT videos, outperforming the best baselines (SlowFast at 55.72% and MViT at 15.06%) by 13.28% and 34.62% respectively. On standard CV datasets evaluated on Joint videos, MP reaches 20.38% on NTU RGB+D 60 and 42.83% on NW-UCLA, surpassing the best baselines (ResNet3D at 6.89% and X3D at 16.59%) by 13.49% and 26.24%. On natural RGB videos, MP achieves 96.45%, slightly below the best baselines (R(2+1)D at 99.02%) but competitive overall. The maximum improvement over all existing AI models reaches 29% in top-1 accuracy on the most challenging BMP conditions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MP dramatically outperforms all nine baselines on BMP stimuli: on J-6P Joint videos, MP (69.00%) beats the strongest baseline SlowFast (55.72%) by 13.28% and MViT (52.00%) by 17%; on SP-8P-1LT videos, MP (49.68%) surpasses MViT (15.06%) by 34.62% and SlowFast (20.29%) by 29.39%. On the standard CV datasets tested on Joint videos, MP leads by wide margins: 20.38% vs. 6.89% (ResNet3D) on NTU RGB+D 60 and 42.83% vs. 16.59% (X3D) on NW-UCLA. The additional baselines E2-S-X3D (10.2%), VideoMAE (9.9%), and TwoStream-CNN (15.7%) on J-6P are all far below MP's 69.00%. On natural RGB videos, MP (96.45%) is slightly behind R(2+1)D (99.02%), MViT (99.02%), and I3D (98.74%), but this gap is small and expected since baselines are pre-trained on Kinetics 400 while MP uses only image-pretrained DINO features. The consistent pattern across all comparisons is that MP's advantage grows as visual information decreases, confirming its superior motion-only generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the slot attention mechanism [63] and contrastive walk loss [102] to introduce flow snapshot neurons that capture prototypical motion patterns from patch-level optical flows, drawing conceptual inspiration from neuroscience findings on neurons selective for complex optic flow patterns [33]. It departs from prior optical flow-based action recognition [90, 91, 86, 99] by computing flows at the patch level on DINO/ViT feature maps rather than at the pixel level, and introduces motion invariant neurons that aggregate flow magnitudes along four axes to achieve invariance to temporal ordering. The paper also extends the BMP evaluation paradigm from single-condition psychophysics studies [45, 6, 44] to a systematic multi-property benchmark, positioning itself at the intersection of neuroscience-inspired modeling and computer vision action recognition.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 62,656-stimulus BMP benchmark with human behavioral data across 24 conditions provides the first systematic, quantitative evaluation framework for comparing AI and human biological motion perception, filling a gap where prior work focused on individual stimuli or single properties. The finding that a model trained solely on RGB videos (with no BMP-specific training) can generalize to point-light displays and mirror human behavioral patterns while all Kinetics-pretrained baselines fail dramatically suggests that motion-specific architectural inductive biases matter more than video pre-training scale for this task. Open questions include whether the approach extends to more complex actions, noisy backgrounds, and whether integrating ventral (form) and dorsal (motion) pathway information could close the remaining gap to human performance.
