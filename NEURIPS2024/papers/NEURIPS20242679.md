## Problem

Constrained RLHF for LLM safety alignment typically relies on iterative primal-dual policy optimization, which is computationally expensive (requiring repeated LM retraining for each dual variable update) and suffers from training instability. This paper asks whether constrained alignment can be accomplished in a one-shot manner—training the LM only once with a fixed objective—by exploiting the structure of the dual problem.

## Input / Output

MOCAN takes a reference LM, a prompt dataset, explicit reward and safety models (e.g., beaver-7b), and per-constraint safety margins b_j; PECAN replaces the explicit models with a human-annotated preference dataset containing binary safety and helpfulness comparisons. Both produce a single safety-aligned LM after one supervised training pass, along with the optimal dual variables λ* (a vector of dimension m, one per constraint). The dual optimization stage consumes only offline score tuples (1000 prompts × 128 responses per prompt in experiments), decoupling it from the expensive LM training.

## Method

The key insight is that the dual function of the KL-regularized constrained alignment problem admits a closed form via the Donsker–Varadhan variational formula and is smooth and locally strongly convex, enabling efficient minimization with standard optimizers like PGD over just m scalar variables. The two-stage CAN strategy first optimizes this explicit dual function on offline data to obtain λ*, then reduces the constrained problem to an unconstrained alignment with the modified reward r + ⟨λ*, h⟩. MOCAN instantiates this by constructing pseudo-preferences from the modified reward and training via a DPO-style negative log-likelihood; PECAN avoids explicit reward/safety models entirely by representing them implicitly through log-ratios of pre-aligned LMs (obtained via DPO on safety and helpfulness preferences), so the dual function and pseudo-preferences are expressed purely in terms of log-probability ratios. This eliminates all primal-dual iterations: the LM is trained exactly once, at a cost comparable to standard unconstrained RLHF.

## Results

On the PKU-SafeRLHF-30K dataset, MOCAN achieves an empirically optimal Pareto tradeoff between helpfulness and safety win rates (under GPT-4-turbo evaluation), dominating all baselines including DPO and Safe-RLHF for any given helpfulness level. The dual optimization accurately predicts the empirical safety improvement of aligned LMs, with safety constraints nearly satisfied as expected across a margin grid of b ∈ [-1.4, 5.4]. The optimal dual variable stabilizes with approximately 600 prompts (128 responses per prompt), and the dual function exhibits geometric convergence under PGD with a constant step size of one.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares MOCAN and PECAN against three baselines: SFT (Alpaca-7b-reproduced reference model), DPO (safety-only DPO_S,β and helpfulness-only DPO_H,β), and Safe-RLHF (beaver-7b-v1.0 trained via primal-dual PPO). In the GPT-based evaluation (Figure 3), MOCAN achieves an empirically optimal Pareto tradeoff curve, meaning that for any fixed helpfulness win rate, MOCAN attains the highest safety win rate among all methods including both DPO variants and Safe-RLHF. PECAN, which operates in a preference-based setting without explicit reward/safety models, slightly underperforms MOCAN, attributed to imperfect pre-alignment where log-probability ratios are inaccurate proxies for ground-truth preferences. The model-based MOCAN also shows visible distributional improvement in both safety and helpfulness scores (Figure 2, Middle & Right) compared to the SFT reference, with the dual variable λ=0.75 yielding a clear shift in the safety score distribution.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on DPO (Rafailov et al., 2024) and the theoretical equivalence between preference optimization and RL objectives (Azar et al., 2024) for the policy-update stage, while departing fundamentally from iterative primal-dual constrained RLHF methods (Safe RLHF, C-DPO, stepwise alignment) that alternately update the policy and Lagrange multipliers. The key conceptual departure is a dualization perspective: by exploiting the KL-regularized structure, the authors show the Lagrangian dual admits a closed form (via Donsker–Varadhan) with smoothness and local strong convexity, enabling the dual to be optimized independently on offline data before a single policy update. This reduces constrained alignment to a one-shot unconstrained alignment problem, in contrast to the multi-round primal-dual loops in prior work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The one-shot dualization strategy eliminates the computational and stability costs of iterative primal-dual training, making constrained safety alignment as cheap as standard RLHF in practice. The theoretical guarantees (strong duality, convergence rate for PGD, robustness to proxy model error) provide a principled foundation that prior heuristic primal-dual approaches lack. However, experiments are limited to a single safety constraint and a 7B model, and PECAN underperforms MOCAN due to imperfect pre-alignment; extending to multiple constraints, larger models, and more general preference models (beyond Bradley–Terry) remains open.
