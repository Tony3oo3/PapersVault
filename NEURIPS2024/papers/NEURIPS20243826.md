## Problem

Volume rendering in NeRF requires evaluating color MLPs at hundreds of densely sampled points per ray, making rendering prohibitively slow. Existing acceleration methods (e.g., DONeRF, AutoInt, DIVeR) introduce additional neural networks or data structures that require per-scene retraining, limiting their plug-and-play applicability. The paper asks whether the volume rendering integral itself can be computed with far fewer samples using a purely mathematical reformulation, without any new components or training.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an already-trained NeRF model (or variant such as TensoRF or InstantNGP) along with standard camera parameters (ray origin and direction) to produce rendered novel-view RGB images. No retraining or fine-tuning is required; the pretrained density and color networks are used as-is. The density estimation still requires per-point queries (which can be made cheap via factorized tensors), but the expensive color MLP calls are reduced from 100+ to as few as 4 per ray, yielding 1.2–2× speedup in vanilla NeRF and near real-time WebGL rendering with TensoRF.

## Method

The core insight is a change of variable x(t) = ∫σ(r(s))ds that transforms the volume rendering integral into ∫₀^∞ e^{-x} c(r(t(x)), d) dx — a pure exponentially weighted integral of color, which is exactly the form targeted by the Gauss-Laguerre quadrature. The algorithm walks along the ray in small steps, accumulating the transmittance integral, and places a color sample whenever the accumulated value crosses a precomputed root of the nth-degree Laguerre polynomial (stored in a look-up table). Because the volume density is near zero in empty space and spikes near surfaces, the Laguerre roots naturally concentrate samples near the surface where color contribution is highest, mimicking the behavior of depth-oracle methods without any additional network. The Stone-Weierstrass theorem justifies that the color function can be approximated by polynomials, and the Gauss-Laguerre quadrature then guarantees the highest algebraic precision (2n+1) for that approximation. This makes GL-NeRF a drop-in replacement for the sampling stage in any NeRF-based renderer, reducing color MLP calls from 100+ to 4–32 while preserving rendering quality.

## Results

GL-NeRF reduces the average number of color MLP calls from 118.51 (TensoRF on LLFF) and 31.08 (TensoRF on NeRF-Synthetic) to just 4, while maintaining comparable rendering quality. On LLFF, PSNR drops from 26.51 to 25.63 and SSIM from 0.832 to 0.797; on NeRF-Synthetic, PSNR drops from 32.39 to 30.99 and SSIM from 0.957 to 0.945. When used for training Vanilla NeRF (32 points vs. 100+), PSNR drops from 30.63 to 29.18 on NeRF-Synthetic and from 27.62 to 27.21 on LLFF. Rendering speed improves by 1.2–2× over vanilla NeRF, and a WebGL-based TensoRF+GL-NeRF renderer achieves 22.34 FPS on an AMD Ryzen 9 5900HS CPU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against TensoRF (the primary baseline), GL-NeRF trades a modest quality drop for a dramatic reduction in MLP calls: on LLFF, PSNR decreases by 0.88 dB (26.51→25.63) and SSIM by 0.035 while MLP calls drop from 118.51 to 4; on NeRF-Synthetic, PSNR decreases by 1.40 dB (32.39→30.99) and SSIM by 0.012 with MLP calls dropping from 31.08 to 4. Against Vanilla NeRF (used in the training experiment), GL-NeRF with 32 points achieves PSNR 29.18 vs. 30.63 on NeRF-Synthetic and 27.21 vs. 27.62 on LLFF, a smaller gap than the inference-only setting. Against InstantNGP on the Blender dataset, GL-NeRF (4 MLP calls) scores 30.35 average PSNR versus InstantNGP's 32.05, with per-scene drops ranging from 0.5 dB (Drums) to 2.55 dB (Mat). In all comparisons, GL-NeRF consistently loses on absolute quality metrics but achieves its core goal of reducing computational cost by an order of magnitude without any additional network or data structure.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the volume rendering formulation from NeRF [27] and the classical Gauss quadrature framework, but departs sharply from prior sample-efficient NeRF methods (DONeRF, AutoInt, DIVeR, MC-NeRF, PL-NeRF) that all introduce additional neural networks or data structures to reduce sampling. The key conceptual move is a change of variables that reveals the volume rendering integral is exactly a Gauss-Laguerre quadrature problem, requiring no new components. The plug-and-play property is demonstrated by dropping the method into TensoRF [6] and InstantNGP [28] without retraining, in contrast to prior acceleration works that each require scene-specific optimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free, architecture-agnostic nature of GL-NeRF makes it a practical drop-in for any existing NeRF-based system, which is rare in a field where most acceleration methods demand retraining. However, the quality drop (1–2 dB PSNR) is non-trivial, and the theoretical guarantee of highest algebraic precision does not fully materialize because the color function is only approximately polynomial. An open question is how to close the gap between the quadrature's theoretical precision and the observed degradation, and whether combining GL-NeRF with cheap density representations (e.g., factorized tensors) can push toward true real-time rendering without further quality loss.
