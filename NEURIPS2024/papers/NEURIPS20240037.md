## Problem

Diffusion Transformers (DiT) suffer from quadratic self-attention complexity that becomes a dominant computational bottleneck at high resolutions (e.g., 2K×2K images tokenized into 16k tokens require several seconds per attention call on an A100). Existing efficiency techniques such as Local Attention, Swin, and GQA require costly retraining, leaving a gap for post-training compression methods that can accelerate pre-trained DiTs without additional training data or compute.

## Input / Output

The method operates on pre-trained DiT models (DiT-XL-2-512, PixArt-Sigma-XL at 1024×1024 and 2048×2048, OpenSora for 240p/16-frame video) and takes text prompts or class labels as generation input. The compression plan search takes the model, total denoising steps, a strategy list, and a loss threshold δ as inputs, and outputs a per-layer, per-step dictionary specifying which compression technique (AST, WA-RS, ASC, or WA-RS+ASC) to apply. The final output is the generated image or video, with the compression transparently reducing attention FLOPs by up to 76% while preserving FID/IS/CLIP quality.

## Method

DiTFastAttn identifies three redundancies in DiT attention and proposes corresponding post-training techniques: (1) Window Attention with Residual Sharing (WA-RS) replaces full attention with fixed-size window attention in spatially-local layers, then caches the residual between full and window attention outputs at one step and reuses it across subsequent steps to avoid performance loss from discarded long-range dependencies; (2) Attention Sharing across Timesteps (AST) caches the attention output at one step and reuses it for temporally similar neighboring steps, skipping redundant computation; (3) Attention Sharing across CFG (ASC) reuses the conditional attention output for the unconditional pass in classifier-free guidance, halving attention cost for those layers. A greedy search algorithm (Algorithm 1) selects the optimal technique per layer and per step by evaluating each candidate strategy's output loss against a layer-indexed threshold, ensuring the compression plan is tailored to the specific redundancy distribution of each model.

## Results

On PixArt-Sigma-XL-2048×2048, DiTFastAttn at D6 reduces attention FLOPs by 76% (to 24% of raw) while maintaining FID of 49.34 (vs. 51.89 raw), IS of 22.38 (vs. 23.67), and CLIP score of 31.28 (vs. 31.47), achieving up to 1.8× end-to-end speedup. On DiT-XL-2-512×512, D6 compresses attention to 34% of raw FLOPs with FID 16.80 (vs. 25.43) and IS 352.20 (vs. 408.16). For video generation with OpenSora at 240p/16 frames, the method achieves 1.02×–1.15× speedup with 11.68%–55.76% attention FLOPs reduction. At lower compression settings (D1–D3), generative quality nearly matches the raw model across all three image models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not compare against other compression or acceleration methods; instead, it benchmarks the compressed model against the raw (uncompressed) model at each resolution. At mild compression (D1–D3), DiTFastAttn matches or even slightly exceeds the raw model on IS (e.g., DiT-XL-2 D1: IS 412.24 vs. 408.16 raw; PixArt-Sigma-1024 D1: FID 55.73 vs. 55.65 raw), confirming near-lossless compression. At aggressive compression (D6), quality degrades noticeably on DiT-XL-2 (FID 16.80 vs. 25.43, IS 352.20 vs. 408.16) but remains relatively stable on PixArt-Sigma-2K (FID 49.34 vs. 51.89, CLIP 31.28 vs. 31.47), indicating that higher-resolution models tolerate more compression. The ablation in Fig. 9 further shows that DiTFastAttn outperforms any single technique (AST, WA-RS, or ASC alone) at the same FLOPs budget, and that the residual-sharing component of WA-RS is essential—plain window attention causes significant quality drops at equivalent compression ratios.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of local attention mechanisms (Swin Transformer, Longformer, BigBird), attention sharing (PSViT, GQA), and diffusion model acceleration (DeepCache, TGATE), but departs from all of them by being a training-free, post-training compression method targeting the self-attention module specifically in diffusion transformers. While prior local attention work requires architectural changes and retraining, and DeepCache/TGATE target U-Net features or cross-attention in text-to-image models, DiTFastAttn repurposes window attention, temporal caching, and CFG symmetry as orthogonal, composable techniques applied to a frozen DiT. The implementation builds on FlashAttention-2, and the greedy plan-search algorithm is a practical addition not seen in prior attention-sharing work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: up to 76% attention FLOPs reduction and 1.8× end-to-end speedup at 2K resolution with minimal quality degradation (FID within ~2 points) makes high-resolution DiT inference substantially more accessible. The observation that compression benefits scale with resolution (quadratic attention redundancy grows with token count) suggests the method becomes increasingly valuable as models push toward 4K and video generation. Key limitations include the post-training-only constraint (no training-time adaptation), additional VRAM from caching attention states, and the fact that only the attention module is compressed while FFN and other components remain unchanged. Open questions include whether training-aware variants could push compression further, whether the greedy plan search can be replaced by learned policies, and how the method interacts with emerging architectural changes like linear attention or state-space models in diffusion transformers.
