## Problem

Self-supervised real-world image denoising requires relating distant pixels to overcome spatial noise correlation, but existing transformer-based blind-spot networks (LG-BSN, SS-BSN, SwinIA) either fail to properly enforce blind-spot constraints or do not fully exploit long-range dependencies, resulting in performance that does not surpass recent CNN-based approaches. The paper asks how to natively embed a blind-spot mechanism into a transformer's self-attention while preserving its long-range interaction capability.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240442/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single noisy RGB image (no clean reference or paired data required) and outputs a denoised image of the same spatial dimensions. Training uses only the noisy images from SIDD-Medium (320 images), with patches of 24542 samples. At inference, only the single-branch SelfFormer-F is deployed, operating on 256×256 patches with a grid size of image_size/16, yielding a 10.8M-parameter model with ~1.8s inference time per image on an A6000 GPU.

## Method

SelfFormer introduces Directional Self-Attention (DSA), which constrains each token's attention window to a half-plane grid excluding the token itself, so that information broadcast by a token never returns to it across multiple layers—creating a built-in blind-spot without input masking. Four DSA branches (left, right, up, down) with shared weights cover all spatial directions, while a grid-based acceleration scheme reduces computational cost. A pseudo-Siamese architecture pairs SelfFormer-D (four DSA branches) with SelfFormer-F (one full-grid SA branch); both share identical learnable weights except the final 1×1 convolution, and are jointly trained with a self-reconstruction loss on SelfFormer-D plus a mutual L1 loss between the two sub-networks. Channel attention (NAFBlock) is included with a blind-spot variant (BSCA) using directional convolutions. At inference, only the faster single-branch SelfFormer-F is used, leveraging its unconstrained full attention grid for superior long-range feature exploitation.

## Results

SelfFormer achieves the best PSNR/SSIM among all clean-image-free methods on all three benchmark datasets: 37.69 dB / 0.937 on SIDD-Benchmark, 38.92 dB / 0.943 on DND, and 34.43 dB / 0.857 (ISO 3200) and 33.55 dB / 0.847 (ISO 5000) on NIND. The closest competitor, PUCA (a CNN-based self-supervised method), scores 37.54 / 0.936 on SIDD and 38.83 / 0.942 on DND. In terms of efficiency, SelfFormer-F has 10.8M parameters and 1812 ms inference time for a 256×256 image, substantially faster than other transformer-based self-supervised denoisers (LG-BPN: 5208 ms, SS-BSN: 3976 ms).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SelfFormer outperforms all 12 self-supervised baselines and all non-learning/zero-shot methods on every reported metric. Against the strongest CNN-based self-supervised competitor PUCA [30], SelfFormer gains +0.15 dB PSNR on SIDD (37.69 vs 37.54), +0.09 dB on DND (38.92 vs 38.83), +0.19 dB on NIND ISO 3200, and +0.06 dB on NIND ISO 5000. Against the transformer-based self-supervised methods, the margins are larger: +0.41 dB over LG-BPN [28] and +0.27 dB over SS-BSN [29] on SIDD, and +0.49 dB over LG-BPN on DND. On NIND ISO 3200, SelfFormer (34.43) narrowly edges out AP-BSN [27] (34.41) by 0.02 dB, while on ISO 5000 it leads AP-BSN by 0.06 dB. The paper also reports that SelfFormer-F is roughly 2.9× faster at inference than LG-BPN (1812 ms vs 5208 ms) and 2.2× faster than SS-BSN (1812 ms vs 3976 ms) for a 256×256 image, while using fewer parameters than PUCA (10.8M vs 12.8M).

## Relation to Prior Work

SelfFormer sits at the intersection of Blind-Spot Networks (BSNs) and transformer-based denoising, building directly on Laine et al.'s directional convolution [23] for the blind-spot mechanism and NAFNet's channel attention [11] for feature recalibration. It departs from prior transformer-BSN hybrids (LG-BSN, SS-BSN, SwinIA) by embedding the blind-spot constraint natively into the attention geometry via a half-plane grid (DSA) rather than masking input pixels or the attention diagonal, and introduces a pseudo-Siamese pairing between the directional (blind-spot) and full-grid (non-blind-spot) variants for mutual regularization. This contrasts with masking-based BSNs (Noise2Void, Noise2Self) that discard pixel information, and with simple SA+BSN combinations that may compromise the blind-spot property.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Self-supervised denoising remains critical for domains where clean references are unavailable (medical, scientific imaging), and this paper demonstrates that transformer long-range attention can be harnessed to reduce noise correlation more effectively than CNN-based BSNs. However, the gains over the previous SOTA (PUCA) are modest (~0.15 dB on SIDD), a significant gap to supervised methods persists, and training memory is substantially higher than CNN baselines due to the four-branch DSA structure. Open questions include whether the pseudo-Siamese mutual-learning paradigm generalizes beyond denoising, and whether more efficient attention formulations can close the compute gap with CNNs.
