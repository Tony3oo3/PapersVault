## Problem

Existing publicly available video datasets (WebVid-10M, Panda-70M, HD-VILA-100M) are inadequate for Sora-like video generation because they contain short clips (5–18 s), low motion intensity, and brief captions (12–30 words) that cannot support long-duration generation with fine-grained textual control. Additionally, prior benchmarks lack comprehensive evaluation of 3D consistency and tracking-based motion strength in generated videos.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241550/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

MiraDiT consumes text prompts encoded by Flan-T5-XXL (up to 512 tokens, supporting short, dense, or structured captions) plus a scalar FPS condition (range 5–30) injected via AdaLN modulation, and produces videos at 384×240 resolution with 5 s length for benchmarking. The underlying MiraData dataset provides 720p clips averaging 72.1 s with six caption types totaling ~318 words per video. An optional RIFE post-processing step applies 4× frame interpolation to raise output to 24 fps.

## Method

The paper contributes three components. (1) MiraData is built via a five-stage pipeline: manual channel selection across YouTube/Videvo/Pixabay/Pexels, two-stage splitting-and-stitching using Qwen-VL-Chat, LLaVA, ImageBind, and DINOv2 to preserve semantic coherence, multi-criteria filtering (color, Laion-Aesthetic score, RAFT optical flow, NSFW), and GPT-4V captioning over 8-frame 2×4 grids to produce hierarchical structured captions (short, dense, main-object, background, camera, style). (2) MiraBench introduces 17 metrics across six axes, notably a CoTracker-based tracking-strength metric that captures long-range displacement (unlike instantaneous optical flow) and 3D-consistency MAE/RMSE from geometric reconstruction. (3) MiraDiT is a DiT-based generator with separate spatial and temporal self-attention layers (spatial layers initialized from PixArt-α), a hybrid 2D-encoder/3D-decoder VAE following SVD, FPS-conditioned AdaLN for motion-strength control at inference, and a Frame-n'-Pack strategy with temporal masking to support variable-length training.

## Results

On MiraBench (384×240, 5s), MiraDiT trained on MiraData achieves the highest motion strength among all compared methods: Dynamic Degree of 15.46 and Tracking Strength of 49.47, roughly 2× and 3× those of OpenSora (7.65 / 16.07) and VideoCrafter2 (1.71 / 6.72). It also leads on temporal consistency (DINO TC 43.78, CLIP TC 45.95, TMS 47.24) and ranks second on 3D consistency (MAE 85.27, RMSE 11.74) behind OpenSora. On visual quality, VideoCrafter2 retains the edge (AQ 58.69 vs. 49.90; IQ 64.96 vs. 63.71), while MiraDiT(MiraData) is competitive on text-video alignment (Overall Alignment 16.66 vs. 16.90 for VideoCrafter2).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MiraDiT trained on MiraData decisively outperforms both OpenSora (DiT-based) and VideoCrafter2 (U-Net-based) on motion strength: Dynamic Degree 15.46 vs. 7.65 (OpenSora) and 1.71 (VideoCrafter2), and Tracking Strength 49.47 vs. 16.07 and 6.72 respectively. It also leads on all three temporal-consistency metrics (DINO TC, CLIP TC, TMS) by a wide margin over both baselines. On 3D consistency, OpenSora retains a slight edge (MAE 75.45 vs. 85.27; RMSE 10.39 vs. 11.74), while MiraDiT(MiraData) clearly beats VideoCrafter2. For visual quality, VideoCrafter2 is strongest (AQ 58.69, IQ 64.96), with MiraDiT(MiraData) in second place (AQ 49.90, IQ 63.71) and OpenSora third. On text-video alignment, OpenSora leads on Main Object Alignment (18.12 vs. 14.67) and VideoCrafter2 on Overall Alignment (16.90 vs. 16.66), but MiraDiT(MiraData) wins on Camera, Background, and Style alignment. In the human evaluation (Table 4), MiraDiT(MiraData) receives the best rank on motion strength (1.3), 3D consistency (2.1), and text alignment (2.1), confirming the automatic-metric trends.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Diffusion Transformer (DiT) architecture and Stable Video Diffusion's hybrid VAE for its generation backbone (MiraDiT), while drawing on W.A.L.T's spatial query conditioning for cross-attention. Its primary contribution, however, is a dataset (MiraData) that departs from prior video-text datasets (WebVid-10M, Panda-70M, HD-VILA-100M) by targeting long-duration clips (avg. 72.1s vs. 5–18s), high motion intensity, and structured multi-perspective captions generated via GPT-4V rather than short alt-text or single-pass generated captions. The benchmark (MiraBench) extends existing evaluation suites like VBench by introducing tracking-based motion strength (via CoTracker) and 3D consistency metrics (MAE/RMSE from 3D reconstruction), addressing gaps the authors identify in prior benchmarks' treatment of motion and geometric coherence.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MiraData and MiraBench address a concrete bottleneck for the field: existing open-source video datasets are too short, too static, and too sparsely captioned to train Sora-class long-video generators, and prior benchmarks under-evaluate motion and 3D consistency. The finding that a 16K-hour dataset with careful curation and structured captions substantially outperforms 100M+ clip datasets in motion strength suggests data quality and temporal length may matter more than raw scale for video generation. Open questions include whether the structured caption hierarchy (short → dense → structured) generalizes beyond GPT-4V-generated annotations, whether the FPS-conditioned modulation for motion control transfers to other architectures, and whether the relatively small dataset size limits diversity coverage for truly open-domain generation.
