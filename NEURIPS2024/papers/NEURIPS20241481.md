## Problem

Gradient-based discrete samplers such as DMALA and Any-scale Balanced (AB) are highly effective at characterizing individual modes but become trapped in local modes when the target distribution is multimodal, failing to capture the full distribution. While continuous-space MCMC has well-developed strategies for multimodal exploration (cyclical step sizes, parallel tempering, flat histograms), no analogous methodology exists for gradient-based discrete samplers, which face an even more severe trapping problem due to the inherent discontinuities of discrete spaces.

## Input / Output

The method requires an energy function U(θ) defined over a coordinate-wise factorized discrete domain Θ = Πᵢ Θᵢ that can be extended to a differentiable function on ℝᵈ, and produces samples from the target distribution π(θ) ∝ exp(U(θ)). For EBM learning, the input is a parameterized energy network E_φ trained via Persistent Contrastive Divergence, producing trained model parameters evaluated by AIS log-likelihood. For text infilling, the input is a sentence with 50% of words masked, and the output is a completed sentence sampled from a RoBERTa-based discrete distribution.

## Method

The Automatic Cyclical Sampler (ACS) parameterizes a family of gradient-based proposals Q_{α,β} using a Gaussian kernel K_α (step size) and a power balancing function g(t)=t^β (balancing parameter), where α→0, β=0.5 gives a locally-balanced proposal and α→∞, β=1 gives a globally-balanced proposal. A cosine-based cyclical step size schedule alternates between large exploratory steps and small exploitative steps, while a coupled cyclical β schedule ensures each step size maintains a high Metropolis-Hastings acceptance rate by shifting β toward 1 for large α and toward 0.5 for small α. An automatic tuning algorithm estimates α_max, α_min, and the full β schedule by targeting a user-specified acceptance rate ρ*, using an adaptive search that starts from α_ceil and decreases (or from α_floor and increases) to handle the non-monotonic acceptance-rate-vs-step-size relationship observed in discrete spaces. The paper further proves the first non-asymptotic geometric convergence bound for gradient-based discrete sampling, showing ‖Pⁿ − π‖_TV ≤ (1−ε)^n under M-Lipschitz gradient and local strong concavity assumptions.

## Results

On deep convolutional EBM learning (Table 1), ACS achieves the best log-likelihood across all four datasets: -79.905 on Static MNIST, -79.634 on Dynamic MNIST, -91.487 on Omniglot, and -89.262 on Caltech Silhouettes, outperforming DMALA by up to 7.76 nats (Omniglot) and GWG by up to 6.94 nats (Caltech). On RBM learning (Table 4, Appendix D.4), ACS achieves -249.55 on MNIST, -304.96 on eMNIST, -407.39 on kMNIST, -220.71 on Omniglot, and -396.04 on Caltech, consistently outperforming both DMALA and GWG. For text infilling (Table 2), ACS generates more diverse sentences with lower Self-BLEU (36.70 vs 41.83 on Grimm; 32.42 vs 37.47 on SST2) and higher unique n-gram ratios, though at the cost of higher perplexity (369.44 vs 280.82 on Grimm).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DMALA (Zhang et al., 2022b), ACS wins on EBM learning across all four datasets, with the largest margin on Omniglot (-91.487 vs -99.243, a 7.76 nat improvement) and Caltech (-89.262 vs -98.001, 8.74 nats). ACS also outperforms DMALA on RBM learning across all five datasets (e.g., -249.55 vs -278.35 on MNIST) and on text-infilling diversity metrics (lower Self-BLEU, higher unique n-grams), though DMALA achieves lower perplexity. Against GWG (Grathwohl et al., 2021), ACS achieves better EBM log-likelihood on all four datasets and better RBM log-likelihood on all five, with the gap widening on more complex distributions (Caltech: -89.262 vs -96.20 for EBM; -396.04 vs -827.45 for RBM). Against the Any-scale Balanced sampler (Sun et al., 2023a), ACS produces visually sharper mode characterizations in the 2D multimodal toy experiment (Figure 1) and competitive convergence on RBM/EBM sampling (Figure 3), though AB is not included in the learning or text-infilling comparisons. Overall, ACS consistently outperforms all three baselines on the primary learning metrics while matching or exceeding them on sampling quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the gradient-based discrete sampling lineage (Zanella's informed proposals, DMALA's discrete Langevin, and the any-scale balanced sampler) by introducing the first method explicitly designed for multimodal discrete distributions. It adapts the cyclical step size schedule from continuous-space MCMC (Zhang et al., 2020) to the discrete setting and, crucially, adds a novel cyclical balancing parameter schedule that has no direct analogue in prior discrete or continuous work. The automatic tuning algorithm departs from the any-scale sampler's approach by targeting a specific Metropolis-Hastings acceptance rate and exploiting the discrete structure, rather than maximizing average coordinates changed per step.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The non-asymptotic convergence and CLT guarantees are the first of their kind for gradient-based discrete samplers, providing a theoretical foundation that was previously absent (prior work only had asymptotic or relative convergence results). Practically, the method offers a drop-in replacement for existing gradient-based discrete samplers with minimal tuning overhead, applicable to RBM/EBM learning and LLM text infilling. Key limitations include the requirement that the energy function be twice differentiable and locally strongly concave, and the proof does not cover the specific automatic tuning algorithm, leaving a gap between theory and practice. Open questions include whether the cyclical scheduling approach scales to very high-dimensional discrete spaces (e.g., full vocabulary LLMs) and whether the theoretical assumptions hold for modern deep energy-based models.
