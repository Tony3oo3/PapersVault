## Problem

Existing work has established uniform stability upper bounds for gradient-based bilevel hyperparameter optimization algorithms, but whether these bounds are tight remains an open question. This paper addresses this gap by establishing the first stability lower bounds for both unrolling-differentiation (UD)-based and implicit-function-theorem (IFT)-based algorithms, revealing that uniform stability has reached its limit in characterizing the generalization of the UD-based algorithm.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240486/cropped_page5_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The analysis takes twin validation sets (differing in a single data point), a training set, bilevel loss functions, and a step size schedule, and produces stability lower bounds expressed as functions of outer steps T, inner steps K, and validation set size m. The bounds are derived by constructing a specific quadratic loss example with an indefinite symmetric matrix rather than analyzing a particular trained model, making the results worst-case and distribution-free. The output includes both recursive-form bounds (matching the upper bound structure term-by-term) and deformed polynomial bounds in T under decreasing step sizes.

## Method

The paper introduces lower-bounded expansion properties (σ-divergent update rules, ρ-growing update rules, and μ-expansive loss functions) as general tools for characterizing guaranteed divergence in iterative algorithms, mirroring the upper-bounded properties of Hardt et al. but focused on lower bounds. These properties are applied at two levels: at the outer level they yield a recursive stability lower bound (Theorem 5.1) that structurally mirrors the existing upper bound, while at the inner level they provide a lower bound on the Lipschitz constant of the inner solution θ_K(λ) (Theorem 5.2), which controls the hypergradient magnitude. The central technical challenge—resolving the dependency of each outer update on the concurrent inner optimization stage—is overcome by constructing a quadratic example ℓ = ½θᵀAθ + λᵀθ − yxᵀθ with an indefinite matrix A, ensuring all expansion properties hold simultaneously at both levels. This yields tight lower bounds matching the upper bounds of Bao et al. (2021) for the UD-based algorithm with constant step sizes, and nearly tight bounds with linearly decreasing step sizes, with the small residual gap attributable to the log vs. linear scaling in the exponent.

## Results

The paper establishes that the uniform argument stability lower bound for the UD-based algorithm matches the existing upper bound [17] in order, proving tightness. For constant outer step sizes, ε_arg ≍ (1+c(1−1/m)γ)^T / m where γ ≍ (1+ηγ^tr)^{2K}. For decreasing step sizes α_t = c/t, the deformed bound is T^{ln(1+(1−1/m)cγ')}/m ≲ ε_arg ≲ T^{(1−1/m)cγ}/m, with γ = γ' ≍ (1+ηγ^tr)^{2K} and L' ≍ (1+ηγ^tr)^K. For the IFT-based algorithm, the lower bound has γ' ≍ (1+ηγ^tr)^{2K} while the upper bound has γ ≲ K(1+ηγ^tr)^{2K}, leaving a factor-of-K gap.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is with Bao et al. [17, NeurIPS 2021], which established the first stability upper bounds for UD-based HO algorithms. The paper's lower bounds match [17]'s upper bounds exactly in order for the UD-based algorithm (both have γ ≍ (1+ηγ^tr)^{2K} and L ≍ (1+ηγ^tr)^K), confirming that uniform stability has reached its limit in characterizing generalization for this algorithm. For the IFT-based algorithm, the paper provides the first lower bounds, but a factor-of-K gap remains between the lower bound (γ' ≍ (1+ηγ^tr)^{2K}) and the upper bound (γ ≲ K(1+ηγ^tr)^{2K}), leaving tightness open. The paper also notes that its upper bound for the UD-based algorithm (without the bounded loss assumption) is not contradictory to [17]'s deformed bound T^{(1−1/m)γc/((1−1/m)γc+1)}/m, as the bounded loss assumption was removed for fair comparison.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the stability analysis framework for bilevel hyperparameter optimization initiated by Bao et al. (NeurIPS 2021), who established the first uniform stability upper bounds for UD-based algorithms. The key methodological departure is the introduction of lower-bounded expansion properties (σ-divergent, ρ-growing, μ-expansive) that mirror the upper-bounded counterparts from Hardt et al. (ICML 2016) but are oriented toward proving instability rather than bounding it. The construction-based lower bound technique follows the approach of Bassily et al. (NeurIPS 2020) and Zhang et al. (UAI 2022) for single-level SGD, but is adapted to the significantly more complex bilevel setting where each outer update depends on the concurrent inner optimization state. The paper also extends the analysis to IFT-based algorithms (Lorraine et al., AISTATS 2020), for which no prior stability bounds existed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central result—that the existing upper bound for UD-based algorithms is tight—closes an important open question and implies that uniform stability cannot yield sharper generalization guarantees for this class of algorithms, redirecting future theoretical effort toward alternative notions (e.g., on-average or local stability). The lower-bounded expansion properties are positioned as general tools applicable beyond bilevel optimization, potentially enabling tightness analysis for other iterative algorithms. However, the analysis is restricted to smooth losses and a single quadratic example, and the paper explicitly acknowledges that a stability lower bound does not directly imply a generalization lower bound, leaving a gap between the theoretical tightness result and practical generalization guarantees. Non-smooth scenarios and more complex loss landscapes remain open.
