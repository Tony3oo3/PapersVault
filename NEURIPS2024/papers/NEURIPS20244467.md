## Problem

Current state-of-the-art synchrony-based models for unsupervised object discovery use complex-valued activations but real-valued weights, requiring additional inductive biases (gating mechanisms, contrastive losses, or depth masks) to implement binding. These models also systematically exploit color as a shortcut feature, failing to separate similarly colored objects without extra supervision. The paper asks whether a recurrent architecture with complex-valued weights can implement binding purely through the algebra of complex multiplication and addition, eliminating these auxiliary mechanisms.

## Input / Output

The model takes RGB images (35×35, 64×64, or 96×96 depending on dataset) with non-negative pixel values as input. At each iteration, the magnitude component is clamped to the input image while the phase component is fed back from the previous iteration's output, starting from a random von-Mises-initialized phase map. Outputs include the reconstructed image (magnitude), continuous phase maps at intermediate layers, and discrete per-pixel object segmentation masks extracted by k-Means clustering of unit-magnitude latent-level phase vectors.

## Method

SynCx is a fully convolutional autoencoder where every layer uses complex-valued weights to process complex-valued activations, with modReLU applied only to the magnitude component. The core mechanism is iterative constraint satisfaction: the autoencoder is applied N times (3–4 iterations), with the output phase map at iteration n fed as the input phase at iteration n+1 while the image magnitude remains clamped, allowing spatially local grouping constraints to propagate across the image. Binding is implemented purely by the matrix-vector product between complex weights and activations—constructive and destructive interference of phases encodes which features belong to the same object—eliminating the need for χ-binding gates, cosine-distance binding, or contrastive training. A spatial bottleneck (strided convolutions reducing resolution) forces the model to compress feature relationships into phase components, driving phase specialization toward object boundaries. Training minimizes the average pixel-wise L2 reconstruction loss across all iterations with a simple Adam optimizer and no auxiliary losses.

## Results

On Tetrominoes, SynCx achieves an ARI of 0.89±0.01, outperforming all synchrony-based baselines (CtCAE 0.84, CAE++ 0.78, RF 0.42). On dSprites, SynCx reaches 0.82±0.01 ARI, closely matching RF (0.84±0.03) while substantially exceeding CAE++ (0.51) and CtCAE (0.56). On CLEVR, SynCx scores 0.59±0.03, competitive with RF (0.65±0.01) and well above CtCAE (0.54) and CAE++ (0.27). SynCx uses only ~1M parameters (6–23× fewer than baselines) and trains in 1h35m on Tetrominoes vs. 7h20m for RF.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SynCx outperforms all four synchrony-based baselines on Tetrominoes, achieving 0.89 ARI versus CtCAE's 0.84, CAE++'s 0.78, and RF's 0.42. On dSprites, SynCx (0.82) is within 0.02 of RF (0.84) but exceeds CAE++ (0.51) and CtCAE (0.56) by over 0.25 ARI. On CLEVR, SynCx (0.59) trails RF (0.65) by 0.06 but surpasses CtCAE (0.54) and CAE++ (0.27) substantially. A key qualitative advantage is that SynCx separates similarly colored objects without additional supervision, whereas RF systematically groups them together; on grayscale CLEVR, SynCx (0.45 ARI) more than doubles RF's performance (0.22), revealing RF's heavy reliance on color cues. SlotAttention remains the strongest overall method (0.91–0.99 ARI), but SynCx narrows the gap to slot-based approaches while using 6–23× fewer parameters and no contrastive losses, gating mechanisms, or depth masks.

## Relation to Prior Work

SynCx returns to the original iterative constraint-satisfaction intuition of Mozer et al. (1992) for synchrony-based binding, which was abandoned in favor of purely feedforward architectures in recent state-of-the-art models (CAE, CtCAE, RF). The key architectural departure is the use of complex-valued weights (rather than real-valued weights with weight-sharing across real/imaginary components) combined with a recurrent, stateful processing loop, which mechanistically implements binding through matrix-vector products alone. This eliminates the need for the additional inductive biases—χ-binding gates, cosine-distance binding, or contrastive losses—that prior synchrony-based models required to achieve comparable grouping performance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a conceptually simpler model (no gates, no contrastive training, 6–23× fewer parameters) can match or exceed more complex synchrony-based baselines on synthetic benchmarks, and is notably more robust to color shortcuts (grayscale CLEVR: 0.45 ARI vs. RF's 0.22). However, a significant gap to slot-based methods like SlotAttention remains (0.59 vs. 0.99 ARI on CLEVR), and the approach has not been validated on naturalistic or large-scale multi-object benchmarks. Open questions include whether the iterative phase-synchronization mechanism scales to more complex scenes, how to improve the k-Means-based object extraction to handle non-Gaussian phase distributions, and whether temporal-difference-style losses or spatial priors can close the remaining performance gap.
