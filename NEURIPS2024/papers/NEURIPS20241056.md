## Problem

State-of-the-art audio foundation models (e.g., LAION-CLAP) are trained exclusively on non-spatial audio-text pairs and cannot capture spatial attributes like direction, distance, or room acoustics. Conversely, sound event localization models (e.g., SeldNET, PILOT) localize sources to absolute positions but are restricted to fixed class vocabularies and cannot generalize to open-vocabulary natural language descriptions of spatial scenes. No prior model jointly represents both the semantics and spatial attributes of audio in a shared embedding space aligned with natural language.

## Input / Output

The model ingests first-order ambisonics (FOA) spatial audio (4 channels) or mono non-spatial audio (replicated across all 4 channels), which are converted to log-mel spectrograms (omni channel only) and active/reactive intensity vectors. Text captions are tokenized via BPE and processed by RoBERTa-base. Both modalities are projected to a shared 512-dimensional embedding space. Downstream, this embedding supports semantic retrieval, 3D source localization (azimuth, elevation, distance), zero-shot spatial attribute classification, and spatial audio caption generation via a GPT-2 decoder.

## Method

ELSA uses a two-branch audio encoder: an HTSAT transformer (30M params) processes the omni-channel mel-spectrogram for semantics, while a two-branched CNN (486k params) processes active and reactive intensity vectors for spatial attributes; their outputs (768-dim + 192-dim) are concatenated and projected to 512-dim via an MLP. The text encoder is RoBERTa-base (125M params) also projected to 512-dim. Training combines the standard CLIP/InfoNCE contrastive loss with three auxiliary spatial regression heads (cosine loss for direction of arrival, MSE for distance and room floor area), leveraging the parametric ground-truth labels from the room simulator. The training corpus is synthesized by spatially augmenting Clotho, AudioCaps, and Freesound audio in 8,972 parametric rooms and rewriting captions with LLaMA-13B to include spatial descriptors (direction, distance, elevation, room size, reverberation). A key design choice is using intensity vectors rather than raw ambisonic channels, making the spatial representation device-agnostic and naturally handling non-spatial audio (where IVs become identical across channels).

## Results

ELSA achieves 44.2 mAP@10 on AudioCaps semantic retrieval, outperforming LAION-CLAP (43.8) by +0.4 absolute (+2.8% relative), while simultaneously achieving 14.97° mean-absolute-error on 3D source localization on the TUT Sound Events 2018 REAL benchmark. This 3D localization result improves over SeldNET (26.6°) by 11.6° and is within 2.6° of the Spatial LibriSpeech baseline (12.4°), though it trails the specialized PILOT model (4.2°). On non-spatial retrieval (Table 3), ELSA matches the locally-trained CLAP baseline (e.g., 33.2 vs 32.7 R@1 text-to-audio on AudioCaps), confirming that spatial training does not degrade semantic capabilities.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against LAION-CLAP, ELSA wins on both metrics: it improves AudioCaps mAP@10 from 43.8 to 44.2 and reduces 3D localization error from 95.29° (near-random) to 14.97°, demonstrating that LAION-CLAP simply cannot encode spatial attributes. Against SeldNET, ELSA improves 3D localization by 11.6° (14.97° vs 26.6°) while also supporting open-vocabulary semantic retrieval that SeldNET lacks entirely. Against Spatial LibriSpeech, ELSA is slightly worse on 3D localization (14.97° vs 12.4°, a 2.6° gap) but uniquely provides open-vocabulary text alignment. Against PILOT, ELSA underperforms on 3D localization (14.97° vs 4.2°), though PILOT was specifically tuned for that single task on TUT-derived data and offers no semantic or language capabilities. On non-spatial retrieval (Table 3), ELSA is essentially on par with the locally-trained CLAP baseline (e.g., 33.2 vs 32.7 R@1 text-to-audio on AudioCaps), confirming that the addition of spatial training does not sacrifice semantic performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

ELSA extends the LAION-CLAP contrastive audio-text architecture by adding a spatial attributes branch (a two-branched CNN derived from Spatial LibriSpeech) that processes intensity vectors from first-order ambisonics, and by augmenting training data with simulated spatial audio and LLM-rewritten spatial captions. It departs from prior audio foundation models (CLAP, Pengi, LTU, SALMONN) that handle only non-spatial audio and from SELD models (SeldNET, PILOT) that localize sources but are limited to fixed vocabularies, by unifying open-vocabulary language understanding with spatial audio in a single contrastive representation. The spatial augmentation pipeline—simulating rooms parametrically and using LLaMA-13B to rephrase captions with spatial descriptors—is a novel data construction approach not present in prior audio-language work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ELSA is the first model to jointly represent spatial and semantic attributes of audio in a shared embedding space aligned with natural language, enabling tasks like spatially-aware retrieval, zero-shot spatial classification, and direction swapping via vector arithmetic. Key limitations include reliance on synthetic room simulations (with a notable real-world transfer gap, e.g., 35.8% direction accuracy), restriction to first-order ambisonics, and LLM caption hallucinations that may corrupt training signals. Open questions include whether the structured embedding space generalizes to higher-order ambisonics, moving or overlapping sources, and whether the linear direction-swapping property persists with more diverse spatial training data.
