## Problem

Training-free loss-based diffusion guidance (e.g., FreeDoM, UG) uses pretrained networks on clean images to steer diffusion without any additional training, yet its successes and failures remain theoretically unexplained. This paper identifies two fundamental limitations: training-free guidance is more susceptible to misaligned (adversarial) gradients and exhibits slower ODE convergence than classifier-based guidance, both stemming from the reduced smoothness of off-the-shelf networks compared to noise-trained classifiers.

## Input / Output

At each reverse diffusion step, the method consumes the current noisy state x_t, a condition y (text prompt, segmentation map, facial sketch, target/obstacle coordinates), and a frozen pretrained guidance network f_φ (e.g., ResNet-50, CLIP, BiSeNet). The guidance network is applied to the Tweedie-estimated clean image x̂₀ = (x_t − σ_t·ε_θ(x_t,t))/√α_t rather than to x_t directly, and the resulting gradient is backpropagated through both the guidance network and the diffusion backbone. The final output is a fully denoised image (256×256 for CelebA-HQ/ImageNet) or a joint-coordinate motion sequence, guided to satisfy the specified condition.

## Method

The paper provides an optimization-theoretic analysis showing that training-free guidance monotonically decreases the guidance loss under PL and Lipschitz conditions, with a step size η ∝ √α_t that explains the empirically chosen guidance weights. It then proves that adding Gaussian noise (as in time-dependent classifiers) improves Lipschitz smoothness, making off-the-shelf networks more vulnerable to misaligned gradients and slower ODE convergence (error O((1+L^M)/M) in M DDIM steps). To address these issues, the method introduces two plug-and-play techniques: (1) random augmentation, which applies |T| differentiable transformations (translation, resize, color, cutout) to the Tweedie estimate and averages the resulting gradients to implicitly smooth the guidance landscape; and (2) Polyak step size, which scales each guidance update by ‖ε_θ(x_t,t)‖/‖g_t‖² to adaptively balance the diffusion and guidance terms and accelerate convergence. Both are built on top of FreeDoM and validated on CelebA-HQ, ImageNet, and human motion diffusion.

## Results

On CelebA-HQ, the proposed method (FreeDoM + Polyak step size + random augmentation) achieves the best Distance and FID across all three zero-shot guidance types: segmentation (1575.7 Distance, 33.31 FID), sketch (30.41 Distance, 41.26 FID), and text (10.72 Distance, 41.25 FID). On unconditional ImageNet with CLIP text guidance, the method reaches a CLIP score of 27.7 (measured by CLIP-L/14), outperforming the strongest baseline FreeDoM (25.9) by 1.8 points. On human motion diffusion (MDM), the method reduces targeting loss by up to 97% (e.g., 0.43 vs. 144.84 for unconditional on 'Jogging') and object avoidance loss by up to 99% (e.g., 1.15 vs. 7.83 for FreeDoM on 'Jogging') while maintaining competitive MotionCLIP scores.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against four training-free baselines (UG, LGD-MC, FreeDoM, MPGD-Z) and one training-based method (PPAP). On CelebA-HQ, the proposed method wins on all six metrics (Distance and FID for segmentation, sketch, and text), with the largest margin over FreeDoM on sketch FID (41.26 vs. 52.18, a 21% improvement) and segmentation FID (33.31 vs. 38.65). On ImageNet text guidance, the method achieves 27.7 CLIP score, beating FreeDoM (25.9), UG (25.7), MPGD-Z (25.1), and LGD-MC (24.3). On human motion diffusion, the method dominates on both targeting and avoidance losses across all four prompts (e.g., 0.68+1.32 vs. 1.09+6.63 for FreeDoM on 'Backwards'), while matching or slightly exceeding baselines on MotionCLIP scores. The training-based PPAP baseline (Table 5) shows that at 20 DDIM steps, FreeDoM+Polyak (2190) nearly matches PPAP (2032), whereas vanilla FreeDoM (2439) lags significantly, confirming that the Polyak step size closes the convergence gap with training-based methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the training-free loss-based diffusion guidance paradigm established by FreeDoM [49], Universal Guidance [2], and LGD-MC [37], which use Tweedie's formula to estimate clean images and apply pretrained networks for zero-shot conditional generation. It departs from the energy-based framing (which [27] showed is inexact for training-free methods) by providing an optimization-theoretic analysis grounded in PL conditions and Lipschitz continuity. A key conceptual departure is the explicit connection to adversarial robustness literature: the paper reframes the 'misaligned gradient' problem as an adversarial gradient problem and borrows smoothed-classifier theory [34] and differentiable augmentation [51] to address it, while also importing Polyak step size [15] from optimization to accelerate convergence.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first systematic theoretical explanation for why training-free guidance works (loss decreases under PL conditions) and why it fails (reduced Lipschitz smoothness leads to misaligned gradients and slower ODE convergence), filling a gap left by prior work that treated these methods as heuristic. The practical techniques (random augmentation + Polyak step size) are simple, plug-and-play, and improve results across image and motion generation without additional training. However, the authors acknowledge that misaligned gradients cannot be fully eliminated without training, and the method still requires more NFEs than classifier-free guidance, leaving open the question of whether adversarial training or other robustness techniques can close this gap. Generalization to molecular generation and offline RL is suggested but not empirically validated.
