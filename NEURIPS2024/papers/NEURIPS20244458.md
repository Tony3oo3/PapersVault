## Problem

High-dimensional Bayesian optimization (HDBO) methods for discrete sequences have been proposed across many subfields, but the survey reveals highly heterogeneous experimental setups (initialization ranging from 0 to 1000+ points, inconsistent budgets) and technical barriers to replicability, making it impossible for practitioners to judge expected performance on real-world problems in chemistry and biology. Prior work typically tests on sequence lengths and category counts well below the hundreds that practitioners actually encounter, and no unified framework exists to compare optimizers under identical conditions.

## Input / Output

The benchmark consumes discrete sequences as inputs: amino acid sequences (alphabet of 20, lengths 5/15/64) for Ehrlich function oracles, and SELFIES molecular strings (alphabet of 64 tokens, padded to length 70) for PMO tasks. Two MLP VAEs trained on ZINC250k provide 2D and 128D continuous latent representations for the molecular tasks. Each solver runs for a fixed budget of 300 oracle evaluations (100 for the 2D PMO case), initialized with 10 Sobol-sampled points (1000 for the largest Ehrlich problem), and the output is the best objective value achieved, reported as mean ± std over 3–5 seeds. The poli library exposes 35 black box functions that accept any valid string from the corresponding alphabet, enabling free-form optimization beyond precompiled observation pools.

## Method

The paper contributes a refined seven-family taxonomy of HDBO methods (variable selection, additive models, trust regions, linear embeddings, non-linear embeddings, gradient information, and structured spaces) that separates methods optimizing directly in discrete space from those using latent representations. The core technical artifact is a unified benchmarking framework built from two software libraries: poli (providing isolated, queryable black box oracles including Ehrlich functions, PMO tasks, and Dockstring) and poli-baselines (providing standardized, weekly-tested environments for 11 solvers with a common solve interface). The benchmark evaluates continuous-space BO methods (SAASBO, Turbo, BAxUS, RandomLineBO, VanillaBO) and discrete-space methods (Bounce, ProbRep) alongside simple baselines (DirectedEvolution, HillClimbing, CMA-ES, GeneticAlgorithm) on both one-hot sequence representations and VAE latent spaces. Key findings show that discrete-space optimizers handle low-complexity problems well but fail to scale to longer sequences/larger alphabets, while latent-space methods have an advantage on molecular tasks—yet simple baselines remain competitive at low budgets, echoing recent criticisms of molecular optimization benchmarks.

## Results

On the PMO benchmark (128D latent space, 300 iterations), CMA-ES achieves the highest normalized sum score of 21.15±1.57, followed by SAASBO (20.42±3.26) and Turbo (16.30±4.08). Discrete-space methods Bounce (1.59±2.47) and ProbRep (3.95±3.57) perform dramatically worse, confirming they do not scale to high-dimensional latent spaces. On the Ehrlich sequence design tasks (Table 1), the naive DirectedEvolution baseline achieves the best sum (2.23±0.27), outperforming all BO methods including Turbo (1.86±0.40) and Bounce (2.00±0.33) on the more complex problems (sequence length 15 and 64).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the 128D PMO benchmark, CMA-ES (a non-BO evolutionary strategy) outperforms all BO methods, achieving a normalized sum of 21.15 vs. SAASBO's 20.42 and Turbo's 16.30. SAASBO (Eriksson & Jankowiak, UAI 2021) is the strongest dedicated BO method in 128D, winning on tasks like ranolazine_mpo (0.63) and amlodipine_mpo (0.41), but is highly variable (±3.26). Turbo (Eriksson et al., NeurIPS 2019) and BAxUS (Papenmeier et al., NeurIPS 2022) are competitive but trail SAASBO by 4–5 points in aggregate. Hvarfner's VanillaBO (2024) and RandomLineBO (Kirschner et al., ICML 2019) perform moderately (11.99 and 5.81 respectively), with RandomLineBO showing extreme variance (±5.82). The discrete-space methods Bounce (Papenmeier et al., 2024) and ProbRep (Daulton et al., NeurIPS 2022) are clearly the worst performers in latent space (1.59 and 3.95), with Bounce consistently running out of memory and ProbRep requiring 24-hour timeouts. On the Ehrlich sequence design tasks (Table 1), the naive DirectedEvolution baseline beats all BO methods on the harder problems (L=15: 0.448 vs. best BO 0.480 for Turbo; L=64: 0.114 vs. best BO 0.124 for Turbo), suggesting BO offers no clear advantage over greedy mutation at these scales.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the survey lineage of Binois and Wycoff (2022) and Santoni et al. (2023) by refining their taxonomy of high-dimensional Bayesian optimization into seven method groups, with particular emphasis on discrete sequence spaces relevant to biology and chemistry. It departs from prior surveys by highlighting the gap between the low-dimensional test problems used in the literature and the high-dimensional discrete problems practitioners actually face (sequence lengths ~10², >10 categories). The benchmarking component builds directly on the PMO benchmark (Gao et al., 2022) and Ehrlich functions (Stanton et al., 2024), but unifies them with a new software framework (poli, poli-baselines) that addresses the reproducibility and environment-isolation problems identified across the HDBO literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified benchmark reveals that discrete-space optimizers (Bounce, ProbRep) fail to scale to realistic sequence lengths, while latent-space methods retain an edge—yet even those are matched or beaten by simple baselines like Directed Evolution and CMA-ES at low budgets, echoing recent criticisms of molecular optimization benchmarks. The practical impact lies in the poli/poli-baselines infrastructure, which provides isolated, tested environments for 35 black box tasks and a standardized solver interface, lowering the barrier for fair HDBO comparisons. Open questions include whether any HDBO method can truly optimize in high-dimensional discrete spaces sample-efficiently, and the field still lacks black box oracles with sufficient real-world relevance beyond closed-form or data-driven surrogates.
