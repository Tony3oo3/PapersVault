## Problem

Conventional LLM evaluation metrics (cross-entropy loss, accuracy, BLEU) are 'extrinsic' — they assess model predictions rather than internal representations. While it is widely believed that larger models eliminate redundant information during training (a 'noise reduction' process), no principled metric exists to quantify this reduction directly from hidden states. The paper asks: can we define an information-theoretic, geometry-grounded metric that measures how effectively a model compresses its representation space through training?

## Input / Output

For single-modal evaluation, the method takes a text sequence and processes it through both a randomly initialized model M₀ and a compute-optimal trained model M₁, extracting the hidden states (pre-classification-head representations) for each token. These representations are centered, L₂-normalized, and assembled into a fixed-size covariance matrix whose singular values determine the eRank. For multi-modal evaluation, the pipeline additionally ingests RGB images through a vision encoder (CLIP-ViT or EVA-ViT) and a connector, producing five eRank values at different architectural stages. The final outputs are a single scalar Diff-eRank (for language) or two composite scores — Image Reduction Ratio and Image-Text Alignment (for MLLMs).

## Method

The core mechanism constructs a covariance matrix Σ from L₂-normalized, centered token representations, then computes its effective rank eRank(Σ) = exp(−Σᵢ pᵢ log pᵢ) where pᵢ = σᵢ/Σⱼσⱼ are normalized singular values — equivalently exp of the Von Neumann (matrix) entropy. Diff-eRank is defined as eRank(Σ_{M₀(x)}) − eRank(Σ_{M₁(x)}), quantifying the uncertainty eliminated by training; for a dataset, the average matrix entropy is exponentiated before differencing (Algorithm a), with an alternative per-sample averaging scheme (Algorithm b) shown to yield the same monotonic trend. The construction is grounded in quantum information theory: the normalized-representation covariance matrix plays the role of a density matrix, and eRank corresponds to the effective number of qubits needed to encode the token states under the quantum noiseless coding theorem. For multi-modal models, two derived metrics are introduced: Image Reduction Ratio = (eRank₁ − eRank₂)/eRank₁ measures the connector's condensation efficiency, and Image-Text Alignment = avg(eRank₃,eRank₄,eRank₅)/max(eRank₃,eRank₄,eRank₅) gauges how closely the LLM's internal representations of image, text, and image-text pairs match in information content.

## Results

On the OPT family (125M–13B), Diff-eRank increases monotonically with model size across all four datasets: from 54.35 to 89.81 on dolly-15k, 83.55 to 162.88 on Wikipedia, 103.23 to 262.70 on openwebtext2, and 53.02 to 93.30 on hh-rlhf. On benchmark tasks, Diff-eRank correlates with accuracy: on OpenBookQA it rises from 1.410 (125M, ACC 0.276) to 3.032 (13B, ACC 0.366), and on PIQA from 4.647 (125M, ACC 0.619) to 7.267 (13B, ACC 0.767). For multi-modal evaluation, LLaVA-1.5 achieves an Image-Text Alignment score of 0.8566 on detail_23k versus 0.7084 for MiniGPT-v2, while both models show similar Image Reduction Ratios (~0.385).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not propose a new model architecture to be compared against competing methods; instead, it introduces Diff-eRank as an evaluation metric and validates it by showing correlation with established metrics. Across the OPT family, Diff-eRank tracks the same upward trend as reduced cross-entropy loss and downstream accuracy as model size scales from 125M to 13B, with only minor deviations (e.g., OpenBookQA accuracy dips slightly at 6.7B while Diff-eRank continues rising). In the multi-modal setting, LLaVA-1.5 outperforms MiniGPT-v2 on Image-Text Alignment (0.8566 vs. 0.7084 on detail_23k; 0.7618 vs. 0.6955 on cc_sbu_align), consistent with LLaVA-1.5's superior benchmark performance, while both models achieve nearly identical Image Reduction Ratios (~0.385), indicating comparable connector efficiency. Ablation across three model families (OPT, Cerebras-GPT, OpenELM) confirms the scaling trend is not family-specific, and a rotation perturbation experiment shows that both alignment metrics degrade when image semantics are altered, validating their sensitivity to modality coherence.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the effective rank (eRank) formulation of Roy and Vetterli (2007) and the closed-form equivalence between eRank and matrix (Von Neumann) entropy established by Zhang et al. (2023), repurposing these tools from signal processing and self-supervised learning into an LLM evaluation metric. It positions itself as an 'intrinsic' evaluation approach based on hidden representations, in contrast to the dominant 'extrinsic' metrics (cross-entropy loss, perplexity, BLEU, ROUGE, benchmark accuracy) that rely on model predictions. The quantum information theory interpretation (density matrices, noiseless coding theorem) provides a principled information-theoretic grounding that distinguishes it from prior probing-based or compression-based analyses of LLM representations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Diff-eRank offers a representation-level diagnostic that is independent of prediction quality, potentially useful for monitoring training dynamics, guiding pruning/quantization/distillation, and diagnosing multi-modal alignment failures. However, the paper's empirical validation is somewhat limited: the correlation with loss and accuracy is consistent but not perfect (noted outliers), the full training-curve analysis is deferred to a small appendix experiment, and the multi-modal evaluation is restricted to two architectures with simple linear/MLP connectors. Open questions include whether the metric generalizes to more complex multi-modal architectures (e.g., Q-Former, cross-attention connectors), whether it can be computed efficiently at scale during training, and whether the 'noise reduction' interpretation holds for non-transformer architectures.
