## Problem

3D Gaussian Splatting degrades significantly under sparse-view conditions, producing overfitted Gaussians, geometric artifacts, and under-reconstructed regions. Existing few-shot Gaussian splatting methods address this by relying on pre-trained depth estimation networks or diffusion priors, which are sensitive to domain shifts and add substantial training overhead. FewViewGS tackles this gap by achieving state-of-the-art few-shot rendering without any such external priors.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20244039/cropped_page6_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes 3 (or 6/9) sparse RGB images with known camera poses as input, using 1/4 resolution on DTU and 1/8 on LLFF. Gaussians are initialized from an SfM point cloud (or randomly), and RoMa dense feature matching is precomputed between all training image pairs. The output is a set of novel view renderings from viewpoints between training frames plus the optimized 3D Gaussian scene representation, trained over 10,000 iterations on a single A6000 GPU (~5.8 min).

## Method

FewViewGS introduces a three-stage training scheme (pre-training → intermediate → tuning) that first establishes a coarse scene and scaled depth maps, then enforces novel-view consistency, and finally refines on known views. During the intermediate stage, RoMa matches are extracted between training image pairs, a novel view pose is sampled between them, and matched pixels are warped into the novel view; geometry, color, and semantic (VGG16 feature) losses are applied at projected pixels using an agreement mask and color-gradient weighting to filter unreliable correspondences. A min operation over the two source views further reduces noise from inaccurate projections. A locality-preserving regularization enforces color smoothness among K-nearest 3D Gaussians, eliminating floating and flickering artifacts that arise in few-shot settings. The entire pipeline avoids pre-trained depth estimators and diffusion models, relying solely on 2D feature matching and photometric supervision.

## Results

On DTU (3 training views), FewViewGS achieves 19.74 PSNR, 0.861 SSIM, and 0.127 LPIPS, outperforming the 3DGS baseline (16.94 PSNR) by 2.8 dB and all concurrent Gaussian-based methods. On LLFF (3 views), it reaches 20.54 PSNR and 0.693 SSIM, surpassing FSGS (20.43 PSNR) and DNGaussian (18.86 PSNR). On the synthetic Blender dataset (8 views), FewViewGS attains 25.55 PSNR, exceeding DNGaussian (24.31) and FreeNeRF (24.26) by over 1 dB. Training time is 5.82 minutes on a single A6000 GPU, comparable to 3DGS (1.17 min) and far faster than SparseGS (51.78 min) and FSGS (10.90 min).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FewViewGS outperforms the 3DGS baseline by 2.8 dB PSNR on DTU (19.74 vs. 16.94) and 1.1 dB on LLFF (20.54 vs. 19.48), while also achieving the best SSIM and LPIPS on both datasets. Among concurrent Gaussian-based methods, it beats DNGaussian by 1.5 dB on DTU and 1.7 dB on LLFF, SparseGS by 0.9 dB on DTU, and FSGS by 0.11 dB on LLFF. Against the strongest NeRF-based few-shot methods, FewViewGS slightly trails FreeNeRF on DTU PSNR (19.74 vs. 19.92) but surpasses it on SSIM (0.861 vs. 0.787) and LPIPS (0.127 vs. 0.182), and clearly outperforms RegNeRF (18.89 PSNR) and SparseNeRF (19.55 PSNR) on DTU. On the Blender synthetic benchmark, FewViewGS achieves the highest PSNR (25.55) among all methods, exceeding DNGaussian (24.31) and FreeNeRF (24.26) by more than 1 dB, while matching DNGaussian's SSIM (0.886). Notably, even with random Gaussian initialization (no SfM), the method still reaches 19.13 PSNR on DTU, surpassing SparseGS and DNGaussian, demonstrating robustness to initialization quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

FewViewGS builds directly on 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) as its scene representation and addresses the few-shot novel view synthesis task that has been extensively explored with NeRF-based methods (DietNeRF, RegNeRF, FreeNeRF, SparseNeRF) and concurrent Gaussian-based approaches (DNGaussian, FSGS, DRGS, SparseGS). The key departure from concurrent Gaussian-based few-shot methods is the rejection of pre-trained depth estimation networks and diffusion priors in favor of a simpler pipeline: 2D feature matching (RoMa) between training views, warping of matched pixels to sampled novel views, and multi-stage training with geometry/color/semantic consistency losses. The locality-preserving regularization on 3D Gaussian colors is a new contribution not present in prior Gaussian splatting work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that FewViewGS achieves competitive or SOTA few-shot NVS results without the domain-shift sensitivity of depth estimation networks or the training-time overhead of diffusion models, making the pipeline simpler and more robust to out-of-distribution scenes. The paper acknowledges limitations in texture-rich regions and heavy dependence on feature matching quality, leaving open whether denser or more accurate correspondences (e.g., from next-generation matchers) could further close the gap. For practitioners, the multi-stage training scheme and matching-based consistency constraint offer a reusable recipe for any explicit 3D representation that suffers from overfitting under sparse supervision.
