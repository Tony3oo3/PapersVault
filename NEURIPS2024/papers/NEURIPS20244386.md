## Problem

Existing quantitative bounds on the smallest eigenvalue of the neural tangent kernel (NTK) require both distributional assumptions on the data (e.g., Lipschitz concentration or uniformity on the sphere) and a high-dimensional regime where the input dimension d₀ scales at least as √n or polylog(n). This leaves a gap for data sampled from fixed- or low-dimensional spaces, where the NTK's conditioning is poorly understood despite being critical for convergence and memorization guarantees.

## Input / Output

The method takes n data points on the unit sphere S^{d₀−1} that satisfy a δ-separation condition (min_{i≠k} min(‖x_i−x_k‖, ‖x_i+x_k‖) ≥ δ) with no distributional assumptions, along with randomly initialized Gaussian ReLU network parameters. It produces explicit lower and upper bounds on λ_min(K): for shallow networks the lower bound scales as Õ(d₀⁻³δ²) requiring hidden width d₁ = Õ(‖X‖²d₀³δ⁻²), while the upper bound is O(δ′) where δ′ is the minimum pairwise distance. For depth-L networks under a pyramidal width condition, the bounds become Õ(d₀⁻³δ⁴) and O(L) respectively, with the first layer width scaling as Õ(nd₀³δ⁻⁴).

## Method

The core technique is a novel application of the hemisphere transform to the infinite-width NTK. The NTK is decomposed into inner-weight and outer-weight Gram matrices, each of which is bounded via a matrix Chernoff argument relative to its infinite-width limit. The infinite-width kernel is then reinterpreted as the L² norm of the hemisphere transform T_ψ of a signed Radon measure supported on the data points. Since spherical harmonics are eigenfunctions of T_ψ (via the Funk-Hecke formula with explicitly computed Gegenbauer-polynomial eigenvalues), a Bessel inequality reduces the problem to bounding the minimum singular value of a matrix whose entries are spherical harmonics evaluated at the data. The addition formula for spherical harmonics, combined with the δ-separation condition, controls the off-diagonal entries of this matrix, yielding the separation-dependent lower bound. For deep networks, a layerwise NTK decomposition (Lemma 9) reduces the problem to the shallow case applied to the first-layer feature Gram matrix, with inductive norm bounds on intermediate features and backpropagation terms completing the argument.

## Results

For shallow ReLU networks (Theorem 1), if the hidden width satisfies d₁ = Õ(||X||² d₀³ δ⁻²), then with high probability λ_min(K) = Õ(d₀⁻³ δ²) and λ_min(K) = O(δ′), where δ is the minimum pairwise separation and δ′ is the minimum pairwise distance. For deep L-layer networks with pyramidal widths (Theorem 8), if d₁ = Õ(n d₀³ δ⁻⁴) and d_{L-1} ≳ 2^L log(nL/ε), then λ_min(K) = Õ(d₀⁻³ δ⁴) and λ_min(K) = O(L). Critically, these bounds hold for arbitrary data on the sphere of arbitrary dimension d₀ (including constant d₀) with no distributional assumptions, requiring only δ-separation. For uniform spherical data (Corollary 2), the bounds specialize to λ_min(K) = Õ(n^{-4/(d₀-1)}) and λ_min(K) = Õ(n^{-2/(d₀-1)}), which are tight up to log factors when d₀ = Ω(log n).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's main contribution is removing two key restrictions present in all prior NTK eigenvalue bounds: (1) distributional assumptions on the data, and (2) the requirement that input dimension d₀ grows with sample size n. Nguyen et al. (2021) require d₀ = Ω(log n) and a Lipschitz concentration property on the data distribution; Bombari et al. (2022) require d₀ = Ω(√n) and smooth (non-ReLU) activations; Montanari & Zhong (2022) require d₀ = Õ(√n) and uniform spherical data. In contrast, this paper's Theorem 1 and Theorem 8 hold for any d₀ ≥ 3 (including constant) and any δ-separated data on the sphere, with the separation parameter δ serving as the sole data-dependent quantity. The trade-off is that the width requirement scales as Õ(d₀³ δ⁻²) for shallow networks (vs. Õ(n) in Nguyen et al. 2021), and the lower bound for deep networks degrades by a factor of δ² compared to the shallow case. The upper bound for deep networks (O(L)) does not capture data separation, matching a limitation shared with Nguyen et al. (2021) and Bombari et al. (2022).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the NTK eigenvalue bounding framework established by Nguyen et al. (2021) and Bombari et al. (2022), which both rely on a two-step strategy: first bound the shallow-network NTK, then extend to depth via layerwise decomposition. The key departure is replacing distributional assumptions (Lipschitz concentration, uniform-on-sphere sampling) and the requirement that input dimension d₀ scale with n with a purely geometric δ-separation condition on the data. Technically, the paper introduces the hemisphere transform and the addition formula for spherical harmonics (via the Funk-Hecke formula) as a novel analytical tool for lower-bounding the infinite-width NTK, which prior works accessed only through concentration-of-measure arguments that inherently required high dimensionality.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By showing that λ_min(NTK) can be bounded away from zero even when d₀ is held constant, this work closes a significant gap: prior convergence and memorization guarantees for gradient descent on neural networks were inapplicable to the most common practical setting of low-dimensional data (e.g., 2D images, 3D point clouds). The δ-separation condition is also strictly weaker than the distributional assumptions in prior work, making the results applicable to adversarially chosen or structured datasets. Limitations include restriction to ReLU activations, the requirement that data lie on a sphere, and the fact that the deep-network upper bound (O(L)) does not exploit data separation. Open questions include extending the hemisphere-transform technique to non-homogeneous activations (tanh, sigmoid) and to other architectures such as CNNs or transformers.
