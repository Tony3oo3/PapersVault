## Problem

Existing exemplar selection methods for in-context learning either rely on retrieval-based approaches that require test-time computation and increase data exposure to LLM providers, or use fixed-set selection that ignores the well-documented impact of exemplar ordering on ICL performance. Additionally, the instruction component of the prompt is typically optimized in isolation from exemplars, missing their interaction. The paper asks: can we efficiently find a single fixed ordered exemplar sequence (and optionally instruction) that generalizes well across all test queries, without test-time computation?

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pool of n input-label exemplar pairs, a held-out validation set D_V, black-box API access to a target LLM (e.g., GPT-3.5-Turbo), a pretrained embedding model (e.g., MPNet), and optionally a set P of candidate instructions. It outputs a single fixed ordered sequence of k exemplars (default k=5) that is prepended to every test query, plus an optionally optimized instruction. The search space is the full permutation space Ω of size n^k, and the method operates under a fixed query budget T=165 black-box evaluations, making it suitable for API-based LLMs where each evaluation incurs cost.

## Method

EASE formulates exemplar selection as black-box optimization over the permutation space Ω of size n^k, where each candidate is an ordered sequence of k exemplars. A pretrained embedding model h(·) maps each ordered sequence to a fixed-dimensional vector, and because different orderings of the same subset yield different embeddings, the representation inherently captures ordering information. A neural network trained on observed (embedding, validation-score) pairs predicts ICL performance, and the NeuralUCB acquisition function balances exploration of unexplored sequences against exploitation of high-predicted-score sequences. To handle the combinatorial explosion (e.g., 100^5 for k=5, n=100), EASE samples a large domain Q_t of candidate sequences and uses optimal transport distance to the validation set's embedding distribution to select a smaller relevant subset Q'_t on which the acquisition function is evaluated, achieving 3.8×–14.1× wall-clock speedups. The method extends naturally to joint instruction optimization by augmenting the search space with candidate instructions from P, treating each instruction as an additional 'exemplar' position.

## Results

On the Instruction Induction benchmark (19 non-trivial tasks, k=5 exemplars, 165 query budget), EASE achieves the best validation accuracy in 17 out of 19 tasks, with notable margins such as 30.0% on auto_categorization (vs. 25.0% for the best baseline Cosine) and 88.3% on taxonomy_animal (vs. 85.0% for Cosine). On the authors' novel out-of-distribution tasks (LR, LP-variant, AG News Remap, SST5 Reverse), EASE outperforms the strongest baselines by 6.7%–10% in absolute accuracy, with the gap widening under higher noise ratios. Jointly optimizing the instruction alongside exemplars yields additional gains of up to 46.7% (second_word_letter) and 26.7% (auto_categorization) over EASE without instructions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EASE consistently outperforms all seven baselines across the 19 Instruction Induction tasks, winning 17 of 19 (tying on synonyms and word_sorting). The subset-selection baselines (DPP [48], MMD [39], OT [42]) perform poorly overall (best in only 2 tasks each), confirming that ignoring exemplar ordering is a critical limitation. The retrieval-based baselines (Cosine [23], BM25 [37]) are more competitive (best in 6 and 4 tasks respectively) but still trail EASE by 3–5% on difficult tasks like taxonomy_animal (85.0 vs. 88.3) and sentence_similarity (53.3 vs. 56.7). The RL-based Active baseline [50] and influence-based Inf baseline [31] are the weakest among the learned methods (best in 1 and 5 tasks), with Active particularly struggling under the 165-query budget. The two strongest baselines, Evo (evolutionary mutation) and Best-of-N (uniform random search), each win 9 tasks but are still surpassed by EASE on the remaining 8, with EASE showing the largest margins on tasks requiring in-context reasoning (e.g., +8.3% on LR, +6.7% on AG News Remap).

## Relation to Prior Work

EASE adapts the NeuralUCB acquisition function from Lin et al.'s instruction optimization work [21] to the distinct problem of exemplar sequence selection, replacing the finite instruction candidate space with the combinatorial space of ordered exemplar permutations. It departs from retrieval-based exemplar selection (Liu et al. [23], Rubin et al. [38], Ye et al. [48]) by finding a single fixed ordered set that generalizes across all test queries rather than retrieving per-query exemplars, and from subset-selection methods (DPP, MMD, OT) by explicitly modeling exemplar ordering through sequence-level embeddings. The optimal transport component borrows the OT distance framework from Villani [42] but repurposes it as a computational filter over sampled permutations rather than a selection criterion, and the joint instruction+exemplar optimization extends the APE/PromptBreeder line of work [54, 10] by treating instructions as an additional dimension in the same search space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical value of EASE lies in eliminating test-time retrieval computation and reducing data exposure to LLM providers, making it suitable for API-based deployment where privacy and latency matter. The progressive finetuning experiment providing empirical evidence that exemplar selection importance diminishes with model task knowledge is a useful diagnostic for practitioners deciding when exemplar optimization is worthwhile. Key limitations include the requirement for a held-out validation set, the computational bottleneck of computing embeddings for ordered sequences, and the observation that gains can be negative when jointly optimizing instructions on noisy tasks (e.g., LR with 10% noise drops 28.3%). Open questions include whether the ordering-aware embedding approach scales to very long exemplar sequences and whether the OT-based filtering generalizes to non-text modalities.
