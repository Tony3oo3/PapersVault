## Problem

Existing topology-based generalization bounds (fractal dimension, persistent homology dimension, magnitude dimension) rely on continuous-time or infinite-time training dynamics and restrictive geometric assumptions (Ahlfors-regularity, geometric stability) that do not apply to practical discrete-time stochastic optimizers like SGD or ADAM with finite iterations. This creates a fundamental gap between the theoretical guarantees and the TDA-inspired numerical estimators actually used in practice, leaving no rigorous justification for the observed correlations between topological trajectory properties and generalization in real training runs.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a finite sequence of weight vectors {w_i} from a discrete-time optimizer (typically 5×10³ iterations after reaching a local minimum) and a chosen pseudometric (Euclidean ‖·‖₂, data-dependent ρ_S, or 01-loss distance) to compute a distance matrix over the trajectory. For large transformers, Euclidean distances are computed via sparse Johnson-Lindenstrauss projections (5% relative distortion), and data-dependent pseudometrics use 10% random subsampling of the training set for tractability. The output is a scalar topological complexity value (E_α, Mag(s), or PMag(s)) that provably upper-bounds the worst-case generalization gap over the trajectory, evaluated empirically via granulated Kendall's coefficients against the actual test-train accuracy gap.

## Method

The key insight is to replace intrinsic dimensions (PH-dim, magnitude dimension) with their finite, directly computable 'intermediary quantities'—α-weighted lifetime sums E_α (equivalent to MST edge-cost sums) and a new positive magnitude PMag that sums only the positive part of magnitude weightings—thereby obtaining non-asymptotic bounds valid for any finite n and any finite discrete trajectory. The proof for E_α leverages a PAC-Bayesian framework for random sets [22] combined with a covering argument, while the PMag bound relies on a newly discovered link between Rademacher complexity and positive magnitude (a result of independent interest that extends to compact sets). Both bounds share a total mutual information term I_∞(S, W) capturing data-trajectory dependence, and require only a bounded-loss assumption plus a general (q,L,ρ)-Lipschitz condition on the loss. The framework is pseudometric-agnostic, enabling seamless application across vision transformers (ViT, CaiT, Swin on CIFAR-10/100) and graph neural networks (GraphSage, GatedGCN on Superpixel MNIST) without architecture-specific analysis.

## Results

The paper evaluates topological complexity measures using Granulated Kendall's Coefficients (GKC) against generalization error across 4 model-dataset pairs (ViT-CIFAR10, Swin-CIFAR100, GraphSage-MNIST, GatedGCN-MNIST). The proposed E_α (α-weighted lifetime sums) achieves averaged GKC (Ψ) of 0.81 on ViT-CIFAR10, 0.58 on Swin-CIFAR100, 0.70 on GraphSage-MNIST, and 0.72 on GatedGCN-MNIST using the ρ_S pseudometric. PMag(√n) achieves comparable values (0.79, 0.58, 0.53, 0.85 respectively). Both proposed measures substantially outperform the persistent homology dimension (dim_PH) baseline, which yields Ψ values as low as −0.27 (GraphSage-MNIST) and −0.06 (GatedGCN-MNIST).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the persistent homology dimension (dim_PH) from Dupuis et al. [21] and Birdal et al. [10], the proposed E_α and PMag measures consistently and substantially outperform across all four model-dataset pairs. The gap is most dramatic on GNNs: dim_PH yields negative Ψ (−0.27, −0.06) on GraphSage-MNIST and GatedGCN-MNIST, while E_α achieves 0.70 and 0.72 respectively. On ViT-CIFAR10, E_α reaches Ψ=0.81 versus dim_PH's 0.13, a 6× improvement. Against the magnitude baseline (Mag) from Andreeva et al. [3], the proposed methods are competitive: E_α matches or slightly exceeds Mag(√n) on transformers (0.81 vs 0.65 on ViT-CIFAR10) but Mag(√n) retains a slight edge on GatedGCN-MNIST (0.91 vs 0.85 for PMag). Notably, E_α with the Euclidean distance (‖·‖₂) achieves Ψ=0.74 on Swin-CIFAR100, outperforming all ρ_S-based baselines on that setting, suggesting robustness across distance choices.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of fractal-dimension-based generalization bounds (Birdal et al. 2021, Dupuis et al. 2023, Simsekli et al. 2020) by replacing the intrinsic dimension—which requires continuous-time or infinite-iteration assumptions—with the finite intermediary quantities (α-weighted lifetime sums E_α and positive magnitude PMag) that are actually used to estimate those dimensions numerically. The proof technique draws on a recent PAC-Bayesian framework for random sets (Dupuis et al. 2024) and a newly established link between Rademacher complexity and positive magnitude. Conceptually, the paper departs from prior work by working directly with discrete, finite trajectories of practical stochastic optimizers (ADAM, SGD) rather than assuming stationary measures or continuous Langevin dynamics, and by introducing PMag as a theoretically grounded variant of Leinster's magnitude that is more amenable to learning-theoretic analysis.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first set of rigorous generalization bounds that provably apply to discrete-time stochastic optimizers with a finite number of iterations, closing a gap between theory and the TDA-based estimators actually used in practice. The experimental evaluation on industry-standard architectures (ViT, Swin, CaiT, GatedGCN, GraphSage) is substantially broader than prior topological generalization studies, which were limited to small networks. Key limitations include the intractability of the total mutual information term (rendering bounds not fully computable) and the reliance on correlation rather than tight quantitative prediction. Open questions include understanding the behavior of PMag at small scales, refining estimation techniques, and scaling the analysis to large language models.
