## Problem

Molecule inverse folding has been tackled separately for small molecules (atom-level) and macromolecules (residue-level), with no unified model handling both. Existing approaches differ in basic units, geometric featurization strategies, and system-size handling, creating redundant research efforts and preventing cross-domain transfer. This paper asks whether a single architecture can perform inverse folding across proteins, RNAs, and inorganic materials without task-specific modifications.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20244313/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a 3D molecular structure as input: for macromolecules, the backbone atom coordinates (N, Cα, C, O for proteins; P, C5–C1, O5–O2 for RNA) with predefined local frames; for small molecules and materials, all atomic coordinates with atom-type embeddings and learned local frames. A kNN graph connects blocks (residues or individual atoms) into a fixed-size block graph. The output is a per-block classification: one of 20 amino acids, one of 4 nucleotides, or one of 54 elements, depending on the task. Notably, for RNA design the model uses only tertiary structure (no secondary-structure annotation), making it more challenging than baselines like RDesign.

## Method

UniIF introduces a frame-based block representation that unifies all molecules: each block (amino acid, nucleotide, or atom) is encoded as a local SE(3) frame (rotation matrix + translation) plus an invariant feature vector, giving a fixed-dimensional representation regardless of block size. A geometric featurizer initializes node and edge features by projecting global coordinates into local frames and computing dot products of virtual intra-atoms to capture angle information. The core BlockGAT layer combines three components: (1) a geometric interaction extractor that generates virtual inter-atoms conditioned on edge features (unlike prior work that conditions only on node features), (2) global virtual blocks connected to all real blocks to capture long-range dependencies at near-linear cost, and (3) a gated edge-attention mechanism that aggregates only edge features (not node features) for node updates. The full model is 10 layers of BlockGAT with hidden dimension 128, trained with cross-entropy loss via Adam (lr=1e-3, batch=8, 50 epochs), and uses 5% node/edge dropout for regularization.

## Results

On protein design (CATH4.3 full set), UniIF achieves 53% median recovery without ESM2, outperforming PiFold (51%), ProteinMPNN (44%), and AlphaDesign (42%). On the time-split NovelPro benchmark with novel sequences, UniIF reaches 66% recovery, surpassing ESM2-based methods KWDesign (64%) and LMDesign (59%). For RNA design, UniIF achieves 48.94% overall recovery using only tertiary structure input, beating RDesign (41.53%) which additionally uses secondary structure. On material design (CHILI-3K), UniIF reaches 75.3% recovery, a 12.1-point margin over the next-best EdgeCNN (63.2%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

UniIF outperforms all 17 baselines across the three tasks. In protein design, UniIF beats the strongest non-ESM2 baseline PiFold by 2 points on CATH4.3 full (53% vs 51%) and by 9 points on NovelPro (66% vs 57%), while using fewer parameters (5.4M vs 5.8M). Against ESM2-augmented methods, UniIF still leads on NovelPro (66% vs KWDesign 64%, LMDesign 59%), demonstrating superior generalization to unseen sequences. In RNA design, UniIF (48.94% overall) surpasses RDesign (41.53%) by 7.4 points despite not using secondary structure input, and outperforms PiFold (24.48%) by over 24 points. In material design, UniIF (75.3%) leads EdgeCNN (63.2%) by 12.1 points and GIN (58.7%) by 16.6 points. The ablation in Table 1 shows that replacing the geometric interaction extractor with VFN's operation drops NovelPro from 66% to 63%, and removing the global virtual frame drops CASP from 51% to 49%.

## Relation to Prior Work

UniIF extends the PiFold line of work (Gao et al., ICLR 2023) by generalizing its frame-based featurizer and GNN into a unified architecture that handles both macromolecules (amino acids, nucleotides) and small molecules (individual atoms) under a single block graph representation. The frame-based block is explicitly positioned as a generalization of AlphaFold2's local frame, while the virtual inter-atom mechanism departs from VFN's node-conditioned virtual atoms by conditioning on edge features instead. Unlike AlphaFold3 and GET/EPT, which use token-level representations without per-block vector bases, UniIF retains equivariant basis vectors per block and avoids the atom-bond graph that RFAA requires for small molecules.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first model to achieve state-of-the-art inverse folding simultaneously across protein, RNA, and material design, demonstrating that a single architecture can handle the unit-size, geometric-featurization, and system-scale discrepancies that previously forced separate research lines. The RNA design result is particularly notable: UniIF outperforms RDesign (which uses secondary structure as auxiliary input) using only tertiary structure, suggesting the geometric featurizer captures sufficient structural information. Open questions include whether the unified approach extends to protein-RNA complexes, how it scales to very large macromolecular systems beyond the kNN neighborhood, and whether the learned local frames transfer across chemical domains. The paper does not discuss limitations (no wet-lab validation, no analysis of failure modes), and code is not yet released.
