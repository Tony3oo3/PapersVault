## Problem

Feint behaviors—deceptive actions that create temporal and spatial advantages over opponents—are fundamental tactics in competitive games, yet no prior work provides a comprehensive formalization at both the action level (how to generate physically realistic deceptive movements) and the strategy level (how Feint impacts game rewards in multi-player settings). Existing attempts either treat Feint as implicit animation glitches in two-player boxing scenarios or neglect its strategic implications entirely, leaving no generalizable framework for incorporating deception into multi-agent reinforcement learning.

## Input / Output

The method consumes game state vectors tracking body-part positions, orientations, and velocities, along with pre-computed attack behavior sequences (22 Mixamo boxing behaviors comprising 250+ unit actions) and the agent's existing regular policy model. Feint templates are pre-computed once before training via pairwise behavior matching, and during gameplay an imaginary play module performs a one-pass backward search to compose Dual-Behavior Models constrained by the last known action and the intended high-reward target action. The output is a Feint action sequence followed by a high-reward action (the Dual-Behavior Model), which replaces regular policy sampling for that episode segment, and an updated Feint policy trained only on accumulated real-game rewards after each completed Dual-Behavior cycle.

## Method

At the action level, the paper introduces Palindrome-directed Generation of Feint Templates, which decomposes each attack behavior into three sequences (Stretch-out, Reward, Retract) and extracts semi-symmetric action subsets to synthesize deceptive movements that mimic real attacks while returning to a physically compatible rest state; three template variants control where the cut occurs. These Feint behaviors are combined with follow-up high-reward actions via a Dual-Behavior Model using a Reverse Search Principle that selects the intended attack first and then constrains the Feint's ending state to match the attack's starting state. At the strategy level, the paper formalizes Feint impacts through a Dynamic Short-Long-Term temporal reward (weighted Feint regularizer plus emphasized attack reward for the short-term cycle, plus a discounted long-term average), a spatial reward based on f-divergence of occupancy measures between joint policies with and without Feint, and a collective reward aggregating both dimensions across all agents and time steps. The implementation adds a separate Feint policy model per agent within standard MARL frameworks (MADDPG, MASAC, MATD3, MAD3PG), with an imaginary play module that activates only when the current state is near a physically viable attack position but the regular policy assigns low probability to that attack, ensuring only one policy is inferred per game step and keeping overhead below 5%.

## Results

In both 1v1 and 3v3 boxing scenarios across all four MARL models (MADDPG, MASAC, MATD3, MAD3PG), incorporating Feint behaviors yields a significant and consistent reward advantage for the 'Good' player over all opponents and teammates. For diversity, Feint reduces Exploitability from ~9.7×10⁻² to ~4.9×10⁻² (a 1.98× improvement) and increases Population Efficacy from ~-1.2×10⁻² to ~-5.3×10⁻² in AlphaStar. The computational overhead of the Feint implementation is less than 5% per game episode across all tested MARL models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against other Feint or deception methods; instead, it evaluates the same four MARL models (MADDPG, MASAC, MATD3, MAD3PG) with and without the proposed Feint formalization as the baseline. In the 1v1 scenario, baseline runs show both players converging to similar reward levels (except MASAC, which shows mild instability), while the Feint-incorporating 'Good' player achieves a clear and sustained reward advantage in all four models. In the 3v3 scenario, the single Feint-equipped agent ('Good 3') outperforms both its three opponents and its two non-Feint teammates, demonstrating that the advantage generalizes beyond direct one-on-one interactions. The diversity metrics (Exploitability and Population Efficacy) in AlphaStar confirm that Feint expands the explored policy space by roughly 2×. The <5% overhead indicates the formalization is practically deployable without modifying the underlying MARL architectures.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the limited prior work on Feint (deceptive) behaviors in game AI—where [34] provided a proof-of-concept animation for two-player boxing and [35] learned nuanced control strategies from motion clips—into a comprehensive formalization at both action-level (Palindrome-directed Templates, Dual-Behavior Model) and strategy-level (temporal, spatial, and collective reward components). The strategy-level formalization draws on occupancy measures and f-divergence from [19] for the spatial dimension and long-term average reward from [14] for the temporal dimension, but restructures them into a Dynamic Short-Long-Term scheme specifically designed for deceptive action cycles. Unlike prior works restricted to two-player settings, the paper explicitly generalizes to multi-player scenarios where a single Feint can influence multiple agents simultaneously.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first unified, model-agnostic implementation scheme for incorporating deceptive behaviors into standard MARL frameworks, demonstrated across four architectures (MADDPG, MASAC, MATD3, MAD3PG) with <5% overhead, making it practically adoptable. The finding that a single agent using Feint gains advantage not only over opponents but also over its own teammates in a 3v3 setting highlights the non-trivial spatial interactions in multi-player deception. However, the evaluation relies on a custom boxing testbed without error bars or statistical significance tests, and the AlphaStar diversity evaluation is limited to exploitability/PE metrics without head-to-head win-rate comparisons, leaving open whether the formalization transfers to other game genres or scales to larger player counts.
