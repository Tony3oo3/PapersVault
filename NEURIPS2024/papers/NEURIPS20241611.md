## Problem

MARL research is bottlenecked by CPU-based environments that severely limit parallelism, making thorough multi-environment evaluation impractical with typical academic compute. While JAX-based GPU acceleration has transformed single-agent RL (PureJaxRL achieving 4000× speedups), no open-source Python library had brought these benefits to multi-agent settings. Compounding this, the field suffers from an evaluation crisis where 50% of recent MARL papers test on only one environment, risking overfitting and biased algorithm comparisons.

## Input / Output

Environments accept per-agent actions (discrete or continuous, structured as dictionaries keyed by agent names) plus a JAX PRNG key for stochasticity, and return per-agent observations, a shared reward, done signals, and info dictionaries. A critical JAX constraint is that the number of agents must be fixed at instantiation to satisfy static-shape requirements for JIT compilation; terminated agents pass dummy actions until episode end. The environment state is an explicit object passed between step calls rather than being internal, enabling the pure-function semantics required by JAX's JIT and vmap transformations.

## Method

JaxMARL reimplements a suite of MARL environments (SMAX, STORM, MPE, Hanabi, Overcooked, MABrax, Coin Game, Switch Riddle) and baseline algorithms (IPPO, MAPPO, IQL, VDN, QMIX) entirely in JAX, leveraging vmap for massive parallelization, JIT compilation, and GPU co-location of environment and policy to eliminate CPU-GPU transfer overhead. SMAX replaces the StarCraft II engine with lightweight 2D disc-based dynamics and a decentralized heuristic AI that guarantees a 50% win-rate baseline, while STORM extends matrix games to stochastic 8×8 grid worlds with temporally-extended actions and partial observability. The training pipeline is fully compatible with JAX's vmap, enabling thousands of independent training runs to be vectorized on a single GPU, yielding up to 14× speedup over CPU-based pipelines for single runs and up to 12,500× for vectorized training. The paper also provides recommended minimal evaluation environment sets per research setting (CTDE, zero-shot coordination, general-sum, continuous control) and inter-quartile mean aggregation protocols to standardize MARL benchmarking.

## Results

JaxMARL achieves a 14x wall-clock speedup over PyMARL for single training runs (e.g., 130s vs >1h for QMIX on MPE Simple Spread), and up to 12,500x when 1024 training runs are vectorized on a single GPU (198.4s for 1024 QMIX runs vs 70min for one PyMARL run). SMAX training is 40,000x faster than SMAC under vectorized training. In terms of agent performance, PPO-based methods (IPPO, MAPPO) consistently outperform Q-Learning baselines (IQL, VDN, QMIX) across SMAX, MPE, and Overcooked, with PPO also training 6x faster in SMAX and 10x faster in Overcooked.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against PyMARL [53], JaxMARL's QMIX on MPE completes a single training run in 130s versus over 1 hour, and 1024 parallel QMIX runs finish in 198.4s (21,500x per-agent speedup); for IPPO on SMAX vs. SMAC, JaxMARL achieves up to 31x speedup across varying rollout threads. Against MARLLIB [25], JaxMARL's IPPO trains multiple seeds in parallel on a single GPU, reaching 12,500x speedup over MARLLIB's single-seed training time on MPE Simple Spread. Against Sun et al.'s MAPPO on SMAC [58], JaxMARL's IPPO and MAPPO on SMAX show comparable learning dynamics, though some maps are significantly harder (e.g., 10m_vs_11m) or easier (e.g., 3s_vs_5z) in SMAX due to different AI and reward structures. On Hanabi, JaxMARL's IPPO achieves 24.18±0.04 (2-player) and 23.83 (3-player average), matching or slightly exceeding the original IPPO scores of 24.00 and 23.25 reported by Yu et al. [67], while MAPPO reaches 23.95±0.09 (2-player), comparable to the 24.19 best reported there.

## Relation to Prior Work

JaxMARL extends the single-agent JAX-based RL paradigm established by PureJaxRL [38] to the multi-agent setting, combining JAX's vmap parallelism and JIT compilation with a broad suite of MARL environments and algorithms. Its API design draws from PettingZoo [61] and Gymnax [32], while its Q-Learning baselines (IQL, VDN, QMIX) follow the PyMARL codebase [53] and its PPO implementations are based on PureJaxRL. The new SMAX environment is a JAX reimplementation of SMAC/SMACv2 [53, 15] that removes the StarCraft II engine dependency, and STORM extends Melting Pot 2.0's [1] matrix-game concept to grid-world settings with stochasticity. Unlike prior MARL libraries (PyMARL, MARLLib, MALib) which are CPU-bound, or hardware-accelerated simulators (Isaac Gym, Madrona, VMAS) that are GPU-specific or require C++, JaxMARL is the first open-source Python library unifying GPU-accelerated MARL environments with both value-based and actor-critic baselines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

JaxMARL directly addresses MARL's evaluation crisis—where 50% of recent papers test on only one environment—by making broad multi-environment evaluation computationally feasible through 14x single-run and 12,500x vectorized speedups. The SMAX environment removes the StarCraft II engine bottleneck and enables self-play and custom unit types previously infeasible, while STORM provides a new stochastic general-sum benchmark for studying opponent shaping. Key limitations include reduced speedups for off-policy value-based methods (due to replay buffer memory and unbalanced sampling ratios on GPU), difficulty handling variable agent counts, and the fact that most environments reimplement existing suites rather than pushing MARL's capability boundaries. Future work on novel JAX-based environments for continuous-action and general-sum settings, and distributed training frameworks separating sampling from learning, would further strengthen the library's utility.
