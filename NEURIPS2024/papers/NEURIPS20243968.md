## Problem

Learning under arbitrary covariate shift faces two fundamental barriers: PQ learning algorithms require oracles that are computationally intractable even for halfspaces under Gaussian marginals (exponential in d), while TDS learning algorithms reject entire test distributions even when the TV distance from the training distribution is o(ε). No prior approach simultaneously achieves dimension-efficient computation and tolerance to moderate distribution shift.

## Input / Output

The learner receives labeled training samples from a distribution D_train with a known well-behaved marginal D (standard Gaussian or uniform over {±1}^d) plus unlabeled test samples from an arbitrary, potentially adversarial distribution D_test. For PQ learning, the output is a hypothesis h: X→{±1} paired with a selector g: X→{0,1} that abstains on at most ε fraction of training-distribution points while achieving ε-accuracy on non-abstained test points. For TDS learning, the output is either a full rejection of the test set or an accepted hypothesis with O(λ) error, with the guarantee that acceptance is guaranteed whenever d_TV(D, D_test) ≤ θ for some moderate tolerance θ = O(ε).

## Method

The core mechanism is an iterative spectral outlier-removal procedure that repeatedly solves a quadratic program to find a degree-k polynomial p whose second moment under the remaining test data is disproportionately large relative to the reference distribution, then rejects all points where p(x)² exceeds a calibrated threshold τ. Unlike prior outlier removal (e.g., DKS18), the analysis uses a trace-based potential function to bound iterations independently of sample size, and the resulting moment bound after removal is O(1/α) without degree dependence, enabling handling of arbitrarily large outlier fractions. This filtered test set is then certified via L2-sandwiching polynomials (replacing the overly strict low-degree moment-matching test) to guarantee that polynomial regression on the training data generalizes to the selected test region. The combination yields the first dimension-efficient PQ learners for halfspaces, halfspace intersections, decision trees, and Boolean formulas, as well as the first tolerant TDS learners that accept test sets with O(ε) TV distance from the training marginal.

## Results

The paper provides the first dimension-efficient PQ learning algorithms for fundamental concept classes, achieving runtimes of d^{O(log(1/ε))} for realizable halfspaces under Gaussian marginals (previously requiring 2^{d^{Ω(1)}} time). For TDS learning, the algorithms are the first to be tolerant, accepting test distributions with d_TV(D_train, D_test) = O(ε), whereas prior methods rejected even when d_TV = o(ε). For agnostic learning of halfspaces, intersections of ℓ halfspaces, and size-s decision trees, the runtimes are d^{\tilde{O}(1/ε^4)}, d^{\tilde{O}(ℓ^6/ε^4)}, and d^{O(log(s/ε))} respectively for PQ learning, with corresponding TDS runtimes at d^{\tilde{O}(1/ε^2)}, d^{\tilde{O}(ℓ^6/ε^2)}, and d^{O(log(s/ε))}. As a corollary, the paper yields robust learners under Ω(ε) nasty noise achieving error ε, improving upon the √ε bound of [KKM18].

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper is purely theoretical and does not run empirical experiments, so comparisons are against prior theoretical guarantees. Against [DKS18] (nasty noise outlier removal), the key improvement is that the moment bounds after outlier removal do not depend on the polynomial degree, whereas [DKS18]'s bounds scale with degree, making their results vacuous when degree scales with target error; additionally, the paper handles arbitrary fractions of outliers while [DKS18] requires a bounded outlier fraction. Against [KSV24b] (TDS learning), the paper's algorithms are tolerant (accepting when d_TV = O(ε)) while [KSV24b]'s low-degree moment-matching approach rejects distributions even when d_TV = o(ε). Against [GKKM20, KK21] (PQ learning), the paper provides the first dimension-efficient algorithms, breaking the exp(Ω(√d)) barrier that was believed necessary for halfspaces. For learning with nasty noise, the paper achieves error ε under Ω(ε) corruption, improving upon the √ε error bound of [KKM18], though the results are incomparable with [DKS18] which achieves different tradeoffs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate threads: the PQ learning framework of Goldwasser et al. (2020), which required computationally intractable oracles even for halfspaces under Gaussian marginals, and the TDS learning framework of Klivans et al. (2024), which was dimension-efficient but intolerant (rejecting test sets with vanishingly small distribution shift). The core technical contribution is an improved analysis of spectral outlier removal from the nasty noise literature (Diakonikolas et al., 2018), extended to handle arbitrary outlier fractions and yielding degree-independent moment bounds, combined with L2-sandwiching polynomial approximations from Klivans et al. (2024) to produce the first dimension-efficient PQ learners and the first tolerant TDS learners for fundamental concept classes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The results close a significant gap: prior PQ learning was computationally infeasible for basic classes, and prior TDS learning was practically useless because it rejected even o(ε)-close distributions. The degree-independent moment bounds after outlier removal also yield new insights for polynomial regression under distribution shift and produce novel tolerant testable learning algorithms. Key limitations include restriction to specific training marginals (Gaussian, uniform over hypercube) and the open question of whether the completeness criterion can be extended to accept shifts toward any distribution in a broad well-behaved class rather than a single reference distribution.
