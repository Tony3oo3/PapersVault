## Problem

Diffusion models generate high-fidelity content but cannot guarantee that outputs satisfy hard constraints or physical laws, a critical gap in scientific and engineering applications. Existing remedies—classifier-free conditioning and single-step post-processing projections—either lack compliance guarantees or cause the generated samples to diverge significantly from the learned data distribution. This paper asks how to simultaneously enforce arbitrary (including non-convex) constraints and preserve fidelity to the original data distribution during diffusion sampling.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard Gaussian noise as the starting sample, a user-defined constraint set C (ranging from simple porosity thresholds on 256×256 microstructure images to ODE-governed position constraints on video frames and non-convex collision-avoidance regions for trajectories), and optionally a conditioning variable. A notable property is that the training data need not contain any feasible points: PDM can generate out-of-distribution samples that satisfy constraints never present in the training set (e.g., moon-gravity trajectories from earth-gravity data). Outputs are structured identically to the underlying diffusion model's native format—images, 3D pose sequences, point sequences, or video frames—but are certified to lie within C.

## Method

PDM recasts the reverse diffusion sampling process (discretized SGLD) as a constrained optimization problem: minimize the negative log-likelihood of the data distribution at each time step, subject to x_t ∈ C. At every denoising step t and every inner SGLD iteration i, the algorithm applies a projection operator P_C onto the constraint set after the gradient-plus-noise update, so feasibility is enforced throughout the entire reverse chain rather than only at the final step. For convex C this yields a formal feasibility guarantee (Corollary 5.4), and the projection error is shown to decrease monotonically as the step size γ_t shrinks toward zero (Corollary 5.3). For non-convex constraints, warm-started interior-point solvers handle the projections. Crucially, the optimization objective remains the score-matching negative log-likelihood, keeping the sampling objective aligned with the training objective—unlike post-processing baselines that optimize an external criterion and thereby degrade FID scores.

## Results

On constrained trajectory planning, PDM achieves 100% success rate on both topographies (Tp1 and Tp2) with path lengths of 2.21±0.26 and 2.05±0.17, compared to 77.1% and 53.3% for the conditional baseline. On 3D human motion (HumanML3D), PDM achieves zero penetration and zero float violations with an FID of 0.71, whereas the best PhysDiff configuration reports 0.918 penetration and 3.173 float violations at FID 0.51. On constrained materials, PDM achieves exact porosity constraint satisfaction with FID scores on par with the conditional model, while post-processing baselines (Post+, Cond+) show significant image quality degradation. On physics-informed motion, PDM produces zero-tolerance constraint satisfaction for both in-distribution (Earth) and out-of-distribution (Moon) gravitational settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PDM decisively outperforms the conditional diffusion baseline (Cond, based on classifier-free guidance [13]) on constraint satisfaction across all four experiments: 100% vs. 77.1%/53.3% success on trajectories, exact porosity satisfaction vs. frequent violations on materials, and zero physical violations vs. non-zero on human motion and physics-informed settings. The post-processing baselines (Cond+ emulating [10, 21]) provide no improvement over Cond on trajectory success rates and degrade image quality (higher FID) on materials and physics-informed motion. Against PhysDiff [32] on HumanML3D, PDM achieves zero penetration and float violations (vs. 0.918–0.998 and 2.601–3.173 for PhysDiff) but at a slightly higher FID (0.71 vs. 0.43–0.51), though PhysDiff relies on an external physics simulator that dramatically alters the diffusion model's outputs. On path length optimality, PDM is within ~0.13 of the conditional baselines on trajectories, confirming that constraint enforcement does not come at a meaningful optimality cost. The SGLD framework [30] serves as the theoretical foundation for the sampling process rather than a direct empirical baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on score-based diffusion models (Song & Ermon 2019; Song et al. 2020) and their SGLD-based sampling procedure, recasting the reverse diffusion process as a constrained optimization problem solvable via projected gradient descent. It departs from conditional diffusion (Ho & Salimans 2022), which provides only soft guidance without compliance guarantees, and from post-processing approaches (Giannone et al. 2023; Power et al. 2023) that apply constraints only at the final step, thereby decoupling the diffusion objective from the constraint objective. Prior constrained-domain work (Fishman et al. 2023/2024; Frerix et al. 2020; Liu et al. 2024) handles only simple convex or linear constraint sets, whereas PDM extends the framework to arbitrary (including non-convex) constraints via general projection operators.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PDM provides the first framework that simultaneously guarantees constraint compliance (including for non-convex and ODE-based physical constraints), maintains FID scores comparable to unconstrained sampling, and generalizes to out-of-distribution constraints not present in training data — a combination that prior methods could not achieve. The practical impact is significant for scientific and engineering domains (materials science, robotics, physics simulation) where generated outputs must meet verifiable specifications. Key limitations include substantial computational overhead from iterative projections (up to ~50× slower than conditional baselines), theoretical guarantees restricted to convex sets, and open questions about scaling the approach to large multi-task generative models with complex constraint representations.
