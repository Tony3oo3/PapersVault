## Problem

Mean-field theory explains how SGD on overparametrized shallow networks converges to a Wasserstein Gradient Flow, and algebraic/probabilistic work characterizes the benefits of symmetry-leveraging techniques (Data Augmentation, Feature Averaging, Equivariant Architectures), but it remains unclear how these two perspectives interact in the infinite-width limit. Specifically, whether and how symmetries of the data are preserved by the limiting distributional dynamics, and whether different SL techniques yield equivalent or distinct MF behaviors, had not been systematically addressed for general compact group actions.

## Input / Output

The framework takes a data distribution π over (X, Y) that may be equivariant under a compact group G, a shallow NN architecture specified by a jointly equivariant activation σ*: X×Z→Y and width N, and group actions ρ, ρ̂, M on the feature, label, and parameter spaces respectively. It produces: (i) explicit WGF PDEs governing the MF limit of vanilla, DA, FA, and EA training; (ii) invariance/preservation theorems for WI and SI parameter distributions; and (iii) a data-driven heuristic that iteratively discovers the largest equivariant parameter subspace E^G. Experiments use a controlled teacher-student setup with X=Y=R², Z=R^{2×2}, G=C₂ (coordinate permutation), and sigmoidal activations, with N ranging from 5 to 5000.

## Method

The paper introduces two notions of symmetric parameter distributions—Weakly-Invariant (G-invariant laws) and Strongly-Invariant (laws supported on the fixed-point subspace E^G of the group action on Z)—and shows that the symmetrization operator Q_G on functions corresponds exactly to symmetrizing the parameter measure (Proposition 1). The key theoretical results are: (1) DA, FA, and freely-trained networks obey identical WGFs when the data is equivariant (Theorem 4); (2) the space of WI distributions is preserved by the MF dynamics (Corollary 3); and most remarkably, (3) the space of SI distributions is also preserved by the MF dynamics even for freely-trained networks with no explicit parameter constraint (Theorem 5), a large-N phenomenon with no finite-N analogue. A counterexample (Proposition 4) shows SI measures can fail to attain the global risk minimum when E^G is too restrictive. The paper further derives a data-driven heuristic that starts from E₀={0} and iteratively expands the parameter subspace by training and measuring escape, converging to the 'true' E^G for the task.

## Results

The paper's main contributions are theoretical: Theorem 2 establishes that Data Augmentation (DA) and Feature Averaging (FA) solve the same optimization problem over weakly-invariant (WI) measures in the mean-field limit; Theorem 4 shows their Wasserstein Gradient Flows (WGFs) coincide, and with equivariant data, the freely-trained (vanilla) WGF also matches them. Theorem 5 proves the striking result that strongly-invariant (SI) parameter distributions are preserved throughout the MF dynamics even under unconstrained SGD with no SL technique applied. Empirically, in a teacher-student setup with G=C₂ acting on R², the Relative Measure Distance (RMD) between the vanilla-trained particle distribution and its SI/WI symmetrized version decreases toward zero as N grows from 5 to 5000, confirming the theoretical predictions at finite but large N.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares four training schemes — vanilla (no SL), Data Augmentation (DA), Feature Averaging (FA), and Equivariant Architectures (EA) — in a controlled teacher-student setting with N ∈ {5, 10, 50, 100, 500, 1000, 5000} particles. In the MF limit with equivariant data, all four schemes produce identical WGF trajectories (Theorem 4), meaning DA and FA provide no asymptotic advantage over free training. At finite N, DA and FA remain within the equivariant subspace E^G almost independently of N, while vanilla training only converges to this behavior as N → ∞. The key empirical finding is that SI-initialized vanilla training (with a WI teacher) stays within E^G even at moderate N (≈1000), a phenomenon with no finite-N explanation. For non-equivariant teachers, vanilla training correctly escapes E^G to better approximate the target, while DA/FA remain constrained — illustrating the expressivity trade-off of EA. No single scheme uniformly dominates; the choice depends on whether the target function is equivariant and on the available width N.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the mean-field theory of overparametrized shallow neural networks (Chizat & Bach, Mei et al., Rotskoff & Vanden-Eijnden) to the setting of distributional symmetries under a general compact group, and unifies the analysis of Data Augmentation, Feature Averaging, and Equivariant Architectures within a single Wasserstein Gradient Flow framework. It directly generalizes Hajjar & Chizat's study of symmetries in wide 2-layer ReLU networks under a single orthogonal transformation to arbitrary compact groups, multi-layer units, and all three major SL techniques simultaneously. The algebraic-probabilistic treatment of equivariant architectures builds on the parameter-sharing perspective of Kondor & Trivedi, Ravanbakhsh et al., and Flinth & Ohlsson, while the group-theoretic formulation of DA follows Chen et al. and Lyle et al.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first unified MF-level characterization showing that DA, FA, and free training are dynamically equivalent for equivariant data, and that SI parameter distributions (encoding equivariant architectures) are preserved throughout free training in the infinite-width limit—a large-N phenomenon with no finite-N analogue. This reframes how one should think about equivariant architectures: they emerge naturally from the MF dynamics rather than requiring explicit architectural constraints. Key open questions include quantifying convergence rates to the MF limit across SL techniques, extending the analysis to unbounded activations and deeper architectures, and validating the architecture-discovery heuristic on real-world equivariant datasets. The bounded-activation assumption and restriction to synthetic C2-group experiments limit immediate practical applicability.
