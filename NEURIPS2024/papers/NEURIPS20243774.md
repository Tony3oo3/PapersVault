## Problem

Convergence analysis of iterative differentiation has predominantly focused on deterministic algorithms such as gradient descent, leaving the stochastic setting largely unexplored. In parametric optimization (e.g., hyperparameter tuning), the derivatives of SGD iterates are themselves stochastic sequences whose convergence to the solution derivative is non-trivial due to the joint effect of noise on both the iterate sequence and its derivatives. This paper provides the first systematic convergence analysis of SGD derivatives in the strongly convex parametric setting.

## Input / Output

The method operates on a parametric stochastic objective f(x, θ; ξ) where both the objective and the initialization x₀(θ) depend on a parameter θ ∈ Θ ⊂ ℝᵐ, with randomness drawn i.i.d. from a distribution P. The key structural detail is that the derivative recursion (SGD') is analyzed jointly with the original SGD recursion using the same random samples ξ_k, rather than independent copies as in prior stochastic bilevel optimization work. The output is the Jacobian ∂_θ x_k(θ) ∈ ℝ^{d×m} at each iterate, with the main result being a mean-squared-error bound on ‖∂_θ x_k(θ) − ∂_θ x⋆(θ)‖² that depends on the step-size schedule and the noise level σ.

## Method

The central insight is to rewrite the derivative recursion (SGD') as an inexact SGD sequence applied to a quadratic parametric problem (Opt') whose objective involves the expected second-order derivatives at the solution x⋆(θ). The inexactness arises from the difference between second-order derivatives evaluated at x⋆ versus at the current iterate x_k, yielding an error term of order η_k‖x_k − x⋆‖(1 + ‖∂_θ x_k‖). The paper develops a general abstract convergence theory for inexact SGD with non-centered, non-stationary error terms (not required to have zero mean), which is a result of independent interest. Applying this theory yields three regimes: constant step-sizes produce a noise ball of radius O(√η) around the solution derivative, vanishing 1/k step-sizes give O(log(k)²/k) MSE convergence, and the interpolation regime (σ = 0) yields exponential convergence with constant step-size. The analysis requires strong convexity, Lipschitz Hessian, and second-moment control of both the gradient and the second-derivative terms at the solution.

## Results

The main theoretical contribution (Theorem 2.2) establishes three convergence regimes for the MSE of SGD derivative error ‖∂_θ x_k(θ) − ∂_θ x*(θ)‖². With constant step-size η, derivatives stabilize within a ball of radius O(√η) around the true solution derivative. With vanishing step-sizes η_k = 2/(μk + 8κ²), the derivative error converges at rate O(log(k+8κ²)²/(k+8κ²)). In the interpolation regime (σ=0, constant step-size η = μ/(4L²)), convergence is exponential: O(k(1−1/(8κ²))^k). Numerical experiments on synthetic regression tasks (OLS, ridge, logistic, Huber, SVM) and the ijcnn1 dataset (49,990 samples, 22 features) confirm these qualitative behaviors.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper is primarily a theoretical contribution and does not benchmark its method against competing algorithms; the `compares_to` list is empty. The numerical experiments serve as qualitative illustrations of the theoretical predictions rather than head-to-head comparisons. The authors note that their approach (jointly analyzing the iterate and derivative recursions with the same stochastic samples) differs from prior stochastic bilevel optimization work (Grazzi et al. 2021, 2023, 2024; Ji et al. 2021, 2022) which typically uses independent copies or separate batches for the derivative recursion. The inexact SGD analysis (Proposition 3.1) is positioned as a result of independent interest, extending prior biased SGD analyses (Demidovich et al. 2023) by handling random non-stationary bias terms whose magnitude depends on the iteration counter. The SVM (hinge loss) experiment in Figure 2 is noted as a potential extension to the nonsmooth case, referencing Bolte et al. (2022) for formal treatment, but no quantitative comparison with smooth baselines is provided.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the deterministic iterative differentiation framework (Gilbert 1992; Mehmood and Ochs 2020) to the stochastic setting by showing that the derivative recursion of SGD is itself an inexact SGD sequence for a quadratic parametric problem. It departs from prior stochastic bilevel optimization work (Grazzi et al. 2021, 2023) that typically uses independent samples for the iterate and derivative recursions, instead jointly analyzing both with the same samples. The inexact SGD convergence analysis (Proposition 3.1) is developed as a standalone result of independent interest, building on the stochastic approximation robustness literature (Ermoliev 1983; Nedić and Bertsekas 2010) but handling random non-stationary bias terms that depend on the iterate sequence.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This provides the first mean-squared-error convergence guarantees for iterative differentiation through SGD, filling a gap that has been explicitly called out in the literature (Pedregosa 2016; Maclaurin et al. 2015). The practical implication is that practitioners performing hyperparameter optimization or meta-learning via backpropagation through SGD can now rely on theoretical convergence of their gradient estimates. Key limitations include the restriction to strongly convex objectives, small step sizes (η₀ ≤ μ/(4L²)), and the absence of a minibatch version; open questions include extending to non-strongly convex or nonsmooth settings, larger step sizes, and comparing stochastic iterative versus implicit differentiation.
