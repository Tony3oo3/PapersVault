## Problem

Current spatial reasoning-enhanced VLMs are fine-tuned on spatial VQA datasets and learn surface-level image-text associations, limiting their ability to generalize to higher-level 3D-aware tasks such as articulating dynamic scene changes, scene comparisons, and trajectory planning. The paper asks whether VLMs can be imbued with genuine 3D spatial understanding without any fine-tuning, by injecting explicit 3D scene knowledge from off-the-shelf 3D foundation models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242197/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image (standardized to 640×480) and a spatial task question as input. It produces multi-level outputs: qualitative/quantitative spatial VQA answers, fine-grained 3D object poses (6D: rotation + translation in cm), object sizes and principal axes, spatial relationship summaries, and for robotics applications, full 3D trajectories via RRT* or axes-constrained waypoint-based motion plans. The VLM never receives hardcoded relative distances or angles; instead it receives raw 3D coordinates, axes, and sizes in physical units and must reason over them itself.

## Method

SpatialPIN is a fully modular, zero-shot, training-free pipeline that progressively enhances a VLM's spatial understanding through three stages. First, 2D scene understanding: the VLM identifies and describes objects by color, texture, and 2D location; these descriptions drive language-guided segmentation (Language Segment-Anything) and a two-step inpainting-resegmentation process (LaMa) to obtain occlusion-free object masks. Second, coarse 3D understanding: metric depth (ZoeDepth) and perspective fields estimate camera intrinsics and scene dimensions, which the VLM uses to canonicalize the camera shot angle (horizontal/top-down/bottom-up). Third, fine-grained 3D understanding: single-view 3D reconstruction (One-2-3-45++) generates object meshes, which are positioned via raycasting from camera-plane to background-plane using metric depth, scale-calibrated by rendered contour comparison, and assigned principal axes via minimal oriented bounding boxes. The reconstructed scene is rendered in Blender, summarized into a physical-unit spatial context paragraph, and fed back to the VLM alongside a visualization with colored object axes. For downstream robotics tasks, the VLM reasons about object properties and proposes axes-constrained waypoints, which are converted to collision-free 3D trajectories using RRT* with voxelized obstacle representation.

## Results

On the standard qualitative IaOR-VQA benchmark, SpatialPIN + GPT-4o achieves 87.3% accuracy, surpassing the SOTA trained baseline SpatialVLM (76.7%) by 10.6 points, and improving GPT-4o's standalone performance (69.0%) by 18.3 points. On quantitative IaOR-VQA, SpatialPIN + GPT-4o reaches 70.5% within the [75×, 133×] ground-truth range, compared to only 20.5% for SpatialVLM. On the novel IaAD-VQA and IrSD-VQA tasks, SpatialPIN + GPT-4o scores 84% and 82% qualitatively, outperforming SpatialVLM (62% and 54%) by 22 and 28 points respectively. On the pick-and-stack robotics task, SpatialPIN + GPT-4o achieves a 36% full success rate versus 10% for SpatialVLM + RRT*.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SpatialPIN consistently outperforms SpatialVLM [12], the primary SOTA trained baseline, across all spatial VQA forms: by 10.6 points on qualitative IaOR-VQA (87.3% vs 76.7%), by 50 points on quantitative IaOR-VQA within the tight [90×, 111×] range (55.0% vs 7.5%), and by 22–28 points on the novel IaAD-VQA and IrSD-VQA tasks. In the ablation study, replacing the full 2D+3D pipeline with ShAPO [27] (mesh-free pose estimation) drops qualitative accuracy to 36.7%, and with SAM-6D [36] (mesh-based pose estimation) to 48.0%, confirming that language-driven, multi-model interaction is critical for generalization. On the pick-and-stack robotics task, SpatialPIN + GPT-4o (36% full success) more than triples SpatialVLM + RRT* (10%) and doubles the direct 3D-info-only variant (16%). For task diversity, SpatialPIN's proposed tasks achieve the lowest Self-BLEU (0.269) and embedding similarity (0.154) compared to RoboGen [61] (0.284/0.165), BEHAVIOR-100 [54] (0.299/0.210), RLbench [28] (0.317/0.200), MetaWorld [72] (0.322/0.263), and ManiSkill2 [23] (0.674/0.194), indicating the most semantically diverse task generation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm of enhancing VLM spatial reasoning via fine-tuning on spatial VQA datasets (exemplified by SpatialVLM) by instead leveraging explicit 3D priors from off-the-shelf foundation models (One-2-3-45++, ZoeDepth, Perspective Fields) through progressive prompting in a zero-shot, training-free manner. Conceptually, it extends the Socratic Models framework [73]—which composes multiple foundation models for 2D image tasks—into the 3D scene understanding domain, and builds on 3DAxiesPrompts [37] for visualizing spatial context to VLMs. The key departure is treating 3D scene reconstruction not as a standalone task but as a 'free lunch' information source that, when summarized and prompted into a VLM, unlocks generalization to tasks outside any training distribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central finding—that injecting explicit 3D scene geometry via prompting can outperform dedicated spatial VQA training—challenges the assumption that spatial reasoning must be learned from large curated QA datasets, suggesting a more composable path to 3D-aware VLMs as 3D foundation models improve. The modular design means each component (segmentation, depth, reconstruction, VLM backbone) can be independently upgraded, making the framework future-proof. However, practical limitations remain significant: ~20-second per-image inference dominated by 3D reconstruction, dependence on closed-source VLMs (GPT-4o), small evaluation datasets (50–300 questions per task), and single-view reconstruction quality that may degrade in cluttered or novel scenes. Open questions include whether the approach scales to multi-view or video inputs, whether open-source VLMs can match the gains, and whether the 3D reconstruction bottleneck can be eliminated with faster models.
