## Problem

While the Bottleneck Stability Theorem guarantees OT_∞-stability of Čech persistence diagrams under Hausdorff perturbations, the behavior under finer Wasserstein-type OT_p distances remained poorly understood. In particular, it was unclear under what conditions on the sampling and the underlying geometry the OT_p distance between the PD of a sample and the PD of the true shape converges to zero, and how the total α-persistence scales with sample size. Prior bounds required the ambient dimension d, leaving a gap when data lies on a lower-dimensional submanifold.

## Input / Output

The method takes a point cloud A ⊂ M where M is a compact m-dimensional submanifold of R^d (with positive reach, and generically a topological Morse function for the distance function), and produces explicit convergence rates for OT_p(dgm_i(A), dgm_i(M)) as a function of the Hausdorff gap ε or sample size n. The key output is a sharp threshold: OT_p convergence holds if and only if p > m, and the total α-persistence satisfies Pers_α(dgm_i(A_n)) = Pers_α(dgm_i(M)) + C_i n^{1−α/m} + o(n^{1−α/m}), replacing the ambient dimension d in prior bounds with the intrinsic dimension m.

## Method

The core mechanism is a three-region decomposition of the Čech PD of A into microscopic (Region 1, both coordinates ≤ O(ε)), mixed (Region 2, birth ≤ ε but death ≥ τ(M)−O(ε²)), and macroscopic (Region 3, both coordinates ≥ τ(M)−O(ε²)) points, where τ(M) is the reach. A quadratic improvement on the Bottleneck Stability Theorem (Lemma 2.1) shows that for points in Regions 2–3, the optimal matching distance is O(ε²) rather than O(ε), exploiting the curvature bound from the reach. For generic submanifolds, the distance function d_M is a topological Morse function with finitely many critical points, which bounds the number of Region 3 points independently of ε. In the random sampling setting, Region 1 is rescaled by n^{1/m} and shown to converge in OT_p to a universal limit measure (extending the cube result of Divol–Polonik to manifolds), while Regions 2–3 are controlled by the genericity structure, yielding the exact p > m threshold and a law of large numbers for total persistence.

## Results

The paper establishes that for random samplings of an m-dimensional generic submanifold, OT_p convergence of Čech persistence diagrams to the manifold's PD holds if and only if p > m. The α-total persistence satisfies the law of large numbers Pers_α(dgm_i(A_n)) = Pers_α(dgm_i(M)) + C_i·n^{1−α/m} + o_{L¹}(n^{1−α/m}) + O_{L¹}((log n/n)^{1/m}), implying Pers_α stays bounded if and only if α ≥ m (for 0 ≤ i < m). The Improved Bottleneck Stability Theorem yields a quadratic O(ε²) bound on coordinate displacements (vs. the standard O(ε) bound) for points with coordinates exceeding 2ε. In the critical case p = m, E[OT_p^p] converges to the constant Pers_p(μ_{∞,i,m})·Vol(M) rather than to 0.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper improves upon the standard Bottleneck Stability Theorem [Cohen-Steiner et al., 2005] by showing a quadratic O(ε²) bound on coordinate displacements for large-scale features, replacing the linear O(ε) bound. Compared to Cohen-Steiner et al. [2010], which required α > d (ambient dimension) for bounded total persistence, this work replaces d with the intrinsic dimension m, so Pers_α is bounded for all α ≥ m rather than α > d. Relative to Divol & Polonik [2019], who established total persistence laws for random samples in the unit cube, this paper generalizes to submanifolds with nontrivial topology, requiring additional control of large-scale features in Regions (2)–(3). Compared to Goel, Trinh & Tsunoda [2019], who proved vague convergence of rescaled PDs on manifolds, this paper establishes the stronger OT_p convergence (Theorem 4.1) and provides explicit rates. The numerical experiments (Figures 4–6) confirm the predicted convergence rates on log-log plots for circle and torus samples, and demonstrate the discontinuity of persistence images at p = m.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Bottleneck Stability Theorem (Cohen-Steiner et al. 2005) by showing a quadratic improvement (O(ε²) vs. O(ε)) for large-coordinate PD points when the underlying set is a submanifold, leveraging the reach-based geometry of distance functions. It generalizes the total persistence bounds of Cohen-Steiner et al. (2010), which required α > d (ambient dimension), to the intrinsic dimension m of the submanifold, and extends the vague convergence results of Goel, Trinh & Tsunoda (2019) from the cube to generic submanifolds by establishing full OT_p convergence. The genericity framework of Arnal, Cohen-Steiner & Divol (2023) on distance functions to submanifolds is essential for controlling the number of large-persistence points in Region (3).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The clean threshold result—OT_p convergence holds if and only if p > m—gives TDA practitioners a concrete, actionable criterion for choosing feature map weights (e.g., persistence images with pers^p weighting) when data lies on m-dimensional manifolds, directly impacting the soundness of ML pipelines using PersLay, PLLay, or similar layers. The law of large numbers for total α-persistence provides the first precise asymptotics in the submanifold setting, replacing the ambient dimension d with the intrinsic m. Key limitations include the assumption of noise-free sampling from a submanifold and the restriction to Čech complexes; extending to Vietoris-Rips complexes, noisy observations, and non-manifold data remain open.
