## Problem

Contrastively trained vision-language representations like CLIP have been shown to fail at providing the fine-grained visuo-spatial scene understanding that embodied control agents require, yet text-to-image diffusion models—which are explicitly optimized to generate images from text and thus encode highly detailed spatial information—have remained largely unexplored as a source of representations for control. This paper asks whether intermediate features from a pre-trained text-to-image diffusion model can serve as versatile, task-agnostic visual-language representations that match or exceed purpose-built control representations across manipulation and navigation tasks.

## Input / Output

The method takes a 256×256 RGB image (optionally paired with a text prompt) as input. The image is first encoded by the Stable Diffusion VQ-VAE into a 32×32×4 latent, noised to a chosen diffusion timestep, and passed through the U-Net denoiser. The output is a set of spatial feature maps extracted from the U-Net's mid and downsampling blocks, bilinearly interpolated to a common 8×8 spatial grid, concatenated channel-wise (~102,400 dimensions), and reduced via a learnable 1×1 convolutional compression layer to 2048–8192 channels. These frozen feature maps are then fed to a task-specific policy network (MLP for manipulation, LSTM for navigation) that outputs discrete or continuous control actions.

## Method

Stable Control Representations (SCR) extract visual-language features by running a partially noised image latent through the frozen U-Net denoiser of Stable Diffusion v1.5 at a selected diffusion timestep t, then aggregating intermediate feature maps from the mid block and the last three downsampling blocks. The key design insight is that lower timesteps (less noise) preserve the fine spatial detail needed for control, while the multi-layer aggregation captures both high-level semantics and low-level geometry in a single representation. An optional variant (SCR-ATTN) additionally fuses word-level cross-attention maps between visual features and CLIP text embeddings to provide category-agnostic localization signals for open-vocabulary tasks. A second variant (SCR-FT) fine-tunes the U-Net on ~1.3M image-text pairs from robotics datasets (EpicKitchens, SS-v2, Bridge-v2) using the same text-conditioned generation objective as the base model—avoiding the complex negative-sampling pipeline required for contrastive fine-tuning of CLIP. Downstream, the frozen representations are paired with task-specific policies trained via behavior cloning (few-shot manipulation) or distributed PPO/DD-PPO (navigation), with no gradient flow back into the diffusion model.

## Results

On Meta-World, SCR-FT achieves 94.9% success rate, competitive with R3M (96.0%) and surpassing VC-1 (92.3%) and CLIP (90.1%). On ImageNav, SCR achieves 73.9% success rate, outperforming the prior SOTA VC-1 (70.3%) and CLIP-B (52.2%). On OVMM, SCR-FT-ATTN achieves 43.6% success rate, beating VC-1 (40.6%), CLIP (38.7%), and Detic (36.7%). On Franka-Kitchen, SCR-FT reaches 49.9%, surpassing VC-1 (47.5%) and CLIP (36.3%) but trailing R3M (57.6%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SCR and its variants are competitive with or superior to all baselines across the four main benchmarks. On ImageNav, SCR (73.9%) clearly outperforms VC-1 (70.3%), the prior SOTA visual representation, by 3.6 points, while R3M—despite being the best on Meta-World—collapses to 30.6%, revealing its poor transferability to navigation. On OVMM, SCR-FT-ATTN (43.6%) surpasses VC-1 (40.6%) by 3.0 points and CLIP (38.7%) by 4.9 points, and also beats the Detic detector baseline (36.7%), though a gap to the Oracle (77.6%) remains. On Meta-World, SCR-FT (94.9%) is within 1.1 points of R3M (96.0%) and beats VC-1 (92.3%) and CLIP (90.1%). On Franka-Kitchen, SCR-FT (49.9%) edges out VC-1 (47.5%) and substantially outperforms CLIP (36.3%), but R3M (57.6%) retains a clear advantage, consistent with its design for few-shot manipulation. The overall normalized ranking (Table 11) places SCR-FT first (0.949), VC-1 second (0.927), and SCR third (0.922), confirming that diffusion-model representations are all-round competitive with purpose-built control representations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the Stable Diffusion U-Net as a frozen feature extractor for robotic control, following the representation-extraction protocol established by Wang et al. (2023) and Traub (2022) for vision tasks like segmentation and classification. It departs from the dominant paradigm in embodied AI representation learning—contrastive objectives (CLIP, R3M, Voltron) and masked auto-encoding (MVP, VC-1)—by showing that a model trained solely for conditional image generation encodes fine-grained visuo-spatial information sufficient for downstream policy learning. The cross-attention map extraction for open-vocabulary generalization builds on observations from Wang et al. (2023) and Tang et al. (2023) about text-aligned spatial attention in diffusion models, but is the first to fuse these maps into a control policy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that a generative model's intermediate features match or exceed purpose-built representation learners (VC-1, R3M) across manipulation, navigation, and open-vocabulary tasks suggests that the field may be underutilizing the representational capacity of large-scale generative pre-training for embodied AI. The compression-layer aggregation finding is practically important: it shows that prior conclusions about CLIP's inability to encode fine-grained spatial detail may have been confounded by suboptimal spatial pooling, and the technique transfers to other ViT-based representations. Open questions include whether the approach scales to newer diffusion architectures (SDXL, DiT-based models), whether the 1.5× inference overhead is acceptable for real-time control, and whether the modest text-prompt sensitivity limits the method's advantage in truly language-grounded settings.
