## Problem

Existing language-guided robotic manipulation models map instructions directly to low-level actions at every timestep, neglecting the modeling of critical waypoints (e.g., 'close to', 'grab', 'move up') that humans intuitively use to structure manipulation. This causes models to memorize surface patterns rather than acquire transferable dynamics knowledge, and the sequential execution of all model modules at identical frequencies introduces substantial computational redundancy.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a natural-language instruction, a window of h=3 historical RGB observation frames, and the 6-DoF robot arm state (x, y, z, roll, pitch, yaw). The VLM parses the instruction into one of 10 predefined primitive actions at low frequency, which are then encoded via CLIP as text prompts. The scene prediction module outputs a 49×512 waypoint feature tensor (not pixel-level images, following I-JEPA), and the action prediction module outputs a 7-dimensional action vector (delta XYZ, delta Euler angles, and binary gripper state) with each continuous dimension discretized into 256 uniform bins following RT-1.

## Method

PIVOT-R introduces a Waypoint-Aware World Model (WAWM) that uses a frozen VLM (LLaVA) to parse primitive actions from the instruction at low frequency, then feeds these as prompts to a 12-layer transformer scene prediction module that predicts task-relevant waypoint features from historical observations. A lightweight 3-layer transformer action prediction module then decodes low-level actions conditioned on the predicted waypoint features, historical images, and robot state. The key architectural insight is treating waypoints as a structural chunking mechanism analogous to tokenization in NLP, segmenting dense trajectories into meaningful sections so that critical manipulation dynamics are not submerged in trivial per-frame predictions. An Asynchronous Hierarchical Executor (AHE) runs the three modules at different frequencies (v₁=3, v₂=10, v₃=30 Hz) via multithreading with shared buffers, yielding a 28× speedup over synchronous execution with only a 2.9% performance drop. Training uses imitation learning on SeaWave with a combined loss of I-JEPA-style L2 feature distance for scene prediction and cross-entropy for action prediction, with only 30M trainable parameters (VLM and CLIP encoders frozen).

## Results

On the SeaWave benchmark, PIVOT-R achieves a mean success rate of 74.19% across four progressive instruction levels (88.06% L1, 77.55% L2, 73.33% L3, 57.82% L4), representing a 19.45% relative improvement over the best baseline Surfer (54.74%). The model runs at 27 ms per step, comparable to other lightweight baselines (12–35 ms) and far faster than SUSIE (434 ms) and Gato (139 ms). On real-world robot tasks, PIVOT-R reaches 40.28% mean success rate, a 6% absolute gain over Surfer (34.26%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PIVOT-R outperforms all seven SeaWave baselines at every instruction level. Against the strongest baseline Surfer (a world-model-based method), PIVOT-R gains +13.32, +16.50, +28.07, and +19.93 percentage points on Levels 1–4 respectively, with the largest gap on Level 3 (intention inference) where waypoint-aware modeling most directly helps. Compared to video-generation-based GR-1 (51.07%) and RT-1 (47.64%), PIVOT-R leads by 23.1 and 26.6 points, confirming that explicit waypoint prediction is more effective than dense frame-level video prediction for manipulation. Against the end-to-end VLA baselines Octo (46.64%) and Gato (27.11%), the advantage is even larger (27.6 and 47.1 points), highlighting the cost of skipping intermediate waypoint structure. On the SIMPLER benchmark, PIVOT-R (0.393% mean) surpasses Octo-Small (0.295%) by roughly 10% relative, while RT-1-X and Octo-Base remain near zero. On real-world tasks, PIVOT-R (40.28%) beats Surfer (34.26%) and RT-1 (27.31%), with the most notable gap on the 'Pick up' task (54.17% vs. 41.67% for Surfer).

## Relation to Prior Work

PIVOT-R synthesizes three research threads that had previously been pursued in isolation: VLM-based instruction parsing (RT-H, RoboFlamingo), world modeling for manipulation (Surfer, Daydreamer, 3D-VLA), and primitive-action decomposition (CLIPort, PerAct, SUSIE). Its key conceptual departure is from prior world-model approaches that predict the full scene at every timestep; instead, PIVOT-R uses a VLM to parse a small set of 10 primitive actions as prompts and predicts only task-relevant waypoint features, decoupling high-level semantic planning from low-level action execution. The asynchronous hierarchical executor draws inspiration from MResT's multi-resolution scheduling but applies it across semantic modules (VLM parsing → scene prediction → action prediction) rather than spatial/temporal resolutions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 19.45% average improvement over the SoTA baseline (Surfer) on SeaWave, combined with a 28× inference speedup from asynchronous execution at only 2.9% performance cost, makes the waypoint-centric world model a practically attractive architecture for real-time manipulation. The finding that VLM choice (LLaVA vs. Qwen-VL vs. GPT-4) has negligible impact suggests the scene prediction module compensates for VLM limitations, reducing deployment cost. However, the paper acknowledges a critical open problem: inconsistency between high-level language instructions and executed primitive actions (e.g., 'push left' becoming 'push front'), and evaluation is limited to a single simulation benchmark plus three simple real-world tasks, leaving scalability to more complex, multi-object, long-horizon manipulation largely untested.
