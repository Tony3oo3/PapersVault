## Problem

In MaskDINO, the SOTA joint detection and segmentation model, object detection performance lags behind instance segmentation at the first transformer decoder layer, creating a detection-segmentation imbalance that constrains the upper bound of final performance. This imbalance arises because detection requires global patch-to-patch relations for bounding box regression while segmentation benefits from local pixel-level features, and because dense pixel-level supervision for segmentation provides stronger optimization signals than sparse 4D box supervision for detection. No prior work has explicitly identified or addressed this imbalance.

## Input / Output

The model takes a standard RGB image and produces bounding boxes, class labels, and binary instance masks simultaneously. The backbone extracts multi-scale features flattened into approximately 20k initial feature tokens, which are compressed to 300 balance-aware queries by the DI module. The segmentation head additionally consumes 1/4-resolution CNN features alongside the refined query and initial feature tokens to predict masks, while the detection head operates solely on the refined query.

## Method

DI-MaskDINO extends MaskDINO with two modules. The De-Imbalance (DI) module applies a residual double-selection mechanism: it first selects top-600 tokens by category score, applies two self-attention layers to learn global geometric and semantic patch-to-patch relations (critical for detection), then selects top-300 tokens, and finally fuses the selected tokens with the full initial feature set via Multi-Head Cross-Attention to produce a balance-aware query Q_bal. The Balance-Aware Tokens Optimization (BATO) module uses Q_bal to generate mask and box guiding tokens through separate MLP networks, fuses them additively, and applies cross-attention to optimize the ~20k initial feature tokens into T_bal, which serves as Key&Value for the transformer decoder. The key insight is that the token interaction step before the second selection specifically strengthens detection by enabling patches belonging to the same object to interact, compensating for the local-feature bias that favors segmentation at early decoder layers.

## Results

On COCO val2017 with ResNet50 backbone and 12 training epochs, DI-MaskDINO achieves 46.9 AP^box and 42.3 AP^mask, improving over MaskDINO by +1.2 AP^box and +0.9 AP^mask. With Swin-L backbone at 50 epochs, DI-MaskDINO reaches 57.8 AP^box and 51.8 AP^mask (+1.0 and +0.8 over MaskDINO). Notably, DI-MaskDINO surpasses the specialized SOTA detector DINO by +1.0 AP^box and the specialized SOTA segmenter Mask2Former by +3.0 AP^mask on COCO. On the more complex BDD100K dataset, DI-MaskDINO gains +1.4 AP^box and +0.4 AP^mask over MaskDINO (ResNet50, 68 epochs).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DI-MaskDINO consistently outperforms MaskDINO (the primary joint detection-segmentation baseline) across all backbone and epoch settings: +1.2 AP^box / +0.9 AP^mask at 12 epochs (ResNet50), +1.2 / +0.6 at 24 epochs, +0.2 / +0.4 at 50 epochs (ResNet50), and +1.0 / +0.8 at 50 epochs (Swin-L). Against the specialized SOTA detector DINO (50.9 AP^box, ResNet50, 36 epochs), DI-MaskDINO at 50 epochs achieves 51.9 AP^box (+1.0), and against the specialized SOTA segmenter Mask2Former (43.7 AP^mask, ResNet50, 50 epochs), DI-MaskDINO achieves 46.7 AP^mask (+3.0). On BDD100K, DI-MaskDINO also beats DINO by +0.6 AP^box (29.5 vs. 28.9, ResNet50), whereas MaskDINO actually underperforms DINO on this dataset. Compared to earlier joint models (Mask R-CNN, HTC, SOLQ), DI-MaskDINO leads by a wide margin (e.g., +10.9 AP^box over Mask R-CNN and +8.4 AP^box over SOLQ on COCO with ResNet50). The gains are most pronounced at shorter training schedules (12 epochs), suggesting DI-MaskDINO converges faster, and the FPS cost is minimal (7.7 vs. 8.0 for MaskDINO with ResNet50).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on MaskDINO, which itself extends DINO (a DETR variant with improved denoising anchor boxes) by adding a mask prediction branch for joint detection and segmentation. The key departure is the identification of a detection-segmentation performance imbalance at the first transformer decoder layer, which the authors argue no prior joint detection-segmentation work has explicitly addressed. The proposed DI module (residual double-selection with self-attention token interaction) and BATO module (cross-attention-guided feature token optimization) are inserted between the encoder output and the transformer decoder, modifying how queries and key-value tokens are constructed rather than altering the decoder or prediction heads themselves.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a useful diagnostic lens on multi-task transformer models: the observation that dense pixel-level supervision (segmentation) and sparse 4D box supervision (detection) create asynchronous optimization speeds, and that this imbalance at early decoder layers constrains the final performance ceiling. The imbalance tolerance experiments (Table 3) offer a practical tool for diagnosing similar issues in other multi-task architectures. However, the method is limited to joint detection + instance segmentation and does not extend to semantic or panoptic segmentation, and the improvements (+1.2 AP^box, +0.9 AP^mask) are modest. An open question is whether the imbalance phenomenon and the double-selection remedy generalize to other multi-task settings (e.g., detection + depth, or detection + keypoint estimation) where supervision densities differ.
