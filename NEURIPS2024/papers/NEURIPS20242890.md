## Problem

While learning symmetries from data has been explored extensively in discriminative settings—where labels are invariant and symmetry information can simply be removed—generative models must explicitly capture the factors of variation corresponding to symmetry transformations. No prior generative model has been designed to learn, in an unsupervised manner, the distribution over naturally occurring symmetry transformations present in a dataset, leaving a gap in how generative models can exploit symmetry structure for data efficiency and interpretability.

## Input / Output

The method takes an image observation x (grayscale or RGB, e.g., 28×28 MNIST digits or 64×64 galaxy images) and a prespecified group of transformations {T_η} (affine: x-shift, y-shift, rotation, x-scale, y-scale; or color: hue, saturation, value) as input. It produces an invariant prototype x̂ (a canonical image with all symmetry transformations removed), the inferred transformation parameters η, and a learned conditional distribution p_ψ(η|x̂) parameterized by a Neural Spline Flow. Crucially, the model does not learn p(x̂), so it can operate without a full generative model over prototypes, and it can resample new 'naturally' augmented examples by drawing η from p_ψ(η|x̂) and applying T_η to x̂.

## Method

The SGM decomposes p(x) into a distribution over prototypes p(x̂) and a distribution over transformation parameters p_ψ(η|x̂), with x = T_η(x̂). Learning proceeds in two stages: first, a self-supervised objective (inspired by BYOL/BINCE) trains an inference network f_ω(x) to output transformation parameters η such that T_{f_ω(x)}^{-1}(x) is invariant to all transformations in the orbit, using a composed-transformation MSE loss to minimize discretization artifacts; second, a Neural Spline Flow is trained by maximum likelihood on the (η, x̂) pairs to model p_ψ(η|x̂). The key design choices are: (1) the prototype is fully invariant (one unique prototype per orbit), not partially invariant, to ensure p_ψ(η|x̂) represents the true distribution of natural transformations; (2) η is conditioned on x̂ so different prototypes can have different transformation distributions (e.g., an '8' allows ±180° rotation while a '2' does not); and (3) the flow provides the flexibility to capture multi-modal transformation distributions. When combined with a VAE, the SGM enables data-efficient training by either resampling natural augmentations (AugVAE) or feeding only invariant prototypes to the VAE (InvVAE).

## Results

On rotated MNIST, InvVAE (which feeds SGM prototypes to a VAE) achieves approximately 500 nats higher importance-weighted lower bound (IWLB) than all other models, and its performance changes by less than 10% when trained on only half the data. AugVAE (which uses SGM to resample natural augmentations at training time) is robust to reducing training observations by three quarters and shows significantly smaller performance degradation under larger random rotations compared to a standard VAE. On GalaxyMNIST with both affine and color transformations, AugVAE outperforms the standard VAE and remains robust when trained on only half the dataset, with the SGM capturing the true data distribution from as few as 3,500 training examples.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares four VAE variants on rotated MNIST and GalaxyMNIST: a standard VAE, a VAE with standard (independent) data augmentation, AugVAE (SGM-guided augmentation at training time), and InvVAE (SGM prototypes at both train and test time). InvVAE dominates all baselines in absolute IWLB (~500 nats higher) and is nearly immune to both reduced data and added rotation. AugVAE consistently outperforms the standard VAE, particularly in low-data regimes, and unlike standard data augmentation, its performance does not degrade with more training data (standard augmentation actually hurts performance at larger dataset sizes because augmentations are independent of the sample). The standard VAE degrades monotonically as training data decreases or rotation increases, since it sees fewer examples per degree of rotation. On GalaxyMNIST, the same pattern holds: AugVAE beats the standard VAE and is robust to halving the training set, while the SGM captures the full transformation distribution from only 3,500 examples.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant line of work on learning symmetries from data, which has focused almost exclusively on the discriminative setting (e.g., van der Wilk et al. 2018, Benton et al. 2020, van der Ouderaa and van der Wilk 2022), where symmetry information is removed to produce invariant representations. The SGM instead takes a generative perspective: rather than discarding symmetry factors, it explicitly models the distribution over transformations present in the data using a normalizing flow conditioned on an invariant prototype. The self-supervised training of the prototype inference network draws directly on BINCE (Dubois et al. 2021) and BYOL (Grill et al. 2020), while the decision to avoid learning p(x̂) follows the same practical observation made in that compression literature. Prior symmetry-aware latent-space work (Winter et al. 2022, Shu et al. 2018) uses geometric architectures or exact reconstruction, whereas SGM uses a flexible flow-based generative model that can sample new 'natural' augmentations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The SGM provides the first principled generative framework for learning which symmetries are actually present in a dataset and to what degree, enabling interpretable probing of natural variation (e.g., bimodal rotation distributions for '8's) and data augmentation that respects the true data distribution. The practical impact is demonstrated through a 500-nat likelihood improvement and robustness to halving the training set, but the main limitation is the requirement to prespecify a superset of possible symmetries, which constrains applicability to settings where the transformation group is known a priori. Open questions include whether this requirement can be relaxed, how the method scales to larger or more complex transformation groups, and whether the SGM-based augmentation strategy transfers to discriminative settings where it could complement methods like Benton et al. (2020) or Miao et al. (2023).
