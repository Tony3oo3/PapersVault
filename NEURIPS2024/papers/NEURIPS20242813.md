## Problem

Research on implicit neural representations (INRs) has been constrained by the absence of large-scale, high-quality INR datasets, primarily because training individual INRs is computationally expensive. Existing INR datasets are limited in both scale and application diversity, and transformer-based methods on INRs lack benchmarks for tasks like 3D pose regression from neural radiance fields. Additionally, standard fixed patch-based tokenization in Vision Transformers is suboptimal when the input is a continuous function rather than a discrete pixel grid.

## Input / Output

For 2D tasks, the input is a trained SIREN MLP (3–5 layers, 64–256 width) that maps 2D coordinates in [-1,1] to normalized RGB values; for 3D pose regression, the input is a trained NeRF (4 layers, 128 width) queried over a 32×32×32 volume to obtain RGB and density, concatenated with a 2D image. Outputs are class labels (10 or 100 classes), dense pixel-wise segmentation maps (Cityscapes 19 classes), or 6D camera pose parameters (translation in cm + rotation in degrees). A key distinction from standard image pipelines is that the 'image' is never materialized as a pixel grid—coordinates are queried on-the-fly from the frozen INR, and the learnable tokenizer jointly optimizes which coordinates to sample.

## Method

The paper contributes three interlocking components. First, it constructs the Implicit-Zoo dataset (1.5M+ INRs across CIFAR-10, ImageNet-1K, Cityscapes, and OmniObject3D) over ~1000 GPU days, enforcing a PSNR ≥ 30 quality threshold through iterative re-training and filtering. Second, it proposes a learnable tokenizer that replaces fixed patch coordinates with differentiable, jointly-optimized sampling locations queried from the frozen INR; variants include learnable scaling, learnable centers, and fully learnable pixel coordinates, with an L1 non-closure regularization (Eq. 1) to prevent coordinate collapse. Differentiable data augmentation is implemented in weight-space (geometric transforms modify first-layer W and b) and RGB-space (color transforms) to preserve gradient flow. Third, for 3D pose regression, a transformer encoder processes 3D volume tokens (pretrained via a masked-autoencoder scheme following MAE) alongside 2D image features to directly regress coarse 6D pose, followed by photometric refinement via gradient descent on the frozen NeRF.

## Results

On CIFAR-10-INRs classification, the proposed learnable tokenizer (ViT+LP+Reg) achieves 81.57% accuracy, outperforming the standard ViT baseline (80.82%) by 0.75%. On Cityscapes-INRs semantic segmentation, MiT-B0+LP+Reg reaches 40.61% mIOU (fine) versus 39.95% for the baseline. For the novel 3D pose regression benchmark on OmniObject3D, the best configuration (Ours+Pre+LP) achieves a rotational error of 20.02° on unseen scenes, with 79.75% of poses below 30° error and 8.09° after photometric refinement. The dataset itself comprises over 1.5 million INRs trained over ~1000 GPU days, with PSNR ≥ 30 dB across all entries.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the ViT baseline [18], the proposed learnable tokenizer with regularization (LP+Reg) improves CIFAR-10 accuracy by 0.75% (81.57% vs. 80.82%) and ImageNet-100 accuracy by 0.11% (84.92% vs. 84.81%), while the simpler Learnable Centers variant (LC) gains 0.51% on CIFAR-10. For CNN architectures, VGG19 [68] improves from 82.06% to 82.28% and ResNet18 [69] from 83.34% to 83.57% with the same LP+Reg strategy, confirming generalizability beyond transformers. In segmentation, MiT-B0 (SegFormer) [19] gains 0.66% mIOU on Cityscapes fine annotations (40.61% vs. 39.95%) with LP+Reg. Weight-space-only methods DWSNet [11] (38.12%) and HyperRepresentation [82] (63.14%) perform substantially worse than RGB-space INR querying (81.57%), highlighting the advantage of the proposed dataset's coordinate-based access. The random-initialization variant (LP+rand) collapses to 59.43%, confirming that preserving local geometry via grid initialization is critical.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the INR dataset line of work (Navon et al. 2023, De Luigi et al. 2023, Zama Ramirez et al. 2023) by scaling from thousands to over 1.5 million implicit functions across 2D (SIREN) and 3D (NeRF) representations, addressing the computational bottleneck that limited prior efforts. The learnable tokenizer builds on T2T-ViT's progressive tokenization and MSViT's dynamic mixed-scale schemes, but departs by making spatial coordinates themselves differentiable through frozen INR queries, enabling joint optimization with transformer modules. For 3D pose regression, the paper moves beyond per-scene optimization methods (iNeRF, Loc-NeRF, LENS) that require coarse initialization or scene-dependent regressors, instead training a generalizable transformer that samples volume features from pretrained NeRFs. The differentiable weight-space augmentation strategy is a novel implementation detail that enables gradient flow through geometric transforms without retraining INRs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dataset provides the first infrastructure for training transformer-scale models directly on implicit representations, opening a research direction (learnable tokenization) that could reshape how ViTs interface with continuous signals. However, scalability remains a significant limitation: INR querying constrains batch sizes and model complexity, preventing from-scratch training of large models, and the PSNR-30 threshold may introduce artifacts in repetitive backgrounds. The 3D pose regression benchmark is the first of its kind but struggles with symmetric objects, and the modest performance gains (0.5–0.75% on CIFAR-10) suggest the learnable tokenizer's full potential may require larger-scale training that current INR querying cannot support. Open questions include whether the approach transfers to higher-fidelity INRs (e.g., Instant-NGP) and whether the tokenizer insights generalize beyond the specific architectures tested.
