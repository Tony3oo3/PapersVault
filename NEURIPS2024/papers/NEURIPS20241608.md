## Problem

Continual Generalized Category Discovery (C-GCD) requires incrementally discovering new classes from unlabeled data while retaining previously learned classes, but prior settings are unrealistic (few stages, stored exemplars, known old/new sample ratios). The paper identifies two concrete failure modes that prior work overlooks: prediction bias, where the model's overconfidence in supervised old classes collapses new-class accuracy, and hardness bias, where classes with higher mutual similarity suffer disproportionate forgetting, leading to unstable performance across stages.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241608/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At Stage-0 the model receives labeled RGB images from initial classes (e.g., 50 of 100 classes in CIFAR-100); at each subsequent stage it receives only unlabeled images mixing old and new classes, with old-class samples being fewer per class than new-class samples and the ratio unknown. The model (ViT-B/16 pretrained with DINO, fine-tuning only the last transformer block) outputs a probability vector over all seen classes via an ℓ₂-normalized prototypical classifier in a 768-dim hyperspherical feature space. Evaluation uses the Hungarian algorithm to find the optimal permutation between predicted and ground-truth labels, reporting separate accuracies for old, new, and all classes.

## Method

Happy is a debiased learning framework with two complementary mechanisms. To counter prediction bias, it initializes new-class heads via KMeans centroids least similar to existing old heads (clustering-guided initialization) and applies group-wise soft entropy regularization that treats all old classes as one group and all new classes as another, forcing the model to allocate sufficient probability mass to new classes without requiring knowledge of the old/new sample ratio. To counter hardness bias and catastrophic forgetting in a rehearsal-free setting, it models each learned class as a Gaussian in feature space, computes a per-class hardness score from inter-prototype cosine similarity, and samples prototypes preferentially from harder classes for a classification loss, supplemented by knowledge distillation from the previous stage's model. The overall objective combines self-distillation for new-class discovery, hardness-aware prototype sampling plus distillation for old-class retention, and self-supervised contrastive learning, all trained with a single unified loss per stage.

## Results

On ImageNet-100 (5-stage C-GCD), Happy achieves 78.58% overall accuracy at Stage-5, surpassing the previous best (GM†) by 7.50% and MetaGCD by 11.90%. On CIFAR-100, Happy reaches 59.99% overall accuracy at Stage-5 (vs. 54.11% for GM† and 55.78% for MetaGCD), with a particularly large gap on new-class accuracy (51.30% vs. 48.40% for GM† and 31.60% for MetaGCD). On Tiny-ImageNet, Happy attains 54.56% overall accuracy at Stage-5, outperforming GM† (46.90%) by 7.66%. The method also achieves the best forgetting metric (M_f = 11.22 on C100, 9.75 on Tiny) while simultaneously achieving the highest discovery metric (M_d = 51.36 on C100, 43.38 on Tiny).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Happy outperforms all seven baselines on overall accuracy at Stage-5 across CIFAR-100, ImageNet-100, and Tiny-ImageNet. The largest gains are over MetaGCD (11.90% on IN100, 4.21% on C100) and GM† (7.50% on IN100, 5.88% on C100), which are the strongest prior continual methods. On new-class discovery specifically, Happy's advantage is most dramatic: 73.80% vs. 65.00% (GM†) and 43.00% (MetaGCD) on IN100, and 51.30% vs. 31.60% (MetaGCD) on C100. Static GCD baselines (SimGCD, SimGCD+LwF) suffer severe new-class collapse (4.60% and 19.80% on C100) because they lack any continual-learning mechanism. FRoST, designed for continual novel class discovery, achieves reasonable new-class accuracy but at the cost of old-class forgetting (48.17% old on C100 vs. 60.96% for Happy). On CUB, Happy ties MetaGCD at Stage-5 (49.60% All) but leads substantially at earlier stages (82.10% vs. 67.08% at Stage-1), suggesting the paper's CUB Stage-2+ numbers may contain a reporting artifact.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Continual Generalized Category Discovery (C-GCD) from prior works like GM (Zhang et al., NeurIPS 2022) and MetaGCD (Wu et al., ICCV 2023) to a more realistic setting with more stages, rehearsal-free learning, and unknown old-sample ratios. Methodologically, it builds on the parametric self-training framework of SimGCD (Wen et al., ICCV 2023) and the GCD formulation of Vaze et al. (CVPR 2022), but departs from prior C-GCD methods by replacing stored exemplars with Gaussian prototype modeling and introducing a hardness-aware sampling criterion. The key conceptual departure is the explicit diagnosis of prediction bias (overconfidence in old classes) and hardness bias (uneven class difficulty) as the root causes of the new-vs-old conflict, rather than treating forgetting and discovery as separate engineering problems.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap: prior C-GCD methods either store old samples (privacy/storage issues), assume known old-sample ratios, or test only 2–3 stages, none of which reflect real-world lifelong learning. The finding that injecting ground-truth class-ratio priors actually degrades performance (Section 5.4) is a useful caution against over-parameterizing the learning objective with imperfect information. Open questions remain around confidence calibration between old and new classes, robust unsupervised class-number estimation, and extension beyond classification to detection, segmentation, and multi-modal settings.
