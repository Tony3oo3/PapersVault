## Problem

MMLU has reached performance saturation, with frontier models (GPT-4o, Claude-3-Opus, Gemini-1.5-Pro) all clustering at 86–87% accuracy, making it nearly impossible to differentiate between them. Beyond saturation, MMLU scores are highly sensitive to prompt phrasing (4–5% variance across 24 prompt styles), and its questions are predominantly knowledge-recall rather than reasoning-intensive, so models can exploit shortcuts without genuine understanding.

## Input / Output

Each evaluation item is a text question with ten lettered options (A–J), triple the distractors of MMLU's four-option format, preceded by five discipline-specific Chain-of-Thought demonstration examples drawn from a held-out validation subset. The model produces a free-form reasoning chain ending in a letter answer, which is extracted via a two-stage regex pipeline (primary: 'answer is (X)'; secondary: 'Answer: X'), falling back to a random option if both patterns fail. The final output is a per-discipline and overall accuracy percentage across 12,032 questions spanning 14 domains.

## Method

The dataset is built through a four-stage pipeline: (1) filtering 5,886 'too easy' questions from MMLU by excluding items answered correctly by more than 4 of 8 reference models; (2) integrating new questions from STEM Website, TheoremQA, and SciBench, converting them to multiple-choice format via GPT-4-Turbo; (3) augmenting all questions from four to ten options using GPT-4-Turbo to generate six plausible distractors, cutting the chance-guessing rate from 25% to 10%; and (4) conducting two rounds of expert review—human verification of answer correctness plus Gemini-1.5-Pro-based identification of false-negative distractors for targeted human re-checking. Evaluation uses 5-shot CoT prompting adapted from Chain-of-Thought Hub with discipline-specific demonstrations. A key empirical finding is that CoT yields dramatically larger gains on MMLU-Pro (e.g., +19.1% for GPT-4o) than on MMLU (+1.5%), confirming the benchmark's shift toward reasoning-intensive tasks, while prompt-sensitivity drops from 4–5% to ~2%.

## Results

On MMLU-Pro (5-shot CoT), GPT-4o achieves the highest overall accuracy at 72.6%, followed by Gemini-1.5-Pro (69.0%), Claude-3-Opus (68.5%), and GPT-4-Turbo (63.7%). Among open-source models, Llama-3-70B-Instruct leads at 56.2%, while the weakest model (Gemma-2B) scores only 15.9%. Chain-of-Thought prompting provides a substantial boost on MMLU-Pro: GPT-4o gains 19.1% over direct answering (72.6% vs. 53.5%), and GPT-4-Turbo gains 15.3% (63.7% vs. 48.4%), in stark contrast to MMLU where CoT offers negligible or negative gains.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is MMLU-Pro against the original MMLU benchmark. On MMLU, top models cluster tightly: GPT-4o (88.7%), GPT-4-Turbo (86.5%), and Gemini-1.5-Flash/Llama-3-70B-Instruct/Phi-3/Qwen1.5-110B all fall within a narrow 78–82% range, making differentiation difficult. On MMLU-Pro, the same models spread across a ~10% range, and the gap between GPT-4o and GPT-4-Turbo widens from ~1% (MMLU) to 9% (MMLU-Pro). CoT prompting, which slightly hurts or has no effect on MMLU (e.g., GPT-4-Turbo: −0.2%), yields large gains on MMLU-Pro (GPT-4o: +19.1%, GPT-4-Turbo: +15.3%), confirming that MMLU-Pro demands genuine multi-step reasoning. Prompt sensitivity also drops substantially: score variation across 24 prompt styles is 4–5% (up to 10.98%) on MMLU versus ~2% (up to 3.74%) on MMLU-Pro, making MMLU-Pro a more stable and discriminative evaluation.

## Relation to Prior Work

MMLU-Pro directly extends the MMLU benchmark (Hendrycks et al., 2020) by addressing three identified weaknesses: insufficient distractors (4→10 options), over-reliance on knowledge recall over reasoning, and dataset noise. The construction pipeline draws on TheoremQA and SciBench for reasoning-intensive questions and adapts the Chain-of-Thought Hub (Fu et al., 2023) prompting framework for evaluation. Conceptually, the paper positions itself within the broader trend of LLM evaluation benchmarks (GLUE, SuperGLUE, BigBench, ARC) that have progressively faced saturation, and specifically responds to prior observations of prompt sensitivity and leaderboard instability in multiple-choice evaluation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As MMLU has saturated at ~87% for frontier models, MMLU-Pro provides a necessary successor that differentiates models (9% gap between GPT-4o and GPT-4-Turbo vs. 1% on MMLU) and leaves 27% headroom for future progress. The finding that CoT dramatically helps on MMLU-Pro (+19% for GPT-4o) while hurting on MMLU is a useful diagnostic: it confirms the benchmark genuinely tests reasoning rather than knowledge retrieval. Limitations include the continued reliance on multiple-choice format (which may not capture open-ended reasoning), exclusion of multimodal evaluation, and the risk that this benchmark will also eventually saturate as models improve.
