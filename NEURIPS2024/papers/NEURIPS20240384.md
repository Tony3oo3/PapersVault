## Problem

Existing differentiable mesh representations either fix topology to a template mesh or rely on implicit functions followed by iso-surface extraction, which produces unnecessarily dense meshes with geometric misalignment at sharp regions. The fundamental challenge is formulating both vertex geometry and discrete connectivity (edges, faces) in a fully differentiable way that allows topology to change during gradient-based optimization.

## Input / Output

The method accepts oriented point clouds (100K sampled points, optionally with normals), multi-view images (64 views of diffuse and depth renderings), or ground truth triangular meshes as input. Each vertex is represented as a 5-dimensional feature vector (3D position, WDT weight, and a scalar 'real' value ψ ∈ [0,1]). The output is a 3D triangular mesh where faces are selected from a WDT tessellation by thresholding their existence probability Λ(F) > 0.5, yielding compact meshes (typically 3–10K vertices) that handle open, closed, mixed, and non-orientable surfaces.

## Method

DMesh tessellates a convex domain into tetrahedra via Weighted Delaunay Triangulation (WDT), then defines the mesh as a subset of triangular faces ('real part') selected from the tetrahedra, with the remainder serving as structural support ('imaginary part'). Face existence probability is factored as Λ(F) = Λ_wdt(F) · Λ_real(F), where Λ_wdt is computed efficiently (O(N) vs. O(N²) prior work) using the dual power diagram and signed distances to reduced power cells, and Λ_real is a soft-min of per-vertex ψ values. Optimization proceeds in two phases per epoch: Phase 1 updates all point attributes using a tile-based semi-transparent renderer (adapted from 3D Gaussian Splatting) combined with a visibility-gradient renderer, while Phase 2 fixes positions and weights and refines only real values using a tessellation-traversal renderer with exact depth testing. Three regularizations—weight (reduces WDT complexity for mesh simplification), real (enforces spatial coherence of ψ to remove holes), and quality (minimizes expected triangle aspect ratio)—are combined with reconstruction losses (Chamfer Distance for point clouds, L1 image loss for multi-view) in a single differentiable objective optimized via Adam.

## Results

For point cloud reconstruction, DMesh (with normals) achieves the best Chamfer Distance (3.364×10⁻⁵), F-Score (0.886), Normal Consistency (0.952), and Edge F-Score (0.438) among all methods, while producing dramatically fewer vertices (3.56K) and faces (7.54K) than competitors (e.g., NDC uses 20.7K/42.8K, PSR uses 159K/319K). For multi-view reconstruction, DMesh achieves the best CD (34.6×10⁻⁵), F1 (0.685), and NC (0.892) with only 4.19K vertices and 8.80K faces, compared to FlexiCube (10.9K/21.9K) and NIE (74.5K/149K). In the mesh-to-DMesh conversion experiment, over 99% of ground-truth faces are recovered with under 1% false positives across Bunny, Dragon, and Buddha models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

In point cloud reconstruction, DMesh (with normals) outperforms NDC on CD (3.364 vs 3.611), F1 (0.886 vs 0.874), NC (0.952 vs 0.936), and EF1 (0.438 vs 0.421), while producing 5.8× fewer vertices (3.56K vs 20.7K) and 5.7× fewer faces (7.54K vs 42.8K); however, NDC achieves a lower ECD (0.022 vs 0.141), reflecting DMesh's residual non-manifold edge issues. PSR and VoroMesh are dominated on all accuracy metrics and produce meshes 30–40× denser. In multi-view reconstruction, DMesh substantially outperforms FlexiCube on CD (34.6 vs 273), F1 (0.685 vs 0.591), and NC (0.892 vs 0.881), and produces 2.6× fewer vertices (4.19K vs 10.9K); it also far surpasses NIE on all accuracy metrics (CD 34.6 vs 585, F1 0.685 vs 0.439). The main trade-off is that DMesh is slower than NDC (743s vs 3.49s for PC) and FlexiCube (1434s vs 56.47s for MV) due to per-step WDT construction, and it loses to FlexiCube on ECD (0.094 vs 0.039) and EF1 (0.113 vs 0.152) for multi-view tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

DMesh extends the 2D differentiable Weighted Delaunay Triangulation framework of Rakotosaona et al. (2021) to 3D, replacing their O(N²) exhaustive power-cell evaluation with an O(N) algorithm that leverages a precomputed Power Diagram to obtain reliable lower bounds on simplex existence. It also builds conceptually on VoroMesh (Maruani et al., 2023), which used Voronoi diagrams for point cloud reconstruction, but departs from it by supporting open and non-orientable surfaces through a per-point 'real value' mechanism that selects a subset of WDT faces. Unlike the dominant paradigm of neural implicit functions (SDF/UDF) followed by iso-surface extraction, DMesh directly parameterizes mesh connectivity as differentiable face-existence probabilities, avoiding the dense, grid-aligned meshes that iso-surface methods produce.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DMesh is the first representation that makes both vertex positions and mesh topology simultaneously differentiable without assuming a fixed template or a volumetric intermediate, enabling gradient-based optimization of arbitrary-topology meshes (open, closed, non-orientable, varying genus) from point clouds or multi-view images. Its practical advantage is mesh compactness—reconstructed meshes use 3–4K vertices versus 10K–159K for baselines—making outputs more suitable for downstream animation and simulation. Key open questions include scaling beyond ~20K vertices (WDT construction is the bottleneck), eliminating the 5–7% non-manifold edge rate, and whether the representation can be extended to real-world image reconstruction or generative 3D modeling.
