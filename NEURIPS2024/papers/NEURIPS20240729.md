## Problem

Generative MLLMs suffer from hallucinations and weak object discrimination, while discriminative models like CLIP struggle with complex interleaved sequences and fine-grained semantic differentiation. The paper identifies two root causes for generative models' failure at discriminative tasks: a U-shaped attention bias that prevents comprehensive capture of global semantics, and an inability to keenly differentiate detailed semantics between similar inputs. Prior attempts to add discriminative ability to generative MLLMs either compromise generative performance or generalize poorly.

## Input / Output

The method treats interleaved image-text sequences as the general input format, where images (encoded by CLIP ViT-L/14 at 336px) and text are alternately arranged and processed by a Vicuna-7B LLM. For generative tasks, the model produces a text answer via autoregressive decoding; for discriminative tasks, it outputs a similarity score computed by the Global Alignment Kernel over the MLLM's hidden states, with the last token (or average-pooled image tokens) mapped through an MLP to represent the full in-context sequence. The same model can also perform retrieval-augmented generation, using its own discriminative scores to retrieve relevant knowledge from a knowledge base and condition generation on it.

## Method

Sugar introduces a structure-induced discriminative loss L_d that explicitly imposes semantic relationships between input samples as a constraint on the MLLM's hidden states, combined with the standard autoregressive generative loss L_g (total loss L = L_g + α·L_d). The core mechanism formulates the similarity between two interleaved sequences as a Dynamic Sequence Alignment problem solved by the Global Alignment Kernel (GAK), a sum-product variant of DTW that computes a positive-definite kernel over variable-length sequences of slice embeddings. To capture fine-grained semantics, a Triple Kernel concatenates DINOv2-base and CLIP ViT-L/14 embeddings for images (and BGE-base and CLIP for text), selecting the appropriate sub-embedding based on whether the comparison is uni-modal or cross-modal. The discriminative loss is an MSE between the GAK-computed similarity matrix and a label matrix, requiring no pre-defined positive/negative pairs and enabling multiple samples to be drawn from the same sequence. The approach is implemented by fine-tuning VILA-7B with LoRA (rank 128) on 8×A800 GPUs for ~12 hours.

## Results

On 11 visual-language benchmarks, Sugar (built on VILA-7B with LoRA fine-tuning) achieves state-of-the-art results on 5 benchmarks and second-best on 3, with standout improvements on fine-grained and discrimination tasks: LLaVA-Bench (In-the-Wild) 75.6 vs. VILA's 70.0 (+8%), VizWiz 60.4 vs. 57.8 (+4.5%), and POPE hallucination detection 86.6 vs. 85.5. On the DEMON benchmark (29 sub-tasks), Sugar surpasses the previous SOTA (VPG-C) in 6 of 7 categories, including +36.1% on Text-Rich Images QA and +17.2% on Visual Relation Inference. For retrieval, Sugar achieves R@5 of 46.7 on VIST interleaved retrieval (5c+4i setting), outperforming FROMAGe (42.7) and CLIP (21.3), and dominates Winoground fine-grained retrieval with 36.3% Image accuracy vs. 20.5% for the best discriminative baseline (FLAVA_ITM).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against VILA-7B (the base model), Sugar wins on 5 of 11 benchmarks (VizWiz +4.5%, SQA-I +1.8%, POPE +1.1%, MME-P +17.8, LLaVA-Wd +8%) but loses on VQA-v2 (−3.9), GQA (−3.6), VQA-T (−6.9), MMB (−4.0), and MM-Vet (−3.6), indicating the discriminative loss trades some general generative performance for stronger discrimination. On DEMON, Sugar outperforms VPG-C (previous SOTA) in 6/7 categories, with the largest gains on TRQA (+36.1%) and VRI (+17.2%), while VILA-7B itself lags behind VPG-C on most categories. For interleaved retrieval on VIST (5c+4i), Sugar achieves R@1 21.9 / R@5 46.7 / R@10 59.2, surpassing FROMAGe (18.2/42.7/51.8) and CLIP (2.4/21.3/34.0) by large margins. On Winoground fine-grained retrieval, Sugar (Text 40.0, Image 36.3, Group 27.0) outperforms all discriminative baselines including FLAVA_ITM (32.3/20.5/14.3) and CLIP ViT-B/32 (30.8/10.5/8.0). In the RAG setting, Sugar+RAG improves VizWiz by +2.5% and SQA-I by +3.6%, whereas VILA+CLIP degrades VizWiz by −14.7%, demonstrating that Sugar's built-in retrieval is more compatible than external CLIP/BLIP-2 retrievers.

## Relation to Prior Work

Sugar builds on VILA as its base MLLM and repurposes the Global Alignment Kernel (Cuturi et al., 2007) — originally designed for time-series similarity — as a structural constraint on MLLM hidden states, framing pairwise sample relationships as a Dynamic Time Warping problem over interleaved image-text sequences. This departs from prior attempts to inject discriminative ability into generative MLLMs (e.g., FROMAGe, which uses simple contrastive losses) by explicitly modeling sequence-level alignment rather than pointwise similarity. The Triple Kernel further extends the framework by concatenating embeddings from DINOv2, CLIP, and BGE, leveraging each model's complementary strengths for fine-grained semantic discrimination within a single positive-definite kernel.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By unifying generative and discriminative objectives in a single training loop, Sugar eliminates the need for a separate retrieval module in RAG pipelines, sidestepping the well-known component-optimization dilemma where a suboptimal retriever degrades the generator. The approach also reveals a diagnostic 'U-shape' positional bias in causal MLLMs and demonstrates that discriminative constraints measurably reduce hallucinations (POPE) and improve fine-grained discrimination (Winoground, MMVP-VLM). Open questions include whether the DTW-based alignment scales efficiently to very long interleaved sequences, whether the Triple Kernel's reliance on three frozen encoders limits adaptability, and whether the synergistic gains persist when the base MLLM is scaled beyond 7B parameters.
