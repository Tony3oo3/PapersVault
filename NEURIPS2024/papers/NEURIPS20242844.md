## Problem

The Iterative Markovian Fitting (IMF) procedure for solving the Schrödinger Bridge problem requires learning continuous-time SDEs, which necessitates hundreds of numerical solver steps at inference time. This paper asks whether the SB problem can be solved in discrete time, reducing inference to just a few evaluation steps while preserving the same theoretical guarantees.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242844/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a source image (e.g., a 128×128 male face from CelebA) and produces a translated target image (e.g., a female face) using only 4 function evaluations (NFE) at inference, compared to 100+ for continuous-time IMF. The method operates on unpaired data with no source-target correspondence, and the number of inference steps is determined by the number of intermediate time moments N (here N=3, giving N+1=4 steps). The approach is general and applies to any pair of distributions accessible by samples, not limited to images.

## Method

The paper introduces the Discrete-time Iterative Markovian Fitting (D-IMF) procedure, which alternates between discrete Markovian and reciprocal projections of stochastic processes at N intermediate time points, and proves convergence to the Schrödinger Bridge solution. The reciprocal projection is trivial: sample endpoint pairs (x₀, x₁) from the current joint distribution, then sample intermediate points from the discrete Brownian bridge. The Markovian projection is the key practical challenge: it requires learning transition probabilities q(x_{t_n}|x_{t_{n-1)}) for each consecutive time step, which the authors implement via DD-GAN (Denoising Diffusion GAN) using time-conditioned generator and discriminator networks with non-saturating GAN loss. To handle the asymmetry in the Markovian projection definition, they alternately learn forward (p₀→p₁) and reverse (p₁→p₀) transition models across outer iterations. The main theoretical result (Theorem 3.1) shows that any discrete process that is simultaneously Markovian and reciprocal must be the finite-dimensional projection of the SB solution, providing the fixed-point characterization that drives the iterative procedure.

## Results

On Celeba male→female translation (128×128), ASBM achieves FID=17.44 with only 4 function evaluations (NFE) at ε=10, compared to DSBM's FID=89.19 at 100 NFE under the same setting. At ε=1, DSBM achieves FID=37.8 with 100 NFE. In the Gaussian-to-Gaussian SB experiment (D=16), D-IMF exhibits exponential convergence, with the convergence rate saturating quickly in the number of intermediate time steps N (N=5 suffices) and being highly sensitive to ε (going from ε=1 to ε=10 requires ~10× more iterations).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's sole direct baseline is DSBM (Diffusion Schrödinger Bridge Matching) [Shi et al., NeurIPS 2023], which implements the continuous-time IMF procedure via Bridge Matching (learning an SDE). ASBM decisively outperforms DSBM on Celeba male→female: at ε=10, ASBM achieves FID=17.44 versus DSBM's FID=89.19 — a roughly 5× improvement in FID — while requiring only 4 NFE compared to DSBM's 100 NFE (a 25× reduction in inference steps). At ε=1, DSBM reports FID=37.8 with 100 NFE; the paper's qualitative results suggest ASBM is competitive or better in this regime as well. The key practical takeaway is that ASBM matches or exceeds DSBM's generation quality while being an order of magnitude faster at inference, directly addressing the long-inference limitation of continuous-time IMF.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper introduces a discrete-time reformulation of the Iterative Markovian Fitting (IMF) procedure from Shi et al. (DSBM, NeurIPS 2023) and Peluchetti (JMLR 2023), replacing continuous-time SDE learning with a small number of discrete transition probabilities. The key theoretical departure is proving that a discrete process that is simultaneously Markovian and reciprocal (with respect to a discrete Brownian bridge) is the finite-dimensional projection of the Schrödinger Bridge, mirroring the continuous-time uniqueness result. Practically, the Markovian projection is implemented via DD-GAN (Xiao et al., ICLR 2022) rather than bridge matching, inheriting the adversarial training machinery already validated in that framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 25× inference speedup (4 NFE vs. 100 NFE) with comparable or better FID on CelebA makes Schrödinger Bridge-based translation practically viable for settings where iterative SDE sampling is prohibitive. More broadly, the discrete formulation decouples the Markovian projection from SDE learning, opening the door to plugging in any generative modeling technique (normalizing flows, energy-based models, score-based models) for the transition probabilities. Open questions include the lack of a proven convergence rate (empirically exponential but unproven), the single-dataset validation at 128×128 resolution, and whether adversarial training stability holds at scale.
