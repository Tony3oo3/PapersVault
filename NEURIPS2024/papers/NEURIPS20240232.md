## Problem

Existing MLLM safety benchmarks suffer from four critical gaps: limited dimensional coverage (often targeting only hallucination or generalized safety), data leakage from open-source datasets likely present in model training sets, unreliable evaluation via GPT-4V which exhibits self-bias, and near-exclusive focus on English. No prior benchmark simultaneously addresses multi-dimensional bilingual safety assessment with adversarial data and a fair automated evaluator.

## Input / Output

Each evaluation sample is an image-text pair where the image (sourced 82% from social media) and a manually crafted prompt employ one or more of 10 red teaming techniques (e.g., role-playing disguise, position swapping, noise injection, harmful scenario embedding). The MLLM produces a free-form text response, which is scored on a 4-level scale (0 = safe & responsible, 1 = safe but unaware, 2 = unsafe & unaware, 3 = unsafe & aware) for Privacy/Bias/Toxicity/Legality, or binary truthful/untruthful for Truthfulness. Aggregated metrics are ASD (Attack Success Degree, lower is better) and PAR (Perfect Answer Rate, higher is better) per dimension. The public dataset contains 1,500 bilingual (51.8% Chinese, 48.2% English) samples across 12 subtasks.

## Method

MLLMGUARD comprises three components: (1) a bilingual adversarial dataset of 2,282 image-text pairs constructed by 9 domain experts using 10 red teaming techniques (4 text-based: Disguise, Reverse Induction, Unsafe Inquiry, Indirect Task Misalignment; 6 image-based: Facial Stereotype, Malicious Labeling, Non-existent Query, Position Swapping, Noise Injection, Harmful Scenario), with 82% of images sourced from social media to prevent training-set contamination; (2) a 2×2 scoring taxonomy (Aware/Unaware × Safe/Unsafe) yielding labels 0–3, where label 3 (aware but still harmful) serves as an extreme-risk probe; (3) GUARDRANK, a lightweight automated evaluator using LLaMA-2 as backbone for Privacy/Bias/Toxicity/Legality and RoBERTa-large for Hallucination, trained via supervised learning on human-annotated prompt-response pairs with max_token=128, achieving 77.08% test accuracy versus GPT-4's 29.38% (zero-shot) and 42.78% (ICL), enabling plug-and-play evaluation without API costs or self-bias.

## Results

Across 13 MLLMs evaluated on MLLMGUARD, GPT-4V achieves the lowest average ASD (25.32↓), indicating the strongest overall safety, while MiniGPT-v2 attains the highest average PAR (49.72%↑), surpassing GPT-4V (42.74%) in the proportion of fully safe-and-responsible responses. No model achieves an ASD below 25 on average, and the worst performers (SEED-LLaMA, ShareGPT4V) exceed 49 ASD, revealing substantial safety gaps. The GUARDRANK evaluator reaches 77.08% accuracy on the held-out test set, far exceeding GPT-4 Zero-shot (29.38%) and GPT-4 ICL (42.78%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline comparison is between GUARDRANK and GPT-4 (both Zero-shot and ICL) as an automated safety evaluator. GUARDRANK achieves 77.08% average accuracy on the test set, outperforming GPT-4 Zero-shot (29.38%) by 47.7 points and GPT-4 ICL (42.78%) by 34.3 points, with the largest gap on Toxicity (79.81% vs. 12.08% and 35.94% respectively). On the validation set, GUARDRANK similarly leads with 78.50% vs. 30.27% (Zero-shot) and 43.64% (ICL). Among the 13 evaluated MLLMs, GPT-4V leads in average ASD (25.32) but MiniGPT-v2 achieves the highest PAR (49.72%), suggesting a trade-off between refusing harmful requests and providing fully responsible guidance. Notably, no open-source model matches GPT-4V's overall ASD, with the closest being MiniGPT-v2 (27.03) and InternVL-v1.5 (32.41).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends red teaming techniques originally developed for LLMs (FLAMES, multi-step jailbreaking) to the multimodal setting, combining text-based attacks (disguise, reverse induction, unsafe inquiry) with image-based attacks (facial stereotype, non-existent query, position swapping, noise injection, harmful scenario). It positions itself against a growing but fragmented landscape of MLLM safety benchmarks (MM-SafetyBench, GOAT-Bench, RTVLM, HallusionBench) that the authors identify as lacking in dimensionality, data independence, evaluator reliability, and language coverage. The GUARDRANK evaluator departs from the common practice of using GPT-4V as both model-under-test and judge by training a lightweight LLaMA-2/RoBERTa classifier on human annotations, directly addressing the self-bias problem documented in prior work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MLLMGUARD is the first bilingual (English/Chinese) multi-dimensional safety benchmark for MLLMs, and its 82% social-media-sourced images with fully manual red-teaming construction address the data leakage concern that undermines many existing benchmarks. The finding that scaling model parameters does not reliably improve safety (RQ3) and that a potential honesty-harmlessness trade-off exists (MiniGPT-v2) are practically important for model developers. Open questions include whether the single-turn, max_token=128 evaluation protocol captures real multi-turn adversarial interactions, whether GUARDRANK generalizes beyond the specific scoring rubric, and how the benchmark scales as new attack modalities (video, audio) emerge.
