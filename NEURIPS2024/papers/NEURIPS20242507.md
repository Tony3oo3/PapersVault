## Problem

Secluded partitions of R^d are the geometric backbone of list-replicable learning algorithms, where the degree k controls list complexity and the tolerance ε controls sample complexity. Prior to this work, only two partition constructions were known (the standard grid and the (d+1, 1/2d) partition), and it was completely open whether the tolerance parameter ε was near-optimal or whether substantially better partitions existed for intermediate values of k. Additionally, the classical Sperner/KKM lemma guarantees only d+1 colors in any neighborhood, leaving open whether a quantitative neighborhood variant with exponentially many colors could be established.

## Input / Output

The upper bound result takes any partition of R^d whose members have outer Lebesgue measure at most 1 and produces a point whose open ε-ball (in the ℓ∞ norm) intersects at least (1+2ε)^d members, yielding the constraint k ≥ (1+2ε)^d. The construction takes a dimension d and a target degree k ∈ [2^d] and produces an explicit (k,ε)-secluded unit cube partition with ε ≥ log₄(k)/(8d·log₄(d+1)). The neighborhood Sperner/KKM lemma takes a valid Sperner/KKM coloring of [0,1]^d and any ε ∈ (0, 1/2] and produces a point whose open ε-ball contains at least (1+2ε/3)^d points with distinct colors.

## Method

The upper bound proof employs a key change of perspective: rather than counting partition members intersecting an ε-ball at p, each member X is replaced by its Minkowski sum X + B°(ε, 0), and one counts how many enlarged sets contain p. The Generalized Brunn-Minkowski Inequality (with the constraint that each member has measure ≤ 1) yields the lower bound m(X + B°(ε,0)) ≥ m(X)·(1+2ε)^d, and a Continuous Multi-Pigeonhole Principle (a Blichfeldt-style averaging argument) then guarantees a point covered by at least (1+2ε)^d enlarged sets. The near-optimal construction uses product partitions: the d-dimensional space is split into n = ⌈d/d'⌉ blocks of dimension d', each partitioned by the known (d'+1, 1/2d')-secluded construction, so the product has degree (d'+1)^n and tolerance 1/(2d'). The neighborhood Sperner/KKM lemma overcomes a volume-cancellation issue on the bounded cube by extending the coloring to a larger cube and performing non-uniform enlargement (Minkowski sum with only one orthant of the ε-ball), ensuring each color's measure grows by a factor of (1+ε/(1+ε))^d while the containing region's measure stays fixed.

## Results

The paper establishes a near-tight upper bound: for any (k,ε)-secluded partition of ℝ^d with unit-measure members, k ≥ (1+2ε)^d, implying ε ≤ log₄(k)/d. Complementing this, the authors construct (k,ε)-secluded unit cube partitions achieving ε ≥ log₄(k)/(8d·log₄(d+1)) for all k ∈ [2^d] \ [d], matching the upper bound within a logarithmic factor. As a corollary, the d-coin bias estimation problem admits a k-list replicable algorithm with sample complexity Õ(d²log²d/(ν²log²k)) per coin. The neighborhood Sperner/KKM lemma guarantees that in any valid coloring of [0,1]^d, some ε-ball contains at least (1+2ε/3)^d distinct colors, exponentially improving on the classical d+1 bound.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical baselines. The key comparison is between the new upper bound (Theorem 3.1) and the prior state-of-the-art constructions. The standard grid partition is (2^d, 1/2)-secluded, and the best prior non-trivial construction [44, 30] achieves (d+1, 1/2d)-secluded. The paper proves the (d+1, 1/2d) construction is near-optimal: even allowing k to be polynomial in d, the tolerance can only improve to O(log d / d). The new product-partition construction (Theorem 3.2) fills the gap by providing near-optimal partitions for every intermediate k between d+1 and 2^d, which were previously unknown. The neighborhood Sperner/KKM lemma (Theorem 3.4) generalizes the classical Sperner/KKM lemma (which guarantees d+1 colors) to an exponential color bound of (1+2ε/3)^d, though the authors note the constant 2/3 is not tight for small ε.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the geometric framework for replicable learning established by Dixon et al. [17] and Vander Woude et al. [44], who introduced secluded partitions as a tool for designing list-replicable algorithms and proved the existence of (d+1, 1/2d)-secluded partitions. Prior to this work, only two specific degree values were known to admit secluded partition constructions (k = d+1 and k = 2^d), and no optimality bounds on the tolerance parameter ε were established. The paper also builds on the topological lower-bound techniques of Chase, Moran, and Yehudayoff [13] and Chase et al. [12], who used Sperner/KKM, Poincaré-Miranda, and Borsuk-Ulam theorems to prove impossibility results for replicable learning, by introducing a quantitative neighborhood variant of the Sperner/KKM lemma.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The near-optimality result (k ≥ (1+2ε)^d) closes a natural line of improvement: even allowing polynomial list complexity, the tolerance cannot exceed O(log d / d), confirming that the sample-complexity blow-up inherent in partition-based replicable algorithms is essentially unavoidable. The construction of near-optimal partitions for all k ∈ [2^d] provides a smooth tradeoff curve between list and sample complexity, which is directly useful for practitioners designing replicable learners. Open questions include whether the logarithmic gap in the construction can be closed, whether analogous results hold for other norms (ℓ_p), and whether the neighborhood Sperner/KKM lemma can be sharpened so that the classical d+1 color bound emerges as a special case.
