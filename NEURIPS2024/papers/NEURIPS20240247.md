## Problem

Hybrid deep generative models (hybrid-DGMs) that integrate known physics-based expressions with neural networks have shown strong performance for physics system modeling, but their identifiability has never been theoretically analyzed. Existing identifiability theory for DGMs relies on observed auxiliary variables (e.g., class labels or domain indices) to condition the generative model, which are unavailable when modeling physics systems with continuous, unknown parameters. This paper establishes that naive unconditional hybrid-DGMs are unidentifiable and presents meta-learning as a novel condition to construct identifiable hybrid-DGMs without requiring any observed auxiliary variable.

## Input / Output

The model takes k-shot context samples D^s (time-series observations from the same underlying physics system) and a query sample x, producing interpretable physics parameters z_P (e.g., pendulum frequency ω) and abstract latent variables z_N (absorbing missing physics like damping and forcing). The context samples are consumed through a neural network h_ζ that maps each context sample to sufficient statistics of an exponential family distribution, which are then averaged to form the conditional prior parameters λ_ζ(D^s). The output x can be either a reconstruction of the observed query or a prediction for a new sample from the same generation process with different initial conditions, the latter serving as a practical identifiability surrogate when ground-truth latents are unavailable.

## Method

The key mechanism is replacing the unconditional prior on the neural latent variable z_N with a conditional exponential family prior p_ζ(z_N | D^s) whose parameters are computed by averaging a neural network h_ζ over k context samples. Training follows an episodic meta-learning procedure: for each of M distinct data-generation processes, a subset of k samples forms the context set and the remaining l samples form the query set, with the ELBO of query samples conditioned on context maximized over {η, ζ, θ} via the reparameterization trick. The identifiability theorem (Theorem 1) requires de+1 distinct context sets (where d is the dimension of z_N and e is the dimension of sufficient statistics), establishing that the sufficient statistics T(z_N) are recoverable up to an invertible linear transformation. The physics component z_P retains an unconditional Gaussian prior with physically-meaningful hyperparameters, while the neural component's identifiability is guaranteed by the conditional structure, preventing the highly expressive neural network from trivializing the physics parameters.

## Results

On all four physics benchmarks, Meta-Hybrid-VAE achieves MCC (mean correlation coefficient) of 0.98–0.99 for the abstract latent variable z_N, compared to 0.50–0.79 for APHYNITY and 0.51–0.94 for Hybrid-VAE, providing strong empirical evidence of identifiability. The most dramatic gain is in prediction MSE: on the Forced Damped Pendulum, Meta-Hybrid-VAE achieves 6.85e-2 while baselines degrade to 1.74 (Hybrid-VAE) and 2.37 (APHYNITY) — a 25–35× improvement. On the Real Double Pendulum (no ground-truth latents), Meta-Hybrid-VAE's prediction MSE of 2.88e-2 is 2–3 orders of magnitude lower than both baselines (1.87 and 5.16). Reconstruction MSE remains comparable across all methods, confirming that the gains reflect true identifiability rather than overfitting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against APHYNITY [1] (Yin et al., JSTAT 2021), Meta-Hybrid-VAE wins decisively on MCC (0.99 vs. 0.79 on Forced Damped Pendulum; 0.98 vs. 0.50 on Double Pendulum) and by 1–2 orders of magnitude on prediction MSE across all datasets, while reconstruction MSE is comparable. Against Hybrid-VAE [2] (Takeishi & Kalousis, NeurIPS 2021), the same pattern holds: MCC jumps from 0.59 to 0.99 (Forced Damped Pendulum) and from 0.51 to 0.98 (Double Pendulum), and prediction MSE improves from 1.74 to 6.85e-2 (Forced Damped Pendulum) and from 5.52 to 2.80e-1 (Double Pendulum). Both baselines achieve reasonable reconstruction MSE, confirming that the performance gap is specifically in identifiability (the ability to recover latent parameters and generalize to unobserved samples) rather than in fitting observed data. In the non-hybrid setting (Appendix D), Meta-VAE achieves MCC 0.88 on non-stationary Gaussian time-series, approaching iVAE [6] (0.91, which uses observed class labels) while substantially outperforming standard VAE [19] (0.67), suggesting the meta-formulation is a viable alternative to observed auxiliary variables for identifiability in general DGMs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the nonlinear ICA identifiability framework (Khemakhem et al., 2020; Hyvarinen et al., 2019) to the hybrid-DGM setting, where the mixing function combines a known physics-based expression with a neural network. Prior hybrid-DGM work (Takeishi & Kalousis, 2021; Yin et al., 2021) focused on regularization strategies to prevent the expressive neural component from trivializing the physics component, but never addressed the fundamental identifiability question. The key departure is replacing the observed auxiliary variable (class labels, domain indices) required by prior identifiability theory with few-shot context samples via a meta-learning formulation, establishing conditional independence without any explicitly observed side information.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first theoretical and empirical treatment of identifiability for hybrid-DGMs, a gap that undermines the interpretability and predictive reliability claims of the entire physics-informed generative modeling literature. The meta-learning-as-identifiability connection is potentially generalizable beyond hybrid models to any DGM lacking observed auxiliary variables, opening a new design principle for identifiable generative models. Key limitations include the requirement to pair context and query samples from the same generation process (assumed via temporal adjacency in real data), the need for de+1 distinct tasks, and the fact that the physics component's identifiability, while theoretically guaranteed, still benefits from the meta-formulation in practice. Open questions include the effect of context set size k, the dimension of z_N relative to true generative factors, and whether the meta-formulation extends to settings where no pairing information is available.
