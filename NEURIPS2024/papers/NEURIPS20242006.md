## Problem

Inherently interpretable models such as B-cos networks enforce architectural constraints that yield faithful, human-readable explanations by design, but they must be trained from scratch—a cost that becomes prohibitive for large pre-trained foundation models. This paper asks whether existing pre-trained DNNs can be efficiently fine-tuned into B-cos models, preserving both classification performance and the interpretability benefits, at a fraction of the original training cost.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242006/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes pre-trained DNN weights (ResNet, DenseNet, ViT variants, or CLIP) and RGB images, which are re-encoded into a 6-channel representation [r,g,b,1-r,1-g,1-b] to enable color visualization of the dynamic linear summary. For CLIP, text prompts are additionally consumed via the frozen text encoder. The model outputs class labels alongside a complete, bias-free linear summary W(x) that serves as a model-faithful color explanation map, and for CLIP, zero-shot similarity scores between image and text embeddings.

## Method

The key insight is that any conventional piece-wise linear DNN can be reframed as a B-cos model with B=1 and bias terms, so B-cosification requires only three targeted modifications: (1) splitting the first-layer weights to accept 6-channel inputs via a simple halving-and-negating scheme (Eq. 2), (2) removing all bias terms so that W(x) constitutes a complete explanation, and (3) setting the B-cos alignment parameter B from 1 to 2 to introduce weight-input alignment pressure. The authors show that immediately setting B=2 and zeroing biases (rather than using gradual schedules or learnable parameters) is both simplest and most effective, requiring only ~90 epochs of fine-tuning with AdamW. For CLIP, the B-cosified model is trained with a SigLIP contrastive loss against the frozen original CLIP's image embeddings, preserving zero-shot transferability while gaining inherent interpretability. Weight normalization in the B-cos layers is deliberately omitted, as the authors prove that BatchNorm layers cancel any scaling invariance, making unnormalized weights functionally equivalent and better preserving pre-trained performance.

## Results

On ImageNet top-1 accuracy, B-cosified models outperform B-cos models trained from scratch by an average of 2.5 percentage points across 12 architectures (e.g., ViT-Ti: 69.3% vs. 60.0%, +9.3pp; ViT-S: 75.2% vs. 69.2%, +6.0pp), while achieving the same accuracy with an average 5.2× training speedup (up to 9× for ViT-Ti, ViT-S, and ResNet-50-v2). On interpretability (GridPG localisation), B-cosified models match B-cos models trained from scratch (e.g., ResNet-18: 87.6 vs. 87.4) while dramatically outperforming conventional DNNs (32.7–71.0pp improvement). For CLIP, B-cosified variants trained on ImageNet or CC3M remain competitive with standard CLIP on zero-shot accuracy across 38 datasets while yielding highly interpretable color explanations that significantly outperform GradCAM localisation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against B-cos models trained from scratch [11], B-cosified models win on 10 of 12 architectures in top-1 accuracy (avg. +2.5pp) and match them on GridPG localisation, while requiring only 1/5.2 of the training time on average; the two exceptions are ViTc-B and ViTc-L where B-cosification underperforms by 0.4–0.7pp. Against post-hoc attribution methods (GradCAM [46], DeepLIFT/IxG [48], Gradient [49], Guided Backprop [51], IntGrad [53]), B-cosified models' inherent W(x)x explanations achieve near-perfect GridPG localisation (e.g., ~88–93 for ResNet-18/50), substantially outperforming all post-hoc baselines which score 21–47 on the same metric. For CLIP zero-shot evaluation, B-cosified CLIP (trained on ImageNet or CC3M) significantly outperforms the Text2Concept approach [30] across nearly all 38 benchmark datasets (e.g., 0.59 vs. 0.52 on ImageNet-1k, 0.62 vs. 0.29 on SUN397), while remaining within 1–3pp of standard CLIP [39] on most natural-image datasets. On linear probing, B-cosified CLIP matches or slightly exceeds standard CLIP on several datasets (e.g., 0.98 vs. 0.97 on STL-10, 0.84 vs. 0.82 on PCAM) but trails by 1–5pp on others (e.g., 0.71 vs. 0.80 on Cars). Overall, B-cosification consistently wins on interpretability and training efficiency, wins or ties on accuracy for most architectures, and is competitive with standard CLIP on zero-shot generalisation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on B-cos Networks [Bohle et al., 2022, 2024], which replace linear layers with B-cos transformations to yield inherently interpretable models, but which require expensive training from scratch. The key conceptual departure is showing that conventional pre-trained DNNs can be reframed as a special case of B-cos models (B=1, with biases) and converted via a small set of targeted modifications (6-channel input encoding, bias removal, B→2) followed by brief fine-tuning. It also positions against the broader post-hoc attribution literature (GradCAM, DeepLIFT, IntGrad) by offering model-faithful explanations by design, and extends the inherently interpretable model paradigm to foundation models (CLIP) where from-scratch training is prohibitively expensive.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

B-cosification provides a simple, general recipe (set B=2, remove biases, fine-tune) that makes any pre-trained CNN or ViT inherently interpretable at a fraction of the training cost, with the practical implication that the large ecosystem of existing pre-trained weights becomes immediately accessible for interpretable deployment. The extension to CLIP demonstrates that even large foundation models can be made interpretable with limited data and compute, opening a path toward interpretable VLMs. Limitations include slower convergence for larger ViTc architectures (no speedup achieved) and the fact that the approach is currently validated only on vision models; whether it generalizes to language models or other modalities remains open. The finding that stronger pre-training (V2, DINO) yields larger B-cosification gains suggests the method's effectiveness is tightly coupled to initialization quality.
