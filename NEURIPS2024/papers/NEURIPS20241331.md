## Problem

Existing open-world video instance segmentation methods assign only one-word labels to detected objects and suffer from spatial information loss in their object queries, limiting discovery of never-before-seen objects. No prior work unifies open-world video instance segmentation with dense, free-form object-centric captioning, despite captions being more descriptive and naturally facilitating zero-shot fine-grained description of unseen objects.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241331/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a video broken into short clips of T frames (T=1 or T=2 in practice) and processes them online in a promptless setting. For each detected object instance it outputs a segmentation mask (BURST) or bounding box (VidSTG), a persistent track identity across clips, a free-form object-centric caption generated by a frozen OPT-2.7B LLM, and a closed-world category label. The object-to-text abstractor was trained only on VidSTG captions yet generalizes to generate meaningful captions for unseen objects on BURST, which provides no caption annotations.

## Method

OW-VISCapTor introduces two abstractors connecting a multi-scale visual feature extractor (Mask2Former-style with SwinL/ResNet-50 backbone and Deformable-DETR pixel decoder) to a frozen LLM. The object abstractor generates spatially-rich open-world object queries by encoding a 7×7 grid of equally-spaced points through a SAM prompt encoder, then modulating these embeddings in a 3-layer transformer decoder alongside learned closed-world embeddings; an inter-query contrastive loss (maximizing L1 distance between all query pairs) enforces diversity and suppresses overlapping false positives. The object-to-text abstractor (11 transformer layers initialized from BLIP-2) concatenates each object query with 32 learned text embeddings and applies masked cross-attention—restricting attention to the foreground region defined by the predicted segmentation mask—to produce object-centric text queries that the frozen OPT-2.7B LLM decodes into captions. Training uses a combined objective of the contrastive loss, a standard captioning loss, a closed-world detection/classification loss, and an open-world detection-only loss, with ground-truth objects matched twice (to open-world and closed-world predictions) via the Hungarian algorithm.

## Results

On the BURST validation set (OW-VIS), OW-VISCapTor+DEVA achieves the best unseen-category OWTA of 55.2, surpassing the previous best (EntitySeg+DEVA at 49.6) by ~6 points, while maintaining competitive seen-category performance (73.5 vs. 74.6). On the VidSTG dataset (Dense VOC), OW-VISCapTor+CAROQ achieves the best captioning accuracy (CapA) of 43.9 and the best overall CHOTA of 53.1, outperforming the offline DVOC-DS (CapA 36.8, CHOTA 51.6) by ~7 and ~1.5 points respectively. Compared to the generalized baseline (Mask2Former+DEVA+BLIP2), the method improves unseen OWTA by 13 points (55.2 vs. 42.3) and CapA by 10 points (43.9 vs. 34.0).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the specialized OW-VIS SOTA (EntitySeg+DEVA, unseen OWTA 49.6), OW-VISCapTor+DEVA wins by ~6 points (55.2) on unseen categories but trails slightly on seen categories (73.5 vs. 72.7) and overall (69.0 vs. 68.8). Against the specialized Dense VOC SOTA (DVOC-DS joint training, CapA 36.8, CHOTA 51.6), OW-VISCapTor+CAROQ wins on CapA by ~7 points (43.9) and CHOTA by ~1.5 points (53.1), while slightly underperforming on DetA (60.1 vs. 65.5) due to using SwinL instead of ViT backbone. Against the generalized baseline (Mask2Former+DEVA+BLIP2), the method dominates on both unseen OWTA (55.2 vs. 42.3, +13 pts) and CapA (43.9 vs. 34.0, +10 pts), demonstrating that jointly learning object queries for segmentation and captioning is superior to a pipeline that crops bounding boxes and feeds them to a separate captioning model. On the closed-world OVIS benchmark, OW-VISCapTor (AP 25.4) performs comparably to the specialized CAROQ (AP 25.8), confirming generalizability without sacrificing closed-world performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

OW-VISCapTor composes three established components into a unified pipeline: Mask2Former's transformer-decoder meta-architecture for the object abstractor, SAM's prompt encoder (fine-tuned on a grid of spatial points) to generate spatially-rich open-world object queries, and BLIP-2's masked cross-attention + frozen-LLM design for the object-to-text abstractor. Conceptually, the paper departs from prior OW-VIS methods (DEVA, EntitySeg, OWVISFormer) that suffer from spatial information loss in their abstractors by explicitly encoding spatial prompts, and from prior dense video captioning (DVOC-DS) by replacing bounding-box cropping with mask-restricted cross-attention for object-centric focus. The inter-query contrastive loss is a lightweight addition not present in prior abstractor-based detectors, encouraging query diversity to aid open-world discovery.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first method to jointly perform open-world video instance segmentation and object-centric captioning in an online, prompt-less setting, addressing a gap where prior OW-VIS methods assign only one-word labels and prior captioning methods are restricted to closed-world or offline processing. The dramatic 23-point CapA drop when masked attention is removed (Table 3) reveals that simply concatenating an object query with text embeddings is fundamentally insufficient for object-centric captioning—an insight that may inform future vision-language architectures. Open questions include whether the approach scales to very long videos, whether the grid-of-points prompt strategy generalizes beyond the tested 7×7 resolution, and how to handle the acknowledged failure modes (small-object captioning, identity loss after prolonged occlusion) without stronger trackers or caption generators.
