## Problem

While LLMs have demonstrated strong performance on predictive and generative chemistry tasks (property prediction, reaction prediction), their ability to perform multi-step chemical reasoning—specifically molecular structure elucidation from integrated spectral data—remains entirely unexplored. This task requires integrating clues from diverse spectroscopic sources (IR, MASS, NMR) through iterative hypothesis testing, a fundamentally different cognitive challenge from single-step prediction.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20244280/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each puzzle instance provides a molecular formula (derived from mass spectrometry), four spectrum images (IR, MASS, ¹H-NMR, ¹³C-NMR), and a pool of candidate SMILES fragments. The model must produce a final SMILES string through a sequential three-stage process: first identifying building blocks from the formula, then interpreting spectral images to confirm/revise hypotheses, and finally iteratively selecting and assembling fragments guided by NMR connectivity data. Stage 2 consumes spectrum images directly (VQA format), while Stage 3 uses text-formatted NMR peak descriptions with multiplicity and coupling constants, and the assembly loop runs up to 7 iterations with RDKit validation at each step.

## Method

MolPuzzle structures molecular structure elucidation as a sequential, three-stage puzzle analogous to a crossword: Stage 1 (Molecule Understanding) applies 26 question templates to extract saturation, aromaticity, and functional group information from molecular formulas; Stage 2 (Spectrum Interpretation) uses 17 IR and 12 per-NMR-type VQA templates to query spectrum images for specific structural features; Stage 3 (Molecule Construction) employs 27 templates for iterative fragment selection from a SMILES pool, guided by NMR hydrogen/carbon environment data, with RDKit-based connectivity validation and NMR matching at each assembly step. The benchmark comprises 217 puzzle instances yielding over 23,000 QA samples, with Stage 3 fragments manually verified by chemistry experts (67.4% initial accuracy in automated generation, corrected to 100%). Evaluation is conducted zero-shot across 12 LLMs (GPT-4o, Claude-3-opus, Gemini-pro, LLaVA, Llama-3, etc.) using F1/accuracy for individual QA tasks and exact-match accuracy, Levenshtein distance, and fingerprint Tanimoto similarity (MACCS, RDK, Morgan) for full puzzle solutions, with a human baseline of six chemistry Ph.D. candidates.

## Results

On the full MolPuzzle benchmark (217 instances), the best-performing LLM, GPT-4o, achieves only 1.4% exact-match accuracy on the final SMILES string, compared to 66.7% for the human baseline. GPT-4o's MACCS fingerprint Tanimoto similarity is 0.431 versus 0.985 for humans, and its Levenshtein distance to ground truth is 11.65 versus 1.33 for humans. In the per-stage breakdown (Table 1), GPT-4o scores near-perfect F1 (1.00) on Stage-1 saturation identification but drops to ~0.61–0.66 F1 on Stage-2 spectrum interpretation and ~0.51–0.52 F1 on Stage-3 molecule construction, while humans achieve 0.73–0.77 F1 in Stage 2 and 0.73–0.87 F1 in Stage 3.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GPT-4o outperforms all other LLMs across every metric in the full-puzzle evaluation (Table 2), achieving the highest exact-match accuracy (1.4%), lowest Levenshtein distance (11.65), and highest MACCS FTS (0.431), but remains dramatically below the human baseline (66.7% accuracy, 1.33 Levenshtein, 0.985 MACCS FTS). Claude-3-opus is a close second among LLMs (1.3% accuracy, 0.383 MACCS FTS), while Gemini-pro trails further (0% accuracy, 0.340 MACCS FTS). In the per-stage breakdown (Table 1), GPT-4o matches or exceeds human performance on Stage-1 molecule understanding (F1 1.00 on SI, 0.943 on ARI, 0.934 on FGI vs. human 1.00, 1.00, 0.890), but falls well short in Stage-2 spectrum interpretation (F1 ~0.61–0.66 vs. human 0.73–0.77) and Stage-3 molecule construction (F1 ~0.51–0.52 vs. human 0.73–0.87). Open-source models (Llama-3, Vicuna-13B, Mistral-7B) perform substantially worse than proprietary models in all stages, with Llama-3 scoring 0.0 on saturation degree calculation and F1 below 0.35 on Stage-3 tasks. The gap between LLMs and humans is most pronounced in the iterative fragment assembly of Stage 3, where error accumulation across steps leads to catastrophic failures in GPT-4o's final molecule construction.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of two established research threads: automated molecular structure elucidation (rule-based systems like SENECA, ML approaches like transformer-based IR-to-SMILES models) and multimodal LLM benchmarking (MMMU, MathVista, LabSafetyBench). Prior molecular elucidation work has focused on single-spectrum prediction or rule-based expert systems, while prior LLM benchmarks have not addressed chemistry-domain multi-step reasoning. MolPuzzle repurposes the well-established chemistry task of structure elucidation into a sequential, multi-spectrum puzzle format specifically designed to stress-test the planning and visual-reasoning capabilities of general-purpose multimodal LLMs, rather than training specialized encoder-decoder models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals a stark and informative performance gradient: GPT-4o achieves near-perfect accuracy on molecular formula understanding (Stage 1) yet only 1.4% exact match on full puzzle solving, with humans at 66.7%. This pinpoints spectrum interpretation (especially NMR peak reading) and iterative fragment assembly as the critical bottlenecks for LLMs in chemistry. Notably, chain-of-thought prompting worsened performance relative to zero-shot, suggesting that current reasoning scaffolds are ill-suited to the iterative, constraint-satisfaction nature of molecular assembly. Open questions include whether domain-specific fine-tuning, agent-based feedback loops, or specialized spectral vision encoders can close the gap, and whether the benchmark's 217-instance scale is sufficient for robust conclusions.
