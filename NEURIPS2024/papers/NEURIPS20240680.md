## Problem

Secure two-party inference for Transformer models is impractical due to two efficiency bottlenecks: (1) linear layers rely on window encoding that produces sparse HE ciphertexts, causing redundant communication and computation, and (2) non-linear activations (GELU, Softmax) require high-degree piecewise polynomials over large rings to maintain accuracy, inflating both communication rounds and fixed-point error. This paper tackles how to make secure Transformer inference practical without sacrificing accuracy.

## Input / Output

The client holds a text sequence encoded as additive secret shares over a ring Z_{2^ℓ}, while the server holds the Transformer model weights (also as shares). During a one-time setup, the server encrypts all model weights via RLWE-based HE and sends the ciphertexts to the client for disk storage; subsequent queries only require the client to send compact output ciphertexts (k/⌊N/n⌋ ciphertexts per linear layer) to the server. The final inference result (classification score or regression value) is reconstructed from the additive secret shares held by both parties, with the client learning nothing about the model beyond the result and the server learning nothing about the input.

## Method

Nimbus introduces two key mechanisms. For linear layers, the Client-Side Outer Product (COP) protocol shifts the server's encrypted weights to the client during setup, eliminating all input communication in the online phase; the client then performs scalar-polynomial multiplications (O(kmN) complexity) instead of the prior polynomial-polynomial multiplications, and packs output ciphertexts via a free right-shift operation to minimize communication. For non-linear layers, a distribution-aware fitting strategy allocates approximation budget according to the empirical input distribution (e.g., GELU inputs peak near −3 with <10% probability above zero), enabling a quadratic polynomial for GELU and a cubic for exp instead of degree-6 polynomials, which in turn permits computation on a smaller ring Z_{32} with precision s=12. A fused truncation-upcast protocol converts shares between the small and large rings at zero additional communication cost. Together these optimizations yield 2.7×–4.7× end-to-end speedup over BumbleBee on BERT-base with only 0.08% average accuracy loss after lightweight fine-tuning.

## Results

On BERT-base (sequence length 128), Nimbus achieves 2.7×–4.7× end-to-end speedup over BumbleBee (SOTA) and 4.8×–5.9× over Iron across LAN (3 Gbps, 1 ms) and WAN (400 Mbps, 10 ms) settings. Linear layers see 7.2×–12.5× speedup (LAN) and 2.9×–4.0× (WAN) over BumbleBee, while GELU and Softmax are 3.3×–4.0× faster. Total communication for linear layers is reduced to 11.51% of Iron and 65% of BumbleBee, with 60% overall communication reduction. The distribution-aware polynomial approximation incurs only 0.08% average accuracy loss on GLUE (with lightweight fine-tuning) versus the floating-point baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Iron [14], Nimbus achieves 4.8×–5.9× end-to-end speedup and reduces linear-layer communication to just 11.51% of Iron's (e.g., 10.35 MB vs. 74.64 MB for Linear_qkv), while also halving the number of communication rounds from 2 to 1. Against the stronger BumbleBee [26], Nimbus delivers 2.7×–4.7× end-to-end speedup with 60% less total communication; the COP protocol eliminates input ciphertext transmission entirely, and the distribution-aware polynomial approximation cuts GELU communication from 185 MB to 53 MB (3.5×) and Softmax from 354 MB to 115 MB (3.1×) with fewer rounds. Against MPCFormer [22], Nimbus is faster on Softmax (0.56 s vs. 0.71 s on LAN) while maintaining far better accuracy (0.08% avg. loss vs. 2.26% for MPCFormer even after knowledge distillation). Against BOLT [29], Nimbus is approximately 10× faster in all settings (e.g., 0.28 s vs. 3.18 s for GLUE on LAN), benefiting from lower-degree polynomials, a smaller ring (Z_{2^32} vs. field-based), and the free truncation-upcast fusion that avoids BOLT's extra upcasting communication.

## Relation to Prior Work

Nimbus builds directly on the BumbleBee [26] framework (lattice-based additive HE, additive secret sharing, and the overall 2PC architecture for Transformer inference) but departs fundamentally in two ways. For linear layers, it replaces the server-side inner product (SIP) protocol used by Iron [14], Cheetah [17], and BumbleBee with a client-side outer product (COP) protocol that shifts encrypted weights to a one-time setup and uses row-wise encoding to eliminate input communication. For non-linear layers, it replaces the uniform-distribution piecewise polynomial approximation with a distribution-aware fitting strategy that exploits the regular input distributions observed in Transformer activations, enabling lower-degree polynomials and smaller computation rings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Nimbus makes secure two-party Transformer inference substantially more practical, achieving 2.7×–4.7× end-to-end speedup over the prior SOTA (BumbleBee) while maintaining near-lossless accuracy (0.08% average drop with light fine-tuning). The distribution-aware approximation insight is broadly applicable to any privacy-preserving setting where activation distributions are known or estimable. Key limitations include evaluation only on BERT-base (not larger LLMs), a semi-honest threat model, and a requirement for a resource-heavy client (64 vCPUs, 128 GB RAM) to store encrypted weights. Open questions include whether the COP protocol and distribution-aware fitting scale to multi-billion-parameter models, whether the approach extends to malicious adversaries, and how to optimize the remaining unoptimized layers (attention activation multiplication, LayerNorm).
