## Problem

Classical reinforcement learning models (MDPs, POMDPs, Dec-POMDPs) assume highly regular, Markovian information structures, while real-world sequential decision-making problems involve complex, time-varying causal dependencies among system variables. No general theory connects the information structure of a problem to the statistical complexity of learning it, leaving a gap between the expressiveness needed for real-world problems and the tractability guarantees available for restricted model classes.

## Input / Output

The framework takes a POST/POSG specification consisting of a directed acyclic graph encoding causal dependencies among system and action variables, variable spaces, and an observability assignment, together with the weakly-revealing parameters m (number of future steps) and α (robustness constant). The algorithm only requires m and α, not the full information structure, and produces an ε-optimal policy (or ε-Nash/coarse-correlated equilibrium in the game setting) along with a generalized PSR representation. The sample complexity bound is expressed as (1/ε²)·poly(1/α, max_h |I†_h|, Q_m, A, H), where |I†_h| is the cardinality of the information-structural state at each time step.

## Method

The paper introduces POSTs and POSGs, which explicitly encode information structure as a labeled DAG, and defines the 'information-structural state' I†_h as the minimal d-separating set between past and future observations after removing edges into action variables. Theorem 1 bounds the rank of observable dynamics by max_h |I†_h|, establishing that information structure directly governs dynamical complexity. Under an m-step I†-weakly-revealing identifiability condition, m-step observable futures serve as core test sets, enabling construction of a generalized PSR with operators M_h derived from the mapping m_h that expresses any future probability as a linear combination of core-test probabilities. Theorem 2 shows this PSR is γ-well-conditioned with γ = α/max_h |I†_h|^{1/2} when the revealing condition is α-robust. Finally, a UCB-type model-based algorithm (adapted from Huang et al.) achieves the sample complexity bound in Theorem 3, recovering known tractability results for POMDPs and Dec-POMDPs as special cases while identifying a strictly larger class of learnable problems.

## Results

The paper's main quantitative result (Theorem 3) establishes that an α-robustly m-step I†-weakly revealing POST can be learned to ε-optimality with sample complexity (1/ε²) × poly(1/α, max_h |I_h†|, Q_m, A, H), where |I_h†| is the size of the information-structural state, Q_m is the size of m-step observable trajectories, A is the largest action space, and H is the horizon. Theorem 1 characterizes the rank of observable dynamics as bounded by max_h |I_h†|, and Theorem 2 shows the constructed generalized PSR is γ-well-conditioned with γ = α / max_h |I_h†|^{1/2}. For the special case of POMDPs, this recovers the known poly(S, O, A, H, α^{-1})·ε^{-2} sample complexity bound.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical baselines or experimental comparisons. The 'comparisons' are to prior theoretical frameworks: the POST/POSG model subsumes MDPs, POMDPs, Dec-POMDPs, and POMGs as special cases, and Theorem 1 recovers the known rank bound |S| for POMDPs/Dec-POMDPs (where the information-structural state coincides with the latent Markovian state). The I†-weakly-revealing condition (Definition 6) generalizes the weakly-revealing condition for POMDPs from Liu et al. [36], and the learning algorithm extends the UCB-type algorithm of Huang et al. [35] from standard PSRs to the generalized PSR setting. The paper identifies a strictly larger class of statistically tractable sequential decision-making problems than prior work, since the information-structural state can be smaller than the full history even without a latent Markovian state (e.g., limited-memory structures yield rank bounded by |O|^m|A|^m rather than exponential in H).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper draws on Witsenhausen's intrinsic model from the control literature and extends it with partial observability to create a reinforcement learning framework (POST/POSG) where information structure is an explicit, arbitrary DAG rather than a fixed assumption. It generalizes predictive state representations (Littman & Sutton; Jaeger) to allow arbitrary interleaving of observations and actions, and extends the weakly-revealing identifiability condition of Liu et al. to the information-structural setting. The learning algorithm adapts the UCB-type approach of Huang et al. for standard PSRs to the generalized PSR constructed here. The key departure from prior work is that classical models (MDPs, POMDPs, Dec-POMDPs, POMGs) each assume a specific, fixed information structure, whereas this paper treats the information structure as a free parameter and characterizes complexity as a function of it.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a unified, graph-theoretic lens through which the statistical complexity of any partially-observable sequential decision-making problem can be read off from its information structure DAG, identifying a broader class of tractable problems and recovering known results (e.g., POMDP rank bounded by state space size) as special cases. A notable conceptual point is that unobservable variables can reduce the effective complexity of observable dynamics by acting as d-separators, which is counterintuitive from a purely observational standpoint. The main limitation is that the proposed algorithm is explicitly not computationally practical, and the authors suggest the theory could inform inductive biases for deep learning architectures (e.g., RNNs) but leave this as future work. Open questions include whether the information-structural state can be learned end-to-end and how to handle continuous or infinite variable spaces.
