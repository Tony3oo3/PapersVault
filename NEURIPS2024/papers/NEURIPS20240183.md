## Problem

Existing 3D Gaussian human avatar methods focus on body-level reconstruction and fail to capture the fine-grained expressiveness of hands and faces, which are critical for realistic digital humans. This is compounded by two practical challenges: off-the-shelf SMPL-X estimators produce misaligned meshes on in-the-wild videos, and the large granularity differences between body parts (e.g., small hands with many degrees of freedom vs. large torso) make uniform Gaussian density control suboptimal.

## Input / Output

The method takes a monocular RGB video (1200×800 on XHumans, 1920×1080 on UPB) as input, extracting per-frame human masks (via SGHM) and SMPL-X parameters. It produces a per-subject 3D Gaussian avatar in canonical space (~20K Gaussians) that can be re-posed via linear blend skinning and rendered at 361 fps for 1080p output, supporting both novel view synthesis and novel pose synthesis. The UPB dataset, introduced by the authors, contains in-the-wild sign language videos without ground-truth SMPL-X annotations, making it more representative of real deployment scenarios.

## Method

EVA represents the avatar as 3D Gaussians in a canonical SMPL-X rest pose, animated via LBS with learnable weight offsets (MLP on positionally encoded Gaussian centers) and per-frame pose fine-tuning. Three key components address expressiveness: (1) a fitting-based SMPL-X alignment module that jointly optimizes pose, shape, and expression using 2D keypoint reprojection, VPoser body priors guided by 3D body joints, and 3D hand joint constraints, producing significantly better mesh-to-video alignment than regression-based estimators; (2) a context-aware adaptive density control (CADC) strategy that sets per-Gaussian densification thresholds based on body-part attributes (body/hand/face) and the difference between recent and prior gradient windows, allowing finer Gaussians for small regions like hands; and (3) a confidence-aware loss where a small CNN predicts per-pixel confidence from the rendered image and depth, adaptively weighting the L1 photometric loss to suppress noisy or misaligned regions during optimization.

## Results

On the controlled XHumans dataset, EVA achieves the best full-body PSNR of 29.67 dB and the lowest LPIPS of 33.05, outperforming the next-best baseline (Splatting+SMPL-X, LPIPS 44.39) by 19.7% relative LPIPS reduction. On the more challenging real-world UPB dataset (no ground-truth SMPL-X), EVA achieves 26.78 dB full-body PSNR and 45.90 LPIPS on the hand region, surpassing the best baseline (GART+SMPL-X, 61.44 LPIPS) by over 25% relative LPIPS gain. EVA also leads on face-region LPIPS in both settings (72.30 on XHumans vs. 92.51 for Splatting+SMPL-X; 65.90 on UPB vs. 93.28 for GART+SMPL-X), confirming its advantage in fine-grained expressive details.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EVA outperforms all four baselines across every metric and region on both datasets. On XHumans, the closest competitor is Splatting+SMPL-X (103K Gaussians), which leads on full-body PSNR (29.33 vs. 29.67) but trails EVA by 11.5 LPIPS points on the hand region (78.53 vs. 72.95) and 20.2 on the face (92.51 vs. 72.30), while using 5× more Gaussians. On the real-world UPB dataset, the gap widens substantially: EVA achieves 27.00 dB hand PSNR vs. 25.25 for GART+SMPL-X (the best baseline), and 45.90 vs. 61.44 hand LPIPS — a 25%+ relative improvement. GauHuman+SMPL-X, the architecturally closest method, uses the fewest Gaussians (12K on UPB) but still lags EVA by 16.7 LPIPS on the hand region. The consistent advantage on hand and face metrics, particularly in the real-world setting where EVA's SMPL-X alignment module is active, confirms that the expressiveness-focused design choices (adaptive density control, confidence-aware loss, and alignment) are the primary sources of the performance gain.

## Relation to Prior Work

EVA extends the line of 3D Gaussian Splatting-based human avatar methods (GART, GauHuman, SplattingAvatar) from body-level modeling to the expressive setting by swapping the SMPL driving signal for SMPL-X, which adds hand articulation and facial expression parameters. It also builds on the SMPL-X parametric model for topology-aware Gaussian initialization and linear blend skinning, following the articulated 3DGS formulation. The key conceptual departure from prior expressive avatar work (X-Avatar, GVA, AvatarRex) is the relaxation of input requirements: those methods rely on multi-view captures or RGB-D data, whereas EVA operates from a single in-the-wild monocular video, necessitating a dedicated SMPL-X alignment module and adaptive optimization strategies to compensate for the lack of ground-truth pose.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Expressive detail in hands and face is essential for realistic digital humans in AR/VR, film, and sign-language applications, yet prior monocular avatar methods largely ignored these regions. EVA's demonstration that SMPL-X alignment quality is the dominant bottleneck for real-world expressive avatars (with >25% LPIPS gain on hands when the alignment module is included) provides a clear diagnostic for the field. Open questions remain around non-rigid elements (clothes, hair), generalization to new subjects without per-subject optimization, and robustness to occlusion or few-shot inputs, all of which the authors identify as future directions.
