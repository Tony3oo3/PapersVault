## Problem

Standard offline policy evaluation assumes the MDP transition kernel is fixed between data collection and deployment, but in practice the environment may shift due to unobserved confounding, non-stationarity, or adversarial dynamics. Prior robust OPE methods either rely solely on Q-function estimates (yielding slow nonparametric rates and no confidence intervals) or fail to achieve semiparametric efficiency. This paper asks: can we estimate the sharp (tightest possible) bounds on policy value under bounded transition-kernel perturbations while remaining statistically efficient, asymptotically normal, and robust to slow nuisance estimation?

## Input / Output

The method consumes n i.i.d. transition tuples (s, a, r, s') drawn from an arbitrary data-generating distribution ν (which need not correspond to a valid MDP, accommodating confounding), a fixed target policy π_t, and a practitioner-chosen sensitivity parameter Λ(s,a) ∈ [1, ∞) that bounds the multiplicative ratio between the true and observed transition kernels. Beyond the point estimates of worst- and best-case policy values, the method outputs Wald confidence intervals via the asymptotic variance of the EIF, and remains valid (though possibly not sharp) even when nuisance functions are estimated inconsistently—a property critical for sensitivity analysis where the practitioner may not trust their nuisance estimates.

## Method

The core estimator is built on the efficient influence function (EIF) of the robust policy value, which decomposes into three nuisance functions: the worst-case Q-function, the worst-case visitation density ratio, and a quantile threshold characterizing the worst-case transition kernel. The robust Bellman operator admits a closed form as a convex combination of the nominal expectation and a CVaR under the observed kernel (Lemma 3.1), making the Q-function identifiable via iterative RobustFQE with orthogonal CVaR estimation (achieving second-order dependence on quantile errors). The density ratio is estimated via RobustMIL, a minimax indirect learning procedure that solves the Bellman flow equation in the worst-case MDP using importance-weighted next-state samples. The final orthogonal estimator (Algorithm 3) uses K-fold cross-fitting to plug nuisance estimates into the EIF and take an empirical average; the product structure of nuisance errors in the EIF ensures Neyman orthogonality, √n-rates, asymptotic normality, and semiparametric efficiency even when individual nuisances converge at slow nonparametric rates (e.g., n^{-1/4}).

## Results

On a synthetic 1-D control MDP with 20,000 transitions, the orthogonal estimator (Orth) achieves the lowest MSE to the true worst-case policy value across all sensitivity levels Λ ∈ {1, 2, 4, 8}. At Λ=1 (no confounding), Orth attains an MSE of 0.000005, roughly 48× lower than the direct Q-based estimator (0.000240) and 544× lower than the w-based estimator (0.002722). At the most adversarial setting Λ=8, Orth achieves MSE 0.005713, outperforming Q (0.006247) and dramatically outperforming W (0.052643). All three estimators produce valid bounds (containing the true worst-case value) in every replication.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three estimators for the worst-case policy value: (1) Q, a direct method using only the RobustFQE-estimated Q-function; (2) W, a direct method using only the RobustMIL-estimated visitation density ratio; and (3) Orth, the proposed orthogonal estimator combining both nuisances via the efficient influence function. Orth consistently achieves the lowest MSE at every Λ value, confirming its semiparametric efficiency. The advantage is most pronounced at Λ=1, where Orth's MSE (0.000005) is nearly two orders of magnitude below Q (0.000240), and at Λ=8, where W degrades severely (MSE 0.052643) while Orth remains stable (0.005713). The W baseline alone is the noisiest estimator, with standard deviations up to 0.050839 at Λ=8, illustrating that importance-weighting alone is unreliable under high sensitivity. The Q baseline is competitive at moderate Λ but still loses to Orth, consistent with the theory that the orthogonal combination is insensitive to nuisance estimation errors. The paper also reports a real-world evaluation on MIMIC-III (sepsis management) in Appendix L, though detailed numbers are not in the main text.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Marginal Sensitivity Model (MSM) from Tan (2006) and its causal-inference applications (Dorn et al. 2021, Bonvini et al. 2022) from single-time-step decision-making to infinite-horizon discounted MDPs, constraining the ratio of transition kernel densities rather than policy densities. It builds on the Double RL (DRL) estimator of Kallus & Uehara (2020, 2022) and Minimax Indirect Learning (Uehara et al. 2021) by generalizing both to the robust MDP setting, and improves upon prior robust FQE work (Bruns-Smith & Zhou 2023, Panaganti et al. 2022) by introducing an orthogonalized estimator that combines the worst-case Q-function, robust visitation density ratio, and a CVaR threshold function. The key departure from prior robust OPE is that previous methods relied solely on the worst-case Q-function for evaluation, so any estimation error propagated directly; here, Neyman orthogonality ensures only second-order sensitivity to nuisance errors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first semiparametrically efficient estimator for sharp robust OPE bounds, enabling valid confidence intervals on worst-case policy values that account for both environment shift and finite-sample uncertainty. The guarantee that bounds remain valid (though wider) even under inconsistent nuisance estimation is particularly valuable for sensitivity analysis, where practitioners cannot verify their modeling assumptions. Open questions include the practical selection of the sensitivity parameter Λ, the (s,a)-rectangularity assumption that prevents the problem from becoming a POMDP, and whether the approach scales to high-dimensional continuous state spaces beyond the simple synthetic and MIMIC-III evaluations presented.
