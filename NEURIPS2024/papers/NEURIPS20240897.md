## Problem

Universal Transformers share parameters across layers, reducing parameter count by a factor of L relative to a non-shared Transformer of the same dimensionality. Naively widening the shared layer to compensate makes compute and memory requirements prohibitive, so no prior work has produced a shared-layer Transformer competitive with standard Transformers on parameter-dominated tasks like language modeling. This paper addresses that fundamental parameter-compute ratio gap.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240897/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes tokenized text sequences (SentencePiece, 8000-token vocabulary) with a context length of 1024 and produces a next-token probability distribution over the vocabulary. Evaluation is primarily via perplexity on held-out subsets of C4, SlimPajama, peS2o, and The Stack, supplemented by zero-shot accuracy on six downstream benchmarks (LAMBADA, BLiMP, CBT, HellaSwag, PIQA, ARC-E). Because the architecture is fully MoE, the number of parameters, active compute (MACs), and memory are decoupled, giving designers independent knobs for each resource dimension.

## Method

MoEUT combines three MoE techniques with two novel shared-layer-specific designs. For feedforward blocks it uses σ-MoE with fine-grained experts (d_expert=128, hundreds of experts, top-K selection with sigmoid gating and sequence-level entropy regularization); for self-attention it uses SwitchHead, which applies independent MoE routing to the value and output projections of each head while keeping query/key projections dense. Two key innovations address the shared-layer setting: (1) layer grouping, where 2–4 non-shared MoE layers form a group that is then recurrently repeated, reducing per-layer expert count while increasing total attention heads; and (2) a peri-layernorm scheme that places layer normalization only before linear layers immediately preceding sigmoid or softmax activations (query/key projections, expert selection, final classifier) but omits it from the main residual data path (value projection, MoE feedforward), thereby avoiding the residual-norm growth problem that plagues pre-layernorm UTs while preserving gradient flow.

## Results

On C4, MoEUT achieves 10.90 perplexity at 1.04B parameters, outperforming the parameter-matched dense Transformer (11.15), with the gap growing from 0.67 PPL at 44M to 0.25 PPL at 1.04B. On SlimPajama at 1.04B, MoEUT reaches 9.36 PPL vs. 9.56 for the baseline. Zero-shot downstream average accuracy reaches 60.7% (MoEUT) vs. 59.8% (baseline) on C4 at 1.04B, with consistent small gains across BLiMP, PIQA, and ARC-E. In compute-matched comparisons (MACs), MoEUT outperforms the dense baseline by a large margin, and it also outperforms the non-shared σ-MoE model, confirming the benefit of layer sharing.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the standard pre-layernorm Transformer baseline, MoEUT consistently achieves lower perplexity across all scales (44M–1.04B) on C4, SlimPajama, and peS2o, with the PPL gap widening at larger scales (e.g., 0.25 PPL at 1.04B on C4). On zero-shot downstream tasks, MoEUT wins or ties on most benchmarks, with the clearest gains on BLiMP (+4.7 pp at 44M C4) and PIQA (+1.3 pp at 1.04B C4). Against the non-shared σ-MoE baseline (same feedforward shape, no layer sharing), MoEUT significantly outperforms, demonstrating that shared layers provide a clear advantage beyond mere sparsity. Against Sparse Universal Transformer (SUT), MoEUT wins by a large margin: at 44M on C4, SUT achieves 40.50 PPL vs. MoEUT's 18.30, and even after removing SUT's ACT mechanism (which the authors identify as a major source of degradation), SUT still underperforms both MoEUT and the dense baseline. In compute-matched comparisons (total MACs during training), MoEUT outperforms the dense baseline by a large margin, and wall-clock measurements show MoEUT uses 9.0 GB memory vs. 25.9 GB for a naive UT, while training 1.7× slower than the dense Transformer due to a suboptimal MoE kernel.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper combines three existing MoE techniques—σ-MoE for feedforward layers, SwitchHead for attention layers, and fine-grained expert sizing—with two novel components designed specifically for shared-layer (Universal Transformer) architectures: layer grouping (recurrently stacking small groups of non-shared MoE layers) and a peri-layernorm scheme that places normalization only before sigmoid/softmax activations rather than on the residual path. It departs from prior UT work (Dehghani et al. 2019, Tan et al. 2023) by making layer-shared Transformers competitive on parameter-dominated language modeling for the first time, and from prior MoE-for-Transformers work by showing that the shared-layer context demands different design choices (grouping, peri-layernorm) that are absent in non-shared MoE models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This breaks a long-standing barrier: UTs, despite their demonstrated superior compositional generalization on synthetic tasks, have never been competitive on real-world language modeling due to the parameter-compute ratio problem. By making UTs viable at up to 1B parameters, the paper opens the door to transferring UTs' generalization advantages to practical settings. Key limitations include the modest training regime (100k steps), a suboptimal Triton kernel making training 1.5–2× slower than dense Transformers, and the absence of massive-scale validation. Whether the thin performance margins persist at much larger scales, and whether the compositional generalization benefits actually manifest on real-world tasks, remain open questions.
