## Problem

Sharpness-aware minimization (SAM) and related flat-minima methods improve uni-modal domain generalization but yield limited gains in multi-modal domain generalization (MMDG). The paper identifies two root causes: modality competition during joint training inflates loss minima for each modality, and discrepant uni-modal flatness means the stronger modality dominates gradient perturbations, preventing simultaneous flat-minima discovery across modalities.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242132/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes paired multi-modal inputs (video clips, audio waveforms, and optical flow frames) from EPIC-Kitchens (8 actions across 3 kitchen domains) or HAC (7 actions across human/animal/cartoon domains). Each modality is encoded by a dedicated backbone (SlowFast for video and flow, ResNet-18 for audio) producing features of differing dimensions (2304, 2048, 512), which are then projected into a shared 128-dimensional representation space. The output is a single action class label, and the framework supports both multi-source (train on 2 domains, test on 1) and single-source (train on 1 domain, test on 2) generalization settings.

## Method

CMRF shifts flat-minima optimization from parameter space to a shared representation-space loss landscape, where modality-specific projectors map heterogeneous features into a common space. A moving-averaged teacher model generates stable representations that are cross-modally interpolated via Beta-distributed mixing ratios; the interpolation closer to each modality serves as its distillation target, flattening the high-loss region between modality-specific minima. A multi-modal supervised contrastive loss further narrows the semantic gap between modalities in the shared space, while an adaptive weighting scheme (based on teacher-model validation accuracy) assigns stronger distillation pressure to the better-generalizing modality. The final training objective combines multi-modal classification loss, per-modality classification losses, weighted distillation losses, and the contrastive term, with only the online student model receiving gradient updates.

## Results

On the multi-modal multi-source DG benchmark, CMRF achieves 63.91% average top-1 accuracy on EPIC-Kitchens and 71.91% on HAC with video+audio modalities, outperforming the strongest baselines (SAGM at 62.08%/66.20% and SimMMDG at 61.95%/67.83%) by 1.8–4.1 points. With all three modalities (video+audio+flow), CMRF reaches 67.36% on EPIC-Kitchens and 72.44% on HAC, surpassing EoA (65.08%/70.08%) and SimMMDG (64.20%/70.13%). Notably, CMRF also improves uni-modal generalization beyond standalone uni-modal training (e.g., 60.66% vs. 58.73% for video on EPIC-Kitchens), demonstrating that cross-modal knowledge transfer alleviates modality competition.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CMRF outperforms all nine baselines across every modality combination on both EPIC-Kitchens and HAC. Against the strongest uni-modal DG methods extended to multi-modal (SAGM, EoA), CMRF gains 1.8–4.4 points on average, with the largest margin on HAC video+audio (71.91% vs. SAGM 66.20%, +5.71%). Against the dedicated MMDG methods (RNA-Net, SimMMDG), CMRF leads by 2.3–8.8 points, with SimMMDG being the closest competitor (e.g., 67.83% vs. 71.91% on HAC V+A). The improvement is most pronounced for the weaker modality (audio/flow), where CMRF lifts audio accuracy from 38.62% (Base) to 43.13% on EPIC-Kitchens, exceeding even standalone uni-modal audio training (40.04%). In the single-source DG setting (Table 3), CMRF achieves 64.09% average on HAC vs. 62.73% (EoA) and 59.49% (SimMMDG), confirming robustness without domain labels. Comparison with modality-competition-specific methods (Grad Blending, OGM-GE, PMR in Table 7) shows CMRF achieves 71.91% test accuracy vs. 66.82% (Grad Blending), 64.33% (OGM-GE), and 65.24% (PMR), while also maintaining competitive in-domain validation (93.21% vs. 92.70–94.90%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the flat-minima-seeking lineage (SAM, SAGM, SWAD, EoA) from parameter-space to representation-space loss landscapes, drawing on the representation-space landscape concept from [23]. It departs from prior multi-modal DG work (RNA-Net, SimMMDG) by identifying two specific failure modes—modality competition and discrepant uni-modal flatness—and addressing them through cross-modal representation interpolation with knowledge distillation rather than norm alignment or unified training frameworks. The method repurposes Manifold Mixup-style interpolation and moving-average teacher-student distillation in a multi-modal context where parameter spaces are heterogeneous, making direct parameter-space SAM inapplicable.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first systematic diagnosis of why standard flatness-based DG methods underperform in multi-modal settings, showing that SAM can actively harm the weaker modality while helping the stronger one—a non-obvious interaction that practitioners extending SAM to multi-modal pipelines should be aware of. The representation-space formulation is architecturally agnostic and could transfer to other multi-modal DG scenarios beyond action recognition. Key limitations include the need for a validation set to estimate per-modality generalization for adaptive weighting, and evaluation restricted to two action-recognition datasets with at most three modalities, leaving open whether the approach scales to more heterogeneous modality combinations or larger-scale tasks.
