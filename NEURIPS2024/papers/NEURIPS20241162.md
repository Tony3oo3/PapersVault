## Problem

Neural network potentials and Hamiltonian prediction models require large, diverse datasets for training, but no existing QC dataset simultaneously provides energies, forces, Hamiltonian/overlap matrices, full wavefunction objects, and geometry optimization trajectories for drug-like molecules. The prior nablaDFT dataset covered ~1M molecules but lacked optimization trajectories and had fewer conformations, limiting its utility for conformational optimization research and multi-task model training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241162/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Models consume a molecular conformation (atom numbers and 3D Cartesian coordinates; 8–62 atoms from H, C, N, O, S, Cl, F, Br) and produce task-specific outputs: full Fock (Hamiltonian) and overlap matrices of dimension n_s × n_s for Hamiltonian prediction, a scalar energy plus an n×3 interatomic force matrix for NNP tasks, or an optimized 3D geometry for conformational optimization. Each of the 12.7M conformations carries 30+ QC properties computed at ωB97X-D/def2-SVP, including full Psi4 wavefunction objects that enable post-hoc derivation of additional properties such as Mulliken/Löwdin charges, Wiberg/Mayer bond indices, and electrostatic potentials without re-running DFT.

## Method

The dataset is built by generating 1–100 conformations per molecule from the MOSES drug-like set via RDKit, applying Butina clustering to retain 1–69 representative conformations per molecule (12.7M total), and computing all QC properties with Psi4 at ωB97X-D/def2-SVP, followed by a multi-step filtration protocol that removes unphysical samples. The benchmark defines 12 predefined train/test splits (four training sizes × conformation/structure/scaffold test types) to systematically study data scaling and generalization to unseen geometries or novel scaffolds. Ten models are implemented across three tasks: Hamiltonian prediction (SchNOrb, PhiSNet, QHNet), energy/force prediction (SchNet, PaiNN, DimeNet++, Graphormer3D, GemNet-OC, EquiformerV2, eSCN), and conformational optimization where NNP-predicted forces serve as anti-gradients for L-BFGS. A unique ∇²DFT_opt subset adds ~3M geometries from relaxation trajectories of ~60K conformations, enabling fine-tuning of NNPs specifically for iterative geometry optimization and demonstrating that trajectory-aware training substantially improves pct_success over standard NNP training.

## Results

On the ∇²DFT benchmark, the best energy prediction MAE is 0.08×10⁻² E_h (DimeNet++ on the structure test split trained on D_large), and the best forces prediction MAE is 0.02×10⁻² E_h/Å (GemNet-OC and eSCN, same setting). For conformational optimization, GemNet-OC-finetune achieves 94.55% success rate and 100.01% pct_T, while non-neural baselines (RDKit MMFF and xTB) achieve only 1.9% and 3.1% success rate respectively. For Hamiltonian prediction, PhiSNet achieves the lowest MAE of 0.18×10⁻³ E_h on the conformation test split, substantially outperforming SchNOrb (~20×10⁻³ E_h) and QHNet (~0.84×10⁻³ E_h).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among Hamiltonian prediction models, PhiSNet dominates with MAE ~0.18–0.37×10⁻³ E_h across all test splits, while SchNOrb is an order of magnitude worse (~19–21×10⁻³ E_h) and QHNet sits in between (~0.52–0.98×10⁻³ E_h). For energy and forces prediction, DimeNet++ and GemNet-OC achieve the best MAE on the structure test split (0.08 and 0.22×10⁻² E_h for energy; 0.07 and 0.02×10⁻² E_h/Å for forces with D_large training), while Transformer-based models (Graphormer3D, EquiformerV2) consistently underperform MPNN-based architectures. In conformational optimization, GemNet-OC-finetune (94.55% success) and PaiNN-finetune (84.35% success) dramatically outperform non-neural baselines: RDKit MMFF (1.9% success) and xTB (3.1% success), despite the latter having comparable RMSD values. All models show clear scaling benefits with training set size, with D_large training yielding 2–10× improvements over D_tiny for most metrics. The paper notes that models with separate force prediction heads (GemNet-OC, eSCN, EquiformerV2) achieve better forces MAE but slightly worse energy MAE compared to gradient-based force computation (SchNet, PaiNN, DimeNet++).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends nablaDFT [11] (Khrabrov et al., PCCP 2022), doubling the number of molecules and conformations while adding forces, full Hamiltonian/overlap matrices, Psi4 wavefunction objects, and—uniquely—geometry optimization (relaxation) trajectories for drug-like molecules. It positions itself against a broad landscape of QC datasets (QM9, QM7-X, ANI-1x, GEOM, QMugs, PubChemQC, SPICE) by being the first to combine all these properties at the ωB97X-D/def2-SVP DFT level for commercially available drug-like molecules sourced from MOSES/ZINC. The benchmark builds on the NNP literature (SchNet, PaiNN, DimeNet++, GemNet-OC, EquiformerV2, eSCN) and Hamiltonian prediction work (SchNOrb, PhiSNet, QHNet), while the conformational optimization evaluation extends the GOLF framework [46] to a much larger and more diverse molecular set.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dataset fills a critical gap: no prior resource provides the full suite of QC properties (energies, forces, Hamiltonians, overlap matrices, wavefunction objects) together with relaxation trajectories for drug-like molecules, making it a one-stop resource for training and evaluating NNPs, Hamiltonian predictors, and wavefunction models in pharmaceutical contexts. The 12 predefined train/test splits (conformation, scaffold, structure) enable systematic study of generalization to unseen geometries vs. unseen molecules, which prior benchmarks lacked. Key limitations include the absence of solvated systems, protein-ligand pairs, charged/open-shell species, and the fact that models were only trained on subsets (up to ~100K molecules) rather than the full 1.6M-molecule training set, leaving the true scaling behavior open. The finding that RMSD is a poor proxy for optimization quality (NNPs match non-neural methods in RMSD but far exceed them in pct_success) challenges a widely used evaluation metric in the field.
