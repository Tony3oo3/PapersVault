## Problem

Image pyramids, which process the same image at multiple resolutions with an identical large model, incur quadratic computational cost growth and remain the dominant strategy for top-performing detection and segmentation systems. Feature pyramids reduce this overhead but cannot fully replace image pyramids for very small or very large objects. The paper asks whether the multi-scale benefits of image pyramids can be preserved while substantially reducing computation by exploiting the fact that different resolutions do not require equally sized models.

## Input / Output

The network takes a single RGB image that is bilinearly resized to M different resolutions (e.g., 1568/1120/672 for a three-branch PIIP-TSB), with the highest-resolution copy fed to the smallest ViT and the lowest-resolution copy to the largest ViT. The output is a single merged feature map at the highest spatial resolution and the largest feature dimension (that of the smallest-model branch), which is then passed to a standard FPN for detection/segmentation or to per-branch classification heads for ImageNet. For classification, the final score is the average of logits from all branches rather than a merged feature.

## Method

PIIP inverts the conventional parameter-resolution pairing: smaller ViTs (e.g., ViT-T) process the highest-resolution images to capture local detail, while larger ViTs (e.g., ViT-B or ViT-L) process lower-resolution images to extract rich global semantics, since smaller images make large models computationally affordable. Three components compose the architecture: (1) multi-resolution branches initialized from off-the-shelf pre-trained ViTs of different sizes, (2) cross-branch interaction units inserted every few blocks that perform bidirectional deformable cross-attention between adjacent branches (with linear projections to align feature dimensions and FFN with 0.25 hidden ratio), and (3) a branch-merging module that projects all branch outputs to a common dimension, upsamples to the largest spatial size, and combines them via learnable scalar weights. Interaction scalars are initialized to zero so that pre-trained branch weights are not disrupted during early training, and a layer-wise learning-rate decay ratio compensates for differing branch depths. This design lets each resolution level leverage existing pre-trained foundation models without training the pyramid from scratch, while the cross-branch interactions ensure complementary information flows between scales.

## Results

On COCO val2017 with Mask R-CNN 1× schedule, PIIP-TSB achieves 47.9 APb and 42.6 APm, outperforming ViTDet-B (43.2 APb) by 4.7 points while using fewer FLOPs. At the Large scale, PIIP-SBL reaches 49.9 APb and 44.6 APm, surpassing ViTDet-L (46.2 APb) by 3.7 points. With the DINO detector and 3×+MS training, PIIP-SBL achieves 57.9 APb. On the InternViT-6B foundation model, PIIP-LH6B improves detection by 1.9% (55.7 vs 53.8 APb) and segmentation by 1.3% (59.65 vs 58.36 mIoU) while reducing computation by 43% and 58% respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PIIP consistently outperforms all single-branch ViT baselines: PIIP-TSB beats ViTDet-B by 4.7 APb (47.9 vs 43.2) and ViT-Adapter-B by 0.9 APb (47.9 vs 47.0); PIIP-SBL beats ViTDet-L by 3.7 APb (49.9 vs 46.2) and ViT-Adapter-L by 1.2 APb (49.9 vs 48.7). Against non-ViT backbones, PIIP-TSB surpasses Swin-B (46.9) and PVTv2-B5 (47.4) in detection, while PIIP-SBL edges out Swin-L (53.9) and RepLKNet-31L (53.9) under Cascade R-CNN 3×+MS, though ConvNeXt-L (54.8) retains a slight 0.3 APb lead. On semantic segmentation (ADE20K), PIIP-TSB exceeds InternImage-B by 1.4 mIoU (51.6 vs 50.2) and PIIP-SBL surpasses InternImage-L by 0.4 mIoU (54.3 vs 53.9). The most striking result is on InternViT-6B: PIIP-LH6B improves APb by 1.9 points (55.7 vs 53.8) and mIoU by 1.3 points (59.65 vs 58.36) while cutting FLOPs by 43% and 58% respectively, demonstrating that the parameter-inverted design scales effectively to billion-parameter foundation models.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of image pyramids (SNIP, AutoFocus), feature pyramids (FPN, NAS-FPN), and multi-branch architectures (CrossViT, HRNet), but introduces a fundamentally different design principle: the parameter-inverted paradigm, where smaller models process higher-resolution images and larger models process lower-resolution images. Unlike prior multi-branch approaches that use equivalent-sized models across scales or follow an intuitive parameter-direct assignment, PIIP repurposes existing pre-trained ViTs of different sizes (e.g., ViT-T/S/B/L) as branches, leveraging their complementary receptive fields and semantic levels through deformable cross-attention interactions. The architecture is positioned as a drop-in replacement for single-branch ViTDet-style backbones, requiring no training from scratch.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: PIIP reduces computational cost by 40–60% while matching or exceeding single-branch baselines, and notably improves InternViT-6B by 1.9% APb and 1.3% mIoU at 43–58% less FLOPs, offering a concrete path to deploying very large vision foundation models more efficiently. Key limitations include higher memory consumption due to increased parameter count, restriction to ViT-based backbones, and unexplored applicability to hierarchical CNNs or heterogeneous (CNN+ViT) architectures. Open questions include whether the parameter-inverted principle generalizes to non-transformer architectures, whether more advanced attention mechanisms (e.g., linear or sparse attention) can further reduce the interaction cost, and how the approach scales beyond three or four branches.
