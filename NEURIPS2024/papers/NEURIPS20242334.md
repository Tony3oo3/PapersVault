## Problem

Existing neural GED estimators either assume uniform edit costs or reduce GED to Euclidean distance between graph embeddings, making them unable to model the four edit operations (edge/node addition and deletion) with distinct, domain-specific costs. This paper addresses the gap by designing a neural GED estimator that explicitly incorporates general, non-uniform costs for all four edit operations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242334/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes two undirected graphs G and G' (padded with isolated nodes to a common size N) and four fixed scalar edit costs (a⊖, a⊕, b⊖, b⊕) specified at training time, and produces a single scalar GED prediction. The graphs are encoded independently via a shared GNN (late interaction), so corpus embeddings can be pre-computed and indexed for retrieval. The four costs remain constant across all training and test instances within a dataset, and the model is trained with MSE loss against exact GED ground truth computed by the F2 solver.

## Method

GRAPHEDX formulates GED as a quadratic assignment problem with four asymmetric ReLU-based distance terms, then replaces each QAP term with a differentiable neural set divergence surrogate (ALIGNDIFF, DIFFALIGN, or XOR-DIFFALIGN) computed over node and node-pair embedding sets. A Gumbel-Sinkhorn network (PERMNET) produces a soft node permutation matrix P from contextual node embeddings, and the node-pair alignment S is derived from P via S[(u,v),(u',v')] = P[u,u']P[v,v'] + P[u,v']P[v,u'], ensuring edge-consistent alignment. Both edges and non-edges receive non-zero trainable embeddings: an MPNN computes node representations, and a decoupled MLP maps concatenated node-pair features plus edge-presence indicators to node-pair embeddings, capturing sensitivity to global structure. The XOR-DIFFALIGN variant applies a XOR gate on adjacency entries to zero out costs for edge-to-edge and non-edge-to-non-edge mappings, aligning the surrogate with the combinatorial structure of GED. The late-interaction design (graph pairs interact only at the final divergence layer) enables efficient ANN-based retrieval via LSH or inverted indexes.

## Results

GRAPHEDX achieves the lowest MSE across all five main datasets under both uniform and non-uniform cost settings. Under uniform costs, it reaches MSE of 0.492 (Mutag), 0.429 (Code2), 0.781 (Molhiv), 0.764 (Molpcba), and 0.565 (AIDS). Under non-uniform costs (b⊖=3, b⊕=1, a⊖=2, a⊕=1), it achieves 1.134, 1.478, 1.804, 1.677, and 1.252 respectively. The advantage over the best baseline is particularly large for non-uniform costs, where GMN-Match and GREED degrade catastrophically (MSE > 11 on Code2) while GRAPHEDX remains stable.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GRAPHEDX outperforms all nine baselines on every dataset under both cost settings. Under uniform costs, the closest competitors are ERIC (best on Mutag at 0.719 vs. 0.492) and EGSC (best on AIDS at 0.627 vs. 0.565), with GRAPHEDX improving MSE by 15–51% depending on the dataset. Under non-uniform costs, the gap widens dramatically: GMN-Match, GMN-Embed, and GREED collapse to MSE values above 11 (e.g., 69.210 for GMN-Match on Mutag), while GRAPHEDX stays below 1.8. Among the more robust baselines, EGSC is the strongest competitor on Code2 (3.957 vs. 1.478) and Molhiv (2.371 vs. 1.804), while ERIC leads on Molpcba (2.057 vs. 1.677) and AIDS (1.581 vs. 1.252). The paper further shows that even when the cost-guided distance is retrofitted onto GMN-Match and GMN-Embed (replacing their Euclidean distance), their MSE drops substantially (e.g., GMN-Match on Mutag non-uniform: 69.210 → 1.592), confirming that the cost-aware distance formulation itself is a key contributor to GRAPHEDX's advantage.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the classical formulation of GED as a Quadratic Assignment Problem (QAP) and the Gumbel-Sinkhorn framework for differentiable permutation matrices, but departs from prior neural GED methods (SimGNN, ERIC, EGSC, GMN, GREED) in two key ways: it explicitly models four asymmetric edit costs rather than assuming uniform costs, and it replaces the QAP objective with a family of differentiable neural set divergence surrogates (ALIGNDIFF, DIFFALIGN, XOR-DIFFALIGN) that incorporate GED-specific inductive biases. The XOR-DIFFALIGN surrogate, which zeros out edge-to-edge and non-edge-to-non-edge mapping costs via an XOR operator on adjacency entries, is a novel design that no prior neural GED method employs. The late-interaction architecture (independent graph encoding followed by alignment-based distance) also distinguishes it from early-interaction methods like H2MN and GraphSim, enabling a-priori indexing for retrieval.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ability to handle non-uniform edit costs opens GED to applications like subgraph isomorphism, maximum common subgraph detection, and domain-specific graph comparison where different edit operations carry different semantic weights. The late-interaction design makes the model amenable to approximate nearest neighbor search (LSH, inverted index, HNSW), which is practically important for large-scale graph retrieval. Key limitations include the O(N²) memory requirement for node-pair embeddings (both edges and non-edges), which constrains scalability to larger graphs, and the assumption of fixed costs across all graph pairs in a dataset. Open questions include extending the formulation to richly-attributed graphs with per-node-pair variable costs and understanding whether the non-edge embedding strategy generalizes beyond the small-graph regimes tested here.
