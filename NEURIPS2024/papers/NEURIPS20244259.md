## Problem

Randomized Smoothing (RS) provides certified adversarial robustness but is static at test time, unable to adapt its computation to individual inputs. Existing adaptive defences are either heuristic and unproven, or offer only limited adaptivity (e.g., input-dependent variance). No prior work provides a sound, high-dimensional framework for certifying multi-step defences where later steps condition on earlier steps' outputs, and RS in particular suffers a √d dimensionality penalty under the L∞ threat model that limits certification on high-dimensional images.

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images as input (32×32×3 for CIFAR-10, 160×160 for CelebA, standard resolution for ImageNet; the 20kBG benchmark extends CIFAR-10 to up to 96×96×3 by compositing onto distractor backgrounds) and produces a class label together with a certified L∞ radius r_X^∞. Certification uses Monte Carlo estimation with 100 samples to select the most probable class and 50,000 samples to estimate p+, with a 0.05 failure probability. The two-step pipeline internally produces a noisy intermediate image m1, a pixel-wise mask w(m1) ∈ [0,1]^d, a second noisy image m2, and a variance-minimizing weighted average of m1 and m2 before the base classifier.

## Method

ARS reconnects RS to f-Differential Privacy (f-DP), enabling the use of DP's adaptive composition theorem (μ = √(μ₁² + μ₂²) for Gaussian mechanisms) to certify multi-step computation where later steps depend on earlier outputs. The architecture has two steps: M1 adds Gaussian noise (σ₁) to the input, and a U-Net mask model w(·) post-processes the noisy image into a per-pixel mask in [0,1]^d; M2 then adds Gaussian noise with variance scaled by ‖w(m₁)‖₂²/d to the masked input w(m₁)⊙X. The key mechanism is that the mask adaptively reduces the effective input dimension, which shrinks the noise variance in M2 (scaling as ‖w‖₂² ≤ d rather than d), directly improving both accuracy and the certified radius under L∞ attacks. A variance-minimizing weighted average of m1 and m2 is fed to a ResNet base classifier, and the noise budget σ is split between steps via the f-DP composition formula (in practice σ₁ = σ₂ = √2·σ). The entire pipeline is trained end-to-end with supervised classification, and the certified radius is r_X^∞ = (1/(2√(d(1/σ₁² + 1/σ₂²))))(Φ⁻¹(p₊) − Φ⁻¹(p₋)).

## Results

On the CIFAR-10 20kBG benchmark, ARS achieves up to 15 percentage points higher standard accuracy than the best baseline at k=96, σ=1.0 (56.3% vs. 41.3% for Cohen et al.), and maintains stable or increasing accuracy as input dimension grows from k=32 to k=96 while all baselines degrade. On CelebA (unaligned, 160×160), ARS improves standard accuracy by up to 7.7 points (91.0% vs. 83.3% at σ=1.0) and certified accuracy at r∞=0.004 from 45.0% to 79.3% at σ=0.5. On ImageNet, ARS (end-to-end) improves certified accuracy at r∞=0.001 from 48.9% to 50.5% at σ=0.5, and at r∞=0.005 from 21.7% to 23.1% at σ=1.0, while maintaining or slightly improving standard accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ARS outperforms all three baselines across nearly all settings. Against standard RS (Cohen et al., 2019), ARS wins by 1–15 percentage points depending on dimensionality and noise, with the gap widening as k increases (e.g., 13.2 points at k=96, σ=0.5; 22.5 points at k=96, σ=1.0). Against UniCR (Hong et al., 2022), which adapts the noise distribution at train time but is static at test time, ARS consistently leads by 3–15 points, with the largest margins at high k and high σ. Against Sükenik et al. (2021), the only prior sound input-adaptive method (which adapts variance based on train-test distance), ARS dominates by 5–59 points, demonstrating that high-dimensional adaptive masking is far more expressive than scalar variance adaptation. The one exception is at k=32 (original CIFAR-10 with no distractor background), where the static mask baseline slightly edges out ARS, likely because there is insufficient irrelevant information for the adaptive mask to exploit.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reconnects Randomized Smoothing (RS) to its Differential Privacy (DP) roots, which had been set aside in favor of the tighter hypothesis-testing analysis of Cohen et al. (2019). By recasting RS through f-Differential Privacy (Dong et al., 2019, 2022), the authors unlock DP's adaptive composition theorem to certify multi-step, input-dependent computation for the first time in a sound manner. This departs from prior adaptive RS attempts (Sukenik et al., 2021; Alfarra et al., 2022a; Hong et al., 2022), which were either unsound in their end-to-end analysis or restricted to low-dimensional (scalar variance) adaptation. The practical instantiation—a two-step U-Net mask followed by a ResNet classifier with input-dependent noise variance—has no direct precedent in the certified-robustness literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ARS provides the first rigorous framework for certifying test-time adaptive defences, opening a path to formally analyzing a broad class of previously heuristic adaptive methods (input purification, test-time optimization, adaptive variance). The 'no cost to adaptivity' result—that multi-step Gaussian measurements compose to a single measurement with no loss in certified radius—is theoretically clean and suggests further multi-step architectures are possible. Key limitations include a ~2× inference overhead from the extra forward pass, the fact that masking-based variance reduction only benefits L∞ certification (not L2), and the absence of experiments combining ARS with other RS improvements (adversarial training, diffusion denoising, double sampling). Open questions include whether privacy odometers can enable fully-adaptive per-input variance, and whether the framework extends to other norms or generative-model-based purification.
