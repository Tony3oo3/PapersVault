## Problem

Standard LM evaluations report perplexity on a single monolithic held-out distribution, implicitly assuming that fit to one mix of domains extrapolates to all others. This obscures whether scaling lifts all domains equally, which pretraining corpora produce the most robust fit, and where models have complete gaps in exposure. No prior benchmark provides fine-grained, domain-level perplexity with the experimental controls (decontamination, fixed vocabulary, fixed training order) needed for fair cross-model comparison.

## Input / Output

PALOMA takes a pretrained autoregressive LM and evaluates it on 546 fine-grained domains drawn from 16 sources (English text and code), subsampled to ~100K tokens per domain and ~1M per source. Documents are fed individually (not concatenated) with a fixed GPT-NeoX-20B vocabulary of ~32K tokens, and outputs include per-domain perplexity, per-source macro-averaged perplexity, average log-likelihood per vocabulary type, and bits-per-byte for cross-vocabulary comparisons. The evaluation code records per-type likelihoods and token counts during a single inference pass, enabling decomposition of aggregate perplexity into contributions from individual vocabulary strings.

## Method

PALOMA curates 16 existing and 2 new datasets (top-100 subreddits, top-100 programming languages from DOLMA) into 546 domains using metadata such as URLs, subreddit names, file extensions, and Wikipedia ontology. Five experimental controls ensure comparability: (G1) Bloom-filter-based sub-document decontamination removing any training document containing a contaminated paragraph, (G2) fixed training data order via shared tokenization, seed, and device-invariant dataloading, (G3) stratified subsampling to equalize metric variance across domains, (G4) a fixed vocabulary, and (G5) document-by-document inference without concatenation or overlap. Six 1B-parameter transformer baselines are pretrained for ~150B tokens on DOLMA, The Pile, RedPajama, Falcon RefinedWeb, C4, and MC4-EN under identical hyperparameters to isolate the effect of pretraining corpus composition. Case studies then use per-domain and per-vocabulary-type perplexity to reveal that Common Crawl-only models exhibit anomalous, non-monotonic fit gaps on specific domains, and that ~5% of high-frequency vocabulary types account for roughly 50% of total loss.

## Results

Across 546 domains from 16 sources, the 6 baseline 1B models reveal that pretraining on Common Crawl-only data (C4, MC4-EN, Falcon RefinedWeb) produces extreme and non-monotonic perplexity spikes: the C4 model reaches 391,171 perplexity on the arXiv domain in RedPajama, while Falcon RefinedWeb and MC4-EN hit 21,652 and 1,409 on the Max music programming language domain. In contrast, models pretrained on heterogeneous corpora (DOLMA, The Pile, RedPajama) show stable, monotonic improvement across domains. Additionally, only 5% of vocabulary types account for roughly 50% of total perplexity loss, and the C4 and MC4-EN baselines actually worsen with more training on 65 and 43 domains respectively, while the median improvement gap between most- and least-improved domains reaches 1.94× when excluding these two baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's central comparison is among 6 self-trained 1B-parameter baselines (each trained on ~150B tokens with identical hyperparameters, tokenizer, and decontamination) that differ only in pretraining corpus: C4, MC4-EN, Falcon RefinedWeb, DOLMA, The Pile, and RedPajama. The three Common Crawl-only baselines (C4, MC4-EN, Falcon RefinedWeb) consistently exhibit higher and more erratic perplexity on non-web domains (e.g., academic papers, code, fringe communities), with the C4 model showing a 391,171 perplexity outlier on arXiv in RedPajama versus 14 on the overlapping academic domain in DOLMA. The three heterogeneous-corpus baselines (DOLMA, The Pile, RedPajama) maintain stable relative gaps across domains throughout training, suggesting short training runs may predict final fit. For scaling analysis, Pythia models (160M, 1B, 7B) are used as external references, confirming that parameter scaling universally improves perplexity but at unequal rates across domains (2.02× gap between most- and least-improved domains). Compared to prior benchmarks, PALOMA is the first to implement sub-document-level decontamination, fixed training order, stratified domain sampling, and a fixed vocabulary, covering 546 domains versus 22 (The Pile), 216 (M2D2), 99 (C4-100-Domains), or 14 (HELM).

## Relation to Prior Work

PALOMA extends the line of multi-domain perplexity benchmarks (The Pile, M2D2, C4-100-Domains) by scaling from tens to 546 fine-grained domains and, critically, by being the first to implement sub-document-level decontamination, fixed training data order, stratified domain sampling, and a fixed vocabulary (GPT-NeoX-20B) as standard experimental controls. It inherits the evaluation format and bits-per-byte metric from The Pile (Gao et al., 2020) and draws evaluation data from DOLMA (Soldaini et al., 2024), from which it introduces two new datasets (top-100 subreddits and top-100 programming languages). Conceptually, the paper departs from the prevailing practice of reporting a single monolithic validation perplexity by arguing that perplexity must be decomposed across domains to reveal what models actually learn, following the model-behavior analysis tradition of Holtzman et al. (2023) and McCoy et al. (2023).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PALOMA provides the most fine-grained and methodologically controlled perplexity benchmark to date, with 6 matched 1B baseline models that enable fair comparisons of pretraining corpus composition. The finding that Common Crawl-only models exhibit extreme, non-monotonic perplexity spikes on specific domains (e.g., 391,171 on arXiv) while models with heterogeneous data show stable, predictable scaling is practically important for data curation decisions. The discovery that just 5% of high-frequency vocabulary types account for roughly 50% of total perplexity loss challenges the interpretability of aggregated perplexity and motivates finer-grained metrics. Key limitations include the restriction to English and code, heuristic domain definitions based on metadata, and the fact that decontamination removes entire documents (potentially over-removing frequently quoted texts), leaving open questions about proper code decontamination and cross-lingual generalization.
