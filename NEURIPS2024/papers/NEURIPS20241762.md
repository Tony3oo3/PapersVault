## Problem

Active learning for 3D molecular graphs lacks a principled algorithm that accounts for 3D geometric information in sample selection. Existing AL methods for 3D GNNs do not specifically capture molecular geometry, and diversity computing between 3D molecules with different planar graphs (different atom counts, topologies) remains unsolved. This is critical because molecular annotation via DFT is prohibitively expensive (O(n_e^3) complexity), making efficient sample selection essential.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 3D molecular graphs where atoms are nodes with Cartesian coordinates and edges are defined by a cutoff distance, partitioned into a labeled set and an unlabeled pool. For each unlabeled molecule, it computes a 48-dimensional distributional vector (16 dims each for reference distances, triangles, and cross angles, each derived from 4 statistical moments across 4 reference points) and a scalar uncertainty score from the BGGNN. The output is a binary selection vector identifying k molecules per AL iteration for annotation, plus molecular property predictions (e.g., dipole moment, HOMO/LUMO, polarizability, atomic forces) and their predictive variances.

## Method

The framework combines two novel components in a quadratic programming formulation. For diversity, the paper introduces three levels of 3D graph isometries (reference distance, triangular, cross-angular) that are provably at least as expressive as the Geometric Weisfeiler-Leman test, setting a theoretical upper bound on all existing 3D GNNs; distributions of the associated geometries are summarized via translated moments (mean, variance, skewness, kurtosis) at four reference points (centroid, closest, farthest, farthest-from-farthest) to yield a fixed 48-dim vector per molecule, enabling diversity computation between molecules of different sizes. For uncertainty, a Bayesian geometric GNN (BGGNN) wraps SphereNet or DimeNet++ with concrete dropout layers after linear layers, using MC-dropout to approximate the Bayesian posterior and compute heteroscedastic predictive variance per molecule. The final batch selection solves a QP problem maximizing z^T r + λ z^T D z subject to a cardinality constraint, using a GPU-accelerated OSQP solver with a greedy rounding step to recover the binary solution.

## Results

On QM9 (SphereNet backbone), the proposed method achieves the lowest MAE across all four properties (mu, alpha, homo, lumo) at every AL iteration, with improvements statistically significant (p < 10⁻⁴) against all baselines. On MD17 (Aspirin atomic forces), the method similarly attains the lowest MAE across 7 AL iterations. The paired t-test p-values range from 1.7×10⁻⁸ (homo vs. Evidential) to 8.1×10⁻⁴ (alpha vs. Learning Loss), confirming consistent superiority. Computation time per AL iteration is 64.9±7.5 min, slightly above non-iterative baselines (~53–56 min) but well below Coreset (127±3.5 min).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all four main baselines (Random, Learning Loss, Coreset, Evidential Uncertainty) on every property and dataset tested, with all p-values well below 0.05. Coreset is the strongest baseline, marginally outperforming Random and Learning Loss, but is still consistently beaten by the proposed method (e.g., p = 1.51×10⁻⁴ on mu). Evidential Uncertainty performs worst across all properties, with error values so high that it was excluded from the main figures for readability. In the SOAP comparison (Appendix A.10), the proposed diversity component reduces mu MAE from 1322 (SOAP, iteration 7) to 1092 (Ours, iteration 7) and lumo MAE from 667 to 604, with p-values of 4.20×10⁻⁶ and 2.51×10⁻⁶ respectively. Against BatchBALD (Appendix A.10), the proposed method wins on all four properties with p-values ranging from 1.54×10⁻⁴ (homo) to 4.86×10⁻⁶ (alpha). The framework is also robust to backbone choice, showing the same pattern with DimeNet++ on mu and lumo (p-values down to 1.56×10⁻⁶).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends active learning for molecular property prediction (Smith et al. 2018, 2021; van der Oord et al. 2023) by introducing a geometrically-aware diversity component specifically designed for 3D molecular graphs, where prior AL methods either ignored 3D geometry or relied on learned embeddings. The diversity method draws conceptual inspiration from the USR algorithm (Ballester and Richards 2007) for distributional shape descriptors, but replaces distance-only statistics with a hierarchy of three geometric isometries (reference distance, triangular, cross-angular) that are provably at least as expressive as the Geometric Weisfeiler-Leman test (Joshi et al. 2023), which itself upper-bounds all existing 3D GNNs. For uncertainty, the paper adapts MC-dropout Bayesian inference (Gal and Ghahramani 2016) to 3D GNN backbones (SphereNet, DimeNet++), and formulates batch selection as a QP problem in the spirit of Coreset (Sener and Savarese 2018) and BatchBALD (Kirsch et al. 2019).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The geometric isometries provide a model-agnostic, theoretically grounded diversity measure that sets an upper bound on the expressiveness of all existing 3D GNNs, making it a useful diagnostic tool for evaluating 3D GNN architectures beyond this AL application. The framework's practical impact is tempered by its evaluation on small-molecule benchmarks (QM9, MD17); scalability to larger datasets like OC20 and to expensive annotation regimes (e.g., Schrödinger equation-based ground states) remains open. The finding that the diversity component alone nearly matches the full uncertainty+diversity method suggests that geometric structure may carry most of the informative signal for molecular AL, a hypothesis worth testing at scale.
