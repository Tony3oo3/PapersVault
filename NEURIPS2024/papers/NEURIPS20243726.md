## Problem

Global 3D semantic understanding from monocular remote sensing imagery is hindered by the high cost of height annotations, the geographic skew of existing datasets toward developed regions, and the absence of synthetic-to-real domain adaptation methods for multi-task dense prediction in RS. No prior work has combined a large-scale, geographically diverse synthetic RS dataset with a UDA pipeline that jointly adapts both land cover mapping and height estimation to real-world scenes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243726/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 512×512 monocular RGB RS tiles (GSD 0.05–1 m, nadir or oblique) from the synthetic SynRS3D source domain (69,667 images with 8-class land cover labels and normalized DSM height maps) and unlabeled real RS images from the target domain. It produces per-pixel outputs: an 8-class land cover segmentation (Bareland, Rangeland, Developed Space, Roads, Trees, Water, Agricultural Land, Buildings) and a continuous height map in meters (nDSM, i.e., height above ground). The DINOv2 ViT-L encoder feeds a DPT decoder that branches into the two prediction heads, with random 392×392 crops during training to maintain 14-pixel divisibility.

## Method

RS3DAda is a multi-task self-training UDA framework built on a teacher-student (EMA) architecture. Source-domain images are first style-translated via Fourier Domain Adaptation, Histogram Matching, and Pixel Distribution Matching to narrow the synthetic-to-real appearance gap before supervised training. For target-domain pseudo-labels, the land cover branch generates confidence-thresholded class maps, while the height branch applies a ground-guided refinement that zeros out pixels classified as ground (leveraging the empirical observation that the network learns ground early) and a consistency check comparing predictions on original vs. strongly augmented (ClassMix, ColorJitter, GaussianBlur) inputs. A frozen DINOv2 encoder provides a cosine-similarity feature constraint to prevent the student encoder from drifting. The key insight is the mutual reinforcement between the two branches: the land cover branch supplies the ground mask that corrects height pseudo-labels, and the stabilized height regression in turn improves land cover confidence, yielding a 1.94 mIoU gain over DAFormer on OEM and outperforming real-data-trained models on challenging Target Domain 2 height estimation.

## Results

On height estimation with DINOv2+DPT, RS3DAda reduces average MAE from 6.117 m (source-only) to 4.866 m on Target Domain 2 (challenging regions: Tokyo, Nagoya, ATL, ARG, Vaihingen), outperforming models trained directly on real Target Domain 1 data (5.378 m MAE). On Target Domain 1 (6 common US/EU datasets), RS3DAda achieves 2.148 m MAE, still below the 1.272 m of real-data-trained models. For land cover mapping on OEM, RS3DAda reaches 48.23 mIoU, surpassing DAFormer (46.29) and source-only (40.70), though a 20.11 mIoU gap remains versus the oracle (68.34). In source-only scenarios, SynRS3D outperforms SMARS and GTAH on 9 of 11 real height estimation datasets and achieves the best mIoU across all four land cover benchmarks (JAX, OMA, Vaihingen, Potsdam).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against synthetic dataset baselines, SynRS3D outperforms SMARS [76] and GTAH [104] in source-only height estimation on 9 of 11 real datasets, attributed to its smaller domain gap and precise nDSM height calculation (vs. noisy DSM-to-nDSM conversion in competitors). In source-only land cover mapping, SynRS3D beats both SMARS and SyntheWorld [86] on all four benchmarks (e.g., mIoU 67.89 vs. 64.52 vs. 60.14 on JAX). Among UDA methods, RS3DAda outperforms DAFormer [35] by 1.94 mIoU on OEM (48.23 vs. 46.29) and surpasses both AdaptSeg [91] and DADA [94] on the DeepLabv2+ResNet101 architecture: RS3DAda achieves 3.275 m MAE on T.D.1 (vs. DADA's 3.615) and 6.708 m on T.D.2 (vs. DADA's 6.997), while also leading in land cover mIoU (22.55 on OEM vs. DADA's 21.24 and AdaptSeg's 20.06; 47.28 on V+P+J+O vs. DADA's 46.44 and AdaptSeg's 40.00). The key differentiator is that RS3DAda is the first synthetic-to-real multi-task UDA method for RS, leveraging cross-task pseudo-label refinement (ground-guided height correction) and DINOv2 feature constraints that are ineffective with weaker ImageNet-pretrained CNN backbones.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of synthetic RS dataset construction (SMARS, GTAH, SyntheWorld, Synthinel-1) by introducing a procedural generation pipeline in Blender augmented with GPT-4 text prompts and Stable Diffusion textures, producing the largest synthetic RS dataset to date with six city styles and eight land cover classes. For the adaptation side, it builds on self-training and teacher-student frameworks from semantic segmentation UDA (Mean Teacher, DAFormer, DADA) but departs by being the first to formulate synthetic-to-real multi-task UDA for RS, coupling a land cover segmentation branch with a height regression branch through ground-guided pseudo-label refinement. The use of DINOv2 features as a frozen constraint and statistical image translation (FDA, histogram matching) are borrowed from broader CV domain adaptation literature and repurposed for the RS-specific synthetic-to-real setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper directly addresses the geographic bias in RS datasets (most originating from Europe and North America) by providing a geographically diverse synthetic alternative, and demonstrates that a model trained solely on synthetic data with RS3DAda can outperform models trained on real data in challenging, underrepresented regions (Target Domain 2). However, a 20-point mIoU gap to the oracle on OEM and a persistent MAE gap on Target Domain 1 indicate the synthetic-to-real appearance gap remains substantial, and the dataset does not yet capture the full diversity of global urban morphology. Open questions include whether the multi-task coupling (land cover stabilizing height pseudo-labels) generalizes beyond the specific 8-class/height formulation, and whether more photorealistic rendering or generative augmentation can close the remaining domain gap without sacrificing the precise geometric annotations that make the dataset valuable.
