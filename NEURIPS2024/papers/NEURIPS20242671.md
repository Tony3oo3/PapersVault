## Problem

Existing shuffle coding for unordered data structures (multisets, graphs) suffers from two critical practical limitations: complete joint shuffle coding incurs a prohibitive initial bit cost that renders it useless for one-shot compression of a single object, and it requires exact computation of automorphism groups, which is intractable for large graphs. No prior method achieves near-optimal lossless compression rates on large-scale unordered structures at practical speeds.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single unordered object — a multiset (represented as a string over an alphabet) or an unlabeled/labeled graph (represented as an ordered adjacency structure) — and produces a lossless compressed bitstream using rANS as the underlying entropy coder. Unlike joint shuffle coding, it operates in a one-shot setting where no prior compressed data is assumed, and it scales to graphs with up to one billion edges (3.4 GB uncompressed) at speeds of megabytes per second. The compressed output can be decoded back to the original object without any side information beyond the model parameters.

## Method

The paper introduces two composable variants of shuffle coding. Autoregressive shuffle coding generalizes the multiset codec of Severo et al. to arbitrary unordered objects by defining a prefixing chain and progressively decoding orbit information via bits-back while autoregressively encoding slices, thereby realizing the order discount in the one-shot rate rather than only in the net rate. Incomplete shuffle coding replaces exact automorphism group computation with color refinement (1-WL), approximating symmetries at negligible rate cost and enabling polynomial-time runtimes for graphs. A chunking mechanism further reduces the number of expensive color refinement passes from n to a fixed constant c, yielding an overall runtime of O(c·m + n log n) for graphs with m edges. Adaptive entropy coding via weighted order-statistic trees (or Fenwick trees for chunked settings) avoids recomputing codecs at each recursive step, and the method is model-agnostic, supporting any autoregressive distribution over slices such as Erdős-Rényi or the autoregressive Pólya urn model.

## Results

On the SZIP graph dataset, incomplete autoregressive shuffle coding with the autoregressive Pólya urn (AP) model achieves state-of-the-art compression rates of 3.27–7.32 bits per edge, outperforming SZIP on 4 of 6 graphs (e.g., 3.32 vs. 3.81 bpe on USAir97, 5.24 vs. 5.28 bpe on geom). Compression speeds reach up to 1.4 MB/s on 8 threads, scaling near-linearly to a 1-billion-edge graph (3.4 GB) where the method saves 0.8 GB. On multisets, the implementation matches the optimal rate of Severo et al. (2023a) within 64 bits while being over two orders of magnitude faster.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SZIP (Choi & Szpankowski, 2012), the AP model achieves lower bits-per-edge on 4 of 6 SZIP graphs (e.g., 3.32 vs. 3.81 on USAir97, 7.55 vs. 8.49 on homo) while being 10–150× faster in compression speed (e.g., 1025 vs. 7 kB/s on Internet-as); SZIP retains a slight edge only on Collaboration-Erdos (5.08 vs. 5.55 bpe). Against complete joint shuffle coding (Kunze et al., 2024), the incomplete variant achieves up to a 1,000,000× speedup on social network graphs (e.g., 215 vs. 0.44 kB/s on TU social networks) with a rate increase of only 0.1–7.1%. Against the multiset codec of Severo et al. (2023a), the proposed autoregressive implementation matches compression rates within 64 bits across all multiset sizes (1k to 10M elements) while running over 100× faster, confirming that the speed bottleneck in the original was implementation rather than algorithmic.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the shuffle coding framework introduced by Kunze et al. (2024) for sequences of unordered objects and the one-shot multiset codec of Severo et al. (2023a) to arbitrary unordered data structures (graphs, hypergraphs) in the one-shot setting. It departs from complete joint shuffle coding by replacing the intractable exact automorphism group computation with color refinement (Weisfeiler-Lehman), and from the multiset-specific autoregressive codec by introducing a general prefixing-chain abstraction with pop/push operations. The bits-back + ANS machinery from Townsend et al. (2019) remains the underlying entropy coding substrate, but the paper adds chunking and adaptive entropy coding (Fenwick trees, weighted AVL trees) to achieve near-linear runtime O(c·m + n log n).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper makes lossless compression of large unordered graphs practical for the first time, handling a 3.4 GB / 1-billion-edge graph at ~0.4 MB/s while saving 0.8 GB, a regime where prior methods (complete joint shuffle coding, SZIP) are infeasible or suboptimal. Because shuffle coding is a pure entropy coder, the statistical model can be swapped freely (Erdős-Rényi, Pólya urn, or future neural graph models), making the framework a general-purpose compression layer rather than a fixed algorithm. Open questions include optimizing the ordered autoregressive model (currently ~50% of runtime), nonuniform chunk sizes, and parallelization via vectorized ANS; the method also does not yet handle attributed or directed graphs in its experiments.
