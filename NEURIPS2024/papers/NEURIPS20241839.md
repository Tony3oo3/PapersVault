## Problem

In interactive ML systems with multiple specialized services, the initialization of service parameters critically determines the quality of downstream learning dynamics, yet two practical barriers make good initialization hard: (i) user preference data is only available through bandit (zeroth-order) feedback after services are deployed, and (ii) the total loss landscape is non-convex, so gradient-based methods from random starts get stuck in arbitrarily poor local minima. No prior method provides a principled, sample-efficient initialization with approximation guarantees under these information constraints.

## Input / Output

The algorithm receives a set of n users with unknown preference parameters φ_i ∈ R^d, a target number of services k, and only scalar loss evaluations L_i(θ, φ_i) at deployed service parameters (bandit access). It outputs k service parameter vectors Θ_k = {θ_1, …, θ_k} ⊂ R^d, each centered on the queried preference of a selected user. In the fair variant, demographic group assignments γ: [n] → A are additionally provided, and the selection probabilities are reweighted by 1/|γ(i)| to balance across groups of varying sizes.

## Method

AcQUIre is a sequential, adaptive algorithm that generalizes k-means++ to unknown, heterogeneous loss functions with only bandit feedback. At each step t, it observes all users' losses on the current t−1 services, then samples a new user with probability proportional to their current loss (so poorly-served users are more likely to be selected), queries that user's full preference φ_l, and centers the next service at φ_l. The key theoretical contribution is an approximation ratio of K_OPT(2 + log k) on expected total loss, where K_OPT is an instance-dependent constant capturing the alignment of loss geometries across users via pairwise constants c_ij from approximate triangle inequalities (Assumption 2.2). A fair variant (Fair AcQUIre) reweights selection by inverse group size, yielding an m·K_fair(2 + log k) guarantee on the maximum average group loss. For linear predictors with sub-Gaussian features, the paper further provides a finite-sample generalization bound requiring n_i = Ω(σ_i⁴√N log(2/δ)/ε²) samples per subpopulation.

## Results

On the Census commute-time prediction task (N=7025 subpopulations), AcQUIre and Fair AcQUIre consistently achieve the lowest average loss across all values of k, while greedy and epsilon-greedy baselines are competitive only for k < 10 before becoming myopic. On the MovieLens10M recommendation task (N=1000 subpopulations), AcQUIre achieves the lowest average excess error on the held-out test set. In terms of runtime, AcQUIre completes initialization in ~300 seconds for 1 billion users and <900 seconds for 5000 services, whereas greedy and epsilon-greedy exceed 10^5 seconds even for 10 million users. The theoretical guarantee bounds the expected total loss within K_OPT·(2+log k) of the global optimum, generalizing the k-means++ ratio of 8(2+log k).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares AcQUIre against five user-selection baselines: Random, Greedy (argmax loss), Epsilon-Greedy (argmax loss + noise), and their group-size-balanced variants. On the Census task, greedy and epsilon-greedy perform well for small k (<10) but become myopic as k grows, at which point even random sampling surpasses them; AcQUIre and Fair AcQUIre remain the top two methods across the full range of k. On the MovieLens10M task, AcQUIre is the best data-collection strategy throughout, with epsilon-greedy as the closest competitor. For the fairness objective (worst-group average loss), Fair AcQUIre outperforms Balanced Random, Balanced Greedy, and Balanced Epsilon Greedy. In runtime, AcQUIre is orders of magnitude faster than greedy and epsilon-greedy (which require evaluating all n candidates at each step), matching the speed of random initialization while achieving substantially better loss. In downstream optimization experiments (generalized k-means and multiplicative weights), services initialized by AcQUIre converge faster and to a lower final total loss than those initialized by any baseline, confirming that initialization quality directly affects the quality of the converged solution.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly generalizes the k-means++ initialization algorithm (Arthur & Vassilvitskii, 2007) from the setting of known points with known Euclidean distances to a much broader class where user preferences are unknown, loss functions are diverse (satisfying only approximate triangle inequalities), and the provider has only bandit (zeroth-order) feedback. The algorithmic structure—sequentially adding services by sampling users with probability proportional to their current loss—mirrors k-means++'s distance-proportional sampling, but the analysis must account for heterogeneous loss geometries captured by pairwise constants c_ij. The fairness variant extends the fair k-means framework of Ghadiri et al. (2021) to the bandit-feedback, unknown-preference setting, and the learning dynamics connection to Dean et al. (2022) positions initialization as the critical upstream step for multi-learner risk reduction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap in multi-service ML systems (federated learning, recommendation, crowd-sourcing) where data collection is inherently conditional on deploying services, making the conventional 'data-first, model-second' paradigm infeasible. The logarithmic approximation ratio generalizing k-means++ to diverse loss geometries is of independent theoretical interest and provides the first principled initialization guarantee under bandit feedback. Open questions include robustness to noisy observations, heavy-tailed feature distributions, and whether the adaptive user selection strategy remains effective when users' service choices are strategic rather than purely loss-minimizing. The empirical results on Census and MovieLens data confirm the theory but are limited to linear predictors and semi-synthetic settings.
