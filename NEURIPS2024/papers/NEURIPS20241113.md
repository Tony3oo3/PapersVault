## Problem

Conventional outlier detection follows a two-stage procedure—first detect and remove outliers, then estimate—leaving the removal step uninformed by the downstream estimation task. This separation can cause overfitting to particular outlier types or overly conservative removal that wastes statistical efficiency. The paper asks whether outlier rectification and parameter estimation can be unified into a single joint optimization that automatically identifies and corrects outliers as part of the estimation process itself.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a contaminated sample {z'_i}_{i=1}^n, a scalar rectification budget δ (tuned via cross-validation), and a user-specified loss function ℓ(θ, z). It produces three coupled outputs: the estimated parameter θ, the optimal rectified probability distribution Q* (which explicitly repositions outlier mass to new locations), and the implicit identification of which points were rectified (the 'knot point' k(θ) demarcates clean from outlier observations). The budget δ controls the total transport cost available, effectively setting the quantile at which points are flagged as outliers, and the concave exponent r ∈ (0,1) governs the aggressiveness of long-haul repositioning.

## Method

The core mechanism constructs a rectification set R(P'_n) = {Q : D_c(Q, P'_n) ≤ δ} using an optimal transport distance with a concave cost c(z,z') = ‖z−z'‖^r for r ∈ (0,1), then solves a min-min problem: minimize expected loss over both θ and Q in the rectification set. The concave cost is the critical design choice—it exhibits decreasing marginal transport cost, so the optimal plan moves only a small fraction of mass (the outliers) over large distances ('long hauls') while leaving the bulk of clean data fixed, in contrast to convex costs that spread small movements across all points. Strong duality (Proposition 1) reduces the infinite-dimensional inner minimization over probability measures to a finite-dimensional concave maximization over a dual variable λ, which for mean estimation and LAD regression admits a closed-form solution via the quick-select algorithm, revealing the estimator as an iteratively adaptive quantile (regression) estimator. The outer optimization over θ proceeds via subgradient descent on the rectified data, with the same fixed budget δ applied at each iteration so that the effective quantile adapts as θ changes.

## Results

On the kernelized implied volatility surface (IVS) estimation task using ~2,000 US stock option chains (WRDS, 2019–2021), the proposed estimator achieves a mean MAPE of 0.225 and a mean discretized surface gradient (DSG) of 6.5, outperforming kernel smoothing (MAPE 0.294, DSG 20.2) by 23% and 67% respectively. On the deep learning Dupire NN volatility surface task (DAX index options), the method reduces RMSE by 33% (0.029 vs. 0.044) and MAPE by 34% (0.074 vs. 0.111) under soft arbitrage constraints. In mean estimation simulations at 45% corruption, the estimator achieves a loss of 2.251 on clean data, beating the median (2.843) and the trimmed mean with oracle corruption level (3.047).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against kernel smoothing (KS, Ait-Sahalia & Lo 1998), the proposed estimator wins decisively on both accuracy (MAPE 0.225 vs. 0.294, a 23% reduction) and surface smoothness (DSG 6.5 vs. 20.2, a 67% reduction), confirming that joint rectification-estimation outperforms naive local averaging. Against the two-step Tukey-fence-then-KS baseline (2SKS), the improvement is more modest but consistent: 5% lower MAPE and 13% lower DSG, showing that the explicit outlier-removal step in 2SKS is inferior to the integrated optimal-transport rectification. Against the Dupire neural network (Chataigner et al. 2020) on DAX index options, the method wins across all three constraint types (hard, hybrid, soft), with the largest gains under soft constraints where RMSE drops from 0.044 to 0.029 and MAPE from 0.111 to 0.074, even though the Dupire NN already enforces no-arbitrage structure. In the LAD regression simulation (Appendix E.2, Figure 7), the estimator produces a line of best fit closest to the uncontaminated ground truth, while OLS, standard LAD, and Huber regression (threshold 1.5) are all visibly pulled toward the contaminating distribution. In mean estimation (Table 1), the method outperforms the trimmed mean even when the trimmed mean is given the true corruption level as an oracle, and only rectifies ~10% of points regardless of corruption level (20%–49%), indicating efficient outlier identification.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the standard optimal transport framework (which assumes convex cost functions like r-norms with r ≥ 1) by introducing a concave cost (r ∈ (0,1)) to construct a rectification set in probability space, enabling a min-min (optimistic) robust estimation strategy. It positions itself against two-stage outlier removal (Tukey fences, RANSAC), M-estimators (Huber loss), and minimum distance functionals (Donoho-Liu), arguing that all of these separate the rectification and estimation steps. The min-min formulation connects to Jiang and Xie [2024], but whereas that work uses artificially constructed sets to recover known robust estimators, this paper introduces a novel OT-based rectification set whose concave cost induces a 'long haul' structure that automatically identifies and relocates outliers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical contribution is a single-step optimization that jointly rectifies outliers and estimates parameters, with the budget δ tunable via cross-validation and the method degrading gracefully to the median when δ→0. The equivalence to adaptive quantile regression (Theorems 2 and 4) provides interpretable guarantees, and the 30.4% smoother volatility surfaces with 6.3% MAPE reduction demonstrate real-world value in finance. Limitations include the heuristic nature of Algorithm 2 for general deep learning models (no reformulation guarantee), the non-convexity and 'downward-facing cusps' in the objective for general losses, and the acknowledged inapplicability to classification tasks where the OT-based budget mechanism is less effective. Open questions include whether the concave-cost OT framework can be extended to high-dimensional nonparametric settings and whether the φ-divergence alternative suggested for classification can be unified with the OT approach.
