## Problem

Existing image matting methods typically predict the alpha matte first and then estimate foreground color via post-processing (e.g., local smoothness assumptions), leading to accumulated errors and poor foreground fidelity, especially for semi-transparent objects. High-quality semi-transparent matting data is scarce, making it difficult for purely data-driven approaches to generalize. The paper asks whether the rich natural-image priors embedded in large pre-trained latent diffusion models can be unleashed to jointly predict both foreground and alpha with higher quality and better generalization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242536/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 512×512 RGB image and a single-channel trimap (encoding definite foreground, unknown, and background regions) as conditioning inputs. It produces a 3-channel foreground color image and a 1-channel alpha matte in [0,1]. Both the trimap and alpha are triplicated to three channels before VAE encoding to match the latent space dimensionality, and the foreground is pre-processed with Gaussian blurring in fully transparent regions to avoid edge artifacts.

## Method

Drip fine-tunes a Stable Diffusion v2 U-Net in the VAE latent space to jointly denoise foreground and alpha latents conditioned on the input image and trimap. A switcher mechanism—implemented as a low-dimensional positional encoding vector combined with the time embedding—tells the single U-Net whether to generate the foreground or alpha domain, avoiding the need for two separate networks. A cross-domain self-attention layer replaces standard self-attention by concatenating foreground and alpha latent embeddings as keys and values, enabling mutual spatial alignment between the two modalities. To counteract the VAE's inherent reconstruction loss, a latent transparency decoder (inspired by ControlNet's zero-conv architecture) injects early encoder features into the VAE decoder, aligning the decoded RGBA output with the input image's fine details. Training uses annealed multi-resolution noise and a standard ε-prediction diffusion objective, with inference via a 10-step DPM-Solver.

## Results

On the Composition-1k benchmark, Drip achieves state-of-the-art alpha prediction with SAD 20.8, MSE 3.1, Grad 6.8, and Conn 17.8, improving SAD by 3.3% and MSE by 6.1% over the previous best (ViTMatte). On the natural-image AIM-500 benchmark, Drip dramatically outperforms all prior methods, achieving SAD 17.3 and MSE 1.5 versus AIM's 43.9 and 16.1. For foreground color prediction on Composition-1k, Drip reaches SAD 34.1 and MSE 4.3, improving SAD by 12.1% and MSE by 28.3% over FBA-Matting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On Composition-1k alpha prediction, Drip outperforms the strongest trimap-based baselines: ViTMatte (SAD 21.5, MSE 3.3), DiffMat (SAD 22.8, MSE 4.0), and MFormer (SAD 23.8, MSE 4.0), with the largest gap over FBA (SAD 25.8, MSE 5.2) and DIM (SAD 50.4, MSE 14.0). On AIM-500, Drip's advantage is far more dramatic: it reduces SAD from 43.9 (AIM) to 17.3 and MSE from 16.1 to 1.5, and outperforms the trimap-free HATT by a factor of ~28 in SAD (479.2 vs. 17.3). For foreground color prediction on Composition-1k, Drip (SAD 34.1, MSE 4.3) beats FBA (SAD 38.8, MSE 6.0), LBS (SAD 49.7, MSE 8.6), and the classical Global/KNN methods by wide margins, though Context-Aware matting [62] achieves a slightly lower MSE (3.24 vs. 4.3) while Drip leads on SAD (34.1 vs. 61.72). The diffusion-based DiffMat [43] is the closest alpha competitor on Composition-1k but does not operate in latent space and does not leverage natural-image priors, explaining its larger gap on the natural AIM-500 benchmark where it was not evaluated.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper is the first to repurpose a pretrained latent diffusion model (Stable Diffusion v2) for image matting, extending the broader trend of leveraging diffusion priors for visual perception tasks (e.g., Marigold for depth, GeoWizard for 3D geometry). It departs from the dominant two-stage paradigm (alpha prediction followed by post-processing foreground estimation) by jointly generating foreground and alpha within a single diffusion model, using a domain-aware switcher and cross-domain attention inspired by Wonder3D's cross-domain diffusion. The latent transparency decoder, motivated by LayerDiffusion, addresses a specific gap: the VAE compression error that is more detrimental in matting than in coarser tasks like segmentation or depth.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dramatic generalization gap on AIM-500 (SAD 17.3 vs. 43.9 for the prior best) suggests that diffusion priors learned from billions of natural images provide a substantial inductive bias for matting that purely supervised CNN/Transformer approaches lack, particularly for semi-transparent and high-frequency-detail objects. However, the method inherits biases from the generative prior and introduces significant computational overhead (LDM backbone + transparent decoder), limiting real-time deployment. Open questions include whether the approach extends to video matting, whether the switcher mechanism generalizes to more than two output modalities, and how performance degrades on domains poorly represented in LAION-5B (e.g., medical or industrial imagery).
