## Problem

Spatial representation learning (SRL) lacks a community-shared deep learning framework and benchmark for systematic model development and evaluation, unlike analogous efforts in computer vision (Torchvision) or graph learning (PyTorch Geometric). No existing benchmark systematically evaluates how different location encoders affect model performance across diverse task setups, dataset sizes, and geographic coverage. Additionally, no universally applicable quantitative metric exists to measure the geographic bias introduced by geo-aware AI models.

## Input / Output

The framework consumes RGB images (species photos, satellite imagery, or street-level photos) paired with geolocation coordinates (lat/lon) as input. For classification tasks, it outputs class probabilities computed via element-wise multiplication of separate image and location classifier logits; for regression tasks, it outputs continuous values (population density, forest cover, elevation, etc.) from an MLP over concatenated embeddings. Beyond task predictions, the evaluation pipeline produces unmarked and marked SSI Geo-Bias Scores at user-defined spatial scales (e.g., 100 km or 1000 km radius neighborhoods) to quantify geographic bias.

## Method

TorchSpatial implements a unified location encoding architecture Enc(x) = NN(PE(x)), where PE maps coordinates to a W-dimensional position embedding and NN is a learnable MLP producing a d-dimensional location embedding. Fifteen location encoders are consolidated into two groups: 7 operating in projected 2D space (tile, wrap, wrap+ffn, RBF, RFF, Space2Vec-grid, Space2Vec-theory) and 8 operating in 3D spherical space (xyz, NeRF, five Sphere2Vec variants, Siren-SH). For classification, image and location are encoded by separate supervised classifiers whose outputs are multiplied element-wise (a Bayesian spatio-temporal prior); for regression, their embeddings are concatenated and passed through an MLP. The Geo-Bias Score framework uses spatial self-information (SSI), an information-theoretic generalization of Moran's I that ensures numerical comparability across spatial patterns, computed over local neighborhoods of low-performance observations against a background grid to separately measure intrinsic sampling bias (unmarked) and model-induced performance clustering (marked).

## Results

On the 7 geo-aware image classification datasets, adding a location encoder boosts Top-1 accuracy substantially over the image-only No Prior baseline: the best 3D encoder (Sphere2Vec-sphereC+) reaches 75.38% average across five species recognition datasets vs. 67.94% for No Prior, and 70.93% on fMoW vs. 69.83%. On the 10 geo-aware image regression datasets, location encoders improve R² markedly (e.g., population density: 0.38 → 0.64; forest cover: 0.52 → 0.75; elevation: 0.27 → 0.82). GPT-4V in zero-shot mode performs far worse than fine-tuned geo-aware models (e.g., 18.00% on iNat2018 vs. 73.45% for Sphere2Vec-sphereC+). The Geo-Bias Score analysis reveals that most location encoders increase geographic bias relative to No Prior, except tile which has minimal impact on both accuracy and bias.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Sphere2Vec-sphereC+ [53] is the strongest 3D location encoder, winning on 4 of 5 species recognition datasets (BirdSnap 72.15%, BirdSnap† 80.90%, NABirds† 82.13%, iNat2018 73.45%) and achieving the best average (75.38%) over No Prior (67.94%), a 7.4-point gain. Among 2D encoders, wrap [42] leads with 74.89% average species accuracy, while Space2Vec-theory [48] edges it out slightly at 75.07%. The tile [71] discretization baseline shows negligible improvement over No Prior (e.g., 70.20% vs. 70.07% on BirdSnap) and is the only encoder that does not significantly increase geographic bias. GPT-4V in zero-shot mode is dramatically outperformed by all fine-tuned geo-aware models (e.g., 18.00% vs. 73.45% on iNat2018), likely due to the thousands of fine-grained species classes and domain gap in remote sensing images. On regression tasks, Sphere2Vec-sphereC+ achieves the best R² on population density (0.64 vs. 0.38 No Prior) and elevation (0.82 vs. 0.27), while Space2Vec-theory [48] leads on SustainBench tasks (e.g., 0.70 on Asset Index vs. 0.40 No Prior). Siren(SH) [67] and NeRF [59] are competitive but consistently trail the top Sphere2Vec variants by 0.5–1.0 points on classification.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper consolidates the location encoding framework formalized by Mai et al. [45] (Enc(x) = NN(PE(x))) into a unified, reproducible implementation covering 15 previously scattered encoders (tile, wrap, RBF, RFF, Space2Vec, Sphere2Vec, NeRF, Siren, etc.), and adopts the Bayesian spatio-temporal prior inference structure from Mac Aodha et al. [42] for classification. For geographic bias quantification, it departs from classic spatial autocorrelation statistics (Moran's I, Geary's C) by grounding its Geo-Bias Score in the information-theoretic spatial self-information measure of Wang et al. [76], which ensures numerical comparability across different spatial patterns. The paper carves new ground by being the first to systematically benchmark location encoders across diverse task setups, geographic coverages, and dataset sizes, and by proposing the first universally applicable geographic bias evaluation framework for AI models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TorchSpatial and LocBench fill a critical infrastructure gap in GeoAI by providing a standardized, community-shared framework and 17-dataset benchmark that eliminates the need for each research project to rebuild spatial data pipelines and baselines from scratch. The finding that location encoders consistently boost overall accuracy while simultaneously amplifying geographic bias (except for the naive tile encoder) challenges the implicit assumption that geo-aware models are inherently fairer, and the Geo-Bias Score offers a practical tool to monitor this trade-off. Key limitations include support only for point/location encoders (not polylines, polygons, or networks), absence of error bars due to computational cost, and the fact that the bias metric's hyperparameters (neighborhood radius, grid density) require domain-specific tuning. Open questions include whether the bias-amplification effect persists with larger, more geographically balanced training sets, and whether the Geo-Bias Score transfers cleanly to non-image modalities such as LLM geographic knowledge.
