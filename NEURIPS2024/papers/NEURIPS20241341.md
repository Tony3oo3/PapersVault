## Problem

Existing deep clustering methods, despite leveraging data augmentation and pseudo-labeling, suffer from a performance ceiling because they cannot reliably discriminate hard boundary samples whose cluster assignments are inherently uncertain. Pseudo-labeling strategies focus on high-confidence easy samples, while approaches that simply ignore hard samples sidestep rather than solve the core problem, leaving boundary regions permanently inseparable.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241341/cropped_page1_idx4.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained clustering model (with its learned feature representations and cluster centers) as input, along with user feedback collected by presenting 500 selected samples each with T=5 nearest cluster center candidates for the user to affirm or reject. The output is a set of refined cluster assignments obtained directly from the fine-tuned model's cluster head, requiring no additional inference step. The interaction interface is deliberately simplified: users judge semantic alignment relative to cluster prototypes rather than assigning absolute class labels or comparing sample pairs.

## Method

IDC is a two-stage plug-and-play framework. In the user interaction stage, it selects high-value samples by jointly scoring hardness (log of the cosine-similarity gap between the two nearest cluster centers), representativeness (negative log of summed squared distances to K=20 nearest neighbors, favoring dense regions over outliers), and diversity (minimum log-distance to already-selected samples, preventing collapse into few clusters); Theorem 1 proves the selected value monotonically decreases across iterations. In the model optimization stage, it fine-tunes the pre-trained model with three losses: a positive loss pulling the sample toward the user-confirmed cluster centroid, a negative loss pushing it away from all rejected candidates, and a regularization loss that preserves the original model's confident predictions (threshold τ=0.99) to prevent overfitting the small set of inquired samples. The method is model-agnostic and can be attached to any pre-trained deep clustering model, whether or not it natively has a cluster head.

## Results

IDC boosts the clustering accuracy of TCL by 16.3% on CIFAR-20 (53.1→69.4) and 14.4% on ImageNet-Dogs (64.4→78.8), and improves ProPos by 19.2% on CIFAR-20 (59.1→78.3) and 9.2% on ImageNet-Dogs (76.9→86.1), all with only 500 user queries. On ImageNet-10, IDC_TCL reaches 97.2% ACC and IDC_ProPos reaches 97.3% ACC, surpassing the best prior deep clustering method CoNR (95.8%). The method also outperforms the semi-supervised baseline FixMatch (67.2% ACC) and constrained clustering baseline Cop-Kmeans (52.4% ACC) on the challenging CIFAR-20 dataset.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IDC consistently outperforms all 14 deep clustering baselines on the most challenging datasets. On CIFAR-20, IDC_ProPos (78.3% ACC) surpasses the best deep clustering method CoNR (59.0%) by 19.3 points and the semi-supervised FixMatch (67.2%) by 11.1 points. On ImageNet-Dogs, IDC_ProPos (86.1% ACC) beats CoNR (80.2%) by 5.9 points and IDC_TCL (78.8%) beats CoNR (80.2%) by a narrower margin, while both IDC variants substantially outperform Cop-Kmeans (63.5%). On ImageNet-10, both IDC variants reach ~97.2–97.3% ACC, exceeding CoNR (95.8%) and all other baselines. On the easier CIFAR-10, IDC_TCL (92.7% ACC) is slightly below CoNR (93.3%) and SeCu (93.0%), while IDC_ProPos (95.7%) leads all methods. The paper also shows that merely correcting 500 sample labels without model finetuning (TCL†, ProPos†) yields only marginal gains (~0.4–1.2% ACC), confirming that the value-mining selection and loss-based optimization are essential.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by repurposing active learning's query-and-feedback paradigm for the unsupervised deep clustering setting, where no class priors exist to guide sample selection. It departs from constrained clustering (Cop-Kmeans, must-link/cannot-link) by operating on a pre-trained deep model and using a learned value-mining strategy (hardness + representativeness + diversity) rather than raw pairwise constraints. It also differs from semi-supervised baselines like FixMatch by being model-agnostic and tailoring query selection to the specific pre-trained clustering model's failure modes, rather than applying a generic confidence-based selection. The theoretical contribution (Theorem 1 on monotonically decreasing sample value) provides a formal guarantee for the greedy selection algorithm that prior interactive clustering work lacked.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a concrete bottleneck in deep clustering—poor discrimination of hard boundary samples—and shows that a small number of targeted human queries (500) can break through it, yielding 14–19% ACC gains on challenging datasets. The plug-and-play design means any existing deep clustering pipeline can benefit without architectural changes, lowering the adoption barrier. Key open questions include robustness to noisy or inconsistent user feedback (acknowledged but not addressed), whether the interaction protocol generalizes to non-visual modalities, and how to align clustering with personalized user partition criteria rather than ground-truth class semantics. The requirement for human interaction also limits applicability to fully automated deployment scenarios.
