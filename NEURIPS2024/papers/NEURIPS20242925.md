## Problem

Existing online federated learning algorithms achieve the same sublinear regret bounds as purely local online gradient descent, leaving it theoretically unclear whether federated collaboration provides any benefit for real-time prediction. Moreover, non-convex models such as neural networks suffer from catastrophic forgetting when fine-tuned on streaming data, making it infeasible to derive a single model parameter that tracks the best model in hindsight. The paper addresses both gaps by proposing a personalized ensemble that adaptively combines local, federated, and historically stored models.

## Input / Output

Each client receives one data sample per time step in a streaming fashion (non-i.i.d., potentially nonstationary across clients), and the server periodically archives federated model snapshots every n steps up to a horizon U. At each step, a client downloads at most M archived model parameters from the server, constructs a personalized ensemble, and outputs a single prediction for the current sample. The method also produces updated local and federated model parameters that are sent back to the server for the next aggregation round, with batch size b controlling how many recent samples are used for gradient updates to mitigate forgetting.

## Method

Fed-POE constructs a two-level personalized ensemble at each client. The inner level combines the locally fine-tuned model and the current federated model using multiplicative weight updates (α, β) driven by observed prediction losses. The outer level combines this inner ensemble with a weighted average of up to M server-stored model snapshots, where the client selects which snapshots to include via importance sampling proportional to performance weights (w_ij,t) updated with an importance-sampling loss. The server stores a federated model snapshot every n steps, and clients prune low-performing snapshots over time, keeping memory and computation bounded at O(C_F + M·C_I) per step. For non-convex models, a batch of b recent samples is used for gradient updates to reduce overfitting to the most recent observation. Theoretical analysis proves O(√T) sublinear regret with respect to the best model in hindsight among all local, federated, and stored models, for both convex and non-convex settings.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, Fed-POE achieves 66.54% average accuracy, outperforming the best baseline Fed-OMD (65.09%) and the local-only method (50.35%). On Fashion-MNIST, Fed-POE reaches 79.23% accuracy, edging out Fed-Rep (79.04%) and Local (78.81%). For online regression, Fed-POE attains the lowest MSE on both Air (9.06×10⁻³) and WEC (11.83×10⁻³) datasets, beating the next-best methods by 1.6×10⁻³ and 5.11×10⁻³ respectively. Fed-POE is the only method that consistently outperforms both local training and all federated baselines across all four datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Fed-POE outperforms all seven baselines on every dataset tested. On CIFAR-10, it beats Fed-OMD (65.09%) by 1.45 points, Fed-Rep (63.86%) by 2.68 points, and the local-only method (50.35%) by 16.19 points, demonstrating that the ensemble of local, federated, and stored models captures advantages neither approach achieves alone. On Fashion-MNIST, the margin over Fed-Rep (79.04%) is smaller (0.19 points), but Fed-POE still leads while maintaining lower variance (±1.88% vs ±1.77%). For regression on Air, Fed-POE (9.06×10⁻³ MSE) marginally beats Local (9.12×10⁻³) and substantially outperforms POF-MKL (10.66×10⁻³) and eM-KOFL (11.51×10⁻³). On WEC, Fed-POE (11.83×10⁻³) achieves a 30% reduction in MSE over POF-MKL (16.94×10⁻³) and a 33% reduction over Local (17.64×10⁻³), highlighting its advantage in non-i.i.d. streaming settings. Notably, the relative ranking of Local vs. federated methods flips between datasets (Local wins on Air but is worst on CIFAR-10), underscoring why an adaptive ensemble is necessary.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the online federated learning framework (Mitra et al., 2021; Hong & Chae, 2022) by introducing a personalized ensemble mechanism that adaptively combines local, federated, and historically stored models. It departs from prior personalized federated learning methods (Ditto, Fed-Rep, Fed-ALA) that assume batch training, and from online federated learning baselines (Fed-OMD, eM-KOFL, POF-MKL) that use a single global model, by showing theoretically that federated averaging provides no tighter regret bound than local online gradient descent. The model selection via importance sampling (Algorithm 1) and the server-side periodic model storage are novel components that address catastrophic forgetting in non-convex models, building on the random feature kernel approximation framework (Rahimi & Recht, 2007) for the convex regression experiments.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key theoretical finding—that federated learning with online gradient descent achieves the same O(√T) regret bound as purely local learning—challenges the implicit assumption that collaboration always benefits individual clients in non-stationary, non-i.i.d. settings. Fed-POE provides a practical resolution: by ensembling local and federated models with adaptive weights, clients can exploit whichever source is better at any given time without prior knowledge of data distributions. Limitations include the additional computational cost of maintaining multiple models (O(C_F + M·C_I) per step), the relatively small experimental scale (20–400 clients), and the fact that non-convex theoretical guarantees rely on bounded loss rather than structural properties of neural networks. Open questions include scalability to thousands of clients, applicability to foundation-model fine-tuning at scale, and whether the model storage strategy remains effective under highly non-stationary concept drift.
