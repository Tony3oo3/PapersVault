## Problem

Safety fine-tuning methods (SSFT, DPO, Unlearning) are widely deployed to align LLMs, yet the mechanistic question of *how* they encode safety and *why* jailbreaks and adversarial attacks bypass them remains largely unexplored. Prior work showed that fine-tuning makes minimal parameter changes, but did not connect this observation to the specific failure modes of safety alignment. This paper fills that gap by asking: what is the safety mechanism learned in weight space, and how do adversarial inputs evade it?

## Input / Output

In the synthetic setup, each input is a sequence of two task tokens (bijective mappings over a PCFG vocabulary) followed by 15–25 text tokens generated by traversing a depth-6 probabilistic context-free grammar; the model must output either the composed bijective mapping (safe) or a single null token (refusal). For real-world validation, the authors construct 500 operator-operand natural language instructions (e.g., 'design a cycle' vs. 'design a bomb') and evaluate on Llama-2 7B/7B-chat and Llama-3 8B/8B-chat. Jailbreak variants modify either the task tokens (competing objectives), the text-token sampling (mismatched generalization via OOD tokens), or append optimized soft-prompt embeddings (adversarial attack).

## Method

The paper introduces a PCFG-based synthetic data generation framework that decomposes instructions into operators (task tokens) and operands (text tokens), with safe/unsafe labels determined by contextual co-occurrence of specific task-token and non-terminal-node combinations. The core mechanistic finding is that safety fine-tuning learns a small weight update ΔW on the first MLP layer of deep transformer blocks whose column space is nearly orthogonal to the original weights' column space—i.e., ΔW projects unsafe-sample activations into the left null space of W_IT, creating a sharp activation-space cluster separation between safe and unsafe inputs. This transformation is specialized: the row space of ΔW preferentially activates on unsafe pre-activations (near-zero effect on safe ones), and safety fine-tuning simultaneously reduces the local Lipschitz constant for unsafe samples. Jailbreaks and adversarial attacks succeed because their intermediate activations align with the safe cluster rather than the unsafe one, so ΔW is not triggered and the model processes them as safe inputs.

## Results

All three safety fine-tuning methods (SSFT, DPO, Unlearning) achieve near-perfect in-distribution safety: ≥98.6% Safe(Instruct) and ≥99.6% Unsafe(NULL) accuracy. However, robustness to jailbreaks diverges sharply: against JB-CO-Task, DPO (η_M) limits attack success to 31.5% and Unlearning (η_M) to 27.1%, while SSFT (η_M) is broken at 88.1%. Against the stronger JB-CO-Text and JB-MisGen attacks, all methods remain highly vulnerable (93–100% attack success), with DPO (η_M) at 93.5% and 93.6% respectively. Mechanistically, the paper shows that safety fine-tuning minimally transforms MLP weights (ΔW) to project unsafe-sample activations into the null space of the instruction-tuned weights, and that successful jailbreaks produce activations nearly indistinguishable from safe samples, thereby bypassing this transformation entirely.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three safety fine-tuning protocols—SSFT (Ouyang et al., 2022), DPO (Rafailov et al., 2023), and Unlearning (Liu et al., 2024)—on both a synthetic PCFG-based setup and Llama-2/3 models. DPO and Unlearning substantially outperform SSFT in robustness to competing-objectives jailbreaks (JB-CO-Task): at medium learning rate, DPO limits attack success to 31.5% and Unlearning to 27.1%, versus 88.1% for SSFT. However, all three methods are nearly equally vulnerable to the stronger JB-CO-Text (93.5–100%) and JB-MisGen (92.3–100%) attacks, indicating a shared mechanistic weakness. The mechanistic analysis reveals that all three methods learn a similar transformation ΔW whose column space aligns with the null space of the instruction-tuned MLP weights, but DPO and Unlearning produce a stronger effect (larger projection onto the null space, greater reduction in local Lipschitzness for unsafe samples). The paper further shows that extrapolating along ΔW (α > 1) can substantially improve even SSFT's robustness, suggesting the transformation direction is the key factor rather than the specific optimization protocol.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of mechanistic fine-tuning analyses (Jain et al. 2023b, Lee et al. 2024, Prakash et al. 2024, Wei et al. 2024) that showed safety fine-tuning makes minimal parameter changes, by explicitly connecting those changes to the failure mode of jailbreaks and adversarial attacks. It formalizes the jailbreak taxonomy of Wei et al. (2023) and the adversarial attack setup of Carlini et al. (2023) within a PCFG-based synthetic framework (following Allen-Zhu & Li 2023 and Ramesh et al. 2023), enabling controlled mechanistic analysis that prior works on real LLMs could not achieve. The key departure from prior mechanistic studies is the explicit null-space geometric characterization of the learned transformation ΔW and its differential activation on safe versus unsafe inputs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that all three safety fine-tuning protocols (SSFT, DPO, Unlearning) converge to the same mechanism—projecting unsafe activations into the null space of the instruction-tuned weights—provides a unified geometric explanation for both why safety works and why it is fragile. The intervention experiments showing that extrapolating along ΔW (α>1) can substantially improve SSFT robustness offer a concrete, low-cost direction for strengthening safety alignment. Limitations include reliance on a small synthetic model (~3M params) for the core mechanistic claims, with Llama corroboration being more limited (no access to the instruction-tuned checkpoint for Llama-2), and the open question of whether the null-space projection mechanism persists unchanged at the scale of frontier models with more complex safety training data.
