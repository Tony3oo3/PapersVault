## Problem

Existing datasets for benchmarking pre-trained vision models like CLIP face a fundamental trade-off: synthetic 3D renders offer precise control over factors of variation but lack photo-realism, while real-world datasets provide realism but are expensive, unscalable, and lack fine-grained factor control. This realism gap means that evaluations on 3D renders may not reflect how models actually behave on real images, and the paper demonstrates a significant performance drop when shifting from 3D to photo-realistic conditions.

## Input / Output

The pipeline takes a parametric 3D human model (with 17 programmatically controllable factors such as age, gender, race, hair color, shirt color, camera angle, etc.) and renders it via Blender's path-traced Cycles engine to produce a 3D render, depth map, normal map, and segmentation mask. These spatial attributes, combined with a text prompt derived from the factor values, condition a fine-tuned Stable Diffusion 1.5 model to generate a photo-realistic output image. The released datasets (BLURD 3D, BLURD SD, BLURD Mask) total 1.7M images and include three conditioning ablation levels (Unleashed, Tempered, Harbinger) that progressively tighten spatial constraints while preserving realism.

## Method

BLURD unifies a deterministic 3D rendering stage with a data-driven diffusion generation stage. In the rendering stage, the Blender Python API programmatically sets 17 factors of variation on high-poly human assets (particle hair systems, PBR textures, morph targets) and renders via the unbiased path-traced Cycles engine, exporting depth maps, normal maps, segmentation masks, and the 3D render itself. In the diffusion stage, a Realistic Vision v5.1 fine-tuned SD 1.5 model is conditioned on these spatial attributes through purpose-trained ControlNets (for depth, normal maps, and a spatial color palette) and image-to-image diffusion (for the 3D render), ensuring the generated photo-realistic image preserves the exact factor configuration of the source 3D scene. Three ablation levels—Unleashed (text-only conditioning), Tempered (text + depth + normal ControlNets), and Harbinger (full conditioning including 3D render and spatial palette)—allow probing how much spatial constraint is needed to prevent factor entanglement while maintaining photo-realism. The key insight is that the deterministic 3D stage guarantees factor identity and controllability, while the diffusion stage bridges the realism gap, and the ControlNet conditioning preserves factor structure across the domain shift without sacrificing the photorealistic quality of the output.

## Results

On single-factor zero-shot accuracy, BLURD SD: Harbinger consistently outperforms BLURD 3D across all CLIP backbones (e.g., ViT-g-14/Laion2b: 0.65 vs. 0.60; ViT-B-32/Laion2b: 0.67 vs. 0.57), confirming a significant realism gap in 3D-rendered benchmarks. Mean text equivariance is highest for ViT-H-14/Laion2b (0.40 on BLURD 3D, 0.35 on Harbinger), while the best single-factor accuracy reaches 0.71 (ViT-g-14/Laion2b on Unleashed). In the human photo-realism evaluation, BLURD SD: Harbinger scores 3.98/5 versus 2.42/5 for BLURD 3D, approaching CelebA-HQ's 4.70/5. For zero-shot domain adaptation to CelebAMask-HQ segmentation, U-Net trained on BLURD SD achieves higher IoU than BLURD 3D under all three augmentation regimes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a benchmarking paper rather than a method-vs-method comparison, so no external baselines are directly pitted against BLURD. Instead, the paper compares multiple CLIP backbones (ViT-B-32, ViT-L-14, ViT-H-14, ViT-g-14) trained on different datasets (OpenAI, Laion400m, Laion2b, Datacomp XL) against each other on the BLURD benchmarks. Laion2b-trained models consistently outperform OpenAI and Laion400m in both equivariance and zero-shot accuracy (e.g., ViT-B-32: 0.31 equivariance and 0.67 accuracy on Harbinger for Laion2b vs. 0.26/0.61 for OpenAI). Larger backbones (ViT-H-14, ViT-g-14) achieve higher equivariance (0.35–0.40 on 3D) but do not uniformly dominate on accuracy, revealing a trade-off between representation structure and linear separability. In the human photo-realism study, BLURD SD: Harbinger (3.98/5) is significantly more realistic than BLURD 3D (2.42/5) but still trails real CelebA-HQ photographs (4.70/5). For domain adaptation, BLURD SD-trained U-Nets beat BLURD 3D-trained U-Nets on CelebAMask-HQ IoU across all augmentation settings, demonstrating that photo-realism from diffusion conditioning is necessary for effective synthetic-to-real transfer.

## Relation to Prior Work

BLURD sits at the intersection of two well-developed lines of work: controllable synthetic dataset generation (3dshapes, CLEVR, MPI3D, PUG) and diffusion-based image synthesis (Latent Diffusion Models, ControlNet, SDXL). Prior synthetic datasets achieved controllability but sacrificed photorealism, while prior diffusion-based face generators (GANDiffFace, SynFace) achieved realism but lacked fine-grained factor-level control. BLURD's key conceptual departure is the tight coupling of a path-traced 3D render (Blender/Cycles) as a *spatial conditioning signal* for Stable Diffusion via ControlNets, rather than using 3D rendering as the final output or diffusion as an independent generator. This unification is what enables simultaneous photorealism and deterministic factor manipulation, a combination no prior dataset achieves.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical, open-source pipeline for creating photorealistic yet fully controllable datasets at scale, addressing a long-standing gap between synthetic controllability and real-world fidelity in representation learning benchmarks. The equivariance analysis reveals that zero-shot accuracy and representation-space structure can diverge (e.g., shirt color has high equivariance but low accuracy), suggesting that class-name-only evaluation is insufficient for understanding what CLIP models actually encode. However, the equivariance findings rest on a small number of model-dataset combinations, and the domain adaptation results, while positive, are limited to a single segmentation task. Open questions include whether the 3D-to-photorealism representation gap persists with next-generation models (e.g., DINOv2, SigLIP) and whether the method generalizes beyond human-centric scenes to arbitrary object categories.
