## Problem

Most theoretical and algorithmic results for Gaussian Mixture Model clustering assume isotropic (identity) covariance matrices, leaving the fundamental limits and optimal algorithms for anisotropic settings—where covariance matrices are unknown and potentially distinct across clusters—largely unexplored. This paper addresses two gaps: deriving minimax lower bounds that capture how anisotropic covariance structures interact with cluster centers to determine clustering difficulty, and providing a computationally feasible algorithm that achieves these bounds.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243609/cropped_page3_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The algorithm takes n observations in R^d, the known number of clusters k, an initialization z^(0) (obtainable from spectral clustering or vanilla Lloyd's), and a number of iterations T, and outputs a cluster assignment vector z^(T). The procedure is fully adaptive to unknown centers and covariance matrices—no distributional parameters need to be specified. Convergence to the minimax-optimal misclustering error rate is guaranteed within log n iterations, and the only structural assumption is that the covariance matrices are well-conditioned (bounded condition number).

## Method

The paper proposes an adjusted Lloyd's algorithm (equivalently, a hard EM) that iteratively updates cluster centers, estimates covariance matrices (one shared matrix for the homogeneous Model 1, k distinct matrices for the heterogeneous Model 2), and reassigns points using Mahalanobis distances—adding a log-determinant penalty in Model 2 to account for differing covariances. This contrasts with vanilla Lloyd's, which uses only Euclidean distances and is suboptimal when covariances are anisotropic. The theoretical analysis employs a one-step contraction argument: under well-conditioned covariance assumptions, each iteration halves the weighted misclustering loss ℓ(z,z*) up to an additive ideal error term, yielding convergence to the minimax rate exp(−(1+o(1))·SNR²/8) within log n iterations. The minimax lower bounds are derived by reducing the clustering problem to pairwise hypothesis testing via LDA (Model 1) or QDA (Model 2), revealing that the effective signal-to-noise ratio depends on the geometry of the covariance matrices relative to the center separations.

## Results

On Fashion-MNIST (2-class: T-shirt/top vs. Trouser, 12,000 images reduced to 50 dimensions via PCA), Algorithm 2 achieves a misclustering error of 5.71%, compared to 8.24% for vanilla Lloyd's algorithm. In the 3-class setting (adding Ankle boot, 18,000 images), Algorithm 2 achieves 3.97% error versus 5.64% for vanilla Lloyd. On synthetic anisotropic GMM data (Model 1: n=1200, d=50, k=30; Model 2: n=1200, d=9, k=2), both Algorithm 1 and Algorithm 2 match the minimax-optimal exponent (−SNR²/8 and −SNR′²/8 respectively) after just 3 iterations, while spectral clustering and vanilla Lloyd's algorithm remain substantially above the optimal bound.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares its adjusted Lloyd's algorithms (Algorithm 1 for homogeneous covariance, Algorithm 2 for heterogeneous covariance) against two baselines: spectral clustering [14] and vanilla Lloyd's algorithm [15]. On both synthetic anisotropic GMM settings, both proposed algorithms significantly outperform spectral clustering and vanilla Lloyd's algorithm in misclustering error rate, with the gap widening as the anisotropy increases. On Fashion-MNIST, Algorithm 2 reduces the misclustering error from 8.24% to 5.71% (2-class) and from 5.64% to 3.97% (3-class) relative to vanilla Lloyd's algorithm, a relative improvement of approximately 31% and 30% respectively. Both proposed algorithms converge to the minimax-optimal exponent within 3 iterations, whereas the baselines do not reach the optimal rate, confirming that ignoring covariance structure (as vanilla Lloyd does) or relying solely on spectral decomposition (as spectral clustering does) is suboptimal under anisotropic settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the minimax theory of GMM clustering from the isotropic setting (where all covariance matrices are identity) to anisotropic settings with unknown, potentially heterogeneous covariance matrices. The lower bounds draw a direct connection to LDA (homogeneous case) and QDA (heterogeneous case), while the upper-bound proof adopts the one-step contraction analysis framework from Gao & Zhang [8] and the minimax lower-bound construction from Lu & Zhou [15]. The proposed adjusted Lloyd's algorithm (a hard EM variant) departs from vanilla Lloyd's by iteratively estimating and inverting covariance matrices, and from soft EM by replacing the E-step with a hard argmin assignment. Prior anisotropic results [3, 11, 22] either required strong separation, limited to two clusters, or did not establish fundamental limits.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work closes a notable gap by establishing that the fundamental limit of anisotropic GMM clustering depends on the covariance geometry (via the SNR) rather than merely center distances, and provides a simple, rate-optimal algorithm achieving that limit in log n iterations. However, the results are constrained to well-conditioned covariances and low-to-moderate dimensions (d = O(√n) for Model 1, d = O(1) for Model 2), leaving high-dimensional and ill-conditioned regimes open. For practitioners, the algorithm is straightforward to implement and shows clear empirical gains over spectral clustering and vanilla Lloyd's on Fashion-MNIST, but the restrictive assumptions limit immediate applicability to very high-dimensional or degenerate covariance settings.
