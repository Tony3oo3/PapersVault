## Problem

Existing OOD generalization methods for time series address either sample-level or group-level distributional uncertainty, but not both simultaneously, and the application of pre-trained LLMs to time series OOD generalization remains largely unexplored. This paper tackles the gap by formulating a unified tri-level optimization that jointly handles both uncertainty types and leverages LLM representation learning for robust time series classification under distribution shift.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243510/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes multivariate time series of shape T×F (T timesteps, F sensor channels) drawn from K source domains and produces a discrete class label. For the LLM variant, raw time series are first projected to GPT-2's embedding dimension via a learned input projection layer, then positional encoding is applied to preserve temporal order before feeding into the frozen transformer. Evaluation follows a leave-one-domain-out protocol across six wearable-sensor datasets (HHAR, PAMAP, WESAD, SWELL, USC-HAD, DSADS), where one domain is held out as the unseen target.

## Method

TTSO formulates time series OOD generalization as a tri-level optimization: the outer level minimizes a contrastive alignment loss over model parameters θ, the middle level maximizes over domain-mixture weights q to capture worst-case group-level distribution shift, and the inner level maximizes over GMM-parameterized perturbations δ to capture worst-case sample-level augmentation. A stratified localization algorithm with cutting planes solves this nested problem without computing hypergradients, achieving O(1/ε²) iteration complexity for an ε-stationary point. For LLM integration, a dual-stage fine-tuning procedure first applies TTSO to GPT-2 with a time-series contrastive loss, then transfers the learned weights to a constrained downstream classification stage where only Layer Normalization affine parameters are updated within a trust region around the pre-trained weights, preserving the LLM's learned representations while adapting to the target task.

## Results

On the leave-one-domain-out protocol across six time series datasets, TTSO* (with GPT-2 fine-tuning) achieves the best overall average accuracy of 72.8%, outperforming the second-best baseline (Mixup, 69.9%) by 2.9 percentage points. Per-dataset, TTSO* leads by 2.0% on HHAR (73.9% vs. 71.9%), 3.5% on PAMAP (76.1% vs. 72.6%), and 3.2% on WESAD (68.4% vs. 65.2%). Even without LLM fine-tuning, TTSO alone reaches 71.4% overall, still surpassing all 13 baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TTSO* (72.8% ALL AVG) outperforms all 13 baselines across every dataset. The largest margins are against time-series-specific methods: DIVERSIFY (69.3%), DFDG (67.3%), and CCDG (68.4%), with gaps of 3.5, 5.5, and 4.4 points respectively. Among general OOD methods, Mixup (69.9%) and RSC (68.8%) are the strongest baselines, yet TTSO* still leads by 2.9 and 4.0 points. On PAMAP specifically, TTSO* achieves 76.1% versus RSC's 72.6% and Mixup's 72.5%, a 3.5–4.0 point advantage. On WESAD, the gap widens further: TTSO* reaches 68.4% while the best baseline (Mixup) manages only 65.2%. Notably, TTSO without LLM fine-tuning (71.4%) also surpasses every baseline, confirming that the tri-level optimization framework itself—rather than the LLM component alone—drives the improvement.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the OOD generalization literature by unifying two previously separate lines of work—group-level distributionally robust optimization (GroupDRO, IRM) and sample-level augmentation-robust contrastive learning (ARCL, TS2Vec)—into a single tri-level min-max-max formulation. The algorithmic core draws on cutting-plane localization methods from convex optimization (Boyd & Vandenberghe; Jiao et al., 2023), adapted here to avoid the expensive hypergradient computation inherent in nested bilevel/trilevel problems. The practical instantiation connects to the emerging LLM-for-time-series thread (Time-LLM, OFA, LLM4TS) by using the tri-level framework as a structured fine-tuning objective for GPT-2, with Layer Normalization Tuning to preserve pre-trained knowledge.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The tri-level formulation offers a principled way to simultaneously address within-domain sample variability and between-domain distribution shift, which prior methods treat in isolation; the VC-dimension-based generalization bound (Theorem 2) makes explicit which terms the optimization targets. However, the empirical gains (2.8–4.9% over second-best) are measured on six small-scale activity-recognition datasets with a single LLM (GPT-2), leaving open whether the tri-level structure and cutting-plane solver scale to larger, higher-dimensional time series or to forecasting tasks. A key open question is whether the added complexity of the third (sample-level) level is truly necessary over a well-tuned bi-level GroupDRO+contrastive baseline, since the ablation does not isolate the marginal contribution of the δ-optimization level from the group-level reweighting.
