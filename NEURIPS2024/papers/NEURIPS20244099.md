## Problem

Standard diffusion models for MRI reconstruction treat each image independently, ignoring the strong spatial and anatomical dependencies between adjacent slices in a volumetric sequence. This limits their ability to produce sequentially coherent reconstructions and makes them prone to hallucinating structures inconsistent with neighboring slices. The paper asks how to model the joint distribution of an image sequence so that inter-image dependencies are explicitly captured and leveraged as a generative prior for accelerated MRI.

## Input / Output

For sequence generation, the model takes a conditioning image sequence of length N (e.g., 10 slices for brain at 320×320, 42 frames for cardiac at 256×256) plus Gaussian noise and produces the next image in the sequence; complex-valued MRI images are fed as two channels (real and imaginary). For accelerated MRI reconstruction, the input is undersampled multi-coil k-space data (12× undersampling with random or equispaced masks, with or without ACS lines) combined with the conditioning sequence, and the output is a reconstructed image sequence along with pseudo-confidence intervals derived from the variance of multiple posterior samples. Prospective generation uses a sliding window that appends newly generated images and discards the oldest, enabling unbounded sequence extension from a warm or cold start.

## Method

The AID model factorizes the joint distribution of an image sequence autoregressively as p(x) = q(x₁|x₀) ∏ q(xₙ|x_{<n}), where each conditional q(xₙ|x_{<n}) is modeled by a standard DDPM diffusion process that adds noise to xₙ independently of its position in the sequence. The architecture is a Unet backbone augmented with a Temporal-Spatial Conditioning (TSC) block: a modified DiTBlock with a causal (upper-triangular) attention mask over the conditioning sequence ensures each target image attends only to preceding images, followed by a ResNet block whose features are injected into the corresponding Unet encoder layer. During training, the noise-prediction loss ε_θ(xₙᵗ, x_{<n}⁰, t) is computed in parallel for all N target images given the conditioning sequence, enabling efficient optimization. For MRI reconstruction, posterior sampling (Algorithm 1) alternates DDIM reverse steps with gradient-based data-fidelity steps on the complex k-space likelihood, sequentially generating each slice conditioned on all previously reconstructed slices, which reduces hallucinations relative to a standard diffusion prior.

## Results

On 12× undersampled fastMRI validation data (20 volumes), AID outperforms both the standard diffusion model (Guide) and the CSGM baseline (NCSNv2) in PSNR and NRMSE across all four sampling mask types (random/equispaced, with/without ACS), with the largest gains observed in the absence of ACS lines. In the single-coil unfolding experiment (2× undersampling), AID significantly reduces folding-artifact errors compared to Guide, and the confidence intervals computed from posterior sample variance indicate more trustworthy reconstructions in the folding region. Visually, AID produces fewer hallucinations than Guide while maintaining aliased-free reconstructions. The model also generates sequentially coherent image sequences from both warm-start (given conditioning) and cold-start (zero initialization) conditions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares AID against two baselines: (1) CSGM from Jalal et al. [11], which uses a score-based model (NCSNv2) trained on fastMRI as the image prior, and (2) Guide, a standard (non-autoregressive) diffusion model using the same Unet architecture and training hyperparameters as AID but without the temporal-spatial conditioning block. AID outperforms both baselines in PSNR and NRMSE across all four 12× undersampling conditions (random/equispaced × with/without ACS), with the most pronounced advantage in the no-ACS settings where inter-image dependencies provide the strongest prior. Compared to Guide specifically, AID also produces fewer hallucinations in reconstructed images (visible as spurious structures pointed by red arrows in Figure 6) and more reliably resolves folding artifacts in the single-coil unfolding task. The key differentiator is that AID's autoregressive conditioning on preceding images x_{<n}^0 provides additional structural information that neither the independent-image prior (Guide) nor the score-based prior (CSGM) can exploit.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper combines two established generative paradigms—autoregressive sequence modeling (as in PixelCNN, ImageGPT, DALL-E) and denoising diffusion (DDPM/DDIM)—into a unified framework for image sequence generation. The diffusion component follows the standard DDPM training objective and DDIM reverse step, while the autoregressive conditioning is implemented via a causal-attention DiTBlock that enforces x_n depends only on x_{<n}. For MRI reconstruction, the paper extends the Bayesian generative-prior framework (Jalal et al. 2021, Chung & Ye 2022, Luo et al. 2023) by replacing the independent-image prior with a sequence-aware prior, enabling the model to exploit inter-slice dependencies during posterior sampling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical contribution is that conditioning on preceding slices measurably reduces hallucinations in accelerated MRI reconstruction compared to an otherwise-identical independent diffusion model, making reconstructions more clinically trustworthy—particularly in regions with large undersampling artifacts. This is directly relevant to volumetric MRI acquisition where sequences of 12–46 slices are standard. Limitations include the absence of standard generative-model metrics (FID, Inception Score) and evaluation on large-scale natural image benchmarks, and the authors note that optimizing k-space acquisition trajectories and extending to multi-contrast/dynamic MRI remain open. The privacy implications of generating coherent patient-specific sequences also warrant careful deployment protocols.
