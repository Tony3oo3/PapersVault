## Problem

While prior work shows that finetuning aligned LLMs with as few as 10 harmful examples can completely dismantle safety guardrails, the underlying mechanism remains unexplained and no task-agnostic metric exists to quantify a model's vulnerability to finetuning before it occurs. This paper asks: why can simple finetuning so easily compromise safety alignment, and how can we measure and visualize that risk in the model's parameter space?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243031/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes the full parameter weights of an aligned open-source LLM (7B–13B) and perturbs them along randomly sampled Gaussian directions or linear-interpolation directions between two checkpoints, with each direction layer-normalized to the Frobenius norm of the corresponding weight matrix to ensure scale invariance across layers. At each perturbed point the model is evaluated on safety benchmarks (AdvBench, JailbreakBench, POSE) using refusal-keyword detection or LLaMA Guard 2 to compute attack success rate. The primary outputs are the VISAGE score (expected safety margin over uniform random perturbations within ±0.5 norm, averaged over three directions) and 1D/2D landscape visualizations that expose the basin geometry and finetuning trajectory.

## Method

The core contribution is the discovery of the 'safety basin': a local region in parameter space where random weight perturbations preserve the aligned model's safety level, bounded by a sharp step-like drop in safety outside the basin—a geometry fundamentally different from the smooth, gradually declining capability landscape. To construct the landscape, the paper samples random Gaussian directions (layer-normalized per Eq. 2) for single-model analysis, or uses linear interpolation between two model checkpoints for finetuning trajectory analysis, with 2D directions orthogonalized via Gram-Schmidt and rescaled for equivalence. The VISAGE metric is defined as E[S_max − S(α, β, …)] over uniform perturbations in [−0.5, 0.5], yielding a task-agnostic scalar that predicts how quickly a model will lose safety under finetuning without assumptions on the downstream dataset. The landscape framework further explains why mixing safe and harmful finetuning data keeps the model within the basin, why system prompts shift basin geometry across models, and why jailbreak prompts are hypersensitive to small weight perturbations, suggesting a naive defense of pre-response weight perturbation.

## Results

The paper discovers a universal 'safety basin' phenomenon: random weight perturbations maintain safety within a local neighborhood of aligned LLMs, but safety drops sharply (step-like) outside this region. The proposed VISAGE metric quantifies this basin depth, yielding scores of 85.32 for LLaMA2-7B-chat and 73.26 for Vicuna-7B-v1.5, correctly predicting that LLaMA2 is more robust to harmful finetuning. After 10-shot harmful finetuning, LLaMA2's ASR rises to 90.0% (AdvBench-80) while Vicuna reaches 95.0%; with 100-shot, both reach 100%. Mixing 100-shot harmful with 100-shot safe data keeps ASR at 0% (LLaMA2) and 1.3% (Vicuna), confirming that staying within the safety basin preserves alignment.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is an empirical discovery paper rather than a method-vs-baseline comparison, so the key contrasts are between models and finetuning strategies. LLaMA2-7B-chat (VISAGE 85.32) is consistently safer than Vicuna-7B-v1.5 (VISAGE 73.26) across all finetuning conditions: at 10-shot harmful, LLaMA2's ASR is 90.0% vs. Vicuna's 95.0% on AdvBench-80, and this gap persists at 50-shot and 100-shot. The VISAGE ranking across four models (LLaMA3 > LLaMA2 > Mistral > Vicuna) aligns with their AdvBench-520 ASR ordering (0.38, 0.19, 1.15, 2.5), validating VISAGE as a task-agnostic predictor of finetuning risk. On the system prompt front (Table 2), removing the default prompt drops Mistral-7B-instruct-v0.1's VISAGE by 53.33pp (from 74.11 to 20.78), while applying LLaMA2's system prompt universally improves safety across all tested models. For jailbreak attacks, the aligned LLaMA2 achieves 66.67% ASR on JailbreakBench, but slight weight perturbations within the safety basin can significantly reduce this, suggesting a naive perturbation-based defense.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the neural network loss landscape visualization methodology of Li et al. [31]—originally developed to study generalization and optimization—to the novel domain of LLM safety, introducing the concept of a 'safety landscape' in parameter space. It builds on the line of work showing that few-shot harmful finetuning breaks safety alignment (Qi et al. [37], Zhan et al. [48]) and on jailbreak attack literature (GCG [51], PAIR [8]), but departs from these by providing a geometric explanation for *why* finetuning compromises safety: the optimization trajectory drags the model out of a local 'safety basin.' The VISAGE metric is positioned as a task-agnostic complement to existing point-wise safety benchmarks like AdvBench, capturing robustness in the local parameter neighborhood rather than a single model's performance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The safety basin discovery reframes LLM safety as a geometric property of parameter space, offering a principled explanation for the fragility of alignment under finetuning and a new lens for designing defenses (e.g., keeping updates within the basin via mixed safe/harmful data). The finding that jailbreak prompts are highly sensitive to small weight perturbations—where some perturbed models are *safer* than the aligned model—opens a new, underexplored defense direction, though the paper acknowledges attackers can target multiple perturbed variants. Key limitations include evaluation restricted to 7B–8B models, reliance on keyword-based refusal detection as the primary metric, and the VISAGE score's dependence on only three random directions, leaving open questions about basin width, smoothness, and whether the phenomenon persists at larger scales.
