## Problem

Face feature aggregation for long-range, low-resolution recognition is hindered by existing methods that either scale quadratically with probe set size (MHA, CoNAN), require high-dimensional intermediate feature maps (CAFace), or depend on external metadata predictors (TADPool). These dependencies make them incompatible with legacy biometric template stores that retain only pre-computed penultimate feature vectors, and they limit real-time inference on large probe sets of hundreds of thousands of frames.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a set of N frozen d-dimensional face embeddings (e.g., 512-d Adaface or ArcFace vectors) extracted by a separate pretrained backbone, with no access to intermediate feature maps, spatial maps, or metadata. It produces a single aggregated template vector t ∈ R^(K̂·d) formed by concatenating K̂ expert-pooled sub-vectors. The input is both order-invariant and length-agnostic, and the method operates identically whether the feature set contains 100 or 1 million vectors.

## Method

ProxyFusion introduces K learnable proxy vectors that implicitly encode latent facial attributes; proxy relevancy scores (summed dot products between projected proxies and all projected features) select the top-K̂ experts from a bank of K three-layer MLPs, yielding O(N) inference. Each selected expert is conditioned on the feature-set's mean, variance, and its associated proxy to produce a set-center, which then computes softmax attention weights over all N features via un-normalized dot-product divergence. The weighted sum per expert yields a pooled sub-vector, and the final template is their concatenation. Training combines a supervised contrastive loss on probe-gallery template pairs with a proxy loss that pushes projected proxies toward K equidistant basis vectors on the unit hypersphere in R^(K−1), encouraging mutually exclusive expert specializations.

## Results

On the BTS 3.1 Face-Included Treatment protocol (RetinaFace + Adaface, Briar-trained), ProxyFusion achieves 68.9% TAR@FAR=10⁻², 53.9% at 10⁻³, and 40.1% at 10⁻⁴, outperforming the strongest baseline CoNAN (64.3/49.6/36.8) by 4.6, 4.3, and 3.3 points respectively. On the Face-Included Control protocol, ProxyFusion reaches 96.8% TAR@FAR=10⁻² versus CoNAN's 96.2%. On DroneSURF (trained on DroneSURF), ProxyFusion attains 83.33% Rank-1 accuracy on Active surveillance, surpassing CoNAN (80.21%), while matching it at 13.54% on Passive surveillance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ProxyFusion outperforms all four linear-time and quadratic-time baselines on BTS 3.1 Treatment across every FAR threshold. Against the strongest baseline CoNAN [5] (O(N²) intra-set attention), ProxyFusion gains 4.6 points at FAR=10⁻² (68.9 vs 64.3) while running in O(N) time and using fewer inference parameters (12.4M vs 13.6M). Against the linear-time baselines NAN [20] and MCN [19], the margins are larger: +7.7 and +6.0 points respectively at FAR=10⁻². Against the simple GAP [11] baseline, ProxyFusion improves by 10.5 points. On DroneSURF (trained on DroneSURF), ProxyFusion beats CoNAN on Active (83.33 vs 80.21) and ties on Passive (13.54 vs 13.54); in the cross-dataset setting (trained on BRS, tested on DroneSURF), ProxyFusion trails CoNAN on Active (80.21 vs 83.33) but leads on Passive (13.54 vs 12.50). In the WebFace-4M-trained comparison (Table 4), ProxyFusion marginally outperforms CAFace [9] (51.5 vs 51.3 at FAR=10⁻² Treatment) while being O(N) and not requiring intermediate feature maps, unlike CAFace's O(N²) approach.

## Relation to Prior Work

ProxyFusion builds on the mixture-of-experts (MoE) paradigm for sparse routing, repurposing it from language modeling to face feature aggregation. It departs from prior fusion methods like CoNAN and CAFace, which rely on quadratic intra-set attention or high-dimensional intermediate feature maps, by operating exclusively on final penultimate feature vectors with a proxy-based expert selection mechanism. The set-center conditioning on feature-set mean and variance follows CoNAN's distributional conditioning strategy, while the sparse top-K expert activation draws from sparsely-gated MoE literature. The key conceptual departure is that learnable proxies serve as implicit attribute queries that route each feature-set to a small subset of specialized pooling experts, eliminating the need for metadata predictors or spatial feature maps.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in enabling linear-time feature fusion compatible with existing biometric template stores that only store final feature vectors, which is critical for deploying on large-scale probe sets (hundreds of thousands of frames) in real-time or edge settings. The cross-distribution matching capability (low-resolution long-range probes vs. high-resolution gallery) addresses a genuine operational gap in surveillance and biometric verification. A key limitation acknowledged by the authors is that relying solely on set-level distribution statistics (mean/variance) for expert conditioning overlooks fine-grained intra-set relationships, suggesting future work could hierarchically combine this approach with local attention mechanisms. Open questions include whether the proxy-based routing generalizes beyond face recognition to other set-aggregation tasks and whether the fixed number of experts (K=11, K̂=4) is optimal across different feature extractor backbones.
