## Problem

Traditional image captioning models capture holistic scene descriptions but cannot pinpoint specific objects or attributes, while object detection systems localize elements without rich descriptive vocabulary. Dense captioning bridges this gap but is constrained by the limited expressiveness of existing annotation datasets. The paper addresses the missing ability to controllably describe any image region at a user-specified level of detail, from a single object label to a multi-word contextual caption.

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB image (224×224 during training, 448×448 at inference), a bounding box as four normalized coordinates, and a length-conditioning token (e.g., 'LENGTH-4') as input, and produces an autoregressively decoded caption of exactly that many words. For the FlexCap-LLM VQA pipeline, the image is first decomposed into top-128 OWL-ViTv2 box proposals, each described by FlexCap at multiple lengths with optional text prefixes, and the resulting text list (with box coordinates) is formatted as a prompt for PALM2-S. The model uses greedy decoding by default and stops at an end-of-sentence token or a maximum of 32 tokens.

## Method

FlexCap pairs a SOViT-400M/14 vision encoder (initialized from SigLIP contrastive weights and trained end-to-end) with a 12-layer, 768-dimensional causal transformer text decoder. Vision patch features (n×d) and a linearly projected bounding-box vector (1×d) are concatenated into an (n+1)×d token sequence that the decoder attends over while generating text autoregressively. The key mechanism is length conditioning: a 'LENGTH-K' token prepended to the target caption modulates information density, reducing prefix-collision ambiguity from 30.8% to 11.1% and enabling exact word-count control at inference. Training data is generated at scale by extracting n-grams (n=1–8) from web alt-text, filtering for grammatical completeness, and using them as text queries to OWL-ViT to obtain image-box-caption triplets—yielding 32 billion triplets from WebLI. The model is trained with standard next-token cross-entropy loss for 400K steps (batch 4096, AdamW, cosine schedule), and for VQA the generated region descriptions are passed as plain text context to an LLM rather than as visual feature tokens.

## Results

On MS-COCO region classification, FlexCap achieves 85.0 mAP with top-20 captions, substantially outperforming RegionCLIP (62.8) and CLIM (62.2). For zero-shot visual question answering, FlexCap-LLM reaches 65.6% on VQAv2, 48.8% on GQA, 52.1% on OKVQA, and 41.8% on VizWiz, matching or exceeding other zero-shot baselines like BLIP-2 and ViperGPT. On dense captioning over Visual Genome, FlexCap achieves 46.9 mAP on ground-truth boxes (vs. 36.29 for CAG-Net) and 16.2 mAP with GRiT proposals (vs. 15.52 for GRiT itself). For zero-shot video VQA, FlexCap-LLM scores 25.0% on MSRVTT-QA and 39.5% on MSVD-QA, surpassing Flamingo (17.4/35.6) and Emu (8.3/18.8).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among zero-shot VQA baselines, FlexCap-LLM (65.6%) narrowly beats BLIP-2 (65.2%) on VQAv2 and ViperGPT (51.9%) on OKVQA, while trailing InstructBLIP (49.5%) by 0.7 points on GQA. On VizWiz, FlexCap-LLM (41.8%) significantly outperforms InstructBLIP (34.5%), Emu (34.2%), and Flamingo (31.6%), demonstrating robustness to atypical images from visually impaired users. For dense captioning on Visual Genome, FlexCap (46.9 mAP) exceeds CAG-Net (36.29) and FCLN (27.11) on ground-truth boxes, and FlexCap+GRiT boxes (16.2) surpasses GRiT (15.52) and TDC+ROCSU (11.49) in the full localization-plus-captioning setting. On MS-COCO region classification, FlexCap with top-20 captions (85.0 mAP) dramatically outperforms contrastive region classifiers RegionCLIP (62.8) and CLIM (62.2). All supervised VQA baselines (PaLI-17B, BEIT-3, VLMO, PalmE-562B) substantially outperform FlexCap-LLM, as expected given they are fine-tuned on VQA data, but FlexCap-LLM achieves competitive results purely through zero-shot composition of a captioning model and an LLM without any task-specific training.

## Relation to Prior Work

FlexCap extends dense captioning (DenseCap, CAG-Net, GRiT) by adding length conditioning at the region level, building on prior length-controlled captioning work (ZeroCap, LIC) that was limited to full-image captions. For localization, it relies on OWL-ViT's open-vocabulary detection to generate training triplets and to propose boxes at inference, positioning itself as a 'localize-then-describe' pipeline rather than an end-to-end detector. For VQA, it departs from the dominant trend of tightly-coupled VLMs (Flamingo, BLIP-2, PaLI) by decoupling perception from reasoning: FlexCap produces human-interpretable text descriptions of regions, which are then fed to a separate LLM (PALM2) for question answering, a strategy more aligned with ViperGPT's tool-use paradigm but using a single unified vision tool.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in showing that a decoupled VLM+LLM system with text-only intermediate representations can match or exceed end-to-end VLMs on zero-shot VQA, suggesting that the 'visual features as tokens' paradigm is not the only path to strong multimodal reasoning. The 32-billion-triplet Localized Captions Dataset is a major reusable asset, and the length-conditioning mechanism provides a simple, effective knob for controlling information density. Key limitations include the non-end-to-end nature of the FlexCap-LLM pipeline, dependence on OWL-ViT's detection quality, and biases inherited from web alt-text; an open question is whether training a single VLM directly on the localized captioning dataset could close the gap while enabling joint optimization.
