## Problem

Existing text-to-image retrieval benchmarks (Flickr30K, COCO) are small, contain simple single-match queries, and are saturated by current models, leaving no way to evaluate retrieval on expert-level, domain-specific queries over large image collections. No benchmark exists that tests whether multimodal models can handle the nuanced, multi-match retrieval tasks that scientists actually perform on biodiversity image archives.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20244017/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark takes a short expert-level text query (e.g., 'Alligator lizards mating') and a pool of 5 million natural world images (iNat24, spanning 10,000 species classes), producing a ranked list of images by relevance. Unlike standard retrieval benchmarks where each query matches exactly one image, INQUIRE queries have 1–1.5k relevant matches each (33,000 total), requiring metrics that capture both recall and ranking quality. The RERANK task fixes the initial top-100 retrieval (from CLIP ViT-H-14) so that reranking methods are evaluated on a consistent, smaller candidate set of 4,000–16,000 images.

## Method

INQUIRE is a benchmark and evaluation protocol rather than a new model. It introduces 250 ecologically motivated queries, sourced from interviews with domain experts (ecologists, ornithologists, entomologists, oceanographers, forestry specialists) and academic literature, each comprehensively labeled over the full 5M-image iNat24 dataset. Two retrieval tasks are defined: INQUIRE-FULLRANK (end-to-end ranking over all 5M images) and INQUIRE-RERANK (reordering a fixed top-100 candidate set). For evaluation, CLIP-style embedding models are used for full-rank retrieval via cosine similarity over pre-computed image embeddings, while larger multimodal LLMs (GPT-4o, VILA, LLaVA, etc.) serve as rerankers prompted with a binary 'Does this image show {query}?' question, using the Yes/No logit ratio as a continuous relevance score. Metrics are adapted from text retrieval (mAP@50, nDCG@50, MRR) to handle the multi-match setting, a departure from the recall@k convention in image retrieval.

## Results

On INQUIRE-FULLRANK, the best CLIP-style model (SigLIP SO400m-14@384) achieves only mAP@50 of 34.2 and nDCG@50 of 49.1, while smaller models like CLIP RN50 score just 6.8 mAP@50. In the two-stage setting (Table 3), reranking the top-100 CLIP ViT-H-14 retrievals with GPT-4o improves mAP@50 from 33.3 to 43.7, yet the oracle best-possible rerank reaches 65.6, leaving a 22-point gap. On the dedicated INQUIRE-RERANK task, GPT-4o achieves the highest AP of 59.6 (nDCG 78.9, MRR 0.78), far below the perfect score of 100, confirming that even the strongest current models struggle substantially with expert-level natural world retrieval.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among CLIP-style embedding models, DFN-trained variants consistently outperform OpenAI CLIP of the same architecture (e.g., DFN ViT-H-14@378 achieves mAP@50 of 33.3 vs. OpenAI ViT-L-14 at 14.4), and SigLIP SO400m-14@384 edges out DFN ViT-H-14@378 (34.2 vs. 33.3 mAP@50). Domain-specific models BioCLIP and WildCLIP underperform generalist CLIP on non-species queries, with BioCLIP scoring only 5.0 mAP@50 overall despite strong species-query performance (44.8 AP on Species in Table 5). For reranking, GPT-4o is the clear leader, improving mAP@50 by 10.4 points over the initial CLIP ranking (33.3→43.7), followed by VILA-40B (+6.9) and GPT-4V (+3.2). Open-source models like LLaVA-v1.6-7B and BLIP-2 actually degrade performance relative to the initial ranking (32.3 and 31.2 vs. 33.3 mAP@50), suggesting weaker models can be confused by visually similar but irrelevant images. On the RERANK task, GPT-4o (AP 59.6) outperforms SigLIP SO400m-14 (50.1), VILA-40B (54.5 on no-lingo queries), and all LLaVA variants, while CLIP ViT-B-32 (30.2 AP) barely exceeds random (22.1). Notably, on Species queries requiring fine-grained discrimination, the specialized BioCLIP (44.8 AP) beats GPT-4o (44.3 AP), highlighting that generalist multimodal LLMs still lack fine-grained visual expertise.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by repurposing the two-stage retrieval paradigm (efficient initial ranking followed by expensive reranking) from text retrieval (TREC) into the image domain, where it had not been systematically evaluated due to the absence of suitable benchmarks. Conceptually, it extends text-to-image retrieval from the small, single-match, caption-derived setting of Flickr30K and COCO to a large-scale (5M images), multi-match, expert-level formulation grounded in real scientific use cases from iNaturalist. The evaluation draws on the existing landscape of contrastive VLMs (CLIP, SigLIP, BioCLIP, WildCLIP, DFN) and generative multimodal LLMs (GPT-4o, LLaVA, VILA, BLIP-2) as both initial retrievers and rerankers, but introduces no new model architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

INQUIRE fills a critical evaluation gap: prior text-to-image retrieval benchmarks are saturated (BLIP-2 scores 98.9 on Flickr30K) and lack the multi-match, expert-level structure of real scientific retrieval, making them unable to track progress on practically important problems. The finding that even GPT-4o achieves only ~60% AP on reranking and that domain-specific BioCLIP outperforms it on species queries highlights that general-purpose scaling alone is insufficient for fine-grained scientific retrieval, pointing toward the need for better domain-knowledge integration. Open questions include how to close the gap between current models and the 'best possible rerank' ceiling, how to improve comprehension of scientific terminology, and whether progress on natural world images transfers to other expert domains like biomedical imaging.
