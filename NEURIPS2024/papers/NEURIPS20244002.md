## Problem

Large multimodal models learn image-language grounding only implicitly through data-intensive end-to-end training, causing them to misattend to instruction-irrelevant visual regions and hallucinate even at billion-scale data. Existing visual grounding methods are restricted to predefined object categories or fixed instruction formats, making them inapplicable to the diverse, complex instructions encountered in real multimodal tasks such as VQA and robotic control.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20244002/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

Given an RGB image and a free-form textual instruction, IVM produces a pixel-level heatmap of the same spatial resolution as the input image, where activated regions correspond to all instruction-relevant content (which may span multiple objects, spatial areas, or even the full image for captioning tasks). The heatmap is consumed via a simple post-processing step—by default, overlaying a mask on inactive pixels and cropping to the bounding box of all activated (>0) values—before the simplified image is fed to a downstream LMM without any fine-tuning.

## Method

The IVM generator combines a frozen SAM-H vision backbone (for dense image features) with a LoRA-tuned LLaVA-7B (for multimodal instruction understanding), fusing both through two lightweight transformer decoder blocks that output a dense segmentation heatmap. Training uses a Discriminator-Weighted Supervised Learning (DWSL) framework: a GAN-inspired discriminator is first trained to separate 10K human-annotated samples from ~1M machine-generated ones, then its output serves as a per-sample weight (clipped to [0.1, 1]) on the BCE+DICE segmentation loss, dynamically prioritizing reliable annotations and suppressing noisy ones. The 1M training pairs are produced by an LLM-empowered Mixture-of-Expert pipeline that uses GPT-4 to generate or simplify instructions and ensembles four SOTA grounding models (Grounding-SAM, LISA, AlphaCLIP, OwlViT) to propose candidate masks. The entire IVM module is plug-and-play: it adds ~7B parameters of inference overhead but requires zero modification or retraining of the downstream LMM or robot policy it augments.

## Results

On V*Bench, IVM-Enhanced GPT4-V achieves 81.2% overall accuracy (87.0% Attribute, 72.4% Spatial), a +26.2% improvement over vanilla GPT4-V (55.0%) and surpassing the task-specialized SEAL model (75.4%). On the broader multi-benchmark evaluation (Table 2), IVM-Enhanced LLaVA-7B (14B total params) consistently outperforms vanilla LLaVA-7B across EgoThink (+3.4), POPE (+1.3), MME (+58), GQA (+0.2), and VQAv2 (+0.5), reaching or exceeding LLaVA-13B on EgoThink, POPE, and MME. On RefCoCo/RefCoCo+/RefCoCoG, IVM achieves 90.1/83.3/82.9 (IoU-50%), matching specialist model G-DINO-L (90.56/82.75/86.13) while substantially outperforming generalist LLaVA-7B (76.29/66.76/70.4).

## Baselines & Comparisons

IVM-Enhanced GPT4-V (81.2% overall) decisively beats vanilla GPT4-V (55.0%) by +26.2 points on V*Bench, and also surpasses the dedicated visual-search model SEAL (75.4%) by +5.8 points overall, though SEAL retains a narrow edge on the Spatial sub-metric (76.3 vs. 72.4). Against open-sourced LMMs (BLIP2, MiniGPT-4, InstructBLIP, Otter, LLaVA-1.5) and commercial chatbots (Bard, Gemini-Pro), IVM-Enhanced GPT4-V leads by 26–47 points on V*Bench overall. In the multi-benchmark comparison (Table 2), IVM-Enhanced LLaVA-7B outperforms LISA-Enhanced LLaVA-7B on every metric (e.g., EgoThink 54.5 vs. 47.9, MME 1806 vs. 1560, GQA 62.2 vs. 56.6), showing that LISA's single-object segmentation design is ill-suited for complex instruction following. IVM-Enhanced LLaVA-7B also beats Qwen-7B on GQA (62.2 vs. 58.3), SQA (70.2 vs. 67.1), and VQAv2 (79.0 vs. 78.8), and InstructBLIP on POPE (87.2 vs. 78.9) and MME (1806 vs. 1212.8). On RefCoCo, IVM (90.1) nearly matches specialist G-DINO-L (90.56) while far exceeding generalist LLaVA-7B (76.29), confirming that IVM's generic instruction-following capability does not sacrifice traditional grounding accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

IVM extends the visual grounding lineage from LISA (reasoning segmentation) and RefCOCO-style referring expression comprehension, which are limited to simple instructions targeting single objects, to a more general formulation that handles arbitrary complex instructions and multi-region localization. Architecturally, the heatmap generator follows LISA's design of combining a frozen SAM vision backbone with an LMM via a lightweight decoder, but the training framework departs significantly by introducing a GAN-inspired discriminator (borrowed from offline imitation learning) to weight mixed-quality annotations. The plug-and-play deployment strategy contrasts with end-to-end approaches like VisualCot and V*/SEAL, which require complex iterative search pipelines or task-specific fine-tuning, and with implicit grounding improvements like ViGor that focus on the grounding task itself rather than downstream instruction following.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

IVM offers a practical, model-agnostic way to inject strong visual grounding into any LMM or robot policy without retraining, which is valuable given that even billion-parameter models like GPT-4V still struggle with instruction-relevant region localization. The IVM-Mix-1M dataset and the LLM-empowered Mixture-of-Expert annotation pipeline provide a reusable resource for the community. Key open questions include whether end-to-end integration of heatmaps as an additional input channel (rather than heuristic post-processing) can close the remaining gap, whether the DWSL weighting strategy generalizes to other mixed-quality annotation settings, and how to handle the computational overhead of running a separate 7B grounding model at inference time. The robot manipulation results suggest broader applicability to embodied AI, but the evaluation is limited to four pick-and-place tasks.
