## Problem

Existing first-order methods for convex optimization with strongly convex function constraints achieve only O(1/ε) iteration complexity, failing to exploit the constraint strong convexity. The order-optimal O(1/√ε) rate known for strongly-convex-concave saddle point problems remained unattainable because the strong convexity of the Lagrangian L(·,y) is substantially harder to estimate than that of the constraint functions, due to its coupling with the varying dual sequence. The paper asks whether this gap can be closed and whether the resulting acceleration enables finite-time sparsity identification.

## Input / Output

The algorithm requires a convex (possibly non-smooth) objective f, strongly convex constraint functions g_i with known moduli μ_i, a lower bound r on the subdifferential norm at the optimum (e.g., r=1 for the L1 regularizer), and Lipschitz constants for constraint gradients over a bounded feasible region. It outputs an ε-accurate primal-dual pair, and the restart variant rAPDPro additionally identifies the exact sparsity pattern (active set) of the optimal solution within a finite number of iterations. The dual update involves solving a quadratic program with a novel cut constraint that keeps the dual sequence separated from the origin, which is critical for maintaining Lagrangian strong convexity.

## Method

APDPro augments the standard accelerated primal-dual method with two mechanisms: a cut constraint on the dual update that separates the dual sequence from the origin (ensuring the Lagrangian remains strongly convex along the solution path), and a progressive estimation routine that uses the latest primal iterates to construct a non-decreasing sequence of lower bounds on the strong convexity modulus of L(·,y). The step sizes are updated via τ_{k+1} = τ_k/√(1+ρ_{k+1}τ_k), where ρ_{k+1} is the progressively refined estimate, enabling increasingly aggressive steps and yielding O(1/√ε) complexity. A restart variant (rAPDPro) calls APDPro repeatedly with dynamically adjusted epoch lengths based on the current strong convexity estimate, reducing the primal diameter dependence from O(D_X/√ε) to O(log(D_X/√ε)). A multi-stage variant (msAPD) achieves comparable rates without the cut constraint by using different step sizes across stages, and the restart algorithm provably identifies the optimal sparsity pattern in finite iterations for group-Lasso constrained problems.

## Results

The paper's headline theoretical result is an improved iteration complexity of O(1/√ε) for APDPro (and its restart variant rAPDPro), compared to the O(1/ε) bound of prior accelerated primal-dual methods (APD [11], ConEx [4]) for convex problems with strongly convex constraints. For the last-iterate convergence, rAPDPro achieves O(log(D_X/√ε) + D_Y/√ε), improving the primal dependence from linear in D_X to logarithmic. Empirically, on three personalized PageRank datasets (bio-CE-HT, bio-CE-LC, econ-beaflw), rAPDPro and msAPD converge to high-precision solutions substantially faster than APD, APD+restart, and Mirror-Prox in both optimality gap and constraint violation, and identify the optimal sparsity pattern within a small number of iterations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares rAPDPro and msAPD against three baselines: the accelerated primal-dual method (APD) [11], APD with fixed-iteration restart (APD+restart), and Mirror-Prox [13], all evaluated on three graph-based personalized PageRank datasets (bio-CE-HT, bio-CE-LC, econ-beaflw). In both optimality gap (relative function value) and feasibility violation, rAPDPro and msAPD converge significantly faster than all three baselines, reaching high-precision solutions in fewer iterations. APD+restart offers only marginal improvement over vanilla APD, confirming that the fixed-iteration restart strategy is inferior to the progressive strong-convexity-based restart in rAPDPro. For active-set (sparsity pattern) identification, rAPDPro and msAPD correctly identify the optimal zero-block structure within a small number of iterations, while APD and Mirror-Prox show slower or incomplete identification. The msAPD method additionally exhibits a periodic convergence pattern consistent with its multi-stage step-size design, distinguishing it from the smoother convergence of rAPDPro.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the accelerated primal-dual (APD) framework of Hamedani & Aybat (2021), which itself generalizes the Chambolle-Pock primal-dual hybrid gradient method, by introducing progressive strong convexity estimation of the Lagrangian and cut constraints on the dual update. The key departure is that all prior first-order methods (APD, ConEx, augmented Lagrangian variants) achieve only O(1/ε) when the *constraints* are strongly convex but the objective is merely convex, despite the O(1/√ε) lower bound being known for strongly-convex-concave saddle point problems. The paper's core technical contribution is a novel strategy to progressively estimate the strong convexity modulus of L(·,y) using subdifferential separation, which was previously intractable due to the interplay between constraint functions and the evolving dual sequence.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work closes a notable gap in first-order optimization theory by showing that constraint strong convexity is algorithmically exploitable, achieving the order-optimal O(1/√ε) rate that was previously unattainable for this problem class. The active-set identification result for rAPDPro is particularly significant as it extends finite-time sparsity pattern recovery (previously known only for regularized Lasso via proximal gradient) to the constrained setting, with practical implications for large-scale PageRank and sparse learning. Key limitations include the requirement of a known lower bound on the subgradient norm at the optimum and smoothness of constraints; open directions include adaptive strategies when dual bounds are unavailable, extension to non-smooth constraints, and incorporating active constraint identification for problems with many constraints.
