## Problem

Universal online learning aims to achieve optimal regret across convex, exp-concave, and strongly convex functions without prior curvature knowledge, but the first universal gradient-variation guarantee (Yan et al., 2023) left a gap in the convex case—achieving only O(√(V_T log V_T)) instead of the optimal O(√V_T)—and required an inefficient three-layer ensemble with O((log T)²) base learners. This paper resolves that open problem by closing the convex-case gap while simultaneously reducing the ensemble to a simpler two-layer structure with O(log T) base learners and only 1 gradient query per round.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes exactly one gradient evaluation ∇f_t(x_t) per round (matching standard OGD's query complexity) on a convex compact domain X ⊆ R^d with bounded diameter D and gradient norm G, under L-smoothness. It outputs a single decision point x_t = Σ p_{t,i} x_{t,i} per round as a weighted combination of base-learner decisions, and the theoretical guarantee is expressed in terms of the gradient variation V_T = Σ‖∇f_t(x) − ∇f_{t−1}(x)‖² rather than the time horizon T, yielding problem-dependent bounds that tighten when the environment is stable.

## Method

The approach is a two-layer online ensemble: O(log T) base learners (grouped by curvature type with discretized coefficient guesses) run optimistic online mirror descent on surrogate functions h_{t,i} that require only the single shared gradient ∇f_t(x_t), while a meta learner (OPTIMISTIC-ADAPT-ML-PROD) tracks the best base learner on linearized losses. The key technical novelty is a novel analysis of the empirical gradient variation ‖∇f_t(x_t) − ∇f_{t−1}(x_{t−1})‖² that bypasses the need to control algorithmic stability ‖x_t − x_{t−1}‖²—previously the source of sub-optimality and the three-layer structure—by exploiting (i) a negative Bregman divergence term D_{f_t}(x⋆, x_t) arising from convex linearization, which cancels positive terms in the regret decomposition, and (ii) the tighter smoothness inequality ‖∇f(x) − ∇f(y)‖² ≤ 2L·D_f(y, x) from Nesterov's theory. For surrogate-based base learners, a second novel analysis bounds the cross-term ‖(x_{t,i} − x_t) − (x_{t−1,i} − x_{t−1})‖² by aggregating over time rather than controlling it per-round, enabling cancellation with curvature negative terms in the meta regret.

## Results

The proposed two-layer online ensemble achieves the optimal gradient-variation regret of O(log V_T) for strongly convex, O(d log V_T) for exp-concave, and O(√V_T) for convex functions, all with only 1 gradient query per round and O(log T) base learners. The key improvement over the prior best universal gradient-variation result (Yan et al. 2023) is closing the gap from O(√(V_T log V_T)) to O(√V_T) in the convex case while reducing base learners from O((log T)²) to O(log T). In the SEA model, the same single algorithm matches the state-of-the-art bounds of Chen et al. [2024] for all three curvature types without requiring curvature information.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against van Erven and Koolen [2016] and Wang et al. [2019], this paper strictly improves all three curvature cases by replacing the time-horizon dependence (T) with the problem-dependent gradient variation V_T, yielding tighter bounds whenever V_T < T. Against Zhang et al. [2022a], the convex-case regret improves from O(√T) to O(√V_T), and the gradient query complexity drops from O(log T) per round to just 1 per round, while matching the strongly convex and exp-concave bounds. Against Yan et al. [2023], the convex-case regret improves from O(√(V_T log V_T)) to the optimal O(√V_T), and the number of base learners decreases from O((log T)²) to O(log T), making the algorithm more efficient. In the SEA model (Table 2), the paper matches Chen et al. [2024] on all three curvature types (e.g., O((σ_max² + Σ_max²) log((σ²+Σ²)/(σ_max²+Σ_max²))) for strongly convex) but does so with a single universal algorithm, whereas Chen et al. require separate algorithms per curvature type. The paper also matches Sachs et al. [2022] on the convex SEA bound O(√(σ²+Σ²)) while additionally providing the strongly convex and exp-concave guarantees.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the universal online learning framework of Zhang et al. [2022a] and Yan et al. [2023], which use online ensembles to adapt to unknown curvature (convex, exp-concave, strongly convex) without prior knowledge. The key departure is analytical rather than algorithmic: instead of controlling algorithmic stability (which forced Yan et al. into a three-layer ensemble with O((log T)²) base learners and a suboptimal √(V_T log V_T) convex bound), the authors exploit a Bregman divergence negative term from linearization combined with a smoothness property (‖∇f(x)−∇f(y)‖² ≤ 2L·D_f(y,x)), inspired by Joulani et al. [2020] in stochastic optimization. This allows a simpler two-layer ensemble with only 1 gradient query per round and O(log T) base learners to achieve the optimal √(V_T) convex bound.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This resolves a major open problem in universal online learning by closing the gap between the best known gradient-variation regret with and without curvature information, while simultaneously improving computational efficiency. The result directly implies optimal small-loss regret and achieves state-of-the-art guarantees in the SEA model with a single algorithm, broadening applicability to stochastic-adversarial hybrid settings. Open questions include reducing the number of projections to 1 per round and extending to unconstrained domains via parameter-free online learning, which would further improve practical efficiency.
