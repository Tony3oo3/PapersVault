## Problem

3D Gaussian Splatting achieves real-time novel view synthesis but cannot represent the volumetric light transport of subsurface scattering (SSS), causing translucent materials like wax, marble, and skin to appear flat and unnatural. Existing NeRF-based SSS methods capture these effects but require expensive volumetric rendering with large MLPs, making them impractical for interactive use. This paper introduces the first 3D GS-based method for capturing detailed SSS effects on single objects with real-time rendering and relighting.

## Input / Output

The method consumes object-centric 360° multi-view OLAT (one-light-at-a-time) image collections with known light source positions and camera poses, acquired either synthetically in Blender (800×800, 112 light positions on 7 rings) or via a physical light stage with a motorized camera (158 camera positions × 167 light positions per object). It produces a 3D Gaussian representation augmented with per-Gaussian PBR parameters (basecolor, roughness, metalness, normal), a learned SSS residual, a per-Gaussian subsurfaceness scalar, and a neural incident light field. The output supports real-time novel view synthesis (~155–161 FPS on an RTX 4090), arbitrary relighting, and per-pixel material editing (basecolor, roughness, metalness, SSS intensity).

## Method

The core contribution is a hybrid representation that extends 3D Gaussian Splatting with a lightweight 3-layer MLP (64-32-32 neurons, Leaky-ReLU) that jointly predicts both the outgoing SSS radiance and the incident illumination for each Gaussian, using a shared backbone with separate output heads. This joint prediction forces the network to implicitly learn local and global light transport, while a per-Gaussian subsurfaceness parameter blends the SSS residual with the explicit Disney BRDF (diffuse + specular) evaluated in image space via deferred shading rather than at the Gaussian center. Ray-traced visibility through a BVH provides per-Gaussian shadowing supervision, and the entire pipeline is optimized end-to-end via differentiable rendering with L1, SSIM, LPIPS, normal, mask, and bilateral smoothing losses. The deferred shading step projects rasterized surface positions back to 3D to evaluate the BRDF per pixel, overcoming the resolution limit of individual Gaussians and enabling crisp specular highlights independent of Gaussian size.

## Results

On the synthetic OLAT SSS dataset (800×800, 11,200 images), the method achieves 37.35±2.13 PSNR, 0.986±0.006 SSIM, and 0.03±0.01 LPIPS at 161.2 FPS with ~2h training. On the real-world dataset (800px, ~12,000 images), it reaches 31.12±2.11 PSNR and 0.96±0.02 SSIM at 155.25 FPS. In the sparse setting (256×256, 500 images) matching KiloOSF's protocol, the method achieves 35.01±1.01 PSNR (synthetic) and 26.61±0.09 PSNR (real-world) in under 1 hour of training, compared to KiloOSF's 25.91±1.88 and 23.24±1.58 PSNR respectively after >20 hours.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against KiloOSF [46] (the only direct quantitative baseline), the proposed method wins decisively on all metrics in the matched 256²/500-image setting: 35.01 vs. 25.91 PSNR on synthetic data (+9.1 dB) and 26.61 vs. 23.24 PSNR on real-world data (+3.4 dB), while rendering at 154.8 vs. 14.4 FPS (~10× faster) and training in <1h vs. >20h. The LPIPS gap is particularly large on real-world data (0.08 vs. 0.21), indicating substantially better perceptual quality. Against R3DGS [10], the comparison is primarily qualitative and ablation-based: R3DGS with an attached incident light field achieves only 24.81±5.29 PSNR (Table 2) versus 36.16±3.76 for the full method, and visually fails to relight SSS objects because it bakes the scattering into the basecolor under a single static illumination. The paper notes that NeRF-based SSS methods (e.g., [49, 25]) achieve comparable quality on synthetic scenes but cannot be directly compared due to unavailable datasets, and their training/inference times are substantially longer.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 3D Gaussian Splatting framework (Kerbl et al., 2023) and its PBR relighting extension R3DGS (Gao et al., 2023) by adding an implicit neural component for subsurface scattering. It departs from prior NeRF-based SSS methods (Neural Radiance Transfer Fields, OSF/KiloOSF, Neural Relighting with SSS) that rely on volumetric rendering and large MLPs, instead coupling a lightweight 3-layer MLP with explicit per-Gaussian PBR parameters and deferred shading in image space. The key conceptual move is jointly predicting the SSS residual and incident illumination in a single shared MLP, constrained by ray-traced visibility, to implicitly capture local and global light transport without explicit BSSRDF integration.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper delivers the first public OLAT SSS dataset with real-world objects captured in a light stage, filling a gap where prior NeRF-based SSS methods had no released data. The 10× rendering speedup (161 FPS vs. 14 FPS for KiloOSF) with comparable or better quality makes SSS relighting practical for interactive applications like medical visualization, VFX, and VR. Open questions remain around heterogeneous materials, angularly dependent SSS, transparent refracting surfaces, and reconstruction under unconstrained (non-OLAT) illumination, all of which the current formulation cannot handle without retraining.
