## Problem

Existing protein representation methods either encode sequences or 3D structures in isolation, or fuse them via simple concatenation that fails to model the intricate inter-dependencies between the two. Current protein GNNs suffer from message attenuation over long-range dependencies, assume localized neighborhood relationships that miss complex long-range amino acid interactions, and lack a principled way to capture both local conformational details and global structural arrangements simultaneously.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a protein's amino acid sequence (residue types and positions) together with its 3D backbone coordinates (Cα, C, N, O atoms), which are converted into a dynamically constructed two-type graph where node features encode residue identity, physicochemical properties, and backbone torsion angles (Φ, Ψ, Ω), while edge features encode complete SE(3)-invariant geometric representations (Cα distances, interresidue dihedral and planar angles). The output is a single graph-level protein embedding obtained after eight message-passing layers interleaved with four sequence-averaging pooling layers, which is then fed to task-specific classifiers for fold, superfamily, family, enzyme reaction, GO term, and EC number prediction.

## Method

CoupleNet constructs two complementary graphs per protein: a sequential graph connecting residues within sequence distance l and a radius graph connecting residues within Cα distance r, with the edge set defined by the intersection of both conditions. The key architectural insight is performing convolutions on nodes and edges simultaneously (h_i^(l) = h_i^(l) + Dropout(FC(σ(BN(Σ_j W e_ij h_j^(l-1)))))) rather than passing messages separately, so that geometric edge features directly modulate how node features are aggregated. Completeness at the amino acid and backbone levels is guaranteed by using local coordinate systems (LCS) for each residue and six interresidue geometries (d_Cβ, ω, θ, φ) that fully determine backbone atom positions. The graphs are dynamically re-parameterized with network depth: after each pooling layer halves the sequence length, the radius r doubles (4→8→16), progressively expanding the receptive field from local contacts to global structural relationships while reducing computational cost.

## Results

CoupleNet achieves state-of-the-art performance across all four benchmark tasks. On fold classification, it reaches 60.6% accuracy (Fold), 82.1% (SuperFamily), and 99.7% (Family), surpassing the second-best method CDConv by 3.9%, 4.4%, and 0.1% respectively. On enzyme reaction classification, CoupleNet attains 89.0% accuracy. For GO term and EC number prediction (measured by F_max), CoupleNet scores 0.467/0.669/0.494 on GO-BP/MF/CC and 0.866 on EC, outperforming all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoupleNet outperforms all 10 compared baselines across every task. The closest competitor is CDConv [28], which also uses sequence-structure convolution; CoupleNet leads by 3.9% on Fold, 4.4% on SuperFamily, and 0.5% on EC F_max, indicating that the dynamic graph construction and simultaneous node-edge convolution provide meaningful gains over static continuous-discrete convolution. Among structure-based methods, IEConv (atom level) [12] achieves 45.0% Fold accuracy, well below CoupleNet's 60.6%, highlighting the benefit of jointly modeling sequence and structure. ProNet [15], which also uses complete geometric representations but at fixed hierarchy levels, reaches 52.7% Fold accuracy, 7.9% below CoupleNet. GearNet [14] and its variants (best: GearNet-Edge-IEConv at 48.3% Fold) underperform CoupleNet by over 12% on Fold, attributed to GearNet's fixed radius threshold and alternating node-edge message passing. Sequence-only baselines from TAPE [22] (ResNet, Transformer) and structure-only baselines (GCN [37], GAT [48], 3DCNN_MQA [24]) are substantially outperformed, confirming that coupling both modalities is critical for protein representation learning.

## Relation to Prior Work

CoupleNet extends the complete 3D graph representation framework established by ProNet and ComENet, which guarantee SE(3)-invariant geometric encodings at the amino acid and backbone levels, and adapts the local coordinate system from Ingraham et al. and the six interresidue geometries from trRosetta. Its key departure from GearNet and CDConv is the construction of two dynamically re-parameterized graphs (sequential and radius) whose thresholds expand with network depth, combined with simultaneous node-edge convolution rather than alternating node/edge message passing. This dual-graph, dynamic-threshold design is the paper's architectural novelty, positioned between the single-graph GNN approaches (GearNet) and the multi-level hierarchical encoders (ProNet, IEConv).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CoupleNet sets new SOTA on four standard protein representation benchmarks and, notably, maintains strong performance under low sequence-identity splits, suggesting the learned embeddings capture remote homology relationships that sequence-only or static-graph methods miss. The practical limitation is that the method requires both sequence and 3D structure as input, excluding the large pool of sequence-only proteins (e.g., from UniProt without AlphaFold predictions). An open question is whether the dynamic dual-graph strategy can be adapted to a sequence-only or structure-predicted setting, and whether the completeness guarantee translates to side-chain-level representations for drug-binding pocket prediction.
