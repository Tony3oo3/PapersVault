## Problem

Transformer-based WSI analysis faces a fundamental low-rank bottleneck: when the number of patch instances n far exceeds the embedding dimension d (n >> 1024 > d), the attention matrix rank is capped at d, limiting the model's ability to simultaneously capture local context and global interactions. Existing workarounds—Nyströmformer approximation (TransMIL) and non-overlapping region slicing (HIPT)—further degrade this already-constrained representation by discarding cross-region or cross-approximation information, while full self-attention's O(n²) complexity becomes prohibitive at 40× magnification where instance counts exceed 50,000.

## Input / Output

The method operates on the WSI aggregation head, consuming pre-computed patch feature embeddings (e.g., 384-d from ViT-Small, 1024-d from ResNet-50, or 1024-d from UNI/GigaPath) along with their 2D spatial coordinates, extracted from foreground tissue regions segmented at 20× or 40× magnification into non-overlapping 224×224 or 256×256 patches. It produces a single slide-level output: a tumor subtype probability vector (3-class BRACS or 2-class TCGA-BRCA) or a survival risk score (C-index), trained end-to-end with only slide-level weak supervision.

## Method

LongMIL is a local-global hybrid Transformer that first applies two layers of 2D local self-attention with a fixed bandwidth mask (zeroing attention scores beyond Euclidean distance b from the query position), which provably raises the attention matrix rank from ≤ d to ≥ n−b while reducing per-layer complexity from O(n²d) to O(bnd) via FlashAttention-style chunked computation. After the local layers, a 2×2 spatial pooling reduces the token count by 4×, followed by a single full self-attention layer (with RoPE positional encoding) to capture global long-range dependencies before a linear classifier. The 2D local mask treats x- and y-axis distances symmetrically (unlike 1D z-scan approaches), and the locality inductive bias enables extrapolation to unseen or under-fitted spatial positions in shape-varying WSIs where absolute positional embeddings fail. The theoretical contribution establishes that the low-rank constraint rank(QKᵀ) ≤ min(n,d) = d is the root cause of suboptimal WSI modeling, and that restricting attention to a local band is the minimal intervention that simultaneously resolves the rank deficiency, the quadratic complexity, and the positional generalization gap.

## Results

On BRACS 3-class tumor subtyping with ViT-S Lunit embeddings, LongMIL achieves 0.706 F1 and 0.888 AUC, outperforming Full Attention (0.689 F1, 0.870 AUC) and TransMIL (0.648 F1, 0.835 AUC). On survival prediction (C-index), LongMIL leads across all three TCGA datasets: COADREAD 0.624, STAD 0.589, and BRCA 0.619, versus 0.603/0.568/0.601 for Full Attention. With pathology foundation models (UNI), LongMIL reaches 0.728 F1 and 0.887 AUC on BRACS, surpassing GigaPath's pretrained WSI-head (0.677 F1, 0.862 AUC on its own encoder). The method also converts quadratic O(n²d) attention complexity to linear O(bnd) via local masking, enabling practical 40× magnification training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LongMIL consistently outperforms all 13 baselines on the primary BRACS tumor subtyping task. Against vanilla attention MIL methods (AB-MIL, DS-MIL, CLAM-SB, DTFD-MIL), LongMIL gains 1.8–10.9 F1 points, confirming that pairwise interaction modelling is essential. Against TransMIL (Nyströmformer approximation), LongMIL improves F1 by 5.8 points (0.706 vs 0.648) and AUC by 5.3 points, validating the paper's low-rank bottleneck analysis. Against Full Attention with RoPE, LongMIL gains 1.7 F1 and 1.8 AUC points while reducing complexity from O(n²d) to O(bnd). On survival prediction, LongMIL beats HIPT (region slicing) by 1.6 C-index on COADREAD and 1.9 on STAD, and outperforms GCN-MIL and AMISL by 5–10 C-index points. With pathology foundation models, LongMIL+UNI (0.728 F1) surpasses GigaPath's pretrained WSI-head (0.677 F1) by 5.1 F1 points, and LongMIL+GigaPath (0.673 F1) matches GigaPath-pretrained (0.677 F1) while using a different patch encoder. Linear attention variants (RetNet, GLA, Mamba, V-Mamba) all underperform Full Attention when used standalone, but when combined as the global layer in LongMIL (e.g., LongMIL+V-Mamba: 0.693 F1), they approach Full Attention performance with linear complexity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Transformer-based WSI analysis lineage initiated by TransMIL (Nyströmformer approximation) and HIPT (region slicing), both of which attempted to handle the O(n²) complexity of self-attention on long WSI sequences but at the cost of information loss. The key conceptual departure is a theoretical diagnosis: the attention matrix in WSI Transformers is fundamentally low-rank (rank ≤ d << n), which constrains the model's ability to simultaneously capture local and global interactions. Building on FlashAttention's IO-aware chunking, the authors introduce a 2D local attention mask that provably raises the rank to ≥ n−b while reducing complexity to O(bnd), and combine it with a global full-attention layer in a hybrid architecture (LongMIL).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The low-rank bottleneck analysis provides a unifying explanation for why multiple prior approaches (Nyströmformer, linear attention, region slicing) underperform for WSI, which is valuable diagnostic insight for the digital pathology community. Practically, the linear-complexity local attention makes 40× magnification WSI analysis feasible (previously ~30× slower with FlashAttention), opening the door to finer-grained histopathological features. Limitations include reliance on fixed embedding sizes from pre-trained patch encoders, modest absolute C-index scores for survival prediction, and the method's restriction to very large images. Open questions include whether larger embedding dimensions or multi-modality fusion could further close the gap to clinical utility.
