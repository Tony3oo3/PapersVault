## Problem

Logits-based zero-shot detectors (DetectGPT, DNA-GPT, Fast-DetectGPT) rely on a surrogate model to approximate the target black-box LLM's probability distribution, but distribution misalignment between the surrogate and the often-undisclosed target model causes significant performance degradation—especially as closed-source models are frequently updated. No prior method provides a practical way to align a surrogate model's distribution with an unknown target model's distribution without access to the target's internal logits.

## Input / Output

At inference time, the method takes a text passage and a sampling model that generates alternative token-level samples conditioned on the passage; the fine-tuned surrogate model then computes conditional probability curvature scores in a single forward pass, yielding a binary AI-generated vs. human-written classification. At training time, the input is a small dataset (<10K prompt–response pairs) collected from the target model's publicly available outputs (e.g., WildChat for GPT-3.5/GPT-4, API-generated data for Claude-3), where only the response tokens contribute to the loss while prompt gradients are masked.

## Method

DALD fine-tunes an open-source surrogate model (e.g., Llama-2-7B) via LoRA using self-supervised next-token prediction on a compact corpus of the target model's outputs, aligning the surrogate's token-level probability distribution with that of the target. The aligned surrogate is then plugged directly into existing logits-based detection pipelines (Fast-DetectGPT, DetectGPT, DNA-GPT) as a drop-in replacement for the original surrogate, requiring no changes to the detection algorithm itself. A theoretical guarantee (Theorem 1) shows that with Ω(poly(Δ/L)) fine-tuning samples, the conditional probability curvature gap between machine- and human-generated text is preserved within Δ/3 when scoring with the aligned surrogate instead of the target model. The approach is model-agnostic (works with any open-source surrogate), requires only ~5K training samples and <10 minutes on 4 A6000 GPUs, and generalizes across model versions and even to unknown source models in a one-for-all setting.

## Results

DALD achieves AUROC scores above 0.96 on all seven dataset–model combinations, with headline results of 0.9954 (GPT-4 XSum), 0.9980 (GPT-4 Writing), and 0.9981 (Claude-3 Writing). The most dramatic gain is on GPT-4 PubMed, where DALD reaches 0.9785 AUROC versus 0.8179 for the strongest baseline Fast-DetectGPT — a 16-point improvement. On the hardest setting (Claude-3 PubMed), DALD scores 0.9630, still surpassing Fast-DetectGPT (0.8900) by over 7 points. Training requires only ~5K samples and under 10 minutes on 4×A6000 GPUs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DALD outperforms all seven baseline families across every dataset–model combination. Against the strongest zero-shot baseline Fast-DetectGPT [Bao 2029], DALD gains 5.4 points on ChatGPT PubMed (0.9853 vs. 0.9309), 16.1 points on GPT-4 PubMed (0.9785 vs. 0.8179), and 5.4 points on Claude-3 Writing (0.9981 vs. 0.9445). DNA-GPT [Yang 2028] and DetectGPT [Mitchell 2027] trail further behind, with DetectGPT scoring as low as 0.5806 on GPT-4 PubMed. The training-based RoBERTa classifiers [Solaiman 2019] are the weakest, often falling below 0.60 AUROC on GPT-4 and Claude-3 settings. Simple statistical features from GLTR [Gehrmann 2019] (Likelihood, Entropy) and DetectLLM [Su 2023] (LogRank, LRR) also underperform, with Entropy scoring near chance (0.29–0.45) on most settings. Notably, DALD's plug-and-play design means it can be layered onto any of these logits-based detectors (DetectGPT, DNA-GPT, Fast-DetectGPT) to boost their scores, as confirmed in the ablation (Table 4).

## Relation to Prior Work

DALD builds directly on the logits-based zero-shot detection lineage (DetectGPT, DNA-GPT, Fast-DetectGPT), which all rely on a surrogate model to approximate the target model's probability distribution in black-box settings. The paper identifies the distribution misalignment between surrogate and target models as the core bottleneck and proposes a minimal intervention: fine-tuning the surrogate with LoRA on <10K publicly available outputs from the target model. This positions DALD as a plug-and-play enhancement layer rather than a new detection paradigm, and the theoretical analysis (Theorem 1) is framed specifically in terms of Fast-DetectGPT's conditional probability curvature metric.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is substantial: as closed-source LLMs update frequently, static surrogate models become obsolete, and DALD offers a lightweight fix (5K samples, <10 min on 4 GPUs) that keeps detection current without retraining the full pipeline. The one-for-all finding—that a surrogate trained solely on GPT-4 outputs generalizes to GPT-3.5, GPT-4o, and Claude-3 with >98% AUROC—suggests current frontier LLMs share more distributional similarity than previously assumed, potentially enabling a single universal detector. Open questions include multilingual generalization (only German tested), robustness to genuinely novel architectures, and whether the approach degrades when target model outputs are scarce or stylistically narrow.
