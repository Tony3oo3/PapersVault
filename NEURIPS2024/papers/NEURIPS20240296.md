## Problem

Pareto-optimal learning-augmented algorithms, which optimize the tradeoff between consistency (performance under perfect predictions) and robustness (performance under adversarial predictions), suffer from brittleness: their performance can degrade to the robustness bound even under arbitrarily small prediction errors. This means a Pareto-optimal algorithm may perform worse than the best no-prediction competitive algorithm in realistic near-perfect prediction scenarios. Additionally, existing Pareto-optimal algorithms for one-way trading are tailored exclusively to worst-case input sequences (continuously increasing rates followed by a drop to 1) that never arise in real markets.

## Input / Output

The algorithm receives a sequence of exchange rates p_i ∈ [1,M] revealed one at a time, a maximum-rate prediction p̂, and (for the profile-based setting) a user-specified step function F over l intervals partitioning [1,M], where F maps each interval to a desired upper bound on the performance ratio. The prediction p̂ must fall within one designated interval of F. The output is a sequence of fractional exchange amounts at each revealed rate, guided by a threshold function Φ mapping utilization to reservation rates. Notably, for ADA-PO the prediction need not be given ahead of time—it can be revealed during execution since it is only used in the second phase. The PROFILE algorithm additionally outputs a feasibility decision for F in O(l) time.

## Method

The paper introduces two algorithmic contributions built on online threshold algorithms (OTAs). First, PROFILE constructs a threshold function Φ iteratively over the l profile intervals, handling two distinct update types: for the increasing part of the profile (where the ratio bound relaxes), it extends Φ with an exponential segment starting at the current utilization; for the decreasing part (where the ratio bound tightens), it first increases utilization to meet the stricter constraint, then extends Φ from the new point. This global, incremental construction ensures all interval constraints are simultaneously satisfied and decides feasibility by checking whether final utilization exceeds 1. Second, ADA-PO is an adaptive Pareto-optimal algorithm with two phases: a threat-based phase below p̂ (exchanging minimum amounts to guarantee r-robustness) and an optimization phase above p̂ where it solves a per-rate optimization problem to maximize the exchange amount while maintaining r-robustness, yielding a dynamic threshold function that updates at each revealed rate. ADA-PO dominates all other Pareto-optimal algorithms via a lexicographic profit comparison over rates at or above p̂, and unlike static OTAs, it does not require the prediction in advance.

## Results

On 100 randomly generated worst-case sequences with prediction error in [0.9p̂, 1.1p̂], PROFILE achieves an average 22% improvement in performance ratio over the state-of-the-art Pareto-optimal algorithm (PO) of Sun et al. When the actual maximum rate p* is below the prediction p̂, PROFILE improves by 20–50%; when p* exceeds p̂, PROFILE is inferior by only 10–20%. On real BTC/USD daily exchange rates (1000 rates, prediction from first 200), PROFILE maintains smooth degradation within the profile interval while PO exhibits full brittleness (ratio = r = 4) for near-miss predictions. In the adaptive setting, ADA-PO outperforms PO on every rate exceeding p̂ in BTC sequences, at the cost of increased brittleness below p̂.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is the Pareto-optimal threshold algorithm (PO) of Sun et al. (NeurIPS 2021), which is the state-of-the-art for one-way trading with maximum-rate prediction. PROFILE (the profile-based algorithm) wins decisively on smoothness: it guarantees a bounded performance ratio t₂ < r across the entire interval [0.9p̂, 1.1p̂], whereas PO degrades to its full robustness ratio r even for arbitrarily small prediction errors below p̂ (Theorem 3.1). In terms of average performance, PROFILE outperforms PO by 22% on synthetic worst-case sequences and by 15–30% on 12 out of 20 real BTC sequences; PO recovers the advantage only at the exact prediction point p* = p̂ (by definition of Pareto optimality) and by at most 10% on the 8 BTC sequences where it wins. ADA-PO (the adaptive algorithm) dominates PO on every non-worst-case sequence via a lexicographic profit comparison (Theorem 5.1), but inherits the brittleness of Pareto-optimality for sequences where p* < p̂, making it best suited when the prediction is conservative or revealed online.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the threshold-algorithm (OTA) framework introduced by Zhou et al. [41] and the Pareto-optimal learning-augmented algorithm for online conversion by Sun et al. [38], while drawing on the threat-based policies of El-Yaniv et al. [19] for the adaptive setting. The key conceptual departure is the introduction of a user-specified performance profile that enforces smoothness across the entire prediction-error spectrum, rather than the standard consistency/robustness tradeoff that only constrains performance at the two extremes. The profile framework is further applied to contract scheduling, building on Angelopoulos and Kamali [7], demonstrating its generality beyond one-way trading.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The brittleness theorem (Theorem 3.1) reveals a fundamental limitation of the dominant Pareto-optimality criterion: a Pareto-optimal algorithm can perform as poorly as its robustness bound even under near-perfect predictions, meaning it may be worse than the best no-prediction algorithm in realistic settings. The profile framework provides a practical remedy that is generalizable to other learning-augmented problems suffering from brittleness. Open questions include extending the framework to dynamic prediction settings, two-way trading, and portfolio optimization, as well as studying how profiles interact with more complex prediction structures (e.g., vector-valued predictions).
