## Problem

Fine-tuning vision-language models like CLIP on downstream tasks requires accurately labeled data, but real-world datasets frequently contain noisy labels that degrade performance. Existing noisy-label learning methods are designed for training from scratch and do not exploit the strong image-text alignment in pre-trained vision-language models, leaving a gap in how to leverage multi-modal features for robust adaptation under label noise.

## Input / Output

The method takes a noisy labeled image dataset (e.g., CIFAR-100, Tiny-ImageNet, Stanford-Cars, CUB-200-2011, Clothing1M, WebVision) where a fraction of labels are corrupted, along with a pre-trained CLIP model (ViT-B/16 image encoder + Transformer text encoder). It produces two outputs: a curated clean subset of training samples identified via learned dual textual prompts (requiring no prior knowledge of the noise ratio), and final class predictions from a fully fine-tuned model. On real-world datasets, an additional constraint requiring label-prediction consistency is applied to handle more complex noise patterns.

## Method

DEFT is a two-phase denoising fine-tuning framework. In Phase 1, it learns a class-specific pair of positive and negative textual prompts: the positive prompt maximizes alignment with class-specific image features to reveal distinctive patterns, while the negative prompt acts as a learnable, sample-dependent similarity threshold. A training sample is deemed clean if its image-text similarity with the positive prompt exceeds that with the negative prompt, eliminating the need for noise-ratio priors. The visual encoder is adapted via PEFT (VPT) rather than full fine-tuning to preserve pre-trained representations under noise, and the prompts are optimized with a negative learning objective using pseudo labels. In Phase 2, PEFT modules are removed and the pre-trained model is fully fine-tuned with a linear classifier on only the selected clean samples for 10 epochs, leveraging FFT's superior discriminative ability on clean data to handle domain shifts, particularly in fine-grained tasks.

## Results

On synthetic noisy datasets, DEFT achieves the best test accuracy across all four benchmarks and six noise settings. On Stanford-Cars with 60% symmetric noise, DEFT reaches 85.72% (best) vs. 79.09% for SCE and 56.90% for GMM; on CUB-200-2011 with 60% symmetric noise, DEFT achieves 73.08% vs. 63.31% for SCE and 42.84% for GMM. On real-world datasets, DEFT attains 79.04% on CIFAR-100N, 72.44% on Clothing1M, and 85.12% on WebVision, outperforming the strongest baselines (UNICON 77.68%, ProMix 70.71%, LongReMix 84.96%) by 1.36%, 1.73%, and 0.16% respectively. For noisy label detection, DEFT achieves up to 99.56% precision and 99.33% recall on Stanford-Cars (Sym.0.2), surpassing the small-loss strategy by 3.00% and 2.63% in precision and recall.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DEFT consistently outperforms all nine baselines across synthetic and real-world datasets. Against noise-robust loss methods (ELR, SCE), DEFT wins on nearly all settings; ELR is occasionally competitive on CIFAR-100 (e.g., 85.78% vs. 85.75% at Ins.0.4) but degrades sharply on fine-grained datasets (61.58% vs. 85.72% on Stanford-Cars Sym.0.6). GMM (DivideMix) is the strongest sample-selection baseline on CIFAR-100 and Tiny-ImageNet but collapses on fine-grained tasks (42.84% on CUB-200-2011 Sym.0.6 vs. DEFT's 73.08%). On real-world datasets, DEFT surpasses the four recent sample-selection methods (RoLT, UNICON, LongReMix, ProMix) by 1.36–4.51% on CIFAR-100N, 1.73% on Clothing1M, and 0.16% on WebVision. On Clothing1M with diverse backbones (Table 4), DEFT outperforms GCE by 4.63–4.43% and TURN by 4.51% (ResNet-50) and 3.27% (MAE-ViT-B), confirming generality across architectures.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of noisy label learning and vision-language model adaptation. It builds directly on CLIP's contrastive pre-training (Radford et al.), CoOp's learnable textual prompts (Zhou et al.), and Visual Prompt Tuning (Jia et al.), repurposing the pre-trained image-text alignment as a noisy label detector via a novel dual-prompt (positive/negative) mechanism. Conceptually, it departs from prior sample-selection methods (Co-teaching, DivideMix) that rely on loss-based criteria or require knowledge of the noise ratio, instead using a data-driven, modality-informed threshold. A key empirical departure is the finding that PEFT (specifically VPT) preserves pre-trained representations under noisy labels while FFT distorts them, motivating a two-phase design: PEFT for detection, FFT for adaptation on curated clean data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DEFT offers a practical, plug-and-play recipe for adapting pre-trained vision-language models to noisy downstream datasets without requiring prior knowledge of noise ratios, and its model adaptation phase generalizes to arbitrary visual backbones (ResNet, MAE, ConvNeXt). The two-phase PEFT-then-FFT design provides actionable guidance for practitioners facing domain shift combined with label noise. Limitations include restriction to single-label multi-class settings and reliance on CLIP's text encoder for the detection phase; extending to multi-label classification, noisy image-text pairs, or other modalities remains open. The paper also raises the broader question of whether multi-modal pre-training can serve as a general-purpose signal for data quality assessment beyond label noise.
