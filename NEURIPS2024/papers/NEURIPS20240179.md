## Problem

Higher-order graph neural networks that pass messages between subgraphs must be equivariant to the automorphism group of each local subgraph to faithfully exploit its structure, but explicitly determining automorphism groups and their irreducible representations for arbitrary subgraph templates is infeasible. Prior work (Autobahn) handles only a few hand-crafted subgraph types, leaving a gap for general, scalable architectures that leverage local topology without group-theoretic enumeration.

## Input / Output

The method consumes molecular graphs with vertex and edge features, processed simultaneously at 0th order (scalar node/edge representations) and 1st order (vectors indexed by the vertices of each detected subgraph template, e.g., cycles of length 3–8). The Schur layer operates on the 1st-order subgraph representations, projecting them onto eigenspaces of the subgraph Laplacian before applying per-eigenspace learnable weight matrices. The final output is a graph-level scalar (regression of log P on ZINC-12K) or binary classification score (OGB-HIV), obtained by summing node-level outputs through a readout.

## Method

The key insight is that the eigenspaces of a subgraph's combinatorial Laplacian are invariant under its automorphism group, so projecting a 1st-order P-tensor onto these eigenspaces and applying independent learnable weight matrices per eigenspace yields an automorphism-equivariant linear map without ever computing Aut(S) or its irreps. Formally, the Schur layer computes φ(T) = Σᵢ MᵢMᵢᵀTWᵢ where Mᵢ spans the i-th eigenspace of L and Wᵢ are learnable channel-mixing matrices. This is embedded within the P-tensor framework for higher-order message passing, where subgraph representations communicate via intersection-based transfer maps and the Schur layer replaces or augments the standard Sₘ-equivariant linmaps. The approach generalizes to k-th order tensors via Kronecker products of eigen-projectors, and the number of channels in the Schur layer naturally corresponds to the number of distinct Laplacian eigenvalues, providing a principled capacity control.

## Results

On ZINC-12K (molecular logP regression), Schur-Net achieves a test MAE of 0.064±0.002, ranking second only to the second-order N²-GNN (0.059±0.002) while using only first-order activations. On OGB-HIV (binary classification), Schur-Net achieves 81.6±0.295% ROC-AUC, outperforming all 13 baselines including the closest competitor CIN (80.94±0.57%). The method also consistently improves over its direct predecessor P-tensors (0.071 MAE on ZINC-12K, 80.76% on OGB-HIV) by replacing full symmetric-group equivariant maps (linmaps) with the more expressive automorphism-group equivariant Schur layers.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Schur-Net outperforms all 13 baselines on OGB-HIV, with the largest margins over classical MPNNs (GCN: +5.53%, GIN: +6.02%) and subgraph-GNNs (DS-GNN: +4.2%, GNN-AK+: +2.0%). On ZINC-12K, Schur-Net (0.064 MAE) is surpassed only by N²-GNN (0.059), which is a second-order model, while Schur-Net uses only first-order activations. Against its direct predecessor P-tensors (which uses the same architecture but with full S_m-equivariant linmaps), Schur-Net improves by 0.007 MAE on ZINC-12K and 0.84% ROC-AUC on OGB-HIV, confirming the benefit of exploiting subgraph automorphism structure. Notably, Autobahn—the only other method explicitly using automorphism-group equivariance—underperforms Schur-Net by 0.042 MAE on ZINC-12K and 3.6% on OGB-HIV, which the authors attribute to Autobahn's limited subgraph coverage and lack of a general inter-subgraph message passing framework. Among higher-order MPNNs, Schur-Net beats CIN (0.079→0.064 MAE) and SUN (0.084→0.064 MAE) by substantial margins on ZINC-12K.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the P-tensors framework [21] for higher-order message passing between subgraphs and on Autobahn [37], which first introduced equivariance to subgraph automorphism groups. The key departure is replacing the group-theoretic machinery (enumerating automorphism groups, computing irreducible representations, constructing equivariant maps via representation theory) with a purely spectral construction based on the eigendecomposition of the subgraph's combinatorial Laplacian. This yields the 'Schur layer,' a practical equivariant operation that works for any subgraph template without hand-crafting group-specific convolutions, while remaining a special case of the full group-theoretic approach.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The main practical impact is that automorphism-equivariant operations become trivially implementable for arbitrary subgraph templates (cycles with branches, stars, paths, etc.), removing the primary barrier that kept Autobahn-style architectures from being widely adopted. However, the spectral approach is provably not the finest possible decomposition: for subgraphs with small automorphism groups (e.g., branched cycles), the number of equivariant maps captured by the Laplacian eigenspaces is strictly smaller than the group-theoretic count (7 vs. 20 for a 5-cycle with one branch). The gap between the spectral and full group-theoretic approaches in higher-order settings remains an open theoretical question, and the paper's experiments are limited to first-order activations on molecular benchmarks, leaving the broader potential of higher-order Schur layers unexplored.
