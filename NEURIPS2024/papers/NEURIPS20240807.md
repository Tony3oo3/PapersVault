## Problem

State-of-the-art video text spotting (VTS) models suffer from a significant recognition bottleneck: their image-level spotting F1-scores lag far behind both their own detection performance and equivalent image text spotting (ITS) models, especially on curved or arbitrary-shaped text. The paper attributes this to two factors—reliance on RoI-based recognition and optimization conflicts in end-to-end multi-task training—and notes that existing video datasets lack curved text, further constraining recognition diversity.

## Input / Output

GoMatching consumes RGB video frames processed independently by a frozen DeepSolo spotter at shorter-side resolutions of 800–1440 pixels (scale-and-crop augmentation at 1280 during training). During inference, a memory bank stores trajectories from H history frames (H = T−1, with T = 6 optimal) for long-term association, and instances with fusion scores above 0.3 pass a threshold before entering the matcher. Outputs are text bounding boxes (quadrilaterals for straight text, 14-point polygons for curved text), recognized text content, and persistent trajectory IDs linking instances across frames, with an association score threshold of 0.2 for trajectory assignment.

## Method

GoMatching freezes the entire DeepSolo image text spotter and trains only two lightweight modules: a rescoring head (a single linear layer initialized from DeepSolo's classification head) that recalibrates per-query confidence via a max-fusion with the original score to bridge the image–video domain gap, and an LST-Matcher tracker. LST-Matcher comprises two structurally identical sub-modules—ST-Matcher (adjacent-frame association) and LT-Matcher (long-term memory-bank association)—each built from a one-layer Transformer encoder and one-layer Transformer decoder that operate on MLP-projected frozen query embeddings rather than RoI features. During inference, ST-Matcher first links current-frame instances to previous-frame trajectories, then LT-Matcher resolves remaining unmatched instances (e.g., after occlusion or motion blur) against the full history, and instances exceeding the score threshold are assigned to the highest-scoring trajectory or spawn new ones. Training uses focal loss for the rescoring head (with Hungarian matching) and log-likelihood association losses for both matchers, optimized for 30k iterations on a single RTX 3090 with only ~7.5M trainable parameters versus ~39.6M for TransDETR.

## Results

On ICDAR15-video, GoMatching (size:1000) achieves 72.04 MOTA, 78.53 MOTP, and 80.11 IDF1, ranking first on all three metrics and surpassing the previous best single-model method TransDETR by 11.08% MOTA. On the bilingual BOVText dataset, GoMatching reaches 52.9 MOTA, 87.2 MOTP, and 62.6 IDF1, a 41.5% MOTA improvement over the prior SOTA CoText. On DSText (dense/small text), GoMatching attains 22.83 MOTA as a single model, outperforming the best single-model baseline TransDETR by 45.46% MOTA. On the newly proposed ArTVideo (arbitrary-shaped text), GoMatching achieves 68.8 MOTA in end-to-end spotting versus TransDETR's 2.8 MOTA, with the gap widening to 123.5 MOTA on curved-text-only spotting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GoMatching decisively outperforms TransDETR [12], the prior SOTA single-model VTS method, across all four datasets: +11.08 MOTA on ICDAR15-video (72.04 vs 60.96), +45.46 MOTA on DSText (22.83 vs −22.63), and +66.0 MOTA on ArTVideo end-to-end spotting (68.8 vs 2.8). On BOVText, GoMatching surpasses CoText [11] by 41.5% MOTA (52.9 vs 11.4) and 14.3% IDF1 (62.6 vs 48.3), demonstrating strong bilingual (Chinese + English) capability. Compared to FREE [9], GoMatching gains 19.06 MOTA on ICDAR15-video, reflecting the advantage of a modern RoI-free query-based spotter over the older Mask R-CNN-based pipeline. On BOVText, GoMatching also far exceeds the earlier baselines from Wu et al. [10] (TransVTSpotter at −1.4 MOTA, DB+CRNN at −13.2 MOTA), confirming that the frozen DeepSolo backbone plus LST-Matcher substantially closes the recognition gap that plagued prior VTS methods. Notably, GoMatching achieves these gains while training only 75.38M parameters (vs. 39.58M for TransDETR) and saving 197 GPU hours, since the image text spotter remains frozen.

## Relation to Prior Work

GoMatching departs from the dominant end-to-end paradigm in video text spotting (TransDETR, CoText) by adopting a tracking-by-detection strategy: it freezes a state-of-the-art RoI-free image text spotter (DeepSolo) and trains only a lightweight rescoring head and a Transformer-based tracker. The tracker's architecture and log-likelihood association loss are directly inherited from GTR (Global Tracking Transformers), repurposed here for text instance queries rather than generic object features. Conceptually, the paper positions itself at the intersection of image text spotting (where query-based DETR-style architectures have matured) and multi-object tracking (where optimization conflicts between detection and association are well-documented), arguing that the two communities' insights can be combined without joint multi-task training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies text recognition—not tracking—as the primary bottleneck in current VTS models, a diagnosis supported by the large spotting-vs-detection F1 gap in TransDETR. The finding that freezing a strong image spotter and training only the tracker outperforms end-to-end joint optimization (even at very low decoder learning rates) challenges the prevailing assumption that multi-task end-to-end training is always beneficial, and offers a practical recipe for adapting powerful ITS models to video with minimal compute. Open questions include whether this paradigm generalizes to weaker or differently-architected image spotters, whether larger and more diverse video text datasets would further close the remaining gap, and how the approach scales to real-time deployment on edge devices. The ArTVideo benchmark also opens a new evaluation axis (curved/arbitrary-shaped text in video) that prior datasets entirely lack.
