## Problem

Standard RL theory for continuous state-action spaces yields slow convergence rates: 1/√n value gap decay in the offline setting and √T cumulative regret in the online setting. This paper asks under what structural conditions on the MDP these rates can be dramatically improved, and identifies two stability properties—naturally satisfied in many continuous control problems—that enable fast rates of 1/n and log T respectively.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242278/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

In the offline setting, the method consumes a pre-collected dataset of (state, action, next_state, reward) tuples along with a d-dimensional linear feature map φ: S×A → R^d; in the online setting, it consumes sequential environment interactions over T episodes. The output is a greedy policy π̂ and Q-function estimate f̂, with the key theoretical deliverable being a value sub-optimality bound that scales quadratically (ε²) with the Bellman residual norm rather than linearly (ε). All analysis is conducted under L² norms induced by the optimal policy's state-action occupation measure, and the function class must satisfy a Bellman-completeness condition (T_h^π F ⊆ F).

## Method

The core contribution is a theoretical framework built on two Lipschitz-type stability conditions: (1) Bellman stability, requiring the Bellman optimality operator to be contractive in the occupation-measure L² norm, and (2) occupation measure stability, requiring that small policy perturbations induce proportionally small changes in multi-step occupation measures. The proof refines the standard telescope bound by handling the difference of expectations (E_{π*} − E_{π̂}) directly rather than nullifying one term via pessimism or optimism; under the stability conditions, the occupation measure discrepancy is of the same order as the Bellman residual, so their product in the telescope expansion yields a quadratic (ε²) bound on value sub-optimality. For linear function approximation with ridge regression (FQI), this translates to a sample complexity of n_fast(ε) ≍ d^{3/2}H³/ε + d²H³ offline (versus d²H³/ε² for pessimistic methods) and a two-phase online scheme achieving O(log T) regret. The framework further reveals that neither pessimism nor optimism is strictly necessary when stability holds and a sufficiently accurate pilot estimate is available.

## Results

Under the proposed stability conditions (Stb(T) and Stb(ξ)), Theorem 1 establishes that value sub-optimality scales quadratically in the Bellman residual ε, yielding a value gap bound of cH³ε² rather than the conventional linear cHε. For off-line FQI with d-dimensional linear function approximation (Corollary 1), this translates to a value gap of c·d^(3/2)·H³·n⁻¹·log(dH/δ) and a sample complexity of n_fast(ε) ≍ d^(3/2)H³/ε + d²H³. For on-line RL (Corollary 2), the two-phase FQI scheme achieves cumulative regret growing as O(d√d·H⁴·log T) rather than the standard O(√T). Empirically, FQI on the Mountain Car problem confirms the fast rate, with a log-log slope of approximately −1.0 (95% CI: (−1.084, −0.905)) versus the typical −0.5 slow rate.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is with the pessimistic actor-critic analysis of Zanette et al. [37], which establishes a sample complexity of n_Zan(ε) ≍ d²H³/ε² for off-line RL with linear function approximation. Under the paper's stability conditions, the sample complexity improves to n_fast(ε) ≍ d^(3/2)H³/ε + d²H³, which is strictly better for small target error ε ∈ (0,1) due to the 1/ε versus 1/ε² dependence. The paper notes that the lower bounds of 1/ε² established in prior work (e.g., [21, 37]) do not contradict this fast rate, because the hard instances used in those lower bound proofs violate the occupation measure stability condition (Stb(ξ))—even infinitesimally small policy perturbations lead to significantly different occupation measures. For on-line RL, the log T regret bound contrasts with the √T rate shown to be unimprovable in general by Jin et al. [18, 20], though again the worst-case instances achieving √T regret violate the stability assumptions. The Mountain Car experiment provides empirical validation, showing FQI achieves a value sub-optimality decay slope of approximately −1.0 (significantly faster than the −0.5 slow rate) across sample sizes from ~36K to ~442K.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the standard pessimism/optimism framework that underlies most offline and online RL analyses (e.g., Jin et al. [21], Zanette et al. [37], Jin et al. [18,20]) by showing that under two natural stability conditions—Bellman operator stability and occupation measure stability—the telescope bound can be exploited directly to yield quadratic (rather than linear) dependence on Bellman residuals. Conceptually, it draws an analogy to fast rates in stochastic optimization (exp-concave losses, strict saddle geometry) but develops a new stability framework specific to value-based RL in continuous spaces. It also connects to the LQR literature [24,29] where value sub-optimality relates to squared system identification error, and to policy iteration convergence analysis [26,3] where smoothness of transition operators yields superlinear convergence, but extends these ideas to the stochastic, finite-sample setting with function approximation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled explanation for when fast rates (1/n offline, log T online) are achievable in continuous RL, and identifies stability conditions that are naturally satisfied in many practical control problems (robotics, clinical dosing, plasma control). It challenges the near-universal assumption that pessimism or optimism is necessary for provable guarantees, showing that a sufficiently accurate pilot estimate suffices under stability. Open questions include: lower bounds for the class of stable MDPs, extensions to non-parametric function approximation (kernels, neural networks), handling model mis-specification, and characterizing covariate shift in LQR. The empirical validation is limited to a single benchmark (Mountain Car) with one method (FQI + linear features), so the practical scope of the stability conditions remains to be established more broadly.
