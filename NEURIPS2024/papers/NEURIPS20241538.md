## Problem

Existing 3D scene editing methods either modify the entire scene globally (e.g., Instruct-NeRF2NeRF), cannot remove objects, struggle to synthesize complex texture patterns, or require manual object placement and blending. No prior method can seamlessly replace or add objects in 3D scenes using only text prompts while preserving multi-view consistency, realistic lighting/shadows, and scene coherence across both forward-facing and 360° scenes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241538/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a collection of multi-view scene images (downsampled to 512px shortest side), their camera poses, and two text prompts (one describing the object to erase, one describing the replacement). Masks are obtained via LangSAM and dilated to form halo regions; the LDM denoiser requires 512×512 square crops of the rendered output. The output is a set of multi-view consistent edited images that are then used to reconstruct a full 3D scene via off-the-shelf NeRF or 3D Gaussian Splatting, enabling novel view synthesis of the edited scene.

## Method

RAM3D adopts an Erase-and-Replace strategy using a compositional scene representation with two separate NeRF fields (θ_bg for background, θ_fg for foreground) confined to a localized 'Bubble-NeRF' region (the inpainting mask plus a dilated halo), rather than modeling the entire scene. Both stages distill a frozen text-to-image inpainting LDM via the HiFA loss (Eq. 1), which computes gradients explicitly in latent space. The Erase stage optimizes θ_bg with additional reconstruction, VGG perceptual, and depth losses on the halo region to accurately inpaint the occluded background; the Replace stage optimizes θ_fg conditioned on the replacement prompt, composites it onto the background via alpha blending, and applies random background augmentation every k-th step to enforce foreground/background disentanglement. This avoids the need to modify existing scene geometry or appearance, and the final edited images are reconstructed into a full 3D representation for novel view rendering.

## Results

On the GARDEN (360°) and FACE (avatar) scenes, RAM3D achieves higher CLIP Text-Image Direction Similarity than GaussianEditor on 6 of 7 prompts (e.g., Pineapple: 0.2246 vs. −0.0631; Popcorn: 0.2024 vs. 0.0400) and lower optical-flow warping error across all prompts (e.g., 1.20×10⁻² vs. 1.39×10⁻² for Mushroom). On the FERN (forward-facing) scene, RAM3D outperforms BlendedNeRF on both CLIP similarity (Strawberry: 0.3165 vs. 0.2224) and warping error (2.38×10⁻² vs. 3.23×10⁻²). A user study with 15 participants confirms RAM3D is preferred for both prompt matching and visual quality across all tested scenes and prompts.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GaussianEditor [35], RAM3D wins on 6 of 7 CLIP similarity scores and all 7 warping-error scores, though GaussianEditor edges ahead on the Chess prompt (0.1857 vs. 0.0874); qualitatively, RAM3D produces more realistic objects with correct lighting and geometry (e.g., shirt collars, jacket lapels) while GaussianEditor leaves visible artifacts at object–scene boundaries and requires manual depth adjustment. Against BlendedNeRF [50], RAM3D achieves higher CLIP similarity and lower warping error on both FERN prompts, and generates objects at 2× higher resolution (1008×756 vs. 504×378) with better scene integration. Against Instruct-NeRF2NeRF [3], RAM3D scores substantially higher CLIP Text-Image Direction Similarity (e.g., Pineapple: 0.2041 vs. 0.0661; Chess: 0.1200 vs. 0.0061) and is the only method that can fully remove objects; IN2N sometimes scores marginally higher on CLIP Direction Consistency but this is attributed to its failure mode of flattening backgrounds. Against the three InstructPix2Pix-based follow-ups (CSD [32], ViCA-NeRF [31], EfficientNeRF2NeRF [30]), RAM3D produces clearly superior texture detail (checkered and tartan patterns) while all three baselines fail to synthesize intricate patterns. Against RePaint-NeRF [34], RAM3D generates more complete object shapes without the boundary-clipping artifact caused by RePaint-NeRF's whole-scene diffusion guidance. Against DreamEditor [33], RAM3D is competitive on object addition but avoids DreamEditor's reliance on object-centric NeuS meshes, which limits it to bounded scenes and makes topology-changing edits (e.g., removal) infeasible. Against the single-image reference-guided method [28], RAM3D achieves comparable replacement quality on forward-facing scenes while additionally handling 360° scenes and complex inter-object shadows.

## Relation to Prior Work

RAM3D builds on the HiFA distillation framework for text-to-3D generation and NeRF-based scene representations, but departs from both by conditioning the distillation on a text-to-image inpainting LDM rather than a text-to-image model, and by restricting optimization to a localized 'Bubble-NeRF' region rather than the full scene. Conceptually, it positions 3D object replacement as a 3D scene inpainting problem, contrasting with Instruct-NeRF2NeRF-style methods that modify the entire scene via InstructPix2Pix, and with GaussianEditor which generates replacement objects in isolation and manually places them. The compositional foreground/background decomposition with random background augmentation is the key architectural departure from monolithic scene-editing approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RAM3D provides a practical, text-only pipeline for 3D object replacement that works across forward-facing, 360°, and human-avatar scenes without manual 3D modeling, which is directly relevant to VR/MR content creation and film production. Key limitations include the Janus multi-face problem inherited from diffusion distillation, incoherent compositions for multi-object prompts, and degraded quality when erased and replacement objects differ significantly in size. Open questions include extending the Bubble-NeRF formulation to 3D Gaussian splats, disentangling geometry from appearance for finer control, and developing amortized models to reduce the ~12-hour training time per edit.
