## Problem

Existing 3D face texture reconstruction methods assume single, uniform environmental illumination, causing shadows from external occlusions (hats, hair) to be baked directly into the recovered texture. This paper addresses the gap of recovering clean, accurate facial textures from images or videos where illumination is distorted by such external occlusions, without requiring physical knowledge of the occluding objects.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243227/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB face image (256×256 or 512×512) or a short video sequence (8 sampled frames) under occlusion-affected illumination as input. It produces a 3D face UV texture map with diffuse, specular, and roughness albedos (initialized from AlbedoMM and refined), 3DMM shape/expression/pose coefficients, n separate Spherical Harmonics light condition coefficients, spatial-temporal light condition masks, and a continuous face region mask. The final rendered output is a per-pixel composition of n ray-traced renders under different light conditions, merged with the original image's background via the face region mask.

## Method

The core insight is to replace the single-illumination assumption with n separate Spherical Harmonics light conditions, each independently optimized to imitate the local illumination in a different facial region affected by external occlusion, rather than physically modeling the occluder. Two spatial-temporal MLP neural representations predict per-pixel light condition assignment masks f(·) and a face region mask g(·) (distilled from a pre-trained face parsing network). An Adaptive Condition Estimation (ACE) strategy prunes redundant light conditions during optimization by thresholding mask areas, with L_area and L_bin regularizations encouraging sparse, binary mask assignments. Rendering uses differentiable ray-tracing with a Blinn-Phong reflectance model. A three-stage optimization pipeline first fits shape/expression/pose, then jointly optimizes all coefficients and neural representations, and finally refines the texture with realistic constraints: a global hue prior via K-means on diffuse albedo, a local smoothness prior matching 5×5 neighborhood variations to the AlbedoMM initialization, and a human prior that maximizes FaceNet recognition confidence on the multi-light rendered faces.

## Results

On single images from VoxCeleb2, the proposed method achieves the best LPIPS (6.40) on source reconstruction and the best PSNR (29.22) and LPIPS (6.36) on target synthesis, substantially outperforming all baselines on the target metric that measures texture transfer quality. On video sequences, the method similarly leads with PSNR 29.15 and LPIPS 6.92 on target synthesis. The method also achieves competitive source reconstruction (PSNR 32.85, SSIM 0.94), slightly trailing NextFace (PSNR 33.41, SSIM 0.95) which over-fits by baking shadows into textures.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method decisively outperforms all five baselines on the target synthesis metric (PSNR 29.22 vs. 23.74–27.23; LPIPS 6.36 vs. 7.19–10.52), confirming that its light-decoupling approach recovers cleaner textures that transfer correctly to unoccluded views. Against NextFace (the strongest baseline on source PSNR/SSIM), the proposed method trades a small source-reconstruction gap (PSNR 32.85 vs. 33.41) for a large target-synthesis gain (PSNR +5.48, LPIPS −4.16), which the authors attribute to NextFace baking external-occlusion shadows into the texture. Compared to FFHQ-UV, the method improves target PSNR by 4.19 dB and LPIPS by 0.83, while also surpassing it on source LPIPS (6.40 vs. 8.32). Against CPEM and D3DFR, which use vertex-color rather than UV-texture representations, the proposed method leads by 4–7 dB in target PSNR. On the diverse-shadow benchmark [41], the method again achieves the best target PSNR (28.97) and LPIPS (7.00), extending its advantage beyond the VoxCeleb2 evaluation set.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the NextFace [12] framework, which combines 3DMM statistical coefficients with differentiable ray-tracing rendering and a single global Spherical Harmonics illumination model, by replacing the single-illumination assumption with multiple decoupled light conditions. The key conceptual departure is treating external-occlusion shadows not as texture artifacts to be removed but as distinct illumination states, predicted spatially via a spatial-temporal MLP and selected adaptively through the ACE strategy. The realistic constraints (K-means global hue prior, local smoothness prior, and FaceNet-based human prior) draw on standard 3DMM and face-recognition tooling but are applied in a multi-light-condition setting that prior ray-tracing-based face work did not require.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical and previously unhandled failure mode in 3D face texture recovery: external occlusions (hats, hair) that create spatially non-uniform illumination, which single-SH methods bake into textures as permanent artifacts. The light-decoupling formulation is conceptually clean and generalizes naturally to video via shared temporal representations. Open questions include whether the AlbedoMM initialization bottleneck can be replaced with non-linear texture decoders (as the authors note), whether the fixed 9-band SH per condition is sufficient for very high-frequency shadow boundaries, and whether the approach extends to more complex multi-occluder scenes beyond the single-hat/hair cases tested.
