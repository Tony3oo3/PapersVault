## Problem

Previous motion quantization methods encode an entire body pose (all joints in one frame) into a single codebook entry, making the quantization task inherently complex and discarding the spatial relationships among individual joints. This limits both the fidelity of the discrete representation and the ability of downstream networks to exploit joint-level spatial structure during generation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20244154/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a free-form text prompt as input, encoded via CLIP into a single text token, and produces a continuous 3D human motion sequence (joint positions, velocities, and rotations over T frames for J=22 or 21 SMPL joints, plus global root information). Internally, the motion is restructured into a 2D joint-time map of shape T×J, where each cell is a discrete token from a codebook of 256 entries (dimension 1024), and generation proceeds by iteratively unmasking this 2D map over N=10 iterations before decoding back to continuous motion.

## Method

The core idea is to quantize each joint independently rather than the whole pose, producing a 2D spatial-temporal token map that enables image-like 2D operations. A joint VQ-VAE with 2D convolutional encoder/decoder (residual blocks, downscale 4) maps the T×J joint-angle grid to 2D vectors and quantizes each vector to its nearest codebook entry, with a residual VQ structure (5 layers) for finer approximation. For generation, a temporal-spatial 2D masking strategy first masks entire frames (all joints together) and then masks individual joints within unmasked frames, using a cosine mask-ratio schedule and BERT-style 80/10/10 remasking. The prediction transformer applies three sequential attention mechanisms: a full spatial-temporal 2D attention over the flattened T×J+1 token sequence (with 2D sinusoidal positional encoding), a joint spatial attention that attends only across joints within each frame, and a joint temporal attention that attends only across frames for each joint, regularizing the complex bidimensional attention. Classifier-free guidance (scale 4) is applied at the final projection layer, and inference starts from a fully masked map, progressively unmasking low-confidence tokens over 10 iterations before the residual transformer refines the remaining layers.

## Results

On HumanML3D, MoGenTS achieves a FID of 0.033±0.001, a 26.6% reduction over the previous SOTA MoMask (0.045), along with the best R-Precision (Top-1: 0.529, Top-3: 0.812) and lowest MM-Dist (2.867). On KIT-ML, the method achieves FID 0.143±0.004, a 29.9% reduction over MoMask (0.204), with Top-1 of 0.445 and MM-Dist of 2.711. For motion quantization, the 2D joint VQ-VAE reduces MPJPE to 13.8 mm on HumanML3D and 17.4 mm on KIT-ML, roughly halving the error of MoMask (29.5 mm and 37.2 mm respectively).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MoGenTS outperforms all 16 baselines on FID, R-Precision (Top-1/2/3), and MM-Dist across both HumanML3D and KIT-ML. The most direct comparison is with MoMask [17], the previous SOTA masked-modeling method: MoGenTS reduces FID by 26.6% (0.045→0.033) on HumanML3D and 29.9% (0.204→0.143) on KIT-ML, while also improving Top-1 by 0.8 and 1.2 points respectively. Against continuous-regression methods like MLD [6] and MotionDiffuse [8], the gap is even larger (FID 0.473 and 0.630 on HumanML3D vs. 0.033). Among other quantization-based methods, DiverseMotion [43] (FID 0.072) and ParCo [42] (FID 0.109) are the closest competitors on HumanML3D, but both are still 2–3× worse in FID. The only metric where MoGenTS does not lead is Diversity: on HumanML3D it scores 9.570, slightly below DiverseMotion (9.683) and ParCo (9.576), and on KIT-ML it scores 10.918, below M2DM (11.417) and MotionDiffuse (11.10), suggesting a mild trade-off between fidelity and sample diversity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the quantization-based text-to-motion generation paradigm (MoMask, T2M-GPT, ParCo) by shifting the unit of quantization from the whole-body pose to individual joints, producing a 2D spatial-temporal token map rather than a 1D token sequence. It inherits the masked-modeling framework from BERT and the progressive unmasking schedule from MaskGIT, and adopts the residual VQ-VAE structure and EMA/codebook-reset techniques from MoMask and T2M-GPT. The key conceptual departure is treating the joint-time grid as analogous to a 2D image, which enables 2D convolutional encoders/decoders, 2D positional encoding, and a tripartite attention scheme (spatial-temporal 2D, joint-spatial, joint-temporal) that has no direct counterpart in prior 1D motion token models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The per-joint quantization substantially reduces approximation error (MPJPE drops from ~29.5 mm to ~13.8 mm on HumanML3D), raising the ceiling for downstream generation quality and suggesting that the granularity of the quantization unit is a more important design choice than previously appreciated. The 2D formulation opens a path to borrowing the rich toolbox of 2D image processing (convolutions, positional encodings, attention patterns) for motion tasks, which could accelerate future work. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger pretraining corpora (the authors note current motion datasets are far smaller than image datasets), whether combining masked and autoregressive generation yields further gains, and whether the tight coupling between 2D VQ and 2D masking/attention (the ablation shows 2D VQ alone *hurts* generation) generalizes to other structured-sequence tasks.
