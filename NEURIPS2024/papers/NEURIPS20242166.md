## Problem

Existing multimodal fusion methods struggle to scale beyond 2–3 modalities, cannot handle arbitrary missing modalities without imputation, and largely ignore the temporal irregularity inherent in real-world data such as clinical records. FuseMoE addresses this gap by proposing a unified, scalable framework that simultaneously accommodates a variable number of modalities, missing data, and irregularly sampled trajectories.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242166/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes heterogeneous inputs—irregularly sampled time series (vitals, labs, sensor signals), free-text clinical notes, medical images (X-rays), ECG waveforms, video frames, and audio—where any subset of modalities may be absent and sampling times differ across dimensions. Missing modalities are replaced with learnable 'missing indicator' embeddings rather than imputed values, and irregularly sampled sequences are discretized into fixed-length bins via multi-time attention before fusion. Outputs are task-specific: binary labels (48-hour mortality, discharge), multilabel phenotype predictions, sentiment polarity/intensity scores, activity class labels, or image class labels.

## Method

FuseMoE encodes each modality through modality-specific feature extractors (pre-trained T5, EfficientNet, BioClinicalBERT, DenseNet-121, or a convolutional autoencoder for ECGs) followed by a discretized multi-time attention (mTAND) module that maps irregularly sampled observations into regularly spaced bins. The core fusion component is a sparse Top-K MoE layer with a novel Laplace gating function that computes expert scores via L2 distance (exp(−‖W−x‖₂)) rather than the standard softmax inner product, yielding provably faster parameter estimation rates (O(n^{−1/4}) for gating and expert parameters versus slower, non-homogeneous rates under softmax gating). Three router designs—joint, per-modality, and disjoint—offer varying degrees of modality separation, and an entropy regularization loss encourages balanced expert utilization while routing missing-modality embeddings toward underused experts. Theoretically, the paper establishes parametric convergence rates (O(√log n / n)) for density estimation and derives Voronoi-based parameter estimation rates for the Laplace gating Gaussian MoE under both exact- and over-specified settings.

## Results

On MIMIC-IV, FuseMoE with Laplace gating achieves the best AUROC across all three clinical tasks: 81.03 on 48-hour in-hospital mortality (48-IHM), 82.91 on length-of-stay (LOS), and 71.23 on 25-type phenotype classification (25-PHE), outperforming the strongest baselines (HAIM at 79.65, 82.58, and 63.39 respectively). On CMU-MOSI, the per-modality router variant achieves the best MAE of 0.65 and F1 of 87.39, surpassing Softmax-MoE (MAE 0.69, F1 87.29) and all non-MoE baselines (TFN, MulT, MAG). On MOSEI, the joint experts & router configuration reaches the best MAE of 0.54 and F1 of 85.43, again exceeding Softmax-MoE (MAE 0.55, F1 84.97) and all other baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FuseMoE with Laplace gating consistently outperforms all non-MoE baselines (TFN, MulT, MAG, MISTS) across all three MIMIC-IV tasks, with the largest margin on 25-PHE AUROC (71.23 vs. 69.55 for MAG, a +1.7 point gain) and 48-IHM AUROC (81.03 vs. 78.76 for TFN, +2.3 points). Against the strongest clinical pipeline baseline HAIM [83], FuseMoE wins on 48-IHM AUROC (81.03 vs. 79.65) and 25-PHE AUROC (71.23 vs. 63.39, a substantial +7.8 point gain), but HAIM retains an advantage on 25-PHE F1 (42.13 vs. 31.33), attributed to XGBoost's handling of missing minority classes. On CMU-MOSI, the per-modality router variant (MAE 0.65, F1 87.39) beats Softmax-MoE (MAE 0.69, F1 87.29) and all cross-attention baselines (MAG: MAE 0.71, F1 86.00; MulT: MAE 0.86, F1 83.90; TFN: MAE 0.90, F1 80.70). On MOSEI, the joint experts & router (MAE 0.54, F1 85.43) edges out Softmax-MoE (MAE 0.55, F1 84.97) and MAG (MAE 0.57, F1 84.50). The Laplace gating also surpasses the Gaussian gating [93] on 48-IHM AUROC (81.03 vs. 80.76) and LOS AUROC (82.91 vs. 81.92), supporting the theoretical convergence advantage.

## Relation to Prior Work

FuseMoE builds on the sparsely-gated MoE architecture of Shazeer et al. [79] and the mTAND irregularity encoder of Shukla & Marlin [82], combining them into a unified multimodal fusion framework. It departs from prior multimodal fusion methods (TFN, MulT, MAG, MISTS) that are limited to 2–3 fixed modalities and lack mechanisms for missingness or variable modality counts, and from prior MoE-for-fusion work (e.g., LIMO [58]) that targets only language-image alignment. The key conceptual departure is the introduction of a Laplace (L2-distance) gating function, for which the authors derive convergence rates that improve upon the Softmax-gating analysis of Nguyen et al. [64], and the 'FlexiModal' framing that unifies arbitrary modality subsets, missingness, and temporal irregularity into a single problem statement.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The FlexiModal formulation directly addresses a persistent gap in clinical ML, where EHR data inherently exhibits variable modality availability, irregular sampling, and high missingness—conditions under which most existing fusion pipelines degrade or require ad-hoc imputation. The theoretical result that Laplace gating yields faster parameter estimation rates (O(n^{-1/4}) for gating and expert parameters vs. rates dependent on polynomial-system solvability under Softmax) provides a principled justification for the design choice beyond empirical tuning. Open questions include whether the mTAND discretization's information loss and over-parameterization on small inputs can be mitigated, whether the convergence-rate advantage translates to meaningful gains at the scale of modern LLMs, and how the framework behaves when modality distributions shift across deployment sites.
