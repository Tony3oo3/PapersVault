## Problem

Reinforcement learning with rich observations (images, text) is common in practice, but the underlying dynamics are often governed by a simpler latent state space. Outside restrictive settings like tabular latent spaces, it is fundamentally unclear what statistical conditions on the latent dynamics are necessary and sufficient for sample-efficient learning, and whether existing RL algorithms with guarantees can be modularly lifted from the latent space to the observation space. Prior theoretical work was tailored to specific latent dynamics classes, had unwieldy analyses, and was not modular in its coupling of representation learning with the RL algorithm.

## Input / Output

The framework takes as input a sequence of rich observations x_h ∈ X (generated from an unknown decodable emission process), a realizable decoder class Φ ⊆ {X → S}, a black-box base RL algorithm ALG_lat designed for the latent MDP, and a representation learning oracle. The output is a near-optimal observation-space policy, constructed as a uniform mixture of per-epoch policies π̂_lat^(t) ∘ φ̂^(t) that compose the latent policy with the learned decoder. A key structural constraint is that the emission process must be decodable (disjoint supports across latent states), ensuring the true decoder exists in Φ, and the base MDP must belong to a specified class M_lat that is statistically tractable when latent states are directly observed.

## Method

The paper introduces the O2L (Observable-to-Latent) meta-algorithm, which alternates between a representation learning oracle that produces a decoder φ̂^(t): X → S and a black-box base RL algorithm that operates on the φ̂^(t)-compressed dynamics, yielding a universal reduction from latent-space RL to observation-space RL. On the statistical side, the paper proves a lower bound showing that most well-studied function approximation settings (Bellman rank, eluder dimension, linear Q*/V*, etc.) do NOT admit statistical modularity—tractability of the base class does not extend to the latent-dynamics setting—even when the base MDP is fully known. The positive statistical result identifies pushforward coverability as a general condition enabling modularity, proven via a structural lemma showing low-dimensional linear embeddings of latent models approximate Bellman updates in observation space. Two algorithmic instantiations bypass the lower bound: (i) under hindsight observability, an online classification oracle (EXPWEIGHTS.DR) achieves O2L risk bounded by base risk plus Õ(H log|Φ|)·K/T; and (ii) in the general setting, an optimistic self-predictive estimation objective (SELFPREDICT.OPT) jointly fits a decoder and latent model, with regret controlled by coverability and a mismatch completeness assumption on the decoder class.

## Results

The paper's central negative result (Theorem 3.1) shows that even when the base MDP is fully known (statistical complexity = 0), learning under latent dynamics requires Ω(N/log N) episodes where N = |Φ| is the decoder class size, proving that most well-studied function approximation settings (Bellman rank, eluder dimension, linear Q*/V*, etc.) are intractable under latent dynamics. On the positive side, pushforward coverability (Theorem 3.2) yields a sample complexity of poly(C_push, |A|, H, log|M_lat|, log|Φ|, ε^{-1}, log(δ^{-1}), log log|S|). Under hindsight observability (Corollary 4.1), the O2L reduction achieves ε-risk with Õ(H log|Φ| / ε^5) trajectories; under self-predictive estimation (Corollary A.1), it requires Õ(H^3 C_cov,st |A| (log(|M_lat||L_lat||Φ|))^2 / ε^14) trajectories.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical baselines; comparisons are between MDP classes and their (in)tractability under latent dynamics. The paper shows that all major function approximation settings from the RL literature—Bellman rank [Jin et al. 2021], eluder dimension [Russo & Van Roy 2013], linear Q*/V* [Du et al. 2021], linear mixture MDPs [Zhou et al. 2021], and Q*-irrelevant state abstraction [Dong et al. 2019]—are intractable under latent dynamics, even when the base MDP is known a priori. In contrast, settings with structural assumptions on transitions (tabular, low-rank, pushforward-coverable) remain tractable. The lower bound generalizes and strengthens the result of Song et al. [2024] for continuous latent dynamics. The O2L reduction (Theorem 4.1) subsumes the hindsight-observability framework of Lee et al. [2023] by providing a fully modular reduction that works with any base algorithm, while the self-predictive result (Theorem A.1) extends Schwarzer et al. [2020] with a principled optimism mechanism and end-to-end sample complexity guarantees.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper generalizes the Block MDP framework (Du et al. 2019; Krishnamurthy et al. 2016) and related restricted-settings work (LQR, factored MDPs) to a unified setting where the base latent MDP can itself require function approximation, introducing formal notions of statistical and algorithmic modularity. It builds on hindsight observability (Lee et al. 2023) and self-predictive representations (Schwarzer et al. 2020) to construct the O2L observable-to-latent reduction, and generalizes the lower bound of Song et al. 2024 to show that most standard function-approximation RL settings become intractable under latent dynamics. The positive result leverages pushforward coverability and the GOLF algorithm (Jin et al. 2021) to identify a general condition enabling statistical tractability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The negative result—that Bellman rank, eluder dimension, linear Q*/V*, and other widely-used function approximation conditions do not extend to the latent-dynamics setting even when the base MDP is known—fundamentally reshapes the landscape of provable RL with rich observations and explains why prior approaches required strong structural assumptions. The O2L reduction provides a practical algorithmic template: any sample-efficient base algorithm can be lifted to the observation space given a suitable representation learning oracle. Key open questions include whether standard (non-pushforward) coverability suffices, whether self-predictive estimation can achieve modularity without optimism, and whether computationally efficient implementations of the self-predictive objective exist.
