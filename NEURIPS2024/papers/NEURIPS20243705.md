## Problem

Existing LLM attack methods either lack understanding of internal safety mechanisms (prompt-level attacks like GCG and AutoDAN) or rely on potentially misleading heuristics for embedding-level perturbations (RepE, JRE), resulting in suboptimal attack success rates, low-quality repetitive outputs, and time-consuming grid search over hyperparameters. No principled framework existed to accurately model LLM safety mechanisms in a way that could simultaneously guide both embedding-level and prompt-level attacks with automatic hyperparameter selection.

## Input / Output

The method requires white-box access to LLM parameters and a small set of malicious/safe instruction pairs (as few as 5 pairs suffice, versus 280 for baselines) to train the safety classifier. For embedding-level attacks, it outputs per-layer perturbation vectors (direction and magnitude) computed in closed form; for prompt-level attacks, it outputs a text prompt to prepend to the user instruction. The embedding-level attack operates on intermediate-layer hidden states of dimension d, while the prompt-level attack only requires the final instruction text, making it applicable to black-box APIs like GPT-4.

## Method

SCAV trains a logistic regression classifier on intermediate-layer embeddings to model the probability P_m(e) that the LLM considers an embedding malicious, exploiting the finding that safety concepts become linearly separable from layer 10 onward in aligned LLMs. For embedding-level attacks, it solves a constrained optimization (minimize perturbation magnitude subject to P_m dropping below a threshold) that yields a closed-form solution: perturb along the normalized classifier weight vector w with magnitude determined by the sigmoid inverse of the threshold, automatically selecting only layers where the classifier test accuracy exceeds 90%. For prompt-level attacks, it replaces AutoDAN's heuristic target-response objective with a product-form objective that simultaneously minimizes P_m of the perturbed final-layer embedding and the L2 distance from the original embedding, solved via AutoDAN's hierarchical genetic algorithm. This principled approach eliminates grid search, requires far less training data, and consistently outperforms baselines by 8–20% in ASR while reducing language flaws by 14–42%.

## Results

On embedding-level attacks, SCAV achieves 100% ASR-keyword on both LLaMA-2-7B-Chat and LLaMA-2-13B-Chat across Advbench and StrongREJECT, with ASR-answer of 96–100% and ASR-useful of 92–98%, while reducing language flaws to 0–10% (vs. 18–70% for baselines). On prompt-level attacks, SCAV improves ASR-answer by 20–28% and ASR-useful by 24–30% over the best baseline (GCG/AutoDAN). Across seven open-source LLMs, SCAV achieves an average ASR-keyword of 99.14% and ASR-answer above 85% for all models, demonstrating that current safety alignment is extensively reversible.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SCAV dominates all six baselines on embedding-level attacks. Against RepE (the strongest embedding-level baseline), SCAV improves ASR-keyword by +20/+4 points (7B/13B), ASR-useful by +6/+4, and reduces language flaws by −42/−14 (7B) and −20/−16 (13B). JRE and Soft prompt perform substantially worse, with Soft prompt achieving only 56/64% ASR-keyword on 7B. On prompt-level attacks, SCAV outperforms GCG by +12/+14 ASR-keyword and +30/+24 ASR-useful on 7B/13B, and beats AutoDAN by +30/+36 ASR-useful. The paper also shows SCAV requires only 5 training pairs to reach near-100% ASR-keyword, whereas RepE drops to 0% with 1 pair, highlighting the data efficiency gained from principled linear-classifier guidance over heuristic perturbation directions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the representation engineering line of work (RepE, JRE) that perturbs intermediate-layer embeddings to bypass LLM safety alignment, but replaces their heuristic perturbation directions (PCA on random instruction-pair differences) with a principled linear classifier (logistic regression) trained on the safety concept. It also extends the prompt-level jailbreak literature (GCG, AutoDAN, DeepInception) by using the same linear safety classifier as the optimization objective rather than a fixed target-response logit. The conceptual departure is framing the attack as a constrained optimization with a closed-form solution, unifying embedding-level and prompt-level attacks under a single interpretability-based framework derived from the TCAV literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The near-universal vulnerability (99.14% average ASR across seven open-source LLMs, 84% useful responses from GPT-4) and the demonstrated failure of both unlearning (Eraser) and adversarial-training defenses (circuit breakers) underscore that current safety alignment is far more fragile than assumed, with urgent implications for open-sourcing high-capability models. The clean finding that safety is linearly separable from layer ~10 onward provides a concrete mechanistic target for future defense research. Open questions include understanding when and why embedding-level and prompt-level attacks transfer across architectures, and developing defenses that operate at the representation level rather than the prompt level.
