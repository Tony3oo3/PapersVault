## Problem

Existing transformer-based 3D hand reconstruction methods (METRO, MeshGraphormer, HaMeR) fail to robustly reconstruct hand meshes in challenging in-the-wild scenarios with occlusions, truncation, and hand-object interactions, primarily because attention over all image tokens inefficiently models spatial relations between joints and introduces unnecessary background correlations. Additionally, directly applying Mamba's unidirectional scanning to 3D hand reconstruction yields inaccurate meshes due to the lack of graph-structured joint topology and bidirectional context.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single 256×192×3 RGB hand image and regresses MANO parameters (pose θ∈ℝ⁴⁸, shape β∈ℝ¹⁰, camera translation π∈ℝ³) via an MLP head, which are then passed through the MANO model to produce a 778-vertex 3D hand mesh. A key design constraint is extreme token efficiency: the decoder operates on only 22 tokens (21 joint tokens + 1 global mean token) rather than the 192 image patch tokens used by attention-based methods, achieving an 88.5% token reduction. The ViT-H backbone (630M parameters, 50 layers) is initialized from HaMeR's released checkpoint and kept unfrozen during training.

## Method

Hamba reformulates Mamba's unidirectional scanning into a Graph-guided Bidirectional Scan (GBS) that follows the hand joint skeleton topology, enabling the state space model to learn joint spatial sequence relations with only 22 effective tokens. The architecture consists of: (1) a Joints Regressor (JR) using stacked SS2D blocks to predict initial MANO parameters and 2D joint locations; (2) a Token Sampler (TS) that uses bilinear interpolation at predicted joint positions to extract 21 joint-level tokens from the downsampled feature map; (3) four Graph-guided State Space (GSS) blocks, each combining a PGraphConv layer (with a learnable adjacency matrix over the 21-joint MANO skeleton) with an SS2D selective scan block, where the GCN output is concatenated with a global mean token before entering the state space scan; and (4) a fusion module that integrates GSS tokens, sampled tokens, 2D joint features, and global mean features before the final MLP regression. The GCN captures input-independent local joint relations via graph propagation, while the bidirectional SS2D scan captures long-range spatial sequence dependencies along the joint topology, and the global mean token provides whole-image context. Training uses a combined loss of 2D/3D joint L1 losses, MANO parameter L2 losses, and an adversarial loss on pose and shape, trained on 2.7M samples from 10 datasets.

## Results

On FreiHAND, Hamba achieves a PA-MPVPE of 5.3 mm and F@15mm of 0.992 with TTA (5.5 mm / 0.991 without TTA), outperforming the previous SOTA HaMeR (5.7 mm / 0.990). On HO3Dv2, Hamba reaches PA-MPVPE 7.7 mm and AUC_V 0.846, surpassing HaMeR (7.9 mm / 0.841). On HO3Dv3, Hamba achieves PA-MPVPE 6.8 mm and AUC_V 0.864, a substantial margin over the next best method AMVUR (8.3 mm / 0.834). In in-the-wild evaluation on HInt, Hamba outperforms HaMeR across all three subsets (NewDays, VISOR, Ego4D) for both visible and occluded joints, with the largest gains on occluded joints (e.g., PCK@0.15 of 84.3% vs. 80.7% on VISOR).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Hamba outperforms all 40 compared baselines across every benchmark. Against the closest competitor HaMeR (CVPR 2024), Hamba reduces PA-MPVPE from 5.7 to 5.5 mm on FreiHAND and from 7.9 to 7.7 mm on HO3Dv2, while also improving F@5mm (0.798 vs. 0.785) and AUC_V (0.846 vs. 0.841). On HO3Dv3, Hamba's advantage is most dramatic: PA-MPVPE of 6.8 mm vs. 8.3 mm for AMVUR (CVPR 2023), a 1.5 mm gap. In in-the-wild HInt evaluation, Hamba surpasses HaMeR on all subsets, with the largest relative gains on occluded joints (e.g., PCK@0.15 of 84.3% vs. 80.7% on VISOR), confirming the benefit of graph-guided state-space modeling for handling occlusions. The ablation in Table S1 further shows that replacing the SS2D block with a Transformer attention (GCN+Attention from Graformer) degrades PA-MPVPE from 6.3 to 6.6 mm, validating the state-space design choice. Hamba also uses 88.5% fewer tokens (22 vs. 192) and 83.7% less GPU memory (3.4 GB vs. 20.9 GB) than the GCN+Transformer variant, making it substantially more efficient.

## Relation to Prior Work

Hamba builds on the Mamba state space model (Gu & Dao, 2023) and its visual adaptation VMamba (Liu et al., 2024), which introduced the SS2D block for 2D selective scanning of image patches. It departs from the dominant transformer-based 3D hand reconstruction line (METRO, MeshGraphormer, HaMeR) by replacing attention over all image tokens with a graph-guided bidirectional scan over a small set of joint-level tokens, and integrates Semantic GCN (Zhao et al., 2019) to encode hand skeleton topology within the state space decoder. Unlike prior Graph-Mamba works (Behrouz et al., Wang et al.) that apply SSM to general graph representation learning, Hamba is the first to reformulate Mamba's scanning along a hand-joint spatial sequence specifically for 3D mesh reconstruction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 88.5% token reduction (22 vs. 192 tokens in the decoder) while simultaneously improving SOTA accuracy offers a practical efficiency path for 3D hand reconstruction, and the GSS block's demonstrated transferability to 3D human mesh recovery suggests broader applicability as a plug-and-play decoder component. However, the method remains limited to single-frame reconstruction without temporal modeling, and the authors acknowledge that even 2.7M training samples may not cover all in-the-wild conditions. Open questions include whether the graph-guided SSM paradigm extends naturally to multi-hand or hand-object interaction settings, and whether the token-efficiency advantage persists at higher resolutions or with more complex articulated structures.
