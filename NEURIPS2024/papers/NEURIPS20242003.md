## Problem

Existing spike-guided motion deblurring methods rely on supervised training with synthetic blurry-sharp-spike triplets, causing severe performance degradation (color distortion, brightness inconsistency, texture loss) when deployed on real-world data due to domain gaps in spike density, generation mechanism, and blur formation. This paper addresses the gap by proposing the first self-supervised framework for spike-guided deblurring that requires no ground-truth sharp sequences for training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242003/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single high-spatial/low-temporal blurry RGB image (H×W×3) and a low-spatial/high-temporal binary spike stream (K×H/4×W/4, where K is the number of spike frames within the exposure) as input, and outputs a sequence of K full-resolution sharp RGB images. The spike camera operates at approximately one-quarter the spatial resolution of the RGB camera, and the spike stream is centered around a target moment t within the exposure window T. The framework is designed to be fine-tuned directly on real-world data without any paired ground-truth sequences.

## Method

The core contribution is a three-stage self-supervised pipeline: (1) a theoretical Spike-guided Deblurring Model (SDM) derived from the spike camera's threshold-firing mechanism, which expresses each sharp frame as the blurry image scaled by the ratio of short-exposure to full-exposure spike accumulation (Eq. 8); (2) a cascaded processing pipeline that applies a Blind Spot Network to denoise the short-exposure spike accumulation and a frozen EDSR super-resolution network to upsample the spike frame to match RGB resolution before SDM fusion; (3) a knowledge distillation stage where this pipeline acts as a teacher generating pseudo-labels for a lightweight student network (LDN) that fuses the blurry image and raw spike stream via Concat + CBAM + ResBlocks. The LDN is trained with a combined LPIPS teacher loss and a reblur loss (MSE between the temporal average of reconstructed frames and the original blurry input), which enforces motion consistency and brightness fidelity without requiring ground truth.

## Results

On the GOPRO dataset, S-SDM achieves PSNR 26.893 / SSIM 0.757 at V_th=1, 26.367 / 0.740 at V_th=2, and 25.433 / 0.699 at V_th=4, maintaining consistent performance across spike density levels. In contrast, the strongest supervised spike-based baseline SpkDeblurNet drops from 28.307 PSNR at V_th=1 to 11.621 at V_th=4. On the real-world RSB dataset, S-SDM achieves an average LIQE score of 2.1987, outperforming SpkDeblurNet (1.8648) and all non-spike methods (≤1.1367). The lightweight LDN also surpasses the SAN architecture from GEM in both PSNR (27.928 vs. 27.283) and efficiency (0.23M vs. 2.36M parameters, 33.6G vs. 107.8G FLOPs).

## Baselines & Comparisons

S-SDM is the only method that generalizes across spike density levels: while SpkDeblurNet [8] leads at V_th=1 (28.307 PSNR) but collapses to 11.621 at V_th=4, S-SDM degrades gracefully from 26.893 to 25.433. Among event-based methods adapted to spike streams, REFID+DASR [25] and TRMD+DASR [4] also suffer severe degradation (REFID drops from 28.124 to 13.623 PSNR), whereas the self-supervised RED+DASR [29] is more stable but still trails S-SDM by 3.5–4.5 dB at V_th=2 and 4. On the real-world RSB dataset, S-SDM achieves an average LIQE of 2.1987, beating SpkDeblurNet (1.8648) by 0.33 and all non-spike baselines (LEVS 1.0433, Motion-ETR 1.0449, BiT 1.1367) by over 1.0 point. The proposed LDN outperforms the SAN architecture from GEM [39] in both quality (27.928 vs. 27.283 PSNR) and efficiency (0.23M vs. 2.36M params; 33.6G vs. 107.8G FLOPs), confirming that the self-supervised pseudo-label quality, not architectural complexity, drives restoration performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the spike-guided motion deblurring line initiated by SpkDeblurNet [8] and the broader event-based deblurring literature (TRMD, RED, REFID), but departs fundamentally by replacing the supervised training paradigm with a self-supervised cascaded pipeline. The theoretical derivation of the Spike-guided Deblurring Model (SDM, Eq. 8) formalizes the mathematical link between spike accumulation, blurry exposure, and latent sharp frames—a relationship that prior learning-based spike/event deblurring methods treated implicitly. The self-supervised framework repurposes Blind Spot Networks for spike denoising and EDSR for cross-modal super-resolution, then distills the resulting teacher pipeline into a lightweight student (LDN) trained with LPIPS and reblur losses, drawing on the self-supervised event deblurring ideas in RED [29] and GEM [39].

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The synthetic-to-real domain gap is a critical bottleneck for deploying neuromorphic-camera vision systems, and this paper demonstrates that self-supervision can bridge it without requiring expensive high-speed ground-truth cameras. The RSB dataset is the first spatially-temporally calibrated real-world spike-blur benchmark, providing the community a much-needed evaluation resource. However, the method's dependence on strict spatial-temporal calibration limits its plug-and-play applicability, and the small RSB dataset (10 sequences) leaves open questions about robustness to more complex motion patterns, occlusions, and varying scene dynamics. Future work could explore calibration-free alignment, extension to multi-frame spike sequences, and integration with real-time neuromorphic pipelines.
