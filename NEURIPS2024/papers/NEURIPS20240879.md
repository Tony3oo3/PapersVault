## Problem

Existing motion comprehension and generation methods handle only a single control modality (text or music) and treat understanding and synthesis as separate problems. No prior framework unifies multiple motion-relevant modalities—text, music, and 3D motion/dance—within a single model capable of bidirectional comprehension and generation across all of them.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240879/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Inputs are consumed as discrete token sequences within a shared vocabulary: text is tokenized normally, 3D motion (22-joint SMPL/SMPL-X poses at 30 fps) is compressed by a VQ-VAE with a 512-entry codebook and 4× temporal downsampling, and music (22050 Hz audio) is segmented into 5-second clips and encoded by the pre-trained Jukebox VQ-VAE (2048-entry codebook, 128× downsampling). Outputs are decoded back to continuous space by the corresponding de-tokenizers, yielding 3D pose sequences, natural-language captions, or music waveforms depending on the task instruction.

## Method

M³GPT builds a unified autoregressive framework on a T5 backbone whose vocabulary is expanded with motion and music codebook indices, so all modalities share a single token space. Two key training innovations distinguish it from prior motion-LLM work: (1) joint optimization of the LLM and the motion de-tokenizer, which backpropagates L1 reconstruction error from raw motion space into the LLM, forcing it to capture fine-grained motion details that discrete-token cross-entropy alone cannot; and (2) synergy learning that uses text as a semantic bridge—two auxiliary tasks (music-to-text and text-to-dance) implicitly decompose the hard music-to-dance problem into simpler subtasks, while a shared motion/dance tokenizer lets text-to-motion and text-to-dance mutually reinforce each other. Training proceeds in three stages: multimodal tokenizer pre-training, modality-alignment pre-training over a mixture of eight comprehension/generation tasks, and GPT-4-templated instruction fine-tuning across eleven task formats.

## Results

On the Motion-X dataset, M³GPT (instruction-tuned) achieves competitive performance across all four evaluated tasks: text-to-motion (R-TOP1 0.615, FID 0.093, Div 2.253), motion-to-text (R-TOP3 0.845, CIDEr 42.93), motion prediction (FID 0.682, Div 1.838), and motion in-between (FID 0.612, Div 1.900). When instruction-tuned solely on text-to-motion, M³GPT reaches R-TOP1 0.661 and FID 0.076, surpassing the single-task baseline (0.656, 0.078). On AIST++, M³GPT achieves the best FID_k of 24.34 for music-to-dance and the best BHS/F1 of 94.0/94.9 for dance-to-music, outperforming all prior methods on these metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On text-to-motion (Motion-X), M³GPT (instruction-tuned only T2M) achieves R-TOP1 0.661 and FID 0.076, slightly trailing MoMask (0.668, 0.074) but surpassing MotionGPT (0.659, 0.078) and MotionDiffuse (0.659, 0.075) on R-TOP1. On motion-to-text, M³GPT substantially outperforms all baselines on CIDEr (42.93 vs. MotionGPT's 31.36 and TM2T's 20.16), while matching MotionGPT on R-TOP3 (0.845 vs. 0.840). For motion prediction and in-between, M³GPT (instruction-tuned) achieves the best FID (0.682 and 0.612) and diversity (1.838 and 1.900), beating MotionGPT (0.701/0.648 FID) and MDM (1.028/0.831 FID) by a clear margin. On music-to-dance (AIST++), M³GPT achieves the best FID_k of 24.34, outperforming Bailando (28.16), EDGE (42.16), and FACT (35.35), though Lodge reports a higher BAS (0.2423 vs. 0.2056). On dance-to-music (AIST++), M³GPT achieves the best BHS (94.0) and F1 (94.9), surpassing LORIS (90.8/94.5), D2MGAN (88.7/93.1), and CDCD (89.3/92.7). Overall, M³GPT is the only method that handles all six core tasks in a single framework, and its multitask pre-training consistently improves individual task performance over single-task training across both GPU and NPU platforms.

## Relation to Prior Work

M³GPT extends the MotionGPT line of work, which represented 3D motion as discrete tokens within a T5-based LLM, by adding a music modality (via Jukebox's pre-trained VQ-VAE) and unifying six core tasks—text-to-motion, motion-to-text, music-to-dance, dance-to-music, motion prediction, and motion in-between—into a single autoregressive framework. It departs from prior unified motion frameworks (TM2D, UDE, MotionGPT variants) that either lacked motion comprehension or could not handle music, and introduces two key architectural choices: jointly optimizing the LLM with the motion de-tokenizer to backpropagate raw-space reconstruction errors, and using text as a semantic bridge with auxiliary tasks (music-to-text, text-to-dance) to establish cross-task synergies. The shared motion/dance tokenizer and the instruction-tuning pipeline built with GPT-4-generated templates further distinguish it from earlier single-modality or two-task approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

M³GPT demonstrates that a single LLM-based architecture can handle bidirectional comprehension and generation across text, motion, and music, with zero-shot generalization to previously unseen compositions (long-term dance, music+text-conditioned dance) that prior single-task or two-task systems cannot address. The synergy-learning finding—that text-bridged auxiliary tasks overcome the typical multitask interference and improve both text-to-motion and music-to-dance simultaneously—offers a practical recipe for integrating unfamiliar modalities into LLMs. Key limitations include the exclusion of hands and facial details, reliance on a relatively small T5-base backbone, and the fact that music-to-dance performance on FineDance still trails the specialized LODGE method, suggesting that unified frameworks may not yet match task-specific architectures on every axis.
