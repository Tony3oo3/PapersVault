## Problem

Existing convergence guarantees for stochastic gradient descent in performative prediction (where the data distribution depends on the deployed model) are restricted to strongly convex losses, leaving the far more common non-convex setting (e.g., neural network training) without theoretical analysis. The standard performative stable (PS) solution concept breaks down for non-convex losses because the argmin map defining PS may be multi-valued or ill-defined, and the mean field of SGD-GD is not a gradient of any function.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes stochastic samples (x, y) drawn from a decision-dependent distribution D(θ_t) at each iteration, where the distribution itself shifts as the model parameters θ_t evolve. The sensitivity of this shift is characterized by a parameter ε under either Wasserstein-1 or total-variation distance. The output is a model parameter vector θ_T whose quality is measured by the SPS stationarity criterion ‖∇J(θ_T; θ_T)‖², which differs from the standard gradient norm ‖∇V(θ)‖² because ∇J(θ;θ) is only a partial gradient of the performative risk V(θ).

## Method

The paper introduces the stationary performative stable (SPS) solution, defined by ‖∇J(θ;θ)‖² ≤ δ, as a non-convex relaxation of the PS fixed-point condition. The key analytical tool is a time-varying Lyapunov function J_t(θ) := J(θ; θ_t) that captures the descent of the decoupled performative risk while accounting for the distribution shift between consecutive iterates. Under either (i) W1 sensitivity with Lipschitz loss or (ii) TV sensitivity with bounded loss, the paper proves SGD-GD converges at rate O(T^{-1/2}) to an O(ε)-SPS solution, with the bias level depending on the stochastic gradient variance. A lazy deployment variant (deploying the model once every K SGD steps) is shown to reduce the asymptotic bias to O(ε²) as K grows, since each epoch approximately solves the inner non-convex subproblem min_θ J(θ; θ_t).

## Results

The paper establishes the first convergence guarantee for SGD-GD in performative prediction with smooth non-convex loss: SGD-GD converges at rate O(T^{-1/2}) in expectation to an O(ε)-SPS solution, where ε is the distribution sensitivity parameter (Theorem 1, Corollary 1). The asymptotic bias is O(σ₀ε + (1+σ₁²)ε²), which reduces to O(ε²) when the gradient is exact (σ₀=σ₁=0). The lazy deployment scheme (Theorem 2) achieves the same O(T^{-1/2}) rate but with a reduced bias of O((L̃ε)²) as the epoch length K→∞. Numerical experiments on synthetic linear models and a neural network on Spambase confirm the predicted O(ε) bias scaling and the bias reduction from lazy deployment.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This work is the first to handle non-convex loss (Ncvx-ℓ) with a stochastic first-order method in performative prediction, a gap not addressed by any prior baseline. Compared to Mendler-Dünner et al. (2020), which achieves O(T^{-1}) convergence to an exact PS solution, this work trades the faster rate for O(T^{-1/2}) but removes the strong convexity requirement on ℓ(θ;z), enabling analysis of neural network training. Against Mofakhami et al. (2023), which achieves a linear rate to a PS solution, this work requires only smoothness (not the restrictive ℓ(θ;z)=ℓ̃(f_θ(x);y) form with strongly convex ℓ̃) and uses SGD rather than exact repeated risk minimization, at the cost of a slower O(T^{-1/2}) rate and convergence to a biased SPS rather than exact PS. Versus the two-phase methods of Izzo et al. (2021) and Miller et al. (2021), which require learning D(θ) separately and assume non-convex V(θ), this work uses only the partial gradient ∇J(θ;θ) available from a single sample, avoiding the need for distribution knowledge. The lazy deployment scheme further improves the bias from O(ε) to O(ε²) as K grows, outperforming the greedy scheme in asymptotic stationarity quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the performative prediction framework of Perdomo et al. (2020) and the SGD-GD convergence analysis of Mendler-Dünner et al. (2020) from strongly convex losses to smooth but possibly non-convex losses, which is the more common regime in practice (e.g., neural network training). The key technical departure is the introduction of the stationary performative stable (SPS) solution as a relaxation of the performative stable (PS) fixed-point condition, which is necessary because the argmin map defining PS is no longer well-defined under non-convexity. The analysis draws on the non-gradient stochastic approximation framework of Dieuleveut et al. (2023) and the Lipschitz continuity results for J(θ;·) from Drusvyatskiy and Xiao (2023), but introduces a time-varying Lyapunov function J_t(θ) := J(θ; θ_t) to handle the dynamic distribution shifts inherent in the greedy deployment scheme.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This provides the first convergence guarantee for first-order stochastic methods in performative prediction with non-convex loss, closing a significant gap between theory and the neural-network-scale models that motivate the performative prediction setting. The finding that stochastic gradient noise introduces an O(ε) bias (versus O(ε²) for exact gradients) reveals a unique sensitivity of non-convex performative prediction to minibatch size that has no analogue in standard non-convex optimization. Open questions include whether SPS solutions are unique, whether the analysis extends to more complex or state-dependent distribution shifts, and whether the bias reduction from lazy deployment translates to meaningful accuracy gains in large-scale deployments. The experiments are limited to small-scale settings (linear models on synthetic data, a 3-layer NN on Spambase), leaving scalability untested.
