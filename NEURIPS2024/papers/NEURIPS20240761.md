## Problem

Existing federated unlearning methods address client-, class-, or sample-level removal but do not handle feature-level unlearning, where a client wants to erase the influence of a specific attribute (e.g., a facial region, a backdoor trigger, or a biased feature) across all their data points. Two practical barriers remain: prior feature unlearning techniques require access to all clients' data (impractical in FL), and there is no principled metric to evaluate whether a feature has been effectively unlearned without constructing a 'feature-removed' dataset that degrades model accuracy.

## Input / Output

Ferrari consumes the trained global model parameters θ, the unlearning client's local dataset D_u (images at 28×28 to 224×224, tabular rows, or text sequences), the index set F specifying which feature dimensions to unlearn, and a Gaussian noise level σ ∈ [0.05, 1.0]. It outputs updated model parameters θ^u that are uploaded back to the server as the new global model. Notably, no other client's data or compute is required; the perturbation δ_F is nonzero only on the F indices and zero everywhere else, so the method is agnostic to the total input dimensionality d.

## Method

Ferrari introduces a local Lipschitz-based metric called feature sensitivity s = E_{δ_F} ‖f(x) − f(x+δ_F)‖₂ / ‖δ_F‖₂, which quantifies how strongly the model output responds to perturbations confined to the target feature region. The unlearning objective minimizes a Monte Carlo approximation of s (N=20 samples per data point) over the unlearning client's local data via gradient descent on θ, effectively flattening the model's response surface along the unlearned feature axes. A theoretical guarantee (Theorem 1) shows that under zero training loss and a natural monotonicity assumption on perturbation magnitude, Ferrari's utility loss is upper-bounded by that of exact feature unlearning (retraining on feature-removed data), which itself suffers >27% accuracy degradation in practice. The procedure runs for a single epoch on local data only, making it orders of magnitude cheaper than retraining or gradient-residual-based baselines while matching or exceeding their unlearning effectiveness across sensitive, backdoor, and biased feature scenarios.

## Results

On sensitive feature unlearning, Ferrari achieves 92.26% test accuracy on CelebA (mouth), 81.02% on Adult (marriage), 79.53% on Diabetes (pregnancies), and 89.15% on IMDB (names), closely matching the original baseline (94.87%, 82.45%, 82.11%, 91.39%) while reducing feature sensitivity to 0.05–0.09 (vs. 0.85–1.52 for all other methods) and MIA attack success rate to 42–51% (vs. 71–92%). On backdoor unlearning, Ferrari achieves 95.83% on MNIST, 89.52% on CIFAR-10, and 65.36% on ImageNet while driving unlearn-client accuracy to near 0% (0.09–0.90%), confirming effective backdoor removal. On biased feature unlearning, Ferrari achieves 83.85% on CMNIST and 94.62% on CelebA with near-equal accuracy on biased and unbiased splits (e.g., 84.31% vs. 84.62% on CMNIST), demonstrating fairness restoration.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Ferrari substantially outperforms both federated unlearning baselines across all scenarios. Against FedCDP [65] (class-discriminative pruning), Ferrari gains 58.23 points on CelebA sensitive unlearning (92.26% vs. 34.03%), 30.51 points on MNIST backdoor (95.83% vs. 65.31%), and 56.29 points on CMNIST biased unlearning (83.85% vs. 27.56%). Against FedRecovery [61] (differentially private gradient-based unlearning), the margins are even larger: 62.48 points on CelebA sensitive (92.26% vs. 29.78%), 55.31 points on MNIST backdoor (95.83% vs. 40.52%), and 74.43 points on CMNIST biased (83.85% vs. 25.05%). In feature sensitivity, Ferrari reduces the metric to 0.05–0.09, comparable to the Retrain oracle, while FedCDP and FedRecovery remain above 0.78. In MIA attack success rate, Ferrari achieves 42–51% (near the Retrain level of 39–49%), whereas FedCDP and FedRecovery retain 71–84% ASR, indicating they fail to remove sensitive features. On backdoor unlearning, Ferrari drives unlearn-client accuracy to 0.09–0.90% (matching Retrain's 0.00%), while FedCDP and FedRecovery leave 46–72% accuracy on backdoor samples, confirming they do not effectively remove the trigger.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes Lipschitz continuity — previously used as a training regularizer for robustness (spectral norm, Lipschitz-regularized networks) — as an unlearning objective, defining 'feature sensitivity' as the local rate of change of model output to perturbations in a target feature. It departs from centralized feature unlearning methods (Warnecke et al., Guo et al.) that require access to all client data, and from federated unlearning methods (FedCDP, FedRecovery, FedEraser) that operate at the client, class, or sample level but not the feature level. The key conceptual move is showing that minimizing this sensitivity metric on only the unlearning client's local data suffices to remove feature memorization without any other client's participation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first framework to address feature-level unlearning in FL without requiring all clients to participate, which is critical for real-world FL deployments where clients may be unwilling or unable to cooperate. The theoretical guarantee that Ferrari achieves lower utility loss than exact feature unlearning (retraining without the feature) is a useful result, since exact unlearning via data perturbation causes severe accuracy degradation (beyond 27% in their experiments). Open questions include whether the approach extends to generative models and LLMs, how it performs under strongly non-IID data with very small Dirichlet parameters, and whether it remains effective when only a small fraction of the unlearning client's data is available.
