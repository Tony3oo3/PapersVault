## Problem

Offline policy evaluation (OPE) offers many estimators (importance sampling, FQE, doubly robust, minimax-style), but no principled, assumption-light method exists to aggregate their outputs into a single lower-MSE estimate. Prior approaches either select one estimator via heuristics or structural assumptions (e.g., nested bias-variance ordering), or reformulate the selection as a hyperparameter search, leaving the general mix-and-match combination problem unsolved.

## Input / Output

OPERA consumes an offline trajectory dataset D_n, an evaluation policy π_e, and a heterogeneous set of k OPE estimators (any type: IS, WIS, FQE, DR, Dual-DICE, etc.) along with two bootstrap hyperparameters: the number of resamples B and a subsample coefficient η ∈ [0,1] that controls the bootstrap sample size n₁ = n^η. It outputs a single scalar performance estimate for π_e and the learned weight vector α̂ ∈ ℝ^k. Notably, the weights are unconstrained (can be negative), and no ground-truth policy value or held-out validation set is required—unlike supervised stacked generalization.

## Method

OPERA frames the combination of k OPE scores as a linear weighted stack and solves for the optimal weights by minimizing the estimated MSE of the aggregate, formulated as the convex problem min_α αᵀÂα subject to Σαᵢ = 1. The key technical challenge is estimating the cross-estimator MSE matrix A without access to the true policy value V^π; OPERA resolves this via a subsampling bootstrap procedure (following Hall, 1990) that draws B resamples of size n₁ = n^η from D_n, re-runs each estimator on each resample, and constructs Â from the deviations of resampled scores from full-data scores. This avoids both the need for ground truth and the difficulty of computing closed-form variances for complex estimators like FQE with neural function approximation. The paper proves that OPERA is consistent (provided at least one base estimator is consistent), achieves MSE no worse than the best base estimator under optimal weights, and satisfies a finite-sample bound of k²/n^{2λ} + Δ_c where λ governs the bootstrap convergence rate. The convex formulation permits negative weights, enabling bias cancellation between estimators with same-signed biases.

## Results

On D4RL continuous control tasks (Hopper, HalfCheetah, Walker2d; medium and medium-replay datasets), OPERA achieves the lowest RMSE across all six settings, ranging from 8.5 (Hopper medium) to 149.0 (Walker2d medium), outperforming the next-best multi-estimator method (BestOPE) by 15–35% and single-estimator baselines (Dual-DICE, MB) by 3–20×. On the Sepsis ICU simulator, OPERA achieves MSE of 0.1705–0.2750 across MDP/POMDP settings with 200–1000 trajectories, consistently matching or exceeding the best individual estimator (IS, WIS, or FQE). On the ToyGraph domain, OPERA outperforms BestOPE and AvgOPE in 3 of 4 stochasticity/observability configurations, with the sole exception being the deterministic POMDP setting where WIS alone is strongest.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OPERA outperforms all five compared baselines on the D4RL benchmark. Against the single-estimator baselines, OPERA's RMSE is 3–20× lower than Dual-DICE (Nachum et al., 2019) and 2–8× lower than the model-based (MB) estimator, while also beating IS (Precup, 2000) and FQE (Le et al., 2019) by wide margins on Sepsis (e.g., MSE 0.2205 vs. 0.2753 for IS at N=200). Against the multi-estimator baselines, OPERA beats the Doubly Robust estimator (Jiang and Li, 2016) by 2–10× on D4RL (e.g., 13.0 vs. 112.7 RMSE on Hopper medium-replay) and the BVFT tournament method (Zhang and Jiang, 2021) by 1.2–5.7× (e.g., 8.5 vs. 16.4 on Hopper medium). OPERA also consistently beats the simple AvgOPE and BestOPE aggregation baselines across all six D4RL tasks and 7 of 8 Sepsis/Graph settings, with the only loss occurring in the deterministic POMDP Graph setting where a single WIS estimator is already near-optimal and bootstrap MSE estimation error prevents OPERA from concentrating weight on it.

## Relation to Prior Work

OPERA adapts the stacked generalization framework (Wolpert, 1992) to offline policy evaluation, where the key challenge is the absence of ground-truth labels for computing MSE. It leverages Hall's (1990) subsampling bootstrap procedure to estimate the cross-estimator bias-variance matrix without requiring access to true policy values. This departs from prior OPE combination methods (DR, MAGIC) that blend only two specific estimator types with parametric hyperparameters, and from estimator-selection methods (SLOPE, BVFT) that require structural assumptions like nested bias-variance ordering. OPERA is the first to treat the OPE ensemble problem as a general convex optimization over arbitrary mixes of estimator families (IS, FQE, model-based, minimax) with data-driven weights.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

OPERA provides a practical, estimator-agnostic framework that removes the need for practitioners to manually select or tune OPE algorithms for each domain—a persistent pain point in offline RL deployment in healthcare and robotics. The finite-sample guarantee (MSE bounded by k²/n^{2λ} + Δ_c) and the performance-improvement theorem give theoretical grounding, while the D4RL results demonstrate utility even when base estimators (FQE with neural nets) violate consistency assumptions. Key limitations include: in small-data regimes, bootstrap estimation error can cause OPERA to underperform simple BestOPE selection (as seen in the Graph deterministic POMDP case), and the theoretical guarantees require at least one consistent base estimator. Open questions include whether more complex meta-aggregators (beyond linear stacking) could further improve performance, and whether OPERA can provably match any individual estimator without additional assumptions.
