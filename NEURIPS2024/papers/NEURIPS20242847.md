## Problem

Translating visual text design (fonts, textures, color gradients) across different writing systems—e.g., from English to Korean or Arabic—remains unsolved because existing text-to-image models rely on textual style descriptions that cannot capture intricate typographic aesthetics. No benchmark exists to evaluate whether a model can preserve design intent while switching scripts, and prior subject-driven typography methods like DS-Fusion are limited to a single glyph per style and lack mechanisms to ensure character legibility.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242847/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, SIGIL takes only a text prompt (e.g., "a typography of 'K'") and internally uses the source-language character images as a style prior plus rendered target glyphs (white background, black glyph produced via Pillow) as shape guidance. During training, 3–5 style images per subject are provided alongside glyph images and prompts, but the ground-truth styled character is never shown to the model. Generated character patches are resized to 25×25 pixels for OCR-based evaluation, and the benchmark evaluates 1,040 styled characters per language across 10 style subjects and 26 prompts sampled 4 times each.

## Method

SIGIL comprises a Generator and a Corrector built on a Dreambooth-style U-Net (Stable Diffusion v1.5). The Generator introduces glyph latent guidance: the same pre-trained VAE encoder maps rendered target glyphs to latent vectors z_g, and a glyph loss L_glyph = ‖ε̂_t − z_g‖² is added to the standard diffusion MSE loss with a dynamic coefficient f(t) = λ(1 − e^{−t/τ}) (λ=0.1, τ=50), enabling cross-language shape comparison without a separate classifier. The Corrector applies PPO reinforcement learning where an off-the-shelf OCR model (EasyOCR) scores decoded character images as rewards, with a character-count penalty d(n) = e^{−(n−1)} to prevent the model from generating multiple small copies of the same letter to exploit the reward. This two-stage design lets SIGIL train on just 3–5 style images without ground-truth styled characters, generate two glyphs per style (versus one for DS-Fusion), and simultaneously optimize for both style fidelity and character legibility.

## Results

On MuST-Bench, SIGIL achieves OCR accuracy of 0.7163 (English), 0.7481 (Chinese), and 0.6577 (Korean), substantially outperforming all baselines. For style fidelity measured by CLIP-I, SIGIL scores 0.8673/0.8618/0.8692 across English/Chinese/Korean, competitive with AnyText (0.8731/0.8688/0.8671) but clearly ahead of DALL-E3 (0.8210/0.8237/0.8255) and DS-Fusion (0.8596/0.8531/0.8615). In MLLM-based style fidelity evaluation (GPT-4V, 5-point Likert), SIGIL scores 3.89/3.50/4.00 versus 3.00/2.12/2.11 for DALL-E3, 2.44/2.88/2.56 for AnyText, and 1.89/2.12/2.22 for DS-Fusion. In the 60-participant user study, SIGIL achieves ranking scores of ~0.40 for style fidelity and 0.30–0.38 for legibility, roughly double the next-best method.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SIGIL decisively outperforms all three baselines on OCR accuracy across all three languages, with the largest margins over DALL-E3 (which scores 0 on Chinese and Korean OCR) and DS-Fusion (e.g., 0.7481 vs. 0.2471 on Chinese OCR). On CLIP-I style similarity, AnyText slightly edges out SIGIL on English (0.8731 vs. 0.8673) and Chinese (0.8688 vs. 0.8618), which the authors attribute to AnyText's image-editing approach including background in the similarity computation; SIGIL wins on Korean (0.8692 vs. 0.8671). In the MLLM-based style fidelity evaluation (GPT-4V and Claude-3-OPUS), SIGIL achieves the highest scores across all six language–evaluator combinations, with GPT-4V scores of 3.89/3.50/4.00 versus the next-best DALL-E3 at 3.00/2.12/2.11. The 60-user study confirms these findings: SIGIL's style fidelity ranking score (~0.40) is roughly double that of AnyText (~0.22) and DALL-E3 (~0.19), and its legibility scores (0.30–0.38) also lead all baselines. Overall, SIGIL wins on readability (OCR) and perceived style fidelity, while AnyText marginally leads on raw CLIP-I image similarity for two of three languages.

## Relation to Prior Work

SIGIL extends the Dreambooth-style subject-driven diffusion framework (Ruiz et al., 2023) to the typography domain, replacing the single-subject personalization with a dual-glyph setup that simultaneously encodes a style prior from source-language character images and a shape constraint from rendered target-language glyphs in a shared VAE latent space. It departs from DS-Fusion (Tanveer et al., 2023), which could only train one glyph per style and relied on GAN-based training, by using a diffusion-based generator with OCR-rewarded PPO (following Black et al., 2023) to improve legibility. The paper also positions itself against text-prompt-driven approaches (DALL-E3, AnyText) by arguing that textual descriptions are insufficient to convey typographic design intent, motivating direct image-based style conditioning instead.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MuST-Bench is the first benchmark providing instance-level multilingual typography pairs with character-level bounding boxes, filling a gap that existing datasets (LAION-5B, MARIO-10M, AnyWord-3M) cannot address for cross-language style transfer evaluation. The OCR-reward RL corrector is a practical and transferable technique for improving text legibility in diffusion-based generators, though its effectiveness depends on the OCR model's vocabulary coverage and accuracy. Key limitations include the restriction to two glyphs per style, reliance on low-resolution movie poster crops, and the use of EasyOCR whose false negatives and limited vocabulary constrain the reward signal; scaling to more glyphs, higher-resolution inputs, and more robust OCR models remain open.
