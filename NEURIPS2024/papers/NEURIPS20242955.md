## Problem

Batched Bayesian Optimization with large query sizes (Q=100+) requires acquisition functions that are both tractable and controllable, but existing batch methods like q-UCB and q-EI rely on Monte Carlo integration or reparameterization tricks that lose predictable explore-exploit behavior as batch size grows. The paper addresses the need for a batch acquisition function that natively handles arbitrary batch sizes, provides tight user control over exploration via a single hyperparameter, and extends to heteroskedastic noise without sampling-based approximations.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a trained GP surrogate (with kernel, training data, and optionally a learned noise function σ²(x)), a batch of Q continuous candidate points, and a dimensionless temperature T′ (scaled by the kernel amplitude A to match UCB's κ). It outputs a jointly optimized batch of Q input points via gradient descent, with all acquisition values computed in closed form—no Monte Carlo samples or reparameterization draws are needed. The softmax variant (maxBEEBO) additionally takes an inverse temperature β (default A^{-1/2}) and a minimal probability mass parameter α to prevent vanishing gradients.

## Method

BEEBO decomposes the acquisition function into a free-energy-like sum of an energy term E(x) (exploitation) and a temperature-scaled information gain I(x) (exploration): a_BEEBO(x) = −E(x) + T·I(x). The information gain I(x) = ½ln det(C(x)) − ½ln det(C_aug(x)) is computed analytically from the GP posterior covariance before and after fantasizing observations at the batch points, exploiting the fact that C(x) depends only on input locations and noise, not on observed y values. The energy term uses a softmax-weighted sum of GP means, whose expectation under a multivariate normal is approximated via a second-order Taylor expansion of the log-softmax denominator, yielding a fully differentiable closed-form expression. This enables parallel gradient-based optimization of all Q points simultaneously without MC integration, and the temperature T provides Q-independent control of the explore-exploit balance. Under heteroskedastic noise, the information gain automatically downweights high-noise regions, producing risk-averse behavior without additional penalty terms.

## Results

On 33 benchmark experiments (9 test functions × multiple dimensions × 3 explore-exploit settings) with batch size Q=100, meanBEEBO achieves a mean normalized best-observed value of 0.795–0.828 across κ settings, outperforming q-UCB (0.758–0.801) at every setting, with the largest gap at high exploration (√κ=10: 0.788 vs 0.678). On the relative batch instantaneous regret R_rel (lower is better), BEEBO achieves 0.26–0.29 versus q-UCB's 0.87–0.90, indicating BEEBO produces substantially higher-quality final batches. Under heteroskedastic noise on the Branin function, BEEBO is risk-averse and preferentially converges to the low-noise optimum, while q-UCB remains risk-neutral.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against q-UCB (the primary baseline with a matching explore-exploit hyperparameter), meanBEEBO outperforms on mean normalized best-observed value at all three κ settings (0.795/0.828/0.788 vs 0.758/0.801/0.678), with the advantage growing at higher exploration rates. The gap is most dramatic on the relative batch instantaneous regret R_rel, where BEEBO achieves 0.26–0.29 versus q-UCB's 0.87–0.90, meaning BEEBO's final exploitative batch is roughly 3× closer to the optimum than q-UCB's. Against Kriging Believer with LogEI, BEEBO is competitive on most synthetic benchmarks but KB can be slower to optimize; KB is particularly strong on the two robot control problems. GIBBON is orders of magnitude slower to compute than BEEBO and the paper notes it fails in practice for Q>50 without heuristic scaling. Thompson Sampling, q-EI (LogEI), and TuRBO lack an explicit explore-exploit hyperparameter, so comparisons are at their default settings; BEEBO's controllable temperature gives it a structural advantage in tuning the trade-off for a given application.

## Relation to Prior Work

BEEBO synthesizes three existing ideas into a new batched acquisition function: the explicit explore-exploit trade-off of UCB (controlled by a single hyperparameter), the information-theoretic entropy reduction criterion (but applied to global uncertainty rather than optimum location, distinguishing it from entropy search and GIBBON), and the free-energy decomposition from statistical physics (E + T·I). Unlike reparameterization-based q-methods (q-UCB, q-EI) that rely on Monte Carlo integration and lose controllability at large batch sizes, BEEBO computes both its energy and information-gain terms in closed form, enabling direct gradient-based optimization of the entire batch. The paper further shows that under homoskedastic noise, BEEBO is mathematically equivalent to a Determinantal Point Process with a specific quality-diversity decomposition, connecting it to the DPP-based batch BO literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

BEEBO addresses a practical gap: existing batched acquisition functions (q-UCB, GIBBON) lose their explore-exploit controllability or fail entirely at large batch sizes (Q > 50), which is common in drug discovery and materials design where parallel experiments are dispatched. The risk-averse behavior under heteroskedastic noise is a natural and useful property for experimental settings where measurement uncertainty varies across the domain. Open questions include: whether a fully Bayesian treatment of unknown noise landscapes is needed, how to dynamically schedule the temperature T across optimization rounds, and whether the O(N²Q) Cholesky-update complexity remains tractable for very large training sets without scalable GP approximations.
