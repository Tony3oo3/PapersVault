## Problem

In heterogeneous distributed stochastic convex optimization, no local update method had been shown to provably outperform the Minibatch-SGD baseline. While Local-SGD and variants like SCAFFOLD match or fall short of Minibatch-SGD in communication complexity, the gap between the number of gradient queries and the number of model updates in Minibatch-SGD suggested room for improvement. This paper closes that gap by designing the first local method that provably requires fewer communication rounds than Minibatch-SGD under data heterogeneity.

## Input / Output

Each of M machines independently draws i.i.d. samples from its own distribution D_i (which may differ across machines) and computes local gradient estimates. The algorithm is parameterized by R communication rounds, K local steps per round, a learning rate η, and a weight sequence {α_t}; the dissimilarity between machines is characterized by G_* (the norm of per-machine gradients at the optimum). The output is a single point x_T = x_{KR} computed by the parameter server after R rounds of aggregation, with convergence measured in expected excess loss f(x_T) − f(w*).

## Method

SLowcal-SGD customizes the Anytime-GD technique to the distributed local-update setting: each machine maintains two sequences, w_t (updated by gradient steps at x_t) and x_t (a weighted average of past w_t values), and queries stochastic gradients at the slowly-changing x_t rather than at w_t itself. This reduces the inter-machine bias during local updates because the query points drift much less than the iterates would under standard Local-SGD. A crucial design choice is the use of increasing importance weights α_t = t+1 (rather than uniform weights), which emphasizes later rounds where inter-machine bias has shrunk due to convergence; the authors show that uniform weights actually make SLowcal-SGD worse than Minibatch-SGD. The resulting convergence rate achieves a linear speedup in M with fewer communication rounds than Minibatch-SGD, and the analysis decomposes the error into a standard regret term and a bias term V_T that captures the accumulated inter-machine divergence.

## Results

The primary contribution is theoretical: SLowcal-SGD achieves a convergence rate of O((σ^{1/2}+G_*^{1/2})/(K^{1/4}R) + 1/(KR) + 1/(K^{1/3}R^{4/3}) + 1/R² + σ/√(MKR)) for heterogeneous stochastic convex optimization, requiring only R_min = G_*·MK^{1/2} + MK^{1/2} communication rounds for a linear speedup in M — substantially fewer than Minibatch-SGD (R_min = MK) or Local-SGD (R_min = G⁴·(MK)³ + M³K). Experimentally, on MNIST with logistic regression under Dirichlet(α=0.1) non-IID splits, SLowcal-SGD achieves consistently higher test accuracy and lower test loss than both Local-SGD and Minibatch-SGD across 16, 32, and 64 workers, with the gap widening as the number of local steps K and workers M increase.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SLowcal-SGD provably outperforms Minibatch-SGD [10] in the heterogeneous SCO setting: it requires R_min = G_*·MK^{1/2} + MK^{1/2} communication rounds for linear speedup versus MK for Minibatch-SGD, a reduction by a factor of roughly √(MK/G_*). Against Local-SGD [37], SLowcal-SGD improves both the convergence rate (replacing the G^{2/3}/R^{2/3} term with the milder (σ^{1/2}+G_*^{1/2})/(K^{1/4}R)) and the communication complexity (R_min drops from G⁴·(MK)³ + M³K to G_*·MK^{1/2} + MK^{1/2}). SCAFFOLD [21] matches Minibatch-SGD's rate but requires the same R_min = MK, so SLowcal-SGD still needs fewer rounds. The paper does not claim to beat Accelerated Minibatch SGD [10,25] (R_min = (MK)^{1/3}), and explicitly notes this remains an open question. Experimentally on MNIST, SLowcal-SGD outperforms both Local-SGD and Minibatch-SGD in test accuracy and test loss, with the advantage growing as K and M increase; however, for very small K the differences are negligible and Minibatch-SGD has a slight edge.

## Relation to Prior Work

SLowcal-SGD adapts the slowly-changing query points technique from Anytime-GD/Anytime-SGD (Cutkosky 2019; Kavis et al. 2019) to the distributed local-update setting, combining it with the Local-SGD/Federated Averaging framework (McMahan et al. 2017). Prior work established that standard Local-SGD is inferior to Minibatch-SGD in both homogeneous and heterogeneous settings, and that methods like SCAFFOLD match but do not provably beat the Minibatch baseline in the heterogeneous case. This paper is the first to design a local-update method that provably surpasses Minibatch-SGD in heterogeneous stochastic convex optimization, with the key insight that importance weighting (α_t = t+1) is essential—without it, the method actually underperforms Minibatch-SGD.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This result closes a notable gap in the federated learning literature by showing that local updates can provably outperform the dominant Minibatch-SGD baseline under data heterogeneity, reducing the communication rounds needed for linear speedup. However, the practical impact is tempered by the limited experimental validation (MNIST with logistic regression only) and the requirement to know problem parameters (σ, L) in advance. A major open question remains whether any local method can beat Accelerated Minibatch-SGD, which currently holds the best known rate in both homogeneous and heterogeneous SCO settings.
