## Problem

Existing video anomaly detection models are trained and evaluated within a single dataset, but the criteria for what constitutes an abnormal event differ fundamentally across VAD datasets (e.g., a pedestrian on a road is normal in UCF-Crimes but abnormal in TAD). This 'abnormal conflict' prevents naive multi-task or multi-domain learning from producing a general model, as inconsistent labels across domains cause confusion during joint training. The paper introduces MDVAD as a new task and benchmark to study how to build a general VAD model that distinguishes truly general normal/abnormal patterns while remaining aware of domain-specific conflicts.

## Input / Output

Input videos are split into T=32 uniformly sampled snippets of 16 frames each, processed through an I3D backbone (pretrained on Kinetics, C=2048) with 224×224 crops and 10-crop augmentation. The output is a frame-level abnormal score per snippet; for a known target domain the score comes from that domain's dedicated head, while for unseen domains the score is computed as the maximum across all heads (Null-MIL) or via cosine-similarity-weighted combination of abnormal and normal head scores (NullAng-MIL). The AC classifier is a training-only auxiliary branch that is discarded at inference, so it adds no test-time cost.

## Method

The framework consists of shared domain-agnostic layers (I3D backbone, feature aggregation with split-max channel squeezing, and temporal convolution) followed by M domain-specific abnormal heads (and normal heads for NullAng-MIL), where M equals the number of source datasets. The Null-MIL loss activates only the head matching the input domain and assigns Null values to all other heads, so gradients update only the relevant head's weights and prevent cross-domain label confusion. NullAng-MIL extends this by normalizing features and head weights to unit length, then imposing an angular margin m in cosine space so that the maximum abnormal cosine similarity exceeds the maximum normal cosine similarity by at least m, tightening intra-class compactness. An auxiliary Abnormal Conflict (AC) classifier, trained with focal loss, predicts whether a snippet's scores across heads deviate beyond a threshold τ, forcing the shared domain-agnostic features to explicitly encode conflict-aware representations. The total loss is L = L_NullAng-MIL + λ·L_AC, and the AC branch is removed at test time.

## Results

On the MDVAD benchmark (six VAD datasets), the proposed NullAng-MIL + AC classifier achieves the best average AUC in three of four evaluation protocols: 86.26% (E1 Held-in), 75.96% (E2 Leave-one-out), and 79.78% (E3 Low-shot Adaptation), outperforming the strongest baseline COMO by 0.41, 1.04, and 3.98 points respectively. In E4 (Full Fine-tuning), COMO edges out the proposed method (87.70% vs. 87.31%), though the gap is narrow. The advantage is most pronounced in E3, where the conflict-aware multi-head design with only 10% target samples yields a 3.98-point margin over COMO, demonstrating the value of general pre-training for adaptation to unseen domains.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares four existing WVAD models—MMIL (CVPR 2018), ARNet (ICME 2020), WSAL (IEEE TIP 2021), and COMO (CVPR 2023)—on the MDVAD benchmark across four protocols. The proposed NullAng-MIL + AC classifier wins in E1 (86.26% vs. COMO's 85.85%), E2 (75.96% vs. 74.92%), and E3 (79.78% vs. 75.80%), with the largest margin in E3 where low-shot adaptation amplifies the benefit of conflict-aware multi-domain pre-training. In E4 (full fine-tuning on the target domain), COMO slightly outperforms the proposed method (87.70% vs. 87.31%), suggesting that when full target-domain data is available for fine-tuning, the single-domain COMO architecture can recover its in-domain advantage. WSAL consistently underperforms across all protocols, particularly in E4 (83.86%), indicating its localization-based approach is less suited to multi-domain settings. The results highlight that the proposed method's advantage is most significant in unseen-domain and low-shot scenarios, where the AC classifier and angular-margin multi-head design provide meaningful gains over single-head baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by transplanting the multi-domain learning paradigm (established in NLP, segmentation, and detection) into video anomaly detection, where it had not been formulated. It departs from prior VAD work in two key ways: (1) unlike single-domain WVAD methods (MMIL, ARNet, WSAL, COMO) that assume a fixed anomaly definition, it confronts the 'abnormal conflict' where the same visual event is labeled differently across datasets; and (2) unlike domain adaptation or open-set VAD, it trains simultaneously on multiple source domains with conflicting label semantics rather than adapting from one source to one target. The Null-class strategy from multi-dataset segmentation [18] inspires the Null-MIL loss, but the angular-margin extension (NullAng-MIL) and the AC classifier are novel adaptations to the binary normal/abnormal setting where explicit label conflicts are replaced by implicit definitional conflicts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a concrete, previously unaddressed failure mode in VAD—abnormal conflict—and provides the first benchmark (six datasets, four evaluation protocols) to measure it, which will be practically useful for anyone deploying VAD across diverse surveillance environments. The finding that multi-domain pre-training followed by fine-tuning can match or exceed single-domain training (E4) suggests a viable path toward a single general-purpose VAD model, reducing the need for domain-specific retraining. Limitations include reliance on a fixed I3D backbone, only six source domains, and the AC classifier being training-only; open questions include whether the approach scales to dozens of domains, whether more powerful backbones (e.g., video transformers) change the conflict dynamics, and how to handle truly novel anomaly types absent from all source domains.
