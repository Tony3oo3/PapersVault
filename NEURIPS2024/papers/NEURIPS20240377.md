## Problem

Existing decentralized DML frameworks rely on linear aggregation, which cannot simultaneously mitigate model divergence caused by non-IID data across devices and information loss from time-varying communication links. Prior work addresses these barriers in isolation, and no method provides a unified non-linear aggregation mechanism with theoretical convergence guarantees under both conditions. HyperPrism is the first framework to jointly tackle data heterogeneity and dynamic topology in realistic decentralized settings.

## Input / Output

Each device i holds a private dataset D_i (MNIST or CIFAR-10 partitioned via Dirichlet distribution with α ∈ {0.1, 1, 10}) and a local model split into a representation part θ (convolutional/embedding layers) and a decision part φ (fully-connected layers). The communication topology G(t) changes every round, simulated via NS3 with WiFi 802.11a in Ad-Hoc mode, producing a doubly stochastic weight matrix A^(t) from pairwise connectivity. The output is the converged local model w_i^(T) after T rounds, along with per-layer adaptive power degrees p_{θ,i} and p_{φ,i} generated by a local hypernetwork that takes an embedding vector v_i and the current local gradient as input.

## Method

HyperPrism replaces linear averaging with a Weighted Power Mean (WPM) computed in a mirror-descent dual space: instead of w̄_i = Σ a_ij w_j, it computes w̄_i = [∇φ]^{-1}(Σ a_ij ∇φ(w_j)) where ∇φ(w) = w^p (termwise exponentiation), so aggregation raises parameters to power p, averages, then takes the p-th root. A per-device hypernetwork (3 fully-connected layers with softmax outputs) adaptively selects distinct p values for the representation and decision parts each round, updated via chain-rule gradients through the aggregation step. The theoretical loss bound is O((m^{p+2}/T)^{1/(p+1)}), improving the device-count dependence from m√m (linear aggregation, p=1) to m·m^{1/p}, with p→∞ yielding the tightest synchronization. The p=1 case exactly recovers standard linear aggregation (DPSGD, SwarmSGD, etc.), positioning all prior linear methods as special cases within the HyperPrism framework.

## Results

On the most challenging non-IID setting (Dirichlet α=0.1, 50 devices, density 0.5), HyperPrism achieves 90.61% top-1 accuracy on MNIST (Logistic Regression) in just 6 convergence rounds, and 20.45% top-1 accuracy on CIFAR-10 (CNN) in 86 rounds. This represents up to 98.63% reduction in convergence rounds compared to the best baseline (e.g., 6 vs. 186 rounds for DPSGD on MNIST) and up to 26.70% absolute accuracy improvement over SwarmSGD. On CIFAR-10, ADOM fails to converge entirely (∞ rounds) while HyperPrism converges in 86 rounds with the highest accuracy (20.45%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

HyperPrism outperforms all four baselines on both datasets under the extreme non-IID setting (α=0.1). On MNIST, it matches ADOM's accuracy (90.61% vs. 90.58%) while converging in 6 rounds versus ADOM's 11, and surpasses Mudag (86.40%) by 4.21 points with 73% fewer rounds. On CIFAR-10, HyperPrism achieves the highest accuracy (20.45%) and is the only method besides Mudag that converges at all—ADOM diverges entirely (∞ rounds) and SwarmSGD/DPSGD plateau near 18%. The convergence speed advantage is most dramatic against DPSGD (96.77% fewer rounds on MNIST) and SwarmSGD (97.48% fewer rounds at density 0.2). As device count scales to 100, baselines degrade sharply (ADOM still ∞, Mudag drops to 11.46% on CIFAR-10) while HyperPrism maintains 19.32% accuracy in 151 rounds, demonstrating superior scalability.

## Relation to Prior Work

HyperPrism unifies and extends three research threads: (1) distributed mirror descent, where prior work (Nedic, Yuan, Shahrampour) takes local mirror descent steps but still aggregates linearly in the primal space; (2) Kolmogorov/Quasi-Arithmetic Means (Kolesárová), which provide the mathematical family of non-linear averaging operators, with the Weighted Power Mean serving as a computationally tractable instantiation; and (3) HyperNetworks (Galanti & Wolf, Shamsian, Ma), repurposed here not for personalization but for adaptively selecting the power degree p per model layer. The paper's key departure is performing aggregation in the mirror-descent dual space rather than the primal, and it positions all prior linear-aggregation DML methods (DPSGD, SwarmSGD, ADOM, Mudag) as the p=1 special case of its framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The theoretical improvement from an m√m dependence to m∜m (p-th root) in the convergence bound, combined with the practical 98.63% convergence speedup, suggests that non-linear aggregation could be a broadly applicable drop-in replacement for linear averaging in decentralized settings with limited communication. However, the experimental validation is confined to MNIST/CIFAR-10 with logistic regression and a small CNN under convex analysis, leaving open whether the gains persist for non-convex deep networks, larger-scale datasets, or real (non-simulated) time-varying topologies. The per-round hypernetwork overhead and the requirement for odd-integer p (for the sign-preserving power mean) are practical constraints that limit immediate deployment. A key open question is whether the adaptive p selection generalizes beyond the two-part (representation/decision) decomposition used here.
