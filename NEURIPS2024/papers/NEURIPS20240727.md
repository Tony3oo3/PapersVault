## Problem

U-statistics are the unique minimum-variance unbiased estimators for parameters of the form E[h(X₁,…,Xₖ)], yet privatizing them under central differential privacy has received little attention. Existing private mean estimation algorithms applied in a black-box manner yield suboptimal private error—constant-factor inflation in the leading term for non-degenerate kernels, or Θ(1/n) rather than O(1/n²) in degenerate settings—making them inadequate for applications like uniformity testing and sparse graph estimation.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes n i.i.d. samples from an unknown distribution, a known symmetric kernel h: X^k → R (bounded or sub-Gaussian with variance proxy τ), the degree k, and a privacy parameter ε = O(1). It outputs an ε-differentially private estimate θ̃ of θ = E[h(X₁,…,Xₖ)] with a high-probability error guarantee parameterized by failure probability α. For sub-Gaussian kernels, the algorithm uses a two-stage procedure: a coarse private estimate on half the data to determine a projection interval, then the main reweighting algorithm on the remaining half.

## Method

The core mechanism is a thresholding-based reweighting scheme built on local Hajek projections: for each index i, the local projection ĥ(i) averages h(X_S) over all k-subsets S containing i, giving the best linear representation of the U-statistic at that point. Indices whose local projections deviate from the empirical mean by more than a concentration threshold ξ + 6kCL/n are classified as 'bad,' and their contribution to the estimator is blended with the empirical mean via a weight that decays linearly with distance. This reweighting bounds the local sensitivity of the modified U-statistic, and a smooth upper bound on that sensitivity (calibrated by the number L of bad indices) determines the noise scale added via a smoothed-sensitivity mechanism. For sub-Gaussian kernels, a coarse private mean estimate is first computed to project h onto a bounded interval, reducing the problem to the bounded case. The resulting algorithm achieves private error Õ(k√τ/(nε)) for non-degenerate kernels—matching the lower bound up to log factors—and Õ(k^{3/2}C/(n^{3/2}ε)) for bounded degenerate kernels, which is near-optimal by a matching lower bound.

## Results

For non-degenerate sub-Gaussian kernels, the proposed algorithm (Corollary 1) achieves private error $\tilde{O}(k\sqrt{\tau}/(n\epsilon))$, matching the lower bound (Theorem 1) up to logarithmic factors. For bounded degenerate kernels, the algorithm achieves private error $\tilde{O}(k^{3/2}C/(n^{3/2}\epsilon))$ (Corollary 2), which matches the lower bound (Theorem 3) and improves over the $\Theta(1/n)$ error of off-the-shelf methods. In the uniformity testing application, the algorithm requires $n = \Omega(m^{1/2}/\min(\delta^2, \delta\epsilon))$ samples, which is quadratically better in $m$ than the $\Omega(m/\delta^2\epsilon)$ requirement of the all-tuples CoinPress approach.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three approaches: (1) a naive estimator that partitions data into disjoint chunks and applies CoinPress, (2) an all-tuples estimator that applies a generalized CoinPress to all $\binom{n}{k}$ subsets, and (3) the proposed local Hajek projection algorithm. The naive estimator has suboptimal non-private error ($\sqrt{k\zeta_k/n}$ instead of $\sqrt{k^2\zeta_1/n}$) and does not match the U-statistic variance. The all-tuples CoinPress approach achieves the correct non-private error but incurs a $\sqrt{k}$-factor inflation in the private error term ($k^{3/2}\sqrt{\tau}/(n\epsilon)$ vs. $k\sqrt{\tau}/(n\epsilon)$). The main algorithm matches the non-private variance and achieves the optimal private error for non-degenerate kernels (up to log factors). For degenerate kernels, the main algorithm improves the private error from $\tilde{O}(kC/(n\epsilon))$ to $\tilde{O}(k^{3/2}C/(n^{3/2}\epsilon))$, a $\sqrt{n/k}$ improvement that is critical in applications like uniformity testing where the non-private error is $O(1/n)$.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the CoinPress private mean estimation framework [10] to the setting of U-statistics, where the observations are weakly dependent k-tuples rather than i.i.d. scalars. It also generalizes the node-differential-privacy approach of Ullman and Sealfon [57] for Erdős-Rényi edge density to arbitrary U-statistic kernels via a new 'local Hajek projection' reweighting scheme. The authors explicitly position their work against prior private U-statistic results (Ghazi et al. [29], Bell et al. [8]), which are restricted to local DP and discrete data, and show that black-box application of existing central-DP mean estimators incurs a √k-factor inflation or degrades from O(1/n²) to Θ(1/n) error in degenerate settings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

U-statistics underpin a wide range of statistical procedures (Mann-Whitney, Cramér-von Mises, subgraph counts, random forests), yet their private estimation was essentially unaddressed in the central DP model for continuous data. The near-optimality results (matching lower bounds up to log factors) for both non-degenerate sub-Gaussian and bounded degenerate kernels close a significant gap, and the application to uniformity testing demonstrates a quadratic improvement in sample complexity over prior private testers. Open questions include extending the degenerate-kernel analysis to sub-Gaussian (unbounded) kernels and reducing the Õ(choose(n,k)) computational cost of the main algorithm beyond the O(n²) subsampled variant.
