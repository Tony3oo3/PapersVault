## Problem

Long-context LLMs suffer from the 'lost-in-the-middle' problem, where they reliably retrieve information from the beginning and end of a context but fail to access information in the middle. The authors hypothesize this stems from an unintentional position bias in standard training data: autoregressive pre-training emphasizes nearby tokens, and instruction-tuning places system prompts at the context start, so the model never learns that crucial information can appear at any position.

## Input / Output

The model receives a long context of 4K–32K tokens (constructed by shuffling ~128-token segments from a natural language corpus) followed by a question or instruction, and must produce a free-form answer. The questions are designed to require either pinpointing a specific fact from exactly one segment or integrating and reasoning over information scattered across two or more segments that may be far apart in the context. For evaluation, VAL Probing extends this to three context styles (natural-language sentences, Python code, and structured Wikidata entities) and three retrieval patterns (forward, backward, and bi-directional), each with ~3K examples at ~32K context length.

## Method

IN2 (INformation-INtensive) training is a purely data-driven post-training procedure that explicitly teaches the model that any position in a long context can hold crucial information. The authors synthesize ~1.6M QA pairs by (1) extracting ~128-token segments from the C4 corpus, (2) prompting GPT-4-Turbo to generate questions whose answers require fine-grained retrieval from one segment or multi-hop reasoning across two or more segments, and (3) concatenating the relevant segments with randomly sampled distractor segments in shuffled order to form a 4K–32K-token context. The dataset is balanced across context lengths and mixed with ~10% short-context QA and ~11% general instruction-tuning data (OpenOrca) to prevent catastrophic forgetting. This data is used to instruction-tune Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.2 for one epoch (~14K steps, batch 128, cosine LR decay to 1e-6) on 128 A100 GPUs, yielding FILM-7B. The paper also introduces VAL Probing, a benchmark with three context styles and three retrieval patterns, to more rigorously evaluate long-context utilization beyond the forward-retrieval-only Needle-in-the-Haystack task.

## Results

On the novel VAL Probing benchmark (32K-token contexts), FILM-7B achieves an overall average of 85.9% with a min-max position gap of only 13.9, compared to 47.3% and 56.2 gap for the Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.2 backbone. On real-world LongBench tasks, FILM-7B improves the backbone's average from 30.6 to 39.9 (e.g., NarrativeQA F1: 23.5→26.9, HotpotQA F1: 42.4→62.1), while maintaining short-context performance (MMLU accuracy: 59.3→59.2). On the RULER benchmark, FILM-7B is the first ≤7B model to achieve a 32K effective context length (86.9% at 32K vs. 75.4% for Mistral-7B).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FILM-7B dramatically outperforms its backbone Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.2 on VAL Probing (85.9 vs. 47.3 Avg; 13.9 vs. 56.2 Gap), confirming that IN2 training specifically targets the lost-in-the-middle problem. Against GPT-4-Turbo, FILM-7B surpasses it on Document (85.4 vs. 81.3) and Code (83.3 vs. 66.1) probing tasks, is comparable on Database (89.0 vs. 89.6), and achieves a substantially smaller position gap (13.9 vs. 32.1). Among open-source long-context models, FILM-7B outperforms LongAlign-13B-64K (85.9 vs. 68.5 Avg) and InternLM2-chat-20B (85.9 vs. 68.2 Avg) by a wide margin, despite being only 7B parameters. On real-world LongBench tasks, FILM-7B (39.9 Avg) surpasses all open-source baselines including InternLM2-7B-LongWanjuan (37.1) and ChatGLM3-6B-32K (36.6), approaching GPT-4-Turbo (44.7). On short-context tasks (MMLU, GSM8K, MATH, etc.), FILM-7B maintains performance nearly identical to the backbone, confirming no catastrophic forgetting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself within the data-engineering branch of long-context LLM training, departing from architectural or positional-encoding modifications (e.g., YaRN, LongRoPE, Mamba) in favor of a purely data-driven solution. It builds on the lost-in-the-middle problem formulation (Liu et al., 2024b) and the observation that induction heads make forward retrieval trivially easy (Olsson et al., 2022; Berglund et al., 2024), but carves new ground by hypothesizing that the root cause is an unintentional position bias in standard training data and addressing it through synthesized information-intensive QA pairs. The VAL Probing benchmark extends prior probing tasks (Needle-in-the-Haystack, passkey retrieval, RULER) by explicitly varying both context style and retrieval direction, filling a gap the authors identify in existing evaluations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a simple, reproducible data synthesis recipe (no architectural changes, no positional encoding modifications) can reduce the lost-in-the-middle gap by ~75% on a 7B model, offering a practical and low-cost intervention for practitioners extending context windows. The controlled comparison showing that equivalent amounts of normal instruction tuning fail to produce the same effect isolates the causal mechanism (position-agnostic information placement) from mere data volume. Key open questions remain: whether the approach scales beyond 32K tokens (the YaRN extension to 64K still suffers lost-in-the-middle), whether it generalizes to larger model families, and whether the synthesized data distribution adequately captures the information structure of real-world long documents. The paper's own limitation section acknowledges insufficient hyperparameter and data-mixture ablations.
