## Problem

Classical MDS, when applied to non-Euclidean dissimilarity matrices, discards negative eigenvalues of the centered Gram matrix, producing suboptimal STRESS and the counter-intuitive 'dimensionality paradox' where increasing the embedding dimension worsens the approximation. No principled method existed for dimension reduction that respects the indefinite geometry of general symmetric bilinear forms.

## Input / Output

The method takes a symmetric hollow n×n dissimilarity matrix D (which may have negative entries and need not satisfy the triangle inequality) and a target dimension k ≤ n. It produces k-dimensional coordinate vectors for each data point (which may be complex, or equivalently real coordinates paired with a signed diagonal bilinear form A ∈ ℝ^{k×k} with ±1 diagonal entries) and a reconstructed dissimilarity matrix that can also contain negative values. For large datasets, a landmark-based variant reduces computation to O(kn²) via partial SVD.

## Method

Neuc-MDS generalizes the Euclidean inner product to a symmetric bilinear form Φ(u,v) = uᵀAv where A is an indefinite diagonal matrix, preserving the geometry of the full Gram matrix including negative eigenvalues. The algorithm eigendecomposes the centered matrix B = −CDC/2, then selects k eigenvalues via a greedy EV-Selection procedure that picks the eigenvalue of largest magnitude whose sign matches the running sum of unselected eigenvalues, thereby jointly minimizing the two dominant STRESS error terms C₁ (sum of squared dropped eigenvalues) and C₂ (square of the sum of dropped eigenvalues). The embedding is X = √Λ · Diag(w) · Uᵀ with unselected rows dropped, and the bilinear form A encodes the sign of each retained eigenvalue. A stronger variant, Neuc-MDS⁺, replaces binary selection with optimal linear combinations of eigenvalues (scaling each selected eigenvalue by a shared factor derived from Cauchy-Schwarz), yielding a tighter STRESS lower bound. Theoretical analysis via Wigner's semicircle law shows that for random symmetric matrices, Neuc-MDS STRESS decreases monotonically to zero as k→n, while cMDS STRESS plateaus at a positive constant.

## Results

On STRESS (the primary metric), Neuc-MDS and Neuc-MDS+ consistently outperform cMDS and Lower-MDS by large margins across all 10 datasets. For example, on Random-Simplex, Neuc-MDS+ achieves STRESS 0.194 versus 80.520 for cMDS and 31.542 for Lower-MDS; on MNIST, Neuc-MDS+ reaches 9.885 versus 65.107 for cMDS. On genomics datasets (e.g., Brain, Breast, Colorectal), Neuc-MDS reduces STRESS to 0.017–0.078, compared to 0.490–2.958 for cMDS. SMACOF achieves competitive STRESS on a few genomics datasets (e.g., 0.005 on Leukemia) but diverges to values of 10⁵–10⁶ on image and synthetic datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Neuc-MDS and Neuc-MDS+ dominate cMDS on every dataset: STRESS is reduced by 1–2 orders of magnitude (e.g., 80.520→1.179 on Random-Simplex, 65.107→9.935 on MNIST, 2.958→0.078 on Leukemia). Against Lower-MDS [Sonthalia et al., NeurIPS 2021], the proposed methods also win on all 10 datasets, with particularly large gaps on synthetic data (31.542→1.179 on Random-Simplex) and image data (37.896→9.935 on MNIST). Against SMACOF, the picture is mixed: SMACOF achieves the lowest STRESS on Leukemia (0.005 vs. 0.078) and Renal (0.017 vs. 0.026), but it diverges catastrophically on Euclidean Ball (4×10⁶), MNIST (2.35×10⁵), Fashion-MNIST (2.80×10⁵), and CIFAR-10 (1.63×10⁵), where Neuc-MDS remains stable. On average geometric distortion, Neuc-MDS also outperforms cMDS and Lower-MDS on genomics datasets (e.g., 5.809 vs. 8.160 and 42.705 on Brain), while all methods are comparable on synthetic and image data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends classical MDS (Torgerson, 1952) by generalizing the inner product to symmetric bilinear forms, directly addressing the 'dimensionality paradox' identified by Sonthalia et al. (NeurIPS 2021) and Tsang & Pereira (2016), who showed that cMDS produces suboptimal and counter-intuitive results on non-Euclidean inputs. While prior non-Euclidean embedding work (hyperbolic, spherical MDS) targets specific geometries, this paper works with the full family of indefinite bilinear forms, retaining negative eigenvalues of the Gram matrix rather than discarding them. The error analysis framework follows Sonthalia et al.'s three-term STRESS decomposition, but the eigenvalue selection problem and its O(n) greedy solution are novel contributions that go beyond the binary keep/drop paradigm of cMDS.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first principled, theoretically-grounded resolution to the dimensionality paradox in MDS, showing that negative eigenvalues carry essential geometric information for non-Euclidean dissimilarities (cosine similarity, KL-divergence, graph distances) that are ubiquitous in practice. The asymptotic analysis via Wigner's semicircle law gives clean separation: Neuc-MDS error vanishes as k→n while cMDS plateaus at Θ(n³σ²). Key open questions remain: how to use output dissimilarity matrices containing negative values in downstream learning pipelines, and the broader geometry of general (p,q)-signature bilinear forms is largely unexplored. The method is computationally efficient (O(n) eigenvalue selection, compatible with randomized SVD) and resolves a long-standing practical limitation of one of the most widely used dimensionality reduction tools.
