## Problem

Previous asymptotic analyses of random feature ridge regression (RFRR) generalization require artificial proportional or polynomial scalings of n, p, and d, making it difficult to answer practical questions like the minimum number of features needed for optimal error rates. This paper provides a dimension-free, non-asymptotic deterministic equivalent for the RFRR test error that holds for infinite-dimensional feature maps, and uses it to derive a tight (not just upper-bound) expression for the optimal feature count under power-law source and capacity conditions.

## Input / Output

The deterministic equivalent is computed from the squared eigenvalues of the Fredholm integral operator (feature covariance spectrum), the target function's coefficients in the eigenbasis, and the hyperparameters n, p, λ, and σ²_ε. It is defined as the solution to a pair of self-consistent fixed-point equations (ν₁, ν₂) and yields a closed-form bias-variance decomposition. The approximation guarantee is multiplicative (relative to the risk scale) and scales as Õ(n^{-1/2} + p^{-1/2}), independent of the feature map dimension, which is what enables the infinite-dimensional setting.

## Method

Under a concentration assumption on the feature map eigenfunctions (satisfied by sub-Gaussian and log-Sobolev settings), the paper proves that the RFRR test error concentrates around a deterministic equivalent R_{n,p} defined by self-consistent equations involving traces of (Σ + ν₂)^{-1} and (Σ + ν₂)^{-2}. The proof proceeds in two stages: first, the bias and variance conditional on the weight feature matrix F are approximated by functionals of F at rate Õ(n^{-1/2}) using dimension-free deterministic equivalents for sample covariance matrices; second, those functionals of F are approximated by the fully deterministic R_{n,p} at rate Õ(p^{-1/2}). Under power-law spectrum ξ_k² = k^{-α} and target decay β_k = k^{-(1+2αr)/2}, the deterministic equivalent yields explicit scaling exponents γ_B(ℓ,q) and γ_V(ℓ,q) for bias and variance, revealing distinct variance-dominated and bias-dominated regimes with identifiable crossover points. The optimal feature count p* = n^{q*} achieving the minimax rate 2α(r∧1)/(2α(r∧1)+1) is shown to be strictly smaller than the previous upper bound from Rudi and Rosasco [2017].

## Results

The main theoretical result (Theorem 3.3) establishes a dimension-free, multiplicative approximation: |R_test − R_{n,p}| ≤ Õ(p^{-1/2} + n^{-1/2}) · R_{n,p}, where R_{n,p} is a closed-form deterministic equivalent depending only on the feature map eigenvalues. Under power-law source and capacity conditions (ξ_k² = k^{-α}, β_{*,k} = k^{-(1+2αr)/2}), the optimal excess risk rate is γ* = 2α(r∧1)/(2α(r∧1)+1), matching the minimax rate of Caponnetto & De Vito [2007]. The minimum number of features achieving this rate is p* = n^{q*} with q* = 1 − α(2r∧1)/(2α(r∧1)+1), which is strictly smaller than the previous upper bound p_0 of Rudi & Rosasco [2017] by a gap of 2(1−r)(α−1)/(2αr+1) for r ∈ [1/2,1). Empirical validation on FashionMNIST (n=300) and a spiked synthetic model (n=500) shows the deterministic equivalent curves closely tracking numerical simulations across regularization strengths.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical paper, so comparisons are against prior theoretical results rather than empirical baselines. The deterministic equivalent recovers and unifies the asymptotic formulas of Mei & Montanari [2022], Loureiro et al. [2022], and Schröder et al. [2023] as special limits (proportional and polynomial scaling), and the kernel limit (p→∞) exactly matches the dimension-free results of Cheng & Montanari [2022] and Misiakiewicz & Saeed [2024]. The key improvement over prior work is in the minimum feature count: for r ∈ [1/2,1), the paper's p* = n^{q*} is strictly smaller than Rudi & Rosasco's upper bound p_0 = n^{q_0}, closing a gap that had remained open. The optimal rate γ* matches the minimax rate of Caponnetto & De Vito [2007], confirming tightness. The scaling law exponents in Theorem 4.1 are consistent with the linear-model analyses of Bahri et al. [2024] and Maloney et al. [2022], and correspond one-to-one with the rates in Atanasov et al. [2024], but extend them to infinite-dimensional features without requiring a specific asymptotic limit in d.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the dimension-free deterministic equivalent framework from ridge regression (Cheng & Montanari, 2022) and kernel ridge regression (Misiakiewicz & Saeed, 2024) to the random feature setting, unifying a long list of asymptotic formulas that previously required proportional or polynomial scaling of n, p, and d. It also builds on the random features generalization literature of Rudi & Rosasco (2017) and Caponnetto & De Vito (2007), providing a tight characterization of the minimum number of features p* needed to achieve minimax optimal rates—closing a gap where prior work only gave an upper bound. The key methodological departure is that all prior RFRR results were derived in the asymptotic limit of large data dimension, whereas this paper's guarantees are non-asymptotic and independent of the feature map dimension, allowing infinite-dimensional features.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dimension-free deterministic equivalent provides a single closed-form expression that recovers all prior asymptotic results as special limits, offering a unified and rigorous tool for analyzing RFRR generalization without committing to a specific scaling regime. The tight result for p* (strictly smaller than the Rudi–Rosasco lower bound) resolves a long-standing open question about the computational cost of achieving minimax optimal rates. However, the concentration assumption (Assumption 3.1) excludes common non-linear settings such as Gaussian inputs with non-linear activations, and the authors acknowledge that relaxing this condition—e.g., via the approach in Misiakiewicz & Saeed (2024)—is an important direction for future work. Empirically, the deterministic equivalent appears accurate well beyond the theoretical assumptions, suggesting the framework may be broadly applicable.
