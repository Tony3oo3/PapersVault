## Problem

Existing co-speech gesture synthesis methods face a fundamental efficiency-quality tradeoff: RNNs suffer from long-term forgetting, Transformers scale quadratically with sequence length, and diffusion models incur high computational cost from iterative sampling. While selective state space models (Mamba) offer linear-time sequence modeling, directly applying them to gesture synthesis produces jittery outputs and causes limb movements across different body parts to average out, making them unsuitable for holistic full-body animation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240633/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes multi-modal speech features—CNN-extracted audio features (from amplitude, raw waveform, and Wav2Vec2CTC decoded tokens), FastText word embeddings for text, and speaker ID embeddings—fused via learned gating weights. It produces per-frame holistic 3D motion: 55 pose joints in Rot6D representation, 100 FLAME facial parameters, 4 foot contact labels, and 3 global translation values, covering face, hands, upper body, and lower body simultaneously. The VQ-VAE codebooks compress the temporal dimension (P frames per latent unit), reducing the sequence length the SSM must process.

## Method

MambaTalk employs a two-stage strategy. In Stage 1, multiple CNN-based VQ-VAEs are trained with velocity and acceleration losses to learn discrete motion codebooks for face, hands, upper body, and lower body separately, establishing motion priors that suppress jittering. In Stage 2, a speech-conditioned SSM operates in the quantized latent space: a global scan module fuses audio and text features through multi-head self-attention with learnable queries and Mamba temporal modeling, while local scan modules apply separate Mamba layers (preceded by self-attention for body parts) to each body region, with multi-head cross-attention allowing queries to extract relevant speech information. The key architectural insight is the hybrid global-to-local scanning: global features provide cross-body coordination while local Mamba modules preserve the distinct deformation magnitudes and periodicities of each body part, avoiding the averaging problem of a single unified SSM. Training combines MSE latent reconstruction loss with NLL code classification loss to encourage both accuracy and diversity.

## Results

On BEAT2, MambaTalk achieves the lowest FGD (5.366×10⁻¹) and highest Beat Constancy (7.812×10⁻¹) among all holistic gesture synthesis methods, outperforming EMAGE (FGD 5.512, BC 7.724) and TalkShow (FGD 6.209, BC 6.947). For facial motion, MambaTalk reduces vertex MSE by 18.11% (6.289×10⁻⁷ vs. EMAGE's 7.680×10⁻⁷) and LVD by 8.72% (6.897×10⁻⁵ vs. 7.556×10⁻⁵). On the BEAT dataset, MambaTalk achieves FGD 51.3, substantially outperforming CaMN (123.7) and TalkShow (91.0), while also attaining the highest SRGR (0.256) and BeatAlign (0.852).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MambaTalk outperforms the strongest holistic baseline EMAGE [32] on FGD (5.366 vs. 5.512), BC (7.812 vs. 7.724), MSE (6.289 vs. 7.680, a 18.11% reduction), and LVD (6.897 vs. 7.556, an 8.72% reduction), while matching its Diversity (13.05 vs. 13.06). Against TalkShow [68], MambaTalk improves FGD by 13.5% (5.366 vs. 6.209) and BC by 12.6% (7.812 vs. 6.947). Among non-facial baselines, MambaTalk's FGD of 5.366 substantially surpasses CaMN [33] (6.644) and DiffStyleGesture [63] (8.811), though those methods do not produce facial output. On the BEAT dataset, MambaTalk nearly halves the FGD of CaMN (51.3 vs. 123.7) and beats TalkShow (51.3 vs. 91.0), while also achieving the best SRGR (0.256 vs. 0.239) and BeatAlign (0.852 vs. 0.840). The user study confirms these gains: MambaTalk scores 4.08/5 on average versus EMAGE's 3.99 and CaMN's 3.29, with the largest margin on smoothness (4.35 vs. 4.21 and 3.50 respectively).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper is the first to apply selective state space models (Mamba) to co-speech gesture synthesis, departing from the dominant paradigms of RNNs (long-term forgetting), Transformers (quadratic complexity), and diffusion models (iterative sampling cost). It builds on the VQ-VAE discrete motion quantization framework from QPGesture and EMAGE, but introduces a two-stage strategy where discrete motion priors serve as a bridge to stabilize the otherwise jittery SSM outputs. The hybrid global-to-local scan architecture, combining multi-head attention with selective scanning for different body parts, is a novel architectural contribution not present in prior gesture synthesis or SSM literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ~33× inference speedup over diffusion-based methods (0.019s vs 0.64s per second of gesture) makes real-time, interactive gesture generation feasible, addressing a key bottleneck for applications in virtual reality, robotics, and digital humans. However, the SOTA improvement over EMAGE on BEAT2 is modest (FGD 5.366 vs 5.512), and the separate-module design for different body parts introduces architectural complexity and residual latency. Open questions include whether a unified SSM can capture all body parts simultaneously, how to better leverage pre-trained audio encoders (Whisper excels for face, Wav2Vec2 for body), and how to address cross-cultural gesture diversity.
