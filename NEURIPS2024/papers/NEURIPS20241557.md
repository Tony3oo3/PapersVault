## Problem

For general (biased) halfspaces under Gaussian marginals, it was open whether pool-based active learning could achieve non-trivial label complexity improvements over passive learning. The paper resolves this by proving an information-theoretic lower bound showing that beating the passive rate of Õ(d/ε) requires an exponentially large pool (2^poly(d)), and then demonstrates that the stronger membership-query model circumvents this barrier with near-optimal query complexity.

## Input / Output

The lower bound considers a fixed pool S of m i.i.d. Gaussian points with hidden labels from a p-biased halfspace, where the learner adaptively selects points from S to reveal (label queries). The positive result operates in the membership-query model, where the learner can query y(x) at any point x ∈ ℝ^d it chooses, with error parameter ε and confidence δ. The output is a halfspace sign(ŵ·x + t̂) achieving error O(opt + ε) in the agnostic setting, where opt is the 0-1 loss of the optimal halfspace and p is its bias.

## Method

The lower bound encodes any active learner as a decision tree and shows that when m < 2^d, every k-tuple of pool points is nearly orthogonal (‖AAᵀ − dI‖₂ ≤ O(d/t*²)), so a random p-biased halfspace labels all k points negative with probability only O(p log(1/p))^k, forcing Õ(d/(p log(m))) queries to find d negative examples. The positive algorithm (Theorem 1.2) first estimates p up to a constant factor, grid-searches over the threshold t*, then uses a smoothed-label technique to obtain a warm-start direction w₀ with Õ(1/p + d log(1/ε)) queries. Refinement proceeds via projected gradient descent where each round constructs a random gradient Gᵢ = proj_{wᵢ⊥} z·y(Aᵢ^{1/2}z − t̃wᵢ) using a localization (rejection-sampling) transform that converts the problem into estimating a Chow-Parameter with constant-length signal, achieving geometric convergence in Õ(d·polylog(1/ε)) total queries.

## Results

The paper establishes two main theoretical results. The lower bound (Theorem 1.1) shows that any active learner for general halfspaces under N(0,I) requires label complexity of Ω̃(d/(p·log(m))) over a pool of m unlabeled examples, meaning that to beat the passive complexity of Õ(d/ε), the pool must be exponentially large (2^poly(d)). The upper bound (Theorem 1.2) provides a computationally efficient agnostic learner with membership queries achieving query complexity Õ(min{1/p, 1/ε} + d·polylog(1/ε)) with error O(opt + ε), where p is the bias of the optimal halfspace. Together, these results characterize the query complexity landscape and establish a strong separation between the label-query (active) and membership-query models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical baselines or experimental comparisons. The central 'comparison' is between two query models: pool-based active learning (label queries) versus PAC learning with membership queries. The paper shows that active learning with label queries cannot achieve non-trivial savings over passive learning for general halfspaces unless the unlabeled pool is exponentially large (2^poly(d)), while membership queries achieve the near-optimal query complexity of Õ(min{1/p, 1/ε} + d·polylog(1/ε)) even in the agnostic setting. The lower bound is information-theoretic (applies to all algorithms), while the upper bound is computationally efficient (poly(d,M) time). The paper also notes that the Õ(d·polylog(1/ε)) term is near-optimal due to the d·log(1/ε) bits needed to describe a halfspace, and the min{1/p, 1/ε} overhead is unavoidable in the agnostic setting by reduction from [HKL20].

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the classical study of halfspace learning from the homogeneous to the general (biased) case in interactive query models. The algorithmic side builds directly on the localization and (v,s,σ)-rejection technique of Diakonikolas, Kane, and Stewart (STOC 2018) for the gradient construction, and on the smoothed-label method from Diakonikolas et al. (FOCS 2024) for initialization. The lower bound for active learning departs from prior sphere-based analyses (Dasgupta 2005, Hopkins-Kane-Lovett 2020) by exploiting the near-orthogonality of Gaussian samples in high dimensions to show that pool-based active learning fundamentally cannot overcome the 1/p barrier without an exponentially large pool.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clean, near-tight characterization of the query complexity landscape for general halfspaces under Gaussian marginals, establishing a strong separation between pool-based active learning and membership-query learning. The negative result—that active learning offers no non-trivial advantage over passive learning for general halfspaces unless the pool is exponentially large—resolves a long-standing open question and redirects attention to the MQ model as the appropriate framework for query-efficient learning of biased halfspaces. Open questions include whether the d·polylog(1/ε) term can be improved to d·log(1/ε), and whether similar separations hold for other concept classes or distributions.
