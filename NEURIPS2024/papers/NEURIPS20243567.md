## Problem

Language models trained with left-to-right next-token prediction suffer from the reversal curse: they cannot retrieve information when probed in a different token order than encountered during training. This paper reframes the reversal curse as a more fundamental 'factorization curse'—a failure to model the same joint distribution under different factorizations—and demonstrates through controlled and realistic experiments that scale, reversed-token augmentation, and fixed-rate bidirectional training all fail to resolve it.

## Input / Output

The method operates on variable-length token sequences: synthetic key-value pairs, Wikipedia passages with entity-relation annotations (WikiReversal), or graph edge lists with start/end nodes (Star Graph Task). During MLM-U training, a masking rate is uniformly sampled from U(0,1) per sequence, so any subset of tokens can serve as the prediction target conditioned on the remaining context, including multi-token entities of arbitrary length. At inference, all models generate tokens autoregressively left-to-right, producing entity answers for QA or path node sequences for the planning task.

## Method

The paper formalizes the factorization curse: a model trained with a specific factorization order σ₀ (e.g., left-to-right AR) assigns different joint probabilities under a different factorization σ, even though the chain rule guarantees they should be equal. The proposed solution, MLM-U (Uniform-Rate Masked Language Modeling), averages the autoregressive loss over all D! permutations by uniformly sampling the masking rate r ~ U(0,1), making the objective invariant to which tokens are context versus prediction. This is shown to be mathematically equivalent to a discrete diffusion model with an absorbing masking state (derived from Kitouni et al., 2024) and to Permutation Language Modeling in the two-token case. The critical practical difference from standard MLM is the uniformly sampled masking rate rather than a fixed 15%, ensuring the model encounters all possible context lengths and entity spans. An encoder-decoder architecture with GPT-like blocks, RoPE positional encoding, and cross-attention implements MLM-U, with inference performed in standard left-to-right autoregressive fashion.

## Results

On the controlled retrieval task, AR achieves 100% forward but 0% backward exact-match accuracy, while MLM-U achieves 100% on both directions. On the more realistic WikiReversal benchmark (100M-parameter models trained from scratch), MLM-U attains 11% forward and 7.9% backward accuracy, outperforming AR (14%/4.3%) and MLM (3.4%/2.7%), and approaching Mistral 7B's 21%/5.2% despite being 70× smaller. On the Star Graph planning task, MLM-U reaches 100% accuracy versus 50% for AR (chance level). On BioS, MLM-U achieves 68% backward retrieval where AR and AR w/reverse both score 0%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against standard autoregressive training (GPT-2, Radford et al. 2019), MLM-U wins decisively on backward retrieval across all tasks: 100% vs. 0% on the retrieval task, 68% vs. 0% on BioS, and 7.9% vs. 4.3% on WikiReversal, while matching AR's forward performance (100% vs. 100% on retrieval). Against AR w/reverse (Golovneva et al. 2024), MLM-U avoids the 44% incorrect-inference rate that entity reversal without delimiters produces on the relationship task, and generalizes without requiring entity-level parsing. Against fixed-rate MLM/BERT (Devlin et al. 2019), MLM-U achieves 100% vs. 21–22% on the retrieval task and 68% vs. 0% backward on BioS, demonstrating that bidirectionality alone is insufficient without variable masking rates. Against the much larger Mistral 7B pretrained model (Jiang et al. 2023), the 100M-parameter MLM-U model trained from scratch achieves comparable backward accuracy (7.9% vs. 5.2%) and, with different train/test seeds for forward and backward questions, substantially outperforms it (46% vs. 9.0% backward), suggesting factorization-agnostic training extracts more knowledge from the same data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reframes the reversal curse (Berglund et al., 2023; Allen-Zhu & Li, 2023) as a more general 'factorization curse' rooted in the learning objective rather than data ordering, departing from prior mitigation strategies that relied on token-level or entity-level data augmentation (Golovneva et al., 2024). The proposed MLM-U objective is derived from the discrete diffusion formulation of Kitouni et al. (2024) and Austin et al. (2023), and generalizes the permutation language modeling of XLNet (Yang et al., 2020) by averaging over all factorizations rather than only the last few tokens. The paper positions itself against both the AR (GPT-style) and fixed-rate MLM (BERT-style) paradigms, showing that neither resolves the underlying factorization dependency.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that scale, reversed-token training, and naive bidirectional attention all fail to resolve the reversal curse challenges the common assumption that data augmentation or model size will fix knowledge retrieval failures, pointing instead to the training objective as the bottleneck. MLM-U provides a concrete, implementable factorization-agnostic objective that works across synthetic and semi-realistic settings, and its connection to planning (star graph task) suggests broader implications beyond retrieval. However, the realistic WikiReversal results with 100M-parameter models remain modest in absolute terms (11% forward, 7.9% backward), and the acknowledged optimization difficulty and delayed generalization of MLM-U leave open the question of how to scale factorization-agnostic training to the multi-billion-parameter regime where current LLMs operate.
