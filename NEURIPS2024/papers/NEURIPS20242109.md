## Problem

Fine-tuning zero-shot foundation models like CLIP distorts pretrained features and degrades out-of-distribution robustness. Existing robust fine-tuning methods (FLYP, WiSE-FT, LP-FT) aim to preserve pretrained features in general but are indifferent to *which* features matter, while empirical risk minimization tends to exploit non-core (spurious) features even when core features are clear. The paper asks how to selectively preserve the core features that essentially define target classes during fine-tuning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242109/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes standard RGB images (processed with CLIP's standard resize/crop/normalize pipeline) and produces a class label probability distribution. A key detail is that the WRM supervision signal is pre-computed before fine-tuning: GPT-4-generated concept descriptions (short sentences describing core visual features per class) are encoded by the frozen pretrained CLIP text encoder, and image-text affinities are min-max normalized per class to yield soft labels. At inference, the final prediction is a weighted mixture of two classifiers induced by different prompt sets, with the mixing weight β = 1/(1+λ) tied to the training hyperparameter.

## Method

DRM formulates fine-tuning as a dual objective: min_θ R_s(θ; T^df) + λ R_s^c(θ; T^cd), where the ERM term uses default prompts (e.g., 'an image of [class]') with the FLYP contrastive loss, and the WRM term uses LLM-generated concept descriptions as prompts with soft-label cross-entropy. The concept descriptions are crafted via a GPT-4 prompt that explicitly excludes context, function, and environment, focusing solely on visual appearance. To mitigate artifact terms in the raw affinities, a per-class min-max normalization is applied before constructing the soft labels, ensuring balanced probability mass across classes. The theoretical grounding comes from showing that the risk with respect to core-feature-based predictions serves as a valid proxy for worst-case risk over all domains, and that strong duality reduces the idealized constrained problem to this unconstrained dual form. At inference, the two classifiers (default-prompt and concept-description) are combined via a convex mixture, which empirically outperforms either alone on both ID and OOD metrics.

## Results

On CLIP ViT-L/14@336 with WiSE-FT, DRM achieves 77.1% average OOD accuracy on ImageNet (vs. 75.9% for FLYP+WiSE-FT), 51.8% macro F1 on iWildCam (vs. 47.1%), and 53.1% worst-region accuracy on FMoW (vs. 50.7%), setting new state-of-the-art on all three benchmarks. On the smaller CLIP ViT-B/16 without WiSE-FT, DRM attains 5.0%, 12.4%, and 11.1% relative OOD improvements over FLYP on ImageNet, iWildCam, and FMoW respectively, while maintaining comparable ID accuracy. The method is relatively insensitive to the balancing hyperparameter λ, maintaining high OOD performance across λ ∈ {1, 2, 3, 4, 5}.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DRM consistently outperforms FLYP (the primary baseline) on OOD across all datasets and model sizes: on CLIP ViT-B/16 without WiSE-FT, DRM improves OOD by +3.0 on ImageNet (63.2 vs. 60.2), +4.4 on iWildCam (40.0 vs. 35.6), and +4.6 on FMoW (45.9 vs. 41.3), while ID accuracy remains within 0.6 points. Against FLYP+WiSE-FT on the largest model (ViT-L/14@336), DRM+WiSE-FT achieves 77.1 vs. 75.9 on ImageNet OOD, 51.8 vs. 47.1 on iWildCam OOD, and 53.1 vs. 50.7 on FMoW OOD. DRM also substantially outperforms older baselines L2-SP and LP-FT on OOD (e.g., 63.2 vs. 57.9 and 60.5 on ImageNet). Among recent concurrent methods (CLIPood, TPGM, LipSum-FT, CaRot), DRM leads on OOD by 3.4–4.6 points on iWildCam and 4.6–6.3 points on FMoW, confirming that its targeted preservation of core features via concept descriptions provides a clear advantage over generic parameter-regularization approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

DRM builds directly on FLYP (Goyal et al., 2023), adopting its fine-tuning setup (jointly updating image and text encoders with CLIP contrastive loss) such that DRM reduces exactly to FLYP when the WRM weight λ=0. The key conceptual departure is the addition of a worst-case risk minimization term, drawing from the domain generalization literature (IRM, GroupDRO), but circumventing their requirement for multi-domain training data by using LLM-generated concept descriptions to construct soft labels that proxy the worst-case risk. This positions DRM as more targeted than prior robust fine-tuning methods (LP-FT, WiSE-FT, L2-SP) that preserve all pre-trained features indiscriminately, whereas DRM specifically preserves core features that define the target classes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DRM establishes a practical recipe for robust fine-tuning that is easy to adopt on top of existing FLYP pipelines, with consistent 5%+ relative OOD improvements across ImageNet, iWildCam, and FMoW at multiple model scales. The finding that LLM-generated concept descriptions serve as reliable proxies for worst-case risk—validated by quantitative affinity studies on Hard ImageNet—opens a path toward incorporating causal/structural knowledge into fine-tuning without requiring multi-domain data. Open questions include whether the approach extends to non-classification tasks (detection, segmentation), the theoretical characterization of when concept descriptions faithfully capture core features, and whether the LLM dependency introduces a new failure mode for domains where visual core features are hard to verbalize.
