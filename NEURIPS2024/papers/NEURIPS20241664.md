## Problem

Existing vision-language models rely on pre-trained vision encoders (e.g., CLIP) that impose fixed resolution, aspect ratio, and semantic prior constraints, creating deployment overhead and capacity-matching difficulties between the vision and language components. While encoder-free VLMs like Fuyu-8B bypass these constraints, they suffer from slow convergence and large performance gaps due to the difficulty of learning visual perception from scratch within a unified decoder. This paper identifies and addresses two core obstacles: establishing stable vision-language representation unity inside a single decoder, and preserving fine-grained visual recognition capability without an explicit encoder.

## Input / Output

EVE accepts RGB images at arbitrary resolution and aspect ratio (longest edge up to 672px for standard, 1344px for HD) without any resizing, padding, or tiling operations, alongside free-form text instructions. Images are encoded through a lightweight patch embedding layer (convolution with stride 14, average pooling with factor 2, and local cross-attention) rather than a deep vision encoder, producing patch tokens that are concatenated with text embeddings in a single decoder. The model outputs free-form text responses including captions, VQA answers, and instruction-following dialogue, with a special <SPL> newline token inserted at each row boundary to preserve 2D spatial structure.

## Method

EVE-7B is a decoder-only VLM initialized from Vicuna-7B, augmented with a Patch Embedding Layer (PEL) that transmits images nearly losslessly via convolution, average pooling, and two cross-attention mechanisms (local receptive-field CA1 and holistic CLS-token CA2), and a Patch Aligning Layer (PAL) that hierarchically aggregates intermediate features across L/4 layers and aligns them to CLIP-ViT-L/14 patch outputs via MSE loss. Training follows three stages: (1) LLM-guided pre-alignment where only PEL and PAL are trainable against a frozen Vicuna-7B on 16M image-text pairs, (2) generative pre-training with the full model unfrozen on 33M pairs using both cross-entropy text loss and MSE patch-alignment loss, and (3) supervised fine-tuning on 665K–1.8M instruction data. The critical insight is that Stage 1 prevents catastrophic collapse and optimization interference when transitioning an LLM into a VLM, while PAL implicitly distills the vision encoder's fine-grained perception into the decoder without requiring it at inference time.

## Results

EVE-7B (HD), trained with only 35M publicly accessible samples, achieves 78.6 on VQAv2, 62.6 on GQA, 51.1 on VizWiz, 56.8 on TextVQA, 85.0 on POPE, 1305.7 on MME, 52.3 on MMBench, 56.8 on SEED-Bench, and 25.7 on MM-Vet. These scores are competitive with encoder-based VLMs of similar capacity (e.g., LLaVA-1.5: 78.5/62.0/50.0/58.2/85.9/1510.7/64.3/58.6/30.5) while using a simpler decoder-only architecture with no vision encoder. The standard EVE-7B (672px max edge) scores 75.4/60.8/41.8/51.9/83.6/1217.3/49.5/54.3/25.6, while the HD variant (1344px) closes most of the gap to encoder-based models. Inference latency drops from 0.13s (LLaVA-1.6 HD vision part) to 0.013s (EVE-7B HD vision part), an order-of-magnitude speedup.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EVE-7B (HD) significantly outperforms the only other encoder-free model, Fuyu-8B (HD), across every shared benchmark: VQAv2 78.6 vs 74.2, POPE 85.0 vs 74.1, MME 1305.7 vs 728.6, MMBench 52.3 vs 10.7, and MM-Vet 25.7 vs 21.4. Against encoder-based VLMs, EVE-7B (HD) is within 1–3 points on VQAv2 (78.6 vs LLaVA-1.5's 78.5, InternVL-Chat's 79.3, mPLUG-Owl2's 79.4) and GQA (62.6 vs LLaVA-1.5's 62.0, LVIS-4V's 62.6), but trails on MME (1305.7 vs 1510.7 for LLaVA-1.5), MMBench (52.3 vs 64.3), and MM-Vet (25.7 vs 30.5), which the authors attribute to language capability degradation from extensive vision-only pre-training. EVE-7B (HD) outperforms InstructBLIP, IDEFICS-9B, and QwenVL-Chat on most available metrics, and is competitive with LVIS-4V and ShareGPT4V on VQAv2/GQA but lags on TextVQA (56.8 vs 58.7/60.4) and MMBench. The standard EVE-7B (non-HD) lags further behind encoder-based models, particularly on VizWiz (41.8 vs 50.0 for LLaVA-1.5) and MMBench (49.5 vs 64.3), highlighting the importance of high-resolution fine-tuning data for closing the gap.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the encoder-free VLM concept first demonstrated by Fuyu-8B (Adept AI), which showed that a pure decoder-only architecture can process raw image patches without a dedicated vision encoder. However, Fuyu-8B's training procedure and data were undisclosed, and its performance was mediocre. EVE departs from both encoder-based VLMs (LLaVA, InternVL, mPLUG-Owl2) and the opaque Fuyu-8B by providing a transparent three-stage training recipe: LLM-guided pre-alignment with a frozen Vicuna-7B backbone, generative pre-training with visual supervision via a Patch Aligning Layer that distills CLIP features, and supervised fine-tuning. The key conceptual departure is treating the LLM as the central pivot and 'implicitly' compressing a vision encoder's inductive bias into the decoder through hierarchical patch-feature alignment, rather than relying on an explicit encoder at inference time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

EVE demonstrates that encoder-free VLMs can rival encoder-based counterparts of similar capacity using only 35M publicly available samples, offering a transparent and reproducible training recipe that the field lacked. The practical efficiency gains are substantial: image encoding is ~10× faster, and arbitrary resolution/aspect-ratio inputs are natively supported without tiling or distortion. Key limitations remain: EVE-7B still trails SOTA encoder-based models (e.g., LLaVA-1.6 HD, ShareGPT4V) on several benchmarks, and extensive vision-language pre-training degrades linguistic capability (SQA drops from 65.3% to 63.0%). Open questions include whether scaling LLM capacity and data will close or reverse the gap, how to mitigate catastrophic forgetting of language knowledge, and whether the LLM-centric pre-alignment strategy generalizes to additional modalities (audio, video, depth).
