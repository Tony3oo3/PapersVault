## Problem

Zero-shot change detection—detecting class-agnostic changes in bitemporal remote sensing images without any training on change detection tasks and generalizing to unseen change types and geographic distributions—remains an open problem. Existing deep change detection models are confined to the specific scenarios and data distributions seen during training, and while SAM demonstrates strong zero-shot generalization for single-image segmentation, adapting it to bitemporal change detection without the prohibitively expensive large-scale bitemporal change annotations required for promptable training has not been explored.

## Input / Output

The method takes a bitemporal RGB remote sensing image pair (pre-event and post-event) and optionally a set of single-temporal point queries (user clicks on objects of a target category). It produces pixel-level binary change masks, instance-level change masks as polygons, and per-instance change confidence scores. Three application modes are supported: fully automatic (top-k or threshold-based selection), semi-automatic (user-specified angle threshold), and interactive (point queries to filter class-agnostic masks into object-centric changes). The image embeddings are demodulated by removing the affine transformation of SAM's final layer normalization to ensure constant L2 norm, which is critical for the cosine-similarity-based matching.

## Method

AnyChange is built on SAM via a training-free adaptation called bitemporal latent matching, which exploits empirically discovered intra-image and inter-image semantic similarities in SAM's latent space for satellite imagery. For each object proposal generated by SAM's grid-point prompting, a mask embedding is computed by averaging the image embedding over the proposal's spatial extent; because SAM's encoder uses layer normalization, these embeddings lie on a hypersphere of fixed radius √d_m, making negative cosine similarity a natural change confidence score. The key design is bidirectional matching: each proposal from time t is projected onto the t+1 image embedding and vice versa, guaranteeing temporal symmetry of the class-agnostic change signal. A point query mechanism further enables object-centric detection by averaging mask embeddings from user-clicked points and filtering all proposals via cosine similarity against this semantic anchor, converting class-agnostic change masks into changes centered on a specific object category.

## Results

On the SECOND benchmark for zero-shot change detection, AnyChange (ViT-B) achieves 44.6% F1, outperforming the strongest baseline DINOv2+CVA (ViT-G/14) by 3.2% F1. For instance-level zero-shot change proposals, AnyChange (ViT-B) attains mask AR@1000 of 32.6 (LEVIR-CD), 48.3 (S2Looking), 27.8 (xView2), and 27.0 (SECOND), substantially exceeding SAM+Mask Match (6.8–10.2) and SAM+CVA Match (9.0–12.2). In the unsupervised change detection setting, AnyChange (ViT-B) pseudo-labels + ChangeStar(1×256) set a new SOTA of 48.2% F1 on SECOND, surpassing the previous best I3PE (43.8%) by 4.4%. For label-efficient supervised change detection on S2Looking, AnyChange pseudo-labels yield 40.2% F1 with only 3,500 annotated pixels (~10⁻⁵% of full annotations), outperforming models trained on 1% GT (37.2% F1) and 0.1% GT (9.2% F1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

AnyChange (ViT-B) outperforms the strongest zero-shot baseline DINOv2+CVA (ViT-G/14, 1.1B params) on SECOND F1 (44.6% vs. 41.4%) using only 86M parameters, and achieves comparable or better recall across all four datasets. Against the two SAM-based baselines (SAM+Mask Match and SAM+CVA Match), AnyChange wins by large margins on instance-level mask AR (e.g., 32.6 vs. 6.8 and 9.0 on LEVIR-CD; 48.3 vs. 15.1 and 31.5 on S2Looking), confirming that semantic latent matching is far more effective than geometric IoU or pixel-wise CVA for change detection. In the unsupervised setting, AnyChange (ViT-B) pseudo-labels + ChangeStar(1×256) achieves 48.2% F1 on SECOND, beating the previous SOTA I3PE (43.8%) by 4.4% and DINOv2+CVA (41.4%) by 6.8%. For label-efficient supervised change detection on S2Looking, AnyChange pseudo-labels (40.2% F1, 3,500 pixels) surpass models trained on 1% ground truth (37.2% F1) and 0.1% GT (9.2% F1), though they remain below the 100% GT upper bound (66.3% F1). The point query mechanism adds ~15% F1 over class-agnostic AnyChange on building-centric datasets (e.g., 38.6% vs. 23.0% on LEVIR-CD), at the cost of reduced recall, demonstrating effective interactive object-centric filtering.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on SAM (Segment Anything Model) but departs from prior SAM-based change detection work (e.g., SAM-CD) that relied on parameter-efficient fine-tuning with supervised change labels. Instead of any training, the authors empirically discover that SAM's latent space—trained exclusively on natural images—already encodes intra-image and inter-image semantic similarities for satellite imagery, enabling a training-free bitemporal latent matching procedure. Conceptually, the work extends SAM's zero-shot generalization from single-image tasks (edge detection, object proposal) to the bitemporal setting, and positions zero-shot change detection as strictly harder than unsupervised change detection (CVA, I3PE) because it additionally requires generalization to unseen data distributions without retraining.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By framing zero-shot change detection as a new problem and showing that a training-free adaptation of SAM can address it, this paper removes the need for expensive bitemporal change annotations and opens a path toward foundation-model-style generalization in Earth observation. The practical impact is significant: AnyChange can serve as an out-of-the-box change data engine (pseudo-labels) and achieves competitive unsupervised change detection performance (48.2% F1 on SECOND) with negligible annotation cost. Key limitations include sensitivity to radiation variation (~2% mask AR jitter), difficulty detecting tiny object changes without upsampling, and a large gap to the supervised Oracle (44.6% vs. 69.5% F1 on SECOND), suggesting the latent-space matching signal is still far from saturating. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond optical satellite imagery to SAR or hyperspectral data, and whether the discovered semantic structure in SAM's latent space is robust enough for operational deployment.
