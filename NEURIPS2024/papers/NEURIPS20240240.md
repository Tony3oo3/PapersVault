## Problem

Existing long-tailed learning methods assume the test distribution matches the training distribution and produce a single fixed head-tail trade-off, making them brittle under real-world distribution shift and unable to accommodate varying user preferences (e.g., prioritizing tail-class coverage in medical screening versus head-class accuracy in wildlife population monitoring). This paper is the first to jointly address both challenges: robustness to arbitrary test distributions and user-controllable head-tail trade-off in a single framework.

## Input / Output

The model takes standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR100-LT, 224×224 for ImageNet-LT/Places-LT/iNaturalist) and, at test time, a user-specified 3-dimensional preference vector α* on the simplex Δ³ that encodes the desired emphasis across head, middle, and tail class groups. The output is a class label prediction whose head-tail accuracy balance shifts smoothly as α* is varied, without any retraining. The preference vector is combined with a learned reference vector r via a normalized Hadamard product before being fed to the hypernetwork to regenerate expert head weights on the fly.

## Method

A shared CNN backbone (ResNet-32/50/152 or ResNeXt-50) extracts features, while a small 3-layer MLP hypernetwork h_ψ maps Dirichlet-sampled noise vectors z_i to the weight matrices of T=3 distinct classifier heads, producing diverse experts that collectively approximate the Pareto front of the multi-objective loss landscape. Training minimizes a Stochastic Convex Ensemble (SCE) objective — a log-sum-exp relaxation of the worst-case convex combination of expert losses — which encourages the experts to specialize across different distributional regimes rather than converging to a single solution. At inference, the user's preference vector α* is fused with the trained reference vector r to form r̂, which is passed through the same hypernetwork to generate new head parameters, enabling continuous, retraining-free adjustment of the head-tail trade-off. The theoretical analysis introduces the Environment Total Variation Distance (ETVD) and proves that ensembling diverse experts tightens the generalization bound compared to single-environment ERM, with the bound decomposing into average empirical risk, average train-test TVD, and a weighted ETVD term.

## Results

PRL achieves the highest top-1 accuracy across all four benchmark datasets under the standard uniform test distribution: 65.6% on CIFAR100-LT (IR=10), 57.3% (IR=50), 52.8% (IR=100), 41.6% on Places-LT, 75.1% on iNaturalist 2018, and 60.8% on ImageNet-LT. These results surpass the previous state-of-the-art methods LSC and BalPoE by 0.6–1.0 percentage points on CIFAR100-LT and by 0.3–0.6 points on the larger datasets. Under distribution-shift settings (forward/backward long-tailed test distributions), PRL maintains its lead, e.g., 72.7% vs. 72.2% (BalPoE) on ImageNet-LT forward-50 and 57.5% vs. 56.6% (BalPoE) on backward-50.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PRL outperforms all eight baselines on every dataset and every imbalance ratio tested. The closest competitors are LSC [33] and BalPoE [1], which are the prior state-of-the-art: on CIFAR100-LT (IR=100), PRL achieves 52.8% vs. BalPoE's 52.0% and LSC's 51.8%; on ImageNet-LT, PRL reaches 60.8% vs. LSC's 60.2% and BalPoE's 59.3%. The gap widens substantially against ensemble-based methods: PRL beats SADE [38] by 4.0 points on CIFAR100-LT (IR=100) and by 2.0 points on ImageNet-LT, and beats RIDE [32] by 4.8 and 4.5 points respectively. Against logit-adjusted methods (Balanced Softmax, LADE) and two-stage methods (MiSLAS), the margins are even larger (5–15 points). Notably, PRL also leads under distribution-shift test distributions (Tables 2–3), where methods like LADE with known priors (LADE√) still trail PRL by 0.5–1.5 points on ImageNet-LT, confirming that the hypernetwork-generated diverse experts provide a genuine advantage over fixed-expert ensembles.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the multi-expert paradigm for test-agnostic long-tailed recognition (RIDE, SADE, LADE) by replacing fixed, individually-trained experts with a hypernetwork that generates diverse classifier heads from sampled preference vectors drawn from a Dirichlet distribution. The hypernetwork-based Pareto front approximation is directly inherited from the controllable multi-task learning framework of Lin et al. (2020), and the Stochastic Convex Ensemble training strategy draws on smooth Chebyshev scalarization (Lin et al., ICML 2024). The key departure from SADE and RIDE is that instead of learning a fixed set of experts for predetermined distributions, PRL learns a continuous mapping from preference space to expert parameters, enabling test-time adjustment of the head-tail trade-off without retraining.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper introduces the first controllable head-tail trade-off mechanism in long-tailed learning under distribution shift, which addresses a practical gap: real-world deployment often requires different sensitivity to rare vs. common classes depending on the application (e.g., cancer screening vs. routine screening). The preference vector interface is simple and interpretable, making the approach potentially adoptable in safety-critical settings. However, the empirical gains over strong baselines (LSC, BalPoE) are modest (0.5–1% top-1), the hypernetwork substantially increases parameter count (e.g., 38M → 633M for ResNeXt-50), and the paper acknowledges training stability concerns without fully resolving them. Open questions include whether the approach scales to more than three experts, whether the Dirichlet sampling adequately covers the Pareto front in higher-dimensional preference spaces, and whether the controllability mechanism transfers to non-image modalities or open-set settings.
