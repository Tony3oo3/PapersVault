## Problem

In functional mixed models, the fixed effect function μ cannot be reliably recovered because phase (x-axis) and amplitude (y-axis) variability are confounded with additive measurement error, and topological features of μ (e.g., number of peaks) may be destroyed by the random terms. The paper asks whether a complementary geometric property of μ—its size-and-shape, invariant to a family of joint space-time transformations—can be reliably inferred instead, and whether a Bayesian framework can provide principled uncertainty quantification for this property without restrictive finite-rank covariance assumptions on the error.

## Input / Output

The method takes n discretized functions f_i observed at a common set of T time points on [0,1] (the common-discretization assumption can be relaxed to sparse/irregular sampling). It produces centered posterior samples of μ as size-and-shape representatives in equivalence classes under the norm-preserving action, from which pointwise posterior means and credible intervals are computed; it also yields posterior distributions for each object-level phase function γ_i and the variance parameters σ² and σ_c². Centering is performed by composing each posterior sample of μ with the average estimated phase function, analogous to orbit centering in elastic shape analysis.

## Method

The core model is f_i = D_{γ_i}(μ + v_i + ε_i) = [(μ + v_i + ε_i) ∘ γ_i]√γ̇_i, where D_γ is the norm-preserving (isometric) action of the Lie group Γ of orientation-preserving diffeomorphisms on L², interpreted as a unitary rotation of the Hilbert space. The fixed effect μ is expanded in a truncated modified Fourier or B-spline basis (B_f functions) and the size-and-shape-altering random effect v_i in a B-spline basis (B_r functions); the size-and-shape-preserving random effect γ_i is regularized by either a one-parameter prior (PM1) or a Dirichlet-process-based prior on phase increments (PM2). The likelihood is obtained by marginalizing out v_i, yielding a multivariate normal with covariance σ²·diag(γ̇_i) + σ_c²·Φ̃_iΦ̃_iᵀ, and posterior inference is conducted via Metropolis-Hastings MCMC with adaptive proposals that exploit the group structure of Γ for phase updates. The key insight is that the norm-preserving action rotates the chosen orthonormal basis toward the data, effectively learning a data-optimal coordinate system that better captures population-level structure than a fixed FPCA basis, while the larger equivalence class under norm-preserving (versus value-preserving) action makes the size-and-shape of μ more robust to perturbation by v_i + ε_i.

## Results

The primary evaluation metric is the integrated squared estimation error Δ_μ for the fixed effect function μ. On the three warpMix-generated test functions, the best proposed Bayesian model achieves the lowest error in all three cases: 0.0151 (Model 1-B) on μ₁, 0.0070 (Model 2-F) on μ₂, and 0.0033 (Model 2-B) on μ₃, compared to warpMix errors of 0.0179, 0.0394, and 0.0077 respectively. The most dramatic improvement is on μ₂, where Model 2-F reduces the estimation error by 82% over warpMix. On real data, the proposed model recovers two growth spurts in the Berkeley data (vs. one for warpMix) and successfully estimates the PQRST pattern where warpMix fails entirely.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two baselines: warpMix (Claeskens et al., 2021), a state-of-the-art frequentist functional mixed model using value-preserving warping, and BRFC (Cheng et al., 2016), a Bayesian registration approach using the square-root velocity transformation. Against warpMix, the proposed Bayesian models achieve the lowest estimation error in all three test function cases (μ₁, μ₂, μ₃), with the largest margin on μ₂ where Model 2-F (0.0070) reduces error by 82% relative to warpMix (0.0394). On the Berkeley growth data, the proposed model recovers two growth spurts in the posterior mean of μ while warpMix recovers only one; on PQRST complexes, warpMix fails to produce an estimate at all. Against BRFC, the proposed models dominate across all five simulation scenarios in Table 2 (Appendix A), with BRFC errors ranging from 0.0103 to 2.5738 compared to proposed model errors of 0.0033 to 0.4039. The advantage is most pronounced when data is generated from the proposed model's own norm-preserving action (PM1-F and PM2-F rows), where BRFC errors are 30–50× larger than the best proposed model.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the standard functional mixed model literature (Raket et al. 2014, Claeskens et al. 2021) that models phase variability via the value-preserving action f∘γ, by instead employing the norm-preserving action (f∘γ)√γ̇, which is an isometry (unitary transformation) of the L² Hilbert space. This shift reinterprets phase functions as data-driven rotations of a fixed orthonormal basis, drawing on the Lie group structure of the diffeomorphism group Γ and the Dirichlet process prior on phase increments from Bharath and Kurtek (2020). The conceptual framing is inspired by classical size-and-shape analysis of landmark configurations (Lele and McCulloch 2002; Dryden and Mardia 2016), where only the size-and-shape of a template is recoverable under isometric perturbations, and transplants this geometric invariant to the infinite-dimensional functional setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a fundamental identifiability limitation: under standard functional mixed models with phase variation, additive noise and random effects can irrevocably alter the topological features (number and nature of critical points) of the fixed effect μ, making pointwise recovery unreliable. By targeting the size-and-shape (invariant under the norm-preserving isometry group) rather than the function itself, the authors provide a geometrically well-posed inference target with a principled Bayesian framework. Key limitations include the absence of theoretical guarantees (acknowledged as work in progress), the computational cost of MCMC (~93–111 minutes for n=30, T=50), and the a priori specification of basis dimensions. Open questions include adaptive basis selection, extension to multivariate curves and surfaces, and whether the norm-preserving perspective can inform geometric invariance in neural network architectures for functional data.
