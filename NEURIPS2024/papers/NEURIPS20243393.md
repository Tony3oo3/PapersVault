## Problem

Depth-wise convolution (DW-conv) accounts for over 82% of DSConv inference time despite having only ~1% of parameters, yet existing pruning methods fail on it: coarse-grained structured pruning causes severe accuracy loss due to the tiny parameter count, and the GEMV execution format underutilizes GPU parallelism, rendering standard weight pruning ineffective. No prior method achieves both high pruning ratios and practical GPU speedup specifically for DW-conv.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243393/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on pre-trained DSConv-based CNNs (MobileNet-V2, MobileNet-V3, EfficientNet-B0) and produces pruned models with structured sparsity patterns aligned to GPU tile boundaries. Inference is evaluated on ImageNet (224×224 RGB) and CIFAR-10 (32×32 RGB) for image classification, with timing measured via NVIDIA CUTLASS at batch size 32 on RTX 2080 Ti. The pruned output retains the same class-label prediction interface but with a hardware-friendly zero-column structure in the DR-rearranged DW-conv weight matrices.

## Method

DEPrune's core mechanism, DCP, applies magnitude pruning to the Diagonal-wise Refactorization (DR) rearranged DW-conv weight matrix: because DR places non-zero weights diagonally with zero-padding, zeroing a single weight element eliminates an entire column, converting fine-grained element-wise pruning into structured column sparsity without the accuracy cost of channel-level pruning. Two enhancements follow: BWT groups non-zero values within each 32×k_h×k_w sub-GEMM and equalizes the pruning ratio across all sub-GEMMs to eliminate workload imbalance between SMs, and HSR recalibrates per-layer PR to align the number of pruned vectors with GPU tile size (multiples of 32), converting unaligned partial tiles into fully removed tiles for step-function speed gains. For PW-conv, DEPrune selectively applies filter pruning only to PW-conv2 (not PW-conv1) so that channel removal cascades into the next block's PW-conv1 rather than into DW-conv, preserving the fine-grained DCP treatment of depth-wise layers.

## Results

On ImageNet, DEPrune-BH achieves up to 3.74× total inference speedup on EfficientNet-B0 (6650→1775 µs) with only 0.8% top-1 accuracy drop (77.6%→76.8%), while pruning 84.7% of DW-conv and 62.0% of PW-conv parameters. On MobileNet-V3-Small, it reaches 4.12× total speedup (1857→450 µs) with 0.6% accuracy drop. On MobileNet-V2, DEPrune-BH prunes 77.9% of DW-conv and 52.7% of PW-conv parameters (56.1% FLOPs reduction) with just 0.3% accuracy loss, achieving 2.48× total speedup. The DW-conv-specific speedup ranges from 3.52× (MobileNet-V2) to 6.15× (EfficientNet-B0).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DEPrune-BH dominates all compared structured pruning methods on both pruning ratio and accuracy preservation. On MobileNet-V2, it prunes 77.9% of DW-conv parameters versus 37.1% for CafeNet-R [44] and 42.8% for GFS [52], while suffering only 0.3% accuracy drop compared to 5.5% and 3.2% respectively; its 2.48× total speedup also exceeds CafeNet-R's 1.46× and GFS's 1.60×. On EfficientNet-B0, DEPrune-BH prunes 84.7% of DW-conv (vs. ~30% for CafeNet-R/E) with 0.8% accuracy loss, achieving 3.74× total speedup versus 1.37× for CafeNet-R. On MobileNet-V3-Large, DEPrune-BH is 3.32× faster than FPGM [20] (2.83× vs. 1.47× total speedup) with 0.6% less accuracy drop (−0.3% vs. −0.9%). For PW-conv pruning specifically (Table 5), DEPrune-BH with ℓ₂-norm filter pruning [27] outperforms BCBP [37] by 0.9–1.7% accuracy across all three models, while FPGM [20] is marginally better (0.09–0.33%) on EfficientNet-B0. In the appendix comparison with NVIDIA n:m sparsity [23] at 50% PR on CIFAR-10, DEPrune-B achieves 0.31% higher accuracy (93.30% vs. 92.99%) without requiring specialized A100 hardware.

## Relation to Prior Work

DEPrune builds directly on Diagonal-wise Refactorization (DR), which rearranges DW-conv into multiple sub-GEMMs to exploit GPU parallelism, and on magnitude pruning as the core element-wise pruning operation. It departs from prior structured, balanced, and block pruning methods that were designed for standard convolutions and fail on DW-conv due to its small parameter count and GEMV structure. The key conceptual departure is recognizing that applying fine-grained (magnitude) pruning to the DR-rearranged weight matrix naturally produces structured sparsity (entire zero columns) without the accuracy cost of coarse-grained channel pruning, effectively bridging the fine-grained/structured pruning dichotomy for this specific layer type.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Since DW-conv consumes over 82% of inference time in DSConv models despite comprising only ~1% of parameters, accelerating it has outsized impact on overall latency for widely deployed architectures (MobileNet, EfficientNet). The paper's hardware-aware co-design (BWT for workload balance, HSR for tile alignment) demonstrates that GPU-specific execution details can yield 3–16% additional speedup beyond the base pruning ratio, a practical insight for deployment engineers. However, the method is explicitly limited to DW-conv and does not extend to Vision Transformers or self-attention layers, and the structured sparsity it produces requires a custom GPU kernel rather than leveraging existing sparse tensor core hardware, limiting its portability across GPU generations.
