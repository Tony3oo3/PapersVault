## Problem

Existing tensor-based multi-view subspace clustering (TMSC) methods suffer from three coupled limitations: (1) quadratic or cubic computational complexity and fragility when the observed data dictionary is incomplete or corrupted, (2) neglect of local geometric manifold structure within individual views when forming the tensor representation, and (3) uniform penalization of all tensor singular values, which over-penalizes informative large singular values while under-penalizing noise-related small ones.

## Input / Output

The method takes m view-specific feature matrices {X^v ∈ R^{d_v × n}} as input and produces cluster assignments by applying k-means to the left singular vectors of the concatenated anchor connectivity matrix Z̄ = (1/√m)[Z^1, …, Z^m] ∈ R^{n×lm}. A key design choice is that each view's subspace representation Z^v has size n×l (where l ≪ n is the number of selected anchor points) rather than the conventional n×n, yielding linear scalability in n. The anchor matrix A^v ∈ R^{d_v × l} is constrained to be orthonormal, and the projection matrix P^v ∈ R^{d_v × d_v} absorbs unobserved dictionary components.

## Method

STONE recovers a 3-D tensor Z ∈ R^{l×m×n} from view-specific anchor subspace representations using three synergistic regularizers. First, an Enhanced Anchor Dictionary (EAD) strategy selects l discriminative anchors and augments them with a latent projection term P^v X^v (inspired by LatLRR) to form the reconstruction constraint X^v = A^v(Z^v)^T + P^v X^v + E^v, reducing complexity from O(n²) to O(nl) while guarding against dictionary under-sampling. Second, a Hyperbolic Tangent Rank (HTR) defined via f(x) = tanh(δx) applied to tensor-SVD singular values provides a non-convex, variable-penalty surrogate for tensor rank that strongly suppresses near-zero singular values while saturating at 1 for large ones. Third, an Anchor Hypergraph Laplacian Regularization (AHR) term Tr(Z^v L_h^v (Z^v)^T) encodes local geometric manifold structure within each view through hypergraph Laplacian matrices built on the anchor set. The full objective is solved via ADMM with auxiliary variables S and Q^v to decouple the non-convex HTR and AHR terms, and convergence to a KKT point is guaranteed.

## Results

STONE achieves perfect or near-perfect clustering on most datasets: ACC/NMI of 1.000 on NGs, BBCSport, HW, MSRCV1, and ALOI-100; 0.977/0.962 on Scene15; and 0.994/0.984 on CIFAR-10. On the challenging Caltech101-all (9,144 samples, 102 clusters, 6 views), STONE reaches ACC 0.650 and NMI 0.862, substantially outperforming the next best method GCFAgg (ACC 0.197, NMI 0.431). In terms of efficiency, STONE runs in 6.52 s on Scene15 (4,485 samples) versus 1,279.9 s for TBGL and 639.95 s for ETLMSC, and completes ALOI-100 (10,800 samples) in 66.57 s while TBGL requires 26,109 s and several methods exceed memory limits.

## Baselines & Comparisons

STONE outperforms all 11 baselines on 7 out of 8 datasets across all five metrics (ACC, NMI, PUR, F-score, ARI). The most dramatic margin is on Scene15, where STONE (ACC 0.977) surpasses the second-best ETLMSC (ACC 0.847) by 13 percentage points, while matrix-based methods like GMC (0.140) and TBGL (0.298) struggle severely. On Caltech101-all, STONE (ACC 0.650, NMI 0.862) more than triples the performance of the next-best GCFAgg (ACC 0.197, NMI 0.431), and TBGL/ETLMSC fail entirely with out-of-memory errors. On CIFAR-10 (50,000 samples), STONE (ACC 0.994) narrowly edges out MFLVC (0.993) and GCFAgg (0.989), while GMC, MSC²D, MVCtopl, MVSCTM, ETLMSC, and TBGL all exceed memory limits. In efficiency, STONE is 196× faster than TBGL on Scene15 (6.52 s vs 1,279.9 s) and 392× faster on ALOI-100 (66.57 s vs 26,109 s), confirming the scalability advantage of the anchor dictionary design. The only dataset where STONE does not achieve a perfect score is Caltech101-all, where the high number of clusters (102) and views (6) present the greatest challenge, yet it still dominates all competitors by a wide margin.

## Relation to Prior Work

STONE extends the tensor-based multi-view subspace clustering (TMSC) lineage initiated by Xie et al. (2016) and subsequent works (Jia et al. 2021, Guo et al. 2023, Sun et al. 2023) that consolidate view-specific subspace representations into a 3D tensor with global low-rank constraints. It departs from this line in three specific ways: (1) replacing self-representation with an Enhanced Anchor Dictionary (EAD) that borrows the latent dictionary idea from LatLRR (Liu & Yan, 2011) to reduce complexity and handle under-sampled dictionaries; (2) substituting the standard Tensor Nuclear Norm with a Hyperbolic Tangent Rank (HTR) that applies non-uniform penalties to singular values; and (3) adding an anchor hypergraph Laplacian regularizer (building on Xie et al., 2020) to preserve local geometric structure within each view. The optimization follows the standard ADMM framework used throughout the LRR/TMSC literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The primary practical contribution is reducing TMSC complexity from quadratic/cubic to linear in sample size, making tensor-based multi-view clustering feasible on datasets with 50,000+ samples where prior methods run out of memory. The triple-enhancement design (EAD + HTR + AHR) is well-motivated and the ablation confirms each component contributes, though the perfect scores (1.000) on small, well-separated datasets like NGs and BBCSport limit the informativeness of those comparisons. Open questions include how STONE handles genuinely incomplete multi-view data (unlike EDISON, which targets that setting), whether the five hyperparameters can be reduced or made parameter-free, and whether the shallow tensor framework can be extended to deep feature extractors without losing the linear-scalability advantage.
