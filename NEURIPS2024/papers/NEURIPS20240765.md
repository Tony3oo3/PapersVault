## Problem

Prior work on corruption-robust linear bandits left a gap between upper and lower bounds for the weak corruption measure C_∞ (upper: d√T + dC_∞, lower: d√T + √d C_∞), and no regret bounds depending on C_∞ or C existed for adversarial linear bandits. Additionally, the optimal misspecification tolerance ρ for gap-dependent misspecification in linear bandits was unknown, with prior results requiring ρ ≤ Õ(1/d) while conjecturing ρ = Θ(1) might suffice.

## Input / Output

The algorithms require the corruption level (either C = Σ|ε_t(a_t)| or C_∞ = Σ max_a |ε_t(a)|) as a known input to set error thresholds, along with the action set A ⊂ R^d, horizon T, and confidence δ. A critical requirement is that the stochastic algorithm must sample actions from the exact G-optimal distribution p_k each round (not merely match per-action counts), making it inherently randomized—deterministic variants provably cannot achieve the √d C_∞ rate. The output is a sequence of action selections a_t with high-probability regret guarantees of the form Õ(d√T + √d C_∞) or Õ(d√T + dC).

## Method

For stochastic linear bandits, the paper introduces Randomized Phased Elimination (Algorithm 1), which at each epoch samples from a G-optimal design over the active set and eliminates actions outside a confidence threshold; the key distinction from prior elimination algorithms is the requirement to sample from the exact distribution rather than matching per-action counts, which is necessary to achieve the √d C_∞ rate. For adversarial C_∞, Algorithm 2 uses FTRL with a log-determinant barrier regularizer over lifted covariance matrices, combined with a novel bonus sequence B_t defined as the minimal positive-definite matrix dominating both B_{t-1} and Σ_t^{-1} (geometrically, the minimal ellipsoid containing both), which cancels the corruption bias with only Õ(d) overhead. For adversarial C, Algorithm 3 runs continuous exponential weights over the (d+1)×(d+1) lifted space with rejection sampling to ensure ‖a_t‖_{Σ_t^{-1}} ≤ Õ(√d), enabling the tight dC dependence. A general reduction then maps gap-dependent misspecification (|f(a) − a^Tθ| ≤ ρ·Δ(a)) to corruption, yielding the first results for gap-dependent misspecified linear MDPs, while a specialized algorithm achieves the optimal ρ = O(1/√d) threshold for linear bandits.

## Results

For stochastic linear bandits, Algorithm 1 achieves the minimax-optimal regret bound of $\tilde{O}(d\sqrt{T} + \min\{dC, \sqrt{d}\,C_\infty\})$, closing the gap between the prior $\tilde{O}(d\sqrt{T} + dC_\infty)$ upper bound and the $\Omega(d\sqrt{T} + \sqrt{d}\,C_\infty)$ lower bound. For adversarial linear bandits, the paper provides the first bounds with linear dependence on corruption: $\tilde{O}(d\sqrt{T} + \sqrt{d}\,C_\infty)$ for weak corruption (Algorithm 2) and $\tilde{O}(\sqrt{d^3 T} + dC)$ for strong corruption (Algorithm 3). For gap-dependent misspecification in linear bandits, a specialized algorithm achieves $\tilde{O}(d\sqrt{T})$ regret under the condition $\rho \leq O(1/\sqrt{d})$, improving on the prior requirement of $\rho \leq \tilde{O}(1/d)$ and resolving an open question from Liu et al. (2023a).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is against prior corruption-robust linear bandit algorithms. For stochastic linear bandits under weak corruption ($C_\infty$), the paper's Algorithm 1 achieves $\tilde{O}(d\sqrt{T} + \sqrt{d}\,C_\infty)$, matching the lower bound of Lattimore et al. (2020) and improving on the prior best upper bound of $\tilde{O}(d\sqrt{T} + dC_\infty)$ (from He et al., 2022 and others) by a factor of $\sqrt{d}$ in the corruption term. Under strong corruption ($C$), the paper matches the state-of-the-art $d\sqrt{T} + dC$ bound of He et al. (2022) and Bogunovic et al. (2021), confirming minimax optimality. For adversarial linear bandits, no prior $C_\infty$ or $C$ bounds existed; the paper provides the first, with $\tilde{O}(d\sqrt{T} + \sqrt{d}\,C_\infty)$ for weak corruption and $\tilde{O}(\sqrt{d^3 T} + dC)$ for strong corruption. The gap-dependent misspecification result with $\rho \leq O(1/\sqrt{d})$ strictly improves on Liu et al. (2023a), which required $\rho \leq \tilde{O}(1/d)$, and the paper shows via a lower bound that $\rho \approx 1/\sqrt{d}$ is the best possible, disproving their conjecture that $\rho = \Theta(1)$ might suffice.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the phased-elimination framework of Lattimore et al. (2020) and Bogunovic et al. (2021) for corruption-robust stochastic linear bandits, and on the log-determinant FTRL machinery of Zimmert and Lattimore (2022) for adversarial linear bandits. The key departure is a subtle but critical modification: prior elimination algorithms specify only per-action sampling counts per epoch, whereas Algorithm 1 requires exact distribution sampling from a G-optimal design, which is necessary to achieve the tight √d·C_∞ bound (deterministic variants provably cannot). For the adversarial setting, the paper introduces a novel inductively-constructed bonus matrix B_t that dominates all prior covariance inverses, enabling a linear-in-C_∞ regret term where prior bonus designs only yielded C_sq,∞. The gap-dependent misspecification reduction generalizes Liu et al. (2023a) from a specialized linear bandit algorithm to a black-box reduction applicable to any corruption-robust oracle, extending the setting to linear MDPs for the first time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper closes the minimax gap for corruption-robust stochastic linear bandits (matching the √d·C_∞ lower bound) and provides the first provably efficient bounds for corrupted adversarial linear bandits, a setting with no prior results. The gap-dependent misspecification reduction is broadly applicable: it immediately yields the first learnability results for linear MDPs with policy-gap-dependent misspecification, and the specialized algorithm resolves an open question from Liu et al. (2023a) by showing ρ = O(1/√d) suffices (not O(1/d)) and that ρ = Θ(1) is impossible. Open problems include achieving d√T + dC regret in the adversarial setting, tight C_∞ bounds for linear contextual bandits, and whether the strong/weak adversary separation extends to general interactive decision-making.
