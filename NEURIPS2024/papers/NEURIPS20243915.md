## Problem

While Multi-Modal In-Context Learning (MM-ICL) has achieved strong performance across diverse vision-language tasks, the underlying factors that determine its effectiveness remain largely unexplored. Existing work focuses on how to optimize MM-ICL through template design or task-specific tuning, but lacks a systematic understanding of which components of the prompting pipeline—retrieval, ordering, and prompt construction—actually drive performance and how they interact.

## Input / Output

The method takes a test query consisting of an RGB image and a textual prompt, retrieves k=3 multi-modal demonstrations (each containing an image, text input, and text output) from a same-task validation pool, and assembles them into a structured prompt with [REQUEST]/[RESPONSE] delimiters and a task-specific instruction. The output is a free-form textual response whose format varies by task: a descriptive caption, a short VQA answer, a class label, or a multi-step chain-of-thought rationale. The default 3-shot setting and in-domain retrieval constraint are held fixed across all ablation experiments to isolate individual factor effects.

## Method

The paper conducts a controlled empirical study of 20 strategies across three sequential stages of the MM-ICL pipeline, evaluated on 6 VLLMs (OpenFlamingo, Otter, Qwen-VL, GPT-4V, IDEFICS2, Gemini-Pro) over 4 tasks (captioning, VQA, classification, CoT reasoning). For demonstration retrieval, it compares single-modal (RoBERTa text, CLIP vision) versus multi-modal (BridgeTower) encoders, cosine vs. L2 similarity metrics, and selection criteria including domain relevance, visual style, and inter-modality token distance. For demonstration ordering, it isolates intra-demonstration modality sequencing (image-first vs. text-first) from inter-demonstration arrangement (similar-first, similar-last, random). For prompt construction, it ablates three instruction placements (introductory, summative, intra-demonstration), the number of shots, and the presence of explicit delimiters. Key findings are that multi-modal alignment—not parameter scale—is the primary bottleneck, that placing the image before text within each demonstration substantially outperforms text-first ordering, and that a single introductory instruction before the demonstrations consistently outperforms both summative and per-demonstration instructions.

## Results

Across 6 VLLMs (OpenFlamingo, Otter, Qwen-VL, GPT-4V, IDEFICS2, Gemini-Pro) and 4 tasks (captioning, VQA, classification, reasoning), the Multi-Modal Retriever consistently achieves the highest average score, e.g., 68.16 for Gemini-Pro vs. 61.75 (Zero-shot) and 63.75 (Random Few-shot). Increasing model parameters from 8B to >100B does not significantly close the gap between retrieval strategies, confirming that multi-modal alignment—not scale—is the bottleneck. For demonstration ordering, placing the image first (Image-Text-Text) yields the best performance, while inter-demonstration ordering (similar-first vs. similar-last vs. random) has negligible impact. For prompt construction, introductory instructions before demonstrations consistently outperform summative and intra-demonstration instructions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is an empirical study comparing retrieval, ordering, and prompt-construction strategies rather than a single proposed method against external baselines. For demonstration retrieval, the Multi-Modal Retriever (BridgeTower) outperforms the Textual Retriever (RoBERTa) and Visual Retriever (CLIP) on average across all 6 models, with the largest gains on Gemini-Pro (68.16 vs. 63.99 and 64.81) and Qwen-VL (62.22 vs. 57.69 and 60.84). Notably, the Visual Retriever beats the Textual Retriever on captioning and VQA but loses on classification and reasoning, where textual semantics dominate. For demonstration ordering, the Image-Text-Text (IOP^vt t) intra-demonstration ordering significantly outperforms Text-Image-Text and Text-Text-Image configurations, while inter-demonstration ordering (similar-first, similar-last, random) shows negligible differences. For prompt construction, the Introductory Instruction consistently improves average performance over both Summative Instruction and Intra-demonstration Instruction, which generally decrease performance. Cosine similarity outperforms L2 similarity for sample comparison, while semantic diversity provides limited benefit in MM-ICL (unlike text-only ICL). In-domain samples outperform out-of-domain samples by ~4%, and 3-shot is the optimal number of demonstrations for most tasks, with performance declining beyond 3 shots for reasoning tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the text-only ICL analysis framework (demonstration retrieval, ordering, prompt construction) to the multi-modal setting, building on the MM-ICL paradigm established by Flamingo, OpenFlamingo, and MIMIC-IT. While prior work (Baldassini et al., Chen et al.) performed isolated explorations of multi-modal retrieval, and most MM-ICL literature focused on *how to optimize* the pipeline (new templates, task-specific tuning), this paper is the first to systematically ask *what factors* govern MM-ICL effectiveness across 6 VLLMs and 20 strategies. It repurposes well-studied text-ICL variables (retriever modality, similarity metrics, diversity, ordering, instruction placement) and tests whether their known effects transfer to the multi-modal regime.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical, factor-level guide for practitioners building MM-ICL pipelines: use a multi-modal retriever, place images before text within each demonstration, and prepend an introductory instruction. The finding that multi-modal alignment—not parameter scale—is the bottleneck reframes where investment should go (better alignment in encoders and VLLMs rather than simply scaling up). Key limitations include restriction to image+text modalities, a limited set of 4 tasks, and the absence of video or multi-lingual scenarios, leaving open whether these findings generalize to richer multi-modal settings. The observation that MM-ICL is less sensitive to demonstration selection and diversity than text ICL suggests the field may be over-engineering retrieval pipelines for multi-modal settings.
