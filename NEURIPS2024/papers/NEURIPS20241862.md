## Problem

Vision Transformers process all visual tokens with equal computational emphasis, ignoring the strong spatial and spatio-temporal redundancy inherent in images and videos. While Mixture of Experts (MoE) enables conditional computation, it increases parameter count and routes all tokens through experts of identical compute, offering no mechanism to reduce per-token FLOPs. The paper asks: can we dynamically allocate varying amounts of compute to individual tokens within a fixed parameter budget, so that redundant tokens are processed cheaply while informative tokens receive full capacity?

## Input / Output

The model accepts RGB images (H×W×3) or video clips (H×W×3×T), tokenized into N tokens of dimension D via ViT or ViViT patching. At inference, a single scalar compute budget (effective capacity e_c ∈ (0,1)) is specified, and the router internally assigns each token to one of E=4 nested experts with model dimensions [D/8, D/4, D/2, D]. The final output is a class label obtained by global average pooling over token features followed by a linear classifier; for video, predictions are averaged over 8 temporal clips. Notably, the same trained model can be evaluated at different e_c values without retraining, enabling runtime-adaptive inference.

## Method

MoNE replaces each ViT/ViViT block's FFN and attention projections with a nested structure (following MatFormer) where the first D/m dimensions form a valid submodel, yielding E=4 experts with exponentially-spaced dimensions that share parameters (e_i ⊂ e_{i+1}). A single linear router at the first layer produces per-token probability distributions over the E experts, and the proposed Expert Preferred Routing (EPR) algorithm greedily assigns tokens from largest to smallest expert under capacity constraints, ensuring the most important tokens receive the most compute. The capacity distribution across experts is determined by solving a constrained optimization that incentivizes larger-model usage while maintaining entropy for flexibility. Tokens are never dropped—redundant tokens are processed by smaller nested models rather than skipped entirely—and router logits are multiplied into block outputs to enable gradient flow through the routing decision. For video, MoNE is integrated into the spatial transformer of the ViViT Factorized Encoder with frame-wise routing, while the temporal transformer retains full capacity.

## Results

On Something-Something-v2, MoNE (e_c=0.3) achieves 64.6% top-1 accuracy at 162.8G FLOPs, matching the ViViT-FE-B/16 baseline (64.4% at 376.3G FLOPs) while reducing inference latency from 129.2 ms to 65.5 ms and doubling throughput from 15.8 to 30.7 clips/sec on a single V100 GPU. On ImageNet-1K, MoNE (ViT-Ti/16) reaches 71.4% top-1 at 0.8G FLOPs, surpassing the ViT baseline (71.3% at 1.3G FLOPs). On ImageNet-21K, MoNE matches baseline accuracy with approximately 2× FLOP reduction across S/16, B/16, and L/16 model sizes, with additional gains under isoFLOPs training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MoNE outperforms MatFormer/MatViT nested models across all FLOP budgets on both ImageNet-21K and video datasets, with the gap widening at lower FLOPs where adaptive routing matters most. Against Mixture-of-Depths (MoD), which hard-skips tokens, MoNE's fuzzy routing to intermediate-sized nested experts yields consistently higher accuracy, particularly in the low-compute regime. On video (SSv2), MoNE matches the ViViT-FE-B/16 baseline accuracy (64.6% vs 64.4%) while cutting FLOPs by 2.3× (162.8G vs 376.3G) and halving wall-clock latency. On ImageNet-1K, MoNE surpasses all adaptive-computation baselines—PonderNet (66.2%), DepthAdapt (69.4%), ACT (71.0%), and A-ViT (71.0%)—achieving 71.4% top-1 at 0.8G FLOPs, the lowest compute among all compared methods. The paper also demonstrates that MoNE is complementary to Token Merging (ToMe), with combined application yielding further latency gains over ToMe applied to vanilla ViT.

## Relation to Prior Work

MoNE sits at the intersection of two research threads: nested transformer architectures (MatFormer, which derives submodels by slicing projection matrices) and Mixture-of-Experts routing (which dynamically assigns tokens to specialized subnetworks). The key departure from both is that MoNE uses nested models as the experts themselves, so the parameter count stays identical to the baseline ViT/ViViT while still enabling per-token compute allocation. Unlike Mixture of Depths, which makes a binary skip-or-process decision, MoNE routes tokens to intermediate-sized nested models, preserving information at lower compute. The Expert Preferred Routing (EPR) algorithm is a novel greedy expert-choice strategy that prioritizes larger models, ensuring the most informative tokens receive the most compute under a fixed budget.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a single trained model delivers over 2× inference FLOP and wall-clock latency reduction on video classification while matching baseline accuracy, and can be re-evaluated at different compute budgets without retraining. This makes ViT/ViViT-based models substantially more deployable in latency-constrained or resource-limited settings. Limitations include the encoder-only design (auto-regressive decoding is non-trivial), evaluation restricted to classification tasks, and the observation that performance degrades when inference capacity diverges sharply from training capacity. Open questions include extending MoNE to dense prediction tasks (detection, segmentation), combining it with other inference-time optimizations like Token Merging, and whether the approach transfers to generative or language-modeling settings.
