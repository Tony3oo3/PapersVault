## Problem

Most Latent Bayesian Optimization (LBO) methods suffer from a 'misalignment problem' where VAE reconstruction error causes a single latent vector to be associated with two different objective function values, degrading surrogate model accuracy and solution quality. Additionally, existing trust-region-based LBO methods select the trust region anchor based solely on observed objective function values, ignoring the potential of the trust region to benefit the optimization process.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242179/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes discrete string-form data (SELFIES-encoded molecules or arithmetic expressions), a black-box objective function, and a pretrained VAE (encoder q_φ and decoder p_θ) as input. It produces optimized discrete data and, critically, an aligned latent dataset of decoder triplets (x, z_inv, y) where x = p_θ(z_inv) exactly, without consuming any additional oracle calls. The inversion step uses a task-specific distance metric (normalized Levenshtein distance for strings, Tanimoto similarity for molecules) to verify reconstruction, and the method is designed as a plug-and-play module that can be inserted into any existing VAE-based LBO pipeline.

## Method

InvBO consists of two components. First, the inversion method iteratively optimizes a latent vector z via gradient descent (starting from the encoder output q_φ(x)) to minimize the distance d_X(x, p_θ(z)) using the VAE cross-entropy loss, until the decoder exactly reconstructs the target data x; this produces aligned decoder triplets without additional oracle calls, and Proposition 1 theoretically shows this reduces the upper bound of the GP prediction error within the trust region by shrinking the reconstruction gap γ. Second, the potential-aware trust region anchor selection computes a potential score for each candidate trust region by maximizing a Thompson Sampling acquisition function over sampled points within the region, rescales this score to match the dynamic range of observed objective values, and adds it to the anchor's observed score to form a final selection criterion that balances exploitation (observed value) with exploration (model-predicted potential). The full pipeline (Algorithm 3) integrates both components into CoBO: inversion is applied at dataset construction and after each VAE update, while potential-aware anchor selection replaces the standard best-score-based anchor choice at each trust region scheduling step.

## Results

On the Guacamol benchmark (500 oracle calls), CoBO+InvBO achieves a best score of 0.214±0.006 on med2 and 0.348±0.107 on valt, substantially outperforming all baselines. The most dramatic improvement is on valt, where all trust-region baselines (TuRBO-L, LOL-BO, CoBO) without InvBO fail completely (score 0.000), while with InvBO they achieve scores of 0.212, 0.171, and 0.348 respectively. On the arithmetic expression fitting task (500 oracle calls), CoBO+InvBO achieves 0.252±0.067, outperforming CoBO (0.391±0.006) and all other baselines. Across all nine benchmark tasks (Guacamol, DRD3, arithmetic fitting) and both small and large budget settings, CoBO+InvBO consistently achieves state-of-the-art performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

InvBO is presented as a plug-and-play module that consistently improves every baseline it is applied to. On the trust-region-based methods (TuRBO-L, LOL-BO, CoBO), adding InvBO yields large gains: on valt at 500 oracle calls, CoBO goes from 0.000 to 0.348, TuRBO-L from 0.000 to 0.212, and LOL-BO from 0.000 to 0.171. Among non-trust-region baselines (LS-BO, W-LBO, PG-LBO), only the inversion component (INV) is applied, and it still produces consistent improvements (e.g., W-LBO on med2 goes from 0.186 to 0.207 at 500 calls; PG-LBO on osmb from 0.771 to 0.804). Against the genetic algorithm baseline GB-GA, CoBO+InvBO achieves superior best scores on all four Guacamol tasks shown in Figure 4. The paper also validates PAS (potential-aware anchor selection) on standard BO: applying PAS to TuRBO on the 40-dimensional Ackley function consistently improves optimization over vanilla TuRBO, confirming the anchor selection component generalizes beyond LBO.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the trust-region-based latent Bayesian optimization (LBO) framework established by TuRBO (Eriksson et al., 2019) and adapted to structured inputs by LOL-BO (Maus et al., 2022) and CoBO (Lee et al., 2023). It identifies a previously overlooked 'misalignment problem' in VAE-based LBO—where encoder triplets associate a latent vector with the wrong objective value due to reconstruction error—and addresses it by importing the optimization-based inversion concept from GAN and diffusion model literature. The potential-aware anchor selection departs from the standard objective-value-based anchor choice in TuRBO/LOL-BO/CoBO by incorporating the surrogate model's predicted potential within each trust region, making it a drop-in improvement rather than a new optimization framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The misalignment problem is a fundamental data-quality issue that silently degrades surrogate model accuracy across all VAE-based LBO methods, and the inversion-based fix eliminates the costly recentering oracle calls that prior methods (LOL-BO, CoBO) required to work around it. The plug-and-play design means practitioners can immediately improve existing LBO pipelines without architectural changes. However, the approach's effectiveness is bounded by the quality of the underlying generative model (VAE), and the inversion step adds per-iteration computational overhead (up to 1000 gradient steps per data point). Open questions include whether the method extends naturally to non-VAE generative models (e.g., diffusion-based molecular generators) and whether the Lipschitz continuity assumptions in Proposition 1 hold for highly non-smooth molecular property functions.
