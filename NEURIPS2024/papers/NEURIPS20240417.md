## Problem

Federated learning on heterogeneous edge devices suffers from two coupled challenges: low-capability clients fail to complete local training (system heterogeneity), and clients collect data from different domains causing domain shifts. Existing model-compression FL frameworks assume a uniform data domain and are prone to overfitting, while domain-generalization FL methods ignore client resource constraints. No prior framework addresses both challenges simultaneously.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240417/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each client receives the global model (ResNet10 for Digits, ResNet18 for Office Caltech) and a local image dataset (1% of Digits, 20% of Office Caltech) from one of four domains. The client produces a personalized pruned local model whose footprint is determined by a client-specific pruning ratio (20%–80% depending on device capability level), and the server aggregates these heterogeneous-structure models into a single global model that outputs class labels. The pruned models use channel-level structural pruning, making them directly deployable on edge hardware without runtime un-pruning.

## Method

DapperFL introduces two coupled modules. The Model Fusion Pruning (MFP) module first fine-tunes the global model for one epoch on local data, then fuses the global model back into the fine-tuned local model via a dynamic convex combination with a decaying fusion factor α^t (initialized at 0.9, decreasing geometrically toward a floor of 0.1), embedding cross-domain knowledge before pruning. It then applies channel-wise ℓ₁-norm pruning at a client-specific ratio to produce a compact model. The Domain Adaptive Regularization (DAR) module segments the pruned model into an encoder and a predictor and adds a squared-ℓ₂-norm penalty on encoder representations to the cross-entropy loss, implicitly encouraging domain-invariant features. A dedicated heterogeneous aggregation step recovers each pruned model's full architecture by filling pruned channels with the previous global model's values before performing data-size-weighted averaging, preserving both local and global knowledge in the aggregated global model.

## Results

On the Digits benchmark (MNIST, USPS, SVHN, SYN), DapperFL achieves a global top-1 accuracy of 74.30%, outperforming the best non-heterogeneous baseline FPL (74.17%) by 0.13% and the best heterogeneous baseline FedMP (72.29%) by 2.01%. On the Office Caltech benchmark (Caltech, Amazon, Webcam, DSLR), DapperFL reaches 67.75% global accuracy, surpassing the runner-up FedSR (65.47%) by 2.28%. Additionally, DapperFL achieves adaptive model volume reductions of 20%–80% across heterogeneous clients while maintaining superior accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DapperFL outperforms all 8 baselines on both benchmarks. On Digits, it edges out FPL (74.17%) by 0.13% and FedSR (73.89%) by 0.41%, while leading the best heterogeneous framework FedMP (72.29%) by 2.01%. On Office Caltech, the margin is larger: DapperFL (67.75%) beats FedSR (65.47%) by 2.28% and FPL (65.45%) by 2.30%, and surpasses the best heterogeneous baseline FedMP (62.34%) by 5.41%. Among the four heterogeneous FL frameworks (FedDrop, FedProx, FedMP, NeFL), DapperFL consistently achieves the highest global accuracy on both benchmarks, demonstrating that its MFP module effectively handles system heterogeneity without sacrificing domain generalization. Notably, DapperFL also achieves the best per-domain accuracy on SYN (37.26%), Amazon (81.58%), and DSLR (66.67%), indicating that the DAR module prevents over-fitting to any single domain.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by jointly addressing two challenges in federated learning that prior work has treated independently: system heterogeneity (handled by model-compression frameworks like FedDrop, FedMP, NeFL) and domain shifts (handled by domain-generalization frameworks like FedSR, FPL, MOON). The MFP module repurposes transfer-learning-style model fusion (blending the global model into the fine-tuned local model before pruning) with channel-wise l1-norm pruning, while the DAR module borrows the simple l2-norm-on-representations regularization from centralized DG literature and applies it to the pruned encoder. The heterogeneous aggregation algorithm extends the structure-recovery idea from FedMP's R2SP scheme but frames it explicitly as a local-knowledge/global-knowledge decomposition.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DapperFL fills a practical gap for real-world edge FL deployments where low-capability devices and non-IID domain distributions coexist, achieving 20–80% model compression while matching or exceeding full-model baselines. However, the evaluation is limited to small-scale benchmarks (Digits, Office Caltech) with ResNet-10/18 and only 10 simulated clients, leaving open whether the approach scales to larger architectures, more domains, or real heterogeneous hardware. The four framework-specific hyperparameters (α₀, α_min, ε, γ) require manual tuning, and the authors themselves flag automatic selection as future work. A key open question is whether the l2 representation regularization generalizes beyond classification to tasks like detection or segmentation under domain shift.
