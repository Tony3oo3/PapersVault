## Problem

Existing interactive scene reconstruction methods (CoNeRF, CoGS, K-Planes) are limited to single objects in simple backgrounds; scaling to multiple articulated objects in complex scenes causes the (3+α)-dimensional interaction space to become intractable, requiring 4× A100 GPUs for weeks or 500M Gaussian storage. Furthermore, language grounding in interactive scenes fails because topology changes induced by interactions (e.g., a cabinet opening) break static 3D feature distillation approaches like LERF.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a single casually captured monocular video with per-frame camera poses and per-object control variables (joint rotation vectors), plus a natural language query for grounding. It produces novel-view RGB renderings at arbitrary interaction states, per-object interactive probability masks, and language-grounded segmentation masks. The model maintains a constant 39M parameter budget (multi-scale feature planes at 512×256×128, 32-dim features) regardless of the number of interactive objects, and is trained self-supervised via volume rendering with 80k–100k steps on a single A100 GPU.

## Method

LiveScene factorizes the (3+α)-dimensional interactive space into disjoint local 4D deformable fields, one per interactive object, by projecting high-dimensional sampling points onto compact 4D rays via a lightweight interaction probability decoder (a 1-layer MLP) trained with focal loss on object masks. Each local 4D field is further compressed into C(4,2)=6 feature planes following K-Planes, with coarse-to-fine multi-scale sampling to retrieve the maximum-probability interaction state at each scale. A feature repulsion loss enforces separation between adjacent deformable regions, while a probability rejection threshold truncates low-confidence samples to the background, preventing boundary sampling conflicts. For language grounding, instead of distilling CLIP features into a full 3D field (as in LERF), LiveScene stores language embeddings in lightweight planes indexed by the maximum-probability cluster, then performs bilinear interpolation along the interaction variable axis to produce state-adaptive embeddings with O(C×α×dim) storage, enabling natural-language localization and control of individual objects under arbitrary interaction states.

## Results

On the CoNeRF Synthetic benchmark, LiveScene achieves 43.349 PSNR, 0.986 SSIM, and 0.011 LPIPS, outperforming the second-best method (CoGS, 33.455 PSNR) by +9.89 dB. On the OmniSim dataset (20 scenes), LiveScene attains average PSNR of 33.158, SSIM of 0.962, and LPIPS of 0.074, with a +1.99 dB advantage over CoGS on the #challenging subset. On InterReal, LiveScene achieves 28.436 PSNR on the #challenging subset where CoGS fails to converge entirely. For language grounding, LiveScene reaches 86.86 mIOU on OmniSim, surpassing LERF (21.74) by +65.12 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LiveScene decisively outperforms all baselines on the CoNeRF Synthetic set, achieving 43.349 PSNR versus CoGS's 33.455 (+9.89 dB) and K-Planes's 33.301 (+10.05 dB), with LPIPS of 0.011 versus 0.064 for CoGS. On OmniSim, LiveScene leads on average PSNR (33.158) over CoGS (32.187) and K-Planes (32.573), with the gap widening on #challenging scenes (31.645 vs. 28.701 for CoGS, +2.94 dB) where multi-object interactions stress existing methods. On InterReal #challenging, CoGS fails to converge entirely while LiveScene achieves 28.436 PSNR and 0.185 LPIPS; on #medium, LiveScene (30.815) narrowly edges CoGS (30.774). For language grounding, LiveScene (86.86 mIOU) dramatically surpasses both LERF (21.74) and SAM (59.11) on OmniSim, with the advantage over LERF reaching +70.54 points on #challenging scenes. The extended K-Planes variants (MK-Planes, MK-Planes*) show that simply increasing plane dimensions without the factorization strategy degrades on complex scenes, confirming the necessity of LiveScene's local deformable field decomposition.

## Relation to Prior Work

LiveScene extends the K-Planes feature-plane representation from 4D (space+time) to a (3+α)-dimensional interactive space, but rather than naively expanding to C(3+α,2) planes, it factorizes the space into local 4D deformable fields per object via an interaction probability decoder. It builds on CoNeRF's controllable radiance field formulation (control variables κ for articulated motion) but generalizes from single-object to multi-object scenes, and on LERF's CLIP feature distillation but replaces 3D field indexing with plane-based retrieval to handle topology changes induced by interactions. The multi-scale coarse-to-fine sampling and feature repulsion loss are novel architectural components not present in any single prior work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first method to jointly handle multi-object interactive scene reconstruction, novel view synthesis, and language-based grounding in a single parameter-efficient framework (39M params constant regardless of scene complexity), and it introduces the first scene-level physical interaction datasets (OmniSim, InterReal) with 28 scenes and 70 objects. Key limitations include dependence on label density for control quality and restriction to closed-vocabulary language grounding tied to OpenCLIP. Open questions include whether the factorization approach scales to scenes with dozens of interacting objects, whether open-vocabulary grounding is achievable without 3D distillation, and how the method handles non-articulated deformable interactions (e.g., cloth, fluids).
