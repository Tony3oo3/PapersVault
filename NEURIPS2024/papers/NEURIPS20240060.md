## Problem

Existing Vision-Language Models (VLMs) cannot align their attention with human users' gaze, leading to imprecise responses in complex AR/VR scenes where users naturally direct attention to specific objects. While gaze tracking is the most intuitive way for users to communicate intent in head-mounted display scenarios, seamlessly integrating gaze information into large-scale pre-trained VLMs without catastrophic forgetting remains an unsolved challenge.

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB image (224×224, processed by CLIP ViT-L/14), a 2D gaze heatmap (derived from gaze fixations or downsampled mouse traces via Gaussian blur), and a text instruction (direct or coreference query) as input, producing a free-form text answer. The gaze heatmap is spatially aligned with image patches and consumed through a dedicated attention pathway rather than being concatenated as additional tokens. Training data follows a chatbot format with special tokens [image], [fixation], [answer], and [endofchunk] to delineate modalities and mask prediction targets.

## Method

Voila-A introduces a Voila Perceiver Resampler (VPR) that processes image features and gaze heatmap patches jointly through stacked Voila Perceiver Blocks (VPBs). The key mechanism is a gated attention where gaze patches modulate the key embeddings via a learnable scalar g (initialized to 0) multiplied by tanh(g), enabling gradual integration of gaze guidance without disrupting pre-trained distributions. The gaze heatmap is divided into patches, linearly projected, and layer-normalized before entering the attention computation, where it is concatenated with image and latent features for key generation. Training follows a two-stage unfreezing schedule: first training only the gaze-related linear layers for one epoch, then fine-tuning the full Perceiver resampler, cross-attention layers, and LLM input/output embeddings for a second epoch (~1.3B trainable parameters on the Otter/MPT-7B backbone). The training data (VOILA-COCO, 72k QA pairs) is automatically generated by GPT-4 from Localized Narratives mouse traces, which are downsampled at rate 25 to statistically match real gaze fixation distributions (minimizing Earth Mover's Distance).

## Results

On the VOILA-COCO test set (1,900 QA pairs), VOILA achieves the highest GPT-4 RANKING score, outperforming both Otter and Kosmos-2 in a balanced manner across helpfulness and fact grounding. On the real-life VOILA-GAZE benchmark (200 gaze-QA pairs), VOILA again surpasses both baselines, with fewer ties observed compared to the Otter-vs-Kosmos-2 comparison, indicating stronger intent understanding in challenging real-world scenarios. For zero-shot generalizability, VOILA achieves 49.6% accuracy on the VizWiz grounding validation set, substantially outperforming the Otter baseline at 33.2%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VOILA is compared against two baselines: Otter (MPT-7B + CLIP ViT-L/14 with in-context instruction tuning) and Kosmos-2 (grounded multimodal LLM using discrete position tokens). On the VOILA-COCO test set, VOILA outperforms Otter on grounding (both benefit from fine-grained grounded facts) and surpasses Kosmos-2 on helpfulness, achieving a balanced profile that neither baseline matches. On the more challenging VOILA-GAZE real-life benchmark, VOILA continues to lead both models, while Otter and Kosmos-2 produce many more ties due to the difficulty of the questions. On coreference queries specifically, VOILA maintains a 0.41 winning rate over Otter-base (vs. 0.51 for direct queries), whereas Otter's reward score drops sharply to −1.91 without gaze guidance, demonstrating that gaze input is critical for resolving ambiguous references. On VizWiz (zero-shot), VOILA's 49.6% accuracy exceeds Otter's 33.2% by 16.4 points, confirming generalizability beyond the training distribution.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the OpenFlamingo/Otter VLM architecture by introducing a gaze heatmap as an additional spatial input modality, processed through a modified Perceiver Resampler (Voila Perceiver Resampler) with a learnable gating mechanism to preserve pre-trained knowledge. It departs from prior gaze-related work (gaze-directed visual grounding, eye-gaze-guided ViTs) that operated on small-scale models or narrow tasks, by integrating continuous gaze signals into a 7B-parameter VLM. A key methodological departure is the use of mouse trace data from Localized Narratives as a scalable proxy for gaze, validated through EMD analysis, rather than relying solely on expensive eye-tracking recordings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work opens a practical pathway for AR/VR head-mounted displays to use gaze as a natural, hands-free interaction channel for VLMs, with particular relevance for visually impaired users who rely on gaze to communicate intent. The trace-as-gaze-proxy strategy significantly lowers the data acquisition barrier, making gaze-aligned VLMs more accessible to the research community. Open questions include scaling to larger models and datasets, reducing hallucination, improving inference efficiency for real-time HMD deployment, and extending to dynamic multi-turn egocentric scenarios. The limited training data (70k QA pairs) and modest performance margins over strong baselines suggest the approach has not yet reached its full potential.
