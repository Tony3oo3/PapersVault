## Problem

While elastic costs (ℓ₂² + γτ) were recently shown to shape Monge map displacements through the proximal operator of a regularizer τ, two critical gaps remained: there was no general procedure to compute ground-truth OT maps for arbitrary elastic costs (analogous to the Brenier theorem for ℓ₂²), and no mechanism to learn the regularizer parameters from data. Without ground-truth maps, one cannot benchmark estimators; without a learning procedure, the choice of τ remains ad hoc.

## Input / Output

The method consumes paired source and target point clouds X ∈ ℝ^{n×d}, Y ∈ ℝ^{m×d} with probability weight vectors a, b, along with a user-specified subspace dimension p̂ and regularization strength γ. It outputs an OT Monge map T_ε: ℝ^d → ℝ^d whose displacements T(x) − x are shaped by the proximal operator of the learned regularizer, and a Stiefel-constrained matrix Â ∈ ℝ^{p̂×d} (satisfying ÂÂᵀ = I) that parameterizes the learned displacement subspace. The map is evaluated via the MBO estimator, which requires solving an entropic OT problem (Sinkhorn) with the elastic cost and then applying the proximal operator of τ_{Â⊥} to the barycentric projection.

## Method

The paper contributes three interlocking components. First, Proposition 2 provides a provably convergent proximal-gradient iteration to compute the h-transform of a concave potential for any elastic cost h = ½‖z‖² + γτ, yielding ground-truth Monge maps T_g^h = Id − ∇h*∘∇ḡ^h that enable synthetic benchmark generation beyond the Brenier (ℓ₂²) case. Second, the subspace elastic cost τ_{A⊥}(z) = ½‖A⊥z‖² penalizes displacement components orthogonal to a p-dimensional subspace spanned by rows of A ∈ S_{p,d}, with a closed-form proximal operator (1+γ)⁻¹(I_d + γAᵀA)z that avoids expensive linear solves. Third, a bilevel loss L(A) = ⟨P*(A), R(A)⟩ is minimized over the Stiefel manifold via Riemannian gradient descent, where P*(A) is the entropic OT coupling under the elastic cost and R(A) encodes the regularizer values of all pairwise displacements; the gradient is computed by differentiating through Sinkhorn (via OTT-JAX unrolling) and through the regularizer, with the Riemannian gradient projected as G − AGᵀA to maintain orthonormality of A.

## Results

On synthetic ground-truth tasks (d=5 and d=10, p=2), the MBO estimator with elastic costs (γ>0) achieves a lower MSE ratio relative to the unregularized ℓ₂² baseline across all tested regularization strengths, with the ratio consistently below 1 (Figure 3). For subspace recovery, the learned matrix Â achieves a normalized squared-residual error often below 1% when projecting the ground-truth A* onto span(Â), with performance improving as the share of displacement inertia captured by p* increases from 50% to 90% (Figure 4). On single-cell RNA-seq data (d=256, three cell lines × five drug perturbations), the learned subspace elastic cost reduces the ℓ₂² Sinkhorn divergence compared to the naive ℓ₂² baseline, with improvements most pronounced at moderate γ and p̂ values (Figure 5).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is the standard ℓ₂² entropic map estimator (γ=0, equivalent to Pooladian & Niles-Weed 2021), which serves as the unregularized MBO estimator. On synthetic ground-truth displacement tasks (Figure 3), the elastic-cost MBO estimator with positive γ outperforms the ℓ₂² baseline for both the ℓ₁ and subspace (‖A⊥z‖₂²) regularizers, across all tested γ values in dimensions d=5 and d=10. On the single-cell drug-perturbation task (Figure 5), the learned subspace elastic cost (with varying γ and p̂) consistently achieves lower ℓ₂² Sinkhorn divergence than the ℓ₂²-only baseline, demonstrating that promoting displacements in a learned low-dimensional subspace improves predictive accuracy even when the evaluation metric is the standard squared-Euclidean distance. The paper does not compare against other structured-transport baselines (e.g., projection-robust Wasserstein or spiked-transport estimators), so the comparison is limited to the unregularized entropic map.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the MBO estimator framework of Cuturi et al. (2023), which introduced elastic costs h(z) = ½‖z‖² + γτ(z) and showed that resulting Monge maps are shaped by the proximal operator of τ. The key departure is providing a provable numerical procedure (Propositions 1–2) to generate ground-truth Monge maps for *any* elastic cost, filling a gap that the Brenier theorem (which only characterizes optimal maps for the ℓ₂² cost) leaves open. The paper also connects its subspace elastic cost τ_{A⊥} to the spiked transport model of Niles-Weed and Rigollet (2022) in the γ→∞ limit, and builds on the entropic map estimator of Pooladian and Niles-Weed (2021) for its sample-complexity analysis.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ground-truth map generation routine is a much-needed benchmarking tool that enables rigorous evaluation of OT map estimators beyond the standard ℓ₂² setting, and the bilevel loss for learning cost parameters opens a path toward adaptive, data-driven cost design. The single-cell results demonstrate that promoting displacements in a learned low-dimensional subspace improves predictive accuracy even under ℓ₂² evaluation, suggesting the structure is genuinely informative rather than an artifact. Open questions include extending the framework to other learnable proximal operators (beyond subspace projections), making the γ→∞ connection to the spiked transport model quantitative, and understanding how the learned subspace dimension p̂ should be selected in practice.
