## Problem

In resource-aware federated self-supervised learning, clients train heterogeneous architectures (e.g., ResNet18 vs. VGG9) under skewed class distributions, creating two coupled challenges: deviated representation abilities across models and inconsistent representation spaces. Existing methods either assume identical client architectures (FedU, FedEMA, FedX) or fail to produce a global representation model (Hetero-SSFL, FedFoA), leaving no framework that can aggregate knowledge from heterogeneous clients into a unified global encoder covering all classes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240321/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each client receives unlabeled 32×32 RGB images (CIFAR-10/100) and trains a local BYOL-style Siamese encoder (ResNet18 or VGG9 depending on available resources). The server holds a small public unlabeled dataset (4,000 samples) and a larger global ResNet18 encoder. Clients upload only their online encoder parameters per round; the server produces a global representation model that encodes all classes into a unified latent space, evaluated by freezing the encoder for linear probing or fine-tuning with 1% labels for semi-supervised classification.

## Method

FedMKD is a multi-teacher knowledge distillation framework with three components. First, an adaptive knowledge integration module uses an attention mechanism where the global model's projected representation serves as the query and all local models' projected representations serve as keys/values, producing an instance-level weighted aggregate that adaptively emphasizes the most relevant teacher per sample. Second, the global model is trained with a weighted combination of a BYOL-style self-supervised loss on the public dataset and an NT-Xent distillation loss against the aggregated teacher representation, enabling it to encode skewed classes from all clients into a unified space. Third, a global knowledge anchored alignment module constructs 'twin' networks on the server (local online encoder as the online model, global online encoder as the target) and performs contrastive alignment, then sends the updated local online network back to each client while preserving its target network to retain local knowledge. The paper also provides a non-convex convergence guarantee under standard Lipschitz and bounded-variance assumptions.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with IID public dataset, FedMKD achieves 69.07% top-1 accuracy under linear probing (IID data split), 66.98% (Dir β=0.5), and 64.81% (Class), outperforming the best baseline Hetero-SSFL by 3.46%, 2.94%, and 5.68% respectively. On CIFAR-100, FedMKD reaches 35.94% (IID), 35.59% (Dir β=0.5), and 36.33% (Class), improving over Hetero-SSFL by 7.05%, 5.96%, and 5.49%. The paper reports an average improvement of 4.22% on CIFAR-10 and 5.31% on CIFAR-100 over the second-best method under linear evaluation, and 3.66% and 2.07% under semi-supervised evaluation (1% labeled data).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedMKD consistently outperforms all five baselines across every data distribution setting on both datasets. Against the strongest baseline Hetero-SSFL [20], FedMKD gains 3.46–5.68% on CIFAR-10 and 5.49–7.05% on CIFAR-100 under linear probing; the gap is largest under the Class (extreme skew) setting, confirming the method's advantage under severe class imbalance. Compared to MOON [15], which uses model-contrastive alignment locally, FedMKD improves by 13.93% (CIFAR-10 IID) and 9.24% (CIFAR-100 IID), demonstrating that server-side multi-teacher distillation is more effective than client-side alignment for heterogeneous architectures. FedET [2], which also trains a larger global model but relies on class-probability distillation, is outperformed by 7.64% on CIFAR-10 IID and 11.46% on CIFAR-100 Class, highlighting the benefit of representation-level (rather than logit-level) distillation in the self-supervised setting. FedMD [14] and FedDF [18], which were originally designed for supervised federated distillation, show the weakest performance (45–49% on CIFAR-10), underscoring that their logit-based distillation is poorly suited to unlabeled representation learning. FedU and FedEMA are inapplicable under model heterogeneity and thus excluded from the comparison.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on BYOL's asymmetric Siamese architecture (online/target encoders with EMA and predictor) as the per-client self-supervised training backbone, and extends it into a federated setting with heterogeneous client models. It departs from homogeneous Fed-SSL methods (FedU, FedEMA, FedX, L-DAWA) that rely on parameter averaging by introducing multi-teacher knowledge distillation as the aggregation mechanism, and from heterogeneous Fed-SSL methods (Hetero-SSFL, FedFoA) that handle architectural diversity but cannot produce a global representation model. The key conceptual departure is framing the aggregation problem as one of 'deviated representation abilities' and 'inconsistent representation spaces,' addressed via attention-based adaptive weighting and a server-side alignment module rather than kernel alignment or feature correlation matrices.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FedMKD is the first framework to simultaneously handle heterogeneous client architectures, class skew, and global model training in federated self-supervised learning, which is practically important for resource-constrained edge deployments where clients cannot share model sizes. The authors acknowledge that the theoretical convergence proof treats the multi-teacher distillation loss as a monolithic objective rather than analyzing its specific properties, and the experiments are limited to CIFAR-10/100 with only 5 clients, leaving open questions about scalability to ImageNet-scale data, larger client populations, and more diverse architecture families. Privacy implications of the public dataset and the server-side alignment step (which requires reconstructing local twin models) remain underexplored.
