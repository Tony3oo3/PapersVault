## Problem

Small consumer electronics (in-ear headphones, smartwatches, AR glasses) cannot run state-of-the-art ANNs in real-time due to limited MCU processing power, while model sizes grow faster than Moore's Law can compensate. Existing online inference methods like STMC handle strided convolutions by maintaining separate states for odd and even positions, causing exponential memory growth with each additional strided layer. SOI addresses this by replacing exact state tracking with partial state prediction, trading a small performance loss for substantial computational savings.

## Input / Output

The method consumes time-series data one element (frame) at a time in online mode, producing a prediction at each step. For speech separation, a single 16 kHz audio frame yields a speech mask; for acoustic scene classification, a single audio frame yields a scene label; for video action recognition, a single video frame yields an action label. A critical constraint is that skip connections between the downsampling and upsampling layers are required to inject current-frame information into deeper layers, since the optimized portion of the network operates on predicted (stale) partial states rather than freshly computed ones.

## Method

SOI modifies the inference pattern of a CNN by inserting Strided-Cloned Convolution (S-CC) pairs—strided convolutions followed by extrapolation (frame duplication) to restore temporal resolution—and optionally Shifted Convolution (SC) layers that advance the time axis. In Partially Predictive (PP) mode, the most recent frame carries information for both the current and next output, so only the first inference updates all network states while subsequent ones reuse cached partial states, reducing average complexity. In Fully Predictive (FP) mode, an additional time shift means the most recent frame predicts two future outputs, allowing a portion of the network to be precomputed between data arrivals and reducing both peak complexity and latency. The key mechanism is that layers between the downsampling and upsampling points skip computation on odd-numbered inferences (reusing the previous state), while the upsampling layer duplicates its output and concatenates skip-connection data to maintain causality. This is combined with pruning and is compatible with STMC's strided-convolution handler, enabling coexistence of multiple compression strategies within a single network.

## Results

On the DNS Challenge speech separation task, SOI (partially predictive) achieves up to 64.4% computational complexity reduction (from 1819.2 to 648.5 MMAC/s) at the cost of a 9.8% SI-SNRi drop (7.69 → 6.94 dB). The fully predictive variant reaches 50% complexity reduction with 11.3% SI-SNRi loss while precomputing 83.7% of the network on past data only. On Acoustic Scene Classification with ResNet-18, SOI actually *improves* top-1 accuracy from 85.13% (STMC) to 91.55% while reducing complexity from 15.56 to 12.35 GMAC/s. On HMDB-51 video action recognition, ResNet-10 with SOI achieves 33.34% top-1 accuracy (vs. 32.63% baseline) at 40.69 GMAC/s (vs. 48.54 GMAC/s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against STMC (the direct predecessor), SOI trades a small SI-SNRi loss (1–18%) for 35–71% complexity reduction in speech separation, while in Acoustic Scene Classification the trade-off is nearly free: SOI matches or exceeds STMC accuracy (e.g., +6.42 pp on ResNet-18, +1.69 pp on GhostNet-III) while cutting complexity by ~16–20%. Against resampling-based baselines (SoX, Kaiser, polyphase, linear) at the same 50% complexity budget, SOI retains 7.23–7.47 dB SI-SNRi versus 3.49–5.83 dB for the best resampler, a gap of 1.4–3.7 dB. The paper also shows that combining SOI with unstructured magnitude pruning yields an additional ~300 MMAC/s (≈16%) reduction at equal SI-SNRi compared to pruning STMC alone. Overall, SOI consistently wins on the efficiency axis and ties or wins on the quality axis for tasks with slowly varying outputs (classification), while incurring a controlled, tunable quality drop for fast-varying outputs (speech masks).

## Relation to Prior Work

SOI is a direct extension of STMC (Stefański et al., 2023), which enabled single-frame online inference of CNNs by caching partial states and avoiding redundant recomputation. Where STMC handles strided convolutions by maintaining separate odd/even states (causing exponential state growth), SOI instead introduces strided-convolution-based compression with simple extrapolation (frame duplication) to predict partial network states, trading a small performance loss for dramatically simpler state management. Conceptually, SOI sits alongside learned routing and skipping methods (Skip RNN, SkipNet, early-exit networks) but differs in using a fixed, architecture-level pattern rather than input-dependent learned decisions, and it is explicitly composable with pruning and quantization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SOI addresses a practical bottleneck for deploying neural networks on resource-constrained devices (wearables, AR glasses, MCUs) where even moderately sized CNNs exceed available compute budgets, and it does so with minimal architectural changes and no need for specialized sparse inference hardware. The method's composability with pruning (showing ~16% additional complexity reduction at equal quality) and its applicability across U-Net, GhostNet, ResNet, and MoViNet architectures suggest broad utility for edge inference. Open questions include whether learned extrapolation could close the remaining performance gap, how cumulative prediction errors behave over very long sequences, and whether the accuracy improvements observed in classification tasks (attributed to receptive-field expansion) generalize beyond the tested datasets.
