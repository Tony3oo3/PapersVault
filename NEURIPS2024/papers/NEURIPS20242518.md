## Problem

Traditional ANNs rely on linear integration followed by a nonlinear activation function, which limits their ability to directly capture second-order statistics (correlation) within structured data. Recent neuroscience findings show that biological dendrites follow a quadratic integration rule for synaptic inputs, yet this property has not been quantified or integrated into CNNs in a biologically plausible, channel-wise manner. The paper asks whether quadratic neurons inherently capture data correlation and whether this can be exploited to improve generalization with minimal architectural changes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242518/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes standard RGB images as input (28×28 for MNIST/Arabic MNIST, 32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 224×224 for ImageNet-1K) and outputs a class label via a standard classification head. The key structural detail is that the quadratic integration term X^T A X is computed channel-wise at each spatial location: for an input with C_in channels, the quadratic coefficient matrix A ∈ R^{C_out × C_in × C_in} captures pairwise interactions across channels, mirroring how a dendritic neuron integrates inputs from multiple presynaptic neurons of different types. This contrasts with pixel-wise quadratic convolutions that only model self-interactions within a single channel.

## Method

The core mechanism replaces the standard linear-then-activation neuron f(x) = σ(w·x + b) with a quadratic neuron f(x) = x^T Ax + w·x + b, where A is a learnable symmetric matrix. The paper proves (Theorems 1–2, 5) that for Gaussian class-conditional distributions, the optimal quadratic neuron parameters directly encode the inverse covariance matrices, meaning the neuron inherently captures data correlation without requiring additional layers or attention mechanisms. To integrate this into CNNs with biological plausibility, the quadratic term is applied channel-wise: at each spatial location, the output neuron's response includes an additional X^T[:,j,k] A[i,:,:] X[:,j,k] term that models dendritic integration across input channels, inspired by how retinal ganglion cells receive inputs from multiple cone cell types. Only a single layer (the second layer of the first block in stage 2 for ResNet; the first layer of block 3 for ConvNeXt) is modified, adding roughly 1% parameters. A Layer Normalization layer is placed before the quadratic layer to stabilize gradients, and quadratic parameters are initialized to zero with a separate learning rate.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, Dit-ResNet-110 achieves 94.33% test accuracy (vs. 93.57% for ResNet-110) with only 1.76M parameters, and on CIFAR-100 it reaches 72.40% (vs. 70.84%). On ImageNet-1K, Dit-ConvNeXt-T/S/B achieve 82.6%/83.6%/84.2% top-1 accuracy, improving over ConvNeXt-T/S/B (82.1%/83.1%/83.8%) by an average of 0.5% with only a ~1% increase in parameters. The quadratic neurons are integrated into a single layer, keeping the overall architecture close to the original CNN design.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On CIFAR-10/100, Dit-ResNets consistently outperform both the original ResNet baselines (e.g., 94.33% vs. 93.57% on CIFAR-10 for the 110-layer variant) and existing quadratic-neuron adaptations QResNet [12] and QuadraResNet [54], achieving higher accuracy with roughly 3× fewer parameters (1.76M vs. 5.17M for the 110-layer models). On ImageNet-1K, Dit-ConvNeXt-T (82.6%) surpasses ConvNeXt-T (82.1%) and matches or exceeds SLaK-T (82.5%) and DeepMAD-29M (82.5%) at the same parameter budget, while Dit-ConvNeXt-B (84.2%) edges out SLaK-B (84.0%), QuadraNet36-B (84.1%), and DeepMAD-89M (84.0%). Against Transformer and SSM baselines, Dit-ConvNeXt-S (83.6%) trails VMamba-S (83.5%) by a narrow margin but uses fewer FLOPs (9.2G vs. 11.2G), and Dit-ConvNeXt-B (84.2%) exceeds Swin-B (83.5%) and VMamba-B (83.7%). The ablation in Table 5 confirms that the channel-wise quadratic formulation outperforms pixel-wise quadratic convolution by 0.4–0.7% top-1 accuracy across all three model scales, validating the biological plausibility of the design choice.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the quadratic neuron model of Redlapalli et al. [41] by grounding it in the recently discovered dendritic quadratic integration rule [15, 28], and integrates it into CNNs (ResNet, ConvNeXt) via a channel-wise quadratic term that models cross-channel synaptic interactions. It departs from prior quadratic neuron works [54, 12, 4, 53] and quadratic convolution approaches [48, 20, 35, 60] in two key ways: (1) the quadratic operation is applied channel-wise rather than pixel-wise, providing a clear biological interpretation as dendritic integration across different input channels, and (2) the paper supplies a theoretical analysis (existence and uniqueness of the optimal classifier) that prior quadratic neuron papers lacked. The architectural modifications are minimal—replacing a single layer in ResNet or ConvNeXt—making the contribution orthogonal to the backbone design.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clean theoretical explanation for why quadratic neurons generalize better: the quadratic term x^T Ax directly encodes the covariance structure of the data distribution, enabling correlation capture that linear neurons must approximate through depth. This opens a path toward brain-inspired architectures where different integration rules (quadratic, higher-order) could be assigned to different layers based on their biological counterparts. Key limitations include the Gaussian assumption in the theory, the increased FLOPs from the quadratic term, and the fact that only a single layer is modified—scaling the approach to multiple layers or non-Gaussian data remains open. The modest but consistent ImageNet gains (~0.5%) suggest the technique is a useful but not transformative architectural ingredient.
