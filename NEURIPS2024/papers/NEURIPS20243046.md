## Problem

Reconstructing dynamic 3D structure and camera poses from casual monocular videos remains challenging because the problem is ill-posed without additional constraints, and existing methods either require lengthy per-video optimization (e.g., CasualSAM takes over two hours), rely on unrealistic assumptions like orthographic cameras or rigid bones, or are restricted to specific object classes. No prior approach achieves fast, feed-forward inference on arbitrary casual videos without 3D supervision.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243046/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes as input a pre-extracted 2D point track tensor M ∈ R^{N×P×3} (where each entry stores x, y coordinates and a visibility flag o) produced by CoTracker from a 15×15 grid sampled at every 20th frame, along with known camera intrinsics used to normalize tracks. It outputs per-frame camera poses (rotation and translation), per-frame 3D point clouds parameterized as linear combinations of K=12 input-specific basis elements, and per-point movement level values γ that weight reprojection errors. The network handles up to ~1000 point tracks across 50 frames in a single forward pass on one GPU.

## Method

TRACKSTO4D uses an equivariant transformer architecture with alternating multi-head attention over the temporal (N) and point (P) dimensions, designed to respect the symmetry group G = C_N × S_P (cyclic time translation × point permutation). The key architectural constraint is a low-rank movement assumption: output point clouds are expressed as B_1 + Σ c_ik·B_k, where B_1 captures static structure and K−1 additional bases encode dynamic deviations, making the ill-posed problem tractable. Training is fully unsupervised, minimizing a combination of reprojection error, a Cauchy-distribution-weighted static loss that encourages B_1 and camera poses to explain static 2D motion, a negative-depth regularizer, and a sparsity loss on dynamic basis elements. This design allows the network to learn camera poses, 3D structure, and dynamic/static separation purely from 2D track reprojection, achieving inference in seconds versus hours for optimization-based baselines.

## Results

On the pet test set, TRACKSTO4D (trained on cats only) achieves a dynamic depth Abs Rel of 0.11 and δ<1.25 of 0.88 in a single 0.15-minute feed-forward pass, compared to CasualSAM's 0.09 and 0.93 but requiring 130 minutes. After a short Bundle Adjustment step (still 0.15 min total), camera pose accuracy reaches ATE 4.22 mm and RPE Trans 2.86 mm, outperforming D-SLAM (ATE 5.08 mm) and CasualSAM (ATE 6.90 mm). With 4.86 minutes of per-sequence fine-tuning, depth accuracy matches CasualSAM exactly (Abs Rel 0.09/0.06, δ<1.25 0.90/0.96) while remaining ~27× faster. On the out-of-domain Nvidia Dynamic Scenes dataset, the method generalizes well, achieving ATE 8.67 mm with BA versus CasualSAM's 7.81 mm in 22 minutes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TRACKSTO4D is compared against five baselines: D-SLAM, ParticleSFM, RCVD, CasualSAM, and MiDaS. In terms of depth accuracy, the method (Ours C) is slightly behind CasualSAM (Abs Rel 0.11 vs 0.09 on dynamic points) but dramatically faster (0.15 min vs 130 min, a ~870× speedup); after fine-tuning, it matches CasualSAM exactly. For camera poses, Ours(C)+BA achieves the best ATE (4.22 mm) and RPE Trans (2.86 mm), beating D-SLAM (5.08/3.60), CasualSAM (6.90/3.95), and ParticleSFM (12.79/6.95). RCVD performs poorly on dynamic depth (Abs Rel 0.40) and has the worst camera accuracy (ATE 43.95 mm). MiDaS is fast (0.15 min) but provides no camera poses and has much worse dynamic depth (Abs Rel 0.16) and fails catastrophically on all points (6.2E+04). The key trade-off is that TRACKSTO4D's raw inference is slightly less accurate than CasualSAM on depth, but the gap closes entirely with minimal fine-tuning while retaining a 27× speed advantage.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the equivariant learning framework of Maron et al. (DSS layers, ICML 2020) to the specific structure of point track tensors, combining temporal cyclic symmetry with point permutation symmetry in an alternating multi-head attention architecture. It adapts the classical low-rank basis decomposition from Bregler et al.'s orthographic NRSfM (CVPR 2000) to a pinhole camera setting and embeds it as an architectural constraint rather than a post-hoc factorization. The key departure from prior dynamic reconstruction methods (CasualSAM, RCVD, ParticleSfM) is the shift from per-scene optimization to a single feed-forward pass on pre-extracted 2D point tracks, trained entirely without 3D supervision via reprojection losses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 95% runtime reduction (0.15 min vs. 130 min for CasualSAM) while maintaining comparable depth and camera accuracy makes dynamic 3D reconstruction from casual videos practically deployable in settings where per-scene optimization is infeasible (robotics, AR, real-time applications). The finding that point tracks as input enable better cross-category generalization than image-based approaches is a useful design principle. Open questions include: whether the fixed K=12 basis can be adapted per scene, how the method handles rapid motion beyond CoTracker's tracking limits, and whether incorporating depth priors (e.g., MiDaS) can improve accuracy in low-parallax scenarios without the current approach's failure mode.
