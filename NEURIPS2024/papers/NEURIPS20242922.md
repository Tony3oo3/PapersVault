## Problem

Existing non-autoregressive draft models for speculative decoding (e.g., Medusa) predict each future token independently from the base model's hidden states, ignoring inter-token dependencies. This yields fast drafting but low acceptance rates, which bottleneck the overall inference speedup. The paper asks how to introduce sequential correlations among draft tokens while preserving the parallel, non-autoregressive generation paradigm.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242922/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The draft module consumes the hidden-state vector from the base LLM's final transformer layer (not raw text) and outputs per-position probability distributions over the vocabulary augmented with a blank token ε. At inference, top-k tokens per position are combined into a token tree of candidate sequences; a CTC transform then collapses consecutive duplicates and removes blanks, and the base model's attention map is masked at removed positions before parallel verification. The final output is the longest verified prefix of accepted tokens appended to the decoding history.

## Method

CTC-drafter replaces Medusa's independent linear heads with a single transformer layer trained under a sequence-level CTC loss rather than per-position cross-entropy. Training labels are obtained via knowledge distillation: the frozen base model greedily decodes ShareGPT prompts to produce target sequences, and the draft module is optimized to maximize the CTC log-likelihood over all valid alignments of those sequences. Because CTC aggregates probability across every alignment that maps to the target, candidates with coherent sequential dependencies receive higher joint probability, biasing the draft toward more plausible token orderings. At inference, the draft module produces non-autoregressive per-position distributions; a CTC transform module removes repeated tokens and blanks from candidate sequences and adjusts the verification attention mask accordingly, enabling the base model to verify all refined candidates in a single parallel pass.

## Results

On MT-bench with Vicuna-7B, CTC-drafter achieves a 2.78× speedup ratio (γ) with 3.56 average accepted tokens per decoding step (β), outperforming Medusa (2.13×, 2.58) and Hydra (2.36×, 3.04). On GSM8K, CTC-drafter reaches 2.66× speedup with Vicuna-13B (3.53 accepted tokens), surpassing Medusa's 2.21× (2.68). Across all three Vicuna model sizes (7B, 13B, 33B), CTC-drafter consistently maintains the highest β (3.51–3.56) on MT-bench, indicating superior draft quality regardless of base model scale.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CTC-drafter outperforms Medusa on both benchmarks across all model sizes: on MT-bench it achieves 2.78× vs. 2.13× (Vicuna-7B), 2.52× vs. 1.97× (13B), and 2.20× vs. 1.93× (33B) speedup, with β consistently ~1.0 higher (3.56 vs. 2.58). On GSM8K, the advantage is 2.43× vs. 2.33× (7B) and 2.66× vs. 2.21× (13B), narrowing to 2.16× vs. 2.10× at 33B where the larger base model widens the capability gap. Against Hydra (evaluated only on MT-bench), CTC-drafter leads by 0.42× on 7B (2.78× vs. 2.36×), 0.35× on 13B (2.52× vs. 2.17×), and 0.05× on 33B (2.20× vs. 2.15×), with β advantages of 0.52, 0.45, and 0.58 respectively. The gap over Hydra shrinks as base model size grows, suggesting the fixed-size draft module struggles to match larger base models' prediction behavior. On GSM8K specifically, CTC-drafter's 7B speedup (2.43×) is slightly lower than its MT-bench result (2.78×), attributed to the model's weaker math capability without task-specific fine-tuning, though this recovers at 13B (2.66×).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of speculative decoding (Leviathan et al., 2023) and Connectionist Temporal Classification (Graves et al., 2006), repurposing a sequence-to-sequence alignment technique from speech recognition for LLM inference acceleration. It directly modifies the Medusa framework (Cai et al., 2023) by replacing its independent linear prediction heads with a transformer layer trained under CTC loss, and by augmenting the token-tree verification with a CTC transform that removes blank and repeated tokens to produce adaptive-length candidates. The key conceptual departure from prior NAR speculative decoders (Medusa, Hydra) is that CTC's sequence-level probability aggregation over all valid alignments implicitly models inter-token dependencies during drafting, addressing the independence assumption that limits acceptance rates in existing methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that introducing sequence-level dependency modeling into the draft phase can meaningfully close the gap between draft speed and acceptance quality, yielding consistent 10–30% speedup improvements over strong baselines (Medusa, Hydra) across model sizes and benchmarks. However, the gains diminish with larger base models (Vicuna-33B), suggesting an unresolved ability-gap problem between the lightweight draft module and the target LLM. Open questions include whether the added CTC transform overhead (5.36% of decoding time) can be reduced, whether alternative sequence models (CRF, DAG) might capture dependencies more efficiently, and whether the approach generalizes beyond the Vicuna/LLaMA-2 family to more diverse architectures.
