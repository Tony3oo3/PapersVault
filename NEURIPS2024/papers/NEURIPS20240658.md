## Problem

Existing ViT adversarial attacks such as TGR aggressively zero out the largest token gradients in each surrogate layer, discarding potentially important feature information and ignoring gradient variance interactions across layers. This limits the transferability of crafted perturbations to black-box target models. The paper addresses how to regularize token gradients while preserving feature information, manage input diversity more rationally than random patch discarding, and mitigate attention-induced overfitting to improve cross-model transferability.

## Input / Output

The method takes a 224×224 RGB image with its ground-truth label, partitioned into 16×16 patches, and produces an adversarial example constrained by an ℓ∞ norm bound of ε=16 over 10 iterations. Grad-CAM is computed on the penultimate surrogate ViT layer to extract patch-level feature importance that guides the self-paced patch out mask. The resulting adversarial examples are evaluated for transferability against eight black-box ViT models, four undefended CNNs, and three adversarially trained defended CNNs.

## Method

ATT combines three complementary optimization strategies applied during perturbation training on a surrogate ViT. (1) Adaptive Variance Reduced token gradient (AVR) mildly re-scales the largest (and correlated extreme) token gradients by a penalty factor γ∈(0,1) rather than zeroing them, and adaptively modulates the re-scaling across layers using the ratio of gradient variances between consecutive layers (Eq. 2), smoothing variance transitions while preserving feature information. (2) Self-Paced Patch Out (SPPO) constructs a semantic-guided sparse mask using Grad-CAM patch-level feature importance, where the number of discarded perturbation patches increases progressively over iterations via a self-paced schedule, replacing random PatchOut with a more rational, efficiency-aware discarding strategy. (3) Hybrid token gradient truncation applies hard truncation (τ=0) to attention gradients in deep ViT layers and soft truncation (τ>0) in shallow layers, while QKV and MLP modules receive soft truncation throughout; attention truncation factors are set lower than QKV/MLP factors to systematically weaken the attention mechanism's influence on perturbation updates.

## Results

The proposed ATT method (with Self-Paced Patch Out) achieves an average attack success rate of 92.6% on ViTs, 75.4% on undefended CNNs, and 58.3% on defended CNNs when using CaiT-S/24 as the surrogate model — the best across all four surrogates. Across all surrogate models, ATT improves the black-box average ASR by 10.1% over the strongest baseline (TGR), with gains of 4.9% on ViTs and 12.8% on CNNs. The method also demonstrates superior efficiency, requiring only ~5 iterations to achieve first misclassification versus ~9 for TGR+PO.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ATT consistently outperforms all five baselines across every surrogate model and target type. The strongest baseline, TGR (Token Gradient Regularization, CVPR 2023), is surpassed by ATT by 4.3–13.4 percentage points on ViTs and 7.0–15.7 points on CNNs; for example, with CaiT-S/24 as surrogate, ATT+SPPO reaches 92.6% on ViTs vs. TGR+PO's 88.8%, and 75.4% on CNNs vs. 60.5%. PNA (AAAI 2022) is the second-strongest baseline but trails ATT by 11.0–14.8 points on ViTs and 13.8–22.8 points on CNNs. Among the gradient-optimization baselines, VMI (CVPR 2021) generally outperforms SGM (ICLR 2020) and MIM (CVPR 2018), yet all three remain 10–30 points below ATT on cross-architecture (ViT→CNN) attacks. The gap is largest on defended CNNs, where ATT+SPPO achieves 58.3% (CaiT-S/24) versus TGR+PO's 40.5% and PNA+PO's 39.3%, confirming that adaptive token tuning is particularly effective against adversarially trained models.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Token Gradient Regularization (TGR, Zhang et al. 2023), which regularizes intermediate ViT layer gradients by zeroing the largest token gradient per module, and on PatchOut (Wei et al. 2022), which randomly discards perturbation patches to enhance input diversity. The key departure is replacing TGR's aggressive zeroing with a mild adaptive re-scaling that preserves feature information while smoothing gradient variance across layers, and replacing PatchOut's random discarding with a semantic-guided self-paced strategy. The adaptive cross-layer variance smoothing draws inspiration from large-batch optimization literature (You et al. 2020, Xue et al. 2022), repurposing the idea of using gradient statistics across layers to stabilize updates.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical and well-motivated recipe for improving transferable ViT attacks, with the 10.1% average ASR gain and 58.3% on defended CNNs representing a meaningful security concern for deployed ViT systems. However, the authors explicitly acknowledge the lack of theoretical grounding for why gradient variance reduction improves transferability, leaving an open question about the fundamental mechanism. The finding that the method is more effective cross-architecture (12.8% on CNNs vs. 4.9% on ViTs) suggests the variance smoothing captures more generalizable features, but this observation is not deeply analyzed. Future work on robust ViT defenses and theoretical justification for the variance-transferability link would be valuable.
