## Problem

Prior work on the sample complexity of training diffusion model scores (Block et al., 2020) yields bounds with polynomial dependence on the inverse Wasserstein error 1/γ, while recent sampling analyses show that only polylogarithmic dependence on 1/γ is needed for the number of DDPM steps. This paper asks whether the training side can also achieve polylog(1/γ) sample complexity, and shows that the standard L² accuracy requirement is information-theoretically impossible to meet with fewer than poly(1/γ) samples, motivating a weaker error measure.

## Input / Output

The method takes m i.i.d. samples x_i from the target distribution q₀ over ℝ^d, and for each discretized time t_k draws independent Gaussian noise z_i ~ N(0, σ_t² I_d) to form noisy inputs x_i = e^{-t}x_i + z_i. It outputs a score estimate ŝ_t for each discretized time t_k, implemented as a fully connected ReLU network with depth D, P parameters, and weights bounded by Θ, trained by empirical minimization of the denoising score-matching objective. These score estimates are then plugged into the DDPM reverse SDE to produce samples ε-close in TV to a distribution γm₂-close in 2-Wasserstein to q₀.

## Method

The core contribution is replacing the L² score estimation error with a (1−δ)-quantile error D_p^δ(f,g) ≤ ε (i.e., the probability of pointwise error exceeding ε is at most δ), which is robust to rare large errors that dominate L². The key technical lemma shows that for any fixed time t, the ERM of the score-matching objective achieves this quantile accuracy with sample complexity independent of σ_t, by a two-stage concentration argument: first showing a clipped intermediate quantity concentrates over the data points x, then applying Bernstein's inequality over the Gaussian noise z|x. For the sampling side, the paper adapts the BBDD24 analysis by conditioning on the event that all score estimates are pointwise bounded at every discretization step (which holds with probability 1−δ via union bound), then using Girsanov's theorem and Pinsker's inequality to bound the TV distance. A net argument over the neural network parameter space (with size ~PD log Θ) converts the finite-class result to the network setting, yielding the final bound Õ(d²PD/ε³ · log Θ · log³(1/γ)) samples.

## Results

The main result (Theorem 1.2) establishes a sample complexity bound of Õ(d²PD/ε³ · log Θ · log³(1/γ)) for training ReLU neural network score estimates via the standard score-matching objective, sufficient for DDPM to sample within ε in TV of a distribution γm₂-close in 2-Wasserstein to q₀. This improves upon the prior bound from Block et al. [BMR20] of Õ(d^{5/2}/(γ³ε²) · (Θ²P)^D · √D), achieving an exponential improvement in the dependence on the Wasserstein error γ (from poly(1/γ) to polylog(1/γ)), the weight bound Θ (from (Θ²P)^D to PD·log Θ), and network depth D (from (Θ²P)^D to PD), at the cost of a slightly worse polynomial in ε (ε³ vs. ε²). The key technical ingredient is a new (1−δ)-quantile error measure D_p^δ that replaces the standard L² error, which is shown to be information-theoretically impossible to achieve with fewer than poly(1/γ) samples (Section 4).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is with Block, Mroueh, and Rakhlin [BMR20], who first analyzed the sample complexity of score-matching ERM for diffusion models. Their bound scales as Õ(d^{5/2}/(γ³ε²) · (Θ²P)^D · √D), which is polynomial in 1/γ and exponential in both D and log Θ. This paper's bound of Õ(d²PD/ε³ · log Θ · log³(1/γ)) is exponentially better in γ, Θ, and D, while also improving the polynomial dependence on d (d² vs. d^{5/2}) and P (linear vs. P^D). The trade-off is a worse ε dependence (ε³ vs. ε²). The paper also contrasts with the sampling-side results of [BBDD24] and [CCL+23b], which show that polylog(1/γ) steps suffice for sampling given L²-accurate scores; this paper bridges the gap by showing that the score-matching ERM achieves the necessary (weaker) accuracy with only polylog(1/γ) samples, closing the loop between training and sampling complexity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously separate lines of diffusion model theory: the sample complexity of training score estimates (Block et al. 2020) and the iteration complexity of sampling via the reverse SDE (Chen et al. 2023; Benton et al. 2024). While BMR20 established that the score-matching ERM achieves L²-accurate scores with poly(1/γ) samples, and BBDD24 showed that polylog(1/γ) sampling steps suffice given L²-accurate scores, no prior work connected these two sides. The key departure is showing that L² accuracy is information-theoretically impossible to achieve with polylog(1/γ) samples, and that a weaker (1−δ)-quantile error measure—introduced here—suffices for both training and sampling, thereby closing the gap.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This result provides the first theoretical justification for why the standard score-matching training procedure used in practice (DDPM) achieves near-optimal sample complexity in the Wasserstein error parameter, resolving a gap between what training can achieve and what sampling requires. The (1−δ)-quantile error is a natural, outlier-robust measure that practitioners can interpret: rare large errors in the score don't degrade sample quality. A major open question the authors explicitly flag is that the analysis covers only the empirical risk minimizer, not SGD, which is what is actually used in practice—understanding why SGD over ReLU networks performs well remains one of the biggest open problems in theoretical ML.
