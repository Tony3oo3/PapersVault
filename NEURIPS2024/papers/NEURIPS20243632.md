## Problem

Low-rank optimal transport methods based on factored couplings (Forrow et al. 2019; Scotbon et al. 2021) parameterize the transport plan with a diagonal inner matrix, forcing the two sub-couplings to share a single inner marginal. This coupling of variables prevents the optimization from decoupling into independent sub-problems, complicates extensions to unbalanced and semi-relaxed settings, and limits interpretability. The latent coupling (LC) factorization of Lin et al. (2021), which introduces a general non-diagonal coupling T linking two distinct inner marginals, had not been explored as a parameterization for the primal low-rank OT problem.

## Input / Output

The method takes two point clouds of sizes n and m with probability weight vectors a and b, a ground cost matrix C (or intra-domain cost matrices A, B for Gromov-Wasserstein), and a target rank r. It outputs a low-rank transport plan P = Q diag(1/g_Q) T diag(1/g_R) R^T where Q ∈ R^{n×r}, R ∈ R^{m×r}, and T ∈ R^{r₁×r₂} is a general (possibly non-square) coupling between the two distinct inner marginals g_Q and g_R. The non-square T allows different latent dimensions for source and target, enabling modeling of mass-splitting between clusters of unequal number. LC-projections Y⁽¹⁾ and Y⁽²⁾ provide barycentric coarse-grainings of the input datasets.

## Method

FRLC optimizes the low-rank OT objective over the LC factorization using coordinate mirror descent that alternates between two block updates: a 'factor relaxation' step on (Q, R) and a latent coupling step on T. The factor relaxation step adds KL penalties on the inner marginals of Q and R relative to the previous iterate, which decouples the joint update into two independent semi-relaxed OT problems (one per sub-coupling), each solved by a Sinkhorn-type iteration. The T step, with Q and R fixed, reduces to a balanced OT problem on the r×r (or r₁×r₂) latent coupling, also solved by Sinkhorn. Because T is a valid coupling between g_Q and g_R by construction, the resulting P automatically satisfies the outer marginal constraints without requiring Dykstra projections onto the intersection of constraint sets. The scheme extends to GW and FGW objectives by replacing the linear cost gradient with the quadratic GW gradient, and to unbalanced or semi-relaxed marginal constraints by swapping the Sinkhorn sub-routine for the appropriate relaxed projection, all within a single unified algorithm. A non-asymptotic convergence guarantee is established via a block coordinate descent lemma, with the τ-weighted KL regularization on inner marginals ensuring a uniform lower bound that yields objective smoothness.

## Results

On synthetic balanced Wasserstein OT (two moons + 8 Gaussians, 2D/10D Gaussian mixtures, n=m=1000–5000), FRLC consistently achieves lower transport cost ⟨C,P⟩_F than LOT across all ranks (20–200) and all initializations, while running at half the CPU runtime of LOT. On the large-scale spatial transcriptomics alignment task (E11.5 vs E12.5 mouse embryo, ~30k vs ~50k cells), semi-relaxed FRLC (FRLC-SR) achieves the best Spearman correlation, ARI, and AMI on all three OT objectives (W, GW, FGW) by a large margin over LOT-U. On unsupervised graph partitioning, FRLC outperforms GWL on 9/12 and SpecGWL on 11/12 real-world graph datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against LOT (Scetbon et al., 2021), FRLC wins on every synthetic benchmark: lower primal transport cost at every rank from 20 to 200, tighter marginal constraints, and roughly half the runtime (CPU) on n=m=1000, r=100. Against LOT-U (Scetbon et al., 2023) on spatial transcriptomics alignment, unbalanced FRLC (FRLC-U) matches or slightly exceeds LOT-U on all three objectives, while the semi-relaxed variant FRLC-SR dominates on all three downstream metrics (Spearman ρ, ARI, AMI) by a large margin, motivated by the biological asymmetry in cell lineage. Against GWL (Xu et al., 2019) and SpecGWL (Chowdhury & Needham, 2021) on graph partitioning, FRLC (semi-relaxed GW) achieves better clustering on 9/12 and 11/12 datasets respectively. Against Latent OT (Lin et al., 2021), FRLC achieves significantly lower primal OT cost on all synthetic experiments, since Latent OT solves only a k-Wasserstein barycenter proxy rather than the primal rank-constrained problem.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate lines of work on low-rank optimal transport: the factored coupling parameterization of Forrow et al. (2019) / Scotbon et al. (2021), which optimizes a primal OT cost over a diagonal inner matrix, and the latent coupling (LC) factorization of Lin et al. (2021), which was introduced for a k-Wasserstein barycenter proxy. The key observation is that factored couplings are a special case of LC factorizations (with diagonal T), making LC a strict generalization. The algorithmic departure from Scotbon et al. (2021) is the use of coordinate mirror descent over the LC factors, which decouples the optimization into three independent OT sub-problems (two semi-relaxed for Q and R, one balanced for T), eliminating the need for Dykstra projections onto the intersection of constraint sets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The decoupling into three OT sub-problems provides a simpler, faster algorithm (half the runtime of LOT) that handles all combinations of OT objectives (W, GW, FGW) and marginal constraints (balanced, unbalanced, semi-relaxed) within a single framework. The non-diagonal, potentially non-square latent coupling T offers genuine interpretability—capturing mass-splitting and differing cluster counts between source and target—that the diagonal factored coupling cannot express, which is particularly valuable for spatial transcriptomics alignment. Open questions include principled rank selection, strengthening the non-asymptotic convergence criterion, and understanding what structure LC factorizations capture when the true optimal plan is full-rank (e.g., when a Monge map exists).
