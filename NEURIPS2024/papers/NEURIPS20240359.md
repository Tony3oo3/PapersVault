## Problem

In cross-domain few-shot classification, methods like URL implicitly assume that prototype and image instance embeddings share the same linear transformation during adaptation. This paper shows that a natural 'modality-like' gap exists between prototype and image embeddings (analogous to the text-image gap in CLIP), and that a shared transformation shrinks this gap, removes discriminative information from gradients, and prevents learning compact, well-separated class clusters.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240359/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a frozen pre-trained ResNet-18 backbone (distilled from 8 domain-specific backbones following URL) and operates on vary-way vary-shot tasks sampled from Meta-Dataset. During adaptation, labeled support images are fed through the backbone to produce embeddings, from which class prototypes (mean vectors) are computed; two separate transformation heads then map these into a shared metric space where query images are classified by nearest-prototype similarity. The output is a single class label per query image, with the key structural difference from URL being that prototypes and images pass through distinct parameterized heads rather than a single shared one.

## Method

CoPA (Contrastive Prototype-Image Adaptation) replaces URL's single shared linear head with two independent transformation heads: h_θP for prototype embeddings and h_θI for image instance embeddings, mirroring CLIP's dual-encoder design by treating prototypes as 'text prompts.' The training objective is a symmetric cross-entropy (SCE) loss over the support set, where each image is matched to its own prototype and contrasted against all other prototypes (and vice versa), with a learnable temperature. The paper proves (Theorem 3.1) that the NCC loss lower bound decomposes into maximizing instance-prototype similarity and minimizing inter-sample similarity, and that a shared transformation collapses the distinct gradient signals for the two heads into one, losing discriminative information. The SCE loss naturally enlarges the prototype-image gap (from ~0.22 to ~1.38 in embedding norm), which the paper shows corresponds to the global minimum of validation loss, and the two-head design expands the hypothesis space to allow each modality to find its optimal representation distribution.

## Results

Under the 'train on all datasets' setting, CoPA achieves 76.8% mean accuracy across all 13 Meta-Dataset domains (vary-way vary-shot tasks), outperforming URL (75.8%) and achieving the best average rank (3.0) among all methods. CoPA+TSA (CoPA with extra task-specific adapter modules) reaches 79.9% average accuracy and 78.7% on unseen domains, surpassing TSA (79.2%/77.3%) and TA²-Net (78.3%/75.2%). Under the harder 'train on ImageNet only' setting, CoPA achieves 67.7% average accuracy vs. URL's 65.9%, and CoPA+TSA reaches 72.0% average accuracy, the best among all methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoPA consistently outperforms its direct predecessor URL on all 13 datasets under both evaluation settings, with average improvements of +1.0% (all domains) and +1.5% (unseen) under 'train on all datasets,' and +1.8% (all) and +2.1% (unseen) under 'train on ImageNet only.' Against the strongest competitors TSA and TA²-Net (which plug extra adapter modules into the backbone), CoPA alone is competitive on seen domains (80.6% vs. 80.1%/80.2%) but trails on unseen domains (70.9% vs. 77.3%/75.2%), reflecting that TSA/TA²-Net benefit from additional task-specific parameters. However, CoPA+TSA (combining CoPA's dual-head contrastive adaptation with TSA's adapter modules) achieves the best overall performance at 79.9% average accuracy and 78.7% on unseen domains, surpassing TSA by +0.7% (all) and +1.4% (unseen) and TA²-Net by +1.6% (all) and +3.5% (unseen). Under the more challenging 'train on ImageNet only' setting, CoPA+TSA reaches 72.0% average accuracy, beating TSA (71.6%) and TA²-Net (70.8%), with particularly large gains on unseen domains such as Aircraft (+4.0% over URL) and Quick Draw (+3.4% over URL).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the URL framework (Li et al., ICCV 2021), which applies a single shared linear transformation to both prototype and image instance embeddings for cross-domain few-shot classification, by identifying that this shared transformation implicitly assumes the two embedding types are interchangeable. Drawing an analogy to the modality gap in CLIP (Radford et al., ICML 2021) and the analysis of Liang et al. (NeurIPS 2022), the authors show that prototypes and images occupy distinct regions of the feature space and should be transformed separately. CoPA thus repurposes CLIP's dual-encoder contrastive learning paradigm—treating prototypes as 'text prompts'—within the single-modality few-shot classification setting, replacing the nearest-centroid-classifier loss with a symmetric cross-entropy objective over two independent transformation heads.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of a 'modality gap' between prototypes and images in a purely visual, single-modality setting is a useful conceptual reframing that challenges the standard practice of sharing a transformation in prototype-based few-shot methods. The practical takeaway is straightforward: two linear heads plus SCE loss yield consistent SOTA improvements on Meta-Dataset with less compute than URL+TSA. However, the paper acknowledges that SCE loss degrades with small batch sizes, limiting applicability in very few-shot regimes, and the overall gains over URL are modest (0.7–1.5% average). An open question is whether the gap-enlargement mechanism generalizes to other prototype-based or metric-learning frameworks beyond the specific URL/CoPA pipeline.
