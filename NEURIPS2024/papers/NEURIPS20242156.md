## Problem

Existing sequential recommendation models assume uniform time intervals between consecutive interactions, failing to capture how user preference uncertainty grows as the gap widens. NODE-based methods, while modeling continuous-time dynamics, lack any uncertainty quantification mechanism, making them unable to adaptively explore diverse items when a user has been inactive for a long period.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242156/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a user's chronological interaction sequence with actual UNIX timestamps (yielding variable time gaps Δt between consecutive items) and initial user/item embeddings. It outputs a next-item ranking with a predicted rating score γ, plus four evidential parameters (γ, ν, α, β) from which epistemic uncertainty Var[μ] = β/(ν(α−1)) and aleatoric uncertainty E[σ²] = β/(α−1) are derived. The monotonic network specifically ingests Δt to produce uncertainty parameters with a guaranteed monotonic relationship to the time gap.

## Method

E-NSDE integrates a Neural Stochastic Differential Equation module (with drift and diffusion functions solved via the SDE adjoint method) for continuous user/item representation learning, with an Evidential Deep Learning module that places a Normal-Inverse-Gamma prior over the rating to quantify both aleatoric and epistemic uncertainty. A monotonic network enforces the constraint that larger interaction gaps increase total uncertainty β (via all-positive weights) while decreasing pseudo-observations α and ν (via negative last-layer weights), guaranteeing that epistemic uncertainty Var[μ] grows monotonically with Δt. Training combines an evidential loss (NLL + epistemic-aware regularizer) with a Weighted BPR loss that up-weights negative items similar to future positives using uncertainty-adjusted scores, enabling uncertainty-guided exploration of diverse items. The entire pipeline is trained end-to-end with the SDE module updated via the adjoint method and the EDL/monotonic networks via Adam.

## Results

E-NSDE achieves the best P@5 and nDCG@5 across all four primary datasets: 0.4711/0.4112 on MovieLens-100K, 0.4551/0.3982 on MovieLens-1M, 0.4194/0.3637 on Netflix, and 0.4021/0.3621 on Amazon Book. The gains over the strongest ODE-based baseline GRU-ODE range from +2.0 to +3.5 percentage points in P@5, and over the best sequential baseline ResAct from +2.5 to +3.5 points. On the sparser Amazon Book dataset (99.98% sparsity), E-NSDE also leads at higher cutoffs (P@10=0.7021, P@20=0.8175) compared to all baselines including GDERec and DuoRec.

## Baselines & Comparisons

E-NSDE outperforms all 18 baselines on every dataset and metric. Against the closest ODE-based competitor GRU-ODE, E-NSDE gains +3.13 P@5 on MovieLens-100K (0.4711 vs 0.4398), +2.76 on MovieLens-1M (0.4551 vs 0.4275), +2.00 on Netflix (0.4194 vs 0.3994), and +1.65 on Amazon Book (0.4021 vs 0.3856). Against the strongest sequential baseline ResAct, the margins are +3.45 (ML-100K), +2.65 (ML-1M), +3.27 (Netflix), and +1.37 (Amazon Book) in P@5. Dynamic models (timeSVD++, CKF) and graph-based models (NGCF, LightGCN) trail by 5–11 points in P@5, confirming that continuous-time modeling with uncertainty quantification is critical. On the sparser Amazon Book dataset, E-NSDE also leads at P@10 (0.7021) and P@20 (0.8175), surpassing the next-best GDERec (0.6808/0.7928) and DuoRec (0.6831/0.7945). The ablation confirms that all three components (NSDE, EDL, WBPR) contribute additively, with the full model reaching 0.4711 P@5 versus 0.4065 for NSDE alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: neural ODE/SDE-based continuous-time modeling (Chen et al. 2018; Li et al. 2020), evidential deep learning for uncertainty quantification (Sensoy et al. 2018; Amini et al. 2020), and monotonic network constraints (Sill 1997). It departs from prior ODE-based recommenders (GRU-ODE, LT-OCF) by replacing deterministic ODEs with stochastic differential equations to capture inherent noise in user-item interactions, and from standard sequential models (BERT4Rec, SASRec) by removing the uniform-interval assumption. The key conceptual departure is the monotonic network that mathematically enforces a positive correlation between interaction time gap and epistemic uncertainty, bridging the NSDE representation learning and EDL prediction modules in a way no prior recommender has done.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap: real user interactions have highly variable time gaps, yet most sequential recommenders treat them as uniform, and ODE-based recommenders lack any uncertainty signal to guide exploration. The theoretical results (Theorem 1, Lemmas 1–2, Theorem 2) provide a clean mathematical justification for why longer gaps should increase uncertainty, which is useful beyond this specific architecture. A stated limitation is that the time-aware component becomes less impactful when interactions are uniformly spaced (e.g., click-through streams), and the approach has only been validated on rating-based datasets. Open questions include whether the NSDE+EDL combination scales to industrial-scale implicit-feedback settings and whether the monotonic constraint generalizes to other time-dependent uncertainty relationships.
