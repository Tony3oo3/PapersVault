## Problem

Standard robot controllers trained with RL cannot be reused when a robot's morphology changes (e.g., adding a pair of legs), even if the new robot is built from the same standardized assemblies. Existing structure-transfer approaches like NerveNet and MetaMorph require large, diverse robot datasets for pretraining and struggle in the highly data-scarce setting of transferring from a single robot. The paper asks: can we learn modular controllers from one robot and one task that meaningfully capture per-assembly coordination mechanics, so that transferring to a new morphology reduces to learning only inter-assembly coordination?

## Input / Output

The boss controller receives the full observation vector comprising global state (base position, velocity, angular velocity, orientation) and local per-joint observations (joint angle, velocity, relative position and orientation within the module frame). Each worker module receives only the local observations of its assigned joints concatenated with a D-dimensional signal from the boss, enforcing information asymmetry. The output is a set of per-actuator action means, with modules of the same type (e.g., all 'leg' modules) sharing parameters across instances, so the architecture scales to robots with more or fewer instances of the same assembly type.

## Method

MeMo introduces a hierarchical modular architecture where a boss controller B outputs a latent signal to type-shared worker modules W_k^i, each responsible for decoding actions for a physical assembly. The key technical contribution is a modularity objective—minimizing the output difference D(s_i, η) = W_φ(B_θ(s_i)+η) − W_φ(B_θ(s_i)) under Gaussian noise η injected into B's output—that forces modules to be insensitive to perturbations in the boss signal, effectively reducing the interface dimensionality and pushing coordination responsibility into the modules. This objective is optimized simultaneously with standard behavior cloning via a single noise-injection loss, which the paper proves decomposes into the sum of the two objectives plus a negligible product term. The training pipeline proceeds in three phases: (1) train an expert monolithic controller with PPO, (2) pretrain the modular architecture via DAgger imitation learning with noise injected into B's output, and (3) transfer the frozen modules to a new morphology or task and retrain only the boss controller (or finetune end-to-end) with RL.

## Results

On structure transfer, MeMo is 2× more sample efficient than the best baseline on the 6→12 leg centipede and 6→10 leg hybrid tasks, while also converging to significantly higher final reward than any baseline. On the 6→10 leg worm and 4→5 finger claw transfers, MeMo outperforms all baselines in training efficiency and matches or exceeds their final performance. On task transfer (Frozen→Ridged/Gap/Stepped terrains and cube→sphere grasping), MeMo achieves improved training efficiency over MetaMorph and pretrained MLP/modular baselines, and outperforms NerveNet baselines on the Stepped Terrain and sphere grasping.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MeMo outperforms all three external baselines (NerveNet-Conv, NerveNet-Snowflake, and MetaMorph) on every structure transfer task. Against NerveNet-Conv and NerveNet-Snowflake, MeMo achieves 2× sample efficiency on the 12 leg centipede and 10 leg hybrid while also reaching higher final reward; on the 10 leg worm, MeMo matches NerveNet-Conv's final performance but trains faster. MetaMorph struggles with locomotion tasks due to the high dimensionalities of the transfer robots and is outperformed by MeMo on all locomotion transfers. On task transfer, MeMo outperforms NerveNet baselines on the Stepped Terrain (climbing) and sphere grasping, where the pretrained NerveNets struggle to coordinate arm and claw components, resulting in high variance across seeds. MeMo also beats the internal baselines (RL from scratch with MLP or modular architecture), confirming that the noise-injection pretraining—not just the modular architecture—drives the transfer gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

MeMo sits at the intersection of modular robot control (Devin et al., Huang et al., Pathak et al.) and structure transfer via GNN/Transformer policies (NerveNet, Snowflake, MetaMorph). It departs from the data-driven universal-controller paradigm of NerveNet and MetaMorph, which require large diverse robot datasets, by learning transferable modules from a single robot and task. The key methodological departure is the noise-injection modularity objective, which repurposes Gaussian noise (previously studied as a regularizer in Camuto et al. and Igl et al.) not for generalization per se, but to enforce a high-dimensional null space in the worker module's Jacobian, thereby narrowing the effective interface between boss and worker. The DAgger-based IL pretraining pipeline (Ross et al.) is integral, as the paper shows noise injection must be applied during imitation learning rather than RL to produce transferable modules.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MeMo offers a practical path to reducing the cost of robot re-design: instead of retraining from scratch or curating large multi-morphology datasets, a single robot's controller can be decomposed into reusable assembly-level modules. The ablation showing that noise injection is the critical component (L1/L2 regularization and Jacobian penalties all fail) provides a clear mechanistic explanation for why the modules transfer. However, the framework requires a domain expert to specify the module partitioning, is limited to incrementally different morphologies, and has only been validated in simulation. Open questions include whether modules can be discovered automatically, whether the approach scales to multi-robot/multi-task pretraining, and whether the sim-to-real gap introduces additional challenges beyond those faced by standard RL policies.
