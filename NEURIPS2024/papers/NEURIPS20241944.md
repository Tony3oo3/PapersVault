## Problem

Existing preference-based RL and RLHF methods optimize a risk-neutral mean reward, yet critical applications like autonomous driving, healthcare, and AI safety require risk-awareness. The core technical barrier is that PbRL operates under a one-episode-reward setting where rewards are defined over entire trajectories rather than per-step, rendering standard risk-aware objectives (which depend on state-action rewards) inapplicable. This paper proves that nested and static quantile risk objectives remain well-defined and computable in this setting, and provides the first provably efficient algorithm with finite-time regret guarantees for risk-aware PbRL.

## Input / Output

At each episode, the algorithm receives two trajectories generated by two policies and a single binary preference label sampled from a Bradley-Terry model over the trajectory reward difference. The trajectory reward is computed as a dot product between a known trajectory embedding φ(ξ_H) ∈ R^{dim_T} and an unknown weight vector w_r*, with embedding components bounded in {0} ∪ [b, B]. Because individual step rewards are unobservable, the output policy must be history-dependent (mapping full trajectory prefixes to actions) rather than Markovian, and the algorithm also produces sublinear regret upper bounds that depend on the minimum state-action visitation probability and the minimum trajectory-dimension visitation probability.

## Method

RA-PbRL maintains confidence sets for both the transition kernel (via least-squares regression on observed transitions) and the trajectory reward function (via least-squares regression on preference labels), then constructs a policy confidence set containing all policies that are near-optimal under some parameterization within those confidence sets. The algorithm selects the most exploratory policy pair from this set (maximizing the gap between the best and worst estimated values) to balance exploitation and exploration. The key distinction between nested and static objectives lies in the value iteration: nested uses a Bellman-type recursion applying the quantile risk measure Φ at each step, while static uses a distributional Bellman equation over the entire trajectory reward distribution. To handle the history-dependence inherent in one-episode rewards, the state space is expanded to trajectory prefixes, and the non-linearity introduced by the quantile function is managed through Lipschitz-based concentration arguments. The paper establishes sublinear regret upper bounds for both objectives and constructs hard-to-learn instances proving matching lower bounds.

## Results

On a tabular MDP (H=6, A=3, S=4) with risk levels α ∈ {0.05, 0.10, 0.20, 0.40}, RA-PbRL's cumulative regret for both static and nested CVaR initially grows linearly with K before transitioning to sublinear growth, consistent with the theoretical O(√K) bounds. On MuJoCo Half-Cheetah (10,000 preference signals over 10,000,000 timesteps), RA-PbRL similarly exhibits sublinear regret growth. The paper reports that RA-PbRL's regret in worst-case scenarios is significantly lower than competing baselines, and that increasing α (less risk-averse) consistently reduces regret across all settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares RA-PbRL against three baselines: PbOP (Chen et al., 2022), a risk-neutral PbRL algorithm with general function approximation; ICVaR-RLHF (Chen et al., 2023), a nested CVaR-based RLHF method; and TRC (Kim and Oh, 2022), a trust-region CVaR method for safe RL. RA-PbRL outperforms all three baselines in worst-case (bad-scenario) regret across both the tabular MDP and MuJoCo Half-Cheetah settings, with the gap most pronounced at low α (high risk aversion). Compared to ICVaR-RLHF, RA-PbRL achieves the key advantage of driving both policies toward optimality simultaneously, whereas ICVaR-RLHF suffers from an unavoidable linear strong nested CVaR regret due to its random reference trajectory selection. Against PbOP, RA-PbRL adds risk-awareness without sacrificing the sublinear regret guarantee, while PbOP only optimizes mean reward. TRC is included primarily in the MuJoCo experiments as a continuous-space safe RL baseline; RA-PbRL's confidence-set-based exploration strategy provides tighter regret control than TRC's trust-region approach in the preference-based setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the PbOP algorithm (Chen et al., 2022) for preference-based RL with general function approximation by incorporating risk-aware objectives (nested and static CVaR) that were previously only defined for standard RL with per-step rewards. It departs from prior risk-aware RL work (Fei et al. 2020, Du et al. 2022, Bastani et al. 2022) by proving these objectives remain computable and uniquely solvable under the one-episode-reward constraint of PbRL, where only trajectory-level preferences are available. The key technical departure is handling the non-linearity introduced by quantile functions in the Bellman equation and the history-dependent policy structure required when per-step rewards are unobservable.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first provably efficient risk-aware PbRL algorithm with finite-time regret guarantees, addressing a critical gap in safety-critical applications (autonomous driving, healthcare, LLM alignment) where mean-reward optimization is insufficient. The sublinear regret bounds for both nested and static objectives provide theoretical grounding for deploying risk-aware preference learning in practice. Key limitations include the restriction to two-trajectory comparisons, linear reward parameterization, and a gap between upper and lower bounds that remains open. Future work on n-wise comparisons, non-linear rewards, and tighter bounds would strengthen practical applicability.
