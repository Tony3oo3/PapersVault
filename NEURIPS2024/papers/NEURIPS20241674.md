## Problem

Text-conditioned 3D human-object interaction (HOI) generation is severely limited by the scarcity of paired text-interaction data, making supervised approaches that work well for text-to-motion infeasible at scale. Existing HOI datasets are orders of magnitude smaller than text-motion corpora like HumanML3D, and annotating them to match is impractical. This paper asks whether text-aligned HOI sequences can be generated without any direct text-interaction supervision.

## Input / Output

The method takes a free-form natural language description of an interaction (e.g., 'A person holds a plastic container with their right hand and moves it in different directions') and produces a variable-length sequence of SMPL human pose parameters paired with rigid object poses (3D position and orientation). The sequence length is dynamically determined by the text-to-motion model based on the input text, and the human body shape is fixed to a canonical SMPL shape consistent with HumanML3D. The object is treated as a single rigid body, and the output is a per-frame tuple of (human pose, object pose) at 30 Hz.

## Method

InterDreamer formulates HOI generation as a Markov decision process that decouples interaction semantics from dynamics. A large language model (GPT-4 or Llama-2) performs high-level planning to identify the object category, contact body parts, and rewrite the text to bridge the distribution gap with text-to-motion training data. A pre-trained text-to-motion model (MDM, MotionDiffuse, ReMoDiffuse, or MotionGPT) then generates human actions autoregressively, while a handcrafted or learning-based retrieval module establishes the initial object pose from a contact-map database. The core novel component is a world model that predicts object state transitions using only contact vertices (sampled via signed distance fields) as actions, with a two-branch cross-attention architecture (unconditional branch for free motion, conditional branch for contact) that generalizes across object shapes. A gradient-based optimization step refines both human and object poses using fitting, velocity, contact, and penetration losses. Only the world model requires training (on BEHAVE); all other components leverage pre-trained models, enabling zero-shot generalization to unseen objects and datasets.

## Results

On the BEHAVE dataset, InterDreamer with high-level planning consistently improves all text-to-motion backbones: MotionGPT achieves the best FID of 4.751 (vs. 5.497 without planning) and R-Precision Top-1 of 0.234, while ReMoDiffuse reaches FID 5.237 (vs. 6.385). On the dynamics model evaluation (Table 1), the proposed contact-vertex action representation achieves the lowest CMD of 0.151 and penetration of 443×10⁻²%, outperforming the unconditional model (CMD 0.424), InterDiff markers (CMD 0.219), and raw human motion (CMD 0.325). On OMOMO, the same trend holds with MotionGPT+planning reaching FID 17.512 vs. 18.472 without planning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares four text-to-motion backbones (MDM, MotionDiffuse, ReMoDiffuse, MotionGPT) with and without the proposed high-level planning, plus InterDiff as a dynamics-model baseline. Adding planning consistently improves FID across all backbones: MDM drops from 12.279 to 10.374, MotionDiffuse from 10.208 to 9.015, ReMoDiffuse from 6.385 to 5.237, and MotionGPT from 5.497 to 4.751 on BEHAVE. MotionGPT with planning achieves the best overall motion quality (lowest FID, highest R-Precision Top-1 of 0.234), while ReMoDiffuse with planning offers the best MM Dist (4.784). For the dynamics model, the proposed contact-vertex action (CMD 0.151, Pene 443) outperforms InterDiff's contact markers (CMD 0.219, Pene 484) and raw human motion input (CMD 0.325, Pene 957), confirming that vertex-level abstraction provides more generalizable and fine-grained interaction control. The framework also generalizes zero-shot to CHAIRS and OMOMO objects without retraining the dynamics model, a capability none of the supervised baselines offer.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant supervised paradigm for text-to-HOI generation (CG-HOI, HOI-Diff, HOIAnimator, THOR), which requires large-scale text-interaction pair annotations that are unavailable at the scale needed for complex whole-body interactions. Instead, it repurposes pre-trained text-to-motion diffusion models (MDM, MotionDiffuse, ReMoDiffuse, MotionGPT) as the low-level action generator and LLMs (GPT-4, Llama-2) as a high-level semantic planner, while introducing a lightweight world model trained on unlabeled HOI data to handle object dynamics. The MDP formulation and the ControlNet-style two-branch architecture for the world model draw from robotics and image editing literature, but the specific decoupling of interaction semantics from dynamics via contact-vertex-based actions is novel to this work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that text-aligned 3D HOI generation is feasible without any text-interaction supervision, addressing a fundamental data bottleneck that has limited the field. The modular design—where only the world model requires training and all other components are plug-and-play—makes the framework easy to extend to new objects, datasets, or motion generators. However, limitations remain: results can exhibit foot skating and physically implausible artifacts, hand poses are coarse due to dataset constraints, and quantitative comparison to supervised methods is inherently unfair, leaving open whether zero-shot quality is sufficient for production applications. A key open question is whether incorporating physics simulators or model-based learning could close the gap to physically grounded interaction synthesis.
