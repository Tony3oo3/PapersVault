## Problem

Existing federated fine-tuning methods like FedIT independently average local LoRA modules A and B, which introduces mathematically incorrect cross-product noise terms into the global model update, slowing convergence. Additionally, these methods cannot aggregate LoRA adapters with heterogeneous ranks, preventing clients with varying data complexity or computational resources from participating in federated fine-tuning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240708/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each client receives the full pre-trained LLM (Llama-7B, Llama2-7B, or TinyLlama-1.1B) and fine-tunes LoRA adapters (A ∈ R^{r_k×n}, B ∈ R^{m×r_k}) on local instruction data (Dolly, Alpaca, Wizard, or ShareGPT). The server aggregates by stacking, producing global LoRA modules whose rank equals the sum of all local ranks (e.g., 160 for 10 clients with ranks [64,32,16,16,8,8,4,4,4,4]). The scaling factor p_k is applied only to one side (A_k) to prevent p_k² from appearing in the final BA product, a critical design choice absent in FedIT.

## Method

FLORA replaces FedIT's independent averaging of LoRA modules with a stacking-based aggregation: all local A_k matrices are concatenated vertically and all local B_k matrices are concatenated horizontally, so that the global update BA exactly equals the weighted sum of local updates Σ p_k B_k A_k without any cross-product noise terms. This stacking operation (denoted ⊕) is proven to be mathematically exact (Equation 10) and inherently supports heterogeneous ranks since it only requires matching outer dimensions m and n, not the inner rank dimension r_k. The paper provides a convergence analysis under standard FedAvg assumptions plus an unbiased LoRA gradient assumption, showing FLORA achieves O(1/T) convergence while FedIT's averaging introduces non-gradient noise that breaks this rate. FLORA is also compatible with AdaLoRA for dynamic rank adaptation on individual clients, reducing total trainable parameters while maintaining or improving performance.

## Results

FLORA consistently outperforms FedIT across all evaluated models (TinyLlama, Llama, Llama2) and tasks in both homogeneous and heterogeneous LoRA settings. On MT-bench (Wizard dataset), FLORA achieves 4.21 with Llama vs. FedIT's 3.07, and 3.13 with TinyLlama vs. FedIT's 2.92. On MMLU, FLORA nearly doubles FedIT's accuracy for TinyLlama on Dolly (30.80 vs. 16.35) and surpasses centralized LoRA in several settings (e.g., TinyLlama on Alpaca: 31.92 vs. 28.03). In heterogeneous settings, FLORA maintains strong performance (e.g., Llama MT-bench Wizard: 4.14) while Zero-Padding degrades severely (Llama MMLU Alpaca drops to 7.97%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against FedIT (the SOTA federated fine-tuning method), FLORA wins on every homogeneous-setting comparison across all three models and both evaluation benchmarks, with the largest gap on Llama MT-bench Wizard (4.21 vs. 3.07, a +1.14 improvement) and TinyLlama MMLU Dolly (30.80 vs. 16.35, nearly 2×). Against Zero-Padding (the only baseline supporting heterogeneous LoRA), FLORA dominates across all heterogeneous settings; Zero-Padding suffers catastrophic degradation in some cases (Llama MMLU Alpaca collapses to 7.97% vs. FLORA's 29.54%), while FLORA maintains performance close to its homogeneous counterpart. Against centralized LoRA fine-tuning, FLORA is competitive and occasionally exceeds it (e.g., TinyLlama MMLU Alpaca 31.92 vs. 28.03; Llama MT-bench ShareGPT 3.93 vs. 3.99), suggesting that the smaller per-client data volume in federated settings may mitigate overfitting. FLORA also integrates with AdaLoRA to reduce the sum of local ranks from 160 to 120–140 while preserving or slightly improving MT-bench scores, demonstrating that the stacking mechanism is compatible with adaptive rank allocation.

## Relation to Prior Work

FLoRA builds directly on the federated fine-tuning framework established by FedIT (Zhang et al., 2023), which combined LoRA with FedAvg to enable privacy-preserving LLM adaptation. The key departure is a mathematical correction: FedIT's independent averaging of LoRA matrices A and B introduces cross-product noise terms that deviate from the true weighted sum of local updates, whereas FLoRA's stacking-based aggregation is provably noise-free. This also naturally subsumes the zero-padding workaround (Cho et al., 2023) for heterogeneous ranks, eliminating the need for rank alignment across clients.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of a fundamental mathematical flaw in the most natural LoRA aggregation strategy, and its resolution via a simple stacking operation, provides a clean theoretical foundation for federated PEFT that prior work lacked. Practically, the heterogeneity support broadens participation to resource-constrained devices, and the integration with AdaLoRA shows the framework can further reduce trainable parameters. Open questions include how the linearly growing global rank behaves over many communication rounds, whether the approach composes well with differential privacy, and whether the scaling factor's dataset-dependent sensitivity can be addressed with adaptive schemes.
