## Problem

Research in auditory (ASR), visual (VSR), and audiovisual (AVSR) speech recognition has developed independently, and even recent self-supervised methods that address multiple tasks still produce separate models per task, leading to disjoint inference pipelines and redundant computation. Existing unified approaches like u-HuBERT can fine-tune a single model for all three tasks but fail to match the performance of separately fine-tuned models, partly due to well-known optimisation difficulties in VSR training from scratch.

## Input / Output

The model consumes three input types simultaneously: 96×96 grayscale lip-movement video frames (zero-masked up to 0.4 s per second), raw audio waveforms subsampled to the video frame rate (zero-masked up to 0.6 s per second), and their channel-concatenated audiovisual counterpart. All three modalities are batched together in a single forward pass through shared ResNet-18 feature extractors (2D with 3D stem for video, 1D for audio) followed by a pre-LN Transformer encoder-decoder. The output is a sequence of SentencePiece subword tokens (vocabulary size 1,000) decoded via CTC-attention hybrid beam search with optional shallow-fusion language model scoring.

## Method

USR trains a single pre-LN Transformer encoder-decoder (12-block Base, 512 hidden dim) to perform ASR, VSR, and AVSR by sharing all parameters across modalities, which the authors show overcomes VSR optimisation difficulties because audio's denser verbal information improves the optimisation landscape for visual training. The training pipeline combines self-supervised pre-training (masked prediction against EMA-teacher targets from unmasked audiovisual inputs, using a 2-block Transformer predictor and cosine-similarity loss) with semi-supervised fine-tuning that generates greedy pseudo-labels via argmax over both CTC and attention teacher outputs, filtered by a token-wise confidence threshold inspired by FixMatch. A key efficiency insight is that all three modalities share the same pseudo-labels and feature-extractor forward passes, amortising generation costs. The semi-supervised stage uses modality-specific labelled/unlabelled loss weights (γ_a, γ_v) to balance quantity versus quality, and the authors demonstrate that this approach is complementary to self-supervision, with audiovisual pre-training targets outperforming audio-only targets specifically under semi-supervised fine-tuning.

## Results

On LRS3 (high-resource, 433h labelled), the Large USR model with LRS3+VoxCeleb2 pre-training achieves 22.3% WER for VSR, 1.2% for ASR, and 1.1% for AVSR using a single shared model. In the low-resource setting (30h labelled), the Base model with LRS3 pre-training reaches 36.0% VSR, 3.2% ASR, and 3.0% AVSR, substantially outperforming the previous best self-supervised method BRAVEn (43.4% VSR, 4.0% ASR, 4.0% AVSR). On LRS2, USR achieves 15.4% VSR (with LM), 1.9% ASR, and 1.9% AVSR, surpassing RAVEn with self-training (17.9% VSR). On the challenging WildVSR benchmark, USR attains 46.4% VSR, matching RAVEn with self-training (46.7%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

USR consistently outperforms all self-supervised baselines across both low- and high-resource settings on LRS3. Against BRAVEn (the strongest prior self-supervised method), USR (Large, LRS3+Vox2) improves VSR by 4.3 points (22.3% vs. 26.6%) in the high-resource setting and by 6.7 points (28.4% vs. 35.1%) in the low-resource Base+ setting, while also matching or exceeding ASR/AVSR performance. Compared to u-HuBERT—the only other single-model approach—USR achieves substantially better VSR (22.3% vs. 29.1% HR) and competitive ASR/AVSR (1.2%/1.1% vs. 1.5%/1.3%). Against supervised methods on LRS3 (Table 5), USR with a language model (21.5% VSR) surpasses Auto-AVSR (23.5% VSR) despite using far less labelled data (433h vs. 1,902h) and no external ASR models. On LRS2, USR (15.4% VSR with LM) outperforms RAVEn with self-training (17.9%) and approaches Auto-AVSR (14.6%) which uses 4× more labelled data. On WildVSR, USR (46.4%) slightly edges out RAVEn with self-training (46.7%) and substantially outperforms AV-HuBERT with self-training (48.7%). The only comparison where USR does not lead is against the heavily data-hungry supervised methods like LP Conf (12.8% VSR with 100,000h) and SynthVSR (16.9% with 6,720h), which use orders of magnitude more labelled data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the self-supervised audiovisual speech representation learning line (AV-HuBERT, RAVEn, BRAVEn, AV-data2vec), which pre-trains on unlabelled data but then fine-tunes separate models per task, and on u-HuBERT, which demonstrated a single model can serve all three modalities but fell short of modality-specific performance. The key departure is a unified training framework that combines self-supervised pre-training with a greedy EMA-based pseudo-labelling semi-supervised stage, inspired by FixMatch's confidence thresholding from image recognition, to leverage unlabelled data throughout fine-tuning rather than only during pre-training. The paper also draws on the CTC-attention hybrid decoding framework and the observation that audio provides denser verbal information that improves the optimization landscape for visual modalities.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that a single Transformer model can achieve state-of-the-art performance across ASR, VSR, and AVSR simultaneously, this work reduces deployment complexity and memory overhead compared to maintaining three separate models. The finding that semi-supervised pseudo-labelling without pre-training outperforms self-supervised methods with pre-training (37.8% vs. 43.4% WER on LRS3 low-resource) challenges the assumption that self-supervised pre-training is necessary for competitive VSR. Open questions include whether the approach scales to alternative encoder architectures, how to improve pseudo-label quality beyond greedy argmax (e.g., beam search or language model integration), and how to incorporate large audio-only datasets. The computational cost of pseudo-labelling and the reliance on a single confidence threshold remain practical limitations.
