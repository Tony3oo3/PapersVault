## Problem

Existing motion modeling paradigms for flow-based video frame interpolation either combine bidirectional flows under strong linear/overlapped assumptions or predict bilateral flows at discrete timestamps without leveraging motion priors from input frames. Neither approach effectively captures complex spatiotemporal dynamics (e.g., occlusions, deformations, non-linear motion) in real-world videos, and discrete-time modeling is inherently unsuitable for arbitrary-timestep interpolation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242035/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

GIMM takes bidirectional optical flows (F_{0→1}, F_{1→0}) extracted by a pre-trained flow estimator (RAFT or FlowFormer) from two adjacent RGB frames, along with a continuous timestep t ∈ [0,1], and produces bilateral flows (F_{t→0}, F_{t→1}) at that timestep. The internal coordinate-based network operates on 3D spatiotemporal coordinates in [-1,1]³ and a D-dimensional motion latent, predicting normalized flows in [0,1] that are then denormalized via an instance-specific scaling factor. The full GIMM-VFI pipeline additionally consumes context and correlation features from the flow estimator to synthesize the final interpolated frame at any resolution.

## Method

GIMM introduces a generalizable implicit motion modeling framework built around a five-layer SIREN coordinate-based network conditioned on instance-specific motion latent. A Motion Encoder extracts features from normalized bidirectional flows; these features are forward-warped to the target timestep using scaled bidirectional flows with splatting weights derived from flow consistency and variance metrics, then refined by a Latent Refiner into the motion latent L_t. The SIREN network maps 3D spatiotemporal coordinates (x, y, t) together with L_t to predict the normalized flow at that coordinate, enabling continuous and resolution-agnostic motion prediction. Unlike per-scene INR approaches (e.g., IFE), GIMM achieves cross-video generalizability through the motion latent conditioning rather than test-time optimization, reducing parameters from ~44M (meta-learning variant) to 0.25M. The module is trained with MSE loss on normalized flows and integrated into an AMT-based frame synthesis pipeline for end-to-end VFI training on Vimeo90K.

## Results

On the arbitrary-timestep interpolation benchmarks, GIMM-VFI-R (RAFT-based) achieves 32.71 dB PSNR on XTest-2K, 31.88 dB on XTest-4K, 37.02 dB on SNU-FILM-arb-Medium (4X), 32.62 dB on Hard (8X), and 27.99 dB on Extreme (16X), outperforming the previous SOTA EMA-VFI by 0.18 dB, 0.67 dB, and ~0.30 dB respectively. GIMM-VFI-F (FlowFormer-based) achieves the best PSNR on XTest-2K (32.91 dB) and Extreme (28.01 dB). On motion modeling, GIMM achieves 37.56 dB PSNR / 0.34 EPE on Vimeo-Triplet-Flow and 30.45 dB / 2.68 EPE on Vimeo-Septuplet-Flow, surpassing all baselines including Linear (35.03/0.44) and End-to-End (29.23/1.02).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GIMM-VFI outperforms all non-INR baselines (RIFE, M2M, IFRNet, AMT, UPR-Net, EMA-VFI) on PSNR across every benchmark, with the largest gains over EMA-VFI (previous SOTA) of 0.67 dB on XTest-4K and 0.27 dB on SNU-FILM-arb-Extreme. On perceptual metrics, GIMM-VFI-R achieves the best FID on all five benchmarks (e.g., 6.52 vs. 7.21 for EMA-VFI on XTest-2K), while GIMM-VFI-F achieves the best LPIPS on XTest-2K (0.103) and XTest-4K (0.142). The INR-based baseline CURE is consistently outperformed, with GIMM-VFI-R leading by 2.47 dB PSNR on XTest-2K and 1.38 dB on Extreme. On motion modeling (Table 1), GIMM surpasses the Linear combination (Super SloMo), Forward Warp (Splatting-based), End-to-End (EMA-VFI), and BMBC baselines on both PSNR and EPE, with the largest EPE reduction over End-to-End (0.34 vs. 1.02 on VTF). When integrated into TTVFI and IFRNet, GIMM yields +1.07 to +1.58 dB PSNR improvements, confirming its plug-and-play utility. The only metric where GIMM-VFI does not lead is LPIPS on SNU-FILM-arb-Medium, where UPR-Net (0.033) and GIMM-VFI-R (0.033) tie, and M2M (0.036) is close.

## Relation to Prior Work

GIMM extends the implicit flow encoding idea from IFE (Figueirêdo et al., WACV 2023), which performs per-scene optimization of a coordinate-based network for each video, into a generalizable framework that operates across arbitrary videos by conditioning a SIREN network on instance-specific motion latent extracted from pre-trained bidirectional flows (RAFT or FlowFormer). It also draws on the broader generalizable INR literature (VideoINR, MoTIF, CURE) but is the first to apply this paradigm specifically to motion modeling within flow-based VFI, rather than to full video or image representation. The method is designed as a drop-in motion module that integrates with existing frame synthesis backbones (AMT, TTVFI, IFRNet), positioning it as a complementary component rather than a full end-to-end VFI system.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GIMM demonstrates that a 0.25M-parameter SIREN network, conditioned on motion latent, can outperform both a 4.27M U-Net and a 43.92M meta-learning Transformer for continuous flow prediction, suggesting that implicit representations are particularly well-suited to the continuous spatiotemporal structure of optical flow. The plug-and-play integration (showing ~1.2 dB gains when added to TTVFI or IFRNet) makes it practically useful for the VFI community. Key limitations include dependence on the quality of the pre-trained flow estimator and restriction to two consecutive input frames, leaving open whether the approach can handle longer sequences with large nonlinear motions or be extended to multi-frame conditioning.
