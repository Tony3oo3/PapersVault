## Problem

Evaluation of methods for link prediction on multi-relational temporal graphs suffers from two critical gaps: inconsistent evaluation protocols (mixed metrics, single-negative sampling, dataset version mismatches) and the absence of large-scale datasets that reflect real-world network sizes. Existing TKG and THG benchmarks contain fewer than two million edges, while real-world networks span tens of millions, making it impossible to assess whether methods scale to practical deployment.

## Input / Output

The benchmark consumes temporal multi-relational graph quadruples (s, r, o, t) where TKGs use discrete snapshot-based time (year or day granularity) and THGs use continuous second-wise timestamps with an additional node-type function. For evaluation, the model receives a query with one entity masked (e.g., (s, r, ?, t⁺)) and must rank all candidate entities; for TKGs both subject and object positions are predicted via inverse relations. On large datasets (tkgl-wikidata, thgl-github, thgl-myket), full 1-vs-all ranking is infeasible, so the benchmark uses edge-type-aware negative sampling (1-vs-q with q=1000, 100, or 20) that restricts negatives to entities sharing the true destination's edge type or node type, yielding MRR values closer to the exhaustive evaluation than random sampling.

## Method

TGB 2.0 is a benchmarking framework rather than a single model: it introduces eight new datasets (four TKGs spanning knowledge and political domains, four THGs spanning software, social, and interaction domains) with up to 53 million edges, and provides an automated pipeline for data downloading, processing, and evaluation using time-aware filtered MRR with pre-generated negative samples. The evaluation protocol treats link prediction as a single-step ranking task where temporal conflicts (edges known true at the same timestamp) are filtered from the candidate set. Experiments benchmark seven methods—RE-GCN, CEN, TLogic for TKGs; TGN, TGN with edge-type features, and STHN for THGs—against two deterministic heuristics (Recurrency Baseline and EdgeBank). Key empirical findings: leveraging edge-type information substantially improves THG performance (TGN with edge-type features jumps from 0.324 to 0.424 MRR on thgl-software), heuristic baselines are competitive with or outperform learned methods on several datasets, and all learned methods except EdgeBank fail to run on the largest datasets due to memory or time limits, exposing a critical scalability gap.

## Results

TGB 2.0 evaluates 7 methods across 8 multi-relational temporal graph datasets using time-aware filtered MRR. On TKG datasets, no single method dominates: RecB_train achieves the best test MRR on tkgl-smallpedia (0.605) and tkgl-icews (0.211), while TLogic leads on tkgl-polecat (0.228). On THG datasets, STHN achieves the highest MRR on thgl-software (0.731) and TGN with edge-type embeddings leads on thgl-forum (0.729). Critically, all deep learning methods (RE-GCN, CEN, TLogic, TGN, STHN) fail with OOM or OOT errors on the largest datasets (tkgl-wikidata, thgl-github, thgl-myket), leaving only the lightweight EdgeBank heuristic as the sole scalable method on those.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The Recurrency Baseline (RecB, Gastinger et al. 2024) is the standout finding: this simple heuristic achieves the best test MRR on tkgl-smallpedia (0.605) and tkgl-icews (0.211), and remains competitive on tkgl-polecat (0.198 vs. TLogic's 0.228), underscoring that complex learned methods have not yet surpassed history-based scoring. EdgeBank (Poursafaei et al. 2022), designed for homogeneous temporal graphs, performs poorly on TKGs (e.g., 0.020 on tkgl-icews) but is the only method that scales to tkgl-wikidata (0.535 MRR), highlighting the scalability gap. Among deep TKG methods, TLogic (Liu et al. 2022) leads on tkgl-polecat (0.228) while CEN (Li et al. 2022) and RE-GCN (Li et al. 2021) are close behind on other datasets, but all three fail on tkgl-wikidata. For THGs, STHN (Li et al. 2023) achieves the best MRR on thgl-software (0.731) but is the least scalable, requiring 185 GB RAM and failing on all other THG datasets. TGN with edge-type embeddings (Rossi et al. 2020, modified) shows a large improvement over vanilla TGN on thgl-forum (0.729 vs. 0.649), confirming that leveraging edge-type information is critical, yet both variants OOM on thgl-github and thgl-myket. Overall, the benchmark reveals that heuristic baselines are often competitive with or superior to deep methods, and that scalability remains the dominant unsolved challenge.

## Relation to Prior Work

TGB 2.0 directly extends the Temporal Graph Benchmark (TGB) [29], which was limited to single-relation temporal graphs, by introducing multi-relational temporal graph datasets (TKGs and THGs) with a standardized evaluation pipeline. It addresses specific evaluation inconsistencies previously identified for TKGs [16] and for dynamic link prediction more broadly [57], including the use of only one negative sample per positive edge in prior THG evaluations. The benchmark follows the OGB [27] philosophy of providing large-scale, reproducible datasets with automated pipelines, while the experimental baselines draw from the TKG forecasting literature (RE-GCN, TLogic, CEN) and THG literature (TGN, STHN), plus the recently proposed Recurrency Baseline [15] and EdgeBank [57] as simple heuristics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals a critical scalability gap: nearly all deep learning methods (RE-GCN, CEN, TGN, STHN) fail with OOM or OOT errors on the largest datasets (tkgl-wikidata, thgl-myket, thgl-github), while only EdgeBank can scale to the largest TKG. This highlights that the field lacks methods capable of handling real-world scale (tens of millions of edges, millions of nodes). The finding that simple heuristic baselines are competitive with or outperform complex methods across most datasets underscores that current deep learning approaches have not yet learned to exploit multi-relational temporal structure in a way that justifies their computational cost. Open questions include whether the continuous-time-only setting for THGs limits applicability, whether the five represented domains are sufficient, and how methods can be made scalable without sacrificing the edge-type information that the paper shows is crucial for performance.
