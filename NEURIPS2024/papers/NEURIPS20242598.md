## Problem

Current Video LLMs face a fundamental trade-off under fixed token budgets: increasing the number of sampled frames (video-level temporal coverage) necessarily reduces the number of visual tokens per frame (frame-level semantic fidelity), and vice versa. This forces models to either miss fine-grained temporal details or lose spatial/semantic context within each frame, severely degrading performance on tasks requiring precise temporal localization and reasoning over brief, specific events.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242598/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a video (resized to 224×224, low-frequency sampled at 1 fps yielding 64 tokens per frame) and a text question as input, but processes them in a two-round dialogue: the first round asks the LLM to output a normalized temporal segment (two integers in 000–999 from a discretized space of N=1000), and the second round feeds both the global low-frequency tokens and densely sampled high-frequency tokens from that segment to produce the final text answer. The model also outputs segmented captions, temporal grounding predictions, and multi-turn reasoning responses across the FineAction-CGR benchmark's four task types.

## Method

SlowFocus resolves the sampling-frequency vs. per-frame-token trade-off by dynamically allocating the token budget: it first uses low-frequency frames to let the LLM identify the query-relevant temporal segment τ, then densely samples τ at a high frequency (N_H=20 frames) to extract local high-frequency visual tokens. A Multi-frequency Mixing Attention (MMA) module performs cross-attention from high-frequency tokens to low-frequency tokens (π(h_L, h_H) = softmax(h_H h_L^T / √d) h_L) to inject global context into local details before feeding the combined representation to the LLM. A temporal encoder discretizes frame positions into N=1000 temporal tokens that are added to visual embeddings to explicitly model inter-frame temporal relationships. Training proceeds in three stages: modality alignment on LCS-558K + WebVid, boundary enhancement on InternVid-10M-FLT for temporal grounding, and SlowFocus adaption on 100K+ QA pairs from FineAction and ActivityNet Captions where the model is given ground-truth segments and trained with mixed-frequency sampling.

## Results

On the proposed FineAction-CGR benchmark, SlowFocus achieves a temporal grounding mIoU of 66.68 and a temporal reasoning accuracy of 53.10%, substantially outperforming the strongest baseline LLaMA-VID† (mIoU 22.38, Acc 24.81%) and VTimeLLM (mIoU 27.69, Acc 9.96%). On coarse-grained video QA benchmarks, the method attains 70.1% accuracy on MSVD-QA, 58.3% on MSRVTT-QA, and 48.4% on ActivityNet-QA, matching or slightly exceeding LLaMA-VID (69.7%, 57.7%, 47.4%). On long-video benchmarks, SlowFocus reaches 58.6% on MovieChat-1K (global mode) and 39.7% on EgoSchema, remaining competitive despite no targeted long-video training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the proposed FineAction-CGR benchmark, SlowFocus dominates all baselines by a wide margin: it achieves 66.68 mIoU in temporal grounding versus 27.69 (VTimeLLM) and 22.38 (LLaMA-VID†), and 53.10% reasoning accuracy versus 24.81% (LLaMA-VID†) and 9.96% (VTimeLLM). Models without temporal grounding training (VideoLLaMA, Video-ChatGPT, LLaMA-VID) score near zero on grounding (mIoU < 0.5), confirming that fine-grained temporal tasks are essentially unsolved without explicit temporal supervision. On coarse-grained QA (Table 2), SlowFocus slightly edges out LLaMA-VID (70.1% vs. 69.7% on MSVD-QA; 48.4% vs. 47.4% on ActivityNet-QA) and clearly surpasses Video-ChatGPT (64.9%) and VideoLLaMA (51.6%). On MovieChat-1K, SlowFocus (58.6% global) trails MovieChat (62.3%) which was specifically trained for long videos, but outperforms VideoChat (57.8%) and VideoLLaMA (51.7%). On EgoSchema, SlowFocus (39.7%) is competitive with LangRepo-12B (41.2%) and Vamos (36.7%) despite using a smaller 7B LLM and no long-video-specific training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the modular Vid-LLM architecture established by LLaMA-VID (frozen visual encoder + projector adapter + LLM) and adopts the temporal grounding training paradigm from VTimeLLM, while following LLaVA's projector design for visual-language alignment. Its key departure is the SlowFocus mechanism: rather than uniformly sampling frames at a fixed low frequency, the model first performs a query-aware temporal grounding step to identify relevant segments, then densely samples only those segments at high frequency. This mixed-frequency strategy, combined with a novel multi-frequency mixing attention (MMA) module and a discretized temporal encoder, directly addresses the token-budget trade-off between temporal coverage and per-frame detail that plagues existing Vid-LLMs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper tackles a fundamental and persistent limitation in video LLMs—the inability to simultaneously retain fine per-frame detail and broad temporal coverage under fixed compute budgets—and offers a practical, query-conditioned solution that avoids uniformly expensive dense sampling. The introduction of FineAction-CGR fills a notable gap in evaluation: prior benchmarks (MSRVTT-QA, ActivityNet-QA) test coarse video understanding but do not stress fine-grained temporal reasoning (action sequencing, counting, boundary prediction). However, the method's reliance on a 224×224 spatial resolution and its evaluation primarily on action-centric FineAction videos leave open questions about generalization to longer, more diverse video content and to spatially demanding tasks. The large performance gap over baselines on the self-designed benchmark, while compelling, would benefit from validation on independent fine-grained temporal datasets.
