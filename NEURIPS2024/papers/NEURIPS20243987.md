## Problem

Design patents inherently lack detailed textual descriptions of their figures—containing only a title, a single claim, and brief viewpoint labels—making them poorly suited for multimodal tasks like classification and retrieval. Existing patent datasets either focus exclusively on utility patents (omitting visual data) or cover design patents at small scale with uninformative captions. This gap prevents the development of comprehensive multimodal patent analysis tools and limits design inspiration and prior-art search capabilities.

## Input / Output

The pipeline ingests raw USPTO BDSS tar files containing per-patent XML metadata and multiple TIFF drawing sheets (each sheet may hold several viewpoint figures), parsing them into 11 structured fields stored in year-wise CSV files alongside original-resolution TIF images organized in per-patent folders (e.g., USD0534331-20070102). For downstream evaluation, the system consumes patent titles, LLaVA-generated captions, and CLIP/RoBERTa feature vectors to produce classification labels across 835 design subclasses or ranked lists of retrieved patent images. The generated captions specifically describe shape and functionality of each design figure, going beyond the sparse viewpoint labels (e.g., 'front view') that appear natively in the XML.

## Method

The dataset construction pipeline parses USPTO XML files to extract 11 metadata fields, then generates descriptive captions for all 3.6 million figures using LLaVA prompted with the patent title to elicit shape and functionality descriptions—chosen over GPT-4o (higher cost) and Qwen-VL (lower quality, occasional Chinese leakage) based on a zero-shot retrieval quality benchmark. For empirical evaluation, the authors fine-tune CLIP (ResNet50/101, ViT-B-32, ViT-L-14 backbones) on 113k image-caption pairs for 30 epochs using the ITM loss to produce PatentCLIP, and combine RoBERTa text embeddings with CLIP image embeddings fed into SVM, MultiNB, LR, and MLP classifiers for the 835-class design patent classification task. The key finding is that concatenating title, generated caption, and image features consistently outperforms any single or dual-modality combination by up to 36% in accuracy, and that fine-tuned PatentCLIP on image-caption pairs achieves up to 550% and 830% improvement over zero-shot CLIP at R@1 for text-to-image and image-to-text retrieval respectively.

## Results

On the patent classification task (835 subclasses, 25,500 patents), combining title, caption, and image features with SVM achieves 60.0% accuracy and 58.0% F1, outperforming caption+image alone by up to 36% in accuracy. For multimodal retrieval, finetuned PatentCLIP with ViT-L-14 on image-caption pairs reaches R@1 of 20.58% (text-to-image) and 20.44% (image-to-text), with R@10 of 53.00% and 52.56% respectively, representing up to 550% and 830% improvement over zero-shot at R@1. On image retrieval, PatentCLIP-ViT-B+ArcFace achieves mAP of 0.657, surpassing the prior state-of-the-art ViT-B+ArcFace [17] (0.614) and standard CLIP-ViT-B+ArcFace (0.645).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's only direct external comparison is against Higuchi & Yanai's ViT-B+ArcFace [17] on the image retrieval task. PatentCLIP-ViT-B+ArcFace (finetuned on IMPACT image-caption pairs) achieves mAP 0.657, outperforming ViT-B+ArcFace [17] (mAP 0.614) by 4.3 points and standard CLIP-ViT-B+ArcFace (mAP 0.645) by 1.2 points. Internally, the key comparison is between image-caption and image-title pairs: across all backbones and both zero-shot and finetuned settings, image-caption pairs consistently yield higher recall, with the gap widening substantially after finetuning (e.g., ViT-L-14 finetuned: R@1 of 20.58% for caption vs. 14.88% for title on T2I). The paper also shows that larger backbones (ViT-L-14) dominate smaller ones (ResNet50) in all settings, confirming that model capacity amplifies the benefit of richer caption data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the patent analysis literature by shifting focus from utility patents (covered by USPTO2M, BIGPATENT, HUPD) to design patents, which prior datasets either omit entirely or address with limited scope (DeepPatent: 45K patents; DeepPatent2: 2.7M drawings but only brief viewpoint labels). Methodologically, it builds directly on CLIP's contrastive image-text pretraining, fine-tuning it into PatentCLIP on the new dataset, and leverages LLaVA as a vision-language model to generate the descriptive captions that design patents inherently lack. The key departure from prior work is the integration of VLM-generated elaborated captions into a structured multimodal dataset, creating a resource that supports both NLP and computer vision tasks simultaneously rather than treating them in isolation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dataset fills a concrete gap: design patents protect aesthetic/ornamental features critical to consumer electronics, fashion, and automotive industries, yet no large-scale, well-organized multimodal dataset existed for them. The generated captions address the fundamental information poverty of design patents (a single-line claim and a 3-word title), enabling tasks like prior art search and design trend analysis that were previously infeasible. Open questions include whether domain-specific patent foundation models will outperform generic VLMs, whether the 60% human agreement rate on caption quality is sufficient for high-stakes legal applications, and whether the proposed 3D reconstruction and PatentVQA tasks can be meaningfully benchmarked given the high variance in figure counts across patents. The US-only, English-only, 2007–2022 scope limits generalizability to other jurisdictions and earlier design eras.
