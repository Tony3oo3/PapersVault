## Problem

Existing model heterogeneous federated learning (MHeteroFL) methods rely on training loss to transfer knowledge between heterogeneous client models and a shared server model, resulting in limited knowledge exchange, performance bottlenecks, and high communication/computation costs. This paper addresses how to enable richer, multi-perspective knowledge interaction between a global homogeneous model and heterogeneous client models while preserving model and data privacy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242124/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each client processes 32×32 RGB images (CIFAR-10 with 10 classes, CIFAR-100 with 100 classes) through its heterogeneous local CNN and a shared homogeneous small CNN simultaneously. The two feature extractors produce representations of potentially different dimensions (d₁ ≤ d₂), which are spliced and projected into a fused representation of dimension d₂. The final output is a class label predicted by the client's local heterogeneous model header, with the global header providing an auxiliary coarse-granularity prediction during training only.

## Method

FedMRL adds a shared global homogeneous small model alongside each client's heterogeneous local model, with two key mechanisms. First, Adaptive Representation Fusion: the feature extractors of both models produce generalized and personalized representations that are spliced and mapped through a lightweight per-client representation projector (a single linear layer), enabling the fusion to adapt to local non-IID data distributions. Second, Multi-Granularity Representation Learning: the fused representation is split into a low-dimension coarse-granularity slice (first d₁ dimensions) processed by the global header and a high-dimension fine-granularity slice (first d₂ dimensions) processed by the local header, forming Matryoshka Representations whose combined weighted losses update all three components (global model, local model, projector) end-to-end. Only the small homogeneous model is transmitted between server and clients, keeping heterogeneous local models and data private while incurring low communication and computation overhead. Theoretical analysis proves an O(1/T) non-convex convergence rate under standard smoothness and bounded-variance assumptions.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 (N=100, C=10%), FedMRL achieves 95.85% average test accuracy, outperforming the best baseline FedProto (92.49%) by 3.36%. On CIFAR-100 under the same setting, FedMRL reaches 62.15%, a 8.48% improvement over FedProto (53.67%). Compared to the best same-category mutual learning baseline FedKD (37.21% on CIFAR-100, N=100, C=10%), FedMRL achieves a 24.94% accuracy gain. FedMRL also requires fewer communication rounds and lower total computation FLOPs to reach 90%/50% target accuracy than FedProto.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedMRL consistently outperforms all six baselines across all six FL settings (3 client counts × 2 datasets) in the model-heterogeneous scenario. Against FedProto [43], the strongest overall baseline, FedMRL gains 0.10–3.36% on CIFAR-10 and 1.78–8.48% on CIFAR-100, with the largest gap appearing under the most challenging setting (N=100, C=10%). Against the best same-category mutual learning baseline FedKD [45], FedMRL achieves a dramatic 16.43–24.94% accuracy improvement, demonstrating that Matryoshka-based multi-granularity representation learning transfers far more knowledge than simple output-distance mutual loss. FML [41] fails to converge in most settings (only reporting results for N=10 and N=50 on CIFAR-100), while FedAPEN [37] fails entirely in the model-heterogeneous scenario. LG-FedAvg [27] and FD [21] are competitive on CIFAR-10 with N=10 but degrade substantially as non-IIDness increases, whereas FedMRL maintains robust performance. In the model-homogeneous setting (Table 3), FedMRL similarly leads all baselines, with up to 5.71% improvement over the best baseline on CIFAR-100 (N=100, C=10%).

## Relation to Prior Work

FedMRL extends the mutual learning paradigm for model-heterogeneous federated learning (established by FML, FedKD, and FedAPEN), where a shared global homogeneous small model interacts with each client's heterogeneous local model. The key departure is replacing the simple output-distance mutual loss with two mechanisms borrowed from Matryoshka Representation Learning (Kusupati et al., NeurIPS 2022): (1) a personalized lightweight projector that fuses generalized and personalized feature representations adaptively to local non-IID data, and (2) multi-dimensional, multi-granularity embedded representations processed by both the global and local prediction headers. This positions FedMRL as a principled upgrade over prior mutual learning methods that the authors argue transfer only limited knowledge between the two models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FedMRL addresses a practical bottleneck in MHeteroFL: the mutual loss used by prior methods (FML, FedKD) provides a weak knowledge-transfer signal, capping model performance. By introducing multi-granularity representation learning, FedMRL achieves up to 24.94% accuracy improvement over the best same-category baseline while preserving model and data privacy (only the small homogeneous model is communicated). However, the gains over the strongest overall baseline (FedProto) are more modest (3–8%), and experiments are limited to CIFAR-10/100 with small CNNs. The authors note that the global header adds storage and communication overhead, and future work should explore the MRL-E variant (removing the global header) and scaling to larger architectures and datasets.
