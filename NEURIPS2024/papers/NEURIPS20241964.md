## Problem

Conventional offline RL requires concentrability assumptions bounding the ratio between policy visitation and data distributions, which is infeasible when data coverage is poor, partial, or undocumented (e.g., autonomous driving, healthcare). Without such coverage, the standard policy value J(π) cannot even be estimated, rendering the problem ill-posed. This paper asks whether a meaningful performance guarantee can be obtained with no data-support conditions at all.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a fixed offline dataset of (s, a, r, s′) tuples drawn from an arbitrary behavior distribution μ(s)β(a|s), along with three function approximator classes: a value class V ⊂ ℝ₊^S, a parameterized class Θ mapping to (f, w, π), and a discriminator class Ξ. It outputs a policy π_θ that is ε-optimal with respect to the worst-case policy value J̃(π) (defined over all environments indistinguishable from the true one given the data), with a sample complexity of O(ε⁻²(1−γ)⁻⁴ ln(N/δ)) requiring only realizability of the worst-case optimal policy, no concentrability, and no action-value-gap conditions.

## Method

The key insight is to replace the infeasible conventional value J(π) with a worst-case value J̃(π) = inf_{M′∈𝔘} J(π|M′), which is shown to equal the value in a truncated environment M̃ where out-of-support state-action pairs transition to a zero-reward absorbing state. This truncated value is then connected to a regularized Lagrangian K(v,f) = (1−γ)E_μ[v(s)] + E_{μβ}[f(s,a)δ^TD v(s,a)] + (1−γ)²/2 ‖v‖²_{2,μ̄}, whose saddle points (ṽ*, f̌^π) encode the worst-case optimal policy. The strong convexity in v stabilizes the saddle point against function approximation error, unlike the unregularized Lagrangian which collapses to zero under even tiny approximation gaps. Policy extraction from the dual variable f is performed via an integral probability metric loss D_Ξ(f; w, π) that matches f(s,a)μβ(s,a) to w̲(s)μ(s)π(a|s), and the overall objective L(θ) = L_SP(θ) + L_X(θ) is minimized empirically using minimax optimizers, yielding the WMRL algorithm with no hyperparameters beyond the function approximator choice.

## Results

The paper's main theoretical result (Corollary 6.3) establishes a sample complexity bound of O(ε^{-2}(1-γ)^{-4} ln(N/δ)) for worst-case offline RL, where ε is the policy suboptimality in the worst-case metric, (1-γ)^{-1} is the effective horizon, and N is the product of function-class cardinalities. This is the first result to achieve the statistically optimal O(ε^{-2}) rate without any concentrability or data-support assumption, requiring only realizability of the worst-case optimal policy π̃*. The bound has no dependency on the action value gap C_gap or any lower-tail exponent β_gap, and no explicit dependency on the sizes of the state or action spaces.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper strictly dominates all four baselines on both the weakness of assumptions and the strength of the bound. Compared to Zhan et al. (2022), which achieves only O(ε^{-6}) under single-policy concentrability and a sample-size-dependent realizability condition, the proposed method removes concentrability entirely and improves the rate to O(ε^{-2}). Against Chen and Jiang (2022) and Ozdaglar et al. (2023), both of which require the action value gap C_gap to be bounded away from zero (causing the bound to blow up when two actions are near-optimal), the proposed method has no such gap dependency and also removes the concentrability requirement. Relative to Uehara et al. (2023), which depends on a soft action value gap exponent β_gap yielding a rate of O(ε^{-2-4/β_gap}), the proposed method achieves the clean O(ε^{-2}) rate. Additionally, the (1-γ) dependency of (1-γ)^{-4} is strictly better than the (1-γ)^{-6} (or worse) dependencies in the other three gap-based methods, and the method has no explicit dependence on |S| or |A| in the algorithm or the bound.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Lagrangian-based offline RL framework (Zhan et al., 2022; Nachum et al., 2019) by replacing the conventional policy value J(π) with a worst-case policy value J̃(π) defined over all environments indistinguishable from the true one given the data. The key conceptual departure from prior work is that pessimism-in-the-face-of-uncertainty (Liu et al., 2020; Xie et al., 2021) is no longer an additional regularization objective but is built directly into the performance metric via a truncated environment construction. For policy extraction, the paper adapts the IPM-based approach from ALGAEDICE (Nachum et al., 2019) and OPT-DICE (Lee et al., 2021), while the regularized saddle-point formulation draws on the strong-convexity stabilization ideas from Uehara et al. (2023).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the concentrability assumption entirely, this work makes offline RL theoretically well-posed for domains with inherently poor or unknown state-action coverage (autonomous driving, healthcare, public policy), where prior methods either fail or require unverifiable coverage conditions. The O(ε^{-2}) sample complexity without action-value-gap dependencies strictly improves the state of the art under the weakest known assumptions. A key open question is the extension to continuous state-action spaces, where the uniform truncated concentrability coefficient is no longer guaranteed finite, and the localized variants in Appendix F.3 remain only partially characterized. The absence of any experimental validation also leaves the practical competitiveness of WMRL versus existing pessimistic methods (CQL, MOPO, etc.) untested.
