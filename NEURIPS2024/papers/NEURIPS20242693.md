## Problem

Standard autoregressive image generation flattens 2D VQ token grids into 1D sequences and predicts tokens in raster-scan order, which violates the unidirectional dependency assumption (VQVAE tokens retain bidirectional correlations), destroys spatial locality, and incurs O(n⁶) computational cost. These fundamental limitations cause AR models to significantly underperform diffusion transformers in image quality, speed, and scalability, leaving the promising scaling-law and zero-shot-generalization properties of LLMs unrealized in vision.

## Input / Output

For class-conditional generation, the model takes a single class label as a start token [s] and AdaLN condition, and autoregressively produces K multi-scale token maps from 1×1 up to 16×16 (for 256×256 images) or 32×32 (for 512×512), which are decoded by the VQVAE into the final RGB image. For zero-shot tasks like in-painting, out-painting, and class-conditional editing, ground-truth tokens outside a mask or bounding box are teacher-forced, and the model generates only the masked region without any architectural modification or fine-tuning. All scales share a single codebook of V=4096 entries, so the autoregressive unit at each step is an entire h_k×w_k token map rather than a single token.

## Method

VAR redefines the autoregressive unit from a single token to an entire token map at a given resolution, formulating image generation as 'next-scale prediction': the transformer predicts the full token map r_k at scale k conditioned on all coarser maps (r_1, …, r_{k-1}), with all h_k×w_k tokens within r_k generated in parallel. A new multi-scale VQVAE with residual quantization encodes images into K token maps using a shared codebook, where each scale's quantized representation is subtracted from the feature map before the next (finer) scale is extracted, ensuring each r_k depends only on its prefix. The transformer is a standard GPT-2-style decoder-only architecture with AdaLN and a block-wise causal attention mask during training (no mask needed at inference with KV-caching), reducing generation complexity from O(n⁶) to O(n⁴). The model scales with a simple rule (width=64d, heads=d, dropout=0.1·d/24), and the paper empirically verifies power-law scaling in both model parameters and training compute with Pearson coefficients near −0.998.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256, VAR-d30 (2.0B params) achieves FID 1.73 and IS 350.2 with rejection sampling, surpassing the validation set lower bound of FID 1.78. Without rejection sampling, VAR-d30 reaches FID 1.92 and IS 323.1. Inference requires only 10 autoregressive steps (one per scale), yielding wall-clock time ~20× faster than VQGAN and ~45× faster than DiT-XL/2. On ImageNet 512×512, VAR-d36-s achieves FID 2.63 and IS 303.2, outperforming DiT-XL/2 (FID 3.04, IS 240.8) with 81× less inference time.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VAR-d30 (2.0B) outperforms all diffusion transformer baselines: it beats DiT-XL/2 (675M) by 0.54 FID (1.73 vs 2.27) and 72.0 IS points (350.2 vs 278.2), while being 45× faster in wall-clock time. Against the larger L-DiT-3B (FID 2.10) and L-DiT-7B (FID 2.28), VAR-d30 achieves lower FID and higher IS with 250 fewer inference steps. Compared to the AR baseline VQGAN (FID 18.65, IS 80.4), VAR improves FID by 16.92 points and IS by 269.8 points while being 19× faster. Against other AR models, VAR-d30 surpasses ViTVQ (FID 4.17) by 2.44 FID and RQ-Transformer (FID 7.55) by 5.82 FID. Among GANs, VAR-d30-re beats StyleGAN-XL (FID 2.30) by 0.57 FID and 85.1 IS points, though StyleGAN-XL is slightly faster (0.3× vs 1× relative time). On 512×512, VAR-d36-s (FID 2.63) outperforms DiT-XL/2 (FID 3.04) by 0.41 FID with 81× less inference time.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the standard raster-scan 'next-token prediction' paradigm used by VQGAN, ViT-VQGAN, and RQ-Transformer, redefining autoregressive image generation as a coarse-to-fine 'next-scale prediction' over multi-scale token maps. It reuses the VQGAN tokenizer architecture (with a modified multi-scale quantization layer) and the GPT-2 decoder-only transformer backbone, but the core conceptual shift is in how the 2D token grid is ordered: instead of flattening to a 1D sequence, the model autoregressively generates entire token maps from 1×1 up to full resolution. The scaling-law analysis directly extends Kaplan et al. (2020) and Henighan et al. (2020) from language to visual generation, and the DiT comparison positions VAR against the architecture underlying Stable Diffusion 3 and SORA.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

VAR establishes that GPT-style autoregressive models can surpass diffusion transformers in image quality, speed, and data efficiency for the first time, challenging the prevailing assumption that diffusion is the dominant paradigm for high-fidelity image synthesis. The verified power-law scaling (Pearson ≈ −0.998) and zero-shot generalization to inpainting/outpainting/editing suggest visual AR models can inherit the two hallmark properties of LLMs, opening a path toward unified multimodal generation. Key open questions include text-prompted generation, video extension via 3D next-scale prediction, and whether improved tokenizers (orthogonal to this work) can push performance further. The paper's limitations are modest: no error bars due to compute constraints, and the VQVAE tokenizer is kept unchanged from VQGAN to isolate the paradigm contribution.
