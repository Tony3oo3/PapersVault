## Problem

Existing 2D-foundation-model-guided 3D instance segmentation methods (e.g., SAI3D, SAM-graph) compute superpoints using only geometric normals, causing under-segmentation when distinct instances share similar surface orientations (e.g., a blackboard on a wall). Additionally, SAM's inherent part-level segmentation tendency transfers to 3D space, producing over-segmented instances. Both failures stem from the under-exploitation of 3D priors available in the data.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a 3D point cloud P ∈ R^{N×6} (xyz + RGB per point) together with M posed RGB-D views and their camera intrinsics/extrinsics, and produces class-agnostic 3D instance masks (one label per point). No training is required; the pipeline runs in a few seconds on a single RTX 4090. A 3D detector (e.g., V-DETR) is invoked as an auxiliary component during the refinement stage, adding a detection-based constraint not present in prior SAM-guided pipelines.

## Method

SA3DIP is a three-stage zero-shot pipeline. First, complementary 3D primitives are generated by graph-cut over-segmentation that combines cosine similarity of estimated normals with normalized Euclidean distance of RGB colors (weighted w_n=0.96, w_c=0.04), yielding finer primitives than geometry-only baselines. Second, a superpoint graph is built by projecting each primitive onto every 2D view, matching its rendered pixels against SAM masks via histogram cosine similarity, and aggregating per-view affinity scores with visibility-based weighting. Third, affinity- and distance-aware region growing merges nodes using a decayed confidence score δ_{i,j} = A_{i,j}/D_{i,j} against dynamic thresholds, followed by an instance-aware refinement step that queries a 3D detector's bounding boxes (sorted by volume descending) to re-label over-segmented fragments whose point proportion within a box exceeds δ_2=0.75, thereby correcting SAM-induced part splits while preserving small objects.

## Results

SA3DIP achieves the best class-agnostic 3D instance segmentation across all three ScanNet benchmarks: 41.6 mAP / 64.6 AP50 / 81.3 AP25 on ScanNetV2, 36.1 mAP / 58.6 AP50 / 76.3 AP25 on the newly proposed ScanNetV2-INS, and 21.4 mAP / 36.4 AP50 / 53.6 AP25 on the more challenging ScanNet++. On Matterport3D and Replica, the full method (w_n=0.9, w_c=0.1, with 3D space prior) reaches 20.6 AP and 22.6 AP respectively, outperforming the strongest prior open-vocabulary baselines (SAI3D at 18.9 AP on Matterport3D and 20.4 AP on Replica). All experiments run on a single RTX 4090 in a few seconds per scene, with no training required.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SA3DIP outperforms all eight baselines on every reported dataset. The closest competitor, SAMPro3D [9], is surpassed by +7.9 mAP on ScanNetV2 (41.6 vs 33.7), +3.6 mAP on ScanNetV2-INS (36.1 vs 32.5), and +2.5 mAP on ScanNet++ (21.4 vs 18.9). SAI3D [8] trails by a wider margin of +10.8 mAP on ScanNetV2 and +4.3 mAP on ScanNet++. SAM-graph [7] and SAM3D [10] (both with and without ensemble) are substantially behind, with SAM3D w/ ensemble reaching only 20.1 mAP on ScanNetV2. The closed-vocabulary Mask3D [2] baseline (31.1 mAP on ScanNetV2) is also outperformed, highlighting the advantage of the zero-shot 2D-foundation-model-guided pipeline. On Matterport3D, SA3DIP (20.6 AP) beats SAI3D (18.9 AP) and SAM3D w/ ensemble (10.1 AP); on Replica, it leads SAI3D (22.6 vs 20.4 AP) and SAMPro3D (22.6 vs 13.1 AP). Notably, all methods except SAM3D show a drop in precision on ScanNetV2-INS versus vanilla ScanNetV2, confirming that the revised benchmark is more challenging and yields fairer comparisons.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the SAM-to-3D pipeline established by SAM3D, SAM-graph, and SAI3D, which decompose scenes into geometric superpoints and merge them using 2D multi-view mask affinities. The key departure is twofold: (1) superpoint generation is augmented with textural (color) priors alongside the standard geometric (normal) priors, addressing under-segmentation of instances with similar surface orientations; and (2) a 3D detector is introduced at the merging stage to impose spatial constraints that rectify SAM's part-level over-segmentation. The paper also contributes ScanNetV2-INS, a point-level corrected benchmark that addresses annotation deficiencies in the widely-used ScanNetV2 validation set.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of systematic annotation errors in ScanNetV2 and the release of ScanNetV2-INS provides the community with a fairer evaluation ground for zero-shot 3D instance segmentation, which is practically important as all prior comparisons were potentially biased. The finding that simple 3D priors (color channels and a 3D detector) can yield substantial gains over geometry-only pipelines suggests that the field has been under-exploiting readily available 3D information. Open questions remain: the method still depends heavily on 2D foundation model accuracy for the affinity matrix, and the superpoint approach may struggle with high-resolution scenes with complex lighting. Future work could explore learned pre-segmentation with semantic awareness or more robust multi-model 2D fusion.
