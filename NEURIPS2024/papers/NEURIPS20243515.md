## Problem

No existing 3D foundation model can perform universal, interactive segmentation across the full range of anatomical categories in volumetric medical images. Task-specific models fail to generalize across datasets with inconsistent label spaces, cannot leverage spatial user interaction to resolve ambiguity, and rely on computationally expensive sliding-window inference that limits the field of view.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243515/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

SegVol consumes a full-resolution 3D CT volume together with one or more prompts: spatial prompts (a set of positive/negative 3D points, or a bounding box defined by two diagonal vertices) and/or a semantic text prompt (e.g., 'liver', 'cervical spine C2') encoded via a frozen CLIP text encoder using the template 'A computerized tomography of a [text prompt]'. The model outputs a full-volume 3D binary segmentation mask; when driven by spatial prompts alone, it can additionally decode a ranked list of semantic category probabilities from a fixed vocabulary of 200+ anatomical terms.

## Method

SegVol pairs a 3D ViT image encoder (patch 4×16×16, 12 layers, 768 hidden dim) pre-trained with SimMIM on 90K unlabeled CTs, a frozen CLIP text encoder, a prompt encoder, and a mask decoder that fuses image and prompt embeddings via bidirectional self- and cross-attention plus a parallel text-input branch to strengthen semantic conditioning. A key architectural contribution is the zoom-out-zoom-in inference mechanism: the full volume is first downsampled for a single coarse global pass, the resulting ROI is cropped from the original resolution, and a sliding window refines only that region, cutting inference time from ~3.3 s (full sliding window) to ~190 ms while improving mean Dice from 0.65 to 0.73. To handle the partial-label problem across 25 heterogeneous datasets, pseudo masks are generated with the Felzenszwalb-Huttenlocher graph-based segmentation algorithm, filtered for size, and used as additional supervision (weighted at α=0.1) alongside ground-truth masks. Supervised fine-tuning on 6K labeled CTs (150K masks) uses a combined BCE + Dice loss with AdamW, and the training loop samples all six prompt-combination types (text, point, bbox, and their pairwise combinations) per category to learn multi-prompt generalization.

## Results

On AMOS22 (15 abdominal organs), SegVol achieves a median Dice score of 0.8593, outperforming the runner-up SAM-MED2D (0.6668) by 19.25%. On SegTHOR (4 thoracic organs), SegVol scores 0.8155, surpassing SAM-MED3D (0.7727) by 4.28%. On ULS23 (lesion segmentation), SegVol achieves 0.7046, matching MedSAM (0.7046) while outperforming all other methods. Across all 22 tasks, SegVol outperforms competitors in 19 tasks, with improvements up to 37.24% over the runner-up.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SegVol (bbox+text prompt) decisively outperforms SAM-MED2D on AMOS22 by 19.25% average Dice (0.8593 vs. 0.6668) and on SegTHOR by 16.62% (0.8155 vs. 0.6493). Against SAM-MED3D, SegVol leads on SegTHOR by 4.28% (0.8155 vs. 0.7727) and on AMOS22 by 23.93% (0.8593 vs. 0.6200). On ULS23, SegVol ties MedSAM at 0.7046 average Dice, with MedSAM slightly ahead on DeepLesion3D (0.7680 vs. 0.7065) but SegVol winning on PancreasLesion (0.7265 vs. 0.6561). The original SAM (both point and bbox variants) is substantially outperformed across all three datasets, with SAM(Point) averaging only 0.4050 on AMOS22 versus SegVol's 0.8593. In comparisons with task-specific models, SegVol achieves 14.76% higher average Dice than nnU-Net on lesion tasks (lung tumor, colon cancer, liver tumor) and generally outperforms SwinUNETR and 3DUX-Net on organ segmentation, though nnU-Net remains competitive on a few individual organs like spleen (0.9681 vs. 0.9642) and left kidney (0.9395 vs. 0.9296).

## Relation to Prior Work

SegVol extends the SAM (Segment Anything) prompt-based interactive segmentation paradigm from 2D natural images to 3D volumetric medical images, replacing SAM's 2D ViT encoder with a 3D ViT pre-trained via SimMIM on 90K unlabeled CTs. It departs from prior medical SAM-like methods (MedSAM, SAM-MED2D, SAM-MED3D) by adding CLIP-based semantic (text) prompts alongside spatial prompts, enabling cross-dataset generalization across 200+ anatomical categories rather than task-specific label spaces. The pseudo-label strategy using the Felzenszwalb-Huttenlocher graph-based segmentation algorithm is a novel data pipeline contribution that addresses the partial-label problem inherent in heterogeneous medical datasets, and the zoom-out-zoom-in inference mechanism is a new design not present in any prior 3D segmentation method.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SegVol is the first publicly available 3D foundation model for volumetric medical segmentation that unifies 200+ anatomical categories under a single interactive interface, which could substantially reduce the per-task model development burden in clinical AI. The demonstrated generalization from CT-only training to MRI (80–85% Dice on unseen modality) suggests the model captures anatomical structure rather than modality-specific texture, opening the door to multi-modality foundation models. Key open questions include whether the gap with true referring-expression segmentation (complex spatial-linguistic queries) can be closed, whether joint multi-modality pre-training further improves performance, and whether the model has truly reached its scaling ceiling given that ablations show continued gains with more data.
