## Problem

Neural operators for PDE simulation remain problem-specific in design—GNNs for Lagrangian/particle-based simulations and grid-based models for Eulerian simulations—preventing the kind of unified, scalable architecture that transformers have enabled in other domains. This paper asks how to build a single neural operator paradigm that flexibly handles both mesh-based and particle-based inputs while scaling efficiently to large-scale simulations with a fixed-size latent representation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240793/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a variable-size point cloud (mesh nodes or SPH particles) with associated field values (e.g., velocity, pressure) plus conditioning signals (timestep, inflow velocity). The encoder compresses this into a fixed-size latent representation (n_latent × h, e.g., 512×128) regardless of whether the input has 3K or 59K points. The decoder queries this latent space via cross-attention at any number of arbitrary spatial positions, enabling evaluation of the predicted field on grids, meshes, or particle sets that differ from the input discretization.

## Method

UPTs use a three-stage encoder-approximator-decoder architecture. The hierarchical encoder embeds input points, aggregates local information via message passing into n_s randomly selected supernodes (preserving mesh/particle density), processes them with transformer blocks, and pools into n_latent learned queries via a perceiver layer, yielding a fixed-size latent code. A transformer approximator then propagates dynamics forward in this compressed latent space. The decoder is a perceiver-like cross-attention layer that queries the latent representation at arbitrary output positions. The key training innovation is inverse encoding and decoding reconstruction losses that isolate encoder and decoder responsibilities, enabling latent rollouts where dynamics are propagated purely in the latent space without mapping back to the spatial domain at each step—yielding >5× faster inference than autoregressive rollouts. DiT-style feature modulation conditions all transformer and perceiver blocks on timestep and boundary conditions.

## Results

On the ShapeNet-Car steady-state benchmark, UPT with only 64 latent tokens achieves a test MSE of 2.31 (×100), outperforming GINO with 110K grid tokens (MSE 2.58) while running 225× faster per epoch (4s vs. 900s). On large-scale transient pipe-flow simulations (29K–59K mesh points), UPT-68M outperforms GINO, U-Net, and FNO by a large margin on both test MSE and rollout correlation time. In the timing comparison (Table 1), UPT's latent rollout completes a full 100-step trajectory in 0.3s on GPU, a 400× speedup over the pisoFoam reference solver (120s on 16 CPUs).

| UPT-68M (autoreg.) | 46 s | 2.0 s | 60× |
| UPT-68M (latent) | 3 s | 0.3 s | **400×** |

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GINO [54], UPT wins decisively on transient flows (large-margin MSE and correlation-time improvements at all model scales, Fig. 5) and on steady-state efficiency: UPT with 64 tokens (MSE 2.31) beats GINO with 110K tokens (MSE 2.58) at 225× lower per-epoch cost; GINO edges UPT slightly on steady-state accuracy with full feature engineering (2.14 vs. 2.24) but at 900s vs. 4s per epoch. Against U-Net [31] and FNO [51], UPT outperforms both on transient-flow MSE and rollout correlation time across all tested model sizes. On the Lagrangian TGV3D benchmark, UPT achieves lower velocity error than both GNS [87] and SEGNN [14] as the rollout progresses, demonstrating that field-based modeling captures dynamics more faithfully than particle-acceleration integration. Finally, UPT's latent rollout is 400× faster than the pisoFoam [110] reference solver, compared to 100× for GINO's autoregressive rollout.

## Relation to Prior Work

UPTs build on transformer-based neural operators (Cao, Li et al., Hao et al.) that encode spatial points into tokenized latent spaces, but depart critically by compressing into a fixed-size latent representation (n_latent × h) independent of input size, using a hierarchical encoder with message passing and perceiver pooling. The inverse encoding/decoding training procedure for enabling latent rollouts draws on latent dynamics modeling (Wiewel et al., Lee & Carlberg) and latent diffusion (Rombach et al.), but is learned end-to-end rather than in a two-stage pipeline. The unified treatment of both Eulerian (mesh/grid) and Lagrangian (particle/SPH) inputs within a single architecture is the key conceptual departure from prior work, which typically requires problem-specific designs (GNNs for particles, FNO/CNN for grids).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 400x speedup over traditional finite-volume solvers (pisoFoam) with comparable accuracy, combined with demonstrated scaling to 4.2M input points, makes UPTs a practically compelling option for large-scale PDE surrogates where inference speed is critical. The discretization convergence property—generalizing to unseen numbers of input/output points without retraining—reduces the need for retraining when simulation resolution changes. Open questions include whether the latent rollout can be improved via multi-stage training (akin to diffusion models), whether UPTs can be pretrained across heterogeneous physical systems as a foundation model, and whether the framework extends to truly large-scale Lagrangian simulations (10M+ particles) where no public datasets currently exist.
