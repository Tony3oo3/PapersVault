## Problem

Principled decoding for LLM alignment requires oracle access to the optimal token-level Q-function Q* for a target reward, which is unavailable in practice. The current state-of-the-art (Controlled Decoding) approximates Q* using Q^{π_sft} from the unaligned SFT reference model, introducing a distribution shift that causes significant sub-optimality. The paper asks whether a more accurate estimate of Q* can be obtained without retraining the LLM.

## Input / Output

At inference time, the method takes a text prompt x, a trajectory-level baseline aligned model ρ_BL (produced by DPO or similar fine-tuning, aligned with either the target reward r or a different baseline reward r_BL), the target reward model r, and the corresponding token-level baseline policy π_BL. It produces an aligned text response y generated token-by-token, where at each step only the top-k (k=10) candidate tokens are scored by sampling trajectory completions from ρ_BL. No model parameters are updated; alignment is achieved entirely through the decoding procedure.

## Method

Transfer Q* estimates the optimal Q-function by leveraging an already-available aligned baseline model rather than the SFT proxy. In direct transfer (baseline aligned with target reward r), TQ*(s_t, z) is computed as the expected trajectory reward under ρ_BL conditioned on the candidate token z. In indirect transfer (baseline aligned with a different reward r_BL), an importance-sampling reweighting w = ρ_r(τ|s_t,z)/ρ_BL(τ|s_t,z) is applied, where ρ_r is the closed-form RLHF-optimal policy for the target reward derived from the ratio of reward functions. The resulting decoding policy has a closed form π*_Alg(z|s_t) ∝ π_BL(z|s_t)·exp(TQ*(s_t,z)/α), with KL regularization against the baseline token policy. Theoretical analysis provides an upper bound on the sub-optimality gap (controlled by the trajectory-level alignment parameter β) and a bound on KL divergence to the SFT policy (controlled jointly by β and the decoding parameter α), giving users principled hyperparameter control over the reward–deviation trade-off.

## Results

Across six evaluation setups (UltraFeedback, HH-RLHF, Berkeley Nectar with various 7B models), TQ* consistently achieves the highest normalized average reward, reaching up to 1.45× improvement over the prior SoTA decoding method CD. In GPT-4-based win-tie evaluation (300 prompts), TQ* attains 86.67% win-tie rate against ARGS-SFT, 77.34% against DPO, and 67.34% against CD. TQ* also achieves the highest coherence (SimCSE cosine similarity) and diversity (n-gram repetition) scores among all compared decoding strategies.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TQ* outperforms all three baselines across every evaluation setup and metric. Against CD [Mudgal et al., 2024], the prior SoTA decoding method, TQ* achieves up to 1.45× higher average reward and a 67.34% GPT-4 win-tie rate (Evaluation-3), with the gap widening under indirect transfer where reward distributions shift significantly. Against ARGS [Khanov et al., 2024], TQ* wins by a larger margin (86.67% win-tie vs. ARGS-SFT in Evaluation-1), reflecting that ARGS relies on short-term reward heuristics rather than a principled Q-function estimate. Against DPO [Rafailov et al., 2023] used as a direct generation baseline, TQ* still achieves 70–77.34% win-tie rates, demonstrating that token-level decoding with a transferred Q* estimate adds meaningful alignment gains beyond trajectory-level DPO fine-tuning alone. TQ* also leads on secondary quality metrics: it produces the most coherent (highest SimCSE cosine similarity) and diverse (lowest n-gram repetition) responses among all methods tested.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Controlled Decoding (CD) by Mudgal et al. [33], which approximates the optimal token-level Q-function using the SFT model's Q^{π_sft}. The key departure is replacing this SFT-based proxy with an estimate derived from an already-aligned baseline model (e.g., a DPO fine-tuned model), which the authors call 'transfer decoding.' The indirect transfer variant further extends this by using importance sampling to bridge a baseline model aligned to a different reward function, drawing on the closed-form RLHF/DPO solution structure. The approach also relates to ARGS [26] (reward-guided search at decoding time) and leverages the proliferation of open-source DPO-aligned models as a practical resource.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Transfer Q* provides a principled, training-free alternative to parameter-level fine-tuning for LLM alignment, with a clean theoretical characterization (suboptimality gap bound and KL-divergence control via hyperparameters α and β). The indirect transfer setting is practically significant because open-source aligned models are typically trained on heterogeneous reward functions, making exact reward matching rare. A key limitation is the increased inference-time complexity from generating trajectory responses per token, which the authors acknowledge could be mitigated with a learned value function adapter. Open questions include whether the approach scales to longer sequences and whether the importance sampling variance in indirect transfer remains manageable under large reward distribution shifts.
