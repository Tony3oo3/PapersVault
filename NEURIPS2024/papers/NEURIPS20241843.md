## Problem

Continual instruction tuning for Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) has been largely overlooked, with no public benchmark to assess how these models behave when sequentially fine-tuned on diverse tasks. A critical open question is whether catastrophic forgetting in MLLMs stems from loss of reasoning knowledge or from degradation in instruction-following ability, which has not been disentangled in prior work.

## Input / Output

Each training and evaluation sample consists of an RGB image paired with a text instruction in a unified instruction-tuning template format (e.g., 'Answer the question using a single word or phrase'). Outputs are task-specific: free-form text answers for the six QA tasks, a single class label for ImageNet classification, and bounding box coordinates for RefCOCO grounding. Evaluation is dual-track: Truth Alignment compares outputs word-for-word against ground truth (IoU > 0.5 for grounding), while Reasoning Capability uses Qwen-1.5-32B as an LLM judge to score retained knowledge on a 0–10 scale, ignoring output format.

## Method

CoIN constructs a benchmark of 10 datasets spanning 8 tasks (grounding, classification, and six IQA subtypes) and sequentially fine-tunes MLLMs (LLaVA, Qwen-VL, MiniGPT-v2) using LoRA with frozen base LLM and vision encoder parameters. The key empirical finding is that catastrophic forgetting is primarily a failure of instruction alignment rather than reasoning knowledge loss, as Reasoning Capability degrades far less than Truth Alignment. To mitigate this, the paper introduces MoELoRA, which replaces the single LoRA low-rank pair (A, B) with N expert pairs (A_i, B_i) each of rank r/N, combined via a softmax gate G(x) = Softmax(xW_g) that routes input to task-relevant experts. This preserves the same total trainable parameter count as vanilla LoRA while allowing distinct experts to specialize in different instruction intents, consistently improving MAA and BWT as expert count increases from 1 to 8.

## Results

On the CoIN benchmark (8 tasks, 10 datasets), LLaVA suffers severe catastrophic forgetting with a Backward Transfer (BWT) of −32.62 and Mean Average Accuracy (MAA) of 32.97 under sequential LoRA fine-tuning, while Qwen-VL achieves BWT = −16.94 and MAA = 43.35. A key finding is that Reasoning Capability degrades far less than Truth Alignment (e.g., LLaVA's grounding drops from 58→52 in reasoning but 31.27→0.00 in truth alignment), confirming that forgetting is driven by instruction-alignment failure rather than knowledge loss. The proposed MoELoRA with 8 experts improves LLaVA's MAA from 32.97 to 42.76 and BWT from −32.62 to −25.91, with consistent gains as expert count increases from 1 to 8.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MoELoRA achieves the best MAA (41.05) among all methods, outperforming EWC (40.36) by 0.69 and LwF (35.89) by 5.16, and improving over the vanilla LoRA baseline (33.18) by 7.87. However, on BWT, EWC is the strongest (−17.94), followed by LwF (−19.27), while MoELoRA records −22.50, indicating slightly more forgetting than the regularization-based baselines. The paper attributes this BWT gap to MoELoRA's superior plasticity: it achieves substantially higher per-task accuracy (e.g., 25.57 on Grounding vs. 2.48–2.67 for LwF/EWC, and 44.25 on VizWiz vs. 14.53–37.88), meaning the model learns new tasks more effectively at the cost of marginally more backward interference. Both LwF and EWC struggle most on Grounding and ImageNet, where they retain only 2.48–2.67 and 7.22–8.90 accuracy respectively, whereas MoELoRA preserves 2.45 on Grounding but 8.85 on ImageNet. The authors also note that EWC and LwF are harder to integrate into distributed MLLM training pipelines compared to the architecture-level MoELoRA approach.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the continual instruction tuning paradigm from LLMs (where benchmarks like TRACE and CITB exist) to Multimodal Large Language Models, a setting the authors identify as entirely unexplored. Methodologically, it builds on LoRA as the parameter-efficient fine-tuning backbone and adopts MoELoRA/LoRAMoE as the architectural solution, drawing an analogy to expansion-based continual learning methods (e.g., Progressive Neural Networks). The dual evaluation design (Truth Alignment + LLM-judged Reasoning Capability) departs from prior MLLM forgetting studies like EMT, which only examined classification and used standard accuracy metrics, by explicitly decomposing forgetting into instruction-following degradation versus knowledge loss.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CoIN is the first public benchmark for continual instruction tuning of MLLMs, filling a clear gap for practitioners deploying MLLMs in sequential task-adaptation scenarios. The finding that forgetting is dominated by instruction alignment failure rather than reasoning knowledge loss reframes the problem and suggests that lightweight architectural interventions (MoELoRA) can be more effective than regularization-based approaches. Key limitations include evaluation restricted to 7–9B LLaMA-based models, a single training order per experiment, and task diversity still skewed toward QA; open questions include whether the instruction-alignment-dominance finding holds for larger models, full fine-tuning, and more heterogeneous task families (e.g., video, audio, generation).
