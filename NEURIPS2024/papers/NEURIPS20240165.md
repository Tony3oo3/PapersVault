## Problem

Extreme LLM compression to 1–2 bits per parameter relies on fine-tuning quantized weights, but existing approaches either optimize only continuous parameters (scales, codebooks) or use straight-through estimation (STE) for discrete code assignments. STE is a heuristic with no convergence guarantees and is known to be unstable for heavily quantized models, yet it remains the default fine-tuning strategy in SOTA methods like QuIP# and AQLM. The paper asks whether there is a principled, convergent alternative for updating discrete quantized weights during post-training fine-tuning.

## Input / Output

The method takes an already-quantized LLM (using any representation: scalar GPTQ, vector quantization, additive quantization, or lattice-based QuIP#) along with a calibration text corpus (RedPajama, ~128 sequences). It produces a re-optimized set of quantized weights that preserves the same underlying representation and bit-width, making the output directly compatible with existing high-performance inference kernels (e.g., ExLlamaV2 for GPTQ, AQLM/QuIP# kernels for VQ). The fine-tuning objective is a global loss (KL divergence or cross-entropy) over the full model, not a layer-wise MSE.

## Method

PV-Tuning alternates between two steps: a P step that optimizes continuous parameters (codebooks, scales) via standard backpropagation, and a V step that updates discrete code assignments. The key insight is that naive gradient-based updates to discrete weights stall because the desired step is smaller than the minimum quantization increment. To overcome this, the V step is linearized (replacing the objective with a quadratic surrogate around the current point) and restricted to a subspace of τ weights selected greedily by largest Adam update magnitude. Within this subspace, the algorithm performs an exact discrete search over the codebook to find the assignment minimizing the quadratic surrogate, enabling large, non-trivial updates without STE or stochastic rounding. Theoretically, the full PV method (without subspace restriction) is shown to monotonically decrease the objective and converge. In practice, combining the subspace V step with STE yields slightly better accuracy than either alone, and the framework is representation-agnostic, improving GPTQ, VQ, AQLM, and QuIP# uniformly.

## Results

PV-Tuning with vector quantization achieves state-of-the-art compression-accuracy trade-offs at 1–2 bits per parameter across Llama-2 and Llama-3 families. On Llama-2 7B at ~2 bits, PV-Tuning reaches WikiText-2 perplexity of 5.84 and 61.35% average zero-shot accuracy, outperforming QuIP# (6.19 / 57.51%) and AQLM (6.64 / 56.47%). At 1 bit, PV(gs8) achieves 7.62 WikiText-2 perplexity and 53.77% accuracy on Llama-2 7B, beating OneBit (9.73 / 50.06%) by a wide margin. The method also pushes the Pareto-optimal frontier for Llama-2 to 2.0 bits, where a 2-bit 13B model (Wiki2=5.12) matches the 16-bit 7B model (Wiki2=5.12).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PV-Tuning consistently outperforms all baselines at 1–2 bits per parameter. Against QuIP# (the strongest prior method), PV-Tuning reduces WikiText-2 perplexity from 6.19 to 5.84 on Llama-2 7B (2-bit) and from 3.91 to 3.78 on Llama-2 70B, while improving zero-shot accuracy by ~3–4 points. Against AQLM, the gap is even larger: 5.84 vs. 6.64 perplexity on Llama-2 7B and 5.12 vs. 5.65 on Llama-2 13B. At 1 bit, PV(gs8) dramatically beats OneBit (7.62 vs. 9.73 WikiText-2 perplexity on Llama-2 7B) and BiLLM (7.62 vs. 32.48), though BiLLM and PB-LLM are particularly weak on Llama-3 where they suffer catastrophic perplexity (257 and 197.56 respectively). DB-LLM is competitive at 2-bit on Llama-2 (7.23 vs. 5.84 for PV-Tuning on 7B) but degrades sharply on Llama-3 (12.77 vs. 6.99 on 8B). QuIP and PB-LLM are far behind at 2-bit on Llama-3 (76.95 and 21.74 perplexity vs. 6.99 for PV-Tuning). In the fine-tuning ablation (Table 1), PV-Tuning also beats STE (8.49 vs. 8.79 WikiText-2 on GPTQ 2-bit) and stochastic rounding (8.49 vs. 11.97), confirming that the subspace discrete update strategy is superior to both heuristic gradient estimators.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the coordinate descent / subspace descent literature (Gower & Richtárik 2015; Richtárik & Takáč 2016) to address a specific gap in post-training LLM quantization: how to fine-tune discrete quantized weights. Prior SOTA methods (AQLM, QuIP#, OneBit) either fine-tune only continuous parameters (codebooks, scales) or use straight-through estimation (STE) for discrete codes, both of which lack convergence guarantees and, as the paper shows, are sub-optimal at extreme bit-widths. PV-Tuning departs from this by replacing the full-parameter STE update with a greedy subspace V step that updates only a small subset of weights per iteration with sufficiently large steps, drawing directly on the coordinate descent framework. The method is representation-agnostic and composable with existing quantized representations (GPTQ, VQ, AQLM, QuIP#) and their inference kernels.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PV-Tuning pushes the Pareto-optimal frontier for Llama-2 from 2.5 bits to 2.0 bits per parameter, making near-lossless 2-bit deployment of large LLMs practical on resource-constrained devices. The conceptual finding that STE is sub-optimal for extreme quantization—where gradient updates are smaller than the quantization grid—challenges a widely-used default and provides a principled alternative with convergence guarantees. Open questions include better subspace selection strategies (greedy is used here), application to weight+activation or KV-cache quantization, and whether the approach extends to quantization-aware training from scratch. A key limitation is the additional compute and memory overhead during fine-tuning (up to 1.5× longer than prior fine-tuning, requiring 8×A100 for 70B models).
