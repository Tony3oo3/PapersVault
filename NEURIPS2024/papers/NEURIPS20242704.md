## Problem

Statistical heterogeneity in federated learning creates a fundamental trade-off: global models trained via FedAvg converge slowly under non-IID data and perform poorly on individual client distributions, while personalized FL methods improve local accuracy at the cost of global performance. No existing method simultaneously achieves state-of-the-art global and local test accuracy without significant computational overhead in cross-silo settings.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242704/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images (32×32 CIFAR-10, FEMNIST digits) distributed non-IID across up to 100 clients, and produces class labels at inference time. Unusually, the training output is not a single model but a set of M+1 simplex endpoints in the last fully-connected layer's parameter space; the global model is the centroid of these endpoints, while each client's personalized model is a convex combination weighted by its projected position in the standard simplex. Only the classification layer parameters are represented as simplex vertices; all other layers are point-estimated, keeping communication and compute overhead negligible relative to the feature extractor.

## Method

FLOCO trains a solution simplex—a linearly connected low-loss region in parameter space—where each client is assigned an L1-ball subregion based on its gradient signal. Client positions in the simplex are determined by concatenating endpoint gradient updates, projecting via PCA to M dimensions, and solving a Riesz s-Energy minimization with Euclidean simplex projection to ensure well-spread yet similarity-preserving placements. During each communication round, clients sample points uniformly from their subregion, perform local gradient descent on the sampled model, and backpropagate updates to the simplex endpoints; the server aggregates endpoint updates via weighted averaging (FedAvg on vertices). Simplex learning is restricted to the last fully-connected layer, making the approach compatible with pre-trained backbones and reducing overhead to O(G_C·M) versus O(G_FE) for the feature extractor. FLOCO+ further personalizes by fine-tuning each client's subregion endpoints with a Ditto-style proximity-regularized objective after the global simplex is trained.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with CifarCNN (5-Fold split), FLOCO achieves 62.93% global and 71.78% local test accuracy, while FLOCO+ reaches 75.08% local accuracy—outperforming the best personalized baseline (FedRoD, 74.03% local) and all global baselines (FedAvg 60.36%, FedProx 60.68%). On FEMNIST with SqueezeNet, FLOCO+ achieves 82.41% local accuracy versus Ditto's 65.06% and FedAvg's 75.51%. The paper reports up to 6% improvement in local accuracy and up to 5% in global accuracy over baselines, with FLOCO also achieving the lowest Expected Calibration Error (ECE) in most settings (e.g., 18.44% vs. 25.61% for FedAvg on CIFAR-10 5-Fold local ECE).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FLOCO consistently outperforms FedAvg and FedProx on both global and local accuracy across all six dataset/model settings, with the largest global gains on FEMNIST SqueezeNet (75.86% vs. 75.13% for FedAvg) and CIFAR-10 ResNet-18 Dir(0.3) (73.62% vs. 68.59%). Against personalized FL baselines, FLOCO+ achieves the best local accuracy in 5 of 6 settings, beating Ditto by up to 17.4% (FEMNIST SqueezeNet: 82.41% vs. 65.06%) and FedRoD by up to 81% (FEMNIST SqueezeNet: 82.41% vs. 4.95%). The one exception is CIFAR-10 ResNet-18 Dir(0.3), where FedPer achieves 85.05% local accuracy slightly above FLOCO's 80.38%, though FLOCO+ recovers the lead at 85.89%. APFL and FedRoD fail catastrophically on FEMNIST (≈5% accuracy), while FLOCO maintains 77–84% local accuracy. In the Appendix E comparison, FLOCO also outperforms SuPerFed on both global and local accuracy for all CIFAR-10 settings (e.g., 68.26%/80.92% vs. 63.22%/76.65% for CifarCNN 5-Fold). FLOCO additionally achieves the lowest ECE in most settings and the best worst-5%-client performance (61.73% vs. 60.20% for FedRoD on CIFAR-10 5-Fold).

## Relation to Prior Work

FLOCO sits at the intersection of two research threads: mode connectivity in neural network parameter space (Freeman & Bruna, Garipov et al., Nagarajan & Kolter) and simplex learning for multi-task/continual training (Benton et al., Wortsman et al.). The closest prior work is SuPerFed (Hahn et al., 2022), which enforces a 1D low-loss line segment between a global and a client model in FL. FLOCO departs from SuPerFed in three key ways: it generalizes to higher-dimensional simplices (M+1 vertices), restricts simplex learning to only the last fully-connected layer (enabling use with pre-trained models and reducing cost), and introduces a Riesz s-Energy-based projection that assigns each client a personalized subregion of the simplex based on gradient similarity, rather than a single interpolation coefficient.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FLOCO addresses the persistent global-vs-personalized FL trade-off by showing that a shared low-loss simplex can simultaneously serve as a strong global model (at its centroid) and a personalized model (at client-specific points), with minimal overhead since only the classification layer is simplex-trained. This makes the approach directly applicable to the increasingly common setting of fine-tuning pre-trained models in cross-silo FL. Key open questions include extending the method to cross-device FL with thousands of stateless clients, providing theoretical convergence guarantees, and understanding whether the subregion assignment mechanism generalizes to highly dynamic client populations. The paper also notes that a thorough theoretical analysis remains future work.
