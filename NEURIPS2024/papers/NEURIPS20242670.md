## Problem

Existing NR-PCQA methods learn content and distortion representations indiscriminately within a single network, producing highly entangled features that violate the human vision system's separate processing of high-level semantics and low-level distortions. This entanglement is exacerbated by data imbalance in PCQA datasets—distortion types are diverse but content variety is extremely limited—causing models to overfit to specific content and produce inconsistent quality scores when content changes under the same distortion pattern.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242670/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a distorted 3D point cloud as input, renders it into 6-view 512×512 RGB images via PyTorch3D, and produces a single scalar quality score (MOS prediction). The content branch consumes the full rendered images (with 50% patch masking during pretraining), while the distortion branch consumes a mini-patch map of the same spatial resolution generated by grid mini-patch sampling that scatters local patches to preserve distortion patterns while blurring global content. The two branch representations are concatenated and regressed through fully-connected layers to yield the final score.

## Method

DisPA employs a dual-branch architecture: a frozen ViT-B content-aware encoder pretrained via masked autoencoding (reconstructing reference point cloud images from partially masked distorted images to capture semantics independent of distortion), and a Swin-T distortion-aware encoder that ingests mini-patch maps to focus on low-level degradation patterns. The key technical contribution is an explicit MI-based disentanglement regularizer: a lightweight variational network Q_φ (modeled as an isotropic Gaussian) estimates a tight upper bound of the mutual information between the two branch representations following the CLUB framework, and this upper bound is minimized during training to enforce statistical independence. The total training objective combines MSE regression, a differential ranking loss for fine-grained quality ordering, and the MI penalty with separate alternating updates for the variational network and the main encoders.

## Results

DisPA achieves SROCC of 0.625, 0.908, and 0.788 on LS-PCQA, SJTU-PCQA, and WPC respectively, outperforming the strongest NR-PCQA baseline CoPA by ~1.3% SROCC on each dataset. On SJTU-PCQA, DisPA reduces RMSE to 0.089 (vs. 0.092 for CoPA) and reaches PLCC of 0.919. Notably, DisPA surpasses all full-reference (FR) PCQA methods on both SJTU-PCQA and WPC, with the best FR method (MS-GraphSIM) achieving only 0.888 SROCC on SJTU-PCQA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DisPA outperforms the closest NR-PCQA competitor CoPA [33] on SROCC across all three datasets (0.625 vs. 0.621 on LS-PCQA, 0.908 vs. 0.897 on SJTU-PCQA, 0.788 vs. 0.779 on WPC) and on RMSE (0.160 vs. 0.161, 0.089 vs. 0.092, 0.138 vs. 0.144), though CoPA retains a marginal PLCC edge on LS-PCQA (0.636 vs. 0.631). Against other NR-PCQA methods, DisPA leads GPA-Net [32] by 3.0% SROCC on SJTU-PCQA (0.908 vs. 0.878) and by 3.0% on WPC (0.788 vs. 0.758), and surpasses MM-PCQA [51] by 3.2% SROCC on SJTU-PCQA (0.908 vs. 0.876). DisPA also exceeds all full-reference baselines on SJTU-PCQA and WPC, with the strongest FR method MS-GraphSIM [49] reaching only 0.888 SROCC on SJTU-PCQA and 0.704 on WPC. In cross-dataset validation (Table 2), DisPA maintains competitive generalizability, achieving 0.653 SROCC when trained on LS-PCQA and tested on SJTU-PCQA, and 0.657 when trained on WPC and tested on SJTU-PCQA, slightly ahead of CoPA in both settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

DisPA sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) the NR-PCQA literature (PQA-Net, GPA-Net, CoPA, MM-PCQA), where prior methods learn a single unified quality representation or alternate training objectives within one network; (2) the IQA/VQA line of work that separates content from distortion perspectives (DOVER, Re-IQA, QPT), which motivates the dual-branch design but relies on contrastive or masked-modeling objectives rather than explicit statistical independence; and (3) the mutual information estimation literature (CLUB, β-VAE, InfoGAN), from which the paper borrows the variational log-ratio upper bound to explicitly minimize dependence between the two branches. The key departure from all prior PCQA methods is the use of MI minimization as a first-class regularizer to enforce disentanglement, combined with a masked-autoencoding pretraining strategy adapted from MAE and a mini-patch map input adapted from Fast-VQA to bias the distortion branch toward low-level patterns.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DisPA is the first framework to formalize content–distortion disentanglement in point cloud quality assessment, and the MI-based regularizer offers a principled alternative to ad-hoc correlation penalties (e.g., cosine similarity) that prior multi-task PCQA models use implicitly. The practical relevance is tempered by the modest absolute gains (~1–2% SROCC over CoPA) and the narrow domain of PCQA; the authors themselves note that pretraining is limited to the LS-PCQA scale and that more efficient MI estimators for high-dimensional features remain an open problem. Whether the disentanglement philosophy transfers to other quality-assessment or perception tasks (e.g., video, audio) is an open question that could broaden the impact of the MI-minimization recipe.
