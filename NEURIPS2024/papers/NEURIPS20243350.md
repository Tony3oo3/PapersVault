## Problem

Existing Best Policy Identification (BPI) methods identify an optimal policy for a single, pre-specified reward function, while reward-free RL explores for any possible reward (impractical in practice) and multi-objective RL targets Pareto frontiers rather than pure exploration. This paper tackles the Multi-Reward BPI (MR-BPI) problem: efficiently identifying the optimal policy for every reward in a user-specified finite set R with a prescribed confidence level, a setting that has remained largely unexplored in the RL literature.

## Input / Output

The method takes a finite set of reward vectors R ⊂ [0,1]^{SA} (one scalar per state-action pair), the MDP transition dynamics, and a confidence parameter δ, and produces an optimal deterministic policy π̂_{τ,r} for each r ∈ R along with a data-dependent stopping time τ. The exploration allocation ω* must satisfy stationarity (flow-balance) constraints coupling state visitation frequencies to transition probabilities, and the relaxed characteristic rate U(ω;M) is convex in ω only for finite reward sets, which constrains the optimization to a discrete collection of reward vectors rather than a continuous space.

## Method

The paper derives an instance-specific sample-complexity lower bound via a change-of-measure argument that generalizes single-reward BPI, where the opponent must jointly select both a confusing model and a reward from R. Since the resulting characteristic rate T*(M) is non-convex, the authors construct a convex upper bound U(ω;M) (the relaxed characteristic rate) expressed in terms of sub-optimality gaps, next-state variances, and maximum deviations, solvable by standard convex optimization. MR-NaS is a model-based δ-PC algorithm that tracks the optimal allocation ω* via certainty equivalence on the empirical MDP, mixes it with a softmax forcing policy to guarantee ergodicity, and stops when a generalized likelihood-ratio statistic exceeds a threshold calibrated for δ-PC guarantees. For deep RL, DBMR-BPI adapts the model-free DBMF-BPI framework by learning Q-values for all rewards simultaneously (A·|R| outputs per state), using an ensemble of bootstrapped Q-networks with randomized prior functions to estimate parametric uncertainty, and allocating exploration across rewards proportionally to 1/Δ²_{t,r} to prioritize harder-to-identify reward functions.

## Results

On Cartpole swing-up (k=3, T=150k), DBMR-BPI achieves an average reward-collection rate of 21.3% across base rewards and 23.1% on unseen random rewards, compared to 1.3% (RND), 0.3% (APT), and 0.9% (Disagreement). On DeepSea (N=30, T=100k), DBMR-BPI achieves a geometric mean reward of 3.1% with 0 unvisited terminal cells, outperforming RND (1.8%, 0.3 unvisited), APT (0.0%, 3.3 unvisited), and Disagreement (0.0%, 11.5 unvisited). On tabular MDPs (Riverswim, Forked Riverswim, DoubleChain, NArms), MR-NaS reaches low policy-identification error faster than RF-UCRL, ID3AL, and MR-PSRL across all environments. Notably, DBMR-BPI generalizes to unseen rewards (Rrnd) with performance comparable to or exceeding its performance on the training reward set.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DBMR-BPI dominates all three DRL baselines on Cartpole swing-up by a wide margin: at k=3 it collects ~16× more useful reward than RND (21.3% vs 1.3%), ~70× more than APT (0.3%), and ~24× more than Disagreement (0.9%). On DeepSea, the gap narrows at N=20 where DBMR-BPI matches RND (both 4.6% GM, 0 unvisited cells), but at N=30 DBMR-BPI clearly wins (3.1% GM, 0 unvisited) while RND drops to 1.8% and both APT and Disagreement fail entirely (0.0% GM, 3.3 and 11.5 unvisited cells respectively). A key finding is that DBMR-BPI generalizes to unseen rewards (Rrnd) at least as well as to its training rewards, suggesting the collected data supports optimization of similar but novel reward functions. On tabular MDPs, MR-NaS reaches low estimation error faster than RF-UCRL (reward-free), ID3AL (max-entropy), and MR-PSRL (posterior sampling) across Riverswim, Forked Riverswim, DoubleChain, and NArms, though the relative ordering varies by environment.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the single-reward Best Policy Identification (BPI) framework—specifically the NaS/MDP-NaS algorithm and its change-of-measure lower bound [17]—to a multi-reward setting (MR-BPI), where the agent must identify optimal policies for an entire set of rewards simultaneously. The theoretical machinery (characteristic rate, Track-and-Stop, convex relaxation of the non-convex allocation problem) is inherited directly from the BPI literature [16, 17, 20], while the DRL extension adapts DBMF-BPI [53] (a model-free, bootstrapped exploration method) to handle multiple reward signals. The authors carefully distinguish MR-BPI from reward-free RL (which targets any possible reward), unsupervised RL (which does not address pure exploration with fixed confidence), and multi-objective RL (which seeks a Pareto frontier over potentially conflicting objectives), positioning their contribution as a distinct pure-exploration problem with a known finite reward set.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MR-BPI addresses a practically relevant gap: in applications like intent-based radio network optimization, robotics goal-reaching, and recommender systems, the agent often needs to perform well across a set of reward functions rather than a single one, and the pure-exploration (fixed-confidence) formulation is more sample-efficient than regret-based approaches when the goal is policy identification. Key limitations include the restriction to tabular MDPs for theoretical guarantees, the unique-optimal-policy assumption, finite action spaces in the DRL extension, and potential scalability issues with large reward sets. Open questions include extending the theory to continuous action spaces, handling multiple optimal policies via overlapping hypothesis tests, and whether the convex relaxation gap (U* vs T*) can be tightened. The observed generalization to unseen rewards (DBMR-BPI performing well on rewards not used during exploration) suggests the collected data may serve as a versatile offline dataset, connecting to the broader offline RL literature.
