## Problem

Existing programmatic RL methods synthesize single concise programs that can only tackle short-horizon tasks, failing on long and repetitive problems (e.g., robotic manipulation, autonomous driving) that require diverse, reusable skills and thousands of actions. The paper asks: can we retrieve a set of human-readable programs as reusable options and learn a high-level policy to compose them, achieving both interpretability and generalization to arbitrarily longer horizons?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20244023/cropped_page3_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework takes grid-world state observations (Karel domain, e.g., 8×8 or 16×16 grids) and a pre-retrieved set of 3–5 programmatic options written in a Karel DSL (with control flows, perceptions, and actions) as input. The high-level policy outputs a probability distribution over the option set (plus a termination token) at each decision step, and the selected program is executed to completion (up to 220 function calls) before the next selection. The retrieved programs themselves are the interpretable low-level policies, and the framework additionally extracts a finite state machine from the high-level policy to visualize option-transition dynamics.

## Method

HIPO is a three-stage hierarchical framework. First, a GRU-based VAE (following Trivedi et al.) learns a continuous program embedding space where any vector decodes to a valid Karel DSL program. Second, a modified Cross-Entropy Method (CEM+diversity+compatibility) iteratively retrieves |M| programs: a diversity multiplier (Sigmoid of negative max cosine similarity to previously found embeddings) pushes each new program into underexplored behavioral regions, while a compatibility score evaluates each candidate by sampling random sequences of previously found programs plus the candidate and measuring the joint execution return. Third, a CNN+FC high-level policy trained with PPO takes the current grid state and the last selected option as input and predicts the next option, maximizing cumulative task return. This design ensures the retrieved options are simultaneously effective, behaviorally diverse, and sequentially compatible, enabling the high-level policy to compose them for tasks requiring thousands of steps.

## Results

On the KAREL-LONG benchmark (long-horizon tasks requiring >3000 steps), HIPO achieves mean returns of 0.53 (SEESAW), 0.76 (UP-N-DOWN), 0.62 (FARMER), 0.66 (INF-DOORKEY), and 0.79 (INF-HARVESTER), outperforming all baselines on four of five tasks. On the shorter-horizon KAREL and KAREL-HARD sets, HIPO achieves perfect 1.00 returns on 7 of 10 tasks, outperforming LEAPS and HPRL on 8 of 10 tasks. HIPO also demonstrates superior inductive generalization, with the smallest performance drop when test horizons are extended up to 16× the training horizon, and better program sample efficiency than LEAPS and HPRL.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HIPO outperforms all programmatic RL baselines (LEAPS, HPRL, HC) and deep RL baselines (DRL/PPO, Option-Critic, PAO) on four of five KAREL-LONG tasks. The largest margins are on SEESAW (0.53 vs. 0.28 for CEM+diversity×|M|, the next-best) and UP-N-DOWN (0.76 vs. 0.58), where the diversity and compatibility of retrieved programmatic options are critical. On INF-HARVESTER, Hill Climbing (HC) achieves a higher return (0.88 vs. 0.79), likely because this task is more amenable to a single well-optimized program rather than a set of diverse options. On the shorter KAREL/KAREL-HARD tasks (Table 1), HIPO matches or exceeds HPRL on 8/10 tasks and substantially outperforms LEAPS, confirming that the retrieved programs are effective even for short-horizon problems. The ablation (Table 2 variants) shows that removing either the diversity multiplier or the compatibility evaluation degrades performance, validating both components of the CEM+diversity+compatibility search.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two research threads: programmatic RL (LEAPS [74], HPRL [46], Hill Climbing [10]), which represents policies as human-readable programs, and hierarchical RL / option frameworks [4, 72], which decompose long-horizon tasks into reusable skills. HIPO repurposes the VAE-based program embedding space from LEAPS as a search space for retrieving a *set* of programs (rather than a single one), then treats these programs as interpretable options selected by a learned high-level policy. The key methodological departure from prior programmatic RL is the CEM+diversity+compatibility search, which explicitly optimizes for behavioral diversity and sequential compatibility among retrieved programs—properties that prior single-program or independent-program search methods do not enforce.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete gap: prior programmatic RL methods produce concise programs that cannot handle long-horizon, repetitive tasks (e.g., thousands of steps), which are common in robotics and autonomous driving. The KAREL-LONG benchmark and the inductive generalization results (minimal performance drop at 16× horizon extension) provide evidence that hierarchical programmatic options can generalize beyond training horizons without fine-tuning. Open questions include scalability to larger state/action spaces beyond grid worlds, quantitative verification of programmatic policies, and whether LLM-guided search or offline datasets could accelerate the option retrieval phase. The narrow domain (Karel grid world) limits immediate practical impact but establishes a clear testbed for the framework.
