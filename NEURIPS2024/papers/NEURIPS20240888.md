## Problem

Noisy preferences (NPs), where chosen/rejected labels in alignment datasets are flipped, cause significant degradation in LLM alignment performance, potentially leading to the generation of harmful content. Existing robust alignment methods (cDPO, rDPO) mitigate NPs from the loss perspective by adjusting the alignment loss based on an estimated noise proportion, but they overlook the essential distributional differences between noisy and clean preferences that could enable direct detection and correction of mislabelled data points.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a noisy preference dataset (triples of prompt, chosen response, rejected response), a small clean validation set (~50 samples), and a pre-trained LLM (Llama2-7B or phi-2 fine-tuned via QLoRA). It produces a denoised training dataset with corrected labels, an estimated residual NP proportion (ε′_PC) computed from the intersection area of two fitted normal distributions, and a robustly aligned LLM. The PPLDiff metric is agnostic to the downstream alignment algorithm, making the denoised dataset compatible with DPO, PPO, SLiC, IPO, cDPO, and rDPO without modification.

## Method

PerpCorrect introduces PPLDiff — the log-perplexity difference between chosen and rejected responses computed by an aligned surrogate LLM — as a discriminator between clean and noisy preferences, exploiting the intuition that a mislabelled 'chosen' response will have higher perplexity under an aligned model. The method proceeds in three stages: (1) align a surrogate LLM on the clean validation set so that PPLDiff values of CPs and NPs form two separable distributions; (2) iteratively re-align the surrogate using the most extreme PPLDiff values (smallest for reliable clean data, largest for reliable noisy data after label-flipping) to progressively reduce overlap between the two distributions; (3) fit two normal distributions to the PPLDiff density via the Levenberg-Marquardt algorithm, set the detection threshold at their intersection, and flip labels of data points below the threshold to produce the denoised dataset. The optimal denoised dataset across iterations is selected by minimizing the estimated residual NP proportion (the integral of the minimum of the two fitted densities), and the resulting ε′_PC can also replace the expensive cross-validation used by cDPO/rDPO to estimate noise proportion.

## Results

On the Golden HH dataset with Llama2-7B, PerpCorrect-DPO achieves 94.92% reward accuracy at 40% noisy preferences, compared to 53.15% for vanilla DPO, 65.60% for cDPO, and 90.45% for rDPO — a 41.77 percentage-point improvement over vanilla DPO. The metric used throughout is reward accuracy (winning rate of policy generations against selected preferences on the test set). Across all tested configurations (Llama2-7B and phi-2 on Golden HH and OASST1), PerpCorrect consistently outperforms all baselines, with the largest gains at higher noise proportions (30–40% NPs).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PerpCorrect outperforms all six baselines across nearly all noise proportions. Against vanilla DPO (Rafailov et al., NeurIPS 2023), PerpCorrect-DPO gains up to +41.77 pp at 40% NPs (94.92% vs. 53.15% reward accuracy). Against cDPO (Mitchell, 2023), PerpCorrect-cDPO improves by up to +22.76 pp at 40% NPs (88.36% vs. 65.60%). Against rDPO (Chowdhury et al., ICML 2024), the gain is more modest at +5.28 pp at 40% NPs (95.73% vs. 90.45%), and PerpCorrect-rDPO actually underperforms rDPO by ~0.2–0.7 pp at 10–20% NPs. For the PPO series (Ouyang et al., NeurIPS 2022), PerpCorrect-PPO achieves 93.17% at 40% NPs versus 86.29% for vanilla PPO. Against SLiC (Zhao et al., 2023) and IPO (Azar et al., 2023), PerpCorrect yields the largest relative gains (+36.02 pp and +35.92 pp respectively at 40% NPs), with PerpCorrect-IPO reaching 97.56% — the single best result in the paper. The method also generalizes to phi-2 and the OASST1 dataset, where other robust baselines (cDPO, rDPO) often fail to beat vanilla DPO, while PerpCorrect-DPO still maintains 68–71% reward accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the loss-oriented robust alignment paradigm established by cDPO (Mitchell, 2023) and rDPO (Chowdhury et al., ICML 2024), which adjust the alignment loss using an estimated noise proportion from clean validation data. Instead, PerpCorrect operates from the data perspective, using a perplexity-difference metric (PPLDiff) computed by a surrogate LLM to detect and correct mislabeled preference pairs before alignment. The method repurposes perplexity—a standard language modeling diagnostic—as a noise-detection signal, and leverages the Levenberg-Marquardt algorithm to fit a two-component Gaussian mixture over PPLDiff values, enabling threshold-based label flipping without oracle access.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PerpCorrect offers a practical, plug-and-play data-cleaning step that is agnostic to the downstream alignment algorithm (DPO, PPO, SLiC, IPO, cDPO, rDPO) and requires only ~50 clean validation samples, making it accessible in settings where large-scale re-annotation is infeasible. Key limitations include the iterative PPLDiff computation and repeated surrogate training (roughly doubling wall-clock time), and the dependence on a manually annotated validation set whose quality significantly affects performance (10 vs. 50 samples yields an ~14-point gap at 40% noise). Open questions include whether the validation requirement can be eliminated entirely, whether the two-Gaussian assumption holds under structured or adversarial noise rather than random label flipping, and whether the approach extends to multi-turn or multi-preference settings.
