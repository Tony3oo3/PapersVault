## Problem

Language models can memorize and regurgitate copyrighted content from training data or retrieved context, yet no prior work has systematically evaluated whether proposed takedown mechanisms actually prevent such regurgitation without destroying the model's factual knowledge or inference efficiency. This paper introduces the first evaluation framework (COTAEVAL) to assess the feasibility and side effects of copyright takedowns for LMs, addressing the question of whether 'takedown' can be operationalized in practice.

## Input / Output

The evaluation feeds a length-l hint (prefix of blocklisted content) to the model, optionally prepended with the full blocklisted passage in the RAG scenario, and the model generates a continuation that is compared against the ground-truth suffix using eight similarity metrics spanning exact match (character/word LCS), near-duplicate (ROUGE-1, ROUGE-L, word-level ACS, Levenshtein distance, MinHash), and semantic similarity (cosine via MiniLM embeddings). Utility is measured via word-level F1 on news QA, ROUGE-L on book summarization, plus MMLU and MT-Bench for general capability, while efficiency is reported as calibrated tokens-per-second relative to vanilla decoding.

## Method

COTAEVAL evaluates eight takedown methods across two regurgitation scenarios (memorization, simulated by fine-tuning Llama2-7B-chat on NewsQA; and RAG, where blocklisted content is injected as retrieved context) and three desiderata: low similarity to blocklisted content, high utility on uncopyrightable facts, and low computational overhead. The methods span three intervention stages: generic prevention (System Prompt, Top-k Perturbation adding Gaussian noise to top-k logits), decoding-time takedowns (MemFree using a Bloom-filter n-gram check, and R-CAD which subtracts the blocklisted content's logit contribution scaled by α), and training-based unlearning (Gradient Ascent, Gradient Difference, KL Minimization, and Preference Optimization). The key finding is that no method dominates: System Prompt and MemFree reduce risk but cannot eliminate all regurgitation; unlearning and Top-k Perturbation reduce similarity but cause 30–60% utility loss; and R-CAD is effective but halves inference speed and risks utility collapse when the retriever returns the gold document.

## Results

In the memorization scenario (fine-tuned Llama2-7B-chat, news domain), no takedown method excels across all three desiderata (low similarity, high utility, low overhead). UnlearningPO achieves the highest regurgitation risk reduction win rate (66.2%) but drops Blocklisted F1 from 40.5 to 28.3 and In-Domain F1 from 40.6 to 24.7. R-CAD achieves a 41.7% win rate while preserving utility scores (Blocklisted F1 = 40.5, In-Domain F1 = 40.6) but halves inference speed to 0.53×. In the RAG scenario (Llama2-7B-chat, news), System Prompt achieves the best win rate (59.1%) with minimal utility loss, while MemFree preserves MMLU (48.2) and MT-Bench (6.3) at the vanilla level but only reaches 45.7% win rate.

## Baselines & Comparisons

System Prompt achieves the highest win rate in the RAG scenario (59.1% on news, 43.5% on books for Llama2-7B) with zero overhead, but still fails to prevent all regurgitation and drops Blocklisted F1 from 40.5 to 33.4 in the memorization setting. MemFree preserves general utility perfectly (MMLU and MT-Bench unchanged from vanilla) and is effective against exact-match regurgitation, but its win rate is modest (45.7% RAG, 24.8% memorization) and it is easily bypassed by minor perturbations like extra whitespace. Top-k Perturbation achieves high win rates (68.9–73.0% for Llama2-70B in RAG) but at catastrophic utility cost: Blocklisted F1 collapses to 12.0 and In-Domain F1 to 7.7 (from ~60), a >60% loss. Among unlearning methods, UnlearningPO (66.2% win rate) and UnlearningGD (64.1%) reduce similarity most effectively, but both suffer 30–60% utility loss (e.g., UnlearningGD drops MMLU from 35.3 to 15.8). R-CAD uniquely preserves utility scores when not triggered (Blocklisted F1 = 40.5, matching vanilla) but halves inference speed (0.53×) and in the worst case (when the gold document is retrieved) drops Blocklisted F1 to 5.7. Overall, the paper's central finding is that no single method dominates: the best risk-reduction methods (unlearning, Top-k) sacrifice utility, while the best utility-preserving methods (System Prompt, MemFree) leave residual regurgitation risk.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by introducing the concept of 'copyright takedown for LMs' as a distinct post-training procedure, deliberately differentiating it from both DMCA takedowns and prior machine unlearning work. The key conceptual departure from unlearning benchmarks like TOFU (Maini et al., 2024a) is the evaluation criterion: rather than asking whether all information about training data is removed, COTAEVAL asks whether interventions prevent near-similar outputs while *retaining* uncopyrightable factual knowledge—a distinction grounded in copyright law (facts are not copyrightable). The paper repurposes existing decoding-time methods (MemFree, R-CAD, Top-k Perturbation) and training-based unlearning methods (GA, GD, KL, PO) under a unified evaluation framework, adding the RAG scenario that prior unlearning work does not address.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first systematic evaluation of whether copyright takedown is actually feasible for deployed LMs, directly informing ongoing policy discussions and litigation. The finding that no off-the-shelf method simultaneously achieves low similarity, high utility, and low overhead suggests that current technical capabilities lag behind legal and policy expectations. Key open questions include: whether the gap between preventing verbatim matches and preventing semantically similar outputs can be closed, how takedown methods scale to large blocklists, and whether the 30–60% factual knowledge loss from unlearning is acceptable given that facts are legally non-copyrightable. The paper also highlights that the evaluation metrics are proxies for legal 'substantial similarity' rather than dispositive measures, leaving the legal-technical interface unresolved.
