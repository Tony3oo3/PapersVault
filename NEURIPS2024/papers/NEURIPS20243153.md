## Problem

Existing unlearnable data protection strategies are designed for 2D images and cannot be directly transferred to 3D point clouds due to the structural disparity between pixel space and point space. Moreover, most prior unlearnable works prevent unauthorized learning but simultaneously impede authorized users from extracting knowledge from the protected data, creating a practical usability gap that has been overlooked in the literature.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243153/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes clean 3D point cloud samples X ∈ R^{3×p} (1024 points for most datasets, 256 for KITTI) with class labels Y and produces unlearnable point cloud data by left-multiplying each sample with a class-specific 3×3 transformation matrix T_k. The protector also outputs a lightweight message M containing the class-wise transformation parameters (angles, scaling factors, shear/twist coefficients) transmitted over a secure channel. Authorized users apply the inverse matrix T_k^{-1} to restore learnable data, requiring no access to clean samples.

## Method

UMT (Multi Class-wise Transformation) assigns a unique combination of up to four 3D transformations (rotation, scaling, twisting, shear) to each class via a category-adaptive allocation strategy that samples angles and factors from uniform distributions, ensuring A_N = ⌈∛N⌉ distinct values per axis so that A_N³ ≥ N combinations exist. The key insight is that the class-wise setting causes DNNs to learn a shortcut mapping between the transformation pattern and the class label rather than genuine shape features, so models trained on UMT data achieve high accuracy on identically-transformed test sets but fail on clean test sets. A data restoration scheme exploits the invertibility of all four transformation matrices (Property 2) to multiply unlearnable samples by T_k^{-1}, recovering learnable data for authorized users. Theoretically, the paper proves via GMM modeling and Bayesian decision boundary analysis that the UMT dataset's classification accuracy under the Bayes classifier is strictly lower than the clean dataset's, establishing a formal unlearnability guarantee.

## Results

On ModelNet10, UMT(k=2) using rotation+scaling (RS) reduces average test accuracy across 12 point cloud models from 89.61% (clean) to 23.61%, a drop of ~66 percentage points. On ModelNet40, UMT(k=3) (RSW) further reduces accuracy to 14.09% from a clean baseline of 85.89%. On the real-world ScanObjectNN dataset, UMT(k=3) drops average accuracy from 66.91% to 16.32%. The RS combination (k=2) consistently achieves the strongest unlearnable effect across all datasets, with the data restoration scheme recovering accuracy to near-clean levels.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not directly benchmark against other unlearnable methods; instead, it compares clean-data baselines against UMT-transformed data to demonstrate the unlearnable effect. The key comparison is between clean training (high accuracy) and UMT training (low accuracy) across 16 point cloud architectures. UMT(k=2) with RS achieves the strongest unlearnable effect on ModelNet10 (23.6% vs. 89.6% clean), while UMT(k=3) with RSW is most effective on ModelNet40 (14.1% vs. 85.9% clean). Notably, rotation-invariant networks (RIConv++, 3DGCN) partially resist single rigid transformations (e.g., RIConv++ retains 75.9% on ModelNet40 under UMT(k=2)), but combining rigid and non-rigid transformations (k=3, k=4) defeats these invariances, dropping RIConv++ to 10.5%. The data restoration scheme recovers accuracy to near-clean levels, addressing a gap in prior unlearnable work where even authorized users cannot learn from protected data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper transplants the class-wise unlearnable example paradigm from 2D images (Huang et al., ICLR 2021; Sadasivan et al., CVPR 2023) to 3D point clouds, replacing pixel-space perturbations with geometric transformations (rotation, scaling, twisting, shear). The twisting transformation is borrowed from TPC (Chu et al., ICML 2022), and the GMM-based theoretical analysis follows the Bayesian decision boundary framework of Javanmard & Soltanolkotabi. The key departure is the addition of a data restoration scheme via class-wise inverse matrices, addressing a practical gap the authors identify in most prior unlearnable works where even authorized users cannot recover learnability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first integral 3D unlearnable framework combining protection and authorized restoration, filling a gap where prior 2D unlearnable methods cannot be directly applied due to the structural differences between pixel grids and unordered point sets. The acknowledged vulnerability to transformation-invariant networks (RIConv, 3DGCN, SE(3)-Transformer) is a significant open question: the scheme's robustness depends on the absence of networks invariant to non-rigid transformations like twisting and shear, which do not yet exist. If such networks are developed, UMT would be defeated, making the long-term security of this approach contingent on the pace of invariant architecture research.
