## Problem

Existing latent-space dynamics models (MLPs, NODEs, RNNs, Lagrangian Neural Networks) cannot support closed-form model-based control because they lack a physical energy structure, do not inherently conserve stability properties of the real system, and have no invertible mapping between control inputs and latent-space forcing. This prevents leveraging well-understood control strategies like potential-energy shaping in learned low-dimensional spaces.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 32×32-pixel RGB images of physical systems (pendulums, mass-springs, continuum soft robots) along with a control input vector u(t) ∈ ℝ^m. It produces reconstructed images (both static and multi-step dynamic predictions) and, for control, a latent-space forcing vector τ ∈ ℝ^n that is decoded back to an actuation input u via a learned forcing decoder η(τ) = E(τ)τ. The latent state ξ = [z, ż] ∈ ℝ^{2n_z} has dimension 2–24 depending on the system, and the entire pipeline (β-VAE encoder, CON dynamics, decoder, forcing decoder) is trained jointly.

## Method

The CON architecture consists of n damped harmonic oscillators coupled through non-diagonal stiffness K, damping D, and a tanh(Wx+b) nonlinear coupling, excited by an input-dependent forcing τ = B(u)u. A key insight is a coordinate transformation into W-coordinates (x_w = Wx) that renders the generalized force symmetric, enabling derivation of a well-defined potential energy and kinetic energy, which in turn permits a strict Lyapunov proof of global asymptotic stability (unforced) and global input-to-state stability (forced) under mild positive-definiteness conditions on M_w, K_w, D_w. For efficient rollout, CFA-CON splits the dynamics into decoupled linear components (integrated via a closed-form damped-oscillator solution) and residual coupled nonlinear components (integrated numerically at a coarser timescale), yielding ~2× training speedup. The control law combines a feedforward term compensating the learned potential forces (K_w z^d + tanh(z^d + b)) with an integral-saturated P-satI-D feedback term, and a forcing decoder η trained to approximate g^{-1} maps the desired latent forcing back to a physical input.

## Results

On the six benchmark datasets (three unactuated mechanical systems and three actuated soft robots), CON-M achieves the best or near-best RMSE across all settings, with particularly strong results on the soft robot datasets: 0.1063 on CS (vs. 0.2537 for coRNN, a ~58% reduction) and 0.1785 on PCC-NS-3 (vs. 0.2474 for coRNN). On the unactuated mechanical datasets (M-SP+F, S-P+F), CON matches NODE within ~15% RMSE while using two orders of magnitude fewer parameters (34 vs. 3368 for M-SP+F). For latent-space control of a two-segment soft robot, the P-satI-D+FF controller exploiting CON's potential structure achieves an RMSE of 2.12 rad/m, a 26% improvement over the MECH-NODE P-satI-D baseline (2.88 rad/m), with a >55% faster response time.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against NODE (Neural ODE), CON is roughly on par on the unactuated mechanical datasets (M-SP+F: 0.0303 vs. 0.0264; S-P+F: 0.1303 vs. 0.1260) but with ~100× fewer parameters (34 vs. 3368), and CFA-CON slightly edges out NODE on D-P+F (0.1307 vs. 0.1324). Against coRNN, CON-M delivers a decisive win on the soft robot datasets: 0.1063 vs. 0.2537 on CS (~58% lower RMSE) and 0.1785 vs. 0.2474 on PCC-NS-3 (~28% lower), while coRNN is competitive on the simpler mechanical datasets. Against MECH-NODE, CON-M achieves a 6% lower RMSE on PCC-NS-3 (0.1785 vs. 0.1900) and a 26% lower trajectory-tracking RMSE in the closed-loop control experiment (2.12 vs. 2.88 rad/m). Against RNN and GRU, CON consistently outperforms on the soft robot datasets (e.g., PCC-NS-3: 0.1785 vs. 0.2232 for RNN and 0.2148 for GRU) while being competitive on the mechanical benchmarks. The CFA-CON variant (closed-form approximation) maintains within ~2–5% RMSE of full CON-M across all datasets while doubling training speed.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of coupled oscillatory recurrent networks (coRNN, UniCoRNN, Random Oscillators Network) by reformulating the coupling structure to admit a Lagrangian interpretation and proving global Input-to-State Stability via Lyapunov arguments—properties that prior oscillatory networks lacked (they only had local stability under stringent conditions). It also draws on classical control theory (potential shaping à la Bloch et al., integral-saturated PID à la Pustina et al.) to design a latent-space controller, and introduces a closed-form approximation (CFA-CON) that splits the ODE into decoupled linear and coupled nonlinear parts for efficient training. The key departure from prior latent-dynamics models (NODE, MECH-NODE, LNNs) is that CON simultaneously provides physical structure, formal stability guarantees, and an invertible input-forcing mapping—three properties that no single prior architecture satisfied.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete gap: existing learned latent dynamics (NODEs, RNNs, LNNs) cannot be directly combined with well-understood closed-form control strategies like potential shaping because they lack physical structure, stability guarantees, and invertible input mappings. The ISS proof is a clean theoretical contribution that makes the learned dynamics provably bounded, which is rare in the learned-dynamics literature. However, the method assumes a single globally asymptotically stable equilibrium, Markovian dynamics, and mechanical structure—limiting applicability to systems with multiple equilibria, nonholonomic constraints, or hidden states. Open questions include how performance degrades when latent dimension ≠ input dimension, whether the CFA-CON approximation holds for strongly nonlinear coupling regimes, and whether the approach transfers to real-world (non-simulated) soft robots.
