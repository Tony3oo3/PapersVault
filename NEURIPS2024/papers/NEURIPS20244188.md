## Problem

Nonparametric instrumental variable (NPIV) regression is an ill-posed inverse problem, and existing methods either minimize empirical surrogates of the populational risk or enforce large collections of moment restrictions, neither of which directly optimizes the true risk. Moreover, these approaches are tightly coupled to quadratic losses and the additive-noise structure, making them difficult to extend to important settings like binary outcomes. The paper asks whether one can formulate NPIV estimation as a direct functional stochastic gradient descent problem on the populational risk, with a gradient that is both analytically tractable and amenable to flexible estimator choices.

## Input / Output

The method requires two separate datasets: N i.i.d. (X, Z, Y) triplets used to pre-compute the density-ratio estimator Φ̂, the conditional-mean estimator r̂, and the conditional-expectation-operator estimator P̂, and M i.i.d. Z samples that drive the SAGD loop. Notably, the inner SAGD loop consumes only Z values (no X or Y), which decouples the expensive regression estimators from the iterative optimization. The output is the structural function ĥ(X) ∈ L²(X), obtained as the average of the M iterates, from which causal effects of X on Y can be read off.

## Method

The key insight is to compute the Fréchet gradient of the populational risk R(h) = E[ℓ(r(Z), P[h](Z))] analytically as ∇R(h)(x) = E[Φ(x,Z) ∂₂ℓ(r(Z), P[h](Z))], where Φ = p_{X,Z}/(p_X p_Z) is the density-ratio kernel. This replaces the need to estimate the adjoint operator P* (which appears in the naive gradient expression) with the simpler task of estimating Φ, a well-studied density-ratio problem solvable via uLSIF or neural networks. At each SAGD step, the algorithm evaluates u_m = Φ̂(·, z_m) ∂₂ℓ(r̂(z_m), P̂[h_{m-1}](z_m)) and updates h_m = proj_H[h_{m-1} − α_m u_m], with the final estimator being the iterate average. The framework is agnostic to the choice of Φ̂, r̂, and P̂ (kernel or neural network), and because the loss ℓ appears only through its second-argument derivative, non-quadratic losses such as binary cross-entropy can be plugged in to handle binary outcomes without architectural changes. A finite-sample excess-risk bound is provided, decomposing into a vanishing SGD term and a term quantifying the estimation errors of Φ̂, r̂, and P̂.

## Results

The paper evaluates SAGD-IV (Kernel and Deep variants) on synthetic data with four response functions (step, abs, linear, sin) using log-MSE as the primary metric over 20 realizations. 2SLS dominates the linear scenario as expected but performs poorly on all nonlinear functions. Among kernel methods, Kernel SAGD-IV achieves the best stability/performance tradeoff with the smallest variance across scenarios, while Deep SAGD-IV consistently outperforms the other two neural-network-based baselines (DeepIV and DeepGMM). In the binary response setting, Kernel SAGD-IV achieves results on par with the continuous response scenario, while Deep SAGD-IV struggles to recover the true structural function.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against 2SLS, SAGD-IV clearly wins on all nonlinear response functions (step, abs, sin) while 2SLS only dominates the linear case, confirming the necessity of nonparametric approaches. Against KIV, both SAGD-IV variants are competitive on step and sin, but Kernel SAGD-IV significantly outperforms KIV on the abs response function where KIV's performance degrades substantially; Kernel SAGD-IV also exhibits lower variance than KIV. Against DualIV, SAGD-IV shows markedly better stability — DualIV exhibits the highest variance across all scenarios, which the authors attribute to its fragile hyperparameter selection procedure requiring an unguided 'small constant' regularization parameter. Against DeepIV and DeepGMM, Deep SAGD-IV consistently achieves lower log-MSE, performing better than both neural-network-based baselines across all four response functions. In the binary outcome setting, Kernel SAGD-IV performs remarkably well (on par with its continuous-response performance), while Deep SAGD-IV has more difficulty, and no other baseline method is directly applicable without significant modification.

## Relation to Prior Work

SAGD-IV departs from the dominant NPIV estimation paradigms—empirical risk minimization (KIV, DualIV, DeepIV) and empirical moment restriction satisfaction (DeepGMM, 2SLS)—by instead computing an analytical functional gradient of the true populational risk and applying stochastic gradient descent in function space. It builds on the conditional expectation operator formulation of Newey & Powell (2003) and the stochastic gradient framework for statistical inverse problems of Fonseca & Saporito (2022), while borrowing kernel mean embeddings from KIV (Singh et al., 2019) for estimating the conditional expectation operator P. A key conceptual departure is replacing the need to estimate the adjoint operator P* with the simpler task of density ratio estimation Φ, which is a well-studied problem in machine learning and avoids nested operator evaluations at each gradient step.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework's ability to accommodate non-quadratic losses provides a natural and low-effort path to binary outcome NPIV, a setting that has received minimal attention in recent NPIV literature and where existing methods (KIV, DualIV, GMM-based) would require substantial reformulation. The excess risk bound explicitly quantifies how estimation errors in Φ, r, and P propagate to the final estimator, giving practitioners a clear diagnostic. Open questions include establishing consistency of the estimator (which requires additional assumptions like strong convexity of the risk or a continuous inverse of P), and the scalability limitation of relying on kernel methods for P estimation in high-dimensional or large-data settings. All experiments are on synthetic data, leaving open the question of performance on real-world causal inference problems.
