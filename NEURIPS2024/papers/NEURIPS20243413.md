## Problem

Motion time series from wearable and mobile devices are critical for human activity recognition, but collecting large-scale labeled datasets is hindered by privacy concerns, and existing models train and test on the same dataset, failing to generalize across three key deployment variations: device body location, device mounting orientation, and activity type. No unified pre-training procedure existed that could address all three generalization challenges simultaneously.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243413/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes tri-axial acceleration and angular velocity signals sampled at 20 Hz, which during inference are assigned to the nearest joint in a 22-joint SMPL skeleton graph (remaining joints zero-padded to emulate the random joint masking used in pre-training). Text label candidates are encoded by a CLIP text encoder and compared via inner-product similarity against the graph embedding to produce the predicted activity class. For fine-tuning, a linear classifier head is appended to the frozen graph encoder and trained with cross-entropy loss on one-hot labels.

## Method

UniMTS synthesizes large-scale motion time series from HumanML3D skeleton data via a physics engine that computes accelerations (second derivatives of joint positions) and angular velocities (first derivatives of orientation quaternions), then adds Gaussian sensor noise. A spatio-temporal graph convolutional network (ST-GCN) encodes the multi-joint signals, with random joint masking (1–5 joints selected per iteration) to simulate partial device coverage and enable location generalization. Rotation-invariant augmentation applies a random SO(3) rotation matrix per joint per iteration, making the model agnostic to device orientation. The text side uses CLIP's pre-trained text encoder on GPT-3.5-augmented paraphrases of the original motion descriptions. Pre-training maximizes InfoNCE contrastive similarity between graph embeddings and text embeddings, enabling zero-shot classification by matching real signal embeddings against label text embeddings at inference time.

## Results

In the zero-shot setting (no downstream training data), UniMTS achieves 43.5% average accuracy, 34.3% macro-F1, and 58.9% R@2 across 18 real-world motion time series datasets, outperforming the best baseline (ImageBind at 12.5% accuracy) by 340%. In the few-shot setting (1–10 samples per class), UniMTS improves over the best baseline by 16.3%. In the full-shot setting, UniMTS reaches 90.6% average accuracy and 87.5% macro-F1, surpassing the strongest baselines (BioBankSSL and SHARE at 85.8% accuracy) by 9.2%. The model uses only 4.94M parameters in its graph encoder and requires ~1.3 minutes to fine-tune on UCI-HAR, compared to ~9.8 minutes for ImageBind.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UniMTS outperforms all 16 baselines across every evaluation setting. In zero-shot, the closest competitor is HARGPT (12.9% avg accuracy) and ImageBind (12.5%), both of which are limited by single-location training data (ImageBind/IMU2CLIP use head-mounted devices) or lack of native time-series training (HARGPT, LLaVA); UniMTS achieves 43.5%, a 3.4× improvement. IMUGPT (11.1%) struggles because it requires per-dataset training and cannot generalize across activities. In full-shot fine-tuning, the strongest baselines are BioBankSSL (85.8% acc, trained on 700K person-days of UK Biobank wrist data) and SHARE (85.8%, leveraging shared label structures), while UniMTS reaches 90.6% acc / 87.5% F1. Self-supervised methods (TST 79.9%, TS2Vec 84.5%, TARNet 75.1%) and conventional models (THAT 80.1%, TimesNet 78.0%, GPT4TS 78.7%, XGBoost 79.4%, MA-CNN 73.2%, DeepConvLSTM 74.8%) all trail UniMTS by 5–17 points. The gap is largest on hard-level datasets (UTD-MHAD, MMAct with 27–35 classes), where UniMTS's contrastive semantic alignment provides the most benefit over baselines that rely on dataset-specific training.

## Relation to Prior Work

UniMTS builds on the spatio-temporal graph convolutional network (ST-GCN) architecture for modeling joint relationships and on physics-based inertial sensor simulation (IMUSim) for generating training data, but departs fundamentally from prior motion time series work in its pre-training paradigm. Conventional HAR models (MA-CNN, DeepConvLSTM, THAT) and self-supervised representation learners (TST, TS2Vec, BioBankSSL) all train and evaluate on the same or highly similar datasets, while prior pre-trained multimodal models (ImageBind, IMU2CLIP) are restricted to head-mounted device data. UniMTS introduces a CLIP-style contrastive learning objective that aligns physics-simulated motion time series with LLM-augmented text descriptions, combined with rotation-invariant augmentation and random joint masking, to simultaneously address three generalization axes (device location, orientation, activity) that no prior method tackles jointly.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first pre-trained motion time series model that generalizes across 18 diverse real-world datasets without dataset-specific training, which is practically significant for privacy-preserving deployment on edge devices where collecting large labeled datasets is infeasible. The 340% zero-shot improvement, while partly attributable to weak baselines not designed for this cross-domain scenario, demonstrates that physics-simulated data from skeleton motion can serve as a viable pre-training corpus. Key open questions include whether simulated-to-real gap can be further reduced (the authors note sensors are near joints, not on them), whether the framework extends to other time series tasks like inertial navigation, and whether enriching the pre-training corpus with video-based pose estimation would yield further gains.
