## Problem

Paired human captures—of the same subject from systems of diverse quality and availability—are almost exclusively available within dedicated industrial labs, stalling research on photorealistic avatars that are complete (full body), driveable (from lightweight sensors), and generalizable (adaptable to new identities). Without such paired data, it is impossible to build or evaluate mechanisms that drive avatars from headset cameras or to study generalization from low-quality phone scans to high-resolution multi-view captures.

## Input / Output

Ava-256 pairs 256 subjects captured in a 170-camera RGB dome (4096×2668, 30 fps) with the same subjects on a Quest Pro headset (5 IR cameras, 400×400, 72 fps); Goliath-4 extends to 4 subjects across 8 modalities including relightable time-multiplexed head/hand scans, full-body captures in minimal and regular clothing, and corresponding phone captures with RGB video (1080×1920) and depth (360×640). The released models consume headset IR images, expression codes, pose and latent codes, joint angles, and light-source parameters to produce photorealistic head renders, relightable head and hand renders, full-body avatar renders, and person-specific latent expression codes. Data is distributed in AVIF format at four size tiers (4/8/16/32 TB) with progressive downsampling of resolution, camera count, and frame rate to fit consumer storage.

## Method

Codec Avatar Studio's core contribution is the joint release of two paired-capture datasets with pre-trained state-of-the-art avatar models under CC-by-NC 4.0. The multi-identity face model represents faces as Mixture of Volumetric Primitives (MVP) rendered via a differentiable volumetric renderer, with a 2D CNN decoder that disentangles identity (initialized from geometry and UV-texture biases of a neutral expression) from expression (encoded by a variational encoder), enabling consistent expression transfer across subjects. A generalizable face encoder maps headset IR images to the multi-person latent expression space, trained by relating person-specific codes to multi-person codes via pseudo-frontal renderings, with pseudo ground truth derived from cycle consistency between personalized avatar renders and headset images. Relightable heads use 3D Gaussians rendered via EWA splatting with global light transport modeled as learnable diffuse spherical harmonics and specular spherical Gaussians; relightable hands use articulated MVP with Deep Shadow Maps for explicit shadow features; and full-body avatars decode a mesh and view-dependent texture from a pose-conditioned CNN rendered with a differentiable rasterizer.

## Results

This is a dataset and code release paper rather than a method paper, so there are no standard quantitative benchmark metrics (e.g., PSNR, FID) reported in the main text. The key contributions are two paired-capture datasets: Ava-256 (256 subjects × 2 modalities: ~170-camera dome + Quest Pro headset IR) and Goliath-4 (4 subjects × 8 modalities: relightable head/hand, clothed/minimal full-body, and corresponding phone captures). The paper provides qualitative demonstrations (Figures 8–9) of pre-trained relightable head (RGCA), relightable hand (RelightableHands), and full-body (driving-signal-aware codec) models, along with a generalizable face encoder that drives multi-identity avatars from headset IR images. Four size-friendly release tiers (4/8/16/32 TB) are offered using AVIF compression to balance quality and accessibility.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not propose a new method to benchmark against baselines; it is a dataset and toolkit release. The closest comparison is Table 2, which positions Ava-256 against existing multi-view face datasets. Ava-256 is the only dataset in the table that includes paired headset (Quest Pro IR) captures, which is essential for driveability research. In terms of scale, it offers 256 subjects with 80 views and 35 sentences/expressions, comparable to NeRSemble (222 IDs, 16 views) and RenderMe-360 (500 IDs, 60 views), while providing substantially more images (217M) at a practical resolution. For full-body and hand data, Goliath-4 is compared qualitatively to Humman [12] (1000 subjects, multimodal) and 4D-Dress [59] (78K scans, garment variety); Goliath-4 trades subject count for higher per-subject quality, including relightable captures and paired phone data. The paper explicitly notes limitations: Goliath-4's 4-subject size restricts generalization studies, and no multi-identity relightable head decoder can be trained from the current release.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper is a dataset and toolkit release rather than a methodological contribution; it provides paired captures (high-resolution multi-view dome + commercial headset, and multi-view + phone) that were previously confined to industrial labs. The accompanying models are direct implementations of prior work: the multi-identity face model follows Cao et al. [13] (MVP-based volumetric avatars), relightable heads use Saito et al. [49] (3D Gaussian splatting with spherical harmonics/Gaussians), hands use Iwase et al. [23] (articulated MVP with Deep Shadow Maps), and full-body avatars use Bagautdinov et al. [4] (mesh-based neural textures). The generalizable face encoder builds on Wei et al. [60] (cycle-consistency-based pseudo ground truth) and Bai et al. [5] (universal encoding via pseudo-frontal renderings), but the key novelty is the data infrastructure that enables these methods to be trained and evaluated on paired, multi-modality captures simultaneously.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By releasing 256 subjects with paired dome+headset captures and 4 subjects across 8 modalities (including relightable, clothed/minimal full-body, and phone scans), this work removes a major barrier for academic research on driveable, complete, and generalizable avatars—problems that previously required access to proprietary industrial capture rigs. The practical impact is substantial: the four-tier release sizes (4–32 TB) with AVIF compression make the data accessible to labs with modest storage, and the included pre-trained models and code lower the entry barrier further. Key limitations include the small Goliath-4 subject count (n=4) restricting generalization studies, the inability to train multi-identity relightable head decoders from the current release, and the absence of long-tail expressivity (sweat, fatigue, blood flow). Open questions include whether phone-to-high-quality transfer scales beyond four subjects and how to extend relightability to multi-identity settings.
