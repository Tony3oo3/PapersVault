## Problem

In repeated multi-unit uniform price auctions under bandit feedback, the best known regret bound was O~(K^{7/4}T^{3/4}), strictly worse than the O~(KT^{2/3}) achievable under discriminatory pricing, suggesting uniform pricing is inherently harder to learn. This paper closes that gap by showing O~(K^{4/3}T^{2/3}) regret is achievable, and further introduces an 'all-winner' feedback model (inspired by European electricity reserve markets) under which O~(K^{5/2}√T) regret is obtained.

## Input / Output

At each round t, the learner receives feedback of one of three types: bandit (allocation x^t and price p^t only if x^t>0), all-winner (allocation, price, and all winning adversary bids), or full information (all bids). The learner's valuations (v_k)_{k∈[K]} are known and fixed. The output is a K-unit non-increasing bid profile (b_1≥…≥b_K)∈[0,1]^K drawn from an ε-discretized pseudo-bid space H_ε, with the theoretical guarantee expressed as a regret bound over the T-round horizon.

## Method

The key technical contribution is a new pseudo-bid space H_ε that bijectively maps the discretized bid space B_ε to a set of binary variables: 'bids' h_{k,j} indicating b_k=jε and 'bid-gaps' h_{k+1/2,j} indicating b_k≥(j+1)ε>jε≥b_{k+1}. This representation decomposes the utility into a sum of sub-utilities w(h_{k,j},β), each depending on only one binary variable, which is the critical structural property enabling improved analysis. The algorithm combines component-wise exponential weighting (an EXP3-type update on individual bids and bid-gaps) with a weight-pushing sampling procedure that exploits the lexicographic graph structure of H_ε to sample valid pseudo-bids in O(K/ε) time rather than enumerating all O(1/ε^K) profiles. Because the estimator variance now scales with the number of individual components (O(1/ε)) rather than the number of full bid profiles (O(1/ε^K)), the standard EXP3 regret analysis yields the improved O~(K^{4/3}T^{2/3}) bound under bandit feedback, matching the T-dependency of discriminatory pricing up to a K^{1/3} factor.

## Results

Under bandit feedback, the proposed algorithm achieves a regret of Õ(K^{4/3} T^{2/3}), improving over the previous best bound of Õ(K^{7/4} T^{3/4}) from Branzei et al. (2024). This rate matches the T-dependency of the optimal Õ(KT^{2/3}) achievable in discriminatory pricing auctions, closing the gap between uniform and discriminatory pricing. Under the newly introduced all-winner feedback, the algorithm achieves Õ(K^{5/2}√T) regret, nearly matching the full-information rate of Õ(K^{3/2}√T) up to a factor of K. A matching Ω(T^{2/3}) lower bound is established for the bandit setting, confirming the T-dependency is tight up to logarithmic terms.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is Branzei et al. (2024), which achieves Õ(K^{7/4}T^{3/4}) regret under bandit feedback using a DAG-based equivalent action space. This paper improves the T-dependency from T^{3/4} to T^{2/3} and the K-dependency from K^{7/4} to K^{4/3}, by introducing a new pseudo-bid space H_ε that decomposes the utility into independent sub-utilities depending on single bids or bid-gaps rather than pairs of consecutive bids. Under full information, the paper recovers the same Õ(K^{3/2}√T) rate as Branzei et al., confirming no regression. The all-winner feedback is a novel contribution with no prior baseline; the achieved Õ(K^{5/2}√T) rate is within a factor of K of the full-information bound and matches the Ω(K√T) lower bound up to a K^{3/2} factor. The Ω(T^{2/3}) lower bound (extending Balseiro et al., 2019) confirms the bandit regret rate is optimal in T, partially resolving an open question raised by Branzei et al.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the DAG-based action space of Branzei et al. (2024) for multi-unit uniform auctions by introducing a 'pseudo-bid space' H_ε that encodes both bids and bid-gaps as binary variables, enabling a finer decomposition of utility into independent sub-utilities. The sampling procedure leverages the weight-pushing technique of Takimoto and Warmuth (2002) to efficiently draw actions from this structured space, while the lower bound extends the first-price auction result of Balseiro et al. (2019) to the multi-unit setting. The key departure from prior work is that the bid-gap variables capture the regularity of outcomes when the adversary (rather than the learner) sets the price, reducing estimator variance from depending on the number of bid profiles (|B_ε| ~ ε^{-K}) to the number of individual bids or gaps (~1/ε).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that uniform pricing achieves the same T^{2/3} regret dependence as discriminatory pricing under bandit feedback closes a gap previously thought to be inherent to the pricing rule, suggesting the combinatorial structure of the action space (not the pricing mechanism) is the true source of learning difficulty. The all-winner feedback model, motivated by European electricity reserve markets, is a practically relevant intermediate between bandit and full-information that achieves near-full-information rates (O~(K^{5/2}√T) vs. O~(K^{3/2}√T)). Open questions include whether the remaining K^{1/3} gap versus discriminatory pricing can be closed, whether similar rates hold in contextual settings with time-varying valuations, and whether the adversarial assumption can be relaxed to stochastic opposing bids.
