## Problem

Existing multi-task peer prediction mechanisms require tasks to be ex-ante identical with iid private signals, but pairwise comparison data (e.g., preferences for LLM outputs, product rankings) naturally exhibit transitivity structure that violates this assumption. This paper asks how to truthfully elicit such structured comparison data from rational agents without any ground-truth verification, and more generally, how to design truthful mechanisms for any information structure where one agent's signal is more correlated with a second agent's than with a third's.

## Input / Output

Each agent is assigned a pair of items to compare and observes a binary signal (±1); the assignment must be 'admissible,' meaning for every assigned pair (a, a′) there exists a third item a″ such that (a″, a′) and (a″, a) are also assigned to other agents. The mechanism consumes three binary reports per agent (the agent's own plus two designated peers) and outputs a scalar payment per agent. For the networked extension, the input additionally requires the social network graph (V, E), and the mechanism only needs the graph topology—not the Ising model parameters—to compute payments.

## Method

The mechanism uses a bonus-penalty payment U^BPP(s_i, s_j, s_k) = s_i·s_j − s_i·s_k that rewards agent i for agreeing with j but punishes agreement with k, where j and k are chosen so that j's signal uniformly dominates k's for i's (i.e., Pr[S_j = s_i | S_i = s_i] > Pr[S_k = s_i | S_i = s_i] for both s_i values). The key structural insight is that Bayesian Strong Stochastic Transitivity (encompassing Bradley-Terry-Luce, Thurstone, and Mallows models) guarantees this uniform dominance when three comparisons (a,a′), (a″,a′), (a″,a) are grouped together. The paper proves the mechanism is symmetrically strongly truthful (truth-telling is a strict BNE yielding the highest payment among all symmetric equilibria) and establishes uniqueness: BPP is the only payment function, up to positive affine transformation, that induces strict truthfulness for all uniformly dominant tuples. For networked data under Ising models, uniform dominance is proven via Griffiths' inequality (lower-bounding friend correlation) and Weitz's self-avoiding walk reduction (upper-bounding non-friend correlation), yielding an explicit condition on the ratio β̲/d relative to β̄.

## Results

On the SUSHI preference dataset (249 agents, 10 items), the BPP mechanism yields positive payments for over 75% of agents under truth-telling, compared to only ~50% under the uninformed (random) strategy. On the Last.fm networked dataset (1892 agents, 12704 edges), for the most popular artist (Lady Gaga, 32.3% listener fraction), truth-telling produces an average payment of 0.37 with >76% of agents receiving positive payments, while the uninformed strategy yields a near-zero average payment with only ~50% positive. Under unilateral deviation on Last.fm, the average payment drops from 0.37 to near zero, confirming the incentive to report truthfully.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not compare its BPP mechanism against other peer prediction mechanisms (e.g., Bayesian Truth Serum, multi-task peer prediction of Dasgupta & Ghosh). Instead, it evaluates the mechanism against two internal baselines: (1) the uninformed strategy (all agents report random bits) and (2) unilateral deviation (one agent deviates while all others are truthful). In both the SUSHI and Last.fm experiments, the truth-telling ECDF first-order stochastically dominates both the uninformed and unilateral-deviation ECDFs, meaning truth-telling yields higher payments at every quantile. The paper notes that prior multi-task peer prediction mechanisms are inapplicable here because comparison tasks are not ex-ante identical (they exhibit stochastic transitivity), so no direct head-to-head comparison with those methods is possible. The mechanism's advantage over the uninformed baseline is substantial: on Last.fm, the average payment drops from 0.37 to near zero, and the fraction of agents receiving positive payments falls from >76% to ~50%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the multi-task peer prediction framework of Dasgupta and Ghosh [11] and Shnayder et al. [57], which require tasks to be ex-ante identical (iid), to settings where agents' private signals are correlated through structural properties like stochastic transitivity (comparison data) or homophily (networked data). The key conceptual departure is the identification of 'uniform dominance' as the necessary and sufficient information structure under which the simple bonus-penalty payment induces a symmetrically strongly truthful equilibrium. The paper also draws on tools from statistical physics (Griffiths' inequality, Weitz's self-avoiding walk) to prove uniform dominance holds in Ising models, connecting mechanism design to correlation bounds in graphical models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of uniform dominance as a general design principle, combined with the uniqueness theorem showing the bonus-penalty payment is the only payment (up to affine transformation) that works for all uniformly dominant tuples, provides a clear template for designing truthful elicitation mechanisms in other non-iid settings (time-series, contextual feedback). However, the mechanism assumes agents are a priori similar, which breaks down for highly polarized preferences (e.g., political opinions), and does not account for effort or non-symmetric equilibria. Open questions include extending to multi-level effort, incorporating downstream learning objectives into the payment design, and whether the mechanism remains robust when the transitivity or homophily assumptions are only approximately satisfied.
