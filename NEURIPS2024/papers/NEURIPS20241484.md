## Problem

LLM performance on GSM8k, the standard benchmark for elementary mathematical reasoning, may be inflated by data contamination where benchmark questions leak into training corpora. Without a held-out equivalent benchmark, it is impossible to distinguish genuine reasoning ability from memorization of test-set answers. This paper addresses that gap by constructing GSM1k, a human-authored benchmark designed to be statistically indistinguishable from GSM8k, and using the performance gap between the two to quantify overfitting across leading models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241484/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each evaluation presents a grade school math word problem (solvable with only +, −, ×, ÷ and yielding a positive integer answer) alongside 5 randomly drawn few-shot examples from the GSM8k training split, with a maximum of 1000 generated tokens for chain-of-thought reasoning. The primary output is an accuracy score per benchmark, and the key derived metric is the overfitting gap (GSM8k accuracy minus GSM1k accuracy). A secondary output is the per-character log-likelihood of generating GSM8k test-set sequences, used as a proxy for memorization.

## Method

GSM1k is constructed entirely by human annotators (no LLMs) who are prompted with 3 GSM8k examples and asked to produce novel problems of matched difficulty, defined by the number of arithmetic resolution steps. Three quality layers are applied: trusted-annotator review, independent re-solving (discarding any problem where the second solver disagrees), and a general quality audit, reducing 2108 initial problems to 1205. A final winnowing step selects problems so that the answer-magnitude distribution of GSM1k closely matches GSM8k, and a human differentiation test confirms annotators cannot distinguish GSM1k from GSM8k items better than chance (21.83% vs. 20%). Models are evaluated via a fork of the LM Evaluation Harness at temperature 0 (open-source) or default API settings (closed-source), and overfitting is quantified as the GSM8k-to-GSM1k accuracy drop. The paper further correlates each model's per-character log-likelihood on GSM8k test sequences with its overfitting gap (Spearman's ρ = 0.36, p = 0.03), providing evidence that partial memorization of the test set contributes to inflated benchmark scores, while also identifying outliers that suggest indirect contamination pathways (e.g., reward-model training on synthetic process data).

## Results

Evaluating leading open- and closed-source LLMs on GSM1k reveals accuracy drops of up to 8% relative to GSM8k, with the Phi and Mistral families showing systematic overfitting across nearly all model sizes. A Spearman rank correlation of r²=0.36 (p=0.03) is found between a model's per-character log-likelihood of generating GSM8k test examples and its GSM8k-to-GSM1k performance gap, with each percentage-point gap associated with a 1.2×10⁻² increase in per-character log-likelihood. Human solve rates confirm comparable difficulty (GSM8k: 4.07±0.93 vs. GSM1k: 4.36±1.11 problems in 15 minutes), and human annotators identify the GSM1k odd-one-out at only 21.83% (vs. 20% chance), validating dataset similarity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Frontier models (GPT-4, Gemini, Claude) show minimal to no overfitting, performing similarly on GSM8k and GSM1k, suggesting either strong generalizable reasoning or careful data hygiene. The Phi family (Phi-2, Phi-3) exhibits systematic overfitting across sizes, with Phi-2 dropping 6% in accuracy; Mistral 7B and Mixtral models show similar patterns, though Mistral Large is the sole exception in the family. Llama and Llama 2 models display moderate overfitting, while pre-GSM8k models (GPT-2, GPT-NeoX-20B) confirm the datasets are difficulty-matched by showing negligible performance differences. Notable outliers include Math-Shepherd-Mistral-7B-RL (high overfit but low log-likelihood, suggesting reward-model leakage) and Llemma (high log-likelihood but minimal overfit, indicating small contamination does not necessarily cause overfitting). Overall, the paper demonstrates that overfitting is not purely explained by data contamination, as several models with high memorization signals do not overfit and vice versa.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends the overfitting-measurement methodology of Recht et al. (2019), who created new versions of CIFAR10 and ImageNet to quantify how much ImageNet classifier performance reflected memorization rather than generalization. Applied to the NLP/math-reasoning setting, it constructs GSM1k as a human-annotated, difficulty-matched counterpart to GSM8k, positioning itself alongside data-contamination detection work (Xu et al. 2024, Srivastava et al. 2024) but departing from purely functional evaluations by producing a fixed, non-synthetic dataset. The evaluation protocol builds on the LM Evaluation Harness (Gao et al. 2023a) and the standard 5-shot GSM8k prompting convention, while the log-likelihood analysis draws on memorization-quantification techniques from Carlini et al. (2023).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Because GSM8k is one of the most widely cited benchmarks for LLM mathematical reasoning, the finding that many open-source model families (Phi, Mistral, Llama) show systematic 4–8% overfitting gaps has immediate practical implications for how practitioners should interpret published scores. The moderate Spearman correlation (r²=0.36) between per-character log-likelihood on GSM8k and the overfitting gap, combined with notable outliers (Math-Shepherd overfitting without high memorization; Llemma memorizing without overfitting), reveals that data contamination is a necessary but not sufficient explanation, pointing to indirect mechanisms like reward-model leakage or checkpoint selection on benchmark performance. Open questions include whether the 1205-problem GSM1k is large enough to capture all overfitting effects, whether the difficulty-matching (via step count and answer magnitude) fully controls for distributional differences, and whether the frontier-model 'clean' result reflects genuine generalization or simply more aggressive data-hygiene practices.
