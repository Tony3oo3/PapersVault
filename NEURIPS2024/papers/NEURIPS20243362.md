## Problem

Deep learning-based multichannel speech enhancement and source localization systems are predominantly trained on simulated room impulse responses and theoretical diffuse noise, yet significant acoustic mismatches (microphone/source directivity, frequency-dependent wall absorption, irregular room geometry, and non-stationary noise spatial correlation) cause substantial performance degradation in real-world deployment. No existing dataset provides a large-scale collection of real-recorded multichannel speech with both direct-path target clean speech and source location annotations across diverse scenes, leaving a critical gap for training and benchmarking under realistic conditions.

## Input / Output

The dataset provides 32-channel microphone array recordings at 48 kHz (downsampled to 16 kHz for model training in 4-second clips) spanning 83.7 hours of Mandarin speech (static and moving loudspeaker) and 144.5 hours of ambient noise across 32 and 31 scenes respectively. Source location annotations are provided at 100 ms temporal resolution (azimuth, elevation, distance) via fisheye camera tracking of an LED marker, and direct-path target clean speech is estimated per-utterance using generalized cross-correlation between the known source signal and a preliminary enhancement output. The 32-channel geometry encompasses planar linear, circular, and 3D sub-arrays, enabling variable-array training with randomly selected 2–8 channel subsets.

## Method

The core contribution is a real-recording pipeline that captures multichannel speech and noise in 63 distinct scenes using a 32-microphone array (Audio-Technica BP899, 48 kHz), a high-fidelity loudspeaker playing 35 hours of Mandarin speech, and a 360° fisheye camera with an LED marker for source localization. Direct-path target clean speech is estimated by first running a source-signal-guided AlignSpatialNet (a dual-branch SpatialNet variant with multi-head cross-attention between source and recording branches) to obtain a rough direct-path estimate, then computing sample-level time shift and segment-level attenuation via generalized cross-correlation, with Pchip interpolation and resampling to handle moving-source Doppler effects. For benchmarking, the paper trains FaSNet-TAC and SpatialNet for speech enhancement, and CRNN and IPDnet for source localization, comparing four training-data configurations (simulated/real speech × simulated/real noise) to quantify the simulation-to-real gap. Variable-array generalization is demonstrated by training on random 2–8 channel sub-arrays of the 32-mic array and evaluating on an unseen 5-channel linear array, showing only modest performance loss relative to fixed-array models.

## Results

On the RealMAN test set, training with real-recorded speech and noise (real+real) consistently outperforms simulated training for both speech enhancement and source localization. For speech enhancement, SpatialNet trained with real+real data achieves SI-SDR of 7.3 dB (static) and 4.3 dB (moving), compared to −5.2 dB and −4.8 dB with sim+sim, and MOS-OVR of 2.71/2.63 vs. 2.46/2.40. For source localization, the CRNN trained with real+real data reaches 89.2% ACC(5°) and 2.2° MAE (static) versus 60.4% and 7.8° (sim+sim). Variable-array networks trained on sub-arrays of the 32-channel array achieve near fixed-array performance (e.g., 87.1% vs. 87.0% ACC(5°) for IPDnet on static speakers).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper benchmarks two speech enhancement networks (FaSNet-TAC and SpatialNet) and two source localization networks (CRNN and IPDnet) under four training-data configurations (sim/sim, sim/real, real/sim, real/real). For FaSNet-TAC, real+real training yields the best MOS-OVR (2.34 static, 2.26 moving) and lowest CER (21.9% static), though the gap over sim+real is modest (e.g., 2.34 vs. 2.36 MOS-OVR static), suggesting FaSNet-TAC is relatively robust to speech simulation. SpatialNet is far more sensitive: sim+real training actually degrades performance below unprocessed (MOS-OVR 1.32 vs. 1.52), while real+real achieves the best SI-SDR (7.3 dB static) and MOS-OVR (2.71 static). For source localization, the CRNN shows the largest real-vs-sim gap: ACC(5°) jumps from 60.4% (sim+sim) to 89.2% (real+real) for static speakers, with MAE dropping from 7.8° to 2.2°. The IPDnet variable-array experiment shows that training on random 2–8 channel sub-arrays generalizes well to an unseen 5-channel linear array, with only a small drop (87.1% vs. 87.0% ACC(5°) static) compared to a fixed-array model trained directly on that array.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground in the microphone array dataset space by providing the first real-recorded dataset with direct-path clean speech annotations, which the authors note 'has never been solved in the field' for real recordings. Prior datasets either rely on RIR measurements (MIR, BUTReverb, Reverb, DCASE, ACE, dEchorate) that limit scene diversity, or provide multi-channel recordings without direct-path targets (CHiME, LibriCSS, AISHELL-4, AliMeeting), forcing training on simulated data. The annotation pipeline builds on SpatialNet [34] via the AlignSpatialNet architecture for source-signal-guided direct-path estimation, and the variable-array experiments extend the permutation/number-invariant framework of FaSNet-TAC [13] and IPDnet [15] to a 32-channel real recording.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dataset directly addresses the simulation-to-real gap that degrades multichannel speech enhancement and localization in deployment, and its 32-channel geometry enables training variable-array networks generalizable to unseen hardware—a practically critical capability. Key limitations include the use of a single loudspeaker (whose frontal directivity cannot capture the full range of human speaker directivities), Mandarin-only source speech (~35 hours), and the residual unclear mismatches that still degrade SpatialNet even with real training data. Open questions include whether the direct-path annotation method generalizes to non-omni-directional microphones, how to incorporate speaker directivity variation, and whether the remaining simulation-to-real gap for spatially-sensitive architectures can be fully closed.
