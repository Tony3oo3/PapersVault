## Problem

Neural constructive solvers for VRPs rely on feasibility masking to ensure constraint satisfaction, but this mechanism breaks down for problems with complex interdependent constraints (e.g., TSPTW, TSPDL): local feasibility of each node selection does not guarantee global feasibility, while computing globally correct masks is itself NP-hard. This 'masking dilemma' causes existing neural methods to produce 100% infeasible solutions even on easy instances of these variants.

## Input / Output

The method takes a complete graph instance with node coordinates (normalized to [0,1]) and constraint-specific features per node (time window bounds [l_i, u_i] for TSPTW, or demand δ_i and draft limit d_i for TSPDL) and produces a node visit sequence (tour) minimizing total Euclidean distance. It is applied as a plug-in to both autoregressive solvers (AM, POMO at n=50,100) and a non-autoregressive solver (GFACS at n=500), with the PIP decoder outputting a binary feasibility prediction per candidate node at each decoding step.

## Method

PIP combines two mechanisms: (1) a Lagrangian multiplier that augments the RL reward with constraint violation costs (total time-window exceedance or draft-limit exceedance plus a count of infeasible nodes), promoting constraint awareness during policy optimization; and (2) a one-step preventative infeasibility (PI) masking that, at each construction step, checks whether selecting a candidate node would render any remaining unvisited node infeasible in the next step, and masks out such candidates to proactively confine the search to near-feasible regions. PIP-D replaces the expensive PI mask computation with an auxiliary decoder (mirroring the backbone architecture with a Sigmoid output) trained via weighted binary cross-entropy, using two adaptive strategies: periodic updates (training the PIP decoder every E_p epochs rather than every step) and hardness-adaptive label weighting to handle the severe imbalance between infeasible and feasible PI signals on harder instances.

## Results

On the most challenging Hard TSPTW-100, POMO*+PIP-D reduces the instance-level infeasible rate from 100% (POMO*) to 6.48%, a 93.52% reduction, while achieving a 0.31% optimality gap vs. LKH3. On Easy TSPTW-50, POMO*+PIP-D reaches 0.28% infeasible rate and 2.51% gap (vs. LKH3's 0.00% gap). On large-scale TSPTW-500 with GFACS, PIP-D cuts the solution-level infeasible rate from 58.20% to 0.03% and the optimality gap from 21.32% to 11.95%. PIP-D also trains 1.5× faster (n=50) and 5.8× faster (n=100) than PIP while matching or exceeding its solution quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the original neural backbones (AM, POMO, GFACS), PIP-D is transformative: it reduces infeasible rates from 100% to under 7% on Hard TSPTW and from 58.20% to 0.03% on large-scale TSPTW-500 (GFACS). Compared to the strong heuristic LKH3, PIP-D is competitive on Easy and Medium TSPTW (gaps of 2.51% and 3.32% vs. 0.00%) but actually outperforms LKH3 under equal per-instance time budgets on Hard TSPTW (2.67–3.07% infeasible vs. 30.60–97.28% for LKH3 at 0.37s). Against ORTools, PIP-D consistently achieves lower infeasibility and smaller gaps across all hardness levels. Versus the prior TSPTW-specific method MUSLA (and its adaptive variant), PIP-D delivers comparable or better gaps (e.g., 2.51% vs. 5.63% on Easy TSPTW-50) while being fully generic and not requiring problem-specific features or supervised datasets. On TSPDL, PIP-D similarly dominates: on Hard TSPDL-100, it reduces infeasibility from 99.98% (POMO*) to 12.84%, while ORTools and Greedy-L remain at 100% infeasible.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the standard encoder-decoder RL framework for constructive VRP solvers (AM, POMO) and the newer GFlowNet-based NAR solver (GFACS), all of which rely on feasibility masking to ensure constraint satisfaction during autoregressive solution construction. It departs from prior constraint-handling approaches—soft-constraint relaxation (Tang et al., Zhang et al.), problem-specific supervised features (MUSLA), and iterative solvers that tolerate temporary violations—by identifying a fundamental 'masking dilemma': for VRPs with complex interdependent constraints (TSPTW, TSPDL), local feasibility masking cannot guarantee global feasibility, while computing globally correct masks is itself NP-hard. The PIP framework addresses this gap by combining a Lagrangian multiplier for constraint-aware policy optimization with a one-step preventative infeasibility mask, and PIP-D further learns these masks via an auxiliary decoder with adaptive training strategies.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and formally articulates a structural limitation of all existing neural constructive VRP solvers when applied to problems with interdependent constraints, a gap that has been implicitly present but not previously addressed in the constructive-solver literature. The PIP framework's generality—demonstrated across AR (AM, POMO) and NAR (GFACS) backbones and two distinct constraint types—makes it a practical plug-in for the broader neural VRP community. Open questions include whether the one-step PI approximation suffices for even more complex constraint structures, whether theoretical guarantees can be established for the learned masks, and whether the approach extends to iterative solvers or other combinatorial domains like job-shop scheduling. A noted limitation is that PIP may not improve all backbone solvers on all VRP variants, and the computational cost of PI masking (O(n²)) remains a concern at very large scales.
