## Problem

In continuous-time RL, action values become indistinguishable as decision frequency increases, causing Q-learning to fail. While advantage-based methods (e.g., DAU) were shown to mitigate this for value-based agents, it was unknown whether distributional RL agents suffer similarly. This paper proves that action-conditioned return distributions collapse to the policy's return distribution at high frequency, and that the collapse rate (h^{1/2}) differs fundamentally from the value-based gap rate (h), meaning existing remedies do not directly transfer.

## Input / Output

The method operates in continuous-time MDPs where state dynamics are governed by SDEs, consuming state observations x ∈ R^n and time t at discrete decision intervals of length h. It produces m-quantile representations of both the policy return distribution η^π(t,x) and the superiority distribution ψ_{h;q}^π(t,x,a), with the latter structured as the quantile difference F^{-1}_{ζ_h}(τ) − F^{-1}_{η}(τ) rescaled by h^q. Action selection is made by maximizing a distortion risk measure (e.g., α-CVaR) over the superiority quantiles, ensuring the greedy action's superiority is identically zero.

## Method

The paper defines the distributional action gap as the minimum W_p distance between pairs of action-conditioned return distributions and proves it collapses at rate h^{1/2} (Theorems 3.6–3.7), slower than the O(h) rate of value-based gaps. It then introduces the superiority S_h^π as the W_p-optimal coupled difference representation between ζ_h^π(t,x,a) and η^π(t,x), uniquely characterized by the quantile difference F^{-1}_{ζ_h} − F^{-1}_{η} (Theorem 4.3). Rescaling by h^{1/2} preserves distributional action gaps while maintaining distortion-risk-measure action rankings (Theorem 4.8). The DSUP(1/2) algorithm learns the superiority via quantile TD-learning: predictions are θ(t,x) + h^{1/2}(φ(t,x,a) − φ(t,x,a*)) and bootstrap targets are hr(t,x) + γ^h θ(t+h, x_{t+h}), trained with a Quantile Huber loss. A two-timescale variant DAU+DSUP(1/2) additionally estimates the advantage function to preserve both mean and distributional action gaps simultaneously.

## Results

The paper's headline theoretical result is that W_p distributional action gaps of h-dependent action-conditioned return distributions collapse at rate h^{1/2} (Theorems 3.6–3.7), which is strictly slower than the O(h) rate for value-based action gaps (Eq. 2.6). This motivates the 1/2-rescaled superiority distribution, which preserves O(1) W_p action gaps across decision frequencies. Empirically, in a high-frequency option-trading benchmark (10 seeds × 10 stock dynamics models), DSUP(1/2) is the most consistent performer, outperforming all competitors at all but the two lowest frequencies in the risk-neutral setting and conclusively best in the risk-sensitive (α-CVaR) setting at ω=35 Hz.

## Baselines & Comparisons

In the risk-neutral option-trading experiments, DSUP(1/2) outperforms QR-DQN, DAU, DSUP(1), and DAU+DSUP(1/2) at all but the two lowest decision frequencies, where its performance is still very close to the best. DAU and DSUP(1) (which implicitly estimates A_h^π) exhibit nearly identical behavior, both underperforming DSUP(1/2) at higher frequencies. DAU+DSUP(1/2), despite preserving both value-based and distributional action gaps, is inconsistent and sometimes poor, likely because the variances of the distributions underlying A_h^π cause estimation difficulty at the tested frequencies. In the risk-sensitive setting (α-CVaR greedy selection), DSUP(1/2) is conclusively the best performer compared to QR-DQN. The paper identifies a fundamental dichotomy: existing methods can maintain O(1) expected return estimates or O(1) return variance estimates, but not both, and DSUP(1/2) exploits the latter for superior high-frequency performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Baird's advantage-based RL (Advantage Updating, Advantage Learning) and Tallec et al.'s Deep Advantage Updating (DAU) into the distributional and continuous-time settings. It builds on the quantile regression framework of QR-DQN (Dabney et al.) and the continuous-time DRL formalism of Wiltzer et al., introducing the 'superiority distribution' as a distributional analogue of the advantage function, defined via W_p-optimal couplings from optimal transport. The paper departs from prior work by showing that the distributional action gap collapses at rate h^{1/2} (rather than h for value-based gaps), which means that advantage-based remedies cannot be naively transliterated to distributional algorithms.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes that distributional RL agents suffer from the same high-frequency collapse as value-based agents, but at a different rate, which has practical implications for algorithm design in high-frequency domains like quantitative finance and robotics. The h^{1/2}-rescaled superiority (DSUP(1/2)) provides a principled, theoretically grounded alternative that outperforms both QR-DQN and DAU in option-trading simulations. A key open question is the fundamental dichotomy identified: existing methods can maintain O(1) expected return estimates or O(1) return variance estimates, but not both, and the paper leaves open whether a unified approach can resolve this tension. The empirical validation is limited to a single domain (option trading), and the two-timescale DAU+DSUP(1/2) variant underperforms, suggesting the theory-to-practice gap remains partially unresolved.
