## Problem

Existing face-swapping methods are designed for full-face replacement and cannot independently swap individual facial parts (eyes, nose, mouth) from multiple different source images in a single step. The core technical challenge is designing a fusion mechanism that efficiently and effectively combines features from multiple reference facial parts while preserving fine-grained spatial alignment, which conventional cross-attention adapters handle sub-optimally.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242568/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a target face image and up to three facial part reference images (eyes, nose, mouth) from potentially different individuals, all processed at 512×512 resolution (trained at 1024×1024). An open-set detector (Grounding-DINO) derives region masks from text prompts, and a CLIP encoder extracts spatial feature maps from the penultimate layer rather than the final pooled embedding to retain positional detail. The output is a single composited face where the specified parts are replaced while the remaining regions of the target are preserved, generated via a 50-step DDIM sampling process with an empty text prompt.

## Method

FuseAnyPart introduces two key modules. The Mask-based Fusion Module decomposes each face into regional CLIP feature maps, then assembles a complete latent-space face by interpolating source part features into the target's corresponding mask regions (Eq. 3), enabling simultaneous multi-source part replacement. The Addition-based Injection Module replaces the conventional cross-attention adapter by directly adding a linearly-projected and resized condition feature map to the UNet's latent features at cross-attention layers (Eq. 4), which preserves positional alignment of fine-grained facial details with fewer added parameters. Training follows a reconstruction objective where the model learns to regenerate a face conditioned on its own decomposed parts, with the masked target latent, mask, and noised latent concatenated as the UNet query (following Diffute). Ablations confirm that addition outperforms cross-attention for part similarity, that injection at cross-attention layers beats convolutional layers, and that hierarchical CLIP features degrade generation quality.

## Results

On FaceForensics++, FuseAnyPart achieves the best FID of 10.54, outperforming the next-best Diffswap (12.07) and StableDiffusion (18.57). For facial part similarity, FuseAnyPart attains FPSim-E of 0.3186 (best among generative methods), FPSim-N of 0.2234, and FPSim-M of 0.2196, all leading or near-leading among the compared approaches. The MSE of 0.77 is second only to Diffswap (0.15), which the authors attribute to Diffswap's tendency to over-copy the source face rather than faithfully preserving the target.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FuseAnyPart achieves the lowest FID (10.54) among all five baselines, indicating superior generation quality and naturalness. Against StableDiffusion (FID 18.57) and IP-Adapter (FID 69.35), which are not designed for part-level swapping, FuseAnyPart substantially outperforms on both fidelity and part similarity. Compared to E4S (FID 30.61), FuseAnyPart wins on FID, FPSim-E, and FPSim-M, though E4S achieves a notably higher FPSim-N (0.4047 vs. 0.2234), suggesting E4S preserves nose identity better in some cases. Against Diffswap (FID 12.07), FuseAnyPart improves FID by ~1.5 points and leads on all three FPSim metrics (e.g., FPSim-E 0.3186 vs. 0.2461), while Diffswap's lower MSE (0.15 vs. 0.77) is explained by its bias toward reproducing the source face rather than the target. FacePartsSwap, a non-generative cut-and-paste method, shows the highest FPSim-E (0.3269) and FPSim-M (0.2220) but has the worst FID (44.23) and MSE (24.40), confirming its limitations as a generative approach.

## Relation to Prior Work

FuseAnyPart builds on the Stable Diffusion latent diffusion framework and adopts the masked-latent concatenation training strategy from Diffute, but departs from the dominant cross-attention-based feature injection paradigm (as in IP-Adapter) by proposing a simpler Addition-based Injection Module that directly adds spatially-aligned feature maps to UNet latents. It extends prior partial face-swapping methods (E4S, Diffswap) that handle single-source region replacement to a multi-source, single-step setting where facial parts from different individuals are simultaneously assembled in latent space via a Mask-based Fusion Module. The use of an open-set detector (Grounding-DINO) rather than a fixed segmentation model for region extraction is a practical departure that improves generalization across styles.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first diffusion-based method enabling simultaneous multi-source fine-grained facial parts swapping, which opens the door to more flexible character design and privacy-preserving face editing workflows. The finding that a parameter-light addition mechanism outperforms cross-attention for spatially-aligned feature injection challenges the prevailing assumption that attention is necessary for effective conditioning in diffusion UNets, potentially informing broader feature-injection design. However, the method remains limited to three facial regions, struggles with extreme poses and occlusions, does not preserve expressions, and inherits the computational cost of iterative diffusion sampling—open questions that constrain its practical deployment.
