## Problem

Existing MLLMs process images in a fixed-grain manner as a single black box, lacking the ability to dynamically focus on small, information-rich regions that are critical for answering questions. This leads to hallucinations, poor interpretability, and degraded performance when the key visual evidence occupies only a small fraction of the input image. Additionally, no large-scale dataset provides intermediate visual chain-of-thought supervision (bounding boxes identifying key regions) to train MLLMs on this focused reasoning behavior.

## Input / Output

The pipeline takes an RGB image (typically high-resolution, downsampled to 224² or 336² for the vision encoder) and a text question as input. It first produces bounding box coordinates identifying the key region, then a visual sampler crops a square region centered on that bbox—expanding it to at least the encoder's minimum input size and shifting it inward if it exceeds image boundaries. The final output is a text answer generated by integrating visual tokens from both the full original image and the localized crop, effectively doubling the visual token budget for the answer generation step.

## Method

VisCoT introduces a multi-turn visual chain-of-thought pipeline built on a standard CLIP ViT-L/14 + linear projector + Vicuna-7/13B architecture without specialized modifications. The key mechanism is a CoT prompt appended to the question that instructs the model to predict a bounding box for the most informative region; a visual sampler then crops and resizes that region, and the same vision encoder extracts a second set of visual tokens from the crop. The LLM receives both token sets (H₀ from the full image and H₁ from the localized crop) to generate the final answer, enabling it to reason over both global context and fine-grained detail. Training is two-stage: the projector is first aligned on image-caption pairs with frozen encoder and LLM, then all weights are fine-tuned on a 438k Visual CoT dataset (376k of which are newly annotated with bounding boxes across five domains, plus LLaVA-1.5 and Shikra data for spatial grounding). The dataset construction leverages GPT-4 for linguistic annotation and PaddleOCR for visual region detection, with ~98k samples additionally containing step-by-step reasoning chains generated from GQA scene graphs.

## Results

On the proposed Visual CoT benchmark (12 datasets across 5 domains), VisCoT-7B at 336² resolution achieves an average score of 0.580, outperforming LLaVA-1.5-13B at 336² (0.478) by 10.2 points and SPHINX-13B at 224² (0.419) by 16.1 points. Notably, VisCoT-7B at 224² (0.550) already surpasses LLaVA-1.5-13B at 336², demonstrating that the visual CoT pipeline provides superior token efficiency — matching or exceeding 448² standard inference while using half the visual tokens. On SROIE (scanned receipt OCR), VisCoT achieves an 8× improvement over its own w/o-CoT ablation (0.341 vs. 0.044), and on Open Images (relation reasoning) it reaches 0.833 versus 0.412 for LLaVA-1.5-7B.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VisCoT-7B (224²) outperforms all three baselines on the Visual CoT benchmark average: it beats LLaVA-1.5-7B (336²) by 9.6 points (0.550 vs. 0.454), LLaVA-1.5-13B (336²) by 7.2 points (0.550 vs. 0.478), and SPHINX-13B (224²) by 13.1 points (0.550 vs. 0.419), despite using a smaller LLM and lower resolution than LLaVA-1.5. The largest per-dataset gains appear on SROIE (0.341 vs. 0.136 for LLaVA-1.5-7B) and Open Images (0.833 vs. 0.412), where the CoT bounding-box mechanism provides the most benefit. On standard MLLM benchmarks (Table 8, no CoT), VisCoT-7B (224²) still matches or exceeds LLaVA-1.5-7B (336²) on MMBench (67.9 vs. 64.3) and POPE (86.0 vs. 85.9), while VisCoT-13B (336²) achieves the best ScienceQA score (73.6) among all compared models. On Referring Expression Comprehension (Table 9), VisCoT-7B (224²) surpasses specialist detectors G-DINO-L (384²) and UNINEXT (640²) on RefCOCO+ val (85.68 vs. 82.75 and 85.24), and VisCoT-7B (336²) leads all generalist models on RefCOCO test-A (94.25). The ablation confirms that the CoT process is essential: removing it drops the average from 0.550 to 0.443, and using ground-truth bboxes instead of predicted ones raises it to 0.752, establishing the upper bound.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the LLaVA-1.5 architecture (CLIP ViT + linear projector + Vicuna LLM, two-stage training) and extends the text-domain chain-of-thought paradigm (Wei et al., 2022) into the visual modality by introducing intermediate bounding boxes as visual CoT supervision. It departs from prior visual grounding work (Shikra, KOSMOS-2, V*, CogCoM) that either modifies model architecture or collects manipulation steps, instead proposing a multi-turn pipeline where a standard MLLM first predicts a region, then re-encodes the cropped sub-image before answering. The dataset construction leverages GPT-4 for linguistic annotation and PaddleOCR for visual annotation across twelve existing VQA/captioning datasets, positioning itself as a data-centric contribution rather than an architectural one.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 438k Visual CoT dataset with intermediate bbox annotations fills a genuine gap in MLLM training resources, and the finding that visual CoT at 224² resolution outperforms the standard pipeline at 448² resolution (using half the visual tokens) offers a practical efficiency win for deployment. However, the model's bbox detection accuracy remains modest (37.2% average Top-1@0.5), and the large gap between model-predicted bboxes (0.550 avg) and ground-truth bboxes (0.752 avg) reveals that the bottleneck is region identification quality, not the zoom-and-answer mechanism itself. Open questions include whether multi-step or multi-region visual CoT could further improve performance, and whether the approach generalizes to video or higher-resolution inputs beyond the single-crop design.
