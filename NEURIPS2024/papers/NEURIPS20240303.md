## Problem

Existing fast text-to-3D generators either bake illumination into textures (making outputs look wrong under new lighting) or rely on slow SDS-based optimization (minutes to hours per asset). No feed-forward method simultaneously produces high-quality mesh geometry and physically-based rendering (PBR) materials—albedo, metalness, and roughness—that support realistic relighting in novel environments.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240303/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For text-to-3D, the method takes a text prompt and internally generates a 4-view, 6-channel image grid (3 shaded RGB channels + 3 albedo channels) at fixed canonical viewpoints (20° elevation; 0°, 90°, 180°, 270° azimuth). For image-to-3D, it accepts N≥1 posed RGB images at 512² resolution. The output is a textured 3D mesh extracted via Marching Tetrahedra from a triplane-supported SDF (128×128 resolution), with a 5-channel PBR texture map (albedo RGB, metalness, roughness) parameterized in UV space via xAtlas.

## Method

AssetGen is a two-stage pipeline. Stage 1 fine-tunes an Emu-like text-to-image diffusion model to output the 6-channel 4-view grid, deliberately avoiding direct prediction of metalness/roughness (which deviate from natural-image statistics) and instead providing shaded + albedo channels to reduce the statistical gap. Stage 2, MetaILRM, is a transformer-based reconstruction model built on LightplaneLRM that predicts an SDF and PBR fields; its key innovations are (i) a VolSDF renderer fused into custom Triton kernels for memory-efficient differentiable rendering at large batch sizes, (ii) a direct SDF loss implemented in the same fused kernels, and (iii) a deferred shading loss that evaluates the Disney GGX BRDF once per pixel (rather than per 3D point) to supervise PBR channels cheaply. A final texture refiner U-Net operates in UV space, fusing back-projected information from all input views via cross-attention to sharpen the coarse PBR texture beyond the volumetric representation's resolution limit.

## Results

On the four-view reconstruction benchmark (GSO, 332 meshes), MetaILRM achieves LPIPS 0.057, PSNR 22.49, Depth error 0.173, IoU 0.968, Chamfer Distance 1.137×10⁻², and Normal Correctness 0.885, outperforming all baselines across every metric. This represents a 40% reduction in LPIPS and 17% reduction in Chamfer Distance over the best concurrent method (InstantMesh). In the text-to-3D user study (11,080 responses, 404 prompts), AssetGen achieves 72–99% win rates in visual quality and 72–97% in text fidelity against all compared baselines at a ~30-second generation budget.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MetaILRM decisively outperforms all four academic few-view reconstruction baselines on GSO: it beats Instant3D-LRM by 54% in LPIPS (0.057 vs 0.124) and 30% in CD (1.137 vs 1.630), GRM by 43% in LPIPS and 24% in CD, and InstantMesh by 49% in LPIPS and 17% in CD. Against the industry baselines in the user study, AssetGen wins 96.7% of visual-quality votes vs GRM, 99.3% vs InstantMesh, 94.6% vs Meshy v3 (which supports PBR), and 72.3% vs Luma Genie 1.0 (also PBR-capable). The comparison against LightplaneLRM (the direct architectural base) shows a 66.6% user-study win rate, with the gap driven primarily by the SDF representation and texture refiner. MeshLRM [97] is compared only qualitatively (no open-source release), and the paper reports visually superior texture detail. Notably, AssetGen matches or exceeds the slower 'refinement' stages of Meshy v3 (300 s) and Luma Genie (600 s) in the extended user study (Table 4) while being 10–20× faster.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Instant3D two-stage paradigm (text-to-image grid generation followed by feed-forward 3D reconstruction) by upgrading the reconstruction model to output PBR materials and use an SDF representation. It builds directly on LightplaneLRM's memory-efficient triplane kernels, extending them with VolSDF rendering and a fused direct SDF loss in custom Triton kernels. The key conceptual departure is the 6-channel (shaded + albedo) image grid: the authors show that neither pure RGB input nor full PBR channel prediction in the text-to-image stage works well, and that this intermediate decomposition is the sweet spot for downstream material inference. A novel UV-space texture refiner with cross-view attention further distinguishes the pipeline from prior feed-forward reconstructors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first fast (≈30 s) text-to-3D system that outputs physically-based rendering materials (albedo, metalness, roughness) as spatially-varying maps, making generated assets directly usable in game and animation pipelines without manual material reassignment. The deferred shading loss is a practical training trick that makes PBR supervision tractable within a feed-forward setting, and the fused SDF kernels enable larger batches and higher-resolution photometric supervision that prior occupancy-based approaches could not support. Limitations include object-level scope only, no emissivity or ambient occlusion, SDF struggles with translucent or thin structures, and the triplane representation wastes capacity on empty space. Open questions include whether these techniques extend to scene-scale generation and whether more expressive representations (octrees, hash grids) can subsume the separate texture refiner.
