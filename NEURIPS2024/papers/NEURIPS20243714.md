## Problem

In standard LoRA finetuning, one adapter matrix is initialized to zero and the other to random values so that the product BA starts at zero. Prior work treated the two resulting schemes—Init[A] (A random, B zero) and Init[B] (B random, A zero)—as equivalent, yet no systematic study had examined whether the choice of which matrix to zero out affects training dynamics. This paper asks whether these seemingly symmetric initializations truly yield the same finetuning behavior and, if not, why.

## Input / Output

The analysis takes a pretrained model with LoRA adapters on selected weight matrices, a finetuning dataset, and standard LoRA hyperparameters (rank r, scaling α), with the critical input distinction being the initialization scheme (Init[A] vs. Init[B]) that determines the variance scaling of the randomly initialized matrix. The theoretical output is a closed-form characterization of stability conditions and optimal learning-rate scaling as a function of network width n, while the empirical output is task performance (accuracy, perplexity) across GLUE, MMLU, GSM8k, and WikiText-2 benchmarks using RoBERTa-Large, Llama-7b, and TinyLlama.

## Method

The paper applies the infinite-width limit framework using a γ-operator that tracks the polynomial exponent of each quantity in network width n. By deriving recursive formulas for the asymptotic behavior of LoRA features Z_A = A·Z and the B matrix, they show that Init[A] permits learning rates up to Θ(n^{-1/2}) before the LoRA output Z_B becomes unstable, whereas Init[B] is limited to Θ(n^{-1}). At the maximal stable rate, Init[A] achieves efficient feature learning (both linear update terms δ_t^1 and δ_t^2 are Θ(1)) but suffers 'internal instability' where Z_A grows as Θ(n^{1/2}), creating a feature-learning/stability tradeoff that pushes the practical optimal rate below Θ(n^{-1/2}). Init[B] avoids internal instability but is fundamentally suboptimal because B is undertrained (δ_t^2 → 0), making the limiting dynamics asymptotically equivalent to freezing B entirely. Empirical validation on a controlled toy model and LLMs confirms that Init[A] yields larger optimal learning rates and consistently better downstream performance.

## Results

On MNLI (the hardest GLUE task), Init[A] achieves 90.69% test accuracy versus 89.47% for Init[B] with RoBERTa-Large (LoRA rank 8), with optimal learning rates of 8e-5 and 1e-5 respectively. Across all evaluated benchmarks (GLUE, WikiText-2, MMLU, GSM8k), Init[A] consistently yields better performance and permits larger optimal learning rates than Init[B]. The theoretical analysis predicts maximal stable learning rates scaling as Θ(n^{-1/2}) for Init[A] versus Θ(n^{-1}) for Init[B], which is confirmed empirically: for large widths (n ≥ 2^9), the optimal LR with Init[A] exceeds that of Init[B].

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's central comparison is between two initialization schemes for vanilla LoRA: Init[A] (A random, B zero — the PEFT default) and Init[B] (B random, A zero). Init[A] wins on every benchmark tested: it achieves higher accuracy on MNLI (90.69% vs 89.47%), SST-2, and QNLI with RoBERTa-Large; lower perplexity on WikiText-2 with TinyLlama; and higher MMLU and GSM8k accuracy with Llama-7B. The performance gap is most pronounced on harder tasks (MNLI shows a ~1.2-point gap) and when the model width is large enough for the asymptotic learning-rate separation to manifest. On easier tasks (QNLI, MMLU) the optimal learning rates from grid search coincide for both initializations, yet Init[A] still edges out Init[B] in accuracy, suggesting the advantage is not solely driven by the learning-rate gap. No external LoRA variants (LoRA+, DoRA, PiSSA, etc.) are directly benchmarked; the paper positions its contribution as a zero-cost initialization choice within vanilla LoRA.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the infinite-width analysis framework developed in LoRA+ (Hayou et al. [44]) — specifically the γ-operator, Assumption 1, and the large-width scaling methodology — to a new question: how the choice of which LoRA matrix (A or B) is zero-initialized affects finetuning dynamics. While LoRA (Hu et al. [19]) established the default Init[A] scheme and Zhu et al. [50] empirically suggested the two schemes yield similar performance, no prior work provided a theoretical characterization of their difference. The paper also situates its findings within the broader μP / edge-of-stability literature on learning rate scaling, showing that the maximal stable learning rate differs by a factor of √n between the two initializations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical takeaway is a zero-cost recommendation: use Init[A] (the PEFT default) over Init[B], which is already the standard practice but now has theoretical justification. More broadly, the result reveals that 'equivalent' initializations (both yielding BA=0) can have fundamentally different asymptotic learning dynamics, a lesson that may generalize to other parameter-efficient methods. Key open questions include whether the suboptimal limiting behavior (internal instability with Init[A], undertrained B with Init[B]) can be resolved by methods like LoRA+, and how data-dependent constants in the Θ(·) bounds affect the learning-rate gap at practical widths.
