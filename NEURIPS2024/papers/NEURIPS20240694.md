## Problem

Existing adaptive extensions of the STORM variance reduction method either rely on strong assumptions (bounded gradients and bounded function values) or incur an extra O(log T) factor in the convergence rate. The paper asks whether an adaptive STORM method can achieve the optimal O(T^{-1/3}) rate for non-convex functions without these restrictive assumptions and without the logarithmic penalty.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes only noisy stochastic gradient samples ∇f(x;ξ) and an initial point x_1, requiring no prior knowledge of the smoothness constant L, gradient bound G, or variance bound σ. It outputs a randomly selected iterate x_τ with a guaranteed convergence rate of O(T^{-1/3}) for non-convex problems, O(T^{-1/3}) for compositional optimization, and O(n^{1/4}T^{-1/2}) for finite-sum problems. A doubling trick eliminates the need to specify T in advance by running the algorithm in geometrically growing stages.

## Method

Ada-STORM retains the STORM momentum-based variance reduction estimator but introduces a novel adaptive learning rate η_t = min{1/T^{1/3}, 1/(T^{(1-α)/3}(Σ_{i≤t}||v_i||²)^α)} with a fixed momentum parameter β = T^{-2/3}, where 0 < α < 1/3. The key insight is that the learning rate starts at a small value proportional to T^{-1/3} and then shrinks adaptively based on the cumulative norm of past gradient estimators, ensuring stability in early iterations without requiring knowledge of L or G. For the finite-sum variant, a SAG-inspired correction term (g_t^{i_t} − (1/n)Σg_t^i) is added to the STORM estimator to exploit the finite-sum structure, achieving O(n^{1/4}T^{-1/2}) without the O(log(nT)) factor present in prior adaptive methods. The compositional extension maintains separate STORM-style estimators for the inner function g(x) and the composed gradient, applying the same adaptive learning rate schedule.

## Results

The paper evaluates Ada-STORM on CIFAR-10 (ResNet-18), CIFAR-100 (ResNet-34), and WikiText-2 (2-layer Transformer), comparing against SGD, Adam, AdaBelief, STORM, STORM+, and META-STORM. On image classification tasks, Ada-STORM achieves comparable training loss and accuracy to other methods but outperforms all baselines in testing loss, yielding better testing accuracy. On the WikiText-2 language modeling task, Ada-STORM converges more quickly than all other methods and attains a slightly better perplexity. Theoretically, Ada-STORM achieves the optimal O(T^{-1/3}) convergence rate without the O(log T) factor and without requiring bounded gradient or bounded function value assumptions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Ada-STORM outperforms STORM [Cutkosky & Orabona, 2019] both theoretically (eliminating the O(log T) factor) and empirically (better testing loss/accuracy on CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100). Against STORM+ [Levy et al., 2021], Ada-STORM matches the optimal O(T^{-1/3}) rate but removes the bounded gradient and bounded function value assumptions that STORM+ requires, while also achieving better generalization (lower testing loss). Compared to META-STORM [Liu et al., 2022], Ada-STORM eliminates the extra O(log T) term in the convergence rate and drops the bounded gradient assumption entirely. Against standard adaptive optimizers Adam [Kingma & Ba, 2015] and AdaBelief [Zhuang et al., 2020], Ada-STORM shows comparable training performance but superior testing accuracy on image tasks and faster convergence with lower perplexity on WikiText-2, suggesting that the variance-reduction mechanism provides a meaningful generalization benefit over pure adaptive step-size methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the STORM momentum-based variance reduction framework (Cutkosky and Orabona, 2019) by designing a novel adaptive learning rate that eliminates the need for bounded gradients and bounded function values assumptions required by prior adaptive variants (STORM+, META-STORM). For the finite-sum setting, it incorporates SAG-style gradient tracking (Roux et al., 2012) into the STORM estimator, and offers an SVRG-style (Zhang et al., 2013) alternative to avoid storing past gradients. The key departure from prior work is the two-phase learning rate schedule—initially T^{-1/3}, then adapting to cumulative gradient norms—which simultaneously removes the O(log T) factor and relaxes the assumption set.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By achieving the optimal O(T^{-1/3}) rate under only average smoothness, bounded variance, and bounded initial gap, Ada-STORM removes practical barriers that made prior adaptive STORM methods inapplicable to problems with unbounded gradients or function values. The extension to compositional optimization and finite-sum problems demonstrates the learning rate design's generality, and the authors note that STORM-based techniques are already deployed in bi-level optimization, federated learning, and min-max optimization, suggesting the adaptive variant could transfer to those settings. Open questions include whether the doubling trick's constant-factor overhead matters in practice and whether the approach extends to constrained or multi-level compositional problems.
