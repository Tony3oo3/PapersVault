## Problem

Time series diffusion models typically adopt noise schedules designed for other domains (e.g., the fixed linear schedule in TSDiff) without accounting for the non-stationarity characteristics of the specific TS dataset, leading to suboptimal corruption dynamics where data is either destroyed too abruptly or too gradually. This paper asks: can we automatically select a noise schedule that is well-matched to the statistical structure of a given TS dataset, without requiring additional training or architectural changes?

## Input / Output

ANT takes a TS dataset (a collection of univariate or multivariate series) and a finite set of candidate schedules parameterized by noise function (linear, cosine, sigmoid), temperature τ, and total steps T (35 candidates in experiments). It simulates the forward diffusion process for each candidate, computes the integrated absolute autocorrelation time (IAAT) at every step, and outputs a single selected schedule. The selected schedule is then plugged into an existing TS diffusion model (TSDiff) for downstream tasks—probabilistic forecasting (CRPS), refinement of base-forecaster outputs, or synthetic TS generation—without any modification to the model architecture or training procedure.

## Method

ANT scores each candidate schedule by measuring how closely its non-stationarity curve (IAAT vs. step fraction) follows an ideal linear decrease from the initial data to pure noise, using the area-under-the-curve (AUC) discrepancy as the primary term λ_linear. Two auxiliary multiplicative terms enforce additional desiderata: λ_noise penalizes schedules that do not fully collapse the data to random noise by the final step, and λ_step mildly favors larger T. The schedule with the lowest composite ANT score is selected, and this entire procedure is computed offline in seconds (negligible relative to training). A secondary finding is that diffusion step embedding (DE) can be removed when a linear schedule is chosen, since step information is inherently encoded in the data's noise level; conversely, non-linear schedules are more robust to variations in T in both performance and the optimal self-guidance scale parameter s.

## Results

On the primary TS forecasting task (CRPS, lower is better), TSDiff+ANT achieves the best performance across all eight datasets, with an average CRPS of 0.150 compared to TSDiff's 0.166 (a 9.5% average improvement). The largest gains are on M4 (+27.8%, 0.026 vs 0.036) and Exchange (+25.1%, 0.009 vs 0.012), while the smallest is on KDDCup (+3.0%, 0.325 vs 0.335). ANT also outperforms fixed cosine (0.160) and zero-SNR (0.185) schedules from the computer vision literature, confirming the benefit of dataset-adaptive schedule selection.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TSDiff+ANT outperforms all six forecasting baselines (DeepAR, DeepState, Transformer, TFT, CSDI, TSDiff) on every one of the eight datasets in terms of CRPS. Against the direct baseline TSDiff (which uses a fixed Lin(100) schedule), ANT yields gains ranging from +3.0% (KDDCup) to +27.8% (M4), with an average improvement of 9.5%. Compared to non-adaptive schedules from the CV literature, ANT (avg CRPS 0.150) beats the cosine schedule of Nichol & Dhariwal [24] (0.160) and the zero-terminal-SNR schedule of Lin et al. [21] (0.185), demonstrating that a one-size-fits-all schedule is suboptimal for TS data. In the generation task, TSDiff+ANT also surpasses TimeVAE and TimeGAN as a synthetic data generator, improving downstream forecaster CRPS by up to 55.2% (Exchange with DeepAR). The only case where ANT does not match the oracle schedule is Traffic, where it selects Lin(50) instead of Cos(75,2.0), but the performance gap is negligible (0.101 vs 0.099).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on TSDiff (Kollovieh et al., NeurIPS 2023), the state-of-the-art unconditional time series diffusion model with self-guidance, by replacing its fixed linear noise schedule with an adaptively selected one. It departs from prior noise schedule design work in computer vision (cosine schedules from Nichol & Dhariwal, zero-SNR schedules from Lin et al.) by grounding schedule selection in time series-specific statistics of non-stationarity (autocorrelation-based measures like IAAT) rather than handcrafting schedules per dataset. The broader DDPM framework and the observation that noise schedules are a critical yet underexplored component (Chen, 2023) motivate the work, while the paper also repurposes classical time series diagnostics (integrated autocorrelation time) as a principled criterion for diffusion model configuration.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ANT provides a practical, training-free procedure for selecting noise schedules that eliminates expensive per-dataset hyperparameter search, with statistics computable offline in negligible time. The finding that diffusion step embeddings are unnecessary under linear schedules and that non-linear schedules are more robust to the number of steps T offers actionable simplifications for practitioners. However, the method is limited to selecting from a finite candidate set (35 schedules) rather than deriving an optimal schedule analytically, and experiments are primarily on univariate TS with a single backbone (TSDiff), leaving open whether the criterion generalizes to multivariate settings, conditional models, or other diffusion architectures. The broader implication is that domain-specific data statistics can inform architectural and training choices in generative models, a direction the authors explicitly encourage.
