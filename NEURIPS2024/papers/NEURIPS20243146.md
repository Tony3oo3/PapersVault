## Problem

Existing differentiable neural architecture search (DNAS) methods for generating logic circuits from input-output examples suffer from three fundamental issues: overfitting to skip-connections that waste network capacity, a structural mismatch between rectangular search architectures and the inherent triangle-shaped topology of real circuits, and severe learning imbalance across input-output examples of varying difficulty. These challenges cause DNAS to fail at generating exact circuits at scale (up to 1200 AIG nodes) and exhibit high sensitivity to hyperparameters such as random initialization.

## Input / Output

The method takes a complete truth table (all 2^K input-output pairs for K input bits) as training data and produces a functionally exact And-Inverter Graph (AIG) circuit, where each node is a two-input NAND gate and edges represent wires. To handle the exponential growth of truth tables with input width, the method applies recursive Shannon decomposition to partition the table into sub-tables that are merged into a multi-output format, halving input bits while doubling outputs. The final circuit is further optimized via an evolutionary algorithm with RL-guided operator sequencing, with quality measured by AIG node count (area proxy) and depth (delay proxy).

## Method

T-Net combines three key mechanisms: (1) a triangle-shaped network architecture with a wide, shallow bottom block and a narrow, deep upper block that mirrors the inherent node distribution of synthesized circuits, reducing redundant search space in deep layers; (2) a regularized skip-connection loss that applies a non-learnable depth-dependent weight vector to the Gumbel-softmax connection probabilities, softly suppressing long-range skip connections while preserving expressive power; and (3) a Boolean hardness-aware loss that multiplies per-sample MSE by an exponential weight on prediction error (focal-loss-inspired) and a global wrong-rate factor to maintain training signal on hard examples. For circuit optimization, T-Net feeds diverse generated circuits into an evolutionary algorithm where a reinforcement learning agent selects sequences of nine ABC logic operators, with a restart strategy that reinitializes agent parameters while retaining encoder weights to escape local optima.

## Results

On 18 circuits across four benchmarks (Espresso, LogicNets, Random, Arithmetic), T-Net achieves 99.99% average generation accuracy with only 1.89 average wrong bits, compared to 82.51% (Basic DNAS), 91.99% (DNAS Skip), and 86.31% (Darts-). In terms of final optimized circuit size, T-Net produces an average of 83.33 nodes, a 68.70% reduction over the traditional SOP+resyn2 baseline (380.29 nodes), a 25.78% improvement over the 2022 IWLS winner EPFL (112.28 nodes), and a 5.36% improvement over the 2023 IWLS winner Google DeepMind (88.06 nodes). T-Net also generates significantly smaller initial circuits (262.78 avg nodes vs. 459.39 for SOP, a 33.42% reduction), which translates to better post-optimization results (230.06 vs. 366.39 optimized nodes).

## Baselines & Comparisons

T-Net decisively outperforms all DNAS-based generation baselines: it achieves 99.99% average accuracy versus 82.51% for Basic DNAS (Petersen et al., NeurIPS 2022), 91.99% for DNAS Skip (Belcak et al., MATH-AI@NeurIPS 2022 / Google DeepMind IWLS 2023), and 86.31% for Darts- (Chu et al., 2020), with wrong bits reduced from thousands to under 2 on average. Against the traditional SOP+resyn2 pipeline (Minato, 1992), T-Net's end-to-end synthesis achieves a 68.70% reduction in final optimized node count (83.33 vs. 380.29 average). Compared to the IWLS 2022 champion EPFL team (Grosnit et al., DATE 2022, using Bayesian optimization within an EA framework), T-Net improves by 25.78% in average optimized nodes. Against the IWLS 2023 champion Google DeepMind (using DNAS generation + three weeks of optimization), T-Net still achieves a 5.36% improvement, demonstrating that the triangle-shaped architecture, regularized skip-connections, and hardness-aware loss collectively yield a more compact and accurate circuit than the prior state-of-the-art.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the DNAS-based circuit generation line initiated by Petersen et al. [6] and Belcak & Wattenhofer [5], which Google DeepMind extended to win IWLS 2023 [7]. Rather than proposing a fundamentally new search paradigm, the paper's core contribution is a systematic empirical diagnosis of why existing DNAS methods fail (skip-connection overfitting, rectangular-vs-triangular structure mismatch, imbalanced sample difficulty) and targeted architectural/loss modifications (triangle-shaped two-block network, weighted layer regularization, Boolean hardness-aware loss) to address each failure mode. The optimization stage combines RL-based operator sequence search [39] with an evolutionary algorithm framework, extending the Bayesian-optimization-based EA used by the 2022 IWLS champion (EPFL) [30] with a restart strategy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that DNAS can achieve 100% functional accuracy on circuits up to 1200 AIG nodes, a scale where prior DNAS methods failed on 14 of 16 test circuits, and produces circuits 68% smaller than traditional SOP+resyn2 baselines. The Shannon decomposition trick for reducing input bit-width while doubling outputs is a practical scalability enabler that could generalize to other truth-table-based synthesis settings. Open questions include whether the approach extends to sequential circuits, timing-constrained synthesis, and circuits beyond ~1200 nodes; the legalization step for near-miss circuits also hints at residual fragility in the generation process. The triangle-shaped architecture insight—that circuits are naturally wider at the input layers—may inform future differentiable search designs beyond logic synthesis.
