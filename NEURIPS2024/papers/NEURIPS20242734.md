## Problem

Existing face forgery detectors rely on spatial artifacts or short-range temporal features, making them brittle to common perturbations (compression, noise) and unseen manipulation techniques. The paper asks whether semantically rich, long-range audio-visual speech representations—learned entirely on real videos—can expose the lip-movement inconsistencies that local lip-sync methods like Wav2Lip fail to eliminate.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242734/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The detector consumes 96×96 mouth-region crops at 25 fps (aligned via RetinaFace + FAN landmarks) and 16 kHz audio converted to MFCC features with 4-frame concatenation to match the visual frame rate. It outputs a single video-level matching score: the mean frame-wise cosine similarity between visual-only and audio-only speech embeddings, optionally corrected for time offset via a sliding window (fixed offset) or DTW (dynamic offset). Videos with low scores are flagged as forgeries; no classifier head or fine-tuning step is required.

## Method

SpeechForensics is a two-stage, training-free-at-detection pipeline. Stage 1 self-supervisedly learns unified audio-visual speech representations on real videos (LRS3 + VoxCeleb2) using an AVHuBERT-style architecture: a 3D-conv ResNet-18 visual frontend and a linear audio frontend feed a 24-block transformer encoder, trained with (a) frame-wise k-means cluster alignment for local phonetic/viseme information and (b) a masked-prediction objective over both modalities for global long-range temporal dependencies. Stage 2 directly transfers the frozen model to forgery detection by feeding lip-region frames and audio separately (modality dropout enables unimodal inference) and measuring the cosine similarity between the resulting visual and audio speech embeddings. The critical design choice is that global masked prediction captures long-range temporal coherence that local lip-sync forgeries (e.g., Wav2Lip) cannot replicate, while the unsupervised, semantic-level learning avoids overfitting to low-level forgery artifacts, yielding strong cross-dataset generalization and perturbation robustness without any fake video in training.

## Results

On the cross-dataset generalization benchmark, SpeechForensics achieves 99.0% AUC on FakeAVCeleb and 91.7% AUC on KoDF, outperforming the best supervised method RealForensics by 8.8% and 7.4% respectively, all without using any fake videos in training. On FF++ cross-manipulation, it reaches 97.6% average AUC, matching or exceeding all supervised baselines. Under seven types of perturbations, it maintains 94.1% average AUC with only a 3.5-point drop from clean performance, compared to 7.6–21.6-point drops for supervised baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SpeechForensics outperforms all ten baselines on the cross-dataset generalization task. Against the strongest supervised method RealForensics [29] (90.2% on FakeAVCeleb, 84.3% on KoDF), it gains 8.8 and 7.4 points respectively. FTCN [71] and LipForensics [30], which model temporal features, score 82.3% and 89.8% on FakeAVCeleb but drop sharply on KoDF (76.5% and 59.6%), while SpeechForensics maintains 91.7%. Image-based methods Face X-ray [43] (78.4%) and Patch-based [12] (83.6%) are further behind, and Xception [55] (61.6%) performs near chance on FakeAVCeleb. Among unsupervised methods, AVAD [24] reaches 85.0% on FakeAVCeleb but collapses to 58.0% on KoDF, whereas SpeechForensics stays above 91% on both. The multimodal baselines MDS [15] (76.7%), VFD [14] (82.5%), and Avoid-DF [65] (85.8%) on FakeAVCeleb are all substantially outperformed by the 99.0% achieved by SpeechForensics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes audio-visual speech representation learning (specifically the AVHuBERT masked-prediction framework) as a face forgery detection mechanism, departing from the dominant paradigm of detecting low-level spatial artifacts (blending boundaries, frequency anomalies) or short-range temporal inconsistencies. Prior temporal methods like FTCN and RealForensics model only ~1-second windows of facial motion, while this work simultaneously captures local phonetic alignment and global linguistic coherence through a unified transformer encoder. The key conceptual departure is that forgery detection is framed as an audio-visual speech consistency problem rather than an artifact-detection problem, enabling a fully unsupervised pipeline that requires no fake video samples during training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method's ability to achieve 99.0% AUC on FakeAVCeleb and 100% on Wav2Lip forgeries without any fake training data, while remaining robust to common perturbations (only 3.5% average drop vs. 7.6–21.6% for supervised baselines), offers a practical path toward deployment in real-world settings where new manipulation techniques emerge continuously. A critical open question is whether the method generalizes to forgeries that do not alter the mouth region, and whether the reliance on clean audio (no background music, no silent segments) limits real-world applicability. The finding that local lip synchronization (Wav2Lip) is completely undetectable by phonetic-level features but trivially detectable through global semantic analysis suggests that future forgery generators will need to maintain long-range linguistic coherence, raising the bar for adversarial attacks.
