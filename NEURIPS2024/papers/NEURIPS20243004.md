## Problem

Online composite optimization has been studied separately under purely stochastic (i.i.d.) and purely adversarial environments, but real-world settings typically fall in between. The Stochastically Extended Adversarial (SEA) model bridges this gap for non-composite optimization, yet extending it to the composite setting is non-trivial: standard optimistic methods rely on smoothness of the full loss, which is destroyed by the non-smooth regularizer r(·), and naively treating f_t + r as one function forfeits the structural benefits (e.g., sparsity) that the regularizer provides.

## Input / Output

At each round t, the learner receives a data point (x_t, y_t) sampled from a distribution D_t that may be stochastic, adversarial, or intermediate, observes the gradient ∇f_t(x_t) of the smooth time-varying component, and has access to a fixed non-smooth convex regularizer r(·) (e.g., ℓ1-norm) from the first round. The algorithm outputs a decision vector x_t ∈ X ⊆ R^d and, crucially, the theoretical guarantees are parameterized by two environment-dependent quantities—cumulative stochastic variance σ²_{1:T} and cumulative adversarial variation Σ²_{1:T}—rather than a fixed horizon T, so the bounds tighten automatically when the environment is more benign.

## Method

The paper reanalyzes Optimistic Composite Mirror Descent (OptCMD) with tailored configurations for three function classes: for general convex F_t, a time-varying step size η_t = min{D/√(1+V̄_{t-1}), D/δ} with optimism M_{t+1} = ∇f_t(x_t) yields O(√σ²_{1:T} + √Σ²_{1:T}); for strongly convex F_t, η_t = 2/(δ+λt) gives O((σ²_max+Σ²_max) log(σ²_{1:T}+Σ²_{1:T})); and for exp-concave f_t, a matrix-valued regularizer H_t = δI + (βG²/2)I + (β/2)Σh_s yields O((d/α) log(σ²_{1:T}+Σ²_{1:T})). The key mechanism is keeping r(·) non-linearized inside the proximal subproblem, which preserves regularizer-induced structure (sparsity, low-rankness) while the optimistic gradient prediction exploits smoothness of f_t alone. For the unknown-function-type setting, a two-layer MsMwC meta-algorithm tracks O(log T) OptCMD experts, with the critical design choice of explicitly inserting r(·) into both the expert losses l_t and optimisms m_t so that the non-smooth component cancels in the meta-regret analysis, enabling the meta-layer to leverage smoothness of f_t without requiring smoothness of the composite loss.

## Results

The paper's primary contributions are theoretical regret bounds for the composite SEA model: O(√(σ²₁:ₜ) + √(Σ²₁:ₜ)) for general convex, O((σ²_max + Σ²_max) log(σ²₁:ₜ + Σ²₁:ₜ)) for strongly convex, and O(d log(σ²₁:ₜ + Σ²₁:ₜ)) for exp-concave time-varying functions. Empirically, on the Mushroom dataset (d=112, D=20, 10% adversarial label corruption), both OptCMD and the proposed universal algorithm USC-SEA achieve lower instantaneous, cumulative, and average losses than all baselines across the three function types. The universal algorithm USC-SEA matches OptCMD's performance without requiring prior knowledge of the function type or curvature parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against five baselines across three function-type settings. For the general convex and strongly convex cases, OGD (Zinkevich, 2003) and COMID (Duchi et al., 2010) serve as standard composite optimization baselines, while Optimistic-OMD (Chen et al., 2023) represents the best non-composite SEA method. For the exp-concave case, ONS (Hazan et al., 2007) and ProxONS (Yang et al., 2024) are the relevant baselines. Across all three settings, OptCMD and USC-SEA consistently achieve lower cumulative and average losses than all baselines, with the advantage being most pronounced under adversarial corruption (10% label flips). The key distinction is that Optimistic-OMD, ONS, and ProxONS do not explicitly handle the non-smooth regularizer r(·), whereas OptCMD and USC-SEA maintain the composite structure throughout optimization, yielding sparser decisions and better adaptation to the intermediate stochastic-adversarial environment.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Stochastically Extended Adversarial (SEA) model—originally developed for non-composite online optimization by Sachs et al. (2022) and Chen et al. (2023)—into the composite setting with a fixed non-smooth regularizer, and reanalyzes Optimistic Composite Mirror Descent (OptCMD) from Scroccaro et al. (2023) under this intermediate stochastic-adversarial spectrum. For the universal algorithm, it adapts the two-layer MsMwC meta-expert framework of Yan et al. (2023) by explicitly incorporating the regularizer into both losses and optimisms, overcoming the smoothness requirement that prevented prior universal SEA methods from handling composite losses. The key departure from prior work is showing that the non-smooth regularizer can be disentangled from the time-varying smooth component during optimization without degrading the regret guarantees.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central finding—that composite SEA regret bounds match those of the non-composite SEA model exactly, meaning the regularizer imposes 'no price'—is a clean theoretical guarantee that practitioners can rely on when adding sparsity or low-rank structure to online learning in mixed environments. The universal algorithm removes the need to know the function type (convex, strongly convex, exp-concave) in advance, which is critical for practical deployment. Open questions include extending to unbounded domains and gradients, handling delayed feedback, and simplifying the three-layer universal structure to two layers, all of which the authors identify as future directions.
