## Problem

Estimating conditional distributions from joint data is fundamental to uncertainty quantification, yet all four existing strategies—non-parametric density estimation, localization, direct kernel-based learning, and conditional training—suffer from the curse of dimensionality, require retraining whenever the conditioning changes, or demand separate models per conditioning point. No prior approach learns a single model from which all conditional statistics (density, quantiles, moments, confidence regions) can be extracted analytically without retraining or Monte Carlo sampling.

## Input / Output

The method is trained once on paired observations (x_i, y_i) from the joint distribution, consuming two independent random batches per epoch to obtain unbiased estimates of the loss. At inference, any conditioning value x or set A and any test function f or set B can be queried against the trained operator to produce conditional expectations, CDFs, quantiles, probabilities, and confidence intervals—all computed analytically without retraining, resampling, or Monte Carlo integration. The output is structured as a finite-rank operator in a d-dimensional latent space, from which statistics are derived by applying the operator to indicator functions or moment functions.

## Method

NCP parameterizes the truncated SVD of the conditional expectation operator E_{Y|X} using two neural networks u^θ: X→R^d and v^θ: Y→R^d for the left and right singular functions, plus a vector w^θ whose Gaussian-transformed entries (exp(−w_i²)) give the singular values. The training loss combines a correlation-based term—equivalent to solving the Eckart-Young-Mirsky finite-rank approximation problem for the deflated operator—with a regularization penalty enforcing orthonormality of the learned features in L² spaces, ensuring the latent metric aligns with the data-space metric. Training proceeds in a single unconditional phase using two independent batches per epoch for unbiased estimation, followed by an optional whitening post-processing step that further improves statistical accuracy. The key distinction from prior methods is that NCP learns the operator mapping L²(Y)→L²(X) rather than the conditional density itself, so all conditional statistics are extracted by applying the trained operator to appropriate test functions without any retraining, per-conditioning model, or sampling.

## Results

On the conditional density estimation benchmark (Table 1, n=10⁵, 10 repetitions), NCP with whitening (NCP-W) achieves the lowest Kolmogorov-Smirnov distance on 4 of 6 datasets: 0.005 (EconDensity), 0.010 (ArmaJump), 0.008 (SkewNormal), and 0.015 (GaussianMixture), while tying FlexCode at 0.047 on LGGMD and ranking a close second behind Normalizing Flows (0.010 vs 0.008) on LinearGaussian. For conditional confidence intervals on Laplace and Cauchy distributions, NCP achieves 88% empirical coverage (target 90%) with stable intervals, whereas Conditional Conformal Prediction (CCP) achieves only 79% coverage and Normalizing Flows produce degenerate regions near x=0. In high-dimensional experiments (d up to 1000), NCP scales with only a 20% increase in computation time from d=100 to d=1000 while maintaining strong statistical performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NCP-W (a 2-hidden-layer MLP with GELU activations) outperforms all 10 baselines on 4 of 6 CDE datasets, with particularly large margins over diffusion models (DDPM: 0.010 vs 0.410 on LinearGaussian), kernel mixture networks (KMN: 0.010 vs 0.601), and random forest CDE (RFCDE: 0.010 vs 0.128). Against Normalizing Flows (NF), NCP-W wins decisively on ArmaJump (0.010 vs 0.143), SkewNormal (0.008 vs 0.032), and GaussianMixture (0.015 vs 0.107), but NF edges out NCP-W on LinearGaussian (0.008 vs 0.010) and LGGMD (0.254 vs 0.047—actually NCP-W wins here too; NF's 0.254 is much worse). Against the strongest non-generative baselines, NCP-W beats FlexCode (FC) on 5/6 datasets and ties on LGGMD (both 0.047), while outperforming CKDE and NNKCDE by 2–4× on most datasets. For confidence regions, NCP produces stable intervals with 88% coverage (target 90%), while CCP achieves only 79% coverage and NF confidence regions explode near degenerate conditioning points (x=0); on the heavy-tailed Cauchy distribution, both NF and CCP collapse entirely while NCP still yields reasonable estimates. In high-dimensional settings (d=100–1000), NCP matches NF on Gaussian conditionals and outperforms it on discrete conditionals, with only a 20% runtime increase from d=100 to d=1000.

## Relation to Prior Work

NCP departs from all four strategies for conditional distribution learning catalogued by Gao and Hastie (2022) — non-parametric density estimation, localization, direct dictionary/kernel learning, and conditional training — by instead learning the conditional expectation operator E_{Y|X} as a compact operator between Hilbert spaces. The method is directly built on the operator-theoretic framework connecting conditional expectation to Deep CCA (Andrew et al., 2013; Kostic et al., 2024) and extends the finite-dimensional regularized CCA results of Wells et al. (2024) to infinite-dimensional settings with a new loss that enforces orthonormality of the learned singular functions. The parameterization of the truncated SVD via neural networks and the resulting separable density form p_θ(x,y) = 1 + ⟨σ^θ ⊙ u^θ(x), v^θ(y)⟩ constitute the core architectural novelty.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical advantage is that a single unconditional training phase yields a model from which all conditional statistics — density, mean, covariance, CDF, quantiles, and confidence regions — can be extracted analytically without retraining or Monte Carlo sampling, addressing a major bottleneck in conditional generative models. The non-asymptotic statistical guarantees (Theorem 2, Corollary 1) provide rare theoretical grounding for conditional probability estimation with neural operators. However, the authors note that NCP requires relatively large sample sizes (n ≳ 10⁴) to outperform competitors, and future work on incorporating prior knowledge for data efficiency, multi-dimensional time series, and causal/sensitivity analysis remains open.
