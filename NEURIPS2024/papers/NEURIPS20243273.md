## Problem

Painting-specific aesthetic datasets are severely underdeveloped relative to photography: the painting domain has only ~60,000 images for basic IAQA tasks versus 685,000+ for photography, and no publicly available painting dataset exists for aesthetic captioning. Existing painting datasets (BAID, AACP, VAPS, JenAesthetics) are limited in scale, scoring dimensions, and annotation richness, constraining the development of automatic aesthetic evaluation for artistic images. APDDv2 addresses this gap by providing the first painting dataset exceeding 10,000 images with 24 artistic categories, 10 aesthetic attributes, and detailed language comments from 37 domain experts.

## Input / Output

The model accepts a single artistic image (painting or drawing) and outputs a total aesthetic score on a 0–100 scale plus 10 individual attribute scores on a 0–10 scale (Theme & Logic, Creativity, Layout & Composition, Space & Perspective, Sense of Order, Light & Shadow, Color, Details & Texture, The Overall, Mood). The total score is not a linear aggregation of attribute scores but reflects holistic artistic perception. The dataset additionally provides 6,249 free-text aesthetic comments, enabling multimodal training and downstream sentiment analysis.

## Method

ArtCLIP builds on CLIP's image encoder and is pre-trained on the DPC2022 photography captioning dataset using a multi-attribute contrastive learning scheme: comments are categorized into five aesthetic attributes, and a multimodal fusion module merges image and text embeddings so that attribute-aligned pairs are pulled together while mismatched pairs are pushed apart. For painting aesthetic evaluation, the pre-trained ArtCLIP is fine-tuned on APDDv2, where the image encoder extracts feature vectors that are fed into an MLP regression head to predict both the total aesthetic score and all 10 attribute scores simultaneously. The approach adapts AesCLIP's contrastive framework to the painting domain, leveraging APDDv2's rich attribute-structured annotations and language comments to learn style-specific aesthetic representations. Training uses a batch size of 80 over 20 epochs (lr 1e-4, linear decay) for pre-training and a batch size of 64 over 10 fine-tuning iterations (lr 5e-5) on a single RTX 4090 GPU.

## Results

On the APDDv2 dataset, ArtCLIP achieves a SROCC of 0.99 on the Total Aesthetic Score (TAS), substantially outperforming AANSPS (0.88) and SAAN (0.79). Across the 10 individual aesthetic attributes, ArtCLIP consistently attains the highest SROCC, ranging from 0.78 (Creativity) to 0.91 (Space & Perspective, Light & Shadow, Color). The evaluation protocol reports SROCC, PLCC, and ACC for each of the 11 score types (TAS + 10 attributes), with all experiments conducted on a single NVIDIA RTX 4090 GPU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ArtCLIP is compared against two baselines trained on the same APDDv2 dataset: AANSPS (Jin et al., IJCAI 2024), which uses EfficientNet-B4 with ECA modules, and SAAN (Yi et al., CVPR 2023), which employs self-supervised learning with adaptive instance normalization. On the headline Total Aesthetic Score (TAS), ArtCLIP achieves SROCC 0.99, surpassing AANSPS (0.88) by 0.11 and SAAN (0.79) by 0.20. Across the 10 individual aesthetic attributes, ArtCLIP attains the highest SROCC in 9 out of 10 cases, with the sole exception being Details & Texture where AANSPS edges ahead (0.90 vs. 0.82). SAAN shows competitive SROCC on several attributes (e.g., 0.91 on Space & Perspective and Color) but is consistently outperformed by ArtCLIP on the overall score. The paper notes that ArtCLIP's advantage stems from its multi-attribute contrastive pre-training on the DPC2022 dataset followed by fine-tuning on APDDv2, whereas the baselines are trained end-to-end on APDDv2 alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the authors' own APDDv1 (IJCAI 2024) by roughly doubling the image count to 10,023 and introducing aesthetic language comments, while also proposing ArtCLIP as a model upgrade over their prior AANSPS architecture. For the model, ArtCLIP adapts the multi-attribute contrastive learning framework of AesCLIP (Sheng et al., 2023)—originally designed for photographic aesthetics—to the painting domain, pre-training on the DPC2022 photography captioning dataset before fine-tuning on APDDv2. Conceptually, the work carves out a niche that prior painting datasets (BAID, AACP, VAPS, JenAesthetics, MART) left unaddressed: none combined 24 artistic categories, 10 fine-grained aesthetic attributes, and free-text expert commentary in a single resource.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper quantifies a stark data imbalance—over 685K photographic images versus ~60K painting images for aesthetic scoring, and 198K+ versus zero for aesthetic captions—making APDDv2 the first painting-domain resource to partially close the captioning gap. However, the model evaluation table contains apparent numerical inconsistencies (e.g., PLCC values exceeding 1.0 for SAAN), and the pre-training relies on a photography captioning dataset (DPC2022) due to the absence of a painting-specific commentary corpus, limiting confidence in the claimed SOTA. Open questions include whether the expert-only annotation protocol generalizes to public aesthetic perception, how cultural homogeneity of the annotator team (all Chinese institutions) affects cross-cultural applicability, and whether the 10-attribute taxonomy transfers to non-Western or emerging art forms.
