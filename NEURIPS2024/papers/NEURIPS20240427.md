## Problem

Most LLM-based dialogue systems operate in half-duplex (turn-based) mode, where each party must finish speaking before the other responds, resulting in high latency and an inability to naturally interrupt or be interrupted. Full-duplex dialogue—where both parties can simultaneously speak and listen—is essential for human-like conversation but is nontrivial with LLMs, which lack awareness of perception/motor functions and do not support input during generation.

## Input / Output

The system consumes user speech as audio, processed by a streaming ASR model (U2++ Conformer) in 640 ms chunks that are appended as text tokens to the LLM's generation tape in real time. The LLM produces two types of output: textual tokens streamed to a TTS module (XTTS-v2) for voice synthesis, and special control tokens ([S.SPEAK], [C.SPEAK], [S.LISTEN], [C.LISTEN]) that signal state transitions in a two-state neural FSM governing who speaks. The entire dialogue is serialized onto a single causal token tape, so external user input can be injected mid-generation without breaking the autoregressive process.

## Method

The core contribution is a 'neural FSM' with two states (SPEAK and LISTEN) and four control tokens, whose operation is unified into the LLM's standard next-token prediction. The LLM (Llama-3-8B-Instruct) is instruction-tuned on 1,500 GPT-4-generated dialogue transcripts annotated with control tokens at appropriate interruption, concession, and continuation points, using 20 steps of supervised fine-tuning (batch 256, lr 1e-5, AdamW, 8×A100). At inference, the LLM operates on a virtual one-directional token tape where each decoding step is triggered by one of three events in priority order: a control token, a new ASR chunk, or TTS completion of the previous token. The perception (streaming ASR) and motor (streaming TTS) modules run continuously and in parallel, observing the FSM state to decide whether to forward audio chunks or produce voice, requiring no external moderation or synchronization logic. This design reduces average first-token emission delay from 2.28 s (half-duplex baseline) to 0.68 s while achieving 54.7% interruption precision, outperforming GPT-4o and GPT-3.5-turbo-0125.

## Results

The proposed full-duplex system (Configuration 4: streaming ASR + duplex LLM + streaming TTS) achieves an average first-token emission delay (FTED) of 0.68s, reducing latency by more than 3× compared to the half-duplex baseline (2.28s), with 50% of interactions responding within 0.41s. On the duplex-dialogue-3k benchmark, Llama-3-8B-Instruct-fd achieves a machine-interruption precision of 54.7% and recall of 62.8%, outperforming GPT-4o (46.6% / 47.2%) and GPT-3.5-turbo-0125 (24.7% / 25.7%). For user-initiated interruptions, the model attains a 96.7% average proper response rate (PRR), slightly exceeding GPT-4o (96.1%) and substantially surpassing GPT-3.5-turbo-0125 (84.4%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares Llama-3-8B-Instruct-fd against three baselines: GPT-4o, GPT-3.5-turbo-0125, and the unmodified Llama-3-8B-Instruct. On machine-initiated interruption (the core full-duplex capability), the proposed model achieves 54.7% precision and 62.8% recall, beating GPT-4o by 8.1 and 15.6 percentage points respectively, and GPT-3.5-turbo-0125 by 30 and 37 points. GPT-4o and GPT-3.5-turbo-0125 adopt a more conservative strategy (interrupting mid-sentence only 17.5% and 11.3% of the time), which yields higher per-interruption accuracy (PIR_mid 95.6% and 91.1%) but at the cost of much lower recall and higher latency (1.48s vs 0.83s when not interrupting). The unmodified Llama-3-8B-Instruct over-interrupts (75.1% mid-sentence) but with only 36.1% precision, confirming that the full-duplex fine-tuning is essential for balanced behavior. On user-initiated interruptions, Llama-3-8B-Instruct-fd edges out GPT-4o (96.7% vs 96.1% PRR_avg) and clearly outperforms GPT-3.5-turbo-0125 (84.4%), with the largest gap in handling noise interruptions (95.2% vs 84.7% for GPT-4o). On the regression benchmarks (MMLU, GSM-8K, etc.), fine-tuning causes only minor degradation (≤4.9 points on HumanEval), confirming that full-duplex capability is added without substantially harming general LLM abilities.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the standard LLM next-token prediction paradigm for real-time speech dialogue management, departing from the traditional pipeline architecture (streaming ASR → intent recognition → dialogue management → TTS) that prior full-duplex systems like Lin et al. (2022) and Jin et al. (2021) relied on. Rather than building on a specific prior dialogue architecture, it introduces the neural FSM abstraction—a two-state (SPEAK/LISTEN) finite state machine whose transitions are emitted as control tokens by the LLM—unifying dialogue management, response generation, and interruption handling into a single autoregressive task. The approach is conceptually closest to GPT-4o's undisclosed full-duplex capability but achieves it through instruction tuning a small open-source LLM (Llama-3-8B-Instruct) with a serialized 'tape' of interleaved perception inputs and generated tokens, rather than through native multimodal training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that full-duplex speech dialogue with sub-500ms median response latency is achievable with an 8B-parameter model and only 20 steps of fine-tuning on 1,500 synthetic transcripts, making the approach accessible to resource-constrained settings. The 8% interruption-precision advantage over GPT-4o on the authors' benchmark is notable, though the evaluation relies on a self-constructed dataset and GPT-4-as-judge, limiting generalizability claims. Key open questions include whether the neural FSM formulation transfers to truly end-to-end multimodal LLMs (eliminating the ASR/TTS pipeline latency the authors acknowledge), how the system handles multi-speaker or noisy real-world environments beyond the simulated benchmark, and whether the modest regression in reasoning benchmarks (e.g., −4.9 on HumanEval) becomes problematic at scale or with more aggressive fine-tuning.
