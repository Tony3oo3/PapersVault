## Problem

Pre-trained neural image compression models produce sub-optimal latent representations due to limited encoder capacity and imperfect optimization. While Stochastic Gumbel Annealing (SGA) refines these latents at inference time, its use of the atanh function for computing rounding probabilities leads to gradients that diverge to infinity at the boundaries, causing instability and sensitivity to the temperature hyperparameter. The paper asks whether alternative probability functions can yield more robust and higher-performing latent refinement.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained hyperprior-based VAE compression model (trained on CLIC 2020 Mobile + ImageNet at 256×256 patches) and a single RGB image, then iteratively refines the continuous latent over 500–2000 optimization steps at inference time without modifying encoder or decoder weights. The output is a refined quantized latent that, when decoded, yields a reconstructed image with improved PSNR at the same BPP (or lower BPP at the same PSNR). Refinement of a single Kodak image takes approximately 21–35 minutes on an A100 GPU depending on the model's channel size.

## Method

SGA+ replaces the atanh-based logit computation in SGA with three alternative two-class rounding probability functions: linear (p = 1 − v_L, constant gradient everywhere), cosine (p = cos²(v_L·π/2), low gradients at corners), and sigmoid scaled logit (SSL, p = σ(−a·σ⁻¹(v_L)), which smoothly interpolates between linear at a=1, cosine at a≈1.6, and atanh at a≈0.65 via a single shape hyperparameter a). The key insight is that atanh's infinite boundary gradients cause overshooting and instability, while the proposed functions maintain bounded gradients and are less sensitive to the temperature schedule τ_max. SSL with a=2.3 achieves the best R-D performance, outperforming atanh by 2.8–6.2% BD-Rate across Kodak, Tecnick, and CLIC. The framework further generalizes to three-class rounding (allowing rounding to floor−1, floor, or floor+1) via a clipped power function f_{3c}(w|r,n) = f(clip(w·r))^n, which accelerates convergence under limited iteration budgets, and supports semi-multi-rate behavior by refining latents with a target λ different from the model's training λ.

## Results

On the Kodak dataset, SSL (a=2.3) achieves a BD-Rate reduction of −16.23% and BD-PSNR gain of 0.82 dB over the base model at 2000 refinement iterations, compared to −13.82% and 0.69 dB for SGA (atanh). On Tecnick and CLIC, SSL reaches −18.77%/0.95 dB and −20.11%/0.89 dB respectively, consistently outperforming atanh by 2.8–6.2% BD-Rate. At 500 iterations (a more practical budget), SSL already achieves nearly double the BD-Rate reduction of atanh (e.g., −11.60% vs −5.91% on Kodak), demonstrating faster convergence.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SSL consistently outperforms SGA (atanh) across all three datasets (Kodak, Tecnick, CLIC) and both evaluation budgets (500 and 2000 iterations), with BD-Rate gains of 2.8–6.2% over atanh. The advantage is most pronounced at 500 iterations, where SSL achieves nearly double the BD-Rate reduction of atanh (e.g., −11.60% vs −5.91% on Kodak), reflecting faster convergence. Against the Straight-Through Estimator (STE), SSL is decisively superior: STE struggles to converge and in some settings performs worse than the unrefined base model. Uniform noise quantization converges quickly but exhibits a large gap between its surrogate loss and the true R-D loss, and is outperformed by both atanh and SSL in final R-D performance. SSL also shows greater robustness to hyperparameter choices (temperature τ_max) compared to atanh, with the linear variant of SGA+ being the least sensitive overall.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the latent refinement line initiated by Campos et al. (2019), who first proposed iteratively optimizing latents at inference time, and Yang et al. (2020), who introduced Stochastic Gumbel Annealing (SGA) using the atanh function to compute rounding logits. SGA+ replaces the atanh-based probability computation with three alternatives—linear, cosine, and sigmoid scaled logit (SSL)—motivated by a proof that atanh produces infinite gradients at the probability boundaries. The SSL function is positioned as a unifying interpolation between the other methods, and the paper further generalizes all three to three-class rounding and to semi-multi-rate refinement where the inference-time λ differs from the training λ.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SGA+ offers a practical drop-in replacement for SGA that improves BD-Rate by roughly 5–6% over atanh across Kodak, Tecnick, and CLIC without modifying the pre-trained model, making it attractive for deployment where retraining is infeasible. The SSL interpolation provides a single hyperparameter (a) to tune the probability shape, and the linear variant is shown to be the most robust when no tuning budget exists. Key limitations include the high computational cost of 2000 refinement iterations per image, the need to re-tune the SSL hyperparameter per model architecture, and the fact that three-class rounding adds extra hyperparameters (r, n) for modest gains. Open questions include whether the number of refinement iterations can be reduced while preserving gains, and how SGA+ compares to the more recent code-editing and bit-allocation approaches for flexible rate control.
