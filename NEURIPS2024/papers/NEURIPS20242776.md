## Problem

Anchor-based incomplete multi-view clustering methods suffer from an anchor-shift problem: when samples are missing, anchors learned from partial data deviate from their true positions, causing misaligned anchor graphs across views and degraded clustering. Additionally, existing reconstruction strategies rely on convex combinations of anchors, which provably cannot represent samples lying outside the convex hull of the anchor set, creating systematic blind spots in missing-sample recovery.

## Input / Output

The method takes v incomplete views {X^(p) ∈ R^{d_p × n}} where each view p contains only n_p out of n samples, with binary index matrices G^(p) encoding which samples are present. It outputs cluster assignments obtained by running k-means on the left singular vectors of the concatenated anchor graph Z = [Z^(1);...;Z^(v)] ∈ R^{vm × n}, along with reconstructed missing samples X_m^(p) = A^(p) Z^(p) E^(p). The space complexity is O(n(m + Σd_p)), linear in the number of samples, enabling scalability to datasets with tens of thousands of samples.

## Method

AIMC-CVR combines two modules solved via a four-step alternating iteration with closed-form updates. The cross-view anchor learning module uses a symmetrized projection mechanism W^(pq) (identity when d_p ≤ d_q, optimized orthogonal matrix otherwise) to project existing samples from view q into the anchor space of view p, filling missing columns of each anchor graph Z^(p) and implicitly correcting anchor positions. The affine combination-based reconstruction module relaxes the non-negativity constraint on Z^(p) (keeping only Z^(p)ᵀ1 = 1), allowing reconstructed missing samples to lie outside the convex hull of anchors; Theorem 2 proves this yields strictly closer reconstructions for out-of-hull samples. The overall objective jointly optimizes reconstruction error, anchor graph sparsity (β‖Z‖²_F), and cross-view consistency (λ-weighted projection alignment), with W^(pq) updated via SVD of a cross-view alignment matrix B^(pq).

## Results

AIMC-CVR achieves the highest ACC across all seven datasets, with headline values of 83.52% on MSRCV, 91.37% on WebKB, 48.64% on Wiki, 82.29% on Hdigit, 83.73% on YTF10, 76.98% on YTF20, and 69.42% on MNIST. On the three large-scale datasets (YTF10, YTF20, MNIST) where traditional similarity-based methods cannot run due to memory constraints, AIMC-CVR outperforms the best anchor-based baselines (SCBGL, DVSAI, IMVC-CBG) by 3.18%, 0.42%, and 7.47% ACC respectively. The method also leads on NMI for MSRCV (72.17%), WebKB (51.65%), and MNIST (61.68%), and on Purity for all seven datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On ACC, AIMC-CVR outperforms all eight baselines on every dataset. The largest margins are on WebKB (91.37% vs. 83.48% for IMVC-CBG, a +7.89% gap) and MNIST (69.42% vs. 61.95% for DVSAI, a +7.47% gap). Among the three anchor-based large-scale baselines, SCBGL is the strongest competitor on YTF10 (80.55% vs. 83.73%) and YTF20 (76.56% vs. 76.98%), while DVSAI leads on MNIST (61.95% vs. 69.42%). On NMI, AIMC-CVR trails V³H on Hdigit (67.05% vs. 81.05%) and DVSAI on YTF10 (81.81% vs. 83.79%), but leads on WebKB by a wide margin (51.65% vs. 21.92% for IMVC-CBG). On Fscore, AIMC-CVR is surpassed by V³H on Hdigit (69.19% vs. 75.85%) and DVSAI on YTF20 (70.97% vs. 72.38%), but dominates on MSRCV (70.77% vs. 66.18% for DVSAI) and MNIST (58.86% vs. 51.75% for SCBGL). Overall, the method's advantage is most pronounced on ACC and Purity, while NMI and Fscore show a few competitive losses to V³H and DVSAI on specific datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the anchor-based incomplete multi-view clustering (IMC) line of work (IMVC-CBG, SCBGL, DVSAI) by identifying and addressing the 'anchor-shift' problem—where anchors learned from partial data deviate from those learned from complete data. It draws on cross-view learning literature for a symmetrized projection mechanism to construct complete anchor graphs across view pairs, and departs from the standard convex/non-negative constraints used in prior anchor methods by replacing them with affine combinations for missing-sample reconstruction. The theoretical grounding (Theorems 1–2) formalizes why convex combinations create blind spots in reconstruction, providing a principled justification for the affine relaxation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a targeted fix for a previously overlooked failure mode in scalable anchor-based IMC, with clean theoretical analysis and consistent improvements across seven datasets including three large-scale ones (38K–70K samples). However, the cross-view projection mechanism introduces O(d_p³) complexity that limits applicability to high-dimensional data, and the method assumes each sample appears in at least one view. Open questions include whether a unified cross-view metric space could eliminate the per-pair projection overhead, and whether confidence-weighted cross-view relationships could further improve robustness under extreme missingness.
