## Problem

Automatically tuning the parameters of black-box, non-differentiable image processing pipelines (e.g., Adobe Lightroom) remains challenging: zeroth-order search methods require hundreds of pipeline queries, while first-order methods depend on differentiable proxies that are difficult to train and fail to capture complex or reconfigurable pipelines. No existing approach efficiently searches the parameter space of an arbitrary black-box ISP without requiring gradient access or a learned surrogate.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241470/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an input image and a goal image (either a pixel-aligned target for photo finishing or a style reference with different content for stylization tuning). During inference, both images are resized to 64×64 and a 3-level Laplacian pyramid is constructed to preserve high-frequency details; the full-resolution images are then passed through the actual ISP using the predicted parameters. The output is a set of 9 continuous pipeline parameters (exposure, color temperature, tint, saturation, contrast, highlights, shadows, texture) applied over 10 iterative steps, yielding the final tuned image.

## Method

The paper formulates photo finishing tuning as a goal-conditioned POMDP and trains a deterministic policy with TD3 that iteratively predicts ISP parameter updates. The key architectural contribution is a three-part state representation: (1) a dual-path CNN encoder that fuses local spatial features with a global scene summary vector, (2) a photo statistics vector containing RGB histograms, luminance, median, contrast, and saturation of both input and goal images, and (3) an embedding of all historical actions and their ℓ₂ distances to the goal. The reward is the step-to-step PSNR gain for finishing tuning, or a VGG-19 Gram-matrix style score penalized by content-feature drift for stylization. Because the policy treats the ISP as a black box and conditions directly on the goal image, it requires no differentiable proxy and generalizes to unseen styles and datasets (FiveK → HDR+) with only 10 pipeline queries versus 200 for CMA-ES baselines.

## Results

On the MIT-Adobe FiveK validation set, the proposed RL-based method achieves 35.89 dB PSNR and 0.9764 SSIM on expert-C targets, and 38.46 dB PSNR and 0.9814 SSIM on random targets, using only 10 pipeline queries. This substantially outperforms CMA-ES (28.53/32.29 dB PSNR with 200 queries) and both proxy-based methods (21.71–22.31 dB PSNR). On the out-of-distribution HDR+ dataset, the method still leads with 31.54 dB PSNR versus 28.08 dB for CMA-ES, confirming generalization beyond the training distribution.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed goal-conditioned RL policy wins on every metric against all four baselines on both FiveK target types. Against CMA-ES [20] (zeroth-order search, 200 queries), the RL method gains +7.36 dB PSNR on expert-C targets (35.89 vs. 28.53) and +6.17 dB on random targets (38.46 vs. 32.29), while requiring only 10 queries instead of 200—a 20× reduction in pipeline evaluations. Against the Monolithic Proxy [28] (single UNet differentiable surrogate), the gap is even larger: +14.18 dB PSNR on expert-C targets (35.89 vs. 21.71), indicating the proxy fails to faithfully model the full pipeline. The Cascaded Proxy [29] (per-module surrogates) slightly improves over the monolithic version (+0.6 dB) but still trails the RL method by over 13 dB, suffering from accumulated approximation errors. On the out-of-distribution HDR+ dataset, the RL method (31.54 dB) also outperforms Greedy Search [16] (25.79 dB) and CMA-ES (28.08 dB), while both proxy methods degrade severely (17.80–18.90 dB) because their surrogates do not generalize across datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes goal-conditioned reinforcement learning (TD3) for the photo finishing tuning task, which prior work has addressed exclusively through zeroth-order search (CMA-ES, genetic algorithms) or first-order optimization via differentiable neural proxies of the ISP pipeline. Rather than building on a specific prior RL-for-ISP line, it carves new ground by treating the pipeline as a pure black box and learning a policy that directly predicts parameter adjustments from image observations. The state representation design—combining dual-path CNN features, precomputed photo statistics (histograms, luminance, contrast), and historical action embeddings—is a task-specific contribution not found in prior ISP tuning or general goal-conditioned RL work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 20× reduction in pipeline queries (10 vs. 200) while simultaneously improving PSNR by ~7 dB over CMA-ES makes automated ISP tuning practically viable for real-time or near-real-time applications, and the black-box formulation removes the engineering burden of training differentiable proxies for each pipeline variant. Generalization to the unseen HDR+ dataset and to out-of-distribution style targets without retraining is a meaningful step toward deployable tuning systems. Key open questions include scalability beyond the 9-parameter space used here, whether the approach extends to larger or dynamically reconfigurable pipelines, and the current limitation to image-only goals (no text or other modalities).
