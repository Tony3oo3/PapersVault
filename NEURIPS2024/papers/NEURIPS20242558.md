## Problem

Existing neural population models are brain-region-specific and narrowly tailored to particular experimental contexts, limiting their applicability to distributed brain function. Recent foundation models for spiking data (NDT2, POYO) are restricted to the sensorimotor network and treat the recorded population as a homogeneous set of neurons without explicitly modeling brain-region structure. This paper addresses the need for a self-supervised approach that can learn representations across all spatial scales—single-neuron, region-level, and population-level—across diverse brain areas.

## Input / Output

The model consumes spike-sorted neural activity binned into 20 ms windows, trial-aligned to 2 seconds (200 time bins), with per-neuron brain-region annotations from the IBL repeated site dataset (~676 neurons per session across five regions: secondary visual areas, hippocampus, and thalamus). It outputs time-varying firing rates under a Poisson emission model, which are then used for activity reconstruction (measured in bits-per-spike) or fed into a linear probe for behavior decoding (choice accuracy, whisker motion energy R²). A key design choice is test-time zero-masking of input channels to construct held-out subsets for evaluation, replacing the traditional fixed held-out-neuron protocol and enabling a single model to be scored on all neurons and regions simultaneously.

## Method

The core contribution is a multi-task-masking (MtM) training objective in which, for each batch, one of four masking schemes—causal (predict future timesteps), neuron (predict masked neurons from the full population), intra-region (predict masked neurons from same-region context only), or inter-region (predict masked neurons from other-region context only)—is sampled uniformly and applied to the input. A learnable D-dimensional prompt token is prepended to the tokenized neural data to condition the transformer on the active masking scheme, enabling the model to specialize its internal computation per task. At inference, the prompt token associated with the desired downstream task is selected to perform 'mode switching,' adapting the frozen model without retraining. The approach is architecture-agnostic (validated on NDT1 temporal-token transformers, NDT2 ViT-style patch transformers, and LFADS sequential VAEs) and uses a Poisson emission model for reconstruction. The key insight is that each masking scheme teaches a distinct structural aspect of neural populations, and jointly training across all four with prompt-based conditioning yields a single model that generalizes across all spatial scales and downstream tasks.

## Results

On the IBL repeated site dataset (34-session pretraining, 5 held-out sessions), multi-session MtM with NDT1-stitch achieves 1.11 bps on co-smoothing, 0.46 bps on forward prediction, 0.85 bps on intra-region prediction, and 0.96 bps on inter-region prediction, substantially outperforming the temporal masking baseline (0.79, 0.43, −0.08, 0.34 bps respectively). For behavior decoding, MtM achieves 0.88 choice accuracy and 0.68 R² for whisker motion energy, comparable to the temporal baseline (0.89, 0.67). Scaling from 1 to 34 pretraining sessions continues to improve MtM performance on all activity metrics, while temporal masking saturates at 10 sessions. Cross-species transfer to the NLB MC_RTT monkey dataset yields 0.19 bps, exceeding NDT1's previous best of 0.16 bps.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against NDT1 with temporal masking (the primary baseline), MtM achieves large gains on all four activity reconstruction metrics: co-smoothing improves from 0.79 to 1.11 bps, intra-region from −0.08 to 0.85 bps, and inter-region from 0.34 to 0.96 bps in the multi-session setting; forward prediction improves modestly (0.43→0.46 bps). Choice decoding is essentially tied (0.88 vs 0.89 accuracy). Against NDT2 with random token masking, MtM is dramatically superior: the NDT2 baseline achieves negative co-smoothing (−0.87 single-session, 0.26 multi-session) and negative inter-region scores, while MtM-NDT2 reaches 0.97 and 0.87 bps respectively. Against LFADS (a sequential VAE), MtM also wins on all activity metrics (e.g., intra-region 0.65 vs −0.34 bps; inter-region 0.76 vs 0.50 bps), confirming MtM's architecture-agnosticism. The paper also shows that MtM's advantage is most pronounced for region-level tasks (intra- and inter-region), where temporal masking cannot capture spatial structure, while the gap narrows for population-level tasks like choice decoding where the full population is used.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Neural Data Transformer line of work (NDT1's temporal masking, NDT2's ViT-style patch masking) by introducing a multi-task-masking (MtM) objective that alternates between four structurally distinct masking schemes (causal, neuron, intra-region, inter-region) during training. The prompt-token mechanism for mode switching is borrowed from UL2's unification of language learning paradigms and repurposed here to let a single model switch between masking objectives at inference time. A key conceptual departure from NDT2 is the explicit modeling of brain-region identity: rather than treating the population as a homogeneous set of neurons, MtM's intra- and inter-region masking schemes force the model to learn region-specific and cross-region structure, which prior neural population models did not address.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that a single self-supervised model for neural spiking data can simultaneously perform well across population-level, region-level, and single-neuron-level prediction tasks, and that multi-animal pretraining yields continued scaling benefits (unlike temporal masking, which saturates at ~10 sessions). The multi-region benchmark and the architecture-agnostic MtM objective (validated on both transformers and LFADS) provide a reusable recipe for future neural foundation models. Key limitations include evaluation on only 5 brain regions and 39 mice (far from the 300-region IBL brain-wide map), reliance on simple temporal-token architectures, and the absence of error bars or multi-seed runs, leaving open whether the scaling advantages persist at larger scale and with more sophisticated tokenization.
