## Problem

Concept bottleneck models (CBMs) allow users to intervene on predicted concept values to steer downstream predictions, but this capability is restricted to models explicitly designed as CBMs. Pretrained black-box neural networks—the standard in practice—do not support such instance-specific concept-based interventions, and prior post-hoc CBM conversion methods generally do not explore per-instance interventions. This paper asks how to make arbitrary pretrained black boxes intervenable using only a small validation set with concept labels, and how to fine-tune them to improve intervention effectiveness.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242698/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pretrained black-box network (any architecture from FCNN to Stable Diffusion), a data point x, user-specified concept values c', and a small validation set (as small as 0.5% of the original) annotated with concept labels. It produces an edited intermediate activation vector z' at a chosen layer and an updated prediction ŷ' = g_ψ(z'), with the network's architecture and backbone representations left unchanged. Interventions are performed per-instance, and the procedure is computationally feasible online (seconds on GPU) for batched data points.

## Method

The intervention procedure has three steps: (1) train a probe q_ξ on the small validation set to predict concepts from intermediate activations z = h_φ(x); (2) edit z to z' by minimizing the relaxed objective λL^c(q_ξ(z'), c') + d(z, z'), balancing consistency with the given concept values and proximity to the original representation; (3) compute the updated prediction ŷ' = g_ψ(z'). The key novelty is formalizing 'intervenability' as a differentiable measure of intervention effectiveness (the expected gap between pre- and post-intervention target prediction loss) and using it as a fine-tuning objective. During fine-tuning, the head parameters ψ are updated by performing interventions on validation batches while the backbone h_φ is kept fixed, preserving representations for downstream transfer. This explicit maximization of intervenability outperforms common-sense alternatives (multi-task learning, concept-appending, post-hoc CBM conversion) and remains effective when concept labels are generated by VLMs rather than human annotators.

## Results

On MIMIC-CXR, the proposed fine-tuning for intervenability (FINE-TUNED, I) achieves the best target AUROC of 0.808±0.007 and AUPR of 0.733±0.009, surpassing both the vanilla CBM (0.765/0.699) and the post hoc CBM (0.801/0.727). On AwA2, all methods reach near-saturated target AUROC (~0.996), but FINE-TUNED, I produces the steepest intervention curves, surpassing CBMs in intervention effectiveness. On CIFAR-10 with VLM-generated concepts and a Stable Diffusion backbone, FINE-TUNED, I achieves 0.873 AUROC and outperforms all baselines in intervention effectiveness. Critically, on MIMIC-CXR, untuned black-box models are not intervenable at all, while fine-tuning for intervenability makes them more intervenable than CBMs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the vanilla CBM (Koh et al., 2020), FINE-TUNED, I achieves a higher target AUROC on MIMIC-CXR (0.808 vs. 0.765) and, more importantly, produces steeper intervention curves that surpass the CBM's, whereas the untuned black box is not intervenable at all on this dataset. On AwA2, FINE-TUNED, I also surpasses the CBM in intervention effectiveness despite comparable static accuracy. Against the post hoc CBM baselines (Yuksekgonul et al., 2023; Oikarinen et al., 2023), FINE-TUNED, I wins on MIMIC-CXR (AUROC 0.808 vs. 0.801) and in intervention effectiveness; on the synthetic incomplete-concept setting, post hoc CBM interventions actually degrade performance while FINE-TUNED, I continues to improve. The two additional fine-tuning baselines (multitask learning FINE-TUNED, MT and concept-appending FINE-TUNED, A) are consistently outperformed by FINE-TUNED, I in intervention effectiveness across all datasets, with FINE-TUNED, A sometimes being harmful. On CIFAR-10 with VLM-generated concepts, FINE-TUNED, I outperforms the post hoc CBM in intervention curves, and the CBM baseline is omitted entirely due to the computational infeasibility of retraining the Stable Diffusion backbone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the concept bottleneck model (CBM) intervention paradigm (Koh et al., 2020) to arbitrary pretrained black-box neural networks, departing from prior post-hoc CBM conversion methods (Yuksekgonul et al., 2023; Oikarinen et al., 2023) that restructure the network into a bottleneck architecture. The intervention procedure repurposes probing (Alain & Bengio, 2016) and representation editing ideas from counterfactual explanations (Wachter et al., 2017) in a novel way: rather than flipping a label, the method injects user-specified concept values into intermediate activations. The formal intervenability measure generalises the standard CBM evaluation protocol and is distinct from model reliance (Fisher et al., 2019) in using a difference-of-expectations rather than a ratio-of-losses formulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is strongest in high-stakes settings like medical imaging, where the paper demonstrates that fine-tuned black boxes become more intervenable than CBMs on chest X-ray classification—a finding that challenges the assumption that CBMs are inherently superior for human-model interaction. The method's compatibility with VLM-generated concept labels (CLIP/GPT-3) alleviates the costly human annotation bottleneck, and the observed calibration improvement (lower Brier scores) is a useful side effect. Open questions include whether end-to-end fine-tuning of all parameters (including the probe and backbone) further improves intervenability, whether an optimal intervention strategy can be learned jointly with the weights, and whether the approach extends to generative models.
