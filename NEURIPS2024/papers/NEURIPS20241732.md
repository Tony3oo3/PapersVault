## Problem

Existing heterogeneous federated learning methods that aggregate diverse client architectures either lack controllability over the size of generated personalized models (e.g., pFedHR produces models up to 4.8× larger than the originals), require prior knowledge of model structures, or raise privacy concerns by exchanging intermediate representations. This paper tackles the gap of generating personalized models that are both functionally faithful to each client's original architecture and strictly bounded in parameter size.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241732/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The server receives M heterogeneous client models per round (e.g., MobileNetV1/V2/V3 or custom CNNs with 2–6 convolutional blocks and 2–4 fully connected layers) along with a public dataset (labeled or unlabeled, 8% of the split). It outputs one personalized teacher model per client whose block count matches the original and whose parameter size can be constrained to at most (1+η)× the original via an explicit size budget. The client then consumes this teacher through KL-divergence distillation on its private data to update its local model.

## Method

pFedClub decomposes each uploaded model into functional blocks, clusters them via K-means on CKA-based input/output similarity, and then applies the CMSR algorithm to generate candidate personalized models through three steps: (1) random anchor-block selection from the same functional group to preserve diversity, (2) order-constrained block search that enforces monotonically increasing block indices across substitutions to guarantee functional coherence, and (3) block completion that appends the remaining original blocks if the order constraint halts early. Candidates are dimension-aligned via a ReLU(Linear) stitching layer fine-tuned on public data (cross-entropy for labeled, contrastive loss for unlabeled), and the best candidate is selected by maximum cosine similarity of logits with the original model. The selected teacher then guides the client's next-round training through a combined cross-entropy + KL-divergence distillation loss, keeping the client's own architecture unchanged.

## Results

On the most diverse heterogeneous setting (Model Zoo I, 7 different architectures), pFedClub achieves 82.65% accuracy on SVHN (non-IID, labeled) and 73.62% on CIFAR-10 (non-IID, labeled), outperforming the closest baseline pFedHR by 1.77 and 3.74 percentage points respectively. Under unlabeled public data, pFedClub reaches 81.76% on SVHN and 72.87% on CIFAR-10 (non-IID), again surpassing pFedHR (78.93% and 68.54%). On MNIST, pFedClub is competitive but slightly trails pFedHR on IID (94.02% vs 94.67%) while leading on non-IID (93.20% vs 92.88%). The controllability analysis shows pFedClub keeps generated model sizes within ~1.5× the original, versus ~4.8× for pFedHR on the smallest client.

## Baselines & Comparisons

pFedClub consistently outperforms the primary baseline pFedHR on SVHN and CIFAR-10 across all settings (e.g., +1.77% on SVHN non-IID labeled, +3.74% on CIFAR-10 non-IID labeled), though it trails pFedHR by 0.65% on MNIST IID (94.02% vs 94.67%). Against distillation-based baselines, pFedClub leads FedMD by 3.5–10.5% and FedGH by 3.3–6.3% on SVHN/CIFAR-10 non-IID (labeled). Under unlabeled public data, pFedClub surpasses FedKEMF by 4.2–7.8% and FCCL by 4.5–8.1% on SVHN/CIFAR-10 non-IID. On the homogeneous setting (Table 2), pFedClub also beats all baselines on SVHN and CIFAR-10 (e.g., 85.26% vs 83.88% for pFedHR on SVHN with MobileNetV2), confirming its effectiveness beyond the heterogeneous scenario. The only consistent loss is on MNIST IID where the task is near-saturated and pFedHR's larger reassembled models provide a marginal edge.

## Relation to Prior Work

pFedClub extends the model reassembly paradigm introduced by pFedHR [23] for heterogeneous federated learning, replacing its unconstrained layer-wise reassembly with a controllable block-wise substitution mechanism (CMSR). The order-constrained block search draws directly from the deep model reassembly framework of Yang et al. [33], while the functionality-based block grouping via CKA similarity and K-means clustering is a new organizational step. Unlike distillation-based approaches (FedMD, FedKEMF) or information-exchange methods (FCCL, FedGH) that raise privacy concerns or require predefined structures, pFedClub operates purely on model parameters and does not require prior knowledge of client architectures, positioning it as a more flexible successor to pFedHR with an added controllability dimension.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The controllability of generated personalized model sizes addresses a critical practical gap: in real-world FL deployments on resource-constrained devices (smartphones, IoT), receiving a teacher model 4–5× larger than the original (as pFedHR does) is infeasible. The η-constrained variant (pFedClub+) demonstrates that size constraints can be enforced with only marginal accuracy loss, making the framework directly applicable to edge FL. However, the CMSR algorithm remains heuristic, server-side computation is still substantially longer than parameter averaging, and evaluation is limited to image classification on three small datasets—open questions include whether the approach generalizes to NLP, tabular, or generative tasks, and whether more principled (non-greedy) search strategies could improve candidate quality.
