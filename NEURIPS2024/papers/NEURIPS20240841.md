## Problem

Prior universality results for AdaGrad stepsizes covered only the smooth or nonsmooth extremes separately, and only under uniformly bounded oracle variance. The general Hölder smooth case with stochastic oracles, acceleration, and refined (state-dependent) variance assumptions remained open. This paper closes these gaps by showing that AdaGrad stepsizes work in a unified manner across all these settings for approximately smooth composite problems with potentially inexact oracles.

## Input / Output

The only problem-dependent parameter required is the feasible set diameter D; all other constants (smoothness L_f, variance σ, inexactness δ_f, variance smoothness L_g) are adapted automatically on the fly. The stochastic oracle may be biased (inexact), and the composite part ψ need only admit a computable proximal mapping. The output is an approximate minimizer whose expected function residual is bounded by rates matching state-of-the-art for each problem class, with the bound expressed in terms of whichever constants are actually small for the given instance.

## Method

The core technical contribution is identifying a single inequality (2) that AdaGrad stepsize update rules satisfy, which serves as the sole analytical requirement for all convergence proofs. This inequality involves a 'virtual stepsize' M̄ that exists only in the analysis; the actual stepsizes adapt via the telescoping error term [min{M_{k+1}, M̄} − M_k]_+ Ω, whose cumulative impact is bounded by M̄D². Building on this, the paper presents four algorithms: UniSgd and UniFastSgd (basic and accelerated SGD with AdaGrad), and UniSvrg and UniFastSvrg (epoch-based SVRG-type variance reduction with AdaGrad). Under uniformly bounded variance the methods achieve O(L_fD²/N) + O(σD/√N) rates; under refined variance assumptions they express complexity in terms of variance at the minimizer σ*; with explicit SVRG they achieve exponential (2^−t) or O(1/t²) convergence. The framework handles (δ,L)-approximately smooth objectives, recovering Hölder smooth, inexact, and bilevel problems as corollaries without any algorithmic modification.

## Results

The paper establishes state-of-the-art oracle complexity bounds for four universal adaptive methods. UniSgd achieves O(L_f D²/k + σD/√k + δ_f) under bounded variance and O((L_f+L_g)D²/k + σ_*D/√k + δ_f + δ_g) under implicit variance reduction. UniFastSgd achieves O(L_f D²/k² + σD/√k + kδ_f) (accelerated rate in the smooth term). UniSvrg achieves linear convergence O((L_f+L_g)D²/2^t + δ_f + δ_g) with SO complexity 2^t + n log t, while UniFastSvrg achieves O((L_f+L_g)D²/(n(t−log log n)²) + t(δ_f+δ_g)) with SO complexity nt. All methods require only the feasible-set diameter D as a user-specified parameter.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the polyhedron feasibility problem (n=10⁴, d=10³, R=10⁶, q∈{1, 1.3, 1.6, 2}), UniSvrg converges consistently faster than AdaSVRG [16] across all smoothness levels, benefiting from its gradient-difference accumulation rather than gradient accumulation. Among accelerated methods, UniFastSvrg outperforms both AdaVRAE and AdaVRAG [36] in convergence speed. Critically, FastSvrg (the primal VRADA method [53] with a fixed stepsize) fails to converge entirely when the problem is not Lipschitz smooth (q<2), whereas all four universal AdaGrad-based methods converge successfully, demonstrating their adaptivity to unknown Hölder smoothness. The mini-batch experiments (Fig. 2) confirm that increasing batch size b yields the predicted speedup under idealized parallelism, with the benefit scaling as 1/b in the variance-dependent terms.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies and extends the line of work on AdaGrad's universality property, which was first demonstrated for the two extreme cases of smooth and nonsmooth problems by Levy et al. [34] and later extended to Hölder smooth problems by Rodomanov et al. [49]. The key departure is a single abstract stepsize-update inequality (2) that subsumes both the classical AdaGrad rule and the modified rule from [49], allowing the authors to prove convergence guarantees in a unified manner across three fundamentally different variance regimes: uniformly bounded variance, state-dependent (implicit) variance reduction, and explicit SVRG-type variance reduction. The accelerated methods build on Lan's Method of Similar Triangles [33] and the VRADA scheme [53], while the SVRG variants extend the AdaSVRG construction of Dubois-Taine et al. [16] to the more general (δ,L)-approximately smooth composite setting with inexact oracle.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that practitioners can use a single adaptive algorithm (UniSgd, UniFastSgd, UniSvrg, or UniFastSvrg) without knowing any problem-dependent constants beyond the feasible-set diameter D, and automatically obtain the best rate applicable to their specific problem instance. This is particularly valuable for Hölder smooth problems where the smoothness exponent ν and constants are typically unknown. Open questions noted by the authors include: whether the diameter D can be eliminated entirely (connecting to parameter-free methods like DoG [25] and Prodigy [41]), whether Assumption 9 can be removed from the UniSvrg analysis, and whether the framework extends to nonconvex problems. The accelerated methods' sensitivity to oracle inexactness (O(kδ) accumulation) remains a limitation for practical use with inexact oracles.
