## Problem

Cloud removal research in satellite imagery has been held back by the absence of a comprehensive benchmark and a sufficiently large, geographically diverse training dataset. Existing datasets suffer from limited spatial coverage, filtering out heavily cloudy images, temporally misaligned ground truth (input and target captured months apart), and reliance on a single sensor modality (Sentinel-2), all of which limit both training effectiveness and evaluation rigor.

## Input / Output

The benchmark is a sequence-to-point task where each instance provides three cloudy Sentinel-2 images (13 spectral bands, 256×256 pixels at 10 m resolution) as primary input, optionally augmented with co-registered Sentinel-1 SAR (VV/VH channels) and Landsat-8/9 (11 bands) images within a two-day temporal window, along with cloud/shadow masks and geolocation metadata. The target is a single cloud-free Sentinel-2 image selected from four consecutive spatiotemporal patches to ensure temporal alignment, with less than 10% combined cloud and shadow coverage. Metrics are computed only over cloud-free pixels using the provided masks.

## Method

AllClear is constructed via grid-based ROI sampling (every 0.1° latitude and 0.1°·cos(θ) longitude) followed by iterative land-cover-balanced selection using Dynamic World LULC probabilities, yielding 23,742 globally distributed ROIs with 4.35 million images from Sentinel-1, Sentinel-2, and Landsat-8/9 over the full year 2022. The benchmark design addresses temporal misalignment by constraining target images to four consecutive patches and auxiliary sensors to a two-day window, and it retains all cloud coverage levels without filtering. Empirically, the paper demonstrates a scaling law (PSNR rises from 28.47 to 33.87 with 30× more training data using UnCRtainTS), shows that 12-frame temporal inputs outperform the standard 3-frame setup, and reveals that filling Sentinel-1 gaps with zeros (rather than ones) provides a beneficial inductive bias for multi-modal fusion.

## Results

On the AllClear benchmark, the best learned model UnCRtainTS (trained on SEN12MS-CR-TS) achieves PSNR 29.009, SSIM 0.898, SAM 5.972, and MAE 0.039, slightly outperforming the simple 'Least Cloudy' baseline (PSNR 28.864) on SSIM and SAM but trailing the 'Mosaicing' baseline (PSNR 29.824) on PSNR and MAE. Models pre-trained on smaller datasets (CTGAN, PMAA, DiffCR) all fall below the simple baselines, indicating they are undertrained. Scaling UnCRtainTS training from 3.4% to 100% of AllClear (10,167 → 278,613 samples, a ~27× increase) raises PSNR from 28.47 to 33.87, demonstrating a clear scaling law. With the improved zero-fill preprocessing for Sentinel-1 gaps, full-dataset training further pushes PSNR to 34.148.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UnCRtainTS (Ebel et al., 2023), trained on SEN12MS-CR-TS, is the strongest learned model on AllClear, achieving the best SSIM (0.898) and SAM (5.972) among all entries, and narrowly beating the 'Least Cloudy' baseline on PSNR (29.009 vs. 28.864). However, it is outperformed by the 'Mosaicing' baseline on PSNR (29.824) and MAE (0.045 vs. 0.039), suggesting that the small temporal gap in AllClear makes simple pixel-averaging strategies highly competitive. U-TILISE (Stucker et al., 2023) underperforms all baselines substantially (PSNR 24.660, MAE 0.083), likely due to a distribution mismatch between its synthetic cloud masks during training and real clouds. CTGAN (Huang & Wu, 2022), PMAA (Zou et al., 2023a), and DiffCR (Zou et al., 2023b), all pre-trained on the much smaller STGAN or Sen2_MTC datasets, score below even the simplest baselines, confirming that models trained on prior datasets are undertrained. The paper's key empirical finding is that retraining UnCRtainTS on the full AllClear training set (278,613 samples) lifts PSNR from 28.47 to 33.87 (and 34.15 with zero-fill preprocessing), a >10% improvement that no prior dataset enabled.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground in the cloud removal dataset space rather than building on a specific prior method. It addresses four concrete gaps identified across STGAN, Sen2_MTC, SEN12MS-CR/TS, and EarthNet2021: limited geographic diversity, aggressive filtering of heavily cloudy images, temporal misalignment between input and target captures, and reliance on a single sensor modality. The benchmark task (sequence-to-point cloud removal) follows the formulation established by STGAN and SEN12MS-CR-TS, but the paper tightens temporal alignment by constraining target images to four consecutive spatiotemporal patches and restricting auxiliary sensor pairing to a two-day window. For empirical evaluation, the paper adopts UnCRtainTS (Ebel et al., 2023) as its backbone architecture, positioning AllClear as a training and evaluation resource rather than proposing a new cloud removal method.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AllClear's 4-million-image scale and global ROI distribution make it the de facto reference dataset for cloud removal, and the demonstrated scaling law (PSNR 28.47→33.87 with 30× more data) provides strong evidence that prior models were undertrained rather than architecturally limited. The finding that trivial baselines (Least Cloudy, Mosaicing) match or exceed complex learned models pre-trained on smaller datasets is a useful reality check for the field. Key open questions remain: the lack of manual ground-truth annotations (reliance on s2cloudless), the use of non-atmospherically-corrected Level-1C imagery, the observed spatial generalization gap (North America held-out drops ~3 PSNR), and whether the scaling law holds across architectures beyond UnCRtainTS. The paper also flags that Landsat integration currently hurts performance, suggesting model redesign is needed to exploit that modality.
