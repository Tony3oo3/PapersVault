## Problem

Prior work on eliciting reasoning from LLMs relies on prompting techniques (few-shot or zero-shot CoT) that encode task-specific human priors, making it difficult to assess a model's intrinsic reasoning ability. The paper asks whether pre-trained LLMs can reason without any prompting at all, and finds that CoT reasoning paths are already present in the top-k alternative decoding tokens but are obscured by the standard greedy decoding procedure.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242122/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a reasoning question formatted in the standard QA prefix 'Q: [question]\nA:' and produces a selected CoT reasoning path with a final answer. At the first decoding step, the model branches into the top-k alternative tokens (default k=10), each followed by greedy decoding to completion. The answer span is identified either by extracting the last numerical value (for math) or by appending 'So the answer is' and aligning the continuation, and a confidence score Δ is computed over those answer tokens to rank paths.

## Method

The core mechanism is CoT-decoding: instead of following only the greedy (top-1) token at the first decoding step, the method branches into the top-k alternative tokens and continues greedy decoding from each branch. The key insight is that the presence of a CoT reasoning path in a decoding trajectory correlates with significantly higher model confidence in the final answer, measured as Δ = (1/|answer|) Σ (p(top-1 token) − p(top-2 token)) averaged over answer tokens. This confidence metric, inspired by minimum-margin scoring, reliably distinguishes CoT paths from non-CoT direct-answer paths (88% of highest-confidence paths in top-10 contain CoT on GSM8K). The final output is the path with the maximum Δ, or a weighted aggregation across paths sharing the same answer. The approach is entirely unsupervised—no prompting, no fine-tuning, no auxiliary models—and is shown to outperform greedy decoding, temperature sampling, top-k sampling, nucleus sampling, beam search, and self-consistency without prompts across PaLM-2, Mistral-7B, and Gemma-7B on math, commonsense, and symbolic reasoning benchmarks.

## Results

On GSM8K, CoT-decoding (k=10) achieves 25.1% accuracy on Mistral-7B (pre-trained) and 63.2% on PaLM-2 Large, compared to 9.9% and 34.8% for greedy decoding respectively — a +15.2 and +28.4 absolute gain. On Year Parity, CoT-decoding reaches 95.0% on PaLM-2 Large versus 57.0% for greedy decoding. CoT-decoding is the only decoding strategy among those tested (top-k sampling, nucleus sampling, beam search, temperature sampling, self-consistency) that effectively elicits reasoning, with all other methods performing at or below greedy decoding on GSM8K.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoT-decoding decisively outperforms all standard decoding strategies on GSM8K (Mistral-7B): 25.1% vs. top-k sampling (4.9%), nucleus sampling (6.4%), beam search (6.7%), and temperature sampling (7.5%), with even greedy decoding (9.9%) trailing significantly. Against self-consistency without CoT prompting (Wang et al., 2023a), CoT-decoding more than doubles accuracy (25.1% vs. 12.9% on Mistral-7B; 63.2% vs. 40.6% on PaLM-2 L), because sampling without prompts fails to generate diverse first tokens. Compared to zero-shot CoT prompting (Kojima et al., 2022), CoT-decoding without any prompt beats it on Mistral-7B (25.1% vs. 17.5%) but falls short on PaLM-2 Large (63.2% vs. 75.1%); however, combining CoT-decoding with zero-shot CoT prompting yields the best results (48.4% and 87.0% respectively), surpassing self-consistency with the same prompt (39.4% and 85.3%). On MultiArith, CoT-decoding gains are even larger (+31.4% on pre-trained Mistral-7B, +28.7% on instruction-tuned), and on Year Parity it nearly eliminates the scaling plateau observed with greedy decoding.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm of eliciting LLM reasoning through prompting (few-shot CoT, zero-shot CoT) or instruction-tuning, instead showing that reasoning paths are already present in the model's decoding space but obscured by greedy decoding. The confidence metric (Δ) draws on the minimum-margin idea from active learning (Jiang and Gupta, 2019), repurposed here as a path-selection signal over top-k decoding trajectories. It contrasts with self-consistency (Wang et al., 2023a), which aggregates over sampled CoT-prompted paths, by operating entirely without prompts and selecting paths based on answer-token confidence rather than majority vote. The work also positions itself against contrastive decoding and diverse beam search, which require auxiliary models or optimize for diversity rather than reasoning accuracy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By removing prompting as a confound, CoT-decoding offers a cleaner window into what models can reason about intrinsically versus what requires human 'teaching' via demonstrations, revealing that prompting largely surfaces already-present CoT paths rather than creating new reasoning ability. The method also exposes specific model vulnerabilities (state tracking in multi-step tasks, left-to-right arithmetic instead of order of operations) that prompting may mask. Key limitations include the O(k) computational overhead, the restriction to first-token branching (later branching is task-dependent and harder to optimize), and the reduced precision of the confidence metric for open-ended answers. Open questions include whether branching at arbitrary positions with search could further improve results, and whether the approach extends to tasks where no correct CoT path exists in the top-k at all.
