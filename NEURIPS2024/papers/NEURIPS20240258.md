## Problem

Existing offline optimizers regularize surrogates or search models using strategies that are tightly coupled to specific model architectures or search procedures, making them difficult to transfer across different surrogate or optimizer choices. This paper asks whether a model-agnostic regularizer—based on the geometry of the surrogate's loss landscape rather than input-space heuristics—can uniformly suppress out-of-distribution prediction errors and be plugged into any existing offline optimizer.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a static offline dataset of input-output pairs (x_i, z_i) from a black-box oracle and a parametric surrogate g(x; ω) to be trained on that data. It outputs the surrogate parameters ω* that minimize the empirical prediction loss subject to a user-specified upper bound ε on the surrogate's gradient norm (a tractable proxy for sharpness). The regularized surrogate is then used in a downstream search procedure (gradient ascent, REINFORCE, CMA-ES, etc.) to produce 128 candidate designs ranked by oracle performance at the 50th, 75th, and 100th percentile levels.

## Method

IGNITE defines surrogate sharpness as the worst-case change in the surrogate's expected prediction under ℓ₂-norm-bounded parameter perturbations, then shows via first-order Taylor expansion and Cauchy-Schwarz that this sharpness is tightly approximated by ρ‖∇_ω h(ω)‖, where h(ω) is the expected surrogate output over the offline data. This reduces the regularization to a constrained optimization problem (minimize prediction loss s.t. ρ‖∇_ω h(ω)‖ ≤ ε), solved via the basic differential multiplier method: simultaneous gradient descent on ω and gradient ascent on the Lagrange multiplier λ. The expensive Hessian-vector product needed for ∇_ω‖∇_ω h(ω)‖ is approximated by a finite-difference step of size r along the normalized gradient direction, avoiding explicit Hessian computation. A PAC-Bayes-based theoretical analysis (Theorem 2) proves that reducing the empirical sharpness on the offline data provably bounds the worst-case generalized sharpness on unseen data, with the gap depending on model dimension, dataset size, and a curvature constant.

## Results

IGNITE is evaluated as a plug-in regularizer on 11 offline optimization baselines across 4 Design-Bench tasks (Ant Morphology, D'Kitty Morphology, TF-Bind-8, TF-Bind-10) at the 100th percentile level. Across all 44 baseline-task combinations, IGNITE improves performance in 35 cases (79.55%), maintains it in 5, and degrades it in only 4 (9.09%), yielding a 91% non-degradation rate. The average improvement is ~1.91%, with a peak gain of 9.6% (REINFORCE on D'Kitty Morphology) and a worst-case degradation of just 0.7% (COMs on TF-Bind-10). On noisy-oracle tasks (Superconductor, Chembl), IGNITE still provides up to 2.1% improvement.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IGNITE is applied as an additional regularizer on top of 11 existing offline optimizers. The largest gains appear with REINFORCE (+2.7% to +9.6%), ENS-MIN (+0.8% to +8.3%), and RoMA (+0.3% to +4.6%), suggesting that methods with less built-in conservatism benefit most from sharpness regularization. CbAS, MINs, and ICT show moderate but consistent improvements (+0.1% to +3.8%). COMs is the only baseline where IGNITE produces small negative gains (−0.7% to +0.4%), likely because COMs already enforces conservative predictions. CMA-ES and BO-qEI show near-zero changes, indicating their search procedures are already robust to surrogate sharpness. In a direct ablation (Table 2), IGNITE outperforms SAM (loss sharpness), L1, and L2 regularization in most cases; for example, on REINFORCE/TF-Bind-10, IGNITE yields +3.5% versus SAM's +0.2%, L1's +0.3%, and L2's +0.1%, while SAM and L2 actually degrade GA's performance on D'Kitty (−1.3% and −0.7% respectively) where IGNITE still improves it (+0.5%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the sharpness-aware minimization (SAM) framework [14] from the loss landscape to the surrogate prediction landscape, arguing that in offline optimization the search procedure operates on the surrogate's output rather than the loss, making prediction sharpness the more relevant quantity to regularize. It departs from prior offline optimization regularizers (COMs, CbAS, RoMA, MINs, ICT) that use ad-hoc, model-specific characterizations of out-of-distribution regions, by proposing a model-agnostic constraint on the surrogate's gradient norm that can be plugged into any existing optimizer. The theoretical analysis extends PAC-Bayes-style bounds from loss sharpness to surrogate prediction sharpness, providing a new generalization guarantee specific to the offline optimization setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The model-agnostic nature of IGNITE means it can serve as a drop-in regularizer for any existing offline optimizer without requiring modifications to the search procedure or surrogate architecture, lowering the barrier to adoption. The theoretical guarantee (Theorem 2) that reducing empirical surrogate sharpness provably bounds generalized sharpness provides a principled justification beyond empirical observation. However, the average improvement is modest (~1.9%), the method introduces five additional hyperparameters, and the Hessian-vector product approximation adds computational overhead. Open questions include whether the approach extends to online or few-shot settings, whether the bounded-output assumption (Assumption 1) limits practical applicability, and whether the gains persist at larger dataset scales.
