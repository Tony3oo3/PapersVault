## Problem

Airfoil inverse design lacks large-scale open-source benchmarks: existing datasets contain only thousands of samples, provide a single aerodynamic condition rather than multi-modal geometric and aerodynamic constraints, and do not support progressive editing of existing designs. These limitations prevent data-driven generative models from generalizing to the multi-condition, iterative refinement workflows used in real industrial airfoil design.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242630/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For generation, the model receives 11 PARSEC physical parameters (leading edge radius, upper/lower crest positions and curvatures, trailing edge position, thickness, and two trailing edge angles) plus a set of control keypoints (approximately 1/20 of the 257 surface points) and outputs a 257-point 2D airfoil contour. For editing, the input is a source airfoil paired with either target physical parameters or target keypoints, and the output is a modified 257-point airfoil that satisfies the new condition while preserving the unchanged constraint. All airfoils are uniformly represented as 257 coordinate points via B-spline interpolation, and aerodynamic labels are computed under 66 work conditions (Mach 0.2–0.7, Cl 0–2).

## Method

The paper introduces a family of conditional generative architectures (PK-VAE, PK-GAN, PKVAE-GAN, PK-DIFF, PK-DiT) that embed PARSEC parameters and control keypoints as geometric constraints at multiple scales within VAE, GAN, and diffusion backbones. PK-VAE² extends this to editing by composing an EK-VAE (keypoint→parameter predictor) and an EP-VAE (parameter→keypoint predictor) with the base PK-VAE, enabling bidirectional editing of either modality. PK-DIT operates in a VAE-encoded latent space using DiT transformer blocks with MLP-mapped time steps and conditions, while PK-DIFF uses a U-Net on raw coordinates. The AF-200K dataset is constructed by an automatic data engine that combines CST-parameterized supercritical airfoils perturbed via Latin Hypercube Sampling, unconditional BezierGAN and diffusion-generated shapes, and CFD-annotated labels from XFoil, followed by RANS-based filtering of non-convergent airfoils.

## Results

On the AF-200K dataset, PK-DIT achieves the best mean label error (σ̄ = 99.7×0.01), highest diversity score (D = −93.2), and lowest smoothness error (M = 1.04×0.01) among all tested methods. PK-DIFF (U-Net-based) follows with σ̄ = 120.6×0.01 and M = 1.52×0.01, while the strongest non-diffusion method, PKVAE-GAN, reaches σ̄ = 163.3×0.01. Ablation across datasets confirms that scaling from UIUC (1,600) to Supercritical (21,500) to AF-200K (200,000) monotonically improves all three metrics for PK-DIT (σ̄: 345.6 → 125.0 → 99.7).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper benchmarks against two classical conditional generative baselines: CVAE (Kingma & Welling, 2013) and CGAN (Mirza & Osindero, 2014), both evaluated on AF-200K. PK-DIT on AF-200K outperforms CVAE by a factor of ~4.2× in mean label error (99.7 vs. 413.1) and by ~6.8× in smoothness (1.04 vs. 7.09), while also achieving substantially higher diversity (−93.2 vs. −155.4). Against CGAN, the gap is even wider: PK-DIT reduces label error by ~7.6× (99.7 vs. 759.6) and smoothness error by ~7× (1.04 vs. 7.31). Among the proposed PK variants, the diffusion-based methods (PK-DIFF, PK-DIT) consistently dominate the VAE- and GAN-based counterparts on all three metrics, with PK-DIT (latent-space DiT) edging out PK-DIFF (raw-data U-Net) on both label error and smoothness. The dataset scaling ablation further shows that even the weakest baseline (CGAN) benefits from AF-200K over smaller datasets, but the relative advantage of diffusion architectures grows with dataset size.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on three methodological lineages: parametric VAEs with geometric constraints (Xie et al., 2024), Bezier-curve-based GANs for airfoil synthesis (Chen et al., 2020), and denoising diffusion probabilistic models (Ho et al., 2020), adapting each to incorporate PARSEC physical parameters and control keypoints as multimodal conditions. Conceptually, it departs from prior airfoil inverse design work in two ways: scaling the training corpus from thousands (UIUC, NACA) to 200K airfoils via a CST-perturbation and unconditional generative data engine, and reframing the task from single-condition aerodynamic generation to multi-condition geometric+aerodynamic generation plus a new progressive editing task. The PK-VAE² composite architecture, which chains EK-VAE and EP-VAE with PK-VAE for editing, is a novel compositional design not present in prior airfoil literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AFBench is the first large-scale, open-source benchmark for data-driven airfoil inverse design, filling a gap that has limited reproducibility and cross-method comparison in this niche. The inclusion of 2,150 manually designed supercritical airfoils from COMAC engineers and the introduction of the editing task directly address industrial workflows where airfoils undergo years of progressive refinement. Key open questions remain: the aerodynamic labels rely on the relatively coarse XFoil solver, the 11 PARSEC parameters are coupled (changing one may yield infeasible shapes), and no shape optimization is integrated into the generation loop. Extending to 3D airfoils and decoupling conflicting multi-condition constraints are identified as important future directions.
