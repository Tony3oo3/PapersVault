## Problem

The implicit bias of gradient descent is well-characterized for generalization (max-margin solutions) but has rarely been analyzed for its effect on adversarial robustness. Prior work by Frei et al. showed a generalization-robustness tradeoff but ignored architectural factors and could not extend beyond two layers. This paper asks whether adjacent layers in a neural network actively collaborate to resist adversarial perturbations during gradient descent, or whether the optimization process itself undermines such collaboration.

## Input / Output

The method consumes neural network weights initialized from N(0, 1/m^(1+2q)) with q > 0, L2-normalized input images (MNIST 28×28 grayscale, CIFAR-10 32×32 RGB), and a specified network architecture (linear, 2-layer MLP with widths 2^4–2^13, ResNet-50, or Wide-ResNet-50). It produces the co-correlation metric between adjacent layers, the Dirichlet energy as a per-layer and whole-network robustness proxy, and their temporal trajectories under gradient descent. Co-correlation is estimated via Power Iteration on Jacobian-vector products (implemented with PyTorch's Functorch), avoiding explicit Jacobian construction and enabling parallel computation over the full test set.

## Method

The paper introduces co-correlation, defined as the ratio of the L2-operator-norm of the composed Jacobian J_{φ∘ψ}(x) to the product of individual layer Jacobian norms ‖J_φ(ψ)‖₂·‖J_ψ(x)‖₂, quantifying feature-selection alignment between adjacent layers. A robustness decomposition theorem (Theorem 4.5) expresses the full-network Dirichlet energy as co-correlation × linear-correlation × variance × per-layer Dirichlet energies, with experiments confirming co-correlation dominates. The central theoretical result (Theorems 5.3, 5.5) proves that under Gaussian initialization with variance 1/m^(1+2q) and unit-norm inputs, co-correlation grows monotonically during gradient descent, with accumulation rate inversely proportional to the operator norm of the weight matrix. This implies gradient descent's implicit bias drives layers toward feature alignment, increasing Dirichlet energy and thereby reducing adversarial robustness. A striking width-dependent phenomenon emerges: narrow networks (width ≤ 32) improve accuracy by fostering co-correlation, while wide networks (width ≥ 512) achieve robustness precisely by training with less reliance on inter-layer alignment.

## Results

On MNIST, the paper validates that co-correlation ϱ increases monotonically during gradient descent across MLP widths from 2⁴ to 2¹³, with the increase being most pronounced for wider networks (width 8192) and larger initialization parameters (q=0.25). Lower Dirichlet energy 𝔖(f) consistently corresponds to higher robust accuracy under L₂ Auto-attack (ε=0.5). A key quantitative finding: when weight initialization is adjusted to drive co-correlation from 0.25 to 0.83, test accuracy drops from 0.93 to 0.68. The best robustness is achieved by MLPs with width 8192 and the smallest initialization (q=−0.15), while narrow networks (width 16) paradoxically improve performance as co-correlation increases.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is primarily a theoretical analysis paper without traditional method-vs-method baseline comparisons. The empirical validation compares different architectural configurations (widths 32, 512, 2048, 8192) and weight initialization settings (q from −0.15 to 0.25) on MNIST, and ResNet-50 vs. Wide-ResNet-50 on CIFAR-10. The key comparative finding is that narrow networks (width ≤ 32) benefit from increased inter-layer co-correlation during training, while wide networks (width ≥ 512) achieve better adversarial robustness precisely because they rely less on such co-correlation. The paper challenges Huang et al. [18]'s claim that wider networks do not necessarily yield better adversarial robustness, showing that under controlled initialization, wider networks are more robust. The Dirichlet energy framework extends Dohmatob and Bietti [8], who only compared it to the Lipschitz constant, by formally linking it to the gap between natural and adversarial risk (Theorem 4.1).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Dirichlet energy framework from Dohmatob and Bietti [8]—who showed it outperforms the Lipschitz constant as a robustness measure—by decomposing it across layers to define a new 'co-correlation' metric capturing inter-layer alignment. It builds on Frei et al. [13], who demonstrated that gradient descent's implicit bias improves generalization while degrading adversarial robustness in two-layer ReLU networks, but generalizes the analysis to quantify *how* layers interact during training rather than treating the network as a monolith. The work also connects to the broader implicit bias literature (Chizat & Bach, Lyu & Li, Ji & Telgarsky) on max-margin convergence, but redirects the analysis toward adversarial vulnerability rather than generalization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a mechanistic explanation for why gradient descent erodes adversarial robustness: layers progressively align their feature selections (increasing co-correlation), which amplifies sensitivity to perturbations. The width-dependent finding—that narrow networks actively exploit co-correlation to improve accuracy while wide networks do not—offers a principled lens on the width-robustness relationship and partially challenges Huang et al.'s claim that width alone doesn't guarantee robustness. However, the theoretical results are restricted to two-layer MLPs with specific initialization assumptions, and whether the co-correlation dynamics hold in deeper, more complex architectures (beyond the empirical ResNet/WRN checks) remains an open question. The practical takeaway is limited: the paper identifies a diagnostic metric but does not propose a training procedure to counteract the co-correlation buildup.
