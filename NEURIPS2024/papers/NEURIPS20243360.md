## Problem

Vision foundation models (CLIP, DINOv2, etc.) possess strong global and local image understanding from large-scale pre-training, yet their application to object detection without fine-tuning has been largely overlooked. Existing approaches that use foundation models as backbones suffer from architecture constraints, the need for heavy necks/heads, and expensive fine-tuning of large models. The paper asks whether a frozen foundation model can instead serve as a lightweight, plug-and-play feature enhancer that injects high-level semantic understanding into a standard query-based detector without modifying either the detector's structure or the foundation model's weights.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image and produces standard detection outputs (bounding boxes with class labels) from a DETR-style decoder. A key design detail is the asymmetric input resolution: the frozen foundation model receives a much smaller image (e.g., 336×336 for CLIP ViT-L-14) while the detector backbone processes a larger one (e.g., 1333×1333), keeping the additional computation burden modest. The foundation model runs in parallel with the backbone and is entirely frozen during training, so no gradient flows through it.

## Method

Frozen-DETR integrates a frozen foundation model into a query-based detector via two complementary mechanisms. First, the class token from the foundation model is projected and concatenated with object queries in each decoder self-attention layer as an 'image query' that provides a compact global scene context; after the self-attention computation, the image query is discarded. Second, the patch tokens from the foundation model's last layer are reshaped into a 2D feature map (e.g., 24×24) and concatenated with the backbone's multi-scale feature maps before the encoder, where deformable attention adaptively fuses them; the patch tokens are then discarded after fusion. To provide local context, the image can be split into sub-images, each with its own image query extracted via replicated class tokens with masked attention (a single forward pass). The approach is architecture-agnostic (any ViT, CNN, or hybrid foundation model works), requires no modification to the detector's training recipe, and can be plugged into multiple DETR variants (DINO, Co-DINO, MS-DETR, etc.) without changing hyperparameters.

## Results

On COCO minival with R50 backbone and 12 training epochs, Frozen-DETR(DINO-det-4scale) achieves 51.9% AP, improving over the DINO-det-4scale baseline (49.0% AP) by +2.9% AP. With 24 epochs, the gap widens to 53.2% AP vs. 50.4% AP. Combining two foundation models (CLIP + DINOv2) further boosts DINO-det to 53.8% AP (+4.8% AP). On the large-vocabulary LVIS v1 dataset, Frozen-DETR achieves 41.0% AP vs. 34.4% AP for DINO-det-4scale (+6.6% AP), and on the domain-shifted COCO-O benchmark it reaches 50.2% mAP compared to 22.5% mAP for the baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Frozen-DETR consistently outperforms its direct baselines across all tested detectors: DINO-det-4scale (49.0→51.9 AP, +2.9), DAB-DETR-DC5 (38.0→42.0, +4.0), DN-DETR-DC5 (41.7→44.4, +2.7), MS-DETR (50.0→53.0, +3.0), DDQ with HPR (52.4→55.7, +3.3), and Co-DINO-4scale (52.1→54.0, +1.9). Notably, Frozen-DETR(DINO-det-4scale) at 12 epochs (51.9 AP) surpasses DINO-det-5scale at 24 epochs (51.3 AP) while using only 400 vs. 860 GFLOPs, demonstrating that the foundation model's semantic enrichment is more effective than simply adding another feature pyramid level. On the LVIS large-vocabulary benchmark, Frozen-DETR (41.0 AP) outperforms DINO-det-4scale (34.4 AP) by 6.6 AP and also beats open-vocabulary-specific methods like VLDet (33.4 AP) and DK-DETR (33.5 AP) on rare-class AP (24.0 vs. 22.9 and 22.2). On the COCO-O domain generalization benchmark, Frozen-DETR achieves 50.2 mAP versus 22.5 mAP for DINO-det-4scale and 17.1 mAP for DETR, a more than 2× improvement, while even surpassing ViTDet with ViT-H backbone (34.3 mAP). The gains are most pronounced for large objects (APl: 63.0→69.3, +6.3) and rare classes on LVIS (+8.7 AP), confirming that the foundation model's global scene understanding primarily benefits classification over localization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm of using vision foundation models as (frozen or fine-tuned) backbones for object detection (ViTDet, ViT-Adapter, and frozen-backbone approaches) by repurposing them as plug-and-play feature enhancers that run in parallel with the detector's own backbone. Specifically, it builds on DINO's query-based detection architecture and CLIP's/DINOv2's pre-trained representations, introducing two novel mechanisms: the class token as a global 'image query' concatenated with object queries in the decoder, and patch tokens as an additional low-resolution feature map fused in the encoder. The key conceptual departure is the asymmetric input size (336 for the foundation model vs. 1333 for the backbone), which avoids the architecture mismatch and computational burden of using a large ViT as a backbone while still inheriting its high-level semantic understanding.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play paradigm removes the architecture constraint and fine-tuning cost that have limited the adoption of large foundation models in detection, making it straightforward to enhance any existing query-based detector. The striking domain generalization results (nearly 2× mAP on COCO-O vs. baselines) suggest that frozen foundation models encode domain-invariant semantics that transfer without any adaptation, opening a path to robust detection under distribution shift. Limitations include the added inference latency (6.5 FPS vs. 9.7 FPS baseline), the reliance on foundation models pre-trained on natural images (limiting applicability to medical or satellite imagery), and the open question of whether distilling the enhanced detector into a standard architecture can recover the speed while retaining the gains.
