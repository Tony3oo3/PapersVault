## Problem

Existing distributed minimax methods under second-order similarity face a fundamental trade-off: full-participation methods (SMMDS, EGS) achieve optimal communication rounds O(δD²/ε) but incur O(nδD²/ε) communication complexity, while partial-participation methods (TPAPP) reduce communication volume at the cost of inflated communication rounds O(nδ²D²/ε). No prior method simultaneously achieves near-optimal bounds on all three metrics—communication rounds, communication complexity, and local gradient calls—under the second-order similarity assumption.

## Input / Output

The method operates in a client-server framework where the global objective f = (1/n)Σf_i is decomposed into a server-resident function f_1 and n−1 client-resident functions f_2,…,f_n, each defined over convex constraint sets X⊆ℝ^{d_x} and Y⊆ℝ^{d_y} with diameter D. Each communication round samples a mini-batch of b=⌈√n⌉ clients to compute a variance-reduced optimistic gradient estimate, and the server solves a local sub-problem (a strongly-convex-strongly-concave minimax with added quadratic regularization) to produce the next iterate. The output is an averaged iterate u_avg^K achieving E[Gap]≤ε, or equivalently a point with small gradient mapping norm E[‖F_τ(z)‖²]≤ε, with complexity depending on the second-order similarity parameter δ rather than the full smoothness L.

## Method

SVOGS reformulates the objective as f_1(x,y)+g(x,y) where g=f−f_1 is δ-smooth under second-order similarity, then applies gradient sliding by iteratively solving a sub-problem built from a quadratic approximation of g whose linear term uses the optimistic gradient (F(z^k)+F(z^k)−F(z^{k−1})). The key innovation is replacing the exact optimistic gradient with a stochastic variance-reduced estimator that samples a mini-batch S^k of b=⌈√n⌉ clients, combines a snapshot-based correction (F_j(z^k)−F_j(w^{k−1})) with a momentum term α(F_j(z^k)−F_j(z^{k−1})), and updates the snapshot w^k with probability p=1/(√n+8). This mini-batch design with b=Θ(√n) simultaneously balances the three complexity metrics: communication rounds remain O(δD²/ε) (matching full-participation methods), communication complexity drops to O(n+√n·δD²/ε) (matching partial-participation methods), and local gradient calls achieve Õ(n+(√n·δ+L)D²/ε·log(1/ε)). The convergence proof relies on a Lyapunov function incorporating distance to the solution, cross-terms from the optimistic gradient, and snapshot tracking, yielding linear convergence in the strongly-convex-strongly-concave case.

## Results

SVOGS achieves near-optimal complexity for distributed convex-concave minimax optimization under second-order similarity: O(δD²/ε) communication rounds, O(n + √n·δD²/ε) communication complexity, and Õ(n + (√n·δ + L)D²/ε · log(1/ε)) local gradient calls, all matching the corresponding lower bounds (Theorems 3–5). For the strongly-convex-strongly-concave case, the bounds improve to O(δ/μ · log(1/ε)) rounds, O((n + √n·δ/μ) · log(1/ε)) communication, and Õ((n + (√n·δ + L)/μ) · log(1/ε)) gradient calls. Empirically, on robust regression (a9a, w8a, covtype with n=500 nodes), SVOGS outperforms all baselines in local gradient complexity and requires significantly fewer communication rounds and communication volume than full-participation methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SVOGS dominates all five baselines across the three complexity metrics. Compared to EG [24], SVOGS replaces the smoothness parameter L with the (typically much smaller) second-order similarity δ in communication rounds, and reduces communication complexity from O(nLD²/ε) to O(n + √n·δD²/ε) by using mini-batch sampling of size √n instead of full participation. Against SMMDS [5] and EGS [25], SVOGS ties on communication rounds (O(δD²/ε)) but improves communication complexity from O(nδD²/ε) to O(n + √n·δD²/ε) and local gradient calls from O(nδD²/ε) to Õ(n + √n·δD²/ε), a √n-factor gain when n is large. Against TPAPP [8] (partial participation), SVOGS achieves O(δD²/ε) rounds versus TPAPP's O(nδ²D²/ε), a significant improvement in rounds, while also matching or beating TPAPP on communication complexity and local gradient calls. Against OMASHA [4] (permutation compression), SVOGS achieves O(δ/μ · log(1/ε)) rounds in the strongly-convex-strongly-concave case versus OMASHA's O(L/μ · log(1/ε)), again benefiting from the second-order similarity assumption. Empirically on a9a and w8a, SVOGS consistently requires fewer local gradient evaluations and less total communication than all baselines, confirming the theoretical advantages.

## Relation to Prior Work

SVOGS sits at the intersection of three research threads: gradient sliding for minimax problems (Lan 2016; Beznosikov et al. 2021 SMMDS; Kovalev et al. 2022 EGS), optimistic gradient methods (Popov 1980 OGDA), and variance-reduced stochastic optimization with momentum (Allen-Zhu 2018 Katyusha). The key departure from prior work is the use of mini-batch client sampling with b = O(√n) to simultaneously balance communication rounds, communication complexity, and local gradient calls. Full-participation methods (SMMDS, EGS) achieve optimal rounds but require all n nodes to communicate every round, while partial-participation methods (TPAPP) reduce per-round cost at the expense of more rounds; SVOGS occupies the intermediate regime that no prior method had analyzed for minimax problems under second-order similarity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first distributed minimax algorithm that simultaneously achieves (near-)optimal bounds on all three complexity measures—communication rounds, communication complexity, and local gradient calls—under second-order similarity, with matching lower bounds. This resolves a tradeoff that previously forced practitioners to choose between parallel communication efficiency (full participation) and low per-round communication cost (partial participation). The authors note as future work extending these ideas to nonconvex minimax optimization under second-order similarity, which remains open. The practical impact is most relevant to federated or distributed settings where the objective has finite-sum structure and local functions exhibit Hessian homogeneity.
