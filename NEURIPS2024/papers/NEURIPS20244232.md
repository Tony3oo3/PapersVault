## Problem

Counterfactual image generation requires edits that respect causal relationships in the data-generating process, yet evaluating such methods is substantially harder than standard image editing because ground truths are unobservable and causal constraints must be verified. Although several Deep-SCM-based generative models and evaluation metrics have been proposed, no prior work provides a comprehensive, unified comparison of these methods across diverse datasets, causal graph structures, and multiple quality dimensions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20244232/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework takes a factual image (32×32 synthetic digits, 64×64 face photos, or 192×192 brain MRI slices), its associated attribute values, a known causal DAG, and a do-operator intervention on one or more parent variables, and produces a single counterfactual image reflecting the hypothetical change. Causal graphs range from simple (two disentangled attributes on CelebA) to complex (four interrelated attributes including age, gender, beard, and baldness), and the same factual image can be queried under multiple interventions to assess compositional consistency.

## Method

The benchmark unifies three invertible mechanism families under the Deep-SCM Abduction-Action-Prediction paradigm: Conditional Normalising Flows (invertible by design) for attribute mechanisms, Conditional VAEs and Hierarchical VAEs (amortized explicit via reparametrization) for image mechanisms, and Conditional GANs (amortized implicit via adversarial training). Each model is trained independently per SCM variable, and counterfactual inference proceeds by independently abducting posterior noise for all non-intervened variables, replacing intervened variables with constants, and propagating forward through the modified structural assignments. Evaluation employs four complementary metric families: Composition (null-intervention reconstruction via ℓ₁ and LPIPS over 1 and 10 cycles), Effectiveness (anti-causal ResNet-18 predictors measuring whether the intervened attribute actually changed), Realism (FID against the factual distribution), and Minimality (Counterfactual Latent Divergence measuring proximity to the factual in latent space). The key empirical finding is that HVAE's hierarchical latent structure provides superior noise abduction fidelity, consistently outperforming VAE and GAN across most datasets and metrics, with the advantage growing as data complexity increases.

## Results

HVAE consistently outperforms VAE and GAN across most datasets and metrics. On MorphoMNIST, HVAE achieves a FID of 5.36 vs. VAE (10.12) and GAN (35.57), and a 1-cycle LPIPS composition score of 0.006 vs. VAE (0.026) and GAN (0.049). On ADNI brain MRIs, HVAE achieves a FID of 74.70 vs. VAE (278.25) and GAN (113.75), and a 1-cycle LPIPS of 0.101 vs. VAE (0.306) and GAN (0.268). For effectiveness on MorphoMNIST, HVAE achieves the lowest thickness MAE (0.086) and intensity MAE (1.99) under do(thickness) interventions, compared to VAE (0.109, 3.15) and GAN (0.228, 9.43).

## Baselines & Comparisons

HVAE (De Sousa Ribeiro et al., ICML 2023) dominates the benchmark, achieving the best FID on MorphoMNIST (5.36 vs. 10.12 VAE, 35.57 GAN), CelebA-simple (22.05 vs. 66.41 VAE, 31.56 GAN), and ADNI (74.70 vs. 278.25 VAE, 113.75 GAN), as well as the best LPIPS composition scores across all three datasets. VAE (Pawlowski et al., NeurIPS 2020) is a consistent second-place performer, maintaining structural fidelity but producing blurrier reconstructions than HVAE. GAN (Dash et al., WACV 2022) is generally the weakest model, with the highest FID on MorphoMNIST and the worst effectiveness scores (e.g., digit accuracy of 0.451 vs. 0.972 for HVAE on MorphoMNIST). The one notable exception is CelebA with the complex causal graph, where GAN achieves the best FID (27.86 vs. HVAE 35.71), though at the cost of identity and background preservation. The adapted diffusion model (Sanchez et al., MICCAI Workshop 2022) underperforms all three families, with FID of 29.62 on CelebA-simple and poor multi-cycle composition, confirming that current diffusion-based counterfactual methods are not yet competitive in the multi-variable SCM setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of the Deep-SCM counterfactual inference paradigm (Pawlowski et al., 2020) and the axiomatic evaluation framework for counterfactual images (Monteiro et al., 2022), unifying them into the first comprehensive benchmark. It systematically compares three model families—Conditional VAEs, Hierarchical VAEs (De Sousa Ribeiro et al., 2023), and Conditional GANs (Dash et al., 2022)—all operating under the Abduction-Action-Prediction procedure, while incorporating the CLD minimality metric from Sanchez & Tsaftaris (2022). The paper departs from prior work by extending HVAE and GAN to previously untested causal graphs (complex CelebA, ADNI brain MRIs) and by including a preliminary diffusion-model baseline, though it explicitly excludes backtracking counterfactuals and deep twin networks as out of scope.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This benchmark fills a clear gap: no prior work offered a unified, multi-dataset, multi-metric comparison of all published Deep-SCM counterfactual image generation methods, making it difficult for the community to assess relative strengths. The consistent superiority of HVAE across composition, effectiveness, and realism metrics provides a clear direction for future method development, while the identified failure to scale beyond 64×64/192×192 resolutions highlights a fundamental limitation of the current Abduction-Action-Prediction pipeline. Key open questions include adapting diffusion models to multi-variable SCM conditioning (current approaches only handle single-attribute graphs) and developing resolution-independent noise abduction mechanisms that do not rely on classifier-guided counterfactual fine-tuning.
