## Problem

Conventional graph homophily metrics (edge, node) quantify node similarity solely as label consistency across topology, ignoring the structural and feature information that are equally fundamental components of graph data. Recent empirical studies show that GNN performance does not reliably align with these label-only metrics, leaving the actual drivers of GNN behavior unclear. The paper asks: what is missing in our current understanding of graph homophily?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242184/cropped_page2_idx6.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a graph with adjacency matrix, node labels, and node features, and produces three disentangled homophily scores (label h_L, structural h_S, feature h_F) plus a composite Tri-Hom scalar for both graph-aware (J_h^G) and graph-agnostic (J_h^{-G}) models. Feature homophily estimation requires solving an optimization that minimizes intra-class distances of inferred structural-agnostic features, and is provably invariant to feature shifts, scaling, and variance changes. The Tri-Hom score is computed directly from the graph without training any model, making it a lightweight diagnostic for predicting GNN performance.

## Method

The paper disentangles graph homophily into three aspects: label homophily h_L (existing), structural homophily h_S (consistency of neighbor class distributions within each class, measured via standard deviation), and feature homophily h_F (degree of feature dependencies across topology, modeled via a linear graph diffusion process X = (I − h_F/ρ(A)·A)^{−1}X(0) that breaks the i.i.d. feature assumption). To study their joint impact, the authors propose CSBM-3H, a Contextual Stochastic Block Model where topology generation is controlled by h_L and h_S through a class-sampling matrix with Gaussian noise, and feature generation propagates structural-agnostic features through the graph with h_F. From theoretical analysis of CSBM-3H using a node distinguishability ratio J (intra-class to inter-class representation distance), they derive Tri-Hom (J_h^G and J_h^{-G}), a composite metric whose partial derivatives with respect to each homophily type yield closed-form theorems. The key insight is that GNN performance is governed by the synergy of all three aspects rather than any single one, which explains previously puzzling phenomena such as 'good' heterophily, the mid-homophily pitfall, and the effect of feature shuffling.

## Results

On 31 real-world datasets, Tri-Hom (J_h^G) achieves the highest Pearson correlation with node classification accuracy across all four models: 0.5471 (MLP), 0.6650 (GCN), 0.6223 (GraphSage), and 0.6731 (GAT), all with p-values ≤ 0.0014. Its average rank across models is 1.50, significantly outperforming the best single-aspect metrics: h_class (label-based, rank 2.50, correlations 0.58–0.62) and h_agg (structural-based, rank 3.75, correlations 0.52–0.60). The Tri-Hom variant for graph-agnostic models (J_h^{-G}) also ranks highly (average rank 2.50), confirming the presence of structural-aware features in real-world graphs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Tri-Hom (J_h^G) outperforms all 17 existing single-aspect homophily metrics in Pearson correlation with GNN performance across all four models (MLP, GCN, GraphSage, GAT), achieving an average rank of 1.50 versus 2.50 for the best label-based metric (h_class) and 3.75 for the best structural-based metric (h_agg). The gap is most pronounced for graph-aware models: J_h^G reaches 0.6650 for GCN and 0.6731 for GAT, exceeding h_class (0.6120/0.6169) by ~5–6 percentage points. Among feature-based metrics, the proposed h_F (rank 8.50) substantially outperforms prior feature homophily measures like h_GE (rank 11.75), h_LS-cos (rank 13.00), and h_CF (rank 15.25), while classifier-based metrics (h_KR, h_GNB) show negative correlations, indicating they are poorly aligned with actual GNN performance. The structural homophily h_S alone (rank 17.50) shows weak correlation, underscoring that no single aspect suffices—only their combination in Tri-Hom captures the full picture. The graph-agnostic Tri-Hom variant J_h^{-G} (rank 2.50) also beats all single-aspect metrics, validating the CSBM-3H assumption that real-world node features are structurally aware (X ⊥̸ A | Y).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the CSBM-based analysis of GNN performance initiated by Luan et al. [39] (node distinguishability via the J metric) and Ma et al. [46] (good vs. bad heterophily), both of which controlled only label homophily and assumed node features were conditionally independent of topology given labels. It departs from this line by introducing structural homophily (neighbor-distribution consistency within classes) and feature homophily (topology-dependent feature coupling via a graph diffusion process borrowed from GRAND [6] and interactive particle systems [55]), thereby breaking the i.i.d. feature assumption. The resulting CSBM-3H generative model and the derived Tri-Hom composite metric represent a conceptual unification of previously fragmented homophily metrics spanning label, structural, and feature aspects.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled explanation for long-standing puzzles—why GNNs can succeed under heterophily, why performance is worst at intermediate label homophily (h_L = 1/C), and why feature shuffling helps—by showing these arise from the synergy of all three homophily types rather than label homophily alone. Tri-Hom's consistently highest Pearson correlation with model accuracy across 31 datasets and four architectures offers practitioners a single diagnostic metric that subsumes 17 prior single-aspect metrics. Open questions include extending the theory beyond uniform degrees, balanced classes, and linear feature dependencies; estimating the three homophily components in unsupervised or label-scarce settings; and leveraging the disentangled signals for class-specific or feature-aware GNN architecture design.
