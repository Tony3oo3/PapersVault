## Problem

Online strategic classification lacked an instance-optimal characterization of the optimal mistake bound, even in the basic realizable setting. Previous bounds parametrized by the classical Littlestone dimension and the manipulation graph's maximum out-degree were not tight for all instances (e.g., they could be infinite while the true optimal bound was finite). Additionally, no positive results existed for the agnostic or unknown-graph settings when the learner only observes post-manipulation features rather than original ones.

## Input / Output

The learner receives a hypothesis class H and a manipulation graph G (or a graph class G in the unknown-graph setting), and at each round commits to a classifier before observing the agent's post-manipulation feature vector v_t and true label y_t. Crucially, the original feature x_t is never observed, creating an information asymmetry: false-negative mistakes reveal the original node, but false-positive mistakes only reveal the manipulated node, leaving the original node ambiguous among its in-neighbors. The output is a sequence of binary predictions h_t(v_t) with regret or mistake bounds characterized by the Strategic Littlestone Dimension SLdim(H, G).

## Method

The paper introduces the Strategic Littlestone Dimension (SLdim), a combinatorial measure built on a generalized shattered-tree structure where each node branches into one false-negative edge and multiple false-positive edges (one per out-neighbor), with a consistency rule that accounts for the learner's partial information about original features. The Strategic Standard Optimal Algorithm (SSOA) achieves the instance-optimal bound by maintaining a version space and labeling each node positive only if doing so would decrease the SLdim of the version space, ensuring a 'progress on mistakes' property. For the agnostic setting, the paper constructs a finite set of representative experts that 'guess' every possible manipulation direction (via in-neighbor indices), then applies the Biased Weighted Majority Vote algorithm to handle the partial-information challenge. For unknown manipulation graphs, the expert set is further expanded by a factor of |G| (one SSOA instance per candidate graph), and the Biased Weighted Majority Vote is run under the union graph, which serves as a pessimistic supergraph of the true graph.

## Results

The paper establishes that the Strategic Littlestone Dimension SLdim(H,G) exactly characterizes the minmax optimal mistake bound in the realizable setting (Theorem 3.1: M(H,G) = SLdim(H,G)), achieved by the SSOA algorithm. In the agnostic setting, the regret bound is O(Δ⁺_G · OPT + Δ⁺_G · SLdim(H,G) · (log T + log Δ⁻_G)) (Theorem 4.1). For unknown manipulation graphs, the realizable graph class setting yields regret O(Δ⁺_G · (OPT_H + d_G · (log T + log Δ⁻_G) + log|G|)) (Theorem 5.1), and the agnostic graph class setting yields O(Δ⁺_G · (OPT_H + (d_G + N) · (log T + log Δ⁻_G) + log|G|)) (Theorem D.2).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's main comparison is against two prior theoretical bounds: O(Δ⁺_G · log|H|) from Ahmadi et al. [2023] and Õ(Δ⁺_G · Ldim(H)) from Cohen et al. [2024]. The authors demonstrate that the gap between these bounds and SLdim(H,G) can be arbitrarily large. For a complete graph K_N on an unbounded domain, SLdim(H,G) = 1 while both previous bounds are infinite. For graphs with a large clique of size N attached to a subgraph G', the gap is at least N/Δ⁺_{G'}, which grows without bound. For random graphs G(n,p) with p = ω(1/n), the previous bound is Ω(n·log(np)) while SLdim(H,G) ≤ n, yielding a gap of Ω(np) = ω(1). Additionally, in the unknown-graph setting, the paper is the first to provide positive results when the learner only observes post-manipulation features, whereas Cohen et al. [2024] assume original features are observable.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the classical Littlestone Dimension (Littlestone, 1988) to the strategic classification setting by introducing the Strategic Littlestone Dimension, which captures the joint complexity of a hypothesis class and a manipulation graph. It builds directly on the manipulation graph model of Zhang and Conitzer (2021) and the Biased Weighted Majority Vote algorithm of Ahmadi et al. (2023), while adapting the agnostic-to-realizable reduction framework of Ben-David et al. (2009) to handle the partial-information challenge of observing only post-manipulation features. The key departure from prior work (Ahmadi et al. 2023, Cohen et al. 2024) is that those bounds are parameterized by the classical Littlestone dimension and graph out-degree separately, whereas this paper's instance-optimal bound can be arbitrarily tighter, and the paper additionally handles unknown manipulation graphs where prior online results required observing original features.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The instance-optimal characterization via SLdim closes a gap that had persisted even in the basic realizable setting, providing a complete and tight understanding of learnability for online strategic classification. The extension to unknown manipulation graphs (both realizable and agnostic) is practically significant since in real deployments the exact manipulation structure is rarely known. Open questions include tightening the agnostic bound (which carries an extra Δ_G^+ factor from the expert-advice subroutine), characterizing the optimal regret for randomized learners (where an exponential gap from deterministic algorithms is shown to exist), and understanding whether the SLdim framework extends to multiclass or cost-sensitive settings.
