## Problem

Directly trained spiking transformers are limited to straight-through architectures because Spiking Self Attention (SSA) has quadratic space complexity in the number of tokens, making hierarchical multi-scale designs infeasible without memory explosion. This architectural constraint, combined with the high computational cost of processing data across the time domain, keeps a substantial performance gap between SNNs and ANNs (e.g., 74.81% vs. 85%+ on ImageNet).
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240416/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For static image datasets (ImageNet-1K, CIFAR-10/100), the input is a single RGB frame (T₀=1, n=3) at 224×224, 288×288, or 384×384 resolution; for neuromorphic datasets (DVS128 Gesture, CIFAR10-DVS), the input is a temporal event stream (T₀=T, n=2) with 10 or 16 time steps. The input is split into 4×4 patches and encoded into spike form, and the model outputs a single class label (1000, 10, 100, or 11 classes respectively) after a final fully connected layer.

## Method

QKFormer introduces a spike-form Q-K attention that uses only Query and Key (no Value) as binary spike vectors, reducing complexity from O(N²D) to O(N) or O(D) by computing a 1-D attention vector via row/column summation of Q and applying it as a mask to K. This linear complexity enables a three-stage hierarchical architecture with progressively halved token counts (H/4×W/4 → H/8×W/8 → H/16×W/16) and doubled channels, mixing QKTA in early stages with SSA in the final stage. A Spiking Patch Embedding with Deformed Shortcut (SPEDS) module adds a residual connection across downsampling blocks via a lightweight strided 1×1 convolution, preserving spiking information through resolution changes. The model is trained end-to-end with LIF neurons and sigmoid surrogate gradients (α=4), and the low variance of Q-K attention (≈1/200 of SSA) eliminates the need for a scaling factor, further reducing energy consumption.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K, QKFormer (HST-10-768, 64.96M params) achieves 85.65% top-1 accuracy with 4 time steps at 384² input resolution, and 84.22% at 224² resolution — the first directly-trained SNN to surpass 85% on ImageNet-1K. On CIFAR-100, QKFormer (6.74M params) reaches 81.15% top-1 accuracy with 4 time steps, and on CIFAR10-DVS it achieves 84.0% with 16 time steps using only 1.50M parameters. The model also achieves 98.6% on DVS128 Gesture (16 time steps) and 96.18% on CIFAR-10 (4 time steps).

## Baselines & Comparisons

QKFormer (64.96M, 85.65%) outperforms Spikformer (66.34M, 74.81%) by 10.84% on ImageNet-1K, Spikingformer (66.34M, 75.85%) by 9.80%, and S-Transformer (66.34M, 77.07%) by 8.58% (at 384² input) or 7.55% (at 288² input), all with comparable or fewer parameters. Against ANN baselines, QKFormer surpasses Swin Transformer-B (87.77M, 84.50%) by 1.15%, DeiT-B (86.59M, 83.10%) by 2.55%, and ViT-B/16 (86.59M, 77.90%) by 7.75%, while consuming roughly 2× less energy (113.64 mJ vs. 216–255 mJ). On CIFAR-100, QKFormer (6.74M) beats Spikformer (9.32M, 78.21%) by 2.94% and CML (9.32M, 80.02%) by 1.13%; on CIFAR10-DVS it leads Spikingformer (2.57M, 81.3%) by 2.7% and Spikformer (2.57M, 80.9%) by 3.1% with fewer parameters. On DVS128 Gesture, QKFormer (1.50M, 98.6%) slightly trails S-Transformer (2.57M, 99.3%) by 0.7% but exceeds Spikformer (2.57M, 98.3%) by 0.3%. The ANN-to-SNN baseline MST (28.50M, 78.51%, 512 time steps) is outperformed by QKFormer by 7.14% with only 4 time steps.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the spiking transformer line initiated by Spikformer (ICLR 2023), which introduced Spiking Self Attention (SSA) using spike-form Q, K, V without softmax, and builds on the LIF neuron and residual connection framework from SEW ResNet (NeurIPS 2021). The key departure is replacing SSA's quadratic-complexity Q-K-V computation with a linear-complexity Q-K attention that drops the Value component entirely, using only row/column summation of the Q spike matrix followed by a mask operation on K. This enables a hierarchical architecture with decreasing token counts across stages—something previously infeasible for spiking transformers due to memory explosion—alongside a new SPEDS module that adds deformed residual shortcuts across downsampling blocks, addressing an information-loss gap absent in prior spiking transformers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

QKFormer's 85.65% ImageNet-1K accuracy (first direct-training SNN above 85%) and 10.84% gain over Spikformer substantially narrow the SNN-to-ANN performance gap while maintaining event-driven energy efficiency (113.64 mJ vs. 216.20 mJ for Swin Transformer-B). The linear-complexity Q-K attention and hierarchical design open the door to larger spiking transformer models for dense prediction tasks. However, the model is currently limited to classification, still requires 4 time steps, and training time is ~1.35× that of Spikformer; extending to detection, segmentation, and language tasks, and reducing time steps via pre-training or more efficient modules, remain open challenges.
