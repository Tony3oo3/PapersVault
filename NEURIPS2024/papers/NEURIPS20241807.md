## Problem

Existing data augmentation methods for offline RL face a fundamental tension: traditional noise-injection approaches (e.g., S4RL) can only smooth small local regions of the observed state-action space and cannot discover novel behaviors, while generative methods (e.g., SynthER) produce data whose reward distribution is bounded by the offline dataset's support, yielding suboptimal samples. This paper addresses how to augment offline data with trajectories that are simultaneously high-rewarding, novel, and dynamically plausible—going beyond what the original data supports without violating environment dynamics.

## Input / Output

The method consumes offline trajectory datasets (states, actions, rewards) segmented into subtrajectories of horizon H (typically 32–128 steps), and conditions generation on an amplified return value (α × original discounted return, with α > 1). It outputs augmented subtrajectories that are converted into standard (s, a, r, s') transitions for training any offline RL algorithm. For pixel-based observations, the method operates in the embedding space of a pretrained CNN encoder rather than raw pixels, and the augmented latent states are used to fine-tune the policy. A reweighted sampling strategy optionally biases trajectory selection toward higher-return subtrajectories before augmentation.

## Method

GTA operates in three stages: (1) train a conditional diffusion model (Temporal U-Net with MLP-Mixer blocks under the EDM framework) to approximate p(τ | y(τ)), where y(τ) is the discounted return of a subtrajectory; (2) augment data via a partial noising and denoising framework—apply the diffusion forward process to an original trajectory with a controlled noising ratio μ (0 < μ ≤ 1) to partially erase information, then denoise using classifier-free guidance conditioned on the amplified return α·y(τ⁰); (3) train any offline RL algorithm on the augmented dataset. The key insight is that partial noising (rather than starting from pure Gaussian noise) preserves the original trajectory's dynamic structure while the amplified return guidance gently pushes the denoised trajectory toward higher-reward regions, avoiding the collision between forceful fixed-return conditioning and the pre-existing trajectory context. This design decouples the computational cost of diffusion sampling from decision-making, shifting it entirely to the offline data-preparation stage.

## Results

On D4RL Gym locomotion tasks, GTA achieves the highest average normalized score across all four offline RL algorithms: TD3BC (84.63), CQL (85.27), IQL (86.11), and MCQ (85.91), outperforming the no-augmentation baseline by 6–8 points and SynthER by 3–4 points. On complex robotics tasks (Adroit, FrankaKitchen), GTA with IQL reaches 42.73 and 53.3 average respectively, surpassing SynthER (37.95, 50.85) and the no-augmentation baseline (36.43, 50.23). On pixel-based VD4RL tasks, GTA with DrQ+BC achieves 45.4 average versus 42.7 (no augmentation) and 27.5 (SynthER). Statistical significance is confirmed via Welch's t-test with p-values below 0.05 for all four algorithms.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GTA outperforms S4RL (noise injection) by 6–8 points on average across all four algorithms on locomotion tasks, confirming that simple state perturbation is insufficient for meaningful data enrichment. Against SynthER (unconditional diffusion at transition level), GTA gains 3–4 points on locomotion and 5–6 points on complex robotics (Adroit: 42.73 vs 37.95; FrankaKitchen: 53.3 vs 50.85), with the gap widening on sparse-reward Maze2d tasks where SynthER degrades IQL from 66.50 to 17.13 on medium-play while GTA maintains 66.50. On pixel-based VD4RL tasks, GTA (45.4) substantially outperforms SynthER (27.5), a 17.9-point gap, suggesting trajectory-level generation is critical for visual observations. Compared to diffusion planners (Diffuser, Decision Diffuser, AdaptDiffuser) and model-based offline RL (MOPO, MOReL, COMBO) in Table 26, GTA matches or exceeds their scores on most locomotion tasks (e.g., halfcheetah-medium-expert: GTA 93.13 vs COMBO 90.0; hopper-medium-replay: GTA 98.88 vs COMBO 89.5) while eliminating the expensive test-time sampling cost inherent to planners. The key differentiator is that GTA shifts diffusion computation to the data-preparation stage, enabling plug-and-play integration with any offline RL algorithm without modifying the decision-making loop.

## Relation to Prior Work

GTA builds on the trajectory-level generation paradigm and Temporal U-Net architecture from Diffuser [21], the classifier-free guidance mechanism from Ho & Salimans [23], and the EDM diffusion framework [30], but repurposes them for a fundamentally different purpose: data augmentation rather than online planning. It departs from prior generative augmentation (SynthER [13]) by generating at the trajectory level with return conditioning rather than transition-level from pure noise, and from diffusion planners by shifting the diffusion sampling burden from decision time to a one-time data preparation step. The partial noising strategy—starting from real trajectories rather than Gaussian noise—combined with amplified return guidance (scaling the original return by α > 1 rather than using a fixed target) is the paper's core conceptual departure from both prior augmentation methods and prior diffusion-based RL planners.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GTA provides a practical, orthogonal augmentation strategy that can be plugged into any offline RL algorithm without modification, addressing the data quality bottleneck that regularization-based methods cannot fix. The finding that SynthER degrades performance on sparse-reward tasks while GTA consistently helps suggests that trajectory-level generation with return conditioning is critical for capturing long-term dynamics in challenging environments. Limitations include the absence of an explicit dynamics model (risky in environments where dynamic violations are catastrophic) and the current focus on purely offline settings; the authors note that off-to-online and online extensions remain open. The technique's broad applicability across 31 datasets, 9 environments, and 5+ algorithms (including Decision Transformer and pixel-based DrQ+BC) makes it a potentially widely adopted preprocessing step for offline RL pipelines.
