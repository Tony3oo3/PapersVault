## Problem

Existing emotion analysis datasets and models operate from a third-person perspective, which is misaligned with how embodied agents perceive the world through their own sensors. No large-scale first-person view (FPV) video dataset exists that captures the camera wearer's active emotional participation in daily social interactions, and current video understanding models ignore the acoustic modality that becomes critical when the camera wearer is not visible in frame.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243752/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes 10–30 second FPV video clips (original resolution, MP4), their synchronized audio tracks, and a free-form text query specifying a person and emotion (e.g., 'why is the girl in black afraid'). Outputs are task-specific: a polarity label with integer intensity from −3 to +3 (recognition), one of 8 emotion categories with intensity 1–3 (classification), start and end timestamps in seconds (localization), or a free-text causal explanation (reasoning). The dataset spans Mandarin, Cantonese, English, Japanese, and Korean, with 21,998 clips and over 80k annotations across four tasks.

## Method

Emotion-LlaMa extends MiniGPT4-Video by adding a dedicated audio branch to address the key limitation that FPV camera wearers are frequently absent from the visual frame. Visual features are extracted per-frame via EVA-CLIP and compressed by pooling 4 adjacent tokens into one; audio features are encoded by a pretrained AudioMAE model and projected into the LLM's language space through a learned linear layer. These visual, audio, and subtitle-text tokens are interleaved and fed to a LLaMA-based language model for generation. The model is fine-tuned end-to-end on E³ using supervised learning, with the audio branch providing complementary prosodic cues (pitch, rhythm, crying, laughing) that visual-only baselines cannot access. Ablations confirm that removing either the audio or subtitle modality degrades performance, and that their combination yields the strongest results across all four benchmark tasks.

## Results

On the E³ benchmark, Emotion-LlaMa achieves the best performance across all four embodied emotion analysis tasks. For emotion recognition, it reaches 85.93% accuracy and 93.70 F1, outperforming the strongest MLLM baseline VAST (84.92% Acc, 92.71 F1) and all task-specific models (≤77.11 Acc). For emotion reasoning, it scores 84.06 CIDEr and 2.39 on all four GPT-evaluated dimensions (IC, DO, CU, TUC), surpassing the next-best fine-tuned MINI (81.22 CIDEr, 2.22 IC). For emotion classification, it achieves 45.62 average F1, edging out MINI (44.95) and the best task-specific model CTEN (42.37).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Emotion-LlaMa outperforms all 16 baselines across the four benchmark tasks. Against task-specific emotion models (MMIM, CENET, ALMT for recognition; DFAN, VAANet, CTEN for classification; HCRN, UMT, MGN for reasoning), Emotion-LlaMa achieves substantially higher scores, e.g., 85.93% Acc vs. 77.11% (ALMT) on recognition and 84.06 CIDEr vs. 18.27 (MGN) on reasoning, confirming that MLLM-based approaches are far superior for FPV emotion analysis. Among MLLM baselines, Emotion-LlaMa consistently beats fine-tuned MINI (its closest competitor) by 1.02 Acc on recognition, 0.67 avg-F1 on classification, and 2.84 CIDEr on reasoning, with the audio branch providing the key advantage. VAST, which already incorporates audio, is the second-strongest MLLM baseline but still trails Emotion-LlaMa by 1.01 Acc (recognition) and 8.55 CIDEr (reasoning). MALMM underperforms notably, with its memory bank storing contradictory reference histories that degrade fine-tuned classification (18.10 avg-F1 vs. 35.44 zero-shot). For emotion localization, the paper does not present its own method; the best result is TimeChat fine-tuned (R1@0.3=30.31, mIoU=20.58), which outperforms MSAT and r2-tuning, suggesting that timestamp-aware encoders are critical for this task.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously disjoint research lines: egocentric video datasets (Ego4D, EPIC, Charades-Ego) that capture first-person actions but lack emotional content, and emotion analysis datasets (IEMOCAP, MOSI, MOSEI) that provide rich affective labels but are exclusively third-person. Methodologically, Emotion-LlaMa extends MiniGPT4-Video by appending an AudioMAE-based audio branch, motivated by the observation that camera wearers are frequently absent from the visual frame in FPV videos, making acoustic cues (tone, rhythm) essential for emotion inference. The four benchmark tasks—recognition, classification, localization, and reasoning—collectively extend the standard emotion recognition/classification paradigm by adding temporal grounding and causal explanation in the embodied setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

E³ is the first large-scale FPV emotion dataset (50+ hours, 80k annotations, 8 emotion types including 'hidden' emotions like Shy and Sarcastic, multiple languages), filling a critical gap for training empathetic embodied agents in robotics and AR. The four-task benchmark suite, particularly emotion localization and reasoning, pushes beyond surface-level affect detection toward contextual and causal understanding. Key open questions include whether the vlog-sourced data generalizes to real-time embodied agent deployment, whether the 8-category taxonomy (extending Ekman with two hidden emotions) is sufficient, and how to handle the inherent subjectivity in emotion intensity annotation (mitigated here by 3-annotator averaging). The very low per-class F1 for hidden emotions (~10%) signals that contextual reasoning for subtle affect remains largely unsolved.
