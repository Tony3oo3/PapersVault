## Problem

Federated fine-tuning of LLMs with LoRA suffers from a 'bucket effect': traditional aggregation schemes (e.g., FedAvg) require all clients to share the same LoRA rank, forcing well-resourced clients to downscale to the least-resourced participant's rank. This underutilizes available client resources and produces global models with overly task-specific, poorly generalized knowledge, especially under heterogeneous task and resource distributions across thousands of cross-device clients.

## Input / Output

Each client contributes a composed full-rank LoRA weight matrix W_l^i = s·B_l^i·A_l^i (where the rank r^i varies per client based on its resource budget, ranging from r=8 to r=200 across four configuration types). The server outputs a single aggregated global weight W_g, then redistributes per-client rank-constrained approximations W_g^i by retaining only the top r^i singular vectors from the SVD of W_g. The redistribution is lossy by design—clients with smaller budgets receive lower-rank projections—yet the global model retains the full-rank information for the next aggregation round.

## Method

FlexLoRA replaces the standard element-wise averaging of decomposed LoRA matrices (A, B) with a two-step compose-then-decompose pipeline: (1) each client's local LoRA is first multiplied into a single full matrix W_l^i = s·B_l^i·A_l^i, enabling weighted averaging across heterogeneous ranks without padding or truncation; (2) the resulting global weight W_g is decomposed via SVD (W_g = UΣV^T), and each client i receives the top-r^i approximation W_g^i = U[:,:r^i]·Σ[:r^i,:r^i]·V[:r^i,:]^T, which is then re-split into B_g^i and A_g^i for the next local training round. The scheme is hyperparameter-free, requires only one SVD per communication round (independent of client count), and is plug-and-play with existing LoRA-based FL methods such as FedAvg, FedIT, and SLoRA. A generalization bound derived from Baxter's model shows that increasing per-client rank r^i reduces the SVD approximation error φ^i, while increasing the number of clients |C| improves generalization at rate O(1/|C|), both of which are empirically validated across 1600+ NLP tasks and up to 200 clients.

## Results

On the Natural Instructions dataset (1600+ NLP tasks, 1.3B DataJuicer model), FlexLoRA-integrated FedIT achieves the best zero-shot Rouge-L score of 62.20±1.12 under the Heavy-Tail-Strong resource distribution, compared to 61.29±0.93 for homogeneous-rank FedIT. Across all four resource distributions and three FL baselines, FlexLoRA yields average improvements of 2.14% (FedAvg), 0.86% (FedIT), and 1.94% (SLoRA) over their homogeneous-rank counterparts. On cross-task generalization (119 NLP tasks), FlexLoRA delivers up to 4% improvement in overlap extraction and ~2.5% in textual entailment and cause-effect classification, with an average 1.00% gain over baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FlexLoRA is evaluated as a plug-in aggregation scheme on top of three homogeneous-rank FL baselines (FedAvg, FedIT, SLoRA) and directly compared against HETLORA, a concurrent heterogeneous-rank method. FlexLoRA consistently outperforms HETLORA in all 12 comparisons (3 FL methods × 4 resource distributions), with the largest margin under Heavy-Tail-Strong (e.g., FedAvg: 57.73 vs. 55.74; SLoRA: 61.86 vs. 61.29). Against their own homogeneous-rank versions, FlexLoRA delivers the strongest relative gains on SLoRA (up to +3.09% in Heavy-Tail-Strong) and FedAvg (up to +2.72% in Uniform), while FedIT shows smaller but still positive gains (up to +1.24%). The one notable exception is SLoRA under Heavy-Tail-Light in cross-task evaluation (Table 3), where FlexLoRA yields a −0.47% average, attributed to the predominance of rank-8 clients limiting language processing capacity. Statistical significance tests (Appendix F) confirm that improvements are significant (p < 0.05) in most settings, with the strongest evidence for FedIT under Heavy-Tail-Strong (p < 0.001) and Normal (p = 0.006).

## Relation to Prior Work

FlexLoRA builds directly on LoRA's low-rank matrix decomposition (Hu et al., ICLR 2022) and FedAvg's weighted averaging (McMahan et al., AISTATS 2017), but departs from both by operating on the synthesized full-size LoRA weight (s·B·A) rather than the individual A and B matrices. It contrasts with HETLORA (Cho et al., 2024), a concurrent work that handles heterogeneous LoRA ranks via zero-padding, element-wise averaging, and truncation with sparsity regularization; FlexLoRA replaces this pipeline with a single SVD decomposition and top-r truncation, eliminating all additional hyperparameters. The intrinsic dimension literature (Aghajanyan et al., ACL 2021) provides the conceptual motivation: larger LoRA ranks capture broader, less task-specific solution subspaces, which is precisely what a well-generalized global model needs under heterogeneous data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FlexLoRA offers a plug-and-play, hyperparameter-free aggregation that can be dropped into any existing LoRA-based FL method (FedAvg, FedIT, SLoRA) to unlock underutilized client resources, making it immediately practical for cross-device LLM fine-tuning. The paper's scale—thousands of clients across 1600+ NLP tasks—is the first demonstration of federated LLM tuning in a truly heterogeneous cross-device setting, though the largest model tested (LLaMA-3 8B) was only evaluated with 200 clients, leaving the full-scale combination untested. Open questions include whether the SVD overhead remains negligible at very large model sizes, whether the approach extends cleanly to other PEFT methods (adapters, prefix tuning), and how the method behaves under more extreme data heterogeneity or adversarial client participation.
