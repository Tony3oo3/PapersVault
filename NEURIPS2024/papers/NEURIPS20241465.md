## Problem

DDIM's inversion formula uses a different linear relationship than its forward sampling formula, causing inexact reconstruction when encoding and decoding are composed. While heuristic exact inversion samplers (EDICT, BDIA) resolve this mismatch in a training-free manner, their theoretical properties (stability, convergence, error order) remain uncharacterized and their sampling quality is often compromised. No systematic, theoretically grounded framework existed that simultaneously guarantees mathematically exact inversion and high-order local accuracy.

## Input / Output

The method operates entirely in the latent space of pre-trained diffusion models (Stable Diffusion 1.5/2.0-base at 512×512, CIFAR-10 at 32×32, CelebA-HQ at 256×256) without any retraining. For generation, it takes initial Gaussian noise and optional text prompts; for inversion/editing, it takes an input image and produces the corresponding initial noise (inverted latent) plus the reconstructed or edited image. A practical constraint is that exact inversion requires storing two intermediate states per step (x_i and x_{i+1}) to compute x_{i-1}, which is feasible in editing pipelines but adds memory overhead compared to single-step samplers like DDIM.

## Method

The paper introduces the BELM framework, a generic formulation of exact inversion samplers derived from variable-stepsize-variable-formula (VSVF) linear multi-step methods augmented with a bidirectional explicit constraint: the linear relationship between x_{i-1}, x_i, x_{i+1}, and ε_θ(x_i, i) must exclude derivatives at both endpoints (ε_θ(x_{i-1}, i-1) and ε_θ(x_{i+1}, i+1)), making the formula explicitly solvable in both forward and reverse directions from the same equation. This single unifying relationship guarantees exact inversion by construction (Proposition 2), and the paper shows EDICT and BDIA are special cases within this framework. The Optimal-BELM (O-BELM) sampler is then derived by minimizing the Local Truncation Error via Taylor expansion, yielding timestep-dependent coefficients a_{i,1}=(h_{i+1}²-h_i²)/h_{i+1}², a_{i,2}=h_i²/h_{i+1}², b_{i,1}=-(h_i+h_{i+1})/h_{i+1} that achieve O(α_i(h_i+h_{i+1})³) local error—one order higher than BDIA's O(α_i(h_i+h_{i+1})²). The paper further proves O-BELM is zero-stable (via a contraction-mapping argument on the root matrix) and globally convergent, making it the first theoretically guaranteed exact inversion diffusion sampler.

## Results

On image reconstruction (COCO-14), O-BELM achieves 0.004 MSE at all step counts (10–100), matching the autoencoder lower bound and confirming exact inversion, while DDIM ranges from 0.026 (10 steps) to 0.007 (100 steps). On unconditional generation, O-BELM attains the best FID across all settings: 4.18 on CIFAR-10 and 10.17 on CelebA-HQ at 100 steps, outperforming DDIM (5.72 / 10.19), EDICT (5.31 / 12.24), and BDIA (5.07 / 10.30). On conditional text-to-image generation with SD-1.5, O-BELM achieves FID 17.96 at 50 steps versus 18.93 (DDIM), 21.30 (EDICT), and 19.38 (BDIA).

## Baselines & Comparisons

All three exact-inversion baselines (EDICT, BDIA) and O-BELM achieve identical MSE reconstruction (0.004, the AE lower bound) on COCO-14, confirming that the bidirectional explicit constraint guarantees exact inversion regardless of the specific coefficient choice. The differentiation lies in sampling quality: O-BELM consistently achieves the lowest FID across all datasets and step counts. Against DDIM, O-BELM improves FID by 1.54 on CIFAR-10 (4.18 vs 5.72) and by 0.02 on CelebA-HQ (10.17 vs 10.19) at 100 steps, with larger gaps at fewer steps (e.g., 6.47 FID improvement on CIFAR-10 at 10 steps). Against EDICT, O-BELM is dramatically superior at low step counts (10.98 vs 87.11 FID on CIFAR-10 at 10 steps) and still leads at 100 steps (4.18 vs 5.31). Against BDIA, O-BELM wins on CIFAR-10 at all step counts (e.g., 4.18 vs 5.07 at 100 steps) and on CelebA-HQ at 100 steps (10.17 vs 10.30), with the gap narrowing as steps increase. On conditional generation (SD-1.5, 50 steps), O-BELM achieves 17.96 FID versus 18.93 (DDIM), 21.30 (EDICT), and 19.38 (BDIA), confirming the LTE advantage translates to practical text-to-image quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on DDIM's explicit Euler discretization of the probability flow ODE and the classical variable-stepsize-variable-formula (VSVF) linear multi-step method framework from numerical analysis. It departs from the heuristic exact inversion samplers EDICT (inspired by affine coupling layers in normalizing flows) and BDIA (inspired by symmetric bidirectional integration) by showing both are special cases of a unified Bidirectional Explicit Linear Multi-step (BELM) formulation, then systematically optimizing within that framework via Local Truncation Error minimization. The key conceptual departure is replacing ad-hoc formula design with a principled LTE-minimization approach that yields the O-BELM sampler with provably higher-order local error.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

O-BELM is the first diffusion sampler with a theoretical guarantee of exact inversion combined with zero-stability and global convergence, providing practitioners with a principled drop-in replacement for DDIM in editing and interpolation pipelines. The unification of EDICT and BDIA under BELM clarifies why these heuristics work and reveals their sub-optimality (EDICT has zero-order accuracy, BDIA first-order, versus O-BELM's second-order). Limitations include the need to store two intermediate states, the restriction to 2-step in practice (higher orders oscillate in stiff image spaces), and the fact that the method is orthogonal to but does not replace domain-specific editing techniques like attention modification or test-time finetuning. Open questions include extending the framework to SDE-based sampling and combining O-BELM with commercial-grade editing pipelines.
