## Problem

Meta-RL agents trained on diverse task distributions are bottlenecked by imbalanced training losses when tasks have returns on different scales, creating a 'multi-task barrier' that limits progress far below the task diversity needed for true generalization. Existing multi-task RL solutions (PopArt, gradient surgery, task-specific heads) require explicit task labels, which conflicts with meta-RL's goal of inferring task identity from context without any label supervision.

## Input / Output

The agent consumes trajectory context sequences of up to l timesteps, where each timestep merges observation, previous action, reward, and done signal into a single embedding. Observations are modality-dependent: state vectors (Meta-World, POPGym), RGB frames processed through an IMPALA CNN with random pad/crop augmentation (Procgen, Atari), or a combination of grid images, language token sequences, and scalar features (BabyAI). The policy outputs a stochastic action, and the critic outputs a probability distribution over B discrete return bins (two-hot classification) rather than a scalar Q-value, with the scalar recovered via symexp of the bin-weighted sum.

## Method

The paper replaces both the actor and critic objectives in the AMAGO Transformer actor-critic framework with scale-resistant classification terms that decouple optimization from the current magnitude of returns. The critic loss converts standard Q-value regression (MSE on TD targets) into two-hot multi-label classification over B bins, with a symlog/symexp transform compressing a wide return range into a fixed label space so that the same relative prediction error maps to similar loss values across orders of magnitude. The actor loss replaces advantage-weighted regression with binary advantage-filtered imitation learning (CRR-Binary-Mean), which imitates only actions whose one-step advantage is positive, making the policy update independent of Q-scale. Both losses are computed in parallel over the context length with a critic ensemble and multi-horizon discounting, and the entire system is trained off-policy from a shared replay buffer with zero knowledge of task identity or count.

## Results

On Meta-World ML45, the scale-resistant update (Ind. Actor / Ind. Critic) more than doubles the success rate of the original RL²-PPO result at 100M timesteps and maintains a 1.4× improvement at 400M timesteps, matching MuZero's performance at its 50M timestep budget. On Multi-Game Atari with unclipped rewards, the classification-based update yields dramatic improvements in 8 out of 10 games compared to the standard Q-dependent update. In Multi-Game Procgen, when reward scales are artificially distorted (Coinrun ×100, Climber ÷10), the Ind./Ind. agent retains performance across all five games while the Q-dependent agent fails in four of five.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against RL²-PPO [17] on Meta-World ML45, the proposed Ind./Ind. update more than doubles success rate at the same 100M timestep budget and achieves 1.4× the performance of RL²-PPO at 400M timesteps. Against MuZero [73], the paper's simple one-step Q-learning matches MuZero's reported score at the 50M timestep budget on ML45. On Multi-Task POPGym, both Transformer variants (Ind. and Dep.) outperform the PPO-GRU baseline from the POPGym codebase [27], and exceed the single-task reference scores from Morad et al. [99] (15M timesteps per task) despite training on a shared 100M budget across 27 tasks. On Meta-World ML45, results are comparable to HTrMRL [97] (success rate ≈ 85%), a method using architectures specialized for multi-episodic RL, while using a general-purpose Transformer. The key differentiator is that the proposed method requires no task labels, no per-task normalization, and no task-specific network heads, unlike PopArt-style MTRL approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the AMAGO framework [57] for Transformer-based off-policy meta-RL by replacing both the actor and critic objectives with scale-invariant classification terms. The critic change draws on the two-hot classification trick from distributional RL (C51) and the symlog transform from DreamerV3, while the actor change adopts the binary advantage filter from CRR-Binary-Mean [32], effectively converting policy improvement to filtered imitation learning. Conceptually, the paper departs from the PopArt [25] and gradient-surgery line of multi-task RL work by eliminating the need for task labels entirely: rather than balancing per-task losses, it makes each loss term inherently insensitive to the absolute scale of returns. The connection to Decision Transformer [36] and supervised sequence modeling is explicitly drawn, positioning the two classification heads as a bridge between online RL self-improvement and the engineering simplicity of supervised learning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The multi-task barrier identified here—where optimization collapses when return scales diverge across tasks—is a fundamental bottleneck for scaling meta-RL toward truly general agents that can adapt to entirely new games or domains. The solution is remarkably simple (two loss function changes) and requires no task labels, per-task parameters, or gradient editing, making it directly applicable to unstructured domains where N is large or unknown. Key open questions remain: whether 45 training tasks (ML45) is sufficient to demonstrate genuine test-time task generalization, whether the CRR actor's loss of optimistic exploration becomes a liability in harder exploration settings, and whether the classification approach's benefits persist when return scales are naturally bounded (as in POPGym, where gains were minimal). The paper also highlights that the next step is evaluating meta-learning over k>2 episodes in domains with sufficient task diversity, pointing toward environments like XLand-Minigrid.
