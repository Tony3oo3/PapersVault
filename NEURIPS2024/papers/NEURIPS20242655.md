## Problem

Equivariant neural networks, despite their strong generalization guarantees, are notoriously difficult to optimize even when the model symmetry matches the data symmetry. Existing work on relaxed equivariance targets model mis-specification rather than the optimization process itself. This paper isolates the optimization challenge, arguing that the severely constrained intertwiner parameter space is a primary cause of training difficulty, and proposes a framework that relaxes equivariance constraints during training to improve optimization while recovering an equivariant model at inference.

## Input / Output

The framework is applied to multiple equivariant architectures across diverse tasks: 3D point clouds subsampled to 300 points for classification (ModelNet40), initial positions/velocities/charges of 5 charged particles for N-body simulation, molecular configurations (950 per molecule from MD17) for energy and force prediction, and 64×64 2D image sequences for smoke flow velocity prediction. During training the network operates in a relaxed approximately-equivariant space, but at inference the model is projected back to the exact equivariant space by zeroing the relaxation coefficient θ, yielding a model with identical architecture and parameter count to the standard equivariant baseline.

## Method

Each equivariant linear layer f_e(x) is augmented with an unconstrained term to form f(x) = f_e(x) + θWx, expanding the hypothesis space from the intertwiner set to a superset of approximately equivariant maps. Two regularization terms keep the relaxed model close to the equivariant manifold: a Lie derivative regularizer (computed over the generators of the symmetry group) penalizes deviation from equivariance, and a norm penalty on ||Wx|| controls the projection error incurred when θ is set to zero. The coefficient θ is scheduled cyclically over training—increasing in the first half of epochs and decreasing in the second—so the model explores the broader space early and smoothly converges back to the equivariant solution. This differs from Residual Pathway Priors, where θ is fixed as a prior on expected equivariance; here θ is an active training-time control whose scheduling is shown to be critical for avoiding a performance drop upon projection. The framework is instantiated for Vector Neurons, SEGNN, Equiformer, and approximately equivariant steerable convolutions, with the Lie derivative regularizer replaced by a discrete-group analogue for finite symmetry groups.

## Results

On the MD17 molecular dynamics task, Equiformer+Ours reduces MAE over standard Equiformer training (e.g., Ethanol energy 2.2→2.0 meV, forces 3.1→2.9 meV/Å; Salicylic acid energy 4.5→4.1 meV). On the synthetic smoke plume task with approximate rotational symmetry, RSsteer+Ours achieves 0.58 RMSE in the Domain setting, outperforming the best baseline RSteer (0.67) by 13%. On ModelNet40 point cloud classification, the framework consistently improves test accuracy over standard training of VN-PointNet and VN-DGCNN, with larger gains for the smaller PointNet architecture. On Nbody particle simulation, the method achieves lower mean average error across all model sizes and dataset sizes, with reduced variance across random seeds.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed framework consistently outperforms all equivariant and approximate-equivariant baselines across four experimental settings. On ModelNet40, it improves over standard Vector Neurons training (Deng et al., 2021) for both VN-PointNet and VN-DGCNN, with the gap widening for the smaller PointNet. On Nbody simulation, it beats standard SEGNN (Brandstetter et al., 2021) at every model and dataset size tested, while also reducing training variance. On MD17, Equiformer+Ours (Liao & Smidt, 2023) achieves lower MAE than vanilla Equiformer on 3 of 4 molecules for energy and 2 of 4 for forces. On the smoke plume task, RSsteer+Ours dominates all six baselines (MLP, Conv, Equiv [Weiler & Cesa, 2019], RPP [Finzi et al., 2021], Lift [Wang et al., 2021], RSteer [Wang et al., 2022]) in both rotation and scale settings, with the largest margin over RSteer (0.58 vs 0.67 RMSE in Rotation Domain). In the appendix comparison (Table 4), the method (74.5%) outperforms Equiv-Adapt (Mondal et al., 2023; 66.3%) but is marginally below Equi-Tuning (Basu et al., 2023; 74.9%), which requires multiple forward passes at inference.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on two prior works: the Residual Pathway Priors (RPP) of Finzi et al. (2021), which introduced the additive relaxation structure f(x) = f_e(x) + θWx, and the Lie derivative metric of Gruver et al. (2023) for measuring equivariance error. The key departure is in motivation and mechanism: RPP treats θ as a fixed prior encoding expected data symmetry mismatch, whereas this paper treats θ as a dynamically scheduled parameter that progressively tightens the equivariant constraint during training, even when the model is correctly specified. The paper explicitly positions itself against the broader relaxed-equivariance literature (Finzi, Romero, van der Ouderaa, Wang, Huang, Petrache & Trivedi), which addresses model misspecification, arguing instead that the optimization difficulty of GCNNs is an independent problem solvable by temporary constraint relaxation followed by projection back to the equivariant space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work addresses a well-recognized practical pain point—equivariant networks are notoriously difficult to optimize—and provides a simple, architecture-agnostic training recipe (add an unconstrained linear term, schedule θ, apply Lie derivative regularization) that consistently improves final equivariant model performance across Vector Neurons, SEGNN, Equiformer, and steerable CNNs. The finding that smaller, under-parametrized networks benefit most suggests the constrained intertwiner space is particularly restrictive when capacity is limited. However, the empirical gains are modest (especially on MD17 where improvements are often <0.5 meV), no theoretical analysis is provided, and the method is limited to matrix Lie groups or discrete finite groups with layer-wise equivariance assumptions. Open questions include a formal characterization of why relaxation aids optimization in the correctly-specified regime, and extension to arbitrary symmetry groups.
