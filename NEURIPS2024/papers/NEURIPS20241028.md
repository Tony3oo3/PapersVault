## Problem

Convolution-based GNNs are fundamentally constrained by the neighborhood aggregation operator X' = ÂX, which simultaneously causes limited expressiveness (bounded by the WL isomorphism test), over-smoothing (exponential decay of inter-node dissimilarity), and over-squashing (vanishing sensitivity to distant nodes). No prior walk-based or kernel-based GNN addresses all three pathologies jointly, and most remain de facto convolutional or inapplicable to node-level tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241028/cropped_page5_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a graph's adjacency matrix and D-dimensional node feature embeddings, then stochastically samples l-step unbiased random walks terminating at each node during both training and inference. Node representations are formed by averaging the RUM output over k sampled walks (default k=4), and graph-level representations are obtained by summing all node representations. The runtime complexity is O(|V|lkD), independent of the number of edges |E|, making it agnostic to graph sparsity and naturally compatible with mini-batching on subsets of nodes.

## Method

RUM (Random Walk with Unifying Memory) replaces the convolution operator entirely with a per-node random walk pipeline: for each node, l-step unbiased random walks are sampled, and two sequences are extracted — the semantic trajectory ω_x (node embeddings visited) and the topological trajectory ω_u (anonymous experiment, which labels each node by its first-occurrence order in the walk). Both sequences are projected through GRU-based RNNs into latent spaces and combined via a feed-forward function f, forming the 'unifying memory' h(w). Node representations are the Monte Carlo average of h(w) over sampled walks, with no iterative neighborhood pooling. Theoretically, the anonymous experiment makes RUM strictly more expressive than the WL test (up to the Reconstruction Conjecture), while the non-contractive RNN mapping guarantees that Dirichlet energy does not diminish with walk length, and the GRU's gated recurrence prevents the vanishing inter-node Jacobian that causes over-squashing in convolutional GNNs.

## Results

On standard node classification benchmarks, RUM achieves 84.1% on Cora, 75.5% on CiteSeer, 82.2% on PubMed, and 93.2% on Coauthor CS, outperforming GCN, GAT, and GraphSAGE on all but the dense Computer dataset (77.8% vs. GCN's 82.6%). On graph-level classification (TU datasets), RUM leads on IMDB-B (81.1%), PROTEINS (77.3%), and PTC (69.8%), while on the CSL graph isomorphism task it reaches 93.2% accuracy with O(N) complexity, matching the O(N²) 3WLGNN (95.7%) while all WL-equivalent convolutional GNNs fail at the 10% random baseline. On heterophilic datasets, RUM achieves 80.0% (Texas), 85.8% (Wisconsin), and 71.1% (Cornell), surpassing GCNII and Graphomer on Texas and Wisconsin.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RUM outperforms GCN, GAT, GraphSAGE, and MoNet on 5 of 6 node classification datasets (Cora, CiteSeer, PubMed, Coauthor CS, Photo), with the sole exception being the dense Computer dataset where GCN (82.6%) and MoNet (83.5%) lead due to high average node degree (18.36) causing high-variance walk representations. Against over-smoothing mitigations, RUM beats GCNII on CiteSeer (75.5 vs. 73.4) and PubMed (82.2 vs. 80.3) but trails on Cora (84.1 vs. 85.5); it also surpasses PairNorm and GraphCON on all three Planetoid datasets. On graph-level TU benchmarks, RUM leads on IMDB-B (81.1% vs. GSN's 77.8%), PROTEINS (77.3% vs. AgentNet's 76.7%), and PTC (69.8% vs. GSN's 68.2%), though it trails AgentNet on MUTAG (91.0 vs. 93.6) and WLK on NCI1 (81.7 vs. 86.0). On the CSL isomorphism task, RUM (93.2%) approaches 3WLGNN (95.7%) at linear vs. quadratic complexity, while all O(N) convolutional GNNs (GCN, GAT, GIN, GraphSAGE) collapse to the 10% random baseline. On heterophilic datasets, RUM beats GCNII on Texas (80.0 vs. 77.6) and Wisconsin (85.8 vs. 80.4), and outperforms Graphomer on all three (Texas 80.0 vs. 76.8, Wisconsin 85.8 vs. 77.7, Cornell 71.1 vs. 68.4). In robustness tests, RUM degrades less than 10% with 10% fake edges added, outperforming both GCN/GAT and the purpose-built Graph Random NN [65].

## Relation to Prior Work

RUM repurposes the anonymous experiment formalism from Micali and Zhu's Markov process reconstruction theory [39] as a topological encoding for random walks on graphs, combining it with semantic node embeddings processed by a GRU. It explicitly departs from the dominant convolutional GNN paradigm (GCN, GAT, GraphSAGE) and from prior walk-based GNNs (RAW-GNN, CRaWl, RWK, GSN, AgentNet) that still embed walks as features within a message-passing/convolutional loop. The key architectural departure is that RUM performs no neighborhood aggregation via adjacency-matrix multiplication at all; the RNN over walk trajectories replaces the iterative smoothing operator, making parameter sharing natural and the model agnostic to graph sparsity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RUM offers a unified, theoretically grounded remedy for three pathologies (limited expressiveness, over-smoothing, over-squashing) that prior work typically addresses with separate, sometimes conflicting, fixes (residual connections, DropEdge, graph rewiring, higher-order GNNs). The expressiveness guarantee (more expressive than k-WL up to the Reconstruction Conjecture) is a clean theoretical result, and the GPU speed advantage over GCN is practically useful for large-scale deployment. However, open questions remain: performance degrades on very dense graphs (Computer dataset), directed graphs require symmetrization with information loss, the tottering problem (2-cycles in walks) is unaddressed, and the over-squashing alleviation is partial—the structural (Â^l)_{uv} bottleneck term persists even though the gradient-power term is eliminated.
