## Problem

Tabular data augmentation for small datasets remains unreliable: joint-distribution generators fail to preserve label stratification, while class-conditional generators that share a single model across all classes overfit and suffer mode collapse, especially under data scarcity or imbalance. No existing method constructs distinct per-class generative models that produce high-fidelity synthetic data from extremely few samples without additional training.

## Input / Output

TabEBM takes a tabular dataset with D numerical features (after Z-score normalisation and leave-one-out encoding of categoricals) and class labels, and produces N_syn synthetic samples (default 500) with the same class distribution as the real training data. The method is entirely training-free: it requires no gradient updates to any model parameters, relying instead on TabPFN's in-context inference. Sampling is performed via SGLD initialised near real class-c points with small Gaussian perturbation (σ_start=0.01), and the method scales to any number of classes without architectural changes.

## Method

For each class c, TabEBM constructs a surrogate binary classification task by pairing the real class-c samples (label 1) with a small set of synthetic negative samples (label 0) placed at random corners of a hypercube at 5 standard deviations from the origin in each dimension. A pre-trained TabPFN in-context classifier is then applied to this surrogate task (no parameter updates), and its two output logits are reinterpreted as an energy function E_c(x) = −log(exp(f(x)[0]) + exp(f(x)[1])), yielding a class-conditional density p(x|y=c) ∝ exp(−E_c(x)). The key insight is that placing negatives far from real data exploits TabPFN's distance-based uncertainty: logits remain high near real data and decrease smoothly with distance, producing a robust, well-shaped energy landscape even under noise and class imbalance. Synthetic samples are then drawn from each class-specific EBM via Stochastic Gradient Langevin Dynamics (200 steps, step size 0.1, noise 0.01), and the final dataset is assembled by sampling classes from the empirical label distribution.

## Results

TabEBM achieves the best average accuracy rank of 1.21±0.74 across eight datasets and five sample sizes (N_real ∈ {20, 50, 100, 200, 500}), compared to 3.15±1.27 for the closest competitor TabPFGen and 3.30±1.02 for the no-augmentation Baseline. In the most challenging low-sample regime (N_real=20), TabEBM improves balanced accuracy over Baseline by 5.7% on protein (33.84% vs. 28.14%), 5.8% on steel (63.27% vs. 57.51%), and 6.2% on energy (23.98% vs. 17.77%). TabEBM is the only generator that consistently improves over Baseline across all sample sizes and all eight datasets, while most modern generative baselines (TVAE, CTGAN, NFLOW, TabDDPM, ARF, GOGGLE) underperform even the no-augmentation baseline in the low-data regime.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TabEBM outperforms all seven benchmark generators across nearly all dataset-size combinations, with its closest competitor being TabPFGen (average rank 3.15 vs. TabEBM's 1.21). Against SMOTE, TabEBM wins on all datasets where both are applicable, with the largest gaps in the low-data regime (e.g., 33.84% vs. N/A on protein at N_real=20, and 80.50% vs. 65.63% on steel at N_real=50). The deep generative baselines (TVAE, CTGAN, NFLOW, TabDDPM, ARF, GOGGLE) all underperform the no-augmentation Baseline in the N_real=20 and N_real=50 regimes, with average ranks of 6.39–8.94, while TabEBM consistently improves over Baseline. On the multi-class datasets (energy with 23 classes, collins with 26 classes, texture with 11 classes), TabEBM is the only method that improves over Baseline, as TabPFGen cannot scale beyond 10 classes and SMOTE is inapplicable. TabEBM also demonstrates superior robustness to class imbalance, maintaining performance gains as imbalance increases, whereas other generators degrade. In terms of computational efficiency, TabEBM operates 3–30× faster than most generative baselines while achieving higher downstream accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

TabEBM repurposes the observation from Grathwohl et al. (2019) that classifier logits can be reinterpreted as energy-based model densities, but applies it in a fundamentally different direction: instead of training an EBM for classification, it converts a pre-trained tabular classifier (TabPFN) into a generative model without any additional training. The key conceptual departure from prior tabular generative methods (CTGAN, TabDDPM, GOGGLE, TabPFGen) is the use of distinct class-specific EBMs rather than a single shared model to approximate all class-conditional densities. The surrogate binary classification task—comparing each class's real samples against distant hypercube-corner negatives—is a novel mechanism that enables this per-class decomposition while leveraging TabPFN's in-context learning capability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that most modern tabular generative methods (VAEs, GANs, diffusion, flows, GNNs) actually degrade downstream performance below the no-augmentation baseline in the small-data regime, a finding that challenges the common assumption that synthetic data always helps. TabEBM's training-free, class-specific design offers a practical solution for data-scarce domains (medicine, chemistry, physics) where collecting more data is infeasible, and it extends naturally to privacy-preserving data sharing. Limitations include inheriting TabPFN's constraints on feature count, and the method's effectiveness is tied to the quality of the underlying classifier; open questions include whether the approach generalizes to multivariate distributional fidelity and how it integrates with future tabular foundation models.
