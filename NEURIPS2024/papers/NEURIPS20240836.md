## Problem

Diffusion models inherit biases from their training data, and existing methods for imposing domain requirements (fairness, adaptation) are task-specific and lack principled guarantees on how constraints are satisfied. This paper asks: can we formulate diffusion model training as a general constrained optimization problem over distributions, with provable characterizations of the optimal trade-off between data fidelity and constraint satisfaction?

## Input / Output

The method takes a primary training dataset (e.g., MNIST digits, CelebA faces, ImageNet images), one or more constraint data distributions (e.g., samples from underrepresented classes or a new fine-tuning dataset), and KL divergence thresholds specifying how close the model must remain to each constraint distribution. For high-resolution ImageNet, constraints are imposed in latent space via a latent diffusion scheme. The output is a trained diffusion model whose generated samples follow an optimal mixture of the original and constraint distributions, with mixture weights determined by learned Lagrangian multipliers.

## Method

The paper casts diffusion model training as a KL-divergence-constrained optimization in infinite-dimensional distribution space, where the objective minimizes divergence to the original data and constraints bound divergences to m reference distributions. A key theoretical result (Theorem 1) shows that strong duality holds and the optimal constrained model is equivalent to an unconstrained diffusion model trained on a mixture distribution whose weights are set by the optimal dual variables. Training proceeds via a Lagrangian dual algorithm (Algorithm 1) that alternates between (i) solving an unconstrained score-matching problem on the current mixture distribution and (ii) updating dual variables via projected gradient ascent on the constraint violations. The paper further provides a TV-distance optimality bound (Theorem 3) decomposing the gap into diffusion-step error O(1/√T), a parametrization gap O(√ν), and a score-matching error O(ε_score), establishing that the trained model converges to the optimal mixture distribution as T grows and the function class becomes expressive.

## Results

On the fairness task, the constrained model reduces FID from 15.9 to 13.4 on MNIST (minority digits 4, 5, 7), from 19.6 to 11.6 on CelebA (male minority), and from 36.0 to 27.3 on ImageNet (minority classes: Cassette player, French horn, Golf ball). On the model-adaptation task (fine-tuning a pretrained MNIST model on the excluded digit 9), the constrained fine-tuned model achieves FID 25.2 versus 45.9 for the unconstrained fine-tuned model, while preserving generation quality across all original classes. In all settings, the constrained model produces a more uniform sampling frequency across classes compared to the unconstrained model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against external baselines; the sole comparison is between the proposed constrained diffusion model and the standard unconstrained diffusion model trained on the same data. The constrained model consistently achieves lower FID across all four experimental settings, with the largest relative improvement on the fine-tuning task (45.9 → 25.2, a 45% reduction) and CelebA (19.6 → 11.6, a 41% reduction). Qualitatively, the constrained model shifts sampling frequency toward underrepresented classes (e.g., increasing male face generation on CelebA from the dataset's 42% toward parity) and prevents catastrophic overfitting during fine-tuning, where the unconstrained model collapses to generating only the new class. The ImageNet experiment further demonstrates that the approach extends to latent diffusion models at high resolution.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper generalizes constrained learning theory (Chamon & Ribeiro, Chamon et al.) to the diffusion model setting, formulating requirement-aware training as a KL-divergence-constrained optimization over joint distributions. It departs from first-principle fairness methods that revise per-sample losses, from importance re-weighting approaches that require a pre-trained debiased density ratio, and from reward-guided fine-tuning (RL, control-based) that relies on heuristically tuned reward functions. The key conceptual move is exploiting strong duality in the convex unparametrized problem to show the constrained model equals an unconstrained model trained on a Lagrangian-weighted mixture distribution, then bridging to the parametrized setting via a dual ascent algorithm with convergence guarantees borrowed from DDPM convergence theory (Li et al., ICLR 2024).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first systematic constrained-optimization framework for diffusion models with end-to-end theoretical guarantees (strong duality, TV-distance convergence bounds), filling a gap the authors note is absent in the diffusion literature. Practically, it offers a transparent, tunable mechanism (Lagrangian multipliers and constraint thresholds) for balancing fidelity to the original data against fairness or adaptation requirements, without needing pre-trained reward models or debiased density ratios. Open questions include extending to conditional/text-to-image generation, relaxing the disjoint-support assumption in the closed-form dual characterization, and scaling to high-resolution latent diffusion where constraint distributions may be intractable. The empirical validation, while spanning three datasets, uses relatively small-scale experiments (MNIST, Celeb-A, a 10-class ImageNet subset), leaving open whether the approach maintains its advantages at full ImageNet scale.
