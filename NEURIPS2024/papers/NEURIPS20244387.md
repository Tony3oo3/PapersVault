## Problem

All existing theoretical results establishing the optimality of Deep Neural Collapse (DNC) are restricted to simplified settings: linear models, only two layers, or binary classification. This paper closes the gap for the general multi-class, multi-layer non-linear case and reveals a surprising qualitative shift: DNC is not an optimal solution of the Deep Unconstrained Features Model (DUFM), with the root cause being a low-rank bias inherent to multi-layer l2 regularization that makes the orthogonal-frame property of DNC too expensive in representation cost.

## Input / Output

The L-DUFM takes training data X ∈ R^{d×N} with K balanced classes (n per class), one-hot labels Y, and per-layer Frobenius-norm regularization parameters, treating the first layer's features H₁ as a free variable jointly optimized with all L weight matrices. For end-to-end experiments, a ResNet20 backbone produces the unconstrained features fed into a 4- or 5-layer MLP head trained on MNIST (28×28 grayscale) or CIFAR-10 (32×32 RGB). Outputs include the optimized feature and weight matrices, DNC1/DNC2 metrics, singular value spectra at convergence, and the rank of intermediate class-mean matrices, which the paper shows is consistently far below K.

## Method

The core contribution is an explicit combinatorial construction of 'Strongly Regular Graph' (SRG) solutions to the L-DUFM that provably outperform DNC when K≥6, L≥4 or K≥10, L=3, with the loss gap scaling as O(K^{(3−L)/(2(L+1))}). The SRG construction leverages the line graph of a complete graph K_r (where K = C(r,2)) to build class-mean matrices whose rank is only Θ(√K) instead of K, exploiting the fact that the triangular graph's adjacency matrix has many eigenvalues equal to −2 that vanish after a diagonal shift. The low-rank bias is explained via the variational form of the Schatten-(2/L) quasi-norm: multi-layer l2 regularization on the deep linear part induces a cost well-approximated by rank for large L, making lower-rank intermediate features strictly cheaper. The paper further proves that DNC1 (within-class variability collapse) remains strictly optimal at the last layer for any L-DUFM problem and approximately optimal at all layers under smoothed ReLU. Empirical validation across three regimes—DUFM training, DUFM-like regularization, and standard weight decay—confirms that gradient descent routinely converges to low-rank solutions matching the SRG construction, with weight decay, learning rate, and width controlling the degree of rank deficiency.

## Results

The main theoretical result (Theorem 5) proves that for K≥6, L≥4 or K≥10, L=3, the SRG solution strictly outperforms the DNC solution in loss, with the ratio L_SRG/L_DNC = O(K^((3-L)/(2(L+1)))) as K→∞. Empirically, gradient descent on 4-DUFM with K=10 finds solutions with intermediate-layer ranks of 5–8 (vs. rank 10 for DNC), and the training loss closely matches the SRG loss rather than the DNC loss. On CIFAR-10 with a ResNet20 + 5-layer MLP head, the third-layer rank ranges 8–9 (vs. K=10 for DNC), with effective ranks of (8.96, 7.46, 6.88, 7.04, 7.73) across layers, confirming a mid-layer rank bottleneck. DNC1 (within-class collapse) holds in all layers across all experimental settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's central comparison is between the DNC solution (orthogonal class-mean matrices, rank K in all layers) and the proposed SRG solution (low-rank intermediate features, rank Θ(√K)). Theoretically, the SRG solution achieves strictly lower loss than DNC whenever K≥6, L≥4 or K≥10, L=3, with the gap growing as K increases. Empirically, gradient descent consistently finds low-rank solutions that outperform the DNC loss in all three experimental regimes (DUFM training, DUFM-like regularization on CIFAR-10/MNIST, and standard regularization). The DNC solution is recovered only under specific hyperparameter settings (small weight decay, small learning rate, or large width), suggesting it is an implicit bias artifact rather than a global optimum. For L=2 or K=2, DNC remains optimal as shown in prior work [48, 51], confirming the qualitative shift occurs only beyond these simplified settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the deep neural collapse (DNC) optimality analysis from restricted settings (two layers [51], deep linear [7], or binary classification [48]) to the general case of arbitrary depth with multiple classes and non-linear activations in the deep unconstrained features model (DUFM). It builds on the low-rank bias framework of Ongie & Willett [37] and the Schatten-p quasi-norm variational characterization [46] to explain why multi-layer l2 regularization induces a representation cost that favors low-rank solutions. The key departure is conceptual: all prior DNC optimality results assumed settings where the orthogonal-frame property (DNC2) was compatible with the regularization, but this paper shows that compatibility breaks down once both depth and class count exceed small thresholds.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This result fundamentally reframes our understanding of DNC: when a DNC solution is observed in practice, it is an artifact of the optimization's implicit bias rather than a consequence of global optimality. The finding that larger network width paradoxically leads to worse (higher-loss) solutions because it biases toward the suboptimal DNC structure has direct practical implications for architecture design. Open questions include whether DNC1 (within-class collapse) remains strictly optimal across all layers (proved only for the last layer and approximately for intermediate layers with smoothed ReLU), whether the results extend to cross-entropy loss, and whether the SRG construction generalizes to other loss functions or imbalanced settings.
