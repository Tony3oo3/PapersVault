## Problem

Existing offline model-based RL methods attribute all performance gaps to dynamics model errors and apply uncertainty penalties accordingly, yet they catastrophically fail when the learned model is replaced by the true error-free dynamics. This paper identifies the overlooked root cause: truncated k-step rollouts create 'edge-of-reach' states that appear only as next-states in the dataset and are never themselves updated, leading to bootstrapping from untrained values and pathological Q-value explosion. The paper shows that prior methods' dynamics penalties are inadvertently addressing this edge-of-reach problem rather than model inaccuracy, making them fragile and dependent on model imperfection.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a fixed offline transition dataset D_offline and a learned dynamics model (a deep ensemble of transition and reward functions) to generate k-step synthetic rollouts starting from states in D_offline. The Q-function is maintained as an ensemble of N critics (typically N=10, up to N=30 for harder tasks), and the policy is a SAC actor. The method operates on both proprioceptive state vectors (D4RL MuJoCo locomotion) and pixel observations processed through a latent-space world model (V-D4RL with DreamerV2), with the value pessimism applied identically in both settings.

## Method

RAVL (Reach-Aware Value Learning) replaces the standard dynamics uncertainty penalty with value pessimism via minimization over an ensemble of N Q-functions in the Bellman target: Q^n(s,a) ← r + γ min_i Q^i(s', π(s')). The key insight is that edge-of-reach states are out-of-distribution with respect to the Q-ensemble's training distribution (they appear as s' but never as s in the rollout data), so ensemble variance naturally flags them and the min operation suppresses overestimation there. This is combined with EDAC's ensemble diversity regularizer to maintain critic disagreement. Because the penalty is on value uncertainty rather than dynamics uncertainty, it remains active even under a perfect model, making RAVL robust to dynamics model improvements. The approach requires no additional forward passes beyond the ensemble evaluation, making it ~70% faster in total runtime than the prior SOTA (MOBILE).

## Results

On the D4RL MuJoCo benchmark, RAVL achieves competitive performance with SOTA MOBILE across 12 environments, e.g. 78.7±2.0 on Halfcheetah-medium (vs. MOBILE's 74.6), 115.5±2.4 on Walker2d-medexp (vs. 115.2), and 103.1±1.1 on Hopper-mixed (vs. 103.9). Critically, when the learned dynamics model is replaced by the true error-free dynamics, all existing methods collapse (e.g. Hopper-medexp drops from 112.6 to 6.1 for MOBILE), while RAVL retains near-optimal performance (110.1→107.2). On the pixel-based V-D4RL benchmark, RAVL achieves a new SOTA overall score of 37.6±7.0, outperforming Offline DreamerV2 (35.5±12.0) and LOMPO (26.9±10.9). RAVL also runs ~70% faster in total wall-clock time than MOBILE due to requiring fewer epochs and no extra forward passes for uncertainty computation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RAVL largely matches or slightly trails the SOTA model-based method MOBILE on the D4RL MuJoCo benchmark, with the largest gaps on Hopper-medium (90.6 vs. 106.6) and Walker2d-mixed (83.0 vs. 89.9), but it wins on Halfcheetah-medium (78.7 vs. 74.6), Halfcheetah-mixed (74.9 vs. 71.7), and Walker2d-medexp (115.5 vs. 115.2). Against the model-free EDAC, RAVL shows clear advantages on Halfcheetah (e.g. 78.7 vs. 65.9 on medium, 74.9 vs. 61.3 on mixed), which the authors attribute to value pessimism being less restrictive in the model-based setting where both a and a′ are on-policy. RAVL consistently outperforms MOPO and COMBO across most environments. The most striking comparison is under true (error-free) dynamics: all dynamics-penalty-based methods (MOPO, MOBILE, COMBO, RAMBO) collapse to near-zero performance (e.g. Hopper-medexp: 112.6→6.1), while RAVL degrades only marginally (110.1→107.2), demonstrating its robustness to model quality. On the pixel-based V-D4RL benchmark, RAVL achieves a new SOTA overall score of 37.6, beating both Offline DreamerV2 (35.5) and LOMPO (26.9), with the largest gains on medium-level datasets where edge-of-reach effects are most pronounced.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the MBPO base procedure (Janner et al., 2019) of k-step model rollouts and the value pessimism mechanism of EDAC (An et al., 2021), transferring Q-ensemble minimization from the model-free to the model-based setting. It departs sharply from the dominant framing in model-based offline RL (MOPO, MOReL, MOBILE, COMBO, RAMBO), which attributes all failures to dynamics model errors and proposes corresponding uncertainty penalties. The paper's central conceptual departure is identifying the 'edge-of-reach problem'—a consequence of truncated rollouts that is orthogonal to model accuracy—and showing that existing dynamics penalties accidentally address this problem rather than the model errors they were designed to correct.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that all existing model-based offline RL methods catastrophically fail under true error-free dynamics challenges the field's core working assumption and reveals a fragile, unanticipated dependence on model quality that will become critical as world models improve. The unified perspective connecting model-free (out-of-sample action) and model-based (edge-of-reach state) problems opens a path for cross-pollination of techniques between the two subfields. Open questions include how to balance edge-of-reach corrections with genuine dynamics error penalties in less accurate model regimes, and whether the edge-of-reach effect acts as an implicit exploration bias in online model-based RL.
