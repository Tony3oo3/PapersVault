## Problem

Constrained resource allocation (e.g., reviewer assignment) requires optimizing welfare under uncertainty in agent valuations, but prior robust optimization work only addressed utilitarian welfare and relied on computationally expensive adversarial projected subgradient ascent. This paper fills the gap by extending uncertainty-aware optimization to group egalitarian welfare and by deriving tractable reformulations (LPs, iterated QPs) that replace the generic max-min approach for both utilitarian and egalitarian objectives under both robust and CVaR paradigms.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a valuation matrix estimated via supervised learning (logistic matrix factorization for binarized bids or Gaussian process matrix factorization for continuous scores), along with uncertainty set parameters (ellipsoidal covariance/radius or polyhedral linear constraints) derived from statistical error bounds on the predictive model. Assignment constraints specify per-agent load bounds, per-item capacity limits, and pairwise conflict restrictions. The output is a fractional allocation matrix (agent × item type) that can be converted to an integer allocation via randomized rounding generalizing the Birkhoff-von Neumann decomposition; the robust approach outputs fractional allocations directly without rounding.

## Method

The core technique is taking the Lagrangian dual of the inner minimization over the uncertainty set and applying the change of variables ξ = a − ζ (where ζ is the dual variable for non-negativity), which collapses the max-min problem into a single concave maximization over (ξ, λ, β). For polyhedral uncertainty sets this reduces to a linear program; for a single ellipsoidal constraint per group, an iterated QP alternates between a closed-form update λ = √(ξᵀSξ)/(2r) and a concave QP in ξ, converging far faster than adversarial projected subgradient ascent. Under the CVaR paradigm with Gaussian valuations, the utilitarian objective admits a closed-form reformulation involving the standard normal PDF/CDF that is optimized via projected gradient ascent without sampling. For general monotone concave welfare functions, the paper proves a decomposition (Proposition 3.8) that separates the worst-case valuation per group from the joint allocation optimization, enabling application of standard convex-concave minimax solvers.

## Results

On the AAMAS 2015 binarized dataset (Table 2), each allocation method achieves a normalized score of 1.00 on its target objective, but non-robust methods (USW, GESW, CVaR) score 0 on robust welfare metrics, demonstrating that uncertainty-aware objectives are essential for robustness. The robust USW allocation achieves 0.92 USW and 0.90 GESW while scoring 1.00 on both robust metrics; robust GESW achieves 0.89 USW and 0.85 GESW with 1.00 robust GESW. On the Gaussian AAMAS 2015 dataset (Table 5), the robust USW allocation achieves 0.86 USW and 0.76 GESW while maintaining 1.00 robust USW, compared to 0.61 robust USW for the naive USW-optimal allocation. The Iterated QP approach converges in far fewer iterations than adversarial projected subgradient ascent, which fails to converge within 1,000 iterations for robust GESW on all three datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is the adversarial projected subgradient ascent method from Cousins et al. [16], which solves the original max-min robust optimization problem without exploiting problem structure. The paper's Iterated QP approach (Corollary 3.3) converges substantially faster on both AAMAS 2015 (Figure 1) and AAMAS 2016 (Figure 3), reaching the optimal solution in a small number of alternating QP steps. Critically, adversarial projected subgradient ascent fails to converge within 1,000 iterations for the robust GESW objective on all three datasets and for the robust USW objective on AAMAS 2021, while Iterated QP converges reliably. In terms of allocation quality, the paper shows that naive welfare-maximizing allocations (USW or GESW) achieve zero robust welfare, meaning they are completely vulnerable to adversarial valuation perturbations within the uncertainty set. The robust allocations sacrifice 8–11% in nominal USW/GESW (e.g., 0.92 vs. 1.00 on AAMAS 2015) to guarantee worst-case performance, and the CVaR allocations provide an intermediate trade-off that retains most nominal welfare while improving tail-risk performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Cousins et al. (SAGT 2023), which studied robust reviewer assignment under a utilitarian objective using adversarial projected subgradient ascent, by (1) adding group egalitarian welfare as a second objective, (2) showing that under polyhedral or single-ellipsoidal uncertainty sets the robust problems reduce to LPs or iterated quadratic programs rather than requiring the expensive adversarial loop, and (3) introducing a parallel CVaR-based stochastic optimization track built on Rockafellar and Uryasev's formulation. The decomposition result for monotonic welfare functions under independent group uncertainty (Proposition 3.8) is a new structural insight that unifies the utilitarian and egalitarian cases. The paper also connects to the broader minimax-regret and distributionally robust optimization literatures, but is the first to address the intersection of group fairness and uncertainty in the constrained multi-matching setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is that market designers can now choose between LP, iterated QP, or sampling-based CVaR solvers depending on their uncertainty model and welfare objective, rather than being limited to a single expensive adversarial algorithm. A key limitation is that the CVaR sampling approach requires thousands of samples and large LPs, making it computationally heavy, and the robust approach produces fractional allocations requiring randomized rounding. Open questions include extending the analysis to other fairness notions (Nash welfare, envy-freeness), handling correlated group uncertainties, and scaling to settings where the number of agents and items grows substantially beyond the ~hundreds seen in the AAMAS experiments.
