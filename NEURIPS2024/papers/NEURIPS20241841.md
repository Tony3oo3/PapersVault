## Problem

Existing metric-based exploration bonuses suffer from two critical limitations: L_p-norm methods (RIDE, NovelD) depend on episodic count-based scaling factors that become meaningless when every state is unique (e.g., high-dimensional visual observations), while bisimulation-metric methods (LIBERTY) introduce approximation gaps by relaxing W1 to W2 and shifting reward expectations outside absolute values, breaking the theoretical value-difference bound. These issues collectively limit scalability to hard exploration tasks in realistic, noisy, or high-dimensional environments.

## Input / Output

The method consumes environment state observations in diverse formats (low-dimensional state vectors for Robosuite, 7×7×3 integer-encoded grid tiles for MiniGrid, photorealistic RGB frames for Habitat) along with actions sampled from the current policy. It produces a scalar exploration bonus b_{t+1} = d_E(s_t, s_{t+1}) × min{max{ζ(r_{s_t}), 1}, M} that is added to the external reward, plus updated policy parameters via policy gradient. The ensemble of k reward models (default k=6) is trained on replay-buffer transitions to predict ground-truth rewards, and their prediction variance ζ serves dual roles: correcting the reward-expectation term in metric learning and acting as the dynamic scaling factor.

## Method

EME defines a new state-discrepancy metric d_E via the contraction operator F(d_E, π) = |E_π r_{s_i} − E_π r_{s_j}| + γ E_{a_i,a_j~π} d_E(s_i', s_j') + γ D_KL(π(·|s_i) ‖ π(·|s_j)), which replaces the intractable Wasserstein distance with sampled next-state distances (following MICo-style representation learning) and adds KL divergence between policy distributions to address the Noisy-TV problem. The reward-expectation term is made tractable without approximation gap by Proposition 3, which decomposes |E r_i − E r_j| into a computable pairwise-difference term minus the per-state reward variances, approximated by the ensemble prediction variance ζ. A diversity-enhanced scaling factor dynamically amplifies the bonus in unexplored regions (high ensemble variance) and suppresses it in well-visited regions (low variance), eliminating the need for episodic counts. Theorem 1 guarantees a unique fixed point via Banach's theorem, and Theorem 2 provides a guaranteed upper bound |V^π(s_i) − V^π(s_j)| ≤ d_E(s_i, s_j), ensuring the bonus is tightly coupled to value differences and thus to TD-error-driven learning efficiency.

## Results

On MiniGrid hard exploration tasks, EME achieves the best success rate in 4 out of 6 settings, notably solving KCS4R3-NT (0.91) where all other baselines fail (0.00) or underperform (RIDE: 0.81). On MuJoCo delayed-reward tasks (delay=10/20/30), EME achieves the best or second-best performance in 15 out of 18 tasks, e.g., 539±62 on Ant (delay=10) vs. LIBERTY's 292±68. On Robosuite continuous control and Habitat real-indoor navigation, EME consistently outperforms all baselines in mean success rate, with the largest gap observed in Habitat where count-based methods (RIDE, NovelD, E3B) plateau around 0.17–0.19 while EME reaches substantially higher coverage.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EME outperforms all six baselines on the hardest MiniGrid variant KCS4R3-NT (0.91 vs. RIDE 0.81, NovelD/E3B/LIBERTY 0.00) and OM2D1h-NT (0.95 vs. NovelD 0.91, RIDE 0.89, E3B 0.57, LIBERTY 0.51), while matching RIDE and NovelD on the easier MultiRoom and KeyCorridor tasks. ICM and RND fail entirely on most MiniGrid tasks (0.00) and underperform on Atari and Habitat due to insufficient exploration. RIDE and NovelD, which rely on episodic count-based scaling, degrade sharply when the episodic term is removed (Table 2: RIDE drops from 0.52 to 0.05 on Robosuite; NovelD from 0.55 to 0.11), confirming their scalability limitation. LIBERTY, despite its bisimulation-metric foundation, fails completely on KCS4R3 (0.00) and MiniGrid Noisy-TV variants, and underperforms on Habitat (0.13 vs. EME's higher coverage), attributed to the W₁→W₂ relaxation and shifted-expectation approximation gaps identified in the paper. On MuJoCo delayed-reward benchmarks, EME matches or exceeds LIBERTY (the strongest baseline) in 15/18 settings, with the largest margin on Ant (539 vs. 292 at delay=10), while curiosity-driven ICM and RND fall further behind as sparsity increases.

## Relation to Prior Work

EME sits at the intersection of two lines of metric-based exploration work: Lp-norm state discrepancy methods (RIDE, NovelD) that rely on episodic count scaling factors, and bisimulation metric methods (LIBERTY, MICo, Castro et al.) that link state differences to value differences. The paper's key departure is identifying and formally proving that LIBERTY's W1→W2 relaxation and expectation-shifting introduce approximation gaps that break the theoretical value-bound guarantee (Propositions 1–2), then proposing a metric that avoids Wasserstein distance entirely by using sampled next-state distances plus KL divergence between policy distributions. The ensemble-of-reward-models trick to approximate reward expectations without relaxation draws on the MICo/Chen-Pan representation learning lineage, while the diversity-enhanced scaling factor repurposes ensemble variance as a novelty signal, replacing the brittle episodic count.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck: exploration in high-dimensional, noisy, realistic environments (Habitat, Robosuite) where episodic counts are meaningless and bisimulation approximations degrade. The theoretical analysis (Propositions 1–2) is valuable for the bisimulation-metric community as it pinpoints exactly where prior methods lose their guarantees. Open questions include whether the iterative fixed-point convergence of the EME metric remains tractable in very large state spaces, whether the ensemble size trade-off (accuracy vs. exploration intensity) generalizes beyond the tested environments, and whether the KL-divergence term alone is sufficient for the Noisy-TV problem in truly adversarial stochastic settings. The method's dependence on an ensemble of reward models adds computational overhead that may limit deployment in compute-constrained settings.
