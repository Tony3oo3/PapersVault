## Problem

Existing attention mechanisms in end-to-end rPPG architectures compute attention separately across spatial, temporal, and channel dimensions by squeezing features into single dimensions, which is ill-suited for the task of extracting a single underlying BVP signal from multidimensional voxel embeddings. This paper asks whether a joint multidimensional attention that simultaneously captures spatial, temporal, and channel correlations can improve cross-dataset generalization for rPPG signal estimation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243062/cropped_page5_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes raw facial RGB video frames (72×72 pixels, 160 frames per chunk after a temporal difference layer, 30 FPS) as input and produces a continuous rPPG/BVP waveform as output, from which heart rate is derived via FFT peak detection. A key design choice is that the temporal dimension of the 4D voxel embeddings (τ×κ×α×β) is mapped to the vector axis of the NMF factorization matrix, while the combined spatial-channel dimensions form the feature axis, aligning the factorization with the single-signal nature of the BVP output.

## Method

The Factorized Self-Attention Module (FSAM) applies rank-1 nonnegative matrix factorization to 4D voxel embeddings to jointly compute spatial, temporal, and channel attention without squeezing any individual dimension. The embeddings are pre-processed with a 1×1×1 convolution and ReLU to ensure non-negativity, then factorized using a one-step gradient optimization (from the Hamburger module) inside a no_grad block, so no backpropagation flows through the NMF. The resulting low-rank approximation is reshaped back to embedding space, post-processed, and multiplied element-wise with the original embeddings as an excitation, followed by instance normalization and a residual connection. FSAM is deployed in FactorizePhys, a 3D-CNN architecture that uses a Diff layer, Instance Normalization, and same-padding in the temporal dimension to preserve the full temporal resolution. The same module is adapted for the TSM-based 2D-CNN architecture EfficientPhys by mapping the TSM frame-depth parameter to the vector axis. Ablations confirm that rank-1 factorization is optimal (higher ranks degrade to baseline performance) and that FSAM can be skipped at inference time for FactorizePhys without performance loss, reducing latency.

## Results

On cross-dataset rPPG estimation, FactorizePhys with FSAM achieves the best MAE(HR) of 0.48 bpm (trained on UBFC-rPPG, tested on PURE) and 1.04 bpm (trained on iBVP or PURE, tested on UBFC-rPPG), consistently outperforming all baselines. The most dramatic gains appear when trained on the synthetic SCAMPS dataset: on iBVP, FactorizePhys achieves MAE=2.71 bpm versus 26.19 (EfficientPhys+SASN), 31.85 (PhysNet), and 41.73 (PhysFormer). FactorizePhys also achieves the highest SNR (up to 15.19 dB on PURE) and MACC (up to 0.78 on PURE) across all cross-dataset settings, while using only 52K parameters—roughly 40× fewer than EfficientPhys+SASN (2.16M) and 140× fewer than PhysFormer (7.38M).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against PhysNet [83] (3D-CNN, no attention), FactorizePhys with FSAM wins on virtually every cross-dataset evaluation, with the gap most extreme when trained on SCAMPS (e.g., MAE 2.71 vs. 31.85 bpm on iBVP; SNR 2.36 vs. −6.11 dB). Against PhysFormer [77] (transformer with temporal-difference multi-head self-attention), FactorizePhys also dominates on cross-dataset transfer, particularly on iBVP and SCAMPS-trained settings where PhysFormer's MAE degrades to 41.73 bpm while FactorizePhys stays at 2.71 bpm. Against the strongest baseline, EfficientPhys with SASN [37] (2D-CNN + TSM + self-attention shifted network), FactorizePhys wins on most cross-dataset settings—most notably when trained on SCAMPS (MAE 5.43 vs. 6.21 on PURE; 2.71 vs. 26.19 on iBVP) and when trained on UBFC-rPPG (MAE 0.48 vs. 4.71 on PURE). The one setting where EfficientPhys+SASN edges out FactorizePhys is PURE trained on iBVP (MAE 0.56 vs. 0.60), though FactorizePhys still leads on SNR (15.19 vs. 11.96 dB) and MACC (0.77 vs. 0.73). Paired t-tests (p < 0.001 on all six metrics) confirm the gains over EfficientPhys+SASN are statistically significant. Notably, FactorizePhys achieves these results with only 52K parameters and can skip FSAM at inference without performance loss, yielding near-identical latency to the 2D-CNN EfficientPhys.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Hamburger module (Geng et al., ICLR 2021), which used NMF as a global context block for semantic segmentation, into a multidimensional attention mechanism specifically designed for spatial-temporal signal extraction. It departs from existing rPPG attention mechanisms (CBAM, ST-Attention, SASN, CTJA/STJA in JAMSNet and GLISNet) that compute attention by squeezing individual dimensions or jointly attending over only two of three axes; FSAM is the first to jointly derive spatial, temporal, and channel attention without any dimension reduction. The architecture FactorizePhys builds on the 3D-CNN lineage of PhysNet and RTrPPG, while the 2D-CNN adaptation directly modifies EfficientPhys by replacing its SASN attention with FSAM.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The rank-1 factorization result provides a clean empirical confirmation that BVP is fundamentally a single-source signal, offering a principled (rather than heuristic) justification for the attention design. The finding that FSAM can be dropped at inference time for FactorizePhys while retaining performance suggests it functions as a training-time regularizer that shapes 3D convolutional kernels, enabling deployment with reduced latency. Open questions include whether FSAM generalizes to other spatial-temporal tasks (video understanding, segmentation), whether NMF variants with temporal/frequency constraints improve attention for HRV and other physiological signals, and whether the approach remains robust under active head motion and dynamic lighting.
