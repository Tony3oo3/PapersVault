## Problem

Prior work on fair multi-agent multi-armed bandits (Hossain et al. 2021, Jones et al. 2023) uses the product of agents' utilities (NSW_prod) as the fairness objective, which is homogeneous of degree N and thus scale-sensitive. This paper asks whether no-regret learning is possible with the more natural Nash Social Welfare (NSW, the geometric mean), and provides a complete answer: sublinear regret is achievable in stochastic bandit and adversarial full-information settings, but linear regret is unavoidable in the adversarial bandit setting despite NSW being concave.

## Input / Output

At each round t, the environment presents a K×N utility matrix u_t ∈ [0,1]^{K×N} (K arms, N agents). Under bandit feedback the learner only observes the N utilities of the sampled arm i_t; under full-information feedback she observes the entire matrix. The learner outputs a probability vector p_t ∈ Δ_K over arms and samples i_t ~ p_t. The theoretical output is a regret bound measured against the best fixed distribution p* maximizing the SWF of expected utilities, not the expected SWF of realized utilities.

## Method

The central technical challenge is that NSW is not Lipschitz when any agent's expected utility is near zero, breaking standard UCB and OCO analyses that work for NSW_prod. In the stochastic bandit setting, the paper uses a UCB algorithm with Bernstein-type (rather than Hoeffding-type) confidence widths and a two-case analysis (low-utility vs. bounded-utility agents) to achieve Õ(K^{2/N} T^{(N-1)/N}) regret, matching a lower bound. In the adversarial full-information setting, two FTRL variants are designed: one with a log-barrier regularizer that yields O(√(KT log T)) regret with no dependence on N and generalizes to all concave Pareto-optimal SWFs, and another with a Tsallis entropy regularizer (β=2/N) giving Õ(K^{1/2−1/N} √(NT)) regret with better K-dependence. A special case where at least M agents are indifferent per round admits logarithmic regret via the EWOO algorithm, exploiting the exp-concavity of −NSW in that regime.

## Results

In the stochastic N-agent K-armed bandit setting, the paper's UCB-based algorithm (Algorithm 1) achieves $\tilde{O}(K^{2/N} T^{(N-1)/N} + K)$ expected regret with respect to NSW, and a matching lower bound of $\tilde{\Omega}(K^{1/N} T^{(N-1)/N})$ confirms the $T$-dependence is tight. In the adversarial bandit-feedback setting, an impossibility result shows $\Omega(T)$ regret is unavoidable for both NSW and NSW_prod. Under adversarial full-information feedback, two FTRL-based algorithms achieve $\mathcal{O}(\sqrt{KT\log T})$ regret (log-barrier, no dependence on N) and $\tilde{O}(K^{1/2-1/N}\sqrt{NT})$ regret (Tsallis entropy, better K-dependence when K≫N). A special case with at least one indifferent agent yields logarithmic regret $\mathcal{O}((N-M)/M \cdot K\log T)$.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is against Hossain et al. [2021] and Jones et al. [2023], which study the same multi-agent bandit problem but with NSW_prod (product of utilities) as the objective. Those works achieve $\tilde{O}(\sqrt{T})$ regret in the stochastic setting, while this paper shows that replacing NSW_prod with the more natural NSW (geometric mean) worsens the stochastic regret to $\tilde{O}(K^{2/N} T^{(N-1)/N})$ — a sharp separation that demonstrates the fundamental difficulty introduced by NSW's lack of Lipschitzness near zero utility. The paper also contrasts its adversarial bandit-feedback impossibility ($\Omega(T)$) with the well-known $\Theta(\sqrt{T})$ minimax regret of Bandit Convex Optimization (BCO), highlighting that the slightly different feedback model (observing per-agent utilities of the pulled arm vs. the full NSW value) is what makes the problem intractable. In the adversarial full-information setting, the log-barrier FTRL algorithm (Theorem 4.2) is notable for having zero dependence on the number of agents N, a property not available in prior multi-agent bandit results, and generalizes to any concave Pareto-optimal SWF.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the multi-agent multi-armed bandit framework of Hossain et al. (2021) and Jones et al. (2023), which studied fairness via the product of agent utilities (NSW_prod), by replacing the objective with the geometric mean (NSW). The key technical departure is that NSW is non-Lipschitz in the small-utility regime, which invalidates the direct Lipschitz-bandit analysis used in prior work and necessitates Bernstein-type confidence intervals and novel decomposition arguments. The adversarial full-information results build on FTRL with log-barrier and Tsallis entropy regularizers (from the OMD literature), while the logarithmic-regret special case leverages the EWOO algorithm for exp-concave losses (Hazan et al., 2007). The paper also explicitly contrasts its problem with Bandit Convex Optimization, showing that a subtle difference in the feedback model (observing per-agent utilities vs. the aggregate welfare) creates a sharp separation in learnability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes a clean taxonomy of what is and isn't possible for fair multi-agent learning: sublinear (but worse than √T) regret in stochastic bandit feedback, linear regret (impossibility) in adversarial bandit feedback, and √T regret in adversarial full-information feedback. The impossibility result is notable because it shows that concavity of the welfare function alone is insufficient for learnability when the feedback is restricted to bandit observations. Open questions include whether the K and N dependencies in the stochastic bound are tight, whether a single algorithm can handle a broad class of social welfare functions simultaneously (an 'omniprediction' analogue), and whether the adversarial bandit impossibility can be circumvented with richer but still partial feedback models.
