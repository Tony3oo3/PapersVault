## Problem

While diffusion models have begun to close the human-machine gap in one-shot drawing, the role of representational inductive biases in shaping the latent space of Latent Diffusion Models for this task remains largely unexplored. The paper asks whether biases proven effective in one-shot classification (prototype-based clustering, redundancy reduction, contrastive learning) can also improve one-shot generative performance and align machine perceptual strategies with human ones.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243051/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a single 48×48 grayscale exemplar drawing (a 'prototypical' sample selected at the center of a category cluster) as conditioning input and generates new 48×48 grayscale drawing variations of the same visual concept. Beyond generated samples, the method also produces pixel-space feature importance maps by projecting diffusion scores through the RAE decoder Jacobian, which are then compared against human psychophysical saliency maps collected via a ClickMe-style experiment.

## Method

The paper studies six regularizers applied to the Regularized AutoEncoder (RAE) component of a Latent Diffusion Model: standard KL and VQ, supervised classification and prototype-based losses, and contrastive SimCLR and Barlow-twins objectives. The RAE (a 4-layer CNN encoder/decoder with a 1D bottleneck of size 64 or 128) is trained with reconstruction plus the chosen regularization, after which a 1D UNet diffusion model is trained on the latent space using classifier-free guidance conditioned on the exemplar's latent code. The key finding is that prototype-based and Barlow regularizers produce the most human-like drawings (closest to the human data point in the originality-vs-recognizability framework) and yield feature importance maps with the highest Spearman correlation to human saliency. A novel explainability method is derived via the chain rule, multiplying each diffusion score by the RAE decoder Jacobian to obtain interpretable pixel-space heatmaps of category-diagnostic features.

## Results

On the QuickDraw-FS dataset, the prototype-based regularizer achieves the smallest distance to human performance (0.04 at β_PR = 5×10²), followed by Barlow (0.08 at β_BAR = 30), SimCLR (0.12 at β_SimCLR = 10⁻²), and classification (0.15 at β_CL = 5). In the feature importance map analysis, the prototype-based LDM achieves the highest Spearman rank correlation with human psychophysical saliency maps, significantly outperforming all other regularizers (p < 10⁻³), with Barlow in second place (also p < 10⁻³ vs. KL, classification, and SimCLR). Combining the prototype-based regularizer with either Barlow or KL further improves human-likeness, while VQ+Prototype and SimCLR+Prototype show negligible gains.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is the pixel-space diffusion model from Boutin et al. (ICML 2023) [30], which was the closest prior machine to human performance on the one-shot drawing task. The standard LDM regularizers (KL and VQ) only match this pixel-space diffusion model's recognizability and do not improve upon it. In contrast, the prototype-based regularizer reduces the distance to human by a factor of ~4 compared to the pixel-space DM (0.04 vs. ~0.15), and Barlow reduces it by ~2× (0.08). In the feature importance map comparison, all regularized LDMs significantly outperform the non-regularized LDM in alignment with human saliency, and the prototype-based LDM is the only one that approaches the human inter-subject consistency level (0.88). The paper thus demonstrates that representational inductive biases borrowed from one-shot classification (prototype clustering, redundancy reduction) close the remaining gap that pure architectural/conditioning improvements (classifier-free guidance, pixel-space diffusion) left open.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes representational inductive biases originally designed for one-shot classification (Prototypical Networks, SimCLR, Barlow Twins) as regularizers for the latent space of Latent Diffusion Models, bridging a gap that prior one-shot generation work left unexplored. While previous LDM research focused on architectural innovations (attention, contextual integration) or conditioning mechanisms (classifier-free guidance, context vectors), this work systematically investigates how shaping the RAE's latent geometry affects downstream generation quality. The evaluation framework (originality vs. recognizability) and human psychophysics data are inherited from Boutin et al. (2022, 2023), and the base LDM architecture follows Rombach et al. (2022), but the key departure is treating the autoencoder's regularization as the primary lever for human-like generalization rather than the diffusion process itself.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that prototype-based and Barlow (redundancy-reduction) regularizers—both with direct neuroscience analogues—produce the most human-like one-shot drawings suggests a principled path toward generative models whose internal representations align with human perceptual strategies, not just output statistics. However, the scope is limited to simple line drawings (Omniglot, QuickDraw-FS at 48×48), and the evaluation relies on pre-trained critic networks whose alignment with human judgment is assumed rather than verified. A major open question is whether these regularizers transfer to natural image generation (e.g., Stable Diffusion-style models), where the paper only speculates rather than demonstrates. The limited number of tested biases (6 out of hundreds in the literature) also leaves the search space largely unexplored.
