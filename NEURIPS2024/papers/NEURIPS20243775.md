## Problem

2D-prior-based 3D generation methods (e.g., Score Distillation Sampling) overfit to specific viewpoints, producing spatial perception hallucinations such as the Janus problem—duplicated faces, extra limbs, and multi-view inconsistencies. Existing remedies either require scarce 3D training data or are tightly coupled to specific frameworks, leaving a gap for a data-independent, tuning-free solution that works across diverse 3D generation pipelines.

## Input / Output

For text-to-3D, the method takes a text prompt and optimizes a 3D representation (NeRF or 3D Gaussian Splatting) from scratch; for image-to-3D, it takes a single frontal reference image (256×256 in experiments) and reconstructs the full 3D object. Internally, the pipeline renders four simultaneous views per iteration (batch size 4), feeds one rendered image to a locally deployed LMM (LLaVA-34B) for hallucination detection, and outputs an enhanced negative prompt that modulates the diffusion denoiser. The final output is a 3D asset (Gaussian Splatting or NeRF) that can be rendered from arbitrary viewpoints.

## Method

Hallo3D follows a generation–detection–correction paradigm built on three modules. (1) Multi-view Appearance Alignment introduces a cross-attention mechanism inside the U-Net denoiser where a focal view (selected by vertical FOV > 120% of default) supplies key and value features while all four views compute distinct query features, aligning color and texture across views during SDS. (2) Multi-modal Hallucination Detection queries an LMM with a 3D-aware prompt and a one-shot reference to identify structural inconsistencies (extra heads, distorted limbs, unnatural lighting) in a rendered image and emit them as a standardized 'enhanced negative prompt.' (3) Prompt-Enhanced Re-consistency inverts the rendered image to noise via DDIM, then re-samples it using the enhanced negative prompt in place of the standard null prompt, producing a geometrically corrected image; the 3D model is then updated with an additional MSE loss (L_CG) between the original and re-consistent renderings. The total training objective is L_SDS + w·L_CG (w=0.1), applied only when the LMM confirms the rendering has sufficient semantic completeness, otherwise falling back to L_SDS alone.

## Results

On text-driven 3D generation, Hallo3D consistently improves CLIP-Score across all four baselines: e.g., DreamFusion-IF jumps from 14.09 to 22.15 (CLIP-Score B/32) and from 18.19 to 26.72 (CLIP-Score L/14), while GaussianDreamer improves from 21.31 to 24.53 (B/32). On image-driven 3D generation (GSO/Objaverse), Hallo3D reduces Chamfer Distance from 0.0370 to 0.0283 over Zero-1-to-3 and from 0.0185 to 0.0171 over DreamGaussian, while improving Volume IoU from 0.4824 to 0.5602 and LPIPS from 0.3926 to 0.3328. In the user study (58 raters, 1–10 scale), multi-view consistency scores rise by 2.3–3.1 points across all text-driven baselines, with the largest gain on SJC (4.53→7.63).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Hallo3D outperforms all eight baselines on every reported metric. In text-to-3D, the largest relative gains appear over DreamFusion-IF (CLIP-Score L/14: 18.19→26.72, +47%) and Magic3D (17.99→27.72, +54%), indicating the method is most beneficial when the underlying 2D prior is weakest. GaussianDreamer and SJC, which already produce stronger single-view outputs, still see 15–20% CLIP-Score improvements. In image-to-3D, Hallo3D beats Zero-1-to-3 on all five geometric and perceptual metrics (e.g., CD 0.0370→0.0283, LPIPS 0.3926→0.3328) and marginally improves DreamGaussian (CD 0.0185→0.0171, SSIM 0.8543→0.8793), confirming plug-and-play applicability. In the user study, Hallo3D wins on multi-view consistency and overall quality for every baseline, though it slightly trails DreamGaussian on prompt alignment (8.00 vs. 8.55) in the image-driven setting. Appendix C.2 further shows Hallo3D outperforming the dedicated consistency methods Perp-Neg [2] and Debias [17] on both visual quality and CLIP-Score for the Janus problem.

## Relation to Prior Work

Hallo3D builds directly on the Score Distillation Sampling (SDS) paradigm introduced by DreamFusion, which uses 2D diffusion priors to supervise 3D generation, and extends it with a generation-detection-correction loop that leverages large multi-modal models (GPT-4V, LLaVA) as geometric hallucination detectors. It departs from prior consistency-enhancement approaches that require fine-tuning diffusion models on 3D data or adjusting prompt embeddings for specific viewpoints, instead proposing a tuning-free, framework-agnostic plug-in that works across NeRF- and 3DGS-based pipelines. The multi-view appearance alignment module draws on cross-attention insights from recent diffusion editing work (MasaCtrl, Prompt-to-Prompt), while the Prompt-Enhanced Re-consistency step repurposes DDIM inversion and classifier-free guidance with LMM-generated negative prompts—a combination not previously applied to 3D generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses the well-known Janus problem and multi-view inconsistency that plague SDS-based 3D generation, offering a practical, drop-in solution that does not require retraining or access to 3D training data. Its reliance on LMMs for geometric reasoning is notable but introduces a dependency on expensive or locally-deployed models, and the quality ceiling remains tied to the underlying baseline. Open questions include whether lighter-weight detectors can replace LMMs, how the method scales to complex multi-object scenes, and whether the per-pixel L_CG loss can be made more efficient without sacrificing the consistency gains. The tuning-free, framework-agnostic design makes it broadly applicable, but the additional compute overhead (~35% for GaussianDreamer, ~30% for DreamFusion) limits its use in time-sensitive pipelines.
