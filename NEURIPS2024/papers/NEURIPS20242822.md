## Problem

Deploying LLMs on memory-constrained edge devices requires aggressive compression, but existing post-training quantization methods (e.g., QuIP#, OPTQ) treat low-rank approximation and quantization independently, and represent low-rank factors in high precision (16/32-bit), wasting the bit budget. This paper asks whether a joint, fully low-precision decomposition of the form W ≈ Q + LR can be obtained with theoretical guarantees, and whether the resulting low-rank factors can be further adapted via LoRA to recover zero-shot performance in the sub-2.5-bit regime.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a full-precision weight matrix W ∈ R^{n×d}, a calibration matrix X ∈ R^{m×d} (input activations from a calibration set like RedPajama), a target rank k (64/128/256 in experiments), and per-component bit budgets B_Q, B_L, B_R. Unlike prior work that keeps L and R in 16-bit, CALDERA quantizes all three components—Q, L, and R—to low-precision formats (e.g., 2-bit E8 lattice for Q, 4-bit for L and R), so the entire compressed layer Q+LR fits within ~2.1–2.4 bits per parameter. The calibration data is used offline to compute the Hessian H = (1/m)X^T X, which drives both the LDLQ feedback matrix and the rank-constrained regression initialization of L and R.

## Method

CALDERA solves min_{Q,L,R} ‖(Q+LR−W)X^T‖_F^2 subject to Q, L, R being representable in their respective low-precision codebooks, via an alternating optimization loop (Alg. 1). In each outer iteration, the backbone Q is quantized using LDLQ (from QuIP) with error feedback from the LDL decomposition of the Hessian, and the residual W−Q is factored into low-rank L and R by the LPLRFACTORIZE submodule (Alg. 2), which initializes L and R via a globally optimal rank-constrained regression (two SVDs) and then iteratively refines them under quantization constraints using closed-form least-squares updates. The key distinction from prior work is that L and R themselves are quantized (e.g., to 4-bit E8 lattice), allowing more singular components to be captured within the same total bit budget compared to keeping them in 16-bit. A randomized Hadamard transform is applied to W before decomposition to equalize entry magnitudes and improve quantization robustness. Theoretically, the paper derives an upper bound on the approximation error via a rank-constrained regression framework, showing the bound decomposes into a rank-dependent term (trailing singular values) and an additive quantization error ε controlled by B_L and B_R, and that the bound can be made arbitrarily small by trading off bit budget between Q and the low-rank factors. The low-rank factors L and R are also amenable to LoRA fine-tuning on small task-specific datasets, recovering zero-shot accuracy lost to aggressive 2-bit quantization of Q.

## Results

CALDERA achieves the best zero-shot perplexity across all tested models in the sub-2.5 bits/parameter regime. For LLaMa-2 7B at 2.4 avg bits (rank-256, 4-bit factors), CALDERA reaches 6.19 Wiki2 and 8.14 C4 perplexity, compared to 7.73/10.0 for QuIP# with rank-64 16-bit factors and 8.23/10.8 for QuIP# without low-rank factors. For LLaMa-3 8B, the gap is even larger: CALDERA (rank-256, 4-bit) achieves 8.22 Wiki2 vs 10.9 for QuIP# (rank-64, 16-bit) and 13.8 for QuIP# (no LR). After LoRA fine-tuning (rank-64, BF16), CALDERA on LLaMa-2 7B reaches 5.55 Wiki2 perplexity at 2.7 avg bits, outperforming LoftQ (5.78 at 2.9 bits) and LQ-LoRA (5.67 at 2.95 bits).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against QuIP# (the primary baseline), CALDERA consistently wins on perplexity across all model sizes. For LLaMa-2 7B at comparable 2.4 avg bits, CALDERA (rank-256, 4-bit) achieves 6.19 Wiki2 vs 7.73 for QuIP# (rank-64, 16-bit), a 20% reduction; against QuIP# without low-rank factors (2-bit), the gap widens to 6.19 vs 8.23. For LLaMa-3 8B, the advantage is most pronounced: CALDERA reaches 8.22 Wiki2 vs 10.9 (QuIP# rank-64) and 13.8 (QuIP# no LR), confirming that quantized low-rank factors capture more singular components than half-precision ones at the same bit budget. Against LoftQ (rank-64, 2.5-bit backbone, 16-bit LR at 2.9 avg bits), CALDERA (rank-256, 2-bit backbone, 4-bit LR at 2.7 avg bits) achieves 5.61 vs 5.78 Wiki2 perplexity on LLaMa-2 7B after LoRA fine-tuning, at a lower bit budget. Against LQ-LoRA (rank-64, 2.75-bit backbone, 8-bit LR at 2.95 avg bits), CALDERA achieves 5.61 vs 5.67 Wiki2 perplexity, again at fewer bits. On zero-shot accuracy, CALDERA generally matches or exceeds QuIP# on most tasks (e.g., PiQA 75.6 vs 71.7, ArcE 63.6 vs 63.2 for 7B), though RTE shows some variance due to the small test set.

## Relation to Prior Work

CALDERA builds directly on the QuIP/QuIP# framework (LDLQ quantization, E8 lattice codebooks, Randomized Hadamard Transform) and the LPLR joint low-rank/low-precision factorization algorithm from the same group's prior work [31]. The key departure from prior LLM compression methods (LoftQ, LQ-LoRA, ASVD, ZeroQuant-V2) is that those works represent low-rank factors L and R in full 16/32-bit precision, whereas CALDERA quantizes all three components (Q, L, R) to low-precision, enabling more singular components to be captured within the same bit budget. The theoretical analysis departs from rank-agnostic quantization error bounds (e.g., Chee et al.) by using a rank-constrained regression framework that explicitly separates the approximation error into a rank-dependent term and an additive quantization term.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CALDERA provides a principled, theoretically grounded method for sub-2.5 bits-per-parameter LLM compression that outperforms existing post-training quantization in this challenging regime, making it relevant for deploying LLMs on memory-constrained edge devices. The tunable tradeoff between backbone bit-budget B_Q and target rank k (formalized in Thm. 4.1) gives practitioners a clear knob for navigating the compression-performance frontier. Limitations include a notable throughput penalty (~47% slower than QuIP#) due to dequantizing and multiplying low-rank factors, and the observation that LLaMA-3 8B is substantially harder to compress than LLaMA-2 models. Open questions include whether custom fused kernels can close the throughput gap, and whether the approach extends to non-Transformer architectures or multimodal models.
