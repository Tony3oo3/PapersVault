## Problem

A prevailing belief in the adversarial attack community is that flatter adversarial examples (those in flat regions of the loss landscape) necessarily transfer better across models, motivating widespread use of sharpness-aware minimization variants. However, no theoretical bound connecting transferability to flatness has been established, leaving this belief unverified. This paper derives the first theoretical bound on adversarial transferability and shows that flatness alone is insufficient to guarantee improved transferability, as a second-order gradient component also plays a critical role.

## Input / Output

Given a natural RGB image x from ImageNet (224×224), its ground-truth label y, and a proxy model F (e.g., ResNet50), TPA produces an L∞-bounded perturbation δ (ε=16/255) such that x+δ misleads multiple unseen target models. The method additionally samples N=10 uniform perturbations Δ from U(-b,b) around x+δ to estimate neighborhood flatness, and uses a small step k=0.05 for Hessian-free gradient approximation. The output adversarial example is evaluated by attack success rate across 14 target architectures including CNNs and transformers (ViT, Swin).

## Method

TPA jointly optimizes the negative cross-entropy loss on the proxy model and a surrogate penalty term that averages the L2 gradient norms of N uniformly sampled points around the adversarial example, rather than penalizing the gradient at the adversarial point alone. This neighborhood-based penalty is theoretically shown to implicitly control both the first-order (flatness) and second-order (Hessian diagonal) gradient components in the derived transferability bound, unlike prior methods that only regulate the former. To avoid explicit Hessian computation, TPA approximates H·(∇L/||∇L||) via a first-order Taylor expansion along the normalized gradient direction with a small step k, reducing the per-iteration cost from cubic to linear. The optimization is solved with standard gradient-based methods over 20 iterations with step size 1.6, using hyperparameters λ=5, b=16, k=0.05, and N=10.

## Results

On ImageNet (10,000 images), TPA achieves an average attack success rate of ~99.5% across 12 CNN target models when using ResNet50 as the proxy model, and notably reaches 93.52% on ViT and 90.98% on Swin — the first transfer-based attack to exceed 90% ASR against transformer architectures. TPA outperforms the previous state-of-the-art (SSA) by an average margin of roughly 9 percentage points. Against secured models (adversarial training, robust training), TPA achieves 74.57% ASR on AdvIncV3 and 99.52% on SIN, again surpassing all baselines by large margins.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TPA dominates all 10 baselines across every proxy-target model pair. Against the strongest prior methods, TPA outperforms BSR (ICCV 2024) by 11.2 points on ViT (93.52% vs. 82.32%) and 13.9 points on Swin (90.98% vs. 77.05%), and by 2.7 points on DenseNet121 (99.69% vs. 97.02%). Against SSA (frequency-domain augmentation), TPA gains 41.1 points on ViT (93.52% vs. 52.41%) and 40.1 points on Swin (90.98% vs. 50.94%), highlighting that flatness-based methods (RAP, BSR) and frequency-domain methods (SSA) struggle most on transformer architectures. Against optimization-based baselines (MI, NI, DI, TI), TPA leads by 10–50+ points depending on the target. In targeted attacks (Table 3), TPA achieves 31.9% on DenseNet121 and 13.4% on ViT, surpassing Self-Universality (22.0% and 9.7% respectively) by 9.9 and 3.7 points. On secured models (Table 2), TPA leads BSR by 10.5 points on AdvIncV3 (74.57% vs. 64.02%) and by 11.0 points on SIN (99.52% vs. 88.47%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new theoretical ground at the intersection of adversarial transferability and loss-landscape flatness. Prior transferability-enhancing methods (RAP, GNP, SSA) borrowed the flatness intuition from SAM and assumed that minimizing gradient norms of adversarial examples would improve cross-model transfer, but no prior work had derived a formal bound connecting transferability to flatness. TPA departs from these methods by showing that the second-order gradient component is equally critical, and by using a neighborhood-based surrogate (averaging gradient norms over a local region) that implicitly captures both first- and second-order effects, rather than penalizing only the pointwise gradient norm. The Hessian-free approximation via Taylor expansion (Eq. 10) is a practical contribution that avoids the cubic-cost Hessian evaluation while retaining theoretical grounding.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper challenges a widely-held working assumption in the adversarial ML community—that flat adversarial examples are inherently more transferable—by providing the first theoretical bound showing flatness is necessary but not sufficient. This recalibration could redirect research away from purely first-order flatness optimization toward methods that account for curvature. Limitations include the reliance on a ResNet-like architecture assumption (Assumption 3.5), the single-trial experimental protocol without error bars, and evaluation restricted to ImageNet. Open questions include whether the bound is tight, whether the surrogate is the optimal computationally-feasible proxy, and whether the theoretical insights extend to non-ResNet architectures or generative models.
