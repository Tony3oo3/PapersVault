## Problem

Existing causal video QA datasets (NextQA, CausalVidQA, IntentQA) require only surface-level understanding or simple word substitution, feature short causal chains (~1.1–1.3 links), single static scenes, and lack hard incorrect options that force genuine causal reasoning. This paper addresses these gaps by constructing a dataset where causal chains are longer (avg. 1.9 links), span multiple dynamic scenes (~4 per clip), demand a wide spectrum of reasoning types, and include causally confusing distractors that defeat object/verb-matching shortcuts.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242945/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For MCQA, the model receives a silent Tom & Jerry video clip (avg. ~358 frames, ~4 scenes with abrupt shot changes), a text question, and five answer options (1 correct + 4 mined incorrect), and must select the correct option for both the primary answer and the deeper explanation (chance = 1/25). For OEAG, the model receives only the clip and question and must generate both the answer and explanation in natural language, evaluated via CapsMIX (a normalized integration of BLEU, METEOR, ROUGE, SPICE, CIDEr, and Sentence-BERT scores). The multi-level answer structure requires each explanation to extend the causal chain from the primary answer by at least one additional causal link.

## Method

The core contribution is the CausalChaos! dataset: 4,945 Question-Answer-Explanation sets annotated from 161 silent Tom & Jerry episodes, leveraging animation principles (staging, exaggeration, squash-and-stretch) to create unambiguous yet complex causal relationships across dynamic scene changes. Incorrect options are mined via two strategies: vanilla hard negatives selected by weighted Sentence-BERT cosine similarity over questions and answers, and Causal-Confusion negatives generated by LLMs that preserve the same objects/actors but invert or swap causal relationships. Three test splits (Uniformly Distributed, Partially Seen, Unseen episodes) probe different levels of prior exposure. The paper also introduces MIST-CC, a multitask variant of MIST that adds an auxiliary GRU-based causal chain generation loss (β=0.1) to the MCQA objective, and CapsMIX as a unified OEAG evaluation metric. Benchmarking spans traditional VideoQA models (BlindQA through MIST), VLMs (BLIP-2, Video-LLaMA, VideoChat2, VILA), and GPT-4o, revealing that even the best models (GPT-4o at ~64% MCQA, ~93% human) struggle significantly on open-ended generation and drop sharply on Causal-Confusion negatives.

## Results

On the CausalChaos! MCQA benchmark (UD split), the best finetuned model VILA achieves 77.22% accuracy on Answer selection and 62.80% on Answer+Explanation, while the best zero-shot model GPT-4o reaches 63.64% and 48.17% respectively. Traditional VideoQA models (BlindQA, EVQA, CoMem, HME, HCRN, HGA) achieve only 25–32% on Answer and 10–17% on Answer+Explanation, barely above the 20%/4% chance levels. On the Causal-Confusion subset, all top models drop 5–9 points (e.g., GPT-4o from 63.64% to 54.95%), revealing weak causal relationship modeling. On open-ended answer generation (OEAG), even the best model VILA scores only 8.03 CapsMIX, and GPT-4o scores just 2.99, indicating substantial room for improvement.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VILA (finetuned) is the strongest overall model at 77.22% A / 62.80% A+E, outperforming GPT-4o (zero-shot) by ~14 points on Answer accuracy, though GPT-4o remains the best zero-shot performer at 63.64% / 48.17%. Among traditional VideoQA architectures, MIST-CC (63.34% / 46.80%) and MIST (62.22% / 44.88%) dominate, while earlier models like HCRN (32.48% / 16.98%), HME (32.35% / 14.02%), and HGA (31.40% / 15.36%) cluster near chance. Zero-shot VLMs BLIP-2 (43.67%), VideoChat2 (38.14%), and Video-LLaMA (35.00%) all fall well below GPT-4o, with Video-LLaMA performing worst among VLMs. On the Causal-Confusion subset, GPT-4o drops most sharply (63.64→54.95, −8.7 pts), while MIST-CC is most robust (63.34→58.76, −4.6 pts), suggesting its auxiliary causal-chain task partially compensates. On OEAG, VILA leads at 8.03 CapsMIX, followed by VisionGPT-2 (6.76) and BlindGPT-2 (6.60), while GPT-4o surprisingly scores only 2.99, indicating that MCQA success does not translate to open-ended causal explanation generation. Humans achieve 94.80% / 93.40%, leaving a 17–31 point gap over the best models.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the causal video QA line of work (NextQA, CausalVidQA, IntentQA, CLEVRER) by addressing specific gaps: shorter causal chains, single-scene settings, lack of hard negatives, and single-level answers. It builds on MIST as the backbone for its MIST-CC multitask variant, which adds an auxiliary causal-chain generation objective. Conceptually, it introduces 'Dynamic Scene Linking' as distinct from traditional temporal modeling, and repurposes animation principles (staging, exaggeration) as a form of data-design-driven visual prompting to make causal relationships unambiguous yet complex.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Causal-Confusion evaluation reveals that even GPT-4o drops ~9 points when causal relationships are inverted while objects/actors remain unchanged, and S-BERT similarity exceeds 90% between causally opposite sentences—suggesting causal relationship modeling is a genuine blind spot in current VLMs/MLLMs. The finding that a small cartoon dataset improves real-world NextQA performance across multiple models (including non-why questions) is notable, though the gains are modest. Open questions include how to explicitly model causal relationships in video understanding, how to achieve effective joint vision-language modeling for open-ended causal answer generation (where even GPT-4o scores poorly), and whether the Dynamic Scene Linking challenge generalizes beyond cartoons.
