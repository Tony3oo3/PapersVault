## Problem

HE-based private DNN inference suffers from orders-of-magnitude latency overhead, with linear layers (GEMMs and convolutions) accounting for over 75% of total time due to costly HE multiplications and rotations. While block circulant weight matrices can convert GEMVs into HE-friendly 1D convolutions, existing encoding algorithms are incompatible with this structure, and prior circulant approaches (CirCNN, Falcon) either use uniform block sizes, lack custom encoding, or fail to integrate with SOTA protocols like Bolt's BSGS, leaving significant efficiency on the table.

## Input / Output

In the hybrid HE/MPC protocol, the client encrypts an RGB image (32×32 for CIFAR, 64×64 for Tiny ImageNet, 224×224 for ImageNet) under the BFV scheme and sends the ciphertext to the server, which holds the proprietary model weights. The server performs all linear-layer computations over ciphertexts using CirEncode, while nonlinear layers (ReLU, GELU) are evaluated via MPC. The client ultimately decrypts the final ciphertext to obtain a class label; the DNN architecture and layer-wise block sizes are public to both parties under an honest-but-curious threat model.

## Method

PrivCirNet transforms each weight matrix into a block circulant form with layer-specific block sizes, converting GEMVs into 1D convolutions that are HE-friendly. The key encoding contribution, CirEncode, nests coefficient encoding within each circulant block (a single HE-Pmult computes the full block convolution with zero HE-Rot) and SIMD diagonal encoding across blocks (with an extended BSGS tiling), reducing HE-Pmult by a factor of b and HE-Rot by √b relative to Bolt. For initialization, the paper replaces Frobenius-norm minimization with a loss-aware criterion derived from second-order Taylor expansion using a diagonal Fisher approximation, so that each layer's circulant approximation minimizes its actual contribution to task loss. Layer-wise block sizes are then assigned via an integer linear program that minimizes total network sensitivity subject to a latency budget, and two fusion techniques (circulant ConvBN fusion and inverted-residual layer fusion) further reduce communication and computation without breaking the circulant structure.

## Results

On Tiny ImageNet, PrivCirNet achieves 1.9×, 5.0×, and 1.3× latency reduction with iso-accuracy over Bolt on MobileNetV2, ResNet-18, and ViT, respectively. Compared to the SOTA HE-friendly pruning method SpENCNN, PrivCirNet improves top-1 accuracy by 5.2% (MobileNetV2), 4.1% (ResNet-18), and 12% (ViT) on Tiny ImageNet. On ImageNet with MobileNetV2, PrivCirNet achieves 1.7× lower latency than Bolt and 4.2% higher accuracy than SpENCNN. At the micro-benchmark level, PrivCirNet (b8) reduces HE-Rot by ~7× and HE-Pmult by ~8.5× on average compared to Bolt and Neujeans.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PrivCirNet consistently outperforms all prior-art HE-based frameworks (Bolt, Neujeans, Cheetah, Falcon, CrypTFlow2, Iron, Bumblebee) in both ConvNets and Transformers. Against Bolt (IEEE S&P 2024), PrivCirNet achieves 1.9×/5.0×/1.3× latency reduction with iso-accuracy on MobileNetV2/ResNet-18/ViT on Tiny ImageNet, and 1.7× lower latency on ImageNet. Against Neujeans (CCS 2024), PrivCirNet achieves 1.4× latency reduction on ImageNet. Against Cheetah (USENIX Security 2022), PrivCirNet achieves 1.3–4.8× latency reduction with iso-accuracy. Against SpENCNN (ICML 2023), the SOTA HE-friendly pruning method, PrivCirNet achieves substantially higher accuracy at comparable or lower latency: +5.2% (MobileNetV2), +4.1% (ResNet-18), and +12% (ViT) on Tiny ImageNet, and +4.2% on ImageNet. The key advantage is that PrivCirNet supports both GEMMs and convolutions efficiently, whereas Bolt performs poorly on convolutions (needing to transform them into GEMMs, inflating hidden dimensions by 9× for 3×3 convs) and Neujeans performs poorly on GEMMs.

## Relation to Prior Work

PrivCirNet sits at the intersection of two previously separate optimization tracks in HE-based private inference: protocol-level encoding design (Bolt, Neujeans, Cheetah) and network-level architecture modification (SpENCNN, Hunter, CirCNN). It departs from prior work by co-designing both: the block circulant transformation (borrowed from plaintext compression work like CirCNN) is made fully compatible with a new nested encoding (CirEncode) that combines coefficient encoding within circulant blocks and SIMD encoding across blocks, extending the DFT-based nested encoding idea from Neujeans. The loss-aware initialization using diagonal Fisher information and the latency-aware ILP-based block size assignment represent a departure from the uniform or Frobenius-norm-based initialization used in prior circulant matrix work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

HE-based private inference remains orders of magnitude slower than plaintext inference, and linear layers dominate the latency bottleneck. PrivCirNet's 5× speedup on ResNet-18 and 12% accuracy gain over the SOTA pruning baseline on ViT demonstrate that protocol-network co-optimization can substantially close this gap. However, the approach only addresses linear layers (~75% of latency); ViT self-attention layers lack weight matrices and cannot benefit from circulant transformation, and the paper acknowledges that joint linear/nonlinear optimization (e.g., combining with ReLU pruning) is left as future work. The practical impact is significant for the HE/MPC community but remains confined to a relatively narrow deployment niche.
