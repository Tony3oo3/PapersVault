## Problem

Open-weights LLMs struggle to effectively integrate retrieved or user-provided context for conversational QA and RAG, falling well short of proprietary models like GPT-4. Additionally, multi-turn retrieval remains poorly addressed—single-turn dense retrievers generalize poorly to follow-up questions with pronouns and anaphoric references—and no comprehensive benchmark exists that jointly evaluates RAG, table reasoning, arithmetic, and unanswerable-question detection in a conversational setting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240493/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives a multi-turn dialogue (system prompt, prior user-assistant turns, and the latest user question) concatenated with retrieved context chunks (top-5 for ~300-word segments or top-20 for shorter segments, depending on the dataset). Documents may contain plain text or markdown-formatted tables with headers appended to each cell for better cell-to-header linking. The model produces a natural-language answer, a numeric/arithmetic result, or the fixed refusal string 'Sorry. I cannot find the answer based on the context' when the answer is absent from the provided context.

## Method

ChatQA employs a two-stage instruction tuning recipe. Stage 1 performs standard supervised fine-tuning on 128K instruction-following and dialog samples (Soda, ELI5, FLAN, Self-Instruct, OpenAssistant, Dolly, etc.) to establish general instruction-following. Stage 2—context-enhanced instruction tuning—appends the relevant document context and a task-specific instruction (short-answer, long-answer, or arithmetic) before each question in a unified conversational template, training on a blend of 7K human-annotated or GPT-3.5-Turbo-synthesized multi-turn QA dialogues, eight single-turn QA datasets (SQuAD, DROP, NarrativeQA, TATQA, etc.), and the stage-1 data, plus ~1.5K unanswerable samples to teach explicit refusal. For retrieval, a single-turn dense retriever (Dragon or E5) is fine-tuned on conversational query–context pairs extracted from the same QA data, achieving zero-shot multi-turn recall comparable to GPT-3.5-Turbo query rewriting while eliminating the autoregressive generation cost and API dependency of the rewriting approach.

## Results

On CHATRAG BENCH (10 conversational QA datasets), ChatQA-1.0-70B achieves an average F1 score of 54.14, slightly outperforming GPT-4-0613 (53.90) and GPT-4-Turbo-2024-04-09 (54.03) without using any synthetic data from OpenAI models. Llama3-ChatQA-1.5-70B further raises the average to 58.25, surpassing GPT-4-Turbo by over 4 points. On single-turn RAG benchmarks (NQ, TriviaQA, HotpotQA via KILT), Llama3-ChatQA-1.5-70B achieves 58.7 average exact match, outperforming the 65B RA-DIT model (50.1) and Llama3-Instruct-70B (53.6).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ChatQA-1.0-70B outperforms its Llama2-Chat-70B base by 8.93 average F1 (54.14 vs. 45.21), and Llama3-ChatQA-1.5-70B outperforms Llama3-Instruct-70B by 4.19 (w/o HDial: 57.14 vs. 52.95). Against OpenAI models, ChatQA-1.0-70B edges out GPT-4-0613 (54.14 vs. 53.90) and GPT-4-Turbo (54.14 vs. 54.03) on Avg. All, while clearly surpassing GPT-3.5-Turbo (54.14 vs. 50.37) and Command R+ (54.14 vs. 50.93); Llama3-ChatQA-1.5-70B extends the lead to 58.25, beating GPT-4-Turbo by 4.22 points. On single-turn RAG (KILT), Llama3-ChatQA-1.5-70B (58.7 avg EM) surpasses RA-DIT-65B (50.1), RePlug-65B (44.5), RECOMP-20B (42.1), InstructRetro-43B, Raven-11B, and Atlas-11B (39.4), while even the 8B variant (52.3) beats RA-DIT-65B. For retrieval, fine-tuning Dragon on conversational data yields results comparable to Dragon+GPT-3.5-Turbo rewriting (52.72 vs. 54.46 avg top-1 recall), and fine-tuning E5-unsupervised produces a >15% improvement over E5+Rewrite, demonstrating that the fine-tuning approach avoids the extra inference cost and API dependency of query rewriting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the instruction tuning lineage (FLAN, Self-Instruct, Llama2-Chat, Llama3-Instruct) by proposing a two-stage recipe—standard SFT followed by context-enhanced instruction tuning specifically targeting RAG and conversational QA—rather than treating context integration as an afterthought. For retrieval, it departs from the dominant query-rewriting paradigm (GPT-3.5-Turbo-based rewriters, RL-based rewriters) by showing that fine-tuning a single-turn dense retriever (Dragon, E5) on high-quality multi-turn QA pairs achieves comparable zero-shot recall, eliminating the autoregressive generation cost of rewriting. The CHATRAG BENCH consolidates ten previously separate conversational QA datasets into a unified evaluation, filling a gap where no single benchmark covered RAG, tabular reasoning, arithmetic, and unanswerable-question detection simultaneously.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The fully open-sourced package (model weights, 7k human-annotated dialogues, benchmark, fine-tuned retriever) removes the dependency on OpenAI APIs that has constrained open research in conversational RAG, and the finding that a small amount of unanswerable training data (~1.5k samples) substantially reduces hallucination offers a practical, low-cost mitigation strategy. The retrieval result—that fine-tuning matches query rewriting—has direct deployment implications by removing an extra LLM inference step and associated API costs. Open questions include whether the two-stage recipe generalizes to non-QA tasks (code, math), whether the small margin over GPT-4-0613 on CHATRAG BENCH (54.14 vs. 53.90) holds on newer GPT-4 variants, and how the approach scales to truly long-context settings beyond top-k chunk retrieval.
