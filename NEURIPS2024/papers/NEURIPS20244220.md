## Problem

It was an open question whether estimating full return distributions in reinforcement learning is statistically harder than estimating mean returns in the generative model setting. Zhang et al. (2023) provided an upper bound with (1−γ)^(−4) sample complexity and a lower bound with (1−γ)^(−3), leaving a gap, and their proposed algorithm was impractical due to infinite-dimensional distribution representations. This paper closes the gap by exhibiting a tractable algorithm that matches the lower bound up to logarithmic factors.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes N i.i.d. next-state samples per state from a generative model, a known deterministic reward function r: X → [0,1], a discount factor γ ∈ [0,1), and a number of categories m (which must satisfy m ≥ 4(1−γ)^(−2)ε^(−2)+1 for the theoretical guarantee). It outputs CDF values F̂ ∈ R^(X×m) at equally spaced grid points over [0, (1−γ)^(−1)], from which categorical return distributions are decoded as probability masses at each grid point. Notably, there is no direct dependence of the required sample size N on m, so increasing the number of categories carries no statistical penalty.

## Method

The core algorithm, DCFP (Direct Categorical Fixed-Point), reformulates the projected categorical Bellman operator Π_m T as a linear map on CDF values, yielding a matrix T_P whose entries are P(y|x)(H_{i,j}^x − H_{i,j+1}^x). By fixing the last CDF coordinate to 1 (encoding the probability-mass constraint) and removing the redundant row, DCFP reduces to solving a single inhomogeneous linear system (I − T̃_P)F̃ = H̃, obtaining the exact categorical fixed point in one shot rather than iterating CDP. In the generative model setting, empirical transition probabilities P̂ replace P. The sample complexity proof introduces a new distributional Bellman equation—the stochastic categorical CDF Bellman equation—and defines local and global squared-Cramér variation quantities that play the role of variance in a Bernstein-type concentration argument, ultimately yielding the near-minimax (1−γ)^(−3) sample bound.

## Results

The main theoretical result (Theorem 5.1) establishes that model-based DCFP requires N = Ω(ε⁻²(1−γ)⁻³ polylog(|X|/δ)) samples to achieve ε-accurate return distribution estimation in Wasserstein-1 distance with probability at least 1−δ, matching the minimax lower bound of Zhang et al. (2023) up to logarithmic factors. This is the first near-minimax-optimal sample complexity guarantee for any distributional RL algorithm. Empirically, on a 5-state MRP with m=100 atoms and N=10⁶ samples, DCFP achieves lower wallclock time than both QDP and CDP, while CDP (iterative) requires many more DP iterations to converge at high discount factors (γ close to 1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares DCFP against two baselines: Quantile Dynamic Programming (QDP; Dabney et al., 2018b) and iterative Categorical Dynamic Programming (CDP; Rowland et al., 2018). Against QDP, DCFP is generally faster in wallclock time across all settings, but QDP achieves lower asymptotic w₁-estimation error at low discount factors and small atom counts (m), owing to QDP's ability to adaptively place atoms where return mass is concentrated. However, at larger discount factors and/or larger atom counts, DCFP outperforms QDP on both estimation error and speed. Against CDP, DCFP consistently achieves lower wallclock time because it solves a linear system directly rather than iterating to convergence; this advantage is particularly pronounced at high discount factors (γ → 1), where CDP requires many iterations due to the slow contraction rate (√γ). The paper also notes that QDP works particularly well in near-deterministic environments, while DCFP excels in settings with short high-probability self-transition paths.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the categorical distributional RL framework of Bellemare et al. (2017) and the convergence theory of categorical dynamic programming (CDP) from Rowland et al. (2018), but departs from the iterative DP paradigm by formulating the projected Bellman operator as a linear system in CDF space, enabling direct solution via a linear solver (DCFP). It also extends the generative-model RL analysis of Azar et al. (2013) and Agarwal et al. (2020) from mean-return estimation to full return-distribution estimation, and closes the gap between the upper and lower bounds established by Zhang et al. (2023) for distributional DP with a generative model. The introduction of the stochastic categorical CDF Bellman equation is a new theoretical object with no direct analogue in prior distributional RL literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper resolves a foundational open question by showing that, in the generative model setting, estimating return distributions is statistically no harder (up to log factors) than estimating mean returns, which validates the practical use of distributional RL without incurring a sample-efficiency penalty. The DCFP algorithm and the stochastic categorical CDF Bellman equation provide new tools likely to be useful in future theoretical and algorithmic work on distributional RL. Open questions include extending the analysis to quantile-based methods (where the DP operator is non-linear), to risk-sensitive criteria, to online settings, and to removing the restriction ε < (1−γ)^(−1/2) on the accuracy parameter.
