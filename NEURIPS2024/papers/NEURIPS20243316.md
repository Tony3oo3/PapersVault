## Problem

Current LLM alignment methods such as DPO, KTO, and IPO enforce preferences at the individual sample level, ensuring that each chosen response scores higher than its paired rejected response. However, this pointwise constraint does not guarantee that the reward distribution of chosen responses stochastically dominates that of rejected responses across all quantiles, leaving the model vulnerable to distributional misalignment. The paper asks whether one can directly enforce first-order stochastic dominance (FSD) of the chosen reward distribution over the rejected reward distribution as an alignment objective.

## Input / Output

The method accepts either paired preference triplets (prompt, chosen response, rejected response) or unpaired marginals consisting of separate distributions of chosen and rejected prompt/response pairs, along with a fixed reference LLM policy π_ref. Rewards are computed as log-likelihood ratios between the policy being optimized and the reference policy, clamped to a bounded interval. The output is a fine-tuned LLM policy (via LoRA adapters) whose reward distribution for chosen responses FSD-dominates that for rejected responses, verified through quantile-plot margins and benchmark evaluations.

## Method

AOT recasts the pointwise preference constraint as a distributional FSD constraint and relaxes it with a smooth convex cost h (logistic, β-squared-hinge, or β-least-squares) applied to quantile differences, yielding the objective min_θ ∫₀¹ h(Q_{U_θ}(t) − Q_{V_θ}(t)) dt. By a theorem of Santambrogio, this integral equals a one-dimensional optimal transport cost OT_h with convex cost, whose optimal coupling is the north-west corner (sorted) matching, giving the closed-form empirical loss (1/n)Σᵢ h(u_θ⁽ⁱ⁾ − v_θ⁽ⁱ⁾) where u and v are sorted log-likelihood ratios. Training alternates between computing sorted rewards and updating θ via gradient descent, with SoftSorting (entropic Sinkhorn regularization) optionally used to backpropagate gradients through the permutation. The dual formulation reveals a min-max game between the policy and c-concave potentials, from which the paper derives a parametric n^{−1/2} sample complexity bound for the FSD violation.

## Results

On the UltraFeedback Binarized dataset with Merlinite-7B as the base model, AOT unpaired achieves the best AlpacaEval (GPT4) score of 31.3%, a significant improvement over the base model's 17.1% (an 83% relative gain). AOT paired scores 29.9% on AlpacaEval, also outperforming all baselines. On the Open LLM Leaderboard benchmarks (ARC, HellaSwag, MMLU, TruthfulQA, Winogrande, GSM8K), AOT is competitive with baselines, with the largest gains appearing on TruthfulQA (51.1% for AOT unpaired vs. 42.0% for the base model) and HellaSwag (66.2% vs. 63.2%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

AOT unpaired outperforms DPO by 3.9 points on AlpacaEval (31.3 vs. 27.4) and KTO by 6.4 points (31.3 vs. 24.9), making it the strongest 7B model on the AlpacaEval leaderboard at the time of writing. Against IPO, AOT unpaired leads by 3.6 points on AlpacaEval (31.3 vs. 27.7) and by 4.6 points on TruthfulQA (51.1 vs. 46.5). On the Open LLM Leaderboard benchmarks, AOT is competitive but not uniformly dominant: DPO edges out AOT on ARC (82.8 vs. 82.5) and MMLU (63.1 vs. 62.8), while KTO leads on GSM8K (53.9 vs. 51.8) and Winogrande (74.9 vs. 74.4). The base Merlinite-7B model is substantially outperformed by all alignment methods, with AOT unpaired yielding the largest AlpacaEval gain (+14.2 points). Across five different 7B base models (Merlinite, OpenHermes, Starling, Llama3-8B, Mistral-7B), AOT consistently achieves the best or near-best AlpacaEval scores, confirming the robustness of the distributional alignment approach.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the pointwise preference optimization paradigm established by DPO, KTO, and IPO, which enforce reward ordering on a per-sample basis, by instead imposing first-order stochastic dominance (FSD) of the chosen reward distribution over the rejected reward distribution. The FSD constraint is relaxed via a convex surrogate and cast as a one-dimensional optimal transport problem with a smooth convex cost, drawing on classical OT theory (Santambrogio, 2015) and the north-west corner solution for 1D transport. The connection to 'learning with stochastic orders' (Domingo-Enrich et al., 2022) and 'almost stochastic order' (Del Barrio et al., 2018) provides the theoretical scaffolding, while the practical algorithm leverages SoftSorting (Blondel et al., 2020) for differentiable permutation. The unpaired variant (uAOT) extends the applicability beyond the paired-triplet requirement of DPO, competing directly with KTO's prospect-theoretic approach to unpaired data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The shift from pointwise to distributional alignment addresses a genuine gap: sample-level preference optimization does not guarantee that the chosen reward distribution stochastically dominates the rejected one across all quantiles, which AOT enforces directly. The closed-form sorting solution makes the method computationally trivial to implement (a few lines in HuggingFace TRL), and the parametric-rate convergence guarantee provides theoretical reassurance. However, the paper acknowledges that alignment performance on benchmarks does not guarantee safety or generalization, and the method remains bounded by the quality and bias of the training data. Open questions include whether distributional alignment provides additional benefits in multi-turn or safety-critical settings, and whether the 1D OT formulation generalizes naturally to multi-dimensional reward spaces.
