## Problem

Prior EEG-to-fMRI translation studies have been confined to task conditions (e.g., cued eye opening/closing), a small number of brain regions, and subject-specific training, leaving the fully eyes-closed resting-state condition and cross-subject generalization largely unexplored. The complex, region-dependent projection from neural activity to BOLD hemodynamic responses, combined with EEG's spatial ambiguity from volume conduction, makes this translation particularly challenging in the noisy resting-state regime.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a 16-second window of multi-channel EEG (26 channels in resting-state, 31 in task) resampled to 200 Hz and amplitude-normalized by dividing by 100 µV; this window length is chosen to span the full hemodynamic response function preceding each fMRI volume. Output is a single scalar BOLD prediction per ROI per time point (sequence-to-one), with ROIs defined by the DiFuMo 64-parcel functional parcellation, enabling prediction across primary sensory, high-level cognitive, and deep subcortical regions without assuming a fixed hemodynamic delay.

## Method

NeuroBOLT employs two parallel encoding branches whose outputs are summed before a GELU + linear regression head. The spatiotemporal branch fine-tunes the LaBraM EEG foundation model (pre-trained on ~2,500 hours of EEG) by segmenting EEG into 1-second patches, adding learnable temporal and spatial embeddings, and encoding with a standard Transformer followed by average pooling. The spectral branch applies STFT at four window scales (0.5 s, 1 s, 2 s, 4 s), learns frequency and window embeddings per scale, sums them into a unified spectrum embedding, and encodes with a linear-complexity Transformer to handle the quadratic cost of multi-channel spectrograms. The multi-scale design retains high temporal resolution from small windows and high frequency resolution from large windows, capturing complementary oscillatory dynamics that a single fixed STFT window would miss. Training is supervised with MSE loss under an AdamW optimizer and cosine schedule, and the model is evaluated in intra-subject, inter-subject, zero-shot cross-condition, and joint-training settings.

## Results

NeuroBOLT achieves a mean Pearson correlation (R) of 0.531 in intra-subject resting-state fMRI reconstruction and 0.473 in inter-subject (unseen subject) reconstruction, outperforming all baselines across all seven ROIs and the global signal. In the inter-subject setting, the best individual ROI performances are Heschl's Gyrus (R = 0.561) and Global Signal (R = 0.564), while the weakest is Putamen (R = 0.335). The model also generalizes to an auditory-task dataset collected at a different site, achieving R = 0.420 with joint resting-state + task training, and R = 0.380 in zero-shot transfer from resting-state pretraining alone.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NeuroBOLT outperforms all seven baselines in both intra-subject and inter-subject resting-state reconstruction. In the intra-subject setting, it leads LaBraM (0.484) and BIOT (0.473) by 12.26% and 9.71% in average correlation, respectively, and substantially exceeds the EEG-to-fMRI-specific baselines BEIRA (0.341) and Li et al. (0.445). In the more challenging inter-subject setting, NeuroBOLT (0.473) surpasses the next-best BIOT (0.435) by 8.74%, while LaBraM drops sharply to 0.151, indicating poor cross-subject generalization of the foundation model without the spectral module. The EEG-to-fMRI baselines BEIRA (0.412) and Li et al. (0.419) remain competitive but trail NeuroBOLT by 5–13% across most ROIs. Notably, NeuroBOLT's advantage is most pronounced in primary sensory regions (Heschl's Gyrus: 0.561 vs. 0.512 for BIOT) and the global signal (0.564 vs. 0.529), while the gap narrows in subcortical regions (Putamen: 0.335 vs. 0.299). The ablation confirms that the multi-scale spectral module contributes +0.285 R over the spatiotemporal module alone, and that four spectral scales (l₃) yield the best average performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the EEG-to-fMRI translation line initiated by ridge-regression approaches (Meir-Hasson et al., Or-Borichev et al.) and deep encoder-decoder models (BEIRA, Li et al., Liu & Sajda), which were largely confined to task conditions (cued eyes-open/eyes-closed) and a handful of brain regions with subject-specific training. NeuroBOLT departs from this lineage in two key ways: it adopts the patch-based transformer paradigm from EEG foundation models (LaBraM, BIOT) and introduces a multi-scale STFT spectral branch that fuses frequency representations at multiple window sizes, rather than relying on a single fixed STFT window. The sequence-to-one formulation (predicting one fMRI value from a 16-second EEG window) also differs from prior sequence-to-sequence designs, avoiding an explicit hemodynamic delay assumption.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that eyes-closed resting-state fMRI can be reconstructed from raw EEG, and the first to show cross-subject generalization across entire 20-minute scans covering primary sensory, high-level cognitive, and deep subcortical regions. The zero-shot transfer from resting-state-trained models to a task condition at a different site suggests the learned EEG-fMRI mapping captures condition-invariant neural dynamics. Key limitations temper the impact: the sample sizes are small (22 resting-state, 10 task subjects), models are trained per-region rather than jointly, and results use a single random seed. Open questions include whether joint multi-region training can enable high-resolution volumetric fMRI reconstruction, and whether performance holds on larger, more diverse simultaneous EEG-fMRI datasets.
