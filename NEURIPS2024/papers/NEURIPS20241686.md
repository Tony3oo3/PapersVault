## Problem

Existing parameterization-based dataset distillation methods reduce storage by compressing spatial or frequency representations but overlook redundancy in the color space. Since images within the same class share similar color distributions and low-level discriminative features (edges, shapes) are preserved even with fewer unique colors, dedicating storage to replicated color information is wasteful. This paper tackles the gap by explicitly minimizing color redundancy at both the individual-image and dataset levels.

## Input / Output

The method takes standard RGB image datasets (CIFAR-10/100 at 32×32, ImageNet subsets at 128×128) with class labels and produces a small synthetic dataset where each image is stored in a reduced-bit format (e.g., 6-bit instead of 8-bit, mapping 256 colors per channel down to 64). The output consists of color-condensed images encoded via bitmap indices referencing a per-image K-color palette, plus the palette network's probability maps. The synthetic set size is governed by an images-per-class (IPC) budget, and the total storage is capped at 8 × IPC × C × H × W bits per class.

## Method

AutoPalette has two core components. First, a lightweight palette network (two 1×1 convolutional layers with a ReLU) predicts a per-pixel probability map over K color buckets, from which a K-color palette is computed as the mean of assigned pixels and the condensed image is reconstructed via argmax index lookup. Three auxiliary losses shape the palette: a maximum color loss ensuring every bucket receives at least one pixel, a palette balance loss promoting uniform pixel distribution across buckets via spatial entropy, and a regularization loss aligning the learned allocation with Median Cut quantization for initialization consistency. Second, a color-guided initialization module applies a generalized graph cut submodular function over Median-Cut-quantized images, measuring sample similarity via cosine similarity of last-layer cross-entropy gradients, to greedily select diverse, representative seeds for the synthetic set. The palette network is trained with the combined palette loss (task + auxiliary terms), while the synthetic base images are updated solely by the task distillation loss (trajectory or distribution matching), making the framework plug-and-play across DD objectives.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 (IPC=50), AutoPalette achieves 79.4% test accuracy with 64-color quantized synthetic images, outperforming the best parameterization baseline SPEED (77.7%) by 1.7%. On CIFAR-100 (IPC=50), it reaches 53.3%, surpassing SPEED (49.1%) by 4.2%. At IPC=10, AutoPalette scores 74.3% on CIFAR-10 and 52.6% on CIFAR-100, exceeding all other parameterization methods by 0.8% and 6.7% respectively. On ImageNet subsets (IPC=10), AutoPalette leads on 4 of 6 subsets, with the largest gains on ImageWoof (44.3% vs 44.1%) and ImageYellow (72.0% vs 70.5%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among parameterization baselines, AutoPalette (64-color) outperforms SPEED [41] by 1.7% on CIFAR-10 IPC=50 (79.4 vs 77.7) and 4.2% on CIFAR-100 IPC=50 (53.3 vs 49.1), and beats FReD [35] by 3.6% and 5.5% on the same settings. Against RTP [9], gains are 5.8% (CIFAR-10 IPC=50) and against HaBa [24] the margin exceeds 30% on CIFAR-100. Among non-parameterization distillation methods, AutoPalette surpasses TM [3] by 7.8% (CIFAR-10 IPC=50) and 12.5% (CIFAR-100 IPC=10), and DM [46] by 16.4% and 9.7% respectively. The closest non-parameterization competitor, DATM [12], achieves 55.0% on CIFAR-100 IPC=50, slightly exceeding AutoPalette's 53.3%, but DATM stores full 8-bit images while AutoPalette uses only 6-bit storage. Traditional color quantization methods (Median Cut [15], OCTree [11]) applied post-hoc to distilled images yield substantially lower accuracy (e.g., 60.7% vs 66.2% at 64 colors on CIFAR-10 IPC=10), confirming the learned palette network's advantage. Coreset baselines (Herding [42], K-Center [33]) trail by 20–50% across all settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

AutoPalette extends the parameterization-based dataset distillation (PDD) line of work, which has previously targeted redundancy in spatial resolution (IDC), factorized bases (HaBa, RTP), latent embeddings, and frequency bands (SPEED, FReD). The paper departs from these by identifying the color space as an underexplored axis of redundancy, introducing a lightweight palette network that learns pixel-level color allocation in a reduced K-color space, and a submodular graph-cut initialization that selects diverse samples based on information gain over quantized images. The distillation objective itself is inherited from trajectory matching (TM/DATM) and distribution matching (DM), positioning AutoPalette as a plug-and-play parameterization layer rather than a new matching formulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is a 4× storage reduction (8-bit → 4-bit per channel) that not only preserves but in several settings improves distillation accuracy, suggesting that much of the color information in synthetic images is redundant for downstream classification. This opens a straightforward path for practitioners to halve or quarter the memory footprint of distilled datasets without retraining. Key limitations include degraded performance on fine-grained subsets where subtle color differences carry discriminative signal, and a uniform color budget across classes that may be suboptimal when classes have heterogeneous color complexity. The authors identify dynamic per-class color depth allocation as a natural next step, and the approach's transfer to video, 3D, or multi-modal distillation remains unexplored.
