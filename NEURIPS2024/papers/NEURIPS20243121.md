## Problem

Existing optimal subsampling probabilities for rare-events data are scale-dependent: rescaling covariates can arbitrarily magnify the influence of inactive features on sampling weights, leading to inefficient or misleading subsamples. Additionally, variable selection for rare-events data has not been studied in the subsampling literature, leaving a gap in how to simultaneously select active features and sample efficiently when the number of ones is very small.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a full dataset of N binary-response observations with p-dimensional covariates (where N₁ ≪ N₀) and a small pilot subsample of size N_pl for lasso-based screening. It outputs a two-stage result: first, an estimated active set from the lasso pilot; second, optimal Poisson subsampling probabilities for zeros (π(x,y) = y + (1−y)ρφ(x)) and an adaptive lasso MSCL estimator yielding sparse parameter estimates and predicted probabilities. The key structural constraint is that the optimal function φ depends only on active variables in theory, but in practice the pilot step must conservatively over-select to avoid excluding true actives.

## Method

The paper introduces a P-optimality criterion that minimizes the asymptotic mean squared prediction error (MSPE) of the IPW adaptive lasso, yielding a scale-invariant optimal subsampling function φ_P-OS(x) ∝ p(x;θ_t)‖Ω_{(A)}^{1/2} M_{(A)}^{-1} ḡ_{(A)}(x;θ_t)‖, where Ω captures second-moment information of the score. Proposition 2 proves invariance to rescaling under a mild condition on the link function g that is satisfied by logistic regression and multi-layer neural networks. The estimation uses a penalized MSCL objective that incorporates the sampling correction l_i = −log(ρφ(x_i)) directly into the log-odds, combined with an adaptive lasso penalty for sparsity. Theorem 4 shows the MSCL estimator dominates the IPW estimator in Loewner ordering of asymptotic variance. A practical two-step algorithm first runs lasso on a small pilot subsample to screen variables and estimate optimal probabilities, then applies Poisson subsampling with the P-OS function and solves the adaptive lasso MSCL on the resulting subsample.

## Results

In simulations (N=500,000, p=50, 4 active variables), the proposed P-OS estimator achieves the lowest eMSE and eMSPE among all optimal subsampling methods across all three parameter settings, and outperforms uniform sampling at every sampling rate. For variable selection, P-OS attains the lowest false negative rate (0.084 at ρ=0.0025) compared to A-OS (0.086) and L-OS (0.088), while uniform sampling has a substantially higher rate (0.168). Computationally, all subsampling methods run in ~0.3–1.3 seconds versus ~113–130 seconds for full-data lasso/adaptive lasso, yielding roughly a 100× speedup. On the real Covertype dataset (N=581,012, 0.47% positive rate), P-OS achieves the highest empirical median AUC among optimal sampling methods; on the Font dataset (N=832,670, 0.5% positive rate), P-OS is significantly better than both A-OS and L-OS.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is the A-OS and L-OS optimal subsampling from Wang et al. (NeurIPS 2021, [22]), which are scale-dependent. In simulations, P-OS consistently achieves lower eMSE and eMSPE than both A-OS and L-OS across all three parameter settings and all sampling rates, with the gap widening at lower sampling rates. For variable selection, P-OS matches or slightly beats A-OS and L-OS on false negative rates (e.g., 0.084 vs. 0.086/0.088 at ρ=0.0025), while all three optimal methods substantially outperform uniform sampling (0.168). On the real Covertype dataset, P-OS achieves the best AUC among the three optimal methods, with L-OS performing worst; on the Font dataset, P-OS is significantly better than both A-OS and L-OS, confirming the scale-invariance advantage in high-dimensional settings. Computationally, all three optimal subsampling methods have similar runtimes (~1 second) and are roughly 100× faster than full-data adaptive lasso (~130 seconds), so the scale-invariance gain of P-OS comes at no additional computational cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the nonuniform negative sampling framework of Wang et al. (NeurIPS 2021), which introduced A- and L-optimal subsampling probabilities for rare-events logistic regression, by incorporating sparse model structure through the adaptive lasso and identifying a critical scale-dependence flaw in the existing optimal functions. The key conceptual departure is the introduction of a prediction-oriented P-optimality criterion that yields scale-invariant sampling probabilities, addressing the problem that inactive features can be arbitrarily rescaled without changing the underlying model yet still distort A-OS or L-OS probabilities. The work also bridges the optimal subsampling literature (Ai et al., Wang & Ma, Keret & Gorfine, etc.) with the penalized variable selection literature (Zou, Zhang & Lu), unifying both into a single two-step framework for massive rare-events data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification that existing optimal subsampling probabilities are scale-dependent and can degrade to uniform sampling performance under certain rescalings is a practically important finding, particularly for variable selection where inactive features may be arbitrarily transformed. The paper fills a recognized gap by establishing oracle properties for adaptive lasso in the rare-events setting and providing a computationally efficient subsampling algorithm that reduces runtime by over 99% compared to full-data estimation. Key limitations include reliance on asymptotic theory (limiting applicability to dense or overparameterized models), assumption of correct model specification, and the use of lasso as the sole pilot estimator; open questions include non-asymptotic guarantees, robustness to model misspecification, and criteria that directly optimize variable selection performance rather than prediction error.
