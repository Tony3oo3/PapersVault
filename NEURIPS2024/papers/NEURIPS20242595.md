## Problem

RLHF policies exploit errors in the proxy reward model, causing overoptimization where proxy reward rises while true reward falls. Existing mitigations rely on ensembles of reward models (full fine-tuning or LoRA adapters) to estimate per-sample uncertainty, incurring prohibitive memory and inference costs that scale poorly with model size. The paper asks whether reward uncertainty can be quantified from a single reward model's last-layer embeddings, and whether a distributionally robust objective built on such estimates can outperform per-sample uncertainty penalization.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a single trained reward model (e.g., LLaMA-7B) and extracts only its last-layer embedding e(x,y) ∈ ℝ^d for each prompt–response pair, along with a pre-computed matrix M_D = λI + Σ e(x_i,y)e(x_i,y)^T built from the preference training set. It also takes a set of reference responses (e.g., chosen responses from the dataset or SFT-policy outputs) to anchor optimization. The outputs are a per-sample scalar uncertainty U_{x,y}^{CI} = b√(e(x,y)^T M_D^{-1} e(x,y)) computed in O(d²) per sample, and a modified reward signal r_{φ̂}(x,y) − (1/λ*) e(x,y)^T M_D^{-1} g that replaces the point reward inside the PPO objective, ultimately yielding an optimized policy.

## Method

The core insight is that the reward model decomposes into a frozen representation encoder e(x,y) and a learned projection weight φ, and that all reward uncertainty stems from estimation error in φ under the Bradley-Terry objective. Under an infinitely-wide NTK assumption, Theorem 3.1 derives a confidence region ‖φ* − φ̂‖_{M_D} ≤ b, yielding the closed-form uncertainty U_{x,y}^{CI} that requires no ensemble and only a single forward pass for e(x,y). Building on this, ADVPO formulates a max-min distributionally robust objective: the outer max optimizes the policy while the inner min searches for the most pessimistic projection weight φ within the confidence ball, rather than penalizing each sample's reward individually. Theorem 4.1 shows the inner minimization admits a closed-form solution φ* = φ̂ − (1/λ*) M_D^{-1} g, collapsing the max-min into a standard gradient-ascent objective with a direction-dependent reward correction. Reference responses are subtracted from the policy's expected embedding to prevent the pessimistic search from stalling the policy, and Lemma 4.2 proves this DRO relaxation is strictly less conservative than per-sample uncertainty penalization because it bounds the expected deviation rather than the expectation of per-sample deviations.

## Results

ADVPO consistently outperforms all baselines in human-assisted pairwise evaluation. On TL;DR, ADVPO achieves a 75.0% win rate against PPO (Δ↑57.0) and an 84.0% win rate against LoraEns (Δ↑68.0). On Anthropic HH, improvements are more modest (e.g., 31.0% win vs PPO, Δ↑11.0) due to noisier reference responses, but ADVPO still leads all baselines. The lightweight CI uncertainty estimator achieves a Pearson correlation of 0.984 (Anthropic HH) and 0.994 (TL;DR) with gold-proxy reward divergence, matching or exceeding the 3×7B ensemble (0.980/0.909) while requiring only a single 7B model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against standard PPO, ADVPO wins 75.0% of pairwise comparisons on TL;DR (Δ↑57.0) and 31.0% on Anthropic HH (Δ↑11.0), confirming that distributionally robust optimization mitigates overoptimization more effectively than vanilla reward maximization. Against LoraEns (Zhai et al., 2023), which uses five LoRA-based reward ensembles with sample-wise uncertainty penalties, ADVPO achieves a dominant 84.0% win rate on TL;DR (Δ↑68.0) and 65.5% on Anthropic HH (Δ↑46.5), while requiring only a single 7B reward model versus five LoRA adapters. Against ENS-s (Coste et al., 2023), the 3×3B ensemble with sample-wise penalization, ADVPO wins 77.0% on TL;DR and 43.0% on Anthropic HH. Notably, ADVPO also outperforms ENS-s-7B (three 7B ensembles) on TL;DR (60.0% win, Δ↑27.0), demonstrating that the distributionally robust formulation is more effective than simply scaling up ensemble size. The ablation AdvPO-noRef (without reference responses) still beats PPO but underperforms full ADVPO, confirming that reference responses prevent excessive pessimism.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the neural contextual bandits framework (Xu et al., 2020) to derive a confidence interval for reward predictions from last-layer embeddings, and extends PPO (Schulman et al., 2017) with a distributionally robust max-min objective over the reward's confidence region. It departs from the dominant line of work on reward overoptimization (Coste et al., 2023; Eisenstein et al., 2023; Zhai et al., 2023), which relies on ensembles of reward models for sample-wise uncertainty penalization, by instead formulating a holistic adversarial optimization over projection weights that is provably less pessimistic. The key conceptual shift is from per-sample reward subtraction to a distributionally robust objective that operates on the reward function space rather than individual samples.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is substantial: a single 7B reward model with a pre-computed M_D^{-1} matrix replaces ensembles of 3–5 models, eliminating the memory and inference bottleneck that has limited real-world deployment of uncertainty-aware RLHF. The theoretical guarantee that ADVPO is less pessimistic than sample-wise penalization (Lemma 4.2) provides a principled reason for the empirical gains. However, the evaluation is confined to 7B LLaMA models on two datasets with a synthetic gold-reward setup, and the human-assisted evaluation uses only 100–200 prompts, leaving open whether the approach scales to 70B+ models or generalizes beyond summarization and dialogue. The authors also note that intermediate-layer embeddings and uncertainty-guided active data selection remain unexplored.
