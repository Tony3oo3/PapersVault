## Problem

While Birkhoff polytope-based relaxations (Sinkhorn, Gumbel-Sinkhorn) have become standard for differentiable permutation optimization, equally effective relaxation methods within the orthogonal group remain unexplored. The orthogonal group offers a lower-dimensional parameterization (n(n−1)/2 vs. (n−1)²) and preserves inner products, yet no temperature-controlled differentiable transformation existed to concentrate orthogonal matrices near permutation matrices without penalty terms.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an unconstrained vector in R^{n(n−1)/2} (the Lie algebra dimension, filled into an upper-triangular zero-diagonal matrix), a temperature parameter τ ∈ (0,1], and a fixed orthogonal matrix B ∈ SO(n) for boundary shifting. It outputs an orthogonal matrix guaranteed to lie on SO(n) that converges to the closest permutation matrix as τ→0. Internally, the Hungarian algorithm solves a linear assignment problem to identify the nearest permutation matrix, and eigendecomposition computes the matrix power for geodesic interpolation.

## Method

OT4P is a two-step differentiable transformation: (I) map the unconstrained vector to SO(n) via the Lie exponential of its skew-symmetric part, with a left-multiplication by B to shift the injectivity boundary away from permutation matrices with eigenvalue −1; (II) move the resulting orthogonal matrix along the geodesic toward its closest permutation matrix P (found by the Hungarian algorithm) using the matrix power (PᵀO)^τ, computed via eigendecomposition. The key geometric insight is performing the interpolation in the tangent space T_P SO(n) rather than the Lie algebra, avoiding slow or failed convergence of the logm series when O or P are far from the identity. For stochastic optimization over latent permutations, OT4P is combined with the re-parameterization trick: sampling P = ψ_τ(φ(A + Bε)) decouples the stochasticity from the parameters, yielding a low-variance Monte Carlo gradient estimator for variational inference.

## Results

On the mode connectivity task (Table 1), OT4P(τ=0.3) achieves a log(1+ℓ₁) distance of 0.000 and 100% precision across all three architectures (MLP5, VGG11, ResNet18), perfectly recovering the ground-truth permutation. On the neuron identity inference task (Table 2), OT4P(τ=0.3) attains 100% precision and a marginal log-likelihood of −130.9 in the hardest setting (Known 5%), compared to −2256 for Gumbel-Sinkhorn and −3040 for Naive. On permutation synchronization (Figure 3), OT4P consistently achieves the highest F-score across all problem sizes on WILLOW-ObjectClass, with F-scores exceeding 97% on the CMU House case study (k=110) in under 4 seconds.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OT4P decisively outperforms Sinkhorn [54] on VGG11 mode connectivity (0.000 vs. 11.61 log(1+ℓ₁), 100% vs. 63.08% precision), which the authors attribute to unreliable local minima in the Birkhoff polytope relaxation. Weight Matching [2] matches OT4P on MLP5 and VGG11 but fails on ResNet18 (1.215 vs. 0.000 log(1+ℓ₁)) due to sensitivity to random initialization. On neuron identity inference, OT4P(τ=0.3) achieves 100% precision in the Known 5% setting, while Gumbel-Sinkhorn [49] drops to 62.08% and Naive [43] to 8.96%. On permutation synchronization (WILLOW-ObjectClass), OT4P consistently surpasses OrthReg [72], RiemanBirk [6], and Sinkhorn [49] across all problem sizes; OrthReg is the closest competitor but suffers from instability in its regularization term, while RiemanBirk and Sinkhorn produce unreliable local minima. The only setting where OT4P is suboptimal is neuron inference at τ=0.7 with Known 5% (74.16% precision), suggesting that higher temperatures can hinder convergence in the hardest probabilistic scenarios.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Lie group trivialization framework of Lezcano Casado [39] for mapping unconstrained vectors to the special orthogonal group via the matrix exponential, and extends the reparameterization trick for Lie groups from Falorsi et al. [14] to the permutation relaxation setting. It departs from the dominant Birkhoff polytope-based relaxation paradigm (Sinkhorn, Gumbel-Sinkhorn, Linderman et al.) by proposing the first penalty-free, temperature-controlled relaxation on the orthogonal group that also supports stochastic optimization. Prior orthogonal group approaches (Zavlanos & Pappas, Jiang et al.) relied on explicit regularization terms to push matrices toward permutation vertices, whereas OT4P achieves this through geodesic interpolation controlled by a temperature parameter, unifying the deterministic and probabilistic settings under a single transformation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

OT4P provides a principled alternative to Birkhoff polytope methods with a lower-dimensional parameterization (n(n-1)/2 vs (n-1)²) and inner-product preservation, which the experiments suggest translates to more reliable optimization landscapes (fewer spurious local minima). Key limitations include the O(n³) eigendecomposition cost for very large n (>1000), the need for careful noise distribution design in the reparameterization step, and boundary issues that currently prevent direct use as a building block in deep networks. Open questions include whether the orthogonal group advantage persists at much larger scales, whether the noise distribution can be tailored to the geometry of SO(n), and whether an analytical solution exists to relocate all permutation matrices into the injectivity domain U.
