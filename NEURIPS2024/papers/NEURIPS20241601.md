## Problem

While Zhang et al. (2024) empirically observed that DNNs learn symbolic interactions in two distinct phases—first eliminating medium/high-order interactions, then gradually learning increasingly complex ones—no mathematical proof existed for this two-phase dynamics. This gap left the theoretical mechanism by which a DNN transitions from under-fitting to over-fitting unexplained, despite interactions being established as faithful primitive inference patterns of DNNs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241601/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a DNN's parameters at a given training epoch (characterized by a noise level σ² on the interaction triggering functions) and an input sample with n input variables (image patches, text token embeddings, or point clusters). It produces an analytic solution ŵ for interaction weights as a function of σ², from which the theoretical distribution of interaction strength I_theo^(k) over orders k=1,...,n is computed. This theoretical distribution is then matched against the real distribution I_real^(k) extracted from the actual DNN at each training epoch, with σ² serving as the free parameter that aligns theory to practice.

## Method

The proof proceeds in three steps: (1) rewriting DNN inference on a masked sample as a weighted sum of interaction triggering functions J_T(x) derived from the Taylor expansion of the network output at the fully-masked baseline; (2) reformulating DNN learning as a linear regression of the converged outputs y_S on these triggering functions, with the true interaction weights w* as the target; (3) introducing parameter noise (Var[ε_T] = 2^|T|σ²) into the triggering functions and solving the resulting regularized least-squares problem to obtain the analytic solution ŵ = (JᵀJ + 2ⁿdiag(c))⁻¹JᵀJw*. The key result (Proposition 1) shows that the norm ratio ‖m̂_T‖₂/‖m̂_T'‖₂ for |T|<|T'| decreases monotonically as σ² decreases, proving that the DNN suppresses high-order interactions relative to low-order ones at high noise (start of phase 2) and progressively learns higher-order interactions as noise diminishes. The first phase is characterized as the convergence of chaotic random interactions to this noisy optimum under large σ².

## Results

The paper's primary contribution is a theoretical proof (Theorem 3, Proposition 1, Theorem 4–5) that the two-phase dynamics of interaction learning in DNNs can be derived from a linear regression formulation under parameter noise. The key quantitative validation (Figure 4) shows that the theoretical interaction-strength distribution $I_{\text{theo}}^{(k)}$, computed from the analytic solution $\hat{w} = \hat{M}w^*$ at varying noise levels $\sigma^2$, closely matches the empirically measured distribution $I_{\text{real}}^{(k)}$ across training epochs for AlexNet, VGG, BERT-Tiny, BERT-Medium, and DGCNN on six datasets. Figure 3 confirms Proposition 1: the relative strength ratio $r^{(k)} = \|\hat{m}_T\|_2 / \|\hat{m}_{T'}\|_2$ monotonically decreases as $\sigma^2$ decreases for all orders $k = 1, \ldots, n-1$, validating the predicted shift from low-order to high-order interactions in the second phase.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical paper with no competing methods to benchmark against; the `compares_to` list is empty. The empirical validation instead compares the paper's theoretical predictions against ground-truth measurements from real DNNs. Specifically, the theoretical interaction-strength distribution $I_{\text{theo}}^{(k)}$ (derived from the closed-form solution in Theorem 3 at a chosen $\sigma^2$) is matched against the real distribution $I_{\text{real}}^{(k)}$ extracted from trained networks at each epoch (Figure 4). The match is reported to be close across all tested architectures (AlexNet, VGG, BERT-Tiny, BERT-Medium, DGCNN) and datasets (MNIST, CIFAR-10, CUB-200-2011, Tiny-ImageNet, SST-2, ShapeNet), with the $\sigma^2$ value selected per epoch to achieve the best fit. The two-phase dynamics (spindle-shaped initial distribution → low-order dominance → gradual increase in order) are confirmed to hold universally across these settings, consistent with the prior empirical observation in [41].

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper provides the mathematical proof of the two-phase dynamics of interaction complexity that was empirically observed by Zhang et al. [41], completing a key piece of the interaction theory system developed over three years by the same group. The proof builds on the sparsity property [27], the universal matching property [4], and the finding that DNNs learn simple interactions more easily than complex ones [22, 26]. The central technical move is reformulating DNN learning as a linear regression on interaction triggering functions (derived via Taylor expansion), where the key assumption is that parameter noise on higher-order interactions scales exponentially with order (2^|T|σ²), which naturally produces the observed phase transition from low-order to high-order interaction learning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The proven two-phase dynamics offer a per-sample theoretical mechanism for tracking the onset of overfitting: the time when high-order interactions begin to grow marks the transition from generalizable to non-generalizable inference patterns. This shifts overfitting monitoring from a dataset-level metric to a sample-level diagnostic, potentially enabling targeted early stopping or removal of 'hard samples.' However, the proof relies on strong simplifying assumptions (independent noise terms, uniform exponential variance scaling, and treating learning as regression toward pre-defined true interactions), and the practical utility of per-sample interaction tracking remains to be validated at scale. Open questions include whether the theory extends to multi-task or continual learning settings where the 'true interactions' w* are not fixed.
