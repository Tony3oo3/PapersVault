## Problem

Noisy label learning research has been dominated by classification, leaving regression with only a handful of prior approaches that either require known noise rates or provide only indirect regularization benefits. Additionally, no standardized benchmark datasets existed for evaluating noisy label regression across diverse domains, making systematic comparison impossible.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20244050/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes either RGB face images (128×128, processed by ResNet-50), pre-extracted VGG-16 penultimate features (for clothing price prediction), or tabular audio features (for music year estimation), each paired with a noisy continuous scalar label. It outputs a clean subset S of the training data (selected via a Mixture of Neighboring Fragments scoring function) that is then used to train a downstream regression model producing the final continuous prediction. The fragment count F is fixed at 4 across all experiments, and the method is noise-rate-agnostic, requiring no prior knowledge of the corruption level.

## Method

ConFrag discretizes the continuous label space into F fragments, then finds F/2 maximally contrasting fragment pairs by solving a perfect matching problem on a complete graph where edge weights are inter-fragment label distances (maximizing the minimum edge weight). Each pair trains a small binary-classification expert feature extractor (ResNet-18 or smaller MLP), which generalizes better than a single multi-class extractor due to larger inter-class margins and the conversion of closed-set noise into less harmful open-set noise. Sample selection uses a Mixture of Neighboring Fragments model: a fragment prior (softmax over inverse distance to fragment means) gates a neighborhood agreement score that requires both self-agreement (the expert for fragment f predicts f over its contrast f⁺) and at least one adjacent fragment's self-agreement. Two complementary agreement variants—predictive (binary classifier output) and representational (KNN in learned feature space)—are computed and their selected sets are unioned. A neighborhood jittering regularizer randomly shifts fragment boundaries each epoch within a bounded range, creating a partially overlapping mixture that expands each expert's effective coverage and prevents overfitting to noisy samples.

## Results

ConFrag and its co-teaching variant Co-ConFrag achieve the lowest MRAE (Mean Relative Absolute Error, %) across all six curated benchmark datasets and all noise conditions. On the most challenging setting (IMDB-Clean-B, 40% symmetric noise), ConFrag reduces MRAE to 12.64% versus 32.05% for vanilla training, outperforming the best baseline (BMM at 21.54%) by nearly 9 points. On the real-noise IMDB-WIKI-B dataset, Co-ConFrag achieves −8.86% MRAE (i.e., better than the noise-free model), compared to −5.26% for the next-best baseline (C-Mixup). The advantage is consistent across age prediction, clothing price prediction, and music year estimation domains, with ConFrag also introducing the Error Residual Ratio (ERR) metric to capture varying noise severity in regression.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ConFrag/Co-ConFrag outperforms all fourteen baselines on every dataset and noise condition tested. The strongest competing baselines vary by domain: on age prediction (AFAD-B, IMDB-Clean-B), C-Mixup and BMM are the closest challengers (e.g., C-Mixup at 14.80 vs. ConFrag's 8.16 on AFAD-B Sym40; BMM at 21.54 vs. ConFrag's 12.64 on IMDB-Clean-B Sym40), while on SHIFT15M-B, Selfie (10.22) and DY-S (11.94) come closest to ConFrag (6.18). On MSD-B, CNLCU-H (4.98) and Co-Selfie (4.41) are the nearest baselines to ConFrag (4.94) under symmetric 40% noise, but Co-ConFrag (2.98) still leads by 1.4–1.5 points. Small-loss selection methods (CNLCU-S/H, Sigua, BMM, DY-S) and refurbishment methods (Selfie, Co-Selfie, AUX) generally degrade more severely under high noise rates (60–80% symmetric), where ConFrag maintains a clear margin. Regularization-based baselines (C-Mixup, RDI, CDR, D2L) show the most competitive results under moderate noise but still trail ConFrag by 3–10 MRAE points across all settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

ConFrag builds directly on the Mixture of Experts framework (Jacobs et al., 1991), repurposing it for noisy label regression by using fragment priors as soft gating and neighborhood agreement as expert outputs. It departs from the dominant noisy label learning literature, which has focused almost exclusively on classification, by exploiting the continuous ordered correlation between label and feature spaces that is unique to regression. The contrastive fragment pairing idea—converting a multi-class discretization into F/2 binary sub-problems to transform closed-set noise into open-set noise—is a novel conceptual departure from prior approaches like small-loss selection, transition matrix estimation, and MixUp-based regularization that have been adapted to regression. The neighborhood jittering regularization, which creates a partially overlapping mixture model, is also new and addresses a known limitation of MoE where experts model disjoint subsets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This paper provides the first comprehensive benchmark for noisy label regression (six balanced datasets across age prediction, price prediction, and music year estimation, with fourteen baselines), filling a clear gap in the literature where only three prior works addressed this problem. The ERR metric addresses a real evaluation gap by accounting for varying noise severity in continuous labels, which standard classification-oriented metrics cannot capture. A key open question is scalability: the MoE-based parameter size grows linearly with the number of fragments F, and the authors identify parameter sharing and MPO decomposition as promising but unexplored directions. The finding that open-set noise is less harmful than closed-set noise in regression, and that contrastive pairing exploits this, could inform broader noisy label strategies beyond regression.
