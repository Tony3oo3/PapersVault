## Problem

Self-supervised adversarial training (SAT) still suffers from a large robust generalization gap (~19–22%) and natural accuracy degradation, mirroring issues in supervised adversarial training, yet lacks a well-established formulation connecting the two paradigms. The paper attributes these problems to insufficient data complexity and model regularization, and challenges the prior conclusion that strong and diverse augmentations are harmful to SAT robustness.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 32×32 RGB images (CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100) or 96×96 images (STL-10) through four parallel input streams defined by the combination of augmentation type (basic: random resized crop + horizontal flip; diverse: TrivialAugment + basic) and perturbation type (clean vs. PGD-adversarial). The output is a pretrained robust CNN encoder (ResNet-18/34/50) whose features are distilled from a naturally trained SSL teacher; downstream classification is obtained via simple linear probing or adversarial full finetuning, yielding both clean and robust accuracy metrics.

## Method

DAQ-SDP combines two mechanisms within a two-stage SAT framework. First, Diverse Augmented Query (DAQ) feeds diversely augmented samples as pseudo-supervised queries into a multi-stream adversarial student, where an Aug-Adv Pairwise-BatchNorm strategy assigns a dedicated BatchNorm layer to each of the four training distributions so that features from different augmentation/perturbation regimes fit their clean-model counterparts without interfering. Second, Self-supervised Double Perturbation (SDP) introduces adversarial weight perturbation directly into the SSL cosine-similarity loss (not just sample-level PGD), regularizing the smoothness of the feature-similarity landscape; this weight perturbation is applied only after ~60 epochs once pseudo-supervised features have stabilized, to avoid impeding early natural-feature learning. The approach is SSL-framework-agnostic—plugging into SimCLR, SimSiam, or BYOL without modifying their learning objectives—and after training only the basic-adversarial BatchNorm layers are retained for inference.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with ResNet-34 and SimCLR, DAQ-SDP achieves 84.57% clean accuracy, 58.57% PGD robust accuracy, and 49.22% AutoAttack accuracy, outperforming the strongest baseline DecoupledACL by +2.11%, +1.71%, and +1.23% respectively. On CIFAR-100 with the same setup, DAQ-SDP reaches 53.54% clean, 33.09% PGD, and 23.42% AutoAttack, improving over DecoupledACL by +2.10%, +2.41%, and +2.11%. The method also generalizes to positive-pair-only frameworks (SimSiam, BYOL) and larger models (ResNet-50), where it achieves 85.22% clean and 58.05% PGD on CIFAR-10.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DAQ-SDP outperforms all three baselines on every metric across both CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100. Against DecoupledACL [38] (the two-stage SAT baseline), DAQ-SDP gains +2.11% clean and +1.71% PGD on CIFAR-10, and +2.10% clean and +2.41% PGD on CIFAR-100. Against TARO [23], which achieves slightly higher clean accuracy on CIFAR-10 (84.23% vs 84.57%), DAQ-SDP leads by a large margin on robustness: +5.21% PGD (58.57% vs 53.36%) and +3.54% AutoAttack (49.22% vs 45.68%). Against DynACL+AIR [24,37], the gains are the largest, with +4.78% PGD on CIFAR-10 and +9.18% PGD on CIFAR-100. Notably, DAQ-SDP is the only method that generalizes to positive-pair-only frameworks (SimSiam, BYOL), where it still improves PGD accuracy by +1.36% over DecoupledACL on SimSiam and +1.31% on BYOL.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the two-stage SAT framework of Zhang et al. (DecoupledACL, ECCV 2022), which decouples SSL pretraining from adversarial training using pseudo-supervision from clean features. It adapts the adversarial weight perturbation idea from Wu et al. (2020) — originally designed for supervised classification loss — into the label-free SSL pretext task, and borrows the multi-stream augmentation structure from Addepalli et al. (NeurIPS 2022). The key departure is a conceptual challenge to prior SAT works (Zhang et al. 2022, Luo et al. 2023) that concluded strong and diverse augmentations are harmful for robustness; here, diverse augmentations are shown to be beneficial when paired with per-distribution BatchNorm layers, and model-level perturbation is introduced alongside the sample-level perturbation that prior SAT methods exclusively used.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework-agnostic design (applicable to SimCLR, SimSiam, BYOL without modifying learning objectives) makes this a practical drop-in improvement for existing SSL pipelines, and the unified analysis of SAT and supervised AT generalization gaps provides a clearer diagnostic lens for the field. However, the evaluation is confined to CIFAR-10/100 and STL-10 with ResNet backbones, and the authors note that smaller models (ResNet-18) do not benefit, suggesting the method requires sufficient capacity. Open questions include whether the diverse-augmentation finding and weight-perturbation transfer hold at ImageNet scale, whether the multi-stream BatchNorm overhead is acceptable in practice, and whether the modest 1–2% robust accuracy gains persist under stronger attacks or more diverse downstream tasks.
