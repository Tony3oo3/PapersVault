## Problem

Existing protein language models capture mutations only implicitly through evolutionary plausibility scores, which are too vague to provide human-understandable explanations of mutational effects and too misaligned with human preferences to guide directed evolution in practice. The paper addresses this by asking how to make PLMs explicitly model mutations in a unified feature space that is aligned with natural language semantics, enabling both explainable interpretation and instruction-driven engineering.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242533/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For mutation explanation, the model takes a wild-type amino acid sequence and a single-site substitution (position + original/mutated residue) and generates a free-text description of the functional consequences. For mutation engineering, it takes the same wild-type sequence plus a natural-language instruction describing desired effects and outputs a proposed mutation as a position-amino-acid pair. Internally, both tasks operate through K=32 fixed-length delta feature vectors z_Δ that bridge the variable-length protein representations and the LLM's textual embedding space, and the fine-tuning uses a two-round dialogue format where protein function descriptions are generated before mutation-specific reasoning.

## Method

The core architecture is a protein delta network with an encoder-decoder design: the delta encoder uses cross-attention (inspired by BLIP) to compress the element-wise subtraction of ESM-2 (650M) representations between mutant and wild-type (h_Δ = h_mt − h_wt) into K=32 latent vectors z_Δ, while the delta decoder reconstructs h_Δ from z_Δ conditioned on wild-type representations, feeding into position and amino-acid prediction heads. The LLM (BioMedGPT-LM, built on LLaMA2-7B) is connected via K trainable soft tokens that summarize textual semantics into the same z_Δ space. Training follows a two-stage transfer learning pipeline: pre-training on 1.1M protein-text pairs from biomedical literature using next-token prediction (protein→text) and conditional masked language modeling (text→protein), followed by fine-tuning on MutaDescribe (171K mutations with enriched textual annotations) using a chain-of-thought strategy that first elicits protein function descriptions before reasoning about mutational effects. Only 75M parameters are trained via LoRA (rank 16) on the LLM plus the delta network components, keeping the remaining 7.4B frozen.

## Results

On the MutaDescribe benchmark for mutation explanation, MutaPLM achieves an average ROUGE-L of 21.34 and BLEU-2 of 13.61, outperforming the strongest baseline (Fine-tuned ESM-2) by 6.46% in ROUGE-L and 7.25 in BLEU-2. For mutation engineering, MutaPLM reaches 53.51% accuracy and 40.94% top-50 recall, improving over the original ESM-2 by 1.6-fold in recall (40.94% vs. 25.15%). In human-AI collaborative evaluation, 40.22% of MutaPLM's mutation explanations are categorized as Accurate or Relevant by domain experts, and multi-round optimization experiments on 6 protein benchmarks show MutaPLM consistently yields higher-fitness mutants than EvoProtGrad and random sampling.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MutaPLM decisively outperforms all baselines on both mutation explanation and engineering. In mutation explanation, it surpasses Fine-tuned ESM-2 (the strongest supervised baseline) by 6.46% ROUGE-L and 7.25 BLEU-2, and beats GPT-4+ESM-2 (the strongest LLM-assisted PLM) by 8.89% ROUGE-L and 3.24 BLEU-2. Zero-shot LLMs (Galactica-6.7B, ProLLaMA, Mol-Instructions) perform poorly, with ProLLaMA scoring only 1.02 ROUGE-L, confirming that instruction-tuned protein LLMs lack the cross-modal knowledge needed for mutation explanation. Few-shot GPT-4 variants (1-shot, 5-shot, 5-shot kNN) achieve moderate ROUGE-L (9.14–12.31) but are far below MutaPLM, and the kNN retrieval strategy helps BLEU-2 (9.69) without closing the gap. In mutation engineering, MutaPLM (53.51% Acc, 40.94% Rec@50) improves over Fine-tuned ESM-2 (51.43%, 33.77%) by 2.09% accuracy and 6.17% recall, and over zero-shot ESM-2 (35.84%, 25.15%) by 1.6-fold in recall. ProtST (ESM-2) [42], which aligns protein and text representations via contrastive learning, performs near-random (6.49% Acc), demonstrating that simple cross-modal alignment is insufficient for mutation modeling. EvoProtGrad [87], a gradient-based multi-round optimizer, is consistently outperformed by MutaPLM across all 6 protein benchmarks in the multi-round optimization visualization (Fig. 5), particularly in early optimization rounds.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm of protein language models (ESM-2, ProtTrans, ProtGPT2) that capture mutations only implicitly through evolutionary plausibility scores, and from LLM-based protein models (ProLLaMA, Mol-Instructions, Galactica) that treat protein sequences as static inputs without modeling the delta between wild-type and mutant. The key architectural novelty is the protein delta network, which explicitly encodes the subtraction of PLM representations (h_Δ = h_mt − h_wt) into a unified feature space aligned with textual semantics via cross-attention modules inspired by BLIP. The transfer learning pipeline with a chain-of-thought strategy that chains protein function descriptions to mutational effects in a two-round dialog is a novel application of CoT reasoning to the protein domain, and the text-instruction-guided mutation engineering formulation replaces the conventional fitness-score navigation used in prior directed evolution work (EvoProtGrad, AdaLEAD, diffusion-based design).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MutaPLM and MutaDescribe open a new interface for protein mutation studies by making natural language the medium for both explaining and navigating mutations, which is directly relevant to directed evolution in industry, biotechnology, and therapeutics. The MutaDescribe dataset (171K mutations with textual annotations) is the first of its kind and could become a standard benchmark, though it is limited to single-site substitutions and lacks 3D structural information. Key open questions include extending to multi-point mutations and indels, incorporating structural changes, and building active learning loops with wet-lab feedback. The authors also flag safety concerns about potential misuse for generating pathogenic mutations, underscoring the need for responsible deployment.
