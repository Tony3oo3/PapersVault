## Problem

Label Distribution Learning (LDL), which predicts a full probability distribution over classes for each instance, has been extensively studied on IID data such as images and texts but has not been generalized to graph-structured data. Real-world graphs are typically heterogeneous, with diverse node types, attributes, and neighborhood structures that complicate message-passing and create inconsistencies between topological proximity and feature similarity. This paper pioneers the heterogeneous graph label distribution learning (HGDL) problem, addressing how to jointly handle graph heterogeneity and topology-feature inconsistency for distribution prediction.

## Input / Output

The method takes a heterogeneous graph with multiple node and edge types, feature vectors for target nodes only (non-target nodes serve purely as structural intermediaries), and a predefined set of meta-paths that connect target nodes through intermediate types. In a transductive regime, ground-truth label distributions are available for a subset of target nodes during training. The output is a q-dimensional probability distribution for each target node, where each entry represents the descriptive degree of that node belonging to a given class and all entries sum to one.

## Method

HGDL's key innovation is a proactive meta-path aggregation paradigm: rather than learning node embeddings from each meta-path graph separately and then fusing them (the reactive approach used by HAN and SeHGNN), it first learns a single optimal graph topology by applying GAT-style attention over k meta-path adjacency matrices, then learns embeddings from this unified topology. A topology-and-feature consistency-aware graph transformer then harmonizes local topological structure with global feature similarity by computing a feature-based attention matrix (via query-key projections) and intersecting it element-wise with the learned topology, effectively performing a learnable, consistency-driven edge dropout. The entire pipeline is trained end-to-end with a KL-divergence loss between predicted and ground-truth distributions, augmented by a regularization term that penalizes uniform attention across meta-paths to encourage discriminative topology selection. PAC-Bayes analysis provides a generalization bound showing HGDL's learned topology outperforms any single meta-path graph, with the sparsification from the consistency mechanism further tightening the error bound.

## Results

HGDL wins in 99 out of 180 evaluation settings (5 datasets × 6 metrics × 6 baselines) and achieves the best KL-divergence by a large margin across all five datasets, with an average 15% improvement over the second-best method. On COD, CHD, IND, and KL metrics, HGDL excels in 20 out of 30 settings on average; on CAD and CLD, it wins in 11 and 8 out of 30 settings respectively. The method's advantage is most pronounced on KL-divergence (its training objective), where it converges well to minimize distribution distance, as confirmed by validation loss curves.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HGDL outperforms all three baselines (GCN_KL, HAN_KL, SeHGNN_KL) on KL-divergence across all five datasets by a large margin, with an average 15% improvement over the second-best result. On COD, CHD, and IND metrics (which are strongly related to KL-divergence), HGDL also enjoys significant improvements over all baselines on DRUG, ACM, and URBAN. However, HAN_KL and SeHGNN_KL achieve better CAD and CLD scores on the YELP and DBLP datasets specifically, which the authors attribute to YELP's limited feature dimensionality (19 features) and DBLP's small number of classes (4) combined with rich bag-of-words features. The ablation variants confirm that both the proactive topology homogenization (HGDL_TH) and the consistency-aware transformer (HGDL_transformer, HGDL_ED) are necessary: removing either component degrades performance on most datasets, with HGDL_ED (random edge dropout) showing no improvement over HGDL_transformer (zero dropout) in KL-divergence.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Label Distribution Learning (LDL), originally developed for IID data such as images and text [5,6,7], into the heterogeneous graph domain, where no prior work has addressed the problem. It departs from existing heterogeneous GNN methods like HAN [25] and SeHGNN [27] by inverting their meta-path aggregation paradigm: rather than learning embeddings from each meta-path graph and then fusing them (reactive), HGDL proactively learns an optimal aggregated topology before any embedding learning occurs. The graph transformer component draws on graph transformer architectures [30] and the edge dropout idea [31], but repurposes the attention mechanism to enforce a neighbor-set intersection between feature-space and topology-space consistencies rather than simply weighting or dropping edges.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

HGDL provides the first formalization and end-to-end solution for predicting label distributions on heterogeneous graphs, with practical implications for urban functionality delineation, drug-disease association, and scholarly profiling where single-label or multi-label classification is too coarse. The PAC-Bayes generalization bound (Theorems 1–2) gives theoretical grounding that the learned meta-path topology outperforms any single meta-path, and the sparsification effect of the consistency-aware transformer tightens the bound. Limitations include the transductive setting (ground-truth distributions required for a training subset), the O(n) parameter complexity of topology homogenization, and the need for meta-paths to be predefined by domain knowledge. Open questions include whether the proactive paradigm extends to inductive or dynamic graphs, and whether meta-paths can be discovered automatically rather than hand-specified.
