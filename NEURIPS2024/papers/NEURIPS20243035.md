## Problem

Automated organoid quantification in high-throughput microscopy is bottlenecked by the absence of large, open biomedical datasets that account for annotation subjectivity. Unlike natural image domains, very few biomedical object-detection datasets provide multiple independent label sets, making it impossible to quantify label noise or assess how annotation variability affects model evaluation. MultiOrg addresses this gap by releasing the first organoid detection dataset with three distinct label sets from two expert annotators at two time points, enabling systematic study of intra- and inter-rater uncertainty.

## Input / Output

Input consists of full-well 2D bright-field microscopy images (approximately 6390×5724 px at 1.29 μm/px) generated by maximum projection of 3D z-stacks, split into 512×512 px patches for training. At inference, a dual-scale sliding window (512 px and 2048 px windows with down-sampling factors of 2 and 8, 50% overlap) is applied to the full image, followed by NMS at 0.5. Output is a set of single-class bounding boxes with confidence scores; the accompanying napari plugin additionally allows interactive curation of predictions before extracting per-organoid features for downstream biological analysis.

## Method

The core contribution is a 411-image, 63,000-organoid dataset with three test label sets (test⁰ from two annotators at time t⁰, and test_A¹/test_B¹ from re-annotation at t¹), enabling multi-rater uncertainty quantification via intra- and inter-rater F1, Precision, and Recall at IoU 0.5. Four standard CNN detectors (Faster R-CNN, SSD, YOLOv3, RTMDet) are benchmarked using mmdetection with COCO-pretrained initialization, AdamW at lr 1e-5, 400 epochs, and standard augmentations. A key empirical finding is that models trained and validated on t⁰ labels achieve their best P-R curves against t¹ labels, demonstrating that deep learning is resilient to the higher label noise present in the initial annotations. The best trade-off model (YOLOv3) is packaged as the napari-organoid-counter v0.2.2 plugin with adjustable confidence thresholds and multi-scale sliding-window inference for real-time organoid quantification.

## Results

On the MultiOrg test set, SSD achieves the best overall mAP@0.5 IoU of 68.09% (mean across three label sets), followed by YOLOv3 (64.64%), RTMDet (61.60%), and Faster R-CNN (60.67%). At the standard 0.5 model confidence threshold, YOLOv3 attains the highest Precision (0.66) and F1-score (0.62), while SSD achieves the highest Recall (0.76) among the top-performing models. Notably, although all models were trained and validated on t⁰ labels, they show the best agreement with the t¹ re-annotations (test_B¹), confirming the resilience of deep learning to label noise.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SSD outperforms all other models on mAP@0.5 IoU (68.09% vs. 64.64% for YOLOv3, 61.60% for RTMDet, and 60.67% for Faster R-CNN), making it the best-performing model overall. However, at the standard 0.5 model confidence threshold, YOLOv3 matches or exceeds SSD on Precision (0.66 vs. 0.52) and F1-score (0.62 vs. 0.61), and is selected as the backbone for the released Napari plugin due to its superior inference speed (13 s/image vs. 59 s for SSD). Faster R-CNN exhibits an extreme Precision–Recall trade-off: it achieves the highest Recall (0.91) but the lowest Precision (0.19), resulting in the worst F1-score (0.31). RTMDet occupies a middle position, with mAP@0.5 IoU of 61.60% and the highest mAP@0.75 IoU among non-SSD models (28.51%), but its inference time (114 s/image) is the slowest of the four. All models show a consistent pattern of better agreement with the t¹ re-annotations than with the t⁰ labels they were trained on, suggesting the initial annotations contained more noise that the models did not fully learn.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground in the organoid detection literature by introducing the first multi-rater organoid dataset with multiple label sets, departing from prior single-annotation datasets (OrganoQuant, OrganoID, GOAT, Tellu) that treat one set of labels as ground truth. Conceptually, it bridges two previously separate threads: the biomedical object detection literature (which has lacked multi-rater annotations) and the uncertainty quantification literature (which has multi-rater datasets for segmentation and classification but very few for detection, e.g., VinDr-CXR and NuCLS). The benchmark itself repurposes standard CNN-based detectors (Faster R-CNN, SSD, YOLOv3, RTMDet) via mmdetection, with no architectural novelty, positioning the contribution squarely in dataset and evaluation methodology rather than model design.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dataset addresses a critical gap: biomedical object detection has lacked open multi-rater resources to quantify how annotation subjectivity affects model evaluation, and this is the second-largest organoid dataset available. The finding that models trained on t⁰ labels align better with t¹ re-annotations (particularly test_B¹) provides empirical evidence for DL resilience to label noise in a real biomedical setting, though this is consistent with prior theoretical work. Key limitations include the inability to incorporate multiple label sets into the training loop, the absence of multi-class annotation for the two organoid types present, and the reliance on pseudo-labels as annotation starting points. Open questions include how to leverage multiple label sets during training, whether DL-assisted annotation baselines would shift the inter-rater dynamics, and whether the findings generalize beyond lung organoids.
