## Problem

Sharpness Aware Minimization (SAM) improves generalization across architectures, but its scaling behaviour as networks grow wider is poorly understood. This paper shows that under standard SAM with any width-dependent global perturbation scaling, the infinite-width limit reduces to perturbing only the last layer, rendering the inner backward pass computationally wasteful and preventing the perturbation radius ρ from transferring across model widths alongside the learning rate η.

## Input / Output

The method takes a neural network architecture (MLP, ResNet-18, or ViT-S/16), a supervised training dataset (CIFAR-10 or ImageNet-1K), and two hyperparameters: a base learning rate η and a base perturbation radius ρ. The key unusual aspect is that ρ is not applied uniformly but is rescaled layerwise according to the derived μP² exponents (e.g., n^{1/2} for input-like layers, n^{-1/2} for hidden-like layers, n^{-3/2} for output-like layers). The output is a trained model whose optimal (η, ρ) pair transfers zero-shot across widths, eliminating the need to re-tune the perturbation radius at each scale.

## Method

The paper extends the Tensor Programs framework by introducing bcd-parameterizations, which augment the existing abc-parameterizations (layerwise initialization variance and learning rate scalings) with layerwise perturbation radius scalings. Using NE⊗OR⊤ computation rules, it rigorously tracks all width-dependent scalings through both the ascent (perturbation) and descent (update) steps of SAM, proving that standard SAM with global scaling ρ·n^{-1/2} yields vanishing perturbations in all hidden layers. The core insight is that perturbations must scale like updates in spectral norm (‖ε^l‖_* = Θ(‖δW^l‖_*)) to be effective, since both are gradient-based and correlated with incoming activations. This yields a unique layerwise perturbation scaling (Theorem 11) that, combined with μP's initialization and learning rate scalings, defines μP² — the only parameterization achieving both feature learning and effective perturbations in every layer at infinite width. A general spectral condition (*) is also provided to derive μP² scalings for SAM variants (ASAM, SAM-ON) and architectures (ResNets, ViTs) without re-deriving the full tensor program.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with ResNet-18 (width multiplier 4, SGD base optimizer), the best μP² variant (SAM-ON) achieves 97.34% ± 0.08 test accuracy, improving over SAM-ON in SP (97.29%) and SAM with global perturbation scaling in μP (97.19%). The improvement of SAM over the base SGD optimizer is approximately 1.08–1.26% in μP² versus 0.93–0.96% in SP or μP with global scaling. For ViT-S/16 on ImageNet-1K, μP² achieves ρ-transfer across widths with large improvements over AdamW in μP. The key qualitative result is that μP² is the unique parameterization achieving joint hyperparameter transfer of both learning rate η and perturbation radius ρ across model widths.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Compared to standard SAM with global perturbation scaling (Foret et al., 2021), μP² achieves consistent joint transfer of both η and ρ across widths, whereas global scaling in μP shows a width-invariant stability threshold in ρ but the optimal ρ shifts toward that threshold, and no significant improvement over SGD is observed. Compared to SAM-ON (Müller et al., 2024), μP² with SAM-ON achieves 97.34% on CIFAR-10 versus 97.29% in SP, and the theory explains why SAM-ON outperforms standard SAM in SP: only SAM-ON and elementwise ASAM effectively perturb normalization layers under global scaling. Compared to ASAM (Kwon et al., 2021), elementwise ASAM in μP² reaches 97.32%, slightly below SAM-ON in μP² (97.34%) but well above its SP counterpart (97.15%). All μP² variants perform within a 2σ confidence interval of the best-performing SAM-ON in μP², suggesting that for ResNets the normalization layer perturbations may suffice and performance differences in SP are primarily caused by varying degrees to which normalization layers are perturbed.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Tensor Programs framework (Yang, 2019; Yang and Hu, 2021) from abc-parameterizations (layerwise initialization and learning rate scaling) to bcd-parameterizations by adding layerwise perturbation radius scaling, providing the first infinite-width theory for SAM. It builds directly on the μP (Maximal Update Parameterization) framework, which established that standard parameterizations fail to achieve feature learning in all layers at infinite width, and on the SAM/ASAM/SAM-ON literature that has empirically demonstrated generalization benefits without a scaling theory. The key departure is showing that standard SAM's global perturbation scaling is fundamentally insufficient—only the last layer is effectively perturbed in the limit—and deriving μP² as the unique parameterization achieving both feature learning and effective perturbations in every layer.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: μP² enables joint hyperparameter transfer of both learning rate η and perturbation radius ρ across model scales, reducing the cost of tuning SAM for large models. The conceptual finding that standard SAM wastes computation on vanishing perturbations in hidden layers suggests efficiency gains (removing the inner backward pass beyond the last layer) and reframes understanding of why SAM works. Open questions include how to optimally adapt SAM when increasing network depth, the precise role of normalization layer perturbations (SAM-ON), and why finite-width SP sometimes exhibits hyperparameter transfer after multi-epoch training, contradicting the infinite-width predictions. The spectral condition (*) provides a general recipe for deriving correct perturbation scalings for arbitrary SAM variants and architectures.
