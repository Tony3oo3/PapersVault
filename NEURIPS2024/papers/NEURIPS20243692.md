## Problem

While the optimal universal learning rates for general learners are fully characterized by a trichotomy (exponential, linear, arbitrarily slow), the corresponding rates achievable specifically by ERM algorithms remained unknown. This paper asks: given a concept class H, what are the possible universal learning rates by ERM, and what structural properties of H determine which rate applies?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243692/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The analysis takes as input a binary concept class H over an instance space X and a realizable distribution P (i.e., inf_{h∈H} er_P(h) = 0), and produces a complete characterization of the exact universal rate at which every ERM algorithm learns H under P. The output is a classification into one of four rate categories (e^{-n}, 1/n, log(n)/n, or arbitrarily slow) determined by combinatorial properties of H, along with target-specified refinements that identify which target concepts h* achieve which rate. The fine-grained analysis further yields sharp distribution-free constant factors parameterized by two new dimensions (SE and VCE).

## Method

The paper introduces three new combinatorial structures—eluder sequences, star-eluder sequences, and VC-eluder sequences—that capture progressively stronger forms of infinite complexity in a concept class. The main tetrachotomy (Theorem 1) shows that ERM's exact universal rate is e^{-n} iff H is finite, 1/n iff H is infinite but has no infinite star-eluder sequence, log(n)/n iff H has an infinite star-eluder sequence but finite VC dimension, and arbitrarily slow iff VC(H) = ∞. The proof strategy constructs hard distributions on the relevant infinite sequences for lower bounds and adapts classical PAC concentration arguments for upper bounds. A key technical subtlety is that the star number s_H does NOT characterize the 1/n vs. log(n)/n boundary (Proposition 2), necessitating the more refined star-eluder sequence. The fine-grained analysis (Appendix C) introduces the star-eluder dimension SE(H) and VC-eluder dimension VCE(H) to pin down sharp constant factors, showing that VCE(H) can be strictly smaller than VC(H), yielding improved bounds over classical uniform analysis.

## Results

The main result (Theorem 1) establishes a fundamental tetrachotomy: for any concept class H with |H| ≥ 3, the universal learning rate by ERM is exactly one of four possibilities — e^{-n} (finite classes), 1/n (infinite classes with no infinite star-eluder sequence), log(n)/n (infinite star-eluder sequence but finite VC dimension), or arbitrarily slow (infinite VC dimension). Theorem 2 refines this to target-specified rates, showing which target concepts h* yield each rate based on centered eluder/star-eluder/VC-eluder sequences. The fine-grained analysis (Theorem 8) provides sharp distribution-free constant factors: when VCE(H) < ∞, the rate is VCE(H)·log(n)/n; when SE(H) < ∞, the rate is VCE(H)/n · log(SE(H)/VCE(H)), with matching lower bounds up to constants α=1/20 and β=160.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's central comparison is between ERM algorithms and optimal universal learning algorithms (Bousquet et al., 2021). ERM is strictly suboptimal in all five gap scenarios: classes with optimal e^{-n} rate can degrade to 1/n, log(n)/n, or arbitrarily slow under ERM; classes with optimal 1/n rate can degrade to log(n)/n or arbitrarily slow. This contrasts with the uniform (PAC) setting where ERM achieves the optimal VC(H)/n rate up to a logarithmic factor. Compared to uniform PAC rates, the universal ERM rates can be faster (e.g., finite classes get e^{-n} instead of 1/n) or match them (e.g., singletons on ℕ get log(n)/n in both settings). The star number s_H, which characterizes the uniform ERM trichotomy, does NOT characterize the universal ERM tetrachotomy — the star-eluder sequence is the correct structure, and the paper constructs explicit counterexamples (Ex. 19) where s_H = ∞ but no infinite star-eluder sequence exists.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the universal learning framework of Bousquet et al. (2021), which established a trichotomy (exponential, linear, arbitrarily slow) for optimal universal learners, to the specific and practically dominant case of ERM algorithms. It builds on the uniform ERM rate characterization via the star number from Hanneke (2016b) and the fine-grained distribution-free constant analysis from Bousquet et al. (2023), but introduces three new combinatorial structures—eluder sequences, star-eluder sequences, and VC-eluder sequences—that are necessary because the classical measures (VC dimension, star number, Littlestone dimension) do not suffice to characterize ERM-specific universal rates. The key conceptual departure is that while optimal universal learning admits a trichotomy, ERM-specific universal learning admits a tetrachotomy, revealing that ERM can be strictly suboptimal relative to the best possible universal learner in multiple distinct ways.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ERM underpins the most widely deployed learning algorithms in practice (logistic regression, SVM, gradient descent in deep learning), yet its distribution-dependent performance was previously uncharacterized. The tetrachotomy provides a complete and clean answer to what rates ERM can achieve, and the constructed examples demonstrate that ERM can be strictly worse than optimal universal learners by orders of magnitude (e.g., e^{-n} vs. arbitrarily slow). A notable open question is whether a simpler combinatorial quantity than the star-eluder sequence can characterize the 1/n vs. log(n)/n gap—the natural candidate (star number) provably fails. Extensions to the agnostic case, multiclass classification, and 'best-case' ERM are identified as future directions.
