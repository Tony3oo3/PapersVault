## Problem

Existing unsupervised 3D object discovery methods rely on iterative self-training (5–10 rounds) to add static objects to the training set after initially learning from dynamic objects only, which is computationally expensive and introduces confirmation bias. Moreover, these methods perform only class-agnostic detection (foreground vs. background) and do not exploit camera and LiDAR modalities jointly for pseudo-label generation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240693/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes raw, unannotated multi-modal data from a mobile robot: per time step, one LiDAR point cloud (P_t ∈ R^{L×3}) and Q multi-view RGB camera images (I_{q,t} ∈ R^{H×W×3}), with calibrated extrinsics and ego-motion available for cross-sensor and cross-time alignment. It outputs, for each time step, a set of N pseudo-instances where each instance is a 3D upright bounding box (x, y, z, l, w, h, θ) paired with a pseudo-class label from K₂ appearance-based clusters, directly usable to train existing detectors like CenterPoint under their standard training protocol.

## Method

UNION operates in two stages. First, object proposals are generated by removing ground points via RANSAC, aggregating 15 consecutive non-ground scans, and clustering with HDBSCAN; each proposal's motion is estimated with a modified ICP-Flow (2D translation + yaw) to split proposals into static and dynamic sets, and its visual appearance is encoded by projecting its LiDAR points onto camera images and averaging the corresponding DINOv2 ViT-L/14 feature vectors. Second, all appearance embeddings are clustered with K-Means (K₁=20), and clusters containing at least 5% dynamic proposals are retained as mobile, thereby identifying static foreground objects (e.g., parked cars) without any self-training. A second K-Means pass over the retained mobile objects yields K₂ pseudo-class labels, extending the pipeline from class-agnostic discovery to multi-class detection; at inference, pseudo-classes are matched to real classes via one-shot cosine similarity between appearance prototypes.

## Results

On the nuScenes validation set for class-agnostic 3D object detection, UNION achieves an average precision (AP) of 38.4 and nuScenes Detection Score (NDS) of 31.2, more than doubling the AP of the best unsupervised baseline (HDBSCAN at 13.8). UNION also outperforms supervised training with only 1% of labels (AP 27.8) but remains below the 10% supervised setting (AP 61.2). For multi-class detection (vehicle, pedestrian, cyclist), UNION with 5 pseudo-classes achieves the best AP of 24.0, outperforming both HDBSCAN+size prior (5.0) and UNION+size prior (12.7).

## Baselines & Comparisons

UNION decisively outperforms all three unsupervised baselines on both AP and NDS. Against HDBSCAN (the strongest unsupervised baseline), UNION more than doubles AP from 13.8 to 38.4 and NDS from 15.9 to 31.2, while also achieving substantially lower orientation error (AOE 0.874 vs. 1.601) and velocity error (AVE 0.836 vs. 1.531). Against OYSTER (AP 9.1) and LISO (AP 10.9), both of which rely on self-training and tracking, UNION achieves over 3× higher AP without any iterative training rounds. Compared to supervised training, UNION with zero labels surpasses the 1%-label setting (AP 38.4 vs. 27.8) but falls short of the 10%-label setting (AP 38.4 vs. 61.2), indicating that pseudo-label quality and data volume remain key factors. The ablation confirms that appearance clustering is the dominant contributor: adding it to motion estimation alone jumps AP from 14.1 to 38.4.

## Relation to Prior Work

UNION builds on the LiDAR-based unsupervised 3D object discovery line (MODEST, DRIFT, LISO) that relies on iterative self-training to recover static objects, and on LSMOL's multi-modal self-training that alternates between LiDAR and camera detectors. It departs from this lineage by jointly exploiting camera appearance (via DINOv2 embeddings), LiDAR spatial clustering (HDBSCAN), and temporal scene flow (ICP-Flow) in a single pass to generate both static and dynamic pseudo-labels, eliminating the need for self-training entirely. The extension from class-agnostic discovery to multi-class detection via K-Means pseudo-class labels draws a parallel to LOST's use of DINO CLS tokens in 2D, but applies it to 3D spatial clusters rather than 2D image regions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By replacing 5–10 rounds of iterative self-training with a single training pass, UNION substantially reduces computational cost while more than doubling the AP over prior unsupervised baselines on nuScenes (38.4 vs. 13.8). The extension to multi-class detection opens a path toward fully unsupervised 3D detection pipelines, though the current multi-class results are weaker (cyclist AP is zero across all configurations) and evaluation is limited to a single dataset and detector. Open questions include how the method handles rare object classes that may be absorbed into background clusters, whether radar-based motion estimation could improve robustness, and whether the approach generalizes beyond the nuScenes sensor suite.
