## Problem

Existing 4D generation methods either rely on score distillation sampling (SDS) for slow, computationally expensive optimization, or combine separate spatial and temporal generative models whose assumption of spatial-temporal conditional independence inherently produces 4D geometry inconsistencies. The paper asks whether spatial and temporal consistency can be unified within a single video diffusion model to obtain multi-timestamp cross-view supervision in one shot.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243518/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts a text prompt, a single front-view image, or a static 3D orbital video (24 views at 256×256) as conditioning, and produces a 24-frame orbital video (256×256) of a dynamic 3D asset captured along a fixed camera trajectory (constant elevation and 2 m distance, azimuth sweeping 0–360°). This orbital video then serves as photometric supervision for explicit 4D Gaussian splatting reconstruction (4DGS or SC-GS), yielding a renderable dynamic 3D representation. A key design detail is that the motion magnitude condition is computed by comparing the dynamic orbital video against a static orbital video rendered at identical camera poses, isolating object motion from camera-induced appearance change.

## Method

Diffusion4D fine-tunes a 3D-aware video diffusion model (VideoMV) on a curated 81K-asset dynamic 3D dataset from Objaverse, repurposing the model's temporal consistency prior to enforce spatial-temporal consistency across both camera views and timestamps simultaneously. Three novel mechanisms control dynamics: (1) a 3D-to-4D motion magnitude metric injected as a conditioning signal via an MLP embedding, (2) a motion magnitude reconstruction loss in latent space that explicitly supervises the model to learn 3D-to-4D dynamics, and (3) a 3D-aware classifier-free guidance that combines the 4D-aware model's prediction with the pretrained 3D-aware model's prediction to augment motion at inference. For explicit 4D construction, a coarse-to-fine Gaussian splatting strategy first trains on a merged set of dynamic orbital views and static orbital views (for geometry anchoring), then refines using only the dynamic views (for temporal consistency), with L1, LPIPS, and depth-smoothness losses.

## Results

Diffusion4D achieves a generation time of 8 minutes per 4D asset, compared to 23 hours (4DFY), 9 hours (Animate124), 1h30m (4DGen), and 2h30m (STAG4D). On Image-to-4D, Diffusion4D attains CLIP-F 0.89, SSIM 0.83, PSNR 16.7, LPIPS 0.21, and FVD 560.8, outperforming all baselines on every metric. In the human study, Diffusion4D receives 54% overall preference for Image-to-4D and 49% for Text-to-4D, with particularly strong scores in 3D geometry consistency (67%) and motion fidelity (56%). The diffusion-model-only outputs (Diffusion4D*) slightly exceed the 4D-construction outputs, e.g. FVD 490.2 vs 560.8 on Image-to-4D.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the two Text-to-4D baselines, Diffusion4D outperforms 4DFY on CLIP-F (0.81 vs 0.78), CLIP-O (0.65 vs 0.61), and reduces generation time from 23 hours to 8 minutes, while also earning 49% vs 29% overall human preference. Animate124 is surpassed on every reported metric, with Diffusion4D gaining +0.06 CLIP-F and +0.07 CLIP-O. For Image-to-4D, Diffusion4D dominates both 4DGen and STAG4D across all quantitative metrics: it improves SSIM from 0.69/0.76 to 0.83, PSNR from 14.4/15.2 to 16.7, LPIPS from 0.31/0.27 to 0.21, and FVD from 736.6/675.4 to 560.8, while cutting generation time from 1.5–2.5 hours to 8 minutes. In the human study, Diffusion4D receives 54% overall preference versus 22% (4DGen) and 24% (STAG4D), with the largest margins in 3D geometry consistency (67% vs 18%/15%) and motion fidelity (56% vs 18%/26%). The paper notes that the diffusion-model-only outputs (Diffusion4D*) consistently score slightly higher than the 4D-construction outputs, suggesting the Gaussian splatting reconstruction introduces minor quality loss.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the key insight from 3D-aware video diffusion models (SV3D, VideoMV) that repurpose temporal consistency in video generation for spatial consistency in static 3D, by training on dynamic 3D assets so that the same temporal axis simultaneously encodes spatial view consistency and temporal motion coherence. It departs from SDS-based 4D generation (4DFY, Animate124, MAV3D) that rely on slow iterative optimization, and from multi-model approaches (Efficient4D, Diffusion²) that assume spatial-temporal conditional independence, by integrating both consistencies into a single 4D-aware video diffusion model. For explicit 4D reconstruction, it leverages off-the-shelf Gaussian splatting pipelines (4DGS, SC-GS) with a coarse-to-fine strategy rather than proposing a new representation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ~170× speedup over SDS-based methods (8 min vs. 23 h) makes 4D generation practically accessible for animation, AR, and content creation pipelines, and the motion magnitude metric provides a simple, interpretable control knob for dynamic strength that prior methods lacked. Key limitations include the 256×256 resolution, a curated dataset of 81K assets with restricted motion vocabulary (walking, jumping, arm-stretching), and reconstruction quality bounded by off-the-shelf 4D pipelines that produce floaters and blur under large motions. Open questions include whether the single-model spatial-temporal consistency strategy scales to higher resolutions and longer temporal sequences, and whether more diverse and complex dynamics can be learned without the current dataset's action limitations.
