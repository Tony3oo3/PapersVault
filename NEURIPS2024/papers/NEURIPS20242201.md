## Problem

Despite the widespread success of Transformers in sequence modeling, the theoretical understanding of their approximation rates—how approximation error scales with model complexity—remains largely unexplored. While universal approximation properties have been established, no explicit Jackson-type rate characterizing which sequential relationships Transformers approximate efficiently has been derived. Additionally, a principled comparison of when Transformers outperform or underperform RNNs based on the temporal structure of the target was missing.

## Input / Output

The input is a finite-length sequence x = (x(1),...,x(τ)) with each token x(s) ∈ [0,1]^d, and the output is a scalar sequence y = (y(1),...,y(τ)) with y(t) = H_t(x). Position encoding is applied to map each token into a disjoint compact subset of R^d, ensuring no two tokens are indistinguishable and breaking the Transformer's inherent permutation equivariance. The target space consists of continuous mappings from the encoded input space to the output space, with the approximation budget parameterized by the attention head dimension m_h and the feed-forward network complexity m_FF.

## Method

The paper establishes a representation theorem showing any continuous sequence-to-sequence target can be exactly expressed in the Transformer form with a temporal coupling function ρ(u,v) and pointwise components F, f. It then defines a complexity measure C₁ based on the Proper Orthogonal Decomposition (POD) of ρ, where the decay rate of singular values σ_k ~ k^{-α} governs approximation quality. The main result (Theorem 4.2) yields a Jackson-type rate: the approximation error is bounded by τ²C₀(H)·(C₁(H)/m_h^{2α−1} + C₂(H,m_h)·m_h^{β+1}/m_FF^β), revealing that low-rank temporal coupling enables efficient approximation with small attention dimension. The paper further shows that Transformers are invariant to temporal reordering but sensitive to temporal mixing (convolution of inputs), while RNNs exhibit the opposite behavior, providing a concrete characterization of when each architecture is preferable.

## Results

The main theoretical result (Theorem 4.2) establishes that the Transformer approximation error is bounded by τ²C₀(H)(C₁^(α)(H)/m_h^(2α−1) + C₂^(β)(H,m_h)·m_h^(β+1)/m_FF^β), showing that the rate is governed by the singular value decay of the temporal coupling term ρ. Empirically, on ViT-B/16 with CIFAR-10, the training error decays as O(1/m_h^0.27) with increasing attention head dimension, confirming the predicted power-law behavior. In the RNN-vs-Transformer comparison (Table 2), the Transformer is robust to temporal ordering changes (MSE 2.18e-5 → 2.51e-5) but degrades under temporal mixing (2.18e-5 → 2.39e-4), while the RNN shows the opposite pattern (1.02e-7 → 3.57e-2 under ordering change, but 1.02e-7 → 1.58e-7 under mixing).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares the Transformer against linear RNNs (using the approximation framework of Li et al. [24]) on two types of temporal structure modifications. Under temporal ordering changes (permuting input indices), the RNN fails catastrophically (MSE jumps from 1.02e-7 to 3.57e-2) because the permuted target is no longer causal, while the Transformer remains essentially unaffected (2.18e-5 → 2.51e-5). Under temporal mixing (convolving the input with a random filter), the RNN is robust (1.02e-7 → 1.58e-7) because the altered kernel retains exponential decay, but the Transformer degrades (2.18e-5 → 2.39e-4) because mixing increases the effective rank of the temporal coupling. On targets without inherent temporal ordering (Table 3, random kernel ρ(s)~U[0,1]), the Transformer achieves MSE 5.43e-4 while the RNN fails entirely (MSE 5.58), and this gap persists under permutation. The key takeaway is that neither architecture dominates: the Transformer excels when temporal ordering is irrelevant but is sensitive to mixing, while the RNN excels with causal, exponentially-decaying kernels but cannot handle non-causal or order-independent relationships.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Jackson-type approximation framework—originally developed for polynomial approximation (Jackson [15], DeVore [9]) and subsequently applied to RNNs [24], CNNs [16], and encoder-decoder architectures [23]—to the Transformer architecture. It departs from prior universal approximation results for Transformers [33, 34, 17] and earlier rate results for specific target structures [14, 1, 31, 29] by introducing a temporal-structure-aware complexity measure built on Proper Orthogonal Decomposition (POD) [3], which characterizes the low-rank structure of the attention mechanism's temporal coupling. The key conceptual departure is framing the Transformer's approximation bottleneck as a low-rank approximation problem on the pairwise interaction function ρ(u,v), rather than as a smoothness or sparsity condition.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first explicit Jackson-type rate for Transformers, identifying low-rank temporal coupling as the structural property that makes a target easy for the architecture to learn. The comparison with RNNs reveals a clean duality: Transformers are invariant to temporal reordering but sensitive to temporal mixing, while RNNs show the opposite behavior—offering practitioners concrete guidance on which architecture suits which temporal structure. Limitations include the single-layer, single-head, fixed-length setting without layer normalization or residual connections, and the analysis does not yet address multi-head attention or deeper stacks. Open questions include whether the low-rank characterization extends to multi-head settings and whether preprocessing to remove temporal mixing can recover Transformer performance on mixing-heavy targets.
