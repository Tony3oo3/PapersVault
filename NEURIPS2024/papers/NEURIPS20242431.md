## Problem

In 3D Gaussian Splatting, surface normals are disconnected from the rendering pipeline: pixel colors are computed via Spherical Harmonics along the ray direction, so normals receive no gradient signal during backpropagation and cannot contribute to rendering quality. This creates a fundamental seesaw where regularizing geometry degrades appearance and vice versa, making it difficult to achieve both high-fidelity novel view synthesis and accurate surface normals simultaneously.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242431/cropped_page1_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view RGB images and a sparse SfM point cloud as initialization, following the standard 3DGS training protocol (30k iterations, COLMAP for camera poses). Beyond standard novel-view renderings, the method explicitly outputs per-pixel surface normals and a 3D Gaussian scene representation where each Gaussian's color is no longer a free attribute but is computed as a function of its normal vector and a locally shared illumination vector. The anchor-based structure means per-Gaussian colors are decoded from a compact set of anchor features via MLPs rather than stored independently.

## Method

Normal-GS re-parameterizes each Gaussian's surface color as the dot product of its normal vector and an Integrated Directional Illumination Vector (IDIV), derived by factoring the normal out of the Lambertian rendering equation (c = k_D · n · l). This makes normals computationally involved in the forward pass, so photometric loss gradients propagate back to normal vectors via the chain rule, enabling joint optimization of appearance and geometry. To regularize the introduced IDIV parameters without heavy hand-crafted losses, the method uses Scaffold-GS anchor features decoded by a global MLP to produce locally shared IDIVs, exploiting the low-frequency spatial coherence of incident lighting. For specular effects, the method applies Integrated Directional Encoding (IDE) from Ref-NeRF on the reflection direction (which itself depends on the normal), feeding the result plus the normal and anchor feature into a second MLP. A self-regularized depth-normal loss (cross product of depth-image gradients vs. rendered normals) is added after 5k iterations to align Gaussian shortest axes with true surface normals, completing the training objective.

## Results

On Mip-NeRF 360, Normal-GS achieves 29.341 PSNR / 0.869 SSIM / 0.194 LPIPS, matching or slightly exceeding SpecGaussian (29.287 PSNR) and outperforming 3DGS (28.691 PSNR) and Scaffold-GS (29.267 PSNR). On Tanks & Temples, Normal-GS reaches 24.219 PSNR, surpassing 3DGS (23.142) and GShader (21.262) by over 1 dB. On the DTU geometry benchmark, Normal-GS achieves 0.94 mCD and 37.63 PSNR, beating 3DGS (1.96 mCD, 35.76 PSNR) and SuGaR (1.33 mCD, 34.57 PSNR) on both metrics, while 2DGS achieves slightly better mCD (0.80) but substantially lower PSNR (34.52).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Normal-GS outperforms 3DGS by 0.65 dB PSNR on Mip-NeRF 360 and 1.08 dB on Tanks & Temples, confirming that explicit normal involvement in shading improves rendering over the vanilla pipeline. Against Scaffold-GS (its architectural base), Normal-GS gains 0.07–0.74 dB PSNR across datasets, while the ScaffoldGS w/N variant (which feeds normals into the color MLP implicitly) shows no improvement, validating the paper's claim that explicit normal-color coupling is necessary. GShader, which relies on a learned global environment map, is substantially outperformed (29.341 vs. 26.060 PSNR on Mip-NeRF 360) and fails entirely on Deep Blending. SpecGaussian achieves the closest rendering quality (29.287 vs. 29.341 PSNR on Mip-NeRF 360) but lags on Tanks & Temples (24.502 vs. 24.219) and, critically, produces significantly noisier normals; Normal-GS achieves the best normal MAE on NeRF Synthetic. On DTU geometry, Normal-GS (0.94 mCD, 37.63 PSNR) beats both 3DGS and SuGaR on both metrics, while 2DGS achieves marginally better mCD (0.80) but sacrifices 3.1 dB PSNR, illustrating the appearance-geometry trade-off that Normal-GS resolves.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) and its anchor-based variant Scaffold-GS, but departs from the standard 3DGS paradigm where surface colors are modeled as view-dependent Spherical Harmonics independent of geometry. It also positions itself against two competing lines of work: geometry-focused methods (SuGaR, 2DGS, Gaussian Surfels) that regularize normals at the cost of rendering fidelity, and inverse-rendering methods (GShader, SpecGaussian) that use environment maps or Spherical Gaussians queried by reflection direction. The key conceptual departure is re-parameterizing diffuse color as an explicit dot product between the surface normal and a locally-learned Integrated Directional Illumination Vector (IDIV), derived from the physically-based rendering equation, so that normals receive gradient signals during the forward rendering pass rather than only through a separate loss term.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clean, principled resolution to the 'seesaw' problem in 3DGS where improving geometry degrades rendering quality, demonstrated quantitatively by a 2.24 dB PSNR drop when simply adding a depth-normal loss to Scaffold-GS. The IDIV formulation is a reusable design pattern that could be plugged into other 3DGS variants, and the anchor-based MLP regularization avoids the heavy hand-tuned regularizers (Laplacian, total variation) used in prior shape-from-shading work. Open questions include whether the approach extends to relighting and texture editing (the authors note finer IDIV/specular decomposition as future work), and the acknowledged failure mode where the self-regularized normal loss degrades distant outdoor regions. The per-scene optimization requirement and lack of released code at submission time limit immediate practical adoption.
