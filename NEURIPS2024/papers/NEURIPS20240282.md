## Problem

Clipped-SGD is the standard tool for streaming statistical estimation under heavy-tailed data, but the best known high-probability rates for smooth strongly convex objectives scale as √(Tr(Σ)·ln(1/δ)/T), which is far from the optimal sub-Gaussian rate √((Tr(Σ)+‖Σ‖·ln(1/δ))/T). No prior work achieved near-sub-Gaussian rates in the streaming (batch-size-1, O(d)-memory) setting, and no such guarantees existed at all for smooth convex or Lipschitz convex objectives without strong convexity.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes one data sample per iteration (batch size 1) from a heavy-tailed distribution P, requiring only O(d) memory and a finite second moment on the stochastic gradient noise (higher moments may be infinite). It outputs either the last iterate or the average iterate as the parameter estimate, accompanied by a high-probability (1−δ) error bound of the form √((Tr(Σ)+√(Tr(Σ)‖Σ‖₂)·ln(ln(T)/δ))/T), where Σ is the covariance of the clipped gradient. The near-sub-Gaussian structure means the confidence-level dependence ln(ln(T)/δ) multiplies only the lower-order √(Tr(Σ)‖Σ‖₂) term rather than the dominant Tr(Σ) term.

## Method

The key technical contribution is an iterative refinement strategy for vector-valued martingale concentration: starting from a coarse Freedman's inequality bound, the authors apply the Donsker-Varadhan Variational Principle in a PAC-Bayes loop to successively tighten the concentration over K ≈ log log(T log d) refinement steps, yielding a bound of the form √(Tr(Σ)+‖Σ‖₂·ln(K/δ)) rather than the prior √(Tr(Σ)·ln(d/δ)). In the Clipped-SGD analysis, the clipped stochastic gradient is decomposed into a zero-mean variance component v_t (bounded by 2Γ) and a deterministic bias b_t from clipping. The refined martingale concentration is applied to control the quadratic variation (1/T)Σ‖v_t‖², which is the critical random term in the SGD recursion, while the bias is bounded using contractivity arguments on the clipping operator. This yields near-sub-Gaussian rates for smooth strongly convex, smooth convex, and Lipschitz convex objectives, and is instantiated for streaming mean estimation, linear regression, logistic regression, and LAD regression.

## Results

This is a theoretical paper with no empirical experiments; the main result is a near-sub-Gaussian high-probability convergence rate for Clipped-SGD in the streaming (batch-size-1) setting. For smooth strongly convex objectives with bounded second-moment gradient noise, the method achieves an error of $\sqrt{\frac{\operatorname{Tr}(\Sigma)+\sqrt{\operatorname{Tr}(\Sigma)\|\Sigma\|_2}\ln(\ln(T)/\delta)}{T}}$ with probability $1-\delta$, improving the prior best streaming rate of $\sqrt{\frac{\operatorname{Tr}(\Sigma)\ln(1/\delta)}{T}}$ [Tsai et al., 2022]. In sample-complexity terms (Table 1, instantiated for $\Sigma=I$), the method requires $\frac{d+\sqrt{d}\log\delta^{-1}}{\epsilon}+\frac{D_1\log^2(\delta^{-1}\log T)}{\sqrt{\epsilon}}$ samples, where the $\log\delta^{-1}$ factor multiplies only $\sqrt{d}$ rather than $d$ as in all prior streaming or batch methods. The results extend to smooth convex and Lipschitz convex objectives, yielding the first near-sub-Gaussian streaming guarantees without strong convexity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The closest prior streaming method is Clipped-SGD of Tsai et al. [52], which also uses batch size 1 but achieves a sub-optimal rate with $\log\delta^{-1}$ multiplying the full $d/\epsilon$ term; the present method reduces this to $\sqrt{d}\,\log\delta^{-1}/\epsilon$, a $\sqrt{d}$-fold improvement in the confidence dependence for the leading term. All other baselines in Table 1 (Gorbunov et al. [21], Prasad et al. [45], Davis et al. [14], Nazin et al. [40]) require batch size $\Omega(d/\epsilon)$, making them unsuitable for the streaming setting, and additionally carry $\log\delta^{-1}$ or $\log\log$ factors on the $d/\epsilon$ term. For heavy-tailed mean estimation (Corollary 1), the method matches the optimal $\sqrt{\operatorname{Tr}(\Sigma)/N}$ rate of the trimmed-mean estimator of Lee and Valiant [32] in the regime $N=\omega(\log^3\delta^{-1})$, $d=\omega(\log^2\delta^{-1})$, while being more general (valid for all $N,d$). For heavy-tailed linear regression (Corollary 2), the rate improves upon [52] from $\frac{\sigma}{\lambda_{\min}(\Sigma)}\sqrt{\frac{\|\Sigma\|_2\, d\,\ln(1/\delta)}{N}}$ to $\frac{\sigma}{\lambda_{\min}(\Sigma)}\sqrt{\frac{\operatorname{Tr}(\Sigma)+\sqrt{\|\Sigma\|_2\operatorname{Tr}(\Sigma)}\ln(\ln N/\delta)}{N}}$, which gracefully adapts to the effective dimension $d_{\mathrm{eff}}=\operatorname{Tr}(\Sigma)/\|\Sigma\|$ and yields significant speedups when $d_{\mathrm{eff}}\ll d$. The paper also provides the first known streaming estimators with near-sub-Gaussian rates for heavy-tailed logistic regression and LAD regression, problems for which no prior streaming guarantees existed.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the PAC-Bayes concentration framework of Catoni and Giulini [8] and the streaming heavy-tailed SCO analysis of Tsai et al. [52], which established the closest prior rate of √(Tr(Σ)·ln(1/δ)/T) for Clipped SGD under bounded second moments. The key departure is a novel iterative refinement strategy that bootstraps the Donsker-Varadhan Variational Principle into Freedman's inequality, yielding tighter vector-martingale concentration bounds than prior PAC-Bayes or Matrix Freedman approaches. This sharper concentration enables near-sub-Gaussian rates (with the confidence dependence reduced from Tr(Σ)·log(1/δ) to √(Tr(Σ)·‖Σ‖)·log(log(T)/δ)) and extends the analysis beyond strongly convex objectives to smooth convex and Lipschitz convex settings, where no prior streaming heavy-tailed guarantees existed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result is significant because Clipped SGD is the de facto standard for training deep networks with heavy-tailed gradients, yet its high-probability guarantees in the streaming (batch-size-1) regime were previously sub-optimal; this paper closes much of that gap and provides the first streaming estimators for heavy-tailed logistic and LAD regression. The iterative refinement technique for martingale concentration is flagged as potentially of independent interest for developing fine-grained concentration bounds in other settings. Remaining limitations include the residual log(log(T)/δ) factor (vs. the truly optimal log(1/δ)), the restriction to convex objectives (non-convex landscapes are out of scope), and the requirement to know problem-dependent parameters like ‖Σ‖ and δ. Open questions include whether truly sub-Gaussian rates are achievable with clipped-mean-type estimators, and whether the techniques extend to bounded p-th moment assumptions for p < 2.
