## Problem

Existing diffusion-model-based inverse problem solvers rely on approximations to the likelihood term or heuristic guidance updates that deviate from the true Bayesian posterior, meaning they do not actually sample p(x|y). This paper addresses the gap by proposing a principled MCMC algorithm that rigorously samples the posterior using pretrained diffusion models as priors, without approximating the generative process.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243758/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes sparse noisy measurements y ∈ R^m, a forward operator A (linear or nonlinear), and a pretrained diffusion model checkpoint (converted to the EDM formulation via VP preconditioning). It produces a collection of posterior samples from which the mean reconstruction and per-pixel standard deviation (uncertainty) are computed. The method operates on 256×256 RGB or grayscale images and supports arbitrary noise levels ρ within the DM's noise schedule range, with the number of reverse-diffusion steps in the prior step adapting to the current ρ value during the annealing schedule.

## Method

PnP-DM builds on the Split Gibbs Sampler, alternating between a likelihood step (exact Gaussian sampling for linear problems, or Langevin MCMC for nonlinear problems) and a prior step that solves a Bayesian denoising problem. The key insight is identifying that the prior step's conditional distribution exactly matches the posterior p(x₀|x_t) of the EDM reverse diffusion at a specific noise level ρ, so any pretrained DM in EDM form can serve as a rigorous denoising solver without retraining. An annealing schedule on the coupling parameter ρ accelerates mixing and prevents convergence to bad local minima in ill-posed problems. The paper further provides a non-asymptotic convergence guarantee showing the average Fisher information between the non-stationary and stationary processes decays at O(1/K), requiring only L²-accurate score estimates under the stationary measure.

## Results

On three linear inverse problems (Gaussian deblur, motion deblur, 4× super-resolution) over 100 FFHQ color images, PnP-DM(EDM) achieves the best PSNR across all tasks (29.66, 30.35, 29.60 dB), while PnP-DM(VE) achieves the best SSIM (0.8399, 0.8547, 0.8431) and LPIPS (0.2090, 0.1971, 0.2108). On two nonlinear problems, PnP-DM(EDM) achieves the best PSNR for Fourier phase retrieval (31.14 dB vs. 29.28 for DPnP and 23.60 for DPS) and PnP-DM(iDDPM) achieves the best PSNR for coded diffraction patterns (33.35 dB). On the real M87 black hole data, PnP-DM produces high-fidelity posterior samples with consistent ring diameters matching the official EHT reconstruction, while DPS produces samples with inconsistent ring sizes and poor measurement fit.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PnP-DM outperforms all optimization-based baselines (PnP-ADMM, DPIR) by a wide margin across all linear and nonlinear tasks, with PSNR gains of 2–3 dB over DPIR and 3–4 dB over PnP-ADMM. Among DM-based guidance methods, PnP-DM surpasses DPS on all linear problems (e.g., 30.35 vs. 27.87 dB PSNR on motion deblur) and on Fourier phase retrieval (31.14 vs. 23.60 dB), though it performs on par with DPS on coded diffraction patterns (33.25 vs. 33.43 dB PSNR). Against the closest SGS-based competitor DPnP [78], PnP-DM(VE) and PnP-DM(EDM) achieve slightly higher PSNR and SSIM on all three linear tasks (e.g., 30.38 vs. 30.21 dB on motion deblur) and substantially outperform it on Fourier phase retrieval (31.14 vs. 29.28 dB). PnP-SGS [19] struggles severely on the highly ill-posed Fourier phase retrieval problem (15.36 dB PSNR), while PnP-DM(EDM) achieves 31.14 dB, demonstrating the importance of the rigorous EDM-based prior step and annealing schedule. The classical HIO algorithm [29] is far outperformed on Fourier phase retrieval (20.66 dB vs. 31.14 dB PSNR). In uncertainty quantification, PnP-DM captures 97.46% of ground-truth pixels within the 3-sigma credible interval versus 96.20% for DPS and 88.77% for PnP-SGS.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Split Gibbs Sampler (SGS) [71] as its MCMC backbone and the EDM formulation [37] as a unified interface for diffusion models, connecting the two in a way that prior SGS-based methods (PnP-SGS [19], DPnP [78]) did not. It explicitly departs from the dominant 'guidance' paradigm of DPS [17], DDRM [39], and related methods, arguing that their approximations to ∇log p_t(y|x_t) mean they do not sample the true posterior. The key conceptual departure is identifying that the SGS prior step is exactly a Bayesian denoising problem solvable by running the EDM reverse SDE from an intermediate noise level, enabling any pretrained DM (DDPM, VE-SDE, iDDPM, EDM) to serve as a rigorous plug-and-play prior without model-specific customization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By providing a principled posterior sampling framework rather than a guidance heuristic, PnP-DM enables reliable uncertainty quantification and multi-modal posterior characterization—demonstrated on the multi-modal black hole imaging problem where DPS fails to capture the correct modes. The unified EDM interface lowers the barrier to incorporating new diffusion models into Bayesian imaging pipelines. Open questions include scaling to 3D or PDE-constrained inverse problems where full-image likelihood evaluation is prohibitive, and extending the theoretical analysis to account for finite-iteration approximation error in the nonlinear likelihood step.
