## Problem

Knowledge editing for Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) lacks a comprehensive evaluation benchmark. The existing MMEdit benchmark relies on synthesized images for Generality testing (introducing content inconsistency), omits the Portability metric entirely, and provides limited data volume. This paper addresses these gaps by constructing a new benchmark with real images, extending evaluation to Portability, and scaling up the dataset.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240294/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each editing case consists of a real image from the MMKG knowledge graph paired with a VQA-style text question, along with an editing target that reassigns the image's associated entity (e.g., Messi → Ronaldo). Evaluation produces four scores: Reliability (correct target answer), Text/Image Generality (rephrased questions or same-entity different-view images), Text/Image Locality (unrelated or similar-but-unedited content), and Portability (multi-hop reasoning questions derived from knowledge graph triples connected to the edited entity). The dataset contains 8,174 editing cases with 18,434 images, split into 5,000 train and 3,174 test, plus 4,819 Portability cases spanning 1- to 4-hop reasoning.

## Method

The benchmark is constructed from the MMKG multi-modal knowledge graph, where each image is explicitly bound to a knowledge entity. Image pairs for Generality are selected via CLIP similarity scoring followed by manual verification of same-entity/different-view criteria. Entity pairs for editing are identified through maximum weight bipartite matching on shared relations from FB15K, ensuring semantically coherent edits. GPT generates VQA questions and Portability queries from knowledge graph triples. The key methodological extension is Portability: relational triples (s,r,o) where the edited entity serves as subject are sampled to form one-hop and multi-hop reasoning test cases, evaluating whether the model can propagate edited knowledge through connected facts. The paper then systematically evaluates five editing methods (Fine-tuning, Knowledge Editor, IKE, SERAC, MEND) across five LVLMs under both single and sequential editing settings, revealing that parameter-update methods suffer from forgetting and MEND collapses under sequential edits, while in-context methods (IKE) achieve the best Portability but at the cost of Locality.

## Results

In single editing on VLKEB, most methods achieve near-perfect Reliability (94–100%) across all five LVLMs, confirming that individual edits are straightforward. The key differentiator is Portability: IKE consistently achieves the highest scores (44.2–64.8%), followed by FT-Vis (27.2–74.1%) and KE (34.7–48.6%), while parameter-update methods like FT-LLM and MEND score lower (16.2–42.8%). For Image Locality, MEND dominates (78.7–92.7%), whereas SERAC is worst (0.8–4.5%), and for Text Locality, SERAC and FT-Vis reach ~100% while IKE lags (58.0–70.1%). In sequential editing, MEND collapses to NaN after 14–50 edits, FT suffers progressive forgetting (Reliability drops from ~100% to ~47–72% at gap=100), and SERAC maintains Reliability but degrades in Text Generality due to retrieval confusion.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IKE (in-context knowledge editing) achieves the highest Portability across nearly all models (44.2–64.8%), outperforming MEND (28.8–40.4%), SERAC (15.3–49.2%), and KE (34.7–48.6%), because it explicitly appends the edited fact as a prompt, enabling the model to reason over related knowledge. MEND provides the best Image Locality (78.7–92.7%), significantly outperforming SERAC (0.8–4.5%), KE (12.2–52.6%), and IKE (9.7–16.6%), thanks to its locality constraint during training and token-wise gradient-based updates. SERAC achieves near-perfect Text Locality (~100%) but catastrophically fails on Image Locality, as its scope classifier misroutes visually similar but textually similar inputs to the counterfactual model. KE underperforms on Reliability (89.1–99.1%) compared to IKE (99.0–100.0%) and MEND (98.5–99.5%), particularly on mPLUG-Owl2 where it drops to 89.1%. In sequential editing, all parameter-update methods degrade: FT-LLM loses 25–53% Reliability at gap=100, while MEND collapses entirely (NaN outputs after 14–50 edits), and SERAC maintains Reliability but drops Text Generality by 40–60 points due to retrieval confusion among accumulated edits.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends MMEdit (Cheng et al., EMNLP 2023), the first benchmark for LVLM knowledge editing, by addressing its two key limitations: reliance on Stable Diffusion–synthesized images for Generality evaluation and the absence of Portability assessment. It sources data from the multi-modal knowledge graph MMKG, which binds images to knowledge entities and enables construction of Portability test cases via relational triples. The editing methods evaluated (MEND, SERAC, IKE, KE, fine-tuning) are all adapted from the LLM knowledge editing literature, positioning this work as a systematic empirical study of how those methods transfer to the multi-modal setting rather than proposing a new editing algorithm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

VLKEB fills a critical gap as the first LVLM editing benchmark that evaluates whether edited knowledge is actually applied to related content (Portability), a dimension essential for real-world deployment. The finding that parameter-update methods (FT, KE, MEND) achieve near-perfect Reliability yet score poorly on Portability, while the simple prompting-based IKE method leads on Portability, suggests that current editing paradigms are fundamentally case-centric rather than knowledge-integrative. Open questions include whether direct LVLM editing methods that account for cross-modal interactions can close the Portability gap, and how to prevent the observed MEND collapse (NaN logits) in sequential editing. The demonstration that explicitly editing one-hop knowledge dramatically improves Portability (e.g., Qwen-VL FT-LLM: 16→89) points toward a practical recipe but raises questions about scalability to deeper knowledge chains.
