## Problem

Current LLM inference treats every token identically, allocating the full depth of the transformer network regardless of token importance or task difficulty. This wastes substantial compute on dispensable tokens (articles, punctuation) and simple questions, making deployment on resource-constrained platforms impractical. The paper addresses the gap of adaptive, per-token, per-layer compute allocation at inference time without requiring full model retraining.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240055/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes token embeddings from a pre-trained decoder-only LLM (LLaMA2-7B or LLaMA3-8B, 32 layers each) and produces autoregressive text generation alongside a binary execution/skip decision at every transformer layer for every token. A key structural detail is the dynamically modified attention mask: when a token skips a layer, its KV embeddings are evicted from subsequent tokens' attention calculations in that layer, reducing both compute and KV-cache storage. The first m=2 tokens are always forced to execute all layers to preserve positional context.

## Method

D-LLMs inserts a lightweight dynamic decision module (two linear layers with hidden dimension 512) before each transformer layer, which outputs a 2-way categorical distribution over {skip, execute} conditioned on the token's current embedding. Training uses Gumbel-Softmax reparameterization with a straight-through estimator so that hard skip/execute decisions in the forward pass remain differentiable in the backward pass. A KV-cache eviction mask is applied to the self-attention computation, setting attention scores to −∞ for tokens that skipped the current layer (except the first m tokens), thereby eliminating the need to store their KV embeddings. The overall loss combines cross-entropy with an L1 acceleration-ratio loss that steers the average skip rate toward a user-defined target Ω, enabling customizable speedup. The entire framework is trained via LoRA on pre-trained LLMs, adding only ~1% extra parameters and ~0.9% FLOPs overhead from the decision modules.

## Results

On LLaMA2-7B, D-LLM achieves the best or near-best performance across all nine benchmarks while using the lowest computational cost (0.52–0.59 FLOPs, i.e., ~41–48% savings). On math solving, D-LLM dramatically outperforms all baselines: 74% accuracy on MaWPS (vs. 52% for Shortened-LLaMA-PPL) and 29% on GSM8K (vs. 18% for Shortened-LLaMA-Taylor). On commonsense reasoning, D-LLM matches or exceeds the best baseline on every dataset (e.g., 84% on PIQA vs. 83% for Shortened-LLaMA-Taylor; 82% on SIQA vs. 81%) while using ~12–14% fewer FLOPs. On Q&A/summarization, D-LLM achieves the lowest PPL (6.01 on Alpaca, 3.18 on SAMSum) with the lowest FLOPs among all methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

D-LLM outperforms all three baselines on every dataset while using the lowest FLOPs (0.52–0.59 vs. 0.56–0.90 for baselines). Against Mixture-of-Depths (MoD), D-LLM wins decisively on math (74% vs. 33% on MaWPS; 29% vs. 8% on GSM8K) and commonsense reasoning (e.g., 84% vs. 49% on PIQA), and on Q&A (PPL 6.01 vs. 10.32 on Alpaca). Against Shortened-LLaMA (both PPL and Taylor variants), D-LLM matches or beats the best variant on all datasets with ~12–14% fewer FLOPs (e.g., 84% vs. 83% on PIQA at 0.52 vs. 0.66 FLOPs; 74% vs. 52% on MaWPS). Against Ada-Infer, D-LLM is dramatically superior on math (74% vs. 0% on MaWPS) and Q&A (PPL 6.01 vs. 319 on Alpaca), and slightly better on commonsense reasoning with lower cost. The only case where a baseline matches D-LLM is MMLU, where Shortened-LLaMA-Taylor also achieves 53% accuracy but at 0.66 FLOPs versus D-LLM's 0.55.

## Relation to Prior Work

D-LLMs draws its conceptual lineage from dynamic inference networks in computer vision (ConvNet-AIG, SkipNet, CoDiNet), transplanting the per-sample layer-skipping paradigm into the LLM setting. It departs from prior LLM acceleration methods in a specific way: unlike static depth pruning (Shortened-LLaMA) which permanently removes layers, or early-exit schemes (Ada-Infer) that halt inference at a single intermediate layer, D-LLMs makes independent per-token, per-layer execution decisions via lightweight Gumbel-Softmax decision modules. The KV-cache eviction strategy is a novel contribution that addresses a practical gap none of the prior dynamic-pruning-for-LLMs works (MoD, Ada-Infer) tackled—making selective layer skipping compatible with the KV-cache mechanism that underpins all autoregressive LLM deployment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ~45–50% reduction in both FLOPs and KV-cache storage with comparable accuracy is practically significant for deploying LLMs on resource-constrained or edge platforms, and the customizable acceleration-rate loss gives practitioners a direct knob to trade speed for quality. However, the evaluation is limited to two model sizes (LLaMA2-7B, LLaMA3-8B) under few-shot fine-tuning, and several baselines (Ada-Infer, MoD) show implausibly poor numbers that suggest suboptimal reproduction, weakening the comparative claims. Open questions include whether the per-token granularity generalizes to longer contexts, different architectures (encoder-decoder, MoE), and whether coarser-grained decisions (per-sentence, per-task) could capture most of the benefit at lower decision-module overhead.
