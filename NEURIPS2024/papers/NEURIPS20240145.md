## Problem

Single-image 3D reconstruction methods suffer from geometric distortions because 3D shape is inherently ambiguous from one view, and multi-view generation approaches lack guaranteed consistency. Existing image-point-cloud alignment methods (e.g., ULIP) rely on contrastive loss with low-dimensional feature vectors (512×1 or 1024×1) that are too small to capture the rich 3D geometry needed for mesh reconstruction.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single 512×512 RGB image as input, encoded by a frozen DINO ViT into a 1025×768 token sequence (32×32 patches plus one global token). It outputs a watertight 3D mesh extracted via Marching Cubes from SDF values queried on a reconstructed tri-plane. Inference completes in approximately 6 seconds on a single NVIDIA V100 GPU, and no camera parameters are required at test time.

## Method

LAM3D uses a two-stage pipeline. Stage 1 compresses point clouds into a compact latent tri-plane (3×2×32×32) via a transformer-based point feature extractor (FPS + PointNet + transformer) and a hierarchical tri-plane autoencoder with a plane refiner (three small UNets, one per plane) and dual SDF supervision on both latent and reconstructed tri-planes. Stage 2 aligns DINO image features to the latent tri-plane using three parallel diffusion UNets—one per plane (XY, XZ, YZ)—with z₀-prediction and cross-attention conditioning on the image tokens. The key design choices are: (1) using point cloud priors to inject accurate 3D structure into the alignment target, (2) employing a probabilistic diffusion process rather than deterministic regression to handle the ill-posed occlusion problem, and (3) using independent per-plane UNets to preserve 3D structural information and avoid cross-plane convolutional artifacts.

## Results

On the Google Scanned Objects (GSO) benchmark, LAM3D achieves a Chamfer Distance of 0.0083, Volume IoU of 0.6235, and F-Score of 85.40%, outperforming all baselines on every metric. The closest competitor, CRM, records CD 0.0094, Vol. IoU 0.6131, and F-Score 79.38%, meaning LAM3D improves CD by ~12%, Vol. IoU by ~1.7%, and F-Score by ~6 points. Inference requires only 6 seconds on a single NVIDIA V100 GPU, matching the speed of other single-image-to-3D methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LAM3D outperforms all eight baselines on all three geometry metrics (CD, Vol. IoU, F-Score) on the GSO dataset. The strongest baseline is CRM (CD 0.0094, Vol. IoU 0.6131, F-Score 79.38%), which LAM3D surpasses by 12% in CD, 1.7% in Vol. IoU, and 6.0 points in F-Score. LGM is the second-closest on CD (0.0117) but trails LAM3D by 29% on that metric and by 15.7 points on F-Score. Multi-view diffusion-based methods (One-2-3-45, SyncDreamer, Wonder3D) and single-view regression methods (OpenLRM, Magic123, TGS) all show substantially larger geometric errors, with CD values ranging from 0.0140 to 0.0188. In the ablation against ULIP's contrastive alignment (Table 2), LAM3D's tri-plane latent representation reduces CD from 35.23×10⁴ (ULIP) to 1.81×10⁴, demonstrating that the diffusion-based tri-plane alignment is far more effective than contrastive vector alignment for 3D reconstruction.

## Relation to Prior Work

LAM3D extends the Large Reconstruction Model (LRM) paradigm for single-image 3D reconstruction by introducing 3D point cloud priors as an intermediate representation for image-to-3D feature alignment. It departs from prior contrastive-alignment approaches (ULIP, ULIP2, Michelangelo) that compress 3D structure into small fixed-size vectors (512×1 or 1024×1) by instead using a hierarchical tri-plane latent (3×2×32×32) with a diffusion-based alignment process, and from multi-view diffusion pipelines (One-2-3-45, CRM, LGM) that suffer from inter-view inconsistency. The use of parallel UNets for per-plane denoising, a plane refiner with asymmetric decoding, and a latent SDF loss to preserve 3D structure in the compressed representation are the key architectural departures from prior tri-plane autoencoders (3DGen, NeuSDFusion).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LAM3D demonstrates that injecting explicit 3D geometric priors via point cloud compression can substantially reduce the geometric distortions inherent to single-view reconstruction, achieving SOTA Chamfer Distance (0.0083) and F-Score (85.40) on GSO in just 6 seconds. The main limitation is the absence of texture reconstruction, leaving textured mesh generation as an open extension. A broader open question is whether the point cloud prior can be learned implicitly (e.g., from multi-view data) rather than requiring explicit point cloud supervision, which would make the approach more scalable to in-the-wild images without paired 3D data.
