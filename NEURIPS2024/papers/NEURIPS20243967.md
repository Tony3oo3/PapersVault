## Problem

Weakly-supervised object detection with large-scale image-level labels (e.g., ImageNet-21K) suffers from image-to-box label mismatch: image-level labels are ambiguous and do not convey precise object-level information. Existing label-to-box assignment strategies are restricted by the raw labels, while self-training generates error-prone pseudo box labels without leveraging the provided image-level supervision. The paper addresses how to effectively combine image-level weak supervision with self-training to resolve this mismatch.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243967/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The detector takes RGB images (896×896 for box-level LVIS training, 448×448 for image-level ImageNet-21K training) and category name text prompts encoded via the CLIP language encoder as input, producing bounding boxes with open-vocabulary class labels as output. The classification layer is replaced with fixed CLIP text embeddings, enabling detection of arbitrary unseen categories at test time without fine-tuning. Evaluation follows a zero-shot cross-dataset protocol where the model trained on LVIS + ImageNet-21K is directly tested on 14 diverse target datasets.

## Method

DetLH combines two WordNet-based components: Language Hierarchical Self-training (LHST) and Language Hierarchical Prompt Generation (LHPG). LHST expands each image-level label into a multi-label annotation by recursively finding all hypernyms and hyponyms in WordNet, then merges the expanded labels with predicted pseudo box labels via logical OR. A reliability score (the model's own prediction confidence) adaptively re-weights expanded labels, enabling co-regularization: expanded labels provide richer supervision to stabilize noisy self-training, while self-training assesses and selects which expanded labels are trustworthy. LHPG bridges the training-test vocabulary gap by mapping each test concept to its best-matched WordNet synset via CLIP embedding distance, generating standardized prompts aligned with the WordNet-trained detector. The base architecture is CenterNet2 with a Swin-B backbone, trained jointly on LVIS (box-level) and ImageNet-21K (image-level) with a 1:16 sampling ratio.

## Results

Averaged over 14 zero-shot cross-detection datasets, DetLH achieves AP 34.6, AP50 49.3, AP75 36.4, APs 16.0, APm 28.4, and APl 49.5, outperforming the strongest baseline Detic (AP 31.0, AP50 44.0) by +3.6 AP and +5.3 AP50. On the common-object benchmark Object365, DetLH reaches AP 23.6 / AP50 32.5 versus Detic's 21.6 / 29.4, and on Pascal VOC it attains AP 64.4 / AP50 86.1 versus Detic's 62.4 / 83.3. The gains are largest on fine-grained wildlife detection (avg AP 51.8 vs. 45.8 for Detic) and autonomous driving (avg AP 27.6 vs. 23.8), where vocabulary and distribution gaps are most severe.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DetLH consistently outperforms all four WSOD baselines (WSDDN, YOLO9000, DLWL, Detic) across every one of the 14 evaluation datasets, with the largest margins on fine-grained and domain-shifted benchmarks (e.g., +12.3 AP50 on Arthropod Detection, +8.4 AP50 on Vistas). Against the knowledge-distillation-based RKD [17], DetLH wins on Object365 (AP 23.6 vs. 22.3). On Pascal VOC, DetLH (AP 64.4) surpasses all visual-grounding and multi-dataset methods including GLIP (61.7), GLIPv2 (62.8), OWL-ViT (60.3), DetCLIP (56.7), OMDet (60.8), and UniDet (60.1). On the ODinW benchmark (35 datasets), GLIP achieves a slightly higher average (19.7 vs. 18.2) due to its language encoder at inference, but DetLH runs over 10× faster (46 ms vs. 8333 ms for GLIP). On OpenImages, DetLH (AP 17.6) outperforms hierarchy-aware classification methods [101, 70] (both at 16.5), confirming that the co-regularization design is more effective than simply incorporating hierarchical losses.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Detic (Zhou, 2022), which established the framework of training open-vocabulary detectors with CLIP text embeddings as the classifier using box-level supervision from LVIS and image-level supervision from ImageNet-21K. It departs from prior WSOD methods (WSDDN, YOLO9000, DLWL) that address image-to-box label mismatch via rigid label-to-box assignment strategies by instead introducing WordNet's language hierarchy to expand image-level labels into richer multi-label supervision. The key conceptual departure is the co-regularization design: expanded hierarchical labels regularize noisy self-training pseudo-labels, while self-training reliability scores adaptively re-weight the expanded labels, creating a mutual correction loop absent in either pure self-training or direct hierarchical labeling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled explanation for why image-to-box label mismatch persists in WSOD (language hierarchy ambiguity) and a clean mechanism to address it, yielding consistent 2–4 AP gains across 14 diverse detection benchmarks and four backbone architectures. The LHPG component offers a lightweight, training-free way to bridge vocabulary gaps between training and testing, which is practically valuable for deploying open-vocabulary detectors on arbitrary downstream tasks. Limitations include reliance on ImageNet-21K's closed 21K vocabulary for the hierarchy, the fixed WordNet structure, and the absence of image-text pair data (e.g., LAION) that could further scale the approach. Open questions include extending the hierarchical expansion to truly open-vocabulary settings and integrating large-scale image-text pair data for WSOD training.
