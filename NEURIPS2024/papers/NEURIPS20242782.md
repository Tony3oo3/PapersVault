## Problem

Training diffusion models as priors for Bayesian inverse problems typically requires large amounts of clean latent data, which is unavailable in many scientific settings (e.g., earth/space sciences, medical imaging). Previous attempts to train diffusion models from corrupted observations (AmbientDiffusion, GSURE-based methods) produce networks that are not proper denoisers and cannot parameterize standard diffusion models, while classical empirical Bayes methods are incompatible with diffusion models because they require differentiable densities or samples with respect to parameters.

## Input / Output

The method consumes pairs (y, A) where y is a noisy/incomplete observation and A is a potentially observation-specific measurement matrix defining a linear Gaussian forward process p(y|x,A) = N(y|Ax, Σ_y). For CIFAR-10, A is a binary diagonal matrix encoding random pixel deletion; for MRI, A encodes random horizontal k-space sub-sampling at acceleration factor R. The output is a standard diffusion prior q_θ(x) parameterized by a denoiser d_θ(x_t, t), which at every EM iteration is a fully functional diffusion model compatible with any posterior sampling scheme, enabling downstream Bayesian inference on new observations.

## Method

The core contribution is an EM pipeline that alternates between (E) drawing posterior samples x ~ q_θ(x|y,A) via a novel moment matching posterior sampling (MMPS) scheme and (M) training the denoiser via unmodified denoising score matching on those samples. MMPS estimates the posterior score by decomposing it into a prior score (from the denoiser) and a likelihood score computed using the full posterior covariance V[x|x_t] estimated via Tweedie's covariance formula (Σ_t ∇_{x_t}^T d_θ(x_t,t)), rather than the isotropic heuristics used by prior methods. The resulting linear system (Σ_y + A V[x|x_t] A^T) v = y − A x̂ is solved with a truncated conjugate gradient method (1–3 iterations), exploiting the SPD structure and accessing the Jacobian only through vector-Jacobian products in automatic differentiation, avoiding any explicit N×N matrix materialization. This covariance-aware correction is critical: on manifold-structured data, heuristics produce posteriors that are orders of magnitude less accurate (measured by Sinkhorn divergence), and the EM algorithm is highly sensitive to posterior sample quality.

## Results

On corrupted CIFAR-10, the proposed EM method with Tweedie's covariance achieves FID 5.88 and IS 8.83 at 25% pixel corruption, FID 6.76 and IS 8.75 at 50%, and FID 13.18 and IS 8.14 at 75%. At 75% corruption, the method matches AmbientDiffusion's performance at only 20% corruption (FID 13.18 vs. 11.70). In the independent MMPS evaluation on FFHQ, MMPS with 3 solver iterations achieves the best LPIPS across all four inverse problems (e.g., 0.15 for random inpainting at 100 steps, 0.13 for motion deblur at 100 steps), outperforming DPS, DiffPIR, IIGDM, and TMPD.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against AmbientDiffusion [80], the closest competitor for training diffusion models from corrupted data, the proposed EM method achieves substantially lower FID at every comparable corruption level: at 75% corruption the EM method reaches FID 13.18 while AmbientDiffusion at 60% corruption only reaches FID 28.88, and the EM method at 25% corruption (FID 5.88) beats AmbientDiffusion at 20% (FID 11.70). For posterior sampling on FFHQ, MMPS with 3 solver iterations outperforms DPS [21] on LPIPS across all four tasks (e.g., 0.15 vs. 0.19 for random inpainting at 100 steps), DiffPIR [26] (0.15 vs. 0.68), IIGDM [22] (0.15 vs. 0.39), and TMPD [25] (0.15 vs. 0.58). MMPS also requires far fewer sampling steps to reach competitive quality, whereas DPS and IIGDM degrade significantly at low step counts (e.g., IIGDM at 10 steps gives LPIPS 0.59 for random inpainting vs. 0.21 for MMPS). The ablation within the EM pipeline confirms that replacing Tweedie's covariance with simple heuristics (Σₜ or (I+Σₜ⁻¹)⁻¹) causes severe degradation, with FID jumping from 13.18 to 39.94 or 118.69 at 75% corruption.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the classical EM algorithm (Dempster et al., 1977) to the empirical Bayes problem of learning a diffusion prior from incomplete/noisy observations, a setting where prior empirical Bayes methods (Louppe et al., 2019; Dockhorn et al., 2020; Vandegar et al., 2021) are inapplicable because they require differentiable densities or samples. It departs from the closest prior work on training diffusion models from corrupted data—AmbientDiffusion (Daras et al., 2023) and Kawar et al. (2024)—by producing proper denoisers compatible with standard diffusion sampling at every EM iteration, rather than surrogate networks that cannot parameterize a valid diffusion model. The moment matching posterior sampling (MMPS) contribution extends the posterior score decomposition of Chung et al. (2023) and the covariance-aware improvements of Song et al. (2023) and Boys et al. (2023) by replacing heuristic covariance estimates with a proper Tweedie-based estimate solved via conjugate gradient.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first principled EM framework for training proper diffusion priors from observations only, which is critical in scientific domains (gravitational lensing, MRI, data assimilation) where clean latent data is inaccessible. MMPS is a standalone, broadly applicable improvement for any linear inverse problem with a pre-trained diffusion prior, and its sensitivity to covariance accuracy reveals a previously underappreciated fragility: heuristics that suffice for single-shot posterior sampling cause total EM collapse. Key limitations include restriction to linear Gaussian forward processes and the ill-posed nature of empirical Bayes (the learned prior need not match the ground-truth distribution), leaving open the extension to non-linear or black-box forward processes and the incorporation of maximum-entropy regularization.
