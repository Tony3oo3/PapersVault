## Problem

Rendering-based 3D generation methods rely on 2D rendered image supervision, which inherently limits them to reconstructing only the visible outer surface of objects while neglecting hidden interior surfaces. This produces incomplete meshes with missing internal geometry, a fundamental limitation that no existing rendering-based pipeline can overcome without multi-view optimization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20244326/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image as input and produces a complete 3D mesh with per-vertex color and normal. Internally, the 3D object is encoded as an X-Ray: a sequence of L surface-layer frames (typically 8), each of resolution H×W with 8 channels (1 hit, 1 depth, 3 normal, 3 color), formatted identically to a video clip. The low-resolution stage operates at 8×64×64 and the upsampler produces 8×256×256, after which the X-Ray is decoded into a point cloud and converted to a mesh via Screened Poisson reconstruction in approximately 7 seconds total.

## Method

The core contribution is the X-Ray representation, which applies ray casting from the camera center to sequentially record depth, normal, color, and a binary hit flag at every surface intersection along each ray, then transposes the resulting grid into a multi-frame video-like tensor (L×8×H×W). For generation, a two-stage pipeline is used: first, a Spatial-Temporal 3D U-Net (adapted from Stable Video Diffusion, reduced to 10% parameters and trained from scratch) denoises a low-resolution 64×64×8 X-Ray conditioned on the input image via cross-attention with a ViT embedding; second, a spatial-temporal VAE decoder upsamples the X-Ray fourfold to 256×256 while preserving the layer count. The spatial-temporal attention alternates between 2D frame-space and 1D layer-sequence dimensions, treating surface layers as the temporal axis of a video. This design lets the model inherit the inductive biases and pretrained priors of video diffusion while operating on a compact, surface-only 3D representation that captures both visible and hidden geometry in a single view.

## Results

On the GSO dataset, X-Ray achieves a Chamfer Distance of 0.056 and F-Score@0.1 of 0.973, outperforming the best prior method TripoSR (CD 0.084, FS@0.1 0.878) by 33% in CD and 9.5 points in FS@0.1. On OmniObject3D, X-Ray similarly leads with CD 0.054 and FS@0.1 0.972 versus TripoSR's CD 0.080 and FS@0.1 0.892. The method also reports an inference time of approximately 7 seconds total (1s diffusion + 1s upsampling + 5s mesh decoding) with 4.8 GB GPU memory for the diffusion model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

X-Ray outperforms all five baselines on both GSO and OmniObject3D across both metrics (CD and FS@0.1). The closest competitor is TripoSR [49], which X-Ray surpasses by 33% in Chamfer Distance on GSO (0.056 vs. 0.084) and by 9.5 points in FS@0.1 (0.973 vs. 0.878); on OmniObject3D the gap is similar (CD 0.054 vs. 0.080, FS@0.1 0.972 vs. 0.892). TGS [8] ranks second on both datasets (CD 0.096/0.091, FS@0.1 0.803/0.822), still trailing X-Ray by roughly 40% in CD. ZeroShape [18] and OpenLRM [13] occupy the middle tier with CD around 0.136–0.148, while One-2-3-45 [25] performs weakest (CD 0.175–0.187, FS@0.1 0.465–0.490), highlighting the advantage of X-Ray's ability to capture hidden interior surfaces that rendering-based methods inherently miss.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant rendering-based 3D generation paradigm (LRM, TripoSR, LGM, DMV3D) that relies on multi-view 2D supervision and inherently cannot reconstruct hidden inner surfaces. Conceptually, it draws on depth peeling (used for transparent surface rendering) and medical x-ray imaging to propose a sequential multi-layer surface representation, then repurposes the Stable Video Diffusion spatial-temporal U-Net and VAE decoder as the generative backbone by treating the X-Ray frames as a video sequence. The key lineage shift is from multi-view image supervision to single-view ray-cast sequential surface encoding, enabling a video-diffusion-based generator rather than a multi-view diffusion or SDS-based optimizer.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The X-Ray representation addresses a fundamental gap in rendering-based 3D generation—the inability to produce complete objects with inner surfaces—while maintaining single-image input and ~7-second inference. However, the fixed 8-layer limit causes visible failures on complex multi-surface objects (e.g., a hamburger), and the generated meshes lack smoothness, leaving open the question of how to handle variable and sparse layer sequences. The paper suggests exploring LLM-based architectures and sparsity-aware generation as future directions, and the video-format compatibility of the representation could influence how future 3D generators are structured beyond this specific pipeline.
