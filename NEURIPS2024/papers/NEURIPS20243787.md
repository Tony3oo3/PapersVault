## Problem

As the number and diversity of human experts contributing to complex societal problems grows, it becomes increasingly difficult to identify, combine, and refine complementary knowledge into superior solutions. Existing model-combination methods (ensembling, Mixture-of-Experts) cannot innovate beyond the scaffolding provided by individual experts, and evolution from scratch struggles in the vast high-dimensional policy spaces typical of real-world domains. The paper addresses the gap of lacking a systematic AI framework that can distill diverse expert solutions into a canonical form and then recombine and refine them to discover policies beyond what any single expert or AI alone could produce.

## Input / Output

The method takes 169 human expert prescriptors (each a runnable program mapping a geo, 90-day time frame, and relative IP cost weights to a 90×12 matrix of intervention stringency levels in ℤ₅) and a surrogate predictor that forecasts daily cases given a context and policy schedule. Distillation queries each prescriptor over 212,400 (date-range, geo) pairs to generate training data. The output is a Pareto front of 90-day IP schedules across 20 high-mortality regions, trading off predicted new cases against average daily policy stringency (cost ∈ [0,34]), structured so that decision-makers can select a tradeoff point matching their specific political and economic constraints.

## Method

RHEA (Realizing Human Expertise through AI) operates in four steps: Define, Gather, Distill, and Evolve. In the Distill step, each expert prescriptor is converted into an autoregressive neural network via behavioral cloning, minimizing L1 loss over 212,400 query-response pairs using Adam in Keras, so the NN replicates the expert's full input-output behavior in a canonical, evolvable form. In the Evolve step, the 169 distilled networks are injected into the initial population of the ESP (Evolutionary Surrogate-assisted Prescription) framework, replacing the random initialization used in standard ESP; NSGA-II then performs 100 generations of multi-objective crossover and mutation to recombine and refine the expert-derived solutions. The key insight is that seeding evolution with diverse, high-quality expert models (rather than random weights) dramatically reduces the effective jump size in the optimization landscape, enabling reliable convergence to the full Pareto front even as the action space grows, while crossover recombines complementary expert knowledge and mutation innovates beyond it. The resulting solutions are traceable back to their expert ancestors, enabling credit attribution and auditability.

## Results

On the XPRIZE Pandemic Response Challenge (235 geos, 12 intervention policies, 90-day prescriptions), RHEA achieves a REM (R1 Empirical Metric) of nearly 100%, meaning a decision-maker with a real-world stringency budget would almost always select a RHEA policy over alternatives. RHEA's Pareto front dominates approximately 75% of the Distilled (human-only) Pareto front (DR ≈ 0.75), and substantially outperforms both evolution-from-scratch and random initialization on all metrics (DR, MCR, HVI, REM, RUN). Distillation fidelity was confirmed with Spearman rank correlations of ≈ 0.7 (p < 10⁻²⁰) between submitted expert models and their distilled neural network counterparts on both cases and cost objectives.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RHEA is compared against four baselines: (1) **Distilled** (169 human expert models converted to neural networks, representing human-only performance), (2) **Evolved from scratch** (same evolutionary algorithm but initialized with random weights instead of distilled models), (3) **Random** (randomly initialized neural networks without evolution), and (4) **MORL methods** (GPI-LS, GPI-PD, and Envelope Q-Learning from the MORL benchmark suite). RHEA decisively outperforms all baselines: it dominates ~75% of the Distilled Pareto front (DR ≈ 0.75), achieves REM ≈ 100% versus much lower values for all other methods, and produces the largest MCR and HVI improvements. Evolution from scratch also improves over random initialization but falls well short of RHEA, confirming that expert knowledge is essential in this high-dimensional search space (≈10⁶²⁰ possible schedules per geo). In the synthetic illustrative domain, RHEA discovers the full optimal Pareto front reliably even as the number of interventions grows, while evolution alone and MORL methods (GPI-LS specifically) degrade sharply with increasing action-space size, converging to only the most convex portions of the front. The paper also notes that some expert models with unimpressive standalone performance make outsized contributions to the final RHEA Pareto front through recombination, highlighting the value of soliciting diverse expertise even when individual contributions appear weak.

## Relation to Prior Work

RHEA builds directly on the Evolutionary Surrogate-assisted Prescription (ESP) framework [24], which previously evolved COVID-19 intervention prescriptors from randomly initialized neural networks; RHEA replaces this random initialization with distilled versions of 169 diverse human expert models, fundamentally changing the search starting point. Conceptually, the paper departs from standard model-combination approaches (Mixture-of-Experts, Weighted Ensembles) by arguing that neither can innovate beyond the scaffolding provided by individual experts, and from multi-objective reinforcement learning by showing that evolutionary recombination of knowledge blocks outperforms gradient-based methods in high-dimensional, non-convex policy spaces. The distillation step draws on behavioral cloning and knowledge distillation literature [30, 31], while the evolutionary step uses NSGA-II [16], but the novelty lies in their specific combination into a pipeline that treats human expertise as evolvable building blocks rather than fixed inputs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates a concrete, reproducible pipeline for systematically unlocking latent potential in diverse human expert solutions, with the notable finding that low-performing expert models can make outsized contributions through evolutionary recombination—arguing that broad solicitation of expertise has value even when individual contributions appear suboptimal. Key limitations include evaluation entirely within a surrogate predictor (no real-world deployment or user studies), a uniform cost measure that doesn't reflect geo-specific economics, and a single application domain. Open questions include generalizability to other complex policy domains (climate, sustainability), handling of uncertainty and compounding approximation errors over time, and whether the framework scales to settings where expert models use heterogeneous external data sources. The modular design and low compute requirements (~3.9×10⁶ J per run) position it as a practical tool for resource-constrained decision-makers, but hands-on political and civil evaluation remains a critical gap.
