## Problem

Group-convolutional architectures, particularly graph neural networks, suffer from instabilities as depth increases: node representations converge (over-smoothing) after only a few message-passing layers, and gradients can vanish or explode. While ad-hoc interventions like skip connections and graph rewiring have been proposed, no principled architectural modification with provable guarantees against over-smoothing existed. This paper asks whether enforcing unitarity on the convolution operator itself can fundamentally resolve these stability issues.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a graph adjacency matrix A ∈ R^{n×n} (typically normalized as D^{-1/2}AD^{-1/2}) and a node feature matrix X ∈ C^{n×d} in the complex domain, and produces transformed node embeddings of the same shape that can be pooled or read out for graph classification, node classification, or regression. A key structural constraint is that the message-passing operator must be unitary (or orthogonal in the real case), which is enforced by parameterizing the linear map in the Lie algebra and applying the matrix exponential, rather than learning an unconstrained weight matrix. The complex-valued intermediate representations are a necessary byproduct of enforcing unitarity on the node-mixing step.

## Method

The paper introduces two unitary graph convolution operators. UniConv (Definition 1) applies a separable unitary map f(X) = exp(iAt)·X·U, where exp(iAt) is the unitary node-mixing operator (a quantum-walk-like propagation) and U ∈ U(d) is a unitary feature transformation; Lie UniConv (Definition 3) instead parameterizes the full operator in the Lie algebra as g_conv(X) = AXW with W skew-Hermitian, then applies the exponential map exp(g_conv) to obtain a fully unitary (or orthogonal, if W is real) transformation. The exponential map is computed via a K-th order Taylor truncation (K=10–12 in practice), giving constant-factor overhead over standard message passing. The central theoretical result is that the Rayleigh quotient R_G(X) is invariant under unitary convolution (Proposition 6), which provably prevents over-smoothing, whereas vanilla convolution provably decreases it (Proposition 7). The approach generalizes to arbitrary finite groups via Lie-algebra parameterization (Algorithm 1) or block-diagonal Fourier-domain implementation (Algorithm 3), and compositions of unitary layers with isometric activations (e.g., GroupSort) yield perfect dynamical isometry, eliminating vanishing/exploding gradients without residual connections or batch normalization.

## Results

On the LRGB benchmark, Lie UniGCN achieves the best Peptides-func AP (0.7173) and UniGCN achieves the best Peptides-struct MAE (0.2425), both outperforming all message-passing baselines. On the Heterophilous Graph Datasets, Unitary GCN attains the top AP on Roman-empire (87.21), Amazon-ratings (55.34), Tolokers (84.83), and Questions (79.21), while Lie UniGCN achieves the best ROC AUC on Minesweeper (96.11) and Questions (80.01). On the ring-graph toy model, unitary GCN is the only message-passing architecture able to learn long-range node distances, whereas standard GCN, GINE, and GatedGCN all fail.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On LRGB, Lie UniGCN (0.7173 Peptides-func AP) outperforms all message-passing baselines including GCN (0.6860), GINE (0.6621), GatedGCN (0.6765), and GUMP (0.6843), and is competitive with attention-based methods like DREW (0.7150) and CRAWL (0.7074). UniGCN achieves the best Peptides-struct MAE (0.2425) among all methods, beating even GRAPH ViT (0.2449). However, on COCO and PASCAL-VOC, global-attention architectures (GPS at 0.3884/0.4440, CRAWL at 0.4588) still outperform the unitary variants (0.3153/0.4005), suggesting unitary message passing alone does not fully match attention for very long-range signals. On the Heterophilous Graph Datasets, Unitary GCN dominates all baselines on four of five datasets (Roman-empire 87.21 vs GT 86.51; Amazon-ratings 55.34 vs SAGE 53.63; Tolokers 84.83 vs GAT 83.70; Questions 79.21 vs GT 77.95), while Lie UniGCN achieves the best Minesweeper ROC AUC (96.11 vs SAGE 93.51). The toy ring-graph experiment shows a qualitative gap: unitary GCN successfully learns node distances up to 50 hops while all standard message-passing architectures (GCN, GINE, GatedGCN) fail entirely.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the unitary RNN literature (Arjovsky et al. 2016; Lezcano-Casado & Martinez-Rubio 2019) to the graph domain by replacing the standard message-passing operator with a unitary one via the exponential map exp(iAt), building on the Lie algebra parameterization framework used in orthogonal CNNs (Singla & Feizi 2021; Trockman & Kolter 2021). It departs from prior unitary GNN proposals (Guo et al. 2022; Abbahaddou et al. 2024) that enforce orthogonality only in the feature transformation matrix W, by enforcing unitarity in the node-level message passing itself where over-smoothing actually occurs. The approach also generalizes beyond graphs to arbitrary finite group convolutions, a setting where no prior unitary convolution framework existed, and achieves strict unitarity at O(T|V||E|) cost versus the O(|E|³) scaling of the closest competing method (Qiu et al. 2024).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first provable architectural guarantee against over-smoothing in GNNs: the Rayleigh quotient is invariant under unitary graph convolution, eliminating the need for ad-hoc interventions like skip connections, rewiring, or batch normalization. The dynamical isometry property further ensures stable gradient flow in deep networks without additional regularization. Key limitations include the computational overhead of the exponential map (mitigated by Taylor truncation), the requirement for complex-valued representations, and the acknowledgment that unitarity may be inappropriate for tasks where the target function is inherently non-invertible or non-isometric. Open questions include whether approximately unitary layers suffice, how to efficiently incorporate edge features, and whether the certified adversarial robustness guarantee translates to practical improvements on geometric data.
