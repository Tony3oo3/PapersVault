## Problem

Training generalist robotic policies is blocked by heterogeneity: different embodiments have distinct proprioception dimensions, camera configurations, action spaces, and operating environments, making it difficult to jointly train on large-scale multi-robot data. Prior generalist policies (RT-X, Octo, OpenVLA) either ignore proprioception during pre-training or assume a unified data format, limiting how much cross-embodiment diversity can be exploited. This paper asks whether a shared policy representation can be pre-trained across 50+ heterogeneous datasets spanning real robots, simulations, and human videos while explicitly handling both proprioceptive and visual heterogeneity.

## Input / Output

Each embodiment provides RGB images (resized to 224×224, one or more views) and a proprioceptive vector of embodiment-specific dimension (e.g., 7 for end-effector pose, or joint positions), optionally with language instructions. The stem compresses these variable-length, variable-dimension inputs into a fixed 16 tokens per modality (40 total with language), so the trunk always sees a constant-length sequence regardless of embodiment. The head outputs a normalized action trajectory of fixed horizon 8, which is un-normalized using per-dataset statistics at inference; the head architecture (MLP, transformer decoder, or diffusion policy) is swappable per task.

## Method

HPT modularizes the policy into three parts: embodiment-specific stems, a shared transformer trunk, and task-specific heads. Each stem uses a frozen ResNet-18 (or other encoder) for vision and an MLP for proprioception, then applies cross-attention with 16 learnable query tokens to compress each modality into a fixed-size token sequence, analogous to Perceiver's mechanism. The trunk is a decoder-only transformer (3M–1.1B parameters) that processes the concatenated token sequence and is the only component updated at every training iteration; stems and heads are updated only when their embodiment is sampled, resembling a mixture-of-experts routing scheme. Pre-training uses supervised behavior cloning with Huber loss on normalized actions across all datasets simultaneously, and transfer to a new embodiment reinitializes the stem and head while freezing the trunk. The key architectural insight is that by tokenizing each embodiment into a shared latent space, the trunk can be trained on the union of all heterogeneous data without requiring a homogeneous data format, and scaling experiments show consistent improvements in validation loss with more data, more embodiments, and larger models.

## Results

On the real-world Sweep Leftover task (Table 3), HPT-XL Finetuned achieves 76.7±3.3% success rate, outperforming the From Scratch baseline (43.3±3.8%) by 33.4 percentage points and the best vision-only pre-trained baseline VC-1 (53.3±2.6%) by 23.4 points. Across four simulation benchmarks (Meta-World, RoboMimic, Fleet-Tools, Simpler), pre-trained HPT trunks consistently improve closed-loop task success rates over from-scratch and no-trunk baselines, with larger trunks (HPT-B → HPT-XL) yielding further gains. Under the LeRobot implementation, HPT matches state-of-the-art single-task policies: 78% on PushT (matching Diffusion Policy) and 60% on Aloha Transfer Box (matching ACT).

## Baselines & Comparisons

HPT clearly wins against all from-scratch baselines: on Sweep Leftover, HPT-XL Finetuned (76.7%) beats From Scratch (43.3%) by 33.4 points and No-Trunk (26.7%) by 50 points. Against vision-only pre-trained models (R3M 50.0%, Voltron 46.7%, VC-1 53.3%), HPT-XL Finetuned leads by 23–30 points, demonstrating that jointly pre-training proprioception and vision (rather than adding proprioception post hoc) is critical. The No Prop. Finetuned ablation (63.3%) confirms that including proprioception during pre-training adds ~13 points over vision-only transfer. On the Simpler benchmark, HPT is compared against Octo, RT-1-X, and RT-2-X, though the paper notes the simulation tasks lack proprioception inputs, making them less ideal for HPT. Under LeRobot, HPT ties Diffusion Policy (78% on PushT) and ACT (60% on Aloha Transfer Box), showing the trunk is compatible with SOTA policy heads without degrading performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

HPT extends the Perceiver-style cross-attention mechanism (fixed learnable tokens attending to variable-length features) and the BLIP-2-style lightweight projector for aligning frozen encoder outputs into a shared embedding space, repurposing both for the robotics domain where the 'modalities' are heterogeneous embodiments rather than image/text. It departs from prior robot pre-training work (R3M, VC-1, Voltron, Octo, RT-X) that either pre-trains only the vision backbone or excludes proprioception during pre-training and adds it post hoc; HPT jointly pre-trains proprioception and vision across 52 heterogeneous datasets using a modular stem-trunk-head architecture analogous to a mixture-of-experts where each embodiment owns its stem/head but shares the transformer trunk.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first systematic study of scaling behaviors (data, model, compute) for heterogeneous robot policy pre-training that explicitly includes proprioception, simulation, and human video data alongside real-robot teleoperation, offering a practical open-source framework (stem-trunk-head) that practitioners can adopt for new embodiments with minimal fine-tuning. Key limitations include success rates still below 90%, evaluation restricted to short-horizon manipulation, moderate scale relative to LLM pre-training (1B params, ~155M samples), and reliance on supervised behavior cloning as the sole pre-training objective. Open questions include whether self-supervised or world-model objectives would improve the trunk, how to handle longer-horizon and bimanual tasks, and what dataset mixtures and curation strategies yield the best representations.
