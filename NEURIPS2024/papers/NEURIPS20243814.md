## Problem

Whether hybrid RL (combining offline and online data) can provably break the minimax lower bounds for purely offline or online RL in linear MDPs without requiring the behavior policy to cover every state-action pair visited by the optimal policy remained an open question. Prior hybrid RL results either imposed single-policy concentrability, were limited to tabular MDPs, or yielded suboptimal worst-case bounds that did not improve on the best offline-only or online-only guarantees.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243814/cropped_page8_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The algorithms consume an offline dataset of N_off episodes collected by an arbitrary behavior policy (no quality assumption), N_on sequential online episodes, known feature maps φ_h: S×A→R^d with ‖φ‖≤1, and a tolerance parameter τ for reward-agnostic exploration. Algorithm 1 (RAPPEL) outputs a fixed near-optimal policy with a PAC sub-optimality gap bound, while Algorithm 2 (HYRULE) outputs a regret-minimizing policy that also yields a PAC guarantee via regret-to-PAC conversion. Notably, Algorithm 2 is partition-unaware and automatically adapts to the best state-action partition without the user specifying d_on or τ.

## Method

The paper proposes two complementary algorithms. RAPPEL (online-to-offline) first runs reward-agnostic exploration (OPTCOV) to collect online covariates that fill coverage gaps in the offline dataset, then applies the minimax-optimal pessimistic offline RL algorithm LinPEVI-ADV+ on the combined dataset. HYRULE (offline-to-online) warm-starts LSVI-UCB++ by initializing its covariance matrices and weight estimates from the offline dataset, effectively treating offline episodes as a pre-populated experience replay buffer. Both algorithms partition the state-action space into an offline partition X_off (well-covered by the behavior policy) and an online partition X_on (requiring exploration), then decompose the error or regret into contributions from each partition. The key technical insight is sharpening the dimensional dependence from d to d_on (the dimension of the online partition's feature subspace) and c_off(X_off) (a partial all-policy concentrability coefficient) via orthogonal projections, combined with a novel truncation argument using the total variance lemma to maintain an H³ horizon dependence rather than H⁴ or H⁶.

## Results

The paper establishes two main theoretical guarantees for hybrid RL in linear MDPs. For the online-to-offline setting (RAPPEL, Algorithm 1), the sub-optimality gap is bounded by √(c_off(X_off)²dH³/(N_off·α_off)) + √(d_on²dH³/(N_on·α_on)), improving on the prior best hybrid bound of √(d²H⁷/N) by at least a factor of H^{3/2}. For the offline-to-online setting (HYRULE, Algorithm 2), the regret is bounded by √(c_off(X_off)²dH³N_on²/N_off) + √(d_on·dH³N_on), improving on the prior best hybrid bound (Tan & Xu, 2024) by a factor of H². Both bounds achieve an H³ horizon dependence (vs. H⁴–H⁷ in prior work) and reduce the dimension dependence from d² to d_on·d and c_off(X_off)·d via partition-based projections.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the only prior hybrid RL algorithm for linear MDPs (Wagenmaker & Pacchiano, 2023), RAPPEL improves the worst-case error bound from √(d²H⁷/N) to √(c_off·dH³·min{c_off,H}/N_off) + √(d_on·dH³·min{d_on,H}/N_on), a gain of at least H^{3/2} in the horizon dependence. For the offline-to-online setting, HYRULE beats Tan & Xu (2024) by a factor of H² (H³ vs. H⁵ horizon dependence) and beats Song et al. (2023) and Nakamoto et al. (2023) (both with C*√(d²H⁶N_on)) and Amortila et al. (2024) (√((C*+c_on)d³H⁶N_on)) by at least H² while also removing the dependence on the global concentrability coefficient C*. Both algorithms match or improve upon the offline-only minimax-optimal bound (Xiong et al., 2023) and the online-only minimax-optimal regret bound (He et al., 2023; Zhou et al., 2021) respectively, with provable gains when the offline data is of high quality. Empirically on a scaled-down Tetris environment, hybrid RL with even adversarially collected offline data achieves better coverage than online-only exploration, and warm-starting LSVI-UCB++ with offline data (Algorithm 2) yields lower regret than LSVI-UCB++ alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the hybrid RL framework of Xie et al. (2022b) and Li et al. (2023b) from tabular MDPs to linear MDPs, combining three existing algorithmic components: OPTCOV (Wagenmaker and Jamieson, 2023) for reward-agnostic exploration, LinPEVI-ADV+ (Xiong et al., 2023) for minimax-optimal offline RL, and LSVI-UCB++ (He et al., 2023) for online regret minimization. The key technical departure from prior hybrid RL work (Wagenmaker and Pacchiano, 2023; Tan and Xu, 2024) is a partition-based error decomposition that separates the state-action space into offline-covered and online-explored regions, enabling dimension-dependent bounds (d_on, c_off) rather than full-dimension (d) bounds. The truncation argument combined with the total variance lemma to achieve H^3 horizon dependence without well-covered assumptions is a novel analytical technique not present in prior offline or online RL analyses for linear MDPs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to establish that hybrid RL provably breaks both the offline-only and online-only minimax sample complexity barriers in linear MDPs, resolving a question posed by Xie et al. (2022b) beyond the tabular setting. The practical significance is that Algorithm 1 (RAPPEL) outputs a fixed policy suitable for deployment in safety-critical domains (medicine, defense) where randomized policies are unacceptable, while Algorithm 2 (HYRULE) minimizes regret during exploration for settings like mobile health. Key open questions remain: achieving a single algorithm that simultaneously improves both offline and online lower bounds, obtaining H^3 dependence for general (non-tabular) linear MDPs in the offline-only setting, and extending these results beyond linear function approximation to broader function classes.
