## Problem

Existing digital watermarking methods for meshes, point clouds, and implicit NeRF representations cannot be directly applied to 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) models, which use explicit per-Gaussian parameters without neural networks. Naively embedding perturbations into 3D Gaussian parameters causes visible geometric distortion in rendered images, and prior NeRF watermarking methods can only extract messages from 2D renderings, leaving the 3D model itself unprotected. The paper addresses how to achieve invisible, robust copyright protection that covers both the 3D Gaussian parameters and the 2D rendered images simultaneously.

## Input / Output

The method takes a fully trained 3DGS model (with per-Gaussian parameters: position μ, rotation R, scale S, SH color coefficients c, and opacity α) and a 48-bit binary copyright message as input, and produces a watermarked 3DGS model where the original Gaussians remain unchanged and new densified 'GaussianMarker' Gaussians are appended as perturbations. Decoding is dual-path: a PointNet-based 3D decoder samples 10k–50k Gaussians from the watermarked model to recover the message directly from 3D parameters, while a pre-trained HiDDeN 2D decoder extracts the message from any rendered novel view. The watermarked model is compatible with standard 3DGS rendering and point-cloud export pipelines.

## Method

The key insight is to use Laplace approximation of the posterior over 3DGS parameters to compute per-Gaussian uncertainty via the Fisher information (Hessian of rendered images w.r.t. parameters, summed across training views); only Gaussians whose uncertainty exceeds the mean threshold are selected for perturbation. Rather than modifying existing Gaussians, the method densifies high-uncertainty Gaussians by sampling new positions from N(μ_i, Σ_i) and cloning other parameters, producing a set of 'GaussianMarker' perturbations that are imperceptible in rendered views. Training proceeds in two phases: first, a pre-trained HiDDeN 2D decoder acts as a teacher to distill watermarking knowledge into the 3D GaussianMarker via photometric and message-decoding losses on rendered images; second, a PointNet-based 3D message decoder is trained with adversarial loss and a 3D distortion layer (Gaussian noise, random rotation, translation, cropout) to robustly extract the message from the watermarked point set. This uncertainty-gated densification strategy preserves low-uncertainty (detail-critical) Gaussians untouched while concentrating all perturbations in regions where the model is already under-constrained.

## Results

On the Blender dataset, GaussianMarker achieves the best reconstruction quality (PSNR 31.53, SSIM 0.9082, LPIPS 0.0759) while simultaneously attaining 97.91% bit accuracy, outperforming all baselines on both metrics. On LLFF and MipNeRF360, the method maintains PSNR of 28.61 and 29.16 respectively with bit accuracies of 98.33% and 97.32%. For 3D message extraction, the 3D decoder achieves near-perfect decoding (100% clean, 99.91% under translation, 98.95% under rotation) with the smallest geometry difference (L1 Diff = 0.00003, SNR = 43.23) among all methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GaussianMarker dominates all four baselines on both reconstruction quality and bit accuracy across all three datasets. Compared to CopyRNeRF [4], which achieves only ~60% bit accuracy and moderate PSNR (30.29 on Blender), GaussianMarker improves accuracy by ~37 percentage points while also achieving higher PSNR (31.53). HiDDeN+3DGS [11,1] fails to transmit watermarks into 3D Gaussians, yielding near-random 50% accuracy despite reasonable PSNR, confirming that 2D image watermarks cannot be recovered from 3D parameters. The 3DGS-with-messages baseline (MLP embedding) achieves higher accuracy (~80%) but at severe quality cost (PSNR drops to 22.65 on Blender, LPIPS 0.1584), while 3DGS-with-fine-tuning shows moderate quality but limited accuracy (~67%). For 3D message extraction (Table 2), the 3D decoder achieves 100% clean accuracy and >92% under all 3D attacks (noise, translation, rotation, cropout), far exceeding the 64–85% range of baselines, while maintaining the smallest geometry difference (L1 Diff = 0.00003 vs. 0.009–0.105 for baselines).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the digital watermarking lineage from implicit NeRF representations (CopyRNeRF, StegaNeRF) and explicit mesh/point-cloud methods to the 3D Gaussian Splatting format, which uniquely combines explicit per-Gaussian parameters with differentiable 2D volume-splatting rendering. It repurposes Fisher information–based uncertainty estimation (from FisherRF) to select which Gaussians to densify, and adapts HiDDeN (2D image watermarking) and PointNet (3D point-set classification) as dual decoders. The key conceptual departure from prior NeRF watermarking is avoiding MLP-based perturbation of existing parameters; instead, the method leaves original Gaussians untouched and adds new densified Gaussians as the watermark carrier, guided by a Bayesian uncertainty criterion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As 3DGS becomes a dominant format for creating and distributing 3D assets, a practical, fast (≤20 min on a single GPU), and invisible watermarking method is needed to enable ownership claims over both the 3D model and its 2D renderings. The dual 3D+2D decoding pathway addresses a gap left by NeRF watermarking (which only decodes from images) and mesh watermarking (which only decodes from geometry). Open questions include robustness under dynamic 3DGS (temporal editing), compatibility with aggressive compression pipelines, and defense against adversarial removal attacks beyond the tested noise/rotation/cropping suite. The authors note that the method may still be vulnerable to more sophisticated malicious operations and plan to explore motion-transfer-based augmentation for dynamic scenes.
