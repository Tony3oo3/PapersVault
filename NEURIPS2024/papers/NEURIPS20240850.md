## Problem

Current multi-modal benchmarks for large vision-language models (LVLMs) suffer from two critical flaws: many evaluation samples can be answered correctly without any visual input (relying solely on LLM world knowledge or answers embedded in the question text), and unintentional data leakage during LLM/LVLM training allows models to 'recall' answers to visual questions without seeing images. These issues inflate reported scores and obscure the true multi-modal capabilities of LVLMs, potentially misguiding the field.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240850/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each MMStar sample pairs an RGB image with a text question and four multiple-choice options, requiring the model to select the correct answer. Beyond standard accuracy, the evaluation produces two novel per-benchmark metrics: Multi-modal Gain (MG = score_with_image − score_without_image) quantifying the actual benefit of visual input, and Multi-modal Leakage (ML = max(0, LVLM_text_score − LLM_base_score)) quantifying how much the LVLM's text-only performance exceeds its LLM backbone, indicating memorized training data. The 1,500 samples are balanced across 6 core capabilities (coarse perception, fine-grained perception, instance reasoning, logical reasoning, science & technology, mathematics) and 18 detailed axes, with 250 samples per core capability.

## Method

MMStar is constructed through a three-stage curation pipeline starting from 22,401 samples drawn from six existing benchmarks (MMBench, SEED, MMMU, ScienceQA, AI2D, MathVista). First, an automated filter uses eight LLMs (GPT4-Turbo, GeminiPro, and six open-source models ≥20B) as inspectors under 2-shot inference; samples answered correctly by more than 2 of the 8 LLMs are discarded as visually unnecessary or leaked, reducing the pool to 11,607. Second, sixteen LVLMs score the remaining samples to assign four difficulty tiers (easy: 12–16 hits, moderate: 8–11, hard: 4–7, tough: 0–3). Third, three domain experts perform manual review with cross-validation, selecting 1,500 samples that guarantee visual dependency, minimal data leakage, and require advanced multi-modal reasoning, while balancing difficulty and capability coverage. The paper further introduces MG and ML metrics computed by evaluating each LVLM with and without image input and comparing against its LLM backbone, enabling benchmark-specific quantification of genuine multi-modal gain versus memorization.

## Results

On the proposed MMStar benchmark (1,500 vision-indispensable samples), GPT-4V with high-resolution input achieves the best average accuracy of 57.1%, with the strongest multi-modal gain (MG=43.6) and minimal multi-modal leakage (ML=1.3). Among open-source models, InternLM-XComposer2 (7B) reaches 55.4% average, while LLaVA-Next (34B) surpasses both GPT-4V and GeminiPro-Vision in the mathematics dimension (53.6% vs. 49.8% and 50.0%). No LVLM exceeds 60% in fine-grained perception, logical reasoning, science & technology, or mathematics, highlighting these as the most challenging dimensions. LLMs without visual input score near random chance (24.6%) on MMStar, confirming the benchmark's visual dependency.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GPT-4V (high-resolution) leads all 16 evaluated LVLMs on MMStar with 57.1% average accuracy, outperforming GeminiPro-Vision (42.6%) by 14.5 points and the best open-source model InternLM-XComposer2 (55.4%) by 1.7 points. Increasing GPT-4V's input resolution boosts its average from 46.1% to 57.1%, a 11-point gain that underscores the importance of visual detail. LLaVA-Next (34B) is the only open-source model to surpass both GPT-4V and GeminiPro-Vision in mathematics (53.6% vs. 49.8% and 50.0%), though it trails in other dimensions. The MG/ML analysis reveals that GPT-4V achieves the highest multi-modal gain (43.6 on MMStar) with minimal leakage (1.3), while Sphinx-X-MoE (57B) shows the highest average ML (12.3) across all benchmarks, indicating substantial data contamination in its multi-modal training. LLMs without visual input score 12.2–26.4% on MMStar (vs. 24.6% random), confirming that the benchmark effectively isolates true multi-modal capability from text-only world knowledge.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the existing line of multimodal benchmark construction (MMMU, MMBench, ScienceQA, AI2D, SEED, MathVista) by identifying two systemic flaws in how LVLMs are evaluated: many samples are answerable without visual content, and unintentional data leakage during LLM/LVLM training inflates scores. Rather than proposing a new model architecture or training method, it repurposes the evaluation task itself, introducing an automated LLM-inspector pipeline and human review to curate a vision-indispensable benchmark (MMStar), along with two new metrics (multi-modal gain and multi-modal leakage) that have no direct precedent in prior evaluation literature. The curation process draws on the sample pools of existing benchmarks but applies a fundamentally different selection criterion—visual necessity and leakage resistance—rather than coverage of capability axes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that leading LLMs achieve 42–43% on MMMU without any visual input, and that Sphinx-X-MoE scores 43.6% on MMMU text-only (vs. 17.9% for its LLM backbone), fundamentally undermines confidence in prior LVLM rankings and suggests the field has been partially measuring memorization rather than genuine multimodal understanding. The MG/ML metrics offer a practical, benchmark-agnostic tool for future evaluations, and MMStar's 1,500 human-verified samples provide a cleaner arena for comparison. Open questions include whether the curation process can scale to new domains, whether future model training will still inadvertently include MMStar samples, and whether a held-out test set (acknowledged as a limitation) is needed to fully prevent contamination. The paper also highlights that no LVLM reaches 60% on fine-grained perception, logical reasoning, science & technology, or mathematics, pointing to clear capability gaps.
