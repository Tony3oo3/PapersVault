## Problem

Most multimodal deep learning approaches for biomedical data either concatenate raw inputs (early fusion, losing structural information) or train separate modality-specific encoders (late/intermediate fusion, preventing cross-modal interaction learning). There is no fusion paradigm that simultaneously preserves modality-specific structure, learns cross-modal interactions on raw data, handles missing modalities without noise injection, and remains interpretable to domain experts.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242056/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

HEALNet consumes structurally heterogeneous inputs: H&E-stained whole slide images (downsampled to 2x/4x, tiled into non-overlapping 256×256 patches encoded as 2048-dim ResNet50 feature vectors) and multi-omic tabular data (RNAseq expression, WGS mutations, copy number variations) treated as separate modalities. The model outputs logits over four survival-risk quartile bins, converted to hazard via sigmoid and to survival probability via the product of (1 − hazard) across bins. In the MIMIC-III extension, inputs are clinical tabular variables plus 24-step vital-sign time series, producing multi-class mortality and binary ICD-9 disease predictions.

## Method

HEALNet maintains a shared latent array S ∈ R^{a×b} that is iteratively updated across d fusion layers, where at each step the latent serves as the query (Q_m = W_m^(q) S) while each modality m supplies modality-specific keys and values (K_m = W_m^(k) X_m, V_m = W_m^(v) X_m) via scaled dot-product cross-attention. The modality-specific attention weights are shared across all d layers for parameter efficiency, and a self-normalizing network (SNN) block is applied after each modality update to provide built-in regularisation. This hybrid design lets the shared latent accumulate cross-modal context while the per-modality weights preserve each data source's internal structure, avoiding both the information loss of concatenation and the dimensionality explosion of Kronecker-product fusion. Missing modalities are handled by simply skipping their update step at inference, introducing no imputation noise. L1 and L2 regularisation combined with SNN blocks address overfitting on the small patient cohorts typical of biomedical datasets.

## Results

On four TCGA cancer survival analysis tasks (BLCA, BRCA, KIRP, UCEC), HEALNet achieves concordance Index (c-Index) of 0.668, 0.638, 0.812, and 0.626 respectively, outperforming all multimodal fusion baselines on every dataset. Compared to the best unimodal baseline, HEALNet gains up to ~10% c-Index on BLCA and BRCA, ~4% on KIRP, and is near-equivalent on UCEC (a modality-dominance case). On the secondary MIMIC-III classification tasks, HEALNet achieves 0.767 AUC (ICD-9) and 0.748 Macro-AUC (MORT), again surpassing all unimodal and multimodal baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HEALNet outperforms all five multimodal baselines across all four TCGA datasets. Against the strongest intermediate-fusion baseline MOTCAT, HEALNet gains +0.037 c-Index on BLCA (0.668 vs 0.631), +0.031 on BRCA (0.638 vs 0.607), +0.002 on KIRP (0.812 vs 0.810), and +0.039 on UCEC (0.626 vs 0.587). Against the late-fusion Porpoise, gains are +0.048 (BLCA), +0.008 (BRCA), +0.022 (KIRP), and +0.036 (UCEC). The early-fusion Perceiver (concatenation) is the weakest multimodal baseline, with HEALNet leading by +0.103 on BLCA and +0.060 on BRCA. MCAT and MultiModN are consistently outperformed by 0.03–0.12 c-Index depending on the dataset. Notably, HEALNet also exhibits lower standard deviation across folds in 3 of 4 datasets, indicating more stable performance than the multimodal baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

HEALNet adapts the iterative cross-attention paradigm of the Perceiver [Jaegle et al., 2021] from unimodal to multimodal settings by introducing a shared latent array that is sequentially updated via modality-specific attention weights, rather than concatenating inputs or encoding modalities independently. It positions itself as 'hybrid early-fusion,' deliberately occupying a middle ground between simple early fusion (concatenation, which destroys structure) and intermediate/late fusion (separate encoders + gating, which prevents cross-modal learning). The key conceptual departure from co-attention approaches like MCAT and MOTCAT is that HEALNet treats all modalities symmetrically as mutual context for a shared latent, avoiding the primary/secondary modality asymmetry and the quadratic scaling of pairwise co-attention.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The hybrid early-fusion framing addresses two practical pain points in clinical multimodal modelling: graceful degradation when modalities are missing at inference (by simply skipping update steps rather than imputing noisy embeddings), and built-in interpretability through inspectable attention weights on raw inputs. Limitations include sensitivity to regularisation choices (L1 + SNN), evaluation restricted to survival analysis on four TCGA sites plus MIMIC-III, and reliance on a frozen ResNet-50 encoder for WSI patches rather than end-to-end image learning. Open questions include whether the approach scales to more than four modalities, whether it transfers to other clinical tasks (diagnosis, treatment response), and whether the modest c-Index gains translate to clinically meaningful decisions.
