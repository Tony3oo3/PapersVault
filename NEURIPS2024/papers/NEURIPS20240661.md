## Problem

Neural bandit algorithms using ReLU-based DNNs suffer from super-linear regret growth (Õ(T^{(d-1)/d+1/2})) due to polynomial eigenvalue decay of the ReLU NTK, making no-regret guarantees infeasible without complex algorithmic modifications. Tree ensembles, despite strong empirical performance on tabular data, remain largely unexplored in the stochastic bandit setting. This paper bridges that gap by extending the NTK-based analysis of neural bandits to soft tree ensembles, yielding a simple UCB algorithm with Õ(√T) regret.

## Input / Output

At each round t, the algorithm receives an action set X_t ⊂ S^{d-1} (context vectors on the unit sphere) and selects one action x_t via argmax of the predicted reward plus a confidence width β·σ̃_{t-1}(x). The confidence width is computed from the gradient of the initial soft tree model evaluated at x, weighted by the inverse of a regularized Gram matrix of past gradients. The model is retrained at each step via J steps of gradient descent on a regularized squared loss over all past observations, with the regularization anchoring parameters to the initial zero-output configuration.

## Method

ST-UCB replaces the ReLU DNN regressor in NN-UCB with an ensemble of M perfect binary soft trees of depth D, where each internal node applies a probabilistic split via a scaled error function σ(w_n^⊤x) = ½·erf(αw_n^⊤x)+½ rather than a hard threshold. The key theoretical insight is that the tree neural tangent kernel (TNTK) admits exponential eigenvalue decay λ_n ≤ C·exp(−nD·ln(1+1/(4α²))) via spherical harmonic decomposition, in contrast to the polynomial decay of the ReLU NTK. This yields logarithmic maximum information gain γ_T = O(ln^d T), which combined with a soft-tree-specific confidence bound (requiring new concentration and Hessian spectral norm lemmas) gives the Õ(√T) regret guarantee. The initialization pairs M/2 trees with standard normal parameters against M/2 trees with negated leaf parameters, ensuring the initial model output is identically zero, which is essential for the kernel-based analysis.

## Results

Theoretically, ST-UCB achieves a cumulative regret bound of Õ(√T) (i.e., O(√T · ln^d T)), which is sublinear and holds without additional assumptions on the action set. In contrast, NN-UCB with ReLU-based NTK yields a superlinear regret of Õ(T^((d-1)/d + 1/2)) in general action spaces. Empirically, on the Energy Efficiency dataset (K=60 arms), ST-UCB's cumulative regret plateaus after approximately round 150, while ε-greedy baselines continue to accumulate regret linearly. On synthetic reward functions f^(1) and f^(2), ST-UCB outperforms all other policies; on f^(3), ST-UCB and NN-UCB show comparable convergence stability.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is against NN-UCB [Zhou et al., ICML 2020], the standard neural contextual bandit algorithm using a ReLU-based DNN regressor. Theoretically, ST-UCB dominates NN-UCB: ST-UCB achieves Õ(√T) regret in general action spaces, while NN-UCB only achieves Õ(T^((d-1)/d + 1/2)) (superlinear) without additional structural assumptions, requiring more complex algorithms (sup-UCB or phased elimination) to recover sublinear regret. Empirically, on the Energy Efficiency dataset, ST-UCB initially shows higher regret in early rounds (due to aggressive exploration of unvisited arms) but its regret growth becomes much more gradual, eventually plateauing (e.g., no further regret increase after round 150 for K=60), whereas NN-UCB continues to accumulate regret more steadily. On synthetic tasks f^(1) and f^(2), ST-UCB clearly outperforms NN-UCB; on f^(3) (a non-smooth cosine reward), the two are comparable. The ε-greedy variants (ST-greedy, NN-greedy) serve as secondary baselines and show steadily increasing regret over long horizons due to their fixed exploration rate, confirming the advantage of UCB-style adaptive exploration.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the neural bandit (NB) framework of Zhou et al. (NN-UCB, ICML 2020) by replacing the ReLU-based neural tangent kernel (NTK) with the tree neural tangent kernel (TNTK) derived from soft tree ensembles (Kanoh & Sugiyama, ICLR 2021/2022). The key technical departure is that TNTK exhibits exponential eigenvalue decay (via spherical harmonics decomposition) rather than the polynomial decay of ReLU NTK, which the authors exploit to derive logarithmic maximum information gain bounds and a clean Õ(√T) regret guarantee using a standard UCB structure. The analysis also draws on kernel bandit machinery (Srinivas et al., ICML 2010) for confidence bounds and MIG, but requires new soft-tree-specific lemmas for TNTK concentration and Hessian spectral norm bounds that have no direct ReLU-NTK analogues.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result is notable because it shows that a structurally simpler and less expressive model (soft trees with a strictly smaller RKHS than ReLU networks) can yield a no-regret guarantee with a straightforward UCB algorithm, whereas NN-UCB requires complex sup-UCB or phased-elimination variants to achieve any meaningful bound in general action spaces. The trade-off is explicit: the TNTK hypothesis space is a proper subset of the ReLU NTK space, so the guarantee applies to a narrower class of reward functions. Open questions include whether the result extends to hard trees (α → ∞), to greedy tree-training procedures more common in practice, and whether a less smooth soft-decision function could align the TNTK and NTK hypothesis spaces while preserving the regret bound.
