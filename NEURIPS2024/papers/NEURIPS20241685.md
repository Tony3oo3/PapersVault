## Problem

The AI community has focused predominantly on novel model architectures for virtual screening while neglecting standardized, high-quality benchmarking practices. Existing benchmarks like MoleculeNet and TDC suffer from inconsistent chemical representations, undefined stereochemistry, and noisy experimental data, making reliable model comparison and real-world translation difficult. WelQrate addresses this gap by establishing a gold-standard benchmark with rigorously curated datasets and a unified evaluation protocol.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241685/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Models consume molecular representations in multiple standardized formats: isomeric SMILES, InChI (1S/ prefix), 2D graphs (bond-connectivity edges with pre-defined edge attributes), 3D graphs (edges within 6 Å Euclidean distance, with coordinate node attributes), and BCL molecular descriptors. The 3D conformations are pre-generated by Corina v5.0 at low energy. Output is a binary active/inactive prediction scored with four ranking-focused metrics (logAUC_[0.001,0.1], BEDROC, EF100, DCG100) rather than standard AUC, reflecting the real-world scenario where only top-ranked compounds are pursued. Datasets are highly imbalanced, with active fractions ranging from 0.039% to 0.682%.

## Method

WelQrate contributes a 9-dataset collection spanning 5 therapeutic target classes (GPCR, ion channel, transporter, kinase, enzyme), each curated through a hierarchical pipeline of primary, confirmatory, and counter screens sourced from PubChem bioassays, followed by PAINS filtering, Lipinski druglikeness filtering, neutralization, aromatization, and expert verification. The evaluation framework standardizes featurization (28-dim node features, pre-defined edge attributes), 3D conformation generation, and two split schemes: an adapted 5-fold cross-validation for random splits (selecting a single inner fold to limit model count while ensuring full test coverage) and a Bemis-Murcko scaffold split at 3:1:1 ratio with >10% scaffold bins assigned to training. Benchmarking experiments across four research questions evaluate 11 models (sequence-based, 2D/3D GNNs, and two baselines) under varying dataset quality, featurization, and split strategies, revealing that domain-expert descriptors (BCL + MLP) often outperform complex GNNs, especially under scaffold splitting where distribution shift exposes overfitting in deeper architectures.

## Results

The paper presents its main benchmarking results as bar charts (Figs. 4–6) rather than numerical tables, comparing ten models across four virtual-screening metrics (logAUC_[0.001,0.1], BEDROC, EF100, DCG100) on nine WelQrate datasets. The headline finding is that the Domain Baseline (BCL molecular descriptors + MLP) achieves the best performance on three of four metrics (logAUC, BEDROC, EF100), outperforming all deep learning architectures despite its simple architecture. Among deep learning models, performance generally increases with representational complexity (Sequence → 2D Graph → 3D Graph), with SphereNet and DimeNet++ leading the 3D category. Under scaffold split (RQ4), all models degrade, and the advantage of complex 2D/3D GNNs largely vanishes, while the Domain Baseline remains the most robust.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The Domain Baseline (BCL descriptors + MLP, ref [39]) is the standout performer, beating all nine deep learning models on logAUC, BEDROC, and EF100, and trailing only slightly on DCG100. Among the deep learning baselines, the 3D Graph models (SchNet [36], DimeNet++ [37], SphereNet [38]) consistently outperform the 2D Graph models (GCN [33], GIN [34], GAT [35]), which in turn outperform the Sequence models (SMILES2Vec [31], TextCNN [32]) and the Naive Baseline. Under the scaffold split (RQ4), the performance gap between 2D/3D GNNs and simpler models narrows substantially, suggesting overfitting to scaffold patterns in random splits. In the dataset-quality experiment (RQ2), the Naive Baseline and Sequence models suffer significant degradation when trained on the uncurated Control dataset, while 2D/3D Graph models show mixed effects (better on logAUC/BEDROC but worse on EF100/DCG100). The featurization experiment (RQ3) confirms that WelQrate's predefined 28-dim atom features outperform standard one-hot encoding for all graph-based models, with the Naive Baseline being the only exception where performance remains stable.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the PubChem bioassay curation methodology established by Butkiewicz et al. [9, 13], extending their hierarchical primary/confirmatory/counter screen framework with additional domain-driven preprocessing (PAINS filtering, neutralization, aromatization, druglikeness checks) and expert verification. It positions itself as a corrective to existing benchmarks MoleculeNet and TDC, which the authors argue suffer from inconsistent chemical representations, undefined stereochemistry, and noisy labels. The evaluation framework standardizes aspects (featurization, 3D conformation via Corina, scaffold-based splits, ranking-focused metrics) that prior benchmarks left to individual researchers, creating a common protocol for comparing sequence-based, 2D graph, and 3D graph models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a critical gap: the AI community has been iterating on model architectures without a reliable, realistic evaluation substrate, slowing translation into actual drug discovery pipelines. The finding that a simple MLP over BCL molecular descriptors outperforms most deep learning architectures (except on DCG100) and that the advantage of complex 2D/3D GNNs vanishes under scaffold split challenges the field's implicit assumption that architectural complexity is the primary driver of performance. Open questions include how to handle the extreme class imbalance inherent in realistic HTS data, whether models can be made robust to the distribution shift of scaffold hopping, and whether the framework can be extended to binding conformations and ADMET properties.
