## Problem

Interactive 3D segmentation for 3D Gaussian Splatting scenes has been limited to methods that augment each Gaussian with additional semantic features during optimization, increasing fitting time and memory footprint. This paper addresses the challenge of segmenting objects from an already-optimized 3DGS model without any modification to the underlying representation or retraining, enabling post-hoc, interactive object selection.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242829/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts an optimized 3DGS model (30,000-step training, images resized to 1008px) and sparse user input—point clicks, coarse scribbles, or text queries—on a single arbitrary viewpoint. It produces a binary partition of all Gaussians into foreground and background sets, which can be rendered from any viewpoint in real time. The pipeline internally uses SAM-Track to propagate the single-view input into dense multi-view masks (up to 30 views for front-facing, 40 for 360° scenes), but can also operate with a single mask or raw scribbles alone.

## Method

GaussianCut first uses inverse rendering with multi-view segmentation masks to compute a per-Gaussian foreground likelihood weight w_g, representing the ratio of each Gaussian's contribution to masked versus total pixels. It then constructs an undirected weighted graph where each Gaussian is a node and edges connect k=10 spatial nearest neighbors, with edge weights combining spatial distance and zero-degree spherical harmonic color similarity via an exponential kernel. The energy function (Eq. 3) has unary t-links that combine w_g with a cluster-similarity term to high-confidence foreground/background nodes, and pairwise n-links encoding neighbor correlation. Minimizing this energy via the Boykov-Kolmogorov max-flow algorithm partitions the graph into foreground and background sets, refining the noisy 2D-mask-derived estimates into a 3D-consistent segmentation without any additional training.

## Results

On the NVOS benchmark (7 LLFF scenes), GaussianCut achieves 92.5% IoU and 98.4% pixel accuracy, outperforming all baselines including SAGA (90.9% IoU) and Gaussian Grouping (90.6% IoU). On the SPIn-NeRF dataset (10 scenes including 360° inward-facing), GaussianCut reaches 92.9% IoU and 99.2% Acc, surpassing SA3D (92.4% IoU) and MVSeg (90.4% IoU). For object rendering quality against a black background, GaussianCut achieves 22.45 dB PSNR and 0.132 LPIPS, a +4.05 dB PSNR improvement over NVOS and +1.69 dB over SA3D. On the Shiny dataset, GaussianCut attains 95.0% IoU, and on 3D-OVS with text queries it averages 95.4% IoU across scenes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GaussianCut outperforms all 10 baselines on the primary NVOS benchmark, with the closest competitors being SAGA (90.9% IoU, −1.6%) and Gaussian Grouping (90.6% IoU, −1.9%), both of which require modifying the 3DGS optimization to learn per-Gaussian features. The gap is much larger against NeRF-based methods: SA3D (90.3% IoU), ISRF (83.8%), and NVOS (70.1%). On SPIn-NeRF, GaussianCut (92.9% IoU) edges out SA3D (92.4%) and MVSeg (90.4%), with particularly large gains on 360° scenes (e.g., +14.3% IoU over MVSeg on Lego). On the Shiny dataset, GaussianCut (95.0% IoU) beats SA3D (93.3%) and SAGD (83.3%), and also improves over its own Coarse Splatting baseline (94.3%) by +0.7%. On 3D-OVS with text queries, GaussianCut (95.4% avg IoU) matches or exceeds CGC (95.2%) and substantially outperforms LangSplat (34.3–95.8% depending on scene), though Gaussian Grouping remains competitive on some individual objects (e.g., 97.3% avg on Bed vs. 96.8% for GaussianCut). A key practical advantage is that GaussianCut requires no changes to the 3DGS fitting process, saving ~900–1600 seconds of optimization time compared to feature-based methods like SAGA and Gaussian Grouping.

## Relation to Prior Work

GaussianCut sits at the intersection of two well-studied lines: interactive graph-cut segmentation (Boykov & Jolly, GrabCut) and 3D Gaussian Splatting scene representation. It departs from feature-based 3DGS segmentation methods (SAGA, Gaussian Grouping, LangSplat) that require augmenting each Gaussian with a learned feature and re-optimizing the scene, instead operating as a purely post-hoc partitioning on an already-trained 3DGS model. The graph construction is specifically tailored to 3DGS properties—using 3D position, zero-degree spherical harmonic color, and per-Gaussian opacity/transmittance from inverse rendering—rather than generic spatial or learned-feature edges. The method also builds on the 2D-to-3D mask propagation paradigm (SAM-Track for multiview masks) but replaces the typical thresholding step with an energy-minimizing graph cut that fuses user-input likelihoods with scene-structure priors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical value is that GaussianCut enables interactive object extraction from existing 3DGS reconstructions without any retraining, additional per-Gaussian parameters, or changes to the optimization pipeline—making it a drop-in tool for 3D editing workflows. Key limitations include non-real-time inference (graph cut takes ~90s), dependence on the quality of 2D/video segmentation models (especially for 360° scenes where tracking degrades), and boundary artifacts inherited from the unconstrained Gaussian representation. Open questions include whether the energy function can be extended with learned feature-similarity terms, whether graph cut can be applied to a subset of Gaussians for speedup, and how the method generalizes to scenes with highly overlapping or transparent objects.
