## Problem

Existing satellite road-extraction datasets provide only coarse semantic-level labels at relatively low resolution (≤0.3 m/pixel), cover fewer than 10k images, and are not aligned with autonomous driving benchmarks, making them inadequate for fine-grained, large-scale map construction. OpenSatMap addresses these gaps by introducing instance-level vectorized annotations, level-20 imagery (0.15 m/pixel), 38k images across 60 cities in 19 countries, and explicit GPS alignment with nuScenes and Argoverse 2.

## Input / Output

The primary task takes a single high-resolution satellite image (4096×4096 at level 20 or 2048×2048 at level 19) and outputs a set of vectorized polyline instances, each annotated with 3 categories (curb, lane line, virtual line) and 8 fine-grained attributes (color, line type, number of lines, function, bidirection, boundary, occlusion, clearness). For the autonomous driving application, the input is a camera image augmented with a 60 m × 30 m satellite tile, and the output is a semantic map segmentation (divider, crossing, boundary) evaluated by IoU on nuScenes.

## Method

The paper's core contribution is the dataset and its annotation protocol, but it also provides a three-stage baseline for instance-level line detection: (1) semantic segmentation with SegNeXt, (2) instance separation via the watershed algorithm on per-category binary masks, and (3) instance vectorization through a sample-then-reconstruct denoising pipeline followed by uniform point sampling to produce polylines. For evaluation, the authors introduce Chamfer-distance-based AP (AP^C_D) as a complement to mask-IoU-based AP, motivated by the fact that narrow line markings make mask IoU thresholds excessively strict. For the autonomous driving track, they extend SatforHDMap by adding a satellite-image mask as an extra input branch, demonstrating that corresponding satellite imagery significantly boosts online HD map construction IoU on nuScenes (from 32.9 to 50.9 overall).

## Results

On the new OpenSatMap benchmark, the proposed baseline (SegNeXt + watershed + vectorization) achieves mIoU of 28.71±0.38 and 33.69±0.45 on OpenSatMap19 and OpenSatMap20 respectively, while instance-level AP_50:95^M is only 3.66±0.15 and 6.98±0.21, highlighting the substantial difficulty of instance-level line detection versus semantic segmentation. For the autonomous driving application, SatforHDMap augmented with OpenSatMap satellite imagery achieves 50.9 IoU (All) on the nuScenes validation set, compared to 32.9 for HDMapNet without satellite input.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares SatforHDMap (using OpenSatMap satellite imagery as additional input) against HDMapNet on the nuScenes validation set for semantic map segmentation. SatforHDMap with OpenSatMap wins decisively across all map element categories: Divider IoU improves from 40.6 to 50.2 (+9.6), Crossing IoU from 18.7 to 53.2 (+34.5), Boundary IoU from 39.5 to 49.4 (+9.9), and overall IoU from 32.9 to 50.9 (+18.0). The largest gain is on Crossing elements, suggesting that satellite imagery is particularly informative for detecting crosswalks and intersections that are difficult to perceive from onboard cameras alone. No other baselines are directly compared on the instance-level line detection task, as OpenSatMap introduces a new benchmark; the reported numbers serve as initial reference points rather than a competitive comparison.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of satellite road extraction datasets (Massachusetts, DeepGlobe, Roadtracer, SpaceNet, Ottawa, CHN6-CUG) by moving from coarse semantic-level segmentation to fine-grained instance-level annotations with 8 attributes, higher resolution (level-20, 0.15 m/pixel vs. prior max of 0.3 m/pixel), and substantially larger scale (~38k images vs. <10k). For the autonomous driving application, it builds directly on SatforHDMap [23], which demonstrated that satellite imagery can serve as an additional input modality for online HD map construction, and on the HDMapNet [34] evaluation framework. The baseline method itself is a straightforward pipeline (SegNeXt segmentation + watershed instance detection + polyline vectorization) rather than a novel architecture, positioning the contribution squarely as a dataset and benchmark.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

OpenSatMap fills a critical gap by providing the first large-scale, high-resolution, instance-level satellite dataset aligned with mainstream autonomous driving benchmarks (nuScenes, Argoverse 2), enabling satellite-enhanced map construction research at scale. The notably low baseline AP scores (≈20–30 Chamfer AP, ≈7–16 Mask AP) despite visually reasonable qualitative results highlight that instance-level line detection from satellite imagery remains an open and substantially harder problem than semantic segmentation, motivating future end-to-end methods. Key limitations include potential temporal misalignment between satellite captures and driving data, annotation subjectivity across ~60 annotators, and restricted availability of true level-20 imagery in some regions, all of which constrain the dataset's applicability to real-time or safety-critical deployment.
