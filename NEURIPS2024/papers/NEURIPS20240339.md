## Problem

Deep state space models (SSMs) lack any method to optimize their state dimension, forcing practitioners to either over-parameterize (wasting compute and memory) or restrict the search space and stability guarantees to keep dimensions manageable. No structured pruning criterion exists that can identify insignificant states across multiple stacked SSM layers with nonlinearity, making it impossible to compress trained SSMs without retraining.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240339/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes the trained continuous-time diagonal SSM parameters (Λ, B, C, D) from any layer of a deep SSM (S4D multi-SISO or S5 MIMO) along with a target model-level compression rate R. It produces per-state LAST importance scores computed in closed form from the Hurwitz-parameterized poles and I/O matrices, then outputs a pruned SSM where all parameters (λ_i, B_i row, C_i column) associated with the lowest-scoring states are removed. No retraining is required; the pruned model is evaluated directly, and the method handles bidirectional SSMs by averaging the forward and backward C norms.

## Method

LAST extends modal truncation from a single LTI system to a deep multi-layer SSM with GELU nonlinearity by deriving an H-infinity-norm-based upper bound on the model-level output energy loss caused by pruning. For a diagonal subsystem, the H-infinity score has a closed form: ‖C_i‖²‖B̄_i‖²/(1−|λ̄_i|)², which captures both the I/O gain and the pole's proximity to the unit circle. The key innovation is energy normalization: each state's score is divided by the cumulative sum of scores of all states with equal or higher H-infinity norms in the same layer, converting local per-layer scores into a globally comparable metric that accounts for layer-wise signal amplification differences. This enables a single global ranking across all states in all layers, so that a target compression rate R is met by pruning the lowest-scoring states regardless of which layer they belong to, yielding layer-adaptive pruning ratios without any per-layer heuristic. The derivation leverages the 1-Lipschitzness of GELU, Parseval's theorem, and the diagonal structure to bound the multi-layer distortion, showing that the cross-layer comparison reduces to the ratio of a subsystem's H-infinity norm to its layer's remaining-system H-infinity norm.

## Results

LAST achieves an average 33% state pruning on S5 (MIMO) models with only 0.52% accuracy loss (0.58% excluding non-compressible cases) across 10 sequence tasks, without any retraining. On S4D (multi-SISO) models, LAST achieves 25% average pruning with 0.32% accuracy loss. On the LRA benchmark specifically, LAST maintains 86.53% average accuracy on S5 at 40% average compression (vs. 87.09% full model), and 80.77% on S4D at 33.3% compression (vs. 81.05% full model). Pruning also yields practical efficiency gains, e.g., 1.6× inference speedup and 0.9× GPU memory reduction on the Text task.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LAST is compared against two baselines: Uniform H∞ (local per-layer pruning with equal ratios) and Global H∞ (global H∞ scores without energy normalization, i.e., LAST ablated). On S5 models, LAST dramatically outperforms both: 0.52% average accuracy loss vs. 4.32% (Uniform) and 7.51% (Global) at the same 33% pruning ratio. The gap is most extreme on Pathfinder and Path-X, where at 30% pruning LAST retains 94.45% and 97.95% accuracy respectively, while Uniform H∞ drops to 71.38% and 90.90%, and Global H∞ collapses to 57.20% and 69.21%. On S4D models, LAST also wins but with a smaller margin (0.32% vs. 0.39% Uniform, 1.16% Global), consistent with the paper's observation that multi-SISO states are less compressible due to channel-specific role assignment. The energy normalization step (the key difference between LAST and Global H∞) is critical: without it, layers with low overall H∞ norm scale are over-pruned, causing the large accuracy drops seen in Global H∞.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends two distinct research lineages to the setting of deep diagonal state space models. From linear system theory, it generalizes modal truncation (Green & Limebeer, 2012), which reduces a single LTI system by removing states with the smallest H-infinity norms, to a multi-layer setting with nonlinear activations and cross-layer comparison. From neural network pruning, it adapts the layer-adaptive global pruning framework of Lee et al. (2021) and Xu et al. (2023), but replaces magnitude-based importance scores with H-infinity norm-based scores that account for the unique frequency-domain transfer functions of SSMs. The key conceptual departure is the energy normalization step, which rescales per-layer H-infinity scores by the cumulative layer gain to enable meaningful cross-layer state comparison—a step that has no direct analogue in either the classical MOR or standard NN pruning literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for SSM deployment: pruning 33% of states in MIMO models (S5) with only 0.52% average accuracy loss and no retraining directly reduces inference cost and memory footprint, as confirmed by measured speedups (up to 1.7×) and memory savings (up to 0.6×). The observation that pruned states tend to have large |Re{λ}| (fast-decaying) or large |Im{λ}| (high-frequency) poles provides actionable insight for future training strategies, such as constraining pole placement to avoid generating redundant dynamics. Open questions remain about optimal pruning schedules during training (when and how often to prune), generalization beyond the tested task set, and whether the method extends to non-diagonal or selective SSMs like Mamba.
