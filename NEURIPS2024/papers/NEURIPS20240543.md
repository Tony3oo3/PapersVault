## Problem

Existing off-policy evaluation (OPE) methods face a fundamental trade-off: importance sampling estimators are consistent but suffer from variance that grows exponentially with horizon, while model-based estimators have lower variance but introduce irreducible bias from model class mismatch and lack consistency guarantees. The paper asks whether a framework can be constructed that yields OPE predictions that are simultaneously consistent (asymptotically correct) and low variance, without requiring model class assumptions even for continuous-state MDPs.

## Input / Output

The method consumes logged trajectories generated by a behavior policy π_b, along with the evaluation policy π_e, the behavior policy π_b (for computing importance weights), and a state abstraction function φ that partitions the (potentially continuous) state space into a finite set of discrete abstract states. It produces a tabular abstract reward process (ARP) model—comprising a transition matrix, reward vector, and initial state distribution over abstract states—from which the expected return J(π_e) is computed via a linear solver. Notably, the state abstraction can be as coarse as a single abstract state (recovering WPDIS) or as fine as the full state space (recovering an MRP-based estimator), and the importance weights are embedded within the model estimation step rather than applied to a final prediction.

## Method

STAR constructs a finite tabular model called an abstract reward process (ARP) over discrete abstract states produced by a state abstraction function φ, then estimates this model from off-policy data via weighted maximum likelihood estimation, where importance weights ρ_{0:t} are incorporated directly into the numerator and denominator of the transition and reward function estimates. The key theoretical insight is that mapping to a finite discrete state space eliminates model class mismatch (tabular models represent any distribution over discrete variables), and the ARP is provably performance-preserving (Theorem 3.1), so the weighted MLE of the ARP yields a consistent estimate of J(π_e) (Theorem 4.1). A second tunable parameter, the weight clipping factor c, limits importance weights to the c most recent action ratios; in this framework, clipping has a natural interpretation as assuming the c most recent abstract states suffice to predict the current transition, which introduces no asymptotic bias when φ is c-th order Markov (Theorem 4.3). The two knobs (φ, c) instantiate a continuum of estimators where existing methods (WPDIS, MRP-based) appear as special cases at the endpoints, and intermediate configurations inherit favorable properties of both. For practical abstraction, the paper uses CluSTAR, a simple k-means clustering of the state space, which already yields competitive performance.

## Results

Across three domains (CartPole, ICU-Sepsis, Asterix) and four data sizes each (12 total cases), the best STAR estimator achieves the lowest mean squared prediction error (MSPE) in all 12 cases, while the median STAR estimator outperforms all baselines in 7 out of 12 cases. In the clinically critical ICU-Sepsis domain, the best STAR estimator achieves prediction errors an order of magnitude lower than the best baseline. The best-performing ARPs use compact abstractions: 32 abstract states for CartPole (horizon 50), 16 for ICU-Sepsis (horizon 120), and 8 for Asterix (horizon 75). All results are averaged over 200 trials.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against nine baselines spanning three OPE categories. Among IS-based methods (Vanilla IS, Per-Decision IS, Weighted IS, WPDIS [Precup 2000]), the best STAR estimator dominates all variants across every domain and data size, with WPDIS representing one theoretical endpoint of the STAR framework. Among model-based methods, MBased [Sutton & Barto 2018] (the other STAR endpoint) and Q-value estimators FQE [Le et al. 2019] and MRDR [Farajtabar et al. 2018] are all outperformed by the best STAR estimator in all 12 cases; notably, MRDR and FQE were computationally intractable on ICU-Sepsis due to its 747-state space. Among mixture methods, both DR [Jiang & Li 2016] and MAGIC [Thomas & Brunskill 2016] are consistently outperformed. The median STAR estimator (averaged over 35 configurations of abstraction size and weight clipping) still beats all baselines in 7/12 cases, suggesting that even without careful hyperparameter tuning, STAR estimators are competitive with or superior to existing methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

STAR departs from the standard OPE taxonomy (IS-based, model-based, and mixture methods) by merging the *machinery* of importance sampling and model-based estimation rather than merely combining their predictions. It builds on the state abstraction framework for MDPs (Li et al., 2006) to define finite abstract reward processes that eliminate model class mismatch, and on weighted maximum likelihood estimation (Field & Smith, 1994) to consistently estimate these ARPs from off-policy data using importance weights. The paper positions existing estimators—WPDIS (Precup, 2000) and the approximate model estimator (Sutton & Barto, 2018)—as special cases at the endpoints of the STAR framework, with intermediate configurations of (abstraction, clipping) yielding a continuum of new estimators.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

STAR provides the first model-based OPE method with a consistency guarantee that requires no model class assumptions, even for continuous-state MDPs, which is a meaningful theoretical advance for high-stakes applications like healthcare where biased estimates are unacceptable. Two key open questions limit immediate adoption: (1) a principled, automated method for discovering good abstraction functions remains elusive (the paper uses naive k-means clustering), and (2) the framework requires known behavior-policy action probabilities, which are often unavailable in practice. The unification of IS and model-based methods into a single estimation procedure also reframes weight clipping as a Markov-order assumption on the abstraction rather than a crude variance hack, opening a new research direction in abstraction discovery for OPE.
