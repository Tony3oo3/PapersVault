## Problem

Automated data quality control for LLM fine-tuning becomes significantly harder in collaborative settings (model merging, federated learning) where private data cannot be shared across silos. Two compounding challenges arise: quality heterogeneity (clients have unknown, varying proportions of low-quality data) and domain heterogeneity (different vertical domains have non-unified quality standards). Existing data selection methods (perplexity, IFD, DataInf) were designed for centralized training and lack a principled way to set a unified selection threshold across heterogeneous private domains.

## Input / Output

The method consumes per-client private QA training sets (medical, multilingual, financial), a tiny public anchor set of only 10 high-quality samples, and a shared pre-trained LLM backbone (LLaMA2-7B or Mistral-7B) with LoRA adapters (rank 16). It produces per-sample scalar quality scores computed from gradient inner products at multiple training checkpoints, a single global threshold broadcast to all clients, and a final merged LoRA adapter. A key constraint is that scoring operates only on first-layer LoRA gradients (not full model gradients) to keep memory tractable for 7B-parameter models, and the entire pipeline requires no raw data to leave any client.

## Method

CLUES traces training dynamics to score each sample: during a local fine-tuning pass, it saves model checkpoints and computes per-sample gradients with respect to both the private training data and a public validation set, then scores each sample z as the sum over validation samples and checkpoints of the learning-rate-weighted inner product of their AdamW-adjusted gradients (S(z) = Σ_{z'} Σ_t η̄_t · L(z',θ_t)·L(z,θ_t)). The server computes the same score for 10 anchor samples and uses their average as a global threshold, which clients apply to filter their local data before a second collaborative training phase (TIES-merging or federated averaging of LoRA adapters). The approach is framed as a bi-level optimization—inner loop on clients minimizing local loss, outer loop on the server minimizing anchor-set loss—and is scalable because it exploits the sparse low-rank structure of LoRA gradients and requires no repeated full training runs.

## Results

On the Medical QA task (federated setting, 20 clients), CLUES achieves 0.161 GPT-4 scoring with Mistral-7B (100.6% of the Oracle upper bound of 0.160) and 0.210 with Llama2-7B (212.1% of Oracle at 0.099), substantially outperforming all baselines. On the Knowledge Avg metric, CLUES scores 0.309 (Mistral) and 0.356 (Llama2), remaining within acceptable range of the Oracle (0.233 and 0.440 respectively). On MMedBench (multilingual QA), CLUES reaches 96.5–106.2% of Oracle across federated and model-merging settings for both models. On FiQA (financial QA with heterogeneous quality across 4 clients), CLUES achieves 97.44% precision, 99.38% recall, and 98.39% F1 for data selection, versus 79.17% for ratio-based selection.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CLUES decisively outperforms both IFD [24] and DataInf [21] on the primary GPT-4 scoring metric across both model backbones: on Mistral-7B, CLUES scores 0.161 versus IFD's 0.087 and DataInf's 0.093; on Llama2-7B, CLUES scores 0.210 versus IFD's 0.050 and DataInf's 0.103. On the Knowledge Avg metric (measuring retention of pre-trained knowledge), IFD is competitive with CLUES (0.287 vs. 0.309 on Mistral; 0.346 vs. 0.356 on Llama2), while DataInf suffers severe knowledge forgetting on Mistral (0.106 vs. 0.309) though it remains close on Llama2 (0.335 vs. 0.356). The perplexity (PPL) baseline, while not formally listed in the comparison set, performs worst on GPT-4 scoring (0.079 Mistral, 0.045 Llama2), confirming that simple perplexity filtering is inadequate for collaborative quality control. Overall, CLUES is the only method that simultaneously approaches or exceeds the Oracle upper bound on GPT-4 scoring while maintaining acceptable knowledge retention, whereas the baselines trade off one metric for the other.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends centralized data-selection methods for LLMs (Perplexity scoring, IFD, DataInf) into a collaborative private-domain setting where data cannot be shared across silos. It repurposes the training-dynamics influence scoring idea from dataset cartography (Swayamdipta et al.) and influence-function literature, but introduces a global threshold derived from a small public anchor set to unify quality standards across heterogeneous clients. On the aggregation side, it integrates with existing model-merging techniques (Task Arithmetic, TIES) and federated averaging, positioning data selection as a server-side modification rather than a new training paradigm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first data-selection pipeline explicitly designed for collaborative LLM fine-tuning, addressing quality heterogeneity and domain heterogeneity that centralized methods cannot handle. The practical implication is that private LLM vendors can improve fine-tuning quality without sharing raw data, which is critical for GDPR-compliant medical, financial, and multilingual applications. Key limitations include the assumption of identical model architectures across clients, restriction to 7B-scale models, and the absence of continual-learning or dynamic-client scenarios. Open questions include whether the gradient-influence scoring scales to 70B+ models and whether the anchor-data threshold remains robust under extreme domain shifts.
