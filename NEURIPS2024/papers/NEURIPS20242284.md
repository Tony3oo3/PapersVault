## Problem

Prior approaches to training assistive agents either require inferring the human's reward function (as in inverse RL), which is fragile and difficult to scale, or use empowerment objectives based on random rollouts that fail in high-dimensional settings. This paper asks whether an assistive agent can be trained to maximize the human's influence over future outcomes without modeling their reward, using a scalable model-free objective that works in image-based environments.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242284/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes joint robot-human states (grid coordinates or RGB images from Overcooked) with one-hot encoded human and robot actions concatenated as additional input channels. It produces an assistive agent policy trained with SAC and a scalar empowerment reward computed as the inner-product difference between two learned contrastive representations. The representations are trained to encode log-ratios of conditional versus marginal future-state distributions, effectively learning a successor representation of the states reachable under the human's current action.

## Method

ESR learns three contrastive representations—φ(s, a^R, a^H) for the controlled future, φ'(s, a^R) for the uncontrolled future, and ψ(s⁺) for the future state—using a symmetrized infoNCE loss. The key insight is that the difference φ − φ' encodes the log probability ratio P(s⁺|s, a^H)/P(s⁺|s), whose expectation equals the conditional mutual information I(a^H; s⁺|s), i.e., the empowerment of the human's action. This mutual information estimate serves as a reward signal for SAC to train the assistive agent policy, alternating between contrastive representation updates and policy optimization. The paper further proves (Theorem 3.1) that under uniform reward coverage and ergodicity, maximizing effective empowerment yields a lower bound on the human's average-case reward for sufficiently long horizons, providing the first formal link between empowerment maximization and reward maximization.

## Results

On the Overcooked benchmark, ESR achieves 91.33±4.08 reward on Cramped Room, 72.00±5.37 on Asymmetric Advantages, and 8.40±0.69 on Coordination Ring, outperforming all baselines on every layout. On the Obstacle Gridworld, ESR and AvE achieve similar asymptotic reward on the easiest task (N=2 obstacles), but ESR significantly outperforms AvE on harder variants (N=5, 7, 10), where AvE degrades below random performance. The Cramped Room result is particularly striking: ESR nearly doubles the Reward Inference baseline (91.33 vs. 39.24) and outperforms AvE by over 17×.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ESR outperforms AvE [6] on all three Overcooked layouts, with the largest margin on Cramped Room (91.33 vs. 5.13, a ~17× improvement) and Asymmetric Advantages (72.00 vs. 36.71). On the Obstacle Gridworld, AvE matches ESR on the simplest task but collapses below random performance as the number of obstacles increases to 5, 7, or 10, while ESR continues to improve. ESR also beats the Reward Inference baseline (IQ-Learn [59]) on every Overcooked layout: 72.00 vs. 60.33 (Asymmetric Advantages), 8.40 vs. 5.96 (Coordination Ring), and 91.33 vs. 39.24 (Cramped Room). On the gridworld, Reward Inference also fails on the harder variants, confirming that both prior approaches struggle with scalability while ESR's contrastive successor representations handle the increased state-space complexity gracefully.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the empowerment-based assistance framework of Du et al. (AvE, NeurIPS 2020), which approximated human empowerment via variance of random rollouts, by replacing that mechanism with contrastive successor representations trained via symmetrized infoNCE (van den Oord et al., 2018). The mutual information formulation of empowerment follows Salge et al. (2014) and Klyubin et al. (2005), but the paper introduces 'effective empowerment'—computed under the actual behavior policies rather than an optimal policy—which simplifies estimation and enables a model-free, scalable objective. The theoretical contribution (Theorem 3.1) draws on information geometry and the data processing inequality to provide the first formal lower bound linking empowerment maximization to average-case reward under Boltzmann-rational humans, connecting ideas from information theory, neuroscience (successor representations), and RL.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical path toward assistive agents that do not require inferring human reward functions, addressing a key safety concern in AI alignment where misspecified rewards can lead to harmful behavior. The scalability to image-based Overcooked environments is a meaningful step toward real-world deployment, though the method remains on-policy, requires direct access to human actions, and has not been validated with real human users. Open questions include whether the empowerment objective can be extended to partial observability, off-policy settings, and multi-human scenarios, and how to handle the tension between maximizing empowerment and respecting a human's specific (possibly narrow) goals.
