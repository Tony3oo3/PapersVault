## Problem

Existing harmful meme detection research is almost entirely focused on English memes, leaving Chinese harmful memes largely unexplored due to the absence of reliable datasets and effective detectors. Chinese memes present unique challenges: they include not only targeted harmful content but also general offense, sexual innuendo, and dispirited culture without specific targets, and their semantics often rely on Chinese-specific linguistic phenomena (homophones, slang, cultural idioms) that standard models fail to capture.

## Input / Output

The method takes a multimodal meme as input, consisting of an RGB image and Chinese inline text (extracted via Baidu-OCR with manual review for accuracy). It produces two progressive outputs: a binary harmful/benign label, and for harmful samples, a four-class harmful type label (targeted harmful, general offense, sexual innuendo, dispirited culture). The TOXICN MM dataset (12,000 samples from Weibo and Baidu Tieba) additionally annotates modality combination features (text-image fusion, harmful text only, harmful image only) to characterize how toxicity is expressed across modalities.

## Method

The MKE detector integrates LLM-generated contextual knowledge into a trainable multimodal backbone (CLIP or RoBERTa+ViT fusion). In a knowledge mining phase with frozen LLM parameters, GPT-4 generates enhanced captions for both the inline text (using a Chinese-linguistics-aware prompt) and the image (using a Chinese-culture-aware prompt via a multimodal LLM). These captions are encoded into the text vector space as K_s and K_v. In the knowledge integration phase, a cross-attention mechanism fuses the original text feature S with both caption features (S_Ks and S_Kv), and the resulting enhanced text representation is averaged with S to form S_K. This is concatenated with the original image feature V to produce the final meme representation, which is classified by a linear layer with softmax. The key insight is that LLM-generated captions provide the cultural and linguistic context (e.g., recognizing 'vegetable dog' as an insult or 'shy things' as sexual innuendo) that the trainable detector alone cannot infer.

## Results

On TOXICN MM, CLIP+MKE achieves the best harmful meme detection F1 of 80.23% and the best harmful type identification macro-F1 of 61.47%. MKE provides an average macro-F1 improvement of 0.73% over the Fusion (RoBERTa+ViT) backbone and 3.22% over the CLIP backbone across both tasks. Text-only RoBERTa (F1=76.36%) substantially outperforms image-only models (ResNet F1=66.76%, ViT F1=68.78%), confirming that inline text is the dominant signal for Chinese harmful meme detection. GPT-4 in zero-shot multimodal mode reaches F1=70.11% for detection and 43.89% for type identification, trailing fine-tuned models by a wide margin.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CLIP+MKE outperforms all baselines on both tasks. For harmful meme detection, it beats the strongest fine-tuned baseline CLIP by 0.69% F1 (80.23 vs. 79.54) and the Fusion backbone by 1.84% (80.23 vs. 78.39). Against zero-shot LLMs, the gap is much larger: GPT-4 multimodal achieves only 70.11% F1, a 10.12% deficit, while GPT-3.5 text-only reaches 67.46%. For harmful type identification, CLIP+MKE (61.47% macro-F1) surpasses CLIP by 3.62% and GPT-4 multimodal by 17.58%, indicating that LLMs struggle significantly with fine-grained Chinese harmful type discrimination. Ablation within MKE shows that both text captions (Ks) and image captions (Kv) contribute, with image captions providing a larger boost for targeted harmful memes (+2.07%) while text captions help more for dispirited culture memes (+2.58%), aligning with the modality combination distribution in the dataset.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the English harmful meme detection literature (HMC, MMHS, Harm-C/P) to the Chinese setting and broadens the task definition beyond targeted harm to include general offense, sexual innuendo, and dispirited culture without specific targets. The MKE detector repurposes the caption-enrichment idea from prior multimodal meme detection work (Blaier et al., 2021; Cao et al., 2022) but generalizes it by using LLMs to generate contextual captions for both text and image modalities, then integrating them via cross-attention. Conceptually, the paper positions itself at the intersection of toxic language detection (which handles Chinese slang and homophony) and multimodal meme understanding, arguing that neither alone suffices for the Chinese online environment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As the first Chinese harmful meme dataset with fine-grained annotations, TOXICN MM fills a critical gap for content moderation research in a major non-English language, and its expanded definition (beyond targeted harm) better reflects the actual distribution of harmful content on Chinese platforms. The finding that GPT-4's detection performance degrades when images are added for general-offense and dispirited-culture memes—because benign visual content misleads the model—highlights a non-trivial failure mode of current MLLMs in multimodal toxicity assessment. Open questions include whether LLMs can be fine-tuned to reliably detect culturally-specific Chinese harmful expressions (homophones, metaphors), whether the dataset's sampling strategy introduces bias, and how to extend detection to explanation generation for transparency.
