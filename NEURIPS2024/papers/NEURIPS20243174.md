## Problem

Transformer-based point cloud recognition models tend to overfit to specific local geometric patterns, making them fragile when those patterns are degraded by real-world corruptions such as sensor noise, point dropping, or point addition. Existing robustness approaches rely on data augmentation or feature-extractor modifications but do not directly address the root cause: the model's reliance on a narrow set of dominant patterns for classification.

## Input / Output

The method takes a raw 3D point cloud P ∈ R^{N×3} and partitions it into n discrete patches via a miniPointNet encoder, producing tokens with C-dimensional features (64×384 for ModelNet, 128×384 for ScanObjectNN). For classification, it outputs a single class label; for the segmentation extension on ShapeNet-C, it outputs per-point part labels. The adversarial dropping mechanism operates internally on the token representations during training and is removed at inference, so the forward pass at test time is identical to a standard transformer.

## Method

APCT introduces two coupled modules within each transformer stage: an Adversarial Significance Identifier (ASI) and a Target-guided Promptor (TGP). The ASI uses stage-specific auxiliary classification heads to compute per-token significance via top-k feature-channel response indexing, then maps this to a per-token dropout rate through a bounded scaling function Φ. The TGP replaces uniform random dropout in self-attention with a targeted mechanism: for each query, it stochastically sets the key values of the most significant tokens to −∞ (with probability proportional to their ASI-derived rate), effectively forcing attention to redistribute toward less-dominant tokens. This adversarial loop is applied iteratively across training, progressively excavating sub-optimal patterns so the model learns a broader, more distributed set of discriminative cues. The total loss combines standard cross-entropy with an auxiliary token-identification loss (L_A) from the per-stage heads, weighted by λ=1.

## Results

On ModelNet-C, APCT achieves a state-of-the-art mCE of 72.2%, outperforming the previous best (PointGPT at 83.4%) by 11.2 points. On the more challenging real-world ScanObjectNN-C benchmark, APCT reaches 74.2% mCE, surpassing PointM2AE (78.2%) by 4.0 points and PointGPT (84.7%) by 10.5 points. For part segmentation on ShapeNet-C, APCT attains 81.4% mCE, improving over GDANet (92.3%) and Point-MAE (92.7%) by 10.9 and 11.3 points respectively. The method also improves clean-dataset accuracy on ScanObjectNN PB-T50-RS from 85.3% to 86.2% OA, with only ~6% additional training/inference overhead and no extra memory cost.

## Baselines & Comparisons

APCT outperforms all 14 compared baselines on the primary ModelNet-C benchmark (mCE 72.2% vs. 83.4% for PointGPT, 83.9% for PointM2AE, and 86.3% for the corruption-specific RPC). The advantage is particularly pronounced on Add-Global corruption (28.5% vs. 92.9% for RPC, a >60-point gap) and Drop-Global (46.8% vs. 49.2% for RPC). On real-world ScanObjectNN-C, APCT leads PointM2AE by 4.0 mCE points and PointGPT by 10.5 points, achieving SOTA on every individual corruption subcategory. For segmentation on ShapeNet-C, APCT (81.4% mCE) surpasses the previous best GDANet (92.3%) and the pretrained Point-MAE (92.7%) by over 10 points. On clean ScanObjectNN PB-T50-RS, APCT improves its own baseline by 0.9% OA (86.2% vs. 85.3%) but remains slightly below PointNeXt (87.3%), indicating a small clean-accuracy trade-off. The adversarial mechanism also transfers to PointM2AE and PointGPT, reducing their mCE by 1.0 and 1.4 points respectively, confirming generality across architectures.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the standard dropout regularization mechanism into a targeted, adversarial strategy within transformer self-attention for point clouds. While prior robustness work (RPC, PointASNL) modifies the feature extractor architecture and augmentation-based methods (PointMixup, WOLFMix) alter training data, APCT instead intervenes at the attention level by progressively identifying and suppressing dominant tokens via an auxiliary supervision-guided significance identifier. The conceptual lineage draws from the observation that transformer-based point cloud models overfit to localized patterns, but the solution—adversarial key dropping conditioned on per-token importance scores—is a new mechanism not present in prior point cloud or vision transformer literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method addresses a practically critical gap: transformer-based point cloud classifiers degrade sharply under real-world sensor corruptions (jitter, point dropout, noise addition) that are ubiquitous in robotics and autonomous driving. The technique is lightweight (zero additional memory, ~6% speed overhead) and composable with existing augmentation pipelines, making it an attractive drop-in improvement for practitioners. However, the paper acknowledges that the optimal utilization of the diverse patterns discovered through adversarial dropping remains unexplored, and the approach has only been validated on classification and segmentation tasks—its applicability to dense prediction or open-vocabulary settings is an open question.
