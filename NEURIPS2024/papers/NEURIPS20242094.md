## Problem

Neural Collapse (NC) with fixed classifiers (e.g., ETF) mitigates minority collapse in imbalanced learning but still underperforms classical methods with learnable classifiers. The paper identifies that the standard 1-nearest-neighbor classification rule induced by NC is suboptimal for hard-to-predict samples, and that minor classes should be assigned more classification directions to improve accuracy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242094/cropped_page8_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 32×32 for SVHN/STL-10, 224×224 for ImageNet) and outputs a single class label. The classifier is fixed as a multi-center frame with fK total directions (f centers per class on average), requiring an extra linear projection layer to expand backbone features to dimension > fK. At inference, classification uses the original K-classifier W₀ (not the multi-center frame W), with batch normalization applied to features before the dot-product decision.

## Method

The paper introduces Neural Collapse to Multiple Centers (NCMC), where each class k is assigned f_k center directions w_j^(k) = v_j^(k)cos θ + w_k sin θ forming a multi-center frame, with f_k increasing for smaller classes via a Class-Aware Strategy. A Cosine Loss trains backbone features to align with the mean of their class's centers while regularizing feature norm to 1, and the classifier remains fixed throughout training. Under the Unconstrained Features Model, the paper proves (Theorems 3.3–3.4) that at the global optimum, all features of class k collapse to the mean of its centers (not to the classifier itself), and the standard RCR with respect to W₀ becomes a surrogate of the Generalized Classification Rule (GCR) that assigns more 'neighbors' to minor classes. The approach is compatible with Mixup augmentation and is validated on five datasets with ResNet-50 and DenseNet-150, achieving long-tail accuracy comparable to LDAM-DRW, KCL, and ARBLoss.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with ResNet-50, CAL achieves 80.0% top-1 accuracy at the most extreme imbalance ratio (τ=0.005), outperforming CE+Mixup (72.3%) by 7.7 points and the best NC-inspired baseline ARBloss (79.5%) by 0.5 points. On CIFAR-100 (τ=0.005), CAL reaches 46.5%, surpassing CE+Mixup (42.4%) by 4.1 points and SETF (43.0%) by 3.5 points. On ImageNet, CAL achieves 49.7%, which is competitive but trails KCL (51.5%) and ARBloss (52.8%), reflecting the limitation of the fixed-classifier design on large-scale data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CAL consistently outperforms SETF (the closest NC-inspired fixed-classifier method) across all CIFAR-10 imbalance ratios (e.g., 80.0% vs 74.2% at τ=0.005) and CIFAR-100 at τ≤0.02 (46.5% vs 43.0% at τ=0.005), with the gap widening as imbalance increases. Against the margin-based LDAM-DRW, CAL wins on every CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100 setting (e.g., 46.5% vs 39.5% on CIFAR-100 at τ=0.005), confirming that the multi-center structure provides stronger supervision than simple logit adjustment. CAL also beats the contrastive-learning method KCL on CIFAR-10 (80.0% vs 75.0% at τ=0.005) and CIFAR-100 (τ≤0.02), but loses to KCL on ImageNet (49.7% vs 51.5%), where the fixed-classifier constraint limits flexibility. Compared to ARBloss, CAL is competitive on CIFAR-10 (80.0% vs 79.5% at τ=0.005) and clearly superior on CIFAR-100 (46.5% vs 42.7% at τ=0.005), but again trails on ImageNet (49.7% vs 52.8%). Overall, CAL's advantage is most pronounced under heavy imbalance (τ≤0.01) on smaller datasets, while its fixed-classifier design becomes a bottleneck on large-scale ImageNet where learnable classifiers (KCL, ARBloss) retain an edge.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Neural Collapse (NC) literature by replacing the standard single-center ETF structure with a multi-center frame, where each class is represented by f_k orthogonal directions rather than one. It builds directly on the Unconstrained Features Model (UFM) framework of Mixon et al. [12] and the fixed-classifier paradigm of SETF [26], but departs from both by showing that features should collapse to the *mean* of multiple class centers rather than aligning with a single classifier weight. The Generalized Classification Rule (GCR) is motivated by a probabilistic analysis of hard-to-predict features, arguing that minor classes benefit from more 'neighbors' in the decision rule, which the multi-center structure implicitly encodes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled explanation for why standard NC (ETF) is suboptimal under class imbalance and offers a concrete mechanism—multi-center collapse with a class-aware number of centers per class—that closes the gap between fixed-classifier and learnable-classifier methods on long-tail benchmarks. However, the method's limitations are significant: it underperforms CE in balanced settings, does not match KCL or ARBLoss on ImageNet, and the fixed classifier constrains the flexibility of learned representations. Open questions include whether the class-aware strategy generalizes beyond the exponential long-tail assumption, whether NCMC can be induced in deeper or non-linear architectures without the UFM simplification, and whether the multi-center structure helps in other distribution-shift scenarios beyond long-tail imbalance.
