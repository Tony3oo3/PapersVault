## Problem

Existing data augmentation for state-based continuous control relies on perturbation-based methods (Gaussian noise, random amplitude scaling) that produce physically invalid transitions, yielding limited or negative improvements in data efficiency. The paper identifies that Euclidean symmetries (rotations about the gravity axis) are a principled alternative but remain underexploited because standard benchmarks use joint-based state representations whose features are mostly invariant to such rotations, making them unsuitable for symmetry-based augmentation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242864/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The agent observes a limb-based kinematic state representation in an egocentric frame (torso as origin), consisting of the torso's orientation matrix and angular velocity, each limb's translational position and velocity relative to the torso, and rotation axes for joints with more than one degree of freedom, plus task features (target direction or target-to-limb vector) and task-specific sensory observations. The output is a vector of joint torques (one scalar per actuated degree of freedom). Unlike the standard joint-based representation used by DMControl and Gym-MuJoCo, this limb-based representation is equivariant under SO_g(3) rotations, enabling meaningful data augmentation.

## Method

The method replaces the default joint-based state representation with limb-based kinematic features that are equivariant under rotations about the gravity axis (SO_g(3)), then applies random yaw rotations to a fraction ρ_aug of transitions sampled from the DDPG replay buffer. Under each rotation, equivariant features (positions, velocities, orientation matrices, rotation axes, task vectors) are rotated by the rotation matrix, while invariant features (non-torso joint angles, actions, rewards) remain unchanged, and the torso's yaw angle is offset by the rotation angle. This guarantees that augmented transitions are valid samples from the task's dynamics and reward, unlike perturbation-based augmentation. The approach introduces only one additional hyperparameter (ρ_aug) on top of standard DDPG, and the paper shows that the limb-based representation alone improves data efficiency on most tasks, while the rotation augmentation is crucial for the hardest 3D tasks with many degrees of freedom (e.g., Humanoid_run, Hopper3D_hop).

## Results

The method is evaluated on 10 continuous control tasks from DeepMind Control Suite (7 original 2D/3D tasks plus 3 new 3D variants: Cheetah3D_run, Hopper3D_hop, Walker3D_run), using episodic reward (averaged over 10 episodes) as the metric. On the hardest tasks, improvements are dramatic: on Humanoid_run (5M steps), standard DDPG achieves an episode reward below 100 while the proposed method reaches 150; on Hopper3D_hop (2M steps), DDPG stays below 40 while the method exceeds 200. The limb-based state representation alone (ρ_aug=0%) already improves data efficiency over DDPG on 6 of 9 tasks, and adding SO_g(3) rotation augmentation is crucial for the hardest 3D tasks (Hopper3D_hop, Humanoid_run, Humanoid_stand). The method introduces only one additional hyperparameter (ρ_aug) and requires roughly 2 hours per 1M steps on a single V100 GPU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against standard DDPG, the proposed method (limb-based state + SO_g(3) augmentation) consistently outperforms, with the largest gains on high-DoF 3D tasks: Humanoid_run improves from <100 to 150 episode reward, and Hopper3D_hop from <40 to >200. Against SAC, DDPG itself is comparable or superior on 7 of 9 tasks, justifying its use as the base algorithm. Against prior perturbation-based augmentation (RAD's Gaussian noise and random amplitude scaling), the proposed Euclidean augmentation is clearly superior: GN and RAS provide no significant data-efficiency gains and often degrade performance, whereas SO_g(3) rotation reliably improves results. Against SEGNN (equivariant neural network architecture), the proposed method is slightly less data-efficient on Reacher_hard but dramatically more computationally efficient (~2 hours vs. >70 hours per 1M steps), making it far more practical for larger tasks where SEGNN was infeasible to run.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on DDPG as the base actor-critic algorithm and departs from the dominant perturbation-based data augmentation paradigm (Gaussian noise, random amplitude scaling, image cropping) that has characterized prior RL augmentation work (RAD, CURL, DrQ). It also distinguishes itself from prior Euclidean-symmetry-in-RL work (Abdolhosseini et al., Van der Pol et al., Wang et al.), which either employed computationally expensive equivariant neural networks or focused on image-based tasks. The key conceptual departure is identifying that the default joint-based state representation in standard benchmarks is fundamentally incompatible with Euclidean transformations (being largely rotation-invariant), and proposing a limb-based kinematic representation that restores equivariance, thereby unlocking SO_g(3) rotations as a principled, symmetry-preserving augmentation for state-based control.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a simple, principled recipe—switch to limb-based features and apply random yaw rotations—that significantly improves data efficiency and asymptotic performance on hard continuous control tasks (e.g., Humanoid_run, Hopper3D_hop) where standard DDPG fails, while introducing only one hyperparameter and negligible compute overhead compared to equivariant architectures like SEGNN (which are ~35× slower). Open questions include the task-specific nature of the optimal augmentation ratio ρ_aug (100% helps Humanoid but hurts Hopper3D), the need for known/annotated Euclidean symmetries, and whether the approach extends to tasks with approximate or irregular symmetries (e.g., noisy external forces). The finding that perturbation-based augmentation is actively harmful for state-based control, in contrast to its success in the image-based setting, clarifies an important boundary condition for the broader data-augmentation-in-RL literature.
