## Problem

Training diffusion models for audiovisual generation typically requires a separate model for each conditional task (audio-to-video, video-to-audio, continuation, interpolation), which is expensive and impractical. Existing unified approaches like MM-Diffusion rely on unconditional joint training with inference-time reconstruction-guided sampling, which fails to produce temporally consistent sequences. The paper asks whether a single model can be trained in a task-agnostic manner to support arbitrary conditional distributions across modalities and time segments.

## Input / Output

The model operates in compressed latent space: video latents from MAGVIT-v2 (spatial compression 8×, temporal compression 4×, embedding dim 8) and audio latents from SoundStream (temporal compression 320×, embedding dim 1024), both projected to a shared 1024-dim space. At inference, the user specifies which portions of the M×N modality-by-time-segment grid are conditioning (set to t=0, i.e., clean) and which are to be generated (set to the current diffusion step t), enabling flexible configurations such as 5 video frames + 0.625s audio as condition for 1.5s continuation, or a single frame + 0.125s audio for 2s generation. Outputs are decoded back to 128×128 video at 8fps (Monologues) or 64×64 at 10fps (AIST++/Landscape) and 16kHz audio.

## Method

The core contribution is Mixture of Noise Levels (MoNL), which replaces the scalar diffusion timestep with a vector t ∈ R^{M×N} so that each modality and time-segment can receive an independent noise level during the forward process. During training, one of four timestep strategies is randomly selected per sample: Vanilla (uniform across all elements), Per-Modality (one timestep per modality), Per-Time-segment (one timestep per time position shared across modalities), and Per-Time-segment-and-Per-Modality (fully independent per element). The denoising network is AVDiT, a 24-layer, 16-head transformer (420M parameters) that embeds the full timestep vector via positional encodings and uses it to modulate AdaLN scaling/shifting at every layer, allowing the network to attend to variable noise levels spatially and temporally. Conditional inference requires no architectural changes or inference-time tricks: the conditioning portions are simply fed as clean inputs (t=0) while target portions follow the standard reverse diffusion schedule, and classifier-free guidance is obtained 'for free' by injecting pure noise into conditioning slots (t=T) instead of using a null token.

## Results

On the Monologues dataset, AVDiT trained with MoNL achieves an average FAD of 4.9 and FVD of 34.6 across all five tasks (Joint, A2V, V2A, AV-inpaint, AV-continue), outperforming task-specific conditional models (FAD 7.8, FVD 35.3) and the Vanilla joint-diffusion baseline (FAD 7.4, FVD 121.1). On the AIST++ and Landscape datasets, MoNL substantially outperforms MM-Diffusion: A2V FVD drops from 184.45 to 38.04 (AIST++) and from 238.33 to 86.79 (Landscape), while V2A FAD drops from 13.30 to 1.11 (AIST++) and from 13.60 to 0.78 (Landscape). In user studies, MoNL is preferred over MMD in person consistency (up to 0.93 for AV-continue) and AV alignment (0.69–0.77) across tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary external baseline is MM-Diffusion (MMD) [Ruan et al., CVPR 2023], a U-Net-based model that also handles joint audio-video generation in a single model. MoNL AVDiT decisively outperforms MMD on all quantitative metrics: A2V FVD improves by ~5× on AIST++ (38.04 vs. 184.45) and ~2.7× on Landscape (86.79 vs. 238.33), while V2A FAD improves by ~12× on AIST++ (1.11 vs. 13.30) and ~17× on Landscape (0.78 vs. 13.60). KVD (keyframe video distance) for A2V also improves substantially (5.27 vs. 33.91 on AIST++; 4.30 vs. 15.14 on Landscape). In the user study, MoNL is preferred over MMD in AV alignment (0.69–0.77) and person consistency (0.60–0.93) across all four tasks, with the largest margin in person consistency for AV-continue (0.93). The one exception is V2A AV quality, where MMD is slightly preferred (0.49), likely because the SoundStream audio encoder used by MoNL is not optimized for music generation as in MMD.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the UniDiffuser [3] line of work, which introduced variable noise levels across modalities for joint text-image diffusion, by generalizing the diffusion timestep from a scalar to a vector spanning both modalities and time segments (MoNL). The transformer architecture (AVDiT) draws directly on W.A.L.T [12] for its timestep embedding, AdaLN conditioning, and self-conditioning mechanism, while the core diffusion formulation modifies the standard DDPM [16] forward process to support per-element noise levels. The key conceptual departure from MM-Diffusion [40] is eliminating inference-time reconstruction-guided sampling tricks in favor of task-agnostic training that natively supports conditional inference through selective noise injection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MoNL provides a clean, minimal-modification recipe for training a single diffusion model to handle an arbitrary set of conditional generation tasks without per-task training or inference-time adjustments, which is practically important as the combinatorial explosion of input-output configurations makes separate models infeasible. The demonstrated temporal consistency advantage over MM-Diffusion (FVD 38 vs. 184 on AIST++ A2V) addresses a critical failure mode in prior AV generation. Open questions include whether the simple uniform mixture of timestep strategies is optimal (weighted or learned mixing), how the approach scales to more than two modalities or longer sequences, and whether the human-centric evaluation generalizes to open-domain AV content. The authors note that visual and speech quality still require super-resolution and text conditioning to reach production utility.
