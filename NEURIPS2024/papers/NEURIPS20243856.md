## Problem

Contrastive multi-view clustering methods assume faultless cross-view correspondence, but in practice both positive pairs (contaminated by data collection/transmission errors) and negative pairs (contaminated by the inherent one-to-many contrastive sampling) are noisy. This Dual Noisy Correspondence (DNC) problem is more challenging than the partially view-aligned problem because it provides no guarantee of any clean correspondence, rendering existing PVP-oriented methods infeasible.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243856/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view data samples (e.g., PHOG/GIST image features, DECAF/VGG19 deep features, LBP/PHOG satellite features, autoencoder-projected multilingual text, or VGG19/Sentence-CNN image-text pairs) and produces k-means cluster assignments on concatenated realigned representations. The key intermediate artifact is a denoised pseudo target matrix C ∈ R^{n×n} that replaces the identity matrix in the contrastive loss, where n is the batch size (fixed at 1024). The method is designed as a plug-and-play module that only modifies the loss function of an existing contrastive MvC backbone (DIVIDE).

## Method

CANDY introduces two modules to generate a noise-resilient pseudo target for a soft contrastive loss. First, the Context-based Semantic Mining (CSM) module constructs a cross-view affinity graph via Gaussian kernel similarity, then reformulates each node's row-wise connection probabilities as a 'context' embedding to induce a high-order affinity graph G = A^(v1→v2)·A^(v2→v2)ᵀ, which captures global structural information to reveal semantically-associated false negatives hidden among randomly sampled negatives. Second, the Spectral-based Correspondence Denoising (SCD) module performs SVD on G and retains only singular values above a threshold η=0.2, filtering out noise components tied to false positives. The final pseudo target C = 0.2·I + G̃ is plugged into a row-wise cross-entropy contrastive loss (Eq. 1) that trains view-specific encoders, with a 20-epoch warmup using the vanilla identity target for initialization.

## Results

On the average across five multi-view benchmarks, CANDY achieves 51.9% ACC, 48.3% NMI, and 35.0% ARI at 0% false-positive (FP) ratio, outperforming the strongest baseline DIVIDE (49.6/48.4/29.7). Under 80% FP noise, CANDY retains 42.4% ACC, 39.2% NMI, and 27.1% ARI, while DIVIDE drops to 35.2/32.5/18.2 and most other baselines fall below 30% ACC. The gap widens with increasing noise: at 50% FP, CANDY leads DIVIDE by 9.5 points in ACC and 12.0 points in ARI on average.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CANDY consistently outperforms all eight baselines across every FP ratio and dataset. Against its direct backbone DIVIDE (AAAI'24), CANDY gains +2.3 ACC and +5.3 ARI at 0% FP, and the advantage grows to +7.2 ACC and +8.9 ARI at 80% FP, confirming that the CSM and SCD modules add substantial robustness beyond the base architecture. Among PVP-oriented methods, CGCN (TCSVT'24) is the strongest competitor at 80% FP (30.9 ACC), yet CANDY still leads by 11.5 ACC points; SURE (TPAMI'23) and MvCLN (CVPR'21) degrade more sharply under noise. The false-negative-robust method GCFAgg (CVPR'23) and the classic MvC methods DCCAE and BMVC all suffer severe degradation (often below 25% ACC at 80% FP), while CANDY maintains 42.4% ACC. In a dedicated comparison (Table 5), the SOTA noisy-correspondence retrieval method RCL (TPAMI'23) applied to the same DIVIDE backbone drops to 16.7% ACC on Caltech-101 at 80% FP, whereas CANDY retains 52.6%, demonstrating that existing noisy-correspondence learning approaches are insufficient for the DNC problem in MvC.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on DIVIDE (Lu et al., AAAI 2024), a contrastive MvC method using high-order random walks, retaining its architecture while replacing the identity-matrix target with a denoised pseudo target. Conceptually, it extends the Partially View-Aligned (PVP) clustering line (Huang et al., NeurIPS 2020; Yang et al., CVPR 2021) by removing the assumption that any correctly-associated pairs exist, formulating the more general Dual Noisy Correspondence (DNC) problem. The spectral denoising component borrows from SVD-based image denoising (Rajwade et al., 2013; Guo et al., 2016), repurposing it for affinity-graph purification in a clustering context, while the context-based semantic mining module departs from point-to-point similarity by treating row-wise connection probabilities as a new embedding space for global false-negative discovery.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The formalization of DNC as a distinct, more practical problem than PVP is a useful conceptual contribution, and the plug-and-play design means CANDY can be dropped into existing contrastive MvC pipelines without architectural changes. However, the evaluation is limited to two-view settings and five relatively small benchmarks, hyperparameters (η=0.2, λ=0.2, σ=0.07) are fixed without extensive tuning, and no statistical significance is reported. An important open question is whether the approach generalizes to more than two views, to larger-scale datasets, and to the combined challenge of noisy correspondence plus missing modalities, which the authors acknowledge as future work.
