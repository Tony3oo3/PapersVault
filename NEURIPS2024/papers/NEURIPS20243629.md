## Problem

Contrastive preference optimization methods (DPO, IPO, SLiC) maximize relative reward differences between chosen and rejected responses but ignore absolute reward values, causing the likelihood of chosen responses to decrease during training. This leads to suboptimal alignment, particularly on reasoning and mathematical tasks where chosen responses are often ground-truth answers. The paper asks how to design a preference objective that prevents chosen-response likelihood from degrading while still guaranteeing convergence to the optimal policy.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard preference triplets (prompt x, chosen response y_w, rejected response y_l) along with a frozen reference policy π_ref. When no explicit reward scores are available, Cal-DPO assigns proxy rewards r(x,y_w)=1/2 and r(x,y_l)=-1/2 to the preference pair, requiring no additional hyperparameters beyond the existing β. The output is a fine-tuned language model policy π_θ whose implicit reward log(π_θ(y|x)/π_ref(y|x)) is constrained to match the ground-truth reward scale r(x,y)/β, ensuring chosen responses have positive implicit reward and rejected responses have negative implicit reward.

## Method

Cal-DPO augments the standard Bradley-Terry preference loss (equivalent to DPO without β) with a squared calibration loss that regresses the implicit reward of each response toward its ground-truth value: the chosen response's implicit reward is pushed toward 1/(2β) and the rejected response's toward −1/(2β). This single modification—implementable as one additional line of code on top of DPO—prevents the chosen-response likelihood from monotonically decreasing during training. Theoretically, the BT loss term minimizes forward KL divergence while retaining a contrastive negative gradient (pushing down low-reward responses), and the calibration term bridges the gap between forward and reverse KL, so that the full Cal-DPO objective upper-bounds the reverse KL divergence, yielding mode-seeking behavior analogous to RLHF. The approach generalizes directly to other contrastive objectives (IPO, SLiC) by adding the same calibration term to their respective pairwise losses.

## Results

On the UltraFeedback Binarized dataset with Zephyr-7b-sft, Cal-DPO achieves the best scores across all seven reasoning benchmarks, with particularly large gains on IFEval (21.72 vs. 10.49 for DPO, a >100% relative improvement) and GSM8K (34.87 vs. 21.76 for DPO, ~60% relative improvement). On the IMDb controlled generation task, Cal-DPO attains the highest reward (0.645) and lowest perplexity (32.31) among all methods including PPO (0.626 / 35.05). On GPT-4-evaluated summarization and dialogue tasks, Cal-DPO achieves average win rates of 67.49% (TL;DR) and 69.07% (Anthropic-HH) against the SFT model, outperforming DPO (64.40% and 64.34%) and CPO (66.01% and 67.86%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Cal-DPO consistently outperforms all nine baselines across every benchmark. Against DPO (the primary baseline it builds on), Cal-DPO improves IFEval by 11.23 points (21.72 vs. 10.49), GSM8K by 13.11 points (34.87 vs. 21.76), and MATH by 1.06 points (2.42 vs. 1.36), while also leading on MMLU-PRO, BBH, GPQA, and ARC. Against CPO, which already augments DPO with a negative log-likelihood loss on chosen responses, Cal-DPO still wins on all seven benchmarks, suggesting that calibrating both chosen and rejected rewards is more effective than only boosting the chosen response. On the IMDb task, Cal-DPO surpasses PPO (reward 0.645 vs. 0.626; perplexity 32.31 vs. 35.05), confirming that the offline calibrated objective can match or exceed the online RL approach. On GPT-4-evaluated dialogue and summarization, Cal-DPO beats DPO by ~4.7 and ~4.7 points in average win rate, and also outperforms DPOP and DPO+NLL, which were specifically designed to address the chosen-response likelihood drop. The calibrated variants Cal-IPO and Cal-SLiC similarly improve over their uncalibrated counterparts on Anthropic-HH, confirming the generality of the calibration objective.

## Relation to Prior Work

Cal-DPO builds directly on DPO by adding a squared-error calibration term that constrains the implicit reward (log-ratio of policy to reference) to match the scale of ground-truth rewards, addressing a known limitation of all contrastive preference methods (DPO, IPO, SLiC) that only optimize relative reward differences while ignoring absolute values. The theoretical analysis positions Cal-DPO between MLE (forward KL, mode-covering) and RLHF (reverse KL, mode-seeking), showing that the calibration loss minimizes the gap between these two divergences. The paper also connects to the broader calibration literature in classification and RL, but applies the concept specifically to implicit rewards in preference optimization rather than to confidence scores.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The one-line code modification makes Cal-DPO trivially adoptable on top of any existing DPO pipeline, and the consistent improvements across reasoning, summarization, dialogue, and controlled generation benchmarks suggest it could become a default addition to preference fine-tuning. The theoretical guarantee that Cal-DPO minimizes an upper bound on the reverse KL objective (matching RLHF's mode-seeking behavior) provides a principled justification beyond empirical observation. A key limitation is that Cal-DPO is restricted to offline learning; extending the calibration idea to on-policy or iterative settings remains open. The absolute gains on math benchmarks (e.g., MATH: 2.42 vs. 2.15 for CPO) remain modest, suggesting calibration alone may not fully resolve reasoning performance.
