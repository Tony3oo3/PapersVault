## Problem

Despite the rapid progress of text-to-image generative models, there is no comprehensive benchmark specifically designed to evaluate their numerical reasoning capabilities. Existing benchmarks either contain only a handful of numerical prompts or use complex, multi-skill prompts (e.g., combining counting with spatial relations and attribute binding) that confound numerical reasoning with other competencies, making it impossible to isolate and measure how well models handle numbers, quantities, and zero.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241335/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark consists of 1,386 text prompts systematically generated from 12 templates that sweep over number magnitudes (1–10), number representations (Arabic digits vs. words), noun frequencies (frequent vs. rare), and prompt structures (simple, additive, spatial, fractional). Each prompt is fed to 12 models across 5 families, producing 52,721 images (5 seeds per prompt-model pair), which are then annotated by 5 human raters each (479,570 annotations total). Annotation format varies by task: free-form object counts for exact generation, multiple-choice line selection for approximate quantities, and auto-generated yes/no scene-graph questions for conceptual quantitative reasoning.

## Method

GECKONUM formalizes numerical reasoning evaluation into three tasks—exact number generation, approximate number generation (including zero), and conceptual quantitative reasoning (fractions, part-whole)—each probed by a set of controlled prompt types that isolate specific variables such as number magnitude, digit-vs-word representation, noun frequency, and compositional complexity. The key design principle is systematic control: 12 prompt templates are constructed so that each factor (e.g., adding a color adjective, introducing a spatial preposition, or switching from one entity to two) can be varied independently, enabling fine-grained attribution of performance drops. Evaluation relies on human annotations (5 per image, >96% inter-annotator agreement) rather than automatic metrics, with accuracy computed as exact match for counts, selection match for quantifiers, and mean yes/no accuracy for scene-graph questions. The paper further uses GECKONUM to benchmark five auto-eval metrics (CLIPscore, TIFA, Gecko, DSG, VNLI) on their ability to discriminate correct from incorrect numerical generations, and to evaluate PaLIGemma VLMs on counting, demonstrating the benchmark's utility beyond its primary T2I evaluation role.

## Results

On GECKONUM, DALL-E 3 achieves the highest accuracy on exact number generation (Task 1: 45.2%) and approximate number generation (Task 2: 48.7%), while Imagen-C leads on conceptual quantitative reasoning (Task 3: 50.6%). Even the best-performing model stays below 50% on Tasks 1 and 2, and most models perform at or below the random-chance baseline of 50% on Task 3. Accuracy drops sharply with number magnitude (e.g., DALL-E 3 loses 18 p.p. going from 1→2 objects) and with prompt complexity (spatial relations, additive compositions). For auto-eval metrics, only Gecko, DSG, and VNLI reliably discriminate correct from incorrect generations across all five tested models, and only VNLI (8/10) and Gecko (7/10) correctly rank models in pairwise comparisons.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DALL-E 3 outperforms all 11 other models on both Task 1 (45.2% vs. next-best Midjourney v6 at 42.5%) and Task 2 (48.7% vs. Midjourney v6 at 35.0%), but its Task 3 accuracy (48.8%) is actually below the 50% random-chance baseline, where Imagen-C leads at 50.6%. Within the Stable Diffusion family, SD3 (40.0% / 33.9% / 47.8%) substantially outperforms SD 1.5 (20.3% / 20.6% / 44.6%) and SDXL (22.8% / 31.2% / 43.8%), while within the Imagen family, Imagen-C (34.9% / 27.0% / 50.6%) beats the larger Imagen-D (28.5% / 28.7% / 43.8%) on Task 1 and Task 3. For auto-eval metrics, CLIPscore fails to discriminate correct from incorrect generations for 4 out of 5 models, TIFA succeeds for 4/5, while Gecko, DSG, and VNLI succeed for all 5; however, only VNLI (8/10 pairwise comparisons) and Gecko (7/10) correctly rank models. On the VLM counting sub-experiment, PaLIGemma fine-tuned on TallyQA achieves 73.3% on GECKONUM versus 68.4% for the base model, with the gap concentrated on counts ≥ 5.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves out numerical reasoning as a distinct evaluation axis for text-to-image models, building on the broader T2I evaluation landscape (Gecko, TIFA, DSG, T2I-CompBench) but addressing a gap the authors identify: no prior benchmark systematically evaluates numerical reasoning across multiple dimensions. The closest prior work is Rane et al. [22], which tested number generation with only 59 prompts and the simplest structure; GECKONUM extends this to 1,386 prompts spanning exact counts, linguistic quantifiers, zero, and part-whole/fractional reasoning. The task design draws on numerical cognition literature (the abstraction principle, developmental milestones for zero and fractions) to structure evaluation, and the auto-eval and VLM counting experiments repurpose existing metrics (CLIPscore, TIFA, Gecko, DSG, VNLI) and datasets (TallyQA) in a new context.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark provides the first comprehensive, controlled evaluation of numerical reasoning in T2I models, and its 479,570 human annotations make it a reusable resource for the community. The near-random performance on conceptual quantitative reasoning (Task 3) across all 12 models—including 8B-parameter Imagen-D—suggests that scaling alone will not fix these deficits and that architectural or representational innovations may be needed. Open questions include how to evaluate more complex numerical concepts (dates, expressions like '4k'), whether synthetic data from T2I models can meaningfully improve VLM counting, and how to design annotation protocols for increasingly sophisticated quantitative reasoning as models improve.
