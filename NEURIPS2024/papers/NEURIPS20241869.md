## Problem

Efficiently exploring the vast chemical space (estimated 10^23–10^60 drug-like molecules) requires understanding the structure of the latent space learned by molecule generative models. Existing approaches—gradient-based optimization, linear latent traversal (ChemSpace), and disentangled representation learning—are fragmented and rely on strong assumptions like linear separability. This paper asks whether a single dynamical-systems framework can unify these methods and introduce new, physically motivated traversal dynamics that are more flexible and effective.

## Input / Output

The method operates on a pre-trained VAE with a 1024-dimensional latent space that encodes/decodes molecules as SELFIES strings. In supervised mode, a trained MLP property predictor provides gradient guidance; in unsupervised mode, the generator's Jacobian-vector product drives structural diversity without any property labels. Traversal proceeds over T discrete steps (10 for manipulation, up to 1000 for optimization) with a fixed step size of 0.1, producing a sequence of decoded molecules whose properties shift along the learned flow trajectory.

## Method

ChemFlow parameterizes K scalar-valued energy functions φ^k(t,z) via MLPs, whose spatial gradients ∇_z φ^k define velocity fields that transport the molecular distribution through latent space over time. The key unifying insight is that gradient flow, linear traversal, and disentangled traversal are all special cases of flows governed by different PDE constraints: the Hamilton-Jacobi equation enforces optimal-transport (shortest-path) dynamics, the wave equation produces diverse sinusoidal trajectories, and the Fokker-Planck equation corresponds to Langevin dynamics with global convergence guarantees. In supervised settings, the flow is steered by aligning with the negative gradient of a surrogate property predictor; in unsupervised settings, a Jacobian-vector product loss maximizes structural change while an auxiliary cross-entropy classifier enforces disentanglement across the K learned flows. The total loss combines the PDE residual, boundary conditions, and the task-specific guidance term, trained end-to-end on 9,000 random latent samples.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ChemSpace [11] (linear latent traversal via SVM boundary) is the strongest baseline on ESR1 docking (1st score -11.66 vs. -11.05 for Wave SPV) and ranks 3rd on molecule manipulation with a 6.17% strict success rate, but it underperforms all ChemFlow variants on plogP (3.74 vs. 5.30 for Wave UNSUP) and ACAA1 docking (-9.81 vs. -10.48 for Wave SPV). Gradient Flow / LIMO [13] (gradient-based latent optimization) achieves the best molecule manipulation strict success rate (7.62% vs. 6.17% for ChemSpace) and competitive QED scores (0.944), but is outperformed by Wave (SPV) and LD on unconstrained plogP (4.06 vs. 4.76/4.74) and both docking tasks. Among ChemFlow's own variants, Langevin Dynamics (LD) stands out for similarity-constrained optimization (Table 2), achieving the highest success rates at all similarity thresholds (e.g., 94.4% at δ=0.2 vs. 90.2% for ChemSpace) and the fastest convergence over 1,000 steps. Wave (UNSUP) dominates unconstrained plogP optimization (5.30) without any property labels, while Wave (SPV) and LD tie for best QED (0.947). Overall, no single method dominates all tasks, but the ChemFlow framework's PDE-regularized flows consistently match or exceed both baselines across the evaluated properties.

## Relation to Prior Work

ChemFlow unifies three previously separate paradigms for navigating molecular latent space—gradient-based optimization (LIMO, Eckmann et al. 2022), linear latent traversal (ChemSpace, Du et al. 2023), and disentangled traversal—under a single dynamical-systems framework where traversal is governed by a learned vector field. The theoretical backbone draws on the Benamou-Brenier optimal transport formulation and the wave-equation potential flows of Song et al. (2023), extending them to the molecular domain and adding Hamilton-Jacobi and Fokker-Planck/Langevin dynamics as alternative physical priors. The key departure from prior work is the explicit PDE-based regularization on the flow itself (rather than just the direction) and the introduction of a Jacobian-based unsupervised guidance that replaces the need for a trained property surrogate.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides a principled, extensible lens for studying how molecular generative models organize their latent space, and the unsupervised setting (exploiting structure-activity correlation via Jacobian guidance) opens a path toward property discovery without labeled data—a genuinely under-studied regime. However, the study is limited to a single VAE architecture on small molecules, several methods (ChemSpace, unsupervised Wave/HJ) suffer from out-of-distribution collapse after extended optimization, and the empirical gains over strong baselines like Langevin dynamics are modest. Open questions include whether the PDE-regularized latent space learning (Appendix B) can be scaled to larger molecular systems, whether the norm-property correlation discovered in the latent space is an artifact of the VAE prior or a fundamental property, and how the framework transfers to diffusion-based or graph-based molecular generators.
