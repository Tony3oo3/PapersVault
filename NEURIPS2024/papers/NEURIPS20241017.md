## Problem

Diffusion model denoisers are poorly estimated in regions far outside the standard training distribution (OOD), yet the sampling process—especially parallel sampling via Picard iteration—inevitably evaluates the denoiser in these OOD regions, causing significant sample quality degradation. Standard MSE diffusion training only evaluates the denoiser on samples from the matching noisy distribution, leaving asymmetric (noise-level, sample-distribution) pairs untrained and the denoiser unreliable where it is most needed during sampling.

## Input / Output

The method operates as a fine-tuning regularizer on pre-trained diffusion model checkpoints (DDPM and EDM architectures). At inference, the model takes isotropic Gaussian noise (optionally conditioned on a class label) and produces generated images at 32×32 (CIFAR-10) or 64×64 (FFHQ, AFHQv2), or 2D data points for synthetic experiments. During CDL training, the loss consumes a clean data sample x₀ and a noisier version x_ζ = √σ(ζ)·x₀ + √σ(−ζ)·ε, requiring the model to evaluate its denoiser at mismatched noise levels to compute pointwise log-likelihoods for both p(x) and p_ζ(x).

## Method

The paper proves that the optimal MSE denoiser in a diffusion model implicitly defines a log-likelihood ratio (LLR) between the data distribution p(x) and any noisier mixture distribution p_ζ(x), making it an optimal binary noise classifier. Building on this, the Contrastive Diffusion Loss (CDL) is a self-supervised cross-entropy objective that trains the denoiser to distinguish samples drawn from p(x) (label y=1) from samples drawn from p_ζ(x) (label y=−1), where ζ is sampled from Uniform[6,15] or logistic[6,15]. Both log-likelihoods in the LLR are estimated via the denoiser using the information-theoretic identity from Kong et al. (2023), with constants canceling. Crucially, this forces the denoiser to be evaluated on asymmetric pairs—e.g., a sample from p_ζ at noise level α—providing gradient signal in OOD regions that standard diffusion loss never touches. CDL is applied as an additional regularizer during fine-tuning on top of the standard diffusion loss, with the pointwise NLL estimated by duplicating a single data point N=100 times to approximate the integral over α.

## Results

On parallel DDPM sampling, CDL-regularized models consistently outperform baselines: CDL-VE achieves 2.38 FID on unconditional CIFAR-10 (vs. 3.00 for VE) and 3.29 on FFHQ (vs. 3.65), while CDL-VP reaches 2.51 on CIFAR-10 (vs. 3.24 for VP). On sequential deterministic EDM sampling, CDL-VE matches or slightly improves baselines (e.g., 2.11 vs. 2.17 FID on AFHQv2, 2.47 vs. 2.56 on FFHQ). On the 2D Dino synthetic dataset, CDL-regularized parallel sampling converges in 27 Picard iterations with MMD 0.0012, versus 36 iterations and MMD 0.0031 for the DDPM baseline, while also reducing wall-clock time from 1,870 ns to 1,368 ns.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares CDL-regularized models against two baseline families: DDPM (Ho et al. 2020) and EDM (Karras et al. 2022, both VP and VE variants). On parallel sampling, CDL-regularized models win decisively across all datasets: CDL-VE improves FID by 0.62 on CIFAR-10 uncond (3.00→2.38), 0.36 on FFHQ (3.65→3.29), and 0.34 on CIFAR-10 cond (2.76→2.25). On sequential deterministic EDM sampling, gains are smaller but consistent: CDL-VE improves AFHQv2 from 2.17 to 2.11 and FFHQ from 2.56 to 2.47, while matching CIFAR-10 performance. On the synthetic Dino dataset, CDL reduces Picard iterations from 36 to 27 (25% fewer) and cuts wall-clock time by 27%, while halving the MMD score. The paper also shows CDL eliminates the need for the S_noise=1.007 hyperparameter hack in stochastic EDM sampling, outperforming even the optimally tuned EDM baseline on CIFAR-10.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the information-theoretic formulation of diffusion (Kong et al. 2023), which expresses data log-likelihood directly in terms of the optimal denoiser, and connects it to the density ratio estimation / noise contrastive estimation framework (Gutmann & Hyvärinen 2010). The key conceptual departure is recognizing that the standard MSE diffusion denoiser implicitly defines an optimal classifier for distinguishing noise levels, and leveraging this to construct a contrastive training signal (CDL) that regularizes the denoiser in OOD regions. For sampling, the paper positions itself relative to both accelerated sequential solvers (DDIM, EDM) and the newer parallel Picard-iteration sampler (Shih et al. 2024), arguing that the latter is particularly vulnerable to OOD denoising errors that CDL addresses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of OOD denoising error as a fundamental cause of sample quality degradation—especially for parallel samplers—reframes how practitioners think about diffusion training robustness. CDL is a simple, architecture-agnostic regularizer that consistently improves FID and parallel convergence speed across multiple datasets and model families, and it eliminates the need for the S_noise hyperparameter in EDM stochastic sampling. However, the significant training cost increase (N=100 pointwise NLL evaluations per data point) is a substantial practical limitation that limits immediate adoption, and it remains open whether cheaper approximations of the contrastive signal can capture most of the benefit. The approach also opens the door to using diffusion models more explicitly as density ratio estimators for other tasks.
