## Problem

No machine learning dataset or structurally faithful modeling approach exists for Agda, a dependently-typed proof assistant that is otherwise well-represented in the formal verification ecosystem. Existing sequence-based and nominal AST-encoding methods treat variable names and lemma references as fixed-vocabulary tokens, breaking α-equivalence invariance and sacrificing completeness under naming shifts, while tree and graph architectures struggle with the long-range, depth-variable structure of dependent types.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a file-level collection of Agda type ASTs extracted at the internal compilation-state resolution (not pretty-printed surface syntax), partitioned into global imports, local definitions, and private helpers, each carrying a full typing context and de Bruijn-resolved variable pointers. Batches are variable-sized per file, with AST counts and depths exhibiting high variance. The output is a single scalar relevance score for every hole–lemma pair within a file, produced by a linear head over the encoder's per-AST [SOS] representations.

## Method

QUILL is a sub-6M-parameter Transformer variant that replaces softmax attention with a linearized mechanism using a second-degree Taylor-polynomial feature map (emulating exponentiation) and injects tree-structure inductive biases via binary-tree positional encodings: each AST node's query and key vectors are rotated by position-dependent orthogonal matrices so that the dot product encodes the relative tree path between nodes. Variable references are resolved structurally—de Bruijn indices are replaced by pointers to their binder nodes, and the pointer is represented as the composition of the two nodes' positional matrices applied to a trainable embedding, yielding a name-free, α-equivalence-respecting representation. Inter-AST lemma references are handled by a dynamic embedding table populated in topological (dependency) order, where each lemma's [SOS] token serves as its aggregate summary. The model is trained with a multi-positive InfoNCE contrastive objective over hole–lemma pairs, using SwiGLU feed-forward layers, RMSNorm, and residual connections, with positional encodings shared across layers and heads.

## Results

QUILL achieves 50.2±0.5 AVEP and 40.3±0.4 R-PREC on the in-distribution Agda standard library evaluation set, where relevant lemmas constitute less than 1.5% of all candidates. Performance degrades gracefully under distribution shift: 38.7 AVEP on out-of-distribution stdlib files (≈2× larger), 27.0 AVEP on Unimath, and 22.5 AVEP on TypeTopology (both zero-shot transfer). The vanilla Transformer baseline, which removes all structural inductive biases, collapses to 10.9 AVEP and 3.7 R-PREC on the same in-distribution set—a roughly 4.6× gap in AVEP.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against external premise-selection systems; instead, it ablates its own architectural components against a vanilla Transformer baseline. QUILL outperforms the vanilla Transformer by 39.3 AVEP points (50.2 vs. 10.9) on the in-distribution set, and by 16.7 AVEP points (22.5 vs. 5.8) on the most challenging zero-shot set (TypeTopology). Among the individual ablations, removing the structural de Bruijn variable resolution is the most damaging single change (−14.4 AVEP), followed by removing the tree positional encoding (−5.7 AVEP) and the Taylor-expansion feature map (−3.2 AVEP). Removing all three simultaneously (reducing QUILL to a standard Transformer) causes a catastrophic 39.3-point drop, confirming that the structural inductive biases—rather than raw model capacity—are the primary driver of performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two lines of work: (1) the growing body of ML-guided theorem proving, where the dominant paradigm has shifted toward LLM-based autoregressive models operating on string representations (LeanStep, lean-gym, Lisa, Magnushammer), and (2) the smaller but principled line of structurally-faithful encoders using tree/graph architectures (Wang et al. 2017, Paliwal et al. 2020, Blaauwbroek et al. 2024). QUILL departs from both: it rejects the nominal, sequence-based representation in favor of a fully structural encoding that resolves de Bruijn indices to positional-encoding-based pointers, and it combines linear attention (Katharopoulos et al.) with algebraic tree positional encodings (Kogkalidis et al.) to handle variable-sized ASTs efficiently. The training objective is a multi-positive variant of InfoNCE (Oord et al.), and the feature map follows the Taylor-expansion design of Arora et al. (2023).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper fills a concrete ecosystem gap by releasing the first ML-ready dataset for Agda (AGDA2TRAIN), extracted at sub-type-level resolution, and demonstrates that a 6M-parameter, 26MB model can perform premise selection competitively without any LLM or pretraining — a practical win for local, low-resource deployment. The ablation study cleanly isolates the contribution of each structural component (variable resolution > tree PE > Taylor expansion), reinforcing the paper's central thesis that nominal embeddings are fundamentally misaligned with dependent-type structure. Open questions include: whether the approach extends to other proof assistants or unifying frameworks (Dedukti), how to handle mutual inductions (currently excluded), and whether the unidirectional pipeline can be made interactive with Agda's auto for end-to-end proof completion.
