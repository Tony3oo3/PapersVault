## Problem

Approximate gradient methods for local Bayesian optimization (e.g., GIBO) only exploit the posterior distribution of the gradient at a single point, ignoring most of the information encoded in the Gaussian process surrogate over the local region. Alternative approaches like MPD attempt multi-step descent but suffer from numerical instability and suboptimal performance. The paper asks whether a local exploitation strategy exists that fully leverages GP posterior information while still guaranteeing convergence to local optima.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20244150/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The algorithm operates under a zeroth-order oracle: at each iteration it receives only noisy scalar evaluations y = f(x) + ε with ε ~ N(0, σ²) over a bounded domain X ⊂ ℝᵈ, with no access to gradients or higher-order derivatives. Each iteration involves a batch of b_t^(1) resampled points at the current location plus b_t^(2) new exploration points, followed by a single exploitation step that selects the next point by minimizing the UCB over the full design space. The output is a sequence of points converging to a local optimum, with the final iterate x_T = argmin_x μ_{D_T}(x) + β_T σ_{D_T}(x) reported as the solution.

## Method

The key insight is that the UCB bound μ_D(x) + βσ_D(x) is tighter than the quadratic upper bounds used in gradient descent (Eq. 1) and GIBO (Eq. 2), because the GP standard deviation term does not grow quadratically with distance, allowing the minimizer to be further from the current point and thus achieve a lower function value. MinUCB replaces GIBO's gradient descent step x_{t+1} = x_t − η_t ∇μ_{D_t}(x_t) with x_{t+1} = argmin_x μ_{D_t}(x) + β_t σ_{D_t}(x), while retaining GIBO's trace-based local exploration acquisition function, and is proven to converge to local optima at a polynomial rate in T and d. LA-MinUCB further improves the exploration step with a look-ahead acquisition function E_{y_Z} min_x UCB(x, D_{t−1}, Z, y_Z), which is shown to be one-step Bayes-optimal and eliminates the need for any gradient estimation entirely, behaving as a regularized Knowledge Gradient where the σ term prevents over-exploration.

## Results

The paper evaluates MinUCB and LA-MinUCB on synthetic GP functions (dimensions 25, 50, 100; 500 evaluations) and MuJoCo RL tasks (CartPole-v1, Swimmer-v1, Hopper-v1). Results are presented as progressive optimized reward curves (Figures 2 and 3) rather than tabulated numbers. LA-MinUCB achieves the fastest convergence and best final reward across all settings, with MinUCB also outperforming GIBO but lagging behind LA-MinUCB. On the 33-parameter Hopper-v1 task, LA-MinUCB shows the most pronounced advantage, reaching a better local optimum faster than all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GIBO [22], both MinUCB and LA-MinUCB achieve faster convergence and comparable or better final reward; GIBO reaches near-optimal values but its conservative gradient-descent step makes it significantly slower, especially in high dimensions. Against MPD [23], LA-MinUCB and MinUCB avoid the numerical instability that causes MPD to diverge after an initially rapid ascent, resulting in more reliable final performance. Against TurBO [7], both proposed methods clearly outperform in high-dimensional settings (d=50, 100), where TurBO's trust-region approach degrades substantially. LA-MinUCB consistently dominates all three baselines on the MuJoCo tasks, with the gap being most visible on Hopper-v1 (33 parameters), while MinUCB shows a smaller but still consistent advantage over GIBO in the same settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the local Bayesian optimization line initiated by GIBO (Muller et al., 2021) and its convergence analysis (Wu et al., 2024), replacing the gradient descent exploitation step with UCB minimization. It departs from MPD (Nguyen et al., 2022), which attempted to improve local exploitation through multi-step descent but suffers from numerical instability, and from TurBO (Eriksson et al., 2019), which uses trust regions. The LA-MinUCB algorithm draws structurally from Knowledge Gradient (Frazier, 2018) but adds a posterior standard deviation term as a local regularization, and the paper explicitly argues that efficient local BO need not rely on gradient estimates at all.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key conceptual contribution is showing that UCB minimization—a standard global BO acquisition function—serves as a tighter and more informative local exploitation objective than gradient descent under a GP surrogate, opening a new design axis for local BO algorithms. The one-step Bayes-optimality of LA-MinUCB (Theorem 3) provides a clean theoretical justification for a greedy local strategy without gradients. However, the convergence proof for LA-MinUCB is left open, the empirical gains over GIBO are modest (LA-MinUCB is 'slightly better'), and the practical advantage is most visible in reinforcement learning tasks rather than synthetic benchmarks, limiting the breadth of the evidence.
