## Problem

Existing molecular diffusion models (e.g., GeoDiff) inject uniform Gaussian noise into every atomic coordinate independently, treating each atom as an isolated particle and ignoring the structural dependencies within molecular substructures. This makes it difficult for the denoising network to capture the joint distribution of global 3D geometry and local substructure, limiting downstream performance on conformation-sensitive tasks such as molecular force prediction.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240932/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a 2D topological molecular graph (atom and bond feature matrices) together with a 3D conformation (atomic coordinate matrix R ∈ R^{|V|×3}). During training it produces a masked noise prediction (only for atoms in the selected subgraph) and a binary subgraph mask vector; during inference it generates a full 3D conformation and, as a byproduct, molecular node representations from the shared SE(3)-equivariant Graph Field Network encoder. The subgraph is sampled from a torsional-based decomposition that splits the molecule along rotatable bonds, yielding complementary mask pairs.

## Method

SubgDiff modifies the DDPM forward process by introducing a binary mask vector s_t that restricts Gaussian noise injection to a randomly selected connected subgraph at each step, leaving unselected atoms unchanged. Three techniques make this tractable: (i) a subgraph predictor (node classifier sharing the encoder with the noise network) is trained with a BCE loss to identify which subgraph is being diffused, explicitly forcing the encoder to encode substructure information; (ii) expectation-state diffusion replaces the previous state R^{t-1} with its expectation over the mask history, yielding a closed-form posterior that depends only on the current mask s_t rather than the full series s_{1:t}, enabling step-by-step sampling; (iii) k-step same-subgraph diffusion holds the same mask for k consecutive steps before resampling, accumulating enough noise contrast for the subgraph predictor to distinguish masked from unmasked regions. The training objective is a masked denoising loss (‖diag(s_t)(ε − ε_θ)‖²) plus a weighted BCE subgraph prediction loss, and the model is plugged into the MoleculeSDE multimodal pretraining framework (2D→3D and 3D→2D) for self-supervised representation learning.

## Results

On MD17 force prediction (MAE, lower is better), SubgDiff achieves 0.880 on Aspirin, 0.252 on Benzene, and 0.325 on Naphthalene, outperforming the strongest baseline MoleculeSDE(VE) (1.112, 0.304, 0.455) by 21–29% across all eight molecules. On MoleculeNet 2D property prediction, SubgDiff reaches 74.85 average ROC-AUC, surpassing MoleculeSDE(VP) (73.73) and GraphMVP (73.66). For conformation generation on GEOM-QM9, SubgDiff with only 500 diffusion steps achieves 89.78% COV-R and 0.2417 Å MAT-R, beating GeoDiff with 5000 steps (80.36% COV-R, 0.2820 Å MAT-R) on 5 out of 8 metrics—a 10× sampling speedup.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SubgDiff achieves the best MAE on all eight MD17 molecules, with the largest margins over MoleculeSDE(VE) [23] (e.g., 0.880 vs. 1.112 on Aspirin, 0.325 vs. 0.455 on Naphthalene) and GeoSSL [24] (0.880 vs. 1.107 on Aspirin). On MoleculeNet 2D tasks, SubgDiff (74.85 avg ROC-AUC) outperforms MoleculeSDE(VP) [23] (73.73), GraphMVP [22] (73.66), 3D InfoMax [44] (72.47), and all contrastive/masked baselines including MolCLR [47], InfoGraph [45], and AttrMask/ContextPred [13]. For conformation generation, SubgDiff with 500 steps (89.78% COV-R, 0.2417 Å MAT-R) substantially outperforms GeoDiff [52] at the same 500 steps (80.20%, 0.3617 Å) and even beats GeoDiff at 5000 steps on 5 of 8 metrics, demonstrating a 10× sampling efficiency gain. In cross-domain generalization (train on Drugs, test on QM9), SubgDiff achieves 83.50% COV-R versus GeoDiff's 74.94%, and all in-domain baselines (CVGAE [26], GraphDG [39], CGCF [50], ConfVAE [51], GeoMol [8]) score below 79%. The denoising pretraining baseline of Zaidi et al. [56] (1.364 MAE on Aspirin) is notably weaker than SubgDiff, confirming that substructure-aware diffusion is critical for force prediction. SubgDiff also outperforms the vanilla GeoDiff pretraining on QM9 quantum property prediction (Table 9), winning on 10 of 12 tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

SubgDiff extends the DDPM/GeoDiff line of molecular conformation diffusion by introducing a discrete subgraph mask into the continuous Gaussian forward process, making it the first diffusion model to fuse subgraph-level (discrete) structure with atom-level (continuous) noise for molecular learning. It builds directly on GeoDiff's geometric diffusion framework and MoleculeSDE's multi-modal pretraining architecture, while borrowing the torsional-based decomposition from Torsional Diffusion to define the subgraph sampling distribution. The two supporting techniques—expectation state diffusion and k-step same-subgraph diffusion—are novel contributions that address practical challenges (inaccessible mask history during sampling, and small per-step noise making subgraph prediction difficult) that arise specifically from the subgraph masking design.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that explicitly preserving substructure during diffusion-based pretraining yields consistently better molecular representations, particularly for 3D-conformation-sensitive tasks like force prediction, suggesting that the atom-independence assumption in prior diffusion models is a meaningful bottleneck. The 10× sampling efficiency gain (500 steps matching or exceeding 5000-step GeoDiff) is practically significant for conformation generation workloads. However, the method does not align with the Boltzmann distribution, cannot estimate conformation likelihoods, and requires matching training/sampling step counts, limiting its use in thermodynamic applications. Open questions include whether more chemically-informed mask distributions (beyond torsional bonds) and extension to protein secondary structures would further improve representation quality.
