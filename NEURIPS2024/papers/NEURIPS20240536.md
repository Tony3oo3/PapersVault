## Problem

Prior convergence analyses of BFGS either established only local asymptotic superlinear rates or provided global guarantees without explicit non-asymptotic bounds. The few existing global non-asymptotic results relied on exact line search or yielded rates no better than gradient descent, leaving the global convergence behavior of BFGS with the practically standard Armijo-Wolfe inexact line search uncharacterized.

## Input / Output

The analysis takes a μ-strongly convex, L-smooth objective f (optionally with M-Lipschitz Hessian), an arbitrary initial point x_0, a symmetric positive definite initial Hessian approximation B_0, and Armijo-Wolfe parameters α, β with 0 < α < β < 1 and α < 1/2. The output is a three-phase convergence characterization: a condition-number-dependent linear rate (1−1/κ)^t, a condition-number-independent linear rate (1−2α(1−β)/3)^t, and a global superlinear rate O((C'/t)^t), each with explicit iteration thresholds depending on Ψ(B_0), the dimension d, and the initial suboptimality C_0. The step size is selected via a log bisection algorithm whose per-iteration cost is also bounded.

## Method

The paper introduces a unified decomposition (Proposition 3.1) expressing the per-iteration function value contraction as a product of four terms: a sufficient-decrease factor p_t, a gradient-to-suboptimality ratio q_t, a curvature ratio n_t, and an angle-to-curvature term cos²(θ_t)/m_t. By choosing the weight matrix P as LI or ∇²f(x*), different lower bounds on these products yield the three convergence phases. The key insight is that the Armijo-Wolfe conditions give p_t ≥ α and n_t ≥ 1−β, which eliminates the √κ penalty present in exact line search analyses, while the potential function Ψ(A) = Tr(A) − d − log Det(A) tracks Hessian approximation quality and decays monotonically. For the superlinear phase, the paper shows that the unit step size η_t = 1 satisfies Armijo-Wolfe for all but O(Ψ(B̃_0) + C_0κ) iterations, at which point tighter bounds p_t ≥ 1 − (1+C_t)/(2ρ_t) and n_t ≥ 1/((1+C_t)ρ_t) drive the contraction factor to zero superlinearly.

## Results

For a μ-strongly convex function with L-Lipschitz gradient (condition number κ = L/μ), BFGS with Armijo-Wolfe line search achieves a global linear rate of (1 − 1/κ)^t from the first iteration when B₀ = LI, matching the gradient descent rate. Under the additional assumption that the Hessian is M-Lipschitz, BFGS achieves a condition-number-independent linear rate of (1 − 1/3)^t after O(dκ + C₀κ) iterations (for B₀ = LI) or O((1+C₀)d log κ + C₀κ) iterations (for B₀ = μI). A global superlinear rate of O((C′/t)^t) is also established, where C′ depends on κ, d, and the initial suboptimality C₀. The total iteration complexity for B₀ = LI is O(min{κ log(1/ε), (d+C₀)κ + log(1/ε), superlinear term}), consistently improving upon the O(κ log(1/ε)) complexity of gradient descent.

| B₀ (general) | Superlinear | ((Ψ(B̃₀)+C₀Ψ(B̄₀)+C₀κ)/t)^t | Ψ(B̃₀) + C₀Ψ(B̄₀) + C₀κ |

where Ψ(A) = Tr(A) − d − log Det(A), B̄₀ = B₀/L, B̃₀ = ∇²f(x*)^{-1/2} B₀ ∇²f(x*)^{-1/2}, and C₀ measures the weighted initial distance to the optimum.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is gradient descent, which achieves a linear rate of (1 − 1/κ)^t. BFGS with Armijo-Wolfe and B₀ = LI matches this rate from iteration 1 (Corollary 4.2), while B₀ = μI matches it after d log κ iterations. Compared to the prior exact-line-search analysis in [38], which gave a rate of (1 − e^{−Ψ(B₀)/t} · 1/(κ(1+√κ)))^t, the Armijo-Wolfe result eliminates the √κ penalty in the denominator, yielding the cleaner (1 − 1/κ)^t rate. The earlier inexact-line-search result in [40] only established a global linear rate of (1 − μ²/L²)^t = (1 − 1/κ²)^t, which is strictly slower than gradient descent; the present work improves this to (1 − 1/κ)^t. The condition-number-free rate of (1 − 1/3)^t (Theorem 5.2) has no counterpart in any prior global analysis of BFGS and represents the first result showing BFGS can escape the κ-dependence entirely. Numerical experiments on a cubic test function confirm that BFGS with B₀ = μI transitions to superlinear convergence around t ≈ d, while B₀ = LI transitions later (around t ≈ dκ), consistent with the theoretical thresholds.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the non-asymptotic global convergence framework of Jin, Jiang, and Mokhtari [38]—which analyzed BFGS with exact line search—to the practically dominant Armijo-Wolfe inexact line search setting. It reuses the potential function Ψ(A) from Byrd and Nocedal [32] as the central analytical tool, but replaces the exact-line-search lower bounds (which introduced a √κ penalty) with Armijo-Wolfe parameter-based bounds (α, β), yielding tighter rates. Prior inexact-line-search analyses (e.g., Rodomanov [40]) provided only local superlinear or weaker global linear rates, while classic quasi-Newton theory (Dennis-Moré, Broyden) established only asymptotic guarantees without explicit iteration counts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to provide a complete global complexity characterization of BFGS with the line search actually used in practice, closing a long-standing gap between asymptotic theory and non-asymptotic guarantees. The condition-number-independent linear rate under Lipschitz Hessian (rate depends only on α, β) is a notable theoretical advance suggesting BFGS can outperform gradient descent in well-conditioned-Hessian regimes. Key limitations include restriction to strongly convex objectives and to BFGS specifically; extending to general convexity or the full Broyden class (DFP, SR1) remains open, as does understanding whether the initialization trade-off (B₀=LI vs. B₀=μI) observed here persists in high-dimensional or non-strongly-convex settings.
