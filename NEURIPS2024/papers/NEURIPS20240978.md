## Problem

Existing delta-compression methods (low-rank approximation and uniform 1-bit quantization like BitDelta) significantly degrade the performance of task-specific fine-tuned LLMs (e.g., math and code models), even though they achieve near-lossless results on chat models. This gap is unexplored in prior work, which only validated compression on chat LLMs, leaving a critical question: how to compress delta weights at the same 1/16 ratio while preserving the specialized capabilities that make fine-tuned models valuable.

## Input / Output

The method takes a full-precision (FP16/BF16) delta weight matrix ΔW = θ_a − θ_b for each layer of the aligned model and produces a mixed-precision SVD representation at a fixed compression ratio α = 1/16. The compressed delta is stored as quantized singular vectors (U, V) at varying bit-widths plus a small diagonal singular-value matrix Σ. At inference, the aligned model is reconstructed as θ̂_a = θ_b + Δ̂, requiring only one backbone copy plus N compressed deltas instead of N full models.

## Method

Delta-CoMe performs a training-free, mixed-precision compression of delta weights by exploiting the long-tail distribution of singular values. Each delta matrix is decomposed via SVD, and singular vectors are partitioned into groups by their singular-value magnitude: the top group (largest singular values) is quantized at 8-bit, the middle group at 3-bit, and the tail at 2-bit, with extremely small singular values omitted entirely (0-bit). For k > 1 groups, GPTQ (group_size=128) is used; for 1-bit, BitDelta is applied. The default '8+3+2' triple-precision setting is selected via a greedy search that fixes the total delta size to match BitDelta's 1/16 ratio. A custom Triton kernel achieves ~3× inference speedup over the PyTorch implementation, enabling up to 50 compressed models on a single 80 GB A100 GPU.

## Results

On 7B aligned models across eight tasks (GSM8K, MATH, HumanEval, MBPP, SafetyBench, TruthfulQA, GQA, TextVQA), Delta-CoMe achieves an average score of 53.2 at a 1/16 compression ratio, closely matching the full aligned model's 53.5. On 13B models, Delta-CoMe reaches 53.5 average versus 54.5 for the uncompressed aligned model. The method also achieves over 10× GPU memory savings, enabling up to 50 models on a single 80 GB A100 GPU, and a ~3× inference speedup via a custom Triton kernel.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Delta-CoMe outperforms the SVD-based Low-Rank baseline (Ryu et al., 2023) by 5.4 points on average (53.2 vs. 47.8) across all eight tasks on 7B models, with the largest gaps on GSM8K (+10.4) and MATH (+2.3). Against BitDelta (Liu et al., 2024), Delta-CoMe leads by 3.9 points on average (53.2 vs. 49.3); notably, BitDelta is near-lossless on chat tasks (59.3 vs. 59.5 on SafetyBench) but degrades significantly on math (45.6 vs. 53.6 on GSM8K) and code (57.3 vs. 67.1 on HumanEval), a failure mode the original BitDelta paper did not investigate. In the delta-compression vs. delta-tuning comparison (Table 6), Delta-CoMe achieves 41.9 average versus LoRA's 29.8, a 12.1-point margin, demonstrating that post-hoc compression of a fully fine-tuned model substantially outperforms parameter-efficient fine-tuning at the same delta size. The method also generalizes across backbones: on Mistral-7B (OpenChat) and Llama-3-8B (Instruct), Delta-CoMe scores 66.8 average versus 57.8 (Low-Rank) and 62.0 (BitDelta).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two delta-compression paradigms: SVD-based low-rank approximation (Ryu et al., 2023) and uniform low-bit quantization (BitDelta, Liu et al., 2024). Rather than treating these as competing approaches, Delta-CoMe unifies them by decomposing delta weights via SVD and then applying GPTQ-style quantization at varying bit-widths to different groups of singular vectors, with higher bits allocated to vectors corresponding to larger singular values. This is the first application of mixed-precision quantization specifically to delta weights, as prior mixed-precision work (SpQR, Agile-Quant, layer-wise schemes) operated on full model weights at the token or layer level. The paper also positions itself against delta-tuning (LoRA), arguing that post-hoc compression of a fully fine-tuned model is more effective than parameter-efficient fine-tuning for multi-model serving.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: Delta-CoMe enables deploying up to 50 aligned 7B models on a single 80GB GPU (vs. ~3 without compression), making multi-tenant LLM serving feasible on commodity hardware. A key open question is the compression boundary—performance degrades noticeably at 32× compression, and the paper does not explore whether adaptive per-layer or per-task bit allocation could push this further. The finding that BitDelta's 1-bit quantization is near-lossless for chat models but significantly degrades math and code LLMs challenges the generality of the 'one bit suffices' claim and suggests task-specific compression strategies may be necessary. The authors also note their Triton kernel is basic compared to more advanced lookup-table kernels, leaving room for further inference speedups.
