## Problem

GNNs overfit and generalize poorly in Few-Shot Node Classification (FSNC) where only a handful of labeled instances exist per novel class. Sharpness-Aware Minimization (SAM) can improve generalization by seeking flat minima, but its two forward-backward steps per iteration double training cost, and no prior work has adapted SAM to exploit graph structure for acceleration.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20244206/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes an attributed graph G=(V,E) with node feature matrix X∈R^{n×d₀}, adjacency matrix A, and a small support set S of labeled nodes from novel classes (e.g., 3 or 5 shots per class in N-way tasks). It outputs predicted class labels for query nodes Q in the novel classes. A key structural detail is that message-passing is active during the perturbation phase but removed during the minimization phase, with MP reintroduced only at inference time.

## Method

FGSAM splits SAM's two-step update asymmetrically: the GNN (with message-passing) computes the sharpness-aware perturbation ε̂, while a PeerMLP (the same GNN with MP removed, equivalent to a plain MLP) minimizes the perturbed loss, cutting per-step cost by ~8× on the minimization pass. The gradient already computed during the GNN perturbation step is reused to inject topology information into the minimization objective at near-zero extra cost (Eq. 6). FGSAM+ further reduces cost by performing the full GNN perturbation only every k steps and, in intermediate steps, reusing preserved flatness-gradient g_v and topology-gradient g_G (both shown to change slowly) to approximate the FGSAM gradient, yielding a method that is faster than the base Adam optimizer in most settings. A CSBM-based theorem (Thm 4.1) justifies the design by proving that for linear classifiers on synthetic graphs, the optimal decision boundary is identical with or without message-passing, so the MLP minimization does not lose the optimal solution.

## Results

On the FSNC task (5-way 3-shot), FGSAM+ achieves the best average accuracy across all four baselines: 67.71% on CoraFull (vs. 64.32% Adam, 64.75% SAM), 76.95% on DBLP (vs. 76.15% Adam, 76.77% SAM), and 43.88% on ogbn-arXiv (vs. 40.64% Adam, 41.72% SAM). Critically, FGSAM+ is faster than Adam in most settings (e.g., 6.51s vs. 9.48s for Meta-GCN on CoraFull; 1.83s vs. 1.89s for GPN), while SAM requires roughly 2× the training time of Adam. On standard node classification, FGSAM improves average accuracy over Adam by 1.6% (GCN: 65.73% vs. 64.11%) and 1.7% (GraphSAGE: 70.05% vs. 68.40%), with the largest gains on heterophilic graphs (e.g., Cornell: 67.30% vs. 61.08% for GCN).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against standard SAM [12], FGSAM and FGSAM+ consistently achieve equal or higher accuracy while reducing training time by 30–50% (e.g., GPN on CoraFull: FGSAM+ 69.40% at 1.83s vs. SAM 67.28% at 3.62s). Compared to ESAM [9], FGSAM outperforms it in both accuracy (69.54% vs. 67.32% on CoraFull 5N3K) and speed (2.33s vs. 3.75s), with the paper noting that ESAM's sorting overhead is disproportionately costly for smaller GNNs. Against LookSAM [23], FGSAM+ achieves higher accuracy (69.40% vs. 68.38% on CoraFull) and is faster (1.62s vs. 2.91s), attributed to the graph-specific gradient reuse of topology information. AE-SAM [19] is similarly outperformed by FGSAM in accuracy (69.54% vs. 67.48%) and speed (2.33s vs. 2.93s). Finally, in the prompt-based FSNC setting, FGSAM+ improves over ProG [33] by 0.83% accuracy and 0.68 F1 at 3-shot on Citeseer (60.33% vs. 59.50%), demonstrating transferability beyond standard training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts Sharpness-Aware Minimization (SAM) from the vision domain to graph neural networks, specifically for few-shot node classification. It builds on LookSAM's strategy of periodic exact perturbation with gradient reuse in intermediate steps, but departs fundamentally by using two different models—GNN for perturbation and MLP (PeerMLP) for minimization—exploiting the fact that GNNs without message-passing reduce to MLPs. Unlike prior SAM acceleration methods (ESAM, LookSAM, AE-SAM) that were not designed for graphs, FGSAM is the first SAM variant crafted to leverage graph structure, achieving the notable property of being faster than the base optimizer (Adam) while improving generalization. The CSBM-based theoretical analysis also extends prior GNN-MLP connection work by proving that linear classifiers converge to identical optimal solutions regardless of whether message-passing is applied.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that FGSAM+ provides a drop-in optimization replacement that is both faster and more accurate than Adam for GNN training in few-shot and standard node classification, removing the 2× computational overhead that has limited SAM's adoption in the graph domain. The counterintuitive finding that dropping message-passing during minimization (while retaining it for perturbation) outperforms full GNN-based SAM suggests the adversarial game imbalance in standard SAM is a real bottleneck, opening questions about whether similar model-asymmetry tricks apply to other optimization schemes. Limitations include that for architectures with substantial non-GNN components (e.g., TENT), FGSAM+ may still be slower than Adam, and the method has only been validated on node classification tasks rather than broader graph learning problems like link prediction or graph classification.
