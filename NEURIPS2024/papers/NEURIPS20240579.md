## Problem

Fitting recurrent neural networks to neural data has been largely restricted to deterministic transitions, yet trial-to-trial variability in recordings cannot generally be explained by initial-condition variability alone. While low-rank RNNs offer interpretable, tractable dynamics, it remained unclear how to fit stochastic low-rank RNNs to noisy high-dimensional observations, and how to efficiently analyze the resulting dynamics (e.g., finding all fixed points) for large networks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240579/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes noisy neural observations—spike counts binned into 10–25 ms bins, 64-channel EEG sampled at 160 Hz, or continuous rate data—along with optional time-varying conditioning inputs (e.g., reach target positions). It produces a fitted stochastic low-rank RNN (rank R ≤ N, with Euler-Maruyama discretization) that serves as a generative model: one can sample arbitrarily long trajectories, compute posterior latent states via filtering, and enumerate all fixed points of the learned dynamics. The observation model is modality-specific (Gaussian for EEG/rates, Poisson for spikes), and the latent dimensionality R is chosen to be much smaller than the number of recorded units.

## Method

The core approach fits stochastic low-rank RNNs by maximizing an ELBO estimated via variational sequential Monte Carlo (particle filtering). At each timestep, the proposal distribution adaptively interpolates between the RNN's predicted state F(z_{t-1}) and a data-inferred state from a learned causal CNN encoder, with the interpolation weight determined by the relative noise levels (analogous to a Kalman filter gain), enabling stable backpropagation through time without the fixed alpha of Generalized Teacher Forcing. For linear-Gaussian observations the optimal closed-form proposal is used directly; for non-linear observations (e.g., Poisson spikes) the encoder is jointly optimized. A second key contribution is a theoretical result: for low-rank RNNs with piecewise-linear activations, all fixed points can be found by solving at most O(N^R) linear systems of R equations (for fixed rank R), reducing the naive exponential (D+1)^N cost via Zaslavsky's theorem on hyperplane arrangements, since only a polynomial subset of linear regions is reachable within the R-dimensional column space of M.

## Results

On the 64-channel EEG dataset, the proposed stochastic low-rank RNN with SMC achieves the same reconstruction quality as GTF (D_stsp = 2.1±0.1 vs 2.1±0.2; D_H = 0.11±0.01 vs 0.11±0.01) while reducing the latent dimensionality from 16 to just 3 and using fewer parameters (3,920 vs 17,952). On the Neural Latents Benchmark (MC_Maze), the method achieves vel R² = 0.8902 (non-causal encoder) matching LFADS (0.8906) and NDT (0.8897), with a latent dimensionality of 36 versus 100 (LFADS) and 274 (NDT). On the macaque reaching task, a rank-5 model decodes reach velocity with R² = 0.90, and on the rat hippocampal linear-track data, posterior latents predict position with R² = 0.79±0.05.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GTF [8] on EEG, the proposed method matches reconstruction quality exactly (D_stsp = 2.1, D_H = 0.11) while using only 3 latents instead of 16 and ~4.5× fewer parameters, demonstrating that stochastic transitions allow much simpler underlying dynamics. On the Neural Latents Benchmark, the method slightly trails LFADS [7] and NDT [70] on co-bps (0.3407 vs 0.3554/0.3597) but matches them on vel R² (0.8902 vs 0.8906/0.8897) with far lower dimensionality (36 vs 100/274); it clearly outperforms GPFA [69] and SLDS [30] on all metrics. On the HPC-2 hippocampal dataset, LFADS-generated latents fail to show coherence with the LFP, whereas the proposed method's posterior latents exhibit strong LFP coherence despite being trained solely on spikes. For fixed-point enumeration, the analytic method (Proposition 1) finds all 17 fixed points of a test RNN with a fixed, predictable cost, while the approximate method of Eisenmann et al. [25] fails to find all fixed points within the same number of matrix inverses and can fail to converge entirely in some settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies three previously separate threads: low-rank RNNs for interpretable neural dynamics (Mastrogiuseppe & Ostojic, 2018), variational sequential Monte Carlo for state-space model learning (Le et al., 2018; Naesseth et al., 2018), and fixed-point analysis of piecewise-linear RNNs (Durstewitz, 2017; Brenner et al., 2022). It explicitly positions itself as a stochastic generalization of Generalized Teacher Forcing (Hess et al., 2023), replacing GTF's fixed interpolation coefficient with an adaptive, data-driven Kalman-gain-like weighting, and extending the framework to non-invertable (e.g., Poisson) observations via a learned encoder. The theoretical contribution reduces the exponential-cost fixed-point enumeration of Durstewitz to polynomial cost by exploiting the low-rank constraint through Zaslavsky's theorem on hyperplane arrangements.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The demonstration that stochastic transitions reduce required latent dimensionality from 16 to 3 on EEG while matching reconstruction quality provides a principled argument that much of the 'complexity' attributed to deterministic neural dynamics may actually be stochasticity, with broad implications for how neuroscientists interpret population recordings. The polynomial-time fixed-point algorithm makes dynamical analysis tractable for large low-rank RNNs with a completeness guarantee that approximate methods lack. Key limitations include the assumption that recurrent noise lies in the column space of M (ruling out arbitrary full-space covariances in the nonlinear setting), the use of Euler-Maruyama discretization rather than adjoint methods, and the restriction to Gaussian latent noise. Open questions include extending to non-Gaussian and spiking noise processes, multi-modal (spike + LFP) fitting, and whether the correlated-noise assumption is sufficient for capturing biologically relevant variability.
