## Problem

Existing HDR novel view synthesis methods rely on NeRF-based ray tracing, requiring ~9 hours of training and ~8.2 s per inference, which precludes real-time applications. While 3D Gaussian Splatting offers 1000× faster rendering, it cannot handle HDR imaging: its dynamic range is capped at [0,255], it fails to converge when trained on images with varying exposures, and it provides no mechanism to control the exposure of rendered views. This paper presents the first Gaussian Splatting-based framework for 3D HDR reconstruction.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242185/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view LDR images (400×400) captured at 35 viewpoints across 5 discrete exposure times, along with a user-specified exposure time Δt that controls the brightness of the rendered LDR output. It produces both an HDR novel view (unbounded radiance) and an LDR novel view at the requested exposure, plus the learned 3D Gaussian point cloud. A key preprocessing step recalibrates camera intrinsics and extrinsics via SfM because the original HDR-NeRF datasets only provide poses in normalized device coordinates, which are incompatible with explicit 3D Gaussian projection.

## Method

HDR-GS introduces a Dual Dynamic Range (DDR) Gaussian point cloud where each Gaussian stores spherical-harmonic coefficients that model log-domain HDR color (avoiding numerical overflow from direct multiplication with exposure), and three independent per-channel MLPs that learn the inverse camera response function g_θ to tone-map log(HDR) + log(Δt) into LDR color. The HDR and LDR colors are then fed into two Parallel Differentiable Rasterization (PDR) pipelines that simultaneously render the HDR and LDR images via standard alpha-blending of projected Gaussians. Training is supervised with a weighted L1 + D-SSIM loss on LDR images plus a μ-law-compressed HDR consistency loss (synthetic scenes only), and the point cloud is initialized from SfM-computed 3D points rather than random placement. The log-domain formulation converts the multiplicative exposure relationship into an additive one, which the ablation shows improves PSNR by up to 12 dB over a linear-domain MLP tone-mapper.

## Results

On the synthetic HDR-NeRF dataset, HDR-GS achieves 41.10 dB PSNR on LDR-OE, 36.33 dB on LDR-NE, and 38.31 dB on HDR, outperforming the SOTA NeRF-based method HDR-NeRF by 2.03 dB (LDR-OE) and 1.91 dB (HDR). On the real dataset, HDR-GS reaches 35.47 dB PSNR on LDR-OE, surpassing HDR-NeRF by 3.84 dB. In terms of efficiency, HDR-GS runs at 126 fps inference speed (≈1000× faster than HDR-NeRF's 0.122 fps) and requires only 34 minutes of training (6.3% of HDR-NeRF's 542 minutes).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the SOTA NeRF-based method HDR-NeRF [14], HDR-GS wins on LDR-OE (41.10 vs 39.07 dB, +2.03 dB) and HDR (38.31 vs 36.40 dB, +1.91 dB) on synthetic data, and on LDR-OE (35.47 vs 31.63 dB, +3.84 dB) on real data, while slightly losing on LDR-NE synthetic (36.33 vs 37.53 dB, −1.20 dB) but winning on LDR-NE real (31.66 vs 31.43 dB, +0.23 dB). Against the original 3DGS [15], HDR-GS gains a massive 21.64 dB on LDR-OE and 17.36 dB on LDR-NE (synthetic), because 3DGS cannot handle multi-exposure training and produces severe color distortion. Against NeRF [13] and NeRF-W [77], HDR-GS leads by over 11 dB and 11 dB respectively on LDR-OE (synthetic), and additionally renders HDR views and supports controllable exposure—capabilities neither baseline possesses. The efficiency gap is dramatic: HDR-GS trains in 34 min vs 542 min for HDR-NeRF (6.3%) and infers at 126 fps vs 0.122 fps (~1000× faster).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper transplants the 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) rendering paradigm into the HDR novel view synthesis domain, where prior work has been dominated by NeRF-based ray tracing (notably HDR-NeRF). The core architectural contribution is a Dual Dynamic Range Gaussian point cloud that uses spherical harmonics to model HDR color and three small MLPs to learn a log-domain camera response function (CRF) for tone-mapping to LDR, following the classical Debevec and Malik calibration formulation. The paper also addresses an infrastructure gap by recalibrating camera parameters from the NDC system (incompatible with explicit 3D representations) to world coordinates via SfM, establishing a data foundation for future 3DGS-based HDR research.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 1000× inference speedup (126 fps vs. 0.122 fps) combined with superior quality (3.84 dB on LDR, 1.91 dB on HDR over HDR-NeRF) makes real-time HDR rendering practically feasible for AR/VR, film, and gaming applications that were previously blocked by NeRF's ray-tracing bottleneck. The recalibrated dataset and SfM initialization pipeline lower the barrier for subsequent 3DGS-based HDR work. Key limitations include the non-trivial memory footprint of millions of Gaussians (unaffordable on low-RAM mobile devices) and evaluation restricted to static scenes with only 35 viewpoints; open questions include extension to dynamic HDR scenes, larger-scale environments, and whether the per-scene MLP tone-mapper generalizes across different camera hardware.
