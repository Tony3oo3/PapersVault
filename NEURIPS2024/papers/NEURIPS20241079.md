## Problem

Offline safe RL with function approximation has traditionally required full data coverage, meaning the dataset must include state-action pairs visited by every possible policy—including all dangerous ones—making it impractical for safety-critical applications. Additionally, convex MDPs (which generalize standard RL to non-linear objectives like KL-divergence for imitation learning) lack theoretical guarantees in the offline setting because the Bellman equation fails without reward additivity. This paper is the first to provide a provable algorithm for offline convex CMDPs under only partial data coverage.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a finite offline dataset of (state, action, reward, cost) tuples collected i.i.d. from a behavior policy, along with a cost threshold τ and relaxed hyperparameters ζ and κ that encode distribution-shift and safety uncertainty. The behavior policy π_μ is either known or estimated via behavior cloning (counting state-action frequencies in the dataset). The output is a safe policy extracted from a learned marginalized importance weight function w, parameterized as a single-hidden-layer neural network, via the formula π(a|s) = w(s,a)·π_μ(a|s) / Σ_{a'} w(s,a')·π_μ(a'|s), with a uniform fallback when the denominator is zero.

## Method

The core reformulation replaces the occupancy-measure optimization with a marginalized importance weight (MIW) variable w(s,a) = d(s,a)/μ(s,a), sidestepping the Bellman equation failure in convex MDPs. An empirical relaxed problem is constructed where the validity constraint (global balance) and safety constraint are loosened by uncertainty parameters ζ = O(B_w/√n) and κ = O(L_g·B_w/√n), which capture the distribution shift between the offline data and the true environment. The POCC algorithm then solves this via a primal-dual subgradient method: the primal step performs projected gradient descent on w within a convex function class W, while dual steps update Lagrange multipliers for the validity and safety constraints. The key theoretical result shows that when iterations K ≥ n, both the objective gap and constraint violation scale as O(1/((1-γ)√n)), improving the prior state-of-the-art O(1/((1-γ)²√n)) by a factor of 1/(1-γ), under only π*-concentrability (partial coverage) rather than full coverage.

## Results

On SafetyGym (cost threshold = 1), the proposed method achieves the lowest cost across 4 out of 5 tasks while maintaining competitive rewards. On AntRun, it attains reward 0.6 with cost 0.01 (tied with COptiDICE on reward but 94× lower cost). On BallRun, it is the only method that is both safe (cost 0.0) and achieves positive reward (0.24), whereas all baselines violate the constraint by 2.3×–3.5×. On FrozenLake, the method consistently finds the optimal safe path even with 100% random (safety-violating) data, while COptiDICE fails to learn a logical policy under the same conditions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against COptiDICE, the proposed method matches its reward on AntRun (0.60) while reducing cost from 0.94 to 0.01, and is the only safe method on BallRun (cost 0.0 vs. 3.52). On CarRun, PDCA edges out the proposed method slightly (0.91 vs. 0.90 reward, both with zero cost), while COptiDICE is close behind (0.87). Against CPQ, the proposed method wins on AntRun (0.60 vs. 0.03 reward) and BallRun (0.24 vs. 0.22, and CPQ is unsafe at cost 1.27), but CPQ achieves higher reward on BallCircle (0.64 vs. 0.39) while both remain safe. Against BEAR-Lag, the proposed method dominates on AntRun (0.60 vs. 0.15) and BallRun (0.24 vs. −0.47), with BEAR-Lag being unsafe on CarRun (cost 7.78) and BallRun (cost 5.03). Overall, the proposed method is the only algorithm that is simultaneously safe and high-reward on AntRun and BallRun, and is the safest method across all five tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the LP-based offline RL framework of Ozdaglar et al. (2023), which uses marginalized importance weights (MIW) to sidestep Bellman-equation-based methods. It extends the offline safe RL line (Hong et al. 2024, Chen et al. 2022, Le et al. 2019) in two key directions: replacing full data coverage with the weaker π*-concentrability (partial coverage) assumption, and generalizing from linear (standard) CMDP objectives to convex MDP objectives where the Bellman equation no longer holds. The convex MDP literature (Zhang et al. 2020b, Zahavy et al. 2021, Geist et al. 2022) has been almost entirely online, making this the first offline theoretical treatment of that setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result is practically significant because partial coverage is far more realistic in safety-critical domains—requiring a dataset to visit every hazardous state-action pair (full coverage) is often infeasible or dangerous. The 1/(1-γ) sample-complexity improvement over prior work is meaningful for high-discount settings common in long-horizon tasks. Open questions include whether the completeness assumption (Assumption 3) can be replaced by the weaker realizability assumption used in prior work, whether the approach extends to multiple constraints without additional complexity, and whether the theoretical guarantees hold under more realistic non-i.i.d. data collection. The experimental validation, while supportive, is limited to small grid-world and SafetyGym environments, leaving open the question of scalability to high-dimensional continuous control.
