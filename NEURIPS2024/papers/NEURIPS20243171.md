## Problem

Feature attribution explanations can predict labels far better than their selected values alone would suggest, a phenomenon called encoding, yet no general statistical characterization existed to identify or quantify this extra predictive power. Consequently, existing quantitative evaluations (ROAR, FRESH, EVAL-X) either fail to detect encoding at all or only detect it weakly, making them unreliable for selecting explanations whose values genuinely carry the predictive signal.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243171/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The evaluation method takes as input the joint distribution q(y, x) over labels and d-dimensional inputs, plus an explanation method e that maps x to a binary selection mask in {0,1}^d. The explanation x_{e(x)} is a pair consisting of the selection mask and the values at the selected positions, preserving positional information (the same value at different indices can carry different predictive meaning). The output is a single scalar score per explanation method, where higher is better, enabling ranking of competing explanation methods.

## Method

The paper defines encoding formally as a conditional dependence: an explanation encodes if y is not conditionally independent of the explanation indicator E_v = 1[e(x)=v] given the explanation's values x_v. To quantify this, ENCODE-METER computes the expected conditional mutual information I(E_v; y | x_v = a) over the explanation's sampling distribution. STRIPE-X then subtracts a weighted ENCODE-METER penalty (weight α) from the EVAL-X log-likelihood score, yielding STRIPE-X_α(q,e) = EVAL-X(q,e) − α·φ_q(e). The paper proves that for sufficiently large α, STRIPE-X is a strong detector: every non-encoding explanation scores strictly above every encoding explanation, while showing ROAR and FRESH are not even weak detectors and EVAL-X is weak but not strong. Empirically, STRIPE-X correctly flags encoding in simulated POSI/PRED/MARG constructions, in REAL-X-learned explanations on image patches, and in Llama 3-generated sentiment explanations that select adjectives while omitting the control-flow sentence that determines which adjective is relevant.

## Results

On the cats_vs_dogs image recognition task, ROAR and FRESH both assign the encoding explanations PRED and MARG the same score as the optimal explanation (ROAR: 0.69; FRESH: -0.23), confirming they are not even weak detectors of encoding. EVAL-X separates encoding from optimal (scoring MARG at -0.53 vs. optimal at -0.27) but still ranks MARG and PRED above the non-encoding 'fixed' explanation (-0.64), so it is not a strong detector. STRIPE-X correctly scores all three encoding constructions (POSI: -5.98, PRED: -1.40, MARG: -1.02) below the negative marginal entropy threshold of -0.69, while non-encoding explanations (optimal: -0.31, fixed: -0.64) score above it. In the LLM experiment, Llama 3's explanations receive a STRIPE-X score of -2.78, well below the -0.69 threshold, revealing that despite explicit prompting to avoid encoding, the LLM still encodes on >70% of examples.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three existing evaluation methods—ROAR, FRESH, and EVAL-X—against the proposed STRIPE-X on their ability to detect encoding explanations. ROAR and FRESH completely fail: both assign PRED and MARG the identical score as the optimal explanation (ROAR: 0.69; FRESH: -0.23), meaning they cannot even weakly detect encoding. EVAL-X improves by scoring all encoding explanations below the optimal (MARG: -0.53 vs. optimal: -0.27), establishing it as a weak detector, but it still ranks MARG (-0.53) and PRED (-0.51) above the non-encoding fixed explanation (-0.64), so it fails strong detection. STRIPE-X is the only method that achieves strong detection: it pushes all encoding explanations below the -0.69 negative-entropy threshold while keeping non-encoding explanations above it, with the largest gap for POSI (-5.98 vs. -0.31 for optimal). In the LLM sentiment analysis experiment, STRIPE-X reveals that Llama 3's explanations encode (score -2.78 vs. threshold -0.69), a finding that EVAL-X alone would not surface since it only provides a weak signal.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Jethani et al. [20] (EVAL-X), which first identified encoding in explanations and proposed the EVAL-X surrogate-model score, and on Hsia et al. [21], who showed that evaluation benchmarks can be gamed by encoding explanations. However, prior work only provided ad hoc examples of encoding (position-based, prediction-based, marginal) without a general statistical characterization. This paper formalizes encoding as a conditional dependence between the label and the explanation indicator given the explanation's values (Definition 1), unifying all prior examples under one definition, and introduces STRIPE-X by augmenting EVAL-X with an ENCODE-METER penalty based on instantaneous conditional mutual information.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first provable guarantees about which evaluation methods can and cannot detect encoding, showing that widely-used scores (ROAR, FRESH) fail even weak detection and that EVAL-X, while a weak detector, can still rank encoding explanations above non-encoding ones under reductive constraints. The finding that Llama 3 generates encoding explanations despite explicit prompting to include all relevant words raises practical concerns for the growing use of LLMs to produce rationales for smaller models. Open questions include extending the framework to free-text rationales, handling model misestimation in the ENCODE-METER, and clarifying whether explanations should target the data distribution or the model's conditional.
