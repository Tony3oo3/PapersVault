## Problem

State-of-the-art graph alignment methods are 'mediated': they reduce the NP-hard quadratic assignment problem (QAP) over adjacency matrices to a linear assignment over intermediate representations such as embeddings, spectra, or graph signals. This restriction to an intermediate space incurs information loss and predisposes methods to miss high-quality alignments. FUGAL addresses this gap by proposing an 'unrestricted' formulation that retains the full edge-aware QAP while still leveraging mediated structural features to guide optimization.

## Input / Output

FUGAL takes as input the adjacency matrices of two unlabelled, undirected graphs (of equal or unequal size; unequal sizes are handled by padding the smaller graph with isolated dummy nodes whose mappings are discarded). It also extracts four NETSIMILE structural features per node (degree, clustering coefficient, mean neighbor degree, mean neighbor clustering coefficient) to build a pairwise distance matrix D. The output is a binary permutation matrix P that defines a one-to-one node correspondence between the two graphs, obtained by rounding a quasi-permutation matrix via the Hungarian algorithm.

## Method

FUGAL formulates alignment as a unified objective combining the QAP term tr(APB^T P^T) on adjacency matrices with a LAP regularizer μ·tr(P^T D) on structural features, then relaxes the permutation constraint to the convex set of doubly stochastic matrices. The relaxed problem is solved by a customized Frank-Wolfe algorithm in which the linear minimization oracle is answered by the Sinkhorn-Knopp entropic optimal transport algorithm (O(n²)) rather than the Hungarian algorithm (O(n³)), yielding a doubly stochastic update at each step. A key mechanism is an iteratively increasing regularization parameter λ on tr(P^T(J−P)), which—by Theorem 2.5—steers the doubly stochastic solution toward a quasi-permutation matrix (entries near 0 or 1) without sacrificing the QAP objective. The final quasi-permutation is rounded to an exact permutation via the Hungarian algorithm, completing the node alignment in O(T·n³) total time.

## Results

On the ACM-DBLP co-authorship alignment task (6,325 shared authors), FUGAL achieves 48.7% accuracy, a 30% improvement over the closest baseline CONE (18.3%). On MultiMagna (yeast PPI network), FUGAL's advantage over S-GWL grows from 4% at 5% noise to 16% at 25% noise. On synthetic Newman-Watts graphs (1000 nodes, k=7) with 5% one-way noise, FUGAL attains a 60% accuracy gain over S-GWL, and at rewiring probability p=0.25, FUGAL reaches 92% accuracy versus S-GWL's 54%. FUGAL also achieves up to 3× speedup over S-GWL on larger networks, while S-GWL fails to terminate on ACM-DBLP within 5 hours.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FUGAL outperforms all 13 baselines across every evaluated dataset and noise condition. S-GWL is the closest competitor on most datasets: on MultiMagna, FUGAL leads by 4–16% depending on noise level, and on Newman-Watts graphs with 5% one-way noise, FUGAL gains 60% over S-GWL. CONE is the second-closest on Voles (83% vs. FUGAL's 90% at 80% edge retention) and ACM-DBLP (18.3% vs. 48.7%). FAQ and PARROT match FUGAL on the HighSchool dataset but degrade substantially on MultiMagna and Voles. On sparse graphs (p=0.25), FUGAL achieves 92% accuracy while S-GWL drops to 54%, and all other methods fail entirely. In terms of efficiency, FUGAL is up to 3× faster than S-GWL on larger networks, and S-GWL cannot scale to the ~10K-node ACM-DBLP graphs at all.

## Relation to Prior Work

FUGAL extends the doubly-stochastic relaxation framework of FAQ [42], which solved the QAP via Frank-Wolfe but addressed only the quadratic term. The key departure is the introduction of a 'unrestricted' formulation that retains the full adjacency-matrix QAP while augmenting it with a linear assignment regularizer built from NETSIMILE structural features, and replaces FAQ's Hungarian-based linear minimization step with a Sinkhorn-Knopp entropic-OT update for efficiency. This positions FUGAL between the 'mediated' methods (S-GWL, CONE, GRAMPA, IsoRank, etc.) that discard graph structure into embeddings, and the 'unmediated' methods (FAQ, GLAG) that relax constraints without supplementary guidance, arguing that the information loss in mediated approaches is the primary source of suboptimal alignments.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The consistent 4–60% accuracy advantage over the strongest baseline (S-GWL) across nine real-world and synthetic datasets, combined with O(n³) complexity and demonstrated scalability to ~10K-node graphs (ACM-DBLP), makes FUGAL a practical default for graph alignment in bioinformatics, social network analysis, and infrastructure mapping. The paper's broader-impact discussion rightly flags that improved alignment also aids de-anonymization attacks on sensitive networks, underscoring the need for anonymization before publication. Open questions include whether the per-dataset tuning of μ can be automated, whether the Sinkhorn-guided FW can be accelerated for graphs beyond 100K nodes, and whether the approach extends naturally to attributed or directed graphs without the current adjacency-matrix-only assumption.
