## Problem

Existing LLM-based agents for knowledge-intensive tasks suffer from uncontrollable free-form reasoning logic, static model capabilities that cannot adapt to new domains, and sparse outcome-only feedback that fails to guide intermediate reasoning steps. No prior agent simultaneously provides structured reasoning, domain adaptation, and fine-grained process-level human supervision.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20244006/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

AMOR takes a natural-language question and a text corpus knowledge base (e.g., Wikipedia articles, PubMed abstracts, or NLP papers) as input, and produces a final answer along with a structured reasoning process consisting of a sequence of (state, module output) pairs. The knowledge base is accessed hierarchically through retrieval tools (SearchDoc for document-level retrieval, SearchPsg for passage-level retrieval within a document, and NextDoc for additional documents), and the reasoning process is guided by special branch tokens (e.g., [NEXT], [FINISH], [RELEVANT], [IRRELEVANT], [ANSWERABLE], [UNANSWERABLE]) that determine state transitions within the FSM.

## Method

AMOR formalizes agent reasoning as a finite state machine (FSM) with disentangled modules (Decompose, Judge, Answer, Complete for LLM modules; SearchDoc, SearchPsg, NextDoc for tool modules) connected by a transition function driven by branch tokens, enabling structured multi-hop question decomposition and evidence collection. The LLM backbone uses a Module-Aware Mixture-of-Experts (MA-MoE) architecture where distinct FFN parameters in the final quarter of Transformer blocks are learned per module, balancing multi-task performance with inference efficiency. Training proceeds in two stages: a warm-up stage fine-tunes the MA-MoE model on ~50k examples constructed from four public QA datasets using standard language modeling loss, and an adaptation stage performs exploration-exploitation where the agent autonomously answers questions, collects per-module process feedback (binary right/wrong or refined outputs), and optimizes via KTO with MLE regularization. The process feedback mechanism is the key differentiator: unlike outcome feedback that only signals final-answer correctness, it provides direct supervision at each reasoning step, enabling targeted improvement of individual modules and significantly more data-efficient adaptation.

## Results

On HotpotQA, AMOR_Process* (L-7B) achieves 45.8 EM / 54.9 F1, outperforming the strongest fine-tuned baseline LUMOS (29.4 EM) by 16.4 EM points. On PubMedQA, AMOR_Process* reaches 81.1% ACC, and on QASPER it achieves 19.1 EM / 35.3 F1. Without any fine-tuning, AMOR w/o FT built on GPT-4 delivers 55.2 EM / 65.2 F1 on HotpotQA and 80.0% ACC on PubMedQA, improving 41.2% over ReAct with the same LLM. The FSM-based reasoning paradigm yields 30%–42% average improvements over ReAct across L-7B, GPT-3.5, and GPT-4 backbones.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AMOR dominates all seven baselines across all three datasets. Against ReAct, AMOR w/o FT improves by 41.9% (L-7B), 32.1% (GPT-3.5), and 41.2% (GPT-4) on average, demonstrating the FSM paradigm's advantage over free-form reasoning. Against CoT (GPT-4, 45.0 EM on HotpotQA), AMOR w/o FT (GPT-4) leads by 10.2 EM. Among fine-tuned baselines, AMOR_Process* (L-7B, 45.8 EM) outperforms LUMOS (29.4 EM) by 16.4 EM, FIREACT (26.2 EM) by 19.6 EM, AgentLM (22.0 EM) by 23.8 EM, and Self-RAG (22.4 EM) by 23.4 EM on HotpotQA. On PubMedQA, AMOR_Process* (81.1% ACC) surpasses the next-best fine-tuned method OneR* (75.3%) by 5.8 points. The ablation AMOR_Outcome* (40.8 EM) confirms that process feedback, not merely outcome feedback, drives the largest gains over the warm-up-only variant AMOR_WFT (33.6 EM).

## Relation to Prior Work

AMOR synthesizes ideas from three distinct research threads: (1) modular agent architectures (MetaGPT, LUMOS, ReWoo) that decompose tasks into specialized modules, (2) the Mixture-of-Experts paradigm (Shazeer et al.) repurposed here as a Module-Aware MoE to give each LLM module distinct FFN parameters, and (3) preference-based alignment (KTO) adapted to optimize non-differentiable process-feedback losses. It departs from free-form reasoning agents (ReAct, CoT, ToT) by replacing their undefined inter-step dependencies with an explicit finite state machine, and from prior fine-tuning approaches (AgentLM, FIREACT) that rely on outcome feedback from GPT-4 trajectories by introducing per-module process feedback collected during autonomous exploration.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that structured FSM-based reasoning with process feedback can substantially outperform both free-form prompting and outcome-feedback fine-tuning, particularly when built on open-source LLMs that lack the reasoning capacity of GPT-4. This offers a practical recipe for deploying capable knowledge agents without proprietary model access. Key limitations include the requirement for human-driven FSM design (the authors acknowledge this as a major constraint), evaluation restricted to text-corpus knowledge bases, and the use of silver (automatic) feedback rather than true human annotation in most experiments. Open questions include whether LLMs can autonomously instantiate FSMs for new tasks, and whether the process-feedback advantage generalizes to structured or multimodal knowledge bases.
