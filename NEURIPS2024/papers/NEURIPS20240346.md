## Problem

Existing convergence analyses of Adam either require bounded gradients, ignore the corrective terms, rely on random-reshuffling schemes, or restrict to coordinate-wise affine variance noise. No prior work established a high-probability convergence guarantee for full Adam (with corrective terms) under the standard affine variance noise model with potentially unbounded gradients, nor under the empirically validated (L₀, L_q)-smoothness condition that captures practical objective functions beyond L-smoothness.

## Input / Output

The analysis takes a stochastic gradient oracle satisfying an unbiasedness condition and a generalized sub-exponential noise model (A3) that simultaneously covers almost-surely affine variance noise, bounded noise, and sub-Gaussian noise, along with hyperparameters (β₁, β₂, η, ε) and an initial point x₁. The output is a high-probability bound (with probability ≥ 1−2δ) on the time-averaged squared gradient norm, yielding a Õ(1/√T) rate for full Adam and a noise-adaptive Õ(1/T + σ₀/√T) rate for Adam without corrective terms and RMSProp. The bound depends on the noise parameters σ₀, σ₁, the smoothness constants, and logarithmic factors in T/δ, but not on problem-specific quantities like the smoothness parameter L for the main Adam result.

## Method

The proof introduces a novel proxy step-size a_s constructed from a local gradient bound G_T(s) to decouple the stochastic gradient from the adaptive denominator, enabling a martingale concentration argument for the noise term. A proxy iterative sequence y_s (borrowed from heavy-ball momentum analysis) allows the descent lemma to be applied to Adam's coupled momentum-adaptive update. An induction argument then bounds the gradient norms along the trajectory, handling the unbounded-gradient case by showing G² (a constant depending on T, δ, and noise parameters) self-consistently upper-bounds all iterates. The error terms from the proxy step-size, momentum ratio Δ_s, and corrective terms are decomposed and controlled using logarithmic summation lemmas for the adaptive denominators. The framework extends to (L₀, L_q)-smoothness by replacing the uniform L with a gradient-dependent smoothness parameter and tuning the step-size accordingly.

## Results

Under L-smoothness and a general affine variance noise model (Assumption A3), Adam with full corrective terms achieves a high-probability convergence rate of $\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(1/\sqrt{T})$ for the average squared gradient norm (Theorem 3.1), matching the lower bound for stochastic first-order methods up to logarithmic factors. Adam without corrective terms and RMSProp achieve a noise-adaptive rate of $\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(1/T + \sigma_0/\sqrt{T})$ (Theorem 3.2), which accelerates to $\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(1/T)$ when the noise floor $\sigma_0$ is sufficiently small. Under the relaxed $(L_0, L_q)$-smoothness condition with $q \in [0,2)$, the same $\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(1/\sqrt{T})$ high-probability rate is preserved (Theorem 4.1), with the noise-adaptive rate also extending to the generalized smooth setting (Theorem 4.2).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper is the first to establish a high-probability $\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(1/\sqrt{T})$ convergence guarantee for Adam with full corrective terms under a general (non-coordinate-wise) affine variance noise model without requiring bounded gradients. Compared to [40] Wang et al. and [20] Hong & Lin, which handle only coordinate-wise affine variance noise, this paper's Assumption (A3) is strictly more general, covering bounded noise, sub-Gaussian noise, and standard affine variance noise simultaneously. Against [25] Li et al., which achieves high-probability convergence under sub-Gaussian noise and $(L_0,L_1)$-smoothness, this paper extends the smoothness to the broader $(L_0,L_q)$ class with $q\in[0,2)$ and replaces the sub-Gaussian requirement with the milder generalized affine variance condition. Compared to [53] Zhang et al. and [42] Wang et al., which analyze Adam under finite-sum objectives with affine growth, this paper handles the general stochastic setting and provides a tighter high-probability rate rather than an expected bound. The noise-adaptive rate $\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(1/T+\sigma_0/\sqrt{T})$ for Adam without corrective terms and RMSProp (Theorems 3.2, 4.2) matches the best known rates for SGD [4] and AdaGrad-Norm [2, 14, 41], closing the gap between adaptive methods and SGD under affine variance noise.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the AdaGrad-Norm convergence framework (Faw et al. 2022, Wang et al. 2023, Attia & Koren 2023) to the full Adam algorithm, which is substantially more challenging due to coordinate-wise adaptive step-sizes, heavy-ball momentum, and corrective terms. The analysis borrows the proxy step-size technique from Ward et al. (2020) and Defossez et al. (2022), but constructs a novel proxy a_s that depends on a local gradient bound G_T(s) to handle unbounded gradients under standard (non-coordinate-wise) affine variance noise. The generalized smoothness extension builds on the (L₀, L_q) framework of Zhang et al. (2020) and Zhang et al. (2020), applying it to Adam for the first time under a general noise model. Prior Adam analyses either required bounded gradients, used the stronger coordinate-wise affine noise assumption, dropped corrective terms, or only achieved expected convergence — this paper is the first to handle all of these simultaneously.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work closes a notable gap in the theoretical understanding of Adam by proving that the full algorithm (with corrective terms) achieves the near-optimal Õ(1/√T) rate under standard affine variance noise with unbounded gradients, matching SGD's lower bound up to logarithmic factors. The noise-adaptive rate Õ(1/T + σ₀/√T) for RMSProp and Adam without corrective terms provides a clean characterization of when Adam's adaptivity yields faster convergence. Limitations include the absence of experimental validation of the hyperparameter settings, the logarithmic gap to the lower bound, and the requirement that β₂ = 1 − O(1/T) which may not always align with practical choices. Open questions include whether the log factors can be removed and whether the analysis extends to finite-sum or federated settings.
