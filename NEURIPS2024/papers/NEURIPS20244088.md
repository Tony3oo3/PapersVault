## Problem

Existing LLM safety evaluations largely ignore the geo-diversity of cultural norms and legal standards, treating safety as a single-culture or Western-centric concern. This paper addresses two gaps: (1) the absence of a benchmark that evaluates whether LLMs generate culturally sensitive and legally compliant responses across diverse global contexts, and (2) the lack of alignment methods that specifically train models to elicit and apply geo-diverse safety knowledge in their responses.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20244088/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark takes 2,775 human-verified user queries (text) as input, each grounded in specific cultural-legal guidelines from 50 countries and 493 regions/races, and categorized into four types (SPECIFICANSWER, COMPREANSWER, REFUSETOANSWER, DOANSWER) with a corresponding expected response type. The model produces a free-form text response, which is then scored on three automatic dimensions—coverage and faithfulness of referenced norms/policies (via GPT-4-turbo extraction and set-overlap with ground-truth guidelines) and reference-free factuality (via Command-R web verification)—plus a binary response-type match score.

## Method

The paper introduces GEOSAFEDB, a bottom-up cultural-legal database of 7,447 norms and 6,652 policies validated by retrieval-augmented LLMs and native human annotators, from which SAFEWORLD test queries are generated via GPT-4-turbo prompting with K-Means clustering (using INSTRUCTOR embeddings) to create coherent multi-guideline scenarios. For alignment, the authors construct SAFEWORLDALIGN (45,746 DPO preference pairs) with two distinct negative-response strategies: Category 1 pairs correct guideline content with the wrong response type (training behavioral appropriateness), and Category 2 pairs the correct response type with perturbed, incorrect guidelines (training factual precision). DPO training is performed on Zephyr-7B-SFT-Full with β=0.01, lr=5e-7, batch size 32 on 4×A100 80GB GPUs for one epoch, yielding SAFEWORLDLM which outperforms GPT-4o across all evaluation dimensions with a 44.7% improvement in response-type matching.

## Results

On the SAFEWORLD benchmark, SAFEWORLDLM (7B) achieves 0.501 coverage, 0.219 faithfulness, 0.642 factuality, and 0.731 response type matching, outperforming all proprietary models including GPT-4-turbo (0.382/0.157/0.632/0.284) and GPT-4o (0.343/0.155/0.602/0.272) across all four dimensions. The most dramatic gain is in response type matching, where SAFEWORLDLM improves by 44.7% over GPT-4-turbo and 38.0% over GPT-4o. On general safety benchmarks, SAFEWORLDALIGN training yields 96.5% and 80.2% harmless response ratios on HH-RLHF and BeaverTails respectively, versus 59.3% and 74.2% for the base Zephyr-7B-SFT-Full, while maintaining general NLP performance (MMLU 56.6%, HellaSwag 78.5%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SAFEWORLDLM decisively outperforms GPT-4-turbo and GPT-4o on all four SAFEWORLD dimensions, with the largest margin in response type matching (0.731 vs. 0.284 and 0.272, respectively), indicating that even models with the underlying cultural-legal knowledge in their parametric memory fail to elicit it without targeted alignment. Against LLaMA-series models (LLaMA-2-7B/13B-chat, LLaMA-3-8B-Instruct), SAFEWORLDLM leads by 0.14–0.21 in coverage and 0.07–0.11 in faithfulness, while the LLaMA models only marginally exceed GPT-3.5-turbo on some dimensions. Compared to the Zephyr-7B-SFT-Full base model, SAFEWORLDLM improves coverage from 0.173 to 0.501 and response type matching from 0.277 to 0.731, demonstrating the substantial benefit of the DPO alignment training. On general safety benchmarks, SAFEWORLDALIGN outperforms SAFER-INSTRUCT on HH-RLHF (96.5% vs. 98.0% for SAFER-INSTRUCT alone, but 98.4% when combined) and BeaverTails (80.2% vs. 78.3%), showing that geo-diverse safety alignment data also strengthens general safety without degrading NLP performance (MMLU 56.6% vs. 56.8% for Zephyr). In human evaluation by annotators from 9 countries, SAFEWORLDLM achieves an 18–20% higher winning rate than GPT-4o on both helpfulness and harmlessness.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three prior research threads: (1) cultural knowledge base construction (Normsage, CultureBank), which typically uses top-down web scraping and yields noisy data; (2) LLM safety evaluation benchmarks (ToxicChat, SafetyBench, Safer-Instruct, BeaverTails), which assess toxicity or general harm but ignore geo-diverse cultural-legal standards; and (3) DPO-based alignment (Rafailov et al., Zephyr), which the authors repurpose for a new safety objective. The key departure is a bottom-up, human-validated construction of cultural-legal guidelines (GEOSAFEDB) and the explicit framing of safety as a function of geographic context, rather than a universal binary (safe/unsafe).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark and alignment method are practically important for any team deploying LLMs across multiple jurisdictions, where a single 'safe' response may be culturally offensive or legally non-compliant in another region. The finding that a 7B DPO-aligned model outperforms GPT-4o on all evaluation dimensions suggests that targeted alignment data can be more effective than raw scale for geo-diverse safety, a useful signal for resource-constrained practitioners. Key limitations include coverage of only 50 countries, a fixed set of four query types that may not capture real-world nuance, and an evaluation framework that relies on GPT-4-turbo as judge (with acknowledged circularity since GPT-4-turbo also generated the underlying guidelines). Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to less populous nations, multilingual settings, and dynamic legal changes.
