## Problem

High-quality diffusion transformers require large models with substantial training and inference costs, and while multi-expert denoising alleviates inference overhead by activating only one expert per timestep, training N independent experts incurs linearly growing training costs. The paper asks whether it is possible to craft an arbitrary number of denoising experts from a small set of shared basis models, with learnable coefficients that adaptively allocate model capacity across timesteps rather than relying on manual, uniform partitioning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243414/cropped_page1_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a noisy latent (Gaussian noise at a given timestep) and a class label as input, and outputs a 256×256 RGB image after a multi-step denoising chain. During inference, N pre-computed expert models—each architecturally identical to a standard DiT—are chained sequentially, with each expert handling a contiguous interval of timesteps. The effective per-step compute (Gflops) matches a single standard DiT, while total stored parameters scale as K× the base model size (K basis models) rather than N×.

## Method

Remix-DiT trains K (K<N) basis DiT models by expanding each linear layer's width by a factor of K, then crafts N expert models via a weighted average of basis parameters using learnable softmax mixing coefficients stored in a small N×K embedding table. During training, a hierarchical sampling strategy selects one expert index per step and then a timestep within that expert's interval, so only one mixed expert is activated per forward/backward pass while all K basis models receive gradients. A cross-entropy prior regularization (annealed to zero) encourages the initially identical basis models—initialized by replicating a pre-trained DiT K times—to specialize into distinct experts. At inference, the N mixed experts are pre-computed via a single matrix multiplication (Θ = αᵀβ), yielding a standard multi-expert denoising pipeline with per-step latency matching a plain DiT.

## Results

On ImageNet-256×256, Remix-DiT fine-tuned for 100K steps from pre-trained DiT checkpoints achieves FID 3.22 (IS 207.54) for the L/2 model, FID 9.02 (IS 127.42) for B/2, and FID 22.84 (IS 69.23) for S/2, all outperforming both continual training and 8-expert multi-expert baselines under the same 100K-step budget. Compared to prior diffusion methods, Remix-B/2-4-20 achieves FID 9.02 with 130M effective parameters per step, surpassing ADM (FID 10.94, 554M params), IDDPM (FID 12.26, 270M), and MEME (FID 13.19, 103M). The method uses 4 basis DiTs to craft 20 experts, maintaining the same per-step inference cost (80.79 Gflops for L, 23.04 for B, 6.07 for S) as a single standard DiT.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against continual training (same architecture, 100K extra steps), Remix-DiT wins on all three model sizes: Remix-L improves FID from 3.57 to 3.22 and IS from 200.16 to 207.54; Remix-B improves FID from 9.31 to 9.02; Remix-S improves FID from 26.05 to 22.84. Against the 8-expert multi-expert baseline (E-Diff style, where 8 independent experts are trained with the total 100K budget split evenly), Remix-DiT again wins across all sizes, e.g., FID 3.22 vs. 3.41 (L), 9.02 vs. 9.28 (B), and 22.84 vs. 25.00 (S), while using only 4 basis models instead of 8 independent ones. In the broader comparison (Figure 3), Remix-B/2-4-20 achieves FID 9.02 with 130M effective parameters per step, outperforming ADM (FID 10.94, 554M params), IDDPM (FID 12.26, 270M), VQ-Diffusion (FID 11.89), and MEME (FID 13.19, 103M), while matching the per-step parameter count of the original DiT-B/2. The key advantage over multi-expert baselines is that Remix-DiT trains shared basis models throughout the entire 100K budget, so each basis receives full gradient updates, whereas independent experts receive only 1/8 of the steps each.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper combines two previously separate lines of work: multi-expert denoising (E-Diff, MEME, OMS-DPM) and parameter-level model mixing (Deep Model Reassembly, Factorizing Knowledge in Neural Networks). While prior multi-expert methods train N independent models or use a single model with task routing (DTR), Remix-DiT departs by training only K < N basis models and using learnable softmax mixing coefficients to synthesize N experts, borrowing the width-expansion trick from factorized knowledge networks. The approach is positioned as a more efficient alternative to both retraining a larger DiT and independently training multiple experts, with the key architectural modification being a K-fold expansion of linear layer widths in the DiT backbone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method offers a practical path to multi-expert denoising without the linear training cost of independent experts, and the learned coefficients provide interpretable insights into how denoising difficulty varies across timesteps (more capacity at low-noise steps). However, the evaluation is limited to ImageNet-256 with DiT-S/B/L and only 100K fine-tuning steps, leaving open whether the approach generalizes to text-to-image, video generation, or larger-scale training. The sparse-gradient limitation when scaling to many experts (e.g., 1000) is acknowledged but not fully resolved, and the modest FID improvements over independent multi-expert baselines suggest the gains may diminish at scale.
