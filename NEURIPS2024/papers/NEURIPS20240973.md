## Problem

Existing VLMs train image-text alignment by treating all text tokens equally in an autoregressive MLE objective, which over-emphasizes tokens that are visually irrelevant or even contradictory to the input image. This leads to sub-optimal cross-modal alignment, manifesting as hallucinations and responses that fail to depend on visual conditions. No prior work had identified this per-token discrepancy or provided a lightweight training-time mechanism to address it.

## Input / Output

The method operates on standard VLM architectures (LLaVA-1.5/NeXT, Mini-Gemini) that take an RGB image (from 336×336 low-res to multi-crop high-res) and a text prompt, producing text answers, captions, or grounding descriptions. During training, each sample requires two forward passes through the LLM—one with image tokens and one without—to compute the per-token contrastive logit difference. At inference time, no additional computation is needed beyond the standard single forward pass, making the method deployment-free.

## Method

CAL (Contrastive ALIGNment) is a token re-weighting strategy that assigns distinct training contributions to each text token based on its visual correlation with the input image. The core insight is that the difference in prediction logits between the with-image and without-image conditions (Δo) naturally highlights visually correlated tokens while suppressing irrelevant or contradictory ones, as verified by heat-map visualization and human evaluation. Concretely, Δo is clamped to [α, β] (set to [1, 5] in practice), smoothed via average pooling with window W=3, and used as per-token weights in a normalized weighted MLE loss. This requires only one additional gradient-free forward pass per training step (~20% overhead), with no changes to model architecture or inference. CAL is applied during both pre-training and instruction-tuning stages, with the instruction-tuning stage contributing the majority of the improvement.

## Results

CAL consistently improves VLMs across all tested architectures and scales. On LLaVA-NeXT-13B, CAL yields +1.7 ANLS on VQA^Doc (78.4→80.1), +3.4 relaxed accuracy on VQA^Chart (63.8→67.2), and +21 points on OCR-Bench (553→574). On image captioning and grounding, LLaVA-NeXT-13B+CAL achieves +2.1 CIDEr on COCO Caption (118.5→120.6), +6.2 CIDEr on TextCaps (118.2→124.4), and +0.6/+0.7 IoU on RefCOCOg val/test. The smallest model (MGM-2B) sees the largest relative gains, e.g., +21.7 CIDEr on COCO Caption (8.0→29.7) and +25 points on OCR-Bench (335→360).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against LLaVA-NeXT, CAL wins on 6–7 out of 8 VQA benchmarks for both 7B and 13B variants, with the largest gains on OCR-centric tasks (VQA^Doc +1.7, VQA^Chart +3.4, OCR-Bench +21 for 13B); the only loss is a marginal −0.3 on SQA^I for 13B. Against LLaVA-1.5, CAL improves on 5–7 out of 7 benchmarks, with the clearest gains on VQA^Doc (+2.1 for 13B) and OCR-Bench (+6 for 13B), while VQA^Chart and VQA^Text remain essentially unchanged. Against Mini-Gemini (MGM/MGM-HD), CAL is the most consistently effective: it wins on 6/7 benchmarks for MGM-2B (with a dramatic +5.0 on VQA^Doc and +25 on OCR-Bench), 6/7 for MGM-7B/13B, and 6/7 for MGM-HD-7B/13B. On captioning and grounding (Table 2), CAL outperforms all three baselines on 3–4 out of 4 benchmarks, with the most striking improvement being +21.7 CIDEr on COCO Caption for MGM-2B and +6.2 CIDEr on TextCaps for LLaVA-NeXT-13B.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the contrastive image-input signal from training-free decoding methods (VCD, IBD) into a training-time loss re-weighting strategy, shifting the contrastive signal from inference to optimization. It departs from the dominant paradigm in VLM training—treating all autoregressive tokens equally under MLE—by arguing that token-level visual correlation should modulate the learning objective. Conceptually, it also connects to token-importance weighting in NLP (e.g., Rho-1), but the weighting signal here is specifically derived from the visual modality rather than text-level heuristics. The paper positions CAL as a complement to, rather than a replacement for, the prevailing strategies of data scaling and resolution scaling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CAL offers a drop-in, ~20%-overhead training modification that consistently improves VLMs across architectures (CLIP-ViT, ConvNeXt), resolutions, and model sizes (2B–13B), making it practically attractive for practitioners already training VLMs. The noise-robustness result (graceful degradation under 10–30% label corruption) is particularly relevant given the heavy reliance on model-generated training data in current pipelines. Open questions include whether the clamping bounds can be made adaptive, whether the method extends to non-autoregressive or diffusion-based VLMs, and whether the three-way token taxonomy (correlated/irrelevant/contradictory) admits a more principled quantitative boundary.
