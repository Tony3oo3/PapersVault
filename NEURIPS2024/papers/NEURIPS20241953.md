## Problem

Existing diffusion prior-based denoising methods either assume i.i.d. Gaussian noise or rely on hard data-consistency terms with approximate posterior inference, making them ineffective for real-world noise that is signal-dependent and spatially correlated. The core difficulty is that a structured heteroscedastic Gaussian likelihood, while theoretically appropriate, is computationally intractable due to its large non-diagonal covariance matrix and unknown noise variance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241953/cropped_page5_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single noisy RGB image at any resolution (256×256 or 512×512 in experiments) and a pre-trained 256×256 unconditional DDPM as the diffusion prior, producing a denoised image of the same resolution. Notably, high-resolution 512×512 images are processed directly by the 256×256 diffusion model without patching or resizing, exploiting the local prior property of low-resolution diffusion models. The method is fully unsupervised, requiring no paired clean/noisy data or training on the test set.

## Method

The paper introduces adaptive likelihood estimation via mean-field variational Bayes during the reverse diffusion process. At each denoising step, an i.n.i.d. Gaussian likelihood with a Gamma precision prior is used, and the noise precision posterior is jointly inferred with the clean image estimate through alternating coordinate updates. The MAP solution at each step is a convex combination of the re-corrupted observation and the diffusion model's denoised estimate, with the mixing weight determined by the estimated noise precision. A local 2D Gaussian convolution rectifies the estimated noise variance to compensate for the spatial correlation ignored by the diagonal likelihood. The method also demonstrates that low-resolution diffusion models exhibit a local prior that enables direct denoising of higher-resolution images without additional operations.

## Results

On the four real-world denoising benchmarks (SIDD, FMDD, PolyU, CC), the proposed method achieves an average PSNR/SSIM of 36.16/0.919, outperforming all unsupervised baselines. The best individual results are 38.71/0.970 on PolyU and 38.01/0.959 on CC. The method also extends to synthetic non-Gaussian noise (Poisson, Bernoulli) and image demosaicing, where it surpasses DDRM by ~1.4 dB PSNR on Set14 demosaicing (26.02 vs 24.68).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all eight unsupervised baselines on average PSNR/SSIM (36.16/0.919 vs. next-best ScoreDVI at 35.68/0.906). Among diffusion-prior-based methods, the advantage is most pronounced: GDP (29.77/0.784) and DR2 (32.30/0.836) suffer from hard data-consistency approximations, while DDRM (33.72/0.831) is limited by its i.i.d. Gaussian noise assumption, leaving residual noise in outputs. The self-supervised APBSN achieves higher PSNR on SIDD (36.80 vs. 34.76) but introduces color artifacts and degrades significantly on smaller datasets (FMDD: 31.99 vs. 33.14; CC: 34.88 vs. 38.01), highlighting the data-efficiency advantage of the proposed approach. On the demosaicing task, the method surpasses DDRM by 1.34 dB on Set14 (26.02 vs. 24.68) and 0.91 dB on CBSD68 (25.43 vs. 24.52). For synthetic Poisson noise, the method achieves 29.24/0.833 on CBSD68 versus ZS-N2N's 27.55/0.781, while on Bernoulli noise it trades a small SSIM gap for substantially better PSNR (26.11 vs. 20.20 on CBSD68).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the diffusion-prior framework for image restoration (DDPM, DDRM) by replacing the fixed or approximate likelihood terms used in prior methods with an adaptively estimated i.n.i.d. Gaussian likelihood inferred via variational Bayes at each reverse diffusion step. Where DDRM and related methods assume i.i.d. Gaussian noise, and methods like GDP and DR2 rely on hard data-consistency constraints with approximate posteriors, this work introduces a Gamma precision hyperprior and cycles between updating the image posterior and the noise-precision posterior, yielding a signal-dependent likelihood that better matches real-world structured noise. The re-corruption strategy for bridging the distribution gap between the noisy observation and intermediate diffusion states follows Song et al. (ICLR 2022) and DR2, but the variational inference machinery and the local Gaussian convolution rectification are novel additions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The adaptive likelihood estimation provides a principled, data-efficient alternative to both the oversimplified noise assumptions in linear-inversion diffusion methods and the heuristic data-consistency terms in nonlinear-inversion methods, offering a middle ground that handles signal-dependent, spatially correlated noise without requiring paired training data or full covariance modeling. The observation that low-resolution diffusion models exhibit local priors effective for high-resolution denoising is a practical insight that could simplify resolution-mismatch handling in restoration pipelines. However, the 230-second per-image inference time (1000 DDPM steps) and the need to tune β per dataset limit immediate practical adoption, and the authors identify accelerated sampling as the key open direction.
