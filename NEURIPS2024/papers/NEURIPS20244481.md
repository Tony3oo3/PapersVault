## Problem

Existing relative encodings for temporal link prediction suffer from three limitations: they lack a unified theoretical framework connecting different construction heuristics, most ignore temporal information by merely counting structural walks, and they are computationally expensive due to time-consuming graph queries and per-link re-extraction. This paper asks whether relative encodings can be unified under a single principled formulation and whether the resulting temporal walk matrices can be maintained efficiently without explicit enumeration.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a chronological sequence of timestamped interactions (with optional node and link features) and, at query time, a target node pair (u, v) with timestamp t. It outputs a scalar link likelihood in [0,1] via a sigmoid MLP. A key operational detail is that node representations are maintained incrementally in O(k·d_R) time per new interaction (where d_R ≈ 10·log(2E) is the representation dimension), so no graph traversal or walk sampling is needed at prediction time—pairwise features are decoded purely from cached inner products.

## Method

TPNet unifies all existing relative encodings as a function g([A_{u,w}^{(0)},…,A_{u,w}^{(k)}]) of temporal walk matrix entries, then proposes a new walk matrix whose score function s(W)=∏e^{−λ(t−t_i)} incorporates exponential time decay. Rather than explicitly storing the O(n²) walk matrices, TPNet maintains low-dimensional node representations H^{(l)}∈R^{n×d_R} via random feature propagation: H^{(0)} is initialized with i.i.d. N(0,1/d_R) entries, and upon each interaction (u,v,t), H_u^{(l)} += e^{λt}·H_v^{(l−1)} (and symmetrically for v). Theorem 1 proves H^{(l)}(t)=e^{λlt}·A^{(l)}(t)·P, i.e., the representations are a random projection of the walk matrix, and Theorem 2 (via the Johnson-Lindenstrauss lemma) guarantees inner-product preservation with high probability when d_R ≥ (24/ε²)log(4^{1/3}(k+1)n). The Link Likelihood Computing module decodes a 4(k+1)²-dimensional pairwise feature from cross-layer inner products, applies log(ReLU(·)+1) scaling for numerical stability, and feeds it alongside auxiliary features (node/link features, time encodings) into an MLP-Mixer sequential encoder to produce the final link probability.

## Results

On 13 benchmark datasets under the transductive setting with random negative sampling, TPNet achieves the best average rank of 1.08 for both AP and AUC, compared to 3.15/3.23 for the SOTA baseline DyGFormer and 4.15/4.08 for PINT. The largest gains appear on sparse or long-tail datasets: MOOC (96.39 vs. 87.52 AP over DyGFormer), UN Trade (87.24 vs. 66.46), and UN Vote (75.12 vs. 55.55). In terms of efficiency, TPNet achieves a 33.3× speedup over DyGFormer and 71.5× over CAWN on LastFM, while maintaining near-linear scalability in both time and GPU memory up to 10⁸ edges.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TPNet outperforms all 11 baselines on 12 out of 13 datasets in AP (and AUC), with the sole exception being Can. Parl. where DyGFormer achieves 97.36 AP versus TPNet's 90.28. Against the strongest baseline DyGFormer [9], TPNet gains the most on structurally sparse or long-tail datasets: +8.87 AP on MOOC, +20.78 on UN Trade, and +19.57 on UN Vote, while matching DyGFormer on Social Evo. (94.73). Against PINT [8], the second-best baseline, TPNet leads by +8.31 AP on MOOC and +17.04 on UN Trade, and PINT's explicit temporal walk matrix storage causes OOM at 10⁷ edges whereas TPNet scales linearly. Against CAWN [6], which uses temporal walk sampling, TPNet is 71.5× faster on LastFM while also achieving higher AP (94.50 vs. 86.99). Node-wise baselines (JODIE, DyRep, TGAT, TGN, EdgeBank, TCL, GraphMixer) are consistently outperformed, confirming the importance of pairwise relative encodings. The link-wise baselines NAT [7] and PINT [8] are the closest competitors in average rank (4.15 and 3.69 vs. TPNet's 1.08), but both ignore temporal decay in their walk scoring, which TPNet explicitly models.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new conceptual ground by unifying four previously fragmented relative encoding schemes (DyGFormer, PINT, NAT, CAWN) into a single function of temporal walk matrices, revealing that they all implicitly construct different temporal walk matrices via different score functions. Methodologically, TPNet departs from prior link-wise methods that either count walks (PINT, NAT, DyGFormer) or sample them (CAWN) by introducing a time-decay-weighted temporal walk matrix and maintaining it implicitly through random feature propagation grounded in the Johnson-Lindenstrauss lemma. The auxiliary feature learning module follows the MLP-Mixer architecture from GraphMixer, while the overall link-wise prediction paradigm is inherited from the broader line of work on injecting pairwise information into temporal graph learning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified temporal walk matrix framework provides a principled lens for analyzing and designing future relative encodings, moving the field beyond ad-hoc heuristics. The 33.3× speedup over DyGFormer and 71.5× over CAWN, combined with linear scalability, makes link-wise temporal link prediction practical at scale where it was previously a computational bottleneck. A key limitation acknowledged by the authors is that the temporal walk matrix construction (e.g., the exponential time decay) is manually predefined and may be suboptimal for networks with long-term dependencies, leaving adaptive matrix construction as an open direction. The theoretical guarantee on inner product preservation also opens the door to applying the random feature propagation mechanism to other types of temporal walk matrices beyond the one proposed here.
