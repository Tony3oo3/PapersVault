## Problem

Disentangled Representation Learning has been predominantly validated on synthetic datasets with perfectly independent, fully annotated factors of variation, leaving its potential on real images largely unexplored due to correlated factors, occlusion, and the absence of ground-truth FoV labels. This paper investigates whether a disentangled representation learned on a synthetic Source (where FoVs are known) can be transferred to a real Target (where FoVs are unknown), and which disentanglement properties survive such a transfer.

## Input / Output

The method consumes paired images from a Source dataset that differ in exactly one FoV (for weakly supervised Ada-GVAE training) and unlabeled images from a Target dataset (for unsupervised fine-tuning as a plain VAE). Images range from 64×64 (dSprites, Shapes3D) to 128×128 (Isaac3D, Coil100) to 256×256 (RGBD-Objects), with RGB or depth channels. The output is a 10-dimensional continuous latent code; evaluation produces per-FoV classification accuracy (GBT/MLP) on both the full representation and a 1-dimensional 'pruned' version selected via the OMES association matrix, plus modularity and compactness scores.

## Method

The paper introduces OMES (Overlap Multiple Encoding Scores), a classifier-free intervention-based metric that builds an m×n association matrix S by computing, for each FoV j and latent dimension h, the Pearson correlation between paired representations (images differing only in FoV j) along dimension h, then inverting so high values indicate strong encoding. From S, an Overlap Score (OS) penalizes multiple FoVs sharing a dimension (measuring modularity) and a Multiple Encoding Score (MES) penalizes one FoV spread across dimensions (measuring compactness); the final score is a weighted average controlled by α. For transfer, an Ada-GVAE is weakly supervised on the Source using pairs differing in one FoV (β∈{1,2}), then unsupervised fine-tuned on the Target for 50k steps as a standard VAE. The key distinction from prior intervention-based metrics is the opposite pairing convention (samples differ in one FoV rather than share one), which requires less supervision and provides stronger disentanglement guarantees, and the use of correlation in place of mutual information to avoid estimator sensitivity.

## Results

On the synthetic-to-real transfer (Color-dSprites → Coil100), the transferred representation achieves 24.5% average FoV classification accuracy (full representation) with fine-tuning improving it by +10.4%, while the pruned (single-dimension) representation drops to 14.9%. OMES (α=0.5) shows high rank correlation with DCI (72) and MIG (48) on Noisy-dSprites, confirming it captures similar disentanglement structure, while producing a wider score distribution (more descriptive) than both. Fine-tuning consistently improves explicitness across all transfer scenarios (e.g., +13.6% on Shapes3D, +16.1% on Coil) with no observed degradation, and modularity/compactness metrics (OS, MES) improve by 2–6 points after fine-tuning on synthetic targets but show limited or negative gains on real targets with hidden factors.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OMES is compared against six established disentanglement metrics (DCI, MIG, BetaVAE, FactorVAE, Modularity, SAP) via rank correlation on 5,400 pre-trained models. OMES (α=0.5) achieves the highest correlation with DCI (72) and MIG (48) on Noisy-dSprites, confirming it captures similar structural information, while its mild correlation with BetaVAE (7) and FactorVAE (25) is expected since OMES uses the opposite intervention pairing (samples differ in one FoV vs. share one FoV). Unlike DCI, BetaVAE, FactorVAE, and SAP, OMES is entirely classifier-free, eliminating sensitivity to classifier choice and hyperparameters. OMES also produces a wider score distribution than MIG and DCI, making it more discriminative between models of varying quality. In the transfer experiments, OMES's interpretability (per-FoV, per-dimension scores) enables selecting the best single dimension for pruned classification, a capability not available in the baseline metrics. On real targets with hidden factors (RGBD-Objects), modularity scores (OS, DCI) degrade more than on synthetic targets, and fine-tuning provides limited recovery, whereas on DRL-compliant synthetic targets all metrics improve consistently after fine-tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the disentangled representation transfer paradigm initiated by Dittadi et al. [11] and Gondal et al. [20], which studied transfer in very specific simulated-to-real settings with carefully matched datasets. Here, the authors adopt a more general and straightforward pipeline: weakly supervised Ada-GVAE [41] training on synthetic Source datasets with known FoVs, followed by unsupervised fine-tuning on Target datasets where FoVs are unknown or unavailable. The paper also introduces OMES, a classifier-free intervention-based metric that departs from existing metrics (DCI, MIG, BetaVAE, FactorVAE) by requiring no learned classifier and using correlation rather than mutual information estimation, while simultaneously measuring both modularity and compactness.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first systematic empirical characterization of which disentanglement properties (explicitness, modularity, compactness) survive transfer from synthetic to real data, showing that explicitness is well preserved while modularity and compactness degrade, particularly when the Target contains hidden unannotated factors. The finding that real-to-real transfer from a DRL-compliant source offers limited benefit is a useful negative result that tempers expectations for this approach. Key open questions include quantifying the Source-Target distance that determines transfer effectiveness, generalizing beyond VAE-based models to more recent vector-quantized or diffusion-based approaches, and applying the framework to specific domains like biomedical imaging or video action recognition.
