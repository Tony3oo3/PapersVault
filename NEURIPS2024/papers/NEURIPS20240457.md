## Problem

Training multi-modal LLMs on long interleaved documents is prohibitively expensive: OpenFlamingo-9B requires 32 A100 GPUs for over three days to process just 15M samples, and must sub-sample text to 256 tokens, discarding most of the available context. Existing context-extension techniques (memory banks, novel attention) operate at the LLM level and do not exploit the large parameter asymmetry between the lightweight visual encoder (~300M) and the heavy text decoder (56B MOE) already present in Flamingo-style architectures.

## Input / Output

The method consumes interleaved image-text documents (web pages, PDFs) where the in-context text length can reach 2048+ tokens, but only 128 text tokens are fed directly to the LLM; the remaining text is rendered into fixed-size RGB images (16×8192 px, 512 patches, HERSHEY font at 10 px) and processed by the frozen ViT-L visual encoder. At inference, the maximum in-context text length extends to ~9192 tokens on a single 80 GB H100 for the 56B MOE model. Outputs include VQA answers, image captions, class labels, and retrieved next-image/next-text pairs for the sequential document retrieval task.

## Method

VisInContext renders omitted in-context text into images and routes them through the shared frozen visual encoder, reducing the token count needed to represent equivalent text by roughly 3× (a 224×224 rendered image holds ~294 tokens but requires only ~98 visual patches). Two learnable resamplers extract fixed-length token sequences from raw image features and rendered-text-image features respectively. A Token Masking mechanism forces the model to learn text semantics from rendered images by masking all raw-image tokens (ratio 1) during pretraining, while at inference both token streams are summed. A Text-Centric Contrastive Loss (TCCL) aligns the resampler's visual-text embeddings with the LLM's text-tokenizer embeddings via a cosine-similarity contrastive objective, effectively turning the vision encoder + resampler into a 'visual text tokenizer.' This input-level compression is complementary to attention-level or memory-bank context extensions and reduces both FLOPs and GPU memory while increasing effective in-context length from 256 to 2048 tokens at nearly identical compute cost.

## Results

With a 56B MOE language model, VisInContext increases the in-context text length from 256 to 2048 tokens while maintaining nearly the same FLOPs. On the 32-shot in-context few-shot evaluation, the mean score improves from 55.8% (baseline) to 57.8%, with the largest gain on TextVQA (23.2% → 31.2%). On document understanding, VisInContext achieves 48.5 ANLS on DocVQA-val (vs. 45.3) and 58.4 on OCRVQA (vs. 51.5). At inference, the method extends the maximum manageable in-context text length from ~1K to 9,192 tokens on a single 80GB H100 GPU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Open-Flamingo MOE baseline (256 ICL tokens), VisInContext (2048 ICL tokens) wins on the mean score at every shot setting: 49.7 vs. 47.8 (0-shot), 54.4 vs. 52.1 (4-shot), and 57.8 vs. 55.8 (32-shot). The most pronounced gain is on TextVQA, where 32-shot accuracy jumps from 23.2% to 31.2% (+8.0 points), reflecting the benefit of longer in-context text for reading-heavy tasks. On the text-only in-context setting (Table 2), VisInContext matches the baseline's 32-shot mean (33.6 vs. 34.4) while reducing LLM input tokens from 426 to just 10, yielding a large inference-cost reduction. On document understanding (Table 3), VisInContext outperforms the Open-Flamingo-9B baseline by +3.2 ANLS on DocVQA-val, +4.0 on DocVQA-test, and +6.9 on OCRVQA. Against the FuYu-8B baseline (which uses linear embeddings rather than a visual encoder), VisInContext improves DocVQA-val from 42.3 to 44.5 (+2.2), confirming the method's generality across MLLM architectures.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Flamingo/OpenFlamingo architecture (dual-stream encoder with gated cross-attention) and extends it by offloading long in-context text to the lightweight visual encoder via text-to-image rendering. It departs from the dominant long-context strategies in LLMs—novel attention mechanisms (Ring Attention), memory banks, and compressed tokens—by instead exploiting the asymmetry between the small ViT encoder (~300M) and the large LLM decoder (56B MOE), treating rendered text images as a cheap input-level compression. Two novel training components, Token Masking and Text-Centric Contrastive Learning (TCCL), are introduced to force the model to learn text semantics from visual tokens and align them with the LLM's text embedding space. The method is explicitly positioned as complementary to, rather than competing with, existing long-context techniques.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical efficiency gain—7× longer in-context text at nearly identical FLOPs, and inference extended to ~9K tokens on a single 80GB GPU—is significant for practitioners training or deploying multi-modal models on interleaved document data where context truncation was previously unavoidable. The method also yields a useful side effect: substantially improved OCR and document understanding (e.g., +6.9% on OCRVQA), suggesting that forcing a model to read text through a visual channel strengthens its text-in-image capabilities. Key limitations include the fixed-size rendered image (wasteful for short texts) and the dependence on a Flamingo-style architecture with a separate visual encoder; the authors note that dynamic token counts and broader architectural generalization remain open. Whether the modest average performance gain (~2%) scales further with longer contexts or larger models is an open question.
