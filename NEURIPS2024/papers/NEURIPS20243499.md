## Problem

Existing multimodal knowledge editing methods for MLLMs struggle to simultaneously achieve reliability, generality, and locality: intrinsic editing (e.g., T-Patcher) stores knowledge as rote parametric neurons with weak generalizability, while external knowledge resorting (e.g., in-context editing) retrieves generalizable examples but often injects semantically irrelevant content that disrupts locality. Simply combining the two fails because they operate at inconsistent semantic levels—abstract parametric neurons versus low-level descriptive examples—preventing synergistic interaction.

## Input / Output

At inference, the MLLM receives only the original image and text question (no in-context examples in the prompt). External knowledge is consumed as pre-extracted hidden states (top-40 retrieved by cosine similarity from a memory of 15K+ triplets) that are injected via feature shifting in the last four transformer layers, rather than as raw text or images appended to the context window. The output is a corrected text response (VQA answer or image caption), with the editing performed entirely in the latent space of the frozen backbone (BLIP-2 OPT 3.8B or MiniGPT-4 7.3B).

## Method

UniKE unifies intrinsic and external knowledge as vectorized key-value memories operating at the same semantic level within the same transformer layers: intrinsic editing (assimilation) adds extra key-value pairs to the FFN, while external editing (accommodation) uses retrieved hidden states as keys/values for self-attention feature shifting, with the post-attention states feeding directly into the FFN and vice versa across layers. To enhance collaboration, contrastive learning disentangles knowledge representations into a semantic space (where positive and negative hidden states share meaning) and a truthfulness space (where they are distinctly separated). In the truthfulness space, an editing direction ζ derived from the distributional gap between truthful and hallucinated representations is mapped back to the representation space and added to the intrinsic knowledge values, steering them toward generalizable truthfulness. In the semantic space, the inclusion magnitude α of external knowledge is dynamically set to the cosine similarity between the injected in-context representation and the input's last-token hidden state, adaptively reducing the influence of semantically irrelevant external knowledge to preserve locality.

## Results

On the MMEdit benchmark (one-step editing, BLIP-2 OPT 3.8B and MiniGPT-4 7.3B), UniKE achieves an average reliability of 98.0%, generality of 95.1%, and locality of 93.8%, simultaneously excelling on all three target properties. For example, on BLIP-2 OPT E-VQA, UniKE scores 98.8% Reliability, 98.4% T-Generality, 94.8% M-Generality, 98.3% T-Locality, and 96.7% M-Locality. In sequential editing (K=10, 20 steps), UniKE consistently outperforms MEND with advantages that grow as K increases (e.g., 91.5% Reliability vs. 90.6% at K=10 on VQA). In cross-task editing (K=10, mixing VQA and captioning), UniKE achieves 90.7% Reliability, 88.2% T-Generality, 86.8% M-Generality, 90.4% T-Locality, and 83.8% M-Locality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UniKE outperforms the previous SOTA method MEND on both generality (95.1% vs. 88.6% average) and locality (93.8% vs. 89.4% average) while maintaining comparable reliability (98.0% vs. 98.6%). Against intrinsic editing baselines, UniKE dramatically surpasses T-Patcher in generality (95.1% vs. 71.6%) while also achieving higher locality (93.8% vs. 87.2%), and dominates KE across all metrics. Against external knowledge resorting baselines, UniKE resolves the critical locality failure: IKE achieves only 26.8% average locality and SERAC only 52.5%, whereas UniKE reaches 93.8% while maintaining competitive generality (95.1% vs. 88.6% for IKE, 91.3% for SERAC). In sequential editing, UniKE's advantage over MEND widens with more editing steps (e.g., 88.9% Reliability vs. 85.1% at K=20 on VQA), while SERAC's locality collapses to 4.8–12.5% regardless of K. In cross-task editing, UniKE is the only method that simultaneously maintains high reliability (>90%), generality (>86%), and locality (>83%) across both VQA and captioning tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate paradigms of knowledge editing—parametric intrinsic editing (T-Patcher, ROME-style FFN key-value modification) and external in-context resorting (IKE, SERAC)—into a single framework where both are recast as vectorized key-value memory operations at the same semantic level within transformer layers. It departs from prior work by moving in-context editing from the input prompt to latent-space feature shifting, and by introducing contrastive learning to disentangle knowledge representations into semantic and truthfulness spaces, enabling each editing type to compensate for the other's weakness (generality vs. locality). The FFN-as-key-value-memory formulation is inherited directly from Meng et al. (ROME) and Dai et al. (KE), while the in-context editing reformulation builds on Zheng et al.'s IKE but reinterprets it as a controlled attention-based feature shift rather than a prompt concatenation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete and well-motivated gap: existing MLLM editing methods consistently fail to balance reliability, generality, and locality simultaneously, and the paper shows that naively combining intrinsic and external editing actually degrades both generality and locality due to semantic-level mismatch. The unified key-value memory perspective offers a cleaner conceptual foundation for future multimodal editing work, and the knowledge disentangling mechanism (semantic vs. truthfulness spaces) is a reusable design pattern. Limitations include evaluation only on visual comprehension tasks (not generation) and relatively small backbones (3.8B, 7.3B), leaving open whether the approach scales to larger models or generative multimodal settings.
