## Problem

Traditional influence maximization treats each user as an independent target, but in many viral marketing scenarios the actual customers are motifs—small strongly connected groups where activating only one or a few members is insufficient (e.g., a group of friends must collectively agree on a restaurant). This paper formalizes the motif-oriented IM problem, proves it is NP-hard with an objective function that is neither submodular nor supermodular, and provides the first algorithm with a guaranteed approximation ratio for this setting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20244314/cropped_page4_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a directed or undirected social network graph G=(V,E) with edge activation probabilities, a set of h target motifs (each a strongly connected subgraph) with per-motif activation thresholds r_i ∈ {1, k, k/2} and optional weights w_i, a seed budget k, and a cascade model (LT or IC). It outputs a size-k seed set S* that maximizes the expected weighted count of activated motifs, where a motif is activated only when more than r_i of its member nodes are simultaneously active. Unlike classical IM where each node is an independent target, a single node can belong to multiple motifs and the activation criterion is group-level rather than individual.

## Method

The core insight is a sandwich strategy: since the motif influence function is neither submodular nor supermodular, the authors derive a submodular upper bound f̄(S) (by replacing each motif with a super-node activated if any member is active, reducing to weighted IM) and two submodular lower bounds f̲₁(S) and f̲₂(S) (using the minimum probability of a semi-spanning tree within each motif to account for the r_i threshold). All three bounds share the same weighted node-level influence formalism, enabling a single greedy algorithm over reverse reachable sets to optimize them simultaneously. The algorithm generates θ RR sets rooted at super-nodes sampled proportionally to motif weights, applies standard greedy node selection, and uses a KPT estimation procedure (generalized from TIM) to set θ without knowing OPT. This yields an approximation ratio of τ·(1−1/e−ε) where τ is the minimum spanning-tree probability across motifs, with near-linear expected time complexity O((k+l)(m+η)log η/ε²).

## Results

The paper evaluates LBMOIM on five real social networks (Flickr: 2M nodes/33.1M edges, Amazon: 334K, Catster: 623K, Youtube: 1M, Douban: 154K) using randomly sampled strongly connected subgraphs as target motifs (size 2–3, thresholds r_i ∈ {1, 2, 3}). LBMOIM consistently achieves the highest expected motif influence across all datasets under both the Linear Threshold and Independent Cascade models, outperforming all five baselines. In terms of efficiency, LBMOIM exhibits only a minor time increase as the seed budget k grows, confirming its near-linear O((k+l)(m+η)log η/ε²) complexity, while GIM shows the highest time consumption due to optimizing two separate objective functions. The method also generalizes to the IC model, where it again outperforms all baselines on the Douban dataset for r_i = 1, 2, and 3.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LBMOIM outperforms all five baselines on expected motif influence across every dataset and cascade model tested. Against node-level methods (TIM+, OPIM, DeepIM), the gap is explained by the different objective functions: these methods maximize activated node count rather than activated motif count, so they cannot directly optimize the motif-oriented target. Against group-level methods, LBMOIM beats GIM (Zhu et al., TCSS 2019) in both influence and runtime, since GIM must optimize two separate node-level objective functions (upper and lower bounds with different formalisms) while LBMOIM optimizes a single unified submodular bound. GIA (Pham et al., CC 2023) shows performance closest to LBMOIM, particularly in the IC model with r_i=2 (Fig. 5b), but GIA lacks an approximation ratio guarantee and exhibits fluctuating performance across r_i settings (Figs. 5a, 5c), whereas LBMOIM maintains a guaranteed τ·(1−1/e−ε) approximation ratio. Overall, LBMOIM is the only method combining a provable approximation guarantee with the best empirical motif influence on all tested configurations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the TIM framework (Tang et al., SIGMOD 2014) — specifically its reverse-reachable-set generation, greedy node selection, and KPT parameter estimation — to a new objective: maximizing activated motifs (strongly connected subgraphs) rather than individual nodes. It builds on the LT/IC cascade models from Kempe et al. (KDD 2003) and positions itself against group-level IM work (Zhu et al. 2019; Pham et al. 2023), but departs from those by restricting targets to strongly connected subgraphs with threshold-based activation and by deriving upper and lower bounds that share the same formalism, enabling simultaneous optimization. The key conceptual departure from classical IM is the proof that the motif influence function is neither submodular nor supermodular, necessitating a sandwich strategy rather than a direct greedy approach.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first motif-oriented IM algorithm with a guaranteed approximation ratio (τ·(1−1/e−ε)), addressing a gap where prior group-level methods (GIM, GIA) offered no such guarantee. The non-submodularity/non-supermodularity result is a useful negative finding that clarifies why standard greedy IM techniques cannot be directly applied to group-targeted influence problems. Open questions include tightening the τ factor (which depends on the minimum spanning-tree probability within motifs), extending to dynamic or heterogeneous network structures, and handling larger or more complex motif topologies beyond size-3 strongly connected subgraphs.
