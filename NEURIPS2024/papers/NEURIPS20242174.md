## Problem

Existing methods for finding the performatively optimal model either require full parametric knowledge of the data distribution (score function estimators) or assume the performative effect is small relative to loss convexity. This paper asks whether the performative gradient can be estimated using only the shift operator, and whether convexity of the performative risk can be guaranteed without bounding the magnitude of the performative effect.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242174/cropped_page6_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes data samples (x, y) drawn from the current θ-dependent distribution, along with the performative shift operator Π(θ) (either known a priori or estimated online via ridge regression across successive deployments). It produces the performatively optimal parameters θ* — distinct from the stable point found by iterative retraining — along with an unbiased reparameterization-based gradient estimate whose variance remains independent of model dimension and does not degrade as the performative effect strengthens.

## Method

The performative effect is modeled as a push-forward measure Z = φ(U; θ), yielding a reparameterization-based gradient estimator that requires only knowledge of the shift operator φ rather than the full density p_θ, and whose variance provably stays bounded in high dimensions unlike the score function alternative. For binary classification with a linear-in-parameters shift Π(θ) = Πθ, the paper proves the performative risk is convex whenever Π is positive semidefinite, regardless of the effect's magnitude, extending prior results that required small-effect assumptions. A variational reformulation then shows the performative risk equals an adversarially robust classification objective where class-0 points may shift toward class-1 with a budget depending on both Π and θ, revealing an implicit regularization that shrinks the optimal parameter norm as the performative effect strengthens.

## Results

The paper's experiments are primarily qualitative, presented as accuracy curves and parameter trajectories rather than a single numeric table. On synthetic Gaussian classification tasks (2D and 7D), RPPerfGD achieves higher accuracy and faster convergence than non-performative baselines (RGD, RRGD) as the performative effect magnitude γ increases from 0 to 1. On the 7D Gaussian task with quadratic loss, the score-function-based SFPerfGD becomes numerically unstable for small variance σ, while RPPerfGD maintains stable convergence. On the binarized Housing dataset (18,000 samples, 20 runs), RPPerfGD outperforms RGD in accuracy as the performative shift magnitude λ on coordinates 0, 4, and 6 increases, and converges faster than the non-performative approach.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the three baselines from Perdomo et al. (ICML 2020): Repeated Risk Minimization (RRM) diverges as soon as the performative effect is non-negligible, making it unusable in practice. Repeated Gradient Descent (RGD) and its regularized variant (RRGD) ignore the performative effect entirely; RRGD provides a reasonable tradeoff by implicitly moderating the parameter magnitude through ridge regularization, but RPPerfGD consistently achieves higher accuracy and faster convergence once performative effects are present (γ > 0). Against the Score Function Performative Gradient Descent (SFPerfGD) from Izzo et al. (AISTATS 2022), which uses the score-function estimator for the performative gradient, RPPerfGD demonstrates a clear advantage in stability: in the 7D Gaussian experiment, SFPerfGD's estimator variance grows with dimension (scaling as d²) and becomes unstable for small noise σ, while RPPerfGD's reparameterization-based estimator maintains a covariance independent of dimension and converges reliably. On the Housing dataset, RPPerfGD also outperforms RGD as the performative shift magnitude increases, confirming the theoretical variance advantage in a realistic tabular setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the performative prediction framework of Perdomo et al. [2020] and the gradient-based optimization approaches of Izzo et al. [2022] and Miller et al. [2021], but departs from them in two key ways. First, it replaces the requirement of a full parametric distribution (needed by score-function estimators) with a push-forward measure model that only requires knowledge of the shift operator, enabling a reparameterization-based gradient estimator with provably lower variance. Second, it moves beyond the 'small performative effect' assumption (bounded Wasserstein sensitivity) that underpins prior convexity results, instead proving convexity under a directional condition (PSD shift matrix) that permits arbitrarily strong performative effects.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The convexity result without magnitude restrictions and the variational link to adversarially robust classification provide new theoretical justification for regularization in performative settings and open a path to transferring the rich robustness literature into performative learning. The reparameterization estimator is immediately more practical than score-function alternatives since it avoids needing a closed-form density. However, the scope remains limited to linear-in-parameters classifiers with shift operators and binary classification; extending to non-linear performative effects, multi-class settings, and using the robustness reformulation for model-free optimization are important open questions the authors identify.
