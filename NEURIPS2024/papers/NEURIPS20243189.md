## Problem

In agnostic boosting, where no realizability assumption is made, ERM achieves ε-excess error with O((log|H|)/ε²) samples, while all known agnostic boosting algorithms require O((log|H|)/ε⁴) samples—a quadratic gap in sample efficiency. This paper closes part of that gap by constructing a boosting algorithm with O((log|B|)/γ³ε³) sample complexity, improving upon the O(1/ε⁴) dependence of prior work while maintaining comparable oracle complexity.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes a sampling oracle for the population distribution over (feature, ±1 label) pairs and a γ-agnostic weak learning oracle that, given m samples from any relabeled distribution, returns a base-class hypothesis with correlation at least γ times the best in-class correlation minus a fixed tolerance ε₀. It outputs an improper classifier (a weighted sum of weak hypotheses, not necessarily in the base class) selected via post-hoc empirical risk minimization over all intermediate ensembles. Unusually, the weak learner receives a distribution constructed by blending fresh samples with relabeled samples from prior rounds, where pseudo-labels are generated via a second-order Taylor expansion of the potential function.

## Method

Algorithm 1 is a potential-based boosting procedure that, at each of T ≈ (log|B|)/γ²ε² rounds, draws only S ≈ 1/(γε) fresh samples and constructs a relabeled distribution D_t by mixing (with parameter σ) the previous round's distribution with new pseudo-labeled samples whose labels are determined by a second-order estimator of the potential φ. The key analytical innovation is a martingale concentration argument (via Freedman's inequality) on the base class B rather than a uniform convergence argument on the growing boosted class, showing that the empirical correlation on D_t tracks the population potential derivative Φ'₍D₎(H_t, ·) for all base hypotheses simultaneously. A modified branching criterion—adding the weak hypothesis if its empirical correlation exceeds a threshold τ, otherwise adding −sign(H_t)—circumvents an error in KK09's original logic and avoids needing fresh samples to evaluate the out-of-class negation. The potential φ(z) = max(2−z, (z+2)e^{−z}) is C² and 1-smooth, enabling the telescoping potential-decrease argument that yields the final ε-excess error guarantee.

## Results

Theoretically, the proposed agnostic boosting algorithm (Theorem 4) achieves O((log|B|)/γ³ε³) sample complexity, improving upon O((log|B|)/γ⁴ε⁴) for [KK09] and O((log|B|)/γ⁴ε⁶) for [BCHM20], while ERM requires O((log|B|)/γ²ε²) but is computationally intractable. Empirically, on 6 UCI classification datasets with decision stumps as weak learners, the proposed algorithm outperforms both baselines simultaneously on 18 out of 24 evaluation instances (across 0%, 5%, 10%, and 20% label noise). For example, on Ionosphere with no added noise, it achieves 0.97 accuracy versus 0.92 ([KK09]) and 0.89 ([BCHM20]); on German with 20% noise, it reaches 0.84 versus 0.78 and 0.74 respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against [KK09] (potential-based agnostic boosting, NeurIPS 2009), the proposed method wins on 5 of 6 datasets under no noise (losing only on Spambase, a data-rich dataset with 4601 samples where sample reuse benefits are less pronounced) and on 5 of 6 under 20% noise (again losing on Spambase). The margin is most pronounced on German (+6% at 20% noise) and Sonar (+5% at 20% noise). Against [BCHM20] (online agnostic boosting via regret minimization, NeurIPS 2020), the proposed method wins on 5 of 6 datasets under no noise (losing on Sonar where [BCHM20] achieves 0.91 vs. 0.88) and on 4 of 6 under 20% noise (losing on Spambase and Sonar). In the reinforcement learning application (Table 2), the proposed method improves sample complexity from O(1/γ⁴ε⁵) to O(1/γ³ε⁴) in the episodic model and from O(1/γ⁴ε⁶) to O(1/γ³ε⁵) in the ν-reset model compared to [BHS22] (NeurIPS 2022), a polynomial improvement in both ε and γ.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the potential-based agnostic boosting framework of Kanade and Kalai (KK09), which first achieved proper excess-error guarantees in the agnostic setting, by introducing a sample-reuse mechanism across boosting rounds inspired by variance reduction in convex optimization. It departs from both KK09 (which draws fresh samples each round) and Brukhim et al. (BCHM20, which reuses the entire dataset but relies on uniform convergence over the boosted class) by using a martingale concentration argument (Freedman's inequality) on the smaller base class to control generalization, avoiding the linear-in-rounds complexity penalty of uniform convergence over the improper boosted ensemble. The paper also identifies and corrects a subtle error in KK09's branching criteria that, while rectifiable in the original setting, would have blocked the improved sample complexity here.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result narrows the sample complexity gap between computationally efficient agnostic boosting (1/ε³) and the computationally intractable ERM baseline (1/ε²) from a factor of ε² to ε, a meaningful step toward making boosting a viable alternative to ERM in the agnostic regime. The sample-reuse technique, combined with the martingale-based analysis, is a reusable analytical tool that the authors demonstrate also improves reinforcement learning and halfspace learning bounds. However, the remaining gap to ERM, the increased oracle complexity in the main theorem (mitigated but not eliminated in Theorem 9), and the preliminary nature of the experiments (small UCI datasets, decision stumps only) leave open whether the theoretical gains translate to practical advantages at scale.
