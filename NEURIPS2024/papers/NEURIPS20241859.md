## Problem

Prior computationally efficient agnostic learners for Gaussian single-index models required the link function to be monotone and Lipschitz, while the more general class of link functions characterized by a finite information exponent k* (e.g., phase-retrieval activations with k*=2) had only been handled in the realizable or semi-random noise settings. This paper asks whether a sample- and computationally-efficient constant-factor agnostic learner exists for Gaussian SIMs under the general Assumption 1 (bounded Hermite coefficients, bounded fourth moment, bounded derivative moment), and answers affirmatively.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes n = Õ(d^{⌈k*/2⌉} + d/ε) i.i.d. labeled examples (x, y) where the x-marginal is N(0, I_d) and y is completely arbitrary (no realizability or noise-distribution assumption). It outputs a unit vector ŵ ∈ S^{d-1} satisfying L_2^σ(ŵ) ≤ C·OPT + ε with high probability, where C = O(C_{k*}) is a constant depending only on the link function's Hermite structure. The total runtime is poly(n, d), and the sample bound nearly matches known CSQ lower bounds even in the realizable case.

## Method

The algorithm has two stages. First, an initialization step applies the tensor unfolding algorithm of Richard & Montanari to the empirical k*-Chow tensor C_{k*} = E[y·He_{k*}(x]): it unfolds the tensor into a d^{⌊k*/2⌋} × d^{⌈k*/2⌉} matrix, extracts the top left singular vector, and performs a second unfolding to recover a vector w⁰ with w⁰·w* ≥ 1 − min{1/k*, 1/2}. This works despite adversarial (non-Gaussian) noise by exploiting the special signal structure of the Chow tensor and a recent matrix concentration inequality. Second, a Riemannian minibatch SGD procedure optimizes a truncated loss that retains only the first non-zero Hermite term of σ. A key 'alignment sharpness' lemma shows that whenever the iterate is sufficiently aligned with w* and the angle is above a threshold proportional to √OPT, the Riemannian gradient of the truncated loss points strongly toward w*, guaranteeing geometric contraction of the distance to w* and convergence in O(log(1/ε)) iterations.

## Results

The main result (Theorem 3.5) establishes a sample- and computationally-efficient agnostic proper learner for Gaussian SIMs under Assumption 1, achieving L₂²-error of O(C_{k*}·OPT) + ε using Õ(d^{⌈k*/2⌉} + d/ε) samples and O(log(C_{k*}/ε)) iterations of Riemannian minibatch SGD. The initialization step (Proposition 2.1) via tensor PCA on the k*-Chow tensor guarantees w⁰·w* ≥ 1 − min{1/k*, 1/2} with O(d^{⌈k*/2⌉}) samples. The sample complexity nearly matches known CSQ lower bounds, and the algorithm runs in poly(n,d) time with batch size Θ(C_{k*}·d·e^{k*}·log^{k*+1}(B₄/ε)/(εδ)).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical comparison table; comparisons are made at the level of assumptions and guarantees. Compared to [DNGL23], which achieves Õ(d^{k*/2} + d/ε) samples in the realizable setting, this paper extends the guarantee to the agnostic (adversarial label noise) setting with the same sample complexity, at the cost of a constant-factor approximation C = O(C_{k*}) to OPT rather than OPT + ε. Compared to prior agnostic learners [DGK+20, DKTZ22, WZDD23, ZWDD24], which require monotone and Lipschitz link functions (a subclass of Assumption 1 with k* = 1), this paper handles the full class of link functions with arbitrary information exponent k*, including non-monotone activations like σ(z) = z² and σ(z) = |z| (k* = 2). The paper explicitly shows that the smoothing-based algorithm of [DNGL23] provably fails in the agnostic regime (Claim B.1: the noise term dominates the signal unless OPT ≲ d^{-k*/4}), and that the partial-trace tensor PCA method of [DPVLB24] fails under adversarial noise (the noise can bury the signal unless OPT ≲ d^{-(k-2)/2}). The sample complexity Õ(d^{⌈k*/2⌉} + d/ε) nearly matches the CSQ lower bounds of [DLS22, AAM23], confirming near-optimality within the CSQ framework.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the alignment-sharpness framework for robust SIM learning (WZDD23, ZWDD24) from monotone/Lipschitz activations to the broader class of link functions characterized by the information exponent k* (Assumption 1), which includes non-monotone functions like z² and |z|. The initialization step repurposes the tensor unfolding algorithm from Richard & Montanari (RM14) in a novel way: despite being designed for Gaussian noise, it provably works under adversarial label noise by leveraging a recent matrix concentration inequality (BvH22) and Gaussian hypercontractivity. The optimization phase builds on the Riemannian gradient descent framework of DNGL23 and BAGJ21, but replaces the full loss with a 'truncated loss' retaining only the first non-zero Hermite term, which is the key conceptual departure enabling the agnostic analysis.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first polynomial-time constant-factor agnostic learner for Gaussian SIMs under the general Assumption 1, closing a gap where prior robust learners required monotonicity and prior general-activation learners required clean labels. The sample complexity of Õ(d^{⌈k*/2⌉} + d/ε) nearly matches known CSQ lower bounds, suggesting the approach is near-optimal within the statistical query framework. Open questions include whether the constant factor C = O(C_{k*}) can be tightened, whether the tensor PCA initialization extends to non-Gaussian marginals, and whether the truncated-loss trick generalizes to multi-index or deep models. The result also clarifies the boundary between what is computationally feasible (constant-factor approximation) and what is believed infeasible (OPT + ε) under cryptographic hardness assumptions.
