## Problem

Despite the empirical success of Diffusion Transformers (DiTs) in generative AI, no prior work provided theoretical guarantees for their score approximation, sample complexity, or computational limits. Previous diffusion model analyses either assumed a perfectly trained score estimator or restricted to simpler architectures like U-Nets, leaving the fundamental statistical and computational boundaries of the transformer-based DiT architecture uncharacterized. This paper asks: what are the approximation, estimation, and computational limits of latent DiTs under a low-dimensional linear subspace data assumption?

## Input / Output

The DiT score network receives a noisy data point x_t ∈ R^D and a diffusion time step t ∈ [T_0, T]. Internally, x_t is projected to a d_0-dimensional latent space via W_B^T, then reshaped into a d×L token matrix (where d = p² is the per-token feature dimension and L = (i/p)² is the sequence length for an i×i image). The transformer backbone processes this sequence and outputs a score estimate s_W(x_t, t) ∈ R^D, which drives the discretized backward SDE to produce generated samples. The key structural constraint is that the true data lies on an unknown d_0-dimensional linear subspace (x = Bh), making d_0 the governing parameter for all rates.

## Method

The paper provides a unified theoretical framework with three pillars. Statistically, it leverages the score decomposition into on-support and orthogonal components (from Chen et al. 2023) and proves that a transformer with K = O(ε^{-2L}) blocks can approximate the DiT score function to error O(√d_0/σ(t) · ε), with model size depending only on the latent dimension d_0 rather than the ambient dimension D. It then derives a sample complexity bound of Õ(n^{-1/3} · 2^{(1/ε)^{2L}} + n^{-1/3} + ε²) for score estimation and shows the generated distribution converges in total variation. Computationally, under SETH, it identifies a norm-based efficiency threshold (B = o(√log L) for attention weight-input products) below which almost-linear-time L^{1+o(1)} algorithms exist for both forward inference and backward gradient computation. The backward result exploits the inherent low-rank structure in DiT training gradients, casting the gradient as a chained low-rank approximation via the tensor trick, achieving 1/poly(L) accuracy in near-linear time when d = O(log L).

## Results

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical experiments or datasets. The key quantitative results are: (1) a score approximation bound of O(ε·√d₀/σ(t)) in L²(P_t) norm, requiring K = O(ε^(−2L)) transformer blocks with parameter norms (1/ε)^O(1); (2) a sample complexity bound of Õ(1/(n^(1/3)·T₀·T) · 2^((1/ε)^(2L)) + 1/(n^(1/3)·T₀·T) + ε²/(T₀·T)) for score estimation; (3) a total variation distance bound of Õ(√(ξ(n,ε,L)·log n)) for distribution recovery; and (4) L^(1+o(1))-time algorithms for both forward inference and backward training when d = O(log L) and entry norms are o(√(log L)), with a SETH-based lower bound ruling out sub-quadratic algorithms when norms reach O(√(log L)).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper is theoretical and does not run empirical comparisons against baselines. However, it makes several theoretical comparisons with prior work. Compared to Chen et al. (ICML 2023), this paper is the first to provide score approximation and sample complexity results specifically for DiTs (Chen et al. analyzed U-Net-based diffusion models), and its sample complexity first term achieves a 1/T convergence rate, improving upon Chen et al.'s T-independent first term. Compared to Zhu et al. (NeurIPS 2023), who assume an accurately trained score estimator, this paper derives rates based on the learned (imperfect) transformer score network. Compared to Oko et al. (ICML 2023), whose distribution estimation requires the initial density to be supported on [−1,1]^D and smooth at the boundary, this paper's Assumption 2.2 (Gaussian tail behavior) is noted as more practically relevant. For computational results, the paper builds directly on Alman and Song (NeurIPS 2023) for forward inference hardness and on Alman and Song (NeurIPS 2024b) plus the tensor trick (Diao et al., 2019) for backward computation, extending their results to the latent DiT setting with reshaping and latent embedding.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the score decomposition framework of Chen et al. (ICML 2023) from U-Net-based diffusion models to transformer-based DiTs, and leverages the universal approximation theorem of Yun et al. (ICLR 2020) to prove the first score approximation guarantee for DiTs. For computational analysis, it directly applies the fine-grained complexity results of Alman and Song (NeurIPS 2023) to characterize forward inference hardness, and uses the tensor trick of Diao et al. (NeurIPS 2019) to construct almost-linear-time gradient algorithms. The key departure from prior work is that all previous theoretical analyses of diffusion models either assumed U-Net architectures or assumed a perfectly trained score estimator; this is the first to provide end-to-end statistical and computational guarantees for the transformer-based DiT architecture specifically.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As DiTs become the dominant architecture in modern generative AI (Sora, Stable Diffusion 3, PixArt), this is the first theoretical characterization of their fundamental limits, providing practitioners with concrete design guidance: latent dimension should scale as O(log L), and normalization of K, Q, V projections is critical for both inference and training efficiency. The double exponential factor in the sample complexity bound (Remark 3.4) reveals a significant gap between current transformer universality proofs and what is likely achievable, motivating new proof techniques. The SETH-based norm threshold for efficient algorithms provides a principled framework for understanding when sub-quadratic DiT inference is possible, though the results are conditional and do not yield immediate practical implementations.
