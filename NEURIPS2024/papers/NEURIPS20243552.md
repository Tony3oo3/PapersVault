## Problem

Inferring 3D structure from sparse multi-view images requires jointly solving camera pose estimation and 3D reconstruction, which are co-dependent: accurate reconstruction needs precise poses, and pose estimation implicitly requires 3D understanding. Existing analysis-by-synthesis approaches that co-optimize both via photometric gradients overfit in sparse-view settings, and large initial pose errors from off-the-shelf systems cannot be easily corrected, degrading both outputs. The paper addresses how to robustly perform joint pose refinement and 3D reconstruction when only a handful of views are available and initial poses may contain significant outliers.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243552/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes 6–16 unposed RGB images of a single object along with initial 6-DoF camera poses from any off-the-shelf system (e.g., DUSt3R, Ray Diffusion, RelPose++), which may contain large errors or outliers. It outputs a 3D Gaussian Splatting representation (convertible to a textured mesh via Marching Cubes), refined 6-DoF camera poses, and novel view renderings. The 6-DoF camera parameterization is an 18-dimensional vector comprising the flattened relative extrinsic matrix and log focal-length ratios, enabling handling of real-world in-plane rotations and scale changes that 3-DoF parameterizations cannot represent.

## Method

SparseAGS extends analysis-by-synthesis with diffusion-based generative priors, termed 'analysis by generative synthesis.' It builds on DreamGaussian's 3D Gaussian Splatting pipeline, replacing the original 3-DoF Zero-1-to-3 conditioning with a finetuned 6-DoF variant and extending the Score Distillation Sampling loss to average noise predictions across all input views (Multi-view SDS). The framework iteratively co-optimizes 3D Gaussians and camera poses via gradient descent on photometric loss, while the SDS loss regularizes novel-view renderings for geometric consistency. A key robustness mechanism identifies outlier images by checking whether their inclusion in training significantly increases reprojection error on other views, then excludes them from 3D optimization. Outlier poses are subsequently corrected via a discrete render-and-compare search over a sphere of pose candidates (ranked by MSE and LPIPS), followed by continuous gradient-based refinement, before a final reconstruction integrates all views.

## Results

On NAVI with 8 input images, SparseAGS substantially improves pose accuracy over all three initialization baselines: with DUSt3R, Rot.@5° rises from 52.3% to 83.7% (+31.4) and CC@0.1 from 82.2% to 93.5% (+11.3); with Ray Diffusion, Rot.@5° jumps from 13.5% to 60.3% (+46.8). For 3D reconstruction (N=8, DUSt3R init), SparseAGS achieves PSNR 17.03 and LPIPS 0.1870, outperforming LEAP (PSNR 12.93, LPIPS 0.2902) and UpFusion (PSNR 13.27, LPIPS 0.2744). The full pipeline runs in 5–10 minutes on a single GPU, versus >10 hours for SPARF.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SPARF (the closest prior method for joint pose-3D co-optimization), SparseAGS wins on every metric across all three initializations; SPARF actually degrades accuracy in several cases (e.g., RelPose++ Rot.@15° drops 4.5 pts, DUSt3R CC@0.1 drops 0.3 pts), while SparseAGS consistently improves. For 3D reconstruction quality, SparseAGS (DUSt3R init, N=8) achieves PSNR 17.03 / LPIPS 0.1870, substantially outperforming the unposed baselines LEAP (12.93 / 0.2902) and UpFusion (13.27 / 0.2744), with the gap widening as more input views are added (at N=10, SparseAGS reaches PSNR 18.03 while LEAP plateaus at 12.98). The three pose estimation systems (RelPose++, Ray Diffusion, DUSt3R) serve as initializations rather than direct competitors; SparseAGS improves them by 16–70 points on Rot.@5° depending on the base method's initial quality. On synthetic datasets (GSO, ABO, OmniObject3D), SparseAGS applied to ID-Pose yields consistent ~16–20 point gains in Rot.@15°, confirming generalization beyond real-world captures.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the analysis-by-synthesis framework (BARF, NeRF-SP, SPARF) by injecting diffusion-based generative priors (via Score Distillation Sampling) into the joint pose-and-3D optimization loop, addressing the overfitting problem that arises with sparse views. It builds directly on DreamGaussian's 3D Gaussian Splatting pipeline, extending it from single-view to multi-view and from 3-DoF to 6-DoF camera parameterization by finetuning Zero-1-to-3. Unlike SPARF, which relies on estimated pixel correspondences that can be unreliable, SparseAGS uses a discrete render-and-compare search for outlier correction, and unlike pose-free methods (LEAP, UpFusion), it explicitly models camera geometry to preserve input-image details.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SparseAGS provides a practical, fast (5–10 min) pipeline that unifies off-the-shelf pose estimation with high-fidelity 3D reconstruction in sparse-view settings, removing the need for precise ground-truth cameras that prior reconstruction methods require. Key limitations include dependence on reasonably accurate initial poses (fails if a large fraction are wrong), inability to handle occlusion or truncation, and restriction to object-centric scenes. Open questions include extending the framework to scene-level novel-view generative priors, reducing sensitivity to initial pose quality, and scaling to more complex multi-object or cluttered environments.
