## Problem

3D Gaussian Splatting achieves real-time rendering quality comparable to NeRFs but fails on in-the-wild photo collections characterized by varying illumination, exposure changes, and dynamic occluders (pedestrians, vehicles). While NeRF-based methods handle these conditions via per-image appearance embeddings and uncertainty modeling, 3DGS's explicit per-primitive color storage and lack of shared parameters make direct adaptation infeasible—incorrect training signals from occluded or differently-lit regions can irreversibly corrupt geometry through the densification/pruning pipeline.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240670/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an unconstrained photo collection (e.g., 100–500+ images from Photo Tourism or NeRF On-the-go) with SfM-estimated camera poses and a 3D point cloud initialization, and produces a 3D Gaussian scene representation renderable at ~108 FPS. At test time, a novel camera pose and an appearance embedding (optimized in 128 Adam steps for unseen images) condition the rendering. The appearance MLP can be 'baked' back into standard 3DGS spherical harmonics, yielding a representation with no additional inference cost. A per-pixel binary uncertainty mask is also produced to identify occluded regions during training.

## Method

WildGaussians introduces two coupled modules on top of 3DGS. For appearance modeling, each Gaussian carries a trainable embedding (initialized with Fourier features for locality) and each training image carries its own embedding; both are fed with the Gaussian's 0th-order SH color into a small MLP (2×128, ReLU) that outputs per-channel affine parameters (β, γ) applied to the view-dependent color. Training uses DSSIM on the un-toned render (for geometry) and L1 on the toned render (for appearance), decoupling structural and photometric supervision. For occlusion handling, DINOv2 ViT-S/14 patch features from the ground-truth image are passed through a single trainable affine layer + softplus to produce per-patch uncertainty; the uncertainty is optimized via a DINO cosine-similarity loss between training and rendered image features, which—unlike MSE or DSSIM—remains robust under large appearance shifts. The uncertainty is converted to a binary mask (gradient scaling ≤ 1) before weighting the color loss, preventing disruption of 3DGS's absolute-gradient-based densification. Sky regions are initialized via Fibonacci sphere sampling at 10× scene radius.

## Results

On the Photo Tourism dataset (Brandenburg Gate, Sacre Coeur, Trevi Fountain), WildGaussians achieves 27.77 / 22.56 / 23.63 PSNR, outperforming the best NeRF-based baseline (K-Planes: 25.49 / 20.61 / 22.67) and the best 3DGS-based baseline (SWAG: 26.33 / 21.16 / 23.10) while rendering at 117 FPS. On the NeRF On-the-go dataset, WildGaussians achieves 22.15 average PSNR (0.756 SSIM, 0.167 LPIPS), surpassing NeRF On-the-go (21.71 PSNR) and 3DGS (19.30 PSNR) with 400× faster rendering than the NeRF baseline. Training takes 7.2 GPU hours on Photo Tourism and 0.50 GPU hours on NeRF On-the-go, with real-time rendering at 108–117 FPS on an RTX 4090.

## Baselines & Comparisons

WildGaussians outperforms all 12 baselines on both datasets. Against NeRF-based methods, it beats K-Planes by +2.28 PSNR on Brandenburg Gate and +0.96 on Trevi Fountain, while rendering at 117 FPS versus <1 FPS. Against the closest 3DGS-based competitor SWAG, it gains +1.44 PSNR on Brandenburg Gate and +0.50 on Sacre Coeur, though SWAG trains 9× faster (0.8 vs 7.2 GPU hrs) and renders at only 15 FPS. On the NeRF On-the-go dataset, WildGaussians surpasses NeRF On-the-go by +0.44 PSNR (22.15 vs 21.71) with 400× faster rendering, and beats 3DGS by +2.85 PSNR (22.15 vs 19.30), with the gap widening to +3.94 PSNR under high occlusion (23.03 vs 19.03). The advantage over GS-W is +2.59 PSNR on average (22.15 vs 19.56), and over Mip-Splatting +3.03 PSNR (22.15 vs 19.12). The only metric where a baseline edges out WildGaussians is LPIPS on NeRF On-the-go (0.167 vs 0.163 for NeRF On-the-go), a negligible difference.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) to handle in-the-wild photo collections by adapting two ideas from the NeRF literature: per-image appearance embeddings (from NeRF-W) and DINO-based uncertainty modeling (from NeRF On-the-go). The key architectural departure is that 3DGS stores colors explicitly per Gaussian, so the paper introduces per-Gaussian appearance embeddings coupled with a small MLP that predicts an affine color transformation, which can be 'baked' back into standard 3DGS after training. For occlusion handling, it replaces the MSE/DSSIM-based uncertainty losses used by prior NeRF methods with a DINO cosine similarity loss, which is more robust to appearance changes. Compared to concurrent 3DGS-based approaches (SWAG, GS-W, Scaffold-GS) that rely on external hash grids or CNN features for appearance conditioning, WildGaussians uses a simpler per-Gaussian embedding strategy that preserves the locality and editability of the 3DGS representation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work bridges a practical gap: NeRF-based methods handle appearance variation and occlusions well but render slowly, while 3DGS renders in real time but struggles with uncontrolled capture conditions. The ability to 'bake' the learned appearance back into a standard 3DGS representation after training is a practically important property for downstream applications (editing, compression, deployment). Limitations remain: the method cannot capture specular highlights, and regions occluded in nearly all training images are still poorly reconstructed. The authors suggest incorporating diffusion model priors for under-observed regions as future work, and the DINO-based uncertainty loss, while more robust than MSE/DSSIM, still fails in some challenging scenarios.
