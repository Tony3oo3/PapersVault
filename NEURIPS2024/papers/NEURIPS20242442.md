## Problem

Large-scale text-to-image diffusion models like Stable Diffusion v1.5 require 3.44 GB of storage, making deployment on resource-constrained devices impractical. Existing quantization methods for diffusion models focus on 4-bit or higher precision on small-scale models trained on constrained datasets, leaving the challenge of extremely low-bit (sub-2-bit) quantization of large-scale models like SD-v1.5 largely unexplored and lacking fair, extensive evaluation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242442/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The quantized model takes a text prompt as input and produces a synthesized RGB image, using the PNDM sampler with 50 inference steps and classifier-free guidance (CFG) at inference time. The UNet weights are stored at 1.99 bits (219 MB vs. 1.72 GB in FP16), and time embeddings are pre-computed and cached offline rather than computed through projection layers at inference, reducing storage by over 25× for those components. The first and last convolutional layers remain at 8 bits, while all other layers use a mixed-precision recipe of 1–4 bits.

## Method

BitsFusion compresses the SD-v1.5 UNet to 1.99 bits through three key components. First, a mixed-precision strategy assigns per-layer bit widths (1, 2, 3, or 4 bits) by jointly analyzing MSE (pixel-level quality) and CLIP score (semantic alignment) quantization errors, weighted by a parameter-size factor η to favor compressing large, insensitive layers. Second, initialization techniques include pre-computing and caching time embeddings, adding a balance integer to exploit symmetric weight distributions (treating n-bit as log(2ⁿ+1) bits), and alternating optimization for scaling factor initialization inspired by the Lloyd-Max algorithm. Third, a two-stage training pipeline is used: Stage-I performs CFG-aware quantization distillation and feature distillation from the full-precision teacher with a Beta(3,1) time-step sampling distribution that oversamples high-error regions near t=999, while Stage-II fine-tunes with vanilla noise prediction for stable convergence.

## Results

BitsFusion quantizes the SD-v1.5 UNet to 1.99 bits (219 MB, 7.9× smaller than the 1.72 GB FP16 original) while achieving a CLIP score of 0.3212 on 1K PartiPrompts, surpassing the full-precision SD-v1.5 (0.3175). On the 30K MS-COCO 2014 validation set, the Stage-II model yields 0.002–0.003 higher CLIP scores than SD-v1.5 across CFG scales 2.5–9.5. On GenEval, BitsFusion outperforms SD-v1.5 at all CFG scales (e.g., 0.4747 vs 0.4271 overall at CFG=8.5). In human evaluation on PartiPrompts, 54.4% of users prefer BitsFusion images over SD-v1.5 for aesthetics and text-image alignment.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BitsFusion (1.99-bit) outperforms all five baselines on CLIP score. Against the full-precision SD-v1.5 (32-bit, 0.3175), BitsFusion achieves 0.3212, a +0.0037 gain, while being 7.9× smaller in storage. Compared to the closest quantization baseline, Q-Diffusion (4-bit, 0.3137), BitsFusion uses roughly half the bits yet scores 0.0075 higher. Against other 2-bit methods—LSQ (0.2849), EfficientDM (0.2918), and Apple-MBP (0.3023)—BitsFusion leads by 0.0163, 0.0294, and 0.0189 respectively. The advantage extends beyond CLIP score: on TIFA and GenEval, the Stage-II model consistently surpasses SD-v1.5 across all CFG scales, and in human evaluation 54.4% of raters prefer BitsFusion outputs. Notably, the FID metric tells a different story (Stage-II FID is slightly worse than SD-v1.5), which the authors attribute to FID's sensitivity to training data distribution rather than perceptual quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of diffusion model quantization work (PTQ4DM, Q-Diffusion, EfficientDM, Q-DM) that has primarily studied 4-bit or higher quantization on small-scale models trained on constrained datasets like CIFAR-10. BitsFusion departs from this by targeting the large-scale Stable Diffusion v1.5 UNet at an extreme 1.99-bit precision, introducing a mixed-precision recipe derived from per-layer sensitivity analysis (MSE + CLIP score), novel initialization techniques (balance integer, time embedding caching, alternating scaling-factor optimization), and a two-stage training pipeline combining CFG-aware distillation with quantization-error-aware time step sampling. The base QAT framework builds on LSQ (learned step size quantization), and the training objective follows the standard denoising diffusion probabilistic model formulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Achieving 7.9× compression of the SD-v1.5 UNet (1.72 GB → 219 MB) while matching or exceeding full-precision quality is practically significant for deploying text-to-image generation on resource-constrained devices. The finding that a heavily quantized model can outperform its full-precision counterpart (via distillation and targeted training) challenges the assumption that quantization necessarily degrades generative quality. Limitations include: only the UNet is quantized (VAE and text encoder remain full-precision), results are specific to SD-v1.5 and may not transfer to newer architectures like SDXL, and the FID score actually worsens in Stage-II despite better human preference. Open questions include whether these techniques extend to activation quantization, newer diffusion architectures, and whether the 1.99-bit recipe generalizes across model scales.
