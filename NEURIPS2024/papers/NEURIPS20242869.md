## Problem

Non-autoregressive Transformers (NATs) generate images by iteratively revealing masked tokens, but it remains unclear why this progressive paradigm works and how computation is actually allocated across token types and generation steps. The paper identifies that NATs implicitly treat visible and [MASK] tokens symmetrically and recompute all tokens at every step, despite these choices being suboptimal for the underlying information flow.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242869/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model operates in the discrete VQGAN token space (16×16 grid for 256×256, 32×32 for 512×512) with a codebook of 1024 entries. Conditioning is provided via adaptive layer normalization for class labels (ImageNet) or by concatenating text embeddings with visual tokens (MS-COCO). Generation proceeds over 8–48 iterative steps, each revealing a subset of tokens, and the final token map is decoded to pixels by the frozen VQGAN decoder.

## Method

ENAT introduces two mechanisms derived from empirical analysis of NAT internals. Spatially, it disentangles visible and [MASK] token processing: visible tokens pass through a deep encoder (15 layers) to build fully contextualized representations, while [MASK] tokens are decoded by a single-layer decoder using a novel SC-Attention that simultaneously handles [MASK]-to-[MASK] self-interaction and [MASK]-to-visible cross-interaction. Temporally, at each generation step only the newly decoded tokens are re-encoded; previously computed visible-token features are projected by a lightweight module and injected via SC-Attention to supplement the current step's decoding, eliminating redundant recomputation. Training alternates (50/50) between standard forward passes and a 'reuse forward' mode where a random subset of visible tokens is masked to simulate the previous step's features, with a stop-gradient applied to the reused features.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256, ENAT-L achieves a FID of 2.79 with only 0.3 TFLOPs (8 steps), surpassing the strongest diffusion baseline U-ViT-H (FID 3.37, 2.4 TFLOPs) by 17% in FID while requiring 8× less computation. ENAT-B reaches FID 3.53 at just 0.2 TFLOPs, outperforming all prior NAT baselines (MaskGIT: 4.92, Token-Critic: 4.69, AdaNAT: 2.86 at 0.9 TFLOPs). On ImageNet 512×512, ENAT-L achieves FID 4.00 with 1.3 TFLOPs, beating U-ViT-H (4.60, 3.4 TFLOPs) and MaskGIT-RS (4.46, 13.1 TFLOPs). On MS-COCO text-to-image, ENAT-B attains FID 6.82 at 0.3 TFLOPs, outperforming U-ViT's 8-step variant (6.92, 0.5 TFLOPs) with lower cost.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ENAT dominates all NAT baselines on ImageNet 256×256: ENAT-L (FID 2.79, 0.3 TFLOPs) beats AdaNAT (2.86, 0.9 TFLOPs), Draft-and-revise (3.41), MaskGIT-FSQ (4.53, 0.8 TFLOPs), Token-Critic (4.69, 1.9 TFLOPs), MaskGIT (4.92, 1.2 TFLOPs), and MAGE (6.93, 1.0 TFLOPs) on both FID and computational cost. Against diffusion models, ENAT-L outperforms U-ViT-H (3.37, 2.4 TFLOPs), MDT-XL (4.00, 2.2 TFLOPs), DiT-XL (5.18, 2.2 TFLOPs), and LDM (3.60, 52.3 TFLOPs) with 7–8× lower TFLOPs. The only method achieving a lower FID is StyleGAN-XL (2.30, 1.5 TFLOPs), but ENAT-L still requires 5× less computation. On ImageNet 512×512, ENAT-L (4.00, 1.3 TFLOPs) surpasses U-ViT-H (4.60, 3.4 TFLOPs), MaskGIT-RS (4.46, 13.1 TFLOPs), and Token-Critic-RS (4.03, 34.8 TFLOPs). On MS-COCO, ENAT-B (6.82, 0.3 TFLOPs) edges out U-ViT 8-step (6.92, 0.5 TFLOPs) and substantially outperforms U-ViT 4-step (16.20, 0.4 TFLOPs) and Frido (8.97).

## Relation to Prior Work

ENAT builds directly on the MaskGIT progressive generation paradigm for non-autoregressive Transformers (NATs), where [MASK] and visible tokens are processed jointly at each step. The key departure is an empirical analysis that reveals NATs naturally learn asymmetric spatial interactions (visible tokens primarily encode, [MASK] tokens primarily decode) and repetitive temporal computation (only newly decoded tokens change significantly between steps). ENAT then redesigns the architecture to explicitly exploit these patterns via a disentangled encoder-decoder split with SC-Attention and a cross-step computation reuse mechanism, drawing conceptual inspiration from masked image modeling (MAE, CrossMAE) but applied to discrete token generation rather than continuous pixel reconstruction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled explanation for why NATs work and translates that understanding into a concrete architectural redesign that achieves 8× lower inference cost than U-ViT-H while matching or exceeding its FID, making high-quality token-based image generation substantially more accessible. The spatial and temporal interaction findings are broadly applicable to any NAT-based system (including text-to-image at scale, e.g., Muse) and suggest that future NAT designs should allocate computation asymmetrically rather than uniformly. Open questions include whether these patterns persist at multi-billion-parameter scale, on larger datasets like LAION-5B, and for unconditional or video generation, all of which the authors acknowledge as future work.
