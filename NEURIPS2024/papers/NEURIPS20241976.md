## Problem

Existing any-to-any multimodal models such as 4M and Unified-IO are limited to a small set of modalities (7 for 4M), raising the question of whether scaling to tens of highly diverse modalities—spanning image-like, geometric, semantic, structured, and text data—causes the performance degradation typical of multitask learning. This paper demonstrates that a single model can be trained on 21 modalities without sacrificing per-task performance compared to specialized models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241976/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts any subset of 21 modalities as input at 224×224 resolution and generates any other subset via iterative token decoding (progressive unmasking). All modalities are first converted to discrete token sequences by modality-specific tokenizers: ViT-based VQ-VAEs for image-like data (RGB, edges, feature maps), Bottleneck MLP VQ-VAEs with Memcodes quantization for non-spatial data (global embeddings, 3D human poses), and a shared WordPiece vocabulary for text, bounding boxes, color palettes, and metadata. Notably, raw RGB pixels and T5-XXL text embeddings are consumed as continuous inputs (bypassing the discrete bottleneck) rather than being tokenized, while all other modalities pass through a discrete token representation.

## Method

The core mechanism is a modality-specific discrete tokenization pipeline that maps every input modality—whether a 224×224 image, a 1024-dim global embedding, a parameterized SMPL pose, or a scalar metadata value—into a short sequence of discrete tokens, thereby reducing all 21 tasks to a single per-token cross-entropy classification objective. The architecture is an encoder-decoder Transformer (inheriting 4M's design) with learnable modality embeddings and fixed positional encodings, trained with a multimodal masked objective where random subsets of tokens from all modalities serve as inputs and targets. Training is co-conducted across three datasets (CC12M with all 21 pseudo-labeled modalities, COYO700M with 7 modalities, and C4 text) using a diversified mixture of masking strategies governed by Dirichlet sampling, including all-to-all, RGB-to-all, and caption-biased variants. The model is scaled to 3B parameters (XL) over 500B tokens, and the encoder can be extracted as a standalone ViT backbone for downstream transfer. This tokenization-plus-masking formulation eliminates the need for task-specific heads, loss balancing, or gradient surgery, which are conventional requirements in multitask learning.

## Results

On out-of-the-box (zero-shot) evaluation, 4M-21 XL achieves 20.8° mean angle error on DIODE surface normals, 0.68 relative depth error, 48.1 mIoU on COCO semantic segmentation, 32.0 mIoU on COCO instance segmentation, 78.3/92.4 top-1/top-5 kNN retrieval on ImageNet-1K, and 92.0 mm 3D human keypoint error on 3DPW. Critically, these numbers match or approach the dedicated pseudo-labeler models (e.g., 20.8° vs. Omnidata's 22.5° for normals; 48.1 vs. M2F-L's 48.0 for semantic segmentation) while simultaneously solving three times more tasks than 4M-7. In transfer experiments, 4M-21 XL reaches 87.1% ImageNet-1K top-1, 56.0 mIoU ADE20K, 96.5% NYUv2 δ₁, and 48.4 AP³ᴰ ARKitScenes, matching or slightly exceeding 4M-7 XL on all four tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the dedicated pseudo-labelers, 4M-21 XL is competitive or superior on most tasks: it beats Omnidata on surface normals (20.8° vs. 22.5°), matches it on depth (0.68 vs. 0.68), matches M2F-L on semantic segmentation (48.1 vs. 48.0), is close to SAM on instance segmentation (32.0 vs. 32.9), and outperforms HMR on 3D human keypoints (92.0 vs. 130.0 mm), though it trails 4D-Humans (92.0 vs. 81.3) and DINOv2 on kNN retrieval (78.3 vs. 82.1 top-1). Compared to the 7-modality predecessor 4M-7 XL, 4M-21 XL preserves performance on all shared tasks (20.8 vs. 20.6 normals; 48.1 vs. 48.1 semantic segmentation) while additionally solving instance segmentation, kNN retrieval, and 3D pose estimation. Against Unified-IO and Unified-IO 2, 4M-21 XL shows a large margin: 20.8° vs. 31.0–37.4° on normals, 0.68 vs. 0.82–1.00 on depth, and 48.1 vs. 32.9–44.3 mIoU on semantic segmentation, while also solving tasks those models cannot handle at all. In transfer experiments (Table 2), 4M-21 XL matches 4M-7 XL on ImageNet-1K (87.1 vs. 87.0), ADE20K (56.0 vs. 55.0), NYUv2 (96.5 vs. 96.1), and ARKitScenes (48.4 vs. 48.1), and in multimodal transfer (Table 3) it improves over 4M-7 XL on all three tasks when depth is available (e.g., 52.5 vs. 51.0 Hypersim mIoU, 51.3 vs. 50.1 ARKS AP³ᴰ). For text-to-image generation (Table 11), 4M-21 XL achieves FID 26.0 and CLIP score 24.3 on COCO, outperforming 4M-7 XL (27.0/23.7) but remaining well below Stable Diffusion 2.1-base (10.1/25.5), which is trained on orders of magnitude more data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends 4M (Mizrahi et al., NeurIPS 2023), which introduced massively multimodal masked modeling over 7 modalities, by scaling to 21 modalities through modality-specific discrete tokenizers (ViT-based VQ-VAEs for image-like data, Bottleneck MLP VAEs with Memcodes for structured data like poses and global embeddings, and WordPiece for text/sequence modalities). It departs from autoregressive unified models like Unified-IO and Pix2Seq by retaining the encoder-decoder masked modeling objective, which enables any-to-any prediction from any subset of modalities rather than fixed input-output pairs. The tokenization of diverse data types—SAM instances, 3D human poses, image metadata, color palettes, and feature maps from DINOv2/ImageBind—represents the main methodological contribution beyond 4M's original scope.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper empirically confirms that tripling the number of training modalities (7→21) does not degrade per-task performance, addressing a key open question about negative transfer in multitask learning and providing a practical recipe for scaling multimodal models. It unlocks new capabilities—cross-modal retrieval via predicted global embeddings, steerable generation conditioned on metadata or color palettes, and multimodal editing—that were not possible with prior any-to-any models. However, the authors note that transfer/emergent capabilities remain largely untapped, tokenization quality is a bottleneck, and the approach has not been validated with decoder-only architectures, leaving open whether the masked encoder-decoder design is optimal for future scaling.
