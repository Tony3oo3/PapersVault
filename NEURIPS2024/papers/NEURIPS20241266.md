## Problem

Existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for robotics face two intertwined challenges: pre-trained MLLMs lack sufficient reasoning ability for complex robotic tasks (e.g., long-horizon planning, affordance discrimination), and their attention-based LLM backbones incur high computational costs for both fine-tuning and real-time inference. The paper asks whether an efficient VLA model can simultaneously possess strong robotic reasoning and acquire manipulation skills in a cost-effective manner.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241266/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB image (224×224 or 336×336) and a text prompt as input, processed through a CLIP/SigLIP ViT-Large encoder and a pre-trained tokenizer respectively. It produces two simultaneous outputs: a natural language response (for reasoning, planning, or VQA) generated by detokenizing Mamba's output, and a 6-DoF end-effector pose (3D position vector + 3×3 rotation matrix, extended to 7-DoF with gripper state for grasping) predicted by a lightweight MLP policy head that consumes a single global token obtained by pooling the language output tokens. In the SAPIEN simulation, the model predicts a 2D contact pixel that is back-projected to 3D using depth information.

## Method

RoboMamba replaces the attention-based LLM in standard VLA architectures with Mamba's selective State Space Model, achieving linear inference complexity while retaining context-aware reasoning. The architecture connects a frozen CLIP vision encoder to Mamba via a simple MLP projection layer, concatenating visual and text tokens before feeding them into the Mamba backbone. Training proceeds in two stages: Stage 1 (alignment pre-training on 558K image-text pairs, then instruction co-training on general + RoboVQA data) builds visual common sense and robotic reasoning; Stage 2 freezes all RoboMamba parameters and trains only a 3.7M-parameter MLP policy head (0.1% of total parameters) on 10K SAPIEN simulation samples to learn SE(3) pose prediction. The key finding is that once sufficient reasoning capability is established, manipulation skills can be acquired with minimal fine-tuning—requiring only a few dozen minutes on a single A100 GPU—yielding 3× faster inference and 10× fewer updated parameters compared to prior VLA approaches like ManipLLM and RoboFlamingo.

## Results

On general reasoning benchmarks, RoboMamba (2.7B Mamba LLM, 224×224 input) achieves 63.3 on OKVQA, 64.2 on GQA, 57.1 on VizWiz, and 86.3 on POPE, outperforming all 7B MLLM baselines on these four benchmarks despite using less than half the parameters. On the robotic-specific RoboVQA benchmark, RoboMamba scores 42.8 BLEU-4, substantially exceeding TinyLLaVA (29.6) and LLaMA-AdapterV2 (8.1). For manipulation in SAPIEN, RoboMamba achieves a 7.0% improvement on seen tasks and 2.0% on unseen tasks over the previous SOTA ManipLLM, while fine-tuning only 0.1% of model parameters (3.7M) and running 3× faster at inference.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RoboMamba (2.7B) outperforms all 7B MLLM baselines (LLaVA 1.5, SPHINX, Qwen-VL, LLaMA-AdapterV2) on OKVQA, GQA, VizWiz, and POPE, demonstrating that the smaller Mamba backbone with co-training is competitive on core VQA tasks. However, it trails LLaVA 1.5 and SPHINX on MME (1297 vs. 1511/1476), MMB (60.9 vs. 64.3/66.9), and MM-Vet (29.4 vs. 30.5/36.0), which the authors attribute to the 2.7B model capacity. Against the same-size TinyLLaVA (2.7B), RoboMamba wins decisively on RoboVQA (42.8 vs. 29.6 BLEU-4; 62.7 vs. 43.5 BLEU-1) but loses on MMB (60.9 vs. 68.3) and MM-Vet (29.4 vs. 31.7). For manipulation, RoboMamba surpasses ManipLLM by 7.0% on seen and 2.0% on unseen SAPIEN tasks, while fine-tuning 10× fewer parameters (3.7M vs. 41.3M) and achieving 3× faster inference; it also outperforms RoboFlamingo, which updates 35.5% of model parameters. The ablation in Figure 3a confirms Mamba-2.7B outperforms RWKV-3B on both general and robotic reasoning under identical training, validating the SSM choice.

## Relation to Prior Work

RoboMamba follows the LLaVA-style two-stage training paradigm (alignment pre-training followed by instruction co-training) but replaces the attention-based LLM backbone with Mamba's selective state space model, making it the first work to introduce Mamba to robotic VLA. It departs from existing VLA models like ManipLLM and RoboFlamingo, which fine-tune the LLM backbone during the manipulation stage, by freezing the entire Mamba backbone and training only a 3.7M-parameter policy head (0.1% of total parameters). The co-training strategy that mixes general vision instruction data with RoboVQA robotic data is a deliberate extension of prior MLLM training to simultaneously build common-sense and robotic-specific reasoning before any manipulation fine-tuning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central finding—that sufficient robotic reasoning capability allows manipulation skills to be acquired with minimal fine-tuning (7MB, a few dozen minutes)—offers a practical pathway to deploying VLA models on resource-constrained robots without the heavy compute of full LLM fine-tuning. The 3× inference speedup over attention-based VLA models is directly relevant to real-time robotic control. However, the evaluation is limited to open-loop simulation in SAPIEN with a single Franka Panda arm, and the 2.7B model shows clear gaps on complex reasoning compared to 7B+ MLLMs. Open questions include whether the 'reasoning-first, manipulation-cheap' paradigm generalizes to closed-loop, multi-step manipulation and whether scaling to larger Mamba models preserves the efficiency advantage.
