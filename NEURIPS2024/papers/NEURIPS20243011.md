## Problem

Existing bilevel optimization (BLO) algorithms either handle unconstrained problems or require expensive joint projections onto the coupled constraint set {(x,y): g^c(x,y)≤0}, which become computationally intractable at scale or when g^c is not jointly convex. This paper asks whether an efficient fully first-order algorithm can bypass joint projections entirely while solving BLO with general coupled inequality constraints.

## Input / Output

The method takes an upper-level objective f(x,y), a lower-level objective g(x,y) that is strongly convex in y, coupled inequality constraints g^c(x,y) that need only be convex in y (not jointly convex in (x,y)), and easily-projectable domain sets X and Y. It produces the optimal upper-level variable x*, the corresponding lower-level solution y*, and associated Lagrange multipliers μ*, using only first-order gradient information with no Hessian or Hessian-vector products. The LICQ condition must hold for the constraints in a neighborhood of the lower-level optimum, and the penalty parameter γ must satisfy γ > l_{f,1}/α_g to preserve strong convexity in y.

## Method

The core contribution is a primal-dual-assisted penalty reformulation that decouples x and y by combining a penalty term γ(g(x,y)−v(x)) to regulate the lower-level optimality gap with a Lagrangian term ⟨μ, g^c(x,y)⟩ to enforce constraints without joint projection. This transforms the BLO problem into a single-level minimization over x of F_γ(x) = max_{μ≥0} min_y [f(x,y) + γ(g(x,y)−v(x)) + ⟨μ, g^c(x,y)⟩], where the inner max-min is solved by an accelerated projected gradient method on the concave dual function D(μ). The gradient ∇F_γ(x) is derived via a Danskin-like theorem that augments the standard penalty gradient with Lagrange multiplier terms ⟨μ*, ∇_x g^c(x,y*)⟩, which are essential for correctness under coupled constraints and absent in prior unconstrained formulations. The BLOCC algorithm alternates outer projected gradient steps on x with inner MaxMin solver iterations (accelerated PGD on μ with inner PGD on y), achieving Õ(ε^{-2.5}) complexity for general convex-in-y constraints and Õ(ε^{-1.5}) when g^c is affine in y, where the inner solver enjoys linear convergence.

## Results

On the SVM hyperparameter optimization task, BLOCC achieves 0.767±0.039 test accuracy on the diabetes dataset and 0.761±0.014 on fourclass, outperforming GAM (0.721 and 0.715) and matching or slightly exceeding LV-HBA (0.765 and 0.748), while running ~5× faster than GAM (1.7s vs 8.8s on diabetes). On the transportation network design problem, BLOCC scales to a 26-node Seville network (49,216 variables, 13,336 constraints) achieving an upper-level utility of 138.78 in ~67,000s, whereas LV-HBA fails to converge on any network with 9 or more nodes. The theoretical iteration complexity is Õ(ε^{-2.5}) for general coupled constraints, tightening to Õ(ε^{-1.5}) when constraints are affine in y.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against LV-HBA [75], BLOCC achieves comparable or slightly better SVM accuracy (0.767 vs 0.765 on diabetes; 0.761 vs 0.748 on fourclass) while being ~1.7× faster on diabetes (1.7s vs 2.9s). The decisive advantage appears in the transportation network experiment: LV-HBA fails to converge within 24 hours on networks with 9+ nodes due to the intractable joint projection step, while BLOCC successfully solves the 26-node Seville network (49,216 variables) in ~67,000s. Against GAM [72], BLOCC wins decisively on both accuracy and speed: 0.767 vs 0.721 accuracy on diabetes (a 4.6-point gap) and 0.761 vs 0.715 on fourclass (a 4.6-point gap), with runtimes of 1.7s vs 8.8s and 1.9s vs 13.5s respectively. GAM is not included in the transportation comparison because it cannot handle lower-level domain constraints, relying on hypergradient computation in an unconstrained space.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the penalty-based bilevel optimization framework of Shen et al. [62], which used g(x,y) − v(x) as a penalty term for unconstrained BLO, to the setting where the lower-level feasible set depends on the upper-level variable through coupled inequality constraints. The key conceptual departure from the closest prior work, LV-HBA [75], is replacing the computationally expensive joint projection of (x,y) onto {g^c(x,y) ≤ 0} with a Lagrangian duality-based primal-dual reformulation that decouples x and y. This avoids the need for g^c to be jointly convex and sidesteps a projection that may lack a closed form. The paper also builds on Danskin-type theorems and accelerated gradient methods for the inner max-min subproblems, establishing the first linear convergence result for a strongly convex-concave max-min problem with a constrained maximization parameter.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is demonstrated by the transportation network experiment on Seville (26 nodes, 49K variables, 13K constraints), where LV-HBA fails entirely while BLOCC converges in under 70 seconds, highlighting that joint-projection-based methods are fundamentally infeasible at scale. The O(ε^{-2.5}) complexity (tightening to O(ε^{-1.5}) for affine constraints) improves upon the O(ε^{-3}) of LV-HBA and matches the best known rates for unconstrained BLO. Limitations include the requirement of strong convexity of g in y, LICQ, and convexity of g^c in y (not jointly convex), which restricts applicability to non-convex or stochastic lower-level problems. Open questions include extending the framework to non-convex lower-level objectives, stochastic settings, and relaxing the LICQ condition.
