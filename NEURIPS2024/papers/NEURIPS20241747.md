## Problem

Pre-trained policies for long-horizon RL tasks still require substantial online RL interactions to adapt to unseen tasks, because existing methods either finetune the entire policy or train a separate high-level policy for goal switching. The core gap is that pre-trained goal-conditioned policies cannot sequentially achieve multiple goals in a single attempt, forcing the costly online phase to learn when and how to transition between subgoals.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241747/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The policy takes a concatenated sequence of a goal-sequence prompt (up to K goals, with the task goal always last) followed by the trajectory of observations and actions, processed autoregressively. Observations are environment-specific: 6-dim vectors in MazeRunner, 59-dim vectors in Kitchen, 9-dim vectors in GridWorld, or 64×64 RGB images in Crafter. During online adaptation, the output is an optimized discrete goal sequence (the prompt) rather than updated model parameters, with the policy frozen and only the prompt varying across episodes.

## Method

MGPO pre-trains a causal Transformer (GPT-2 backbone, 0.6M parameters) via behavior cloning on task-agnostic trajectories, where prompts are constructed by hindsight multi-goal relabeling: a random sub-sequence of goals is sampled from each trajectory with the final goal always retained, so the policy learns to stitch together behaviors aligned with arbitrary goal sequences. During online adaptation, the model parameters are frozen and only the prompt is optimized using a multi-armed bandit formulation (UCB or Bayesian posterior estimation), where new candidate prompts are sampled from the highest-return trajectory collected so far rather than explored in the full combinatorial goal space. This trajectory-based sampling keeps prompts in-distribution and interpretable, while the MAB selection balances exploration of uncertain prompts against exploitation of known high-return ones. The prompt length naturally modulates exploration: shorter prompts provide less information and induce more stochastic behavior, while longer prompts yield more deterministic, exploitative trajectories.

## Results

MGPO achieves the best or near-best average return across all five test environments within a budget of 100 online episodes. On MazeRunner-30 (the longest-horizon task, 500 steps), MGPO reaches -14.21±0.19, nearly doubling the performance of the strongest baseline PTGM (-26.74±1.71). On Kitchen and GridWorld, MGPO scores 0.63±0.02 and 0.58±0.03 respectively, roughly 2.5× the performance of PTGM (0.25±0.03 in both). On Crafter, MGPO (15.66±0.14) is essentially tied with PTGM (15.72±0.12), while both substantially outperform SPiRL (10.96±0.25) and BC-finetune (1.88±0.19).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MGPO outperforms both SPiRL and PTGM in four of five environments. Against SPiRL [38], MGPO gains are substantial: on MazeRunner-30, MGPO (-14.21) improves over SPiRL (-28.94) by roughly 14.7 return points, and on Kitchen MGPO (0.63) is nearly 3× SPiRL (0.22). Against PTGM [60], the closest competitor, MGPO leads by 0.55 on MazeRunner-15, 12.5 on MazeRunner-30, 0.38 on Kitchen, and 0.33 on GridWorld. The only environment where MGPO does not clearly win is Crafter, where PTGM (15.72) marginally edges out MGPO (15.66) by 0.06 — a difference well within the reported standard deviations. The paper attributes PTGM's relative strength in Crafter to the limited diversity of the pre-training dataset, which makes the policy less sensitive to prompt content, thereby reducing the advantage of MGPO's prompt optimization over PTGM's high-level RL policy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Decision Transformer architecture (Chen et al., 2021) and hindsight relabeling (Andrychowicz et al., 2017) into a multi-goal pre-training framework, building on AMAGO's (Grigsby et al., 2024) multi-goal relabeling strategy but repurposing it for policy pre-training rather than exploration. It departs from the dominant paradigm of task-agnostic pre-training followed by online RL (SPiRL, PTGM, BC-finetune) by replacing the costly online RL phase with a bandit-based prompt optimization process over goal sequences, drawing a direct analogy to prompt tuning in language models. The key conceptual shift is treating the goal sequence as a 'prompt' that a frozen Transformer policy interprets, eliminating the need to train a separate high-level policy for goal switching.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating online RL entirely and replacing it with interpretable bandit-based prompt search, MGPO addresses a core bottleneck in sample-efficient RL for long-horizon tasks, adapting within just 100 episodes across diverse domains. The robustness finding that discrete, trajectory-derived prompts (UCB/BPE) are significantly more stable under observation/action noise than continuous or genetic-search prompts (BBT/GRIPS) provides practical guidance for deployment. Open questions include scaling to real-world robotic settings, handling out-of-distribution prompts that can trigger unpredictable behaviors, and whether the approach generalizes to tasks where goal decomposition is not naturally available.
