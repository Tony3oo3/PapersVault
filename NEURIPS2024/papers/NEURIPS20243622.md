## Problem

Pre-trained vision-language models like CLIP exhibit significant performance drops under domain shift (e.g., 83.0% on real images vs. 14.2% on quickdraw), and existing adaptation methods require labeled data or domain-specific prompts. The paper identifies that this degradation stems from two inherent biases: the visual encoder prioritizes domain information over category information, and the text encoder develops a preference for class names semantically related to the domain. The goal is to correct these biases using only unlabeled multi-domain data, without any labels, domain names, or parameter updates.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a set of unlabeled RGB images (resized to 224×224) drawn from multiple unknown domains, along with a list of class names and a fixed text prompt template (e.g., 'a photo of a [class]'). No domain labels, class labels, or per-domain prompts are required. The output is a standard K-way class prediction computed via softmax over cosine similarities between calibrated image and text features. A single hyperparameter M (number of K-Means clusters) controls the granularity of domain estimation, and the method is robust to M not matching the true number of domains.

## Method

UMFC is a training-free, label-free feature calibration framework with two complementary modules. The Image Feature Calibration (IFC) module applies K-Means clustering to unlabeled image features, computes each cluster's mean vector μ_i as a proxy for domain-specific visual bias, and subtracts it from every image feature before L2-normalization, thereby aligning cross-domain feature distributions within each class. The Text Feature Calibration (TFC) module exploits the observation that the transition direction between domains is consistent across image and text embedding spaces; it estimates per-domain text shift vectors as t̂^i = μ_i − μ_avg (where μ_avg is the global mean), then for each class text feature t_j computes a domain-averaged calibrated feature by subtracting each t̂^i and averaging the normalized results across all M clusters. The final prediction uses the standard CLIP softmax over cosine similarities between the calibrated image and text features, requiring no gradient updates or additional training.

## Results

On DomainNet under multi-domain Unsupervised Calibration, UMFC achieves 60.79% average accuracy, improving over zero-shot CLIP (57.88%) by ~3% and matching CLIP-D (61.03%) without requiring domain-specific prompts. Combined with CLIP-E's prompt ensemble, UMFC+CLIP-E reaches 61.68% average, surpassing all label-free baselines. Under Transductive Learning, UMFC attains 61.11% on DomainNet and 65.19% (with CLIP-E) on ImageNet-Variants, outperforming PromptStyler (59.40%) and MIRO (54.00%). In Test-Time Adaptation, UMFC-Memory achieves 60.85% on DomainNet, beating TPT (59.62%) while requiring only 296 seconds vs. TPT's 197 minutes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UMFC (60.79% avg) outperforms zero-shot CLIP (57.88%) by ~3% and CLIP-E (60.33%) by ~0.5% without any labels or training. Against the oracle CLIP-D (61.03%), which requires knowing each test sample's domain to select a prompt, UMFC+CLIP-E (61.68%) achieves higher accuracy without any domain information. UMFC matches or exceeds the unsupervised fine-tuning method MUST (60.89%) while being training-free (57 seconds vs. 10 hours on 2 GPUs). Compared to few-shot methods, UMFC (60.79%) beats CoOp with 1 shot per class (60.48%) and CLIP-Adapter with 1 shot (60.00%) without any labeled data; UMFC+CLIP-E (61.68%) is within 0.4% of CoOp with 4 shots (62.09%) and matches CLIP-Adapter with 4 shots (61.66%). In Test-Time Adaptation, UMFC-Memory (60.85%) surpasses TPT (59.62%) by 1.23% on DomainNet while running ~40× faster (296 s vs. 197 min). Under Transductive Learning, UMFC (61.11%) outperforms PromptStyler (59.40%) and MIRO (54.00%) on DomainNet, and UMFC+CLIP-E (65.19%) leads on ImageNet-Variants over CLIP-E (64.53%).

## Relation to Prior Work

UMFC builds directly on CLIP's dual-encoder architecture and the cross-modality transition direction concept from Dunlap et al. (ICLR 2023), which posits that domain shift directions are shared across image and text embedding spaces. It departs from the dominant line of CLIP adaptation work—prompt tuning (CoOp, CoCoOp), adapter learning (CLIP-Adapter), and test-time prompt optimization (TPT)—by requiring neither labeled data nor any parameter updates. Instead, it repurposes a simple K-Means clustering step and mean-subtraction calibration to achieve domain-invariant features, positioning itself as a training-free alternative to methods that rely on optimization or domain-name priors (PODA, LADS, PromptStyler).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

UMFC offers a practically attractive recipe for multi-domain adaptation: no labels, no domain names, no gradient steps—just cluster and subtract. The diagnostic of dual encoder biases (visual encoder encoding domain over category; text encoder favoring domain-related class names) provides a useful lens for understanding CLIP's domain sensitivity that may inform future calibration or debiasing work. However, the method still requires unlabeled data from the target domain to estimate calibration vectors, limiting its applicability in truly source-free or single-sample scenarios. Open questions include whether the cross-modality direction assumption holds for more extreme domain gaps and whether the approach extends to tasks beyond classification (e.g., retrieval, segmentation).
