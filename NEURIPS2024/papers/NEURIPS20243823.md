## Problem

Geometric GNNs rely on radius graphs with distance cutoffs, which inherently limits them to capturing short-range interactions and fails on large molecules or periodic systems where long-range effects are critical. Existing remedies—LSRM's fragmentation-based hierarchy and Ewald MP's reciprocal-space message passing—each carry structural constraints (specific fragmentation algorithms or lack of discrete spatial resolution) that limit flexibility across diverse molecular systems.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243823/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 3D atom coordinates, atom type indices, and (for periodic systems) cell vectors, then internally constructs a regular grid of mesh points within the cell via a principal-component-aligned bounding box with padding. It outputs a scalar molecule-level energy (sum of atom-wise and mesh-wise energy contributions) and atom-level forces computed as the negative gradient of the predicted energy with respect to coordinates, rather than being directly regressed. The mesh grid resolution (N_x × N_y × N_z) and the atom-to-mesh assignment cutoff are the two additional hyperparameters introduced beyond the base GNN.

## Method

Neural P³M augments any geometric GNN with a parallel mesh-point representation and three interacting sub-blocks per layer: (1) an Atom2Atom short-range block (the base GNN on a radius graph), (2) a Mesh2Mesh long-range block implemented as a Fourier Neural Operator that parameterizes the P³M influence function G directly in the Fourier domain and applies FFT-accelerated convolution over the mesh, and (3) bidirectional Atom2Mesh / Mesh2Atom representation-assignment blocks that perform trainable continuous-filter convolution (derived from SchNet's CFconv) to exchange information between the two scales. The final atom and mesh representations are updated via residual connections with layer-normalized cross-scale contributions, and the decoder sums atom-wise and mesh-wise energies separately before combining them. This design reimagines the classical P³M pipeline—charge assignment, FFT convolution, back-interpolation—as differentiable, learnable operations, making the framework a drop-in enhancer compatible with SchNet, PaiNN, DimeNet++, GemNet-T, and ViSNet without requiring any specific molecular fragmentation strategy.

## Results

On the MD22 dataset, ViSNet-NP³M achieves state-of-the-art energy and force MAEs across the four largest molecules (e.g., 0.7751 kcal/mol energy and 0.2561 kcal/mol/Å force on the double-walled nanotube, diameter 32.39 Å), and the best energy or force on the three smaller molecules. Compared to vanilla ViSNet, the framework yields an average 34.6% improvement in energy MAE and 21.2% in force MAE. On the OE62 dataset, integrating Neural P³M with SchNet, PaiNN, DimeNet++, and GemNet-T delivers an average 22% reduction in energy MAE over the baselines, with the largest gain (47.4%) for SchNet (131.3 → 69.1 meV).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On MD22, ViSNet-NP³M outperforms all competing methods (sGDML, SO3KRATES, Allegro, Equiformer, MACE) on energy MAE for every molecule, and also achieves the best force MAE on the four largest molecules (AT-AT, AT-AT-CG-CG, Buckyball catcher, Double-walled nanotube). Against the two other long-range extensions of ViSNet—ViSNet-Ewald and ViSNet-LSRM—Neural P³M wins on all seven molecules for both energy and force, with the most dramatic gap on the double-walled nanotube (0.7751 vs. 0.7909 for Ewald and 1.8230 for LSRM in energy). On OE62, Neural P³M consistently beats the Ewald MP baseline across all four integrated GNNs (e.g., SchNet: 69.1 vs. 81.1 meV; DimeNet++: 41.5 vs. 48.1 meV) and also surpasses LSRM (Table 6), while running faster than Ewald MP due to FFT-based convolution. The framework also improves Equiformer on the two largest MD22 molecules (energy: 0.3978→0.3038 for Buckyball catcher; 1.1945→0.6208 for the nanotube), confirming its versatility beyond ViSNet.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reimagines the classical Particle-Particle Particle-Mesh (P3M) algorithm from computational physics as a trainable neural network framework, bridging Ewald summation, FFT-accelerated mesh methods, and geometric GNNs. It departs from prior long-range interaction approaches in two key ways: unlike LSRM, it avoids dependence on fragmentation algorithms (e.g., BRICS) by using a uniform mesh grid, and unlike Ewald MP, it introduces explicit mesh-point representations that enable bidirectional information flow between short-range (atom) and long-range (mesh) subnetworks via learnable continuous filter convolutions derived from SchNet. The long-range block is parameterized as a Fourier Neural Operator with learnable spectral weights, replacing the fixed physical Green's function of classical P3M.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework addresses a fundamental limitation of geometric GNNs—their inability to capture long-range interactions beyond a local cutoff—while remaining architecture-agnostic and compatible with six different GNN backbones. Its practical impact is significant for molecular dynamics simulation and drug discovery, where accurate force prediction in large or periodic systems is essential. Open questions include whether the mesh resolution and assignment cutoff can be learned end-to-end, whether alternatives to FFT (e.g., sparse or hierarchical methods) could further improve efficiency, and how the framework scales to systems with thousands of atoms under periodic boundary conditions.
