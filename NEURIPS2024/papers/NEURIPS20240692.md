## Problem

Existing RAG evaluation metrics are either coarse-grained (n-gram, embedding similarity) and fail to capture fine distinctions in long-form responses, or they evaluate only end-to-end quality without decomposing errors into retriever vs. generator causes. Moreover, the reliability of these metrics relative to human judgment remains largely unvalidated, leaving practitioners without actionable diagnostic signals for improving specific RAG components.

## Input / Output

RAGCHECKER consumes a tuple of (query, document corpus, ground-truth answer) along with the RAG system's retrieved top-k chunks and generated response. All texts are decomposed into atomic claims by Llama3-70B (via RefChecker), and pairwise entailment is checked between claims and reference texts (ground truth, retrieved chunks, or the response itself). The output is a structured suite of scores: a single overall F1 for system ranking, two retriever metrics (claim recall, chunk-level context precision), and six generator metrics (faithfulness, relevant/irrelevant noise sensitivity, hallucination, self-knowledge, context utilization) that jointly decompose every claim in the response into its provenance and correctness.

## Method

The core mechanism is claim-level entailment checking: a text-to-claim extractor decomposes any text into a set of atomic claims, and a claim-entailment checker (both implemented with Llama3-70B via the RefChecker framework) determines whether each claim is entailed in a given reference text. This enables a Venn-diagram-style decomposition where response claims are partitioned into correct (entailed in ground truth) vs. incorrect, and incorrect claims are further categorized by source: entailed in a relevant chunk (relevant noise sensitivity), entailed in an irrelevant chunk (irrelevant noise sensitivity), or entailed in no chunk (hallucination). Correct claims are similarly split into those supported by retrieved context (context utilization) versus those generated from the model's self-knowledge. The framework is validated through a meta-evaluation on 280 pairwise human preference judgments across 10 domains, where RAGCHECKER achieves the highest Pearson/Spearman correlation with human annotators (61.93/60.90 on overall assessment) compared to all baseline frameworks (RAGAS, TruLens, ARES, CRUD-RAG, BLEU, ROUGE, BERTScore).

## Results

In the meta evaluation, RAGCHECKER achieves the highest correlation with human judgments across all three aspects: Overall Assessment (Pearson 61.93, Spearman 60.90), Completeness (Pearson 60.67, Spearman 58.11), and Correctness (Pearson 49.66, Spearman 46.95). The strongest baseline, RAGAS Answer Similarity, reaches only Pearson 48.31 / Spearman 57.23 on Overall Assessment. When applied to 8 RAG systems across 10 domains, the best-performing system (E5-Mistral + GPT-4) achieves an overall F1 of 52.7, while the worst (BM25 + Llama3-8B) scores 42.1, with retriever choice (E5-Mistral vs. BM25) consistently yielding ~5-point F1 gains regardless of generator.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RAGCHECKER outperforms all seven baseline evaluation frameworks on every aspect of the meta evaluation. The closest competitor is RAGAS Answer Similarity (embedding-based, using text-embedding-ada-002), which achieves Pearson 48.31 on Overall Assessment versus RAGCHECKER's 61.93 — a gap of ~14 points. Traditional n-gram and embedding metrics (BLEU, ROUGE-L, BERTScore) all fall below Pearson 44 on Overall Assessment, confirming their inadequacy for long-form RAG evaluation. Among LLM-based frameworks, TruLens (35.15), ARES (17.81), and CRUD-RAG (41.25) are all substantially outperformed, with ARES performing worst overall. The human annotator agreement ceiling (Pearson 70.09) indicates RAGCHECKER captures roughly 88% of the inter-annotator signal, leaving meaningful room for further improvement. In the diagnostic evaluation of 8 RAG systems, RAGCHECKER reveals that E5-Mistral consistently improves claim recall (83.5 vs. 74.0) and context precision (61.8 vs. 52.3) over BM25, and that GPT-4 achieves the highest context utilization (61.4–60.4) and lowest noise sensitivity among all four generators.

## Relation to Prior Work

RAGCHECKER builds directly on RefChecker's claim extraction and entailment checking pipeline, repurposing it from hallucination detection to fine-grained RAG evaluation. It departs from prior RAG evaluation frameworks (TruLens, RAGAS, ARES, CRUD-RAG) that provide coarse-grained end-to-end scores, and from generator-only evaluation approaches (RGB, RECALL, NoMIRACL) that cannot disentangle retrieval errors from generation errors. The key conceptual departure is applying claim-level entailment symmetrically to both the retriever (claim recall, context precision) and the generator (faithfulness, noise sensitivity, hallucination, self-knowledge, context utilization), enabling module-specific diagnosis rather than a single holistic score.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RAGCHECKER provides practitioners with actionable diagnostic signals that pinpoint whether performance gaps stem from retrieval or generation, enabling targeted improvements rather than blind hyperparameter tuning. The identified trilemma among context utilization, noise sensitivity, and faithfulness, along with the finding that open-source models exhibit chunk-level blind trust, offers concrete guidance for RAG system design. Limitations include less insightful retriever metrics, inability to distinguish neutral from contradiction entailment results, and restriction to English text-only evaluation, leaving open questions about multi-modal and multilingual RAG assessment.
