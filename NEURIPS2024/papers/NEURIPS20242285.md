## Problem

All existing fairness attacks on GNNs require modifying the connectivity or features of existing nodes (e.g., adding/removing edges between real users), which is typically infeasible in real-world settings where attackers lack such authority. Node injection—adding fake nodes to the graph—is a more practical attack vector but has remained unexplored for GNN fairness. This paper asks whether GNN fairness can be significantly undermined through node injection alone, without touching the original graph structure.

## Input / Output

The method takes an original graph G=(V, A, X) with node labels Y and binary sensitive attributes s under a gray-box setting (attacker sees the graph, labels, and sensitive attributes but not model architecture or parameters). It produces a poisoned graph G' augmented with |V_I| ≤ b injected nodes, each of degree ≤ d, along with an optimized feature matrix X_I for those nodes. The injected features are clamped to the range of original features (and rounded for discrete features) to remain undetectable, and the true label set Y is never modified.

## Method

NIFA is a gray-box poisoning attack that operates in two stages. First, it performs node injection guided by two principles: (1) an uncertainty-maximization principle that uses a Bayesian GNN with Monte Carlo dropout to estimate per-node prediction variance and selects the top-k% highest-uncertainty nodes in each sensitive group as targets, and (2) a homophily-increase principle that assigns injected nodes equally across sensitive groups and connects each injected node exclusively to d target nodes sharing its sensitive attribute, provably increasing node-level homophily ratio. Second, it optimizes the injected nodes' feature matrix X_I via an iterative procedure that alternates between training a surrogate GNN (cross-entropy loss) and updating X_I with a composite objective combining cross-entropy (utility preservation), statistical-parity and equal-opportunity fairness losses (to widen inter-group prediction gaps), and a feature-separation constraint (to ensure injected features for different groups remain distinct). The iterative co-training of surrogate and features prevents overfitting to a single model configuration, and the entire attack requires only ~1% node injection with negligible accuracy degradation.

## Results

NIFA achieves state-of-the-art fairness attack performance across all three datasets with GCN as the victim model, increasing ΔSP from 7.13% to 17.36% and ΔEO from 5.10% to 15.59% on Pokec-z, while maintaining accuracy at 70.50% (vs. 71.22% clean). On DBLP, NIFA pushes ΔEO to 20.33% (from 1.91% clean) with only a 2.5% accuracy drop. The attack also effectively targets fairness-aware GNNs: FairVGNN's ΔEO on Pokec-z nearly quadruples from 2.59% to 9.28%, and FairSIN's ΔEO on DBLP surges from 0.45% to 23.65%, all with a mere 1% node injection budget.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NIFA dominates all six baselines on every fairness metric where comparisons are available. Against the three fairness-attack baselines, NIFA outperforms FA-GNN by 10.74% in ΔSP and 6.92% in ΔEO on Pokec-z (17.36% vs. 6.62%; 15.59% vs. 8.67%), and on DBLP it surpasses G-FairAttack by 6.69% in ΔSP (13.49% vs. 6.80%) and 17.39% in ΔEO (20.33% vs. 2.94%), while FATE achieves only 3.62% ΔSP and 2.12% ΔEO on DBLP. The utility-attack baselines (AFGSM, TDGIA, G²A2C) are far less effective at degrading fairness—TDGIA actually reduces ΔSP below clean levels on Pokec-z (1.66% vs. 7.13%)—confirming that utility and fairness objectives are largely orthogonal. Notably, FATE and G-FairAttack could not be deployed on Pokec-z and Pokec-n due to scalability issues (OOM or >72h runtime), whereas NIFA completes attacks on all three datasets in under 170 seconds with ~4–6 GB memory.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of two previously separate attack paradigms on GNNs: node injection attacks (AFGSM, TDGIA, G²A2C), which target model utility, and fairness attacks (FA-GNN, FATE, G-FairAttack), which require modifying existing node connectivity. NIFA is explicitly positioned as the first work to combine these, using node injection as the attack vector to target fairness rather than accuracy. The uncertainty-maximization principle borrows the Monte Carlo dropout framework from Bayesian GNN work (UD-GNN), while the homophily-increase principle draws on graph fairness literature showing that intra-group message propagation amplifies bias. The paper does not build on a single prior method but rather repurposes structural attack ideas for a fairness objective that prior node-injection work ignored.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that fairness-aware GNNs (FairGNN, FairVGNN, FairSIN) remain significantly vulnerable to a mere 1% node injection is practically important for anyone deploying GNNs in fairness-sensitive applications (recruitment, lending, healthcare), as it suggests current fairness mechanisms are insufficient against realistic attack vectors like fake account creation. The paper's defense discussion (masking high-uncertainty nodes, strengthening cross-group connections, fairness auditing) is preliminary and the authors acknowledge that dedicated defense mechanisms are still lacking. Open questions include extending to black-box settings, multi-sensitive-attribute scenarios, structure-based fairness, and developing robust defenses that do not sacrifice utility.
