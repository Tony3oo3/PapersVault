## Problem

Existing audio-driven talking face methods achieve precise lip synchronization but produce rigid, unconvincing faces because expressive non-lip dynamics (eye gaze, blinking, subtle expressions) and natural head movements are either neglected or modeled with separate, interleaved regression/generation pipelines. Additionally, recent diffusion-based approaches that improve quality are too computationally expensive for real-time interactive applications, leaving a gap between high-fidelity synthesis and low-latency deployment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240021/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a single arbitrary face image (any identity, including non-photorealistic), a speech audio clip of arbitrary length, and three optional scalar/vector control signals: main gaze direction (spherical coordinates), normalized head-to-camera distance, and a global emotion offset. It outputs a 512×512 talking face video at up to 40 FPS in online streaming mode with only 170 ms preceding latency on a single RTX 4090, generated via a sliding-window scheme over the audio to handle sequences longer than the model's fixed window length W.

## Method

VASA-1 first constructs an expressive, disentangled face latent space by extending the 3D-aided MegaPortraits architecture with two new loss functions: a pairwise consistency loss (l_consist) that enforces decoupling of head pose from subtle facial dynamics, and a cross-identity cosine similarity loss (l_cross_id) that preserves identity during motion transfer. On top of this latent space, an 8-layer Diffusion Transformer (29M params) jointly generates holistic facial dynamics (lip motion, non-lip expression, eye gaze, blinking) and head pose as a single latent sequence, conditioned on Wav2Vec2 audio features plus optional gaze/distance/emotion signals via classifier-free guidance. The key architectural insight is treating all facial dynamics as one unified latent variable rather than decomposing them into separate regression and generative sub-problems, enabling the model to learn natural co-occurrence patterns across the full spectrum of facial behavior. Trained on ~500K clips from ~9.5K identities, the system achieves real-time inference by operating in the compact latent space rather than pixel space, with 50 denoising steps per window.

## Results

On the VoxCeleb2 benchmark, VASA-1 achieves the best lip-sync confidence (S_C = 8.841) and lowest lip-sync distance (S_D = 6.312), surpassing even real videos (S_C = 7.640, S_D = 7.189). On the OneMin-32 benchmark, VASA-1 attains CAPP = 0.465 (vs. 0.383 for SadTalker, 0.205 for Audio2Head, 0.002 for MakeItTalk) and the lowest FVD_25 of 105.88, roughly half that of the next-best baseline (209.77 for Audio2Head). The pose variation intensity ΔP reaches 0.316 on OneMin-32, the highest among all methods, though still below real videos (0.405). VASA-1 also supports real-time 512×512 generation at up to 40 FPS with only 170 ms latency on a single RTX 4090.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VASA-1 outperforms all three baselines on every reported metric across both benchmarks. Against MakeItTalk [77], the gap is the largest: VASA-1 improves S_C from 4.176 to 8.841 on VoxCeleb2 and from −0.123 to 7.957 on OneMin-32, while reducing FVD_25 from 304.83 to 105.88. Against Audio2Head [62], VASA-1 gains +1.97 in S_C and +0.26 in CAPP on OneMin-32, and nearly halves FVD_25 (105.88 vs. 209.77). Against SadTalker [74], the closest competitor, VASA-1 still leads by +2.46 in S_C, +0.08 in CAPP, and +0.06 in ΔP on OneMin-32, with FVD_25 roughly halved (105.88 vs. 214.51). Notably, VASA-1's lip-sync scores (S_C, S_D) exceed those of real videos, attributed to the audio classifier-free guidance, while CAPP and ΔP remain below real-video levels, indicating residual gaps in audio-pose alignment and head-motion vividness.

## Relation to Prior Work

VASA-1 builds on the 3D-aided face reenactment framework from MegaPortraits [19] and [64] for its disentangled face latent space, but introduces new pairwise transfer and cross-identity losses (l_consist, l_cross_id) to achieve stronger disentanglement between facial dynamics, head pose, and identity. Conceptually, it departs from the prevailing trend in talking face generation toward further factor separation (e.g., SadTalker [74] splitting into lip 3DMM, blinks, and head pose; [71] decomposing lip vs. non-lip features) by instead modeling all facial dynamics as a single holistic latent variable generated by a Diffusion Transformer conditioned on Wav2Vec2 audio features. This unified generative formulation, combined with classifier-free guidance over multiple control signals, replaces the interleaved regression-and-generation pipelines used by prior methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ability to generate 512×512 talking face video at 40 FPS with negligible latency (170 ms) is a significant practical advance that makes lifelike AI avatars viable for real-time communication, education, and accessibility applications. The holistic dynamics modeling challenges the field's assumption that finer-grained factor separation is necessary for quality, suggesting that a well-constructed expressive latent space plus a unified diffusion model can capture the full spectrum of natural facial behavior more coherently. Open questions include extending to full upper body, handling non-rigid elements (hair, clothing), and whether the approach generalizes to more diverse talking styles and non-English speech without additional training data. The CAPP metric also provides the community with a more principled audio-pose alignment evaluation than the previously used Beat Align Score.
