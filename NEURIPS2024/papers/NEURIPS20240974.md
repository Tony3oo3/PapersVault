## Problem

Existing reinforcement learning methods cannot directly solve the minimum-cost reach-avoid problem, where a policy must simultaneously reach a goal, avoid unsafe states, and minimize cumulative cost. Standard approaches either treat goal-reaching as a reward (making cost optimization secondary) or reformulate the problem as a constrained MDP with a fixed cost threshold, both of which yield suboptimal policies that do not truly minimize cost subject to the reach-avoid constraints.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an augmented state vector (x, y, z) where x is the original continuous system state, y ∈ {−1, 1} is a binary safety flag tracking whether the trajectory has entered the unsafe set, and z ∈ ℝ is a cost upper-bound that decreases by the per-step cost each timestep. It outputs a deterministic control policy (the mode of a stochastic policy learned in phase 1) and the optimal cost upper-bound z*, which is computed online via 1D bisection on the learned reach value function or learned offline as a regression map (x, y) → z*. The horizon T is flexible and determined by when the augmented state first enters the augmented goal region.

## Method

RC-PPO converts the minimum-cost reach-avoid problem into a pure reach problem on an augmented system by introducing a binary safety indicator y and a cost-budget variable z, with augmented dynamics that track cumulative cost and safety violations. The augmented goal function ĝ(x,y,z) = max{g(x), Cy, −z} encodes all three constraints (goal reached, no unsafe states visited, cost ≤ z₀) into a single reach specification, making the problem an epigraph reformulation where minimizing z₀ subject to the reach value function being ≤ 0 yields the optimal policy. Training proceeds in two phases: Phase 1 uses a PPO-style actor-critic with a reachability Bellman equation (using min instead of max in the backup) and GAE to learn a z-conditioned stochastic policy and value function, with z₀ uniformly sampled per rollout; Phase 2 converts the policy to deterministic, fine-tunes the value function, and solves for the optimal z* via bisection. The paper proves a policy gradient theorem for the resulting Reachability MDP (with an absorbing state) and establishes almost-sure convergence to a locally optimal policy under standard step-size and Lipschitz assumptions.

## Results

RC-PPO achieves comparable or higher reach rates than all baselines while reducing cumulative cost by up to 57% across six MuJoCo-based benchmarks (Pendulum, PointGoal, Safety Hopper, Safety HalfCheetah, WindField, FixedWing). On the Pendulum environment with added control noise, RC-PPO incurs an additional cumulative cost of only 35.3, compared to 92.0 for RESPO, 97.7 for PPO_βL, 156.3 for SAC_βL, and 223.2 for CPPO_XM. A Pareto-front analysis (Figure 6) confirms that RC-PPO dominates the entire achievable trade-off curve of the surrogate CMDP formulation solved with PPO, demonstrating the gain comes from problem structure rather than hyperparameter tuning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RC-PPO outperforms all five baseline families on cumulative cost while matching or exceeding their reach rates. Against the goal-conditioned CRL [Eysenbach, NeurIPS 2022], RC-PPO achieves similar reach rates on simple tasks (Pendulum) but substantially lower cost, since CRL does not optimize for cost at all. Against the CMDP-based CPPO [Stooke, ICML 2020] and RESPO [Ganai, NeurIPS 2024], RC-PPO achieves 2–6× lower cumulative cost (e.g., 35.3 vs. 223.2 for CPPO_XM on Pendulum); RESPO, which incorporates reachability analysis to adapt Lagrange multipliers, performs better than CPPO but still trails RC-PPO by ~2.6×. Against the static-Lagrangian PPO_β [Schulman, 2017] and SAC_β [Haarnoja, ICML 2018] variants, RC-PPO dominates: high-β variants (PPO_βH, SAC_βH) sacrifice reach rate (0.31 and 0.21 respectively) to reduce cost, while low-β variants maintain reach rate but incur 2.7–4.4× higher cost. Critically, the Pareto-front grid search (Figure 6) shows that no combination of reward coefficients in the surrogate CMDP recovers RC-PPO's cost–reach-rate trade-off, confirming the advantage is structural rather than a tuning artifact.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the reach-avoid RL framework of Hsu et al. (RSS 2021), which uses Hamilton-Jacobi reachability to solve reach-avoid problems via a modified Bellman equation, by adding cumulative cost minimization through a state-augmentation trick that converts the constrained problem into a pure reach problem on an augmented system. It departs from the CMDP-based safe RL line (CPPO, RESPO) by showing that the fixed-threshold constraint formulation is fundamentally incapable of recovering the optimal minimum-cost policy, and instead uses a two-phase PPO algorithm that first learns a z-conditioned policy and then solves for the optimal cost upper-bound via 1D root-finding. The connection to epigraph reformulation from convex optimization provides a clean theoretical justification for why the augmented formulation is equivalent to the original problem.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The minimum-cost reach-avoid formulation is practically important for energy-constrained systems (quadrotors, spacecraft, autonomous driving) where simply reaching a goal safely is insufficient and the manner of reaching it matters. The paper's key limitation is the reliance on deterministic dynamics, which precludes domain randomization and limits real-world deployment; extending to stochastic dynamics remains an open challenge. The proof that no CMDP hyperparameter choice can recover the optimal minimum-cost policy (Appendix I) is a useful negative result for the safe RL community, clarifying when the CMDP abstraction is fundamentally inadequate versus merely poorly tuned.
