## Problem

Existing methods for re-posing dynamic objects from video either rely on category-specific templates (restricting them to humans or animals) or, in the template-free setting, produce low visual quality and cannot render in real-time. The paper addresses the gap of automatically discovering a skeleton model for arbitrary articulated objects from multi-view video while simultaneously achieving high-fidelity, real-time novel view synthesis.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241979/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes multi-view video sequences (50–200 frames, 6–18 training views) with known camera poses and produces a reposable articulated 3D model: a set of 3D Gaussians in canonical space bound to a discovered skeleton of joints and bones. Rendering operates at 400×400 or 800×800 resolution at over 100 FPS, and the user can generate novel poses by interpolating joint rotations between user-defined keyframes, producing animated video of the reposed object.

## Method

The approach represents appearance as 3D Gaussians in a canonical space and introduces learnable superpoints that act as rigid parts, each governed by a time-variant 6-DoF transformation predicted by a deformable field (an 8-layer MLP). Linear blend skinning interpolates superpoint transformations to drive individual Gaussian motion. Skeleton discovery exploits three intuitive cues—joint proximity, relative-pose invariance at joints, and merging of parts with constant relative pose—combined with a minimum spanning tree over smoothed pairwise distances to yield a tree-structured skeleton. A second deformable field then predicts per-joint rotations in a kinematic stage, where the full transformation of each superpoint is computed as a product of ancestor bone transforms. An adaptive control strategy (prune, densify, merge) with as-rigid-as-possible and sparsity regularization losses keeps the superpoint count minimal (typically 42–188 from an initial 512), simplifying the skeleton for pose editing.

## Results

On the D-NeRF dataset (800×800), the method achieves 38.80 PSNR, 0.9870 SSIM, and 0.0095 LPIPS while rendering at 103.98 FPS, outperforming the closest template-free articulated baselines WIM (25.21 PSNR, 0.16 FPS) and AP-NeRF (30.91 PSNR, 1.33 FPS) by a large margin in both quality and speed. On the Robots dataset, it reaches 34.34 PSNR at 137.76 FPS, surpassing AP-NeRF (32.45 PSNR, 0.89 FPS) and WIM (29.11 PSNR, 0.11 FPS). On the real-world ZJU-MoCap dataset, it achieves 198.23 FPS—over 150× faster than AP-NeRF (1.31 FPS)—though with slightly lower PSNR (29.11 vs. 31.08 for WIM) due to camera calibration imperfections in the dataset.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the two template-free articulated baselines, the method dominates: it outperforms WIM by 13.6 dB PSNR (38.80 vs. 25.21) and renders ~650× faster (104 vs. 0.16 FPS), and beats AP-NeRF by 7.9 dB PSNR (38.80 vs. 30.91) at ~78× the rendering speed (104 vs. 1.33 FPS). Among non-articulated 3D-GS-based dynamic methods, the method trails SC-GS (42.98 PSNR) and D-3D-GS (40.11 PSNR) in raw reconstruction quality, but surpasses 4D-GS (34.39 PSNR) and SP-GS (37.55 PSNR), while uniquely providing skeleton-based reposing capability. On the Robots dataset, the method leads all baselines in PSNR (34.34 vs. 32.45 for AP-NeRF and 29.11 for WIM) and achieves 137.76 FPS—over 150× faster than AP-NeRF. On the real-world ZJU-MoCap dataset, the method achieves the highest rendering speed (198.23 FPS) but slightly lower PSNR than WIM (29.11 vs. 31.08), which the authors attribute to camera calibration inaccuracies in that dataset rather than a methodological weakness.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two research threads: template-free articulated object reconstruction (WIM, AP-NeRF) and real-time dynamic scene rendering via 3D Gaussian Splatting (3D-GS, SC-GS, SP-GS). It adopts WIM's intuitive-cue-based skeleton discovery (joint proximity, relative-pose invariance, minimum spanning tree) and SC-GS's superpoint-based deformation with adaptive density control, but replaces the slow NeRF/point-based rendering pipeline with 3D-GS to achieve real-time speeds. The key departure from WIM and AP-NeRF is the use of superpoints as learnable rigid parts with LBS, combined with a two-stage training strategy (dynamic → kinematic) that separates appearance learning from skeleton optimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: template-free articulated reposing at >100 FPS opens the door to interactive applications (games, VR, education) that were previously limited to sub-2-FPS rendering. However, the learned skeleton is constrained to the motion manifold observed in the training video, limiting extrapolation to truly novel poses, and the method currently handles only a single articulated object per scene. Open questions include extending to multi-object scenes, improving robustness to imperfect camera calibration (where it underperforms WIM on ZJU-MoCap), and whether the kinematic model can be generalized beyond the observed motion space.
