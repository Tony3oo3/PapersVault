## Problem

In environments with constant observation delay, restoring the Markovian property requires augmenting the state with all actions in the delay window, causing the state space to grow as |S||A|^Δ. This exponential expansion makes temporal-difference learning in the delayed MDP extremely sample-inefficient, and existing SOTA methods (e.g., AD-SAC, BPQL) only partially mitigate the issue while remaining fundamentally bound by the TD learning paradigm in high-dimensional augmented spaces. The paper asks: can one improve sample efficiency in delayed RL without sacrificing the performance that augmentation-based methods guarantee?

## Input / Output

The delayed policy π_Δ receives the augmented state x_t = {s_{t-Δ}, a_{t-Δ}, …, a_{t-1}}, which is serialized as a sequence of (state, action) pairs {(s_{t-Δ}, a_{t-i})}_{i=Δ}^{1} and fed into a transformer. It outputs a stochastic action distribution over the continuous action space. Additionally, a belief head within the same transformer reconstructs the Δ unobserved true states {s_{t-Δ+1}, …, s_t} from x_t, providing the ground-truth states needed to compute the KL loss against the reference policy during training.

## Method

VDPO reformulates delayed RL as a variational inference problem by introducing a reference policy π and decomposing the log-evidence into an ELBO: maximize E_{τ~p_π(τ)}[log p(O=1|τ)] (term A) while minimizing KL(p_π(τ)‖p_{π_Δ}(τ)) (term B). Term A is solved by standard SAC in the delay-free MDP (small state space |S||A|), and a key proposition shows the trajectory-level KL in term B decomposes into a state-level KL(π(a_t|s_t)‖π_Δ(a_t|x_t)) plus a constant, reducing the problem to efficient state-level behavior cloning. The delayed policy is implemented as a two-head transformer with a shared encoder: the belief head (trained with MSE reconstruction of true states) and the policy head (trained with KL divergence against the reference policy), where the encoder gradient is frozen during policy-head updates following the information bottleneck principle. Theoretically, VDPO achieves the same fixed point as DIDA, BPQL, and AD-SAC while requiring O(|S||A|/(1-γ)³ε² · ln(1/σ)) samples for the reference policy instead of O(|X||A|/(1-γ)³ε² · ln(1/σ)) for full TD learning in the augmented space.

## Results

On MuJoCo with 5 constant delays, VDPO achieves the best sample efficiency in 7 out of 9 tasks, requiring only ~51% of the steps needed by the SOTA baseline AD-SAC (e.g., 0.42M vs. >1M on Ant-v4, 0.67M vs. >1M on Humanoid-v4 where no baseline reaches the delay-free threshold). In terms of normalized performance (Ret_nor), VDPO matches or exceeds AD-SAC on most tasks: 1.11 on Ant-v4, 1.22 on Hopper-v4, 1.15 on Humanoid-v4, 1.29 on HumanoidStandup-v4, and 2.30 on Swimmer-v4. Under 25 and 50 delays, VDPO remains competitive with AD-SAC and BPQL, though all methods degrade significantly on harder tasks like Ant-v4 and Walker2d-v4.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VDPO is compared against five delayed RL baselines: A-SAC (augmented SAC), DC/AC, DIDA, BPQL, and AD-SAC (the current SOTA). On sample efficiency with 5 delays, VDPO requires approximately 51% fewer steps than AD-SAC across HalfCheetah, Hopper, Pusher, Swimmer, and Walker2d, and is the only method to reach the delay-free performance threshold on Ant-v4 (0.42M steps) and Humanoid-v4 (0.67M steps) where all baselines fail within 1M steps. On normalized performance (Ret_nor) with 5 delays, VDPO outperforms AD-SAC on Ant-v4 (1.11 vs. 0.72), Hopper-v4 (1.22 vs. 1.07), Humanoid-v4 (1.15 vs. 0.98), HumanoidStandup-v4 (1.29 vs. 1.22), Swimmer-v4 (2.30 vs. 1.82), and Walker2d-v4 (1.27 vs. 1.12), while AD-SAC edges out VDPO on HalfCheetah-v4 (1.07 vs. 1.03) and Pusher-v4 (1.36 vs. 1.17). Under 25 and 50 delays, VDPO and AD-SAC remain closely competitive, with BPQL sometimes pulling ahead on HalfCheetah and Hopper. The theoretical analysis confirms VDPO converges to the same fixed point as DIDA, BPQL, and AD-SAC, explaining the comparable asymptotic performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

VDPO sits at the intersection of two research threads: variational RL (MPO, Virel), which reformulates policy optimization as maximizing an ELBO, and augmentation-based delayed RL (DC/AC, DIDA, BPQL, AD-SAC), which restores the Markov property by expanding the state with past actions. All prior delayed RL methods perform TD learning directly in the high-dimensional augmented state space, inheriting its curse of dimensionality. VDPO departs from this by using the variational decomposition to split the problem: TD learning (SAC) operates in the small delay-free state space, while the high-dimensional delayed policy is trained via behavior cloning rather than TD updates. The two-head transformer with a belief decoder follows the architecture of Liotet et al. (2021), but is repurposed here as the cloning target rather than a value-function input.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ~50% reduction in sample complexity for delayed RL is practically significant for robotics and cyber-physical systems where interaction costs are high and delays are unavoidable. The theoretical guarantee that VDPO converges to the same fixed point as SOTA methods (DIDA, BPQL, AD-SAC) while requiring fewer samples provides a clean justification for the approach. However, the paper is limited to deterministic MuJoCo benchmarks; the authors acknowledge that stochastic environments cause performance degeneration and suggest integrating with ADRL's auxiliary-task mechanism as future work. An open question is whether the variational decomposition generalizes to other structured MDP variants (e.g., partial observability, multi-agent delays) where the state-space blowup is similarly problematic.
