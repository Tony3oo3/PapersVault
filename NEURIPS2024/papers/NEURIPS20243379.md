## Problem

End-to-end neural image compression systems rely on uniform scalar quantization for simplicity, which cannot exploit inter-feature dependencies in the latent space. Lattice vector quantization (LVQ) offers a structured middle ground between scalar and general vector quantization, but traditional LVQ codebooks are designed for uniform source distributions and remain nonadaptive to the actual latent feature statistics of learned compression models. The paper addresses how to learn LVQ codebooks that are rate-distortion optimal for the specific latent distributions encountered in practice.

## Input / Output

The system takes RGB images (256×256 random crops from ImageNet for training; Kodak and CLIC validation sets for evaluation) and produces a compressed bitstream plus a reconstructed image. Latent features of shape H×W×C are grouped by channels into vectors of dimension 32, so the quantized representation becomes H×W×(C/32) — a thicker cube with the same spatial extent but fewer channels. The integer combination coefficients of the learned basis vectors are entropy-coded via arithmetic coding rather than the lattice points themselves.

## Method

The core contribution is learning the basis matrix B of the LVQ generator matrix end-to-end so that Voronoi cell shapes and orientations adapt to the latent feature distribution. During training, Babai's Rounding Technique provides a differentiable approximation to nearest-lattice-point search, with uniform noise replacing the rounding operation for gradient flow. Orthogonal constraints on the basis vectors are enforced via a regularization term in the loss to minimize the train-inference gap of BRT and to enable decomposition of the joint rate-estimation integral into a product of univariate Gaussian mixture integrals. The overall objective jointly minimizes cross-entropy rate, reconstruction distortion, and the orthogonality penalty, trained from scratch on three network architectures (Bmshj2018, SwinT-ChARM, LIC-TCM) paired with four context models. The learned lattice consistently outperforms both scalar quantization and classical pre-defined lattices (E8, A16, A24) while retaining inference times close to scalar quantization (18–40 ms vs. 123–454 ms for general VQ).

## Results

The proposed OLVQ method achieves BD-rate savings of up to −22.60% over uniform scalar quantization on Bmshj2018 with a Factorized context model, and up to −12.44% on SwinT-ChARM with the same context model. Savings decrease as context model complexity increases, ranging from −8.31% (Bmshj2018 + Spatial-wise Autoregressive) to −0.95% (LIC-TCM + Spatial-wise Autoregressive). Inference time for 32-dimensional LVQ is approximately 40 ms, compared to ~5 ms for scalar quantization and ~454 ms for general vector quantization, confirming the efficiency advantage of the lattice structure.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OLVQ consistently outperforms scalar quantization across all three network architectures (Bmshj2018, SwinT-ChARM, LIC-TCM) and all four context models, with the largest gains (−22.60%) on the simplest configuration (Bmshj2018 + Factorized). Against the VQ-based baselines, OLVQ (24-dim) achieves −20.49% BD-rate on Bmshj2018+Factorized, substantially beating SHVQ (−8.23%) and McQUIC (−15.11%); on SwinT-ChARM+Factorized, OLVQ reaches −10.68% versus SHVQ's −6.55% and McQUIC's −7.24%. Compared to classical pre-defined lattices (E8, A16, A24), the learned OLVQ codebooks yield 4–8 percentage points additional savings (e.g., −20.49% vs. −12.82% for A24 Leech lattice on Bmshj2018+Factorized). OLVQ approaches the performance of general vector quantization (GVQ) within ~2 percentage points (e.g., −20.49% vs. −22.74% at 24-dim) while reducing inference time by roughly 10× (40 ms vs. 454 ms at 32-dim). The gains diminish for heavier models and more complex autoregressive context models, with LIC-TCM + Spatial-wise Autoregressive showing only −0.95% savings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the end-to-end neural image compression framework of Ballé et al. (ICLR 2017) by replacing the standard uniform scalar quantizer with a learnable lattice vector quantizer (OLVQ). It directly builds on the authors' prior LVQ-AC work (CVPR 2023), which introduced pre-defined lattice structures (Gosset, Barnes-Wall, Leech) into learned compression, and on Zhu et al.'s (CVPR 2022) multivariate Gaussian mixture rate estimation. The key departure is abandoning the traditional LVQ design goal of congruent near-spherical Voronoi cells optimized for uniform sources, instead learning the lattice basis matrix end-to-end to adapt cell shape and orientation to the actual latent feature distribution, while imposing orthogonal constraints to ensure Babai's Rounding Technique remains accurate at inference.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method achieves up to ~22% BD-rate savings over scalar quantization while maintaining inference times an order of magnitude lower than general vector quantization (~40ms vs ~454ms at 32 dimensions), offering a practical path to exploit inter-feature dependencies without the computational cost of nearest-neighbor search. A notable finding is that OLVQ coupled with a simple factorized entropy model can approach the performance of autoregressive entropy models at low bitrates, suggesting a viable efficiency/performance trade-off for resource-constrained deployment. However, the authors acknowledge that gains diminish substantially for heavier networks (LIC-TCM) and complex context models (spatial-wise autoregressive: only -0.95%), limiting universal applicability. Open questions include whether the learned lattice structure can be further generalized to video compression, 3D data, or other DNN model compression tasks, and whether relaxing the orthogonal constraint with better CVP solvers could close the remaining gap to general VQ.
