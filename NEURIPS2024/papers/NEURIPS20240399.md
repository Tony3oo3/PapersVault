## Problem

Prior provable robust RL algorithms rely on a generative model or pre-collected offline data with good coverage, leaving open whether sample-efficient robust RL is possible through purely interactive data collection in the training environment. This paper shows that in general it is impossible: the worst-case transition kernel can send the agent to states unreachable under the nominal dynamics (the curse of support shift), yielding an Ω(ρ·HK) linear regret lower bound. The paper then identifies a vanishing minimal value assumption that eliminates this pathology and enables sample-efficient learning.

## Input / Output

The learner interacts only with the nominal training environment P* for K episodes, collecting (s_h, a_h, R_h, s_{h+1}) tuples at each step h ∈ [H]; no access to testing environments or a generative model is available. The TV robust set radius ρ ∈ [0,1) parameterizes the uncertainty set of all possible testing transition kernels. The algorithm outputs a uniformly random policy from the K executed policies, which is ε-optimal in robust value with sample complexity Õ(min{H, ρ^{-1}} H² SA / ε²) episodes — a rate that decreases as ρ grows, meaning larger uncertainty sets are paradoxically easier to learn robustly.

## Method

OPROVI-TV performs optimistic robust value iteration: at each episode it estimates the nominal transition kernel via a vanilla empirical count, then recursively computes upper and lower bounds on the robust Q-function using a Bernstein-style variance-aware bonus tailored to robust value functions. The key theoretical insight is that under the vanishing minimal value assumption (min_s V* = 0), the TV robust expectation is equivalent to an infimum over a bounded-ratio robust set that shares the nominal kernel's support, thereby eliminating the support shift that causes the hardness. The robust expectation is evaluated via a strong duality representation requiring only the nominal transition kernel, and the bonus is designed so its cumulative sum over time steps is tightly controlled. This yields a sublinear regret of Õ(√(min{H,ρ^{-1}} H² SA K)) and a sample complexity matching the minimax-optimal rate for generative-model robust RL (Shi et al., 2023), but without requiring any generative oracle.

## Results

The paper establishes a fundamental hardness result: any algorithm for robust RL with interactive data collection on general RMDPs with TV robust sets incurs an Ω(ρ·HK) online regret lower bound (Theorem 3.2), showing sample-efficient learning is impossible without additional assumptions. Under the vanishing minimal value assumption, the proposed OPROVI-TV algorithm achieves Õ(√(min{H,ρ^{-1}} H²SAK)) online regret (Theorem 4.3), translating to a sample complexity of Õ(min{H,ρ^{-1}} H²SA/ε²) episodes to find an ε-optimal robust policy (Corollary 4.4). When ρ=0 (standard MDP), this recovers the minimax-optimal Õ(H³SA/ε²) rate, and for ρ>0 it matches the generative-model rate of Shi et al. (2023) without requiring a generative model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OPROVI-TV is the first algorithm to achieve provably sample-efficient robust RL purely through interactive data collection, a setting where the paper proves a linear regret lower bound (Ω(ρ·HK)) in the general case. Compared to the generative-model baselines RPVL (Xu et al., 2023) and DRVI (Shi et al., 2023), OPROVI-TV matches the minimax-optimal rate Õ(min{H,ρ⁻¹} H²SA/ε²) of DRVI but eliminates the need for a generative model oracle, operating solely through trial-and-error interaction with the training environment. Against the offline baselines P2MPO (Blanchet et al., 2023) and RFQI (Panaganti et al., 2022), OPROVI-TV avoids the dependence on coverage coefficients (C_rob*, C_full) and the ρ⁻² factor, instead depending only on min{H,ρ⁻¹}·H²SA/ε². The vanishing minimal value assumption required by OPROVI-TV strictly generalizes the fail-state assumption used by RFQI, making it applicable to a broader class of RMDPs including sparse-reward settings. The key separation is that in the general case (no structural assumptions), interactive data collection is fundamentally intractable while generative model and well-covered offline settings remain solvable, highlighting the unique challenge of the support shift problem in the interactive paradigm.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the robust MDP (RMDP) framework from Iyengar (2005) and the TV robust set formulation studied by Yang et al. (2022), Shi et al. (2023), and others, but shifts the data oracle from generative models or offline datasets to interactive data collection. It builds on the optimistic value iteration paradigm of UCBVI (Azar et al., 2017) and Bernstein-style variance-aware bonuses (Maurer and Pontil, 2009), adapting them to handle distributional robustness via a novel equivalence between TV robust sets and bounded-ratio robust sets under a vanishing minimal value condition. The key departure is identifying and proving a fundamental hardness barrier (support shift) that does not exist in the generative model or offline settings, then circumventing it with a structural assumption.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to establish that robust RL via interactive data collection is fundamentally impossible in general (linear regret lower bound), and the first to provide a provably sample-efficient algorithm for this setting under a natural structural condition. The vanishing minimal value assumption is strictly more general than the 'fail-state' condition used in prior offline robust RL work, and the resulting sample complexity matches the minimax-optimal rate for the generative model setting, closing a gap in the theory. Open questions include extending to function approximation, other divergence-based robust sets (e.g., KL divergence), and continuous state spaces, where the support shift issue may manifest differently.
