## Problem

Infinite horizon average reward constrained MDPs (CMDPs) with general policy parameterization had no known algorithm with sublinear regret and constraint violation guarantees. Prior work covered only tabular or linear MDP structures, and while unconstrained average reward policy gradient analysis existed, extending it to the constrained setting introduced fundamental obstacles: the strong duality property fails under general parameterization, and the cost estimate is biased in the average reward case.

## Input / Output

The algorithm operates online and model-free: at each time step it receives a state observation, selects an action from a parameterized policy (e.g., neural network weights), and observes a scalar reward and cost signal. It does not require access to transition probabilities or a simulator. The output is a sequence of updated policy parameters and Lagrange multipliers, with theoretical guarantees of Õ(T^{4/5}) expected regret and constraint violation over horizon T. The episode length H scales as Õ(T^{2/5}), meaning the number of policy updates K = T/H grows sublinearly.

## Method

The paper proposes a primal-dual policy gradient algorithm that jointly updates policy parameters θ (via a stochastic gradient ascent step on the Lagrangian) and a Lagrange multiplier λ (via projected gradient descent on the constraint). Advantage estimation is performed by searching for disjoint sub-trajectories of length N = 4t_mix(log T) within each epoch, yielding a model-free gradient estimator with Õ(T^{-ξ}) error. The key technical novelty is a novel constraint violation analysis (Lemmas 16–18) that disentangles regret and violation rates from the Lagrange convergence, since strong duality does not hold under general parameterization. The learning rate β for the dual update is set to T^{-2/5}, carefully balancing the competing effects on regret and violation, ultimately yielding Õ(T^{-1/5}) average convergence rates and Õ(T^{4/5}) cumulative bounds.

## Results

The paper establishes the first sublinear regret and constraint violation bounds for infinite-horizon average reward CMDPs with general policy parameterization. The proposed primal-dual policy gradient algorithm achieves Õ(T^{4/5}) expected regret and Õ(T^{4/5}) expected constraint violation, corresponding to an average optimality rate of Õ(T^{-1/5}) and average constraint violation rate of Õ(T^{-1/5}). This improves upon the state-of-the-art model-free tabular result of Õ(T^{5/6}) regret [8], though it remains worse than the unconstrained general-parameterization bound of Õ(T^{3/4}) [13].

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is against Triple-QA [8], the only other model-free algorithm for average reward CMDPs, which achieves Õ(T^{5/6}) regret with zero constraint violation in the tabular setting. This paper improves the regret rate to Õ(T^{4/5}) but at the cost of non-zero constraint violation (also Õ(T^{4/5})), and crucially extends to general parameterization rather than requiring a tabular state space. Against the model-based tabular algorithms of Chen et al. [6] (Õ(√T) regret) and Agarwal et al. [5] (Õ(√T) regret, zero violation), this paper trades a worse regret rate for model-free operation and applicability to large state spaces via neural network parameterization. Compared to the linear MDP algorithms of Ghosh et al. [9] (Õ(√T) regret), this paper removes the restrictive linear transition structure assumption but achieves a worse rate. The paper acknowledges that its Õ(T^{4/5}) bound is worse than the unconstrained general-parameterization result of Õ(T^{3/4}) [13], noting this gap between constrained and unconstrained settings is a common observation across the literature.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the unconstrained average reward policy gradient analysis of Bai et al. [13] to the constrained setting, and adapts the advantage estimation algorithm from Wei et al. [17] with a critical modification (epoch length H = Õ(T^ξ) rather than Õ(1)). The key departure from prior CMDP work is the move from tabular or linear MDP structures to general finite-dimensional parameterization, which breaks the strong duality property that underpins convergence proofs in simpler settings. The authors must therefore develop a novel constraint violation analysis using biased cost estimates and a series of intermediate lemmas to disentangle regret from violation rates—techniques not available in the discounted-reward or tabular literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to establish sublinear regret and constraint violation bounds for average reward CMDPs with general parameterization, improving the model-free tabular state-of-the-art from Õ(T^{5/6}) to Õ(T^{4/5}). However, a notable gap remains: the unconstrained general-parameterization counterpart achieves Õ(T^{3/4}), and closing this gap is explicitly identified as an open problem. The algorithm also requires knowledge of the mixing time and assumes ergodicity, limiting practical applicability. The failure of strong duality under general parameterization is a fundamental structural insight that will likely inform future constrained RL analyses beyond this specific setting.
