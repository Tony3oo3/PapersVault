## Problem

Expectation propagation (EP) updates are not robust to Monte Carlo noise when moments are estimated from samples, because the nonlinear backward mapping from moments to natural parameters introduces bias. Prior fixes rely on family-specific debiasing estimators that require hundreds of samples, sample thinning, and multiple interacting hyperparameters, making them difficult to tune and computationally expensive. No existing EP variant achieves both stability and sample-efficiency with MC noise without these debiasing mechanisms.

## Input / Output

The method takes a probabilistic model expressed as a product of a base exponential-family prior and m likelihood factors, along with MC samples drawn from m tilted distributions (each of constant dimensionality regardless of m). It produces an approximate posterior as a member of the same exponential family, parameterised by site parameters, from which posterior moments and samples can be read off. Notably, the updates remain stable and most sample-efficient with just a single MC sample per site per iteration, eliminating the need for sample thinning or large batch sizes.

## Method

The key insight is that EP's moment-matching updates are equivalent to natural gradient descent (NGD) on the EP variational objective with respect to a specific surrogate distribution p̃_i^(t), and the MC bias arises from a nonlinear reparameterisation between successive NGD steps. By performing NGD with respect to the natural parameters of p̃_i^(t) (EP-η), the reparameterisation becomes linear and updates are exactly unbiased; by performing NGD with respect to mean parameters but treating the step size ε as a free parameter (EP-μ), the bias shrinks as O(ε²) while progress in L remains O(ε). EP-η requires Jacobian-vector products through the backward mapping, while EP-μ is cheaper but retains small residual bias. Both variants use a single step-size hyperparameter ε, are stable with one MC sample, and do not require debiasing estimators or sample thinning.

## Results

The paper evaluates EP-η and EP-μ against baselines on four probabilistic inference tasks (hierarchical logistic regression with MVN and NIW priors, a cosmic radiation model, and a neural response model), measuring KL divergence to an MCMC-estimated optimum as a function of NUTS steps. Across all four experiments, EP-η and EP-μ consistently reach a given KL divergence level faster than EP, often significantly so, while using only a single MC sample per update (n_samp=1, n_inner=1) and a single step-size hyperparameter ε. SNEP is found to be significantly slower than all other methods. The two new variants perform very similarly to each other, with EP-μ having slightly lower computational cost (no JVPs required).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against EP with the debiasing estimator of Xu et al. [52] (as implemented by Vehtari et al. [48]), EP-η and EP-μ achieve the same or better KL divergence at substantially fewer NUTS steps across all four experiments, while requiring only 1 sample per update versus the hundreds of samples and sample thinning that EP needs. Against SNEP [17], both new variants are significantly faster to converge, consistent with prior findings by Vehtari et al. [48]; the authors attribute SNEP's slowness to its use of site-potential pseudo-distributions whose geometry is poorly aligned with the true curvature of the variational objective. Against CVI [31] (Figure 3), EP-η produces more faithful posterior approximations (lower forward and reverse KL to an MCMC reference) but is slower in wall-clock time due to the cost of NUTS sampling; when an oracle sampler is used, the sample efficiency of EP-η is roughly equivalent to CVI. The paper does not report a single numerical summary table; all comparisons are presented as Pareto frontiers (KL divergence vs. NUTS steps) in Figure 2, with 500 random-search hyperparameter settings per method and 5 random seeds.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reinterprets the moment-matching updates of Expectation Propagation (Minka, 2001) as natural gradient descent (Amari, 1998) with respect to a specific auxiliary distribution, building on the variational objective formulation of Hasenclever et al. (2017). Prior stochastic EP methods either relied on family-specific debiasing estimators (Xu et al., 2014; Vehtari et al., 2020) or performed NGD with respect to site potentials treated as distributions (SNEP). The key departure is showing that simply changing the NGD parameterisation—from mean parameters (as in standard EP) to natural parameters (EP-η) or decoupling the step size (EP-μ)—eliminates or drastically reduces the bias introduced by MC noise, without requiring debiasing estimators or sample thinning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: EP-η and EP-μ require only a single MC sample per update and a single step-size hyperparameter, eliminating the need to tune sample count, thinning ratio, and damping simultaneously. This makes stochastic EP substantially more accessible and sample-efficient for scalable Bayesian inference on partitioned data. However, the methods remain constrained by the efficiency of the underlying MCMC sampler (e.g., NUTS), and the paper acknowledges that wall-clock performance can still lag behind purpose-built variational methods like CVI. Open questions include leveraging the current approximation to guide sampling (e.g., as a mass matrix), parallel/distributed implementations, and whether the NGD-parameterisation insight extends to other approximate inference frameworks.
