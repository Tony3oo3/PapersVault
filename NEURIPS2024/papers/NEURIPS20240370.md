## Problem

Standard State Space Models maintain a single monolithic state vector that entangles the dynamics of all entities in a sequence, making them poorly suited for inherently modular processes like multi-object physical interactions. While RNN-based modular approaches (e.g., RIM) preserve per-object independence, they cannot parallelize training and suffer from vanishing gradients, limiting them to short sequences. The paper asks whether SSMs can be redesigned with built-in inductive biases for independent mechanisms while retaining parallelizable training and long-range memory.

## Input / Output

The model consumes unstructured video frames (e.g., 128×128 RGB images) that are first encoded by a CNN into a 2D feature map (64×64 for object-centric tasks) or patchified into a long token sequence (up to 2560 tokens for the Blinking Color Balls benchmark). At each time step, the input is decomposed into K slot vectors (K=6 for most experiments, K=11 for MOVi), and the model outputs per-slot representations that can be decoded into object images with alpha masks, segmentation masks, predicted future frames, or a quantized 6×6 grid location for the CATER snitch task. The slot structure is preserved throughout the network, with the number of slots optionally varying across layers to allow different abstraction levels.

## Method

SlotSSMs replace the monolithic hidden state with K independent slot states, each updated by its own block-diagonal transition matrix conditioned only on the corresponding input slot, so that h_t^k integrates information exclusively from the history of slot k. Sparse cross-slot interactions are introduced via a Slot Mixer that applies self-attention over the K slot outputs followed by a per-slot MLP, allowing slots to correct misassignments and model interactions without full entanglement. A Slot Encoder (a small Transformer with cross-attention from learnable CLS tokens to input tokens) extracts slot representations from unstructured inputs; in the object-centric variant (OC-SlotSSMs), this is replaced by inverted attention that enforces competition among slots over input tokens. The SSM blocks themselves follow the Mamba architecture with input-dependent diagonal transition matrices discretized via zero-order hold, and all K slots share the same SSM weights (parallel runs of identical Mamba blocks). The full architecture stacks Slot Encoder → SlotSSM → Slot Mixer per layer, trained end-to-end with reconstruction or task-specific objectives.

## Results

On the CATER Snitch Localization task, OC-SlotSSM achieves 69.27% Top-1 and 90.48% Top-5 accuracy with pre-training, substantially outperforming SlotTransformer (49.21% / 70.24%) and Single State SSM (41.15% / 65.70%). In the multi-object video prediction task, SlotSSM achieves the lowest MSE over a 20-frame autoregressive rollout, outperforming Single State SSM, SlotTransformer, and RIM. On the Blinking Color Balls long-context benchmark, SlotSSM maintains consistent performance across sequence lengths up to 2560 tokens, while SlotTransformer degrades beyond 640 tokens and RIM fails to generalize at any length. On MOVi-A and MOVi-B, OC-SlotSSM outperforms SAVi in both unsupervised object segmentation (FGARI, mIoU) and attribute prediction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against RIM (Goyal et al., 2021), SlotSSM consistently outperforms across all tasks: in multi-object video prediction, RIM performs better than single-state baselines but still lags behind SlotSSM and SlotTransformer; in long-context reasoning, RIM fails to generalize at any sequence length due to optimization issues from separate RNN weights per slot. Against SAVi (Kipf et al., 2021), OC-SlotSSM achieves higher FGARI and mIoU on both MOVi-A and MOVi-B, with tighter object boundaries and fewer object splitting artifacts, and also shows superior training stability (SAVi tends to collapse into a single slot). Against SAVi++ (Elsayed et al., 2022) on real-world depth estimation, OC-SlotSSM achieves lower MSE across all three datasets (e.g., 0.464 vs 0.589 on UT Egocentric, 0.653 vs 0.804 on Waymo, 1.180 vs 1.412 on TikTok), and exhibits emergent scene decomposition that SAVi++ does not. Notably, SlotSSM underperforms SlotTransformer in direct training on CATER (25.64% vs 41.09% Top-1) but surpasses it with pre-training (54.73% vs 49.21%), suggesting SSMs benefit more from task-free pre-training on sparse-signal tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

SlotSSMs sit at the intersection of two research threads: state space models (S4, S5, Mamba) that offer efficient long-range sequence modeling, and object-centric learning (Slot Attention, SAVi, RIM) that imposes modular structure on representations. The paper departs from monolithic SSMs by decomposing the hidden state into independent slot vectors with block-diagonal transitions, and from RNN-based object-centric methods (which cannot parallelize training) by leveraging the scan-based parallelism of SSMs. The Mamba architecture [24] provides the concrete SSM block, while inverted attention from the Slot Attention lineage [46, 67] is repurposed in the OC-SlotSSM variant to enforce competitive slot assignment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first SSM architecture with built-in inductive biases for modular processes, bridging a gap where object-centric learning was stuck with RNNs (vanishing gradients, sequential training) and Transformers (quadratic cost). The Blinking Color Balls benchmark provides a controlled multi-object long-range reasoning evaluation that existing single-object benchmarks (e.g., Long Range Arena) lack. Key open questions include whether SlotSSMs scale to industry-level model and data sizes, whether the pre-training dependency observed in 3D visual reasoning generalizes, and whether the modular slot design transfers to non-visual modalities like text and audio.
