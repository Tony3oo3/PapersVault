## Problem

Standard low-rank approximation and column subset selection minimize a global loss, potentially underserving specific sub-populations in the data. This paper studies the socially fair variant, where the goal is to minimize the maximum reconstruction loss across all groups, and shows that even a constant-factor approximation is NP-hard under standard complexity assumptions. The paper provides the first polynomial-time bicriteria approximation algorithms and a 2^poly(k) exact-approximation algorithm for this problem.

## Input / Output

The method takes ℓ grouped data matrices A^(i) ∈ R^{n_i × d} (one per sub-population), a rank parameter k, and either an accuracy parameter ε ∈ (0,1) or a trade-off parameter c ∈ (0,1). For the (1+ε)-approximation, it outputs a factor matrix Ṽ ∈ R^{k×d} minimizing max_i ||A^(i)Ṽ†Ṽ − A^(i)||_F. For the bicriteria variant, it outputs a taller factor Ṽ ∈ R^{t×d} with t = O(k log log k · log² d), and for column subset selection it additionally outputs a set S of O(k log k) selected columns plus per-group regression matrices M^(i) that reconstruct each group's data through the selected columns.

## Method

The key technical insight is converting the intractable max-of-Frobenius-loss objective into a tractable Lp loss via Dvoretzky's theorem: Gaussian matrices G and H are generated so that (1±ε)||GMH||_p ≈ ||M||_F for all matrices M, reducing the problem to minimizing ||GAHSX − GAH||_p. A Lewis weight sampling matrix T then provides an Lp subspace embedding, and the closed-form solution (TGAHS)†(TGA) yields the bicriteria factor in polynomial time. For the (1+ε)-approximation, a polynomial system solver is applied to an affine embedding of the problem, with a binary search over the optimal value α, achieving 2^poly(k) runtime. For fair column subset selection, the bicriteria factor Ṽ from the low-rank algorithm serves as a starting point, and leverage score sampling of O(k log k) columns of Ṽ provides the final column subset with per-group regression matrices.

## Results

The paper's primary contributions are theoretical: fair low-rank approximation is NP-hard to approximate within any constant factor (Theorem 1.1), and under the Exponential Time Hypothesis requires 2^{k^{Ω(1)}} time (Theorem 1.2). On the positive side, a (1+ε)-approximation algorithm runs in (1/ε)·poly(n)·(2ℓ)^{O(N)} time (Theorem 1.3), and a bicriteria approximation achieves an O(k(log log k)(log d)) approximation factor in polynomial time (Theorem 1.4). Empirically, on the Default of Credit Card Clients dataset (30,000 observations, 23 features, split by gender), the bicriteria algorithm achieves a fair LRA cost ratio (bicriteria/SVD) as low as 0.6 in some subsample settings, with most ratios below 1 across k ∈ {1,...,8} and s ∈ {2,...,21}.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares its bicriteria fair low-rank approximation algorithm (Algorithm 3) against the standard non-fair low-rank approximation baseline, which outputs the top k right singular vectors from the SVD. On the Default of Credit Card Clients dataset, the bicriteria algorithm consistently achieves a lower max-over-groups Frobenius reconstruction error than the SVD baseline: all ratios in the subsample experiments (Figure 1a, s ∈ {2,...,21}, k=1) and most ratios in the rank sweep (Figure 1b, s=1000, k ∈ {1,...,8}) are below 1, with some reaching 0.6, indicating up to 40% lower fair cost. Notably, the bicriteria algorithm achieves this improvement even when not permitted a larger rank than the baseline (though in Figure 1b it is allowed rank 2k). On runtime, the SVD baseline is faster overall because the bicriteria algorithm's bottleneck is generating Gaussian matrices and performing 10 iterations of Lewis weight sampling (each requiring a matrix inversion), though the final factor extraction step (bicrit2) is faster than SVD due to operating on a smaller matrix.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of fair PCA work (Samadi et al. 2018, Tantipongpipat et al. 2019) by adopting the reconstruction-error (Frobenius norm) objective with a min-max structure across groups, rather than the average-loss or projection-norm objectives used previously. The technical toolkit draws heavily from randomized numerical linear algebra: Dvoretzky's theorem for embedding L2 into Lp, Lewis weight sampling for Lp subspace embeddings (Cohen & Peng 2015), and the column-sampling dimensionality reduction of Woodruff & Yasuda (STOC 2023). The NP-hardness reduction follows the Subspace(n-1,∞) hardness of Brieden et al. (2000) and Deshpande et al. (2011), while the polynomial-system-solver approach for the (1+ε)-approximation adapts the guessing strategy of Razenshteyn et al. (STOC 2016). The 'socially fair' terminology and min-max formulation are borrowed from the socially fair clustering literature (Ghadiri et al. 2021, Makarychev & Vakilian 2021).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The NP-hardness of constant-factor approximation for fair low-rank approximation is a significant negative result that clarifies the computational boundary of fair numerical linear algebra, showing that the min-max structure fundamentally changes the complexity landscape compared to standard LRA. The polynomial-time bicriteria approximation algorithms provide the first tractable guarantees for this problem, and the 2^poly(k) algorithm offers a practical middle ground for constant group counts. Open questions include extending these results to other fairness notions (disparate impact, individual fairness, representative fairness), distributed/streaming settings, and constructing ε-coresets for socially fair subset selection. The empirical validation is limited to a single real-world dataset (Default of Credit Card Clients) and serves primarily as a proof-of-concept.
