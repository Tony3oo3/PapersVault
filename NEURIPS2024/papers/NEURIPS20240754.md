## Problem

No existing vision pre-training method can effectively exploit interleaved image-text data—the most prevalent format on the Internet—to learn robust visual representations from scratch. Paired-data methods like CLIP cannot handle free-format interleaved sequences, while interleaved-data methods (Flamingo, Kosmos) only perform incremental alignment on already-pretrained vision encoders. Moreover, naively applying auto-regressive text generation to interleaved data causes visual representation collapse, as the model relies on redundant text context rather than image information.

## Input / Output

The method consumes free-format interleaved image-text documents (e.g., from MMC4 or OBELICS) where images are randomly placed before or after their surrounding text, with up to 6 images per 2048-token sequence. Images are resized to 224×224 and encoded by a ViT into patch token sequences, while text tokens use one-hot vocabulary vectors; special <BoI> and <EoI> tokens delimit image spans. The output is a pre-trained ViT vision encoder (ViT-L/14 or ViT-B/16) whose latent representations are aligned with text semantics; the causal language model and text encoder used during training can be discarded at transfer time.

## Method

LCL frames vision pre-training as latent compression: maximizing the mutual information I(y;z) between the input latent z (visual embeddings from a ViT plus one-hot text tokens) and the output y of a causal attention model. The paper proves this objective decomposes into minimizing cross-entropy (compression) plus maximizing the entropy of each latent element (preventing collapse), which in practice reduces to two complementary losses: (1) a bidirectional contrastive loss (InfoNCE with Boltzmann distributions and learnable linear projections) between each image's attention-pooled global representation and the causal model's output at the preceding <BoI> token, and (2) a standard auto-regressive next-token prediction loss over text tokens. The total loss is L = λ·L_con + L_gen with λ=0.1, jointly optimizing the ViT encoder and the randomly initialized OPT-125M causal model. This design ensures the visual latent retains both information predictable from preceding context (contrastive) and information needed to predict subsequent text (generation), avoiding the collapse observed in pure auto-regressive baselines.

## Results

On the MMC4 interleaved dataset (ViT-B/16, frozen transfer), LCL achieves 75.2% ImageNet-1K top-1 accuracy, 48.5/34.5 COCO retrieval TR@1/IR@1, and 87.5/82.5 CIDEr on COCO/NoCaps captioning, outperforming all baselines on captioning while matching the best paired-data methods (CoCa: 75.4% IN-1k, 48.6/34.3 COCO ret.) on classification and retrieval. At the larger ViT-L/14 scale trained on LAION-400M+MMC4 (13B images), LCL reaches 82.0% IN-1k frozen accuracy and 113.7 CIDEr on COCO captioning, surpassing OpenCLIP (82.1% IN-1k, 111.5 CIDEr) on captioning and retrieval while matching it on classification. Auto-regressive baselines (Flamingo, Emu) suffer severe representation collapse, achieving only 24.0% and 5.7% IN-1k accuracy respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LCL significantly outperforms all baselines on captioning tasks: it achieves 87.5 CIDEr on COCO and 82.5 on NoCaps, beating the strongest paired-data baseline CoCa (84.8/80.5) by 2.7/2.0 points, and CLIP (82.9/78.4) by 4.6/4.1 points. On classification and retrieval, LCL is essentially on par with CoCa (75.2 vs 75.4 IN-1k; 48.5 vs 48.6 COCO TR@1) and slightly ahead of CLIP (75.2 vs 74.8 IN-1k; 48.5 vs 46.4 COCO TR@1), despite using interleaved data rather than paired data. The auto-regressive baselines Flamingo (24.0% IN-1k) and Emu (5.7% IN-1k) catastrophically fail due to visual representation collapse, confirming that text generation alone cannot provide effective visual supervision from scratch. BEiT3 (masked modeling) achieves moderate results (73.3% IN-1k) but lags LCL by ~2 points on classification and ~6 points on COCO CIDEr. At the ViT-L/14 scale (Table 3), LCL trained on LAION+MMC4 matches or exceeds OpenCLIP on all metrics (e.g., 113.7 vs 111.5 COCO CIDEr, 87.6 vs 86.9 Flickr30k TR@1) while using half the pre-training epochs (16 vs 32), and remains within 1.7 points of OpenAI CLIP's private-data results on IN-1k.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the compression-learning perspective from NLP (Huang et al., 2024) to the multi-modal domain, arguing that auto-regressive language modeling is equivalent to compressing data into model parameters and that this principle should apply to interleaved image-text sequences. It builds on the mutual-information decomposition framework from Su et al. (2023) to derive a training objective that decomposes into a contrastive loss (aligning visual latents with preceding context) and a generative loss (predicting subsequent text). The key departure from CLIP and CoCa is that LCL operates on free-format interleaved sequences rather than requiring image-text pairs, and unlike Flamingo/Kosmos/Emu which perform incremental alignment on already-pre-trained vision encoders, LCL pre-trains the vision encoder from scratch.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Interleaved image-text data is far more prevalent on the web than carefully curated image-text pairs, so a method that can pre-train vision models from scratch on this data type could substantially expand the effective training corpus. The paper's finding that pure auto-regressive generation (Flamingo, Emu) causes catastrophic visual representation collapse when training from scratch (24% and 5.7% ImageNet accuracy vs. 75% for LCL) is an important negative result that clarifies why the contrastive component is essential. However, the scaling properties remain unexplored—experiments are limited to ViT-L/14 with 13B images—and it is unclear whether the approach maintains its advantage at the scale of ViT-G or with hundreds of billions of images.
