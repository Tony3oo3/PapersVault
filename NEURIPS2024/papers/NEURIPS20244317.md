## Problem

Learning the correct symmetries of a dynamical system from data is difficult because standard training objectives that only measure data fit cannot distinguish between symmetric and non-symmetric solutions that fit equally well. Prior approaches to automatic symmetry discovery relied on separate validation data, hand-tuned regularisers, or Laplace approximations that limit the expressiveness of the posterior. This paper asks whether symmetries can be learned end-to-end from training data alone via a differentiable Bayesian model selection objective on deep neural networks.

## Input / Output

The method consumes phase space trajectories as pairs (x_t, x_{t+Δt}) of position-momentum vectors sampled at fixed time intervals, and produces a symmetrised Hamiltonian neural network along with a set of K quadratic conserved quantities parameterised by a symmetric matrix A and offset vector b. The symmetrisation is computed via Monte Carlo averaging over the analytically solvable flow of each conserved quantity (using a matrix exponential on homogeneous coordinates), yielding an unbiased estimate of the invariant Hamiltonian. Predictions are made by integrating the symmetrised Hamiltonian forward in time via Euler steps.

## Method

The core idea is to use Noether's theorem to parameterise symmetries as learnable quadratic conserved quantities C(x) = xᵀAx/2 + bᵀx, whose generated flows are affine and admit closed-form solutions via matrix exponentials. The Hamiltonian is symmetrised by averaging the neural network output over the group orbit induced by these conserved quantities, approximated with S Monte Carlo samples. Training uses a variational lower bound (ELBO) on the marginal likelihood with a layer-factored matrix normal posterior over the network weights, which jointly optimises the Hamiltonian parameters θ and the symmetry parameters η in a single backpropagation loop. The marginal likelihood's Occam's razor effect automatically penalises unnecessary symmetry complexity, avoiding collapse to trivial conserved quantities without explicit regularisers. This is, to the authors' knowledge, the first demonstration of differentiable Bayesian model selection via variational inference scaled to deep neural networks, as opposed to prior work relying on Laplace approximations.

## Results

On the 2D 3-body system, Noether's Razor achieves a test MSE of 0.0006 (in-distribution), 0.0004 (moved), and 0.0006 (wider), outperforming the vanilla HNN (0.0016, 0.0035, 0.0016) by 2.7×–8.7× and matching or exceeding the SE(2) oracle. On 3-fold harmonic oscillators, the method reduces test MSE from 2.14×10⁻⁵ (vanilla HNN) to 9.94×10⁻⁶, approaching the U(3) oracle's 8.98×10⁻⁶. The method correctly recovers the full 7-dimensional symmetry group 𝒢 for the 3-body system (parallelness >0.95) and the U(n) group for n-oscillators (parallelness >0.99), confirming that the learned conserved quantities span the correct generator subspace.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against a vanilla Hamiltonian Neural Network (HNN) [Greydanus et al., 2019] without any symmetry constraint, as well as an oracle model with the correct symmetry group (SO(2), U(n), or SE(2)) hard-coded into the architecture. Noether's Razor consistently outperforms the vanilla HNN across all three experiments and all test conditions: on the 3-body system, test MSE improves from 0.0016 to 0.0006 (in-distribution) and from 0.0035 to 0.0004 (moved test set), a 2.7× and 8.7× reduction respectively. On n-harmonic oscillators, test MSE drops from 2.14×10⁻⁵ to 9.94×10⁻⁶. Against the oracle, the learned-symmetry method matches or slightly exceeds performance on test MSE (e.g., 0.0004 vs. 0.0006 on the moved 3-body test set) while achieving a better ELBO (−12.81 vs. −11.32), indicating that the Bayesian model selection objective not only recovers the correct symmetry but does so with a tighter evidence bound. The key distinction is that the oracle requires prior knowledge of the symmetry group, whereas Noether's Razor discovers it entirely from training data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate research threads: physics-informed Hamiltonian neural networks (Greydanus et al., 2019) and Bayesian model selection for automatic symmetry discovery (van der Wilk et al., 2018; Immer et al., 2022). The key conceptual bridge is Noether's theorem, which allows symmetries to be parameterised as learnable quadratic conserved quantities, turning the symmetry-learning problem into a differentiable symmetrisation of the Hamiltonian. The methodological departure from prior work is the use of variational inference with matrix normal posteriors (Louizos and Welling, 2016) for Bayesian model selection on deep networks, where prior work relied on Laplace approximations (Immer et al., 2022) or was limited to single layers (van der Ouderaa and van der Wilk, 2021).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that the Occam's razor effect of the marginal likelihood can automatically select correct symmetry groups (U(n), SE(2)) without validation data or hand-tuned regularisers, matching oracle performance on small-scale dynamical systems. The claim that VI-based Bayesian model selection scales to deep neural networks is methodologically significant, as it opens a path to more expressive posterior approximations than Laplace for inductive bias selection. However, the experiments are limited to textbook systems (harmonic oscillators, 3-body in 2D) with quadratic conserved quantities only, single seeds, and no error bars; scaling to larger, non-integrable, or higher-dimensional systems and richer function classes for conserved quantities remains open.
