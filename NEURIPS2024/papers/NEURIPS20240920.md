## Problem

Vision Transformers are typically trained and deployed at a fixed input resolution (e.g., 224×224), but real-world images naturally vary in size and aspect ratio, and resizing to a preset resolution significantly degrades performance. Existing multi-resolution solutions like FlexiViT, ResFormer, and NaViT either require full-model retraining (high cost) or show insufficient accuracy at lower resolutions, and are often incompatible with pre-trained ViT architectures.

## Input / Output

The method accepts an RGB image at any resolution and aspect ratio without any resizing or cropping, directly computing patch embeddings on the original image. The patch kernel size is dynamically set to (⌊h/N⌋, ⌊w/N⌋) where N is the target number of patches, so the token count remains consistent regardless of input size. Outputs are task-specific: class probabilities for classification, per-pixel masks for segmentation, and bounding boxes with instance masks for detection, all produced by the unchanged Transformer encoder and task head.

## Method

MSPE replaces the single fixed-size patch embedding convolution in a ViT with K learnable adaptive convolution kernels of different base sizes, partitioning the resolution domain into K subsets that share weights. For a given input resolution, the appropriate kernel is selected and its weights are dynamically resized to the target kernel size via PI-resize (pseudo-inverse resize), enabling direct convolution on the original image without resizing. Training uses mixed-resolution sampling across K subsets for only 5 epochs, optimizing exclusively the patch embedding parameters while freezing the entire Transformer encoder, with a λ-weighted regularization term preserving original-resolution accuracy. Position embeddings are handled by standard bilinear interpolation, and the total parameter increase is minimal (2×–3× on the patch embedding layer only, negligible relative to the full model).

## Results

On ImageNet-1K classification, MSPE (trained for only 5 epochs) maintains 85.10% top-1 accuracy at the native 224×224 resolution and 85.11% at 448×448, while the vanilla ViT-B drops to 84.92% at 448×448; at the extreme low resolution of 28×28, MSPE achieves 56.41% versus 52.99% for the vanilla model. On Cityscapes segmentation (128×128), MSPE reaches 39.65 mIOU compared to 31.44 for FlexiViT and 2.89 for vanilla. On COCO2017 detection at 256 resolution, MSPE achieves 0.30 AP^b versus 0.19 for FlexiViT and 0.03 for vanilla, demonstrating the largest relative gains at low resolutions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MSPE consistently outperforms FlexiViT [15] across all three tasks (classification, segmentation, detection) and all tested resolutions, with the largest margins at low resolutions (e.g., 39.65 vs. 31.44 mIOU on Cityscapes at 128×128; 0.30 vs. 0.19 AP^b on COCO at 256). Against ResFormer [16], MSPE achieves superior multi-resolution ImageNet-1K accuracy while requiring only 5 training epochs versus ResFormer's 200 epochs, and MSPE is compatible with overlapping-patch architectures (PVT, MViT) that ResFormer's modified architecture cannot handle. Compared to NaViT [17], which relies on a large JFT pre-training dataset and up to 920,000 training steps, MSPE matches or exceeds its multi-resolution performance using only a standard ImageNet-21K pre-trained ViT and 5 epochs of fine-tuning. At the native training resolution (224×224 or 512×512/768×768), all methods including MSPE preserve the original model's performance, confirming that the patch-embedding-only modification does not degrade the well-trained encoder.

## Relation to Prior Work

MSPE builds directly on FlexiViT's PI-resize mechanism for dynamically adjusting patch embedding kernel sizes, but departs fundamentally in its optimization philosophy: rather than enforcing patch embedding consistency across resolutions (FlexiViT's goal), MSPE optimizes the patch embedding layer directly through the task loss function. It also shares the multi-resolution training paradigm with ResFormer and NaViT, but critically differs by freezing the Transformer encoder and modifying only the patch embedding layer, reducing training from 200+ epochs to just 5. The paper's key conceptual departure is the demonstration that high patch embedding similarity across resolutions does not guarantee optimal downstream performance, challenging the design rationale underlying FlexiViT.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is substantial: any well-trained ViT, DeiT, PVT, or MViT model can be made resolution-adaptive with minimal compute (5 epochs, ~3 hours on 8×RTX 4090), eliminating the need for image resizing at inference and avoiding the performance cliff at non-preset resolutions. The finding that the patch embedding layer is the critical bottleneck for resolution adaptability—while the encoder can remain frozen—provides a clear, actionable insight for practitioners deploying ViTs in variable-resolution settings. Limitations include the reliance on bilinear position embedding interpolation (acknowledged in Appendix A) and the absence of joint encoder-embedding optimization, which the authors identify as a natural extension. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to video transformers, 3D vision, or extremely low resolutions where the patch count becomes very small.
