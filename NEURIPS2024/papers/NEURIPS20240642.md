## Problem

Transformers excel at language modeling by combining contextual (in-context) information with global knowledge, yet the theoretical basis for how they learn both capabilities simultaneously remains unclear. This paper introduces the Sparse Contextual Bigram (SCB) model—a minimal extension of the classical bigram where the next token depends on a sparse, context-dependent set of prior positions—to provide a tractable setting for analyzing the training dynamics and sample complexity of transformers learning both in-context and global information.

## Input / Output

The model takes a token sequence of length T+1 (T context tokens drawn i.i.d. from a stationary distribution μ over an N-state vocabulary, plus one query token) and produces a distribution over the next token. The learned parameters are an N×N transition matrix P (global knowledge) and a T×N attention matrix Q that is Q-sparse per column (Q ≪ T), encoding which prior positions to attend to for each possible query token. The data-generating process first samples a position s according to the sparse vector q^(k) determined by the last token k, then runs one step of the Markov chain P from x_s to produce the output.

## Method

The learner is a one-layer single-head linear transformer F(x, x_{T+1}; V, A) = VXa^{(x_{T+1})} with cross-attention, trained via a three-stage preconditioned projected proximal gradient descent on ℓ₁-regularized MSE loss. Stage 1 (λ=0) boosts the signal from zero to a nontrivial value using poly(T) samples; Stage 2 introduces ℓ₁ regularization whose proximal step naturally zeros out irrelevant attention entries, enabling convergence with only poly(N,Q) samples; Stage 3 freezes A and fine-tunes V to target accuracy. The key analytical insight is that the population dynamics reduce to a 2D ODE in correlation coefficients (α_V, α_A), and the ℓ₁ proximal step implements the sparse attention selection that softmax attention achieves implicitly through its preconditioning matrix. For transfer learning, initializing V from a pretrained model with nontrivial correlation to the downstream task bypasses the sample-intensive Stage 1 entirely.

## Results

The main theoretical result (Theorem 3.1) shows that training a one-layer linear transformer from scratch on the SCB task requires poly(T, δ) samples in an initial signal-boosting stage, followed by only poly(N, Q, 1/ε, log T, δ) samples to reach ε-accuracy in both the transition matrix V and attention matrix A. The transfer learning result (Theorem 4.1) shows that with a nontrivial correlation between pretraining and downstream tasks, the sample-intensive first stage is entirely bypassed, requiring only poly(N, Q, 1/ε, log T) samples. Empirically (N=3, Q=2, T=5000, batch size 64), the ℓ₁-regularized proximal gradient descent converges to the ground truth within 1000 iterations, while vanilla SGD either diverges due to large gradient variance or converges far too slowly with a smaller learning rate.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary empirical comparison is between their ℓ₁-regularized proximal gradient descent and vanilla SGD on the same SCB task (N=3, Q=2, T=5000, B=64). After the signal-boosting stage (τ=400), the proximal method rapidly reduces ‖V−P‖_μ and ‖A−Q‖_μ to near zero, while SGD with the same learning rate (η₂=0.005) fails to converge due to the large variance of the unregularized stochastic gradient (which scales with T). Reducing SGD's learning rate to η₂′=0.001 prevents instability but yields an impractically slow convergence rate. The paper also compares linear attention (their model) against softmax attention on a smaller SCB task (T=50, N=10, Q=2), showing that both architectures learn nearly identical attention patterns and value matrices and converge to approximately the same loss, supporting the claim that the linear model captures the essential training dynamics of softmax transformers.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work analyzing transformer training dynamics from a Markov chain perspective (Bietti et al., 2024; Nichani et al., 2024; Edelman et al., 2024; Makkuva et al., 2024) by introducing the Sparse Contextual Bigram (SCB) model, which requires learning both in-context sparse attention and global transition probabilities simultaneously. Unlike prior works that analyze population loss or in-context learning tasks, this paper provides sample complexity guarantees for stochastic gradient-based training from scratch, and is (to the authors' knowledge) the first to study transfer learning for transformers. The architecture follows the one-layer linear transformer framework of Ahn et al. (2023) and Zhang et al. (2023b), using the same key-query reparameterization, but introduces a non-standard l1-regularized proximal gradient algorithm with preconditioning and projection to handle the high-variance regime when T ≫ N, Q.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clean theoretical explanation for the two-phase training dynamics observed in practice: an initial sample-intensive stage (poly(T) samples) to boost signal from zero, followed by a sample-efficient stage (poly(N,Q) samples) for convergence. The transfer learning result—that even an o(1)-sized correlation between pretraining and downstream tasks suffices to bypass the expensive initial stage—offers a principled justification for why pretraining helps with surprisingly weak task overlap. Key limitations include the one-layer single-head linear architecture, the non-standard multi-stage algorithm with manual hyperparameter switching, and the single-token (non-sequential) data generation, all of which limit direct practical applicability. Open questions include whether the poly(T) dependence in Stage 1 is truly unavoidable and how results extend to full sequence-to-sequence language modeling.
