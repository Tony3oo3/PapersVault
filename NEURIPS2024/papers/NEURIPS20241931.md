## Problem

Prior work on transductive online learning (Ben-David et al. 1997; Hanneke et al. 2023b) established minimax mistake bounds only for binary or finite label spaces, and their techniques break down when the label space is unbounded (e.g., |Y| ≥ 2^T). This paper resolves the open question of characterizing the optimal mistake bound for multiclass transductive online learning with an arbitrary, possibly uncountable label space.

## Input / Output

The learner receives the entire sequence of T instances (x_1,...,x_T) from an arbitrary instance space X before the game begins (the transductive property), and at each round t predicts a label ŷ_t from a label space Y that need not be countable. The adversary then reveals the true label y_t and the learner incurs 0-1 loss. The theoretical outputs are tight minimax bounds: the expected number of mistakes M*(T,C) in the realizable setting and the expected regret R*(T,C) in the agnostic setting, both expressed as functions of T and the concept class C via the new combinatorial dimensions.

## Method

The paper introduces two new combinatorial parameters: the Level-constrained Littlestone dimension D(C), defined via trees where the same instance labels all internal nodes at each level, and the Level-constrained Branching dimension B(C), which relaxes the requirement that outgoing edge labels be distinct. These yield a trichotomy: M*(T,C) is Θ(1) when B(C)<∞, Θ(log T) when D(C)<∞ but B(C)=∞, and Θ(T) when D(C)=∞. The key algorithmic innovation is a new notion of shattering that exploits the sequential structure of transductive learning, enabling a Halving-style algorithm to work without bounding the projection size of C (which can be infinite for unbounded Y). For the O(1) bound, the authors modify the Standard Optimal Algorithm to track the instance-dependent branching complexity B(V, x_{t:T}) as a potential function that decreases by at least 1 on each mistake. For the O(log T) bound, they count shattered subsequences of the instance sequence and show the count halves on each mistake, yielding a log-factor bound via a Sauer-Shelah-type argument adapted to the level-constrained setting.

## Results

The paper establishes a trichotomy for the minimax expected number of mistakes M*(T,C) in multiclass transductive online learning with unbounded label spaces: M*(T,C) is Θ(1) if B(C)<∞, Θ(log T) if D(C)<∞ and B(C)=∞, or Θ(T) if D(C)=∞, where D(C) and B(C) are the new Level-constrained Littlestone and Branching dimensions. The quantitative mistake bound is (1/2)min{max{D(C), ⌊log T⌋·1[B(C)=∞]}, T} ≤ M*(T,C) ≤ min{B(C), D(C)·log(eT/D(C)), T}. In the agnostic setting, the minimax regret satisfies √(TD(C)/8) ≤ R*(T,C) ≤ √(TD(C))·log(eT/D(C)), with a logarithmic gap between upper and lower bounds. These results remove the dependence on label set size |Y| present in prior bounds from Hanneke et al. [2023b].

**Trichotomy of minimax rates (Theorem 1 / Corollary 1).**

| Condition on C | Rate of M*(T, C) | Characterizing dimension |
|---|---|---|
| B(C) < ∞ | Θ(1) | Level-constrained Branching dim. B(C) |
| D(C) < ∞ and B(C) = ∞ | Θ(log T) | Level-constrained Littlestone dim. D(C) |
| D(C) = ∞ | Θ(T) | — |

**Agnostic regret bound (Theorem 2 / Theorem 4).**

| Setting | Lower bound | Upper bound | Gap |
|---|---|---|---|
| T ≥ D(C) | √(T·D(C)/8) | √(T·D(C))·log(eT/D(C)) | O(log T) |

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical baselines, but it provides extensive comparisons between the new dimensions and existing combinatorial parameters. The Level-constrained Littlestone dimension D(C) dominates the DS dimension (DS(C) ≤ D(C) for all C, with strict separation shown), and the Level-constrained Branching dimension B(C) is bounded above by the standard Littlestone dimension L(C) (B(C) ≤ L(C), with a class where B(C)≤2 but L(C)=∞). The paper also shows D(C) and the Graph dimension G(C) are incomparable (there exist classes with G(C)=1, D(C)=∞ and vice versa), and that log(NT(C)) ≤ B(C) with strict separation. Compared to the prior multiclass bounds of Hanneke et al. [2023b], the new bounds remove the explicit dependence on |Y|, which was the key limitation that broke down when |Y| ≥ 2^T. The Level-constrained Branching dimension is shown to be exactly equal to the rank-based dimension of Ben-David et al. [1997], providing a cleaner formulation of that earlier notion.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the transductive online learning framework initiated by Ben-David et al. [1997] and the binary trichotomy established by Hanneke et al. [2023b] to the multiclass setting with unbounded label spaces. The prior work by Hanneke et al. [2023b] showed that their techniques (relying on the Sauer-Shelah-Perles lemma and Halving algorithm) fundamentally break down when the label space is unbounded, leaving the characterization of the optimal mistake bound as an open question. The authors resolve this by introducing two new combinatorial dimensions—the Level-constrained Littlestone dimension and the Level-constrained Branching dimension—along with a novel shattering notion that exploits the sequential structure of transductive learning, enabling a modified Standard Optimal Algorithm (from Littlestone [1988]) and a new Halving-style argument that avoids dependence on label set size.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work provides the first complete characterization of minimax rates for multiclass transductive online learning with arbitrary (even uncountable) label spaces, confirming that the trichotomy of Θ(1), Θ(log T), and Θ(T) rates persists beyond the binary and finite-label settings. The new dimensions are shown to be incomparable with existing multiclass dimensions (DS, Graph, Natarajan Threshold), revealing that transductive online learning has a genuinely distinct combinatorial structure. Open questions include closing the logarithmic gap in the agnostic regret bounds, extending results to bandit feedback and real-valued regression settings, and understanding whether the new shattering technique applies to self-directed and best-order learning variants.
