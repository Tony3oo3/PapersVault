## Problem

Quantization errors in diffusion models accumulate across the multi-step denoising process, causing the sampling trajectory to deviate from its intended path and significantly degrading output quality. Existing remedies either only address temporal feature disturbance without correcting the latent-space distribution shift (TFMQ-DM) or are restricted to stochastic samplers (PTQD), leaving a gap for a general correction that works across both deterministic and stochastic samplers without altering the underlying quantization configuration.

## Input / Output

StepbaQ operates as a post-hoc correction layer on an already-quantized diffusion model: it takes a text prompt and produces a generated RGB image, identical in interface to the unmodified quantized pipeline. During a one-time calibration phase, it consumes a small dataset of 128 samples to compute per-step quantization error statistics (mean and variance) by running both the floating-point and quantized noise prediction networks in parallel. At inference, it applies the precomputed corrected sampling steps and error means without any additional forward passes, making it a zero-overhead plug-in that does not change the quantization bit-widths or calibration settings of the host model.

## Method

The key insight is that, under a Gaussian quantization-error assumption, the variance inflation of the latent variable at step t_i is statistically equivalent to sampling from a later (noisier) step τ_i, i.e., a 'stepback' in the denoising trajectory. StepbaQ computes τ_i by solving α_{τ_i} = α_{t_i}/(1+σ_i²) over the discrete α schedule, then applies three coordinated corrections: (1) temporal information alignment, feeding τ_i instead of t_i as the time embedding so the noise-prediction network receives the correct noise-level signal; (2) latent adjustment, subtracting the channel-wise error mean and rescaling by √(α_{τ_i}/α_{t_i}) to realign the latent distribution; and (3) step-size adaptation, replacing the original step size √(1−α_{t_i}) with the larger √(1−α_{τ_i}) to cover the increased distance to x_0. Crucially, StepbaQ measures quantization error cumulatively across multiple steps rather than independently, so even small per-step errors that individually fall below the correction threshold are still captured once they accumulate, preventing silent trajectory drift.

## Results

On SD v1.4 (DDIM, 20 steps), StepbaQ achieves FID 8.23 and 8.81 on MS-COCO and SDprompts respectively under W8A8, and FID 9.61 and 11.19 under W4A8, outperforming all baselines on both datasets and bit-widths. As a plug-and-play improvement over off-the-shelf quantization (NeuroPilot), StepbaQ reduces FID by 7.30 on SDprompts for SD v1.5 under W8A8 (23.66→16.36) and by 17.31 for SDXL-Turbo under W4A8 (45.45→28.14). CLIP scores also improve consistently, e.g., from 28.16 to 28.69 on SDprompts for SD v1.5 W8A8.

## Baselines & Comparisons

StepbaQ outperforms all three baselines (Q-Diffusion, PTQD, TFMQ-DM) on every metric across both W8A8 and W4A8 settings for SD v1.4. Against Q-Diffusion, StepbaQ reduces FID by 0.51 (MS-COCO) and 0.73 (SDprompts) under W8A8, and by 0.43 and 1.31 under W4A8. Against PTQD, the gains are larger: FID drops by 0.84 and 1.41 under W8A8, and by 0.62 and 0.63 under W4A8; notably, PTQD's correlated-noise elimination actually degrades performance below the Q-Diffusion baseline due to weak observed R² correlations. Against TFMQ-DM, StepbaQ improves FID by 0.40 and 0.60 under W8A8, and by 0.76 and 1.54 under W4A8, where TFMQ-DM's temporal-feature-only correction fails to address the latent-space distribution shift. StepbaQ also achieves the highest CLIP scores in all settings, confirming better text-image alignment.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on PTQD (He et al., NeurIPS 2024), which first established that quantization error in diffusion models follows an approximately Gaussian distribution and can be characterized by its statistics, and on DDIM (Song et al., ICLR 2021), whose deterministic sampling formula provides the mathematical substrate for the step correction. The key conceptual departure is reframing the distribution shift caused by accumulated quantization error as a temporal 'stepback' in the denoising trajectory, rather than treating it as noise to be absorbed (PTQD, limited to stochastic samplers) or as a temporal feature disturbance (TFMQ-DM, which ignores latent-space distribution shift). This positions StepbaQ as a complementary, sampler-agnostic correction layer that can be stacked on top of any existing PTQ pipeline without altering quantization settings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play nature of StepbaQ—requiring only 128 calibration samples and ~20 minutes of correction time—makes it immediately deployable within industry quantization toolchains (demonstrated with MediaTek's NeuroPilot), which is practically significant for on-device inference of diffusion models. The ablation revealing that step size adaptation is the dominant correction component (while temporal alignment alone yields only marginal gains) provides useful guidance for practitioners. A clear limitation is that the stepback mechanism is inapplicable to single-step generation (e.g., 1-step SDXL-Turbo), leaving an open question about how to handle quantization error in few-step or single-step regimes where there is no trajectory to correct.
