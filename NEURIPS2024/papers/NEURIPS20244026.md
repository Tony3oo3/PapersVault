## Problem

While the truncated eigen-decomposition is known to be optimal for low-rank kernel matrix approximation in spectral and Frobenius norms, the statistical behaviour of individual entries remained largely uncharacterized. This gap is practically important in applications where per-entry errors carry high cost (e.g., system control, healthcare) and theoretically important for analyses of learning algorithms that consume individual kernel entries.

## Input / Output

The method takes n i.i.d. samples from a fixed distribution ρ, a symmetric positive-definite continuous bounded kernel k, and a rank parameter d, and produces the best rank-d approximation via truncated eigen-decomposition. The output is a high-probability entrywise (max-norm) error bound whose rate depends on the eigenvalue decay of the associated integral operator: polynomial decay λ_i = O(i^{-α}) requires d = Ω(n^{1/α}) for entrywise consistency, while exponential decay λ_i = O(e^{-βi^γ}) requires only poly-logarithmic rank d > log^{1/γ}(n^{1/β}).

## Method

The core technical contribution is an eigenvector delocalisation result (Theorem 3) showing that eigenvectors corresponding to small eigenvalues satisfy ‖û_l‖_∞ = O(n^{-1/2}) with overwhelming probability — the first such result for a random matrix with non-zero mean and dependent entries. The proof adapts the principal-minor identity of Tao and Vu [2011], which expresses the first coordinate of an eigenvector in terms of the projection of a data vector onto a subspace spanned by eigenvectors of the (n−1)×(n−1) principal minor. A novel concentration inequality (Lemma 1) for the distance between a random vector with potentially non-zero mean and a subspace is then applied, generalising a zero-mean result from the same Tao–Vu paper. The entrywise error is ultimately bounded by decomposing it into a sum of tail eigenvalues (controlled via relative concentration bounds of Valdivia [2018]) times the maximum squared eigenvector entry (controlled by the delocalisation result).

## Results

The main theoretical result (Theorem 1) establishes that under polynomial eigenvalue decay λ_i = O(i^{-α}), the maximum entrywise error of the rank-d truncated eigen-decomposition is O(n^{-(α-1)/α} log(n)) with overwhelming probability, requiring rank d = Ω(n^{1/α}). Under exponential eigenvalue decay λ_i = O(e^{-βi^γ}), the entrywise error achieves O(n^{-1}) with only poly-logarithmic rank d > log^{1/γ}(n^{1/β}). Empirically, on low-dimensional datasets (GMM, Abalone, Wine Quality, MNIST), the maximum entrywise error decays rapidly with rank and the decay rate is ordered by kernel smoothness (ν=∞ fastest, ν=1/2 slowest). On high-dimensional datasets (20 Newsgroups, Zebrafish), even the smoothest kernel (ν=∞) shows very slow entrywise error decay, suggesting the theory does not directly extend to the high-dimensional regime.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is against randomised low-rank approximations via the Johnson-Lindenstrauss lemma (Theorem 2), which gives an entrywise error of O(√(log(n)/d)) for arbitrary positive semi-definite matrices with bounded entries. To achieve a polynomial entrywise error rate O(n^{-c}) with random projections, the rank d must be polynomial in n. In contrast, under the paper's exponential decay hypothesis (E), the spectral low-rank approximation achieves O(n^{-1}) entrywise error with only poly-logarithmic rank. Furthermore, the paper's bounds are o(n^{-1/2}) for the considered cases, a rate that random projections can never achieve regardless of the choice of rank d. The trade-off is that the paper's result requires structural assumptions on the kernel (positive-definite, eigenvalue decay, eigengap conditions), while the Johnson-Lindenstrauss bound holds for any PSD matrix with bounded entries.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the eigenvector delocalization program from Random Matrix Theory (Tao and Vu, 2011; Erdős et al., 2009) to the setting of kernel matrices, which have non-zero mean and dependent entries—a setting where prior delocalization results do not apply. The proof strategy directly adapts the principal-minor identity from Tao and Vu, but requires a novel concentration inequality (Lemma 1) for random vectors with non-zero mean to handle the dependence structure. On the kernel side, the paper builds on relative eigenvalue concentration bounds (Valdivia, 2018; Barzilai and Shamir, 2023) that improve upon classical absolute bounds (Rosasco et al., 2010), and positions itself within the broader entrywise error bounds literature for matrix estimation (covariance estimation, matrix completion, phase synchronization) where analogous results already exist but had not been established for kernel matrices.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper fills a genuine gap: while the truncated eigen-decomposition is known to be optimal in spectral and Frobenius norms, the statistical behavior of individual entries was previously uncharacterized, limiting the ability to analyze downstream algorithms that depend on entrywise accuracy. The key open question is whether the theory extends to the high-dimensional regime, where the empirical results (20 Newsgroups, Zebrafish) suggest it does not, and whether the bounds are tight—no lower bounds are established. The delocalization result for non-zero-mean, dependent-entry random matrices may find applications beyond kernel methods, and the entrywise analysis of randomized low-rank approximations (Nyström, random Fourier features) is identified as a natural next step.
