## Problem

Persistent homology (PH) dimension has been proposed as a generalization measure for overparameterized neural networks, with theoretical bounds linking it to the generalization gap. However, the practical reliability of these bounds under realistic training conditions—varying hyperparameters, non-standard initializations, and model-width scaling—remains untested. This paper asks whether the observed correlation between PH dimension and generalization gap is causal or merely an artifact of confounding hyperparameters, and identifies concrete failure modes where the measure breaks down.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 5,000 post-convergence SGD parameter vectors (the optimization trajectory about a local minimum) and the corresponding training data to compute two PH dimension estimates: one in the Euclidean parameter space and one in a data-dependent loss-based pseudometric space. Outputs include Spearman/Kendall correlation coefficients between each PH dimension and the generalization gap, partial correlations conditioned on learning rate at fixed batch sizes, and conditional mutual information (CMI) p-values testing whether PH dimension and generalization gap are conditionally independent given the hyperparameters. The generalization gap is defined as absolute accuracy gap for classification and absolute loss gap for regression.

## Method

The paper performs a multi-pronged empirical evaluation of PH dimension as a generalization measure across FCN-5, FCN-7, AlexNet, and a standard CNN on California Housing, MNIST, CIFAR-10, and CIFAR-100. First, it replicates the 6×6 learning-rate/batch-size grid of Dupuis et al. and extends the statistical analysis with partial correlations (conditioning on learning rate at fixed batch sizes) and a conditional independence test using CMI with local permutation null distributions, revealing that on MNIST the PH dimensions are conditionally independent of generalization gap given learning rate. Second, it trains models from adversarial initializations (pre-trained on random labels) to generate poorly generalizing models, showing that both Euclidean and loss-based PH dimensions fail to assign higher values to these models on FCN-5 MNIST and CNN CIFAR-10. Third, it varies CNN width multipliers to reproduce model-wise double descent, finding that neither PH dimension correlates with the (monotonic) generalization gap in this regime, though the Euclidean PH dimension itself exhibits a double-descent-like non-monotonicity with test accuracy.

## Results

On CHD and MNIST, both Euclidean and loss-based PH dimensions show positive Spearman's ρ correlations with generalization gap (ranging from 0.45 to 0.88), broadly consistent with the theoretical predictions of Birdal et al. and Dupuis et al. However, on AlexNet CIFAR-10, the loss-based PH dimension exhibits a negative correlation (ρ = −0.31), directly contradicting the theory. Conditional independence tests using conditional mutual information reveal that on MNIST, both PH dimensions are conditionally independent of generalization gap given learning rate for most batch sizes (p-values > 0.05), indicating the observed correlation is confounded by hyperparameters rather than reflecting a causal relationship.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper evaluates two PH dimension-based generalization measures: the Euclidean PH dimension from Birdal et al. (NeurIPS 2021) and the loss-based (data-dependent pseudometric) PH dimension from Dupuis et al. (ICML 2023). On CHD and MNIST, the loss-based measure consistently achieves stronger correlations with generalization gap than the Euclidean measure (e.g., ρ = 0.88 vs. 0.78 for FCN-7 MNIST), supporting Dupuis et al.'s theoretical relaxation of the Lipschitz assumption. However, on AlexNet CIFAR-10, the loss-based measure fails catastrophically with a negative correlation (ρ = −0.31) while the Euclidean measure retains a positive one (ρ = 0.85), inverting the expected ranking. Both measures fail to correctly identify poorly generalizing models under adversarial initialization on FCN-5 MNIST and CNN CIFAR-10, and neither correlates with generalization gap in the model-wise double descent regime. Notably, the simple ℓ² parameter norm achieves the strongest absolute correlations across all experiments (|ρ| = 0.87–0.98), and the learning rate/batch size ratio shows near-perfect correlation on classification tasks (ρ = −0.95 to −0.98), suggesting that much of the apparent PH-dimension–generalization relationship is a hyperparameter confound rather than a genuine topological signal.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper is a direct empirical stress-test of the PH dimension generalization framework established by Birdal et al. (NeurIPS 2021) and extended by Dupuis et al. (ICML 2023), which themselves build on the fractal dimension theory of Schweinhart (2021) and the Hausdorff-dimension-based bounds of Şimşekli et al. (2020). Rather than proposing a new measure, the authors reproduce the grid experiments of Dupuis et al., extend the statistical analysis with partial correlations and conditional mutual information tests, and probe failure modes (adversarial initialization, model-wise double descent) that the original theoretical works did not consider. The paper positions itself as a critical evaluation that reveals a gap between the theoretical guarantees and empirical behavior of these topological generalization measures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The findings challenge the practical utility of PH dimension as a generalization predictor: the measure fails for adversarially initialized models, does not correlate with generalization gap under model-width variation in the double descent regime, and in some settings is conditionally independent of generalization given hyperparameters. This suggests that the theoretical bounds, while mathematically valid, may be vacuous in practice due to dominating mutual information terms or unmet regularity assumptions. Open questions include whether probabilistic or multifractal formulations of fractal dimension could better capture the interplay between architecture, hyperparameters, and generalization, and whether the observed confounding by learning rate reflects a fundamental limitation of trajectory-based measures or an artifact of the specific experimental protocol.
