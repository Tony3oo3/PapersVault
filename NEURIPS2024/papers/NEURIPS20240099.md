## Problem

Mutual information estimation in high dimensions remains a fundamental challenge: classical estimators (KSG, WKL) suffer from the curse of dimensionality, and neural critics (MINE, NWJ) lack rigorous error bounds and can be unstable. While normalizing flows are powerful density estimators, prior work using them for MI either reconstructs full PDFs via expensive Monte Carlo integration or applies only marginal Gaussianization, which is insufficient to compute MI. The paper asks whether a normalizing-flow-based pipeline can yield direct, closed-form MI estimates with non-asymptotic error guarantees.

## Input / Output

The method consumes N paired samples {(x_k, y_k)} drawn from an absolutely continuous joint distribution over R^n × R^m, where n and m can be as large as 1024 (e.g., 32×32 grayscale images flattened). The output is a single scalar MI estimate computed via a closed-form log-determinant expression (for the Gaussian variant) or a sum of per-component log terms (for the tridiagonal variant), requiring no Monte Carlo integration, nearest-neighbor search, or auxiliary critic network at estimation time. The tridiagonal variant adds only min(n, m) learnable scalar parameters (the ρ_j correlation coefficients) on top of the flow architecture.

## Method

The core mechanism trains a Cartesian product of two normalizing flows f_X × f_Y via maximum likelihood so that the joint latent distribution p_{ξ,η} is well-approximated by a Gaussian (or tridiagonal Gaussian) base distribution, exploiting the invariance of MI under injective mappings (I(f_X(X); f_Y(Y)) = I(X;Y)). Because the log-likelihood of the joint flow is invariant under affine reparameterizations of the latent space, the refined tridiagonal variant fixes the marginal covariances to identity and restricts the cross-covariance to a diagonal matrix diag(ρ_j), reducing the problem to learning only min(d_ξ, d_η) correlation parameters. The MI estimate is then computed in closed form as −½ Σ_j log(1 − ρ̂_j²), which simultaneously performs a nonlinear canonical correlation analysis. Non-asymptotic error bounds are derived via KL divergence between the true latent distribution and the Gaussian model, and the estimator is proven consistent under standard universality assumptions on the flow family.

## Results

On high-dimensional synthetic image benchmarks (16×16 and 32×32 Gaussians and rectangles, 10⁴ samples), tridiag-N-MIENF and N-MIENF track the ground-truth MI closely across the full range of MI values, outperforming MINE, NWJ, Nishiyama, KSG, and WKL+CNN-AE (Figure 3). On incompressible high-dimensional data (uniform, smoothed uniform, Student, arcsinh-Student), both variants yield near-accurate estimates with small bias, while DINE-Gaussian fails entirely on the high-dimensional smoothed uniform case due to numerical instabilities (Figures 4–5). The 5-NN WKL estimator's RMSE degrades catastrophically with dimensionality: from 1.0 at d=4 to 522.4 at d=32 (Table 1), whereas MIENF maintains reasonable accuracy in the same regime.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MIENF (both N-MIENF and tridiag-N-MIENF) outperforms all six baselines on the primary synthetic image benchmarks (Figure 3), where MINE, NWJ, and Nishiyama show noticeable bias and wider confidence intervals, while KSG and WKL+CNN-AE are competitive only because the synthetic images are easily compressed by a CNN autoencoder. On incompressible high-dimensional data (Figure 4), MIENF maintains low bias across all tested distributions, whereas DINE-Gaussian [25] fails to produce reliable estimates on the high-dimensional smoothed uniform distribution due to numerical instabilities (Figure 5). The WKL estimator [27], which performs acceptably at d=4 (RMSE 1.0), degrades to RMSE 522.4 at d=32 (Table 1), confirming its unsuitability for high-dimensional MI estimation. MINE [7], described by the authors as the best-performing critic-based baseline, still exhibits larger variance and bias than MIENF on the image benchmarks. The key practical advantage of MIENF is that it requires no nearest-neighbor search, no MC integration of a learned PMI, and no explicit PDF reconstruction—only a standard normalizing flow training loop with O(d) additional parameters for the tridiagonal variant.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the well-known invariance of mutual information under injective mappings (Theorem 2.1) and the universality of normalizing flows, combining them into a practical MI estimator. It departs from critic-based estimators (MINE, NWJ) by avoiding adversarial training, and from DINE-Gaussian [25] by Gaussianizing the joint distribution rather than only the marginals, which enables non-asymptotic error bounds. The tridiagonal-covariance restriction is a novel connection to nonlinear canonical correlation analysis, reducing the problem to estimating O(d) correlation parameters with a closed-form MI expression and no Monte Carlo integration.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MI estimation in high dimensions is a fundamental bottleneck for information-theoretic analysis of deep networks, and this paper provides one of the few flow-based estimators with rigorous non-asymptotic error bounds and consistency guarantees. The tridiagonal variant is computationally lightweight (no MC integration, sparse covariance, O(d) extra parameters), making it attractive for practical use. The main open limitation is that consistency is proven only for distributions equivalent to Gaussian via diffeomorphisms, and the method's performance on truly non-Gaussianizable data, while empirically decent, lacks theoretical backing. Extending the framework to diffusion-based injective models and mixture base distributions is identified as a natural next step.
