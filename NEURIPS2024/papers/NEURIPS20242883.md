## Problem

Current LLM alignment methods (RLHF, DPO, SFT) require training and loading multiple models (actor, critic, reward, reference), consume substantial computational resources that scale with the upstream model size, and are model-specific, making rapid iteration and deployment across diverse models impractical. The paper asks whether an efficient, lightweight, and model-agnostic alignment approach can be developed that operates as a plug-and-play module without requiring access to upstream model parameters.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242883/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The Aligner receives a user query x and the initial answer y_o generated by any upstream LLM (API-based or open-source), and produces a corrected answer y_c that is better aligned with the 3H standards (helpfulness, harmlessness, honesty). It operates as a conditional seq2seq model stacked on top of the upstream model, requiring no parameter access during either training or inference. The same trained Aligner can be applied zero-shot to 11 different upstream models of varying sizes (2B to 70B) without any retraining or parameter adjustment.

## Method

Inspired by residual learning (ResNet), the Aligner learns correctional residuals between preferred and non-preferred responses rather than the full query-to-answer mapping. It is a small conditional seq2seq model μ_φ(y_c | y_o, x) trained with a simple maximum-likelihood objective on a Q-A-C dataset (query, original answer, corrected answer), which only requires changing one line of standard SFT training code. A two-stage training strategy is employed: first, a warm-up phase trains an identity Aligner on a Q-A-A subset to learn copy behavior, then the full Q-A-C residual correction training builds upon this foundation. The approach is model-agnostic because the Aligner's computational cost depends solely on its own parameter size, not the upstream model's scale, and it can even iteratively bootstrap upstream models by generating synthetic preference data from corrected responses for multi-round RLHF/DPO training.

## Results

Aligner-7B, trained once on a 50K Q-A-C preference dataset, improves 11 different upstream LLMs (GPT-4, GPT-3.5, Claude 2, Beaver-7B, Alpaca-7B, Vicuna-7B/13B/33B, Llama2-7B/13B/70B-Chat) in a zero-shot manner. Across the 3H evaluation (helpfulness, harmlessness, honesty), Aligner-7B achieves an average improvement of 68.9% in helpfulness and 22.8% in harmlessness (per the abstract), with the most dramatic gains on unaligned models (e.g., +99.4% empathy and +78.7% reasoning on Alpaca-7B). On the Alpaca-Eval leaderboard, stacking Aligner-2B on GPT-4 Turbo raises the LC Win Rate from 55.0% to 58.3%, surpassing GPT-4 Omni's 57.5%. Aligner also shows OOD generalization, improving HumanEval, MMLU, MATH, and MT-Bench scores across all tested upstream models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against inference-time self-refinement baselines (Table 2), Aligner-7B outperforms Constitutional AI on harmlessness for GPT-4 (+26.88% vs +9.65%) and Alpaca2-7B (+58.86% vs +24.08%), and beats Self-Critique on harmlessness for GPT-4 (+26.88% vs +15.30%) and Beaver-7B (+9.00% vs −11.6%, where Self-Critique actually degrades safety). However, Self-Critique edges out Aligner on helpfulness for GPT-4 (+26.56% vs +17.47%) and Alpaca2-7B (+30.07% vs +36.55%—actually Aligner wins here). Against RLHF and DPO fine-tuning (Table 3), Aligner-7B consistently surpasses both: on the Q-A-C dataset it beats RLHF by +24.4% helpfulness and +21.9% harmlessness, and DPO by +49.1% helpfulness; on Ultra-Feedback it beats RLHF by +25.47% helpfulness and DPO by +27.21% helpfulness. Against Best-of-N selection (BoN N=5, N=10) and Beam Search (Table 12), Aligner-7B outperforms all inference-time intervention methods on average across most 3H metrics, e.g., achieving 82.5% average empathy improvement vs. 68.73% (BoN-5) and 72.59% (BS), while requiring only a single forward pass rather than N generations.

## Relation to Prior Work

Aligner carves a distinct niche between two existing families of alignment methods: (1) parameter-level approaches (SFT, RLHF, DPO) that require access to and modification of the target model's weights, and (2) inference-time methods (IPA, prompt optimization, BoN selectors) that are model-agnostic but typically sensitive to the specific upstream model. Conceptually, the paper repurposes the residual learning paradigm from ResNet—where a network learns a correction on top of an identity mapping—into the alignment setting, training a small seq2seq model to learn correctional residuals between preferred and dispreferred responses. It also draws on representation engineering and activation steering for interpretability, and positions itself against self-refinement methods (CAI, Self-Critique, Self-Refine) by using a separate trained model rather than relying on the upstream model's own self-correction ability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play, model-agnostic design is practically significant because it enables alignment of API-based models (GPT-4, Claude) without parameter access, a setting where RLHF/DPO are inapplicable. The multi-round RLHF bootstrap capability—using Aligner-corrected outputs as synthetic preference data to iteratively improve the upstream model—offers a potential path to break through a model's performance ceiling without retraining from scratch. Key limitations include the added inference latency (an extra forward pass through the Aligner), the open question of whether the correction paradigm generalizes to multi-turn dialogues and domain-specific alignment, and the fact that the interpretability evidence (early-layer correction decisions) is demonstrated on a single 7B model. Future work on smaller Aligners (0.5B) and parallelized correction could address the efficiency trade-off.
