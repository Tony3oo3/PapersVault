## Problem

Existing protein mutation effect prediction methods rely on global representations such as multiple sequence alignments or domain-level structure clustering, which fail to capture the local biochemical microenvironments that primarily determine point mutation effects. There is no efficient mechanism to retrieve atom-level structural information from the entire protein universe (rather than restricting to a single protein family) to augment mutation effect prediction.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241555/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a protein backbone structure (C, Cα, N heavy-atom coordinates) and a mutation specification (wild-type and mutant residue types) as input. It retrieves 16-residue local structure motifs from a 130M+ residue PDB database via GPU-accelerated kNN search in ESM-IF embedding space, performing separate retrievals for the wild-type and mutant central residues. The output is a ΔΔG score (binding free energy change) for PPI surface mutations or a stability change prediction, with per-structure ranking being the primary evaluation criterion.

## Method

The framework builds a Structure Motif Embedding Database (SMEDB) by encoding all PDB residues with ESM-IF's GVP-Transformer encoder into 512-dim embeddings, indexed in a CUHNSW graph for GPU-accelerated approximate kNN search. Multiple Structure Motif Alignment (MSMA) retrieves top-k neighbors, filters by central amino acid identity, and scores candidates by a weighted contact-count function to retain 16 motifs per query, which are superimposed onto the raw motif via central-frame alignment. The MSM-IPA module (inspired by AlphaFold2's Invariant Point Attention) performs cross-attention with the raw motif as query and the combined raw+retrieved motifs as keys, maintaining SE(3) invariance through scalar, vector, and predicted-point attention channels. The full MSM-Mut model is trained in two phases: self-supervised pretraining on PDB-REDO (masked residue prediction, first without then with retrieved motifs) followed by supervised finetuning on cDNA120K and SKEMPI. The key insight is that an inverse-folding encoder captures spatial context around a residue (not just contiguous sequence positions), enabling retrieval of structurally analogous but sequence-unrelated local motifs that are complementary to MSA-derived information.

## Results

On the SKEMPI2.0 PPI surface mutation benchmark, MSM-Mut achieves a per-structure Pearson correlation of 0.4736 and overall Pearson of 0.6814, with RMSE of 1.4703 and MAE of 1.0212, setting new state-of-the-art across all six evaluation metrics. On the S669 protein stability dataset, MSM-Mut reaches a Pearson correlation of 0.54 (RMSE 1.51), surpassing Stability Oracle (0.52) and all other baselines. On the Novozymes thermostability dataset (a novel enzyme with <30% sequence identity to PDB), MSM-Mut achieves a Spearman correlation of 0.484, outperforming Rosetta (0.438) and FoldX (0.415).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MSM-Mut outperforms all 14 baselines on SKEMPI2.0 across every metric. The closest competitor, RDE-Net, achieves per-structure Pearson of 0.4448 vs. MSM-Mut's 0.4736 (+0.029), and overall Pearson of 0.6447 vs. 0.6814 (+0.037); DiffAffinity trails by 0.052 in per-structure Pearson and 0.025 in overall Pearson. Among unsupervised methods, ESM-IF is the strongest (per-structure Pearson 0.2241) but is still 0.25 below MSM-Mut, highlighting the value of the retrieval-augmented supervised pipeline. On S669, MSM-Mut (Pearson 0.54) edges out Stability Oracle (0.52) on correlation but is slightly worse on RMSE (1.51 vs. 1.43), while decisively beating ProteinMPNN (0.26), ESM-IF (0.27), and Rosetta (0.39). On the Novozymes thermostability dataset, MSM-Mut (Spearman 0.484) surpasses Rosetta (0.438) and FoldX (0.415) by 4.6 and 6.9 points respectively, demonstrating generalization to a novel enzyme with no close PDB homologs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant MSA-based paradigm for extracting coevolutionary information in mutation effect prediction, instead retrieving local structure motifs from the entire PDB via an inverse-folding encoder (ESM-IF) and GPU-accelerated kNN search (CUHNSW). The MSM-IPA architecture adapts AlphaFold2's Invariant Point Attention to aggregate information from multiple retrieved structure motifs, which the authors note is the first use of multiple structures for single mutation effect prediction. Conceptually, the work bridges retrieval-augmented generation (as in ReALM for language) with geometric deep learning for proteins, replacing global sequence/structure alignment with a microscopic, local-motif-level retrieval that is specialized to point-mutation scenarios.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a scalable and interpretable path for protein engineering: by indexing the full PDB as a vector database, it can retrieve relevant local environments even for novel proteins with no close PDB homologs (demonstrated on the Novozymes enzyme). The finding that MSM-profile and MSA-profile capture complementary coevolutionary signals suggests future models could fuse both retrieval channels. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond point mutations to larger redesigns, how performance scales as PDB coverage grows, and whether the ESM-IF encoder is truly optimal versus purpose-built structure encoders.
