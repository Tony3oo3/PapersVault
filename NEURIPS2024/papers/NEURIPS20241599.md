## Problem

Certified risk control methods like selective prediction and conformal prediction require correctness labels, but standard metrics (EM, F1) fail for language generation because multiple valid answers exist for the same question. This 'metric misalignment' makes it impossible to directly apply these principled methods to control hallucination in generative language models, and existing selective generation approaches are purely supervised, requiring expensive human entailment annotations for every sample.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241599/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a question as input to a frozen GLM (GPT-3.5-Turbo or Alpaca-7B), which produces a generated answer via greedy decoding. The selective generator then either returns the generated answer or abstains with 'IDK', and additionally outputs a certified FDR-E upper bound. Entailment labels are constructed by treating the generated answer as a premise and the reference answer (converted to declarative form) as a hypothesis, annotated using a DeBERTa-v2-xxlarge-mnli entailment classifier. The system operates on the Natural Questions dataset with approximately 7.3k labeled samples for Alpaca-7B and 4.6k for GPT-3.5-Turbo, plus up to 27k unlabeled samples for semi-supervised learning.

## Method

The paper defines FDR-E (false discovery rate with respect to textual entailment) as the risk metric, where a generated answer is correct if it entails the reference answer. SGen^Semi is a semi-supervised algorithm that (1) learns an entailment set function via PAC conformal prediction on labeled data to control the false entailment rate (FER) of pseudo-labels, (2) uses this set to pseudo-label unlabeled examples, and (3) learns a selection function over both labeled and pseudo-labeled data with a binomial-tail-bound FDR-E guarantee. To improve selection efficiency, the method introduces neuro-selection functions—multiple-threshold indicator functions with learnable bias terms—that combine two scoring functions (conditional probability and self-consistency score) and automatically select the optimal combination among candidates. The overall algorithm provides a PAC guarantee that the FDR-E of the learned selective generator is bounded by a user-specified level ε_S with confidence 1−δ, without requiring full human annotation of entailment labels.

## Results

On the Natural Questions dataset with a target FDR-E of 0.25, SGen^Semi achieves an FDR-E of 0.1785 with 77.7% selection efficiency on GPT-3.5-Turbo (using the self-consistency score f_M2), and an FDR-E of 0.0698 with 32.0% efficiency on Alpaca-7B. The average efficiency across both scoring functions is 0.7334 for GPT-3.5-Turbo and 0.3173 for Alpaca-7B. The method satisfies the desired FDR-E guarantee (≤0.25) in all certified runs, while the single-threshold variant SGen_NoMS^Semi occasionally fails to meet the target (e.g., FDR-E of 0.1338 with f_M1 on GPT-3.5-Turbo).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is SGen_EM [9], a certified unsupervised selective generation method that uses exact match (EM) as the correctness metric. SGen^Semi outperforms SGen_EM on selection efficiency for GPT-3.5-Turbo (0.7769 vs. 0.7334) while maintaining a comparable FDR-E (0.1785 vs. 0.1589), demonstrating that the entailment-based metric avoids the overly conservative abstention caused by EM's metric misalignment. On Alpaca-7B, both methods achieve similar efficiency (~0.32) and FDR-E (~0.07), with SGen^Semi slightly edging out on efficiency. The heuristic semi-supervised baselines (SGen_PL^H-Semi, SGen_PFL^H-Semi) achieve higher raw efficiency (up to 0.8183 on GPT-3.5) but provide no theoretical FDR-E guarantee, and SGen_PFL^H-Semi actually exceeds the 0.25 target on GPT-3.5 (FDR-E = 0.2002 is below target but the method offers no bound). The single-threshold certified variant SGen_NoMS^Semi achieves lower FDR-E (0.0914) but at the cost of significantly reduced efficiency (0.5332 vs. 0.7769), highlighting the benefit of the neuro-selection function in SGen^Semi for choosing optimal scoring function combinations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the selective classification framework of Geifman & El-Yaniv (2017) from classification to open-ended language generation, replacing the exact-match correctness metric with textual entailment to address the 'metric misalignment' problem where multiple valid answers exist. It further builds on PAC conformal prediction (Park et al., 2020; Vovk et al., 2005) to construct an entailment set function that enables certified pseudo-labeling of unlabeled data, a novel application of conformal prediction to semi-supervised selective generation. The paper departs from concurrent supervised selective generation methods (Mohri et al., 2023; Gui et al., 2024) by introducing a semi-supervised algorithm and a general class of 'neuro-selection functions' that learn to combine multiple scoring functions, rather than relying on a single pre-specified confidence score.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first principled semi-supervised framework for controlling hallucination in language models with PAC guarantees, reducing the expensive human annotation burden by leveraging unlabeled data through conformal pseudo-labeling. The introduced NQ Entailment dataset (7.3k and 4.6k samples for Alpaca-7B and GPT-3.5-Turbo) fills a gap in the community's resources for entailment-based hallucination evaluation. However, the i.i.d. assumption is a significant practical limitation, and the authors acknowledge that empirical FDR-E is not tightly bounded under the target ε (especially on Alpaca-7B), suggesting the theoretical guarantees may be loose in practice. Open questions include extending to non-i.i.d. settings, fully learnable neuro-selection functions, and tighter risk bounds.
