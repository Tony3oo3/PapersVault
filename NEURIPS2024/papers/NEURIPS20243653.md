## Problem

Massive offline RL datasets (million-level, collected by sub-optimal policies) make policy training inefficient, but directly distilling them into small expert behavioral data is non-trivial because policy performance cannot be measured without environment interaction. The paper formalizes offline behavior distillation (OBD) and identifies that the natural surrogate objectives (DBC, PBC) suffer from a quadratic discount complexity O(1/(1-γ)²) performance guarantee that collapses under the intractable bi-level optimization, leaving a gap between the objective value and true policy return.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a large offline RL dataset of (state, action, next_state, reward) 4-tuples (0.2M–2M samples) along with a near-optimal policy π* and action-value function q_π* pre-extracted via Cal-QL. It produces a tiny synthetic dataset of N_syn=256 (state, action) 2-tuples — notably dropping the next-state and reward — which is exclusively usable for supervised behavioral cloning rather than Bellman-backup RL. The 2-tuple constraint means the distilled data injects decision-making knowledge into policy networks but cannot support conventional offline RL algorithms.

## Method

OBD is solved as a bi-level optimization: the inner loop trains a 4-layer MLP policy via BC on the synthetic data for T=100 steps, while the outer loop updates the synthetic (s,a) pairs by backpropagating through time (BPTT) to minimize a surrogate loss H. The key contribution is the Av-PBC objective H(π, D_off) = E_{s~D_off}[q_π*(s,·)(π(·|s) − π*(·|s))²], which weights the squared decision difference between the distilled policy and the near-optimal policy by the action-value function. The paper proves (Theorem 2) that the policy performance gap J(π*)−J(π) equals the action-value-weighted decision difference up to a 1/(1−γ) factor, yielding a linear discount complexity O(1/(1−γ)) guarantee (Corollary 1) that is strictly tighter than the quadratic O(1/(1−γ)²) bound of unweighted PBC. In practice, each outer step samples states from D_off, draws actions from π*, computes the weighted loss, and performs a gradient descent update on the synthetic data with momentum 0.9.

## Results

On the D4RL benchmark (9 datasets across Halfcheetah, Hopper, and Walker2D), Av-PBC achieves an average normalized return of 38.2, outperforming PBC (30.4) by 25.7% and DBC (20.9) by 82.8%. Av-PBC also converges in roughly one-quarter of the distillation steps required by DBC and PBC, and training a policy on the 256-sample synthetic dataset takes only 0.2 s (100 steps) versus 25k–125k steps for BC on the full offline data, a >99.5% reduction in training time. With policy ensemble (10 networks), Av-PBC further gains 25.8% over its single-policy result, reaching an average of 48.1.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is among three OBD objectives (DBC, PBC, Av-PBC) plus a random-selection baseline, all distilling only 256 (state, action) pairs from the full offline dataset. Av-PBC wins on 8 out of 9 datasets against PBC and on all 9 against DBC, with the largest margins on low-quality medium-replay data (e.g., Halfcheetah M-R: 35.9 vs. 19.4 for PBC and 2.5 for DBC). Against the full-data baselines, Av-PBC (38.2 avg) falls short of BC trained on the entire offline dataset (45.7) and Cal-QL offline RL (70.1), which is expected given the 256-sample budget versus millions of transitions; however, Av-PBC surpasses BC(Whole) on the medium-replay datasets (e.g., Walker2D M-R: 55.0 vs. 14.6), showing that distilled data can outperform full-data BC when the source data is noisy. The ensemble experiment (Table 3) further confirms Av-PBC's advantage: with 10-policy ensembles, Av-PBC gains +9.9 over its single-policy score versus +7.0/+7.3 for DBC/PBC, reaching 48.1 average normalized return.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the bi-level optimization framework of dataset distillation (Wang et al., 2018) to the offline RL setting, where the inner loop trains a policy via behavioral cloning on synthetic (state, action) pairs and the outer loop updates those pairs to minimize a surrogate loss. It explicitly positions itself as parallel to, rather than an extension of, the online behavior distillation of Lupu et al. (2024), arguing that the offline setting introduces a fundamentally different challenge: the absence of environment interaction forces the use of a data-based surrogate objective rather than true policy return. The theoretical core builds on the TV-distance performance bound of Xu et al. (2020) (Theorem 1) and departs from it by proving an equivalence between policy performance gap and action-value-weighted decision difference (Theorem 2), which motivates the Av-PBC objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

OBD offers a practical path to reducing the training cost of offline RL by orders of magnitude (99.5% training time reduction) while also addressing data privacy concerns for sensitive domains like healthcare. The clean theoretical result—improving the discount complexity from quadratic to linear via a simple action-value weighting—provides a principled design principle for surrogate objectives in bi-level distillation problems more broadly. However, two significant limitations remain: the bi-level optimization is still computationally expensive (~25 hours for 50k steps on a V100), and a substantial performance gap persists between distilled-data BC (38.2) and full offline RL (70.1), leaving open the question of whether the 2-tuple (state, action) representation is sufficient to capture the decision-making knowledge in 4-tuple offline data.
