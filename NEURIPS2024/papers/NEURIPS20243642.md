## Problem

Existing parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) methods for ViT adaptation reduce tunable parameters but do not reduce inference computation compared to full tuning, limiting deployment of large pre-trained ViTs on downstream tasks. This paper addresses the unexplored question of how to simultaneously improve both parameter efficiency and inference efficiency during ViT adaptation across diverse visual tasks.

## Input / Output

The method accepts RGB images resized to 224×224 (or 512×512 for segmentation) and 8-frame video clips at 224×224, producing class labels, per-pixel segmentation masks, or bounding boxes depending on the task. A key design constraint is that DyT never reduces the total token count—deactivated tokens skip the MLP block but still pass through the adapter—preserving full spatial resolution and making the approach directly compatible with dense prediction without architectural modifications.

## Method

DyT inserts a token dispatcher (TD) before each MLP block that learns, via a Gumbel-Sigmoid mechanism, which tokens to activate; only activated tokens traverse the frozen MLP while all tokens (including deactivated ones) are processed by a parallel lightweight adapter. The paper identifies MLP dispatch as the optimal variant over attention, attention-MLP, or full-layer dispatch, since skipping attention tokens disrupts token mixing. An MoE-adapter variant replaces the single adapter with N experts whose weights are computed by a routing layer from a global image representation, then combined via a mathematically equivalent single-matrix multiplication, yielding N-adapter capacity at single-adapter FLOPs. Training uses a composite loss combining classification, a rate-constraint loss to control activation ratio, and a self-distillation term where the complete (non-dynamic) model supervises the dynamic model, a capability unique to this architecture.

## Results

On the VTAB-1K benchmark (19 datasets, 1K training samples each), DyT with r=0.5 achieves a group-mean accuracy of 77.14% using only 0.16M trainable parameters and 12.54 GFLOPs (71% of ViT-B/16's 17.58 GFLOPs), surpassing the best PEFT baseline Res-Tuning (76.32%) by 0.82 points while reducing FLOPs by 29%. With r=0.7, DyT reaches 77.57% at 14.92 GFLOPs. On video action recognition (K400/SSv2, 8 frames), DyT† with 4 MoE experts achieves 60.98% average accuracy at 105.45 GFLOPs, compared to 142.53 GFLOPs for full tuning. On ADE20K semantic segmentation, DyT†N=4 reaches 47.67 mIoU at 584.40 GFLOPs, nearly matching full tuning (47.66 mIoU at 605.36 GFLOPs).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DyT outperforms all 12 PEFT baselines on VTAB-1K group-mean accuracy, beating the strongest competitor Res-Tuning (76.32%) by 0.82 points at r=0.5 and 1.25 points at r=0.7, while using 29–29% fewer FLOPs (12.54 vs. 17.67 G). Against AdaptFormer (74.75%, 17.61 G) and LoRA (74.60%, 17.58 G), DyT gains 2.4–3.0 points with the same 0.16M parameter budget but substantially lower inference cost. On video recognition (Table 6), DyT (60.36% avg) is marginally below AdaptFormer (60.45%) and LoRA (60.55%) in accuracy but reduces FLOPs from ~142.5 G to 108.3 G (24% reduction); DyT†N=4 closes this gap to 60.98% at 105.45 G. On ADE20K segmentation, DyT†N=4 (47.67 mIoU) essentially matches full tuning (47.66) while saving 21 G FLOPs, and clearly beats AdaptFormer (47.52) and LoRA (47.34). On COCO object detection, DyT (39.78 BBox mAP) surpasses AdaptFormer (38.71) by 1.07 points at fewer FLOPs (468.4 vs. 564.5 G), though full tuning (44.67) remains significantly ahead, indicating a larger domain gap for detection.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two research threads: parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) methods like adapters (Houlsby et al., 2019), LoRA, and VPT, which reduce tunable parameters but leave inference FLOPs unchanged; and dynamic neural networks like DynamicViT and EViT, which reduce inference cost via token pruning but require training from scratch or full fine-tuning on the same dataset. DyT departs from both by introducing a learnable token dispatcher that skips MLP blocks during inference while keeping the backbone frozen, combined with lightweight adapters for cross-domain adaptation. The differentiable token selection mechanism is derived from the Gumbel-Softmax reparameterization (Jang et al., 2016), adapted here into a Gumbel-Sigmoid formulation for binary token activation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By unifying parameter and inference efficiency in a single framework, DyT addresses a practical bottleneck for deploying large ViTs in resource-constrained settings, and its token-skipping (rather than pruning) design preserves full spatial resolution, making it directly applicable to dense prediction tasks like segmentation without architectural modifications. The finding that skipping only MLP blocks (not attention) yields the best accuracy-FLOPs trade-off is a useful practical guideline for future efficient ViT designs. Limitations include the current restriction to vision tasks, the 1.8× training overhead from the self-distillation component, and the observation that full tuning still outperforms DyT on challenging tasks like object detection, suggesting the PEFT constraint remains a ceiling for some downstream objectives.
