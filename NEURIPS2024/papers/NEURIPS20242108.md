## Problem

Euler-Maruyama discretization of Langevin Dynamics (used in DDPM) requires a large number of small steps for high-quality samples, with quality degrading rapidly at fewer steps. The Randomized Midpoint Method offers a better discretization but only has theoretical guarantees under strong log-concavity, while practical applications like diffusion models involve non-log-concave densities with time-varying drift. This paper bridges that gap by proving quadratic speedup under much weaker assumptions.

## Input / Output

The method is a training-free scheduler applied to a pre-trained Latent Diffusion Model: it takes a standard Gaussian noise vector in latent space and the pre-trained score network (estimating ∇log p_τ at discrete timesteps) as input, and outputs a 256×256 RGB image. The computational cost is measured in neural network (drift) evaluations rather than wall-clock time, and the method reduces this from 1000 calls (standard DDPM) to 40–80 calls while maintaining sample quality. The score network is only trained at 1000 discrete timesteps, which naturally aligns with the method's discrete midpoint structure.

## Method

The Poisson Midpoint Method approximates K steps of Euler-Maruyama with step-size α/K by a single step of step-size α via stochastic correction. It first computes a cheap interpolation of the trajectory using only the initial drift evaluation, then randomly selects midpoints (each with probability 1/K in Option 1, or one uniform midpoint in Option 2) and evaluates the drift at those points. The difference between the drift at the interpolated midpoint and the initial point serves as a correction term that reduces the per-step bias from O(α) to O(α²), yielding a quadratic speedup. The theoretical analysis uses a CLT-based framework to show the accumulated stochastic correction is close to Gaussian noise, requiring only L-smoothness of the potential and mild initialization conditions—no isoperimetry or strong log-concavity. For diffusion models, this becomes a scheduler that replaces K consecutive DDPM denoising steps with one corrected step, with a covariance correction applied to control the additional variance introduced by the random midpoint selection.

## Results

On four image generation datasets (CelebAHQ 256, LSUN Churches, LSUN Bedrooms, FFHQ), the Poisson Midpoint Method matches the quality of DDPM with 1000 neural network calls using only 40–80 neural network calls (a 12–25× reduction in compute). For example, on CelebAHQ 256 the best PMM variant achieves a Clip-FID of ~2.15 at 50 NN calls, while on LSUN Churches it reaches FID ~4.75 at 50 NN calls. The method outperforms ODE-based schedulers (DDIM, DPM-Solver) at comparable compute budgets across all four datasets, though DPM-Solver remains superior in the very low-step regime (~10 NN calls).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DDPM (1000-step SDE scheduler), PMM with 40–80 NN calls achieves comparable or better FID/Clip-FID on all four datasets, representing a 12–25× reduction in neural network evaluations. Against DDIM (ODE-based, η=0), PMM at 50–80 NN calls outperforms DDIM at the same compute on CelebAHQ 256 (Clip-FID 2.15 vs. ~2.5+), LSUN Bedrooms (FID 4.46 vs. ~4.9+), and FFHQ (Clip-FID 2.63 vs. ~3.3+). Against DPM-Solver (tuned over 6 configurations of order and skip_type), PMM wins on CelebAHQ 256 (2.15 vs. 2.50 at 100 calls), LSUN Bedrooms (4.46 vs. 4.82 at 100 calls), and FFHQ (2.63 vs. 3.28 at 100 calls), but DPM-Solver retains an edge on LSUN Churches at 100 calls (3.99 vs. 3.81 for PMM at 100 calls, though PMM at 50 calls gives 4.75). In the very low-step regime (~10 NN calls), DPM-Solver remains the best option, while PMM's quality degrades rapidly below ~40 NN calls due to the stochastic approximation breaking down at large step-sizes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Randomized Midpoint Method (RLMC) of Shen & Lee [40] and subsequent analyses [18, 50], which were restricted to strongly log-concave targets, by replacing the continuous uniform midpoint with a discrete Poisson-like sampling of midpoints. This discrete structure enables a CLT-based analysis (following Das et al. [10]) that yields error bounds under only L-smoothness and mild initialization conditions—no strong log-concavity or isoperimetry required. The method is further generalized to underdamped Langevin dynamics and time-varying drifts, making it applicable to diffusion model sampling where prior midpoint methods had no theoretical guarantees.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The quadratic speedup over Euler-Maruyama under Logarithmic Sobolev Inequality (a strictly weaker condition than strong log-concavity) is a meaningful theoretical advance for MCMC sampling, and the practical demonstration that 50–80 neural network calls match 1000-step DDPM quality (while outperforming ODE-based solvers like DPM-Solver at similar compute) offers a concrete 4× inference efficiency gain for diffusion models. A clear limitation is that quality degrades rapidly below ~40–50 calls, suggesting the stochastic approximation breaks down at very large step-sizes. Open questions include deriving convergence bounds for time-dependent drifts (as in DDPM), extending to Poincaré inequality conditions, and handling Hölder-continuous gradients.
