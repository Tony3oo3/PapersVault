## Problem

Offline safe RL methods that rely on regularization to prevent out-of-distribution actions are fundamentally limited by the Safe Dataset Mismatch (SDM) problem: when the offline dataset is collected by a behavior policy that is either too aggressive (tempting, high-reward but unsafe) or too conservative (low-cost but low-reward), regularization pushes the learned policy toward the suboptimal behavior policy, causing either safety violations or severe reward degradation. Existing model-centric approaches—constrained Q-learning, distribution correction estimation, and sequential modeling—fail to resolve this distributional gap, and no systematic data-centric strategy had been applied to offline safe RL.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pre-collected offline trajectory dataset (state, action, next-state, reward, cost tuples from potentially imperfect behavior policies) along with a scalar safety threshold κ, and produces a reshaped trajectory dataset of generated sub-trajectories (length L=32) whose reward and cost returns are steered toward target conditions [Ĉ, R̂] with Ĉ ≤ κ. The generated dataset is then fed to a downstream BCQ-Lag agent to produce the final safe policy. Unlike standard data augmentation that merely resamples or reweights existing transitions, OASIS synthesizes entirely new state sequences via a diffusion process, with actions recovered through a learned inverse dynamics model and rewards/costs assigned by learned labeling models.

## Method

OASIS trains a conditional diffusion model (U-Net denoising core) on sub-trajectories from the raw dataset, using classifier-free guidance with the condition vector y(τ) = [C(τ), R(τ)] and masking the condition with probability p=0.25 during training. At generation time, the model denoises from Gaussian noise toward a target condition [Ĉ, R̂] with Ĉ ≤ κ, fixing the initial state at each denoising step to anchor the trajectory, and applying a guidance scale w_α to amplify the conditional signal. An inverse dynamics model f̂(s, s') converts the generated state sequence into actions, and learned reward/cost models label the transitions, yielding a new dataset D_g whose distribution is shaped toward the optimal policy's stationary distribution. The theoretical contribution (Theorems 1–2) bounds the total-variation distance between the generated and optimal state-action distributions and, consequently, the constraint violation of the policy trained on D_g, providing a safety guarantee that depends on score estimation error, inverse dynamics error, and regularization gap.

## Results

On the OSRL benchmark (tempting dataset, κ=20), OASIS is the only method that achieves safety (normalized cost ≤ 1) across all six Bullet-Safety-Gym tasks while maintaining competitive reward. For example, on Car-Circle OASIS achieves normalized reward 0.76 with cost 0.89, versus BCQ-Lag's reward 0.79 but cost 3.25 (unsafe), and FISOR's cost 0.15 but reward only 0.24. On Ball-Circle, OASIS reaches reward 0.70 with cost 0.45, outperforming all other safe agents. The method also demonstrates high data efficiency, learning a good policy with less than 2% of the original dataset size after distribution shaping.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OASIS is the only method that simultaneously satisfies the safety constraint (normalized cost ≤ 1) and achieves competitive reward across all six tasks. Against the Q-learning baselines (BCQ-Lag, BEAR-Lag, CPQ), OASIS wins decisively on safety: BCQ-Lag and BEAR-Lag violate constraints in every task (costs ranging from 1.96 to 8.44), while CPQ is safe only on Car-Circle (cost 0.12) but suffers severe reward degradation (0.01 on Drone-Circle). Against the sequential modeling baselines, CDT is safe only on Car-Run (cost 0.67) and FISOR is safe on five of six tasks but is severely over-conservative, achieving reward as low as 0.17–0.28 versus OASIS's 0.28–0.85. The data-centric CVAE-BCQL baseline fails to incorporate conditions effectively, remaining unsafe on four of six tasks. Overall, OASIS achieves the highest reward among all safe agents in 5 out of 6 tasks, with the sole exception being Car-Run where FISOR matches its cost (0.15 vs. 0.02) but OASIS still achieves comparable reward (0.85 vs. 0.85).

## Relation to Prior Work

OASIS departs from the dominant model-centric paradigm in offline safe RL (BCQ-Lag, BEAR-Lag, CPQ, COptiDICE, CDT, FISOR) by introducing a data-centric distribution shaping step before policy training. It repurposes the conditional diffusion framework from Decision Diffuser [80] and AdaptDiffuser [82]—originally designed for trajectory planning or data augmentation in standard offline RL—as a dataset curation tool conditioned on safety preferences (cost ≤ κ, reward targets). The paper positions this as the first successful application of distribution shaping specifically to the offline safe RL setting, identifying the Safe Dataset Mismatch (SDM) problem as a distinct failure mode that regularization-based model-centric methods cannot resolve when datasets are biased toward tempting or conservative behavior.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The SDM problem is a practical bottleneck: real-world offline safe RL datasets are rarely balanced, and regularization toward imperfect demonstrations either causes safety violations (tempting data) or reward degradation (conservative data). OASIS's data-centric framing is complementary to model-centric improvements and demonstrates that dataset quality can be a more effective lever than algorithmic sophistication in constrained settings. Key limitations include the additional offline training cost (~4 hours for the diffusion generator), the reliance on learned reward/cost models and inverse dynamics for labeling, and the fact that zero constraint violations remain unattainable with imperfect demonstrations. Open questions include scalability to high-dimensional continuous control, multi-constraint settings, and whether the approach transfers to domains where the diffusion model's coverage assumptions break down.
