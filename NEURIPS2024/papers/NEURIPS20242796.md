## Problem

All existing differentially private reinforcement learning algorithms are restricted to the single-agent (MDP) setting, leaving the multi-agent case entirely unexplored. This paper asks whether a provably efficient self-play algorithm can solve two-player zero-sum Markov Games while satisfying trajectory-wise privacy guarantees (Joint DP or Local DP), and whether near-optimal regret bounds are achievable under these constraints.

## Input / Output

The algorithm takes a tabular two-player zero-sum episodic Markov Game with state space S, action spaces A and B, horizon H, deterministic known rewards in [0,1], a privacy budget ε, number of episodes K, and a choice of Privatizer (Central for JDP or Local for LDP). It outputs a pair of marginal Markov policies (μ^out, ν^out) — one for each player — that are single, deployable policies (not correlated), along with high-probability regret bounds and PAC guarantees. The output policy is selected as the episode policy with the smallest UCB–LCB gap at the initial state.

## Method

DP-Nash-VI combines optimistic Nash value iteration with privatization of Bernstein-type bonuses. At each episode, it constructs private transition kernel estimates from privatized visitation counts (via the K-bounded Binary Mechanism for JDP or per-step Laplace noise for LDP), then builds UCB and LCB Q-functions using a private variance-based bonus plus an additional privacy-error term scaled by E_{ε,β}. Policies are computed via Coarse Correlated Equilibrium (a polynomial-time LP relaxation of Nash equilibrium) applied to the UCB/LCB Q-functions, and a post-processing step (solved as a small LP) ensures private counts remain non-negative and sum-consistent so the private transition kernel is a valid distribution. The regret analysis proceeds by backward induction to prove UCB/LCB validity, then telescopes the value gap over episodes, bounding the private Bernstein term via Cauchy-Schwarz and the privacy term via pigeonhole, yielding Regret(K) = Õ(√(H²SABT) + H²S²AB·E_{ε,β}) which recovers the best known non-private multi-agent bound when ε→∞ and strictly generalizes the best single-agent DP results when B=1.

## Results

Under ε-JDP, DP-Nash-VI achieves a regret of Õ(√(H²SABT) + H³S²AB/ε), where the main exploration term is nearly optimal (matching the lower bound Õ(√(H²S(A+B)T) + HS(A+B)/ε) up to a factor of √(AB/(A+B))) and the privacy-dependent term has optimal 1/ε dependence. Under ε-LDP, the regret is Õ(√(H²SABT) + S²AB√(H⁵T)/ε), with optimal dependence on ε and K per the lower bound Õ(√(H²S(A+B)T) + √(HS(A+B)T)/ε). In the no-privacy limit (ε→∞), the regret reduces to Õ(√(H²SABT)), matching the best known non-private multi-agent RL bound. When restricted to the single-agent MDP case (B=1), all bounds exactly match the state-of-the-art results of Qiao and Wang (2023).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DP-Nash-VI is the first algorithm to provide regret guarantees for multi-agent (two-player zero-sum) RL under differential privacy, so it does not directly compete with any existing multi-agent DP method. Against the non-private Nash VI [Liu et al., 2021], DP-Nash-VI recovers the identical Õ(√(H²SABT)) regret when the privacy budget is infinite, confirming no overhead in the unconstrained setting. Against the single-agent DP baselines (PUCB, LDP-OBI, Private-UCB-VI, DP-UCBVI), DP-Nash-VI strictly generalizes all of them: setting B=1 yields exactly the same regret bounds as DP-UCBVI [Qiao and Wang, 2023], which is the best known for single-agent MDPs, while the earlier methods (PUCB, LDP-OBI, Private-UCB-VI) have worse dependence on H (H³ vs. H² in the main term). Compared to the theoretical lower bounds, the main exploration term is nearly optimal (gap of √(AB/(A+B)) in the state-action factor), and the privacy-dependent term achieves optimal 1/ε scaling for JDP and optimal √(T)/ε scaling for LDP, though the dependence on H, S, A, B in the privacy term remains sub-optimal and is noted as an open problem.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate research threads: differentially private single-agent RL (Vietri et al. 2020, Garcelon et al. 2021, Chowdhury and Zhou 2022, Qiao and Wang 2023) and non-private multi-agent RL via optimistic Nash value iteration (Bai and Jin 2020, Liu et al. 2021). The key technical departure is extending the Bernstein-type bonus privatization from Qiao and Wang [2023] to the two-player setting, where the variance term must be handled under the joint action space of both players. The algorithm also leverages Coarse Correlated Equilibrium (CCE) as a computationally tractable relaxation of Nash equilibrium, following Liu et al. [2021], and uses the Binary Mechanism (Chan et al. 2011) for JDP and Laplace noise for LDP as privatization backends.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to provide trajectory-wise privacy guarantees in multi-agent RL, filling a gap where all prior DP-RL results were restricted to single-agent MDPs. The regret bounds strictly generalize the best known single-agent DP results and reduce to the best known non-private multi-agent bounds when privacy is not required, establishing a clean theoretical foundation. Open questions include achieving optimal dependence on H, S, A, B in the DP cost term (currently sub-optimal and inherent to model-based approaches), extending to model-free algorithms like private Q-learning, and handling function approximation in Markov Games.
