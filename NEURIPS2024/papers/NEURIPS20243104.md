## Problem

In semi-supervised multi-label learning, the prevalent self-training paradigm with binary losses and negative sampling suffers from a variance bias problem: the variance difference between feature distributions of positive and negative samples for each label is significantly larger than in fully supervised settings, because pseudo-labels are difficult to guarantee accurate. This causes trained binary boundaries to deviate from the Bayesian optimal ones, leading to unfair class-wise accuracy and performance degradation.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a small set of labeled samples (5%–20% of training data) and a large set of unlabeled samples, where images are resized to 224×224 and processed by a pre-trained ResNet-50 backbone, while text is encoded by BERT-base-uncased. It produces a K-dimensional multi-label indicator vector per sample, with pseudo-labels for unlabeled data generated via class-aware thresholding that matches the label distribution estimated from labeled samples. The output logits are cosine similarities between latent features and per-class weight vectors, making the angular geometry central to the loss computation.

## Method

The core contribution is the Balanced Binary Angular Margin (BBAM) loss, which extends the standard BAM loss by modeling per-label positive and negative sample angles as draws from label-specific Gaussian distributions and applying linear Gaussian transformations to balance their variances to a common value σ̂²ₖ. These transformations are iteratively re-estimated each epoch from labeled and pseudo-labeled samples using label prototypes (mean of positive latent features) and per-class angle statistics. A prototype-based negative sampling strategy selects the top-M most similar negative samples to each label's prototype to maintain high-quality, informative negatives. Training proceeds in two phases: a warm-up stage optimizing the standard BAM loss on labeled data only, followed by self-training with the BBAM loss over both labeled and pseudo-labeled batches, with per-epoch updates of pseudo-labels, prototypes, angle distributions, and negative sample sets.

## Results

On VOC 2012 at π=5% labeled data, S²ML²-BBAM achieves Micro-F1 of 0.7897, Macro-F1 of 0.7306, and mAP of 0.7866, substantially outperforming the strongest SSMLL baseline CAP (0.6162 / 0.5822 / 0.7616). On the text dataset Ohsumed at π=5%, the method reaches Micro-F1 of 0.6671 and Macro-F1 of 0.6058, improving over CAP by +0.11 and +0.13 respectively. Across all five datasets and all labeled proportions (5%–20%), S²ML²-BBAM ranks first on average in Micro-F1, Macro-F1, and Hamming Loss, with the largest gains observed in the low-label regime (π=5%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the closest SSMLL competitor CAP [16], S²ML²-BBAM wins on Micro-F1, Macro-F1, and mAP across all five datasets; the largest gap is on VOC where Micro-F1 jumps from 0.6162 to 0.7897 (+17.4 pp) and Macro-F1 from 0.5822 to 0.7306 (+14.8 pp) at π=5%. Against the other SSMLL method DRML [15], the proposed method also dominates on every metric, e.g. on Ohsumed mAP improves from 0.1833 to 0.5537. Compared to the SSL baselines SoftMatch [32] and FlatMatch [33], S²ML²-BBAM achieves higher Micro-F1 and Macro-F1 on all datasets (e.g. VOC Micro-F1: 0.7897 vs. 0.6542 for SoftMatch), though SoftMatch occasionally edges out on mAP for VOC at higher π (0.7867 vs. 0.8458 at π=20%). Against the SPMLL method MIME [34], the advantage is overwhelming—MIME's Micro-F1 on VOC at π=5% is only 0.3650 versus 0.7897 for S²ML²-BBAM—because MIME cannot exploit the large unlabeled pool effectively. The one noted weakness is that on VOC and COCO, mAP slightly decreases as the labeled proportion increases beyond 15%, a limitation the authors acknowledge.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on CAP (NeurIPS 2023) for class-aware pseudo-label generation and extends the Binary Angular Margin (BAM) loss by introducing Gaussian linear transformations on label-specific angular distributions to balance variance between positive and negative samples. It departs from prior SSMLL methods (DRML, CAP) that focus on label correlation or pseudo-label quality, instead identifying and addressing the variance bias problem in feature angle distributions as a distinct source of performance degradation. The theoretical analysis draws on fairness literature (variance of class-wise accuracy) to formalize why unbalanced variances lead to suboptimal decision boundaries, connecting semi-supervised multi-label learning to a broader fairness-in-classification perspective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a concrete and previously unaddressed failure mode in semi-supervised multi-label learning—variance bias between positive and negative feature distributions—and provides both a theoretical characterization (via Theorems 2.1 and 2.3) and a practical fix (BBAM loss with prototype-based negative sampling). The method achieves substantial improvements over the strongest SSMLL baseline (CAP) across both image and text datasets, suggesting the variance bias problem is broadly impactful. However, the authors note a limitation where mAP slightly degrades at higher labeled-data proportions on VOC and COCO, and the Gaussian distribution assumption on angular features may not hold in all practical settings, leaving open whether the approach generalizes to extreme multi-label or highly imbalanced scenarios.
