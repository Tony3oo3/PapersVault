## Problem

Existing diffusion-based image restoration methods are task-specific: each requires training a large conditional backbone (~10–100M params) from scratch for a single degradation type, and inference demands hundreds of neural function evaluations. This makes DPMs impractical for practitioners who need to switch between restoration tasks or lack the compute and data for full retraining.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243120/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework handles three degradation types with task-specific IR backbones: a burst of 14 noisy raw LR frames (handshake motion) for JDD-SR, a single blurred 720p frame for dynamic deblurring, and a bicubic-downsampled LR image for 4× SISR. In all cases the output is a full-resolution sRGB image. Notably, the same denoising module trained on sRGB Gaussian noise is reused across all three tasks, including the raw-burst case, demonstrating cross-modality generalization.

## Method

DP-IR decomposes the conditional score function ∇log p(x_t|y) into two easier terms via the approximation E[x₀|y,x_t] ≈ f(E[x₀|y], E[x₀|x_t]), enabling a three-module architecture: a frozen pre-trained IR network (BSRT, FFTFormer, or SwinIR) approximating E[x₀|y], a shared denoising network (MIRNet-S) approximating E[x₀|x_t], and a small 0.7M-parameter fusion network (dense blocks + Convex Combination Channel Attention with sinusoidal timestep encoding) that is the only component trained per task. An accelerated sampling strategy exploits the fact that for early reverse steps x_t carries little information about x₀, so the first T−τ steps are collapsed into a single closed-form Gaussian transition (Lemma 3.2), reducing NFEs from T+1 to τ+1 (up to 200× speedup for deblurring, 4× for SISR, further combinable with DDIM for 20× total reduction). The fusion module is trained for 300k iterations with frozen IR and denoising weights, requiring only standard task-specific datasets (ZurichRAW2RGB, GoPro, DIV2K).

## Results

On dynamic scene deblurring (GoPro), DP-IR achieves the best perceptual metrics among all methods (LPIPS 0.053, TOPIQΔ 0.011) with a competitive PSNR of 33.72 dB, while requiring only 6 NFEs compared to 500 for DvSR/icDPM and 10 for InDI. On 4× SISR (DIV2K), the method attains the best fidelity among all perceptual-oriented methods (PSNR 28.12 dB, SSIM 0.793) and the best TOPIQΔ (0.002) across all methods, using 51 NFEs versus 100–2000 for competing diffusion models. On burst JDD-SR, DP-IR achieves the best LPIPS (0.084) and TOPIQΔ (0.02) while maintaining a competitive PSNR of 35.53 dB, outperforming the underlying BSRT-Small IR module on all perceptual metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against diffusion-based deblurring methods, DP-IR dominates on both fidelity and perceptual quality: it outperforms DvSR by +2.06 dB PSNR and 0.006 LPIPS on GoPro while using 6 NFEs versus 500, and beats InDI by +2.23 dB PSNR and 0.005 LPIPS with 6 NFEs versus 10. Against the strongest reconstruction baseline FFTFormer, DP-IR trades 0.49 dB PSNR for a substantial 0.018 LPIPS improvement and a 0.001 TOPIQΔ gain, indicating a better perception–distortion trade-off. On 4× SISR, DP-IR surpasses all diffusion-based competitors (SRDiff, LDM, InDI, IDM) in PSNR by 0.77–4.82 dB and in TOPIQΔ by 0.006–0.017, while remaining within 0.011 LPIPS of the best (SRDiff at 0.129 vs. 0.140) and using 51 NFEs versus 100–2000. For burst JDD-SR, DP-IR improves LPIPS from 0.109 to 0.084 and TOPIQΔ from 0.12 to 0.02 over the underlying BSRT-Small module at a cost of only 0.38 dB PSNR, and it outperforms BSRT-Large on both perceptual metrics (LPIPS 0.084 vs. 0.095, TOPIQΔ 0.02 vs. 0.16) despite a 1.45 dB PSNR gap. Overall, the method consistently wins on perceptual metrics across all three tasks while maintaining competitive fidelity, and it achieves a 4–200× reduction in NFEs relative to competing diffusion approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the DDPM framework (Ho et al., 2020) and the conditional score-matching formulation for inverse problems, but departs fundamentally from prior conditional diffusion IR methods (SRDiff, DvSR, InDI, icDPM) that each train a single large task-specific network. Instead, it decomposes the conditional expectation E[x₀|y, xₜ] into two independently learnable components — a pre-trained IR network approximating E[x₀|y] and a shared denoising network approximating E[x₀|xₜ] — combined by a tiny (0.7M) fusion module. The accelerated sampling strategy, which skips the first T−τ reverse steps by exploiting the information-free regime of x_T, is a novel contribution that generalizes the 'start from a noisy observation' idea of SDEdit and Come-Closer-Diffuse-Faster into a closed-form multi-step transition.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: practitioners can adopt DPM-based perceptual quality improvements for any IR task by training only a 0.7M-parameter fusion module on a small dataset, rather than retraining a 10–100M-parameter conditional diffusion model from scratch. The 4×–200× NFE reduction makes DPM inference feasible on modest hardware. Key open questions remain: the optimal τ selection is task-dependent and lacks a principled per-sample criterion, the fusion architecture is explicitly a proof-of-concept, and the framework is inapplicable to novel IR tasks lacking a pre-trained IR network. The theoretical justification (Proposition C.1) relies on strong simplifications (linear observation model, identity denoiser), leaving the general case empirically validated but not formally guaranteed.
