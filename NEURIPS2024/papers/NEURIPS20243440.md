## Problem

Vision-based 3D mapping for autonomous driving is hindered by dynamic objects that break multi-view consistency, and existing solutions depend on human-annotated segmentation models and expensive LiDAR sensors. The paper asks whether a self-supervised, camera-only system can identify and memorize only the permanent environmental structures across repeated traversals of the same region, without any human supervision or LiDAR input.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243440/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes unposed RGB images from multiple traversals (10–16 passes per location) of the same driving region, along with camera poses recovered via COLMAP/SfM and precomputed denoised DINOv2 feature maps (compressed from 768 to 64 dimensions via PCA, at 110×180 spatial resolution). It produces two outputs simultaneously: a 3D Gaussian environment map where each Gaussian carries position, rotation, scale, opacity, spherical-harmonics color, and a 64-d semantic feature vector, and per-image 2D ephemerality masks that segment transient objects (vehicles, pedestrians, parked cars, traffic cones) without any training labels. The entire pipeline runs on a single RTX 3090 GPU.

## Method

3DGM formulates multitraverse environmental mapping as a robust 3D representation learning problem in which environment pixels are inliers and transient-object pixels are outliers, grounded in 3D Gaussian Splatting. The pipeline has three stages: (1) COLMAP initialization exploiting the broader viewpoint coverage of multiple traversals for more robust SfM; (2) EmerSeg, which distills denoised DINOv2 features into the Gaussians and then mines the spatial structure of the feature residual map—normalizing residuals, thresholding, extracting contours, filtering small and sky regions, merging nearby contours, and computing convex hulls—to produce ephemerality masks without additional training; (3) EnvGS, which finetunes the Gaussians under a robust L1 rendering loss weighted by the ephemerality masks, optionally augmented with inverse-depth smoothness and sky opacity losses. The self-supervision signal is the consensus across traversals: because the environment is stable while objects change, inlier pixels dominate the gradient and steer the Gaussians toward permanent features, while high feature-space loss naturally flags outlier pixels for masking.

## Results

On Mapverse-Ithaca365, EmerSeg achieves a mean IoU of ~45% for unsupervised 2D ephemerality segmentation, substantially outperforming five supervised segmentation models (PSPNet, SegViT, InternImage, Mask2Former, SegFormer) which score only 19–24% IoU. For neural rendering, EnvGS achieves LPIPS 0.213, SSIM 0.806, and PSNR 22.78, matching the supervised 3DGS+SegFormer baseline (LPIPS 0.212, SSIM 0.806) while requiring no human annotations. For 3D reconstruction, EnvGS achieves an average Chamfer Distance of ~0.9 m, roughly halving the ~1.9 m error of the zero-shot DepthAnything baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EmerSeg outperforms all five supervised segmentation baselines (PSPNet, SegViT, InternImage, Mask2Former, SegFormer) by roughly 20+ IoU points on average, demonstrating that the multitraverse consensus signal provides a stronger segmentation cue than dense pixel-level annotations. Against unsupervised baselines STEGO and CAUSE, EmerSeg achieves a 21.36-point IoU improvement over STEGO (using SegFormer pseudo-GT), and critically, it understands ephemerality (transient vs. permanent) rather than merely clustering pixels. For neural rendering (Table 3), EnvGS (LPIPS 0.213, SSIM 0.806, PSNR 22.78) is essentially tied with 3DGS+SegFormer (0.212, 0.806, 22.81) while being fully self-supervised, and clearly outperforms vanilla 3DGS (0.227, 0.798, 22.92) and all NeRF-based methods (LPIPS ≥ 0.416). The RobustNeRF loss, when added to 3DGS, does not improve rendering quality in driving scenes (LPIPS 0.416 vs. 0.227 for plain 3DGS), indicating that robust loss alone is insufficient without explicit mask-based outlier removal. For 3D geometry, EnvGS halves the Chamfer Distance of DepthAnything (0.9 m vs. 1.9 m) without requiring any LiDAR during training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends 3D Gaussian Splatting by augmenting each Gaussian with self-supervised DINOv2 feature attributes and reframing multitraverse environmental mapping as a robust representation learning problem, where environment pixels are inliers and transient objects are outliers. It departs from prior multitraverse driving work (Hindsight, persistence-prior methods, SUDS) that all rely on LiDAR for cross-traversal consistency, and from single-traversal decomposition methods (EmerNeRF, D²NeRF) that require LiDAR or operate on one video. The feature residual mining strategy for extracting segmentation masks from the rendering loss map is a novel technical contribution not present in NeRF-W, RobustNeRF, or Block-NeRF, which use uncertainty estimation or adaptive weighting rather than spatially structured residual analysis.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

3DGM provides a practical, LiDAR-free, annotation-free pipeline for building 3D environment maps from repeated driving passes, with 2D ephemerality segmentation as a free byproduct—directly addressing cost and portability barriers in autonomous driving perception. The Mapverse benchmark (40 locations, 467 videos, 35K images) is the first dedicated multitraverse driving dataset, filling a gap where prior NeRF/3DGS driving benchmarks focused on single traversals. Open questions include scalability to large environmental changes (night, seasons, adversarial weather), handling of small/distant objects limited by feature resolution, and whether the approach extends to online or incremental mapping settings. The method's ability to also mask non-environmental elements like shadows suggests broader utility for change detection and autolabeling.
