## Problem

Existing video semantic segmentation UDA methods depend on pretrained optical flows to warp pseudo-labels across frames, but these flows become unreliable under adverse weather (nighttime, fog, rain) due to low-level degradations like noise, glare, and occlusions. This paper introduces the first end-to-end, optical-flow-free video domain adaptation strategy specifically designed for adverse weather conditions.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a current video frame and its immediately preceding adjacent frame, both processed through a DeepLabV2 encoder with an Accel backbone. For the student model, the current frame is randomly cropped to a 25% rectangular segment, forcing reliance on the adjacent frame for missing information. Both frames undergo temporal weather degradation augmentation (correlated noise, moving glare, fog patches, illumination shifts) applied to corresponding spatial regions with gradual intensity variation. The output is a per-pixel semantic segmentation mask over the common class set between source and target datasets.

## Method

The core mechanism is a fusion block composed of alternating deformable-convolution offset layers and fusion convolutions that learn to match and merge feature-level pixels from the adjacent frame into the current frame's prediction, entirely replacing pretrained optical flow. Training uses a dual-teacher-student scheme: a temporal teacher (full frames + fusion block) produces pseudo-labels that the student (cropped current frame + full adjacent frame + fusion block) must match, compelling it to extract temporal cues; a spatial teacher (high-resolution cropped segment, no fusion block) provides spatial pseudo-labels that align the student's pre-fusion features, preserving spatial accuracy. A temporal weather degradation augmentation applies correlated, intensity-varying weather effects to corresponding regions across consecutive frames, training the model to exploit inter-frame degradation differences to recover the true scene. The entire pipeline is trained end-to-end with a combined supervised loss (source) and temporal/spatial consistency losses (target), with the fusion block shaped specifically by segmentation objectives rather than generic motion estimation.

## Results

On the primary adverse-weather benchmark (VIPER → MVSS), the proposed optical-flow-free method achieves 25.4% mIoU, surpassing the best video-based baseline MoDA (20.8%) by 4.6 mIoU and the best image-based baseline RDA (21.1%) by 4.3 mIoU. On the second adaptation setting (Synthia → MVSS), it reaches 33.0% mIoU, outperforming the best baseline TPS (27.8%) by 5.2 mIoU. On ideal-condition Cityscapes-Seq, the method achieves 51.2% (VIPER→) and 51.0% (Synthia→) mIoU, remaining competitive with optical-flow-based methods like SFC (51.7% and 55.3%) despite not using any pretrained optical flow. Inference time is 0.11 s per frame, faster than FlowNet2+TPS (0.17 s) and FlowNet2+DA-VSN (0.35 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against 8 baselines spanning image-based UDA (AdvEnt, FDA, RDA) and video-based UDA (DA-VSN, SFC, TPS, MoDA, VAT-VST). On the adverse-weather MVSS benchmark, the proposed method decisively outperforms all baselines: it beats the strongest video method MoDA by 4.6 mIoU (VIPER→MVSS) and TPS by 5.2 mIoU (Synthia→MVSS), while also surpassing the best image-based method RDA by 4.3 and 5.5 mIoU respectively. Notably, all video-based methods that rely on pretrained optical flow (DA-VSN, SFC, TPS) underperform the image-based RDA on MVSS, confirming the paper's argument that optical flow degrades under adverse weather. On ideal-condition Cityscapes-Seq, the method is competitive but not dominant: it trails SFC by 0.5 mIoU (VIPER→) and 4.3 mIoU (Synthia→), while beating TPS and DA-VSN. The ablation (Table 3) shows that removing the fusion block from the Accel baseline drops mIoU from 21.7 to 18.6, confirming the fusion block's role as a viable optical-flow replacement.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in video semantic segmentation UDA (DA-VSN, VAT-VST, TPS, SFC), which all rely on pretrained optical flow models (e.g., FlowNet2) to warp pseudo-labels or features across frames. Instead, it introduces a deformable-convolution-based fusion block that learns to match and merge feature-level information from adjacent frames end-to-end, guided by segmentation losses. The spatial teacher component directly builds on HRDA's high-resolution pseudo-labeling strategy [13], while the overall teacher-student framework follows the standard EMA-based paradigm. The temporal weather degradation augmentation extends prior weather-specific augmentation ideas by enforcing correlated, intensity-varying degradations across consecutive frames rather than treating each frame independently.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical motivation is strong: adverse weather (nighttime, fog, rain) is a known failure mode for autonomous driving perception, and the paper demonstrates that the optical flow dependency in existing video UDA methods is not just a convenience but an active source of error under these conditions. The fusion block's lower inference cost (0.11s vs 0.17–0.35s) makes it attractive for real-time deployment. However, the evaluation is limited to a single adverse-weather target dataset (MVSS) and a single backbone (DeepLabV2), and the method's generalization to other domain gaps or weather types beyond those in MVSS remains untested. Open questions include whether the fusion block scales to higher-resolution or longer-sequence settings, and whether the approach transfers to other video tasks (e.g., tracking, depth estimation) under adverse conditions.
