## Problem

Creating animatable 3D avatars for interacting hands from a single image remains challenging because limited input views provide insufficient geometry and texture information, while intra- and inter-hand interactions introduce complex deformations and occlusions. Existing GS-based methods designed for single subjects fail under these conditions, and prior one-shot NeRF methods either require expensive per-scene optimization or lack dedicated modules to detect and handle hand interactions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240452/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single 256×256 RGB crop of two interacting hands as input, along with MANO pose parameters (θ∈R⁴⁸), shape parameters (β∈R¹⁰), and camera parameters (c∈R²⁵) obtained from off-the-shelf regressors. It produces an animatable 3D Gaussian Splatting avatar (up to 49,281 Gaussians per hand) that can render novel views and novel poses in real time. The one-shot fitting stage requires only 50 optimization steps (~2.5 min on an A6000 GPU), and the resulting avatar supports downstream applications including texture editing, duplication, re-scaling, and text-to-avatar generation via ControlNet.

## Method

The core contribution is a two-stage interaction-aware GS framework that disentangles 3D hand representation into learning-based features (pose embeddings via MLP, geometric features via PointNet + transformer decoder, and neural texture maps) and optimization-based spatial identity maps (UV-plane feature maps). In the cross-subject pre-training stage, the network learns hand priors jointly with per-subject identity maps using L1 + VGG perceptual losses. Two key modules enhance rendering: an interaction-aware attention module that detects interacting Gaussians by comparing nearest-neighbor sets on canonical vs. posed MANO meshes and applies self-attention only on flagged points, and a self-adaptive Gaussian refinement module that predicts per-Gaussian validity (removing or splitting points) and positional offsets to adapt density to complex regions. In the one-shot fitting stage, only a zero-initialized identity map and a shared texture bias Δt are optimized (50 steps), preserving spatial texture structure and avoiding the cost of network fine-tuning used by prior inversion-based methods.

## Results

On the InterHand2.6M test set, the proposed method achieves PSNR 26.14 / SSIM 0.869 / LPIPS 0.161 for novel view synthesis and PSNR 26.56 / SSIM 0.890 / LPIPS 0.133 for novel pose synthesis, outperforming all four baselines on every metric in both tasks. The one-shot fitting stage requires only 50 optimization steps (~2.5 min on an A6000 GPU), and the full pre-training uses 21 subjects over 8 epochs on three A6000 GPUs. Statistical significance is confirmed with mean PSNR 26.60 ± 0.02 over five random identity-map initializations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all four baselines on every reported metric for both novel view and novel pose synthesis. Against the closest competitor OHTA* (adapted with the paper's pre-trained model), the gains are modest but consistent: +0.83 PSNR on novel view (26.14 vs 25.31) and +0.63 PSNR on novel pose (26.56 vs 25.93), with LPIPS improving from 0.184→0.161 and 0.156→0.133 respectively. The gap widens substantially against NeRF-based baselines: VANeRF trails by 0.76 PSNR on novel view but by 2.14 PSNR on novel pose (26.56 vs 24.42), reflecting its weaker pose generalization. SMPLpix, an image-space method, lags by 1.64 PSNR on novel view and 2.30 PSNR on novel pose, while KeypointNeRF (which does not support novel pose synthesis) is 2.59 PSNR behind on novel view. The authors attribute the advantage over OHTA* to the spatial identity map and shared texture-map bias, which capture fine-grained per-identity texture more faithfully than OHTA's vector-based identity code inversion.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) rendering paradigm and the one-shot hand avatar line of work initiated by OHTA (vector-based identity inversion) and extended by VANeRF (visibility-aware NeRF for interacting hands). It departs from OHTA by replacing the identity code (a flat vector that omits spatial texture structure) with a spatially-structured identity map, and from NeRF-based methods by adopting explicit Gaussian point representations for real-time rendering. The two-stage design—cross-subject pre-training of geometric/texture encoders followed by per-subject identity-map optimization—sits between fully learned generalizable NeRFs and per-scene optimization, while the interaction-aware attention and self-adaptive Gaussian refinement modules are novel architectural contributions specific to the interacting-hand setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical pipeline for creating animatable, high-fidelity interacting hand avatars from a single image in ~2.5 minutes, which is directly applicable to sign-language production, virtual try-on, and content creation. The disentanglement into learning-based priors (generalizable across subjects) and optimization-based identity maps (capturing per-subject OOD textures) is a design pattern that could transfer to other one-shot avatar tasks. Key limitations remain: the per-identity optimization step prevents true single-forward-pass inference, the method is sensitive to upstream pose-estimation errors, and the interaction detection relies on a hand-crafted neighbor-set overlap heuristic that may not generalize to highly non-rigid deformations beyond finger-level contact.
