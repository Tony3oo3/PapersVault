## Problem

Existing video datasets pair videos with brief captions that limit detailed video understanding and controllability of text-to-video generation. Naively feeding multiple frames to GPT4V produces temporally confused and detail-poor captions, and no scalable strategy exists for captioning arbitrary-length videos with precise temporal descriptions. The paper addresses this by designing a captioning pipeline that is simultaneously stable, scalable, and detail-preserving.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240614/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The DiffSW captioning pipeline consumes pairs of adjacent keyframes (sampled at 2-second intervals, further reduced by semantic-aware extraction) along with the previous frame's differential caption, producing per-frame differential descriptions that are summarized into 200–400 word video captions with explicit timestamps. For the LVLM (ShareGPT4Video-8B), 16 uniformly sampled frames are arranged into a 4×4 image grid as input alongside a text question, yielding a text answer. For the T2VM, a dense text prompt drives generation of 10-second videos at 512×512 resolution via a DiT-based architecture.

## Method

The core contribution is the Differential Sliding-Window Captioning (DiffSW) strategy, which decomposes the all-frames-to-caption task into a sequence of pairwise differential description tasks: GPT4V receives the previous keyframe, its differential caption, and the current keyframe, and is prompted (via a hierarchical prompt with Character/Skills/Constraints/Structured-Input sections) to describe only the inter-frame changes in camera movement, object motion, and scene transitions. The constant two-frame input keeps GPT4V's attention focused and prevents the detail loss and temporal confusion observed with multi-frame or frame-concatenation inputs. All differential captions with timestamps are then passed to GPT-4-Turbo with a Summary Prompt to produce a coherent, temporally precise video caption. A semantic-aware keyframe extraction step (using CLIP-Large CLS-token similarity) removes temporally redundant frames before captioning, and a semantic-based data filtering step (using BERT CLS-token similarity of short captions) ensures content diversity across the 40K-video dataset. The resulting ShareGPT4Video dataset is used to fine-tune IXC2-4KHD into ShareCaptioner-Video (supporting fast, sliding, summarizing, and re-captioning modes) and to train ShareGPT4Video-8B from LLaVA-Next-8B with LoRA on the LLM, achieving SOTA on TempCompass, VideoBench, and MVBench.

## Results

ShareGPT4Video-8B achieves an average accuracy of 61.5% on TempCompass, surpassing the previous best (VideoLLaVA-7B at 49.9%) by 11.6 points. On VideoBench, it reaches 41.2% average, outperforming the prior SOTA (VideoChatGPT-7B at 38.5%) by 2.7 points. On MVBench, it achieves 51.2% average, an 8.2-point improvement over the previous state-of-the-art. The model also demonstrates that integrating 28K high-quality captions into existing LVLMs (VideoLLaVA-7B, LLaMA-VID-7B/13B) yields consistent gains of 1.1–2.3 points across all three benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ShareGPT4Video-8B outperforms all 10 video understanding baselines across every benchmark. The largest margin is on TempCompass, where it beats VideoLLaVA-7B (49.9→61.5, +11.6) and PandaGPT-13B (40.4→61.5, +21.1), with the gap driven primarily by Multi-Choice QA (59.4 vs. 44.4 for VideoLLaVA-7B) and Caption Generation (50.6 vs. 33.5). On VideoBench, it surpasses VideoChatGPT-7B (38.5→41.2, +2.7) and mPLUG-Owl-7B (33.2→41.2, +8.0), with the strongest per-dimension gains on ANet (50.8 vs. 46.6) and SQA3D (42.9 vs. 37.2). On MVBench, it leads by 8.2 points over the prior best. In the captioning comparison (Table 7), ShareCaptioner-Video is essentially on par with GPT4V (36.5% vs. 37.8% human preference, 23.7% rated comparable), confirming that the fine-tuned model matches the proprietary API's quality at a fraction of the cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the ShareGPT4V image-captioning pipeline to the video domain, but identifies that naive multi-frame or frame-concatenation inputs to GPT4V produce temporally confused and detail-poor captions. The key methodological departure is the Differential Sliding-Window Captioning (DiffSW) strategy, which decomposes the all-frames-to-caption problem into pairwise differential descriptions, leveraging GPT4V's strength at single-image understanding while preserving temporal order. For the downstream models, ShareCaptioner-Video is built by fine-tuning IXC2-4KHD, and ShareGPT4Video-8B is built on LLaVA-Next-8B with the IG-VLM image-grid strategy, while the T2VM experiments use Latte-XL as the base diffusion architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 40K GPT4V-annotated captions plus 4.8M ShareCaptioner-Video captions (~3000 hours) constitute a substantial open resource for both LVLM training and T2VM fine-tuning, addressing a recognized data bottleneck. The consistent gains across VideoLLaVA and LLaMA-VID architectures (1.1–2.3 average points) and the 11.6% TempCompass improvement demonstrate that caption quality is a first-order factor for temporal understanding, not just a data-volume issue. Key limitations include the absence of audio information, empirically fixed sampling intervals and window lengths, and dependence on GPT4V's image-only interface; the authors note that adaptive hyperparameters and audio integration are natural next steps.
