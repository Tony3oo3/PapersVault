## Problem

Control as Inference (CaI) with KL divergence yields MaxEnt control, but it was unclear what objective CaI solves when the KL divergence is replaced by the more general Rényi divergence, and how the Rényi order parameter affects the resulting policy. This paper identifies the missing equivalence: CaI with Rényi divergence solves a log-probability regularized risk-sensitive control problem, and derives practical risk-sensitive RL algorithms from this connection.

## Input / Output

The method operates on continuous-state MDPs with state x_t ∈ X ⊆ R^{n_x}, control u_t ∈ U ⊆ R^{n_u}, finite horizon T, and transition density p(x_{t+1}|x_t, u_t). Given a state observation x_t, the policy π_t(u_t|x_t) outputs a stochastic control action sampled from a Gibbs distribution over U. The risk-sensitivity parameter η > -1, η ≠ 0 (corresponding to Rényi order 1+η) is a scalar hyperparameter that determines whether the policy is risk-averse (η > 0) or risk-seeking (η < 0), and the value function V_t and Q-function Q_t are computed via a soft Bellman recursion.

## Method

The key insight is that minimizing the Rényi (1+η)-divergence between the variational trajectory distribution and the CaI optimal posterior is equivalent to minimizing an exponential-utility risk-sensitive cost with log-probability regularization of the policy (Theorem 2). The optimal policy takes the Gibbs form π*_t(u_t|x_t) = exp(−Q_t(x_t,u_t) + V_t(x_t)) where V_t satisfies a soft Bellman equation involving a softmin over U (Theorem 3). For RL, the paper derives a risk-sensitive policy gradient estimator (Proposition 7) and a risk-sensitive soft actor-critic (RSAC) that replaces the standard SAC Bellman residuals with the transformation T_η(v) = (e^{ηv}−1)/η, requiring only minor modifications to SAC. A second variant using Rényi entropy regularization (instead of log-probability) is shown via a duality lemma between exponential integrals and Rényi entropy to yield the same optimal policy structure, differing only in the soft Bellman coefficient.

## Results

The experiment evaluates RSAC on Pendulum-v1 (OpenAI Gymnasium) by training policies with various risk-sensitivity parameters η and testing robustness under system perturbations (pendulum length changed from 1.0 to 1.25 and 1.5). The key finding is that larger η (more risk-averse) policies exhibit smaller performance degradation under environmental changes, while only η=0.02 maintains a stable cost distribution across all perturbation levels. Standard SAC (η=0) develops a secondary high-cost peak in its empirical cost distribution at l=1.5, indicating a high probability of incurring large costs. The paper notes that |η| > 0.03 causes numerical instability and learning failure, limiting the practical range of risk sensitivity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares RSAC against standard SAC (η=0) on Pendulum-v1 under system perturbations. RSAC with larger η consistently outperforms SAC in robustness: the average episode cost degrades less under increased pendulum length (Fig. 3), and the empirical cost distribution remains unimodal and stable (Fig. 4). In contrast, SAC (η=0) shows significant performance degradation at l=1.5, with a new high-cost peak appearing in the distribution, indicating a substantial probability of failure. The comparison is qualitative (no tabulated numerical scores are reported), but the visual evidence clearly favors risk-averse RSAC over SAC for robustness. The trade-off is that more risk-seeking policies (smaller or negative η) become progressively less robust, confirming the expected risk-sensitivity–robustness relationship.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Control as Inference (CaI) by replacing the KL divergence with Rényi divergence in the variational inference formulation, revealing that the resulting problem is equivalent to log-probability regularized risk-sensitive control with exponential utility. It builds on the MaxEnt control framework (Levine, Ziebart), Rényi divergence variational inference (Li & Turner), and Whittle's risk-sensitive control theory, while also connecting to Todorov's linearly-solvable control and the soft actor-critic algorithm. The paper departs from prior CaI work by showing that the optimal posterior for stochastic systems (previously uncharacterized) corresponds to the risk-seeking limit, and by providing a second risk-sensitive generalization via Rényi entropy regularization that yields the same optimal policy structure through a fundamentally different derivation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unifying framework clarifies the relationship between several previously disconnected areas (CaI, MaxEnt control, risk-sensitive control, linearly-solvable control), providing theoretical grounding for why MaxEnt control is robust and how risk sensitivity can be naturally incorporated. Practically, the derived risk-sensitive soft actor-critic (RSAC) requires only minor modifications to standard SAC, making it accessible to practitioners. Key limitations include numerical instability for large |η| (failing beyond |η| > 0.03 in experiments), the single-environment validation (Pendulum-v1 only), and the lack of theoretical robustness guarantees for the regularized case. Open questions include how to choose the risk-sensitivity parameter, compatibility with function approximators, and extending the analysis to infinite-horizon settings.
