## Problem

State-of-the-art cumulative-reward meta-RL methods (RL², VariBAD, HyperX) fail to learn effective exploration when optimal behavior requires forgoing immediate reward in early episodes. Because the agent cannot yet reliably exploit, exploratory actions appear to reduce total reward, training the agent to avoid exploration entirely and locking it into a local optimum. This failure occurs even on trivially solvable domains like multi-arm bandits, where standard RL easily solves each individual environment.

## Input / Output

The agent consumes a sequence of (action, observation, reward) tuples spanning all prior episodes in the meta-rollout as context, plus the current timestep's observation. Observations vary by domain: (x,y) coordinates in Dark Treasure Rooms, 15 lidar distance/orientation readings in Ray Maze, and arm indices in bandits. The shared GPT-2 transformer trunk encodes this context, and two separate linear heads produce discrete action distributions for exploration and exploitation respectively. At inference, actions are selected greedily (argmax) rather than sampled.

## Method

First-Explore decouples exploration and exploitation into two policies trained with a GPT-2-style causal transformer (shared trunk, separate heads). The exploit policy is incentivized to maximize immediate episode return given the exploration context, while the explore policy is incentivized to produce episodes that increase subsequent exploit returns—decoupling exploration from its own immediate reward. Training uses a cross-entropy loss where successor policies learn to emulate 'maximal' exploit episodes (matching or surpassing prior returns) and 'informative' explore episodes (followed by higher exploit returns), with rollout policies updated every T epochs. After training, the two policies are combined at inference by exploring for k episodes then exploiting for n−k episodes, where k is selected by evaluating candidate combinations on held-out environments without retraining.

## Results

On the deceptive Bandits with One Fixed Arm domain, First-Explore achieves 127.7±2.0 mean cumulative reward over 100 pulls, outperforming RL² (56.1±12.2), Thompson Sampling (123.3±2.5), and UCB-1 (116.8±0.5). On the deceptive Dark Treasure Rooms domain, First-Explore achieves 2.0±0.3 cumulative reward over 10 episodes, a 10× improvement over RL² and VariBAD (both 0.2±0.1) and HyperX (−0.2±0.2). On the complex Ray Maze domain, First-Explore achieves 0.47±0.01 mean reward versus 0 for RL² and VariBAD, and 0.07±0.07 for HyperX, approaching the optimal upper bound of 0.64. All comparisons are statistically significant (p < 10⁻⁵, two-tailed Mann-Whitney U tests).

## Baselines & Comparisons

First-Explore decisively outperforms all three cumulative-reward meta-RL baselines (RL², VariBAD, HyperX) across every domain where they are tested. Against RL², the gap is most dramatic in Dark Treasure Rooms (2.0 vs. 0.2, a 10× margin) and Ray Maze (0.47 vs. 0, where RL² learns to never move); in the deceptive bandit setting RL² achieves only 56.1 vs. First-Explore's 127.7. VariBAD and HyperX similarly collapse to near-zero or negative reward in the deceptive settings, with HyperX's slight movement in Ray Maze (0.07) still far below First-Explore. Notably, First-Explore also surpasses the specialized non-meta-RL bandit algorithms Thompson Sampling (123.3) and UCB-1 (116.8) on the deceptive bandit domain, despite being a general-purpose meta-RL method. The paper further shows that when the deceptive property is removed (e.g., μ₁=0 in bandits, ρ=0 in Dark Treasure Rooms), the meta-RL controls recover substantially, confirming that the need to forgo immediate reward is the specific failure mode First-Explore addresses.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the standard cumulative-reward meta-RL formulation (RL², VariBAD, HyperX) that trains a single policy to maximize total reward across an episode sequence. Rather than directly optimizing cumulative reward, First-Explore trains two separate policies—an explore policy and an exploit policy—using a cross-entropy (behavioral-cloning) objective, then combines them at inference with a fixed exploration-to-exploitation switch. The GPT-2 transformer architecture and the interleaved rollout structure draw on sequence-modeling meta-RL, while the conceptual framing (exploration must be decoupled from immediate reward to avoid a training-time local optimum) is the paper's core departure from prior work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of a failure mode where SOTA cumulative-reward meta-RL converges to never exploring—even on bandit problems solvable by standard RL—challenges a core assumption in the field and motivates rethinking how exploration is incentivized during meta-training. Key open questions include whether the myopic explore policy (trained only to improve the next exploit episode) can be extended to plan multi-step exploration sequences, whether the fixed k switch can be replaced by a learned classifier, and whether the approach scales to the long-context, high-compute settings needed to replace standard RL. Safety concerns also arise: the explore policy does not avoid negative rewards, which could be problematic in physical deployments.
