## Problem

Existing multi-modal image fusion methods treat degradation removal and information fusion as separate or loosely coupled steps, failing to address compound degradations (color casts, noise, improper lighting) present in real-world source images. Additionally, they apply uniform fusion rules indiscriminately to foreground and background, offering no mechanism for users to customize fusion behavior around objects of interest.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241250/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pair of degraded multi-modal images (e.g., infrared + visible, or CT-MRI, PET-MRI, SPECT-MRI) affected by compound degradation, plus an optional natural-language text command specifying objects to emphasize. The color image is decomposed into brightness and chrominance channels; the chrominance channel undergoes independent diffusion-based restoration, while the brightness channel is jointly diffused with the grayscale partner through the fusion process. The output is a clean fused image with corrected color, suppressed noise, and proper exposure, and optionally a re-modulated version where text-specified objects receive enhanced contrast and saliency via a pixel-level mask.

## Method

Text-DiFuse introduces an explicit coupling paradigm that embeds multi-modal feature fusion directly inside the T-step reverse diffusion sampling, rather than treating fusion as a pre- or post-processing step. A diffusion model (based on iDDPM) is first trained on degraded data to embed degradation-removal priors into its encoder-decoder; a Fusion Control Module (FCM) with CBAM attention is then inserted between encoder and decoder, generating per-step weight coefficients that fuse multi-modal encoded features via Hadamard product at every diffusion timestep. Training is guided by intensity and gradient losses computed on the predicted clean image at each step, encouraging preservation of contrast and texture from both modalities. For interactive control, OWL-VIT performs open-vocabulary object detection from the user's text command and SAM produces a pixel-level mask; this mask drives a re-modulation block that injects contrast-enhancement coefficients into the fusion weights, selectively boosting object saliency while leaving background regions unchanged.

## Results

On the MSRS dataset (infrared-visible fusion), Text-DiFuse achieves the best scores across all five metrics: EN 7.08, AG 3.31, SD 47.44, SCD 1.44, and VIF 0.76, outperforming the strongest competitor MRFS (CVPR'24) by 2.5% on EN and 24.1% on SD. On the Harvard Medicine dataset (medical fusion), it leads on AG (7.31), SD (80.19), SCD (1.69), and VIF (0.49), though MRFS edges it on EN (7.24 vs. 6.44). For downstream tasks, the text-controlled re-modulated fused image yields mIoU 58.63 in RGB-T semantic segmentation (vs. 58.31 for the best dedicated RGB-T method CMX-B2) and mAP@0.5 of 89.7 in object detection (vs. 88.6 for DDFM).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On MSRS, Text-DiFuse outperforms all nine baselines on every metric; the closest competitor MRFS (CVPR'24) trails by 0.17 on EN, 0.64 on AG, 6.49 on SD, 0.21 on SCD, and 0.01 on VIF. On Harvard Medicine, Text-DiFuse dominates on AG (7.31 vs. 6.22 for SDNet), SD (80.19 vs. 70.75 for MRFS), SCD (1.69 vs. 1.59 for DDFM), and VIF (0.49 vs. 0.48 for DDFM), but concedes EN to MRFS (6.44 vs. 7.24). The most directly comparable diffusion-based baseline, DDFM (ICCV'23), is surpassed by a wide margin on MSRS (e.g., SD 47.44 vs. 24.98) and on most Harvard Medicine metrics, confirming that explicitly coupling fusion within the diffusion process is more effective than treating multi-modal images as implicit conditions. For the re-modulation strategy, the text-controlled version improves mIoU from 57.53 (basic) to 58.63, surpassing all six dedicated RGB-T segmentation methods (best: CMX-B2 at 58.31), and boosts detection mAP@0.5 from 87.3 to 89.7, exceeding DDFM (88.6) and MRFS (82.0). On generalization to unseen datasets (LLVIP, RoadScene), Text-DiFuse maintains top or near-top scores, e.g., SD 41.78 on LLVIP and SD 52.84 on RoadScene, indicating robustness beyond the training distribution.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from prior diffusion-based fusion methods (DDFM, Diff-Fusion) that either treat multi-modal images as implicit conditions or inject features into separate CNN pipelines, by explicitly embedding a Fusion Control Module (FCM) between the encoder and decoder of the diffusion model so that feature-level fusion occurs at every denoising step. The training objective and variance parameterization are drawn directly from iDDPM, while the text-controlled re-modulation strategy repurposes OWL-VIT and SAM—originally designed for open-vocabulary detection and segmentation—as tools to generate spatially localized modulation masks for the fusion process. Conceptually, the paper reframes multi-modal fusion as a conditional denoising problem where the 'clean' target is the fused image and the 'degradation' encompasses both sensor-specific artifacts and the absence of complementary modal information.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The explicit coupling paradigm offers a principled solution to the compound degradation challenge (noise, color casts, improper lighting) that plagues real-world multi-modal fusion, and the ablation (AE route vs. diffusion route) provides evidence that the coupling itself—not just the network capacity—drives the gains. The text-controlled re-modulation opens a path toward user-interactive fusion, though the semantic gains (≈1 mIoU, ≈2–4 mAP) are modest and the T-step sampling bottleneck limits real-time deployment. Open questions include whether the coupling paradigm generalizes beyond the two modalities tested (infrared-visible, medical pairs), whether accelerated samplers (DDIM, consistency models) can preserve the fusion quality, and whether the zero-shot localization component degrades gracefully on out-of-domain scenes.
