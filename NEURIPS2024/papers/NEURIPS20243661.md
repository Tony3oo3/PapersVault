## Problem

Weak convergence of stochastic processes ignores the underlying filtration (information flow over time), so two processes with nearly identical laws can yield drastically different prices for American options or other multi-period decisions. Aldous' extended weak convergence captures the filtration via prediction processes, but no efficient numerical implementation existed. This paper provides the first computable metric (HRPCFD) that metrises extended weak convergence and enables practical generative modelling.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243661/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The generator takes a past path segment X_[0,p] (a d-dimensional sequence of length p+1) and a noise vector z, producing a future path X_(p,T] of length T-p via iterative autoregressive steps. The discriminator consumes full sample paths from both real and generated distributions, but critically requires estimating the conditional distribution P(X|F_t) at each time t via a trained sequence-to-sequence regression module that maps the full path to the unitary feature of the future sub-path. The HRPCFD output is a scalar Hilbert-Schmidt distance between two matrix-valued expectations, computed over a finite ensemble of random linear maps (M, M-cal).

## Method

The core contribution is a two-stage unitary path development for measure-valued paths: first, each conditional distribution P(X|F_t) is mapped to a matrix via the rank-1 PCF under M ∈ L(R^d, u(n)), producing a C^{n×n}-valued path; second, a rank-2 unitary feature under M-cal ∈ L(C^{n×n}, u(m)) is applied to this matrix-valued path, exploiting noncommutativity to encode the temporal ordering of information. The resulting HRPCFD inherits boundedness, MMD, and characteristicity properties from classical PCFD while metrising the stronger extended weak topology. For the GAN, an LSTM-based autoregressive generator with a path-embedding module produces future steps, while HRPCFD serves as the discriminator. Training follows a three-step decoupled procedure: (1) optimise the rank-1 maps M_i to maximise EPCFD, (2) train autoregressive regression modules F_θ for each M_i to estimate conditional unitary features, and (3) optimise the rank-2 maps M-cal_i to maximise the full EHRPCFD, avoiding the computational infeasibility of joint optimisation.

## Results

On the fBM (H=1/4) dataset, HRPCF-GAN achieves the best scores across all five metrics: Auto-Correlation (.082), Cross-Correlation (.013), Discriminative Score (.151), Sig-W1 Distance (.169), and Conditional Expectation (1.693), with Auto-Correlation and Cross-Correlation approximately 21.9% and 72.3% lower than the second-best model. On the Stock dataset, HRPCF-GAN again leads on all six metrics, including the American Put option pricing error (.179 vs. .202 for PCFGAN). In hypothesis testing (Table 1), HRPCFD achieves test power of 1.0 (perfect discrimination) for H ∈ {0.4, 0.425, 0.575, 0.6} and 0.97–0.99 for H ∈ {0.45, 0.55}, consistently outperforming PCFD, signature MMDs, and classical MMDs, particularly near H=0.5 where all other methods degrade sharply.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HRPCF-GAN outperforms all three generative baselines (RCGAN, TimeGAN, PCFGAN) on every metric for both the fBM and Stock datasets. The most direct comparison is against PCFGAN [19], the rank-1 predecessor: HRPCF-GAN improves by approximately 34% on fBM and 14% on Stock across the aggregate metrics, with the largest gains in Cross-Correlation (0.013 vs. 0.047 on fBM) and Conditional Expectation (1.693 vs. 2.278 on fBM), confirming that the high-rank development captures filtration structure that rank-1 PCF misses. RCGAN [10] and TimeGAN [23] are substantially weaker, with TimeGAN performing worst on fBM (Auto-C. .459, Discriminative .480). In the hypothesis testing experiment, HRPCFD dominates PCFD [15,19] (e.g., 0.31 vs. 0.06 test power at H=0.475), High Rank signature MMD [21] (0.31 vs. 0.12 at H=0.475), and both linear/RBF signature MMDs [8,20] and classical MMDs, with the advantage most pronounced near H=0.5 where all competing methods drop to near-chance performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the PCF-GAN framework (Lou et al., 2023) from weak convergence to the stronger extended weak convergence by introducing a two-level (high rank) unitary path development that operates on measure-valued prediction processes. Conceptually, it shares the philosophy of Salvi et al. (2021) in transforming measure-valued paths into linear-space-valued paths, but departs by using finite-dimensional matrix-valued paths via unitary development rather than infinite-dimensional Hilbert space lifting, thereby avoiding the need to solve families of PDEs. The theoretical foundation draws on Aldous's extended weak convergence, the adapted weak topology results of Backhoff-Veraguas et al. (2020), and the characteristicity/universality theorems for unitary features from Chevyrev & Lyons (2016) and Lou et al. (2022).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a fundamental gap: weak convergence ignores the filtration (information flow) of stochastic processes, which can cause arbitrarily large errors in multi-period decision problems like American option pricing. By providing the first efficient numerical implementation of a metric for extended weak convergence, it opens the door to data-driven, non-parametric solutions for financial and economic problems where model misspecification of the information structure is costly. Key open questions include how generator architecture choices affect the learned filtration structure, and whether the sequence-to-sequence regression module for conditional expectation estimation can be made more accurate and stable. The approach is currently limited to discrete-time processes and bounded-variation paths.
