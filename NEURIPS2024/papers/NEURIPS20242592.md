## Problem

Transitionary score-based generative models (SGMs) for inverse problems suffer from instability and poor generalizability under complex, nonlinear, or unknown degradation, yet the theoretical reasons for this failure remain unexplored. Existing approaches are largely limited to linear inverse problems and lack a principled framework for understanding how cumulative errors and distribution biases arise during the forward-reverse SDE process. This paper identifies the root cause as Temporal Distribution Discrepancy—the gap between the finite-time forward process distribution and the theoretical stationary distribution—and provides a theoretical framework to address it.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242592/cropped_page5_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a degraded measurement y = A(x) + n as input, where A is an arbitrary (potentially nonlinear, partial, or unknown) forward operator and n is measurement noise, and outputs a restored high-quality image x. For MRI reconstruction, the input is complex-valued k-space data with Cartesian undersampling masks (8× or 16× acceleration); for MRI super-resolution, the input is low-resolution T2-weighted scans with kernel degradation or k-space truncation. The method operates on 128×128 patches during training and handles both natural RGB images and medical MRI without task-specific architectural modifications.

## Method

D³GM reformulates transitionary SGMs as Ornstein-Uhlenbeck (OU) processes and identifies Temporal Distribution Discrepancy (Proposition 2) as a lower bound on reconstruction error caused by the finite-time forward process failing to reach the theoretical stationary distribution. To resolve this, the method imposes a measure-preserving constraint σ²_t/(2θ_t) = λ² from Random Dynamical Systems theory, which reduces the regularization to a single variable and characterizes the attractor as N(μ, λ²), ensuring the forward-reverse processes remain consistent. A calibration parameter τ > 1 is introduced to widen the finite-time distribution toward the stationary distribution, trading off reverse-process stability against distributional fidelity. The score network is trained with a weighted score-matching objective (Eq. 8) using cosine schedules for θ_t and σ_t, and spectral normalization is applied to enforce Lipschitz continuity for convergence guarantees. The framework extends to a two-sided solution operator with flow maps, enabling the Poincaré recurrence theorem to guarantee that the reverse process returns to a neighborhood of the high-quality image.

## Results

On Rain100H, D³GM achieves 32.41 PSNR / 0.912 SSIM / 0.040 LPIPS, outperforming the strongest diffusion baseline IR-SDE (31.65/0.904/0.047) and all CNN baselines. On the fastMRI knee dataset at 8× undersampling, D³GM reaches 27.92 PSNR and 0.175 LPIPS (best LPIPS), while at 16× it achieves 0.153 LPIPS (best perceptual quality) though CDiffMR edges ahead on PSNR (25.77 vs 25.26). For cross-domain MRI super-resolution on IXI (trained on HH, tested on Guys and IOP), D³GM attains 25.13/0.799 and 28.60/0.863 PSNR/SSIM respectively, surpassing all baselines including test-time adaptation methods. On real-world dehazing (O-HAZE), D³GM scores 26.23 PSNR / 0.786 SSIM, the best among all compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

D³GM consistently outperforms the closest diffusion-based baselines: on Rain100H it beats IR-SDE (OU SDE) by 0.76 dB PSNR and VPB (Coefficient Decoupled SDE) by 1.52 dB, while also surpassing all CNN deraining methods (PreNet, MPRNet, MAXIM) by 1.9–6.2 dB. On real-world dehazing (O-HAZE), D³GM leads IR-SDE by 1.71 dB and I²SB by 4.72 dB in PSNR, confirming the advantage of measure-preserving dynamics over mean-reverting or Schrödinger-bridge formulations. For fastMRI reconstruction at 16×, D³GM achieves the best LPIPS (0.153) but trails CDiffMR by 0.51 dB PSNR and 0.006 SSIM, indicating that task-specific frequency-domain diffusion models still hold an edge in that narrow setting. On cross-domain IXI MRI super-resolution, D³GM surpasses test-time adaptation methods (ACT, CST) by 1.7–2.2 dB PSNR on both Guys and IOP, demonstrating superior generalizability under domain shift. On rain200H, D³GM matches DRSformer (32.21 vs 32.17 PSNR) while using roughly 57% fewer FLOPs (104.7G vs 242.9G), suggesting comparable quality at substantially lower computational cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the score-based SDE diffusion framework (Song et al., ICLR 2020) and the mean-reverting/OU-process interpretation of transitionary SGMs (Luo et al., ICML 2023) by recasting them within the theory of Random Dynamical Systems (RDS). The key departure is identifying that standard OU-based transitionary SGMs violate the measure-preserving cocycle property of RDS, which the authors formalize as a Temporal Distribution Discrepancy (a lower bound on reconstruction error). D³GM enforces the constraint σ²/(2θ) = λ² to restore measure-preserving dynamics, distinguishing it from prior transitionary SGMs (IR-SDE, I²SB, VPB) that either decouple coefficients or assume strict Dirac priors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The RDS/measure-preserving lens provides a principled explanation for why transitionary SGMs accumulate error under complex or unknown degradation, and the Temporal Distribution Discrepancy bound (Prop. 2) gives a concrete diagnostic for when a given noise schedule will fail. Practically, D³GM handles a diverse range of degradation types (deraining, real dehazing, MRI undersampling, cross-domain MRI SR) under a single unified framework without task-specific architectural changes. Limitations include the hand-tuned τ hyperparameter (set based on degradation severity rather than learned), modest gains on severely corrupted data (Dense-Haze), and the fact that the RDS stability guarantee is asymptotic rather than finite-time. Open questions include whether τ can be learned adaptively and whether the framework extends to non-Gaussian degradation models.
