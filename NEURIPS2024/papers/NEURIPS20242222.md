## Problem

Open-ended object detection requires discovering and localizing objects without any predefined category list, a setting more practical than open-set detection where categories must be specified in advance. While VLMs can recognize rare or novel objects, their localization is imprecise; SAM segments well but cannot identify what it segments. No prior training-free framework connects these two complementary capabilities into a unified open-ended detection and segmentation pipeline.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242222/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image (processed at 490×490, split into 35×35 patches by the VLM image encoder) and produces three outputs: free-form object category names generated by the VLM, bounding boxes, and pixel-level instance segmentation masks from SAM. For multi-scale processing, the image is additionally split into four H/2×W/2 corner sub-images, and ten different question prompts are used to query the VLM, with all results ensembled. Object names are parsed from VLM text via Tag2Text and mapped to dataset categories using CLIP text-encoder similarity for evaluation.

## Method

VL-SAM connects CogVLM-17B (recognition) and SAM ViT-Huge (localization) via attention maps as intermediate prompts, entirely training-free. The attention map generation module aggregates across all transformer heads using mean-max head weights, then propagates attention across layers via a regularized attention rollout that multiplies each column by 1−(L₀−1)/L to prevent the collapse caused by causal masks. A prompt generation module thresholds the attention map to identify the maximum connected positive region, then samples one positive point (max activation) and one negative point (min activation) as SAM point prompts. An iterative refinement loop feeds initial SAM masks back as additional prompts (PerSAM-style cascaded refinement) and re-samples points from the masked attention map until convergence, with final results aggregated via NMS. Multi-scale ensemble (four corner crops) and question-prompt ensemble (ten VLM-generated questions) further improve recall for small objects and comprehensive scene coverage.

## Results

On LVIS minival, VL-SAM achieves 23.4 box AP_rare and 22.7 mask AP_rare in a fully training-free manner, surpassing the only other open-ended method GenerateU (20.0 box AP_rare, trained) by 3.4 AP. On the CODA corner-case detection benchmark, VL-SAM achieves 40.1 mAR (90.1 AR_50, 50.5 AR_75) without any training, dramatically outperforming the previous open-ended method LLaVA-Grounding (18.4 mAR) by over 21 mAR. VL-SAM reaches 74.1% of the oracle upper bound (54.1 mAR with ground-truth boxes fed to SAM), demonstrating strong localization quality from attention-map prompts alone.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VL-SAM's most striking win is over LLaVA-Grounding (18.4 mAR) on CODA, where it more than doubles the mAR to 40.1 while requiring no training at all, versus LLaVA-Grounding's joint training of VLM and grounding models. On LVIS, VL-SAM (23.4 box AP_rare) beats the only other open-ended method GenerateU (20.0) by 3.4 AP and additionally provides segmentation masks (22.7 mask AP_rare) that GenerateU cannot produce. Against open-set methods on LVIS, VL-SAM trails GroundingDINO (27.4), YOLO-World (27.1), DetCLIP (26.9), and Mask R-CNN (26.3) by 3–4 AP, but remains competitive given it requires no predefined categories or training. On CODA, VL-SAM surpasses all close-set detectors (best: RetinaNet at 12.8 mAR) and all open-set detectors (best: YOLO-World at 16.1 mAR) by a wide margin, with the gap to the strongest open-set method (YOLO-World) exceeding 24 mAR. The few-shot open-set baselines (FsDet 4.2, DeFRCN 4.5) and ORE (8.3) are far outperformed, confirming that proposal-based or few-shot approaches struggle with corner cases. The only method that outperforms VL-SAM is the oracle GT+SAM upper bound (54.1 mAR), which uses ground-truth boxes and is not a fair comparison; VL-SAM closes 74.1% of this gap using only VLM-generated attention maps as prompts.

## Relation to Prior Work

VL-SAM sits at the intersection of two model families: vision-language models (CogVLM, LLaVA, MiniGPT-4) for open-ended object recognition and prompt-based segmentation models (SAM) for precise localization. It departs from open-set detectors (GroundingDINO, YOLO-World) that require predefined category names, and from the prior open-ended method GenerateU, which requires joint fine-tuning of an image encoder and language model. The key conceptual move is repurposing VLM attention maps—augmented with head aggregation and a regularized attention flow adapted from Abnar & Zuidema—as point prompts for SAM, eliminating any training. The multi-scale splitting follows SPHINX, and the iterative mask refinement follows PerSAM's cascaded post-refinement.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free nature of VL-SAM is practically significant: it avoids the substantial training cost of GenerateU (fine-tuning on VG and GRIT) and the joint training of LLaVA-Grounding, while simultaneously producing both bounding boxes and segmentation masks. The 2× mAR improvement over LLaVA-Grounding on CODA (18.4→40.1) suggests that the bottleneck in prior open-ended methods was not recognition but the lack of a precise localization mechanism. Open questions include whether the approach can be made fast enough for real-time autonomous driving, whether VLM hallucinations can be mitigated without retraining, and whether the attention-collapse regularization generalizes to non-causal architectures. The framework's modularity (swappable VLM and SAM) positions it as a template for future plug-and-play perception pipelines.
