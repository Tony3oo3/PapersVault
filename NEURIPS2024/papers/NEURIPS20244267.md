## Problem

Standard PCA reconstruction errors on penultimate DNN features fail to separate InD from OoD data because the two distributions are linearly inseparable in the original z-space. While Kernel PCA offers a natural non-linear remedy, finding appropriate kernels that characterize the specific non-linear structure of InD/OoD features—and doing so without the prohibitive O(N_tr) cost of kernel-matrix operations—remains an open challenge.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20244267/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes penultimate-layer features z ∈ R^m extracted from a pre-trained DNN (ResNet-18/50 or MobileNet) on RGB images; no retraining of the backbone is required. At inference, a single new feature vector is mapped through the explicit feature mapping Φ and projected onto a pre-computed q-dimensional principal subspace, yielding a scalar reconstruction error e^Φ(x̂) that serves as the detection score S(x̂) = −e^Φ(x̂). The inference cost is O(1) for the cosine-only variant (CoP) or O(M) for the cosine-Gaussian variant (CoRP) with M ≈ 4096 RFFs, versus O(N_tr) for KNN-based baselines.

## Method

The paper derives two task-specific kernels by taking a kernel perspective on the KNN OoD detector: the ℓ₂-normalization step is recognized as the explicit feature mapping φ_cos(z) = z/‖z‖₂ inducing the cosine kernel, and the ℓ₂-distance on normalized features motivates a Gaussian kernel k_gau applied on top of φ_cos. For the cosine-Gaussian variant (CoRP), the infinite-dimensional Gaussian feature mapping is approximated via Random Fourier Features, giving the composite mapping Φ(z) = φ_RFF(φ_cos(z)). PCA is then executed in the mapped Φ(z)-space: the covariance matrix Σ^Φ is eigendecomposed, the top-q principal components U_q^Φ are retained, and the reconstruction error of a new sample is the norm of its projection onto the residual subspace. This avoids the N_tr × N_tr kernel matrix entirely, reducing inference to a single matrix-vector product with the pre-stored U_q^Φ and mean μ^Φ, while Propositions 1–2 formally bridge the covariance-based and kernel-function formulations of KPCA.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K with ResNet50 (standard training), CoRP achieves an average FPR95 of 48.60 and AUROC of 87.53 across four OoD datasets, outperforming KNN (FPR 53.97, AUROC 85.01) and Energy (FPR 58.41, AUROC 86.17). Under supervised contrastive learning, CoRP further improves to FPR 32.11 and AUROC 92.86, surpassing KNN (FPR 38.47, AUROC 90.91). When fused with ReAct, CoRP achieves new SOTA performance with FPR 17.68 and AUROC 95.98. Inference cost drops dramatically: CoRP requires only ~0.086 ms and ~29 MiB per sample versus ~15.59 ms and ~20 GiB for KNN.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoRP consistently outperforms KNN [7] on ImageNet-1K in both standard training (FPR 48.60 vs 53.97; AUROC 87.53 vs 85.01) and supervised contrastive learning (FPR 32.11 vs 38.47; AUROC 92.86 vs 90.91), while reducing inference time from ~15.6 ms to ~0.09 ms. CoRP also beats logits-based baselines (Energy, ODIN, MSP) and feature-based methods (Mahala, ViM) in standard training. When fused with ReAct, CoRP achieves the best SOTA result (FPR 17.68, AUROC 95.98), narrowly surpassing PCA+ReAct (FPR 18.66, AUROC 95.76) and significantly outperforming ReAct alone (FPR 31.43). Fused with Energy, CoRP yields a large margin over PCA (FPR 32.65 vs 49.50), confirming the advantage of the non-linear mapping. CoP alone is slightly weaker than KNN on some individual OoD sets in standard training (e.g., iNaturalist FPR 67.25 vs 59.00), but the addition of the Gaussian kernel in CoRP recovers and surpasses KNN across all datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the PCA reconstruction-error framework for OoD detection (Guan et al., ICCV 2023) by replacing linear PCA with Kernel PCA in a non-linear feature space, motivated by the observation that InD and OoD penultimate features are linearly inseparable. The two task-specific kernels (cosine and cosine-Gaussian) are derived by taking a kernel-theoretic perspective on the KNN OoD detector (Sun et al., ICML 2022), recognizing its ℓ₂-normalization step as a cosine kernel and its ℓ₂-distance search as a Gaussian kernel. Random Fourier Features (Rahimi & Recht, NeurIPS 2007) are employed to obtain explicit finite-dimensional feature mappings, avoiding the prohibitively large kernel matrices of classic KPCA (Schölkopf et al., 1997).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled explanation for why standard PCA fails at OoD detection (linear inseparability) and shows that a simple non-linear mapping can resolve it, with the practical benefit of reducing inference from O(N_tr) to O(1) or O(M) with ~700× less storage. A key open question the authors highlight is whether the manually selected kernels and their hyperparameters (γ, M, q) can be learned end-to-end via deep kernel learning or other optimization objectives. The proposed kernels are positioned as a useful inductive prior that could guide future work on learning stronger, data-adaptive kernels for OoD detection.
