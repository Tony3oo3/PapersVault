## Problem

Vision models trained on seen domains and classes struggle to generalize to unseen domains (multi-domain generalization) or discover novel categories (generalized category discovery). Existing regularization terms like L2 are parametric-based with ambiguous interpretability, and while logical reasoning has shown promise in language tasks, its connection to visual classification generalization remains largely unexplored. This paper asks how logical reasoning can be formalized for image classification and how a logic-derived regularization can simultaneously improve generalization and interpretability.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images in two forms: labeled pairs from seen domains for supervised training, and unlabeled images (potentially from seen or unseen domains) that may contain both known and unknown classes. It produces a K-dimensional softmax prediction vector for known classes and, for samples lacking sufficient semantic support of any known class, assigns them to discovered novel clusters. L-Reg operates on the latent feature vector Z ∈ R^M extracted by the encoder, requiring that each dimension represents an independent semantic (best satisfied by deep-layer features such as the bottleneck of a RegNetY-16GF or DINO ViT-B/16 backbone).

## Method

L-Reg is a sample-based logical regularization derived by mapping image classification onto a formal logic framework where the encoder g and classifier h must jointly form atomic formulas (h(g(x), y, d) → True/False). The key mechanism minimizes the conditional entropy of predictions given each individual semantic dimension z_i while maximizing entropy given the negation of the semantic support Γ̄, yielding a loss that forces the model to rely on minimal sufficient semantics per class and keeps semantic supports for different classes distinct (γ^{y_i} ≠ γ^{y_j}). Concretely, L-Reg computes a softmax over Ŷ^T Z and applies an entropy difference across the M feature dimensions, reducing classifier weight complexity (fewer extreme-valued weights) and balancing the latent feature distribution. It is a plug-and-play additive term (α·L_L-Reg) compatible with existing frameworks like GMDG, PIM, and MIRO, and is most effective when applied to deep-layer features where semantic independence holds.

## Results

On the mDG task (Table 1), GMDG+L-Reg achieves 75.8% average accuracy across five benchmarks, improving over GMDG (75.1%) and MIRO (74.1%), with the largest gain on TerraIncognita (+2.2 pp). On the GCD task (Table 2), PIM+L-Reg reaches 68.8% all-class accuracy and 62.7% unknown-class accuracy, surpassing PIM (67.4% / 59.9%) and TIM (62.7% / 56.4%). In the combined mDG+GCD setting (Table 3), GMDG+L-Reg achieves 51.94% all-class and 27.68% unknown-class accuracy, outperforming GMDG by +4.00 pp (all) and +7.01 pp (unknown). On CircuitNet congestion prediction, CircuitFormer+L-Reg improves Pearson correlation from 0.6374 to 0.6553.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GMDG+L-Reg consistently outperforms GMDG across all five mDG benchmarks, with the most substantial gain on TerraIncognita (+2.2 pp, 60.7→62.9) and DomainNet (+0.8 pp), while matching or slightly exceeding MIRO on every dataset (avg +1.7 pp). In the GCD setting, PIM+L-Reg improves unknown-class accuracy by +2.8 pp over PIM (59.9→62.7) and +6.3 pp over TIM (56.4), at a negligible cost of −0.3 pp on known classes. In the combined mDG+GCD scenario, GMDG+L-Reg dominates all four baselines (ERM, PIM, MIRO, GMDG), achieving the best unknown-class accuracy of 27.68% versus 20.68% for GMDG and 25.79% for MIRO. On CircuitNet, CircuitFormer+L-Reg surpasses the SOTA CircuitFormer on all three correlation metrics (Pearson 0.6553 vs. 0.6374, Spearman 0.5289 vs. 0.5282, Kendall 0.3944 vs. 0.3935). The only consistent trade-off is a slight known-class accuracy drop in GCD (−0.3 pp), which the authors attribute to L-Reg narrowing the semantic support set.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges formal logical reasoning (rooted in universal algebraic logic [4] and conditional entropy-based inference [43]) with visual classification generalization, a connection that prior work has only explored tangentially through entropy-based logical explanations [6] or length/symbolic generalization in NLP [1, 10]. It positions L-Reg as a sample-based, interpretable alternative to parametric regularizers like L2 weight decay, and as a complement to domain-invariance methods (GMDG, MIRO) and information-maximization methods (PIM) rather than a replacement. The 'semantic support' concept and the requirement that encoder-classifier outputs form atomic formulas provide a principled derivation that departs from ad-hoc regularization design.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

L-Reg offers a plug-and-play loss that consistently improves generalization across mDG, GCD, and the combined mDG+GCD setting while providing interpretable feature focus (e.g., faces for 'person'), which is valuable for safety-critical deployment. A key limitation is that narrowing semantic support can slightly degrade known-class accuracy, and the method's effectiveness depends on the latent features being semantically independent—a condition better satisfied in deep layers. Open questions include whether combining L-Reg with stronger independence constraints (e.g., Ortho-Reg) or architectural changes can eliminate the known-class trade-off, and whether the logical framework extends to other modalities or tasks beyond classification.
