## Problem

Online reconstruction of dynamic scenes from multi-view video streams requires balancing training speed, rendering quality, and storage/transmission efficiency, but existing 3DGS-based methods like 3DGStream suffer from overfitting under limited camera views, excessive Gaussian growth, and implicit neural motion fields that misalign with the explicit, discrete nature of 3D Gaussian primitives. This leads to high per-frame storage, slow convergence, and instability in the sequential learning pipeline.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242562/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes synchronized multi-view RGB video frames (N3DV at 1352×1039 after 2× downsampling, Meet Room at 1280×720) with pre-computed camera poses from COLMAP, processing 300 frames per scene. Rather than outputting a full scene representation per frame, HiCoM produces a compact per-frame delta consisting of hierarchical motion parameters (3D translation vectors and 4D rotation quaternions shared across region-level groups of Gaussians) plus a small set of newly cloned Gaussians, reducing per-frame storage to ~0.4–0.9 MB versus 4–8 MB for prior methods. The initial frame is a standard 3DGS trained with noise perturbation, and subsequent frames are updated in-place by applying learned motions and merging new Gaussians while pruning low-opacity ones.

## Method

HiCoM introduces three coupled mechanisms for efficient online 3DGS learning. First, a perturbation smoothing strategy adds Gaussian noise (λ_noise=0.01) to 3D Gaussian positions during initial-frame training, preventing overfitting to sparse views and significantly reducing the converged Gaussian count. Second, the Hierarchical Coherent Motion mechanism partitions the scene into a grid of regions at multiple spatial scales (fine, medium, coarse); because 3D Gaussians are non-uniformly distributed, most regions are empty and only non-empty regions receive shared translation and rotation parameters, with each Gaussian's total motion computed as the sum of contributions from all levels it inhabits—this explicit, discrete motion representation requires far fewer parameters than per-Gaussian or neural-network-based approaches and converges in ~100 steps. Third, Continual Refinement clones Gaussians in high-gradient regions to capture significant scene changes, merges them into the base 3DGS for subsequent frames, and removes an equivalent number of low-opacity Gaussians to maintain a fixed, compact representation. A parallel training variant further reduces wall time by using frame t as a reference to simultaneously optimize frames t+1 through t+k, cutting average per-frame training to under 2 seconds with 4-frame parallelism.

## Results

On the N3DV dataset, HiCoM achieves 31.17 dB PSNR with 0.7 MB per-frame storage and 6.7 s average training time, outperforming 3DGStream (30.15 dB, 7.6 MB, 8.2 s) and StreamRF (30.68 dB, 17.7 MB, 10.0 s). On Meet Room, HiCoM reaches 26.73 dB PSNR with 0.4 MB storage and 3.9 s training, again surpassing 3DGStream (25.96 dB, 4.0 MB, 4.7 s). The parallel variant HiCoM-P4 reduces per-frame training to 1.7 s (N3DV) and 1.0 s (Meet Room) with only ~0.2 dB PSNR loss, while rendering speed reaches 274–284 FPS across both datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HiCoM outperforms all online baselines on both primary datasets. Against 3DGStream (the closest online competitor), HiCoM gains +1.02 dB PSNR on N3DV (31.17 vs 30.15) and +0.77 dB on Meet Room (26.73 vs 25.96), while reducing per-frame storage by ~91% (0.7 vs 7.6 MB) and training time by ~18% (6.7 vs 8.2 s). Against StreamRF, HiCoM improves PSNR by +0.49 dB on N3DV and matches on Meet Room (+0.01 dB), while achieving 21× lower storage and 20× faster rendering (274 vs 13 FPS). Against the Naive 3DGS baseline, HiCoM improves PSNR by +0.48 dB on N3DV while reducing storage by 99% and training time by 98%. On the large-motion PanopticSports dataset, HiCoM with a color-consistency trick (29.08 dB) surpasses Dynamic 3DGS (28.70 dB) and 3DGStream (23.15 dB); on the synthetic ParticleNeRF dataset, HiCoM (31.05 dB) outperforms ParticleNeRF (27.47 dB) but trails Dynamic3DGS (39.49 dB), which benefits from per-timestamp Gaussian attributes.

## Relation to Prior Work

HiCoM extends the online/streamable dynamic scene reconstruction pipeline pioneered by 3DGStream, which uses a Neural Transformation Cache (implicit MLP-based motion field) to update 3DGS frame-by-frame. The key conceptual departure is replacing this implicit motion modeling with an explicit hierarchical coherent motion mechanism that assigns shared translation/rotation parameters to spatially partitioned regions of Gaussians, better aligning with the discrete, explicit nature of 3DGS primitives. The perturbation smoothing strategy for initial 3DGS learning draws from data augmentation ideas in NeRF training, while the continual refinement (carrying over new Gaussians and pruning low-opacity ones) addresses a specific gap in 3DGStream where added Gaussians were discarded between frames.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 85% per-frame storage reduction and sub-2-second wall-time per frame (with 4-frame parallel training) make streamable free-viewpoint video substantially more practical for real-world deployment, where bandwidth and latency are critical constraints. The explicit hierarchical motion representation is a clean conceptual contribution that could inform future 3DGS-based dynamic methods beyond the streaming setting. However, the paper acknowledges that error accumulation over long sequences remains unresolved, experiments are limited to indoor scenes, and the quality of the initial 3DGS representation still heavily constrains downstream performance—open questions that limit the method's generalizability to outdoor or long-duration scenarios.
