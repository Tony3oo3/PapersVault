## Problem

Existing test-time adaptation (TTA) methods handle only a single pre-trained source model, while multi-source domain adaptation methods require either access to source data or the full target dataset available offline. No prior method addresses the practical setting where multiple source models must be combined and adapted to a dynamically shifting test distribution that arrives in small streaming batches, without source data, while simultaneously preventing catastrophic forgetting over prolonged adaptation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20244159/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes N pre-trained source models (ResNet-18 for classification at 224×224, DeepLab v3+ with ResNet-18 encoder for segmentation at 512×512) and consumes unlabeled test batches of size 128 (classification) or 16 (segmentation) arriving sequentially over time. For each batch it outputs a set of combination weights over the N sources, a class prediction or pixel-wise segmentation mask, and updated parameters for exactly one source model (the most correlated). The test distribution at each time step is modeled as a linear combination of source distributions, and the framework assumes all samples within a batch share the same distribution.

## Method

CONTRAST performs two operations per incoming test batch. First, it learns combination weights by minimizing the Shannon entropy of weighted pseudo-labels over the batch, subject to simplex constraints; the weights are initialized via a softmax of negative KL divergences between each source model's stored BatchNorm statistics and the current test batch's BN statistics, and the step size is set by a closed-form approximate Newton's method for fast convergence in ~5 iterations. Second, it updates only the single source model with the largest weight using a pluggable TTA method (TENT, CoTTA, or EaTA), leaving all other source models frozen. This selective update is the key anti-forgetting mechanism: the most correlated model produces the most confident pseudo-labels, yielding the smallest gradients during adaptation, so its parameters drift least from the source state. Theoretical analysis provides a convergence rate for the weight optimization (Theorem 1) and a generalization bound (Theorem 2) showing that the excess risk is upper-bounded by the minimum over weights of a domain-shift term plus a pseudo-label-quality term, with the discrete model-selection step (updating only the argmax-weight source) yielding the tightest bound within the one-hot simplex.

## Results

On CIFAR-100C (dynamic adaptation across 15 sequential corruption domains), CONTRAST consistently outperforms the best single-source baseline: EaTA+CONTRAST achieves 34.2% mean error vs. EaTA-Best at 39.9%, CoTTA+CONTRAST achieves 34.4% vs. CoTTA-Best at 38.5%, and Tent+CONTRAST achieves 37.1% vs. Tent-Best at 68.2%. On the static Office-Home benchmark, Tent+CONTRAST reduces mean error from 39.9% (Tent-Best) to 37.0%, and on the Digits dataset, Tent+CONTRAST achieves 14.3% mean error vs. 17.1% for Tent-Best. In the forgetting analysis, CONTRAST exhibits virtually no degradation in source-domain accuracy over 15 sequential adaptation steps, while Tent drops sharply and CoTTA/EaTA degrade gradually.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CONTRAST is evaluated against three single-source TTA methods (Tent, CoTTA, EaTA), the multi-source source-free UDA method DECISION, and Model Soups. Against Tent, CONTRAST delivers a dramatic improvement: Tent+CONTRAST achieves 37.1% mean error on CIFAR-100C versus 68.2% for Tent-Best, a 31-point reduction, because the ensemble prevents the catastrophic forgetting that plagues Tent during sequential adaptation. Against CoTTA and EaTA (which are designed to resist forgetting), CONTRAST still wins by 4.1 and 5.7 percentage points respectively on CIFAR-100C mean error (34.4% and 34.2% vs. 38.5% and 39.9%). Against DECISION [18], which is designed for offline multi-source UDA, CONTRAST+Tent achieves 37.1% mean error versus 93.3% for DECISION in the online streaming setting, confirming that offline MSDA methods are inapplicable to small-batch test-time adaptation. Against Model Soups [30], which averages model parameters, CONTRAST+Tent achieves 37.1% versus 95.72% mean error, showing that parameter averaging is fundamentally unsuitable for models trained on different source domains. On the static Office-Home benchmark, all three CONTRAST variants (37.0–37.8% mean) outperform their respective single-source best counterparts (39.9–41.5% mean), confirming the benefit of optimal weight combination even without distribution shift over time.

## Relation to Prior Work

CONTRAST sits at the intersection of three established lines of work: test-time adaptation (TENT, CoTTA, EaTA) which handles single-source dynamic distribution shifts; multi-source domain adaptation (MSDA) which leverages multiple source models but requires full offline target data; and source-free adaptation which removes the need for source data but is typically offline. The paper's key departure is combining all four criteria simultaneously—source-free, on-the-fly, dynamic target, and multi-source—as formalized in Table 1, where no prior method satisfies all. The selective model-update mechanism (adapting only the most correlated source) is a novel architectural choice that repurposes the ensemble weighting step to also serve as a forgetting-prevention strategy, rather than relying on explicit regularization as in CoTTA or EaTA.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework addresses a practically important deployment scenario where multiple pre-trained models exist but source data is unavailable and test data arrives in streaming batches—common in autonomous driving, robotics, and edge deployment. The inherent forgetting mitigation (only updating the most correlated model) is a design-level solution rather than a post-hoc regularization, which is more elegant and generalizable. A key limitation is the assumption that all samples within a batch share the same distribution; extending to mixed-distribution batches is identified as future work. The finding that lightweight TENT performs comparably to forgetting-specific methods (CoTTA, EaTA) within CONTRAST suggests the selective-update architecture may be more important than the specific TTA algorithm, opening questions about whether simpler adaptation suffices when the ensemble structure handles forgetting.
