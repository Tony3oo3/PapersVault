## Problem

While many quantitative metrics for disentangled representations have been proposed, it remains unclear what properties these metrics truly quantify and how they relate to formal logical definitions of disentanglement. There is no systematic, theoretically grounded procedure for converting the logical predicates that define properties like modularity and informativeness into real-valued, optimizable metrics with guaranteed minimizers.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242296/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a representation function m: Y → Z where Y = Y₁ × Y₂ is a product of factor spaces and Z = Z₁ × Z₂ is a product of code spaces, along with a choice of strict premetric on each space and an aggregator (e.g., max, sum, mean, mean square) for finite factor sets. It produces two families of real-valued scores: a modularity score measuring how well m preserves the product structure (i.e., how close m is to a product function m₁₁ × m₂₂), and an informativeness score measuring how well m preserves distinctness (i.e., how close m is to being injective or retractable). The metrics are designed to be directly differentiable and usable as learning objectives without requiring auxiliary regressors or hyperparameter tuning.

## Method

The core contribution is a compositional conversion procedure that transforms higher-order equational predicates into real-valued quantities by systematically replacing: equality with a strict premetric, the Heyting algebra {⊤,⊥} with the quantale [0,∞], conjunction with addition, disjunction with minimum, implication with truncated subtraction, universal quantifiers with aggregators (max, sum, mean, mean square), and existential quantifiers with infimum. For modularity, two logically equivalent formulations yield two metric families: (i) product approximation, which computes the distance from m to its best product-function approximation via inner optimization, and (ii) constancy of curried functions, which measures pairwise distances among outputs with one factor fixed, eliminating the optimization step entirely. For informativeness, analogous constructions yield retraction approximation (distance to best left inverse) and contraction (truncated subtraction of input distance by output distance). Theorem 1 guarantees that a metric score of zero implies the corresponding logical property holds, and the converse holds iff the definition contains no implication, providing a rigorous link between the logical definition and the quantitative metric.

## Results

The paper's main empirical evaluation (Table 2) uses 10 synthetic functions with known ground-truth properties (modular/non-modular, informative/non-informative) to test whether metrics correctly assign perfect scores (1.00) only when the property holds. The proposed modularity metrics (variance, diameter, mean pairwise distance) and informativeness metrics (max/mean contraction) correctly assign 1.00 to all functions satisfying the property and lower scores (0.01–0.96) to those that do not, confirming Theorem 1's guarantee that minimizers are precisely the functions satisfying the logical definition. On image datasets (dSprites, 3D Shapes, 3D Cars, MPI3D in Appendix E), the proposed metrics are further validated against existing models. The variance-based modularity metric (Eq. 19) and mean contraction (Eq. 29) are highlighted as the most practically useful: they are differentiable, require no hyperparameter tuning, and can serve directly as learning objectives.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares its proposed metrics against four existing disentanglement evaluation frameworks: β-VAE (Higgins et al., 2017), FactorVAE (Kim & Mnih, 2018), MIG (Chen et al., 2018), and DCI (Eastwood & Williams, 2018). The key advantage of the proposed metrics is their ability to *isolate* modularity from informativeness: for the 'constant' function (modular but uninformative), the proposed modularity metrics correctly give 1.00 while the contraction metric gives only 0.48–0.90, whereas MIG and DCI-Info both give 0.00 (correctly flagging non-informativeness) but cannot separately confirm modularity. For the 'entanglement' function (neither modular nor informative), the proposed variance-based modularity metric gives 0.75 and mean contraction gives 0.44, providing a fine-grained diagnosis, while β-VAE gives a single conflated score of 0.44. The existing metrics (β-VAE, FactorVAE, MIG) provide only a single scalar that entangles modularity and informativeness, making it impossible to determine which aspect a representation fails on. DCI provides separate Dis/Com/Info scores but requires training regressors with hyperparameter tuning, whereas the proposed contraction and variance metrics are closed-form and differentiable.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the category-theoretical framework of Zhang and Sugiyama (ICML 2023), which identified the product structure underlying modularity and defined informativeness via retractability, by developing a systematic compositional procedure (Table 1) that converts higher-order logical predicates into real-valued metrics. It departs from the prevailing paradigm in disentanglement evaluation—where metrics like β-VAE, FactorVAE, MIG, and DCI are typically introduced alongside specific learning methods without proof that the method optimizes the metric—by deriving metrics directly from logical definitions with a theorem guaranteeing that zero-metric functions are precisely those satisfying the property. The mathematical machinery draws on enriched category theory (replacing the Heyting algebra of truth values with the quantale [0,∞]) and topos theory, but the main text presents only the algebraic homomorphism perspective to remain accessible to ML practitioners.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The compositional conversion provides a principled recipe for generating theoretically grounded, potentially differentiable metrics for any logically defined property of a learning model, which could extend beyond disentanglement to invariance, equivariance, or other structural properties. A key limitation is that the implication conversion (truncated subtraction) sacrifices logical equivalence for continuity, meaning the injectivity metric penalizes non-contractive functions beyond mere non-injectivity. The experiments evaluate metrics on hand-constructed synthetic functions rather than learned representations from real datasets, leaving open whether the theoretical guarantees translate into practical advantages when optimizing these metrics with gradient-based training on high-dimensional data. Future work on handling partial factor annotations, correlated factors, and noisy labels would strengthen the practical applicability.
