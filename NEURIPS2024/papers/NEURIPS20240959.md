## Problem

No existing algorithm for stochastic combinatorial semi-bandits simultaneously achieves covariance-adaptive regret and an O(√T) gap-free bound: prior covariance-adaptive methods (ESCB-C, OLS-UCB) either require a known covariance proxy or incur a Δ_min^{-2} additive constant that blocks gap-free conversion, while sampling-based methods (CTS) carry a Δ_min^{-m} term. Additionally, existing analyses fail to demonstrate the advantage of semi-bandit feedback over simple bandit feedback when the number of actions P is not exponentially larger than the number of items d.

## Input / Output

The algorithms receive as prior information only a known reward bound vector B ∈ R_+^d (no covariance proxy is needed) and the action set A ⊆ {0,1}^d where each action selects at most m items. At each round the player observes individual item rewards Y_{t,i} for all items i in the chosen action (semi-bandit feedback), and the algorithm outputs the next action as a binary vector in {0,1}^d. The theoretical output is a regret upper bound that explicitly depends on the true covariance matrix Σ and the action structure, rather than worst-case constants.

## Method

The paper introduces two algorithms sharing a common estimation backbone. OLS-UCB-C is a deterministic UCB algorithm that estimates covariance coefficients online via a coefficient-wise upper confidence bound (Eq. 4) and constructs a regularized empirical design matrix Z_t incorporating these estimates (Eq. 5); the action is chosen by maximizing the estimated reward plus a bonus involving ‖N_t^{-1}a‖_{Z_t}. This yields closed-form ellipsoidal confidence regions, avoiding the per-round convex optimization required by ESCB-C. COS-V is a Thompson-Sampling-inspired algorithm that samples each item's reward from a 1D Gaussian biased toward the positive orthant (Eq. 7), using only the diagonal (variance) entries of the estimated covariance, and selects the action maximizing the inner product with the sampled vector. The key analytical contribution is a peeling-trick concentration argument (Propositions 1–2) that produces covariance-aware confidence regions without Δ_min^{-2} or Δ_min^{-m} terms, enabling the first covariance-adaptive O(√T) gap-free bound for a deterministic algorithm and the first O(√T) gap-free bound for any sampling-based combinatorial semi-bandit algorithm.

## Results

OLS-UCB-C achieves the first covariance-adaptive gap-free regret bound of $\tilde{O}(\log(m)\sqrt{T}\sqrt{\sum_i \max_{a:\, i\in a}\sigma_{a,i}^2})$ for combinatorial semi-bandits, matching the gap-dependent bound of ESCB-C up to poly-log factors while eliminating the $\Delta_{\min}^{-2}$ additive constant that prevented a gap-free rate. A matching lower bound of $\frac{1}{8}(T\sum_i \max_{a:\, i\in a}\sum_{j\in a}\Sigma_{i,j})^{1/2}$ (Theorem 2) confirms optimality. COS-V, the sampling-based variant, achieves the first $\tilde{O}(\sqrt{T})$ gap-free regret for a Thompson-Sampling-style algorithm: $\tilde{O}(\log(m)\sqrt{T}\sqrt{m\sum_i \Sigma_{i,i}})$, with per-round complexity poly(d) versus $m^2 + Pd^2$ for OLS-UCB-C.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OLS-UCB-C dominates ESCB-C (Perrault et al., 2020b) by achieving the same gap-dependent bound while adding the first gap-free $\tilde{O}(\sqrt{T})$ rate and replacing the per-round convex optimization (cost $C_{1/T}^{\text{opt}}$) with a closed-form ellipsoidal UCB (cost $m^2+Pd^2$). Against CUCB (Kveton et al., 2015), OLS-UCB-C's gap-free bound strictly improves in all covariance structures since $(Tmd)^{1/2}\|B\|_\infty \gtrsim (T\,\text{Tr}(\Sigma))^{1/2}$, and gains an additional $\sqrt{m}$ factor when $\Sigma$ is diagonal. For non-negative correlations ($\Sigma_{i,j}\geq 0$), OLS-UCB-C uniformly improves over UCBV's $(T\sum_a a^\top\Sigma a)^{1/2}$ bound. COS-V matches CTS-Gaussian (Perrault et al., 2020a) in per-round complexity (poly(d)) but removes the need for a known variance-proxy $\Gamma$ and eliminates the $\Delta_{\min}^{-m}$ term, yielding the first gap-free sampling bound. The trade-off is that COS-V only uses diagonal variances $\Sigma_{i,i}$ (not full covariances), making its regret suboptimal relative to OLS-UCB-C when off-diagonal correlations are informative.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the covariance-adaptive combinatorial semi-bandit line initiated by Degenne and Perchet (2016, OLS-UCB) and Perrault et al. (2020b, ESCB-C), both of which required a known covariance-proxy matrix Γ or suffered from a Δ_min^{-2} additive term that blocked a gap-free Õ(√T) bound. For the sampling side, it builds on Wang and Chen (2018) and Perrault et al. (2020a, CTS-Gaussian), whose analyses incurred a Δ_min^{-m} term. The key departure is replacing the axis-realignment technique of ESCB-C with ellipsoidal confidence regions (yielding closed-form UCBs) and introducing a peeling-trick-based concentration argument that simultaneously controls mean and covariance estimation, enabling the first gap-free bounds in both the deterministic and sampling settings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The results close a notable gap in the combinatorial semi-bandit literature by providing the first covariance-adaptive Õ(√T) gap-free regret upper bound (OLS-UCB-C) and the first such bound for a sampling algorithm (COS-V), with a matching lower bound confirming optimality. The finding that semi-bandit feedback consistently outperforms bandit feedback even when P ≤ d challenges the conventional wisdom derived from coarse bounds and has practical implications for settings where the action set is not exponentially large. Open questions include whether negative correlations in Σ can be exploited to further tighten the rate, whether COS-V can be made fully covariance-adaptive (currently variance-only), and whether the computational advantage of COS-V over OLS-UCB-C persists in practice for very large P.
