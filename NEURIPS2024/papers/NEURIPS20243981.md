## Problem

While balanced initialization of GATs improves trainability, the rescale symmetry is broken during training by finite learning rates, momentum, and weight decay, causing the network to topple out of balance and potentially degrade training conditions. It remains unexplored whether maintaining balance throughout training via loss-invariant parameter rescaling can improve GNN training dynamics, and what the optimal training modes are for different graph structures (homophilic vs. heterophilic).

## Input / Output

The method operates on standard graph node classification data: node feature vectors and an adjacency matrix define the input graph, and the output is a class label per node. The rescaling procedure does not alter the input data or network architecture; instead, it manipulates the internal parameterization at the neuron level during optimization, scaling incoming weights, outgoing weights, and attention vectors by complementary factors so that the network's computed function and loss remain exactly invariant while gradient magnitudes are reshaped.

## Method

The paper proves that under the rescale invariance of GATs, scaling a parameter θ by λ scales its gradient by λ⁻¹ (Lemma 2.1), providing degrees of freedom to reshape gradient norms without changing the network function. Building on this, it derives a general iterative procedure to balance a GAT w.r.t. any criterion g(θ, ∇θ) by solving for per-neuron scaling factors λ_i^(l) that satisfy the criterion, applied periodically (e.g., every 10 epochs) during training. The key novel criterion balances relative gradient norms (‖∇L/θ‖²) across incoming weights, attention vectors, and outgoing weights of each neuron, rather than the previously studied weight-norm balance. This enables two practical uses: (i) training in balance to support larger learning rates and improve generalization, particularly on heterophilic graphs, and (ii) deliberately inducing imbalance by scaling down specific layers' weight norms at initialization to control the order in which layers learn, revealing that homophilic graphs benefit from early-layer-focused learning while heterophilic graphs prefer balanced training across all layers.

## Results

On the heterophilic roman-empire dataset, the combined dynamic rescaling (DRC) achieves 67.31% ± 1.49% accuracy, a substantial improvement over the no-rescaling baseline (49.78% ± 2.09%) and the relative-gradient-only variant (54.22% ± 2.34%). On tolokers, DRC reaches 66.42% ± 0.64% ROC AUC versus 64.93% ± 0.80% without rescaling. On the remaining datasets (amazon-ratings, questions, minesweeper), all DR variants perform comparably to the baseline, with DRRG showing slight gains on tolokers and minesweeper. The paper also reports that DRC with a larger learning rate (0.01) further improves generalization on roman-empire, demonstrating a synergistic effect between balanced training and higher learning rates.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against external GNN architectures but instead ablates the dynamic rescaling strategy itself, using no rescaling (w/o DR) as the primary baseline. The combined criterion (DRC, balancing both weight norms and relative gradients) dominates on the most challenging heterophilic dataset roman-empire, improving accuracy by 17.5 percentage points over the baseline (67.31% vs. 49.78%). The relative-gradient-only variant (DRRG) also outperforms the baseline on roman-empire (+4.4 pp) and tolokers (+1.4 pp), while the weight-norm-only variant (DRW) degrades performance on roman-empire (33.07% vs. 49.78%), confirming that balancing parameter norms alone is insufficient. On the remaining four datasets, all variants are statistically indistinguishable from the baseline, suggesting the benefits of dynamic rescaling are most pronounced on heterophilic graphs where multi-layer feature learning is critical. The paper further shows that DRC combined with a larger learning rate (0.01) yields additional gains on roman-empire, a synergy not available to the unbalanced baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the rescale invariance analysis for GATs established by Brody et al. (ICLR 2022) and the balanced initialization framework of Mustafa & Burkholz (NeurIPS 2023) by moving from a one-time initialization procedure to continuous dynamic rescaling during training. It draws on the broader DNN literature on loss-invariant symmetries and conservation laws (Du et al., Neyshabur et al.) and on symmetry teleportation for optimization (Zhao et al.), but is the first to identify and exploit these invariances in the GNN setting, where node-wise neighborhood aggregation makes the rescale structure non-trivial. The paper also connects to the grokking literature (Power et al., Liu et al.) by showing that layer-level learning order control can induce grokking-like behavior, extending the prior narrative that grokking is tied specifically to delayed last-layer learning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first systematic exploration of how rescale invariance can be exploited during GNN training, yielding a practical tool (dynamic rescaling) and, more importantly, conceptual insights: homophilic graphs benefit from early-layer-focused learning while heterophilic graphs benefit from balanced training across layers. These findings offer a mechanistic explanation grounded in NTK alignment with the adjacency matrix and open questions about whether the observed trends generalize beyond GAT/GCN architectures and whether the grokking-like phenomena can be harnessed as a deliberate training strategy. Limitations include the requirement of positively homogeneous activations, the narrow range of learning rates where benefits appear, and the fact that gains on real-world benchmarks are modest (a few percentage points), leaving open whether the technique scales to larger or more diverse graph learning problems.
