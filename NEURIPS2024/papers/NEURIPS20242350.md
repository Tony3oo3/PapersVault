## Problem

Existing LoRA-based fine-tuning of diffusion models requires training a separate LoRA for each domain, since a single LoRA cannot effectively express knowledge of multiple domains. This makes cross-domain generation, style/subject morphing, and domain shift mitigation in UDA and DG tasks parameter-inefficient and unable to produce smooth intermediate domains. The paper asks whether a single low-rank structure can be made to span a continuous parameter manifold across domains.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242350/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

Terra takes a text prompt (e.g., 'A [class]'), a random noise vector, and a continuous time variable t as inputs to the fine-tuned SDXL model (1024×1024 output). For UDA, source images are additionally consumed via DDIM inversion at t=0 and denoised at t=1 to produce domain-transformed images. For DG, t becomes a 2D vector predicted per-sample by a contrastive-learning network, and the model outputs diverse intermediate-domain images by sampling t over a grid. The key unusual aspect is that t is a learned, differentiable control variable that positions generation along a domain manifold rather than a fixed per-domain parameter set.

## Method

Terra modifies the standard LoRA update ΔW = W_up W_down by inserting a time-varying middle matrix K(t) = F(W_mid, t), yielding ΔW(t) = W_up K(t) W_down, where W_mid ∈ R^{r×r} is the only additional parameter (negligible cost since r ≪ min(m,n)). The simplest form is K(t) = tW_mid + I (linear flow), while a cosine-sine variant is used for multi-domain DG to capture bounded, nonlinear manifolds. Training assigns t=0 to source-domain images and t=1 to target-domain images (or sample-level t vectors from a contrastive t-predictor for DG), so a single set of LoRA parameters encodes the entire domain flow. For UDA, the framework generates a transitive source domain by (i) synthesizing target-style images from noise at t=1 and (ii) transforming source images via DDIM inversion at t=0 followed by denoising at t=1, then plugs this expanded domain into any off-the-shelf UDA method. Theoretical analysis (Theorems 1–2) proves Terra can exactly represent two LoRAs with rank k ≤ p+q and provides approximation error bounds no worse than using two separate LoRAs of equal total parameters.

## Results

On the Office-Home UDA benchmark, ELS+Terra achieves 76.26% average transfer accuracy, improving over ELS alone by 4.42% and surpassing all baselines including MCC (72.24%), SDAT (71.85%), and MSGD (72.40%). On VisDA, ELS+Terra reaches 86.86% mean accuracy (+3.46% over ELS). For domain generalization on PACS, SWAD+Terra attains 89.34% average accuracy, outperforming SWAD (88.27%), SAGM (85.23%), and MIRO (84.92%). For generative interpolation, Terra achieves FID 62.25 and PPL 0.95 on image-pair morphing, beating DiffMorpher (FID 78.26) and LoRA Interpolation (FID 89.37), while also uniquely supporting style morphing (FID 187.88, PPL 0.32) and subject morphing (FID 181.85, PPL 0.72).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Terra consistently improves every UDA baseline it is paired with: ELS gains +4.42% on Office-Home and +3.46% on VisDA, while MCC gains +3.21% on Office-Home, both surpassing all standalone baselines (DANN, AFN, CDAN, MDD, SDAT, MSGD). For domain generalization, SWAD+Terra outperforms SWAD by +1.07% on PACS and +1.35% on Office-Home, and SAGM+Terra improves SAGM by +2.57% on PACS; both also beat MIRO and CDGA. Notably, Terra outperforms DomainDiff (which trains a separate LoRA per source domain) on both PACS and Office-Home while using only a single low-rank structure, demonstrating superior parameter efficiency. For generative interpolation, Terra beats DiffMorpher on image-pair morphing (FID 62.25 vs. 78.26) and uniquely supports style and subject morphing where DiffMorpher has no results; Terra also outperforms DGP (FID 223.82) and DDIM (FID 176.34) by large margins. In the ablation comparing Terra against SDXL's prior-knowledge-based data augmentation (Table 12), Terra (76.26%) outperforms even the best SDXL variant with active learning (73.99%), confirming that the time-varying parameter manifold captures domain structure that generic text-prompt generation cannot.

## Relation to Prior Work

Terra directly extends LoRA (Hu et al., ICLR 2022) by inserting a time-varying middle matrix K(t) between the up and down projection matrices, creating a continuous parameter manifold over a single low-rank structure. Conceptually, it inherits the 'domain flow' idea from DLow (Gong et al., CVPR 2019) and MSGD (Xia et al., TPAMI 2023), which use intermediate domains to bridge source-target gaps, but realizes it through a differentiable time parameter rather than explicit generative models. It departs from prior diffusion-based domain adaptation methods like DomainDiff (Miao et al., ICASSP 2024) and CDGA (Hemati et al., 2023), which train separate LoRAs or use the full latent diffusion model per domain, by showing that a single adapter with varying t suffices. The theoretical analysis builds on Zeng & Lee (ICLR 2023) for LoRA expressive power, extending it to the multi-task time-varying setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Terra offers a clean, parameter-efficient plug-in that can be combined with any off-the-shelf UDA or DG method, and the theoretical guarantees (Theorems 1–2) formally justify why one time-varying adapter can replace multiple LoRAs without loss of expressiveness. The consistent 3–4% UDA and 1–2% DG improvements across Office-Home, VisDA, Office31, PACS, and VLCS, validated with multiple baselines and three random seeds, make it a practical tool for cross-domain learning practitioners. Limitations include reliance on access to both source and target images for Terra training, a simple contrastive t-predictor for multi-domain DG, and evaluation limited to classification with ResNet backbones. Open questions include whether the approach scales to dense prediction tasks, larger diffusion models, or settings where target domain images are entirely unavailable.
