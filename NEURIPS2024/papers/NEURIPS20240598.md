## Problem

Existing propagation-based imputation methods for scRNA-seq data construct k-NN graphs that connect only associating (co-occurring) genes, overlooking well-established dissociating (anti-correlated) gene-gene relationships documented in genetic evidence. Additionally, the heterogeneous value distributions across genes cause scale mixing during propagation, degrading recovery accuracy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240598/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-processed scRNA-seq cell-gene matrix (C×G, log-normalized with top-2000 highly variable genes retained) containing dropouts and noise as input, and produces a fully imputed and denoised cell-gene matrix of the same dimensions. The method is entirely parameter-free (no trainable weights), requiring only hyperparameters for k-NN graph construction (k=15 cell-cell, k=2 gene-gene), propagation steps (K=40), and weighted-sum coefficients (α, β, γ). For dropout recovery evaluation, the denoising stage is bypassed (γ=1).

## Method

scCR is a three-stage, parameter-free propagation framework. In the pre-imputation stage, cell-cell Feature Propagation (FP) on a k-NN graph fills zero values via diffusion of nonzero values among similar cells. The complete relationship stage is the key novelty: the pre-imputed matrix is concatenated with its column-wise negation (X, −X), then standardized column-wise so every gene has zero mean and unit variance; a gene-gene k-NN graph is built on this 2G-dimensional standardized space, where dissociating genes (originally negatively correlated) now appear as positively correlated neighbors and are thus connected. Gene-gene FP is performed on this graph, followed by rescaling back to original distributions and a second cell-cell FP. The denoising stage applies Soft FP (which does not preserve observed values) on a cell-cell k-NN graph built from the complete-relationship output, and the final result is a weighted sum of the imputed and denoised matrices. The concatenation trick is the central mechanism: it transforms anti-correlated gene pairs into correlated pairs in the augmented space, enabling the k-NN graph to capture the full spectrum of gene-gene relationships.

## Results

On cell clustering (the primary evaluation), scCR achieves ARI of 0.827 on Baron Mouse (+25.3% over scBFP), 0.902 on Zeisel (+6.4% over scFP), and 0.823 on Baron Human (+21.6% over scBFP). On dropout recovery (RMSE), scCR reduces error by up to 74% over scBFP on Worm Neuron (0.049 vs. 0.190) and 34% on Mouse Bladder (0.170 vs. 0.374) under MNAR settings. scCR also achieves the lowest running time across all datasets regardless of cell count, and requires no trainable parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

scCR outperforms the most competitive propagation-based baseline scBFP on four of six datasets in ARI: Baron Mouse (0.827 vs. 0.660, +25.3%), Zeisel (0.902 vs. 0.835, +8.0%), Baron Human (0.823 vs. 0.677, +21.6%), and Mouse Bladder (0.704 vs. 0.694, +1.7%). However, scCR underperforms scBFP on Pancreas (0.812 vs. 0.864) and Worm Neuron (0.520 vs. 0.608). Against scFP, scCR wins on Baron Mouse, Mouse Bladder, Zeisel, and Baron Human but loses on Pancreas and Worm Neuron. All GNN-based baselines (scTAG, scGNN 2.0, scGCL) and non-graph-based methods (DCA, AutoClass) are consistently outperformed by scCR across all six datasets, with gaps of 10–40 ARI points. MAGIC, the earliest propagation-based method, is also uniformly outperformed. In dropout recovery under realistic MNAR settings, scCR achieves the lowest RMSE on all six datasets, with the largest margin on Worm Neuron (0.049 vs. 0.190 for scBFP, a 74% reduction).

## Relation to Prior Work

scCR extends the propagation-based imputation lineage initiated by Feature Propagation (FP) [Rossi et al., 2022] and its scRNA-seq adaptations scFP [Yun et al., 2023] and scBFP [Lee et al., 2024]. The key departure is that prior methods construct gene-gene k-NN graphs based solely on cosine similarity, which captures only associating (co-occurring) gene pairs while ignoring dissociating (mutually exclusive) relationships documented in genetic evidence. scCR addresses this by concatenating the cell-gene matrix with its negation before graph construction, and adds column-wise standardization to prevent scale-mixing during cross-gene propagation. The denoising stage reuses Soft FP from scFP, making scCR a direct architectural extension of the bi-level FP framework with two targeted modifications.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a simple negation-concatenation trick, grounded in well-known genetic evidence about dissociating gene relationships, yields substantial and consistent improvements (up to 25.3% ARI) across six diverse scRNA-seq datasets without any trainable parameters. This validates that negative gene-gene correlations carry imputation-relevant signal that prior propagation methods systematically discard. The method's parameter-free, fast, and memory-efficient nature makes it practically attractive for large-scale single-cell atlases. However, the approach is specifically designed for scRNA-seq data, and the authors note but do not explore extension to general tabular imputation where positive and negative channel correlations also exist. An open question is whether the concatenation strategy generalizes to other domains with mixed-sign correlations, and whether the fixed hyperparameters (α=0.05, β=0.99, γ=0.01, k=2) remain optimal at much larger scale.
