## Problem

In multi-armed bandits with graph feedback, the independence number α(G) fully characterizes the optimal regret, but for the contextual counterpart only a looser upper bound involving the MAS number m(G) was known, with no matching lower bound for general graphs. It remained open whether the shift from α(G) to m(G) is genuinely driven by the number of contexts M, and what the precise dependence on M is for intermediate values.

## Input / Output

The learner receives a context c_t ∈ [M] each round, selects an action a_t ∈ [K], and observes rewards for all actions in the out-neighborhood of a_t under every context simultaneously (complete cross-learning). The feedback graph G is a strongly observable directed graph over [K], and the output is a policy whose minimax regret is characterized by β_M(G), a new graph quantity that equals α(G) at M=1 and m(G) at M≥m(G), with the regret scaling as Õ(√(β_M(G)T)) for self-avoiding contexts and Õ(√(min{β̄_M(G), m(G)}·T)) for general contexts.

## Method

The paper introduces β_M(G), defined as the maximum total size of M disjoint independent sets I_1,…,I_M with no edges from I_i to I_j for i<j, which interpolates between α(G) and m(G) as M grows. The lower bound is proved via a hard instance where the best action under context c is uniform over I_c, combined with a delicate exploration-exploitation tradeoff showing that pure exploration outside I_c incurs too much regret when T≥β_M(G)³. For upper bounds, the paper reduces exploration to two purely combinatorial sequential games on G: Game I (batch selection per context, tight for self-avoiding contexts with minimax value Θ(β_M(G))) and Game II (single-action selection per round, for general contexts). The final algorithms use arm elimination with a layering technique, where each layer solves the corresponding sequential game to choose which actions to explore, yielding polynomial-time policies that match the lower bound for self-avoiding contexts and achieve near-optimal regret for transitively closed or undirected graphs.

## Results

The paper establishes a minimax lower bound R_T^*(G,M) = Ω(√(β_M(G)·T)) for T ≥ β_M(G)³, where β_M(G) is a new graph-theoretic quantity that interpolates between the independence number α(G) (when M=1) and the MAS number m(G) (when M ≥ m(G)). Matching upper bounds Õ(√(β_M(G)·T)) are achieved for self-avoiding context sequences (Theorem 1.3) and for undirected or transitively closed feedback graphs (Corollary 1.5). For general context sequences, the upper bound is Õ(√(min{β̄_M(G), m(G)}·T)), with a gap to the lower bound bounded by a factor of max{ρ(G)/M, 1} (Lemma 4.1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's lower bound Ω(√(β_M(G)·T)) strictly improves upon the prior lower bound Ω(√(α(G)·T)) from Dann et al. (2020), which only matched the multi-armed bandit rate regardless of M. The key advance is showing that the gap between α(G) and m(G) is not an artifact of the upper bound analysis but is fundamental: when M ≥ m(G), the regret is provably Ω(√(m(G)·T)), confirming that the MAS number (not the independence number) characterizes the statistical complexity in the many-contexts regime. For self-avoiding contexts and transitively closed graphs, the paper closes the gap entirely, achieving tight Õ(√(β_M(G)·T)) bounds that improve upon Han et al. (2024)'s Õ(√(min{α(G)·M, K}·T)) by exploiting the graph structure more finely. For general contexts, a gap remains between the lower bound β_M(G) and the upper bound min{β̄_M(G), m(G)}, bounded by a factor of max{ρ(G)/M, 1}, which vanishes when M is large or the graph has short paths.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the well-characterized multi-armed bandit with graph feedback setting (where the independence number α(G) determines optimal regret, per Alon et al. 2015) to the contextual bandit setting with complete cross-learning across contexts. It builds directly on the framework of Han et al. (2024) and Dann et al. (2020), who established loose upper bounds involving the MAS number m(G) but could not prove matching lower bounds. The paper introduces a new graph-theoretic quantity β_M(G) that interpolates between α(G) and m(G), and uses sequential games on graphs combined with the arm-elimination and layering techniques from Lykouris et al. (2020) to achieve tight regret characterizations for important graph and context classes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central finding—that the MAS number, not the independence number, characterizes the statistical complexity of contextual bandits with graph feedback when the number of contexts is large—resolves a long-standing gap between upper and lower bounds in this setting and partially answers an open problem in Hao et al. (2022) about the dependence on the number of contexts. The tight characterization for self-avoiding contexts and transitively closed graphs (which arise in auctions and inventory control) provides actionable guidance for practitioners in these domains. Open questions remain for general context sequences (where a gap between β_M(G) and β̄_M(G) persists), for small time horizons T < β_M(G)³, and for whether the UCB algorithm's m(G) dependence is fundamental rather than an artifact of the analysis.
