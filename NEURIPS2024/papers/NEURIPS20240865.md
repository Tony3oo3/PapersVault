## Problem

Scaling reinforcement learning to multiple embodied tasks is hindered by the need for hand-crafted reward functions per task, while vision-language models that could specify tasks naturally require fine-tuning or domain-specific adaptations that are infeasible when multimodal (vision-language) data is unavailable in embodied domains. The paper asks how to leverage pretrained foundation VLMs for multi-task generalization in embodied RL without any language annotations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240865/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The agent receives 64×64×3 RGB observations from the environment and a task specification given either as a free-form language prompt or a short video clip (8 frames for InternVideo2). It outputs continuous control actions in [-1,1] via a learned actor-critic policy. Notably, no reward signal is required during training: rewards are computed internally as cosine similarity between the policy's imagined latent states and prompt-derived latent targets, enabling a fully data-free mode where even the pre-training dataset is unnecessary for learning new tasks.

## Method

GenRL builds a multimodal-foundation world model (MFWM) by connecting a frozen pretrained video-language model (InternVideo2) to a DreamerV3-style generative world model using two lightweight modules trained on vision-only data: a GRU-based connector that maps VLM embeddings to sequences of discrete latent states (trained via KL divergence to the world model encoder), and a U-Net aligner that warps language embeddings into the visual embedding neighborhood to bridge the multimodality gap without any text annotations. Policy learning is formulated as trajectory matching in latent space: the actor-critic maximizes a reward defined as cosine similarity between linear projections of dynamically predicted states and prompt-derived target states, with a best-matching-trajectory technique to handle temporal misalignment between the agent's current state and the prompt's implied starting state. After pre-training the MFWM on a large mixed dataset, new tasks can be learned entirely in imagination by sampling initial latent states internally, requiring no dataset access (data-free policy learning).

## Results

On the in-distribution language-to-action benchmark (Table 1), GenRL achieves an overall rescaled episodic reward of 0.80±0.02 using a video-language VLM (InternVideo2), substantially outperforming the best model-free baseline TD3 (0.41±0.05) and the model-based WM-CLIP baseline (0.70±0.04). GenRL's advantage is most pronounced on dynamic locomotion tasks: quadruped walk (0.93 vs. 0.28 for TD3), quadruped run (0.86 vs. 0.26), and cheetah run (0.74 vs. 0.37). In the data-free policy learning setting (Figure 6), GenRL retains performance close to its offline variant while eliminating all data dependencies, converging in under 30 minutes per task.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GenRL (0.80 overall) decisively outperforms all three model-free offline RL baselines in the video-language setting: IQL (0.29), TD3+BC (0.24), and TD3 (0.41), with the largest margins on dynamic locomotion tasks (e.g., quadruped walk: 0.93 vs. 0.28 for TD3; cheetah run: 0.74 vs. 0.37). The model-based WM-CLIP baseline (0.70), which learns a 'reversed connector' to predict VLM embeddings from world-model states, is the closest competitor but still trails GenRL by 0.10 overall, particularly on dynamic tasks where GenRL's direct latent-space trajectory matching provides a clearer learning signal. In the kitchen manipulation domain, WM-CLIP occasionally matches or slightly exceeds GenRL on static tasks (e.g., kitchen burner: 0.78 vs. 0.62), which the authors attribute to GenRL's target sequences being slightly in motion even for static prompts. On out-of-distribution generalization tasks (Figure 4), GenRL's advantage over model-free baselines widens further, approaching specialized-agent performance in the quadruped and cheetah domains, while IQL and TD3+BC degrade most due to their conservative imitation objectives being ill-suited for unseen behaviors.

## Relation to Prior Work

GenRL builds directly on the DreamerV3 world model architecture (GRU-based sequence model, discrete latent states, actor-critic training in imagination) and the InternVideo2 video-language foundation model, but introduces a novel 'multimodal-foundation world model' (MFWM) that connects and aligns the VLM's embedding space with the world model's latent space using only vision data. This departs from prior VLM-for-RL work (Baumli et al., Rocamonde et al.) that uses VLMs as external reward functions via cosine similarity, and from prior world model work that operates entirely within its own learned representation. The connector-aligner mechanism is the conceptual inverse of the 'reversed connector' baseline (WM-CLIP), which predicts VLM embeddings from world model states rather than the other way around.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The data-free policy learning paradigm—where an agent can learn entirely new tasks after pre-training without accessing any dataset—is a significant step toward foundation models for embodied RL, mirroring how LLMs and VLMs generalize to new tasks without retraining on task-specific data. Key limitations include reconstruction quality degrading for complex observations (demonstrated in Minecraft), continued dependence on prompt tuning, and the need for some expert data during pre-training for complex behaviors. Open questions include whether the GRU-based connector can be replaced with transformers or diffusion models for better reconstruction, how to handle temporal horizons beyond the VLM's comprehension window, and whether the approach scales to precise manipulation tasks where exploration data is less informative.
