## Problem

Existing vision encoder models (ViTs) excel at image recognition but cannot simultaneously perform text recognition like humans do. Prior solutions either retrain ViTs exclusively on OCR (discarding image encoding capability) or ensemble separate image and text models (doubling computational cost), leaving no single model that handles both without prior knowledge of input type.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243882/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts natural images at low resolution (e.g., 224×224) and document images at high resolution (e.g., 896×896, 4× the base scale) as RGB inputs, using Cropped Position Embedding to interpolate positional encodings for variable-length patch sequences. It produces visual feature tokens from the ViT backbone, auto-regressive text sequences (OCR or captions) from the language decoder, and reconstructed visual features from the vision decoder. Critically, the original ViT architecture is retained, so inference cost and deployment footprint remain unchanged from the base encoder.

## Method

UNIT upgrades a pre-trained OpenCLIP ViT-H by attaching a lightweight OPT-125M language decoder (projected via a Q-Former input embedding layer) for auto-regressive text prediction and a two-layer MLP vision decoder for token-wise feature reconstruction against the frozen teacher ViT, preventing catastrophic forgetting. Training proceeds in two stages: intra-scale pretraining jointly optimizes OCR loss on high-resolution documents, image captioning loss on low-resolution images, and a weighted cosine+L1 feature reconstruction loss; inter-scale finetuning then swaps resolutions (high-res images, low-res documents) to build scale robustness, with random feature sampling to prevent gradient overflow at higher token counts. The multi-task objective (λ=2 for reconstruction) preserves CLIP alignment for zero-shot classification while the language decoder captures fine-grained sequential and spatial information needed for dense text recognition.

## Results

On document-level OCR, UNIT achieves 95.33 F1 on SYN-L-val (dense small-font documents) and 67.14 F1 on FUNSD, substantially outperforming all baselines except Nougat on markdown conversion (78.50 vs 86.71 F1 on MD-val). On image recognition, UNIT preserves and slightly exceeds its teacher model RADIO-H, reaching 78.76% zero-shot ImageNet-1K accuracy, 84.18% kNN accuracy, and 50.19 mIoU on ADE20K segmentation. When integrated into LVLMs, UNIT achieves 43.0 on DocQA and 46.0 on ChartQA (vs. 18.2 and 20.2 for RADIO-H) while maintaining comparable image understanding scores (72.3 VQAv2, 60.4 GQA).

## Baselines & Comparisons

UNIT dominates all four OCR benchmarks (FUNSD, SROIE, CORD, SYN-L-val) over every baseline, with the largest margins against Donut (e.g., 67.14 vs 9.08 on FUNSD) and RADIO (e.g., 58.87 vs 10.01 on CORD). The only loss is on markdown conversion (MD-val), where Nougat's 86.71 F1 exceeds UNIT's 78.50, which the authors attribute to Nougat being exhaustively trained on markdown data. On image recognition (Table 2), UNIT marginally outperforms its teacher RADIO-H (78.76 vs 78.62 zero-shot, 50.19 vs 49.01 segmentation mIoU) and all other compared encoders (OpenCLIP-H, E-RADIO, NFNet, MaxViT, etc.), confirming that text recognition training does not degrade image encoding. In downstream LVLM evaluation (Table 6), UNIT achieves 43.0 DocQA and 46.0 ChartQA, roughly 2.4× the scores of RADIO-H (18.2, 20.2) and 3× those of CLIP-L (22.3, 23.6), while image understanding scores (VQAv2 72.3, GQA 60.4) remain within 1–1.5 points of RADIO-H. In the high-resolution grid-slicing setting (Table 7), UNIT leads SigLIP by 4.5 points on ChartQA (61.0 vs 56.5) and 3.5 on DocVQA (65.5 vs 62.0), and surpasses CLIP-L by 9.0 on ChartQA (61.0 vs 52.0), while matching or slightly exceeding both on GQA and OKVQA.

## Relation to Prior Work

UNIT departs from two dominant paradigms for document understanding: (1) document-specific OCR models like Nougat and Donut that retrain ViTs exclusively on text recognition, discarding the original image encoding capability, and (2) ensemble approaches like Vary and RADIO that concatenate features from separately trained image and text encoders, doubling computational cost. Instead, UNIT upgrades a pre-trained OpenCLIP ViT-H in-place by attaching a lightweight OPT-125M language decoder (via a Q-Former input embedding) for autoregressive text prediction and a two-layer MLP vision decoder for token-wise feature reconstruction against the original encoder as a teacher. The two-stage multi-scale training (intra-scale at conventional resolutions, then inter-scale with exchanged resolutions) and the Cropped Position Embedding augmentation for handling variable input sizes are the key methodological departures from prior single-resolution ViT training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

UNIT offers a practical, cost-free path to adding text recognition to any pre-trained ViT without architectural changes or increased inference latency, which is directly relevant to the growing ecosystem of LVLMs that need document understanding. The inter-scale finetuning stage addresses a real gap: without it, the model catastrophically fails at non-standard resolutions (0.40% zero-shot accuracy at exchanged resolution), showing that scale robustness is not a free byproduct of multi-task training. Open questions include generalization to non-English scripts, highly irregular layouts (handwritten, multi-column, rotated text), and whether the approach extends to video or 3D document understanding. The paper acknowledges a trade-off: UNIT still trails Nougat on the highly specialized markdown conversion task, suggesting that unified models may not match dedicated ones on narrow sub-tasks.
