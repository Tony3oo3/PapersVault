## Problem

While OGDA achieves an O(1/√T) last-iterate convergence rate in two-player zero-sum games with no dependence on game-specific constants beyond dimension and smoothness, the best existing last-iterate bound for OMWU depends on a game-dependent constant that can be arbitrarily large. This paper resolves the open question of whether this gap is an inherent limitation of OMWU or merely a loose analysis, showing it is the former: no game-independent last-iterate convergence rate exists for OMWU or more broadly for any non-forgetful OFTRL algorithm.

## Input / Output

The analysis operates on a parameterized 2×2 zero-sum game A_δ = [[1/2+δ, 1/2], [0, 1]] whose unique Nash equilibrium places one player O(δ) from the simplex boundary, and on per-iteration loss vectors (gradients) derived from the current opponent's strategy. The algorithm outputs mixed strategy profiles (x^t, y^t) at each iteration, and the key quantity measured is the duality gap DualityGap(x^T, y^T) = max_y x^T A y − min_x x'^T A y at the last iterate. The negative result shows that for any δ > 0, there exists an iteration t ≥ Ω(1/δ) where the duality gap remains a positive constant c_2, regardless of the step size (as long as η ≤ 1/(4L)).

## Method

The paper identifies a broad family of Optimistic FTRL algorithms that are 'non-forgetful'—they base decisions on cumulative loss differences E^t rather than recent gradients—and proves impossibility results for all of them. The core mechanism is a three-stage argument on the hard game A_δ: (I) the x-player's strategy x[1] increases past the equilibrium threshold 1/(1+δ) while y[1] drops below 1/2 − c_1; (II) y[1] must climb back at rate at most ηLδ per step (since e_y^t ≥ −δ always), requiring Ω(1/δ) iterations, during which the x-player's cumulative loss E_x drops by Ω(1/δ); (III) because OFTRL uses cumulative losses, x[1] remains near 1 for another Ω(1/δ) steps even though the current gradient favors the other action, allowing y[1] to overshoot past 1/2 + c_2 and producing a constant duality gap. The proof verifies that the required regularizer conditions hold for negative entropy (OMWU), squared Euclidean norm, log barrier, and Tsallis entropy regularizers, and extends the result to 2n×2n games via a loss-duplication equivalence argument.

## Results

The paper proves an impossibility result: for any δ > 0, there exists a 2×2 two-player zero-sum game A_δ such that OFTRL (including OMWU) maintains a duality gap of at least a constant c₂ > 0 even after Ω(1/δ) iterations (Theorem 1). Consequently, no function f(d₁, d₂, T) that vanishes as T → ∞ can uniformly bound the last-iterate duality gap of OFTRL across all games (Theorem 2). In stark contrast, OGDA achieves an O(1/√T) last-iterate convergence rate depending only on dimension and smoothness. The negative result extends to 2n × 2n games for all regularizers tested (negative entropy, squared Euclidean norm, log barrier, and Tsallis entropy).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The central comparison is between OFTRL (and its most prominent instantiation, OMWU) and OGDA. OGDA, as analyzed by Cai et al. (2022) and Gorbunov et al. (2022), achieves an O(1/√T) last-iterate duality gap with dependence only on the number of actions and the smoothness of the payoff matrix. This paper proves that no analogous game-independent rate is possible for OFTRL: on the hard 2×2 game A_δ, OFTRL's duality gap remains a positive constant c₂ even after Ω(1/δ) iterations, and the required number of iterations grows without bound as δ → 0. The paper also connects this to the practical observation in extensive-form game solving that regret matching+ (equivalent to OGD) dramatically outperforms classical regret matching (equivalent to FTRL), confirming that the lack of forgetfulness in FTRL-type algorithms is the root cause of slow last-iterate convergence rather than a limitation of prior analyses.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of the last-iterate convergence literature for online learning in games and the FTRL-vs-OMD distinction. It builds on Wei et al. (2021), who first showed OMWU's last-iterate rate depends on a game-dependent condition number, by proving this dependence is inherent rather than an artifact of loose analysis. The result also formalizes and explains the empirical observation from extensive-form game solving that regret-matching+ (equivalent to OGD) dramatically outperforms regret matching (equivalent to FTRL) [Farina et al., 2021; Flaspohler et al., 2021], and connects to the mean-based learning framework of Braverman et al. (2018) that identifies exploitability of non-forgetful algorithms.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This negative result resolves a fundamental open question: OMWU and more generally OFTRL cannot achieve any game-independent last-iterate convergence rate, no matter how sophisticated the analysis. This has direct practical implications for algorithm selection in game solving, explaining why OMD-based methods (OGDA) are preferred over FTRL-based methods (OMWU) when last-iterate quality matters. Open questions include whether the impossibility extends to best-iterate convergence, whether dynamic step sizes can circumvent the result (numerical evidence suggests not), and whether a general formal condition for 'forgetfulness' can be identified that cleanly separates fast from slow last-iterate convergence.
