## Problem

Aligning LLMs to simultaneously satisfy multiple criteria (e.g., helpfulness and safety) requires solving a constrained policy optimization problem, but existing methods like Safe RLHF demand complex PPO-Lagrangian training with separate reward and safety models, making them unstable, computationally expensive, and inflexible in the choice of alignment algorithms or dataset formats. No prior work provides a theoretically justified way to decompose this joint optimization into independent, sequential alignment steps using simple RL-free algorithms.

## Input / Output

SACPO takes a reference LM policy (the SFT model), a reward preference dataset D_r, and a safety preference dataset D_g, where the two datasets need not share prompts and can each be either paired (for DPO) or unpaired (for KTO). The method outputs a single aligned LM policy parameterized by the same architecture as the reference, which generates text responses balancing both metrics. A key practical detail is that the safety realignment step uses a modified KL penalty β/λ* rather than β, and the reference policy in the loss is the reward-aligned model rather than the original SFT model.

## Method

The core theoretical result (Theorem 1) shows that the optimal policy for the constrained problem max R(π,β) s.t. G(π)≥b can be written as a reweighting of the reward-aligned Gibbs policy π_r* by exp(λ*g*/β), meaning the optimal policy is obtained by sequentially aligning first with reward and then with safety. This justifies using any standard RL-free alignment algorithm (DPO, KTO, IPO) independently at each step, with the only modification being that the safety step uses the reward-aligned policy as reference and β/λ* as the KL coefficient. The practical variant P-SACPO avoids the difficulty of estimating λ* during training by instead merging the reward-aligned model with a conservatively safety-realigned model (using a large λ̄ > λ*) via linear weight averaging at a mixing ratio q, effectively interpolating between the two extremes to approximate the optimal trade-off without re-optimizing.

## Results

SACPO variants (DPO(H)→DPO(S), KTO(H)→DPO(S)) achieve higher win rates against the SFT model on both helpfulness (AlpacaEval prompts) and harmlessness (Safe RLHF red-teaming prompts) compared to Safe RLHF (beaver-7b-v1.0/v2.0/v3.0). The best SACPO configuration (KTO(H)→DPO(S)) demonstrates that mixing different alignment algorithms per step is effective, while DPO(H)→KTO(S) underperforms, suggesting KTO is less suitable for safety alignment. P-SACPO (linear model merging with q∈{0.25, 0.5, 0.75}) outperforms naive weight averaging of DPO(H) and DPO(S) models, confirming that the stepwise structure matters. Varying β/λ allows fine-tuning the helpfulness–safety trade-off along a near-Pareto front.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Safe RLHF (beaver-7b-v1.0, v2.0, v3.0), all SACPO variants that use DPO for the safety step (DPO(H)→DPO(S), KTO(H)→DPO(S)) achieve higher win rates on both helpfulness and harmlessness, despite operating under less favorable data conditions (v1.0-level data vs. the additional iterations used for v2.0/v3.0). The SFT model (Alpaca-7B) serves as the reference baseline; SACPO consistently improves over it on both metrics, with the first alignment step boosting helpfulness and the second boosting harmlessness at only a slight helpfulness cost. P-SACPO (model merging) outperforms the naive baseline of simply averaging DPO(H) and DPO(S) weights, validating that the stepwise realignment structure (using π_r as reference for safety alignment) is essential rather than just combining two independently aligned models. The DPO(H)→KTO(S) variant is the one SACPO configuration that underperforms, losing to DPO(H)→DPO(S) on harmlessness, indicating algorithm choice per step matters.

## Relation to Prior Work

SACPO builds directly on the RL-free alignment algorithms DPO and KTO, which analytically derive the optimal Gibbs policy under a reverse KL constraint, and on Safe RLHF's formulation of LLM alignment as a constrained policy optimization problem. The key departure from Safe RLHF (which requires PPO-Lagrangian and jointly trained reward/safety models) and from MODPO/C-DPO (which require weighted-sum objectives and joint datasets) is the theoretical result that the optimal constrained policy factors as a reward-aligned policy reweighted by the safety function, enabling sequential application of off-the-shelf alignment algorithms. The P-SACPO variant further draws on model merging (Wortsman et al., 2022) to approximate the optimal Lagrangian multiplier without iterative policy re-optimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SACPO offers a practically simpler and more flexible pipeline for constrained LLM alignment: practitioners can mix different algorithms (DPO vs. KTO) and dataset formats (paired vs. unpaired) per metric without architectural changes, and P-SACPO's model-merging trick avoids the instability of online λ optimization. However, the empirical validation is limited to a single 7B model and one preference dataset, and the theoretical guarantees rely on linear realizability assumptions that may not hold for modern LLMs. Open questions include whether the stepwise decomposition holds at scale (70B+), how to handle multiple interacting safety constraints in practice, and why alignment order produces non-trivial performance gaps despite the commutative law in theory.
