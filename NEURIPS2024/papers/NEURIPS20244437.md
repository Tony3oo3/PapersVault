key_contribution: Proposes OneRef, a minimalist one-tower referring framework on BEiT-3's modality-shared transformer, and introduces Mask Referring Modeling (MRefM) — a pre-training paradigm combining referring-aware mask image modeling (reconstructing visual tokens plus 4D target-relation scores with dynamic region-aware masking) and referring-aware mask language modeling (reconstructing text tokens plus semantic target-relation scores) — that eliminates the need for cross-modal fusion decoders, interaction adapters, and special grounding tokens while achieving SoTA on REC, PG, and RES across five datasets.
limitations: The unsupervised pre-training setting relies on approximate FH-algorithm regions and a teacher model for region selection, and has not been scaled to truly large-scale data; the method is limited to 2D image referring tasks and depends on the BEiT-3 backbone, whose ecosystem is less mature than CLIP-based alternatives. The paper notes that large-scale unsupervised MRefM pre-training remains a future direction.

surprise_factor: The conceptual surprise is that eliminating all cross-modal fusion decoders, interaction adapters, and special grounding tokens — components the field has spent years engineering — yields *better* results than architectures that include them, provided the pre-training properly models referring relations. This is well-supported across three training settings, five datasets, and two model scales, and the generality study on DETR/CLIP backbones adds credibility. However, the result is not entirely unexpected given the unified feature space argument, so the surprise is moderate rather than paradigm-shifting.

## Problem

Existing visual grounding and referring segmentation methods rely on separate modality encoders, necessitating bulky cross-modal Transformer fusion en-/decoders and a variety of carefully designed early-stage interaction modules to bridge the visual and linguistic feature spaces. Simultaneously, the standard mask vision language modeling (MVLM) paradigm used in pre-training lacks fine-grained cross-modal referring capability, failing to model the nuanced referential relationship between image regions and text expressions needed for regression-based grounding tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20244437/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image (resized to 384×384 with long-edge alignment and short-edge padding) and a referring expression text (padded to a fixed length of 64 tokens with [SEP] and [EOS] markers) as input. For REC/PG tasks, it outputs a single bounding box (center coordinates and width/height); for RES, it outputs a full-resolution segmentation mask (H×W) produced by a 3-layer deconvolution followed by bilinear interpolation. Notably, no special grounding tokens, query anchors, or external fusion modules are required at inference time.

## Method

OneRef builds on BEiT-3's modality-shared one-tower Transformer, unifying visual and linguistic features in a single shared feature space and eliminating the need for separate encoders, cross-modal fusion decoders, and special grounding tokens. The key pre-training contribution is Mask Referring Modeling (MRefM), which augments standard MIM and MLM with cross-modal referring objectives: Referring MIM reconstructs both masked visual tokens and a 4-dimensional visual target-relation score (normalized x/y distances and w/h proportions relative to the referred region) conditioned on the dot product with the aggregated text token, while Referring MLM reconstructs masked text tokens and semantic target-relation scores conditioned on the dot product with the aggregated visual token. A referring-aware dynamic masking strategy applies a high mask ratio (γ=0.75) within the referred region and a low ratio (β=0.35) outside, directing reconstruction attention to the target area. At transfer time, the REC head computes a softmax-normalized similarity mask between visual tokens and the aggregated language token, then regresses the box via a 3-layer MLP; the RES head upsamples visual tokens via deconvolution and uses cosine similarity with the language token as the predicted mask, with focal and Dice losses providing supervision.

## Results

On the single-dataset fine-tuning setting (Table 1), OneRef-B achieves 85.34% IoU on RefCOCO testB, 74.26% on RefCOCO+ testB, 83.52% on RefCOCOg test, 77.17% on ReferIt, and 83.61% on Flickr30k, surpassing the previous SoTA HiVG-B by 2.07%, 6.15%, 4.73%, 1.95%, and 1.50% respectively. The larger OneRef-L model further improves to 90.19% (RefCOCO testB), 83.73% (RefCOCO+ testB), and 89.29% (RefCOCOg test). In the dataset-mixed pre-training setting (Table 2), OneRef-B† reaches 88.58% on RefCOCO testB and 87.32% on RefCOCOg test, outperforming Grounding-DINO-B† by 2.59% and 2.38%, and even surpassing the 4B-parameter LION-4B† GMLLM by 3.76% and 1.69%. For referring segmentation (Table 3), OneRef-B achieves 75.11% mIoU on RefCOCO testB and 69.70% on RefCOCOg test, beating RISCLIP-B by 2.65% and 1.73%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OneRef-B outperforms the previous SoTA HiVG-B [92] across all five datasets in the single-dataset fine-tuning setting, with the largest gains on RefCOCO+ testB (+6.15%) and RefCOCOg test (+4.73%), indicating stronger handling of complex/ambiguous expressions. Compared to the traditional detector-based TransVG++ [15], OneRef-B leads by 4.37% (RefCOCO testB) to 7.98% (RefCOCO+ testB), demonstrating the advantage of the unified one-tower architecture over modality-dependent encoders with fusion decoders. In the dataset-mixed pre-training setting, OneRef-B† surpasses Grounding-DINO-B† [55] by 2.59% (RefCOCO testB) and 2.38% (RefCOCOg test), and OFA-B† [85] by 5.28% and 5.01%, while also beating the 4B-parameter LION-4B† [9] GMLLM by 3.76% (RefCOCO testB) and 1.69% (RefCOCOg test) with a model 20–60× smaller. For referring segmentation, OneRef-B exceeds RISCLIP-B [35] by 2.65% (RefCOCO testB) and 1.73% (RefCOCOg test) in the single-dataset setting, and by 4.53% and 5.39% in the dataset-mixed setting. The generality study (Table 6) further shows that applying MRefM to TransVG and CLIP-VG backbones yields ~2% average improvement, confirming the paradigm's transferability beyond the BEiT-3 architecture.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on BEiT-3's modality-shared one-tower transformer (with MoE heads) and extends its standard Mask Vision Language Modeling (MVLM) pre-training into a novel Mask Referring Modeling (MRefM) paradigm that explicitly reconstructs cross-modal referring content alongside unimodal content. It departs fundamentally from the dominant two-tower architecture (separate visual/language encoders + cross-modal fusion decoder + special grounding tokens) used by TransVG, MDETR, Grounding-DINO, and CLIP-based methods, arguing that a unified feature space with proper referring-aware pre-training obviates the need for elaborate interaction modules, fusion Transformers, and query anchors. The referring-aware dynamic masking strategy also distinguishes it from prior masked modeling work (MAE, BEiT, R-MAE, SemMAE) by conditioning mask placement on the referred region rather than using fixed-ratio random or semantic-guided schemes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that architectural simplicity (one-tower, no fusion decoder, no special tokens) can outperform substantially more complex multi-tower grounding/segmentation systems by 2–7% across five datasets, challenging the field's assumption that elaborate cross-modal interaction modules are necessary. The generality study showing MRefM transfers to DETR and CLIP backbones (~2% gain) suggests the paradigm is not tied to BEiT-3 specifically. Open questions include whether MRefM can be scaled to truly large-scale unsupervised pre-training (the paper only demonstrates a small unsupervised setting using FH regions), whether the approach extends to video or 3D referring, and whether the BEiT-3 dependency limits adoption given its less widespread ecosystem compared to CLIP.
