## Problem

Parameter sharing in cooperative MARL causes agents to converge on similar behaviors, reducing exploration diversity. Existing mutual-information-based methods (e.g., MAVEN, EOI, SCDS) that maximize MI between trajectories and fixed agent identities via variational inference force agents to revisit known trajectories where identity information is already high, leading to overfitting and inefficient exploration rather than genuine discovery of new behaviors.

## Input / Output

The CTR encoder takes the concatenation of an agent's partial observation at time t and its previous one-hot action as input, producing a latent embedding that is passed through a GRU autoregressive model to yield a trajectory representation of dimension H. This trajectory representation replaces the standard RNN hidden state in the agent utility network (QMIX) or actor network (MAPPO) for action decisions. Additionally, a learnable identity vector of the same dimension H is maintained per agent and trained jointly; it is not a fixed one-hot encoding but a free parameter optimized by the contrastive loss.

## Method

CTR learns distinguishable trajectory representations by maximizing mutual information between trajectory representations and learnable per-agent identity vectors using an InfoNCE contrastive loss, rather than the variational lower bounds used by prior MI-based methods. The key architectural insight is that identity representations are trainable parameters (not fixed one-hot labels) that act as linear classifiers on a representation hypersphere, pulling same-agent trajectories together while pushing different-agent trajectories apart. To address the small dataset size (only |A| samples per timestep), the authors extend the contrastive loss to a multi-agent variant that increases negative samples from O(|A|) to O(|A|²) by cross-pairing all trajectory representations against all identity representations. The total training objective combines the standard RL loss (TD loss for QMIX, PPO objective for MAPPO) with the contrastive loss weighted by α, trained end-to-end in a centralized manner while preserving the IGM rule of value decomposition.

## Results

CTR+QMIX achieves the highest win rate across all 10 evaluated scenarios (Pac-Men, 6 SMAC, 3 SMACv2). In the super hard SMAC scenarios where agents face stronger enemies, CTR+QMIX reaches 0.82 (6h_vs_8z), 0.79 (corridor), and 0.82 (3s5z_vs_3s6z), compared to 0.23, 0.57, and 0.57 for QMIX respectively. In the stochastic SMACv2 benchmark, CTR+QMIX achieves 0.85, 0.83, and 0.75 win rates versus 0.68, 0.53, and 0.41 for QMIX. In the custom Pac-Men grid-world, CTR+QMIX scores 0.78 versus 0.21 for QMIX and 0.49 for MAPPO.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CTR+QMIX outperforms all seven baselines across every evaluated scenario. Against QMIX (the base value-decomposition method), CTR+QMIX gains the largest margins in super hard SMAC scenarios: +0.56 on 6h_vs_8z (0.79 vs 0.23), +0.25 on corridor (0.82 vs 0.57), and +0.28 on 3s5z_vs_3s6z (0.85 vs 0.57). Among mutual information-based baselines, FoX is the closest competitor (e.g., 0.56 on 6h_vs_8z, 0.52 on corridor), yet CTR+QMIX still leads by 0.23–0.33 in those scenarios. EOI and SCDS collapse in super hard settings (EOI: 0.08 on 6h_vs_8z; SCDS: 0.03 on 6h_vs_8z, 0.00 on 3s5z_vs_3s6z), confirming the overfitting-to-known-trajectories issue the paper identifies. In the stochastic SMACv2 benchmark, CTR+QMIX leads QMIX by 0.15–0.34 and FoX by 0.29–0.31, demonstrating that contrastive trajectory representations yield more robust exploration under distributional shift. On the easy 3s5z scenario, CTR+QMIX (0.95) also surpasses EOI (0.87) and QMIX (0.72), showing that the method does not impede homogeneous 'focus fire' behaviors when they are optimal.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the QMIX value-decomposition framework and the InfoNCE contrastive learning objective, repurposing the latter from self-supervised representation learning to a fully supervised multi-agent diversity objective. It departs from the dominant line of mutual-information-based diversity methods (MAVEN, EOI, SCDS, PMIC, LIPO, FoX) that use variational inference with fixed one-hot agent identities, arguing that this formulation inherently overfits agents to known trajectories. The key conceptual shift is replacing fixed identities with learnable identity vectors and replacing the VI lower bound with a contrastive lower bound in an abstract trajectory representation space, so that distinguishability is enforced geometrically rather than through trajectory-level classification.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clean theoretical explanation (Appendix A) for why MI-based intrinsic rewards penalize exploration, and shows that contrastive learning in representation space sidesteps this trap while still enforcing agent distinguishability. The scalability results (QMIX degrading from 0.68 to 0.11 win rate as agents grow from 5 to 20, while CTR+QMIX holds at ~0.82–0.87) suggest the method could be important for large-scale cooperative MARL. Open questions include whether the approach extends to fully decentralized settings (it currently requires centralized trajectory collection), how to automatically modulate the diversity pressure when homogeneous behavior is optimal, and how to augment the limited negative-sample pool when the number of agents is small.
