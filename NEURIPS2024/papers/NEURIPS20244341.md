## Problem

Existing incremental object detection methods (e.g., CL-DETR, iDETR) degrade the zero-shot generalization ability of Vision-Language Object Detection Models when applied to specialized domains, and no prior work has formulated the task of simultaneously preserving zero-shot performance on the general domain while incrementally adapting to multiple downstream tasks. This paper introduces IVLOD to formalize this dual-forgetting challenge and proposes a memory-efficient solution that avoids storing exemplars or full model copies.

## Input / Output

The model consumes RGB images paired with free-form category text prompts (e.g., 'a pipe.pear.banana.apple') and outputs class-agnostic bounding boxes whose visual features are matched to refined text classification features via cosine similarity. During incremental training, the 13 ODinW-13 sub-datasets are presented sequentially in a random order, with the model fine-tuned for two epochs per task using only the RDB parameters while the entire pre-trained VLODM (Grounding DINO or OV-DINO with Swin-Tiny backbone) remains frozen. At inference, all RDB sub-branches and the frozen pre-trained branch are merged into a single consolidated branch via reparameterization, eliminating any additional computational overhead.

## Method

ZiRa inserts a Reparameterizable Dual Branch (RDB) in parallel with pre-trained layers on both the vision side (convolutional, between backbone and detector) and the language side (linear, between detector and language backbone). Each RDB contains a High-learning-rate Branch (HLRB) for rapid task adaptation and a Low-learning-rate Branch (LLRB) for stable knowledge retention; after each task, the HLRB is reparameterized into the LLRB, preventing unbounded parameter growth. The key novel element is the Zero-interference Loss (ZiL), which applies L1-norm penalties to both the full RDB output (constraining total interference with pre-trained zero-shot performance) and the HLRB output specifically (penalizing the incremental change relative to the previous task's model, thus preventing downstream forgetting). The underlying insight is that pre-trained VLODMs are empirically robust to small-norm input perturbations, so keeping the RDB residual small preserves zero-shot accuracy while still permitting sufficient adaptation capacity for new concepts.

## Results

In the full-shot IVLOD setting on ODinW-13 (Grounding DINO backbone), ZiRa achieves 46.06 mAP on zero-shot COCO (ZCOCO) and 59.73 average mAP across the 13 downstream domains, outperforming CL-DETR (32.15 ZCOCO, 57.26 Avg) by 13.91 AP on ZCOCO and iDETR (37.32 ZCOCO, 58.71 Avg) by 8.74 AP on ZCOCO. ZiRa also leads on downstream Avg mAP, surpassing CL-DETR by 2.47 AP and iDETR by 1.02 AP. On the OV-DINO backbone, ZiRa further extends its lead, achieving 49.07 ZCOCO and 50.21 Avg, beating CL-DETR by 14.55 AP and iDETR by 11.36 AP on ZCOCO.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ZiRa decisively outperforms all five baselines on both ZCOCO and Avg in the full-shot setting. Against CL-DETR [23], which relies on knowledge distillation plus exemplar replay and requires a full model copy, ZiRa gains 13.91 AP on ZCOCO (46.06 vs. 32.15) while also improving Avg by 2.47 AP, and does so without storing any exemplars or model copies. Against iDETR [10], which tunes the DETR projection layer via self-supervised distillation, ZiRa leads by 8.74 AP on ZCOCO and 1.02 AP on Avg. OW-DETR [13], an open-world DETR using exemplar replay, is the weakest on ZCOCO (31.22) and also trails ZiRa by 4.15 AP on Avg. Adapting-tuning (AT) [14], a parameter-efficient adapter, preserves ZCOCO reasonably well (42.30) but lags ZiRa by 3.76 AP on ZCOCO and 8.59 AP on Avg, indicating that simple adapter tuning is insufficient for downstream adaptation. TFA [36], a linear-probing few-shot baseline, is the overall weakest method (30.97 ZCOCO, 47.93 Avg), confirming that naive fine-tuning severely degrades zero-shot generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Reparameterizable Dual Branch (RDB) concept from Zhang et al. (CVPR 2022) for continual semantic segmentation, adapting it to the vision-language object detection setting with two key modifications: differentiated learning rates between the high- and low-learning-rate branches, and a novel Zero-interference Loss that penalizes output norms. It departs from existing incremental object detection methods (CL-DETR, iDETR, OW-DETR) by arguing that their reliance on exemplar replay or full model copies for knowledge distillation is both memory-prohibitive and fundamentally misaligned with the goal of preserving zero-shot generalization in VLODMs. The paper also positions IVLOD as distinct from open-world object detection, where unknown-object detection is learned during training rather than inherited from pre-training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By formalizing IVLOD as a distinct task and showing that a lightweight norm-constrained reparameterizable branch can simultaneously protect zero-shot generalization and prevent downstream forgetting—without storing exemplars or model copies—this work offers a practical, memory-efficient recipe for deploying VLODMs in streaming, multi-domain settings (e.g., edge devices). The 13.91 AP gap over CL-DETR on zero-shot COCO underscores how much existing IOD methods degrade the very capability that makes VLODMs valuable. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond DETR-based architectures, whether the perturbation-robustness assumption holds for all pre-trained VLODMs, and how the method scales to very long task sequences where the LLRB accumulates substantial drift.
