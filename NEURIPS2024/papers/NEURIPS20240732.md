## Problem

Most existing PDE solvers and ML-based forecasting models cannot be applied out-of-the-box for data assimilation, yet real-world scenarios like weather prediction require a single model that both forecasts future states and conditions on incoming sparse observations. Prior diffusion-based approaches either sample full trajectories all-at-once (which fails for long-range forecasting due to limited information propagation) or use amortised conditioning with a fixed history length (which is unstable across different conditioning scenarios). This paper identifies and resolves these shortcomings to enable a unified model for combined forecasting and data assimilation.

## Input / Output

The model consumes sequences of discretised PDE states (e.g., 256 spatial points per state for 1D KS, 64×64 vorticity fields for 2D Kolmogorov) along with sparse space-time observations specified by a binary masking matrix over the space-time vectorised trajectory. At inference, the local score network operates on windows of 2k+1 consecutive noised states (window 9 for KS, window 5 for Kolmogorov), with binary mask channels indicating which positions are conditioned. The output is a full conditional trajectory: future states given a history (forecasting), the complete trajectory given sparse observations (offline DA), or iteratively refined forecasts updated at each observation arrival (online DA).

## Method

The paper proposes three key modifications to score-based diffusion models for PDEs. First, an autoregressive (AR) sampling strategy for joint score models: instead of reconstructing the full score and sampling the entire trajectory all-at-once, the model generates P states at a time conditioned on C previous states (P+C=2k+1), which provably yields an effective Markov order of 2k versus k for all-at-once sampling and eliminates the information-propagation bottleneck. Second, a universal amortised model that samples the conditioning length C uniformly from {0,…,2k} during training, producing a single model stable across all history lengths without retraining. Third, a hybrid conditioning scheme that combines architecture-based conditioning on previous states (via extra input channels with binary masks) with reconstruction guidance (using Tweedie's formula to estimate the likelihood gradient) for sparse observations, enabling the same model to handle both forecasting and DA. The score network is a U-Net trained with denoising score matching, and sampling uses the DPM-Solver with 128 discretisation steps plus optional Langevin corrector steps.

## Results

On the combined online data assimilation task (forecasting + DA), the universal amortised model achieves the lowest RMSD: 7.7±0.6 on Kuramoto-Sivashinsky and 4.9±0.4 on Kolmogorov flow, substantially outperforming Joint AR (11.6±0.6 and 7.8±0.5) and Joint AAO (25.3±0.1 and 27.4±0.2 with 0 correctors). For pure forecasting, the universal amortised model matches the MSE-trained U-Net in high correlation time on both datasets, while the Joint AR model is comparable to the MSE-trained FNO (on par for KS, slightly better for Kolmogorov). The AAO sampling strategy fails at forecasting, with predicted samples rapidly diverging from ground truth, whereas AR sampling is essential for coherent long rollouts.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares its diffusion-based models against PDE-Refiner [42] (state-of-the-art neural PDE solver) and MSE-trained FNO [39] for forecasting. In pure forecasting, the universal amortised model performs similarly to the MSE-trained U-Net on both KS and Kolmogorov, while the Joint AR model is comparable to the MSE-trained FNO (on par for KS, slightly outperforming it for Kolmogorov). However, the diffusion models do not match PDE-Refiner's forecasting accuracy. The critical advantage is flexibility: unlike the MSE-trained baselines which can only perform forecasting, the diffusion models also handle data assimilation. In the combined online DA task (Table 2), the universal amortised model achieves RMSD of 7.7 on KS and 4.9 on Kolmogorov, beating Joint AR by ~34% and ~37% respectively, while AAO sampling is completely unsuitable (RMSD >25 on both datasets). The paper positions its contribution as achieving competitive forecasting performance with significantly greater task flexibility rather than pure forecasting SOTA.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the local score decomposition and all-at-once (AAO) sampling framework of Rozet and Louppe [60] by proposing autoregressive (AR) sampling as a superior alternative, with a theoretical argument that AR achieves an effective Markov order of 2k versus k for AAO with the same window size. The AR rollout strategy draws on the ancestor-sampling idea from video diffusion [27], while the universal amortised model (amortising over both predictive horizon P and conditioning length C) is inspired by the masked video diffusion approach of Höppe et al. [28]. The paper also builds on the SDE-based score matching framework of Song et al. [68] and the reconstruction guidance mechanism for conditional sampling, combining architecture-based conditioning with guidance in a hybrid model that had not been previously explored for PDEs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first controlled quantitative comparison between joint and amortised score-based diffusion models for PDEs, and demonstrates that a single model can handle both forecasting and data assimilation—addressing a practical need in applications like weather prediction where these tasks are currently handled by separate, expensive systems. Key limitations include that the models still trail dedicated MSE-trained solvers (e.g., PDE-Refiner) in pure forecasting accuracy, AR sampling is computationally expensive due to sequential denoising at each step, and the guidance strength hyperparameter requires manual tuning. Open questions include how PDE-specific characteristics (spatial/temporal discretisation, frequency spectrum) influence model behaviour, and whether the computational cost of AR can be reduced (e.g., by reusing previously sampled states).
