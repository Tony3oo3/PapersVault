## Problem

Existing multimodal VAEs enforce hard constraints on latent representations by aggregating unimodal posteriors into a joint posterior (via product-of-experts, mixture-of-experts, or averaging), which imposes overly restrictive assumptions that degrade both reconstruction quality and the informativeness of learned representations. The paper asks whether a softer mechanism can share information across modalities without forcing them into a single shared latent code, thereby preserving modality-specific details while still capturing shared structure.

## Input / Output

The model receives a multimodal tuple X = {x_1, ..., x_M} where each x_m is a distinct observation of the same underlying instance (e.g., a frontal and lateral chest X-ray at 224×224, three translated MNIST digits at 28×28, a face image plus a shuffled text-attribute string, or a bird image plus a 10-caption text embedding). Each modality is encoded by its own dedicated encoder into a Gaussian posterior q_φ^m(z_m|x_m), and the data-dependent prior h(z|X) is conditioned on the full multimodal input. Outputs include per-modality latent codes, per-modality reconstructions, and conditionally generated samples of a missing modality given one or more available modalities.

## Method

The MMVM VAE replaces the aggregated joint posterior with a data-dependent mixture-of-experts prior h(z_m|X) = (1/M) Σ_m̃ q_φ^m̃(z_m|x_m̃), constructed by mixing all unimodal posterior approximations in modality space and replicating this mixture as the prior for every modality. The key insight is that the KL rate term between each unimodal posterior and this mixture prior equals M times the Jensen-Shannon divergence across all M unimodal posteriors, so maximizing the ELBO simultaneously minimizes inter-modality JS divergence (soft alignment, analogous to contrastive learning on positive pairs) while the per-modality reconstruction loss preserves modality-specific information. A Lagrange-multiplier proof (Lemma 4.1) shows this mixture prior is the unique minimizer of cross-entropy, making it the optimal data-dependent prior under the chosen factorized variational family. The model is trained as a β-VAE with separate ResNet-based encoders and decoders per modality (no weight sharing), using Laplace likelihoods for image data and categorical likelihoods for text, with β swept across a range to expose the reconstruction–representation trade-off.

## Results

On MIMIC-CXR, MMVM VAE achieves 73.1±8.8% average AUROC across all cardiopulmonary labels, surpassing the best aggregation-based baseline (PoE at 70.4±8.3%) and even the fully-supervised ResNet classifier (70.5±12.1%). On the three benchmark datasets (Translated PolyMNIST, Bimodal CelebA, CUB image-captions), MMVM consistently achieves higher latent representation classification accuracy and conditional generation coherence at the same or lower reconstruction loss compared to all five baselines. On hippocampal neural activity data from five rats, MMVM produces clear odor separation in the latent space while maintaining low reconstruction error, outperforming all aggregation-based alternatives on both classification accuracy and coherence.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MMVM VAE outperforms all five baselines (Independent, AVG, MoE, MoPoE, PoE) on every evaluated dataset and metric. On MIMIC-CXR, MMVM achieves 73.1% average AUROC, beating the strongest baseline PoE (70.4%) by 2.7 points and the fully-supervised ResNet (70.5%) by 2.6 points; the gap is largest on Cardiomegaly (79.6% vs. 76.7% for PoE) and Edema (85.3% vs. 81.8% for PoE). On the benchmark scatter plots (Figure 2), MMVM dominates the Pareto frontier: it achieves higher latent representation classification accuracy and coherence at the same reconstruction loss, or lower reconstruction loss at the same accuracy, compared to all aggregation-based methods. On hippocampal neural activity, only MoPoE approaches MMVM's classification accuracy but at substantially higher reconstruction loss, while Independent and AVG VAEs fail to connect the five rat modalities in latent space (AVG clusters by rat rather than by odor). The key advantage over aggregation-based methods (AVG, PoE, MoE, MoPoE) is that MMVM avoids the reconstruction-quality trade-off identified by Daunhawer et al. [2022], because it uses a soft data-dependent prior rather than a hard joint posterior constraint.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the VAMP-prior mechanism (Tomczak and Welling, 2017) from data space to modality space: instead of mixing posteriors across different samples, it mixes unimodal posterior approximations across modalities of the same sample to form a data-dependent prior. It departs fundamentally from aggregation-based multimodal VAEs (PoE, MoE, MoPoE, AVG) that impose hard constraints by sharing encoder outputs or decoder inputs, replacing them with a soft Jensen-Shannon divergence regularization that keeps per-modality encoders and decoders independent. The resulting objective is conceptually linked to contrastive learning (minimizing distance between positive pairs), but realized within a generative VAE framework rather than a discriminative one.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a well-documented limitation of aggregation-based multimodal VAEs—their inability to simultaneously achieve high reconstruction quality and meaningful shared representations—and shows that a simple soft constraint via a mixture-of-experts prior resolves this trade-off consistently across benchmarks and real-world applications (neuroscience, medical imaging). Key limitations include the loss of unconditional generation (the prior is data-dependent), untested scalability to very large numbers of modalities, and the requirement for substantial compute. The authors suggest promising future directions, including applying the MMVM regularization as an auxiliary signal for diffusion models and multimodal masked autoencoders, which could broaden its impact beyond the VAE family.
