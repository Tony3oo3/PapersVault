## Problem

Existing LLM pruning methods (Wanda, SparseGPT, Magnitude) apply a uniform sparsity ratio to every layer, ignoring the well-documented variation in each layer's contribution to final performance. While OWL introduces non-uniform layerwise sparsity, it relies on manually tuned outlier thresholds and sparsity bounds without theoretical justification, making it sensitive and hard to transfer across models. This paper asks: can sparsity allocation across layers be formulated as a principled, globally optimal optimization problem rather than a heuristic or greedy per-layer procedure?

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pre-trained LLM (6.7B–70B parameters), 16 calibration samples from C4, and a target model size budget B. It first computes an L×L Redundancy Metric matrix (taking ~90 s) and then solves a linear program in ~160 ms to produce per-layer sparsity ratios at 0.5% granularity; these ratios are handed to any base pruning method (Wanda, SparseGPT, or Magnitude) which performs the actual weight zeroing. The final output is a sparse LLM whose total parameter count does not exceed B, with the option of a second-pass intra-layer component-level allocation for finer control.

## Method

ALS derives a Redundancy Metric (RM) between every pair of layers by approximating mutual information via Monte Carlo sampling and kernel density estimation, simplified to a ratio of Frobenius norms of feature-matrix inner products (RM ∈ [0,1], where 0 = independent, 1 = fully redundant). The per-layer redundancy ρ_i is the row-sum of the RM matrix minus 1, and an importance factor ω_i = exp(−ρ_i/μ) is computed with a dynamic hyperparameter μ that smooths the decay. The sparsity allocation is then cast as a linear program: maximize the sum of sparsity-weighted importance factors across layers, subject to the constraint that total remaining parameters ≤ B, solvable by any standard LP solver. This is, to the authors' knowledge, the first formulation of LLM sparsity allocation as a linear program, enabling global (rather than greedy layer-by-layer) optimization. The approach is training-free and can be extended hierarchically to intra-layer components, and the entire pipeline completes in under 20 minutes for a 70B model on a single A100-80 GB GPU.

## Results

At 50% sparsity, ALS consistently reduces WikiText-2 perplexity across all tested model families. The most dramatic gains appear on LLaMA-V1 7B (Magnitude PPL drops from 42.26 to 16.80) and LLaMA-V3 8B (Wanda PPL drops from 15.01 to 12.30, a 18% relative reduction). On zero-shot tasks, ALS improves average accuracy by up to 14.14% over Magnitude and 9.87% over SparseGPT on LLaMA-V3 8B. The method also outperforms OWL on all sparsity settings (50%, 2:4, 4:8) on LLaMA-V2-13B, with PPL of 9.86 vs. 10.11 at 50%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ALS is evaluated as a plug-in sparsity allocation strategy on top of three pruning metrics (Magnitude, SparseGPT, Wanda) and one competing allocation method (OWL). Against Magnitude, ALS yields the largest absolute improvements, e.g., reducing LLaMA-V1 7B PPL from 42.26 to 16.80 and LLaMA-V2 7B from 32.87 to 15.19 at 50% sparsity. Against SparseGPT, ALS consistently lowers PPL (e.g., LLaMA-V2 7B: 17.21→10.99; LLaMA-V3 8B: 16.87→10.23), though one exception exists where LLaMA-V1 13B PPL slightly increases (9.90→9.97). Against Wanda, ALS provides steady but smaller gains (e.g., LLaMA-V2 13B: 11.21→9.86; LLaMA-V2 70B: 6.51→6.36), with the largest relative improvement on LLaMA-V3 8B (15.01→12.30). Against OWL, ALS outperforms on all three sparsity settings tested on LLaMA-V2-13B: 9.86 vs. 10.11 (50%), 15.52 vs. 16.47 (2:4), and 11.65 vs. 12.09 (4:8), while requiring no manual hyperparameter tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

ALS builds on the training-free LLM pruning lineage (SparseGPT's Hessian-based reconstruction, Wanda's weight-activation magnitude criterion, Magnitude pruning) and the emerging recognition that uniform per-layer sparsity is suboptimal, as explored by OWL (outlier-ratio-based non-uniform allocation) and BESA/DSOT (intra-layer sparsity configuration). It departs from these by replacing empirical heuristics (OWL's manually tuned outlier thresholds) and training-based search (BESA's 5+ hour optimization) with an information-theoretic redundancy metric derived from mutual information between layer activations, then solving the resulting global allocation as a linear program. The conceptual bridge from information bottleneck theory to a practical Frobenius-norm-based redundancy score, and the LP formulation of sparsity allocation, are the paper's primary departures from prior work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ALS offers a principled, training-free, and computationally cheap (minutes on a single A100) alternative to both uniform pruning and empirically tuned non-uniform methods, with consistent gains across LLaMA V1/V2/V3 and OPT at 6.7B–70B scale. However, the mutual information approximation via Frobenius inner products is quite loose, the 'decreasing function' step to bound the metric is somewhat ad hoc, and the objective function's structure (q_i/(L−i+1)·Σω_j) lacks a clear information-theoretic justification beyond the stated redundancy-minimization goal. Open questions include whether the RM truly captures the relevant inter-layer dependencies, how the method interacts with structured (N:M) sparsity at higher ratios, and whether the LP solution remains optimal when combined with dynamic or input-dependent sparsity.
