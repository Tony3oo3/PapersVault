## Problem

Prior activation-based lie detectors for LLMs, such as the MLP of Azaria and Mitchell, fail to generalize from affirmative to negated statements, and the underlying cause of this failure remained unclear. This paper identifies that the truth representation in LLMs is not one-dimensional but spans a two-dimensional subspace, and that prior classifiers conflated a general truth direction with a polarity-sensitive direction, explaining the generalization breakdown.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a text statement (or a prompt plus LLM-generated response for real-world lie detection), feeds it through the target LLM, and extracts the residual stream activation vector at a fixed intermediate layer over the final token. The output is a binary true/false label produced by a 2D logistic regression classifier operating on projections of this activation vector. For real-world scenarios, the full prompt and response are concatenated and passed through the model, with activations extracted from the last token of the response.

## Method

The key insight is that LLM activations encode truthfulness along a two-dimensional subspace spanned by a general truth direction t_G (pointing from false to true for both affirmative and negated statements) and a polarity-sensitive truth direction t_P (pointing from false to true for affirmative but from true to false for negated statements). The affirmative truth direction t_A found by prior work is a linear combination of t_G and t_P, which explains its failure on negated statements. The proposed TTPD method learns t_G via ordinary least squares on balanced affirmative and negated statements across topics, learns a polarity direction p via logistic regression, projects activations onto the 2D space spanned by t_G and p, and trains a final logistic regression classifier in that space. This 2D truth subspace is shown to be universal across LLaMA3-8B, LLaMA2-13B, Mistral-7B, Gemma-7B, and Gemma-2-27B, and TTPD achieves 94% accuracy on real-world lies generated by LLMs in role-playing scenarios.

## Results

TTPD achieves 93.9±0.2% accuracy on unseen topics and 93.8±1.5% on real-world lies (208 LLaMA3-8B-Instruct responses from role-playing scenarios), outperforming all baselines on the latter. On unseen statement types (logical conjunctions, disjunctions, German translations), TTPD consistently outperforms LR and CCS, with only a few percentage points lower accuracy on German vs. English. The general truth direction t_G separates true from false statements with AUROC 0.98, and the 2D truth subspace (spanned by t_G and t_P) captures >60% of truth-related variance in preprocessed activations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TTPD outperforms CCS (Burns et al., 2023) across all evaluation settings, with the largest gap on real-world lies (93.8% vs. 73%) and unseen topics (93.9% vs. 84.8%). Against Logistic Regression (LR), TTPD wins decisively on real-world lies (93.8% vs. 79%) and on unseen statement types such as logical disjunctions and German translations, while LR edges out TTPD by ~0.7% on unseen topics (94.6% vs. 93.9%). Against Mass Mean probing (MM, Marks & Tegmark, 2023), TTPD achieves higher accuracy on real-world lies (93.8% vs. 90.5%) and unseen statement types, while MM is competitive on unseen topics (92.2% vs. 93.9%). The paper attributes TTPD's advantage over LR on real-world lies to its two-stage training: learning the truth direction first (via the 2D subspace decomposition) and then the bias, which allows the truth direction to generalize better than the jointly-learned direction in LR.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on linear truth directions in LLM activations initiated by Azaria and Mitchell (2023) and formalized by Marks and Tegmark (2023), who demonstrated that true and false statements separate along a direction in residual stream space. The key conceptual departure is the identification of a two-dimensional truth subspace rather than a single direction: a general truth direction t_G that is polarity-invariant, and a polarity-sensitive direction t_P that reverses sign for negated statements. This cleanly explains the well-documented generalization failure of affirmative-only classifiers on negated statements (Levinstein and Herrmann, 2024) and subsumes the Contrast Consistent Search and Mass Mean probing methods as approximations to t_G. The TTPD method builds on the supervised linear-probing framework but adds an explicit polarity disentanglement step and a 2D logistic regression head.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 2D truth subspace finding provides a mechanistic explanation for why prior lie detectors were fragile, and the universality across five model families (LLaMA2/3, Mistral, Gemma) supports the Platonic Representation Hypothesis for truth-related features. The 94% accuracy on real-world role-playing lies—trained only on simple factual statements—is the first quantification of activation-based lie detection in realistic deception scenarios, moving the field beyond toy true/false classification. Key limitations include using only one of the two dimensions for classification, testing on a limited set of statement types, and the subspace being shown to be 'at least' 2D rather than exactly 2D. Open questions include whether interventions on the truth subspace can alter model outputs, whether the structure persists in larger or multimodal models, and whether additional linear or nonlinear dimensions emerge with more complex statement types.
