## Problem

Single-cell ATAC-seq data suffers from extreme sparsity due to dropout events, hindering accurate analysis and motivating the need for high-quality in silico data generation. Existing generative and analysis methods for scATAC-seq are task-specific (clustering, denoising, generation handled separately), and standard diffusion models produce latent variables without semantic meaning, making them unsuitable for capturing the biological heterogeneity inherent in single-cell epigenomic data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241444/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes scATAC-seq fragment counts (a bag-of-words vector of peak accessibility per cell, with no upper bound on counts and order-invariant peaks) as input, first compressed into a continuous latent space by a pretrained autoencoder. Optionally, categorical or continuous conditional information (cell type, tissue, other omics data) is provided via MLP embeddings. Outputs include generated scATAC-seq data (unconditional or cell-type-conditional), low-dimensional latent cell embeddings usable for clustering and UMAP visualization, denoised single-cell profiles, and imputed values for dropout-masked entries.

## Method

ATAC-Diff is a conditional latent diffusion model augmented with an informative auxiliary module. A transformer-based auxiliary encoder maps the autoencoder latent representation x_0 to semantically meaningful low-dimensional variables z, with a Gaussian Mixture Model (GMM) serving as the prior to capture multi-modal cell-type structure. A mutual information maximization term between z and x_0 is added to the ELBO to prevent the diffusion decoder from ignoring the latent variables (avoiding posterior collapse into unconditional generation). The diffusion decoder is a transformer that predicts x_0 (rather than ε, for faster convergence on sparse data) at each denoising step, conditioned on z and additional attributes y via cross-attention. Training jointly optimizes the diffusion ELBO, an auxiliary reconstruction loss, the MI term, and a GMM KL regularization, with hyperparameters α=0.1 and λ=1.1001.

## Results

On clustering (latent representation quality), ATAC-Diff achieves the best NMI across all three datasets: 0.740 (Forebrain), 0.647 (Hematopoiesis), and 0.733 (PBMC10k), outperforming the strongest baseline SCALE by 3.1%, 6.6%, and 8.6% respectively. For unconditional scATAC-seq generation, ATAC-Diff achieves SCC of 0.925–0.964 and PCC of 0.976–0.997 across the three datasets, substantially exceeding SCALE (SCC 0.548–0.892) and PeakVI (SCC 0.330–0.824). For denoising and imputation, ATAC-Diff achieves the highest PCC on PBMC10k (0.950 denoising, 0.949 imputation) and competitive results on the other datasets, demonstrating robustness across tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ATAC-Diff outperforms all four baselines on the majority of clustering metrics across the three datasets, achieving 8 best scores out of 12 (3 datasets × 4 metrics). Against SCALE (the strongest VAE-based baseline), ATAC-Diff leads by 3.1% NMI on Forebrain (0.740 vs 0.718), 6.6% on Hematopoiesis (0.647 vs 0.608), and 8.6% on PBMC10k (0.733 vs 0.675); the only metric where SCALE edges ahead is Homogeneity on Forebrain (0.722 vs 0.673). Against PeakVI, ATAC-Diff's advantage is most pronounced on Forebrain (NMI 0.740 vs 0.499, a 50% relative gain) and Hematopoiesis (NMI 0.647 vs 0.593). Against cisTopic, ATAC-Diff wins on Forebrain (NMI 0.740 vs 0.665) and Hematopoiesis (0.647 vs 0.639) but is marginally behind on PBMC10k NMI (0.733 vs 0.736) while matching its Homogeneity score (0.800 vs 0.802). For unconditional generation, ATAC-Diff dominates all baselines by a wide margin (e.g., SCC 0.925 vs 0.548 for SCALE on Forebrain), and for denoising/imputation it achieves the best or near-best PCC on all three datasets, confirming that the GMM-prior auxiliary module and mutual information regularization provide consistent gains over task-specific VAE baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

ATAC-Diff builds on the latent diffusion model architecture (Rombach et al., 2022) and the DDPM forward/reverse process formulation (Ho et al., 2020), adapting them to the discrete, highly sparse scATAC-seq domain via a pretrained autoencoder. It draws conceptual inspiration from Diffusion Autoencoders (Preechakul et al., 2022) for the auxiliary encoder and from InfoVAE (Zhao et al., 2017) for mutual information regularization, but departs from both by replacing the standard isotropic Gaussian prior with a GMM to capture multi-modal cell-type structure and by using MI maximization to prevent posterior collapse in the conditional diffusion decoder. Unlike prior scATAC-seq methods (SCALE, PeakVI, cisTopic) that are task-specific, ATAC-Diff unifies generation, denoising, imputation, and clustering within a single conditional latent diffusion framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As the first diffusion model applied to scATAC-seq, ATAC-Diff opens a path toward high-fidelity in silico data augmentation for a modality where wet-lab sequencing is expensive and sparse, potentially reducing the need for additional cell sequencing. The GMM-prior + MI-regularization combination offers a practical recipe for injecting semantically meaningful structure into diffusion-based generative models, which could transfer to other sparse single-cell modalities. However, the evaluation is limited to three datasets of modest size (2K–10K cells), the unconditional generation comparison relies on modified VAE baselines not originally designed for that task, and no error bars or multi-seed results are reported, leaving open questions about robustness and scalability to larger, more heterogeneous datasets. The authors also note future directions including cross-omics conditional generation (e.g., scRNA-seq-conditioned ATAC-seq) and perturbation prediction.
