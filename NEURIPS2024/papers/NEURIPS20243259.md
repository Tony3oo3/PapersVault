## Problem

The conditional quantile treatment effect (CQTE) captures distributional heterogeneity beyond the CATE but its estimation depends on the smoothness of individual conditional quantile functions rather than the treatment effect itself, and requires estimating difficult nuisance parameters like reciprocal conditional densities. The paper introduces the conditional quantile comparator (CQC) as a new estimand that retains full quantile-level information while admitting natural smoothness conditions and enabling doubly robust estimation analogous to the CATE literature.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard observational data triples (Y, X, A) with a continuous outcome Y, covariates X ∈ ℝ^d, and binary treatment A, and is queried at a specific untreated response y₀ and covariate point x to return the treated response g*(y₀|x) at the equivalent quantile. The output is a function over the response space (not the quantile space [0,1]), which makes comparisons across covariate values more direct than the CQTE. A derived quantity Δ*(y|x) = g*(y|x) − y indicates whether the treatment is beneficial at each untreated response level.

## Method

The estimation proceeds in two stages: first, the CCDF contrasting function h*(y₀,y₁|x) = F₁(y₁|x) − F₀(y₀|x) is estimated by reframing it as a CATE problem with transformed response W = 1{Y ≤ y_A}, then applying Kennedy's pseudo-outcome regression with sample splitting to achieve double robustness. Second, the monotonic h* estimate is isotonic-projected via PAVA and inverted to find g*(y₀|x) as the y₁ where the projected h* crosses zero. The key insight is that h* can be smooth even when individual CCDFs are not, and the product structure of the nuisance parameter errors (ε_α · ε_β) in the finite-sample bound ensures oracle-rate convergence even when propensity score and CCDF estimates are suboptimal. Nadaraya-Watson kernel regression is used as the non-parametric smoother throughout, and the theory provides explicit high-probability error bounds for both h* and g*.

## Results

In the primary 1D simulation (2n=1000, γ∈[0,10]), the proposed DR-CQC estimator achieves the lowest mean absolute error among all non-oracle methods, outperforming the Separate CCDF, IPW, and Kallus–Oprescu CQTE baselines, and is only marginally outperformed by the oracle DR estimator (which uses true nuisance parameters). As the nuisance-parameter frequency γ increases, the Kallus–Oprescu CQTE estimator degrades substantially while the DR-CQC estimator remains stable, confirming its ability to exploit CQC smoothness. In the sample-size experiment (γ=6, 2n∈[200,5000]), the CQTE estimator plateaus due to instability in estimating the reciprocal of the conditional PDF, whereas the DR-CQC estimator continues to improve. On the real-world Work First employment data (2,000 participants), the CQC reveals that participants aged ~23 and 32–37 benefit most, with the lowest income quantile showing minimal treatment effect.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares the proposed DR-CQC estimator against four baselines: (1) Separate CCDF estimation (estimating F₀ and F₁ independently and taking their difference), (2) an IPW pseudo-outcome estimator, (3) the Kallus & Oprescu [14] CQTE estimator (transformed to CQC space), and (4) an oracle DR estimator using true nuisance parameters. The DR-CQC method clearly wins against the Separate and IPW baselines across all simulation settings (1D and 10D, varying γ and sample size). Against the Kallus & Oprescu CQTE estimator, the DR-CQC method shows a decisive advantage as nuisance-parameter complexity (γ) increases, with the CQTE estimator's error growing sharply while DR-CQC remains near-oracle; the CQTE also plateaus with increasing sample size due to instability in estimating 1/f₁. The DR-CQC method is only marginally outperformed by the oracle estimator, confirming that the double-robustness property is effectively realized in practice. In the 10D experiment, the DR-CQC estimator performs close to oracle at low sample sizes and low frequency, with a small gap emerging as γ increases.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper introduces the Conditional Quantile Comparator (CQC), a new estimand that re-expresses the CQTE in response space rather than quantile space, and builds its estimation procedure directly on Kennedy's [15] doubly robust pseudo-outcome regression framework for CATE. It departs from Kallus and Oprescu's [14] CQTE estimator, which requires estimating reciprocal conditional densities (a notoriously unstable nuisance parameter), by instead estimating a CCDF contrasting function h* that can be re-framed as a CATE problem. The key conceptual move is showing that the CQC can be smooth (even constant) while the individual conditional CDFs are highly non-smooth, providing a natural smoothness assumption that is more interpretable than the relative smoothness of the CQTE itself.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The CQC offers a more interpretable quantile-level treatment effect tool that retains the double robustness properties of CATE estimation, potentially making quantile-based HTE analysis as tractable as mean-based analysis. Open questions include: (1) whether the two-step estimation (estimate h*, then invert) can be replaced by a direct CQC estimator analogous to the DR-learner; (2) the precise relationship between smoothness of g* and h*, since the current theory requires smoothness of h* which appears stronger than needed; and (3) the fundamental limitation that quantile-based estimands lack individual-level causal interpretation, unlike the CATE. The colon cancer application revealing that treatment increases the proportion of event-free patients rather than delaying events demonstrates the estimand's practical value in uncovering distributional structure invisible to mean-based methods.
