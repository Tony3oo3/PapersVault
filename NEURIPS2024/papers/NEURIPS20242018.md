## Problem

Single-particle cryo-EM reconstruction pipelines rely on particle picking and pose estimation models that are bottlenecked by the scarcity of high-quality annotated training datasets, which are extremely labor-intensive to produce. Existing generative approaches for scientific imaging either lack physical fidelity or fail to model the complex, random noise distributions inherent to cryo-EM micrographs, making them unsuitable for training downstream models that must generalize to real experimental conditions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242018/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

CryoGEM takes a coarse 3D density volume (from cryoSPARC ab-initio reconstruction refined by cryoDRGN for conformational heterogeneity), 100 unannotated real micrographs at 1024×1024 resolution, ice gradient weight maps estimated by IceBreaker, and CTF/PSF parameters from CTFFIND4. It outputs synthetic cryo-EM micrographs with free ground-truth annotations (particle positions, orientations, and conformations) derived from the random sampling during virtual specimen construction. The synthetic dataset is then used to fine-tune particle picking (Topaz) and pose estimation (CryoFIRE) models, which are evaluated on held-out real micrographs.

## Method

CryoGEM combines a physics-based simulation stage with a contrastive noise generation stage. The simulation constructs a virtual specimen by randomly placing N particle copies (with orientations from SO(3), conformations from cryoDRGN's embedding space, and translations from unoccupied regions), then applies weak phase object approximation projection, ice gradient multiplication, and CTF convolution to produce a clean 2D image. For noise generation, a UNet generator and PatchGAN discriminator perform unpaired image-to-image translation from simulated to real micrograph domains, but with two key modifications: (1) zero-mean Gaussian noise (SNR=0.1) is injected into the physical simulation to make the mapping non-deterministic, enabling a single clean image to produce infinitely many noisy variants; (2) a novel MaskNCE loss uses the particle-background mask to guide patch sampling, ensuring positive and negative contrastive pairs are semantically distinct (particle vs. background) rather than randomly selected, which is critical because cryo-EM micrographs contain densely packed particles that would otherwise be confused as similar patches. Multi-scale features from the first L encoder layers (aligned to particle size) are used for the contrastive objective, and the total loss is L_GAN + 10·L_MaskNCE.

## Results

CryoGEM achieves the best FID of 37.91 (avg) across five cryo-EM datasets, outperforming CUT (60.84), CycleGAN (146.42), and CycleDiffusion (415.42). For downstream particle picking, the fine-tuned Topaz model using CryoGEM's synthetic data reaches 0.674 average AUPRC and 5.27 Å average reconstruction resolution, a 44% improvement in picking over the original Topaz (0.466 AUPRC) and 22% better resolution. For pose estimation, the pre-trained CryoFIRE achieves 4.29 px average reconstruction resolution and 0.69 rad average rotation error, substantially outperforming the original CryoFIRE (12.18 px, 1.08 rad).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CryoGEM outperforms all five baselines across every dataset and every metric. Against CUT (the closest competitor), CryoGEM improves average AUPRC from 0.421 to 0.674 (+60%) and average reconstruction resolution from 7.30 Å to 5.27 Å (−28%), with the largest gains on Ribosome (AUPRC 0.224→0.797) and Integrin (AUPRC 0.335→0.606). CycleGAN performs poorly due to its bijection assumption breaking down on cryo-EM's random noise, yielding 0.409 avg AUPRC and 7.28 Å resolution. CycleDiffusion is the weakest deep baseline (0.329 AUPRC, 6.66 Å), failing to learn authentic noise patterns. Compared to the original Topaz picker without fine-tuning (0.466 AUPRC, 6.71 Å), CryoGEM's synthetic data yields a 44% AUPRC improvement. For pose estimation, CryoGEM-pretrained CryoFIRE achieves 4.29 px resolution and 0.69 rad rotation error versus the original CryoFIRE's 12.18 px and 1.08 rad, a nearly 3× improvement in reconstruction resolution.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the unpaired image-to-image translation line (CycleGAN, CUT, CycleDiffusion) to the cryo-EM domain by coupling a physics-based simulation (WPOA projection, ice gradient, CTF/PSF) as a structural prior with a contrastive noise-translation generator. It departs from CUT's random patch sampling by introducing a mask-guided scheme that exploits the known particle/background layout from the simulation, and from deterministic GAN-based translation by injecting non-deterministic Gaussian noise into the input to model the stochastic nature of detector shot noise. The physics simulation module is simplified relative to multi-slice methods like InsilicoTEM, trading fidelity for speed and controllability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CryoGEM is the first generative framework to produce physically consistent, annotated synthetic cryo-EM micrographs from only 100 unannotated real images, directly addressing the data-scarcity bottleneck in particle picking and pose estimation. The 44% AUPRC improvement and 22% resolution gain demonstrate that synthetic data augmentation can meaningfully close the gap left by labor-intensive manual annotation. Key limitations include the dependence on a coarse 3D reconstruction as input (hard for very small or highly dynamic molecules) and the sacrifice of cross-dataset generalization for a lightweight per-dataset training protocol; a generalized version remains an open question.
