## Problem

While optical Earth Observation datasets are abundant and ML-ready, SAR data—particularly interferometric and coherence products requiring complex-valued phase processing—lacks large-scale, multi-continental datasets in formats compatible with deep learning pipelines. Existing inSAR datasets are limited to specific events, single locations, or lack the full diversity of SAR datatypes (polarimetry, interferometry, coherence) simultaneously, hindering the development of geographically generalisable SAR foundation models.

## Input / Output

The dataset provides 17.2M chips of 4.48×4.48 km tiles across six AOIs, with S1GRD polarimetric amplitude (VV, VH) at 10 m/pixel as four seasonal averages per year (2018–2020), GUNW interferograms and GSSIC coherence at ~90 m/pixel (2020), Sentinel-2 multispectral at 10–20 m/pixel, and five auxiliary layers (ESAWC 10 m, SRTM 90 m, MODISVEG 250 m, GHSBUILTS 100 m, AGB 90 m). All chips share identical geographic extents via .geojson tile definitions, and the framework consumes them as multi-channel tensors (e.g., 5 channels for S1GRD: seasonal, VV, VH, VV−VH, ascending flag) at 448×448 px. Outputs include self-supervised embeddings from a ViT-B MAE encoder, and supervised predictions for land cover segmentation (11-class mIoU), biomass regression (RMSE in Mg ha⁻¹), and built surface regression (RMSE in m²).

## Method

The core contribution is a multi-modal dataset pipeline that pre-processes raw Sentinel-1/2 and auxiliary products into ML-ready tiled chips, abstracting domain-expert parameter choices (acquisition pair selection, seasonal averaging, geocoding) into a uniform format. A modular PyTorch Lightning framework parameterised by Hydra provides datasets, self-supervised pretraining modules (MAE, CLIP, DINO), and tools (geetiles, sartiles) to tile any GEE-retrievable or GeoTIFF data into the same schema. For the distribution-shift analysis, a ViT-B masked autoencoder (88.8M params, 75% masking ratio, 75 epochs) is pretrained on S1GRD across all six AOIs; embeddings are reduced via max-pooling and UMAP to 2D, and covariate/appearance shift is quantified using sliced Wasserstein distance between conditional distributions p(x|y) across AOIs. Supervised baselines use a channel-halved UNet with bilinear upsampling (no data augmentation, due to SAR-specific invariance concerns) trained on a 10% random subset, evaluated on the full test set for ESAWC segmentation, AGB and GHSBUILTS regression.

## Results

On the supervised baseline experiments (UNet, 75 epochs, 10% data subset), S1GRD with both VV+VH polarisations achieves the best single-source results for ESAWC land cover segmentation (mIoU 0.4185) and AGB regression (RMSE 27.467 Mg ha⁻¹), while S2RGB achieves the best single-source result for GHSBUILTS built-surface regression (RMSE 131.968 m²). Fusion of S2RGB with S1GRD (VV+VH) yields the best overall performance across all three tasks: ESAWC mIoU 0.4634, AGB RMSE 25.137 Mg ha⁻¹, and GHSBUILTS RMSE 124.852 m². The distribution shift analysis reveals substantial covariate shift in MAE embeddings across AOIs (sliced Wasserstein distance), and conditioning on terrain properties (e.g., ESAWC land cover) does not substantially reduce the appearance shift, indicating that geographic region introduces non-terrain-related variation in SAR appearance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a dataset paper, so comparisons are between input modalities rather than competing methods. S1GRD (VV+VH) and S2RGB perform similarly as standalone inputs (e.g., ESAWC mIoU 0.4185 vs. 0.4094; AGB RMSE 27.467 vs. 28.689), confirming that polarimetric SAR carries information comparable to multispectral optical data. However, fusing S2RGB with S1GRD (VV+VH) significantly outperforms either source alone on all three tasks (e.g., ESAWC mIoU improves from 0.4185 to 0.4634, a ~11% relative gain; AGB RMSE drops from 27.467 to 25.137). GSSIC coherence as a standalone input performs worst across all tasks (ESAWC mIoU 0.2906, AGB RMSE 39.314), likely due to its lower native resolution (90 m vs. 10 m). Adding GSSIC to S2RGB provides only marginal improvement over S2RGB alone, suggesting coherence requires more nuanced integration (e.g., self-supervised pretraining) rather than naive channel stacking. The distribution shift analysis further shows that MAE embeddings exhibit substantial appearance shift across geographic regions even when conditioning on terrain properties, in contrast to DINO-based embeddings which cluster tightly by label across AOIs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on large-scale Earth observation datasets (SSL4EO-S12, SSL4EO-L, SEN12MS, SatlasPretrain) by being the first to provide interferometric SAR (inSAR) and coherence data at multi-continental scale alongside polarimetric SAR and optical imagery in ML-ready tiled format. It builds directly on the authors' prior MAE pretraining work [65] and DINO/CLIP-based self-supervised studies [67–69], which used subsets of this data, and departs from event-specific or localised inSAR datasets (Hephaestus, ISSLIDE, Pol-InSAR-Island) by providing interferograms without reference to specific geophysical events. The accompanying PyTorch Lightning + Hydra framework and the geetiles/sartiles processing tools are designed to lower the barrier for integrating arbitrary Google Earth Engine data into ML pipelines, a practical gap not addressed by prior dataset releases.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

M3LEO fills a critical gap in the EO-ML ecosystem: the full diversity of SAR datatypes (polarimetry, interferometry, coherence) has been largely inaccessible to deep learning practitioners due to processing complexity and lack of ML-ready formats, limiting geographic and temporal generalisability of SAR models. The preliminary finding that MAE-based embeddings exhibit substantial appearance shift across AOIs even when conditioning on terrain properties—contrasting with DINO embeddings that cluster by label—raises open questions about which self-supervised objectives best capture geographically invariant features. Key limitations include the absence of temporal auxiliary labels (most auxiliary data is 2020-only), the exclusion of regions with non-IW acquisition modes or single-orbit-direction coverage, and the unresolved question of how to productively incorporate lower-resolution coherence and interferometric data into deep learning architectures beyond naive channel stacking.
