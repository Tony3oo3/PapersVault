## Problem

Existing continual learning benchmarks are confined to single-modality data (image, text, audio, or video) or still-image+text pairs with clean class annotations, leaving the video-language domain entirely unexplored. No standardized benchmark exists for evaluating CL models on long egocentric videos with open-ended natural language queries, episodic memory recall, and non-classification tasks such as temporal localization and object detection.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242243/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark consumes 8–10 minute egocentric video clips (from Ego4D, ~85× longer than UCF101) paired with one of three query types: action-category labels (MQ), free-form natural language sentences (NLQ), or object images (VQ). Outputs are dense temporal windows (start/end timestamps) for MQ and NLQ, or 2D bounding boxes with anchor-level confidence for VQ. Video narrations from Ego4D are additionally used as auxiliary supervision signals for self-supervised contrastive training.

## Method

ViLCo introduces a Query-Incremental Learning (QIL) setting with three task formulations (Moment Query, Natural Language Query, Visual Query) and a memory-efficient model built from four modules: a video encoder (EgoVLPv2 or DINO ViT), a CLIP ViT-L/14 text encoder, a Transformer cross-modal encoder with a feature pyramid network, and task-specific prediction heads. The key architectural contribution is a dual episodic memory module: a short-term buffer storing current-task embeddings for contrastive negative sampling, and a long-term buffer of learnable prompt–key pairs {P_i, K_i} that are retrieved via query–key similarity to inject task-agnostic episodic knowledge into the query embedding, making the framework task-agnostic. Self-supervised contrastive learning on Ego4D narrations (bidirectional video-to-text and text-to-video InfoNCE losses) further reduces video-language misalignment and mitigates forgetting by sampling narration negatives from prior tasks.

## Results

On the Moment Query (MQ) task with 5 sub-tasks, ViLCo achieves an average Recall@1 (IoU=0.5) of 26.24% and Recall@5 (IoU=0.5) of 42.70%, with a Backward Forgetting (BwF) score of 2.9, outperforming all four baselines. On the more challenging Natural Language Query (NLQ) task with 13 sub-tasks, ViLCo reaches 6.21% Avg R@1 (IoU=0.5) with BwF of 10.60, reducing forgetting by 3.5–5× compared to regularization-based methods. On Visual Query (VQ), ViLCo attains 17.85% Avg tAP25 and 33.38% Avg Success rate, roughly 1.5× the performance of the best baseline (MAS).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ViLCo outperforms all four baselines on accuracy across all three tasks. Against EWC [22], ViLCo more than doubles Avg R@1 (IoU=0.5) from 12.51% to 26.24% on MQ while reducing BwF from 24.2 to 2.9; on NLQ, the gap widens to 4.05% vs 6.21% with BwF dropping from 50.05 to 10.60. Against MAS [1], ViLCo improves MQ Avg R@1 (IoU=0.5) by 16.36 points (9.88% → 26.24%) and VQ Avg tAP25 by 5.72 points (12.13% → 17.85%). Against iCaRL [35], the closest replay-based competitor, ViLCo gains 2.58 points on MQ Avg R@1 (IoU=0.5) (23.66% → 26.24%) with a lower BwF (4.6 → 2.9). Against BiC [51], ViLCo achieves a dramatic accuracy advantage (26.24% vs 3.39% on MQ Avg R@1 IoU=0.5), though BiC reports a marginally lower BwF (1.4 vs 2.9) on MQ—however, BiC's near-zero accuracy renders this forgetting metric practically meaningless, and on NLQ and VQ ViLCo also achieves the lowest BwF among all methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the continual learning benchmark lineage (CoRE50, CLeAR, vCLIMB, CLiMB) from single-modality or image-text settings into the video-language domain, and repurposes EgoVLPv2's episodic memory tasks (Moment Query, Natural Language Query, Visual Query) into a novel query-incremental continual learning formulation. The architectural design draws on iCaRL's replay buffer, extended into a dual short-term/long-term episodic memory module with learnable prompt-key pairs, and incorporates CLIP and DINO as frozen encoders. It departs from prior video CL work (vCLIMB, PIVOT) by moving beyond class-incremental classification to open-vocabulary temporal localization and detection over 10-minute egocentric videos, and from prior multimodal CL (CLiMB, TIC-CLIP) by introducing video as a first-class modality with temporal complexity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As the first dedicated benchmark for video-language continual learning, ViLCo-Bench fills a clear gap for researchers targeting embodied AI applications (household robots, autonomous systems) that must continuously process long video streams with natural language queries. The finding that regularization-based CL methods (EWC, MAS) degrade severely in multimodal settings while replay-based methods still suffer substantial forgetting (BwF > 40% on NLQ) highlights that existing CL toolkits are inadequate for this domain. Open questions include extending the benchmark to third-person video, additional modalities (audio, depth), and generative tasks like VQA and video captioning, as well as addressing the US-centric data bias and the heavy reliance on annotated streams that limits real-world applicability.
