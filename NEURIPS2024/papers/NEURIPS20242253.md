## Problem

Existing activation mechanisms in ANNs (e.g., ReLU) achieve partial sparsity but fail to fully suppress responses from irrelevant channels, leaving residual noise that measurably degrades classification accuracy. The paper identifies that each category is only correlated with a sparse subset of channels, yet no prior activation can selectively eliminate the non-zero responses persisting in the remaining channels.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242253/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

DPA is a drop-in replacement for any existing activation function, operating on internal feature maps (H×W×C) within the network. For Vision Transformers it is applied to every block using the class token or global average pool as the global feature vector; for CNNs it is applied only to the last block. At inference, only the threshold activation unit (TAU) is active, so the input/output interface and latency are identical to a standard ReLU-based network.

## Method

DPA comprises three components: a Threshold Activation Unit (TAU) that gates inputs above a threshold τ, a Pre-Activation Forward Memory (PreA-FM) that maintains per-category moving averages of pre-activation global feature values, and a Post-Activation Backward Memory (PostA-BM) that maintains per-category moving averages of binarized post-activation gradients. The forward criterion flags channels whose historical mean value falls below τ as irrelevant; the backward criterion flags channels whose historical mean gradient is negative as irrelevant. The joint criterion is the logical intersection of both, and a channel-denoising loss (L_ch) is added to the task loss to suppress activation responses on jointly-irrelevant channels while preserving relevant ones. The mechanism is parameter-free, end-to-end trainable, and introduces no inference overhead since the memory units are training-only.

## Results

On ViT-Tiny, DPA achieves 91.3% top-1 accuracy on CIFAR-10, 70.5% on CIFAR-100, 80.4% on ImageNet-100, and 72.2% on ImageNet-1K, consistently outperforming all seven activation baselines. The gains are most pronounced on CIFAR-100 (+4.8% over ReLU) and ImageNet-100 (+5.0% over ReLU), while on CNNs (e.g., ResNet-18) improvements are more modest (+1.1% on CIFAR-100, +0.6% on ImageNet-1K). DPA also generalizes to non-vision tasks, improving AUC by 1.2% on DGraph node classification and top-1 accuracy by 1.1% on 20News text classification with BERT.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DPA outperforms all seven activation baselines across every dataset and architecture tested. Against the strongest conventional baselines, ReLU and GELU, DPA gains 1.4–5.0% on ViTs (e.g., +4.8% on CIFAR-100, +5.0% on ImageNet-100 with ViT-Tiny) and 0.1–1.1% on CNNs (e.g., +1.1% on CIFAR-100 with ResNet-18). SiLU is the next-best baseline on ViTs but still trails DPA by 1.4–6.6%. Softplus, ELU, SELU, and GDN are consistently the weakest, with GDN and SELU often 5–10% below DPA on CIFAR-100. The advantage of DPA is largest on smaller datasets (CIFAR-100) and on Transformer architectures, suggesting the channel-denoising mechanism is most beneficial when irrelevant channels carry substantial noise relative to the signal.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by repurposing two well-studied concepts—threshold activation (forward perspective) and gradient attribution (backward perspective, as in Grad-CAM and LRP)—into a unified, online, parameter-free activation mechanism. It explicitly departs from prior activation functions (ReLU, GELU, SiLU, etc.) by arguing they achieve only partial sparsity and fail to fully suppress irrelevant channels, and it distinguishes its channel denoising from channel pruning by targeting responses rather than weights. The biological motivation of sparse cortical connectivity and the pulse-neuron threshold model provide conceptual grounding, but the method itself is a novel synthesis rather than an extension of any single prior line of work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DPA offers a drop-in, parameter-free replacement for standard activations that yields 3–5% accuracy gains across diverse architectures (ViT, DeiT, CaiT, PVT, TNT, ResNet, MobileNet, etc.) and domains (vision, graph, text) with negligible inference overhead, making it practically attractive. A key limitation is that the per-category criterion requires known class labels during training, restricting applicability to unsupervised or open-set settings, and the authors acknowledge that the joint criterion is not always precise when categories share representation-level similarities. Open questions include whether the mechanism can be adapted to label-free or regression tasks (e.g., box regression in detection) and whether the moving-mean statistics remain reliable under distribution shift.
