## Problem

Traditional time series forecasting methods rely on historical numerical patterns and cannot account for sudden disruptions caused by external events such as policy changes, natural disasters, or shifts in public behavior. While prior work has attempted to incorporate textual information, these approaches reduce news to keyword counts or dummy variables, missing nuanced causal relationships. No existing study leverages LLMs' reasoning capabilities to dynamically filter relevant news and iteratively refine selection logic based on prediction errors.

## Input / Output

The model consumes three types of input formatted as a single textual prompt: (1) historical time series values tokenized as individual digit characters (e.g., '7015.7' becomes tokens '7','0','1','5','.','7'), (2) agent-filtered news expressed as JSON entries with summary, region, timestamp, and rationale, and (3) supplementary context (weather, calendar, economic indicators) rendered as natural-language sentences. The output is a sequence of future digit tokens generated autoregressively, with the model operating on raw-scale values (no normalization) to preserve physical interpretability. Granularity varies by domain: half-hourly for Australian electricity, daily for exchange rates and Bitcoin, hourly for California traffic.

## Method

The framework reformulates time series forecasting as conditional next-token prediction P(x_{t+1}|x_{0:t}, e_{0:u}), where e_{0:u} are news text tokens, and fine-tunes LLaMA 2 via LoRA (rank 8–16, lr=1e-4) on paired time-series-and-news prompts. A GPT-4 Turbo reasoning agent uses chain-of-thought and few-shot prompting to filter candidate news into short-term, long-term, and real-time impact categories with explicit rationale, while a separate evaluation agent compares model predictions against ground truth, identifies overlooked news correlated with prediction errors, and generates updated filtering logic. The pipeline iterates: the reasoning agent selects news → the LLM is fine-tuned → the evaluation agent diagnoses missed events → the reasoning agent consolidates refined logic, typically converging in two iterations. This reflection loop allows the system to discover non-obvious causal links (e.g., geopolitical tensions affecting exchange rates) that static keyword-based filtering would miss.

## Results

On the Electricity domain, the proposed method achieves RMSE 280.39 and MAPE 5.15%, outperforming the best baseline (Pyraformer, RMSE 312.42, MAPE 6.87%) by ~10% on RMSE. On Bitcoin, it achieves MAE 2.68×10⁻³ and MAPE 4.95%, edging out PatchTST (MAE 2.85×10⁻³, MAPE 5.13%) and GPT4TS (MAE 2.84×10⁻³, MAPE 5.08%). On Exchange, it attains the best MSE (0.42×10⁻⁴), RMSE (0.65×10⁻²), and MAPE (0.65%), though Informer achieves a lower MAE (1.75×10⁻³ vs. 4.83×10⁻³). On Traffic, the method wins only on MAE (1.43×10⁻²) while Pyraformer leads on MSE (0.98×10⁻³) and RMSE (3.13×10⁻²), reflecting the coarse granularity of available news for road-level traffic.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against 10 baselines spanning transformer-based (Informer, Autoformer, FEDformer, Pyraformer, PatchTST, iTransformer, FiLM), CNN-based (TimesNet), MLP-based (DLinear), and LLM-based (GPT4TS) methods. On Electricity, the proposed method dominates all baselines on every metric, with the largest margin over Autoformer (RMSE 280.39 vs. 501.78, a 44% reduction) and a clear lead over the nearest competitor Pyraformer (RMSE 280.39 vs. 312.42). On Bitcoin, it achieves the best MAE (2.68×10⁻³), MSE (13.41×10⁻⁶), RMSE (3.67×10⁻³), and MAPE (4.95%), narrowly beating PatchTST and GPT4TS which are the closest competitors. On Exchange, the method wins on MSE, RMSE, and MAPE but loses MAE to Informer (4.83 vs. 1.75 ×10⁻³), suggesting Informer captures typical-case errors better while the proposed method better controls outlier magnitudes. On Traffic, the advantage is modest: the method wins only on MAE (1.43×10⁻²) while Pyraformer (MSE 0.98×10⁻³, RMSE 3.13×10⁻²) and TimesNet (RMSE 3.86×10⁻²) outperform it on error-magnitude metrics, which the authors attribute to the coarse geographic granularity of available news relative to road-level traffic data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LLM-for-time-series line initiated by Gruver et al. [17] (next-token prediction for forecasting) and TIME-LLM/TEMPO/FPT, which treat LLMs primarily as numerical regression engines. It departs from that paradigm by leveraging LLMs' language understanding and reasoning capabilities to process unstructured news text as conditional context, building on prior (pre-LLM) attempts to incorporate text into forecasting (keyword counting, sentiment features) but replacing hand-engineered features with agent-driven, chain-of-thought reasoning. The iterative reasoning-agent + evaluation-agent architecture draws on generative agent and Reflexion-style self-reflection work, applied here to the specific problem of news relevance filtering for forecasting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates a practical, end-to-end pipeline for conditioning time series forecasts on filtered news, showing that the approach yields substantial gains in event-driven domains (electricity, exchange, Bitcoin) while remaining modest in domains where news granularity mismatches the signal (traffic). Key limitations include token-length constraints on sequence length, domain-specificity (least useful where human activity has minimal impact), and the non-monotonic behavior of iterative refinement. Open questions include attribution analysis of which news factors drive accuracy, scaling to multi-sequence or longer-horizon forecasting, and real-time deployment where news arrives continuously.
