## Problem

The rapid proliferation of generative models for image and video creation has outpaced the development of trustworthy evaluation metrics; traditional automatic measures like FID, CLIP, and FVD fail to capture nuanced quality and user satisfaction. No existing platform provides a unified, transparent, community-driven evaluation across text-to-image, image editing, and text-to-video tasks simultaneously, and it remains unclear whether current MLLMs can serve as reliable automated judges for generated visual content.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242537/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

For the arena, users submit a text prompt (or a source image plus editing instruction) and receive two anonymous model outputs side-by-side at standardized resolutions (512×512 to 4K for images, 256×256 to 576×1024 for video), then cast one of four votes: left better, right better, tie, or both bad. For GenAI-Bench evaluation, MLLM judges receive a generated image or video frame sequence paired with the original prompt and must output a structured preference label (e.g., [[A>B]]). The platform produces Elo leaderboards with 95% confidence intervals, and the released GenAI-Bench dataset contains ~3.7k filtered preference pairs across the three tasks under an MIT license.

## Method

GenAI-Arena standardizes inference across 35 open-source generative models by fixing hyperparameters and prompt formats, using ImagenHub for image models and a new VideoGenHub library for video models, with a pre-computed GenAI-Museum pool to handle the 5–120 second diffusion inference latency. Rankings are computed via the Bradley-Terry model (logistic regression with MLE over all pairwise comparisons) rather than online Elo updates, with ties handled by splitting votes 50/50 and confidence intervals estimated through 100 rounds of bootstrap resampling using sandwich standard errors. The paper further constructs GenAI-Bench by filtering collected votes with Llama Guard (removing ~85% of T2I prompts due to sexual content) and prompting MLLM judges with task-specific rubrics covering prompt adherence, naturalness, artifacts, and aesthetics. Key findings include that GPT-4o achieves only 49.19% average accuracy against human preference, open-source MLLMs perform far worse, and the Elo system can be biased by imbalanced 'easy' vs. 'hard' game distributions.

## Results

On GenAI-Bench, the best MLLM judge GPT-4o achieves only 49.19% average accuracy in matching human preference votes across text-to-image (45.59%), image editing (53.54%), and text-to-video (48.46%) generation tasks. Gemini-1.5-Pro follows closely at 48.94% average. The best open-source MLLM, LLaVA-1.5-7B, reaches only 31.17% average accuracy, while a random baseline scores 25.47%. Notably, several open-source models (Idefics1 at 2.22%, InstructBLIP at 8.89%, QwenVL at 14.56%) perform substantially worse than random guessing.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper benchmarks 12 MLLM judges against human preference votes collected via GenAI-Arena. GPT-4o (49.19%) narrowly edges out Gemini-1.5-Pro (48.94%) on average accuracy, with both achieving their highest scores on image editing (53.54% and 55.93% respectively) and lowest on image generation (~45%). Among open-source models, LLaVA-1.5-7B leads at 31.17% average, followed by Idefics2 (28.67%), LLaVA-1.6-7B (23.24%), and MiniCPM-V-2.5 (23.20%), all of which remain far below the closed-source leaders. Several models perform worse than the 25.47% random baseline: Idefics1 (2.22%), InstructBLIP (8.89%), QwenVL (14.56%), and CogVLM (17.98%), indicating a severe lack of instruction-following and visual reasoning ability in complex generative evaluation scenarios. The gap between the best closed-source model and the best open-source model is approximately 18 percentage points, highlighting a significant capability gap in MLLM-based visual quality assessment.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends the Chatbot Arena paradigm (Chiang et al., 2024) from LLM evaluation to generative AI, adapting the side-by-side human voting and Bradley-Terry Elo rating framework to image and video generation. It builds on ImagenHub (Ku et al., 2024) for standardized inference, extending the same philosophy to video via the new VideoGenHub library. Unlike prior evaluation platforms (T2I-CompBench, HEIM, VBench, EvalCrafter) that rely on model-based automatic metrics, GenAI-Arena is the first to combine community-driven human preference voting across all three major generative tasks (text-to-image, image editing, text-to-video) with a transparent, open voting process.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The platform fills a critical gap by providing a unified, transparent, and continuously updated leaderboard for 35 generative models across three tasks, which no prior platform achieved simultaneously. The finding that even GPT-4o achieves only 49.19% accuracy in mimicking human preference judgments (barely above chance for 4-way classification) is a significant negative result that challenges the optimistic narrative from prior MLLM-as-judge work (e.g., VIEScore) and highlights that automatic evaluation of visual generation quality remains an open problem. Key limitations include potential bias in the user base, Elo rating sensitivity to 'easy game' vs 'hard game' imbalance, and the relatively small vote counts per model pair, all of which constrain the robustness of the rankings.
