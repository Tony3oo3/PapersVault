## Problem

Diffusion models require hundreds to thousands of neural network evaluations per sample, making inference prohibitively expensive. Existing distributional distillation methods based on score matching are limited to one-step generation, and it is unclear how to extend them to the multi-step regime where the sampling trajectory introduces dependencies that break the score-matching equivalence. This paper asks whether a principled multi-step distillation objective exists that can match or even exceed the quality of the many-step teacher.

## Input / Output

The distilled model takes a class label (ImageNet 64×64, 128×128) or a text prompt encoded by T5-XXL (512×512 text-to-image) together with an initial noise sample z₁ ~ N(0, I), and produces a single RGB image in 1–8 forward passes. Notably, the text-to-image model operates directly in pixel space without an autoencoder or upsampler, and the UViT architecture is shared between teacher and student. The teacher uses 512–1024 NFEs with classifier-free guidance, while the student uses as few as 8 NFEs without guidance.

## Method

The core contribution is a moment matching distillation objective that minimizes the L2 distance between the conditional expectation of clean data given noisy data, E_g[x̃|z_s] under the generator and E_q[x|z_s] under the data distribution, along the multi-step sampling trajectory. Two practical variants are proposed: (1) an alternating optimization scheme that trains an auxiliary denoising model g_φ on generator samples to approximate E_g[x̃|z_s], and (2) an instant parameter-space variant that replaces the auxiliary model with a single infinitesimal gradient step of the teacher, reducing the loss to matching expected teacher gradients between real and generated data (a special case of the Efficient Method of Moments). The key insight is that in the one-step limit the moment matching loss recovers the score-matching objectives of Diff-Instruct and DMD, but the moment matching perspective provides a valid justification for extending to multi-step sampling using the conditional q(z_s|z_t, x̃) rather than the marginal q(z_s|x̃), which is critical for preserving sample diversity. This conditional sampling choice, combined with the moment matching loss, allows the 8-step student to outperform its 1024-step teacher on ImageNet FID.

## Results

On ImageNet 64×64, the 8-step Moment Matching (Alternating) distilled model achieves FID 1.24, outperforming its 1024-step teacher (FID 1.42) and all prior distillation methods at the same NFE budget. On ImageNet 128×128, the 8-step model reaches FID 1.49 versus the teacher's FID 1.76. For text-to-image at 512×512, the 8-step distilled model achieves COCO FID 7.25 (guidance 0), beating the 512-step base model (FID 9.6) and all one-step baselines (DMD: 11.5, InstaFlow: 11.8, UFOGen: 12.8).

## Baselines & Comparisons

At 8 NFE on ImageNet 64×64, Moment Matching (FID 1.24) outperforms MultiStep-CD (FID 1.4, 1.2B params), PD (FID 1.7), TRACT (FID 2.41), and the 1024-step base model (FID 1.42), establishing a new state-of-the-art. At 4 NFE, Moment Matching (FID 1.50) beats MultiStep-CD (FID 1.6) and PD (FID 2.4). At 1 NFE, DMD (FID 2.62) and iCT-deep (FID 3.25) still outperform Moment Matching (FID 3.0), though the gap is small. On ImageNet 128×128, the 8-step model (FID 1.49) again surpasses the teacher (FID 1.76) and MultiStep-CD (FID 2.1). For text-to-image, the 8-step Moment Matching model (COCO FID 7.25) substantially outperforms all one-step methods including DMD (11.5), InstaFlow (11.8), PeRFlow (11.3), and UFOGen (12.8), as well as the 512-step base model (9.6). The Instant 2-batch variant performs comparably to the Alternating version at 4+ NFE but is slightly weaker at 8 NFE for text-to-image (FID 9.5 vs. 7.25).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reframes one-step diffusion distillation methods (Diff-Instruct, DMD, Variational Score Distillation) through the lens of the Generalized Method of Moments, showing that the one-step case is a special case of a broader moment-matching objective that naturally extends to multi-step sampling. The key conceptual departure is that in the multi-step regime, the auxiliary denoising model no longer performs score matching (since z_s is conditioned on z_t rather than being independent), yet the moment matching formulation still provides a valid training signal. The paper also connects to adversarial multi-step distillation methods (Xiao et al., Xu et al.) that share the same conditional q(z_s|z_t, x̃) but use discriminators rather than moment conditions, and to the Efficient Method of Moments (Gallant & Tauchen, 1996) for the parameter-space variant.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: 8-step distilled models achieve state-of-the-art FID on ImageNet, outperforming even their 1000+ step teacher models, which challenges the assumption that more sampling steps always yield better quality. The moment matching perspective provides a clean theoretical justification for why multi-step distillation works when the score matching interpretation breaks down, and the two practical algorithms (alternating optimization and instant parameter-space) offer flexible implementation choices. Open questions include: why exactly does distillation improve over the teacher (the paper speculates about prediction inconsistency across timesteps but does not validate this), whether the approach scales to video or other modalities, and the lack of human evaluation and error bars limits confidence in the reported metric improvements.
