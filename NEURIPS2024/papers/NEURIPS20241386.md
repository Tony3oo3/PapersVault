## Problem

Subseasonal-to-seasonal (S2S) prediction (15–60 days) is doubly sensitive to both initial and boundary conditions, yet existing benchmarks target either short/medium-range weather (≤15 days) or long-range climate (years–decades), leaving S2S largely unexplored. Prior S2S efforts are limited to regional domains, short temporal coverage (<20 years), and lack physics-based evaluation metrics or diverse operational NWP baselines, making it difficult to assess whether data-driven emulators can genuinely extend predictability into this critical regime.

## Input / Output

The benchmark consumes 124 gridded variables at 1.5° daily resolution from three coupled reanalysis products—ERA5 (60 atmospheric variables across 10 pressure levels), ORAS5 (21 ocean/sea-ice variables), and ERA5-Land (43 terrestrial variables)—spanning 1979–present, with physics-based forecasts from four national agencies (UKMO, NCEP, CMA, ECMWF) covering 2016–present at 44-day lead time. Outputs are evaluated with latitude-weighted deterministic metrics (RMSE, ACC, MS-SSIM), probabilistic ensemble metrics (CRPS, CRPSS, Spread/Skill), and two novel spectral-domain physics metrics (Spectral Divergence and Spectral Residual) that quantify how well forecasts preserve multi-scale frequency structure.

## Method

ChaosBench is a benchmark and empirical study rather than a single new architecture. Its core technical contributions are: (1) a fully-coupled Earth-system dataset merging atmospheric, oceanic, and land reanalysis into a unified 124-channel input/target space; (2) two differentiable physics-based loss/metric functions—Spectral Divergence (KL divergence between normalized power spectra of forecast and target over high-wavenumber quantiles) and Spectral Residual (root-mean-squared spectral residual)—that penalize the blurring and high-frequency signal loss observed in long rollouts; (3) a systematic comparison protocol evaluating four state-of-the-art data-driven models (PanguWeather, GraphCast, FourCastNetV2, ViT/ClimaX) against four operational NWP ensembles under both autoregressive and direct training strategies. The key empirical finding is that all current models collapse to unskilled climatology beyond ~15–20 days, and that predictability can be extended through ensemble averaging, direct (non-autoregressive) training to suppress error propagation, and physics-informed architectures (e.g., FNO) that explicitly learn spectral structure.

## Results

At the S2S lead time of Δt=44 days, all data-driven models perform worse than the unskilled climatology baseline: ViT/ClimaX (direct) achieves RMSE of 3.56 K (t-850) vs. climatology's 3.39 K, MS-SSIM of 0.83 vs. 0.85, and SpecDiv of 0.20 vs. 0.01. Among autoregressive models, FourCastNetV2 is best on RMSE (5.11 K) while GraphCast is best on SpecDiv (0.05 for t-850), but all remain substantially below climatology. Physics-based operational forecasts from four national agencies (UKMO, NCEP, CMA, ECMWF) similarly collapse to climatology beyond ~15 days, with ECMWF's high-resolution ensemble retaining the longest predictability range (~15–20 days) before CRPSS drops to zero.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The central finding is that no data-driven model outperforms climatology at 44-day lead time: ViT/ClimaX (direct training) is the closest, with RMSE only 5% above climatology on t-850 (3.56 vs. 3.39 K) and MS-SSIM within 0.02, but still worse on SpecDiv (0.20 vs. 0.01). Among autoregressive models, FourCastNetV2 achieves the lowest RMSE (5.11 K on t-850) while GraphCast achieves the lowest SpecDiv (0.05 on t-850, 0.03 on z-500), yet both remain 50–70% worse than climatology on RMSE. The direct-training strategy (ViT/ClimaX) clearly outperforms all autoregressive counterparts (PW, GC, FCN2) across every metric, suggesting error accumulation over iterative rollouts is a dominant failure mode. On the physics-based side, all four operational NWP systems (UKMO GloSea6, NCEP CFSv2, CMA BCC-CSM2-HR, ECMWF IFS CY41R1) degrade to climatology beyond ~15 days, with ECMWF's 51-member ensemble retaining the longest skillful range (~15–20 days) in CRPSS. Ensembles consistently improve over deterministic forecasts (RMSE ratio < 1, MS-SSIM ratio > 1), and physics-informed architectures (e.g., FNO) outperform pixel-based baselines (ResNet, UNet, Lagged Autoencoder) at matched parameter budgets, though none close the gap to climatology at 44 days.

## Relation to Prior Work

ChaosBench extends the WeatherBench lineage (Rasp et al., 2020; WeatherBench 2, 2023) from short/medium-range (≤15 days) to the subseasonal-to-seasonal (S2S) regime (44 days), while also addressing gaps in existing S2S benchmarks (SubseasonalRodeo, SubseasonalClimateUSA, CliMetLab) that were limited to regional scope, fewer than 30 variables, and lacked physics-based evaluation criteria. Conceptually, it repurposes information-theoretic (KL divergence) and error-based (RMSE) principles into spectral-domain metrics (SpecDiv, SpecRes) to quantify multi-scale signal preservation, and positions data-driven emulators (PanguWeather, GraphCast, FourCastNetV2, ViT/ClimaX) against four operational NWP systems from national agencies as a unified intercomparison. The multi-system reanalysis design (ERA5 + ORAS5 + LRA5) draws on the full Earth system emulation philosophy of recent hybrid physics-ML work (Cresswell-Clay et al., 2024; Qu & Shi, 2023).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark provides the first systematic, global, multi-variable S2S evaluation framework with physics-based and probabilistic metrics, filling a critical gap between weather-scale and climate-scale benchmarks. The central finding—that all current state-of-the-art data-driven and physics-based models collapse to unskilled climatology beyond ~15–20 days—quantifies a long-suspected limitation and establishes a concrete target for future work. Open questions include whether higher-resolution inputs, better ensemble construction, or physics-informed architectures (e.g., FNO) can meaningfully extend the predictability horizon, and whether the spectral-domain metrics can serve as differentiable training objectives. Limitations include the coarse 1.5° resolution matched to NWP outputs, only 8 years of physics-based forecasts, and the absence of data assimilation in the data-driven baselines.
