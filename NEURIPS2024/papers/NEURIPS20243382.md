## Problem

Mainstream motion forecasting methods adopt a one-query-one-trajectory paradigm where each query produces a full trajectory in a single shot, providing only an approximate directional signal without explicit modeling of how agent states evolve across future time steps. This lack of detailed spatiotemporal representation leads to suboptimal accuracy and poor temporal consistency, particularly for long-horizon or high-frequency predictions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243382/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes vectorized HD map polylines (segmented into L sub-segments per line) and agent historical trajectories with per-timestep motion states (position, heading, velocity), encoded within a 150-meter agent-centric radius. It outputs K multi-modal future trajectories per agent of interest along with per-trajectory probability scores. Notably, the internal state queries can operate at a different temporal resolution (T_s) than the output trajectory steps (T_f), allowing the model to balance expressiveness and efficiency for long-horizon or high-frequency forecasting.

## Method

DeMo decouples the standard trajectory query into two orthogonal components: mode queries (K per agent) that capture distinct directional intentions, and state queries (T_s per agent) initialized from time-difference encodings that track dynamic state evolution. Three dedicated modules process these queries: a Mode Localization Module (cross-attention with scene context + self-attention among modes), a State Consistency Module (cross-attention + Bidirectional Mamba for forward/backward temporal scanning), and a Hybrid Coupling Module that adds the two query types and applies cross-attention, a hybrid self-attention spanning both time and mode dimensions, mode-level self-attention, and Bidirectional Mamba. The encoder uses PointNet for map polylines and Unidirectional Mamba blocks for agent history, followed by a Transformer for scene-context interaction. Training is end-to-end with four losses (smooth-L1 regression, cross-entropy classification, and two auxiliary losses enforcing temporal coherence of state queries and directional distinctness of mode queries) under a winner-take-all assignment strategy.

## Results

On the Argoverse 2 single-agent test set, DeMo achieves minFDE₁ of 3.74, minADE₁ of 1.49, minFDE₆ of 1.17, and b-minFDE₆ of 1.84, surpassing the previous SOTA QCNet (minFDE₁=4.30, minADE₁=1.69) by 13.02% and 11.83% respectively. On nuScenes, DeMo attains minFDE₁ of 6.60, minADE₁₀ of 0.89, and MR₁₀ of 0.34, outperforming all baselines except on minADE₅ where it is marginally behind PGP (1.22 vs. 1.27). In the multi-agent setting on Argoverse 2, DeMo achieves avgMinFDE₆ of 1.24 and actorMR₆ of 0.16, leading all compared methods. With model ensembling, DeMo further improves to b-minFDE₆ of 1.73, ranking 5th on the Argoverse 2 leaderboard as of September 2024.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DeMo outperforms all 45 compared baselines across the Argoverse 2 single-agent, multi-agent, and nuScenes benchmarks. Against the closest competitor QCNet [77], DeMo improves minFDE₁ by 13.02% (3.74 vs. 4.30) and minADE₁ by 11.83% (1.49 vs. 1.69) in the single-model setting, and further extends the lead with ensembling (b-minFDE₆: 1.73 vs. 1.78). SmartRefine [76], a post-refinement enhancement of QCNet, is also surpassed on every metric (e.g., minFDE₆: 1.17 vs. 1.23). On nuScenes, DeMo leads on minFDE₁ (6.60 vs. 6.71 for THOMAS), minADE₁₀ (0.89 vs. 0.94 for PGP), and MR₅ (0.43 vs. 0.48 for LAformer), though it trails PGP slightly on minADE₅ (1.22 vs. 1.27). In the multi-agent Argoverse 2 setting, DeMo beats Gnet [19] by 8.8% on avgMinFDE₆ (1.24 vs. 1.46) and by 15.8% on actorMR₆ (0.16 vs. 0.19), despite lacking specialized multi-agent interaction modules. On the Waymo Open Motion Dataset, DeMo (minFDE₆=1.59) falls short of WayFormer (1.46) and MTR (1.78), indicating the method's advantage is most pronounced on Argoverse and nuScenes.

## Relation to Prior Work

DeMo extends the one-query-one-trajectory paradigm established by MTR and QCNet by decoupling trajectory queries into two specialized types: mode queries for directional intentions and state queries for temporal dynamics. This builds on the query-centric formulation of QCNet and the intention-localization idea of MTR, but reorganizes the representation so that multi-modality and temporal consistency are optimized separately before being coupled. The paper also introduces Bidirectional Mamba as a complement to Transformer attention for sequence modeling in motion forecasting, positioning itself as the first work to integrate Mamba with the mainstream Transformer-based trajectory prediction architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The decoupled query paradigm offers a more principled decomposition of the trajectory prediction problem, and the resulting model is simultaneously more accurate (13% minFDE1 improvement over QCNet) and more efficient (5.9M parameters, 38ms inference vs. 7.7M and 94ms for QCNet), making it practically attractive for deployment. The authors acknowledge that the current design does not fully address model efficiency for longer trajectory horizons and plan to explore sparse state representations for deployment-friendliness. An open question is whether the Attention-Mamba hybrid can be further streamlined or whether the decoupling generalizes to other sequential prediction tasks beyond driving.
