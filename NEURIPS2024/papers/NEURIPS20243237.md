## Problem

Heatmap-based human pose estimation discretizes the inherently continuous Gaussian heatmap into fixed-resolution 2D pixel arrays, introducing quantization errors that degrade localization accuracy—especially at low input resolutions where heatmap-based methods can underperform coordinate regression. Existing post-processing tricks (e.g., 0.25-pixel shifts, Taylor-series decoding) impose subjective assumptions and fail to fundamentally resolve the information loss from spatial sampling.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243237/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a cropped person patch (RGB image) processed by a standard backbone encoder (ResNet, HRNet, or TokenPose) to produce downsampled feature maps of size 8×8, and accepts arbitrary 2D query coordinates as additional input to the MLP decoder. The output is a continuous confidence score for each of K body joints at any queried position, which can be sampled at arbitrary resolution during inference without retraining. A progressive coordinate decoding scheme then converts the continuous heatmap into final 2D keypoint coordinates via coarse-to-fine argmax retrieval.

## Method

NerPE generalizes the upsampling factor of sub-pixel convolution to infinity, replacing the discrete PixelShuffle layer with an MLP-based implicit neural representation that maps (local feature vector, normalized relative coordinate) → confidence score, yielding a continuous upsampling function over the 2D image plane. During training, positions are uniformly sampled within each cell (with local ensemble bilinear interpolation at cell boundaries to ensure spatial continuity), and ground-truth confidence scores are computed analytically from continuous Gaussian or Laplace functions centered at the true joint coordinates. The loss is a simple L2 between predicted and ground-truth confidence at each queried position. At inference, the decoupling from spatial resolution allows outputting heatmaps at any resolution; a progressive coordinate decoding strategy first generates a coarse 32×32 heatmap, then iteratively subdivides the region around the maximum activation (4×4 splits with 2-pixel overlap) to achieve sub-pixel localization without computing the full high-resolution heatmap.

## Results

On the COCO validation set, NerPE consistently outperforms discrete heatmap baselines across all three backbones and input resolutions, with the largest gains at low resolution: at 64×64 input, NerPE achieves 62.5 AP with HRNet-W48 (vs. 48.5 for HRNet and 59.7 for SimCC) and 40.8 AP with ResNet-50 (vs. 34.4 for SimBa). At the standard 256×192 resolution, improvements are smaller but consistent (e.g., 76.1 AP vs. 75.1 for HRNet-W48). On MPII (128×128), NerPE achieves PCKh@0.1 of 34.6 vs. 32.6 for SimCC and 26.5 for HRNet, confirming superior sub-pixel localization. On CrowdPose at 64×64, NerPE improves HRNet by 5.0 AP (47.4 vs. 42.4), demonstrating robustness in crowded scenes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NerPE outperforms all five baselines (SimBa, SimCC, HRNet, DARK, and TokenPose) across every backbone and resolution tested, with no losses observed. The most dramatic advantage appears at 64×64 input: NerPE gains +14.0 AP over HRNet and +2.8 AP over SimCC on HRNet-W48, and +7.3 AP over DARK on TokenPose-S, confirming that quantization errors in discrete heatmaps are the dominant failure mode at low resolution. At the standard 256×192 resolution, gains shrink to +0.2–1.4 AP, indicating that discrete methods are already near their ceiling when spatial sampling is fine enough. On MPII, NerPE's PCKh@0.1 score of 34.6 exceeds SimCC (32.6) by 2.0 points and HRNet (26.5) by 8.1 points, showing that the continuous representation yields more precise sub-pixel localization rather than merely higher recall. On CrowdPose at 64×64, NerPE improves HRNet by 5.0 AP (47.4 vs. 42.4) and SimCC by 0.9 AP, demonstrating that the benefit persists in crowded, occluded scenes where localization is hardest.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper generalizes sub-pixel convolution (Shi et al., CVPR 2016) by taking the upsampling factor to infinity, yielding a continuous upsampling function parameterized by an MLP that regresses confidence scores at arbitrary 2D coordinates. It also adopts the local ensemble / bilinear interpolation strategy from the local implicit image function (Chen et al., CVPR 2021) to ensure continuity across cell boundaries. Conceptually, the paper departs from the entire lineage of discrete heatmap regression methods (SimBa, HRNet, TokenPose, SimCC, DARK) that represent heatmaps as fixed-resolution 2D pixel arrays, arguing that the discretization itself—not the heatmap paradigm—is the source of quantization errors that existing post-processing tricks (0.25-pixel shift, Taylor-series decoding) only partially compensate.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical impact is that NerPE decouples heatmap resolution from input resolution, enabling sub-pixel localization at arbitrary output resolution without retraining, and the gains are most pronounced at low input resolutions (e.g., +2.8 AP at 64×64 with HRNet-W48) where discrete methods degrade most. The progressive coordinate decoding further makes high-resolution inference computationally feasible by avoiding full heatmap generation. However, the INR decoder is deliberately kept as a simple MLP to isolate the representation benefit, leaving open whether richer INR architectures (e.g., with attention or adaptive frequency) could yield further gains. The approach is plug-and-play with existing backbones, which lowers the barrier to adoption, but the marginal improvements at standard high-resolution settings (256×192) limit its appeal for practitioners already operating at those resolutions.
