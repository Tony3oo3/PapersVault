## Problem

Existing robotics benchmarks either test only low-level manipulation skills or abstract them away with oracle policies, leaving a gap in evaluating whether vision-language models can compose learned motor skills into novel, higher-level tasks. The paper asks: if a model is taught basic skills like pick, place, and throw, can it generalize to unseen compositional tasks (e.g., sorting by throwing, balancing a scale) without explicit training on those tasks?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241209/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The agent receives RGB images from two cameras (an end-effector-mounted 'first-person' view and a base 'third-person' view) at 256×256 resolution, along with a multi-modal task prompt consisting of interleaved text tokens and reference images (object crops, texture swatches, or keystep goal images). The output is a 7-dimensional continuous action vector: a 6DOF end-effector pose delta (3D translation + 3D rotation) plus a 1D gripper open/close scalar, executed in a closed loop at each timestep.

## Method

ClevrSkills introduces a 33-task curriculum across three compositional levels (L0: 12 basic motor skills, L1: 15 intermediate compositions, L2: 6 complex compositions) built on ManiSkill2, with 330k oracle trajectories generated via RRT-based motion planning (MPLib) for imitation learning. The key architectural contribution is StreamRoboLM, which adapts the LRR model to auto-regressively ingest streaming video during token generation (unlike RoboFlamingo's single-image conditioning), using OPT-1B or Llama-3.2 3B as the base LLM, a ViT vision encoder with cross-attention layers, and an LSTM policy head for continuous action prediction. LoRA fine-tuning preserves the LLM's language capabilities while the vision encoder and policy head are trained from scratch. The evaluation protocol systematically tests zero-shot and fine-tuned generalization from L0→L1 and L0+L1→L2, revealing that all SOTA VLM baselines (JAT, Octo, RoboFlamingo, StreamRoboLM) fail to compose skills in novel ways despite reasonable L0 performance.

## Results

On L0 (base motor skills) tasks, StreamRoboLM achieves 62.91% success rate (OPT) and 62.50% (Llama3), substantially outperforming JAT (23.75%), RoboFlamingo (35.41%), and Octo (34.16%). However, on the core compositional generalization evaluation (L1 and L2 tasks), all models fail: zero-shot success rates are 0% for all models on L1 (except Octo at 0.33%), and even after fine-tuning, the best L2 success rate is only 5.83% (Octo). The oracle policy achieves 100% success across all levels, confirming the tasks are solvable. These results demonstrate that current SOTA VLM-based robotics policies cannot compose learned motor skills into novel higher-level tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

StreamRoboLM clearly outperforms all three baselines on L0 base-skill tasks, achieving 62.91% success (OPT) vs. 23.75% (JAT), 35.41% (RoboFlamingo), and 34.16% (Octo), with the advantage widening on unseen objects/textures (55.41% for Llama3 vs. 24–28% for others). However, on the compositional L1 and L2 tasks that form the paper's core evaluation, the advantage disappears: all models achieve near-zero success rates in the zero-shot setting, and even after fine-tuning, no model exceeds 5.83% on L2. Octo actually achieves the highest fine-tuned L2 success rate (5.83%) and average reward (2106), slightly edging out StreamRoboLM (4.16% / 1047 for OPT). The paper's central finding is that none of the compared models—regardless of architecture (transformer with image tokens, cross-attention VLM, or streaming video LLM)—can reliably compose L0 skills into novel L1/L2 task solutions, highlighting a fundamental gap in current VLM-based robotics policies.

## Relation to Prior Work

ClevrSkills extends the VIMA benchmark [20] by replacing its object-pose action space with low-level end-effector delta actions, enabling explicit testing of whether models can compose learned motor skills (pick, place, throw, push) in novel ways rather than merely generalizing to new objects or scenes. It also builds on the LRR streaming video architecture [4] to propose StreamRoboLM, which processes video auto-regressively rather than conditioning on a single image as in RoboFlamingo [28]. The three-level curriculum (L0→L1→L2) departs from CALVIN [32], which tests simple skill chaining, by requiring genuinely compositional reasoning where higher-level tasks demand non-trivial recombination of skills (e.g., sorting by throwing requires composing the throw skill with texture-based sorting).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The systematic demonstration that all evaluated SOTA VLM-based robotics models (JAT, Octo, RoboFlamingo, StreamRoboLM) achieve near-zero success on L1/L2 compositional tasks—even after fine-tuning on those tasks—confirms that current architectures lack the compositional generalization capability that is a hallmark of human intelligence. This provides a concrete, reproducible benchmark for tracking progress toward this goal. Key limitations include the fully simulated setting, single embodiment (xArm 6), and the absence of error bars, which limits the strength of the negative conclusions. Open questions include whether architectural changes (e.g., explicit planning modules, world models) or scaling can bridge the compositional gap, and whether results transfer to real-world manipulation.
