## Problem

Fine-tuning a pre-trained classifier on a subset of its classes is known to drastically degrade accuracy on the remaining (absent) classes, a phenomenon previously attributed to catastrophic forgetting. This paper asks the fundamental question of what is actually damaged in the fine-tuned model, and discovers that the feature extractor and inter-class relationships are not only preserved but often improved — the sole source of degradation is a logit-scale bias toward fine-tuning classes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20244322/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on standard RGB images (e.g., 224×224 for ResNet-50/ViT-B/32) and outputs a class label over the full pre-trained label space (e.g., all 1000 ImageNet classes), even though fine-tuning data covers only a subset (e.g., 500 classes). At inference, a scalar calibration factor γ is added uniformly to the logits of all absent classes before argmax, requiring no access to absent-class data. The NCM classifier is used purely as a diagnostic probe to isolate feature quality from classifier behavior, not as the deployed classifier.

## Method

The paper systematically decomposes the fine-tuned model's prediction into a binary gate (fine-tuning vs. absent classes) and a multi-class classifier within each group, revealing that the gate is the sole point of failure while within-group discrimination is intact or improved. A simple post-processing calibration — adding a constant γ to all absent-class logits (Eq. 5) — reclaims absent-class accuracy and even surpasses the SOTA Holistic Transfer method [49] on ImageNet-R/S, VTAB, and Office-Home. Two data-free estimation strategies for γ are proposed: Average Logit Gap (ALG), which measures the non-GT logit disparity between fine-tuning and absent classes on training data, and Pseudo Cross-Validation (PCV), which simulates the fine-tuning/absent split on a held-out portion of training data. A gradient-based derivation for a two-layer linear network (Eq. 6) provides a preliminary theoretical explanation: absent-class features are updated in the direction of their most similar fine-tuning-class examples, so if fine-tuning gradients capture downstream domain shift, absent features inherit that improvement.

## Results

On ImageNet-{R,S}, fine-tuning + calibration (γ_ALG) achieves 55.9% overall accuracy (Acc_Y/Y) and 30.5% absent-class accuracy (Acc_U/Y), compared to 47.5% and 23.1% for the SOTA method of Tu et al. On VTAB, the calibrated fine-tuned model reaches 66.8% overall and 48.2% absent-class accuracy, surpassing Tu et al.'s 63.8% and 43.5%. On Office-Home, γ_PCV achieves 72.2% overall accuracy (vs. 72.0% for Tu et al.) and 63.1% absent-class accuracy. The AUSUC metric (area under the seen-unseen accuracy curve) confirms the fine-tuned model's superior trade-off: 0.439 vs. 0.317 on ImageNet-R, 0.586 vs. 0.553 on ImageNet-S, and 0.632 vs. 0.618 on VTAB.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is the SOTA method from Tu et al. (NeurIPS 2023), which combines a novel SGD strategy, feature rank regularization, distillation, and frozen classifiers. The paper's simple post-processing calibration (adding a bias γ to absent-class logits) outperforms Tu et al. on overall accuracy (Acc_Y/Y) across all three benchmarks: by 8.4 points on ImageNet-{R,S} (55.9 vs. 47.5 with γ_ALG), by 3.0 points on VTAB (66.8 vs. 63.8), and by 0.2 points on Office-Home (72.2 vs. 72.0 with γ_PCV). On absent-class accuracy (Acc_U/Y), calibration wins decisively on ImageNet-{R,S} (30.5–54.0 vs. 23.1) and VTAB (48.2–67.8 vs. 43.5), but trails Tu et al. on Office-Home (44.9–63.1 vs. 66.6) unless the oracle γ_★ is used (66.9 vs. 66.6). In terms of the AUSUC trade-off metric, the fine-tuned model with calibration consistently dominates: 0.439 vs. 0.317 (ImageNet-R), 0.586 vs. 0.553 (ImageNet-S), and 0.632 vs. 0.618 (VTAB), indicating a superior seen-unseen accuracy frontier across the full range of γ values.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Tu et al. (NeurIPS 2023), who formalized the Holistic Transfer setting and proposed a complex training-time solution (novel SGD strategy, feature rank regularization, distillation, and frozen classifiers) to preserve absent-class accuracy after fine-tuning with a subset of classes. The key departure is diagnostic: rather than treating the accuracy drop as catastrophic forgetting requiring elaborate regularization, this paper shows the damage is confined to a logit-scale bias, making a borrowed post-processing calibration trick from zero-shot learning [Chao et al., ECCV 2016] sufficient. The analysis also connects to the broader literature on class-imbalanced calibration, continual learning, and domain adaptation, but positions itself as an interpretive study rather than a methodological contribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that fine-tuning with a subset of classes actually improves (not degrades) absent-class features and preserves class relationships challenges the prevailing catastrophic-forgetting narrative and suggests that complex training-time safeguards may be unnecessary in many practical settings. The practical implication is significant: a single scalar added to absent-class logits at inference time recovers pre-trained capability and even surpasses the SOTA training-time method on AUSUC across ImageNet-R/S, VTAB, and Office-Home. Open questions include a rigorous theoretical account of why absent-class features improve (the paper offers only a preliminary gradient-based argument), whether the benign behavior holds under extreme class-size imbalances or with adaptive optimizers at default hyperparameters, and whether the insight transfers to parameter-efficient fine-tuning or multi-modal foundation models.
