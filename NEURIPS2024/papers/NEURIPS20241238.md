## Problem

When adapting CLIP to downstream classification via soft prompt tuning, a two-level misalignment arises: task misalignment (pre-training objective vs. downstream objective) and data misalignment (training/test class and distribution mismatch). While prompt tuning addresses the former, the latter causes the learned prompts to overfit base classes, degrading new-class recognition. The paper identifies, via a structural causal model, that task-irrelevant generative factors retained by the prompt act as unobservable confounders between images and new-class labels, and that the back-door adjustment is inapplicable because these factors cannot be directly observed.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a single RGB image, which is encoded M times (once per template) using different augmentation strategies (e.g., random crop, color jitter, translation) through a frozen CLIP ViT-B/16 image encoder. On the text side, M learnable prompt templates (each with 2 learnable tokens at multiple prompt depths) are fed through the frozen text encoder to produce per-template class embeddings. The output is a fused class probability vector obtained by iteratively combining per-template beliefs and uncertainties via Dempster-Shafer evidence fusion, yielding both a predicted class label and an explicit uncertainty estimate for the prediction.

## Method

CDC implements the front-door adjustment by introducing task-relevant semantics S as an intermediate variable between images X and predicted labels Ŷ, decomposing the causal effect into P(s|x) and P(y|s). The first component, Visual-Language Dual Semantic Decoupling (VSD), enforces that M templates capture distinct semantic sets: on the image branch, each template is paired with a unique augmentation to encourage different feature invariances; on the text branch, a decoupling loss (Eq. 3) maximizes the entropy of cross-template classification, while a consistency loss (Eq. 5) anchors learned embeddings to hand-crafted CLIP prompts. The second component, Decoupled Semantic Trusted Classification (DSTC), computes per-template evidence e_c^m = h(sim(w_c^m, v)), converts it into Dirichlet-based beliefs and uncertainties (Eq. 6), and iteratively fuses evidence across all M templates (Eq. 8) to produce the final prediction (Eq. 9). During training, the standard cross-entropy is replaced by a trusted cross-entropy loss (Eq. 10) that models the Dirichlet concentration parameters, and the total objective (Eq. 11) combines this with the decoupling and consistency losses weighted by β and γ.

## Results

On the base-to-novel generalization setting (16-shot, 11 datasets), CDC achieves an average HM of 80.25%, improving over MaPLe (78.55%) by +1.70%, CoCoOp (75.83%) by +4.42%, and CoOp (71.66%) by +8.59%. The gains are most pronounced on challenging fine-grained and texture datasets: EuroSAT (+5.91% HM over MaPLe), DTD (+4.06%), and UCF-101 (+2.90%). In the cross-domain setting, CDC improves average accuracy over MaPLe by 0.65% across ImageNetV2, ImageNet-S, ImageNet-A, and ImageNet-R, with the largest gain on ImageNet-S (+1.18%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CDC is compared against three prompt-tuning baselines (CoOp, CoCoOp, MaPLe) and the zero-shot CLIP model. Against MaPLe (the strongest baseline), CDC wins on all 11 datasets for base-class accuracy and on 10 of 11 for new-class accuracy, yielding a +1.70% average HM improvement; the largest per-dataset gains are on EuroSAT (+5.91% HM), DTD (+4.06%), and UCF-101 (+2.90%). Against CoCoOp, CDC improves average HM by +4.42%, with particularly large margins on DTD (+7.37%) and EuroSAT (+17.05%), where CoCoOp's conditional prompts struggle with distribution shift. Against CoOp, the gap is even wider (+8.59% HM), reflecting CoOp's severe overfitting to base classes (63.22% new-class accuracy vs. CDC's 77.38%). In the cross-domain setting (Table 3), CDC outperforms MaPLe on 3 of 4 ImageNet variants (ImageNetV2 +0.80%, ImageNet-S +1.18%, ImageNet-R +1.12%) but slightly underperforms on ImageNet-A (−0.50%), while still surpassing zero-shot CLIP by 3.75% on average.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the CLIP prompt-tuning lineage (CoOp → CoCoOp → MaPLe) by providing a causal explanation for the well-observed but poorly understood overfitting-to-base-classes phenomenon. It departs from prior prompt methods by introducing a structural causal model that identifies task-irrelevant generative factors as unobservable confounders, then applies Pearl's front-door adjustment via multiple semantically decoupled templates (VSD) and Dempster-Shafer evidence fusion (DSTC). The closest prior work is ArGue, which also leverages multiple semantic perspectives but relies on external LLMs for attribute generation; CDC achieves comparable results end-to-end without external knowledge. The Dempster-Shafer fusion mechanism is borrowed from trusted multi-view classification literature and repurposed here to handle the varying reliability of predictions from different semantic sets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The causal framing offers a principled, interpretable explanation for why prompt tuning degrades novel-class performance, moving beyond the empirical observation of overfitting to identify the specific confounding mechanism. This could inform future work on other VLM adaptation problems where unobservable factors create spurious correlations. However, the practical gains are modest (1.70% HM over MaPLe on average), and the computational overhead of encoding images M times limits real-world deployment. Open questions include whether the front-door framework generalizes to non-classification tasks (detection, segmentation), whether the number of templates M can be adapted per-dataset, and whether the approach transfers to other VLMs beyond CLIP.
