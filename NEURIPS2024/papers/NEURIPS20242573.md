## Problem

Semi-parametric language models like kNN-LM can provide token-level attribution by interpolating LM outputs with non-parametric retrieval distributions, but they suffer from slow inference and degraded text fluency. In-context retrieval augmentation improves factual grounding but does not reliably guarantee faithful span-level attribution of generated content to its sources. The paper addresses how to incorporate real-world text spans of arbitrary length into LM generations with direct attribution while simultaneously improving generation speed.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt (e.g., a question or article prefix) and a pre-built corpus index (Wikipedia ~33M passages or Pile of Law ~15M passages, each under 200 tokens) as input, alongside a frozen Llama-2-Chat model (7B/13B/70B). At each generation step, it produces a segment of 1 to n tokens (n=64 max) rather than a single token, where multi-token segments are verbatim spans copied from the corpus, enabling span-level attribution. The output is a generated text with explicit provenance: consecutive tokens from the same source document are tracked, yielding an attribution ratio (33–95% of tokens traceable to corpus) and average attributed span length (3–28 tokens).

## Method

NEST performs a two-stage k-NN search at each inference step: a first-stage hybrid dense (DRAGON+) and sparse (BM25) passage retrieval narrows the corpus to top-b=40 passages, and a second-stage token-level kNN search builds a key-value datastore on the fly from those passages using the LM's hidden states. A novel Relative Retrieval Confidence (RRC) score, computed from the min-max ratio of retrieval similarities, dynamically sets the interpolation coefficient λ_t between the parametric LM distribution and the non-parametric kNN distribution, replacing the fixed λ in standard kNN-LM. When retrieval confidence exceeds a threshold δ, NEST extends the selected token to its n-gram continuation in the corpus (dynamic span selection), and then applies a relaxed speculative decoding procedure that accepts only the prefix of the span deemed highly probable by the mixture model, using a relaxation factor γ to control leniency. This multi-token acceptance amortizes the per-step retrieval cost and yields a 1.8× speedup on Llama-2-Chat 70B while maintaining or improving generation quality.

## Results

On Llama-2-Chat 70B, NEST achieves a 42.3% ROUGE-1 improvement over the base LM on WikiText-103 (32.6 vs. 22.9) and a 21.6% FACTSCORE improvement on Biography (43.8 vs. 37.9 base, though RA reaches 45.9). NEST is competitive with in-context retrieval augmentation (RA) on MMLU (44.4 vs. 46.2 for RA) and TruthfulQA (ΔBLEU 0.17 vs. −0.13 for RA), while the combined RA-NEST achieves the best average QA score (49.8 vs. 49.9 for RA). NEST delivers a 1.8× inference speedup on Llama-2-Chat 70B and provides span-level attribution for 33–95% of generated tokens depending on task and model size.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Two-Stage kNN-LM, NEST consistently wins: on WikiText-103 (70B) it improves ROUGE-1 from 26.1 to 32.6 and PPL from 7.1 to 6.3; on TruthfulQA it raises ΔBLEU from 0.08 to 0.17; and on Biography it lifts FACTSCORE from 44.0 to 43.8 (nearly tied) while increasing #Facts from 37.4 to 38.0. Against In-Context RA, NEST is competitive but does not uniformly dominate: RA achieves higher QA averages (49.9 vs. 45.2 for 70B) and higher Biography FACTSCORE (45.9 vs. 43.8), while NEST outperforms RA on TruthfulQA (ΔBLEU 0.17 vs. −0.13) where in-context retrieval is hurt by adversarial prompts. The combined RA-NEST matches or slightly exceeds RA on QA (49.8 vs. 49.9) and MMLU (46.2 vs. 46.2) while adding span-level attribution that neither baseline provides. The paper notes that the gap between base LMs and retrieval methods narrows for the 70B model, consistent with prior findings that retrieval benefits smaller models most.

## Relation to Prior Work

NEST extends kNN-LM (Khandelwal et al., 2020) by replacing its fixed interpolation coefficient with a dynamic Relative Retrieval Confidence score, and by adding span-level selection inspired by Copy Generator (Lan et al., 2023) followed by a relaxed speculative decoding procedure adapted from Leviathan et al. (2023). Unlike in-context retrieval augmentation (Ram et al., 2023) which prepends passages to the prompt, NEST operates at the output level, interpolating the LM distribution with a non-parametric distribution computed on-the-fly from a two-stage (passage-then-token) retrieval pipeline. The two-stage retrieval design borrows from standard information-retrieval practice (dense + sparse hybrid search), and the relaxed acceptance criterion departs from classical speculative decoding by upper-bounding the acceptance probability since the proposal distribution is unknown beyond the first token.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

NEST addresses a practical gap: prior semi-parametric LMs like kNN-LM provide token-level attribution but degrade fluency and offer no speed benefit, while in-context RA improves quality but lacks fine-grained source attribution and inflates context length. By generating multi-token spans with span-level attribution and a 1.8× inference speedup, NEST makes retrieval-grounded generation more deployable. Open questions include whether the approach scales to much larger corpora (the two-stage design still requires on-the-fly encoding of retrieved passages), how it interacts with in-context few-shot demonstrations (which the current retrievers cannot exploit), and whether fine-tuning the integrated system would close the remaining gap to in-context RA on adversarial benchmarks like TruthfulQA.
