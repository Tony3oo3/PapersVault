## Problem

Off-dynamics RL methods like DARC train a policy in a source domain using modified rewards to match target optimal trajectory distributions, but deploying the resulting policy in the target domain causes significant performance degradation because the learned policy is inherently suboptimal under dynamics shift. The prior theoretical guarantee relies on a restrictive 'no-exploit' assumption that the optimal target policy behaves similarly in both domains, which fails under large dynamics shifts (e.g., broken source environments). This paper identifies this suboptimality gap and asks how to transfer the source-domain behavior of the DARC policy to the target domain without requiring the no-exploit assumption.

## Input / Output

The method consumes three data streams: (1) source domain rollouts (s_t, a_t, s_{t+1}) used for the bulk of training, (2) a small amount of target domain transitions (s_t, a_t, s_{t+1}) — only 1% of source rollouts — used solely to estimate importance weights via domain classifiers, and (3) DARC expert trajectories collected from the source domain serving as imitation demonstrations. Critically, the target domain reward is never accessed. The output is a single target-domain policy ζ trained with SAC, evaluated by expected cumulative reward under target dynamics. The imitation component operates on state-only pairs (s_t, s_{t+1}) rather than (s_t, a_t), deliberately avoiding action-level mimicry that would recover the suboptimal DARC policy.

## Method

DARAIL is a two-stage algorithm. Stage 1 trains a DARC policy in the source domain using a modified reward r + Δr (where Δr = log p_trg/p_src estimated via two binary domain classifiers), producing expert trajectories whose source-domain distribution matches the target optimal trajectory distribution. Stage 2 performs generative adversarial imitation from observation (GAILfo): a discriminator D_ω is trained to distinguish DARC source trajectories from the generator policy's rollouts (re-weighted by importance sampling), and the generator policy ζ is optimized via SAC using a novel Reward Augmented Estimator R_AE = -log D_ω + ρ(r_src + log D_ω). This R_AE combines the discriminator's local reward signal with an importance-weighted correction from the true source reward, structurally analogous to a doubly robust estimator, which stabilizes learning under dynamics shift and reduces sensitivity to importance weight clipping. Theoretically, the paper proves a finite-sample error bound decomposed into DARC source-domain error and imitation learning generalization error (approximation + estimation), requiring only a bounded importance weight rather than the no-exploit assumption.

## Results

On the broken source environment setting, DARAIL achieves target-domain rewards of 7067 (HalfCheetah), 5357 (Ant), 4366 (Walker2d), and −13.7 (Reacher), outperforming DARC's target-domain evaluation by approximately 40% on average (e.g., 7067 vs. 4133 on HalfCheetah, 4366 vs. 2669 on Walker2d). Under the more severe 1.5× gravity shift, the advantage is even larger: DARAIL reaches 4409 on Walker2d versus DARC's 257, and 3472 on Ant versus DARC's 1587. DARAIL also matches or exceeds DARC's source-domain training reward (the expert it imitates), confirming that the imitation step successfully transfers behavior without losing the source-domain performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DARAIL outperforms DARC's target-domain evaluation in all four environments, with the gap widening under larger dynamics shifts (e.g., Walker2d under 1.5× gravity: 4409 vs. 257). Against the importance-sampling baselines, DARAIL beats IS-R in all four environments and IS-ACL in Ant (5357 vs. 2753) and Walker2d (4366 vs. 3881), while achieving comparable results on HalfCheetah (7067 vs. 6934) and Reacher (−13.7 vs. −14.1); notably, IS-ACL is highly sensitive to the importance-weight clipping interval, whereas DARAIL's reward-augmented estimator R_AE is robust across intervals. The ablation DAIL (imitation without reward augmentation) lags DARAIL substantially (e.g., 3239 vs. 5357 on Ant), confirming the critical role of the doubly-robust-style reward correction. DARAIL also dominates the model-based MBPO (e.g., 4366 vs. 1012 on Walker2d), the reward-alignment MATL (which requires target-domain reward access), and the action-transformation GARAT in every environment tested.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on DARC (Eysenbach et al., 2020), which trains a policy in a source domain using a modified reward derived from reverse KL divergence matching between source and target optimal trajectory distributions. The key departure is recognizing that DARC's learned policy, while producing source-domain trajectories resembling target-optimal ones, suffers systematic performance degradation when deployed in the target domain. DARAIL addresses this by appending a generative adversarial imitation learning from observation (GAILfo) stage that transfers the DARC policy's state-space behavior to the target domain, with a novel reward augmented estimator (R_AE) inspired by doubly robust off-policy evaluation to stabilize policy optimization under dynamics shift.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical recipe for off-dynamics RL in settings where target-domain interaction is expensive or risky (medical treatment, autonomous driving), requiring only ~1% target rollouts. The theoretical contribution relaxes DARC's restrictive 'no exploit set' assumption (π* ∈ Π_noexploit) to a milder bounded-importance-weight condition, making the analysis applicable to larger dynamics shifts. Limitations include evaluation on only four low-dimensional Mujoco environments, dependence on accurate importance weight estimation, and the two-stage training pipeline (DARC then GAILfo) adding complexity. Open questions include extension to safety-constrained settings, more severe domain gaps, and higher-dimensional continuous control tasks.
