## Problem

LLM and VLM agents rely on high-quality in-context exemplars for decision-making, but existing methods either require domain experts to hand-write examples, store raw trajectories without abstraction, or only introspect on failures. This paper asks whether VLMs can automatically generate their own useful examples from noisy, sub-optimal demonstrations by distilling cognitive abstractions that capture task dynamics, causal structure, and state transitions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242417/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes noisy trajectories (observation-action pairs) sourced from human non-experts, passive RGB video (with actions inferred by a DETR-based inverse dynamics model), or agent-generated paths, alongside a text instruction, visual observations (egocentric RGB images, webpage screenshots, or 12 video frames annotated with set-of-marks), a textual state description, and a fixed action API. At deployment, the VLM produces an action sequence (Python program for TEACh, individual click/type actions for web tasks, or a verb+noun pair for Ego4D action anticipation) conditioned on the current observation and top-K retrieved abstracted examples.

## Method

ICAL operates in two phases per demonstration. First, a VLM (GPT-4V) transforms a noisy trajectory into an optimized action sequence while generating four types of language abstractions: task/causal relationships, object state changes, temporal subgoal decomposition, and task-relevant state variables. Second, the optimized trajectory is executed in the environment under human observation; on failure, the human provides natural-language feedback and the VLM revises both the trajectory and abstractions, iterating until success or a feedback budget is exhausted. Successful examples are stored in a growing memory. At inference, retrieval-augmented generation selects top-K examples via a weighted combination of instruction, textual-state, and visual-state cosine similarity (text-embedding-ada-002 and CLIP ViT-B/32) and injects them into the VLM prompt. The key distinction from prior work is that ICAL distills generalizable cognitive abstractions rather than storing raw action plans, and it leverages previously learned abstractions during new-example creation, making learning progressively more efficient.

## Results

On TEACh unseen validation, ICAL achieves 35.1% task success rate and 49.3% goal-condition success, surpassing the prior SOTA HELPER (34.5% SR, 36.7% GC) by 12.6% in GC. On VisualWebArena, ICAL improves average task success from 14.3% (GPT4V+SoM) to 22.7% and from 18.9% (GPT4o+SoM) to 23.4%. On Ego4D action anticipation, ICAL achieves 0.6934 noun edit distance and 0.9242 action edit distance, outperforming few-shot GPT4V CoT (0.7575 noun, 0.9414 action) while remaining competitive with the fully supervised baseline (0.7393 noun, 0.9235 action) despite using 639× less in-domain training data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ICAL outperforms HELPER [68] (the prior SOTA on TEACh) by 12.6% in goal-condition success (49.3% vs. 36.7%) and 0.6% in task success (35.1% vs. 34.5%), while requiring no domain-expert hand-written examples. Against Zero-Shot CoT [39], ICAL achieves a 23.3% absolute improvement in task success (35.1% vs. 11.8%) and 24.7% in GC. On VisualWebArena, ICAL with GPT4V improves average success from 14.3% to 22.7% over GPT4V+SoM [37] (a 58.7% relative gain), and with GPT4o from 18.9% to 23.4%. On Ego4D, ICAL matches the fully supervised SlowFast+Transformer baseline [28] in action edit distance (0.9242 vs. 0.9235) using 639× less in-domain data, and beats few-shot GPT4V CoT by 6.4 in noun edit distance. In the RGB-only TEACh setting (Table S5), ICAL (10.5% SR, 15.4% GC) substantially outperforms E.T. [64] (1.0%, 1.4%), JARVIS [91] (1.7%, 5.4%), FILM [57] (5.5%, 5.8%), and DANLI [89] (5.0%, 10.5%), approaching HELPER's 12.2% SR without hand-designed preconditions or examples.

## Relation to Prior Work

ICAL extends the memory-augmented VLM agent paradigm (Voyager, HELPER, Code as Policies) by replacing raw trajectory storage or expert-written examples with VLM-generated cognitive abstractions (causal relationships, state changes, temporal subgoals, task-relevant visual elements) extracted from noisy demonstrations. Conceptually, the human-in-the-loop verification phase draws on DAgger's iterative expert-feedback loop, but departs by accepting natural language corrections rather than explicit action labels and by updating both the action sequence and the associated abstractions. The retrieval-augmented deployment mechanism builds on standard RAG with multimodal similarity scoring (text-embedding-ada-002 + CLIP), but the key novelty is that the retrieved items are *abstracted* programs rather than raw trajectories, and that the abstraction process itself improves over time as the memory grows.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ICAL addresses a practical bottleneck in embodied and web-agent research: the heavy reliance on domain experts to hand-write high-quality in-context examples (HELPER requires 48–107 lines of expert text per example). By showing that a VLM can distill noisy, sub-optimal demonstrations into effective abstractions with only brief non-expert feedback, the method lowers the barrier to deploying VLM agents in new domains. The demonstrated learning-efficiency curve (71.6% less human feedback in the second half of learning) suggests a path toward self-improving agents that require diminishing human supervision. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond fixed action APIs, whether it can handle adversarially misleading demonstrations, and whether the GPT-4V visual grounding limitations that persist despite in-context learning can be addressed without architectural changes.
