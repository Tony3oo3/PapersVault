## Problem

Existing complexity measures for deep learning models (VC dimension, Rademacher complexity) are intractable to compute for modern architectures, while the earlier effective dimension based on Fisher box-covering requires Lipschitz regularity of log(FIM) that excludes over-parametrized models and demands a global, non-modular computation. This paper seeks a complexity measure that provably bounds generalization error under milder assumptions, is computable layer-by-layer for feedforward networks, and provides pre-training information consistent with post-training performance.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a parametric statistical model (a feedforward neural network with its parameter space Θ ⊂ ℝ^d), a training dataset of N samples, and two scale parameters: a micro-scale covering radius ε ∈ (0,1) and a meso-scale exponent ζ ∈ [0,1). Because standard DNNs are deterministic, the paper introduces a Gaussian stochastic perturbation (variance σ²) at each block to make them compatible with the probabilistic framework. The output is a scalar complexity value (2sED or its layerwise lower bound) and, via Theorem 5.1, an explicit high-probability upper bound on the generalization error of the form C·ε_n with probability at least 1 − H·ε_n^{−d_ζ(ε_n)}·n^{−K/γ}.

## Method

The two-scale effective dimension (2sED) is defined as a convex combination of the parameter-space dimension d and a Fisher-information-based term involving the expected determinant of (I + ε^{ζ−1} F̂(ϑ)^{1/2}), where F̂ is the trace-normalized Fisher information matrix. The two scales arise from a covering argument: the meso-scale δ = ε^ζ partitions the parameter space into cubes, and within each cube the micro-scale ε controls the size of β-Fisher boxes used for local covering, with β = ε^{2−2ζ} regularizing small eigenvalues. For Markovian (feedforward) models, a single application of Jensen's inequality yields the lower 2sED, which decomposes additively across layers and can be computed sequentially, drastically reducing memory and computation. The generalization bound (Theorem 5.1) is proved by combining the covering estimate with Hoeffding-type concentration, requiring only L-Lipschitz continuity of the FIM field (not of log FIM) and ζ ≥ 2/3, thereby accommodating over-parametrized architectures where the FIM degenerates.

## Results

The lower 2sED is empirically a tight lower bound of the full 2sED, with the two quantities being nearly indistinguishable across all tested architectures and covering radii (Figure 1). On Covertype with 10,000 training samples, MLP 54-16-7 (976 params) achieves the lowest training loss, consistent with its highest lower 2sED; with 100,000 samples, MLP 54-13-11-9-7 (1007 params) becomes the best performer, matching the shift in lower 2sED ordering at smaller ε. The bottleneck architecture MLP 54-10-2-10-25-7 (1005 params) exhibits the lowest lower 2sED, confirming its reduced expressiveness despite having more parameters than MLP 54-16-7. Monte Carlo estimates of the lower 2sED remain stable across 100–1000 samples and parameter vectors, and the impact of the stochastic perturbation variance σ² is negligible.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not benchmark 2sED against other complexity measures (e.g., VC dimension, Rademacher complexity, or the original effective dimension of [2]) with quantitative comparisons; instead, it validates the measure internally. The key empirical validation is that the lower 2sED correctly orders architectures by their training loss: on Covertype with 10,000 samples, MLP 54-16-7 (highest lower 2sED) achieves the lowest training loss, while the bottleneck model MLP 54-10-2-10-25-7 (lowest lower 2sED) performs worst, despite having more parameters. With 100,000 samples the ordering shifts to favor MLP 54-13-11-9-7, which the lower 2sED also predicts at the corresponding smaller covering radius ε. For CNNs on CIFAR-10, the three architectures (CNN 7-5, CNN 3-5-3-6, CNN 3-6-5-3) are similarly ordered by their lower 2sED values and their training loss curves confirm this ranking. The paper positions the lower 2sED as a training-free model selection tool, contrasting it qualitatively with VC dimension and Rademacher complexity, which it notes are intractable to compute for modern deep networks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the effective dimension framework introduced by Abbas et al. [2] and Berezniuk et al. [5], which defined a box-counting dimension of the statistical manifold using Fisher balls. The key departure is the introduction of a two-scale structure (micro-scale ε for Fisher ball size and meso-scale ε^ζ for partition components), which allows the generalization bound to hold under weaker assumptions—specifically removing the Lipschitz regularity requirement on log(FIM) that previously excluded over-parametrized models. Additionally, the paper introduces a 'lower 2sED' for Markovian (feedforward) models that exploits the layer-by-layer structure via Jensen's inequality, addressing the scalability bottleneck of computing the full Fisher information matrix eigenvalues.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 2sED provides a theoretically grounded, data-independent complexity measure that provably bounds generalization error, filling a gap left by VC dimension and Rademacher complexity which are intractable for modern DNNs. The modular lower 2sED is a practical advance for model selection in feedforward architectures, as it can be computed sequentially layer-by-layer without full FIM diagonalization. However, the empirical validation is limited to small models (~1,000–10,000 parameters) on small datasets, and the authors acknowledge that computing FIM eigenvalues remains intractable for large-scale networks. Open questions include whether efficient spectral approximations (e.g., via K-FAC or stochastic methods) can make the lower 2sED practical for modern architectures, and whether the two-scale structure captures meaningful architectural differences at scale.
