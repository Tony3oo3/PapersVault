## Problem

Transformer-based diffusion models (DiT, SiT) face two fundamental barriers for arbitrary-resolution image generation: quadratic O(n²) attention cost that scales poorly with resolution, and absolute position embeddings that overfit to training resolution, hindering extrapolation to unseen dimensions. The paper asks whether modern convolutional architectures—specifically deformable convolutions with their linear complexity and relative-position inductive bias—can match or surpass transformer-based generators while natively supporting flexible resolution handling.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242791/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes Gaussian noise, a class label (injected via adaLN-Zero conditioning), and a target spatial resolution, producing a class-conditional RGB image at that resolution. Trained exclusively on center-cropped 256×256 ImageNet, a single FlowDCN-XL/2 checkpoint generates images at arbitrary resolutions (e.g., 384×384, 224×448, 448×224, 512×512) without retraining or multi-aspect-ratio data augmentation. The only resolution-dependent adjustment is a simple scaling of the S_max hyperparameter by the train-to-test aspect ratio, making the inference pipeline resolution-agnostic beyond one scalar.

## Method

FlowDCN replaces the transformer backbone with a stack of Group-wise Multi-Scale Deformable Convolution (MultiScaleDCN) blocks interleaved with SwiGLU feed-forward layers, all conditioned via adaLN-Zero and normalized with RMSNorm. The key architectural insight is decoupling the deformable field into a learnable per-group scale (bounded by sigmoid × S_max) and a fixed direction prior, so that different groups aggregate features at different spatial ranges—small-scale groups capture high-frequency local detail while large-scale groups capture long-range semantics—without progressive downsampling or long residual connections. Training uses linear-based flow matching, regressing the velocity field v_t = x − ε with a simple L2 loss. For resolution extrapolation, the paper proposes Scale Adjustment: multiplying the predicted scale by H_test/H_train and W_test/W_train to preserve the effective receptive field at unseen resolutions, a single-line change that yields comparable extrapolation quality to purpose-built methods like FiT's RoPE2D.

## Results

On the 256×256 ImageNet benchmark, FlowDCN-XL/2 achieves a SoTA sFID of 4.30 and FID of 2.13 using Euler ODE solver with CFG=1.375, trained on only 384M images (≈1/5 of transformer counterparts). On 512×512 ImageNet, after just 100K finetuning steps, FlowDCN-XL/2 reaches 2.44 FID and 4.53 sFID. The model also achieves 8% fewer parameters, 20% fewer FLOPs, and 20% lower per-step latency compared to DiT/SiT at the same model size, while on 32×32 CIFAR-10, FlowDCN-S/2 outperforms SiT-S/2 by 2.0 FID (5.47 vs 7.42).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FlowDCN-XL/2 achieves a SoTA sFID of 4.30 on 256×256 ImageNet, beating SiT-XL/2 (4.50), DiffusionSSM-XL (4.49), and DiT-XL/2 (4.60), while using only 384M training images versus 1792M for SiT/DiT (≈1/5). On FID, FlowDCN (2.13) slightly trails SiT-XL/2 (2.06) but outperforms DiT-XL/2 (2.27), DiffusionSSM-XL (2.28), U-ViT-H/2 (2.29), ADM-U (3.60), LDM-4 (3.95), and FiT-XL/2 (4.27). On 512×512 ImageNet, FlowDCN-XL/2 (2.44 FID, 4.53 sFID) surpasses SiT-XL/2-G (2.62 FID, 4.18 sFID) on FID and DiT-XL/2-G (3.04 FID, 5.02 sFID) on both metrics after only 100K finetuning steps. On 32×32 CIFAR-10, FlowDCN-S/2 (5.47 FID) outperforms SiT-S/2 (7.42 FID) by a 2.0 FID margin. For resolution extrapolation, FlowDCN-B/2 achieves 34.4 FID at 320×320 (vs FiT-B's 61.4 and DiT-B's 95.5), and FlowDCN-XL/2 shows comparable performance to FiT-XL/2 at 320×320 (5.86 vs 5.42 FID) without any multi-aspect-ratio training.

## Relation to Prior Work

FlowDCN departs from the dominant transformer-based paradigm for diffusion image generation (DiT, SiT) by proposing a purely convolutional architecture built on a novel group-wise multiscale deformable convolution block. It inherits the flow matching training framework from Lipman et al. and the no-long-residual, adaLN-Zero conditioning design from DiT, but replaces self-attention with a decoupled scale-and-direction deformable convolution that assigns different scale priors to different groups. The efficient deformable convolution operator builds on DCNv4 (Xiong et al.), re-implemented in Triton-lang for generation-specific input shapes, while the resolution extrapolation strategy (Scale Adjustment) offers a simpler alternative to FiT's RoPE-based positional encoding approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a linear-complexity convolutional architecture can match or exceed transformer-based diffusion models in image quality while using 20% fewer FLOPs, 8% fewer parameters, and roughly 1/5 the training data, challenging the assumption that attention is necessary for high-fidelity generative modeling. The Scale Adjustment technique provides a lightweight path to arbitrary-resolution generation without multi-aspect-ratio training, though its benefit is primarily visual rather than metric-driven. Key open questions include whether the approach scales to text-to-image generation, whether the acknowledged inefficiency of the deformable convolution backward pass limits practical training at larger scales, and whether the convergence advantage persists beyond ImageNet-class-conditional settings.
