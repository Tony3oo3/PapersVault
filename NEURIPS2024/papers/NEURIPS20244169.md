## Problem

Scaling multimodal LLMs has focused almost exclusively on the LLM side—larger language backbones and more text-image training data—while vision-side scaling (multiple encoders, larger ViTs, complex connectors) inflates activated parameters and visual token counts, making it inefficient. Although Mixture-of-Experts has become the de-facto scaling strategy for LLMs, its application to the vision encoder and vision-language connector of multimodal LLMs remains largely unexplored.

## Input / Output

CuMo accepts an RGB image (processed at 336×336 through CLIP ViT-L, with an additional 3× resolution pass whose feature maps are concatenated channel-wise to keep the total visual token count unchanged) paired with a free-form text instruction or question. The model produces a single text response generated by the LLM (Mistral-7B or Mixtral-8×7B) using greedy decoding, evaluated across VQA, OCR, and open-ended instruction-following benchmarks.

## Method

CuMo replaces every dense MLP block in both the CLIP ViT-L vision encoder and the two-layer MLP connector with a Top-K sparsely-gated MoE block (Top-2-in-4 for CLIP, Top-2-in-4 for the connector), adding only ~0.22 B activated parameters over the baseline. To stabilize training, each expert is co-upcycled—initialized from the corresponding pre-trained dense MLP—rather than trained from scratch, and a three-stage recipe is used: (1) pre-train the MLP connector on LLaVA-558K, (2) pre-finetune all parameters on ALLaVA caption data as a warm-up, and (3) perform visual instruction tuning on ~1.65 M open-source samples with the MoE blocks active. Auxiliary load-balance and router-z losses (coefficients 0.1 and 0.01) are applied to every MoE block to prevent expert collapse. The LLM itself is not co-upcycled; instead, the pre-trained Mixtral-8×7B is used directly, since experiments show pre-trained MoE LLMs substantially outperform upcycled ones.

## Results

CuMo with Mistral-7B (7.8B activated params) achieves 73.9 on ScienceQA, 67.0 on TextVQA, 1548.6 on MME, 73.0 on MMBench-EN, and 51.0 on MM-Vet, outperforming all 7B-based baselines on most benchmarks. The CuMo Mixtral-8x7B variant (13.5B activated params) reaches 77.9 on ScienceQA, 1639.5 on MME, 75.3 on MMBench-EN, and 45.0 on MMMU, leading the 7B MoE group. Notably, CuMo adds only 0.22B activated parameters over the Mistral-7B baseline (7.58B → 7.80B) while delivering consistent gains across VQA and instruction-following benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

In the 7B group, CuMo (Mistral-7B) outperforms LLaVA-NeXT (Mistral-7B) on ScienceQA (73.9 vs 72.8), MME (1548.6 vs 1498), MMBench-EN (73.0 vs 68.7), and MM-Vet (51.0 vs 47.3), while matching on GQA (64.9 vs 64.8). Against MM1-7B, CuMo wins on ScienceQA (73.9 vs 72.6), MME (1548.6 vs 1529.3), MM-Vet (51.0 vs 42.1), LLaVA-Wild (85.7 vs 81.5), and MMMU (39.1 vs 37.0), but trails on TextVQA (67.0 vs 72.8) and MMBench-EN (73.0 vs 79.0). In the 7B MoE group, CuMo (Mixtral-8×7B) leads SPHINX-MoE on ScienceQA (77.9 vs 74.5), MME (1639.5 vs 1485.3), MM-Vet (48.7 vs 40.9), and MMMU (45.0 vs 31.1), but loses on TextVQA (66.0 vs 68.0) and POPE (85.7 vs 89.6). Compared to MM1-7B-MoE, CuMo wins on ScienceQA (77.9 vs 75.3), MME (1639.5 vs 1629.0), MM-Vet (48.7 vs 47.0), SEED (73.2 vs 70.4), and MMMU (45.0 vs 40.9), while trailing on TextVQA (66.0 vs 72.8) and MMBench-EN (75.3 vs 79.7). Overall, CuMo achieves the best or near-best results across the majority of benchmarks in both model-size groups, with the most consistent gaps on instruction-following metrics (MM-Vet, MMBench) and the hardest reasoning benchmark (MMMU).

## Relation to Prior Work

CuMo extends the sparse Mixture-of-Experts paradigm, which has become standard in large language models (Mixtral, DBRX, Gemini), to the vision side of multimodal LLMs — specifically the CLIP ViT encoder and the MLP connector — a region that prior work had largely neglected in favor of scaling LLMs or adding more visual tokens. The training methodology directly adopts the sparse upcycling strategy from Komatsuzaki et al. [31] to initialize MoE experts from pre-trained dense MLP checkpoints, and the auxiliary load-balancing losses follow ST-MoE [73]. The baseline architecture follows LLaVA v1.5 [44], and multi-resolution visual inputs are borrowed from S² [58]. A key departure from prior MoE-in-LLM work is the explicit finding that co-upcycling the LLM itself is inferior to using a pre-trained MoE LLM (Mixtral), so CuMo restricts MoE to the vision components only.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CuMo demonstrates that meaningful performance gains in multimodal LLMs can be achieved by scaling the vision side with sparse MoE while adding only ~0.2B activated parameters at inference, offering a practical efficiency path for practitioners who cannot afford larger LLMs. The three-stage training recipe (MLP pre-training → pre-finetuning → MoE visual instruction tuning) provides a reproducible blueprint for integrating MoE into existing multimodal pipelines. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger vision encoders (e.g., InternVL's 6B ViT), whether it extends to video understanding, and how to mitigate the hallucination issues the authors acknowledge. The reliance on open-source data alone, while a strength for reproducibility, may limit the ceiling of achievable performance compared to models trained on proprietary data.
