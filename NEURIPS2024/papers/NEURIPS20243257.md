## Problem

Data-driven models fail to generalize under subpopulation shifts where spurious correlations (SC) and group imbalance (GI) co-occur, causing models to latch onto non-causal features and underperform on minority groups. Prior robustness methods either avoid spurious attributes entirely or use them only for reweighting, leaving unexplored the potential of actively leveraging spurious attributes as a constructive tool for disentanglement and targeted data augmentation.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images (28×28 colored MNIST digits or 160×160 CelebA face crops) paired with binary class labels and a known spurious attribute (color or gender). It produces two disentangled latent codes per image — z (spurious/variant) and t (causal/invariant) — plus L synthetic samples per input generated by recombining z from other batch members with the target's t. The final output is a class prediction from a robust classifier trained jointly on original and synthetic samples.

## Method

CODA operates in two stages. In stage one (Correlation-Oriented Disentanglement), a bi-branch VAE with a variance encoder q_ψ(z|x) and an invariance encoder q_φ(t|x) is trained with a decoy classifier p_ω(a|z) that lures spurious attribute information into z, while a heavy KL weight (γ>1) on I(x;z) caps z's capacity so it retains only what the decoy needs; the decoder reconstruction loss then forces t to carry all remaining complementary information. In stage two, synthetic samples are generated by pairing each sample's t_i with z_h drawn randomly from other batch members, producing images that vary in spurious attributes while preserving class identity. A reweighted consistency loss (Eq. 10) then trains the final classifier to produce uniform predictions across the original and all L synthetic variants, downweighting low-confidence synthetic predictions to prevent them from corrupting the consistency target. The framework is modular: the robust classifier can be swapped with ERM, RWG, or GDRO, and the theoretical analysis (Lemma 1) proves the decoder converges to an entropy-regularized version of the data distribution under Soft-IntroVAE training.

## Results

On ColoredMNIST, CODA+RWG achieves the best worst-group accuracy of 72.11% with a maximum group accuracy gap of 2.36%, while CODA+GDRO achieves 71.98% worst-group accuracy and 2.37% max gap. On CelebA, CODA+GDRO attains the highest worst-group accuracy of 89.26% and the smallest max gap of 4.01%. Across both datasets, CODA consistently improves worst-group accuracy over its base method (ERM, RWG, or GDRO) and significantly narrows the group accuracy gap, with the most dramatic improvement seen when combined with ERM (e.g., worst-group accuracy jumps from 2.08% to 71.74% on ColoredMNIST).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CODA is evaluated against four baselines: ERM, RWG (group reweighting), GDRO (group distributionally robust optimization), LfF (Learning from Failure), and JTT (Just Train Twice). Against ERM, CODA delivers a dramatic improvement in worst-group accuracy, lifting it from 2.08% to 71.74% on ColoredMNIST and from 47.78% to 83.65% on CelebA, while reducing the max group gap from 92.70% to 2.45% and from 51.80% to 9.87% respectively. Against RWG, CODA+RWG achieves a modest worst-group accuracy gain (72.11% vs. 71.64% on ColoredMNIST; 86.56% vs. 83.44% on CelebA) and a substantially smaller max gap (2.36% vs. 2.70%; 7.28% vs. 10.78%). Against GDRO, CODA+GDRO improves worst-group accuracy by ~0.9% on ColoredMNIST (71.98% vs. 71.08%) and ~1.0% on CelebA (89.26% vs. 88.22%), while achieving the smallest max gap on both datasets (2.37% and 4.01%). Against LfF and JTT, all CODA variants substantially outperform on worst-group accuracy (e.g., CODA+RWG at 72.11% vs. LfF's 50.91% and JTT's 71.01% on ColoredMNIST), confirming that the disentanglement-and-augmentation strategy provides a more effective defense against spurious correlations than prior debiasing approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

CODA is built directly on the VAE/β-VAE framework for disentangled representation learning, with its training objective (Eq. 8) structurally derived from FFVAE (Creager et al., 2019) but replacing the adversarial density-ratio estimation term with a simpler weighted KL penalty and a decoy classifier. For reconstruction quality on complex images, it adopts Soft-IntroVAE (Daniel & Tamar, 2021). Conceptually, the paper departs from prior subpopulation-shift defenses (GDRO, RWG, LfF, JTT) by reframing spurious attributes not as obstacles to eliminate but as a resource for strategic data augmentation, and it contrasts itself with CAMEL (Goel et al., 2020) by replacing deterministic one-to-one CycleGAN transformations with flexible many-to-many VAE-based feature mixing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a practical, modular recipe—disentangle via a bi-branch VAE, augment by mixing variant features, then train with a reweighted consistency loss—that can be plugged into any existing robust classifier (ERM, RWG, GDRO) and consistently improves worst-group accuracy across multiple shift intensities. However, the approach requires pre-identified spurious attributes (a potentially costly labeling effort) and incurs significant training overhead (e.g., ~45 hours on CelebA), limiting immediate deployment in settings where spurious structure is unknown. Open questions include whether the disentanglement quality degrades with more complex or entangled spurious attributes, and whether the method extends to tabular or non-visual modalities where 'reconstruction' is less natural.
