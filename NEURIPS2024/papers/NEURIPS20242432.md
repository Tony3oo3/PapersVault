## Problem

Text-to-image models remain vulnerable to adversarial prompts that appear innocuous to humans yet steer diffusion models toward generating NSFW content, and existing classifier-based moderators (OpenAI-Moderation, Azure, AWS) lack generalizability to unseen NSFW concepts, provide no interpretability, and are easily bypassed by adaptive attacks. Training-interference defenses like concept erasing further degrade image quality on benign prompts and remain fragile against novel attack strategies.

## Input / Output

GUARDT2I consumes the text guidance embedding e ∈ R^d produced by the T2I model's text encoder (e.g., Stable Diffusion v1.5) together with the original user prompt string. It outputs a natural-language 'prompt interpretation' generated by the c-LLM, plus a binary accept/reject decision determined by a two-level parse: a keyword-based Verbalizer (25 predefined NSFW terms) and a Sentence Similarity Checker (sentence-transformer cosine similarity against the original prompt). The system runs in parallel with the diffusion process and can trigger an early stop before all 50 diffusion steps complete, adding only ~0.06 s of inference time versus ~17.8 s for the full T2I generation.

## Method

The key insight is that adversarial prompts, despite appearing semantically different in text space, encode the same NSFW intent in the T2I model's latent guidance embedding. GUARDT2I exploits this by framing defense as a generative task: a conditional LLM (c-LLM), a 24-layer decoder-only transformer (1024 hidden dims) initialized from a pre-trained checkpoint and augmented with cross-attention layers in each block, is fine-tuned to 'translate' the guidance embedding back into plain text. Training uses teacher forcing with cross-entropy loss on 10M unfiltered LAION-COCO prompts, so the c-LLM learns to reconstruct benign prompts faithfully while exposing the hidden NSFW semantics of adversarial ones. At inference, a Verbalizer scans the interpretation for explicit NSFW words and a Sentence Similarity Checker flags interpretations that diverge significantly from the original prompt; either trigger halts generation. This generative paradigm avoids the fixed decision boundaries of classifiers, provides human-readable explanations for every rejection, and creates conflicting optimization pressures that make adaptive attacks substantially less effective (≤16% attack success rate vs. >85% for SafetyChecker).

## Results

GuardT2I achieves an average AUROC of 96.77% (±3.15) and AUPRC of 96.16% (±4.35) across six adversarial prompt datasets, substantially outperforming all baselines. Its average FPR@TPR95 is 19.26% and average ASR is 11.68%, both significantly lower than the best competing methods. The method also preserves normal-use image quality, matching the CLIP-Score of unmodified Stable Diffusion (0.1502 vs. 0.1501) while reducing ASR from 61.86% (ESD) to 11.68%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GuardT2I outperforms all seven baselines across every metric. Against the strongest commercial moderator, OpenAI-Moderation, GuardT2I gains +5.26 AUROC points (96.77 vs. 91.51) and +8.48 AUPRC points (96.16 vs. 87.68), with a notably lower standard deviation (±3.15 vs. ±11.59) indicating more consistent performance across attack types. Microsoft Azure and AWS Comprehend trail further behind at 77.19% and 87.48% average AUROC respectively, with Azure particularly weak on human-crafted prompts (54–55% on I2P variants). Among open-source classifiers, NSFW-text-classifier and Detoxify achieve only 73.04% and 73.22% average AUROC, and their FPR@TPR95 values (79.33% and 54.41%) are 3–4× higher than GuardT2I's 19.26%. Against concept-erasing defenses (ESD, SLD-medium, SLD-strong), GuardT2I reduces ASR from 61.86–72.36% to 11.68% while maintaining equivalent image fidelity (FID 52.10 vs. 49.24–56.44) and text alignment (CLIP-Score 0.1502 vs. 0.1455–0.1501).

## Relation to Prior Work

GUARDT2I departs from both dominant paradigms in T2I safety: training-interference methods (ESD, SLD) that modify model weights to erase concepts, and post-hoc classifiers (OpenAI-Moderation, SafetyChecker, Detoxify) that perform binary NSFW detection on prompts or images. Its key conceptual departure is reframing prompt moderation as a conditional generation task: a c-LLM with cross-attention decodes the T2I text encoder's guidance embedding back into natural language, exploiting the observation that adversarial prompts retain their true semantic content in the model's latent space even when they appear innocuous in text. The bi-level parsing (Verbalizer + Sentence Similarity Checker) then operates on this generated interpretation rather than the raw prompt, drawing on sentence-transformer similarity scoring and a simple keyword list.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The generative paradigm offers interpretability (the prompt interpretation reveals the attacker's actual intent) and adaptability (the NSFW word list is updatable, and the LLM generalizes to unseen NSFW themes) that classifier-based moderators lack. The adaptive-attack analysis is a notable contribution: the conflicting optimization directions (bypassing GUARDT2I requires the prompt to resemble its interpretation, while generating NSFW content requires it to differ semantically) cap the adaptive attack success rate at ~16%, far below the 85%+ reported for SafetyChecker. Limitations include evaluation on a single target model (SD v1.5), a single random seed, and the Verbalizer's reliance on a fixed 25-word list that misses political or culturally specific threats. Open questions include whether the approach transfers to newer T2I architectures with different text encoders (e.g., SDXL, DALL-E 3) and whether the c-LLM's training on LAION-COCO sub-samples is sufficient for long-tail NSFW concepts.
