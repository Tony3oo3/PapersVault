## Problem

SAM's predictions are highly sensitive to minor prompt perturbations and object granularity, a property typically treated as a robustness flaw. This paper reframes that sensitivity as an asset for ambiguous segmentation, where multiple valid expert annotations exist for the same object, and asks whether SAM's prompt- and granularity-induced variability can be probabilistically modeled to controllably reproduce the true ambiguous label distribution.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241448/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and a spatial prompt (box or point) as input, but rather than using the prompt deterministically, it samples prompt embeddings from a learned axisymmetric Gaussian whose parameters are conditioned on the image context. The image embedding is similarly augmented with a learned Gaussian perturbation before being fed to SAM's decoder. At inference, the network is applied m times to the same image, each time drawing fresh samples from both latent distributions, yielding a set of m diverse segmentation masks that collectively approximate the ambiguous ground-truth distribution. SAM ViT-B is used with the image encoder output downsampled by 1/8 and configured for 8 multi-scale outputs.

## Method

A-SAM wraps SAM in a conditional VAE that models ambiguity at two strata. A Prompt Generation Network (PGN) takes the prompt embedding and image features to produce the mean and standard deviation of an axisymmetric Gaussian over prompt embeddings, while an Image Generation Network (IGN) produces analogous parameters for a Gaussian perturbation concatenated to the image embedding; each sampled pair is decoded by SAM's frozen decoder into one mask candidate. During training, posterior versions of both networks (which additionally receive the ground-truth label) generate teacher distributions, and KL-divergence losses align the inference-time (label-free) distributions to the teacher distributions, implementing a posterior distillation scheme. SAM's 8 multi-scale candidate masks are combined via learnable weights initialized to 1/8, and the total loss is a weighted sum of cross-entropy segmentation loss plus the two KL terms. The key architectural insight is that SAM's own sensitivity to prompt shifts and granularity changes is harnessed as the generative mechanism for diversity, rather than being regularized away.

## Results

On LIDC-IDRI, A-SAM achieves GED 0.228, HM-IOU 0.717, Dmax 0.948, and Dmean 0.356, substantially outperforming the best prompted baseline (SAM w/ Box shift: GED 0.361, HM-IOU 0.380). On BraTS 2017, A-SAM reaches GED 0.193 and HM-IOU 0.610, versus 0.239 and 0.242 for SAM w/ Box shift. On ISBI 2016, A-SAM attains GED 0.276, HM-IOU 0.835, Dmax 0.926, and Dmean 0.904, surpassing SEEM w/ Mask shift (GED 0.522, HM-IOU 0.821). Against conventional ambiguous segmentation methods on LIDC, A-SAM (GED 0.230, HM-IOU 0.763, Dmax 0.959) outperforms the best prior method PixelSeg (GED 0.243, HM-IOU 0.614, Dmax 0.814) by a wide margin on HM-IOU and Dmax.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against prompted segmentation models (SegGPT, SEEM, SAM) adapted via random prompt perturbation, A-SAM wins on all four metrics across all four datasets. On LIDC, A-SAM's HM-IOU of 0.717 nearly doubles the best prompted baseline (SAM w/ Box shift at 0.380), and its GED of 0.228 is 37% lower than SAM's 0.361. On Sim10K, A-SAM's HM-IOU of 0.637 is 2.4× that of SEEM w/ Mask shift (0.271), the strongest prompted baseline there. Against conventional ambiguous segmentation methods on LIDC (Table 2), A-SAM (GED 0.230, HM-IOU 0.763, Dmax 0.959) outperforms PixelSeg (0.243, 0.614, 0.814), Mose (0.234, 0.623, 0.702), and all cVAE-based methods (Prob UNet, HProb UNet, PhiSeg, SSN) by a clear margin. On BraTS (Table 3), A-SAM achieves GED 0.192 and Dmean 0.438, beating Prob UNet (0.225, 0.364) and PixelSeg (0.419, 0.361). On ISBI (Table 4), A-SAM (GED 0.267, Dmean 0.905) edges out cFlow (0.306, 0.892) and Prob UNet (0.329, 0.894), while matching cFlow on Dmax (0.918). The ablation (Table 6) confirms that removing either prompt or object ambiguity modeling degrades performance, with posterior distillation removal causing the largest HM-IOU drop (0.717→0.385).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two previously separate research threads: prompt-based segmentation foundation models (SAM, SegGPT, SEEM) and probabilistic/ambiguous segmentation (Probabilistic U-Net, PHiseg, PixelSeg). While prior work on SAM has focused on stabilizing its prompt sensitivity (adapters, fine-tuning), and prior ambiguous segmentation work has relied on U-Net backbones with cVAE latent spaces, this paper is the first to repurpose SAM's inherent prompt and granularity sensitivity as a probabilistic signal rather than a defect to be suppressed. The technical framework draws directly on the cVAE formulation (Gaussian latent distributions, KL regularization, reparameterization) from the Probabilistic U-Net lineage, but transplants it into a vision-transformer prompt-encoder architecture with a novel posterior distillation mechanism for both prompt and image embeddings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that foundation models' well-known instability can be harnessed as a structured source of diversity for tasks where multiple valid outputs are expected, offering a practical path toward probabilistic medical image segmentation without training from scratch. A key limitation acknowledged by the authors is that the uncertainty remains uncontrolled and unquantifiable—there is no mechanism to calibrate the spread of outputs or to express confidence in individual samples. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to 3D volumetric segmentation natively (current BraTS results use 2D slices), whether the axisymmetric Gaussian assumption is sufficient for highly multimodal ambiguity, and how the method scales when the number of expert annotations grows beyond the 3–4 used here.
