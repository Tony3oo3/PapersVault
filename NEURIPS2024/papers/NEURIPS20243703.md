## Problem

In partially observable reinforcement learning, oracle critics that exploit full-state information during training can improve sample efficiency but introduce high variance in practice, particularly in high-uncertainty environments with large state spaces. Prior asymmetric actor-critic methods either use state-conditioned critics (unbiased but high-variance) or history-only critics (low-variance but slower to learn), with no principled mechanism to balance both. The paper asks how to adaptively harness full-state information for efficiency while provably controlling the variance it introduces.

## Input / Output

The actor network receives a sequence of first-person view observations (7×7×3 grid-encoded images in MiniGrid, 80×60×3 grayscale RGB in MiniWorld) processed through a CNN backbone and LSTM, and outputs a discrete action distribution over navigation and interaction primitives. The oracle critic additionally receives the full aerial-view state image concatenated with the observation history, while the standard critic receives only the observation history. This asymmetric input structure means the two critics operate on different information sets, with the state available exclusively during training and never at deployment.

## Method

DCRL introduces a dual-critic architecture where an oracle critic V(h,s) conditioned on the <history, state> tuple and a standard critic V(h) conditioned on history alone are combined via a dual value function V_dual(h,s) = (1−β)V(h,s) + βV(h). The paper proves this dual value is an unbiased estimate of V(h) and has variance bounded by (1−β)² times the oracle critic's variance, strictly reducing gradient variance while preserving the policy gradient in expectation. A key mechanism is a dynamic weighting rule: β is clipped to zero whenever the standard critic's n-step advantage δ_φ(h_t, a_t) ≤ 0, so the oracle critic drives updates only when the agent is underperforming relative to its own value estimate, while the standard critic takes over for positive-advantage samples. This clipping is shown to be equivalent to a form of lower-bound-soft-Q-learning, preventing large destabilizing updates and breaking the vicious cycle of poor policies and bad value estimates. The framework is implemented on top of both A2C and PPO with identical network architectures across all baselines.

## Results

On MiniGrid (27 games, A2C), DCRL achieves an average score of 0.89±0.01 after 1e6 training frames, substantially outperforming the strongest baseline Unbiased Asymmetric Actor-Critic (0.39±0.09) and Recurrent Actor-Critic (0.28±0.09). Under PPO, DCRL reaches 0.84±0.03 at 1e6 frames versus 0.59±0.08 for UAAC. DCRL wins 26 out of 27 MiniGrid games and also improves PPO performance across all four MiniWorld (Box3D) environments, where UAAC actually underperforms plain Recurrent Actor-Critic.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DCRL outperforms all five baselines on MiniGrid, winning 26 of 27 games under both A2C and PPO. The most direct comparison is against Unbiased Asymmetric Actor-Critic (UAAC) [10], the state-of-the-art method that also uses the <history, state> tuple for the critic: DCRL achieves 0.89 vs. 0.39 (A2C, 1e6) and 0.84 vs. 0.59 (PPO, 1e6), a gap of roughly 0.5 and 0.25 respectively. Recurrent Actor-Critic (no state info) and Oracle Guiding (teacher-student with state dropout) are far behind, scoring 0.28 and 0.27 (A2C, 1e6). Asymmetric Actor-Critic [8], which feeds only the state (not history) to the critic, is also well below DCRL (0.32 vs. 0.89). On MiniWorld (Box3D, PPO), the advantage is even more pronounced: UAAC underperforms plain Recurrent Actor-Critic on all four tasks due to high variance from noisy pixel-based states, while DCRL consistently converges to superior policies by blending the oracle and standard critics.

## Relation to Prior Work

DCRL extends the Unbiased Asymmetric Actor-Critic (UAAC) framework, which proved that conditioning the critic on the <history, state> tuple yields unbiased policy gradients under partial observability. However, UAAC's oracle critic introduces high variance in practice, particularly in high-uncertainty belief states and large state spaces. DCRL departs from this single-critic paradigm by introducing a dual-critic architecture that convexly combines the oracle critic (for efficiency) with a standard history-only critic (for variance reduction), coupled with a clipping mechanism inspired by lower-bound-soft-Q-learning. The base algorithms are recurrent A2C and PPO, with the actor remaining a standard LSTM-based policy conditioned only on observation history.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap: theoretically unbiased oracle critics can destabilize training when state information is noisy or the belief state is highly uncertain, a situation common in real-world deployment (e.g., autonomous driving with RGB-only inference but full-sensor training). The clean theoretical guarantee—unbiasedness preserved while variance is provably reduced—gives practitioners a principled way to exploit privileged training information. Key limitations include the assumption that full state is available during training (which may not hold in all settings) and the observation that state information can sometimes hinder performance when it is redundant with observations. Open questions include how DCRL behaves when only partial state is accessible and whether the dual-critic idea transfers to model-based or multi-agent settings.
