## Problem

Existing ultra image segmentation methods suffer from two fundamental flaws: a scalability issue (reliance on dataset-specific parameters that degrade with increasing resolution) and an architectural issue (inability to handle arbitrarily large images without either exceeding GPU memory or losing detail through downsampling). Directly applying general segmentation models via sliding window inference introduces an information bottleneck (isolated patches cannot capture cross-patch correlations) and a fragmentation phenomenon (inconsistent predictions at patch boundaries).
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20244104/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes an ultra-high resolution RGB image (tested at resolutions from 2048×1024 to 7200×6800) and produces a per-pixel semantic segmentation mask. During inference, the image is tiled into non-overlapping 512×512 local patches; for each local patch, a 1024×1024 surrounding patch (2× side length) is extracted centered at the same position. The local patch feeds the general segmentation module while the surrounding patch feeds the lightweight SCB branch, and their features are fused before a standard segmentation head produces the final mask.

## Method

SGNet is a plug-and-play framework that augments any general segmentation model with a Surrounding Context-Guided Branch (SCB). The SCB uses a lightweight backbone (first four stages of STDC) to extract features from the larger surrounding patch, then applies a Surrounding Context Integration Module (SCI) that performs window-based multi-head self-attention within spatial regions, global average pooling to obtain per-window representations, cross-window self-attention for inter-region information exchange, and an FFN to refine the integrated features. The SCB features are coordinate-aligned to the local patch region and concatenated (late fusion) with the general model's features before a standard segmentation head. A boundary consistency loss (L1 distance between the local patch prediction and the corresponding cropped region of the surrounding patch prediction) explicitly enforces coherence across adjacent patches, alleviating fragmentation. The overall training objective is a weighted sum of cross-entropy losses for both branches plus the consistency loss, and the entire framework requires no modification to the backbone of the underlying general segmentation model.

## Results

SGNet achieves consistent mIoU improvements over all five general segmentation backbones across five ultra image datasets. The best configuration (DeepLabV3Plus+SGNet) reaches 75.44% mIoU on DeepGlobe, 63.18% on FBP, 81.21% on Inria Aerial, 61.21% on Gleason, and 76.72% on Cityscapes. Improvements range from +1.33% (FBP) to +6.19% (Gleason), with the largest gains on the medical Gleason dataset where surrounding context is most informative. The method also demonstrates robustness under JPEG compression, improving by +3.92% over the baseline (vs. +2.22% on clean images).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against dedicated ultra image segmentation (UIS) methods, SGNet (DeepLabV3Plus) outperforms ISDNet by +1.24% on Gleason (61.21 vs 59.97), +6.98% on Inria Aerial (81.21 vs 74.23), and +1.56% on FBP (63.18 vs 61.62 vs ElegantSeg). On DeepGlobe, GPWFormer (75.80) slightly edges out SGNet (75.44), and on Cityscapes GPWFormer (78.10) leads SGNet (76.72) by 1.38%, though GPWFormer does not report FBP or Gleason results. The key advantage of SGNet is consistency: it improves every backbone it is attached to, whereas UIS methods like ISDNet drop to 21.98% on FBP (6800×7200) due to whole-image inference constraints. Among general segmentation baselines, DeepLabV3Plus+SGNet achieves the best overall results, with the largest absolute gains on Gleason (+5.76%) and FBP (+1.33%), confirming that surrounding context is most beneficial for medical and very large remote sensing images. The efficiency comparison (Table 6) shows SGNet (ISDNet-Style) reaches 25.59 FPS with 74.28% mIoU, surpassing ISDNet's 22.67 FPS / 73.30% mIoU while using comparable memory (2043 MB vs 1948 MB).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant global-local and shallow-deep architectures in ultra image segmentation (GLNet, ISDNet, WSDNet, GPWFormer) by reframing the problem as a plug-in enhancement to any general segmentation model using sliding window inference. The Surrounding Context Integration Module draws on window-based multi-head self-attention (Swin Transformer lineage) and cross-window global interaction via GAP, while the boundary consistency loss is a straightforward L1 constraint between overlapping predictions. Rather than proposing a new end-to-end UIS architecture, SGNet repurposes the standard sliding inference paradigm and augments it with a lightweight surrounding-context branch, positioning itself as a model-agnostic add-on rather than a standalone method.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play design means practitioners can immediately benefit from SGNet by attaching it to their existing segmentation backbone without re-architecting the pipeline, which is practically valuable for real-world ultra image deployment where image sizes are unbounded. However, the paper does not report error bars or multiple random seeds, and the improvements, while consistent, are modest (1.7–6.2 mIoU). Open questions include whether the surrounding context approach generalizes to instance or panoptic segmentation, whether the fixed 2× surrounding scale is optimal across all domains, and how the method behaves under extreme resolution scales (e.g., 10,000+ pixel images) where even the surrounding patch becomes memory-constrained.
