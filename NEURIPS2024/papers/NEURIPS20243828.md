## Problem

Existing dataset ownership verification (DOV) methods all directly use watermarked samples as verification queries, thereby disclosing the watermark pattern to the suspicious model during the verification process. This relies on an assumption that verification is a one-time, privacy-preserving event, which fails in practice because adversaries can observe the watermark and subsequently remove it from their stolen models. The paper asks whether ownership can be verified without ever revealing the dataset-specified watermark to the queried model.

## Input / Output

ZeroMark takes benign (unwatermarked) validation samples and a black-box model API that returns only predicted labels (no logits, gradients, or parameters) as its operational inputs; the watermark pattern is used solely on the defender's side for cosine-similarity scoring and is never transmitted to the suspicious model. The method operates on RGB images at 32×32 (CIFAR-10) or 64×64 (Tiny-ImageNet) with CNN backbones (ResNet-18/34, VGG-19). It outputs a binary ownership-verification decision backed by a pairwise t-test p-value and a confidence score ΔP (the difference between the largest Q% cosine-similarity values for target-label and benign-label boundary gradients).

## Method

The key insight is that the gradient of the logit margin at the closest decision-boundary point (the 'boundary gradient') of a watermarked DNN aligns in direction with the watermark pattern, as formalized in Theorem 1 (cosine similarity → 1 at rate O(t^{q−1}), q∈(1/2,1)). ZeroMark proceeds in three steps: (1) it generates closest boundary samples from benign validation inputs by adapting the Fast Adaptive Boundary Attack (FAB) with Monte Carlo gradient estimation to handle the label-only black-box constraint; (2) it estimates boundary gradients at those samples via Monte Carlo averaging over random Gaussian perturbations; (3) it computes a normalized cosine similarity between each boundary gradient and the defender's watermark pattern (normalized against random-pattern baselines to suppress variance) and performs a pairwise t-test between the target-label and benign-label similarity distributions to reject or retain the null hypothesis of no unauthorized training. Because the samples actually sent to the suspicious model are boundary points rather than watermarked inputs, the watermark pattern is never disclosed, and t-SNE visualizations confirm that boundary samples remain far from the watermark-sample distribution in feature space.

## Results

On the verification task (Table 4), ZeroMark achieves p-values as low as 10⁻⁶⁷ (Blended, CIFAR-10) and 10⁻⁵⁸ (BadNets, Tiny-ImageNet) in the Malicious (unauthorized training) scenario, while maintaining p-values ≥ 0.74 in both Independent-W and Independent-M scenarios, confirming no false positives. In terms of watermark disclosure (Tables 2–3), ZeroMark achieves the highest MSE (e.g., 0.314 vs. 0.187 for Minimal on BadNets/CIFAR-10) and lowest MI (e.g., 27.7 vs. 57.3 for Minimal), indicating it leaks the least watermark information while still enabling successful verification. The averaged largest Q% cosine similarity for the target label ranges from 0.099–0.368 across watermarks on CIFAR-10, significantly exceeding the benign baseline of ~0.027.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ZeroMark is compared against three watermark-disclosure baselines: Vanilla (using original watermarked samples), Minimal (minimally-sized trigger via Neural Cleanse-style optimization), and Distortion (adding maximum random noise while preserving watermark efficacy). On CIFAR-10 (Table 2), ZeroMark achieves the best (highest) MSE for BadNets (0.392 vs. 0.193 Minimal, 0.286 Distortion) and WaNet (0.199 vs. 0.121 Minimal, 0.087 Distortion), and the best (lowest) MI for BadNets (18.1 vs. 54.8 Minimal, 51.5 Distortion) and DW (29.7 vs. 52.3 Minimal, 44.5 Distortion). On Tiny-ImageNet (Table 3), ZeroMark similarly dominates: it achieves the highest MSE for BadNets (0.314 vs. 0.187 Minimal) and the lowest MI across all four watermarks (e.g., 27.7 vs. 57.3 for Minimal on BadNets). The Vanilla baseline trivially discloses the full watermark (MSE = 0, MI = 68.1 for BadNets), while Minimal and Distortion reduce disclosure but still leak substantially more information than ZeroMark. In verification accuracy (Table 4), all methods implicitly succeed in the Malicious case, but ZeroMark is the only approach that does so without exposing the watermark pattern, making it the only method robust to repeated verification or adaptive watermark removal.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the entire existing DOV literature (BadNets-based, Blended, WaNet, Domain Watermark) by challenging their shared assumption that verification is a one-time, privacy-preserving process where watermarked samples can be safely queried. It repurposes the fast adaptive boundary attack (FAB, Croce & Hein, ICML 2020) and decision boundary dynamics (Xu et al., ICLR 2023) not for adversarial robustness but for watermark-free ownership verification, and borrows the extreme-value cosine-similarity analysis from AEVA (Guo et al., ICLR 2022) to turn boundary gradients into a statistical test. The key conceptual leap is showing that the gradient direction at the decision boundary of a watermarked model encodes the watermark pattern, even though the watermark itself is never presented to the model during verification.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ZeroMark closes a practical security gap in dataset copyright protection: once a watermark is leaked during verification, an adversary can strip it from their stolen model or craft verification samples that defeat future checks. The theoretical guarantee (Theorem 1) linking boundary-gradient direction to the watermark pattern is a useful formal anchor, though the empirical signal is noisy (~74% overlap between target and benign cosine-similarity distributions) and the method requires ~30 minutes of black-box queries. Open questions include whether the watermark can be provably unrecoverable from boundary samples (currently only empirical), whether the approach extends to untargeted watermarks without a predefined target label, and whether it generalizes to discrete modalities (text, tabular) where boundary-sample generation is less well-studied.
