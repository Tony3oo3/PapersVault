## Problem

The Spherically Constrained Least Squares (SCLS) method is the state-of-the-art solver for the Stackelberg Prediction Game with least squares loss (SPG-LS), yet it lacks any theoretical error analysis. Without guarantees on how closely the SCLS-estimated learner approximates the true Stackelberg equilibrium, its reliability for large-scale adversarial learning applications remains unvalidated.

## Input / Output

The method operates on a sample set S = {(x_i, y_i, z_i)}_{i=1}^n where x_i ∈ R^d are Gaussian-drawn inputs, y_i are true output labels generated through the attacker's optimal modification of x_i, and z_i are the attacker's target labels, with a regularization parameter γ > 0 controlling the trade-off between data deviation and target closeness. The output is the Stackelberg equilibrium weight vector w* recovered from the SCLS solution, and the paper's analytical target is the estimation error ||w* − w_0|| under the asymptotic regime lim_{n→∞} d/n ∈ (0,1).

## Method

The paper reframes the SCLS estimation error as a Primary Optimization (PO) problem via the Fenchel-Moreau conjugate of the squared norm, then applies the Convex Gaussian Min-Max Theorem (CGMT) to replace the PO with a simpler Auxiliary Optimization (AO) problem involving only vectors of dimension d or n rather than the n×d data matrix. A first-order Taylor approximation of the spherical constraint α̃(w) = √(1−||w||²) around the true parameter w̃_0 linearizes the objective, yielding a tractable approximated SCLS problem whose tightness is proven as ||β||→0. The AO problem is further reduced via concentration of measure (||g||², (h^T β)², etc. concentrating around their means) to a deterministic scalar optimization min_β ||β||² + (c^T β)² + Ω(β) + 4σ²/γ. The Hessian of this objective is shown to be positive definite (I_d + cc^T + (1/n)I_d), establishing strong convexity and proving the unique global minimum is β* = 0, which by CGMT implies ||w* − w_0|| →^P 0.

## Results

The main theoretical result (Theorem 4.3) establishes that the estimation error of the SCLS method converges to zero in probability: lim_{n→∞} ‖w* − w₀‖ →ᵖ 0, under the condition that lim_{n→∞} d/n ∈ (0,1). Experimental validation on synthetic Gaussian data (d/n = 0.5 and d/n = 1/3) confirms this convergence: the estimation error ‖w* − w₀‖ decreases monotonically toward 0 as n grows, across all tested sparsity levels (k/d = 1, 0.1, 0.01, 0.001) and regularization parameters (γ = 0.1, 0.01). The noise level is fixed at σ = 0.1, and results are averaged over 10 trials.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper is a theoretical analysis contribution and does not include head-to-head empirical comparisons against other SPG-LS solvers (e.g., the QFP/bisection method of [1] or the SOCP method of [2]). The experiments are designed solely to validate the paper's own convergence theorems (Theorems 4.1, 4.3, and D.1–D.2) on synthetic data. The SCLS method [3] is the subject of analysis rather than a competitor; the paper's contribution is proving that the SCLS solution w* reliably recovers the true parameter w₀ as n → ∞, filling a theoretical gap left by the original SCLS paper. No quantitative performance comparison (e.g., runtime or solution quality) against prior SPG-LS algorithms is reported.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper provides the first theoretical error analysis for the SCLS method (Wang et al., ICML 2022), which itself builds on a progression of solvers for the Stackelberg Prediction Game with Least Squares loss: from the QFP/bisection approach of Bishop et al. (NeurIPS 2020) to the SOCP reduction of Wang et al. (ICML 2021) to the SCLS reformulation. The analytical machinery is drawn from the Convex Gaussian Min-max Theorem (CGMT) framework of Thrampoulidis et al. (2018), previously applied to regularized regression and adversarial training but not to bilevel optimization problems. The paper's conceptual departure is applying this high-dimensional asymptotic analysis toolkit to a nested (bilevel) optimization structure arising from adversarial data manipulation, rather than to a single-level estimation problem.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The SCLS method won the ICML 2022 Outstanding Paper Award and is the current state-of-the-art for SPG-LS, yet it lacked any theoretical guarantee on the quality of its solution; this paper closes that gap by proving consistency (estimation error → 0 in probability) under Gaussian data assumptions. The main limitation is the reliance on Gaussian inputs and noise, which the authors acknowledge restricts practical applicability to real-world data distributions. An open question is whether the CGMT-based analysis can be extended to non-Gaussian settings or to more general loss functions beyond least squares, which would broaden the theoretical foundation for the entire SPG literature.
