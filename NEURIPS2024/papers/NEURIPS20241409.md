## Problem

Existing training-free methods for generating 3D objects and complex scenes from pretrained 2D diffusion models (SDS/DreamFusion and Score Jacobian Chaining) perform gradient ascent to find the single most likely differentiable representation, i.e., mode-seeking rather than true sampling. This produces overly smoothed, low-diversity outputs that fail to reflect the full distribution of the diffusion model, especially at low guidance levels. The paper identifies that the root cause is a differential-geometry error: prior methods pull back the score function (a covector field) but omit the metric transformation needed to obtain the correct vector field in the parameter space.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a text prompt and a frozen pretrained image diffusion model (Stable Diffusion 1.5) to produce the parameters θ of a differentiable representation. For images and panoramas, the diffrep is a 3-layer SIREN MLP (width 256, sinusoidal activations) mapping 2D pixel coordinates to the 4-channel SDv1.5 latent space, rendered on a 64×64 grid and decoded via VQ-VAE to 512×512 RGB; panoramas additionally enforce horizontal wrap-around over a 360° azimuth with a 45° field-of-view camera. For 3D, the diffrep is a voxNeRF whose parameters are updated by rendering 8 views per iteration and matching them to the diffusion model's denoised predictions, yielding a full 3D scene renderable from arbitrary viewpoints.

## Method

The core contribution is a mathematically correct pullback of the probability-flow ODE from image space to the diffrep parameter space, yielding dθ/dt = −σ̇(t)σ(t)(JᵀJ)⁻¹Jᵀ∇log p_t(f(θ)), where the (JᵀJ)⁻¹ term—absent in SDS and SJC—is essential for unbiased sampling because it converts the pulled-back covector field into the correct tangent vector field via the pulled-back Riemannian metric. To make this tractable, the method separates the noisy sample into a noiseless signal (represented by the diffrep) and a fixed noise term, improving Jacobian conditioning, and replaces the explicit (JᵀJ)⁻¹ solve with a per-step nonlinear least-squares sub-optimization over Δθ using Adam. For multi-view and panorama settings where the render function is non-invertible, the paper adapts RePaint by interleaving DDIM forward and reverse steps to harmonize the diffrep's implicit consistency constraints (e.g., cross-view coherence, wrap-around continuity) into the sampling trajectory, steering the process toward high-density regions of the diffusion model that are actually renderable by the representation.

## Results

On MS-COCO 2017 prompts, the method's SIREN-rendered images achieve PSNR of 29.71±4.23, SSIM of 0.899±0.086, and LPIPS of 0.044±0.050 against SDv1.5 reference images at CFG scale 0, indicating near-identical quality to the reference diffusion model. The KID metric (Figure 3) confirms that sampled SIRENs are statistically indistinguishable from direct SDv1.5 samples, while SJC's KID is noticeably worse. Sampling a batch of 8 SIRENs takes 82 seconds, compared to 694 seconds for SJC (3000 iterations), a ~8.5× speedup. For 3D NeRFs, each generation requires ~24 minutes (199 NFEs) versus ~34 minutes for SJC (10,000 steps).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Score Jacobian Chaining (SJC, Wang et al. 2022), the proposed method wins decisively on sample diversity: Figure 4 shows that eight SJC samples from the same prompt are nearly identical (mode-seeking), while the proposed method and SD reference produce visibly distinct outputs. The KID metric in Figure 3 (right) quantitatively confirms this, with the proposed method's SIRENs tracking the SD reference curve closely while SJC's KID is substantially higher across all CFG scales. The method also runs ~8.5× faster than SJC for image SIRENs (82 s vs. 694 s for 8 samples) and ~1.4× faster for NeRFs (~24 min vs. ~34 min). Against the SDv1.5 reference (Rombach et al. 2022), the proposed method matches quality closely at low CFG (PSNR ≈ 29.7, SSIM ≈ 0.90 at CFG 0) but degrades at very high CFG (PSNR drops to 13.6 at CFG 100), reflecting the inherent difficulty of fitting a SIREN to highly structured, high-frequency content. The paper does not report a direct head-to-head numerical table against SJC; the comparison is primarily qualitative (side-by-side renders) and via the KID distribution plot.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the SDS (DreamFusion) and SJC lineage of training-free 3D generation, which both perform gradient ascent on the pulled-back score covector field J^T ∇log p_t, effectively seeking a mode in the diffrep parameter space. The key conceptual correction is recognizing that the PF-ODE defines a vector field, not a covector field, and that pulling back the vector field requires the (J^T J)^{-1} metric term that SDS/SJC implicitly discard (where it acts only as a preconditioner for optimization but is essential for correct ODE trajectories). The method also adapts RePaint's forward/reverse step interleaving to enforce consistency constraints induced by the diffrep, extending a technique originally designed for inpainting to the multi-view rendering setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By replacing mode-seeking with true sampling, this method captures the full diversity of the diffusion model's distribution in the diffrep space, which is particularly important at low guidance levels where mode-seeking produces overly smoothed, low-detail outputs. The approach is general-purpose and training-free, potentially extending to any differentiable representation (Gaussian splats, heightmaps, physics renderers) without architecture-specific modifications. Open questions include the computational overhead of RePaint steps (a priori unknown how many are needed), the variance of the Monte Carlo pullback score estimate over views, and whether the method scales to more complex or amortized settings like full game environments.
