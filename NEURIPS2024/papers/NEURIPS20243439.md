## Problem

Diffusion-based samplers (e.g., Langevin, proximal schemes) provide strong convergence guarantees in KL or Rényi divergence for unconstrained distributions, but when adapted to convex-body sampling they incur polynomially worse rates than geometric random walks like Ball walk. The paper asks whether a diffusion-based approach can match the state-of-the-art complexity of Ball walk/Speedy walk while delivering the stronger Rényi divergence guarantees that the conductance-based framework cannot provide.

## Input / Output

The algorithm takes a convex body K ⊂ ℝ^d specified by a well-defined membership oracle (a point x₀, diameter D with B₁(x₀) ⊂ K ⊂ B_D(x₀), and a YES/NO query oracle), an M-warm initial distribution, a Gaussian step variance h, and a rejection threshold N. It outputs a single point x_{T+1} that is an unbiased uniform sample from K conditioned on the algorithm not declaring failure. The second step of each iteration uses rejection sampling from a truncated Gaussian N(y, hI_d)|_K, so the only oracle interaction is membership queries; no projection oracle is needed.

## Method

In-and-Out is a two-step Gibbs sampler on an augmented joint distribution π(x,y) ∝ exp(−‖x−y‖²/2h)·1_K(x): first take an unconstrained Gaussian step (forward heat flow), then sample from the truncated Gaussian conditioned on K (backward heat flow). The key insight is that this discrete algorithm exactly interpolates the forward and backward heat flows of the Fokker-Planck equation, allowing the contraction analysis to be carried out in continuous time using the proximal-sampling framework of Chen et al. (2022). A technical extension (Lemma 12) handles the non-smooth uniform target by showing weak solutions exist and f-divergences are lower-semicontinuous, so the smooth-distribution contraction bounds carry over. The per-iteration failure probability is controlled via a local-conductance argument that exploits convexity of K to bound the probability of landing far outside the body, yielding an expected O(log N) rejection count rather than N. The net result is a Rényi-q contraction rate governed by the Poincaré or log-Sobolev constant of the uniform distribution, with total complexity Õ(qd²Λ log²(M/ηε)) proper steps—matching Ball walk's asymptotics while providing strictly stronger output guarantees.

## Results

The main result (Theorem 1) shows that In-and-Out, starting from an M-warm distribution, achieves R_q(μ‖π^K) ≤ ε in Õ(qd²Λ log²(M/(ηε))) proper steps and Õ(qMd²Λ log⁶(1/(ηε))) expected membership queries, where Λ is the largest eigenvalue of the covariance of the uniform distribution over K. For near-isotropic convex bodies (Corollary 1), the proper-step complexity improves to Õ(qd² log²(M/(ηε))). The convergence rate per iteration (Theorem 2) is O(qh⁻¹ C_PI log(M/ε)) for q ≥ 2 under Poincaré inequality, or O(qh⁻¹ C_LSI log(log M/ε)) for q ≥ 1 under log-Sobolev inequality, with a doubly-logarithmic dependence on M in the latter case.

## Baselines & Comparisons

In-and-Out matches the asymptotic query complexity of Ball walk (Õ(Md²D² log(1/ε)) in the general case) while providing a strictly stronger guarantee: Rényi divergence R_q (which implies TV, W₂, KL, and χ²) rather than TV distance alone. Compared to Hit-and-Run, In-and-Out has a polynomial dependence on M (vs. poly-logarithmic for Hit-and-Run) but does not require the line-intersection oracle. Against prior diffusion-based constrained samplers (Projected Langevin at Õ(d²D³/ε⁴), Moreau-Yosida at Õ(d⁵/ε⁶), Penalized Langevin at Õ(d/ε¹⁰)), In-and-Out achieves dramatically better dependence on ε (polylog vs. polynomial) and avoids the need for a projection oracle entirely. The paper also shows that In-and-Out's mixing guarantee holds for general non-convex bodies (Theorem 2), a property not known for Ball walk or Speedy walk. The key conceptual advance is that the convergence rate is governed directly by isoperimetric constants (Poincaré or log-Sobolev) of the target, rather than requiring the geometric conductance analysis used by all prior convex-body samplers.

## Relation to Prior Work

The In-and-Out sampler is identified as exactly the proximal sampling scheme (Lee et al. 2021; Chen et al. 2022) applied to uniform distributions, and its two-step structure mirrors the restricted Gaussian oracle. However, the analysis departs fundamentally from prior work: instead of the conductance/isoperimetric framework used for Ball walk and Speedy walk, the paper interprets each iteration as a composition of forward and backward heat flows, reducing the mixing analysis to functional inequalities (Poincaré, log-Sobolev) of the target. This places the algorithm in the diffusion-based sampling lineage (Unadjusted Langevin, proximal Langevin) while recovering the state-of-the-art O~(d²) complexity of geometric random walks for the first time in a constrained setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper resolves a long-standing open question—whether diffusion-based approaches can be used for constrained sampling at competitive rates—by showing that a simple Gibbs scheme analyzed through heat flows achieves Rényi divergence guarantees (implying TV, W₂, KL, χ²) at the same asymptotic complexity as Ball walk, without requiring a projection oracle. The proof technique is modular and extends to non-convex bodies for the mixing bound, and the Rényi guarantee is directly relevant to differential privacy. Open questions include extending the framework to general log-concave distributions and to non-log-concave distributions satisfying only a Poincaré inequality, where no polytime sampler is currently known.
