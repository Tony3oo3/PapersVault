## Problem

Classical auction design maximizes revenue but ignores fairness, while existing fair auction mechanisms are limited to single-round (static) settings or a single protected group. This paper tackles the open problem of designing a revenue-maximizing, incentive-compatible auction over T rounds that guarantees a minimum discounted allocation to each of two buyer groups, where buyers can strategically underreport values to preserve future winning chances.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20244000/cropped_page5_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

At each round the mechanism consumes bids from 2n buyers (n per group), the public history of past allocations, and operates under known value distributions F₁, F₂, fairness targets α₁, α₂ (with α₁+α₂≤1), and discount factor δ. The output is a full mechanism (x, p) specifying which buyer wins each round and what each buyer pays, where the payment rule has an unusual three-part structure: a second-price-like term, a participation reward paid by the seller to the winning group's buyers, and an entry fee charged to all buyers that depends on the probability their group would win without them.

## Method

The paper characterizes the optimal mechanism via backward induction over recursive functions parameterized by residual minimum allocations (R₁ᵗ, R₂ᵗ). In the static case, the optimal fair allocation deviates from the Myerson auction along two dimensions: a boundary shift γ that subsidizes the under-allocated group, and a reserve-price reduction η that increases total allocation probability. In the dynamic case, three regimes arise (infeasible, forced allocation, both-feasible); in the both-feasible regime the group-level allocation boundary depends on the 'net utility of not winning' Δᵢ (the difference in a buyer's future expected utility conditional on their group winning vs. not winning this round) and the seller's future utility difference Δ₀. The payment rule combines a standard second-price term with a participation reward of δΔᵢ to the winning group (compensating for lost future winning probability) and an entry fee of δΔᵢζᵢ to all buyers (where ζᵢ is the group's winning probability with one fewer buyer). For computational tractability when δ<1, the paper provides an early-stopping approximation requiring O(ε^{-1/log(1/δ)}) oracle calls and a bucketed-discounting scheme requiring Poly(1/(1−δ)) calls.

## Results

The paper's main quantitative contributions are theoretical approximation guarantees: for any ε > 0, the proposed scheme achieves a fairness level of (1−ε)(αᵢ − ε) with O(ε^{−1/log(1/δ)}) oracle calls (Proposition 3), and a constant-factor approximation with Poly(1/(1−δ)) oracle calls when δ is close to 1 (Proposition 4). In the numerical experiment (δ=0.99, n=1, T=2,4; v₁~Uniform(0.5,1.5), v₂~Uniform(0,1)), the unconstrained allocation probabilities are 0.69 and 0.31 for groups one and two respectively. Seller utility is decreasing in α₁ and α₂ only after the constraints bind; group one's utility decreases in α₂ (for fixed α₁) while group two's utility remains relatively constant in α₁ (for fixed α₂), reflecting the asymmetric reallocation from the no-allocation region.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical paper that does not compare against competing algorithmic baselines. The implicit comparison is between the optimal fair allocation and the unconstrained Myerson (second-price with reserve) auction. The paper shows that the fair mechanism introduces two types of subsidization: a relative subsidy (parameter γ) that shifts allocation from the over-allocated to the under-allocated group, and an absolute subsidy (parameters η₁, η₂) that increases total allocation probability by lowering the effective reserve price. In the dynamic setting, the optimal mechanism additionally requires a participation reward (equal to the net utility of not winning, δΔᵢᵗ) and an entry fee (δΔᵢᵗζᵢᵗ) to maintain ex post incentive compatibility. The numerical experiments confirm that the revenue cost of fairness is concentrated in the region where constraints are binding, and that the cost is asymmetric across groups depending on their relative value distributions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Myerson's optimal auction design (virtual values, second-price auction with reserve) to a dynamic multi-round setting with proportional fairness constraints across two buyer groups. It builds most directly on Pai and Vohra's static fair auction, which considers a minimum allocation constraint for a single group in one round, by generalizing to two groups and T rounds with a discounted revenue objective. The dynamic mechanism design framework (ex post incentive compatibility, backward induction) follows Pavan, Segal, and Toikka, while the proportional fairness notion connects to the fair division and fair mechanism design literatures. The paper departs from prior fair mechanism design work by optimizing seller revenue rather than social welfare and by treating the dynamic strategic environment explicitly.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a complete characterization of the optimal fair dynamic auction, revealing a non-standard payment structure where the seller must pay a participation reward to the winning group (compensating for reduced future winning probability) while charging an entry fee to all buyers. The two-type subsidization structure (relative fairness subsidy plus absolute allocation subsidy) gives a clean policy-relevant decomposition. Limitations include the restriction to two groups and one indivisible item per round, the reliance on Assumption 2 (must allocate except possibly in the last round), and the exponential computational complexity of the exact solution. Open questions include extending to more than two groups, heterogeneous items, and whether the approximation schemes perform well in practice for realistic parameter regimes.
