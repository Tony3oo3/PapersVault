## Problem

Training LLMs with long sequences is severely constrained by the intermediate memory of MLP blocks (≈7× input size) and LM-Head blocks (≈32× input size in Llama3), which dominate HBM usage beyond what activation recomputation can address. This bottleneck limits maximum trainable sequence length to roughly 5–8K tokens on a single A100-80GB GPU, preventing efficient long-context pre-training and fine-tuning without distributed systems or lossy approximations.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a text token sequence of length S (as a 3D tensor of shape B×S×d) and corresponding training labels, partitioning the sequence dimension into M mini-sequences of length S/M before feeding them to MLP and LM-Head blocks. It produces the exact same cross-entropy loss and weight gradients as a standard transformer forward/backward pass, with no approximation. The partitioning is a drop-in replacement for the MLP and LM-Head modules only, leaving attention layers (including FlashAttention2, GQA, or any sparse variant) untouched.

## Method

MST partitions the input sequence along the sequence dimension into M mini-sequences and processes each independently through the MLP (gate/up/down linear layers + SiLU) and LM-Head (linear + cross-entropy) blocks, then concatenates the per-mini-sequence outputs to recover the full-sequence result exactly. In the backward pass, gradients for weights are accumulated across mini-sequences while input gradients are concatenated, reducing peak intermediate HBM occupancy by a factor of M without changing total FLOPs. A chunk-based variant sets the mini-sequence length to the hidden dimension d (so M = S/d) to avoid throughput degradation on short sequences, while the LM-Head uses a fixed M = V/d. Combined with activation recomputation, this yields a theoretical peak memory of W_mem + S×(I_mem/M + √L × A_mem), enabling 12–24× longer sequences on a single A100 with no measurable throughput or convergence loss, and extends linearly to multi-GPU settings via DeepSpeed-Ulysses sequence parallelism.

## Results

MST extends the maximum trainable sequence length of Llama3-8B to 60K tokens on a single A100-80GB GPU, a 12× improvement over the vanilla PyTorch implementation (5K) and 4× over activation recomputation (14K). For Llama2-7B, MST reaches 84K tokens (12× over vanilla's 7K). Training throughput is preserved: MST achieves 3194.90 TFLOPS at batch size 2 (vs. 3271.42 for activation recomputation) and 3386.13 TFLOPS at batch size 8. On the LongAlpaca dataset, training Llama3-8B with 30K context via MST yields a perplexity of 3.45, a 2.7× improvement over the 8K activation-recomputation baseline (9.34).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is activation recomputation (gradient checkpointing) [8], which MST outperforms by 4× in maximum sequence length for Llama3-8B (60K vs. 14K) and by 1.8× for Llama2-7B (84K vs. 45K). Against the vanilla PyTorch implementation, MST achieves a 12× extension for both Llama models. In terms of throughput, MST at batch size 2 achieves 3194.90 TFLOPS, only 2.3% below activation recomputation's 3271.42 TFLOPS, and surpasses it at batch size 8 (3386.13 TFLOPS). MST also outperforms lossy quantization baselines: MST alone (60K) exceeds 4-bit quantization (10K) by 6×, and the combination MST+4-bit pushes the boundary to 140K tokens. On the LongAlpaca perplexity metric, MST at 30K context (3.45 ppl) substantially outperforms activation recomputation at 8K (9.34 ppl), demonstrating that the longer context translates to better model quality without convergence degradation.

## Relation to Prior Work

MST adapts the mini-batch gradient accumulation paradigm (Ott et al. [40]) from the batch dimension to the sequence dimension, applying it specifically to MLP and LM-Head blocks where intermediate values (7× and 32× the hidden size in Llama3) dominate memory. It is orthogonal to FlashAttention [14,15], which already minimized attention's quadratic memory, and composes with activation recomputation [8,39] to further reduce per-layer activation storage. The distributed extension integrates with DeepSpeed-Ulysses [21] for sequence parallelism, positioning MST as a complementary layer on top of existing parallelism strategies rather than a replacement.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By identifying MLP and LM-Head intermediate values (not attention) as the true memory bottleneck for long-sequence training, MST provides a simple, drop-in solution that extends maximum context length by 12–24× on a single A100 with no throughput or convergence degradation, and it composes with quantization (reaching 140K tokens) and multi-GPU sequence parallelism (480K+ tokens). The main limitations are its PyTorch-level implementation (no fused CUDA kernels) and potential throughput loss for short sequences, though the chunk-based variant mitigates the latter. Open questions include whether further gains come from kernel-level fusion, combination with activation offloading, and whether the approach generalizes to non-Transformer architectures with similarly large intermediate projections.
