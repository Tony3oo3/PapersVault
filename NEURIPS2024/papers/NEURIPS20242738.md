## Problem

While prior work characterized the static loss landscape of single-layer transformers trained on Markov chains, the learning dynamics—specifically how parameter initialization and Markovian data properties jointly determine convergence to global versus local minima—remained unexplained. This paper addresses the question of why standard Gaussian initialization leads to different outcomes depending on the switching factor p+q, and provides the first complete characterization of gradient flow convergence basins for this setting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242738/cropped_page1_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a binary first-order Markov chain sequence {x_n} with transition kernel P = [1-p, p; q, 1-q] as input and produces a next-token prediction probability via a single-layer, single-head transformer with ReLU nonlinearity and weight tying (e = a). The theoretical outputs are closed-form characterizations of all critical points (global minima, local minima, local maxima, saddle points) and the exact regions in parameter space (convergence basins) from which gradient flow reaches each type of critical point, yielding data-dependent initialization guidelines.

## Method

The core technique is a canonical low-rank parameterization that reduces the full transformer to 2–3 scalar parameters (embedding scalar e, feed-forward weight w, and optionally attention scalar a), justified by empirical observation that trained weight matrices converge to rank-one structure. On this reduced manifold, the loss becomes a logistic regression-like function with nonlinear logits e²(1+2w|w|)x_n + b_★ − e²/2. The key analytical insight is defining an energy function E(e,w) = e² − (w² + sign(w)·log|w|) that is provably conserved along gradient flow trajectories (Lemma 1), meaning the flow is confined to energy contour lines. Convergence is then determined by where each contour line intersects the set of critical points, yielding explicit convergence basins: for p+q > 1, small Gaussian initialization around the origin falls in the local-minimum basin, while for p+q < 1 it falls in the global-minimum basin. A data-agnostic initialization region (negative w half-plane above the saddle asymptotes) is identified that guarantees global-minimum convergence regardless of the switching factor.

## Results

The paper provides a complete theoretical characterization of the loss landscape and gradient flow dynamics for single-layer transformers trained on first-order binary Markov chains. Theorem 1 identifies all critical points: global minima (matching the Markov kernel), local minima (matching the marginal), local maxima, and a single saddle point at (e,w)=(0,−1/√2). The key dynamical result (Thms. 2 and 8) shows that the switching factor p+q determines whether standard Gaussian initialization around the origin leads to local or global minima: for p+q>1, small Gaussian init converges to a local minimum with high probability, while for p+q<1 it converges to a global minimum. Empirically, for p=0.5, q=0.8 (p+q=1.3>1), the standard initialization converges to the unigram (local) loss while the proposed initialization converges to the optimal bigram (global) loss (Fig. 4).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against external methods but rather contrasts two initialization strategies for the same single-layer transformer architecture. The baseline is the standard Gaussian initialization N(0, σ²I) with small variance (σ=0.02 in experiments), which is the default in practice. The proposed initialization sets the embedding vector e with all coordinates equal to 0.5, W₁ constant with scalar w₁=1, and W₂ constant with w₂=−1, placing the parameters in the global-minimum convergence basin I_★. For p=0.5, q=0.8 (switching factor p+q=1.3>1), the standard initialization converges to the unigram loss H(x_{n+1}) (a local minimum), while the proposed initialization converges to the optimal bigram loss H(x_{n+1}|x_n) (the global minimum), as shown in Fig. 4. The paper further identifies a data-agnostic initialization region I_common = {(e,w): w<0, |e|>√(w²−log(−w))+E_sad} that guarantees global-minimum convergence regardless of the switching factor, providing a practical guideline that does not require knowledge of p+q in advance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the loss landscape analysis of Makkuva et al. [23] ('Attention with Markov'), which characterized global and local minima for single-layer transformers on Markov data, by adding the missing dimension of *learning dynamics*: how gradient flow trajectories converge and how initialization determines the outcome. It also sits alongside the in-context learning literature [8, 14, 24] that uses Markovian inputs to probe transformer mechanisms, but departs from those works by focusing on the full end-to-end gradient flow rather than simplified induction-head architectures or frozen encodings. The canonical low-rank parameterization (reducing the problem to 2–3 scalar parameters) is the key technical device that makes the dynamics tractable, building on empirical observations from [23] about rank-one weight structures at optima.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first result to show that the *same* standard Gaussian initialization can lead to fundamentally different convergence outcomes (local vs. global minima) depending on the Markov switching factor p+q, providing a concrete mechanistic explanation for why transformers sometimes fail to learn the full Markov kernel. The practical initialization guidelines (e.g., initializing in the region I_common above the saddle asymptotes) are actionable but currently limited to single-layer, binary, first-order Markov settings. Major open questions include extending the analysis to deeper architectures, higher-order and multi-state Markov chains, and characterizing the convergence basins in the full 3D (e, w, a) parameter space with attention, which the authors note is technically challenging.
