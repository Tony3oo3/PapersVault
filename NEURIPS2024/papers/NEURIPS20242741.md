## Problem

Symbolic model checking of hardware designs against LTL specifications is PSPACE-complete, and existing algorithms (BDD fixed-points, SAT-based k-liveness, IC3 with SCCs) struggle to scale on designs with word-level arithmetic. This paper introduces the first use of neural networks as formal proof certificates for hardware model checking, exploiting the observation that verifying a certificate is far simpler than solving the model checking problem directly.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242741/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a SystemVerilog hardware design (with bit-vector-typed register and input variables) and an LTL specification as input. The design is simulated via Verilator to generate random trajectories, while the LTL formula is translated to a non-deterministic Büchi automaton via Spot. The output is a quantized feed-forward neural network (the ranking function) whose parameters serve as a formal proof certificate, along with a binary verdict from the SMT solver confirming whether the system satisfies the specification over all possible executions.

## Method

The learn-and-check procedure trains a neural ranking function V(r, q) = V̄(r; θ_q) — one parameter vector per Büchi automaton state — to strictly decrease on transitions from fair states and never increase otherwise, using a REU-based loss over randomly sampled transition pairs from the synchronous composition M ∥ A_{¬Φ}. The architecture comprises a normalization layer, a trainable element-wise scaling layer, and a small MLP with clamped ReLU activations (default: two hidden layers of 8 and 5 neurons). After training to zero loss, the network is quantized to fixed-point integer arithmetic and its validity over the full transition relation is checked via a one-step bounded model checking query delegated to the Bitwuzla SMT solver; if a counterexample transition is found, it is appended to the dataset and training resumes. The entire procedure is unsupervised, formally sound (the SMT check provides the guarantee), and avoids fixed-point iteration entirely.

## Results

Across 194 SystemVerilog hardware model checking tasks, the neural model checking method completes 180 tasks (93%), outperforming ABC (57 tasks, 29%), nuXmv (117 tasks, 60%), and the leading commercial tool (156 tasks, 80%). The method is faster than the academic tools on 67% of tasks, 10× faster on 34%, and 100× faster on 4%; tasks that take nuXmv up to 5 hours are completed in under 8 minutes, and ABC's 5-hour tasks finish in under 3 minutes. Training the neural ranking function typically takes under 5 minutes (70% of tasks under 1 minute), with the SMT verification step dominating total runtime.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two academic model checkers (ABC and nuXmv) and two anonymised commercial tools (X and Y). Against ABC, the neural method completes 60% more tasks on average across the time axis and is faster on 67% of tasks, with 10× speedups on 34% and 100× on 4%. Against nuXmv, the advantage is 34% more tasks completed, with the method finishing all of nuXmv's 5-hour tasks in under 8 minutes. Against the leading commercial tool (X), the method completes 11% more tasks and is faster on 75% of tasks (10× on 29%, 100× on 2%). The one notable loss is the VGA design, where the neural method fails entirely (0/10) due to local minima in gradient descent, while all other tools complete all 10 tasks. The method also underperforms on the trivial UARTt design where all tools finish in under a second, as the sampling/learning/SMT overhead dominates for simple problems.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends neural ranking functions—previously introduced for software termination analysis (Giacobbe et al., FSE 2022)—to LTL model checking of hardware designs, repurposing the fair-termination proof rule (Grumberg et al., 1985) within the automata-theoretic Büchi automaton framework (Vardi, 1991). It departs from traditional symbolic model checking (BDD fixed-points, SAT-based BMC, IC3/PDR) by replacing iterative symbolic computation with a learn-and-check loop: a quantized neural network is trained unsupervised from random executions and then verified via a single one-step SMT query. The approach also connects to the broader neural-certificate literature in control (Lyapunov/barrier certificates) and probabilistic program verification, but is the first to apply this paradigm to hardware LTL model checking.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first formally sound application of neural certificates to temporal-logic model checking, and it outperforms mature academic (ABC, nuXmv) and commercial model checkers on standard SystemVerilog benchmarks, suggesting that the 'check is easier than find' principle can be exploited with learned proof certificates in hardware verification. Key limitations include SMT-check time dominating runtime, susceptibility to local minima during training (VGA design fails entirely), and the requirement for word-level inputs that precludes bit-level netlists. Open questions include whether alternative architectures or specialized quantized-network solvers can close the remaining gap, and whether the approach generalizes beyond the 10 design families tested.
