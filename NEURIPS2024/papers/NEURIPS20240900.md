## Problem

Current video QA benchmarks primarily evaluate text-visual alignment, overlooking the harder challenge of temporal reasoning where the question and answer refer to different video segments. Frontier MLLMs demonstrably struggle with this 'reasoning-across-time' capability, yet no dedicated benchmark exists to quantitatively measure it, making progress in this area difficult to track.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240900/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives a video clip (most exceeding 100 seconds, sourced from ActivityNet and QVHighlights) along with a multiple-choice question (4 options) and must produce two outputs: the selected answer option and the temporal span (start/end timestamps) of the answer event. The dual-output design introduces a stricter evaluation metric—accuracy @ IoU ≥ 0.5—that penalizes models for correct answers paired with incorrect temporal grounding, preventing language-only shortcuts.

## Method

The core contribution is an LLM-assisted data generation pipeline that balances quality and diversity by grounding generation in a structured taxonomy of event relations. Event pairs are extracted from time-aligned captions and classified into three categories (sequential, cause-effect, means-to-an-end) using four GPT-4-scored criteria: directness, necessity, proactiveness, and purpose. QA pairs are then generated via relation-specific in-context learning demonstrations, with an LLM self-verification step filtering low-quality samples before human review, reducing annotation cost by 55%. The pipeline yields 921 validated + 2,143 test samples (human-curated) and 9,695 machine-generated training samples, the latter shown to improve fine-tuned model performance on both VQA and moment localization.

## Results

On the REXTIME mini test split (300 samples), the best frontier MLLM, GPT-4o, achieves 73.67% VQA accuracy and 36.28 mIoU for moment localization, while humans reach 87.98% VQA and 61.11 mIoU — a 14.3% accuracy gap. All frontier MLLMs struggle substantially with temporal grounding: GPT-4o's R@1 at IoU≥0.5 is only 34.00% versus 62.85% for humans. Among open-source models evaluated zero-shot, the best VQA accuracy is TimeChat at 40.04%, far below the frontier models. After fine-tuning on the REXTIME training set (9,695 samples), VTimeLLM improves from 36.16% to 57.58% VQA accuracy, and UniVTG reaches 34.63 mIoU, approaching GPT-4o's localization performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GPT-4o leads all frontier MLLMs with 73.67% VQA accuracy but still trails human performance (87.98%) by 14.3 percentage points; Claude 3 Opus (68.67%) and Gemini 1.5 Pro (68.00%) follow closely, while GPT-4V (63.33%) and Reka Core (59.67%) lag further behind. On moment localization, all MLLMs are far below human level: GPT-4o achieves 36.28 mIoU versus 61.11 for humans, and the gap widens at stricter IoU thresholds (34.00% vs. 62.85% R@1 at IoU≥0.5). Open-source models perform substantially worse zero-shot: TimeChat reaches 40.04% VQA and UniVTG achieves 28.17 mIoU, both well below the best proprietary models. After fine-tuning on REXTIME's 9,695 machine-generated samples, VTimeLLM jumps from 36.16% to 57.58% VQA (approaching Reka Core's zero-shot 59.67%), and UniVTG's mIoU rises from 28.17 to 34.63, becoming competitive with GPT-4o's 36.28. The BlindQA check (GPT-4o: 29.67% without video vs. 73.67% with video) confirms that the benchmark cannot be solved by language-only shortcuts, validating the multimodal nature of the task.

## Relation to Prior Work

REXTIME carves out a specific niche within video question answering by targeting the sub-problem where the question and answer reside in non-overlapping temporal segments, requiring causal or means-to-an-end reasoning across time. Prior benchmarks like NExTQA and NExTGQA address temporal reasoning or grounding but do not explicitly enforce low question-answer temporal overlap, while Ego4D-NLQ and QVHighlights focus on moment retrieval without QA. The paper introduces QA-IoU as a quantitative measure to distinguish this 'across-time' difficulty from existing video QA formulations, and leverages LLM-assisted generation (GPT-4/GPT-4V) with a structured taxonomy of event relations (sequential, cause-effect, means-to-an-end) to scale data creation beyond what manual annotation allows.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 14.3% accuracy gap between the best frontier MLLM (GPT-4o at 73.67%) and humans (87.98%) on a task framed as 'simple' video QA quantifies a concrete weakness in current multimodal models that existing benchmarks fail to expose. The 9,695 machine-generated training samples demonstrate a practical path for academic labs to improve temporal reasoning without prohibitive annotation costs (VTimeLLM improves from 36.25% to 58.15% VQA accuracy). Open questions include whether the gap will close as models improve, whether the LLM-generated training data generalizes beyond the ActivityNet/QVHighlights domain, and whether the moment localization deficit (GPT-4o mIoU 36.28 vs. human 61.11) reflects a fundamental architectural limitation in how MLLMs process video temporally.
