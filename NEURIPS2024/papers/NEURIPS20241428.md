## Problem

Existing pseudo-fake face generation methods for DeepFake detection simulate blending artifacts only in the spatial domain, leaving the frequency-domain distribution of wild fake faces unaddressed. This omission means pseudo-fake faces lack frequency-based forgery clues, limiting detectors from learning generic, manipulation-agnostic forgery features that generalize to unknown DeepFake types.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241428/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a real face image and a fake (or spatial pseudo-fake) face image at 400×400 resolution, converts both to the frequency domain via DCT, and produces a single pseudo-fake face by selectively combining frequency components. The blending is performed in the frequency domain rather than pixel space, and the method is designed to be applied on-the-fly during training with a configurable probability (α=0.2) of replacing spatial-blending pseudo-fakes with frequency-blended ones.

## Method

FreqBlender introduces a Frequency Parsing Network (FPNet) consisting of a shared 4-layer convolutional encoder and three independent decoders (each with convolution + PixelShuffle layers) that adaptively partition the DCT frequency map into semantic, structural, and noise probability maps. Because no ground-truth frequency partitioning exists, training relies on four self-supervised objectives: a facial fidelity loss (ArcFace-based identity preservation of the semantic component), an authenticity-determinative loss (a ResNet-34 detector must classify blended faces as fake iff fake structural information is inserted), a quality-agnostic loss (removing noise should not alter the image), and a prior-and-integrity loss (predicted maps should approximate the empirically derived frequency bands and sum to 1). At deployment, the structural information of a fake face is extracted via its probability map and blended into the semantic and noise components of a real face in the frequency domain, producing a pseudo-fake that matches the frequency distribution of wild fakes while complementing existing spatial-blending methods.

## Results

FreqBlender is trained solely on FaceForensics++ (FF++) and evaluated cross-dataset on four other benchmarks using AUC(%). It achieves 94.59% on Celeb-DF (CDF), 74.59% on DFDC, 87.56% on DFDCP, and 86.14% on FFIW-10k, outperforming the closest spatial-blending baseline SBI by 1.41%, 2.17%, 1.41%, and 1.31% respectively. On the cross-manipulation evaluation within FF++, FreqBlender reaches 96.13% average AUC, surpassing SBI-raw (92.51%) and SBI-c23 (92.24%) by 3.62% and 3.89%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FreqBlender outperforms the most relevant spatial-blending baseline SBI [16] on all four cross-dataset benchmarks (CDF +1.41%, DFDC +2.17%, DFDCP +1.41%, FFIW +1.31%), confirming that frequency-domain blending complements spatial blending. Against other frame-level pseudo-fake methods, FreqBlender leads by large margins: PCL+I2G [15] (+4.56% CDF, +7.07% DFDC, +13.19% DFDCP), Face X-ray [14] (+16.64% DFDCP), F3-Net [29] (+21.66% CDF, +13.43% DFDC, +5.60% DFDCP, +24.56% FFIW), DCL [42] (+12.29% CDF, +10.85% DFDCP, +15.00% FFIW), and BiG-Arts [25] (+17.55% CDF, +7.08% DFDCP). However, FreqBlender does not win on DFDC against TALL-Swin [22] (74.59 vs 76.78) or UCF [12] (74.59 vs 80.50), which leverage temporal or latent-space strategies beyond frame-level blending. Among video-based methods, FreqBlender surpasses all reported scores (e.g., SST [24] 89.0% CDF, FTCN [39] 86.9% CDF, LipForensics [1] 82.4% CDF). The method also improves upon DSP-FWA [10] by +25.29% on CDF and LSDA [23] by +11.59% on CDF, demonstrating broad superiority across the spatial-blending family.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the pseudo-fake face generation line of work (FWA, Face X-ray, PCL/I2G, SBI) that simulates spatial blending artifacts to improve DeepFake detection generalization. Its key departure is shifting the blending operation from the spatial domain to the frequency domain, introducing a Frequency Parsing Network (FPNet) that adaptively partitions DCT frequency maps into semantic, structural, and noise components. The training strategy—using inner correlations among frequency knowledge (facial fidelity, authenticity-determinative, quality-agnostic, and prior-integrity losses) in the absence of ground-truth frequency labels—is a novel self-supervised formulation. The method is explicitly positioned as complementary to, rather than a replacement for, existing spatial-blending approaches, and is demonstrated as a plug-and-play module on top of SBI, Face X-ray, I2G, and DSP-FWA.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and addresses a concrete gap: existing pseudo-fake generators produce faces that match wild fakes in spatial appearance but lack frequency-domain forgery traces, limiting the detector's ability to learn generic, cross-method artifacts. The consistent 1–4% AUC gains across four cross-dataset benchmarks and six network architectures, plus the plug-and-play compatibility with multiple spatial-blending baselines, make this a practically adoptable augmentation strategy for the DeepFake detection community. However, the method inherits the face-swapping assumption and shows degraded performance on whole-face synthesis (StyleGAN) and attribute editing, leaving open whether frequency-domain blending generalizes to non-swap forgery types. The preliminary finding that forgery traces extend into low-frequency ranges (not just high-frequency as previously assumed) also opens questions about whether other frequency-aware detectors are missing relevant signals.
