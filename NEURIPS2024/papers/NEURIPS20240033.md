## Problem

Medical foundation models are typically fine-tuned in a centralized manner, which is infeasible under privacy regulations like HIPAA that prohibit sharing patient data across institutions. No existing benchmark or platform addresses the challenge of collaboratively injecting multi-modal, multi-task medical knowledge from distributed private clients into a server-hosted foundation model, particularly when client model architectures differ from the foundation model's structure, making standard parameter averaging inapplicable.

## Input / Output

Each client processes heterogeneous medical data: chest X-ray images (RSNA, COVQU), 12-channel 10-second ECG signals (PTB-XL), and 48 dynamic clinical features spanning vital signs, lab results, and input/output variables (MIMIC-III). The server-side foundation model MMedLM-2 accepts only text input, so non-text modalities must be converted into aligned representations via the federated encoder before being concatenated with task prompt embeddings. Outputs are binary classification labels for six tasks (lung opacity, COVID-19, ECG abnormality, mortality, sepsis, enlarged cardiomediastinum) and free-text answers for two generation tasks (MedVQA, ECG noise clarification), with data split 7:1:1:1 across clients, public server data, development, and test sets.

## Method

The FEDMEKI platform deploys modality-specific encoders (DeiT-tiny for images, CNN for ECG signals, Transformers for tabular clinical features) with task-specific MLP decoders on each client, trained via FedAvg or FedProx on private data. The server aggregates client models by averaging, then follows an LLaVA-style alignment: the aggregated encoder produces representations of public data samples, which are projected via a linear MLP to match the embedding space of task prompts in the frozen MMedLM-2 foundation model. The concatenated representation and prompt embedding are fed into MMedLM-2 to generate outputs, with only the encoder parameters updated while all foundation model weights remain fixed. This updated encoder is redistributed to clients for the next communication round, iteratively scaling the foundation model's multi-modal capability without any client data leaving its institution.

## Results

The original medical foundation model MMedLM-2 cannot handle any of the four training tasks (all marked 'X'), as it only accepts text input. After FEDMEKI federated scaling, FedAvg_m^F and FedProx_m^F achieve 95.48% and 93.13% accuracy on Lung Opacity Detection, 98.34% and 86.11% on COVID-19 Detection, 43.15% and 45.25% on ECG Abnormal Detection, and 84.11% and 82.41% on Mortality Prediction, respectively. On zero-shot validation tasks, the scaled models achieve 58.54% accuracy on Enlarged Cardiomediastinum Detection and 39.00% on Sepsis Prediction, with BLEU scores of 1.20 (MedVQA) and 0.06 (Signal Noise Clarification), demonstrating the ability to generalize to unseen multi-modal tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MMedLM-2 (the base medical foundation model) is unable to handle any of the four training tasks or four validation tasks, as it only accepts text input; FEDMEKI's primary contribution is enabling it to process images, signals, and clinical features. On task-specific metrics, traditional FL baselines (FedAvg_s, FedProx_s) consistently outperform the FEDMEKI-scaled models (FedAvg_s^F, FedProx_s^F): for example, FedAvg_s achieves 95.31% F1 on Lung Opacity vs. 90.50% for FedAvg_s^F, and 74.50% vs. 18.74% on ECG Abnormal Detection. The FL+Global Fine-tuning variants (FedAvg_s+, FedProx_s+) also surpass the FEDMEKI approaches on most tasks, with FedProx_s+ reaching 96.18% F1 on Lung Opacity and 98.85% on COVID-19. However, the multi-task FEDMEKI models (FedAvg_m^F, FedProx_m^F) significantly outperform their single-task counterparts on image tasks (e.g., 95.51% vs. 90.50% F1 on Lung Opacity), demonstrating the value of joint multi-modal training. The paper acknowledges that the FEDMEKI-scaled models trade task-specific accuracy for the ability to handle diverse modalities and zero-shot generalization, which the base MMedLM-2 cannot achieve at all.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the FedAvg/FedProx federated learning framework and the LLaVA-style modality-to-LLM alignment mechanism, but repositions them into a novel server-side foundation model scaling architecture. It departs from existing federated fine-tuning with foundation models (FedCLIP, FedPETuning) where each client hosts a copy of the FM, instead placing the FM exclusively on the server while clients train lightweight multi-modal encoders. It also extends the FLamby cross-silo medical FL benchmark by adding the foundation model scaling objective and multi-modal coverage (signals, tabular clinical features) that prior medical FL benchmarks lack.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FEDMEKI is the first benchmark to formalize and evaluate federated medical knowledge injection into a foundation model, providing a practical HIPAA-compliant pathway to scale medical FMs beyond text-only inputs. However, the federated-scaled FM consistently underperforms traditional FL on the same tasks, and zero-shot generalization to new modalities (MedVQA, signal noise clarification) remains weak, leaving open the question of whether the LLaVA-style alignment is the right mechanism or whether more sophisticated knowledge distillation is needed. The benchmark's value lies in establishing a reproducible evaluation protocol for this emerging intersection of federated learning and medical foundation models.
