## Problem

Existing event-based eye tracking methods rely on RGB frames for guidance, suffer from label sparsity (gaze coordinates available only at coarse intervals like 100 Hz), use dense 2D frame representations that lose spatio-temporal structure, and are trained on controlled lab datasets that do not reflect real-world conditions such as varying illuminance and head movement. This paper addresses all four gaps by introducing a large-scale in-the-wild event dataset and an unsupervised graph-based tracking pipeline that requires no pixel-level labels.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an asynchronous event stream from a DAVIS346 neuromorphic camera mounted on a head-mounted device, where each event is a (x, y, polarity, timestamp) tuple at sub-microsecond resolution. Events are grouped into fixed volumes and represented as a sparse 3D spatio-temporal point cloud with normalized coordinates [λ₁t, λ₂x, λ₃y]. The output consists of (i) anatomical region clusters (pupil, iris, upper/lower eyelids, eyebrows) and (ii) continuous pupil center-line coordinates estimated via a RANSAC tube model, evaluated against 100 Hz ground-truth labels from the 3ET+ dataset and Pupil-Core reference.

## Method

The pipeline has two stages. First, a dynamic graph is constructed where events are nodes and edges are formed via a GMM-fitted adaptive radius threshold (rather than fixed-radius or kNN) that adjusts to local event density and movement dynamics; spatial edges connect co-temporal nodes within the threshold, while directed temporal edges link spatially and temporally proximate nodes across the event volume, with edge features assigned via a Hawkes-process-based decay mechanism. Second, a Variational Graph Autoencoder with GCN encoder layers learns node embeddings under a joint objective that simultaneously maximizes edge reconstruction (preserving eye-anatomy topology), maximizes graph modularity (ensuring dense intra-cluster and sparse inter-cluster connectivity), and applies KL regularization. After clustering, a custom RANSAC tube model (fitting cylinders and tori) extracts the pupil center-line trajectory from the identified iris/pupil cluster, yielding continuous coordinate estimates without any supervised labels.

## Results

On the 3ET+ benchmark, the proposed unsupervised EyeGraph pipeline achieves p10=91.45%, p5=89.22%, and l2=3.88 px, outperforming all unsupervised baselines (best prior: kmeans-2 at p10=81.45%) while remaining only ~8% below the best supervised method (MambaPupil, p10=99.42%). On the in-the-wild EyeGraph dataset (varying illuminance and mobility), the method achieves p10=93.67% and l2=3.54 px, approaching MambaPupil's 95.88% p10 without any labeled training data. For graph clustering quality, the method attains the highest modularity (Mo=75.70 on EBV-Eye, Mo=69.34 on EyeGraph) and lowest conductance (Cd=5.07 on EBV-Eye), indicating well-separated spatio-temporal anatomical clusters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against supervised methods, EyeGraph's unsupervised pipeline trails MambaPupil by ~8% in p10 on 3ET+ (91.45 vs. 99.42) and by ~2.2% on EyeGraph (93.67 vs. 95.88), but notably surpasses MambaPupil on p5 for EyeGraph (91.78 vs. 87.04) and requires zero labeled training data. Among unsupervised baselines, the method dominates: it outperforms kmeans-2 by 10% in p10 on 3ET+ (91.45 vs. 81.45) and by 7.35% on EyeGraph (93.67 vs. 86.32), and beats DMoN by 14% in p10 on 3ET+ (91.45 vs. 77.45). For graph clustering quality (Table 3), the method achieves the highest modularity on both datasets (Mo=69.34 EyeGraph, Mo=75.70 EBV-Eye) and lowest conductance (Cd=11.30, Cd=5.07), surpassing DMoN (Mo=68.80/69.46) and DGI (Mo=67.04/70.34). For dynamic graph construction (Table 2), the GMM-based adaptive thresholding yields the best silhouette coefficient (SC=0.66 EyeGraph, SC=0.54 EBV-Eye) and modularity, outperforming fixed-radius, kNN, NN, FPS, and Gabriel graph baselines. The method also demonstrates robustness under challenging in-the-wild conditions (24 Lux, free mobility), maintaining 93.67% p10 accuracy where supervised methods were not evaluated under equivalent conditions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of event-based vision and graph-based object tracking, building directly on the VGAE framework (Kipf & Welling, 2016) for topology-preserving graph embeddings and the modularity-maximization objective from DGCLUSTER (Bhowmick et al., AAAI 2024). It departs from prior event-based eye-tracking work (EBV-Eye, EV-Eye, 3ET, 3ET+), which is uniformly supervised and relies on RGB frames or fixed-interval event accumulation, by introducing a fully unsupervised pipeline that processes raw asynchronous events as dynamically evolving spatio-temporal graphs. The GMM-based adaptive edge construction and Hawkes-process edge features are novel contributions that distinguish this work from the fixed-radius and kNN graph constructions used in AEGNN and GSCEventMod.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The EyeGraph dataset (40 participants, varying illuminance, free head/body movement) fills a clear gap: no prior event-based eye-tracking dataset captures in-the-wild conditions, and the unsupervised formulation directly addresses the label-sparsity problem that plagues all existing benchmarks. The ~93.7% p10 accuracy without any labels, within ~2% of the best supervised method, suggests that the anatomical structure of eye movements is sufficiently regular for topology-aware clustering to recover pupil trajectories. Key limitations remain: monocular tracking assumes conjugate eye movement, stimuli are still controlled (moving circle on a screen), and the supervised baseline comparison on EyeGraph is limited to a single method (MambaPupil), leaving open whether the gap narrows or widens against stronger supervised approaches under the same in-the-wild conditions.
