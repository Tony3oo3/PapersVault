## Problem

In bandit convex optimization (BCO) with delayed feedback, existing algorithms (GOLD and its improved variant) achieve a regret bound whose delay-dependent part is O((n d̄)^(1/3) T^(2/3)), leaving a large gap to the known Ω(√(dT)) lower bound in the worst case where average delay d̄ is close to the maximum delay d. This paper closes that gap by showing the delay-dependent term can be tightened to O(√(dT)), matching the lower bound.

## Input / Output

At each round the algorithm selects a continuous action x_t from a convex set K ⊆ R^n and receives only a scalar loss value f_t(x_t) (no gradient), which arrives after an arbitrary delay d_t. The algorithm maintains a running sum of one-point gradient estimates from all received-but-potentially-stale loss values and uses this to compute block-level FTRL updates. The key hyperparameters are the exploration radius δ, block size K, strong-convexity parameter α, and step size η; for the convex case the optimal settings are K = n√T, δ = c√n·T^{-1/4}, and η = 1/max{√(Td), √n·T^{3/4}}.

## Method

The proposed algorithm, D-FTBL (Delayed Follow-The-Bandit-Leader), divides the T rounds into T/K blocks and plays x_t = y_m + δu_t within each block using the classical one-point gradient estimator, but only updates the preparatory action y_m at block boundaries via an FTRL rule that minimizes the linearized cumulative loss plus a quadratic regularization over all *available* (possibly delayed) gradient estimates. The critical insight is that with block size K = O(n²/δ²), the variance of the cumulative per-block gradient becomes O(K) rather than O(Kn/δ), eliminating the extra dependence on the exploration radius, while the block-level delay scales as O(d/K) rather than O(d). These two properties jointly decouple the previously entangled effect of delays and the one-point estimator's variance, yielding the O(√n·T^{3/4} + √(dT)) regret for convex functions, O((nT)^{2/3} log^{1/3}T + d log T) for strongly convex functions, and O(n√(T log T) + d log T) for strongly convex and smooth functions over unconstrained sets.

## Results

The main theoretical result is a regret bound of O(√n T^{3/4} + √(dT)) for convex functions, which improves the delay-dependent term from the previous O((n d̄)^{1/3} T^{2/3}) to O(√(dT)), matching the Ω(√(dT)) lower bound in the worst case (when d̄ ≈ d). For strongly convex functions, the bound improves to O((nT)^{2/3} log^{1/3} T + d log T), and for strongly convex and smooth functions over unconstrained sets, to O(n√(T log T) + d log T). In experiments on ijcnn1 (n=22) and SUSY (n=18) with T=40,000 rounds and delays d ∈ {200, 600, …, 5000}, D-FTBL's total loss grows slowly with d and outperforms both GOLD and improved GOLD from d=600 onward, while being slightly worse at d=200 due to the block-size overhead.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares D-FTBL against two baselines: GOLD (Heliou et al., 2020) with regret O(√n T^{3/4} + (nd)^{1/3} T^{2/3}) and improved GOLD (Bistritz et al., 2022) with regret O(√n T^{3/4} + (n d̄)^{1/3} T^{2/3}). Theoretically, D-FTBL's O(√(dT)) delay-dependent term dominates both baselines whenever d ≤ O((n d̄)^{2/3} T^{1/3}), which includes the worst case where d̄ ≈ d and even partially covers the small-average-delay regime. In the empirical evaluation on ijcnn1 and SUSY (with d̄ ≈ d by construction), D-FTBL achieves lower total loss than both GOLD and improved GOLD for all delay values from d=600 to d=5000, with the gap widening as d increases. At d=200, D-FTBL is slightly worse than both baselines, which the authors attribute to the blocking update mechanism enforcing a minimum effective delay equal to the block size K. Since d̄ ≈ d in the experiments, improved GOLD performs nearly identically to GOLD, confirming that the average-delay improvement in Bistritz et al. [2022] provides no practical benefit in the worst-case delay regime.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the bandit gradient descent (BGD) framework of Flaxman et al. (2005) and the delayed BCO algorithms of Heliou et al. (GOLD, 2020) and Bistritz et al. (2022), which achieved O(√n T^{3/4} + (n d̄)^{1/3} T^{2/3}) regret. The key departure is the incorporation of a blocking update mechanism—previously used in projection-free BCO (Garber and Kretzu, 2020)—combined with FTRL (Hazan et al., 2007) to decouple the joint effect of delays and the one-point gradient estimator's variance. This yields a tighter O(√n T^{3/4} + √(dT)) bound whose delay-dependent term matches the known Ω(√(dT)) lower bound in the worst case, closing a gap that prior work left open.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result provides the first tight characterization of how arbitrary delays affect BCO regret in the worst case, giving practitioners a clear picture of when delayed feedback is essentially free (d = O(n√T)) versus when it fundamentally limits performance. A notable limitation is that all bounds depend on the maximum delay d rather than the average delay d̄, and the blocking mechanism inherently inflates each delay to at least the block size, making it unclear whether the average-delay dependence can be recovered. The authors also identify an open connection to memory-efficient LLM fine-tuning, where the algorithm's reliance on only delayed loss values could enable asynchronous training without backpropagation memory costs.
