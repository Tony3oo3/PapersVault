## Problem

Existing physics-informed Gaussian process methods either incur cubic computational complexity from full covariance matrix operations (e.g., AUTOIP, HELMHOLTZ-GP) or are restricted to the temporal-only setting (e.g., extended Kalman smoothers). No unified framework existed that could handle both linear and non-linear spatio-temporal PDEs while maintaining linear-in-time computational efficiency.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243125/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes spatio-temporal observations on a regular grid (N_t temporal × N_s spatial points), a differential equation g(N_θ f) = 0 enforced as zero-valued virtual observations at collocation points, and boundary conditions. It outputs a full posterior over the latent function and all its spatio-temporal derivatives, yielding predictive means with calibrated uncertainty (NLPD, CRPS). The state-space structure means the temporal dimension is processed sequentially, so the dominant cost scales linearly in N_t but cubically in the spatial state size (N_s · d_s · d), which the paper then reduces via inducing points and mini-batching.

## Method

PHYSS-GP places a GP prior over a latent function and its spatio-temporal derivatives, exploiting Kronecker structure in separable kernels to cast the prior as a discretised LTI-SDE amenable to Kalman filtering/smoothing in O(N_t · (N_s·d_s·d)³). A conjugate variational inference (CVI) scheme using natural gradients is derived, where the block-diagonal structure of the ELL Jacobian maps exactly to a block-diagonal likelihood, making the posterior computable via standard Kalman smoothing. Three spatial approximations—spatial inducing points (PHYSS-SVGP), a structured posterior that conditions spatial derivatives on temporal ones (PHYSS-SVGP_H), and spatial mini-batching—reduce the cubic spatial cost to O(N_t · (M_s·d_s·d)³) or O(N_t · d_s · (M_s·d)³). A Gauss-Newton approximation via Bonnet's and Price's theorems ensures positive semi-definite natural gradient updates for non-linear differential equations. The framework recovers EKS, PMM, and HELMHOLTZ-GP as special cases and is proven optimal (single-step convergence) in the linear Gaussian setting.

## Results

On the damped pendulum (non-linear ODE), PHYSS-GP with 1000 collocation points achieves RMSE 0.06 and NLPD −0.79 in 144 s, while AUTOIP (whitened) achieves RMSE 0.05 and NLPD −1.39 but requires 5657 s (~39× slower). On the diffusion-reaction PDE, PHYSS-EKS achieves the best RMSE (0.09), NLPD (−1.26), and CRPS (0.038) in a single epoch (1.1×10³ s), outperforming AUTOIP (RMSE 0.17, 1.1×10⁴ s). On the curl-free magnetic field, PHYSS-GP matches HELMHOLTZ-GP's R² (0.97) while being 2× faster at spatial size 20, and PHYSS-SVGP_H reduces epoch time from 2.37 s to 0.16 s with a modest R² drop to 0.74.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against AUTOIP [40], PHYSS-GP achieves comparable or better predictive accuracy (RMSE 0.06 vs 0.05 at 1000 collocation points) while being ~39× faster (144 s vs 5657 s), with the gap widening as collocation points increase due to AUTOIP's cubic scaling. On the diffusion-reaction PDE, PHYSS-EKS dramatically outperforms AUTOIP in RMSE (0.09 vs 0.17) and CRPS (0.038 vs 0.065) while requiring only 1 epoch versus 20,000; the lighter PHYSS-SVGP_H variant matches AUTOIP's RMSE (0.17) at over 2× speed (4.8×10³ s vs 1.1×10⁴ s). Against HELMHOLTZ-GP [4], PHYSS-GP is an equivalent model in the conjugate (linear) setting and recovers the same posterior, but is 2× faster at larger spatial sizes (1.16 s vs 2.37 s per epoch at N_s=20). The sparse variant PHYSS-SVGP_H is ~15× faster than HELMHOLTZ-GP (0.16 s vs 2.37 s) at the cost of a modest R² drop (0.74 vs 0.97). On the LASER ocean-currents dataset (N=42,243), HELMHOLTZ-GP is infeasible, while PHYSS-SVGP_H achieves RMSE 0.14 and NLPD −0.52 at 1.86 s per epoch.

## Relation to Prior Work

PHYSS-GP unifies several previously separate lines of work: extended Kalman smoothers (EKS) for temporal ODEs, the probabilistic meshless method (PMM) for collocation-based PDE constraints, HELMHOLTZ-GP for curl/divergence-free vector fields, and AUTOIP for variational physics-informed GPs. The key conceptual departure is from AUTOIP and HELMHOLTZ-GP, which require cubic-complexity batch covariance computations, by recasting the spatio-temporal derivative GP prior as a Kronecker-structured LTI-SDE amenable to Kalman filtering. The variational inference framework builds on conjugate variational inference (CVI) and natural gradients, extending the temporal-only state-space GP literature (Särkkä & Solin) to the spatio-temporal setting with spatial derivatives, and adapts SVGP-style inducing points to exploit the Kronecker structure for further spatial scaling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work provides the first unified, linear-in-time framework for physics-informed GP regression in the spatio-temporal setting, making it feasible to handle large-scale problems (e.g., 42K ocean current observations) that were previously intractable for batch GP methods. The three spatial approximations (inducing points, structured posteriors, mini-batching) offer a practical scaling path, though the bottleneck remains the state dimension, which the authors note could be addressed with nearest-neighbor GPs. A key limitation is that the collocation method enforces the differential equation only pointwise, and the Gauss-Newton approximation for non-linear constraints, while maintaining PSD guarantees, introduces additional approximation error. Open questions include whether the framework extends to non-separable kernels, higher-dimensional PDEs, and weighted-residual formulations beyond pointwise collocation.
