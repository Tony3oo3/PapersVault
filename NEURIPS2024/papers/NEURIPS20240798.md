## Problem

While Wasserstein gradient descent (using the W2 metric) is well-studied for minimizing functionals over probability distributions, alternative optimization algorithms that exploit non-Euclidean geometries—specifically mirror descent and preconditioned gradient descent—have not been systematically extended to the Wasserstein space with discrete-time convergence guarantees. The central difficulty is determining along which curves in the Wasserstein space the objective functional must be relatively smooth and convex, since the standard notion of convexity along geodesics is too restrictive for many practical functionals (e.g., W2 distance itself).

## Input / Output

The scheme takes a current probability distribution μ_k (represented as n particles) and an objective functional F (e.g., KL divergence, interaction energy, Sliced-Wasserstein distance, Sinkhorn divergence, or energy distance), and at each step solves a proximal minimization over transport maps T ∈ L²(μ_k) to produce the next iterate μ_{k+1} = (T_{k+1})_# μ_k. For the single-cell perturbation prediction task, the input is an unpaired source distribution (untreated cells) and target distribution (treated cells), and the output is the predicted perturbed population μ̂ = argmin F(μ) = D(μ, ν). The Bregman potential φ_μ or preconditioner h determines the geometry of the update, and the step size τ controls the trade-off between the linearized objective and the geometric cost.

## Method

The paper lifts mirror descent and preconditioned gradient descent to the Wasserstein space by replacing the Euclidean proximal cost with either a Bregman divergence d_{φ_μ}(T, Id) on L²(μ) (mirror descent) or a translation-invariant OT cost ∫h((x−T(x))/τ)τ dμ (preconditioned GD), yielding updates T_{k+1} = ∇φ*_{μ_k}(∇φ_{μ_k}(Id) − τ∇_{W2}F(μ_k)) and T_{k+1} = Id − τ∇h*∘∇_{W2}F(μ_k) respectively. The key theoretical contribution is a refined notion of relative convexity and smoothness (Definition 3) stated along specific curves t ↦ ((1−t)S + tT)_#μ rather than globally, which is sufficient for convergence and much easier to verify in practice. For mirror descent, convergence requires β-smoothness of F relative to φ along descent directions and α-convexity along optimal curves to the minimizer, yielding O(1/k) or linear rates; for preconditioned GD, the dual conditions apply to the Fenchel transform of the preconditioner relative to the conjugate of the objective. The paper demonstrates that choosing a geometry-aware Bregman potential (e.g., negative entropy for KL, or Σ-adapted kernels for ill-conditioned interaction energies) dramatically accelerates convergence compared to vanilla Wasserstein gradient descent, and that preconditioned GD with a power-law preconditioner outperforms vanilla GD on single-cell perturbation prediction across 43 datasets.

## Results

For single-cell perturbation prediction on 43 datasets (34 treatments on 4i, 9 on scRNAseq), preconditioned GD significantly outperforms vanilla GD in both convergence speed and attained optimal value across all three metrics (SW₂², Sinkhorn divergence S²_{ε,2}, and energy distance). For the Sinkhorn divergence, preconditioned GD converges in approximately 10× fewer iterations while achieving a better estimate of the treated population. For KL minimization toward ill-conditioned Gaussians in ℝ¹⁰, the Negative Entropy Mirror (NEM) scheme is nearly as fast as the ideally preconditioned Forward-Backward (PFB) scheme and substantially faster than the standard Forward-Backward (FB) scheme, without requiring knowledge of the target covariance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against the Forward-Backward (FB) Gaussian variational inference scheme of Diao et al. [43] for KL minimization on ill-conditioned Gaussians. The proposed NEM scheme (using negative entropy as Bregman potential) converges much faster than FB and is nearly as fast as the ideally preconditioned PFB variant, which requires explicit knowledge of the target covariance Σ — a quantity generally unavailable in practice. For the single-cell perturbation prediction task, the main comparison is between preconditioned GD and vanilla (unpreconditioned) GD. Preconditioned GD wins on all 43 datasets (across 4i and scRNAseq, and all three distance metrics) in both the number of iterations to reach convergence (tol=10⁻³) and the final attained objective value F(μ̂). The speedup is most dramatic for the Sinkhorn divergence, where preconditioned GD requires roughly 10× fewer iterations while also achieving a lower final loss.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper lifts two established Euclidean optimization algorithms—mirror descent (Nemirovskij & Yudin, Beck & Teboulle) and preconditioned gradient descent (Maddison et al.)—into the Wasserstein space, providing the first general discrete-time convergence theory for both schemes with arbitrary target functionals (not just KL divergence). It builds on the relative smoothness/convexity framework of Lu et al. [88] and the Wasserstein gradient flow literature [5, 66], but departs from prior Wasserstein mirror descent work [9, 67, 41] by working in the intrinsic Wasserstein-2 geometry rather than flat (TV/L2) geometry on measures. The key technical novelty is identifying the correct curves along which smoothness and convexity must hold, since the minimization at each step occurs in a different tangent space L²(μ_k).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled framework for choosing transport geometries (Bregman potentials) that match the structure of the objective, with concrete convergence rates under relative smoothness/convexity along specific curves. A notable practical finding is that using negative entropy as the Bregman potential (NEM) nearly matches the ideally preconditioned scheme for ill-conditioned KL minimization without requiring knowledge of the target covariance. Open questions remain: verifying the required relative smoothness/convexity conditions is case-by-case for functionals like Sliced-Wasserstein or Sinkhorn divergences, and the theory currently requires pushforward-compatible or specific structural assumptions on the Bregman potential that may not hold in general.
