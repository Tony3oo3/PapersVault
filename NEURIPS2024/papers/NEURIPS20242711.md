## Problem

Existing meta-learning-based OSDG methods (MEDIC, MLDG) rely on a predefined sequential domain scheduler to partition data into meta-train and meta-test sets, but the impact of domain scheduling strategy on OSDG performance has remained unexplored. This paper investigates whether an adaptive domain scheduler that dynamically adjusts the training order based on domain difficulty can yield better generalization to unseen domains and unseen categories than fixed sequential or random schedulers.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images from multiple source domains with standardized category distributions (e.g., 224×224 for ViT-base, standard ResNet input sizes), where all source domains share the same set of seen categories per the MEDIC protocol. It produces a class label via either a conventional multi-class head or a binary seen/unseen head, a calibrated confidence score derived from dual evidential rebiased heads, and a per-sample reliability weight from the follower network that modulates the classification loss during training.

## Method

EBiL-HaDS introduces an adaptive domain scheduler built on three coupled components. First, a max rebiased discrepancy evidential learning (RBE) module uses two rebiased layers with a Gaussian-kernel-based max discrepancy regularization to encourage divergent decision boundaries, combined with deep evidential learning to produce calibrated confidence scores. Second, a follower network (same backbone architecture with a regression head) is trained to predict per-sample domain reliability, supervised by the RBE confidence scores. Third, a bi-level optimization couples the two: the follower's reliability predictions serve as instance-wise weights in the main network's classification loss (upper level), while the follower itself minimizes a regression loss against the confidence scores (lower level). At each meta-training step, the domain with the lowest aggregated reliability under the reserved seen classes is selected as the hardest domain, and data is partitioned so that the hardest domain's reserved-class samples form the meta-test set while the remaining samples form the meta-train set, enabling targeted optimization on the most challenging domain.

## Results

On PACS (ResNet18, open-set ratio 6:1), EBiL-HaDS-bcls achieves 87.31% close-set accuracy, 78.66% H-score, and 80.34% OSCR on average, surpassing the strongest baseline MEDIC-bcls (86.20%, 75.98%, 77.89%) by +1.11%, +2.68%, and +2.45% respectively. On DigitsDG (ConvNet, ratio 6:4), the gains are larger: EBiL-HaDS-bcls reaches 87.02% Acc, 71.13% H-score, and 75.87% OSCR versus MEDIC-bcls at 83.28%, 66.30%, and 71.15% (+3.74%, +4.83%, +4.72%). On OfficeHome (ResNet18, ratio 35:30), EBiL-HaDS-bcls attains 74.69% Acc, 62.97% H-score, and 64.47% OSCR, outperforming MEDIC-bcls (73.42%, 60.82%, 61.61%). The method also extends to ResNet50, ResNet152, and ViT-base backbones with consistent improvements.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EBiL-HaDS outperforms all 18 compared baselines across all three benchmarks and all three metrics (Acc, H-score, OSCR). The most direct comparison is against MEDIC-bcls [54], the state-of-the-art meta-learning OSDG method: EBiL-HaDS-bcls improves OSCR by +2.45% on PACS-ResNet18, +1.27% on PACS-ResNet50, +4.72% on DigitsDG, and +2.86% on OfficeHome. Against the earlier meta-learning baseline MLDG [30/46], the OSCR gap is even larger (+5.65% on PACS-ResNet18, +7.51% on DigitsDG). Non-meta-learning baselines such as ARPL [8], SWAD [6], ODG-Net [4], and MVDG [60] trail by 3–9% in OSCR. Among curriculum-learning comparisons in the appendix, EBiL-HaDS-bcls surpasses Abbe et al. [1] by +3.63% OSCR and Wang et al. [56] by +7.09% OSCR on DigitsDG, confirming that domain-level adaptive scheduling is more effective than instance-level curriculum strategies for OSDG. Notably, on the hardest split (Cartoon as unseen domain in PACS), EBiL-HaDS-bcls achieves 72.22% OSCR versus 69.04% for MEDIC-bcls, highlighting the scheduler's benefit under extreme domain shift.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the meta-learning framework for Open-Set Domain Generalization established by MLDG (Shu et al., CVPR 2021) and MEDIC (Wang et al., ICCV 2023), both of which rely on a predefined sequential domain scheduler to partition data into meta-train and meta-test sets. The key departure is the introduction of an adaptive domain scheduler (EBiL-HaDS) that dynamically selects the 'hardest' domain based on a reliability score predicted by a follower network, replacing the fixed ordering. The reliability signal is derived from a novel max rebiased discrepancy evidential learning objective that encourages divergent decision boundaries across two rebiased heads, and the follower network is optimized via bi-level optimization where the lower-level problem (reliability regression) constrains the upper-level meta-learning objective. The paper also draws on curriculum learning literature for the broader principle that training order matters, but applies it at the domain level rather than the instance level.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that the seemingly minor choice of domain scheduling strategy in meta-learning for OSDG has a measurable and consistent impact on generalization, a factor previously overlooked in the field. The method's consistent gains across ResNet18/50/152, ViT-base, and ConvNet backbones on three benchmarks suggest the adaptive scheduling principle is broadly applicable within the OSDG meta-learning paradigm. However, the approach is limited to image classification with unified category distributions across source domains, and the bi-level optimization with a parallel follower network adds significant training complexity. Open questions include whether the domain reliability signal generalizes to more complex settings (e.g., heterogeneous category distributions, video, or multi-modal OSDG) and whether the scheduling benefit persists when the number of source domains grows substantially.
