## Problem

Prior theoretical results on benign overfitting in shallow neural networks require the input dimension to scale as d = Ω(n² log n) to ensure near-orthogonality of training data, a condition rarely met in practice. This paper asks whether one can characterize benign versus harmful overfitting in leaky ReLU networks trained with gradient descent on the hinge loss under the much weaker requirement d = Ω(n), and identifies the signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) of the data as the key parameter governing the transition.

## Input / Output

The method takes n training pairs (x_i, ŷ_i) ∈ R^d × {±1} drawn from two Gaussian clusters x_i = √γ y_i v + √(1−γ) n_i where n_i ~ N(0, (1/d)(I − vvᵀ)), with k of the n labels flipped. It produces a trained weight matrix W ∈ R^{2m×d} for a two-layer leaky ReLU network and, more importantly, high-probability upper and lower bounds on the generalization error P(y f(W,x) ≤ 0 | X, ŷ) that are exponential in d/k, establishing tight characterizations of when overfitting is benign or harmful.

## Method

The network has the form f(W,x) = Σ_{j=1}^{2m} (−1)^j σ(⟨w_j, x⟩) with leaky ReLU activation σ(z) = max(z, αz), trained by gradient descent on the hinge loss with fixed outer-layer weights. The key technical contribution is the notion of approximate margin maximization: the paper proves that GD on the hinge loss converges in finite time to a solution whose Frobenius norm is bounded by C/(α√m) times the norm of the max-margin linear classifier, without requiring near-orthogonal data. Generalization is then analyzed by decomposing each neuron weight into a signal component a_j v and a noise component z_j ⊥ v, and bounding the ratio A_min/‖Z‖_F (network alignment with signal vs. noise). The proof leverages the well-conditioned singular value spectrum of the noise matrix (guaranteed for d = Ω(n)) rather than pairwise near-orthogonality, yielding benign overfitting when γ = Ω(1/k) and harmful overfitting (error ≥ 1/8) when γ = O(α³/d).

## Results

The paper establishes that a two-layer leaky ReLU network trained with GD on hinge loss converges to zero training loss in at most T ≤ C‖w*‖²/(η α² m) iterations (Theorem 3.1), with the margin-maximizing factor bounded by |A_GD| ≤ C/(α√m). Under the conditions d = Ω(n), k = O(α²n), and γ = Ω(1/k), the network achieves benign overfitting with generalization error at most exp(−C α² d/k) (Corollary 3.2.1). A matching lower bound of exp(−C d/(αk)) is shown (Theorem 3.3), and when γ = O(α³/d) the generalization error is at least 1/8, establishing non-benign overfitting (Theorem 3.4).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical baselines or experimental comparisons. The key comparison is with prior theoretical work on benign overfitting in shallow neural networks (Frei et al. 2022, Xu & Gu 2023, George et al. 2023, Frei et al. 2023, Kornowski et al. 2023), all of which require the input dimension to scale as d = Ω(n² log n) or higher to ensure near-orthogonality of noise vectors. This paper substantially relaxes that requirement to d = Ω(n), a factor of n log n improvement. The convergence proof technique (Theorem 3.1) builds directly on Brutzkus et al. (2018), who proved similar convergence for overparameterized networks on linearly separable data but could not handle label-flipping noise. George et al. (2023) studies the closest analogous setting (shallow ReLU, hinge loss, noisy data) but relies on tracking neuron activation patterns, which is only tractable in the high-dimensional near-orthogonal regime; the present paper instead uses singular value bounds on the noise matrix to handle the moderate-dimension regime.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the benign overfitting literature from linear models and high-dimensional neural networks to a more realistic moderate-dimensional regime for two-layer leaky ReLU networks trained with hinge loss. Prior neural network results (Frei et al. 2022, George et al. 2023, Frei et al. 2023, Kornowski et al. 2023) all require d = Ω(n² log n) to ensure near-orthogonal noise vectors; here the authors show d = Ω(n) suffices by analyzing the singular value distribution of the noise matrix rather than pairwise correlations. The convergence proof adapts the perceptron-style argument of Brutzkus et al. (2018), while the generalization analysis introduces a new 'approximate margin maximization' condition that unifies the treatment of both benign and harmful overfitting regimes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The relaxation from d = Ω(n² log n) to d = Ω(n) makes the theoretical guarantees far more relevant to practical settings where input dimensions are only moderately larger than sample sizes. The approximate margin maximization framework is general enough to accommodate any algorithm satisfying the norm bound, not just GD with hinge loss. Key limitations remain: the data must be linearly separable, the architecture is restricted to two layers with fixed outer weights, and only the hinge loss is analyzed. Open questions include whether analogous results hold for deeper networks, non-separable data, or different loss functions where the implicit bias of GD is not well characterized.
