## Problem

Existing transformer-based methods for medical time series classification fail to simultaneously exploit three distinctive properties of MedTS data: local temporal dynamics (requiring multi-timestamp inputs), inter-channel correlations (e.g., brain connectome in EEG), and multi-scale feature analysis (disease indicators appearing in specific frequency bands). Prior approaches like PatchTST embed single-channel patches, iTransformer embeds entire channels, and Autoformer/Informer use single timestamps—each capturing only a subset of these characteristics. Additionally, the challenging subject-independent evaluation setup, which better reflects real-world diagnosis on unseen patients, remains underexplored.

## Input / Output

The method takes a multivariate MedTS sample x_in ∈ R^{T×C} (e.g., 256 timestamps × 16–33 channels for EEG at 256 Hz, or 250–300 timestamps × 12–15 channels for ECG at 250 Hz) and produces a disease class label y ∈ R^K (binary or multi-class, such as Alzheimer's vs. healthy, or 5 ECG condition classes). The input is segmented into cross-channel non-overlapping patches of varying lengths with zero-padding, and the model is evaluated under both subject-dependent (sample-level splits) and subject-independent (subject-level splits) protocols, with the latter being the primary focus to simulate real-world diagnosis on unseen subjects.

## Method

Medformer introduces three mechanisms: (1) cross-channel multi-granularity patching, where the input is segmented into patches spanning all C channels simultaneously across a list of patch lengths {L_1,...,L_n}, capturing both inter-channel correlations and multi-scale temporal features without handcrafted band filters; (2) a two-stage self-attention design—first intra-granularity self-attention within each patch-length group to extract scale-specific features, then inter-granularity self-attention among one router token per granularity to exchange information across scales, reducing complexity from O((ΣN_i)²) to O(ΣN_i² + n²); and (3) embedding-level data augmentation (masking, jittering) applied to patch embeddings to force different granularities to learn complementary representations. The final representation concatenates all updated patch embeddings across granularities for classification, and the architecture uses shared layer normalization and feed-forward layers across granularities to further reduce parameters.

## Results

Under the subject-independent setup across five datasets (APAVA, TDBrain, ADFTD, PTB, PTB-XL), Medformer achieves the best average rank on all six evaluation metrics (1.4–2.4), with top-1 F1 scores on 4 out of 5 datasets and 15 top-1 / 30 top-3 rankings out of 30 total evaluations. Headline F1 scores include 89.62% on TDBrain (Parkinson's), 79.18% on PTB (Myocardial Infarction), 76.31% on APAVA (Alzheimer's), 62.02% on PTB-XL (multi-class ECG), and 50.65% on ADFTD (3-class dementia). The stark contrast between ADFTD's 97.50% F1 under subject-dependent and 50.65% under subject-independent evaluation underscores the difficulty of generalizing to unseen subjects.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Medformer outperforms all 10 transformer baselines on average rank across every metric, with the closest competitor being Reformer (average rank 3.6–4.0 vs. Medformer's 1.4–2.4). On the subject-dependent ADFTD benchmark, Medformer achieves 97.50% F1, edging out vanilla Transformer (96.86%) and Nonformer (95.96%) by roughly 0.6–1.5 F1 points. In the more challenging subject-independent setting, the gap widens substantially: on TDBrain, Medformer reaches 89.62% F1 versus Reformer's 87.85% and Transformer's 87.10%; on PTB, Medformer scores 79.18% F1 compared to Reformer's 79.25% (a near-tie) and Crossformer's 77.58%. Channel-independent patching methods (PatchTST, MTST, Pathformer) consistently rank in the bottom tier (average rank 7–8.4), confirming that cross-channel patching is critical for MedTS. The forecasting-oriented models (Autoformer, FEDformer, Informer) perform poorly on classification, with Autoformer ranking last (8.4–9.6) on most metrics, suggesting their decomposition and auto-correlation designs are misaligned with disease-detection objectives.

## Relation to Prior Work

Medformer builds on two key lines of work: PatchTST's patch-based token embedding for time series transformers, which it generalizes from single-channel to cross-channel patching, and Crossformer's two-stage self-attention with a router mechanism, which it repurposes from temporal/cross-dimension attention to intra-/inter-granularity attention. The paper positions itself as the first to apply cross-channel patching in transformer-based time series analysis, and extends multi-granularity approaches (MTST, Pathformer, Scaleformer) by introducing explicit inter-granularity attention rather than simple concatenation or weighted aggregation. Its specific focus on medical time series classification—rather than the dominant forecasting task—distinguishes it from most prior transformer-for-time-series work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper highlights a critical evaluation gap: subject-dependent results (97.5% F1 on ADFTD) can be misleadingly optimistic compared to subject-independent results (50.65% F1), which better reflect real-world clinical deployment where models must generalize to unseen patients. This underscores the need for the community to adopt subject-independent protocols as the standard for medical time series. Open questions include automatic selection of patch lengths (current combinations require manual tuning) and developing mechanisms to explicitly decompose subject-specific noise from task-relevant features, which would further close the subject-independent performance gap.
