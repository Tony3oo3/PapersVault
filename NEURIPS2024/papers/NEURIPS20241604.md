## Problem

Existing Learning with Noisy Labels (LNL) methods have been predominantly validated on synthetic noise benchmarks (e.g., CIFAR-10N) with class-dependent or instance-dependent noise, leaving a gap in understanding how these methods perform on real-world, fine-grained taxonomy datasets with extreme class imbalance (ρ=22429), open-set noise, pseudo-classes, and mixed feature/label errors. This paper introduces the Noisy Ostracods dataset to fill that gap and empirically tests whether current robust training and label correction techniques can handle such complex, naturally occurring noise.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241604/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Input consists of 71,466 RGB microscope images (average 476×476 pixels, 40×–300× magnification) of ostracod specimens with noisy genus and species labels, where 5.58% of test/validation samples are confirmed noisy at the genus level. The dataset is split 80/10/10 with 78 genera and 138 species in training versus 51 genera and 81 species in test/validation, plus a negative class of background crops. Outputs include genus/species classification predictions and, for the label-correction task, per-sample noisy flags with suggested corrections (re-label vs. delete).

## Method

The paper's core contribution is a meticulously curated real-world benchmark rather than a new algorithm. Noise is formally categorized into feature errors (photographic, fragmentation, position, preprocess) requiring deletion and label errors (pseudo-classes, mixed-classes, new-classes, hard-classes) requiring re-labeling, with a formal definition of feature error as an out-of-distribution condition (Eq. 1). For robust training, eleven LNL methods (Loss-Clip, Co-teaching, Co-teaching+, DivideMix, SAM, PLM, SDN, Label Wave, MixUp/CutMix, Confident Learning, and a ground-truth transition-matrix approach) are evaluated with ResNet-50 and ViT-B-16 backbones pre-trained on ImageNet. For label correction, five methods (Confident Learning, SimiFeat with CLIP/MAE backbones, AUM, CINCER, and a proposed Naive-Ensemble-Cross-Validation baseline using 14 diverse architectures) are compared against manually verified corrections. The key empirical finding is that standard cross-entropy training with ImageNet pre-training matches or exceeds all LNL methods in accuracy, and the simple NECV ensemble outperforms all sophisticated label-correction approaches in hit rate (71.19% vs. ≤59.37%), suggesting that fine-grained, imbalanced, multi-source real-world noise remains unsolved by current theory.

## Results

On the Noisy Ostracods dataset (71,466 specimens, 78 genera, 138 species, 5.58% noise rate), naive cross-entropy (CE) training with ImageNet pre-training achieves 95.98% accuracy, 88.50% precision, 77.80% recall, and 79.51% F1 with ResNet-50, outperforming or matching all 11 evaluated LNL methods on precision, recall, and F1. PLM achieves the highest accuracy (96.77% ResNet-50, 96.36% ViT-B-16) but still trails CE on F1. For label correction, the simple NECV ensemble baseline achieves the highest error hit rate of 71.19%, substantially outperforming Confident Learning (59.37%), CINCER (53.71%), and all SimiFeat variants (26–31%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's central finding is that none of the 11 robust LNL methods (Loss-Clip, Co-teaching, Co-teaching+, DivideMix, SAM, PLM, SDN, Label Wave, MixUp, CL, Transition-matrix) provide meaningful improvement over plain CE training with ImageNet pre-training on this real-world fine-grained dataset. PLM edges out CE by ~0.8% accuracy (96.77% vs 95.98% ResNet-50) but loses on F1 (73.46 vs 79.51); Co-teaching+ is the closest competitor at 96.19% accuracy but still trails CE on precision and F1. DivideMix and the Transition-matrix approach perform worst, with DivideMix dropping to 54.86% F1 and Transition* to 56.49% F1 (ResNet-50), suggesting class-dependent noise assumptions break down. For label correction (Table 2), NECV (a 14-model ensemble cross-validation) achieves 71.19% hit rate, beating CL (59.37%), CINCER (53.71%), AUM (29.56%), and all SimiFeat variants (26–31%). AUM achieves the highest fix precision (86.72%) but at the cost of very low recall, while SimiFeat's poor performance is attributed to low clusterability of ostracod features in CLIP/MAE embeddings (k-NN accuracy plateaus near the majority class proportion of ~31%). Excluding the majority-wrong Paradoxostomid class, NECV's hit rate jumps to 91.58%, indicating that methods assuming 'majority is correct' are fundamentally challenged by this dataset's noise structure.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by introducing a real-world fine-grained taxonomy dataset (Noisy Ostracods) that combines multiple noise types—open-set classes, pseudo-classes, mixed classes, and feature errors—under extreme class imbalance (ρ=22429), a combination absent from existing noisy-label benchmarks like CIFAR-10N, Clothing1M, or Mini-ImageNet. Rather than proposing a new method, it repurposes the full landscape of LNL techniques (Co-teaching, DivideMix, SAM, PLM, SDN, Label Wave) and label correction approaches (Confident Learning, SimiFeat, AUM, CINCER) as evaluation tools against a simple NECV ensemble baseline. The dataset's noise structure, particularly the co-occurrence of feature errors (requiring deletion) and label errors (requiring re-labeling), departs from the class-dependent or instance-dependent noise assumptions that underpin most prior LNL theory.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central finding—that standard cross-entropy training with ImageNet pre-training matches or exceeds all specialized LNL methods, and that a naive 14-model ensemble cross-validation outperforms every sophisticated label correction technique—challenges the assumption that methods validated on synthetic noise (CIFAR-10N) transfer to real-world fine-grained, imbalanced, multi-source-noise settings. This opens the question of whether LNL research has been overfitting to synthetic noise patterns and whether the field needs new theoretical frameworks for open-set, project-specific, and feature-corruption noise. Key limitations include the absence of a cleaned training set, the single-domain scope (ostracods), and the fact that taxonomy is not static, all of which constrain generalizability but also define concrete next steps for the community.
