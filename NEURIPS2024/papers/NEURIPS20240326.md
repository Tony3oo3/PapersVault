## Problem

In online convex optimization, the optimal regret bound G‖w⋆‖√T requires prior knowledge of either the Lipschitz constant G or the comparator norm ‖w⋆‖. In the fully unconstrained setting where neither is known, prior work established that the optimal bound is unobtainable, but the existing compromise bounds have undesirable properties: they can be much worse than the ideal bound even when the ideal bound is small, and they depend on first-order (rather than second-order) gradient quantities. This paper closes the gap by providing a near-optimal bound that matches the ideal G‖w⋆‖√T in all regimes where sublinear regret is achievable.

## Input / Output

The algorithm takes as input a convex domain W ⊆ R^d, the number of rounds T, and three user-specified scalars: γ (an estimate of G/‖w⋆‖), h₁ (a conservative under-estimate of the maximum gradient norm), and ε (a conservative under-estimate of ‖w⋆‖). At each round t, it receives a gradient vector g_t ∈ R^d and outputs a weight vector w_t ∈ W. Crucially, h₁ and ε can be arbitrarily small (even zero in the limit) with only logarithmic penalty to the regret bound, making the algorithm robust to extreme under-estimation. The regret guarantee is O~(G²/γ + γ‖w⋆‖² + ‖w⋆‖√(Σ‖g_t‖²)), which reduces to the ideal bound up to log factors whenever G‖w⋆‖√T is sublinear in T.

## Method

The method is a sequence of reductions. First, a black-box reduction from [16] reduces the general W case to W = R. Second, the problem is reduced to a regularized protocol with magnitude hints, where gradients are clipped to h_t = max(h₁, |g₁|, …, |g_{t-1}|) and a tiny quadratic regularizer a_t·w_t² is added. The key innovation is the adaptive choice a_t = γ·((h_{t+1}−h_t)/h_{t+1}) / (1 + Σ_{i≤t}(h_{i+1}−h_i)/h_{i+1}), which ensures Σa_t is doubly logarithmic in G/h₁ while being large enough to implicitly constrain the iterates without a hard bound. Third, the regularized problem is solved via an epigraph reformulation: instead of outputting w_t, the learner outputs (x_t, y_t) with y_t ≥ ψ(x_t), and the constraint is enforced by running two instances of a parameter-free base algorithm (from [21]) on the x and y coordinates separately, combined with a constraint-set reduction. This avoids the computational overhead of solving a convex subproblem at each iteration while achieving the desired regret bound.

## Results

The paper achieves a regret bound of $\tilde{O}(G^2/\gamma + \gamma\|w_\star\|^2 + \|w_\star\|\sqrt{\sum_t \|g_t\|^2})$ for G-Lipschitz losses without knowing G or $\|w_\star\|$ in advance. This matches the optimal $\|w_\star\|G\sqrt{T}$ bound (up to logarithmic factors) in all regimes where sublinear regret is achievable, with the extra penalty terms $G^2/\gamma + \gamma\|w_\star\|^2$ only becoming significant when the ideal bound is already $\Omega(T)$. Matching lower bounds (Theorem 2) confirm the result is tight, and the algorithm requires only $O(d)$ space and $O(d)$ time per update.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's main comparison is with the prior fully-unconstrained bound of Mhammedi & Koolen [18] and Cutkosky [23], which achieves $\tilde{O}(G\sqrt{T}/\gamma + \gamma^2\|w_\star\|^3 + \|w_\star\|\sqrt{\sum_t\|g_t\|^2})$. The new bound improves on this in several qualitative ways: (1) the prior bound can be much worse than the ideal $\|w_\star\|G\sqrt{T}$ even when that ideal bound is small, whereas the new bound only degrades when the ideal bound is already $\Omega(T)$; (2) the $\gamma$-dependence is milder ($G^2/\gamma$ vs. $G\sqrt{T}/\gamma$ for decreasing $\gamma$, and $\gamma\|w_\star\|^2$ vs. $\gamma^2\|w_\star\|^3$ for increasing $\gamma$); (3) the new bound uses a second-order $\sum_t\|g_t\|^2$ dependence rather than $\max_t\|g_t\|\cdot\sum_t\|g_t\|$, enabling fast rates in certain settings; and (4) with optimal $\gamma$ tuning, the new bound reduces to $\tilde{O}(\|w_\star\|\sqrt{\sum_t\|g_t\|^2}+\|w_\star\|G)$ versus $\tilde{O}(\|w_\star\|\sqrt{\sum_t\|g_t\|^2}+\|w_\star\|G^{2/3}(\sum_t\|g_t\|)^{1/3})$ for the prior bound. The new bound also ensures $O(1)$ dependence on $T$ when $w_\star=0$, a property the prior bound lacks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the fully unconstrained online learning framework established by Mhammedi & Koolen [18] and Cutkosky [23], which introduced magnitude hints and gradient clipping to handle unknown Lipschitz constants. The key departure is in how the algorithm controls the learner's iterates: prior work enforced a hard constraint |w_t| ≤ D, while this paper replaces it with a 'soft constraint' via a tiny quadratic regularizer handled through an epigraph reformulation (Protocol 3). The base algorithm for the unregularized case draws on the parameter-free mirror descent of Jacobsen & Cutkosky [21], and the general-to-1D reduction uses the black-box reductions of Cutkosky & Orabona [16]. The paper explicitly sidesteps the lower bound of [18] by accepting a quadratic (rather than linear) dependence on max_t ||g_t||, exploiting a technical loophole in that lower bound.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result provides a truly parameter-free regret bound that matches the optimal G||w_*||√T rate (up to logarithmic factors) in all regimes where sublinear regret is achievable, without requiring the user to know G or ||w_*||. This has direct implications for parameter-free stochastic gradient descent, where the algorithm achieves the optimal convergence rate without manual learning-rate tuning. Key limitations include the 'range-ratio' problem (dependence on G/h₁) and a gap between the online lower bound and the stochastic lower bound for the price of adaptivity. Open questions include whether a unified framework can capture both the old and new bounds, whether the lower bound can be tightened to hold for all G and ||w_*|| simultaneously, and whether the approach extends cleanly to heavy-tailed gradient distributions.
