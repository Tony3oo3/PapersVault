## Problem

Existing queueing bandit literature assumes service rates are static per job-server pair, independent of other jobs assigned to the same server, and lacks structured feature-based models. In real-world two-sided markets (ride-hailing, online labor), a server's willingness to serve depends on its relative preferences over all simultaneously assigned jobs, and these preferences are unknown. This paper is the first to model such preference-dependent service rates via a feature-based Multinomial Logit (MNL) function in a dynamic multi-queue, multi-server setting, while also providing regret guarantees that prior queueing bandit work has not addressed.

## Input / Output

At each time step, the algorithm receives the current queue lengths Q_n(t) for N agents, known d-dimensional feature vectors x_n for each agent, and Bernoulli job arrival indicators A_n(t). It outputs a partition of non-empty agents into K assortments S_{k,t} (at most L agents per server), after which each server stochastically accepts at most one agent according to the MNL service probability. A key constraint is that an agent can only be assigned when its queue is non-empty, and the service rate for any agent depends on the entire assortment assigned to its server rather than being a fixed pairwise value.

## Method

Both UCB-QMB and TS-QMB combine a MaxWeight scheduling rule with bandit exploration over MNL preference parameters. An online Newton step estimator tracks each server's unknown parameter θ_k from binary acceptance/rejection feedback, and the scheduling step solves argmax over feasible assortments of Σ Q_n(t)·μ̃(n|S_k, θ̂_k,t), where μ̃ is an optimistic (UCB) or sampled (TS) MNL index. For UCB, the index inflates the MNL utility by a confidence radius β_t‖x_n‖_{V_{k,t}^{-1}}; for TS, M Gaussian samples of θ_k are drawn and the maximum utility across samples is used. The stability proof uses a quadratic Lyapunov function with drift decomposition that isolates a regret-like term, while the regret proof bounds this term via elliptical potential arguments, yielding simultaneous O(min{N,K}/ε) queue-length stability and Õ(min{√T·Q_max, T^{3/4}}) sublinear regret.

## Results

The paper establishes that both UCB-QMB (Algorithm 1) and TS-QMB (Algorithm 2) achieve system stability with an average queue length bound of O(min{N,K}/ε) for large T, matching the oracle MaxWeight bound. The regret bounds are Õ(min{√T·Q_max, T^{3/4}}), which are sublinear even in the worst case. In experiments (N=4, K=2, L=2, d=2, ε=0.1), both proposed algorithms achieve stable queue lengths comparable to the oracle and exhibit sublinear regret, while all baselines show superlinear regret growth due to uncontrolled queue lengths.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares UCB-QMB and TS-QMB against four baselines: Q-UCB [Krishnasamy et al., 2021], DAM-UCB [Freund et al., 2022], MaxWeight-UCB [Yang et al., 2023], and ETC-GS [Liu et al., 2020]. On the stability metric (average queue length), both proposed algorithms match the oracle MaxWeight performance, while the baselines either fail to stabilize or exhibit significantly larger queue lengths. On the regret metric, all four baselines exhibit superlinear regret growth driven by increasing queue lengths Q_n(t), whereas UCB-QMB and TS-QMB maintain sublinear regret consistent with their theoretical guarantees. The key advantage is that the baselines were designed for settings without preference-dependent service rates (MNL model), so they cannot properly account for the relative preference structure among assigned jobs, leading to degraded scheduling decisions as queues grow.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: queueing bandits (learning service rates to stabilize multi-queue systems, e.g., Krishnasamy et al., Freund et al., Yang et al.), matching bandits (two-sided matching with preference feedback in static settings, e.g., Liu et al., Sankararaman et al.), and MNL bandits (assortment optimization under multinomial logit preferences, e.g., Agrawal et al., Oh and Iyengar). The key departure from prior queueing bandit work is that service rates are no longer fixed per job-server pair but depend on the relative MNL preferences over all jobs simultaneously assigned to a server, reflecting real-world scenarios like ride-hailing. The MaxWeight scheduling strategy from Tassiulas and Ephremides provides the exploitation backbone, while the online Newton step estimator and UCB/TS indices are adapted from the MNL bandit literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to combine feature-based MNL preference feedback with dynamic queueing systems, and the first to provide both stability guarantees and sublinear regret bounds in this setting (prior queueing bandit work addressed only stability). The worst-case sublinear regret bound Õ(T^{3/4})—achieved via a min rather than max in the regret expression—improves upon the only prior regret result for queueing bandits (Huang et al.), which does not guarantee sublinearity in the worst case. Open questions include establishing lower bounds for queue lengths and regret, improving the dependence on the MNL regularity parameter κ, and developing more efficient approximation oracles for the NP-hard combinatorial assignment step.
