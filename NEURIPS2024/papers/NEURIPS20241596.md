## Problem

Existing video representations for processing either rely on 2D/2.5D techniques (layered atlases, optical flow warping) that fail under complex occlusions and cannot support 3D-dependent tasks like depth prediction or stereoscopic generation, or use implicit 3D radiance fields that are ill-suited for explicit manipulation tasks such as object removal, motion editing, or appearance transfer. No prior method provides a single explicit 3D representation that simultaneously handles complex motions, occlusions, and a broad range of downstream video processing tasks.

## Input / Output

The method takes a monocular video clip (typically 50–100 frames) without any camera pose information, along with 2D priors extracted from foundation models: RAFT optical flow, Marigold monocular depth, and optionally SAM/DINOv2 features and segmentation masks. It produces a set of ~10⁵–10⁶ dynamic 3D Gaussians in an orthographic camera coordinate space, each carrying appearance (SH coefficients), geometry (position, rotation, scale, opacity), time-dependent motion (polynomial + Fourier coefficients), and optional per-Gaussian attributes (segmentation labels, features). From this single representation, the system renders reconstructed video, dense tracking flow maps, temporally consistent depth/feature maps, geometry- and appearance-edited videos, interpolated frames, novel views, and stereoscopic pairs.

## Method

The core representation embeds a video into explicit 3D Gaussians in an orthographic camera coordinate space (avoiding camera pose estimation by treating camera motion as scene motion), where each Gaussian's position follows a trajectory μ(t) = μ₀ + polynomial basis + Fourier basis, providing flexible modeling of both non-periodic trends and periodic motion. Training is ill-posed due to 3D-to-2D projection ambiguity, so the authors regularize learning via knowledge distillation from 2D priors: a flow loss projects 3D Gaussian displacements onto the image plane and matches RAFT flow (regularizing X–Y motion), a scale-shift-invariant depth loss matches Marigold depth (regularizing Z-axis geometry), and a local rigidity (ARAP) loss constrains neighboring Gaussians to undergo approximately rigid transformations. An optional mask loss lifts segmentation labels onto Gaussians to enable per-object editing. The total objective is a weighted sum of rendering, depth, flow, rigidity, and label losses, optimized with adaptive density control (splitting/cloning/pruning) analogous to vanilla 3D Gaussian Splatting, converging in ~15–20 minutes on a single GPU.

## Results

On the Tap-Vid benchmark (DAVIS), the proposed Video Gaussian Representation achieves the best reconstruction quality with PSNR 28.63, SSIM 0.8373, and LPIPS 0.2283, outperforming the closest competitor CoDeF (PSNR 26.17, SSIM 0.8160, LPIPS 0.2905) by 2.46 dB in PSNR. For dense tracking, it achieves AJ 41.9 and TC 1.82, competitive with the tracking-specialized Omnimotion (AJ 51.7, TC 0.74) while requiring only ~30 minutes of training versus ~24 hours. The method also achieves real-time rendering at 149 FPS with 10 GB GPU memory, matching the efficiency of CoDeF while delivering substantially higher reconstruction fidelity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method clearly outperforms all five baselines on reconstruction quality: it exceeds CoDeF by 2.46 dB PSNR and 0.021 SSIM, and surpasses Omnimotion by 4.52 dB PSNR. On tracking metrics, Omnimotion remains the strongest (AJ 51.7, TC 0.74), but the proposed method achieves 81% of Omnimotion's AJ score (41.9 vs 51.7) and 2.5× better TC (1.82 vs 0.74) while training 48× faster (~30 min vs ~24 h). CoDeF, the closest reconstruction competitor, is significantly outperformed on all quality metrics (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS) and rendering speed (149 vs 8.8 FPS), though CoDeF has a slight edge in training time parity. 4DGS and RoDynRF, which require explicit camera pose estimation, perform poorly on casual monocular videos (PSNR 18.12 and 24.79 respectively), highlighting the advantage of the camera-coordinate-space formulation. Deformable Sprites, a 2.5D layered method, is outperformed on both reconstruction (PSNR 22.83) and tracking (AJ 20.6), confirming the benefit of explicit 3D structure over layered representations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends 3D Gaussian Splatting (Kerbl et al., 2023) from static multi-view scenes to monocular video by operating in an orthographic camera coordinate space (following Omnimotion's formulation) to bypass camera pose estimation, and by attaching time-dependent polynomial/Fourier motion parameters to each Gaussian. It departs from the dominant 2D/2.5D video editing lineage (layered neural atlases, deformable sprites, CoDeF) that struggles with occlusions, and from implicit NeRF-based dynamic representations (Omnimotion, RoDynRF) that cannot support explicit manipulation tasks like object removal or motion editing. The key methodological contribution is using 2D foundation-model priors (RAFT optical flow, Marigold depth) as distillation regularizers to resolve the ill-posed 3D-to-2D lifting problem, combined with local rigidity (ARAP) constraints to prevent non-physical Gaussian motion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a single explicit 3D Gaussian representation can serve as a unified substrate for seven distinct video processing tasks (tracking, consistent depth/features, geometry/appearance editing, interpolation, novel view synthesis, stereoscopic video), which is a practical consolidation that prior 2D-layer or implicit-NeRF methods could not achieve simultaneously. However, the approach remains limited by its reliance on RAFT for correspondence (failing on fast non-rigid motion), its sensitivity to large scene changes, and its inability to handle significant camera rotations without explicit pose estimation. Open questions include whether the representation can scale to longer videos, whether learned motion priors could replace the hand-crafted polynomial/Fourier basis, and whether the orthographic assumption generalizes to wide-angle or telephoto footage.
