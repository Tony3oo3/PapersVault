## Problem

Shape-from-shading is fundamentally ambiguous: the same shading image can be explained by multiple shapes (convex/concave flips, bas-relief transformations), and humans experience this as multistable perception. Yet existing computational models—whether classical inverse-rendering methods or recent neural networks—produce only a single point estimate or a tight unimodal distribution, committing to one explanation and failing to capture the full set of physically valid shape interpretations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241094/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single grayscale shading image (typically 160×160 or 256×256) and produces a multimodal distribution of 3-channel surface normal fields, from which depth maps can be obtained via Frankot-Chellappa integration. At inference the image is divided into non-overlapping 16×16 patches, each processed independently by a small diffusion UNet, with inter-patch consistency enforced through guided sampling. The model is trained exclusively on synthetic Lambertian renderings of everyday objects (UniPS dataset, ~8000 images of 400 objects) and generalizes to ambiguous test images it has never seen.

## Method

The core architecture is a small conditional denoising diffusion UNet (10 MB total) that learns to denoise 3-channel normal patches from 16×16 grayscale image patches, using a linear-attention-based design for efficiency. At inference, all patches are denoised in parallel under a guided DDIM sampling process where the guidance loss combines an inter-patch curvature-smoothness term (penalizing angular discontinuities across seams) and a within-patch integrability term (discrete Horn-Brooks constraint). A key innovation is a multiscale V-cycle sampling scheme that alternates between fine and coarse resolutions, injecting noise at intermediate timesteps (Noise & Resume) to escape local minima and capture global shape ambiguities. An optional discrete lighting-consistency step clusters patches by their nominated dominant light direction and flips the minority cluster's normals (p,q)→(−p,−q), tying regional convex/concave choices into two global modes that match human multistable perception.

## Results

On the Xiong et al. shape-from-shading benchmark (median angular error of predicted normals, lower is better), the model without convex/concave data augmentation achieves the best or near-best error across all seven objects (e.g., 11.49° on 'cat', 9.27° on 'pig', 8.72° on 'turtle'), outperforming Wonder3D and SIRFS on most objects. The augmented model is slightly worse on angular error (e.g., 14.95° on 'cat', 21.46° on 'frog') but produces significantly more diverse multimodal outputs. On the multistable perception stimuli, the augmented model achieves Wasserstein distances of 12.59–29.96 (uniform over two reference shapes), substantially lower than Wonder3D's 27.78–38.96, confirming superior coverage of both convex and concave modes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the SfS angular-error benchmark, the model without data augmentation beats Wonder3D on 5 of 7 objects (e.g., 11.49° vs. 14.29° on 'cat', 14.17° vs. 16.81° on 'hippo') and SIRFS on 6 of 7, while Xiong et al. (which requires known lighting) remains competitive on some objects. The augmented model trades some angular accuracy for greater output diversity, consistent with a quality-versus-diversity trade-off noted in diffusion literature. On the multistable perception stimuli (Wasserstein distance to a uniform distribution over two reference shapes), the augmented model dramatically outperforms Wonder3D (12.59 vs. 30.94 on 'four circles'; 17.32 vs. 27.78 on 'star'), confirming it captures both convex and concave modes while Wonder3D collapses to one mode or a flat plane. Qualitatively, Marigold and Depth Anything each commit to a single interpretation (concave or convex respectively) and cannot express multistability, whereas the proposed model produces a bimodal distribution aligned with human perceptual alternation. The model is also far more parameter-efficient (~10 MB vs. 2–3 GB for Wonder3D/Marigold) while matching or exceeding their accuracy on the SfS benchmark.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the conditional denoising diffusion framework (DDPM/DDIM) for shape-from-shading, but departs sharply from prior diffusion-based shape models (Marigold, Wonder3D) that produce unimodal or tightly concentrated outputs. It draws on a long lineage of patch-based SfS methods and the Horn–Brooks integrability constraint, but introduces a bottom-up, patch-wise diffusion UNet (16×16 patches, ~10 MB) with spatial-consistency guidance and a multiscale V-cycle sampling scheme inspired by MRF optimization. Unlike deterministic inverse-rendering approaches (SIRFS, Derender3D) that commit to a single shape, the paper frames monocular shape inference as a conditional generative process whose output is a multimodal distribution, explicitly targeting the convex/concave and bas-relief ambiguities that are mathematically well-known but rarely captured by learned models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a small, purely bottom-up model trained only on everyday objects can generalize to produce perceptually-aligned multistable outputs on ambiguous images it has never seen, suggesting that global shape ambiguities can emerge from local patch dynamics plus consistency constraints without top-down cues. The ablation showing that multiscale V-cycle sampling is essential (single-scale guidance gets trapped in local minima) is a practical insight potentially transferable to other multimodal generative tasks. Key limitations include restriction to shadowless Lambertian shading, sensitivity to large occluded/shadowed regions, and the sequential (linearly scaling) V-cycle; open questions involve incorporating diverse materials, top-down recognition signals, and more efficient parallel multiscale sampling.
