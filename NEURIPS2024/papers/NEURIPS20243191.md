## Problem

Existing RL benchmark environments for building and data center energy management (CityLearn, Energym, RL-Testbed, Sinergym) rely on precompiled binaries from EnergyPlus or Modelica, introducing cross-platform latency, limited customization, and no straightforward re-parameterization of system behavior. No open-source, fully Python-based environment exists for benchmarking heterogeneous multi-agent RL algorithms on the coupled workload scheduling, cooling optimization, and battery management problems that jointly determine a data center's carbon footprint.

## Input / Output

Each of the three agents receives a distinct observation vector: Agent_LS sees time-of-day (sin/cos encoded), current and L-step forecasted grid CI, and remaining reschedulable workload; Agent_DC sees ambient temperature, IT room temperature, previous-step HVAC/IT energy, and forecasted CI; Agent_BAT sees battery SoC, DC net energy demand, and forecasted CI. All agents output one of three discrete actions per 15-minute step over a 31-day horizon (N=2976 steps). The environments are sequentially coupled: the workload agent's rescheduling decision modifies the IT load fed to the cooling environment, whose resulting HVAC+IT energy drives the battery agent's charge/discharge decision, and the combined energy multiplied by instantaneous grid CI yields the per-step CFP reward.

## Method

SustainDC implements three interconnected Gymnasium environments in pure Python: a Workload Environment that streams FLOP-based traces (Alibaba/Google) with a flexible fraction α reschedulable within a horizon, a Data Center Environment that models IT power via linear CPU/fan curves parameterized by CFD-derived supply and approach temperature deltas, and a Battery Environment using a modified Carbon Explorer charge/discharge model. The cooling chain computes rack outlet temperature from IT load, derives CRAC return temperature, calculates chiller power via COP, and cooling tower fan power via a cubic airflow law, with automatic HVAC sizing to match workload demand. The multi-agent formulation is heterogeneous (different observation and action spaces per agent) with a collaborative reward structure R_i = α·r_i + (1−α)/2·(sum of other agents' rewards), enabling independent-critic MARL algorithms. The paper benchmarks IPPO, MAPPO, HAPPO, HAA2C, HAD3QN, and HASAC across four US locations (New York, Georgia, California, Arizona) with ablations on single vs. multi-agent configurations and the collaboration coefficient α.

## Results

Across all three RL agents operating jointly (Experiment 7), SustainDC achieves a CFP of 155.23 kgCO₂, HVAC energy of 371.8 kWh, and IT energy of 937.4 kWh over a 31-day horizon, compared to 167.61 kgCO₂ and 391.6 kWh HVAC for the all-baseline configuration (a ~7.4% CFP reduction). Among MARL algorithms evaluated in New York, HAPPO achieves the best CFP (177.3 kgCO₂) and lowest water usage (441.9 L), while IPPO performs worst on CFP (179.6 kgCO₂) and task queue (20.9). Collaborative reward sharing (α=0.8) reduces task queue from 12.5 (α=1.0) to 9.5, confirming that cross-agent reward feedback improves performance in this partially observable setting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper benchmarks six MARL algorithms against three non-RL baselines (no workload shifting B_LS, ASHRAE Guideline 36 B_DC, and a 3-hour-horizon Carbon Explorer method B_BAT). The RL-based cooling agent (A_DC) substantially outperforms the ASHRAE Guideline 36 rule-based controller, reducing HVAC energy from ~391 kWh to ~372 kWh and CFP from ~168 to ~154 kgCO₂. Among MARL methods, HAPPO consistently achieves the best or near-best CFP and water usage across New York, California, and Arizona, while MAPPO is competitive but slightly behind. IPPO (independent critic) underperforms both MAPPO and HAPPO on CFP and task queue, with the battery agent struggling to schedule charge/discharge optimally without shared information. Off-policy methods HAD3QN and HASAC show high variance across regions: HASAC achieves the best CFP in Arizona (404.6 kgCO₂) but the worst in New York (181.7 kgCO₂), while HAA2C and HAD3QN are competitive in Georgia. No single algorithm dominates all metrics and all locations, which the authors identify as a key finding motivating further research.

## Relation to Prior Work

SustainDC extends the line of RL-based energy optimization environments (CityLearn, Energym, RL-Testbed, Sinergym, SustainGym) by providing a fully Python-native, customizable simulation of data center operations specifically designed for heterogeneous multi-agent RL. It builds on the battery charging/discharging models from Carbon Explorer (Acun et al., ASPLOS 2023) and the CPU/fan power curves from Sun et al. (Energy and Buildings 2021), but departs from prior environments that rely on precompiled EnergyPlus or Modelica binaries by implementing all thermo-fluid and electrical dynamics directly in Python with Gymnasium interfaces. The key conceptual departure is framing DC sustainability as a joint heterogeneous MARL problem—simultaneously optimizing workload scheduling, cooling setpoints, and battery management—rather than the single-agent or single-component optimization addressed by prior testbeds.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SustainDC provides the first dedicated benchmark for heterogeneous MARL in data center operations, filling a gap where prior environments either lacked multi-agent structure or were not easily customizable for ML research. The finding that no single algorithm (IPPO, MAPPO, HAPPO, HAA2C, HAD3QN, HASAC) dominates across all five evaluation metrics and four geographic locations highlights the sensitivity of DC control to local weather and grid carbon intensity, suggesting that deployment will require location-specific tuning or domain adaptation. Open questions include whether the reduced-order models (pumps, cooling towers) used for computational speed introduce systematic biases, whether the collaborative reward structure generalizes beyond the tested α values, and how trained agents perform under distribution shift during real-world deployment—questions the authors acknowledge but leave for future work.
