## Problem

Pretraining datasets for state-of-the-art open LLMs like Llama 3 and Mixtral are not publicly available and their curation strategies are scarcely documented, creating a growing gap between proprietary and public knowledge about how to build high-quality pretraining corpora. This paper addresses that gap by releasing a 15-trillion-token dataset with full documentation of every design choice, supported by systematic ablation experiments that empirically validate each pipeline stage.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240970/cropped_page7_idx9.webp)

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes raw WARC files (full HTML plus request metadata) from 96 Common Crawl snapshots rather than the commonly used pre-extracted WET text files, applying trafilatura for custom text extraction. The output is a per-document JSONL dataset where each record contains the extracted text, original Common Crawl identifiers (id, dump, url, date, file_path), a fastText language score, and a GPT-2 token count, with splits available per crawl snapshot. FineWeb-Edu additionally assigns each document an integer educational quality score (0–5) from a classifier, with the released subset containing only documents scoring ≥3.

## Method

The curation pipeline proceeds through five empirically validated stages: (1) trafilatura text extraction from WARC files, (2) base filtering via URL blocklist, fastText English classification (score ≥0.65), and MassiveText quality/repetition thresholds, (3) per-snapshot MinHash deduplication using 5-grams with 112 hash functions split into 14 buckets of 8 (targeting ≥75% similarity with transitive clustering), (4) a subset of C4 heuristic filters (curly bracket, word lengths, lorem ipsum, javascript, policy rules) excluding the terminal-punctuation filter that removes ~30% of tokens, and (5) three custom filters derived from a systematic distributional analysis comparing high- and low-quality data (fraction of lines ending with punctuation ≤0.12, fraction of characters in duplicated lines ≥0.1, fraction of lines shorter than 30 characters ≥0.67). A key finding is that per-snapshot deduplication substantially outperforms global cross-snapshot deduplication, which over-removes content from older crawls and upsamples lower-quality data. For FineWeb-Edu, 460K documents are scored by Llama-3-70B-Instruct using an additive 5-point educational rubric, and a linear regression head is fine-tuned on top of frozen Snowflake-arctic-embed-m embeddings to scale scoring to the full 15T-token corpus.

## Results

FineWeb-Edu (1.3T tokens) outperforms all other openly accessible web-based pretraining datasets on the aggregate of 8 benchmarks (CommonSenseQA, HellaSwag, OpenBookQA, PIQA, SIQA, WinoGrande, ARC, MMLU), improving the aggregate score by approximately 2% over base FineWeb. On knowledge-intensive benchmarks, FineWeb-Edu achieves 37% on MMLU (vs. 33% for FineWeb, a ~12% relative gain) and 57% on ARC (vs. 46% for FineWeb, a ~24% relative gain). Notably, FineWeb-Edu matches the MMLU performance of Matrix (1.3T tokens) at only 38B training tokens — roughly 10× fewer tokens — demonstrating the effectiveness of LLM-annotated educational filtering at scale.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FineWeb matches RefinedWeb (500B tokens) in aggregate performance after applying individual per-snapshot MinHash deduplication, then surpasses it by adding C4-derived filters and three custom heuristic filters (lines-with-punctuation ≤ 0.12, duplicated-line-char-ratio ≥ 0.1, lines-shorter-30-ratio ≥ 0.67), which together remove ~22% of tokens and yield a ~1% aggregate gain. Against C4 (172B tokens), FineWeb matches its HellaSwag performance using C4's own filters (excluding the overly aggressive terminal-punctuation filter that removes 30% of data), then exceeds it with the custom filters. FineWeb-Edu decisively outperforms all nine compared public datasets (RefinedWeb, C4, Dolma 1.6/1.7, The Pile, SlimPajama, RedPajama v2, Matrix, CC-100, Colossal-OSCAR) on the aggregate benchmark score. On MMLU specifically, FineWeb-Edu reaches 37% at 350B tokens and 33.6% at just 38B tokens, matching Matrix's final MMLU accuracy (reached at ~300B tokens) with roughly 10× fewer training tokens. The educational filtering also shifts domain fit: FineWeb-Edu achieves lower perplexity on Wikipedia, arXiv, and programming content compared to FineWeb, while FineWeb retains lower perplexity on broad web sources (Reddit, 4chan, Twitter AAE).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the RefinedWeb pipeline (same first author) by replacing WET-based text extraction with trafilatura on WARC files, systematically ablating each filtering and deduplication stage, and introducing a principled method for designing heuristic filters by comparing metric distributions between high- and low-quality data subsets. It draws on MassiveText's repetition/quality thresholds, C4's heuristic filters (selectively applied), and the additive scoring scale from self-rewarding language models (Yuan et al.) for its educational content classifier. The key conceptual departure from prior work is the finding that global cross-snapshot deduplication is counterproductive, leading to per-snapshot MinHash deduplication as the preferred strategy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FineWeb (15T tokens) and FineWeb-Edu (1.3T tokens) become the largest fully documented open pretraining datasets, with all ablation models and the datatrove codebase released for reproducibility. The counterintuitive result that global deduplication harms performance (the 'kept' data was lower quality than the 'removed' data) challenges a widely held assumption and provides concrete guidance for future dataset curation. Open questions include whether these findings hold at much larger model scales (ablations used 1.71B models), whether augmenting with non-web data (books, code, speech) would further improve performance, and whether the educational classifier's gains transfer to instruction-tuned or aligned models. The paper also acknowledges that its evaluation is limited to academic benchmarks without instruction tuning, which may not reflect real-world LLM usage.
