## Problem

Multi-view indoor 3D object detection from RGB images alone requires accurate geometry estimation, but existing self-supervised approaches either naively backproject features to all intersected voxels (ImVoxelNet) or rely on NeRF density fields that produce inaccurate surface geometry (NeRF-Det). The core gap is how to efficiently determine correct 3D feature placement without the computationally prohibitive sampling of many depth planes required by standard multi-view stereo, and without the unreliable implicit geometry of NeRF.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20244221/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes N posed RGB images (resized to 240×320) with known camera intrinsics K and extrinsics P=[R|t]; 40 images feed the detection branch during training and 100 during testing. It outputs 7-DoF oriented bounding boxes (x, y, z, w, h, l, φ) with class labels and centerness scores predicted per voxel in a 40×40×16 3D volume (each voxel ≈ 0.16m × 0.16m × 0.2m). A separate rendering branch consumes 2 additional target views for Gaussian Splatting supervision during training only.

## Method

MVSDet builds a cost volume per view using a variance-based matching metric across 2 nearby source views warped onto M=12 fronto-parallel depth planes, refined by a shallow 3D CNN into a probability volume B. Instead of regressing a single depth, the key contribution is a probabilistic sampling and soft weighting mechanism: for each pixel, the top-k=3 depth proposals from B are selected, and a voxel receives the pixel feature only if its depth falls within ±0.2m of one of these proposals, weighted by the normalized probability score; a multi-view surface score further downweights voxels in free space. Pixel-aligned Gaussian Splatting (PAGS) is added as a lightweight regularizer: Gaussian centers are placed along rays at the predicted depth, and an L2 rendering loss on 2 held-out target views iteratively refines depth accuracy with only +2.6 GB training memory overhead and zero test-time cost. The resulting depth-aware 3D feature volume is processed by a 3D U-Net and a detection head (focal + centerness + IoU losses) inherited from ImVoxelNet.

## Results

On ScanNet, MVSDet achieves 56.2 mAP@0.25 and 31.3 mAP@0.5 without any ground-truth geometry supervision, outperforming the closest self-supervised baseline NeRF-Det (53.5 / 27.4) by +2.7 and +3.9 points respectively. On ARKitScenes, the method reaches 42.9 mAP@0.25 and 27.0 mAP@0.5, improving over NeRF-Det (39.5 / 21.9) by +3.4 and +5.1 points. Notably, MVSDet also surpasses ImGeoNet (which uses ground-truth geometry) by +1.4 mAP@0.25 on ScanNet, while remaining competitive with CN-RMA (58.6 / 36.8) which also relies on GT geometry.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the two self-supervised baselines, MVSDet clearly wins: it beats ImVoxelNet by +9.5 mAP@0.25 and +7.9 mAP@0.5 on ScanNet (and by a much larger margin of +15.6 / +22.7 on ARKitScenes), and beats NeRF-Det by +2.7 / +3.9 on ScanNet and +3.4 / +5.1 on ARKitScenes. Against the two ground-truth-geometry methods, MVSDet surpasses ImGeoNet on mAP@0.25 (56.2 vs 54.8) but trails CN-RMA (58.6 / 36.8) on both metrics, which is expected since CN-RMA uses GT TSDF supervision. In the depth-prediction comparison (Fig. 6), MVSNet with 16 depth planes and BEVStereo (without GT depth supervision) both perform poorly, while MVSDet with only 12 planes approaches the ground-truth-depth upper bound. The ablation in Table 4 further shows that adding PAGS with M=12 planes matches the detection performance of M=16 planes (31.3 vs 31.1 mAP@0.5) while consuming only +2.6 GB extra memory versus +9.4 GB, and PAGS is entirely removed at test time.

## Relation to Prior Work

MVSDet builds on the 3D volume-based detection framework of ImVoxelNet and the cost volume construction of MVSNet, but departs sharply from NeRF-Det by replacing implicit NeRF-based geometry with an explicit plane-sweep cost volume. The key conceptual departure is the probabilistic sampling and soft weighting mechanism, which avoids the need for dense depth plane sampling by selecting only top-k depth proposals per pixel and weighting features by their normalized probability scores. Additionally, the paper repurposes pixel-aligned Gaussian Splatting (PAGS)—a technique originally designed for novel view synthesis—as a lightweight depth prediction regularizer for the detection pipeline, rather than as a primary geometry learner.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work addresses the practical bottleneck of indoor 3D detection without depth sensors by showing that a small number of depth planes (12) combined with probabilistic sampling can approach ground-truth depth performance, making the pipeline significantly cheaper than dense MVS or NeRF-based approaches. The finding that PAGS improves depth RMSE from 0.674 to 0.374 with only +2.6 GB memory (vs. +9.4 GB for adding 4 more planes) suggests a general recipe for using rendering losses as geometry regularizers in detection tasks. Open questions include whether the approach extends to outdoor scenes with larger depth ranges, whether it handles texture-less or reflective surfaces (acknowledged limitation), and whether the probabilistic sampling mechanism generalizes to other volume-based tasks beyond detection.
