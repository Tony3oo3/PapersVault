## Problem

Adversarial Training and Adversarial Robustness Distillation improve overall robustness but exhibit a robust fairness problem: models are strongly robust on easy classes yet highly vulnerable on hard classes. Prior fairness methods address this from the sample perspective (re-weighting loss or margins), but overlook the role of label smoothness in the optimization objective. This paper identifies that the class-wise smoothness degree of soft labels is a previously unexplored factor that directly influences robust fairness.

## Input / Output

The method operates on standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 64×64 for Tiny-ImageNet) and outputs a class probability distribution over C classes. A key structural detail is that the teacher's soft labels are re-tempered independently per class during training, and adversarial examples are generated by maximizing KL divergence between the student's prediction and the teacher's re-tempered soft labels (rather than the student's own logits), making the attack target class-dependent.

## Method

ABSLD extends adversarial robustness distillation by adaptively re-tempering the frozen teacher's soft labels per class based on the student's class-wise robust error risk. When a class has higher-than-average error risk (hard class), its teacher temperature is reduced, producing sharper soft labels that impose stronger supervision; conversely, easy classes receive higher temperatures and smoother labels. Two independent temperature sets are maintained for clean and adversarial examples, updated each epoch via a normalized gradient step proportional to the gap between each class's error risk and the mean. The training loss is a weighted combination of KL divergences between student and re-tempered teacher predictions on both clean and adversarial inputs, with adversarial examples generated by maximizing KL against the teacher's class-specific soft labels under an L∞ budget. The paper provides theoretical analysis (Corollary 1, Theorems 1–2) showing that equal smoothness across classes inevitably produces bias, while differential smoothness provably reduces the class-wise error risk gap.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with ResNet-18, ABSLD achieves 36.5% worst-class robust accuracy under PGD (ε=8/255) and 31.0% under AutoAttack, outperforming the best baselines (CFA: 33.3% PGD worst, FRL: 28.1% AA worst). The Normalized Standard Deviation (NSD), which jointly captures robustness and fairness, is 0.216 for PGD and 0.256 for AA, the lowest among all compared methods. On CIFAR-100 (ResNet-18), ABSLD reaches 10.5% worst-10% class robustness under PGD with NSD 0.500, and on Tiny-ImageNet (PreActResNet-18) it achieves 4.6% worst-class PGD robustness with NSD 0.313, both state-of-the-art among the compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ABSLD consistently outperforms all eight baselines on both worst-class robustness and NSD across every attack (FGSM/PGSM, PGD, CW∞, AutoAttack) on CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100. Against the strongest fairness-oriented baselines, ABSLD improves worst-class PGD robustness by 3.2% over CFA (36.5% vs 33.3%) and by 4.8% over FRL (36.5% vs 31.7%) on CIFAR-10 ResNet-18, while reducing NSD by 0.042 and 0.032 respectively. Compared to the closest ARD-based fairness method Fair-ARD [38], ABSLD gains 6.6% worst-class PGD accuracy (36.5% vs 29.9%) and reduces NSD from 0.296 to 0.216. Against standard AT baselines (SAT, TRADES), ABSLD improves worst-class PGD robustness by 15.5% and 6.1% respectively. The trade-off is modest: ABSLD's average PGD robustness (54.50%) is within 0.1% of the best baseline (CFA at 54.42%), confirming that the fairness gains come at negligible cost to overall robustness. On Tiny-ImageNet, ABSLD wins on worst-class and NSD metrics but trails RSLAD by ~1.3% in average PGD accuracy, indicating a slight robustness-fairness trade-off on harder datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Adversarial Robustness Distillation (ARD) framework, building directly on RSLAD [45] as its baseline, and addresses the robust fairness problem that prior ARD methods (Fair-ARD [38]) and adversarial training methods (FRL [39], BAT [28], CFA [36]) have tackled from a sample-reweighting perspective. The key conceptual departure is shifting from re-weighting samples to re-tempering the teacher's soft labels per class, using temperature as a knob to control class-wise supervision intensity. This draws on the standard KD temperature mechanism [11] but repurposes it as an adaptive fairness control rather than a fixed hyperparameter, and is grounded in a theoretical analysis (Corollary 1, Theorems 1–2) showing that equal smoothness across classes inevitably produces biased error risk.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The robust fairness problem is practically critical: an overall-robust model with a few vulnerable classes gives attackers a clear target, making class-wise security a deployment concern beyond average robustness. ABSLD offers a lightweight, plug-and-play modification to existing KD pipelines (just adjusting per-class temperatures) that consistently improves worst-class robustness and NSD across CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and Tiny-ImageNet without requiring retraining the teacher. However, the robustness-fairness trade-off persists (average robustness is slightly lower than some baselines), and the method is restricted to soft-label distillation settings, limiting direct applicability to one-hot-label adversarial training. Open questions include whether the temperature-based fairness control can be combined with sample re-weighting for complementary gains, and whether the approach extends to non-KD adversarial training or larger-scale datasets.
