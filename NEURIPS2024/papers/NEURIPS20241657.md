## Problem

Existing multimodal fusion detection (MFD) methods rely on multi-step, cascaded training paradigms where the fusion and detection networks are optimized sequentially or in multiple stages, leading to inefficiency and susceptibility to local optima of individual tasks. The inherent optimization barrier between pixel-level fusion objectives and semantic-level detection objectives makes it difficult to balance shared parameters, and no prior work achieves a truly end-to-end, single-stage joint training of both tasks.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pair of aligned visible (RGB) and infrared images as input, processed through a shared parallel backbone to extract multimodal features. It simultaneously produces a fused image (extracted from the V channel of HSV space, preserving brightness and texture) and object bounding boxes with class labels—horizontal boxes (x_c, y_c, w, h) for M3FD and oriented boxes (x_c, y_c, w, h, θ) for DroneVehicle. The detection head operates on 500 proposal boxes via iterative denoising, and the fused image is generated at the same spatial resolution as the input pair.

## Method

E2E-MFD integrates a fusion network (ORPPT) and a diffusion-based detection head (CFDP) into a single end-to-end architecture with shared backbone parameters. ORPPT extracts multi-granularity features through a pixel feature mining module and L region feature refinement modules that use learnable region prompts to generate spatial masks, aggregating region-level representations that are injected into pixel-level features. CFDP replaces the standard RPN with a Markovian diffusion process that iteratively denoises bounding boxes conditioned on the image pair, providing coarse-to-fine box guidance. The key training innovation is Gradient Matrix Task-Alignment (GMTA), which periodically (every n iterations) projects the shared-parameter gradient matrix onto the nearest matrix with condition number 1 via SVD-based Procrustes alignment, ensuring the MF and OD gradients are orthogonal and equal in magnitude to eliminate task dominance and conflicting gradients during joint optimization.

## Results

On the M3FD dataset, E2E-MFD achieves 91.8 mAP50 and 63.8 mAP50:95 for horizontal object detection, surpassing the best fusion-only baseline (PIAFusion, 87.3 mAP50) by 4.5% and the best MF-OD baseline (MetaFusion, 86.7 mAP50) by 5.1%. On the DroneVehicle oriented detection benchmark, E2E-MFD reaches 77.4 mAP50, outperforming the best multimodal baseline CALNet (75.4) by 2.0% and the best single-modality detector LSKNet-OBB (72.8) by 4.6%. For image fusion quality on M3FD, E2E-MFD attains the best EN (6.36), MI (15.47), and VIF (1.65) among all compared methods, while also achieving the fastest training time (2h50m32s) due to its single-stage end-to-end design.

## Baselines & Comparisons

E2E-MFD outperforms all fusion-only baselines (DIDFuse, U2Fusion, PIAFusion, SwinFusion, CDDFuse) by 4.5–6.0 mAP50 on M3FD, confirming that jointly optimizing fusion and detection yields substantially better detection than using independently trained fusion networks. Against the two MF-OD cascaded baselines (Tardal, MetaFusion), E2E-MFD gains 5.1 and 5.8 mAP50 respectively, demonstrating the advantage of synchronous end-to-end training over multi-stage joint cascaded designs. Compared to end-to-end detection methods (CFT, ICA Fusion), E2E-MFD still leads by 2.0 and 4.4 mAP50, showing that the fusion component provides complementary information beyond direct cross-modality detection. On DroneVehicle oriented detection, E2E-MFD (77.4 mAP50) surpasses the strongest multimodal baseline CALNet (75.4) by 2.0% and TSFADet (70.4) by 7.0%, while also exceeding the best single-modality LSKNet-OBB (72.8) by 4.6%. For fusion quality metrics, E2E-MFD achieves the highest EN, MI, and VIF across all three fusion datasets (M3FD, TNO, RoadScene), with the largest margin over MetaFusion (the closest MF-OD competitor) being +0.16 EN, +0.28 MI, and +0.11 VIF on M3FD.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant cascaded MF-OD paradigm (Tardal, MetaFusion) that requires multi-stage progressive training, proposing instead a single-stage end-to-end synchronous optimization. It repurposes DiffusionDet's diffusion-based detection as a detection head (CFDP), reuses MetaFusion's pixel feature mining block, and adapts the condition-number-based gradient alignment from Aligned-MTL (Senushkin et al.) into the GMTA technique specifically tailored to the MF-OD gradient conflict. The ORPPT hierarchical structure is a novel architectural contribution that organizes fusion features from object-level to pixel-level granularity, but its individual components (region prompts, feature fusion blocks) draw from established attention and fusion literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The end-to-end formulation eliminates the tedious multi-stage training pipeline that has been a practical barrier to deploying MF-OD systems, and the GMTA technique offers a principled, lightweight solution to the gradient dominance problem that plagues multi-task fusion-detection training. However, the approach is validated only on visible-infrared modalities and a limited set of datasets, leaving open whether the synchronous optimization strategy generalizes to other modality pairs (e.g., LiDAR-camera, SAR-optical) or to other downstream tasks beyond detection. The finding that deeper semantic branches in ORPPT degrade fusion quality highlights an unresolved tension between semantic abstraction and pixel-level fidelity in shared-backbone multi-task architectures.
