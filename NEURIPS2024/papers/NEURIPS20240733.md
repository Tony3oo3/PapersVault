## Problem

Detecting which individual samples are vulnerable to adversarial attacks in real-time deployment is critical for safety-critical systems, but computing the exact input-space margin (distance to the decision boundary) is intractable for deep networks, and running adversarial attacks per-instance is computationally prohibitive at scale. No prior work has studied whether the readily available logit margin from a single forward pass can serve as a reliable proxy for the input margin in robustly trained classifiers.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a single RGB image (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 224×224 for ImageNet) and performs one forward pass through a pre-trained adversarially-trained model (ResNet, WideResNet, PreActResNet, or XCiT). It outputs a scalar vulnerability score — the logit margin (difference between the top two logits) — which is thresholded to yield a binary non-robust/robust label. For the two weakly margin-consistent models, a small 5-layer MLP (512 neurons, ReLU, sigmoid output) maps the penultimate-layer feature vector to a learned pseudo-margin instead, trained on 5,000 samples with MSE loss.

## Method

The paper introduces margin consistency — a monotonic relationship between input-space margins and logit margins — and proves (Theorem 1) that it is both necessary and sufficient for the logit margin to perfectly separate non-robust from robust samples at any threshold ε. Empirically, across 30+ adversarially-trained models from RobustBench on CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and ImageNet, most exhibit strong margin consistency (Kendall τ up to 0.86), yielding AUROC scores above 95% for non-robust detection at ε=8/255. For the two exceptions (DI0, XU80 with τ≈0.28–0.43), the authors learn a pseudo-margin from the feature representation via a pointwise regression scheme, improving τ to 0.57–0.62 and FPR@95 from 82–83% to 33–51%. As a downstream application, margin consistency enables sample-efficient robust accuracy estimation: attacking only 500 randomly sampled test points and thresholding the logit margin on the full 10,000-sample test set recovers the standard robust accuracy with small absolute error.

## Results

Across 33 adversarially trained models on CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100 (ε=8/255, ℓ∞), most exhibit strong margin consistency with Kendall τ between the input margin and logit margin ranging from 0.68 to 0.86. The logit margin achieves excellent detection performance for non-robust samples: AUROC up to 98.01 (RA11 on CIFAR-100), AUPR up to 99.08, and FPR@95 as low as 12.36. Two models (DI0 and XU80) show weak margin consistency (τ=0.28 and 0.43), yielding poor detection (AUROC 67.49 and 83.30, FPR@95 >82%). For these two, learning a pseudo-margin from feature representations improves Kendall τ to 0.57 and 0.62, boosting AUROC to 88.49 and 93.66 respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not benchmark against other vulnerability detection methods; instead, it evaluates whether the logit margin (a free byproduct of a forward pass) can serve as a reliable proxy for the intractable input space margin. The key comparison is between the logit margin and the learned pseudo-margin for the two weakly margin-consistent models (DI0, XU80): the pseudo-margin improves Kendall τ from 0.28→0.57 (DI0) and 0.43→0.62 (XU80), and reduces FPR@95 from 82.56→51.13 and 83.42→33.00 respectively (Table 2). A secondary comparison (Table 3) shows that using the logit margin versus the exact feature margin (requiring a min over K−1 pairs) yields nearly identical detection scores (e.g., AUROC 97.86 vs. 98.26 for AD10), confirming the logit margin is a valid and computationally cheaper approximation. The paper also demonstrates that margin consistency strength is independent of robust accuracy (Fig. 4), and that the logit margin can estimate robust accuracy from only 500 attacked samples with small absolute error for strongly margin-consistent models (Fig. 10).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves out a new conceptual space at the intersection of adversarial robustness evaluation and confidence estimation. It builds on the input margin / robust radius concept from adversarial attack literature (Szegedy et al., Carlini & Wagner) and the confidence-learning framework of Corbière et al. (2019) for the pseudo-margin component, but the central contribution—formalizing 'margin consistency' as a necessary and sufficient condition linking input-space and logit-space margins for vulnerability detection—is explicitly positioned as absent from prior work. The paper distinguishes its task from adversarial example detection, OOD detection, and misclassification detection, and contrasts its empirical, forward-pass approach with formal verification methods (SMT, MILP, branch-and-bound) that are computationally prohibitive at scale.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for deployment: a single forward pass (logit margin) can flag vulnerable samples without running expensive adversarial attacks, enabling real-time risk monitoring in safety-critical systems. The finding that most adversarially trained models across diverse architectures and training strategies exhibit high margin consistency (τ up to 0.86) suggests this is a robust emergent property of adversarial training rather than an artifact of specific methods. Key open questions include whether the property survives neural collapse in the terminal training phase, whether it holds on OOD or adversarially crafted inputs, and whether other notions of local robustness (e.g., average robustness over an ε-ball) can similarly be extracted from feature representations.
