## Problem

Inverse Reinforcement Learning is inherently ambiguous: many reward functions can explain the same optimal expert's behavior. Prior work on recovering the full feasible reward set addressed only single-expert settings, ignoring the common real-world scenario where multiple agents with varying skill levels (e.g., professional vs. amateur racing drivers) are observable. This paper asks whether and how sub-optimal experts, whose performance gap from the optimal expert is bounded, can shrink the feasible reward set and what the statistical cost of estimating it is.

## Input / Output

The algorithm receives an MDP without a reward function, the optimal expert's policy, n sub-optimal expert policies each paired with a known upper bound ξ_i on its value-function gap from the optimal expert, and a generative model that returns next-state transitions and all experts' actions for any queried state-action pair. The sub-optimality bounds can be crude overestimates (e.g., derived from transition-model mismatches, discount-factor differences, or policy L1-distance). The output is the full feasible reward set R_B̄ — the collection of all reward functions in [0,1]^{S×A} under which the optimal expert is optimal and every sub-optimal expert's value gap stays within its bound — estimated to Hausdorff distance ε with probability 1−δ.

## Method

The key theoretical result (Theorem 3.6) characterizes the feasible reward set with sub-optimal experts as the intersection of the classical single-expert decomposition r = −B̄^{π_E1}ζ + (E−γP)V with additional linear constraints d^{π_E1} π_Ei B̄^{π_E1} ζ ≤ 1_S ξ_i for each sub-optimal expert i. These constraints bound the advantage values ζ(s,a) for state-action pairs that the optimal expert avoids but a sub-optimal expert plays, with the tightest bound scaling as ξ_i / (d^{π_Ei}(s) · π_Ei(a|s)), thereby shrinking the feasible set whenever sub-optimal experts behave differently from the optimal one and are sufficiently close in performance. For statistical analysis, the paper derives a minimax lower bound with an extra π_min^{-1} factor (where π_min is the minimum action probability of any sub-optimal expert) beyond the single-expert rate, and proposes US-IRL-SE, a uniform sampling algorithm that queries each state-action pair equally and solves the resulting linear-constrained feasible set. US-IRL-SE is shown to be minimax optimal whenever π_min^{-1} ξ_i ≤ 1 for all sub-optimal experts, i.e., precisely in the regime where sub-optimal experts provide the most useful ambiguity reduction.

## Results

The paper establishes that the sample complexity lower bound for IRL with sub-optimal experts is Ω(SA/(ε²(1−γ)²)·(log(1/δ)+S)) + Ω(q₀²S·log(1/δ)/(ε²π_min)), where q₀ = π_min^{-1}·max_i ξ_i. The proposed US-IRL-SE algorithm achieves an upper bound of Õ(max{q₁²S·log(1/δ)/(ε²π_min), q₂²SA(S+log(1/δ))/(ε²(1−γ)²)}), matching the lower bound exactly (i.e., minimax optimal) whenever π_min^{-1}ξ_i ≤ 1 for all sub-optimal experts. Theoretically, the feasible reward set volume shrinks from ∏(1−γ)^{-1} (single-expert) to ∏g(s,a) (multi-expert), where g(s,a) ≤ min{k(s,a), (1−γ)^{-1}} and k(s,a) scales with ξ_i/(d^{π_{E_i}}(s)·π_{E_i}(a|s)).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical baselines or dataset comparisons. The primary theoretical comparison is between the IRL-SE (multi sub-optimal expert) setting and the single-expert IRL setting. The paper shows that IRL-SE is always at least as statistically hard as single-expert IRL (the first lower bound term is identical), but introduces an additional complexity factor of π_min^{-1} that arises from estimating the linear constraints imposed by sub-optimal experts. The US-IRL-SE algorithm is shown to be minimax optimal in the regime where sub-optimal experts are sufficiently close to optimal (π_min^{-1}ξ_i ≤ 1), which is precisely the regime where they provide the greatest ambiguity reduction. When this condition is violated, a gap remains between the upper and lower bounds, which the authors identify as an open problem.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the feasible reward set framework introduced by Metelli et al. (2021) [24] for single-expert IRL to the setting of multiple sub-optimal experts, building on the foundational IRL formulation of Ng & Russell (2000) [26]. The statistical analysis directly generalizes the lower bounds for single-expert IRL from Metelli et al. (2023) [22] to the infinite-horizon, multi-expert setting. The key conceptual departure is showing that sub-optimal experts impose additional linear constraints on the advantage function (via the occupancy measure), which simultaneously shrinks the feasible reward set and increases the sample complexity of estimating it.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes a fundamental trade-off: sub-optimal experts reduce the intrinsic ambiguity of IRL by shrinking the feasible reward set, but at the cost of increased statistical complexity (a factor of π_min^{-1} in the sample complexity). This trade-off is unavoidable given the structure of the feasible set, and the minimax optimality of uniform sampling holds only when sub-optimal experts are sufficiently close to optimal. Open questions include closing the gap between upper and lower bounds in the regime where sub-optimal experts are far from optimal, extending to offline settings without generative model access, and scaling to large state-action spaces via feature-based reward representations.
