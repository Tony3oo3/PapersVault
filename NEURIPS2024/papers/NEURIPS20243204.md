## Problem

TAPTR, a DETR-inspired point tracker, concatenates cost-volume features into each point query's content vector to aid position prediction, but this contaminates the content feature and degrades both visibility classification and subsequent cost-volume computation. The paper asks whether the correlation information that cost-volume provides can be exploited without polluting the query's content representation, and whether a simpler, more principled mechanism can replace the explicit cost-volume pipeline.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243204/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB video frames processed in sliding windows of W frames at a time, along with per-point initial 2D coordinates and start-frame IDs. Evaluation is restricted to 256×256 resolution (training uses 512×512), and each query outputs a refined 2D position and a scalar visibility score per frame. The last frame's predicted positions and content features in a window initialize the next window, enabling online tracking over arbitrarily long videos.

## Method

TAPTRv2 replaces TAPTR's cost-volume aggregation with an Attention-based Position Update (APU) operation inside the cross-attention of each decoder layer. It uses key-aware deformable attention, which computes attention weights via explicit dot-product between the query and bilinearly sampled image features, so the weights encode true local similarity (equivalent to cost-volume). APU then treats the deformable sampling offsets as values in a second cross-attention that shares the same keys, producing a weighted position offset added to the query's coordinates. An MLP-based Disentangler splits the attention weights into separate distributions for content-feature update and position update, since the two require different weight sharpness. The architecture uses a ResNet-50 backbone, two deformable-attention encoder layers, and five decoder layers with self-attention, temporal attention, and the APU-augmented cross-attention, trained end-to-end on 11,000 Kubric synthetic videos with AdamW and EMA.

## Results

On TAP-Vid-DAVIS (First mode), TAPTRv2 achieves 63.5 AJ, 75.9 <δxavg, and 91.4 OA, setting the best AJ and OA among all compared methods. On TAP-Vid-DAVIS-S (Strided mode), it reaches 66.4 AJ and 91.3 OA. On TAP-Vid-Kinetics, TAPTRv2 obtains 49.7 AJ and 85.7 OA, trailing BootsTAP (54.7 AJ) and LocoTrack (52.9 AJ) on AJ but matching or exceeding them on OA. The consistent OA improvements across all datasets (e.g., +3.0 OA over BootsTAP on DAVIS) validate the benefit of keeping content features uncontaminated for visibility prediction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TAPTRv2 outperforms its direct predecessor TAPTR by +0.5 AJ and +0.3 OA on DAVIS, and by +0.7 AJ and +0.5 OA on Kinetics, confirming that removing cost-volume contamination and introducing APU yields consistent gains. Against CoTracker2-Single, TAPTRv2 leads by +1.3 AJ and +2.1 OA on DAVIS, and by +0.5 AJ and +1.4 OA on DAVIS-S. On DAVIS, TAPTRv2 surpasses BootsTAP by +2.1 AJ and +3.0 OA despite BootsTAP using 15M additional real-world video clips for training. However, on Kinetics, TAPTRv2 (49.7 AJ) trails both BootsTAP (54.7 AJ) and LocoTrack (52.9 AJ) on the comprehensive AJ metric, though it matches or exceeds them on OA (85.7 vs. 86.3 and 85.3 respectively). On DAVIS-S, LocoTrack achieves a higher AJ (67.8 vs. 66.4) and <δxavg (79.6 vs. 78.8), while TAPTRv2 leads on OA (91.3 vs. 89.9). Overall, TAPTRv2 achieves the best AJ and OA on DAVIS and the best OA on all three datasets, with the Kinetics AJ gap attributed to the larger training data used by concurrent methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

TAPTRv2 is a direct successor to TAPTR, which itself reframed point tracking as a DETR-style detection problem by treating each tracking point as a query with content and positional components. The key departure from TAPTR is the removal of the cost-volume aggregation block, which the authors show contaminates query content features and creates an asymmetry between queries and keys in cross-attention. Instead, TAPTRv2 introduces an attention-based position update (APU) operation that repurposes key-aware deformable attention weights (from Lite DETR) to predict positional offsets, exploiting the observation that local attention and cost-volume are both computed via dot-product between a query and surrounding features. This positions TAPTRv2 as the natural simplification of the TAPTR lineage, bringing the TAP pipeline closer in structure to standard DETR-based detection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is most visible in dense point tracking scenarios: removing cost-volume computation yields ~24% speedup and 20% fewer FLOPs when tracking 5000 points, making large-scale applications like video editing and 3D reconstruction more tractable. The ablation revealing that cost-volume's primary benefit is domain generalization rather than in-domain accuracy is a useful diagnostic for the field, suggesting that correlation-based features serve as a domain-robust signal rather than a fundamental tracking mechanism. Open questions include whether the quadratic self-attention cost (acknowledged as a limitation) can be addressed with linear-attention variants for truly dense tracking, and whether the DETR-aligned framework can be extended to multi-task settings (e.g., joint tracking and segmentation) as the authors envision.
