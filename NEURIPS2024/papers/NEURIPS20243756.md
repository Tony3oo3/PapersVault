## Problem

Personalized federated learning methods that use knowledge distillation typically require public datasets to generate soft labels, raising privacy concerns and complicating deployment. Existing prototype-based approaches like FedHKD improve client-side models but fail to substantially advance hyper-knowledge extraction or achieve simultaneous improvement of both local and global model performance under Non-IID data distributions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243756/cropped_page15_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

Each client receives the global model parameters and a global CKF (a set of per-class prototype feature vectors in a 32-dimensional latent space paired with corresponding soft prediction vectors) from the server. Clients process their private data (32×32 RGB images for CIFAR-10/100, variable-resolution street-view digits for SVHN, or movie review text for IMDB) through a feature extractor and classifier, then return updated model weights, locally computed CKF, and per-class KL divergence values. The server outputs an updated global model and global CKF that are broadcast in the next round, while each client retains a personalized local model for inference.

## Method

FedGMKD introduces two coupled mechanisms: Cluster Knowledge Fusion (CKF) and Discrepancy-Aware Aggregation (DAT). CKF applies Gaussian Mixture Models on each client's extracted features and soft predictions to compute responsibility-weighted prototype features and prototype soft predictions per class, eliminating the need for public datasets. DAT computes aggregation weights by combining an initial volume-based weight (proportion of class samples) with a quality penalty derived from the KL divergence between each client's local soft predictions and the preliminary global soft predictions, passed through a ReLU with tunable sensitivity parameters. The local training objective adds two regularization terms to cross-entropy: a feature-alignment loss (L2 distance between local features and global prototype features) and a knowledge-alignment loss (L2 distance between local classifier outputs on global prototypes and global soft predictions, scaled by a temperature). The paper provides convergence and convergence-rate theorems under standard smoothness, bounded-variance, and bounded-KL-divergence assumptions, and validates the framework with ResNet-18/50 on vision benchmarks and BERT on IMDB sentiment analysis.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 (10 clients, Dirichlet β=0.5), FedGMKD achieves 61.78% local accuracy and 49.78% global accuracy, outperforming all 8 baselines. On SVHN (10 clients), it reaches 86.26% local and 82.64% global accuracy; on CIFAR-100 (10 clients), 17.16% local and 16.97% global. With 50 clients, FedGMKD attains 65.69% local / 60.31% global on CIFAR-10 and 90.17% global on SVHN, consistently leading across all dataset–client configurations. Average per-round wall-clock time is 251.55 s (CIFAR-10, 10 clients), competitive with FedProx (126.56 s) and FjORD (252.34 s) while requiring no public dataset.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedGMKD outperforms all 8 baselines on every dataset and client-count setting. On CIFAR-10 (10 clients), it leads FedAvg by +6.03% local / +3.16% global, FedProto by +2.01% local / +0.81% global, and FPL by +0.83% local / +2.59% global. The closest competitor is FjORD, which achieves 49.18% global accuracy on CIFAR-10 (10 clients) versus FedGMKD's 49.78%, a gap of only 0.60 points; however, FedGMKD pulls ahead more decisively at 50 clients (60.31% vs. 58.74% global). On SVHN, FedGMKD's advantage over FjORD is larger: +1.13% local and +1.08% global at 10 clients, widening to +0.80% local and +0.81% global at 50 clients. FedMD, the only baseline requiring a public dataset, trails FedGMKD by 1.63% local and 1.46% global on CIFAR-10 (10 clients) while incurring 2.4× the training time (410 s vs. 252 s). On the hardest setting (CIFAR-100, 10 clients), FedGMKD gains +1.77% local and +0.04% global over FjORD, and +1.23% local / +1.60% global over FPL, confirming that the GMM-based CKF and DAT aggregation provide the largest benefits under extreme class heterogeneity.

## Relation to Prior Work

FedGMKD builds directly on FedHKD (Chen et al., 2023), which introduced data-free hyper-knowledge distillation by exchanging mean feature representations and soft predictions between clients and server. The key departures are twofold: (1) replacing simple averaging with GMM-based clustering (Cluster Knowledge Fusion) to generate more structured prototype features and soft predictions per class, and (2) introducing a Discrepancy-Aware Aggregation Technique that weights client contributions by both data quantity and quality (via KL divergence) rather than quantity alone. The paper also positions itself against prototype-based FL methods (FedProto, FPL) and KD-based FL methods (FedMD, FedGen), arguing that prior approaches either require public datasets, rely on server-side generative models, or fail to simultaneously improve both local and global model performance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FedGMKD offers a practical path toward personalized FL that avoids two common pain points: the need for public datasets (privacy risk) and server-side generative models (computational overhead). The dual improvement in both local and global accuracy—something FedHKD explicitly does not achieve—makes it more broadly applicable in settings where the global model must also generalize well. However, the computational overhead of GMM fitting per class per round, the reliance on standard convergence assumptions, and evaluation limited to CIFAR-scale datasets leave open questions about scalability to larger, more heterogeneous real-world deployments. The extension to NLP (IMDB/BERT) is encouraging but underexplored.
