## Problem

DETR-based visual grounding methods generate decoder queries via random initialization or linguistic embeddings, providing no target-related information at the start of decoding and increasing learning difficulty. Additionally, these methods rely solely on the deepest backbone features during query learning, discarding texture and fine-grained information present in lower and mid-level features that is crucial for grounding.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241501/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 640×640 RGB image and a natural language referring expression as input, processed through a frozen CLIP ViT-Base/16 (or /32) backbone. For the REC task, it outputs a single bounding box (x, y, h, w) with a binary confidence score indicating whether a target is present; for the RES extension, it additionally produces a binary segmentation mask via a MaskFormer-style head that computes dot products between mask embeddings and the multi-modal feature map.

## Method

RefFormer inserts a Query Adaptation (QA) module at layers {4,6,8,10,12} of a frozen CLIP backbone. Each QA module contains a Condition Aggregation and Multi-modal Fusion (CAMF) block that cross-attends learnable queries with both image and language features, and a Target-related Context Refinement (TR) block that further refines queries against multi-modal enhanced feature maps. The queries are progressively refined layer by layer, capturing target-related context from multi-level features and producing referential queries that serve as prior context for the decoder. The QA module also acts as a parameter-efficient adapter, residually injecting task-specific knowledge into CLIP via up-projected features modulated by learnable regulation tokens, keeping the backbone frozen. The decoder performs language-guided multi-level fusion (concatenating features from layers {4,8,12} after cross-attention with the [SOS] token) and decodes bounding boxes using the referential query plus a zero-initialized query, trained with bipartite matching and auxiliary losses at each QA stage.

## Results

On the REC task with ViT-Base/16 backbone (single dataset), RefFormer achieves 86.52/90.24/81.42% (val/testA/testB) on RefCOCO, 76.58/83.69/67.38% on RefCOCO+, and 77.80/77.60% (val/test) on RefCOCOg. With ViT-Large and combined datasets, it reaches 90.91/93.69/86.56% on RefCOCO and 84.97/84.88% on RefCOCOg. On Flickr30K and ReferItGame, the method scores 82.10% and 82.18% respectively. For the RES task, it achieves 66.26% mIoU on RefCOCOg test, outperforming VG-LAW (65.13%) and PVD (63.62%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RefFormer (ViT-Base/16) outperforms Dynamic MDETR, which shares the same backbone, by +1.90% on RefCOCO testA, +2.44% on RefCOCO+ testA, and +4.94% on RefCOCOg val. Against TransVG++ (also ViT-Base/16), it gains +1.87% on RefCOCO testA and +3.24% on RefCOCO+ testA, though it trails VG-LAW by 1.45% on RefCOCO testB (81.42 vs. 82.87). On the harder RefCOCO+ testB split, the improvement over Dynamic MDETR is the largest at +6.21%, indicating the referential query is particularly effective for attribute-rich expressions. On Flickr30K and ReferItGame, RefFormer achieves 82.10% and 82.18%, surpassing the previous best (QRNeT at 81.95% and VG-LAW at 76.60% respectively). For the RES task, RefFormer leads VG-LAW by +1.13% mIoU on RefCOCOg test (66.26 vs. 65.13) without any task-specific design beyond a standard segmentation head.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the DETR-like visual grounding paradigm (TransVG, RefTR, MDETR, Dynamic MDETR), which predicts bounding boxes from learnable queries via bipartite matching. However, it departs from the dominant trend of strengthening the multi-modal decoder by instead focusing on the query generation stage, drawing an analogy to the DETR variants literature (Anchor DETR, DAB-DETR, DN-DETR) where query design has proven critical for object detection. A key architectural departure is the use of a frozen CLIP backbone with a lightweight Query Adaptation (QA) module inserted at multiple layers, acting as a parameter-efficient adapter that progressively refines target-related context from multi-level features, rather than fine-tuning the entire backbone as prior CLIP-based grounding methods do.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that the vanilla query initialization (random or linguistic embeddings) is a meaningful bottleneck in DETR-like visual grounding, and that a simple iterative query adaptation mechanism can close much of this gap while keeping the backbone frozen. This adapter-style approach reduces training cost and preserves CLIP's pre-trained vision-language alignment, making it attractive for practitioners who want strong grounding without full fine-tuning. The authors acknowledge that RES performance lags behind REC, suggesting the method has not yet been fully optimized for segmentation, and the moderate 1–3% gains over strong transformer-based baselines indicate the field still has room for more substantial architectural innovations in the query-decoder interaction.
