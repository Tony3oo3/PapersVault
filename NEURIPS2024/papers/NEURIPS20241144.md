## Problem

Existing biological datasets for taxonomic classification are predominantly image-only, limited in geographic scope, and lack the multimodal annotations (DNA barcodes, hierarchical taxonomic labels, geographic coordinates) needed for realistic biodiversity monitoring. BIOSCAN-1M, the closest predecessor, covered only 3 countries, the Diptera order, and image classification at the family level without exploiting its DNA barcode data. This paper addresses these gaps by releasing a 5× larger multimodal dataset and establishing benchmark tasks that evaluate models in both closed-world (known species) and open-world (novel species) settings.

## Input / Output

The dataset provides three primary input modalities per specimen: a 648 bp COI DNA barcode sequence (truncated at 660 nucleotides, tokenized via non-overlapping k-mers), a high-resolution RGB image captured at 1024×768 pixels on a Keyence VHX-7000 microscope (cropped and resized to 341×256 via a fine-tuned DETR model), and a 7-rank Linnaean taxonomic label string (phylum through species). Outputs span three benchmark tasks: taxonomic class predictions at species/genus/family/order ranks, cluster assignments from zero-shot agglomerative clustering of pretrained embeddings, and shared embedding vectors enabling cross-modal retrieval (e.g., image query → DNA key). The dataset partitions samples into 'seen' (established species names) and 'unseen' (placeholder species names) sets to support both closed- and open-world evaluation.

## Method

The paper's technical contribution centers on three benchmark experiments over the BIOSCAN-5M dataset. First, a BarcodeBERT-style masked language model (transformer with k-mer tokenization) is pretrained via MLM on 2.3M DNA barcodes, then fine-tuned for species-level classification; the best 8-4-4 architecture (k=8, 4 layers, 4 heads) achieves 99.28% fine-tuned accuracy and 47.03% 1NN-genus probing on unseen species, substantially outperforming general-purpose DNA foundation models (DNABERT-2, DNABERT-S, NT, HyenaDNA) on the open-world task. Second, zero-shot clustering evaluates whether off-the-shelf pretrained encoders (DINO, VICReg, MoCo-v3 for images; BarcodeBERT, HyenaDNA, DNABERT-S for DNA) can group unseen specimens taxonomically without any task-specific training, using UMAP dimensionality reduction followed by Ward's agglomerative clustering and AMI evaluation. Third, a CLIBD-style contrastive learning pipeline (NT-Xent loss) jointly fine-tunes a ViT-B image encoder, BarcodeBERT DNA encoder, and BERT-small text encoder into a shared embedding space, enabling cross-modal taxonomic retrieval via FAISS exact search; training on the full 5M records consistently outperforms a 1M subset across all taxonomic ranks.

## Results

On the DNA-based taxonomic classification benchmark (Table 4), the proposed model (8-4-4 architecture, pretrained on 7B tokens of BIOSCAN-5M DNA barcodes) achieves 99.28% fine-tuned species-level accuracy on seen species and 47.03% genus-level 1NN-probing accuracy on unseen species, outperforming all six baselines on both metrics. For linear probing, DNABERT-S leads with 95.50% versus the proposed model's 94.47%. In the multimodal retrieval task (Table 5), training CLIBD on the full 5M records with all three modalities (image, DNA, text) yields 97.0% DNA-to-DNA and 67.1% Image-to-Image species-level macro accuracy, a substantial improvement over the unaligned baseline (79.8% and 16.6% respectively).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed model (8-4-4) wins on fine-tuned species-level accuracy (99.28%) against all six baselines, with DNABERT-2 (99.23%) being the closest competitor. On the critical open-world 1NN-probe task (assigning unseen species to seen genera), the proposed model achieves 47.03%, nearly doubling the next-best BarcodeBERT (23.15%) and far exceeding the CNN baseline (29.88%), NT (21.67%), DNABERT-2 (17.99%), DNABERT-S (17.70%), and HyenaDNA (19.26%). The sole metric where the proposed model does not lead is linear probing, where DNABERT-S achieves 95.50% versus 94.47%, though both substantially outperform the multi-species pretrained models (NT: 52.41%, DNABERT-2: 67.81%, HyenaDNA: 54.82%). BarcodeBERT, the closest architectural predecessor trained on only 5B barcode tokens, is outperformed across all three metrics, confirming the benefit of the larger BIOSCAN-5M pretraining corpus. The CNN baseline from Badirli et al. (2021) and the state-space model HyenaDNA (Nguyen et al., 2024) are consistently the weakest performers, with HyenaDNA's 128-dim output space likely limiting its representational capacity for fine-grained taxonomic discrimination.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper is a direct expansion of BIOSCAN-1M (Gharaee et al., 2023), scaling from 1.1M to 5.15M arthropod specimens and adding geographic metadata, broader taxonomic coverage, and data cleaning. Methodologically, it repurposes three existing frameworks: BarcodeBERT's masked language model architecture for DNA pretraining, Lowe et al.'s zero-shot clustering protocol extended to DNA barcodes, and CLIBD's three-modality contrastive learning for shared embedding alignment. The paper does not propose new architectures or losses but instead positions BIOSCAN-5M as a large-scale multimodal testbed to evaluate how pretraining data scale and modality combination affect fine-grained taxonomic classification and open-world generalization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

BIOSCAN-5M is the largest multimodal (DNA + image + text) arthropod dataset to date, and its explicit open-world partitions (unseen species, heldout species) address a practically critical gap: real biodiversity monitoring must handle novel species, not just classify known ones. The finding that a model pretrained on 7B barcode tokens achieves 47% genus-level 1NN-probing accuracy—roughly double the next-best model—suggests that domain-specific pretraining data scale matters more than architectural sophistication for open-world taxonomic generalization. Key limitations include sampling bias from collection locations, severe label sparsity below family rank (only 9.2% species-labelled), and the absence of error bars or multi-seed runs, which limits confidence in the magnitude of reported gaps.
