## Problem

Visual-based RL agents using image reconstruction as an auxiliary task tend to overfit to task-irrelevant features (backgrounds, colors, textures), undermining zero-shot generalization to unseen environments. Prior work largely avoided reconstruction loss for this reason, leaving the well-established benefits of reconstruction for representation learning untapped in the generalization setting. The paper asks whether reconstruction can be harnessed for generalization if task-relevant and task-irrelevant features are explicitly separated.

## Input / Output

The method consumes three stacked RGB frames (9×84×84) and an action vector, producing two disentangled latent codes: a task-relevant representation z⁺ (used by the policy and Q-network) and a task-irrelevant representation z⁻. The foreground branch additionally outputs a soft attribution mask M ∈ (0,1) and a reconstructed foreground image, while the background branch outputs a reconstructed background image; the full observation is recovered via o′ = o⁺⊙M + o⁻⊙(1−M). The attribution mask defines the spatial region the agent 'focuses on' and is the key intermediate that drives both the attribution augmentation and the foreground consistency loss.

## Method

SMG introduces a separated-models architecture with a foreground encoder f⁺ and a background encoder f⁻ that jointly reconstruct the observation through a mask-gated combination, circumventing the overfitting risk of a single autoencoder. Five auxiliary losses shape the representations: a cooperative reconstruction loss, a mask-ratio loss acting as an information bottleneck on the foreground proportion, a background reconstruction loss supervised by a novel attribution augmentation (replacing background pixels with random images to simulate video-background deployment), a Bellman Q-value loss, and an empowerment loss via an inverse dynamics model. For generalization, two consistency losses align the agent's focus across augmented views: a foreground consistency loss that matches the predicted attribution under augmented observations to the raw-observation attribution (with stop-gradient), and a Q-value consistency loss that regularizes Q-values under augmented observations toward those under raw observations. The entire module is plug-and-play, integrating with SAC's actor-critic updates without modifying the base algorithm's policy or value network structure.

## Results

On the most challenging DMC-GB video-hard setting (random video backgrounds with removed floor/shadow), SMG achieves state-of-theart zero-shot generalization across all five DMControl tasks, with episode returns of 764 (cartpole-swingup), 910 (finger-spin), 955 (walker-stand), 814 (walker-walk), and 303 (cheetah-run), outperforming the second-best method (SGQN) by 165–180 points in four of five tasks. On the robotic manipulation peg-in-box task, SMG achieves an average return of 234±7 across six evaluation settings, a 102% improvement over the second-best method (SRM at 116±69), while maintaining the lowest standard deviation of all methods. In the reach task, SMG achieves 29±2 average return, a 38% improvement over SGQN (21±17).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SMG outperforms all six baselines (SAC, DrQ, SODA, SVEA, SRM, SGQN) in every task across both video-easy and video-hard settings. The most dramatic gains appear in video-hard: SMG beats SGQN (the previous SOTA) by 165–200 points in four of five tasks (e.g., 910 vs 710 on finger-spin, 814 vs 634 on walker-walk), and by 168 points on cheetah-run (303 vs 135), a 124% relative improvement. In the robotic manipulation peg-in-box task, SMG achieves 234±7 average return versus 116±69 for SRM (second-best) and 66±143 for SGQN, with notably lower variance indicating superior stability. On the reach task, SMG (29±2) edges out SGQN (21±17) by 38% on average. The only settings where SMG does not lead are a few color-easy/color-hard DMControl tasks where it trails SRM or SVEA by 2–3%, suggesting its reconstruction-based approach is most advantageous under severe distribution shifts (video backgrounds) rather than mild color perturbations.

## Relation to Prior Work

SMG builds on SAC as its base off-policy algorithm and incorporates the empowerment objective from Mohamed & Rezende (2015) to enforce control-relevance in the task-relevant representation. Conceptually, it departs from the dominant generalization paradigm of data augmentation (DrQ, SODA, SVEA, SRM) and saliency-guided encoding (SGQN) by reintroducing image reconstruction—a loss previously avoided in generalization settings due to overfitting concerns—through a separated foreground/background architecture inspired by TIA and Denoised MDPs. The key positioning is that reconstruction becomes beneficial for generalization (rather than harmful) when the model is explicitly structured to isolate task-relevant from task-irrelevant features and when consistency losses anchor the agent's focus across augmented views.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that reconstruction-based auxiliary tasks, long considered counterproductive for generalization, can be harnessed effectively with the right architectural separation, offering a new design principle for visual RL. The particularly large and stable gains in video-background settings and robotic manipulation (std of 7 vs. 100+ for baselines in peg-in-box) suggest practical relevance for real-world deployment where backgrounds are uncontrolled. A noted limitation is that performance degrades when many task-relevant objects coexist (e.g., crowded navigation scenes), and the method's reliance on a hand-tuned mask ratio ρ and the bootstrapping quality of early mask predictions remain open practical concerns. Future directions include more advanced backbones, non-static camera viewpoints, and validation on physical robots.
