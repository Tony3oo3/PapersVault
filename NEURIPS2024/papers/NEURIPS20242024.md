## Problem

Despite the prevalence of sparse per-example gradients in large embedding models (e.g., recommendation systems, NLP), the theoretical limits of differentially private optimization under gradient sparsity were largely unexplored. Without sparsity, DP optimization rates are polynomial in the ambient dimension d, making high-dimensional problems intractable; this paper establishes that gradient sparsity (at most s nonzero coordinates per gradient) enables nearly dimension-independent rates, and provides matching upper and lower bounds to characterize the optimal tradeoffs.

## Input / Output

For mean estimation, the input is a dataset of n vectors each s-sparse with bounded ℓ₂-norm L, along with privacy parameters (ε, δ); the output is an estimated mean vector with ℓ₂-error guarantees. For optimization, the input is a loss function f(x,z) whose per-example gradients are s-sparse, a convex domain X ⊆ ℝᵈ, and privacy parameters; the output is either an optimized parameter vector (convex case) or a near-stationary point with small empirical gradient norm (nonconvex case). A key structural constraint is that sparsity is per-example (each individual gradient has at most s nonzero entries), not a global sparsity pattern, and the algorithms require prior knowledge of s.

## Method

The paper introduces three algorithmic families. First, a projection mechanism that adds Laplace/Gaussian noise to the empirical mean and projects onto the ℓ₁-ball (a convex relaxation of the sparse set), yielding near-optimal pure- and approximate-DP mean estimation rates; a sharper approximate-DP variant uses compressed sensing to recover a noisy random projection of the mean, combined with the Approximate Carathéodory Theorem. Second, a subsampled bias-reduced gradient estimator that draws batch sizes from a truncated geometric distribution, applies the projection mechanism at multiple scales, and forms a telescoping difference to cancel bias while keeping variance bounded—this oracle is plugged into SGD with fully-adaptive composition for privacy accounting under random stopping. Third, a regularized output perturbation algorithm that adds noise to the regularized ERM solution and applies an ℓ∞-projection post-processing step, exploiting noise concentration in high dimensions together with convexity and gradient sparsity to achieve nearly optimal excess-risk rates. Lower bounds are established via a novel block-diagonal bootstrapping construction that amplifies low-dimensional sparse packings (pure-DP) or fingerprinting codes (approximate-DP) into high-dimensional hard instances.

## Results

For DP mean estimation with sparse data, the paper establishes near-tight upper and lower bounds across three regimes: constant error for small n, a high-dimensional rate of (s ln(d/s) ln(1/δ))^{1/4}/√(εn) for intermediate n, and √(d ln(1/δ))/(εn) for large n (approximate-DP). For DP-ERM and DP-SCO with sparse gradients, the convex excess risk rate improves from the non-sparse R_{ε,δ} = √(d ln(1/δ))/(εn) to (s ln(d) ln(1/δ))^{1/4}/√(εn), which is nearly dimension-independent when s ≪ d. In the nonconvex setting, the empirical gradient norm bound improves from (R_{ε,δ})^{2/3} to (s ln(d/s) ln³(1/δ))^{1/8}/(εn)^{1/4}, again achieving near dimension-independent rates. The pure-DP convex rate of (s ln(d)/(εn))^{1/3} represents the first nearly dimension-independent rate for this problem.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares its sparse-gradient rates against three non-sparse baselines. Against Bassily et al. (FOCS 2014) for ERM, the sparse rate (s ln d · ln(1/δ))^{1/4}/√(εn) strictly improves upon the non-sparse R_{ε,δ} = √(d ln(1/δ))/(εn) whenever s ≪ d, reducing the dimension dependence from d^{1/2} to s^{1/4}. Against Bassily et al. (NeurIPS 2019) for SCO, the same improvement holds with an additional 1/√n statistical term. Against Arora et al. (ICML 2023) for nonconvex stationarity, the sparse rate (s ln(d/s) · ln³(1/δ))^{1/8}/(εn)^{1/4} improves upon the non-sparse (R_{ε,δ})^{2/3} = (d ln(1/δ))^{1/3}/(εn)^{2/3}, again trading the full dimension d for the sparsity parameter s. The lower bounds (via block-diagonal constructions) confirm these upper bounds are near-optimal, with only a ln(d/s)^{1/4} gap remaining in the approximate-DP high-dimensional regime.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the projection mechanism of Nikolov et al. (STOC 2013) to the sparse-data regime via a convex relaxation onto the ℓ₁-ball, and adapts the multi-level randomization bias-reduction technique of Blanchet & Glynn (WSC 2015) to the DP setting with a truncated geometric batch-size schedule. The closest prior work is Zhang et al. (2021), which studied approximate-DP ERM under a stronger partitioning-sparsity assumption plus an ℓ∞ gradient bound; this paper weakens the assumption to individual gradient sparsity and provides near-optimal rates in both convex and nonconvex settings. For lower bounds, the paper introduces a novel block-diagonal bootstrapping construction that composes sparse packing arguments (pure-DP) or fingerprinting codes (approximate-DP) across blocks, a technique not present in prior DP mean estimation lower bound literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes that gradient sparsity fundamentally changes the dimension dependence of DP optimization, yielding nearly dimension-independent rates that are impossible without sparsity—this provides theoretical justification for the practical success of DP training on large embedding models in industry. The bias-reduction technique, which amortizes estimation error within an iterative method via random batch sizes and telescoping estimators, is flagged as potentially of broader interest beyond the sparse setting. Key open questions include whether the compressed-sensing-based approximate-DP upper bound is tight (a log(d/s)^{1/4} gap remains), whether the nonconvex stationarity rate is optimal, and whether the rates are attainable without prior knowledge of the sparsity parameter s.
