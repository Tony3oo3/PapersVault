## Problem

Universal segmentation models achieve strong pixel-level understanding but lack reasoning and text-instruction controllability, while MLLMs offer powerful vision-language reasoning but cannot produce pixel-level outputs or accept visual prompts. Prior attempts to unify these capabilities either stack multiple visual encoders and decoders (e.g., GLaMM uses two encoders) or sacrifice image-level abilities like captioning when adding grounding. The paper asks whether image-level, object-level, and pixel-level reasoning can be bridged in a single MLLM with only one visual encoder, one LLM, and one decoder.

## Input / Output

The model accepts a 1024×1024 RGB image, a free-form text instruction, and optional visual prompts (point, box, or mask) rendered as attention-mask constraints on the OMG decoder's cross-attention layers. The image is downsampled to 256 pixel-centric visual tokens (64× reduction via pixel shuffle) plus a variable number of object-centric tokens from foreground queries. Output is a token sequence where a special [SEG] token is decoded by the frozen OMG decoder into a segmentation mask, while <Region> tokens in the input allow inserting object-centric tokens from user-provided visual prompts for region-level conversation.

## Method

OMG-LLaVA freezes the entire OMG-Seg perception module (ConvNeXt-L CLIP encoder + masked cross-attention OMG decoder) and connects it to an InternLM2-7B LLM via two MLP projectors. The key architectural contribution is a perception prior embedding that fuses object queries into pixel features using softmax-weighted segmentation masks (MS = Softmax(M⊙S)), so each pixel-centric token carries object identity information the LLM can reason over; foreground object queries are additionally passed as separate object-centric tokens. For visual prompts, box and mask inputs are encoded as attention-mask constraints on the OMG decoder's cross-attention rather than crude point conversions, preserving spatial intent. Training is two-stage: pretraining aligns projectors with a regularization loss that preserves object-centric geometry, then instruction tuning fine-tunes the LLM via LoRA while supervising [SEG] outputs with cross-entropy plus Dice loss on decoded masks, all under a unified instruction template covering captioning, conversation, referring segmentation, reasoning segmentation, and grounded conversation generation.

## Results

OMG-LLaVA achieves 78.0 cIoU on refCOCO, 69.1 METEOR on grounded conversation generation (GCG), and 29.9 METEOR on refCOCOg(C) region captioning, while uniquely retaining universal segmentation capability (53.8 PQ on COCO, 49.8 VPQ on VIPSeg) that no other MLLM provides. On image-level benchmarks trained only with LLaVA data, OMG-LLaVA scores 68.9 on SEED-Bench, 89.7 on POPE, and 61.7 on AI2D, outperforming LLaVA-1.5 on three of five benchmarks. The model uses a single visual encoder (ConvNeXt-L CLIP) and a single LLM (InternLM2-7B), achieving these results with a frozen perception module throughout training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against LISA (2 encoders, SAM-based), OMG-LLaVA wins by 3.9 cIoU on refCOCO (78.0 vs. 74.1), 6.7 METEOR on GCG (69.1 vs. 62.4), 4.7 AP50 on GCG (14.9 vs. 13.0), and 4.7 METEOR on region caption (29.9 vs. 25.2), while using only one visual encoder. Against GLaMM (the strongest baseline, which used the much larger GranD dataset for pretraining), OMG-LLaVA is slightly behind on refCOCO (78.0 vs. 79.5) and GCG METEOR (69.1 vs. 72.6), but matches or exceeds it on GCG AP50 (14.9 vs. 15.2) and region caption (29.9 vs. 28.9), while uniquely retaining universal segmentation (COCO PQ 53.8, VIPSeg VPQ 49.8) that GLaMM cannot perform. Against PixelLM, LaSagnA, GSVA, AnyRef, and NeXT-Chat, OMG-LLaVA consistently outperforms on refCOCO cIoU (by 5.0, 1.2, 1.6, 3.9, and 3.3 points respectively) and GCG METEOR (by 2.8, 2.7, 4.6, 5.0, and 4.0 points). On image-level benchmarks, OMG-LLaVA trained only with LLaVA data surpasses LLaVA-1.5 on SEED-Bench (68.9 vs. 65.9), POPE (89.7 vs. 86.7), and AI2D (61.7 vs. 56.6), while co-trained OMG-LLaVA dramatically outperforms LISA, PixelLM, LaSagnA, and GLaMM on all image-level benchmarks (e.g., MMBench 47.9 vs. 0.4, 17.4, 0.0, 36.8 respectively), demonstrating that the unified architecture does not sacrifice image-level reasoning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on two complementary lines of work: the LLaVA paradigm of projecting visual tokens into an LLM for multimodal understanding, and the OMG-Seg universal segmentation model that unifies image, video, and interactive segmentation in a single architecture. The key departure from prior MLLMs (LISA, GLaMM, PixelLM) is the use of a frozen universal segmentation decoder as the visual encoder, combined with a proposed perception prior embedding that fuses object queries into pixel features via segmentation masks. Unlike prior approaches that either lose image-level capabilities when adding pixel-level ones or require multiple visual encoders and decoders, OMG-LLaVA achieves all three levels (image, object, pixel) with a single encoder, single LLM, and single decoder.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a single elegant architecture can unify referring segmentation, grounded conversation generation, image captioning, and universal segmentation without the system complexity of multi-encoder MLLMs, offering a practical and scalable design principle for future multimodal models. However, co-training with segmentation data causes a notable drop in image-level benchmark performance (e.g., MME drops from 1448 to 1177), and the frozen perception module limits the ceiling for part-level segmentation. Open questions include whether the perception prior embedding generalizes to other perception backbones, whether unfreezing the decoder during instruction tuning (which helps RES but may hurt generalization) can be better balanced, and whether this single-encoder paradigm scales to video or 3D understanding.
