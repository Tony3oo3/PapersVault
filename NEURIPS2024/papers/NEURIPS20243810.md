## Problem

LLM agents in unfamiliar domains (e.g., web navigation) suffer from low success rates because the standard in-context learning paradigm—providing raw trajectory demonstrations—hits context-length limits, prompt-order sensitivity, and complex-reasoning bottlenecks. The paper asks how to distill actionable, domain-specific knowledge from offline experiences into a form that is both concise enough to fit in a prompt and selective enough to avoid confusing the agent with irrelevant information.

## Input / Output

During the offline extraction phase, the method consumes pairs of contrastive trajectories (one successful, one failed) from the same task, where each trajectory is a sequence of observations (text or text+image), actions, and rewards. It produces a dictionary mapping natural-language context descriptions (e.g., "On the Reddit main page") to sets of conditional guideline sentences (e.g., "When on the Reddit main page, if you want to navigate to a specific forum, click 'Forums' above 'Wiki'"). At test time, the agent's current partial trajectory is fed to the context identification module, and the retrieved top-k guidelines are appended to the action-generation prompt alongside the context string.

## Method

AutoGuide extracts context-aware guidelines by contrasting trajectory pairs at their first deviation timestep: a context identification module summarizes the shared prefix into a concise state description, and a guideline extraction module compares the divergent actions to produce a conditional natural-language rule tied to that context. Guidelines are organized in a dictionary keyed by context, with an LLM-based matching step to merge semantically equivalent contexts and reduce redundancy. At inference, the agent's current trajectory is mapped to a context, the corresponding guideline set is retrieved, and a selection module picks the top-k most relevant guidelines for the specific trajectory before the LLM policy generates the next action. The key differentiator from prior work (e.g., ExpeL) is this two-stage context-conditional retrieval: rather than dumping all extracted guidelines into every prompt, AutoGuide filters to only those applicable to the agent's current state, which is critical in complex environments with large guideline pools. The framework is modality-agnostic, supporting both text-only (ALFWorld, WebShop, WebArena) and multi-modal image+text (real-world websites via GPT-4V) observations.

## Results

On ALFWorld, AUTOGUIDE achieves 79.1% success rate and 73.4 reward, substantially outperforming ReAct (54.5% SR, 66.4 reward) and ExpeL (59.0% SR, 60.9 reward). On WebShop, AUTOGUIDE reaches 46% success rate versus 30% for ReAct and 35% for ExpeL. On the more complex WebArena-Reddit benchmark, AUTOGUIDE achieves 47.1% success rate compared to 8.0% for ReAct and 21.8% for ExpeL. When combined with Reflexion, AUTOGUIDE further improves to 88.1% SR on ALFWorld and 57% SR on WebShop, the best results across all settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AUTOGUIDE consistently outperforms ReAct [23] across all three primary benchmarks: by +24.6 pp on ALFWorld (79.1% vs 54.5%), +16 pp on WebShop (46% vs 30%), and +39.1 pp on WebArena (47.1% vs 8.0%). Against ExpeL [31], which extracts guidelines but provides them all without context filtering, AUTOGUIDE gains +20.1 pp on ALFWorld, +11 pp on WebShop, and +25.3 pp on WebArena, with the gap widening in more complex environments where irrelevant guidelines cause greater confusion. When combined with Reflexion [27] for intra-task self-feedback, AUTOGUIDE+Reflexion achieves the best performance (88.1% ALFWorld, 57% WebShop), surpassing both ReAct+Reflexion (67.2%, 51%) and ExpeL+Reflexion (71.6%, 42%), confirming that context-aware inter-task knowledge complements intra-task feedback. On real-world multi-modal web tasks (Table 2), AUTOGUIDE with GPT-4V dramatically outperforms the SoM [36] baseline: 19/30 vs 2/30 on GitHub, 9/20 vs 5/20 on Google Flights, and 14/20 vs 1/20 on Coursera.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends ExpeL (Zhao et al., 2023), which also extracts natural language guidelines from offline trajectories for LLM agents, by introducing a critical distinction: guidelines are conditioned on specific contexts and only contextually relevant ones are retrieved at test time, rather than providing all guidelines indiscriminately. The framework also builds on the broader LLM agent paradigm (ReAct, Reflexion) and repurposes the contrastive-trajectory idea from offline RL into a prompt-engineering pipeline that compresses trajectory knowledge into concise conditional statements. Methodologically, it sits at the intersection of retrieval-augmented generation (selecting relevant guidelines via context matching) and knowledge distillation (summarizing full trajectories into short natural language rules).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck for LLM agents in unfamiliar domains: in-context learning with raw demonstrations is limited by context length, prompt sensitivity, and the inability to selectively apply knowledge. The context-aware selection mechanism is a simple but effective design choice that scales better to complex environments (WebArena) where the number of relevant guidelines grows. Key limitations include dependence on offline data diversity, lack of a standardized metric for guideline quality, and reliance on GPT models for all pipeline components. Open questions include whether guidelines can be iteratively collected through continual learning and how to formally quantify the quality of generated contexts and guidelines for optimization.
