## Problem

Detecting LLM-generated text from closed-source (black-box) models like GPT-4 and Claude3 is difficult because zero-shot detectors require access to internal log probabilities, while supervised detectors generalize poorly across domains. The core challenge is extracting deep intrinsic characteristics of the generative model from text alone, without model internals, while eliminating the confounding influence of diverse prompts on detection.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240516/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single candidate text (up to 32K tokens) as input and outputs a binary label indicating whether the text is LLM-generated or human-written. Internally, the candidate text is first passed to an auxiliary LLM (ChatGPT or Vicuna-7b) for prompt reconstruction and text regeneration, then both the original and regenerated texts are encoded by gte-Qwen1.5-7B-instruct into 4096-dimensional embeddings. The concatenated 8192-dimensional feature vector is fed to a 3-layer fully connected classifier (1024→512→2 with ReLU) trained with binary cross-entropy loss.

## Method

DPIC's key insight is that LLM-generated text is a coupling of the prompt (topic/semantics) and the model's intrinsic characteristics, and detection should focus on the latter. The decoupling process uses an auxiliary LLM to reconstruct the prompt from the candidate text (framed as question generation), then regenerates text conditioned on that reconstructed prompt, aligning both texts at the semantic level. A Siamese Network with a frozen gte-Qwen1.5-7B-instruct encoder produces embeddings for the candidate and regenerated texts, which are concatenated (rather than combined via distance or dot product) and classified by a trainable 3-layer FC head. Unlike DNA-GPT, which performs truncated completion without prompt guidance and uses zero-shot divergence scoring, DPIC uses full prompt-guided regeneration with supervised learning, yielding both higher detection quality and better cross-domain generalizability.

## Results

DPIC achieves average AUROC of 96.34%, 97.34%, and 98.78% when detecting ChatGPT, GPT-4, and Claude3-generated texts respectively (using ChatGPT as auxiliary LLM). With the open-source Vicuna-7b auxiliary model, DPIC still reaches 95.58%, 96.74%, and 98.75% average AUROC on the same three source models. Compared to the strongest open-source baseline Fast-DetectGPT (96.15%, 90.58%, 95.87%), DPIC delivers a 6.76% average improvement on GPT-4 and 2.91% on Claude3. Under DIPPER and back-translation attacks, DPIC retains near-perfect AUROC (≥99.31%) while most baselines degrade substantially.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DPIC outperforms all nine baselines across every source model and dataset. Against the strongest zero-shot baseline Fast-DetectGPT, DPIC (ChatGPT auxiliary) gains 6.76% average AUROC on GPT-4 (97.34% vs. 90.58%) and 2.91% on Claude3 (98.78% vs. 95.87%), while matching it closely on ChatGPT (96.34% vs. 96.15%). The supervised baseline RADAR, the best among supervised methods, trails DPIC by 7.55% on GPT-4 (88.51% vs. 97.34%) and 7.39% on Claude3 (91.39% vs. 98.78%), and shows severe domain sensitivity (e.g., 99.72% on XSum but only 73.72% on PubMed for ChatGPT). Zero-shot methods relying on log-probability features (Likelihood, LogRank, LRR, DetectGPT, NPR) all degrade sharply on GPT-4, with DetectGPT falling below 0.5 AUROC (0.4542), indicating near-random performance. DNA-GPT variants, which use truncated completion without prompt guidance, achieve only 55–82% average AUROC, confirming that prompt-aligned regeneration is critical. Notably, Entropy-based detection performs worse than random guessing (<0.5 AUROC) on all three models, highlighting that token-level entropy is an unreliable signal for modern LLMs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by synthesizing ideas from two previously separate detection paradigms: zero-shot methods (DetectGPT, Fast-DetectGPT, DNA-GPT) that extract intrinsic model characteristics but require white-box access, and supervised classifiers (RADAR, RoBERTa-based) that work with text only but suffer from domain-specific degradation. The key conceptual departure is the 'decoupling' insight — treating generated text as a coupling of prompt and intrinsic characteristics, then using an auxiliary LLM to reconstruct the prompt (repurposing the question-generation task) and regenerate text, so that a Siamese network can compare candidate and regenerated texts under aligned semantic conditions. This effectively gives a supervised detector the prompt-awareness that zero-shot methods get from model internals, without requiring access to those internals.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method addresses a practically critical gap: high-quality detection of text from closed-source models (GPT-4, Claude3) where log-probabilities and internal states are inaccessible. The impressive robustness against paraphrasing (DIPPER) and back-translation attacks — maintaining ~99.7%+ AUROC — suggests the decoupled intrinsic signal is genuinely harder to spoof than surface-level features. However, the regeneration step introduces significant computational overhead (two LLM calls per text), and the reliance on a trained classifier (even if small) means the method is not truly zero-shot. Open questions include performance on multi-turn dialogues, non-English text, and whether the approach degrades when the auxiliary model is substantially weaker than the source model.
