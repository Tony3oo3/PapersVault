## Problem

Matrix denoising with doubly heteroscedastic noise (correlated in both rows and columns) lacks a complete characterization of information-theoretic limits and optimal algorithms. Existing approaches based on whitening and singular value shrinkage are provably suboptimal, and most prior work has been restricted to one-sided heteroscedasticity where only one noise covariance is non-trivial. This paper closes the gap by establishing exact asymptotic MMSE and designing a spectral estimator with rigorous optimality guarantees for the fully doubly heteroscedastic setting.

## Input / Output

The method takes a noisy observation A ∈ R^{n×d} generated as A = (λ/n)u* v*ᵀ + Ξ^{1/2} W̃ Σ^{1/2}, along with the known noise covariance matrices Ξ ∈ R^{n×n} and Σ ∈ R^{d×d} (deterministic, strictly positive definite, with bounded operator norms and converging empirical spectral distributions) and the known SNR λ. It produces unit-norm estimates û ∈ R^n and v̂ ∈ R^d of the true singular vectors, with asymptotic correlations η_u and η_v given by closed-form expressions involving the fixed-point solution of a two-dimensional system. The analysis also yields the exact asymptotic MMSE for estimating the whitened signals Ξ^{-1/2}u* and Σ^{-1/2}v*, which characterizes the fundamental estimation error.

## Method

The spectral estimator is derived from a novel Bayes-AMP algorithm that operates on Ξ^{-1}AΣ^{-1} rather than the standard whitened matrix Ξ^{-1/2}AΣ^{-1/2}; this choice is critical because it makes the AMP fixed point coincide with the information-theoretically optimal fixed point (4.5), whereas whitening yields a strictly suboptimal weak recovery threshold. Concretely, the estimator pre-processes A via A* = λ(λ(μ*+b*)I_n + Ξ)^{-1/2} Ξ^{-1/2} A Σ^{-1/2}(λ(ν*+c*)I_d + Σ)^{-1/2}, takes the top singular vectors of A*, and applies a covariance-dependent rescaling to recover û and v̂. The information-theoretic limits are established via an interpolation argument on the free energy, where the noise covariances are absorbed into modified priors on the whitened signals, reducing the matrix problem to two decoupled Gaussian channels. Under the criticality condition (5.1), the estimator achieves the optimal weak recovery threshold, and when one covariance is identity (one-sided heteroscedasticity), it attains the Bayes-optimal MMSE for the corresponding singular vector.

## Results

The paper establishes that the proposed spectral estimator achieves asymptotic normalized correlation η_u = √(λμ*/(λμ*+1)) with u* and η_v = √(λν*/(λν*+1)) with v*, yielding matrix MSE 1 − η_u²η_v² for u*v*ᵀ (Theorem 5.1). The weak recovery threshold is characterized by the condition αγ²E[Σ̄⁻²]E[Ξ̄⁻²] > 1, below which no estimator can achieve non-trivial error (Proposition 4.1, Corollary 4.3). For one-sided heteroscedasticity (Ξ = I_n), the estimator is provably Bayes-optimal, achieving the exact MMSE for u*u*ᵀ (Corollary 5.2). Numerical experiments (n=4000, d=2000, δ=2) confirm that the theoretical predictions match simulations closely across all SNR values tested.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed spectral estimator outperforms all five baselines—Leeb-Romanov [44], Leeb [45], OptShrink [59], ScreeNOT [27], and HeteroPCA [76]—in both one-sided (Figure 2) and doubly heteroscedastic (Figure 3) settings, with the advantage being most pronounced at low SNR. Leeb-Romanov and Leeb (whitening-based methods) are the closest competitors, achieving comparable performance only at high SNR, while HeteroPCA, OptShrink, and ScreeNOT require substantially larger SNR (greater than 2 and 3, respectively) to perform comparably. The paper further proves that whitening-based approaches (Leeb-Romanov, Leeb) are fundamentally suboptimal: their AMP state evolution fixed points do not match the information-theoretically optimal equations (4.5), yielding a strictly larger weak recovery threshold than the optimal one whenever at least one covariance is not a scalar multiple of the identity. In the one-sided heteroscedastic case (Ξ = I_n), the proposed method additionally achieves the Bayes-optimal error, a guarantee none of the baselines provide. The doubly heteroscedastic case (Figure 3, Toeplitz Ξ and circulant Σ) shows the largest gap, where the spectral estimator maintains strong correlation with both u* and v* at SNR values where all baselines fail.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the heteroscedastic matrix denoising literature, which has primarily studied one-sided noise correlation via whitening and singular value shrinkage (Leeb-Romanov, OptShrink, ScreeNOT, HeteroPCA), to the doubly heteroscedastic setting where both row and column noise correlations are present. It departs fundamentally from the standard random matrix theory toolkit by borrowing the interpolation method from statistical physics (Guerra, Miolane) to derive information-theoretic limits, and from approximate message passing (Montanari-Venkataramanan) to design and analyze the spectral estimator. The key conceptual departure is showing that the widely adopted whitening ansatz (Ξ^{-1/2}AΣ^{-1/2}) is provably suboptimal, and that operating on the full inverse (Ξ^{-1}AΣ^{-1}) is what aligns the AMP fixed point with the information-theoretically optimal free energy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to simultaneously establish information-theoretic limits and an efficient algorithm with optimality guarantees for doubly heteroscedastic noise, filling a gap where prior methods were either suboptimal or only handled one-sided heteroscedasticity. The proof that standard whitening-plus-shrinkage cannot achieve Bayes optimality redirects the field toward structurally different preprocessing. Key open questions include: extending to non-Gaussian priors (where single-letter free energy expressions may not exist), handling unknown covariances in the proportional regime, and verifying the technical condition σ₂* < 1 analytically rather than only numerically. The rank-r extension is expected but non-trivial.
