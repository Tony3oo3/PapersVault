## Problem

Existing VLN agents trained via behavioral cloning suffer from quadratic error accumulation due to covariate shift, as they optimize only the conditional action distribution P(a|s) at each step without modeling the full joint state-action distribution P(s,a) induced by the expert. Reinforcement learning alternatives require manual reward engineering and introduce training instability for transformer-based models. The paper asks whether a principled framework can globally align with the expert policy by exploiting the complete state-action distribution rather than single-step decisions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243435/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At each navigation step, the agent receives an egocentric panoramic visual observation, a natural language instruction, and a history embedding (from recurrent hidden units or an external topological graph), which are jointly encoded into a state representation s_t ∈ R^{K_t × D}. The output is a logit vector over K_t discrete action candidates in a panoramic action space, from which the next action is selected via softmax; in continuous environments (R2R-CE, RxR-CE), a waypoint predictor additionally generates candidate waypoints. A Marginal State Memory of buffer size 100 persists across training steps to store SGLD samples for initializing subsequent sampling iterations.

## Method

ENP reinterprets the VLN policy as an energy-based model where the energy function E_θ(s,a) = −a_t[k] (negative action logits) defines a Boltzmann distribution over joint state-action pairs, with low energy corresponding to expert-likely transitions. The total loss decomposes into a standard cross-entropy action loss L_π and a marginal state distribution matching loss L_S, the latter estimated via SGLD sampling initialized from a Marginal State Memory using persistent contrastive divergence (95% probability of sampling from memory). Theoretically, the optimization minimizes the forward KL divergence between the expert and learned occupancy measures, yielding mode-covering behavior over entire trajectories rather than single-step decisions. ENP is architecture-agnostic and has been validated on Seq2Seq (EnvDrop), transformer (VLN-BERT), dual-scale graph transformer (DUET), and topological planning (ETPNav) frameworks across both discrete and continuous environments.

## Results

On R2R (discrete), ENP achieves 74% SR and 60% SPL with DUET on val unseen, and 71% SR / 60% SPL on test unseen, outperforming the original DUET by 2% SR. On REVERIE, ENP-DUET reaches 48.90% SR and 34.74% RGS on val unseen (+1.92% SR, +2.59% RGS over DUET). On R2R-CE (continuous), ENP-VLN BERT gains 1% SR and 1% SPL over VLN BERT on val unseen (45% vs 44% SR), and ENP-ETPNav achieves 66% SR / 59% SPL on val seen. On RxR-CE, ENP-ETPNav reaches 55.27% SR and 62.97% NDTW on val unseen, surpassing ETPNav by 0.48% SR and 1.07% NDTW.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ENP consistently improves over its host architectures: on R2R, ENP-DUET gains +2% SR (74→72 val unseen; 71→69 test unseen) and ENP-VLN BERT gains +2% SR on test unseen (65 vs 63). On REVERIE, ENP-DUET achieves +1.92% SR and +2.59% RGS over DUET on val unseen (48.90% vs 46.98% SR; 34.74% vs 32.15% RGS), while ENP-VLN BERT gains +0.6% SR and +0.01% RGS. On R2R-CE, ENP-VLN BERT improves 1% SR and 1% SPL over VLN BERT on val unseen, and the paper reports ENP-ETPNav outperforming ETPNav by ~1% OSR and SR on test unseen. On RxR-CE, ENP-ETPNav edges out ETPNav by 0.48% SR and 1.07% NDTW on val unseen. Against top methods using semantic maps (BEVBert, BSG, VER), ENP-DUET remains competitive but slightly behind on R2R val unseen (74% vs 75–76% SR), which the authors attribute to the absence of additional map modules. In the ablation (Table 8), ENP outperforms AIRL by +2% SR and +1% SPL on R2R-CE val, confirming the advantage of forward-KL joint distribution matching over adversarial reward learning.

## Relation to Prior Work

ENP reinterprets VLN policy learning through the lens of energy-based models, departing from the standard discriminative formulation that models only P(a|s) via cross-entropy (BC) or adds hand-crafted rewards (RL). Conceptually, it sits between BC/DAGGER (which minimize reverse KL on conditional action distributions) and AIRL (which minimizes reverse KL on occupancy measures via adversarial reward learning), by instead minimizing forward KL on the joint state-action distribution P(s,a) parameterized as a Boltzmann energy. The SGLD sampler with a persistent Marginal State Memory draws directly from the EBM training literature (persistent contrastive divergence, Langevin dynamics), while the application to four existing VLN architectures (EnvDrop, VLN-BERT, DUET, ETPNav) positions ENP as a drop-in training objective rather than a new model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ENP offers a principled, reward-free alternative to both BC and RL for VLN, addressing the covariate-shift problem through global occupancy-measure alignment rather than per-step action matching. The consistent 1–2% gains across four heterogeneous architectures and four benchmarks (discrete and continuous) suggest the energy-based formulation is a broadly applicable training paradigm for embodied navigation. However, the SGLD inner loop adds ~9% training overhead, the method has only been validated on four architectures, and the paper acknowledges that MCMC-free samplers and active learning from video remain open directions for scaling the approach.
