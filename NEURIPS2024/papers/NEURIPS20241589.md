## Problem

Existing semi-supervised graph domain adaptation methods focus on aligning feature distributions across domains while overlooking the rich topological structure information inherent in graphs. Moreover, adversarial training for domain alignment is unstable and can destroy discriminative feature details, and the extreme label scarcity in the source graph (as low as 1–5% labeled nodes) causes severe overfitting that degrades target-domain generalization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241589/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a partially labeled source graph (adjacency matrix, 6775-dimensional sparse bag-of-words node attributes, and a small labeled node set at 1–10% label rate) and a completely unlabeled target graph of the same format, both drawn from ArnetMiner citation networks (ACMv9, Citationv1, DBLPv7). It produces 5-class node labels for all target graph nodes, where classes correspond to research areas (AI, Computer Vision, Database, Information Security, Networking). The source and target graphs have different node counts, edge structures, and class distributions, creating a realistic cross-domain transfer setting.

## Method

TFGDA combines a 2-layer GCN feature extractor (using PPIM-based adjacency from random-walk sampling) with three complementary strategies. First, STSA samples local subgraphs via random walk, computes persistence diagrams of both the input subgraph and its spherical latent representation using Vietoris-Rips complexes, and minimizes the 1-Wasserstein distance between the two PDs to encode topological structure into the latent space without modifying the network architecture. Second, SDA normalizes features onto a sphere of radius r (lower-bounded by class count and a confidence parameter), projects them onto b great circles via geodesic projection on the Stiefel manifold, and minimizes the spherical sliced-Wasserstein distance for stable cross-domain alignment. Third, RNC introduces trainable per-layer shift parameters (constrained by a Frobenius-norm bound) to perturb node features, then maximizes the mutual information between the soft cluster assignments of original and perturbed features, guiding discriminative clustering of unlabeled nodes without pseudo-labels and naturally avoiding degenerate solutions. The total objective jointly minimizes classification loss, SDA loss, and STSA loss while maximizing the RNC mutual information term, with a progressive schedule on the RNC weight.

## Results

On six transfer tasks (A→C, A→D, C→A, C→D, D→A, D→C) with only 5% source label rate, TFGDA achieves the best Micro-F1 and Macro-F1 across all tasks, ranging from 66.9% to 81.0% (Micro-F1) and 64.3% to 78.9% (Macro-F1). The largest gains over the SOTA baseline SGDA appear on the hardest transfer tasks: C→A (+7.3% Micro-F1, +10.0% Macro-F1) and D→A (+6.3% Micro-F1, +8.3% Macro-F1). TFGDA also exhibits notably smaller standard deviations (e.g., ±0.34 on A→C Micro-F1 vs. ±0.57 for SGDA), indicating greater stability across repeated runs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TFGDA outperforms all 13 compared baselines on every transfer task for both Micro-F1 and Macro-F1. Against the strongest prior method SGDA [2], TFGDA gains +5.4 to +7.3% Micro-F1 and +3.7 to +10.0% Macro-F1, with the largest margins on the hardest tasks C→A and D→A. Among graph semi-supervised baselines, GIN is the strongest (e.g., 64.6% Micro-F1 on A→C) but still trails TFGDA by 16.4 points. Adversarial-based methods (DANN_GCN, CDAN_GCN) and contrastive/structural methods (CoCo, StruRW) cluster in the 65–73% Micro-F1 range, consistently below TFGDA. The ablation variants (TFGDA-T, TFGDA-D, TFGDA-R) each contribute 3–8% over the baseline TFGDA-S, confirming that all three strategies (STSA, SDA, RNC) are individually beneficial and complementary.

## Relation to Prior Work

TFGDA extends the semi-supervised graph domain adaptation (SGDA) formulation introduced by Qiao et al. [2], adopting their PPIM-based GCN architecture and progressive scheduling, while departing from the broader GTL literature's reliance on adversarial or statistical feature-distribution alignment. Its key conceptual departure is the introduction of persistent homology (PH) to encode topological structure from the input graph into the latent spherical space (STSA), a technique previously applied to image segmentation and shape matching but not to graph transfer. The SDA strategy repurposes the spherical sliced-Wasserstein distance [14] and geodesic projection [13]—tools from optimal transport on manifolds—as a stable alternative to adversarial domain confusion, while the RNC strategy adapts the mutual-information clustering objective from Ji et al. [74] to the graph node setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important gap: most GTL methods assume fully labeled source graphs, whereas real-world annotation budgets are limited. The PH-based structure alignment offers a principled way to preserve topological information that GCN feature extractors are known to lose, and the spherical SSW alignment provides a more stable domain-discrepancy measure than adversarial training. However, the evaluation is confined to three citation networks of similar scale (~5K–9K nodes), leaving open whether the approach scales to much larger or structurally heterogeneous graphs (e.g., social networks, molecular graphs). The sensitivity to subgraph sampling parameters and perturbation scale, acknowledged in the paper's own limitations, suggests that tuning may be non-trivial in new domains.
