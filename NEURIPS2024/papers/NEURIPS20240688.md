## Problem

Existing monocular depth estimation models face a fundamental trade-off: discriminative models (e.g., Depth Anything V1) produce robust but coarse predictions because real labeled depth maps contain inherent noise from depth sensors, stereo matching, and SfM, while generative models (e.g., Marigold) capture fine details but are over 10× slower and less robust to complex scenes. The paper asks whether a single efficient discriminative model can simultaneously achieve fine-grained detail, robustness to transparent/reflective surfaces, and scalability across model sizes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240688/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a single RGB image as input, resized so the shorter side is 518 pixels followed by a random crop during training, and outputs an affine-invariant inverse depth map. Four model scales are released (DINOv2-Small/Base/Large/Giant encoders with a DPT decoder, ranging from 25M to 1.3B parameters). When fine-tuned on synthetic metric datasets (Hypersim for indoor, Virtual KITTI for outdoor), the same architecture produces metric depth maps in meters. A notable property is test-time resolution scaling: increasing input resolution at inference (2× or 4× the 518 base) further sharpens predicted depth without retraining.

## Method

The pipeline is a three-stage teacher-student procedure. First, a DINOv2-Giant + DPT teacher is trained purely on 595K synthetic images (BlendedMVS, Hypersim, IRS, TartanAir, Virtual KITTI 2) whose depth labels are pixel-perfect, using a scale-and-shift-invariant loss plus a gradient matching loss (weight ratio 1:2) that is especially effective for sharpening boundaries when labels are precise. Second, this teacher assigns pseudo depth labels to 62M diverse unlabeled real images from eight public datasets. Third, student models (DINOv2-S/B/L/G + DPT) are trained solely on these pseudo-labeled real images, with the top-10% highest-loss regions ignored per sample to filter noisy pseudo labels, plus a feature alignment loss to preserve DINOv2 semantics. The key insight is that synthetic labels eliminate the noise that caps fine-grained prediction in real data, while the pseudo-labeled real image bridge resolves the distribution shift and limited scene coverage that prevent synthetic-only models from generalizing—achieving both precision and robustness without any trade-off.

## Results

On the proposed DA-2K benchmark (sparse relative depth across 8 diverse scenarios), Depth Anything V2 achieves 95.3% (ViT-S), 97.0% (ViT-B), 97.1% (ViT-L), and 97.4% (ViT-G) accuracy, outperforming all SD-based community models (Marigold 86.8%, GeoWizard 88.1%, DepthFM 85.8%) by 7–11 percentage points. On conventional zero-shot benchmarks (KITTI, NYU-D, Sintel, ETH3D, DIODE), V2 is comparable to V1 and superior to MiDaS V3.1. For fine-tuned metric depth, the ViT-L model achieves 0.056 AbsRel / 0.984 δ₁ on NYU-D and 0.045 AbsRel / 0.983 δ₁ on KITTI, surpassing all prior methods including those built on ViT-L encoders. On the NTIRE 2024 Transparent Surface Challenge, V2 scores 83.6% zero-shot (vs. MiDaS 25.9%, V1 53.5%) and 91.2% after simple fine-tuning, nearly matching the first-place score of 91.7%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SD-based models (Marigold, GeoWizard, DepthFM), V2 wins decisively on DA-2K by 7.5–11.6 percentage points while being >10× faster in inference. Against Depth Anything V1, V2 is roughly tied on conventional benchmarks (e.g., KITTI AbsRel 0.074–0.078 vs. 0.076–0.080) but pulls ahead substantially on DA-2K (95.3–97.4% vs. 88.5%) and the Transparent Surface Challenge (83.6% vs. 53.5% zero-shot). Against MiDaS V3.1, V2 is consistently better across all five standard benchmarks. In the metric depth fine-tuning setting (Table 4), V2 (ViT-L) outperforms ZoeDepth (0.056 vs. 0.077 AbsRel on NYU-D; 0.045 vs. 0.054 on KITTI) and all other baselines including SwinV2, AiT, VPD, and IEBins. For semantic segmentation transfer (Table 8), V2-Large achieves 85.6 mIoU on Cityscapes and 58.6 on ADE20K, beating Mask2Former-Swin-L (83.3/56.4), OneFormer-ConvNeXt-XL (83.6/57.4), and ViT-Adapter-BEiT-L (—/58.3). The DINOv2-G encoder used as a raw initialization scores only 75.8% on the Transparent Surface Challenge, whereas V2's pre-trained encoder reaches 91.2% after fine-tuning, confirming the value of the depth-specific pre-training pipeline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Depth Anything V1 by fundamentally rethinking the labeled data strategy: replacing all noisy real-image depth labels with precise synthetic images, then bridging the domain gap via large-scale pseudo-labeled real images from a DINOv2-G teacher. It departs from the generative-model line (Marigold, GeoWizard, DepthFM) that relies on Stable Diffusion for fine details, demonstrating that a discriminative DPT-on-DINOv2 architecture achieves comparable or superior detail without the 10× inference cost. The pseudo-label distillation is conceptually related to knowledge distillation but operates at the prediction level through unlabeled data rather than at the feature or logit level, and the gradient matching loss from MiDaS is shown to be critically important only when paired with synthetic supervision.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that synthetic labels + pseudo-labeled real data strictly dominates labeled real data (even 5% real data degrades fine-grained predictions) challenges the field's default assumption that more real labeled data is always better, and provides a scalable recipe for future MDE work. The DA-2K benchmark addresses concrete limitations of existing test sets (label noise, limited scene diversity, low resolution) and will likely become a standard evaluation complement. Practical impact is broad: models spanning 25M–1.3B parameters are 10× faster than SD-based alternatives, and the transparent-surface zero-shot score jumps from 25.9% (MiDaS) to 83.6%. Open questions include reducing the computational burden of 62M unlabeled images and expanding synthetic training diversity beyond current graphics-engine coverage.
