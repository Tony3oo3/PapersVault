## Problem

Existing graph coarsening methods are predominantly designed for homophilic datasets and are computationally intensive, often exhibiting quadratic or worse time complexity. No prior method handles heterophilic graphs (where nodes preferentially connect to different classes), and there is a persistent trade-off between coarsening speed and preservation of spectral properties. This paper asks whether a single, linear-time coarsening framework can work universally across both homophilic and heterophilic regimes while maintaining spectral fidelity.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a graph G(V, A, X) with adjacency matrix A ∈ R^{N×N}, node feature matrix X ∈ R^{N×d̃}, and node labels (used to compute the heterophily factor α). It produces a coarsened graph G_c with n ≪ N super-nodes, a binary coarsening matrix C ∈ R^{N×n} (one-hot per row, orthogonal columns), and coarsened features F_c. The bin-width hyperparameter r controls the target coarsening ratio and is found via a divide-and-conquer search; the augmented feature vector F_i = (1−α)·X_i ⊕ α·A_i concatenates node features with the binary adjacency row, weighted by heterophily.

## Method

UGC constructs an augmented feature matrix F that blends node attributes and structural (adjacency) information, with the heterophily factor α balancing their relative contribution. It then applies Locality Sensitive Hashing with l random projectors drawn from a p-stable distribution: each node's augmented vector is projected, quantized by bin-width r, and assigned to a super-node via majority-vote over hash indices. The resulting coarsening matrix C is binary and one-hot per row, yielding the coarsened adjacency as A_c = C^TAC in a single matrix product. An optional feature re-learning step (UGC-FL) solves a closed-form convex optimization to enforce Dirichlet smoothness on super-node features, tightening the ε-similarity bound to ε ≤ 1. The overall time complexity is O(N+m), linear in graph size, versus the quadratic or higher costs of spectral and optimization-based baselines.

## Results

UGC (augmented features) achieves the best GCN node-classification accuracy at 50% coarsening on 7 out of 9 datasets, including 86.30% on Cora (vs. 81.57% best baseline), 96.12% on Physics (vs. 93.94%), and 48.7% on Chameleon (vs. 33.3%). In terms of spectral preservation, UGC attains the lowest Relative Eigen Error on 8 of 12 datasets, with particularly dramatic gains on PubMed (0.004 vs. 0.063) and Film (0.075 vs. 5.56). On runtime, UGC is 4×–15× faster than the fastest existing method (e.g., 6.4 s vs. 34.5 s on Physics, 2.14 s vs. 18.0 s on Squirrel) and is the only method that successfully coarsens large-scale datasets like Flickr, Reddit, and Yelp where all baselines run out of memory.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Loukas's spectral coarsening variants (Var. Neigh., Var. Edges, Var. Clique), UGC (fea+Ad) wins on 7 of 9 GCN-accuracy datasets with margins of 2–23 points (e.g., +15.4 on Chameleon, +22.6 on Texas), while also achieving 4–9× lower runtime. Heavy Edge Matching [Dhillon et al.] is the strongest baseline on homophilic datasets (e.g., 93.03% on Physics) but UGC still edges ahead (96.12%) and dominates on heterophilic graphs (31.62% vs. 20.36% on Squirrel, 48.7% vs. 33.3% on Chameleon). Algebraic Distance [Ron et al.] achieves the best REE among baselines on DBLP (0.047) but UGC reduces it further to 0.004; on GCN accuracy, UGC leads on 7/9 datasets. Affinity GS [Livne & Brandt] is the slowest baseline (924.7 s on Physics vs. UGC's 6.4 s, ~144×) and UGC outperforms it on 7/9 accuracy datasets. Kron reduction [Dörfler & Bullo] is competitive on small homophilic graphs (80.71% on Cora) but UGC surpasses it on 7/9 datasets and is 5–10× faster. Notably, UGC is the only method that completes on Flickr, Reddit, and Yelp, where all five baselines fail with OOM.

## Relation to Prior Work

UGC repurposes Locality Sensitive Hashing with p-stable distributions (Datar et al., 2004) — a technique from high-dimensional similarity search — as the core mechanism for generating the graph coarsening matrix, replacing the iterative optimization or heuristic matching used by prior spectral coarsening methods (Loukas 2019, Dhillon 2007, Ron 2010, Livne 2011, Dorfler 2013). It also departs from GNN-based condensation approaches (GCond, SFGC) that require training a GNN on the original graph, by decoupling the coarsening mapping from downstream model training. The key conceptual departure is the introduction of an augmented feature vector that blends node attributes with adjacency information weighted by the heterophily factor, enabling the framework to handle heterophilic graphs — a setting the authors note has never been addressed by prior coarsening work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 4×–15× speedup with linear time complexity makes graph coarsening practical for very large graphs (716k-node Yelp, 233k-node Reddit) where prior methods hit out-of-memory limits, and the model-agnostic design means the coarsened graph can feed any GNN architecture without retraining. The extension to heterophilic graphs opens a previously unexplored regime, though the evidence is limited to small-scale heterophilic benchmarks (Squirrel, Chameleon, Texas, Film) and a single-hidden-layer GCN. Open questions include whether the bin-width hyperparameter can be learned automatically, whether the loose ε-similarity guarantee (ε ≥ 0 without feature re-learning) is sufficient for safety-critical applications, and whether the augmented feature vector's linear growth with graph size remains tractable for extremely dense graphs.
