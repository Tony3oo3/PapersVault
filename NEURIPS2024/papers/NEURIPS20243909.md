## Problem

Existing LLM safety benchmarks evaluate either generative or discriminative safety in isolation and use only standardized inputs, leaving two critical questions unanswered: whether safety-aligned LLMs generalize their safety across both generation and discrimination tasks, and whether common prompt engineering techniques (system prompts, few-shot demonstrations, chain-of-thought) positively or negatively affect safety performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243909/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark consumes 1,442 malicious queries (seeded from AdvBench, HarmfulQA, Beaver-eval, and SaladBench, filtered via LlamaGuard-7B at 93.92% human agreement) and expands them into 4 base evaluation subsets (original queries, 6 jailbreak attack variants, multiple-choice questions with one harmful and one harmless candidate, and safety judgments over both candidates) plus 7 prompt-extended subsets. For generation tasks, LlamaGuard-7B serves as the automated judge producing a binary harmful/safe label per response; for discrimination tasks, regex-based keyword extraction is matched against ground-truth labels. The final output is a Failure Rate (proportion of safety failures) per subset and an Average Failure Rate across all four base subsets.

## Method

SG-Bench is constructed by (1) collecting and quality-filtering 1,442 malicious queries across 6 coarse-grained safety categories, (2) building four task-specific evaluation subsets that jointly probe generative safety (original queries + jailbreak attacks using prefix injection, refusal suppression, distractors negated, Poems, AIM, and evil confidant) and discriminative safety (MCQ selection between a harmful and a harmless response, plus binary safety judgment over each response), and (3) extending each subset with role-oriented prompts, task-oriented prompts, few-shot demonstrations (3 examples from Alpaca or hand-written), and chain-of-thought prompting to isolate the effect of prompt context on safety. Evaluation uses LlamaGuard-7B as an LLM-as-judge for generation and regex matching for discrimination, with cross-validation against ChatGPT, GPT-4, and Claude-3 as alternative evaluators. To explain the observed poor generalization, the authors perform attention-by-gradient visualization showing LLM attention shifts from malicious queries to prompt contexts, and intra/inter-class distance analysis with TSNE visualization demonstrating that discrimination performance correlates with the model's ability to separate harmful from harmless response representations.

## Results

On SG-Bench, Claude-3-haiku achieves the best overall safety performance with a 2.99% Average Failure Rate (Avg FR), while Qwen1.5-7B-chat performs worst at 30.79% Avg FR. A critical finding is the large gap between generation and discrimination: for example, ChatGPT (gpt-3.5-turbo) achieves 0.00% FR on original queries but jumps to 20.53% on multiple-choice questions and 10.64% on safety judgments. Among open-source models, InternLM2-7B-chat is the strongest (14.65% Avg FR), comparable to ChatGPT (13.69%), while LLAMA2-7B-chat and LLAMA2-13B-chat excel at generation (0.28% and 0.42% FR on original queries) but fail badly at discrimination (56.24% and 31.62% on MCQ).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SG-Bench is positioned against seven prior safety benchmarks (AdvBench, SafetyPrompts, DecodingTrust, SafetyBench, EasyJailbreak, Jailbroken, SaladBench) in Table 1; unlike all of them, SG-Bench simultaneously covers generation, MCQ, safety judgment, jailbreak attacks, and prompt engineering, whereas each prior benchmark covers at most two of these five dimensions. At the model level, Claude-3-haiku dominates all four task types (0.00–7.66% FR), outperforming GPT-4-turbo (0.00–15.11%) and GPT-3.5-turbo (0.00–23.58%) by wide margins on discrimination tasks. Among open-source models, InternLM2-7B-chat (14.65% Avg FR) is the strongest and approaches ChatGPT's 13.69%, while LLAMA2-7B-chat and LLAMA2-13B-chat achieve near-zero generation failure rates (0.28%, 0.42%)—even beating GPT-4 on jailbreak defense (11.75%, 8.54% vs. 13.56%)—but collapse on MCQ (56.24%, 31.62%), revealing severe over-alignment. The paper also validates its LlamaGuard-7B evaluator against ChatGPT, GPT-4, and Claude-3 as alternative judges (Table 7), showing consistent relative rankings across evaluators despite absolute score differences of up to ~20 percentage points.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LLM safety evaluation line of work (AdvBench, SafetyBench, EasyJailbreak, SaladBench) by introducing the 'safety generalization' problem: prior benchmarks evaluate safety on a single task type (generation or discrimination) and ignore prompt context effects, whereas SG-Bench integrates both generative and discriminative tasks alongside systematic prompt engineering variations (system prompts, few-shot, CoT). It builds on LlamaGuard-7B as the generation-task evaluator and on EasyJailbreak/Jailbroken for constructing the jailbreak attack subset, but departs from these by treating the interaction between task formulation and prompt type as the central evaluation axis rather than as a secondary robustness check.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that safety-aligned LLMs exhibit a large gap between generation safety (near-zero failure on original queries) and discrimination safety (10–66% failure) challenges the assumption that RLHF/DPO-based alignment transfers across task formulations, with direct implications for content-moderation pipelines that rely on the same model for both generation and judgment. The counterintuitive result that CoT prompting and few-shot demonstrations can degrade safety performance is actionable for practitioners who routinely apply these techniques. Open questions include whether multi-turn dialogue and code-generation settings exhibit similar generalization failures, whether the coarse-grained 6-category taxonomy masks issue-specific vulnerabilities, and whether targeted safety fine-tuning across task/prompt types can close the gap.
