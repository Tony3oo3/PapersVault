## Problem

Species Distribution Models (SDMs) struggle to integrate the rich but heterogeneous biodiversity data now available—millions of biased Presence-Only observations, limited Presence-Absence surveys, and multimodal remote sensing—into unified high-resolution predictions. Prior SDM benchmarks were small-scale, used coarse predictors, or lacked standardized PA evaluation data, making it difficult to fairly compare deep multimodal approaches. GeoPlant fills this gap with a continental-scale European dataset (10k+ species, 5M PO records, 90k PA surveys) and an openly accessible Kaggle benchmark with strong baselines.

## Input / Output

Each observation is accompanied by a 128×128 four-band (R, G, B, NIR) Sentinel-2 image at 10 m resolution, a 20-year monthly climatic cube (4 variables: min/mean/max temperature, precipitation), a 21-year quarterly Landsat cube (6 spectral bands), and scalar values from environmental rasters (elevation, 9 soil properties, 13-band MODIS land cover, 16 human-footprint layers). The model outputs a multi-label species composition per location; evaluation uses sample-averaged F1 (F1s) and Recall computed over the top-25 predicted species per PA survey, with a spatial 10×10 km block hold-out for the test split.

## Method

The strongest baselines are Multimodal Ensembles (MME) where each modality (Sentinel-2 image, climatic cube, Landsat cube) is independently encoded by a lightweight 6-block residual network (ResNet-6), the resulting embeddings are concatenated, and a final fully-connected layer with sigmoid produces per-species logits. A Top-K estimation step refines predictions by binning the training set into 15 quantiles of species richness, predicting the expected number of species per survey, and selecting the top-K+5 most probable species rather than a fixed threshold. Conventional baselines (XGBoost, MaxEnt) and single-modality deep models (ResNet-18, MLP) are also provided for comparison. All PA experiments use AdamW with adaptive learning-rate decay, early stopping on validation F1s, and are averaged over five random seeds; the three-modality MME with Top-K estimation achieves F1s of 36.2% and AUC of 94.0%.

## Results

The best-performing baseline, a three-modality Multimodal Ensemble (MME) with Top-K species estimation (Climatic + Landsat + Sentinel-2), achieves a sample-averaged F1s of 36.2% and AUC of 94.0% on the 4,716-survey PA test set. Single-modality deep models peak at F1s of 30.3% (ResNet-6 with Landsat time-series cubes), while XGBoost with all four predictor groups reaches F1s of 28.7%. The naive baseline of predicting the top-25 most common species per biogeographic zone scores only 20.6% F1s, and MaxEnt achieves just ~17.8% F1s, highlighting the substantial gap between traditional and deep multimodal approaches on this dataset.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MaxEnt (Phillips et al. 2006), the most widely used traditional SDM method, achieves an F1s of only 0.168–0.178 on GeoPlant, roughly half the performance of the best deep multimodal baseline (36.2% F1s). XGBoost, another conventional approach, reaches 28.7% F1s with all predictors combined—competitive with single-modality deep models but still 7.5 points below the three-modality MME. Among deep architectures, ResNet-6 matches or slightly exceeds ResNet-18 on all single modalities at a fraction of the computational cost, while a simple MLP underperforms substantially (F1s ≤ 28.4%). The multimodal ensemble consistently outperforms every single-modality model by 4–6 F1s points, and adding the Top-K species-count estimation further improves F1s by ~2 points at the cost of ~4 points in Recall. Training on PO data alone yields F1s below 15% for all architectures, confirming that the distribution shift between opportunistic and exhaustive survey data remains a major open challenge.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the authors' long-running GeoLifeCLEF benchmark series (2018–2024) by scaling it to a continental European dataset with 10k+ species and adding multimodal predictors (Sentinel-2 images, Landsat time series, CHELSA climate cubes) that were absent in earlier editions. Conceptually, it departs from prior SDM benchmarks (Elith et al. 2006, Charney et al. 2021, SatBird 2024) by combining ~100× more occurrences with exhaustive Presence-Absence surveys for robust evaluation, and by integrating satellite imagery alongside traditional environmental rasters—a combination no prior benchmark offered. The multimodal ensemble baselines build on prior deep SDM work using CNNs on remote sensing (Deneu et al. 2021, 2022; Estopinan et al. 2022) but are the first to jointly encode three heterogeneous modalities (image, climate cube, satellite time-series cube) within a single architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GeoPlant is the largest and most predictor-diverse SDM dataset to date, and its inclusion of standardized PA surveys (90k records) addresses a critical gap: prior deep SDM work relied almost exclusively on biased PO data, making evaluation unreliable. The open challenge of distribution shift (training on PO, evaluating on PA) and the multimodal fusion problem (combining 10m images with 1km rasters and 20-year time series) remain unsolved and are explicitly framed as the key research questions the benchmark targets. Limitations include the geographic concentration of PA data (primarily France, Denmark, Switzerland, Czechia), severe species imbalance, and the computational cost of multimodal training, all of which constrain generalizability and accessibility.
