## Problem

Neighbor embedding (NE) methods like t-SNE and UMAP require computing pairwise attraction and repulsion forces across all data points, but in federated learning each client only has access to its own local data. This creates two fundamental challenges: (1) inter-client repulsion is missing because clients cannot sample non-neighbors from other clients, and (2) local kNN graphs are incomplete, leading to invisible true neighbors and false neighbor connections between distant points. Prior decentralized visualization methods (dSNE, F-dSNE) require a publicly shared reference dataset, which is often unavailable in practice.

## Input / Output

The method takes high-dimensional feature vectors distributed across M clients (784D for MNIST/Fashion-MNIST, 512D ResNet-34 features for CIFAR-10, 50D for scRNA-Seq) and produces a shared 2D embedding via a parametric encoder network (fully connected with 2–3 hidden layers). Each client's local dataset is disjoint from others, and the kNN graph is built locally with a fixed k=7. The surrogate models that are exchanged between clients take 2D grid-sampled points as input and output a scalar repulsive loss value, keeping communication lightweight.

## Method

FEDNE extends the FedAvg pipeline with two complementary mechanisms. First, each client trains a lightweight surrogate MLP (one hidden layer) that approximates the repulsive loss from an arbitrary 2D query point to its local data; these surrogates are shared globally so that every client can incorporate inter-client repulsion into its local contrastive NE loss. The surrogate is trained by grid-sampling 2D points and computing the true repulsive loss to b random local data points as regression targets (MSE loss). Second, an intra-client data mixing strategy (mixup-style interpolation between a point and its kNN neighbors with λ~Beta(0.2)) augments the local dataset to mitigate incomplete kNN graphs. The surrogate loss is integrated into local training only after 30% of rounds to avoid destabilizing early optimization, and the combined objective weights each client's contribution by its dataset size.

## Results

Under the most severe non-IID setting (Dir(0.1), 20 clients), FEDNE achieves kNN classification accuracy of 0.72 on MNIST, 0.66 on Fashion-MNIST, 0.90 on scRNA-Seq, and 0.63 on CIFAR-10, substantially outperforming FedAvg+NE (0.48, 0.60, 0.85, 0.55) and FedProx+NE (0.49, 0.60, 0.83, 0.55). On average across all five metrics and all settings, FEDNE outperforms LocalNE by 2.62% (continuity), 6.12% (trustworthiness), 14.31% (kNN accuracy), 12.69% (steadiness), and 7.31% (cohesiveness). The largest gains appear in trustworthiness and kNN accuracy, where inter-client repulsion and correct neighborhood estimation are most critical.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FEDNE consistently outperforms both FedAvg+NE [McMahan et al., 2017] and FedProx+NE [Li et al., 2020] on trustworthiness, kNN classification accuracy, steadiness, and cohesiveness across all four datasets and both Dirichlet settings. The most dramatic gap is in kNN accuracy under Dir(0.1) with 20 clients: FEDNE reaches 0.72 on MNIST versus 0.48 for FedAvg+NE and 0.49 for FedProx+NE, a 50% relative improvement. On continuity, all methods (including FEDNE) achieve near-saturated scores (0.96–0.99), indicating that pulling local neighbors together is easy; the real challenge is avoiding false neighbors and separating inter-client clusters, where FEDNE's surrogate repulsion and data mixing provide the advantage. FedProx+NE is marginally better than FedAvg+NE on MNIST kNN (0.49 vs 0.48) but slightly worse on steadiness (0.51 vs 0.54), suggesting the proximal term offers limited benefit for the pairwise NE objective. Against the GlobalNE upper bound, FEDNE closes most of the gap on trustworthiness (e.g., 0.85 vs 0.94 on MNIST) but still lags on kNN accuracy (0.72 vs 0.93), indicating room for improvement in neighborhood fidelity under extreme non-IID conditions.

## Relation to Prior Work

FEDNE sits at the intersection of two well-developed lines of work: the FedAvg federated learning framework (McMahan et al., 2017) and the contrastive neighbor embedding formulation that unifies t-SNE and UMAP under a single loss (Damrich et al., 2022). It departs from prior decentralized visualization methods (dSNE, F-dSNE) that rely on a publicly shared reference dataset for cross-client alignment, instead operating under the stricter FL constraint of no data sharing. The key conceptual departure from standard FL is recognizing that the pairwise nature of the NE objective breaks the instance-level loss decomposition that makes FedAvg work for classification, necessitating the surrogate repulsion model and intra-client data mixing as compensatory mechanisms.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is, to the authors' knowledge, the first FL framework for neighbor embedding that requires no shareable data, opening the door to collaborative visualization in privacy-sensitive settings like multi-hospital EHR analysis. The surrogate model approach—training a lightweight MLP to approximate inter-client repulsion—is a clean and generalizable idea that could potentially extend to other pairwise-loss FL problems. However, the work is limited to 2D embeddings on relatively small datasets (≤60K samples), assumes full client participation in main experiments, and provides only informal privacy arguments rather than formal guarantees. Open questions include scalability to larger client counts and datasets, applicability to higher-dimensional embeddings, and whether the grid-sampling strategy for surrogate training remains effective under extreme non-IID conditions.
