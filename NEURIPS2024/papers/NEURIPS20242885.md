## Problem

Existing gradient-based bi-level optimization methods face a fundamental tradeoff in large-scale settings: gradient unrolling (GU) methods require O(MN) or O(TM) memory that scales with model dimension or unrolled depth, while implicit function (IF) and truncated methods introduce biased gradient approximations that degrade performance. No prior approach simultaneously achieves constant memory, unbiased meta-gradient estimation, and practical scalability for deep neural network inner problems.

## Input / Output

The method takes a meta objective f, an inner optimization procedure defined by transition functions Ω₀:ₜ, initial parameters (θ₀, φ₀), an unrolled depth T, and b i.i.d. random direction vectors vᵢ ∈ ℝᴺ (typically Rademacher). It produces an unbiased stochastic meta-gradient estimate ∇̂h(φ) = (1/b)Σᵢ wᵢvᵢᵀ and the updated meta parameter φ. Memory per direction is O(M) regardless of T or N, and the computation is inherently parallelizable across the b directions via JAX vmap/pmap. The zeroth-order variant (FG)²U-ZO replaces JVP with two forward passes and a finite-difference approximation, enabling use with non-differentiable inner solvers such as spectral PDE methods.

## Method

(FG)²U combines forward gradient unrolling (FGU) with the forward gradient (FG) estimator: by right-multiplying the FGU Jacobian recursion Zₜ = AₜZₜ₋₁ + Bₜ by a random vector v, the algorithm tracks only an M-dimensional vector Zₜv instead of the full M×N Jacobian, yielding the unbiased estimate ∇h(φ)vvᵀ per direction. Monte Carlo averaging over b i.i.d. directions reduces variance to O((N-1)/b)·‖∇h‖², and the resulting gradient is used for standard gradient descent on φ with learning rate β = ρ/((ρ+1)Lₕ), giving an O(ε⁻¹ρ⁻¹) convergence rate to an ε-stationary point. The method is implemented via forward-mode AD (JVP) in JAX/PyTorch, requiring only the forward computational graph of the inner loop without storing intermediate states. A two-phase training paradigm is proposed: an initial phase uses efficient but biased methods (TRGU, Hessian-Free) to obtain a good φ₀, followed by a refinement phase with (FG)²U for accurate gradient approximation, exploiting the fact that the convergence bound depends linearly on the initial suboptimality gap.

## Results

On the data condensation task, (FG)²U achieves 82.44% test accuracy on MNIST (IPC=1), 29.37% on CIFAR-10 (IPC=1), and 8.22% on CIFAR-100 (IPC=1), substantially outperforming TRGU (73.76%, 20.78%, 3.96%) and Hessian-Free (65.98%, 19.72%, 3.14%) across all datasets. On meta-learning for online LM adaptation with GPT2-XL (1.5B), (FG)²U reaches 8.89 EM / 14.42 F1 on StreamingQA and 7.37 EM / 10.37 F1 on SQuAD-Seq, surpassing CaMeLS+RGU (6.55/11.67 and 6.70/10.15). For PDE discovery, (FG)²U-ZO with a numerical solver reduces relative L2 error on Burgers' equation to 0.97 (φ) and 0.63 (u), compared to 2143.58 and 336.06 for (FG)²U with PINN.

## Baselines & Comparisons

(FG)²U consistently outperforms all large-scale bi-level optimization baselines (TRGU, Hessian-Free, Neumann) across all three data condensation datasets, with the largest margins at the most challenging IPC=1 setting (e.g., +8.68% over TRGU on MNIST, +8.59% on CIFAR-10, +4.26% on CIFAR-100). The gap narrows at higher IPC values where all methods approach the RGU upper bound, but (FG)²U still leads by 0.3–1.5%. On meta-learning, (FG)²U beats CaMeLS+RGU by 1.34 EM / 2.75 F1 on StreamingQA and 0.67 EM / 0.22 F1 on SQuAD-Seq with GPT2-XL, and the advantage grows with model scale (largest at 1.5B params). For PDE discovery, (FG)²U-ZO with a numerical solver dramatically outperforms (FG)²U with PINN (e.g., 0.97 vs. 2143.58 relative L2 error on Burgers' φ), demonstrating the value of the zeroth-order variant when inner solvers are non-differentiable. RGU remains the performance upper bound but is impractical at scale due to O(TM) memory, which (FG)²U avoids while closing most of the gap.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper combines two established ideas—forward gradient unrolling (FGU) from Franceschi et al. (2018) and forward gradient estimation via random directional perturbations from Baydin et al. (2022)—into a single algorithm that computes the meta gradient as a product of a Jacobian-vector product (JVP) propagated forward through the inner optimization, rather than the full Jacobian or a backward pass. This departs from the GU family (FGU/RGU) by eliminating the O(MN) or O(TM) memory overhead, and from the IF family (Neumann, Hessian-Free) by avoiding Hessian inversion and the associated approximation bias. The zeroth-order variant further extends the method to black-box inner solvers via finite differences, a direction not previously explored in the bi-level optimization literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method provides the first unbiased, constant-memory stochastic gradient estimator for large-scale bi-level optimization, filling a gap between biased-but-efficient methods (TRGU, Hessian-Free) and accurate-but-memory-hungry methods (RGU). The practical two-phase paradigm—using an efficient biased method for warm-starting and (FG)²U for refinement—offers a cost-effective recipe for practitioners. However, the dimension-dependent convergence rate O(ε⁻¹ρ⁻¹) with ρ = b/(N−1) remains a theoretical limitation for very high-dimensional meta-parameters, and experiments are capped at GPT-2 scale (1.5B), leaving open whether the method's advantages persist at truly frontier model sizes. The zeroth-order variant's applicability to PDE-constrained and other black-box bi-level problems is an underexplored but promising direction.
