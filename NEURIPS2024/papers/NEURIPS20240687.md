## Problem

Existing cancer pain datasets (MIMIC, FAERS, eICU, SEER) lack multidisciplinary treatment (MDT) team assessments of medication rationality, patient self-reported perceptions of treatment effects, and long-term multi-visit follow-up records. This gap prevents the construction of systems that can holistically evaluate treatment effectiveness and recommend personalized medication adjustments over a patient's evolving cancer pain trajectory.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240687/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The input consists of 103 structured features per visit across six groups (patient demographics and lab values, pain assessment via NRS scores, prior analgesic use categorized into 7 drug classes, MDT evaluation of drug-related problems using PCNE V8.0, medication decision rationale, and follow-up pain relief status), drawn from both in-hospital EHR records and a patient-initiated online follow-up platform. For the 2,600 multi-visit patients, features form a time series with up to 33 sequential visits. The TEA task outputs a 4-level treatment efficacy classification (complete, partial, mild, ineffective relief), while the MR task outputs continuous regression values for the recommended quantity of each of 7 analgesic categories (ERSO, IRSO, ERWO, IRWO, NSAID, A/A, Others).

## Method

The core contribution is the PEACE dataset construction pipeline: 32 multidisciplinary experts (pharmacists, oncologists, anesthetists, nurses) from nine Chinese provinces participated in a two-round Delphi consensus process to select 20 feature themes expanded into 103 sub-features, with consensus defined by mean score ≥4.0, coefficient of variation <0.15, and no dissenting opinions. Data preprocessing includes standardizing pain terminology, merging duplicate records, and categorizing dozens of individual drugs into 7 pharmacological classes to reduce combinatorial dimensionality. Privacy is protected via randomized patient IDs, per-patient date shifting (30–80 years), and age capping at 89. For the empirical study, 13 supervised models (5 basic ML/NN, 3 GBDT, 3 time-series NN, 2 EHR-specific NN) are trained with 5-fold cross-validation on 80% of data and evaluated on a held-out 20% test set, revealing that gradient-boosted trees (LightGBM, XGBoost) consistently outperform neural architectures on this structured tabular domain.

## Results

On the TEA (classification) task, LightGBM achieves the highest accuracy (80.23%), F1 score (76.16%), recall (72.97%), and AUROC (82.59%), with XGBoost closely following at 79.47% accuracy and 81.85% AUROC. On the MR (regression) task, LightGBM and XGBoost achieve the lowest MSE across most of the seven analgesic categories (e.g., ERSO MSE 0.0189, NSAID MSE 0.0674), outperforming both basic ML models and advanced neural networks. Notably, AdaCare achieves the highest AUROC (85.15%) on TEA despite lower accuracy, while Transtab performs worst overall (58.35% accuracy, F1 of only 31.70%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LightGBM dominates the TEA task, achieving 80.23% accuracy and 82.59% AUROC, outperforming XGBoost (79.47% ACC, 81.85% AUROC) by ~0.8% in accuracy and ~0.7% in AUROC. Among EHR-specific models, AdaCare achieves the highest AUROC (85.15%) but trails LightGBM by 5.2% in accuracy, while StageNet achieves the highest precision (87.25%) and F1 (78.93%) but lower overall accuracy (72.91%). General-purpose neural networks underperform: Mamba reaches 72.72% accuracy, iTransformer only 66.06%, and TransTab is the worst performer at 58.35% accuracy with a notably low F1 of 31.70%. For the MR regression task, tree-based models (LightGBM, XGBoost, Random Forest) consistently achieve the lowest MSE across all seven analgesic categories, with LightGBM posting the best ERSO MSE of 0.0189 and NSAID MSE of 0.0674. The paper concludes that gradient-boosted trees are the most reliable choice for this structured tabular EHR data, while neural networks require extensive tuning and additional architectural modifications (e.g., EHR-specific modules) to be competitive.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground in the cancer pain medication dataset space rather than building on a specific prior method. It explicitly positions itself against widely used clinical datasets (MIMIC-III/IV, eICU, FAERS, SEER, SIDER, TwoSIDES) that either focus on single hospitalization events, lack pharmacist/MDT assessments of medication rationality, or omit long-term patient follow-up and self-reported outcomes. The feature selection process draws on the Delphi consensus method previously applied in pharmaceutical care guidelines (Zhang et al., 2022), but the resulting 103-feature schema integrating in-hospital EHR data with out-of-hospital patient-reported outcomes and MDT decision rationale is novel. The empirical evaluation repurposes 13 existing ML/DL algorithms (GBDT, transformers, state-space models, EHR-specific architectures) as proof-of-concept baselines rather than introducing new methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PEACE addresses a genuine gap: no prior public dataset captures the full multidisciplinary pharmaceutical care workflow for cancer pain, including pharmacist assessments of drug-related problems (PCNE classification), medication adherence (MMAS-8), and longitudinal multi-visit trajectories (up to 33 visits per patient). However, the dataset is limited to a single tertiary hospital in China, the expert panel spans only nine provinces, and the 38K-patient scale may constrain generalizability. The models tested have not undergone clinical validation, and the authors acknowledge that blood drug concentrations and genetic/biochemical features are absent. Open questions include whether the MDT-assessment features genuinely improve predictive performance over standard EHR features, and whether the dataset's workflow-specific structure transfers to other healthcare systems.
