## Problem

Existing skinned motion retargeting methods either ignore body geometries entirely or apply a post-hoc geometry correction stage after skeletal retargeting. This two-stage approach creates an inherent conflict between preserving skeleton interaction semantics and correcting geometric artifacts, producing jittery motions, severe interpenetration, and contact mismatches. No prior method directly models dense geometric interactions among body parts in a single unified pass.

## Input / Output

The method takes a source motion sequence (global root translation in R^{T×3} and local joint rotations in 6D representation in R^{T×N×6}) along with T-pose meshes of both source and target characters, and produces the target character's joint rotation sequence. A key design choice is that the method operates across arbitrary mesh topologies without requiring vertex-level correspondences; instead, it derives 144 semantically consistent sensors per character via a fixed set of semantic coordinates (18 bones × 4 origin fractions × 4 angular directions). Training is fully unsupervised, requiring no paired retargeting ground truth.

## Method

MeshRet introduces two key representations: Semantically Consistent Sensors (SCS), which establish dense mesh correspondence across different topologies by casting rays from bone axes in a MAIT-like transform, and the Dense Mesh Interaction (DMI) field, which encodes pairwise sensor interactions as tangent-space relative positions augmented with semantic coordinates, capturing both contact and non-contact geometry. The DMI field is sparsified by restricting to critical body-part pairs and selecting L/2 nearest plus L/2 furthest target sensors per observation sensor. A PointNet-like encoder processes the DMI field (per-sensor then per-frame), a separate PointNet-like geometry encoder extracts character shape features, and a Transformer encoder-decoder predicts target joint rotations conditioned on source DMI, source geometry, and target geometry latents. Training is unsupervised with four losses: reconstruction (L2 on rotations), DMI consistency (cosine similarity between source and target DMI fields), adversarial (GAN discriminator on motion), and end-effector orientation alignment.

## Results

On the ScanRet dataset, MeshRet achieves an MSE of 0.047 and local MSE of 0.009, reducing joint error by 65%, 29%, and 8% compared to PMnet, R²ET, and SAN respectively. On the more challenging Mixamo+ setting (cartoon-to-human retargeting), MeshRet achieves a Contact Error of 0.772 and a penetration ratio of 3.45%, substantially outperforming all baselines. In the user study, approximately 81% of 600 comparative evaluations favored MeshRet over the strongest baseline (R²ET) in overall quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MeshRet outperforms all three quantitative baselines across every metric. Against PMnet [15], which ignores character geometry entirely, MeshRet reduces Contact Error on Mixamo+ from 2.716 to 0.772 (72% reduction) and penetration from 5.23% to 3.45%. Against SAN [1], also geometry-agnostic, MeshRet achieves a 68% lower Contact Error on Mixamo+ (0.772 vs. 2.432) and a 30% lower penetration ratio (3.45% vs. 4.95%). Against R²ET [32], the closest competitor that does include a geometry correction stage, MeshRet still reduces Contact Error by 65% (0.772 vs. 2.209) and penetration by 18% (3.45% vs. 4.21%), while also eliminating the jitter artifacts caused by R²ET's conflicting two-stage pipeline. A qualitative comparison with Zhang et al. [30] (vision-language-model-based retargeting) shows MeshRet matching or exceeding their results, though no quantitative comparison is provided since their code is not publicly available.

## Relation to Prior Work

MeshRet builds on the unsupervised training framework of Lim et al. [15] (PMnet) but fundamentally departs from the dominant two-stage paradigm (skeleton retargeting followed by geometry correction) exemplified by R²ET [32] and its VLM-extended variant [30]. The key conceptual shift is treating dense geometric interaction as the primary (and sole) signal for retargeting, rather than a post-hoc correction, thereby eliminating the inherent conflict between preserving skeletal semantics and fixing geometric artifacts. The SCS mechanism draws on the Medial Axis Inverse Transform [22] to establish topology-agnostic mesh correspondence, and the DMI field encoding leverages PointNet-style architectures [24] to handle the unordered pairwise sensor features.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The single-pass DMI-based formulation resolves a long-standing practical problem in animation pipelines: the jitter and oscillation that arise when skeleton retargeting and geometry correction pull in opposite directions. The ScanRet dataset (100 actors, 83 contact-rich motions, human-verified) fills a notable gap in the field, as prior benchmarks like Mixamo suffer from pervasive interpenetration and lack real human body diversity. However, the method's dependence on clean source contacts and its inability to handle missing limbs or noisy meshes limit its robustness in production settings, and future work on noise tolerance and proxy-mesh SCS extraction would broaden its applicability.
