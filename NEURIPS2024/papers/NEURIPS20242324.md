## Problem

Existing multicalibration frameworks use grouping functions defined only on covariates X, which limits their robustness guarantees to covariate shift (where Y|X is fixed). In practice, concept shift—where the conditional distribution Y|X changes across environments due to spurious correlations, missing variables, or confounding—is far more prevalent, especially in tabular data. The paper asks whether multicalibration can be extended to capture concept shift and whether it provides a practical optimization pathway to invariant prediction.

## Input / Output

The method takes as input an already-trained predictor f₀ (from ERM or any supervised method), a dataset D, a predictor function class F (e.g., MLP or ResNet), and a grouping function class H. In the multi-environment setting, H is parameterized as linear combinations of environment posterior probabilities p_S(e|x,y) estimated by a classifier; in the single-environment setting, H is built from squared residuals of an identification model f_id. The predictor is discretized into m bins (chosen so that 90% of samples fall in bins with ≥30 samples). The output is a refined predictor f' that is certified α-approximately multicalibrated (and hence invariant) with respect to H, producing scalar regression predictions.

## Method

The core theoretical contribution is extending multicalibration to joint grouping functions h(x,y) defined on both covariates and outcomes, and proving equivalence between this extended multicalibration and invariance (IRM's objective) under concept shift. The maximal grouping function class for any predictor is shown to be a linear space spanned by density ratios of target distributions where the predictor is invariant, yielding an efficient parameterization. The algorithm MC-Pseudolabel is a post-processing loop: at each iteration, it discretizes the current predictor, performs supervised regression with grouping functions on each level set to generate pseudolabels, then retrains the model using those pseudolabels. Unlike LSBoost (which reduces to monotonic risk decrease), the projection step of retraining with pseudolabels disrupts risk monotonicity, enabling a trade-off between accuracy and invariance. The algorithm introduces no extra hyperparameters, requires only linear regression overhead per bin (parallelizable), and achieves certified multicalibration upon convergence, with convergence proven for multivariate Gaussian data.

## Results

On ACSIncome (multi-environment, RMSE↓), MC-Pseudolabel achieves 0.428±0.009 under in-distribution validation and 0.425±0.012 under worst-environment validation, outperforming all 10 baselines (best baseline: CLOvE at 0.455/0.454). On PovertyMap (multi-environment, Worst-U/R Pearson↑), MC-Pseudolabel reaches 0.50±0.06 across all three validation criteria, surpassing the next-best methods (MRI at 0.47 ID, CLOvE at 0.46 ID). On VesselPower (single-environment, RMSE↓), MC-Pseudolabel achieves 1.61±0.20 under ID validation, beating the best baselines (CVaR and χ²-DRO at 1.69). Under oracle validation, MC-Pseudolabel remains competitive, with CLOvE slightly ahead on PovertyMap (0.51 vs 0.50) and CVaR slightly ahead on VesselPower (1.49 vs 1.52).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MC-Pseudolabel dominates all invariance-learning baselines (IRM, MIP, IB-IRM, CLOvE, MRI, REX, Fishr) on ACSIncome under both ID and Worst validation, with a margin of 0.027–0.059 RMSE over the best (CLOvE at 0.455 ID). On PovertyMap, it outperforms all invariance methods by 0.03–0.48 Pearson under ID/Worst validation, with CLOvE being the closest competitor (0.46 ID vs 0.50). Under oracle validation, CLOvE slightly edges MC-Pseudolabel on PovertyMap (0.51 vs 0.50), while MC-Pseudolabel leads on ACSIncome (0.411 vs CLOvE's 0.450). For the single-environment VesselPower setting, MC-Pseudolabel (1.61 ID) beats all reweighting and DRO baselines (CVaR 1.69, JTT 1.75, Tilted-ERM 1.72, χ²-DRO 1.69, C-Mixup 1.72), though CVaR achieves a marginally better oracle result (1.49 vs 1.52). IDGM (gradient matching) fails catastrophically on both datasets, suggesting its alignment objective is incompatible with concept shift. The key practical advantage is that MC-Pseudolabel's superiority is most pronounced under ID and Worst validation—settings where target data is unavailable—making it the most deployable method without oracle access.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends multicalibration (Hébert-Johnson et al. 2018; Globus-Harris et al. 2023) by allowing grouping functions to depend jointly on covariates X and outcomes Y, rather than X alone. This extension enables an equivalence with Invariant Risk Minimization (Arjovsky et al. 2019), which prior work (Wald et al. 2021) had only established for exact calibration in a multi-environment setting. Algorithmically, MC-Pseudolabel generalizes LSBoost (Globus-Harris et al. 2023) from covariate-based to joint grouping functions, replacing the monotonic risk-decrease guarantee with a projection step that trades accuracy for invariance. The paper positions itself as a unifying framework where density ratios parameterize grouping functions, subsuming multi-environment learning and hard-sample learning as special cases.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The equivalence between extended multicalibration and invariance provides a principled, model-agnostic optimization framework for OOD generalization that avoids the heavy bi-level or multi-objective optimization used by IRM and its variants. The absence of extra hyperparameters is practically significant because it sidesteps the model-selection challenge that plagues OOD methods when target validation data is unavailable. Limitations include restriction to regression tasks, convergence guarantees only under Gaussian assumptions, and the need for predictor discretization. Open questions include extending the framework to classification, establishing convergence for non-Gaussian data, and understanding how the accuracy-invariance tradeoff (Theorem 4.3) manifests in high-dimensional settings.
