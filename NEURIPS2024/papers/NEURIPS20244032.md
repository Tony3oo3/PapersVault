## Problem

Second-order optimizers like Shampoo maintain preconditioner matrices and their inverse roots that consume substantial GPU memory, limiting the maximum model size trainable within a fixed budget. While quantization has successfully compressed first-order optimizer states to 4-bit, it has never been applied to second-order optimizers because their states involve matrix-level operations rather than elementwise updates. The central challenge is that naively quantizing the preconditioner at 4-bit precision drastically alters its small singular values, causing large errors in the inverse 4-th root that degrade training performance.

## Input / Output

The method consumes gradient matrices G_t ∈ R^{m×n} and model parameter tensors W_t ∈ R^{m×n} at each step, maintaining optimizer states in compressed 4-bit form. Preconditioners L_t and R_t are stored as pairs (λ, Ū) where λ is the singular value vector and Ū is the 4-bit quantized eigenvector matrix, while inverse 4-th roots are stored as (a, Ā) where a is the diagonal vector and Ā is the 4-bit quantized off-diagonal portion. This representation is approximately 7× smaller in memory than the full 32-bit matrices, with the quantized eigenvector matrices being the dominant storage component.

## Method

The key insight is to quantize the eigenvector matrix U from the SVD of the preconditioner (A = UΛU^T) rather than the preconditioner A itself, since the inverse 4-th root is highly sensitive to perturbations in small singular values that 4-bit quantization of A introduces. After dequantization, Björck orthonormalization (one iteration for preconditioner updates, four for inverse root computation) rectifies the orthogonality of the quantized eigenvector matrix, which both improves approximation quality and enables rapid computation of A^s = VΛ^sV^T without expensive Schur-Newton iteration. Linear square (Linear-2) quantization mapping is used instead of dynamic tree quantization, as it yields marginally lower quantization errors at 4-bit precision. Theoretically, the paper proves that perturbing U yields normwise relative error and angle error bounds in A^s that are independent of the singular values Λ and the exponent s, whereas perturbing A's singular values can produce arbitrarily large errors when the condition number is high.

## Results

On ImageNet-1k, 4-bit Shampoo achieves 76.92% top-1 accuracy with ResNet50+SGDM (vs. 77.07% for 32-bit Shampoo and 76.70% for SGDM alone) and 74.78% with ViT-Base/32+AdamW (vs. 75.03% for 32-bit Shampoo and 72.87% for AdamW alone). On CIFAR-100, 4-bit Shampoo matches or slightly exceeds 32-bit Shampoo across all architectures (e.g., 74.74% vs. 74.54% for VGG19+SGDM, 77.22% vs. 77.50% for ViT-Small+AdamW). Memory savings range from 4.5% to 41% compared to 32-bit Shampoo, while wall-clock time increases by at most 9.5%. Compared to first-order baselines, 4-bit Shampoo incurs only a 0.8%–12.7% memory overhead while delivering 0.2–2.4% higher accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against first-order baselines (SGDM, AdamW), 4-bit Shampoo consistently achieves higher test accuracy (e.g., +0.2% on ResNet50/ImageNet-1k, +2.4% on ViT-Base/32/ImageNet-1k) at the cost of only 0.8%–12.7% additional memory. Against 32-bit Shampoo, 4-bit Shampoo matches accuracy within ±0.7% while reducing memory by 4.5%–41% (e.g., 11396 MB vs. 11937 MB for ResNet50/ImageNet-1k; 10804 MB vs. 12134 MB for ViT-Base/32/ImageNet-1k). In the ablation on other second-order optimizers (Table 4), 4-bit K-FAC, 4-bit AdaBK, and 4-bit CASPR all match their 32-bit counterparts in accuracy (within 0.02–0.36%) while reducing memory by over 20% (e.g., 1878.3 MB vs. 2388.0 MB for K-FAC and AdaBK on Swin-Tiny/CIFAR-100). The 4-bit approach also slightly outperforms 32-bit Shampoo in some cases (e.g., VGG19/CIFAR-100: 74.74% vs. 74.54%; ResNet34/Tiny-ImageNet: 67.95% vs. 67.90%), suggesting the quantization noise does not systematically degrade optimization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the quantization-based compression of optimizer states—previously applied only to first-order optimizers (Dettmers et al., ICLR 2022; Li et al., NeurIPS 2023)—to second-order optimizers, specifically Shampoo. The quantization framework (block-wise normalization, quantizer/dequantizer formulation) is directly inherited from the 8-bit and 4-bit first-order optimizer literature. The key conceptual departure is the insight that quantizing the eigenvector matrix of the preconditioner, rather than the preconditioner itself, dramatically reduces quantization error in the inverse 4-th root, a distinction that has no analogue in first-order settings where states are element-wise. The approach also draws on randomized SVD for efficient eigendecomposition and Björck orthonormalization to rectify quantization-induced loss of orthogonality.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Second-order optimizers have been largely impractical for large-scale training due to the memory cost of storing preconditioners and their inverse roots; 4-bit Shampoo reduces this overhead by roughly 7× for the quantized states while maintaining near-identical convergence to 32-bit Shampoo across CNN and transformer architectures on multiple benchmarks. The approach generalizes to K-FAC, AdaBK, and CASPR, suggesting broad applicability across the second-order optimizer family. Key limitations include: the eigenvector matrix is not symmetric (so 4-bit eigenvector storage equals 8-bit preconditioner storage in raw bytes), evaluation is restricted to models up to ~100M parameters, and the method has not been validated on billion-parameter LLMs where memory pressure is most acute. An open question is whether the eigenvector-quantization strategy remains advantageous at extreme condition numbers encountered in very large or ill-conditioned models.
