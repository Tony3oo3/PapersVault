## Problem

Existing re-weighting methods for imbalanced learning (inverse frequency, effective number, effective area) assign static class weights that do not adapt as the model's learned feature distribution evolves during training. The paper identifies that the discrepancy between the actual data distribution and the ideal balanced distribution is itself dynamic, yet no prior method recalibrates weights in response to this shifting gap, leaving a fundamental limitation in how re-weighting-based losses can close the generalization bound.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100-LT, 224×224 for ImageNet-LT and Places-LT) through a CNN backbone (ResNet-32, ResNet-50, or pre-trained ResNet-152) and outputs a class label via a linear classifier. Unusually, the per-sample re-weighting is computed on the intermediate feature vectors (dimension K) produced by the backbone within each minibatch, rather than on raw pixel inputs, and the weights are normalized with a temperature exponent γ before being applied to the loss.

## Method

RDR estimates the density ratio r(x|y;ω) = P̄(x|y;ω)/P_bal(x|y;ω) between the current feature distribution and a momentum-tracked balanced prototype F_{P_bal} using first-order moment matching in the learned feature space. For each class i, the ratio is solved in closed form as r̂_i = n_i(Φ_P^iᵀΦ_P^i)⁻¹Φ_P^iᵀF_i, where Φ_P^i collects the feature vectors of class i in the current minibatch and F_i is the per-class momentum prototype updated via F ← mF + (1−m)z̄ each batch. The resulting per-sample weight w_i = r̂_i/n_i is applied to the loss (CE, LA, or SAM), so that as the backbone's features shift during training, the weights automatically re-balance the contribution of each class. A warm-up phase (first T₀ epochs use uniform weights) and a temperature coefficient γ stabilize early training, and the overall computational overhead is O(dK³) per minibatch, negligible relative to the forward/backward pass.

## Results

On the four long-tailed benchmarks, RDR+LA achieves the best overall top-1 accuracy across all settings: 90.2% on CIFAR-10-LT (IF=10), 83.4% on CIFAR-10-LT (IF=100), 62.9% on CIFAR-100-LT (IF=10), 49.4% on CIFAR-100-LT (IF=100), 48.1% on ImageNet-LT, and 39.5% on Places-LT. The gains are most pronounced under severe imbalance, where RDR+LA improves over the strongest static baseline (LA) by 1.2% on CIFAR-10-LT (IF=100) and by 2.0% on Places-LT. On CIFAR-10-LT (IF=100), the Few-class accuracy increases by 19.8% over CE and by 9.5% over LA when using RDR+CE and RDR+LA respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RDR+LA outperforms all six baselines (CE, Focal, CB, LA, and the SAM variants) on every dataset and imbalance setting. Against the strongest static re-weighting baseline LA, RDR+LA gains 1.2% on CIFAR-10-LT (IF=100), 1.2% on CIFAR-100-LT (IF=100), 0.2% on ImageNet-LT, and 2.0% on Places-LT. Compared to the SAM-based methods (ImbSAM, CCSAM) in Tables 2 and 4, RDR+SAM achieves higher overall accuracy while maintaining stronger Many-class performance—e.g., on CIFAR-10-LT (IF=100, CE loss), RDR+SAM reaches 83.6% overall versus 81.4% for CCSAM, which sacrifices Many-class accuracy (85.6% vs. 91.8%) to boost Few-class scores. On Places-LT, RDR+LA (39.5%) surpasses LA (37.5%) by 2.0% and CB (32.5%) by 7.0%, demonstrating that the dynamic density-ratio weighting is particularly effective on large-scale, highly imbalanced data (IF=996). The ablation in Table 5 confirms that the dynamic RDR weighting consistently outperforms classic inverse-frequency weighting (1/n) by 0.3–4.3% depending on dataset and loss function.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper revives the re-weighting paradigm for imbalanced learning by grounding it in the generalization bound from Wang et al. (NeurIPS 2023), which showed that re-weighting effectiveness depends on how well weights counteract the imbalanced term √π_y[1−softmax(B_y(m))]. Unlike prior static re-weighting methods (inverse frequency, class-balanced loss, effective-area weighting) that fix weights based on dataset statistics, RDR computes sample-wise weights dynamically via density ratio estimation between the learned feature distribution and a momentum-tracked balanced prototype. The method departs from the broader imbalanced-learning literature (decoupled learning, margin-based methods, SAM variants) by arguing that the core deficiency of re-weighting was not the paradigm itself but the use of fixed, distribution-agnostic coefficients that fail to track evolving feature geometry during training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RDR offers a lightweight, plug-and-play re-weighting mechanism that consistently improves over both simple baselines and more complex SAM-based methods across four large-scale long-tailed benchmarks, with particularly strong gains on severely imbalanced settings (IF=100, IF=500) and the Places-LT dataset. The Hessian analysis (58–66% reduction in λ_max, 55–66% reduction in Tr(H) for minority classes) provides mechanistic evidence that dynamic re-weighting flattens the loss landscape, linking the method to generalization theory. Open questions include scalability to very large class spaces (the O(dK³) matrix inversion cost), whether the momentum-based balanced prototype is the optimal surrogate for P_bal, and how the method interacts with emerging self-supervised or contrastive pre-training pipelines for long-tailed recognition.
