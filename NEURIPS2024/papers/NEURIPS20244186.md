## Problem

Standard L^p-norm adversarial training fails to improve robustness against certain common image corruptions (notably contrast and fog), because L^p distances lack semantic meaning: a contrast-corrupted image and a random noisy perturbation at the same Euclidean distance are treated identically, even though humans perceive them as fundamentally different. Existing perceptual metrics like LPIPS operate in latent feature spaces and require approximation or relaxation to define the adversarial neighborhood, making them difficult to integrate into a principled optimization framework for adversarial training.

## Input / Output

The method operates directly on raw pixel-space RGB images (32×32×3, n=3072 for CIFAR-10; 256×256 for ImageNet evaluation) without any encoder. For divergence training, each batch contains a clean image, one corrupted variant (e.g., contrast-shifted), and m=63 Gaussian-noisy perturbations sampled at a distance proportional to the corruption's L² norm. For adversarial training, the input is a clean image with its ground-truth label, and the output is an adversarial example constrained to lie within the learned Bregman ball B_φ(x, ε), which is then used to update a PreAct ResNet-18 classifier. The learned divergence D_φ(x'∥x) is computed as φ(x') − φ(x) − ⟨Ψ(x), x'−x⟩, where Ψ is the numerically approximated gradient of the ICNN.

## Method

The paper learns a strongly convex base function φ using an Input Convex Neural Network (ICNN) with 12 convolutional and 4 fully connected layers, where non-negative weights and convex non-decreasing activations (Hadamard square and CELU) guarantee convexity by construction, and an added (α/2)‖x‖² term ensures α-strong convexity. A self-supervised Bregman loss l_B trains φ so that D_φ(τ(x)∥x) < D_φ(x̃ⁱ∥x) for all noisy samples, effectively a cross-entropy over negative divergences with the corrupted image as the positive class. The inverse mirror map (∇φ)⁻¹ is approximated by training a second ICNN as the Fenchel conjugate φ̄ via the constraint ‖Ψ̄(Ψ(x)) − x‖₂. For adversarial training, PGD is replaced by mirror descent: each step maps to the dual space via Ψ, takes a gradient step on the classifier loss, maps back via Ψ̄, and projects into the Bregman ball using a binary search along the segment between x and the candidate point. This yields a semantically meaningful threat model where the Bregman ball naturally contains realistic corruptions while excluding random noise, even at large L² radii where standard PGD fails to converge.

## Results

On CIFAR-10-C, the mirror descent AT using the learned Bregman divergence achieves 90.03% average accuracy on contrast corruption (vs. 63.92% for PGD and 62.87% for RLAT), a 27.16% improvement over PGD. For fog corruption, the best MD variant reaches 90.87% (vs. 77.47% PGD, 77.00% RLAT), a ~13% gain. Clean accuracy is preserved at 94–95% across all MD variants, close to the 95.12% standard-training baseline. On the BAPPS 2AFC task, the learned BD outperforms LPIPS in 5 of 6 categories (e.g., 63.65% vs. 51.41% on Traditional distortions).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against three AT baselines (PGD, RLAT/LPIPS, and standard training) and three metric-learning similarity measures (Dino, Unicom, Moco v2). On contrast corruption — the most challenging case identified in prior work — both PGD (63.92%) and RLAT (62.87%) fail to improve over standard training (78.13%), while the mirror descent AT with the zoom-blur BD achieves 90.03%, a 27.16% gain over PGD. For fog, the best MD variant (90.87%) surpasses PGD (77.47%) and RLAT (77.00%) by ~13%. On zoom blur, RLAT (85.88%) and PGD (85.87%) actually outperform all MD variants (79.16–81.84%), indicating the learned BDs do not universally dominate. In the similarity-measure comparison (Fig. 4), the learned BD yields a noise-to-corruption ratio of 4.15–726.5 across noise levels, far exceeding Dino (0.09–0.98), Unicom (0.58–1.05), and Moco v2 (0.71–1.02), confirming superior semantic discrimination. On BAPPS 2AFC, the BD beats LPIPS in 5/6 categories but loses on Frame Interpolation (53.73% vs. 55.55%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of Bregman divergence theory (Bregman 1967), the mirror descent optimization framework (Nemirovskij & Yudin 1983), and adversarial training (Madry et al. 2018). It departs from the dominant paradigm of corruption robustness, which either uses Lp-norm PGD in pixel space or LPIPS-based similarity in latent feature space (Kireev et al. 2022, Laidlaw et al. 2021), by learning the divergence directly in pixel space via an input convex neural network (ICNN, Amos et al. 2017) that guarantees convexity by construction. Prior work on learning Bregman divergences (Siahkamari et al. 2020, Cilingir et al. 2020, Rezaei et al. 2023) relied on piecewise linear approximations or low-dimensional extracted features; this is the first end-to-end learning of a BD on raw high-dimensional pixel data where the mirror descent and Bregman ball machinery can be directly instantiated for adversarial training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a theoretically grounded alternative to LPIPS-based adversarial training, importing the compactness guarantees of Bregman balls and the principled mirror descent update rule in place of heuristic projections. The 27% accuracy improvement on contrast corruption—where both PGD and RLAT fail—demonstrates that the learned divergence captures semantically meaningful structure that Lp and LPIPS miss. However, the evidence base is limited: experiments are confined to CIFAR-10 (32×32) on a single V100 GPU, only 10 of 14 corruptions yielded a successfully trained BD, no error bars are reported, and the projection step relies on a binary-search heuristic without optimality guarantees. Scaling to larger architectures, datasets, and image resolutions, along with better projection methods, are the most pressing open questions for the approach to have broader practical impact.
