## Problem

Safe RL methods (both primal-based like CRPO/PCRPO and primal-dual-based like CUP/PPOLag) use a fixed sample size per iteration, wasting computational resources in simple scenarios and under-exploring in complex ones with conflicting reward-safety objectives. No prior work dynamically adapts sample size based on the optimization landscape, and PCRPO—the three-mode framework this paper builds on—lacks any convergence or sample complexity analysis.

## Input / Output

The method operates in a tabular CMDP with finite state and action spaces, parameterizing the policy via softmax over |S|×|A| weights. At each iteration it consumes the current policy's estimated reward and cost Q-functions (obtained via policy evaluation with a dynamically sized batch) and the angle between the reward and cost gradients. It outputs updated policy weights and an adjusted sample size X_{t+1} for the next iteration, where X_{t+1} = X(1+ζ⁺) under gradient conflict or X(1+ζ⁻) under gradient alignment.

## Method

ESPO combines a three-mode optimization scheme (cost-only on hard safety violation, simultaneous reward+cost in a soft constraint band, reward-only when safe) with a gradient-conflict-driven sample manipulation rule: when the angle θ_{r,c} between reward and cost gradients exceeds 90°, the batch size is increased to gather more informative samples for resolving the conflict; when θ_{r,c} ≤ 90°, the batch size is decreased since aligned objectives are easier to optimize. In the conflict mode, gradient projection (CAGrad-style) is applied to decompose each gradient into its component orthogonal to the other, ensuring the update direction improves both objectives simultaneously. The paper proves a convergence rate of Õ(√(SA/((1−γ)³T))) for both reward suboptimality and constraint violation, shows ESPO stays in the safe region once entered (reducing oscillation vs. CRPO from 35.6% to 99.4% reward-optimizing iterations), and establishes that the adaptive sampling strictly reduces total samples needed to reach a target accuracy ε compared to fixed-size baselines.

## Results

On the Safety-MuJoCo benchmark, ESPO requires 5.1M–6.2M samples versus 8M for both CRPO and PCRPO across SafetyReacher-v4, SafetyWalker-v4, and SafetyHumanoidStandup-v4, achieving 25–36% sample reduction while improving reward and constraint satisfaction. On the Omnisafe benchmark, ESPO needs 7.3M–7.6M samples versus 10M for PCPO, CUP, and PPOLag on SafetyHopperVelocity-v1 and SafetyAntVelocity-v1 (24–27% reduction). Training time is reduced by 21–38% compared to baselines. ESPO also spends 99.4% of iterations optimizing the reward objective within the safe region, compared to only 35.6% for CRPO, indicating substantially reduced oscillation across the safety boundary.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primal-based baselines CRPO and PCRPO on Safety-MuJoCo, ESPO consistently achieves higher reward, better constraint satisfaction, and 25–36% fewer samples (e.g., 5.7M vs. 8M on SafetyReacher-v4). Against the primal-dual baselines on Omnisafe, ESPO outperforms PCPO in reward while matching its safety compliance, and uses 24–27% fewer samples (7.3M vs. 10M). CUP and PPOLag both struggle to maintain safety on SafetyHopperVelocity-v1 and SafetyAntVelocity-v1, whereas ESPO satisfies constraints while achieving superior reward. In all five comparisons, ESPO wins on at least reward and sample efficiency, and additionally wins on safety against CUP and PPOLag. The sample manipulation mechanism is the primary driver of the efficiency gains, as confirmed by ablation studies showing that removing it degrades both sample count and reward performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends PCRPO's three-mode optimization framework (reward-only, simultaneous reward+cost, cost-only) by introducing gradient-conflict-driven adaptive sample size manipulation, a concept borrowed from adaptive stochastic optimization but applied to safe RL for the first time. The convergence analysis is built on CRPO's primal-based framework, and the paper provides the first theoretical guarantees for PCRPO's three-mode scheme, which previously lacked formal analysis. Unlike primal-dual methods (CUP, PPOLag, PCPO) that require dual multiplier tuning, ESPO remains in the primal paradigm while adding a dynamic sampling layer that adjusts batch size based on the angle between reward and cost gradients.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Sample efficiency is a critical practical bottleneck for safe RL deployment in safety-critical domains like autonomous driving and robotics, where each environment interaction carries real-world cost. The 25–29% sample reduction and 21–38% training time savings are meaningful for practitioners, and the theoretical results (convergence rate, oscillation reduction, sample complexity) provide the first rigorous justification for PCRPO's three-mode design. However, the theoretical analysis is restricted to tabular MDPs with softmax policies, and the sample manipulation parameters (ζ⁺, ζ⁻) require manual tuning; extending these guarantees to function approximation and off-policy settings remains open. The paper also notes that sample reduction does not always translate proportionally to wall-clock time savings due to parallelism and task-specific factors.
