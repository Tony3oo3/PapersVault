## Problem

Conventional LLM unlearning methods that maximize forget loss and minimize retain loss suffer from two unresolved challenges: degenerated (nonsensical) outputs caused by the unbounded forget loss, and catastrophic forgetting of retain knowledge caused by the small, under-representative retain set. The fundamental crux is that what the LLM should remember is far more intractable than what it needs to forget, making direct optimization on the target LLM inherently unstable.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained target LLM (e.g., Llama-2-7B or Mistral-7B), a set of forget documents D_f, and a small set of retain documents D_r. At inference, both the frozen target LLM and the lightweight assistant LLM process the same text prompt, and the unlearned output is produced by subtracting the assistant's logits from the target's logits (scaled by a hyperparameter α), yielding generated text that has forgotten the target knowledge while preserving all other capabilities. The assistant LLM shares the target's vocabulary and is constructed from only the first K transformer layers (K=8 out of 32) plus LoRA adapters, requiring just ~20M trainable parameters.

## Method

ULD reverses the conventional unlearning objectives by training an assistant LLM to do the opposite: memorize the forget documents (minimizing cross-entropy on D_f) and output a uniform distribution on retain documents (maximizing cross-entropy against a uniform target on D_r). The assistant is built by extracting the first K layers of the target LLM and adding LoRA adapters (r=32, α=32), with all inherent parameters frozen so only ~20M LoRA parameters are trained. At inference, the unlearned LLM's logits are computed as l_f(Y|X) = l(Y|X;θ) − α·l_a(Y|X;φ), where the subtraction lowers the probability of forget-knowledge tokens while leaving retain-knowledge tokens unchanged (since the assistant outputs a flat distribution there). This reversal naturally resolves both challenges: the forget loss is now a bounded CE minimization rather than an unbounded maximization, and the assistant's task of forgetting retain data is trivially easy even with limited retain documents, eliminating catastrophic forgetting. Data augmentation via GPT-3.5-turbo paraphrasing of forget documents and perturbation of answers further improves generalization and prevents overfitting.

## Results

On the TOFU benchmark, ULD achieves forget quality (F.Q.) of 0.99, 0.73, and 0.52 on TOFU-1%, 5%, and 10% settings respectively, while maintaining model utility (M.U.) at 0.62 across all settings — identical to the original LLM (0% utility loss). The most competitive baseline, NPO, achieves F.Q. of 0.66, 0.68, and 0.09 but sacrifices 17% of model utility on average. On HarryPotter, ULD retains near-original perplexity (9.95 vs. 9.81) and average benchmark accuracy (66.85% vs. 66.93%), while the best baseline (NPO+GD) degrades to 14.85 PPL and 61.77% accuracy. ULD also reduces training time by more than 3× compared to the most efficient baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ULD outperforms all four baseline families on both forget quality and model utility across all TOFU settings. Against the gradient-ascent and DPO objectives from Yao et al. [9] and Maini et al. [10], ULD achieves substantially higher F.Q. (e.g., 0.52 vs. 2e-4 for GA+KL on TOFU-10%) while preserving full model utility (0.62 vs. 0.05). Against NPO from Zhang et al. [11], ULD wins decisively on TOFU-10% (F.Q. 0.52 vs. 0.09) and maintains 0.62 M.U. versus NPO's 0.26, though NPO+GD is competitive on TOFU-1% (0.58 F.Q.) at the cost of 8% utility loss. Against the Offset unlearning method from Huang et al. [19], which also uses an assistant LLM with logit difference but trains it with conventional objectives, ULD achieves higher F.Q. (0.52 vs. 4e-5 on TOFU-10%) and higher M.U. (0.62 vs. 0.48), confirming that the reversed training objective — not merely the logit-subtraction architecture — is the key differentiator.

## Relation to Prior Work

ULD builds on the logit-subtraction / model-arithmetic framework from contrastive decoding (Li et al. 2022; Chuang et al. 2023; Liu et al. 2021), repurposing it for unlearning rather than decoding-time control. It departs from the mainstream LLM unlearning line (GA, DPO, NPO objectives on the target LLM; Yao et al. 2023; Maini et al. 2024; Zhang et al. 2024) by inverting the optimization direction: instead of maximizing forget loss on the target model, it minimizes a bounded cross-entropy forget loss on a small assistant sub-network. A concurrent work (Huang et al. 2024, 'Offset unlearning') also uses an assistant LLM with logit difference but retains conventional unlearning objectives, which the paper shows still suffers from degeneration and catastrophic forgetting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical impact is that ULD achieves 0% model-utility loss on TOFU (vs. ~17% for the best baseline) while cutting training time by over 3×, making LLM unlearning substantially more tractable for privacy and copyright compliance. The conceptual insight—that flipping the optimization sign on a small assistant sub-network naturally bounds both the forget and retain objectives—resolves two long-standing failure modes (degenerated output, catastrophic forgetting) without heuristic target distributions. Open questions include whether the approach scales beyond 7B models, whether inference-time latency from the assistant can be fully mitigated, and whether the data-augmentation requirement (paraphrasing via GPT-3.5) generalizes to domains where automatic paraphrase is difficult.
