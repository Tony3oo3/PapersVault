## Problem

Existing multilingual web corpora (OSCAR, mC4, CC-100) cover at most ~200 languages, and the LID models they rely on (FastText, CLD3) suffer from hash collisions and limited language coverage, producing systematic errors for minority languages. No open, reproducible pipeline exists that simultaneously covers 1000+ minority languages, handles web noise robustly, and produces a rigorously cleaned document-level corpus.

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes raw CommonCrawl snapshots (CC-MAIN-2024-10 in full, plus parts of 2023-40 and 2023-50) and produces a 2TB document-level corpus of 684.7M documents across 1275 language-script pairs. Each document carries rich metadata: GlotLID probability scores, LID consistency score, script consistency score, 14 quality-warning flags, content class (UT1 + wikipedia/religious), py-tlsh hash, and PII-replaced text. The LID model itself takes raw text and outputs one of 2102 labels (language-script pairs plus 157 'und' script labels and 3 'zxx' noise labels), operating on UTF-8 character n-grams of length 2–5.

## Method

GlotLID v3.0 is a FastText-based supervised classifier over character n-grams (minn=2, maxn=5, bucket=10⁶, dim=256, 1 epoch) extended to 2000+ labels by adding minority-language training data, 157 'und' labels for unseen scripts (trained on randomly generated character strings from those scripts to enable proper rejection), and 3 'zxx' labels for six noise types (mis-rendered PDFs, Mojibake Latin/Arabic, binary files, ANT SPEAK, replacement characters) with synthetically or web-scraped training data. The pipeline builds on Ungoliant/OSCAR, replacing its 176-language FastText LID with GlotLID v3.0 and adding 14 quality warnings (tiny, short sentences, header/footer, LID-inconsistent, script-inconsistent, list case, technical characters, cursed regex, repetition, long word, lorem ipsum, policy, JS warning, curly bracket, adult words) that trigger sentence-level removal. Script-aware labeling (ISO 639-3 + ISO 15924, e.g., rus-Cyrl) constrains admissible language-script combinations via GlotScript-T/R, and a self-audit of 653 randomly sampled languages confirms 0.93 macro-average in-language accuracy.

## Results

GlotLID v3.0 achieves an F1 of 0.991 with a false positive rate of 0.000003 on GlotTest (2,102 labels), 0.882 F1 on UDHR (371 labels), and 0.967 F1 on FLORES-200 (199 labels). The three lowest per-language F1 scores on GlotTest are 0.72, 0.75, and 0.76 for the mutually intelligible BCMS varieties (bos_Latn, hrv_Latn, cnr_Latn). The resulting GlotCC v1.0 corpus contains 684.7M documents (512.6B words) across 1,275 language-script labels, with a self-audit macro-average in-language score of 0.93 and median of 1.0 over 653 sampled languages.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GlotLID v3.0 improves over its predecessor GlotLID v1.0 by +0.05 F1 on GlotTest, +0.09 on UDHR, and +0.05 on FLORES-200, while also expanding coverage from 1,665 to over 2,000 labels. In a direct head-to-head comparison with NLLB LID on 20 minority-language pages where the two systems disagree (Table 5), GlotLID is correct 13 times where NLLB LID erred (3 misclassifications + 10 misses), while NLLB LID is correct 5 times where GlotLID erred (3 incorrect calls + 2 incorrect misses); both systems are wrong on 2 pages. The paper attributes GlotLID's advantage to its substantially larger label set (2,000+ vs. NLLB's ~200), which allows it to make more calls without sacrificing overall accuracy. The lower UDHR F1 (0.882 vs. 0.991 on GlotTest) is attributed to domain shift, as GlotLID's training data for some UDHR languages is dominated by religious texts.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Ungoliant/OSCAR pipeline for CommonCrawl text extraction by replacing its 176-language FastText LID with GlotLID v3.0, a 2000+ label model built on the same FastText character n-gram architecture. The key conceptual departure from prior LID work (FastText, CLD3, NLLB LID) is treating language identification as an open-set problem: the model explicitly rejects unseen scripts via 157 'und' labels and six noise types via 'zxx' labels, rather than forcing a closed-set prediction. This builds directly on the authors' prior GlotLID v1.0 (EMNLP 2023) and on GlotScript for script-level consistency checking, while the corpus creation pipeline follows the OSCAR/Ungoliant lineage with additional quality-warning filters adapted from MADLAD-400 and FineWeb.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GlotCC is the first fully open-source, document-level web corpus covering 1000+ languages, filling a critical gap: prior open corpora (OSCAR, CulturaX, mC4) cover at most ~200 languages, and the trend in state-of-the-art LLMs is toward fewer supported languages. The practical impact is significant for minority-language NLP, but the paper itself acknowledges it has not trained or evaluated any language model on the data, leaving open whether the corpus actually improves downstream performance. Limitations include the absence of math/code content, a bias toward monolingual documents due to LID-consistency filtering, and the fact that aggressive filtering reduces total document counts for high-resource languages compared to OSCAR.
