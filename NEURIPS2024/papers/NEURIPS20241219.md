## Problem

Online finetuning of Decision Transformers (DTs) is surprisingly under-explored, and the current state-of-the-art Online Decision Transformer (ODT) struggles when pretrained with low-reward offline data. The paper identifies that conditioning on a high Return-To-Go (RTG) far from the expected return hampers the online finetuning process, as the DT yields ∂a/∂RTG while local policy improvement requires the inverse ∂RTG/∂a, which the transformer cannot easily provide.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241219/cropped_page3_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a trajectory context window of interleaved RTG, state, and action tokens (with context length randomly sampled during training from a near-uniform distribution over [1, T_train]) plus a target RTG for conditioning, and produces a deterministic or stochastic action. A key architectural detail is that the critic is reflective (state-only MLP), effectively serving as an average over policies generated at different context lengths, which the authors show is more stable than a recurrent critic for transformer-based value estimation.

## Method

The core contribution is adding a weighted TD3 actor loss (coefficient α=0.1) to the standard ODT supervised learning objective during online finetuning, while training two MLP critic networks with standard TD3 clipped double Q-learning loss. The actor loss combines the negative Q-value of the DT's predicted action with the behavior cloning term, so the DT is simultaneously pulled toward high-value actions and toward imitating high-RTG trajectories. The theoretical analysis, building on Brandfonbrener et al.'s performance bound, proves that 1/α_f grows as Ω(RTG_eval²) for discrete RTG distributions (or Ω(RTG_eval^1.5) for continuous), showing the bound degrades superlinearly when conditioning on RTGs far from the data support. This explains why ODT cannot improve locally from low-RTG rollouts, and motivates the RL gradient as a 'vitamin' that provides the missing ∂RTG/∂a direction for local policy improvement.

## Results

On MuJoCo (12 environments), TD3+ODT achieves an average normalized reward of 88.38 (+46.59 improvement over pretraining), outperforming TD3 (87.64), TD3+BC (68.52), IQL (55.9), ODT (55.14), PDT (41.97), and DDPG+ODT (22.87). On Adroit (12 environments), TD3+ODT achieves 87.84 (+33.09), dramatically outperforming IQL (61.14), ODT (38.73), and TD3+BC (6.6). On Antmaze (6 environments), TD3+ODT achieves 62.11 (+34.1) on umaze and medium mazes, while IQL (73.83) performs best on large mazes. The method's advantage is most pronounced on low-reward (random) datasets, where ODT and PDT fail to improve at all while TD3+ODT gains 40–74 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TD3+ODT outperforms ODT by a large margin on MuJoCo (88.38 vs. 55.14 average) and Adroit (87.84 vs. 38.73), with the gap widening dramatically on random datasets where ODT gains only +9.82 on Walker2d-R versus +53.27 for TD3+ODT. Against PDT, TD3+ODT wins on MuJoCo (88.38 vs. 41.97) and Adroit (87.84 vs. 25.25), confirming that simply changing what to predict (future trajectory info vs. RTG) does not solve the low-reward finetuning problem. TD3+BC, the MLP-based offline-to-online baseline, is consistently outperformed: 88.38 vs. 68.52 on MuJoCo and 87.84 vs. 6.6 on Adroit, validating the benefit of the transformer architecture. IQL is the strongest competitor, winning on Antmaze-large (73.83 vs. 0.0 for TD3+ODT) but losing on MuJoCo (55.9 vs. 88.38) and Adroit (61.14 vs. 87.84); the authors attribute IQL's weakness on random datasets to its OOD-avoidance objective limiting online exploration. The ablation TD3 (RL gradient only, no supervised loss) achieves 87.64 on MuJoCo, nearly matching TD3+ODT (88.38), but fails catastrophically on Adroit (31.85 vs. 87.84), showing that the supervised component is essential for complex manipulation tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Online Decision Transformer (ODT) by identifying a fundamental limitation in its supervised-learning-only finetuning paradigm: conditioning on a high Return-To-Go that is far from the expected return prevents local policy improvement. The theoretical analysis builds on the performance bound of Brandfonbrener et al. (2022), showing that the DT's autoregressive mechanism yields ∂a/∂RTG rather than the ∂RTG/∂a needed for local improvement. Methodologically, the paper is closest to TD3+BC (Fujimoto & Gu, 2021) but departs by using a transformer backbone and RTG-conditioned selection rather than indiscriminate behavior cloning, and by providing a principled explanation for why the RL gradient is necessary rather than merely beneficial.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Online finetuning of decision transformers is a practically critical but under-explored problem, and this paper provides both a theoretical diagnosis (why ODT fails on low-reward data) and a minimal, drop-in fix (adding a weighted TD3 actor loss). The simplicity of the solution—just one additional loss term with α=0.1—makes it easy to adopt, while the theoretical framing opens questions about whether other RL gradients or value-function-based corrections could further improve DT finetuning. Limitations include reliance on a simple reflective critic (avoiding the difficulty of training transformer-based value estimators), assumptions in the theoretical analysis that the authors acknowledge need to be removed, and additional computational cost over vanilla ODT. Future work on image-based environments and alternative RL gradient formulations remains open.
