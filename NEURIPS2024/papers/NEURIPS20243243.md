## Problem

While LLM bias in in-context learning (e.g., vanilla label bias, recency bias, selection bias) has been documented through external output observations, the internal mechanisms within transformer layers that cause such bias remain unexplored. Prior calibration methods adjust decision boundaries on model outputs without understanding which specific FFN vectors or attention heads introduce biased predictions, leaving the root cause unaddressed.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a standard ICL text prompt (few example-label pairs plus a test query) and a small labeled support set of 20 samples per class used solely for grid-searching identification thresholds. At inference time, it produces the same class label prediction as vanilla ICL, but with specific FFN vector coefficients zeroed and attention head outputs masked via hook operations. The entire procedure is inference-only: no weight updates or retraining occur, and the computational cost at test time is identical to the original LLM.

## Method

UniBias leverages mechanistic interpretability to decompose each transformer layer's contribution into individual FFN vectors and attention heads, then projects each component's output into vocabulary space via the logit lens to obtain label logits. Three criteria—relatedness (high sum of label logits), bias (skewed logit distribution favoring certain labels), and low variance (small coefficient of variation across samples with different ground-truth labels)—jointly identify components that consistently push predictions toward specific labels regardless of input. Thresholds for these criteria are selected by grid search over a small support set, minimizing the bias of average label logits. Identified biased FFN vectors have their activation coefficients reset to zero, and biased attention heads are masked out, effectively removing their contribution to the residual stream. The approach is complementary: FFN vectors primarily encode uncontextual label preferences (vanilla label bias), while attention heads encode positional and contextual biases (recency, selection), and masking both yields the strongest performance gains.

## Results

On 12 NLP datasets under 1-shot ICL, UniBias achieves an average accuracy of 70.46% with Llama-2 7B and 75.51% with Llama-2 13B, outperforming standard ICL by 3.39% and 2.97% respectively. The method also surpasses the strongest calibration baseline (DC for 7B, CC for 13B) by 1.76% and 2.45% on average. Notable individual gains include +7.32% on SST-2 (7B), +11.60% on COPA (13B), and +5.10% on TREC (7B). UniBias also reduces standard deviation across repeated runs, indicating improved robustness (e.g., SST-2 std drops from 6.03 to 0.62 on 7B).

## Baselines & Comparisons

UniBias is compared against three external calibration baselines: Contextual Calibration (CC, Zhao et al. 2021), Domain-Context Calibration (DC, Fei et al. 2023), and Prototypical Calibration (PC, Han et al. 2023). UniBias outperforms all three baselines on average for both model sizes: on Llama-2 7B it beats DC (the strongest baseline) by 1.76% and CC by 1.97%; on Llama-2 13B it beats CC by 2.45% and DC by 3.43%. On individual datasets, UniBias wins on 11 of 12 for Llama-2 7B (losing only on WiC, where PC achieves 57.11% vs. UniBias's 53.71%) and 11 of 12 for Llama-2 13B (losing only on AGNews, where DC reaches 89.34% vs. UniBias's 88.68%). The largest margins over baselines appear on COPA (+11.60% over ICL on 13B), TREC (+7.32% over ICL on 7B), and SST-2 (+7.32% over ICL on 7B), where prompt brittleness is most severe. Unlike the calibration baselines that adjust output probabilities externally, UniBias operates purely at inference time by masking identified biased FFN vectors and attention heads, requiring no retraining and adding zero computational overhead at test time.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges mechanistic interpretability (residual stream decomposition, logit lens, FFN-as-key-value-memory) with the LLM bias mitigation literature. Prior bias mitigation work (Zhao et al. 2021, Fei et al. 2023, Han et al. 2023) operates externally by calibrating output probabilities, while this paper is the first to identify specific internal components—individual FFN vectors and attention heads—as the causal source of bias and to manipulate them directly. The interpretability tools from Elhage et al. (2021), Geva et al. (2021), and Nostalgebraist (2020) provide the mathematical decomposition that makes per-component identification possible, but their application to bias diagnosis and mitigation is novel here.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By grounding bias in identifiable internal components, this work offers a fundamentally different mitigation strategy from output calibration: masking biased components at inference time without retraining. The discovery that a small set of common biased attention heads (8 heads) transfers across diverse tasks suggests a path toward task-agnostic debiasing. Key limitations include the per-task grid search requirement and reliance on a small labeled support set, though the authors show unlabeled samples can substitute. Open questions include whether global biased components can be identified without any task-specific data, and whether this internal-manipulation paradigm extends to other model pathologies beyond label bias.
