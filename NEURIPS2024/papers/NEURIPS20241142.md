## Problem

Multimodal models trained on modality-complete data suffer severe performance degradation when modalities are missing at test time. Existing cross-modal knowledge distillation methods enforce deterministic point alignment between modality-missing and modality-complete representations, but this is overly stringent given the irreparable information asymmetry, causing the model to fit spurious factors rather than transfer meaningful privileged information.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241142/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts any non-empty subset of available modalities (RGB, depth, and infrared for face anti-spoofing classification; RGB and depth for semantic segmentation) and outputs a class label or pixel-wise segmentation mask. During training, modality-complete samples are augmented by randomly sampling one of all possible missing-modality scenarios per epoch (7 cases for trimodal, 3 for bimodal), so the unified model learns to handle every combination. The internal representation is a 512-dimensional Gaussian distribution (for classification) rather than a single vector, with variance estimated by a two-layer MLP head.

## Method

PCD replaces deterministic point alignment with probabilistic alignment by parameterizing each modality-missing representation as an independent Gaussian distribution q(z|x) whose mean comes from the multimodal encoder and whose variance is predicted by a lightweight head module. The distribution is optimized to approximate the unknown PDF of the mapped variable in the modality-complete space via two loss terms: (1) a probability extremum loss that maximizes log-probabilities at positive points (same-class modality-complete features) and minimizes them at negative points (different-class features), and (2) a geometric consistency loss that uses contrastive learning to align the pairwise distance structure among distribution means with that among modality-complete features. The overall framework uses a self-KD teacher-student architecture where the teacher (frozen after warm-up) processes modality-complete data and provides both the target features and geometric structure, while the student processes modality-missing inputs and is trained end-to-end with the combined task loss and distillation losses.

## Results

On the CeFA face anti-spoofing dataset (ACER↓), PCD achieves an average of 22.63% across all seven modality-missing conditions, outperforming the second-best method MMANET (27.94%) by 5.31%. On CASIA-SURF, PCD reaches 2.93% average ACER, a 1.01% improvement over MMANET (3.94%). For segmentation, PCD attains 46.49% mIOU on NYUv2 (+0.91% over MMANET) and 73.03% mIOU on Cityscapes (+0.83% over MMANET). The largest single-condition gain is 7.20% on CeFA under the full {R,D,I} condition (14.39% vs. 21.59% for ETMC).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PCD outperforms all nine baselines across every modality-missing condition on both classification datasets. The closest competitor is MMANET [46] (a margin-aware KD method), which PCD beats by 1.01% on CASIA-SURF and 5.31% on CeFA in average ACER. Among dynamical fusion methods, ETMC [14] is the strongest baseline on CeFA (27.95% avg), yet PCD still leads by 5.32%; on CASIA-SURF, ETMC (4.05%) is slightly ahead of MMANET (3.94%) but both trail PCD (2.93%). On segmentation, MMANET [46] is again the nearest rival, with PCD improving average mIOU by 0.91% on NYUv2 and 0.83% on Cityscapes. Notably, several baselines (e.g., MMANET, ETMC) exhibit performance degradation when more modalities are available (e.g., MMANET's CeFA {R,D,I} score of 23.68% is worse than its {R,D} score of 22.87%), a phenomenon the authors attribute to overfitting from deterministic alignment that PCD's probabilistic formulation avoids.

## Relation to Prior Work

PCD departs from the dominant cross-modal knowledge distillation line (MMANET, MMIN, SMIL) that enforces deterministic point-to-point alignment between modality-missing and modality-complete representations. Motivated by the information-asymmetry argument of Huang et al. [18], it replaces this rigid alignment with fitting a parameterized Gaussian distribution whose peak is expectedxpected at the complete representation, while preserving geometric (conformal) structure via a contrastive-learning term. Architecturally it builds on self-KD [24] for the teacher-student setup and borrows the contrastive similarity form from supervised contrastive learning [23].

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The probabilistic framing is a principled fix for a known failure mode: the paper empirically shows that deterministic alignment can cause models to overfit spurious factors (e.g., MMANET's errorrror rate worsens when more modalities are added on CeFA), and that replacing it with distribution fitting yields consistent 1–5% gains across four datasets with only a lightweight variance-head overhead. A key limitation is that PCD requires modality-complete training data to construct the teacher signal; when training samples themselves have missing modalities, the distillation loss simply cannot be applied to that subset, restricting scalability. Open questions include whether the Gaussian family is sufficient for highly multimodal or high-dimensional feature spaces, and whether the conformal (geometric) term generalizes beyond Euclidean/inner-product distance metrics.
