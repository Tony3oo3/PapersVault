## Problem

Existing diffusion-based blind image restoration methods (GDP, DDRM, DDNM) still require the degradation function type to be known a priori, with only its parameters left unknown. In real-world scenarios, the degradation is often a complex, unknown mixture that cannot be expressed as a simple mathematical operator, limiting the practical applicability of these methods. BIR-D addresses this gap by treating the degradation function itself as an unknown to be learned jointly with the restored image.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240667/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

BIR-D takes a single degraded RGB image (or a set of n degraded images for multi-guidance tasks such as HDR recovery from three LDR exposures) as input, with no degradation function or parameters provided. A pre-trained unconditional DDPM (trained on ImageNet) serves as the generative prior, and the method outputs a single restored high-quality image. For multi-guidance, each input image has its own dedicated degradation kernel and mask, and their distance losses are averaged to drive a unified sampling process.

## Method

BIR-D replaces the fixed degradation operator with a learnable degradation model y = K(x) + M, where K is an optimizable convolutional kernel (e.g., 5×5 or 9×9 depending on task) and M is a pixel-wise mask capturing spatially varying noise. At each reverse diffusion step, the kernel and mask parameters are updated via gradient descent on the MSE between D(x̃₀) and the observed degraded image y, so the degradation model adapts in real time as the estimate x̃₀ improves. The key theoretical contribution is an empirical formula for the guidance scale s, derived by combining a heuristic log-likelihood approximation with a first-order Taylor expansion of log p(y|x_t) around the mean μ, yielding s = −((x_t−μ)ᵀg + C + log N) / L(D(x̃₀), y) that is recomputed at every denoising step. This eliminates the need for manual grid search over a fixed guidance scale and naturally reduces the guidance strength as the sampling progresses toward clean images. A first-stage pre-training model (from DiffBIR) is applied before the diffusion loop to correct large initial deviations in the degradation estimate, improving convergence especially under random kernel initialization.

## Results

On ImageNet 1k linear inverse problems, BIR-D achieves the best Consistency and FID across all four tasks (4× SR, Deblur, 25% Inpainting, Colorization), e.g., FID of 2.32 on Deblur vs. 2.44 for GDP and 4.78 for DDRM, and Consistency of 6.32 on 4× SR vs. 6.49 for GDP. On blind face restoration (LFW/WIDER), BIR-D attains the lowest NIQE (3.94/3.65) and best FID on WIDER (31.49). For low-light enhancement on LOL, BIR-D achieves FID 68.98, LOE 105.42, and PI 4.87, outperforming all zero-shot baselines. On motion blur reduction (GoPro/HIDE) and HDR recovery (NTIRE), BIR-D achieves the highest PSNR/SSIM (34.12/0.968 and 32.09/0.948) and best FID (48.74) respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BIR-D consistently outperforms GDP (the closest diffusion-based baseline) on Consistency and FID across all four ImageNet linear inverse tasks, e.g., FID 2.32 vs. 2.44 on Deblur and Consistency 6.32 vs. 6.49 on 4× SR, while using a universal unknown degradation function rather than a known one. On blind face restoration, BIR-D beats DiffBIR on NIQE for both LFW (3.94 vs. 4.03) and WIDER (3.65 vs. 3.78), and on FID for WIDER (31.49 vs. 32.35), though it slightly trails DiffBIR on LFW FID (40.12 vs. 39.58). For low-light enhancement, BIR-D surpasses all zero-shot methods (Zero-DCE, Zero-DCE++, RRDNet, ExCNet) and GDP on FID, LOE, and PI on both LOL and VE-LOL-L. On motion blur reduction, BIR-D edges out UFPNet (the best supervised baseline) by 0.06 dB PSNR on GoPro and 0.35 dB on HIDE. For HDR recovery, BIR-D achieves the best PSNR (25.03) and FID (48.74) over GDP (24.88/50.05) and all supervised HDR methods. DDRM and DDNM achieve higher PSNR/SSIM on some linear inverse tasks (e.g., DDRM 35.64 PSNR on Deblur) but at the cost of much worse FID and Consistency, indicating less faithful and lower-quality outputs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the diffusion-based image restoration line initiated by GDP, DDRM, and DDNM, which all require the degradation function to be known a priori (with only parameters left to optimize). BIR-D departs from this by introducing an optimizable convolutional kernel that dynamically simulates an entirely unknown degradation function at each denoising step, building on the DDPM prior (Dhariwal & Nichol) and the heuristic guidance approximation from GDP. It also incorporates DiffBIR's pre-training stage as an initialization mechanism, and derives a closed-form empirical formula for the guidance scale via Taylor expansion, replacing the fixed hyperparameter used in all prior diffusion restoration methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ability to handle truly blind degradation (unknown function, not just unknown parameters) broadens the practical applicability of diffusion-based restoration to real-world scenarios where the degradation pipeline is complex and unspecified. The adaptive guidance scale formula eliminates per-task grid search, reducing practical deployment friction. However, the method relies on a single pre-trained unconditional DDPM with patch-based processing for large images, limiting speed and scalability; the authors themselves note that migrating to latent-space diffusion (e.g., Stable Diffusion) is a natural but unexplored next step. Open questions include whether the single-kernel-plus-mask formulation can capture truly non-local or spatially-varying degradations, and how the method scales to video or very high-resolution restoration.
