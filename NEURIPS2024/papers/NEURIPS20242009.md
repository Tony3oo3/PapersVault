## Problem

Existing decentralized bilevel optimization algorithms predominantly rely on gradient tracking (GT) as the sole heterogeneity-correction mechanism and apply identical decentralized strategies to both upper- and lower-level problems, despite the fundamentally different structures at each level. Moreover, prior works impose restrictive assumptions (bounded gradients or bounded data heterogeneity) and fail to characterize transient iteration complexity, making it unclear how quickly linear speedup is achieved over sparse topologies.

## Input / Output

Each of n agents holds private local data distributions for both the upper-level loss f_i and the strongly convex lower-level loss g_i, with no assumption of data homogeneity across agents. The algorithm consumes the network topology through level-specific mixing matrices W_x, W_y, W_z (which may differ across levels to trade off communication cost), and produces the upper-level solution x (e.g., hyper-parameters), the lower-level solution y (e.g., model weights), and an auxiliary variable z approximating the Hessian-inverse-vector product. The method is single-loop and loopless, requiring only one stochastic gradient/Jacobian/Hessian evaluation per agent per iteration.

## Method

SPARKLE decomposes decentralized bilevel optimization into three single-level subproblems (upper-level x, lower-level y, auxiliary-level z for Hessian inversion) and solves each with one iteration per round using a unified primal-dual framework with a moving-average (momentum) term. The framework is parameterized by matrices A_s, B_s, C_s that are polynomial functions of the mixing matrix W_s, allowing it to instantiate ED, EXTRA, or any GT variant (ATC, semi-ATC, non-ATC) per level. The key architectural flexibility is that different heterogeneity-correction strategies can be assigned to different levels (e.g., GT for x but ED for y and z), and different topologies can be used across levels to reduce communication overhead. Momentum is applied only to the upper-level update, which is sufficient to relax smoothness conditions and improve transient convergence. The unified analysis shows that SPARKLE-EXTRA and SPARKLE-ED achieve smaller transient iteration complexity (n³/(1−ρ)²) than SPARKLE-GT (max{n³/(1−ρ)², n/(1−ρ)^{8/3}}), establishing that GT is not the optimal heterogeneity-correction scheme for decentralized bilevel optimization.

## Results

All SPARKLE variants (GT, EXTRA, ED) achieve the optimal asymptotic rate of O(1/√(nK)) with asymptotic gradient complexity O(1/(nε²)) and communication cost O((ap+q)/(nε²)), matching the best single-level decentralized algorithms while requiring no bounded-gradient or bounded-heterogeneity assumptions. The key theoretical advance is in transient iteration complexity: SPARKLE-EXTRA and SPARKLE-ED achieve n³/(1−ρ)², improving over SPARKLE-GT's max{n³/(1−ρ)², n/(1−ρ)^(8/3)} and all prior methods (e.g., D-SOBA's max{n³/(1−ρ)², n³b²/(1−ρ)⁴}). In experiments on FashionMNIST hyper-cleaning, SPARKLE-ED and SPARKLE-EXTRA achieve higher test accuracy than D-SOBA, MA-DSBO-GT, and MDBO across corruption rates p=0.1–0.3, with mixed strategies (ED-GT, EXTRA-GT) matching the best single-strategy variants while outperforming GT-only.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SPARKLE dominates all nine baselines on transient iteration complexity: SPARKLE-EXTRA/ED achieve n³/(1−ρ)², which is strictly better than D-SOBA's max{n³/(1−ρ)², n³b²/(1−ρ)⁴} (which degrades under data heterogeneity), MDBO's n³/(1−ρ)⁸, and Gossip DSBO's n³/(1−ρ)⁴. SPARKLE-EXTRA/ED also outperform SPARKLE-GT (max{n³/(1−ρ)², n/(1−ρ)^(8/3)}), establishing that EXTRA and ED are superior heterogeneity-correction mechanisms for bilevel optimization. In the FashionMNIST hyper-cleaning experiments, all SPARKLE variants achieve higher test accuracy than D-SOBA, MA-DSBO-GT, and MDBO, with ED and EXTRA specifically outperforming GT, and mixed strategies (SPARKLE-ED-GT, SPARKLE-EXTRA-GT) matching the best single-strategy results. In distributed policy evaluation (n=10, 20 agents on a ring graph), SPARKLE converges faster and achieves lower sample complexity than both MDBO and the stochastic SLDBO, with the advantage growing as n increases. In decentralized meta-learning on miniImageNet, SPARKLE demonstrates significant improvement in training accuracy over both D-SOBA and MAML.

## Relation to Prior Work

SPARKLE unifies the primal-dual decentralized framework of Alghunaim & Yuan [1,2] with the three-subproblem bilevel decomposition from SOBA [12], creating a single-loop algorithm that subsumes EXTRA, Exact Diffusion, and multiple Gradient Tracking variants through matrix parameterization. It departs from prior decentralized bilevel work (DSBO, MA-DSBO, MDBO, D-SOBA, LoPA) in two key ways: (i) it does not restrict itself to GT or DGD as the heterogeneity-correction mechanism, and (ii) it permits different decentralized strategies at the upper and lower levels, a flexibility no prior algorithm offered. The momentum/moving-average component extends the single-level framework of [2] to handle the relaxed smoothness conditions needed for bilevel convergence.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central practical takeaway is that EXTRA and Exact Diffusion yield smaller transient iteration complexity than Gradient Tracking for decentralized bilevel optimization, directly challenging the field's default assumption that GT is the go-to heterogeneity-correction technique. The unified framework also gives practitioners a single codebase to instantiate multiple strategies and mix them across levels, with provable state-of-the-art rates under no bounded-gradient or bounded-heterogeneity assumptions. Open questions include extending the analysis to generally-convex (non-strongly-convex) lower-level problems and understanding how the condition number κ of the lower-level problem interacts with network topology in practice.
