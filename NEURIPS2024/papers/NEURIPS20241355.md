## Problem

Diffusion-based super-resolution methods face a fundamental efficiency-performance trade-off: leveraging large-scale pretrained diffusion models (e.g., Stable Diffusion) as generative priors requires dozens of forward passes from random Gaussian noise, while redefining the diffusion process from scratch (e.g., ResShift) sacrifices the powerful generative prior. No existing approach simultaneously exploits pretrained diffusion prior and initiates inference directly from the LR image to reduce sampling steps.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241355/cropped_page1_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a low-resolution RGB image (typically 128×128 for ×4 SR) as input, optionally preprocessed by RealESRNet to mitigate severe degradation artifacts, and produces a high-resolution RGB image (512×512). The LR image serves as the source-domain conditioning signal throughout the diffusion process rather than a simple side condition, and inference requires only 5 function evaluations (NFE) by default, with the model fine-tuned on 512×512 patches using LAControlNet with SD 2.1-base.

## Method

DoSSR introduces a domain shift equation that linearly interpolates between the LR (source) and HR (target) domains via a coefficient η_t, embedded within the standard DDPM noise schedule (α_t, σ_t) so that pretrained diffusion models can be fine-tuned rather than trained from scratch. A carefully designed shifting sequence sets η_t = 1 for t ∈ [t_1, T], allowing inference to start at t_1 instead of T, thereby preserving LR prior information and shortening the diffusion path. The discrete process is extended to continuous Domain Shift SDEs (DoS-SDEs), for which an exact solution is derived via the variation-of-constants formula. From this solution, customized fast solvers of arbitrary order are constructed using Itô-Taylor expansion, introducing a Domain Shift Guidance (DoSG) term that injects source-domain information at each step. The model is fine-tuned for 50k iterations on DIV2K/DIV8K/Flickr2K/OST with the RealESRGAN degradation pipeline, and the third-order solver is used by default to achieve state-of-the-art perceptual quality in just 5 steps.

## Results

On the RealLR200 real-world benchmark, DoSSR achieves the best no-reference scores: CLIPIQA 0.7437, MANIQA 0.5794, and TOPIQ 0.7176, outperforming SeeSR (0.7167/0.5588/0.7142) and StableSR (0.7272/0.4838/0.6517). DoSSR requires only 5 NFE (vs. 200 for StableSR, 50 for DiffBIR/SeeSR, 15 for ResShift), yielding a latency of 1.03s per 128×128 image — a 5–7× speedup over other SD-based methods. On reference-based metrics (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS), DoSSR trails GAN-based methods like DASR and BSRGAN, consistent with the known fidelity-perception trade-off of diffusion models. DoSSR also achieves competitive results with a single inference step (MANIQA 0.5115, CLIPIQA 0.6258 on RealSR).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DoSSR consistently outperforms all diffusion-based baselines on the three primary no-reference metrics (CLIPIQA, MANIQA, TOPIQ) across all four evaluation datasets, with the sole exception of TOPIQ on DIV2K-Val where it ranks second (0.6766 vs. SeeSR's 0.6854). Against the closest competitor SeeSR [51], DoSSR gains +0.027 CLIPIQA, +0.021 MANIQA, and +0.003 TOPIQ on RealLR200 while using 10× fewer NFE (5 vs. 50). Compared to StableSR [45], DoSSR improves MANIQA by +0.096 (0.5794 vs. 0.4838) on RealLR200 and is ~18× faster (1.03s vs. 18.90s). Against ResShift [53], DoSSR achieves comparable latency (1.03s vs. 1.12s) but substantially better quality (MANIQA 0.5794 vs. 0.4713, CLIPIQA 0.7437 vs. 0.6759 on RealLR200), attributed to leveraging the pretrained SD prior. On reference-based metrics (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS), DoSSR underperforms GAN-based methods like DASR and BSRGAN, which is expected given diffusion models prioritize perceptual realism over pixel-level fidelity. The ablation in Table 2 confirms that the domain shift guidance (DoSG) contributes +0.04 CLIPIQA and +0.025 MANIQA over vanilla DDPM with the same 5-step budget.

## Relation to Prior Work

DoSSR bridges two previously incompatible lines of diffusion-based SR: methods that leverage large pretrained diffusion models (StableSR, DiffBIR, SeeSR) but start from Gaussian noise, and methods that start from LR images (ResShift, SinSR) but must train from scratch. The key departure from ResShift is a theoretical insight: ResShift's residual-shifting formulation corresponds to a VE-SDE discretization, which is fundamentally incompatible with the VP-SDE (DDPM) noise schedule used by Stable Diffusion, whereas DoSSR's domain shift equation preserves the DDPM schedule, enabling direct fine-tuning of pretrained models. The continuous SDE formulation and fast solvers build directly on DPM-Solver++ and the authors' prior work on extended reverse-time SDEs, adapted here with a domain-shift guidance term.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: 5 NFE steps matching or exceeding 50-step methods represents a 5–7× speedup for diffusion-based SR, making pretrained-model-based SR viable in latency-sensitive applications. The clean theoretical separation (VP-SDE-compatible vs. VE-SDE-incompatible formulations) provides a principled reason why prior efficient-SR methods couldn't leverage diffusion prior, guiding future work. The acknowledged limitation—sensitivity to random seed initialization—remains an open problem, and the authors suggest per-image seed selection as a promising direction. Broader implications include the potential to apply the domain-shift SDE framework to other inverse problems where a degraded observation is available as a starting point.
