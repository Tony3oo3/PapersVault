## Problem

While ensembling multiple classifiers via majority vote is a standard way to improve accuracy, it remains unclear how many classifiers are actually needed to reach a target performance level. Existing bounds on majority vote error (McAllester's 2× bound, the C-bound, and competence-based bounds) are either too loose to be practically useful or require strong structural assumptions, and the precise quantitative relationship between pairwise disagreement and majority vote accuracy has not been characterized. This paper asks: can we predict the majority vote error rate for N classifiers from measurements on just a few?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242744/cropped_page4_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an ensemble of independently trained classifiers (e.g., ResNet18, ResNet50, VGG11, DenseNet40, MobileNet) and their predictions on a held-out dataset of RGB images (CIFAR-10 at 32×32, MNIST, Fashion-MNIST, Kuzushiji-MNIST). From a small subset of classifiers (as few as three), it computes the average error rate, pairwise disagreement, and the polarization η_ρ, then extrapolates to produce an estimated majority vote error rate for an arbitrarily larger ensemble. The polarization value itself is reported as a dataset-level invariant that is nearly constant across architectures and hyperparameters.

## Method

The paper introduces polarization η_ρ, defined as the ratio P(W_ρ > 1/2) / E[W_ρ²], which measures how concentrated the point-wise error distribution is around the majority-vote threshold. An upper bound (Theorem 1) shows η_ρ ≤ 4/3 for interpolating networks, leading to the conjectured 'neural polarization law' that most interpolating neural networks are 4/3-polarized. Using this, the authors derive a family of tighter majority vote error bounds (Theorems 2–4, Corollaries 1–2) that incorporate entropy restrictions on the classifiers' output distributions, replacing the loose 4(K−1)/K factor with 2η_ρ(K−1)/K or even η_ρ(1+ε). The key asymptotic result (Theorem 5) shows that disagreement among N classifiers decomposes into a hyperbolic term (1−1/N)D_∞ plus a random-walk fluctuation of order 1/√N, via the invariance principle for U-statistics. This yields a practical estimator (equations 14–15) that predicts the majority vote error for N classifiers using only the average error, disagreement, and ε_ρ measured from three classifiers, with η set to 4/3 or estimated from the three-classifier ensemble.

## Results

The paper's headline empirical finding is that the polarization η_ρ of ensembles of independently trained interpolating neural networks is consistently ≈ 4/3 across architectures (ResNet18/50/101, VGG11, DenseNet40, MobileNet), hyperparameters, and datasets (CIFAR-10, CIFAR-10.1, Fashion-MNIST, Kuzushiji-MNIST, MNIST), supporting the proposed Neural Polarization Law (Conjecture 1). The proposed finite-ensemble bound (Corollary 1) is strictly tighter than both the C-bound [LMRR17, MLIS20] and the competence-based bound [TKY+24] across all tested configurations (Figure 3). Using only three classifiers, the extrapolated majority vote error estimates (equations 14–15) closely track the true majority vote error as the number of classifiers grows from 3 to 20, across five (architecture, dataset) pairs (Figure 4).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares its proposed bounds against two prior baselines: the C-bound from [LMRR17] and [MLIS20] (inequality 3), and the competence-based bound from [TKY+24] (inequality 6). In Figure 3, the Corollary 1 bound (using η_ρ ≈ 4/3) is strictly tighter than both the C-bound and the TKY+24 bound for ResNet18, ResNet50, and ResNet101 on both in-distribution CIFAR-10 and out-of-distribution CIFAR-10.1. In Figure 4(a2), the C-bound and TKY+24 bound are shown to be substantially larger than even the average error rate of individual classifiers, whereas the proposed extrapolation from three classifiers (equations 14–15) closely tracks the true majority vote error. The TKY+24 competence bound, while an improvement over the C-bound, still leaves a large gap to the actual majority vote error; the polarization-based bound closes this gap by replacing the universal constant 4(K−1)/K with the empirically observed 2η_ρ(K−1)/K ≈ 2(4/3)(K−1)/K, yielding a meaningfully smaller coefficient. The extrapolation result (Theorem 5 + equation 13) is the paper's most practically useful comparison: it shows that three classifiers suffice to predict the ensemble error for up to 20 classifiers, a capability neither the C-bound nor the TKY+24 bound provides.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on bounding majority vote ensemble error, building directly on the competence condition and second-order bounds from Theisen et al. (2024) and the PAC-Bayesian framework from Masegosa et al. (2020). The key departure is the introduction of 'polarization' (η_ρ), a new quantity that refines the Markov's inequality step connecting point-wise error to majority vote error, yielding strictly tighter bounds than all prior results. The paper further departs from prior disagreement-based analysis by proving asymptotic behavior of disagreement via U-statistics, enabling prediction of large-ensemble performance from just three classifiers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical implication is significant: a practitioner can estimate the majority vote error of an N-classifier ensemble from measurements on only three classifiers, providing a concrete stopping rule for how many models to train. The neural polarization law (Conjecture 1), if ultimately proven, would establish a fundamental invariant property of interpolating neural networks analogous to neural collapse. Open questions include proving the 4/3 polarization bound, understanding whether the entropy-restriction assumptions hold broadly, and whether the proposed alternative metric P(W_ρ > 1/2) (probability of majority agreement on the correct answer) should replace majority vote error as the standard ensemble evaluation criterion.
