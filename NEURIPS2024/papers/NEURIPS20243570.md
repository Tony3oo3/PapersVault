## Problem

Existing form understanding datasets such as FUNSD and XFUND provide only local, entity-level key-value annotations, overlooking the global hierarchical structure that spans words, text lines, entities, and item tables within a document. This limitation prevents models from learning nested, multi-level structural dependencies, constraining comprehensive understanding of complex multilingual forms.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243570/cropped_page1_idx4.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark consumes grayscale form images (PNG for English, JPEG for the other seven languages) together with word-level texts and 2D coordinates. It supports five tasks at increasing granularity: word-to-text-line merging, text-line-to-entity merging, entity category classification into four classes (Header, Question, Answer, Other), item table localization with header and individual row-item annotations, and entity-based full-document hierarchical structure recovery. Detection-based tasks are evaluated with a CLEval-inspired criterion requiring predicted boxes to contain exactly the word-level centers within the ground truth region, rather than standard IoU thresholding, while merging and structure tasks use symmetric or asymmetric relation matrices scored by F1.

## Method

SRFUND is built by refining FUNSD and XFUND through a six-step manual annotation pipeline: correcting word bounding boxes, aggregating semantically continuous words into text lines, drawing polygonal entity boxes for multi-line entities, reassigning entity categories, localizing item tables with their headers and row items, and annotating unidirectional or bidirectional entity relationships. The resulting dataset spans 1,592 forms in eight languages with 529,711 words, 112,662 text lines, 96,824 entities, 591 item tables, and 122,594 entity links, yielding an average form tree depth of 3.049 compared to roughly 1.5–1.7 in prior datasets. The benchmark evaluates three model families—vision-only detectors (YOLOX, Cascade-RCNN, DAB-DETR), text-only multilingual LMs (XLM-RoBERTa, InfoXLM), and multi-modal document pre-trained models (LayoutXLM, LiLT, GraphDoc)—using a Merger relation head by default, with F1 as the unified metric across all five tasks. Additional experiments compare alternative relation heads (Biaffine, GeoLayout), assess cross-lingual transferability, and probe zero-shot performance of GPT-4o and GPT-4o-mini.

## Results

On the novel global hierarchical structure recovery task (unique to SRFUND), GraphDoc achieves the best average F1 of 0.7349, followed by LayoutXLM (0.7013) and LiLT (0.6821), while text-only models (XLM-RoBERTa 0.5830, InfoXLM 0.5732) lag by over 10 points. On item table localization, all models struggle severely, with the best performer (LayoutXLM) reaching only 0.3022 average F1, highlighting the difficulty of this new task. Multi-modal document pre-trained models consistently outperform both vision-only and text-only baselines across all five tasks, and MLLMs (GPT-4o) underperform domain-specific models on most tasks (e.g., 0.1103 vs. 0.7349 on structure recovery).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among multi-modal document pre-trained models, GraphDoc wins on hierarchical structure recovery (0.7349 avg F1) by leveraging entity-level linguistic input aligned with the task, outperforming LayoutXLM (0.7013) and LiLT (0.6821). LayoutXLM dominates the remaining four tasks: word-to-text-line merging (0.9260), text-line-to-entity merging (0.9412), entity category classification (0.9248), and item table localization (0.3022), benefiting from multilingual pre-training across all SRFUND languages. Vision-only detectors (YOLOX, Cascade-RCNN, DAB-DETR) are competitive on spatial tasks like word-to-text-line merging (Cascade-RCNN: 0.8189) but collapse on semantic tasks like entity classification (YOLOX: 0.4969) and item table localization (best: 0.1151). Text-only models (XLM-RoBERTa, InfoXLM) excel on entity-level semantic tasks (e.g., 0.9029 on text-line-to-entity merging) but fail on spatial tasks (0.6509 on word-to-text-line) and structure recovery (0.5830). MLLMs (GPT-4o) achieve substantially lower scores than domain-specific models on all tasks (e.g., 0.1103 vs. 0.7349 on structure recovery, 0.047 vs. 0.3022 on item table localization), though GPT-4o-mini surprisingly outperforms GPT-4o on text-line-to-entity merging (0.7805 vs. 0.2936). Cross-dataset OOD experiments confirm that models trained on SRFUND generalize better to CORD and SIBR than vice versa, validating the benchmark's annotation quality and diversity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the FUNSD and XFUND form understanding benchmarks by adding multi-granularity annotations (word → text-line → entity → item table → global hierarchical structure) that no prior form dataset provides. While existing benchmarks (SROIE, CORD, EPHOIE, SIBR, FUNSD, XFUND) support local entity classification and key-value relationship prediction, SRFUND deliberately generalizes to a full coarse-to-fine structure recovery pipeline and introduces two new tasks: item table localization and global entity-level hierarchical structure recovery. The experimental evaluation repurposes existing detection (YOLOX, Cascade-RCNN, DAB-DETR), language (XLM-RoBERTa, InfoXLM), and document pre-trained (LayoutXLM, LiLT, GraphDoc) models as baselines rather than proposing a new architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SRFUND is the first form understanding benchmark to support global hierarchical structure recovery and item table localization, filling a gap where prior datasets only captured local key-value pairs. The finding that item table localization remains extremely challenging (best F1 ≈ 0.30) and that no single model dominates across all five tasks and eight languages highlights that form structure understanding is far from solved. Key limitations include the small scale (1,592 grayscale images), limited form types (application/registration forms), and reliance on FUNSD/XFUND source images, leaving open the question of whether these annotations generalize to diverse real-world forms with colored backgrounds and varied layouts.
