## Problem

Existing black-box optimizers (CMA-ES, L-SHADE) require manual hyperparameter tuning for each new task, while learned optimizers (LES, LGA) still underperform hand-designed methods on zero-shot benchmarks. No prior pretrained optimizer can be directly applied to an unseen continuous optimization problem of arbitrary dimensionality without any adjustment, limiting their practical utility as general-purpose solvers.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240456/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

POM takes an initial random population X⁰ ∈ ℝ^{N×d}, a black-box objective function f (which need not be differentiable at test time), and a generation count T, and outputs the optimized population X^T from which the best solution is extracted. A key property is dimensional generalization: the model is trained at d=10 but tested at d=30, 100, and 500 without architectural modification, and the population size N is also flexible at inference. During training, f must be differentiable to enable gradient flow through MetaGBT, but this requirement is absent at test time.

## Method

POM is a population-based architecture with three learnable modules: (1) LMM, a multi-head self-attention block that maps population descriptors (normalized fitness and centralized ranking) to a mutation matrix S^t ∈ ℝ^{N×N}, generalizing DE/rand/1 into a fully learnable linear combination of all individuals; (2) LCM, an FFN with Gumbel-Softmax that produces per-individual crossover probabilities, making the otherwise discrete binomial crossover differentiable for end-to-end training; and (3) SM, a 1-to-1 parent-offspring selection. A stochastic mask (probability r_mask) is applied to S^t to prevent full information sharing and preserve population diversity. Training uses MetaGBT, an end-to-end gradient-based meta-learning procedure that iterates over a diverse set of simple training functions (TF1–TF5) with randomly perturbed parameters, optimizing a loss that balances per-step fitness improvement against population spread (standard deviation), thereby encouraging both convergence and exploration without requiring the target task's gradient at test time.

## Results

On the BBOB benchmark (d=100), POM achieves near-optimal values on the majority of 24 functions, e.g., 5.92×10⁻¹² on F1, 2.42×10⁻¹³ on F8, and 1.43×10⁻⁴ on F12, substantially outperforming all six baselines. On the high-dimensional robot control tasks (Bipedal Walker, d=874; Enduro, d=4149), POM attains the highest strategy score with stable convergence, while CMA-ES, L-SHADE, and LGA suffer from premature convergence. Fine-tuning POM with only 25 random function evaluations yields over 30% relative performance improvement (RFI) on held-out functions TF6–TF8. Under a low budget of 3,100 evaluations, POM also outperforms the Bayesian optimizer TurBO in most BBOB cases.

## Baselines & Comparisons

POM decisively outperforms the classical population-based baselines DE (DE/rand/1/bin) and (μ,λ)-ES across all 24 BBOB functions at both d=30 and d=100, often by orders of magnitude (e.g., 10⁹–10¹² improvement on F12). Against the state-of-the-art CMA-ES, POM wins on the vast majority of functions, with the gap widening as dimensionality increases (d=100 vs. d=30). L-SHADE is the closest competitor: POM beats it on most functions but loses on F21–F22 (weak global structure) where L-SHADE's adaptive parameter control gives it an edge; on F24 the two are essentially tied. Among the learned-optimizer baselines, both LES and LGA are consistently outperformed by POM on BBOB and on both robot control tasks, where LES and LGA exhibit premature convergence. In the low-budget comparison against TurBO (3,100 evaluations), POM converges faster and reaches better final values in most of the 24 BBOB functions, while also completing evaluations in under one second versus TurBO's substantially longer runtime.

## Relation to Prior Work

POM extends the meta-learning BBO line (LES, LGA) by replacing their bi-level or RL-based training with an end-to-end gradient-based method (MetaGBT), and by explicitly targeting zero-shot deployment—no hyperparameter tuning on the target task. Conceptually, it repurposes multi-head self-attention as a learnable mutation operator (generalizing DE/rand/1, DE/best/1, etc. into a single matrix S) and an FFN as a learnable crossover controller, unifying classical evolutionary operators under a differentiable framework. It departs from model-based optimizers (Chen et al., Krishnamoorthy et al.) that fit trajectories to specific tasks and from LLM-based approaches that require task-specific prompts, positioning itself as a universal, dimension-agnostic optimizer.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

POM offers a practical zero-shot BBO tool that outperforms CMA-ES and L-SHADE across BBOB dimensions (30–500) and robot control tasks, with >30% gains from fine-tuning on just 25 evaluations—relevant for HPO, neuroevolution, and NAS where per-task tuning is costly. Key limitations include training on only five basic 10D functions (complex functions in the training set degrade performance), a non-monotonic model-scale relationship that is not yet understood, and the fact that on the hardest BBOB functions (F21–F24) POM still trails L-SHADE. Open questions include whether richer training landscapes, better loss formulations balancing convergence and diversity, and larger-scale training can close the remaining gap and extend POM to constrained or mixed-integer problems.
