## Problem

EGNN's success with only 1st-degree Cartesian vectors has led to the assumption that higher-degree steerable representations are unnecessary in equivariant GNNs. This paper disproves that hypothesis by proving, via group theory, that any O(3)-equivariant function with output degree fixed to 1 (or other specific values) degenerates to a zero function on symmetric graphs such as k-fold rotations and regular polyhedra, completely losing orientation recognition ability.

## Input / Output

HEGNN consumes standard geometric graphs with per-node scalar features, 3D coordinates, and optional edge features, and produces node-level invariant scalars, node-level equivariant 3D vectors, and graph-level predictions via a readout layer. A key design detail is that high-degree steerable features are initialized from spherical harmonics of normalized relative coordinates aggregated over neighbors, and the cross-degree message computation reduces each degree pair to a single invariant scalar via inner product before feeding into a shared MLP, rather than performing full Clebsch-Gordan tensor products between all degree combinations.

## Method

HEGNN extends EGNN by maintaining steerable features at multiple degrees (0 through L) while preserving EGNN's scalarization trick for inter-degree interaction. Specifically, it (1) initializes high-degree features via spherical harmonics on normalized relative coordinates, (2) computes invariant messages by taking inner products of same-degree steerable features between node pairs and concatenating all degrees into a single MLP, and (3) updates each degree via scalar-weighted differences analogous to EGNN's coordinate update. This avoids the O(L²) Clebsch-Gordan tensor products of TFN/MACE, keeping parameter counts and inference time close to EGNN (e.g., 163k vs. 86.6M parameters at L≤6 on 100-body). Theoretically, the paper proves that full-degree inner products bijectively recover all edge angles (Theorem 4.1), establishing that HEGNN with sufficiently large L can characterize complete angular information of the input graph.

## Results

On the N-body system (5k/2k/2k split), HEGNN_{l≤6} achieves MSE of 0.69, 0.86, 0.96, and 0.86 (×10⁻²) on 5-, 20-, 50-, and 100-body systems, outperforming EGNN (0.65, 1.01, 1.00, 1.36) and TFN_{l≤2} (1.49, 1.86, 2.20, 3.42) while running ~3× faster than TFN. On MD17, HEGNN_{l≤6} achieves the best prediction error on 6 out of 8 molecules (e.g., 9.94 on Aspirin vs. 14.41 for EGNN, 0.37 on Naphthalene vs. 0.47 for EGNN). On symmetric graph expressivity tests, HEGNN with l≥6 achieves 100% accuracy on all five regular polyhedra, while EGNN and GVP-GNN (l=1 only) score 50% (chance level) across all structures.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HEGNN consistently outperforms EGNN [1] on N-body across all system sizes (e.g., 0.86 vs. 1.36 MSE on 100-body) and on 6/8 MD17 molecules (e.g., 9.94 vs. 14.41 on Aspirin), confirming that high-degree steerable features add expressivity beyond what scalarization alone provides. Against high-degree baselines TFN [19] and SE(3)-Transformer [20], HEGNN achieves substantially lower MSE (e.g., 0.86 vs. 3.42 for TFN on 100-body) while being ~3.5× faster in inference, thanks to the scalarization trick that avoids expensive CG tensor products. On MD17, HEGNN_{l≤6} beats EGNN, TFN, and SE(3)-Tr on all 8 molecules but loses to GMN [45] on Benzene (59.93 vs. 48.12) and Toluene (10.56 vs. 10.22), which the authors attribute to GMN's hand-crafted chemical bond constraints. On the symmetric graph expressivity benchmark, HEGNN with l≥6 achieves 100% accuracy on all regular polyhedra and k-fold structures, while EGNN, GVP-GNN [49], and even TFN/MACE with l≤2 all fail (50% = chance), directly validating the theoretical degeneration theorems. HEGNN also demonstrates superior robustness under perturbation: while EGNN's accuracy on a tetrahedron drops to 45–65% under noise (ε=0.01–0.5), HEGNN_{l=3} maintains 100% accuracy across all noise levels.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on EGNN (Garcia Satorras et al., ICML 2021), which achieves strong performance using only 1st-degree Cartesian vectors via a scalarization trick, and on high-degree steerable models (TFN, SE(3)-Transformer, MACE) that use spherical harmonics and Clebsch-Gordan tensor products for inter-degree interactions. The key conceptual departure is twofold: (1) a theoretical proof that any O(3)-equivariant function with fixed degree l=1 (or other specific values) provably vanishes on symmetric graphs like k-folds and regular polyhedra, and (2) an architectural synthesis where HEGNN inherits EGNN's scalarization trick to mediate interactions between high-degree steerable features, avoiding the quadratic cost of CG tensor products while recovering the expressivity that pure 1st-degree models lack.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work challenges a widely-held assumption in the equivariant GNN community that EGNN's restriction to 1st-degree representations is sufficient, providing a clean group-theoretic criterion (Wigner-D matrix trace of the group average) for when expressivity degeneration occurs. The practical HEGNN architecture offers a simple, efficient path to higher expressivity without the parameter explosion of TFN/MACE (163k vs. 86M parameters at l≤6). Key open questions include: how to bridge the gap between the theoretically required degree (growing with graph size) and the practical L≤6 that works well, whether the approach scales to large molecular systems, and how purely mathematical representations compare to physics-informed features (bond angles, forces) in practice.
