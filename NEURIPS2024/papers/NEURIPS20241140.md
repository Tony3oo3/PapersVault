## Problem

Existing diffusion-based posterior sampling methods for inverse problems are either computationally expensive (sequential Monte Carlo), lack asymptotic consistency guarantees (DPS, DDRM, ReSample), or are restricted to linear forward models (Monte Carlo guided diffusion, filtering-based approaches). The paper asks whether one can develop a practical, provably consistent, and robust algorithm that incorporates unconditional score-based diffusion priors with general, possibly nonlinear, forward models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241140/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes as input noisy measurements y ∈ R^m from an arbitrary forward model A: R^d → R^m (linear or nonlinear), a pre-trained unconditional score function ŝ(τ, x) (or equivalently a noise estimator ε̂) from a diffusion model trained on the image prior, and a user-specified annealing schedule {η_k}. It outputs a single posterior sample x̂_K ∈ R^d (a reconstructed image). Notably, the score functions are entirely unconditional—trained for image generation without knowledge of the forward model—so no task-specific training or fine-tuning is required. Experiments use 256×256 RGB images on FFHQ and ImageNet validation sets.

## Method

DPnP alternates between two modular samplers: a Proximal Consistency Sampler (PCS), which draws from a distribution proportional to exp(L(x;y) − ‖x − x̂_k‖²/(2η_k²)) to enforce measurement consistency (implemented via MALA with an exponential integrator), and a Denoising Diffusion Sampler (DDS), which draws from the posterior p*(x | x + η_k w = x̂_{k+1/2}) to enforce the data prior. The key technical insight is that the denoising posterior can be sampled rigorously by reversing either a heat-flow SDE (yielding DDS-DDPM, stochastic) or an OU-process ODE (yielding DDS-DDIM, deterministic), where the required score functions are derived in closed form from the unconditional generation score via explicit transformations (Lemmas 1–2). Both variants use the exponential integrator for accurate discretization and require no additional training beyond the pre-trained unconditional score. The paper proves asymptotic consistency (convergence to the true posterior under exact scores and exact ODE/SDE solving) and non-asymptotic robustness (graceful degradation bounded by total-variation errors of the sub-samplers), establishing DPnP as the first provably robust posterior sampling method for nonlinear inverse problems using unconditional diffusion priors.

## Results

On FFHQ 256×256, DPnP-DDIM achieves the best LPIPS and PSNR across all three inverse problems: super-resolution (LPIPS 0.301, PSNR 24.2), phase retrieval (LPIPS 0.376, PSNR 22.4), and quantized sensing (LPIPS 0.293, PSNR 24.2). On ImageNet 256×256, DPnP-DDIM similarly leads on all tasks, with the largest margin in quantized sensing (PSNR 23.0 vs. 18.7 for DPS). The most dramatic improvement is in the nonlinear phase retrieval task, where DPnP achieves PSNR 22.4 on FFHQ versus 17.4 (DPS) and 16.4 (LGD-MC). DPnP-DDIM runs in ~90s per sample on FFHQ, slightly slower than DPS/LGD-MC (~60s) but faster than ReSample (~70s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DPnP-DDIM outperforms all three baselines on every task where a comparison is possible, on both FFHQ and ImageNet. Against DPS [CKM+23], the gap is largest in the nonlinear phase retrieval task: DPnP achieves PSNR 22.4 vs. 17.4 on FFHQ (a 5 dB improvement) and 13.4 vs. 13.4 on ImageNet (tied on PSNR but better LPIPS: 0.562 vs. 0.677). Against LGD-MC [SZY+23], DPnP wins on all tasks; on ImageNet super-resolution the LPIPS is tied (0.416) but DPnP gains 0.7 dB in PSNR (21.6 vs. 20.9), and on quantized sensing it leads by 0.7 dB (23.0 vs. 22.3). Against pixel-based ReSample [SKZ+23], DPnP wins on all solvable tasks and, critically, ReSample fails to produce meaningful images for phase retrieval entirely, while DPnP recovers fine structural details. The consistent advantage in the nonlinear tasks (phase retrieval, quantized sensing) highlights DPnP's robustness to general forward models, which is the paper's central theoretical contribution.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the plug-and-play reconstruction paradigm (Venkatakrishnan et al., 2013) from an optimization perspective to a posterior-sampling perspective, replacing the proximal operator with a denoising diffusion sampler (DDS) and the gradient step with a proximal consistency sampler (PCS). The DDS is the key technical contribution: it rigorously shows that image denoising under white Gaussian noise can be solved by both DDPM-type (via reversing a heat flow SDE) and DDIM-type (via reversing an OU process ODE) samplers using the same unconditional score functions trained for generation, without any additional training. This departs from prior diffusion-based inverse problem solvers (DPS, DDGM, ReSample) that either lack consistency guarantees, are restricted to linear forward models, or require expensive Monte Carlo procedures, and from Bayesian plug-and-play samplers (Vono et al., 2019; Cardoso et al., 2023) that did not employ score-based diffusion priors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DPnP provides the first provably consistent and robust posterior sampling framework for general nonlinear inverse problems using unconditional diffusion priors, with both asymptotic and non-asymptotic guarantees that degrade gracefully under score estimation and discretization errors. The plug-and-play modularity means no retraining is needed when switching forward models, making it broadly applicable across imaging modalities. Limitations include the reliance on a black-box total variation bound for sampler errors (whose tight analysis is deferred), the computational overhead (~90–240 s per sample) relative to baselines, and evaluation limited to 256×256 images. Open questions include whether the annealing schedule can be adapted per-task, how the theory extends to conditional or latent diffusion priors, and whether the non-asymptotic constants are practical at scale.
