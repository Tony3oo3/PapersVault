## Problem

Unsupervised domain adaptation methods for time series rely on complex architectures that are impractical for edge deployment, and existing knowledge distillation approaches that compress these models coarsely align teacher and student outputs over all target samples. This ignores the capacity gap between teacher and student and the unreliability of teacher predictions on unlabeled target data, causing negative transfer and poor distillation efficiency.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241495/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 1D time series signals (accelerometer/gyroscope for HAR/HHAR, motor current for fault diagnosis, EEG waveforms for sleep staging) along with softened teacher logits at temperature τ. A batch of unlabeled target samples is processed by the student's 1D-CNN feature extractor to produce state representations, which the dueling DDQN converts into binary retain/discard weights. The final output is a class label per sample, with the student achieving 15–22× parameter and inference-time reduction over the teacher while matching its target-domain accuracy.

## Method

RCD-KD formulates target sample selection as a Markov Decision Process solved by a dueling Double DQN with NoisyNet exploration, where the state is the student's feature representation of a target batch and the action is a binary retain/discard decision per sample. The reward function combines two Boolean signals: uncertainty consistency (whether student and MCD-estimated teacher entropies are both above or below the batch mean, via exclusive-nor) and sample transferability (whether the KL divergence between student and averaged MCD teacher predictions is below the batch mean), weighted by α₁ and α₂ to constrain rewards to [−1, 1]. In parallel, a domain discriminator (reused from the DANN-pretrained teacher) enforces domain-invariant feature alignment between teacher source features and student target features through an adaptor layer. The student is optimized end-to-end with L = L_DC + λτ²L_RKD, where L_RKD is the standard KD loss masked by the RL-selected sample weights, enabling adaptive knowledge transfer that respects the student's limited capacity.

## Results

On four time series datasets, RCD-KD achieves the highest Macro F1-score across all tasks: 94.68±1.62 on HAR, 82.37±1.84 on HHAR, 92.63±0.62 on FD, and 67.49±1.83 on SSC. The method outperforms the best UDA baseline (MDDA at 84.89 on HAR) by ~10 points and the best KD-based DA baseline (UNI-KD at 86.84 on HAR) by ~8 points. The compact student also achieves comparable performance to the full teacher (95.05 on HAR, 92.68 on FD) while reducing parameters by 15.46× and inference time by 21.67× on a Raspberry Pi 3B+.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RCD-KD consistently outperforms all 14 baselines across all four datasets. Against UDA methods applied directly to the compact student (Table 1), the gains are largest on HAR (+9.8 over MDDA) and SSC (+7.7 over HoMM), while the best UDA baselines show high variance (e.g., MDDA drops from 84.89 on HAR to 57.45 on SSC). Among KD-based DA methods (Tables 2–3), the closest competitor is the authors' prior work UNI-KD [4], which RCD-KD surpasses by 7.8 points on HAR (94.68 vs 86.84), 3.1 on HHAR (82.37 vs 79.32), 2.6 on FD (92.63 vs 90.06), and 6.1 on SSC (68.26 vs 62.17). Methods using source-only teachers (AAD, MobileDA) underperform those using cross-domain teachers, confirming the importance of target-domain knowledge. KD-STDA and MLD-DA, which transfer source-domain-specific knowledge, sometimes perform worse than Student-Only on certain scenarios, validating the paper's claim that blind knowledge transfer causes negative transfer. The RL-based sample selection module is the key differentiator: ablation shows that replacing it with explicit uncertainty or transferability thresholds (R₂, R₃) yields 1–3 point drops, while using all target samples (Full Samples) drops performance by 3–8 points.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of knowledge distillation (Hinton et al., 2015), adversarial domain adaptation (DANN, Ganin et al., 2016), and RL-based sample selection (Chen et al., 2020; Wu et al., 2022). Prior KD+DA methods (KD-STDA, MLD-DA, UNI-KD, KA-MCD) transfer teacher knowledge to all or heuristically-filtered target samples, but the paper argues they neglect the student's limited capacity and the unreliability of teacher predictions on unlabeled target data. The key departure is formulating target sample selection as a Markov Decision Process solved by a dueling DDQN with NoisyNet, where the reward combines Monte Carlo Dropout-based uncertainty consistency and KL-divergence-based transferability, rather than relying on fixed thresholds or source-domain confidence scores.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work addresses a practical bottleneck: deploying UDA models on resource-constrained edge devices (smartphones, robots) for real-time time series monitoring, where the student achieves 15× fewer parameters and 21× faster inference while matching teacher-level accuracy on several benchmarks. However, the training-time overhead is substantial (16.4s/epoch vs. 0.9–4.6s for baselines) due to K episodes of experience generation and N MCD forward passes, limiting immediate adoption. Open questions include whether joint teacher-student training could eliminate the need for a pre-trained cumbersome teacher, and whether feature-space (rather than logit-space) transferability metrics would capture richer structural information for sample selection.
