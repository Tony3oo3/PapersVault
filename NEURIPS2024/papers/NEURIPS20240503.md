## Problem

Existing physiological emotion datasets rely on objective stimulus-based labels or coarse scales (SAM, PANAS) that fail to capture the nuanced, subjective, and often mixed emotional experiences of participants. No prior dataset has paired raw self-reported textual descriptions of emotions with physiological signals, and language-guided contrastive pre-training—proven effective in vision, audio, and NLP—has not been applied to physiological signal-based emotion recognition.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240503/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes hand-crafted features extracted from EDA (downsampled to 15.625 Hz, decomposed into SCL and SCR via cvxEDA) and PPG (downsampled to 125 Hz, processed with NeuroKit2 for HRV time/frequency/non-linear features), alongside raw textual emotion descriptions transcribed from semi-structured qualitative interviews. Outputs are binary classification labels (low/high arousal, negative/positive valence, positive/negative emotion) and, during pre-training, a joint signal-text embedding space. All physiological segments are participant-wise min-max normalized, and arousal labels are oversampled to correct class skewness.

## Method

CLSP (Contrastive Language-Signal Pre-training) employs two separate encoders—a two-layer linear network (hidden dims 50, 100) for physiological signal features and a pre-trained DistilBERT for text—that project both modalities into a shared embedding space optimized with a CLIP-style contrastive loss, maximizing similarity of matched signal-text pairs and minimizing it for negatives. Baseline classification uses classical ML models (LR, RF, XGBoost, SVM, MLP) on the same hand-crafted features under leave-one-subject-out cross-validation. The key insight is that qualitative textual descriptions provide richer, more contextualized supervision than objective scale ratings, yielding emotion representations that transfer zero-shot to unseen datasets (Emognition, WESAD, NURSE) and outperform supervised baselines on arousal and valence classification.

## Results

On the EEVR dataset (296 text-signal pairs, LOSO), the proposed CLSP method with EDA+Text achieves 77.25% arousal accuracy (F1=0.81) and 70.38% valence accuracy (F1=0.73), substantially outperforming the hand-crafted features + NN baseline (57.27% arousal, 61.86% valence for EDA alone). Text-only DistilBERT classification reaches 97.44% stimulus-label accuracy and 91.73% valence accuracy, confirming the richness of the textual annotations. In zero-shot transfer, CLSP matches or exceeds supervised MLP baselines on the NURSE dataset (e.g., 84.93% vs 71.83% valence accuracy with EDA) and Emognition (53.23% vs 52.80% arousal with EDA), though it underperforms on WESAD where the supervised MLP reaches 91.67% arousal accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is between CLSP (contrastive text-signal pre-training) and a hand-crafted features + two-layer NN baseline without text supervision. CLSP with EDA+Text wins decisively on arousal (77.25% vs 57.27%, +19.98 pp) and valence (70.38% vs 61.86%, +8.52 pp), but loses on stimulus-label classification (64.19% vs 87.39%), suggesting the contrastive objective better captures dimensional affect than discrete stimulus categories. In zero-shot transfer to three external datasets, CLSP outperforms the supervised MLP on NURSE (e.g., 84.93% vs 71.83% valence with EDA; 55.48% vs 39.88% arousal) and roughly matches it on Emognition (53.23% vs 52.80% arousal with EDA), but falls well short on WESAD (75.00% vs 91.67% arousal with EDA+PPG), where the highly controlled TSST protocol makes supervised learning particularly effective. Text-only DistilBERT achieves near-perfect scores (97.44% stimulus, 91.73% valence, 89.94% arousal), indicating the textual descriptions carry strong signal but are not directly transferable to unseen subjects without the physiological grounding that CLSP provides.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts CLIP's contrastive image-text pre-training (Radford et al., 2021) to the physiological-signal domain, replacing the image encoder with hand-crafted EDA/PPG features and the text encoder with DistilBERT. It departs from prior emotion datasets (MAHNOB-HCI, DEAP, WESAD, VREED, StressID) that rely on objective scales (SAM, PANAS) or stimulus-based labels by introducing raw qualitative textual descriptions collected via semi-structured interviews as an additional supervision signal. The VR-based elicitation protocol follows the line of work by VREED and StressID, but the pairing of free-text emotion narratives with synchronized physiological recordings is, per the authors, unprecedented in the literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

EEVR is the first dataset to align raw textual emotion descriptions with physiological signals, opening a path toward language-supervised pre-training for affective computing that could reduce reliance on costly manual annotation. The CLSP results show meaningful gains in arousal and valence classification (e.g., 77.25% vs. 57.27% accuracy for EDA+Text vs. EDA alone), and zero-shot transfer to the NURSE hospital dataset outperforms supervised baselines, suggesting the learned representations capture more generalizable affective structure. However, the stimulus-label performance drops substantially under CLSP (64.19% vs. 87.39%), the zero-shot WESAD results underperform supervised baselines by wide margins, and the CLSP experiments report a single seed, leaving the robustness of the gains uncertain. Open questions include whether the textual descriptions add information beyond what structured scales already capture, how the approach scales to real-world, non-lab settings, and whether the privileged, educated participant pool limits generalizability.
