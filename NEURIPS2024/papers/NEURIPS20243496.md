## Problem

Existing 3D structure prediction methods rely almost exclusively on diffusion-based generative models, leaving other probability paths (e.g., Optimal Transport) underexplored, and they suffer from persistent hallucinations—bimodal output distributions where the model confidently generates structures far from ground truth—due to the absence of negative samples during training. It also remained unclear whether Direct Preference Optimization, a proven hallucination-mitigation strategy in NLP and vision, is theoretically compatible with arbitrary probability paths beyond standard diffusion.

## Input / Output

For antibody prediction, the model takes a CDR amino acid sequence (one-hot 20-dim per residue) together with the 3D backbone coordinates (N, Cα, C, O in R^{3×4}) of the flanking framework regions and antigen as context, and outputs the 3D backbone coordinates of the target CDR. For crystal prediction, the input is a one-hot atom-type vector, and the output is a 3×3 lattice matrix plus fractional coordinates constrained to the [0,1]³ torus manifold, requiring Wrapped Normal distributions and periodic-translation invariance. All vector fields must be E(3)-equivariant (rotation-equivariant, translation-invariant) to respect the physical symmetries of atomic systems.

## Method

FlowDPO is a two-stage framework. Stage 1 trains a flow matching model on an arbitrary Gaussian path (VE, VP, or OT) via the conditional flow matching MSE objective. Stage 2 constructs an automatic preference dataset by generating M candidates per training sample with the pre-trained model, ranking them by distance to ground truth, and forming (winner, loser) pairs; the model is then fine-tuned with a DPO loss. The key theoretical contribution is a unified derivation showing that, for any Gaussian path of the form x_i ~ N(k_i x_0, σ_i I), the per-step KL divergence in the DPO objective reduces to the same MSE between predicted and true vector fields used in flow matching training, yielding a closed-form DPO loss (Eq. 25) that is path-agnostic. This is achieved by discretizing time into T steps, applying Jensen's inequality to bound the full-path objective, and parameterizing the model's predicted mean via the DDIM-style reparameterization, so that the DPO gradient reuses the same network forward pass as the original flow matching loss.

## Results

On antibody CDR-H3 prediction (the most challenging region), FlowDPO with OT path reduces worst-case Cα-RMSD from 4.81 Å (OT baseline) to 4.28 Å, and from 5.01 Å (VP/DiffAb baseline) to 4.42 Å with VP+DPO. On crystal structure prediction, OT+VE+DPO achieves 62.47% Match Rate on MP-20 (vs. 58.94% for OT+VE without DPO, 51.49% for DiffCSP's VP+VE, and 15.39% for P-cG-SchNet) and 20.27% on MPTS-52 (vs. 18.91% for OT+VE, 12.19% for VP+VE). DPO consistently improves Match Rate across all three crystal datasets and all six CDRs, with the largest gains on the hardest cases (CDR-H3, MPTS-52).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DiffAb (VP path) [20] on antibody CDR-H3, FlowDPO with OT path reduces worst-case Cα-RMSD from 5.01 Å to 4.28 Å (14.6% improvement), and VP+DPO achieves 4.42 Å (11.8% improvement). On crystal prediction, FlowDPO (OT+VE+DPO) substantially outperforms P-cG-SchNet [9] (62.47% vs. 15.39% MR on MP-20; 20.27% vs. 3.67% on MPTS-52) and CDVAE [33] (62.47% vs. 33.90% on MP-20; 20.27% vs. 5.34% on MPTS-52). Compared to DiffCSP (VP+VE) [14], FlowDPO with OT+VE+DPO improves MP-20 Match Rate from 51.49% to 62.47% (+10.98 pp) and MPTS-52 from 12.19% to 20.27% (+8.08 pp), while also achieving lower RMSE (0.0606 vs. 0.0631 on MP-20). The OT path alone (without DPO) already outperforms VP+VE on MP-20 (58.94% vs. 51.49%), and DPO provides a further consistent boost of 1–4 pp across all path combinations and datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of flow matching generative models (Lipman et al., 2022) and Direct Preference Optimization (Rafailov et al., 2024), extending the latter's discretization strategy from diffusion models (Wallace et al., 2023) to the broader class of flow matching models with arbitrary Gaussian paths (VE, VP, OT). Prior 3D structure prediction work (DiffAb for antibodies, DiffCSP for crystals) relied exclusively on diffusion-based VP or VE paths; FlowDPO introduces OT paths as a viable alternative and, more importantly, provides the first theoretical proof that the DPO objective is compatible with any Gaussian path, yielding a unified training formulation. The preference dataset construction is automatic (self-distilled candidates ranked by RMSD to ground truth), distinguishing it from ABDPO which uses energy-based rewards for antibody design.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is the suppression of hallucinated distribution peaks in generative structure prediction: DPO shifts bimodal RMSD distributions toward a single high-quality mode, which is critical for blind selection of generated structures in drug design or materials discovery. However, the theoretical guarantee is restricted to Gaussian paths, and the evaluation relies solely on computational metrics (RMSD, Match Rate) without wet-lab validation, leaving open whether the alignment translates to physically meaningful improvements. A key open question is whether the framework extends to non-Gaussian paths or to tasks where a clean ground-truth reference is unavailable (e.g., de novo design), and whether the preference signal can be replaced by physics-based scoring to reduce dependence on reference structures.
