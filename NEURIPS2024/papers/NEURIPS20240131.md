## Problem

GNNs pre-trained on one graph dataset cannot transfer to datasets with different node feature dimensions, since neural networks require fixed input-output shapes. Existing workarounds either rebuild the model per dataset (non-transferable) or preprocess features via PCA or language models to a uniform shape, causing significant semantic information loss. The paper asks whether a contrastive pre-training framework can inherently handle arbitrary feature shapes without preprocessing, by leveraging the theoretical connection between PCA and contrastive learning.

## Input / Output

The method takes a graph with node feature matrix X ∈ R^{n×d} (where d varies freely across datasets) and adjacency matrix A, and produces node embeddings Z for downstream classification plus a learned basis transformation matrix T ∈ R^{d×k} that losslessly maps any feature dimension to a unified k-dimensional space. The dimension encoder DE(·) consumes only n_s randomly sampled nodes per feature dimension (not all n nodes), making it independent of dataset size, while the graph encoder GE(·) operates on the unified H = XT regardless of the original d.

## Method

FUG comprises three components: (1) a Dimension Encoder DE(·) that applies an MLP to sampled node values along each feature dimension to produce basis transformation vectors, generating T that converts arbitrary-shape features to a unified H = XT; (2) a standard 2-layer GCN graph encoder GE(·) that encodes both topology and unified features; and (3) a Relative Relationship Optimization loss L_RT-FUG with three terms — a global uniformity constraint (L_RT-FUG⁻, forcing mean of normalized embeddings to origin at O(n) cost instead of O(n²) negative sampling), a neighbor-based positive pair L2 loss (L_RT-FUG⁺, replacing data augmentation), and a basis vector uniformity loss (L_DE, ensuring T's columns are uniformly distributed on a hypersphere). The key theoretical insight is that PCA's variance-maximization objective is a special case of the contrastive L_RT loss (with X⁺ = ∅), and the Dim(·) component is precisely what grants PCA its universality across feature shapes — FUG retains this component while adding the homogeneity-encoding and flexible Fea(·) capabilities of contrastive learning.

## Results

On the intra-domain node classification task (10% labels, l2 classifier), FUG(Rebuild) achieves the best or near-best accuracy across all seven datasets: 84.45% on Cora, 72.43% on CiteSeer, 85.47% on PubMed, 93.07% on Photo, 88.42% on Computers, 92.89% on CS, and 95.45% on Physics. In the cross-domain setting (Table 2), FUG-C models pre-trained on a single dataset with fixed hyperparameters achieve performance within ~1% of FUG(Rebuild) on all target datasets, substantially outperforming universal pre-training baselines OFA and GraphControl. FUG also reduces the negative-sampling time complexity from O(n²) to O(n), with per-epoch time on CS of 0.0526s and VRAM of 2,482 MB, compared to GRACE's 0.2169s and 11,960 MB.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FUG outperforms GRACE on all seven datasets (e.g., 84.45 vs. 83.20 on Cora, 88.42 vs. 85.36 on Computers) while using far less VRAM (2,482 vs. 11,960 MB on CS), since FUG replaces O(n²) negative sampling with an O(n) global uniformity constraint. Against the negative-sample-free methods BGRL and GBT, FUG achieves higher accuracy on most datasets (e.g., 84.45 vs. 81.57 on Cora, 93.07 vs. 92.34 on Photo) at comparable or lower computational cost. FUG significantly surpasses PCA (e.g., 84.45 vs. 56.36 on Cora, 93.07 vs. 74.92 on Photo), validating that the learnable dimension encoder preserves far more semantic information than fixed PCA projection. In the cross-domain setting, FUG-C (fixed hyperparameters, pre-trained on one dataset) achieves performance within ~1% of FUG(Rebuild) on all target datasets, while OFA and GraphControl—both of which preprocess features via LLMs or similarity matrices—score substantially lower (e.g., OFA: 75.90 on Cora vs. FUG-C: 82.35–83.35). FUG also matches or approaches the supervised GCN upper bound on most datasets (e.g., 95.45 vs. 95.65 on Physics, 93.07 vs. 92.42 on Photo), demonstrating that the self-supervised pre-training captures near-supervised quality representations without any labels.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the theoretical observation from Shi et al. (ICLR 2023) that contrastive learning generalizes nonlinear PCA by making the PCA-like component explicitly learnable and integrating it into a graph contrastive framework. It departs from standard graph contrastive learning (GRACE, BGRL, GBT, DGI, GCA, ProGCL) by adding a dimensional encoding module that generates a learnable basis transformation matrix, and from recent universal graph pre-training models (OFA, GraphControl) by avoiding lossy preprocessing (LLM-based reshaping or similarity-matrix substitution) in favor of a differentiable, task-aligned encoding. The loss design replaces InfoNCE's O(n²) negative sampling with an O(n) global uniformity constraint and substitutes data augmentation with explicit neighbor-based positive pairs, drawing on augmentation-free GCL work (AFGRL, AFGCL, NeCo).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FUG addresses a practical bottleneck in graph pre-training: the inability of standard GNNs to transfer across datasets with different node feature dimensions without either retraining or lossy preprocessing. The theoretical unification of PCA and contrastive learning under a shared relative-relationship optimization framework provides a principled design template for future universal graph models. Key limitations include reliance on the homophily assumption for positive sampling (limiting applicability to heterophilic graphs) and the absence of absolute semantic constraints in the loss. Open questions include whether the dimensional encoding generalizes to truly heterogeneous feature types (text, images, floats mixed in one graph) and whether the approach scales to very large graphs where the sampled-node approximation may degrade.
