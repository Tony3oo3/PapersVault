## Problem

Existing privacy-preserving spoken language understanding (SLU) systems rely on disentanglement-based encoders that require tens of GFLOPs and tens of millions of parameters, making them impractical for resource-constrained wimpy devices such as STM32H7 microcontrollers with only 1 MB RAM. This paper exploits the observation that SLU intent extraction depends on long-term dependencies across the entire utterance, while ASR (the primary privacy threat) depends on short-term, frame-local phoneme information, to design a lightweight masking-based encoder that protects sensitive entities without degrading intent classification.

## Input / Output

SILENCE takes a raw speech audio waveform (e.g., a 4-second utterance) as input and produces a masked waveform via element-wise multiplication with a binary mask vector Z composed of k uniform contiguous portions of all-0s or all-1s. The masked waveform is transmitted to a cloud SLU model that has been fine-tuned to interpret masked inputs, which then outputs a structured SLU intent label (e.g., {scenario: Calendar, action: Create_entry}). The mask generator itself is extremely lightweight—SILENCE-S requires only 394.9 KB of memory and 912 ms of encoding time on an STM32H7—making it feasible for real-time streaming on wimpy hardware.

## Method

SILENCE's core mechanism is asymmetrical dependency-based masking: it partitions the utterance into segments and zeros out the majority of them, destroying the short-term phoneme information needed for ASR while preserving the long-term attention-based dependencies that SLU intent classification relies on. A differentiable mask generator, derived from interpretable learning (De Cao et al., 2020), automatically determines how many and which segments to mask by minimizing an L0 loss (count of unmasked portions) subject to a KL-divergence constraint ensuring the masked input still yields the correct SLU intent. Because the L0 objective is discontinuous, training uses a sparse relaxation of binary variables, and the generator is implemented as a small gate module atop a pre-trained HuBERT CNN feature extractor or a few HuBERT encoder layers. The system operates in two phases: offline, the mask generator is trained on ~200 audio samples and the cloud SLU model is fine-tuned on masked inputs; online, the client applies the learned mask in real time and forwards only the masked waveform plus the intent label to the cloud, exposing no raw speech to the adversary.

## Results

On SLURP, SILENCE-L achieves 81.1% intent classification accuracy (ACC-SLU) with a WER-SLU of 78.6% under Whisper attack and 81.6% under Azure ASR, while maintaining a 90.7% entity error rate. Under the strongest white-box attack (Whisper fine-tuned on leaked mask generator weights), SILENCE still retains 67.3% WER-SLU and 64.4% WER-ASR. On FSC, SILENCE achieves >99% intent accuracy with >80% WER. In terms of resource efficiency, SILENCE-S requires only 394.9 KB memory and 912.0 ms encoding latency on an STM32H7 microcontroller, achieving up to 53.3× speedup and 134.1× memory reduction over PPSLU on RPI-4B.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SILENCE outperforms PPSLU [15] (the SoTA disentanglement-based SLU privacy system) by 7.2% in intent classification accuracy on SLURP while achieving comparable WER (~78.6% vs. PPSLU's similar level), yet requires 18.1× less encoding latency and 134.1× less memory when both are deployed on RPI-4B. Against the VAE baseline [16], SILENCE achieves higher WER (stronger privacy) while maintaining competitive SLU accuracy, and avoids the costly adversarial training loop that VAE-based methods require. SILENCE also surpasses the unprotected AllOffload baseline in privacy (WER rises from ~14.7% to 78.6%) with only a 7% accuracy drop, and outperforms OnDevice in practicality since it offloads inference to the cloud. Under active inpainting attacks (U-Net, CQT-Diff), SILENCE's WER remains above 74%, indicating that the masked short-dependent frames cannot be reliably reconstructed by these models.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant disentanglement-based encoder paradigm (PPSLU, VAE) for privacy-preserving SLU, which relies on adversarial training of large transformer encoders to separate sensitive from task-relevant information. Instead, SILENCE exploits an asymmetric dependency observation—ASR is short-term dependent on local phoneme frames while SLU intent is long-term dependent on the full utterance—and reduces privacy protection to a simple binary masking operation on the raw waveform. The mask generator is derived from the differentiable masking framework of interpretable learning [27], repurposing it from post-hoc explanation to proactive privacy scrubbing, which is a novel application of that technique.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SILENCE makes privacy-preserving SLU offloading feasible on genuinely resource-constrained hardware (STM32H7, 1 MB RAM) where prior disentanglement encoders are simply infeasible, with 53× speedup and 134× memory reduction. However, the approach requires one-time cloud model fine-tuning per mask generator, and the white-box attack still recovers 33% of words, leaving a meaningful gap versus the 100% WER of on-device inference. Open questions include how the masking strategy generalizes to multi-turn dialogue, non-English languages, and whether the mask generator design space (acknowledged as a 'lock' problem) can be systematically explored for better privacy-accuracy-efficiency trade-offs.
