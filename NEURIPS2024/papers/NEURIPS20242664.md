## Problem

Offline model-based optimization (MBO) seeks to maximize a black-box function using only a pre-existing dataset, but prior forward approaches (robust surrogate + gradient ascent) struggle with out-of-distribution generalization, and inverse approaches (score-to-design mappings) fail to capture the landscape of the target function. Recent trajectory-based methods (BONET, PGS) improve on this by learning to improve solutions along sequences, but they rely on simple sorting heuristics that produce trajectories with inconsistent improvement directions, and autoregressive generation accumulates errors during sampling.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an offline dataset of (design, score) pairs and produces a set of top-Q candidate designs filtered by a learned proxy. During sampling, it conditions on a sub-trajectory of C context data points (inpainting the first C positions) and a target score value y*(τ) = α·H·y*, where α controls exploration. For discrete tasks (e.g., DNA sequences), inputs are converted to continuous vectors via soft interpolation between one-hot and uniform encoding with a mixing factor of 0.6; trajectories are represented as 2D arrays of H input-output pairs.

## Method

GTG constructs synthetic trajectories via a local-search strategy: starting from the p-th percentile of the dataset, each next point is sampled from K nearest neighbors whose score exceeds max{y₁,…,yₕ} − ε, injecting locality bias for consistent improvement while preventing convergence to a single optimum. A conditional diffusion model (temporal U-Net) is then trained to generate the entire trajectory at once, conditioned on the sum of trajectory scores via classifier-free guidance, bypassing the error accumulation of autoregressive models. At sampling time, classifier-free guidance (ω=1.2) steers generation toward high scores, and context conditioning inpaints C context positions from a random sub-trajectory to explore diverse high-scoring regions. Finally, a rank-reweighted MLP proxy filters the top-Q designs from all generated trajectory endpoints, exploiting dataset knowledge to reject low-fidelity candidates.

## Results

On the five Design-Bench tasks (TFBind8, TFBind10, Superconductor, Ant, D'Kitty), GTG achieves a mean rank of 1.6/15 across 16 competing methods, the best of all baselines. GTG attains the highest 100th-percentile score among Q=128 candidates on four of five tasks: TFBind8 (0.976), Superconductor (0.519), Ant (0.963), and D'Kitty (0.971), and is a close runner-up on TFBind10 (0.698 vs. NEMO's 0.705). In the toy Branin 2D experiment, GTG reaches a maximum score of −0.490 ± 0.070, far exceeding the dataset maximum (−6.031) and approaching the global optimum (−0.398). GTG also outperforms recent baselines in sparse (1%–50% data) and noisy (1%–50% Gaussian noise) settings, e.g., achieving 0.948 on TFBind8 with only 1% of the dataset versus 0.898 for BDI.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GTG outperforms all 13 baselines on four of five Design-Bench tasks and is a narrow runner-up on TFBind10 (0.698 vs. NEMO's 0.705). Against the strongest forward-approach baseline NEMO (mean rank 4.8/15), GTG wins by 0.035 on TFBind8, 0.017 on Superconductor, 0.011 on Ant, and 0.021 on D'Kitty. Among inverse/diffusion-based methods, GTG surpasses DDOM (which also uses a conditional diffusion model) on every task, with the largest margin on Superconductor (0.519 vs. 0.476, +0.043), demonstrating that generating full trajectories is more effective than generating a single design. Compared to the closest trajectory-based competitors BONET (autoregressive) and PGS (RL policy), GTG leads by 0.145 on TFBind8, 0.044 on Superconductor, and 0.215 on Ant, confirming that the locality-biased trajectory construction and diffusion-based generation jointly yield more consistent improvement directions. In sparse-data settings (1% of dataset), GTG achieves 0.948 on TFBind8 and 0.949 on D'Kitty, outperforming the next-best methods (BDI at 0.898 and ICT at 0.899/0.946 respectively), and remains competitive under 50% label noise (0.948 on TFBind8, 0.937 on D'Kitty).

## Relation to Prior Work

GTG extends the trajectory-based paradigm for offline MBO introduced by BONET (autoregressive trajectory generation) and PGS (RL policy-guided search) by replacing sequential generation with a conditional diffusion model that produces entire trajectories at once, thereby avoiding error accumulation. It builds directly on the diffusion probabilistic model framework (Ho et al., 2020) and classifier-free guidance (Ho & Salimans, 2021) for conditional generation, but departs from inverse-approach diffusion methods like DDOM that generate a single design by instead modeling the joint distribution over sequences of improving designs. The key methodological departure is the locality-biased trajectory construction strategy, which contrasts with the sorting heuristics (BONET) and high-score sampling (PGS) used by prior trajectory methods, and the context-conditioning mechanism during sampling that has no direct analogue in prior MBO work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GTG demonstrates that learning *how to improve* solutions (via trajectories) is more effective than learning *what high-scoring designs look like* (via inverse mapping) for offline MBO, and that diffusion models are a natural fit for this trajectory-generation objective. The consistent outperformance across five diverse Design-Bench tasks and robustness to 1% data sparsity and 50% label noise suggest the approach generalizes well to practical settings where large clean datasets are unavailable. Open questions include: the reliance on a proxy model for final filtering (which may be unreliable in truly OOD regions), the assumption that the optimal score y* is known, and the performance-diversity trade-off introduced by filtering. The paper also identifies that the superconductor task's oracle is unreliable due to duplicate inputs with different outputs, highlighting a data-quality concern for the benchmark itself.
