## Problem

Self-training under distribution shifts suffers from increasingly noisy pseudo labels, making confidence-based filtering unreliable and forcing reliance on computationally expensive neighborhood or ensemble-based corrections. While early learning regularization (ELR) showed that temporal consistency can help, its impact on self-training was not fully understood and it does not account for the unique characteristics of distribution shifts. The paper asks whether there is a principled, uncertainty-aware way to generalize temporal consistency specifically for self-training under distribution shifts.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained ResNet (ResNet-50 or ResNet-101) trained on a source domain and a stream of unlabeled RGB images from the target domain. At each self-training iteration, it produces a regularized pseudo label — a convex combination of the current one-hot prediction and a weighted temporal ensemble of past hard predictions — which replaces the standard one-hot pseudo label in the cross-entropy loss. No additional forward passes are required; only the running weighted sum of past predictions and an EMA of prediction confidence are stored, keeping computational overhead negligible relative to vanilla self-training.

## Method

AnCon constructs an uncertainty-aware generalized temporal ensemble by assigning non-zero weights only to past predictions whose confidence exceeds an exponential moving average (EMA) threshold, effectively selecting relatively confident predictions for aggregation. This ensemble serves as a smoothing vector in a label-smoothing formulation: the training target becomes (1−λ)·one-hot(current pseudo label) + λ·temporal ensemble, rather than an auxiliary regularization loss as in ELR. The key theoretical results show that (i) the relative-thresholding ensemble is asymptotically correct when the average accuracy of confident predictions exceeds 50% (Theorem 3.1), and (ii) the label-smoothing formulation provably reduces the optimality gap of self-training compared to vanilla self-training by shrinking the effective neighborhood size of the stochastic gradient (Theorem 3.2). The approach connects AnCon to self-distillation via knowledge distillation theory, and the weighting mechanism is shown to be a relaxed solution to an otherwise intractable ensemble-weight optimization problem. A single fixed hyperparameter configuration (λ=0.3, β=0.9) works across all tested benchmarks without per-dataset tuning.

## Results

On the SFDA benchmark (Table 1), AnCon improves vanilla self-training by 5% on Office-31 (81.37 vs. 80.47 avg accuracy), 6% on OfficeHome (63.91 vs. 61.55), and 13% on VisDa (71.11 vs. 67.77). Under ImageNet-C synthetic corruptions, AnCon improves self-training accuracy by 16% on average, with gains reaching 52% at the most extreme corruption intensity (e.g., 22.56% vs. 0.26% for Shot noise at intensity 5). When combined with state-of-the-art methods, NRC+AnCon achieves the best overall results: 89.07% on Office-31, 67.96% on OfficeHome, and 83.70% on VisDa.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AnCon consistently outperforms vanilla self-training across all three SFDA datasets, with the largest gain on VisDa (+3.34 points). Compared to ELR (the closest prior method), AnCon wins on Office-31 (+0.53) and OfficeHome (+0.88) but slightly trails on VisDa (−0.78), though it achieves dramatically better accuracy on low-performing classes (e.g., 50% vs. 39% on 'Skateboard'). When stacked on top of GCE, AnCon improves accuracy by 0.34–3.20 points over GCE alone, and when combined with NRC, it yields the strongest results across all datasets (e.g., 89.07% vs. 87.42% on Office-31, 83.70% vs. 74.30% on VisDa), demonstrating that temporal and local consistency are complementary. In the UDA setting (Table 2), all self-training variants including AnCon slightly degrade CDAN and MDD performance, though AnCon's degradation is the smallest among the three self-training methods tested.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper generalizes Early Learning Regularization (ELR) by replacing its fixed exponential-decay temporal ensemble with an uncertainty-aware ensemble that selectively weights predictions via relative confidence thresholding, and by implementing temporal consistency through label smoothing rather than an auxiliary loss. The label smoothing formulation connects AnCon to knowledge distillation (specifically self-distillation), enabling a convergence analysis via modified partial variance reduction theory. Unlike neighborhood-based methods (NRC, centroid correction) or augmentation-consistency approaches that require expensive forward passes or memory banks, AnCon achieves temporal consistency with only the storage of past prediction vectors, matching ELR's computational footprint.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AnCon provides the first theoretical guarantees (asymptotic correctness of the temporal ensemble and optimality gap reduction) for a temporal-consistency-based self-training method under distribution shifts, moving the field beyond heuristic justifications. The practical impact is notable: 8–16% accuracy gains without additional forward passes, robustness to hyperparameter choices (<1% variation across extreme values), and improved calibration—properties that matter when model selection under shift is inherently unreliable. Open questions include adaptively determining the smoothing coefficient λ, combining local (neighborhood) and temporal consistency for further relaxation of optimality conditions, and extending selective temporal consistency to sequential decision-making settings where reward signals and exploration-exploitation trade-offs fundamentally change the pseudo-label generation problem.
