## Problem

High-resolution (sub-meter) tree crown delineation requires diverse training data spanning multiple biomes and environments, yet existing open-access datasets are small, geographically limited to single countries, or lack instance-level annotations. This gap hinders the development of globally generalizable tree mapping models, particularly for sparse tree cover outside forested areas that is invisible at satellite resolution.

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes 2048×2048 px RGB GeoTIFF tiles at 10 cm/px resolution (EPSG:3395, JPEG-compressed), which can be down-scaled to 0.8 m at 256 px or up-sampled from parent orthomosaics. Outputs are provided in two formats: MS-COCO instance segmentation polygons with two classes (individual tree, closed-canopy group) and binary semantic masks for tree canopy presence. Training uses 1024×1024 random crops (~100 m² spatial context) with standard augmentations (flips, rotation, blur, color jitter) and ImageNet normalization.

## Method

The primary contribution is the OAM-TCD dataset: 5,072 globally sampled tiles from OpenAerialMap with 280k+ individual tree and 56k+ canopy-group polygon annotations, stratified across WWF terrestrial ecoregions. For baseline models, the authors train UNet (ResNet34/50) and SegFormer (mit-b0 through b5) for semantic segmentation, and Mask-RCNN (ResNet50, Detectron2) for instance segmentation, all initialized from MS-COCO or ImageNet pre-trained weights. Annotations were generated via a weak-labeling loop (initial Mask-RCNN predictions refined by professional annotators with ecology-expert review), with a deliberate choice to label dense canopy regions as a single 'group' class rather than forcing individual delineation. Evaluation uses biome-stratified 5-fold cross-validation plus a 10% holdout split with no orthomosaic overlap, and independent benchmarks against Zurich's municipal tree inventory/LIDAR CHM and the WeRobotics Tonga orthomosaic.

## Results

For semantic segmentation on the OAM-TCD holdout set, the best model (SegFormer mit-b5) achieves IoU=0.876, Accuracy=0.890, and F1=0.902, with 5-fold cross-validation yielding IoU=0.887±0.003. For instance segmentation, Mask-RCNN (ResNet50) achieves mAP50=43.22 on the holdout set (41.79±1.38 in cross-validation). On the independent Zurich benchmark, the SegFormer model shows strong agreement with LIDAR-derived canopy height models (IoU=0.791, Accuracy=0.922, F1=0.883), and on the WeRobotics Tonga orthomosaic, recall reaches 0.94 when including canopy regions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not benchmark against external tree-detection methods on a shared dataset, but provides several meaningful comparisons. Among architectures, SegFormer consistently outperforms UNet by 2–4 points in IoU (e.g., mit-b5 at 0.876 vs. ResNet50 at 0.849 on holdout), though gains plateau from mit-b3 onward, suggesting annotation noise limits further improvement. On the Zurich independent benchmark, the SegFormer model achieves IoU=0.791 against a LIDAR-derived canopy height model, with the authors noting that RGB-based delineation can actually be sharper than LIDAR in urban settings due to lower LIDAR resolution and misclassified returns. For instance segmentation, the Mask-RCNN model achieves a recall of 0.36 against the Zurich municipal tree inventory keypoints (0.73 when including canopy regions), and 0.64/0.94 on the WeRobotics Tonga dataset, with the authors attributing the lower keypoint recall to trees being grouped rather than individually segmented. The paper briefly notes that DeepForest reports mAP50=50 on NeonTreeEvaluation, but this is on bounding boxes rather than instance masks and over a single-country dataset, making direct comparison limited.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground primarily through its dataset rather than its methods. Prior open-access tree detection datasets (NeonTreeEvaluation, DetecTree2, AutoArborist) are geographically limited to single countries or require access requests, and most provide bounding boxes or keypoints rather than polygon-level instance masks. OAM-TCD departs from this by sourcing imagery globally from OpenAerialMap, providing instance-level polygon annotations for both individual trees and closed-canopy groups, and releasing everything under permissive licenses. Methodologically, the paper uses standard architectures (UNet, SegFormer, Mask-RCNN) as baselines without novel model contributions, positioning itself as a benchmark and resource rather than a methodological advance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

OAM-TCD fills a critical gap as the largest open-access, globally diverse, sub-meter-resolution tree detection dataset, enabling cross-biome benchmarking that was previously impossible with single-country data. The practical validation against Zurich's municipal tree inventory and LIDAR-derived canopy height model demonstrates real-world applicability for urban and ecological monitoring. Key limitations include under-representation of boreal, tundra, and flooded grassland biomes, annotation inconsistencies from the human labelling process, and the deliberate choice to treat closed canopy as a crowd class rather than segmenting individuals. Open questions include whether the performance plateau across model sizes (SegFormer b0 through b5) reflects a fundamental annotation-quality ceiling, and how the dataset can be expanded to cover currently missing biomes.
