## Problem

Existing LLM unlearning benchmarks (WHP, WMDP, TOFU) rely on accessible forget/retain corpora, use fictitious or narrow knowledge sources, and evaluate only with non-adversarial probes, making them impractical for real-world deployment. There is no comprehensive benchmark that tests unlearning under a realistic zero-shot setting (no corpora provided), uses knowledge already present in mainstream LLMs, and rigorously audits efficacy through adversarial attacks and membership inference.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243116/cropped_page5_idx4.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark takes only an unlearning target (one of 200 real-world famous people) and the original pre-trained LLM as input, with no forget or retain corpus provided. The output is an unlearned model scored across four evaluation axes: a forget set of 13,131 multi-level probes (3,268 fill-in-the-blank, 2,879 QA, 6,984 adversarial-attack), a neighbor set of 11,379 probes for locality, a MIA set (6,198 forget + 7,487 retain fragments) for membership inference, and a utility set spanning MMLU, BBH, TruthfulQA, TriviaQA, and AlpacaEval. To bridge the gap between the zero-shot setting and methods that require a forget corpus, the original model is prompted to generate a synthetic forget corpus aligned with its own internal memory.

## Method

RWKU introduces a zero-shot knowledge unlearning benchmark where the only inputs are the target entity and the model, eliminating secondary information leakage and distribution bias from corpora. The 200 unlearning targets are selected from Wikipedia by popularity ranking and validated via exact-memorization and NLL quantification across Phi-2, LLaMA2, and LLaMA3 to confirm widespread knowledge presence. The evaluation framework operates at two levels: knowledge memorization (fill-in-the-blank probes scored by ROUGE-L plus four MIA methods: LOSS, Zlib Entropy, Min-K% Prob, Min-K%++ Prob) and knowledge manipulation (QA probes plus nine adversarial attack types including prefix injection, role playing, reverse query, and cross-lingual probing). Retain assessment covers locality via neighbor perturbation and utility across five dimensions (general ability, reasoning, truthfulness, factuality, fluency). Six baseline methods (ICU, RepE, GA, DPO, NPO, RT) are evaluated on LLaMA3-8B and Phi-3-3.8B under both single-target and batch-target (10–50 targets) scenarios, revealing that adversarial probes and MIA expose residual knowledge that standard QA probes miss, and that batch unlearning risks model collapse.

## Results

On LLaMA3-Instruct (8B), ICU achieves the best overall forget score (12.8 ROUGE-L, down from 79.6 before unlearning) but severely degrades neighbor knowledge (55.7 vs. 90.7). Among parameter-modifying methods, NPO (Full) attains the best forget score (26.2) while retaining 72.6 neighbor score, and GA (Full) achieves 41.9 forget with 79.0 neighbor. All methods trained on the synthetic forget corpus fail under MIA (FM scores remain close to RM, e.g., GA-LoRA: 224.1 vs. 221.6), indicating that memorized knowledge persists despite apparent behavioral unlearning. DPO (Full) causes the largest utility damage, dropping factuality from 53.5 to 25.8 and truthfulness from 36.8 to 31.5.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ICU (Pawelczyk et al., 2023) achieves the strongest forget score (12.8 All) on LLaMA3 but at a severe locality cost: neighbor score drops from 90.7 to 55.7, and it has almost no effect on Phi-3. Among parameter-modifying methods, NPO (Zhang et al., 2024) delivers the best forget score (26.2 All) with moderate neighbor retention (72.6), while GA (Jang et al., 2023) achieves a more balanced trade-off (41.9 forget, 79.0 neighbor) and best preserves reasoning (42.0 vs. 42.3 baseline). DPO (Rafailov et al., 2023) matches GA on forget (41.9) but causes the largest utility degradation, halving factuality (25.8 vs. 53.5) and dropping truthfulness by 5.3 points. Rejection Tuning (Maini et al., 2024, RT) is most effective against adversarial attacks (22.8 AA) but sacrifices fluency most severely (588.1 vs. 705.8). RepE (Zou et al., 2023) is the weakest overall, with the lowest neighbor score (42.6) and a catastrophic factuality drop (17.9). Across all methods, LoRA variants consistently unlearn less (higher forget scores) but preserve neighbor knowledge better, consistent with the 'LoRA learns less and forgets less' finding.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LLM knowledge unlearning line initiated by Eldan & Russinovich (WHP), Jang et al. (gradient ascent), and Maini et al. (TOFU), but departs from all prior benchmarks in two key ways: it adopts a 'zero-shot' setting where neither the forget corpus nor the retain corpus is accessible (addressing the practical infeasibility of accessing pre-training data), and it introduces adversarial-attack probes and membership inference attacks into the evaluation protocol, which prior benchmarks (WHP, WMDP, TOFU) entirely lack. The unlearning methods evaluated (GA, DPO, NPO, RT, ICU, RepE) are drawn directly from the existing method literature; the paper's contribution is the benchmark and evaluation framework rather than a new unlearning algorithm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark fills a critical gap: prior unlearning evaluations were non-adversarial and relied on accessible corpora, making them optimistic about real-world robustness. The finding that adversarial probes and fill-in-the-blank tests reveal 'forgotten' knowledge far more effectively than standard QA, and that nearly all methods fail under MIA, suggests the field's current methods are substantially weaker than previously reported. Key limitations include reliance on a single knowledge source (famous people), no error bars across seeds, and the zero-shot setting's dependence on the model's own generation quality for the synthetic forget corpus. Open questions include whether truly corpus-free unlearning is possible, how to prevent model collapse in batch settings, and whether the early-layer unlearning observation generalizes across architectures.
