## Problem

AdaBoost is provably sub-optimal for weak-to-strong learning by a logarithmic factor in 1/ε, yet all existing sample-optimal alternatives (Larsen-Ritzert, Bagging+Boosting) invoke the weak learner far more times than AdaBoost, making them impractically slow. The paper asks whether one can achieve optimal sample complexity with AdaBoost-matching runtime, and provides the first empirical comparison among all known optimal weak-to-strong learners.

## Input / Output

The algorithm takes a labeled training set S of m i.i.d. samples from a distribution D over X×{-1,1} and a γ-weak learner W using a hypothesis set H of VC-dimension d. It outputs a binary classifier g: X→{-1,1} formed as a majority vote of 5 independently trained AdaBoost voting classifiers. The weak learner is invoked O(γ^{-2} ln m) times total—matching AdaBoost's invocation count—since each of the 5 AdaBoost runs operates on only m/5 samples.

## Method

MAJORITY-OF-5 partitions the training set into 5 disjoint pieces of size m/5, runs AdaBoost on each piece to produce voting classifiers f_1,…,f_5, and returns the majority vote g(x)=sign(Σ f_t(x)). The key insight is that while a single AdaBoost is provably sub-optimal by a ln(1/ε) factor, combining 5 independently trained AdaBoosts on disjoint subsets eliminates this gap: the probability that 3 of 5 classifiers all err on a point x is p_x^3, which, when summed over a partition of the domain by error-probability bins R_i, yields the optimal O(d/(γ²m)) expected error. The proof leverages Rademacher-complexity-based generalization bounds for large-margin voting classifiers and builds on the partitioning technique of Aden-Ali et al. for optimal PAC learning. The number 5 is conjectured to be an artifact of the proof, with 3 potentially sufficient given tighter margin-based generalization bounds.

## Results

The paper presents a pilot empirical study (no tabular results; findings are shown as accuracy-vs.-number-of-boosters plots in Figures 1 and 2) comparing MAJORITY-OF-5 against AdaBoost, BaggedAdaBoost, and LarsenRitzert on five datasets ranging from 768 to 495,141 samples. On the three large datasets (Higgs, MiniBooNE, Forest Cover), MAJORITY-OF-5 shows a visible accuracy advantage over the other methods, while the two previously known optimal learners (LarsenRitzert and BaggedAdaBoost) perform no better than, or even worse than, plain AdaBoost. On the two small datasets (Diabetes with 768 samples, Adversarial with 1,024 samples), MAJORITY-OF-5 performs poorly because each of its five sub-boosters receives very few samples, and BaggedAdaBoost instead outperforms classic AdaBoost. The authors suggest a practical recipe of running both MAJORITY-OF-X and BaggedAdaBoost and selecting the better classifier via a validation set.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against classic AdaBoost (Freund & Schapire, 1997), MAJORITY-OF-5 achieves higher accuracy on the three large datasets (Higgs, MiniBooNE, Forest Cover) while performing worse on the two small datasets (Diabetes, Adversarial), where the per-partition training sets become too small for effective boosting. Against BaggedAdaBoost (Larsen, COLT 2023), the comparison is reversed: BaggedAdaBoost wins on small datasets due to its bootstrap-with-replacement sampling that keeps each sub-learner well-supplied, while MAJORITY-OF-5 edges ahead on large datasets where the disjoint partitioning provides better diversity. Against LarsenRitzert (Larsen & Ritzert, NeurIPS 2022), the SUBSAMPLE-based algorithm performs no better than or even worse than plain AdaBoost in all experiments, likely due to the high overhead of its m^0.79 sub-samples and the random subsampling of those sub-samples on large datasets that the authors had to introduce for computational feasibility. Overall, no single optimal weak-to-strong learner dominates across all dataset sizes, motivating the authors' recommendation to combine MAJORITY-OF-X with BaggedAdaBoost and select via validation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two recent lines of work: the sample-optimal weak-to-strong learners of Larsen & Ritzert (NeurIPS 2022) and Larsen (COLT 2023), and the majority-vote analysis of Aden-Ali et al. (COLT 2024) for realizable PAC learning. The key departure is conceptual: while prior optimal algorithms either invoke the weak learner m^0.8 times (Larsen-Ritzert) or require O(γ^{-2} ln(m/δ) ln m) invocations (BaggedAdaBoost), MAJORITY-OF-5 matches AdaBoost's O(γ^{-2} ln m) invocation count while achieving optimal sample complexity, simply by partitioning data into 5 disjoint subsets and majority-voting. The proof technique adapts the Rademacher-complexity-based margin analysis from Aden-Ali et al. to the boosting setting, showing that a constant ensemble of a provably sub-optimal algorithm (AdaBoost) becomes sample-optimal.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result is elegant in its simplicity — a 5-line algorithm achieves what required complex sub-sampling or bagging machinery before — and the runtime advantage over prior optimal learners is practically meaningful for large-scale tabular data. However, the guarantee is only in expectation (no high-probability bound), the number 5 is acknowledged as a proof artifact (conjectured reducible to 3), and the empirical evidence is explicitly a pilot study with 5 datasets, 5 random seeds, and no error bars. Open questions include whether the constant can be tightened, whether the expected-error guarantee suffices for practical deployment, and whether the observed large-dataset advantage of MAJORITY-OF-5 over plain AdaBoost holds under more rigorous statistical testing.
