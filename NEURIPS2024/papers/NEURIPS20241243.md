## Problem

All existing identifiability guarantees for interventional causal representation learning (CRL) assume access to an infinite number of observed samples, leaving the fundamental question of how many finite samples are actually needed for probabilistic recovery of latent causal variables and their DAG structure completely open. This paper establishes the first non-asymptotic sample complexity analysis for interventional CRL, bridging the gap between infinite-sample identifiability theory and finite-sample statistical guarantees.

## Input / Output

The method consumes N i.i.d. samples of observed variables X ∈ R^d from each of n+1 environments (one observational plus n single-node soft interventional environments with unknown intervened nodes), where X = G·Z for an unknown full-column-rank linear map G ∈ R^{d×n} and latent variables Z ∈ R^n. It produces three outputs: a DAG estimate whose transitive closure is isomorphic to the true graph (up to the unknown node permutation I), an inverse transformation matrix H ∈ R^{n×d} that recovers latent variables up to mixing with their direct parents (a refinement over the standard ancestor-mixing guarantee), and a valid causal ordering. The graph and variable recovery are fully decoupled, enabling independent estimation.

## Method

The approach is score-based: it exploits the fact that score difference functions d_X^m = s_X^m − s_X between interventional and observational environments encode both the graph structure (nonzero entries correspond to ancestors of the intervened node) and the inverse transformation G†. Three algorithms operate on the correlation matrices R_X^m of these score differences: Algorithm 1 identifies a valid causal order via approximate rank tests on sums of R_X^m, Algorithm 2 recovers edges via approximate subspace orthogonality tests between column and null spaces, and Algorithm 3 constructs the inverse transform row-by-row from column space directions. Robustness to finite-sample score estimation noise is handled through thresholded approximate column/null spaces (parameter η) and approximate orthogonality (parameter γ), with Davis-Kahan sin θ theorems and Weyl's inequality bounding error propagation. Sample complexity is first characterized for any consistent score difference estimator, then specialized to an RKHS-based estimator [12] to yield explicit O((log(1/δ))^4) samples for graph recovery and O(((1/ε)·log(1/δ))^4) samples for ε-accurate variable recovery.

## Results

The paper establishes the first finite-sample complexity bounds for interventional CRL: O((log(8n/δ))^4) samples suffice for latent graph recovery (up to ancestors) with probability 1−δ, and O((1/ε · log(8n/δ))^4) samples suffice for latent variable recovery within ε of the identifiability class. Numerical experiments (n∈{3,5,10}, d∈{n,15}, N up to 10^5) show that the critical model constant η* decays exponentially with latent dimension n, while β and β_min decrease only mildly. In the low score-MSE regime, graph recovery success (1−δ) decays linearly with log of the score estimation MSE, confirming that score estimation error is the dominant bottleneck.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not compare against competing CRL algorithms in the traditional benchmark sense; rather, it provides the first finite-sample (non-asymptotic) identifiability and sample-complexity guarantees for interventional CRL, where all prior work (e.g., [5,7,8,11]) established only infinite-sample identifiability. The key improvement over existing literature is in the latent variable recovery guarantee: the paper recovers latent variables up to mixing with *parents* (Theorem 2), which is a strict refinement of the prior guarantee of recovery up to mixing with *ancestors* [5,7]. The sample complexity depends on model constants (β, β_min, η*, γ*) that are shown to be independent of the observation dimension d, and the explicit n-dependence is O((n·log(n/δ))^4) for graph recovery and O((√n/ε·log(n/δ))^4) for variable recovery.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the score-based CRL framework of Varıcı et al. [5] and the identifiability results under soft interventions of [8] from the infinite-sample regime to the finite-sample regime, establishing the first PAC-style sample complexity guarantees for interventional CRL. The methodology builds on the RKHS-based nonparametric score estimator of Zhou et al. [12] to convert generic consistency conditions into explicit sample complexity bounds, and leverages Davis-Kahan sin θ theorems and Weyl's inequality to propagate score estimation errors through approximate rank and subspace orthogonality tests. A notable departure from prior work is the refinement of latent variable recovery from 'up to mixing with ancestors' (as in [5, 7]) to 'up to mixing with parents,' a tighter identifiability class.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work fills a critical gap: all prior interventional CRL identifiability results assumed infinite samples, making them inapplicable to real-world finite datasets. The established O((log 1/δ)^4) and O((1/ε · log 1/δ)^4) sample complexities provide the first concrete guidance on how many samples are needed for reliable causal structure and latent variable recovery. However, the exponential decay of the critical constant η* with latent dimension n (observed empirically) suggests practical scalability may be limited, and the analysis is restricted to linear transformations and soft interventions. Open directions include extending to hard interventions (for perfect identifiability), general nonlinear transformations, and understanding whether the 4th-power dependence on log(1/δ) is tight or an artifact of the RKHS estimator.
