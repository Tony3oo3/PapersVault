## Problem

Existing methods for estimating 3D interaction regions (human contact and object affordance) rely on exocentric views where both interacting parties are fully visible. In egocentric views, the subject's hands or body are often partially or completely out of sight (e.g., sitting, hand manipulation with head rotation), creating ambiguity between visual observations and actual interaction content that undermines observation-based methods. No prior work jointly captures 3D human contact and object affordance from egocentric videos.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 32-frame egocentric video clips (processed per-frame by HRNet-W40 then joint space-time attention), relative head motion (T×12: translation differences and rotation matrices relative to the first frame), and a 3D object point cloud (2000 points). It produces temporal dense human contact as per-vertex probabilities on the 6890-vertex SMPL body (T×6890×1), per-point 3D object affordance (2000×1), and a 12-class interaction category label. The contact output is decoupled in decoding: feature dimension is mapped to 1 first, then spatial dimension is projected to the SMPL vertex sequence.

## Method

EgoChoir uses three modality encoders (HRNet + joint space-time attention for video, an MLP pre-trained via KL-divergence matching between visual and motion feature discrepancies for head pose, and DGCNN for point clouds) feeding into a two-stage parallel cross-attention pipeline. In the first stage, object geometry concatenated with a functionality semantic token queries video and motion features (scaled by learnable modulation tokens) in parallel to extract 3D affordance; in the second stage, video features plus an intention token query the affordance and motion features in parallel to model human contact, with a final cross-attention between contact and affordance features enforcing spatial synergy. The key mechanism is gradient modulation: learnable tokens (τ) are Hadamard-multiplied with key-value features before mapping, which scales the backpropagated gradients of specific cross-attention layers, enabling the model to adaptively weight visual, motion, and geometric clues differently across interaction scenarios (e.g., relying more on head motion for body interactions where hands are invisible, versus visual appearance for hand interactions). Training uses focal + dice loss for contact and affordance, cross-entropy for category, with the motion encoder pre-trained separately to avoid collapse from joint optimization.

## Results

On the EgoChoir dataset (1,570 egocentric video clips, 300K+ frames, 12 interactions, 18 object categories), EgoChoir achieves 0.78 Precision, 0.79 Recall, 0.76 F1, and 12.62 cm geodesic error for 3D human contact, and 78.02 AUC, 14.94 aIOU, 0.436 SIM for 3D object affordance. These represent relative improvements of up to 85% (Precision) and 55% (aIOU) over the strongest baselines. When whole-body motion replaces head motion, performance further improves to 0.80 Precision, 0.82 Recall, 0.79 F1, 11.24 cm geodesic error, 78.54 AUC, 15.46 aIOU, and 0.448 SIM.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EgoChoir outperforms all five baselines across every metric. For human contact, BSTRO [29] (0.42 F1, 43.21 cm geo.) and DECO [80] (0.53 F1, 29.57 cm geo.) rely heavily on visual observations of interacting parties, which are incomplete in egocentric views, leading to large gaps. LEMON [102] (0.67 F1, 21.43 cm geo.) partially addresses this by modeling human-object geometric correlations, but its parallel architecture still suffers from ambiguous egocentric appearances. For object affordance, O2O [56] (71.52 AUC, 9.65 aIOU) and IAG [101] (74.30 AUC, 11.21 aIOU) estimate affordance from exocentric observations or 2D interaction content, which degrades under egocentric occlusion. EgoChoir's advantage is most pronounced on body interactions (e.g., 'sit', 'lay') where interaction regions are largely invisible, with per-category F1 improvements of up to 65% over LEMON (0.79 vs. 0.48 for 'sit'). Even when foot contact is excluded to prevent metric inflation, EgoChoir retains the best performance (0.65 F1 vs. 0.50 for LEMON).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of 3D interaction region estimation (human contact via DECO/BSTRO, object affordance via O2O-Afford/IAG-Net, joint estimation via LEMON) from exocentric to egocentric settings, where incomplete observations of interacting parties create fundamental ambiguity. It builds on EgoDistill's paradigm of associating head motion feature variation with visual appearance variation for the motion encoder pre-training, and on egocentric video understanding work (action recognition, anticipation) that captures semantic and temporal aspects but neglects spatial ('where') perception. The key conceptual departure is the parallel cross-attention with gradient modulation, which lets the model adaptively weight visual, motion, and geometric clues depending on the interaction scenario (e.g., relying more on head motion when hands are out of sight).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first benchmark and method for jointly estimating 3D human contact and object affordance from egocentric videos, filling a critical spatial gap in egocentric interaction understanding that limits applications in embodied AI, robotics, and AR/VR. The gradient modulation mechanism for scenario-adaptive clue selection is a reusable design pattern beyond this specific task. However, the scope is limited to 12 interaction types and 18 object categories, temporal precision remains imperfect (slight before/after drift), and the absence of 3D scene context and whole-body motion as inputs constrains spatial accuracy. Future work incorporating scene geometry and full-body pose could substantially improve fine-grained estimation.
