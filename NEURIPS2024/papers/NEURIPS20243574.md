## Problem

Multi-instance point cloud registration requires aligning a single CAD model to all its instances in a cluttered scene, but existing methods—whether two-stage (global correspondence + clustering) or one-stage (instance-aware correspondence)—struggle when objects are heavily occluded or densely packed, because learning accurate one-to-many correspondences across the entire scene is inherently difficult. The core gap is that no prior approach decomposes the registration into localized, per-instance pair-wise problems, which would reduce the difficulty of correspondence learning in cluttered environments.

## Input / Output

The method takes a scene point cloud (N×3) and a model/CAD point cloud (M×3) without RGB, voxel-subsampled to 4096 points at 2.5 cm (Scan2CAD) or 0.15 cm (ROBI) resolution. It outputs a rigid transformation (rotation + translation) for each detected instance, derived from pair-wise correspondences extracted via optimal transport and mutual top-k selection within masked local patches, followed by SVD-based pose estimation. Object proposals are generated by ball-querying around predicted centers with a radius of 1.2× the CAD model radius.

## Method

3DFMNet decomposes multi-instance registration into multiple pair-wise registrations via a two-stage pipeline. The 3D multi-object focusing module uses a KPConv-FPN backbone followed by three stacked self-attention and cross-attention layers to learn scene-model feature correlation, then regresses per-point offsets and instance masks via MLPs (with geodesic distance embeddings); DBSCAN clusters the offset points to yield object centers, which seed ball-query proposals. The 3D dual-masking instance matching module, applied independently to each proposal, learns an instance mask (filtering background) and an overlap mask (handling occlusion-induced incompleteness) through a second KPConv encoder with self/cross-attention, then extracts dense correspondences within masked patches using an optimal transport layer and mutual top-k selection, followed by local-to-global registration. Training uses circle loss for feature learning, L1 + direction loss for center regression, and NLL + BCE/dice losses for matching and mask prediction, all under supervised learning with ground-truth centers and poses.

## Results

On the Scan2CAD dataset, 3DFMNet achieves 95.44% MR, 94.15% MP, and 94.79% MF, setting new state-of-the-art on MP and MF. On the more challenging ROBI dataset (industrial bin-picking with reflective metallic objects), 3DFMNet achieves 46.81% MR, 50.61% MP, and 48.63% MF, outperforming the previous best MIRETR by approximately 7–9 percentage points across all three metrics. The upper-bound variant (3DFMNet*, using ground-truth object centers) reaches 97.68% MR / 94.63% MP on Scan2CAD and 52.59% MR / 63.13% MP on ROBI, confirming the pipeline's potential.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against five baselines spanning two-stage (T-Linkage, RansaCov, PointCLM, ECC) and one-stage (MIRETR) multi-instance registration methods. On Scan2CAD, 3DFMNet achieves the best MP (94.15%) and MF (94.79%), surpassing MIRETR (91.21% MP, 93.40% MF) and ECC (89.03% MP, 92.62% MF), though ECC retains a slight MR advantage (96.52% vs. 95.44%). On the significantly harder ROBI dataset, 3DFMNet dominates all baselines on every metric: it improves over MIRETR by +8.3% MR (46.81 vs. 38.51), +9.4% MP (50.61 vs. 41.19), and +8.8% MF (48.63 vs. 39.80), and over ECC by +22.2% MR and +15.8% MP. The two-stage clustering-based methods (T-Linkage, RansaCov) and the contrastive-learning PointCLM are far behind, with ROBI MF scores below 26%, highlighting the difficulty of cluttered industrial scenes. The upper-bound experiment (3DFMNet*) further shows that the focusing-then-matching decomposition itself is the key driver of gains, as even with imperfect localization the method already exceeds all prior work.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Geometric Transformer (Qin et al., CVPR 2022) for its optimal transport-based correspondence extraction, circle loss, and local-to-global registration pipeline, and on MIRETR (Yu et al., CVPR 2024) for the KPConv-FPN backbone and geodesic distance embedding. The key conceptual departure is a pipeline-level reframe: rather than learning one-to-many correspondences between a single CAD model and all scene instances simultaneously (as in MIRETR, PointCLM, ECC), 3DFMNet decomposes the problem into multiple independent one-to-one pair-wise registrations by first regressing object centers via a focusing module and then matching each ball-query proposal separately. This 'focus-then-match' strategy is inspired by object detection paradigms (center regression + proposal generation) applied to the registration setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ~7% absolute improvement on the challenging ROBI dataset (reflective metallic objects in bin-picking) demonstrates that the decomposition strategy effectively handles clutter and occlusion where global correspondence learning struggles. However, the two-stage design introduces a dependency: localization errors in the focusing stage propagate to matching, and the upper-bound analysis reveals that even perfect center detection caps ROBI performance at ~52% MR, suggesting fundamental difficulty with highly reflective, incomplete objects. The decomposition insight (one-to-many → multiple one-to-one) is broadly applicable to other multi-target tasks like tracking and map construction, but the method's slight speed penalty over one-stage MIRETR and its sensitivity to localization accuracy remain open practical concerns.
