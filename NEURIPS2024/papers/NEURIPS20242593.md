## Problem

GFlowNets sample objects in proportion to their reward, but consistently biasing them toward high-reward samples requires tuning a temperature parameter β that is numerically unstable, task-sensitive, and reduces diversity through mode collapse. The paper asks whether greediness can be made an inference-time control knob rather than a training-time hyperparameter, without learning a full family of temperature-conditional models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242593/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a positive reward function R(x) defined over a combinatorial object space (molecular graphs, nucleotide sequences, or bit strings) and a scalar greediness parameter p ∈ [0,1]. It produces a set of diverse, high-reward objects constructed sequentially along a DAG. The key operational detail is that p can be changed at inference time on an already-trained model to sweep a reward–diversity trade-off curve, and the same trained Q and P_F can be recombined under different QGFN variants (p-greedy, p-quantile, p-of-max) without any retraining.

## Method

QGFN jointly trains two off-policy models on the same minibatch: a GFlowNet (forward policy P_F and flow F, trained with Trajectory Balance or Sub-Trajectory Balance) and a deep Q-network (trained with n-step temporal-difference returns, where n close to the maximum trajectory length is critical). At each state, a behavior policy μ is formed by combining P_F and Q in one of three ways: p-greedy mixes P_F with the Q-argmax action; p-quantile masks all actions whose Q-value falls below the p-quantile; p-of-max masks actions whose Q-value is below p times the maximum Q. The core insight is that F captures how many high-reward objects exist in a branch (diversity) while Q estimates the expected reward of a specific action (greediness); combining them lets the agent explore broadly yet focus on high-reward sub-branches. Because both components are off-policy, the trained Q remains a useful lower bound on expected return even when p is changed at inference, enabling a smooth greediness dial without retraining.

## Results

On the fragment-based molecule task (the primary benchmark), QGFN variants achieve average rewards of 0.950–0.969, substantially outperforming GFN-TB (0.780), GFN-SubTB (0.716), and LSL-GFN (0.717), while maintaining pairwise Tanimoto similarity at or below 0.551 (vs. 0.689 for LSL-GFN, which suffers diversity collapse). P-of-max QGFN achieves the best reward (0.969 ± 0.003) with the lowest similarity (0.514 ± 0.001). Across all five main tasks (fragment molecules, QM9, two RNA-binding tasks, and bit sequences), QGFN variants consistently produce higher average rewards and discover more high-reward modes than all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

QGFN variants decisively outperform all GFN baselines on the fragment task: p-of-max QGFN achieves 0.969 reward vs. 0.780 for GFN-TB (+24%) and 0.716 for GFN-SubTB (+35%), while keeping similarity at 0.514 (comparable to SubTB's 0.513). LSL-GFN, the temperature-conditional baseline, shows the worst diversity (similarity 0.689) at a similar reward level to SubTB, confirming that increasing β causes mode collapse. Among RL baselines, DQN systematically underperforms across all tasks, and A2C and MunDQN (Tiapkin et al., 2023) are also outperformed by QGFN in both reward and mode discovery. On the Maximum Independent Set task (Table 12), FL-QGFN(p-greedy) achieves a top-20 metric of 19.06 vs. 18.72 for the FL baseline, a modest but consistent gain. The paper also demonstrates that a trained Q function can be applied at inference to independently trained GFN baselines (Table 2), boosting their reward from ~0.72–0.78 to ~0.92–0.95 without retraining the GFN itself.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of GFlowNets and reinforcement learning, building directly on the GFN flow-balance framework (Bengio et al., 2021a,b) and the Trajectory Balance objective (Malkin et al., 2022a). It departs from Tiapkin et al. (2023), who reformulated GFNs as Soft-RL but still recover the same p(x) ∝ R(x) target, by instead using 'Hard-RL' Q-learning to construct greedier behavior policies. It also contrasts with LSL-GFN (Kim et al., 2023a), which achieves temperature-conditioned greediness by training a family of GFNs, whereas QGFN achieves inference-time controllability with a single trained Q-function and P_F pair. The core novelty is the specific combination of an off-policy GFN forward policy with an off-policy Q-network into a unified behavior policy modulated by a single parameter p.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

QGFN provides a practical, retraining-free knob for controlling the reward-diversity tradeoff in GFN sampling, which is a persistent pain point in the GFN literature (temperature tuning is fragile, and LSL-GFN requires training a model family). The finding that n-step returns near the maximum trajectory length outperform 1-step Q-learning—effectively making Q an on-policy estimate rather than an optimal one—is a useful conceptual insight for the broader RL+generative-models community. Limitations include the doubled training cost (two models), sensitivity to Q-learning quality, and the absence of scaling studies. Open questions include richer combinations of Q and P_F (e.g., MCTS-style search), using Q for constraint satisfaction rather than reward, and whether the approach extends to continuous or stochastic GFN settings.
