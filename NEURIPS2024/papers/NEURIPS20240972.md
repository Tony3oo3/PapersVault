## Problem

Learning an ε-optimal policy in MDPs with low-rank value matrices has been shown to require only Õ((S+A)/ε²) samples, but existing algorithms either require a priori knowledge of anchor states and actions, assume incoherence of the value matrix, or parameterize their analysis with unknown quantities like the smallest singular value of a submatrix. No prior method provides entry-wise estimation guarantees for coherent low-rank matrices without such restrictive assumptions or unknown parameters.

## Input / Output

The algorithm is fully model-free: it interacts with the MDP by sampling trajectories of truncated length τ+1 under the current policy, starting from uniformly random (state, action) pairs, and never accesses the transition kernel or reward function directly. It requires only a total sampling budget T, the discount factor γ, a reward upper bound r_max, and an initial deterministic policy—no knowledge of the matrix rank, condition number, spikiness, or coherence. The output is a deterministic policy π̂ satisfying ‖V⋆ − V^π̂‖∞ ≤ ε with high probability, along with the estimated value matrix Q̂^π at each iteration.

## Method

LoRa-PI alternates between policy improvement (greedy w.r.t. the estimated Q-matrix) and policy evaluation via LME, a two-phase leveraged matrix estimation procedure. In Phase 1, half the sampling budget is used to construct an empirical truncated value matrix from Monte-Carlo rollouts, whose SVD yields estimated singular subspaces and hence leverage scores for each row and column. In Phase 2, the remaining budget samples anchor rows and columns without replacement according to the estimated leverage scores, then completes the full matrix via a CUR decomposition weighted by inverse leverage scores (importance sampling correction). The key technical contribution is an instance-dependent row-wise singular subspace recovery guarantee (Theorem 4) that bounds the error for row i in terms of ‖U_{i,:}‖₂ rather than the global incoherence, enabling entry-wise guarantees that depend only on the matrix spikiness α and condition number κ, not on coherence μ. The algorithm is parameter-free: the rank is estimated by thresholding singular values, and no upper bounds on unknown quantities are required.

## Results

The main theoretical result is that LME achieves entry-wise estimation error ≤ ε with sample complexity Õ(κ⁴α²((S+A)+α²)/((1-γ)³ε²)), where α is spikiness and κ is the condition number — critically, this bound does NOT depend on the matrix coherence μ. LoRa-PI inherits this to learn an ε-optimal policy using Õ(κ⁴α²((S+A)+α²)/((1-γ)⁸ε²)) samples, which is order-optimal in S, A, and ε. The (1-γ)⁸ dependence (vs. the ideal (1-γ)³) is acknowledged as an artifact of the model-free Monte-Carlo estimator. Numerically, leverage-score-based anchors closely match oracle anchors in entry-wise error, while uniform anchors show significantly higher and more variable error.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Shah et al. [35], LME matches the same α²(S+A)/ε² sample complexity but removes the requirement for a priori known anchor states and the restrictive assumptions of deterministic rewards and small discount factor γ. Against Sam et al. [34] (LR-EVI) and Chen et al. [9] (NNM), LME improves the sample complexity by a factor of μ² (the squared coherence), since its guarantees depend only on spikiness α rather than coherence μ — this is a substantial gain for coherent matrices where μ can be as large as √(S/d). Against Chen et al. [7] (Two-phase MC), LME extends the adaptive sampling paradigm from the noiseless exact-recovery setting to the noisy RL setting with entry-wise (not just spectral/Frobenius) guarantees. In the numerical experiments (Appendix A), LoRa-PI with leveraged anchors consistently outperforms both uniform-anchor and full-matrix baselines in entry-wise error, with the gap being most pronounced for matrices with high leverage score concentration.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the low-rank MDP line initiated by Shah et al. (2020) and Sam et al. (2023), which both achieved (S+A)/ε² sample complexity but required either prior knowledge of anchor states/actions or incoherence of the value matrix. The key methodological departure is the introduction of LME, a two-phase leveraged matrix estimation procedure that combines spectral subspace recovery (for leverage score estimation) with CUR-based completion, drawing on adaptive sampling ideas from Chen et al. (2015) and Krishnamurthy & Singh (2013). Unlike prior entry-wise matrix estimation results (Chen et al. 2020, Stojanovic et al. 2024) that scale with coherence μ², LME's guarantees depend only on spikiness α, making it the first adaptive method with non-vacuous entry-wise guarantees for coherent matrices.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Removing the incoherence assumption significantly broadens the class of low-rank MDPs for which sample-efficient RL is provably possible, and the parameter-free nature of LoRa-PI (no need to know rank, condition number, or spikiness) makes it practically deployable. However, the (1−γ)⁻⁸ dependence on the discount factor—far from the ideal (1−γ)⁻³—remains a significant limitation of the model-free Monte-Carlo approach, and the paper acknowledges that a model-based variant would be needed to close this gap. An open question the authors highlight is whether the condition number κ⁴ dependence in the sample complexity can be eliminated entirely.
