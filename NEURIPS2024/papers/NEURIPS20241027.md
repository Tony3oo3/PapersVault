## Problem

Distributional regression via CRPS minimization is widely used for probabilistic forecasting, yet a sound non-asymptotic theory for model fitting, model selection, and convex aggregation was missing. While minimax rates had been recently studied, practical guarantees (concentration bounds, oracle inequalities) for the standard ERM, validation-based selection, and aggregation procedures were not available, leaving common statistical practices without rigorous justification.

## Input / Output

The framework consumes a training sample of n i.i.d. pairs (X_i, Y_i) with X in R^d and Y in R, plus an independent validation sample of N pairs for model selection or convex aggregation. The output is a conditional c.d.f. F̂_n,x(y) (a full predictive distribution, not a point estimate), a selected model index m̂ from M candidates, or convex aggregation weights λ̂ on the M-simplex. For parametric models (EMOS, DRN), the parameter space must be compact and the model Lipschitz in W1; for non-parametric models (KNN, DRF), only model selection and aggregation are considered since ERM is not applicable.

## Method

The core technical contribution is a chain of concentration inequalities built on three pillars: (i) the CRPS is 2-Lipschitz in its first argument with respect to the W1 Wasserstein distance, (ii) sub-Gaussianity of Y and the absolute moment m_1(F_{θ,X}) implies sub-Gaussianity of the CRPS loss, and (iii) compactness plus Lipschitz continuity of the parametric family enables epsilon-net covering arguments. For model fitting, a union bound over an epsilon-net of the parameter space yields an oracle inequality of order √(c_β log(2n^K/δ)/n). For model selection, a union bound over M candidates gives a regret bound of order √(c_n log(2M/δ)/N). For convex aggregation, the model fitting result is applied to the simplex Λ_M with the Lipschitz constant inherited from the W1 geometry of convex combinations of cdfs. All results are extended beyond sub-Gaussianity to finite p-th moment assumptions via Rosenthal-type moment bounds, yielding a rate of n^{-p/(2(p+K))}.

## Results

On the QSAR Aquatic Toxicity dataset, convex aggregation (CA) achieves the lowest test CRPS of 0.612 (SE 0.003), outperforming DRF (0.624), Model Selection (0.632), and KNN (0.643). On the Airfoil Self-Noise dataset, CA achieves a test CRPS of 1.344 (SE 0.005), improving over DRF (1.366) and substantially over KNN (1.864). The theoretical contribution provides oracle concentration inequalities: the estimation error in model fitting is bounded by √(c_β log(2n^K/δ)/n) with probability 1−δ, and the regret in model selection is bounded by 4√(c_n log(2M/δ)/N) conditionally on the training set.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares two base distributional regression models—KNN (the Analog method of Toth, 1989) and Distributional Random Forests (Cevid et al., 2022)—against two aggregation strategies: model selection (MS) and convex aggregation (CA). On the Airfoil dataset, DRF substantially outperforms KNN (1.366 vs. 1.864 CRPS), and MS matches DRF exactly (DRF is systematically selected), while CA provides a further small improvement to 1.344. On the QSAR dataset, the gap between KNN and DRF is smaller (0.643 vs. 0.624), MS slightly underperforms DRF (0.632 vs. 0.624), and CA achieves the best result at 0.612. In both cases, convex aggregation dominates all individual models and the model selection approach, confirming the theoretical guarantee that the regret of CA vanishes as the validation set size N grows.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the CRPS framework established by Gneiting and Raftery (2007), whose key identity linking CRPS excess risk to the L2 distance between cdfs is the foundation of all theoretical results. The technical core relies on the 1D Wasserstein distance identity from Bobkov and Ledoux (2019) to show CRPS is 2-Lipschitz in its first argument, enabling standard concentration and covering-number arguments. While Pic et al. (2023) recently established minimax rates for distributional regression, this paper is the first to provide non-asymptotic oracle inequalities for model fitting, model selection, and convex aggregation via CRPS minimization, extending the aggregation theory of Tsybakov (2003) and Bunea et al. (2007) from point regression to the distributional setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper fills a recognized gap: despite widespread practical use of CRPS-based distributional regression in meteorology, oncology, and energy forecasting, no prior work provided statistical learning guarantees for the standard workflow of fitting, selecting, and aggregating models. The results give practitioners a rigorous justification for cross-validation and convex aggregation in this setting, with rates that are parametric (up to log factors) under sub-Gaussianity and degrade gracefully under weaker moment conditions. Open questions include whether the rates are minimax-optimal, whether empirical process theory can yield sharper bounds, and how the results extend to dependent (non-i.i.d.) data common in time-series forecasting.
