## Problem

Implicit bias theory for gradient descent on separable data is well-developed for binary classification under the exponential tail property, but the multiclass case has only been analyzed for the cross-entropy loss. This paper identifies and fills the gap by extending the exponential tail property to a broad class of multiclass losses, proving that the property is sufficient for gradient descent to directionally converge to the hard-margin multiclass SVM.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242584/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a linearly separable multiclass dataset of N samples in R^d with K class labels, and a PERM loss function whose template ψ: R^{K-1} → R is convex, β-smooth, strictly decreasing, nonnegative, and satisfies the multiclass exponential tail property. The output is the gradient descent trajectory of the weight matrix W ∈ R^{d×K} (one column per class), which asymptotically aligns with the hard-margin multiclass SVM solution ŵ. A sufficiently small fixed learning rate η < 2β^{-1}σ_max^{-2}(X) is required, and the result holds for almost all linearly separable datasets.

## Method

The paper introduces a multiclass exponential tail property (Definition 2.2) that bounds each negative partial derivative of the PERM template ψ by exponential functions of the relative margins, generalizing the binary case where only a single negative derivative is bounded. Using the PERM loss framework's relative margin form L_y(v) = ψ(Υ_y Dv), the proof of directional convergence to the hard-margin SVM closely mirrors the binary argument of Soudry et al. [2018]: the key step is showing that the inner product of the SVM solution with the risk gradient is strictly negative, which follows because the gradient of ψ is entrywise strictly negative and the separability condition Υ_{y_i} DŴ^T x_i ≽ 1 guarantees all relative margins are positive. The second term in the residual expansion is bounded using the upper and lower exponential tail bounds applied to the relative margins, which are shown to grow unboundedly over time. The paper verifies the exponential tail property for cross-entropy, multiclass exponential loss, and PairLogLoss, and provides experimental validation with PairLogLoss.

## Results

The main result (Theorem 3.4) proves that for any PERM loss satisfying the exponential tail (ET) property, gradient descent on almost all linearly separable multiclass datasets directionally converges to the hard-margin multiclass SVM: w(t) = ŵ·log(t) + ρ(t) with bounded ‖ρ(t)‖. The paper verifies the ET property holds for three common losses: cross-entropy (with u±=0, c=1), multiclass exponential loss (u±=0, c=1), and PairLogLoss (u±=0, c=1). Experimental validation in Appendix I confirms implicit bias toward the hard-margin SVM using PairLogLoss on synthetic datasets, and Assumption 4.1 (existence of the auxiliary solution) is verified empirically on 100 instances for each combination of d∈{2,3,4,5,6} and K∈{3,4,5,6}.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical paper with no empirical baseline comparisons; the 'compared' results are prior theoretical guarantees. The paper extends the binary implicit bias result of Soudry et al. [2018] (which required the exponential tail property for binary margin losses) to the multiclass setting, where prior results were restricted to cross-entropy only. The key advance is showing that the multiclass ET property (Definition 2.2) is sufficient for implicit bias for a broader class of losses (cross-entropy, multiclass exponential loss, PairLogLoss), not just cross-entropy. The proof techniques closely mirror the binary case, demonstrating that the PERM framework [Wang and Scott, 2024] provides a clean bridge between binary and multiclass analysis. The paper does not compare convergence rates or generalization bounds against other multiclass methods (e.g., Lyu and Li [2019] for homogeneous predictors), leaving those as future work.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the implicit bias theory of Soudry et al. [2018] from binary to multiclass classification by introducing a multiclass analogue of the exponential tail property, built on the PERM loss framework of Wang and Scott [2024]. Prior multiclass results (Soudry et al. 2018, Lyu and Li 2019) were restricted to cross-entropy loss; here the authors show that any convex, β-smooth, strictly decreasing PERM loss satisfying the new multiclass exponential tail condition yields directional convergence to the hard-margin multiclass SVM. The proof techniques deliberately mirror the binary case step-by-step, demonstrating that the PERM relative-margin form is the key structural bridge that makes the extension tractable.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This fills a notable gap in the implicit bias literature by providing a general loss-class condition (beyond cross-entropy) under which multiclass gradient descent converges to the max-margin solution, and by showing the PERM framework can systematically translate binary results to multiclass. Open questions remain: the analysis is restricted to linear classifiers on linearly separable data, Assumption 4.1 (existence of a specific auxiliary vector) is stated but not fully proven for almost all datasets, and the paper does not address non-separable data, neural networks, or non-vanilla optimizers in the multiclass setting. The practical impact is primarily theoretical, providing a template for future extensions of binary implicit bias results to multiclass.
