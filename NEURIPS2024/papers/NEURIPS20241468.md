## Problem

Training reinforcement learning agents for novel behaviors specified in natural language requires carefully hand-crafted reward functions, which becomes intractable as the number of desired behaviors grows. Existing text-conditioned approaches either demand in-domain video demonstrations, environment-specific generative models, or rely on CLIP-style contrastive rewards that cannot capture motion dynamics. The paper asks whether a frozen, domain-agnostic pretrained diffusion model can serve as a zero-shot dense reward signal for text-aligned policy learning without any task-specific training data.

## Input / Output

The method takes a natural language text prompt (e.g., 'a person walking'), a rendered observation from the environment (a single RGB frame for TADPoLe, or a sliding window of n consecutive frames for Video-TADPoLe), and the environment state. The rendered frame is corrupted with a sampled Gaussian noise vector at a fixed noise level (t_noise ~ U(400,500) for images, U(500,600) for video) before being fed to the frozen diffusion model. The output is a scalar dense reward at each timestep, composed of a symlog-normalized alignment term and reconstruction term, which drives policy optimization via TD-MPC to produce an action sequence.

## Method

TADPoLe uses a pretrained text-to-image diffusion model (StableDiffusion 2.1) in a discriminative manner: at each timestep, the rendered frame is noised with a sampled Gaussian vector, and the reward is the MSE between the text-conditioned and unconditional noise predictions (alignment term) plus a comparative reconstruction term measuring how much closer the conditional prediction is to the true noise than the unconditional one. The key insight is that for well-aligned observations, the conditional model performs 'extra work' to match the text-described mode, producing a larger gap from the unconditional prediction, while the reconstruction term encodes naturalness priors from internet-scale pretraining. Video-TADPoLe generalizes this to sliding windows of frames using a text-to-video model (AnimateDiff), enabling supervision of motion concepts like speed and direction. The symlog normalization is critical, as it normalizes raw reward scales across different diffusion models and environments, enabling fixed hyperparameters (w1=2000, w2=200) to transfer without retuning.

## Results

On the Humanoid standing task (ground-truth reward), TADPoLe achieves 254.43 ± 8.76, competitive with the ground-truth-trained agent (287.68 ± 4.64) and slightly outperforming VLM-RM (247.05 ± 16.90). For novel zero-shot behaviors (lotus position, splits, kneeling, dog standing), TADPoLe is the only method that consistently achieves text-aligned policies according to human evaluation. On continuous locomotion, Video-TADPoLe reaches 145.60 ± 48.20 on Humanoid walking versus 25.51 ± 40.45 for ViCLIP-RM and 275.06 ± 9.21 for ground-truth. On Meta-World robotic manipulation, TADPoLe achieves a 50.6% average success rate across 10 tasks, more than doubling VLM-RM's 25.0%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TADPoLe outperforms all three baselines across every evaluated task. Against VLM-RM (CLIP-based reward), TADPoLe achieves a higher ground-truth reward on Humanoid standing (254.43 vs. 247.05) and is the only method to succeed on kneeling and dog standing; on Meta-World, TADPoLe's 50.6% average success rate more than doubles VLM-RM's 25.0%. Against LIV (CLIP finetuned on robot videos), TADPoLe wins on all novel behaviors where LIV fails (lotus, kneeling, dog standing) and achieves substantially higher locomotion rewards (145.60 vs. 0.65 on Humanoid walking). Against Text2Reward (LLM-generated reward functions with sensor access), TADPoLe still wins on all text-alignment evaluations and achieves higher locomotion scores (145.60 vs. 3.35 on Humanoid walking), despite Text2Reward having access to ground-truth state information like speed and direction that TADPoLe does not. For continuous locomotion specifically, Video-TADPoLe (145.60) dramatically outperforms the extended ViCLIP-RM baseline (25.51) on Humanoid walking, and on Dog walking achieves 60.20 vs. ViCLIP-RM's 14.67, which collapses to a static pose.

## Relation to Prior Work

TADPoLe directly extends DreamFusion's core mechanism—using a frozen text-to-image diffusion model's noise-prediction difference as a discriminative score—from the spatial (3D geometry) domain to the temporal (policy) domain. It departs from prior text-conditioned policy learning methods (LangLfP, UniPi, VLP, VIPER, Diffusion Reward) that all require environment-specific or task-specific video models trained on in-domain demonstrations, by instead leveraging fully frozen, domain-agnostic generative models (StableDiffusion, AnimateDiff) as reward providers. The conceptual framing of a policy as an 'agent-centric implicit video representation' whose rendered frames can be critiqued by a text-conditioned diffusion model is the key positioning that distinguishes it from CLIP-based reward approaches (VLM-RM, LIV) that rely on contrastive alignment rather than generative likelihood.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TADPoLe demonstrates that hand-crafted reward functions can be replaced by dense, text-conditioned signals from off-the-shelf generative models, opening a path toward specifying arbitrary robot behaviors in natural language without per-task reward engineering. However, significant limitations remain: the approach is restricted to visually rendered environments, suffers from high variance due to stochastic noise resampling, lacks fine-grained control over individual prompt words, and the ground-truth reward comparisons are acknowledged as structurally unfavorable for vision-only methods. Open questions include whether multi-view rendering, noise-level scheduling, or learned guidance weights could address the variance and controllability issues, and whether the approach generalizes beyond the relatively simple DM Control and Meta-World settings to more complex, high-dimensional manipulation or navigation tasks.
