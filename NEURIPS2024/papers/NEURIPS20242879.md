## Problem

LLM fine-tuning is dominated by maximum likelihood estimation (MLE) for next-token prediction, which is equivalent to behavior cloning and suffers from compounding errors, exposure bias, and reduced generation diversity. While the broader inverse reinforcement learning (IRL) perspective on imitation has been explored for RLHF, its potential as a scalable, principled alternative to MLE in the supervised fine-tuning stage—particularly in offline form without costly online rollouts—remains underexplored. The paper asks when and how IRL-based imitation can replace or augment MLE for fine-tuning, and what the extracted reward functions reveal about task quality.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pre-trained LLM checkpoint (T5 base/large/xl or PaLM2 Gecko), a fixed SFT dataset of expert demonstrations, and a text prompt at inference time. The primary output is a fine-tuned autoregressive policy that generates text sequences, but a secondary output is an implicit reward function encoded in the learned Q-function (r_t = Q(s_t, a_t) − γV(s_{t+1})), which can be evaluated on arbitrary rollouts to score trajectory quality. A key practical distinction is that the offline IQLearn variant requires no online sampling during training, operating solely on the fixed expert dataset, while the online variant mixes in a configurable fraction (α) of model-generated rollouts to relax the distribution-matching objective.

## Method

The core contribution is a reformulation of inverse soft-Q-learning (IQLearn) that explicitly decomposes the IRL objective into a standard MLE term plus a temporal-difference regularization term, establishing a principled continuum between behavior cloning and full IRL. By selecting the χ²-divergence and rescaling the state value function (v_λ = v/λ), the objective becomes E_{μ_E}[λ(v_λ + log π^r − E_{s'}[γv_λ'])² − log π^r], where setting λ=0 recovers pure MLE and increasing λ strengthens the TD coupling between the policy and a learned value function. This reformulation enables computationally cheap offline training on fixed datasets without the autoregressive sampling bottleneck of online IRL, while retaining the ability to anneal regularization strength. The paper further compares this non-adversarial approach against GAIL (adversarial IRL with a discriminator) and entropy-regularized MLE across T5 and PaLM2 models, evaluating task performance (ROUGE, accuracy, BLEU) against generation diversity (Self-BLEU, per-token entropy) to map Pareto fronts.

## Results

On WMT22 (English-to-German translation, 285M examples), offline IQLearn with PaLM2 achieves up to +2.16 BLEU over MLE (35.68 vs. 32.34 test-BLEU at λ=0.7), with consistent gains across all regularization strengths tested. On GSM8k (math reasoning), IRL-based fine-tuning yields clear accuracy improvements over MLE, particularly for larger T5 models. Across all four benchmarks (XSUM, GSM8k, TLDR, WMT22), IQLearn consistently improves diversity of generations (lower Self-BLEU) while matching or exceeding MLE task performance, with the strongest gains observed on longer-target and smaller-dataset settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against MLE/Behavioral Cloning [8], IQLearn consistently wins on both task performance and diversity: on WMT22 it gains up to +2.16 BLEU (35.68 vs. 32.34), on GSM8k it achieves higher accuracy especially at low sampling temperatures, and on XSUM it demonstrates greater robustness to overfitting on small data subsets where MLE degrades significantly. The diversity advantage is consistent across all benchmarks, with IQLearn producing lower Self-BLEU scores (more diverse generations) at equal or better task performance. Against GAIL [25], IQLearn is more stable to train (no adversarial instability, no requirement for MLE warmup checkpoints) and computationally cheaper (offline, no online sampling needed), while achieving comparable or superior task performance; GAIL requires heuristic stabilization measures and additional MLE loss terms to avoid degenerate behavior such as maximum-length generation. The paper notes that GAIL is particularly difficult to stabilize for PaLM2 decoder-only models, whereas IQLearn transfers cleanly across both T5 encoder-decoder and PaLM2 architectures.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the inverse soft-Q learning framework (IQLearn [20]) to language model fine-tuning, building on the variational distribution-matching formulation of IRL and the value-policy identities from Nachum et al. [42]. Its key conceptual contribution is a reformulation showing that IQLearn is equivalent to MLE with a temporal-difference regularization term, providing a principled interpolation (via λ) between standard supervised fine-tuning and full IRL. Unlike prior IRL-for-language work such as SequenceMatch [15], which required online data and a modified action space (backtracking tokens), this paper demonstrates that the computationally cheaper offline variant already captures most of the benefit, and it situates GAIL [25] and adversarial imitation within the same f-divergence framework for unified comparison.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a practical, drop-in alternative to MLE for SFT that improves generation diversity while matching or slightly exceeding task performance, with the added benefit of extracting reward functions from demonstration data that correlate with task metrics (Spearman ρ up to 0.65 vs. ~0 for MLE) — a potential bridge to more robust RLHF/RLAIF reward modeling. Key open questions include whether the diversity gains translate into measurable improvements in downstream preference-based alignment, whether the approach scales to pretraining-scale data, and whether extending the action space to allow token revision (as in SequenceMatch) would unlock the larger online-IRL benefits seen in classical control. The finding that online IRL provides only marginal gains over offline IRL for concatenation-based autoregressive generation is a useful negative result that tempers expectations for active data collection in this setting.
