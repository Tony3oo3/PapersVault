## Problem

In real-world federated learning, clients are intermittently available with heterogeneous and non-stationary probabilities, yet most prior work either assumes stationary availability, requires prior knowledge of availability dynamics, or demands memory proportional to the number of clients times model dimension. The paper demonstrates through concrete examples that both heterogeneity and non-stationarity in client availability introduce significant bias into FedAvg, the most widely adopted federated learning algorithm, leading to substantial accuracy degradation.

## Input / Output

Each client holds a local subset of RGB images (SVHN, CIFAR-10, CINIC-10) split non-IID via Dirichlet(0.1) class distributions, and participates stochastically each round with a client-specific, time-varying probability p_i^t that is unknown to both clients and the parameter server. The algorithm requires only O(1) additional per-client memory (tracking the last active round τ_i(t)) and O(1) additional computation beyond standard FedAvg, with no direct client-to-client communication. The output is a trained global CNN model for 10-class image classification, evaluated over 2000 global rounds with 100 clients.

## Method

FedAWE introduces two novel algorithmic structures on top of standard FedAvg. First, adaptive innovation echoing multiplies each active client's local gradient innovation G_i^t by (t − τ_i(t)), the number of rounds since the client last participated, so that clients who missed rounds effectively 'catch up' on their missed computation without actually performing extra local SGD steps. Second, implicit gossiping is achieved by having the parameter server aggregate updated local models and multicast the new global model only to currently active clients, rather than broadcasting the global model at the start of each round; this creates a doubly stochastic mixing matrix that evenly diffuses local updates across the system without any direct client-to-client communication. The convergence analysis constructs an auxiliary sequence z_i^t that mimics ideal per-round descent regardless of availability, then bounds the approximation error between x_i^t and z_i^t and the consensus error from implicit gossiping, proving convergence to a stationary point of non-convex objectives with linear speedup O(1/√(skT)) in the number of local steps s and active clients k.

## Results

FedAWE achieves 86.1% test accuracy on SVHN, 66.3% on CIFAR-10, and 47.3% on CINIC-10 under stationary client availability, consistently outperforming all non-memory-aided baselines by 2–5 percentage points. Under the most challenging non-stationary (interleaved sine) dynamics, FedAWE retains 84.6% / 63.3% / 46.6% test accuracy on the three datasets, while FedAvg over active degrades to 80.7% / 60.7% / 41.5%. The algorithm also matches or occasionally exceeds memory-heavy methods (MIFA, FedVARP) and approaches the performance of FedAvg with known availability probabilities, all without requiring prior knowledge of the unavailability dynamics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedAWE consistently outperforms FedAvg over active clients [32] by 3–5 percentage points in test accuracy across all four dynamics and three datasets (e.g., 86.1% vs. 82.4% on SVHN-stationary; 46.6% vs. 41.5% on CINIC-10-interleaved sine). It also beats FedAU [55] and F3AST [44] by a similar margin under every setting, confirming that adaptive innovation echoing and implicit gossiping provide meaningful bias correction beyond simple amplification or Markov-chain-based scheduling. Against FedAvg with known p_i^t [42], FedAWE is competitive but slightly trails under sine and interleaved-sine dynamics (e.g., 85.6% vs. 86.0% on SVHN-sine), though it matches or exceeds it under stationary and staircase dynamics without requiring any prior knowledge of the availability probabilities. Notably, FedAWE occasionally surpasses the memory-heavy baselines MIFA [14] and FedVARP [20] (e.g., 47.3% vs. 46.9% on CINIC-10-stationary; 46.6% vs. 45.9% on CINIC-10-interleaved sine), despite using only O(1) additional memory per client rather than storing full gradient histories for unavailable clients.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the federated learning literature on client unavailability by addressing the joint challenge of heterogeneous and non-stationary availability dynamics, which prior work either assumes to be stationary (FedAU, FedVARP), known a priori (Perazzone et al.), or restricted to Markov-chain structure (Ribero et al.). The algorithmic design builds on two threads: the implicit gossiping mechanism from the authors' earlier CDC 2023 work, and the adaptive innovation echoing that compensates for missed local computations. Departing from memory-aided approaches (MIFA, FedVARP) that store O(m·d) old gradients, FedAWE achieves comparable or better performance with only O(1) additional memory and computation per client, while remaining agnostic to the underlying unavailability dynamics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The O(1) overhead is critical for resource-constrained edge devices in real-world cross-device federated learning, where storing per-client gradient history is infeasible. The proven linear speedup under non-stationary dynamics provides theoretical grounding for scaling to massive client populations with unpredictable participation. Key open questions remain: the analysis requires independent and strictly positive availability probabilities, yet real systems exhibit correlated unavailability and intermittent zero-availability periods (as the interleaved sine experiments partially probe). Future work on variance reduction and correlated/zero-valued dynamics would strengthen the practical applicability.
