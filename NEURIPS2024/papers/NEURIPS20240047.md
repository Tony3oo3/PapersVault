## Problem

Existing permissively-licensed preference datasets (Open Assistant, HH-RLHF, HelpSteer) have become insufficient for training reward models that can distinguish between good and excellent responses from increasingly capable LLMs. Meanwhile, datasets distilled from proprietary models like GPT-4 (Ultrafeedback, Nectar) carry usage restrictions that prohibit commercial deployment. The paper addresses this gap by releasing HelpSteer2, a small (10k pairs), CC-BY-4.0-licensed preference dataset with high-quality multi-attribute annotations that achieves SOTA reward model performance.

## Input / Output

The reward model takes a text prompt paired with a text response and outputs five scalar attribute scores (helpfulness, correctness, coherence, complexity, verbosity) on a 0–4 Likert scale, implemented as a linear projection on the final token representation of the base transformer. The SteerLM 2.0 aligned model additionally accepts target attribute values as conditioning signals and generates a text response calibrated to those targets. The dataset itself consists of 10,681 prompts (29% multi-turn) each with exactly two responses from different model sources, annotated by an average of 3.41 annotators per response.

## Method

HelpSteer2 is constructed by sourcing prompts from ShareGPT (clustered into ~1000 topics via BERTopic for diversity), generating two responses per prompt from a diverse pool of models (Nemotron-2/3/4, Mixtral-8x7B, human annotators), and annotating five attributes with 3+ annotators per response (reaching Cohen's κ of 0.791 for helpfulness after quality filtering). The reward model is a base transformer (Llama 3 70B or Nemotron-4 340B) with a linear head trained via MSE loss to predict the five attribute scores, achieving 92.0% on RewardBench with the 340B variant. SteerLM 2.0 extends the original SteerLM by iteratively minimizing KL divergence between the current policy and an optimal conditional distribution P(y|a,x) derived via Bayes' rule from the reward model, using a Beta distribution to convert regression outputs into probabilistic attribute predictions and importance-weighted gradient estimation for stable training.

## Results

The Nemotron-4-340B Reward Model trained on HelpSteer2 (only 10k response pairs) achieves 92.0% overall accuracy on Reward Bench's primary dataset, setting the state-of-the-art as of June 12, 2024, surpassing all proprietary and open-source models. The Llama 3 70B Reward Model trained on the same dataset achieves 88.8%, outperforming all other models trained with permissively-licensed data by over 9.7%. For downstream alignment, the SteerLM 2.0 model (Iter. 2) reaches 8.28 on MT Bench, matching or exceeding Llama 3 70B Instruct (8.16) and GPT-4-0613 (8.12), while DPO with HelpSteer2 achieves 0.6321 on TruthfulQA MC2 and 42.5 on Arena Hard, both surpassing Llama 3 70B Instruct.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On Reward Bench, the Nemotron-4-340B Reward Model (92.0%) outperforms all proprietary models including Cohere May 2024 (89.5%), Gemini 1.5 Pro (88.1%), GPT-4-0125 (85.9%), and Claude-3-Opus (80.7%), as well as GPT-4-data-trained models like ArmoRM (90.8%) and RLHFlow (87.1%). The Llama 3 70B RM trained on HelpSteer2 (88.8%) beats the same base model trained on Open Assistant (79.1%), HH-RLHF (73.9%), or HelpSteer (66.1%) by 9.7–22.7 percentage points, demonstrating the quality advantage of HelpSteer2 over existing permissive datasets. In the Chat-Hard category specifically, HelpSteer2-trained models lead by 6.5% over the second-best, reflecting the dataset's focus on distinguishing good from excellent responses. For aligned models, SteerLM 2.0 (8.28 MT Bench) and DPO (0.6321 TruthfulQA, 42.5 Arena Hard) match or exceed Llama 3 70B Instruct (8.16, 0.6181, 41.1) and GPT-4-0613 (8.12, 0.5900, 37.9) using only 1% of the training data. PPO slightly underperforms Llama 3 70B Instruct on AlpacaEval 2.0 LC (33.17 vs. 34.40) and shows a notable drop in TruthfulQA (0.5629), likely due to low MCQ representation in HelpSteer2 prompts.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends HelpSteer [12] and SteerLM [18], both from the same NVIDIA team, by upgrading the annotation pipeline (multiple annotators per sample, higher inter-annotator agreement) and the alignment method (SteerLM 2.0 with iterative KL-divergence-based policy refinement). It positions itself against two competing paradigms: older permissive datasets (Open Assistant, HH-RLHF, HelpSteer) that have become less discriminative for well-aligned models, and GPT-4-generated preference datasets (Ultrafeedback, Nectar, Distilabel-Capybara) that carry commercial-use restrictions. The key conceptual departure is demonstrating that a small, high-quality dataset (10k pairs) with multi-attribute regression labels can outperform much larger datasets using the same base model, shifting the emphasis from data volume to annotation quality and model capability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The CC-BY-4.0 license and demonstrated data efficiency (10k pairs achieving 92.0% on RewardBench, SOTA as of June 2024) make this a practically important resource for organizations that cannot use GPT-4-generated data for commercial model training. SteerLM 2.0 introduces a novel alignment paradigm that leverages multi-attribute reward signals rather than scalar preferences, potentially enabling more controllable and interpretable model alignment. Limitations include English-only content, US-based annotators (potential cultural bias), and the fact that the top-performing 340B reward model relies on a proprietary base model, limiting full reproducibility. Open questions include whether the data-efficiency finding generalizes to other languages, smaller model scales, and whether truly probabilistic reward models (rather than the Beta-distribution approximation used here) would further improve SteerLM 2.0.
