## Problem

Most probabilistic prediction methods for tabular data assume parametric response distributions (Gaussian, Poisson), leading to miscalibrated uncertainty when the true conditional is multimodal, skewed, or multivariate. Existing non-parametric alternatives—diffusion models capture complex distributions but are not designed for tabular data, while gradient-boosted trees excel on tables but only produce point predictions. Treeeffuser bridges this gap by making diffusions tree-based and tabular-native.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a standard tabular dataset of (x, y) pairs where x is a feature vector that may include categorical and missing values, and y is a continuous response (univariate or multivariate). Training augments each data point with R Monte Carlo samples of (t, ζ) to form d_y separate GBT training sets. At inference, given a single x, the model solves a reverse SDE via Euler-Maruyama (~50 steps) to produce arbitrary numbers of samples from p(y|x), from which means, quantiles, and probability intervals are estimated empirically.

## Method

Treeeffuser defines a conditional diffusion model in which each covariate value x induces its own forward/reverse SDE pair, and the conditional score ∇_y log p_{x,t}(y) is approximated by gradient-boosted trees (LightGBM). The key technical step is decomposing the joint conditional score objective into d_y independent scalar GBT regression problems (Eq. 12), where each tree predicts the k-th component of the noise ζ_k given the diffused observation h(ζ,t,y), time t, and covariates x. A noise-scaling reparametrization S = U/σ(t) is critical for training stability, as ablations show the model fails without it. Training is fast (53 s on 112k rows on a laptop CPU) because it reduces to standard GBT fitting on augmented data, while sampling requires only ~50 Euler-Maruyama steps per sample.

## Results

On 10 UCI benchmark datasets, Treeeffuser achieves the best CRPS (lower is better) on 9 out of 10 datasets, with values ranging from 1.49×10⁰ (power) to 2.68×10⁷ (movies), and ties with DRF on news (1.98×10³). Notably, Treeeffuser with default hyperparameters (no tuning) remains within 1–3% of its tuned version on every dataset. On the Walmart M5 sales forecasting task (112K training points, 27 features), Treeeffuser achieves the best CRPS of 6.44×10⁻¹, outperforming NGBoost-Poisson (6.86×10⁻¹) and Deep Ensembles (7.05×10⁻¹), while matching NGBoost-Poisson on MAE (0.99) and being within 3% on RMSE (2.09 vs. 2.03 for Deep Ensembles).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Treeeffuser outperforms all five probabilistic baselines on CRPS across the 10 UCI datasets, with the largest margins against NGBoost-Gaussian (e.g., 1.60 vs. 7.15 on bike, a 4.5× improvement) and Quantile Regression (e.g., 2.02 vs. 16.86 on naval). Deep Ensembles are the strongest runner-up, tying Treeeffuser on bike (1.61 vs. 1.60) and coming within 10% on several datasets, but Treeeffuser wins decisively on energy (3.07 vs. 5.00), naval (2.02 vs. 4.11), and power (1.49 vs. 2.06). IBUG (XGBoost) is competitive on smaller datasets (e.g., 1.61 vs. 1.49 on power) but degrades on high-dimensional data (e.g., 4.14 vs. 3.52 on superconductor with 81 features). DRF is the closest non-tree-boosting competitor, tying Treeeffuser on news (1.98 vs. 1.98) but losing on all other datasets. On the Walmart count-data task, Treeeffuser achieves the best CRPS (6.44 vs. 6.86 for NGBoost-Poisson) and matches NGBoost-Poisson on MAE (0.99), while Deep Ensembles edge out Treeeffuser slightly on RMSE (2.03 vs. 2.09), reflecting that point-prediction accuracy and full-distribution quality can diverge.

## Relation to Prior Work

Treeeffuser sits at the intersection of two well-developed lines of work: score-based conditional diffusion models (Batzolis et al. 2021, Tashiro et al. 2021) and gradient-boosted trees for tabular data (LightGBM, XGBoost). The closest prior method is CARD (Han et al. 2022), which uses neural-network-based diffusions for probabilistic regression on tabular data; Treeeffuser replaces the neural network score estimator with GBTs, gaining the inductive biases, categorical-data handling, and CPU-speed training that trees provide. The noise-scaling reparametrization S = U/σ(t) is a key technical departure from standard score-matching objectives, shown to be essential for stable training. On the probabilistic-prediction side, the paper positions itself against parametric tree methods (NGBoost, IBUG), non-parametric tree methods (Quantile Regression Forests, DRF), and neural methods (Deep Ensembles), all of which assume or approximate a fixed distributional family.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Treeeffuser offers a practical, non-parametric alternative for probabilistic prediction on tabular data that trains in seconds on a laptop and requires no dataset-specific hyperparameter tuning to outperform tuned baselines — a meaningful usability win for practitioners in supply chain, finance, and healthcare. Its main limitations are the continuous-response assumption (though it handles count data competitively) and the cost of SDE-based sampling when many samples per input are needed. Open directions include applying progressive distillation or consistency models to accelerate sampling, and extending the framework to natively model discrete outcomes. The noise-scaling ablation (complete failure without it) is a useful cautionary result for anyone adapting score-matching objectives to tree-based estimators.
