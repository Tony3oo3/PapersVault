## Problem

Exact Centroid-Linkage HAC requires essentially quadratic time in high-dimensional Euclidean spaces, and unlike Ward or average linkage, centroid distances can decrease after merges (non-monotonicity), making prior subquadratic approximate HAC techniques inapplicable. Additionally, any dynamic nearest-neighbor data structure used as a subroutine must handle adaptive updates—where updates depend on the data structure's own prior answers—a subtlety overlooked in prior work.

## Input / Output

The algorithm takes n points in R^d, an approximation factor c > 1.01, and lower/upper bounds δ and Δ on pairwise distances (requiring Δ/δ ≤ poly(n)). It produces a c-approximate centroid HAC dendrogram where each merge step selects a pair of clusters whose centroid distance is within a factor of c of the true minimum. The output is a full hierarchical tree of 2n−1 centroids with associated merge costs, from which flat clusterings are obtained by thresholded cuts.

## Method

The paper combines two contributions: (1) a meta-algorithm that reduces approximate Centroid-Linkage HAC to O(n) dynamic ANNS queries using a priority queue of candidate centroid pairs with a lazy-update strategy—stale pairs are only re-queried when dequeued, and the non-monotonicity of centroid distances is handled by allowing the minimum distance to shrink; (2) a dynamic ANNS data structure robust to adaptive updates, constructed via a merge-and-reduce scheme that maintains O(log n) non-adaptive LSH-based ANNS instances of geometrically increasing sizes, combined with a covering net to fix queries to a small set for union-bounding. The reduction from oblivious to adaptive ANNS incurs only an O(log n) overhead in query and insertion time. In practice, the theoretical LSH-based ANNS is replaced by a graph-based DiskANN/Vamana index with a lightweight union-find-based update rule for merged centroids, yielding up to 36× speedup over exact fastcluster while maintaining clustering quality within 7% on ARI/NMI.

## Results

For ε=0.1, the approximate Centroid HAC achieves ARI and NMI scores within 7% and 2% (on average) of exact Centroid HAC across seven benchmark datasets (iris, wine, cancer, digits, faces, MNIST, birds). Dendrogram purity and Dasgupta cost are within 0.3% and 0.03% respectively. On a single core, the heap-based approximate implementation achieves up to 36× speedup (average 5×) over fastcluster's exact Centroid HAC; with 192 parallel threads, speedups reach up to 175× (average 22×).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is the optimized exact Centroid HAC implementation from the fastcluster package, which requires quadratic space and runs sequentially. On clustering quality, the approximate method (ε=0.1) matches or slightly exceeds exact Centroid HAC on average ARI (0.471 vs. 0.453) and NMI (0.564 vs. 0.561), with per-dataset differences attributed to noise. The key advantage is speed: the heap-based approximate implementation achieves up to 36× speedup on a single core and up to 175× with 192 threads, while maintaining comparable quality. A secondary bucket-based approximate baseline (adapted from Abboud et al. [1]) is also compared; it achieves similar parallel speedups but fails to scale on a single thread due to redundant NNS queries (an order of magnitude more queries than the heap-based approach). The paper also notes that the ANNS data structure used empirically (DiskANN/Vamana) is a heuristic without theoretical guarantees, distinct from the LSH-based structure used for the theoretical runtime bound.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the subquadratic approximate HAC line of work initiated by Abboud, Cohen-Addad, and Houdrouge [1] (which handled Ward and average linkage) to centroid linkage, a fundamentally harder case because merge distances can decrease rather than monotonically increase. The theoretical core builds on LSH-based dynamic ANNS data structures [3, 19] via a novel black-box reduction from oblivious to adaptive updates (Theorem 2), using a merge-and-reduce strategy with covering nets. Empirically, the paper departs from the theoretical LSH construction and instead leverages the graph-based DiskANN/Vamana index [38, 18], extending it with a simple neighborhood-pruning update rule for centroid merges, and compares against the fastcluster [29] exact baseline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Centroid linkage is the most widely used HAC variant in practice (available in SciPy, scikit-learn, MATLAB, etc.), yet no subquadratic algorithm existed for it in high dimensions; this paper closes that gap with a c-approximate algorithm running in roughly n^{1+O(1/c²)} time. The adaptive ANNS data structure is of independent interest and fixes an overlooked correctness gap in prior subquadratic HAC work [1] and potentially improves dynamic k-centers [5]. Key open questions include whether a theoretically and practically efficient parallel algorithm exists for approximate centroid HAC, and how to close the large gap between LSH-based theoretical ANNS and graph-based practical ANNS. The 36× single-core speedup with <7% quality loss makes this immediately useful for practitioners working with large point sets.
