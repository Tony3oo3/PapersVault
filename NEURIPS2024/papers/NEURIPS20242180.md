## Problem

Existing adaptive KV cache compression methods rely on accumulated attention scores to identify salient tokens, but this metric is fundamentally biased by the lower-triangular structure of the attention matrix, causing earlier tokens to always appear more important than later ones. Additionally, computing full attention matrices for saliency estimation is incompatible with memory-efficient implementations like FlashAttention, and fine-grained groupwise quantization introduces excessive parameter overhead that erodes the compression ratio.

## Input / Output

The method takes the KV cache (key and value state matrices of shape b×h×l×d) produced during LLM inference, along with attention scores computed only for a small subset of probe tokens (~10%, split 5% recent and 5% random). It produces a mixed-precision quantized KV cache where salient tokens are stored at 4-bit and the remainder at 2-bit, plus the attention output. The key cache uses channelwise quantization while the value cache uses channel-separable tokenwise quantization, and the compression is re-applied every 100 generated tokens during decoding.

## Method

ZipCache combines three techniques: (1) a channel-separable tokenwise quantization scheme that normalizes each channel by √max(|X_i|) before applying tokenwise quantization, reducing quantization parameters from 2bhld/n (groupwise) to hd+2bl while handling channel outliers; (2) a normalized attention score metric that divides each token's accumulated attention by the count of non-zero elements in its column, correcting the positional bias inherent in the lower-triangular attention matrix; and (3) an efficient approximation that computes full attention scores only for probe tokens (5% recent + 5% random), allowing the remaining 90% of tokens to be processed via FlashAttention or FlashDecoding. Salient tokens identified by the normalized metric are quantized to 4-bit while the rest are compressed to 2-bit, achieving up to 4.98× compression with minimal accuracy loss.

## Results

On GSM8k with CoT prompting, ZipCache achieves 41.24% accuracy on Mistral-7B at 4.98× compression (only 0.38% below FP16's 41.62%), and 53.75% on LLaMA3-8B at 4.69× compression (2.13% below FP16's 55.88%). On HumanEval, ZipCache matches FP16 performance exactly on Mistral-7B (29.27%) at 4.94× compression. In terms of efficiency, ZipCache reduces prefill-phase latency by 37.3%, decoding-phase latency by 56.9%, and GPU memory usage by 19.8% compared to MiKV when serving LLaMA3-8B with 4096-token input.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ZipCache outperforms all four baselines across every model and dataset tested. Against MiKV (the closest competitor, also using 4/2-bit mixed precision), ZipCache achieves a dramatic 18.27% accuracy improvement on LLaMA3-8B GSM8k (53.75% vs. 35.48%) and 4.92% on Mistral-7B (41.24% vs. 36.32%), attributed to the normalized attention score metric correcting the bias of accumulated attention scores toward earlier tokens. Against KIVI, which retains recent tokens in full precision, ZipCache achieves higher accuracy at a substantially greater compression ratio (e.g., 4.98× vs. 3.46× on GSM8k, 4.94× vs. 2.55× on HumanEval). Against GEAR (uniform 4-bit), ZipCache matches or exceeds accuracy while compressing 40% of tokens to 2-bit, yielding a higher overall compression ratio. Against H2O (token eviction), ZipCache avoids permanent information loss by quantizing rather than discarding, resulting in far superior accuracy (e.g., 41.24% vs. 1.67% on Mistral-7B GSM8k). On the Line Retrieval task, ZipCache achieves 100% accuracy vs. MiKV's 90% and KIVI's 96%, while also reducing prefill latency by 38% compared to MiKV through FlashAttention integration.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of KV cache compression and post-training quantization for LLMs, building directly on the adaptive compression paradigm established by H2O (token eviction via accumulated attention scores) and MiKV (importance-aware mixed-precision quantization). It departs from these methods in two key ways: replacing the biased accumulated-attention-scores metric with a normalized variant that accounts for the lower-triangular structure of attention matrices, and introducing a probe-token approximation that decouples saliency estimation from full attention computation, thereby enabling FlashAttention/FlashDecoding integration. The channel-separable quantization scheme draws an analogy from depthwise separable convolutions (MobileNet) to reduce quantization parameter overhead relative to the groupwise quantization used by KIVI and GEAR.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

KV cache memory is a primary bottleneck for deploying LLMs at scale, and achieving ~5× compression with <1% accuracy loss while maintaining FlashAttention compatibility addresses a critical practical gap. The normalized attention score metric corrects a systematic bias in the standard saliency criterion that prior methods (H2O, MiKV) inherited, which is particularly impactful for retrieval and long-context tasks where salient tokens are not necessarily recent. Key limitations include the manually specified saliency ratio (no automatic adaptation to task or dataset) and the reliance on a fixed probe-token strategy; open questions include whether the approach extends to multi-modal or non-Transformer architectures and whether the saliency ratio can be learned or inferred online.
