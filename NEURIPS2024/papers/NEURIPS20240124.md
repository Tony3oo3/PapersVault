## Problem

Testable learning extends agnostic learning by adding a tester that verifies distributional assumptions, but it was unclear whether this added verification incurs a computational penalty for complex concept classes. While halfspaces can be testably learned at the same complexity as agnostic learning, no such result existed even for degree-2 polynomial threshold functions (PTFs), which sit between halfspaces and convex sets in agnostic learning complexity. This paper asks whether PTFs of arbitrary constant degree can be testably learned efficiently under the standard Gaussian.

## Input / Output

The algorithm receives m iid labeled samples (x, z) ∈ R^n × {±1} and first runs a moment-matching tester T_AMM(k, η) that checks whether the empirical moments of the sample marginal up to degree k match those of N(0, I_n) within slack η (ignoring labels). If the tester accepts, a polynomial regression learner outputs a classifier f̂: R^n → {±1} achieving loss at most opt + ε. The tester runs in O(m · n^k) time, and the overall sample complexity is dominated by the number of samples needed for empirical moments to concentrate, m = Θ((2kn)^k · η^{-2}).

## Method

The proof proceeds via a fooling-based framework from Gollakota, Klivans & Kothari: if every distribution matching Gaussian moments up to degree k fools the concept class, then testable learning follows in time n^{O(k)}. The key technical contribution is showing that approximate moment-matching distributions (a strictly weaker condition than k-independent Gaussians) fool degree-d PTFs, by extending Kane's construction. For multilinear PTFs, a structure theorem decomposes the PTF into a composition F ∘ P, and a mollified Taylor approximation h = T ∘ P is shown to have close expectations under both the Gaussian and the moment-matching distribution; the critical new step is bounding the inner product ⟨t, u⟩ between Taylor coefficients and moment-difference vectors via a 'change of basis' argument. For general PTFs, each variable is replaced by N copies with correlated Gaussian noise to reduce to the multilinear case, with weaker concentration bounds (using only constant-degree moments) replacing the strong Gaussian concentration available in Kane's setting. As a secondary result, the paper proves that the direct push-forward approach (approximating sign via q ∘ p) provably fails for PTFs of degree ≥ 3, by extending the Bun–Steinke inapproximability theorem to one-sided log-superlinear distributions.

## Results

The main result (Theorem 19) establishes that degree-d polynomial threshold functions can be testably learned w.r.t. the standard Gaussian in time and sample complexity $n^{O_d(\varepsilon^{-4d \cdot 7^d})} \varepsilon^{-O_d(\varepsilon^{-4d \cdot 7^d})}$, which is $n^{\text{poly}(1/\varepsilon)}$ for any fixed constant degree $d$. This qualitatively matches the best known agnostic learning guarantee of $n^{O(d^2/\varepsilon^4)}$, though the dependence on $\varepsilon$ is substantially worse in the testable setting. As a secondary result, Theorem 16 proves that the direct push-forward approximation approach (which works for halfspaces) provably fails for PTFs of degree $\geq 3$, even under natural structural assumptions (square-free polynomial, compact positive set).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a pure theory paper with no empirical baselines or experimental comparisons. The qualitative comparisons are: (1) Against the agnostic model, testable learning of PTFs incurs a worse dependence on $\varepsilon$ (roughly $\varepsilon^{-4d \cdot 7^d}$ vs. $\varepsilon^{-4}$) but remains polynomial in $n$ for fixed $d$, showing no qualitative computational gap between the two models for PTFs. (2) Against the direct push-forward approach of Rubinfeld & Vasilyan [28] (which achieves $n^{\tilde{O}(1/\varepsilon^2)}$ for halfspaces), the paper proves this technique is fundamentally inapplicable to PTFs of degree $\geq 3$ via a one-sided log-superlinear inapproximability result (Theorem 18, extending Bun & Steinke [6]). (3) Against Kane's [21] result that $k$-independent Gaussians fool PTFs, the paper generalizes to the much broader class of distributions that merely approximately match Gaussian moments up to degree $k$, which is the class accepted by the moment-matching tester. The paper notes that no lower bounds specific to testable learning of PTFs are known; existing lower bounds from the agnostic model (SQ hardness $n^{\Omega(d^{2-\beta}/\varepsilon^2)}$) apply a fortiori.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the testable learning framework of Rubinfeld & Vasilyan [28] and the fooling-based approach of Gollakota, Klivans & Kothari [15] from halfspaces to polynomial threshold functions (PTFs). The core technical contribution generalizes Kane's [21] result that k-independent Gaussians fool PTFs to the broader class of distributions that merely approximately match Gaussian moments, which is the class accepted by the moment-matching tester. The impossibility result (Theorem 16) extends the Bun & Steinke [6] inapproximability theorem for log-superlinear distributions to a 'one-sided' variant, showing that the direct push-forward approach that works for halfspaces provably fails for PTFs of degree ≥ 3.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first result showing efficient testable learning for PTFs of any fixed degree, closing a gap between the agnostic and testable models for this important concept class. The impossibility result is notable because it rules out the most natural generalization of the halfspace technique, indicating that the fooling-based approach is not merely convenient but essentially necessary for PTFs. Open questions include whether the polynomial-in-1/ε dependence (with a very large exponent) is inherent or an artifact of the analysis, and whether similar fooling-based techniques can extend to even more complex concept classes where agnostic learning is known to be feasible.
