## Problem

Existing neural rendering methods (NeRF, 3DGS) are designed for multi-sensor autonomous vehicle datasets and assume static scenes, but dash cam videos contain windshield obstructions—reflections, phone holders, stains—that are dynamic (moving with the car) and vary in opacity from transparent to opaque. Naively applying 3DGS to such videos models these obstructions as stationary 3D geometry, producing erroneous scene structure and blurry novel-view renderings. No prior method handles the combined challenge of mixed-opacity obstructions, illumination-dependent reflection intensity, and large-scale driving scenes simultaneously.

## Input / Output

The method takes a dash cam video sequence of approximately 300 frames (extracted from 10-second clips at 30 Hz, undistorted via OpenCV) along with camera poses estimated by COLMAP and HLoc. It produces a 3D Gaussian Splatting scene that renders novel views at 1920×1080 at 120 FPS on an RTX 3090, and simultaneously decomposes each frame into an obstruction-free transmission image and an obstruction image (reflections/occlusions). Sky regions are modeled separately following prior driving-scene reconstruction work, and the lower portion of images containing car hoods is cropped during preprocessing.

## Method

DC-Gaussian extends 3DGS with three modules. First, an Adaptive Image Decomposition learns a per-view 2D opacity map φ that adaptively blends transmission and obstruction, unifying handling of transparent reflections, semi-transparent stains, and opaque holders in a single formulation (I = (1−φ)·I_t + φ·I_o). Second, the Illumination-aware Obstruction Modeling (IOM) represents obstruction appearance with a global-shared multi-resolution hash encoding over 2D image coordinates (exploiting the fact that obstructions are static in camera space), and a Latent Intensity Modulation module applies camera-position-conditioned scaling and offset gates to the latent features to synthesize reflections whose intensity varies with the car's position and ambient lighting. Third, Geometry-Guided Gaussian Enhancement (G3E) runs PatchMatchNet MVS on obstruction-suppressed transmission images, applies geometric consistency filtering to identify multi-view-consistent fragments, and uses the resulting dense point clouds to initialize 3D Gaussians at physically accurate positions, suppressing road-surface artifacts and sharpening details. Training uses a combined photometric + sky + L1 opacity-regularization loss over 30k iterations on an A100 GPU (~30 min per scene).

## Results

On BDD100K, DC-Gaussian achieves 29.44 PSNR, 0.914 SSIM, and 0.143 LPIPS, outperforming the strongest baseline 3DGS (28.02 PSNR) by 1.42 dB. On the self-captured DCVR dataset with stronger reflections, DC-Gaussian reaches 24.74 PSNR, 0.822 SSIM, and 0.202 LPIPS, again leading all baselines. The method renders at 120 FPS at 1920×1080 on an RTX 3090, maintaining real-time performance despite the added obstruction modeling. Ablation studies confirm each module contributes: the full pipeline gains 2.31 dB PSNR over the 3DGS baseline on DCVR obstruction-affected regions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DC-Gaussian outperforms all four novel-view-synthesis baselines on both datasets across every metric. Against 3DGS [19], the closest competitor, it gains 1.42 dB PSNR on BDD100K and 1.01 dB on DCVR, while also improving SSIM by 0.017 and reducing LPIPS by 0.045 on BDD100K. ZipNeRF [4] and GaussianPro [9] achieve competitive scores on obstruction-free scenes but degrade noticeably on dash cam videos because they lack explicit obstruction modeling; DC-Gaussian surpasses them by 1.55 dB (vs. ZipNeRF) and 1.69 dB (vs. GaussianPro) on BDD100K PSNR. NeRF-W [29] trails by a wide margin (6.86 dB PSNR gap on BDD100K) as it was designed for illumination variation, not windshield obstructions. For obstruction removal, DC-Gaussian qualitatively outperforms single-image methods DSRNet [17] and Liu et al. [27], which only marginally suppress reflections, and multi-image layer separation NIR [32], which produces blurry outputs due to inaccurate optical flow in driving scenes. NeRFRen [16], evaluated in the appendix, fails to decompose obstructions from transmission and produces blurry results because its NeRF backbone cannot handle large-scale driving scenes.

## Relation to Prior Work

DC-Gaussian extends 3D Gaussian Splatting to the previously unexplored domain of dash cam videos by integrating an illumination-aware obstruction modeling module built on multi-resolution hash encoding (from Instant-NGP). It departs from the NeRF-with-reflections line (NeRFRen, NeuS-HSR, Neural Transmitted Radiance Fields), which assumes static scenes and independent NeRFs for transmission and reflection, by exploiting the key insight that windshield obstructions are static in camera coordinates while their intensity varies with vehicle position. The Geometry-Guided Gaussian Enhancement module further differentiates the work from standard 3DGS by incorporating MVS-derived depth priors (via PatchMatchNet) to initialize Gaussians at physically accurate positions, a strategy not present in prior 3DGS driving-scene extensions like DrivingGaussian or GaussianPro.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Dash cam videos are orders of magnitude more available and diverse than autonomous-vehicle datasets, making this work a practical bridge between the vast dash cam data ecosystem and real-time neural rendering for driving simulation. The method achieves 120 FPS rendering, preserving the real-time property essential for closed-loop simulators. However, it is currently limited to single-sequence videos and does not explicitly handle fast-moving dynamic objects, leaving open the question of whether the global-shared obstruction prior generalizes to multi-sequence or long-duration settings. The DCVR dataset, while small (10 sequences), provides a useful benchmark for reflection-heavy dash cam scenarios that existing datasets like BDD100K only partially cover.
