## Problem

Standard DP-(S)GD for convex optimization yields excess error that scales linearly with the a priori radius R bounding the data, which can be extremely loose when a small number of outliers inflate the diameter while most points cluster tightly. The paper asks whether one can design an efficient DP algorithm for the geometric median whose excess error instead scales with the effective diameter of the majority of the data (the quantile radius), achieving a nearly multiplicative guarantee that is adaptive to the dataset's intrinsic scale.

## Input / Output

The algorithm takes n points in R^d, a privacy budget (ε,δ) or ρ-zCDP, and an a priori radius R bounding all points. It outputs an approximate geometric median θ̂ ∈ R^d. Crucially, the excess error guarantee is multiplicative in the optimal loss F(θ*;X) with a factor (1 + Õ(√d/(nε))) rather than additive in R; the a priori bound R appears only logarithmically in the sample complexity Õ(√(d log(R/r))/ε) and in the warm-up runtime. A small discretization parameter r (a lower bound on the quantile radius) controls an additive error term of O(r) that vanishes under a mild non-concentration assumption.

## Method

The approach is a two-phase pipeline. In the warm-up phase, a RadiusFinder subroutine privately estimates the quantile radius Δ_{3n/4}(θ*) via the AboveThreshold mechanism over a geometric grid of radii (sensitivity ≤ 3 per query), and then a Localization step runs DP-GD with geometrically decaying step sizes and shrinking feasible balls to produce an initialization θ₀ within O(Δ_{4n/5}(θ*)) of the true GM in Õ(log(R/r)) iterations. The key structural insight enabling fast convergence is a linear growth condition: when θ is farther than Δ_{3n/4}(θ*) from the optimum, the loss increases at least linearly in ‖θ−θ*‖, permitting larger steps than standard convex analysis allows. In the fine-tuning phase, the paper offers two polynomial-time options: LocDPGD (standard DP-GD on the localized ball) and LocDPCuttingPlane (a private cutting-plane method using the John ellipsoid as the centre subroutine, with a novel sensitivity analysis of the exponential mechanism that avoids the R-dependent loss). A third, computationally inefficient algorithm (SInvS) based on the inverse smooth sensitivity mechanism achieves pure (ε,0)-DP with a multiplicative guarantee.

## Results

The proposed polynomial-time algorithms (LocDPSGD and LocDPCuttingPlane) achieve a multiplicative excess error of (1 + √d/(nε))·OPT, where OPT is the optimal geometric median loss, compared to the additive O(R√d/ε) error of the DP-(S)GD baseline. The sample complexity is √(d·log(R/r))/ε, which is nearly optimal given the proven lower bound of Ω̃(√d/ε) for any (ε,δ)-DP algorithm with constant multiplicative error. The pure-DP algorithm (SInvS) achieves (1 + d·log(R/r)/(nε))·OPT but requires exponential runtime. Numerical experiments on synthetic data confirm that LocDPGD's relative loss degrades more gracefully than DP-GD as the a priori radius R increases.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is DP-(S)GD [Bassily et al., FOCS 2014], the standard algorithm for private convex optimization. The key advantage of the proposed methods is a fundamental change in error scaling: DP-(S)GD incurs an additive error of O(R√d/ε) that grows linearly with the a priori radius R, while LocDPSGD and LocDPCuttingPlane achieve a multiplicative guarantee (1 + √d/(nε))·OPT that scales with the quantile radius Δ_{4n/5}(θ*)—the effective diameter of the majority of the data—rather than R. This means the proposed algorithms are adaptive to the data: when most points are tightly clustered (small quantile radius) but a few outliers force R to be large, the proposed methods maintain near-optimal accuracy while DP-(S)GD degrades linearly with R. The numerical experiment in Section 6 confirms this: on a synthetic dataset with a tight cluster plus uniform outliers, LocDPGD's relative loss F(θ̂)/F(θ*) degrades much more slowly than DP-GD as R increases. The trade-off is that the proposed algorithms require √(d·log(R/r))/ε samples (vs. no explicit sample requirement for DP-(S)GD) and have a warm-up phase with n²log(R/r) runtime, though the lower bound in Section 5 shows this sample complexity is nearly optimal.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the standard DP-(S)GD framework [BST14] for private convex optimization, which is known to have a linear dependence on the a priori radius R of the feasible set. The key departure is exploiting a structural property specific to the geometric median loss—linear (rather than quadratic) growth away from the optimum—to design a two-phase algorithm (warm-up via RadiusFinder inspired by [NSV16] and fine-tuning via DP-GD or a private cutting plane method) that reduces the dependence on R from linear to logarithmic. The pure-DP variant repurposes the inverse smooth sensitivity mechanism of [AD20], and the cutting-plane fine-tuning leverages the John ellipsoid [Joh14] for dimension-independent volume reduction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result demonstrates that the linear-in-R dependence of DP convex optimization, while worst-case necessary in general, is not tight for specific problem structures, opening a path toward adaptive private optimization that scales with the effective data scale rather than worst-case bounds. The matching lower bound on sample complexity (Ω̃(√d/ε)) confirms the algorithms are optimal in this regard. Open questions include whether the warm-up strategy generalizes to other non-strongly-convex losses, whether a linear-time fine-tuning algorithm exists, and whether the logarithmic R-dependence can be further improved (e.g., to log* R as in 1D private median results). The practical impact is currently limited to the geometric median task, though the authors note the warm-up subroutine may have broader applications.
