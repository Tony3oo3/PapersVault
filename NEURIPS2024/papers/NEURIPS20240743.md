## Problem

Existing depth completion methods are tailored to specific sensor configurations (e.g., 64-line Velodyne for KITTI, Kinect for NYUv2) and fail to generalize across arbitrary sensors and unseen environments, particularly under few-shot or zero-shot conditions. The paper defines the Universal Depth Completion (UniDC) problem, targeting a framework that adapts to any depth sensor and scene configuration with minimal labeled data, addressing both the high cost of dense depth annotation and the scale variance across sensors.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240743/cropped_page2_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image (resized to preserve aspect ratio for the foundation model) and a sparse depth map from an arbitrary active sensor (LiDAR at 4/8/16/32/64-line, Kinect, ToF, or randomly sampled points) as input, producing a dense metric-scale depth map at the image resolution. Notably, no dedicated depth encoder is required; the sparse depth is consumed directly through a bilateral propagation kernel that weights neighboring sensor readings by hyperbolic feature similarity, making the pipeline sensor-agnostic by design.

## Method

The architecture operates in three stages: (1) multi-scale feature extraction from a pre-trained monocular depth foundation model (MiDaS/DepthAnything/UniDepth) with bias tuning and a scale-invariant loss to bridge relative-to-metric depth; (2) sparse-to-dense conversion via a bilateral filtering mechanism where the range kernel is computed in hyperbolic space (using learned, scene-dependent curvature via a curvature generator) and the spatial kernel uses Euclidean 3D point distances; (3) depth refinement with CSPN++ using multi-kernel affinity maps (kernels 3, 5, 7) each computed in a separate hyperbolic space with its own curvature, enabling hierarchical structure capture that prevents boundary bleeding errors. The key differentiator is the elimination of a dedicated depth encoder in favor of foundation model features, combined with adaptive multi-curvature hyperbolic geometry that encodes implicit 3D hierarchies from sparse seeds, all trained in a few-shot regime (1/10/100-shot) with only ~4.6M learnable parameters.

## Results

In the 1-shot setting on NYUv2, UniDC achieves 0.210 RMSE / 0.108 MAE / 0.975 Δ₁, substantially outperforming the next-best method OGNI-DC (0.365 / 0.200 / 0.921) and DFU (0.754 / 0.589 / 0.637). On KITTI DC 1-shot, UniDC reaches 1.684 RMSE / 0.522 MAE / 0.983 Δ₁, compared to OGNI-DC (2.618 / 0.816 / 0.962) and LRRU_Base (14.889 / 7.454 / 0.526). In the zero-shot (no training) regime, the hyperbolic variant achieves 0.323 / 0.246 RMSE/MAE on NYU and 4.061 / 1.974 on KITTI, while the Euclidean counterpart fails catastrophically (13.889 / 11.507 and 77.642 / 63.109 respectively). Without dense GT supervision (8-line LiDAR input, 64-line supervision), UniDC attains 4.34 / 1.77 RMSE/MAE at 1-shot on KITTI, nearly halving the error of BPNet (11.64 / 3.19) and DepthPrompting (8.15 / 5.67).

## Baselines & Comparisons

UniDC consistently outperforms all 11 baselines across every shot setting and both indoor (NYUv2) and outdoor (KITTI DC) benchmarks. Against the strongest recent competitor OGNI-DC, UniDC reduces RMSE by 42% on NYU 1-shot (0.210 vs 0.365) and by 36% on KITTI 1-shot (1.684 vs 2.618). The advantage is most dramatic against LRRU on KITTI, where LRRU_Base suffers severe domain bias (14.889 RMSE at 1-shot) while UniDC stays at 1.684, attributed to LRRU's IP-Basic algorithm being biased toward KITTI's specific sensor configuration. Against DepthPrompting (the prior sensor-agnostic method), UniDC achieves roughly 2× lower RMSE in the no-dense-GT setting (4.34 vs 8.15 at 1-shot, 8-line LiDAR). On SUN RGB-D (a third dataset with heterogeneous sensors), UniDC leads at 1-shot (0.529 vs 0.706 for DepthPrompting) and 10-shot (0.418 vs 0.466 for OGNI-DC), though at 100-shot it is essentially tied with BPNet (0.345 vs 0.342 RMSE). The hyperbolic embedding provides a consistent ~5% average improvement over its Euclidean counterpart in few-shot regimes, and is essential for zero-shot operation where the Euclidean variant completely fails.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the spatial propagation network (SPN) lineage—particularly CSPN++ and hyperbolic affinity propagation (Park et al., ICML 2023)—by replacing the learned depth encoder with features extracted from a pre-trained monocular depth foundation model (MiDaS, DepthAnything, UniDepth). It departs from both conventional SPNs (which jointly optimize image and depth encoders) and DepthPrompting (which still requires training a depth prompting module) by eliminating any dedicated depth encoder entirely, instead propagating sparse sensor depth through foundation-model features via a bilateral-filtering-inspired mechanism in hyperbolic space. The multi-curvature affinity generation, where different kernel sizes receive scene-adaptive curvature values, is a novel extension of prior fixed-curvature hyperbolic approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The UniDC problem framing—universal depth completion across arbitrary sensors and environments with minimal labeled data—addresses a practical gap where existing methods overfit to specific sensor configurations (e.g., 64-line Velodyne for KITTI, Kinect for NYU). The finding that a 4.6M-parameter model leveraging foundation model knowledge outperforms 80M+ parameter models in few-shot settings suggests a fundamentally different scaling strategy for depth completion. However, limitations remain: the method is restricted to single-viewpoint input, cannot handle highly noisy modalities like radar, and full-dataset training results (Table 11) show it trails SoTA on in-domain benchmarks, indicating the foundation-model advantage is most pronounced in data-scarce regimes.
