## Problem

Existing video-to-4D generation methods are limited to single objects with minor in-place deformations, because view-predictive generative models (e.g., Zero-1-to-3) perform far better on individual objects than on full multi-object scenes, making score distillation infeasible at the scene level. Additionally, rendering error gradients are too weak to recover fast object motions when optimizing deformations directly in the world frame. DreamScene4D is the first approach to generate complete 360° dynamic 4D scenes from monocular videos containing multiple objects with large motions.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single monocular video (no multi-view or calibration required) and produces a 4D scene represented as a set of dynamic 3D Gaussians that can be rendered from arbitrary camera poses. Each object is first cropped to ~65% of the image size and amodally completed via a video inpainting diffusion model before 3D lifting. The output also yields 2D persistent point trajectories by projecting the inferred 3D Gaussian motions, enabling motion evaluation in both reference and novel views. Monocular depth (Depth-Anything) is used only for the final scene composition step to determine relative object scales along camera rays.

## Method

DreamScene4D employs a 'decompose-recompose' pipeline: the video is segmented into object tracks and background, each amodally completed, then individually lifted to static 3D Gaussians via SDS with Zero-1-to-3 in an object-centric cropped frame. The key insight is factorizing 3D object motion into three independently optimized components—(1) object-centric deformation via a Hexplane-based K-plane network predicting per-Gaussian position, rotation, and scale, (2) an object-to-world affine transformation (translation + scaling) initialized from 2D bounding-box tracks, and (3) camera motion estimated by re-rendering static background Gaussians—so that SDS operates in-distribution on object-centric inputs while bounding boxes handle large displacements. A flow rendering loss (masked L1 against GMFlow optical flow) and physical priors (scale smoothness, local rigidity) further constrain the deformation. Finally, monocular depth-guided composition aligns all independently optimized objects into a unified coordinate frame by optimizing per-object depth scaling factors against an affine-invariant depth loss.

## Results

On DAVIS, DreamScene4D achieves a CLIP score of 85.09 and LPIPS of 0.152 for novel-view rendering quality, outperforming Consistent4D (82.14 / 0.141) and DreamGaussian4D (77.81 / 0.181). On Kubric, it reaches CLIP 85.53 and LPIPS 0.112, again leading all baselines. For 3D motion accuracy, DreamScene4D reduces the mean EPE on DAVIS to 8.56 (vs. 26.65 for DreamGaussian4D) and achieves 14.30/18.31 EPE on visible/occluded points in Kubric (vs. 101.79/120.95 for DreamGaussian4D), even surpassing the supervised point tracker PIPS++ (19.61 mean EPE on DAVIS). In user preference studies, DreamScene4D is preferred over Consistent4D (71.7%), DreamGaussian4D (77.9%), and DreamGaussian4D+VSD (69.6%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DreamScene4D outperforms all four video-to-4D generation baselines on both rendering quality and motion accuracy. Against Consistent4D [17], it gains +2.95 CLIP on DAVIS and +5.07 CLIP on Kubric while also improving LPIPS, and wins 71.7% of user preference votes. Against DreamGaussian4D [43], the improvement is larger: +7.28 CLIP on DAVIS and +12.08 CLIP on Kubric, with a 77.9% user preference win. The ablated DreamGaussian4D+VSD variant (adding scene decomposition but not motion factorization) still trails DreamScene4D by 3.7 CLIP on DAVIS, confirming that motion factorization is the critical additional contribution. For motion accuracy, DreamScene4D reduces DAVIS mean EPE from 26.65 (DreamGaussian4D) to 8.56, and Kubric occluded-point EPE from 120.95 to 18.31. Notably, DreamScene4D surpasses the supervised point tracker PIPS++ [65] on DAVIS mean EPE (8.56 vs. 19.61) despite no point-tracking training data, though CoTracker [18] (trained on Kubric) remains stronger on Kubric (2.51 vs. 14.30 EPE on visible points). For video amodal completion (appendix), DreamScene4D's inpainting approach achieves PSNR 22.27 and temporal consistency 93.40, outperforming both Repaint [29] (20.76 / 91.18) and SD-Inpaint [45] (21.07 / 91.72).

## Relation to Prior Work

DreamScene4D extends the video-to-4D generation line (DreamGaussian4D, Consistent4D) from single-object, small-deformation settings to multi-object scenes with large motion. It builds on 3D Gaussian Splatting as the scene representation, DreamGaussian's SDS-based lifting with Zero-1-to-3 as the view-predictive prior, and K-Planes/Hexplane for deformation modeling. The key conceptual departure is a 'decompose-recompose' strategy: rather than applying SDS at the scene level (which fails because view-predictive models are object-centric), the paper segments objects, lifts each independently in an object-centric frame, factorizes motion into object-centric deformation + rigid transform + camera motion, and recomposes using monocular depth. This allows the SDS loss to operate in-distribution while bounding box tracks handle large displacements that rendering gradients alone cannot recover.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first approach to produce 360° novel-view 4D scenes from real-world multi-object monocular videos with fast motion, a setting where prior methods produce broken geometry or blurry artifacts. The motion factorization insight (letting trackers handle large motion while SDS handles appearance) is a practical recipe likely to be adopted in future 4D generation work. However, limitations remain: SDS fails at steep elevation angles, scene composition can fall into local optima with poor depth, heavy occlusions still cause artifacts, and runtime scales linearly with object count. The paper also highlights that data-driven (rather than per-scene optimization) approaches for video-to-4D are a natural next step.
