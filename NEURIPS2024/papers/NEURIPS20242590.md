## Problem

One-shot federated learning (single client-server communication round) suffers severe performance degradation under non-IID data distributions, yet existing solutions either require auxiliary public datasets, expose private label distributions, rely on pre-trained large models, or use computationally expensive distillation/generation pipelines. No prior method can aggregate local models in a single round while accurately capturing the statistics of biased local datasets without any extra data or label leakage.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242590/cropped_page6_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each client uploads three items per layer: the local optimal weight matrix M_k, and two Kronecker factor matrices A_k (input-activation covariance) and B_k (output-gradient covariance) computed via layer-wise Laplace approximation. No auxiliary dataset, label distribution, or raw data is transmitted. The server produces the global model parameters by solving a per-layer quadratic optimization problem, requiring no server-side training data. The additional A_k and B_k matrices roughly double the per-client communication compared to FedAvg (2.272 MB vs. 1.662 MB for a 784-256-64-10 MLP), but only the upper triangular parts of the symmetric matrices are transmitted.

## Method

FedLPA approximates each local posterior as a Gaussian whose covariance is the inverse of a block-diagonal empirical Fisher matrix, where each block is Kronecker-factored (A_k_l ⊗ B_k_l) to capture inter-parameter correlations within a layer while assuming independence across layers. Rather than inverting the prohibitively large aggregated Fisher matrix (O(n³) complexity), the server constructs a multi-variate linear objective f(μ̄) = Σ_k vec(B_k M̄ A_k) − z̄ from the Gaussian product rule and minimizes its squared L2 norm, yielding a convex quadratic problem solvable by gradient descent with linear convergence. This avoids the biased Expectation Approximation (E[A⊗B] ≈ E[A]⊗E[B]) used by prior K-FAC-based methods, which introduces cross-client correlation errors. The approach is data-free, requires no label information, and the block-diagonal structure provides a principled trade-off between capturing within-layer parameter correlations and computational tractability.

## Results

FedLPA achieves the best one-shot accuracy across all four datasets (MNIST, Fashion-MNIST, CIFAR-10, SVHN) and all nine non-IID partition settings. On MNIST with β=1.0, FedLPA reaches 93.37% test accuracy, outperforming the closest baseline DENSE (91.43%) and FedAvg (85.10%). In extreme label-skew settings (#C=2 on MNIST), FedLPA achieves 69.63% versus 52.73% for DENSE and 16.47% for FedAvg, demonstrating a 17-point advantage over the strongest competitor. On SVHN with β=1.0, FedLPA attains 67.80% compared to 54.40% for DENSE and 55.97% for FedAvg.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedLPA outperforms all five main baselines (FedAvg, FedNova, SCAFFOLD, FedProx, DENSE) across every dataset and partition setting in Table 1. DENSE is the closest competitor, achieving comparable accuracy under mild skew (e.g., 72.87% vs. 73.33% on FMNIST β=0.5; 91.43% vs. 93.37% on MNIST β=1.0), but FedLPA pulls significantly ahead as heterogeneity increases (e.g., 69.63% vs. 52.73% on MNIST #C=2; 67.80% vs. 54.40% on SVHN β=1.0). FedAvg, FedNova, SCAFFOLD, and FedProx all degrade sharply under extreme non-IID settings, often falling to near-chance accuracy (10–13%) on #C=1 partitions, while FedLPA maintains 11–21%. In the appendix comparison with DiagonalFisher (which uses diagonal rather than block-diagonal Fisher matrices), FedLPA achieves 76.63% vs. 56.34% with shared initialization and 73.73% vs. 10.51% with independent initialization on FMNIST (β=0.01, MLP), demonstrating that capturing intra-layer parameter correlations is critical for robust aggregation.

## Relation to Prior Work

FedLPA sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) Bayesian posterior aggregation in federated learning (Al-Shedivat et al., 2020), which established the Gaussian product rule for combining local posteriors but required multiple rounds; (2) Kronecker-factored approximate curvature (K-FAC, Martens & Grosse, 2015) and scalable Laplace approximation (Ritter et al., 2018), which provide efficient block-diagonal Fisher approximations for neural networks; and (3) one-shot FL methods that either rely on auxiliary public datasets (FedKT, FedBE), expose client label distributions (FedOV, FEDCVAE), or use data-free distillation (DENSE). The key departure is that FedLPA is the first to combine block-diagonal (non-diagonal, non-low-rank) empirical Fisher matrices with a one-shot posterior aggregation, and it circumvents the intractable inversion of the aggregated Kronecker product by reformulating the global expectation as a convex quadratic optimization problem.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

One-shot FL is practically important in settings where iterative communication is infeasible or where each additional round increases privacy attack surface, and FedLPA offers a data-free, label-free solution that consistently outperforms baselines under severe non-IID conditions. However, the evaluation is limited to small-scale image datasets (MNIST, FMNIST, CIFAR-10, SVHN) and simple 5-layer CNNs, leaving open whether the approach scales to deeper architectures, larger datasets, or non-vision tasks. The additional communication cost of uploading Kronecker factor matrices (A_k, B_k) per client, while modest in absolute terms, grows with layer width and may become prohibitive for very wide networks. The paper also leaves integration with stronger privacy mechanisms (beyond a naive DP variant) and handling of enormous single-layer weight matrices as open problems.
