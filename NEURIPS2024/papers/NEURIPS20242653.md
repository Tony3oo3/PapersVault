## Problem

Prior-data fitted networks (PFNs) like TabPFN achieve state-of-the-art tabular classification on small datasets via in-context learning, but are hard-limited to ≤1000 samples, ≤100 features, and ≤10 classes, making them impractical for most real-world tabular problems. No prior work had investigated context optimization strategies for PFNs, leaving a gap between their strong small-data performance and the need to scale to large datasets without full retraining.

## Input / Output

TuneTables takes a tabular dataset (feature matrix and class labels) and produces class probabilities via a single forward pass through a pretrained TabPFN. The key input detail is a small learned prompt matrix (p×e, where e is the transformer embedding dimension and p is typically 10 or 1000) that is prepended to the real-data embeddings; in the 'no context' (NC) inference setting, only this prompt is fed to the model at test time, eliminating the need for real training data in the context and yielding ~9× faster inference than TabPFNs3000. For datasets exceeding TabPFN's 10-class limit, a new decoder MLP is fitted on top of the frozen transformer.

## Method

TuneTables adapts soft prompt tuning from NLP to tabular PFNs by prepending a randomly initialized parameter matrix to both the continuous feature embeddings and the categorical label embeddings of TabPFN, then optimizing only this prompt (and optionally the decoder MLP) via gradient descent on the downstream task. The approach handles three scaling challenges: (1) for >100 features, a grid search selects the best feature subselection method (random, PCA, mutual information); (2) for >10 classes, the decoder MLP is retrained while freezing all transformer weights; (3) for large sample counts, the tuned prompt compresses the full dataset into a compact learned context. At inference, two settings are evaluated—C (prompt + real data context) and NC (prompt only)—and the better-performing one is selected. Ensembling over multiple tuned prompts with permuted feature/label orderings further improves accuracy, while the total number of optimized parameters remains under 5% of TabPFN's.

## Results

On the TabZilla Benchmark Suite (98 datasets), TuneTables achieves the highest mean accuracy of 0.860 and best mean rank of 7.435, winning on 30 of 98 datasets — more than any other method. On the newly introduced LARGESCALETABLES benchmark (29 datasets up to 1.9M samples and 7,200 features), TuneTables reaches 0.830 mean accuracy, outperforming CatBoost (0.823) and XGBoost (0.826), and achieves 100% accuracy on poker-hand (1,025,009 samples). At inference time, TuneTables is approximately 9× faster than TabPFNs3000, and the efficient TuneTables-medium variant reduces end-to-end runtime by 72% with less than 0.25% accuracy loss.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TuneTables (0.860 mean accuracy, 30 wins) narrowly outperforms CatBoost (0.856, 13 wins) and XGBoost (0.854, 16 wins) on the 98-dataset TabZilla suite, though the Friedman/Wilcoxon tests show the top three are not statistically significantly different. On the LARGESCALETABLES benchmark (datasets up to 1.9M samples), TuneTables (0.830) clearly beats both CatBoost (0.823) and XGBoost (0.826), and achieves 100% accuracy on poker-hand where CatBoost reaches only 91.2%. Against TabPFNs3000 (the zero-shot TabPFN with 3000-sample subsampling), TuneTables improves mean accuracy from 0.781 to 0.830 on LARGESCALETABLES while being ~9× faster at inference. On the 15 large-class datasets (up to 100 classes), XGBoost (mean rank 2.0) slightly edges out TuneTables (rank 2.52), but TuneTables still beats CatBoost (rank 4.5) and all neural net baselines. For fairness, TuneTables with a demographic-parity regularizer reduces mean DP from 0.183 (TabPFN) to 0.109 while maintaining competitive accuracy (0.840 vs. 0.842). LightGBM (20 wins) and XGBoost (16 wins) are the closest competitors in per-dataset wins, but TuneTables dominates on the largest and most feature-rich datasets where GBDTs and other neural nets degrade.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper is the first to investigate context optimization strategies for prior-data fitted networks (PFNs), drawing a direct parallel between in-context learning in LLMs and the conditioning mechanism of PFNs like TabPFN. It adapts soft prompt tuning (Lester et al., 2021) from NLP to the tabular setting, where the model accepts both continuous feature embeddings and categorical label embeddings, and combines this with last-layer retraining (Kirichenko et al., 2022) to extend the number of predictable classes beyond the pretraining limit. The work also introduces the LARGESCALETABLES benchmark, extending the TabZilla suite to datasets with up to 2 million samples and 7200 features, filling a gap in prior tabular evaluation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PFNs represent a promising paradigm for tabular classification that avoids from-scratch training, but their practical adoption has been blocked by hard limits on dataset size, feature count, and class count. TuneTables demonstrates that these limits can be overcome with a lightweight tuning procedure (optimizing <5% of parameters), and the method doubles as a tool for bias mitigation and data interpretability. However, the paper is candid that TuneTables does not simultaneously match GBDTs on both accuracy and runtime, with end-to-end training time exceeding CatBoost by over 7×. Open questions include whether LoRA/QLoRA-style adaptations or sparse mixture-of-experts PFNs can close the efficiency gap, and whether the surprising finding that a distilled prompt can outperform the full real-data context at inference generalizes beyond the current experimental scope.
