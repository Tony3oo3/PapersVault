## Problem

Predicting the evolution of geometric states (e.g., molecular equilibrium structures, catalyst adsorption states) from initial configurations is critical in chemistry and materials science, yet existing approaches face a fundamental trade-off: direct prediction models cannot capture iterative dynamics or leverage trajectory data, machine learning force fields accumulate local errors over long trajectories without preserving joint state distributions, and standard geometric diffusion models generate from a Gaussian prior rather than bridging between two pre-given states. No prior framework simultaneously preserves the joint distribution of initial and target states, respects SE(3) symmetry constraints, and can incorporate trajectory data as fine-grained guidance.

## Input / Output

The method takes an initial geometric state as a set of n atomic coordinates in 3D (processed as CoM-free point clouds with molecular graph connectivity) and optionally a trajectory of N+1 intermediate states. It produces a target geometric state (equilibrium or adsorption coordinates) by simulating the bridge process via a 10-step Euler solver on the probability flow ODE. The initial state R^0 is used as a conditioning input to the score network v_θ(R^t, t; R^0), which is essential for preserving the joint distribution; without this conditioning, performance drops by 6.5–10.3% relative ADwT on OC22.

## Method

GDB constructs an equivariant diffusion bridge by applying a modified Doob's h-transform to a Brownian motion SDE on geometric states, anchoring the process at both the initial state R^0 and target state R^T as fixed endpoints. The h-transform function h_R(z,t;z_0) is defined as an integral of the transition density weighted by the conditional data distribution, ensuring the induced joint distribution q(R^0, R^T) exactly matches q_data(R^{t0}, R^{t1}). For trajectory guidance, a chain of N such bridges is constructed where each bridge models one trajectory segment, with boundary conditions linking consecutive bridges (R_i^0 = R_{i-1}^T), and the training objective extends naturally to N time-dependent loss terms. Training uses a tractable score-matching objective with Gaussian transition kernels (p_R(z',t'|z,t) = N(z, σ²(t'−t)I)), and the score is estimated by an SE(3)-equivariant network (Graph Transformer for molecules, GemNet-OC for catalysts). Theoretically, the paper proves the bridge preserves joint distributions exactly and that the chain of bridges converges to the true underlying dynamics (KL divergence → 0 as N → ∞) under mild assumptions.

## Results

On QM9, GDB achieves a C-RMSD of 0.143 Å (validation) and 0.148 Å (test), representing a ~60% relative reduction over the best baseline GTMGC (0.362/0.367 Å). On Molecule3D (random split), GDB reaches 0.622/0.619 Å C-RMSD, a ~13% improvement over GTMGC (0.712/0.713 Å). On the challenging OC22 structure relaxation task, GDB attains 59.39% average ADwT, surpassing the best MLFF baseline GemNet-OC (57.42%) which was trained on 10× more data (OC20+OC22 vs. OC22-only).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GDB decisively outperforms all direct-prediction baselines (GINE, GATv2, GPS, GTMGC) on both QM9 and Molecule3D, with the largest gap over GTMGC [111] (the strongest prior method) being ~60% relative C-RMSD reduction on QM9 and ~13% on Molecule3D. On OC22 structure relaxation, GDB (59.39% Avg ADwT) surpasses GemNet-OC [34] (57.42%) which was trained on both OC20 and OC22 data (~10× more training data), as well as GemNet-dT [32] (55.46%) and SpinConv [94] (47.49%) trained on OC22-only. Notably, even without trajectory guidance, GDB (58.54% Avg) still beats the best MLFF baseline (57.42%), and adding trajectory guidance further improves to 59.39%. The ablation removing the R⁰ condition causes a 6.5% relative ADwT drop, confirming that coupling preservation is essential. DeeperGCN-DAGNN [116] is only evaluated on Molecule3D and is outperformed by GDB by ~13% C-RMSD on the random split.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends score-based diffusion modeling (Song et al., ICLR 2021) by replacing the standard Gaussian-prior generative process with a diffusion bridge conditioned on both initial and target geometric states, constructed via a modified Doob's h-transform (Rogers & Williams, 2000). It departs from prior geometric diffusion models (e.g., Geodiff, Xu et al. ICLR 2022) that generate conformations from noise, and from direct-prediction equivariant networks (GTMGC, SE(3)-Transformers) that collapse iterative evolution into a single step, by simultaneously preserving the joint state distribution, respecting SE(3) symmetry, and admitting trajectory data as guidance through chains of bridges. The framework is backbone-agnostic, demonstrated with both Graph-Transformer and GemNet-OC architectures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GDB provides the first unified generative framework that provably preserves joint geometric state distributions under symmetry constraints while also leveraging trajectory data, addressing three desiderata that no prior method satisfies simultaneously. The theoretical guarantees (complete coupling preservation, convergence to underlying dynamics) give practitioners confidence that the model captures true evolution physics rather than memorizing patterns. Limitations include reliance on Transformer-based backbones that constrain efficiency at scale, and the framework has not yet been tested with advanced diffusion design strategies (e.g., EDM, flow matching variants) that could further improve performance. Open questions include scaling to very large systems (proteins, materials) and whether the chain-of-bridges approach generalizes to non-Markovian dynamics.
