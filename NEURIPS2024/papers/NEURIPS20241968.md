## Problem

Global polynomial optimization via sum-of-squares (SOS) hierarchies provides rigorous lower bounds and global minimizers for fixed polynomials, but when the objective depends on random parameters ω ~ ν(ω), one needs a lower-bounding *function* c*(ω) valid for all parameter realizations along with parameterized minimizers x*(ω). No quantitative convergence guarantee existed for the stochastic SOS hierarchy, and the resulting SDPs scale prohibitively with the number of variables, limiting practical use to small problems.

## Input / Output

The method takes a polynomial objective f(x,ω) of degree ≤ 2s in joint variables, a probability measure ν(ω) on a compact parameter domain Ω, and optional hard equality constraints fixing certain sensor positions. The primal SDP outputs a polynomial lower-bounding function c_{2s}(ω) of degree ≤ 2s and the optimal expected value p_{2s}* = ∫c_{2s}(ω)dν(ω); the dual SDP outputs a moment vector y from which sensor position means E[x_i] and variances Var[x_i] are extracted as a Gaussian distribution N(E[x], Var[x]) per sensor. The key structural detail is that the output is a *function* of ω (not a single scalar) and the dual moments encode a joint distribution μ(x,ω) whose ω-marginal is constrained to equal ν.

## Method

S-SOS constructs a hierarchy of primal-dual SDPs: the primal maximizes ∫c(ω)dν(ω) subject to f(x,ω)−c(ω) being a sum-of-squares (parameterized by a PSD matrix W), while the dual minimizes ∫f dμ subject to moment-matching constraints that pin the ω-marginal of μ to ν. The paper proves a quantitative ln(s)/s convergence rate for trigonometric polynomials on compact domains by approximating the (generally non-polynomial) optimal lower bound c*(ω) with a degree-s trigonometric polynomial via Jackson's inequality, then applying an integral operator built from Christoffel-Darboux/Jackson kernels to project the residual into the SOS cone. To scale to larger problems, the authors introduce a cluster basis hierarchy that restricts monomial terms to at most b interacting variables with per-variable degree ≤ t, reducing the basis dimension from O(n^{bt}) to O(n^b) and enabling SDP matrices up to 800×800 for 40-variable sensor network localization instances.

## Results

On sensor network localization (SNL) problems, S-SOS achieves Mahalanobis distance δ_M of 0.04±0.02 (1D, 8 hard constraints) to 3.25±1.19 (2D, 15 sensors), consistently outperforming the Monte Carlo baseline (MCPO) by 1–3 orders of magnitude in 2D (e.g., 2.86 vs. 1562.39 for 9 sensors). The cluster basis hierarchy reduces the semidefinite matrix size by 2.5–2.6× in 2D settings (e.g., 406→163, 820→317), enabling S-SOS to handle problems with up to 40 variables and SDP matrices exceeding 800×800. For the simple quadratic test case, the S-SOS hierarchy exhibits exponential convergence to the optimal value p* = π/4 − 2/3 ≈ 0.1187, far exceeding the theoretical ln(s)/s rate.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is MCPO (Monte Carlo Polynomial Optimization), which samples noise realizations ω~ν(ω), solves each pointwise minimization with a local solver (BFGS), and aggregates the resulting sensor positions to estimate E[x] and Var[x]. S-SOS wins on every single problem configuration tested, with the advantage growing dramatically in higher dimensions: in 1D the gap is modest (e.g., 0.04 vs. 0.31 with 8 hard constraints), but in 2D MCPO catastrophically fails, producing δ_M values of ~1500–1850 versus S-SOS's ~3. This suggests MCPO's local solver gets trapped in spurious local minima for the ill-conditioned 2D SNL landscape, while S-SOS's global SDP relaxation avoids this. The cluster basis hierarchy (n-body prior) is essential for making the 2D problems tractable, shrinking the SDP matrix by 2.5–2.6× without degrading solution quality. No other parametric polynomial optimization methods (e.g., polynomial chaos expansion) are directly benchmarked, leaving the comparison limited to this single Monte Carlo baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Lasserre's (2010) 'Joint+Marginal' S-SOS framework for parametric polynomial optimization by providing the first quantitative convergence guarantee (ln s/s rate) for the hierarchy, using the integral-operator and kernel formalism from Bach & Rudi (2023), Fang & Fawzi (2021), and Slot (2022). It also introduces a novel cluster basis hierarchy that borrows n-body priors from condensed matter physics to sparsify the semidefinite program, departing from the standard Lasserre degree-based hierarchy. The convergence proof adapts the 1/s² trigonometric polynomial results to the parametric setting, where the need to approximate the non-polynomial lower-bounding function c*(ω) with a polynomial necessarily degrades the rate.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The quantitative convergence result fills a gap in the S-SOS theory, giving practitioners a concrete rate to reason about when choosing hierarchy degree. The cluster basis hierarchy is a practical sparsification tool that enables S-SOS to handle problems with 40+ variables and 800×800 SDP matrices, though the reduction factor (2.5–2.6× in tested cases) is modest and a full convergence proof for the cluster basis remains open. Key limitations include the restriction to polynomial f and c(ω), the requirement of exact SOS decomposition, and the open problem of scaling to n≫1; relaxing these assumptions or developing approximate solvers that avoid explicit matrix materialization are identified as important future directions.
