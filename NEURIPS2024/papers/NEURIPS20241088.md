## Problem

Existing diffusion-based image restoration methods suffer from two fundamental limitations: they typically assume the degradation operator is fully known (non-blind), and they enforce data consistency by projecting intermediate latents during reverse diffusion, which can push generated images off the data manifold and produce unrealistic outputs. BIRD addresses both issues by jointly estimating the degradation parameters and the restored image at test time while preserving the unaltered reverse sampling trajectory of a pre-trained diffusion model.

## Input / Output

BIRD takes a single degraded RGB image (evaluated at 256×256) together with the known functional form of the degradation operator (e.g., convolution for deblurring, bicubic downsampling for super-resolution, identity for denoising) but not its parameters. It outputs a restored image guaranteed to lie on the data manifold of the pre-trained DDIM, along with the estimated degradation parameters (e.g., a blur kernel). No per-task training or fine-tuning is required; the method is applied zero-shot to any new image and any new degradation instance.

## Method

BIRD casts blind image restoration as a latent optimization problem over the initial noise x_T of a pre-trained DDIM, exploiting the deterministic mapping from x_T to x_0 so that every generated image remains on the data manifold without modifying intermediate latents. At each iteration, the current x_T is mapped through an accelerated DDIM reverse process (DDIMReverse) to produce a candidate clean image x_0, and a restoration loss ‖y − H_η(x_0)‖² is backpropagated to update both x_T and the degradation parameters η via gradient descent, with x_T renormalized to the high-density shell of the standard Gaussian. A key efficiency contribution is leveraging DDIM's ability to skip ahead in the forward diffusion process using large time steps (δt), reducing the number of denoising evaluations per iteration from T=1000 to as few as 10 (with δt=100), cutting runtime from ~5700 s (naive full unrolling) to ~234 s per image while maintaining reconstruction quality.

## Results

On CelebA, BIRD achieves state-of-the-art results across all four blind IR tasks: 23.76 PSNR / 0.263 LPIPS on motion deblurring, 24.67 PSNR / 0.225 LPIPS on Gaussian deblurring, 22.75 PSNR / 0.306 LPIPS on 8× super-resolution, and 28.46 PSNR / 0.227 LPIPS on denoising. On ImageNet, BIRD reaches 22.15 PSNR / 0.354 LPIPS on 4× SR, outperforming all baselines, though BlindDPS edges it on Gaussian deblurring (24.28 vs. 23.76 PSNR). BIRD also runs in 234 s per image with 1.2 GB memory, substantially faster than the 5700 s Naive Inversion baseline and comparable to BlindDPS (270 s) while using far less memory (1.2 GB vs. 6.1 GB).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On CelebA, BIRD outperforms all six baselines on every task it is evaluated on. Against the closest diffusion-based competitor BlindDPS [1], BIRD gains +0.61 dB PSNR on motion deblurring and +1.11 dB on Gaussian deblurring, while also improving LPIPS by 0.018 and 0.032 respectively. Fast Diffusion EM [9] is the strongest baseline on Gaussian deblurring (24.52 PSNR), but BIRD still leads by 0.15 dB and 0.010 LPIPS. On the 8× SR task, BIRD surpasses GDP [6] by 1.97 dB PSNR and DIP [19] by 4.11 dB, confirming a large margin over GAN-inversion and deep-prior methods. On ImageNet 4× SR, BIRD leads BlindDPS by 0.79 dB PSNR and GDP by 1.91 dB. The one exception is ImageNet Gaussian deblurring, where BlindDPS [1] achieves 24.28 PSNR / 0.296 LPIPS versus BIRD's 23.76 / 0.321, suggesting BlindDPS's dedicated score-based kernel estimator has an advantage when the degradation is purely Gaussian on natural images.

## Relation to Prior Work

BIRD builds on the line of diffusion-based image restoration methods (DPS, DDNM, DDRM, GDP, BlindDPS) that use pre-trained diffusion models as learned priors, but departs from them in two key ways: it casts the inverse problem as optimization over only the initial noise (the DDIM latent) rather than projecting onto data consistency at every reverse step, and it exploits DDIM's deterministic step-skipping capability to make the inversion computationally tractable. It also extends the blind IR setting beyond prior methods like BlindDPS (which trains a separate score-based model for the degradation operator) by jointly optimizing degradation parameters at test time without any additional training. The GAN-inversion literature (DGPGAN, etc.) shares the goal of inverting a frozen generative model, but BIRD avoids the fine-tuning or auxiliary encoder networks those methods require.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The core insight—that keeping all intermediate latents fixed and optimizing only the initial noise guarantees manifold membership at every iteration—offers a principled alternative to the projection-based corrections used by DPS and DDNM, and the finding that a strong image prior can compensate for a suboptimal degradation model estimate (outperforming BlindDPS even with a worse kernel) shifts emphasis toward prior quality over degradation-model fidelity. Practical limitations remain: BIRD requires an explicit parametric form for the degradation operator (ruling out deraining, dehazing), inherits biases from the pre-trained diffusion model, and the quantitative gains over strong baselines are moderate (1–3 PSNR). Open questions include whether the latent-optimization formulation extends to non-parametric or partially known degradation operators and whether the DDIM step-skipping trick generalizes to other deterministic samplers.
