## Problem

Neural ODEs are computationally expensive to train for deterministic paired-data tasks because ODE solving is inherently serial, requires many function evaluations, and the adjoint method for backpropagation is numerically unstable. While flow matching enables simulation-free training by directly regressing the dynamics to a target velocity field, applying it directly between paired data induces crossing trajectories that break the per-sample data-label coupling, making it unsuitable for regression and classification where the coupling must be preserved.

## Input / Output

The method accepts either RGB images (MNIST 28×28, SVHN/CIFAR-10 32×32) or tabular feature vectors (UCI regression datasets) as input, and produces either a class label (via argmax over one-hot channels) or a continuous scalar as output. Both the input and the label are projected into a shared latent embedding space by separate learned encoders (f_φ for data, g_φ for labels), and the ODE dynamics operate entirely in this embedding space. At inference, the solved final state is decoded back to the label space by a label decoder d_ψ, so the model's output dimensionality matches the original label structure regardless of the embedding dimension.

## Method

The key insight is to learn the data and label embeddings jointly with the dynamics function using a flow matching loss, so that the predefined (e.g., linear) interpolant z_t = (1−t)z_0 + t z_1 induces non-crossing trajectories in the learned embedding space. A label autoencoding loss (MSE between y and d_ψ(g_φ(y))) regularizes the label encoder to prevent degenerate constant embeddings, and two practical tricks—explicitly sampling t=0 during training and adding Gaussian noise to the label autoencoding target—further stabilize optimization and improve generalization. Training requires only one function evaluation per step (no ODE solving), compared to tens or hundreds for standard NODEs, while inference still uses an ODE solver (Euler or adaptive) to integrate from z_0 to ẑ_1. The paper proves that minimizing the combined flow + autoencoding objective guarantees non-crossing trajectories when the label encoder is injective, and empirically shows that linear dynamics yields the best few-step inference performance since the ideal trajectory is already straight.

## Results

On image classification, the proposed method achieves 99.30% on MNIST, 96.12% on SVHN, and 88.89% on CIFAR-10 with only 1 NFE during training (vs. 43–354 NFE for NODE-based baselines), while matching or exceeding the best baseline accuracy on all three datasets. Training throughput reaches 29.41, 9.48, and 9.00 batch/sec on MNIST, SVHN, and CIFAR-10 respectively, compared to 0.93, 0.42, and 0.34 for standard NODE. In the low-NFE inference regime (1-step Euler), the method achieves 99.33%, 96.16%, and 88.87% accuracy, outperforming all baselines at the same budget. On UCI regression tasks, the method with linear dynamics shows superior RMSE in the 1–2 step Euler regime across all 10 datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Compared to standard NODE [8], the proposed method reduces training NFE from 354/75/91 to 1 on MNIST/SVHN/CIFAR-10 while achieving equal or better final accuracy (99.30% vs. 98.36%, 96.12% vs. 95.09%, 88.89% vs. 86.30%). Against STEER [13] and RNODE [12], which use regularization to reduce NFEs, the method still requires only 1 NFE (vs. 43–130) and outperforms them on SVHN (96.12% vs. 94.55%/95.39%) and CIFAR-10 (88.89% vs. 84.51%/86.08%). Against CARD [16], the closest simulation-free baseline based on diffusion, the method wins on all three datasets even against the longer-trained CARD† variant (99.30% vs. 98.83%, 96.12% vs. 95.23%, 88.89% vs. 86.42%), with the gap most pronounced in the 1-step Euler regime where CARD† drops to 9.95%/10.61% while the proposed method maintains 96.16%/88.87%. The authors attribute the CIFAR-10/SVHN gains partly to accurate gradient computation via direct backpropagation, avoiding the numerical instability of the adjoint method used by NODE-based baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the flow matching framework (Lipman et al., 2023; Liu et al., 2023), originally developed for generative modeling between marginal distributions, to the deterministic paired-data setting of regression and classification. The key departure is identifying that standard flow matching induces crossing trajectories that break per-sample data-label coupling, and proposing a simple fix: learning data and label embeddings jointly with the dynamics function so that the predefined flow is valid in the learned embedding space. Conceptually, the approach parallels the reflow procedure in Rectified Flow but achieves trajectory straightening through learned encoders rather than iterative ODE solving, and shares high-level motivation with Koopman operator theory in seeking linearized dynamics in a learned representation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical path to training NODEs at a fraction of the computational cost (1 NFE per step vs. tens to hundreds), which has been a major barrier to adopting continuous-depth models for standard supervised tasks. The crossing-trajectory analysis is a useful caution for anyone extending flow matching beyond generative settings. Open questions include whether the fixed linear dynamics assumption is truly sufficient for complex mappings, whether learnable per-sample dynamics could adaptively allocate computation, and how the method extends to tasks with structured or hierarchical labels. The lack of error bars on classification experiments (due to compute constraints) limits confidence in the magnitude of reported gains.
