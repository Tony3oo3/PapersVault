## Problem

State-of-the-art uncertainty quantification methods for deep neural networks (Local Ensemble, LLA, SCOD) all require O(kp) memory to store the top-k eigenvectors of the GGN/Fisher matrix, which caps the achievable rank k for large models and degrades uncertainty quality. No existing technique can compute a high-rank low-rank approximation of the GGN within a fixed, small memory budget for architectures with millions of parameters.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240728/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pre-trained network θ*, a training dataset (for GGN-vector products), and a test point x at query time. It outputs a scalar uncertainty score computed as ‖J_{θ*}(x)‖²_F − ‖U_S^T(S J_{θ*}(x)^T)‖²_F, where the sketch is applied to the Jacobian before projection. At query time only the SRFT matrix S (p+s entries) and the sketched orthogonal basis U_S (s×k entries) must be in memory, yielding O(p + s(k+1)) total footprint versus O(pk) for baselines. The score is used directly as an OoD detection signal (higher score = more out-of-distribution).

## Method

SLU combines the Lanczos algorithm with Subsampled Randomized Fourier Transform (SRFT) sketching to compute a low-rank approximation of the GGN at O(k²ε⁻²) memory (up to log terms in p) instead of O(pk). The key theoretical result (Lemma 3.2) proves that sketching and orthogonalization approximately commute: one can sketch each Lanczos vector on the fly with S, then orthogonalize the sketched vectors post-hoc to obtain U_S, without ever materializing the full p×k eigenvector matrix. The uncertainty score uses the M_no eig covariance (projection onto zero-eigenvalue directions), so eigenvalues are discarded and only the sketched orthonormal basis is needed. A preconditioned variant first runs hi-memory Lanczos for k₀ steps to remove the top eigenvalues, improving numerical stability of the subsequent sketched iterations at the cost of k₀·p extra memory.

## Results

The paper evaluates OoD detection performance using AUROC under a fixed memory budget of 3p. SLU consistently outperforms all baselines at larger model scales: on ResNet (p=200K, CIFAR-10→SVHN) it achieves 0.76 AUROC vs. 0.67 for LLA/LE, 0.68 for SCOD, 0.48 for SWAG, and 0.46 for Deep Ensemble. On VisualAttentionNet (p=4M, CelebA→FOOD-101) SLU reaches 0.95 vs. 0.93 (LLA) and 0.94 (SCOD). On SWIN (p=200M, ImageNet hold-out) SLU achieves 0.72 vs. 0.67 (LLA/LE), 0.46 (SWAG), and 0.63 (DE), while SCOD is inapplicable due to memory constraints. The memory footprint of SLU scales as O(k²ε⁻²) rather than O(pk), enabling higher-rank approximations within the same budget.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SLU outperforms LLA, LE, and LE-H across all model scales, with the gap widening as parameter count increases (e.g., +0.09 AUROC on ResNet→SVHN, +0.05 on VAN→FOOD-101, +0.05 on SWIN hold-out). Against SCOD (randomized SVD), SLU wins on ResNet (0.76 vs. 0.68 on SVHN) and SWIN (0.72 vs. inapplicable), while SCOD edges ahead slightly on LeNet (0.84 vs. 0.94 is actually SLU winning; SCOD is 0.84 vs. SLU 0.94). SWAG is consistently the weakest baseline at larger scales (0.48 on ResNet→SVHN, 0.46 on SWIN), and the authors note it is highly sensitive to hyperparameters. Deep Ensemble performs competitively on small models (0.96 on MLP, 0.92 on LeNet) but degrades sharply at scale (0.46 on ResNet→SVHN, 0.63 on SWIN), and becomes inapplicable to ImageNet due to training cost. LLA-D is strong on the smallest model (0.98 on MLP) but collapses on larger architectures (0.55 on ResNet, 0.52 on SWIN), confirming that diagonal approximations lose information as parameter count grows.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Local Ensemble score of Madras et al. (2019) and the Bayesian linearized Laplace framework of Immer et al. (2021), both of which require computing top-k eigenvectors of the GGN/Fisher matrix via hi-memory Lanczos or truncated randomized SVD (SCOD, Sharma et al. 2021), incurring O(pk) memory. The key departure is the integration of SRFT sketching into the Lanczos iteration, enabled by a new theoretical result (Lemma 3.2) showing that sketching and orthogonalization approximately commute. This draws on the randomized numerical linear algebra literature on subspace embeddings (Woodruff et al., 2014; Ailon and Chazelle, 2009) and recent work on sketching Krylov methods (Balabanov and Grigori, 2022; Guttel and Schweitzer, 2023), but is the first to apply such techniques to uncertainty estimation in deep networks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The reduction from O(pk) to O(k²ε⁻²) memory (logarithmic in p) makes low-rank GGN-based uncertainty scores practical for models with hundreds of millions of parameters, where all prior Lanczos/SVD-based methods become infeasible. The empirical finding that sketching noise is outweighed by the ability to use a higher rank k in the low-memory regime is validated across five architectures (15K–200M params) and five datasets, though the AUROC gains over baselines are modest in many settings. A stated limitation is that the sketched data structure supports variance estimation but not posterior sampling, restricting its use in full Bayesian inference. Open questions include whether the approach extends to other GGN-based scores (e.g., M_eig with eigenvalue weighting), whether preconditioning can further improve stability for ill-conditioned matrices, and how the method behaves in the regime where k approaches the intrinsic rank of the GGN.
