## Problem

Existing KV cache quantization methods treat each key or value channel independently (per-channel for keys, per-token for values), which becomes severely sub-optimal at very low bit widths where model quality collapses. The paper identifies that distinct channels within key/value activation embeddings are highly interdependent—their joint entropy grows sub-linearly while the sum of marginal entropies grows linearly—meaning independent quantization wastes representational capacity and prevents practical deployment at extreme compression rates like 1 bit per activation.

## Input / Output

The method takes key and value activation embeddings (before RoPE positional encoding for keys) from each attention head and layer, partitioning the d-dimensional channel space into contiguous non-overlapping groups of c channels. Each group is encoded as a single b-bit index into a learned codebook of 2^b multi-dimensional centroids (each centroid has dimensionality c), so the effective storage is b/c bits per channel. During inference, fused GPU kernels dequantize these shared codes back into full-precision key/value vectors for attention computation, with centroids cached in shared memory per thread block to avoid expensive global-memory random lookups.

## Method

Coupled Quantization (CQ) exploits the high mutual information between adjacent key/value channels by jointly quantizing groups of c contiguous channels with a shared b-bit code, reducing effective bits-per-channel to b/c. Multi-dimensional centroids for each channel group are learned offline via k-means++ (uniform) or Fisher-guided weighted k-means, where the latter uses the diagonal Fisher information matrix to bias centroid placement toward preserving more influential activations. The information-theoretic foundation is that H(X₁,…,Xₙ) < ΣH(Xᵢ) due to non-zero mutual information, so encoding channels together requires fewer total bits than encoding them independently. For efficient inference, the authors design fused GPU kernels that merge dequantization with positional encoding and KQ multiplication (for keys) or attention-score multiplication (for values). Combined with a sliding window of recent tokens stored in FP16, CQ achieves near-native model quality at 1 bit per activation, outperforming KVQuant-2b with half the memory.

## Results

On WikiText-2 perplexity, CQ-8c8b (1 bit per activation) achieves 8.09 on LLaMA-7b and 7.75 on LLaMA-2-7b, compared to 321.58 and NaN for KVQuant-1b at the same bit width. At 2 bits, CQ-4c8b reaches 5.97 (LLaMA-7b) and 5.42 (LLaMA-2-7b), versus 8.17 and 9.75 for KVQuant-2b. CQ-8c8b at 1 bit even outperforms KVQuant-2b at 2 bits with half the memory. Inference throughput improves by 1.4–3.5× over the FP16 baseline, with CQ enabling 3.75×, 7.5×, and 15× larger batch sizes at 4-bit, 2-bit, and 1-bit quantization respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against KVQuant [15], CQ consistently outperforms at every bit width tested. At 1-bit, CQ-8c8b achieves 8.09 perplexity on LLaMA-7b versus 321.58 for KVQuant-1b (a 40× improvement), and CQ-8c8b even beats KVQuant-2b (8.17) while using half the bits. At 2-bit, CQ-4c8b (5.97) outperforms KVQuant-2b (8.17) and is competitive with KVQuant-2b+1% sparse (6.06) despite using fewer bits (2.00 vs 2.32 BPA). Against KIVI [25] on LongBench with LLaMA-2-7b at 2-bit, CQ-4c8b matches or exceeds KIVI-2b on 7 of 8 tasks (e.g., Qasper 9.58 vs 9.26, MultiNews 1.93 vs 0.97), and achieves comparable prefill/decoding latency. Against NormalFloat (NF) quantization from QLoRA [8], CQ-2c8b at 4-bit achieves 5.70 perplexity on LLaMA-7b versus 5.87 for NF4, and at 2-bit CQ-4c8b (5.97) dramatically outperforms NF2 (3210.5), showing that channel coupling is far more effective than per-channel non-uniform quantization at extreme compression.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the KV cache quantization line of work initiated by KIVI (per-channel keys, per-token values) and KVQuant (sensitivity-based dense-and-sparse quantization), both of which treat each channel independently. The key conceptual departure is an information-theoretic observation: joint entropy of coupled channels grows sub-linearly while marginal entropies grow linearly, implying per-channel quantization is fundamentally sub-optimal. The method extends product quantization ideas to the KV cache setting and adapts Fisher-information-guided centroid learning from BRECQ to the multi-channel case, using k-means++ or weighted k-means to learn shared multi-dimensional centroids per channel group.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

KV cache is the dominant memory bottleneck for LLM inference at scale, and this paper demonstrates that a simple architectural change—coupling channels before quantization—unlocks the previously infeasible 1-bit regime with only modest quality degradation (0.3–0.33 perplexity increase with a 128-token FP16 sliding window). The 1.4–3.5× throughput gains and 15× larger batch sizes at 1-bit have direct practical impact on serving cost. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to architectures with different attention patterns (e.g., GQA, MLA), how extreme compression affects safety metrics like hallucination and bias, and whether the calibration requirement (16 sequences of 2048 tokens) limits deployment flexibility.
