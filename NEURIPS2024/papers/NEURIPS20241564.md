## Problem

Existing DETR-based video instance segmentation methods rely on asynchronous designs—either standalone video-level queries or query-sensitive cascade structures—that lose motion information and suffer from polynomially growing bipartite matching complexity as the number of input frames increases. This causes performance degradation on complex, long, or heavily occluded video scenarios, contradicting the expectation that more frames should provide richer motion cues.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241564/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a video clip of T frames (shorter side resized to 360 px for ResNet-50 or 448 px for Swin-L) and produces per-frame instance segmentation masks, per-instance class predictions, and video-level instance trajectories. Internally, it maintains two parallel sets of N=100 learnable embeddings—frame-level (replicated per frame) and video-level (shared across all frames)—which are jointly refined through the transformer decoder before being fed to separate mask and classification heads. During training, the video is further partitioned into sub-clips of size T_s (3 for YouTube-VIS 2019/2021, 2 for OVIS and YouTube-VIS 2022) to bound the bipartite matching cost.

## Method

SyncVIS introduces two key modules built on a Mask2Former-style transformer decoder. First, the synchronized video-frame modeling paradigm replaces the cascade structure with a bidirectional parallel interaction: in every decoder layer, frame-level and video-level embeddings exchange information via cross-attention restricted to the top-N_k=10 most confident key/value embeddings, with a small update momentum (λ=0.05) to prevent either level from being overwhelmed. Second, the synchronized embedding optimization strategy applies a divide-and-conquer approach to the video-level bipartite matching loss, computing it independently on overlapping sub-clips of T_s frames so that occluded or ambiguous instances can be resolved locally without the combinatorial explosion of matching over the full sequence. The overall training objective combines frame- and video-level cross-entropy, binary cross-entropy, and Dice losses, plus a contrastive loss in the online setting, and the model is pre-trained on COCO before fine-tuning on each VIS benchmark.

## Results

SyncVIS achieves state-of-the-art results across all four benchmarks. On YouTube-VIS 2019, it reaches 57.9% AP (online, ResNet-50) and 67.1% AP (offline, Swin-L), surpassing the previous best CTVIS by 2.8 and 1.5 points respectively. On YouTube-VIS 2021, SyncVIS attains 51.9% AP (ResNet-50) and 62.4% AP (Swin-L). On the more challenging YouTube-VIS 2022 long-video split, it achieves 47.0% AP (Swin-L), and on OVIS it reaches 36.3% AP (ResNet-50) and 50.8% AP (Swin-L), outperforming DVIS by 2.2 and 0.9 points respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SyncVIS outperforms all 15 baselines across every setting. Among online methods on YouTube-VIS 2019 (ResNet-50), it beats the strongest prior method CTVIS [37] by 2.8 AP (57.9 vs. 55.1) and DVIS [38] by 6.7 AP (57.9 vs. 51.2), while older methods like CrossVIS [35] (36.3) and MaskTrack R-CNN [34] (38.6) trail by over 19 AP. Among offline methods with Swin-L on YouTube-VIS 2019, SyncVIS leads CTVIS [37] by 1.5 AP (67.1 vs. 65.6) and DVIS [38] by 3.2 AP (67.1 vs. 63.9); earlier offline approaches such as SeqFormer [31] (59.3), Mask2Former-VIS [6] (60.4), and VITA [15] (63.0) are all surpassed by 2.7–7.8 AP. On YouTube-VIS 2021, SyncVIS extends its lead over CTVIS by 1.8 AP (online) and 1.2 AP (offline). On the harder YouTube-VIS 2022 long-video split (Swin-L), it edges out DVIS [38] by 1.1 AP (47.0 vs. 45.9) and VITA [15] by 5.9 AP. On OVIS, SyncVIS surpasses DVIS [38] by 2.2 AP (ResNet-50) and 0.9 AP (Swin-L), and CTVIS [37] by 3.9 AP (Swin-L), confirming consistent gains even in heavily occluded scenarios.

## Relation to Prior Work

SyncVIS builds directly on the DETR-style Mask2Former-VIS framework and extends both the online (CTVIS) and offline (VITA) variants by introducing a synchronized video-frame modeling paradigm. It departs from the prevailing asynchronous cascade design used by SeqFormer, VITA, and DVIS, where frame-level and video-level queries are decoupled and interact only unidirectionally, arguing that this structure causes error accumulation and polynomially growing bipartite matching complexity. The paper's two key contributions—a bidirectional cross-attention synchronization between frame and video embeddings in each decoder layer, and a divide-and-conquer sub-clip optimization strategy for video-level matching—are orthogonal to the backbone and can be grafted onto multiple existing VIS architectures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The synchronized modeling paradigm is demonstrated to be plug-and-play, yielding consistent gains (1.5–7.0 AP) when added to six different VIS methods, making it a broadly applicable design choice for the field. The counter-intuitive finding that Mask2Former-VIS degrades with more input frames (T=2 is optimal) provides a concrete diagnostic for why prior asynchronous designs struggle with complex videos, and the sub-clip optimization strategy offers a practical remedy for the polynomial matching complexity that has limited offline VIS. However, the authors acknowledge that extremely crowded or heavily occluded scenes remain challenging, and the approach's scalability to very long videos (beyond the ~40-frame regime tested) is not fully explored.
