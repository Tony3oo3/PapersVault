## Problem

Transformers suffer from quadratic computational complexity and weak length extrapolation, making them inefficient for long-sequence modeling, while existing sub-quadratic alternatives (linear attention, state space models) empirically underperform Transformers in pretraining efficiency and downstream accuracy. The predecessor MEGA architecture, which combines exponential moving average with gated attention, still exhibits limited expressiveness in its EMA sub-layer, task-specific architectural divergences, and no demonstrated scalability to large-scale (7B+) pretraining.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242293/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For LLM pretraining, MEGALODON-7B consumes token sequences with a 32K pretraining context (4 chunks of 4096) and a 32K vocabulary, producing next-token predictions; at inference it extrapolates to sequences of 2M+ tokens without architectural modification. For multimodal tasks, it ingests raw 16000-sample speech waveforms (no MFCC preprocessing) and flattened image pixel sequences, outputting class labels. The chunk-wise attention mechanism means the model processes sequences in fixed-size blocks, with only the CEMA hidden state and cumulative normalization statistics communicated across chunk boundaries.

## Method

MEGALODON builds on MEGA's gated attention with four key modifications: (1) Complex Exponential Moving Average (CEMA) extends the real-valued damped EMA to the complex domain, parameterizing h uniformly-spaced angles θ_{j,k} = 2πkω_j/h to capture oscillatory dynamics while preserving the decaying kernel structure; (2) Timestep Normalization generalizes Group Normalization to the autoregressive setting by computing cumulative mean and variance over all past timesteps, avoiding future-information leakage; (3) Normalized Attention computes Q and K from the L2-normalized shared representation Z′ rather than the raw projection, removing the need for a learned temperature and SiLU non-linearity; (4) Pre-norm with two-hop residual re-routes the FFN residual to the block input X (skipping the attention output), eliminating the update gate and preventing variance growth in deep stacks. The architecture achieves linear O(nc) complexity via chunk-wise attention and supports a fourth parallelism dimension (chunk parallelism) orthogonal to data, tensor, and pipeline parallelism for distributed long-context training.

## Results

MEGALODON-7B, pretrained on the same 2T tokens as LLAMA2, achieves a training loss of 1.70, landing between LLAMA2-7B (1.75) and LLAMA2-13B (1.67). On standard academic benchmarks, MEGALODON-7B surpasses LLAMA2-7B on all 10 tasks (e.g., MMLU 49.8 vs. 45.3, ARC-c 53.1 vs. 45.9) and is competitive with LLAMA2-13B. On long-context evaluation, perplexity decreases monotonically up to 2M tokens, and MEGALODON achieves the best NarrativeQA F1 (23.9) among 7B-scale models. Computationally, MEGALODON-7B is ~32% faster than LLAMA2-7B at 32K context length while being only ~6% slower at 4K.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary baseline LLAMA2-7B (same 2T tokens, same tokenizer), MEGALODON-7B wins on all 10 academic benchmarks, with the largest gaps on ARC-c (+7.2) and MMLU (+4.5), and ties on NQ (25.7). Compared to the larger LLAMA2-13B, MEGALODON-7B is competitive, winning on PIQA (80.1 vs. 80.5 is a near-tie), ARC-c (53.1 vs. 49.4), and TQA (60.5 vs. 65.1 is a loss), but trailing on MMLU (49.8 vs. 54.8) and BoolQ (80.5 vs. 81.7). Among other 7B-scale non-Transformer models (Mamba-3B, RWKV-7B, MPT-7B), MEGALODON dominates across nearly all reported benchmarks. On long-context Scrolls QA, MEGALODON achieves the best NarrativeQA F1 (23.9) and is within 0.3 of LLAMA2-L on Qasper (28.0 vs. 28.3), while outperforming XGen, MPT, and YaRN on all three tasks. On medium-scale benchmarks, MEGALODON outperforms MEGA on ImageNet-1K (83.1% vs. 82.3%), PG-19 test PPL (25.4 vs. 36.4 for MEGABYTE, 26.5 for Block-Recurrent Transformer), WikiText-103 (17.23 vs. 18.07), LRA average (88.63 vs. 88.21), and Speech Commands (98.14% vs. 97.50% for S4). Mistral-7B and Gemma-8B score higher on Table 1, but the authors note these were pretrained on substantially more data, making direct comparison unfair.

## Relation to Prior Work

MEGALODON is a direct architectural extension of MEGA (Ma et al., 2023), which first combined gated attention with a multi-dimensional damped exponential moving average to achieve linear-time sequence modeling. The key departures are: (1) extending the EMA state to the complex domain (CEMA), inspired by S4's complex diagonal parameterization; (2) generalizing Group Normalization to the autoregressive setting via cumulative statistics (Timestep Normalization); (3) adopting normalized (cosine-style) attention for training stability; and (4) introducing a two-hop residual configuration to replace MEGA's learned update gate. The paper also adds a fourth parallelism dimension (chunk/sequence parallelism) orthogonal to data, tensor, and pipeline parallelism, enabling efficient long-context distributed training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is one of the first controlled, head-to-head demonstrations that a non-Transformer architecture can match a 1.5× larger Transformer (LLAMA2-13B) at the same 7B parameter scale and 2T-token budget, while offering 32% faster training at 32K context and monotonically decreasing perplexity up to 2M tokens. The practical significance is high: it provides a concrete, open-source recipe for unlimited-context LLM pretraining with linear memory. Open questions include whether the advantage persists at 70B+ scale, whether the architecture generalizes to multi-modal pretraining (acknowledged as future work), and whether the early-training convergence gap (first ~500B tokens) can be eliminated. The single-run, single-scale evaluation limits confidence in the generality of the results.
