## Problem

Traditional causal discovery methods require high-quality measured variables typically provided by human experts, but in many real-world applications (e.g., analyzing product reviews, medical diagnosis from clinical notes), the relevant high-level causal variables are not directly observable. This paper asks how LLMs can reliably assist in revealing causal mechanisms from unstructured data, addressing the longstanding gap between the need for well-defined variables and the availability of only raw, unstructured observations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243248/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

COAT takes as input a dataset of paired samples (x_i, y_i) where x_i is an unstructured observation (text review, MRI image, clinical note, or time-series measurement) and y_i is a scalar target variable (e.g., star rating, tumor type, SST change). It produces a set of high-level causal factors defined in natural language with explicit annotation criteria, their parsed structured values forming a matrix Z, and a causal graph (via FCI) over Z ∪ {Y} that constitutes a Markov blanket of Y. The factor values are typically discrete (e.g., {-1, 0, 1}) and the framework supports both LLM-annotated factors and externally measured explicit factors.

## Method

COAT is an iterative framework that alternates between LLM-based factor proposal/parsing and constraint-based causal discovery (FCI). In each round, an LLM proposes candidate high-level factors with definitions and annotation guidelines from a subset of unstructured observations; a second LLM parses the full dataset into structured values following those guidelines; FCI then recovers the causal structure among all factors identified so far. The key mechanism is causal feedback: samples where Y is poorly explained by the current factor set (identified via K-Means clustering on the structured representation, selecting the cluster with largest conditional entropy) are fed back to the LLM to propose additional factors. The paper proves that this feedback provably reduces the residual conditional mutual information I(Y; X | h_{≤t}(X)), and that with positive perception and capacity scores, the Markov blanket is recovered at an exponential rate (Proposition 2.5).

## Results

On the AppleGastronome benchmark, COAT achieves a causal relation F1 of 0.89 with GPT-4o and 0.97 with Mistral-Medium, substantially outperforming the PAIRWISE baseline (0.62 and 0.42 respectively). For factor proposal, COAT with Mistral-Medium reaches F1=0.96 (precision 1.00, recall 0.93) versus 0.79 for DATA+CoT, while with GPT-4 it achieves F1=0.85 with zero irrelevant factors (OT=0.00). On the Neuropathic benchmark, COAT consistently achieves F1≥0.77 across all LLMs, with the weakest model (LLaMA2-7B) still reaching F1=0.86 versus 0.12 for DATA+CoT, demonstrating that the causal feedback loop compensates for weaker LLM capabilities.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is PAIRWISE (Kiciman et al., 2023), which prompts LLMs to directly reason about causal direction for each pair of discovered variables. COAT wins decisively across all 10 LLMs tested: the F1 gap ranges from +0.07 (GPT-3.5: 0.68 vs 0.55) to +0.55 (Mistral-Medium: 0.97 vs 0.42), with the largest gains coming from weaker models where direct LLM reasoning is most unreliable. For factor proposal, COAT is compared against three ablations: META (zero-shot), DATA (one round with samples), and DATA+CoT (one round with chain-of-thought). COAT consistently achieves the highest precision (often 1.00) by filtering out irrelevant factors through causal feedback, while DATA+CoT tends to over-propose (e.g., GPT-3.5 DATA+CoT proposes 5 MB factors but also 1 NMB and 1.33 OT factors, versus COAT's 3.67 MB with 0 NMB and 0 OT). Notably, even the weakest backbone (LLaMA2-7B) with COAT outperforms stronger models (GPT-4, Mistral-Medium) using DATA+CoT on the Neuropathic benchmark, confirming that the iterative causal feedback mechanism is the key driver of performance rather than raw LLM capability.

## Relation to Prior Work

COAT builds on constraint-based causal discovery (FCI [27]) as its structural inference engine, but departs fundamentally from prior LLM+causality work (Kiciman et al. [11], Long et al. [95], Ban et al. [96]) that treats LLMs as causal reasoners over already-structured variables. Instead, COAT repositions LLMs as *representation assistants* that propose high-level factors from unstructured observations, while the CD algorithm handles structure learning with formal guarantees. This also connects to causal representation learning [6], which seeks latent causal variables from low-level observations but typically requires auxiliary information, sparsity, or interventional data; COAT substitutes LLM world knowledge for these conditions, enabling the approach on purely observational unstructured data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

COAT opens a pathway to apply causal discovery to domains where high-level variables are not pre-defined by experts—customer reviews, clinical notes, climate data—by leveraging LLMs to bridge the unstructured-to-structured gap. The theoretical convergence guarantees (Prop. 2.4–2.5) and the provable feedback mechanism (Prop. 2.2) provide a principled foundation that prior LLM+CD heuristics lack. Key limitations remain: the framework targets a single variable's Markov blanket rather than complete causal graphs, requires faithfulness, and depends on LLM annotation quality. Open questions include scaling to multi-target discovery, reducing reliance on specific LLM capabilities, and validating discovered causal hypotheses in downstream interventions (as suggested by the ENSO case study).
