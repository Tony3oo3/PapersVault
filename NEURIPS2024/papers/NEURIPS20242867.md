## Problem

Video scene graph generation (VidSGG) for drone-captured aerial videos remains unaddressed: no dedicated dataset exists, and existing temporal modeling approaches (progressive, batch-progressive, hierarchical) either process frames independently, suffer from diminishing influence over long sequences, or require offline analysis of the entire video before graph construction. This limits the ability to capture periodic, overlapping, and long-range object interactions in high-density aerial scenes.

## Input / Output

The method consumes aerial drone video sequences at 3840×2160 resolution (sourced from ERA and MAVREC), with keyframes annotated at 5 FPS. A frozen DINO detector (ResNet-50 backbone, 1500 queries) extracts object features per frame. The output is a per-frame dynamic scene graph represented as an N×N×|C_p| relation matrix, where each entry encodes the probability of a predicate (from 384 total: 135 positions + 249 relations) between a subject-object pair, forming triplets ⟨s, o, p⟩ that evolve across the video.

## Method

CYCLO combines a Spatial Attention Graph with a Cyclic Temporal Graph Transformer. The spatial component uses a DETR decoder to build bidirectional object-query connections, concatenating query-key vectors across layers with learned gating to produce a per-frame relation matrix. The temporal component introduces cyclic attention (CA), which replaces standard self-attention with a modular-indexed summation over all T frames (K_{η(t+i) mod T}), creating a ring topology where no temporal edge is treated as a boundary. This makes CA non-permutation-equivariant—preserving chronological order—unlike standard scaled dot-product attention. Multi-head cyclic attention blocks refine object representations by integrating features from adjacent frames in a circular manner, then layer normalization and skip connections stabilize the updated scene graph. Training uses a multi-label margin loss for predicate classification plus cross-entropy for object detection, with the DINO detector kept frozen throughout.

## Results

On the proposed AeroEye dataset, CYCLO achieves 56.20% Recall@20 on PredCls, 54.15% on SGCls, and 43.53% on SGDet, outperforming all baselines across all three tasks and recall thresholds. On the PVSG benchmark, CYCLO reaches 5.83% R@20 (1.98% mR@20), surpassing HIG (4.60/1.89) and Transformer (4.02/1.75). On ASPIRe, CYCLO achieves 13.71% R@20 for position and 15.29% for relation, exceeding HIG by 0.69% and 5.03% respectively at R@20.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CYCLO consistently outperforms both baselines across all three evaluation datasets. Against HIG (Nguyen et al., CVPR 2024), CYCLO gains 2.02% on PredCls R@20 (56.20 vs 54.18), 2.12% on SGCls R@20 (54.15 vs 52.03), and a substantial 6.25% on SGDet R@20 (43.53 vs 37.28) on AeroEye; on PVSG it leads by 1.23% at R@20 (5.83 vs 4.60); and on ASPIRe it surpasses HIG by 0.69% in position and 5.03% in relation at R@20. Against the spatio-temporal Transformer (Cong et al., ICCV 2021), CYCLO improves by 2.95% on PredCls R@20, 3.03% on SGCls R@20, and 2.44% on SGDet R@20 on AeroEye, and by 1.81% at R@20 on PVSG (5.83 vs 4.02). CYCLO also exhibits notably lower standard deviation than HIG across all metrics, indicating more stable and robust predictions. The largest relative gains appear in the SGDet task on AeroEye, where the cyclic temporal modeling most effectively corrects spurious relationships by leveraging cross-frame context.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Video Scene Graph Generation (VidSGG) to the aerial/drone video domain, a setting absent from prior benchmarks like PVSG and ASPIRe. Methodologically, CYCLO departs from the two dominant temporal modeling paradigms in VidSGG—linear temporal transformers (CSTTran) and hierarchical interlacement graphs (HIG)—by introducing cyclic attention that treats the frame sequence as a ring, eliminating boundary effects and preserving chronological order without positional encodings. The spatial graph construction repurposes the DETR/DINO decoder's self-attention to extract inter-object relational features, following the EGTR line of work, while the annotation pipeline leverages GPT4RoI for predicate generation, a relatively novel use of LLMs in dataset curation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AeroEye is the first VidSGG benchmark for drone-captured videos, filling a gap for applications in surveillance, disaster response, and precision agriculture where object density and tiny-object scale differ substantially from ground-level footage. The cyclic attention mechanism offers a principled alternative to linear or hierarchical temporal modeling, though its reliance on video continuity means it degrades on discontinuous or incomplete sequences—a limitation the authors acknowledge but do not resolve. Open questions include whether cyclic attention generalizes to variable-length or streaming video, how it interacts with longer context windows, and whether the modest gains over HIG (1–2% on most metrics) persist at larger scale or with stronger backbones.
