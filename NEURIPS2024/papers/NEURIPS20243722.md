## Problem

Existing domain adaptation methods for super-resolution require large numbers of target domain LR images to train GAN-based alignment, making them inapplicable when users provide only a single LR image. While one/zero-shot domain adaptation has been explored for image generation, segmentation, and detection using text-guided CLIP direction vectors, it has not been applied to SR, where text descriptions lack the pixel-level granularity needed to capture texture-detail domain differences and a single target image severely limits distribution diversity, risking pattern collapse.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a single unlabeled target domain LR image (no corresponding HR label) together with a batch of source domain LR images and their SR outputs from the frozen pre-trained source network. It produces a domain-adapted SR network (initialized from the source network) that can then super-resolve any target domain LR image at inference time. The adaptation training takes roughly 9–15 minutes on a single GPU depending on architecture, and the target domain HR images are used only for post-hoc evaluation, never during training.

## Method

IODA replaces the text-level cross-domain direction vector used in prior one/zero-shot adaptation with a pixel-level image-guided direction vector: it computes ΔLR between source and target LR images and ΔSR between source-network SR and target-network SR outputs in CLIP image-encoder space, then enforces their collinearity via a cosine-similarity loss (L_direction) to guide the target network. To combat the limited diversity of a single target image, the method introduces an instance-guided distribution expansion strategy that uses SAM to segment instances in the target LR image, feeds the resulting instance masks as region-range masks into Alpha-CLIP to generate instance-specific spatial features, and combines this with random occlusion masking (inspired by MAE) to further diversify the target domain feature distribution. The source domain images use full-image masks since their abundance obviates the need for instance-level focusing. The approach is plug-and-play, requiring no architectural modification to the underlying SR network, and has been validated across SAFMN, HAT, and SRFormer backbones on multiple source–target dataset pairs.

## Results

On the RealSR_Canon real-world dataset, HAT+IODA achieves the best PSNR of 27.82 dB and SSIM of 0.812, outperforming the standalone HAT baseline (27.48 dB, 0.804 SSIM) and all other compared methods. SRFormer+IODA reaches 27.66 dB / 0.810 SSIM, and SAFMN+IODA reaches 27.46 dB / 0.790 SSIM. Domain adaptation training takes only 9–15 minutes per network, compared to 641 minutes for ZSSR, while text-guided methods (Style-GAN-NADA, P0DA) prove entirely ineffective for the SR task.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IODA consistently outperforms all tested baselines on RealSR_Canon. Against domain-adaptive SR methods, HAT+IODA (27.82 dB) surpasses DASR (27.40 dB) by 0.42 dB and DASR-Domain (26.23 dB) by 1.59 dB; the one-shot variant of DASR collapses to 23.22 dB, far below IODA. Against test-time adaptation, IODA beats ZSSR (26.01 dB) by 1.81 dB while being ~70× faster (9 min vs. 641 min). Against blind SR networks, IODA outperforms BSRGAN (26.91 dB), Real-ESRGAN (26.14 dB), and SwinIR (26.64 dB) by 0.55–1.68 dB. Against the standalone SR backbones, adding IODA improves HAT from 27.48 to 27.82 dB (+0.34), SRFormer from 25.95 to 27.66 dB (+1.71), and SAFMN from 24.82 to 27.46 dB (+2.64). Text-guided domain adaptation methods (Style-GAN-NADA, P0DA) are entirely ineffective for SR, confirming the paper's motivation for pixel-level image-guided adaptation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the cross-domain direction vector alignment paradigm from text-guided one/zero-shot domain adaptation (StyleGAN-NADA, P0DA) for super-resolution, replacing text-level domain difference representation with pixel-level image representation computed via CLIP/Alpha-CLIP encoders. It further introduces an instance-guided distribution expansion strategy that combines SAM-based instance segmentation with Alpha-CLIP's region-range masking to overcome the limited diversity inherent in a single target domain image. The method is positioned as the first one-shot domain adaptation approach for SR, departing from prior SR domain adaptation methods (DASR, DADA) that require large numbers of target domain samples, and from prior one-shot adaptation methods that rely on text prompts unsuitable for texture-critical SR tasks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

IODA addresses a practically important gap: real-world SR users often provide only a single degraded image, yet existing domain adaptation SR methods require many target domain samples. The plug-and-play design across architectures (SAFMN, HAT, SRFormer) and datasets makes it broadly applicable. However, the >10-minute adaptation training time remains a significant barrier for single-photo use cases, and the method still requires access to source domain LR images during adaptation. Open questions include whether adaptation speed can be reduced (e.g., via parameter selection or meta-learning), whether zero-shot variants are feasible, and whether the approach extends to video SR or other restoration tasks.
