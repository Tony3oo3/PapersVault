## Problem

While it is well-known that the dual iterates of Augmented Lagrangian Methods (ALMs) applied to semidefinite programs converge linearly under quadratic growth, the convergence rate of the primal iterates has remained an open question. This asymmetry is unsatisfying given the duality structure of SDPs, and prior results required either uniqueness of the solution or a much stronger Lipschitz condition on the Lagrangian that can easily fail for general conic programs.

## Input / Output

The method takes standard SDP data (cost matrix C ∈ S^n, constraint vector b ∈ R^m, linear map A defined by matrices A_1,...,A_m ∈ S^n) along with initial dual points (y_0, Z_0) and a bounded penalty parameter sequence r_k ↑ r_∞. It produces primal iterates {X_k} and dual iterates {y_k, Z_k} that both converge linearly to their respective optimal solution sets, with the inexactness in solving the subproblem (15a) controlled by summable criteria (A') and (B'). The results hold for both the primal and dual formulations of the SDP, and extend to LPs and SOCPs as special cases.

## Method

The paper establishes quadratic growth and error bound properties for both primal and dual SDPs under the standard assumption of strict complementarity and a bounded solution set, valid over any compact set (not just a small unknown neighborhood). The key technical tool is a new characterization of the preimage of the subdifferential of exact penalty functions for S_+^n, revealing that (∂l)^{-1}(-Z̄) equals the normal cone N_{(S_+^n)^∘}(-Z̄) when the penalty parameter ρ > tr(Z̄), which unifies the treatment of indicator and exact penalty functions. Leveraging this characterization together with bounded linear regularity (guaranteed by strict complementarity), the authors derive growth inequalities that simultaneously control suboptimality, affine feasibility, and conic feasibility. These error bounds then yield linear convergence of both primal and dual ALM iterates via the ALM–PPM equivalence, resolving the open question about primal linear convergence under only strict complementarity.

## Results

The paper's main contributions are theoretical: Theorem 1 establishes quadratic growth and error bound for primal SDPs under strict complementarity over any compact set (extending prior work that only guaranteed local neighborhoods), and Theorem 2 provides the analogous dual result. Theorem 3 is the headline result: both primal and dual iterates of the inexact ALM converge linearly under only strict complementarity and a bounded primal solution set, resolving an open question about primal iterate convergence. Numerically, on Max-Cut (20×20 submatrices) and linear SVM (10×100) instances, all KKT residuals (ε₃ = max{η₁,…,η₅}) and primal/dual cost gaps converge linearly to at least 10⁻⁵ accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical paper without direct algorithmic baselines; the 'comparison' is against prior convergence guarantees. Compared to [27] (Cui et al.), which established quadratic growth only in a small (unknown-size) neighborhood around an optimal solution, Theorems 1–2 hold over any compact set and any sublevel set. Compared to [29,30], which required the exact penalty parameter ρ ≥ 2·sup tr(Z*), this paper only needs ρ > tr(Z̄*) for a single strictly complementary solution. Most critically, compared to [23,26] which guaranteed only dual iterate linear convergence (via the ALM–PPM equivalence), Theorem 3 extends linear convergence to the primal iterates as well, requiring only strict complementarity rather than the much stronger Lipschitz condition on the augmented Lagrangian that [23, Theorem 5] demanded. The paper notes that the Lipschitz property in [23] 'can easily fail for general conic programs,' making the new result strictly more general.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the foundational ALM–PPM equivalence of Rockafellar [23] and the inexact PPM convergence framework of Rockafellar [42] and Luque [25], extending the strict-complementarity-based error bound machinery of Ding & Udell [28] and Cui et al. [27] from a small (often unknown) neighborhood to any compact set containing an optimal solution. The key departure is establishing a new characterization of the preimage of the subdifferential of exact penalty functions (Lemma 3), which unifies the indicator-function and penalty-function analyses and yields quadratic growth for both primal and dual SDPs simultaneously. Prior ALM convergence results (Cui et al. [26], Cui et al. [27]) guaranteed only dual-iterate and KKT-residual linear convergence; the primal-iterate rate was explicitly flagged as open in [21, 26].

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result resolves a long-standing open question by showing that the standard strict-complementarity assumption (which is generic for conic programs) is sufficient for linear convergence of both primal and dual ALM iterates, without the stronger Lipschitz-continuity condition on the augmented Lagrangian required by earlier work [23, Theorem 5]. This provides a clean theoretical justification for the strong empirical performance of ALM-based SDP solvers such as SDPNAL+ [19]. Remaining open questions include the observed oscillation/flattening behavior in the numerical tail (attributed to subproblem solver inaccuracy) and whether analogous results extend to non-self-dual cones or non-convex settings.
