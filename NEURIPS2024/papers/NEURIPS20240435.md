## Problem

Transformer-based 3D object detectors are limited by quadratic computational complexity, forcing them to restrict feature interaction to small groups (e.g., 256 voxels) and thus failing to capture long-range spatial relationships. While linear RNNs (Mamba, RWKV, RetNet) offer linear complexity for long-range modeling, directly applying them to sparse 3D point clouds is non-trivial because flattening voxel features into 1D sequences destroys local 3D spatial structure.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240435/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes single-frame or multi-frame LiDAR point clouds, which are voxelized at dataset-specific resolutions (e.g., 0.32m×0.32m×0.1875m on Waymo, 0.3m×0.3m×0.25m on nuScenes) and partitioned into non-overlapping 3D windows of shape (Tx, Ty, Tz) with equal-size groups K up to 4096 voxels. It outputs 3D bounding boxes with class labels and heading angles via a center-based detection head, evaluated with mAP/mAPH (Waymo), NDS/mAP (nuScenes), or mAP (Argoverse V2, ONCE, KITTI).

## Method

LION is a hierarchical 3D backbone built from N LION blocks, each containing two linear group RNN operators (one for X-axis window partition, one for Y-axis) that enable long-range feature interaction over groups of thousands of voxels at linear cost. A 3D spatial feature descriptor (3D submanifold convolution + LayerNorm + GELU) is inserted before voxel merging to recover local spatial information lost during 1D flattening. A voxel generation strategy selects top-m foreground voxels by channel-averaged feature response, diffuses them with four diagonal offsets, initializes their features to zeros, and leverages the auto-regressive property of the subsequent LION block to synthesize meaningful features for the diffused positions. The hierarchical structure uses voxel merging (down-sampling) and voxel expanding (up-sampling) with index mappings to handle the irregular sparse format, producing multi-scale features fed into a BEV backbone and detection head.

## Results

On the Waymo Open Dataset validation set, LION-Mamba-L achieves 75.9 mAP / 74.0 mAPH (L2), outperforming the previous SOTA DSVT-Voxel (74.0/72.1) by 1.9 mAPH/L2 and HEDNet (75.3/73.4) by 0.6 mAPH/L2. On nuScenes test, LION-Mamba reaches 73.9 NDS, surpassing DSVT (72.7) by 1.2 NDS. On Argoverse V2, LION-Mamba achieves 41.5 mAP, beating SAFDNet (39.7) by 1.8 mAP. On ONCE, LION-Mamba reaches 66.6 mAP, outperforming CenterPoint (60.1) by 6.5 mAP.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LION outperforms all transformer-based 3D backbones (DSVT-Voxel, DSVT-Pillar, SST, SWFormer, OcTr) on Waymo, with LION-Mamba-L leading DSVT-Voxel by 1.9 mAPH/L2 (74.0 vs. 72.1). Against the strongest sparse-convolution baselines, LION-Mamba-L surpasses HEDNet by 0.6 mAPH/L2 and SEED by 1.2 mAPH/L2, while LION-Mamba (base) already exceeds HEDNet by 0.2 mAPH/L2. On nuScenes test, LION-Mamba beats DSVT by 1.2 NDS and 1.4 mAP, and HEDNet by 1.9 NDS. On Argoverse V2 (long-range, 200 m), all three LION variants (RetNet 40.7, RWKV 41.1, Mamba 41.5 mAP) surpass the previous SOTA SAFDNet (39.7 mAP) by 1.0–1.8 mAP. On KITTI, LION-RWKV achieves 73.4 mAP, outperforming DSVT-Voxel (70.8) by 2.6 mAP, demonstrating the framework's generalization across five different linear RNN operators (Mamba, RWKV, RetNet, xLSTM, TTT).

## Relation to Prior Work

LION sits at the intersection of two research threads: (1) transformer-based 3D detection backbones (SST, SWFormer, DSVT, FlatFormer) that partition voxels into small windows for local attention but are capped at small group sizes by quadratic complexity, and (2) linear RNN operators from the LLM literature (Mamba, RWKV, RetNet) that model long sequences at linear cost. The paper's core departure is replacing the attention operator inside these window-based 3D backbones with a pluggable linear group RNN, enabling group sizes 16–64× larger than prior transformer methods. It further introduces a 3D spatial feature descriptor (submanifold conv + LayerNorm + GELU) to patch the local-spatial blind spot of 1D sequential processing, and a zero-initialized voxel generation step that exploits the auto-regressive nature of linear RNNs to densify foreground features—both absent from prior 3D detection work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that a single window-based framework can host five different linear RNN operators (Mamba, RWKV, RetNet, xLSTM, TTT) and achieve SOTA on Waymo, nuScenes, Argoverse V2, and ONCE with fewer FLOPs than DSVT-Voxel, LION provides a practical template for the 3D detection community to adopt whichever linear sequence model is next. The inference-time robustness to window/group-size changes (Table 13) hints at reduced sensitivity to hand-crafted priors. However, the higher inference latency (146 ms vs. 136 ms for DSVT) and the reliance on a fixed 4-block hierarchy limit immediate deployment; open questions include whether the framework scales to even larger group sizes without the memory-capacity degradation observed at 8192, and whether the voxel-generation ratio and spatial descriptor can be made adaptive per scene density.
