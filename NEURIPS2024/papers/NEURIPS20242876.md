## Problem

Instruction tuning data for LLMs is typically sourced from human crowd-sourcing (limited in scale) or GPT-4 distillation (biased, low-diversity, hallucination-prone). The paper argues that the pre-training web corpus already contains vast amounts of naturally existing, high-quality instruction-response pairs across math, science, and engineering, but these are highly dispersed and difficult to discover at scale without costly annotation or proprietary model distillation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242876/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The data pipeline consumes raw HTML documents from Common Crawl (18M recalled documents, ~40B tokens) and produces 10M instruction-response pairs (~5B tokens) in a multi-turn SFT format with question, answer, and intermediate reasoning steps. The trained models take a text question or instruction prompt as input and generate a structured text answer that includes step-by-step reasoning (chain-of-thought) before the final answer. All evaluations are held-out: WEBINSTRUCT contains no in-domain data from the seven reasoning benchmarks used for evaluation.

## Method

The core contribution is a three-step data mining pipeline that harvests instruction data directly from the web without human annotation or GPT-4 distillation. (1) Recall: a fastText classifier trained on 100K crawled quiz-website examples is used to recall 100B tokens from Common Crawl, followed by GPT-4 domain filtering to yield 18M relevant documents. (2) Extract: Qwen-72B is prompted with in-context examples to identify natural Q-A pairs from preprocessed documents, yielding ~5M candidates (only 30% of documents contained usable pairs). (3) Refine: Mixtral-8×7B and Qwen-72B jointly reformat extracted pairs, remove noise, fix formality, and—critically—complete missing intermediate reasoning steps, producing the final 10M-pair WEBINSTRUCT dataset. Base LLMs (Mistral-7B, Llama-3-8B, Mixtral-8×7B, Yi-34B) are then fine-tuned on WEBINSTRUCT using SFT loss with a cosine scheduler over 2 epochs, optionally followed by continued training on public instruction datasets (OpenHermes 2.5, Code-Feedback, Math-Plus) to produce the MAmmoTH2-Plus variants.

## Results

MAmmoTH2-7B (Mistral-7B base) achieves an average of 52.8 across seven reasoning benchmarks, a +14.0 point improvement over the base model, with MATH rising from 11.2% to 36.7% and GSM8K from 36.2% to 68.4%—all on held-out data. The larger MAmmoTH2-8x7B-Plus reaches an average of 62.9, nearly matching Qwen-1.5-110B (63.6) while using only 13B active parameters. On code generation, MAmmoTH2-7B-Plus achieves 66.1 average on HumanEval(+) and 58.2 on MBPP(+), surpassing Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.2 (59.9/53.6). On chat benchmarks, MAmmoTH2-8x7B-Plus leads AlpacaEval 2.0 (33.8) and Arena Hard (32.6), outperforming GPT-3.5-Turbo and Tulu-2-DPO-70B.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GPT-4-Turbo-0409 (AVG 73.6), MAmmoTH2-8x7B-Plus (62.9) trails by ~11 points, as expected given the 13B active parameter budget. The most striking comparison is with Qwen-1.5-110B: MAmmoTH2-8x7B-Plus matches its average (62.9 vs. 63.6) while using only 13B active parameters versus 110B total. Against Mixtral-8x7B-Instruct (50.7), MAmmoTH2-8x7B-Plus gains +12.2 points on average, with particularly large gains on MATH (+24.9) and TheoremQA (+8.8). The apple-to-apple comparison with Llama-3-8B-Instruct (53.4) is especially informative: both are fine-tuned from Llama-3-8B-base on ~10M samples, but MAmmoTH2-8B-Plus outperforms by ~6 points on reasoning benchmarks while matching on general tasks, demonstrating WEBINSTRUCT's cost-effectiveness over human-annotated data. On chat benchmarks, MAmmoTH2-8x7B-Plus surpasses Tulu-2-DPO-70B (AlpacaEval 33.8 vs. 21.2; Arena Hard 32.6 vs. 15.0) and Llama-2-70b-chat (AlpacaEval 33.8 vs. 14.7), and outperforms GPT-3.5-Turbo on AlpacaEval (33.8 vs. 19.3). For the 7B class, MAmmoTH2-7B-Plus achieves 66.1/58.2 on HumanEval(+)/MBPP(+), beating Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.2 (59.9/53.6) and approaching Llama-3-8B-Instruct (65.9/58.0).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the web-recall paradigm introduced by DeepSeekMath (Shao et al., 2024) for mining math content from Common Crawl, but repurposes it for instruction tuning rather than continued pre-training. Where prior work either distilled instructions from GPT-4 (Self-Instruct, WizardMath, XwinMath) or performed expensive continued training on 100B+ token corpora (MINERVA, Galactica, DeepSeekMath), this paper inserts an extraction and refinement step using open-source LLMs (Mixtral-8×7B, Qwen-72B) to convert raw recalled documents into clean Q-A pairs, positioning WEBINSTRUCT between small high-quality SFT datasets and large noisy CT corpora. The key departure is avoiding both GPT-4 distillation (which introduces bias and hallucination) and human annotation (which limits scale), instead harvesting 10M naturally existing instruction-response pairs directly from the web.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates a cost-effective pathway to large-scale instruction data that rivals human-annotated datasets: MAmmoTH2-8B-Plus outperforms Llama-3-8B-Instruct by ~6 points on reasoning benchmarks despite using the same base model and equivalent data volume (10M pairs) without any human annotation. This challenges the assumption that high-quality instruction data requires either expensive human labeling or GPT-4 distillation. Open questions include whether the 10% hallucination rate in the refinement step can be further reduced, whether the approach generalizes to non-reasoning domains (humanities, daily chat) where the dataset is underrepresented, and whether the reliance on open-source LLMs for extraction introduces systematic biases that limit ceiling performance.
