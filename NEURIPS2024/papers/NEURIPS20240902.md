## Problem

Benchmarking vision-based driving policies is stuck between two inadequate paradigms: open-loop displacement errors that ignore safety and multi-modality, and closed-loop simulation that is computationally prohibitive and suffers from a large sensor domain gap. Additionally, existing datasets like nuScenes contain mostly trivial driving scenarios where 'blind' ego-status-only policies achieve state-of-the-art scores, and the lack of standardized evaluation protocols has led to inconsistent and unfair comparisons across the field.

## Input / Output

The driving agent is queried exactly once at the initial frame of each scene, receiving 8 surround-view camera images (1920×1080), a merged 5-sensor LiDAR point cloud, ego status (speed, acceleration, and a 3-class navigation goal one-hot vector), and optionally 3 past frames at 2 Hz (1.5 s). It must commit to a single 4-second trajectory (sequence of future poses) that remains fixed for the entire simulation horizon with no further environmental feedback. Evaluation produces a scalar PDMS in [0,1] decomposed into hard penalty subscores (no-collision, drivable-area compliance) and a weighted average of progress, time-to-collision, and comfort subscores.

## Method

NAVSIM introduces a non-reactive simulation where the ego vehicle's planned trajectory is propagated through a bird's-eye-view abstraction using an LQR controller and kinematic bicycle model at 10 Hz over a 4 s horizon, while background agents are replayed from the dataset without reacting to the ego. The PDM Score (PDMS) multiplies binary penalty subscores (NC, DAC) by a weighted average of EP (weight 5), TTC (weight 5), and Comfort (weight 2), directly inheriting the scoring logic from the PDM-Closed rule-based planner and nuPlan's closed-loop metric. To ensure challenging scenarios, the authors filter the OpenScene redistribution of nuPlan by removing frames where a constant-velocity baseline exceeds PDMS 0.8 (trivial scenes) or where the human trajectory scores below 0.8 (annotation errors), yielding 103k training and 12k test samples where the constant-velocity agent drops from 79% to 22% while the human expert rises from 91% to 95%. The framework is dataset-agnostic and hosted on a public HuggingFace evaluation server, enabling standardized, reproducible benchmarking without the computational cost of full closed-loop simulation.

## Results

On the navtest split (12k challenging scenarios), TransFuser and PARA-Drive achieve the highest PDMS of 84.0, while UniAD reaches 83.4 despite requiring 80 GPUs for 3 days of training versus 1 GPU for 1 day for TransFuser. The camera-only Latent TransFuser (LTF) achieves a near-identical PDMS of 83.8, suggesting the wide-angle front camera is the dominant input. The Ego Status MLP (a 'blind' agent using only kinematic state) scores 65.6, and the Constant Velocity baseline scores 20.6, confirming that sensor-based perception is essential in these challenging scenarios. The human operator achieves 94.8 PDMS, leaving a 10-point gap that highlights the benchmark's difficulty, with drivable area compliance (DAC) and ego progress (EP) being the most challenging subscores.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TransFuser [12] and PARA-Drive [50] tie at PDMS 84.0, both outperforming UniAD [21] (83.4) by 0.6 points despite UniAD's semi-sequential architecture with multiple transformer decoders and 80-GPU training. The camera-only LTF variant [12] achieves 83.8, essentially matching the full TransFuser with LiDAR, suggesting the front camera dominates over surround-view or point-cloud inputs. The Hydra-MDP challenge winner [30] extends TransFuser with trajectory sampling and learned subscore prediction, reaching 91.3 PDMS — a 7.3-point improvement over the TransFuser baseline. In the alignment experiment (Section 4.1), PDMS shows consistently higher Spearman and Pearson correlation with closed-loop scores than the traditional open-loop score (OLS) across all planner types, including IDM planners [48], PDM-Closed variants [16], Urban Driver [42], and PlanCNN [38]. The Ego Status MLP (65.6) confirms that 'blind' agents relying solely on kinematic state are insufficient, in contrast to nuScenes where such agents achieved state-of-the-art displacement errors.

## Relation to Prior Work

NAVSIM positions itself as a middle ground between open-loop displacement-error benchmarks (nuScenes) and fully reactive closed-loop simulators (CARLA, nuPlan). Its scoring function (PDMS) is directly inherited from the PDM rule-based planner (Dauner et al., CoRL 2023) and re-implements nuPlan's closed-loop score, while the non-reactive simulation paradigm departs from both by fixing the ego trajectory over a 4-second horizon and evaluating it against replayed background agents in a BEV abstraction. The benchmarked architectures—TransFuser (CARLA lineage), UniAD and PARA-Drive (nuScenes lineage)—represent the two dominant end-to-end driving research communities, which had previously never been compared under a single independent protocol.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

NAVSIM provides the first standardized, large-scale (103k train / 12k test) real-data benchmark where 'blind' ego-status-only policies demonstrably fail, addressing a long-standing criticism of nuScenes-based evaluation. The 10-point PDMS gap to human performance and the finding that DAC and EP remain the hardest subscores indicate the benchmark is genuinely challenging. Key open questions include whether the non-reactive assumption holds for longer horizons or more dynamic interactions, the absence of traffic-rule compliance (stop signs, lights), and the need for reactive data-driven sensor simulation to close the remaining gap to true closed-loop evaluation.
