## Problem

Existing text-to-video benchmarks evaluate only visual quality and text relevance of general videos, overlooking two dimensions critical for time-lapse generation: metamorphic amplitude (the degree of physical change over time) and temporal coherence (logical progression and continuity). No prior benchmark or automated metric specifically probes whether T2V models can generate videos with large, physically meaningful transformations such as flower blooming, ice melting, or building construction.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240669/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark consumes 1,649 text prompts (capped at 77 tokens for CLIP compatibility) describing time-lapse processes across 75 subcategories in four major types (biological, human creation, meteorological, physical), each paired with a reference video. Evaluation produces four scores per generated video: UMT-FVD (visual quality), UMTScore (text relevance), MTScore/GPT4o-MTScore (metamorphic amplitude), and CHScore (temporal coherence). The companion dataset ChronoMagic-Pro provides 460k 720p video-text pairs with an average clip duration of 234 seconds and captions exceeding 100 words, substantially longer and more physically rich than existing T2V datasets.

## Method

ChronoMagic-Bench introduces two new automated metrics. MTScore leverages InternVideo2 as a video retrieval model: ten hand-crafted retrieval sentences (five describing general videos, five describing time-lapse videos) are scored against the input video, and the ratio of summed time-lapse probabilities to total probabilities yields a coarse-grained metamorphic score; a fine-grained variant (GPT4o-MTScore) uses GPT-4o as a 5-point evaluator on uniformly sampled frames. CHScore is built on CoTracker point tracking: it computes per-frame visibility of a G×G grid of tracking points, then derives five sub-metrics (average missed-point ratio, frame-to-frame variation, cut-frame ratio, cumulative threshold-exceeding changes, and maximum single-frame jump), weighted with coefficients (0.35, 0.25, 0.15, 0.15, 0.10) and inverted to produce a coherence score; points that vanish in the direction of camera/object motion are excluded to avoid penalizing coherent scene changes. For the ChronoMagic-Pro dataset, videos are collected via 75 search-term categories, split into single-scene clips using OpenCV pixel-difference thresholds followed by ImageBind feature-space recombination, and captioned with a time-aware strategy that samples N frames, captions each with ShareGPT4Video, and then summarizes the full video description conditioned on frame positions.

## Results

On ChronoMagic-Bench (1,649 prompts), MagicTime achieves the highest metamorphic amplitude (MTScore 0.478, GPT4o-MTScore 3.13) as the only model trained on time-lapse videos, while CogVideoX-2B leads in visual quality (UMT-FVD 159.31) and text relevance (UMTScore 3.225) among open-source models. Most models cluster in a narrow MTScore range of 0.33–0.48, confirming that nearly all T2V models fail to generate videos with significant state changes. T2V-zero has the worst temporal coherence (CHScore 20.78), while ZeroScope achieves the best (99.67) among open-source models. Fine-tuning OpenSoraPlan v1.1 on 10K ChronoMagic-Pro samples with the Magic Training Strategy improves MTScore from 0.327 to 0.346 and GPT4o-MTScore from 2.19 to 3.05.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The benchmark reveals a stark divide: only MagicTime (MTScore 0.478, GPT4o-MTScore 3.13) and CogVideoX-2B (MTScore 0.404, GPT4o-MTScore 2.92) can generate true time-lapse videos with significant state changes, while all other 14 open-source models produce near-static videos with MTScore ≤ 0.467. Among U-Net-based models, AnimateDiff V3 (0.467) and VideoCrafter2 (0.433) lead in metamorphic amplitude, but none match MagicTime. Among DiT-based models, OpenSora 1.1 (0.444) and CogVideoX-2B (0.404) are strongest, yet still trail the best U-Net results. For temporal coherence, ZeroScope (99.67) and EasyAnimate V3 (90.54) outperform all others, while T2V-zero (20.78) and CogVideoX-2B (43.15) suffer from flickering. In the closed-source comparison (Table 4, ChronoMagic-Bench-150), Dream Machine achieves the highest GPT4o-MTScore (3.11) and MTScore (0.474), confirming that closed-source DiT models surpass open-source counterparts in visual quality and text relevance, though no model—open or closed—fully solves the time-lapse generation problem. Fine-tuning OpenSoraPlan v1.1 on ChronoMagic-Pro with the Magic Training Strategy (‡) improves GPT4o-MTScore from 2.19 to 3.05, approaching MagicTime's level, validating the dataset's utility.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the T2V evaluation paradigm by introducing two new assessment dimensions—metamorphic amplitude and temporal coherence—that prior benchmarks (VBench, FETV, T2VScore) entirely overlook. The CHScore metric is built directly on CoTracker's point-visibility output, while the MTScore leverages InternVideo2's video retrieval probabilities, repurposing these models as evaluation instruments rather than generators. The ChronoMagic-Pro dataset follows the Panda-70M splitting pipeline and the MagicTime annotation strategy, but scales them to 460k time-lapse-specific clips, filling a gap left by general-video datasets like WebVid-10M and InternVid that contain minimal physical transformation content.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark and 460k-clip dataset provide the first systematic tool for evaluating and training T2V models on physically rich time-lapse generation, a capability that the paper shows nearly all current models (open- and closed-source) lack. The finding that UMTScore and UMT-FVD—widely adopted metrics—show the lowest correlation with human judgment specifically on time-lapse videos raises important questions about the transferability of feature-space metrics across video domains. Open questions include whether the gap between automated metrics and human preference can be closed, whether DiT-based architectures can match U-Net stability for metamorphic generation at scale, and whether the Magic Training Strategy generalizes beyond U-Net backbones.
