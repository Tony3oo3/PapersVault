## Problem

Predicting how small molecules alter gene expression in specific cell types is critical for drug discovery, yet the field lacks standardized evaluation datasets and independent benchmarks. Existing perturbation datasets are confined to cancer cell lines, single donors, or single perturbations, making it impossible to fairly assess whether models generalize across chemical space and diverse biological contexts.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240650/cropped_page3_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark provides a 3D tensor of differential expression values (146 compounds × 4 PBMC cell types × 5,317 genes) from three donors, where each entry is −log₁₀(p-value) × sign(log-fold change) computed via limma-voom on pseudobulked counts. Models receive the full DE profile for a subset of training compounds across all cell types and must predict the masked DE values for held-out compounds in held-out cell types; chemical structures are available as SMILES strings, and baseline multiome (snRNA-seq + scATAC-seq) data supports optional gene regulatory network inference.

## Method

The core contribution is the OP3 living benchmark, a cloud-based infrastructure on the Open Problems in Single-Cell Analysis platform that formalizes the perturbation prediction task with a purpose-built dataset (146 compounds in human PBMCs, three donor replicates, four cell types), six positive/negative control methods, and MRRMSE as the primary metric with min-max scaling between controls. A NeurIPS 2023 competition attracted 1,318 participants who submitted 25,529 solutions; the top-performing methods combined diverse deep learning architectures (transformers, LSTMs, GRUs, CNNs, MLPs) with ChemBERTa for SMILES encoding, target encoding and SVD for high-dimensional preprocessing, pseudolabels for semi-supervised training, and model ensembles. The benchmark is continuously updated with retrained competition winners and community-submitted methods, enabling ongoing scrutiny of generalizability across compounds, cell types, and donors.

## Results

The OP3 benchmark evaluates six top-performing methods (top 3 from the NeurIPS 2023 Leaderboard Track and top 3 from the Judges' Prize) plus one control method on the task of predicting cell-type-specific perturbation effects across 146 compounds in PBMCs. The primary metric is MRRMSE (mean row-wise RMSE), with secondary metrics MR MAE and MR Cosine, all min-max scaled between a positive control (ground truth) and a random sample. The top-performing method, NN retraining with pseudolabels, achieves the best overall score but still shows error residuals that correlate with the number of differentially expressed genes, indicating all methods are better at predicting no change than significant change. The competition attracted 1,318 participants from 84 countries who submitted 25,529 solutions, with the winning method leveraging ChemBERTa for molecular SMILES encoding and model ensembling.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The benchmark compares six competition-winning methods against a positive control (ground truth) and a negative control (random sample), with all metrics min-max scaled between these bounds. The top-performing method (NN retraining with pseudolabels) outperforms the other five methods on the arithmetic mean of MR Cosine, MR MAE, and MR RMSE, but all methods share the limitation that prediction error increases with the number of DE genes, meaning they struggle most with compounds that induce strong transcriptional changes. The Judges' Prize methods, which attempted to incorporate gene regulatory networks from ATAC-seq data or molecular interaction databases (e.g., GSEA-MsigDB), did not outperform the leaderboard methods, suggesting that additional biological modalities require further filtering to be useful. Data preprocessing (target encoding, SVD) and model ensembling were identified as the most impactful factors across all top methods, with only the first-place leaderboard method successfully leveraging external data (ChemBERTa pre-trained molecular representations). The paper notes that the single-metric Kaggle competition setup (MRRMSE only) may have incentivized overfitting to that specific metric, a limitation addressed in the updated living benchmark with multi-metric evaluation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by introducing the first standardized, community-extensible benchmark for predicting chemical perturbation effects across cell types, building on the Open Problems in Single-cell Analysis infrastructure. Prior perturbation prediction methods (scGen, scPerturb, scPerturb-BioLORD) were evaluated on cancer cell lines or single-donor datasets without independent cross-validation, and existing datasets like sci-Plex used nuclei sequencing with limited biological relevance. The paper departs from this lineage by generating a purpose-built PBMC dataset with 146 compounds across three donors, explicitly designed to satisfy criteria (disease relevance, balanced heterogeneity, replicates, positive/negative controls) that prior datasets lacked, and by hosting a large-scale NeurIPS 2023 competition to establish a competitive baseline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The OP3 benchmark and its 300K+ cell, 146-compound, 3-donor PBMC dataset provide the field's first rigorous, reproducible evaluation framework for perturbation prediction, enabling fair comparison of methods that were previously assessed under inconsistent protocols. Key open questions remain: the dataset is still small relative to the ~16,600 clinical-stage drugs and the heterogeneity across individuals and genetic backgrounds; the single-metric Kaggle evaluation risks Goodhart's law overfitting; and the finding that gene regulatory network integration from ATAC-seq data consistently degraded performance suggests the field lacks effective strategies for incorporating biological priors. The living benchmark design addresses some of these by enabling multi-task, multi-metric evaluation and continuous community updates.
