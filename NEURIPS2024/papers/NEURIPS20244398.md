## Problem

RLHF pipelines suffer from reward overoptimization: because the reward model is learned from finite preference data, it can assign spuriously high scores to out-of-distribution responses, causing the fine-tuned LLM to drift away from genuinely preferred outputs. Prior theoretical treatments either restrict to linear reward classes or produce objectives that are intractable to implement in practice, leaving a gap between provable guarantees and deployable algorithms.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a fixed offline preference dataset of N tuples (prompt, chosen response, rejected response, binary label) and a frozen reference LLM policy π^ref. It outputs a single aligned LLM policy parameterized by θ, trained end-to-end without maintaining a separate reward model. The SFT regularization term is computed on the chosen responses from the same preference dataset (serving as the baseline policy π^base), so no additional data source is required beyond what DPO already uses.

## Method

The paper first defines a theoretical maximin objective (Algorithm 1) that selects the best policy against an adversarially chosen reward model minimizing the sum of its MLE loss and its own expected reward, yielding a provable suboptimality gap of Õ(C_coverage² √(N_R/N)) under a partial coverage condition. A key equivalence theorem then shows this maximin problem reduces to a minimax objective whose inner maximization has a closed-form KL-regularized optimal policy, allowing reparameterization of the reward model via its optimal policy (à la DPO). The resulting practical algorithm, RPO (Algorithm 2), simply adds a weighted SFT loss (negative log-likelihood of chosen responses) to the standard DPO loss, with weight ηβ controlling the strength of imitation. The SFT term modifies the gradient direction of DPO—rather than merely scaling it as β does—by pulling the policy toward the well-covered chosen-response distribution, which the theory identifies as the mechanism that provably prevents the policy from exploiting reward-model uncertainty on poorly covered responses.

## Results

On MT-Bench, RPO achieves 7.381 (beta) and 7.916 (gemma), outperforming DPO (7.278 / 7.688) and the officially released Zephyr models (7.200 / 7.719). On AlpacaEval 2.0, RPO reaches 23.28% LC win rate (beta) and 15.51% (gemma), exceeding DPO (21.15% / 15.36%) and Zephyr (13.20% / 14.78%). In GPT-4-annotated pairwise evaluations on in-data distributions, RPO wins 56.0% against DPO (beta) and 54.0% against DPO (gemma), while also substantially outperforming the reference models (79.0% and 71.7% respectively).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RPO consistently outperforms DPO across all benchmarks and both model series. On MT-Bench, RPO (beta) scores 7.381 vs. DPO's 7.278 (+0.103), and RPO (gemma) scores 7.916 vs. DPO's 7.688 (+0.228). On AlpacaEval 2.0 LC win rate, RPO (beta) achieves 23.28% vs. DPO's 21.15% (+2.13 pp), and RPO (gemma) achieves 15.51% vs. DPO's 15.36% (+0.15 pp). Against the officially released Zephyr models (trained with DPO by the original authors), RPO shows larger margins: e.g., RPO (beta) gains +0.181 MT-Bench and +10.08 pp LC win rate over zephyr-beta-7b. In GPT-4-annotated pairwise comparisons on in-data distributions, RPO wins 56.0% of matchups against DPO (beta) and 54.0% against DPO (gemma), confirming the theoretical prediction that the SFT regularization mitigates overoptimization. RPO also substantially outperforms the unaligned reference models (79.0% and 71.7% win rates), demonstrating that the preference optimization component remains effective.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on DPO (Rafailov et al., 2023), whose preference optimization loss forms one component of the RPO objective, and on the reward-model-free reparametrization technique that DPO introduced. It departs from prior offline RLHF theory (Zhu et al. 2023, Zhan et al. 2023, Xiong et al. 2023) by working under general function approximation rather than linear models, and by showing that the theoretically motivated adversarial maximin objective is equivalent to a simple DPO + SFT loss. While concurrent works (ORPO, Smaug) also incorporate SFT-style regularization during preference optimization, this paper is the first to provide a theoretical justification for why SFT loss acts as an implicit adversarial regularizer that provably mitigates overoptimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical takeaway—that adding a weighted SFT loss to DPO provably mitigates reward overoptimization—gives practitioners a principled, zero-overhead modification to a widely used alignment pipeline. The main limitation is the offline setting: the theory and experiments assume a fixed preference dataset, and extending the framework to iterative/online RLHF where data is collected adaptively remains open. Additionally, the partial coverage assumption (Assumption 5.2) is necessary for the guarantee, and its practical verification in large-scale LLM training is non-trivial. The modest empirical gains over DPO (e.g., ~0.1 MT-Bench points) suggest the theoretical benefit may be more pronounced in regimes with stronger distributional shift or noisier preference data.
