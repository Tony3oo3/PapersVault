## Problem

Existing vertical federated learning (VFL) methods handle either multi-party collaboration with exact identifiers or two-party fuzzy linkage, but practical multi-party fuzzy VFL—where many parties are linked by imprecise keys like GPS coordinates—suffers from quadratic growth in key pairs, incorrect linkages, overfitting, escalating privacy costs, and communication bottlenecks on the label-holding party. No prior framework simultaneously addresses all these challenges at scale.

## Input / Output

The primary party holds n records with features (reduced to 4-dim PCA keys) and labels, while k secondary parties each hold their own feature subsets plus fuzzy identifiers (keys perturbed with Gaussian noise, scale 0.05). Each primary record is linked to K nearest-neighbor records from each secondary party via privacy-preserving record linkage; the model consumes these K-neighborhoods as variable-length sequences (L=K for secondaries, L=1 for primary) and outputs a regression value or class label from the primary party's decoder, along with updated local encoder parameters at every party.

## Method

FeT distributes a transformer across parties: each secondary party runs an encoder that fuses local features with multi-dimensional positional encoding of its fuzzy identifiers, while the primary party runs an encoder-decoder that aggregates the secondary representations. Three key mechanisms distinguish it from prior split-learning or similarity-based VFL: (1) a trainable dynamic masking MLP generates attention masks directly from identifier values, automatically suppressing incorrectly linked neighbors without transmitting raw similarities; (2) party dropout randomly zeros a fraction r_d of secondary representations each round, cutting primary-party communication by ~80% and regularizing against overfitting; (3) positional encoding averaging periodically syncs PE layers across parties under MPC to fix cross-party encoding misalignment. For privacy, the SplitAvg framework replaces per-party full-noise addition with MPC-based aggregation where each secondary party contributes only N(0, σ/k²) Gaussian noise, combined with per-representation norm clipping (‖R‖₂ ≤ C/k) and privacy amplification via random subsampling of secondary records, yielding a (ε,δ)-DP guarantee whose noise scale is independent of the number of parties.

## Results

On three real-world two-party fuzzy VFL regression datasets, FeT achieves the lowest RMSE across all benchmarks: 39.75 on house, 232.98 on bike, and 26.94 on hdb. In multi-party fuzzy VFL scaled to 50 parties on MNIST, FeT reaches 85.47% accuracy, surpassing the best baseline by up to 46%. The dynamic masking module alone contributes up to 13 percentage points of accuracy gain in 50-party settings, and party dropout at rate 0.8 reduces communication overhead by approximately 80% with minimal accuracy loss.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is FedSim [50], the state-of-the-art two-party fuzzy VFL method that trains on top-K neighboring records using transmitted key similarities. FeT outperforms FedSim on all three real-world datasets: by 2.37 RMSE on house (39.75 vs 42.12), by 2.69 RMSE on bike (232.98 vs 235.67), and by 0.19 RMSE on hdb (26.94 vs 27.13). In multi-party settings (up to 50 parties), FeT's advantage over FedSim grows substantially, reaching up to 46% accuracy improvement on MNIST, because FedSim's similarity-based linkage degrades as parties increase while FeT's positional encoding averaging and dynamic masking scale gracefully. FeT also consistently beats the simpler Top1Sim baseline (which links only the single most similar neighbor) and the Solo baseline (primary-party-only training) across all party counts and neighbor values K. Notably, FeT achieves these gains while providing stronger privacy guarantees, since it does not transfer raw similarity data between parties unlike FedSim.

## Relation to Prior Work

FeT extends the SplitNN paradigm [45] by replacing the conventional MLP-based split architecture with a distributed transformer, where each secondary party hosts an encoder and the primary party hosts both encoder and decoder. It departs from prior multi-party VFL methods that rely on Private Set Intersection with exact keys, and from two-party fuzzy VFL approaches like FedSim [50] that transfer raw key similarities, by encoding identifiers directly into representations via multi-dimensional positional encoding [28] and a trainable dynamic masking module. The SplitAvg privacy framework is a novel hybrid that merges MPC-based secure aggregation with per-party Gaussian noise, a combination not previously explored in the VFL literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Multi-party fuzzy VFL is a practically important but underexplored setting (e.g., cross-hospital or cross-transportation collaborations linked by imprecise addresses), and FeT provides the first scalable transformer-based solution with formal differential privacy guarantees that do not degrade as parties increase. Key limitations include the assumption that all parties share common identifier features, the reliance on a correlation between identifiers and data representations (which fails for randomly generated keys), and significant accuracy loss at low ε values. Open questions include extending FeT to heterogeneous feature spaces without shared identifiers and quantifying the privacy-utility tradeoff more precisely for deployment-critical applications.
