## Problem

Standard multimodal contrastive learning (e.g., InfoNCE) treats all negative samples equally and maximizes shared mutual information across views while suppressing modality-specific unique information, leading to feature suppression and shortcut learning. Existing remedies (reconstruction regularization, implicit feature modification, factorized representations) either introduce noise or rely on augmentation assumptions without explicitly disentangling shared from unique task-related features.

## Input / Output

The method consumes paired multimodal inputs (image + text caption, image + audio, or text + audio) through modality-specific encoders (ViT-B/32 or ResNet-152 for images, Transformer or GRU for text, linear layers for audio). Each encoder output H is split by a shared decoder and a unique decoder into Z^s and Z^u; the cross-product Z^n = Z^s × Z^u forms a normal vector in a quaternion-like embedding space. The final output is a set of shared and unique embeddings used to score cross-modal retrieval rankings (i2t, t2i, i2a, a2i, t2a, a2t) via Recall@K metrics.

## Method

QUEST introduces a dual-decoder architecture (shared + unique) on a shared encoder, trained with three coupled objectives: (1) a Shared Information Constraint (SIC) that applies standard InfoNCE to shared representations to maximize cross-modal agreement; (2) a Unique Information Constraint (UIC) that computes cross-products of shared and unique embeddings to form normal vectors, then applies a quadruple contrastive loss to align these normals across modalities while an orthogonal cosine loss (L_cos) enforces Z^s ⊥ Z^u to prevent degeneration; (3) a Self-Penalization mechanism (P-UIC) that builds a weighted similarity matrix from the shared decoder's output and uses it to reweight negative samples in the UIC, amplifying the gradient for hard negatives that share semantic content with the anchor. The key insight is that the cross-product operation fundamentally alters gradient propagation compared to pairwise InfoNCE, making UIC a weaker constraint that ensures quaternion vectors from different views lie on a common plane without directly forcing unique representations to be similar, thereby preserving modality-specific information while still capturing task-relevant shared structure.

## Results

On Flickr30k (no shortcut), QUEST with CLIP achieves RSUM 546.7 (i2t R@1=89.3, t2i R@1=73.9), outperforming InfoNCE (543.5), LTD (542.8), and IFM (544.9). On MS-COCO (no shortcut), QUEST achieves RSUM 455.6 (i2t R@1=65.4, t2i R@1=48.5) vs. InfoNCE 447.5 and IFM 449.5. The most dramatic gains appear under synthetic shortcuts: on MS-COCO, QUEST achieves i2t R@1=50.8 and t2i R@1=37.9 (RSUM 389.4), compared to InfoNCE's 13.6/7.3 (RSUM 149.4), LTD's 18.9/16.5 (RSUM 223.4), and IFM's 23.4/17.1 (RSUM 235.5), representing an average 97.95% improvement over baselines on the CLIP model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

QUEST consistently outperforms all three baselines (InfoNCE, LTD, IFM) across both datasets and both models (CLIP and VSE++). Without shortcuts, the gains are modest but consistent: on Flickr30k, QUEST improves CLIP RSUM by +3.2 over InfoNCE and +1.8 over IFM; on MS-COCO, the improvement is +8.1 over InfoNCE and +6.1 over IFM. The most striking advantage emerges under synthetic shortcuts, where QUEST dramatically outperforms all baselines — on MS-COCO with CLIP, QUEST achieves RSUM 389.4 vs. 149.4 (InfoNCE), 223.4 (LTD), and 235.5 (IFM), a 97.95% average improvement. For VSE++, QUEST also leads on MS-COCO (RSUM 381.1 vs. 363.8 InfoNCE, 370.5 LTD, 359.3 IFM) but is slightly behind LTD on Flickr30k with shortcuts (RSUM 251.8 vs. 274.6), which the authors attribute to VSE++'s GRU text encoder being less effective at capturing modality-independent information on smaller datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the multimodal contrastive learning (MCL) line of work by introducing a dual-decoder architecture that explicitly separates shared and unique information, building on the information-theoretic motivation from FactorCL [41] and the feature suppression analysis of Robinson et al. [58] and Bleeker et al. [4]. Unlike prior approaches that use data augmentation, reconstruction regularization, or factorized losses to preserve unique information, QUEST employs a quaternion-style embedding space where cross-products of shared and unique representations create a normal vector for indirect contrastive alignment. The self-penalization mechanism, which uses the shared decoder's output to reweight hard negatives in the unique information loss, departs from soft-label approaches [49, 17] by imposing stricter constraints rather than relaxing them, and is positioned as orthogonal to existing MCL training methods like MoCo and SimCLR.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a well-identified failure mode in multimodal contrastive learning—over-reliance on shared information at the expense of modality-specific task-relevant features—with a relatively simple architectural addition (dual decoders) and a novel loss formulation. However, the practical gains on standard benchmarks (Flickr30k, MS-COCO) are modest (1–3 R@1 points), and the dramatic 97.95% improvement is confined to a controlled synthetic shortcut setting where baselines collapse to near-zero. Key limitations include the requirement that embedding dimensions be divisible by 3 for cross-products, the restriction to 3D subspaces that limits theoretical interpretability in high-dimensional spaces, and the lack of modality fusion or cross-modal interactions in the multi-modality experiments. Open questions include whether the quaternion embedding approach generalizes to larger architectures and more than two modalities, and whether the self-penalization mechanism transfers to self-supervised or LLM-augmented settings as the authors themselves suggest.
