## Problem

LiDAR-based 3D object detectors suffer from severe class imbalance, overfitting to majority classes while underfitting to rare ones. Existing copy-and-paste augmentation (GT-Aug, Real-Aug) is constrained by limited positional diversity (objects can only be pasted near their original locations) and limited sample diversity (the same in-domain objects are repeatedly reused). The paper asks whether diverse, out-of-domain pseudo-LiDAR samples can be generated cheaply and inserted flexibly to close the majority–minority performance gap.

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes surround-view video frames of purchased miniatures (~$100 total) and publicly available YouTube/auction-site videos of minority-class vehicles, along with the target dataset's LiDAR sensor configuration (FOV, azimuth resolution, channel count), rasterized map layout, and estimated ground points. It produces a pre-computed pseudo-LiDAR object bank (e.g., 36,960 trucks, 52,800 construction vehicles on nuScenes) where each object is stored at 13 heading angles across the full ±50 m perception range, with objects having fewer than 16 points discarded. During training, sampled objects from this bank are inserted into live LiDAR scenes, yielding an augmented point cloud with balanced class occurrences.

## Method

PGT-Aug is a three-stage pipeline. First, a 2D-to-3D renderer (Plenoxels or Gaussian Splatting) reconstructs a dense volumetric representation from multi-view images, with a view-agnostic color module that averages spherical-harmonic-based RGB values across all camera rays hitting each voxel or Gaussian to produce a single representative color. Second, object-level domain alignment transforms the RGB point cloud into a LiDAR-like one: view-dependent range projection filters occluded points and rearranges survivors onto a regular spherical grid matching the target sensor's FOV and azimuth resolution, while a CycleGAN-based unpaired intensity simulator (using PointNeXt backbones) transfers reflectance statistics, regularized by a novel region-matching loss that applies Hungarian assignment over farthest-point-sampled ball patches to align intensity distributions despite differing point counts. Third, a hybrid ground+map placement strategy determines feasible insertion locations by prioritizing map semantics where ground estimation is sparse, PCA-based axis alignment plus a PointNet++ heading classifier orient each object, and a rigid-body motion model stacks single-scan points into multi-sweep objects for benchmarks that use LiDAR sweep stacking.

## Results

On the nuScenes validation set with CP-Voxel, PGT-Aug achieves 63.52 mAP and 69.11 NDS, outperforming GT-Aug (59.04 mAP, 66.54 NDS) and Real-Aug (62.27 mAP, 68.39 NDS). On the nuScenes test set, PGT-Aug with CP-Voxel reaches 67.1 mAP and 72.3 NDS, a +1.3% mAP and +1.0% NDS improvement over Real-Aug. The method generalizes across architectures (SECOND, CP-Pillar, CP-Voxel, Transfusion-L, VoxelNeXt) and datasets (KITTI: 70.1 mAP vs. 68.5; Lyft: 14.01 mAP vs. 13.84), with the largest gains on minority classes such as Bicycle (+3.6% AP) and Construction Vehicle (+1.3% AP).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PGT-Aug consistently outperforms GT-Aug (random copy-and-paste) across all five architectures, with the largest gap on CP-Voxel (+4.48 mAP, +2.57 NDS) and SECOND (+9.26 mAP, +5.74 NDS). Against Real-Aug (ground-based placement), PGT-Aug wins on mAP and NDS for CP-Voxel (+1.25 mAP, +0.72 NDS), CP-Pillar (+0.36 mAP, +0.64 NDS), and SECOND (+0.28 mAP, +0.07 NDS), though it slightly trails on Transfusion-L mAP (64.20 vs. 63.54) while still leading on NDS. On the KITTI Car benchmark (Table 15), PGT-Aug achieves 76.35 AP (Hard) versus Real-Aug (76.06), LiDAR-Aug (75.35), 3D-VField (75.55), and CA-Aug (75.75), confirming superiority over rendering-based and adversarial augmentation methods. For loss-based imbalance methods, PGT-Aug on PointPillars achieves 50.9 mAP versus DWA (44.6) and CBLoss (49.4), and combining PGT-Aug with CBLoss yields the best result (52.2 mAP), demonstrating that data augmentation and loss reweighting are complementary.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the copy-and-paste augmentation lineage for LiDAR 3D detection (GT-Aug in SECOND, Real-Aug, LiDAR-Aug) by replacing the source of minority-class objects: instead of resampling existing LiDAR points from the same dataset or relying on CAD models, it reconstructs dense 3D volumetric representations from multi-view images of physical miniatures and public web videos using NeRF-based renderers (Plenoxels, Gaussian Splatting). The domain alignment step departs from prior simulation-based approaches by using a CycleGAN-based unpaired intensity transfer with a novel region-matching loss, and the placement strategy extends ground-only composition (Real-Aug) with map-aware context. The pipeline is explicitly renderer-agnostic, building on the broader NeRF/Gaussian Splatting reconstruction literature rather than any single generative model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical takeaway is that a ~$100 budget of physical miniatures plus freely available web videos can produce pseudo-LiDAR objects whose FID scores are competitive with (or better than) real LiDAR data from other datasets, offering a low-cost, scalable path to mitigate class imbalance in 3D detection. Limitations include residual domain gaps between generated and real LiDAR, restriction to rigid objects (no deformable/dynamic shapes like animals), and point-cloud-only output (no rendered images). Open questions include extending the pipeline to deformable objects, reducing the remaining sensor-domain discrepancy, and whether the approach generalizes to datasets without map information or with fundamentally different sensor geometries.
