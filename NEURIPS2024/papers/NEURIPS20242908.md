## Problem

Scaling LLMs requires ever-larger training corpora, yet dominant web-scraped datasets (e.g., C4) carry well-documented issues of toxicity, bias, factual inaccuracy, and lack of precise timestamps. Prior business-oriented NLP datasets sourced from SEC EDGAR were limited to subsets of filings (annual or quarterly reports only) and reached at most ~14.5B tokens, far below the 100B+ scale needed for multi-billion-parameter pre-training. The paper asks whether a comprehensive, large-scale, public corpus of business disclosures can simultaneously be more factual, less toxic, and temporally grounded than web-based alternatives.

## Input / Output

The pipeline ingests all daily EDGAR submission archives (3TB compressed) from 1996 through end-2023, processing both the main filing and every attachment in HTML or fixed-width text/SGML format. It produces three dataset splits: BeanCounter.clean (159.4B tokens, 865GB), BeanCounter.fraud (0.3B tokens from known fraudulent filers), and BeanCounter.final (110.5B tokens after document-level deduplication). For model evaluation, 1B tokens are sampled stratified by year and used to continue pre-training Pythia-1.4B and Phi-1.5, with the resulting models evaluated on financial, toxicity, and general comprehension benchmarks.

## Method

Dataset construction follows a four-stage pipeline: (1) downloading all EDGAR daily archives, (2) extracting text with content-type-specific logic that removes numeric tables via a characters-per-tag density threshold (<10), unbreaks sentences spanning page breaks, strips repeated header content, and preserves HTML/CSS whitespace and indentation, (3) cleaning by excluding standardized short forms, documents under 200 words, and documents exceeding the 99th-percentile whitespace ratio (41%), and (4) document-level deduplication using MinHash with 5-grams, 260 permutations, and LSH at an 80% similarity threshold. Toxicity is assessed at the sentence level using the Perspective API on sentences containing demographic descriptors from the HolisticBias dataset, with a balanced C4-en sample matched to BeanCounter's descriptor frequencies for fair comparison. For model evaluation, continued pre-training uses the original models' optimization settings but with a 50% reduced maximum learning rate and cosine decay to 10% of the maximum without warm-up, trained for 1B tokens on a year-stratified sample of BeanCounter.

## Results

After 1B tokens of continued pre-training on BeanCounter, Pythia-1.4B achieves a 33.5% reduction in RealToxicityPrompts toxicity score (0.0579 → 0.0385) and a 13.8% reduction in Perspective score (0.1297 → 0.1118), while Phi-1.5 shows a 17.9% reduction in toxicity score (0.0224 → 0.0184). On financial domain tasks, Phi-1.5-BeanCounter improves Fin NER F-1 by 4.2% (0.71 → 0.74) and FPB F-1 by 2.2% (0.93 → 0.95), while Pythia-1.4B-BeanCounter gains 1.4% on Fin NER (0.72 → 0.73) and 1.1% on FPB (0.92 → 0.93). On general LLM benchmarks (BBH, MMLU-Pro, MuSR, IFeval, GPQA), Pythia-1.4B performs similarly or slightly better, whereas Phi-1.5 shows modest declines in some tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Pythia-1.4B (base), continued pre-training on BeanCounter wins on all four metrics: Fin NER F-1 improves from 0.72 to 0.73, FPB F-1 from 0.92 to 0.93, toxicity score drops from 0.0579 to 0.0385 (−33.5%), and Perspective score drops from 0.1297 to 0.1118 (−13.8%). Against Phi-1.5 (base), BeanCounter continued pre-training wins on Fin NER (0.71 → 0.74, +4.2%) and FPB (0.93 → 0.95, +2.2%), with a smaller toxicity reduction (0.0224 → 0.0184, −17.9%) and a marginal Perspective score improvement (0.0901 → 0.0887). For the C4-en toxicity comparison, BeanCounter sentences containing demographic descriptors have 59–89% lower average toxicity than matched C4-en sentences across all five demographic axes (e.g., 'Asian' sentences: 0.0122 vs. 0.0529; 'LGBTQ' sentences: 0.0052 vs. 0.0361), with all reductions statistically significant at the 1% level except 'heterosexual'. On general LLM benchmarks, Pythia-1.4B-BeanCounter ties or slightly beats its base on BBH, MMLU-Pro, MuSR, and GPQA, while Phi-1.5-BeanCounter loses on BBH (0.3346 → 0.3124), MMLU-Pro (0.17 → 0.1252), IFeval (0.2051 → 0.1928), and GPQA (0.2732 → 0.2479), suggesting potential out-of-domain trade-offs for that model.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of EDGAR-based business text datasets (EDGAR-CORPUS at 6.5B tokens, BloombergGPT at 14.5B tokens) by extracting from all disclosure types and attachments rather than only annual/quarterly reports, yielding a 159B-token corpus roughly an order of magnitude larger. It also departs from the dominant web-scraping paradigm for large-scale pretraining data (C4, The Pile, FineWeb) by sourcing from a regulated, timestamped, and legally certified domain. The toxicity analysis builds on HolisticBias descriptors and the Perspective API but introduces a targeted methodology—scoring only sentences containing demographic descriptors—rather than the random-span or document-level averaging used in prior corpus audits.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

BeanCounter addresses a concrete gap: the lack of large-scale, publicly available, domain-specific corpora sufficient to pre-train multi-billion-parameter models in the business/finance domain. The 59–89% toxicity reduction relative to C4-en and the 18–33% reduction in toxic generation after continued pretraining suggest a practical pathway toward safer LLMs without sacrificing domain competence. However, the model evaluation is limited to two ~1.4B-parameter models with no error bars, the out-of-domain results for Phi-1.5 show degradation on several general benchmarks, and the paper itself flags that regulated business text may carry subtle deceptive framing that is hard to detect—open questions that will matter as the dataset is adopted at scale.
