## Problem

Existing protein interface prediction (PIP) methods train on bound (post-binding) structures but must test on unbound (pre-binding) ones, and they largely ignore the conformational changes proteins undergo upon binding. This bound-unbound structural mismatch causes significant performance degradation, as demonstrated empirically: using bound structures for testing can greatly boost baseline accuracy. The paper frames protein flexibility as an adversarial attack on the trained model and asks how to make protein representations robust to such structural perturbations.

## Input / Output

The method takes two protein graphs as input, where each protein is represented by its α-carbon 3D coordinates and residue-type one-hot features, with edges constructed via a 10-nearest-neighbor graph (within 30 Å) carrying SE(3)-invariant edge features. Crucially, the input structures can come from any source—native bound, native unbound, AlphaFold2, or ESMFold predictions—without requiring native experimental structures. The output is a binary interaction label for every residue pair across the two proteins (M×N pairwise classifications), evaluated by median AUC across complexes.

## Method

ATProt is an end-to-end adversarial training framework that enforces C-Lipschitz continuity on spectral graph encoders to guarantee bounded representation perturbation under structural flexibility. The core mechanism is a differentiable stability regularization loss L_S tailored to each encoder type: for BernNet (the strongest variant), the paper derives the first Lipschitz bound for Bernstein-based spectral filters, showing the maximum absolute slope C_h* is upper-bounded by max_i K·|θ_i − θ_{i+1}|, which yields the simple regularization L_S = max_i |θ_i − θ_{i+1}|. The architecture comprises a spectral graph encoder (BernNet, ChebyNet, LPF, or SGC) producing stable per-residue representations, a cross-attention module for inter-protein communication, and a binary classifier trained with weighted cross-entropy jointly with L_S. Theoretically, Theorem 3.1 guarantees that the representation perturbation is bounded by ε·C_h·A(Ψ)·||F||_p, where ε is the structural perturbation magnitude, providing a principled defense against the bound-unbound gap without requiring paired bound-unbound training data.

## Results

On DB5.5 (the primary benchmark), ATProt-Bern achieves a MedAUC of 0.928 on Native-Unbound structures and 0.925 on AlphaFold2-predicted structures, maintaining near-bound performance (0.932 on Native-Bound) with only a 0.004 drop. In contrast, all baselines suffer 0.016–0.026 MedAUC degradation when switching from bound to unbound structures. On the larger DIPS dataset, ATProt-Bern reaches 0.906 MedAUC with ESMFold structures, outperforming the best baseline (dMaSIF at 0.885) by 0.021. The ablation (ATProt-Bern w/o SR) confirms the stability regularization is critical: without it, performance drops to 0.901 on Native-Unbound and 0.901 on AlphaFold2.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ATProt-Bern outperforms all five baselines on every non-bound structure setting: it leads BIPSPI by +0.017 on Native-Unbound, +0.033 on ESMFold, and +0.038 on AlphaFold2 (MedAUC). On Native-Bound, BIPSPI retains a slight edge (0.937 vs. 0.932), but ATProt-Bern is competitive with dMaSIF (0.928) and clearly ahead of SASNet (0.902), DTNN (0.912), and NEA (0.916). The most striking comparison is the degradation pattern: baselines lose 0.016–0.026 MedAUC going from bound to unbound structures, while ATProt-Bern loses only 0.004, demonstrating that the stability regularization effectively neutralizes the bound-unbound mismatch. On DIPS (Table 3), ATProt-Bern achieves 0.906 MedAUC with ESMFold inputs, beating the strongest baseline dMaSIF (0.885) by 0.021, while all baselines drop 0.014–0.032 from their bound-structure scores. The w/o SR ablation (0.901 on Unbound, 0.901 on AF2) confirms that the gains are attributable to the proposed Lipschitz-based stability regularization rather than the encoder architecture alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the line of spectral graph neural network encoders (BernNet, ChebyNet, SGC) for protein representation learning, as pioneered by NEA and subsequent GNN-based PIP methods. It departs from prior PIP work by reframing the bound-unbound structure mismatch as an adversarial attack on protein representations, rather than attempting to explicitly predict bound structures (which is data-limited and partner-dependent). The key methodological contribution is importing Lipschitz-continuity-based adversarial training from computer vision into the protein graph setting, and providing the first theoretical stability regularization for Bernstein-based spectral filters. The cross-attention + pairwise classifier architecture follows the standard PIP pipeline established by NEA and SASNet.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The bound-unbound mismatch is a fundamental practical bottleneck in PIP: models are trained on bound structures from PDB but must infer on unbound structures in real applications. ATProt's ability to maintain performance with AlphaFold2 and ESMFold predicted structures enables structure-free inference, which is practically significant for drug discovery pipelines where experimental structures are unavailable. The theoretical guarantee (Lipschitz continuity with a tractable upper bound via Proposition 4.1) provides a principled design criterion for stable protein encoders beyond empirical tuning. Limitations include the restriction to spectral GNN encoders, the absence of adversarial training on the classifier itself, and the modest absolute performance gains (~2-3 AUC points over best baselines on unbound structures).
