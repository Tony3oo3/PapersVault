## Problem

Current multimodal language models perform chain-of-thought reasoning exclusively in text, lacking the ability to generate intermediate visual artifacts (auxiliary lines, bounding boxes, depth maps, plots) that humans naturally use to facilitate spatial and mathematical reasoning. While specialist vision models can produce such visual outputs, existing tool-use frameworks like VisProg and ViperGPT follow rigid, pre-defined plans and cannot adapt based on the visual results they observe, limiting their robustness on complex multimodal tasks.

## Input / Output

SKETCHPAD accepts heterogeneous inputs including geometry diagrams with their underlying Matplotlib code, raw math function expressions, adjacency matrices, chess FEN strings, single or multiple natural images, and a text query. The output is a final text answer accompanied by a sequence of intermediate visual sketches—auxiliary lines on geometry diagrams, function plots, graph renderings, chess board images, bounding boxes, segmentation masks, depth maps, and zoomed-in crops—each generated by executing Python code that the LM synthesizes on the fly. The iterative Thought-Action-Observation loop means the model consumes its own generated images as context for subsequent reasoning steps, with coordinates normalized to [0,1] and bounding boxes in [x, y, w, h] format.

## Method

SKETCHPAD is a training-free, prompting-only framework built on the AutoGen multi-agent framework that gives a multimodal LM an iterative sketching loop: at each step the model produces a Thought (a plan for what visual artifact to create), an Action (Python code calling specialist vision models or plotting libraries), and an Observation (the resulting image or text output), updating its multimodal context until it issues a Terminate action with the final answer. The key mechanism is that the LM synthesizes executable code using provided tool signatures—Matplotlib and NetworkX for math tasks, and Grounding-DINO (detection), Segment Anything/Semantic-SAM (segmentation), DepthAnything (depth estimation), sliding-window search, zoom-in/crop, and image overlay for vision tasks—rather than following a fixed pipeline. Unlike prior modular approaches where vision modules execute a pre-defined sequence, SKETCHPAD lets the LM inspect each visual artifact and dynamically revise its plan, enabling error recovery (e.g., detecting a missed object and switching to sliding-window search). The framework requires no finetuning; the LM is prompted with tool docstrings and in-context examples, and the visual sketches serve as a 'visual chain of thought' that the model reasons over in subsequent turns.

## Results

GPT-4o with SKETCHPAD sets new state-of-the-art on all seven vision tasks, achieving 80.3% on V*Bench (vs. 66.0% baseline, +14.3%), 83.9% on BLINK relative depth (+12.1%), 81.1% on spatial reasoning (+9.1%), and 80.8% on visual correspondence (+7.5%). On math tasks, SKETCHPAD yields an average gain of 11.2% for GPT-4o and 23.4% for GPT-4 Turbo, with the largest improvement on maximum flow (25.0% → 66.3%, +41.3% for GPT-4o). GPT-4o + SKETCHPAD also surpasses the previous SOTA SEAL on V*Bench (80.3% vs. 75.4%) and CogVLM on semantic correspondence (58.3% vs. 33.1%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GPT-4o + SKETCHPAD outperforms all prior multimodal LLMs (Gemini-Pro, Claude 3 OPUS, GPT-4V-preview, LLaVA-NeXT-34B) on every vision task, with the largest margins on V*Bench (+5.3 over SEAL, +25.3 over GPT-4V) and semantic correspondence (+25.2 over CogVLM). On math tasks (Table 1), SKETCHPAD dramatically improves GPT-4o on maximum flow (25.0% → 66.3%) and graph isomorphism (50.8% → 65.3%), while GPT-4 Turbo + SKETCHPAD reaches 96.8% on max flow and 97.6% on connectivity, far exceeding Claude 3 OPUS (56.3% and 82.0% respectively). Against the visual prompting baseline SoM [55], SKETCHPAD wins on V*Bench (80.3% vs. 49.0% for GPT-4o) and depth (83.9% vs. 62.9%), though SoM+orig. is competitive on spatial reasoning (82.5% vs. 81.1%). VisProg [14] performs worse than the base LM on all four single-image tasks (e.g., 32.4% on V*Bench vs. 66.0% for GPT-4o alone), confirming that rigid program-based composition without adaptive visual feedback is inferior to SKETCHPAD's iterative sketch-and-reason loop. Open-source LLaVA-NeXT models benefit from oracle SKETCHPAD artifacts (e.g., LLaVA-NeXT-13B winner ID: 5.8% → 36.7%), suggesting the framework generalizes beyond GPT-4.

## Relation to Prior Work

SKETCHPAD extends textual chain-of-thought reasoning to the visual domain, where intermediate reasoning steps are drawn sketches rather than text tokens. It generalizes visual prompting (SoM) and visual programming (VisProg, ViperGPT) by allowing the LM to autonomously decide what to sketch and to adapt its plan based on the visual artifacts it observes, rather than following a pre-defined pipeline. The framework is built on the AutoGen multi-agent infrastructure and repurposes specialist vision models (Grounding-DINO, Segment Anything, DepthAnything) as callable sketching tools, positioning them as 'visual CoT steps' rather than fixed preprocessing modules.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SKETCHPAD demonstrates that a training-free, prompt-only framework can set new SOTA across a wide range of math and vision benchmarks, suggesting that visual artifact generation is a natural and underexploited reasoning channel for multimodal LMs. The finding that stronger models (GPT-4o) benefit more from sketching than weaker ones (GPT-4 Turbo) implies that visual CoT will become increasingly valuable as base model capabilities grow. Key open questions include whether natively multimodal models (e.g., Unified-IO 2, Chameleon) can be instruction-tuned to produce sketches end-to-end, how to reduce the substantial compute overhead of multi-turn sketching, and whether the approach generalizes to embodied/robotic settings where the 'sketchpad' is the physical environment.
