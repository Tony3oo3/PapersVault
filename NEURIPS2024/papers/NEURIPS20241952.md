## Problem

Given a fixed computational budget, what is the best text embedding model one can obtain by contrastively fine-tuning a pre-trained decoder-only LLM? While scaling laws for pre-training are well established, no systematic study had addressed how model size, data quantity, and parameter-efficient fine-tuning method interact in the compute-constrained embedding fine-tuning regime, leaving practitioners without principled guidance for design choices under resource constraints.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241952/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The algorithm (Algorithm 1) takes a single scalar — a computational budget in FLOP — and returns a complete training recipe: the optimal model size, data quantity, fine-tuning method (full fine-tuning or LoRA), and method-specific hyperparameters such as LoRA rank. The trained embedding model maps an input text sequence (context length 75 tokens in the main experiments) to a fixed-dimensional vector via mean pooling of last-layer hidden states, and is evaluated on a representative subset of the MTEB benchmark spanning eight task categories.

## Method

The paper conducts an extensive grid search over eight Pythia model sizes (14M–2.8B), six computational budgets (1.5e15–1.5e18 FLOP), and four fine-tuning methods (full fine-tuning, block freezing, bias-only tuning, and LoRA with ranks 8–2048), training on 200M query–value pairs from the BAAI BGE dataset. From the resulting IsoFLOP profiles, they fit a modified scaling law L(S, N, D) = C + (a_d·log D + b_d)/N^α + (a_s(1−S)^b_s + c_s)/D^β that incorporates the trainable parameter fraction S, extending the Hoffmann et al. formula to account for partial parameter updates. By fitting power-law trends to the per-method optimal loss on a log-log scale, they identify a crossover at 9.06e16 FLOP: below this budget full fine-tuning is optimal, above it LoRA (rank 32–128) dominates. The final recipe is a simple two-branch lookup that selects the method by budget, reads the optimal model size from the corresponding IsoFLOP figure, and computes the data quantity from the FLOP cost formula C = 6N_F·D (full) or the analogous LoRA expression.

## Results

The paper establishes a compute-optimal frontier for contrastive fine-tuning of decoder-only LLMs into embedding models. Below 9.06e16 FLOP, full fine-tuning achieves the lowest contrastive loss (scaling as ln(loss) = −0.21·ln(budget) + 8.39); above that threshold, LoRA becomes optimal (ln(loss) = −0.22·ln(budget) + 8.93). Bias-only tuning is consistently the weakest method, with a floor loss above 0.4. The Spearman rank correlation between final training loss and MTEB downstream performance is −0.892, confirming loss as a reliable proxy. Mean pooling (avg. MTEB score 0.47) substantially outperforms last-token pooling (0.36), and LoRA rank 32 or 128 is optimal across nearly all model sizes and budgets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares four fine-tuning strategies—full fine-tuning, block freezing, bias-only tuning, and LoRA—under matched IsoFLOP budgets across eight Pythia model sizes (14M–2.8B). Full fine-tuning dominates at lower budgets (≤9.06e16 FLOP), while LoRA surpasses it at higher budgets, making it the recommended method for compute-rich settings. Block freezing performs close to the optimal frontier but is never strictly best; for models ≤160M parameters, freezing any blocks hurts performance, whereas for larger models (≥1B), freezing 50–70% of blocks is near-optimal at most budgets. Bias-only tuning is uniformly the worst method, with its best achievable loss exceeding 0.4 compared to substantially lower losses for the other three methods. The authors also validate generalisability by showing that the scaling-law formula fitted on Pythia models closely predicts losses for Gemma 2B and Gemma 2 2B, and confirm robustness to larger batch sizes (2048) and context lengths (512 tokens).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the compute-optimal scaling law framework from Hoffmann et al. (Chinchilla, 2022) to the specific problem of contrastive fine-tuning decoder-only LLMs into text embedding models, extending the loss formula to incorporate the trainable parameter fraction S following Frantar et al. (2023). It departs from prior embedding model work (Neelakantan et al., Wang et al., Gao et al.) by systematically varying model size, data quantity, and fine-tuning method (full, LoRA, block freezing, bias-only) under fixed FLOP budgets rather than fixing a single configuration. The concurrent work by Fang et al. (2024) studies scaling laws for encoder-based retrieval models, whereas this paper targets decoder-only fine-tuning and explicitly accounts for parameter-efficient methods that alter the compute-cost formula.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a concrete, actionable algorithm (Algorithm 1) that maps a computational budget to an optimal fine-tuning method, model size, data quantity, and hyperparameters—directly useful for practitioners with limited resources. The finding that LoRA surpasses full fine-tuning above ~9e16 FLOP is a practical guideline that challenges the default assumption that full fine-tuning is always preferable with more compute. Key limitations include reliance on a single pre-training dataset (BAAI BGE), single-seed runs, validation on only one alternative model family (Gemma), and exclusion of inference cost and alternative pooling strategies, leaving open whether the recipe generalizes to models with different over-training ratios or to multi-stage training pipelines with hard negatives.
