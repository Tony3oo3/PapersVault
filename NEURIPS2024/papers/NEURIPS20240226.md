## Problem

Self-supervised blind image deblurring (BID) must train neural network generators without ground truth images, making over-fitting a critical risk. Existing self-supervised methods rely solely on a fitting loss (y = k⊗x), which is insufficient to resolve the inherent solution ambiguity of BID, and they lack an effective mechanism to exploit multi-scale structure because standard CNN/MLP representations are resolution-fixed. The paper identifies that the down-sampled blurred image cannot be modeled as a simple convolution of down-sampled image and kernel, which has prevented prior work from using exact cross-scale relations for regularization.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single blurred RGB image as input and jointly produces an estimated blur kernel (a non-negative, sum-to-one 2D array) and a deblurred latent image. The kernel is parameterized by a 3-layer SIREN MLP that maps normalized spatial coordinates to kernel values, while the image is generated by a 5-block U-Net INR with sinusoidal positional encoding of coordinates. Kernel sizes range from 5×5 (microscopy) to 131×131 (Köhler dataset), and the method is trained per-image with 5000 Adam iterations, requiring no external training data or pre-trained model.

## Method

The method reparameterizes both the blur kernel and latent image as implicit neural representations (INRs), exploiting their resolution-free property to generate estimates at arbitrary scales from a single set of network weights. The key theoretical contribution is a cross-scale consistency loss derived from Proposition 1, which establishes the exact identity (x↓₂)⊗(k↓₂) = ¼((x⊗k)↓₂ + Σ(x⊗g_d)↓₂) where {g_d} are quadrature mirror filters of k; this loss enforces consistency between estimates at consecutive dyadic scales and provides the regularization that the fitting loss alone cannot. Training follows a three-stage progressive coarse-to-fine schedule: initialization at the coarsest scale with fitting loss only, progressive refinement from coarse to fine with both fitting and cross-scale losses, and final tuning at the finest scale with fitting loss only. The cross-scale loss is computed in the frequency domain via DFT for efficiency, and the kernel generator includes a centering layer to stabilize training against positional shifts.

## Results

On the Lai et al. synthetic dataset (severe uniform blur), the proposed method achieves an average PSNR/SSIM of 25.16/0.879, outperforming the best self-supervised baseline VDIP (25.12/0.869), MCEM (24.55/0.800), and all supervised methods (best: Restormer* at 18.89/0.555). On the Köhler dataset with modest non-uniform blur, it reaches 30.69 PSNR / 0.942 MSSIM, surpassing MCEM (30.26/0.940) and the best supervised method Kaufman & Fattal (30.17/0.915). On the RealBlur real-world dataset, the method slightly trails the best supervised Restormer (28.31 vs. 28.96 PSNR on RealBlur-J) but outperforms all self-supervised and non-learning baselines. On microscopic deconvolution, it achieves 36.65/0.940 (Gaussian PSF) and 40.71/0.960 (Poisson PSF), approaching supervised INIKNet (37.32/0.941 and 41.56/0.961) while beating all self-supervised competitors.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method achieves the best average PSNR/SSIM (25.16/0.879) on the Lai et al. dataset, narrowly beating the closest self-supervised competitor VDIP (25.12/0.869) by 0.04 dB in PSNR and 0.010 in SSIM, and outperforming MCEM (24.55/0.800) by 0.61 dB. It surpasses all supervised methods by a large margin (best supervised Restormer* at 18.89/0.555, a gap of over 6 dB), which the authors attribute to supervised methods' inability to exploit the convolution physics prior. On the Köhler non-uniform blur dataset, the method leads all 17 compared methods with 30.69 PSNR / 0.942 MSSIM, edging out MCEM (30.26/0.940) and Kaufman & Fattal (30.17/0.915). On the RealBlur real-world dataset, the method trails the best supervised Restormer by ~0.6 dB (28.31 vs. 28.96 on RealBlur-J; 35.62 vs. 36.19 on RealBlur-R) but clearly outperforms all self-supervised baselines (MCEM: 28.17/35.46) and non-learning methods. For microscopic deconvolution, the method is within 0.7 dB of supervised INIKNet while beating all self-supervised methods (best: MCEM at 36.12/39.26). On the Levin dataset with small kernels, it achieves the best PSNR (33.74), SSIM (0.938), and Error Ratio (1.185) among all self-supervised and supervised methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the self-supervised blind image deblurring (BID) line initiated by SelfDeblur [47] and MCEM [33], which train two neural generators (kernel and image) via a fitting loss without ground truth. The key departure is the use of implicit neural representations (INRs) — building on SIRENs [53] and the first INR-based BID by Zhuang et al. [77] — whose resolution-free property enables a principled cross-scale consistency loss derived from quadrature mirror filter (QMF) theory (Proposition 1). Unlike prior self-supervised methods that rely solely on the Deep Image Prior [57] or early stopping for regularization, this work introduces an exact mathematical constraint linking estimates at consecutive dyadic scales, combined with a progressive coarse-to-fine training schedule that was previously impractical with resolution-fixed CNN/MLP generators.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The cross-scale consistency loss provides a theoretically grounded regularization that addresses the fundamental ill-posedness of BID without requiring paired data or pre-trained generative models, making the approach applicable in domains where ground truth is unavailable (e.g., microscopy, archival restoration). Two limitations remain: the per-image training cost (~213 s per 256×256 image) precludes batch processing, and the method is restricted to uniform blur due to its reliance on the convolution model. Open directions include meta-learning or test-time adaptation to amortize the per-image training cost, and extending the cross-scale framework to non-uniform blur where the convolution model breaks down.
