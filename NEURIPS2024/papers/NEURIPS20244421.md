## Problem

Existing query-efficient correlation clustering methods are limited to binary similarity {0,1} and assume access to a strong oracle returning exact values, while real-world similarities are real-valued and inherently noisy. Moreover, no polynomial-time algorithms existed for Pure Exploration in Combinatorial Multi-Armed Bandits (PE-CMAB) when the underlying offline optimization problem is NP-hard, which is the case for correlation clustering.

## Input / Output

The learner receives a set of n objects and can query any pair (x,y) to obtain a stochastic sample (Bernoulli or R-sub-Gaussian) with unknown mean s(x,y) ∈ [0,1]; the true similarity function is never directly observed. In the fixed-confidence setting the learner is given δ ∈ (0,1) and ε > 0 and must stop adaptively, while in the fixed-budget setting it is given a total query budget T and must allocate it across pairs. The output is a partition of V into clusters guaranteed to be a (5, ε)-approximate solution, i.e., cost_s(C_out) ≤ 5·OPT(s) + ε.

## Method

KC-FC (fixed confidence) first runs a novel threshold-bandits subroutine TB-HS that identifies pairs with similarity above 0.5+ε′ using LCB/UCB scores, deliberately tolerating misclassification of pairs near 0.5 to avoid the unbounded sample complexity of standard threshold bandits; the resulting high-similarity set is then fed into KwikCluster to produce the clustering. KC-FB (fixed budget) instead runs KwikCluster in phases, adaptively redistributing surplus query budget from pairs removed in earlier phases to the remaining pairs, so that critical pivot-neighbor pairs receive more samples than a uniform allocation would allow. Both algorithms achieve the same 5-approximation ratio as offline KwikCluster up to additive error ε, and are the first polynomial-time PE-CMAB algorithms whose offline subproblem is NP-hard, bypassing the exact-optimization requirement that invalidates standard LUCB-type stopping conditions.

## Results

On large-scale instances (n ≥ 1,000) in the fixed-budget setting, KC-FB achieves clustering costs of 218k (Email), 3,716k (ego-Facebook), and 10,222k (Wiki-Vote), consistently outperforming Uniform-FB (221k, 3,780k, 10,428k respectively). The gap to the oracle baseline KwikCluster with true similarity access (209k, 3,373k, 9,749k) is within approximately 4–10%, confirming that adaptive budget allocation recovers most of the information lost to noise. In the fixed-confidence setting, KC-FC's sample complexity is dramatically lower than Uniform-FC's O(n⁶/ε² log(n/δ)) bound, and its clustering cost is comparable to KwikCluster with full similarity access.

## Baselines & Comparisons

KC-FB outperforms Uniform-FB on all three large-scale datasets, with cost reductions of ~1.4% (Email), ~1.7% (ego-Facebook), and ~2.0% (Wiki-Vote), demonstrating that the adaptive budget redistribution strategy (allocating more queries to pairs that remain in later phases) yields better similarity estimates where they matter most. Against the oracle baseline KwikCluster(V,s) — which has access to the true unknown similarity — KC-FB incurs a modest overhead of 4.3% (Email), 10.2% (ego-Facebook), and 4.9% (Wiki-Vote), confirming that the noisy-feedback setting degrades quality only mildly. In the fixed-confidence setting, KC-FC's sample complexity is orders of magnitude smaller than Uniform-FC's (which is prohibitive even for n < 1,000), while its clustering cost matches KwikCluster with true similarity access within noise. The paper does not compare against the binary-similarity query-efficient methods of Bonchi et al. [11], Bressan et al. [15], or García-Soriano et al. [40], as those are restricted to {0,1} similarities and a strong oracle, making direct comparison inapplicable.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on KwikCluster (Ailon et al., 2008), the classic 5-approximation algorithm for correlation clustering, and threshold bandits (Kano et al., 2019), a multi-armed bandit variant for identifying arms above a threshold. It departs from prior query-efficient correlation clustering work (Bonchi et al., Bressan et al., García-Soriano et al.), which was restricted to binary similarity and a strong oracle returning exact values, and from Silwal et al. (2023), which introduced a noisy oracle but still for binary similarity. The key conceptual departure is from standard PE-CMAB algorithms (Chen et al., Du et al.), which all assume the underlying offline optimization is polynomial-time solvable; here the authors show that the specific threshold structure of KwikCluster (deciding above/below 0.5) allows a polynomial-time approach even when the full offline problem is APX-hard.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first set of polynomial-time algorithms for PE-CMAB where the offline problem is NP-hard, filling a gap that has blocked the application of pure exploration bandit methods to many combinatorial optimization problems. The instance-dependent sample complexity (governed by the gap from 0.5) provides a clean characterization of when query-efficient clustering is feasible. Open questions include deriving information-theoretic lower bounds for PE-CMAB with NP-hard offline problems, extending to heteroscedastic noise where variance differs across pairs, and investigating whether the 5-approximation ratio can be improved by combining with stronger offline algorithms. The practical impact is currently limited to moderate-scale instances (n < 1,000 in experiments) due to the quadratic number of pairs.
