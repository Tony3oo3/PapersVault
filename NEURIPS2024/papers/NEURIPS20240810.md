## Problem

Existing neural implicit surface reconstruction methods rely exclusively on rendering supervision, producing 3D shapes that are visually plausible but physically unstable when placed in a simulator. This stems from the absence of explicit physics constraints and from overly-smoothed optimization that fails to recover slender structures (e.g., chair legs) critical for object stability, compounded by multi-view and multi-modal inconsistencies in monocular geometric priors.

## Input / Output

The method takes N posed RGB images with corresponding per-object instance masks as input, and additionally consumes monocular depth maps (from Marigold) and surface normal maps (from Omnidata) as soft geometric priors. It outputs a compositional SDF representation with one SDF per foreground object plus a background SDF, from which explicit meshes can be extracted. The scene SDF is the element-wise minimum of all object SDFs, and the physical simulator operates on surface points extracted from individual object SDFs against the background as a supporting plane.

## Method

PHYRECON introduces a differentiable particle-based rigid-body simulator (built on DiffTaichi) that tracks object surface-point trajectories and contact states under gravity, contact, and friction, producing a physical loss that penalizes object displacement at contact points. A novel Surface Points Marching Cubes (SP-MC) algorithm differentiably converts SDF grids to explicit surface points via grid shifting, linear interpolation, and a refinement step that queries the SDF network, enabling gradient flow from the simulator back to the implicit representation. A joint uncertainty model predicts view-dependent depth and normal uncertainty (filtering inconsistent monocular priors) and maintains a 3D physical uncertainty grid updated from simulator contact trajectories, which modulates rendering losses and drives physics-guided pixel sampling to prioritize slender structures. Training follows a three-stage curriculum: rendering losses with rendering uncertainty first, then physical uncertainty and guided sampling, and finally the physical loss with a gradually increasing weight.

## Results

PHYRECON achieves the best reconstruction quality across all three datasets (ScanNet++, ScanNet, Replica) on every metric (CD, F-Score, NC) for both scene and object reconstruction. The headline result is a ≥40% improvement in Stability Ratio (SR) over all baselines: 78.16% on ScanNet++ (vs. 25.28% for ObjectSDF++), 70.31% on ScanNet (vs. 26.56%), and 77.63% on Replica (vs. 30.26%). On ScanNet++, the method reduces scene CD from 3.79 to 3.34 and object CD from 4.08 to 3.28, while improving object F-score from 86.32 to 87.21.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PHYRECON outperforms all three baselines (MonoSDF, RICO, ObjectSDF++) on every reconstruction metric across all three datasets. The most dramatic gap is in physical stability: PHYRECON achieves 78.16% SR on ScanNet++ versus 25.28% (ObjectSDF++) and 26.43% (RICO), a >50-point margin; on ScanNet the gap is 70.31% vs. 29.68% (RICO); on Replica it is 77.63% vs. 32.89% (RICO). In reconstruction quality, the gains are more modest but consistent: on ScanNet++, PHYRECON improves scene F-score by ~2.4 points over ObjectSDF++ (81.53 vs. 79.12) and object CD by 0.80 cm (3.28 vs. 4.08). MonoSDF, which does not perform object-level reconstruction, is only compared on scene metrics and is consistently outperformed. The paper attributes the stability gap to two factors: (1) the physical loss and physical uncertainty aligning reconstructed geometry with the ground plane, and (2) joint uncertainty modeling with physics-guided pixel sampling recovering thin structures (e.g., chair legs) that baselines miss, causing them to topple in Isaac Gym.

## Relation to Prior Work

PHYRECON extends the neural implicit surface reconstruction pipeline (NeuS, MonoSDF, RICO, ObjectSDF++) by introducing a differentiable physics simulation loop that directly backpropagates physical losses into the SDF network. The SP-MC algorithm is a targeted modification of classical Marching Cubes, replacing triangle topology extraction with differentiable surface point extraction via grid shifting and SDF refinement. The joint uncertainty modeling draws on view-dependent uncertainty estimation from NeRF literature (ActiveNeRF, CF-NeRF) but extends it with a physical uncertainty channel derived from simulator contact trajectories. Unlike prior physics-informed reconstruction work that applies collision penalties on bounding boxes or uses physics as a post-hoc evaluation, PHYRECON is the first to co-optimize rendering and rigid-body dynamics through a fully differentiable particle-based simulator (built on DiffTaichi) coupled to the implicit representation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Physically stable reconstructions are a prerequisite for downstream applications in embodied AI, robotics, and physics-based simulation, yet prior neural reconstruction methods produce objects that topple or deform under gravity. The 40%+ stability improvement (from ~25–33% to ~70–78% stability ratio) is practically significant for these applications. Key limitations include: only object-ground support is modeled (not general object-object contacts like a box on a cabinet), training time increases by ~40% (12.56h vs ~9h), and unobserved regions can develop degenerated bulges to satisfy the physical loss. Open questions include extending to general support relationships, incorporating topological regularization to prevent disconnected parts, and scaling to outdoor or dynamic scenes.
