## Problem

Existing backdoor attacks face a fundamental trade-off: sample-minimal-impact methods produce visible triggers detectable by sample-based defenses, while latent-space obfuscation methods (e.g., Adapt-Blend) sacrifice image fidelity, making poisoned samples easily identifiable. No prior method simultaneously achieves high attack effectiveness, high poisoned-sample fidelity, and latent-space stealthiness against both sample-based and latent-space-based detection.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images (3×32×32 for CIFAR-10/100/GTSRB, 3×224×224 for ImageNet-20) along with an adversary-specified target label and poisoning rates (p_a for payload, p_r for regularization). It outputs a backdoored classifier (e.g., ResNet-18) and a U-Net generator that produces sample-specific additive residuals. The trigger is not a fixed pattern but a per-sample residual injected exclusively into the HH (high-frequency) wavelet subband, with an asymmetric scaling coefficient α=1.0 during training and α=100.0 at inference to amplify trigger impact without degrading training-sample fidelity.

## Method

WaveAttack decomposes each image via Haar DWT into four subbands (LL, LH, HL, HH) and injects a learned residual only into the HH high-frequency component, exploiting the fact that DNNs learn high-frequency features effectively while humans remain insensitive to them. A U-Net generator g(HH; ω_g) produces sample-specific residuals, optimized with an L∞ norm to keep perturbations minimal. The classifier is trained jointly on payload samples (triggered, target label), regularization samples (triggered, original label), and benign samples using cross-entropy loss, following the Adapt-Blend training scheme. The key differentiator is asymmetric frequency obfuscation: a small coefficient α=1.0 during training preserves image fidelity and latent-space indistinguishability, while a large α=100.0 at inference amplifies the trigger's activation power to achieve near-100% ASR. This dual-coefficient strategy decouples stealth during training from effectiveness at inference, outperforming both frequency-domain (FTrojan, FIBA) and asymmetric-trigger (Adapt-Blend) baselines on PSNR, SSIM, IS, ASR, and BA across four benchmarks.

## Results

WaveAttack achieves 100% Attack Success Rate (ASR) on all four benchmark datasets (CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, GTSRB, ImageNet) while maintaining Benign Accuracy (BA) within 0.04–0.40% of the no-attack baseline (e.g., 94.55% vs. 94.59% on CIFAR-10, 99.30% vs. 99.00% on GTSRB). In terms of stealthiness, WaveAttack attains the best PSNR (47.49 dB on CIFAR-10, 50.12 dB on CIFAR-100, 45.60 dB on ImageNet), best SSIM (0.9979 on CIFAR-10, 0.9992 on CIFAR-100), and lowest Inception Score (0.011 on CIFAR-10, 0.005 on CIFAR-100, 0.007 on ImageNet) among all compared methods. The paper reports up to 28.27% improvement in PSNR, 1.61% improvement in SSIM, and 70.59% reduction in IS over the best competing method.

## Baselines & Comparisons

WaveAttack outperforms all nine baselines in BA across every dataset where comparisons are available, with the largest BA margins over LIRA (94.55 vs. 93.57 on CIFAR-10) and Fiba (94.55 vs. 93.80). The most dramatic ASR advantage is against Adapt-Blend (100% vs. 71.57% on CIFAR-10, 100% vs. 60.25% on GTSRB) and Fiba (100% vs. 75.40% on CIFAR-10, 100% vs. 85.18% on GTSRB), where both methods suffer significant effectiveness degradation. Against the closest frequency-domain competitor FTrojan, WaveAttack wins on BA by 0.26–1.50% across all four datasets while matching its 100% ASR. In stealthiness (Table 4), WaveAttack surpasses FTrojan in PSNR by 3.42 dB (CIFAR-10), 6.03 dB (CIFAR-100), and 10.05 dB (ImageNet), and reduces IS from 0.019 to 0.011 on CIFAR-10. Against LIRA, which achieves the second-best PSNR on CIFAR-10 (46.77 dB), WaveAttack still leads by 0.72 dB while matching its SSIM (0.9979) and halving its IS (0.011 vs. 0.019). Overall, WaveAttack is the only method that simultaneously achieves 100% ASR, near-untouched BA, and top-tier image fidelity on all four benchmarks.

## Relation to Prior Work

WaveAttack builds directly on the asymmetric trigger framework of Adapt-Blend (Qi et al., ICLR 2023), which introduced the use of regularization samples and asymmetric coefficients to obfuscate latent-space separation between poisoned and benign samples. It departs from prior frequency-domain attacks (FTrojan, FIBA) by replacing DCT with Discrete Wavelet Transform (Haar wavelet) to isolate the HH high-frequency band, arguing that DWT better captures edges and textures while enabling lossless reconstruction. The key conceptual departure is the simultaneous optimization of sample-level fidelity (via a U-Net generator producing minimal L∞-norm residuals) and latent-space stealth (via the asymmetric α coefficient), whereas prior methods addressed these two stealth dimensions separately.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that high-frequency wavelet components are a natural hiding place for backdoor triggers that are both imperceptible to humans and resistant to a broad suite of defenses (sample-based, latent-space-based, model-reconstruction, and trigger-recovery), suggesting that future backdoor defenses must account for frequency-domain manipulation. A notable limitation is the additional generator network required during training, which increases compute overhead, and the threat model assumes full control over the training process rather than dataset-only poisoning. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to non-image modalities (audio, video, text), whether the generator can be distilled or replaced with a closed-form residual, and whether the asymmetric α mechanism transfers to multi-target or multi-trigger settings.
