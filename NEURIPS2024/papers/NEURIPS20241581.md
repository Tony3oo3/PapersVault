## Problem

Learning Successor Features (SFs) from pixel-level observations in deep RL suffers from representation collapse under the canonical SF-TD loss, where basis features degenerate to a constant vector. Existing remedies—reconstruction losses, multi-phase pretraining, or expensive orthogonality constraints—add complexity, extra training phases, or computational overhead that impairs learning efficiency. The paper asks whether SFs can be learned directly from pixels during task engagement with a simple, minimal-overhead loss that avoids collapse by construction.

## Input / Output

The method consumes raw RGB pixel observations (84×84×3 in 2D Minigrid, 84×84 in 3D Miniworld and Mujoco) with no frame stacking or preprocessing beyond standard normalization. It produces three coupled outputs: L2-normalized basis features φ from the shared CNN encoder, action-dependent successor features ψ from an MLP conditioned on the task encoding vector w, and Q-value estimates computed as the dot product ψ^T w that drive an ε-greedy policy. The task encoding vector w (256-dim) is the sole parameter updated by the reward prediction loss, while all upstream network parameters are updated by the Q-SF-TD loss.

## Method

The architecture uses a shared CNN encoder whose L2-normalized output serves as basis features φ, while the raw latent h is concatenated with the task encoding vector w and passed through an MLP to produce one SF vector ψ per action. Training uses two losses: a reward prediction loss L_w = ½‖R_{t+1} − φ(S_{t+1})^T w‖² (with a stop-gradient on φ to decouple its learning from w) and a Q-SF-TD loss L_ψ = ½‖ŷ − ψ(S_t, A_t, w)^T w‖² where the bootstrap target ŷ = R_{t+1} + γ max_{a'} ψ̄(S_{t+1}, a', w)^T w comes from a periodically updated target network. The key insight is that because ŷ and R_{t+1} are state-dependent (not constants), the combined losses cannot be minimized by collapsing φ to a constant—unlike the canonical SF-TD loss, for which the paper provides a proof-by-contradiction showing collapse is a valid zero-loss solution. A theoretical proposition further establishes that minimizing these two losses also minimizes the canonical universal SF-TD loss, confirming the learned representations are true SFs. The stop-gradient on φ in the reward prediction loss is shown to be critical: without it, concurrent optimization of φ and w drives φ toward zero, reintroducing collapse.

## Results

The paper evaluates Simple SF against five baselines in continual RL settings across 2D Minigrid, 3D Miniworld (Four Rooms), and Mujoco, using pixel observations only. In Mujoco (Figure 4), Simple SF consistently outperforms all baselines in both Task 1 and Task 2 across three scenarios (running backwards, running faster, and domain switch from half-cheetah to walker). In the 3D Slippery Four Rooms environment, when stochasticity is high (slippery probability ≥ 0.3), all other SF methods fail to learn effectively in the second task while Simple SF continues to perform well. In terms of efficiency (Figure 8e–g), Simple SF reaches the performance threshold in fewer steps, achieves higher frames-per-second during training, and completes two exposures of two tasks in shorter total wall-clock time than all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Double DQN [Van Hasselt et al., 2016], Simple SF matches or exceeds performance in 2D Minigrid and 3D Four Rooms, and clearly outperforms it in Mujoco continual RL, while also demonstrating better transfer across task transitions. Against SF+Reconstruction [Machado et al., 2020] and SF+Orthogonality [Touati et al., 2022], Simple SF shows superior continual learning performance; the paper notes that 'constraints like reconstruction and orthogonality on basis features can impede learning,' and these agents 'clearly struggle to learn effectively' in later tasks. Against APS Pre-train [Liu and Abbeel, 2021], Simple SF is not directly compared in Mujoco (APS is excluded because it 'struggles in the Continual RL setting, even in simpler environments'), but in 2D/3D settings Simple SF shows better transfer. Against SF+Random Features [Touati et al., 2022], Simple SF outperforms in allocentric settings where feature sparsity is greater. Against SF+Q-TD+Reward [Janz et al., 2019] (Appendix K), Simple SF avoids the learning instabilities that the three-loss agent exhibits, particularly in stochastic environments where simultaneous optimization of all three losses 'significantly impedes the agent's ability to learn effectively.'

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Successor Features framework (Barreto et al., 2017) and the Universal SF formulation (Borsa et al., 2018), which decompose the value function into a task-encoding vector and a transition-dynamics representation. It departs from prior work by showing that the canonical SF-TD loss provably admits representation collapse as a degenerate solution, and that the additional constraints previously proposed to prevent collapse (reconstruction losses, orthogonality penalties, pretraining phases, multi-loss schemes) are unnecessary. Instead, the paper argues that a standard Q-value TD loss combined with a reward prediction loss—both standard RL objectives—suffices to learn valid SFs from pixels, with a stop-gradient on the basis features as the sole architectural safeguard.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is a streamlined, single-phase training procedure for deep SFs that avoids the computational overhead and hyperparameter complexity of prior multi-loss or pretraining pipelines, while matching or exceeding their performance across 2D grid worlds, 3D mazes, and Mujoco continuous control. The proof by contradiction establishing representation collapse in the canonical SF-TD loss provides a clear theoretical explanation for a long-observed failure mode. Limitations include evaluation exclusively in simulated environments and an assumption of good pixel quality; open questions include whether the approach scales to high-dimensional real-world robotics or language-grounded tasks, and whether the stop-gradient design choice generalizes to other representation-learning settings beyond SFs.
