## Problem

Standard RLHF aligns LLMs by maximizing expected reward in a risk-neutral manner, allowing positive trajectory scores to offset negative ones and leaving rare but high-stakes toxic outputs insufficiently constrained. This paper asks whether LLMs can be fine-tuned to be explicitly risk-averse—minimizing the worst-case tail of the reward distribution rather than just the mean—so that even adversarial or highly negative prompts elicit safe completions.

## Input / Output

The model receives a short text prefix (up to 64 tokens for IMDB-Gen, 8 for Jigsaw-Gen, 32 for RealToxicityPrompts-Gen) that may carry negative sentiment or toxicity, and must generate a continuation (up to 48, 32, or 32 tokens respectively) that is positive or non-toxic. A separate frozen scoring model (lvwerra/distilbert-imdb for sentiment, unitary/toxic-bert for toxicity) assigns a scalar reward to the full prompt-plus-completion sequence, which serves as the sparse episode return in the MDP formulation.

## Method

RA-RLHF replaces the expected-reward objective of standard RLHF with a CVaR-based risk-averse objective, implemented via a soft-risk scheduling mechanism over the PPO training loop. For the first i₀ iterations the full batch of B trajectories is used (ensuring the policy still learns to produce good outputs broadly); thereafter the effective batch B₀ is linearly reduced from B to ⌈αB⌉, so that only the worst-returning trajectories drive policy updates, progressively focusing optimization on the riskiest prompts. The per-token reward is the standard KL-regularized dense reward r(s,a) − β·log(π_θ/π_ref), with β adapted by a log-space proportional controller. The actor is the SFT-initialized transformer with an LM head and the critic is the same backbone with a value head, both trained with PPO and GAE. This design requires no additional reward or preference models beyond the single task-specific scorer already used in standard RLHF.

## Results

On IMDB-Gen (GPT-2), RA-RLHF achieves a tail sentiment score of -0.44, substantially outperforming RLHF (-1.16), Quark (-2.008), and the base GPT-2 (-2.607) on the worst-case prompts (score ≤ -2.5). On Jigsaw-Gen, RA-RLHF reaches a negative toxicity score of 2.06 vs. 1.69 for RLHF and 1.52 for Quark; on RealToxicityPrompts-Gen it reaches 2.83 vs. 2.56 for RLHF and 2.59 for Quark. Scaling to GPT-J (6B) on IMDB-Gen, RA-RLHF improves tail reward from -1.51 (RLHF) to -1.11, with only a marginal perplexity increase (23.03 vs. 22.13).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RA-RLHF outperforms all three primary baselines across every task and metric. Against standard RLHF (Ziegler et al., 2019), RA-RLHF improves tail sentiment from -1.16 to -0.44 on IMDB-Gen, negative toxicity from 1.69 to 2.06 on Jigsaw-Gen, and from 2.56 to 2.83 on RealToxicityPrompts-Gen, with the gap widening specifically on the riskiest prompt quantiles. Against Quark (Lu et al., 2022), the reinforced-unlearning method, RA-RLHF leads by a large margin on IMDB-Gen (-0.44 vs. -2.008) and by a moderate margin on both toxicity tasks (2.06 vs. 1.52 on Jigsaw; 2.83 vs. 2.59 on RealToxicityPrompts). Against DExperts (Liu et al., 2021), the decoding-time expert/anti-expert method, RA-RLHF dominates on sentiment (-0.44 vs. -2.635) and toxicity, while DExperts shows lower text diversity (Dist-3 of 0.824 vs. 0.947). The key differentiator is that RA-RLHF's advantage is concentrated in the tail of the prompt reward distribution—precisely the rare, high-stakes scenarios that risk-neutral RLHF and the other baselines handle poorly.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper transplants the CVaR-based risk-averse reinforcement learning framework (Tamar et al., 2015; Greenberg et al., 2022) into the LLM fine-tuning pipeline, specifically replacing the risk-neutral expected-reward objective of standard RLHF (Ziegler et al., 2019) with a Conditional Value at Risk objective that explicitly targets the worst-case tail of the reward distribution. The key adaptation is a soft-risk scheduling mechanism—gradually reducing the effective batch size to focus on the lowest-return episodes—that borrows directly from CeSoR (Greenberg et al., 2022) but is applied to the PPO-based RLHF loop rather than a generic MDP. Unlike Safe-RLHF (Dai et al., 2023), which constrains the *expected* harmfulness score and requires two separate reward models, this approach operates on a single reward model and directly penalizes the probability mass in the toxic tail, making positive trajectories unable to offset negative ones.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The risk-averse framing offers a more principled alternative to expectation-based safety constraints: by optimizing CVaR, the model is explicitly trained to avoid rare but high-stakes toxic outputs rather than merely keeping the average toxicity low. However, the experimental evidence is somewhat limited in scope—experiments are confined to GPT-2 (117M) and GPT-J (6B) on three generative tasks (sentiment completion, toxicity mitigation), with GPT-J results from a single seed and only 70% of training data due to compute constraints. Open questions include whether the approach scales to 70B+ models, whether it generalizes to conversational or Q&A settings, and whether the perplexity trade-off (consistently higher than RLHF) becomes problematic for longer generations or more complex tasks.
