## Problem

Existing generalizable 3D Gaussian Splatting methods rely on heavy transformer backbones for dense multi-view matching, making them computationally intractable for long input sequences and confining supervision to narrow-range view interpolation. This results in poorly localized 3D Gaussians that degrade into floaters under extrapolated views, and simple concatenation of per-view Gaussians introduces significant redundancy in overlapping regions, preventing accurate free-view synthesis across wide view ranges.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243408/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a sparse sequence of posed RGB images (resized to 384×512) with known camera poses, supporting an arbitrary number of input views (2–8 during training, up to 30+ at inference) without requiring depth priors. It outputs a global set of 3D Gaussian primitives (centers, covariances, opacities, spherical harmonic coefficients) that can be rasterized from any novel camera pose to produce photorealistic color images and depth maps. The PTF module reduces the Gaussian count by ~55% compared to naive concatenation, enabling real-time rendering at 72 FPS on a single GPU.

## Method

FreeSplat replaces transformer-based backbones with efficient CNN encoders (EfficientNet/ResNet) and constructs adaptive cost volumes between pose-proximate nearby views via plane sweep stereo, then fuses multi-scale features through a UNet++-like decoder to predict depth maps and pixel-aligned Gaussian triplets (3D centers, weights, latent features). A Pixel-wise Triplet Fusion (PTF) module progressively projects accumulated global Gaussians onto each new view, identifies pixel-wise correspondences within a depth threshold, and fuses them via weighted center averaging and a lightweight GRU for latent feature aggregation, eliminating redundancy while preserving multi-view observations. A Free-View Training (FVT) strategy randomly samples 2–8 context views per iteration and supervises rendering on broader view interpolations, adding stricter geometric constraints that improve depth accuracy and decouple performance from a fixed input view count. The entire pipeline is trained end-to-end with photometric losses (MSE + LPIPS) only, requiring no depth supervision.

## Results

On ScanNet with 2 input views, FreeSplat-spec achieves 28.08 PSNR, 0.837 SSIM, and 0.211 LPIPS, outperforming MVSplat (27.27/0.822/0.221) and pixelSplat (26.03/0.784/0.265) while using only 278k Gaussians vs. 393k and 1180k respectively. With 3 views, FreeSplat-spec reaches 27.45 PSNR and 0.222 LPIPS, maintaining a clear margin over MVSplat (26.68/0.235) and pixelSplat (25.76/0.270). For long-sequence (10-view) reconstruction, FreeSplat-fv achieves 25.90 PSNR on view interpolation and 24.64 PSNR on view extrapolation, substantially surpassing MVSplat (22.78/21.60) and pixelSplat (21.26/20.70). Inference time is 0.103s (2 views) and 0.596s (10 views), roughly 300× faster than NeuRay.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FreeSplat consistently outperforms both pixelSplat and MVSplat across all view counts (2, 3, 10) on PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS, inference time, and Gaussian count. Against pixelSplat, FreeSplat-spec gains +2.05 dB PSNR (2 views) and +1.69 dB (3 views) while using 4.2× fewer Gaussians (278k vs. 1180k) and 2.8× faster inference. Against MVSplat, the PSNR advantage is +0.81 dB (2 views) and +0.77 dB (3 views), with 30% fewer Gaussians and 12–37% faster encoding. NeuRay achieves marginally higher SSIM (0.840 vs. 0.837 at 2 views) but is 300× slower (3.1s vs. 0.103s) and has worse PSNR and LPIPS. In the long-sequence (10-view) setting, FreeSplat-fv leads MVSplat by +3.12 dB PSNR on interpolation and +3.04 dB on extrapolation, demonstrating that prior methods degrade significantly under wide view ranges due to poorly localized Gaussians. Against SurfelNeRF (Appendix), FreeSplat-fv achieves 27.06 PSNR vs. 24.20 and 224 FPS vs. 5 FPS, confirming superiority in both quality and speed for indoor scene reconstruction.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the generalizable 3D Gaussian Splatting line initiated by pixelSplat and MVSplat, which predict pixel-aligned Gaussians in a feed-forward manner but are confined to 2–3 view interpolation due to their quadratically-scaling transformer backbones. FreeSplat departs from this lineage by replacing patch-wise transformers with pure CNN backbones (EfficientNet/ResNet) and adaptive plane-sweep cost volumes between nearby views, making long-sequence feature matching computationally tractable. The Pixel-wise Triplet Fusion module and Free-View Training strategy are novel contributions that address Gaussian redundancy and decouple model performance from a fixed number of input views, respectively, drawing on ideas from TSDF fusion and recurrent spatio-temporal aggregation in prior depth estimation work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FreeSplat demonstrates that feed-forward, depth-prior-free reconstruction of large indoor scenes from long image sequences is feasible with 3DGS, opening a path toward real-time free-view rendering without per-scene optimization or RGB-D sensors. The ~55% reduction in redundant Gaussians via PTF and the ability to process 30+ views where pixelSplat exhausts GPU memory are practically significant for scalable scene reconstruction. However, the unsupervised depth estimation still lags behind RGB-D or 3D-supervised methods on textureless and specular regions, and GPU memory exceeds 40 GB for sequences longer than ~50 views, leaving open the question of whether this approach can scale to truly large-scale or outdoor environments.
