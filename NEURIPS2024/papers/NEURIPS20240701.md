## Problem

Transformers fail to generalize arithmetic tasks such as integer addition to sequences longer than those encountered during training, even though humans length-generalize by understanding the underlying algorithm. The paper asks whether the known structural regularity of a task (e.g., digit significance in addition) can be directly injected into a Transformer's positional encoding to enable automatic length generalization without any additional training on longer sequences.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240701/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes sequences of individual digit tokens (0–9) interleaved with arithmetic operators (+, =) and a shared BOS/EOS token ($), formatted with zero-padding to equalize operand lengths and a reversed response so the model predicts digits from least to most significant. For addition the input is e.g. "$653+049=2070$" and the model performs next-token prediction on the reversed sum; for N×2 multiplication the response is reversed and zero-padded to length N+2. The 2D minesweeper variant flattens a rectangular board into a 1D sequence and applies two independent position-coupling modules (one per spatial axis) before feeding the sequence to the Transformer.

## Method

Position coupling modifies the learned absolute positional embedding by assigning the same position ID to tokens that share semantic relevance within the task: for addition, digits at the same significance across both operands and the sum receive identical position IDs, while the starting ID is randomized during training (up to a max_pos hyperparameter) so every position embedding vector is trained. Unlike index hinting, which doubles sequence length by inserting separate marker tokens and incurs ~4× computational overhead, position coupling merges position and token information into a single embedding vector, letting the model exploit the structural coupling at no extra cost. Theoretically, the paper constructs a 1-layer 2-head Transformer with coupled positions that provably solves addition for operands of length exponential in the embedding dimension, and proves that any 1-layer Transformer without positional encoding cannot solve any permutation-sensitive task. Empirically, a 1-layer 4-head model trained on 1–30-digit additions achieves >95% exact-match accuracy up to 200-digit additions (6.67× extrapolation), and the approach extends to N×2 multiplication (2-layer construction) and a 2D minesweeper generator task.

## Results

With position coupling, a 1-layer 4-head decoder-only Transformer trained on 1–30-digit additions achieves >95% exact-match (EM) accuracy up to 200-digit additions, a 6.67× extrapolation of the trained operand length. The generalizable length scales with training length: 70 digits (trained 1–10), 135 digits (trained 1–20), and 200 digits (trained 1–30 or 1–40). Theoretically, a 1-layer 2-head Transformer with coupled positions provably solves addition for operand lengths up to 2^⌊(d−17)/2⌋ − 2 (exponential in embedding dimension d). For N×2 multiplication, a 2-layer model with position coupling generalizes beyond trained lengths, while for the 2D minesweeper task, position coupling maintains >98% accuracy at board width 12 (trained on widths 5–9).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Position coupling decisively outperforms all baselines on the addition task. Against NoPE (Kazemnejad et al., 2023), which the authors argue should implicitly capture positional information via the causal decoder, position coupling achieves >95% EM at 200 digits while NoPE fails to length-generalize at all, even with 6-layer 8-head models. Against random-start APE (mimicking standard APE with packing/shifting), position coupling again wins by a wide margin: random-start APE cannot reliably generalize beyond trained lengths, and shallower models (1-layer) struggle even to train. Against index hinting (Zhou et al., 2024a), which places position markers as separate tokens in the input sequence, position coupling achieves a generalizable length of 200 versus ≤50 for index hinting, and index hinting completely fails from length 70 onward. The paper attributes index hinting's inferiority to the doubled input sequence length (requiring ~4× training time/memory) and the additional burden of predicting hint tokens in the response. The authors also note that their 6-layer models show slight performance degradation compared to 1–5 layer models, hypothesizing a bad implicit bias in deeper models on low-complexity tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the input-format innovations of Lee et al. (2024) (reversed response, zero-padding, BOS/EOS wrapping) and positions itself against the broader length-generalization literature, including NoPE (Kazemnejad et al., 2023), index hinting (Zhou et al., 2024a), and robustness analyses (Zhou et al., 2024b). Its key conceptual departure is to inject task structure directly into the positional encoding by assigning identical position IDs to semantically relevant tokens (e.g., same-significance digits), rather than augmenting the input sequence or relying on standard APE/RoPE. The theoretical contributions—constructive 1-layer/2-head addition Transformers and an impossibility result for NoPE—extend the line of work on provable Transformer capabilities (Yun et al., 2020; Awasthi and Gupta, 2023) to the length-generalization setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Position coupling provides a simple, drop-in modification to learned APE that yields robust 6.67× length generalization (30→200 digits) with a 1-layer model, addressing a problem that prior methods (index hinting, random-start APE) could not solve reliably. The constructive theoretical proof that a 1-layer 2-head Transformer handles exponentially long additions, combined with the clean impossibility result for NoPE, gives a principled explanation for why the method works. Key limitations include the requirement that task structure be known a priori (making it inapplicable to implicit or black-box tasks) and the open question of whether appropriate couplings can be discovered automatically. The paper also leaves challenging extensions (varying operand counts, full N×M multiplication) for future work.
