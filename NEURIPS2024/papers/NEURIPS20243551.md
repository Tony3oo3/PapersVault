## Problem

In existing VLMs, perception (extracting visual information) and reasoning (generating answers from that information) are tightly entangled, making it impossible to independently diagnose which capability limits a model's performance. Proprietary VLMs are opaque black boxes, and open-source VLMs produce visual embeddings that are difficult to interpret, so no systematic way exists to measure a VLM's true perceptual ability versus its reasoning ability in isolation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243551/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework takes an RGB image and a text query as input, along with an instruction that is either generic (a fixed prompt asking for basic visual description) or query-specific (an incremental instruction generated by the reasoning LLM based on the question). The perception VLM outputs a free-form textual description of visual content (capped at 512 tokens, greedy decoding), which is then concatenated with the original question to form a reformatted query. The reasoning LLM consumes this reformatted text-only query and produces a final answer (e.g., a multiple-choice selection). When the LLM refuses to answer due to insufficient visual clues, the system records a perception failure rather than applying post-processing.

## Method

Prism decomposes visual question answering into two independently swappable modules: a perception stage where a VLM converts image content into natural-language description, and a reasoning stage where an LLM answers the question from that text alone. As an evaluation framework, fixing the reasoning LLM (ChatGPT-3.5) and varying the perception VLM isolates perceptual capability, while fixing the VLM and varying the LLM isolates reasoning capability. As an efficient solver, the paper trains PrismCaptioner (a ~2B LLaVA with SigLip-S0400M vision encoder and InternLM2-1.8B language encoder) via QLoRA on ALLaVA-Caption-4V and Evol-Instruct-GPT4-Turbo-143K, demonstrating that a lightweight perception module paired with a powerful external LLM (ChatGPT-3.5 or Llama-3-70B) matches or exceeds VLMs 10× larger on MMStar, MMMU, MathVista, and AI2D. The key empirical finding driving this design is that perception quality is largely independent of language-model scale, so the expensive reasoning capacity can be outsourced to a separate LLM without sacrificing visual understanding.

## Results

On MMStar, PrismCaptioner-7B-Llama3 achieves 45.9% overall accuracy, surpassing all 7B–13B open-source VLMs (LLaVA-NeXT Vicuna-13B: 40.4%, Mistral-7B: 38.4%) and approaching LLaVA-NeXT (Yi-34B) at 51.6%. The 2B variant with ChatGPT (PrismCaptioner-2B-ChatGPT) reaches 43.3% on MMStar, outperforming VLMs 10× larger such as LLaVA-InternLM2-20B (41.9%), Yi-VL-34B (40.5%), and Emu2-Chat (40.7%). On MMMU, PrismCaptioner-7B-Llama3 attains 53.3%, exceeding InternVL-Chat-v1.5 (46.8%) and LLaVA-NeXT (Yi-34B) (48.8%), and nearly matching GPT-4v (53.8%). On MathVista(F), PrismCaptioner-7B-Llama3 scores 27.3%, beating all 7B–13B baselines (range: 17.1–21.3%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PrismCaptioner-7B-Llama3 (45.9% MMStar) decisively outperforms all 7B–13B open-source VLMs: LLaVA-NeXT Vicuna-13B (40.4%), Mistral-7B (38.4%), Vicuna-7B (37.6%), and LLaVA-v1.5-13B (34.3%), but trails the 34B LLaVA-NeXT (Yi-34B) by 5.7 points and InternVL-Chat-v1.5 by 11.2 points. On MMMU, PrismCaptioner-7B-Llama3 (53.3%) surpasses InternVL-Chat-v1.5 (46.8%) and LLaVA-NeXT (Yi-34B) (48.8%) by 6.5 and 4.5 points respectively, and nearly matches GPT-4v (53.8%). The headline 2B result—PrismCaptioner-2B-ChatGPT at 43.3% MMStar—beats three VLMs 10× or more its size: LLaVA-InternLM2-20B (41.9%), Yi-VL-34B (40.5%), and Emu2-Chat (40.7%). On MathVista(F), PrismCaptioner-7B-Llama3 (27.3%) exceeds LLaVA-NeXT Mistral-7B (21.3%) and Vicuna-7B (17.1%) but remains below GPT-4v (32.0%) and InternLM-XComposer2 (49.0%). On AI2D(F), PrismCaptioner-7B-Llama3 (37.2%) edges out LLaVA-NeXT Vicuna-13B (36.2%) and DeepSeek-VL-7B (36.9%) but falls well short of InternLM-XComposer2 (57.5%) and GPT-4o (59.7%). Overall, Prism's advantage is most pronounced on reasoning-heavy benchmarks (MMMU, MathVista) where the external LLM compensates for the small VLM's limited language capacity, while on pure perception tasks (AI2D) the gap to top proprietary models remains substantial.

## Relation to Prior Work

Prism builds on the LLaVA architecture for its perception module but fundamentally departs from the end-to-end VLM paradigm by explicitly decoupling visual perception (handled by a VLM) from textual reasoning (handled by a separate LLM). Conceptually, it extends the line of VLM capability evaluation work (MMBench, MME, MMMU, POPE, HallusionBench) by providing a modular, model-agnostic pipeline that isolates perception from reasoning rather than measuring them jointly. It also relates to prior capability-breakdown studies (e.g., Zhang et al.'s RPM analysis, InfiMM-Eval) but offers a more systematic and generalizable framework applicable to any VLM-LLM combination across diverse domains.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that perception capability is largely independent of language model size (particularly for LLaVA-v1.5) and that a 2B VLM paired with a strong LLM can match VLMs 10× larger on MMStar challenges the prevailing assumption that scaling the LLM component is the primary lever for VLM improvement. This opens a practical path toward cost-efficient multimodal deployment where expensive large VLMs can be replaced by lightweight captioners plus API-based reasoning. Key limitations include reliance on textual descriptions that may fail for abstract or aesthetic visual content, evaluation restricted to a select set of models, and unexplored domain-specific tuning (medical, GUI, diagrams) that could further narrow or widen the observed gaps.
