## Problem

While Medical Large Vision Language Models (Med-LVLMs) show promising diagnostic performance, their trustworthiness remains largely unverified for safe clinical deployment. Existing evaluations focus on isolated dimensions such as diagnostic accuracy, leaving a systematic, multi-dimensional assessment of trustworthiness—encompassing factuality, fairness, safety, privacy, and robustness—largely unexplored.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20244454/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark takes medical images spanning 16 modalities (X-ray, CT, MRI, fundus, dermatoscopy, pathology, etc.) and 27 anatomical regions, paired with 41K question-answer items in both closed-ended (yes/no, multi-choice) and open-ended formats. Outputs are structured as trustworthiness scores across five dimensions: factuality accuracy and overconfidence ratios, demographic accuracy differences by age/gender/race, jailbreaking success and toxicity scores, privacy abstention rates, and OOD robustness metrics under noise or cross-modality shifts. Open-ended responses are scored via GPT-4 on a 1–10 scale normalized against reference answers, while toxicity is measured using the Perspective API.

## Method

CARES curates 18K images and 41K QA pairs from seven open-source medical datasets, constructing closed-ended questions from classification labels and reports, and open-ended questions generated by GPT-4 from medical reports with a self-checking round. The evaluation framework assesses six models (LLaVA-Med, Med-Flamingo, MedVInT, RadFM, LLaVA-v1.6, Qwen-VL-Chat) across five trustworthiness dimensions using tailored protocols: uncertainty estimation via appended confidence prompts, three jailbreaking scenarios (concealment, exaggeration, incorrect treatment advice), toxicity induction scored by the Perspective API, zero-shot and few-shot privacy queries targeting protected health information (marital status, SSN), and OOD robustness via Gaussian noise injection and cross-modality testing. The key contribution is the first holistic, standardized benchmark that simultaneously evaluates all five dimensions, revealing that current Med-LVLMs are unreliable across the board—exhibiting factual hallucinations, demographic biases, vulnerability to attacks, privacy leaks, and near-zero OOD abstention.

## Results

Across all seven datasets, every evaluated Med-LVLM achieves factuality accuracy below 50%, with LLaVA-Med leading at 40.39% average, followed by MedVInT (39.31%), Qwen-VL-Chat (33.84%), LLaVA-v1.6 (32.28%), Med-Flamingo (29.02%), and RadFM (27.51%). On uncertainty estimation, Qwen-VL-Chat achieves the best uncertainty-based accuracy (50.67%) and lowest overconfidence ratio (16.96%), while Med-Flamingo and RadFM show the worst overconfidence (59.11% and 58.53% respectively). On privacy, Med-LVLMs show near-zero abstention rates (0–2.71% zero-shot) compared to generic LVLMs (10.37–14.02%), and on OOD robustness, abstention rates remain below 7.31% even under significant noise or unfamiliar modalities.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LLaVA-Med achieves the highest average factuality accuracy (40.39%) among Med-LVLMs but is notably weak on skin (HAM10000: 35.55%) and heart CT (OL3I: 34.70%) images, while MedVInT excels on yes/no questions (IU-Xray: 73.34%, OL3I: 61.90%) yet lags on open-ended tasks (PMC-OA: 25.72%). Among generic LVLMs, Qwen-VL-Chat outperforms all Med-LVLMs on uncertainty estimation (50.67% accuracy, 16.96% overconfidence) and privacy abstention (10.37% zero-shot), despite lower factuality scores. On jailbreaking resistance, LLaVA-Med is the only model with a meaningful abstention rate (30.17%), whereas Med-Flamingo, MedVInT, and RadFM show 0% abstention. On fairness, Qwen-VL-Chat and MedVInT demonstrate the most balanced performance across racial groups (demographic accuracy difference ≤ 0.62 on OL3I), while RadFM shows the largest disparity (5.21 on OL3I, 1.11 on MIMIC-CXR). No single model dominates across all five trustworthiness dimensions, highlighting the trade-offs between safety (LLaVA-Med's overcautiousness with 90% abstention on medical advice) and utility.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the trustworthiness evaluation paradigm from general LVLMs (hallucination benchmarks like POPE, safety benchmarks like MMBench-Safety) to the medical domain, and further generalizes from single-dimension assessments (e.g., accuracy-only evaluations in MultiMedEval [51] or Asclepius [68]) to a unified five-dimensional framework covering trustfulness, fairness, safety, privacy, and robustness. The evaluation methodology draws on LLM trustworthiness work (DecodingTrust [66], R-Tuning for uncertainty [92]) and LVLM safety protocols [63], adapting them to medical imaging contexts. The benchmark is constructed from existing open-source medical datasets (MIMIC-CXR, IU-Xray, HAM10000, OL3I, OmniMedVQA, PMC-OA, Harvard-FairVLMed) rather than introducing new data collection, positioning it as a curation and standardization effort.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CARES provides the first standardized, multi-dimensional trustworthiness benchmark for Med-LVLMs, offering a critical reference point before clinical deployment of these models. The finding that all evaluated models fall below 50% accuracy on factuality and exhibit near-zero OOD abstention rates underscores that current Med-LVLMs are not yet deployment-ready, which has immediate implications for healthcare AI policy and development priorities. Key open questions include whether scaling model size or incorporating retrieval-augmented generation (as explored in the authors' related work RULE [80]) can close the trustworthiness gaps, how to resolve the safety-overcautiousness trade-off exemplified by LLaVA-Med, and whether the GPT-4-as-judge protocol for open-ended questions introduces systematic bias. Limitations include evaluation of only four 7B-scale Med-LVLMs and reliance on a limited set of source datasets.
