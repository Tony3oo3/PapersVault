## Problem

Multi-label active learning struggles with large, sparse label spaces where rare labels have few positive instances, making both prediction and uncertainty estimation difficult. Existing mixture-of-Bernoulli approaches either disconnect label correlations from input features (Conditional Bernoulli Mixtures) or rely on expensive Gaussian Process inference with superficial uncertainty (GP-B²M), leaving a gap in efficiently capturing label correlations while providing fine-grained, actionable uncertainty for sample selection.

## Input / Output

The model takes a feature vector x ∈ R^M and a small initial labeled set S_L, operating over an unlabeled pool S_U. It produces a multi-label prediction vector ŷ, per-component weight coefficients π with associated NIG evidence parameters (γ, ν, α, β), and instance-specific proxy pseudo counts that adjust the global Beta-distributed label clusters. The active learning strategy outputs a composite multi-source uncertainty (MSU) score combining epistemic uncertainty from the evidential head, cosine-similarity shift in label clusters, and log-determinant of the predicted label covariance, used to select the next batch of 100 samples per round.

## Method

EMM uses a shared encoder e(x) feeding two decoders: an evidential regression head g_π that predicts mixture weight coefficients under a Normal Inverse Gamma prior (yielding a Student-t predictive distribution with decomposable aleatoric/epistemic uncertainty), and a proxy pseudo-count head g_μ that outputs per-instance corrections to the Beta parameters of K unsupervisedly learned Bernoulli label clusters. Training alternates between evidential fitting of π to EM-learned initial clusters and joint multi-label training with a soft-margin loss, where the proxy pseudo counts are added to the initial Beta parameters to enable instance-level adjustment of label clusters—breaking the ceiling effect that limits rare-label predictions to max_k μ_kl. The AL selection score A(x) = A_πk(x) + λA_μ(x) + ηA_ŷ(x) integrates three uncertainty sources: epistemic uncertainty from the NIG posterior, the cosine dissimilarity between current and proxy-updated label clusters, and the log-determinant of the predicted label covariance (justified via a multivariate CLT argument for the mixture). This design maintains global label clusters (unlike CBM) while allowing per-instance adaptation (unlike GP-B²M), and replaces expensive GP inference with a single forward pass.

## Results

On four real-world multi-label datasets (Delicious, Corel 5k, BibTex, NUS-WIDE), EMM achieves micro-AUC scores of 0.8021, 0.8063, 0.8669, and 0.8625 respectively, and macro-AUC scores of 0.7256, 0.6613, 0.8153, and 0.6396. In active learning experiments (5 rounds × 100 samples), EMM consistently outperforms all baselines in AU-ROC from the first round onward, with the advantage growing as more labels are acquired. For rare labels (50 rarest per dataset), EMM shows significant Average Precision Improvement (API) over GP-B²M, with the largest gains on the most infrequent labels. The multi-source uncertainty (MSU) selection strategy outperforms a simple entropy-based selection (EMM-entropy) using the same model, confirming the value of the combined evidential + covariance + cluster-difference scoring.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EMM outperforms all six baselines (GP-B²M, MMC, Adaptive, CVIRS, AUDI, CS) in AU-ROC across all four main datasets over 5 active learning rounds, with the gap widening in later rounds as the model accumulates more labeled data. Against GP-B²M [30], the closest competitor in model family, EMM achieves the largest gains on rare labels: the API metric (Figure 5) shows positive improvement for the majority of the 50 rarest labels on each dataset, with the strongest gains on labels appearing fewer than ~10 times in the test set. Against MMC [38] (label-ranking based) and CVIRS [26] (margin + inconsistency based), EMM leads from the very first round, attributed to its stronger base classifier (deep evidential model vs. SVM or label-ranking). The Adaptive baseline [18] (SVM margin + cardinality inconsistency) and the compressed-sensing baseline CS [31] are outperformed, with CS becoming computationally infeasible on larger datasets (BibTex, NUS-WIDE). The ablation confirms that replacing the evidential regression with simple ridge regression (EMM⁻ᵣᵣ) or removing the proxy pseudo-count branch (EMM⁻ᶠⁱˣᵉᵈ) both degrade performance, validating each component's contribution.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two research threads: mixture-of-Bernoulli models for multi-label classification (CBM, GP-B²M) and deep evidential regression (Amini et al., NeurIPS 2020). EMM directly extends GP-B²M by replacing its expensive Gaussian Process head with a deep evidential regression model that outputs Normal Inverse Gamma parameters, and by introducing instance-level proxy pseudo-counts that adjust the global label clusters per sample. It also departs from CBM's per-instance label clusters by maintaining a shared global cluster structure while still allowing per-sample correction, and it introduces a multi-source uncertainty selection criterion that combines evidential epistemic uncertainty, cluster-space divergence, and label-prediction covariance—none of which prior multi-label AL methods jointly exploit.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Multi-label active learning with large, sparse label spaces (web service tagging, image annotation, text classification) remains a practical bottleneck where labeling cost is high and rare labels are systematically under-predicted. EMM's proxy pseudo-count mechanism and evidential uncertainty decomposition offer a principled way to target rare-label uncertainty, and the multi-source selection score provides a richer informativeness signal than single-metric approaches. Key open questions include the fixed choice of K (no Bayesian model selection), computational overhead of the evidential training loop, and whether the approach scales to extreme multi-label settings (thousands of labels). The paper also notes that AUC-optimization losses are orthogonal and could be integrated into the joint training step, leaving room for further gains.
