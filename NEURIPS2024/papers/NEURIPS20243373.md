## Problem

Prior work on human visual behavior has treated implicit early-perceptual tasks (saliency, scanpath) and explicit later-stage tasks (aesthetic ratings, preferences) in isolation, with dedicated models for each specific combination of task, input domain, and viewing scenario. No single unified model existed that could predict both attention and preference behavior across diverse visual content types such as natural images, webpages, graphic designs, and mobile UIs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243373/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes an RGB image (resized to 512×512 with aspect-ratio padding) alongside a structured text prompt of the form 'INPUT_TYPE:<type> OUTPUT_TYPE:<type> QUERY:<query>' that specifies the content domain, desired prediction type, and optional task-specific query (e.g., object name for search). Outputs are task-conditional: pixel-wise heatmaps in [0,1] for saliency/importance, autoregressive coordinate sequences in a 1000×1000 bin space for scanpaths, or a single scalar in [0,1] for quality/aesthetic ratings.

## Method

UniAR is a multimodal encoder-decoder transformer (848M parameters) that fuses a ViT-B16 image encoder with a T5-base text encoder, followed by three task-specific prediction heads: a convolutional upsampling head for heatmaps, a T5-base decoder that generates scanpath coordinates as text tokens (spatially binned to 1000×1000), and a small conv-dense head for scalar ratings. The key design choice is conditioning all predictions on a modularized text prompt that encodes input domain, output type, and scenario, allowing a single model to switch between tasks without architectural changes. Training uses multi-task supervised learning across 11 public datasets with equal-probability sampling per dataset, combined with pretraining on WebLI captioning and Spotlight UI-understanding tasks. Losses from the three heads (sequence cross-entropy, heatmap L2, rating L2) are combined with empirically tuned weights [1, 500, 50] to balance scales.

## Results

Across 7 heatmap prediction benchmarks spanning natural images, webpages, mobile UIs, and graphic designs, UniAR achieves the best result in 17 of 27 reported metrics and second-best in 6 cases. Headline improvements include CC of 0.879 (+5.40%) and NSS of 1.008 (+20.14%) on Mobile UI, CC of 0.904 (+3.31%) and R² of 0.823 (+615.65%) on Imp1k, and CC of 0.870 (+6.23%) on CAT2000. For scanpath prediction, UniAR outperforms all baselines on WS-Scanpath (Sequence Score 0.267, +19.20%; MultiMatch 0.887, +17.48%) and COCO-Search18 (SemSS 0.521, +5.04%; MultiMatch 0.874, +2.94%). For rating prediction, UniAR achieves the best PLCC on both KonIQ-10k (0.925) and Web Aesthetics (0.839, +2.31%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

UniAR consistently outperforms domain-specific SOTA baselines on the most challenging datasets: on Mobile UI it surpasses SAM-S2017 (CC 0.879 vs 0.834) and ResNet-Sal (CC 0.657) across all metrics, and on Imp1k it beats UMSI (CC 0.904 vs 0.875, RMSE 0.079 vs 0.134). For scanpath prediction, UniAR outperforms GazeFormer [54] on COCO-Search18 (SemSS 0.521 vs 0.496, MultiMatch 0.874 vs 0.849) and AGD-S [11] on WS-Scanpath (Sequence Score 0.267 vs 0.224, MultiMatch 0.887 vs 0.755), with the latter showing the largest relative gains (+19.2% and +17.5%). On rating prediction, UniAR matches MUSIQ-single [37] on KonIQ-10k SRCC (0.905 vs 0.905) while exceeding it on PLCC (0.925 vs 0.919), and outperforms Calista [21] on Web Aesthetics PLCC (0.839 vs 0.820). UniAR shows minor losses on a few metrics: CC on Salicon (0.900 vs 0.907 for SimpleNet w. PNASNet-5), CC on OSIE (0.742 vs 0.761 for Chen et al. [12]), and CC on FiWI (0.734 vs 0.735 for AGD-F), all within ~1–2.5%. The knowledge-transfer experiment (Table 5) further demonstrates that UniAR trained without any webpage scanpath data still achieves Sequence Score 0.231 and MultiMatch 0.857 on WS-Scanpath, competitive with the previous SOTA (0.224 / 0.755), confirming cross-domain generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the multimodal vision-language model paradigm (image + text prompt → output) from large VLMs like PaLI and Pix2Struct for human behavior prediction, where prior work maintained separate dedicated models for saliency heatmaps (DeepGaze, EML-NET, GazeFormer), scanpath sequences (IRL-based methods, GazeFormer), and subjective ratings (MUSIQ, BRISQUE), each typically restricted to a single input domain. Rather than building on a specific prior architecture, UniAR carves new ground by showing that a single ViT-B16 + T5 encoder-decoder with structured text prompts (specifying input domain, output type, and task query) can subsume all three behavior-prediction tasks across natural images, webpages, graphic designs, and mobile UIs. The scanpath predictor's design—generating coordinates as discrete text tokens via a T5 decoder—borrows from the sequence-interface approach of Chen et al. [13] and Spotlight [50], but applies it to gaze sequences rather than object detection or UI parsing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical value is immediate: a single model can provide designers and content-creation systems with attention heatmaps, viewing sequences, and quality scores for any visual content, enabling rapid iterative evaluation of UIs and designs. The zero-shot transfer experiment (Table 5) is the most compelling open question: a model trained on COCO scanpath + WS saliency outperforms the previous SOTA on WS-Scanpath without ever seeing webpage scanpath data, suggesting meaningful cross-task representation sharing. Key limitations include the absence of personalization (the model converges to a uniform preference), potential reward hacking if used as a reward signal, and dataset demographics skewed toward young university participants. Future work on continual learning, personalized variants, and accessibility (modeling blind/low-vision user behavior) are identified but unresolved.
