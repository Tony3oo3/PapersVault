## Problem

The 'planning as inference' literature uses marginal, MAP, and MMAP inference interchangeably without establishing which type is actually correct for planning. This paper shows that planning corresponds to a distinct inference type—defined by a specific entropy weighting in the variational framework—that does not coincide with any standard type under stochastic dynamics, and that the choice of inference type materially affects planning quality as stochasticity increases.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243704/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a finite-horizon MDP specified by transition dynamics P(x_{t+1}|a_t, x_t), reward functions R_t, an initial state distribution, and a horizon T, with states factorized into N_e entities each of cardinality N_s (yielding an exponentially large state space of N_s^{N_e}). It produces an optimal (or approximate) policy π_t(a_t|x_t) at each time step, a utility estimate F_λ, a value function (backward messages), and occupancy probabilities (forward messages). For factored MDPs, the concave upper bound F̂_λ^planning serves as an admissible heuristic for optimality certificates, and the λ→0⁺ limit yields a tractable linear program over the local polytope.

## Method

The key insight is that planning corresponds to the variational entropy H_q(x_1) + Σ_t H_q(x_{t+1}|a_t, x_t), which differs from marginal inference by dropping the H_q(a_t|x_t) term—equivalently, the policy is the *output* of inference rather than a factor in the graph. This yields a concave variational problem whose λ→0⁺ limit is a novel VI LP formulation of the MDP dual. For factored MDPs with exponentially large state spaces, the authors derive Value Belief Propagation (VBP), an analogue of loopy BP that uses the planning-specific Bethe entropy (including mutual-information correction terms I_q(x_t^{pa(i)})) and a smoothing parameter ε that interpolates between planning and marginal inference. VBP recovers exact value iteration on non-factored MDPs in a single backward-forward pass, and the smoothing ε = αλ is shown to exactly correspond to MaxEnt RL policy regularization, unifying the planning and MaxEnt planning literatures.

## Results

On 5,000 synthetic factored MDPs with controlled stochasticity, VBP (the paper's approximate planning inference) dominates all other inference types at high stochasticity levels, while exact MAP and MMAP dominate at low stochasticity. On the 6 IPPC 2011 domains (10 instances each, 40-step episodes, 30 simulations per instance), VBP is competitive with the best baselines on the four highly deterministic domains (H_MDP < 0.05) and shows a significant advantage over SOGBOFA-LC on the two stochastic domains (Game of Life, H_MDP = 0.18; SysAdmin, H_MDP = 0.23). The concave upper bounds (VI LP and VI CVX) also improve over exact MMAP but not as much as VBP. VBP is the most consistent method across varying stochasticity levels, matching the best-performing method on each dataset.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ARollout (Cui et al., 2015, approximating marginal^U inference), VBP wins decisively at high stochasticity on synthetic MDPs and on the Elevators IPPC domain where ARollout performs noticeably worse; on low-stochasticity domains both methods are competitive since marginal^U is a constant away from the correct utility for pure planning. Against SOGBOFA-LC (Cui et al., 2019, approximating MMAP inference, the IPPC 2018 runner-up), VBP shows a significant advantage on the two stochastic IPPC domains (Game of Life and SysAdmin) and matches or exceeds it on Elevators, while SOGBOFA-LC remains competitive on the four highly deterministic domains where MMAP is theoretically adequate. Against MFVI-Bwd and CSVI-Bwd (Wu and Khardon, 2022), VBP is consistently competitive or superior across all six IPPC domains, with the gap widening as stochasticity increases. The paper's key theoretical contribution is that MMAP (which SOGBOFA-LC approximates) is the tightest lower bound among common inference types but is intractable to optimize exactly, whereas planning inference (which VBP approximates) is the correct type and becomes strictly superior as dynamics become more stochastic.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies and extends the 'planning as inference' literature (Toussaint & Storkey 2006, Levine 2018, Cui et al. 2015/2019) by showing that planning corresponds to a distinct variational inference type with its own entropy structure, rather than the marginal, MAP, or MMAP inference that prior works have variously employed. Methodologically, it builds on the Bethe approximation framework (Yedidia et al., 2005) and the norm-product BP machinery for arbitrary entropy weightings (Hazan & Shashua, 2010) to derive Value Belief Propagation (VBP), a message-passing algorithm that recovers exact value iteration on non-factored MDPs and extends Levine's MaxEnt planning to factored MDPs. The paper also connects to the LP-based MDP literature (Koller & Parr, 1999; Guestrin et al., 2003) by providing a variational derivation of the LP formulation that naturally generalizes to factored settings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper resolves a long-standing inconsistency in the planning-as-inference literature by identifying the correct inference type and showing that previously used types (MAP, MMAP, marginal) are only adequate under low stochasticity, with MMAP being intractable even for non-factored MDPs. The VBP algorithm and VI LP formulation provide practical tools for approximate planning in factored MDPs with exponentially large state spaces, and the stochasticity analysis gives practitioners clear guidance on when different methods are appropriate. Limitations include the lack of convergence guarantees for VBP, empirical validation on relatively small MDPs and IPPC tasks with modest stochasticity (H_MDP < 0.25), and the fact that the non-concave Bethe planning objective can still encounter local minima. Open questions include whether VBP scales to very large factored MDPs, how it compares to learned/planning methods in continuous spaces, and whether the exponential utility parameter λ can be usefully adapted online.
