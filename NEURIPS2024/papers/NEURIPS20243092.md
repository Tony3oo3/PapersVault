## Problem

Unifying visual text comprehension (text generation) and visual text generation (image generation) in a single model instance causes significant performance degradation due to the inherent inconsistency between vision and language modalities in the generation space. Existing multimodal generative models rely on modality-specific supervised fine-tuning, necessitating separate model instances for each modality, while no prior work has addressed this unification challenge specifically in the visual text domain.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243092/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes text-rich images at 896×896 resolution (processed through a Q-Former that compresses visual tokens to 512), text prompts, image-question pairs, and image-mask-text triples. For visual text generation, an all-black image is used as the image input to maintain a unified data format with the editing task. Outputs are interleaved token sequences that detokenize into either text content (via a linear classification layer) or image content (via a denoising diffusion process), enabling a single autoregressive pass to produce both modalities.

## Method

TextHarmony follows the MM-Interleaved paradigm (CLIP-ViT-L/14 vision encoder → Q-Former → Vicuna-13B LLM → Stable Diffusion v2.1 image decoder) and introduces Slide-LoRA, a module inserted into Transformer layers that partially decouples the multimodal generation space. Slide-LoRA comprises a gating network G and three LoRA experts (R^T for text generation, R^I for image generation, R^S for shared knowledge); the gate produces a scalar γ ∈ [0,1] that hard-switches between the text and image experts while always adding the shared expert, formulated as O = ½·{[γ≥0.5]·R^T(x) + [γ<0.5]·R^I(x) + R^S(x)}. Training proceeds in two stages: multi-modal pre-training on MARIO-LAION and DocStruct4M (freezing the vision encoder and LLM, training only Q-Former and image decoder), followed by comprehensive fine-tuning on text-centric tasks where the vision encoder, Q-Former, image decoder, and Slide-LoRA are jointly optimized. A newly constructed dataset, DetailedTextCaps-100K, generated by prompting Gemini Pro with OCR-annotated images, provides high-quality captions that specifically emphasize textual elements, improving image generation quality over the original MARIO-LAION captions.

## Results

On visual text comprehension, TextHarmony achieves 28.5% on InfoVQA, 47.1% on DocVQA, 62.4% on TabFact, 60.2% on TextVQA, and 440 on OCRBench, matching or approaching dedicated text-comprehension models like Monkey and InternLM-XComposer2 while being a unified generation model. On visual text generation, TextHarmony scores 0.75 NED and 0.35 CLIP Score, comparable to specialized diffusion-based methods (AnyText: 0.88 NED, 0.36 CLIP; GlyphControl: 0.36 CLIP). On text grounding (MARIO-Eval), TextHarmony achieves 88.7% Acc@0.5, surpassing TGDoc (82.5%) and DocOwl 1.5 (84.3%). Slide-LoRA contributes an average +2.5% improvement on comprehension and +4.0% on generation over the no-LoRA variant (TextHarmony*).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against dedicated text-comprehension MLLMs, TextHarmony is competitive but not uniformly superior: it trails Monkey by ~3% on DocVQA (47.1 vs 50.1) and TextVQA (60.2 vs 64.3), and lags InternLM-XComposer2 on ChartQA (38.0 vs 51.6) and STVQA (49.7 vs 59.6), yet matches or exceeds them on InfoVQA (28.5 vs 28.6) and TabFact (62.4 vs 62.3). Against other unified multi-modal generation models (SEED-LLaMA-14B, MiniGPT5, MM-Interleaved), TextHarmony dominates by large margins—e.g., 47.1 vs 6.5 on DocVQA and 60.2 vs 14.4 on TextVQA vs SEED-LLaMA—demonstrating that Slide-LoRA effectively mitigates the modality conflict that cripples prior unified generators. On text grounding, TextHarmony (88.7% Acc@0.5) outperforms both TGDoc (82.5%) and DocOwl 1.5 (84.3%). On visual text generation, TextHarmony (0.75 NED, 0.35 CLIP) is comparable to specialized diffusion models like GlyphControl (0.36 CLIP) and TextDiffuser-2 (0.81 NED, 0.35 CLIP), while its image-generation-only variant TextHarmony-Gen (0.86 NED, 0.36 CLIP) approaches AnyText (0.88 NED, 0.36 CLIP). The ablation confirms Slide-LoRA's contribution: removing it drops NED from 0.75 to 0.68 and OCRBench from 440 to 397.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the MM-Interleaved architecture (Vision Encoder + LLM + Diffusion Decoder) and the LoRA parameter-efficient adaptation framework, but departs from prior unified multimodal generation work (Emu, MiniGPT-5, SEED-LLaMA) by targeting the visual text domain specifically and introducing Slide-LoRA—a gating mechanism over modality-specific and modality-agnostic LoRA experts—to partially decouple the conflicting text-generation (cross-entropy) and image-generation (MSE denoising) objectives within a single model. Unlike prior visual text comprehension models (Monkey, TextMonkey) that only output text, or visual text generation models (AnyText, TextDiffuser, GlyphControl) that only output images, TextHarmony unifies both directions. The paper also contributes DetailedTextCaps-100K, a dataset synthesized via Gemini Pro to address the insufficiency of brief MARIO-LAION captions for text-rich image generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first unified model to handle visual text comprehension, perception, and generation in a single instance, demonstrating that the modality inconsistency problem in joint training can be mitigated with a lightweight (2% parameter) LoRA-based decoupling rather than full MoE or separate model instances. The Slide-LoRA mechanism is a generalizable architectural idea that could benefit other unified multimodal generators facing conflicting training objectives. However, TextHarmony still lags behind specialized SOTA models on several comprehension benchmarks (ChartQA, STVQA, OCRBench) and struggles with dense-text image generation, leaving open whether the LoRA-expert approach scales to more complex or higher-resolution visual text scenarios and whether it generalizes beyond the text-centric domain.
