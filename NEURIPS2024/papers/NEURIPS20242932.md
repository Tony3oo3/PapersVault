## Problem

Modern neural networks that employ element-wise multiplication (e.g., self-attention, gated convolutions) capture feature interactions only up to a finite order, confining the interaction space to a finite dimension (e.g., n(n+1)/2 for quadratic). The paper asks how to efficiently extend feature interactions to an infinite-dimensional space without the quadratic or higher computational cost that explicit high-order mappings would incur.

## Input / Output

InfiNet consumes standard RGB images at 224×224 (ImageNet-1K training) or 384×384 (fine-tuning) and produces class logits for classification, bounding boxes with instance masks for COCO detection, or per-pixel class maps for ADE20K segmentation. Internally, the RBF kernel operates on 7-branch depth-wise convolutional feature vectors of shape H×W×C, and the interaction output retains the same H×W×C spatial structure, so no resolution or channel reshaping is required between stages.

## Method

InfiBlock replaces the element-wise multiplication between two feature groups with an RBF kernel K_rbf(Z_a, Z_b) = exp(−‖Z_a − Z_b‖²₂/2), which by Mercer's theorem implicitly maps features into an infinite-dimensional RKHS whose basis consists of all-order monomials {x₁^{n₁}…x_k^{n_k}/√(n₁!…n_k!)}. Concretely, each InfiBlock applies LayerNorm, splits the input into two groups via linear projections, expands each group into 7 branches with a 7×7 depth-wise convolution and ReLU, computes the RBF kernel across the 7 branches, adds a single-branch depth-wise convolution residual path to preserve linear superposition, and passes the result through a two-layer GELU MLP with a skip connection. The full InfiNet stacks these blocks in a four-stage hierarchical architecture with channel widths [C, 2C, 4C, 8C] and 2×2 strided downsampling between stages, trained with AdamW, cosine LR schedule, and standard augmentation (RandAugment, stochastic depth, label smoothing). The key advantage is that the infinite-dimensional interaction is achieved at O(1) per-pair cost (a single exponential evaluation) rather than the O(k) cost of explicit k-order polynomial mappings.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K, InfiNet-T achieves 83.4% top-1 accuracy with only 23M parameters and 3.2G FLOPs, outperforming all comparable baselines including ConvNeXt-T (82.1%), Swin-T (81.3%), and HorNet-T (82.8%). At the larger scale, InfiNet-B reaches 84.5% top-1 with 82M params and 12.8G FLOPs, surpassing ConvNeXt-B (83.8%), HorNet-B (84.3%), and MogaNet-L (83.5%). With ImageNet-21K pretraining and 384² fine-tuning, InfiNet-XL attains 88.2% top-1 accuracy, setting a new state-of-the-art over ConvNeXt-XL (87.8%) and Conv2Former-XL (87.8%). On MS COCO detection, InfiNet-B achieves 53.7 box AP and 47.3 mask AP; on ADE20K segmentation, InfiNet-B reaches 50.2 mIoU (single-scale).

## Baselines & Comparisons

InfiNet-T (83.4%) outperforms all non-interactive baselines (ConvNeXt-T 82.1%, SLaK-T 82.5%) and finite-order interaction models (Swin-T 81.3%, FocalNet-T 82.1%, HorNet-T 82.8%, Conv2Former-T 83.1%) while using ~20% fewer FLOPs than most competitors; it is marginally below MogaNet-S (83.5%) by 0.1%. At the B-scale, InfiNet-B (84.5%) achieves the best top-1 accuracy among all compared models, beating Conv2Former-B (84.4%), HorNet-B (84.3%), CoAtNet-2 (84.1%), SLaK-B (84.0%), ConvNeXt-B (83.8%), and MogaNet-L (83.5%) while also having the lowest FLOPs (12.8G vs. 15.4–17.1G). With ImageNet-21K pretraining, InfiNet-XL (88.2%) surpasses ConvNeXt-XL (87.8%) and Conv2Former-XL (87.8%) by 0.4 points. On MS COCO detection, InfiNet-B leads with 53.7 box AP and 47.3 mask AP, exceeding HorNet-B (53.3/46.1) and ConvNeXt-B (52.7/45.6). On ADE20K segmentation, InfiNet-B achieves 50.2 mIoU (single-scale), outperforming HorNet-B (50.0) and ConvNeXt-B (49.1). The ablation in Table 3(b) confirms a monotonic performance gain as interaction order increases from 0 (addition, 81.6%) → 2 (Hadamard, 82.1%) → 4 (quadratic poly., 82.3%) → 6 (cubic poly., 82.5%) → ∞ (RBF, 83.4%), validating the infinite-dimensional interaction hypothesis.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies the design principle behind element-wise multiplication-based architectures (self-attention in Transformers, gating in HorNet/MogaNet, squeeze-and-excitation in SENet) under a single theoretical lens: the dimensionality of the feature interaction space. It then departs from all prior interaction-based methods by replacing explicit element-wise multiplication with an RBF kernel, which implicitly maps features into an infinite-dimensional RKHS at O(1) per-interaction cost. Architecturally, InfiNet follows the ConvNeXt-style 4-stage hierarchical design with depth-wise convolutions, but swaps the interaction operator in each block from addition or multiplication to the RBF kernel, drawing on classical kernel methods (Mercer's theorem, RKHS theory) that had been largely absent from modern neural architecture design.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clean, quantifiable framework (interaction space dimensionality) for understanding why multiplication-based architectures outperform purely additive ones, and shows that the natural next step—scaling to infinite order—is achievable at negligible extra cost via a single exp() evaluation. The consistent ~20% FLOPs reduction alongside SOTA accuracy across classification, detection, and segmentation suggests a genuinely more efficient inductive bias. Open questions include whether other kernels (Laplace, learnable), other modalities (language, video), and self-supervised pretraining can further exploit the infinite-dimensional interaction space, and whether the fixed σ=1 in the RBF kernel is suboptimal.
