## Problem

Existing shape matching methods face a fundamental trade-off: unique descriptors (e.g., SHOT) yield accurate but non-smooth correspondences, while smooth spectral descriptors (e.g., LBO eigenvectors) produce continuous but non-unique matches. Moreover, methods that attempt to address both (like 2D-GEM) require different user-defined parameters for isometric versus non-isometric deformations, which is impractical when the deformation type is unknown. HOPE seeks a single, parameter-unified refinement pipeline that achieves both accuracy and smoothness across deformation types.

## Input / Output

HOPE takes two 3D meshes with their triangulation adjacency matrices, an initial vertex-to-vertex correspondence map (typically from SHOT descriptors), and precomputed geodesic distance matrices for each shape. It outputs a refined bijective correspondence map. The method assumes correlated triangulations between the pair (i.e., adjacencies derived from a common parent graph), and the LMD threshold ε is swept linearly from 100 to 0.2 over the 60 refinement iterations, with k_max=8 hops cycled per iteration.

## Method

HOPE is an iterative refinement pipeline that alternates between two steps: (1) computing the Local Map Distortion (LMD) at each vertex to partition the map into well-matched landmarks and poorly-matched Nlmks vertices, and (2) re-solving the correspondence for Nlmks vertices only by maximizing the trace of the product of k-hop neighborhood adjacency matrices (A_{M,k}(T(Nlmks), T^{t-1}) · A_{N,k}), solved via the GMWM algorithm. The key mechanism is cycling k from 1 to k_max=8 across iterations, so that progressively larger neighborhoods are enforced as consistency constraints—this addresses the non-uniqueness of small-hop neighborhoods under symmetry while preserving smoothness through neighborhood-based matching. Unlike 2D-GEM, which refines Nlmks using the LBO spectrum (losing uniqueness and failing under non-isometric deformations), HOPE relies solely on k-hop connectivity, a structural constraint that holds for both isometric and non-isometric shapes with correlated triangulations, eliminating the need for deformation-type-specific parameters.

## Results

On the non-isometric TOPKIDS dataset, HOPE achieves 94.9% of vertices correctly matched (geodesic error 0), slightly behind 2D-GEM (96.7%) but well ahead of GRAMPA (84.5%). On the isometric SCAPE dataset, HOPE achieves 80.11% accuracy at geodesic error 0, outperforming 2D-GEM (74.8%) and DIR-1000 (~69.5%). On TOSCA, HOPE matches 2D-GEM at 92.54% accuracy while substantially outperforming DIR-1000 (59.8%). In terms of runtime on a 12,500-vertex SCAPE shape, HOPE takes 52 seconds versus 56 seconds for 2D-GEM and ~118 seconds for GRAMPA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HOPE outperforms 2D-GEM on the isometric SCAPE dataset (80.11% vs. 74.8% at geodesic error 0) and matches it on TOSCA (both 92.54%), while 2D-GEM retains a small edge on the non-isometric TOPKIDS dataset (96.7% vs. 94.9%). Against DIR-1000, HOPE achieves substantially higher accuracy on both SCAPE (80.11% vs. ~69.5%) and TOSCA (92.54% vs. 59.8%). On remeshed datasets (FAUST_r, SCAPE_r, TOSCA_r), HOPE outperforms ZoomOut, whereas 2D-GEM does not outperform ZoomOut in that setting. HOPE is also faster than 2D-GEM (52s vs. 56s per shape) and significantly faster than GRAMPA (~118s). A key practical advantage is that HOPE uses a single set of parameters (ε = linspace(100, 0.2, 10), k_max = 8) across all datasets, whereas 2D-GEM requires different ε thresholds for isometric vs. non-isometric shapes, making it less generalizable in practice.

## Relation to Prior Work

HOPE inherits its two-stage pipeline (LMD-based detection of poorly matched vertices followed by targeted refinement) from 2D-GEM [22] and the Local Map Distortion concept from DIR [49], while its core refinement step—solving a trace-maximization over k-hop adjacency matrices via GMWM—comes from the correlated graph matching literature [52]. The key departure from 2D-GEM is replacing the LBO-spectrum-based refinement of Nlmks vertices with iterative multi-scale k-hop neighborhood matching (k=1 to k_max=8), motivated by the observation that spectral descriptors lose vertex uniqueness (Theorem 3.1) and are not robust to non-isometric deformations. This positions HOPE as a simpler, parameter-unified alternative to 2D-GEM that avoids matrix decomposition and does not require switching between isometric and non-isometric configurations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that HOPE achieves competitive accuracy (92.54% on TOSCA, 80.11% on SCAPE) with a single set of hyperparameters across isometric and non-isometric settings, whereas 2D-GEM requires different LMD thresholds (ε=100 vs. small values) depending on the deformation type—a distinction that is often unknown in practice. The main open limitation is the reliance on correlated triangulations between the two shapes, which degrades performance on remeshed datasets (FAUST_r, SCAPE_r, TOSCA_r) and restricts applicability to settings where consistent mesh connectivity is available. Future work could explore how to relax the correlated-triangulation assumption or combine k-hop matching with more robust geometric descriptors for remeshed or topologically noisy shapes.
