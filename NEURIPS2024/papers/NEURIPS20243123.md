## Problem

Learning the intent of an agent—its goal or motion style—from only a few demonstrations remains a major open problem in robotics and decision-making. Existing approaches either learn policies directly (suffering from covariate shift), learn reward functions (limited to previously seen behavior), or assume new tasks are similar to a pretraining task family. This paper asks whether the strong priors captured by a pretrained generative model can be leveraged to learn genuinely novel task concepts from a handful of unlabeled demonstrations and generalize them to unseen environments.

## Input / Output

The method takes 3–5 unlabeled state-sequence demonstrations of a new task concept, an initial state, and optionally one or more training concept embeddings for composition. It produces a learned concept vector (a latent embedding optimized via backpropagation through the frozen model) and, from it, diverse generated trajectories (state sequences) that exhibit the learned concept. In closed-loop settings, a planner converts the predicted future states into executable actions (e.g., forces in AGENT, discrete lane/speed commands in driving, end-effector poses in manipulation).

## Method

FTL-IGM first pretrains a conditional diffusion model with classifier-free guidance on paired (trajectory, concept) data, where concepts are T5 embeddings of natural-language task descriptions. For few-shot learning, the model is frozen and K≥1 concept vectors (initialized uniformly) along with their guidance weights are optimized by backpropagating the diffusion denoising loss on the new demonstrations—no model weights are updated. The key insight is that the invertibility of the generative model turns concept learning into an inverse problem: finding the latent input that maximizes the likelihood of the observed demonstrations. Compositional generation is achieved by adding extra classifier-free guidance terms for training concepts alongside the learned concept, enabling synthesis of behaviors that combine the new concept with previously seen ones. The diffusion architecture is an MLP for object rearrangement and a temporal U-Net (with a fine-tuned ResNet-18 for manipulation) for sequential domains.

## Results

On Object Rearrangement, FTL-IGM achieves 0.82±0.09 accuracy for new concept generation, outperforming BC (0.67±0.15), VAE (0.08±0.05), and Language conditioning (0.2±0.05). On the AGENT goal-oriented navigation benchmark, FTL-IGM reaches 0.73±0.07 accuracy, surpassing BC (0.46±0.05), VAE (0.6±0.06), In-Context (0.57±0.03), and Language (0.63±0.07). In autonomous driving, FTL-IGM achieves a 24% task completion rate (tied with VAE) with a 32% crash rate, compared to BC (4% success, 64% crash) and In-Context (0% success, 16% crash). For MoCap, FTL-IGM is selected as the best method 73.3% of the time for new concepts versus 26.7% for BC and 0% for VAE.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FTL-IGM consistently outperforms goal-conditioned behavior cloning (BC) across all domains: 0.82 vs. 0.67 on Object Rearrangement, 0.73 vs. 0.46 on AGENT, 24% vs. 4% driving success, and 73.3% vs. 26.7% MoCap new-concept depiction. Against the VAE baseline, FTL-IGM wins decisively on Object Rearrangement (0.82 vs. 0.08) and MoCap (73.3% vs. 0%), and on AGENT (0.73 vs. 0.6), while tying on driving success rate (24% each) but achieving a lower crash rate (32% vs. 48%). The In-Context learning baseline (Xu et al., 2022) is outperformed on AGENT (0.73 vs. 0.57) and driving success (24% vs. 0%), though it achieves the lowest driving crash rate (16%) at the cost of zero task completion. The Language conditioning baseline (T5 embeddings of task descriptions) is substantially weaker, scoring only 0.2 on Object Rearrangement and 0.63 on AGENT, confirming that learning concepts from demonstrations is necessary rather than relying on natural-language descriptions alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the visual concept learning paradigm of Gal et al. (textual inversion) and Liu et al. (compositional concept discovery) from images to sequential agent behavior, using a conditional diffusion model (following Ajay et al.'s Diffuser) as the generative backbone. The key conceptual departure from prior few-shot task learning approaches (behavior cloning, inverse RL, inverse planning, in-context learning) is that no policy, reward, or world model is learned or updated; instead, a latent 'concept' vector is optimized as input to a frozen generative model via classifier-free guidance. This positions the work at the intersection of generative modeling for decision-making and few-shot concept learning, repurposing the inverse problem (finding the input that explains the output) from computer vision to trajectory generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates a clean, weight-free recipe for few-shot task concept learning that generalizes across five diverse domains including real-world robot manipulation, suggesting the approach could be a practical tool for agents that must acquire new skills from minimal demonstrations. The compositional generation capability (combining a newly learned concept with training concepts to produce unseen behavior) is particularly valuable for building flexible agent repertoires. Key limitations include high diffusion inference time, the assumption that new concepts lie within the training concept landscape (no theoretical characterization of this space), and modest generation quality in MoCap. Open questions include whether the approach scales to higher-dimensional observation spaces (e.g., full image-based manipulation without the simplified state representation) and whether the concept space can be made more interpretable or theoretically grounded.
