## Problem

State space models (SSMs) like Mamba achieve attention-level performance with linear-time scaling, but their recurrent formulation makes them inherently causal, restricting them to autoregressive settings. Prior attempts to make SSMs bidirectional—combining forward and backward SSMs via element-wise addition, Hadamard product, or concatenation—remain ad-hoc and lack a principled mathematical justification. The paper also notes the absence of a unified framework for understanding how different sequence mixers (attention, SSMs, convolutions, etc.) relate to each other through their underlying matrix structures.

## Input / Output

For language tasks, Hydra consumes tokenized text sequences (BERT-base uncased tokenizer) at 256 tokens for most GLUE tasks and 128 tokens for others, producing masked token predictions during C4 pretraining and task-specific scores (accuracy, F1, Matthews correlation, Spearman) during GLUE finetuning. For image classification, 224×224 RGB images are split into 16×16 patches, flattened in row-major order without positional embeddings, and the model outputs a 1000-way class label. The bidirectional nature means the model processes both forward and backward sequence directions simultaneously, unlike unidirectional Mamba.

## Method

Hydra formulates its sequence mixer as a quasiseparable (QS) matrix, which generalizes both the semiseparable matrices underlying SSMs and the low-rank matrices of linear attention. The key decomposition is QS(X) = shift(SS(X)) + flip(shift(SS(flip(X)))) + DX, where SS(·) is a semiseparable (SSM) operation, flip reverses the sequence, shift pads and offsets by one position, and D is a learned diagonal—meaning Hydra is implemented as two SSD passes (forward and backward) with simple post-processing, sharing the input projection layer to save parameters. The paper identifies 'sequence alignment' (SAM) as the critical property enabling data-dependent parameterization and extendability, showing that SAM-equipped mixers consistently outperform data-independent ones across all tested matrix families. Hydra uses 23 layers (~112M params) for GLUE and 24 layers (~91M params) for ImageNet, matching BERT and ViT-Base parameter counts respectively, and is trained with established MosaicBERT/M2 recipes for language and standard ViT training for vision.

## Results

On the GLUE benchmark, Hydra (112M params) achieves an average score of 84.3%, outperforming BERT (83.5%) by 0.8 points and M2 (80.9%) by 3.4 points, while also achieving the best C4 pretraining accuracy of 69.1% (vs. BERT's 67.3%). On ImageNet-1K, Hydra-ViT-B (91M params) reaches 81.0% Top-1 accuracy, surpassing ViT-B (78.8%) by 2.2 points and S4ND (79.4%) by 1.6 points. In the bidirectional SSM ablation (Table 3), Hydra's quasiseparable mixer achieves 81.7 GLUE average, beating the addition-based bidirectional Mamba (80.6), Hadamard product (79.6), and concatenation (81.1) variants.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Hydra outperforms all baselines on both language and vision tasks. On GLUE, Hydra (84.3 avg) beats BERT (83.5) by 0.8 points, M2 (80.9) by 3.4 points, MLP-Mixer (77.5) by 6.8 points, and FNet (75.8) by 8.5 points, while using fewer parameters than BERT (112M vs 110M) and M2 (112M vs 116M). On ImageNet-1K, Hydra-ViT-B (81.0% Top-1) surpasses ViT-B (78.8%) by 2.2 points, S4ND (79.4%) by 1.6 points, Hyena (78.4%) by 2.6 points, and unidirectional Mamba (79.1%) by 1.9 points. In the bidirectional SSM ablation, Hydra's quasiseparable mixer (81.7 GLUE) outperforms the addition-based approach used by Vision Mamba (80.6), the Hadamard product variant (79.6), and the concatenation variant (81.1), while using comparable parameter counts (~70M). Notably, Hydra also achieves the best C4 pretraining loss (1.46) and accuracy (69.1%), indicating superior representation quality beyond just downstream task performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Mamba's selective state space model and the SSD framework (which showed SSMs are semiseparable matrices) to propose Hydra, a bidirectional extension parameterized as a quasiseparable matrix mixer. Conceptually, it departs from prior bidirectional SSM approaches (addition, Hadamard product, concatenation of forward/backward passes) by grounding the design in a unified 'matrix mixer' framework that categorizes Transformers, SSMs, Linear Attention, MLP-Mixer, FNet, and others under a common structured-matrix lens. The paper introduces 'sequence alignment' as a new axis of matrix parameterization, arguing that data-dependence (not the specific matrix family) is the key property driving strong performance, and uses this to systematically derive new mixers (Vandermonde, Cauchy) and the quasiseparable Hydra.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The matrix mixer framework provides a principled design space for future sequence models: choose a structured matrix class with desired properties (efficiency, bidirectionality, data-dependence) and instantiate it. The finding that sequence alignment matters more than the specific matrix structure (Cauchy ≈ low-rank when both are data-dependent) challenges the field's focus on the QK^T formulation. Open questions include the representation-computation tradeoff (structured matrices are inherently less expressive than dense attention for memorization tasks), hardware efficiency of structured matrix multiplication versus pure GEMM, and whether the framework's insights hold at much larger scales. The modest but consistent gains over BERT (0.8 GLUE) and ViT (2.2% ImageNet) as a drop-in replacement suggest practical utility for encoder architectures.
