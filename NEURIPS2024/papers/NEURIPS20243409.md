## Problem

Monocular 3D human pose estimation is inherently ill-posed due to depth ambiguity, yet standard single-hypothesis regression models are trained to minimize MPJPE, which provably forces predicted poses off the human pose manifold (violating rigid segment lengths and bilateral symmetry). The paper shows theoretically that no single-hypothesis model can simultaneously minimize joint position error and maintain pose consistency, and that existing multi-hypothesis methods either rely on costly generative models or fail to constrain outputs to the pose manifold.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243409/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a sequence of T video frames (T=243 for Human 3.6M, T=43 for MPI-INF-3DHP) with J=17 2D keypoints per frame, computed by a pre-trained CPN detector (or ground-truth for MPI-INF-3DHP). It outputs K=5 full 3D pose sequences (each with 17 joints in Cartesian coordinates) plus a per-frame likelihood score for each hypothesis, where all hypotheses are guaranteed to share identical segment lengths within a sequence. Evaluation uses both oracle (best hypothesis) and score-weighted aggregation protocols.

## Method

ManiPose combines a manifold constraint with multiple choice learning (MCL) in a deterministic, non-generative architecture built on the MixSTE spatio-temporal transformer backbone. The network is split into a segments module (predicting J-1 shared segment lengths across the sequence) and a rotations module (predicting 6D rotation embeddings per joint per frame), which are decoded via forward kinematics to guarantee all K hypotheses lie on the same rigid-skeleton manifold. Multiple choice learning is implemented with K linear heads over a shared intermediate representation, each producing one rotation hypothesis and a softmax-normalized score, trained with a composite winner-takes-all loss (specializing heads on different modes) plus a cross-entropy scoring loss (calibrating confidence). The paper further proves via Jensen's inequality that the MSE minimizer for a single-hypothesis model necessarily predicts shorter-than-true segment lengths, and via Voronoi tessellation arguments that K-hypothesis models always achieve lower or equal L2-risk than any single-hypothesis model.

## Results

On Human 3.6M, ManiPose achieves an oracle MPJPE of 39.1 mm with 5 hypotheses, while reaching near-perfect pose consistency (MPSSE 0.3 mm, MPSCE 0.5 mm) — a 10–30× improvement over all baselines. On MPI-INF-3DHP, ManiPose attains the best PCK (98.4%) and AUC (77.0%), with an oracle MPJPE of 34.6 mm and consistency errors of 0.6/1.3 mm. The key finding is that MPJPE and pose consistency are not positively correlated for single-hypothesis methods: MixSTE achieves the best single-hypothesis MPJPE (40.9 mm) but has MPSCE of 9.9 mm, while Anatomy3D has good consistency (MPSCE 2.0 mm) at the cost of worse MPJPE (44.1 mm).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ManiPose dominates on pose consistency: its MPSCE of 0.5 mm (oracle) is roughly 12× lower than the next-best multi-hypothesis method (DiffPose, Gong et al. at 6.1 mm) and 20× lower than MixSTE (9.9 mm), while its MPSSE of 0.3 mm is an order of magnitude below all other methods. On oracle MPJPE, ManiPose (39.1 mm) beats MixSTE (40.9 mm), MHFormer (43.0 mm), and D3DP (39.5 mm), and is within 0.2 mm of DiffPose (Gong et al., 39.3 mm), all while using only 5 hypotheses versus 200 for generative baselines. On MPI-INF-3DHP, ManiPose achieves the best PCK (98.4%) and AUC (77.0%), with only P-STMO slightly ahead on MPJPE (32.2 vs. 34.6 mm) but at the cost of 8.5/11.3 mm consistency errors versus ManiPose's 0.6/1.3 mm. The ablation confirms that removing either multi-hypothesis (K=1) or the manifold constraint degrades one of the two objectives: single-hypothesis constrained ManiPose drops to 44.6 mm MPJPE, while unconstrained single-hypothesis (MixSTE) retains 40.9 mm MPJPE but inflates MPSCE to 9.9 mm. Generative multi-hypothesis methods (GFPose, DiffPose, Wehrbein et al.) require 20–200 hypotheses to reach competitive oracle MPJPE and still exhibit 5–17 mm consistency errors, whereas ManiPose's deterministic winner-takes-all framework achieves both goals with just 5 hypotheses.

## Relation to Prior Work

ManiPose builds on the MixSTE spatio-temporal transformer backbone and the Resilient Multiple Choice Learning framework (Letzelter et al., NeurIPS 2024) to produce multiple deterministic pose hypotheses with learned likelihood scores, departing from prior multi-hypothesis 3D-HPE methods that rely on generative models (MDNs, VAEs, normalizing flows, diffusion). The manifold constraint—predicting shared segment lengths and per-joint 6D rotations decoded via forward kinematics—borrows the parametric decomposition idea from SMPL-based human pose and shape reconstruction but applies it to the lighter 3D-HPE lifting task. Crucially, the paper departs from the entire single-hypothesis regression lineage by proving (Proposition 4.2) that any MSE-minimizing single-hypothesis model must produce geometrically inconsistent poses, making the multi-hypothesis + manifold-constrained design not merely preferable but necessary.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The impossibility theorem showing that single-hypothesis regression fundamentally cannot satisfy both low joint error and skeletal rigidity reframes how the field should evaluate and design 3D-HPE models, and the introduction of MPSCE/MPSSE as first-class metrics fills a gap that MPJPE and PCK leave unaddressed. ManiPose's near-perfect consistency (0.3–0.5 mm MPSCE vs. 6–17 mm for baselines) while remaining competitive on MPJPE demonstrates that the consistency-accuracy tradeoff previously assumed to be unavoidable is in fact an artifact of the single-hypothesis formulation. Open questions include whether the inherently sequential forward-kinematics decoding can be parallelized, whether articulation-limit constraints can be added without breaking the manifold structure, and whether the MCL scoring mechanism generalizes to other ambiguous lifting problems beyond human pose.
