## Problem

Popular PEFT methods like LoRA and Adapters parameterize weight updates as low-rank matrices, yet the actual fine-tuning updates are generally not low-rank, providing no principled justification for the rank constraint. This paper asks whether alternative structured matrix classes—unrestricted in rank but still requiring only a linear number of parameters—can serve as more expressive drop-in replacements that improve quality under the same parameter budget.

## Input / Output

The method consumes frozen pre-trained Transformer weights (ViT-B, CLIP-ViT, BERT-base, Llama-2-7B, SAM) and task-specific training data, producing structured weight updates ΔW parameterized as circulant (O(n) params), Toeplitz (O(n+m) params), or Kronecker-product matrices. In the LoRA setting, the SURM update is added to the frozen weight and can be merged at inference with zero added latency; in the adapter setting, the SURM matrices replace the bottleneck down/up projections inside a residual block with a non-linearity. All variants support sub-quadratic O(n log n) matrix-vector multiplication via FFT, and zero-initialization is achieved by setting one factor to a zero vector.

## Method

SURMs replace the low-rank ABᵀ factorization in LoRA and the bottleneck matrices in Adapters with three structured classes: circulant matrices (parameterized by a single first-row vector), Toeplitz matrices (parameterized by first row and column), and Kronecker products of two smaller matrices. The theoretical foundation is the W(G,H) parameterization from Sindhwani et al., which shows that sums of circulant/skew-circulant products can represent all n×n matrices when the displacement rank r equals n, while even r=1–4 captures near-low-rank and low-intrinsic-rank structure effectively. Empirically, circulant and Toeplitz matrices approximate random PSD matrices better than low-rank or Kronecker matrices under a fixed parameter budget, and a rank-1 LoRA layer fails to fit a toy pinwheel dataset while a circulant layer of the same parameter count matches full fine-tuning. The integration is a direct substitution: in LoRA, ΔW = C₁ ⊙ C₂ (Hadamard product of two circulant matrices) or ΔW = g(T₁, g(T₂, ·)) for Toeplitz; in Adapters, the same structured matrices replace A and B in Y = X + σ(XB)A, with GeLU non-linearity applied after both structured multiplications to reduce storage to a single vector.

## Results

On CLIP-ViT-B/16, SURM(Circulant) with only 0.055M trainable parameters achieves 97.0% on CIFAR-10, 84.6% on CIFAR-100, 81.1% on SUN397, 78.6% on DTD, and 99.3% on STL-10, outperforming LoRA (0.219M params) by 1.9–7.0 percentage points across datasets. On ViT-B/16, SURM(Circulant) reaches 80.5% on SUN397 and 75.7% on DTD versus LoRA's 73.6% and 70.4%, a 5–7% gain. On the GLUE benchmark with BERT-base, SURM adapters (0.06M params) achieve 93.0% on SST-2 and 91.55% on MRPC, matching or exceeding Adapter (1.8M params) while using 30× fewer parameters. In the low-resource VTAB-1k setting, SURM(Circulant) is among the top-2 methods on 10 of 12 datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SURM(Circulant) with 0.055M parameters outperforms LoRA (0.219M) by 5.5–7.0 points on SUN397 (84.6 vs. 78.1) and DTD (81.1 vs. 80.8) under CLIP-ViT, and by 6.9–5.3 points on ViT-B (80.5 vs. 73.6 on SUN397; 75.7 vs. 70.4 on DTD), while using 4× fewer parameters. Against Adapter (1.505M params), SURM achieves comparable or better accuracy on all five CLIP datasets with 27× fewer parameters. On the GLUE benchmark, SURM(Circulant-Adapter) reaches 93.0% on SST-2 and 91.55% on MRPC with 0.06M params, matching Adapter(Houlsby) at 1.8M params (92.8% / 91.5%) and exceeding LoRA (92.66% / 90.43%) at the same 0.06M budget. In the low-resource VTAB-1k setting, SURM(Circulant) achieves 80.6% on CIFAR-100 and 74.7% on DTD, ranking top-2 among 15 baselines on 10 of 12 datasets. On the UUID-fitting task with Llama-2-7B, SURM(W(G,H)) converges to fit the data while both LoRA (rank 256) and MoRA fail to converge, demonstrating superior expressiveness at high-rank regimes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the low-rank constraint that underpins both LoRA and Adapter-based PEFT methods, arguing that fine-tuning weight updates are not inherently low-rank and that imposing such a constraint is unjustified. Instead, it introduces Structured Unrestricted-Rank Matrices (SURMs) — specifically circulant, Toeplitz, and Kronecker-product matrices — which maintain a linear parameter budget while allowing full rank, drawing on the low displacement rank matrix theory from Sindhwani et al. (2015). The method is positioned as a direct drop-in replacement for the low-rank factors in both LoRA (replacing AB^T) and Adapters (replacing the bottleneck matrices), preserving the same architectural slots and zero-initialization strategy. Unlike prior structured-matrix work in PEFT (e.g., Compacter's Tucker decomposition, Krona's Kronecker adapters), SURMs do not impose any rank-based condition on the constituent factors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled alternative to the low-rank assumption that has dominated PEFT research, with the practical consequence of a 12x parameter reduction in adapters at virtually no quality loss on GLUE, and 5-7% accuracy gains over LoRA on vision tasks using fewer parameters. The finding that the simplest structure (circulant matrices) consistently outperforms more expressive variants (Toeplitz, W(G,H)) is a useful practical guideline. Open questions include whether the structured-matrix advantage persists at much larger model scales (beyond 7B), whether the FFT-based sub-quadratic multiplication provides meaningful wall-clock speedup in practice versus merely reducing parameter count, and whether the specific structure choice should be task-dependent. The approach also naturally extends to combinations of different LDRMs (e.g., circulant + skew-circulant sums), suggesting a broader design space for future exploration.
