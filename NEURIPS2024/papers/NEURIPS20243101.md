## Problem

Existing high-quality instruction tuning (IT) data selection methods rely heavily on external resources—closed-source LLMs for scoring, fine-tuned open-source models, or additional datasets for calibration—which increases cost and limits broader adoption. This paper asks whether the intrinsic uncertainty of the foundation LLM itself can be exploited to select high-quality IT data without any supplementary models or data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243101/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes instruction-response pairs from an IT dataset (e.g., Alpaca-GPT4) along with K semantically similar rating prompts, and processes them through N open-source foundation LLMs of varying sizes (e.g., LLaMA-2 7B/13B/70B). Rather than generating full responses, it only computes next-token probability distributions over score tokens (1 to K), making it significantly faster than generation-based scoring. The output is a scalar quality score per sample (S^model), from which the top-ranked subset (e.g., top 20%) is extracted as the curated Selective Alpaca dataset.

## Method

SelectIT introduces a three-granularity uncertainty-aware self-reflection pipeline. At the token level, it computes a base score as the argmax over normalized score-token probabilities, then refines it by multiplying with the average absolute disparity between the predicted token's probability and all others, capturing the model's internal confidence. At the sentence level, K semantically similar rating prompts are applied to the same sample, and the resulting token-level scores are combined as their mean divided by (1 + α × their standard deviation), so that high prompt-sensitivity (uncertainty) down-weights the score. At the model level, N foundation LLMs of different parameter counts each produce a sentence-level score, and these are aggregated via a parameter-count-weighted sum, leveraging the observation that larger models are more reliable evaluators. The final S^model score ranks all samples, and the top fraction is selected as high-quality IT data—requiring no external models, no fine-tuning of evaluators, and no auxiliary datasets.

## Results

On the open-instruct benchmark, SelectIT (using Selective Alpaca, top 20% of Alpaca-GPT4) improves LLaMA-2-13B's average score from 44.7 to 47.1 (+2.4) and LLaMA-2-7B from 34.1 to 36.2 (+2.2), outperforming all data-selection baselines. Gains are most pronounced on reasoning tasks: BBH improves from 46.6 to 48.9 and GSM from 30.5 to 33.0 on 13B. On the machine translation task, ALMA-7B with SelectIT achieves 83.7 COMET / 30.5 BLEU (ALL), surpassing the full-data ALMA baseline (83.2 / 29.7) and all prior MT methods (TIM, SWIE, BigTranslate, Bayling).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SelectIT outperforms all four IT data-selection baselines on both LLaMA-2-7B and 13B: it beats Instruction Mining (+1.5) by 0.7 on 7B and (+1.6) by 0.8 on 13B, Q2Q (+1.3/+1.2) by 0.8/1.2, AlpaGasus (+0.7/+0.5) by 1.5/1.9, and LIMA (−0.7/−1.7) by 2.9/4.1 in average score. On the WizardLM and Orca-GPT4 datasets (Table 7), SelectIT improves average scores by +1.4 and +1.7 respectively over the full datasets, with the largest gains on GSM (+1.0 and +3.5). In the machine translation setting (Table 8/11), ALMA-7B with SelectIT (83.7 COMET, 30.5 BLEU) surpasses all prior MT methods—TIM (79.1/26.4), SWIE (80.6/27.6), BigTranslate (78.8/21.9), and Bayling (82.0/27.8)—and also exceeds the full-data ALMA baseline (83.2/29.7). ALMA-13B with SelectIT reaches 84.2 COMET / 32.2 BLEU, approaching GPT-4 (85.8/35.1) while using only 6K selected samples versus GPT-4's proprietary training. Notably, SelectIT requires no external model or external data (marked ×/✓ in Table 1), whereas AlpaGasus, Q2Q, and Instruction Mining all depend on external resources.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits within the instruction tuning data selection lineage initiated by LIMA (quality over quantity) and extended by methods like AlpaGasus (ChatGPT-based scoring), Q2Q (condition-based loss selection), and Instruction Mining (feature-loss fitting). SelectIT departs from these approaches by eliminating all external resources—no closed-source LLMs, no fine-tuned evaluators, no auxiliary datasets—and instead repurposes the LLM's own next-token prediction uncertainty at three granularities (token, sentence, model) as the selection signal. Conceptually, it reframes data quality assessment as an intrinsic property of the foundation model's confidence distribution rather than an external judgment task.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical value lies in removing the cost and access barriers of GPT-4/ChatGPT-based selection (Table 9 shows ~10× speedup and ~100× cost reduction vs. GPT-4 API), making high-quality IT data curation accessible to resource-constrained groups. The Selective Alpaca dataset (10.4K samples) is a reusable artifact that improves multiple model families (LLaMA-2, Mistral, LLaMA-3) and domain tasks (MT). However, open questions remain: the 20% selection threshold is tuned for Alpaca and may not transfer; the method is only validated on models under 30B parameters; and the finding that longer, more computationally intensive data is superior—while consistent with Zhao et al. (2024)—has not been tested on datasets where length is not a proxy for quality.
