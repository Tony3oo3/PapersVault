## Problem

Existing Vector Symbolic Architectures (HRR, VTB, MAP) were designed for hand-crafted symbolic tasks and suffer from numerical instability, O(d log d) or higher computational complexity, or degraded performance when embedded in differentiable deep learning pipelines. The paper asks whether a VSA can be derived that achieves O(d) binding complexity, avoids irrational/complex arithmetic, and matches or exceeds prior VSAs in both classical retrieval benchmarks and modern neural network applications.

## Input / Output

The binding operation takes two vectors x, y ∈ R^d (where d must be a power of 2 for the Hadamard matrix) and produces a single bound vector via element-wise multiplication; unbinding takes a composite representation χ_ρ and a query vector y_i and retrieves the associated x_i via element-wise division. Vectors are initialized from a Mixture-of-Normals distribution Ω(μ, 1/d) that guarantees zero mean but a non-zero absolute mean μ, preventing the division-by-near-zero instability inherent in the unbinding step. In the deep learning experiments, the VSA layer sits inside a CNN (CSPS) or a linear classifier (XML), consuming standard image or text embeddings and producing class probabilities or multi-label relevance scores.

## Method

HLB is derived by replacing the Fourier transform in HRR's circular-convolution binding with the Walsh-Hadamard Transform; because the Hadamard matrix is its own inverse and consists only of ±1 entries, the transform cancels algebraically, reducing the binding to simple element-wise multiplication (x ⊙ y) and unbinding to element-wise division (x ⊙ 1/y), both O(d). A projection step (π(x) = Hx/d) is applied to inputs before binding, which the authors prove reduces the accumulated noise term from η° to η^π in expectation. A key theoretical result (Theorem 3.2) shows the cosine similarity between the true and retrieved vector scales as 1/√ρ, motivating a similarity augmentation that multiplies scores by √ρ to restore a near-1/0 discrimination. The MiND initialization (half elements from N(−μ, 1/d), half from N(μ, 1/d)) ensures E[x]=0 while keeping |x| bounded away from zero, which is critical for the numerical stability of the division-based unbinding.

## Results

On the Extreme Multi-Label (XML) classification task, HLB achieves the best nDCG and PSnDCG on all 8 datasets, e.g., 61.74 nDCG on BibTeX (vs. 60.30 HRR, 57.69 VTB, 59.41 MAP-B) and 83.59 nDCG on Wiki10-31K (vs. 81.07 HRR, 78.03 VTB, 80.24 MAP-B). In the CSPS privacy task, HLB attains the highest geometric-mean Top@1 accuracy of 77.17% (vs. 67.14% HRR, 47.24% VTB, 70.89% MAP-C, 75.90% MAP-B) while maintaining near-zero Adjusted Rand Index scores for information hiding. In classical VSA retrieval, HLB matches HRR and VTB in AUC accuracy and uniquely preserves a constant similarity score of 1 and stable vector magnitude across sequential bindings, unlike HRR/VTB/MAP which degrade.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HLB outperforms all three baseline VSAs (HRR, VTB, MAP) on every reported metric across both deep-learning tasks. Against HRR, HLB gains 1.4–3.7 nDCG points on XML datasets (e.g., +3.71 on EURLEX-4.3K: 88.20 vs. 84.50) and 10.03 geometric-mean Top@1 points on CSPS (77.17 vs. 67.14). Against VTB, the margin is largest in CSPS where VTB collapses to 47.24 GM Top@1 (vs. HLB's 77.17), likely due to VTB's non-symmetric binding and block-diagonal matrix structure; on XML, HLB still leads by 2.4–5.6 nDCG. Against MAP (both continuous and binary variants), HLB wins on all XML datasets by 1.3–6.7 nDCG and on CSPS by 1.27–6.28 GM Top@1 points; MAP-B is the closest CSPS competitor (75.90 GM) but still trails HLB. In classical retrieval, HLB ties HRR and VTB in AUC accuracy but uniquely maintains exact similarity scores and constant vector norms under sequential binding, a property the other three VSAs lack.

## Relation to Prior Work

HLB is derived by replacing the Fourier transform in HRR's circular convolution binding with the Walsh-Hadamard transform, which algebraically collapses the binding to element-wise multiplication and the unbinding to element-wise division. This places it in the same family as MAP (which also uses element-wise multiplication for both binding and unbinding), but the critical departure is the division-based unbinding combined with a Mixture-of-Normals (MiND) initialization that avoids near-zero values. The projection step is inspired by Ganesan et al.'s complex-unit magnitude normalization for HRR, and the overall framework follows the linear-operator view of VSAs from Steinberg and Sompolinsky, where binding is a^T G b with structured matrices G and F.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

HLB offers a practical O(d) binding operation that is numerically stable (no complex arithmetic, no irrational multiplications) and consistently matches or exceeds HRR, VTB, and MAP across both classical VSA retrieval tasks and two deep-learning applications (CSPS privacy and XML classification). The clean theoretical characterization of the noise term and the φ ≈ 1/√ρ cosine similarity relationship give practitioners a principled way to calibrate similarity scores when the number of bound items is known. Open questions include whether the MiND initialization and the √ρ correction generalize to learned (non-random) vector representations, and whether the approach extends to non-power-of-two dimensions or to settings where ρ is unknown and must be estimated from the norm.
