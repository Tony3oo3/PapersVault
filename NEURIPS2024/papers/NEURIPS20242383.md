## Problem

Selecting the best in-context example for Visual In-Context Learning (VICL) is critical for performance, yet prior methods like VPR rely on pair-wise contrastive learning that underutilizes ranking observations and cannot capture inter-sample relationships. Additionally, converting partial ranking predictions over small subsets into a globally consistent ranking over the full alternative set remains unsolved, as naive iterative selection accumulates errors and wastes most available observations.

## Input / Output

The method takes a query image and a candidate pool of K alternatives (typically the 50 most visually similar training images by CLIP cosine similarity), extracting class and patch token features via DINO v2. The list-wise ranker consumes subsets of k=3, 5, or 10 alternatives at a time, and the aggregator produces a single global score vector r ∈ ℝ^K over all candidates. The top-ranked example is then fed as the in-context prompt to MAE-VQGAN for the downstream task (segmentation, detection, or colorization).

## Method

Partial2Global is a two-stage pipeline. First, a transformer-based list-wise ranker concatenates class and patch tokens from k alternatives plus the query, rearranges all class tokens to the front, and processes them through additional transformer layers to model global-local interactions across samples; it is trained with a composite objective combining pairwise margin loss, differentiable NeuralNDCG (list-wise), and MSE regression against ground-truth performance metrics. Second, a consistency-aware ranking aggregator collects partial rankings from many randomly shuffled k-length subsets, converts them into pairwise preference matrices, and solves a least-squares formulation (Eq. 3) to obtain a globally consistent score vector that minimizes disagreement across all partial observations. This avoids the error accumulation of iterative top-k selection and allows multiple ranker sizes (e.g., rank-5 and rank-10) to be fused for richer signal.

## Results

On foreground segmentation (Pascal-5i), Partial2Global achieves 38.40 average mIoU without voting and 42.69 with voting, outperforming SupPR (35.56) by 2.84 mIoU and prompt-SelF (41.02) by 1.67 mIoU. On single object detection (Pascal VOC 2012), it reaches 30.66 mIoU (no voting) and 32.52 (with voting), surpassing SupPR by 2.44 mIoU. On image colorization (ILSVRC2012), it achieves 0.58 MSE, a 0.05 improvement over both UnsupPR and SupPR (0.63), while the gap over random MAE-VQGAN (0.67) is more than doubled compared to prior methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against MAE-VQGAN (random selection), Partial2Global improves segmentation by 10.84 mIoU, detection by 5.21 mIoU, and colorization by 0.09 MSE, confirming that careful prompt selection is critical. Against VPR's supervised variant (SupPR), Partial2Global wins on all three tasks: +2.84 mIoU on segmentation, +2.44 mIoU on detection, and −0.05 MSE on colorization. Notably, SupPR and UnsupPR produce identical colorization results (0.63 MSE), suggesting VPR's contrastive learning metric fails to capture task-relevant ranking signal for colorization, whereas Partial2Global's list-wise ranker achieves a substantial 0.05 MSE reduction. Against prompt-SelF (which uses test-time ensemble), Partial2Global with voting surpasses it by 1.67 mIoU on segmentation and 2.69 mIoU on detection, establishing new state-of-the-art on both tasks. The consistent gains across all three tasks and both with/without voting settings demonstrate the robustness of the list-wise ranking plus consistency-aware aggregation pipeline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the visual in-context learning (VICL) example selection problem introduced by VPR (Zhang et al., 2024), which framed prompt selection as a contrastive-learning-based retrieval task on top of MAE-VQGAN. The key departure is replacing VPR's pair-wise contrastive metric with a transformer-based list-wise ranker that captures inter-alternative relationships, and introducing a consistency-aware ranking aggregator (inspired by HodgeRank) to fuse partial rankings into a globally consistent ordering. The work also builds on the broader learning-to-rank literature (pairwise/listwise objectives, NeuralNDCG) and applies it to the visual domain for the first time in the VICL context.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that the visual-similarity heuristic underlying VPR is an unreliable proxy for in-context example quality, and that list-wise ranking with consistency aggregation yields consistently better prompts across segmentation, detection, and colorization. The 'consistent optimality' property—where top-k ranked examples are all good, not just top-1—opens the door to ensemble-based VICL strategies. However, the oracle gap (Table 8: ~10 mIoU below exhaustive search) indicates the ranking problem remains far from solved, and the entire pipeline is still tied to the early-stage MAE-VQGAN backbone, limiting broader impact until stronger VICL models mature.
