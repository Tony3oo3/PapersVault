## Problem

Existing audio-visual sound separation models assume all sound source classes are known at training time, but real-world deployment requires handling new, unseen sound categories. Fine-tuning on new classes causes catastrophic forgetting of previously learned separation abilities, and no prior work has addressed continual learning specifically for audio-visual sound separation, where the output is a fixed-size separation mask rather than class-specific logits.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242420/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a mixed audio signal (STFT magnitude spectrogram of 256×256 at 11 kHz, ~6 s clips) and visual guidance consisting of object features (CLIP embeddings of Detic-detected objects resized to 224×224) and motion features (VideoMAE embeddings at 1 FPS). It outputs per-source ratio masks that, when element-wise multiplied with the mixed spectrogram and inverse-STFT'd, reconstruct individual sound waveforms. Unlike classification-based continual learning, the mask size remains fixed across all incremental steps and each mask entry does not correspond to a specific class.

## Method

ContAV-Sep builds on the iQuery architecture (U-Net audio encoder/decoder + audio-visual Transformer with class-aware queries) and introduces a Cross-modal Similarity Distillation Constraint (CrossSDC) as the key anti-forgetting mechanism. CrossSDC enforces cross-modal semantic similarity via two contrastive-loss components—instance-aware (same video, different modalities) and class-aware (same class, different videos)—over audio, object, and motion feature pairs. Crucially, for memory-set samples the contrastive loss is computed between features from both the current model and the frozen old model, thereby integrating knowledge distillation directly into the similarity constraint rather than treating it as a separate term. The total training objective combines the main L1 mask loss, an L1 output-mask distillation loss (applied only to memory data), and the CrossSDC, with the object detector, video encoder, and image encoder kept frozen throughout all incremental steps.

## Results

On MUSIC-21 with iQuery as the base separator and a memory set of 1 sample per old class, ContAV-Sep achieves SDR 7.33, SIR 13.55, and SAR 13.01, outperforming the best baseline (PLOP) by 0.30 in SDR, 0.25 in SIR, and 0.41 in SAR. With Co-Separation as the base model, ContAV-Sep reaches SDR 4.06, SIR 10.06, and SAR 11.07, again surpassing all baselines. The upper bound (training on all seen classes simultaneously) reaches SDR 10.36 / SIR 16.64 / SAR 14.68 with iQuery, indicating ContAV-Sep closes roughly 70% of the gap to the oracle. Ablation confirms both the instance-aware and class-aware components of CrossSDC contribute additively to the final performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ContAV-Sep outperforms all five baselines on every metric with both iQuery and Co-Separation base models. Against the strongest baseline PLOP [16] (SDR 7.03, SIR 13.30, SAR 11.90 with iQuery), ContAV-Sep gains +0.30 SDR, +0.25 SIR, and +0.41 SAR. LwF [38] is competitive on SAR (12.60) but trails on SDR (6.76 vs. 7.33) and SIR (12.77 vs. 13.55). EWC [32] achieves the second-best SIR (13.01) but the weakest SAR among memory-based baselines (11.73). EWF [78] underperforms notably on SDR (5.35), suggesting weight-fusion strategies are less suited to the fixed-size mask output of separation tasks. The audio-visual-specific baseline AV-CIL [53] (SDR 6.86, SIR 13.13, SAR 12.31) is the closest competitor but still lags ContAV-Sep by 0.47 in SDR and 0.70 in SAR, highlighting the benefit of the cross-modal similarity distillation constraint over standard output-mask distillation alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two well-developed lines of research: audio-visual sound separation (iQuery, Co-Separation, The Sound of Pixels) and continual learning (EWC, LwF, PLOP, EWF). It departs from prior audio-visual continual learning work (Pian et al. ICCV'23, Mo et al. ICCV'23), which addressed class-incremental classification, by tackling the fundamentally different output structure of separation masks (fixed-size, class-agnostic) rather than progressively growing logits. The proposed CrossSDC loss extends knowledge distillation and contrastive learning into the cross-modal similarity space, a constraint not present in prior continual learning methods that operate on single-modality features or task outputs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first formulation of continual audio-visual sound separation, opening a practically important direction for deploying separation models in open-world settings where new sound sources appear over time. The task-specific insight that a single memory sample can generate many effective training pairs (by mixing with diverse current samples) explains why rehearsal-based methods are particularly effective here, a property absent in standard classification. However, the gap to the upper bound remains substantial (~3 dB SDR), and the reliance on object detectors for visual grounding limits robustness; the paper also acknowledges that its focus on preserving old knowledge may prevent the model from acquiring new knowledge about old classes in subsequent tasks.
