## Problem

Optimizing the latents, conditional information, or parameters of diffusion models with respect to a differentiable loss on the output requires backpropagating through the entire sampling trajectory. Naive backpropagation is memory-intensive (storing all intermediate states) and non-trivial for SDE-based samplers due to injected noise. Prior adjoint-based methods either used black-box ODE solvers, did not exploit the special structure of diffusion models, or did not handle the SDE case.

## Input / Output

The method takes two source face images (256×256 RGB), a pre-trained diffusion autoencoder (Diffusion Autoencoder trained on FFHQ), and a guidance function (ArcFace-based identity loss). It produces a single morphed face image that shares biometric information with both source identities, along with gradients with respect to the initial noise x_T, the conditional embedding z, and model parameters θ. The adjoint solver operates on a decoupled schedule of M steps in the log-SNR domain, independent of the N-step forward sampling schedule, and requires only the recorded forward states {x_{t_i}} (not internal network activations) for the backward pass.

## Method

AdjointDEIS derives bespoke ODE solvers for the continuous adjoint equations of diffusion models by exploiting their unique structure: (1) an integrating factor eliminates the linear drift term in closed form, (2) a change of variables to the log-SNR domain λ = log(α_t/σ_t) converts the remaining integral into an exponentially weighted form, and (3) a Taylor expansion of the vector-Jacobian product yields k-th order solvers (AdjointDEIS-1, AdjointDEIS-2M) where the exponentially weighted integrals are computed analytically via φ-functions. A key theoretical result shows that for diffusion SDEs with a state-independent diffusion coefficient g(t), the adjoint process simplifies to a pure ODE (Theorem 3.1), differing from the ODE case only by a factor of 2 in the noise-prediction term. The solvers are agnostic to the forward sampler (DDIM, DPM-Solver, etc.) and support scheduled conditional information that evolves over time, a generalization to neural ODEs not previously considered.

## Results

On the SYN-MAD 2022 dataset, DiM+AdjointDEIS-1 (ODE) achieves the highest MMPMR across all three face recognition systems: 99.8% (AdaFace), 98.77% (ArcFace), and 99.39% (ElasticFace) at FMR=0.1%, surpassing the commercial state-of-the-art Webmorph (97.96/96.93/98.36). The SDE variant (DiM+AdjointDEIS-1 SDE) also outperforms all prior diffusion-based methods, reaching 98.57/97.96/97.75. Both AdjointDEIS variants use 2250 NFEs, comparable to Morph-PIPE (2350 NFEs) but far fewer than DiM-A (350 NFEs per optimization step × 50 steps).

## Baselines & Comparisons

AdjointDEIS-1 (ODE) decisively outperforms all five baselines on every FR system. Against the commercial Webmorph system, it gains +1.84, +1.84, and +1.03 MMPMR points on AdaFace, ArcFace, and ElasticFace respectively. Against the prior diffusion-based methods DiM-A and Fast-DiM, the improvement is substantial: +7.57 and +7.78 MMPMR on AdaFace, and +8.59 and +8.59 on ArcFace. Morph-PIPE, the strongest prior diffusion-based baseline, is surpassed by +3.89 (AdaFace), +5.93 (ArcFace), and +3.89 (ElasticFace). The GAN-based MIPGAN-I and MIPGAN-II baselines are far behind, with AdjointDEIS-1 (ODE) exceeding them by over 25 MMPMR points on AdaFace. Notably, the SDE variant, while still superior to all prior methods, underperforms the ODE variant by 1.23–1.64 MMPMR points, which the authors attribute to the difficulty of discretizing SDEs with large step sizes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the continuous adjoint equations framework from neural ODEs (Chen et al., 2018) to diffusion models, building directly on the exponential integrator solvers developed for forward diffusion sampling (DPM-Solver, DEIS). It departs from AdjointDPM (Pan et al., 2024) by providing bespoke solvers rather than a black-box ODE solver, supporting both ODE and SDE cases, and being interoperable with arbitrary forward samplers. It also extends DiffPure (Nie et al., 2022), which used Euler-Maruyama for SDE adjoints, by showing the SDE adjoint equations reduce to a pure ODE and by applying exponential integrators to eliminate discretization error in the linear drift term. The time-varying conditional information result (Theorem 2.2) is, to the authors' knowledge, the first such treatment for neural ODEs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AdjointDEIS provides a memory-efficient, training-free mechanism for computing exact gradients through the full diffusion sampling trajectory, enabling guided generation without storing intermediate network states. The clean theoretical result that diffusion SDE adjoints reduce to ODEs (Theorem 3.1) simplifies implementation and opens the door to applying well-studied ODE solver theory to stochastic settings. However, evaluation is limited to a single application (face morphing) with modest hyperparameter exploration, and the counterintuitive finding that the SDE variant underperforms the ODE variant (attributed to discretization difficulty) leaves open whether this gap persists with finer discretization or different noise schedules. Broader validation across diverse guided generation tasks (text-to-image, inpainting, style transfer) would strengthen the case for the method's general utility.
