## Problem

Noisy labels are nearly inevitable in smoke segmentation due to blurry edges and non-rigid shapes, yet no method specifically addresses this problem. Standard noisy-label approaches assume i.i.d. pixel-level noise, but smoke's variable transparency causes inconsistent features among noisy labels—high-transparency regions resemble clean labels while opaque regions differ sharply—making uniform weighting criteria ineffective and potentially dangerous in safety-critical applications.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243388/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB smoke images (cropped to 512×512 for SmokeSeg, resized to 480×480 for SMOKE5K) and produces pixel-level binary segmentation masks distinguishing smoke from background. Unlike image-level classification weighting, CoSW assigns a distinct weight to every pixel based on its distance to the nearest prototype within its matched feature cluster, operating at the per-pixel granularity required for segmentation. The NS-1K synthetic dataset introduces controlled noise (eroded, dilated, edge-distorted masks) at varying ratios (20–80%) and intensities to stress-test robustness.

## Method

CoSW builds a multi-prototype framework where each class is represented by K learnable prototypes, and pixels are matched to prototypes via a Sinkhorn-Knopp transportation problem with equipartition constraints. The core mechanism is Regularized Within-Prototype Entropy (RWE), which maximizes Shannon entropy over per-prototype likelihood values subject to an M-estimation penalty on pixel-prototype distances, yielding closed-form per-pixel weights (Eq. 7) that adaptively down-weight noisy labels differently across feature clusters. These weights modulate a weighted cross-entropy loss and a Regularized Scatter Metric Learning (RSML) objective that regularizes the within-prototype scatter matrix to prevent overfitting under noise. Prototypes are updated via a momentum scheme using the weighted mean (Eq. 8), ensuring stability even when a subset of labels is corrupted. The approach is backbone-agnostic, demonstrated with both CNN (ResNet-50) and Transformer (MiT-B0/B3) encoders.

## Results

On the real-world noisy SmokeSeg dataset with MiT-B3 backbone, CoSW achieves 72.32 F1 and 59.25 mIoU (overall), outperforming the strongest baseline CleanNet (70.17 F1, 56.94 mIoU) by 2.15 F1 and 2.31 mIoU. On SMOKE5K, CoSW reaches 81.71 F1 and 71.24 mIoU, surpassing CleanNet (80.37 F1, 70.23 mIoU). On the synthetic NS-1K dataset at the most challenging setting (80% noise ratio, high intensity), CoSW maintains 42.37 F1 and 29.89 mIoU, while Trans-BVM degrades to 37.38 F1 and 25.58 mIoU, demonstrating substantially better robustness under extreme noise.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoSW outperforms all nine baselines across all three datasets. Against the closest competitor CleanNet [27] (prototype-based sample weighting for classification), CoSW gains +2.15 F1 on SmokeSeg (72.32 vs. 70.17) and +1.34 F1 on SMOKE5K (81.71 vs. 80.37), with the gap widening under synthetic noise (e.g., +2.10 F1 at 80% high noise on NS-1K). The spatial-correction method SC [52] trails CoSW by 2.77 F1 on SmokeSeg and 2.38 F1 on SMOKE5K. The smoke-specific Trans-BVM [51] is competitive on clean or low-noise data (52.79 F1 at 0% noise on NS-1K) but degrades rapidly as noise increases, falling to 37.38 F1 at 80% high noise versus CoSW's 42.37. General segmentation backbones (SegFormer [47], DeepLabV3+ [5], OCRNet [57], SegNeXt [13]) all underperform CoSW by 3–5 F1 on SmokeSeg, confirming that domain-agnostic architectures without noise-robust training are insufficient. Among real-time backbones (MiT-B0), CoSW still leads CleanNet by 1.35 F1 (62.98 vs. 61.63), showing the method's benefit is not backbone-dependent.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the prototype-based sample weighting paradigm of CleanNet [27] from a single-prototype, per-sample similarity criterion to a multi-prototype framework with a unified entropy-based objective (RWE) that simultaneously determines adaptive sample weights and stable prototype updates. The pixel-prototype matching mechanism is borrowed from ProtoSeg [62] and SwAV [4], employing the Sinkhorn-Knopp algorithm with an equipartition constraint to avoid degenerate assignments. The key departure from prior noisy-label segmentation work (e.g., SC [52], Pick-and-learn [63]) is the explicit modeling of feature inconsistency caused by variable smoke transparency, which motivates applying different weighting criteria within different feature clusters rather than a single global criterion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to address noisy labels in smoke segmentation, a safety-critical task where annotation errors from blurry, non-rigid smoke edges are nearly inevitable and model instability can have real-world consequences. The RWE framework provides a clean, unified mechanism for both sample re-weighting and prototype stabilization, and the NS-1K synthetic dataset offers a controlled benchmark for evaluating robustness under varying noise ratios and intensities. A key limitation is the reliance on a majority-clean assumption: when noise is extreme, prototypes may absorb noise features rather than class features, and the authors acknowledge that verifying prototype cleanliness without a clean validation set remains an open problem.
