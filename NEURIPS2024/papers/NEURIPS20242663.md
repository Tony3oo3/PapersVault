## Problem

Outlier Features (OFs)—neurons whose activation magnitudes far exceed the layer average—emerge during standard transformer training and severely hinder low-precision quantisation, yet the mechanisms driving their emergence and the architectural/optimisation choices that influence them remain largely unexplored. This paper asks: why do OFs appear during training, and how can they be minimised without sacrificing convergence speed or model quality?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242663/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard text corpora (CodeParrot, Languini Books, BookCorpus+Wikipedia, FineWeb-Edu) for next-token prediction, with transformer configurations ranging from 100M to 7B parameters. Outputs are evaluated on two axes: a kurtosis-based OF metric computed on residual-stream activations across layers, and post-training int8 weight-and-activation (W8A8) quantised perplexity using the OPT-125m quantisation recipe. The kurtosis metric is scale-invariant and architecture-agnostic, aggregating per-neuron RMS activations over batch inputs before computing the fourth-to-second moment ratio.

## Method

The paper introduces the Outlier Protected (OP) block, which removes all standard normalisation layers from a Pre-Norm transformer and replaces them with three mechanisms: downweighted residual branches (β = O(1/√depth)) to recover Signal Propagation benefits, QK-Norm as an entropy regulation mechanism to prevent attention entropy collapse, and optional MLP input scaling. The OP block matches Pre-LN convergence speed up to 1.2B parameters while reducing peak kurtosis by up to 4 orders of magnitude. Theoretically, the paper links OFs to Signal Propagation via an identity relating the feature-wise Gram matrix diagonal (kurtosis) to the input-wise Gram matrix (input correlations), showing that poor signal propagation (near rank collapse) pressures kurtosis upward. On the optimisation side, the paper demonstrates that non-diagonal preconditioners (SOAP, Shampoo) reduce OFs by rotating the parameter space before applying diagonal adaptive updates, an effect independent of the specific diagonal method used. Combining the OP block with SOAP yields 14.87 W8A8 perplexity (from 14.71 FP16/32) versus 63.4 W8A8 (from 16.00 FP16/32) for the default Pre-LN + AdamW baseline on OPT-125m.

## Results

On OPT-125m models trained on BookCorpus+Wikipedia, the combination of the Outlier Protected (OP) block with the SOAP optimiser achieves 14.87 W8A8 perplexity (only 0.16 above its 14.71 standard-precision perplexity) with a kurtosis of just 1.2, compared to 63.4 W8A8 perplexity (from 16.00 standard precision) and kurtosis 25.6 for the default Pre-LN + AdamW setup. The OP block matches Pre-LN convergence speed at scales up to 1.2B parameters (e.g., 13.9 vs 13.9 eval PPL on Languini Books) while reducing peak training kurtosis by up to 4 orders of magnitude. At 7B parameters, the OP block maintains peak kurtosis under 10 versus over 600 for Pre-Norm, while matching loss performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Gated Attention block of Bondarenko et al. [15], the OP block achieves lower default kurtosis (3.6 vs 4.5) and better W8A8 perplexity (16.01 vs 16.2); with SOAP, OP reaches 14.87 W8A8 PPL versus 15.64 for Gated Attention, while also achieving a lower kurtosis (1.2 vs 16.7). Against AdamW [43], replacing it with SOAP dramatically reduces kurtosis from 25.6 to 5.1 (Pre-LN) and from 3.6 to 1.2 (OP), and brings W8A8 perplexity from 63.4 down to 16.43 for Pre-LN. Against AdaFactor [65], both SOAP and AdaFactor-in-Shampoo-eigenbasis reduce OFs relative to diagonal AdaFactor, confirming that the rotation (not the specific diagonal method) is the key factor. Against Shampoo [67], the paper shows that SOAP (which applies AdamW in Shampoo's eigenbasis) converges slightly faster per step than Shampoo itself while achieving comparable OF reduction. The OP block also matches Pre-LN convergence speed exactly at 100M, 320M, and 1.2B scales (Table 1), demonstrating that removing normalisation layers does not sacrifice training efficiency.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on Outlier Features (OFs) in transformers, which was initiated by Kovaleva et al. and Bondarenko et al. who identified OFs as a quantization bottleneck, and by Wei et al. who attributed them to LayerNorm parameters. It departs from prior work by showing that even parameter-free norms (SRMSNorm) still produce OFs, and by introducing the Outlier Protected block that removes all standard normalization layers while recovering training stability through a combination of Signal Propagation-inspired downweighted residuals and Entropy Collapse prevention (QK-Norm). On the optimizer side, it builds on the SOAP/Shampoo connection established by Vyas et al. to reveal a previously unknown benefit: non-diagonal preconditioning reduces OFs as a side effect of rotating the parameter space, independent of the diagonal preconditioner used.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides both a practical recipe (OP block + SOAP) that achieves near-lossless int8 quantization (14.87 vs 63.4 perplexity) and a mechanistic understanding linking OFs to Signal Propagation degradation during training, which had been largely unexplored beyond initialization. Open questions include why OFs still appear (albeit reduced) in the OP block—likely task-dependent—and whether the Signal Propagation–OFE connection can be made rigorous in feature-learning regimes. Limitations include testing only up to 7B parameters, post-training quantization only (not quantized training), and the SOAP optimizer's computational overhead at scale, which the authors acknowledge as future work.
