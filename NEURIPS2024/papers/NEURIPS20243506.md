## Problem

Hamiltonian Monte Carlo has been a powerful sampling tool when the force field (score) is known, but the potential of deliberately designing force fields within Hamiltonian ODEs for generative modeling and score matching has remained largely unexplored. Prior works either fixed the force field within the diffusion framework or did not leverage the full design space of Hamiltonian dynamics. This paper asks whether the Hamiltonian formalism can yield novel score-matching metrics and a broader family of generative models beyond diffusion and flow matching.

## Input / Output

For score matching (HSM), the method consumes data samples x_i ~ π and produces a learned score vector field F_θ: R^d → R^d. For generative modeling (Oscillation HGFs), the method takes a Gaussian noise sample z ~ N(0, I_d) and produces a generated RGB image (32×32 for CIFAR-10, 64×64 for FFHQ) by integrating a learned velocity predictor ODE backwards from a known terminal distribution. Internally, all computations operate in the extended position-velocity space R^d × R^d, with initial velocities drawn independently from N(0, I_d), and the velocity predictor V_φ maps (x_t, t) to a d-dimensional velocity vector.

## Method

The central contribution is the Hamiltonian Velocity Predictor (HVP): a network V_φ trained to predict the expected velocity conditioned on location after evolving a parameterized Hamiltonian ODE (PH-ODE) with a chosen force field F_θ. Theorem 1 establishes that the true score is uniquely characterized by the property that this conditional velocity vanishes, yielding the Hamiltonian Score Discrepancy (HSD) as a novel score-matching metric whose Taylor expansion recovers the explicit score matching loss scaled by 2t². For generative modeling, the paper shows that the optimal HVP generates the location marginal probability path, so sampling from the data distribution reduces to integrating the velocity predictor ODE backwards from a known terminal distribution. Oscillation HGFs instantiate this with a harmonic oscillator force field F(x) = −α²x, giving closed-form ODE solutions and an in-built scale-invariance that keeps input/output magnitudes constant across time. The paper further proves that diffusion models and OT-flow matching are special cases of HGFs with zero force field, unifying them under the same velocity-prediction framework.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 unconditional (32×32), Oscillation HGFs achieve FID 2.12 with 35 NFE, outperforming VP-SDE (3.01), VE-SDE (3.77), DDPM (3.17), PFGM (2.48), and FM-OT/BNS (2.73) at the same or fewer NFE. On CIFAR-10 class-conditional, Oscillation HGFs reach FID 1.97 (35 NFE), trailing only EDM (1.79). On FFHQ 64×64, Oscillation HGFs achieve FID 2.86 (79 NFE), improving substantially over VP-SDE (3.39) and VE-SDE (25.95) while remaining close to EDM (2.39). Notably, these results are obtained without EDM's optimized preconditioning or skip connections, using the default DDPM architecture.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Oscillation HGFs (FID 2.12, 35 NFE) clearly outperform the original diffusion SDE formulations: VP-SDE (3.01), VE-SDE (3.77), and DDPM (3.17, 1000 NFE) on CIFAR-10 unconditional. They also beat PFGM (2.48, 104 NFE) and FM-OT/BNS (2.73, 8 NFE) on the same benchmark. Against the strongest diffusion baseline EDM (1.98), Oscillation HGFs trail by 0.14 FID on CIFAR-10 unconditional, 0.18 on CIFAR-10 conditional (1.97 vs 1.79), and 0.47 on FFHQ (2.86 vs 2.39). The paper attributes this gap to EDM's heavily optimized preconditioning, skip connections, and architecture choices that are hard to replicate without extensive tuning. On FFHQ, Oscillation HGFs dramatically improve over VE-SDE (25.95 → 2.86) and VP-SDE (3.39 → 2.86), showing the harmonic oscillator force field generalizes well to higher-resolution face generation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies diffusion models (Song et al., 2020) and flow matching (Lipman et al., 2022) as special cases of a broader Hamiltonian Generative Flow (HGF) framework where the force field is the key design parameter, with both prior methods corresponding to the zero-force-field limit. The core conceptual departure from Hamiltonian Monte Carlo (Neal, 2011) is inverting the problem: rather than using a known score as a force to sample, the paper learns a velocity predictor from data and uses it for both score matching (via Hamiltonian Score Discrepancy) and generation. The Oscillation HGFs, inspired by harmonic oscillators, represent a genuinely new point in the force-field design space that has no direct analogue in prior diffusion or flow matching literature, though Appendix K shows a formal equivalence to EDM with a specific time reparameterization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides a principled design axis (force fields) for generative models that is particularly relevant for physical sciences applications where known force fields exist (molecular dynamics, protein structure), yet current generative models largely ignore such structure. Key open questions include whether HGFs converge to known distributions for complex force fields, how to adapt the framework to data on manifolds, and whether the adversarial min-max training of HSM can be replaced with a more stable denoising variant. The empirical results are competitive but not state-of-the-art (Oscillation HGFs trail EDM by ~0.1–0.5 FID), suggesting the practical gains from non-zero force fields may require domain-specific tuning beyond the image benchmarks tested here.
