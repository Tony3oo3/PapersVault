## Problem

Inductive inference asks whether a learner can make only finitely many errors over an infinite observation sequence when deducing the ground-truth hypothesis from a given class. All prior theoretical guarantees, including Solomonoff induction, provided only a sufficient condition (countable hypothesis class size), leaving the necessary-and-sufficient characterization of when inductive inference is possible completely open.

## Input / Output

The learner receives a hypothesis class H ⊆ 2^X over an infinite domain X and a sequential stream of observations x_t ∈ X, producing a binary prediction ŷ_t ∈ {0,1} at each step before the true label h*(x_t) is revealed. The key output is a hypothesis-wise finite error bound m(h*) in the realizable setting, or an Õ(√T) regret bound in the agnostic setting, where the bound may depend on which specific h* Nature chose. The characterization is structural: learnability is determined entirely by whether H can be decomposed as a countable union of classes with finite Littlestone dimension.

## Method

The paper introduces a non-uniform online learning framework where Nature fixes the ground-truth h* in advance (unlike classic online learning's adaptive adversary), and error bounds are allowed to vary per hypothesis. The realizable-setting algorithm (Algorithm 1) uses a structural risk minimization scheme: it maintains a Standard Optimal Algorithm (SOA) instance for each class H_n in a countable decomposition, tracks each instance's error count e_n, and at each step predicts using the algorithm minimizing e_n + n, thereby discarding classes that exceed their Littlestone-dimension error budget. The main theorem proves H is non-uniform online learnable if and only if H = ∪_n H_n with each H_n having finite Littlestone dimension, and shows this characterization is invariant to whether observations are adaptively chosen or iid sampled. In the agnostic setting, the learner nests Hierarchical FPL over Expert subroutines (derived from Ben-David et al.'s agnostic Littlestone-class algorithm) to achieve an Õ((√d_n + log n)√T) regret bound, with a trichotomy showing this is the only non-degenerate rate possible.

## Results

The paper provides a tight characterization: inductive inference is possible if and only if the hypothesis class H is a countable union of classes with finite Littlestone dimension (Theorem 9). In the agnostic setting, the proposed algorithm achieves an expected regret of Õ((√d_n + log n)√T) for any h in H_n (Theorem 12), matching the Ω(√T) lower bound for any non-trivial class (Proposition 14). The trichotomy (Theorem 15) establishes that only three regret rates are possible: 0 (singleton class), Õ(√T) (countable union of Littlestone classes), or arbitrarily slow (everything else). In the realizable setting, the error bound is (d_k + k)², depending on the Littlestone dimension d_k and the index k of the class containing h*.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical baselines or experimental comparisons. The key comparison is with prior inductive inference results: Solomonoff induction and related work provide only a sufficient condition (|H| ≤ ℵ₀, i.e., countable hypothesis class), whereas this paper establishes a necessary and sufficient condition (countable union of Littlestone classes) that strictly generalizes the countable case. The paper also separates its non-uniform online learnability notion from classic uniform online learnability (Corollary 10, via the rational thresholds example) and from the weaker consistency criterion (Theorem 18, via the ℵ₁-size tree condition). In the agnostic setting, the result extends Ben-David et al. [2009] from finite-Ldim classes to countable unions, and the FPL-based aggregation (Hutter et al. [2005]) handles the unknown horizon T that the original agnostic bound required to be known in advance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the inductive inference framework of Gold (1967) and Solomonoff (1964), which provided only a sufficient condition (countable hypothesis class size) for finite-error learnability, by establishing a necessary and sufficient characterization via a novel link to online learning theory. The technical machinery draws on Littlestone's (1988) Standard Optimal Algorithm and Littlestone dimension for the realizable setting, Ben-David et al. (2009) for agnostic online learning, and Hutter et al. (2005)'s Follow the Perturbed Leader for aggregating experts without knowing the time horizon. The paper also generalizes the non-uniform PAC learning notion of Benedek and Itai (1988) to the online setting, and strengthens the EAS consistency result of Wu and Santhanam (2021) by showing non-uniformity equals consistency when the distribution is known.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This resolves a millennium-old philosophical question (Hume's Problem of Induction) with a clean, tight characterization: inductive inference is possible if and only if the hypothesis class is a countable union of Littlestone classes, a condition that subsumes and generalizes the previously known countability requirement. The agnostic trichotomy (rate 0, Õ(√T), or arbitrarily slow) provides a complete picture of what is learnable when the ground truth may not lie in the hypothesis class. Open questions include identifying a sufficient condition for the weaker consistency criterion (the paper conjectures 'no ℵ₁-size tree' is tight), understanding inclusion relations between different learnability notions, and extending results to computable learners for practical applicability.
