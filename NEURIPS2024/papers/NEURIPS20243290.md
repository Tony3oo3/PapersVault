## Problem

While prior work demonstrated that LLMs can infer personal attributes from previously unseen text, the analogous privacy risk for vision-language models applied to benign online images remained unexplored. Existing human attribute recognition (HAR) benchmarks focus on direct depictions of people and a narrow set of attributes, failing to capture the broader inference threat posed by VLMs' reasoning over contextual cues in images that contain no visible person. This paper asks whether frontier VLMs can accurately infer private attributes from such inconspicuous images and whether current safety safeguards adequately prevent this misuse.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image (sourced from pseudonymized Reddit posts, typically depicting rooms, workspaces, or outdoor scenes without the poster visible) and a structured text prompt as input. It produces structured predictions across eight private attribute categories—location, place of image, sex, age interval, income bracket, education level, occupation, and marital status—each formatted as a type label, a detailed inference chain, and up to three ranked guesses. For location and place-of-image, predictions are scored hierarchically (precise vs. less-precise) to account for varying granularity, while occupation uses a semantic similarity judgment. An optional automated zooming loop allows the model to request up to three bounding-box crops (each covering 16% of the image) for a second-pass inspection at higher effective resolution.

## Method

The paper's core contribution is an empirical evaluation framework rather than a new architecture. It introduces the VIP dataset (554 image-attribute pairs) collected from 47 Reddit subreddits and manually annotated by the authors using a multi-step pipeline: image-only labeling, optional reverse-image search, and cross-validation against the poster's last 100 comments to confirm label accuracy. To circumvent the safety alignment of proprietary models (GPT4-V, Gemini-Pro), the authors design a gamified, chain-of-thought-extended prompt that frames the task as a detective guessing game, reducing GPT4-V's refusal rate from 54.5% to 0%. An automated zooming pipeline prompts the model to output bounding boxes for regions it deems informative, crops those regions, and feeds them back in a second request, improving precise location accuracy by up to 6.5%. Seven VLMs (two proprietary, five open-source) are evaluated under matched conditions with greedy decoding, and accuracy is correlated with general visual-reasoning benchmark scores (MMMU) to show that inference capability scales with model capability.

## Results

On the VIP dataset, GPT4-V achieves the highest overall private attribute inference accuracy at 77.6%, followed by CogAgent-VQA (66.4%) and Gemini-Pro (60.8%). GPT4-V excels on SEX (94.5%), LOC (87.0%), EDU (77.8%), and OCC (77.4%), while Gemini-Pro uniquely leads on POI (87.0%). Safeguard evasion via gamified prompting reduces GPT4-V's refusal rate from 54.5% (naive prompt) to 0% (final prompt), and automated zooming further improves precise location accuracy by +6.5 percentage points. VLM-based inferences are approximately 117× cheaper and 480× faster than human annotation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GPT4-V dominates 6 out of 8 attributes, achieving 94.5% on SEX and 87.0% on LOC, substantially outperforming CogAgent-VQA (80.5% SEX, 70.1% LOC) and Gemini-Pro (52.3% SEX, 77.9% LOC). Gemini-Pro is the sole exception, surpassing GPT4-V on POI (87.0% vs. 79.3%) but performing near-random on SEX (52.3%) and MAR (26.9%), largely because it often refuses to infer attributes without a visible human. Among open-source models, CogAgent-VQA (66.4%) and Idefics 80B (65.7%) both outperform the proprietary Gemini-Pro (60.8%), while LLaVa 1.5 13B lags considerably at 53.3%. The paper notes that VIP accuracy rankings closely mirror MMMU benchmark rankings, indicating that inference-based privacy risk scales with general model capability. On the human-free image subset, GPT4-V still achieves 76.4% accuracy versus 64.8% for CogAgent-VQA, highlighting a widening gap in non-human-cue reasoning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends Staab et al. [18], which demonstrated that LLMs can make accurate privacy-infringing inferences from previously unseen text, to the multimodal image domain using vision-language models. It deliberately departs from the Human Attribute Recognition (HAR) literature, which focuses on supervised classification of features from direct human depictions, by targeting a fundamentally different setting: inferring private attributes from contextual cues (logos, objects, text in the environment) in images that do not contain visible persons. The evaluation methodology also builds on established prompt-engineering and jailbreaking techniques [21–23] to circumvent safety alignment, and adopts the hardness-scoring and gamification framework from [18].

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes that the cost-and-time barrier that previously protected users' privacy from image-based inference (requiring human investigators) has been effectively eliminated: VLMs are 480× faster and ~117× cheaper than human annotation, and accuracy scales with general model capability, meaning the threat will only grow. The finding that safety alignment is trivially bypassed (54.5% → 1.2% refusal with a simple gamified prompt) undermines a key assumption in current deployment practices. Key open questions include: effective defenses against inference-based image privacy attacks, whether larger or synthetic benchmarks can replace the small (554-label) non-public VIP dataset, and how to balance utility loss against privacy protection in model alignment. The paper explicitly calls for further research into mitigations, which remain undeveloped.
