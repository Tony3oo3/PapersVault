## Problem

Existing benchmarks for Chinese Classical Literature and Language Arts (CCLLA) are limited in scope, focusing primarily on the Ancient Prose sub-domain or containing only a handful of tasks with a single multiple-choice format. This makes it impossible to holistically assess LLM capabilities across the full CCLLA domain, which encompasses Ancient Prose, Ancient Poetry, and Ancient Literary Culture with diverse cognitive demands (understanding, generation, knowledge).
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241625/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each benchmark instance is a Chinese text question in one of three formats (multiple-choice, fill-in-the-blank, or open-ended Q&A), accompanied by a reference answer and metadata specifying the sub-domain, fine-grained task, and assessed capability. The evaluation pipeline produces per-task scores (0–100) aggregated into domain-level (Ancient Prose, Ancient Poetry, Ancient Literary Culture) and capability-level (Understanding, Generation, Knowledge) scores, with the primary metric being a model-scoring score from ERNIE-3.5 (89.4% human consistency) and supplementary traditional metrics (BLEU for translation, F1 for punctuation) for tasks with definitive answers.

## Method

WenMind comprises 4,875 QA pairs across 42 fine-grained tasks (26 coarse-grained) spanning all three CCLLA sub-domains, constructed from three sources: manually curated Internet content (exam questions, poetry databases), open-source corpora (C2MChn, Daizhige, ACLUE, WYWEB, THU-FSPC), and LLM-generated reference answers for open-ended generation tasks. Data undergoes question segmentation, format standardization into dictionary format, MinHashLSH-based deduplication, regex-based noise removal, and full manual proofreading. Evaluation employs ERNIE-3.5 as a scoring judge with task-specific rubrics (binary for single-choice, partial credit for multi-choice, scoring-point extraction for Q&A, 0–1 rubric for subjective generation), supplemented by BLEU and F1 for translation and punctuation tasks. The paper evaluates 31 LLMs (general Chinese/English, Chinese-fine-tuned English, and specialized ancient Chinese models) under standardized inference settings (bf16, greedy decoding, max 2048 tokens), revealing that the best model (ERNIE-4.0) scores only 64.3 and that domain-specific ancient Chinese models underperform general-purpose Chinese models due to insufficient incremental pre-training data causing catastrophic forgetting.

## Results

On the WenMind benchmark (4,875 QA pairs, 42 tasks), ERNIE-4.0 achieves the highest overall score of 64.3, followed by ERNIE-3.5 (62.2) and Spark-3.5 (60.9). The average score across all 31 evaluated LLMs is only 41.2, with over 64% of models scoring below 50 and the worst performer (LLaMA2-7B-Chat) at just 13.0. Across capability dimensions, LLMs perform best on Generation (avg. 54.5) and Understanding (avg. 49.2), but significantly worse on Knowledge (avg. 27.1), revealing a critical knowledge gap in CCLLA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ERNIE-4.0 (64.3) leads all 31 models, outperforming the next-best ERNIE-3.5 (62.2) by 2.1 points and Spark-3.5 (60.9) by 3.4 points. General-purpose Chinese models consistently outperform English-centric models: Qwen1.5-72B-Chat (58.5) and Yi-1.5-34B-Chat (57.4) substantially exceed GPT-4 (50.2) and GPT-3.5 (35.3), while LLaMA2-7B-Chat (13.0) and LLaMA3-8B-Instruct (34.7) remain far behind. Notably, three specialized ancient-Chinese models (Ancient-Chat-LLM-7B: 32.7, Bloom-7B-Chunhua: 32.5, Xunzi-Qwen1.5-7B: 37.0) average only 34.1, underperforming even mid-size general Chinese models like Qwen1.5-7B-Chat (50.3), suggesting that narrow incremental pre-training causes catastrophic forgetting of broader CCLLA knowledge. Within the Qwen family, scaling from 0.5B (26.1) to 72B (58.5) yields a 32.4-point improvement, confirming that the scaling law holds in the CCLLA domain. The Knowledge capability dimension is the weakest across all models (average 27.1), with even ERNIE-4.0 reaching only 57.8, indicating that factual recall of classical literature remains the primary bottleneck.

## Relation to Prior Work

WenMind extends the line of CCLLA evaluation benchmarks (ACLUE with 15 tasks, WYWEB with 9 tasks, CCLUE with 5 tasks) by covering all three sub-domains—Ancient Prose, Ancient Poetry, and Ancient Literary Culture—with 42 fine-grained tasks and three question formats, a twofold increase over the largest prior benchmark. Methodologically, it follows the LLM-as-judge paradigm popularized by MT-Bench and G-Eval, using ERNIE-3.5 as a scoring model with ~89.4% human consistency, while also incorporating traditional metrics (BLEU, F1) for objective tasks. The data construction pipeline draws from open-source corpora (C2MChn, Daizhige, ACLUE, WYWEB, THU-FSPC) and internet-sourced exam questions, positioning WenMind as a consolidation and substantial expansion of fragmented prior resources rather than a departure from them.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals that even the strongest model (ERNIE-4.0) scores only 64.3, and over 64% of 31 evaluated LLMs score below 50, establishing a clear baseline for a culturally significant but underexplored domain. The counterintuitive finding that domain-specific ancient Chinese models underperform general-purpose Chinese models—attributed to insufficient incremental pre-training data causing catastrophic forgetting—challenges the common assumption that specialized fine-tuning always helps. Open questions include whether larger-scale CCLLA pre-training corpora could close the gap, how to balance domain knowledge with general capability, and whether the LLM-as-judge approach (89.4% human consistency) is sufficient for fine-grained literary evaluation. The benchmark's limitation in using a single scoring model and its focus on general evaluation rather than targeted practical scenarios leave room for more nuanced assessment frameworks.
