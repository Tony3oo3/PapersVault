## Problem

Existing image-to-video diffusion models consistently generate videos with less motion than expected, regardless of the desired motion level. The paper identifies the root cause as 'conditional image leakage': at large diffusion time steps, the noisy input becomes heavily corrupted while the static conditional image retains extensive detail of the target video, biasing the model to over-rely on the image and neglect the motion information carried by the noisy input. This issue had been previously overlooked in the I2V-DM literature.

## Input / Output

The method takes a conditional image (typically the first frame of the target video, or a randomly selected frame in DynamiCrafter), an optional text prompt, and for motion-conditioned models an optional motion score. It produces a 16-frame video at 320×512 resolution and 3 fps. The conditional image is consumed differently across baselines: directly concatenated with the noisy video in DynamiCrafter and SVD, or encoded via CLIP ViT patch tokens in VideoCrafter1. The output video is expected to exhibit dynamic, natural object motion while preserving the appearance and content of the input image.

## Method

The paper proposes two complementary strategies. At inference, it starts generation from an earlier time step M < T to avoid the unreliable large-time steps where leakage is worst, and introduces Analytic-Init, which derives a closed-form optimal initial noise distribution N(μ_p*, σ_p*²I) by minimizing KL divergence to the true marginal at time M, bridging the training-inference gap that arises when M is small. At training, it introduces TimeNoise, a time-dependent logit-normal noise distribution p_t(β_s) applied to the conditional image, where the distribution center μ(t) = 2t^a − 1 shifts monotonically so that higher noise levels are sampled at larger time steps, effectively disrupting the image and reducing the model's dependency on it. The approach is general and applicable to both VP-SDE and VE-SDE frameworks, validated on DynamiCrafter, VideoCrafter1, and SVD.

## Results

On UCF101, the full CIL method (TimeNoise + Analytic-Init) substantially improves motion scores across all three I2V-DMs: DynamiCrafter-CIL achieves MS 73.92 (vs. 50.96 baseline), VideoCrafter1-CIL reaches MS 66.7 (vs. 63.36), and SVD-CIL attains MS 21.44 (vs. 16.64). FVD improves for DynamiCrafter (363.8→332.1) and SVD (388.3→272.4), while IS increases for DynamiCrafter (16.39→22.84) and VideoCrafter1 (18.75→25.11). In the noise-initialization comparison (Table 2), Analytic-Init outperforms FrameInit, FreeInit, and Progressive Noise on all metrics (FVD 342.9, IS 22.71, MS 50.08, user rank 1.77) while being the fastest at 22.6 s/it.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DynamiCrafter [63], the full CIL method wins on all three metrics: FVD drops from 363.8 to 332.1, IS rises from 16.39 to 22.84, and MS jumps from 50.96 to 73.92. Against VideoCrafter1 [12], CIL improves IS (18.75→25.11) and MS (63.36→66.7) but FVD worsens (353.9→443.7), reflecting the trade-off of adding TimeNoise to a model that relies solely on CLIP embeddings. Against SVD [9], CIL substantially reduces FVD (388.3→272.4) and increases MS (16.64→21.44), though IS drops (36.32→25.18) due to the fine-tuning regime. In the noise-initialization comparison, Analytic-Init beats FrameInit [41] on every metric (FVD 342.9 vs. 380.7, MS 50.08 vs. 32.16, user rank 1.77 vs. 4.57) and is faster (22.6 vs. 24.3 s/it). It also outperforms FreeInit [61] on FVD (342.9 vs. 347.4), MS (50.08 vs. 46.24), user rank (1.77 vs. 1.95), and is nearly 2× faster (22.6 vs. 49.6 s/it). Finally, Analytic-Init edges out Progressive Noise [19] on FVD (342.9 vs. 358.1), IS (22.71 vs. 21.35), MS (50.08 vs. 49.76), and user rank (1.77 vs. 3.31).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself as a diagnostic and corrective study for existing I2V-DMs rather than proposing a new architecture. It builds directly on Analytic-DPM [4] for the Analytic-Init inference strategy (deriving optimal initial noise distributions via KL minimization) and applies its TimeNoise training strategy to three representative I2V-DMs spanning both VP-SDE (DynamiCrafter, VideoCrafter1) and VE-SDE (SVD) frameworks. Conceptually, it departs from prior I2V work that treats the conditional image as a fixed, clean signal throughout the diffusion process, instead arguing that the image's information content relative to the noisy input shifts over time and must be actively managed. The paper also reframes existing design choices (e.g., using only CLIP embeddings vs. direct concatenation, adding external motion signals) through the lens of CIL, showing they partially mitigate the issue without addressing its root cause.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of conditional image leakage as a systematic, previously overlooked failure mode provides a unifying explanation for why I2V-DMs consistently under-produce motion regardless of conditioning, and the finding that common noise-schedule adjustments toward higher noise actually exacerbate the problem is a useful caution for practitioners. The proposed strategies are model-agnostic and lightweight (20K fine-tuning iterations on 8 GPUs), making them broadly applicable to existing I2V-DMs. However, the paper acknowledges that hyperparameter tuning of TimeNoise (β_m, exponent a) requires a trade-off between motion and image alignment, and no principled choice is provided for models trained from scratch. Open questions include whether CIL persists in next-generation video diffusion transformers and whether the Analytic-Init framework extends naturally to flow-matching or rectified-flow formulations.
