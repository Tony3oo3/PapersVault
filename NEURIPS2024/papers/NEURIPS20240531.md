## Problem

While implicit regularization of centralized gradient descent for overparameterized sparse regression is well understood, it remains unclear whether decentralized gradient descent (DGD) on an unpenalized, nonconvex overparameterized formulation can also recover statistically optimal sparse solutions. Existing decentralized sparse regression methods rely on explicit ℓ₁ regularization (LASSO, NetLASSO), and the question of whether the implicit bias of DGD itself can promote sparsity over mesh networks was entirely unexplored.

## Input / Output

Each of m agents holds n local noisy linear measurements (x_{i,j}, y_{i,j}) generated from a common sparse ground truth w* with s ≪ d nonzeros, and agents communicate only with one-hop neighbors over an undirected graph with spectral gap ρ. The method operates in the high-dimensional regime d ≫ N = mn, where the ambient dimension far exceeds total samples. The output is the averaged estimator w̄^t = u^t ⊙ u^t − v^t ⊙ v^t at an early-stopping time t, which identifies the support S and estimates w* on S while keeping non-support coordinates at O(√α) magnitude.

## Method

The paper applies standard DGD to the regularization-free nonconvex least-squares problem obtained by reparameterizing w = u ⊙ u − v ⊙ v, with small initialization α·1_d and early stopping. The key technical challenge is that consensus errors from the mesh network turn the exact multiplicative update of centralized GD into an inexact one, with an extra additive error term outside the multiplication and more complex perturbation terms inside it. The authors resolve this by separately bounding consensus errors on support S and non-support S^c using the magnitudes of the respective parameters, a fine-grained analysis distinct from uniform consensus-error bounds in prior decentralized optimization work. They prove that under RIP conditions, sufficiently small α, appropriate step size, and network connectivity (ρ^{1/4} bounded by ratios of global-to-local RIP and noise parameters), DGD with early stopping achieves the minimax-optimal rate O(σ√(s log d / N)) independent of d. A communication-efficient variant T-DGD truncates transmitted vectors to the top-s largest-magnitude elements per round, reducing communication cost from O(d) to O(s) per iteration while preserving the same statistical accuracy under high SNR.

## Results

The main theoretical result (Theorem 1 / Corollary 1) shows that DGD with small initialization (α ≲ 1) and early stopping achieves the minimax-optimal statistical error ‖w̄^t − w⋆‖ ≲ σ√(s log d / N) under the ℓ₂ metric, matching the centralized GD rate, provided the spectral gap satisfies ρ ≲ 1/m⁴ and the step size is chosen as η = O((1−((1+√ρ)/2)^(1/4))/w_max⋆). The iteration complexity is t = O(w_max⋆√N / (σ√(log d)·(1−((1+√ρ)/2)^(1/4)))) · log(1/α), which is logarithmic in the ambient dimension d. The proposed T-DGD (Proposition 1) achieves the same statistical accuracy while reducing per-iteration communication from O(d) to O(s) elements, yielding total communication complexity O(s · w_max⋆√N / (σ√(log d)·(1−((1+√ρ)/2)^(1/4)))) · log(1/α), i.e., logarithmic in d rather than linear.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares DGD and T-DGD against three explicit-regularization baselines: CTADGD (LASSO) [Ji et al., 2023], ATC-DGD (LASSO) [Ji et al., 2023], and DGT (NetLASSO) [Sun et al., 2022]. In the numerical comparison (Fig. 5, d=1000, s=5, m=50, N=280, σ=0.5), DGD achieves the best recovery performance across all three network connectivity settings (ρ = 0.9428, 0.6367, 0.1384) with the fewest iterations, outperforming all three LASSO-based methods. A key theoretical distinction is that DGD's statistical error is network-independent (no dependence on d or m), whereas CTADGD has polynomial dependence on d, and ATC-DGD and DGT, while d-independent in convergence rate, still require explicit ℓ₁ regularization and gradient-tracking/consensus-penalty machinery. In the truncated-communication setting (Fig. 6, d=1000, s=5, m=50, N=550, σ=0.1, ρ=0.2458), T-DGD is the only method to achieve successful sparse recovery when Top-s truncation is applied; all three truncated baselines (Trun-CTA-DGD, Trun-ATC-DGD, Trun-DGT) fail entirely, demonstrating that naively combining sparsification with decentralized LASSO solvers does not guarantee convergence.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the centralized implicit regularization analysis for sparse regression via Hadamard product reparameterization (Vaskevicius et al. 2019; Zhao et al. 2019) to the decentralized DGD setting, where prior work had only studied centralized GD. It departs from the dominant line of decentralized sparse regression literature (Ji et al. 2023; Sun et al. 2022) that relies on explicit LASSO/NetLASSO penalization, instead exploiting the implicit sparsity-inducing behavior of overparameterized unpenalized least squares. The DGD convergence framework from Yuan et al. (2016) provides the algorithmic backbone, but the paper's key technical contribution is a fine-grained consensus error analysis that separately controls errors on support and non-support, going beyond the uniform bounding used in prior decentralized optimization analyses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that vanilla DGD without gradient tracking or correction achieves minimax-optimal statistical rates for sparse regression challenges the prevailing assumption in decentralized optimization that gradient-correction techniques are necessary for heterogeneous data settings. The T-DGD variant further shows that communication complexity can be reduced from linear to logarithmic in ambient dimension while preserving statistical accuracy, though only under high-SNR conditions. Key limitations include the reliance on the RIP condition (rather than the weaker restricted eigenvalue condition), restrictive spectral gap requirements that scale as m^{-4} in the sub-Gaussian corollary, and validation only on synthetic Gaussian data. Open questions include whether the RIP assumption can be relaxed and whether similar implicit regularization phenomena extend to more complex overparameterized architectures in decentralized settings.
