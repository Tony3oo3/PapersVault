## Problem

Current robotic learning methods treat different instruction and observation modalities (text, audio, image, video, point cloud) as separate challenges, with each system specialized to a single modality pair. No unified framework exists that allows a robot to accept arbitrary combinations of instruction and observation modalities—whether in isolation or in tandem—while maintaining strong generalization across tasks and environments.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20244243/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system accepts any subset of four instruction modalities (text, audio, image end-goal, video demonstration) and any subset of three observation modalities (RGB image, video, point cloud), with the policy required to function correctly regardless of which combination is provided at inference time. Token counts before pruning are 81 for images, 81×t for t-frame videos, 256 for point clouds, and 1 for text/audio. The output is a continuous absolute joint-position action vector, with one history action token appended to the input sequence to provide temporal context.

## Method

Any2Policy uses ImageBind as a frozen unified encoder across all five modalities, followed by learnable projection layers that map each modality's features into a shared token space. An embodied alignment module then applies TokenLearner to prune visual tokens (81→8 for images/videos, 256→16 for point clouds), inserts modality-distinguishing tokens, and processes the merged sequence through a single self-attention transformer block. Instruction and observation are fused via cross-attention where the observation serves as keys, the instruction as values, and 16 learnable query embeddings act as queries, enabling the policy to selectively attend to relevant instruction content at each timestep. The policy network consists of L transformer blocks (self-attention + cross-attention + FFN with residual connections) followed by an MLP head, trained end-to-end with behavior cloning MSE loss while keeping ImageBind frozen.

## Results

On the real-world RoboAny dataset (30 tasks, 30 trajectories each), Any2Policy achieves a mean success rate of 62±13% on the Image+Text→Image setting, outperforming VIMA (49%), R3M (42–46%), and T5 (39%) across all evaluated modality combinations. In the multi-modality ablation (Table 4), combining Text+Image End-Goal instructions with Image+Point Cloud observations yields the highest success rate of 69%. On the Franka Kitchen simulation benchmark, Any2Policy outperforms R3M, BLIP-2, and EmbodiedGPT on nearly all tasks with both 10 and 25 demonstrations, except Knobs-left with 25 demos.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Any2Policy outperforms all five compared baselines across the evaluated settings. Against VIMA [59], which uses text goals and object images, Any2Policy achieves 62% vs. 49% success rate on Image+Text→Image, a 13-point gain. Against R3M [109], a universal visual representation, Any2Policy leads by 2 points on Image→Image (44 vs. 42) and by 13 points on Video→Image (59 vs. 46). Against T5 [110], a text-to-text transformer used for language conditioning, Any2Policy achieves 51% vs. 39% on Text→Image, a 12-point improvement. On the Franka Kitchen simulation, Any2Policy surpasses both BLIP-2 [22] and EmbodiedGPT [113] on nearly all five tasks with 10 and 25 demonstrations, with the sole exception being Knobs-left at 25 demos where EmbodiedGPT edges ahead. The consistent advantage over modality-specific baselines (Table 1, where Any2Policy leads by 8–25 points across all 12 instruction-observation pairs) demonstrates that multi-modal training yields better generalization than single-modality training with the same data volume.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the multi-modal representation learning literature (ImageBind for unified encoding, TokenLearner for token pruning) and the robot manipulation line of work (RT-1, VIMA, MUTEX) that has explored individual modalities for task specification or observation. The key departure is treating both instruction and observation as multi-modal inputs simultaneously, rather than the prior approach of fixing one modality for each. The novel 'embodied alignment' module—using learnable cross-attention queries to fuse instruction tokens into observation tokens—addresses the asymmetry between instruction (sparse, task-level) and observation (dense, per-timestep) that prior multi-modal robot systems like VIMA and MUTEX did not explicitly handle.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The RoboAny dataset (30 tasks × 30 trajectories × 5 instructions × multiple observation modalities) is the first robotics dataset to span text, audio, image, video, and point cloud for both instruction and observation, filling a gap that limits reproducibility in multi-modal robot learning. The consistent finding that multi-modal training improves single-modality inference generalization (e.g., 51% vs 39% for text→image) provides empirical grounding for the multi-modal learning hypothesis in embodied AI. Open questions include whether the approach scales beyond 30 tasks in a single environment, how it handles truly novel object categories, and whether the relatively weak point-cloud results (36–62%) can be closed with better 3D-specific fusion.
