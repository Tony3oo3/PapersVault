## Problem

Existing dataset condensation methods are designed for classification, where prediction similarity is binary (same label suffices). For time series forecasting, similarity requires every predicted data point to be close (measured by MSE), a far stricter criterion that classification-based methods fail to satisfy, leading to significant performance degradation when directly applied to TS-forecasting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20244071/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a full time series training set (24-step input windows predicting 24-step future windows), a pre-trained DLinear expert model (and a buffer of k expert models trained from different random initializations), and a small synthetic dataset initialized by random sampling from the training set. It outputs a condensed synthetic dataset of length 48 (a single training pair at the standard ~0.83‰ distill ratio) that, when used to train a new model, yields predictions closely matching those of a model trained on the full dataset. The synthetic data is updated in-place without backpropagation through the backbone condensation method.

## Method

The paper theoretically decomposes the TS-forecasting condensation objective into a gradient term (parameter distance between student and expert models) and a value term (prediction distance on synthetic inputs) via first-order Taylor expansion and Cauchy-Schwarz inequality. The gradient term is optimized by existing trajectory-matching backbones (MTT, PP, TESLA, FTD, DATM), while the value term is optimized by CondTSF: a one-line additive update that replaces each synthetic label with a convex combination of itself and the expert model's prediction on the corresponding synthetic input (s ← (1−β)·s + β·M_θf(s), β=0.01). This update reduces the label error exponentially per iteration and requires no backpropagation. CondTSF is applied every 3 epochs alongside the backbone's trajectory matching, enabling synchronous optimization of both terms. The key enabling observation is that expert models trained from different random initializations on the same full dataset produce nearly identical predictions on any given input, so any buffered expert model can stand in for the unavailable test-time expert.

## Results

On the standard condensation setting (48 synthetic samples), CondTSF plugged into MTT reduces MAE by 23–75% and MSE by 31–94% across all eight datasets (e.g., ExchangeRate MAE drops from 0.778 to 0.195, ETTh2 MSE from 0.827 to 0.205). The improvement is most dramatic at the lowest condensing ratio (0.83‰–6.33‰), where MTT+CondTSF on ExchangeRate achieves MAE 0.195 vs. 0.110 for the full dataset. DC (gradient matching) sees only modest gains (1.7–15.8% MAE reduction) because its indirect gradient matching accumulates parameter error. At 3× the standard ratio, improvements shrink to 0.3–27% MAE reduction, confirming CondTSF's value is greatest under extreme data scarcity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CondTSF is evaluated as a plugin on six parameter-matching backbones (DC, MTT, PP, TESLA, FTD, DATM) and four non-parameter-matching methods (DM, IDM, KIP, FRePo). On trajectory-matching methods (MTT, PP, FTD, DATM), CondTSF yields the largest gains: 23–75% MAE reduction, with MTT+CondTSF on ExchangeRate reaching MAE 0.195 (vs. 0.778 without), approaching the full-dataset MAE of 0.110. TESLA benefits similarly (22–49% MAE reduction). DC (gradient matching) shows the weakest improvement (0.7–15.8% MAE) because its indirect gradient matching accumulates parameter drift, limiting the value-term optimization. Among non-parameter-matching baselines, KIP+CondTSF achieves a striking 59.6% MAE drop on ExchangeRate (0.538→0.217), while FRePo+CondTSF gains 16–48% MAE reduction; DM and IDM see only modest 1–13% improvements. In cross-architecture tests (Table 3), MTT+CondTSF outperforms all other methods across MLP, LSTM, and CNN test models on every dataset, confirming that the condensed data generalizes beyond the DLinear expert architecture.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the dataset condensation/distillation literature (DC, MTT, PP, TESLA, FTD, DATM, KIP, FRePo) from classification to time series forecasting, where the evaluation criterion shifts from label agreement to point-wise prediction similarity. The key conceptual departure is a theoretical decomposition (via Taylor expansion and Cauchy-Schwarz) showing that the TS-forecasting objective splits into a 'gradient term' (already optimized by trajectory-matching methods) and a 'value term' (previously neglected). CondTSF is positioned as a one-line additive-update plugin that optimizes the value term without backpropagation, complementing rather than replacing existing backbones.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first systematic treatment of dataset condensation for TS-forecasting, providing both a theoretical framework (value + gradient term decomposition) and a trivially simple plugin that dramatically improves all existing methods at low condensing ratios (e.g., 75% MAE reduction for MTT on ExchangeRate with only 48 synthetic samples). The main limitation is that the theoretical analysis relies on linear models (DLinear) as expert models, so the guarantees do not formally extend to non-linear architectures, though empirical results in the appendix suggest broader applicability. Open questions include whether the value-term optimization generalizes to non-linear experts, whether the additive update ratio β can be adapted per-dataset, and whether the approach transfers to other regression-style tasks beyond TS-forecasting.
