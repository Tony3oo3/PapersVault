## Problem

Existing theoretical analyses of diffusion models either assume the score function is already well-approximated and study only sampling error, or analyze training in highly specific settings (e.g., Gaussian mixtures, two-layer networks). No prior work provides a unified, non-asymptotic error bound that simultaneously captures both the optimization dynamics of denoising score matching and the sampling error of the backward SDE, leaving the interaction between training and sampling design choices (noise distribution, weighting, time and variance schedules) theoretically unexplored.

## Input / Output

The analysis takes as input a dataset of n samples from an initial distribution P0 with finite second moment, N discrete time points for the forward process, and a deep L-layer ReLU network of width m initialized with Gaussian weights. The output is a KL divergence bound between the true data distribution p_δ and the distribution q_{T-δ} of samples generated by simulating the backward SDE with the trained score network. The bound decomposes into initialization error, discretization error, and score estimation error, with the latter further split into optimization, statistical, estimation, and approximation components.

## Method

The paper establishes three main results. First, it proves exponential convergence of gradient descent for the denoising score matching objective on a deep ReLU network (Theorem 1), developing a new geometric decoupling technique to lower-bound the gradient under the semi-smoothness framework, handling the unbounded Gaussian output and noisy input inherent to the denoising setting. Second, it extends the stochastic localization approach of Benton et al. to the variance-exploding SDE (Theorem 2), obtaining a sampling error bound under only a finite second moment assumption and achieving almost-linear dependence on data dimension under exponential time schedules. Third, it combines both results into a full generation error analysis (Theorem 3) and uses it to derive design principles: a bell-shaped total weighting for training (matching EDM's choice), and a schedule-dependent recommendation where polynomial schedules (EDM) are preferable when score error dominates (less-trained networks) while exponential schedules (Song et al.) are preferable when discretization and initialization errors dominate (well-trained networks).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical baselines or dataset evaluations. The key comparisons are theoretical: (1) For sampling, the paper extends Benton et al. [8] (VPSDE, almost linear in d under exponential schedule) to the VESDE setting, matching the almost-linear d-dependence while requiring only finite second moment (vs. bounded support assumed by Yang et al. [54] for VESDE); (2) For training, the paper improves on Han et al. [25] by proving (rather than assuming) a gradient lower bound, handling general time/variance schedules, and providing a non-asymptotic optimization error bound; (3) The central design-space comparison shows that the polynomial schedule [30] yields a smaller score-error prefactor (favorable when the network is undertrained), while the exponential schedule [46] yields poly-logarithmic dependence on σ̄_max/σ̄_min in iteration complexity (favorable when the score is well-approximated and discretization error dominates). The paper's 'bell-shaped' total weighting prediction qualitatively matches EDM's empirically-derived weighting β_EDM(σ̄).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate lines of diffusion model theory: sampling error analysis (Benton et al. [8] for VPSDE, Yang et al. [54] for VESDE) and training convergence analysis (Shah et al. [44], Han et al. [25]). It extends Benton et al.'s stochastic localization framework from VPSDE to the variance-exploding setting under only a finite second moment assumption, and adapts the semi-smoothness/gradient-lower-bound framework of Allen-Zhu et al. [1] to the denoising score matching objective with its unbounded Gaussian outputs and noisy inputs. The resulting full error analysis (Theorem 3) is the first to simultaneously bound optimization error and sampling error, enabling theoretical guidance on design choices (weighting, time/variance schedules) that prior partial analyses could not provide.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first theoretical work to provide an end-to-end generation error bound for diffusion models, bridging the gap between training dynamics and sampling quality. The qualitative finding that the preferred schedule (polynomial vs. exponential) depends on whether the score function is well-trained or not offers a principled explanation for why EDM and Song et al. designs each work well in different regimes. Key limitations include the use of a simplified deep ReLU architecture (far from practical U-Nets/transformers), the requirement that sample size n be smaller than network width m, and the fact that the full error decomposition (optimization + statistical + estimation + approximation) may not yield tight bounds. Open questions include whether the non-vanishing lower bound on the denoising objective (Remark 1) is intrinsic or an artifact of the analysis, and whether the theoretical design principles extend to more complex architectures.
