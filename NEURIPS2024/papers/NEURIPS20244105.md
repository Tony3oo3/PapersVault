## Problem

Existing OOD detection research is almost entirely confined to unimodal image settings, yet real-world safety-critical applications (autonomous driving, robot-assisted surgery) are inherently multimodal. No benchmark exists for evaluating OOD detection across multiple modalities, and unimodal scoring methods fail to exploit the complementary and interacting information that different modalities provide, leaving a substantial performance gap unaddressed.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20244105/cropped_page2_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes M modalities per sample (video clips, dense optical flow at 24 fps, and audio spectrograms), each processed by a dedicated feature extractor (SlowFast for video/flow, ResNet-18 for audio) initialized from pre-trained Kinetics-400 or VGGSound weights. At inference, a single OOD score is produced from the combined embedding or fused logits, while per-modality classifiers also yield individual predictions that are used during training to enforce the discrepancy objective. The benchmark spans five action-recognition datasets (EPIC-Kitchens, HAC, HMDB51, UCF101, Kinetics-600) with 7–229 classes and 3k–57k clips, evaluated under both Near-OOD (class-split) and Far-OOD (cross-dataset) protocols.

## Method

The paper introduces the MultiOOD benchmark and two training-time components. First, the Agree-to-Disagree (A2D) loss exploits the observed Modality Prediction Discrepancy—per-modality softmax predictions are nearly identical on ID data but diverge on OOD data—by removing the ground-truth class from each modality's prediction vector and maximizing the Hellinger distance between the remaining C−1 probabilities, thereby forcing modalities to agree on the true class while disagreeing on all others. Second, NP-Mix synthesizes outliers by computing per-class prototype embeddings from concatenated multi-modal features, selecting a nearest-neighbor class prototype, and forming a convex combination (λ ~ Beta(α,α) with α>1) of two samples from different classes, so that synthesized outliers span broader embedding regions rather than clustering near ID data. The final training objective combines standard cross-entropy on all classifiers with a weighted discrepancy loss on both real and synthesized samples plus an entropy-maximization term on synthesized outliers, and the approach is architecture-agnostic, validated across CNN (SlowFast, I3D, TSN) and Transformer (Swin-T, AST) backbones.

## Results

On the MultiOOD Near-OOD benchmark (video + optical flow), A2D + NP-Mix consistently improves all 10 baseline OOD detection methods. The most striking improvement is ASH on UCF101 50/51, where FPR95 drops from 32.14% to 10.68% (−21.46 pp absolute). On HMDB51 25/26, VIM++ achieves the best FPR95 of 4.56% (vs. 36.82% baseline), and on Kinetics-600 129/100, Mahalanobis++ gains +14.22 pp AUROC (35.83% → 50.05%). In the Far-OOD setup with HMDB51 as ID, ASH++ reduces average FPR95 from 42.02% to 24.03% (−17.99 pp).

## Baselines & Comparisons

A2D + NP-Mix improves every one of the 10 OOD scoring/training baselines (MSP, Energy, MaxLogit, Mahalanobis, ReAct, ASH, GEN, KNN, VIM, LogitNorm) across all four Near-OOD datasets, with the largest FPR95 reductions seen for ASH (−21.46 pp on UCF101) and VIM (−7.96 pp on UCF101). Among outlier synthesis methods, NP-Mix outperforms VOS, NPOS, and standard Mixup on all four tested scorers (e.g., Energy: NP-Mix FPR95 36.38 vs. VOS 37.69, NPOS 43.14, Mixup 42.27 on HMDB51). Against alternative multimodal training objectives (Contrastive Loss, RNA Loss, Cross-modal Distillation, Cross-modal Translation), A2D + NP-Mix achieves the best FPR95 and AUROC for every scorer; notably, RNA Loss and Cross-modal Distillation actually degrade performance relative to the untrained baseline (e.g., Energy FPR95 rises from 43.36 to 55.55 with RNA Loss), while A2D + NP-Mix reduces it to 36.38. The method is architecture-agnostic, maintaining gains when SlowFast is replaced by I3D or Swin-T and the flow backbone is swapped to TSN or AST.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the well-established unimodal OOD detection literature (MSP, Energy, Mahalanobis, KNN, VIM, ASH, ReAct, GEN, LogitNorm) to a multimodal setting, arguing that prior work's focus on single-modality image data leaves complementary information from other sensors (optical flow, audio, LiDAR) untapped. Conceptually, it repurposes the outlier synthesis paradigm (VOS, NPOS, Mixup) by introducing nearest-neighbor prototype-based mixing (NP-Mix) to explore broader embedding spaces, and introduces a novel discrepancy-maximization loss (A2D) motivated by an observed phenomenon rather than a prior theoretical framework. The paper does not build on a specific prior multimodal OOD method, as it explicitly positions itself as the first to formalize this problem.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MultiOOD fills a clear gap by providing the first systematic benchmark for multimodal OOD detection, enabling fair comparison of methods that exploit complementary sensor information in safety-critical domains like autonomous driving and robotics. The Modality Prediction Discrepancy observation offers a useful diagnostic signal and a principled training objective, though the practical impact is bounded by the benchmark's restriction to action recognition (video/flow/audio) rather than truly heterogeneous modalities. Open questions include whether A2D and NP-Mix generalize to non-video multimodal settings (e.g., LiDAR + camera + radar), whether the approach scales to very large class spaces, and how Outlier Exposure might further sharpen the ID/OOD boundary.
