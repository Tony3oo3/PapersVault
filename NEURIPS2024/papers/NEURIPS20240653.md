## Problem

Nesterov's accelerated gradient descent (NAG) provably achieves O(1/n²) convergence for smooth convex optimization only when the stochastic gradient noise is strictly smaller than the true gradient in the L² sense (σ < 1). In overparametrized machine learning, where minibatch gradient noise scales proportionally to the gradient magnitude (multiplicative noise), the signal-to-noise ratio can be arbitrarily large, causing NAG to diverge. The paper asks whether acceleration can be preserved under arbitrarily large multiplicative noise.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes an L-smooth objective function, an initial point x₀, and unbiased stochastic gradient estimates g(x,ω) whose variance is bounded by σ²‖∇f(x)‖² for any noise level σ ≥ 0. It requires only two hyperparameters (learning rate α and correction step size η) in the convex case and three (adding momentum ρ) in the strongly convex case, improving on the three-to-four parameters needed by competing methods like ACDM or MaSS. The output is a sequence of iterates xₙ converging to a minimizer at the accelerated rate, with the noise level σ affecting only the constant in the bound, not the rate.

## Method

AGNES modifies Nesterov's two-step iteration by decoupling the momentum step from the gradient step: while NAG uses x'ₙ₊₁ = xₙ₊₁ + ρₙ(x'ₙ − ηg'ₙ − xₙ), AGNES introduces a separate parameter α in the momentum term, giving x'ₙ₊₁ = xₙ₊₁ + ρₙ(x'ₙ − αg'ₙ − xₙ). Equivalently, AGNES maintains a velocity variable vₙ updated as vₙ₊₁ = ρₙ(vₙ − g'ₙ), with the position updated as xₙ₊₁ = xₙ + αvₙ₊₁ − ηg'ₙ. The key insight is that NAG's constraint α = η makes the Lyapunov analysis fail when σ ≥ 1, while allowing α < η (specifically α = η/(1+σ²) in the convex case) restores the decreasing Lyapunov sequence for any σ. Convergence is proven via a discrete Lyapunov sequence mirroring the continuous-time heavy ball ODE, yielding O(1/n²) rates for convex and exponential rates for strongly convex objectives regardless of noise magnitude.

## Results

Theoretically, AGNES provably achieves the optimal O(1/n²) convergence rate for convex minimization and exponential convergence for strongly convex minimization at any noise level σ, whereas Nesterov's accelerated gradient descent (NAG) only converges when σ < 1 and diverges for σ ≥ 1. In terms of time complexity, AGNES reduces the convex case from O(L/ε · (1+σ²)) for SGD to O(√(L/ε) · (1+σ²)), and the strongly convex case from O(L/μ · (1+σ²)·|log ε|) to O(√(L/μ) · (1+σ²)·|log ε|). Empirically, AGNES with default hyperparameters (α=10⁻³, η=10⁻², ρ=0.99) consistently outperforms SGD with momentum and NAG in both training loss and test accuracy on CIFAR-10 (ResNet-34, ResNet-50) and MNIST (LeNet-5), with the performance gap widening as batch size decreases (i.e., as gradient noise increases).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against NAG (Nesterov 1983), AGNES wins decisively: in theory, NAG diverges when σ ≥ 1 while AGNES converges for all σ; empirically, AGNES achieves lower training loss and higher test accuracy than NAG across all batch sizes on CIFAR-10 and MNIST, and outperforms all 80 tested NAG hyperparameter combinations (learning rates from 8×10⁻⁵ to 0.5, momentum from 0.2 to 0.99) on ResNet-34/CIFAR-10. Against ACDM (Nesterov 2012) and CNM (Even et al. 2021), AGNES performs comparably in the convex optimization experiments (Figure 3), with all three accelerated methods significantly outperforming SGD after an initial plateau. Against Adam, AGNES matches or exceeds performance at large batch sizes (100) but clearly outperforms Adam at smaller batch sizes (50, 10) where gradient noise is higher. Against SGD with momentum (Polyak 1964), AGNES consistently achieves faster convergence in both training loss and test accuracy across all tested settings, with the advantage growing as batch size shrinks.

## Relation to Prior Work

AGNES is positioned as a reparametrization of Liu and Belkin's (2018) MaSS method that achieves the same theoretical guarantees as Vaswani et al.'s (2019) ACDM but with fewer hyperparameters (2 in convex, 3 in strongly convex, versus 3 and 4 respectively). The paper also builds on the continuum-limit and Lyapunov analysis tradition of Su et al. (2014), Siegel (2019), and Wilson et al. (2021) to derive convergence proofs, and on Even et al. (2021) and Berthier et al. (2021) for the continuized Nesterov method comparison. A key departure from prior work is the explicit demonstration that Nesterov's original method diverges under multiplicative noise with σ ≥ 1, whereas gradient descent remains convergent, motivating the additional parameter α that 'bifurcates' the learning rate.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first provable acceleration guarantee for arbitrary signal-to-noise ratios under multiplicative noise, closing a gap where prior methods (NAG, ACDM) either required σ < 1 or were restricted to specific problem classes. The geometric interpretation of parameters and the continuum-limit analysis revealing Hessian-damping dynamics offer useful intuition for hyperparameter selection in practice. However, the multiplicative noise model is well-motivated at global minimizers in overparametrized settings but not at generic critical points, limiting direct applicability to the non-convex landscape of deep learning. The authors acknowledge they did not test predictions over a broad set of benchmark problems, and the practical gains, while consistent, are moderate relative to well-tuned Adam.
