## Problem

Existing SOTA complete L∞-robustness verifiers treat each DNN execution independently and cannot exploit dependencies between multiple executions, making them imprecise for relational properties like universal adversarial perturbation (UAP) verification. Conversely, the SOTA relational verifier RACoon leverages cross-executional dependencies but applies only a single bounding step without any branching strategy, leaving substantial imprecision from parametric linear relaxations unexploited. No prior method combines scalable branch-and-bound over multiple executions with cross-executional bound refinement.

## Input / Output

RABBit takes a ReLU DNN N, k input images (e.g., 50 from CIFAR-10 or MNIST), an L∞ perturbation bound ε, and an output specification (e.g., correct classification). The relational input specification Φ enforces that the same perturbation δ is applied to all k inputs (x_i* − x_j* = x_i − x_j), which is the key structural constraint distinguishing UAP verification from independent per-input robustness. The output is a verified lower bound on worst-case UAP accuracy (the guaranteed minimum fraction of k inputs remaining correctly classified under the worst common δ), verified top-k accuracy, or a counterexample perturbation if the property is violated.

## Method

RABBit introduces two complementary branch-and-bound algorithms. Strong Bounding constructs a 'product DNN' by duplicating the network n times and branches jointly over all executions, computing at each node a cross-executional bound via a Lagrangian dual that introduces dual variables β for branching constraints and λ parameters to couple linear approximations across executions, optimized by projected gradient ascent. Strong Branching instead branches independently on each execution but first uses RACoon's cross-executional refinement to select fixed 'target coefficients,' then refines only the bias term for each target via BaB with a closed-form inner minimization (Theorem 4.2), keeping the number of linear approximations constant. The final RABBit algorithm greedily selects subsets of executions for strong bounding and target coefficients for strong branching, then combines the resulting refined biases and verified no-common-perturbation subsets into an MILP with only O(k) integer variables, solved by Gurobi to produce the tightest verified UAP accuracy bound.

## Results

RABBit achieves worst-case UAP accuracy improvements of up to +4.8% over RACoon (the SOTA relational verifier) on CIFAR-10 and up to +3.2% on MNIST, across ConvSmall and ConvBig architectures trained with Standard, DiffAI, SABR, and CITRUS methods. For instance, on CIFAR-10 ConvSmall with DiffAI at ε=5/255, RABBit reaches 59.8% vs. RACoon's 51.6% and α,β-CROWN's 53.8%; on MNIST ConvSmall with SABR at ε=0.15, RABBit achieves 63.4% vs. RACoon's 45.8%. RABBit also scales to the larger ConvBig architecture, gaining +1.6% over RACoon on CIFAR-10 ConvBig (DiffAI, ε=2/255). The individual components (Strong Bounding and Strong Branching) each outperform all baselines, with RABBit combining both to achieve the best overall results.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RABBit outperforms all eight baselines across every dataset, network, training method, and perturbation bound evaluated. Against the SOTA relational verifier RACoon, RABBit gains +0.6% to +4.8% in worst-case UAP accuracy, with the largest gains on DiffAI-trained networks where cross-executional dependencies are most exploitable. The best non-relational verifier α,β-CROWN is consistently outperformed (e.g., 62.2% vs. 65.4% on CIFAR-10 ConvSmall Standard; 53.8% vs. 59.8% on CIFAR-10 ConvSmall DiffAI), confirming that ignoring cross-executional dependencies leads to significant imprecision. The I/O formulation [Zeng et al., 2023] and CROWN/α-CROWN baselines are substantially outperformed (by 10–20+ points in many settings), as they either track only input-level relationships or treat executions independently. Against RaVeN (compared at k=5 in Appendix Table 4), RABBit achieves up to +19% improvement (82.0% vs. 66.0% on MNIST ConvSmall DiffAI), demonstrating that branching-based refinement far exceeds the precision of abstract-interpretation-based relational tracking. Time-accuracy curves (Figures 1–3) confirm that RABBit's advantage over α,β-CROWN and RACoon is not an artifact of timeout settings but holds at all timestamps.

## Relation to Prior Work

RABBit sits at the intersection of two lines of DNN verification research: non-relational branch-and-bound verifiers (α,β-CROWN, MN-BaB, GCP-CROWN) that refine bounds through branching over ReLU activations but ignore cross-execution dependencies, and relational verifiers (RaVeN, RACoon) that exploit cross-executional bound refinement but lack any branching strategy. The paper's core idea is to combine these two complementary mechanisms—branching and cross-executional refinement—into two distinct BaB algorithms (strong bounding and strong branching) whose results are then unified in a MILP formulation. This departs from RACoon, which applies only a single bounding step, and from α,β-CROWN, which branches without any cross-executional coupling, by showing that the two techniques are synergistic rather than substitutable.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical relevance is clear: UAP verification is a natural safety question for deployed DNNs, and the +3–5% worst-case accuracy gains over the SOTA relational verifier RACoon translate into meaningfully tighter safety certificates for robustly trained networks. However, the paper is sound but incomplete and does not scale to ImageNet-sized networks, a limitation shared by all deterministic verifiers. An open question is whether the MILP combination step can be replaced by a purely differentiable objective to avoid the Gurobi bottleneck, and whether the approach extends to non-ReLU activations (e.g., Sigmoid) where branching is less straightforward.
