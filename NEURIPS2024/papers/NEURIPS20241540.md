## Problem

Existing permutation-equivariant neural functional networks (NFNs) only account for neuron permutation symmetries in weight spaces, ignoring weight scaling symmetries of ReLU networks and sign-flipping symmetries of sin/tanh networks. Consequently, two weight configurations that represent the same function but differ by a scaling or sign-flip transformation can yield different NFN outputs, a fundamental limitation that also inflates the number of trainable parameters.

## Input / Output

The method consumes the full structured weight space of a target network — weight matrices W^(i) ∈ R^{w_i × n_i × n_{i-1}} and bias vectors b^(i) ∈ R^{b_i × n_i × 1} for each layer i — together with the architecture specification (layer count L, channel counts n_i, and activation type) that determines the monomial matrix group action. Outputs are task-specific: a scalar Kendall's-τ-correlated generalization score, a discrete class label for INR image classification, or a modified weight tensor for style editing. The input is not flattened; the multi-dimensional tensor structure is preserved so that the group action can be applied entry-wise.

## Method

The paper proves that the maximal group of transformations preserving FCNN/CNN functions under ReLU (resp. sin/tanh) activation is a subgroup of the monomial matrix group G_n^{>0} (resp. G_n^{±1}), i.e., the semidirect product of positive diagonal scalings (resp. sign flips) with permutation matrices. It then constructs G-equivariant linear layers by solving the full system of parameter-sharing constraints induced by E(gU) = gE(U), which forces most cross-layer and cross-channel coefficients to zero, yielding a parameter count linear in L, n_0, n_L rather than quadratic as in prior permutation-equivariant NFNs. For G-invariant readout, the method composes a Δ^{>0}-invariant (or Δ^{±1}-invariant) layer built from positively homogeneous degree-zero (or even) functions with a permutation-invariant pooling layer, followed by an MLP. The resulting Monomial-NFN stacks these equivariant and invariant blocks, using the same activation family as the input network between equivariant layers to maintain equivariance.

## Results

On the Small CNN Zoo Tanh subset, Monomial-NFN achieves 0.939 Kendall's τ on original data and 0.943 on augmented data, outperforming HNP (0.933/0.935), NP (0.925/0.928), and STATNN (0.913/0.914). For INR classification, Monomial-NFN reaches 34.23% on CIFAR-10, 68.43% on MNIST, and 61.15% on FashionMNIST. On weight-space editing, it achieves 0.020 MSE for CIFAR-10 contrast and 0.069 MSE for MNIST dilation, matching or approaching the best baselines. Across all tasks, Monomial-NFN uses up to 50% fewer parameters than HNP.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Monomial-NFN outperforms HNP on CIFAR-10 INR classification by 2.62% (34.23% vs 31.61%) and on FashionMNIST by 3.72% (61.15% vs 57.43%), while trailing NP by 1.39% on MNIST (68.43% vs 69.82%). On the Small CNN Zoo Tanh prediction task, Monomial-NFN leads HNP by 0.006 Kendall's τ on original data, and the gap widens to 0.008 on augmented data, demonstrating that the monomial matrix equivariance provides a genuine advantage under scaling/sign-flipping perturbations where HNP and STATNN degrade. On weight-space editing, Monomial-NFN ties NP on CIFAR-10 contrast (0.020 vs 0.020 MSE) and is within 0.001 on MNIST dilation (0.069 vs 0.068), confirming competitive editing quality. A key practical advantage is parameter efficiency: Monomial-NFN's equivariant linear layer scales linearly in L, n₀, n_L versus quadratically in HNP/NP, using up to 50% fewer parameters while matching or exceeding their accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends permutation-equivariant neural functional networks (NFNs), particularly the HNP/NP architectures of Zhou et al. [71], by enlarging the symmetry group from permutation matrices to monomial matrices. The key conceptual departure is recognizing that ReLU networks admit positive scaling symmetries and sin/tanh networks admit sign-flipping symmetries beyond mere neuron permutations, and encoding these via the semidirect product structure Δ_n ⋊ P_n. The theoretical grounding draws on classical results about functional equivalence of neural networks (Hecht-Nielsen, Albertini & Sontag, Kurkova & Kainen) and recent characterizations of activation-preserving matrix groups (Godfrey et al. [24], Wood & Shawe-Taylor [68]), but the paper's novel contribution is proving maximality of the monomial matrix group for specific network architectures and translating this into concrete equivariant/invariant layer constructions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The quadratic-to-linear parameter reduction (from O(cc'(L+n₀+n_L)²) to O(cc'(L+n₀+n_L))) makes NFNs practically feasible for large-scale networks, which was a significant bottleneck for prior permutation-equivariant designs. However, the paper acknowledges a fundamental expressivity trade-off: the larger symmetry group forces many layer coefficients to zero, meaning a weight between two neurons is updated only from its own previous value, ignoring cross-edge interactions. The maximality of the symmetry group for ReLU networks remains an open question, and the inability to use Fourier features or positional embeddings (which break scaling invariance) limits the model's representational toolkit. Future work on equivariant nonlinear layers could recover some of the lost expressivity while maintaining the parameter efficiency.
