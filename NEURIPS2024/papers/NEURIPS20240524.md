## Problem

In large-scale dataset distillation (e.g., ImageNet-1K), the auxiliary soft labels required for post-training exceed the condensed image storage by over 30×, making the framework impractical. This paper asks whether such large-scale soft labels are truly necessary and identifies the root cause: high within-class similarity in synthetic images, which stems from batching samples across different classes during Batch Normalization matching, forcing reliance on extensive data augmentation to provide diverse supervision.

## Input / Output

The method takes a full dataset (e.g., ImageNet-1K at 224×224, Tiny-ImageNet at 64×64, ImageNet-21K-P at 224×224) and a pretrained teacher model (typically ResNet-18) as input. It produces a condensed set of synthetic images (IPC images per class) plus a pruned pool of soft labels, where the label storage is reduced by up to 40× (e.g., from 113 GB to 2.8 GB for ImageNet-1K IPC200). A key structural detail is that class-wise BN running statistics (mean and variance per class per layer) are tracked during the squeeze phase, adding only ~37 MB for ImageNet-1K, and the pruned label pool is organized at batch granularity rather than epoch granularity to preserve augmentation diversity.

## Method

LPLD (Label Pruning for Large-scale Distillation) modifies the SRe2L three-phase framework in two key ways. During the recover phase, images are re-batched within the same class (rather than across classes) and a class-wise BN matching loss is introduced: the cross-entropy loss uses global BN statistics for effective supervision, while the BN regularization loss matches per-class running statistics, encouraging collaborative optimization among same-class images and increasing within-class diversity. During the relabel phase, simple random pruning (outperforming complex metrics like confidence or top-2 difference) is applied at the batch level across epochs to form an improved label pool, breaking fixed augmentation combinations and enabling diverse label reuse. A theoretical bound (combining Chernoff concentration and BN exponential-moving-average convergence) shows that a single epoch of frozen-model training suffices to stabilize class-wise BN statistics for ImageNet-1K, validating the practical feasibility of the approach.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K with ResNet-18, LPLD achieves 55.4% top-1 accuracy at IPC50 with full labels (1×), outperforming SRe2L (46.8%) and CDA (53.5%). The headline result is a 40× soft-label compression (113 GB → 2.8 GB at IPC200) while still gaining 2.6% accuracy over SRe2L's unpruned baseline (59.6% vs 57.0%). On Tiny-ImageNet, the method achieves 48.8% at IPC50 (1×) and retains 38.4% even at 40× pruning, compared to SRe2L's 29.8% and CDA's 30.9% at the same ratio. On ImageNet-21K-P (10,450 classes), IPC20 with 40× pruning yields 29.4% accuracy using only 32 GB of labels versus 1,285 GB for the unpruned baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SRe2L, LPLD consistently wins at every pruning ratio and IPC: at 1× the gap ranges from +1.4% (IPC200) to +14.5% (IPC10), and at 40× the advantage widens to +8.8% (IPC10) to +7.7% (IPC200). Against CDA, LPLD matches or slightly exceeds at 1× (e.g., 55.4% vs 53.5% at IPC50) but pulls decisively ahead under aggressive pruning, achieving 46.7% vs 38.0% at IPC50/40× (a 8.7% gap). Against RDED (optimization-free recovery), LPLD outperforms at all ratios, e.g., 48.7% vs 44.3% at IPC50/40×. Against G-VBSM, which uses a 4-model ensemble for both recovery and relabeling, LPLD with a single model achieves 35.7% vs 31.4% at IPC10/1× and 20.2% vs 18.8% at IPC10/40×. Against FKD's label quantization, LPLD is orthogonal (compressing augmentation count vs. logit dimension) and substantially outperforms: at ~10× compression LPLD reaches 32.7% while FKD reaches only 18.1% on IPC10.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the SRe2L/CDA three-phase framework (squeeze, recover, relabel) for large-scale dataset distillation by identifying an overlooked bottleneck: the relabel phase's soft label storage exceeds image storage by 30×. The key conceptual departure is re-batching images within classes (rather than across classes) during the recover phase and introducing class-wise BN supervision, which increases within-class diversity and enables simple random label pruning in the relabel phase. The paper positions itself against G-VBSM (which also re-batches within classes but for a different motivation and uses ensembles) and FKD (which compresses logits via quantization rather than pruning augmentations), establishing label pruning as an orthogonal and complementary strategy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 40× soft label compression (113 GB → 2.8 GB on ImageNet-1K IPC200) makes large-scale dataset distillation far more practical for downstream applications like Neural Architecture Search, where pre-generated labels are repeatedly reused. The finding that random pruning matches carefully designed metrics (confidence, top-2 difference, etc.) simplifies the pipeline and suggests that image diversity, not label selection, is the critical factor. However, the paper acknowledges that label generation is still required upfront and that training epochs remain unchanged, leaving open the question of whether storage and compute can be reduced simultaneously. The class-wise BN statistics approach is a lightweight, adoptable modification that could benefit other BN-matching-based distillation methods.
