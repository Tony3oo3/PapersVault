## Problem

Existing membership inference methods for diffusion models either require prohibitive computational overhead (e.g., 16 shadow models for LiRA) or measure overfitting only to the marginal image distribution, which fails under realistic training conditions for text-to-image models due to their strong generalization and the stochasticity of diffusion training loss. This paper identifies a previously unexploited 'conditional overfitting' phenomenon: text-to-image diffusion models overfit more to the conditional distribution of images given their text prompts than to the marginal image distribution, providing a stronger signal for distinguishing member from non-member samples.

## Input / Output

The method operates in a grey-box setting, requiring access to the model's noise prediction function ε_θ at intermediate timesteps (typically 440–460) rather than full model parameters. For each image-text pair, it performs Monte Carlo sampling with both the original text condition and a set of reduced conditions (obtained via importance-based word clipping at 30%, 50%, 70% plus null), yielding 15 queries per data point. The output is a binary membership prediction via two variants: a threshold-based score (CLiD_th) that linearly combines the likelihood discrepancy and conditional likelihood, and a feature-vector-based score (CLiD_vec) classified by XGBoost.

## Method

The paper derives the Conditional Likelihood Discrepancy (CLiD) indicator I(x,c) = log p(x|c) − log p(x) by showing that the conditional overfitting assumption, when expressed with KL divergence, implies member samples have strictly higher expected I values than non-members (Theorem 3.2). The log-likelihoods are approximated via ELBOs, and the key computational trick is estimating the ELBO difference in a single Monte Carlo pass rather than independently, halving the query count. To approximate the marginal likelihood log p(x), the method uses a set of reduced text conditions (importance-based word clipping) rather than a single null input, reducing stochasticity in the estimate. Two practical attack variants are constructed: CLiD_th normalizes and linearly combines the discrepancy and conditional likelihood indicators with a learned threshold, while CLiD_vec treats the per-condition discrepancies and conditional likelihood as a feature vector classified by XGBoost.

## Results

Under the real-world fine-tuning setting (with default data augmentation and calibrated training steps), CLiD_th achieves ASR of 88.88%, AUC of 96.13%, and TPR@1%FPR of 67.52% on MS-COCO, while the best baseline (SecMI) reaches only 60.94% ASR, 65.40% AUC, and 3.92% TPR@1%FPR. Across all three datasets (MS-COCO, Flickr, Pokémon), CLiD methods maintain ASR above 86% and AUC above 93%, exceeding the best baseline AUC by approximately 30 percentage points. Under the over-training setting, CLiD_th achieves near-perfect performance (99.08% ASR, 99.94% AUC on MS-COCO), though all methods perform well in this unrealistic regime. In the pretraining setting on Stable Diffusion v1-5 with LAION data, CLiD_th still leads with 64.53% ASR and 67.82% AUC versus 59.08% and 61.11% for the best baseline (PFAMI).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CLiD_th and CLiD_vec decisively outperform all four baselines (Loss, PIA, SecMI, PFAMI) under the real-world training setting, with AUC exceeding the best baseline by ~30 percentage points (96.13% vs. 65.40% on MS-COCO) and TPR@1%FPR exceeding it by over 50 percentage points (67.52% vs. 3.92%). Under the over-training setting, all methods perform well (even the simplest Loss-based method reaches 81.92% ASR), making differentiation difficult and revealing that prior works' impressive numbers rely on unrealistic training configurations. PFAMI [17] is the most sensitive to data augmentation, dropping from 94.48% ASR (over-training) to 57.36% ASR (real-world) on MS-COCO, while CLiD_th drops only from 99.08% to 88.88%. In the pretraining setting on Stable Diffusion v1-5, CLiD_th leads all baselines (64.53% ASR, 67.82% AUC) but the margin narrows compared to fine-tuning, which the authors acknowledge as a limitation. CLiD also demonstrates the smallest performance degradation under data augmentation (−7.88% ASR) compared to PFAMI (−21.84%) and SecMI (−11.08%), and remains largely unaffected by adaptive text-perturbation defenses (rephrasing, word deletion) that do not destroy model utility.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends query-based membership inference for diffusion models (SecMI, PIA, PFAMI, loss-based methods) by identifying a phenomenon specific to conditional (text-to-image) models: overfitting to the conditional distribution p(x|c) is more salient than overfitting to the marginal image distribution p(x). Prior methods measure memorization of individual images and thus struggle with the strong generalization of text-to-image models; CLiD instead exploits the discrepancy between conditional and marginal likelihoods via KL divergence, yielding an analytically derived indicator that reduces stochasticity in ELBO estimation. The approach departs from the LiRA-style shadow-model paradigm (Carlini et al.) by requiring only a single target model and a small number of Monte Carlo queries.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical finding that all prior query-based methods drop to near-random performance (ASR ~60%, TPR@1%FPR ~5%) under realistic fine-tuning steps and default data augmentation, while CLiD retains 86%+ ASR, reframes the evaluation landscape for membership inference on text-to-image models and provides a viable auditing tool for copyright owners. The method's resistance to adaptive text perturbations (rephrasing, word deletion) suggests it is not easily neutralized by trainers who become aware of the attack. Open questions include whether the conditional-overfitting signal persists in the pretraining regime (where gains are only ~5% over baselines), whether it generalizes to other conditional generative architectures (e.g., autoregressive image models), and how it behaves under stronger defenses such as text-condition augmentation.
