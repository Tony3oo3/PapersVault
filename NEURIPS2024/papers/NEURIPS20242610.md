## Problem

While 2D point tracking (TAP-2D) has mature real-world benchmarks like TAPVid-DAVIS, no equivalent benchmark exists for 3D point tracking on real-world video. Existing 3D evaluations either rely on synthetic environments with significant domain gaps or are limited to narrow domains (e.g., driving-only with 180 clips), leaving the community without a comprehensive measure of whether models can recover metric 3D trajectories and occlusion states from monocular footage.

## Input / Output

Given a monocular video clip (25–300 frames at 10 or 30 fps, resolutions ranging from 512×512 to 1920×1280) and a query point (x_q, y_q, t_q) at a single frame, the model must predict the metric 3D position (x, y, z) of that material point at every subsequent frame along with a binary visibility flag. Correctness is assessed with a depth-relative threshold δ_3D = Z·δ_2D/f, so closer points demand tighter absolute accuracy. The benchmark provides 4,569 clips with 50–1,024 trajectories each, split into a 150-clip minival and a full test set.

## Method

The benchmark unifies three real-world data sources—Aria Digital Twins (egocentric studio footage with digital-twin annotations), DriveTrack (Waymo LIDAR + camera), and Panoptic Studio (multi-view dome with dynamic 3D Gaussian Splatting reconstructions)—each requiring a distinct pipeline to extract metric 3D trajectories and visibility flags from its native sensor modalities. For evaluation, the paper extends TAP-2D's Jaccard metric to 3D by defining APD_3D with depth-relative thresholds, occlusion accuracy (OA), and a combined 3D-AJ score that penalizes both positional error and visibility misclassification. A key practical challenge is scale ambiguity in monocular depth: predictions are globally rescaled by the median depth ratio before scoring, with an optional per-trajectory rescaling variant for methods that estimate relative depth more reliably than absolute scale. Baselines combine state-of-the-art 2D trackers (TAPIR, CoTracker, BootsTAPIR) with depth estimators (ZoeDepth, Depth Anything V2) or COLMAP SfM, revealing that 3D-AJ scores (5–11) are dramatically lower than the corresponding 2D-AJ scores (53–59), highlighting the difficulty of the task.

## Results

On the TAPVid-3D benchmark, the best 2D-tracker + depth-estimator combination (CoTracker+COLMAP) achieves an average 3D-AJ of 9.3, with BootsTAPIR+COLMAP matching at 9.3 and SpatialTracker (a dedicated TAP-3D method) reaching 9.0. The headline metric 3D-AJ is dramatically lower than the corresponding 2D-AJ (53–59%), revealing a large gap between 2D tracking accuracy and 3D geometric understanding. ZoeDepth consistently outperforms Depth Anything V2 as the depth component (e.g., BootsTAPIR+ZoeDepth: 8.8 vs. BootsTAPIR+DepthAnythingV2: 5.5 average 3D-AJ), while COLMAP is competitive with or slightly ahead of ZoeDepth for CoTracker and BootsTAPIR.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among the three 2D trackers, BootsTAPIR and CoTracker are the strongest 2D performers (59.1% and 57.2% 2D-AJ respectively, vs. TAPIR's 53.2%), and this advantage carries into 3D: BootsTAPIR+COLMAP and CoTracker+COLMAP tie for the best average 3D-AJ at 9.3. As depth components, ZoeDepth substantially outperforms Depth Anything V2 (e.g., BootsTAPIR: 8.8 vs. 5.5 3D-AJ), while COLMAP is competitive with or slightly ahead of ZoeDepth for CoTracker (9.3 vs. 8.7) and BootsTAPIR (9.3 vs. 8.8). The dedicated TAP-3D method SpatialTracker achieves 9.0 average 3D-AJ, trailing the best 2D+depth combos by ~0.3 but leading on APD (15.5 vs. 15.0). A 3D TAPIR trained only on synthetic Kubric data (TAPIR-3D) performs worst at 3.1 average 3D-AJ, indicating a significant domain gap. Overall, all methods show a stark 2D-to-3D performance drop (e.g., BootsTAPIR: 59.1% 2D-AJ → 8.8–9.3 3D-AJ), highlighting that current models understand 2D motion well but lack accurate 3D geometric reasoning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Tracking Any Point (TAP) task from 2D to 3D, building directly on the TAP-Vid benchmark and its Jaccard-based metrics (Doersch et al., NeurIPS 2022), which it generalizes to handle depth ambiguity, occlusion, and multi-track smoothness in 3D space. For ground-truth generation, it repurposes the DriveTrack LIDAR interpolation pipeline (Balasingam et al., 2023) for the driving split and the Dynamic 3D Gaussians reconstruction (Luiten et al., 3DV 2024) for the Panoptic Studio split, while the Aria Digital Twins split relies on synthetic digital-twin annotations. Conceptually, TAP-3D occupies a middle ground between instantaneous scene flow (which lacks long-range temporal association) and category-specific 3D pose tracking (which requires known object models), positioning itself as a more general, model-free formulation of 3D motion understanding.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first real-world benchmark for 3D point tracking, filling a clear gap: prior TAP evaluations were strictly 2D, and synthetic 3D benchmarks (Kubric, Point Odyssey) suffer from domain gap. The baselines reveal a striking ~6× performance drop from 2D-AJ (~55) to 3D-AJ (~9), with failure modes dominated by depth scale drift and spatially inconsistent depth rather than 2D tracking errors, suggesting that current monocular depth estimators are the primary bottleneck. Open questions include whether jointly learned 2D+3D trackers (like TAPIR-3D or SpatialTracker) can close this gap, whether the benchmark's three domains (egocentric, driving, third-person human action) adequately cover the diversity needed for general 3D motion understanding, and how the metrics should evolve as models improve beyond the current low baseline scores.
