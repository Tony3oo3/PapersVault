## Problem

While fine-grained visual recognition has been accelerated by large-scale community datasets like iNat2021, the audio domain lacks an equivalent: existing large-scale audio datasets (e.g., AudioSet) use coarse event categories rather than species-level labels, and animal-specific bioacoustic datasets are small-scale and geographically limited. This gap hinders progress in automated species identification from soundscapes, which is critical for biodiversity monitoring and conservation.

## Input / Output

The method takes variable-length single-channel audio recordings (resampled to 22.05 kHz) and converts them to 2D mel-spectrograms (128 mel bins over 50 Hz–11.025 kHz, 512 time steps per 3-second window), which are resized to 224×224×3 RGB images for model input. Long recordings are processed via 3-second windows strided by 1.5 seconds, with file-level predictions obtained by averaging window-level scores. Output is a vector of species presence scores across 1,212 evaluation species (5,569 in training), supporting both single-label (Top-1/Top-5) and multi-label (mAP/mF1) evaluation.

## Method

The paper introduces iNatSounds, a 230K-audio-file dataset spanning 5,500+ species across five taxonomic classes, constructed from iNaturalist research-grade observations with year-based train/val/test splits and spatio-temporal clustering for subsampling. Models treat spectrograms as images and are trained with three backbones (MobileNet-V3, ResNet-50, ViT-B-16) under two objectives: a standard multiclass cross-entropy loss, and a multilabel approach combining an assume-absent sigmoid loss with a mean-teacher semi-supervised consistency term (EMA decay α=0.999, λ=1000). Data augmentation includes frequency/time masking and Mixup with beta-distributed weights. A key finding is that the multilabel objective, despite lower iNatSounds accuracy, transfers significantly better to strongly-labeled downstream datasets (Powdermill, SWAMP, AnuraSet), and that geographic priors from SINR range models provide substantial accuracy gains, especially for acoustically similar species.

## Results

On iNatSounds (230K recordings, 5,569 train species, 1,212 eval species), the best multiclass model (ResNet-50 + geo-prior) achieves 60.7% Top-1 accuracy on the test set, while the best multilabel model (ViT-B-16 + Mean Teacher) reaches 65.4 mAP. On strongly labeled downstream datasets, the ViT multilabel model achieves 71.7% Top-1 on SSW60, 40.2 mAP on Powdermill, 39.0 mAP on SWAMP, and 33.8 mAP on AnuraSet, all without any fine-tuning. Multilabel objectives consistently transfer better to downstream tasks than multiclass objectives, despite the latter maximizing iNatSounds Top-1 accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two external systems: BirdNET (v2.4, open-source, ~6,000 species) and Merlin Sound ID (proprietary, ~1,400 species, strongly labeled). On SSW60, the paper's ViT multilabel model (71.7% Top-1) outperforms Merlin Sound ID (62.9% Top-1) by ~9 points, while BirdNET reports 94.6% Top-5 but with a caveat (†) that some test files were likely used in its training. On Powdermill, the paper's best mAP (40.2, ViT ML) trails both Merlin Sound ID (50.4) and BirdNET (60.7†) by 10–20 points. On SWAMP, the paper's best mAP (39.0) also lags Merlin Sound ID (54.2) and BirdNET (41.7†). The authors note that BirdNET's training data is undocumented and overlaps with some evaluation sets, so those comparisons should be interpreted cautiously. The key takeaway is that iNatSounds models trained with weak supervision on 5,500+ species approach but do not yet match strongly supervised systems on the smaller, well-annotated downstream benchmarks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper primarily contributes a new large-scale dataset (iNatSounds) that bridges the gap between coarse-grained audio datasets like AudioSet and small-scale bioacoustic benchmarks like BEANS or SSW60, drawing its construction philosophy from the iNat2021 image dataset built on the same iNaturalist platform. Methodologically, it adopts the 'assume absent' strategy for multilabel learning from single-positive labels (Cole et al., CVPR 2021) and the Mean Teacher student-teacher framework (Tarvainen & Valpola, NeurIPS 2017) to handle the weakly labeled nature of the data. The vision-based spectrogram-to-image pipeline follows established practice in audio recognition, and the geographic prior via SINR extends prior work on species range estimation to the audio domain.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

iNatSounds fills a critical gap as the first freely accessible, large-scale (230K files, 5,500+ species), fine-grained animal sound dataset spanning multiple taxa, enabling systematic research on bioacoustic classification at a scale previously unavailable. Key open questions include how to close the performance gap between weakly supervised iNatSounds models and strongly supervised systems like Merlin Sound ID, how to address the long-tail species distribution (5–10 samples for many species), and how to mitigate the geographic bias toward North America and Europe. The finding that iNatSounds accuracy does not reliably predict downstream performance for larger architectures (ResNet-50, ViT) is an important caution for the community, and the paper explicitly recommends dual evaluation on both weakly and strongly labeled benchmarks.
