## Problem

Variational inference with the standard ELBO objective is nonconvex in the variational parameters, so only convergence to a local optimum can be guaranteed, and the number and quality of such optima are generally unknown. This paper establishes the first global convergence guarantee for variational inference by analyzing the expected forward KL divergence objective in the neural tangent kernel regime, showing that gradient descent on a neural network parameterization recovers a unique global solution in the infinite-width limit.

## Input / Output

The method takes data observations X (numerical vectors or 28×28 grayscale MNIST images) drawn from a generative model and produces an amortized variational posterior Q(Θ; f(X)) where f is a neural network mapping each observation to the natural parameters of an exponential family distribution (Gaussian or von Mises). Gradients are computed via ancestral sampling from the joint P(Θ, X), making the procedure likelihood-free: the density of P(X|Θ) need never be evaluated. The output is a function (not a single distribution) that can be evaluated at any new observation to obtain its posterior parameters.

## Method

The key insight is to lift the nonconvex parametric objective L_P (in network weights φ) to a functional objective L_F (in function space) and show that L_F is strictly convex when the variational family Q is an exponential family, guaranteeing a unique global minimizer f*. In the infinite-width limit, the neural tangent kernel K_φ converges to a fixed positive-definite kernel K_∞, and the gradient flow of L_P in function space reduces to kernel gradient flow in the RKHS associated with K_∞, which provably converges to f*. The proof bridges the parametric and functional objectives by showing that for sufficiently large network width p, the empirical NTK trajectory stays uniformly close to the limiting kernel trajectory over any finite time interval, so L_P optimization reaches an ε-neighborhood of the global optimum. The method is trained with SGD using unbiased gradient estimates obtained by ancestral sampling from the joint model, and while the theory assumes a two-layer ReLU network, experiments demonstrate global convergence for CNNs, permutation-invariant architectures, and other activation functions.

## Results

On the amortized clustering problem (d=5, n=1000), minimizing L_P achieves an average ℓ₁ distance of 1.8 (mean parameterization) and 2.9 (natural parameterization) between the variational mode and the true cluster centers, compared to 77.0 and 86.0 for ELBO-based optimization. The proportion of trials with correct cluster labeling is 1.00 for L_P versus 0.03 (mean) and 0.02 (natural) for ELBO. On rotated MNIST (N=1000 digits, true angle 260°), L_P minimization converges to the correct angle from all initializations, while IWBO-based likelihood optimization frequently converges to shallow local optima caused by approximate rotational symmetries of digits 0, 1, 6, 8, and 9.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares L_P (expected forward KL) minimization against two likelihood-based baselines. Against ELBO-based variational inference (Ranganath et al., 2014), L_P dramatically outperforms on the amortized clustering problem: the average ℓ₁ error drops from 77.0 to 1.8 (mean parameterization) and from 86.0 to 2.9 (natural parameterization), while the correct-labeling rate jumps from 3% to 100%. ELBO optimization consistently converges to a permuted (label-switched) local optimum, whereas L_P recovers the globally correct ordering. Against the importance-weighted bound (IWBO, Burda et al., 2016) on rotated MNIST, L_P converges to the true angle of 260° from all tested initializations, while IWBO frequently gets trapped in shallow local optima arising from the approximate 180° rotational symmetry of several digit classes. In the direct head-to-head comparison (Section 5.4) with identical architecture and hyperparameters, L_P outperforms ELBO on every metric: forward KL, reverse KL, negative log-likelihood, and point-estimate accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of neural tangent kernel (NTK) theory (Jacot et al., 2018) and variational inference, applying the lazy-training/infinite-width framework to a problem it had not previously been applied to: amortized posterior approximation via the expected forward KL objective (also known as NPE or the sleep-phase of reweighted wake-sleep). Prior convergence results for VI target the ELBO and can only guarantee local optima due to nonconvexity; here, the authors instead analyze a different objective whose functional form is strictly convex (for exponential-family variational distributions), then bridge the gap to the nonconvex parametric objective via NTK gradient flow in a vector-valued RKHS. The vector-valued RKHS machinery (Carmeli et al., 2006) is essential because the variational parameters are multivariate, distinguishing this from standard scalar-output NTK analyses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first global convergence guarantee for any variational inference method, which addresses a longstanding practical concern: practitioners have no way to know how suboptimal their ELBO-based posterior approximations are. The surprising empirical finding that a likelihood-free method (expected forward KL) outperforms likelihood-based ELBO optimization on problems with multimodal posteriors (label switching, rotational symmetry) challenges the default assumption that one should always exploit an available likelihood. Key limitations include the infinite-width asymptotic regime, the 2-layer ReLU architecture assumption in the proof, and the requirement of a positive-definite limiting NTK; the authors conjecture these can be relaxed but do not prove it. Open questions include finite-width convergence rates, extension to deeper/more complex architectures, and whether the global-convergence advantage persists on large-scale real-world inference problems beyond the synthetic and semi-synthetic settings tested here.
