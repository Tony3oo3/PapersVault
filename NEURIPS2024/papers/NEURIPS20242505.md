## Problem

Pseudo-labeling in cross-domain semantic segmentation suffers from two persistent failure modes: under severe domain shifts, selected 'reliable' pixels remain speckled and noisy, and in open-set or wild data, pixels from unknown classes can exhibit high confidence while also appearing speckled. Pixel-level selection mechanisms cannot effectively identify or correct these speckled close- and open-set noises, causing error accumulation that degrades subsequent self-training rounds.

## Input / Output

The method consumes pixel-level pseudo-labels (top-50% confidence pixels) from an existing DA/DG model along with target-domain RGB images, and the pre-trained SAM (ViT-H) model for interactive segmentation. It produces connectivity-level pseudo-labels — structured, boundary-accurate masks grouped by semantic category — that replace the original speckled pixel labels for self-training. The entire SeCo pipeline runs offline; the corrected pseudo-label set D_all is then used to retrain the segmentation model, and SAM is not invoked at test time to avoid inference overhead.

## Method

SeCo operates in two stages. Pixel Semantic Aggregation (PSA) interacts with SAM in a category-aware manner: for 'stuff' classes (large, noise-prone regions) it uses semantic alignment to assign the dominant pseudo-label to each SAM-generated connectivity, while for 'things' classes (small, densely adjacent objects) it uses enlarged bounding-box and center-point prompts to guide SAM's segmentation precision. Semantic Connectivity Correction (SCC) then treats each connectivity as a classification object, trains a lightweight classifier (feature extractor + MLP) for only 5000 iterations in an early-learning regime, and exploits the bimodal loss distribution that emerges: a two-component Gaussian Mixture Model fitted via EM separates clean from noisy connectivities, with a noise threshold τ_ns=0.60 for filtering and a correction threshold τ_cr=0.95 for reassigning mislabeled connectivities to their predicted class. The union of retained and corrected connectivities forms the final high-quality pseudo-label set for self-training.

## Results

On GTA5→Cityscapes UDA, SeCo improves AdvEnt by +13.4% mIoU (45.5→58.9), ProDA by +9.4% (53.7→64.1), DAFormer by +5.3% (68.2→73.4), and HRDA by +2.3% (73.8→76.1). In the more challenging source-free setting, SeCo elevates HCL by +10.6% (48.1→58.8) and DTST by +8.4% (52.1→60.5), while in black-box adaptation it boosts DINE by +7.7% (46.7→54.4) and BiMem by +8.5% (48.2→56.7). For domain generalization, SeCo achieves 52.1% average mIoU (ResNet-101) and 59.1% (MiT-B5) across Cityscapes, BDD-100K, and Mapillary, outperforming CLOUDS by 1.4% and 1.0% respectively. On GTA5→BDD-100k open compound adaptation, SeCo improves PyCDA by +11.4% (35.1→46.5) and ProDA by +7.8% (41.7→49.5).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SeCo is designed as a plug-and-play module that enhances existing pseudo-labeling methods rather than competing with them. In UDA, it consistently outperforms all baselines: AdvEnt (45.5→58.9), ProDA (53.7→64.1), DAFormer (68.2→73.4), and HRDA (73.8→76.1), with the largest gains on weaker baselines. In source-free UDA, SeCo surpasses both HCL (48.1→58.8, +10.6%) and the prior SOTA DTST (52.1→60.5, +8.4%), establishing new state-of-the-art results. In black-box UDA, it improves DINE (46.7→54.4) and BiMem (48.2→56.7) by 7.7% and 8.5% respectively. For domain generalization, SeCo outperforms CLOUDS [4] (which also uses SAM) by 1.4% (ResNet-101) and 1.0% (MiT-B5) average mIoU, and surpasses SHADE [96], TLDR [35], and MoDify [32] by 6.8%, 5.0%, and 5.3% respectively. On open compound adaptation (GTA5→BDD-100k), SeCo beats PyCDA [46] by +11.4%, ProDA [90] by +7.8%, and SFOCDA [95] by +6.2%, establishing new SOTA. In the ablation comparing denoising strategies (Table 8), SeCo's connectivity-level SCC outperforms pixel-level DivideMix [41] by 4.0–5.1% mIoU across three DA tasks, confirming the advantage of connectivity-level over pixel-level denoising.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the pseudo-labeling paradigm in cross-domain semantic segmentation by shifting the denoising granularity from pixel-level to connectivity-level. It builds on SAM's interactive segmentation capability for aggregating speckled pseudo-labels into semantically coherent regions, and on early-learning regularization and Gaussian Mixture Model-based loss distribution analysis from noisy label learning literature for identifying and correcting connectivity noise. The key conceptual departure from prior work (e.g., CLOUDS, which also uses SAM for pseudo-label refinement) is the 'stuff vs. things' interaction strategy with SAM and the explicit connectivity classification task, which the authors show is fundamentally easier than pixel-level noise localization. The method is designed as a plug-in enhancement to existing UDA, SF-UDA, BB-UDA, and DG methods rather than a standalone framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The connectivity-level formulation offers a principled way to address a persistent weakness in pseudo-labeling: speckled closed-set and open-set noise that pixel-level confidence thresholds cannot reliably filter, especially under severe domain shifts or with synthetic/wild data. The paper's extensive ablations showing that naive SAM integration (as backbone, CLIP+SAM initial labeling, or SAM on coarse pseudo-labels) actually degrades performance provide important practical guidance for the community attempting to leverage foundation models in cross-domain segmentation. Open questions include whether the approach scales to truly open-world settings with unknown class distributions, whether the lack of explicit category-balanced connectivity filtering limits performance on rare classes, and whether the reliance on SAM's ViT-H backbone introduces prohibitive computational overhead for deployment.
