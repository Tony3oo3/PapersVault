## Problem

In distributed MoE training with expert parallelism, all-to-all communication for token routing constitutes an average of 45% (up to 67%) of total training time, and this ratio remains nearly constant as both model scale and cluster size grow. Existing approaches either modify the gating mechanism or model architecture to reduce communication, limiting their universal applicability, and none address the problem by compressing the forward activations themselves before transmission.

## Input / Output

The method operates on intermediate token activations (hidden-size vectors, e.g., 768-d or 1024-d) that would normally be dispatched via all-to-all communication after the gating network assigns each token to its target expert. Instead of transmitting every token, LSH-MoE sends only the cluster centroids (a compressed subset) across the network, then reconstructs per-token expert outputs locally by adding back the stored residuals. The compression rate is controlled by the number of LSH hash functions (default 6, yielding ~20% compression), and the method is framework-agnostic, plugging into existing MoE training stacks like DeepSpeed-MoE or Tutel.

## Method

LSH-MoE exploits the empirically observed token similarity in all-to-all communication data (revealed via PCA) to compress activations before transmission. It applies cross-polytope hashing (mapping each token to the nearest vertex of a randomly rotated cross-polytope) to rapidly cluster similar tokens into buckets, then transmits only the per-bucket centroids through all-to-all communication. After the expert network processes the centroids, a residual-based error compensation scheme reconstructs each token's output by adding back the pre-stored difference between the token and its cluster centroid. A theoretical analysis shows the all-to-all-to-computation time ratio scales as (FLOPs/6B_inter)·(k/(1+2k))·((w-1)/(wh)), confirming the communication bottleneck persists at scale and motivating the compression approach. The method achieves 1.28×–2.2× end-to-end speedup across RoBERTa-MoE, GPT-MoE, T5-MoE, and Swin-MoE while maintaining comparable accuracy.

## Results

On the GLUE benchmark, LSH-MoE achieves 1.2×–1.5× end-to-end training speedup for GPT-MoE (15B and 52B) while maintaining nearly identical accuracy (within ±0.2% on most tasks, e.g., SST-2: 93.8% vs 93.8% for 15B; MNLI: 84.1% vs 84.3% for 52B). On ImageNet-1K, fine-tuning Swin-MoE with LSH-MoE yields a 1.28× speedup (236.6 vs 184.3 samples/s) with an 11.7% communication compression rate and only a 0.2% drop in Top-1 accuracy (84.5% vs 84.7%). For pre-training convergence, LSH-MoE accelerates RoBERTa-MoE by 1.6× and T5-MoE by 2.2× to reach equivalent perplexity, with the residual-based error compensation preventing a 0.3-point perplexity degradation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against external MoE training systems (e.g., TA-MoE, Pre-gated MoE, DeepSpeed-MoE, SCoMoE); instead, the sole baseline is the standard expert-parallelism MoE training pipeline (labeled 'Origin') without any communication compression. LSH-MoE consistently matches or marginally exceeds the Origin accuracy across all GLUE tasks (differences ≤0.3%) while delivering 1.2×–1.5× speedup, confirming that the LSH-based centroid compression with residual error compensation does not degrade model quality. On ImageNet-1K, Swin-MoE with LSH-MoE achieves 84.5% Top-1 vs 84.7% for Origin (−0.2%) at 1.28× throughput, with an 11.7% reduction in all-to-all communication volume. The ablation further shows that removing the residual compensation step increases perplexity by 0.3 points, validating that the compensation is essential for maintaining parity with the uncompressed baseline. Overall, LSH-MoE is positioned as a drop-in, framework-agnostic accelerator that is orthogonal to and composable with existing MoE training optimizations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the expert parallelism strategy introduced by GShard [17], which distributes MoE experts across GPUs and triggers all-to-all token routing. Prior work on reducing MoE communication overhead has focused on topology-aware routing (TA-MoE), pre-gating mechanisms (Pre-gated MoE), reducing expert count (PR-MoE in DeepSpeed-MoE), or structuring data transfers (SCoMoE), but none compress the forward activations themselves. LSH-MoE departs from this lineage by repurposing locality-sensitive hashing—originally designed for approximate nearest-neighbor search and previously applied to attention sparsity in Reformer [16]—as an online data compression technique for all-to-all payloads, augmented with a residual-based error compensation scheme to preserve model fidelity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that all-to-all communication consumes ~45% of training time (up to 67% for Swin-MoE) and remains nearly constant as both model scale and cluster size grow makes this bottleneck increasingly critical for trillion-parameter MoE deployments. The method's framework-independence means it can be layered onto existing systems (DeepSpeed-MoE, Tutel, Hetu-MoE) without architectural changes, offering a practical path to reducing communication costs. Open questions include whether the observed token similarity generalizes to non-MoE architectures (the authors note data redundancy exists there too), whether higher compression rates become viable with better hash families, and how the approach interacts with emerging communication patterns in heterogeneous or disaggregated clusters.
