## Problem

Psychometric models face a fundamental tension: nomothetic approaches assume identical latent structure across all individuals, while fully idiographic approaches sacrifice generalizability and interpretability. Existing intermediate methods either model observed responses directly rather than latent traits, or lack temporal dynamics, leaving a gap for individualized, dynamic assessment of psychological taxonomies from repeated ordinal survey data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241731/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a three-way tensor of ordinal (Likert-type, C=5 level) responses y_{ijt} indexed by unit i, item j, and time period t, where items are pre-grouped into batteries corresponding to latent trait dimensions. It produces a shared population loading matrix W_pop (D×J), unit-specific low-rank idiographic loading matrices Z^(i) (D*×J with D*=1), unit-specific RBF length scales ℓ_i, and threshold parameters for the ordered probit likelihood. Downstream, the estimated individual correlation matrices enable k-means clustering into distinct personality taxonomy profiles, and the full model supports out-of-sample response prediction and leave-one-trait-out cross-validation.

## Method

IPGP models each unit's latent trait trajectories as a multi-task Gaussian process with a Kronecker-structured covariance K_task^(i) ⊗ K_time^(i), where the task covariance decomposes into a shared population component W_pop^T W_pop and a unit-specific low-rank idiographic deviation Z^(i)^T Z^(i) (linear model of coregionalization). The time kernel is an RBF with unit-specific length scale to capture individual temporal dynamics, and latent factors are projected to ordinal responses via an ordered probit likelihood. Rather than jointly estimating latent factors and loadings (which breaks rotational invariance), IPGP marginalizes out the latent variables and optimizes the loading matrices directly. Scalable inference is achieved through stochastic variational inference with inducing points, where the ELBO is maximized via ADAM over variational parameters, loadings, and likelihood thresholds. The population loading matrix W_pop is initialized from a cross-sectional nomothetic fit (informative prior), which the paper shows is crucial for accurate recovery of individual factor structures.

## Results

In the simulation study (N=10, T=30, D=5, J=20), IPGP achieves 94.2% test accuracy and −0.184 test log-likelihood, substantially outperforming the best baseline GPDM (66.7% test accuracy, −4.377 test LL) and all other methods. On correlation matrix distance (CMD, lower is better), IPGP achieves 0.128 versus 0.262 for GPDM and 0.987 for TVAR, indicating far more accurate recovery of the true factor loading structure. On the real-world longitudinal ESM dataset (93 participants, 8,770 assessments), IPGP reaches 41.7% in-sample accuracy and −1.369 LL/N, with a posterior odds ratio of exp(1.06×10⁴) decisively favoring the idiographic model over the nomothetic baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IPGP dominates all six baselines on every reported metric in the simulation study. The strongest baseline, GPDM [17], achieves 66.7% test accuracy and CMD of 0.262, while IPGP reaches 94.2% test accuracy and CMD of 0.128 — a 27.5-point accuracy gain and a 51% reduction in factor-structure error. TVAR [15] performs worst on CMD (0.987), indicating it essentially fails to recover the correct loading geometry despite moderate accuracy (55.4%). The item-response baselines GRM [37], GPCM [47], and SRM [48] all cluster around 26–56% accuracy with CMD values of 0.30–0.66, reflecting their inability to model temporal dynamics. LSM [13] achieves competitive CMD (0.256) but cannot produce test predictions due to missing-value incompatibility. On the longitudinal ESM dataset, IPGP outperforms GPDM and TVAR (both at 38.2% accuracy) by 3.5 points and is favored over the nomothetic ablation IPGP-NOM by a posterior odds ratio of exp(1.06×10⁴), confirming that the idiographic component captures genuine individual-level structure beyond population-level commonality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper synthesizes three established methodological threads—Gaussian process latent variable models (GPLVM), multi-task Gaussian process coregionalization (LMC), and ordinal item response theory—into a unified framework for dynamic idiographic psychological assessment. It departs from GPLVM by optimizing the factor loading matrix while marginalizing latent variables, and from prior GP dynamical models by introducing a non-Gaussian ordered-probit likelihood and a unit-specific low-rank idiographic component on top of a shared population kernel. The positioning is explicitly intermediate between fully nomothetic (shared loadings for all) and fully idiographic (independent taxonomy per person) approaches, building on the GIMME-style bridging work of Beltz et al. but operating in latent space rather than response space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a principled, scalable (stochastic VI) tool for the long-standing idiographic-versus-nomothetic debate in psychometrics, with the potential to inform individualized diagnosis and treatment by revealing how individuals deviate from a common trait structure. Key limitations include the small longitudinal cohort (93 students, non-representative), the restriction to D*=1 for the idiographic rank, and reliance on a fixed population prior from cross-sectional data. Open questions include whether the discovered four-cluster taxonomy generalizes beyond student populations, how the model scales to larger batteries or shorter time windows, and whether contextual covariates (activity, social context) can be incorporated without sacrificing identifiability.
