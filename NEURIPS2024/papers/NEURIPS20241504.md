## Problem

Distribution Matching Distillation (DMD) can distill multi-step diffusion models into one-step generators by matching output distributions, but it requires an additional regression loss on precomputed noise-image pairs to stabilize training. This regression loss is prohibitively expensive for large-scale text-to-image synthesis (e.g., ~700 A100 days for SDXL-scale data) and inherently caps the student's quality at the teacher's sampling-path fidelity, negating the distribution-matching advantage. The paper asks: can DMD be stabilized without the regression loss, and can the student actually surpass the teacher?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241504/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For class-conditional generation (ImageNet-64×64), the input is a Gaussian noise vector and a class label, producing a 64×64 RGB image in a single forward pass. For text-to-image synthesis, the input is a Gaussian noise vector plus a text prompt encoded by the teacher's text encoder, producing 512×512 (SD v1.5) or 1024×1024 (SDXL) images in 1 or 4 forward passes. The multi-step variant alternates between a denoising step and a forward-diffusion (noise-injection) step at fixed timesteps (e.g., 999→749→499→249 for 4 steps), so intermediate inputs are the generator's own partially-denoised outputs rather than externally noised real images.

## Method

DMD2 removes DMD's regression loss and stabilizes the resulting training with a two time-scale update rule: the fake score network (a diffusion denoiser trained on generator outputs) is updated 5 times per generator update, ensuring it accurately tracks the non-stationary fake distribution before the generator receives its distribution-matching gradient. A GAN objective is then integrated by attaching a classification head to the bottleneck of the fake diffusion denoiser; this discriminator is trained on real images versus generator samples (both passed through forward diffusion at random timesteps), providing distribution-level supervision that is independent of the teacher's sampling trajectories and can push quality beyond the teacher. For multi-step generation, the paper introduces backward simulation: during training, intermediate inputs to the generator are produced by running the generator itself for a few steps (mimicking inference), rather than adding noise to real images, thereby eliminating the train/inference domain mismatch that degrades prior multi-step distillation methods.

## Results

On ImageNet-64×64, DMD2 achieves a FID of 1.28 (with extended training) in a single forward pass, surpassing the EDM teacher model (FID 2.22 with 511 ODE steps) and all prior one-step methods. On zero-shot COCO 2014 with the SDXL backbone, the 4-step DMD2 generator reaches FID 19.32 and Patch FID 20.86, matching the 100-step teacher (FID 19.36, Patch FID 21.38) while using 25× fewer forward passes. For SD v1.5 on 30K COCO prompts, the one-step DMD2 model achieves FID 8.35, outperforming the 50-step PNDM teacher (FID 8.59) and representing a 3.14-point improvement over the original DMD (FID 11.49).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On ImageNet-64×64, DMD2 (FID 1.28) decisively outperforms the original DMD (2.62), CTM (1.92), iCT-deep (3.25), and StyleGAN-XL (1.52), and even surpasses the EDM teacher using 511 ODE steps (2.22) and approaches the SDE teacher (1.36) in a single pass. On COCO 2014 with SDXL (10K prompts), the 4-step DMD2 (FID 19.32, Patch FID 20.86) matches the 100-step teacher (19.36, 21.38) while substantially outperforming LCM-SDXL (22.16), SDXL-Turbo (23.19), and SDXL-Lightning (24.46). For SD v1.5 on 30K COCO prompts, the one-step DMD2 (FID 8.35) beats DMD (11.49), SLAM 4-step (10.06), GigaGAN (9.09), and the 50-step PNDM teacher (8.59), though it remains slightly behind the 200-step SDE teacher (7.21). In the user study, DMD2 outperforms its teacher in perceived image quality for 24% of samples while maintaining comparable prompt alignment, all with 25× fewer forward passes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends DMD (Yin et al., CVPR 2024), which distills diffusion models via approximate KL divergence between score functions, by removing the regression loss that DMD required for stability. The stabilization mechanism borrows the two time-scale update rule from Heusel et al. (NeurIPS 2017), while the GAN component draws on the line of work using diffusion models as discriminators (LADD, SDXL-Lightning, UFOGen). The multi-step sampling schedule follows the consistency model framework (Song et al., ICML 2023), and the backward simulation technique is concurrent with Imagine Flash but differs in that the distribution matching loss is input-independent, avoiding error accumulation along the sampling path.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the need for precomputed noise-image pairs (which cost ~700 A100 days for SDXL-scale distillation), this work makes large-scale diffusion distillation dramatically more accessible and removes the performance ceiling that the regression loss imposed, allowing the student to surpass the teacher. The practical impact is significant: a 500× inference speedup with quality matching or exceeding the teacher (FID 1.28 one-step on ImageNet-64×64 vs. 2.22 for the 511-step ODE teacher). Open questions include the slight diversity gap relative to the teacher, the need for 4 steps on SDXL (vs. 1 on smaller models), the fixed guidance scale limiting user flexibility, and whether the GAN/distribution-matching balance can be further optimized.
