## Problem

Pre-trained antibody language models lack reliable labeled datasets for evaluating binding prediction, as existing benchmarks (e.g., ATUE's antibody discovery task) rely on noisy individual-level disease labels rather than sequence-level binding annotations. This makes it impossible to accurately assess whether a model's representations capture the sequence features that determine antigen-antibody binding.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243687/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark task takes a full-length VHH amino acid sequence (max 179 residues) and an antigen amino acid sequence, producing a binary binder/non-binder prediction. AVIDa-SARS-CoV-2 provides 77,003 labeled pairs across 12 SARS-CoV-2 spike mutants (WT through Omicron BA.1), with ~97% of non-significant pairs excluded to ensure label reliability. VHHCorpus-2M supplies 2,040,988 unique VHH sequences (only those observed more than once by NGS) for pre-training, all derived from five alpacas distinct from those used in the labeled dataset to prevent data leakage.

## Method

The paper introduces three assets: (1) AVIDa-SARS-CoV-2, generated by immunizing two maternal half-sibling alpacas with 13 SARS-CoV-2 spike variants, performing bead and cell biopanning, NGS sequencing, and statistical labeling via a two-proportion Z-test comparing VHH frequencies before and after panning (p≤0.05 threshold), with an additional noise-reduction step against negative controls; (2) VHHCorpus-2M, a 2M+ sequence corpus of full-length VHHs from five alpacas; and (3) VHHBERT, a RoBERTa-base model (86M params, 12 layers, 768-dim, positional embeddings of length 185) pre-trained with standard masked language modeling (15% mask rate) for 20 epochs. For benchmarking, nine models (ProtBert, ESM-2 150M/650M, AbLang-H, AntiBERTa2, AntiBERTa2-CSSP, IgBert, VHHBERT, and VHHBERT without pre-training) are fine-tuned by concatenating mean-pooled last-layer VHH representations with frozen ESM-2 antigen representations into an MLP classifier, trained on Alpaca P samples and tested on Alpaca C to evaluate generalization to unseen antibody clusters.

## Results

On the AVIDa-SARS-CoV-2 binding prediction benchmark (train on Alpaca P, test on Alpaca C; 27,318 test samples), AntiBERTa2-CSSP achieves the best F1-score of 0.652 and AUPRC of 0.690, followed by AntiBERTa2 (F1 0.641, AUPRC 0.660) and IgBert (F1 0.634, AUPRC 0.610). VHHBERT, pre-trained on only 2M VHH sequences, achieves an F1-score of 0.608 and AUPRC of 0.650, outperforming the general protein models ESM-2 650M (F1 0.598) and ProtBert (F1 0.580). The untrained VHHBERT w/o PT variant achieves high precision (0.811) but very low recall (0.392), yielding the worst F1-score (0.528), confirming the importance of pre-training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VHHBERT (F1 0.608) underperforms the larger antibody-specific models AntiBERTa2-CSSP (F1 0.652), AntiBERTa2 (F1 0.641), and IgBert (F1 0.634) by 4–14 percentage points in F1-score, which the authors attribute to VHHBERT's much smaller pre-training corpus (2M vs. 824M–2B sequences). However, VHHBERT outperforms both general protein language models—ESM-2 650M (F1 0.598) and ProtBert (F1 0.580)—as well as the antibody-specific AbLang-H (F1 0.547), confirming that antibody-specific pre-training is beneficial for VHH binding prediction. Notably, VHHBERT achieves the highest recall (0.567) among all pre-trained models, suggesting its representations capture more true binders despite lower precision. The untrained VHHBERT w/o PT variant (F1 0.528) demonstrates that pre-training is essential: without it, the model becomes overly conservative (precision 0.811 but recall only 0.392). Overall, the best model (AntiBERTa2-CSSP) still reaches only ~65% F1, indicating substantial room for improvement in practical antibody discovery applications.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the AVIDa-hIL6 pipeline (Tsuruta et al., NeurIPS 2023) from a single-alpaca, single-antigen setting to a two-alpaca, multi-variant SARS-CoV-2 spike setting, and introduces VHH-specific pre-training (VHHBERT) where all prior antibody LMs (AntiBERTa2, IgBert, AbLang, EATLM) were trained on conventional heavy/light-chain antibodies from the OAS database. The key conceptual departure from existing evaluation benchmarks like ATUE's antibody discovery task is the use of reliable sequence-level binary binding labels derived from statistical biopanning enrichment, rather than noisy individual-level disease labels. The paper also positions VHHs as a more tractable target for language modeling than conventional antibodies because each VHH is a single functional unit, eliminating the need for paired heavy-light chain sequences.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AVIDa-SARS-CoV-2 (77K labeled pairs across 12 SARS-CoV-2 variants) and VHHCorpus-2M (2M full-length VHH sequences) fill a critical gap: no prior dataset provided sequence-level binding labels for diverse VHHs against multiple antigen variants, making reliable evaluation of antibody LMs for binding prediction impossible. The benchmark reveals that even the strongest model (AntiBERTa2-CSSP) achieves only ~65% F1 in the cross-individual generalization setting, indicating substantial room for improvement and highlighting that current antibody LMs have not yet captured the sequence-structure-function relationships needed for practical drug discovery. Open questions include whether the two-alpaca bias limits generalizability, whether VHH-specific pre-training at larger scale (beyond 2M sequences) would close the gap with general antibody LMs, and whether the cross-individual evaluation protocol can be extended to more diverse immunization conditions.
