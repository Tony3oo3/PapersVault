## Problem

Existing diffusion-based text-to-image models struggle to generate a sequence of images depicting the same character (consistent identity and attire) across different scenes and actions, which is essential for visual storytelling. IP-Adapter-style reference guidance reduces text-prompt controllability, while identity-preservation methods like PhotoMaker and InstantID cannot guarantee consistency of clothing and scenario details. For video, prior transition-generation methods (SEINE, SparseCtrl) rely solely on temporal modules in the latent space and fail on large-scale character motions, producing corrupted intermediate frames.

## Input / Output

For image generation, the method takes a batch of text prompts (each describing the same character in a different activity) and optionally a reference ID image, producing a set of subject-consistent images where the character's face and attire remain stable while poses, backgrounds, and actions vary per prompt. For video generation, it takes a start frame and an end frame (typically two consecutive images from the consistent sequence) and outputs a smooth transition video of L intermediate frames. The image pipeline is fully training-free and operates on pre-trained SDXL or SD 1.5 backbones with 50-step DDIM sampling; the video pipeline uses a fine-tuned AnimateDiff V2 temporal module with 7.5 classifier-free guidance.

## Method

The core contribution is Consistent Self-Attention, a training-free, hot-pluggable module that replaces the standard self-attention in the U-Net: for each image feature in a batch, it randomly samples tokens from all other images in the batch, concatenates them with the image's own tokens, and projects the combined set into keys and values (while keeping the original query unchanged), thereby creating cross-image feature interactions that drive convergence of character identity and attire without any parameter updates. A sliding-window variant removes peak-memory dependence on story length for long sequences. For video, the Semantic Motion Predictor encodes the start and end frames into CLIP ViT-H-14 semantic space, linearly interpolates between them, and passes the sequence through 8 transformer layers (hidden dim 1024, 12 heads) to predict per-frame semantic embeddings; these embeddings are then injected as additional keys and values in the cross-attention of the video diffusion U-Net, guiding frame-by-frame generation. The key insight is that predicting motion in the CLIP semantic space—where spatial relationships are globally encoded—yields more stable transitions than operating purely in the pixel latent space, particularly for large displacements. The Semantic Motion Predictor is trained on Webvid-10M with MSE loss for 100k iterations on 8 A100 GPUs, while the image-consistency component requires no training at all.

## Results

For consistent image generation, StoryDiffusion achieves a Text-Image Similarity of 0.6586 and Character Similarity of 0.8950, outperforming IP-Adapter (0.6129 / 0.8802) and PhotoMaker (0.6541 / 0.8924) without any training. For transition video generation, StoryDiffusion attains LPIPS-f of 0.3794, CLIPSIM-f of 0.9606, FVD of 271, and FID of 109, surpassing SEINE (FVD 321, FID 140) and SparseCtrl (FVD 429, FID 181) across all six metrics. In a user study with 79 participants, StoryDiffusion received 68.3% preference for consistent images and 84.5% for transition videos.

## Baselines & Comparisons

For consistent image generation, StoryDiffusion (training-free) beats IP-Adapter by 4.6 points in Text-Image Similarity (0.6586 vs. 0.6129) and by 1.5 points in Character Similarity (0.8950 vs. 0.8802), while also slightly outperforming PhotoMaker (0.6586 vs. 0.6541; 0.8950 vs. 0.8924). Qualitatively, IP-Adapter loses prompt-specified objects (e.g., 'telescope', 'dog') and PhotoMaker fails to preserve attire across images, whereas StoryDiffusion maintains both identity and clothing consistency while retaining full text controllability. For transition video generation, StoryDiffusion outperforms SEINE by 12.5% in LPIPS-f (0.3794 vs. 0.4332) and by 50 FVD points (271 vs. 321), and beats SparseCtrl by 23% in LPIPS-f (0.3794 vs. 0.4913) and by 158 FVD points (271 vs. 429). SEINE produces corrupted intermediate frames with large motions (e.g., underwater kissing), while SparseCtrl generates extra limbs and appearance inconsistencies; StoryDiffusion produces smooth, physically plausible transitions. In the user study, StoryDiffusion dominates with 68.3% preference over IP-Adapter (20.8%) and PhotoMaker (10.9%) for images, and 84.5% over SEINE (5.9%) and SparseCtrl (9.6%) for videos.

## Relation to Prior Work

StoryDiffusion departs from the ID-preservation line (IP-Adapter, PhotoMaker) that anchors consistency to a single reference image, instead building cross-image correlations directly within the self-attention computation of a diffusion U-Net in a training-free manner. For video generation, it extends the AnimateDiff temporal module by introducing a Semantic Motion Predictor that operates in CLIP's image semantic space rather than the pixel/latent space used by SEINE and SparseCtrl, arguing that spatial relationships encoded in semantic embeddings better capture large-scale motion. The method is explicitly positioned as a lightweight, hot-pluggable alternative to the heavier temporal-attention architectures (VDM, MagicVideo) and to fine-tuning-based consistency approaches (DreamBooth, Custom Diffusion, The Chosen One's LoRA self-training).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free, plug-and-play nature of Consistent Self-Attention lowers the barrier to subject-consistent multi-image generation substantially, making it immediately applicable to existing Stable Diffusion pipelines without retraining. The finding that predicting motion in a semantic (CLIP) space yields more stable long-range transitions than latent-space-only temporal prediction opens a direction for video generation that may generalize beyond transition tasks. However, the authors acknowledge that very large inter-frame differences still challenge the method, and minor detail inconsistencies (e.g., ties) persist, leaving long-video generation with global coherence as an open problem. The approach's simplicity also raises the question of how much further consistency can be pushed before the zero-shot attention modification becomes insufficient.
