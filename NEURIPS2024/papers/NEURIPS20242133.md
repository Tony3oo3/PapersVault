## Problem

Cloud platforms like HuggingFace store hundreds of thousands of full fine-tuned foundation models, each at the same size as its pre-trained base, creating enormous storage costs (e.g., ~159 TB for just six popular model families). Traditional lossless compressors (LZMA, Gzip, etc.) achieve almost no compression on raw model bytes because individual parameters lack redundancy. The missing piece is a lossless scheme that exploits the structural similarity between fine-tuned and pre-trained models without altering any user data.

## Input / Output

The method takes a model pair: the pre-trained model (kept uncompressed in cloud) and a fine-tuned model in PyTorch format with 32-bit or 16-bit float parameters (fp32, fp16, or bf16). It produces a compressed delta file consisting of range-coded symbols (sign and most-significant-bit position per element) concatenated with the remaining raw lower bits. Decompression is exact and lossless, reconstructing the fine-tuned model bit-for-bit given the pre-trained model as reference. The cloud stores one full pre-trained model plus one compressed delta per fine-tuned variant.

## Method

FM-Delta first monotonically maps each float parameter of both models to an unsigned integer of the same bit width (flipping the MSB for positive values, flipping all bits for negative values), preserving order and linearity of differences for same-sign, same-exponent floats. It then computes the unsigned integer delta, which—unlike float subtraction—yields a bit-redundant result with many leading zeros because the delta's most significant bit is typically far below the full 32-bit width. The delta is entropy-coded via range coding: each element is decomposed into a sign s, a most-significant-bit position k (forming 2n+1 symbols), and remaining raw bits m; the symbols are encoded with a quasi-static probability modeler while the raw bits are stored directly. Theoretically, the paper proves the model difference grows only as O(T^{1/4}) with fine-tuning steps T under standard SGD assumptions, and that the integer delta's MSB position increases by only a few bits even when the float value changes by orders of magnitude, ensuring robustness across diverse fine-tuning scenarios.

## Results

FM-Delta reduces cloud storage for massive fine-tuned models by approximately 50% on average across all tested model families (from 48% for GPT-NeoX-20B to 68% for ResNet50), compared to only ~90% (i.e., minimal compression) achieved by all five traditional lossless compressors. For the headline case of 10 fine-tuned GPT-NeoX-20B models, storage drops from 423.2 GB to 205.2 GB. FM-Delta achieves a compression throughput of 109.7 MB/s and decompression throughput of 100.9 MB/s, the highest compression speed among all tested schemes. The compression benefit grows with the number of fine-tuned variants stored, since the single uncompressed pre-trained model's share of total storage decreases.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FM-Delta is compared against five state-of-the-art lossless compression schemes: LZMA, Gzip, Zlib, FPZip, and BZip2. All five traditional methods achieve only ~90% compression rates (i.e., they reduce file size by merely 8–10%) when applied directly to model byte streams, regardless of model family or size. In stark contrast, FM-Delta consistently achieves 48–68% compression rates, representing a 2–4× greater storage reduction. The gap is most pronounced for larger models: on GPT-NeoX-20B (10 fine-tuned variants), FM-Delta stores 205.2 GB versus 290.7 GB for the best traditional method (BZip2) and 391.2 GB for FPZip. In terms of throughput, FM-Delta has the highest compression speed (109.7 MB/s vs. 83.5 MB/s for FPZip, the next best), while its decompression speed (100.9 MB/s) ranks third behind Zlib (260.8 MB/s) and Gzip (236.6 MB/s), which benefit from the faster Huffman coding in the DEFLATE algorithm. The paper also shows that applying traditional compressors to the float delta or signed-integer delta still yields only 72–86% compression, confirming that the float-to-unsigned-integer mapping plus range coding in FM-Delta is essential for the large gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

FM-Delta adapts the float-to-integer mapping and delta compression paradigm from FPzip (Lindstrom & Isenburg, 2006) to a new object: the parameter difference between a fine-tuned model and its pre-trained base, rather than adjacent elements within a single array. For entropy coding, it employs range coding with a quasi-static probability modeler (Schindler, 2000). The paper explicitly departs from prior delta-compression work on neural networks (Delta-DNN, checkpoint compression) which use lossy quantization for sequentially updated checkpoints on the same data domain; FM-Delta targets a fundamentally different setting—lossless compression of diverse, independently fine-tuned models from different users and tasks for cloud archival storage.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

With 89% of full fine-tuned models on HuggingFace being inactive yet consuming ~159TB of storage, the ~50% reduction FM-Delta achieves translates to substantial cost savings for cloud providers, and the O(T^{1/4}) theoretical bound provides a principled explanation for why the approach works broadly. Key limitations include the absence of GPU-side compression support and the assumption that a pre-trained base model is available and stable; open questions include whether the method extends to dynamic compression during training/inference and to models with very large distributional shifts between pre-training and fine-tuning domains.
