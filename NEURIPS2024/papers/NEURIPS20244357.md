## Problem

Existing open-vocabulary part segmentation (OVPS) methods suffer from three interrelated failures: they misidentify parts at the object level (e.g., classifying a dog's tail as a sheep's ear), produce overlapping or gappy part boundaries due to ambiguous multi-granularity semantics, and systematically ignore small or infrequent parts. These issues stem from treating each object-specific part in isolation without leveraging the hierarchical relationship between generalized parts and their parent objects.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and an object-specific part category name (e.g., "dog's head"), which is parsed into an object name ("dog") and a generalized part name ("head"). Both names are encoded by a frozen CLIP text encoder, and the resulting embeddings condition the CLIP image encoder features via FiLM. The model simultaneously outputs three levels of segmentation masks: object-specific part masks (the primary target), object-level masks, and generalized part masks, enabling multi-granularity supervision during training.

## Method

PartCLIPSeg builds on the CLIPSeg ViT-B/16 encoder-decoder architecture and introduces three key mechanisms. First, it decomposes each object-specific part name into object and generalized part components, conditions image features on each via separate FiLM operations, then reconstructs object-specific part embeddings through a projection of concatenated features followed by a second FiLM, with multi-level BCE supervision across all three mask granularities. Second, a separation loss binarizes min-max-normalized self-attention maps (smoothed with a Gaussian filter) and penalizes the fraction of pixels where multiple parts co-activate, enforcing disjoint part boundaries. Third, an enhancement loss identifies the part with the weakest peak activation and boosts it, preventing small or rare parts from being suppressed by larger neighbors. The total training objective combines the multi-level mask loss with weighted separation and enhancement terms.

## Results

On Pascal-Part-116, PartCLIPSeg achieves a harmonic mean mIoU of 30.67% in the Pred-All setting and 38.79% in the Oracle-Obj setting, outperforming the best baselines by +3.94% and +3.55% respectively. On the more challenging ADE20K-Part-234 dataset, PartCLIPSeg reaches 11.38% harmonic mIoU (Pred-All) and 38.60% (Oracle-Obj), surpassing the best baseline by +7.85% and +4.45%. On PartImageNet, improvements of +8.14% (Pred-All) and +9.85% (Oracle-Obj) over CLIPSeg are observed. Unseen-category performance shows the largest gains, e.g., 23.56% vs. 25.42% (CAT-Seg) on Pascal-Part-116 Pred-All unseen, and 38.82% vs. 30.92% (CLIPSeg) on ADE20K-Part-234 Oracle-Obj unseen.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PartCLIPSeg outperforms all four baselines across every dataset and evaluation setting. Against ZSSeg+ [52] (ResNet-50), the gap is largest on ADE20K-Part-234 where ZSSeg+ achieves only 1.74% harmonic mIoU (Pred-All) versus PartCLIPSeg's 11.38%, reflecting ZSSeg+'s inability to handle fine-grained part categories. Against VLPart [40], PartCLIPSeg leads by +16.28% harmonic mIoU on Pascal-Part-116 Pred-All (30.67 vs. 14.39), primarily due to VLPart's object-level misidentification (e.g., confusing dog parts with cat/sheep parts). Against CLIPSeg [32,46] (the direct architectural base), PartCLIPSeg gains +12.71% on Pascal-Part-116 Pred-All and +4.45% on ADE20K-Part-234 Oracle-Obj, with the largest unseen-category improvement on PartImageNet (+14.48% Oracle-Obj unseen). Against CAT-Seg [11,46], PartCLIPSeg achieves +3.95% on Pascal-Part-116 Pred-All and +7.85% on ADE20K-Part-234 Pred-All, where CAT-Seg's cost-aggregation approach struggles with the fine-grained part granularity. Notably, PartCLIPSeg also achieves the highest Boundary IoU (37.55% harmonic on Pascal-Part-116 Oracle-Obj) and the best Recall on underrepresented parts, confirming that the separation and enhancement losses address both boundary ambiguity and small-part omission.

## Relation to Prior Work

PartCLIPSeg builds directly on CLIPSeg's CLIP-based encoder-decoder architecture for open-vocabulary segmentation, extending it to the part-segmentation setting. It departs from prior OVPS methods (VLPart, OV-PARTS) by decomposing object-specific part labels into generalized part and object-level components, using FiLM conditioning to separately modulate image features for each, then reconstructing object-specific predictions. The attention control losses (separation and enhancement) are adapted from diffusion-model attention manipulation literature (Attend-and-Excite, Separate-and-Enhance), repurposing them to enforce disjoint part boundaries and boost underrepresented part activations in a segmentation context.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The generalized-parts decomposition offers a clean, parameter-efficient way to share part knowledge across object categories, reducing redundancy as the number of object classes grows. The cross-pollination of diffusion-model attention control into segmentation is a practical technique that could transfer to other fine-grained segmentation tasks. However, the method remains limited to semantic (non-instance) part segmentation, cannot distinguish individual part instances (e.g., left vs. right paw), and the authors acknowledge that structural/relational inductive biases (analogous to keypoint detection) could further improve results. Open questions include whether the approach scales to truly open-ended part vocabularies beyond predefined object-part pairs.
