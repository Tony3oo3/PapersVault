## Problem

Existing generalized-linear dynamical model (GLDM) learning algorithms either model a single time-series or, when handling two sources, cannot explicitly dissociate shared from private dynamics between them. This limits their utility in neuroscience applications where one must jointly model, e.g., neural spiking and behavioral kinematics while identifying which latent dynamics are common to both signals versus specific to one.

## Input / Output

The method consumes two generalized-linear time-series (e.g., Poisson spike counts and Gaussian kinematics, or two Poisson spike-count streams) along with user-specified hyperparameters: shared latent dimension n1, private primary dimension n2, and horizon values i_r and i_z for each observation. It produces a block-structured GLDM with explicit shared dynamics matrix A11, observation matrices C_r and C_z partitioned into shared and private components, baseline offsets, and noise statistics (Q) recovered via a convex semidefinite program. Because the algorithm is purely covariance-based and analytical (non-iterative), it requires only empirical first- and second-order moments of the observations after moment conversion, making it computationally efficient compared to EM-based alternatives.

## Method

PGLDM is a multi-staged covariance-based subspace identification algorithm that exploits a block-structured state matrix where shared latents x^(1) drive both observations, private latents x^(2) drive only the primary, and private latents x^(3) drive only the secondary. In Stage 1, a cross-term Hankel matrix H_zr = Cov(z_future, r_past) is constructed and SVD-decomposed; because the block structure ensures cross-covariances between z and r involve only shared latents, the top n1 singular values isolate the shared observability and controllability subspaces, yielding A11, C_r^(1), and C_z^(1). In Stage 2, a residual Hankel matrix H_r^(2) = H_r − Γ_r^(1)Δ^(1) is formed by subtracting the shared component from the primary Hankel matrix, and its SVD extracts the private primary dynamics [A21 A22] and C_r^(2). The key enabling insight is that moment conversion equations (e.g., log-rate moments from Poisson counts) allow all required covariances to be computed from observed data, making the framework agnostic to the specific GL observation distribution (Poisson, Bernoulli, Gaussian) as long as a tractable moment conversion exists.

## Results

In simulations, PGLDM (stage 1) achieves the best shared mode identification error (log10 scale, lower is better) across all four observation configurations: -2.757±0.070 (Gaussian/Gaussian), -2.707±0.091 (Poisson/Gaussian), -1.969±0.079 (Poisson/Poisson), and -2.864±0.072 (Bernoulli/Gaussian). On the NHP reaching dataset, PGLDM achieves higher kinematic prediction correlation coefficient at all latent dimensions compared to PLDSID and Laplace-EM, reaching comparable performance with as few as 4 shared latent states where PLDSID requires ~12. On the V1/V2 dataset, PGLDM achieves higher V1 decoding AUC while maintaining comparable V2 self-prediction AUC across all tested latent dimensions (n_x ∈ {2,4,6,8}).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PGLDM outperforms all single-source baselines (Laplace-EM, PLDSID, bestLDS, Covariance SSID) in shared mode identification across every supported observation configuration, with margins of roughly 1.3–1.7 in log10 error (e.g., -2.707 vs. -1.319 for Poisson/Gaussian vs. PLDSID). The only method that approaches PGLDM is PSID [14] on the Gaussian/Gaussian case (-2.985 vs. -2.757), but PSID is restricted to continuous Gaussian observations and cannot handle Poisson or Bernoulli data. On the NHP reaching dataset, PGLDM achieves higher kinematic prediction CC at every latent dimension tested, and with only n_1=4 shared states it matches the performance that PLDSID requires ~12 states to reach; PGLDM also requires substantially lower training time than Laplace-EM. On the V1/V2 dataset, PGLDM achieves higher V1 decoding AUC while maintaining comparable V2 self-prediction AUC, confirming that the prioritized shared-dynamics learning transfers across brain regions and observation types. The one limitation is that PGLDM's multi-stage design can accumulate estimation error in stage 2 when shared dynamics are weak, though this is mitigated by inspecting singular values before parameter extraction.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate lines of subspace identification research: (1) covariance-based SSID methods that dissociate shared vs. private dynamics between two Gaussian time-series (PSID, Sani et al. 2021), and (2) moment-conversion-based SSID extensions to generalized-linear observations such as Poisson (PLDSID, Buesing et al. 2012) or Bernoulli (bestLDS, Stone et al. 2023). The key gap is that no prior method supports both capabilities simultaneously — PSID is restricted to Gaussian observations, while PLDSID and bestLDS model only a single time-series. PGLDM bridges this by applying the moment-conversion framework to construct both the standard Hankel matrix and a novel cross-term Hankel matrix (H_zr), then performing a multi-staged SVD decomposition that prioritizes shared dynamics before extracting private dynamics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method provides a principled, computationally efficient (analytical, non-iterative) way to model the dynamical relationship between two heterogeneous time-series sources — a common need in neuroscience (e.g., decoding behavior from spiking, or one cortical area from another) that was previously unaddressed by GLDM learning algorithms. The prioritized latent allocation (shared states get priority over private ones) yields meaningful practical gains: better cross-decoding with substantially fewer latent dimensions. Open questions include handling non-stationarities in chronic recordings, sensitivity to finite-sample moment estimation errors, and whether the block-structure assumptions (particularly A_21 ≠ 0 vs. A_31 = 0) are always appropriate for real neural circuits.
