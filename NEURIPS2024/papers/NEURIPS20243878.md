## Problem

Existing Optimal Transport (OT)-based proxy reward methods for imitation learning treat trajectory frames as an unordered bag, making the reward invariant to temporal order and non-stationary across the trajectory. This temporal blindness introduces noise into the reward signal, causing the agent to receive inconsistent rewards for identical state-action pairs and making it harder to learn correct early behaviors from only a few expert demonstrations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243878/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes two expert video demonstrations (pixel observations at 84×84×3 with 3-frame stacking) and online agent trajectories collected during RL rollouts. A fixed pretrained ResNet-50 encoder extracts visual embeddings from each frame, which are used to compute the transport cost matrix. The output is a per-step OT-based proxy reward that relabels transitions in the DrQ-v2 replay buffer, ultimately yielding a trained continuous-control robot policy evaluated on Meta-world manipulation tasks.

## Method

TemporalOT modifies the standard OT reward pipeline in two stages. First, it replaces the pairwise cosine-similarity transport cost with a context-embedding cost that averages cosine similarity over a window of k_c consecutive frames, making the cost matrix more robust to single-frame noise. Second, it introduces a diagonal-like temporal mask M(i,j)=1 only when |i−j|≤k_m into the OT objective, restricting the transport plan to match each agent frame only with temporally nearby expert frames; the masked problem is solved via Sinkhorn iterations on the masked kernel K=M⊙e^{−C/ε}. This enforces the assumption that the agent moves at a similar speed to the expert, grounding the reward in local temporal structure rather than global frame matching. The resulting per-step rewards are used to relabel transitions for standard DDPG-style RL training (DrQ-v2 backbone) without any task reward.

## Results

On the Meta-world benchmark (9 manipulation tasks, 2 expert video demonstrations, no task reward), TemporalOT achieves a 61.1% average success rate, substantially outperforming the prior SOTA baseline ADS (35.9%) and the OT-based baselines OT0.9 (35.5%) and OT0.99 (27.8%). TemporalOT wins on 8 out of 9 individual tasks versus ADS, with the largest gains on Basketball (94.4% vs 42.2%), Stick-push (97.6% vs 20.0%), and Door-open (78.4% vs 52.0%). The only task where TemporalOT underperforms ADS is Push (8.4% vs 17.2%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TemporalOT (61.1% avg) decisively outperforms the prior SOTA OT-based method ADS (35.9% avg), winning on 8 of 9 tasks; the sole exception is Push where ADS achieves 17.2% vs TemporalOT's 8.4%. Against the OT-based baselines (OTR with γ=0.99 at 27.8% and γ=0.9 at 35.5%), TemporalOT improves by 25.6 and 25.6 percentage points on average, with the most dramatic gains on Basketball (94.4% vs 0.0%/76.6%) and Stick-push (97.6% vs 0.0%/48.8%). The non-OT imitation baselines—BC (2.5%) and GAIfO (5.2%)—perform far worse because they overfit to the limited 2-demonstration dataset, while the oracle TaskReward baseline (DrQ-v2, 20.8%) struggles on most tasks due to sparse reward and exploration difficulty, only excelling on Door-lock (86.2%) and Window-open (85.6%) where the agent can eventually find the goal. Overall, the temporal mask and context-embedding cost matrix together yield a 25.2-point average improvement over ADS, confirming that incorporating temporal order information into the OT reward is the key factor behind the gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the OT-based proxy reward line initiated by OTR (Luo et al., 2023) and refined by ADS (Liu et al., ICLR 2024), which use Sinkhorn-optimized transport plans to relabel agent trajectories with imitation rewards in place of hand-engineered task rewards. The key departure is identifying that both prior methods treat expert trajectory frames as an unordered bag, discarding temporal sequence information, and that the resulting reward is non-stationary (the same state-action pair receives different rewards depending on the rest of the trajectory). TemporalOT addresses this with two targeted modifications: a group-wise (context-embedding) cost matrix replacing pair-wise cosine similarity, and a diagonal temporal mask on the transport plan that restricts matching to a local time window.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work offers a practical recipe for learning robot manipulation policies from only two expert video demonstrations without any task reward, a setting where standard RL and behavior cloning both struggle. The temporal-order observation is a valid gap that prior OT-reward methods overlooked, and the fix is simple enough to be adopted as a drop-in improvement. However, the method inherits IL limitations (dependence on demonstration quality, a fixed pretrained visual encoder, and an implicit assumption of matched agent-expert speed), and evaluation is confined to nine Meta-world tasks. Open directions include camera-view-invariant policies, dynamic mask designs, and scaling to more complex or longer-horizon manipulation tasks.
