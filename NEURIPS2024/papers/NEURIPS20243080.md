## Problem

Existing SDS-based 3D head generation methods rely on RGB diffusion priors that cannot independently control geometry, texture, and lighting, producing artifacts like Janus problems and oversaturated albedo. Additionally, text prompts lack the granularity to specify a subject's identity and expression, and prior approaches require large 3D scan datasets (up to 10,000 scans) for adequate generalization. ID-to-3D addresses all three gaps by introducing task-specific 2D priors, ArcFace-based identity conditioning, and a lightweight fine-tuning strategy that avoids dependence on large 3D datasets.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 1–5 casually captured in-the-wild face images (from which ArcFace identity embeddings are extracted), a free-form text prompt, and expression conditioning via CLIP embeddings of 23 FACS-coded descriptors from FaceWarehouse. It produces a render-ready 3D head asset comprising a DMTET-based mesh, a UV-mapped pseudo-albedo texture, PBR material maps (roughness and specularity), and up to 13 identity-consistent expression variants, each with separate geometric and textural parameters. The output supports relighting, ID-consistent text editing, and physical simulation in standard rendering engines.

## Method

The core contribution is decomposing the single SDS objective into two independent KL-divergence minimizations—one over normal maps (geometry) and one over albedo maps (texture)—each guided by a dedicated 2D diffusion prior. These priors are created by fine-tuning Stable Diffusion with LoRA adapters (only 0.2% of parameters) on rasterized normals and albedo from the NHPM dataset, while injecting ArcFace identity embeddings and CLIP expression embeddings through IP-Adapter-style cross-attention layers. The 3D geometry is represented by an extended DMTET network (a Transformer over a deformable tetrahedral grid) coupled with learnable per-expression latent codes, enabling multiple expressions to be optimized simultaneously within a single identity. A two-stage SDS optimization first carves geometry with random expression sampling and a Laplacian regularizer, then optimizes texture with randomized environment lighting and camera poses to disentangle reflectance. This compositional design—separating geometry and texture guidance, encoding identity as a compact embedding rather than a raw image, and disentangling expression via latent codes—enables high-fidelity, ID-consistent generation without large 3D scan datasets.

## Results

ID-to-3D achieves the lowest FID scores on both texture (153) and geometry (85) among all compared methods, outperforming the next-best baselines Magic123 (texture FID 162) and HumanNorm (geometry FID 98). On identity similarity (CosFace metric), ID-to-3D reports the highest mean similarity and lowest variance across viewpoints, indicating the most ID-consistent 3D heads. User preference surveys confirm these quantitative results, with ID-to-3D receiving the highest preference percentage for both texture and geometry quality over the four strongest baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ID-to-3D outperforms all six baselines on both texture and geometry FID. Against the text-to-3D SDS methods, it beats HumanNorm (the strongest text-to-3D baseline) by 11 points on texture FID (153 vs. 164) and 13 points on geometry FID (85 vs. 98), and substantially outperforms Fantasia3D (252/159), DreamFace (188/145), and TADA (215/118). Against the image-to-3D methods, it surpasses Magic123 on texture FID (153 vs. 162) and by a large margin on geometry FID (85 vs. 180), while also beating DreamCraft3D on both metrics (153 vs. 205 texture; 85 vs. 165 geometry). On identity consistency, ID-to-3D achieves the highest mean CosFace similarity with the lowest variance across viewpoints, whereas DreamCraft3D and Magic123 produce photorealistic front-facing renders but struggle with 3D consistency (highest variance), and DreamFace cannot generate hair or eyes by design (lowest mean similarity). User preference surveys align with the FID results, confirming ID-to-3D's superiority in perceived quality for both geometry and texture.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Score Distillation Sampling (SDS) paradigm from DreamFusion to identity-conditioned 3D head generation, departing from prior text-to-3D human methods (Fantasia3D, TADA, DreamFace, HumanNorm) that rely solely on textual prompts or raw image conditioning. The key architectural departure is the use of ArcFace identity embeddings via IP-Adapter as the primary conditioning signal, combined with task-specific 2D diffusion priors (normal maps for geometry, albedo for texture) created by fine-tuning only 0.2% of Stable Diffusion parameters via LoRA. The expression disentanglement through learnable latent codes in a DMTET-based neural parametric head model builds on NHPM but extends it to support multiple simultaneous expressions per identity, a capability absent in prior SDS-based head generators.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work demonstrates that high-fidelity, identity-consistent 3D head assets with disentangled expressions can be generated from just 1–5 in-the-wild images without requiring large-scale 3D scan datasets, lowering the barrier for applications in gaming, telepresence, and AR/VR. The identity-embedding conditioning approach offers a more precise and less ambiguous control mechanism than text prompts for facial identity, and the two-stage geometry-then-texture pipeline with task-specific priors addresses a known failure mode of single-model SDS (Janus artifacts, texture-geometry mismatch). Open questions include whether the pseudo-albedo limitation (constrained by NHPM's low-resolution UV maps) can be resolved with higher-quality training data, whether the approach generalizes beyond the face embedding network's coverage, and how to reduce the computational cost of PBR rendering for real-time or video-driven applications.
