## Problem

Entropic OT solvers (Sinkhorn-based) are the de-facto tool for computing couplings and transport maps at scale, but their performance is highly sensitive to the regularization parameter ε, which simultaneously governs convergence speed, statistical bias, and coupling entropy. No existing strategy provides a one-size-fits-all solution: too large an ε yields blurry couplings and collapsed maps, while too small an ε causes numerical instability. The paper asks whether the OT problem can be decomposed into a sequence of easier sub-problems so that the choice of ε at each step becomes less critical.

## Input / Output

The solver takes two weighted point clouds (n source points and m target points in R^d with weight vectors a and b summing to 1) plus three schedules: step-lengths (α_k), regularization parameters (ε_k), and Sinkhorn stopping thresholds (τ_k). It outputs two objects: (i) an n×m coupling matrix P whose marginals satisfy ‖P1_m − a‖₁ + ‖P^T 1_n − b‖₁ < τ_K, and (ii) a vector-valued map estimator T_Prog that can be evaluated at arbitrary out-of-sample points by composing K entropic barycentric projections with McCann-type interpolations. The map estimator is stored compactly as the sequence of dual potentials g^(k) rather than as a full matrix.

## Method

PROGOT iteratively pushes the source point cloud toward the target along an estimated McCann interpolation path over K steps. At each step k, it runs Sinkhorn to compute an entropic map E^(k) between the current interpolated source μ̂_ε^(k) and the fixed target ν̂, then constructs a McCann-type interpolator S^(k) = (1−α_k)Id + α_k E^(k) to update the source as μ̂_ε^(k+1) = S^(k)_# μ̂_ε^(k). The final progressive map is the composition T_Prog^(K) = E^(K) ∘ S^(K−1) ∘ … ∘ S^(0). Two key design choices make the solver robust: (1) the regularization ε_k is scheduled from a large initial value (scaled by the source-target cost matrix mean) down to a final ε_K selected by minimizing the identity-map error on the target itself, and (2) Sinkhorn is warm-started at each step using the previous step's duals scaled by (1−α_k). The self-correcting structure means that biases introduced by suboptimal ε at early steps are corrected by later, better-conditioned sub-problems. The paper proves L² consistency of T_Prog at rate n^{−1/d} under standard smoothness and bounded-density assumptions, and shows empirically that PROGOT outperforms EOT, debiased EOT, Monge Gap, and ICNN baselines on single-cell and synthetic data while matching or beating Sinkhorn on coupling quality at comparable iteration counts.

## Results

On the sci-Plex single-cell dataset (5 drugs, PCA dimensions 16/64/256), PROGOT achieves the lowest Sinkhorn divergence D_εD across all settings, ranking #1 overall (e.g., 20.8±0.2 vs. 22.8±0.2 for EOT and 39.8±0.4 for Debiased EOT at d=256, Belinostat). On the large-scale CIFAR-10 de-blurring task (n=60,000, d=1024), PROGOT recovers a coupling with trace 1.000 and KL divergence 0.00000 at σ=2, and trace 0.9989 with 1,590 Sinkhorn iterations at σ=4, compared to Sinkhorn's trace 0.9954 requiring 2,379 iterations. On the Gaussian Mixture benchmark at d=128, PROGOT achieves MSE 0.099±0.009, outperforming EOT (0.12±0.01) and Monge Gap (0.36±0.02).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PROGOT consistently outperforms all four map-estimation baselines on the sci-Plex dataset, with the gap widening at higher PCA dimensions: at d=256, PROGOT achieves 20.8±0.2 (Belinostat) versus 22.8±0.2 for EOT, 34.4±0.3 for Monge Gap, 39.8±0.4 for Debiased EOT, and 42±1 for ICNN. The advantage over Debiased EOT is particularly pronounced (roughly 2× lower divergence at d=256), suggesting that progressive scheduling effectively mitigates the bias that debiasing corrections only partially address. On the 4i dataset (Table 4), PROGOT ranks #2 overall, with Debiased EOT slightly ahead at #1, indicating the advantage is dataset-dependent. For coupling computation on CIFAR-10 (n=60,000, d=1024), PROGOT matches or exceeds Sinkhorn's coupling quality (trace 1.000 vs. 0.9999 at σ=2; 0.9989 vs. 0.9954 at σ=4) while using ~33% fewer total Sinkhorn iterations at σ=4 (1,590 vs. 2,379). On the GMM benchmark at d=256, ICNN (0.117±0.005) slightly edges out PROGOT (0.12±0.01), but PROGOT dominates at d=128 (0.099 vs. 0.177 for ICNN), and both substantially outperform Monge Gap (0.273) and EOT (0.16).

## Relation to Prior Work

PROGOT builds directly on the entropic OT / Sinkhorn framework (Cuturi, 2013) and the entropic map estimator of Pooladian and Niles-Weed (2021), but repositions Sinkhorn from a standalone solver to a subroutine within a progressive scheme inspired by the Benamou–Brenier dynamic formulation and McCann interpolation. The theoretical analysis draws on the recent stability bounds of Divol et al. (2024) for entropic Brenier maps, extending them to variations in the source measure. Conceptually, the paper departs from the static single-shot EOT paradigm by decomposing transport into a sequence of easier sub-problems along an estimated geodesic, and from neural-network OT approaches (ICNN, Monge Gap, flow matching) by remaining fully non-parametric while still matching or exceeding their empirical performance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ε-tuning problem in entropic OT has been a persistent practical pain point; PROGOT's self-correcting progressive structure and automatic ε-scheduling (Algorithm 4) offer a principled, out-of-the-box alternative that reduces sensitivity to this hyperparameter. The provable consistency guarantee (Theorem 3) is valuable, though the convergence rate n^{-1/d} is slower than the n^{-2/(d+4)} rate of the direct entropic map under the same assumptions, and the theoretical parameter choices (ε_k ∝ n^{-1/2d}, α_k ∝ n^{-1/d}) do not reflect practical schedules. Open questions include whether the progressive decomposition can be made to recover the faster rate, how the method behaves when the McCann interpolation path is poorly estimated in high dimensions, and whether the approach extends naturally to unbalanced or multi-marginal OT settings.
