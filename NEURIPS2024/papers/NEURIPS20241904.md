## Problem

Existing methods for learning with imprecise labels (partial, noisy, or missing) are each tailored to a single configuration, making them inapplicable when multiple imprecision types coexist in realistic datasets. No prior framework provides a single, principled formulation that unifies all these settings and scales to their mixtures without ad-hoc redesign.

## Input / Output

The method consumes data instances (32×32 or 224×224 RGB images, or text sequences) paired with imprecise label information I, which can be a candidate label set (partial), a single corrupted label (noisy), or absent (unlabeled). It produces a class probability distribution p(y|x;θ) over C classes. A key structural detail is that the imprecise information I is never treated as the ground-truth label; instead it defines a posterior P(Y|X,I;θ^t) over all possible true labels, and the model is trained against this full distribution rather than a single rectified label.

## Method

ILL treats the true label Y as a latent variable and the imprecise information I as a constraint, then applies EM to maximize the marginal likelihood P(X,I;θ)=Σ_Y P(X,Y,I;θ). The E-step computes the posterior P(Y|X,I;θ^t) (whose form depends on the imprecision type), and the M-step maximizes the resulting ELBO, yielding closed-form cross-entropy losses with the posterior as a soft target. For partial labels, the posterior is uniform over candidates; for unlabeled data, it is the network's own prediction (self-training without thresholding); for noisy labels, a learnable noise transition model T(ŷ|y;ω) conditions both the soft target and the supervised term. The mixed setting (MILL) simply combines all three loss components, and a strong-weak augmentation strategy provides the consistency regularization inherent in each derived objective.

## Results

On partial label learning, ILL achieves 96.37% accuracy on CIFAR-10 (q=0.1) and 75.31% on CIFAR-100 (q=0.01), surpassing the fully-supervised reference (94.91% and 73.56% respectively) and the best prior method PiCO by an average of 2.13% and 2.72%. On semi-supervised learning, ILL achieves the best error rate on STL-10 with 40 labels (11.09%) and Amazon Review with 250 labels (43.96%). On noisy label learning, ILL reaches 96.78% on CIFAR-10 symmetric noise (η=0.2) and 79.37% on WebVision, outperforming SOP and DivideMix. On the novel mixed imprecise label learning setting, ILL achieves 96.47% on CIFAR-10 (q=0.1, η=0.1) and 77.53% on CIFAR-100 (q=0.01, η=0.1), consistently outperforming all partial-noisy-label baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On partial label learning, ILL outperforms all six baselines (LWS, PRODEN, CC, MSE, EXP, PiCO) across every dataset and partial ratio, with the largest gap over the strongest prior method PiCO (avg +2.13% on CIFAR-10, +2.72% on CIFAR-100). On semi-supervised learning, ILL wins on STL-10 (40 labels: 11.09% vs. SimMatch's 11.77%) and Amazon Review (250 labels: 43.96% vs. SoftMatch's 45.29%), but is slightly behind FreeMatch on CIFAR-100 (22.06% vs. 21.40%) and behind SimMatch on IMDB (7.32% vs. 7.44% is actually a win; on 100 labels ILL is 7.64% vs. SimMatch 7.08%). On noisy label learning, ILL achieves the best CIFAR-10 results (96.78% sym η=0.2, 94.75% asym η=0.4) and the best WebVision result (79.37% vs. DivideMix 77.32%), but trails SOP on CIFAR-100 asymmetric noise (75.82% vs. 78.00%). On the mixed imprecise label learning setting, ILL consistently beats PiCO+, IRNet, DALI, and DALI+Mixup across all noise and partial ratios, with margins of 0.6–1.7% on CIFAR-10 and 1.0–1.5% on CIFAR-100.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the classical EM algorithm (Dempster et al., 1977) as its algorithmic backbone, repurposing it to treat imprecise label information as a distribution over latent true labels rather than as a noisy estimate to be corrected. It departs from the dominant paradigm in PLL (label disambiguation), SSL (pseudo-labeling with confidence thresholds), and NLL (noise model correction) by avoiding any single-point label inference and instead training over the full posterior distribution. The framework subsumes earlier EM-based approaches for fuzzy observations (Denœux, Van Rooyen, Quost, Gong) but removes their additional assumptions, and it explicitly positions against the 'infer-then-train' pipeline that prior methods like PiCO, FixMatch, and SOP each implement in task-specific ways.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in real-world annotation pipelines where partial, noisy, and missing labels coexist simultaneously — a setting no prior method handles in a single framework. The paper demonstrates that a simple EM formulation without contrastive losses, dynamic thresholds, or re-weighting can remain competitive across three distinct settings, suggesting that much of the task-specific machinery in prior work may be addressing symptoms rather than the core problem. Key limitations include evaluation only on relatively small datasets (CIFAR-10/100, CUB-200, STL-10), an instance-independent noise transition model that underperforms on asymmetric CIFAR-100 noise, and unverified scalability to large-scale or imbalanced settings — all flagged as open questions by the authors.
