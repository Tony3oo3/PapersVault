## Problem

Existing 3D anomaly detection methods rely on storing normal point features during training, making them inapplicable when target point clouds are unavailable due to privacy constraints or the absence of training data for new products. While zero-shot anomaly detection has been explored for 2D images using vision-language models, zero-shot 3D anomaly detection—detecting and segmenting anomalies on entirely unseen 3D objects—remains an unexplored research gap.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242694/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D point cloud (resized to 336×336) as input, which is rendered offline into 9 multi-view 2D images by rotating the cloud along the X-axis at evenly spaced angles between −π and π. Optionally, a corresponding RGB image is fed into the 2D branch at test time for multimodal (M3D) detection without retraining. The output is a scalar global anomaly score (combining view-averaged CLIP similarity and max point score) and a per-point anomaly score map, obtained by projecting 2D segmentations back to 3D via rendering correspondence with visibility masks and applying a Gaussian filter.

## Method

PointAD transfers CLIP's generalization to 3D by rendering point clouds into multi-view 2D images, encoding them with CLIP's frozen ViT-L/14 vision encoder, and projecting the resulting 2D global and local representations back into 3D point representations using rendering transformations and view-wise visibility masks. The key training mechanism is hybrid representation learning: a MIL-based 3D branch aggregates multi-view representations into point-level scores (with a computational trick that computes segmentation in the 24×24 feature space rather than the 336×336 point space, reducing complexity from O(Knd) to O(Khwd)), while an MTL-based 2D branch treats each view's anomaly recognition as a separate task. Both branches jointly optimize two object-agnostic learnable text prompts (for normality and abnormality) via a combined loss of cross-entropy (global) and Dice/Focal (local) terms, with all CLIP parameters frozen. For M3D detection, RGB images are simply fed to the 2D branch at inference and their representations are projected back to 3D for direct score fusion, enabled by the explicit 2D constraints learned during training.

## Results

On MVTec3D-AD (one-vs-rest ZS 3D), PointAD achieves 82.0% I-AUROC and 94.2% AP for global detection, and 95.5% P-AUROC and 84.4% AUPRO for local segmentation, substantially outperforming the next-best ZS baseline AnomalyCLIP (56.4% I-AUROC, 88.9% P-AUROC). With RGB integration (ZS M3D), performance further improves to 86.9% I-AUROC / 96.1% AP globally and 97.2% P-AUROC / 90.2% AUPRO locally. On Eyecandies and Real3D-AD, PointAD similarly leads all ZS baselines, e.g., 69.1% I-AUROC and 92.1% P-AUROC on Eyecandies, and 74.8% I-AUROC and 73.5% P-AUROC on Real3D-AD.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PointAD dominates all zero-shot baselines across all three datasets and both detection levels. On MVTec3D-AD global detection, PointAD (82.0% I-AUROC) surpasses AnomalyCLIP (56.4%), CLIP+Rendering (61.2%), Cheraghian (53.6%), and PointCLIP V2α (51.1%) by 20–31 points. For local segmentation, PointAD (95.5% P-AUROC, 84.4% AUPRO) improves over AnomalyCLIP (88.9%, 60.9%) by 6.6 and 23.5 points respectively, and over Cheraghian (88.2%, 57.0%) by 7.3 and 27.4 points. The object-agnostic prompt design (PointAD vs. PointAD-CoOp) consistently yields 1–4 point gains, confirming that blocking class semantics in text prompts benefits cross-object generalization. In the M3D setting, PointAD achieves uniform improvement over its 3D-only variant (e.g., 82.0→86.9% I-AUROC on MVTec3D-AD), whereas baselines like PointCLIP V2 suffer metric degradation when RGB is added, highlighting the importance of the hybrid 2D/3D training constraints for effective multimodal fusion. Compared to unsupervised SOTA methods (BTF, SDM, M3DM) that require target-specific training data, PointAD matches or exceeds them on several metrics (e.g., 95.5% P-AUROC vs. BTF's 97.6% is close, but PointAD achieves 82.0% I-AUROC vs. BTF's 76.3%) while operating in a fully zero-shot regime.

## Relation to Prior Work

PointAD extends the zero-shot anomaly detection line initiated by AnomalyCLIP (object-agnostic prompt learning for 2D images) into the 3D point cloud domain, while leveraging the multi-view rendering strategy popularized by PointCLIP/PointCLIP V2 for 3D-to-2D projection. It departs fundamentally from unsupervised 3D anomaly detection methods (BTF, M3DM, CPFM, SDM) that memorize target-specific normal features, instead transferring CLIP's cross-object generalization through a hybrid representation learning scheme that jointly optimizes text prompts via multi-instance learning (3D global/local) and multi-task learning (2D global/local). The plug-and-play RGB integration at test time is a conceptual departure from prior multimodal 3D methods that require separate RGB feature storage and joint training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As the first work to formalize zero-shot 3D anomaly detection, PointAD addresses a practically critical gap where target point clouds are unavailable due to privacy constraints or novel products, a scenario common in industrial inspection. The finding that explicit 2D glocal constraints are necessary for clean multimodal fusion (without them, RGB score maps become heavily noisy) reveals a non-obvious dependency between 3D and 2D representation quality in hybrid frameworks. Key limitations include fixed rendering angles (no adaptive view selection), sensitivity to point occlusions, and the direct averaging-based multimodal fusion that can fail when one modality is severely miscalibrated. Open questions include whether adaptive rendering strategies, learned fusion mechanisms, or larger auxiliary datasets could further close the gap to unsupervised SOTA methods that have access to target-specific training data.
