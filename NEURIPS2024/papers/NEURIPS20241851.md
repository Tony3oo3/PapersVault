## Problem

Existing uncertainty quantification methods for LLMs (log-likelihood thresholding, output entropy, ensembling) conflate epistemic and aleatoric uncertainty, making them unable to detect hallucinations in multi-answer queries where a correct model naturally exhibits high output entropy. This paper addresses the gap by deriving an information-theoretic metric that isolates epistemic uncertainty from aleatoric uncertainty, enabling reliable hallucination detection regardless of whether a query admits one or many valid answers.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text query and an LLM (Gemini 1.0 Pro) as input, iteratively prompting the model n times (typically n=2) by appending each previous response to the prompt, thereby constructing a pseudo-joint distribution over response tuples. From k samples (typically k=10) of this pseudo-joint distribution, it computes a mutual information score that lower-bounds the KL divergence between the LLM's distribution and the ground truth. The final output is a binary abstention decision: if the MI score exceeds a calibrated threshold λ, the system abstains (flagging a likely hallucination); otherwise it returns the LLM's response. Semantic equivalences are handled by clustering responses with F1 score ≥ 0.25 before computing the MI.

## Method

The core mechanism is iterative prompting: the LLM is asked for a response to a query, then asked again with the first response included in the prompt, and so on, constructing a pseudo-joint distribution via the chain rule of probability. The key theoretical result (Theorem 5.5) shows that under a ground-truth independence assumption, the KL divergence between the LLM's pseudo-joint distribution and the ground truth is lower-bounded by the mutual information of the pseudo-joint distribution, which is computable solely from the LLM's outputs without access to the ground truth. A finite-sample MI estimator (Algorithm 1) handles the potentially infinite support of language via stabilization parameters γ₁, γ₂, with non-asymptotic error bounds governed by the expected missing mass that decays rapidly for Zipf-like distributions typical of natural language. For hallucination detection, the MI score is thresholded at a value calibrated on hold-out data, yielding an abstention policy that flags high-epistemic-uncertainty responses while remaining insensitive to aleatoric uncertainty. The paper further provides a mechanistic explanation via a single self-attention head: when the query vector lies in the span of large principal components of the key-query matrix product, the model retrieves in-weight knowledge; otherwise, repeated context elements accumulate softmax weight and get copied, explaining why iterative prompting amplifies incorrect responses precisely when epistemic uncertainty is high.

## Results

The paper evaluates hallucination detection via precision-recall (PR) curves and recall/error-rate plots rather than a single numeric table. On predominantly single-label datasets (TriviaQA, AmbigQA), the proposed mutual information (M.I.) score performs nearly identically to the semantic entropy (S.E.) baseline, with both outperforming the greedy-probability (T0) and self-verification (S.V.) baselines. On mixed single- and multi-label datasets (TriviaQA+WordNet, AmbigQA+WordNet), M.I. significantly outperforms S.E.: on high-entropy queries (output entropy > 0.7), S.E. recall drops to near zero while M.I. maintains high recall with only a few mistakes. The S.V. baseline degrades substantially when recall falls below ~0.8.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the semantic entropy baseline (Kuhn et al., 2023), the proposed M.I. method ties on TriviaQA and AmbigQA (where most queries are single-label and the LLM has low output entropy), but clearly wins on the combined TriviaQA+WordNet and AmbigQA+WordNet datasets: on high-entropy multi-label queries, S.E. recall collapses to near zero while M.I. retains strong recall with comparable error rates, because M.I. is insensitive to aleatoric uncertainty while S.E. conflates it with epistemic uncertainty. Against the self-verification baseline (Kadavath et al., 2022), M.I. outperforms S.V. across all datasets, with the gap widening at lower recall values (below ~0.8) where S.V. degrades significantly. The T0 (greedy response probability) baseline is also consistently outperformed by both M.I. and S.E. on all datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the joint-distribution framework for decoupling epistemic and aleatoric uncertainty introduced by Wen et al. (2022) and Osband et al. (2023), but departs from their training-based approach (label pairs, ensembles) by constructing a pseudo-joint distribution purely through iterative prompting of a single LLM. The information-theoretic metric (KL divergence lower-bounded by mutual information) is inspired by the same independence principle, but the key novelty is making it computable without retraining or access to ground truth. The parallel work of Ahdritz et al. (2024) independently observes the same sensitivity-to-context phenomenon but uses entropy of conditioned outputs rather than mutual information, and lacks the mechanistic attention-head explanation provided here.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled, training-free method for hallucination detection that handles multi-answer queries where first-order methods (semantic entropy, log-likelihood) fundamentally fail, with a clean theoretical guarantee (MI lower-bounds the KL gap to ground truth). The mechanistic explanation via a single self-attention head (in-weight vs. in-context learning) offers interpretable insight into why LLMs are susceptible to prompt manipulation under high epistemic uncertainty. Limitations include reliance on Assumption 5.3 (ground-truth independence of responses), experiments on a single model family (Gemini), and the empirical advantage over semantic entropy being confined to mixed single/multi-label datasets. Open questions include whether the attention-head explanation generalizes across architectures and how the method performs in multi-turn or reasoning-heavy settings where responses are genuinely sequential rather than independent.
