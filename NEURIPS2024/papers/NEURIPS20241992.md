## Problem

Deep neural networks succeed through hierarchical feature learning, yet hyperparameters such as initialization scales and learning rates only provide indirect, poorly understood control over this process. There is no general, interpretable quantity that predicts the speed of feature learning at an arbitrary layer and training time, making it difficult to design principled HP scalings for deep architectures or to understand when feature learning arises versus lazy training. This paper identifies the backward-feature angle (BFA) between feature updates and the backward pass as the missing link connecting HP choices to feature-learning dynamics.

## Input / Output

The framework operates on any differentiable architecture expressed as a layer recursion, consuming per-layer backward pass vectors b_ℓ, gradient norms ‖∇_ℓ L‖₂, and learning rates η_ℓ at any training time (not just initialization). It produces the feature speed ‖ḟ_v‖ at each layer via the BFA θ_v, which is estimated from the spectral moments of the Backward to Feature Kernel (BFK) K_v — a psd matrix that maps a backward pass vector to the corresponding feature velocity. The practical output is explicit scaling rules for initialization variances and learning rates that simultaneously satisfy four dynamical properties: signal propagation, feature learning, loss decay, and balanced contributions.

## Method

The core result is the Feature Speed Formula (Thm 2.1), which expresses the magnitude of feature updates after one GD step as ‖ḟ_v‖₂ = Σ_{ℓ≤v} η_ℓ‖∇_ℓ L‖₂² / (cos θ_v · ‖b_v‖₂), valid at any training time in any architecture with an elementary chain-rule proof. The BFA θ_v is quantified via the spectrum of the BFK: when the backward pass is Gaussian and independent of K_v, cos θ_v converges to M₁/√M₂ (the ratio of first to square-root of second spectral moment), directly linking the angle to the conditioning of layer-to-layer Jacobians studied in the dynamical isometry literature. For ReLU MLPs at random initialization the BFA degenerates as cos θ_ℓ = Θ(ℓ^{-1/2}), while ResNets with branch scale O(L^{-1/2}) maintain a non-degenerate angle cos θ_ℓ = Θ(1). Enforcing the four dynamical properties yields explicit HP constraints that recover depth μP for ResNets and introduce a new FSC scaling for deep ReLU MLPs with output scale √L/m that avoids the vanishing loss decay of prior MF+μP scalings.

## Results

The paper's central quantitative result is the Feature Speed Formula (Thm 2.1), which expresses the magnitude of feature updates as ‖ḟ_v‖₂ = (Σ_{ℓ≤v} η_ℓ‖∇_ℓL‖₂²) / (cos(θ_v)·‖b_v‖₂), linking feature speed to the backward-feature angle (BFA), loss decay, and backward pass magnitude. For ReLU MLPs at random initialization, the BFA degenerates with depth as cos(θ_L) = Θ(L^{-1/2}), while for linear ResNets with branch scale β = O(1/√L), the BFA remains non-degenerate at cos(θ_L) = Θ(1). The key practical result (Prop 5.3) shows that among three HP scalings for deep MLPs, only the proposed FSC scaling (output init scale √(kL)/m, hidden LR 1/L²) simultaneously satisfies all four dynamical properties: signal propagation, feature learning, loss decay, and balanced contributions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three HP scalings for deep ReLU MLPs against four dynamical properties (SP, FL, LD, BC). NTK scaling [Jacot et al., 2018] satisfies signal propagation, balanced contributions, and loss decay, but fails feature learning: the sensitivity S_{L-1} = Θ(1/√m) → 0 as width grows, meaning features become infinitely lazy. MF+μP scaling [Jelassi et al., 2023] satisfies signal propagation, balanced contributions, and feature learning, but fails loss decay: the loss decay rate is Θ(L^{-1/2}), vanishing with depth. The proposed FSC scaling is the unique scaling (among the 6 degrees of freedom) that satisfies all four properties simultaneously, with sensitivity S_{L-1} = Θ(1) independent of both width and depth. For ResNets with β = Θ(L^{-1/2}), the FSC scaling (Table 2) coincides with the previously identified depth μP [Bordelon et al., 2023; Yang et al., 2023b], confirming that the non-degenerate BFA (cos θ = Θ(1)) is the key structural advantage of residual connections over plain MLPs at large depth.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the NTK alignment concept (Jacot et al., 2018) from the output layer to any layer via the backward-feature angle (BFA), and provides a concrete geometric justification for the previously heuristic link between dynamical isometry (Pennington et al., 2018) and successful training. It builds directly on the μP framework (Yang et al., 2021) and the depth-dependent μP analysis for ReLU MLPs (Jelassi et al., 2023), recovering these scalings as special cases of its more general feature speed formula. For ResNets, it leverages signal propagation and singular value concentration results (Marion and Chizat, 2024; Li et al., 2021) to show that the 1/√depth branch scaling maintains a non-degenerate BFA, in contrast to the Θ(1/√depth) degeneration in plain MLPs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The feature speed formula and BFA provide a unified, interpretable lens for designing and comparing HP scalings across architectures, reducing the problem to estimating a single geometric quantity (the angle) and the backward pass magnitude. The new FSC scaling for deep MLPs (output scale √depth/width) fixes the vanishing loss decay of MF+μP, and the framework recovers depth μP for ResNets in an elementary way. Key limitations include applicability only to (S)GD at 'cut nodes' (not inside ResNet blocks), batch-size 1, and the large width-then-depth asymptotic; the authors note that large-depth MLP analysis is 'mostly of theoretical interest' due to other degeneracies. Open questions include extending to larger batch sizes, understanding the BFA quantitatively beyond spectral moments, and removing the cut-node restriction.
