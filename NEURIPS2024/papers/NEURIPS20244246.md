## Problem

Existing RL performance metrics—regret, (δ,ε)-PAC, and uniform-PAC—only bound cumulative performance, permitting an algorithm to play arbitrarily bad policies at any finite round t. This is unacceptable in high-stakes settings like clinical trials where each patient assignment demands increasingly good treatments. The paper asks whether a stronger metric exists that simultaneously captures instantaneous per-round suboptimality and cumulative performance, and whether such a metric is optimally achievable.

## Input / Output

The algorithms take a decision set (K finite arms, a compact subset of R^d for linear bandits, or a tabular MDP with S states, A actions, horizon H), a confidence parameter δ, and for the infinite-armed linear bandit case an optimization oracle over the arm set. The output is a per-round arm or policy selection, with the guarantee that the instantaneous suboptimality Δ_t is bounded by F_ULI(δ,t) = Õ(t^{-1/2}) with probability ≥ 1−δ. Notably, for infinite arm spaces the algorithm only ever plays a finite barycentric spanner subset per phase, yet the guarantee holds uniformly over all arms in the compact set.

## Method

The paper introduces the uniform last-iterate (ULI) guarantee, showing it strictly implies uniform-PAC and high-probability regret bounds (Theorem 2.7), while proving a lower bound of Ω(t^{-1/2}) (Theorem 2.6). For finite arms, an elimination framework (Algorithm 1) is shown to yield near-optimal ULI for successive elimination, phased elimination, and phased elimination for linear bandits; a meta-algorithm extends this to any high-probability adversarial bandit algorithm. A negative result (Theorem 3.3) proves lil'UCB and all optimistic algorithms with log-log bonus functions cannot achieve near-optimal ULI, establishing ULI as strictly stronger than uniform-PAC. For infinite linear bandits, the key technical contribution is an adaptive barycentric spanner that, per phase, identifies a finite set of d arms linearly representing all active arms via polynomial oracle calls, enabling G-optimal design and elimination without discretization. For tabular episodic MDPs, the algorithm performs policy elimination over all deterministic policies, using uncertainty-driven reward functions (adapting UCB-VI's structure) to exhaustively explore the transition model and obtain uniform value-function estimates, achieving F_ULI(δ,t) = Õ(t^{-1/2}√(S³AH⁵)·log(tSAH/δ)).

## Results

The paper establishes that near-optimal ULI requires F_ULI(δ,t) = Ω(t^{-1/2}) (Theorem 2.6) and shows this is achievable by elimination-based algorithms: SE-MAB achieves F_ULI = Õ(t^{-1/2}√K), PE-MAB achieves F_ULI = Õ(t^{-1/2}√K), and PE-linear achieves F_ULI = Õ(t^{-1/2}√d). For infinite-armed linear bandits, the proposed adaptive barycentric spanner algorithm achieves F_ULI = O(t^{-1/2}√(d³ log(δ^{-1}dt))) with only Õ(d³ log_C d) oracle calls per phase. For tabular episodic MDPs, the policy-elimination algorithm achieves F_ULI = O(t^{-1/2}√(S³AH⁵) log(tSAH/δ)).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's central comparison is between three algorithm families in the finite-arm setting. Elimination-based algorithms (SE-MAB, PE-MAB, PE-linear) and high-probability adversarial algorithms (via a meta-algorithm) both achieve near-optimal ULI with Õ(t^{-1/2}) convergence. In contrast, optimistic algorithms provably fail: Theorem 3.3 shows lil'UCB incurs Δ_t = Ω(t^{-1/4}·polylog) on a two-armed instance, strictly worse than the t^{-1/2} rate of elimination methods. This establishes that ULI is strictly stronger than uniform-PAC, since lil'UCB is near-optimal uniform-PAC yet not near-optimal ULI. For infinite-armed linear bandits, the proposed algorithm matches the Õ(d^{3/2}√T) regret of prior work [Dani et al., 2008; Agrawal and Goyal, 2013] but suffers an extra √d factor over the Ω(d√T) lower bound due to the spanner technique.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the uniform-PAC framework of Dann et al. (2017), which unified regret and PAC bounds, by introducing a strictly stronger metric—uniform last-iterate (ULI)—that additionally constrains instantaneous per-round suboptimality. The algorithmic contributions build on phased elimination (Lattimore et al., 2020), the barycentric spanner technique (Awerbuch and Kleinberg, 2008, made adaptive here), and UCB-VI (Azar et al., 2017) for the MDP setting. A key departure from prior work is the negative result showing that optimistic algorithms (UCB, lil'UCB), despite being near-optimal for uniform-PAC, fundamentally cannot achieve near-optimal ULI, thereby establishing ULI as a strictly stronger guarantee than uniform-PAC.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ULI metric addresses a real gap in safety-critical applications (e.g., clinical trials) where an algorithm must not play a bad policy at any finite time, not just in aggregate. The negative result for optimistic algorithms is practically significant: it means the most widely deployed bandit/RL algorithm family cannot guarantee monotonic improvement in instantaneous quality, motivating the use of elimination-based alternatives in high-stakes settings. Open questions include whether ULI can be achieved with horizon-free dependence in MDPs, whether computationally efficient algorithms exist for linear MDPs with infinite state spaces, and whether the extra √d regret factor from the barycentric spanner can be eliminated.
