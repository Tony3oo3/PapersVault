## Problem

Existing evaluation metrics for automatic medical report generation are split into two disconnected families: NLG metrics (BLEU, METEOR, BERTScore) that measure textual overlap without clinical awareness, and clinical efficacy metrics (CheXpert, CheXbert) that check only a limited set of 14 pathology labels while ignoring location, severity, and other report details. This separation creates ambiguity when models rank differently across metric families, and both families show low correlation with radiologist judgments. The paper asks whether report similarity can be meaningfully measured by incorporating the corresponding X-ray image into the evaluation.

## Input / Output

The metric consumes three inputs simultaneously: a chest X-ray image, a ground-truth radiology report, and a generated (candidate) report. All three are encoded by a frozen pre-trained multi-modal embedding model (LIMITR) into a shared visual-textual space. The output is a single scalar VLScore in [0,1], where 1 indicates the generated report is indistinguishable from the ground-truth report with respect to the image, and 0 indicates maximal divergence. The constant C=890 for normalization is calibrated on the ReXVal dataset and is stated to be transferable across datasets within the chest X-ray domain.

## Method

The core mechanism projects the image, ground-truth report, and generated report into a joint embedding space using a pre-trained contrastive vision-language model (LIMITR), then computes the area of the triangle formed by the three resulting embedding vectors via the cross-product formula in high-dimensional space. A smaller triangle area means the generated report lies close to both the image and the reference report, indicating high fidelity. The score is normalized as VLScore = max(1 − T/C, 0), where T is the triangle area and C is a domain-specific constant. The key insight is that the contrastive-trained embedding space inherently encodes clinically discriminative features (pathology presence, anatomical location, severity) while down-weighting non-discriminative boilerplate text, so the geometric distance naturally captures what radiologists consider important. Ablations confirm that the triangle-area formulation outperforms alternatives like cosine similarity, image-centered cosine similarity, and minimal bounding sphere radius, and that LIMITR outperforms other embedding models (ConVIRT, BioViL, MedCLIP, GLoRIA) for this evaluation purpose.

## Results

On the ReXVal dataset (200 report pairs annotated by 6 board-certified radiologists), VLScore achieves a Kendall τ of 0.718 with radiologist judgment, substantially outperforming all prior metrics. The best NLG baseline (BERTScore) reaches τ=0.511, and the best CE baseline (RadGraph F1) reaches τ=0.515, while the weakest baseline (BLEU-4) scores only τ=0.345. On the perturbation dataset, VLScore also shows the highest sensitivity to clinically significant errors (Δ=0.15 for pathology sentence removal; Δ=0.19 for location word change) while remaining robust to non-informative modifications.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VLScore outperforms all seven baselines on the primary ReXVal correlation task, with a margin of +0.207 over the strongest NLG metric (BERTScore, τ=0.511) and +0.203 over the strongest CE metric (RadGraph F1, τ=0.515). Among NLG metrics, BERTScore leads (0.511), followed by ROUGE-L (0.476) and METEOR (0.465), while n-gram-based BLEU-4 is the weakest (0.345). Among CE metrics, RadGraph F1 (0.515) and CheXbert (0.499) lead, with CheXpert trailing at 0.463. On the perturbation experiments, VLScore uniquely penalizes clinically significant modifications (pathology removal, location/severity changes) while remaining stable on non-informative edits, whereas all NLG and CE baselines assign near-identical scores to significant and insignificant perturbations. The ablation further confirms that the triangle-area formulation (τ=0.718) outperforms alternative similarity measures in the same embedding space, including image-centered cosine similarity (0.678) and plain cosine similarity (0.555).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes multi-modal image-report embedding models (specifically LIMITR, a contrastive retrieval-oriented model) for a new purpose: evaluation rather than retrieval or classification. Prior evaluation work in medical report generation has operated in two disconnected silos—NLG metrics (BLEU, METEOR, BERTScore) measuring textual overlap and CE metrics (CheXpert, CheXbert, RadGraph F1) measuring pathology agreement—without leveraging the input image. The key conceptual departure is treating the image as a third anchor point in a shared embedding space, computing the triangle area among image, ground-truth, and generated report embeddings to capture both textual and clinical fidelity simultaneously.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The metric resolves the long-standing evaluation ambiguity where a model can rank higher on NLG but lower on CE (or vice versa), providing a single unified score that aligns 20% better with radiologist judgment than the best prior metric. The accompanying perturbation dataset is a practical contribution that exposes how blind existing metrics are to clinically critical single-word changes (e.g., left vs. right location). Open questions include whether the normalization constant C transfers across imaging modalities beyond chest X-rays, and whether the approach generalizes to report generation tasks where the image-report correspondence is less deterministic (e.g., multi-view or longitudinal studies).
