## Problem

Existing label integration and noise correction algorithms in crowdsourcing improve label quality but fall short on model quality because intractable instances with ambiguous attributes disproportionately harm the learning of classification rules. This paper identifies this gap and proposes a universal post-processing approach that directly targets model quality rather than label quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242721/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

IWBVT takes as input a crowdsourced dataset with instance attributes, multiple noisy labels per instance from R workers, and integrated labels ŷ_i produced by any upstream label integration or noise correction algorithm. It outputs a Naive Bayes classifier f and a set of Q linear regression models H = {h_1, ..., h_Q}, one per class, which are combined at prediction time via argmax(f(c_q|x) + h_q(x)) to produce the final class label. The method is designed as a plug-in post-processor, requiring no modification to the upstream algorithm.

## Method

IWBVT has two components. First, it introduces a novel instance weighting formula w_i = P(ŷ_i|L_i) · Ent(P̄_i)/log(Q−1), where P̄_i is the complementary set of the integrated label's probability within the full noisy label distribution P_i; this combines consensus strength with the entropy of non-consensus classes to robustly down-weight intractable instances, outperforming entropy-only and margin-based alternatives on complex multi-class distributions. Second, after training a weighted Naive Bayes classifier f, it constructs per-class probabilistic loss regression tasks where the target is 1−f(c_q|x_i) for the integrated class and −f(c_q|x_i) for others, and trains linear regression models h_q on these targets. At test time, the combined score f(c_q|x)+h_q(x) asymptotically maximizes the margin between the true class and competing classes, which the paper proves (Theorem 2) to achieve the bias-variance trade-off by ensuring Δ_iq > 0 for the correct class, thereby reducing generalization error.

## Results

On 34 simulated crowdsourced datasets, IWBVT improves the average model quality of all seven compared algorithms. The most striking result is that MV + IWBVT achieves 79.03% average model quality, exceeding the original (unimproved) model quality of every other algorithm, including the strongest noise correction method AVNC (78.57%). The largest absolute gains appear on datasets with high intractable-instance proportions, e.g., sick (46.48% → 93.88% for MV) and vehicle (43.54% → 67.98% for MV). On the two real-world datasets (Leaves, Income), IWBVT also consistently improves model quality across all seven baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IWBVT is applied as a post-processing step on top of each of the seven baselines and improves model quality in every case. Against the simplest baseline MV (75.45% ORI), IWBVT yields the largest relative gain (+3.58 pp to 79.03%), and the resulting 79.03% already surpasses the original quality of all six more sophisticated algorithms. Among label integration methods, IWMV (75.31% → 78.94%) and MNLDP (76.96% → 80.24%) show the most improvement, while LAWMV (78.00% → 80.22%) starts closest to the ceiling and gains a smaller +2.22 pp. Among noise correction methods, NWVNC benefits the most (+4.12 pp to 80.54%), followed by MVNC (+3.28 pp to 80.44%) and AVNC (+2.20 pp to 80.77%). In terms of statistical significance (paired two-tailed t-test, α = 0.05), IWBVT wins on 13–18 datasets and loses on 0–5 datasets depending on the baseline, with LAWMV and MNLDP showing zero significant losses. The consistent pattern across all seven baselines confirms IWBVT's role as a universal post-processing approach rather than one tied to a specific label integration or noise correction strategy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on two established lines of crowdsourcing research: label integration algorithms (MV, IWMV, LAWMV, MNLDP) that aggregate multiple noisy labels, and noise correction algorithms (AVNC, MVNC, NWVNC) that refine integrated labels. It departs from prior work by shifting the optimization objective from label quality (proportion of correct integrated labels) to model quality (proportion of correct test predictions), arguing that intractable instances with ambiguous attributes disproportionately harm model quality despite having minimal impact on label quality. The instance weighting component extends prior weighting schemes (probability-based, entropy-based, margin-based) with a complementary-set-and-entropy formulation, while the bias-variance trade-off component repurposes the classical Kohavi-Wolpert decomposition through a novel probabilistic loss regression mechanism.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical contribution is that IWBVT serves as a universal post-processing step that can be plugged into any existing label integration or noise correction pipeline without modifying the base algorithm, making it broadly applicable. However, the approach is validated only with Naive Bayes as the classification model and linear regression as the regression model, leaving open whether the bias-variance trade-off mechanism generalizes to more complex models such as neural networks or ensemble methods. The authors acknowledge that IWBVT degrades performance on datasets with anomalous numerical attributes (e.g., labor, lymph), and the limited robustness of linear regression to such features is identified as a key limitation for future work.
