## Problem

While calibration is a widely-studied statistical property of binary prediction models, the algorithmic problem of efficiently testing whether a distribution of prediction-outcome pairs is calibrated has remained underexplored. Existing approaches rely on black-box linear programming solvers that require Ω(n^ω) time (where ω > 2 is the matrix multiplication exponent), creating a quadratic runtime barrier. This paper initiates the algorithmic study of calibration testing from the property testing perspective, asking whether near-linear time algorithms exist for distinguishing perfectly calibrated distributions from those ε-far from calibration.

## Input / Output

The algorithm receives n i.i.d. draws (v_i, y_i) from a distribution D over [0,1]×{0,1}, where v_i ∈ [0,1] is a predicted probability and y_i ∈ {0,1} is the observed outcome. The v-coordinates are sorted without loss of generality. The primary output is a yes/no decision distinguishing D with LDTC = 0 from D with LDTC ≥ ε, with the information-theoretic threshold at ε = Θ(n^{-1/2}). A secondary output is the exact value of the empirical smooth calibration error, computed in O(n log²n) time, which serves as a constant-factor proxy for the lower distance to calibration.

## Method

The key insight is that the empirical smooth calibration LP, which enforces O(n²) Lipschitz constraints, can be reduced to a minimum-cost flow on a planar graph that is the union of a star and a path, by exploiting the triangle inequality to restrict constraints to adjacent sorted indices and introducing a dummy variable for box constraints. This flow problem is then solved exactly via a dynamic programming approach that recursively maintains partial solutions as convex piecewise-linear functions, with updates implemented through a custom segment tree data structure supporting range add, range set, and point query operations in O(log n) time, yielding an overall O(n log²n) runtime. For the tolerant calibration testing variant (where both yes and no thresholds are relaxed), the paper instead approximates the lower distance to calibration directly using an augmented Lagrangian framework: constraints are lifted into the objective as soft penalties, and a two-step combinatorial rounding procedure (first fixing (v,y) marginals, then calibrating u-coordinates) ensures the soft-constrained and hard-constrained programs have equal values, solved via box-simplex game algorithms from minimax optimization.

## Results

The proposed O(n log²(n)) dynamic programming solver (Corollary 1, optimized with PyPy) computes the empirical smooth calibration error in 2.05 seconds at n=2^15, compared to 245 seconds for the CVXPY LP solver and 3.19 seconds for the Gurobi minimum-cost flow solver. On the synthetic calibration testing task (Table 1), the smCE tester achieves the ground-truth threshold of ε=0.01 at n=2^10+1, while ConvECE requires ε=0.05 at the same sample size. On postprocessed DenseNet40 predictions on CIFAR-100, temperature scaling reduces empirical smCE from 0.2269 (base) to 0.1542, consistent with findings in Guo et al. (2017).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the CVXPY LP solver, the proposed PyPy-optimized solver is ~120× faster at n=2^15 (2.05s vs. 245s) and ~74× faster at n=2^14 (0.621s vs. 45.7s), with the gap widening superlinearly as n grows, consistent with the theoretical O(n log²(n)) vs. Ω(n^ω) complexity gap. Against the Gurobi minimum-cost flow solver (which exploits the same graph reformulation in Lemma 2), the proposed method is ~1.6× faster at n=2^15 (2.05s vs. 3.19s) and ~2.3× faster at n=2^14 (0.621s vs. 1.45s), though Gurobi remains competitive at smaller n (n≤2^11). In the calibration testing quality comparison (Table 1), the smCE and dCE testers both reach the ground-truth threshold ε=0.01 by n=2^10+1, while the ConvECE baseline from Blasiok & Nakkiran (2023) only reaches ε=0.05 at that sample size, demonstrating a clear statistical advantage of the dCE/smCE-based testing formulation over binned or convolved ECE variants.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on [BGHN23a], which introduced the lower distance to calibration (LDTC) and smooth calibration error (smCE) as principled, prediction-access-only calibration measures, and showed they are constant-factor related. It departs from the ECE-based calibration testing formulation of [LHHD23], arguing that ECE's discontinuity and binning sensitivity make it fundamentally unsuitable for finite-sample testing (indeed, [LHHD23] posed the choice of a better metric as an open question). Algorithmically, the key departure is from black-box LP solvers: the authors observe that the empirical smCE LP is secretly a minimum-cost flow on a planar graph (union of a star and a path), enabling a custom dynamic programming solver that bypasses the Ω(n^ω) matrix-multiplication barrier. For the LDTC, they adapt an augmented Lagrangian / rounding framework from combinatorial optimization [She13, JST19] combined with recent minimax optimization results [JT23].

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first rigorous algorithmic treatment of calibration testing as a property testing problem, providing information-theoretically optimal sample complexity with nearly-linear runtime—a significant improvement over the quadratic barrier imposed by general LP solvers. The structural observation (smCE LP = planar min-cost flow) is independently useful, opening the door to specialized graph algorithms for calibration measurement in practice. Open questions include: whether the constant-factor gap between smCE and LDTC (Lemma 13, B/A ≥ 3/2) can be closed for tighter tolerant testing, whether the DP approach extends to other calibration measures, and whether the sample complexity lower bounds for ECE-based testing (Appendix D) can be matched by improved algorithms. The practical impact is currently moderate—the PyPy-optimized solver shows ~120× speedup over CVXPY at n=32768, but the problem is niche relative to broader ML workloads.
