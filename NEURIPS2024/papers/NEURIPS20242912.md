## Problem

Coarse-to-fine point cloud registration methods propagate errors from unreliable coarse matches into fine-level correspondences, yet existing remedies either improve only coarse-level feature interactions (missing finer-scale local consistency) or filter outliers at the fine stage (failing under high outlier rates from severe noise or low overlap). Multi-scale consistency (MSC), which validates matches across multiple feature resolutions, has been widely used in image matching and retrieval but remains unexplored for the irregular geometry of point clouds.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pair of partially overlapping 3D point clouds (source and target) and produces a rigid transformation (rotation in SO(3) and translation in R³) estimated via RANSAC, weighted SVD, or LGR from refined correspondences. Internally, it first outputs a filtered set of reliable coarse superpoint correspondences validated by multi-scale consistency, which are then refined to dense point-level correspondences through Sinkhorn optimal transport on local patches. The backbone is KPConv-FPN, which provides a feature pyramid across decoder layers with varying point densities, and the method operates on the downsampled superpoint level for coarse matching before hierarchical exploration.

## Method

The core contribution is the Hierarchical Sinkhorn Tree (HST), which models multi-scale consistency for each coarse correspondence as a BFS traversal with pruning of a K-ary tree rooted at the superpoint, where child nodes are k-NN neighbors in successive decoder layers. At each node, a Patch Overlap Prediction (POP) module—combining borderless EdgeConv for local feature aggregation and cross-attention for cross-patch interaction—predicts overlap scores between patch pairs. An overlap-aware Sinkhorn Distance then computes patch similarity by restricting the optimal transport to only the top-k overlapping points (dynamic k) and initializing marginal distributions weighted by overlap scores rather than uniformly, with a dustbin row/column for unmatched points. The most likely overlapping matches from the Sinkhorn assignment are retained to continue exploration at the next finer scale, and consistency measures across all scales are aggregated via a geometrically decaying weighted sum. A probabilistic Softmax selection over the aggregated MSC scores yields the final reliable correspondence set, and the entire pipeline is trained end-to-end with overlap loss, overlap-aware matching loss, and overlap-aware circle loss across all decoder layers.

## Results

On 3DMatch (RANSAC, 5000 samples), HST achieves 93.5% Registration Recall (RR), 98.8% Feature Matching Recall (FMR), and 75.9% Inlier Ratio (IR), surpassing the closest competitor OIF (92.4% RR) by 1.1 pp. On the more challenging 3DLoMatch (low overlap), HST reaches 77.8% RR, 88.8% FMR, and 41.7% IR, outperforming OIF (76.1% RR) by 1.7 pp. On KITTI Odometry, HST achieves the best RTE of 6.3 cm with 99.8% RR. With the LGR estimator (no RANSAC), HST further improves to 93.2% RR on 3DMatch and 77.3% on 3DLoMatch, and with Iterative LGR reaches 94.4% and 79.9% respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HST outperforms all 16 compared baselines on both 3DMatch and 3DLoMatch in Registration Recall. On 3DMatch, it beats the strongest competitors OIF (92.4%) and GeoTR (92.0%) by 1.1 and 1.5 pp respectively; on 3DLoMatch, it surpasses OIF (76.1%) and GeoTR (75.0%) by 1.7 and 2.8 pp. Against outlier-rejection methods (GC-RANSAC, MAC, FastMAC) applied on top of GeoTR, HST achieves 93.2% vs. 91.9–92.2% RR on 3DMatch and 77.3% vs. 73.4–74.4% on 3DLoMatch, showing a particularly large gap (up to 3.9 pp) in the low-overlap setting where these methods degrade. On KITTI Odometry, HST matches the best RR (99.8%) while achieving the lowest RTE (6.3 cm vs. 6.5 cm for OIF/PEAL). On the extreme low-overlap 3DEXMatch dataset (<10% overlap), HST consistently outperforms GeoTR by 2.7–3.3 pp RR without fine-tuning and by 5.2–4.2 pp with fine-tuning. In the ablation (Table 6), replacing overlap-aware SD with vanilla SD drops RR from 93.5% to 90.4% on 3DMatch, confirming the contribution of the overlap filtering and initialization components.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the coarse-to-fine point cloud registration pipeline established by CoFiNet and GeoTransformer by inserting a multi-scale consistency (MSC) filtering stage between coarse and fine matching. It repurposes the Sinkhorn optimal transport framework from SuperGlue (adding a dustbin and cost-matrix formulation) and modifies it with an overlap-aware marginal prior and point filtering, while drawing on EdgeConv for local patch feature aggregation. Conceptually, it transplants the MSC idea from image matching and retrieval (where multi-scale feature consistency is well-studied) into the 3D point cloud domain for the first time, framing coarse correspondence validation as a BFS traversal over a hierarchical tree of local patches across the feature pyramid.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in coarse-to-fine registration: erroneous coarse matches propagate into fine-level outliers that degrade final transformation estimation, especially under low overlap and high noise. By filtering coarse correspondences via hierarchical local consistency before fine matching, HST reduces reliance on RANSAC and improves robustness across estimators (weighted SVD, LGR). Limitations noted by the authors include dependence on the initial coarse match quality in extreme cases and the purely geometric (no color/semantic) nature of the consistency measure. Open directions include integrating HST directly into coarse correspondence generation and incorporating multi-modal priors (normals, semantics) for richer neighborhood characterization.
