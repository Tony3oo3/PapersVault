## Problem

Classical lower bound techniques for passive statistical estimation (Fano, Le Cam, Assouad) and the Decision-Estimation Coefficient (DEC) framework for interactive decision making rely on fundamentally different analysis strategies, with no known unifying principle. The DEC approach leaves a multiplicative log|M| gap between its upper and lower bounds on minimax regret, and no necessary-and-sufficient condition existed for finite-time learnability of general structured bandit problems.

## Input / Output

The framework takes a model class M, an algorithm ALG, a risk function L, and a prior μ over models, and produces minimax lower bounds that are explicitly algorithm-dependent (the outcome distribution P^{M,ALG} depends on ALG's decisions), unlike classical methods that are oblivious to the algorithm. The key new output is the fractional covering number N_frac(M, Δ) = inf_p sup_M 1/p(π: L(M,π) ≤ Δ), which measures the best single-distribution coverage of Δ-optimal decisions and serves as a necessary and sufficient condition for finite-time learnability of any structured bandit problem.

## Method

The interactive Fano method (Theorem 1) generalizes classical Fano by introducing 'ghost data' X drawn from an arbitrary reference distribution Q independent of the true model, replacing the hard pairwise separation condition with a soft quantile ρ_{Δ,Q} = P_{M~μ, X~Q}(L(M,X) < Δ) that measures average separation under this ghost data. The lower bound uses f-divergence D_f(P^{M,ALG}, Q) rather than mutual information, making it tractable for interactive settings where information gain depends on the algorithm's own decisions over T rounds. With KL divergence and Q = E_{M~μ} P^{M,ALG}, it recovers the generalized Fano method; with TV distance, it recovers Le Cam's convex hull method and Assouad's lemma; and with Hellinger distance, it recovers the DEC-based lower bounds of Foster et al. via a new quantile DEC variant. The fractional covering number captures estimation complexity (how hard it is to identify near-optimal decisions), complementing the DEC which captures exploration complexity, and their combination closes the log|M| gap to a polynomial factor for convex model classes.

## Results

The paper's central quantitative contribution is a characterization of minimax sample complexity T*(M, Δ) for interactive decision making. For structured bandits, the fractional covering number N_frac(H, Δ) yields bounds log N_frac(H, 2Δ) ≲ T*(M_H, Δ) ≲ N_frac(H, Δ/2)/Δ² (Eq. 20), with the lower bound being logarithmic and the upper bound polynomial in N_frac. For general convex model classes satisfying mild regularity (Assumptions 2–3), the combined DEC + fractional covering number bounds (Theorem 15) give max{T_DEC(M,Δ), log N_frac(M,2Δ)/C_KL} ≲ T*(M,Δ) ≲ T_DEC(co(M),Δ)·log N_frac(M,Δ/2), closing the prior log|M| gap up to a quadratic factor. As a concrete corollary, the paper recovers the Ω(d/√T) PAC risk lower bound for d-dimensional linear bandits (Corollary 9), matching known minimax rates.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against empirical baselines (it is purely theoretical), but it positions its results against prior theoretical frameworks. Compared to the DEC framework of Foster et al. [40, 42], the fractional covering number adds the missing estimation-complexity term: for linear bandits, T_DEC scales as d/ε² while the true sample complexity is d²/ε², and the fractional covering number recovers the additional factor of d. For convex model classes, Theorem 15 replaces the log|M| factor in the upper bound with log N_frac(M,Δ), which is always ≤ log|M| and can be substantially smaller. Compared to Hanneke and Yang [45], whose maximin volume characterization applies only to noiseless binary-valued bandits, the fractional covering number extends the characterization to the noisy (Gaussian) setting and provides a necessary-and-sufficient condition for finite-time learnability of any structured bandit problem (Theorem 13). The interactive Fano method (Theorem 1) subsumes the classical Fano, Le Cam, and Assouad methods as special cases (Propositions 2–3), while also recovering the DEC-based lower bounds of Foster et al. [42] via the quantile DEC (Proposition 7), thereby unifying previously disjoint lower-bound techniques under a single framework.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies three classical statistical lower bound techniques (Fano's method, Le Cam's two-point/convex hull method, and Assouad's lemma) with the Decision-Estimation Coefficient (DEC) framework of Foster et al. [40, 42] for interactive decision making, which had previously appeared to use fundamentally different proof strategies. The key conceptual move is the 'interactive Fano method,' which replaces the classical hard separation condition with a soft, algorithm-dependent quantile condition via 'ghost data' from a reference distribution, and replaces mutual information with f-divergence to a reference distribution—thereby recovering the DEC's adversarial hard-instance construction as a special case. The fractional covering number, which coincides with the maximin volume of Hanneke and Yang [45] for noiseless bandits, is introduced as a new complexity measure capturing estimation difficulty that complements the DEC's exploration difficulty.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The fractional covering number provides the first necessary and sufficient condition for finite-time learnability of any structured bandit problem in the noisy setting, bypassing the undecidability barriers of Hanneke and Yang [45] that applied only to noiseless problems. For convex model classes, the paper closes the remaining log|M| gap between DEC upper and lower bounds (up to polynomial factors), which was a major open question in the DEC framework. However, a significant exponential gap remains between the lower bound (logarithmic in N_frac) and upper bound (polynomial in N_frac) for general model classes, and the moderate decay assumption on the DEC (Assumption 3) needed for the improved upper bound may not hold in all settings of interest.
