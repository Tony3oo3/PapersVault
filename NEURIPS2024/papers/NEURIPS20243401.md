## Problem

Conformal prediction provides distribution-free prediction intervals with only marginal coverage guarantees, while context-conditional validity is provably unattainable without distributional assumptions. Meanwhile, post-hoc model calibration (e.g., isotonic regression) improves point prediction reliability but is typically decoupled from interval construction. This paper addresses the gap by introducing a dual objective—simultaneously producing calibrated point predictions and prediction intervals whose coverage is valid conditional on those calibrated predictions—without requiring pre-specified binning or distributional assumptions.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a calibration dataset of n i.i.d. (X_i, Y_i) pairs, a black-box regression model f trained independently, a new context x, and a miscoverage level α ∈ (0,1). It outputs a Venn-Abers multi-prediction—a set of calibrated point predictions {f_n^(x,y)(x) : y ∈ Y} indexed by imputed outcomes—and a prediction interval {y ∈ Y : |y − f_n^(x,y)(x)| ≤ ρ_n^(x,y)(x)}. Because the outcome space is typically continuous, the multi-prediction is computed over a finite grid of Y with linear interpolation, and the set-valued interval is converted to a standard interval by taking its range with negligible efficiency loss.

## Method

SC-CP extends Venn-Abers calibration from binary classification to regression by iterating isotonic calibration over all imputed outcomes y ∈ Y: for each y, it applies isotonic regression to f on the augmented dataset C_n ∪ {(x,y)}, yielding a calibrated model f_n^(x,y) and a corresponding point prediction f_n^(x,y)(x). The collection of these predictions forms the Venn-Abers multi-prediction, which is guaranteed in finite samples to contain a perfectly calibrated prediction (Theorem 4.1). For each imputed y, self-calibrating conformity scores S_i^(x,y) = |Y_i − f_n^(x,y)(X_i)| are computed, and the 1−α empirical quantile is taken over scores sharing the same calibrated prediction value as x. The resulting interval inherits self-calibration from an oracle procedure: by construction, the SC-CP interval covers Y_{n+1} if and only if the oracle interval (using the true Y_{n+1}) covers it, thereby achieving prediction-conditional validity (Theorem 4.2) at a miscoverage deviation rate of polylog(n)/n^{2/3}. The key distinction from standard CP is that both the conditioning variable and the conformity scores depend on the calibrated predictions, which the paper's theoretical framework handles via first-order conditions of isotonic regression combined with exchangeability.

## Results

On the MEPS healthcare dataset (n=15,656, d=139), SC-CP achieves the target 90% coverage in both sensitive subgroups (A=0 non-white, A=1 white) while producing the narrowest prediction intervals among all methods, particularly in Setting A where the base XGBoost model is poorly calibrated. SC-CP also achieves the smallest calibration error difference between subgroups (e.g., −0.00376 on STAR vs. −0.00479 for Marginal/Mondrian/Kernel), indicating less discriminatory behavior. Across supplementary datasets (STAR, Bike, Community, Concrete, Bio), SC-CP consistently attains coverage at or above the nominal 1−α=0.9 level in both subgroups, whereas Marginal CP and CQR exhibit under- or over-coverage in at least one subgroup.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SC-CP outperforms Marginal CP [34] on both coverage accuracy and fairness: Marginal CP under-covers in A=0 (0.892 vs. target 0.90) and shows the largest calibration error gap between subgroups (−0.00479), while SC-CP achieves 0.905/0.919 coverage with a smaller gap (−0.00376). Against Mondrian CP [59], SC-CP wins on coverage robustness—Mondrian under-covers severely with 5 bins (0.887/0.882) and 99 bins (0.860/0.872), only matching target coverage when using the exact bin count data-adaptively selected by SC-CP. Versus CQR [48], SC-CP provides finite-sample prediction-conditional validity guarantees that CQR lacks; empirically, CQR under-covers in the A=1 subgroup (0.872 on STAR) while SC-CP maintains 0.919. Against Kernel-smoothed conditional CP [21], SC-CP achieves comparable or better coverage (0.905/0.919 vs. 0.887/0.891) with narrower intervals on MEPS and avoids the curse of dimensionality by operating in 1D prediction space rather than the full d-dimensional context. The key differentiator is that SC-CP is the only method simultaneously providing calibrated point predictions (Desideratum i) and prediction-conditional interval validity (Desideratum ii) with finite-sample guarantees, whereas all baselines sacrifice at least one of these properties.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate post-hoc techniques—Venn-Abers calibration (originally for binary classification, here extended to regression) and conformal prediction—into a single procedure that simultaneously delivers calibrated point predictions and prediction-conditional interval validity. It builds directly on the isotonic calibration framework of van der Laan et al. (2023) and the self-calibration concept of Vovk et al. (2003), while departing from standard CP and Mondrian CP by making the conformity scores and conditioning variable depend on the calibrated predictions rather than the raw model outputs. The key conceptual departure from prior work is the 'dual calibration objective' (self-calibration), which treats point calibration and interval validity as a joint finite-sample guarantee rather than two independent post-hoc steps.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first finite-sample guarantee that a single procedure yields both a perfectly calibrated point prediction (within a Venn-Abers set) and a prediction interval with valid coverage conditional on that prediction, avoiding the curse of dimensionality inherent in context-conditional validity. A practical limitation is that the prediction-conditional validity is with respect to an oracle point prediction that generally cannot be identified without knowing the true outcome, though the Venn-Abers set narrows rapidly with calibration set size. Open questions include extending the framework to non-regression tasks (e.g., conformal quantile regression), incorporating other conformity scores (normalized residuals), and understanding when prediction-conditional validity fails to approximate context-conditional validity in the absence of a mean-variance relationship.
