## Problem

Point cloud registration correspondences from feature matching are heavily contaminated with outliers, and existing robust methods face a trade-off: learning-based approaches lack cross-dataset generalization, geometry-only methods (SC²-PCR, MAC) fail under high outlier ratios or noise, and optimization-based methods (BnB, SDP relaxation) suffer from exponential worst-case complexity or introduce estimation bias. The paper addresses the gap of achieving both high robustness (up to 99% outliers) and computational efficiency simultaneously, without requiring training or global optimization.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a source point cloud P and target point cloud Q along with an initial correspondence set C generated by feature matching (FPFH, FCGF, 3DSmoothNet, or Predator descriptors). Correspondences are organized into N₁ local sets of N₂ points each via a second-order compatibility graph, and the algorithm outputs a rigid transformation (R ∈ SO(3), t ∈ ℝ³) plus the inlier correspondence indices for each local set. The best transformation hypothesis is selected by counting inliers within a threshold τ across all local sets, and the method is designed to handle correspondence counts ranging from 250 to 5000.

## Method

The core contribution reformulates registration as an alignment-error ℓ₀-minimization problem per local set, where the ℓ₀ norm counts non-zero error vectors (outliers) rather than penalizing their magnitude. A two-stage decoupling strategy resolves the non-convex problem: first, relative positions between point pairs cancel the translation, splitting the 6-DOF problem into independent 3-DOF rotation and translation ℓ₀ problems; second, null-space matrices (left null space of the relative-position matrix for rotation, and a matrix annihilating the ones-vector for translation) eliminate the transformation parameter from the constraint, decoupling inlier identification from parameter estimation. The resulting unconstrained problems are solved via a Bayesian MAP formulation with a Frobenius-norm relaxation of ℓ₀, yielding explicit closed-form solutions for the fitting errors. Top-K correspondences with minimal errors are identified as inliers, and rotation is estimated via SVD of the cross-product matrix over inlier pairs, while translation is computed from inlier point differences.

## Results

On KITTI (outdoor), the method achieves 99.56% registration recall (RR) with FPFH and 98.20% with FCGF, with the lowest rotation error (RE) of 0.34°/0.32° and translation error (TE) of 7.85 cm/20.73 cm. On 3DMatch (indoor), it reaches 95.07% RR with 3DSmoothNet (RE 1.75°, TE 5.97 cm) and 83.92% RR with FPFH. On the challenging 3DLoMatch (overlap <30%), it achieves 69.9% RR with 5000 correspondences, improving 7% over TR-DE. The method runs in 0.54 s on KITTI, competitive with TEASER++ (0.03 s) and SC2-PCR (0.31 s) while outperforming MAC (3.29 s) and GORE (orders of magnitude slower).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all 12 baselines on KITTI with FPFH, achieving 99.56% RR vs. SC²-PCR (99.46%), TEAR (99.10%), and PointDSC/VBReg (98.92%), with the lowest RE (0.34°) and TE (7.85 cm). On 3DMatch with 3DSmoothNet, it leads all methods with 95.07% RR (vs. SC²-PCR 94.82%, MAC 94.57%), and with FPFH it edges out SC²-PCR and MAC (83.92% vs. 83.90%). On 3DLoMatch (low overlap), it ties VBReg at 69.9% RR with 5000 correspondences but surpasses TR-DE by 7% (69.9% vs. 64.0%) and MAC by 0.5%. Against optimization-based baselines, FGR and RANSAC degrade sharply at high outlier ratios (>60%) while the proposed method remains robust up to 99% outliers. In terms of efficiency, the method (0.54 s) is ~10⁴× faster than GORE at 2500 correspondences and ~6× faster than MAC (3.29 s), while remaining competitive with TEASER++ (0.03 s) and SC²-PCR (0.31 s). Against EGST (Appendix), it reduces KITTI rotation error from 0.0168 to 0.0059 and 3DMatch rotation error from 0.2086 to 0.0305.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the compatibility-graph framework from SC2-PCR [6] for local set construction and the relative-position trick from TEASER [39] to decouple rotation from translation, but departs fundamentally by reformulating registration as an alignment-error ℓ0-minimization problem rather than a consensus-maximization or truncated-least-squares objective. The two-stage decoupling strategy—first separating 6-DOF into 3-DOF rotation and translation, then using null-space matrices to eliminate each parameter from the inlier-identification constraint—is a novel combination that has no direct precedent in the registration literature. The Bayesian MAP solution with Frobenius-norm convex relaxation of the ℓ0 penalty provides explicit closed-form solutions, contrasting with the iterative non-convex solvers (Geman-McClure in FGR, graduated non-convexity in TEASER++) or exponential-time BnB approaches used by prior optimization-based methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ℓ0 formulation offers a principled, training-free alternative to both learning-based outlier rejection (which lacks cross-dataset generalization) and geometry-only filtering (which degrades under high outlier ratios or noise), achieving robustness to 99% outliers with explicit solutions that are GPU-parallelizable. The modest but consistent improvements over strong baselines like SC2-PCR and MAC (1–2% RR on KITTI/3DMatch) suggest the ℓ0 perspective captures inlier structure that second-order compatibility scores miss, particularly in low-overlap (3DLoMatch) and high-noise settings. Open questions include scalability to very large correspondence sets (the O(N²) relative-position computation dominates runtime), performance on featureless or highly repetitive surfaces where the compatibility graph itself becomes unreliable, and whether the Bayesian regularization parameters can be made adaptive rather than grid-searched per dataset.
