## Problem

Overparameterized convolutional residual networks achieve remarkable empirical performance, but existing generalization theory either requires model size no larger than O(n) (rendering bounds vacuous for practical overparameterized models) or suffers from the curse of dimensionality where sample complexity scales exponentially with ambient dimension. No prior theory explains how overparameterized ConvResNeXts can simultaneously adapt to heterogeneous smoothness (Besov spaces) and exploit low-dimensional manifold structure to achieve minimax-optimal rates.

## Input / Output

The method consumes i.i.d. pairs (x_i, y_i) where x_i lies on a d-dimensional smooth Riemannian manifold M isometrically embedded in R^D (d << D) and y_i ∈ {0,1} follows a Bernoulli logistic model with logit f*(x). The input is padded to a D×w tensor for stride-one convolution, and the network outputs a scalar logit in [0,1]. The target f* is assumed to belong to the Besov space B_{p,q}^α(M) with norm bounded by C_F, allowing spatially heterogeneous smoothness that kernel methods cannot handle optimally.

## Method

The paper establishes that overparameterized ConvResNeXts (N residual blocks × M parallel paths × L convolutional layers each, with ReLU activations and weight decay regularization) achieve a near-minimax estimation rate Õ(n^{-α/d/(2α/d+1)}(1-o(1))) for Besov functions on low-dimensional manifolds, depending only on intrinsic dimension d rather than ambient D. The key mechanism is that weight decay implicitly enforces sparsity: by splitting building blocks into 'small' and 'large' norm groups, the covering number is determined only by the finite number of large blocks (≤ B_res/ε), making overparameterization harmless. The approximation theory decomposes the target Besov function via partition of unity into locally supported pieces, approximates each with cardinal B-splines, and shows each B-spline term can be realized by a single building block whose norm scales as |a_{i,k,s}|^{2/L}. A technical highlight is bounding the metric entropy using Dudley's chaining over the nested residual architecture, yielding tighter critical-radius bounds than single-radius covering arguments. The product MN ≳ n^{1/(2α/d+1)} suffices, and M and N are interchangeable, explaining why residual connections and parallel paths provide complementary architectural flexibility.

## Results

The main theoretical result (Corollary 3.4) establishes that overparameterized ConvResNeXts trained with weight decay achieve a near-minimax estimation rate of $\tilde{O}(n^{-\frac{\alpha/d}{2\alpha/d+1}(1-o(1))})$ for Besov functions on a d-dimensional manifold, where the rate depends only on the intrinsic dimension d rather than the ambient dimension D. In the numerical experiments (Appendix B), ConvResNeXt achieves the lowest MSE at a relatively smaller effective degree of freedom compared to all baselines (Figure 3), remains nearly dimension-independent as ambient dimension D increases while kernel methods (KRR, GP) deteriorate sharply (Figure 4), and exhibits a substantially faster convergence rate with sample size n than kernel-based methods (Figure 5), confirming the theoretical separation between neural and kernel approaches for Besov classes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares ConvResNeXt against six baselines on a simulated 1D-manifold-in-3D regression task: kernel ridge regression (KRR), Gaussian Processes (GP), XGBoost, Decision Tree, Lasso regression, and PNN (the parallel neural network from Zhang and Wang [43]). ConvResNeXt outperforms all methods on MSE at a given degree of freedom, with the most notable margins over KRR and GP. As ambient dimension D increases, KRR and GP degrade rapidly while ConvResNeXt and PNN remain nearly dimension-independent, demonstrating the advantage of representation learning for low-dimensional structure. The log-log convergence plot (Figure 5) shows a clear formal separation: ConvResNeXt converges at a rate consistent with the near-minimax Besov rate, while KRR and GP are suboptimal (lower-bounded by $n^{-(2\alpha-d)/(2\alpha)}$ per the theory in Appendix A). PNN (Zhang and Wang [43]) performs comparably to ConvResNeXt on this simple 1D manifold task, but the theory shows ConvResNeXt additionally provides architectural flexibility (trading off depth N and parallel paths M) that PNN lacks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper synthesizes two prior research threads: the overparameterized weight-decay analysis of Zhang and Wang [43] (limited to parallel FNNs and suffering from curse of dimensionality) and the manifold-based Besov classification theory of Liu et al. [25] (limited to non-overparameterized ConvResNets). It extends both to ConvResNeXts with parallel paths, proving that weight decay implicitly enforces block-level sparsity and that the convergence rate depends only on intrinsic manifold dimension d rather than ambient dimension D. The covering number bound via Dudley's chaining improves on the single-radius approach in prior work, and the B-spline approximation pipeline follows Suzuki [32] and Oono and Suzuki [30].

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first approximation and statistical theory for ConvResNeXts and overparameterized ConvResNets, offering a formal justification for why these architectures generalize well despite having far more parameters than samples. The formal separation from NTKs (which are provably suboptimal for Besov classes) gives a concrete theoretical reason why deep networks outperform kernel methods on heterogeneous-smoothness functions. Key limitations include the absence of end-to-end optimization analysis (assumes global minimizer), restriction to binary classification, and empirical validation limited to a 1D manifold simulation. Open questions include whether the sparsity mechanism and MN tradeoff insights transfer to optimization dynamics and multi-class settings.
