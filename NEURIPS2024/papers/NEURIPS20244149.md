## Problem

Despite LLMs' success on arithmetic, commonsense, and symbolic reasoning tasks, their application to combinatorial problems (CPs)—NP-hard optimization tasks critical to logistics and resource management—remains largely unexplored. Existing prompting strategies such as Chain-of-Thought, Decomposition, and Self-Refine produce inadequate solutions as CP instance sizes grow, and prior LLM-based CP work has been limited to small TSP instances with task-specific prompts. This paper asks whether a general-purpose, autonomous prompting strategy can effectively solve a broad range of CPs without hand-crafted exemplars.

## Input / Output

The method takes a natural-language description of a combinatorial problem (e.g., city coordinates and distances for TSP, job-machine processing times for JSP) as a single text query Q, with no task-specific few-shot exemplars required. It produces a structured combinatorial solution (a route permutation, an assignment matrix, or a job schedule) along with the corresponding optimized cost value. The approach is evaluated on six CPs at 5–30 elements (and up to 200 nodes in appendix experiments), using models with Code Interpreter access to execute generated Python for numerical computation.

## Method

Self-Guiding Exploration (SGE) is a five-phase prompting algorithm that operates as a general-purpose metaheuristic: (1) Exploration generates N candidate heuristic trajectories by prompting the LLM to list all applicable solving methods; (2) Decomposition breaks each trajectory into K sequential subtasks; (3) Subtask resolution executes each step, recursively invoking SGE itself when a subtask is judged too complex (via a yes/no check prompt); (4) Feedback and refinement applies a self-feedback loop to improve each subtask's output; (5) Integration merges the final thoughts from all N trajectories into a single answer. The key distinction from prior exploration/decomposition methods is that SGE uses only five fixed meta-prompts (Z_explore, Z_decomp, Z_check, Z_feedback, Z_integrate) rather than task-specific subqueries or exemplars, allowing it to autonomously select and combine appropriate heuristics—e.g., Hungarian algorithm for Assignment, Nearest Neighbor plus 2-opt for TSP—without any problem-specific prompt engineering.

## Results

On six combinatorial problem tasks (Assignment, Knapsack, Bin Packing, TSP, VRP, Job Scheduling) with GPT-4, SGE achieves 41.33%–75.33% average cost improvement over IO prompting, outperforming the second-best baseline (Decomposition) by 27.84% on average. With Gemini-1.5, SGE achieves 40.46%–68.09% improvement over IO. On reasoning benchmarks (AQUA, GSM8K, SVAMP, ASDiv, StrategyQA, CSQA, ARC, LastLetter), SGE reaches 89.60% average accuracy with GPT-4, exceeding the best baseline (Decomposition at 86.43%) by 2.46%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SGE consistently outperforms all four prompting baselines (CoT, Refine, Decomp, IO) across all six CP tasks and both LLM backbones. Against Decomposition (Zhou et al., ICLR 2023), the closest prompting competitor, SGE gains 27.84% additional improvement on average (e.g., 75.33% vs 38.20% on Job Scheduling with GPT-4). Against CoT (Kojima et al., NeurIPS 2022) and Self-Refine (Madaan et al., 2023), the margins are even larger, with SGE achieving roughly 5–6× the improvement of CoT on NP-hard tasks. On larger-scale JSP and VRP instances (n=50–200), SGE is competitive with Google OR-Tools (61.4–62.9% vs 61.3–63.1% on JSP; 59.6–60.1% vs 60.3–62.5% on VRP) but does not consistently surpass it. SGE also outperforms the Evolution of Heuristics (EoH, Liu et al., 2024) baseline on Job Scheduling by 1.3–5.0 percentage points across all tested configurations (e.g., 62.9% vs 59.6% at n=50, m=20).

## Relation to Prior Work

SGE synthesizes three established prompting strategies—exploration-of-thought (Tree of Thoughts, Yao et al. 2023), decomposition (Least-to-Most, Zhou et al. 2023), and self-refinement (Madaan et al. 2023)—into a single autonomous pipeline that requires no task-specific exemplars or manually designed subqueries. The key conceptual departure is framing the LLM as a metaheuristic: rather than being told which algorithm to apply, the model autonomously enumerates candidate heuristics (e.g., Hungarian, Nearest Neighbor, 2-opt), decomposes them into executable subtasks, and recursively refines them. This contrasts with prior LLM-for-CP work that either targets only TSP at small scales or generates code-based heuristics (EoH, Liu et al. 2024) rather than directly solving the problem through structured prompting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first systematic evaluation of LLM prompting across six distinct combinatorial problem classes (Assignment, Knapsack, Bin Packing, TSP, VRP, JSP) at multiple scales, filling a gap where prior work focused narrowly on TSP. The 27.84% improvement over the best baseline (Decomposition) is meaningful, but the method's practical impact is tempered by its 87.89% higher API-call cost and its continued large gap to optimal solutions (8–24% even at n=5). A notable open question is whether the performance gap to dedicated solvers (OR-Tools, LKH3) on larger instances (n≥100) can be closed, and whether the heavy reliance on Code Interpreter-equipped models (GPT-4, Gemini-1.5) limits the method's accessibility. The finding that Llama-2-7B and 70B perform nearly identically suggests that architectural capability (tool use) matters more than parameter scale for this task.
