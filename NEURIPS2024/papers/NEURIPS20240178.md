## Problem

Diffusion models with high classifier-free guidance (CFG) produce high-quality but low-diversity images because CFG sharpens the conditional distribution, causing mode collapse (e.g., 'a cat on a mat' always yields similar-looking cats). The paper identifies that standard diffusion models implicitly couple 'mode selection' with 'image variation' in a single denoising process, so CFG distorts both simultaneously. The goal is to explicitly decouple these two steps so that diversity is preserved while quality is maintained.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt or ImageNet class label as input and produces a 256×256 RGB image. Before image synthesis, an autoregressive model first generates a sequence of discrete latent tokens—detailed captions, detection bounding boxes, object blobs, or SEED visual tokens—that are human-readable and user-editable. The diffusion model then conditions on both the original prompt and these generated latents to produce the final image, with all experiments conducted at 256×256 resolution on ImageNet and CC12M.

## Method

Kaleido splits generation into two stages: an autoregressive prior (T5-XL decoder) predicts discrete latent tokens z from the condition c, and a latent-augmented diffusion model (NestedUNet from Matryoshka Diffusion Models) generates the image conditioned on both c and z. The key insight is that by sampling z before applying CFG, the mode-selection distribution p(z|c) is not distorted by guidance, while CFG still sharpens the image-variation distribution p(x|z,c), preserving diversity. Four types of latents are explored—textual descriptions, detection bounding boxes, object blobs, and SEED visual tokens—all pre-extracted using Qwen-VL and the SEED tokenizer and stored before training. Training jointly optimizes the AR decoder and diffusion denoiser with a frozen T5 encoder under a combined loss L = L_DM + η·L_AR, and the latents serve as an interpretable, editable interface that users can modify before the diffusion step to control specific image attributes.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256, Kaleido (Ours) achieves FID-50K of 9.0 and Recall of 0.42, substantially outperforming the MDM baseline (FID-50K 15.5, Recall 0.22) while maintaining high Precision (0.85). Combining Kaleido with CADS yields the best overall FID-50K of 5.9 and the highest Vendi(DiNOv2) score of 4.83. On diversity metrics evaluated over 1K×10 samples, Kaleido improves MSS(SSCD) from 0.21 (MDM) to 0.16 and Vendi(SSCD) from 8.42 to 8.82, with the combined Ours+CADS configuration reaching MSS 0.12 and Vendi(DiNOv2) 4.83.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the MDM baseline, Kaleido reduces FID-50K from 15.5 to 9.0 (a 42% improvement) and more than doubles Recall from 0.22 to 0.42, while retaining a high Precision of 0.85 (vs. 0.93 for MDM). Against MDM+CADS, Kaleido achieves a lower FID-50K (9.0 vs. 10.6) and substantially higher Precision (0.85 vs. 0.60), indicating better quality preservation, though CADS alone yields higher Recall (0.62 vs. 0.42) and lower MSS (0.12 vs. 0.16), reflecting greater raw diversity at the cost of image quality. The paper notes that Precision is an unreliable metric for diverse generators since a model producing only a narrow set of high-quality images can score artificially high. The two approaches are complementary: integrating CADS into Kaleido (Ours+CADS) achieves the best FID-50K (5.9), best MSS(SSCD) (0.12), and best Vendi(DiNOv2) (4.83), demonstrating that the autoregressive latent prior and condition-annealed sampling address different aspects of the diversity-quality tradeoff.

## Relation to Prior Work

Kaleido builds directly on Matryoshka Diffusion Models (MDM) as its base denoising architecture and on the classifier-free guidance (CFG) formulation, which the paper reinterprets as temperature-sharpening of the conditional distribution. The key conceptual departure is from prior work that augments diffusion with control signals (bounding boxes, layouts, segmentation masks) or from DALL-E 3's re-captioning: Kaleido instead introduces an autoregressive prior that explicitly models discrete 'mode selection' before diffusion handles 'image variation,' thereby decoupling the two so that CFG sharpens only the image-variation distribution while the mode distribution remains diverse. This is distinct from full autoregressive image generation (which predicts pixel-level tokens) and from sampling-time strategies like CADS, which anneal the conditioning signal rather than enriching it with learned latents.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a fundamental and widely felt tension in diffusion-based generation: high CFG improves fidelity but collapses diversity, a problem that limits practical deployment in creative and long-tail settings. By providing human-readable, editable latent tokens (text, bounding boxes, blobs, visual tokens), Kaleido adds an interpretable and controllable intermediate layer that is absent from standard pipelines, opening a path toward user-in-the-loop generation. Key limitations include roughly 3× slower training due to the T5 decoder's memory footprint, the need for external MLLMs (Qwen-VL, SAM, SEED) to construct training latents, and the open question of whether the approach scales to higher resolutions or benefits from richer latent types (depth, semantic maps). The finding that even purely color-cluster latents (no external knowledge) improve diversity suggests the mechanism is fundamentally about additional conditioning signal rather than semantic content, which is an underexplored direction.
