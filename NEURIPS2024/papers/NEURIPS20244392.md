## Problem

While Flow Matching models have become a prominent class of generative models, existing theoretical guarantees only cover deterministic interpolants. No convergence analysis existed for diffusion-type Flow Matching (DFM) models that use stochastic bridges such as the Brownian bridge, leaving the two main sources of error—drift-estimation and time-discretization—unquantified in the stochastic setting.

## Input / Output

The DFM model takes a sample from a base distribution μ, a coupling π between μ and the target ν*, and a neural-network drift estimator s_θ* trained via L² regression on the Brownian-bridge interpolant. Sampling proceeds by running the Euler-Maruyama scheme on the Markovian-projection SDE over a uniform partition of [0,1] with step size h=1/N_h, producing an approximate sample from ν*. The theoretical output is an explicit upper bound on KL(ν*‖ν₁^θ*) that decomposes into the drift-approximation error ε² and a discretization term of order h(h^{1/8}+1) times moment and score-integrability constants.

## Method

The generative process is the Markovian projection of the Brownian-bridge interpolant, whose drift (the 'mimicking drift') is identified as a conditional expectation and approximated by solving a regression problem. The KL divergence is bounded via a Girsanov-theorem decomposition, reducing the problem to controlling the L² norm of the time-variation of the mimicking drift over each Euler step. The key technical novelty is introducing the 'reciprocal characteristic' (∂_t + L_t^M)β̃_t—a mean-acceleration field of the drift—and bounding it through integration by parts in both time and space, a double change-of-measure argument with auxiliary measures ρ(s)^{-1} that mitigate singularities at t=0 and t=1, and symmetry properties of the heat kernel. This yields Theorem 2 (no early stopping, requiring L⁸ integrability of scores of μ, ν*, and π) and Theorem 3 (early stopping at 1−δ with independent coupling π=μ⊗ν*, relaxing the density requirement on ν* to only needing finite eighth-order moments). The early-stopping variant further converts the KL bound into a Fortet-Mourier distance guarantee via Corollary 1.

## Results

The paper establishes two main theoretical guarantees for Diffusion Flow Matching (DFM) using the Brownian bridge. Theorem 2 (non-early-stopping) shows that KL(ν* | ν₁^θ*) ≲ ε² + h(h^{1/8}+1)(d⁴ + m₈[μ] + m₈[ν*] + ‖∇log π̃‖²_{L⁸(π)} + ‖∇log μ‖²_{L⁸(μ)} + ‖∇log ν*‖²_{L⁸(ν*)}), where ε² is the L² drift-approximation error and h is the time step. Theorem 3 (early-stopping with δ = O(√ε)) relaxes the requirement on ν*'s score, needing only integrability of μ's score when π = μ⊗ν*, yielding a bound with δ^{-4} and δ^{-8} factors. Corollary 1 further shows the Fortet-Mourier distance W²_{2,FM}(ν*_{1-δ} | ν^θ*_{1-δ}) ≲ ε² under the same early-stopping regime.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no experimental baselines, so comparisons are made against existing theoretical results. Compared to [AVE22] and [BDD23] (deterministic FM), which require Lipschitz regularity on the velocity field estimator and ignore discretization error, this paper handles stochastic bridges and accounts for both drift-estimation and time-discretization errors without any Lipschitz assumption on the estimator. Compared to [LWCC24] and [GZ24] (Probability Flow ODEs), which assume L²-Jacobian-estimation error or Lipschitz/smooth score conditions, the present results require only L⁸ integrability of scores. Compared to [CDS23] (state-of-the-art for SGMs without early-stopping, requiring finite Fisher information), the DFM framework avoids the time-horizon trade-off inherent to SGMs and supports general base distributions μ. Compared to [GHJZ24] (early-stopping FM), which requires bounded support, strong log-concavity, or Gaussian-convolution structure on the data, this paper only needs finite 8th-order moments and L⁸ score integrability. The authors note their dimension dependence is d⁴ (vs. d² in [BDBDD23] for SGMs with exponentially decreasing steps), and flag improving this as open work.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Flow Matching (FM) framework of Albergo & Vanden-Eijnden (2022, 2023) from deterministic interpolants to the stochastic (diffusion) case, where the bridge is a Brownian bridge and the generative process is a Markovian projection. The analytical backbone borrows the Girsanov-based KL decomposition standard in score-based generative model (SGM) convergence analysis (Conforti et al. 2023, Chen et al. 2023), but introduces a new quantity—the reciprocal characteristic of the mimicking drift—that has no analogue in SGM theory and requires handling up to three logarithmic derivatives of conditional densities rather than two. Prior FM convergence results (Albergo & Vanden-Eijnden 2022, Benton et al. 2023) relied on Lipschitz regularity of the velocity field and ignored time-discretization error; this paper removes both requirements, matching the assumption level of the best SGM analyses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first non-asymptotic convergence guarantee for diffusion-type flow matching that accounts for all practical error sources (drift estimation and Euler-Maruyama discretization) under only moment and integrability conditions, making the DFM framework theoretically as well-grounded as SGMs. The introduction of the reciprocal characteristic as an analytical tool opens a new direction for studying non-Markovian-to-Markovian projections in generative modeling. Open questions include whether the L^8 integrability requirement can be lowered, whether a full statistical (finite-sample) analysis of the drift estimator is possible, and whether the dimension dependence can be improved in the early-stopping regime.
