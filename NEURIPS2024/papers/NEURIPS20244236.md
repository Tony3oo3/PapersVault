## Problem

3D Gaussian Splatting produces severe artifacts (ellipsoid-like and hollow regions) when trained on sparse input views, particularly in unbounded outdoor scenes where under-sampled areas lack sufficient geometric information. Existing enhancement approaches either depend on noisy auxiliary signals like depth and normals, or apply 2D diffusion priors that fail to maintain the strict multi-view consistency required for 3DGS training. This paper is the first to specifically address enhancing low-quality 3DGS rendering results as a standalone problem.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained 3DGS model (built from sparse input views, e.g., 3/6/9 images with camera poses) and renders a sequence of novel views along a camera trajectory fitted between reference views at 512×512 resolution. It outputs enhanced, view-consistent novel view images and a fine-tuned 3DGS model. The trajectory-free design means no explicit camera path is required at inference; the method interpolates between any pair of known reference views to generate intermediate enhanced views.

## Method

The key insight is reformulating the 3D view-consistency problem as temporal consistency in video generation, enabling the use of a pre-trained Stable Video Diffusion backbone. The pipeline consists of: (1) a video diffusion model conditioned on CLIP sequence features and VAE latent features via cross-attention and classifier-free guidance, where the first and last frames are high-quality reference views and intermediate frames are low-quality rendered views; (2) a spatial-temporal decoder (STD) with additional temporal convolutions, Controllable Feature Warping modules for preserving high-frequency patterns from rendered inputs, and reference-view-anchored color normalization; (3) confidence-aware 3DGS fine-tuning that weights the loss by image-level confidence (inverse distance to reference views) and pixel-level confidence (geometric mean of Gaussian scaling volumes rendered per pixel). The video diffusion model is supervised on a large-scale dataset of >150,000 low/high-quality image pairs generated by rendering from sparse-view and full-view 3DGS models across 130 DL3DV scenes.

## Results

On the DL3DV dataset, 3DGS-Enhancer achieves PSNR of 14.33, 16.94, and 18.50 dB with 3, 6, and 9 input views respectively, outperforming all baselines by 2.4–3.4 dB over the strongest competitor (3DGS) across all settings. On the cross-dataset Mip-NeRF 360 benchmark, the method reaches 13.96 dB (6 views) and 16.22 dB (9 views), again leading all baselines. The method also achieves the best SSIM (up to 0.630) and lowest LPIPS (down to 0.305) on DL3DV, indicating superior perceptual quality alongside pixel-level fidelity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

3DGS-Enhancer outperforms all five baselines on every metric (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS) across all three view-count settings on DL3DV. The largest absolute gain is over standard 3DGS: +3.36 dB PSNR at 3 views (14.33 vs. 10.97), +3.60 dB at 6 views (16.94 vs. 13.34), and +3.51 dB at 9 views (18.50 vs. 14.99). Among the NeRF-based baselines, RegNeRF is the strongest at 3 views (11.46 dB) but still trails 3DGS-Enhancer by 2.87 dB; FreeNeRF and Mip-NeRF are slightly weaker. DNGaussian, which uses depth regularization, achieves 11.10 dB at 3 views and 13.44 dB at 9 views, remaining 3.2–5.1 dB below 3DGS-Enhancer. On the cross-dataset Mip-NeRF 360 test, 3DGS-Enhancer also leads all baselines (13.96 dB at 6 views vs. 13.08 for Mip-NeRF; 16.22 dB at 9 views vs. 13.73 for RegNeRF), confirming generalizability beyond the training distribution.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on 3D Gaussian Splatting as the base representation and Stable Video Diffusion as the generative backbone, but its key conceptual departure is reformulating the 3D multi-view consistency problem as a temporal consistency problem within video generation. This distinguishes it from prior diffusion-prior NVS work (ReconFusion, DreamFusion/SDS) that applies 2D diffusion independently per view and struggles with cross-view coherence, and from geometric-regularization approaches (DNGaussian, RegNeRF) that rely on noisy depth or normal estimates. Unlike concurrent object-level video-diffusion 3D generators (V3D, SVD-MV, IM-3D), 3DGS-Enhancer operates as a post-hoc enhancement pipeline on any existing 3DGS model, targeting unbounded outdoor scenes rather than single-object generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes 3DGS enhancement as a distinct practical problem and provides the first dedicated pipeline and large-scale benchmark (150K+ image pairs on DL3DV) for it, which is valuable given that sparse-view 3DGS artifacts are a common failure mode in deployment. The video-consistency reformulation is an elegant and reusable conceptual bridge, but the method's reliance on adjacent-view trajectory interpolation limits applicability to single-view or highly non-adjacent settings. Open questions include whether confidence maps can be integrated directly into the video generation process (eliminating post-hoc reweighting), and whether the dataset-generation strategy can scale to truly massive 3D-consistent video corpora that teach 2D models 3D structure without explicit geometry.
