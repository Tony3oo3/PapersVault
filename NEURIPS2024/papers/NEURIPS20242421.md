## Problem

When a pretrained diffusion model serves as a prior p(x) and a black-box constraint r(x) (e.g., a classifier, reward model, or Q-function) defines the target, sampling from the posterior p(x)·r(x) is intractable due to the hierarchical, multi-step generative process. Existing approaches either introduce bias through linear or Monte Carlo approximations of the guidance term, or rely on RL-based fine-tuning that is prone to mode collapse and requires careful KL-regularization tuning. This paper provides the first asymptotically unbiased, data-free training objective for amortized posterior sampling under arbitrary diffusion priors.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pretrained diffusion prior (continuous DDPM/latent diffusion or discrete SEDD), a positive constraint function r(x) evaluated only at the final data point x₁ (need not be differentiable), and an optional conditioning variable (class label, text prompt, or state). It outputs a fine-tuned diffusion model that generates full samples from the posterior, with the constraint applied at the data endpoint rather than at intermediate noisy states. Experiments span 32×32 and 512×512 RGB images, token sequences for text infilling, and continuous action vectors for control, with the prior fine-tuned via LoRA (~3% of parameters) or full-parameter updates.

## Method

The core contribution is the Relative Trajectory Balance (RTB) objective, which enforces the constraint Z_φ · p_φ^post(x₀→…→x₁) = r(x₁) · p_θ(x₀→…→x₁) for every denoising trajectory, where p_θ is the frozen prior and p_φ^post is the posterior model being trained. The training loss is the squared log-ratio of the two sides, and Proposition 1 proves that satisfying this constraint guarantees the marginal p_φ^post(x₁) ∝ p_θ(x₁)·r(x₁). RTB is derived by reinterpreting diffusion models as continuous generative flow networks, showing it is a special case of trajectory balance under the prior's reference measure. A key practical advantage is that the gradient with respect to φ does not require backpropagation through the sampling process, enabling off-policy exploration (e.g., replay buffers, noising trajectories from high-reward samples) for improved mode coverage. The posterior drift is initialized as a copy of the prior and optionally parameterized with a Langevin inductive bias that injects ∇log r(x_t) at intermediate steps when r is differentiable.

## Results

On classifier-guided posterior sampling, RTB achieves the best FID on CIFAR-10 (0.4717 vs. 0.5316 for DPOK and 0.7371 for DPS) while maintaining high diversity (0.2440) and high expected log reward (−2.1625). On text infilling with a discrete diffusion prior, RTB outperforms the strongest autoregressive baseline (GFN fine-tuning) on all metrics: BERTScore 0.156 vs. 0.102, GLEU-4 0.045 vs. 0.031, and BLEU-4 0.025 vs. 0.019. On D4RL offline RL, RTB matches state-of-the-art across all nine locomotion tasks, e.g., achieving 100.40 on hopper-medium-replay (vs. 100.7 for D-QL) and 93.57 on walker2d-medium-replay (vs. 94.3 for D-QL).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against classifier-guidance baselines DPS [11] and LGD-MC [70], RTB achieves substantially higher expected log reward (e.g., −2.16 vs. −3.60 on CIFAR-10), indicating that these methods fail to properly model the posterior distribution despite maintaining high diversity. Against RL-based fine-tuning, DDPO [8] (no KL regularization) suffers severe mode collapse with diversity dropping to 0.13–0.16 and FID degrading to 1.58–1.80, while DPOK [16] (with KL) achieves competitive log reward but slightly worse FID (0.5316 vs. 0.4717 on CIFAR-10). On text infilling, RTB with a discrete diffusion prior outperforms the best autoregressive baseline (GFN fine-tuning from [29]) by 53% in BERTScore (0.156 vs. 0.102) and 45% in GLEU-4 (0.045 vs. 0.031), approaching the supervised fine-tuning upper bound (0.182 BERTScore). On D4RL offline RL, RTB matches or slightly trails the strongest baselines D-QL [83] and QGPO [44] on most tasks (within 1–2 reward points), but is competitive on the hardest medium-replay settings where the behavior prior is weakest, e.g., 100.40 on hopper-medium-replay vs. 100.7 for D-QL.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper derives the Relative Trajectory Balance (RTB) objective from the continuous GFlowNet framework (Lahlou et al., 2023), which itself generalizes the discrete Trajectory Balance constraint (Malkin et al., 2022) to diffusion models. The key conceptual departure from prior posterior-inference methods (DPS, LGD-MC, DPOK, DDPO) is that RTB provides an asymptotically unbiased training objective for sampling p(x)·r(x) under a diffusion prior, whereas existing approaches rely on linear approximations, Monte Carlo gradient estimates, or biased per-step KL penalties. The fine-tuning paradigm (initializing the posterior as a copy of the prior and using LoRA) is inspired by Hu et al. (2024) for autoregressive LLMs, and the Langevin inductive bias and off-policy exploration techniques are adapted from Zhang et al. (2022) and Sendera et al. (2024) respectively.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RTB provides the first principled, asymptotically unbiased training objective for amortized posterior sampling under diffusion priors, unifying a problem that has been addressed only approximately across vision, language, and control. The generality of the framework—applicable to any Markovian sequential generative process including discrete diffusion and autoregressive models—makes it a potential foundation for future work on compositional generative modeling and inverse problems. Key limitations include the simulation-based training cost (no local credit assignment, high gradient variance), the lack of error guarantees under imperfect prior fit or finite time discretization, and the open question of whether off-policy techniques (local search, replay buffers) can close the remaining gap to the biased-but-cheaper RL baselines in practice.
