## Problem

Despite significant interest in using offline RL to generate dynamic treatment regimes for longitudinal patient care, no standardized benchmark exists that captures the challenges of this setting—short episodes, partial observations, heterogeneous treatment effects, and sparse rewards. Additionally, the reliability of off-policy evaluation (OPE) methods, on which much of the medical RL literature depends for validation, has never been tested against ground-truth online performance in a longitudinal care simulation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20244148/cropped_page3_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

Each RL model receives as input the current symptom vector (d_o=8 dimensions in [0,1]), the full observation history of the current episode, and the last selected action, and outputs a discrete treatment choice from a set of n_a=16 actions. The environment is a POMDP where observations are noisy, quantized to one decimal place, and drawn from state-dependent multivariate Gaussians with overlapping distributions across hidden states, making state identification non-trivial. Performance is assessed via mean episode returns, adverse event rates (per 10,000 trials), remission rates, and mean time to remission across 8 procedurally generated environment instantiations.

## Method

EpiCare is a POMDP-based benchmark where disease progression is modeled as a sparse, action-modulated transition matrix over n_s=16 hidden states, with treatment-specific remission probabilities and adverse events triggered when any symptom exceeds a threshold. All POMDP parameters (transition structure, observation distributions, treatment effects) are pseudorandomly generated from an environment seed, defining a class of related environments that test generalizability. Three non-learned baseline policies—Oracle (greedy with full state access), Standard of Care (state-agnostic recency-weighted bandit with safety constraints), and SMART (Thompson-sampling-inspired weighted randomization)—serve as both data generators and performance baselines. The paper benchmarks six offline RL methods (CQL, DQN, IQL, TD3+BC, AWAC, EDAC) plus behavior cloning, and evaluates five OPE estimators (IS, WIS, PDIS, WPDIS, DM) by comparing their RMSE against true online returns. Key findings: value-based methods consistently outperform actor-critic methods in this discrete-action setting, CQL and IQL offer safety advantages over DQN via lower adverse event rates, and all OPE methods fail to reliably predict online performance except for policies closely matching the behavior policy.

## Results

Across 8 EpiCare environments (131,072 training episodes each), CQL achieves the best mean return of 78.0 (±0.9), followed by DQN (75.2) and IQL (72.5), all substantially outperforming the Standard of Care baseline (39.3) and the Oracle Policy upper bound of 95.2. In terms of safety, CQL and IQL achieve the lowest adverse event rates (20 and 22 per 10,000 trials respectively), both below SoC (31), while DQN's higher rate (25) reflects its known reward overestimation bias. All value-based methods (CQL, DQN, IQL) outperform all actor-critic methods (TD3+BC: 52.3, AWAC: 17.1, EDAC: 2.4) in mean return, consistent with the small discrete action space (16 treatments). In the data-restricted regime, DQN is the first model to surpass SoC at 2,048 patients, while below 256 patients its performance degrades below random.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CQL (Kumar 2020) achieves the best overall performance with a mean return of 78.0 and the lowest adverse event rate (20/10k), outperforming DQN (Mnih 2013) by 2.8 points in return while also being safer (20 vs. 25 adverse events). IQL (Kostrikov 2021) is competitive on returns (72.5) and matches CQL on safety (22 vs. 20 adverse events), but lags in the data-restricted regime due to sensitivity to training step count. TD3+BC (Fujimoto 2021) as a hybrid outperforms pure actor-critic methods (AWAC: 17.1, EDAC: 2.4) but remains well below value-based methods, achieving only 52.3 mean return. AWAC (Nair 2021) and EDAC (An 2021) both fail to meaningfully exceed the SoC baseline (39.3), with EDAC performing near-random levels. BC (Torabi 2018) is limited by its imitation objective, reaching only 18.5 mean return. Critically, all five OPE methods (IS, WIS, PDIS, WPDIS from Thomas 2015; DM from Guo 2022) fail to reliably predict online performance, with RMSE values ranging from 2.3 (BC, where OPE is trivially accurate) to 112.8 (PDIS on TD3+BC), indicating that current OPE techniques are inadequate for evaluating RL policies in longitudinal healthcare settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the POMDP framework for modeling partially observable sequential decision-making and the SMART clinical trial design for generating off-policy training data. It departs from prior medical RL simulations, which have focused almost exclusively on continuous drug dosage control (HIV, diabetes, Parkinson's), by introducing the first environment with discrete hidden disease states, heterogeneous treatment effects, and sparse state-transition graphs that mirror how clinicians actually model disease progression. The offline RL methods benchmarked (CQL, IQL, DQN, TD3+BC, AWAC, EDAC) are drawn from the general offline RL literature that has cited healthcare as motivation but has been evaluated only on non-medical benchmarks like D4RL.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that all five standard OPE methods fail to reliably predict RL performance in this setting calls into question the validity of much existing medical RL research that depends on OPE for pre-deployment validation. The data-restriction results show that while offline RL can outperform a standard-of-care baseline with ~131K episodes, this advantage vanishes at realistic clinical trial sizes (~2K patients), suggesting the field needs fundamentally new approaches for data-efficient medical RL. Open questions include whether better OPE estimators exist for short-episode, partially observable settings, and how to bridge the gap between simulation benchmarks and real clinical deployment.
