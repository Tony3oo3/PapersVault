## Problem

State-of-the-art universal OCO algorithms (MetaGrad, Maler, Zhang et al. 2022) maintain O(log T) experts, each requiring a separate projection onto the feasible domain per round, making them computationally expensive for complicated domains. While Mhammedi et al. [2019] reduced projections to 1 for exp-concave functions via black-box reduction, this approach provably fails for strongly convex functions. The paper asks: can we achieve 1 projection per round while simultaneously attaining minimax-optimal regret for general convex, exp-concave, and strongly convex functions?

## Input / Output

Each round, the algorithm receives the gradient ∇f_t(x_t) of the convex loss at the current decision and the convex feasible domain X ⊆ R^d (with bounded diameter D and bounded gradient norm G). It outputs a single decision x_t ∈ X obtained by one projection of an aggregated point from a simpler Euclidean ball domain Y. Notably, the gradient of the surrogate loss ∇g_t(y_t) needed for all expert updates is computed directly from x_t and y_t without any additional projection, so the total per-round cost is exactly one projection onto X.

## Method

The algorithm deploys O(log T) experts on a Euclidean ball Y ⊇ X, each minimizing a type-specific expert-loss (linear for convex, quadratic for exp-concave, and a novel strongly-convex loss involving ‖y − x_t‖² for strongly convex functions), and aggregates their predictions via the Adapt-ML-Prod meta-algorithm before performing the single projection onto X. The key technical insight is a regret decomposition into meta-regret (measured by the linearized surrogate loss) and expert-regret, where the strongly convex expert-loss is designed around the projected decision x_t rather than the intermediate y_t. This design, combined with two new properties of Cutkosky's surrogate loss (a tighter linearization gap and a sign condition on ⟨∇g_t(y_t), x_t − y_t⟩), allows the negative quadratic term from strong convexity to absorb the meta-regret's second-order bound, yielding O((1/λ) log T) regret. For smooth functions, the strongly convex expert-loss is enhanced with a gradient-norm-scaled quadratic term and paired with S²OGD to achieve small-loss regret O((1/λ) log L_T).

## Results

The paper achieves minimax-optimal regret bounds for three function classes simultaneously with only 1 projection per round: O(√T) for general convex, O(d/α log T) for α-exp-concave, and O(1/λ log T) for λ-strongly convex functions. Under smoothness (Assumption 3), the algorithm further attains small-loss regret of O(√L_T), O(d/α log L_T), and O(1/λ log L_T) for the three classes, respectively. All bounds match the best-known minimax rates, but the projection complexity is reduced from O(log T) (state-of-the-art) to 1 per round.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary advantage over all baselines is reducing projection complexity from O(log T) to 1 per round while maintaining identical regret guarantees. Compared to Zhang et al. [2022] and Wang et al. [2019] (Maler), which achieve O(√T), O(d log T), and O(log T) regret but require O(log T) projections per round, this work matches all three regret bounds with a single projection. Against Mhammedi et al. [2019], which already achieves 1 projection, this work strictly improves the strongly convex regret from O(d log T) to O(log T) by designing a novel expert-loss that exploits the surrogate loss structure. For the smooth/small-loss setting, the paper matches Wang et al. [2020b] and Zhang et al. [2022] on regret (O(√L_T), O(d log L_T), O(1/λ log L_T)) while reducing projections from O(log T) to 1. The key technical enabler is a new expert-loss for strongly convex functions (Eq. 26) that measures distance to the projected decision x_t rather than the unprojected y_t, combined with refined properties of the Cutkosky [2020] surrogate loss (Lemma 3) that allow the meta-regret to be controlled under strong convexity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the two-layer prediction-with-expert-advice framework for universal OCO (van Erven & Koolen 2016; Wang et al. 2019; Zhang et al. 2022) and the black-box reduction of Cutkosky & Orabona (2018) / Cutkosky (2020) that converts constrained optimization onto a simpler domain. Mhammedi et al. (2019) demonstrated that this reduction yields 1 projection per round for exp-concave functions, but the approach provably fails for strongly convex functions due to an unmanageable term in the regret decomposition. The key departure here is a uniquely designed expert-loss for strongly convex functions that measures distance to the projected decision x_t rather than the unprojected intermediate y_t, combined with refined properties of the Cutkosky (2020) surrogate loss (Lemma 3) that enable the meta-regret to be controlled under strong convexity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Reducing projections from O(log T) to 1 per round is practically significant when the feasible set has a complicated geometry (e.g., polytopes, simplex with many constraints), as each projection can dominate per-round computation. The paper also extends to small-loss regret under smoothness, matching the best known bounds with the same 1-projection guarantee. Open questions highlighted by the authors include extending to unbounded domains/gradients (parameter-free setting), achieving gradient-variation regret with reduced projections, and reducing projection complexity for adaptive regret, where current universal methods require O(log² T) projections per round.
