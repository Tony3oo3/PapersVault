## Problem

While prior work identified that DNN loss valleys can be asymmetric (flat on one side, sharp on the other), the causes of this asymmetry and its practical implications remained unexplored. This paper asks: what factors determine whether a valley is symmetric or asymmetric, and can this understanding be leveraged for model fusion and federated learning?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243688/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The analysis takes a converged DNN parameter vector θ_f and a noise direction ε, which is norm-scaled to ‖θ_f‖ (preserving direction) to produce 1D loss curves over λ ∈ [−1,1]. The key diagnostic is the sign-consistency ratio between ε and θ_f, computed element-wise. For the federated learning application, the input is a global model θ_t received from the server plus each client's private data, and the output is a locally updated model whose parameter signs are regularized to remain close to the global model's signs via a sigmoid-approximated sign penalty.

## Method

The paper systematically varies noise direction (7 common distributions plus 6 special directions) around a fixed converged model, discovering that the degree of sign consistency between noise and θ_f is the determining factor for valley asymmetry: L(θ_f + aη) < L(θ_f − aη) where η = |ε|·sign(θ_f). Theoretical insights attribute this to ReLU activation (sign-consistent perturbation preserves neuron activation with higher probability) and the softmax Hessian (sign-consistent noise drives the probability vector toward one-hot, reducing the Hessian trace and flattening the loss). These findings are then exploited in two applications: (1) explaining why model soups succeed—fine-tuned models retain high sign consistency with their pre-trained initialization, enabling barrier-free interpolation; and (2) proposing FedSign, a federated learning method that adds a sign-alignment regularization term γ·(sgp(θ_t)·σ(θ_t^k) + sgp(−θ_t)·σ(−θ_t^k)) to each client's loss, preventing local models from diverging in sign and improving aggregation under Non-I.I.D. data.

## Results

FedSign achieves the best aggregated accuracy across all six evaluation settings (two datasets × three Dirichlet α levels). On CIFAR-10, FedSign reaches 82.59% (α=10.0), 80.76% (α=1.0), and 78.41% (α=0.5). On CINIC-10, it achieves 77.05% (α=10.0), 74.59% (α=1.0), and 70.63% (α=0.5). The advantage over the strongest baseline grows with data heterogeneity: on CINIC-10 at α=0.5, FedSign outperforms the next-best method (FedPAN) by 0.49 points and FedAvg by 2.39 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedSign outperforms all five baselines (FedAvg, FedProx, MOON, FedDyn, FedPAN) in every one of the six dataset–heterogeneity settings. On the relatively I.I.D. setting (α=10.0), the margin is modest: FedSign leads MOON by 0.15 points on CIFAR-10 and FedPAN by 0.21 points on CINIC-10. As heterogeneity increases (α=1.0 and α=0.5), the gap widens substantially—on CINIC-10 at α=0.5, FedSign beats FedPAN (the strongest competitor) by 0.49 points and FedAvg by 2.39 points. MOON and FedDyn are consistently the weakest baselines, particularly under high heterogeneity. The paper also reports that FedSign's accuracy is stable across five independent runs (e.g., 82.59±0.09 on CIFAR-10 α=10.0), indicating low variance from the sign-regularization term.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the loss landscape visualization literature (Goodfellow & Vinyals 2015; Li et al. 2018; Im et al. 2016) by flipping the standard experimental design: instead of varying the converged point θ_f with fixed Gaussian noise, it fixes θ_f and systematically explores different noise directions. It builds directly on He et al. (2019), who first observed asymmetric valleys but offered no mechanistic explanation, and on the model soups literature (Wortsman et al. 2022; Yadav et al. 2023), which noted sign conflicts as a merging barrier without connecting it to valley geometry. The paper also positions itself against the federated learning regularization line (FedProx, MOON, FedDyn, FedPAN) by proposing a sign-based regularizer (FedSign) motivated by the valley asymmetry finding.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of sign consistency as the key determinant of valley asymmetry provides a unifying explanation for several previously disconnected observations: why model soups work (fine-tuning preserves parameter signs), why BN initialization affects landscape geometry, and why asymmetric noise distributions produce asymmetric valleys. The FedSign application offers a practical, lightweight regularization for federated learning that outperforms existing methods under Non-I.I.D. settings. However, the theoretical insights from ReLU and softmax remain informal (no formal proofs), the phenomenon is only verified on image classification tasks, and the authors themselves note the finding likely requires both ReLU and softmax to manifest, limiting generality to architectures like ViT with different activation patterns.
