## Problem

Despite the empirical success of Transformers, a rigorous theoretical understanding of their optimization dynamics remains lacking. Specifically, it is unclear which architectural configurations (kernel type, embedding size, initialization) allow vanilla gradient descent to reach global optima, and why Softmax attention can sometimes trap training at suboptimal local solutions. This paper bridges that gap by providing convergence guarantees and identifying the precise conditions under which different attention kernels behave differently.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242930/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a dataset of N samples, each consisting of n token embeddings arranged as an n×D matrix, paired with an n-dimensional target label vector. The output is a predicted n-dimensional label vector produced by a single-layer multi-head attention Transformer followed by a fixed output projection. Convergence guarantees require the embedding dimension D to scale as at least O(Nn/H) for the value-matrix-only update, or Dd ≥ Nn² when analyzing query/key extraction, reflecting an overparameterization regime.

## Method

The paper analyzes the loss landscape of a one-layer multi-head attention Transformer under both Softmax and Gaussian kernel attention, deriving closed-form gradients for all parameter blocks (W^Q, W^K, W^V). The key theoretical insight is that the value matrix W^V alone drives global convergence when B₀ (the stacked attention-weighted input matrix) has full rank, while the query/key matrices govern token-correlation extraction. For Gaussian attention, the PL condition is maintained throughout GD training, guaranteeing linear convergence even when only W^Q is updated; for Softmax attention, the PL condition can fail, yielding vanishing gradients at non-optimal stationary points. Empirically, the authors validate these findings on IMDb text classification and Pathfinder spatial reasoning tasks, showing that Gaussian-kernel Transformers converge faster, achieve higher accuracy, and exhibit flatter loss landscapes than their Softmax counterparts.

## Results

On the Pathfinder task, the worst test accuracy after 20,000 epochs is approximately 0.58 for the Softmax attention Transformer versus 0.62 for the Gaussian kernel Transformer, with Softmax also exhibiting significantly higher variance across 10 training runs. On both IMDb text classification and Pathfinder, Gaussian kernel attention achieves faster convergence and higher final test accuracy than Softmax attention under identical model size (D=64, d=128, H=2) and learning rate. Theoretically, the paper proves that with embedding dimension D ≥ O(Nn), gradient descent on a one-layer Transformer with either kernel can reach global optimality when updating W^V, and that Gaussian attention additionally guarantees global convergence when only W^Q is updated, whereas Softmax attention can exhibit vanishing gradients at non-optimal stationary points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares two attention kernel designs: standard Softmax attention (Vaswani et al., 2017) and Gaussian kernel attention (Chen et al., 2021 / Skyformer), using a 2-layer Transformer with identical architecture (D=64, d=128, H=2) on both IMDb text classification and Pathfinder. Gaussian kernel attention consistently outperforms Softmax on both tasks: it converges faster, reaches higher test accuracy, and produces a flatter optimization landscape with fewer local optima (visualized in Figure 4). On Pathfinder specifically, Softmax attention shows training instability with high variance in test accuracy across runs, while Gaussian attention remains stable. The theoretical analysis (Theorem 3) explains this gap: under the same initialization conditions, Gaussian attention satisfies the PL condition throughout training (guaranteeing linear convergence), whereas Softmax attention can fail the PL condition, leading to vanishing gradients at non-global-optimal points. The paper notes these empirical findings align with prior observations in Chen et al. (2021) and Tay et al. (2020) regarding Softmax attention instability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the overparameterized convergence analysis framework from fully-connected and ResNet settings (Allen-Zhu et al. 2019, Du et al. 2019, Nguyen and Mondelli 2020) to the Transformer architecture, where the non-convex Softmax attention kernel introduces fundamentally new challenges. It differentiates itself from Wu et al. (2024) by explicitly decomposing the roles of W^Q, W^K, and W^V in convergence, and from in-context learning convergence works (Huang et al. 2023, Zhang et al. 2023) by analyzing standard empirical loss minimization without prompt construction. The Gaussian kernel comparison draws on Skyformer (Chen et al. 2021) but provides the first theoretical convergence guarantee distinguishing the two kernel types under identical overparameterization conditions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clean theoretical explanation for a well-known practical observation: Softmax attention Transformers can exhibit training instability and slower convergence compared to alternatives. The formal demonstration that Softmax can produce vanishing-gradient stationary points while Gaussian attention avoids them under identical conditions gives practitioners a principled reason to consider kernel alternatives. However, the results require restrictive assumptions (HD ≥ Nn, specific initialization ensuring full-rank B_0, single-layer architecture), and the gap between these theoretical conditions and practical multi-layer Transformer training remains substantial. Open questions include whether the Softmax-specific failure mode persists in deeper architectures, whether practical initialization strategies can satisfy the full-rank condition without the large embedding dimension requirement, and whether the Gaussian kernel advantage holds at scale.
