## Problem

Modern optimizers like AdamW use momentum and adaptive learning rates to explore wide parameter spaces, which is beneficial for training from scratch but causes large deviations from pre-trained initializations during fine-tuning, degrading both in-distribution generalization and out-of-distribution robustness. Uniform regularization (L2-SP, standard weight decay) applied to all parameters creates a fundamental trade-off: strong penalties prevent fitting, while weak penalties fail to constrain deviation. The paper asks whether a simple, selective regularization can resolve this tension by constraining only the layers that are deviating inconsistently.

## Input / Output

SPD is an optimizer-level modification that does not alter the model's input or output format. It operates on the same inputs as the base architecture (RGB images at 224×224 for vision tasks, text sequences for language tasks, image+question pairs for VQA) and produces the same outputs (class labels, pixel-wise segmentation masks, or text answers). The key operational difference is that SPD maintains a copy of the pre-trained weights θ₀ in memory for full fine-tuning (regularizing ‖W_t − W₀‖₂), while for PEFT methods like LoRA or adapters it regularizes the deviation of new parameters from the origin ‖ΔW_t‖₂, eliminating the extra memory cost.

## Method

SPD modifies the Adam update with two selective mechanisms: (1) a per-layer condition c_t = −g_tᵀ(θ_{t−1} − θ₀) that triggers regularization only when the gradient direction is misaligned with the accumulated progress direction (c_t < 0), indicating inconsistent loss reduction; and (2) a deviation ratio r_t = max{0, γ_t − γ_{t−1}}/γ_t that scales the penalty strength based on how much the current deviation exceeds the previous one, derived from the equivalence between L2-SP and projection onto an L2 ball. The update becomes θ_t ← θ̃_t − λr_t(θ̃_t − θ₀), where λ is a single intuitive hyperparameter (recommended starting value 1). The condition is derived by treating λ as a trainable parameter in a hyper-optimization framework, and r_t is derived by equating the L2-SP update to a projection operation. For PEFT, θ₀ is replaced with 0, reducing SPD to selective weight decay on the low-rank adapter matrices without requiring a stored copy of the pre-trained model.

## Results

On ImageNet fine-tuning with CLIP ViT-Base, Adam-SPD achieves 84.21% ID accuracy and 50.13% average OOD accuracy across four ImageNet variants, outperforming L2-SP by 8.8% on OOD and FTP by 0.5%. On PASCAL-Context semantic segmentation with Swin-Tiny, Adam-SPD reaches 74.27% ID mIoU and 60.06% OOD average mIoU, improving over vanilla fine-tuning by 36.5% on OOD. For LLaMA-7B PEFT fine-tuning with LoRA on Commonsense-170K, SPD lifts average accuracy from 74.7% (AdamW) to 77.8%. On VQAv2 with PaliGemma-3B and LoRA, Adam-SPD achieves 87.39% ID accuracy and best OOD performance across both near and far OOD datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Adam-SPD consistently outperforms L2-SP [13] across all benchmarks: on ImageNet it improves OOD average from 46.06% to 50.13% (+8.8%), and on PASCAL-Context from 56.70% to 60.06% mIoU. Against the more complex FTP [11] (which uses hyper-optimization to learn per-layer projection radii), SPD achieves higher OOD on both ImageNet (50.13% vs 49.65%) and PASCAL-Context (60.06% vs 58.45%) while requiring only a few lines of code. SPD also beats LP-FT [19] on ImageNet OOD (50.13% vs 46.56%) and on VQA across all near and far OOD datasets. On PASCAL-Context, SPD surpasses TPGM [10] (60.06% vs 56.96% OOD), MARS-SP [12] (60.06% vs 43.59%), LLRD [48] (60.06% vs 54.47%), Adapter [9] (60.06% vs 56.50%), and BitFit [47] (60.06% vs 52.56%). The one case where SPD does not dominate is WiSE-FT [14] on ImageNet, which achieves higher OOD (55.58%) but at the cost of lower ID accuracy (80.94% vs 84.21%); the paper shows that combining both (WISE-SPD) yields the best overall result (56.48% OOD, 81.70% ID).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on L2-SP (Li et al., ICML 2018), which adds a uniform L2 penalty on the distance between fine-tuned and pre-trained parameters, and identifies its fundamental limitation: a single regularization strength cannot simultaneously prevent over-deviation and allow sufficient fitting. It departs from the more complex per-layer constraint learning approaches (TPGM, FTP) that use bilevel or hyper-optimization to learn layer-specific projection radii, instead deriving a simple per-layer selection condition from the hyper-optimization gradient of the regularization strength itself. The method also connects to PEFT literature (LoRA, adapters) by showing that SPD reduces to selective weight decay on the low-rank update matrices, eliminating the need to store a copy of the pre-trained model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SPD offers a practically attractive few-liner drop-in replacement for standard weight decay in fine-tuning pipelines, matching or exceeding the performance of more complex methods (TPGM, FTP) that require secondary optimizers and additional hyper-parameters. The key open question is whether the selectivity condition generalizes beyond the tested settings, particularly to pre-training or to domains where the foundation model has very limited exposure (as acknowledged in the Quickdraw limitation). The paper also leaves open whether the projection-based strength parameterization (deviation ratio r_t) is optimal or whether other geometric choices could further improve the fitting-regularization trade-off.
