## Problem

Generating K uniform Pareto-optimal solutions that faithfully represent the entire Pareto front is a longstanding challenge in multiobjective optimization, yet prior methods lack formal definitions of representability and uniformity. The natural metric—fill distance (the minimal covering radius of the PF)—involves a nested min-max-min optimization that is nearly intractable to solve directly. This paper bridges the gap by introducing a tractable surrogate (max-packing design) with provable rate-optimality guarantees and a practical algorithm to compute it.

## Input / Output

The method takes a multiobjective optimization problem (defined by its objective functions and decision space), a target number K of solutions, and preference angles in [0, π/2]^{m-1} as input. It produces K Pareto-optimal objective vectors on the PF that maximize the minimal pairwise Euclidean distance, along with a learned neural network model h_φ(ϑ) that maps preference angles to Pareto objectives. The preference angles are the actual decision variables being optimized (rather than weight vectors on the simplex), which simplifies the constraint structure to a box. The PF model is a fully-connected network (architecture (m-1)-128-128-128→m) trained on (ϑ, y) pairs collected during the evolutionary or gradient-based search.

## Method

UMOD formulates the problem of finding K uniform Pareto objectives as a max-packing design: maximizing the minimal pairwise distance among K points on the PF. The key theoretical insight is that this max-packing design is a rate-optimal surrogate for the intractable fill distance minimization, with the fill distance bounded within a factor of 4 of the optimum (Theorem 3). To solve the resulting bi-level optimization, the inner loop (solving the modified Tchebycheff aggregation for each preference angle) is approximated by a neural network trained on historical (ϑ, y) pairs, enabling efficient gradient computation via backpropagation rather than expensive finite differences. The outer loop updates preference angles to maximize the minimal pairwise distance, with gradients computed only for the closest pair (i*, j*). The overall algorithm alternates between (1) generating (ϑ, y) pairs via MOEA/D or gradient-based MOO and (2) updating preference angles using the learned PF model, initialized with Das-Dennis uniform preferences.

## Results

On biobjective benchmarks (ZDT1, RE21), UMOD achieves the best Spacing (0.12 vs. 1.91 for SMS-EMOA), Uniformity (2.07 vs. 1.85), Smooth Uniformity (0.77 vs. 0.74), and Fill Distance (1.04 vs. 1.16) among all 8 methods, while also attaining the lowest IGD (5.19). On the three-objective DTLZ2, UMOD leads in Spacing (0.52 vs. 2.64), Uniformity (3.17 vs. 2.45), and Fill Distance (2.37 vs. 2.59). On fairness classification (Adult, Compass), UMOD achieves the highest δ_Unif (0.1049 vs. 0.0421 for HVGrad on Adult; 0.3801 vs. 0.2199 on Compass) and the best Span (0.0773 vs. 0.0766 on Adult; 0.2156 vs. 0.2089 on Compass), confirming superior PF coverage and uniformity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UMOD decisively outperforms all seven MOEA baselines on uniformity metrics. Against MOEA/D [58] and MOEA/D-AWA [40], UMOD reduces Spacing from ~5.9 to 0.12 on ZDT1 and from ~3.5 to 0.12 on RE21, while improving Uniformity by 30–75%. SMS-EMOA [6] achieves the best HV (1.04 on ZDT1) but trails UMOD on every uniformity indicator (Spacing 1.91 vs. 0.12; Fill Distance 1.16 vs. 1.04), confirming that hypervolume maximization alone does not guarantee uniform coverage. NSGA3 [13] and Subset selection [9,45] are competitive on IGD but produce highly non-uniform distributions (Subset: Spacing 3.51, Uniform 0.89 on ZDT1). DEA-GNG [34] and LMPFE [51], the two most recent preference-adjustment and PF-estimation methods, are consistently outperformed by UMOD on Spacing, Uniformity, and Fill Distance across all problems. On fairness classification, UMOD surpasses HVGrad [17], EPO [36], PMGDA [59], and all aggregation-based methods (Agg-mTche, Agg-LS, Agg-PBI [37,58]) on δ_Unif and Span, with the largest gap over Agg-LS (δ_Unif 0.1049 vs. 0.0006 on Adult), while HVGrad remains the closest competitor on HV (0.6865 vs. 0.6800 on Adult).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the MOEA/D decomposition framework and the space-filling design literature (Pronzato, Auffray, Borodachov et al.) by importing the max-packing design concept into multiobjective optimization. It departs from prior preference adjustment methods (MOEA/D-AWA, DEA-GNG, MOEA/D-SOM) by replacing heuristic weight updates with a principled bi-level optimization that maximizes minimal pairwise distances, backed by a rate-optimality guarantee (4× the optimal fill distance). The use of a neural network surrogate to approximate the inner-loop Tchebycheff subproblem draws on bilevel optimization techniques, while the Das-Dennis initialization and MOEA/D outer loop anchor the method firmly in the evolutionary computation tradition.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to provide a rigorous theoretical characterization of 'uniform' Pareto front coverage in MOO, connecting fill distance, max-packing design, and asymptotic uniformity with clean constant-factor bounds. The empirical finding that maximizing pairwise distance simultaneously optimizes IGD (a widely-used but theoretically disconnected metric) suggests a deeper structural link worth further investigation. Open questions include whether the 4× constant is tight, how the approach scales to many-objective (m > 4) or high-dimensional decision spaces, and whether the PF model requirement limits applicability to problems where the front is highly non-smooth or discontinuous.
