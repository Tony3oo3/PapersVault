## Problem

While robot learning research has focused heavily on policy design, the observation space itself—RGB, RGB-D, or point cloud—has never been systematically compared under controlled conditions, leaving it unclear whether the choice of modality is a hidden bottleneck for performance and generalization. Existing literature lacks a unified framework with matched encoder sizes, identical training protocols, and consistent evaluation across modalities.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242472/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark consumes single-camera observations at 128×128 resolution in three modalities (RGB, 4-channel RGB-D, or point clouds carrying RGB color plus XYZ coordinates) alongside task-specific goals (language prompts for RLBench, goal positions for ManiSkill2). Point clouds are grid-sampled at 0.005 resolution, then reduced to 2048 seed points via FPS with KNN-16 clustering before feeding the policy. The policy outputs 7-DoF action sequences: ACT uses action chunking of 100 (ManiSkill2) or 20 (RLBench) steps with temporal ensembling, while Diffusion Policy uses a horizon of 16 with 8 action steps and 2 observation steps, employing 6D rotation representation for RLBench.

## Method

OBSBench standardizes pipelines across ManiSkill2 and RLBench (125 contact-rich tasks) by matching encoder parameter counts per modality—ResNet50/ViT-B for RGB, MultiViT for RGB-D, SpUNet34/PointNet for point clouds—and pairing each with two policy backbones (ACT, Diffusion Policy) under identical hyperparameters and no data augmentation. The central empirical finding is that point cloud methods achieve the highest mean success rate and best mean rank across all 19 evaluated tasks, outperforming the best RGB/RGB-D baseline by 53–77% under diffusion policy, even with the minimal 0.14M-parameter PointNet. Two critical design choices emerge: post-sampling (FPS on encoder feature maps) substantially outperforms the default pre-sampling used in prior work, and including both color and coordinate features is essential, with coordinate removal causing a larger performance drop than color removal. Additionally, the point cloud PVR PonderV2 achieves comparable or superior gains over 2D PVRs (R3M, VC-1, MultiMAE) despite using orders of magnitude less pre-training data (4.5K vs. millions of images), attributed to its multi-view neural rendering pretext task that imparts rich geometric knowledge.

## Results

Across 19 contact-rich manipulation tasks in ManiSkill2 and RLBench, point cloud encoders achieve the highest Mean Success Rate and best Mean Rank under both ACT and Diffusion Policy. With ACT, PointNet reaches 0.39 Mean S.R. (vs. 0.32 for ResNet50-RGB and 0.34 for ResNet50-RGB-D) and SpUNet reaches 0.37; with Diffusion Policy, PointNet reaches 0.23 (vs. 0.13 for ResNet50-RGB) and SpUNet reaches 0.20. Among pre-trained visual representations, PonderV2 (point cloud) achieves 0.43 Mean S.R. with ACT, outperforming R3M (0.32), VC-1 (0.34), and MultiMAE (0.33), while using only 4.5K pre-training samples versus millions for the 2D PVRs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Point cloud methods (PointNet, SpUNet) consistently outperform all RGB and RGB-D baselines across both policy architectures. With ACT, PointNet (0.39) beats ResNet50-RGB (0.32) by 7 points and ResNet50-RGB-D (0.34) by 5 points in Mean S.R.; with Diffusion Policy, PointNet (0.23) beats ResNet50-RGB (0.13) by 10 points. Among PVRs, PonderV2 (point cloud, 0.43 Mean S.R.) surpasses R3M (0.32), VC-1 (0.34), and MultiMAE (0.33) under ACT, despite using 3–4 orders of magnitude less pre-training data (4.5K vs. 1.28M–5.6M samples). Notably, adding depth to RGB (via channel-stacking or MultiViT) generally degrades performance relative to RGB alone, with MultiViT-B achieving only 0.18 (ACT) and 0.06 (Diffusion) Mean S.R. The paper also shows that point cloud methods exhibit substantially better zero-shot generalization to camera view shifts (5°–10°) and visual perturbations (lighting, noise, background color) compared to all image-based baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by introducing the first unified benchmark (OBSBench) for systematically comparing observation spaces in robot learning, a gap the authors identify as stemming from fragmented prior work where each method uses different simulators, encoders, and training protocols. It repurposes existing encoders (ResNet, ViT, PointNet, SpUNet) and policy architectures (ACT, Diffusion Policy) under standardized conditions, rather than proposing new architectures. The work departs from the dominant 2D-image-centric paradigm in robot learning (RT-1, RT-2, ACT, Diffusion Policy) and from complex 3D-aware methods (CLIPort, Perceiver-Actor, ACT3D) by showing that simple point cloud representations can match or exceed both, and it extends the PVR literature (R3M, VC-1, MultiMAE) by including the 3D PVR PonderV2 in a fair comparison.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that even the simplest point cloud encoder (PointNet, 0.14M params) consistently outperforms much larger RGB/RGB-D models across 19 tasks and two simulators provides practitioners with clear, actionable guidance for observation space selection, potentially redirecting research investment toward 3D representations. The counterintuitive result that adding depth to RGB degrades performance challenges a common assumption in the field. Key open questions remain: point cloud methods show no sample-efficiency advantage, real-world validation is limited to three tasks, and the paper acknowledges that large-scale 3D pre-training datasets (RH20T, DL3DV-10K) and dynamic sampling strategies could further close the gap. The benchmark itself (125 tasks, standardized pipelines, open-source code) is likely to become a reference point for future observation-space research.
