## Problem

Existing multi-modal 3D scene datasets are constrained to object-level understanding and inter-object spatial relationships, lacking hierarchical scene structures that capture region-level context and fine-grained grounding at multiple granularities. Prior annotation approaches rely on rule-based templates or free-form human labeling, producing either narrow coverage or annotator-biased descriptions, and none provide the comprehensive spatial and attribute understanding needed to train and evaluate 3D-LLMs on holistic scene comprehension.

## Input / Output

The dataset is built on 5k real-scanned 3D scenes (from ScanNet, MP3D, 3RScan, etc.) with 109k objects across 285 categories and 7,692 regions of 12 functional types. Annotations are structured hierarchically: region-level captions describe spatial layout, architectural elements, and object-region roles, while object-level captions cover appearance, material, size, condition, placement, functionality, and design. These meta-annotations (1.4M captions, 18.3M tokens) are post-processed into 1.28M visual grounding samples (requiring 3D oriented bounding box output) and 1.76M QA samples spanning five sub-classes (ST-attr, ST-space, OO-attr, OO-space, OR), plus 97k grounded scene captions with phrase-level token-to-object correspondence for training.

## Method

The core pipeline follows a top-down annotation logic that decomposes scenes into non-overlapping regions and objects, soliciting spatial and attribute descriptions at each granularity level. For object-level annotation, optimal 2D views are selected via Laplacian kernel quality filtering and 3D bounding box projection (center in central 25% area, maximizing surface point visibility), then cropped patches are fed to CogVLM and InternVL-Chat-V2 with structured prompts to initialize ~200-word captions, which human annotators refine through a custom UI. For region-level annotation, multi-view images with 3D bounding box overlays and unique object IDs are sent to GPT-4 in a multi-stage JSON-structured dialogue that separately extracts spatial relations, functional relationships, object particularity, and region features. A human-in-the-loop correction step with a 95% quality threshold (random 5-10% inspection per round) ensures accuracy while VLM initialization reduces annotation time by ~43% for objects and ~36% for regions compared to fully manual labeling. The resulting meta-annotations are converted into benchmark samples via ChatGPT-based template filling and rephrasing, and into grounded scene captions by summarizing object annotations, condensing local object groups with GPT-4, and integrating Sr3D spatial relations.

## Results

On the MMScan 3D QA benchmark, fine-tuned LLaVA-3D achieves the best overall GPT-4-evaluated accuracy of 57.4%, while zero-shot 3D-LLM leads at 28.6%. Fine-tuning with MMScan data yields up to 25.6% absolute accuracy gain (LEO: 22.2% → 47.8%) and 27% EM improvement. On the 3D visual grounding benchmark, the best model (EmbodiedScan) achieves only 10.49 AP25, far below the 48.1% SOTA on ScanRefer, highlighting the benchmark's difficulty. Training grounding models with MMScan grounded scene captions yields a 7.17% AP increase (37.27 → 44.44 AP25), and instruction tuning with MMScan captions achieves SOTA on ScanQA (24.7 EM@1) and SQA3D (54.1 EM@1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the QA benchmark, fine-tuned LLaVA-3D (57.4% overall) substantially outperforms fine-tuned LEO (47.8%) and LL3DA (38.5%), with the largest gaps on single-target attribute (64.9% vs. 55.5% vs. 40.4%) and inter-target spatial (56.2% vs. 45.6% vs. 47.1%) sub-tasks. Among zero-shot models, 3D-LLM (28.6%) and Chat3D-v2 (27.9%) lead, while LL3DA (15.8%) and LEO (22.2%) lag significantly, indicating that scene-level understanding requires fine-tuning on comprehensive data. On the visual grounding benchmark, EmbodiedScan (10.49 AP25) nearly doubles the next-best 3D-VisTA (5.24 AP25) and ViL3DRef (5.17 AP25), though all models remain far below the 48.1% SOTA on ScanRefer, confirming the benchmark's increased difficulty from complex multi-target prompts and 9-DoF box estimation. The paper further shows that using MMScan captions for instruction tuning improves LLaVA-3D from 23.1 to 24.7 EM@1 on ScanQA and from 51.6 to 54.1 on SQA3D, establishing SOTA over LEO (17.54/51.13) and LL3DA (16.83/45.35) on these traditional benchmarks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on EmbodiedScan (CVPR 2024) for its foundational 3D scanning data and object annotations, and draws on Grounded 3D-LLM for the grounded scene caption generation pipeline. It departs from prior multi-modal 3D datasets (ScanRefer, ReferIt3D, ScanQA, EmbodiedScan, SceneVerse) in two key ways: introducing a top-down hierarchical annotation logic that covers both region-level and object-level entities, and employing a human-in-the-loop VLM initialization strategy (using CogVLM, InternVL, and GPT-4) rather than purely template-based or fully automated annotation. The resulting dataset is the first to provide phrase-level grounding correspondence across multiple granularities (objects, regions, and their inter-relationships) within a single unified annotation framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MMScan's 6.9M hierarchical annotations and 114M tokens establish a new scale for multi-modal 3D scene understanding, and its benchmarks reveal that current 3D-LLMs and grounding models are far from handling complex prompts that entangle spatial and attribute understanding simultaneously. The finding that fine-tuning with MMScan data yields up to 25.6% accuracy gains on QA and 7.17% AP gains on grounding demonstrates the dataset's practical value for training. Key open questions include reducing the human correction burden (the pipeline still requires substantial manual annotation), scaling scene diversity beyond the 5.2k scenes inherited from EmbodiedScan, and closing the large gap between model and human performance (77.8% human vs. ~45% best model on QA). The scaling law analysis showing scene diversity matters more than sample diversity provides useful guidance for future data collection efforts.
