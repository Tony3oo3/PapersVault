## Problem

While in-context learning of large language models is empirically powerful, the statistical learning theory underpinning ICL remains incomplete. Prior analyses either restricted to single-layer linear attention on linear regression tasks or assumed infinite task and sample limits, leaving open whether multi-layer transformers with representation learning can achieve minimax optimal nonparametric estimation rates in context, and how pretraining task diversity affects the achievable rate.

## Input / Output

The transformer receives n context input-output pairs (x_k, y_k) drawn from a random regression task plus a query token x̃, and outputs a scalar prediction clipped to [-B, B]. Each input x ∈ ℝ^d (d may be infinite for sequential data) is first mapped through a DNN to an N-dimensional representation φ(x), and the linear attention layer then computes a weighted inner product of the query representation with context representations scaled by their response values. The model is pretrained on T such prompts from a family of nonparametric regression tasks defined over general function spaces (Besov, anisotropic Besov, piecewise γ-smooth).

## Method

The architecture consists of a deep ReLU network (depth O(log N), width O(1)) that maps inputs to N-dimensional basis approximations, followed by a single linear attention layer with a symmetric positive semi-definite matrix Γ. During pretraining on T regression tasks, the DNN learns to encode the relevant basis functions (e.g., B-spline wavelets) while the attention matrix learns the optimal shrinkage operator (Σ_Ψ + n^{-1}Σ_β^{-1})^{-1}. The key theoretical contribution is a risk decomposition into three terms: DNN approximation error (N^{-2α/d}), in-context generalization gap (N log N / n), and pretraining generalization gap (N² log N / T), showing that ICL achieves the minimax rate n^{-2α/(2α+d)} when T ≳ n^{(2α+2d)/(2α+d)}. The analysis further shows that when the task basis lives in a coarser function space, pretraining on diverse tasks lets the DNN encode informative representations that improve upon the a priori optimal rate, and information-theoretic lower bounds confirm joint optimality in (n, T).

## Results

The paper establishes that a transformer (DNN + linear attention) pretrained on T nonparametric regression tasks achieves the minimax optimal in-context estimation rate $n^{-2\alpha/(2\alpha+d)}$ in the Besov space $B_{p,q}^\alpha$ when $T \gtrsim n^{(2\alpha+2d)/(2\alpha+d)}$ and $N \asymp n^{d/(2\alpha+d)}$ (Theorem 4.5). In the anisotropic Besov space, ICL attains the nearly dimension-free rate $n^{-2\tilde{\alpha}/(2\tilde{\alpha}+1)}$ (Theorem 4.7). For sequential data in the piecewise $\gamma$-smooth class, the rate $n^{-2\alpha^\dagger/(2\alpha^\dagger+1)}$ is achieved independently of input length (Theorem 4.9). Information-theoretic lower bounds (Corollary 5.2) confirm joint optimality in both $n$ and $T$ in the large-$T$ regime.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is between the ICL rate and the classical minimax optimal rate for nonparametric regression (Donoho and Johnstone, 1998). In the standard Besov space setting, ICL exactly matches the minimax rate $n^{-2\alpha/(2\alpha+d)}$, confirming that in-context learning is statistically as efficient as the best possible estimator with $n$ samples. In the coarser basis setting (Section 4.4), ICL provably *exceeds* the a priori minimax rate $n^{-2\tau/(2\tau+d)}$ by achieving $n^{-2\alpha/(2\alpha+d)}$ when $T$ is sufficiently large, demonstrating that pretraining on diverse tasks encodes informative representations that reduce inference-time complexity. The information-theoretic lower bounds (Corollary 5.2) confirm that no meta-learning method can beat $n^{-2\alpha/(2\alpha+d)}$ in the Besov setting, and that insufficient pretraining ($T=O(1)$) in the coarser basis setting forces the worse rate $n^{-2\tau/(2\alpha+d)}$. A fundamental limitation is identified for $1 \leq p < 2$: ICL is bounded below by the Kolmogorov width $N^{-\alpha/d+(1/p-1/2)_+}$, which is strictly worse than the minimax rate, since ICL cannot adapt its representation at inference time.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the ICL theory of single-layer linear attention on linear regression tasks (Zhang et al. 2023; Wu et al. 2024) to a multi-layer architecture combining a deep ReLU network with one linear attention layer, pretrained on nonparametric regression in general function spaces. It builds directly on Kim and Suzuki (2024), who showed the DNN module learns true features during pretraining but only in the infinite task/sample limit, by providing finite-sample generalization bounds in both context length n and number of pretraining tasks T. The approximation analysis leverages Suzuki's (2019) minimax optimality of ReLU networks in Besov spaces and Schmidt-Hieber's (2020) generalization framework, while the lower bounds extend Yang and Barron's (1999) information-theoretic machinery to the meta-learning setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first statistical learning theory framework that rigorously connects pretraining task diversity to ICL performance, showing that ICL achieves minimax optimal rates when T is large (justifying ICL emergence at LLM scale) and provably improves upon a priori optimal rates by encoding informative basis representations during pretraining. The matching upper and lower bounds in the Besov setting confirm joint optimality in (n, T), while the coarser-basis analysis reveals a task diversity threshold consistent with empirical observations (Raventos et al. 2023). Key limitations include the restriction to a single linear attention layer, non-tight bounds in the coarser-basis regime, and the absence of training dynamics analysis for the DNN component, leaving open whether gradient-based optimization actually reaches the analyzed optimum.
