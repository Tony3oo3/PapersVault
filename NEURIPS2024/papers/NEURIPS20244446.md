## Problem

3D Gaussian Splatting achieves real-time rendering quality but suffers from severe over-parameterization: heuristic densification inflates SfM points to millions of Gaussians, requiring gigabyte-level storage per scene (e.g., 1.4 GB for Bicycle) and slowing rasterization. No prior method simultaneously reduces both the Gaussian count and per-Gaussian feature dimension while preserving specular reflections and view-dependent appearance.

## Input / Output

The method takes a fully trained 3D-GS model (millions of Gaussians with 3D positions, 3-degree SH coefficients, opacity, quaternion rotation, and 3D scale) plus the original training images and camera poses. It produces a compact 3D-GS file (~45 MB vs. 782 MB original) with pruned Gaussians, 2-degree SH, and partially quantized attributes, which renders novel views at 237 FPS on an A6000 GPU. The compressed model remains directly compatible with the standard 3D-GS rasterizer, requiring no architectural changes at inference time.

## Method

LightGaussian applies a three-stage compression pipeline. First, it computes a per-Gaussian global significance score (Eq. 3) that weights ray-hit count by opacity, accumulated transmittance, and a normalized volume term (capped at the 90th percentile to avoid over-pruning background Gaussians), then prunes the lowest-scoring Gaussians and recovers quality via 5,000 iterations of photometric fine-tuning on the survivors. Second, it distills 3-degree SH coefficients into a 2-degree student using a teacher-student loss on pseudo-views synthesized by Gaussian-perturbing training camera positions, preserving specular highlights that direct truncation would lose. Third, it applies vector quantization selectively to the SH coefficients of the least-significant 60% of Gaussians, using a K-means-initialized codebook (size 8192) with significance-weighted moving-average updates, followed by 5,000 iterations of differentiable VQ fine-tuning; all other attributes (position, rotation, scale, opacity, high-significance SH) are stored in FP16 to avoid the severe quality degradation observed when quantizing geometric attributes.

## Results

On Mip-NeRF 360, LightGaussian achieves 237 FPS with a 45 MB model (15× compression from 3D-GS's 782 MB), maintaining PSNR of 27.13 dB, SSIM of 0.806, and LPIPS of 0.237 — only a 0.27 dB PSNR drop and 0.007 SSIM drop relative to the uncompressed 3D-GS baseline (27.40 / 0.813). On Tank & Temple, it reaches 357 FPS at 25 MB with PSNR 23.44, SSIM 0.832, and LPIPS 0.202, nearly tripling the FPS of the retrained 3D-GS baseline (106 FPS) while reducing storage from 433 MB to 25 MB. On the synthetic Blender dataset, average storage drops from 52.4 MB to 7.8 MB with FPS improving from 310 to 411 and only a 0.99 dB PSNR decrease.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the uncompressed 3D-GS baseline, LightGaussian trades a small quality loss (−0.27 dB PSNR, −0.007 SSIM on Mip-NeRF 360) for a 17× size reduction (782→45 MB) and a 65% FPS increase (144→237). Compared to the closest compression baselines, LightGaussian outperforms Compressed 3D-GS [34] on FPS (237 vs. 152) and PSNR (27.13 vs. 27.03) while using slightly more storage (45 vs. 28 MB), and dominates Compact 3D-GS [37] on every metric (FPS 237 vs. 128, PSNR 27.13 vs. 27.08, SSIM 0.806 vs. 0.798, LPIPS 0.237 vs. 0.247) at comparable model size. Against MLP- and voxel-based NeRF methods (Mip-NeRF 360, INGP-Big, Plenoxels, VQ-DVGO), LightGaussian achieves orders-of-magnitude higher FPS (237 vs. 0.06–9.43) while matching or exceeding their PSNR; Mip-NeRF 360 retains a 0.56 dB PSNR edge but is impractical at 0.06 FPS. On Tank & Temple, LightGaussian nearly triples the FPS of retrained 3D-GS (357 vs. 106) and beats Compressed 3D-GS on FPS (357 vs. 202) at a modest 8 MB size increase, while the PSNR gap to 3D-GS narrows to 0.22 dB.

## Relation to Prior Work

LightGaussian builds directly on 3D Gaussian Splatting (Kerbl et al., 2023) as its base representation, applying a post-hoc compression pipeline that repurposes three established techniques—neural network pruning, knowledge distillation, and vector quantization—into the Gaussian splatting context. Its key conceptual contribution is the 'global significance' scoring criterion (combining ray hit count, opacity, transmittance, and normalized volume), which is derived from the splatting rendering equation and distinguishes it from concurrent compression efforts like Compressed 3D-GS (region-based VQ), Compact 3D-GS (learned binary masks), and VQ-DVGO (voxel-level quantization). The method also demonstrates transferability beyond 3D-GS by applying the same pruning logic to Scaffold-GS, positioning it as a general-purpose compression tool for point-based representations rather than a single-architecture solution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 15× storage reduction (782MB → 45MB) with negligible quality loss (SSIM drop of 0.007) and a simultaneous FPS increase (144 → 237) makes 3D-GS-based rendering viable for bandwidth-constrained and compute-limited deployment in VR, AR, and autonomous driving—domains the paper explicitly targets. A notable practical insight is that compression directly accelerates rendering (fewer Gaussians → faster rasterization), turning a storage optimization into a speed win. Open questions include whether the pipeline extends to dynamic scenes, whether zero-shot compression (without the 5,000-iteration co-adaptation and VQ fine-tuning) can close the remaining quality gap, and whether the approach generalizes to even more aggressive compression ratios beyond the ~70% pruning threshold where quality degrades sharply.
