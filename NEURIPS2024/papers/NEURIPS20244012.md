## Problem

Identifying the base model of an LLM after it has undergone fine-tuning, RLHF, or continued pretraining is difficult because parameter updates alter the model, and most popular LLMs do not release their weights. Existing watermarking methods are task-specific or vulnerable to paraphrasing and weight-rearrangement attacks, while post-hoc text detection methods degrade under subsequent training. The paper tackles how to uniquely and robustly identify a base LLM in a black-box setting without interfering with training or exposing parameters publicly.

## Input / Output

The method consumes the weight matrices (W_Q, W_K, W_V, W_O, W_1, W_2) from the last r Transformer layers of an LLM, combined with token embeddings of the K least-frequent tokens from a fixed standard corpus, to form invariant-term tensors M ∈ R^{K×K×C} (where C = 3r). These are processed entirely internally by the model owner; the only public outputs are a natural-image fingerprint (a dog photo generated by StyleGAN2) and a zero-knowledge proof attesting that the fingerprint was honestly derived from the claimed model. The fixed K ensures all invariant terms have identical dimensions across models of different sizes and vocabularies.

## Method

The core insight is that the parameter vector direction of a converged LLM remains stable through all subsequent training steps (SFT, RLHF, continued pretraining), making it both sufficient and necessary for base-model identification. To defend against weight-rearrangement attacks (linear mappings on attention projections, FFN hidden-unit permutations, and embedding-dimension permutations), the authors derive three invariant terms M_a = X̂W_QW_K^T X̂^T, M_b = X̂W_VW_O X̂^T, and M_f = X̂W_1W_2 X̂^T that cancel out all such transformations. A CNN encoder, trained with contrastive learning for locality preservation and a GAN objective for Gaussian output, maps the tiled invariant-term tensor to a 512-dim latent vector, which is then decoded by a pretrained StyleGAN2 (AFHQ-dog) into a human-readable fingerprint image. A zero-knowledge proof with cryptographic commitment guarantees the fingerprint corresponds to the claimed model without revealing any parameter values.

## Results

The method achieves an average ICS of 99.56 between 7 base LLMs (7B–40B) and their offspring models, and 94.14 across 14 LLaMA-7B offspring models undergoing diverse training (SFT, RLHF, LoRA, continued pretraining, modality extension). Between 28 independently trained LLMs (774M–180B), the average ICS drops to 0.38, confirming strong discriminability. The method correctly identifies the base model for all 51 offspring models (100% accuracy), and a human subject study with 72 participants yields 94.74% accuracy in matching offspring fingerprints to their base models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two recent LLM fingerprinting methods: Trap (Gubri et al., 2024), which uses adversarial suffixes to elicit specific responses, and IF (Xu et al., 2024), which fine-tunes LLMs to generate predefined answers. On the FSR metric across 13 LLaMA-7B offspring models, HuRef achieves a perfect 100% average, decisively outperforming Trap (11.94%) and both IF variants (IF¹_adapter at 5.38% using all parameters, IF²_adapter at 84.62% using only the embedding parameter). Notably, HuRef is a black-box method requiring no model modification, whereas IF requires fine-tuning the target model and Trap requires access to model outputs. Even the strongest baseline (IF²_adapter) fails on 2 of 13 models (AlpacaL at 20%, Koala at 50%, Guanaco at 30%), while HuRef correctly identifies all offspring models regardless of the type of subsequent training applied.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves out a new category of LLM protection distinct from both post-hoc text detection (LLMDet, DetectGPT) and traditional watermarking (trigger-based, output-behavior-based). It repurposes the well-studied concept of model fingerprinting/ownership verification for DNNs by deriving architecture-specific invariant terms from the Transformer structure that are robust to weight rearrangement attacks, then combining them with cryptographic commitments and zero-knowledge proofs to enable black-box verification. The image-generation pipeline (CNN encoder + StyleGAN2) borrows from generative modeling to produce human-readable outputs, while the contrastive-learning training of the encoder is a standard technique applied here to a locality-preserving mapping problem.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As LLMs become increasingly valuable and are widely fine-tuned for downstream tasks, a practical mechanism to verify model provenance without exposing weights is a pressing need for both open-source and proprietary model providers. The paper's limitation to Transformer architectures and its reliance on the model owner's cooperation (all fingerprinting steps are internal) constrain immediate adoption, and the ZKP overhead for large models remains an open engineering question. Whether the invariant terms remain stable under more aggressive modifications (e.g., full continued pretraining on substantially different data distributions, or architectural modifications like adding layers) is not fully explored, leaving room for future robustness studies.
