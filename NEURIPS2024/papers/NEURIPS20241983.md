limitations: The authors explicitly note that the deeper causal mechanism behind why specific category labels may favor certain modalities remains unexplored and is left as future work. The method also requires tuning the contrastive loss temperature and batch size, and the bi-level optimization introduces an inner-outer loop structure that increases training overhead relative to single-objective baselines.

surprise_factor: The ablation showing that adding a contrastive loss alone (without any dynamic weighting) collapses the audio-video MAP gap on KineticsSounds from 21.63% to 3.80% is a striking and somewhat counterintuitive result, suggesting that the modality imbalance is largely an artifact of label-fitting dynamics rather than an inherent property of the modalities. ## Problem

Multimodal learning suffers from modality imbalance, where dominant modalities overshadow non-dominant ones during joint training, causing multimodal models to underperform even unimodal baselines. Prior work addressed the symptom of inconsistent convergence speeds but did not identify the root cause. This paper shows that fitting one-hot category labels is the core driver of the imbalance, as it causes modalities to learn at different rates, and demonstrates that label-free (uniform) supervision narrows the inter-modality performance gap.

## Input / Output

The method accepts heterogeneous multimodal pairs or triplets: audio spectrograms (257×1004 or 257×299) paired with video clips (10 frames uniformly sampled to 3), 224×224 RGB images paired with text sequences (max 128 tokens), or RGB + depth + optical-flow triplets. Encoders are ResNet18 (audio/video), ResNet50 + BERT (image/text), or CLIP pre-trained encoders for robustness experiments. The output is a single categorical class label per sample, scored via a fully-connected head over the fused representation.

## Method

The core mechanism is a modality matching loss L_MM (a symmetric InfoNCE-style contrastive objective over cross-modal feature pairs) that is dynamically blended with the standard cross-entropy classification loss L_CLS via a weight α: L_Total = (1−α)L_CLS + αL_MM. Two strategies control α over training: (1) a heuristic schedule α_t = 1 − e^(−1/t) that monotonically decreases the contrastive weight, and (2) a learning-based strategy formulated as a bi-level optimization where the outer loop minimizes L_CLS with respect to α while the inner loop minimizes L_Total with respect to model parameters θ; the outer gradient is computed via the Hessian-based approximation of Ghadimi & Wang. The two losses are optimized in an alternating fashion (inner loop updates θ for a fixed α, outer loop updates α), and the overall complexity remains O(n). The key insight is that the contrastive loss acts as a 'label-free' intervention that equalizes the learning difficulty across modalities, reducing the performance gap that otherwise causes the dominant modality to monopolize the shared classifier.

## Results

On KineticsSounds, the learning-based strategy (Ours-LB) achieves 72.53% ACC and 78.38% MAP, surpassing the strongest baseline ReconBoost (70.85% ACC, 74.24% MAP) by 1.68% and 4.14% respectively. On CREMA-D, Ours-LB reaches 83.62% ACC and 90.06% MAP, outperforming MLA (79.43% ACC, 85.72% MAP) by over 4% in both metrics. On the large-scale VGGSound dataset (310 classes), Ours-LB attains 52.74% ACC and 55.98% MAP, exceeding the best baseline MLA (51.65% ACC, 54.73% MAP). The heuristic strategy (Ours-H) also consistently outperforms all baselines except on NVGesture F1, where ReconBoost edges ahead (86.32% vs. 84.68%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed Ours-LB method outperforms all 17 baselines on nearly every dataset and metric. Against traditional fusion baselines (FiLM/Affine, MMTM/Channel, ML-LSTM, Weight, ETMC), Ours-LB gains 5–20+ points in ACC/MAP, confirming that naive fusion suffers severely from modality imbalance. Among gradient-based rebalancing methods (G-Blend, OGM, PMR, AGM, MSLR, MSES, Greedy, DOMFN), Ours-LB leads by 1.5–17 points depending on the dataset, with the largest margins on CREMA-D (e.g., 83.62% vs. 67.07% ACC over AGM). The most competitive comparisons are against recent SOTA methods: Ours-LB beats MLA by 2.5% ACC on KineticsSounds and 4.2% ACC on CREMA-D, surpasses ReconBoost by 1.7% ACC on KineticsSounds, and exceeds MMPareto by 2.5% ACC on KineticsSounds. The sole exception is NVGesture F1, where ReconBoost (86.32%) slightly edges Ours-LB (84.68%), though Ours-LB still wins on ACC (84.36% vs. 84.13%). When combined with CLIP pretrained encoders on Sarcasm and Twitter2015, Ours-LB further improves over CLIP+MLA by 0.97% and 0.48% in multi-modal F1 respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant line of modality-imbalance research (G-Blend, OGM, AGM, PMR) that adjusts per-modality learning rates or gradients, instead diagnosing the root cause as inconsistent difficulty in fitting category labels across modalities. It repurposes the contrastive learning objective from CLIP (Radford et al., 2021) not as a pretraining strategy but as an in-training 'positive intervention' that reduces the inter-modality performance gap during supervised classification. The dynamic weighting of the two losses is solved via a bilevel optimization formulation whose gradient approximation follows Ghadimi and Wang (2018), making the approach a practical hybrid of supervised classification and unsupervised contrastive alignment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a concrete, testable hypothesis (label-fitting difficulty drives modality imbalance) and a simple remedy (mix in a contrastive loss with a learned weight) that yields large gains across audio-video, image-text, and three-modality settings, including cases where prior multimodal methods underperform the best unimodal baseline. However, the causal analysis remains shallow—the authors themselves note that whether specific label attributes favor particular modalities is left open—and the contrastive loss is applied at the feature level without architectural changes, limiting mechanistic insight. For practitioners, the bilevel-optimization weighting is a drop-in replacement for fixed loss balancing that could be adopted broadly, but the method's dependence on a well-tuned temperature and batch size for the contrastive term remains a practical concern.
