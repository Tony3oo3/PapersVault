## Problem

Existing asynchronous Byzantine ML approaches fail to achieve convergence rates that diminish with the number of honest data samples and updates, and all suffer from an explicit dependence on problem dimension. The inherent delays in asynchronous settings introduce bias that obscures Byzantine disruptions, making standard (unweighted) robust aggregation rules inadequate since faster honest workers contribute disproportionately more updates than slower ones.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240946/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes per-worker stochastic gradient estimates (which may be stale due to asynchronous delays), per-worker cumulative update counts s_t^(i) that serve as aggregation weights, and a known upper bound λ < 1/2 on the fraction of Byzantine updates. It outputs a trained model x_T with a theoretical excess loss bound of O(G*D/T + D*σ̃*√(1+mc_λ)/√T) that is independent of the problem dimension d. The weights are not uniform but reflect each worker's actual contribution, which is the critical distinction from synchronous settings where all workers contribute equally per round.

## Method

The paper introduces a (c_λ, λ)-weighted robust aggregation framework that generalizes standard robust aggregators to accept per-input weights, then derives weighted variants of Geometric Median and Coordinate-Wise Median, and a new Weighted Centered Trimmed Meta Aggregator (ω-CTMA) that achieves the optimal c_λ = O(λ) bound. This weighted robust aggregator is integrated as a black box into μ²-SGD, a double-momentum variance-reduction mechanism where the momentum parameter β_t = 1/s_t^(i) is set per-worker based on individual update counts, effectively capturing each worker's entire gradient history. The key insight is that the per-worker weights in the robust aggregator must match the 1/s_t^(i) error decay of μ²-SGD, so that the collective weighted error across all honest workers scales as O(σ̃/t_G) where t_G is the total honest iteration count. This combination yields the first optimal O(1/√T) convergence rate in asynchronous Byzantine ML that is dimension-independent and matches the Byzantine-free asynchronous rate when λ = 0.

## Results

The paper evaluates its method on MNIST and CIFAR-10 using a two-layer CNN with cross-entropy loss, reporting test accuracy under various Byzantine attack scenarios (label flipping, sign flipping, little attack, empire attack) with 9–17 workers and Byzantine fractions λ ranging from 0.1 to 0.4. No explicit numerical accuracy values are reported in the text; results are presented as accuracy-vs-iteration curves in Figures 2–4 (and corresponding MNIST figures in the appendix). The headline finding is that weighted robust aggregators with ω-CTMA and μ²-SGD consistently achieve the highest test accuracy across all tested Byzantine scenarios, outperforming both non-weighted aggregators and standard SGD.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper makes three key comparisons, all presented as accuracy curves rather than single-point numbers. (1) Weighted vs. non-weighted robust aggregators: weighted variants (ω-GM, ω-CWMed) consistently achieve higher test accuracy than their non-weighted counterparts in imbalanced asynchronous settings where worker arrival probabilities are proportional to the square of their IDs (Figure 2, CIFAR-10, 17 workers, 8 Byzantine). (2) With vs. without ω-CTMA: integrating the weighted Centered Trimmed Meta Aggregator on top of ω-GM or ω-CWMed further improves accuracy across label-flipping (λ=0.3), sign-flipping (λ=0.4), little (λ=0.1), and empire (λ=0.4) attacks (Figure 3, CIFAR-10, 9 workers). (3) Optimizer comparison: μ²-SGD performs on par with standard Polyak momentum [Polyak, 1964], while vanilla SGD [Bottou, 1998] exhibits noticeably poorer accuracy under the same Byzantine conditions (Figure 4, CIFAR-10, 9 workers, 4 Byzantine, λ=0.4). In all three comparisons, the proposed method (weighted robust aggregation + ω-CTMA + μ²-SGD) is the top-performing configuration.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the synchronous Byzantine ML framework of Dahan and Levy (2024) to the asynchronous setting by introducing weighted robust aggregators (weighted GM, weighted CWMed, and a weighted CTMA meta-aggregator) that account for the unequal contribution of workers in async systems. It builds directly on the μ²-SGD double momentum mechanism of Levy (2023) and the Anytime-GD update rule of Cutkosky (2019), but the key departure is recognizing that standard unweighted robust aggregation is fundamentally mismatched to asynchronous dynamics, where faster workers contribute more updates. The paper also reframes the Byzantine threat model from 'fraction of Byzantine workers' to 'fraction of Byzantine updates,' which is the natural measure when workers operate at different speeds.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to achieve an optimal convergence rate in asynchronous Byzantine ML that diminishes with the number of honest samples and is independent of problem dimensionality—a gap that all prior async Byzantine methods (BASGD, Zeno++, AFLGuard, Kardam) left open even in the Byzantine-free case. The weighted robust aggregation framework is a general contribution applicable beyond this specific setting (e.g., synchronous training with heterogeneous batch sizes). Key limitations include the convex-only analysis, the O(dm) memory overhead of storing all workers' outputs, and experiments limited to small-scale image classification (MNIST, CIFAR-10 with 2-layer CNNs). Open questions include extending to non-convex objectives, adapting other momentum schemes to the weighted framework, and validating at scale.
