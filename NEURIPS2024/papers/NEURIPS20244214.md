## Problem

Continual learning methods face a fundamental trade-off: prioritizing retention of old task knowledge limits adaptation to new tasks, and vice versa. This paper identifies the root cause as excessive model sensitivity to parameter updates, which simultaneously drives catastrophic forgetting and overfitting to new tasks, and proposes reducing this sensitivity as an orthogonal solution that improves both objectives simultaneously.

## Input / Output

The method operates on standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 64×64 for Tiny-ImageNet) and outputs class labels, using a ResNet-18 backbone. It functions as a plug-in that wraps around any existing CL method (e.g., DER++, ER, LODE, GPM), maintaining the same number of learnable parameters as the base method. During training, it additionally maintains a per-parameter diagonal covariance vector (σ²) and a reference mean (μ₀) to track the worst-case Gaussian parameter distribution, with the Hessian approximation computed only once per task.

## Method

MACL formulates continual learning as a distributionally robust optimization problem: minimize the worst-case CL loss over all parameter distributions within a KL-divergence neighborhood of the current model's parameter distribution. The worst-case distribution is parameterized as a Gaussian with diagonal covariance, and its mean and covariance are updated via natural gradient descent (NGD) in the expectation parameter space, which by duality avoids explicit Fisher information matrix computation. The mean update (Eq. 13) combines the CL gradient with a regularization term pulling toward the reference mean, scaled by the current covariance; the covariance update (Eq. 14) adapts per-parameter step sizes using a diagonal Hessian approximated by the mean squared per-sample gradient (Eq. 15). At each training step, parameters are sampled from the worst-case Gaussian and then updated via standard SGD, making the approach a drop-in modification to any existing CL pipeline. Theoretically, this implicitly minimizes the variance of the CL loss across parameter perturbations (reducing sensitivity) and tightens the PAC-Bayes generalization bound.

## Results

MACL is a plug-in that consistently improves existing CL methods across all evaluated datasets. On CIFAR-100 Task-IL, integrating MACL with DER++ raises overall accuracy from 75.64% to 77.53% (+1.89%), and with GPM from 72.48% to 74.51% (+2.03%). On Tiny-ImageNet Task-IL, DER+++MACL reaches 54.03% vs. 51.91% for DER++ alone (+2.12%), and LODE+MACL reaches 61.79% vs. 60.18% for LODE (+1.61%). On CIFAR-10 Class-IL, ER+MACL achieves 63.74% vs. 57.74% for ER (+6.0%), and DER+++MACL reaches 74.53% vs. 72.70% for DER++ (+1.83%). Backward transfer improves by up to 5% (e.g., DER++ on CIFAR-100 Class-IL: −53.89 → −48.62).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MACL is designed as a plug-in and is evaluated by integrating it with each baseline; in every case it improves the base method. The largest gains appear on Class-IL: ER+MACL improves CIFAR-10 Class-IL accuracy by 6.0% (57.74→63.74) and DER+++MACL improves CIFAR-100 Class-IL by 3.05% (36.37→39.42). On Task-IL, GPM+MACL surpasses GPM by 2.03% on CIFAR-100 (72.48→74.51) and by 4.34% on Tiny-ImageNet (30.72→35.06). Among the strongest baselines, DER+++MACL outperforms DER++ on all six dataset/scenario combinations (e.g., +1.89% on CIFAR-100 Task-IL, +2.12% on Tiny-ImageNet Task-IL), and LODE+MACL edges out LODE on Tiny-ImageNet Task-IL (60.18→61.79) and CIFAR-100 Class-IL (38.95→40.67). Backward transfer also improves consistently: DER++ on CIFAR-100 Class-IL goes from −53.89 to −48.62, and ER on CIFAR-10 Class-IL from −45.35 to −34.43, confirming reduced forgetting. The method also outperforms regularization-only baselines (oEWC, CPR, SI, LwF) and architecture-based baselines (HAT) when integrated, demonstrating broad compatibility across CL paradigms.

## Relation to Prior Work

MACL builds on the natural gradient descent framework (Martens, JMLR 2020) and its duality between natural and expectation parameter spaces to derive efficient update rules without explicit Fisher information matrix computation, and on PAC-Bayes bounds (McAllester, COLT 1999) for generalization guarantees. Conceptually, it departs from the dominant CL paradigm—where methods either regularize weights, replay memories, project gradients, or modify architectures to trade off forgetting against new-task learning—by reframing the problem as one of model parameter sensitivity and solving it through worst-case optimization over a distributional neighborhood. The authors explicitly distinguish their probabilistic, parameter-specific approach from SAM (deterministic, uniform neighborhood) and from SWA/SWAD (memory-intensive model averaging), positioning MACL as an orthogonal plug-in rather than a replacement for existing CL strategies.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical contribution is a lightweight plug-in that consistently improves both backward transfer and new-task accuracy across a wide range of existing CL methods (DER++, ER, LODE, GPM, iCaRL, etc.) without requiring architectural changes, which could accelerate adoption in the CL community. The theoretical result that worst-case distributional optimization implicitly minimizes loss variance (a proxy for parameter sensitivity) provides a unifying explanation for why the method helps both stability and plasticity simultaneously. However, the 55–61% increase in training time is a non-trivial cost, and the Gaussian/diagonal-covariance assumptions may limit applicability to very high-dimensional or highly non-Gaussian parameter landscapes. Open questions include whether the approach extends to non-visual modalities, whether the Hessian approximation (Eq. 15) remains accurate for deeper architectures, and whether the benefits persist under extremely long task sequences or online settings with very small buffers.
