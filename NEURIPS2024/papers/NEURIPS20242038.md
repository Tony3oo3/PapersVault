## Problem

While text-to-video generation has advanced rapidly (e.g., Sora), there is no comprehensive benchmark for systematically evaluating the safety of T2V models. Existing video benchmarks focus on generation quality, and text-to-image safety benchmarks cover limited aspects while ignoring the unique temporal risk where individual frames appear harmless but the full sequence conveys unsafe content.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242038/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark feeds 5,151 malicious text prompts (from real-world sources, GPT-4 generation, and jailbreak attacks) into 9 T2V models to produce generated videos. For evaluation, one frame is sampled per second from each video and presented alongside the prompt to GPT-4o, which outputs a structured safety judgment in the format 'ANS: X, Yes: a% No: b%'. A video is classified as NSFW if its unsafety score exceeds 0.5, and the final metric reported is the NSFW rate per safety aspect per model.

## Method

T2VSafetyBench introduces a two-level taxonomy of 4 primary risk categories (Content Safety, Legal & Rights-Related, Societal, and Temporal Risks) spanning 14 critical aspects, derived from usage policies of OpenAI, Meta, and Anthropic plus surveys of AI safety practitioners. The malicious prompt dataset is constructed from three complementary sources: real-world prompts filtered from VidProM, I2P, UnsafeBench, and Gate2AI; GPT-4-generated prompts expanded via Self-Instruct; and jailbreak attack prompts produced by RAB, JPA, and BSPA methods that optimize adversarial perturbations in CLIP text-encoder space. The Temporal Risks category is novel to video, capturing sequential action risk, dynamic variation risk, and coherent contextual risk where unsafe content emerges only through frame-to-frame continuity. Evaluation employs a dual protocol: GPT-4o assesses multi-frame video samples with manually designed prompts, and 60 human volunteers perform cross-validated manual reviews, with correlation coefficients (mostly >0.75) validating the automated pipeline.

## Results

Across 14 safety aspects evaluated on Tiny-T2VSafetyBench (689 prompts), no single T2V model achieves the lowest NSFW rate in all dimensions. Open-Sora-Plan (OSP) attains the lowest average NSFW rate (22.3% GPT-4 / 18.9% Human), while Open-Sora has the highest (58.0% / 61.9%). SVD achieves a near-0% NSFW rate for explicit pornography, Gen3 leads in gore (14.8%) and disturbing content (26.7%), and Pika shows the strongest copyright defense (14.3%). The correlation coefficient between GPT-4 and human assessments exceeds 0.75 in 12 of 14 aspects, validating automated evaluation at scale.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper benchmarks 9 T2V models (Pika, Gen3, SVD, Open-Sora, Kling, Ying, Vidu, Luma, Open-Sora-Plan) on 14 safety dimensions. No model dominates all aspects: SVD and Gen3 achieve 0% NSFW for explicit pornography, while Open-Sora reaches 41.2% and Pika 22.4%. For violence, Luma (15.6%) and Ying (17.8%) are safest, whereas Open-Sora is worst at 86.7%. Gen3 leads in gore (14.8%) and disturbing content (26.7%), and Pika uniquely achieves 14.3% for copyright (vs. 61.9–85.7% for most others). Open-Sora-Plan attains the lowest average NSFW rate (22.3%), but this is partly attributed to its weaker generative capability rather than robust safety mechanisms. The key trade-off finding is that models with stronger generation (Pika, Ying, Vidu) show higher temporal-risk NSFW rates (45–57%) compared to weaker models (OSP at 15–46%), indicating that safety risks will escalate as video generation capability improves.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the safety evaluation paradigm established for LLMs (PromptBench, SafetyBench) and text-to-image models (HEIM, UnsafeBench, MMA-Diffusion) to the text-to-video domain, where no prior comprehensive safety benchmark existed. It departs from prior T2V benchmarks (VBench, EvalCrafter, FETV) that focused exclusively on generation quality, and from image safety benchmarks that neglected the unique temporal dimension of video. The prompt construction methodology repurposes existing jailbreak attack techniques for diffusion models (RAB, JPA, BSPA) and LLM-based generation (Self-Instruct) to build a multi-source adversarial prompt dataset, while the evaluation protocol introduces GPT-4o as a validated automated safety judge for multi-frame video content.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central finding—that weaker generative models are paradoxically safer in certain dimensions because they simply cannot produce the harmful content—implies that the rapid capability gains exemplified by Sora will inevitably escalate safety risks across all 14 aspects, making proactive safety engineering urgent. The introduction of temporal risks (sequential action, dynamic variation, coherent contextual) as a distinct risk category opens a genuinely new research direction that has no analogue in image safety. Open questions include how to build effective post-generation detectors for temporal content, how to evaluate misinformation in video where ground truth is ambiguous, and whether the GPT-4o evaluation protocol (which underperforms on coherent contextual risk with CC=0.627) can be improved for the most temporally complex scenarios.
