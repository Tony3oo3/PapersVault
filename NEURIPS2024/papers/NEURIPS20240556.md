## Problem

Prior LLM tensor decomposition methods are weight-centric, which reduces the number of learnable parameters during retraining (losing expressivity) and breaks the spatial weight structure needed to load the original optimizer state. No prior work has explored activation-centric decomposition, despite activations being large, redundant tensors whose compression could yield weight compression at inference as a byproduct of matrix multiplication associativity while keeping all weights fully available for training.

## Input / Output

The method takes standard text input to an LLM along with pre-calibrated static orthonormal projection matrices (one K×L matrix per GEMM layer, where L is the lowest power of two yielding ≥50% layer compression). At inference, the compressed model stores P and the pre-computed product P^T W per layer instead of the full W^T, reducing per-layer parameters from KN to L(K+N). Calibration uses 512 random training sequences to estimate activation auto-correlation matrices, and the six candidate projections are selected per-layer via validation perplexity.

## Method

ESPACE projects each activation tensor X onto a static orthonormal matrix P (K×L, L<<K) calibrated via eigenvalue decomposition of the activation auto-correlation matrix C_X = E[xx^T]. During training, the GEMM becomes W^T(PP^T X), keeping all weights fully trainable and preserving optimizer state compatibility. During inference, matrix multiplication associativity rewrites this as (P^T W)^T (P^T X), where P^T W is pre-computed, yielding compression without modifying the weight structure. The paper derives six closed-form projection constructions by minimizing MSE, NMSE, and upper bounds on GO-MSE and NL-MSE (each with and without L2-normalization), then selects the best candidate per layer via validation. After calibration, the model is retrained (extending pre-training) to heal the approximation error, with dropout disabled since projection acts as structured regularization.

## Results

ESPACE achieves ~50% model compression with minimal accuracy degradation: GPT3-22B at 55% compression shows only +0.18 perplexity increase (6.73 vs. 6.55 baseline) and GPT3-8B at 50% compression shows +0.28 (7.66 vs. 7.38). At lower compression rates (20–40%), ESPACE actually improves accuracy over the baseline, with GPT3-8B at 21% compression reducing perplexity by 0.38 (7.00 vs. 7.38) and GPT3-22B at 40% compression reducing it by 0.26 (6.29 vs. 6.55). Compression also yields 35–49% GEMM latency reduction and up to 43% TTFT speedup on A100 GPUs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares ESPACE against three weight-centric tensor decomposition methods on Llama2-7B: SVD-LoRa [18], ASVD [20], and SliceGPT [21]. SVD-LoRa performs a truncated SVD on weights but sets the intermediate dimension to half the dot-product size, yielding no actual compression. ASVD and SliceGPT achieve at most ~25% compression with measurable perplexity degradation. ESPACE, by contrast, reaches 50% compression on Llama2-7B with only +0.61 perplexity increase (5.67 vs. 5.06), and at 21% compression matches baseline accuracy (5.07 vs. 5.06). In the compression-rate vs. perplexity-increase trade-off (Figure 4), ESPACE dominates all three baselines: it achieves substantially higher compression at comparable or lower accuracy loss, establishing a new Pareto frontier for tensor-decomposition-based LLM compression. The fundamental architectural difference is that ESPACE projects activations rather than decomposing weights, preserving full parameter expressivity during retraining.

## Relation to Prior Work

ESPACE departs fundamentally from the weight-centric tensor decomposition lineage (KnGPT, TSVD, TensorGPT, HEAT, SVD-LoRa, ASVD, SliceGPT) by projecting activations onto a static set of principal components rather than factorizing frozen weight matrices. The authors explicitly note that no prior work has explored activation decomposition for LLM compression, citing the practical obstacle that activations are generated on the fly. The theoretical framework draws on PCA-style eigenvalue decomposition of activation auto-correlation and forward-propagated noise analysis (building on the authors' prior work on numerical precision guarantees for DNNs), but the application to LLM GEMM layers via matrix multiplication associativity is new. The approach is orthogonal to quantization, pruning, and attention optimizations, and compatible with data parallelism.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ESPACE demonstrates that ~50% parameter compression is achievable with minimal accuracy loss (0.18 perplexity increase on GPT3-22B) while preserving full weight availability for training, sidestepping the expressivity loss that plagues weight decomposition. The counterintuitive finding that moderate compression (20–40%) can *improve* accuracy over the uncompressed baseline suggests activation projection acts as a structured regularizer, opening questions about whether this effect generalizes. Key limitations include the requirement for retraining (up to 330B tokens for GPT3), restriction to QKV/Proj/FC1/FC2 GEMM layers (attention untouched), evaluation on only three model families, and the absence of end-to-end serving throughput measurements. Open directions include combining ESPACE with quantization or pruning, extending to attention cross-multiplications, and exploring matrix sketching for higher compressibility.
