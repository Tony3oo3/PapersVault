## Problem

Existing posterior-sampling cGANs like rcGAN enforce correctness in the posterior mean and trace-covariance but not in the principal components of the posterior covariance, meaning generated samples can have the right total variance spread in the wrong directions. Meanwhile, diffusion-based posterior samplers achieve strong quality but generate samples orders of magnitude slower. The paper asks whether a cGAN can be regularized to match the K principal components of the posterior covariance while retaining fast single-forward-pass sampling.

## Input / Output

The generator G_θ takes a measurement vector y (e.g., a noisy 28×28 MNIST digit, a GRO-masked 384×384 multicoil k-space MRI with 8 virtual coils, or a 256×256 FFHQ face with a random mask) and a random code z ~ N(0, I), producing a posterior sample x̂. Multiple samples {x̂_i} are drawn to empirically approximate the posterior mean, trace-covariance, and K principal eigenvectors/eigenvalues of the y-conditional covariance. For MRI, outputs are combined to complex-valued images via SENSE with ESPIRiT sensitivity maps before evaluation on magnitude images.

## Method

pcaGAN extends the rcGAN objective with two PCA regularization terms: L_evec, which minimizes the classical PCA projection loss to drive generated eigenvectors toward true ones, and L_eval, which penalizes the relative error between generated and true eigenvalues. Because training data provides only one x per y, the true posterior mean and eigenvalues are unknown; the method approximates them using StopGrad on the generated sample mean and a (1+P_pca)-term sample average projected onto the current eigenvectors, preventing the generator from gaming the loss by inflating the mean. A 'lazy regularization' schedule computes the SVD of centered samples only every M training steps to reduce overhead, and training is phased: rcGAN alone first, then eigenvector regularization at epoch E_evec, then eigenvalue regularization at E_eval. The generator and discriminator architectures are task-specific (UNet for MNIST, the rcGAN MRI architecture for fastMRI, CoModGAN/StyleGAN2 for FFHQ inpainting), with the PCA terms being the sole architectural addition.

## Results

On FFHQ large-scale inpainting, pcaGAN achieves CFID 7.08, FID 1.98, and LPIPS 0.1230 while generating 40 samples in 325 ms, outperforming all cGAN baselines and diffusion methods (DPS: CFID 7.26, 14 min; DDNM: CFID 11.30, 30 s; DDRM: CFID 13.17, 5 s). On accelerated MRI at R=8, pcaGAN achieves CFID 21.65 and FID 28.35, beating rcGAN (CFID 24.04, FID 28.43) and the Langevin diffusion approach (CFID 48.59, FID 52.62) while generating samples in 217 ms versus 14 min for Langevin. On MNIST denoising, pcaGAN (K=10) achieves the best REM₅ (0.0344) and CFID, though NPPC wins on rMSE due to its dedicated MSE-trained mean network. Across all tasks, pcaGAN generates samples 3–4 orders of magnitude faster than the diffusion-based competitors.

## Baselines & Comparisons

pcaGAN outperforms rcGAN on every metric across all three imaging tasks: on FFHQ inpainting it improves CFID from 7.51 to 7.08 and FID from 2.12 to 1.98; on MRI R=8 it reduces CFID from 24.04 to 21.65 and FID from 28.43 to 28.35; on MNIST it lowers REM₅ from 0.0379 to 0.0344. Against NPPC, pcaGAN wins on REM₅ and CFID but loses on rMSE (0.0418 vs. 0.0397), which is expected since NPPC uses a dedicated network trained to minimize MSE for the conditional mean. Against the diffusion methods on FFHQ inpainting, pcaGAN beats DPS (CFID 7.08 vs. 7.26, FID 1.98 vs. 2.00) while being ~43× faster (325 ms vs. 14 min for 40 samples), and decisively outperforms DDNM and DDRM on all three metrics. On MRI, pcaGAN substantially outperforms the Langevin diffusion approach (CFID 21.65 vs. 48.59 at R=8) and E2E-VarNet (CFID 21.65 vs. 36.86), though E2E-VarNet retains a PSNR advantage (36.49 vs. 33.28 at P=1) reflecting the perception-distortion trade-off. Against pscGAN and CoModGAN, pcaGAN consistently achieves lower CFID, FID, and LPIPS, confirming that principal-component covariance regularization provides a meaningful improvement over mean-only or trace-only regularization.

## Relation to Prior Work

pcaGAN extends the rcGAN regularization framework (Bendel et al., NeurIPS 2023), which itself builds on the Wasserstein cGAN formulation of Adler & Öktem (2018) and the L2-regularized pscGAN of Ohayon et al. (2021). The key departure is the addition of two PCA-based regularization terms that enforce correctness in the K principal eigenvectors and eigenvalues of the generated posterior covariance, drawing conceptual inspiration from NPPC (Nehme et al., NeurIPS 2023), which directly estimates posterior principal components but does not generate samples. The paper positions pcaGAN as a natural next step in the regularization-based cGAN lineage: where rcGAN matched the posterior mean and trace-covariance, pcaGAN additionally aligns the covariance along its dominant directions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Accurate posterior sampling is critical for uncertainty quantification and navigating the perception-distortion trade-off in imaging inverse problems, and pcaGAN provides a fast (3–4 orders of magnitude faster than diffusion models) and accurate sampler that consistently outperforms both prior cGANs and diffusion baselines across denoising, inpainting, and MRI tasks. Key limitations include the memory burden of generating P_pca = 10K samples during training (restricting K=1 for high-dimensional MRI), the absence of error bars due to computational constraints, and the preliminary nature of the MRI application. Open questions include how to translate generated posterior samples into rigorous uncertainty intervals and whether the PCA regularization schedule generalizes to non-Gaussian or highly multimodal posteriors.
