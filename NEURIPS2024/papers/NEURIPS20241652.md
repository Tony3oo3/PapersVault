## Problem

Existing information-theoretic generalization bounds in the supersample setting are almost exclusively based on mutual information or conditional mutual information, and their proof strategies rely on upper-bounding the cumulant-generating function (CGF), which is difficult to generalize beyond KL divergence. This paper asks whether there is a unified, generic way to extend the CMI/supersample framework to arbitrary f-divergence-based dependence measures without depending on CGF upper bounds or regret guarantees from online learning.

## Input / Output

The method takes a stochastic learning algorithm, an n×2 supersample matrix (each entry drawn i.i.d. from the data distribution), and a loss function, and produces an upper bound on the expected generalization error expressed in terms of f-information between per-sample loss differences and Bernoulli membership variables. The framework handles both bounded loss differences (|ΔL| ≤ 1) and unbounded loss differences (via truncation with a tunable threshold C and Hölder's inequality), and the bounds are individual (per-sample) rather than aggregated, making them tighter than whole-sample MI bounds.

## Method

The key insight is a novel variational formula for f-divergence (Lemma 3.1) that sets the CGF term to zero by choosing the measurable function in the variational representation to be the inverse of the convex conjugate of the f-divergence's defining function φ*. This eliminates the need to upper-bound the CGF, which was the main bottleneck in previous MI-based proofs. The remaining task is to lower-bound the inverse convex conjugate (e.g., log(1+x) for KL, x/(1+x) for squared Hellinger, log(2−e^{−x}) for JS) using quadratic lower bounds of the form x − ax², with the parameter a chosen adaptively based on the expected loss difference and its second moment. For the unbounded case, a refined lemma (Lemma 4.1) introduces an indicator function to truncate the measurable function, and the tail contribution is controlled via Hölder's inequality with a tunable f-divergence of order α. The resulting bounds recover and improve upon previous ld-CMI, square-root, and fast-rate bounds, and empirically the squared Hellinger-information bound is the tightest across linear, CNN, ResNet-50, and SGLD experiments.

## Results

The paper evaluates generalization bounds (not prediction accuracy) across six experimental settings: linear classifiers on synthetic Gaussian data (2-class and 10-class), a 4-layer CNN on MNIST '4 vs 9', ResNet-50 on CIFAR-10, and SGLD training on both MNIST and CIFAR-10. The key finding is that the squared Hellinger-information (SHI) bounds—particularly CSHI(Var)—consistently produce the tightest generalization error upper bounds across all complex tasks (Figures 2b–2f), outperforming both the oracle CMI bound and all MI-based baselines. Even the pessimistic CSHI(Worst) variant (which replaces the empirical variance with a constant 1) beats most baselines on MNIST and CIFAR-10. For the simplest two-class linear setting, the Sharpness-based MI bound from [24] remains the tightest, but the gap between CSHI(Var) and Sharpness narrows as task complexity increases.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the ld-CMI bound [24, Theorem 3.1], the proposed CMI(Oracle) bound (Theorem 3.1) is substantially tighter across all settings, revealing that the original ld-CMI bound ignores vanishing quantities (e.g., E[ΔL_i²] and |E[G_i]|) that shrink with sample size. The squared Hellinger-information bounds (CSHI(Oracle) and CSHI(Var)) further tighten the gap, with CSHI(Var) being the tightest bound in all complex settings (Figures 2b–2f). Against the Binary KL bound [23, Theorem 5], the proposed f-information bounds are consistently tighter, particularly for the CNN and ResNet experiments. Against the Sharpness-based MI bound [24, Theorem 4.5], the proposed methods lose on the two-class linear task (Figure 2a) where Sharpness is tightest, but CSHI(Var) and CSHI(Worst) surpass Sharpness on the ten-class linear, CNN (MNIST), and ResNet (CIFAR-10) settings (Figures 2b–2d). The JS-information bound (CJSI(Oracle)), despite carrying pessimistic constants, still outperforms the ld-CMI and Binary KL baselines in most non-trivial settings, confirming that alternative f-divergences beyond KL are beneficial for generalization analysis.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the conditional mutual information (CMI) supersample framework of Steinke and Zakynthinou (2020) and the loss-difference CMI (ld-CMI) of Wang and Mao (2023) from KL-based mutual information to the broader family of f-divergences (squared Hellinger, Jensen-Shannon, chi-squared). The key methodological departure is in the proof strategy: rather than upper-bounding the cumulant-generating function (CGF) as in prior MI-based bounds, the authors select the measurable function in the variational representation of f-divergence to be the inverse of the convex conjugate, causing the CGF term to vanish identically. This approach is partially inspired by the coin-betting framework (Jang et al., 2023) but is explicitly independent of regret guarantees from online learning, and it differs fundamentally from the convex-analysis/regret-analysis framework of Lugosi and Neu (2022, 2023).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 'oracle' CMI bound (Theorem 3.1) provides a clearer diagnosis of why previous square-root MI-based bounds are loose: quantities that vanish with sample size are simply ignored, and the bound explicitly reveals this through the |E[G_i]| term. The empirical demonstration that squared Hellinger-information bounds consistently outperform MI-based bounds across multiple architectures and datasets suggests that the choice of dependence measure matters more than previously appreciated. However, the paper acknowledges that its bounds cannot resolve the 'CMI-accuracy tradeoff' limitation identified in stochastic convex optimization, and the lack of high-probability guarantees remains an open direction. The framework also opens the question of whether intermediate f-divergences (α ∈ (1,2)) can yield tighter bounds than both TV and χ².
