## Problem

Learning Granger causal graphs from topological event sequences in large-scale telecommunication networks is critical for fault diagnosis, yet existing methods either assume i.i.d. event sequences (ignoring device topology) or rely on gradient-free optimization (THP) and neural point processes without analytical likelihoods (TNPAR), making them too slow or unscalable for real-world networks with dozens of event types and hundreds of thousands of alarms.

## Input / Output

The method takes as input a sequence of alarm events X = {(v_i, n_i, t_i, ℓ_i)} (event type, device, start timestamp, duration) and an undirected topological network graph G_N over N devices. Events are discretized into intervals of width Δ, and the topology is encoded as geodesic-distance binary matrices B_{0:k} that restrict which device pairs can interact. The output is a weighted Granger causal adjacency matrix A' ∈ R^{|V|×|V|} (where each entry α_{v_i,v}·A_{v_i,v} quantifies the causal strength from event type v_i to v), which is then thresholded at ρ to yield a binary DAG over event types.

## Method

S²GCSL models event generation as a topological multivariate Hawkes process with a linear kernel: the intensity λ_t^{v,n} equals a baseline μ^{v,n} plus a sum over all active historical events of α_{v_i,v}·A_{v_i,v}·B_{n_i,n}^k, where A encodes Granger causality between event types and B^k encodes topological reachability at geodesic distance ≤ k. The key insight is that superposition of many simple linear activation terms can approximate arbitrarily complex intensity functions, eliminating the need for neural point processes. The log-likelihood is optimized end-to-end via Adam gradient descent, with sparsity (‖A'‖₁ ≤ ε) and acyclicity (DAG-NO TEARS constraint h(A')=0) incorporated as Lagrangian penalty terms, allowing expert knowledge to be seamlessly embedded in the optimization. After convergence, edges with weights below a pruning threshold ρ are removed to produce the final binary causal graph. This design yields near-linear scaling in the number of event types (≈9 min for 100 types) versus hours or infeasibility for NPP-based baselines.

## Results

On the real-world metropolitan telecommunication network alarm dataset (65 devices, 38 event types, 492,877 records), S²GCSL achieves the highest F1 score of 0.40±0.06 and the lowest SHD of 60.6±6.59, outperforming all six baselines. It also achieves a competitive SID of 397±30.2 (second only to PCMCI's 367±17.1, which the authors attribute to PCMCI's tendency to produce overly sparse graphs) while running in 737 seconds—roughly 6× faster than TNPAR (4604s) and 10× faster than CAUSE (7209s). On synthetic experiments scaling to 100 event types, S²GCSL completes in ~542s, the only method alongside ADM4 to finish within one hour at that scale.

## Baselines & Comparisons

S²GCSL clearly outperforms all six baselines on F1 score (0.40 vs. 0.29 for the next-best CAUSE) and SHD (60.6 vs. 75.5 for the next-best PCMCI) on the real-world dataset. Among topology-aware methods, S²GCSL dominates TNPAR (F1 0.40 vs. 0.23; SHD 60.6 vs. 83.1) and CAUSE (F1 0.40 vs. 0.29; SHD 60.6 vs. 78.1) while being 6–10× faster. On SID, PCMCI achieves a slightly lower value (367 vs. 397), but the authors note this is because PCMCI tends to infer very sparse graphs, inflating its SID advantage; S²GCSL's SID is competitive while maintaining far higher F1. On synthetic experiments, S²GCSL is the only method (with ADM4) that scales to 100 event types within one hour, and it is ~1.4× faster than ADM4 at that scale (542s vs. 758s). Methods relying on the i.i.d. assumption (ADM4, MLE_SGL, ACD, PCMCI) all show substantially lower F1 scores on the real-world data, confirming that topological propagation is critical in telecommunication alarm sequences.

## Relation to Prior Work

S²GCSL builds directly on the topological multivariate Hawkes process (TMHP) framework introduced by THP (Cai et al., 2024), which first formalized Granger causal discovery from non-i.i.d. topological event sequences. However, it departs from THP's gradient-free structure-parameter decoupled optimization and from TNPAR's neural point process intensity function, replacing both with a simple linear activation kernel and closed-form likelihood optimized via gradient descent. The acyclicity constraint is borrowed from the NO TEARS continuous DAG formulation (Zheng et al., 2018), and the sparsity constraint follows standard L1 regularization. Conceptually, the paper positions itself as a 'simplification that scales' argument: the TMHP generative model is retained, but the inference machinery is deliberately stripped down to what is computationally tractable at scale.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in telecommunication network fault diagnosis where existing topological causal discovery methods (TNPAR, CAUSE) require hours to produce results, making them unusable in time-critical operational settings. The demonstrated order-of-magnitude efficiency gain (minutes vs. hours) combined with competitive or superior effectiveness makes this approach immediately deployable. Key limitations include the reliance on a linear kernel that may fail under non-stationary or strongly non-linear interaction regimes, and validation on a single real-world dataset. Open questions include whether the framework extends to other infrastructure networks (power grids, transport) and whether the linear assumption holds as network dynamics become more complex over time.
