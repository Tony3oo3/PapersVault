## Problem

Diffusion-based image inpainting models produce technically coherent reconstructions that frequently diverge from human aesthetic preferences, generating structurally illogical or texturally unnatural content. No prior work has aligned inpainting diffusion models with human preference through reinforcement learning, leaving a gap between objective reconstruction quality and subjective visual appeal.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240961/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 512×512 RGB image with a masked region as input, where the mask is created either by warping (simulating viewpoint changes via depth estimation, producing irregular holes) or by boundary cropping (square or rectangular crops for outpainting). It produces a complete inpainted image at the same resolution. For reward model training, three inpainted samples are generated per prompt, yielding 51,000 scored images total. The reward model uses a CLIP ViT-B backbone with a 256-dim MLP head trained in regression mode.

## Method

PrefPaint fine-tunes a pre-trained Runway inpainting diffusion model via a PPO-style RL objective that shifts the sampling distribution toward higher reward while constraining divergence from a reference model. A key theoretical contribution derives an upper bound on the reward model's prediction error, showing it is proportional to ‖z‖_{V⁻¹} (the Mahalanobis norm of the sample's feature embedding), which quantifies per-sample reward reliability. Building on this bound, the method introduces a reward trustiness-aware amplification factor γ = e^{−k‖z‖_{V⁻¹}+b} that scales the RL gradient, amplifying regularization on high-confidence samples while dampening it on uncertain ones. The reward model is trained on 51K human-annotated images scored across three criteria (structural rationality, local texture, overall feeling) with weights [0.15, 0.15, 0.7], using a regression-driven strategy with 70% of CLIP layers frozen.

## Results

On outpainting prompts, PrefPaint achieves a 70.16% WinRate with a single sampling step (S=1), matching Runway's 73.40% at S=3, and reaches 93.14% at S=10. On warping prompts, it attains 72.38% (S=1) and 93.85% (S=10). The reward mean is +0.38 (outpainting) and +0.36 (warping), the highest among all compared methods, with the lowest variance (0.01), indicating consistently high-quality generation. Across secondary metrics (Table 2), PrefPaint also leads in T2I (4.49), CLIP (0.23), BLIP (0.49), Classification Accuracy (0.45), and Inception Score (23.71).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PrefPaint outperforms all 10 compared inpainting methods on both WinRate and Reward across outpainting and warping prompts. The closest competitor, CompVis (latent diffusion), reaches only 50.51% (outpainting) and 47.35% (warping) WinRate at S=1, trailing PrefPaint by roughly 20 percentage points. Among Stable Diffusion variants, SDxl++ is the strongest at 21.15%/18.66%, still far below PrefPaint's 70.16%/72.38%. Non-SD-based methods MAT and Palette perform worst, with WinRates below 16% at S=1. Notably, PrefPaint's single-step WinRate (70.16%) already exceeds Runway's three-step WinRate (73.40%) by a narrow margin while producing a higher reward mean (+0.38 vs. Runway's baseline), and its variance of 0.01 is the lowest among all methods, confirming more consistent generation quality. In the supplementary RL-method comparison (Table E-3), PrefPaint also surpasses DPOK (64.59%), ImageReward (65.10%), Human Preference Score (58.03%), and D3PO (59.74%) on WinRate.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the RLHF-style alignment paradigm—well established for large language models and recently adapted to text-to-image diffusion (e.g., ImageReward, DPOK)—to the specific domain of image inpainting, where the one-to-many nature of masked reconstruction makes per-pixel supervision counterproductive. It builds directly on Zhang et al. [53] for the trust-region RL training loop and per-step probability estimation in DDIM sampling, and on the linear stochastic bandit theory of Abbasi-Yadkori et al. [55] to derive a closed-form upper bound on reward model prediction error. The key conceptual departure is the 'reward trustiness-aware' weighting: rather than treating all reward signals equally, the paper uses the theoretical bound to modulate gradient magnitude per sample, amplifying updates in high-confidence regions and suppressing them where the reward model is uncertain.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 51K human-annotated inpainting dataset and the principled reward-error bound provide the community with both a resource and a theoretical tool for more reliable preference alignment in generative models beyond inpainting. However, the alignment targets a single group's aesthetic preferences, and the authors acknowledge that personalized or reward-free alignment remains open. The method is also demonstrated on a single base model (Runway inpainting), leaving open whether the trustiness-aware scheme transfers across architectures or scales to higher-resolution generation. Broader 3D reconstruction applications (novel view synthesis, FOV enlargement) are shown but remain secondary to the core inpainting contribution.
