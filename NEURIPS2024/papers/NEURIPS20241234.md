## Problem

Reconstructing a full 3D scene from a single RGB image remains extremely challenging due to severely limited geometric information. Existing data-driven methods (PanoRecon, Total3D, Mesh R-CNN) are trained on limited image-scene pairs and fail to generalize to out-of-distribution or open-world inputs. The paper asks whether diverse deep priors from large foundation models can be assembled in a zero-shot manner to solve this task without any task-specific 3D or 2D training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241234/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image of an indoor or outdoor scene as input and produces a textured 3D scene reconstruction: individual object meshes (with appearance from Shap-E) placed at estimated positions, orientations, and scales, plus background geometry (walls, floors) obtained by back-projecting the estimated depth map. The pipeline is entirely zero-shot — all constituent models (Grounded-SAM, Stable-Diffusion, Open-CLIP, Shap-E, Omnidata) are used with frozen, pre-trained parameters and no fine-tuning on scene-reconstruction data. A single scene reconstruction takes under 3 minutes on an RTX 3090 GPU.

## Method

Deep prior assembly decomposes scene reconstruction into five sub-tasks, each assigned to a frozen large-model expert: Grounded-SAM for open-set detection and segmentation, Stable-Diffusion (image-to-image) for enhancing occluded or low-resolution instance crops, Open-CLIP for filtering unreliable diffusion outputs via cosine similarity in embedding space, Shap-E for 2D-to-3D mesh generation, and Omnidata for monocular depth estimation. The key technical contribution is a novel 7-DoF (3 translation + 3 rotation + 1 scale) shape-registration optimization that simultaneously minimizes 3D Chamfer Distance against back-projected depth points and 2D Chamfer Distance against densely sampled mask points, providing complementary geometric and appearance constraints for stable convergence. A RANSAC-like strategy repeats the optimization with random rotation initializations to escape local optima, and the best-scoring proposal among K candidates per instance is selected as the final reconstruction. This dual 2D+3D matching and the CLIP-based quality filtering are the bridging mechanisms that make heterogeneous frozen models cooperate robustly without any joint training.

## Results

On 3D-Front, Deep Prior Assembly achieves CDL1-S of 0.109, CDL1 of 0.134, and F-Score of 35.67, outperforming the closest baseline PanoRecon (0.120, 0.125, 31.94). On the open-world BlendSwap dataset, the method achieves CDL1-S of 0.106, CDL1 of 0.089, and F-Score of 73.19, dramatically surpassing all baselines (best F-Score among baselines: 26.93 by Total3D). On Replica, it reaches CDL1-S of 0.113, CDL1 of 0.110, and F-Score of 70.48, again leading all competitors by a wide margin. The method is entirely zero-shot, requiring no task-specific training, yet consistently outperforms data-driven methods trained on 3D ground-truth annotations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Deep Prior Assembly outperforms all six baselines across every dataset and metric. Against PanoRecon [10] (NeurIPS 2021), the closest competitor on 3D-Front where it was trained, the method improves F-Score from 31.94 to 35.67 and CDL1-S from 0.120 to 0.109; on open-world datasets the gap widens dramatically, with PanoRecon's F-Score collapsing to 17.11 on BlendSwap and 17.13 on Replica versus 73.19 and 70.48 for the proposed method. Total3D [42] (CVPR 2020) and Mesh R-CNN [18] (ICCV 2019) are both significantly outperformed, with Total3D achieving only 26.93 F-Score on BlendSwap and 24.01 on Replica compared to the proposed method's 73.19 and 70.48. In additional visual comparisons (Appendix B), the method also surpasses BUOL [9] (CVPR 2023), Uni-3D [71] (ICCV 2023), and ScenePrior [41] (CVPR 2023), all of which are data-driven and trained on 3D-Front, and thus struggle to generalize to real-world ScanNet images. A key distinction is that all baselines require supervised training on 3D-annotated datasets, whereas Deep Prior Assembly operates entirely zero-shot by assembling frozen large-model priors.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant data-driven paradigm for single-image 3D scene reconstruction (PanoRecon, Total3D, Mesh R-CNN), which trains encoder-decoder networks on limited image-scene pairs and struggles with out-of-distribution generalization. Instead, it introduces 'deep prior assembly': a framework that decomposes scene reconstruction into sub-tasks (detection/segmentation, inpainting, 3D generation, depth estimation, layout registration) and assigns each to a frozen large model (Grounded-SAM, Stable Diffusion, Shap-E, Omnidata) without any task-specific training. The novel technical contributions are the 7-DoF pose/scale registration combining 2D mask matching with 3D Chamfer distance, the Open-CLIP-based filtering of unreliable diffusion outputs, and a RANSAC-like robustness wrapper — none of which have direct predecessors in the scene reconstruction literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that zero-shot 3D scene reconstruction from a single image is viable by simply assembling existing large models, achieving F-Scores of ~70 on BlendSwap/Replica where data-driven baselines (trained on 3D-Front) collapse to ~17–32. This suggests a broader paradigm: complex 3D perception tasks may be solvable by orchestrating frozen foundation models rather than training task-specific networks, reducing data requirements and improving open-world generalization. Key limitations include reliance on depth scale/shift calibration (addressable with metric depth methods), texture randomness introduced by Stable Diffusion, and ~3-minute runtime per scene. Open questions include whether the assembly framework extends to dynamic scenes, multi-view inputs, or very large-scale environments, and whether the individual model choices (e.g., Shap-E vs. newer 3D generators) will shift as the underlying components evolve.
