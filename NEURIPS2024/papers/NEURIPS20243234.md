## Problem

Existing activation sparsity methods for LLMs (MoEfication, Deja Vu) only exploit the sparsity that naturally emerges in pretrained ReLU-based models, overlooking the potential to further amplify this sparsity through training. Moreover, these methods are limited to ReLU activations and rely on noisy top-K Softmax routing, which causes severe accuracy drops in the MoEfication setting due to biased expert score allocation. The paper asks whether LLMs can be trained to learn more structured, efficiency-aware activation sparsity that works even with soft activations like SwiGLU and GeGLU.

## Input / Output

LTE takes a pretrained LLM (RoBERTa-base/large, GPT-2-Medium, or LLaMA-7B) and a downstream fine-tuning dataset as input, and produces a sparsified model where each FFN layer is converted into an MoE layer with 32-neuron experts and a Sigmoid router. The sparsity level is controlled by a single hyperparameter η (efficiency loss coefficient), and expert selection at inference uses a fixed threshold τ=0.5 applied independently per expert, yielding adaptive sparsity that varies across inputs and layers. The structured sparsity (contiguous 32-neuron groups) is specifically designed to enable a custom Triton CUDA kernel that avoids non-coalesced memory access, translating FFN sparsity into wall-clock latency reduction.

## Method

LTE introduces a two-stage training algorithm to build structured MoE sparsity in FFN layers. In Stage 1 (model-router training), the model and Sigmoid routers are jointly trained in a 'soft' mode where all experts are weighted by their Sigmoid scores, with two auxiliary losses: an efficiency loss (L_efficiency = mean of squared expert scores) that penalizes router output magnitudes to drive competition among experts, and a separability loss (L_separability = mean of 1/(score−τ)²) that pushes expert scores away from the threshold τ to make discrete selection well-defined. In Stage 2 (model adaptation), routers are frozen and switched to discrete threshold-based selection, and the model is fine-tuned to recover performance. The key architectural choices—Sigmoid routing (avoiding Softmax's sum-to-1 constraint that causes biased allocation in MoEfication) and threshold-based adaptive selection (allowing different numbers of experts per input and layer)—distinguish LTE from prior MoEfication approaches. A custom Triton kernel exploits the structured 32-neuron grouping to achieve near-linear speedup with sparsity, reducing LLaMA2-7B end-to-end latency by 25% at 50% sparsity.

## Results

On LLaMA-7B NLG tasks, LTE achieves 1.83x–2.59x FLOPs speed-up (5.38 GFLOPs/token on XSum, 4.65 on E2E, 6.59 on WikiText-103 vs. 12.06 for the full model) while permitting at most 0.05 ROUGE-L or 0.5 PPL degradation. On GLUE NLU tasks with RoBERTa-large, LTE maintains competitive accuracy even at >90% FFN neuron sparsity, whereas MoEfication degrades at 70–80% and Deja Vu at ~30%. With a custom Triton kernel, LTE reduces LLaMA2-7B end-to-end generation latency by ~25% at 50% sparsity on a single RTX 3090Ti. On 5-shot MMLU (instruction tuning with Tulu), LTE outperforms all baselines across sparsity levels.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LTE consistently outperforms all four baselines across NLU, NLG, and instruction-tuning tasks. Against MoEfication [49], LTE achieves substantially lower FLOPs (e.g., 5.38 vs. 10.45 GFLOPs on XSum) and avoids the performance collapse MoEfication suffers at 70–80% sparsity on NLU; MoEfication also fails entirely on E2E and WikiText-103. Against Deja Vu [21], LTE maintains quality at much higher sparsity levels (Deja Vu degrades at ~30% sparsity on NLG, while LTE remains competitive up to 50%+). Against Wanda [36] (static pruning), LTE achieves a lower perplexity of 5.95 versus 6.87–11.45 at comparable ~50% overall model sparsity on WikiText-103. Against σ-MoE [4] with further fine-tuning, LTE still outperforms across all sparsity levels on GPT2-Medium/WikiText-103, confirming that the gains come from the efficiency-aware training rather than simply additional fine-tuning steps.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the MoEfication line of work [49] and Deja Vu [21], which exploit naturally occurring activation sparsity in FFN layers via post-training expert selection. The key departure is shifting from passive exploitation of existing sparsity to active, efficiency-aware training: LTE introduces a Sigmoid-based threshold router (replacing the Top-K Softmax that the authors show causes severe expert-score bias in the MoEfication regime), an efficiency loss that penalizes router output magnitudes, and a separability regularizer that makes expert scores cleanly thresholdable. Unlike MoEfication, which requires replacing soft activations (SwiGLU, GeGLU) with ReLU, LTE operates directly on modern LLMs with non-ReLU activations, and its two-stage training (soft routing → discrete routing) addresses the non-differentiability of threshold-based selection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 25% wall-clock latency reduction at 50% sparsity on LLaMA2-7B, combined with 1.83×–2.59× FLOPs savings, makes structured activation sparsity a practical deployment option for latency-sensitive applications. However, LTE requires additional fine-tuning beyond the pretrained checkpoint, and its instruction-tuning results (MMLU) show a weaker sparsity-quality trade-off than downstream tasks, suggesting the router-induced structural changes partially disrupt pretraining-learned generalization. Open questions include whether LTE scales to 70B+ models, whether it can be combined with parameter-efficient fine-tuning to reduce training overhead, and whether pretraining-scale data is needed to fully recover the generalization gap observed in few-shot evaluation.
