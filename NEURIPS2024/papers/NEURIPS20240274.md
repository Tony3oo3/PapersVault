## Problem

While RL theory extensively analyzes statistical and optimization error, the approximation error perspective—specifically how complex a function class must be to represent the underlying model, optimal policy, or optimal value function—remains largely unexplored. Prior work only partially separated model-based from value-based RL and ignored policy-based RL entirely, and none connected representation complexity to the expressiveness of neural networks used in practice.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240274/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The theoretical analysis takes structured MDPs (3-SAT MDPs, NP MDPs, CVP MDPs, P MDPs) whose states encode Boolean formulas, Turing machine configurations, or circuit descriptions, and produces a representation complexity classification for each of the model (reward + transition kernel), optimal policy, and optimal value function. The empirical component takes MuJoCo environment states (HalfCheetah, Humanoid, InvertedPendulum, Ant) and produces normalized approximation errors for MLPs of varying depth and width fitting each target function, with errors scaled by the standard deviation of the target to enable cross-function comparison.

## Method

The paper constructs four MDP families that encode computational problems into MDP structure: 3-SAT MDPs and NP MDPs (where the model is in AC0 but the optimal policy and value are NP-complete), and CVP MDPs and P MDPs (where the model and optimal policy are in AC0 but the optimal value is P-complete). This yields a three-level hierarchy: model < policy < value in representation complexity. The key technical bridge to deep RL is showing that constant-layer, polynomial-width log-precision MLPs (and Transformers) can represent AC0 functions but provably cannot represent NP-complete or P-complete functions under the standard assumptions TC0≠NP and TC0≠P, thereby translating circuit complexity separations into neural network expressiveness limitations. The NP MDP generalizes 3-SAT MDPs by encoding any NP-complete language via a nondeterministic Turing machine's configuration space, while the P MDP generalizes CVP MDPs by encoding any P-complete language via a circuit family.

## Results

The paper establishes a representation complexity hierarchy across RL paradigms: the underlying model (reward + transition) is in AC0 (constant-depth, polynomial-size circuits) for all constructed MDPs, the optimal policy is NP-complete for 3-SAT/NP MDPs but AC0 for CVP/P MDPs, and the optimal value function is NP-complete for 3-SAT/NP MDPs and P-complete for CVP/P MDPs. From the MLP expressiveness perspective, constant-layer ReLU MLPs with polynomial hidden dimension can represent the model (and policy for CVP/P MDPs) but provably cannot represent the optimal policy or value under TC0≠NP and TC0≠P assumptions. Empirically, across four MuJoCo environments (HalfCheetah-v4, Humanoid-v4, InvertedPendulum-v4, Ant-v4) with MLPs of 2–3 layers and 16–32 hidden units, the relative approximation error consistently follows the hierarchy: e_value > e_policy > e_transition ≈ e_reward.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper positions itself against two prior works on representation complexity in RL: Dong et al. [13] (ICML 2020), which used piecewise linear functions and showed the model needs constant pieces while the value function needs exponentially many, and Zhu et al. [62] (arXiv 2023), which used circuit complexity to show the model is in AC0 while the value function is not. This paper surpasses both by (1) extending the analysis to policy-based RL, revealing a three-level hierarchy rather than a two-level separation; (2) establishing NP-completeness (stronger than mere non-representability) for the policy and value in 3-SAT/NP MDPs, and P-completeness for the value in CVP/P MDPs; and (3) introducing a completely new MLP/Transformer expressiveness perspective that directly connects to deep RL practice. Empirically, the paper validates its theory by training TD3 agents on four MuJoCo environments and fitting MLPs of varying depth (2–3 layers) and width (16–32 units) to the transition kernel, reward, optimal policy, and optimal Q-function; in all configurations and environments, the approximation error for the value function exceeds that of the policy, which in turn exceeds that of the model components, confirming the predicted hierarchy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the representation complexity analysis initiated by Dong et al. [13] (piecewise linear functions) and Zhu et al. [62] (circuit complexity), both of which showed the model is easier to represent than the value function but did not consider policy-based RL. The key departures are: (1) introducing policy-based RL into the comparison to establish a three-way hierarchy (model < policy < value), (2) proving a stronger separation (NP-complete vs. AC0 rather than polynomial vs. constant), and (3) introducing MLP/Transformer expressiveness as a completely new lens for RL representation complexity, leveraging the TC0 simulation result of Merrill & Sabharwal [36]. The NP MDP and P MDP constructions generalize the 3-SAT MDP and CVP MDP to encode arbitrary NP- and P-complete problems.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first theoretical justification for why model-based RL typically enjoys superior sample efficiency over model-free RL, by showing the ground-truth model is provably easier to represent (AC0) than the optimal policy or value function (NP-complete or P-complete). The MLP expressiveness results bridge computational complexity theory with practical deep RL, offering guidance on which target (model, policy, or value) is most amenable to neural network approximation. Open questions include a full characterization of the representation hierarchy across all MDPs (the current results hold for specific constructed MDP classes), the interplay between representation complexity and statistical/optimization error, and whether the hierarchy holds under weaker assumptions than TC0 ≠ NP and TC0 ≠ P.
