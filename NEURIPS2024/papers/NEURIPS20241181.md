## Problem

Linear representation methods like PCA and ICA extract a single latent space or one-dimensional components without supervision, while deep generative models (VAEs, HCV) can model multiple subspaces but produce black-box representations that are difficult to interpret. No existing linear method simultaneously decomposes high-dimensional data into multiple independent subspaces, incorporates target supervision, and retains explicit feature-level interpretability through its projection matrices.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241181/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a centered data matrix X ∈ R^{n×p} and m target variables Y ∈ R^{n×m}, each paired with a kernel: a Dirac delta kernel for categorical targets (e.g., cancer type, infection status) or a linear kernel for continuous targets (e.g., age, time). An additional unsupervised subspace is supported by setting its target kernel to the identity matrix. The output is a set of m orthonormal projection matrices U_j ∈ R^{p×d_j} (serving directly as feature importance scores) and the corresponding subspace representations Z_j = XU_j, where the effective dimension of each subspace is bounded by the rank of its supervision kernel.

## Method

sisPCA formulates a multi-subspace optimization that maximizes the sum of HSIC dependences between each subspace Z_j and its target Y_j, minus a λ-weighted sum of pairwise HSIC dependences between all subspace pairs, subject to orthonormality constraints U_j^T U_j = I. With linear kernels, the disentanglement penalty reduces to ‖Z_u^T Z_v‖_F², which vanishes exactly when subspaces are orthogonal, and the full objective is solvable by an alternating eigendecomposition algorithm where each subspace update is the top-d_j eigenvectors of X^T K̃_j X (K̃_j incorporating both the target kernel and the repulsion from other subspaces). The paper proves the linear-kernel objective is equivalent to a regularized linear autoencoder (Remark 3.1) and to regression under a Zellner g-prior (Remark 3.3), and conjectures the absence of spurious local optima under balanced supervision (Conjecture 3.1). For strict statistical independence, sisPCA-general replaces the linear subspace kernel with a Gaussian kernel and solves the problem via gradient descent on the Stiefel manifold, at O(n³) cost versus O(n²) for the linear variant.

## Results

On the Plasmodium liver-stage scRNA-seq dataset (19,053 cells, 2,000 genes), sisPCA achieves the highest Grassmann distance (4.824) between infection and time subspaces, outperforming sPCA (3.771), hsVAE (3.598), and supVAE (3.571). sisPCA also attains the best Silhouette score for infection separation (0.235) while maintaining strong time-point discrimination (0.355). On the TCGA pan-cancer DNA methylation data (9,725 samples, 5,000 CpG sites), sisPCA reduces HSIC-linear between aging and cancer subspaces from 189.7 (sPCA) to 2.4×10⁻⁵ (λ=10) while increasing the max Spearman correlation with age in the aging subspace from 0.213 (PCA) to 0.294. On the breast cancer dataset, sisPCA increases the Grassmann distance between radius and symmetry subspaces from 1.493 (sPCA) to 2.710 and improves the diagnostic Silhouette score from 0.470 to 0.516.

## Baselines & Comparisons

sisPCA consistently outperforms PCA on disentanglement (Grassmann distance 4.824 vs. 0) and infection-specific separation (Silhouette 0.235 vs. −0.014), though PCA captures temporal structure comparably well (0.311). Against sPCA, sisPCA improves both subspace separateness (4.824 vs. 3.771) and infection discrimination (0.235 vs. 0.207), while reducing confounding: the time signal in the infection subspace drops from −0.075 to −0.097, indicating cleaner separation. Compared to the non-linear VAE baseline, sisPCA achieves substantially better disentanglement (4.824 vs. 0) and infection separation (0.235 vs. −0.036), confirming that the linear HSIC-based formulation is competitive with or superior to deep generative alternatives. Against hsVAE (the HCV-style model with Gaussian-kernel HSIC regularization), sisPCA wins on disentanglement (4.824 vs. 3.598) and infection separation (0.235 vs. −0.015), but hsVAE achieves a higher time-point Silhouette (0.582 vs. 0.355), suggesting the non-linear model retains more temporal nuance at the cost of weaker infection-specific signal. On the breast cancer dataset, sisPCA's explicit disentanglement (Grassmann 2.710 vs. sPCA's 1.493) reassigns feature contributions, revealing that nuclear size features are more diagnostically informative than shape features—a distinction obscured in the entangled sPCA representation.

## Relation to Prior Work

sisPCA extends supervised PCA (sPCA) from a single supervised subspace to multiple simultaneously supervised and mutually disentangled subspaces, using HSIC as both the supervision objective and the independence penalty. It occupies a conceptual middle ground between classical linear methods (PCA, ICA, ISA) that lack supervision and deep generative models (HCV, β-VAE) that enforce disentanglement but sacrifice interpretability. The optimization landscape analysis draws on matrix factorization theory (Ge et al., 2017), and the equivalence to regularized regression connects to Zellner's g-prior in Bayesian statistics. The paper explicitly distinguishes itself from contrastive representation learning (cPCA, cVAE), noting that sisPCA decomposes a single dataset via supervision rather than contrasting target vs. background datasets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

For biologists working with high-dimensional omics data, sisPCA offers a rare combination: multi-subspace disentanglement with directly interpretable linear projections (feature importance scores), avoiding the black-box nature of VAE-based alternatives. Key limitations include the linearity constraint (potentially missing nonlinear interactions), the fact that linear-kernel HSIC does not guarantee full statistical independence (only orthogonality), and identifiability issues when subspaces are unsupervised or supervisions are too similar. Open questions include whether the optimization landscape conjecture (no spurious local optima under balanced supervision) can be formally proven, how the method scales to very large single-cell datasets, and whether nonlinear kernel extensions can preserve interpretability.
