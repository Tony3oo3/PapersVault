## Problem

It remains unclear whether LLMs perform genuine causal reasoning or merely retrieve causal patterns memorized from training corpora. Existing causal Q&A benchmarks (COPA, e-CARE, CausalNet) predate or overlap with LLM training data, making it impossible to distinguish level-1 (retrieval-based) from level-2 (genuine) causal reasoning. This paper addresses this gap by introducing a provably fresh benchmark and a method to push LLMs toward genuine causal reasoning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243063/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a causal reasoning question (asking for a cause or effect) paired with a background context passage drawn from recent news articles, and produces a selection from multiple-choice options. CausalProbe-2024 provides three difficulty tiers: CausalProbe-E (single correct answer), CausalProbe-H (includes fabricated false cause-effect pairs as distractors), and CausalProbe-M (1–4 correct answers per question to prevent random guessing). The G²-Reasoner additionally consumes retrieved passages from a ~16 MB general-knowledge Q&A vector database before generating its answer.

## Method

The paper contributes two main components. First, CausalProbe-2024 is constructed by downloading BBC and Guardian articles from January–April 2024 (post-dating all studied LLMs' training cut-offs) and using GPT-3.5 Turbo to generate Q&A pairs under three strategies of increasing difficulty; freshness is further verified via Min-K% Prob membership inference. Second, G²-Reasoner formalizes the causal reasoning task with a structural causal model (cause X → effect Y, confounded by world-knowledge C, observed through language T as a conditioned collider) and implements a two-module pipeline: (1) a RAG system using Faiss and Meta's Contriever to retrieve relevant general knowledge from a small Q&A knowledge base, and (2) a goal-oriented prompt that steers the LLM to iteratively verify causal relationships against the retrieved knowledge and the context before committing to a final answer. Experiments across LLaMA 2/3, GPT-3.5 Turbo, and Claude 3 Opus show that G²-Reasoner consistently outperforms vanilla, CoT, and RAG-only baselines, with the largest gains on the fresh CausalProbe-2024 benchmarks where all models otherwise drop significantly.

## Results

On the freshest benchmark CausalProbe-H, even the strongest model (Claude 3 Opus) achieves only 69.2% exact match under vanilla inference, while G²-Reasoner lifts it to 69.6%. On CausalProbe-E, G²-Reasoner achieves 64.2% (LLaMA 2), 71.8% (LLaMA 3), 74.6% (GPT 3.5), and 75.8% (Claude 3), consistently outperforming vanilla, CoT, and RAG baselines. Performance drops monotonically as benchmark corpora become fresher: e.g., Claude 3 Opus goes from 99.1% on COPA to 69.2% on CausalProbe-H, confirming that LLMs rely primarily on level-1 (parametric) causal knowledge rather than genuine level-2 reasoning. On CausalProbe-M (uncertain multiple-choice), all models struggle under exact match, though GPT and Claude reach ~75% and ~85% under partial match.

## Baselines & Comparisons

G²-Reasoner is compared against two baselines: Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting [Wei et al., NeurIPS 2022] and Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) [Lewis et al., NeurIPS 2020]. On the freshest benchmarks (CausalProbe-E and CausalProbe-H), G²-Reasoner consistently outperforms both baselines across all four LLMs; for example, on CausalProbe-H with GPT 3.5, G²-Reasoner reaches 0.693 EM versus 0.667 (CoT) and 0.678 (RAG). Notably, CoT often performs *worse* than vanilla on the older, more familiar benchmarks (e.g., LLaMA 3 drops from 0.857 to 0.767 on CausalNet), suggesting that step-by-step reasoning does not help when the task is already well-covered by parametric knowledge. RAG, which the authors note is essentially an ablated version of G²-Reasoner (lacking the goal-oriented prompt), frequently underperforms even vanilla inference (e.g., 0.704 vs. 0.715 for LLaMA 3 on CausalProbe-E), highlighting that retrieval alone without goal-directed prompting can be counterproductive. The improvements from G²-Reasoner are modest in absolute terms (typically 1–3 EM points), which the authors attribute to the small (~16 MB) general knowledge base used; they note that a larger knowledge base (e.g., Wikipedia) would yield substantially larger gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the causal-reasoning-in-LLMs literature (COPA, e-CARE, CausalNet, CausalQA, CRAB) by introducing a freshness-controlled evaluation axis: prior benchmarks predate LLM training cutoffs, so high scores may reflect memorization rather than reasoning. Methodologically, it connects the autoregressive next-token-prediction paradigm to Hume's distinction between sequential and logical causality, formalized via a structural causal model with a conditioned collider, to argue that the architecture is not inherently causal. The proposed G²-Reasoner repurposes retrieval-augmented generation and goal-oriented prompting—both well-established techniques—for the specific sub-problem of steering causal inference, rather than introducing a new architectural component.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The level-1/level-2 causal-reasoning taxonomy and the freshness-based benchmarking protocol offer the field a clearer diagnostic for separating parametric recall from genuine inference, which is critical as LLMs are increasingly deployed in safety-sensitive causal decision-making. However, G²-Reasoner's gains are modest (1–3 EM points), and the authors explicitly acknowledge that the method does not achieve level-2 reasoning, leaving the core question—how to enable LLMs to derive novel causal knowledge—open. A key limitation is that 'freshness' is only partially guaranteed (similar events may exist in training data), and the small 16 MB knowledge base constrains the practical ceiling of the RAG component.
