## Problem

No-reference Image Exposure Assessment (IEA) suffers from two long-standing gaps: existing methods produce only a single holistic score per image, making them unable to diagnose complex scenes where overexposure and underexposure coexist in different regions, and their reliance on scenario-specific subjective criteria limits generalizability across devices and lighting conditions. No prior work has attempted pixel-level exposure evaluation, leaving a critical precision gap for practical camera and enhancement pipelines.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20244182/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image (no reference required) at 256×256 or larger and produces a pixel-level exposure residual map where each pixel value encodes the signed deviation from ideal exposure (values near −1 indicate overexposure, near +1 indicate underexposure, and 0 indicates correct exposure). This residual map can be trivially aggregated (e.g., mean absolute value mapped to 0–10) to yield a holistic IEA score, enabling the same model to serve both fine-grained and coarse-grained assessment without retraining. The entire network uses only 2.7 M parameters and runs inference in ~0.083 s on an RTX 3090.

## Method

P-IEANet exploits the observation that Haar Discrete Wavelet Transform cleanly separates an image's exposure into a low-frequency component (lightness) and high-frequency components (structure), with the latter remaining largely invariant across exposure levels. The Structure Feature Module processes the high-frequency sub-bands through multi-directional gradient maps fed into a Transformer-based long-range encoder and a CNN-based short-range encoder, capturing both global layout and local texture cues. The Lightness Feature Module operates on the low-frequency approximation via a channel-attention branch (global average pooling + MLP) and a multi-scale pixel-attention branch (self-attention + multi-kernel convolutions) to model both global brightness distribution and localized exposure anomalies. A Prediction Module recombines the two feature streams through an inverse Haar DWT (implemented as four dedicated convolution kernels) and an MLP to regress the per-pixel exposure residual, trained end-to-end with L1 loss against expert-annotated residuals from the newly introduced IEA40K dataset (40 K images, 17 lighting scenarios, 27 devices, 50+ scenes, pixel-level labels from 10+ experts using Adams' zone system). This frequency-domain decomposition makes the learned features criteria-agnostic, allowing the same pixel-level output to be mapped to different manufacturers' scoring rubrics without fine-tuning.

## Results

On the pixel-level IEA task (IEA40K), P-IEANet achieves MAE of 0.03 and SSIM of 0.75, surpassing the second-best method (LIT: MAE 0.05, SSIM 0.60) by 40% in MAE and 25% in SSIM while using only 2.7M parameters (vs. 146M for LIT, a 97% reduction). On the holistic IEA task (SPAQ), P-IEANet achieves LRCC 0.78 and SRCC 0.73, outperforming the strongest baseline Q-align (7B params, LRCC 0.75, SRCC 0.70). Notably, even without fine-tuning for holistic scoring, P-IEANet reaches LRCC 0.69 and SRCC 0.65, surpassing several fine-tuned baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

P-IEANet decisively outperforms all 12 pixel-level baselines on both MAE and SSIM. Among light enhancement/awareness methods, LIT [51] is the closest competitor (MAE 0.05, SSIM 0.60), but P-IEANet still leads by 40% in MAE and 25% in SSIM with 54× fewer parameters. Among IQA/IAA methods, PyDiff [48] (374M params) and ArnIQA [52] (106M params) achieve the best SSIM (0.50), yet P-IEANet exceeds them by 50% in SSIM. On the holistic SPAQ benchmark, P-IEANet (LRCC 0.78, SRCC 0.73) beats all 18 baselines including the 7B-parameter Q-align [60] (LRCC 0.75, SRCC 0.70) and TANet [58] (LRCC 0.75, SRCC 0.70). The ablation confirms that removing the Haar DWT, Structure module, or Lightness module degrades SSIM by 42.6%, 28.0%, and 44.0% respectively, validating each component's contribution.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of no-reference image quality assessment and photographic exposure theory. Prior IEA work is split between statistical-feature methods (histogram, entropy, gradient-based) that assume a single distortion type, and data-driven methods trained on holistic human scores (e.g., SPAQ) that lack fine-grained supervision. The authors repurpose the Haar DWT—a classical signal-processing tool—as the architectural backbone, motivated by the observation that low-frequency components encode lightness while high-frequency components encode structure, a decomposition aligned with Adams' zone system theory from classical photography. No prior IEA method operates at pixel granularity; the closest analogues (DiffuMask, PixelLM) address pixel-level tasks in segmentation and VQA but are inapplicable to exposure assessment due to fundamentally different annotation requirements.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The IEA40K dataset (40K images, 17 lighting scenarios, 27 devices, pixel-level expert annotations) and the 19-method benchmark fill a clear gap in the exposure-assessment literature, giving the community a standardized evaluation protocol that was previously absent. The practical utility extends beyond assessment: P-IEANet's exposure residual can serve as a plug-and-play loss to improve existing low-light enhancement pipelines (demonstrated with Retinex and GASD). Open questions remain around high-speed moving objects (where multi-exposure alignment fails), the impact of input resolution on pixel-level accuracy, and whether the paradigm extends to AIGC-generated content where 'exposure' may not have a physical camera analogue.
