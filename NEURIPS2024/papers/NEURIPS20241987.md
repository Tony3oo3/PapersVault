## Problem

Transductive inference, which jointly predicts all test samples by exploiting the structure of unlabeled data, has been largely overlooked in Vision-Language Models. Existing transductive few-shot methods rely solely on visual features and ignore the text-encoder knowledge inherent to VLMs, causing them to underperform even inductive zero-shot CLIP predictions when applied directly to multi-modal settings. This paper asks how to design a transductive objective that properly integrates text supervision and scales to large-scale datasets.

## Input / Output

TransCLIP operates entirely in the output embedding space (black-box setting), consuming pre-computed normalized image embeddings f_i from any vision encoder and normalized text class embeddings t_k from any text encoder, plus optionally a small set of labeled support samples in the few-shot setting. It produces soft class-assignment vectors z_i on the K-dimensional probability simplex for each query sample, from which hard predictions are extracted via argmax. Because no model weights or gradients are accessed, the method is compatible with API-only models and multi-billion-parameter encoders, requiring only single-sample inference memory.

## Method

TransCLIP minimizes a three-term objective: a GMM-clustering likelihood with a shared diagonal covariance across classes, a Laplacian regularizer built on a sparse cosine-similarity affinity matrix, and a KL divergence penalty that anchors predictions to the zero-shot text-based softmax scores (the key differentiator from vision-only transductive methods). Optimization proceeds via a Block Majorize-Minimize procedure with three alternating sub-steps: a decoupled per-sample z-update derived from KKT conditions on a tight linear majorizer of the concave Laplacian term, and closed-form updates for the class means μ and covariance Σ. The decoupled z-updates yield parallelizable, sample-wise computation that scales to ImageNet in ~14 seconds, while the KL term's convexity in z guarantees convergence of the overall BMM loop. In the few-shot extension, a cross-entropy term over labeled support samples is added with a weighting hyperparameter γ, and the text-regularization strength λ is reduced to 0.5 to balance visual and textual supervision.

## Results

TransCLIP-ZS improves the zero-shot CLIP-ViT-B/16 average top-1 accuracy from 65.3% to 70.3% (+5.1%) across 11 datasets, with the largest gains on EuroSAT (+16.8%) and SUN397 (+6.3%). Applied on top of 1-shot few-shot methods, it adds 3.7–4.8% average (e.g., CoOp 67.6%→72.4%, TIP-Adapter-F 70.9%→75.1%), while gains shrink to ~1% at 16 shots. In the transductive few-shot setting (Table 4), TransCLIP-FS achieves 73.2% average at 1-shot, massively outperforming the best vision-only transductive baseline TIM (52.8%). The method runs in 14.4 seconds on ImageNet (50K samples), versus >150 minutes for the transductive prompt-learning baseline UPL*.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TransCLIP-ZS consistently outperforms the inductive zero-shot CLIP baseline by 3.7–16.8% per dataset (avg +5.1%), with the largest relative gains on fine-grained and satellite datasets (EuroSAT +16.8%, SUN397 +6.3%). On top of 1-shot few-shot methods (CoOp, TIP-Adapter-F, PLOT, TaskRes, ProGrad), TransCLIP-ZS adds 3.7–4.8% average, while at 16 shots gains shrink to ~1% or occasionally turn negative (e.g., PLOT on EuroSAT drops 7.5%), suggesting the unlabeled-data structure is already partially captured by the shots. In the direct transductive few-shot comparison (Table 4), TransCLIP-FS dominates all vision-only transductive baselines: at 1-shot it reaches 73.2% average vs. TIM 52.8%, LaplacianShot 50.9%, PT-MAP 52.7%, BD-CSPN 50.6%, and TF 42.8%; at 4-shot it reaches 75.9% vs. TIM 71.3%. Ablating the text-guided KL term (λ=0) drops 1-shot performance from 73.2% to 50.2%, confirming that the language constraint is the critical differentiator over vision-only transduction. In runtime, TransCLIP-ZS completes ImageNet inference in 14.4 seconds versus >150 minutes for transductive UPL*, a 2–3 order-of-magnitude speedup, while also achieving higher accuracy (70.3% vs. 69.8%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper introduces transductive inference (joint prediction over all test samples) to the Vision-Language Model context, a paradigm that was previously explored only for vision-only few-shot classifiers (LaplacianShot, TIM, PT-MAP, BD-CSPN, TF) and largely overlooked for VLMs. The method builds on CLIP's dual-encoder architecture by adding a text-guided KL divergence penalty to a GMM-clustering + Laplacian-regularization objective, and derives a Block Majorize-Minimize optimizer (following Razaviyayn et al.) that yields decoupled, parallelizable sample-assignment updates. Unlike prompt-tuning approaches (CoOp, ProGrad, PLOT) that require backpropagation through the text encoder, TransCLIP operates entirely in the output embedding space as a black-box plug-and-play module, making it orthogonal to and composable with any inductive zero- or few-shot method.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper reveals that existing transductive few-shot methods fail dramatically in the VLM setting (often underperforming plain zero-shot CLIP), identifying the absence of text-encoder knowledge as the root cause. The text-guided KL penalty is shown to be the critical success factor, and the resulting method is both highly efficient (~15 seconds on ImageNet vs. >150 minutes for prompt-learning baselines) and scalable to multi-billion-parameter models without gradient computation. Open questions include whether the fixed λ can be made adaptive to shot count and text-embedding quality, and whether the diminishing transductive gains at higher shot counts (16-shot) can be recovered. The plug-and-play, black-box design also makes TransCLIP applicable to API-only models, broadening its practical reach.
