## Problem

Standard MPNNs are bounded by 1-WL expressivity and cannot count cycles, while higher-order WL variants (e.g., 3-WL) that can count longer cycles suffer from exponential computational cost that ignores graph sparsity. The paper asks whether one can design a scalable GNN hierarchy that provably counts cycles of arbitrary length and homomorphism-counts cactus graphs while respecting the sparsity of real-world graphs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243837/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a graph with node features and edge features as input. During preprocessing, r-neighborhoods (simple paths of length ≤ r connecting distinct neighbors of each node, excluding the node itself) are computed via NetworkX; the forward pass then scales linearly with the total number of such paths rather than with the number of all possible paths from each node. The output is a graph-level embedding (via sum readout) used for classification, regression, or subgraph/homomorphism counting tasks.

## Method

The core algorithm, r-ℓ WL, augments the standard 1-WL color update by hashing not only the multiset of neighbor colors but also the multisets of color sequences along all simple paths of length k (for k = 1, …, r) connecting distinct neighbors of each node. The corresponding GNN, r-ℓ GIN, implements this by processing each path with a (shared) GIN layer—chosen because GIN is maximally expressive on paths—and aggregating the resulting path embeddings via an MLP with learnable per-length scaling factors (1+ε_k). This design yields a strict hierarchy: r-ℓ WL is provably more expressive than (r−1)-ℓ WL, can subgraph-count all cycles up to length r+2, and can homomorphism-count all cactus graphs with cycles up to length r+2, while remaining incomparable to any fixed k-WL. The forward complexity is O(|E| + Σ_v Σ_k 2k|N_k(v)|), which is near-linear for sparse graphs, making it roughly 30× faster per epoch than 3-WL GNN on ZINC12K.

## Results

On ZINC12K, 5-ℓGIN achieves a test MAE of 0.072±0.002, outperforming all 13 baselines including DRFWL (0.077), CIN (0.079), and the standard GIN (0.163). On ZINC250K, 5-ℓGIN reaches 0.022±0.001 MAE, matching CIN and beating I2-GNN (0.023), DRFWL (0.025), and NestedGNN (0.029). On QM9, 5-ℓGIN is best or near-best across all 12 molecular properties (e.g., 0.00205 on ε_homo, 0.000127 on ZVPE). On the BREC isomorphism benchmark, 4-ℓGIN distinguishes 257/400 non-isomorphic pairs, surpassing PPGN (233/400) and NestedGNN (166/400).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On ZINC12K, 5-ℓGIN (0.072 MAE) outperforms every baseline, with the closest competitors being DRFWL (0.077), CIN (0.079), and GNNAK+ (0.080); it improves over the standard GIN by 226%. On ZINC250K, 5-ℓGIN (0.022) ties CIN and edges out I2-GNN (0.023) and DRFWL (0.025), while substantially beating NestedGNN (0.029) and HIMP (0.036). On QM9, 5-ℓGIN achieves the best normalized MAE on 9 of 12 properties (e.g., ε_homo: 0.00205 vs. DRFWL 0.00226; ZVPE: 0.000127 vs. I2-GNN 0.00014), though it trails on R² (13.21 vs. 1-GNN 34.1). On the BREC isomorphism benchmark, 4-ℓGIN separates 257/400 pairs, exceeding PPGN (233/400, theoretically 3-WL equivalent) and NestedGNN (166/400), and matching 3-WL on Basic (60/60) while surpassing it on Regular (100/140 vs. 50/140). On SUBGRAPHCOUNT, r-ℓGIN achieves the lowest MAE for all six subgraph-counting motifs (e.g., 0.0003 for 4-cycles vs. 0.020 for Local 2-FGNN) and all three homomorphism-counting motifs. On PEPTIDES (long-range benchmark), 7-ℓGIN improves STRUCT MAE from 0.3420 (GatedGCN) to 0.2513 and FUNC AP from 59.30 (GCN) to 65.70.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the classical 1-WL color refinement algorithm by augmenting each node's neighborhood with simple paths between its distinct neighbors, yielding the r-ℓ WL hierarchy and its GNN counterpart r-ℓ GIN. It builds directly on the GIN architecture (Xu et al., 2019) as the path-processing layer within the MPNN framework (Gilmer et al., 2017), but departs fundamentally from the k-WL hierarchy: rather than considering k-tuple interactions (exponential cost), it collects messages along paths of bounded length, preserving linear complexity on sparse graphs. The approach is conceptually related to PathNN (Michel et al., 2023) but differs by restricting to paths between neighbors rather than all paths from a node, and provides provable cycle-counting and cactus-graph homomorphism-counting guarantees that PathNN lacks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key theoretical contribution is proving that r-ℓ WL is incomparable to k-WL for any fixed k, establishing a genuinely new axis of expressivity measurement orthogonal to the standard hierarchy. This opens the question of whether tight characterizations of r-ℓ WL's homomorphism-counting power exist beyond the cactus-graph lower bound. Practically, the method offers a scalable path to cycle-counting expressivity on sparse graphs (molecular, social networks), but its O(Nd^r) path-enumeration cost limits applicability to dense graphs. The paper also leaves open whether the homomorphism-counting inductive bias translates to improved generalization beyond the observed ablation results on ZINC12K.
