## Problem

Existing continuous unbalanced entropic optimal transport (UEOT) solvers are either heuristic (relying on discrete OT approximations) or heavyweight, requiring multiple neural networks trained via complex GAN-style max-min optimization. No fast, lightweight, and theoretically justified solver for the continuous UEOT problem had been developed, leaving a gap for a simple baseline that handles class imbalance and outliers without prohibitive computational cost.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242977/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes only i.i.d. empirical samples {x_1,...,x_N} from source measure p and {y_1,...,y_M} from target measure q (the measures themselves are never explicitly available). It produces the full UEOT plan density γ_{θ,ω}(x,y) as a product of a Gaussian-mixture marginal u_ω(x) and a closed-form conditional γ_θ(y|x), enabling both out-of-sample generation of y|x_new and sampling from the marginal γ*_x. In the image translation experiments, inputs are 512-dimensional latent codes from a pre-trained ALAE autoencoder on 1024×1024 FFHQ faces, and outputs are translated latent codes that can be decoded back to images.

## Method

The key insight is that minimizing the KL divergence between the true UEOT plan γ* and a parametrized plan γ_{θ,ω} can be reformulated as minimizing a tractable, non-minimax upper bound L(θ,ω) that depends only on expectations under p and q, eliminating the need to know γ*. The plan is parametrized as γ_{θ,ω}(x,y) = u_ω(x) · γ_θ(y|x), where both u_ω and v_θ (which defines the conditional) are unnormalized Gaussian mixtures with K and L components respectively. This parametrization yields closed-form expressions for the normalization constant c_θ(x) and the conditional γ_θ(y|x), making both training (via minibatch SGD on the empirical objective) and inference (direct sampling from Gaussians) lightweight. The paper proves universal approximation of UEOT plans by Gaussian mixtures and establishes O(1/√N) + O(1/√M) generalization bounds, and shows the method subsumes the balanced LightSB solver as a special case.

## Results

On the FFHQ unpaired image-to-image translation task (d=512 latent space), U-LightOT achieves the best accuracy of preserving input class attributes (keep accuracy) among all unbalanced solvers while maintaining competitive target-domain mapping accuracy. In terms of wall-clock time, U-LightOT completes the Young→Adult translation in 2 min 38 s, compared to 3 min 21 s for UOT-FM, 16 min 30 s for UOT-GAN, and 18 min 11 s for UOT-SD. The unbalancedness parameter τ controls the keep/target trade-off: at τ=1 the solver truly learns unbalanced plans, while at τ=10² it recovers balanced EOT plans equivalent to LightSB.

## Baselines & Comparisons

U-LightOT outperforms all unbalanced baselines on the keep-accuracy metric (preserving gender/age class during translation) while maintaining good target-domain accuracy, as shown in the accuracy trade-off plots (Fig. 3). Compared to UOT-FM [16], U-LightOT achieves superior keep accuracy at comparable or better target accuracy across the range of unbalancedness parameters, and is ~25% faster (2:38 vs 3:21). Against the GAN-based solvers UOT-SD [9] and UOT-GAN [70], U-LightOT is 6–7× faster and avoids the training instabilities reported for adversarial approaches; the GAN baselines show unstable behavior and are reported at only a single parameter value. Versus the balanced solvers LightSB [41] and OT-FM [44], U-LightOT at small τ (e.g., τ=1) achieves substantially better keep accuracy by relaxing marginal constraints, directly addressing class imbalance (e.g., adult men outnumbering adult women in FFHQ). At large τ (τ=10²), U-LightOT's results converge to those of LightSB, confirming that the balanced case is a special case of the unbalanced formulation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Gaussian mixture parametrization introduced in Light Schrödinger Bridge (LightSB, Korotin et al., ICLR 2024) from the balanced EOT setting to the unbalanced EOT (UEOT) problem. It departs from the dominant paradigm of prior continuous UEOT solvers—GAN-based semi-dual formulations [70, 9] and flow-matching-on-discrete-OT approaches [16, 36]—by deriving a non-minimax optimization objective directly from the dual UEOT problem via Fenchel conjugate analysis. The paper explicitly shows that its objective subsumes LightSB's balanced objective as a special case, positioning itself as a natural unbalanced generalization of that line of work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The solver provides a theoretically grounded, CPU-friendly baseline for continuous UEOT that converges in minutes rather than hours, which could lower the barrier to adopting unbalanced transport in practical pipelines. The universal approximation guarantee and generalization bounds are valuable, but the Gaussian mixture restriction on both the marginal and conditional densities is a clear limitation for highly multimodal or complex target distributions. Open questions include whether the approach scales to very high-dimensional latent spaces beyond d=512, how sensitive performance is to the number of mixture components in practice, and whether the non-minimax objective can be extended to non-quadratic cost functions.
