## Problem

Existing safe MARL methods (e.g., CMIX, MAPPO-L) rely on centralized value functions over the global state, creating global coupling from safety constraints and exponential state-action space growth that bottleneck scalability in communication- or compute-constrained systems. No prior method simultaneously guarantees both safety constraint satisfaction and monotonic joint policy improvement under a fully decentralized learning framework where agents only access local neighborhood information.

## Input / Output

Each agent i consumes only the local state of itself and its κ-hop graph neighbors (s_{N_κ^i}) along with its own action a^i, never requiring the global state or other agents' actions during training. The output is a set of updated neural-network policy parameters θ_κ^i defining a κ-hop policy π_κ^i(a^i | s_{N_κ^i}). Agents are updated sequentially (agent 1, then 2, …, n), and the practical algorithm replaces the exact KL-divergence trust-region constraint with a PPO-clip ratio bound and Lagrangian multipliers for per-agent cost constraints, making each update a standard first-order gradient step.

## Method

The method builds on spatial correlation decay: under a Dobrushin condition on transition dynamics (Assumption 2.1) and an exponential-decay condition on policies (Assumption 2.2), the advantage function's dependence on distant agents decays as ηφ^κ, bounding the information loss from truncating to κ-hop neighborhoods. This bound is combined with the trust-region policy optimization lower bound to yield a per-agent local surrogate objective (Eq. 13) that includes a KL-penalty term and an explicit truncation-error offset η′φ^κ. A sequential update scheme is then shown (Theorem 3.7) to guarantee both monotonic joint return improvement and satisfaction of all per-agent cost constraints, provided each agent's KL step size δ_κ^i is chosen to respect the remaining cost budget. The practical algorithm Scal-MAPPO-L parameterizes each local policy with a neural network, converts the constrained problem into a min-max Lagrangian form, and replaces the KL constraint with a PPO-clip operator, so each agent independently optimizes a clipped surrogate that depends only on its own action and κ-hop neighbor states.

## Results

On three Safe ManyAgent Ant tasks (2×3, 3×2, 6×1), Scal-MAPPO-L achieves nearly identical cost and reward performance to MAPPO-L (which uses the full global state) while only accessing the states of approximately half the agents via κ-hop truncation. Both Scal-MAPPO-L and MAPPO-L exhibit significantly fewer constraint violations and higher rewards compared to IPPO and HAPPO across all tasks. In the κ-sensitivity study on Safe ManyAgent Ant (6×1), Safe Ant (8×1), and Safe Coupled HalfCheetah (12×1), performance is lowest at κ=1 but improves considerably for κ≥3; at κ=6 on Safe Ant (8×1), Scal-MAPPO-L even outperforms MAPPO-L, suggesting that global-state access can introduce noise that degrades learning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against IPPO [20] and HAPPO [16], both Scal-MAPPO-L and MAPPO-L clearly win on both cost (fewer constraint violations) and reward across all three Safe ManyAgent Ant tasks, confirming the effectiveness of the constrained (Lagrangian) formulation over unconstrained PPO-family baselines. Against MAPPO-L [10], Scal-MAPPO-L achieves almost identical performance while requiring only local (κ-hop) state access rather than the global state, demonstrating that the spatial correlation decay assumption holds in practice. In the κ-sensitivity experiments, Scal-MAPPO-L with κ=1 underperforms MAPPO-L substantially (highest cost, lowest reward), but for κ≥3 the gap closes and in some settings (e.g., κ=6 on Safe Ant 8×1) Scal-MAPPO-L slightly outperforms MAPPO-L, likely because the global-state baseline struggles to extract useful signal from many agents' messages. Overall, the paper's method matches or beats all three baselines while reducing communication and computation to a local neighborhood.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two research threads: (1) safe MARL methods like MAPPO-L and CMIX that use centralized value functions over global state to handle safety constraints, and (2) scalable MARL methods (Qu et al., Ying et al.) that exploit spatial correlation decay to enable local, decentralized learning. The key departure is combining the trust region framework from TRPO/HAPPO with the κ-hop truncation from scalable MARL to derive a local policy optimization objective per agent that simultaneously guarantees monotonic joint policy improvement and safety constraint satisfaction. Prior scalable safe MARL work (Ying et al., 2023) used primal-dual gradient methods without trust region guarantees, while prior safe MARL (MAPPO-L) lacked scalability; this paper bridges both gaps with a Lagrangian PPO-clip formulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The main contribution is a theoretical guarantee—monotonic improvement plus safety constraint satisfaction—that holds under fully decentralized training with only local (κ-hop) information, a property no prior safe MARL method provides. This opens the door to safe multi-agent systems in communication-constrained settings (drone swarms, vehicle fleets) where global state access is impractical. However, the practical algorithm relies on approximations (PPO-clip replacing KL constraints, sampled advantages) that the authors acknowledge may break the theoretical guarantees, and experiments are confined to Safe MAMuJoCo with at most 12 agents. Open questions include whether the spatial correlation decay assumptions hold in real-world deployments, how the method behaves under non-stationary environments, and whether the sequential update scheme introduces unacceptable latency for large agent counts.
