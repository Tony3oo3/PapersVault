## Problem

Existing image-based reasoning segmentation models (e.g., LISA) cannot handle video object segmentation because they lack temporal understanding on the input side and cannot produce temporally consistent masks on the output side. Additionally, no benchmark exists that jointly evaluates complex reasoning, temporal dynamics, and object tracking in video segmentation, leaving a gap in assessing whether LLM-based models can truly reason over video content.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240219/cropped_page17_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a video (uniformly sampled into T_sparse=32 frames, of which T_dense=4 retain full-resolution visual tokens while the rest are pooled to single tokens) and a free-form language instruction that may require multi-step reasoning, world knowledge, or temporal comprehension. It produces a single <TRK> token embedding from the LLM, which is then fed to SAM's mask decoder to generate per-frame binary segmentation masks across the entire video. During inference, all video frames are segmented one-by-one using the same prompt embedding, and an optional XMem++ post-optimization step refines masks for frames not seen at full resolution.

## Method

VideoLISA couples a 3.8B-parameter LLaVA-Phi-3-V LLM with SAM's vision encoder and promptable mask decoder, connected via a special <TRK> token added to the LLM vocabulary. Two key innovations address video-specific challenges: (1) Sparse Dense Sampling preserves full-resolution visual tokens for a subset of frames (dense) while reducing the remaining frames to single pooled tokens (sparse), balancing spatial detail needed for segmentation with temporal context needed for motion understanding; (2) One-Token-Seg-All trains the <TRK> token to segment multiple frames simultaneously during training, forcing it to encode semantic object identity rather than positional shortcuts, so that at inference a single token can track the target across all frames. The model is trained end-to-end on joint image and video segmentation data with a combined text-generation and segmentation loss (BCE + DICE), using LoRA for the LLM and full fine-tuning for the mask decoder.

## Results

On Refer-DAVIS-17, VideoLISA-3.8B with post-optimization achieves state-of-the-art J&F=68.8 (J=64.9, F=72.7), outperforming the previous best SgMg (63.3) by 5.5 points. On MeViS (motion-guided VOS), VideoLISA achieves J&F=44.4, surpassing LMPM (37.2) by 7.2 points. On the newly introduced ReasonVOS benchmark, VideoLISA reaches J&F=47.5, exceeding the best prior method OnlineRefer (38.7) by 8.8 points. On image reasoning segmentation (ReasonSeg val), VideoLISA-3.8B achieves gIoU=61.4 and cIoU=67.1, outperforming LISA-13B (ft) (56.2/62.9) despite using a 3.4× smaller LLM.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among LLM-based reasoning segmentation methods, VideoLISA-3.8B decisively outperforms all larger competitors: on Refer-DAVIS-17 it beats TrackGPT-13B (66.5 J&F) by 2.3 points and LISA-13B (60.7) by 8.1 points, while on Refer-YouTube-VOS it surpasses TrackGPT-13B (59.5) by 4.2 points. Against traditional RVOS methods, VideoLISA achieves SOTA on Refer-DAVIS-17 (68.8 vs. SgMg's 63.3 and OnlineRefer's 64.8), but SgMg retains a slight edge on Refer-YouTube-VOS (65.7 vs. 63.7), likely due to its dedicated Video-Swin backbone. On the motion-guided MeViS benchmark, VideoLISA (44.4 J&F) leads LMPM (37.2) by 7.2 points, and on the new ReasonVOS benchmark it exceeds OnlineRefer (38.7) by 8.8 points, demonstrating superior complex-reasoning and temporal-consistency capabilities. On image reasoning segmentation (ReasonSeg), VideoLISA-3.8B (61.4 gIoU val) outperforms LISA-13B (ft) (56.2) and LISA-7B (ft) (52.9), confirming that the video-oriented temporal module and One-Token-Seg-All training also benefit image tasks. The ablation in Table 6 further shows that One-Token-Seg-All (51.7 J&F on MeViS) substantially outperforms One-Token-Seg-One (46.1) and LISA+XMem (45.6), validating the necessity of multi-frame supervision for the <TRK> token.

## Relation to Prior Work

VideoLISA extends the LISA paradigm (MLLM + SAM + special segmentation token) from images to video, addressing the temporal dimension that image-based reasoning segmentation cannot handle. It departs from standard video-LLM temporal strategies (Q-Former compression, spatial-temporal pooling) by proposing Sparse Dense Sampling, which preserves full-resolution features for a subset of frames while using single-token representations for the rest. The One-Token-Seg-All approach further distinguishes itself from per-frame segmentation pipelines by training a single <TRK> token to serve as a unified spatiotemporal prompt for all frames, drawing on the observation that SAM's promptable decoder can function as a semantic kernel for cross-frame association when given sufficient temporal context.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work bridges a significant gap between the reasoning capabilities of MLLMs and the temporal consistency requirements of video segmentation, producing a model that handles complex language instructions (requiring world knowledge and multi-step reasoning) while maintaining temporally consistent masks. The ReasonVOS benchmark fills a notable evaluation gap for reasoning-based video segmentation. Key open questions remain: the computational cost of MLLM-based segmentation is substantially higher than dedicated RVOS methods, the model's chat capability degrades after segmentation fine-tuning, and integrating a video-pretrained backbone with the LLM+SAM pipeline is non-trivial. The finding that a 3.8B model outperforms 7B/13B LLM-based competitors suggests that architectural design for the video domain matters more than raw LLM scale for this task.
