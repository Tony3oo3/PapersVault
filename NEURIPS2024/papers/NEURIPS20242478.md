## Problem

State-of-the-art protein backbone generative models based on diffusion or flow matching with invariant point attention (IPA) often over-represent α-helices and fail to capture the natural statistical distribution of secondary structure elements (α-helices, β-strands, coils) found in native proteins. This limits the functional diversity of generated designs, since protein function is grounded in structural composition. The paper asks whether a more geometrically expressive attention mechanism can improve both designability and secondary structure fidelity simultaneously.

## Input / Output

The model takes a target protein length N (number of residues, up to 512 for PDB training and up to 128 for SCOPe ablations) as its sole input and generates a protein backbone as a set of N SE(3) frames, where each frame encodes the position and orientation of a residue defined by its [N, Cα, C] backbone atoms. The generation process is a flow matching ODE integrated over 200 timesteps on the Riemannian manifold SE(3)^N using geodesic interpolation. Designability is assessed by inverse-folding with ProteinMPNN (8 candidate sequences) and refolding with ESMFold, requiring scRMSD < 2.0 Å.

## Method

The core contribution is Clifford Frame Attention (CFA), which replaces the point-valued geometric features of AlphaFold2's IPA with multivectors in the projective geometric algebra G_{3,0,1}, where residue frames are represented as motors (even-grade multivectors encoding rotation-then-translation). Messages between residues are constructed via a geometric bilinear layer that computes both the geometric product and the join between node features, enabling the network to capture distances, angles, projections, and incidences in a single operation. Higher-order (3-body) messages are obtained by exploiting bilinearity to multiply aggregated two-body messages, yielding implicit many-body interactions. CFA is embedded within the FrameFlow architecture (flow matching on SE(3)^N with minibatch optimal transport), and a secondary-structure-aware checkpoint selection criterion balances designability against helix/strand content deviation from the training distribution.

## Results

On the PDB dataset (backbones of lengths 100–300), GAFL achieves 0.88 designability (scRMSD < 2.0 Å), 0.36 diversity (TM-score), and 0.71 novelty (TM-score to PDB), while generating backbones in 8.8 s per structure on an A100 GPU. Its secondary structure content (helix 0.53, strand 0.25) is the closest to the PDB reference (helix 0.39, strand 0.23) among all compared models, whereas FoldFlow over-represents helices (0.88–0.91) and nearly eliminates strands (0.00–0.01). On the smaller SCOPe-128 ablation, GAFL reaches 90.5% designability, outperforming the published FrameFlow (81.2%) by 9.3 percentage points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GAFL outperforms FrameDiff [68] on every metric: designability 0.88 vs 0.54, diversity 0.36 vs 0.45, and inference time 8.8 s vs 24.3 s. Against FoldFlow [10], GAFL achieves higher designability (0.88 vs 0.82 for the OT variant), substantially better diversity (0.36 vs 0.44), and far more realistic secondary structure content (helix 0.53 vs 0.88, strand 0.25 vs 0.00), while also being ~3× faster. Compared to FrameFlow [67] (same training dataset), GAFL improves designability from 0.85 to 0.88 and brings helix/strand content closer to the PDB reference, at a modest 2.2 s increase in inference time. Against RFdiffusion [63], GAFL trades a marginal 0.01 drop in designability (0.88 vs 0.89) for better diversity (0.36 vs 0.37), better novelty (0.71 vs 0.74), more balanced secondary structure (helix 0.53 vs 0.58), and 2.4× faster inference (8.8 s vs 21.0 s), all without relying on pre-trained folding weights. For large proteins (lengths up to 500), GAFL achieves 0.74 designability, surpassing VFN [42] (0.44), FrameFlow (0.64), and RFdiffusion (0.71).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the FrameFlow/FrameDiff flow matching framework for protein backbone generation by replacing the Invariant Point Attention (IPA) block from AlphaFold2 with a new Clifford Frame Attention (CFA) mechanism. CFA draws on the projective geometric algebra (PGA) formalism from Brehmer et al.'s Geometric Algebra Transformer to represent residue frames and geometric features as multivectors, enabling geometric bilinear operations (geometric product and join) for message construction. The higher-order message passing is inspired by MACE's use of bilinearity to construct N-body interactions from aggregated two-body messages. The paper positions itself as an architectural drop-in replacement for IPA, which is the central component in most current protein design models (RFdiffusion, FrameDiff, FoldFlow, FrameFlow).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CFA's practical significance lies in its compatibility with the widely-used IPA architecture: since IPA appears in nearly all current protein backbone generation models, CFA could be adopted broadly without redesigning entire pipelines. The paper's key practical finding is that GAFL achieves high designability while preserving the natural distribution of secondary structure elements (particularly β-strands), addressing a known failure mode of competitors like RFdiffusion and FoldFlow that over-represent α-helices. Limitations include unconditional generation only, imperfect secondary structure capture for large proteins (>300 residues), and the need for a secondary-structure-aware checkpoint selection procedure. Open questions include whether CFA transfers to conditional settings (motif scaffolding, symmetry constraints) and other protein-related tasks beyond backbone generation.
