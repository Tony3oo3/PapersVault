## Problem

Fine-grained circuit analysis of transformer LLMs is blocked by MLP sublayers: SAE features are dense linear combinations of many neurons, and standard attribution methods (causal patching, input-times-gradient) yield only input-dependent connections that cannot describe the general input-output behavior of an MLP across all inputs. This prevents researchers from making input-invariant claims about how features connect through MLPs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240768/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For training, transcoders consume post-LayerNorm MLP input activations and the corresponding MLP output activations from a text corpus (OpenWebText, 60M tokens, 128-token contexts). For circuit analysis, a text prompt is run through the model with all activations cached; the method then produces a set of attributed computational paths (sequences of transcoder features and attention heads) that can be merged into a circuit subgraph. A distinctive output is the de-embedding vector for each feature (W_E^T f_enc), which gives an input-invariant score for every token in the vocabulary indicating how strongly that token would directly cause the feature to fire.

## Method

A transcoder is a wide single-hidden-layer ReLU MLP (d_features >> d_model) trained with a loss combining MSE faithfulness to the original MLP sublayer's output and an L1 sparsity penalty on hidden activations. The key insight is that because the transcoder's decoder is linear, the attribution from an earlier-layer feature i to a later-layer feature i' factorizes cleanly into an input-dependent term (the activation z_TC^(l,i)) and an input-invariant term (the dot product f_dec^(l,i) · f_enc^(l',i')), the latter being computable purely from trained weights and describing the MLP's general behavior. Circuit discovery uses a greedy recursive algorithm that propagates a feature vector backward through the computational graph: at each transcoder node the vector is updated via (f' · f_dec) f_enc, and at each attention head via score · (W_OV^T f'), with LayerNorm approximated as a constant scaling. This yields attributed paths that are merged into a circuit subgraph where each node and edge carries a total attribution, enabling both input-dependent (which features fire on this input) and input-invariant (which features are generally connected) analysis.

## Results

In a blind interpretability evaluation on Pythia-410M layer 15, transcoders yielded 41 interpretable features (out of 50) versus 38 for SAEs, with only 1 uninterpretable feature compared to 4 for SAEs. On the sparsity-accuracy Pareto frontier (measured via mean L0 norm and cross-entropy loss on 3.2M OpenWebText tokens), transcoders match or exceed SAEs across GPT2-small (120M), Pythia-410M, and Pythia-1.4B, with the gap widening on larger models. On the GPT2-small greater-than circuit, the transcoder approach outperforms the neuronal (MLP neuron) approach for fewer than 24 features, recovering most of the original model's mean probability difference performance with only a handful of features.

|---|---|---|
| # interpretable | 41 | 38 |
| # maybe interpretable | 8 | 8 |
| # uninterpretable | 1 | 4 |

## Baselines & Comparisons

Compared to SAEs (Bricken et al. [7]), transcoders achieve equal or better performance on the sparsity-accuracy Pareto frontier across all three model sizes (GPT2-small, Pythia-410M, Pythia-1.4B), with the advantage growing on larger models. On the qualitative interpretability metric (Table 1), transcoders identify 41 interpretable features versus 38 for SAEs, and have fewer uninterpretable features (1 vs. 4), though SAEs identify more 'context-free' features (16 vs. 6). Compared to the neuronal approach of Hanna et al. [25] on the greater-than circuit, the transcoder method outperforms MLP neurons for all k < 24 features in the zero-ablation sweep, requiring far fewer components to recover the model's mean probability difference metric. The authors note that compute limitations (shared learning rate, limited hyperparameter sweeps) may have disadvantaged the SAE baseline, so the SAE comparison is not fully conclusive.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper develops the transcoder concept previously sketched but not explored by Templeton et al. [47] ('predicting future activations') and Li et al. [31] ('MLP stretchers') into a full circuit analysis methodology. It builds directly on the SAE framework of Bricken et al. [7] and the transformer circuit formalism of Elhage et al. [15], but departs from SAE-based circuit analysis by training on MLP input-output behavior rather than activation reconstruction, which enables a clean factorization of attributions into input-dependent and input-invariant terms. The greater-than circuit analysis extends the neuronal-level study of Hanna et al. [25] to the feature level, and the recursive greedy path-finding algorithm draws on Dunefsky & Cohan [13].

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The input-invariant factorization is the key conceptual contribution: it allows researchers to make general claims about how MLP sublayers transform features across all inputs, something SAE-based methods fundamentally cannot do. However, the paper is limited to models up to 1.4B parameters, treats attention QK scores as fixed (preventing analysis of how features influence attention patterns), and presents only a handful of qualitative case studies rather than systematic evaluation. Open questions include whether transcoders scale to frontier models, whether their feature spaces differ qualitatively from SAEs, and whether the method can be extended to decompose attention computation itself.
