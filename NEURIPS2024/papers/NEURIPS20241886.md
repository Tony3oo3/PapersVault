## Problem

Diffusion models generate high-quality images but require hundreds of iterative denoising steps through a neural network, resulting in significant latency and energy consumption on digital hardware like GPUs. While algorithmic approaches reduce the number of steps, the repetitive nature of the denoising computation remains a hardware bottleneck. This paper asks whether the denoising step itself can be performed by passive optical components, exploiting the near-zero energy cost and high bandwidth of light propagation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241886/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes a noisy 20×20 grayscale image pattern, encoded as amplitude modulation on a digital micromirror device (DMD), and produces the predicted noise term ε_θ as an intensity pattern on a 130×130-pixel CMOS camera. The optical modulation layers operate at 300×300 pixels per layer with 4 layers per set, and the output intensity is downsampled and normalized to recover the noise prediction. The actual denoising update (subtracting the predicted noise and rescaling) is then applied digitally, meaning the optical system only performs the network forward pass.

## Method

The Optical Denoising Unit (ODU) programs light propagation through passive phase-only modulation layers to implement the denoising network: each layer applies point-wise complex modulation (trainable weights) followed by free-space Fresnel diffraction (implicit convolution), so the output intensity at the detector equals the predicted noise. A time-aware policy eliminates the need for time-embedding by partitioning the T=1000 diffusion timesteps into M=10 subsets, each handled by a fixed set of 4 modulation layers, so a single passive optical configuration processes 100 consecutive timesteps without reconfiguration. Training uses backpropagation through an analytical split-step Fourier model of the optical system, optimizing the phase distributions on the SLM. An online learning algorithm maintains a digital twin that is continuously refined against experimental outputs to correct for alignment and calibration errors, enabling the physical system to converge to near-perfect-calibration performance despite initial misalignments of a few milliradians.

## Results

The Optical Denoising Unit (ODU) achieves FID scores of 206.6 (MNIST), 227.7 (Fashion-MNIST), and 131.4 (Quick, Draw! clock class) at T=1000 timesteps, using 300×300-pixel phase-only modulation layers with 4 layers per set and M=10 timestep sets. In terms of energy efficiency, the ODU consumes 0.23 J/image at 23.0 images/s, compared to 1.74 J/image (Fully Connected) and 5.37 J/image (Convolutional U-Net) on an NVIDIA L4 GPU. Scaling analysis shows the ODU follows a power-law relationship between parameter count and FID with an exponent of −0.15, closely matching the −0.16 reported for large-scale digital generative models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares the ODU against two digital denoising architectures—a 19.6M-parameter Fully Connected network and a 220K-parameter Convolutional U-Net—trained under identical DDPM settings on MNIST. The ODU consistently outperforms both digital baselines in denoising MSE and generation FID across all tested image resolutions (Figure 4), despite having an intermediate parameter count (3.6M). In energy terms, the ODU is approximately 7.6× more efficient than the Fully Connected network (0.23 vs. 1.74 J/image) and 23× more efficient than the U-Net (0.23 vs. 5.37 J/image), while achieving a throughput of 23.0 images/s. The U-Net, despite having far fewer parameters (220K), is the slowest at 13.9 images/s due to its sequential convolutional operations on GPU. The ODU's generation performance scales with parameter count following the same power-law trend (exponent −0.15) as large-scale digital image generation networks (−0.16), suggesting comparable scalability potential.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two well-developed lines of research: denoising diffusion probabilistic models (DDPMs) for image generation and diffractive optical computing for analog signal processing. It departs from prior optical neural network work (which has demonstrated super-resolution, noise removal, and CNN-layer implementation) by programming the full iterative denoising diffusion process into passive phase-only modulation layers, and from prior diffusion model work by replacing the learned time-embedding mechanism with a time-aware partitioning of timesteps into fixed optical layer sets. The online learning algorithm with a digital twin extends the backpropagation-through-physics approach used in prior optical training work by continuously refining the surrogate model during training to absorb calibration drift.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The demonstration that passive optical layers can implement the entire denoising diffusion loop at ~100× lower energy per image than GPU-based digital baselines addresses a critical sustainability concern for generative AI, whose iterative sampling is among the most compute-intensive inference workloads. However, all experiments are confined to 20×20 pixel images on small datasets (MNIST, Fashion-MNIST, Quick Draw clocks), and the digital baselines are relatively simple architectures, so the practical significance at high resolution remains unproven. Open questions include whether the approach scales to high-resolution or text-conditioned generation, whether nonlinear optical effects can replace the need for multiple parallel layer sets, and whether the online learning algorithm generalizes beyond the single-SLM-plus-mirror prototype to more complex multi-device optical systems.
