## Problem

I-JEPA, a leading masked joint-embedding predictive architecture for self-supervised visual representation learning, suffers from two specific deficiencies: its Exponential Moving Average (EMA) target encoder update is insufficient to prevent entire model collapse, and its predictor fails to accurately learn the mean of patch representations across augmented views. This paper addresses both gaps by identifying the theoretical connection between I-JEPA's predictor dynamics and SimSiam/VICReg, then integrating VICReg's variance-invariance-covariance regularization directly into the JEPA masking objective.

## Input / Output

The method consumes unlabeled RGB images resized to 224×224, split into 16×16 patches (yielding 14×14 patch tokens), and applies 4 possibly overlapping block masks to create context and target views. The context encoder processes unmasked patches while the target encoder (updated via EMA) processes masked patches; a predictor then maps context features to predict target patch-level embeddings. The output is a set of patch-level visual representations whose quality is evaluated via linear probing, fine-tuning, and dense prediction tasks (detection, segmentation, video segmentation).

## Method

C-JEPA augments the standard I-JEPA masking loss (mean ℓ₂ distance between predicted and target patch embeddings) with a VICReg-inspired regularization term: L = L_JEPA + β_vicreg · (β_sim·L_sim + β_std·L_std + β_cov·L_cov), where the similarity term enforces invariance of representation means across different mask blocks of the same image, the standard-deviation term (hinge on per-dimension variance) prevents collapse, and the covariance term decorrelates embedding dimensions. The theoretical analysis shows that in the predictor's eigenbasis, representational dynamics follow dẑ/dt = ηλ_k(1−λ_k)ẑ, which is stable only when the predictor's eigenvalues are pushed toward 1; without stop-grad, dynamics become dẑ/dt = −η(1−λ_k)²ẑ, causing exponential decay to zero (collapse). VICReg's variance and covariance terms counteract this collapse, while the invariance term explicitly aligns the mean of patch representations across mask blocks, replacing the role that EMA was intended to play. The full pipeline uses ViT backbones (T/S/B/L) with a 6- or 12-layer predictor, AdamW optimization, batch size 2048, and cosine LR decay over 600 epochs on ImageNet-1K.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K with ViT-B/16 (600 pretrain epochs), C-JEPA achieves 73.7% linear probing accuracy and 84.5% fine-tuning accuracy, outperforming I-JEPA by +0.8 and +1.0 points respectively. On COCO detection/segmentation, C-JEPA reaches 50.7 AP^box and 45.3 AP^mask, and on ADE20K semantic segmentation it achieves 48.7 mIoU, all surpassing I-JEPA. At ViT-L/16 scale, C-JEPA further improves to 78.1% linprob, 86.2% fine-tune, 58.3 (J&F)_m on DAVIS, 86.8 Clevr/Count, and 71.6 Clevr/Dist.

## Baselines & Comparisons

C-JEPA consistently outperforms its direct predecessor I-JEPA across all five metrics: +0.8 linprob, +1.0 fine-tune, +0.8 AP^box, +0.8 AP^mask, and +1.1 mIoU, while using the same 600 pretrain epochs. Against MAE and BEiT, C-JEPA wins on every reported metric (e.g., 84.5 vs. 83.6 fine-tune vs. MAE; 73.7 vs. 56.7 linprob vs. BEiT). Compared to DINO (1600 epochs), C-JEPA achieves higher fine-tune (84.5 vs. 82.8), AP^box (50.7 vs. 50.1), AP^mask (45.3 vs. 43.4), and mIoU (48.7 vs. 46.8), though DINO retains a substantial linprob advantage (78.2 vs. 73.7). Against iBOT (1600 epochs), C-JEPA wins on fine-tune (84.5 vs. 84.0) and AP^mask (45.3 vs. 44.2) but trails on linprob (73.7 vs. 79.5), AP^box (50.7 vs. 51.2), and mIoU (48.7 vs. 50.0). Versus data2vec (800 epochs), C-JEPA leads on linprob (73.7 vs. 60.8), fine-tune (84.5 vs. 84.2), and mIoU (48.7 vs. 48.2).

## Relation to Prior Work

C-JEPA sits at the intersection of two established self-supervised learning threads: the Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture (I-JEPA, Assran et al. 2023) for masked representation prediction, and VICReg (Bardes et al. 2022) for variance-invariance-covariance regularization in Siamese networks. The paper's core conceptual move is to argue that I-JEPA's EMA-based target encoder is an insufficient anti-collapse mechanism and that its predictor struggles to capture the mean of patch representations, then to substitute VICReg's explicit variance and covariance terms (plus an invariance term across mask blocks) for the EMA. A secondary contribution is a theoretical bridge between I-JEPA and SimSiam via NTK-based gradient dynamics in the predictor's eigenbasis, positioning the predictor as a learned whitening operator whose eigenvalues drive mode-wise convergence.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a principled, if modest, recipe for stabilizing non-contrastive JEPA training by borrowing a well-understood regularizer, and the ablations confirm that both the variance/covariance and invariance components contribute independently. However, the gains over I-JEPA are small (≤1.0 point on linear probing, ≤1.0 on fine-tuning) and the theoretical NTK analysis, while suggestive, relies on strong assumptions (symmetric predictor, i.i.d. Gaussian inputs, expectation over identical masks) that are not validated empirically. Open questions include whether the VICReg terms remain beneficial at larger scales (ViT-H, ViT-G), whether the invariance term across mask blocks generalizes to video or 3D settings, and whether the claimed EMA inadequacy manifests more severely in low-data or domain-shift regimes where collapse risk is higher.
