## Problem

Existing visual grounding methods tightly couple multimodal fusion to downstream tasks, relying on limited downstream data to learn image-text interaction, which causes significant performance drops on complex textual expressions. The paper argues that the mutual understanding between modalities is underexplored when fusion is confined to task-specific architectures, and that leveraging the rich inter-modal fusion capability of existing multimodal pre-trained models (e.g., BEiT-3) can substantially improve grounding quality and convergence speed.

## Input / Output

SimVG takes an RGB image (resized to 640×640) and a text caption (trimmed to 20 tokens) as input. The image is compressed to 1/32 scale via visual embedding into N patch tokens, the text is mapped to K token embeddings with a padding mask, and a single learnable object token is appended; all three token types are fed jointly into the multi-modality encoder. The output is one or more bounding boxes for the referred object(s), with the lightweight token branch (a single MLP) producing predictions in 44 ms on a GTX 1080Ti, or the heavier decoder branch (3-layer transformer) in 52 ms. For the GREC variant, the number of object queries is expanded to 10 to handle multi-target or no-target expressions.

## Method

SimVG builds on the BEiT-3 dual-stream-with-fusion-encoder architecture, decoupling multimodal fusion from the grounding head by simultaneously encoding image, text, and a learnable object token in a unified transformer encoder. Two parallel branches consume the encoder output: a DETR-style decoder branch (3-layer transformer with cross-attention and Hungarian matching) and a lightweight token branch (a single MLP applied to the object token augmented by text-guided query generation). A novel Dynamic Weight-Balance Distillation (DWBD) loss trains both branches synchronously, dynamically reweighting the token branch's supervision between ground truth and decoder predictions based on the decoder's current IOU-weighted confidence, so that early training is guided by ground truth and later training increasingly follows the decoder. A Text-Guided Query Generation (TQG) module injects textual priors into object queries via cross-attention and masked max-pooling, enabling adaptive query initialization and seamless extension to multi-target GREC. The full pipeline uses a two-stage distillation schedule (decoder pre-training followed by synchronous DWBD) and achieves SOTA on six benchmarks with only 1.58 M head parameters for the token branch.

## Results

On the RefCOCO/+/g benchmarks, SimVG-DB (ViT-B/32) achieves 87.63/78.65/80.51 (val/val/test-u) and SimVG-DB (ViT-L/32) reaches 90.51/84.88/89.15, setting new state-of-the-art across all splits. On ReferItGame and Flickr30K Entities, SimVG-DB (ViT-B/32) scores 74.83 and 82.04 respectively, surpassing the previous best transformer-based method Dynamic MDETR by ~4.4% on average. On the GREC benchmark (GRefCOCO), SimVG-DB achieves 62.1 Prec@(F1@0.5) on val, outperforming the prior best UNINEXT (58.2) by nearly 4 points. The lightweight token branch (SimVG-TB) runs at 44 ms on a GTX 1080Ti, roughly 7× faster than two-stage methods like MAttNet (320 ms).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SimVG-DB (ViT-B/32) outperforms the strongest prior transformer-based method, Dynamic MDETR (ViT-B/16), by an average of ~4.4 points across RefCOCO/+/g (e.g., 87.63 vs. 85.97 on RefCOCO val, 78.65 vs. 74.83 on RefCOCO+ val, 80.37 vs. 74.14 on RefCOCOg val-u). Against one-stage methods, SimVG achieves a +14% absolute average improvement over ReSC, and against two-stage methods it surpasses MAttNet by ~11 points while running 7× faster (44 ms vs. 320 ms). On the GREC task (GRefCOCO), SimVG-DB (62.1 Prec@F1=1) beats the previous best UNINEXT (58.2) by ~4 points and MDETR (42.7) by ~19 points. In the pre-trained comparison (Table 3), SimVG-DB (ViT-B/32, 28K pre-train images) surpasses GroundingDINO (200K images) by ~2 points on average and MDETR (200K images) by ~5 points, while using only 1.58M head parameters (vs. 17.36M for MDETR). Against 7B-parameter multimodal LLMs (Table 7), SimVG-DB-Large (0.61B) is competitive, e.g., 92.87 vs. 91.73 on RefCOCO val, demonstrating that the decoupled multimodal fusion approach is highly parameter-efficient.

## Relation to Prior Work

SimVG builds on BEiT-3's dual-stream-with-fusion-encoder architecture for the multimodal encoder and DETR's Hungarian matching for the decoder branch, but departs fundamentally from the dominant paradigm of independent modality encoding followed by task-specific fusion modules. Rather than designing complex cross-attention or encoder-decoder heads for query reasoning, the paper decouples multimodal fusion into the pre-trained encoder and replaces the decoder with a lightweight MLP (token branch) trained via a novel dynamic weight-balance distillation loss. This positions SimVG against the three established VG categories (two-stage, one-stage, transformer-based) by arguing that the limited downstream data used for fusion in prior methods underestimates the difficulty of cross-modal understanding, a gap that pre-trained multimodal models already address.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a 1.58M-parameter MLP head achieves SOTA on six benchmarks with 12 hours of single-GPU training and 44 ms inference, offering a compelling efficiency/performance trade-off for practitioners. The finding that the token branch can surpass the decoder branch when scaling to ViT-L suggests that distillation from a stronger teacher can outperform direct supervision, an observation with broader implications for multi-branch training. However, the paper acknowledges it does not exploit hierarchical feature pyramids (e.g., FPN), limiting multi-scale target detection, and validation is confined to bounding-box tasks without segmentation. Open questions include whether the decoupling strategy transfers to dense prediction tasks and whether the DWBD mechanism generalizes beyond the DETR-style matching setup.
