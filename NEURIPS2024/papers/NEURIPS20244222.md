## Problem

Estimating mutual information between high-dimensional variables (hundreds to thousands of dimensions) is intractable with existing techniques: nonparametric estimators like KSG fail beyond ~tens of dimensions, and variational bound estimators (MINE, InfoNCE) degrade with high variance. No method has been shown to reliably estimate MI in the regime of 10³+ dimensions with realistic sample sizes, despite this being the natural setting for genomics, neuroscience, and protein biology.

## Input / Output

The method consumes N joint samples {(x_i, y_i)} of two high-dimensional random variables X ∈ R^{d_X} and Y ∈ R^{d_Y} (d up to 5000 in benchmarks). It splits samples 1:1 into a training set (for learning latent representations) and an estimation set (for KSG evaluation), producing a scalar MI estimate in bits. Optionally, it yields a per-sample pointwise MI decomposition (via a modified KSG) or a log density ratio estimate usable for binary classification of interacting pairs.

## Method

LMI approximation learns low-dimensional latent codes Z_x = E_X(X) and Z_y = E_Y(Y) (default 8 dims each) using a pair of autoencoders augmented with cross-prediction decoders: four MLP decoders reconstruct each variable from both its own and the other variable's latent code. Training minimizes the sum of all four MSE reconstruction losses, where the cross-prediction terms are theoretically shown to upper-bound the conditional entropies h(X|Z_y) and h(Y|Z_x), thereby minimizing the gap I(X;Y) − I(Z_x;Z_y). After training on half the data, the KSG nonparametric estimator is applied to the latent representations of the held-out half, yielding the final MI estimate. The architecture is fully parameterized by default (MLP widths determined by input dimensionality, Leaky ReLU activations, 50% dropout on cross-decoders, Adam optimizer), requiring no user tuning beyond the joint samples.

## Results

On resampled real-world benchmarks (784-dim MNIST digits and 1024-dim ProtTrans5 protein embeddings), LMI achieves near-zero MSE (0.0 and 0.01) and rank correlation of 0.95 and 0.94, respectively, while KSG, MINE, and InfoNCE show substantially higher error. On synthetic multivariate Gaussians spanning d=10 to 5,000 ambient dimensions, LMI provides more accurate estimates than all baselines in 83% (absolute) and 87% (relative) of tested settings. In biological applications, LMI estimates I(L;R)≈2.8 bits and I(K;T)≈0.8 bits for protein interactions (AUC-ROC 0.87 and 0.74), and reveals non-Markovian gene expression dynamics in hematopoietic stem cells with I(X₂;X₆)≈0.31 bits < I(X₆;X₆′)≈0.98 bits.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LMI outperforms all three baselines on both absolute and relative accuracy across both real-data benchmarks. Against KSG [10], LMI matches relative accuracy (rank correlation 0.95 vs. 0.92 on MNIST) but dramatically improves absolute accuracy (MSE 0.00 vs. 0.19), because KSG systematically underestimates MI in high dimensions. Against MINE [15], LMI is far superior on protein embeddings (MSE 0.01 vs. 0.32; rank correlation 0.94 vs. 0.26), where MINE's high variance causes near-complete failure. Against InfoNCE [31], the gap is even starker on protein data, where InfoNCE's rank correlation is negative (−0.06), indicating it produces estimates anti-correlated with ground truth. On convergence, KSG fails entirely for d≥13, MINE for d≥25, and InfoNCE for d≥37, while LMI remains stable up to d=5,000 with only ~4,000 samples needed for 1-bit MI estimation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the KSG nonparametric MI estimator [10] as its estimation engine, but departs from prior work by learning low-dimensional representations via cross-predictive autoencoders before applying it, rather than estimating MI directly in the ambient high-dimensional space. It positions itself as complementary to sliced MI [14, 23], which sacrifices interpretability and equitability for tractability, whereas LMI retains classical MI's properties under the assumption of low-dimensional dependence structure. The cross-prediction regularization is theoretically grounded in a new result (Theorem 1) showing that MSE cross-prediction loss upper-bounds conditional entropy, connecting autoencoder training to information-theoretic objectives in a way that prior autoencoder interpretations [29, 30] did not exploit for MI estimation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LMI is the first method demonstrated to reliably estimate MI for variables exceeding 1000 dimensions with realistic sample sizes, opening the door to information-theoretic analysis in genomics, neuroscience, and other high-dimensional domains where prior techniques (KSG, MINE, InfoNCE) fail completely. The two biology applications—quantifying protein interaction information in pLM embeddings and identifying non-Markovian cell fate dynamics in hematopoietic stem cells—address open problems that prior work could only tackle with heuristic proxies. Key limitations remain: LMI fails when intrinsic dependence dimensionality approaches ambient dimensionality, is blind to symmetric distributions where E[X|Y]=E[X], and inherits KSG's known failure for near-deterministic relationships; theoretical guarantees beyond basic DPI bounds are still lacking.
