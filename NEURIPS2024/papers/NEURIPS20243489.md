## Problem

Mainstream time series classification models assume i.i.d. segments and ignore the natural temporal dependencies between consecutive segments in raw MVD (Multiple classes with Varying Duration) data. Additionally, inconsistent boundary labels from annotators with varying expertise destabilize training, yet existing noisy-label-learning methods for time series also overlook cross-segment contextual dependencies. The paper formally shows that contextual information increases the mutual information between instances and labels, then designs a framework to exploit this while harmonizing inconsistent labels.

## Input / Output

The method takes a long MVD time series that is segmented via a sliding window (window length w, stride r) into L consecutive segments, each labeled by majority vote of the underlying point labels. At inference, the model produces not just independent per-segment class probabilities but a coherent prediction sequence by averaging independent predictions with Tanh-constrained predictions that enforce monotonicity across the time axis. The training input additionally includes the raw MVD boundary labels, which are progressively modified during training to harmonize inconsistencies.

## Method

Con4m comprises three coupled components. First, a Con-Transformer encoder uses a two-branch Con-Attention that fuses standard self-attention with a Gaussian-kernel-weighted aggregation branch (each segment learns its own scale σ) to produce continuous contextual representations. Second, a context-aware prediction head combines a neighbor-class consistency discriminator (which aggregates predictions from segments likely sharing the same class) with a Tanh function-fitting constraint that enforces monotonic prediction behavior across consecutive segments, yielding both independent and constrained predictions. Third, a label consistency training framework applies curriculum learning from core (easy) to boundary (hard) segments with a 5-epoch lag, while progressively replacing raw labels with exponentially averaged model predictions weighted by a dynamic factor η that linearly grows from 0 to 1 over E_η epochs, preventing premature reliance on unreliable predictions.

## Results

Con4m achieves the best F1 score across all four MVD datasets and all label disturbance ratios. On the clean (0%) setting, Con4m scores 71.28% (fNIRS), 80.29% (HHAR), 68.02% (Sleep), and 72.00% (SEEG raw), outperforming the strongest baselines by 3.24%, 7.15%, and 6.45% on HHAR, Sleep, and SEEG respectively. Under 40% boundary disturbance, Con4m degrades by only 2.37% on average across fNIRS and Sleep, compared to 3.01–5.23% for other NLL methods, demonstrating superior robustness to label inconsistency.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Con4m outperforms all 11 baselines on every dataset and disturbance ratio. On fNIRS (0% disturbance), TAS models (MS-TCN2: 71.48%, ASFormer: 71.69%) are competitive with Con4m (71.28%), but Con4m maintains near-constant performance as disturbance increases (70.04% at 40%), while DiffAct drops 13.23% and Scale-T drops 3.89%. On HHAR, Con4m leads by a wide margin (80.29% vs. Scale-T's 77.77% at 0%), with the gap widening under disturbance (75.52% vs. 75.97% at 40%). On Sleep, Con4m achieves 68.02% vs. Sel-CL's 63.48% (0%) and Scale-T's 63.21%, a 4.5–7.2% advantage that persists at 40% (64.31% vs. 61.72%). On the naturally inconsistent SEEG data, Con4m scores 72.00% vs. Scale-T's 67.64%, a 6.45% improvement. The label substitution experiment further confirms that Con4m's harmonized labels boost TimesNet by 7.53% and PatchTST by 4.11%, validating the label consistency framework independently of the architecture.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of time series classification (TSC), noisy label learning (NLL), and temporal action segmentation (TAS), arguing that the MVD (Multiple classes with Varying Duration) setting for segmented TSC has been overlooked by all three lines of work. It departs from mainstream TSC by rejecting the i.i.d. assumption between consecutive segments, from TAS by addressing the absence of pretrained feature extractors for time series and the lack of explicit label-level context, and from NLL (Scale-teaching, SREA) by incorporating temporal contextual dependencies into label harmonization. The theoretical contribution (Theorem 2.1) provides an information-theoretic upper bound on the benefit of contextual information, grounding the design choices in a principled framework rather than purely empirical motivation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The MVD formulation captures a practical reality in medical monitoring (seizure detection, sleep staging) and activity recognition where raw time series naturally contain multiple classes of varying duration with ambiguous boundaries, yet prior work has treated segments as independent. The label harmonization framework offers a practical solution for multi-annotator scenarios where boundary disagreements are inherent rather than intentional noise. Key limitations include the restriction to fully supervised settings, reliance on a single Tanh basis function for prediction behavior constraint (which may not generalize to more complex label dynamics), and the absence of exploration with large-scale or foundation models. Open questions include whether the approach extends to unsupervised segmentation, how it scales to longer sequences with more class transitions, and whether the curriculum learning strategy generalizes beyond the 5-level division.
