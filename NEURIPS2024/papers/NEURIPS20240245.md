## Problem

While Transformer language models excel at natural language generation, their ability to compositionally reuse learned primitive operations to solve compositional algorithmic tasks remains poorly characterized. This paper formalizes the question with four sample-efficiency hypotheses (H1–H4) and asks: can a Transformer model that has already learned all sub-tasks learn their composition with fewer samples than relearning everything from scratch, or does it require even more data than the sum of all sub-tasks?

## Input / Output

For the two novel pointer-execution tasks (PEN, PERM), input is a sequence of words where the matching criterion links the last two characters of one word to the first two characters of its match; PEN outputs the right neighbors of the matched chain while PERM outputs the reversed matched sequence annotated with multicount products. Both tasks embed 'attention traps'—spurious duplicate matches on yellow tokens—to prevent shortcut solutions. For HSS, input is a sequence of integers and output is the maximum non-adjacent subsequence sum; for MUL, input is two multi-digit base-10 numbers and output is their product. In the prompting setting, input additionally includes a natural-language task description and 8 few-shot input-output examples.

## Method

The paper introduces two new compositional pointer-execution tasks (PEN and PERM) whose primitives (left, right, match, reverse, multicount) are each independently observable in designated sub-tasks, then trains 150M-parameter LLaMA models from scratch on all sub-tasks concurrently with the composition task using standard autoregressive cross-entropy loss. Empirically, LLaMA achieves near-perfect accuracy on every sub-task but requires roughly 10× more samples than the sum of all sub-task data to learn the composition, supporting hypothesis H4; a 121-parameter discrete hill-climbing algorithm solves PEN from a single sample, highlighting the gap. For in-context learning, GPT-4 and Gemini-Pro are prompted with few-shot, chain-of-thought, analogical CoT, sub-task CoT, and multi-round code interpreter, all yielding 0–42% task accuracy. Theoretically, the authors prove (via a reduction from NP-hard problems and cryptographic hardness assumptions) that feedforward models trained with gradient descent under a constant-memorization assumption require Ω(k(n)/z(n)) samples—potentially polynomially or exponentially more than an optimal learner—to generalize on compositional concept classes.

## Results

On the PEN task, LLaMA-150M achieves near-perfect accuracy on all sub-tasks (Cpy, RCpy, PE, PEV) with 20–100K samples each, but requires 1000K samples (≈5× the sum of sub-task data) to reach 97% accuracy on the compositional task. For PERM, sub-tasks are learned with 40–150K samples, yet the composition needs 2000K samples (≈9× the sub-task sum) for 91% accuracy. In the prompting setting, GPT-4 and Gemini-Pro achieve 0% task accuracy on PEN across all prompting strategies (few-shot, CoT, sub-task CoT, analogical CoT), while on PERM GPT-4 reaches at most 42% task accuracy (sub-task CoT) and Gemini-Pro only 9%. The GPT-4 code interpreter yields just 10% fully correct answers, with 65% producing wrong outputs and 24% timing out after 10 attempts.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not benchmark against external compositional-learning baselines; instead, it compares internal configurations to isolate the effect of compositionality. Training LLaMA jointly on sub-tasks plus the compositional task yields no significant improvement over training on the compositional task alone (e.g., PEN: 97% at 1000K with sub-tasks vs. 98% at 1000K without), indicating the model does not reuse learned primitives. A smaller 28M LLaMA with auxiliary prediction heads learns PEN in 300K samples (vs. 0% for the 150M model at the same budget), but still falls under H4. Among prompting strategies for GPT-4 on PERM, sub-task CoT with traps removed achieves the best 42% task accuracy, outperforming plain CoT (8%), few-shot (6%), and the code interpreter (13%) by 29–36 percentage points. Gemini-Pro consistently underperforms GPT-4 across all settings, reaching at most 9% task accuracy on PERM versus GPT-4's 42%. The discrete hill-climbing solver (121 parameters) learns PEN from a single sample, highlighting a gap of over one million samples relative to LLaMA.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the compositionality evaluation framework of Dziri et al. (NeurIPS 2023) by training LLaMA models from scratch rather than relying on prompting or fine-tuning, and by relaxing the sub-task definition to allow multi-operation sub-tasks with an independent-observability constraint. It introduces two new pointer-execution-based tasks (PEN, PERM) inspired by the Pointer Value Retrieval benchmark lineage, while also reusing Dziri et al.'s HSS and MUL tasks for cross-validation. A key conceptual departure is the addition of a complexity-theoretic analysis showing that gradient descent on feedforward models is fundamentally data-inefficient for combinatorial (including compositional) problems, connecting empirical observations to hardness assumptions like P≠NP.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The consistent finding that hypothesis H4 holds across all four tasks — requiring roughly 10× more data than the sum of all sub-tasks — and that training with sub-tasks provides no measurable benefit over training on the composition alone, suggests that current Transformer architectures do not genuinely reuse learned primitives. The theoretical result provides a principled explanation rooted in the learning-inference computation gap, implying that the issue is not merely a data or scale problem but a fundamental limitation of gradient descent on fixed-depth feedforward models. Open questions include whether architectural modifications (e.g., adaptive depth, inductive biases toward compositionality) or new training objectives can close this gap, and whether the findings generalize beyond small models and synthetic tasks to the large-scale, natural-language settings where compositionality is most needed.
