## Problem

Few-shot tabular classification is challenging because tabular data lacks the spatial or sequential structure that enables effective augmentation and semantic extraction in vision and NLP, making standard meta-learning and self-supervised approaches poorly transferable. Additionally, tabular features are heterogeneous (mixed numerical and categorical), and embeddings from a single class often exhibit multimodality, undermining standard prototype-based classifiers that rely on per-class averages.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241163/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a large unlabeled tabular dataset (mixed numerical and categorical columns, one-hot encoded and min-max or standardized scaled), a small N-way K-shot labeled support set, and a query set of unlabeled tabular samples. It produces a class label per query sample via nearest-prototype assignment in a learned embedding space of configurable dimension (5–80). The training phase is fully unsupervised on the unlabeled set; labels are only used at inference time to form prototypes, and hyperparameters are tuned via a pseudo-label validation scheme on raw features since no labeled validation split exists in the 1-shot regime.

## Method

D2R2 repurposes a conditional DDPM as a representation extractor: a trainable embedding network z_θ maps raw inputs to a low-dimensional latent vector that conditions the noise-prediction network ε_φ, creating an information bottleneck that forces z_θ to retain only the semantic information needed for denoising (focusing on large timesteps to capture low-frequency structure). A Random Distance Matching (RDM) loss is jointly optimized with the diffusion reconstruction loss, aligning pairwise cosine distances in the embedding space with distances in randomly projected spaces; projections use uniform distributions for numerical features and Bernoulli distributions for categorical features, enabling a single model to handle mixed-type tabular data. At classification time, an instance-wise iterative prototype scheme replaces the standard per-class mean: each K-shot sample retains its own prototype, and prototypes are iteratively refined via soft k-means-style weighted averaging with query embeddings over L steps, accommodating multimodal class structure and reducing prototype variance. Hyperparameters (RDM weight α, embedding dimension, temperature τ) are selected through a pseudo-label validation loop that applies the same iterative prototype classifier to raw features to generate synthetic ground-truth labels.

## Results

D2R2 achieves state-of-the-art accuracy across all nine tabular datasets under both 1-shot and 5-shot settings. In the 1-shot setting, D2R2 reaches 81.13% on optdigits, 81.34% on pixel, 75.82% on income, and 68.00% on dna, outperforming the strongest baseline STUNT by 4.2%, 2.3%, 12.3%, and 1.8% respectively. In the 5-shot setting, D2R2 achieves 90.73% on optdigits, 91.06% on pixel, and 82.38% on dna, surpassing STUNT by 2.3%, 2.0%, and 3.2%. A Wilcoxon signed-ranks test confirms statistical significance (p < 0.05) over the best baseline on all datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

D2R2 outperforms all 14 baselines on every dataset in both 1-shot and 5-shot settings. The strongest competitor is STUNT (the only tabular-specific meta-learning baseline), which D2R2 surpasses by 1.8–12.3% in 1-shot (largest gap on income: 75.82 vs. 63.52) and by 1.0–3.3% in 5-shot. Among self-supervised methods, VIME and SCARF are the closest, but D2R2 still leads by 5–12% in 1-shot (e.g., 81.13 vs. 69.26 on optdigits for VIME). Meta-learning methods designed for images (UMTRA, SES, CACTUs) degrade severely on categorical-only datasets (UMTRA drops to 25.13% on dna, 34.26% on pixel in 1-shot), while D2R2 maintains 68.00% and 81.34% respectively. Supervised methods (CatBoost, KNN, TabPFN) and semi-supervised methods (Mean Teacher, ICT, MPL) trail D2R2 by 10–25% in the 1-shot regime, confirming that explicit semantic knowledge extraction via diffusion is critical when labeled data is extremely scarce.

## Relation to Prior Work

D2R2 repurposes the conditional diffusion framework from Abstreiter et al. (2021) as a representation extractor rather than a generative model, introducing an information bottleneck through a lower-dimensional embedding to force extraction of denoising-relevant semantic knowledge. The random distance matching loss is directly inspired by Wang et al. (2019), who showed that predicting distances in randomly projected spaces yields useful representations, but D2R2 extends this to handle mixed numerical and categorical features via type-specific projection distributions. The paper deliberately departs from both meta-learning (STUNT, CACTUs, UMTRA) and augmentation-based self-supervised learning (VIME, SCARF, SubTab), arguing that tabular data's lack of spatial structure and heterogeneous feature types make these paradigms poorly suited, and instead proposes a general unsupervised training process without self-generated tasks or data augmentation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Tabular few-shot learning is critical for high-stakes applications like fraud detection and rare disease diagnosis where labeled data is inherently scarce, and D2R2's consistent 5–20% improvements over the strongest baseline (STUNT) across nine diverse datasets demonstrate that diffusion-based representation learning is a viable and superior paradigm for this setting. The empirical observation of multimodal class embeddings (Figure 3) provides concrete justification for instance-wise prototypes over standard center-specific prototypes, a finding that could inform classifier design beyond this specific framework. Key limitations include the method's restriction to tabular data (explicitly noted as not generalizing to CV/NLP), the absence of theoretical analysis for the diffusion-based representation learning process, and the reliance on pseudo-label validation that may be unreliable when raw-feature clustering is poor. Open questions include whether the approach scales to very high-dimensional or extremely large tabular datasets, and whether the specific random projection distributions are optimal or whether learned projections could further improve performance.
