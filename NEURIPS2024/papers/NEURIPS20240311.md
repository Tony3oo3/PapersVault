## Problem

Existing GNN reuse methods (Knowledge Distillation, Knowledge Amalgamation) all require resource-intensive re-training of a student model, which is prohibitive for large-scale graphs and large models. This paper introduces Deep Graph Mating (GRAMA), the first fully training-free and label-free model merging task in the non-Euclidean domain, and identifies two unique challenges: GNNs exhibit amplified sensitivity to parameter misalignment compared to CNNs, and the optimal neuron alignment is topology-dependent rather than data-independent.

## Input / Output

The method takes two pre-trained parent GNNs with identical architectures (e.g., GCN, GraphSAGE, GAT, GIN, DGCNN) trained on disjoint data partitions, plus unlabeled graph data. The unlabeled data is consumed via a single forward pass through each parent to extract aggregated messages for alignment—no labels, gradients, or iterative training are involved. The output is a child GNN whose weights are a permuted linear interpolation of the parents, augmented with a learned-free normalisation layer; this child can then perform node classification, graph classification, point-cloud classification, or 3D semantic segmentation.

## Method

The proposed DuMCC framework has two stages. First, Parent Message Coordination (PMC) finds optimal per-layer permutation matrices by minimising the L2 distance between aggregated messages from the two parent GNNs (rather than raw weight vectors as in Git Re-basin), formulated as a linear assignment problem that is topology-aware. Second, Child Message Calibration (CMC) addresses the over-smoothing that interpolation induces (proven via Dirichlet energy analysis showing E(X^ℓ) ≤ max(E(X_a^ℓ), E(X_b^ℓ))): a Learning-Free Message Normalisation (LFNorm) layer rescales and shifts each message in the child GNN using the mean and variance statistics computed from the parent GNNs' messages, restoring feature diversity without any re-training or ground-truth labels.

## Results

On ogbn-arxiv node classification, DuMCC (w/ CMC) achieves 0.6645 and 0.6382 accuracy on the two disjoint test partitions, substantially outperforming VPI (0.3486/0.4361) and VAPI (0.6140/0.5752) while approaching KA (0.7150/0.6687) without any retraining. On ModelNet40 point-cloud classification, DuMCC reaches 0.8920/0.8574 accuracy versus 0.4518/0.4096 for VPI and 0.6538/0.5482 for VAPI. On S3DIS 3D semantic segmentation, DuMCC attains 0.7676 overall mean IoU compared to 0.4791 (VPI) and 0.5060 (VAPI). Notably, DuMCC surpasses KA on ogbn-proteins (0.7341 vs. 0.7215) and ogbg-molbace (0.6296 vs. 0.6135), demonstrating that KA's reliance on teacher soft labels introduces vulnerability to misclassification errors.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against KA [25] (Jing et al., CVPR 2021), which requires full retraining of a student GNN, DuMCC slightly lags on ogbn-arxiv (0.6645 vs. 0.7150) and ModelNet40 (0.8920 vs. 0.9250), but wins on ogbn-proteins (0.7341 vs. 0.7215) and ogbg-molbace (0.6296 vs. 0.6135), where KA's dependence on teacher-generated soft labels amplifies misclassification errors. Against VPI [54] (Wortsman et al., ICML 2022), which performs naive weight averaging without alignment, DuMCC achieves dramatic gains: 0.6645 vs. 0.3486 on ogbn-arxiv, 0.8920 vs. 0.4518 on ModelNet40, and 0.7676 vs. 0.4791 on S3DIS. Against VAPI [1] (Ainsworth et al., ICLR 2023), which aligns neurons via L2 weight matching before interpolation, DuMCC consistently outperforms: 0.6645 vs. 0.6140 on ogbn-arxiv, 0.8920 vs. 0.6538 on ModelNet40, and 0.7676 vs. 0.5060 on S3DIS, confirming that topology-aware message coordination (PMC) and variance calibration (CMC) are essential for GNN merging. Overall, DuMCC is the only training-free method that approaches KA-level performance across all seven benchmarks, while KA itself requires resource-intensive retraining.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Euclidean model merging literature (Git Re-basin, OTFusion, ZipIt!) to the non-Euclidean GNN domain, but identifies that direct porting fails due to GNN-specific challenges: amplified sensitivity to parameter misalignment (Lemma 4.1) and topology-dependent complexity in finding optimal permutation matrices (Conjecture 4.2). It departs from existing GNN reuse paradigms—Knowledge Distillation and Knowledge Amalgamation—by eliminating the re-training step entirely, introducing the first training-free and label-free multi-GNN merging task. The proposed DuMCC framework replaces data-independent weight matching with topology-aware message coordination (PMC) and adds a learning-free variance calibration layer (CMC) to counteract over-smoothing, drawing on the REPAIR rescaling idea but adapting it to GNN message statistics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GRAMA opens a practical pathway for integrating knowledge from isolated, privacy-restricted datasets (e.g., healthcare, retail) without centralizing data or requiring labels, which is a significant deployment advantage over training-based KA. The theoretical identification of increased over-smoothing susceptibility in interpolated GNNs (Proposition 5.1, via Dirichlet energy) provides a principled explanation for why naive merging degrades and motivates the CMC correction. Key open questions include extending to heterogeneous GRAMA (cross-architecture, cross-task-level), achieving fully data-independent merging, and exploring GRAMA as a pre-processing step for downstream knowledge amalgamation—none of which are addressed here.
