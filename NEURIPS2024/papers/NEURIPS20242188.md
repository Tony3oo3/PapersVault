## Problem

Despite the empirical success of few-shot diffusion models that fine-tune pre-trained models with only 5–10 target images, no theoretical work specifically analyzes the approximation or optimization properties of these models. Existing diffusion model theory without a fine-tuning phase yields bounds like n_ta^{-2/d} that suffer from the curse of (latent) dimensionality and cannot explain why few-shot models generalize well with so few target samples.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242188/cropped_page8_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained diffusion model (trained on a large source dataset of n_s images) and a small target dataset of n_ta images (typically 5–10) as input, and produces a fine-tuned diffusion model that generates novel images exhibiting the target feature. The data is assumed to lie on a low-dimensional linear subspace (latent dimension d ≈ 22–43 for common datasets), and the source and target distributions are assumed to share the same latent distribution. Only the low-rank encoder/decoder matrix V ∈ R^{D×d} is updated during fine-tuning, while the backbone ReLU network θ is frozen.

## Method

The paper provides the first theoretical analysis of few-shot diffusion models with a fine-tuning phase under a linear structure distribution. From the approximation perspective, it proves that freezing the pre-trained backbone θ and fine-tuning only the encoder/decoder V_ta yields a Õ(n_s^{-2/d} + n_ta^{-1/2}) score approximation bound, escaping the curse of dimensionality compared to the n_ta^{-2/d} bound without fine-tuning. From the optimization perspective, under a Gaussian latent variable assumption, it shows the expected few-shot score-matching objective has a closed-form minimizer ṼṼᵀ = ((m_t²λ̂²+σ_t²)/λ̂²)(C+σ_t²I)^{-1}C, so the empirical solution can be computed directly from the target covariance matrix without iterative optimization. The empirical closed-form solution achieves a Õ(1/n_ta + 1/(δ√n_s)) subspace recovery bound, with better dependence on the target dataset size. Experiments on CelebA64 confirm that fine-tuning only the encoder and decoder with 10 bald-face images generates novel images with the target feature, while full fine-tuning overfits and exhibits the memory phenomenon.

## Results

The main theoretical contribution is an approximation error bound of Õ(n_s^{-2/d} + n_ta^{-1/2}) for few-shot diffusion models, which escapes the curse of dimensionality compared to the n_ta^{-2/d} bound without fine-tuning. For the optimization problem under a Gaussian latent assumption, the paper proves a closed-form minimizer exists and provides an accuracy bound of Õ(1/n_ta + 1/(δ√n_s)) for the empirical solution. Table 1 shows that only 5–10 target images are needed across popular datasets (CIFAR-10, CelebA, MS-COCO, ImageNet) to match pre-trained model accuracy. Qualitative experiments on CelebA64 confirm that fine-tuning only the encoder and decoder with 10 bald-face images generates novel images with the target feature, while full fine-tuning (DreamBooth-style) suffers from memory/overfitting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is theoretical: the few-shot fine-tuning bound of n_ta^{-1/2} is strictly better than the n_ta^{-2/d} bound that would result from training from scratch on the target data alone (as in Chen et al. 2023c without fine-tuning). For example, with d=25 (CIFAR-10), the non-fine-tuned bound requires ~100× more target samples to achieve the same error. Empirically, the paper compares fine-tuning only the encoder/decoder (V_ta) against full fine-tuning (DreamBooth-style) on CelebA64 with 10 target images. The encoder/decoder-only approach produces diverse, novel bald-face images, while full fine-tuning exhibits the memory phenomenon, generating only slight brightness/angle modifications of the 10 training images. The paper does not provide quantitative FID or IS metrics, so the empirical comparison remains qualitative.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the linear-structure diffusion analysis of Chen et al. (2023c), which established the n_s^{-2/d} approximation bound for diffusion models trained from scratch, by introducing a fine-tuning phase where only the low-rank encoder/decoder matrix V_ta is optimized while the bottleneck network θ is frozen. It also draws on Yuan et al. (2023) for the Gaussian latent variable optimization analysis. The key conceptual departure is showing that the fine-tuning paradigm—practically realized by methods like DreamBooth and LoRA—fundamentally changes the sample complexity of the target data from n_ta^{-2/d} to n_ta^{-1/2}, thereby escaping the curse of latent dimensionality for the few-shot component. No prior theoretical work had analyzed the approximation or optimization properties of diffusion models specifically in the fine-tuning setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first theoretical explanation for why 5–10 target images suffice in practice for few-shot diffusion fine-tuning, bridging a gap between empirical success and theoretical understanding. The closed-form minimizer for the Gaussian latent case suggests that complex optimization may be unnecessary in the few-shot phase, with the empirical solution closely resembling a PCA-like subspace recovery. Key limitations include the linear-structure distribution assumption, the shared latent distribution requirement, and the restriction to Gaussian latents for the optimization analysis; extending to general bounded distributions or log-concave latents remains open. The experimental validation is limited to a single CelebA64 hairstyle experiment, leaving broader empirical confirmation as future work.
