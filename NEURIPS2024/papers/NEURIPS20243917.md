## Problem

Data-driven autonomous driving methods are constrained to their training data distribution and lack the ability to reason about unseen scenarios or continuously improve from experience. Existing knowledge-driven approaches using LLMs/VLMs typically perform only open-loop evaluation, failing to capture the dynamic closed-loop interaction between the ego vehicle and the environment. The paper addresses the need for a driving system that reasons like a human driver—selectively attending to critical objects, accumulating transferable experience, and continuously improving through self-reflection in a closed-loop setting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243917/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system consumes single-frame surrounding camera images at 448×448 resolution, which a fine-tuned Qwen-VL-7B converts into structured linguistic descriptions of only critical objects (semantic category, bounding box/lane/distance, motion direction, and behavioral reasoning), along with the ego vehicle's current speed and steering state. The dual-process decision module outputs discrete meta-actions (AC, DC, IDLE, STOP) at 2 Hz, which a low-level controller (Pure Pursuit + dual PID) translates into continuous steering, throttle, and brake signals for the CARLA simulator. Notably, the VLM is prompted to output only a subset of scene-relevant objects rather than a full scene description, reducing cognitive load on the downstream decision module.

## Method

LeapAD implements a dual-process cognitive architecture with three components: (1) a VLM scene-understanding module that selectively describes critical objects, (2) a slow Analytic Process (GPT-4) that performs chain-of-thought reasoning over scene descriptions to produce high-quality driving decisions and accumulate them in a memory bank, and (3) a fast Heuristic Process (Qwen1.5-1.8B) fine-tuned via SFT on the memory bank for ~5× faster inference. A reflection mechanism triggers when the Heuristic Process causes an accident: the Analytic Process reviews the preceding 10 frames of descriptions, reasoning, and decisions, identifies the error, and injects corrected samples back into the memory bank. For few-shot prompting, the system compresses scene descriptions into short captions (object category, lane, distance), encodes them with a text encoder, and retrieves top-k similar memories via cosine similarity to condition the Heuristic Process. The entire pipeline requires only 11K annotated frames for VLM training and no human annotation for the decision module, achieving competitive closed-loop performance with 1–2 orders of magnitude less data than data-driven baselines.

## Results

On the CARLA Town05 Short benchmark, LeapAD achieves a Driving Score (DS) of 83.11±0.28 and Route Completion (RC) of 94.98±0.54 using only 11K annotated frames and camera-only input. This outperforms all camera-only data-driven baselines by a wide margin (e.g., Roach: DS 65.26, VAD: DS 64.30) and even surpasses TransFuser (DS 54.52), which additionally uses LiDAR. The system requires 1–2 orders of magnitude less labeled data than competing methods (11K vs. 130K–3M annotations), and the Heuristic Process (Qwen1.5-1.8B) runs approximately 5× faster than the GPT-4-powered Analytic Process.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LeapAD (DS 83.11, RC 94.98) decisively outperforms all camera-only data-driven baselines: Roach (DS 65.26), VAD (DS 64.30), WOR (DS 64.79), NEAT (DS 58.70), LBC (DS 30.97), and CILRS (DS 7.47), with margins of 18–76 DS points. It also surpasses TransFuser (DS 54.52, RC 78.41), which relies on both LiDAR and camera inputs, by ~29 DS points. The only method that exceeds LeapAD is InterFuser (DS 94.95, RC 95.19), but InterFuser uses LiDAR+camera and 3M annotations—approximately 272× more data than LeapAD's 11K. Even in the zero-experience setting (LeapAD w/o Town05, DS 75.73), the system beats every camera-only baseline, demonstrating strong generalization from cross-town memory. The VLM+GPT-4 ablation (DS 81.31) confirms that the full dual-process pipeline with accumulated memory and reflection closes the gap to the powerful Analytic Process while using a 1.8B model at inference time.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from two main lines of autonomous driving research: data-driven end-to-end methods (InterFuser, TransFuser, VAD) that are constrained by training data distribution, and knowledge-driven LLM/VLM agents (LMDrive, Agent-Driver, DriveVLM-Dual, DiLU) that typically perform only open-loop evaluation. LeapAD carves new ground by combining closed-loop driving with a dual-process cognitive architecture inspired by Kahneman's System-I/System-II theory, where a powerful LLM (GPT-4) as the Analytic Process accumulates driving experience that is distilled via SFT into a lightweight 1.8B model (Qwen1.5-1.8B) as the Heuristic Process. The reflection mechanism and transferable memory bank distinguish it from prior knowledge-driven approaches that lack a self-improvement loop from failures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The demonstration that a 1.8B model can inherit and surpass GPT-4's driving performance through accumulated experience in a closed-loop setting offers a practical path toward edge-deployable autonomous driving with 1–2 orders of magnitude less labeled data. However, significant open questions remain: the system relies on single-frame camera input without temporal reasoning, the VLM cannot participate in the reflection mechanism, and all results are confined to the CARLA simulator with predefined agent behaviors that differ from real-world traffic. Whether the memory bank and reflection loop scale to the long-tail diversity of real driving, and whether the dual-process architecture generalizes beyond meta-action outputs to continuous trajectory planning, are key directions for future work.
