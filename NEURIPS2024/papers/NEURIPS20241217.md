## Problem

State-of-the-art text-to-image diffusion models frequently fail to faithfully render prompt details such as correct spatial relationships, object counts, and attribute bindings. Existing remedies—supervised fine-tuning on human-annotated pairs, RL/DPO alignment, or layout-guided generation—rely on expensive human annotation or auxiliary grounding signals. SELMA asks whether a T2I model can be taught multiple generation skills entirely from auto-generated image-text data, without any human labels or reward-model feedback.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241217/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the merged multi-skill model takes a free-form text prompt and produces a single RGB image (512×512 for SD v1.4/v2, 1024×1024 for SDXL) using 50 denoising steps with CFG scale 7.5. During training, the pipeline consumes 1K LLM-generated prompts per skill (5K total across five skill categories), each paired with exactly one image generated by the T2I model itself, yielding a 5K image-text training set with no human-collected pairs.

## Method

SELMA is a four-stage pipeline: (1) GPT-3.5 generates skill-specific prompts via in-context learning from three seed examples, with ROUGE-L > 0.8 filtering to enforce diversity; (2) the target T2I model generates one image per prompt; (3) a separate LoRA adapter (rank 128) is fine-tuned on each skill dataset independently, producing N skill-specific experts; (4) all LoRA experts are merged at inference by uniformly averaging their A and B matrices into a single multi-skill adapter. The key insight is that independent expert learning followed by weight merging mitigates the knowledge conflict that degrades a single LoRA trained jointly on mixed datasets, achieving superior faithfulness without any routing mechanism or additional inference cost. The approach also demonstrates weak-to-strong generalization: images generated by a weaker model (SD v2) can improve a stronger model (SDXL) when used as training data.

## Results

On SDXL, SELMA achieves 80.2% DSG accuracy (+6.9% over the 73.3% baseline) and 85.6% TIFA accuracy (+2.1%), while improving human preference metrics by +0.4 PickScore, +0.39 ImageReward, and +3.7 HPS. On SD v2, the gains are even larger: +7.4% DSG (70.3→77.7) and +4.0% TIFA (79.2→83.2). On SD v1.4, SELMA improves DSG by +4.0% (67.3→71.3) and TIFA by +2.9% (76.6→79.5). The method uses only 5K auto-generated image-text pairs (1K per skill) with LoRA rank 128, requiring no human annotation or reward model feedback.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SELMA outperforms all five baselines across every metric where they overlap. Against the training-free methods (SynGen and StructureDiffusion), SELMA leads by 4–5 points on DSG and 2–3 points on TIFA for SD v1.4, while also improving all three human preference scores. Against the RL-based methods (DPOK and DDPO), which require expensive human preference annotations, SELMA achieves 79.5% TIFA vs. 76.4%/76.7% and 0.36 ImageReward vs. −0.26/−0.08 on SD v1.4. Against DreamSync, the closest concurrent method that also uses auto-generated data, SELMA wins on TIFA (79.5 vs. 77.6 on SD v1.4; 85.6 vs. 85.2 on SDXL) and ImageReward (0.36 vs. −0.05 on SD v1.4; 1.09 vs. 0.84 on SDXL), while using only 5K prompts and 1 image per prompt versus DreamSync's 28K prompts and 8 images per prompt (i.e., ~2% of the data). Notably, SELMA also reports DSG scores that DreamSync does not, showing a 6.9-point DSG gain on SDXL.

## Relation to Prior Work

SELMA builds on the LoRA parameter-efficient fine-tuning paradigm (Hu et al., 2022) and extends the LoRA merging technique demonstrated by ZipLoRA (Shah et al., 2023) from two experts to five diverse skill-specific experts in diffusion models. Conceptually, it departs from RL-based alignment methods (DPOK, DDPO) that require expensive human preference annotations, and from the concurrent DreamSync (Sun et al., 2023) which also uses auto-generated data but relies on generating 8 images per prompt and filtering down to 1, whereas SELMA generates exactly 1 image per prompt and uses only 5K prompts total. The skill-specific expert learning and merging strategy is positioned as a solution to the knowledge conflict problem observed in multi-task LoRA training, drawing an analogy to LLaVA-MoLE's findings in multimodal LLMs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SELMA provides a practical, low-cost recipe for improving T2I faithfulness without any human annotation: 5K LLM-generated prompts, one self-generated image per prompt, and simple LoRA merging at inference. The finding that auto-generated data matches or slightly exceeds ground-truth data for fine-tuning, and that a weaker model's images can improve a stronger model (weak-to-strong generalization), opens questions about whether iterative self-improvement loops could further close the gap between T2I models and human-level faithfulness. Limitations include reliance on a strong base model and an instruction-following LLM, and the approach does not guarantee perfect prompt adherence in every detail. An open question is whether the skill-specific expert merging strategy generalizes beyond the five skills tested and to other generative modalities.
