## Problem

Existing Chinese medical LLMs perform sub-optimally in pediatric applications due to two compounding issues: instruction data that loses pediatric specialization through generic rephrasing or simple dialogue aggregation, and training pipelines that rely solely on SFT while ignoring the inconsistency between a model's internal knowledge and externally injected medical knowledge. This leads to hallucinated responses, excessive role-playing over genuine understanding, and an inability to handle multi-task pediatric services in real-world scenarios.

## Input / Output

The model accepts Chinese-language medical text queries, multi-turn patient-doctor dialogues, and structured pediatric instructions (covering 131 disease types across 11 categories) as input, and generates natural-language responses in three task formats: single-turn knowledge Q&A, multi-turn evidence-based diagnosis with progressive questioning, and single-turn treatment recommendations. Responses are expected to be doctor-like in tone, patient-friendly in language, and grounded in pediatric textbooks, guidelines, and knowledge graphs, with explicit safety refusals for malicious or counterfactual queries.

## Method

PediatricsGPT is built on Baichuan2-Base (7B/13B) through a four-stage pipeline: (1) Continuous Pre-Training with a hybrid instruction pre-training mechanism that converts instruction data into completion forms and interleaves them with plain medical texts to bridge format discrepancies between internal and injected knowledge; (2) Full-parameter SFT on multi-department medical data plus safety instructions to establish medical generalist capability; (3) Direct Following Preference Optimization (DFPO), a DPO variant that adds an SFT regularization term (Eq. 5) to the binary cross-entropy preference loss, directly anchoring the policy to preferred responses while learning preference boundaries; (4) LoRA-based parameter-efficient SFT using a Mixture of Universal-specific Experts (MUE) strategy, where three specific LoRA adapters are soft-routed via a noise-regularized gating function (Eq. 6) to specialize on distinct pediatric tasks, while one universal LoRA expert is consistently activated to preserve general medical knowledge and mitigate competency conflict. The entire pipeline is trained on PedCorpus, a 300K+ instruction dataset constructed from pediatric textbooks, guidelines, knowledge graphs, real doctor-patient conversations, and GPT-4-distilled medical data.

## Results

On the three pediatric benchmarks, PediatricsGPT-13B achieves ROUGE-L of 35.64 (MedKQ&A), 21.87 (EviDiag), and 40.82 (TreRecom), along with GLEU scores of 57.22, 37.15, and 54.65 respectively, outperforming all open-source Chinese medical LLMs on the vast majority of metrics. The 13B model surpasses GPT-4 on MedKQ&A (GLEU 57.22 vs. 53.72) and EviDiag (GLEU 37.15 vs. 36.79), while GPT-4 retains a slight edge on TreRecom GLEU (55.26 vs. 54.65). The 7B variant also yields competitive results, e.g., achieving 3.53% and 4.44% absolute gains over HuatuoGPT-II on ROUGE-L and GLEU in EviDiag. On general medical benchmarks (CMD, webMedQA), PediatricsGPT-13B demonstrates strong cross-department generalization, with the 7B model exceeding ChatGPT by 18.8% and 7.1% relative gains in Gynecology and Oncology on CMD.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PediatricsGPT-13B consistently outperforms all open-source Chinese medical LLMs (HuatuoGPT, DISC-MedLLM, Zhongjing, HuatuoGPT-II) across all three pediatric benchmarks, with the largest margins on MedKQ&A where it leads Zhongjing by 6.08 ROUGE-L points (35.64 vs. 29.56) and HuatuoGPT-II by 7.69 points. Against the strongest open-source baseline Meditron-7B, PediatricsGPT-13B gains 5.27 ROUGE-L on MedKQ&A and 7.45 on TreRecom. Compared to GPT-4, PediatricsGPT-13B wins on MedKQ&A (GLEU 57.22 vs. 53.72) and EviDiag (GLEU 37.15 vs. 36.79) but trails slightly on TreRecom GLEU (54.65 vs. 55.26), while ChatGPT is outperformed on all three benchmarks by the 13B model. The 7B variant already surpasses most 13B Chinese medical LLMs and even exceeds ChatGPT on certain general medical departments (e.g., +18.8% relative on Gynecology in CMD), confirming that data quality and the systematic training pipeline outweigh raw parameter scale. On the generalization benchmarks (CMD, webMedQA), PediatricsGPT-13B achieves GLEU scores competitive with or exceeding GPT-4 across diverse medical departments, validating the MUE strategy's ability to balance pediatric specialization with general medical competence.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Chinese medical LLM lineage (HuatuoGPT, DISC-MedLLM, Zhongjing, HuatuoGPT-II) by introducing the first pediatric-specialized model, departing from prior work that treated medicine as a monolithic domain. Methodologically, it builds on the standard CPT → SFT → preference-optimization pipeline but introduces three targeted modifications: a hybrid instruction pre-training mechanism to mitigate knowledge inconsistency during domain adaptation, a Direct Following Preference Optimization (DFPO) loss that augments DPO with an SFT-style regularization term, and a LoRA-based Mixture of Universal-specific Experts (MUE) strategy to resolve the generalist-specialist competency conflict. The dataset construction draws on existing medical corpora (Huatuo-26M, MedDialog, CMeKG) but adds pediatric-specific sources (textbooks, guidelines, knowledge graphs) and a role-playing-driven GPT-4 instruction generation rule.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete and pressing gap—pediatric healthcare resource scarcity in China—by providing a complete, reproducible pipeline (PedCorpus + four-stage training) that can serve as a template for building domain-specialized LLMs in other medical sub-specialties or non-medical verticals. The MUE strategy, which resolves task competition and generalist-specialist conflict with only ~1% trainable parameters, is a broadly applicable technique for multi-task LoRA fine-tuning. Key limitations include the model's restriction to Chinese, acknowledged security vulnerabilities under adversarial prompting, and the absence of error bars or multi-seed experiments, which limits confidence in the modest ablation margins. Open questions include whether the pipeline generalizes to other languages and whether the DFPO advantage over vanilla DPO persists at larger scale.
