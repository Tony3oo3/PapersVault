## Problem

Existing SAM-based few-shot semantic segmentation methods struggle with selecting appropriate point prompts, rely on manually designed hyperparameters and heuristics for mask merging, and suffer from prolonged inference due to iterative or excessive SAM mask generation. A fundamental oversight in these approaches is the lack of explicit modeling of the relationship between fine-grained point prompts (from feature matching) and the coarse-grained masks they produce via SAM, leading to low efficiency and limited automation.

## Input / Output

The method takes a target image and one or more reference images with binary segmentation masks as input. DINOv2 (ViT-L/14) extracts semantic features at 518×518 resolution for point selection, while SAM (ViT-H) generates masks at 1024×1024 resolution in a single forward pass. The output is a pixel-level segmentation mask for the target image, produced entirely without training or external hyperparameters, with an inference time of approximately 1.88 seconds per image on a single RTX 2080Ti.

## Method

The approach comprises three modules operating on a directed graph that bridges fine-grained points and coarse-grained masks. First, the Positive-Negative Alignment (PNA) module computes a mixture similarity map by combining mean and max foreground similarity with background similarity, then selects the top-N points parameter-free (where N equals the sum of the filtered map). Second, the Point-Mask Clustering (PMC) module constructs a directed graph where each vertex represents a point and its corresponding SAM mask, and edges exist when one mask covers another point; weakly connected components of this graph yield automatic, hyperparameter-free clusters of points and masks. Third, a two-stage Post-Gating strategy refines the prediction: positive gating uses a polarity map and iterative Mask Growth to retain masks with net-positive pixel content per cluster, while overshooting gating applies a distance-weighted self-consistency score to eliminate boundary-adjacent false-positive points. The final mask is the union of masks whose points pass both gates.

## Results

On the standard Few-shot Semantic Segmentation benchmarks, the method achieves 72.1% mIoU on Pascal-5i (1-shot), 58.7% on COCO-20i (1-shot), 88.0% on FSS-1000 (1-shot), and 35.2% on LVIS-92i (1-shot), with 5-shot results of 82.6%, 66.8%, 88.9%, and 44.2% respectively. On One-shot Part Segmentation, it reaches 44.5% on PASCAL-Part and 36.3% on PACO-Part. On Cross Domain FSS, it achieves 49.5% on Deepglobe, 48.7% on ISIC, and 47.1% on iSAID-5i (all 1-shot). The method is training-free and requires no external hyperparameters, with an inference speed of 1.88 s/img.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all 9 baselines across every evaluated dataset. Against the strongest generalist competitor Matcher [13], it gains +4.0% on Pascal-5i (72.1 vs 68.1), +6.0% on COCO-20i (58.7 vs 52.7), and +2.2% on LVIS-92i (35.2 vs 33.0) in 1-shot, with even larger margins in 5-shot (e.g., +8.6% on Pascal-5i, +6.1% on COCO-20i). Against the trained specialist AMFormer [45], it leads by +1.4% on Pascal-5i and +7.7% on COCO-20i, while also matching or exceeding specialist models on FSS-1000. On One-shot Part Segmentation, it surpasses Matcher by +1.6% on both PASCAL-Part (44.5 vs 42.9) and PACO-Part (36.3 vs 34.7). On Cross Domain FSS, the margins over Matcher are particularly large: +10.1% on ISIC (48.7 vs 38.6) and +13.8% on iSAID-5i (47.1 vs 33.3), demonstrating strong domain generalization without task-specific tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on SAM as the mask generation backbone and DINOv2 for fine-grained semantic features, following the SAM-based FSS lineage established by PerSAM (iterative refinement) and Matcher (bipartite matching with manual hyperparameters). The key conceptual departure is replacing iterative SAM calls or heuristic mask-merging metrics with a single-pass directed graph whose weakly connected components naturally cluster point prompts and their corresponding masks, eliminating the need for external hyperparameters. The Positive-Negative Alignment module also departs from prior work by explicitly leveraging background context as a negative reference signal, a component typically overlooked in FSS.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free, hyperparameter-free, single-mask-generation pipeline (1.88s/image vs. 12.7s for Matcher) makes SAM-based semantic segmentation practically deployable without per-scenario tuning, which is a significant step toward automation. The graph-based clustering provides an interpretable and principled way to bridge fine-grained feature granularity with coarse-grained mask granularity, a gap that prior methods addressed only heuristically. A noted limitation is coordinate bias for small objects due to the resolution mismatch between DINOv2 features and SAM prompting, leaving small-object segmentation as an open problem. Whether the graph-connectivity paradigm generalizes beyond SAM to other foundation segmentation models remains unexplored.
