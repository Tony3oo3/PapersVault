## Problem

Existing diffusion-based synthetic data generation methods for dichotomous image segmentation suffer from three critical limitations: text-guided generation deviates from real-world scenes (controllability), attention-map-based conditioning introduces noise that degrades pixel-level mask fidelity (precision), and generated samples lack sufficient shape and viewpoint diversity (diversity). These issues are particularly acute for DIS, which demands precise binary masks with fine structural details, and current approaches produce pseudo-labels that introduce errors rather than clean annotations.

## Input / Output

The method takes ground truth binary masks from the DIS5K training set (3,000 images at 512×512), GPT-4-generated textual prompts describing desired shape or category modifications, and relative camera rotation/translation parameters as inputs. It produces paired synthetic datasets of up to 10,000 high-resolution RGB images with pixel-accurate binary segmentation masks, where the masks are guaranteed to be the exact ground truth (not pseudo-labels) since they are derived directly from editing the original annotations. Canny edges are extracted from each synthetic mask as an additional conditioning signal for the image generation stage.

## Method

MaskFactory is a two-stage pipeline. In the mask editing stage, rigid transformations are performed via Zero123's viewpoint-conditioned diffusion model to change observation angles and scales, while non-rigid shape edits use a MasaCtrl-inspired mutual attention mechanism augmented with a topology-preserving adversarial training scheme: edge maps are extracted from source and synthetic masks to construct structural graphs, and a GAN discriminator enforces topological consistency between these graphs. The non-rigid generator is trained with a composite loss combining adversarial, content (L1 mask difference), and structure (L1 graph difference) terms. In the image generation stage, a multi-conditional ControlNet simultaneously injects the synthetic mask as a segmentation condition, its Canny edges as a boundary condition, and a category text prompt into a pre-trained diffusion model to produce the corresponding RGB image, ensuring tight image-mask alignment without relying on noisy pseudo-labels.

## Results

On the DIS5K benchmark, MaskFactory with 10,000 generated images achieves maxF1 of 0.835 on DIS-VD and 0.879 on DIS-TE4, surpassing the IS-Net baseline (0.791, 0.827) by 4.4–5.2 points. The Enhanced-alignment Measure (E_M^φ) reaches 0.923 on DIS-VD and 0.923 on DIS-TE4, while Mean Absolute Error (M) drops to 0.072 and 0.072 respectively. The paper reports an average performance gain of 8.8% over existing synthetic data generation methods across all sub-datasets and metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MaskFactory consistently outperforms both DatasetDM and Dataset Diffusion across all five DIS5K sub-datasets and all five evaluation metrics when using 10,000 generated images. On DIS-VD, MaskFactory achieves maxF1 of 0.835 versus 0.785 (DatasetDM) and 0.767 (Dataset Diffusion), a gap of 5.0 and 6.8 points respectively. A critical finding is that both baselines suffer performance collapse when generated data exceeds 5,000 images (e.g., Dataset Diffusion's maxF1 drops from 0.793 at 2,500 images to 0.767 at 10,000 on DIS-VD), while MaskFactory continues to improve monotonically. Against the IS-Net baseline without any synthetic data augmentation, MaskFactory yields gains of 4.4–5.2 points in maxF1 and 6.7–5.5 points in E_M^φ across sub-datasets. The paper also validates generalizability in Table 2, showing that the same 10,000-image augmentation boosts maxF1 for FP-DIS (0.784→0.805), UDUN (0.784→0.799), BiRefNet (0.866→0.882), and SAM-HQ (0.897→0.905) on DIS-TE1, confirming the method's network-agnostic benefit.

## Relation to Prior Work

MaskFactory composes three established components—Zero123's viewpoint-conditioned diffusion for rigid transformations, MasaCtrl's mutual-attention editing for non-rigid shape changes, and ControlNet's multi-conditional generation for image synthesis—into a pipeline specifically designed for dichotomous image segmentation. Its key departure from prior synthetic-data methods (DatasetDM, Dataset Diffusion) is starting from existing ground-truth masks and editing them rather than generating masks from text prompts, thereby avoiding the noise and imprecision that attention-map-based pseudo-labels introduce. The topology-preserving adversarial training on structural graphs is the paper's principal methodological addition, extending MasaCtrl to maintain binary-mask integrity during shape edits.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

For DIS practitioners who need pixel-precise annotations at scale, MaskFactory offers a practical path to augmenting small datasets (e.g., DIS5K's 3,000 training images) without manual labeling, and the finding that competing text-guided methods degrade in quality beyond ~5,000 generated samples highlights a real risk of naive synthetic-data scaling. However, the method still requires pre-annotated image-mask pairs as input, limits evaluation to a single benchmark (DIS5K), and the ControlNet-based image generation can produce unnatural foreground-background transitions, leaving open whether the approach generalizes to multi-class or instance-level segmentation tasks.
