## Problem

Existing controllable diffusion-based image generation methods struggle to synthesize high-quality, annotation-consistent images for object detection training in domain-specific settings. Three key limitations persist: (1) a domain gap between web-crawled pre-training distributions and specialist domains (e.g., medical imaging, video games), (2) 'concept bleeding' where multiple object categories in a single prompt interfere with each other, and (3) occluded or dense objects being merged or entirely omitted from generated images.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242030/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a set of bounding boxes with associated class names plus a fixed scene name per dataset, structured internally as a padded text list (one 'a <classname>' entry per object), an image list (synthesized 512×512 foreground patches resized and pasted onto an empty canvas at each box location, concatenated in the channel dimension and zero-padded to N channels), and a global text prompt. The output is a synthetic RGB image at the target resolution with the original bounding box annotations retained, after a ResNet50-based foreground/background discriminator filters out any pseudo labels whose regions contain no successfully generated object.

## Method

ODGEN fine-tunes the UNet of Stable Diffusion on both entire images and 512×512 cropped foreground patches from a domain-specific dataset, using templated prompts ('a <scene>' for scenes, 'a <classname>' for objects) to capture both background and foreground distributions. For controllable generation, it extends ControlNet with two object-wise conditioning branches: a text list encoder that separately encodes each class name with frozen CLIP, stacks the N embeddings, and compresses them via a 4-layer convolutional encoder (avoiding the mutual interference of a single global prompt), and an image list encoder that processes the channel-concatenated pasted foreground patches (avoiding occlusion artifacts from overlapping objects on a single canvas). The training loss adds a foreground re-weighting term (γ·L_recon ⊙ M) to emphasize object regions. A dataset synthesis pipeline samples pseudo labels from per-category Gaussian distributions estimated from the training set, generates images via the conditioned model, and filters corrupted labels with a fine-tuned ResNet50 binary classifier, yielding a clean synthetic dataset for detector training.

## Results

On the 7 domain-specific RF7 benchmarks (200 real + 5000 synthetic images), ODGEN achieves the highest mAP@.50:.95 across all datasets, with the largest improvement of +25.3% over the real-data-only baseline on Cotton (YOLOv5s: 16.7→42.0). On COCO-2014 (10k synthetic images, trained from scratch), ODGEN attains the best FID of 16.16 and mAP@.50:.95 of 9.70/14.20 (YOLOv5s/YOLOv7), outperforming all six baselines. When 20k synthetic images are added to the 80k COCO training set, ODGEN further improves mAP@.50:.95 to 33.1/45.9, exceeding the real-data-only baseline (32.6/45.4) and all other methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ODGEN outperforms all six baselines (ReCo, GLIGEN, ControlNet, GeoDiffusion, MIGC, InstanceDiffusion) on every reported metric across both specific and general domains. On RF7, ODGEN's mAP@.50:.95 gains over the real-data-only baseline range from +2.5% (Underwater, YOLOv5s) to +25.3% (Cotton, YOLOv5s), while the best competing method (GeoDiffusion) achieves at most +13.5% (Cotton) and often degrades below baseline. On COCO (10k synthetic, from scratch), ODGEN's mAP@.50:.95 of 9.70/14.20 nearly doubles the next-best InstanceDiffusion (5.42/10.20) and is ~18× ControlNet (0.52/0.38). In FID, ODGEN leads on all 7 RF7 datasets (e.g., 58.21 on Apex Game vs. 88.69 for ReCo) and on COCO (16.16 vs. 18.36 for ReCo). MIGC and InstanceDiffusion, which are only evaluated on COCO, are also clearly surpassed by ODGEN in both fidelity and trainability. The consistent margin across all settings indicates that the object-wise conditioning (image list + text list) and domain-specific fine-tuning are the key differentiators over global-prompt or single-mask-based control strategies.

## Relation to Prior Work

ODGEN builds directly on the ControlNet architecture for controllable latent diffusion and the Stable Diffusion framework, extending both with an object-wise conditioning strategy that departs from the single global text prompt paradigm. Where prior controllable generation methods (ReCo, GeoDiffusion, GLIGEN) encode layout information either into text tokens or a single visual condition, ODGEN introduces per-object text embeddings (via stacked CLIP encodings) and per-object visual patches on separate canvas positions to mitigate concept bleeding and occlusion merging. The fine-tuning on both cropped foreground objects and entire images, combined with a foreground/background discriminator for label filtering, distinguishes it from prior dataset synthesis approaches that either generate-then-annotate or use simple copy-paste augmentation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically critical gap: domain-specific object detection datasets are often small (200 images) and cover specialized domains (medical imaging, video games, underwater scenes) where web-crawled diffusion priors fail. The 25.3% mAP improvement in data-scarce settings and the 5.6% gain on COCO demonstrate that synthetic data from a well-conditioned diffusion model can meaningfully augment detector training. However, the authors acknowledge that synthetic-only training still underperforms real-data training due to residual domain gaps (VAE limitations, imperfect texture synthesis), and the dataset synthesis pipeline (Gaussian sampling of box attributes) is not fully optimized. Open questions include whether next-generation diffusion models will close the real-synthetic gap and whether the object-wise conditioning strategy generalizes to tasks beyond bounding-box-conditioned generation.
