## Problem

Standard continuous-time score-based generative models rely on Brownian motion with independent increments, which is purely Markovian and light-tailed, leading to slow convergence, mode-collapse on imbalanced data, and limited diversity. This paper asks whether replacing BM with fractional Brownian motion (fBM)—a non-Markovian process with correlated increments controlled by a Hurst index—can yield better generative dynamics, while overcoming the theoretical intractability of fBM's non-semimartingale nature.

## Input / Output

The model initializes sampling from a non-isotropic Gaussian with covariance matrix Σ_T of the augmented D(K+1)-dimensional forward process, and optionally conditions on a class label. It produces images at the native resolution of the dataset (28×28 grayscale for MNIST, 32×32 RGB for CIFAR-10). For each data dimension, K+1 coupled trajectories (one data path plus K Ornstein-Uhlenbeck augmenting paths) are simulated backward from noise to data, but only a single D-dimensional score network evaluation is needed per step.

## Method

The forward process replaces BM with a Markov approximation of fBM (MA-fBM), constructed as a linear combination of K Ornstein-Uhlenbeck processes with L²-optimal coefficients on a geometrically spaced grid, driven by a shared Brownian motion. A continuous reparameterization trick yields closed-form marginal mean and variance of the conditional forward process, generalizing the perturbation kernel of standard diffusion. The key training innovation is an augmented score matching loss: by conditioning on the augmenting OU states, the unknown D(K+1)-dimensional score decomposes into a D-dimensional score (learned by a standard U-Net) plus a known closed-form score of the OU processes, so the score model retains data-dimension input/output. The Hurst index H interpolates path roughness—H>0.5 (super-diffusion) regularizes paths and introduces long-term memory, while H<0.5 (sub-diffusion) produces rougher paths—and the number of OU processes K provides an additional diversity knob. Sampling uses either the reverse SDE or the augmented probability flow ODE with any standard solver, requiring the same number of score evaluations as vanilla diffusion models.

## Results

On MNIST, the best GFDM configuration (FVP, H=0.9, K=3) achieves a FID of 0.72, nearly halving the FID of 1.44 from the purely Brownian VP baseline, while also improving pixel-wise diversity (VSp) from 23.64 to 24.18. On CIFAR-10, the best configuration (FVP, H=0.9, K=2) reduces FID from 4.85 (VP) to 3.77 and increases VSp from 3.28 to 3.60. The super-diffusive regime (H>0.5) consistently outperforms both the Brownian (H=0.5) and sub-diffusive (H=0.1) regimes, with FID saturating at a lower level already at 500 NFEs. Class-wise Recall on CIFAR-10 also improves across all 10 classes in the super-diffusive regime compared to VP.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is the retrained purely Brownian VE and VP dynamics from Song et al. [16]. GFDM with MA-fBM driving noise in the super-diffusive regime (H=0.9) clearly outperforms both baselines on both datasets: on MNIST, FVP(H=0.9, K=3) achieves FID 0.72 vs. 1.44 for VP (a 50% reduction) and VSp 24.18 vs. 23.64; on CIFAR-10, FVP(H=0.9, K=2) achieves FID 3.77 vs. 4.85 for VP and VSp 3.60 vs. 3.28. The advantage is most pronounced at H=0.9 with K=2 or K=3 augmenting processes, while performance degrades substantially in the sub-diffusive regime (H=0.1), where FID on CIFAR-10 rises to 5.77–9.57. At H=0.5 (recovering Brownian motion), GFDM with K≥2 augmenting processes still matches or slightly exceeds the VP baseline on VSp but does not improve FID, confirming that the correlated-increment structure (H≠0.5) is the source of the quality gain. The super-diffusive regime also achieves better class-wise Recall across all 10 CIFAR-10 classes compared to VP, indicating improved distribution coverage.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the continuous-time score-based generative model framework of Song et al. [16] by replacing the standard Brownian motion driving noise with a Markov approximation of fractional Brownian motion (MA-fBM), leveraging the infinite-dimensional Markovian representation of fBM from Harms and Stefanovits [26] and the L²-optimal approximation coefficients from Daems et al. [27]. It departs from Yoon et al. [20], who generalized to Lévy processes (which retain independent increments), and from Tong et al. [29], who used correlated noise in a neural SDE but lacked an exact reverse-time model and were restricted to H > 1/3 with Euler-Maruyama solvers. The key technical contribution is an augmented score matching loss that keeps the learned score model at data dimensionality despite the augmented forward process being D(K+1)-dimensional, building on the continuous reparameterization trick generalized from Karras et al. [44].

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper introduces the first continuous-time score-based generative model driven by a process approximating fractional Brownian motion, offering a principled control knob (the Hurst index H) over the roughness of distribution-transforming paths and the degree of long-range memory in the driving noise. The super-diffusive regime (H > 0.5) yields improved FID and pixel-wise diversity over purely Brownian dynamics, but the empirical validation is limited to MNIST and CIFAR10, leaving open whether these gains persist at scale. A notable open question is the behavior of the reverse-time model as the number of augmenting OU processes K → ∞ and whether it connects to a true fBM reverse-time model, whose existence is unknown for H < 0.5. The finding that PF ODE sampling degrades in FID for fractional dynamics while improving diversity suggests a non-trivial quality-diversity trade-off that warrants further investigation.
