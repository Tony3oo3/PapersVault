## Problem

Detecting hallucinations in LLM-generated text requires a truthfulness classifier, but training one demands large-scale human-annotated labels distinguishing truthful from fabricated generations. This paper addresses how to learn effective hallucination detection using only the unlabeled LLM generations that arise organically during real-world deployment, without any manual annotation or extra data collection.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243269/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a text prompt paired with its generated answer, runs a forward pass through the LLM to extract the last-token embedding at a chosen intermediate layer (e.g., layer 14 for LLaMA-2-7b), and produces a continuous truthfulness score in [0,1] via a sigmoid output. A threshold λ on this score yields the binary hallucination label. Notably, the method requires only a single generation per prompt at inference time (no multi-sample consistency checks), giving O(m²) complexity versus O(Km²) for sampling-based baselines.

## Method

HaloScope treats the unlabeled LLM generations as a Huber contamination mixture of truthful and hallucinated distributions. It extracts last-token embeddings from a chosen LLM layer, centers them, and performs SVD to identify a low-dimensional hallucination subspace spanned by the top-k right singular vectors. The membership estimation score ζ_i is the singular-value-weighted squared projection of each embedding onto this subspace; hallucinated samples project farther from the center and thus receive higher scores. Samples above a threshold T are pseudo-labeled as hallucinated, and a two-layer MLP (1024 hidden units, ReLU) is trained with binary sigmoid loss to separate the two pseudo-labeled sets. At test time, the trained MLP produces the final truthfulness score, outperforming direct projection by leveraging the learned decision boundary.

## Results

On the challenging TRUTHFULQA benchmark, HaloScope achieves 78.64% AUROC with LLaMA-2-7b-chat, outperforming the best baseline (CCS* at 67.95%) by 10.69 percentage points and closely matching the supervised upper bound of 81.04%. Across all four QA datasets, HaloScope consistently leads: 77.40% on TRIVIAQA, 76.42% on CoQA, and 94.04% on TYDIQA-GP (LLaMA-2-7b). With OPT-6.7b, the method still achieves 73.17% on TRUTHFULQA and 80.98% on TYDIQA-GP, surpassing all baselines. Notably, HaloScope requires only a single generation per question (O(m²) inference), unlike consistency-based methods that need K repeated samplings (O(Km²)).

## Baselines & Comparisons

HaloScope outperforms all nine baselines across every dataset and both model families. The largest margins appear on TRUTHFULQA: HaloScope (78.64%) beats Semantic Entropy [23] by 16.47 points, EigenScore [6] by 26.71 points, and the strongest baseline CCS* [5] by 10.69 points (LLaMA-2-7b). Among uncertainty-based methods (Perplexity, LN-Entropy, Semantic Entropy), HaloScope leads by 16–27 points on TRUTHFULQA, while consistency-based methods (Lexical Similarity, SelfCKGPT, EigenScore) are competitive on TRIVIAQA but collapse on TYDIQA-GP (44–49% AUROC vs. HaloScope's 94.04%). Prompting-based approaches (Verbalize, Self-evaluation) are the weakest overall, scoring near or below chance on TRIVIAQA and CoQA. The improved CCS* variant (trained on LLM generations rather than human-written answers) narrows the gap to CCS but still trails HaloScope by 10+ points, confirming that the membership estimation via the hallucination subspace is the key differentiator. HaloScope also matches the supervised oracle (81.04% AUROC) within 2.4 points on TRUTHFULQA without any human annotation.

## Relation to Prior Work

HaloScope carves new ground at the intersection of LLM activation analysis and weakly-supervised learning. Prior activation-based methods (INSIDE [6], Representation Engineering [55]) either require multiple generations for covariance estimation or identify a single probing direction, while labeled-data approaches (Azaria & Mitchell [2], Li et al. [26]) depend on costly human annotations. This paper departs from both by formalizing the problem as a Huber contamination mixture and using SVD on unlabeled in-the-wild generations to identify a multi-dimensional hallucination subspace, then training a lightweight MLP classifier on the resulting pseudo-labels. The key conceptual departure from [55] is the use of a subspace (k singular vectors) rather than a single direction, and from [6] is the elimination of the multi-generation requirement.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is substantial: HaloScope achieves near-supervised performance (78.64% vs. 81.04% AUROC on TRUTHFULQA) without any human annotation, using only freely collectible in-the-wild generations and a single inference pass (O(m²) vs. O(Km²) for multi-sampling baselines). This makes hallucination detection deployable in production chat systems where labeled data is unavailable and latency matters. The main open question, acknowledged by the authors, is robustness to distribution shift between the unlabeled training pool and test-time prompts; a distributionally robust training procedure is identified as future work. The finding that the hallucination subspace transfers across domains (76.26% on TRUTHFULQA using a subspace extracted from TRIVIAQA) suggests the learned geometry may be somewhat model-intrinsic rather than dataset-specific.
