## Problem

Generating corrective text instructions that guide a user from a current (source) motion to a desired (target) motion remains an open problem. Existing approaches are limited to specific action categories, static poses, or require costly per-action annotation, and general-purpose LLMs lack the spatial-temporal understanding of motion sequences needed for precise, actionable coaching feedback.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243236/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a source motion sequence x^I and a target motion sequence x^O, both in HumanML3D format (263-dimensional joint positions over T frames, with matching sequence lengths), and produces a natural-language corrective instruction. Before reaching the LLM, each motion is discretized into a sequence of VQ-VAE tokens (64-code codebook, 16-dim), which are organized into a structured prompt template alongside the instruction text. The output instruction is designed to be directly usable as a text condition for a motion editor (e.g., MDM) to reproduce the target from the source.

## Method

CigTime's core insight is to treat corrective instruction generation as the inverse of text-conditioned motion editing. For data collection, a pre-trained MDM diffusion model edits source motions conditioned on corrective instructions to produce target motions, yielding large-scale (source, target, instruction) triplets without manual annotation. A TCN-based VQ-VAE tokenizer (kernel sizes 1/3/9, 64 codes) converts both motions into discrete token sequences. Llama-3-8B is then fully fine-tuned (Adam, lr=1e-5, batch 512, 4×A100, 8 epochs) to auto-regressively generate the corrective instruction from the paired token sequences. A key architectural choice is reusing the LLM's existing vocabulary tokens for motion codes and fine-tuning all embeddings with an anchor loss (L2 regularization toward pre-training weights), rather than introducing new learnable embeddings or relying on LoRA, which the ablations show degrades reconstruction accuracy.

## Results

On HumanML3D, CigTime achieves the best instruction quality across all text metrics: BLEU-4 of 0.24, ROUGE-2 of 0.35, METEOR of 0.52, and CLIPScore of 0.82. For reconstruction accuracy, CigTime attains the lowest MPJPE of 0.13 and FID of 1.44, meaning the generated corrective instruction, when fed back into the motion editor, reproduces the target motion most faithfully. The method is evaluated on three datasets (HumanML3D, Fit3D, KIT) and consistently outperforms all baselines on every metric.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CigTime outperforms all four baseline families on every metric. Against Llama-3-8B (in-context learning), CigTime improves BLEU from 0.15 to 0.24 and MPJPE from 0.21 to 0.13, showing that general-purpose LLMs cannot capture motion-pair discrepancies without task-specific fine-tuning. Against Mistral-7B, the closest competitor on instruction quality (BLEU 0.16, CLIPScore 0.80), CigTime still leads on every metric, with a 50% relative BLEU gain and a 41% MPJPE reduction. Against MotionGPT and its M2T variant, the gap is largest: MotionGPT scores only 0.02 BLEU and 0.80 MPJPE, confirming that models trained for motion captioning lack the ability to articulate differences between two motion sequences. Notably, even LoRA fine-tuning of Llama-3-8B and Mistral-7B degrades instruction quality (BLEU drops to 0.10 and 0.08 respectively) while only marginally improving reconstruction, indicating that parameter-efficient adapters are insufficient for this cross-modal task.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions corrective instruction generation as the inverse of text-conditioned motion editing, building directly on MDM as the motion editor to synthesize training triplets (source, target, instruction) without manual annotation. It departs from prior corrective feedback work (PoseFix, AIFit, Pose Tutor) that was restricted to static poses or narrow action categories, and from motion-language models (MotionGPT, T2M-GPT) that generate captions or motions from text but cannot compare two motion sequences. The VQ-VAE tokenization and LLM fine-tuning pipeline draws on the TM2T/MotionGPT lineage, but the anchor-loss regularization for token embeddings and the structured inverse-problem data collection are novel contributions specific to this task.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper opens a practical pathway toward intelligent coaching systems by showing that the rapidly advancing motion-editing literature can be repurposed to solve the understudied inverse problem of generating corrective text from motion pairs. Key limitations remain: the method requires source and target sequences of identical length, lacks scene/context awareness, and generalization to unseen datasets (Fit3D) shows a dramatic drop in text-metric scores (BLEU 0.24→0.03) even while reconstruction quality holds. Open questions include whether the approach scales to variable-length sequences, multi-person interactions, or domain-specific biomechanical feedback, and whether the reliance on a single motion editor (MDM) constrains the diversity of learnable corrections.
