## Problem

Existing RGB-Event single-object trackers focus on exploiting complementary appearance information across modalities but fail to address the target-distractor association problem in the temporal domain. Event streams contain rich motion cues that could distinguish visually similar targets from distractors, yet prior methods largely ignore this motion information for candidate matching. The paper asks whether introducing a Multi-Object Tracking (MOT) philosophy—simultaneously tracking targets and distractors—into RGB-E SOT, guided by both appearance and motion embeddings, can resolve this association gap.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB frames (template cropped at 2× target area, resized to 128×128; search at 4× target area, resized to 256×256) and event streams voxelized into 1024 template and 4096 search voxels with 16-dimensional features. The appearance model (CEUTrack) generates M candidates for the current frame and N candidates for each of T=15 previous frames via NMS on classification scores. The output is a per-frame target bounding box, determined by matching the current-frame candidate whose tracklet best aligns with the historical target tracklet through an N×M assignment matrix computed by the candidate matching network.

## Method

CSAM is a cascade framework: an appearance model (CEUTrack with ViT backbone) first proposes candidates, then a Candidate Encoding Module (CEM) fuses RGB features with classification scores into appearance embeddings and event features with bounding-box locations into motion embeddings for each candidate. A Spatial-Temporal Transformer Encoder (STTE) models inter-candidate spatial relations via a graph attention mechanism where motion embeddings define edge weights between appearance nodes, followed by a temporal encoder that encodes each candidate's T-frame tracklet. A Dual-branch Transformer Decoder (DBTD) produces the final assignment matrix by summing an appearance-branch score and a motion-branch score, enabling joint appearance-motion matching of tracklets to current-frame candidates. Training proceeds in three stages (RGB pre-training, RGB-E appearance model training, then candidate matching network training with the appearance model frozen), using partial supervision and self-supervised losses adapted from KeepTrack since only target annotations are available in RGB-E datasets.

## Results

CSAM-B (ViT-Base backbone) achieves state-of-the-art performance across all three RGB-E tracking benchmarks: on FE108, it reaches 70.5% RSR and 97.1% RPR; on VisEvent, it achieves 81.6% PR, 78.6% NPR, and 65.9% SR; and on COESOT, it obtains 76.7% PR, 74.8% NPR, and 68.1% SR. The lighter CSAM-T variant (ViT-Tiny) also outperforms all prior methods, reaching 76.1% PR and 72.4% NPR on VisEvent. In terms of efficiency, CSAM-B runs at 53 FPS on an RTX 3090 with 106.9M parameters and 83.2G FLOPS, maintaining real-time performance despite tracking multiple candidates simultaneously.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On FE108, CSAM-B surpasses the strongest event-only tracker HRMonTrack-B [45] by 2.0% RSR (70.5 vs. 68.5) and 0.9% RPR (97.1 vs. 96.2), and outperforms HDETrack [30] by 10.7% RSR. On VisEvent, CSAM-B leads the previous best SeqTrackv2-L256 [4] by 1.0% PR (81.6 vs. 80.6), 0.8% NPR (78.6 vs. 77.8), and 0.7% SR (65.9 vs. 65.2), and beats SDSTrack [31] by 2.3% PR and 3.1% NPR. On COESOT, CSAM-B improves over ViPT [42] by 2.8% PR (76.7 vs. 73.9), 2.6% NPR (74.8 vs. 72.2), and 2.4% SR (68.1 vs. 65.7), and over HRCEUTrack-L [45] by 2.9% PR and 3.1% SR. The ablation in Table 5 further shows that CSAM's candidate matching outperforms KeepTrack [19] (75.1/65.7) and TranSTAM [23] (75.4/66.3) in PR/SR, confirming that leveraging event-stream motion cues for tracklet association is more effective than RGB-only MOT strategies. In the distractor-specific analysis (Table 4), CSAM gains 3.5% PR and 5.1% SR over the appearance-only tracker on sequences containing distractors, validating the MOT philosophy's core benefit.

## Relation to Prior Work

CSAM builds directly on CEUTrack as its appearance model and on KeepTrack's partial-supervision and self-supervised training strategy for the candidate matching network. Conceptually, it departs from the dominant RGB-E tracking paradigm—which focuses on fusing complementary appearance cues across modalities—by importing the Multi-Object Tracking philosophy (track both targets and distractors, then associate via a learned matching network) into the RGB-E SOT setting for the first time. The spatial encoder's use of motion-derived edge weights in a graph-attention structure is a deliberate departure from standard self-attention, motivated by the observation that event-stream motion cues are more discriminative than appearance for establishing inter-candidate spatial affinities.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By showing that motion information from event streams can be explicitly exploited for tracklet-candidate association, the paper opens a path for RGB-E trackers to handle distractor-heavy scenes more robustly than appearance-only methods, with a clean ablation (Table 4) confirming the gain is concentrated on sequences containing distractors. The efficiency result—53 FPS at higher accuracy than SeqTrackv2-L's 15 FPS—suggests the MOT-philosophy overhead is manageable in practice. The authors acknowledge limited effort on supervision-signal efficiency as a limitation, and it remains open whether the graph-attention spatial encoder and dual-branch decoder generalize to other multi-modal tracking settings (e.g., RGB-infrared) or to longer temporal windows without the current T=15 constraint.
