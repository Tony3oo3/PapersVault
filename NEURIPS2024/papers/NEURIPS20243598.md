## Problem

Current geometric deep learning docking methods are specialized for either blind or site-specific docking, neglect protein side-chain structures, struggle with large binding pockets, and generate physically invalid poses (DiffDock achieves only 14% success on PoseBusters when physical validity is enforced, versus 38% without). No existing GDL framework unifies both docking settings while simultaneously ensuring structural plausibility and computational efficiency.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243598/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a fixed (rigid) holo protein structure and an RDKit-generated 3D ligand conformer as input, operating under the rigid-receptor assumption where only ligand flexibility is modeled. For blind docking, candidate pockets are generated by DSDP and P2Rank (residues within 15 Å of predicted site centers); for site-specific docking, the pocket is user-provided. The output is a refined ligand 3D coordinate set that has passed through torsion-angle alignment and, when steric clashes are detected, SMINA-based energy minimization, ensuring bond lengths and angles remain physically plausible.

## Method

DeltaDock is a two-stage framework. Stage 1 (CPLA) reframes pocket prediction as a contrastive pocket-ligand alignment problem: an Attentive-FP encoder maps the ligand and a GVP encoder maps the full protein residue graph into a shared embedding space, and a contrastive loss (Eq. 4) trains the model to rank the true pocket above all candidates from an ensemble of DSDP and P2Rank predictions. Stage 2 (Bi-EGMN) performs site-specific docking via a bi-level coarse-to-fine iterative refinement: a residue-level pass over a 40 Å cubic box handles pose positioning, followed by an atom-level pass within 6 Å of the coarse structure for fine-grained interaction modeling, both using E(3)-equivariant message-passing layers with recycling. The module is initialized by a GPU-accelerated DSDP sampling step, trained with a physics-informed loss combining inter/intra distance-map terms, van der Waals clash penalties, and bond-matrix constraints, and post-processed by torsion alignment plus conditional SMINA energy minimization to guarantee physical validity.

## Results

On the PDBbind blind docking benchmark (Time Split, 363 pairs), DeltaDock achieves a 47.4% docking success rate (RMSD < 2.0 Å), surpassing the previous SOTA GDL method DiffDock (36.0%) by 31% relatively, while running in only 2.97 s per pair versus DiffDock's 80 s. On the PoseBusters dataset, which enforces 18 physical validity checks, DeltaDock's success rate improves by approximately 300% relative to DiffDock when physical validity is considered. In site-specific docking, DeltaDock also outperforms all baselines, including traditional methods like VINA (45.0% → DeltaDock higher) and GDL methods like TANKBind.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DeltaDock outperforms DiffDock (previous SOTA GDL) by 11.4 percentage points on RMSD < 2Å (47.4% vs 36.0%) and by ~300% relative on PoseBusters when physical validity is enforced. Against FABind+ (the strongest GDL competitor), DeltaDock wins on RMSD < 2Å (47.4% vs 43.8%) and Centroid < 2Å (66.7% vs 59.1%), but FABind+ leads on RMSD < 5Å (73.3% vs 66.9%) and Centroid < 5Å (86.2% vs 83.2%). DeltaDock surpasses the top GPU-accelerated sampling method DSDP on all four metrics (e.g., 47.4% vs 40.2% RMSD < 2Å) despite using DSDP's own sampling algorithm only for initialization. Traditional methods (VINA, SMINA, GNINA, QVINA-W, GLIDE) are all substantially outperformed, with VINA achieving only 10.3% RMSD < 2Å in blind docking. On PoseBusters, GDL methods like TANKBind, DeepDock, and Uni-Mol suffer severe drops in success rate when physical validity checks are applied, while DeltaDock maintains strong performance thanks to its physics-informed loss and structure correction steps.

## Relation to Prior Work

DeltaDock sits at the intersection of GDL-based docking (DiffDock, EquiBind, FABind) and GPU-accelerated sampling methods (DSDP, SMINA), explicitly bridging the two paradigms that prior work treated as separate. It builds on AlphaFold's recycling strategy for iterative refinement and on DSDP's GPU-accelerated sampling for initialization, but departs from prior GDL docking by (i) reframing pocket prediction as a contrastive pocket-ligand alignment problem rather than direct site prediction, and (ii) introducing a bi-level residue/atom coarse-to-fine refinement with physics-informed losses and SMINA-based post-processing to address the physical validity failures highlighted by PoseBusters. The key conceptual departure is treating the docking pipeline as a hybrid GDL + physics-informed system rather than a purely learned end-to-end model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ~300% improvement in physically valid docking success rate (PoseBusters) addresses a critical gap that has limited practical adoption of GDL docking methods, making them more trustworthy for structure-based drug design. The 3-second inference time per protein-ligand pair positions DeltaDock as a viable tool for large-scale virtual screening. A key limitation acknowledged by the authors is the reliance on external tools (DSDP sampling for initialization, SMINA for energy minimization), and the ablation showing that removing the sampling initialization drops RMSD<2Å from 47.4% to 16.0% reveals a tension with the 'GDL eliminates sampling' narrative. Future work on pre-training with large-scale docking data or AlphaFold3-generated structures may reduce this dependency.
