## Problem

Existing test-time adaptation methods for vision-language models like CLIP suffer from two key limitations: they treat each test sample independently without accumulating task-specific knowledge over time, and they adapt the model from only a single modality (either textual or visual). This paper addresses both gaps by proposing a method that progressively evolves dual prototypes from both modalities as unlabeled test samples stream in.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241010/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a sequential stream of unlabeled RGB test images and a fixed set of hand-crafted text class descriptions (e.g., 'a photo of a {CLASS}'). For each incoming image, it generates 63 augmented views via random resized cropping to form a batch of 64, then optimizes prototype residuals in a single gradient step. The output is a per-image class label produced by a softmax over cosine similarities between the image feature and the evolved dual prototypes, weighted by an affinity function that balances textual and visual contributions.

## Method

DPE maintains two sets of class-specific prototypes in CLIP's shared embedding space: textual prototypes updated via cumulative averaging of per-sample optimized text features, and visual prototypes computed from a per-class priority queue that retains the top-M highest-confidence image features (sorted by self-entropy). For each test sample, learnable residual parameters are added to both prototype sets and optimized in a single step using a combined objective of entropy minimization across augmented views and a contrastive InfoNCE alignment loss that pulls same-class textual-visual prototype pairs together while pushing cross-class pairs apart. This design eliminates the need to backpropagate through CLIP's text encoder (unlike TPT/DiffTPT), yielding 5× and 10× speedups, while the evolving prototypes enable cumulative knowledge accumulation that single-sample methods lack.

## Results

On the robustness-to-distribution-shifts benchmark (ImageNet + 4 OOD variants), DPE achieves 50.81% average top-1 accuracy with ResNet-50 and 65.93% with ViT-B/16, outperforming zero-shot CLIP by 6.63% and 6.82% respectively. On the cross-dataset generalization benchmark (10 fine-grained datasets), DPE reaches 61.93% (ResNet-50) and 69.40% (ViT-B/16) average accuracy. DPE also demonstrates strong efficiency: 1h50min test time on 50K ImageNet samples, roughly 5× faster than TPT (9h15min) and over 10× faster than DiffTPT (>20h).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DPE consistently outperforms all seven baselines across both evaluation scenarios. Against prompt-tuning methods, DPE surpasses TPT by 3.55% (ResNet-50) / 3.49% (ViT-B/16) and DiffTPT by 2.10% / 3.65% on average, while also being 5× and 10×+ faster at test time. Against cache/memory-based methods, DPE beats TDA by 1.23% / 0.92% and DMN-ZS by 0.84% (ResNet-50), and TPS by 1.43%. On the cross-dataset generalization benchmark, DPE achieves 69.40% average (ViT-B/16), exceeding TPT (65.10%), DiffTPT (65.47%), and TDA (67.53%) by 4.30%, 3.93%, and 1.87% respectively. DPE also outperforms the train-time CoOp baseline (which requires 16-shot labels) by 4.20% / 4.21% on the distribution-shift benchmark, confirming that test-time adaptation can match or exceed supervised fine-tuning in this setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the test-time adaptation line for CLIP initiated by TPT (Shu et al., 2022) and its diffusion-augmented variant DiffTPT (Feng et al., 2023), which optimize learnable prompts via entropy minimization over augmented views. It also draws on the visual-cache idea from TDA (Karmanov et al., 2024) and the dynamic-memory approach of DMN (Zhang et al., 2024). The key departure is that DPE unifies both modalities into a single evolving prototype framework—textual prototypes updated via cumulative averaging and visual prototypes maintained through a priority queue—while replacing prompt backpropagation with lightweight residual optimization in the embedding space, coupled with an InfoNCE-based alignment loss between the two prototype sets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By accumulating task-specific knowledge across both modalities as test samples arrive, DPE addresses a practical gap where prior methods either reset per-sample (TPT, DiffTPT) or adapt only one modality (TDA, TPS). The 5× and 10× efficiency gains over TPT and DiffTPT make test-time VLM adaptation more deployable in streaming settings. However, the method still requires gradient backpropagation for residual learning and incurs memory overhead from priority queues, limiting real-time applicability. Open questions include whether the dual-prototype paradigm extends to generative or open-vocabulary tasks, and whether the alignment loss generalizes beyond classification to detection or segmentation.
