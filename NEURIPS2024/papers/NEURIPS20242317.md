## Problem

Transformer-based LLMs exhibit order dependency: their outputs change significantly when semantically identical sub-sequences (e.g., multiple-choice options) are reordered, undermining reliability and raising fairness concerns in decision-making applications. While this phenomenon is well-documented, no existing method provides a provable guarantee of order-independence without requiring additional training or fine-tuning of the model.

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard text prompt in which a designated set of sub-sequences (e.g., MCQ answer options) is marked as 'parallel' using three special tokens (start-parallel, new-sub-sequence, end-parallel) that are stripped during inference. The model's attention mask and positional encoding are modified at inference time so that tokens within each parallel sub-sequence are indexed relative to their own sub-sequence start, and cross-sub-sequence attention is blocked. The output is standard generated text (or a selected MCQ answer) that is provably identical under any permutation of the parallel sub-sequences, with no change to the model weights.

## Method

Set-Based Prompting is a two-pronged, inference-time modification to any transformer-based LLM that requires no fine-tuning. First, the positional encoding is reset at the beginning of each parallel sub-sequence so that tokens at the same relative position within different sub-sequences receive identical positional vectors (for both absolute and RoPE encodings). Second, a modified attention mask M_{i,j}^{k,f} is introduced that zeros out attention between tokens belonging to different parallel sub-sequences while preserving causal attention within each sub-sequence and to/from tokens outside the parallel region. The key theoretical insight is that the attention operation is a commutative weighted sum, so if both the positional encoding and the mask are invariant under any permutation of the parallel sub-sequences, the output is provably order-independent (Theorem 1). Empirically, the method is a drop-in for GPT-2, Llama 2/3, and Mistral models, and while it slightly reduces accuracy due to out-of-distribution inputs and reduced cross-option information, the degradation is typically within the range of accuracy variation already caused by reordering without the method.

## Results

Set-Based Prompting provably eliminates order dependency for any transformer-based LLM by modifying the attention mask and positional encoding at inference time, with no fine-tuning required. On CommonsenseQA (9,741 questions) and MMLU, the accuracy under Set-Based Prompting falls within the range of accuracy variation caused by simply reordering the multiple-choice options for all tested models (GPT-2 1.5B, Llama 2 7B/13B, Llama 3 8B, Mistral 7B), except Llama 3 8B Instruct which shows mild degradation. Across all 24 permutations of MMLU option orderings, Set-Based Prompting accuracy for Llama 2 models sits above or within the inter-quartile range of the 24 order-dependent runs. In a sentiment classification in-context learning task, Set-Based Prompting applied to the example sentences often outperforms even the Best-of-2 order-dependent baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against other order-independence methods; instead, the baseline is the standard order-dependent LLM run, evaluated under normal and reversed option orderings. The key comparison is whether Set-Based Prompting's single (order-invariant) accuracy falls within the [Worst-of-2, Best-of-2] range of the order-dependent runs. For GPT-2, Llama 2 7B/13B (base and chat), and Mistral 7B Instruct, Set-Based Prompting accuracy is within or above this range on both CSQA and MMLU, indicating the method removes a dimension of spurious variation without adding or losing information. Llama 3 8B Instruct is the notable exception, where Set-Based Prompting accuracy drops below the Best-of-2 range, likely because the method pushes inputs outside the fine-tuning distribution. Ablations show that modifying only the attention mask reduces order-dependent variation more than modifying only the positional encoding, and that adding explicit option numbers (which breaks the order-independence guarantee) improves accuracy across all orderings, confirming the models were trained with enumerated options.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the prior line of work on MCQ order-dependence (Pezeshkpour et al., 2023; Zheng et al., 2024; Adian Liusie, 2024), which all operate by *estimating and correcting* for positional or token biases at inference time, requiring calibration data or knowledge of the bias distribution. In contrast, Set-Based Prompting takes a structural approach: it provably eliminates order dependency by modifying the attention mask and positional encoding so that parallel sub-sequences are processed without any ordering signal, requiring no fine-tuning or calibration. The method repurposes existing transformer components (causal masking, RoPE/absolute positional encodings) in a novel configuration—treating designated sub-sequences as parallel rather than sequential—drawing on the commutativity of the attention sum to establish invariance under arbitrary permutations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The provable order-independence guarantee is a meaningful step toward reliable LLM-based decision systems, where sensitivity to input ordering raises fairness and trust concerns (e.g., candidate comparison, medical summarization). The finding that base models tolerate out-of-distribution positional encodings and non-standard attention masks with only modest accuracy loss suggests a broader, unexplored space of input-representation modifications that could encode 'metadata' (privilege levels, typography, graph structure) without retraining. Open questions include why the method works without training, whether incorporating it during pre-training would close the remaining accuracy gap, and whether the approach extends to more complex sub-sequence topologies (nested parallelism, directed graphs).
