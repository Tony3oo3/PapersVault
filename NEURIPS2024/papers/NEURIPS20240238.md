## Problem

Standard self-attention in Vision Transformers for image restoration connects every patch to all others within a window, incurring quadratic computational cost and incorporating semantically irrelevant information that hinders restoration quality. While KNN-based sparse attention (e.g., KiT) identifies relevant patches, it recomputes the neighbor set at every layer, negating efficiency gains. The paper asks whether a single key-semantic connection structure can be built once per stage and shared across all subsequent layers without degrading performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240238/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

SemanIR accepts a single degraded image (noisy, motion-blurred, low-resolution, JPEG-compressed, Bayer-mosaic, or weather-degraded) at arbitrary resolution and outputs a full-resolution restored image in the same color space (RGB or grayscale). The input is split into non-overlapping patches within local windows; the key-semantic dictionary is constructed per window, so the method scales to high-resolution inputs where standard MSA becomes prohibitive. Two backbone architectures are supported: a columnar multi-stage design for super-resolution and a U-shaped hierarchical design for all other restoration tasks.

## Method

At the start of each transformer stage, SemanIR computes a full patch self-similarity matrix via dot product and applies a KNN threshold to retain only the top-k most semantically related patches per query, forming a sparse key-semantic dictionary D_K. This dictionary is then shared across all transformer layers within the stage, exploiting the permutation-invariance of both MSA and FFN so that the same semantic neighborhood remains valid as features evolve. During attention, each query patch attends only to its k selected key/value patches, reducing per-window complexity from O(M²) to O(k). Three implementation variants are provided—Triton (custom FlashAttention-style GPU kernel for inference), Torch-Gather (index-based selection, no redundant compute but high memory), and Torch-Mask (masking with −∞, affordable memory for training)—and a random top-k training strategy (k sampled from [64,512]) decouples training from inference k for deployment flexibility.

## Results

SemanIR achieves state-of-the-art performance across all 6 evaluated IR tasks. For 4× image SR, SemanIR-B reaches 33.08/29.34/27.98/28.51/32.56 dB PSNR on Set5/Set14/BSD100/Urban100/Manga109, outperforming EDT by up to 0.76 dB on Urban100. For deblurring, SemanIR achieves 33.44 dB PSNR on GoPro (0.52 dB over Restormer) and 31.05 dB on HIDE. For denoising (σ=50, one-for-multi), SemanIR attains 30.38/30.51/30.41 dB on McMaster/Urban100/CBSD68 (color) and 28.14/28.86 dB on BSD68/Urban100 (grayscale), surpassing Xformer and Restormer while using fewer parameters (25.82M vs. 26.13M).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SemanIR consistently outperforms or matches all 48 baselines across the six IR tasks. In 4× SR, SemanIR-B surpasses EDT (the strongest prior method) by 0.76 dB on Urban100 and 0.17 dB on Manga109, while SemanIR-S (12.02M params) already beats SwinIR (11.90M) by 0.89 dB on Urban100 with comparable parameter count. For deblurring, SemanIR leads Restormer by 0.52 dB on GoPro (33.44 vs. 32.92) and is second only to Ren et al. (33.20) on HIDE. In denoising (one-for-multi σ), SemanIR beats Xformer by 0.04–0.15 dB across color datasets and by 0.11–0.15 dB on grayscale Urban100, while using fewer parameters than Restormer (25.82M vs. 26.13M). For JPEG CAR, SemanIR achieves the best PSNR at every quality factor (QF 10–40) on both LIVE1 and BSDSS500, exceeding GRL-S by 0.06–0.18 dB. In adverse weather restoration, SemanIR outperforms TransWeather by 1.61 dB on Test1 (rain+fog) and 2.28 dB on SnowTest100k-L, and matches SwinIR on RainDrop. For demosaicking, SemanIR edges out GRL by 0.05 dB on Kodak and 0.46 dB on McMaster.

## Relation to Prior Work

SemanIR builds on the window-based attention paradigm of SwinIR and the U-shaped hierarchical architecture of Restormer, but replaces position-fixed window connections with content-based KNN-selected connections inspired by KiT's local attention. The key departure is the sharing strategy: rather than recomputing KNN matching at every transformer layer (as KiT does) or applying token pruning/merging (as DynamicViT or A-ViT do), SemanIR constructs a key-semantic dictionary once per stage and reuses it across all layers within that stage, justified by the permutation-invariant property of self-attention and FFN. This positions the work between dense window attention (quadratic in window size) and aggressive token pruning (information loss), offering a sparse yet semantically meaningful middle ground.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The linear-in-k complexity (vs. quadratic-in-window-size for W-MSA) makes high-resolution image restoration substantially more tractable, and the architecture-agnostic design (columnar for SR, U-shaped for other tasks) broadens applicability. However, each IR task still requires separate training, and the tens-of-millions parameter budget remains challenging for on-device deployment. Open questions include whether a single unified model can handle all degradation types, whether per-patch learnable k values could further adapt the sparsity, and whether the sharing assumption degrades when semantic relationships shift significantly across layers within a stage.
