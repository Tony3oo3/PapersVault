## Problem

Existing second-order methods for convex-concave minimax optimization achieve the optimal O(1/T^1.5) rate but require either a line search to resolve the implicit step-size condition or a specialized cubic sub-problem solver, and both rely on exact knowledge of the Hessian Lipschitz constant. This paper addresses the open problem of designing a simple, line-search-free, parameter-free second-order method that achieves the optimal rate without any of these requirements.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes a twice-differentiable convex-concave objective f(x,y) and requires only the gradient and Hessian (Jacobian of the monotone operator F) at each iterate, plus the previous iterate's prediction error. Per iteration it solves a single linear system (λ_t I + η_t F'(z_t))^{-1} with no backtracking or sub-problem solver. The output is a weighted averaged iterate z̄_{T+1} = (Ση_t)^{-1} Ση_t z_{t+1} whose restricted primal-dual gap converges at O(T^{-1.5}), along with a best-iterate guarantee on the operator norm at O(1/T).

## Method

The method builds on the second-order optimistic update, which approximates the proximal point method by combining a linear (Hessian) prediction of the next gradient with a correction term from the previous iteration's prediction error. The key innovation is a closed-form, recursive step-size rule η_t that solves a quadratic equation derived from enforcing the error condition η_t‖e_{t+1}‖ ≤ αλ_t‖z_t − z_{t+1}‖ with equality, using only quantities available at iteration t (‖F(z_t)‖, ‖e_t‖, and the previous step size). Two variants are provided: Option I sets λ_t = L_2 (known Hessian Lipschitz constant), while Option II is fully parameter-free by adaptively tracking a local curvature estimate λ_t = max{λ_{t-1}, 2‖e_t‖/‖z_t − z_{t-1}‖²}, which monotonically grows to an upper bound on L_2. Under the additional assumption of L_1-Lipschitz gradients, the parameter-free variant provably keeps iterates bounded without any compactness assumption, yielding the same O(T^{-1.5}) rate with constants degrading only by a factor of (L_2/λ_1)^{2.5} relative to the known-L_2 case.

## Results

The proposed adaptive second-order optimistic methods achieve the optimal convergence rate of O(1/T^{1.5}) for the restricted primal-dual gap at the averaged iterate, matching the known lower bound for second-order methods. The operator norm at the best iterate converges at O(1/T). In runtime experiments on a synthetic min-max problem (d up to 5×10^5, L_2 up to 10^4) and an AUC maximization problem (d=10^4), both variants (Adaptive SOM I and II) consistently outperform the line-search-based Optimal SOM and HIPNEX, with the gap widening as dimension and L_2 increase due to the per-iteration cost of Hessian inversion in line search.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two baselines: (1) Optimal SOM [21], the second-order optimistic method with a line-search/backtracking step size, and (2) HIPNEX [30], a search-free homotopy inexact proximal-Newton extragradient method. On the synthetic min-max problem at large dimensions (d=10^5 and d=5×10^5 with L_2=10^4), both Adaptive SOM I and II achieve faster wall-clock convergence than Optimal SOM and HIPNEX; the advantage over Optimal SOM grows with dimension because line search requires additional Hessian evaluations and matrix inversions per backtracking step. On the AUC maximization problem (d=10^4, L_2 up to 10^4), both proposed methods again outperform the baselines in runtime, particularly in the early iterations. The key practical distinction is that Adaptive SOM II is fully parameter-free (no knowledge of L_2 required), whereas Optimal SOM and HIPNEX both require the Hessian Lipschitz constant, and Adaptive SOM I requires L_2 but avoids line search entirely.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the second-order optimistic method of Jiang & Mokhtari [21], which approximates the proximal point method using first- and second-order information but requires a line search to select the step size. It also builds on the broader family of second-order minimax methods (Newton proximal extragradient [18,19], cubic-regularized approaches [22,23]) that all achieve the optimal O(1/T^1.5) rate but either require line search, solve auxiliary cubic sub-problems, or demand exact knowledge of the Hessian Lipschitz constant. The key departure is introducing an adaptive regularization parameter λ_t that tracks local curvature, enabling a closed-form, recursive step size update that is simultaneously line-search-free and parameter-free.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first second-order minimax method that is simultaneously line-search-free, sub-problem-free, and parameter-free, removing all three practical barriers that have limited the adoption of optimal second-order methods in saddle-point problems. The boundedness proof for the parameter-free variant (without assuming compact iterates) is a notable technical contribution, as most prior adaptive methods impose this restriction artificially. An open question the authors flag is whether the parameter-free guarantee can be extended without the additional Lipschitz gradient assumption (Assumption 2.3), which currently limits the method's applicability to problems with bounded gradients.
