## Problem

Momentum-based optimization on Lie groups lacks both efficient implementations and quantitative convergence guarantees. Existing manifold momentum methods require costly operations like parallel transport and the logarithm map (which may not be uniquely defined), while the natural splitting discretization of the Lie group momentum ODE (Lie Heavy-Ball) provably fails to accelerate convergence over momentumless Riemannian gradient descent. This paper provides the first quantitative convergence analysis for Lie group momentum optimizers and identifies a discretization that achieves genuine acceleration.

## Input / Output

The optimizers require only the Riemannian gradient oracle and the exponential map on the Lie group — no logarithm map or parallel transport is needed, which is a significant computational advantage over general manifold methods. The left-trivialization of the gradient (T_g L_{g^{-1}} ∇U(g)) converts the tangent-space gradient into a Lie algebra vector, so all momentum updates occur in the fixed Euclidean space g. The output is a sequence of group elements converging to a local minimum, with explicit rate bounds depending on the condition number κ = L/μ and, for NAG-SC, a curvature-dependent term 1/p(a) from the Lie group geometry.

## Method

Starting from the continuous-time momentum ODE on Lie groups (derived via variational optimization and left-trivialization), the paper constructs two structure-preserving time discretizations. Lie Heavy-Ball uses a simple splitting scheme that exactly preserves the manifold geometry but yields only a linear condition-number dependence (1 + μ/(16L))^{-1}, matching momentumless GD. Lie NAG-SC introduces an O(h²) gradient-difference correction term — the Lie group analogue of Nesterov's lookahead — which, thanks to left-trivialization, transfers directly from the Euclidean NAG-SC without requiring parallel transport. A Lyapunov analysis under L-smoothness and local geodesic-μ-strong convexity proves that Lie NAG-SC achieves the accelerated rate (1 + (1/30)√μ · min{1/√(2L), 1/(2p(a))})^{-1}, where p(a) encodes the curvature of the Lie group. The choice of a left-invariant metric making ad skew-adjoint eliminates the Coriolis-like term ad*_ξ ξ, simplifying both the dynamics and the discretization.

## Results

Theoretically, Lie NAG-SC achieves a convergence rate of $(1 + \frac{1}{30}\sqrt{\mu}\min\{1/\sqrt{2L},\, 1/(2p(a))\})^{-1}$, giving square-root condition number dependence $\sqrt{\kappa}$, while Lie Heavy-Ball only achieves $(1 + \mu/(16L))^{-1}$, i.e., linear $\kappa$ dependence matching momentumless Riemannian GD. Empirically, on a 6.3M-parameter Vision Transformer trained from scratch on CIFAR, Lie NAG-SC achieves 8.77% validation error on CIFAR-10 and 31.38% on CIFAR-100, outperforming Lie Heavy-Ball (9.12% / 31.93%) and Euclidean SGD (9.84% / 32.68%). Numerical experiments on the eigenvalue decomposition problem on SO(10) confirm the predicted $\kappa^{-1}$ vs. $\kappa^{-1/2}$ scaling of the convergence rate.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two baselines. First, Riemannian gradient descent (Lie GD, Eq. 1) from Zhang & Sra [32] with step size 1/L serves as the momentumless reference; Lie NAG-SC provably accelerates over it by reducing the condition number dependence from $\kappa$ to $\sqrt{\kappa}$, while Lie Heavy-Ball does not. Second, the existing Lie Heavy-Ball optimizer from Kong et al. [19] (identical to the 'Momentum Stiefel Optimizer' on SO(n)) is the direct momentum-based competitor; Lie NAG-SC outperforms it both in theory (square-root vs. linear condition number dependence) and in practice, achieving 0.35 percentage points lower validation error on CIFAR-10 (8.77% vs. 9.12%) and 0.55 points lower on CIFAR-100 (31.38% vs. 31.93%). On the non-convex eigenvalue decomposition problem, Lie NAG-SC also outperforms Lie Heavy-Ball in local convergence speed, especially at high condition numbers, and successfully escapes local minima to find the global minimum in experiments initialized near the global maximum.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Tao and Ohsawa's [26] variational optimization framework on Lie groups, which provided the continuous-time momentum ODE via left-trivialization and a splitting discretization. The authors rename that splitting scheme 'Lie Heavy-Ball' and show it inherits the well-known Euclidean limitation of no acceleration (linear condition number dependence). The key departure is a new discretization, Lie NAG-SC, inspired by Nesterov's NAG-SC [21] and the high-resolution ODE analysis of Shi et al. [25], which transfers the √κ acceleration to the Lie group setting while avoiding the logarithm map and parallel transport required by general manifold methods [33, 1, 4].

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first quantitative convergence analysis for discrete-time momentum optimizers on Lie groups, filling a gap between continuous-time theory [3] and practical algorithms. The negative result—that the natural splitting discretization does not accelerate—guides practitioners away from a seemingly reasonable choice. Open questions include whether the curvature-dependent factor 1/p(a) in the NAG-SC rate is fundamental to curved geometry or an artifact of the proof technique, whether a monotone modified energy exists for NAG-SC (enabling global convergence guarantees), and whether the acceleration extends to non-compact or negatively curved Lie groups where obstructions are known [16, 10].
