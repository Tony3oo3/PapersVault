## Problem

Existing strand-based hair generation methods extract guide strands by regular grid sampling on a scalp UV map, which introduces irrelevant high-frequency noise into guide curves, misses critical hairstyle details, and produces suboptimal density distributions due to non-area-preserving UV mapping. No prior work leverages the natural frequency hierarchy of hair (smooth principal growth directions vs. high-frequency curls) or provides a theoretically optimal strategy for selecting representative guide strands from dense hair data.

## Input / Output

The model is conditioned on a 1024-dimensional VAE latent code sampled from a learned Gaussian prior and produces a three-level hierarchical output: 512 off-the-grid low-pass filtered guide curves (each a 16-point 3D polyline), an arbitrary number of dense strands (up to 10,000) at any spatial density, and per-strand high-frequency DCT coefficients (32 harmonics) encoding curliness and noise. Guide curve root positions are not constrained to a fixed UV grid; instead, they are sampled from a learned density map, and dense strands can be queried at any root location on the scalp manifold.

## Method

The core contribution is a doubly hierarchical VAE that first generates coarse guide curves via a PVCNN-inspired dual-branch encoder/decoder (PointNet branch for permutation-equivariant point features + 2D convolution branch on a 64×64 UV grid), then densifies to arbitrary strand counts via graph message passing over neighbouring guide curves combined with bilinear grid sampling (an implicit neural representation strategy), and finally refines high-frequency DCT coefficients with a conditional VAE. Guide strands are extracted from dense hair using k-medoids clustering on DCT low-pass filtered curves, which the paper proves (Theorem 1) minimizes the bidirectional chamfer distance to the original dense set. The DCT is chosen over DFT to avoid Gibbs oscillations on open curves, and the entire pipeline is trained end-to-end jointly, eliminating the error accumulation of pretrained strand codecs used in prior work.

## Results

On guide curve reconstruction (Table 2), the proposed method achieves a bidirectional chamfer distance of 0.906×10⁻⁴ to input sampled strands and 8.079×10⁻⁴ to ground-truth dense hair, outperforming the grid-based baseline (1.521 and 9.090) and the grid-based + strand codec baseline (1.597 and 10.843). For densification (Table 3), the method reduces the chamfer distance to ground-truth dense hair from 0.311×10⁻⁴ (nearest-neighbour upsampling) to 0.209×10⁻⁴. In the user study (Table 7), the method scores 6.933/10 overall versus HAAR's 6.106/10, with the largest gap on curly hair (6.656 vs. 4.933).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two main baselines. First, the grid-based + strand codec representation (the approach used by GroomGen [48] and HAAR [41]) maps strands onto a scalp UV grid and processes them with a pretrained off-the-shelf strand VAE [32]; the proposed method outperforms it on both reconstruction metrics (CD 0.906 vs. 1.597 to input strands; 8.079 vs. 10.843 to GT dense hair), attributed to end-to-end joint optimisation avoiding error accumulation from the frozen codec. Second, in a direct user study, the method outperforms the unconditional HAAR model [41] across all hairstyle categories, with the most pronounced advantage on curly hair (6.656 vs. 4.933 out of 10), where the frequency decomposition and high-frequency conditional VAE provide a clear representational benefit. The paper notes that direct quantitative comparison with GroomGen and HAAR is limited by the absence of standardised evaluation metrics for strand hair generation and the use of different training datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in strand-based hair generation (GroomGen, HAAR, Neural Strands) that relies on fixed UV-grid sampling of guide strands paired with pretrained off-the-shelf strand VAE codecs. It replaces grid sampling with k-medoids clustering for optimal guide strand selection (proven optimal for bidirectional chamfer distance in Theorem 1) and DCT-based frequency decomposition to separate low-frequency growth direction from high-frequency curliness, avoiding the Gibbs oscillation that plagues DFT on open curves. Architecturally, it adapts the PVCNN dual-branch design (PointNet + 2D convolution) for off-the-grid point set processing and borrows implicit neural representation and graph message-passing ideas for density-agnostic densification, moving away from the 2D CNN-on-UV-grid formulation used by prior strand codecs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The clean theoretical guarantee that k-medoids medoids minimize bidirectional chamfer distance provides a principled foundation for guide strand selection that grid-based heuristics lack, and the end-to-end VAE training eliminates error accumulation from pretrained strand codecs. However, the work is constrained by a small synthetic dataset (658 hairstyles), the absence of standard evaluation metrics for strand hair (no FID/LPIPS equivalent), and efficiency costs (~12s per generation vs. 0.09s for 2D CNN baselines). Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to real captured hair data, dynamic hair simulation, and whether the frequency decomposition and optimal sampling benefits persist at larger dataset scales with greater hairstyle diversity.
