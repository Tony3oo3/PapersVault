## Problem

Existing second-order and high-order methods for variational inequalities require exact Jacobian computation, making them prohibitively expensive for machine learning applications. No globally convergent inexact second-order method for VIs existed with an explicit dependence on the level of Jacobian inaccuracy, leaving open fundamental questions about lower bounds, optimal algorithm design, and proper Jacobian approximation strategies.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a continuous operator F: X → R^d defined on a closed bounded convex set X of diameter D, along with a δ-inexact Jacobian J(v) satisfying ||∇F(v) − J(v)|| ≤ δ at each query point. It outputs a solution point x̂ with a certified gap bound (monotone case) or residual bound (nonmonotone Minty case), where the bound explicitly decomposes into an exact-derivative term and an inexactness term. The subproblem at each iteration requires solving a regularized VI whose solution is obtained via a bisection segment-search over a scalar parameter τ, reducing the per-iteration cost from O(d³) to Õ(r²d) using the Woodbury identity for low-rank QN updates.

## Method

VIJI extends the Perseus dual-extrapolation framework by replacing the exact Jacobian with a δ-inexact approximation J(v) and introducing a regularization term ηδ(x−v) in the inexact Taylor model Ω_v^η(x) = F(v) + J(v)[x−v] + ηδ(x−v) + 5L₁‖x−v‖(x−v), which ensures the subproblem remains solvable. An adaptive dual stepsize λ_k incorporates an additional parameter β_k = δ to prevent excessively large steps when the Jacobian error dominates near the optimum. The paper proves a matching lower bound Ω(δD²/T + L₁D³/T^{3/2}) establishing optimality in the monotone smooth case, and shows that when δ ≤ L₁D/√T the rate recovers the exact second-order rate O(L₁D³/T^{3/2}). To reduce per-iteration cost, the paper introduces L-Broyden and Damped L-Broyden Quasi-Newton updates for the Jacobian, with the damped variant achieving δ ≤ 2L₀ versus δ ≤ (m+2)L₀ for the classical version, and leverages the Woodbury matrix identity to compute the subproblem solution in Õ(r²d) operations. A tensor generalization (VIHI) extends the framework to p-th order inexact derivatives, and a restarted variant achieves linear convergence for strongly monotone operators.

## Results

For smooth monotone VIs with δ-inexact Jacobians, VIJI achieves a convergence rate of O(δD²/T + L₁D³/T^{3/2}) for the gap function, which matches the newly established lower bound Ω(δD²/T + L₁D³/T^{3/2}), proving optimality. In the nonmonotone (Minty) setting, the rate is O(δD²/√T + L₁D³/T) for the residual. When δ ≤ L₁D/√T, the method recovers the optimal exact second-order rate O(L₁D³/T^{3/2}). For strongly monotone VIs, the restarted version achieves a linear rate of O((LD/μ)^{2/3} + (δ/μ + 1)⌈log(D/ε)⌉) iterations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares VIQA (VIJI with Quasi-Newton approximation) against Extragradient (EG), first-order Perseus (Perseus1), and second-order Perseus with exact Jacobian (Perseus2) on a cubic regularized bilinear min-max problem (d=50, ρ=1e-3). VIQA with Damped Broyden approximation is significantly faster than EG, Perseus1, and VIQA with classical Broyden, confirming that the damped update yields a more accurate Jacobian approximation (δ ≤ 2L₀ vs. δ ≤ (m+2)L₀). Perseus2 with exact Jacobian converges fastest in terms of iterations but is expensive per-iteration due to full Jacobian computation and second-order subproblem solving. Theoretically, VIJI generalizes Inexact-Newton-MinMax [71]: under the same decreasing-δ assumption, the two methods are equivalent for min-max problems, but VIJI additionally handles arbitrary constant δ with an explicit O(ε^{-2/3} + δε^{-1}) rate.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Perseus framework [69]—an optimal high-order method for monotone variational inequalities—to handle inexact Jacobian information, introducing the VIJI algorithm. It generalizes the Inexact-Newton-MinMax method of Lin, Mertikopoulos, and Jordan [71] from convex-concave min-max problems to general VIs, with the key departure that Jacobian inaccuracy directly modulates step sizes rather than requiring δ to decrease with convergence. The Quasi-Newton approximations (L-Broyden and Damped L-Broyden) adapt classical Hessian-approximation ideas from minimization to the non-symmetric Jacobian structure of VIs, while the tensor generalization extends the framework to inexact high-order derivatives.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first globally convergent inexact second-order method for VIs with an explicit rate dependence on Jacobian accuracy, filling a gap between the theoretical optimality of exact methods and the practical infeasibility of computing exact Jacobians in ML applications (GANs, adversarial training, RL). The QN approximations reduce per-iteration cost from O(d³) to O(r²d) via the Woodbury identity, making second-order VI methods practically viable. Open questions include handling inexactness within the operator itself, designing adaptive schemes for dynamic inexactness levels, and developing QN updates tailored to the non-symmetric Jacobian structure with criteria matching Assumption 2.1.
