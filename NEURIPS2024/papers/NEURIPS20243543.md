## Problem

Existing population control methods are designed case-by-case for specific noise-free differential equations and cannot adapt to adversarial perturbations or time-varying cost functions. Prior online control algorithms provide regret guarantees only against strongly stabilizing policies, which is fundamentally incompatible with population dynamics since strong stabilizability would require the population to shrink exponentially to zero. The paper asks whether a generic, computationally efficient controller can achieve low regret for population dynamics under adversarial noise.

## Input / Output

At each timestep t, the controller observes the current population state x_t (a probability vector in the d-dimensional simplex), the perturbation strength γ_t, and the cost function c_t; it also maintains a memory of the last H perturbation vectors w_{t-1:t-H}. The transition matrices A and B (both column-stochastic) are known in advance. The controller outputs a control u_t ∈ Δ_{≤1}^d, which encodes both a distribution over d intervention types and a 'null intervention' weight (1 − ‖u_t‖₁) that determines how much of the natural dynamics A x_t persists versus the controlled dynamics B u_t. The key structural constraint is that all states remain in the simplex, guaranteeing they represent valid population distributions.

## Method

GPC-Simplex adapts the Gradient Perturbation Controller (GPC) to the simplex-constrained setting by replacing the impossible strong-stabilizability requirement with a mixing-time condition on the comparator class. The key architectural change is adding a learnable parameter p ∈ Δ^d representing the desired stationary distribution, since without strong stabilizability different comparator policies mix to different states and a fixed reference policy would be insufficient. The resulting policy is a variant of disturbance-action control: u_t = λ_{t,0}·p + Σ_{j=1}^{H} λ_{t,j}·M^[j]·w_{t-j}, where the weights λ are determined by the observed perturbation strengths. The algorithm performs online convex optimization (Lazy Mirror Descent) over the joint parameter space (p, M^[1:H]), with the loss at each step being the counterfactual cost of following the current policy from the start. The main theoretical result is Õ(τ^{7/2}√(dT)) regret against linear time-invariant policies whose induced transition matrix mixes within time τ, along with a matching lower bound showing the mixing assumption is necessary.

## Results

The main theoretical contribution is a regret bound of Õ(τ^{7/2} √(dT)) for GPC-Simplex against the class of linear time-invariant policies with mixing time τ on a simplex LDS of dimension d over T steps (Theorem 7). A matching lower bound (Theorem 8) shows that without the mixing-time assumption, linear regret Ω(T) is unavoidable. Experimentally, on a controlled SIR model (T=200, β=0.5, θ=0.03, ξ=0.005), GPC-Simplex outperforms both the always-full-prevention and always-no-prevention baselines in cumulative cost, and on the hospital-flows problem (T=100, y_max=0.1, c_2=0.01, c_3=100) it closely reproduces the analytically optimal control from Ketcheson [27].

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares GPC-Simplex against two simple policy baselines on the controlled SIR model: always executing full prevention (u_t=[1,0]) and always executing no prevention (u_t=[0,1]). GPC-Simplex outperforms both in cumulative cost over T=200 steps, learning to apply high prevention early in the outbreak and then relaxing as herd immunity is approached — a phase-transition behavior neither baseline captures. On the hospital-flows benchmark from Ketcheson [27], where a closed-form optimal controller is available via Hamilton-Jacobi-Bellman equations in the noiseless setting, GPC-Simplex's control trajectory and the resulting infected/susceptible curves closely match the analytical optimum. The paper also notes (Appendix H) that on replicator dynamics, GPC-Simplex is more robust to noisy cost functions than a one-step best-response controller, though no formal numerical comparison is provided in the main text.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Gradient Perturbation Controller (GPC) framework for online non-stochastic control of linear dynamical systems [Agarwal et al., 2019] to the simplex-constrained setting relevant to population dynamics. The key departure is that GPC requires the comparator class to strongly stabilize the LDS (forcing the state to zero), which is impossible for stochastic transition matrices modeling populations; GPC-Simplex replaces this with a mixing-time condition on the induced Markov chain, and augments the disturbance-action policy with a learnable stationary distribution parameter p. The paper also draws on online convex optimization (lazy mirror descent) and Markov chain mixing theory, and positions itself against prior population-control work (SIR optimal control, quarantine models) that is case-by-case, noise-free, and cannot handle adversarial perturbations or time-varying costs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first general, provably robust framework for online population control under adversarial perturbations, with near-optimal regret against a natural comparator class. The impossibility results (linear regret against marginally stable policies for general LDS; linear regret without mixing time for simplex LDS) are of independent interest in online control theory and delineate the exact boundary of what is achievable. Open questions include whether the fixed control-norm assumption (‖u_t‖₁ independent of state) can be removed, whether the requirement to observe γ_t can be relaxed, and whether a more natural simplex LDS formulation avoids the technical need for this constraint. Empirically, the method is validated on small-scale SIR and replicator dynamics simulations, but scalability to high-dimensional or real-world population data remains untested.
