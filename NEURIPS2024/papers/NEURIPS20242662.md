## Problem

It was conjectured that variance-dependent (second-order) regret bounds for general contextual bandits could be obtained by simply replacing the horizon T with the total variance Λ in the minimax bound, yielding O~(√(AΛ) + A). This paper shows that conjecture is false: the eluder dimension d_elu of the function class is an unavoidable component of the regret even when variance is zero, and its role differs fundamentally between weak and strong adversary models. The paper characterizes the exact dependence on d_elu, A, and Λ under each adversary type.

## Input / Output

At each round t, the learner receives a context x_t ∈ X and (in the weak adversary setting) the variance σ_t is revealed before action selection; the learner then selects an action a_t from a finite set A and observes a scalar reward r_t ∈ [0,1]. The function class F ⊂ [0,1]^(X×A) encodes mean reward information under realizability, while in the distributional setting (Section 6) a model class M maps context-action pairs to Gaussian distributions N(f_M(x,a), σ_M(x,a)). The output is the action sequence with regret guarantees expressed in terms of the cumulative variance Λ = Σσ_t², the number of actions A, and the eluder dimension d_elu(F) or Hellinger eluder dimension d_elu^H(M).

## Method

The paper's core contribution is a matching pair of lower bounds and algorithms for three adversary/distributional settings. For the weak adversary with revealed variance, the VarCB algorithm combines four mechanisms: (i) restricting the action set to those that are optimal for some function in the current confidence set, (ii) selecting 'discriminative actions' that maximize disagreement among remaining functions to shrink the confidence set at O~(d_elu) cost, (iii) inverse gap weighting (à la SquareCB) for exploitation rounds, and (iv) a Prod-based online regression oracle that achieves a variance-scaled R_sq guarantee of O~((σ²+Δ)log|F|) rather than the O~(log|F|) of standard exponential weights. For the strong adversary, VarUCB generalizes the SAVE algorithm from linear to nonlinear function approximation using weighted regression and a multi-layer confidence structure. In the distributional setting, DistVarCB replaces value disagreement with squared Hellinger distance between model predictions. The key insight is that the regret decomposes into a local estimation term √(min{A,d_elu}·Λ) and a global search term d_elu that persists even at zero variance.

## Results

The paper establishes near-tight variance-dependent regret bounds for realizable contextual bandits with general function approximation. In the weak adversary setting with revealed variance, the algorithm VarCB achieves Õ(√(AΛ) + d_elu), matching the lower bound Ω(√(AΛ) + d_elu) up to log factors. In the strong adversary setting, VarUCB achieves Õ(d_elu·√Λ + d_elu), which is off from the lower bound Ω(√(d_elu·Λ) + d_elu) by a √(d_elu) factor. In the distributional (Gaussian) setting, DistVarCB achieves Õ(√(AΛ_∞) + d_elu), improving the leading term from d_elu (Wang et al., 2024) to the number of actions A, with a matching lower bound.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Wang et al. (2024), the paper shows their Õ(√(d_elu·Λ_circ) + d_elu) bound is unimprovable by providing a matching lower bound Ω(√(d_elu·Λ_circ) + d_elu), confirming that their dependence on d_elu in the leading term cannot be removed in general. However, under the additional Gaussian noise assumption, the paper's DistVarCB algorithm improves the leading term from d_elu to A (the number of actions), yielding Õ(√(AΛ_∞) + d_elu), which is strictly better when A < d_elu. Against Pacchiano (2024), the weak adversary bounds are incomparable: Pacchiano's Õ(√(d_elu·Λ) + d_elu) uses d_elu in the leading term while this paper's Õ(√(A·Λ) + d_elu) uses A, so neither dominates the other across all regimes. For the strong adversary setting, both papers achieve the identical upper bound Õ(d_elu·√Λ + d_elu). The paper's additional contributions over Pacchiano are the lower bounds (showing tightness) and the extension to the distributional model-class setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of variance-dependent (second-order) regret bounds from linear contextual bandits (Zhao et al. 2023, Zhang et al. 2021) to general function approximation, building on the SquareCB framework of Foster and Rakhlin (2020) and the SAVE algorithm of Zhao et al. (2023). It departs from Wang et al. (2024), who studied second-order bounds with a distributional model class, by showing their bound is unimprovable while also establishing a complementary bound that trades the eluder dimension for the number of actions under Gaussian noise. The algorithmic toolkit combines inverse gap weighting (SquareCB), weighted regression for confidence sets, and the Prod algorithm for a refined online regression oracle, with the key conceptual departure being the identification of two distinct hardness sources—local estimation (scaling with min{A, d_elu}·Λ) and global search (scaling with d_elu)—that do not collapse in the general function class setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes that the eluder dimension is fundamentally unavoidable in variance-dependent regret for general contextual bandits, even at zero variance, which challenges the intuition that small variance should smoothly reduce regret to the multi-armed bandit level. This has implications for understanding when function approximation complexity truly matters versus when it can be bypassed. Key open questions include removing the variance-revealing assumption in the weak adversary setting (only solved for σ_t ∈ {0,1}), closing the √d_elu gap between upper and lower bounds in the strong adversary case, and extending the distributional results beyond Gaussian noise. The results provide a near-complete Pareto frontier for variance-dependent regret in this setting, guiding future algorithm design.
