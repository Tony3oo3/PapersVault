## Problem

Prior work established an Õ(√T) worst-case regret for contextual decision-making with knapsacks (CDMK), but it remained unclear whether o(√T) regret is achievable under mild, easily verifiable assumptions, and whether the commonly used fluid LP benchmark faithfully approximates the true online optimum. This paper shows that when the fluid LP has a unique but degenerate solution, an Ω(√T) gap between the fluid and online optima is unavoidable, and then provides the first beyond-worst-case regret guarantees (O(1) or O(log T)) under a simple non-degeneracy condition.

## Input / Output

Each round the agent observes a request θ_t drawn i.i.d. from an unknown distribution U over a finite set Θ, and must irrevocably select an action a_t from a finite set A (including a null action 0 that yields zero reward and consumption). The realized reward r(θ_t,a_t,γ_t) and consumption vector c(θ_t,a_t,γ_t) depend on an external factor γ_t drawn i.i.d. from an unknown distribution V; the functions r and c are known a priori, but the distributions must be learned. The information structure distinguishes full feedback (γ_t observed every round) from partial feedback (γ_t observed only when a_t ≠ 0), and the output is a sequence of actions whose cumulative reward is compared against the fluid LP value V^FL to yield a regret bound.

## Method

The algorithm (Algorithm 1) applies the re-solving (certainty-equivalence) heuristic: in each round t, it solves an estimated linear program Ĵ(ρ_t, H_t) using empirical distributions Ũ_t and V̂_t of the request and external factor, with the remaining average budget ρ_t = B_t/(T−t+1) as the resource constraint, and samples an action according to the optimal LP solution. The key theoretical insight is a stability result (Proposition 3.1, building on Mangasarian–Shiau): under the assumption that the true fluid LP J(ρ_1) has a unique non-degenerate optimal solution, there exists a threshold D > 0 such that whenever the estimation errors in u, v, and ρ are below D, the estimated LP shares the same basic/non-basic variables and binding constraints as the true LP, causing the Lagrangian regret-decomposition terms to vanish. The regret is then bounded by the probability that estimation errors exceed D (exponentially small via Hoeffding/Weissman inequalities) plus a smooth-consumption argument showing the stopping time T_e is within O(n²/D²) of T. Under partial information feedback, a separate concentration argument (Lemma 4.1) shows the agent still observes Ω(1/log T) samples of γ per round after an initial Θ(log T) warm-up, yielding only an O(log T) multiplicative degradation.

## Results

Under the mild assumption that the fluid LP has a unique and non-degenerate optimal solution, Algorithm 1 achieves O(1) regret with full information feedback and O(log T) regret with partial information feedback, both independent of the number of actions m. In the worst case (no regularity assumption), the algorithm achieves O(√T log T) regret for both discrete and continuous settings, matching the state-of-the-art up to a logarithmic factor. The paper also establishes an Ω(√T) lower bound on the gap between the fluid benchmark and the online optimum when the fluid LP is degenerate, showing that the non-degeneracy assumption is essentially necessary for sub-√T regret.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's main comparison is against prior CDMK/CBwK algorithms. Han et al. [24] and Slivkins and Foster [36] achieve O(√T log T) worst-case regret but require specific structural assumptions (e.g., linear realizability of reward-consumption functions) and their lower-bound conditions are complex to verify. Sankararaman and Slivkins [32] achieve O(log T) regret for BwK but restrict to at most 2 resources (n ≤ 2), whereas this paper's O(1)/O(log T) beyond-worst-case results hold for arbitrary n. Badanidiyuru et al. [8] and Agrawal et al. [4] report O(√mT)-type bounds with explicit dependence on the number of actions m, while Algorithm 1's regret is independent of m. The paper's fluid benchmark V^FL is shown to be larger (stronger) than the benchmarks used in [36, 37], making the regret guarantees strictly tighter. Numerical experiments confirm that regret scales as Õ(√T) under degeneracy and remains bounded (or grows only logarithmically) under non-degeneracy, matching the theoretical predictions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper transplants the re-solving (certainty equivalence) heuristic from the network revenue management literature (Jasin & Kumar, Bumpensanti & Wang, Chen et al.) into the contextual decision-making with knapsacks (CDMK) setting, where decisions must be conditioned on observed requests—a complication absent in the simpler NRM and BwK problems where the heuristic was previously applied. The worst-case gap lower bound (Theorem 2.1) extends the dual-multiplicity argument of Vera and Banerjee to CDMK, while the O(1) regret proof adapts the Lagrangian regret decomposition of Chen et al. [16] and the LP stability theory of Mangasarian and Shiau. Unlike prior CDMK/CBwK algorithms that rely on dual updates with UCB or regression oracles (Agrawal et al., Han et al., Slivkins & Foster), this work shows that a simple empirical re-solving approach suffices under mild non-degeneracy, and it introduces a partial-information feedback model intermediate between full and bandit feedback.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that the fluid LP benchmark can be Ω(√T) away from the true online optimum under degeneracy is a clean conceptual warning for practitioners who use fluid solutions as performance targets in dynamic resource allocation. The O(1) regret guarantee via re-solving, independent of the number of actions, suggests that sophisticated exploration mechanisms (UCB, regression oracles) may be unnecessary in well-conditioned instances, simplifying algorithm design for supply-chain and auction applications. Open questions include whether the D^{-2} dependence can be improved, whether the non-degeneracy assumption can be relaxed (e.g., via smoothed analysis), and whether the continuous-randomness extension can handle unbounded or heavy-tailed distributions beyond the Hölder smoothness class.
