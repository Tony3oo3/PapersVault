## Problem

Offline RL faces a fundamental tension: full generalization of value functions toward out-of-distribution actions causes value overestimation and extrapolation error, while in-sample learning methods that entirely eschew generalization are too conservative and cannot leverage the generalization ability of neural networks to find better policies beyond the dataset. The paper asks how to appropriately exploit mild generalization in offline RL without incurring the risks of over-generalization.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a static offline dataset of (s, a, r, s') transitions and produces a policy π, a Q-function Q_θ, and a V-function V_ψ. The learned policy is constrained to a mildly generalized neighborhood of the dataset's action support (bounded by ε_a in action-space distance), rather than being restricted strictly to in-sample actions or allowed to span the full action space. The Bellman target is a convex blend (controlled by λ) of the policy's expected Q-value and the in-sample expectile-regressed V-value, making λ a tunable knob between in-sample learning (λ=0) and full bootstrapping (λ=1).

## Method

DMG introduces two complementary mechanisms. First, mild action generalization: the policy is trained via actor-critic to maximize Q-values while being KL-regularized toward an advantage-reshaped behavior policy β*(a|s) ∝ β̂(a|s)·exp(A(s,a)), constraining it to select actions in a close neighborhood of the dataset rather than the full action space. Second, mild generalization propagation: the Bellman target blends λ·E_{a'~π'}Q(s',a') with (1−λ)·V_ψ(s'), where V_ψ is trained via expectile regression (à la IQL) to capture the in-sample max. This reduces the effective discount through which generalization errors propagate from γ to λγ, suppressing value overestimation amplification while preserving RL learning signals. Theoretically, under oracle generalization DMG provably outperforms the in-sample optimal policy, and under worst-case generalization it upper-bounds overestimation by O(λ·ε_a·K_Q·γ/(1−γ)) and guarantees a performance lower bound within O(ε_a) of optimal.

## Results

DMG achieves the highest total normalized score of 1182.8 on Gym-MuJoCo locomotion tasks (vs. 1041.2 for OneStep RL and 1033.1 for IQL) and 439.4 on AntMaze tasks (vs. 392.4 for IQL and 218.0 for CQL). On the most challenging antmaze-large-diverse task, DMG scores 58.8 compared to 46.0 for IQL and 3.2 for CQL. After 1M steps of online fine-tuning on AntMaze, DMG reaches a total of 556.4, substantially outperforming IQL (490.4) while TD3 from scratch fails entirely (0.0).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DMG outperforms all 12 baselines on both the locomotion total (1182.8) and AntMaze total (439.4). The closest competitor on locomotion is OneStep RL (1041.2), with DMG gaining +141.6 points; on AntMaze the closest is IQL (392.4), with DMG gaining +47.0 points. The largest margins appear on the hardest tasks: antmaze-large-diverse (DMG 58.8 vs. IQL 46.0, CQL 3.2) and hopper-medium (DMG 100.6 vs. OneStep RL 87.5, IQL 66.2). When DMG is combined with in-sample methods XQL and SQL, it consistently improves their scores (XQL total 725.3→792.7, SQL total 747.5→804.2), confirming that the performance gap stems from eschewing generalization. For online fine-tuning on AntMaze, DMG initialized from offline pretraining reaches 556.4 total after 1M steps, substantially outperforming IQL (490.4) while TD3 trained from scratch fails entirely (0.0) on all sparse-reward AntMaze tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself against the binary framing in offline RL between full generalization (TD3, SAC) and complete non-generalization (in-sample methods like IQL, XQL, SQL). It builds directly on IQL's expectile regression for the in-sample max component and on policy-regularized methods (TD3BC, BEAR) for the mild action generalization component, but unifies them under a single 'doubly mild' principle: constrain both the action space explored by the policy AND the discount factor through which generalization errors propagate via bootstrapping. The key departure from prior work is the explicit recognition that in-sample learning, while safe, is too conservative and leaves performance on the table, and that a carefully bounded amount of generalization in both dimensions is both safe and beneficial.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clean theoretical and practical framework for calibrating generalization in offline RL, with guarantees in both oracle and worst-case scenarios (limited overestimation bound and performance lower bound). The consistent improvements over IQL, XQL, and SQL when DMG is layered on top suggest that the in-sample learning paradigm, now dominant in the field, has a systematic performance ceiling that mild generalization can break. The seamless offline-to-online transition is a practical advantage for deployment. A noted limitation is that when the function approximator is highly compatible with a specific task (generalizing well across the entire action space), DMG's conservatism may underperform full generalization methods, leaving open the question of how to adaptively determine the right degree of mildness (λ, ν) per task.
