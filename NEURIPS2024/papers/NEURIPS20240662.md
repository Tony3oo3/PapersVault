## Problem

Existing debiasing methods for Vision-Language Models are narrowly tailored to specific modalities or tasks, often require costly retraining of pre-trained models, and risk distorting the semantic content of embeddings when removing bias-related features. No prior approach provides a unified, retraining-free strategy that simultaneously addresses bias across image and text encoders/decoders while preserving the dimensionality and meaning of representations needed for diverse downstream tasks.

## Input / Output

SFID consumes frozen 2D representations (e.g., 512-dim CLIP ViT-B/32 embeddings) from any VLM component, along with a separate debiasing dataset carrying sensitive attribute labels (FairFace for images, Bias-in-Bios for text), split into training and validation subsets. It outputs a modified embedding of identical dimensionality where the top-k bias-correlated features are replaced with in-distribution imputation values, which can then be fed directly into any downstream task—classification, retrieval, captioning, or generation—without architectural changes. For non-2D decoder tensors (sequence-length or spatial dimensions), global averaging is applied solely to extract feature importance indices and imputation values before the original tensor structure is restored.

## Method

SFID trains a RandomForest classifier on frozen representations to predict the sensitive attribute, leveraging its interpretability to rank feature importance while capturing inter-feature dependencies that mutual-information-based methods (e.g., CLIP-clip) ignore. The top-k features (k=50, chosen via elbow analysis) are identified as bias-correlated and pruned. Rather than zeroing or adding noise—which pushes embeddings out-of-distribution and degrades task performance—SFID introduces Low Confidence Imputation (LCI): it computes the per-feature mean over validation samples that the RandomForest classifies with confidence below a threshold τ=0.7, treating these ambiguous samples as bias-free anchors. For text-to-image generation with gender-specific prompts, a High Confidence Imputation variant instead draws values from samples confidently classified as the requested gender, preserving the user's intent while still neutralizing profession-gender stereotypes. The entire pipeline is retraining-free, transferable across query datasets, and applicable to both encoders and decoders within the same VLM.

## Results

On text-to-image retrieval (Flickr30K), SFID achieves the lowest Skew@100 across all three VLMs: 0.1414 (CLIP-RN50), 0.0744 (CLIP-ViT-B/32), and 0.2032 (XVLM), while maintaining competitive Recall@10 (87.41%, 88.90%, 98.01% respectively). On zero-shot classification (FACET), SFID reduces ΔDP to 9.63 (CLIP-RN50), 10.15 (CLIP-ViT-B/32), and 9.91 (XVLM) compared to baselines of 11.08, 11.60, and 11.72. For image captioning, SFID achieves the lowest composite misclassification rate (1.88 for BLIP, 3.12 for CLIP-CAP), and for text-to-image generation, SFID(HC) reduces the composite mismatch rate to 1.74 (SDXL) and 5.38 (CoDi) versus baselines of 4.42 and 6.85.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SFID consistently outperforms all three baselines on the fairness metric across all four downstream tasks. In text-to-image retrieval, SFID achieves the lowest Skew@100 for every model (e.g., 0.0744 vs. 0.0920 for CLIP-clip and 0.1387 for DeAR on CLIP-ViT-B/32), while in zero-shot classification it attains the best ΔDP for CLIP-RN50 (9.63 vs. 10.04 for DeAR and 10.09 for CLIP-clip) and CLIP-ViT-B/32 (10.15 vs. 10.37 for DeAR). For image captioning, SFID yields the lowest composite misclassification rate (1.88 for BLIP vs. 2.59 for CLIP-clip and 2.84 for DeAR), and for text-to-image generation, SFID(HC) dramatically reduces the composite mismatch rate to 1.74 on SDXL, whereas DeAR catastrophically fails (99.81) and Prompt-Debias degrades to 58.49. The main trade-off is a modest drop in Recall@10 (e.g., 87.41% vs. 88.12% baseline on CLIP-RN50) and a slight accuracy decrease in zero-shot classification (e.g., 50.93% vs. 51.87% for CLIP-RN50), which the authors argue is an acceptable cost for substantially improved fairness.

## Relation to Prior Work

SFID repurposes the feature-pruning paradigm from CLIP-clip [38] but replaces its mutual-information-based feature selection (which assumes column independence) with RandomForest-based importance scoring that captures inter-feature dependencies. It departs from DeAR [34]'s adversarial training framework by substituting a non-adversarial, hyperparameter-light imputation strategy (Low Confidence Imputation) that draws replacement values from ambiguous samples rather than pushing representations toward a decision boundary. The paper positions itself as the first method to apply a single debiasing procedure uniformly across both encoders and decoders and across four distinct downstream tasks without retraining, whereas prior methods (Prompt-Debias, DeAR, CLIP-clip) were each scoped to specific components or tasks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical value of SFID lies in its zero-retraining, component-agnostic design: a practitioner can debias any frozen VLM component in under a minute of RandomForest training, making fairness interventions accessible without the compute cost of fine-tuning. However, the text-to-image generation results reveal a significant open problem — the Skew metric for neutral prompts remains stubbornly high (81–83), indicating that representation-level imputation alone cannot fully resolve generative bias, and the multiple-sensitive-attribute experiments (Appendix F) show only modest DP reductions. Future work should investigate whether combining SFID with decoder-level interventions or training-time regularization can close this gap, and whether the approach generalizes to intersectional biases beyond the pairwise gender/race settings tested here.
