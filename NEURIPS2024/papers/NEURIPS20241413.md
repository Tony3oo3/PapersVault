## Problem

Existing embodied multi-agent cooperation benchmarks use isomorphic agents with symbolic observations and ignore the physical constraints that diverse real-world human partners face (e.g., wheelchair users, children, frail elderly). This paper introduces CHAIC, the first large-scale embodied social intelligence challenge that explicitly models accessibility, requiring a helper agent to infer a constrained partner's goals and limitations purely from egocentric visual observations across indoor and outdoor scenes with emergent events.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241413/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The helper agent receives 512×512 egocentric RGB-D images and a self-state vector (position, orientation, held objects) after each completed or failed action; the constrained partner's true goals and capability limits are hidden, forcing active perception. The output is a high-level plan selected from a fixed list of valid plans (e.g., 'pick up <object_id>', 'goto and put <object_id> in <container_id>'), which the execution module decomposes into discrete low-level actions: move forward, turn left, turn right, pick up A, put A in B, put A on B, or wait. Actions execute asynchronously between agents and may take multiple frames to complete.

## Method

The LLM+BM Helper extends the CoELA modular framework with a novel Behavior Modeling module that combines a fine-tuned TSN action recognition model (pretrained on Kinetics-400, 86.1% top-1 accuracy on 6 partner action classes) with action grounding against a semantic map to identify what the constrained agent is doing and on which object. The perception module uses a fine-tuned Mask R-CNN (94.4% mAP@50 on 61K collected images) to build object-level segmentation, while the memory module maintains a 2D occupancy map and semantic map from depth images. The GPT-4 decision module receives a structured prompt containing task description, self-state, observed partner behaviors, task progress, semantic map, and available plans, then selects and parameterizes a plan. The execution module uses A* pathfinding over the occupancy map for navigation and frontier-based exploration for discovery. This design lets the LLM reason about the partner's inferred goals and constraints without any oracle symbolic information, bridging the gap between raw visual perception and cooperative planning.

## Results

On the CHAIC benchmark, the LLM(GPT-4)+BM Helper achieves the highest Transport Rate (TR) in 6 out of 8 tasks and the largest Efficiency Improvement (EI) in 7 out of 8 tasks. For example, it reaches TR=0.70 (EI=0.27) on Low Target, TR=0.42 (EI=0.89) on Obstacle, and TR=0.58 (EI=0.33) on Shopping, consistently outperforming the Random, RHP, and VLM(GPT-4o) baselines. The Oracle Helper (with ground-truth goals and segmentation) sets the upper bound at TR=0.82 (Low Target) and TR=0.76 (Furniture), revealing a substantial gap in goal inference accuracy (IA) for all non-oracle agents (IA ranges from 0.14 to 0.47 vs. 0.82–0.91 for Oracle). Learning-based baselines (RL, SmartHelp) perform at or below the no-helper baseline on most indoor tasks, with the exception of the outdoor Furniture task where cooperation is essential due to strength constraints.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The LLM(GPT-4)+BM Helper outperforms the VLM(GPT-4o) Helper on 7 of 8 tasks in TR, with the largest gap on Shopping (0.58 vs. 0.50) and High Goalplace (0.36 vs. 0.26, where VLM actually performs worse than no helper). It also beats the Rule-Based Hierarchical Plan (RHP) helper on 6 of 8 tasks, most notably on Shopping (EI 0.33 vs. 0.22) and High Container (EI 0.24 vs. 0.19), while RHP retains an edge on Furniture (TR 0.65 vs. 0.69 is actually LLM+BM winning; RHP wins on IA for Obstacle). Against the SmartHelp baseline (Cao et al., 2024), which uses oracle symbolic observations of the partner's actions, the LLM+BM Helper substantially outperforms it on all indoor tasks (e.g., TR 0.65 vs. 0.46 on No Constraint; 0.49 vs. 0.26 on High Container) and on Shopping (0.58 vs. 0.32), though SmartHelp is competitive on Furniture (0.57 vs. 0.69). The key finding is that all non-oracle helpers struggle with Goal Inference Accuracy (IA ≤ 0.47 vs. Oracle's 0.82–0.91), indicating that inferring partner constraints from raw egocentric RGB-D remains the primary bottleneck, not planning or execution.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the ThreeDWorld embodied AI platform (Gan et al., 2021) and the CoELA modular LLM-based cooperative agent framework (Zhang et al., 2023), extending both to address a gap in embodied social intelligence: the explicit modeling of human physical constraints. Prior multi-agent cooperation benchmarks (Watch-and-Help, NOPA, Social Rearrangement) studied isomorphic agents with symbolic or oracle observations, while Smart Help (Cao et al., 2024) addressed assistive scenarios but only indoors with perfect symbolic perception. CHAIC departs from all of these by requiring egocentric RGB-D visual observation, introducing four physically constrained agent types (child, wheelchair, bicycle, frail), and incorporating outdoor scenes with emergent emergency events.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CHAIC fills a genuine gap in embodied AI by making accessibility a first-class design principle rather than an afterthought, and by requiring agents to infer partner goals and constraints purely from visual observations in rich indoor and outdoor settings. The results reveal that even LLM-augmented agents with behavior modeling remain far from oracle performance on goal inference (IA ~0.25–0.47 vs. ~0.88–0.91), and that VLM helpers can sometimes perform worse than no helper at all, highlighting the difficulty of social perception from raw video. Open questions include how to close the gap between action recognition accuracy (86%) and end-to-end goal inference, how to make LLM agents handle emergencies as effectively as rule-based controllers, and how to diversify constrained agent behavior beyond rule-based policies. The benchmark is publicly available and calls on the community to prioritize inclusivity in embodied agent development.
