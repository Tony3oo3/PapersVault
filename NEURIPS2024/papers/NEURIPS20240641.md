## Problem

SDS-based text-to-3D methods suffer from the Janus Problem, where multiple conflicting faces or features appear at different viewpoints of the same 3D object. The paper identifies the root cause as discrete viewpoint encoding, which assigns the same text condition and shared prior distribution to all cameras within a region, biasing the 3D representation toward locally optimal high-certainty synthesis at multiple positions simultaneously.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240641/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single text prompt (e.g., 'a beagle in a detective's outfit') and produces a 3D NeRF representation optimized via score distillation from a frozen Stable Diffusion 2.1 base model. LCGen is designed as a plug-in module that can be embedded into any existing SDS-based pipeline (DreamFusion, Magic3D, ProlificDreamer) without altering the training paradigm, adding extra data, or requiring fine-tuning of the diffusion model. Training runs for up to 10,000 steps on a single A100 GPU using the threestudio framework.

## Method

LCGen introduces a certainty-based loss that decouples the shared prior distribution across viewpoints by exploiting the observation that diffusion models exhibit higher denoising certainty for feature-rich views (e.g., front) and lower certainty for less characteristic views (e.g., back). The certainty C^c(x_{t-1}|x_t) is modeled as a Gaussian whose mean and variance are derived from the predicted noise at each timestep, and a view-dependent guidance function G(c)=|φ| modulates the loss to suppress certainty for non-frontal azimuths. The resulting L_cert is backpropagated directly to NeRF parameters Θ via a simplified chain-rule derivation that bypasses the frozen U-Net, maintaining computational consistency with the standard SDS gradient. This effectively localizes the probability peak of head generation to a single position on the sphere, reducing the Janus Rate from 56–63% to 17–23% across baselines while preserving or improving CLIP-Score.

## Results

LCGen reduces the Janus Rate (JR) by 3–4× across all three SDS-based baselines: DreamFusion drops from 56.67% to 16.67%, Magic3D from 46.67% to 23.33%, and ProlificDreamer from 63.33% to 20.00%. CLIP-Score (CS), measuring text-image alignment, is preserved or improved (e.g., ProlificDreamer CS rises from 26.23% to 28.94%), confirming that view-consistency gains do not come at the cost of generation quality. In the cross-method comparison, LCGen (on ProlificDreamer) achieves the best CS (28.94%) and ties for the lowest JR (20.00%) among all Janus-mitigation methods evaluated.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the three SDS baselines (DreamFusion, Magic3D, ProlificDreamer), LCGen consistently reduces JR by 30–43 percentage points while maintaining or slightly improving CS, demonstrating plug-and-play effectiveness without architectural changes. In the cross-method comparison (Fig. 6), LCGen matches the best JR (20.00%) shared by MVDream and DreamControl, but surpasses both on CS (28.94% vs. 26.17% and 28.14%). Crucially, LCGen is the only method satisfying all four practical criteria simultaneously: no additional prior data, single-stage training without fine-tuning, and no object-specific design. Perp-Neg achieves a higher JR (26.67%) and requires object-specific negative prompts, while D-SDS depends on an external LLM and yields the lowest CS (24.82%). DreamControl matches LCGen's JR but requires multi-stage 3D self-prior training, making LCGen strictly more efficient at equal or better quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the SDS/VSD framework established by DreamFusion and ProlificDreamer, adding a certainty-based loss (L_cert) that exploits natural variation in diffusion model denoising confidence across viewpoints. It departs from prior Janus Problem mitigations (DreamControl's multi-stage 3D self-prior, Perp-Neg's negative prompts, D-SDS's LLM-based debiasing) by requiring no additional data, no fine-tuning, and no object-specific design—instead treating the problem as a distributional decoupling issue solvable through a single scalar constraint on generation certainty. The probabilistic modeling in Section 3, which shows the Janus Problem is mathematically inevitable under discrete view encoding with shared priors, is a conceptual departure from prior work that treated the problem as an empirical artifact rather than a structural consequence of the SDS paradigm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LCGen offers a lightweight, plug-and-play remedy for the Janus Problem that can be dropped into any SDS-based pipeline without architectural changes, making it immediately useful for practitioners working with DreamFusion, Magic3D, or ProlificDreamer. However, the method's reliance on a fixed G(c) function (|φ|) limits its applicability to unusual poses (e.g., upside-down objects) and multi-object scenes, where certainty-based view separation breaks down. Open questions include whether adaptive or learned G(c) functions could handle diverse object geometries, and whether the approach generalizes beyond NeRF to other 3D representations like 3D Gaussian Splatting. The broader challenge of endowing 2D diffusion priors with genuine 3D spatial understanding remains unresolved.
