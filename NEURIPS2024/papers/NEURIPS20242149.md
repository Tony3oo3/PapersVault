## Problem

Current latent diffusion models suffer from three intertwined limitations—structural visual defects, misalignment with human aesthetic preferences, and slow iterative denoising—that existing methods address in isolation with specialized designs that resist integration (e.g., MoE architectures block acceleration, consistency models break ReFL-style preference tuning). No prior work provides a single framework that simultaneously improves generation quality, aesthetic appeal, and inference speed.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242149/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

UniFL is a post-training framework applied to pre-trained LDMs (SD1.5, SDXL) that takes a text prompt and produces a standard RGB image, operating entirely in the VAE latent space with a DDIM scheduler. During training, it consumes text-image pairs with instance segmentation annotations (COCO2017), human preference pairs across four aesthetic dimensions, and high-aesthetic-score LAION images; at inference it requires only the text prompt and a step count (20 for full quality, 4 for accelerated generation).

## Method

UniFL unifies three feedback-learning components under a single reward-tuning formulation. (1) Perceptual Feedback Learning (PeFL) conditions denoising on the ground-truth image (rather than random noise as in ReFL), partially denoises, then scores the predicted x₀′ against ground-truth annotations using a frozen visual perception model (e.g., SOLO for structure, VGG-16 Gram matrices for style, DeepLab-V3 for layout), yielding targeted per-aspect supervision. (2) Decoupled Aesthetic Feedback Learning trains separate reward models for color, layout, lighting, and detail, applies a per-dimension hinge loss, and uses an active prompt-selection strategy (semantic filtering + nearest-neighbor compression) to prevent reward-model overfitting. (3) Adversarial Feedback Learning treats the reward model as a jointly trained discriminator, so it adapts to the shifting output distribution and can provide valid reward signals even at high-noise timesteps, enabling reliable 4-step inference. Training proceeds in two stages: Stage 1 combines PeFL and decoupled aesthetic losses; Stage 2 adds the adversarial objective while retaining the aesthetic loss to prevent quality degradation.

## Results

On SD1.5 (20 steps), UniFL achieves the best FID of 31.14, CLIP score of 0.318, and aesthetic score of 5.54, outperforming all quality-focused baselines. On SDXL (25 steps), UniFL reaches FID 25.54, CLIP 0.328, and Aes 5.98. Under 4-step inference, UniFL on SDXL achieves FID 26.25 and Aes 5.87, surpassing SDXL-Turbo (FID 30.43) and SDXL-Lightning (FID 28.48) while matching their CLIP scores. In user studies, UniFL is preferred over ImageReward by 17% and over LCM by 57% with 4-step inference.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UniFL outperforms all quality-focused baselines (ImageReward, DreamShaper, DPO) on FID, CLIP score, and aesthetic score for both SD1.5 and SDXL at full inference steps; notably, DPO degrades SDXL FID from 27.92 to 35.30 while UniFL improves it to 25.54. For 4-step acceleration on SDXL, UniFL achieves the best FID (26.25) and aesthetic score (5.87), beating SDXL-Turbo by 4.18 FID and 0.27 Aes, and SDXL-Lightning by 2.23 FID and 0.21 Aes. On SD1.5 4-step inference, UniFL substantially outperforms LCM in FID (33.54 vs. 42.65) and aesthetic score (5.88 vs. 5.71). In user studies, UniFL is preferred over ImageReward by 17%, over LCM by 57%, and over SDXL-Turbo by 20% with 4-step inference, confirming consistent human-preference gains across both quality and speed dimensions.

## Relation to Prior Work

UniFL builds directly on the ReFL preference fine-tuning framework from ImageReward [23] and the LDM denoising pipeline [4], but unifies three previously incompatible improvement directions—perceptual quality (via MoE in RAPHAEL), aesthetic alignment (via reward models in ImageReward/DPO), and inference acceleration (via distillation in LCM/SDXL Turbo)—into a single feedback-learning formulation. The key departure is that prior methods are mutually exclusive in practice (MoE complicates acceleration; consistency models alter the denoising process making ReFL inapplicable), whereas UniFL's unified reward-tuning perspective allows all three objectives to coexist. PeFL is novel in repurposing off-the-shelf perceptual models (SOLO, DeepLab-V3, VGG-16) as targeted feedback providers rather than training new reward models from scratch, and the adversarial feedback learning replaces the heavy teacher-student distillation of SDXL Turbo with a lightweight trainable discriminator.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

UniFL is the first framework to simultaneously improve generation quality, aesthetic appeal, and inference speed for LDMs without architectural modifications, making it broadly applicable to existing checkpoints (SD1.5, SDXL) with modest compute (~200 A100 GPU-hours per stage). The practical impact is significant: 4-step inference matching or exceeding 20-step aesthetic scores (5.88 vs 5.54 on SD1.5) is directly useful for interactive applications. Open questions include whether the two-stage pipeline can be collapsed into single-stage optimization, whether 1-step inference is achievable, and whether larger visual foundation models (e.g., SAM) can further strengthen the perceptual feedback signal. The finding that a simpler dense-supervision segmentation model (SOLO) outperforms a higher-accuracy query-based one (Mask2Former) suggests feedback signal density matters more than raw perceptual accuracy, an insight that may generalize to other feedback-learning settings.
