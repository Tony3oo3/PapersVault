## Problem

Existing neural topic models face a trilemma: VAE-based models are effective but computationally expensive (hours for 10k documents), clustering-based models are fast but produce repetitive topics and inaccurate doc-topic distributions, and all suffer from poor stability across varying domains, vocabulary sizes, and document lengths. This limits practical deployment of topic modeling in real-world applications.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242682/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a collection of text documents and a pretrained Transformer (e.g., Sentence-BERT all-MiniLM-L6-v2) that produces frozen H-dimensional document embeddings; documents are also converted to Bag-of-Words vectors for the reconstruction loss. It outputs a topic-word distribution matrix β ∈ R^{V×K}, per-document topic distributions θ ∈ Δ_K, and top words per topic. For unseen documents, doc-topic distributions are inferred via a distance-based formula (Eq. 9) that regularizes topic-document distances by the learned topic weights, avoiding the degenerate case where a single document's transport plan collapses to the global topic weights.

## Method

FASTopic introduces a new paradigm called Dual Semantic-relation Reconstruction (DSR), which models doc-topic and topic-word distributions as semantic relations between document, topic, and word embeddings, and learns them by reconstructing each document as βθ. To model these relations, the paper proposes the Embedding Transport Plan (ETP), which formulates each relation as an entropic regularized optimal transport problem solved via Sinkhorn's algorithm, rather than the standard parameterized softmax. The transport plans are constrained by learnable weight vectors (s for topics, u for words) that are softmax-parameterized and uniformly initialized, which prevents the 'relation bias issue' where softmax causes most topic embeddings to cluster together and produce repetitive topics. Training minimizes L_ETP + L_DSR, optimizing only four parameters (topic embeddings T, word embeddings W, and weight vectors s, u) with Adam over 200 epochs, yielding running times of 10–60 seconds versus minutes for VAE-based baselines.

## Results

On topic quality, FASTopic achieves the best topic diversity (TD) across all six datasets (0.983–1.000) and the best topic coherence (C_V) on five of six (e.g., 0.457 on WoS, 0.439 on Wikitext-103), with ECRTM slightly ahead only on 20NG C_V (0.431 vs. 0.426). On document clustering, FASTopic attains the highest Purity and NMI on all three labeled datasets (e.g., 0.672/0.365 on WoS). In terms of efficiency, FASTopic runs in 10.6–60.3 seconds across datasets, compared to 274–1270 s for ECRTM and up to 2083 s for LDA-Mallet, making it 20–40× faster than the nearest neural baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FASTopic outperforms all eight baselines on topic diversity (TD) across every dataset, with margins of 0.02–0.75 over the nearest competitor. On topic coherence (C_V), FASTopic leads on five of six datasets; the sole exception is 20NG where ECRTM edges ahead by 0.005 (0.431 vs. 0.426). Among VAE-based baselines, ECRTM is the strongest competitor, achieving C_V within 0.005–0.019 of FASTopic, but FASTopic consistently surpasses it on TD (e.g., 0.999 vs. 0.988 on NYT) and on document clustering Purity/NMI (e.g., 0.672/0.365 vs. 0.650/0.349 on WoS). Clustering-based BERTopic trails FASTopic by 0.02–0.06 in C_V and 0.25–0.35 in TD, while conventional LDA-Mallet and NMF lag by 0.05–0.10 in C_V. In text classification (downstream task), FASTopic achieves 0.604/0.593 Acc/F1 on 20NG, 0.754/0.596 on NYT, and 0.739/0.703 on WoS, surpassing ECRTM by 0.123/0.124, 0.046/0.072, and 0.088/0.122 respectively. On running time, FASTopic (10.6–60.3 s) is 3–40× faster than all baselines, with the largest gap against LDA-Mallet (up to 2083 s on Wikitext-103) and ECRTM (up to 1270 s).

## Relation to Prior Work

FASTopic departs from all three established topic modeling paradigms—conventional (LDA/NMF), VAE-based (CombinedTM, ECRTM, GINopic), and clustering-based (BERTopic)—by introducing the Dual Semantic-relation Reconstruction (DSR) paradigm, which models doc-topic and topic-word distributions as semantic relations between pretrained document embeddings and learnable topic/word embeddings. The Embedding Transport Plan (ETP) method repurposes entropic regularized optimal transport (Peyré & Cuturi, 2019; Cuturi, 2013) not as a distance metric or VAE regularizer (as in ECRTM) but as the explicit mechanism for modeling the relations themselves, with Sinkhorn's algorithm providing differentiability. This yields an objective with only four learnable parameters (topic embeddings, word embeddings, and two weight vectors), in stark contrast to the multi-module encoder-decoder architectures of prior neural topic models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FASTopic addresses a practical bottleneck in topic modeling: existing neural methods are either slow (VAE-based models take minutes to hours), produce repetitive topics (clustering-based methods), or are unstable across hyperparameter settings. By achieving SOTA quality while running in under a minute with minimal hyperparameters, FASTopic makes high-quality topic modeling accessible for large-scale and iterative applications. The identified 'relation bias' issue—where parameterized softmax collapses most topic embeddings together—provides a useful diagnostic for why naive distance-to-probability conversions fail in embedding-space topic modeling. Open questions include whether the DSR paradigm extends beyond bag-of-words reconstruction (e.g., to sequence-level or multimodal settings) and whether the transport plan formulation can be further generalized to hierarchical or dynamic topic structures.
