## Problem

Auto-regressive Decision Trees (ARDTs), originally proposed for time-series prediction, have never been explored for language modeling. It remains unclear whether these simple, interpretable tree-based models can generate coherent text or perform reasoning tasks, and what their theoretical computational power is compared to standard decision trees or Transformer architectures.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241993/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives a sequence of text tokens (e.g., a story beginning or a reasoning question), converts them to 100-dimensional Word2Vec embeddings, and computes a weighted average with exponential decay (α ∈ (0,1)) to form a single input vector. At each auto-regressive step, the tree ensemble outputs a predicted embedding for the next token, which is matched to the nearest vocabulary word via L2 distance; for reasoning tasks, GPT-2 Transformer output vectors can substitute for the Word2Vec embeddings as input features.

## Method

The core method uses XGBoost (gradient-boosted decision trees) as the next-token predictor in an auto-regressive loop: at each step, the weighted-average embedding of the current sequence is fed to the tree ensemble, which predicts the next token's embedding (trained with MSE loss), and the closest vocabulary word is appended to the sequence. Theoretically, the paper proves that ARDTs can simulate finite automata, bounded-memory Turing machines, and sparse circuits by emitting intermediate 'chain-of-thought' tokens, with polynomial size/depth bounds, whereas a single non-auto-regressive decision tree would require exponential size for tasks like parity. Empirically, a 0.3M-parameter ARDT ensemble outperforms a 1M-parameter GPT-Neo Transformer on TinyStories story continuation, and decision trees trained on top of GPT-2 embeddings achieve competitive accuracy on BIG-Bench-Hard reasoning tasks (Boolean Expressions, Navigate, Web-of-Lies, Sports Understanding) compared to InstructGPT and PaLM-540B.

## Results

On TinyStories, ARDTs (0.3M parameters) achieve a grammar score of 7.85, consistency of 7.36, and plot of 5.39, outperforming the 1M-parameter GPT-Neo baseline (4.42/6.32/3.65) while approaching the 33M-parameter GPT-Neo (7.80/9.10/7.65). On BIG-Bench-Hard reasoning tasks, ARDTs with GPT-2 embeddings achieve 77.4% average accuracy across four tasks (Boolean Expressions 85.3%, Navigate 69.2%, Web-of-Lies 71.1%, Sports Understanding 83.9%), surpassing InstructGPT (70.3%), Codex (65.8%), and PaLM-540B (69.3%). The ARDTs model uses only ~0.3M parameters (total tree nodes) for story generation and 200 training examples per reasoning task.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On TinyStories, ARDTs (0.3M params) clearly outperform the TinyStories-1M GPT-Neo baseline across all four metrics (e.g., Grammar 7.85 vs. 4.42, Plot 5.39 vs. 3.65), and match or exceed it on Grammar (7.85 vs. 7.80) relative to the much larger TinyStories-33M model, though they trail on Creativity (4.10 vs. 6.87) and Consistency (7.36 vs. 9.10). On BIG-Bench-Hard, ARDTs with GPT-2 embeddings (77.4% avg) outperform InstructGPT (70.3%), Codex (65.8%), and PaLM-540B (69.3%) by 7.1, 11.6, and 8.1 percentage points respectively, with the largest gains on Web-of-Lies (+19.5 over InstructGPT) and Sports Understanding (+13.5 over PaLM-540B). However, ARDTs still lag behind InstructGPT on Boolean Expressions (85.3% vs. 90.0%) and Navigate (69.2% vs. 68.0% is a narrow win). The linear-embedding variant (ARDTs-Lin) performs close to random (63.2% avg), highlighting that the GPT-2 embedding layer is critical for the reasoning gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Auto-regressive Decision Trees (ARDTs), originally proposed by Meek et al. (2002) for time-series prediction, to the domain of language modeling. The theoretical analysis draws on automata theory, Turing machine simulation, and circuit complexity to characterize the computational power of ARDTs when they perform intermediate 'chain-of-thought' token generation, building on the universal-learner framework of Malach (2023). Empirically, the method repurposes XGBoost gradient-boosted trees with Word2Vec embeddings as next-token predictors, positioning itself as an interpretable alternative to the dominant Transformer architecture and its recent efficiency-focused successors (Mamba, Griffin, RetNet).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The clean theoretical separation (Theorem 4) showing that standard decision trees require exponential size for parity while ARDTs handle it in O(n log|D|) time provides a principled justification for why auto-regressive intermediate computation unlocks super-linear expressiveness. However, the empirical validation is confined to TinyStories (a toy dataset of children's stories) and four BIG-Bench-Hard tasks where the tree ensembles are trained on top of GPT-2 embeddings, limiting the evidence that ARDTs alone drive the reasoning performance. Open questions include whether this approach scales to larger vocabularies and longer contexts, whether the interpretability advantage survives at scale, and whether hybrid tree-Transformer architectures can meaningfully outperform either component alone.
