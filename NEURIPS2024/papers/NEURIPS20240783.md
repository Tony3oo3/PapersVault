## Problem

Surrogate gradient methods for training Spiking Neural Networks suffer from an intrinsic gradient vanishing problem: all surrogate functions are bounded, so their gradients approach zero over most intervals, causing shallow-layer weights to freeze during optimization. This means deeper SNNs (e.g., ResNet34) actually perform worse than shallower ones (e.g., ResNet18), undermining the benefit of depth. The paper identifies and formally demonstrates this issue, then proposes a solution that bypasses the step-by-step gradient propagation entirely.

## Input / Output

The method accepts RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 224×224 for ImageNet) or event-stream frames (48×48 for CIFAR10-DVS) and outputs a class label via a standard fully connected head. During training, the network is partitioned into blocks and each block's output is routed through a global average pooling and FC layer to produce an auxiliary prediction; the final training output is a weighted sum of the main-branch prediction and all shortcut-branch predictions. During inference, only the main branch is used and all shortcut branches are removed, so there is zero additional computational cost at deployment.

## Method

The core mechanism is a shortcut back-propagation architecture: multiple auxiliary branches are added from intermediate ResNet blocks directly to the output, so the gradient from the loss reaches shallow-layer weights in a single step rather than being multiplied through every intermediate surrogate gradient. This transforms the gradient for the first layer from a long product of small terms (Eq. 8) into a sum of direct terms (Eq. 9), completely eliminating the vanishing effect. To balance the trade-off between sufficient shallow-layer updates and final main-branch accuracy, an evolutionary training framework introduces a linearly decaying balance coefficient λ(i) = λ(1 − i/I) that weights the shortcut-branch outputs: early in training the side branches dominate (ensuring shallow weights are well-trained), and late in training the main branch dominates (refining final classification accuracy). The approach is applied on standard LIF-neuron ResNet backbones (ResNet18/19/34) with 1–4 timesteps and is evaluated on both static and neuromorphic datasets.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, the proposed Evolutionary Training with ResNet19 achieves 95.36% top-1 accuracy at only 2 timesteps, matching DSR's 95.40% (which requires 20 timesteps) and surpassing Real Spike (95.31% at 2 timesteps). On CIFAR-100, the same configuration reaches 77.79% at 2 timesteps, outperforming the previous best (TET at 74.47% with 4 timesteps) by approximately 3.3%. On ImageNet, ResNet34 with 4 timesteps achieves 68.14%, exceeding Real Spike (67.69%) and SEW ResNet (67.04%). On the neuromorphic CIFAR10-DVS dataset, ResNet18 at 10 timesteps reaches 83.30%, surpassing Dspike (75.40%) by 7.9%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method consistently outperforms all 17 compared baselines across all four datasets. On CIFAR-10, it matches DSR (95.40% at 20 timesteps) with 95.36% at only 2 timesteps, and beats Real Spike (95.31%), TET (94.44%), Dspike (93.66%), and RecDis-SNN (93.64%) at the same or fewer timesteps. On CIFAR-100, the 3.3% improvement over TET (74.47%) and RecDis-SNN (74.10%) is particularly notable since those methods use 4 timesteps versus the proposed 2. On ImageNet, the method surpasses Real Spike (67.69%) by 0.45% and SEW ResNet (67.04%) by 1.10% with ResNet34 at 4 timesteps, while also outperforming MPBN, MS-ResNet, and GLIF. On CIFAR10-DVS, the 7.9% gain over Dspike (75.40%) and 10.9% over RecDis-SNN (72.42%) demonstrate the method's strength on temporal event-stream data. The ablation study further confirms that the shortcut back-propagation alone provides a 2.5–5.0% boost over vanilla training, with the evolutionary framework adding a further 0.1–0.3% on top.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper operates within the surrogate gradient (SG) training paradigm for SNNs, which itself builds on the broader ANN-to-SNN and direct-training literature. It departs from prior gradient-vanishing mitigations—ResNet architectural variants (SEW-ResNet, MS-ResNet), temporal normalization (tdBN, BNTT, TEBN, MPBN), and regularization losses (RecDis-SNN)—by arguing that all of these still require gradients to propagate layer-by-layer and thus cannot fully solve the problem. The key conceptual departure is the shortcut back-propagation: adding auxiliary branches from intermediate blocks directly to the output so that gradients reach shallow layers without traversing the full depth, a strategy borrowed in spirit from ResNet skip connections but applied at the training-loss level rather than the forward-pass level. The evolutionary training framework with a linearly decaying balance coefficient is a novel scheduling idea layered on top of this architectural modification.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The diagnosis that standard ResNet skip connections are insufficient in SNNs because the bounded surrogate gradient attenuates even the residual path is a useful and somewhat underappreciated observation for the SNN training community. The method's practical appeal is high: it adds no inference cost, requires only a small architectural change during training, and consistently improves accuracy across CIFAR-10/100, ImageNet, and event-based CIFAR10-DVS. Open questions include how the approach generalizes to non-ResNet backbones (e.g., Vision Transformers, U-Nets), whether the hand-tuned balance coefficient λ=0.25 and linear decay schedule are optimal, and how the method interacts with other recent SNN training advances like differentiable spike search or learned membrane time constants. The paper does not discuss limitations, leaving unclear how the added training-time branches affect memory and compute at scale.
