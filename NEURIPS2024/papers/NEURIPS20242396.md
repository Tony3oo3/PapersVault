## Problem

Existing protein fitness prediction methods fine-tune PLMs on experimental data but suffer from two overlooked issues: (1) evolutionary information captured by PLMs is entangled with multiple protein properties, so irrelevant information acts as noise for the target property of interest; and (2) conventional regression objectives (minimizing numerical error) overfit to dataset-specific measurement scales, severely limiting generalization to novel proteins.

## Input / Output

DePLM consumes three inputs per protein: the wildtype amino acid sequence, the per-position 20-dimensional evolutionary likelihood vector produced by a frozen PLM (ESM-1v or ESM-2), and the AlphaFold2-predicted 3D backbone structure encoded by ESM-IF. The output is a denoised property-specific likelihood (a 20×N probability matrix), from which mutation fitness scores are computed as log-likelihood ratios between mutant and wildtype amino acids (Eq. 10), yielding a ranking of all single mutants by their effect on the target property.

## Method

DePLM frames the refinement of noisy evolutionary likelihoods as a rank-based denoising diffusion process. The forward process uses QuickSort trajectories to generate intermediate rank states between the ground-truth property rank and the noisy evolutionary likelihood rank, ensuring a controlled, deterministic corruption path. The backward process operates in likelihood space (since ranks are non-differentiable) and is guided by a feature encoder that fuses PLM hidden states, multi-head attention weights, and ESM-IF structural representations through stacked attention layers with outer-product and outer-difference interactions. Each denoising step subtracts the protein representation from the noisy likelihood's hidden encoding to isolate the noise component, then maps it back via a frozen PLM head. The training objective replaces standard MSE with a modified ELBO that maximizes Spearman rank correlation at each diffusion step, making the model dataset-agnostic. Only 3 diffusion steps are needed because the initial state is already an informative evolutionary likelihood rather than uninformative Gaussian noise.

## Results

On the ProteinGym benchmark (Spearman rank correlation), DePLM(ESM2) achieves 0.707 on Fitness, 0.742 on Expression, 0.764 on Binding, and 0.693 on Activity, outperforming the strongest baseline ProteinNPT (0.668, 0.736, 0.706, 0.680) by 3.9, 0.6, 5.8, and 1.3 points respectively. On the multi-mutant benchmarks, DePLM(ESM2) reaches 0.904 on β-Lactamase and 0.665 on GB1, while DePLM(ESM1v) achieves 0.676 on GB1 and 0.662 on Fluorescence. In the generalization setting (Table 2), DePLM(ESM2) leads all categories (Stability 0.773, Fitness 0.480, Expression 0.510, Binding 0.441, Activity 0.518), surpassing the best supervised baseline SaProt(FT) by 7.0 points on Stability and 2.3 points on Activity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DePLM(ESM2) outperforms ProteinNPT (the strongest supervised baseline) on 4 of 5 ProteinGym categories: Fitness (+3.9), Expression (+0.6), Binding (+5.8), and Activity (+1.3), but trails on Stability (0.897 vs. 0.904). Against structure-aware baselines ProtSSN and SaProt, DePLM(ESM2) leads on Fitness (+1.5/+2.1), Expression (+2.4/+2.6), Binding (+0.7/+1.5), and Activity (+1.6/+1.6). On the multi-mutant benchmarks, DePLM(ESM2) achieves 0.904 on β-Lactamase (vs. 0.823 for OHE) and 0.665 on GB1 (vs. 0.533 for OHE), while DePLM(ESM1v) reaches 0.676 on GB1. In the generalization setting (Table 2), DePLM(ESM2) dominates all five categories, beating the best fine-tuned baseline SaProt(FT) by 7.0 points on Stability (0.773 vs. 0.703) and 2.3 points on Activity (0.518 vs. 0.495), and outperforming unsupervised TranceptEVE by 27.3 points on Stability (0.773 vs. 0.500). The ablation (Table 3) confirms the rank objective is the single most critical component: removing it drops average performance from 0.672 to 0.487, a 18.5-point collapse.

## Relation to Prior Work

DePLM builds on the established paradigm of using protein language models (ESM-1v, ESM-2, Tranception) to capture evolutionary information for mutation effect prediction, and on supervised fine-tuning approaches like ProteinNPT that combine PLM representations with experimental DMS data. Its key departure is reframing the evolutionary likelihood as a noisy signal—where property-irrelevant evolutionary pressures act as 'noise'—and applying a denoising diffusion process in the rank space of mutation likelihoods to isolate the property-relevant component. This draws on diffusion model machinery (forward corruption, learned reverse denoising) but repurposes it for a fundamentally different setting: the forward process is deterministic (using QuickSort trajectories between two pre-defined rank states) rather than stochastic, and the learning objective replaces MSE with Spearman rank correlation to achieve dataset-agnostic training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in protein engineering: DMS assays are protein-specific and expensive, so models that overfit to one dataset fail to generalize to new proteins. By operating in rank space and using a rank-correlation objective, DePLM demonstrates substantially better cross-protein generalization than standard fine-tuning (e.g., 0.773 vs. 0.454 Spearman on stability in the generalization setting), which is critical for scaling protein optimization beyond the handful of well-characterized proteins. A key limitation is the reliance on wild-type marginal probability, which sums individual mutation effects and ignores epistatic interactions; the authors note that masked marginal probability could improve multi-mutation prediction. Open questions include whether the rank-based denoising framework extends to other molecular optimization tasks (e.g., small-molecule property prediction) and whether the QuickSort-based forward process can be replaced with more principled continuous interpolations.
