## Problem

Computing near-optimal deterministic policies for constrained reinforcement learning has been an open challenge since NP-hardness was established nearly 25 years ago. Existing methods cannot simultaneously achieve polynomial-time computation, feasibility guarantees, and optimality: exact methods are exponential, while polynomial-time methods either violate constraints or require restrictive assumptions like constant horizon. The paper asks whether a fully polynomial-time approximation scheme (FPTAS) exists for deterministic policies under general constraint criteria.

## Input / Output

The algorithm takes a tabular finite-horizon cMDP (finite state set S, action set A, transition distributions P, reward and cost functions r and c, horizon H), a time-space-recursive (TSR) cost criterion C, and a budget B. It outputs either a deterministic policy π over the original MDP or a certificate of infeasibility. The policy is internally represented as a value-augmented mapping from (state, value-demand) pairs to (action, future-value-vector) pairs; at execution time the agent tracks its current value demand and selects the corresponding original action, making the policy directly implementable without solving any optimization at decision time.

## Method

The approach converts the original packing problem (maximize value subject to cost ≤ B) into a covering problem (minimize cost subject to value ≥ V*) via a packing-covering reduction, then augments the state space with value demands (s, v) and the action space with future value-demand vectors (a, v⃗) to eliminate cyclic subproblem dependencies. The resulting Bellman update over the exponentially large augmented action space is as hard as knapsack, but the time-space-recursive (TSR) structure of the cost criterion allows decomposing it into a sequential dynamic program that decides one value demand per state, processed in a forward pass. To keep the computation polynomial, value demands are rounded (additively to a δ-grid or geometrically to powers of 1/(1−δ)) and partial sums during the DP are rounded to a finite set, with the rounding parameter δ = ε/(H(S+1)+1) calibrated so that accumulated error over both the H time steps and S state dimensions stays within ε. This yields an additive FPTAS for polynomially-bounded rewards and a relative FPTAS for non-negative rewards, resolving three open complexity questions for anytime, almost-sure, and deterministic expectation-constrained policies.

## Results

The paper provides two FPTAS guarantees for computing near-optimal deterministic policies under any TSR cost criterion (expectation, almost sure, or anytime). The additive FPTAS (Theorem 2) runs in O(H⁷S⁵Ar_max³/ε³) time and produces a policy with value at least V_M* − ε. The relative FPTAS (Theorem 3), requiring non-negative rewards, runs in O(H⁷S⁵A·log(r_max/(r_min·p_min))³/ε³) time and produces a policy with value at least V_M*(1−ε). These are the first polynomial-time approximation guarantees for deterministic policies under almost-sure and anytime constraints, resolving open questions dating back ~25 years.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical baselines or experimental comparisons. The paper positions itself against prior work qualitatively: existing optimal-but-infeasible methods (e.g., [36] for almost-sure/anytime) and feasible-but-suboptimal heuristics (e.g., [29] for expectation) each fail to simultaneously achieve efficiency, feasibility, and optimality. The proposed FPTAS is the first method to achieve all three properties for deterministic policies under TSR criteria. The paper also notes that its TSR condition is necessary in the sense that non-TSR criteria (e.g., chance constraints [51]) are provably inapproximable, and that the mild reward assumptions (polynomially bounded for additive, non-negative for relative) are unavoidable since they correspond to the knapsack problem's inapproximability boundaries.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the anytime-constrained RL framework of McMahan & Zhu (AISTATS 2024) by abstracting the cost criterion into a general time-space-recursive (TSR) condition, thereby unifying expectation, almost-sure, and anytime constraints under one umbrella. Methodologically, it draws on the stochastic dynamic programming FPTAS lineage of Halman et al. (2014) but adapts it to the MDP setting where an innate state space coexists with the constraint dimension, requiring the novel value-demand augmentation and action-space approximate dynamic programming. The paper departs from prior CRL work (Altman 1999, Feinberg 2000) that either assumed stochastic policies or proved NP-hardness for deterministic ones, by showing that the TSR structure is precisely what makes polynomial-time approximation possible.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work resolves three open complexity questions that have persisted for nearly 25 years since Feinberg's NP-hardness result, establishing that polynomial-time approximation is achievable for deterministic policies under expectation, almost-sure, and anytime constraints. The clean separation between TSR criteria (tractable) and non-TSR criteria like chance constraints (provably inapproximable) provides a principled boundary for future work. Open questions remain: whether faster FPTAS exist, whether TSR is truly necessary or a more general condition suffices, and whether multiple simultaneous constraints from the TSR family can be handled efficiently. The practical scope is currently limited to tabular, finite-horizon MDPs, so extending to function-approximation or infinite-horizon settings would be a significant next step.
