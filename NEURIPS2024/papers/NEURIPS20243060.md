## Problem

Zero-shot video editing with pre-trained T2I diffusion models suffers from severe temporal inconsistency (flickering, blurring) because the model lacks temporal constraints. Existing remedies either depend on pre-trained optical-flow models that provide only coarse one-to-one token correspondence, or require additional training and optimization. The paper identifies that diffusion features inherently encode precise one-to-many correspondence across frames, but no prior work has exploited this property for video editing.

## Input / Output

The method takes a source video (512×512, 10–32 frames) and a text editing prompt (e.g., style transfer, background change, subject modification) and produces a temporally consistent edited video. It operates on Stable Diffusion 2.1 latents with 100 DDIM inversion steps and 50 denoising steps; the correspondence map is computed once from the source video before editing begins, using a fixed noise timestep t=261 and a 9×9 sliding window with top-K=3 correspondences per frame.

## Method

COVE extracts normalized diffusion features from each source frame via a single denoising step through the U-Net decoder, then computes one-to-many token correspondence across frames using cosine similarity within a small sliding window (l=9) to keep complexity at O((N−1)·H·W·l²·d) rather than O(N²·H²·W²·d). During both DDIM inversion and denoising, for each token the method samples its K corresponding tokens from the noisy latent in every other frame, merges them along the temporal dimension (50% merge ratio) to reduce redundancy, and performs self-attention with the current token as query and the merged correspondences as keys/values. This correspondence-guided attention is injected into the U-Net alongside standard spatial-temporal attention, requiring no extra training or optimization and plugging directly into the off-the-shelf T2I model.

## Results

COVE achieves state-of-the-art performance across all four VBench metrics and the user study on 115 text-video pairs. Specifically, COVE scores 0.9731 Subject Consistency, 0.9892 Motion Smoothness, 0.7122 Aesthetic Quality, and 0.7441 Imaging Quality, outperforming all five baselines on every metric. In the user study with 45 participants, COVE is preferred 44.5% of the time, nearly triple the next-best method (FLATTEN at 14.8%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

COVE outperforms all five baselines on every reported metric. On Subject Consistency, COVE (0.9731) leads the closest competitor FateZero (0.9622) by ~1.1 points, while FRESCO lags furthest at 0.9358. On Motion Smoothness, COVE (0.9892) edges out TokenFlow (0.9803), which was the previous best, with FateZero trailing significantly at 0.9547. The largest relative gap appears in Aesthetic Quality, where COVE (0.7122) surpasses TokenFlow (0.6904) by over 2 points and FateZero (0.6258) by nearly 9 points. In the user study, COVE's 44.5% preference rate is roughly 3× that of FLATTEN (14.8%) and over 6× that of FateZero (7.4%), indicating a clear perceptual advantage in temporal consistency and visual quality that the automated metrics only partially capture.

## Relation to Prior Work

COVE extends the insight from Tang et al. (NeurIPS 2023) that diffusion features contain emergent spatial correspondence, repurposing it from image-to-image matching to the video editing domain. It departs from prior zero-shot video editing methods (FateZero, TokenFlow, RAVE) that rely on attention blending, feature smoothing, or noise shuffling, and from optical-flow-based approaches (FLATTEN, FRESCO) that enforce one-to-one token trajectories. The key conceptual shift is leveraging one-to-many (K>1) correspondence directly from diffusion features via a sliding-window strategy, combined with token merging [5] and DDIM inversion [64] as the editing backbone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

COVE demonstrates that diffusion feature correspondence is both more accurate (PCK 0.92 vs. 0.87 for optical flow) and more flexible (one-to-many) than the optical-flow-based correspondence that prior methods depend on, removing a key external dependency. The zero-shot, training-free integration into off-the-shelf T2I models makes it practically accessible. Open questions include whether more effective alternatives to self-attention could further exploit the correspondence signal, and whether the method scales to longer videos or higher resolutions without the current GPU memory overhead of correspondence-guided attention.
