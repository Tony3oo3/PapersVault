## Problem

Contrastive learning, the dominant self-supervised pre-training paradigm for endoscopic videos, produces representations with strong instance discriminability but insufficient fine-grained pixel-level information, leading to suboptimal performance on dense prediction tasks like segmentation and detection. Moreover, endoscopic videos exhibit unstable inter-frame variations from manual camera manipulation and small inter-class differences where lesions closely resemble surrounding normal tissue, making standard random masking strategies poorly suited to this domain.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241520/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes endoscopic video clips at 30 fps (~5 s duration) and produces class labels (PolypDiag), pixel-level polyp segmentation masks (CVC-12k), and polyp bounding boxes with categories (KUMC). During pre-training, each video is sampled into 2 global views at 224×224 resolution (8–16 frames) and 8 local views at 96×96 resolution (2–4–8–16 frames), with a 90% masking ratio applied to both. The ViT-B/16 backbone (121M parameters, ~190G FLOPs) is pre-trained for 30 epochs on 7 datasets totaling ~5.5M frames before fine-tuning on downstream tasks.

## Method

M²CRL introduces a multi-view masking strategy that pairs a frame-aggregated attention guided tube mask (FAGTM) on global views with a random tube mask on local views. FAGTM computes a frame-aggregated attention map by averaging the [cls]-token attention from the last block of a teacher ViT (using divided space-time attention) across all frames, then selects the top-γ (0.6) proportion of high-attention patches as candidates from which visible tokens are randomly sampled, ensuring critical regions remain unmasked even at 90% masking ratio. The framework integrates this multi-view mask reconstruction (pixel-level L1 regression of masked patches) with DINO-style self-distillation contrastive learning, where the teacher network processes global views and the student processes both global and local views, with the teacher updated via EMA. The total loss combines four terms: global–global contrastive, global–local contrastive, global reconstruction, and local reconstruction, jointly optimizing holistic discriminative and fine-grained pixel-level representations.

## Results

M²CRL achieves 94.2% F1 on PolypDiag (classification), 81.4% Dice on CVC-12k (segmentation), and 86.3% F1 on KUMC (detection), with a pre-training time of 24.3 hours on 4 A100 GPUs. On the additional Cholec80 surgical phase recognition task, M²CRL reaches 88.7% relaxed-boundary F1. The method outperforms the prior SOTA endoscopic pre-training model Endo-FM by +3.5% F1 (classification), +7.5% Dice (segmentation), and +2.2% F1 (detection), and surpasses VideoMAE V2 by +4.6%, +0.4%, and +2.1% respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

M²CRL outperforms all 13 compared baselines across every downstream task. Against the primary endoscopic SOTA Endo-FM [30], gains are +3.5% F1 (PolypDiag), +7.5% Dice (CVC-12k), and +2.2% F1 (KUMC), with the segmentation improvement being the largest. Versus the latest general video SSL methods, M²CRL beats VideoMAE V2 [23] by +4.6% F1 on classification but only +0.4% Dice on segmentation (81.4 vs 81.0), and surpasses VideoMAE [22] by +2.8% F1 and +3.5% F1 on classification and detection respectively. Against DropMAE [79], M²CRL leads by +6.0% F1 on classification and +4.6% F1 on detection. On the older contrastive baselines (TimeSformer, CORP, FAME, ProViCo, VCL, ST-Adapter), M²CRL achieves margins of 7–10% F1 on classification and 12–25% Dice on segmentation. On the supplementary Cholec80 surgical phase recognition task, M²CRL (88.7 F1) beats Endo-FM (87.5), SwAV (86.1), SimCLR (84.5), MoCo v2 (81.7), and DINO (77.6). The only near-tie is segmentation vs VideoMAE V2, where the 0.4% Dice gap is within the reported standard deviations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate self-supervised paradigms for endoscopic video pre-training: contrastive learning (following Endo-FM's DINO-style self-distillation) and masked video modeling (following SimMIM's pixel-reconstruction approach). The key architectural departure is a multi-view masking strategy that assigns a frame-aggregated attention guided tube mask to global views (capturing spatiotemporal context) and a random tube mask (borrowed from VideoMAE) to local views (capturing fine-grained detail), motivated by the inter-frame instability and low lesion contrast unique to endoscopic footage. Conceptually, the work positions itself as a bridge between the discriminative strength of contrastive methods and the pixel-level sensitivity of generative/masked methods, which prior endoscopic pre-training (Endo-FM, MSN) addressed only through contrastive objectives.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that combining contrastive and masked objectives in a single pre-training pipeline yields complementary gains across classification, segmentation, and detection for endoscopic videos, with the largest improvement (7.5% Dice) on the dense prediction task where contrastive-only methods are weakest. This suggests a general principle for medical video pre-training: discriminative and reconstructive objectives are not redundant but capture orthogonal information. Limitations include reliance on RGB-only input (no audio/text), significant compute requirements (4×A100, 24.3h pre-training), and evaluation confined to the endoscopic domain, leaving open whether the attention-guided multi-view masking transfers to other video domains with different motion characteristics.
