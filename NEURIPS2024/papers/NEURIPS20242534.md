## Problem

Deep Graph Neural Networks suffer from oversmoothing, where node features converge to a common fixed point as layers increase, rendering deep architectures ineffective for capturing long-range dependencies. Existing mitigations like Gradient Gating adaptively suppress message passing, effectively reducing deeper layers to identity mappings. The paper asks whether one can instead learn adaptive, node-specific feature updates that preserve representational diversity while maintaining effective information propagation across many layers.

## Input / Output

The CNA module is a plug-and-play replacement for the activation function in the Update step of any message-passing GNN (GCN, GAT, GraphSAGE, TransformerConv, Dir-GNN). It consumes the aggregated node feature vectors at each layer and produces transformed node embeddings that feed into the next layer. The clustering step operates on the current feature matrix without modifying the adjacency structure, so the graph topology remains fixed while node representations evolve. Outputs are standard node embeddings for classification, regression, or graph-level pooling.

## Method

CNA performs three operations per layer in the Update step: (1) Cluster — k-means partitions the current node features into K groups (super-nodes) based on Euclidean distance, introducing a hierarchical grouping that is independent of graph topology; (2) Normalize — a nonparametric per-cluster, per-feature standardization (mean and variance computed within each cluster) stabilizes numerical ranges without the affine rescaling of standard Instance Normalization; (3) Activate — each cluster receives its own learnable rational activation function of degree (5,4), which subsumes the affine transformation and provides a flexible, smoothly differentiable non-linearity. The key insight is that by applying distinct learned projections to groups of similar nodes, CNA breaks the uniform low-pass filtering that causes oversmoothing, while the rational activations' high plasticity allows the network to adapt its transformation shape during training. The module requires no architectural changes beyond swapping the activation, and ablations confirm all three steps are necessary for the full benefit.

## Results

CNA achieves 94.18% accuracy on Cora and 95.75% on CiteSeer, outperforming the respective PwC leaderboard leaders (SSP at 90.16% and ACMII-Snowball-2 at 82.07%). On the large-scale ogbn-arxiv dataset, GCN+CNA reaches 74.64% test accuracy with only 389.2k parameters, surpassing all baselines including RevGAT-SelfKD (74.26%, 2.10M params) and GAT (73.91%, 1.44M params). In node regression, TransConv+CNA achieves the lowest NMSE of 0.131 on Chameleon and 0.068 on Squirrel. Across 11 node classification benchmarks, CNA outperforms the SOTA on 8 of 11 datasets (73%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CNA consistently outperforms vanilla GNN architectures (GCN, GAT, GraphSAGE, TransformerConv) by 12–14 percentage points on Cora when added as a plug-in module. Against dedicated oversmoothing mitigations, CNA beats PairNorm (93.66% vs 71.0% on Cora), GCN+BatchNorm (73.9%), and Differentiable Group Normalization (82.0%), while also surpassing Gradient Gating (G2-GCN: 0.137 vs 0.131 NMSE on Chameleon) and GCNII (72.74% vs 74.64% on ogbn-arxiv). CNA dominates the PwC leaderboard on 8 of 11 datasets, with the largest margins on CiteSeer (+13.68 pp over ACMII-Snowball-2) and Chameleon (+5.38 pp over DJ-GNN). The only losses are on the small heterophilic graphs Texas (90.00% vs 92.45% for 2-HiGCN) and Wisconsin (89.29% vs 94.99% for 5-HiGCN), as well as a narrow gap on PubMed (90.16% vs 91.44% for ACM-Snowball-3). On ogbn-arxiv, CNA achieves the best accuracy (74.64%) with 389.2k parameters, compared to 2.10M for RevGAT-SelfKD (74.26%) and 3.88M for RevGAT-Wide (74.05%), demonstrating superior parameter efficiency.

## Relation to Prior Work

CNA sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) oversmoothing mitigation in MPNNs, where prior work has focused on regulating message-passing dynamics (Gradient Gating, RevGNN) or enforcing distance constraints (PairNorm, GraphNorm); (2) normalization in deep learning, where CNA's per-cluster normalization is related to Deep Group Normalization but replaces soft, learned grouping with hard k-means; and (3) learnable activation functions, specifically rational (Padé) activations from Molina et al. (2019) and Delfosse et al. (2024), which serve as the per-cluster non-linear projections. The key conceptual departure is from Gradient Gating, which adaptively suppresses updates to prevent convergence; CNA instead preserves full information flow while ensuring distinct representations through cluster-specific transformations, effectively introducing a hierarchical super-node structure into the Update step of any MPNN.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CNA offers a simple, architecture-agnostic plug-in that simultaneously addresses oversmoothing and parameter efficiency, making deeper GNNs practically viable without the parameter explosion seen in methods like GCNII or RevGAT. The 13+ point gap on CiteSeer and the ~5× parameter reduction on ogbn-arxiv suggest that much of the prior SOTA was achieved by over-parameterizing rather than by better inductive biases. Open questions include whether the non-differentiable k-means step can be replaced by learnable clustering (e.g., DGN-style), whether CNA extends to non-convolutional MPNNs or Transformers, and whether the clustering cost becomes a bottleneck on graphs with millions of nodes. The paper also acknowledges that a formal theoretical link between CNA's cluster structure and oversmoothing guarantees remains to be established.
