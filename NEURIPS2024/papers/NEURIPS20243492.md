## Problem

For fair allocation of indivisible goods with additive valuations, constant-factor MMS approximations exist when valuations are public, but Amanatidis et al. (EC 2017) showed that no truthful mechanism for two agents and m items can do better than a floor(m/2)-approximation. This paper bridges the gap between these two regimes by introducing a learning-augmented framework where predictions on the input enable near-optimal consistency while preserving near-optimal robustness when predictions are wrong.

## Input / Output

The mechanism receives agents' reports (preference orders over m indivisible items), a prediction (either a full preference order per agent, a bag-filling termination index plus a bit, or a structured O(log m/ε)-bit summary), and the item set M. It outputs a partition of M into n bundles. Truthfulness must hold for every possible prediction, and the prediction space can range from Ω(m) bits (full orderings) down to O(log m) or O(log m/ε) bits for succinct variants. The mechanism is modular: the allocation procedure A is swappable, yielding different consistency-robustness tradeoffs.

## Method

The core contribution is the Plant-and-Steal framework, a three-phase modular mechanism: (1) apply an allocation procedure A (e.g., Balanced-Round-Robin, 1-2-Round-Robin, Bag-Filling, or Cut-and-Balance) on the predictions to produce initial bundles; (2) each agent 'plants' their predicted-favorite item into the other agent's bundle; (3) each agent 'steals' back their report-favorite item from the other's bundle. Truthfulness is trivial because reports are used only in the stealing phase, where each agent selects a single item from a prediction-determined set. With Balanced-Round-Robin, the mechanism achieves 2-consistency and ⌈m/2⌉-robustness (matching the optimal truthful lower bound); with 1-2-Round-Robin, it improves to 3/2-consistency. Under noisy predictions measured by Kendall tau distance d, the approximation degrades gracefully to O(√d). For n > 2 agents, a recursive plant-and-steal procedure combined with modified Round-Robin yields 2-consistency with relaxed robustness against a 1-out-of-⌈3n/2⌉ MMS benchmark.

## Results

For two agents with ordering predictions, the Plant-and-Steal framework with Balanced-Round-Robin achieves 2-consistency (2-approximation to MMS when predictions are accurate) and ⌈m/2⌉-robustness, matching the optimal truthful mechanism bound from Amanatidis et al. [7]. A refined 1-2-Round-Robin variant improves consistency to 3/2 at the cost of ⌊2m/3⌋ robustness. Under noisy predictions with Kendall tau distance d, the mechanism degrades gracefully to a (2√d+6)-approximation, interpolating between constant consistency (d=0) and O(m) robustness (d=Θ(m²)). For n>2 agents, a 2-consistent mechanism is obtained with m−⌈3n/2⌉−1 robustness against a relaxed MMS benchmark.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is against the trivial truthful mechanism (first agent picks their favorite item, second gets the rest), which achieves ⌊m/2⌋-approximation and was shown by Amanatidis et al. [7] to be optimal for any truthful mechanism without predictions. The Plant-and-Steal framework with Balanced-Round-Robin matches this robustness bound (⌈m/2⌉) while achieving 2-consistency when predictions are accurate, bridging the gap between the trivial mechanism and the 3/2-approximation achievable with public valuations. In the experimental evaluation (m=100 items), Partition-Plant-Steal outperforms Partition (prediction-only) on uncorrelated instances with high noise, and outperforms Random-Steal (stealing-only) across all noise levels, demonstrating the best-of-both-worlds tradeoff. For correlated preferences, the stealing component provides minimal benefit, and Partition-Plant-Steal's planting step gives a small edge over Partition-Steal at low noise (ε=0.02). The impossibility result (Theorem G.1) establishes that no mechanism can achieve consistency below 6/5 while maintaining bounded robustness, regardless of the prediction type used.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two well-studied lines: truthful mechanisms for MMS allocation (Amanatidis et al. [6,7], who showed the ⌊m/2⌋ impossibility for two agents) and the learning-augmented mechanism design framework (Lykouris & Vassilvitskii [34], Agrawal et al. [1], Balkanski et al. [14]). The Plant-and-Steal framework is a novel mechanism-design primitive that modularly combines an allocation procedure (Round-Robin, Bag-Filling, Cut-and-Balance) with a minimal use of reports (a single 'steal' step) to simultaneously achieve consistency from predictions and robustness from reports. The allocation procedures themselves are drawn from prior work: Balanced-Round-Robin from Amanatidis et al. [8], 1-2-Round-Robin from Amanatidis et al. [6], and Bag-Filling from the standard fair-division literature [10].

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper bridges a stark gap: non-truthful algorithms achieve constant-factor MMS approximations (down to 959/720), while truthful mechanisms without predictions are stuck at ⌊m/2⌋. By showing that even a simple preference-order prediction yields a 2-approximation (or 3/2 with 1-2-Round-Robin), the work demonstrates that the incentive-compatibility barrier is largely an information barrier, not a fundamental one. Open questions include whether the 6/5 consistency lower bound for bounded-robustness mechanisms is tight, whether the O(√d) degradation under Kendall tau noise is optimal, and whether the n-agent mechanism's relaxed robustness (competing against MMS for ⌈3n/2⌉ agents) can be tightened. The framework's modularity suggests it could be instantiated with future improved allocation procedures as the state-of-the-art MMS approximation continues to improve.
