## Problem

Structured pruning of neural networks requires deciding which entire structures (neurons, convolutional filters) to remove while maintaining accuracy, but existing Bayesian pruning methods either rely on task-specific threshold tuning (e.g., signal-to-noise ratio cutoffs) or require computationally expensive spike-and-slab priors with complex posteriors. The paper addresses the lack of a principled, threshold-free criterion that automatically determines when a structure can be pruned without sacrificing performance.

## Input / Output

The method operates on a neural network augmented with multiplicative noise layers (one per structural element) trained via variational inference on a standard supervised dataset. It takes the learned variational parameters φ and deterministic weights W as input, computes a closed-form ΔF score per structure, and outputs a pruned network where all structures with ΔF ≥ 0 are removed. The resulting compression rate is determined automatically by the BMR criterion rather than a user-specified target, though BMRS_U allows tuning via a precision parameter p1 that controls the allowable range of noise values.

## Method

BMRS combines multiplicative noise-based structured pruning (Neklyudov et al., 2017) with Bayesian model reduction (Friston et al., 2018) to derive a threshold-free pruning rule. The key mechanism is computing the change in variational free energy (ΔF) under a reduced prior that collapses the noise variable θ toward zero; if ΔF ≥ 0, the structure is pruned. Two closed-form derivations are provided: BMRS_N uses a truncated log-normal reduced prior approximating a Dirac delta at 0, yielding automatic compression without any hyperparameter tuning, while BMRS_U uses a truncated log-uniform reduced prior whose support [1/2^{p2}, 1/2^{p1}] maps to floating-point precision, enabling tunable compression by selecting the precision cutoff p1. The ΔF computation requires only the statistics of the prior, reduced prior, and variational distribution, making it efficient to evaluate per structure during or after training.

## Results

On Lenet5/CIFAR-10, BMRS_U-4 achieves 67.66% accuracy at 68.07% parameter compression, matching the unpruned model (67.62%) while removing over two-thirds of parameters, whereas L2 magnitude pruning collapses to 10.00% (random) at the same compression rate. On ResNet-50/CIFAR-10, BMRS_N reaches 89.62% accuracy at 87.10% compression, slightly outperforming SNR (89.24% at 91.73% compression) with less aggressive pruning. On ViT/CIFAR-10, BMRS_U-4 attains 94.83% accuracy at 67.16% compression, exceeding the unpruned baseline (94.80%) and outperforming SNR (94.78% at 73.03% compression). Across all six MLP/Lenet5 settings, BMRS_N achieves high compression (48–87%) without any threshold tuning, while BMRS_U-4 yields the highest compression in 4 of 6 settings and the highest accuracy in 5 of 6.

## Baselines & Comparisons

L2 magnitude pruning (Li et al., ICLR 2017) is the weakest baseline: at the same compression rate as BMRS_N it degrades to near-random accuracy (10–11%) in every setting, confirming that weight magnitude is a poor proxy for structural importance. The SNR criterion from Neklyudov et al. (NeurIPS 2017) is inconsistent, producing large accuracy drops in 3 of 6 MLP/Lenet5 settings (e.g., 10.00% on Fashion-MNIST MLP, 17.01% on Fashion-MNIST Lenet5) due to its fixed threshold of 1, whereas BMRS_N and BMRS_U maintain stable accuracy across all settings without any threshold. BMRS_N matches or slightly exceeds SNR accuracy at comparable compression (e.g., 66.14% vs. 67.68% on Lenet5/CIFAR-10, 89.62% vs. 89.24% on ResNet-50/CIFAR-10) while requiring no hyperparameter tuning. BMRS_U-4 achieves the highest compression in 4 of 6 settings and the highest accuracy in 5 of 6, but requires selecting the precision cutoff p₁ as a hyperparameter. Gradient-based pruning (LeCun et al. 1989; Hassibi et al. 1992), evaluated in Appendix E, shows similar accuracy-vs-compression trade-offs to SNR but also requires threshold tuning and does not match BMRS's threshold-free operation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper combines two previously separate lines of work: Bayesian structured pruning via multiplicative noise (Neklyudov et al., 2017), which uses truncated log-uniform priors and log-normal variational distributions to induce structural sparsity, and Bayesian model reduction (Friston et al., 2018), which enables efficient model comparison under a change in prior. The key conceptual departure is using BMR to derive principled, threshold-free pruning criteria for multiplicative noise models, rather than relying on ad-hoc signal-to-noise ratio thresholds or computationally expensive spike-and-slab priors. This positions BMRS between the BNN-based BMR pruning of Beckers et al. and Markovic et al. (which operate on weight distributions) and the threshold-based multiplicative noise pruning of Neklyudov et al., bridging the two by applying BMR directly to the noise variables.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The threshold-free property of BMRS_N—automatically halting compression at the accuracy-compression knee point without manual tuning—addresses a persistent practical pain point in structured pruning where practitioners must search over pruning thresholds. The BMRS_U variant's connection to floating-point precision (p1 parameter) provides an interpretable compression knob tied to hardware representability. Open questions include whether BMR-based pruning extends to hierarchical spike-and-slab priors for more aggressive compression, whether the multiplicative noise overhead becomes prohibitive at scale, and whether the approach generalizes to pruning non-linear structures (e.g., attention heads, residual blocks) beyond linear layers and convolutional filters.
