## Problem

Open-source large language and vision models (LLVMs) lag behind closed-source counterparts, and existing remedies—scaling model size, adding vision encoders, or deploying multiple computer vision models—inflate computational cost. The paper asks whether the multifaceted information underlying diverse vision-language capabilities (image understanding, common-sense knowledge, non-object concepts like charts and math, and step-by-step reasoning) can instead be implicitly embedded as rationales within a compact model, eliminating the need for external perception modules at inference time.

## Input / Output

Meteor takes a 490×490 RGB image and a free-form text question as input and produces a text answer. At inference, no explicit rationale is supplied; instead, 10 evenly-spaced special <tor> tokens are inserted into the input sequence and propagated through the Mamba module, which retrieves the rationale-embedded features learned during training. The image is encoded by CLIP-L/14 (positional embeddings interpolated from 24×24 to 35×35), and the final answer is generated autoregressively by the 7B backbone MLM using deterministic beam search (n=3).

## Method

Meteor pairs a Mamba-130M rationale-embedding module (Meteor-Mamba: 24 layers, 768 hidden dim) with an InternLM2-7B backbone (Meteor-MLM: 32 layers, 4096 hidden dim), connected via MLP projectors. The key mechanism is 'traversal of rationale': 10 evenly-distributed <tor> tokens segment a lengthy rationale (avg. 213 tokens) into shorter chunks, and Meteor-Mamba is trained to autoregressively generate the rationale text between consecutive <tor> tokens, progressively embedding each chunk and mitigating the autoregressive forgetting problem. Training is two-stage: (1) Meteor-Mamba and projectors are trained on 1.1M question-rationale pairs with the MLM frozen, and (2) all components are jointly fine-tuned on question-answer pairs via QLoRA (rank 64) under 4-bit quantization. The 1.1M question-rationale-answer triples are curated from 2.1M QA pairs across seven datasets, with rationales generated by Claude Haiku and quality-filtered by GPT-4V scoring (threshold ≥5) plus human review. At inference, only the <tor> tokens pass through Meteor-Mamba, and the resulting rationale-embedded features are projected into Meteor-MLM, enabling answer generation without any external API or explicit rationale.

## Results

Meteor-7B achieves state-of-the-art results across all 13 evaluated benchmarks, scoring 82.9 on MMB, 57.3 on MM-Vet, 53.4 on MathVista, 2229 on MME, and 88.3 on SQA-IMG. On the more challenging MMStar benchmark, Meteor-7B (52.8) surpasses the much larger LLaVA-NeXT-34B (52.1) and InternVL1.2-40B (43.7). On MathVerse, Meteor-7B (21.7) outperforms SPHINX-MoE-7B×8 (16.8) and G-LLaVA-7B (16.6). Meteor also outperforms models equipped with additional vision encoders or computer vision models (e.g., OmniFusion, DeepSeek-VL, MoVA, ASMv2, CoLLaVO, MoAI) on POPE (88.7), SQA-IMG (88.3), and MM-Vet (57.3).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Meteor-7B outperforms all compared open-source LLVMs by substantial margins across every benchmark. Against the strongest 7B-class baselines, Meteor-7B leads LLaVA-NeXT-7B by 13.3 points on MMB (82.9 vs 69.6), 13.4 on MM-Vet (57.3 vs 43.9), and 18.8 on MathVista (53.4 vs 34.6). Against larger 13B models, Meteor-7B surpasses LLaVA-NeXT-13B by 12.9 on MMB (82.9 vs 70.0) and 10.0 on MM-Vet (57.3 vs 47.3), and MiniGemini-HD-13B by 14.3 on MMB (82.9 vs 68.6). Against MoE-based models with more total parameters, Meteor-7B beats SPHINX-MoE-7B×8 (56B total) by 11.6 on MMB and 16.4 on MM-Vet, and MM1-MoE-7B×32 (224B total) by 10.2 on MMB and 12.1 on MM-Vet. On the harder MMStar benchmark, Meteor-7B (52.8) edges out LLaVA-NeXT-34B (52.1) and significantly outperforms InternVL1.2-40B (43.7). Notably, Meteor-7B also surpasses models that rely on additional vision encoders or computer vision modules (OmniFusion, DeepSeek-VL, MoVA, ASMv2, CoLLaVO, MoAI) on POPE, SQA-IMG, and MM-Vet, suggesting that embedded multifaceted rationales provide more effective information than enhanced visual perception alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

Meteor builds on the Mamba state-space model [39] as a rationale-embedding module, departing from the dominant paradigm of either generating chain-of-thought rationales at inference time or scaling up model size and adding vision encoders/CV models. Conceptually, it sits at the intersection of rationale-guided prediction (knowledge distillation, CoT) and efficient sequence modeling, introducing 'traversal of rationale'—evenly distributed special <tor> tokens that segment long rationales for progressive embedding by a small 130M Mamba module. Unlike prior LLVMs that rely on larger backbones or auxiliary perception models (e.g., LLaVA-NeXT, InternVL, CoLLaVO, MoAI), Meteor keeps the backbone at 7B (InternLM2) and offloads multifaceted knowledge into the Mamba module, trained in a two-stage procedure with QLoRA.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a 7B model augmented with a 130M Mamba rationale-embedding module can outperform 34B–40B open-source LLVMs across 13+ benchmarks, suggesting that implicit rationale embedding may be a more parameter-efficient path to diverse capabilities than scaling or stacking vision encoders. However, the approach depends on API-generated rationales (Claude Haiku) for training data curation, requires multi-GPU (48GB) resources for training, and shows data overlap on AI2D/ChartQA benchmarks, limiting the strength of those comparisons. Open questions include whether the traversal-of-rationale mechanism generalizes to smaller backbones (1–3B), whether rationale generation can be done without external APIs, and whether the two-stage training protocol is necessary or can be collapsed.
