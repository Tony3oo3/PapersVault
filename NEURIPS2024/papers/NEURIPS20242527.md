## Problem

Existing 3D occupancy prediction methods rely on dense, regular voxel grids that impose uniform resolution across the entire scene, wasting computation on large objects (e.g., buses) while lacking geometric detail for small ones (e.g., traffic cones). Prior attempts to reduce this overhead—2D plane representations, coarse-to-fine upsampling, or depth-gated sparse modeling—sacrifice spatial fidelity or accumulate estimation errors. No prior work has predicted an adaptive octree structure directly from 2D images to allocate variable granularity per semantic region.

## Input / Output

The method consumes N surround-view RGB images (900×1600) along with camera intrinsics and extrinsics, and produces a 3D semantic occupancy volume at 200×200×16 (nuScenes) or 256×256×32 (SemanticKITTI) resolution with C+1 semantic classes. Internally, the network operates on a sparse set of octree leaf queries (depth 3, initial resolution 50×50×4) rather than the full dense grid, and simultaneously predicts binary octree split masks at each level. The sparse octree queries are expanded back to dense form via index arithmetic before the final MLP classification head.

## Method

OctreeOcc replaces dense voxel queries with a sparse, multi-granularity octree query set whose structure is learned end-to-end. A Semantic-Guided Octree Initialization module segments input images with a UNet, projects 3D voxel centers onto the 2D semantic maps, and assigns unbalanced split weights (foreground > buildings/vegetation > roads) to produce a high-quality initial octree mask, avoiding the pattern collapse that plagues direct neural binarization. A top-k selection ratio (20% at level 1, 60% at level 2) determines which voxels split at each level. The octree encoder then refines queries via deformable-attention-based temporal self-attention and image cross-attention, while an Iterative Structure Rectification module partitions the octree into high-confidence (top 10%/30%) and low-confidence regions, uses a 2-layer MLP to re-predict split probabilities for low-confidence areas, and blends new and old predictions to progressively correct structural errors. Training uses a composite loss of focal, Lovász-softmax, Dice, and affinity terms for occupancy plus focal loss for octree structure supervision.

## Results

On Occ3D-nuScenes, OctreeOcc achieves 44.02 mIoU, surpassing FB-OCC (43.41) and PanoOcc (42.13), with notable gains on foreground classes such as car (54.52 vs. 48.55 for FB-OCC) and traffic cone (37.76 vs. 32.33). On SemanticKITTI, OctreeOcc attains the best IoU of 44.71 (vs. VoxFormer's 44.02 and Symphonies' 41.92), though it trails Symphonies on mIoU (13.12 vs. 14.89). In terms of efficiency, OctreeOcc reduces latency to 386 ms and memory to 26.5 GB on Occ3D-nuScenes, a 15–24% reduction compared to dense-grid methods like PanoOcc (502 ms, 35 GB) and FB-OCC (463 ms, 31 GB).

## Baselines & Comparisons

OctreeOcc outperforms all dense-grid baselines on Occ3D-nuScenes mIoU, beating FB-OCC by 0.61 and PanoOcc by 1.89, with the largest margin on traffic cone (+5.43 over FB-OCC) and car (+5.97 over FB-OCC). On SemanticKITTI, OctreeOcc achieves the highest IoU (44.71) among all methods, exceeding VoxFormer (44.02) and Symphonies (41.92), but falls short of Symphonies on mIoU (13.12 vs. 14.89), suggesting the octree structure excels at geometric completeness but slightly underperforms on rare-class semantics. Compared to 2D-query methods (BEVFormer at 23.67 mIoU, TPVFormer at 28.34), OctreeOcc gains 15.7 and 15.7 mIoU respectively, confirming the benefit of 3D spatial modeling. In efficiency, OctreeOcc (386 ms, 26.5 GB) is faster and lighter than PanoOcc (502 ms, 35 GB) and FB-OCC (463 ms, 31 GB), while remaining only slightly slower than TPVFormer (341 ms) and BEVFormer (302 ms), which use much coarser 2D representations. On SemanticKITTI, OctreeOcc also leads in latency (162 ms) and memory (19 GB) versus all 3D-voxel baselines (172–187 ms, 22–23.2 GB).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant dense-voxel paradigm in camera-based 3D occupancy prediction (MonoScene, OccFormer, SurroundOcc, PanoOcc, FB-OCC) by replacing uniform-resolution voxel queries with sparse, multi-granularity octree queries. While octree structures have been widely used in computer graphics for point cloud processing and as decoders (OGN, OctFormer, OcTr), this is the first work to predict octree structure directly from 2D multi-view images and use it to define the query space during encoding. The method also repurposes deformable attention (from Deformable DETR) for temporal self-attention and image cross-attention over the sparse octree queries, and introduces a UNet-based semantic segmentation prior to initialize the octree structure before iterative rectification.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 15–24% reduction in memory and latency while simultaneously achieving state-of-the-art mIoU (44.02 on Occ3D-nuScenes) makes octree-based occupancy prediction a practical option for real-time autonomous driving deployment where compute budgets are tight. A key open question is how well the approach generalizes when the octree ground truth is derived from sparse LiDAR (as acknowledged in the paper's limitations), and whether adaptive per-scene octree depth could further improve the accuracy-efficiency tradeoff. The technique of predicting hierarchical spatial structure from 2D priors could potentially transfer to other sparse 3D prediction tasks beyond occupancy.
