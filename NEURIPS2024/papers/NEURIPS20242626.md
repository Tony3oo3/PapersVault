## Problem

Existing methods for learning from crowdsourced noisy labels estimate the true label posterior via Bayes' rule and use it to weight loss functions or infer labels, but they ignore potential misspecification in the estimated posterior (due to errors in the prior or noise transition probabilities). This misspecification can severely degrade model performance, particularly under high noise rates, because the model overfits to an inaccurate reference distribution. The paper addresses this gap by formulating the learning problem through conditional distributionally robust optimization (CDRO), which explicitly accounts for distributional uncertainty around the estimated posterior.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, variable resolution for LabelMe and Animal-10N) paired with crowdsourced noisy labels from R annotators per instance (R=5 in main experiments, tested up to R=100; in sparse settings only one annotation per instance is used). Beyond the standard class prediction, the method outputs robust pseudo-labels assigned only when the likelihood ratio of the top two posterior probabilities exceeds a threshold C₁, and a pseudo-empirical reference distribution constructed from these high-confidence pseudo-labels. Two parallel classifiers are trained simultaneously, each serving as an informative prior for the other's posterior estimation.

## Method

AdaptCDRP formulates learning from noisy labels as minimizing the worst-case risk over a Wasserstein ambiguity set centered on a reference true-label posterior, then exploits strong duality in finite-dimensional linear programming to derive a closed-form dual expression. The per-data-point optimal solution takes a likelihood ratio form: a pseudo-label is assigned only when the ratio of the top posterior probability to the second-highest exceeds a threshold determined by ε and the loss function, effectively filtering out uncertain instances. These pseudo-labels construct a pseudo-empirical distribution that serves as the robust reference in the CDRO formulation. The paper further derives a closed-form expression for the empirical robust risk as the nominal loss plus a penalty term on the most confident predictions, and identifies the optimal Lagrange multiplier γ* = α^(s*)/κ^p, enabling an efficient one-step update that balances robustness against model fitting without iterative inner-loop optimization.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with instance-dependent noise (R=5), AdaptCDRP achieves 88.09% (IDN-LOW), 87.37% (IDN-MID), and 86.62% (IDN-HIGH) top-1 accuracy, outperforming the best baseline (CoDis) by 2.8–8.0 percentage points. On CIFAR-100, it reaches 60.20%, 56.65%, and 54.24% under the same noise levels, surpassing the strongest competitor (LogitClip) by 2.5–5.2 points. The advantage is most pronounced in high-noise settings, where AdaptCDRP maintains accuracy above 86% on CIFAR-10 while baselines drop below 79%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AdaptCDRP consistently outperforms all 14 baselines across every noise level and dataset in Table 1. On CIFAR-10 IDN-HIGH (50% error rate), it achieves 86.62% accuracy, beating the strongest baseline CoDis (78.67%) by 7.95 points and LogitClip (75.36%) by 11.26 points. On CIFAR-100 IDN-HIGH, it reaches 54.24% versus LogitClip's 49.00% (best baseline), a 5.24-point margin. The gap widens with noise severity: at IDN-LOW the advantage over CoDis is ~2.8 points on CIFAR-10, growing to ~8 points at IDN-HIGH. On real-world datasets (Table 2), the competitive baselines are CoDis (87.23% on CIFAR-10N), LogitClip (51.50% on CIFAR-100N), Max-MIG (83.25% on LabelMe), and Co-teaching (81.96% on Animal-10N); the paper reports AdaptCDRP outperforms all of these, though its exact row is not fully captured in the extracted table. Notably, methods that rely solely on posterior weighting (MBEM, CrowdLayer, TraceReg, Max-MIG, CoNAL) degrade substantially under high noise, confirming the paper's motivation that posterior misspecification is a critical failure mode.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the conditional distributionally robust optimization (CDRO) framework of Shapiro and Pichler (2023) and the Wasserstein-distance-based DRO of Gao and Kleywegt (2023), applying them to the well-studied problem of learning from crowdsourced noisy labels. It departs from the dominant paradigm in noisy-label learning (MBEM, CrowdLayer, TraceReg, Max-MIG, etc.), which estimates the true label posterior via Bayes' rule and uses it directly as a loss weight or for label inference, by instead treating that estimated posterior as a reference distribution and optimizing worst-case risk within a Wasserstein ambiguity set around it. The likelihood-ratio-test-based pseudo-labeling and the closed-form optimal Lagrange multiplier are novel algorithmic contributions that subsume existing argmax-based pseudo-labeling as a special case.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically critical gap: estimated true label posteriors from noisy annotations are often misspecified, and existing methods ignore this uncertainty, degrading performance especially at high noise rates. The CDRO formulation provides principled generalization bounds and a tunable robustness parameter (ε) that balances model fitting against distributional uncertainty, while the likelihood-ratio pseudo-labeling naturally filters out low-confidence instances. Open questions include handling sparse annotation more effectively, improving noise transition matrix estimation beyond simple frequency counting, and extending the framework to settings where the reference distribution itself is poorly estimated. The consistent outperformance across six datasets and fourteen baselines, particularly approaching clean-label accuracy at 50% noise, suggests the robustness framing is a meaningful improvement over point-estimate approaches.
