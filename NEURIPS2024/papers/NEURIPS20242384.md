## Problem

Most RL algorithms assume episodic learning with resets, but real-world systems often cannot be reset, requiring the agent to learn from a single uninterrupted trajectory. Theoretical guarantees for nonepisodic RL have been limited to linear systems (LQR) or finite state spaces, leaving a gap for general nonlinear dynamical systems with continuous state-action spaces. This paper provides the first regret bound for nonepisodic RL with nonlinear dynamics.

## Input / Output

The agent receives state observations x_t ∈ R^{d_x} and executes control actions u_t ∈ R^{d_u} from a single trajectory that is never reset. The dynamics model is updated only every H_n steps (with H_n doubling across artificial episodes), not at every environment step, and the cost function c(x,u) is assumed known a priori. The output is a sequence of actions generated by MPC planning over the optimistic model, along with a theoretical cumulative regret bound of O(β_T √(TΓ_T)) measured against the optimal average cost under known dynamics.

## Method

NEORL maintains a well-calibrated probabilistic model of the unknown dynamics (GP or Bayesian neural network ensemble) and selects policies that are optimistic with respect to the model's epistemic uncertainty. Time is binned into artificial episodes with doubling horizons; at each episode, the algorithm solves min_{π,f∈M_{n-1}∩M_0} A(π,f) to find the policy that achieves the lowest average cost under the most favorable plausible dynamics within the confidence set. In practice, this is implemented via MPC with the iCEM optimizer, where the reparameterization trick from Curi et al. (2020) introduces hallucinated controls η to directly optimize over the confidence set. The theoretical analysis relies on Lyapunov-based bounded energy assumptions to establish ergodicity of the closed-loop system, showing that the optimistic dynamics retain stability properties of the true system, yielding the sublinear regret bound O(β_T √(TΓ_T)) for RKHS dynamics.

## Results

NEORL converges to the optimal average cost A(π*)=0 across all seven environments (Pendulum-v1, MountainCar, CartPole, Reacher, Swimmer, RaceCar, Soft Arm) while achieving sublinear cumulative regret R_T, using only ~10³ environment interactions. The method works consistently with both Gaussian process and probabilistic ensemble (Bayesian neural network) dynamics models. In the CartPole reset experiment, NEORL also requires fewer resets than all baselines while achieving the lowest cumulative regret.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares NEORL against three nonepisodic model-based RL baselines: NEMEAN (greedy mean model, adapted from PETS/Chua et al. 2018), NEPETS (trajectory sampling, adapted from PETS/Chua et al. 2018), and NETS (Thompson sampling, adapted from Osband & Van Roy 2017). NEORL outperforms all three baselines on every environment in terms of both average cost convergence and cumulative regret. NEMEAN performs worst overall, failing to converge to optimal average cost on Pendulum-v1 (for both GP and ensemble models), failing entirely on MountainCar, and being unstable on Reacher and CartPole. NEPETS and NETS both fail to solve the MountainCar task (a sparse-cost environment) and thus do not achieve sublinear regret, while NEORL solves it successfully. In the CartPole reset experiment (Figure 2), NEORL converges fastest, requires fewer resets, and suffers the smallest cumulative regret compared to all three baselines, which all eventually solve the task.

## Relation to Prior Work

NEORL extends the optimism-in-the-face-of-uncertainty paradigm from episodic model-based RL (Kakade et al., 2020; Curi et al., 2020/HUCRL; Sukhija et al., 2024; Treven et al., 2024) to the nonepisodic setting, where the agent learns from a single uninterrupted trajectory without resets. It borrows the doubling-horizon scheduling from Simchowitz & Foster (2020) and the reparameterization trick from Curi et al. (2020), while grounding the analysis in Lyapunov-based stability assumptions and RKHS/GP calibration. The key departure from prior work is that theoretical guarantees for nonepisodic RL were previously limited to linear systems (LQR) or finite state-action spaces (UCRL2, Jaksch et al., 2010); this paper is the first to establish regret bounds for general nonlinear systems with continuous spaces.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first regret bound for nonepisodic RL with general nonlinear dynamics, closing a significant theoretical gap between the well-studied episodic case and the practically critical single-trajectory setting. The bounded-energy (Lyapunov drift) assumption, while strong, is natural for engineered systems and is shown to be satisfied whenever one stabilizing policy exists with bounded inputs. Open questions include deriving matching lower bounds, extending to bias-optimal criteria, and understanding whether the practical modifications (fixed horizon, MPC with iCEM) preserve the theoretical guarantees. The empirical validation, while consistent, is limited to relatively low-dimensional control benchmarks and does not yet demonstrate the approach on high-dimensional or partially observable tasks.
