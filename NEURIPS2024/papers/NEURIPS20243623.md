## Problem

While the implicit bias of gradient descent on separable data (convergence to the ℓ₂-max-margin classifier) is well understood, the analogous question for mirror descent with general convex potentials remained largely open. Previous results only covered L-homogeneous potentials, where the geometry of the potential is scale-invariant; for non-homogeneous potentials, the shape of sublevel sets changes as iterates diverge, making the limiting direction unclear. This paper formally characterises which direction mirror flow selects for a broad class of non-homogeneous potentials.

## Input / Output

The method takes a linearly separable binary dataset {(x_i, y_i)} with x_i ∈ ℝ^d and y_i ∈ {-1,1}, a twice-differentiable strictly convex coercive potential φ: ℝ^d → ℝ, and an exponential-tailed convex loss ℓ. It produces the φ∞-max-margin direction — a unit vector proportional to argmin φ∞(β̄) subject to min_i y_i⟨x_i, β̄⟩ ≥ 1 — where φ∞ is an asymmetric norm (the Minkowski gauge of the limiting normalised sublevel set S∞). For separable potentials φ(β) = Σᵢ φ(βᵢ), φ∞ admits a closed-form expression involving the inverse of the univariate component function.

## Method

The key construction is the horizon function φ∞, defined as the Minkowski gauge of S∞, the Hausdorff limit of the normalised sublevel sets S_c/R_c as c → ∞. This captures the asymptotic geometry of φ 'at infinity' and generalises the role played by the potential itself in the homogeneous case. The proof proceeds by a time rescaling that removes the loss factor, extracting a convergent subsequence of normalised iterates and their associated softmax weights, and then showing via a graphical convergence argument on subdifferentials that the limiting gradient direction lies in the subdifferential of φ∞ at the limiting direction. This yields the optimality conditions of the φ∞-max-margin problem. For separable even potentials, Theorem 3 provides the explicit formula φ∞(β̄) = λ·lim_{η→0} η·φ⁻¹(φ(β̄/η)), which recovers known results (ℓ_p for φ = ‖·‖_p^p, ℓ_∞ for cosh-entropy, ℓ_1 for hyperbolic entropy) and enables direct computation for new potentials.

## Results

The main theoretical result (Theorem 2) establishes that for any separable dataset and any potential φ admitting a non-degenerate horizon shape, the normalised mirror flow iterates converge in direction towards the φ∞-maximum margin classifier, where φ∞ is the asymmetric norm whose unit ball is the limiting shape of φ's normalised sub-level sets. For separable potentials (Theorem 3), φ∞ is computed via the explicit formula φ∞(β̄) = λ·lim_{η→0} η·φ⁻¹(φ(β̄/η)). Numerically, on a toy 2D dataset (80 points, 40 per class), three potentials — quadratic (φ∞ ∝ ‖·‖₂), cosh-entropy (φ∞ ∝ ‖·‖∞), and hyperbolic entropy (φ∞ ∝ ‖·‖₁) — produce three distinct limit directions, all matching the predicted φ∞-max-margin solutions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical paper that does not benchmark against competing methods; instead, it unifies and extends prior implicit-bias results under a single framework. The quadratic potential recovers the ℓ₂-max-margin result of Soudry et al. [2018] for gradient descent on separable data. The L-homogeneous case (e.g., φ = ‖·‖ₚᵖ) recovers the ℓₚ-max-margin result of Sun et al. [2022, 2023], which was previously only understood for this restricted class. The hyperbolic entropy potential recovers the ℓ₁-max-margin result of Lyu & Li [2020] and Moroshko et al. [2020] for diagonal linear networks. The cosh-entropy potential yields a new ℓ∞-max-margin result not previously characterised. The key advance is that the framework applies to any potential with a well-defined horizon shape (e.g., any o-minimal function), going well beyond the homogeneous or separable cases treated in prior work.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the implicit bias analysis of Soudry et al. (2018) for gradient descent (which recovers the ℓ₂-max-margin) and Sun et al. (2022, 2023) for L-homogeneous mirror descent potentials to the general case of arbitrary convex potentials. The key conceptual departure is the introduction of a 'horizon function' φ∞, constructed via Hausdorff convergence of normalized sub-level sets, which captures the potential's asymptotic geometry when the potential is not homogeneous. This bridges a gap explicitly identified in the literature: for non-homogeneous potentials, the directional limit of mirror descent iterates was previously uncharacterized. The construction draws on o-minimal structure theory and variational analysis (Minkowski gauges, epigraphical convergence) to ensure well-definedness for all 'reasonable' potentials encountered in practice.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result provides a unified framework for predicting the implicit bias of mirror descent (and by extension, gradient descent in architectures where mirror structure emerges, such as diagonal linear networks) for any potential, not just homogeneous ones. Key limitations include the requirement of coercive gradients (excluding log-barrier and negative entropy potentials relevant to deep architectures), the purely asymptotic nature of the result (no convergence rates), and the restriction to continuous-time mirror flow rather than discrete mirror descent. Open questions include extending the analysis to non-coercive gradient potentials where the horizon function may not capture the true limiting behavior (as seen in deep linear networks where the ℓ₁ horizon function does not match the actual ℓ_{2/D}-max-margin limit), and characterizing finite-time convergence rates.
