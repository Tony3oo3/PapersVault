## Problem

In many scientific settings, experiments can only perturb treatment variables indirectly through instruments rather than setting them directly, and unobserved confounding combined with nonlinear, multi-variate mechanisms renders even targeted causal queries (e.g., the effect of one gene on a phenotype) unidentified from any fixed set of experiments. No principled adaptive strategy existed for sequentially selecting which indirect experiments to run in order to narrow uncertainty about such a query when full identification of the underlying mechanism is impossible.

## Input / Output

At each round the method consumes n i.i.d. samples (x_i, y_i, z_i) drawn under the current policy π_t, a bounded linear functional Q (e.g., ∂_i f at a base point x*), RBF kernel matrices K_XX and K_ZZ, and regularization hyperparameters (λ_s, λ_c). It outputs a pair of bounds [Q⁻, Q⁺] on the target query, their gap Δ = Q⁺ − Q⁻, and an updated parametric policy π_{t+1} (a Gaussian mixture over instruments) for the next round. The bounds are guaranteed to contain Q[f₀] under the realizability assumption f₀ ∈ F, and they collapse to a point estimate when the query becomes identified through the accumulated experimental data.

## Method

The paper formulates sequential indirect experiment design as a bi-level optimization: an inner minimax problem over RKHS function spaces estimates upper and lower bounds on a linear functional Q[f] among all hypotheses compatible with conditional moment restrictions, and an outer optimization selects the next experiment policy to minimize the gap Δ between those bounds. The key theoretical contribution (Theorem 2) derives a closed-form kernel-based estimator for the bounds by exploiting the linearity of Q over RKHS coefficients, reducing the minimax to a single matrix inversion involving K_XX K_ZZ K_XX plus a regularization term. For the outer loop, the policy is parameterized as a Gaussian mixture model and updated via a REINFORCE-style gradient (log-derivative trick) on Δ, with the gradient estimated empirically by splitting each round's samples into batches. This yields an adaptive strategy that concentrates experimental mass near the region of interest x* while using the intermediate bound estimates as feedback, outperforming non-adaptive random exploration and simple explore-then-exploit heuristics in synthetic confounded nonlinear settings.

## Results

In the primary synthetic setting (d_x = d_z = 2, n = 250 samples/round, T = 16 rounds, 50 seeds), the proposed adaptive strategy essentially identifies the target query Q[f_0] = 20 (the partial derivative ∂_i f at x* = 0) after T = 16 rounds, with the gap Δ between upper and lower bounds collapsing to near zero and low variance across seeds. The locally guided heuristics (explore-then-exploit and alternating explore-exploit) also confidently bound the target away from zero with a relatively narrow gap, while the non-adaptive random baseline starts with poor performance and improves only mildly over rounds. In higher-dimensional settings (d_z = 5, d_x = 20 and d_z = d_x = 20), the adaptive strategy continues to outperform the other methods, with computation time increases remaining manageable.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares four strategies: a non-adaptive random baseline, two locally guided heuristics (explore-then-exploit [EE] and alternating explore-exploit [AEE]), and the proposed targeted adaptive strategy. The adaptive strategy clearly wins, essentially identifying Q[f_0] = 20 with a near-zero gap after 16 rounds and low inter-seed variance. EE and AEE perform competitively with each other, both bounding the target away from zero with a narrow gap, but do not fully collapse the bounds to the true value. The random baseline is consistently the worst performer, with its ultimate accuracy depending primarily on how much mass the meta-distribution Γ places near x*. The paper notes that EE outperforms random (with the same exploration distribution), confirming that the heuristic of focusing on samples near x* provides substantial improvement, consistent with findings in Singh (2023). The adaptive strategy's advantage over EE/AEE comes from using intermediate bound estimates as feedback to update the policy via REINFORCE-style gradient descent on a Gaussian mixture model, rather than relying solely on distance-to-x* heuristics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the sequential indirect experiment design framework of Ailer et al. (2023) from a fully linear, non-adaptive setting to nonlinear, multi-variate, confounded settings with adaptive policy updates. The core estimation machinery builds on the minimax IV regression formulation of Bennett et al. (2023) and the closed-form kernel-based supremum estimator of Zhang et al. (2020), with the RKHS minimax solution of Dikkala et al. (2020) generalized to penalize a linear functional Q[f] rather than the L2 norm. The key conceptual departure is reframing the goal from full identification of f to bounding a targeted query Q[f], and using the resulting bound gap as a differentiable objective for REINFORCE-style policy optimization over Gaussian mixture experiment designs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

In natural sciences, experiments are typically indirect (perturbing but not randomizing treatments), mechanisms are nonlinear and confounded, and scientists care about specific queries (e.g., local causal effects) rather than full mechanism recovery. This paper provides a principled, computationally tractable framework that naturally handles partial identifiability: if a query is identifiable under the allowed experiments, the bounds collapse; if not, they remain informative. Key open questions include provable finite-sample validity of the bounds, extension to nonlinear functionals and more expressive policy classes, and—critically—validation on real experimental systems, which the authors acknowledge as a major gap.
