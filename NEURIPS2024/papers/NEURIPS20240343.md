## Problem

Matrix completion under missing-not-at-random (MNAR) settings—where the probability of observing an entry depends on the possibly unobserved measurement value—is generally ill-posed and has received very little theoretical attention. Existing methods handle either missing-at-random or non-uniform missing (location-dependent probabilities) but remain biased when missingness depends on the measurement itself. This paper tackles a flexible subclass of MNAR characterized by a separable observation probability that can depend on the matrix entries.

## Input / Output

The method takes a partially observed matrix whose entries follow an exponential family distribution (Gaussian, Bernoulli, Poisson, etc.) with a separable observation probability Pr(T_ij=1|Y_ij)=t_ij·s(Y_ij), where t_ij∈(0,1] and s(·) is an unknown non-negative function. The output is the dispersion-scaled matrix φ^{-1}A_* estimated up to a scale-shift equivalence class (A ~ c_1·A + c_2·J), with the constraint ⟨J, A⟩=0 selecting a unique representer. This equivalence still preserves entry rankings within rows/columns, which is the primary quantity of interest in applications like recommender systems.

## Method

The core contribution is a pairwise pseudo-likelihood objective built from local rank statistics of observation pairs. Under the separable missingness assumption, the conditional probability that one observed entry ranks above another eliminates the unknown functions s(·) and t_ij, yielding a clean pairwise term log(1+exp(-(Y_k−Y_k')(A_k−A_k'))). The full objective sums this term over all observed pairs and adds nuclear norm regularization to promote low-rankness, resulting in a convex optimization problem. The theoretical analysis requires a novel concentration inequality for matrix-valued U-statistics, since the pairwise terms are not mutually independent; the authors develop a grouping lemma to decouple dependencies within each group before applying matrix Bernstein inequalities. The estimator achieves a near-minimax convergence rate matching the well-posed (non-informative) setting, up to logarithmic factors.

## Results

On the Yahoo! Webscope Dataset (subset), the proposed pairwise pseudo-likelihood method achieves the lowest TRMSE of 1.12, outperforming SNN (1.27), MFW (1.28), CZ (1.58), and SoftImpute (1.84). On the Coat Shopping Dataset, it achieves TRMSE 1.20, competitive with CZ (1.19) and MFW (1.07) while substantially outperforming SNN (2.06). On the Tobacco Dataset, it achieves TRMSE 14.14 (SE 0.36), second only to SNN (12.09) and ahead of MFW (15.89), SoftImpute (19.20), and CZ (20.45). In simulations with increasing noise variance (σ² from 0 to 1.0), the proposed method shows progressively larger gains over all baselines as informative missingness becomes more severe.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method achieves the best TRMSE on Yahoo! Webscope (1.12 vs. 1.27 for SNN and 1.28 for MFW), the only dataset where it clearly dominates all baselines. On Coat Shopping, MFW is best (1.07) and the proposed method (1.20) is nearly tied with CZ (1.19), while SNN performs worst (2.06) due to its reliance on observed-entry information that introduces bias under informative missingness. On Tobacco, SNN leads (12.09) because the synthetic missingness mechanism (temporal autocorrelation) violates the paper's separability assumption, yet the proposed method (14.14) still substantially outperforms SoftImpute (19.20) and CZ (20.45). In the simulation study, as σ² increases from 0 to 1.0, the gap between the proposed method and all four baselines widens, confirming its advantage specifically under informative (MNAR) missingness. Computationally, the proposed method (8.67s) is faster than SNN (15.23s) but slower than MFW (1.90s), CZ (0.22s), and SoftImpute (0.01s), reflecting the O(n²) pairwise structure of the pseudo-likelihood objective.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the pairwise pseudo-likelihood framework of Liang and Qin (2000) from i.i.d. regression to the non-identical, high-dimensional setting of matrix completion, introducing a separable missingness assumption (Pr(T=1|Y) = t_ij · s(Y)) that allows missingness to depend on the unobserved measurement. It departs from the dominant matrix completion literature, which assumes missing-at-random or non-uniform missingness (location-dependent but value-independent), and from the few prior MNAR matrix completion works (SNN [2], Jin et al. [15]) by not requiring independence between selection and noise given the true matrix, nor additional covariate information. The theoretical analysis introduces a novel concentration framework for matrix-valued U-statistics, which is distinct from the trace-regression or restricted-eigenvalue tools used in standard matrix completion theory.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is one of the first matrix completion methods with provable guarantees under genuinely informative missingness, achieving the minimax-optimal rate (up to logarithmic factors) despite the MNAR complication—a result that is conceptually non-trivial given the ill-posedness of general MNAR. The fundamental scale-shift identifiability limitation (only φ⁻¹A* up to a constant shift and scaling is recoverable) is a clear and important caveat: the method recovers rankings and relative structure but not absolute values, which constrains its applicability. Open questions include scalability (the pairwise objective is O(n²) in observed entries), extension to unbounded observations, and whether the dispersion parameter can be identified without replicates. For practitioners in recommendation systems or any domain where missingness correlates with unobserved values, this provides a principled alternative to ad-hoc debiasing.
