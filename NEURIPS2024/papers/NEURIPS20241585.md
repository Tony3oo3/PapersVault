## Problem

SSLR models trained on unpaired audio-only data produce unified representations across languages and speakers, limiting their effectiveness in multilingual and multispeaker scenarios. Standard adaptation methods (full fine-tuning, Houlsby adapters) require a separate encoder per task, scale linearly with task count, and suffer from catastrophic forgetting when adapting to multiple tasks simultaneously. The paper asks whether a single frozen SSL encoder can be made condition-aware of language and speaker context with minimal parameter overhead while preserving pretrained behavior and generalizing to unseen tasks.

## Input / Output

The system takes raw speech audio waveforms and processes them through a frozen multilingual SSL encoder (Wav2Vec2-XLSR 300M or mHuBERT 100M), simultaneously producing character-level transcriptions via CTC, language identification labels, and speaker embeddings. The conditioning mechanism operates internally by extracting 256-dimensional LID and SV bottleneck embeddings from within the encoder at regular layer intervals, which then modulate subsequent layers. Only the lightweight conditioners (~18–23M parameters) and task-specific decoder projections are trainable, while all SSL encoder weights remain frozen throughout adaptation.

## Method

CA-SSLR inserts Time-Channel Attention Conditioners (TCACs) after each attention module in the SSL encoder, computing per-time-step, per-channel scaling and bias parameters from intermediate LID/SV conditioning embeddings via FiLM-style linear modulation combined with additive attention over the time dimension. The hierarchical self-conditioning design re-estimates the conditioning feature every three layers for LID and every six layers for SV, progressively refining representations as the signal propagates deeper through the network. A key architectural choice is channel-wise modulation without cross-channel mixing, which prevents new couplings and mitigates overfitting and catastrophic forgetting. The initialization strategy sets α=1, γ=1, β=0 so the conditioned model starts as an exact identity of the pretrained encoder, enabling incremental addition of new conditioning tasks without disrupting previously learned behavior. Multiple conditioning sources are combined multiplicatively for scales and additively for biases, ensuring each task's contribution remains independent and the total trainable parameter count stays low.

## Results

On the ML-SUPERB 1-hour benchmark, CA-XLSR^{L,S}(TCAC) achieves 14.4% ASR CER (vs. 22.7% frozen XLSR baseline, a 36.6% relative reduction), 93.5% LID accuracy, and 1.01% SV EER on VoxCeleb1 (vs. 1.29% baseline, a 21.7% relative reduction). On the 10-minute setting, the same model reaches 18.3% CER, 89.0% LID accuracy, and 1.11% EER. Full fine-tuning of the SSL encoder actually degrades all three tasks simultaneously (e.g., XLSR+FT: 35.6% CER, 81.5% LID, 7.23% EER), while CA-SSLR improves every metric with only ~18–23M trainable parameters versus 200–300M for full fine-tuning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Houlsby adapters, CA-SSLR wins decisively on generalization: when adapted for LID, LID-Houlsby improves unseen ASR CER by only 5.4%/3.9% (Normal/Few-shot), while LID-CA-XLSR achieves 7.3%/6.6%; when adapted for ASR, ASR-Houlsby degrades SV EER from 1.29% to 1.37%, whereas ASR-CA-XLSR improves it to 1.15%. Against LoRA, the gap is stark—LoRA yields only 0.1% CER improvement over the mHuBERT baseline (38.3% vs. 38.2%), while CA-mHuBERT^{L,S} reaches 30.9% CER with comparable parameter counts (15.9M vs. 15.2M). Against full fine-tuning, CA-SSLR is the clear winner: fine-tuning the XLSR encoder for all three tasks simultaneously causes catastrophic degradation (CER worsens from 29.0% to 35.6%, EER from 1.29% to 7.23%), while CA-SSLR improves all metrics. Against the larger MMS-1b model (1B params), CA-XLSR^{L}(TCAC, 3L) achieves lower Normal CER (18.6% vs. 21.3%) but slightly higher Few-shot CER (31.6% vs. 30.2%), demonstrating competitive performance at 1/3 the parameter count. The frozen XLSR and mHuBERT baselines serve as lower bounds that CA-SSLR consistently surpasses across all three tasks without any task-specific encoder retraining.

## Relation to Prior Work

CA-SSLR draws on the FiLM (feature-wise linear modulation) paradigm from vision and the conditioning strategies of ControlNet/IP-Adaptor in image generation and CTRL in NLP, repurposing them for speech SSL encoders. It departs from the dominant adaptation paradigm (full fine-tuning, Houlsby adapters, LoRA) by keeping the SSL backbone entirely frozen and instead injecting lightweight time-channel attention conditioners (TCAC) that modulate hidden states via scale and bias derived from intermediate LID/SV decoder embeddings. The hierarchical self-conditioning design—re-estimating conditioning features every few layers—extends earlier hierarchical CTC work (Sanabria & Metze; Chen et al.) from decoder-side token conditioning to encoder-side representation modulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical contribution is a ~10× reduction in trainable parameters relative to full fine-tuning while matching or exceeding its ASR accuracy, and—critically—preserving and even improving performance on unseen tasks (e.g., LID-adapted encoder improves SV EER), a property that standard adapters and full fine-tuning lack. This positions CA-SSLR as a viable path toward a single generalist speech encoder for multilingual, multi-speaker deployments without the linear compute cost of per-task encoders. Open questions include whether the hierarchical conditioning generalizes beyond LID/SV to other attributes (emotion, channel), whether the 13–34% RTF overhead is acceptable for streaming, and whether the approach scales to larger SSL backbones (e.g., 1B+ parameter models) where the frozen-encoder assumption may interact differently with conditioning.
