## Problem

Existing hairstyle transfer methods face a fundamental speed-quality tradeoff: optimization-based approaches (Barbershop, StyleYourHair) produce high-quality results but require minutes to hours per image, while encoder-based methods (HairCLIP, CtrlHair) run in under a second but suffer from poor reconstruction fidelity and low resolution. Additionally, both families struggle when the source and reference images have large pose differences, either ignoring pose entirely or handling it with inefficient post-processing. HairFastGAN aims to close this gap by achieving optimization-level realism at near real-time speed while robustly handling pose mismatches.

## Input / Output

The method accepts three 1024×1024 RGB images: a source face photo, a reference image providing the desired hair shape, and a reference image providing the desired hair color. Shape and color can be transferred independently or jointly, and the pipeline runs in under 1 second on an Nvidia V100 (0.78 s) or 0.41 s on an A100. The output is a single 1024×1024 edited face image with the transferred hairstyle, preserving the original identity, background, and fine details such as piercings and makeup.

## Method

HairFastGAN decomposes hair transfer into four encoder-only modules operating in StyleGAN's FS latent space. First, a Pose alignment module uses a novel Rotate Encoder to modify the first six W+ vectors of the shape reference so its pose matches the source, then applies CtrlHair's Shape Encoder/Adaptor to produce a pose-aligned hair mask. Second, Shape alignment edits only the F tensor (32×32) by mixing FS-encoder and E4E representations (95/5 blend for editability), generating inpainted hair regions via SEAN, and compositing four F tensors using segmentation masks. Third, Color alignment predicts a delta to the S space using a 1D modulation encoder conditioned on CLIP embeddings of the face and hair regions, trained with cosine-distance CLIP losses rather than LPIPS. Finally, a Refinement alignment module encodes both the blended and source images with a 64-resolution FS encoder, fuses the F tensors through IResNet blocks and the S vectors through a second modulation encoder, and generates the final image—restoring identity details lost during earlier inversion steps without requiring any per-image optimization.

## Results

On CelebA-HQ, HairFast achieves the best FID of 13.12 (full transfer) and FID_CLIP of 5.12, outperforming all optimization-based and encoder-based baselines. For reconstruction (transferring hair from an image to itself), HairFast attains the lowest LPIPS of 0.08 and highest PSNR of 23.45 dB. Inference runs in 0.78 s on an Nvidia V100 (0.41 s on A100), making it ~800× faster than Barbershop (645 s) and ~10× faster than CtrlHair (7.87 s), while only HairCLIP (0.36 s) is faster but with substantially worse quality (FID 34.95).

## Baselines & Comparisons

HairFast outperforms all five directly comparable baselines on every quality metric reported in Table 1. Against the strongest optimization-based method, Barbershop, HairFast improves FID from 15.94 to 13.12 and FID_CLIP from 7.07 to 5.12 while being ~830× faster (0.78 s vs. 645 s on V100). Versus the best encoder-based competitor, CtrlHair, HairFast reduces FID_CLIP from 9.52 to 5.12 and LPIPS from 0.15 to 0.08, and is ~10× faster. HairCLIP is the only method faster than HairFast (0.36 s vs. 0.78 s) but trails by a wide margin on all quality metrics (FID 34.95 vs. 13.12; PSNR 14.08 vs. 23.45). On pose-difference sub-metrics (medium/hard), HairFast also leads all methods, e.g. FID_CLIP of 8.90/10.33 versus Barbershop's 11.27/13.30. On the StyleGAN-Salon benchmark (Table 8), HairFast further outperforms StyleGAN-Salon itself (FID_CLIP 8.45 vs. 9.91) and LOHO (15.12), confirming gains over methods that rely on PTI for detail preservation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two established lines of work on StyleGAN-based hair editing: optimization-based methods (Barbershop, StyleYourHair, HairNet, HairNeRF, StyleGANSalon) that achieve high quality but require minutes-to-hours of per-image optimization, and encoder-based methods (CtrlHair, HairCLIP, HairCLIPv2) that are fast but operate in lower-quality latent spaces (W+ or low-resolution generators). HairFast departs from both by operating in the FS latent space of StyleGAN (inheriting Barbershop's structural decomposition) while replacing all optimization stages with trained encoders, and introduces a novel FS/W+ mixing strategy to enable color editing within the F tensor. The Rotate Encoder for pose alignment generalizes CtrlHair's Shape Adaptor by explicitly learning pose rotation in W+ space, and the Refinement module at 64×64 resolution replaces the PTI and Poisson blending used by prior methods for detail preservation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: near real-time hair transfer (0.78 s on V100) with quality matching or exceeding optimization-based methods opens the door to interactive virtual try-on applications that were previously infeasible. The finding that a single-pass 64×64 FS encoder can outperform PTI-based detail preservation challenges the assumption that iterative latent optimization is necessary for high-fidelity reconstruction. Remaining limitations include failure on complex hair textures (braids, ponytails), long-to-short hair transitions, and illumination mismatches, and the authors note that the architecture naturally extends to text-based color editing and slider-based shape control as future work.
