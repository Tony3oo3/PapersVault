## Problem

Hybrid CNN-Transformer architectures have largely focused on designing efficient attention alternatives (linear, axial, hierarchical) while leaving the macro-level distribution of convolutional versus transformer blocks across network stages largely unexplored. This paper asks whether the current symmetric or alternating arrangements of C and T blocks are optimal, and shows that the asymmetric placement of blocks—more convolutions early where spatial resolution is high, more transformers later where global context matters—yields a superior latency-performance trade-off without requiring any modified attention mechanism.

## Input / Output

For text-to-image generation, the model consumes a text prompt encoded by a frozen Flan-T5-XXL encoder (4096-dim embeddings) and Gaussian noise, producing images at 256×256, 512×512, 1024×1024, or arbitrary multi-aspect-ratio resolutions via a DDPM sampling process in the 4-channel SDXL VAE latent space. For image classification, it takes 224×224 RGB images and outputs 1000-class (ImageNet-1K) or 21K-class labels. For semantic segmentation, 512×512 inputs yield 150-class pixel-level masks. The class-conditional generation variant accepts a class index and produces 256×256 or 512×512 images using an 8-channel VAE latent space.

## Method

AsCAN arranges FusedMBConv blocks (full 3×3 convolutions with 4× channel expansion, SE gating, and 1×1 projection) and vanilla transformer blocks (parallel self-attention + MLP with layer norm) in an asymmetric pattern: the first stage is entirely convolutional, the second stage is 75% C / 25% T, the third is 50/50, and the fourth is 25% C / 75% T, with C always preceding T within each stage. For text-to-image generation, this asymmetric backbone is wrapped in a UNet with skip connections, where T blocks incorporate cross-attention to text embeddings and RoPE positional encodings, and C blocks receive sinusoidal time-step embeddings; RMSNorm on query-key projections stabilizes bfloat16 training. A multi-stage training pipeline first pre-trains the full T2I architecture on ImageNet-1K (256×256, using 'a photo of a <class>' prompts) and then fine-tunes on a large-scale curated dataset through four resolution-increasing phases (256→512→1024→multi-aspect-ratio), reducing large-scale training cost while improving final FID. The key insight is that optimizing the macro architecture—rather than replacing quadratic attention with approximations—enables the use of vanilla attention while still achieving faster inference than models with efficient-attention variants.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K classification, AsCAN-T (55M params) achieves 83.44% top-1 accuracy with 4,295 images/s throughput on A100 (B=64), surpassing MaxViT-T (83.62%, 2,756 img/s) and FasterViT-1 (83.2%, 4,106 img/s) in the accuracy-throughput trade-off. For text-to-image generation, the model scores 0.64 overall on GenEval, outperforming SDXL (0.55) and PixArt-α (0.48), and achieves a validation FID of 15.45 on Set-B-10K versus 35.86 for SDXL and 46.03 for PixArt-α. On class-conditional ImageNet-1K generation (256×256), the asymmetric UNet reaches FID 2.41 with only 52G FLOPs, compared to DiT-XL/2-G's 2.27 at 118G FLOPs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On ImageNet-1K classification, AsCAN-T achieves 83.44% top-1 with 4,295 img/s on A100 (B=64), beating MaxViT-T (83.62%, 2,756 img/s) in throughput by ~1.6× at nearly identical accuracy, and outperforming FasterViT-1 (83.2%, 4,106 img/s) on both accuracy and speed. AsCAN-B (84.73%) matches MOAT-2 (84.7%) in accuracy while delivering 2,393 vs. 2,087 img/s, and AsCAN-L (85.24%) surpasses MaxViT-L (85.17%) with 1,617 vs. 759 img/s and fewer parameters (173M vs. 212M). For T2I generation, the model's GenEval overall score of 0.64 decisively beats SDXL (0.55), PixArt-α (0.48), and PixArt-Σ (0.53), with particularly large margins on position (0.28 vs. 0.15) and color attribution (0.48 vs. 0.23). On the internal validation FID (Set-B-10K), the model achieves 15.45, substantially outperforming SDXL (35.86), PixArt-Σ (40.01), and PixArt-α (46.03). For class-conditional generation, the model's FID of 2.41 at 52G FLOPs is slightly behind DiT-XL/2-G (2.27 at 118G FLOPs) but uses less than half the compute, and beats U-ViT-L/2 (3.40 at 77G FLOPs) on both FID and efficiency.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the hybrid CNN-Transformer architecture line (CoAtNet, MaxViT, FasterViT) but deliberately shifts focus from designing novel attention mechanisms to optimizing the macro-level distribution of convolutional and transformer blocks across stages. For the generation task, it follows the latent diffusion paradigm established by LDM and SDXL, adopting their VAE and UNet structure while introducing an asymmetric block arrangement and a multi-stage training pipeline (ImageNet-1K T2I pre-training → large-scale fine-tuning) that differs from PixArt-α's class-conditional-to-T2I transfer. The key conceptual departure is the argument that vanilla quadratic attention, paired with the right asymmetric C/T distribution, outperforms architectures relying on efficient attention approximations (multi-axial, hierarchical) in real throughput-performance trade-offs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper's central insight—that macro-architecture design (where to place convolutions vs. transformers) matters more than micro-level attention innovations for practical efficiency—challenges the field's prevailing focus on designing faster attention kernels. This has broad implications for architecture design in both recognition and generation, suggesting practitioners can use well-optimized vanilla attention (e.g., FlashAttention) without sacrificing throughput. Open questions include whether the asymmetric principle generalizes to video generation or 3D tasks, whether incorporating efficient attention on top of the asymmetric design yields further gains (acknowledged as future work), and whether the multi-stage T2I training pipeline can be extended to other generative modalities. Limitations noted include residual artifacts at extreme aspect ratios and occasional extra-limb generations.
