## Problem

3D Gaussian Splatting relies on carefully engineered cloning, splitting, and pruning heuristics to place Gaussians, which requires good initialization, multiple tuned hyperparameters, and can produce sub-optimal renderings or wasted compute. The number of Gaussians is difficult to predict or control in advance, and the heuristic-based approach does not generalize well across scenes.

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view RGB images with known camera poses and optionally an SfM point cloud for initialization; for random initialization, 100k Gaussians are uniformly sampled within 3× the camera bounding box. The output is a 3D Gaussian Splat representation with a user-controllable maximum number of Gaussians (e.g., 300k–1.5M), rendered via differentiable α-blending rasterization at real-time rates (~130 fps at 1080p). Indoor MipNeRF 360 scenes are downsampled by 2× and outdoor by 4× for compatibility with prior work.

## Method

The paper reformulates 3DGS training as Markov Chain Monte Carlo sampling via Stochastic Gradient Langevin Dynamics, adding an anisotropic noise term to the standard gradient update that is scaled by each Gaussian's covariance and modulated by a sigmoid of its opacity (high-opacity Gaussians receive less noise). The conventional densification and pruning heuristics are replaced by a deterministic relocation move: dead Gaussians (opacity < 0.005) are teleported to the positions of live Gaussians, with their opacity and covariance analytically adjusted so that the composed rendering is preserved (probability invariance), avoiding the extent-broadening artifact of naive cloning. An L1 regularizer on both opacity and covariance eigenvalues encourages unused Gaussians to shrink and disappear, enabling gradual growth of the live Gaussian count by 5% per step up to a user-specified maximum. This combination eliminates the need for careful initialization and provides robust, high-quality reconstructions regardless of whether Gaussians start from random positions or SfM points.

## Results

On MipNeRF 360, the method achieves 29.89 PSNR / 0.90 SSIM / 0.19 LPIPS with SfM initialization, outperforming 3DGS (29.30/0.88/0.21) and MipNeRF360 (29.23/0.84/0.21). On Tank & Temples, it reaches 24.29 PSNR / 0.86 SSIM / 0.19 LPIPS (SfM), a +0.62 PSNR gain over 3DGS (23.67/0.84/0.22) and +2.07 over MipNeRF360 (22.22/0.76/0.26). With random initialization, the gap widens: 29.72 PSNR on MipNeRF 360 vs. 27.89 for 3DGS, and 24.21 on Tank & Temples vs. 21.93. On the large-scene OMMO dataset, the method achieves 29.52 PSNR (SfM) vs. 28.83 for 3DGS, demonstrating consistent gains across all five evaluated benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary baseline 3DGS [19], the method wins on every dataset under both random and SfM initialization, with the largest PSNR gains on Tank & Temples (+2.28 with random init, +0.62 with SfM) and MipNeRF 360 (+1.83 random, +0.59 SfM). Compared to the revised densification variant 3DGS→[4] (Bulò et al.), the method achieves +1.03 PSNR on MipNeRF 360 (29.72 vs. 28.69) and +1.07 on Tank & Temples (24.21 vs. 23.14) with random initialization. Against NeRF-based methods, the method surpasses MipNeRF360 [2] by +0.66 PSNR on MipNeRF 360 (29.89 vs. 29.23) and by +2.07 on Tank & Temples (24.29 vs. 22.22), and outperforms INGP-Big [29] by +3.07 PSNR on MipNeRF 360. On Deep Blending, gains over 3DGS are modest (+0.07 to +0.17 PSNR), consistent with the dataset's simpler geometry. A notable finding is that the method's performance is nearly identical under random vs. SfM initialization (e.g., 29.72 vs. 29.89 on MipNeRF 360), whereas 3DGS shows a 1.41 PSNR gap, confirming the robustness benefit of the MCMC exploration.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reframes 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) training as Markov Chain Monte Carlo sampling via Stochastic Gradient Langevin Dynamics, revealing that the standard 3DGS gradient update is SGLD minus its noise term. It departs from the heuristic-based densification, pruning, and opacity-resetting strategies in the original 3DGS by replacing them with a principled relocation (cloning) strategy that approximately preserves sample probability, and from concurrent work by Bulò et al. that only partially corrects cloning. The SGLD connection also links to prior work by Kheradmand et al. that used SGLD for NeRF training acceleration, but here the application is fundamentally different: the Gaussians themselves are the MCMC samples rather than a mining criterion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the need for carefully tuned densification heuristics and reducing dependence on SfM initialization, this work makes 3DGS more robust and easier to deploy in practice, while the L1 regularizer on opacity and scale gives practitioners direct control over the number of Gaussians and memory budget. The method's robustness to initialization (1× vs 3× camera extent) is practically significant for real-world scenes where SfM may be incomplete. Open questions include whether the MCMC framing extends naturally to dynamic scenes, whether the approximate probability preservation in the relocation step can be made exact, and whether the approach is compatible with anti-aliasing and reflection extensions that the authors note are still needed.
