## Problem

In-context learning performance is highly sensitive to which demonstrations are selected, yet existing retrieval methods face two critical limitations: the search space grows exponentially with the number of demonstrations to retrieve, and training with randomly sampled demonstration sets assigns uniform scores to demonstrations that contribute unequally to model predictions, causing the retriever to prefer less helpful examples.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text query and a demonstration pool of input-output pairs, partitioning the pool into C semantically coherent clusters via K-means on Sentence-BERT embeddings. At inference, the number of demonstrations drawn from each expert is proportional to the cosine similarity between the query embedding and that expert's centroid, so irrelevant experts contribute zero demonstrations. The final output is a union of L demonstrations fed to a frozen LLM (e.g., GPT-Neo 2.7B) alongside the query to produce a prediction.

## Method

MoD partitions the demonstration pool into C clusters (experts) via K-means on Sentence-BERT embeddings, then trains an independent BERT-based scorer for each expert using contrastive learning. The key training innovation is an expert-wise strategy inspired by coordinate descent: when optimizing the score of one candidate demonstration, the remaining L−1 demonstrations are fixed as the optimal set retrieved from all experts, ensuring the supervision signal reflects the marginal contribution of the candidate in a near-optimal context. Supervision scores are obtained by querying the LLM for the log-probability of the correct answer conditioned on the L−1 fixed demonstrations plus the candidate. At inference, each expert retrieves its allocated number of top-scored demonstrations, and the union across experts forms the final demonstration set, reducing the search space from |D|^L to per-expert sub-problems while preserving diversity across the full pool.

## Results

MoD achieves an average score of 58.63% across 12 NLP datasets (6 classification, 6 generation), outperforming the strongest baseline CEIL (56.76%) by +1.87 points on average. The largest gains over CEIL appear on CommonsenseQA (+6.06) and NL2Bash (+3.03), while classification tasks show a higher average gain (+2.10) than generation tasks (+1.64). On compositional parsing splits (GeoQuery, SMCalFlow-CS), MoD further extends its lead, e.g., 77.38 vs. 73.21 on GeoQuery Standard and 62.95 vs. 60.27 on SMCalFlow-CS single-domain.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MoD outperforms all six baselines on every one of the 12 datasets. Against the strongest learning-based baseline CEIL (DPP-based compositional exemplar selection), MoD gains +1.87 on average, with the most substantial improvements on CommonsenseQA (+6.06, 43.24 vs. 37.18) and NL2Bash (+3.03, 62.94 vs. 59.91), suggesting the multi-expert design is especially beneficial when diverse knowledge is needed. Against EPR (singleton example scorer), MoD leads by +5.26 on average, confirming that jointly modeling demonstration interactions and partitioning the search space both matter. All four learning-free baselines (TopK-BM25, TopK-Contriver, TopK-SimCSE, TopK-BERT) cluster around 45–46% average, roughly 13 points below MoD, highlighting the gap between heuristic similarity retrieval and learned, interaction-aware selection. On the compositional parsing splits (Table 3), MoD extends its lead over CEIL to +4.17 on GeoQuery Standard and +2.68 on SMCalFlow-CS single-domain, where retrieving a diverse set of all-important demonstrations is critical.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the ICL demonstration selection line of work (EPR, CEIL) by introducing a Mixture-of-Experts partitioning of the demonstration pool, drawing on the MoE paradigm from Jacobs et al. and Wang et al.'s prompt-optimization MoE. The key departure from CEIL is replacing its KNN-based candidate narrowing (which limits exploration) with K-means clustering that guarantees full pool coverage across experts, and from EPR by introducing an expert-wise training strategy inspired by coordinate descent that fixes co-selected demonstrations while optimizing one candidate at a time. The contrastive learning objective for each expert's scorer follows the EPR/CEIL formulation but operates in a few-shot context where L-1 demonstrations are held fixed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MoD addresses two structural limitations in ICL demonstration retrieval—exponential search space and noisy optimization from unhelpful demonstrations—that have constrained prior learning-based methods. The coordinate-descent analogy provides a clean, interpretable training procedure, and the MoE partitioning enables retrieval of semantically diverse yet jointly useful demonstrations. However, the framework still requires labeled demonstrations for supervision, and its performance is sensitive to the quality of the K-means expert assignment. Open questions include whether the approach extends to unlabeled or weakly-labeled pools, whether the clustering strategy can be learned end-to-end, and how well the method transfers across fundamentally different task structures (e.g., NLI vs. single-input tasks).
