## Problem

Existing link prediction methods—both heuristic and GNN4LP—apply a uniform strategy to all node pairs, yet the paper demonstrates that different node pairs within the same dataset require distinct types of pairwise information for accurate prediction. No prior method adaptively routes each node pair to the most suitable predictor, leaving complementary strengths of individual models underutilized.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240511/cropped_page2_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

Given a graph with adjacency matrix and node features, the method predicts the probability of an edge between a target node pair. The gating model consumes two types of pairwise inputs: a low-dimensional vector of structural heuristics (CN, AA, RA, Shortest Path, Katz, PPR) and a high-dimensional element-wise product of the two nodes' feature vectors. The output is a single scalar link prediction score obtained by sigmoid of the weighted sum of all expert predictions.

## Method

Link-MoE is a mixture-of-experts framework that treats multiple pre-trained GNN4LP models (NCN, NCNC, Neo-GNN, BUDDY, SEAL, NBFNet, PEG, GCN, MLP, Node2Vec) as experts. A two-branch gating MLP—one branch encoding structural heuristics, the other encoding feature proximity—produces softmax weights over experts for each node pair, addressing the large dimensionality gap between the two input types. Training follows a two-step strategy: each expert is first trained independently with its own optimal hyperparameters, then only the lightweight gating model is trained via cross-entropy on the validation set, avoiding the expert-collapse problem of end-to-end MoE training. The final prediction is the sigmoid of the weighted sum of all expert scores, enabling flexible addition of new experts without retraining existing ones.

## Results

Link-MoE achieves the best performance across all six main benchmark datasets. Headline improvements include a 18.71% relative gain in MRR on Pubmed (53.10 vs. 44.73 for NBFNet), a 9.59% relative gain in Hits@100 on ogbl-ppa (69.39 vs. 63.32 for LPFormer), and a 10.80% relative gain in MRR on Cora (44.03 vs. 39.74 for Mean-Ensemble). On the large-scale ogbl-collab dataset, Link-MoE reaches 71.32 Hits@50, surpassing the previous best (LPFormer at 68.14) by 4.67%. On heterophilic graphs, Link-MoE also leads with 41.20 MRR on Chameleon and 31.98 MRR on Squirrel.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Link-MoE outperforms all 20 baselines across all six main datasets. Against the strongest single GNN4LP method, LPFormer (KDD 2024), Link-MoE wins on every dataset: 44.03 vs. 39.42 MRR on Cora, 67.49 vs. 65.42 on Citeseer, 53.10 vs. 40.17 on Pubmed, 71.32 vs. 68.14 Hits@50 on ogbl-collab, 69.39 vs. 63.32 Hits@100 on ogbl-ppa, and 91.25 vs. 89.81 MRR on ogbl-citation2. Against the best individual GNN4LP experts (NCN, NCNC, Neo-GNN, BUDDY, SEAL, NBFNet, PEG), Link-MoE consistently leads, with the largest gap on Pubmed where NBFNet (44.73 MRR) is the strongest single expert but Link-MoE reaches 53.10. The two ensemble baselines (Mean-Ensemble and Global-Ensemble) that use the same set of experts but apply uniform or globally-learned weights underperform Link-MoE on all datasets, confirming that the per-pair gating mechanism is critical. Against traditional heuristics (CN, AA, RA, Shortest Path, Katz) and embedding methods (Node2Vec, MF, MLP), Link-MoE achieves substantially higher scores, e.g., 53.10 vs. 21.44 (Katz) on Pubmed MRR. On heterophilic graphs (Chameleon, Squirrel), Link-MoE also leads all baselines by a wide margin (41.20 vs. 35.31 on Chameleon; 31.98 vs. 26.97 on Squirrel). Two prior ensemble methods from the literature (Ghasemian et al. 2020, Chen et al. 2022) are also outperformed, with Link-MoE achieving 71.32 vs. 69.65 Hits@50 on ogbl-collab and 53.10 vs. 43.92 MRR on Pubmed.

## Relation to Prior Work

Link-MoE carves new ground by repurposing the Mixture of Experts architecture—previously applied to NLP, vision, and node-level GNN tasks (GMoE, GraphMETRO)—for the first time in link prediction. Rather than proposing a new GNN4LP model, it treats existing link predictors (NCN, NCNC, Neo-GNN, BUDDY, SEAL, NBFNet, PEG, MLP, GCN, Node2Vec) as frozen experts and introduces a heuristic-driven gating network that adaptively routes each node pair to the most suitable expert. This departs from both the one-size-fits-all paradigm of individual GNN4LP methods and from prior ensemble approaches for link prediction (stacking, bagging) that combine predictions without conditioning on pairwise structural features. The two-step training strategy (train experts independently, then train the lightweight gating MLP) is a deliberate departure from end-to-end MoE training, motivated by the collapse problem observed in their ablations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides strong empirical evidence that no single link predictor dominates across all node-pair regimes, and that adaptive per-pair routing yields substantial gains (up to 18.71% relative MRR improvement on Pubmed). This opens a practical path for practitioners to combine their best-performing models without retraining them, and the gating-weight analysis offers interpretable insight into when each model is most useful. Key limitations include the time cost of training multiple pre-trained experts, the reliance on a fixed set of structural heuristics that may not generalize to all domains, and the use of a small validation subset for gating training. Open questions include whether end-to-end MoE training for link prediction can be stabilized, whether learned (rather than hand-crafted) heuristics could improve gating, and how the framework scales as the expert pool grows.
