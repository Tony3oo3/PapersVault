## Problem

Existing promptable medical segmentation methods rely on either textual or visual prompts alone, each with critical weaknesses: textual prompts suffer from data scarcity under long-tailed category distributions and misalignment with complex visual patterns, while visual prompts cannot convey the general concept of objects with high intra-class variability (e.g., tumors across cancer stages). No prior work coordinates both prompt modalities within a unified, fully automatic framework for multi-organ and tumor segmentation.

## Input / Output

The model takes a 3D CT volume (cropped into 96×96×96 patches), a set of 3D cropped volumes serving as anatomical prompts (one per category, unified to a fixed size), and long textual descriptions enriched with medical domain knowledge (generated via GPT-4 and verified by a physician) as textual prompts. It produces N binary segmentation masks (23 organs + 6 anomalies) via dot-product between decoded segmentation queries and a high-resolution pixel embedding map. The system is fully automatic at inference—no human interaction is required to supply prompts, as anatomical prompts are pre-cropped from public dataset annotations and textual prompts are fixed per category.

## Method

CAT adopts a query-based encoder-decoder architecture (Swin UNETR backbone, Clinical-BERT text encoder) with two parallel prompt encoders that project anatomical and textual prompts into prompt queries. A ShareRefiner module refines all queries via cross-attention over multi-scale backbone features, but applies a hard Gumbel-Softmax assignment (with straight-through gradients) exclusively to anatomical queries to enforce non-overlapping spatial attention, while segmentation and textual queries use soft assignment—this disentangles the two prompt types. A PromptRefer module then performs masked cross-attention where each segmentation query interacts only with its corresponding group of anatomical and textual prompt queries, guided by predefined attention masks. Query-level InfoNCE contrastive losses (segmentation-to-prompt and prompt-to-prompt) further align the two modalities and push segmentation queries toward their referenced prompts. Training combines dice loss, cross-entropy classification, and the two contrastive terms, with anatomical prompts randomly sampled per category (excluding the current instance) to reduce domain shift.

## Results

On FLARE22 organ segmentation, CAT achieves 86.8% average DSC across 13 abdominal organs, surpassing the best textual-prompted baselines (Universal† at 84.7%, ZePT* at 82.8%) and all SAM-based methods (ranging from 53.8% to 72.2%). On tumor segmentation, CAT achieves 72.73% DSC on liver tumors, 49.67% on pancreas tumors, 70.11% on hepatic vessel tumors, and 48.31% on colon tumors (MSD), and 53.35% average DSC on the in-house colon tumor dataset spanning Stages T1–T4. Notably, CAT outperforms SegVol by 7% DSC on Stage-IV (T4) colon tumors, where tumors invade adjacent organs and exhibit the greatest shape/density variability.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CAT outperforms all SAM-based visual-prompted methods by large margins: 21% DSC over MedSAM on adrenal glands, 30% over SAM-Med3D, and 20% over SegVol in average organ DSC. Among textual-prompted methods, CAT leads Universal† [13] by 2.1% and ZePT* [12] by 4.0% in average organ DSC, with particularly notable gains on pancreas (+4.9% over Universal†) and esophagus (+3.3% over ZePT*). The closest competitor is CT-SAM3D [41] (88.4% avg), which uses multi-round spatially aligned point prompts; CAT achieves 86.8% with a single anatomical prompt per category, a more practical setting. On tumor segmentation (MSD), CAT surpasses nnUNet [62] and Swin UNETR [48] by at least 4% DSC across all four tumor types, and beats SegVol [39] by 5% on pancreas tumors without using any ground-truth-derived point prompts. On the in-house colon tumor dataset, CAT leads SegVol by 3.1% average DSC and by 7.5% on Stage-IV (T4) tumors, where visual-prompt-only methods struggle with organ invasion and density variation.

## Relation to Prior Work

CAT extends the query-based segmentation architecture of Mask2Former [42] and the textual-prompted medical segmentation line initiated by ZePT [12], which already incorporated GPT-4-generated medical domain knowledge into text prompts. The key departure is the introduction of anatomical prompts—3D cropped volumes from other CT scans—as a complementary visual signal, moving beyond the single-modality prompting paradigm of both SAM-based methods [22, 39, 40, 41] and purely textual approaches [13, 12]. Architecturally, the ShareRefiner (using Gumbel-Softmax hard assignment for anatomical queries) and PromptRefer (with structured attention masks) are novel mechanisms designed to disentangle and coordinate the two prompt types within a unified query framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that anatomical and textual prompts are genuinely complementary for medical segmentation—neither alone suffices for the full range of organs and tumor stages—providing a principled justification for multimodal prompting in clinical AI. The consistent 3–7% DSC gains across tumor subtypes, particularly on advanced-stage (T4) colon tumors that invade adjacent organs, suggest the approach addresses a real clinical gap where visual-only methods fail. However, the reliance on a pre-trained encoder for anatomical prompt features (with no general-purpose CT foundation model yet available) and the limited evaluation to abdominal CT constrain broader applicability. Open questions include whether the dual-prompt schema transfers to other modalities (MRI, ultrasound) and whether a dedicated anatomical prompt encoder could further close the gap.
