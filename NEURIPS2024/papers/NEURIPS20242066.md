## Problem

Existing depth-range-free MVS methods (e.g., DispMVS, RAMDepth) operate in a pair-wise manner, neglecting inter-image correspondences among source views and still relying on depth range for initialization. Adapting multi-frame optical flow frameworks to MVS is non-trivial because source images are unordered (lacking temporal continuity) and exhibit arbitrary viewing angles, illumination changes, and occlusions that demand new geometric modeling.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242066/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes one reference RGB image and N−1 source RGB images (typically 5–7 views) together with their camera intrinsics and extrinsics, and produces a per-pixel depth map for the reference view, which is then fused across views into a 3D point cloud. Notably, no depth range prior is required: initialization is derived purely from the geometrically valid segment of each epipolar line where both reference and source depths are positive, and the midpoint of that segment is selected as the starting disparity. Evaluation is performed at resolutions up to 1920×1024 on Tanks and Temples and 1600×1152 on DTU.

## Method

The core architecture replaces pair-wise disparity estimation with a simultaneous multi-view formulation built around a Multi-view Disparity Attention (MDA) module that performs intra-image self-attention (linear transformer) and inter-frame cross-attention over epipolar disparity features from all source views. A 3D pose embedding injects both image-level relative camera poses (rotation/translation distance) and pixel-level geometric quantities (2D source coordinates, reference/source depths, normalized 3D ray directions) into the attention features, providing implicit epipolar constraints that the attention mechanism can exploit. A per-source disparity hidden state is maintained across GRU iterations and updated with a variance-based uncertainty score computed from the cost volume, allowing the network to dynamically down-weight occluded or low-quality sampling points. The epipolar disparity flow is iteratively refined by a GRU that consumes the MDA-enhanced features, current flow, and context features, with a weighted sum across views producing the fused depth at each iteration. A novel depth-range-free initialization selects the midpoint of the geometrically valid epipolar segment (where d_r > 0 and d_s > 0), fully eliminating the depth prior that DispMVS still requires.

## Results

On the DTU evaluation set, the proposed method achieves an Overall score of 0.335 mm (Acc 0.338, Comp 0.331), the best among all depth-range-free methods, outperforming DispMVS (0.339) and RAMDepth (0.362). On Tanks and Temples, it achieves mean F-scores of 35.95 (advanced) and 59.27 (intermediate), again the best among scale-agnostic methods. A key finding is that the method maintains identical performance (0.335 mm) across all tested depth ranges, while depth-range-dependent methods like CER-MVS degrade from 0.332 to 12.073 mm when the range is expanded.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among depth-range-free methods, the proposed method outperforms DispMVS [14] by 0.004 mm on DTU Overall (0.335 vs. 0.339) and by 1.05 F-score points on Tanks and Temples advanced (35.95 vs. 34.90), and outperforms RAMDepth [15] by 0.027 mm on DTU. Against depth-range-dependent SOTA methods, the method trails MVSFormer++ [71] (0.281), GBiNet [47] (0.289), and GeoMVS [69] (0.295) on DTU Overall, but these methods require accurate depth range priors. The critical advantage is robustness: when the depth range is expanded from (425, 935) mm to (141.6, 2805) mm, CER-MVS degrades from 0.332 to 12.073 mm and GeoMVS from 0.295 to 1.133 mm, while the proposed method remains constant at 0.335 mm. Similarly, when using COLMAP-estimated depth ranges instead of ground truth, MVSFormer++ drops from 0.281 to 0.340 and CER-MVS from 0.332 to 0.571, whereas the proposed method is unaffected. On Tanks and Temples, the method also outperforms DispMVS on both advanced (35.95 vs. 34.90) and intermediate (59.27 vs. 59.07) subsets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends DispMVS [14], which first formulated depth-range-free MVS as an epipolar disparity flow matching problem, by moving from pairwise source-image processing to simultaneous multi-view estimation. The key architectural departure is the Multi-view Disparity Attention (MDA) module with 3D pose embedding, which injects camera geometry and pixel-level triangulation information into a transformer attention mechanism to model inter-view correspondence without relying on sequential or temporal ordering. The uncertainty-driven disparity hidden state mechanism further differentiates this work from both DispMVS and RAMDepth [15], which lack explicit per-source quality tracking across iterations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Eliminating the depth range prior addresses a significant practical bottleneck: in real-world deployment, accurate depth ranges from SfM are often unreliable due to sparse features, occlusion, and viewpoint limitations, as demonstrated by the dramatic performance collapse of methods like CER-MVS (12.073 mm overall) when using COLMAP-derived ranges. The method's perfectly flat performance across depth range settings (Tables 4–5) validates the design, but the acknowledged gap versus SOTA cost-volume methods (0.335 vs. 0.281 mm on DTU) and the lack of real-time capability limit immediate adoption. An open question is whether the multi-view attention paradigm can be made efficient enough for real-time use while closing the accuracy gap with depth-prior-dependent methods.
