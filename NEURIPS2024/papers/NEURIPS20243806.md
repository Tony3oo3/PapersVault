## Problem

Existing Neural Optimal Transport solvers rely on computing a near-exact c-conjugate (c-transform) at each training step, either through non-convex max-min optimization or computationally expensive fine-tuning of an initial transport map guess. This inner optimization loop introduces instability in the joint training of dual potentials and transport maps, causes the sum of potentials to diverge, and adds significant computational overhead that scales poorly with dimensionality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243806/cropped_page6_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes paired minibatches of samples x~α and y~β along with a user-specified cost function c(x,y), and outputs the learned transport map T, both Kantorovich potentials f and g, and an estimate of the Wasserstein distance W_c(α,β). For image-to-image translation, inputs are 64×64 or 128×128 RGB images from unpaired source and target domains, and the output is the transported image. The transport map can be parameterized as an arbitrary neural network (one-directional mode) or expressed through the potential gradient via Brenier's theorem (bidirectional mode for strictly convex costs of the form h(x−y)).

## Method

ENOT replaces the explicit c-conjugate computation with an expectile regression regularizer R_g that implicitly enforces the binding condition g(y) ≈ (g^T)^c(y) during training. Specifically, it computes the τ-expectile (τ > 0.5) of the random variable g^T(x) − c(x,y) conditioned on y, which converges to the true c-conjugate as τ→1, thereby providing an upper-bound estimate without any inner optimization loop. The total training objective splits into two losses: L_f minimizes c(x,T(x)) − g(T(x)) over the transport map parameters, while L_g + λR_g updates the potential g with the expectile penalty pulling it toward c-concavity. In bidirectional mode, the algorithm alternates between forward (α→β) and inverse (β→α) transport steps, applying an analogous regularizer R_f to the inverse potential. This design eliminates the fine-tuning overhead entirely, enabling 5–10× speedup over amortized baselines while achieving lower unexplained variance across all Wasserstein-2 benchmark dimensions.

## Results

On the Wasserstein-2 benchmark (high-dimensional Gaussian mixtures), ENOT achieves L2^UV scores of 0.02 (D=2), 0.14 (D=8), 0.67 (D=32), 0.56 (D=64), 0.30 (D=128), and 0.51 (D=256), outperforming the best baseline (W2OT-Objective with L-BFGS) by up to 3× at D=32 (0.67 vs 2.09) and consistently at all other dimensions. On CelebA64, ENOT reaches 0.04±0.002 L2^UV on the Late Generator task versus 0.09±0.00 for the best amortized baseline. Runtime is reduced by up to 10×: ENOT completes 20k iterations in ~1.3 min versus ~60–300 min for W2OT with L-BFGS, depending on network width. On unpaired image-to-image translation, ENOT achieves the best FID on all four tasks (e.g., 13.12 on CelebA(f)→Anime 64, 17.11 on FFHQ→Comics 128) and best MSE, while training in ~5–16 hours versus ~3 days for GAN and other OT baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ENOT outperforms all eight baselines across every evaluated task and metric. Against the amortized W2OT family (Amos 2023), ENOT achieves 2–3× lower L2^UV at D=32 (0.67 vs 2.09 for L-BFGS, 1.99 for Adam) and eliminates the >100 divergence failures that W2OT-Cycle and W2OT-Objective (without conjugate fine-tuning) suffer at D≥8. Compared to Monge Gap (Uscidda & Cuturi 2023), ENOT converges ~2× faster on 2D synthetic tasks and avoids the divergence observed beyond 1300 iterations; on the HD benchmark, ENOT scores 0.67 vs 7.24 at D=32. Against the older W2-Cycle (Korotin 2019) and MM-Objective/MM-R-Objective (Dam 2019) baselines, ENOT improves L2^UV by 3–10× at higher dimensions (e.g., 0.51 vs 2.7 at D=256). On image-to-image translation, ENOT beats CycleGAN (FID 19.19 vs 23.4 on Handbags→Shoes), StarGAN-v2 (19.19 vs 22.36), Extremal OT (19.19 vs 27.10), and Kernel OT (19.19 vs 26.7) on FID, and similarly leads on MSE (0.34 vs 0.43, 0.24, 0.37, 0.37 respectively), while training ~10× faster than the GAN and OT baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the line of Neural Optimal Transport (NOT) solvers that learn Kantorovich potentials via parametric models (ICNNs, MLPs) and the amortized optimization framework of Amos (2023) for computing c-conjugate transforms. It departs from the prevailing approach of requiring near-exact c-conjugate approximation at every optimization step (via inner L-BFGS or Adam fine-tuning loops) by instead introducing expectile regression as a soft regularization that implicitly estimates the conjugate without any inner optimization. The method also contrasts with the Monge Gap regularizer (Uscidda and Cuturi, 2023), which learns the transport map directly from the Monge formulation, by remaining in the dual formulation and regularizing the potentials rather than the map.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ENOT eliminates the inner optimization loop that has been the primary computational bottleneck of NOT solvers, yielding 5–10× speedups while simultaneously improving accuracy across the Wasserstein-2 benchmark and image-to-image translation tasks. The theoretical guarantee that the expectile converges to the exact c-conjugate as τ→1 provides a principled justification for the regularization, and the ablation studies demonstrate robustness to hyperparameter choices. Open questions include whether the approach extends to Lagrangian costs, non-Euclidean spaces, and dynamical optimal transport settings such as flow matching, and whether the two additional hyperparameters (τ, λ) can be made adaptive rather than fixed per dataset.
