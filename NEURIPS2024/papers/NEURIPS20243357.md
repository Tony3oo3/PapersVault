## Problem

In discrete choice settings, user feedback arrives as selections from small slates (e.g., 'ten blue links' in web search), yet the goal is to predict winning distributions over arbitrarily large slates including the full catalog. The paper asks: what is the minimum slate size k such that observing all winning distributions on slates of size at most k suffices to approximate the winning distribution on every slate to within an arbitrarily small additive error? This question had no tight answer prior to this work.

## Input / Output

The algorithm receives oracle access (either exact winning distributions via MAX-DIST or single samples via MAX-SAMPLE) to all slates of size at most k = Θ(√(n·ln(1/ε))), and must answer queries of the form (s, T) by returning an estimate of R_{T∪{s}}(s) within additive ε. The proper learning algorithm outputs a full RUM (a distribution over all n! permutations) representable in O(n² log n) bits after subsampling; the improper algorithm outputs a coefficient-based data structure of size n^{O(√n)} that answers each query in 2^{O(|T|·ln(1/ε)/k)}·poly(|T|) time. A simulation variant requires only polynomially many sub-slate queries of size O(|T|/ln|T|) for any predetermined target slate.

## Method

The core technical contribution is a tight Θ(√n) bound on the required slate size, proved by reducing RUM learning to polynomial approximation of the bitwise AND function. The upper bound leverages Sherstov's approximate inclusion-exclusion theorem: the winning probability R_{T∪{s}}(s) equals the probability of an intersection of |T| events, which can be approximated by a degree-k polynomial in the probabilities of smaller intersections, where k = Θ(√(|T|·ln(1/ε))) is the approximate degree of AND. The proper learning algorithm solves a linear program over n! permutation variables constrained by the observed small-slate distributions; the improper algorithm instead computes a weighted sum of small-slate winning probabilities using the Huang–Viola polynomial, whose bounded ℓ₁-norm of coefficients (2^{O(n·ln(1/ε)/k)}) directly controls both the number of oracle queries and the query-time complexity. The matching lower bound constructs two RUMs via dual polynomials (Bogdanov et al.) that are indistinguishable on all slates of size o(√n) yet differ by more than 1−ε on the full slate, establishing optimality.

## Results

The paper establishes that k = Θ(√n) is both necessary and sufficient for learning a RUM to within an arbitrarily small constant ℓ∞-error from slates of size at most k. The new improper learning algorithm achieves ℓ∞-error ε in time n^{O(√(n log(1/ε)))} (resp. ℓ₁-error ε in n^{O(√(n log(n/ε))))}, an exponential improvement over the previous best-known 2^{O(n)} bound. A simulation corollary shows that any single slate T can be approximated in polynomial time by querying only O(|T|/log|T|)-sized sub-slates. The lower bound shows that with only o(√n)-sized slates, any algorithm must incur error 1/2 − ε on the full slate.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is against the implicit 2^{O(n)}-time algorithm from prior work (Chierichetti et al., 2018a; Farias et al., 2009), which required access to all slates up to size O(n). The new improper learning algorithm reduces the running time to n^{O(√(n log(1/ε)))}, an exponential improvement, while also reducing the required slate size from O(n) to Θ(√n). The lower bound (Theorem 12) is tight: it matches the upper bound up to constants, showing that no algorithm can do better than Ω(√n) slate size. For the fractional k-deck problem, the paper's lower bound (Theorem 14) improves upon the prior subpolynomial lower bound of e^{Θ(√(log n))} from Dudik and Schulman [2003], establishing that k = Ω(√n) is needed. For fractional trace reconstruction (Theorem 15), the paper provides an unconditional e^{o(√n)} lower bound that holds for all algorithms, not just mean-based ones as in De et al. [2017].

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper establishes a tight Θ(√n) characterization for the minimum slate size needed to uniformly approximate a RUM, building directly on Sherstov's approximate inclusion-exclusion theorem (2008) and the Huang–Viola polynomial for AND approximation (2022). It extends the RUM learning framework of Chierichetti et al. (2018, 2021, 2023) by identifying the precise information-theoretic threshold and providing explicit algorithms, while also strengthening the previously observed connection between RUM learning and the k-deck problem. The lower bound leverages the dual-polynomial indistinguishability result of Bogdanov et al. (2016), originally developed for cryptographic applications, to construct two RUMs that are indistinguishable on small slates but maximally different on the full slate.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The tight Θ(√n) bound resolves a natural open question in RUM learning and provides the first exponential improvement over the prior 2^{O(n)} running time for proper learning. The corollary lower bounds for fractional k-deck and fractional trace reconstruction are unconditional and apply to all algorithms (not just mean-based ones), strengthening the state of the art in those combinatorial problems. Key open questions remain: whether the exponential running time can be improved, whether the simulation algorithm's O(n/ln n) slate size can be reduced while maintaining polynomial time, and whether a PAC-learning variant with distributed slates admits better guarantees.
