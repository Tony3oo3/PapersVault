## Problem

Regression under demographic parity constraints in the unawareness setting—where the sensitive attribute is unavailable at inference time—lacks a computationally efficient, fully theory-driven algorithm. While the awareness case (sensitive attribute known at prediction time) is well understood via Wasserstein barycenters, the unawareness case for regression had no method with end-to-end finite-sample guarantees on both risk and fairness violation.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a stream of unlabeled feature vectors X (no labels required for post-processing), along with a pre-trained base regressor η, a sensitive-attribute predictor τ, and the marginal distribution p of the sensitive variable S. It outputs a randomized prediction function π that assigns a probability distribution over a discrete grid of 2L+1 points in [−B, B], satisfying the demographic parity constraint (independence of prediction from S) up to a user-specified tolerance ε. The post-processing is fully online: each unlabeled sample is used once for a stochastic gradient step without being stored.

## Method

The approach discretizes the prediction space onto a uniform grid, introduces entropic regularization, and derives a dual convex objective F built from a log-sum-exp (LSE) smoothing of the Lagrangian. The key insight is that the stochastic gradient of F requires only one unlabeled sample X (no labels), and that controlling the norm of the gradient mapping—not merely the objective gap—is essential for statistical guarantees, since the Lipschitz constant M scales with the regularization parameter β. The algorithm deploys a refined SGD3 (Allen-Zhu, 2021) with projection onto the non-negative orthant, extended from Foster et al. (2019) to handle the constrained setting and yield a squared-norm gradient-mapping bound. The final prediction is a plug-in: the optimized dual variables (Λ, V) are inserted into a soft-argmax formula to produce the randomized prediction, achieving 1/√T rates for both risk and unfairness.

## Results

The main theoretical guarantee (Theorem 5.1) establishes that both the demographic parity violation and the excess risk converge at rate Õ(1/√T), where T is the number of unlabeled samples, with the fairness bound scaling as Õ(σ/√T · (1 + σ/√T · ‖(Λ*,V*)‖)). Empirically, on both the Law School and Communities and Crime datasets, the proposed DP-postproc method is competitive with or outperforms the ADW baseline (Agarwal et al., 2019) across all tested unfairness thresholds ε ∈ {2⁻¹, 2⁻², 2⁻⁴, 2⁻⁸, 2⁻¹⁶}. The ADW algorithm required 13.5 hours of computation for the full comparison, while the proposed method operates in an online streaming fashion requiring only unlabeled data for post-processing.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against the ADW method of Agarwal et al. (2019) in two configurations: ADW-1 (trained on both labeled and unlabeled data) and ADW-2 (trained on labeled data only), as well as a base LinearRegression model without any fairness constraint. The authors report that their DP-postproc method is 'competitive or eventually outperforms ADW in both training regimes' on both the Communities and Crime and Law School datasets, evaluated across multiple unfairness thresholds. Notably, the proposed method uniquely leverages the unlabeled data stream for post-processing (which ADW-2 cannot exploit), giving it an advantage in the ADW-2 comparison. The paper also notes that no other open-source methods targeting regression with demographic parity in the unaware setting were available for comparison, even the FairLearn package does not handle this setting. The empirical results are presented as convergence plots (Figure 1) and mean/std comparison plots (Figure 2) rather than a single numerical table, showing risk and per-group unfairness (U₀, U₁) as functions of iteration count and ε threshold respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the discretization-based post-processing framework of Chzhen et al. (2020b) and Agarwal et al. (2019) to the unawareness setting, where the sensitive attribute is unavailable at inference time. The key technical departure is the use of entropic regularization to derive a smooth convex dual objective, combined with the SGD3/accelerated-SGD machinery of Allen-Zhu (2021) and Foster et al. (2019) to control the gradient mapping norm rather than the objective function value. Unlike prior fair regression methods that either require the sensitive attribute at inference or rely on black-box solvers, this work provides an end-to-end stochastic algorithm with finite-sample guarantees, handling multiple protected attributes and relaxed fairness constraints simultaneously.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first fully theory-driven, computationally efficient algorithm for fair regression under demographic parity in the unawareness setting, filling a clear gap identified in the literature. The critical insight that gradient norm control (not objective value control) is necessary for statistical consistency—because the Lipschitz constant M scales with the regularization parameter β—opens a path to other constrained optimization problems where the smoothness parameter is itself a tuning knob. Key limitations include the requirement to know a bound B on the regression function (replacing this with tail conditions is an open question) and the empirical validation on only two small tabular datasets, leaving scalability to larger or more complex settings untested.
