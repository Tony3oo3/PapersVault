## Problem

Federated multi-task RL, where each agent has a private reward function but shares the same environment dynamics, lacks provably efficient algorithms with non-asymptotic convergence guarantees in the fully decentralized setting. The heterogeneity of local tasks creates disagreement between global and local value functions, and the absence of a central server makes it difficult to balance consensus with local learning. Prior work had not established near dimension-free global convergence for policy optimization methods in this federated multi-task context.

## Input / Output

Each agent n receives its own private reward function r_n(s,a) and accesses the shared transition kernel P, while communicating only with neighbors over a prescribed undirected graph encoded by a doubly stochastic mixing matrix W. For FedNAC, the input additionally includes a bounded feature map φ: S×A → R^p and an initial state-action distribution ν. The output is a shared policy π̄^(t) defined as the normalized geometric mean of all local policies, along with per-agent estimates T_n^(t) of the global Q-function; the convergence criterion is on the average sub-optimality gap (1/T)Σ(V⋆ − V^{π̄^(t)}) for vanilla FedNPG or the last-iterate gap for the entropy-regularized variant.

## Method

FedNPG operates in log-policy space under softmax parameterization: at each iteration, each agent updates its log-policy by a gossip mix of neighbors' log-policies plus a local Q-tracking term, where the tracking variable T_n^(t) is updated via T^(t+1) = W(T^(t) + Q^(t+1) − Q^(t)) to estimate the global Q-function through dynamic average consensus. This Q-tracking mechanism, borrowed from decentralized optimization, is the key technical novelty—its application to policy optimization is non-trivial due to the nonconcavity of the objective and the use of natural gradients, requiring a 4th-order linear system analysis rather than the 2nd-order system in the centralized case. For the entropy-regularized variant, the log-policy update includes an additional (1−ητ/(1−γ)) damping factor, yielding linear convergence. FedNAC extends FedNPG to function approximation with log-linear policies and stochastic policy evaluation via a Q-Sampler subroutine that produces unbiased Q-value estimates from on-policy trajectories of expected length 1/(1−γ), achieving Õ(1/ε^{7/2}) sample complexity per agent.

## Results

The paper establishes near dimension-free global convergence guarantees for federated policy optimization. For vanilla FedNPG, the average-iterate sub-optimality gap converges at O(1/T^{2/3}) in the decentralized setting (σ>0), recovering the O(1/T) rate of centralized NPG when the network is fully connected (σ=0). For entropy-regularized FedNPG, the last iterate converges linearly at rate ρ(η)^t where ρ(η) ≤ max{1−τη/2, (3+σ)/4}. The FedNAC method achieves a per-agent sample complexity of Õ(1/ε^{7/2}) in the fully decentralized setting, matching the ε-dependency of FedNPG with generative model access while using only on-policy samples.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares FedNPG against two centralized NPG baselines: the vanilla NPG of Agarwal et al. [AKLM21] and the entropy-regularized NPG of Cen et al. [CWC21]. In the fully connected (server-client) limit where σ=0, FedNPG exactly recovers the iteration complexities of both centralized baselines, confirming no overhead from the federated framework when global information is available. In the decentralized setting (σ>0), FedNPG incurs a degradation: the vanilla case slows from O(1/ε) to O(σ√N/((1−σ)ε^{3/2})) iterations, and the regularized case adds a 1/(1−σ) factor to the log(1/ε) dependence. The key advantage over prior decentralized multi-task RL work (e.g., [ZAD+21]) is that FedNPG achieves near dimension-free convergence (logarithmic in |A|, independent of |S|), whereas existing decentralized policy gradient methods have pessimistic polynomial dependencies on the state-action space size. The FedNAC method further extends beyond tabular settings with function approximation, matching the centralized Q-NPG sample complexity of O(1/ε³) per agent when σ=0.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends centralized natural policy gradient (NPG) methods (Agarwal et al. 2021; Cen et al. 2021) to the fully decentralized federated multi-task RL setting by combining them with dynamic average consensus-based gradient tracking (Zhu & Martinez 2010), a technique previously used in decentralized optimization but not in policy optimization. The FedNAC method is a direct decentralized extension of Q-NPG (Yuan et al. 2022), adapting its stochastic Q-Sampler subroutine to a multi-agent gossip protocol. The key departure from prior federated RL work (e.g., Khodadadian et al. 2022, Woo et al. 2023) is the multi-task formulation where agents have heterogeneous private reward functions rather than sharing a common reward, and the fully decentralized architecture that eliminates any central server.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to establish near dimension-free global convergence guarantees for federated multi-task RL via policy optimization, which is significant because prior federated RL methods either assumed shared rewards or lacked non-asymptotic guarantees in the decentralized setting. The quantification of how network connectivity (spectral radius σ) degrades convergence from O(1/T) to O(1/T^{2/3}) provides concrete guidance for network design. Limitations include the tabular setting for main theoretical results, reliance on exact or bounded-error policy evaluation, and empirical validation limited to a small GridWorld environment. Open questions include extending to personalized policy learning, handling non-stationary networks, and scaling to continuous state-action spaces.
