## Problem

Multi-task robot policies trained on large teleoperated datasets remain significantly less effective than independently trained single-task policies, and the prevailing remedy is simply collecting more expert demonstrations. This is especially costly in robotics, where each data point requires physical execution. The paper asks whether a carefully designed, compact architecture can substantially close this gap by more efficiently leveraging the limited demonstrations available per task.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20244483/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

BAKU consumes RGB images from multiple camera views (128×128 for LIBERO and xArm, 84×84 for Meta-World), a robot proprioceptive state vector, and a text task instruction (or alternatively a goal/intermediate image). Only the current observation is fed to the network; no observation history is used in the final architecture. The output is a single concatenated vector of K continuous actions (K=10 for LIBERO/Meta-World, K=3 for DMC, K=20 for xArm), which at inference time is blended with overlapping predictions from previous timesteps via exponential temporal averaging to produce smooth 10 Hz control commands.

## Method

BAKU is a ~10M-parameter decoder-only transformer split into three modular components: (1) modality-specific sensory encoders — a FiLM-conditioned ResNet-18 shared across all camera views, a two-layer MLP for proprioception, and a 6-layer MiniLM for text — each projecting to a common dimensionality; (2) a causal transformer observation trunk (8 layers, 4 heads, 256 hidden dim) that treats each modality's encoding as a separate token alongside a learnable action token; and (3) a pluggable action head (MLP, GMM, BeT, VQ-BeT, or diffusion) that decodes the action token into a chunk of continuous actions. The key architectural choices distinguishing BAKU from prior work are: using a decoder-only transformer rather than the encoder-decoder of MT-ACT, directly using the final 512-dim ResNet feature without RT-1's Token Learner summarization, FiLM-conditioning the vision encoder on the language embedding to produce task-specific visual representations, and predicting the entire action chunk as one concatenated vector rather than per-timestep. Action chunking with exponential temporal ensembling (w_i = exp(−m·i)) smooths the output without additional training cost. Ablations show the transformer trunk, action chunking, and FiLM conditioning each contribute meaningfully, while a multimodal VQ-BeT head provides a further 5% gain on real-robot tasks.

## Results

BAKU achieves a 90% average success rate on LIBERO-90 (90 tasks), a 36% absolute improvement over the prior best (MT-ACT at 54%). On Meta-World (30 tasks), BAKU reaches 79% vs. 65% for RT-1 and 13% for MT-ACT. On the real xArm robot (30 tasks, ~17 demos/task), BAKU with an MLP head achieves 86% success rate, and with a VQ-BeT multimodal action head reaches 91%, outperforming the strongest baseline by 35%. On the simpler DMC locomotion tasks (9 tasks), BAKU achieves 70% vs. 66% for RT-1 and 59% for MT-ACT.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BAKU outperforms both baselines across all four evaluation suites. Against RT-1, BAKU improves by 74% on LIBERO-90 (0.90 vs. 0.16), 14% on Meta-World (0.79 vs. 0.65), 4% on DMC (0.70 vs. 0.66), and 49% on the real robot (0.86 vs. 0.37). Against MT-ACT, BAKU improves by 36% on LIBERO-90 (0.90 vs. 0.54), 66% on Meta-World (0.79 vs. 0.13), 11% on DMC (0.70 vs. 0.59), and 30% on the real robot (0.86 vs. 0.56). The largest gains come on the most complex manipulation benchmarks (LIBERO-90 and Meta-World), where the transformer observation trunk and action chunking are most impactful. On the real robot, replacing the MLP action head with a multimodal VQ-BeT head further boosts success from 86% to 91%, a 5% gain that is specific to real-world deployment where behavioral diversity matters more.

## Relation to Prior Work

BAKU is positioned as a careful architectural synthesis of ideas from RT-1 (FiLM-conditioned vision encoders, decoder-only transformer), MT-ACT/RoboAgent (action chunking, multi-task conditioning), and ACT (temporal smoothing), rather than a fundamentally new algorithmic contribution. Its key departures are structural: a decoder-only transformer trunk (vs. MT-ACT's encoder-decoder), a fully decoupled action head that can be swapped with GMM, BeT, VQ-BeT, or diffusion models, and predicting the entire action chunk as a single concatenated vector rather than per-timestep. The paper's novelty is explicitly framed as the systematic combination and ablation of these components into a unified, simple architecture for multi-task behavior cloning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

BAKU addresses the core data-efficiency bottleneck in robotics by achieving 91% success on 30 real-world tasks with only 17 demonstrations per task, and provides a thorough ablation study that gives practitioners clear guidance on which architectural choices matter (action chunking, transformer trunk, FiLM conditioning) and which do not (observation history, model scale beyond ~10M). Open questions the authors flag include: how to handle tasks of varying difficulty within a single policy (precise manipulation tasks suffer), how to chain multiple skills for true long-horizon autonomy, and whether multi-task generalization emerges as task count grows. The finding that a 114M-parameter model catastrophically underperforms a 10M model on LIBERO-90 raises important questions about overfitting in data-scarce multi-task settings.
