## Problem

Existing clinical benchmarks for LLMs either test general medical knowledge (MedQA, MMLU) or rely on single-note, narrowly scoped QA datasets (emrQA, Yue et al.) that do not reflect the multi-admission, multi-topic inquiries physicians actually pose on accumulated discharge summaries. No benchmark captures the real-world complexity of comparing information across multiple discharge summaries spanning diverse clinical topics, making it impossible to assess whether an LLM can serve as a practical QA agent in clinical settings.

## Input / Output

The benchmark takes a patient's accumulated discharge summaries from MIMIC-IV (preprocessed to remove excess whitespace and annotated with patient/admission IDs and chart times) as context, split into Level 1 (≤3,000 tokens, 1–2 admissions) and Level 2 (3,000–7,000 tokens, 1–3 admissions) to match available LLM context windows. Each of the 962 QA pairs pairs a clinical question with a correct answer and four plausible distractors, supporting both open-ended (free-form answer evaluated by GPT-4 judge) and multi-choice (selected option evaluated by GPT-4 judge) evaluation formats.

## Method

EHRNoteQA is constructed through a three-stage pipeline: (1) 1,000 patients are randomly sampled from MIMIC-IV and stratified by accumulated note length; (2) GPT-4 (on Azure's HIPAA-compliant platform) generates an initial clinically relevant question, a complete answer, and four distractor choices per patient; (3) three clinicians individually review all 1,000 pairs, removing 38 clinically irrelevant questions, modifying 206 ambiguous questions, revising 338 answers, and replacing 966 of 3,842 distractors with more plausible alternatives, yielding 962 final QA pairs spanning 10 clinical topics. For evaluation, GPT-4-Turbo (temperature 0, run 5× per pair with mode aggregation) serves as the automated judge for both formats, validated against clinician scoring (Cohen's κ up to 0.880). The paper further validates EHRNoteQA as a proxy for expert judgment by correlating LLM benchmark scores with clinician-evaluated scores on 300 DiSCQ questions, showing EHRNoteQA achieves the highest Spearman ρ (0.78) and Kendall τ (0.62) among all tested benchmarks.

## Results

GPT-4 achieves the highest scores on EHRNoteQA, scoring 97.16% (multi-choice, Level 1) and 91.30% (open-ended, Level 1), with slight drops to 95.15% and 89.61% on the longer Level 2 data. Llama3-70b-Instruct is the strongest open-source model, reaching 94.33% (multi-choice, Level 1) and 89.04% (open-ended, Level 1), closely approaching GPT-4. Across all 27 evaluated LLMs, performance consistently degrades from Level 1 (≤3k tokens) to Level 2 (3k–7k tokens) and from single-note to multi-note questions, highlighting the challenge of interpreting longer and cumulative clinical contexts. In the clinician-correlation validation (Table 5), EHRNoteQA scores correlate most strongly with clinician-evaluated performance (avg. Spearman ρ ≈ 0.78, Kendall τ ≈ 0.62), outperforming all other benchmarks tested.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EHRNoteQA is validated as a proxy for clinician evaluation by measuring correlation with scores assigned by three clinicians on the independent DiSCQ dataset. EHRNoteQA (both open-ended and multi-choice) consistently achieves the highest Spearman ρ (0.77–0.81) and Kendall τ (0.57–0.66) across all three clinicians, outperforming existing discharge summary QA benchmarks (emrQA: ρ 0.65–0.70; Yue et al.: ρ 0.50–0.54), clinical knowledge benchmarks (MedQA: ρ 0.50–0.68; MedMCQA: ρ 0.50–0.74; PubMedQA: ρ 0.07–0.17; MMLU*: ρ 0.65–0.80), and general benchmarks (ARC, HellaSwag, MMLU, TruthfulQA, Winogrande, GSM8K; avg. ρ 0.58–0.62). For evaluation methodology, GPT-4-based scoring shows substantially higher correlation with clinician judgments than BLEU (ρ 0.01–0.16), ROUGE-L (ρ 0.36–0.50), Exact Match (ρ 0.27–0.42), SentenceBERT (ρ 0.65–0.73), ClinicalBERT (ρ 0.39–0.55), or probability-based multi-choice scoring (ρ 0.51–0.68). Among the 27 LLMs evaluated, GPT-4 leads in both formats, while Llama3-70b-Instruct is the closest open-source competitor; notably, Mistral-7b-OpenOrca (7B) outperforms Llama2-70b-chat (70B) on Level 1 multi-choice (87.15 vs. 84.88), demonstrating that model size alone does not determine performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from two lines of prior work: general clinical knowledge benchmarks (MedQA, MedMCQA, PubMedQA, MMLU) that test abstract medical knowledge without patient-specific records, and existing discharge summary QA datasets (emrQA, Yue et al., Fan, Moon et al.) that use patient records but are constrained to single-note questions and narrow topic scopes derived from predefined annotations (i2b2, n2c2). EHRNoteQA carves new ground by requiring information integration across multiple discharge summaries, spanning 10 diverse clinical topics, and grounding question generation in GPT-4 output refined by clinician review rather than template-based or annotation-driven construction. The evaluation methodology also departs from prior work by using GPT-4 as an automated judge validated against clinician scoring, rather than relying on BLEU, ROUGE, or probability-based scoring.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

EHRNoteQA provides the first benchmark validated as a reliable proxy for clinician evaluation of LLMs on patient-specific discharge summary queries, with correlations (Spearman ρ ≈ 0.78) substantially exceeding those of all other clinical and general benchmarks. This matters practically because institutions deploying LLMs as clinical QA agents need a benchmark that predicts real-world utility rather than abstract knowledge. Key limitations include reliance on MIMIC-IV (the only publicly available EHR), restriction to discharge summaries (excluding radiology and physician notes), and context-length constraints that limit the number of admissions per patient. Open questions include whether findings generalize to non-MIMIC EHR systems and whether the benchmark can be extended as longer-context models become available.
