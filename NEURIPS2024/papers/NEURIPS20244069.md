## Problem

Existing self-supervised foundation models (DINOv2, SAM) for personalized retrieval and segmentation fail to distinguish a specific reference instance when other objects of the same class appear in the target image, because they rely on semantic or coarse visual features rather than instance-level appearance cues. Additionally, standard benchmarks predominantly contain single-instance or cross-class scenes, masking this weakness and making it difficult to evaluate true instance-level discrimination.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20244069/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a reference image (resized to 512×512), a short class-name text prompt, and a target image or video frame. It first performs diffusion inversion of the reference image into the latent space of SDXL-turbo (4 inversion steps, ~0.5 s per image on an A100), then extracts spatial feature maps at the last U-Net layer. For segmentation, the output is a binary mask obtained by thresholding the fused similarity map upsampled to image size; for retrieval, a global scalar score (mean of the similarity map) ranks candidate images. An optional variant feeds the highest-confidence point into SAM for a refined mask.

## Method

PDM (Personalized Diffusion Features Matching) exploits the observation that self-attention query/key matrices in the last U-Net layer of a pre-trained text-to-image diffusion model encode instance-specific texture and color, while cross-attention query vectors encode class-level semantics. Appearance features are defined as the spatial average of Q^SA and K^SA at a fixed timestep; semantic features are the projected Q^CA vectors. A reference mask is derived from the cross-attention map between the reference semantic features and the class-name token, which crops the reference appearance map to the instance region. The final similarity map is the element-wise average of an appearance score (dot product between masked reference and target appearance features, averaged over foreground tokens) and a semantic score (product of the class token with the target semantic map). The entire pipeline is zero-shot—no training, fine-tuning, or prompt optimization—and can also serve as a re-ranking signal on top of other frozen feature extractors.

## Results

On the new PerMIR multi-instance retrieval benchmark, PDM achieves 73.0 mAP, dramatically outperforming the next-best baseline SLIP (35.9) by +37.1 points and DINOv2 (29.7) by +43.3 points. On personalized image segmentation, PDM reaches 95.4 mIoU on PerSeg (matching PerSAM's 95.3) and 42.3 mIoU on the new PerMIS benchmark, more than doubling DINOv2 (20.2) and DIFT (21.9). For video label propagation, PDM achieves 75.1 J&F on PerMIS-Video, surpassing DIFT (69.7) and PerSAM (64.0). On standard single-instance retrieval benchmarks (ROxford-Hard, RParis-Hard), PDM outperforms all self-supervised and weakly-supervised methods but remains below the supervised SuperGlobal (80.2 and 86.7 mAP respectively).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PDM's most decisive advantage appears on the multi-instance PerMIR benchmark, where it scores 73.0 mAP versus 35.9 for SLIP, 33.5 for SuperGlobal (supervised), and 29.7 for DINOv2 — a gap of 37+ points that no other method approaches. On the standard single-instance benchmarks (ROxford-Hard, RParis-Hard), PDM beats all self-supervised (DINOv2, iBOT, MAE) and weakly-supervised (CLIP, OpenCLIP, SLIP, BLIP) baselines but trails the supervised SuperGlobal by 21.9 and 2.0 mAP points respectively, suggesting that on easy single-instance retrieval, supervised global features still hold an edge. For personalized segmentation on PerSeg, PDM (95.4 mIoU) marginally edges out PerSAM (95.3) and SegGPT (94.3), while on the harder PerMIS benchmark it more than doubles DINOv2 (42.3 vs. 20.2 mIoU) and DIFT (42.3 vs. 21.9). In video label propagation, PDM leads on PerMIS-Video (75.1 J&F vs. 69.7 DIFT, 64.0 PerSAM) but is slightly behind PerSAM on DAVIS (75.8 vs. 76.1 J&F). Notably, PDM also functions as a re-ranking module: combining it with DINOv2 or SuperGlobal boosts their PerMIR scores to 70.8 and 69.1 respectively, confirming that diffusion features complement existing retrieval pipelines.

## Relation to Prior Work

PDM repurposes internal features of pre-trained text-to-image diffusion models (specifically self-attention Q/K matrices for appearance and cross-attention Q for semantics at a single U-Net layer and timestep) for instance-level matching, a use case not explored by prior diffusion-feature work (DIFT, Plug-and-Play, Cross-Image-Attention) which focused on generation, editing, or correspondence. It departs from the PerSAM/DINOv2 line of personalized segmentation, which relies on class-level features and fails when multiple same-class instances co-occur, by fusing textural and semantic cues to discriminate specific instances. The paper also extends the evaluation landscape by introducing PerMIR and PerMIS benchmarks built on BURST, addressing a gap where existing retrieval and segmentation benchmarks lack hard same-class negatives.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a single frozen diffusion model's intermediate activations encode instance-level (not merely class-level) features usable for zero-shot matching without any training or fine-tuning offers a practical path to personalized segmentation and retrieval without labeled data. The new multi-instance benchmarks expose a significant blind spot in prior evaluation: methods that appear competitive on standard benchmarks (PerSeg, DAVIS) collapse when same-class distractors are present, suggesting the field has been overestimating instance discrimination. Key limitations include the dependency on diffusion inversion quality (0.5–5 s per image), restriction to U-Net architectures, and the relatively small scale of the new benchmarks (150 videos). Open questions include whether comparable features exist in DiT-based models and whether learnable fusion of appearance and semantic maps (shown to help modestly in the appendix) can be generalized.
