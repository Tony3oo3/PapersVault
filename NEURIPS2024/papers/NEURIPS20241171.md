## Problem

Standard reward-free RLHF methods like DPO assume a single preference distribution, treating all human feedback as exchangeable samples. In practice, preferences come from diverse groups (demographics, ethnicities, teams) with potentially conflicting preferences, and the 'one-size-fits-all' optimization disproportionately favors majority groups at the expense of minority ones. This paper asks: how can we robustly align an LLM so that no group is disproportionately disadvantaged, without learning an explicit reward model?

## Input / Output

The method consumes preference triplets (prompt x, winning response y_w, losing response y_l) partitioned by group g, where group identity is injected into the prompt via string concatenation (x_g = x ⊕ g). The same prompt can appear across multiple groups with different preferred responses. The output is a fine-tuned LLM policy π_θ (via LoRA on Gemma-2B in experiments) that assigns response probabilities optimized to minimize the worst-case group loss, alongside adaptive group weights α that track cumulative per-group losses during training.

## Method

GRPO reformulates the DPO objective as a minimax problem: min_θ max_{α∈Δ_{K-1}} Σ_g α_g · L_DPO(π_θ, D_g), where α are group weights on the simplex. Algorithm 1 solves this via alternating updates: a multiplicative-weights (mirror ascent) step on α that exponentially upweights groups with higher cumulative loss, and a gradient descent step on θ whose gradient is scaled by α_g/N_g to direct updates toward underperforming groups. For the log-linear policy class, the paper proves a Nash equilibrium exists and establishes O(T^{-1/2}) convergence. A key additional contribution is GR-IPO, where the squared-loss IPO objective under log-linear parameterization admits a closed-form weighted regression update θ̂ = (1/2β)(S^T W S)^{-1} S^T W 1, replacing iterative gradient steps with a direct solve at each α update.

## Results

On the GlobalOpinionQA dataset with Gemma-2B (5 countries, 2,554 questions), GR-IPO achieves lower worst-case group loss and higher minimum group reward accuracy than vanilla IPO, while also reducing the gap between best and worst group losses. In synthetic experiments (imbalanced group sizes and differing response distributions), both GR-DPO and GR-IPO yield significantly lower worst-case validation loss and reward error compared to vanilla DPO/IPO and importance-sampling variants. The convergence rate of Algorithm 1 is O(T^{-1/2}) for the average iterate under standard convexity and Lipschitz assumptions on the loss.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares GR-DPO/GR-IPO against two families of baselines: (1) vanilla DPO and IPO [Rafailov et al., Azar et al.], and (2) importance-sampling (IS) variants of DPO and IPO that reweight each data point inversely by its group size. On the GlobalOpinionQA real-data experiment, GR-IPO outperforms IPO on both maximum group loss and minimum group reward accuracy, and additionally reduces cross-group loss imbalance; the worst-performing groups (Groups 2 and 5) receive adaptively higher weights during training, improving their log-probability accuracy. In synthetic experiments, GR-DPO and GR-IPO consistently surpass both vanilla and importance-sampling baselines on worst-case validation loss and reward error, with the largest gains appearing when groups differ in both size and response distribution (scenario iii). Importantly, importance sampling only addresses data-size imbalance and fails when groups also differ in learning difficulty, whereas GRPO handles both sources of disparity simultaneously. When groups are balanced in size and difficulty (scenario i), the advantage of GRPO over importance sampling shrinks, confirming that the robustness benefit is most pronounced under genuine group heterogeneity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) and Identity Preference Optimization (IPO) by formulating a group-robust variant that minimizes worst-case loss across diverse preference groups via a minimax objective. It departs from prior robust RLHF work (e.g., Chakraborty et al. learning multiple reward functions, or Bai et al. weighting losses for reward learning) by embedding group robustness directly into the reward-free tuning paradigm, eliminating the need for explicit reward models. The algorithmic approach draws on distributionally robust optimization and multiplicative-weights mirror descent, while the closed-form weighted regression update for GR-IPO in the log-linear policy class is a novel contribution not present in prior DPO/IPO literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GRPO provides the first principled framework for ensuring equitable alignment across diverse user groups in reward-free RLHF, with practical implications for deploying LLMs in multi-demographic or multi-team settings. Key limitations include the inherent minimax trade-off (improving worst-case at the cost of average performance), the requirement for known group labels, and the observation that GRPO offers no advantage when groups are balanced and comparable in difficulty. Open questions include optimal tuning of the worst-case/average trade-off parameter χ, handling unknown or latent group structures, and scaling the approach to larger models and more complex group hierarchies.
