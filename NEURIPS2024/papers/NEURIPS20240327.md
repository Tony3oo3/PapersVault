## Problem

While prior work established upper and lower bounds on the expressive power of various transformer variants, no exact characterization existed for transformers with hard attention (all attention on a single position) combined with attention masking (each position attends only to one side). This paper closes that gap by proving that masked hard-attention transformers with strict masking and no position embeddings recognize exactly the star-free regular languages, and then leverages this equivalence to derive precise results about how masking strictness, position embeddings, and depth affect expressivity.

## Input / Output

The input is a non-empty string over a finite alphabet Σ, embedded as one-hot vectors at each position. The output is a binary accept/reject decision obtained by linearly projecting the final activation vector at the last position n and thresholding at zero. The base model uses unique hard attention (ties broken left or right), strict masking (a position cannot attend to itself), single-head attention, and no position embeddings; variants with past masking, non-strict masking, multi-head attention, and finite-image position embeddings are also characterized.

## Method

The central technique is Boolean RASP (B-RASP), a restricted version of the RASP programming language limited to Boolean vectors, which serves as a bidirectional intermediate language: every B-RASP program compiles to a masked hard-attention transformer, and every such transformer decompiles to an equivalent B-RASP program. The decompilation relies on a key finiteness lemma showing all intermediate activations and attention scores are drawn from a finite set, so each value can be encoded in O(1) bits and simulated by Boolean predicates. B-RASP is then shown equivalent to linear temporal logic (LTL) via direct translations of since/until operators to attention operations and vice versa, yielding the three-way equivalence B-RASP ≡ LTL ≡ masked hard-attention transformers. This equivalence allows the authors to import classical LTL results—stutter-invariance under non-strict masking, the depth hierarchy via the STAIR languages, and the characterization of position-embedding-augmented transformers via LTL[Mon] and AC⁰ regular languages—directly into the transformer setting.

## Results

The paper establishes exact characterizations rather than reporting quantitative metrics. The headline result (Theorems 1–4) is that masked hard-attention transformers with strict masking and no position embeddings recognize exactly the star-free regular languages, via equivalence with B-RASP and LTL. Further results show that (i) future-masked rightmost-hard attention alone suffices for full star-free expressivity (Theorem 5), (ii) non-strict masking reduces expressivity to stutter-invariant star-free languages (Theorem 6), (iii) rational sinusoidal position embeddings extend the class to regular languages in AC⁰ (Corollary 8), and (iv) arbitrary finite-image position embeddings yield LTL[Mon] (Corollary 9). A strict depth hierarchy is also proven: for every k, there exists a language recognizable at depth k+1 but not at depth k (Theorem 10).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical baselines; the 'comparisons' are between different transformer variants and their expressive power. The paper shows that adding past-masked, non-masked, or leftmost-hard attention does not increase expressivity beyond future-masked rightmost-hard attention (Theorem 5). Removing strict masking (allowing self-attention) strictly reduces expressivity, limiting recognition to stutter-invariant star-free languages (Theorem 6). Adding position embeddings with finite image strictly increases expressivity: rational sinusoidal embeddings reach AC⁰ regular languages (e.g., (aa)*), while arbitrary finite-image embeddings reach LTL[Mon], answering an open question left by Barceló et al. (2024). Finally, unlike feed-forward networks, adding self-attention layers always strictly increases expressive power, yielding an infinite hierarchy (Theorem 10), with the separating language STAIR_{k+1} requiring depth k+1.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the RASP programming language (Weiss et al., 2021) into a Boolean variant (B-RASP) that serves as a bidirectional bridge between masked hard-attention transformers and linear temporal logic (LTL). While prior work on transformer expressivity (Strobl et al., 2024b) has produced numerous one-sided bounds, and Hao et al. (2022) / Barcelo et al. (2024) established matching upper (AC0) and lower (LTL[Mon]) bounds for non-masked hard-attention transformers with position embeddings, this paper delivers the first exact characterization for the masked setting by proving equivalence with star-free languages. The Krohn-Rhodes decomposition of counter-free automata (Maler, 2010) provides an alternative proof path, and the depth hierarchy result imports the LTL since-hierarchy of Etessami and Wilke (2000) directly into the transformer setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The exact characterization resolves the open question left by Barcelo et al. (2024) about whether hard-attention transformers with position embeddings are equivalent to LTL[Mon], and provides a clean, complete picture of what masked hard-attention transformers can and cannot do. The B-RASP intermediate language (with a public simulator) gives researchers a practical tool for reasoning about transformer capabilities without working directly in either the transformer or LTL formalism. Key limitations include the restriction to hard (not softmax) attention and the finite-image constraint on position embeddings, which does not exactly match standard sinusoidal embeddings; extending these results to softmax attention remains the central open question the paper explicitly flags.
