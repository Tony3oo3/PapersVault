## Problem

Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) achieves conditional computation via softmax routing and TopK hard sparsification, but these operations are incompatible with Spiking Neural Networks, which rely on binary spike-driven computation without multiplication or complex nonlinear functions. No prior work has adapted MoE to Spiking Transformers, leaving a gap in how to achieve dynamic, sparse conditional computation within the SNN paradigm.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240322/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts 2D image sequences (RGB images at 224×224 for ImageNet, 32×32 for CIFAR) or event-based DVS streams, which are converted into binary spike sequences via a Convolution-BatchNorm-LIF patch-splitting module. The output is a class label produced by a linear classification head applied to globally pooled spike features. All intermediate representations remain in binary spike form {0,1}^{T×N×D} across T time steps, N patches, and D channels, with no floating-point activations between layers.

## Method

The paper introduces the Spiking Experts Mixture Mechanism (SEMM), which replaces ANN-MoE's softmax+TopK routing with a spiking router (Linear-BatchNorm-LIF) that outputs a binary spike sequence, then combines it with expert outputs via element-wise Hadamard product and summation—operations that are purely spike-driven and avoid multiplication. Two instantiations are developed: EMSA treats each attention head as an expert and uses a temporal-aware spiking router to selectively integrate head-wise attention outputs, while EMSP implements channel-wise experts within the MLP using a 3×3 depthwise convolution per expert channel and a spiking router for channel-level gating. The spiking router's variable firing rate (e.g., ~0.5 for EMSA, decreasing from 0.25 to 0.02 across blocks for EMSP) provides data-dependent dynamic sparsity that replaces the fixed TopK selection, and the overall parameter and operation counts remain comparable to the original SSA and MLP blocks.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K with 4 time steps, SD-Transformer-8-512+SEMM achieves 76.62% top-1 accuracy, surpassing its baseline (74.57%) by 2.05% and outperforming the strongest non-SEMM Spiking Transformer (Spikingformer-8-512 at 74.79%). On CIFAR-10, Spikingformer+SEMM reaches 96.16% and Spike-Driven Transformer+SEMM reaches 96.12%, both setting SOTA among Spiking Transformers. On neuromorphic datasets, SEMM achieves SOTA on CIFAR10-DVS (82.90%) and DVS128 Gesture (99.30%), with consistent 1–2% gains over all baselines across the five evaluated datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SEMM consistently outperforms all three Spiking Transformer baselines it is embedded into: Spikformer (73.38% → 75.93%, +2.55%), SD-Transformer (74.57% → 76.62%, +2.05%), and Spikingformer (74.79% → 76.03%, +1.24%) on ImageNet, while using fewer parameters (28.22M vs. 29.68M). Against non-Transformer SNN baselines, SD-Transformer+SEMM (76.62%) surpasses MS-ResNet-104 (76.02%) and SEW-ResNet-152 (69.26%) on ImageNet. On CIFAR-10, Spikingformer+SEMM (96.16%) beats DSR (95.40%), Dspike (94.30%), and Spikformer (95.19%); on CIFAR-100, Spike-Driven Transformer+SEMM (80.26%) exceeds DSR (78.50%) and Spikformer (77.86%). On neuromorphic data, Spikformer+SEMM (82.90% on CIFAR10-DVS) and Spike-Driven Transformer+SEMM (99.30% on DVS128 Gesture) both achieve SOTA, outperforming PLIF (74.80%/97.60%) and Dspike (75.40%) by substantial margins. The paper does not report any comparison where SEMM underperforms its corresponding baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two research threads: Spiking Transformers (Spikformer, Spike-driven Transformer, Spikingformer) and Mixture-of-Experts (Sparsely-Gated MoE, Switch Transformer). It departs from standard ANN-MoE by arguing that softmax-based routing and TopK hard sparsification are fundamentally incompatible with SNN computation principles (no multiplication, inherent sparsity, event-driven operation). The key conceptual move is reformulating MoE's conditional computation as a spiking router whose binary output performs element-wise masking (Hadamard product) over expert outputs, yielding the Spiking Experts Mixture Mechanism (SEMM) with two instantiations: head-wise routing in attention (EMSA) and channel-wise routing in MLP (EMSP).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SEMM provides the first principled SNN-compatible MoE paradigm, enabling dynamic sparse conditional computation without the fixed TopK overhead or load-imbalance issues of ANN-MoE, which is directly relevant to neuromorphic hardware deployment. However, the improvements are modest (~2% on ImageNet), no error bars are reported, and the hardware deployment discussion remains theoretical rather than demonstrated on actual chips. Open questions include whether SEMM scales to much larger Spiking Transformers (beyond 8 blocks / 512 channels), whether the dynamic sparsity advantage translates to measurable energy savings on real neuromorphic hardware, and whether the approach generalizes to non-vision tasks like language modeling.
