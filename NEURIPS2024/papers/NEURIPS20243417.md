## Problem

Spatio-temporal prediction models suffer from data scarcity due to expensive sensor deployment, and existing causal intervention methods like NuwaDynamics rely on backdoor adjustment with exponential complexity O(T×N_E^M(*)) that can generate physically implausible data distributions. The paper seeks a lower-complexity causal framework that identifies which spatial regions truly drive predictions and selectively augments only the non-causal (environmental) regions without disrupting the data's intrinsic distribution.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243417/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a spatio-temporal observation sequence {X_t}_{t=1}^T where each frame X_t ∈ R^{H×W×C_in} is partitioned into N=HW/p² patches, and produces augmented sequences in which environmental patches are replaced by diffusion-generated content while causal patches remain pixel-identical to the original. The binary causal mask is derived from aggregated ViT attention scores (top-ε% patches are causal), and the augmented sequences are sampled at probability p with r generated copies per time step to form the training set for downstream backbones.

## Method

CaPaint operates in two stages: (1) causal deciphering via a self-supervised ViT reconstruction task, where column-summed attention weights across all heads are normalized into a per-patch importance score, and the lowest-scoring patches are designated as environmental; (2) causal intervention via a fine-tuned DDPM performing RePaint-style inpainting, where the reverse diffusion process preserves causal patches (X_{t-1}^{cau} = √(ᾱ_t)X_0 + (1-ᾱ_t)ε) and denoises environmental patches (X_{t-1}^{env} = (1/√α_t)(X_t - β_t/√(1-ᾱ_t)·ε_θ(X_t,t) + σ_t z)), then merges them via a binary mask. The theoretical justification is the front-door adjustment from Pearl's do-calculus, which routes the causal effect through a surrogate variable X̃_C* generated by the diffusion model, reducing temporal complexity from O(T×N_E^M(*)) to O(T×N_E) and avoiding the generation of non-existent distributions inherent in backdoor traversal.

## Results

CaPaint achieves consistent improvements of 3.7%–77.3% in MAE/MSE across all five ST benchmarks (TaxiBJ+, KTH, SEVIR, DRS, FireSys) and seven backbone models (ViT, Earthfarsser, MmVP, ConvLSTM, PredRNN-V2, MAU, SimVP). The largest gain is on DRS with ViT, where MSE drops from 6.21 to 1.41 (77.3% reduction). In direct comparison with other augmentation strategies (Table 2), CaPaint attains the lowest MAE on every dataset, e.g., 12.87 on TaxiBJ+ and 1.57 on DRS, outperforming the strongest prior method NuwaDynamics (15.11 and 2.02 respectively).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is NuwaDynamics [82], which uses backdoor adjustment with Gaussian sampling to perturb environmental patches. CaPaint outperforms NuwaDynamics on all five datasets in MAE: DRS (1.57 vs 2.02, 22.3% lower), KTH (20.56 vs 22.32, 7.9% lower), SEVIR (14.63 vs 15.14, 3.4% lower), TaxiBJ+ (12.87 vs 15.11, 14.8% lower), and FireSys (15.79 vs 16.68, 5.3% lower). Traditional geometric augmentations (Flip, Rotate, Crop) perform at or below the no-augmentation baseline on most datasets, confirming that they disrupt ST data properties; for example, on FireSys, Rotate actually increases MAE from 17.01 to 17.07. CaPaint's advantage is most pronounced on DRS and TaxiBJ+, where the front-door diffusion inpainting generates more distributionally faithful samples than NuwaDynamics' exhaustive environmental traversal, while also reducing computational complexity from O(T×N_E^M) to O(T×N_E).

## Relation to Prior Work

CaPaint extends the causal ST modeling line initiated by NuwaDynamics [82], which first decomposed ST sequences into causal and non-causal (environmental) patches and used backdoor adjustment with mixup to augment data. The key departure is replacing backdoor adjustment (which traverses all environmental variables at O(T × N_E^M(*)) complexity) with front-door adjustment from Pearl's do-calculus [51], reducing complexity to O(T × N_E) by conditioning a diffusion inpainting model (RePaint-style [42]) on the identified causal patches. Causal patch identification itself is achieved via attention maps from a self-supervised ViT reconstruction, avoiding explicit causal discovery tools that suffer from the curse of dimensionality on high-dimensional ST data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CaPaint offers a practical, architecture-agnostic plug-in that addresses the data-scarcity problem in ST prediction (expensive sensor deployment, uneven coverage) while simultaneously providing interpretability through attention-based causal patch visualization. The complexity reduction from exponential to quasi-linear is a meaningful theoretical contribution that makes causal ST augmentation feasible at scale. However, the paper acknowledges that gains diminish when training data is already abundant, and the method is currently limited to single-channel or fixed-channel ST data; exploring arbitrary-channel generation and alternative generative priors remains open. The finding that conventional augmentations (flip, rotate, crop) can degrade ST prediction performance, while causal-aware inpainting consistently helps, suggests the field needs augmentation strategies that respect spatio-temporal structure.
