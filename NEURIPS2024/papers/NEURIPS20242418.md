## Problem

No-regret reinforcement learning in continuous state-action spaces remains largely unsolved: existing approaches either require restrictive parametric assumptions (e.g., LQR, Linear MDPs) or suffer from an unavoidable exponential dependence on the horizon H in the regret (Lipschitz and Weakly Smooth MDPs), rendering them infeasible. The paper identifies local linearity as the structural property that simultaneously enables sublinear regret and polynomial-in-H feasibility, and formalizes it through a new representation class.

## Input / Output

At each step h of episode k, the algorithm receives the current state s_h^k and uses a known feature map φ_h: Z → R^{d_h} together with a known partition U_h of the state-action space Z into N_h regions (indexed by ρ_h). It outputs an action a_h^k chosen to maximize a piecewise-linear optimistic Q-function Q_h[θ̄_h](z) = φ_h(z)ᵀ θ̄_{h,ρ_h(z)}, where the parameter vector θ̄_{h,n} is region-specific. The Q-function is computed by solving a constrained optimization program (ridge regression plus an uncertainty bonus) at the start of each episode, rather than by a simple closed-form update.

## Method

The paper introduces Locally Linearizable MDPs, a representation class where the Bellman optimality operator's output is approximately linear in a feature map φ_h within each region of a partition U_h, with a region-dependent parameter θ_{h,n} drawn from a compact set B_{h,n}. The CINDERELLA algorithm extends ELEANOR by dividing the design matrix and ridge-regression estimates across regions, so that parameters for different regions are learned independently; this removes the √N_h factor from the linear-in-K regret term that would otherwise arise from a naive feature-extension trick. For continuous spaces, the paper defines Mildly Smooth MDPs (Bellman operator bounded on L∞ → C^ν) and shows they are a special case of Locally Linearizable MDPs by choosing an ε-cover partition and a Taylor-polynomial feature map of degree ν_* = ⌈ν−1⌉, yielding an inherent Bellman error I ≤ 2C_T ε^ν. The resulting regret bound R_K = Õ(H d_{ν_*} K^{(ν+2d)/(2ν+2d)} + H^{(2ν+2d)/ν}) is non-vacuous for all finite d, ν, and achieves √K regret as ν → ∞, outperforming all prior state-of-the-art bounds for Strongly Smooth and Kernelized MDPs.

## Results

The main theoretical result (Theorem 4) shows that CINDERELLA achieves regret Õ(H d_{ν*} K^{(ν+2d)/(2ν+2d)} + H^{(2ν+2d)/ν}) on Mildly Smooth MDPs of smoothness order ν in dimension d. The exponent (ν+2d)/(2ν+2d) is always in (1/2, 1), guaranteeing the no-regret property in every regime, and converges to 1/2 as ν→∞. Crucially, the bound is polynomial in H (not exponential), making it the first non-vacuous regret guarantee for Strongly Smooth and Kernelized MDPs in all regimes of d and ν. For the large-ν regime (ν > log K), Corollary 5 yields the simplified bound Õ(H log(K)^d K^{1/2} + H^2).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CINDERELLA outperforms LEGENDRE-ELEANOR [25] on Mildly Smooth MDPs, improving the exponent from (ν+3d/2)/(2ν+d) to (ν+2d)/(2ν+2d) (e.g., for ν=1, d=2: 0.833 vs 1.0, where the baseline is vacuous). Against GOLF [17], CINDERELLA improves from (2ν+3d)/(4ν) to (ν+2d)/(2ν+2d) (e.g., ν=1, d=1: 0.75 vs 1.25, where GOLF is vacuous). For Strongly Smooth MDPs, CINDERELLA improves over LEGENDRE-LSVI [25] from (ν+2d)/(2ν+d) to (ν+2d)/(2ν+2d), eliminating the vacuity for d>ν. For Kernelized MDPs, CINDERELLA improves over KOVI [41] from (ν+3d/2)/(2ν+d) to (ν+2d)/(2ν+2d). NET-Q-LEARNING [34] achieves a slightly better exponent for the specific case ν=1 (Lipschitz-level smoothness), but does not apply to Mildly Smooth MDPs and cannot exploit higher smoothness, whereas CINDERELLA recovers √K regret as ν→∞.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the ELEANOR algorithm and the low inherent Bellman error framework [42] by introducing spatial locality: the state-action space is partitioned into regions, each with its own linear parameter, enabling the Bellman optimality operator to be approximated by region-dependent linear functions. The key conceptual departure from prior smoothness-based approaches (Weakly/Strongly Smooth MDPs [25], Kernelized MDPs [41]) is the identification of 'Mildly Smooth' MDPs—where the Bellman operator maps L^∞ to C^ν—as the largest known class admitting polynomial-in-H regret, bridging the gap between the infeasible Weakly Smooth family and the more restrictive Strongly Smooth family. The Taylor polynomial feature map connects to the continuous bandit literature [16, 23], while the overall optimistic ridge-regression structure is inherited from ELEANOR, with the critical improvement being independent per-region estimation that removes the √N_h dependence on the number of regions in the approximation-error term.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work provides the first non-vacuous regret bound for Strongly Smooth and Kernelized MDPs across all regimes of d and ν, and identifies a clean theoretical boundary (Mildly Smooth) separating feasible from infeasible continuous MDPs. The regret exponent (ν+2d)/(2ν+2d) strictly dominates all prior results and achieves √K as ν→∞, resolving a key open question about whether smoothness can be fully exploited. Limitations include exponential dependence on d (though argued to be unavoidable even for bandits), a potentially large lower-order H^((2ν+2d)/ν) term when d≫ν, and computational inefficiency inherited from ELEANOR's optimization-based approach. Open questions include whether the computational bottleneck can be resolved and whether the Mildly Smooth boundary is truly tight or whether larger feasible classes exist.
