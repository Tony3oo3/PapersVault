## Problem

In sparse linear bandits, the learner wants regret depending on the number S of nonzero coefficients in the reward vector θ*, but prior sparsity-agnostic results either required additional assumptions on the action set (stochastic generation, fixed unit sphere, nestedness of θ*) or on the sparsity structure. No algorithm was known to achieve sparse regret bounds when S is unknown and the action sets are adaptively chosen by an adversary, leaving a gap between the OFUL bound Õ(d√T) and the optimal Õ(√(SdT)) achievable when S is known.

## Input / Output

Each round the algorithm receives an action set A_t ⊂ R^d (with ‖a‖₂ ≤ 1 for all a ∈ A_t) that may be adaptively chosen by an adversary based on the learner's past random choices. The algorithm is parameterized by a time horizon T, a number of models n = Θ(log d), and a probability distribution q = {q_s}_{s∈[n]} over confidence set indices. It outputs a chosen arm A_t ∈ A_t per round and guarantees expected regret bounds that depend on the unknown sparsity S and the suboptimality gap Δ, without requiring the learner to know S in advance.

## Method

SparseLinUCB builds a hierarchy of nested confidence sets C_t^i with radii α_i = 2^i log T (i = 1,…,n) using the online-to-confidence-set conversion of Abbasi-Yadkori et al. with SeqSEW as the base online regression algorithm. In each round, it draws an index I_t from a fixed distribution q (chosen proportional to 2^{-i}) and plays the optimistic arm A_t^{I_t} maximizing ⟨a, θ̂_{t-1}⟩ + ‖a‖_{V_{t-1}^{-1}}√α_{I_t}. The key technical insight is a peeling argument over blocks defined by det(V_t) growth: for 'good' rounds (I_t ≥ o, where o is the critical safe index with α_o = O(S log T)), standard UCB analysis applies, while for 'bad' rounds (I_t < o), the exploration in good rounds ensures det(V_t) grows fast enough to bound the accumulated regret by Õ(√(SdT/Q)). AdaLinUCB replaces the fixed distribution q with Exp3 to adaptively update confidence set probabilities based on observed rewards, achieving a Õ(√T) regret bound (improving on prior Õ(T^{2/3}) for similar approaches) while empirically outperforming OFUL.

## Results

The paper's main theoretical contribution is SparseLinUCB, which achieves a sparsity-agnostic regret bound of $\widetilde{O}(S\sqrt{dT})$ under adaptive adversarial action sets with no additional assumptions (no nestedness, no stochastic action sets, no compatibility conditions). This is the first such result; prior sparsity-agnostic bounds either required nested sparsity structure (Cutkosky et al. [8], Pacchiano et al. [25]) or stochastic/fixed action sets (Oh et al. [22], Dai et al. [9]). The algorithm also provides an instance-dependent bound of $\widetilde{O}(\max\{d^2, S^2d\}/\Delta)$, and when S is known, recovers the minimax-optimal $\widetilde{O}(\sqrt{SdT})$. In synthetic experiments (d=16, |A|=30, T=10⁴), AdaLinUCB (the Exp3-based variant) outperforms OFUL across all sparsity levels S∈{1,2,4,8,16}, while SparseLinUCB_Theory is competitive with OFUL.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary experimental baseline is OFUL [1], the standard optimistic linear UCB algorithm. In synthetic experiments (d=16, 30 arms on the unit sphere, T=10⁴, 20 repetitions), AdaLinUCB (both uniform and theory-driven priors) consistently achieves lower cumulative regret than OFUL across all sparsity levels S∈{1,2,4,8,16}, with the gap widening at smaller S. SparseLinUCB_Theory (using the geometric prior q_s ∝ 2⁻ˢ) performs comparably to OFUL, while SparseLinUCB_Unif (uniform prior) is slightly worse. Theoretically, the paper's SparseLinUCB is the first method to simultaneously achieve sparsity-agnostic regret, handle adaptive adversarial action sets, and require no additional structural assumptions (nestedness, compatibility, stochastic actions, or smoothed adversaries). Compared to prior sparsity-agnostic results that achieve $\widetilde{O}(S^2\sqrt{T})$ under nestedness (Cutkosky et al. [8], Pacchiano et al. [25]), this paper improves the dependence to $\widetilde{O}(S\sqrt{dT})$ while removing the nestedness requirement. The AdaLinUCB variant improves upon the $\widetilde{O}(T^{2/3})$ regret bound of prior Exp3-based model selection approaches [11, 24] to $\widetilde{O}(\sqrt{T})$, though its bound is not tighter than SparseLinUCB's due to the forced exploration parameter q.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the online-to-confidence-set conversion framework of Abbasi-Yadkori et al. [2], which links confidence set radii to the regret of an underlying online linear regression algorithm (here, SeqSEW for sparse regression). Prior sparse linear bandit results either required knowing the sparsity level S, or imposed structural assumptions on the action set (stochastic generation, fixed unit sphere) or on the sparsity pattern (nestedness: first S coordinates nonzero). The key departure is a randomized model selection strategy over a hierarchy of nested confidence sets with geometrically increasing radii, combined with a peeling argument over determinant growth, to handle the case where S is unknown and actions are adaptively adversarial. AdaLinUCB further extends this by replacing the fixed distribution over radii with an Exp3-based adaptive scheme, connecting the bandit model selection literature (Ding et al., Pacchiano et al.) to the sparse linear bandit setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to achieve sparsity-agnostic regret bounds for linear bandits under fully adversarial action sets with no assumptions on the sparsity structure, closing a gap explicitly noted as surprising in the literature. The achieved bound Õ(S√(dT)) is within a √S factor of the known lower bound Ω(√(SdT)), and the paper identifies open questions about whether the lower bound can be strengthened in the agnostic case. The instance-dependent bound retains a d²/Δ term independent of S, and the AdaLinUCB analysis is pessimistic due to a forced exploration parameter, leaving room for tighter theoretical guarantees that would better reflect the algorithm's empirical advantage over OFUL.
