## Problem

Creating realistic 3D avatars from a single RGB image remains challenging because template-based methods fail on loose clothing, deterministic reconstruction produces blurry textures in occluded regions, and multi-view diffusion models lack guaranteed 3D consistency across views. The paper identifies that 2D multi-view diffusion and 3D reconstruction models provide complementary information—large-scale shape priors versus explicit geometric consistency—but prior work treats them as separate stages, losing the mutual benefits of tight coupling.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243159/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image of a person (plus a fixed text prompt 'Photorealistic 3D human') and produces a 3D Gaussian Splatting representation that can be rendered from arbitrary viewpoints in real time. The multi-view diffusion backbone operates at 256×256 resolution, which limits fine texture detail. A textured triangle mesh can additionally be extracted via Gaussian Opacity Fields, PIFu-HD normal estimation, Bilateral Normal Integration, and TSDF fusion from 36 rendered views. Inference takes 22.6 seconds on an A100 GPU with 11.7 GB VRAM.

## Method

Human-3Diffusion tightly couples a 2D multi-view diffusion model (ImageDream) with a novel 3D Gaussian Splatting generative model (g_φ, an asymmetric U-Net Transformer with Plücker ray embeddings) through two mechanisms. First, at each diffusion timestep t, the one-step clean estimate x̃_0^tgt from the 2D model is fed as an additional condition to g_φ, injecting large-scale 2D shape priors into 3D generation. Second, during reverse sampling, the 3D-consistent renderings of the predicted Gaussians replace the 2D model's clean estimates in the posterior mean computation (Eq. 9), refining the sampling trajectory to prevent 3D inconsistency accumulation. Both models are jointly trained end-to-end on ~6000 high-quality human scans using a combined loss of 2D diffusion MSE and 3D-GS rendering losses (MSE, VGG-19 perceptual, and geometry regularization), with the 2D model initialized from pretrained ImageDream weights.

## Results

On the combined CAPE, Sizer, and IIIT evaluation sets, Human-3Diffusion achieves a Chamfer Distance of 1.41 cm and F-score of 0.557, substantially outperforming the next-best geometry baseline PIFu (CD 2.75 cm, F-score 0.359). For appearance, it attains LPIPS 0.058, PSNR 21.61 dB, and FID 8.45, beating the strongest appearance competitor LGM_human (LPIPS 0.088, FID 14.22) by a wide margin. The method also reports the best Normal Consistency (0.791) and SSIM (0.916) across all compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Human-3Diffusion outperforms all seven baselines on every reported metric. Among template-based methods, ECON is the closest geometry competitor (CD 2.83 cm, F-score 0.291) but still lags by nearly 2× in F-score and 2× in CD; SiTH and SIFU produce blurry textures in unseen regions due to their deterministic regression formulation. The template-free PIFu achieves the best geometry among non-generative baselines (CD 2.75 cm) but its FID of 29.57 reveals poor texture realism in occluded regions. The strongest overall competitor is LGM_human (LGM fine-tuned on human scans), which benefits from 2D diffusion priors and achieves FID 14.22, yet it still trails Human-3Diffusion by 41% in FID and 51% in F-score, confirming that the tight 2D–3D coupling and trajectory refinement provide a clear advantage over the decoupled multi-view-then-reconstruct pipeline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two lines of work: reconstruction-based human digitization (PIFu, SiTH, SIFU, ICON, ECON) and multi-view diffusion for 3D generation (ImageDream, SyncDreamer, LGM). It departs from prior reconstruction methods by being generative rather than deterministic, and from prior multi-view diffusion pipelines (e.g., LGM, InstantMesh) by tightly coupling the 2D diffusion model and the 3D-GS generator at every sampling timestep rather than treating them as sequential stages. The 3D-GS generative model architecture is adapted from LGM's asymmetric U-Net Transformer, and the paradigm of diffusing rendered images of a differentiable 3D representation follows RenderDiffusion and HoloDiffusion, but the bidirectional information flow—2D priors conditioning 3D generation and 3D renderings refining 2D sampling—is the key architectural departure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The tight 2D-3D coupling framework offers a general recipe for improving 3D consistency in diffusion-based generation that could extend beyond human avatars to arbitrary objects and human-object interactions, as the authors note. The 256×256 resolution of the underlying multi-view diffusion model limits texture sharpness, and challenging poses remain a weakness, pointing to the need for higher-resolution MVD backbones and synthetic pose augmentation. The framework's practical efficiency (22.6 s, 11.7 GB VRAM on A100) makes it viable for large-scale avatar generation, and the open question of whether the coupling strategy transfers to compositional scenes (e.g., people with bags, tools) is left for future work.
