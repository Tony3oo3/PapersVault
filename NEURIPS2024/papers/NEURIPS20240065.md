## Problem

Existing multiobjective optimization benchmarks focus almost exclusively on evolutionary (zeroth-order) algorithms that cannot leverage gradient information or scale to neural networks with millions of parameters. There is no standard, GPU-accelerated library for benchmarking the growing family of gradient-based MOO methods (e.g., EPO, PMTL, MOO-SVGD, HVGrad) on large-scale machine learning tasks such as multi-task learning and fairness.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240065/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The library consumes multi-objective loss functions (e.g., cross-entropy + DEO fairness loss), a preference vector on the m-dimensional simplex, and a neural network with up to millions of parameters, along with a dataset. MOO solvers output a finite set of K Pareto solutions; PSL solvers output a single hypernetwork θ_φ(·): Δ_m → R^n that maps any preference to a Pareto solution in one forward pass; MOBO solvers output batches of query points for expensive black-box evaluations. All solvers are GPU-accelerated and support both first-order (backpropagation) and zero-order (evolutionary strategy) gradient estimation.

## Method

LibMOON is a modular PyTorch library organized around three solver categories unified by a common gradient-manipulation interface. MOO solvers either optimize aggregation functions (linear scalarization, Tchebycheff, PBI, COSMOS, Smooth Tchebycheff) via standard backpropagation, or compute a dynamic weight vector α̃ at each iteration (solving an LP/QP as in EPO or PMTL) and backpropagate through a generalized aggregation function g̃_λ(L(θ)) = Σ α̃_i L_i(θ). PSL solvers train a hypernetwork parameterized by φ to minimize E_{λ~Dir(p)}[g̃_λ(L(θ_φ(λ)))] using the chain-rule decomposition ∂ℓ_psl/∂φ = α̃ · B · C, where B is the objective Jacobian (via BP or ES) and C is the hypernetwork Jacobian. MOBO solvers build per-objective Gaussian process surrogates and optimize preference-conditional acquisition functions (TLCB, DirHV-EI) over the preference simplex to generate batch queries. The modular design allows new solvers to be added by implementing only the gradient-manipulation step, and the entire pipeline runs on GPU for problems with n up to millions.

## Results

On the VLMOP2 synthetic problem (non-convex Pareto front), HVGrad achieves the highest hypervolume (0.286), while Agg-COSMOS attains the best preference-alignment metrics (Cross Angle 1.085°, PBI 1.011). On the Adult fairness-classification MTL problem (28K parameters), HVGrad again leads in hypervolume (0.678) and Agg-SmoothmTche achieves the best PBI (0.500). For Pareto Set Learning on MO-MNIST (3.24M parameters), Agg-LS-based PSL significantly outperforms all other PSL variants in HV (0.557 vs. 0.512–0.538 for alternatives), confirming its suitability for convex PFs. GPU acceleration on RTX 4090 reduces PSL training time to 3.27 min vs. 43.43 min on CPU (~13× speedup).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the non-convex VLMOP2 problem, Agg-LS [37] and Random [41] fail catastrophically (HV ≈ 0.043–0.044), finding only PF endpoints or a single point, while EPO [16], PMGDA [40], and Agg-Tche [30] all achieve HV ≈ 0.283 with well-aligned solutions. HVGrad [38] achieves the highest hypervolume (0.286) but at the cost of poor preference alignment (Cross Angle 34.1°, PBI 3.06), whereas Agg-COSMOS [34] achieves the best alignment (Cross Angle 1.09°, PBI 1.01) with near-optimal HV (0.285). MGDA-UB [12] and MOO-SVGD [17] exhibit large variance and suboptimal HV (0.228 and 0.212 respectively), with MOO-SVGD's conflicting diversity/convergence objectives causing instability. On the Adult MTL problem, Agg-mTche [42] and Agg-SmoothmTche [22] perform best among aggregation methods (HV 0.674–0.675), while HVGrad [38] leads overall (HV 0.678). For PSL on MO-MNIST, Agg-LS [37] dominates (HV 0.557) over EPO-based [16] and Agg-Tche [30] PSL variants (HV 0.533–0.538), and in MOBO experiments, PSL-MOBO and PSL-DirHV-EI [23] converge rapidly across ZDT1, RE21, VLMOP1, and VLMOP2 benchmarks.

## Relation to Prior Work

LibMOON departs from the dominant paradigm in MOO libraries (PlatEMO, Pymoo, jMetal, Pagmo), which center on evolutionary/zeroth-order algorithms, by providing the first unified, GPU-accelerated PyTorch framework for gradient-based MOO methods. It builds directly on the Pareto Set Learning (PSL) formulation from Navon et al. (2020) and the expensive-MOP extension from Lin et al. (2022), unifying over twenty solvers—including aggregation-based (LS, Tchebycheff, PBI, COSMOS), gradient-manipulation (EPO, PMTL, MOO-SVGD, HVGrad, PMGDA), and MOBO methods—under a modular architecture. Conceptually, the paper also distinguishes itself from LibMTL, which seeks a single network benefiting all tasks, by explicitly modeling and exploring the Pareto trade-off surface.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By providing the first gradient-based MOO benchmark with GPU acceleration for large-scale (millions of parameters) neural network tasks, LibMOON fills a critical gap: evolutionary methods cannot leverage higher-order gradient information or scale to modern deep learning. The empirical findings offer practical guidance—e.g., Agg-Tche is recommended for non-convex synthetic PFs while Agg-LS suffices for convex MTL PFs—helping practitioners avoid costly trial-and-error. Limitations include the rapid pace of new gradient-based MOO methods making comprehensive coverage difficult, and the known failure mode of gradient-based solvers on problems with many local optima. Open questions include whether PSL with LoRA-based architectures can match full-parameter PSL at scale, and how to maintain the library as the field evolves quickly.
