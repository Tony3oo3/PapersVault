## Problem

Reward models trained on human preference data suffer from poor out-of-distribution generalization, causing reward over-optimization where proxy scores increase while true alignment degrades during RLHF. Existing mitigations focus on constraining policy optimization or training expensive ensembles of reward models, leaving the fundamental issue of feature distortion during preference fine-tuning unaddressed.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241989/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes prompt-response preference pairs (x, y_c, y_r) from datasets like Unified-Feedback, with inputs truncated to 1024 tokens for LoRA training or 4096 for full-parameter training. It produces two outputs from shared hidden states: a scalar reward score via a reward head (linear or two-layer MLP) and next-token probabilities via the retained language model head, which serves purely as a regularizer during training and is discarded at inference.

## Method

GRM retains the base model's LM head alongside a newly initialized reward head, both operating on shared hidden states, and trains them jointly with a weighted combination of the Bradley-Terry reward loss and a text-generation regularization loss (L_total = (1-α)L_reward + αL_reg). The regularization is derived from an adversarial min-max formulation where the reward model is trained against an adversarial policy with KL regularization, yielding closed-form DPO and SFT loss terms as natural regularizers. Three practical variants are implemented—DPO regularization, DPO without reference model, and SFT regularization—where SFT regularization (maximizing log-probability of chosen responses) proves most effective and stable, especially under limited data. The key mechanism is that the text-generation loss anchors the hidden states to preserve their pretrained text-generation capabilities, preventing the randomly initialized reward head from distorting features in ways that harm OOD generalization.

## Results

On the 400K Unified-Feedback training set with gemma-2B-it, GRM w/ SFT regularization achieves 79.8% on HHH-Alignment (vs. 73.4% baseline, +6.4), 73.4% on MT-Bench (vs. 71.2%, +2.2), and 73.2% on the in-distribution Unified-Feedback eval (vs. 72.1%, +1.1). With only 40K training data, the OOD advantage widens: GRM w/ SFT reaches 78.7% on HHH-Alignment (vs. 70.3% baseline, +8.4) and 73.0% on MT-Bench (vs. 69.1%, +3.9). At scale, full-parameter GRM on LLaMA-3-8B-Instruct achieves 87.0 average on RewardBench, surpassing GPT-4 (85.9) and Starling-RM-34B (82.7).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GRM consistently outperforms all baselines on OOD tasks. Against the margin loss [10, 39] and label smoothing [39] baselines, GRM w/ SFT gains +4.8 and +7.7 points on HHH-Alignment respectively (79.8 vs. 75.0 and 72.1), while these baselines show inconsistent or negative effects on MT-Bench. The ensemble baseline [18] (3 models, average aggregation) improves over the vanilla classifier (76.8 vs. 73.4 on HHH-Alignment) but still trails GRM by 3.0 points and requires 3× the training cost. On RewardBench, GRM (linear, Mistral-7B) achieves 79.5 average, beating Starling-RM-7B [50] (76.2), UltraRM-13B [51] (69.8), and PairRM [49] (58.7); at 8B scale, GRM (87.0) surpasses both GPT-4 (85.9) and the same-base-model FsfairX-LLaMA3-RM-8B [40] (84.7). In RLHF experiments, GRM mitigates overoptimization in both BoN and PPO, where baseline proxy scores keep rising while gold scores plateau or drop, whereas GRM maintains aligned proxy and gold score trajectories.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the theoretical observation (Kumar et al., 2022) that randomly initialized task heads distort pretrained features and degrade OOD generalization, applying this insight to the reward modeling setting in RLHF. It adapts the adversarial min-max formulation from recent DPO-robustness work (Liu et al., 2024; Cen et al., 2024) to derive text-generation regularization terms, but departs from prior overoptimization mitigations (ensembles, constrained policy optimization, label smoothing) by regularizing a single reward model's hidden states through a retained LM head rather than modifying the policy or training multiple models. The Bradley-Terry reward loss and PPO/BoN evaluation framework follow standard RLHF practice (Ouyang et al., 2022; Stiennon et al., 2020).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GRM offers a drop-in, low-cost regularization (essentially adding an SFT loss on the LM head) that consistently improves OOD reward model accuracy and mitigates overoptimization in both BoN and PPO without requiring ensembles, extra data, or policy-side constraints. The 8B full-parameter GRM outperforming GPT-4 as a judge on RewardBench (87.0 vs. 85.9) suggests the approach scales well, though the paper acknowledges it has not been tested beyond 10B parameters. Open questions include whether the theoretical derivation's relaxation (decoupling r_θ from π*_θ) is tight, whether the method transfers to other alignment paradigms beyond Bradley-Terry reward modeling, and whether the synthetic 25% label noise experiments faithfully capture real-world preference noise.
