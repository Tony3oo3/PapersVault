## Problem

The Rashomon effect and predictive multiplicity have been studied for linear classifiers, decision trees, and neural networks, but gradient boosting—the dominant algorithm for tabular data—has been entirely overlooked. The sequential, iterative structure of gradient boosting raises the question of whether its training pipeline can be exploited to efficiently explore the Rashomon set and mitigate the conflicting predictions that undermine the credibility of machine decisions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243853/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes tabular feature vectors (10–104 features across 18 UCI datasets, ACS Income with ~1.6M samples, COMPAS) or 32×32 RGB images (CIFAR-10) and produces an exponentially larger empirical Rashomon set (m^T models) at inference time while incurring the same m×T training cost as standard re-training. Downstream outputs include per-sample predictive multiplicity metrics (VPR, Rashomon Capacity, disagreement, discrepancy), fairness-accuracy trade-off curves for group-fairness-constrained model selection, and reduced-disagreement predictions via two mitigation strategies (reweighted model selection and intermediate ensembles).

## Method

RashomonGB exploits gradient boosting's decomposition of the original learning problem into T sequential sub-problems (fitting pseudo-residuals at each iteration). Instead of re-training the full boosting pipeline m times, it trains m weak learners per iteration and, at inference, combines them across iterations to yield m^T distinct models—exponential in T—while sharing the same residual from the previous iteration. Theoretically, the paper derives a high-probability bound on the Rashomon set size via mutual information I(S;H) (Proposition 1) and proves that the conditional mutual information between the model and pseudo-residuals is non-decreasing across boosting iterations (Proposition 3), implying the Rashomon set grows with more iterations. For mitigation, two strategies are proposed: (i) model selection with reweighted loss that exponentially rewards models producing below-average per-sample loss, and (ii) intermediate ensembles that average E randomly selected weak learners within each iteration before passing to the next, reducing disagreement at the cost of E× model complexity.

## Results

RashomonGB generates 2^T = 1024 competing models (tabular) or 972 models (CIFAR-10) with the same training cost as 10 re-training runs, consistently outperforming re-training on all four predictive multiplicity metrics (VPR, Rashomon Capacity, disagreement, discrepancy) on ACS Income and Credit Card. For fair model selection on UCI Adult, RashomonGB yields a model with MEO ≈ 0 and <1% accuracy drop, whereas re-training achieves ~84.5% accuracy but violates MEO by 0.01. On 18 UCI datasets, the Intermediate Ensembles (IE) mitigation method reduces 0-disagreement from 0.208 to 0.041 on Epileptic Seizure with only a 0.014±0.002 accuracy change, outperforming both re-training and Model Selection (MS).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline throughout is re-training gradient boosting with different random seeds, which shares the same m×T training cost as RashomonGB but yields only m final models versus m^T from RashomonGB. RashomonGB wins decisively on predictive multiplicity estimation: on ACS Income and Credit Card it produces higher VPR, Rashomon Capacity, disagreement, and discrepancy values under identical loss-deviation constraints, and on Contraception it is the only method that yields non-zero multiplicity estimates when the loss threshold is tight (ε<0.585). For fair model selection, RashomonGB outperforms re-training on both UCI Adult (MEO ≈ 0 vs. 0.01 violation) and COMPAS (lower SP), and its Rashomon set encompasses nearly all models produced by four fairness-intervention baselines—EqOdds, Rejection, Reduction, and FaiRS—without any explicit fairness constraint. In the mitigation experiment on 18 UCI datasets, the proposed Intermediate Ensembles (IE) method outperforms Model Selection (MS) in reducing 0-disagreement while both maintain accuracy comparable to re-training; IE achieves the largest disagreement reductions (e.g., 0.208→0.041 on Epileptic Seizure) at the cost of E=20× model complexity. Compared to prediction-uncertainty methods (NGBoost, PGBM, IBUG) in Appendix E.9, RashomonGB differs in framing: it restricts to models with statistically similar performance (the Rashomon set) rather than aggregating over all re-trained models, avoiding overestimation of arbitrariness from poorly performing models.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Rashomon set and predictive multiplicity literature—which has been developed for linear classifiers (Marx et al.), sparse decision trees (Xin et al.), and CNNs (Hsu et al.)—to gradient boosting, the dominant algorithm for tabular data. Theoretically, it builds on Xu and Raginsky's information-theoretic generalization bounds to derive the first high-probability characterization of the Rashomon set size in terms of mutual information between the dataset and the learning algorithm. Methodologically, it departs from the standard re-training strategy (which searches the Rashomon set linearly) by exploiting gradient boosting's sequential structure to train m weak learners per iteration, yielding an exponential m^T search space at the same training cost.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Since gradient boosting outperforms deep learning on typical tabular data and is widely deployed in high-stakes domains (finance, healthcare, criminal justice), understanding and mitigating predictive multiplicity in this setting has direct practical implications for fairness and decision credibility. The counter-intuitive finding that residual Rashomon sets grow with boosting iterations (Proposition 3) suggests that simply adding more iterations worsens predictive multiplicity, which challenges the common practice of training deeper boosters. Open questions include extending the analysis to adaptive boosting and other ensemble methods, finding optimal strategies for selecting diverse weak learners per iteration, and developing efficient data structures to manage the exponentially many models RashomonGB generates.
