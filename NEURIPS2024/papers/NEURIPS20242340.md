## Problem

Existing methods for constructing prediction intervals under distribution shift (e.g., weighted conformal prediction) focus primarily on achieving a coverage guarantee but do not minimize interval width. This paper asks whether one can simultaneously achieve adequate coverage and minimal width on a target domain under unsupervised domain shift, where only labeled source samples and unlabeled target covariates are available.

## Input / Output

The method consumes labeled source samples (X_S, Y_S), unlabeled target covariates (X_T), a set of K candidate prediction interval estimators (e.g., quantile regression forests, neural networks, random forests, gradient boosting), and either a density ratio estimator ŵ(x) or a transport map estimator T̂₀. The output is a prediction interval of the form [m(x) ± √(λ̂(α)·f̂_init(x))] on the target domain, where m is the conditional mean, f̂_init captures the optimal band shape, and λ̂(α) is a scalar shrinkage factor calibrated to the desired 1−α coverage level. In the model aggregation setting, the function class is a non-negative linear combination of the K candidates, reducing the shape-estimation step to a linear program.

## Method

The core procedure is a two-step algorithm: (1) shape estimation, which solves a convex optimization problem to find the function f̂_init that minimizes target-domain expected width subject to covering the response variable, using a hinge-loss surrogate h_δ(t)=max{0, t/δ+1} to replace the non-convex indicator; and (2) shrinkage, which scales f̂_init by a factor λ̂(α) determined on a held-out data split to hit the target coverage. Under covariate shift, the objective and constraint are weighted by the density ratio ŵ(x); under domain shift, the interval is transported from source to target via an estimated measure-preserving map T̂₀. The aggregation of K candidate methods is formulated as a linear program over non-negative weights. The paper establishes finite-sample concentration bounds showing that the resulting interval achieves nominal coverage with width within O(R_n(F) + estimation error of ŵ or T̂₀) of the optimal achievable width.

## Results

On the primary Airfoil dataset (200 Monte Carlo runs), the proposed method achieves an average coverage of 0.964 (SD=0.04) with an average prediction interval width of 13.65 (SD=2.22), compared to 0.954 (SD=0.036) coverage and 20.53 (SD=4.13) width for weighted conformal prediction (WVAC) — a ~34% reduction in width at comparable or better coverage. On the Appliances Energy Prediction dataset (a real-world time-shift scenario), the method achieves 0.95 coverage with a bandwidth of 461.69, versus 6809.87 for WVAC and 2032.12 for WQC at 1.00 coverage. Across all five datasets, the method consistently produces the narrowest intervals among those meeting the 95% coverage threshold.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two conformal prediction baselines: Weighted Variance-adjusted Conformal (WVAC, Tibshirani et al. 2019) and Weighted Quantile-adjusted Conformal (WQC, Romano et al. 2019). Against WVAC, the proposed method consistently wins on interval width across all five datasets while matching or exceeding coverage — e.g., on Airfoil the width drops from 20.53 to 13.65, and on Appliances Energy Prediction from 6809.87 to 461.69 (a 93% reduction). Against WQC, the comparison is more nuanced: WQC sometimes achieves a smaller median bandwidth (e.g., 2.84 vs. 4.33 on Energy Efficiency), but it sacrifices coverage (0.973 vs. 0.995 median), and when restricted to runs with ≥95% coverage, WQC's bandwidth becomes substantially larger (4.94 vs. 4.37 on Energy Efficiency; 51.03 vs. 40.77 on Real Estate). The robustness experiment (Table 1) further shows that the proposed method's width remains stable (~2.07) as the variance-estimation model overfits (depth 3→15), while WVAC's width nearly doubles (3.08→5.00), confirming that the aggregation framework is resilient to misspecified component estimators.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the prediction interval aggregation framework of [FGM23] (Utopia) from the i.i.d. setting to unsupervised domain shift, where only unlabeled target covariates are available. It draws on two distinct lines of prior work: conformal prediction under covariate shift (Tibshirani et al. 2019, Gibbs & Candès 2021) for the bounded density ratio case, and domain adaptation via optimal transport maps (Courty et al. 2014, 2016, 2017) for the measure-preserving transformation case. The key methodological departure from [FGM23] is the introduction of a hinge-loss convex surrogate to handle the non-convexity induced by the domain shift, combined with a two-step shape-estimation-plus-shrinkage procedure that decouples interval geometry from coverage calibration.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper fills a notable gap: most existing prediction interval methods under distribution shift guarantee coverage but do not minimize width, and this is the first work to provide finite-sample theoretical guarantees for width-minimal prediction intervals under an unsupervised domain-aligning assumption. Practical limitations include the requirement of either a bounded density ratio or a measure-preserving transport map, the added complexity of density ratio estimation, and the fact that all experiments are on tabular datasets with moderate dimensionality. Open questions include whether the approach extends to high-dimensional or image-based settings, how sensitive the guarantees are to violations of the domain-shift assumptions, and whether the convex surrogate introduces meaningful width inflation relative to the intractable non-convex optimum.
