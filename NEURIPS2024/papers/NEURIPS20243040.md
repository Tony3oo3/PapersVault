## Problem

Existing document understanding benchmarks focus on single-page or short multi-page documents, leaving the evaluation of long-context DU (tens to hundreds of pages) largely unexplored. No unified benchmark simultaneously tests localization across lengthy heterogeneous documents, cross-page comprehension requiring evidence from multiple pages, and hallucination detection via unanswerable questions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243040/cropped_page1_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

For LVLM evaluation, each PDF page is rendered as a PNG screenshot at 144 DPI and all pages are fed end-to-end (concatenated into 1–5 images for open-source models, up to 120 individual images for proprietary models like GPT-4o). For LLM baselines, Tesseract OCR extracts text (lossy, losing chart/image information) and feeds it as plain text. The model produces a free-form response, which is then converted to a short-form answer (integer, float, string, or list) by a GPT-4o-based extractor and scored via a rule-based calculator yielding generalized accuracy and F1.

## Method

MMLONGBENCH-DOC is constructed through a three-stage pipeline: (1) collecting 135 PDF documents (avg. 47.5 pages, 21,214 tokens) across 7 domains from four existing datasets (DUDE, SlideVQA, ChartQA, FinanceBench) and new sources (Arxiv, ManualsLib, Google Search); (2) ten expert annotators edit 425 existing questions (revising 32.2%, removing 46.1%) and create 898 new questions, with quantitative quotas ensuring balanced distribution across evidence sources (text, layout, table, chart, image) and types (494 single-page, 365 cross-page, 223 unanswerable); (3) a three-round semi-automatic quality control uses GPT-4o for document-relevance detection (removing 94 samples answerable without the document), self-reflection (flagging 13.8% problematic annotations), and inter-annotator cross-checking (Cohen's κ = 0.42). The evaluation protocol follows a three-step design: unconstrained response generation, LLM-based answer extraction, and rule-based scoring with format-specific strategies (exact match for integers, 1% tolerance for floats, ANLS for strings, element-wise minimum for lists).

## Results

On MMLONGBENCH-DOC (1,082 questions over 135 documents averaging 47.5 pages), the best-performing LVLM GPT-4o achieves only 44.9% F1 (42.8% ACC), while the second-best GPT-4V scores 31.2% F1. All other LVLMs fall below 21% F1, with the best open-source LVLM (InternVL-Chat-v1.5) reaching just 13.0% F1. A human expert baseline achieves 66.0% F1, revealing a >20-point gap over the best model. Notably, 12 of 14 LVLMs perform worse than their LLM counterparts fed with lossy OCR-parsed text.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GPT-4o dominates as the best LVLM (44.9% F1), outperforming GPT-4V (31.2%) by 13.7 points and all other models by wide margins. When comparing the same model in LVLM vs. LLM (OCR) mode, GPT-4o benefits from visual input (+14.4% F1: 44.9% vs. 30.5%) and GPT-4-turbo also improves (+5.3%: 31.2% vs. 25.9%), validating end-to-end multimodal perception. However, Gemini-1.5-Pro (20.6% LVLM vs. 24.8% LLM) and Claude-3-Opus (18.1% vs. 24.5%) both degrade by 4.2 and 6.4 points respectively when switching from OCR text to document screenshots, indicating their visual pipelines are less effective than their text counterparts. Among open-source models, the best LLM (Mixtral 8x22B, 24.7% F1) surpasses the best LVLM (InternVL-Chat-v1.5, 13.0%) by 11.7 points. All remaining open-source LVLMs (InternLM-XC2-4KHD at 9.8%, MiniCPM at 8.6%, Idefics2 at 6.8%, mPLUG-DocOwl at 6.3%, Monkey-Chat at 5.6%, EMU2-Chat at 5.5%, Qwen-VL-Chat at 5.4%, DeepSeek-VL-Chat at 5.4%, CogVLM2 at 4.0%) score below 10% F1, far trailing even the weakest OCR+LLM baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the document understanding (DU) evaluation lineage from single-page benchmarks (DocVQA, ChartQA, InfoVQA) and short multi-page datasets (DUDE, SlideVQA, MP-DocVQA) to a genuinely long-context regime (47.5 pages, 21K tokens average). It draws conceptual inspiration from long-context LLM benchmarks (SCROLLS, LongBench) but is the first to apply that evaluation philosophy to multimodal, PDF-formatted documents with cross-page comprehension requirements. Rather than building on a specific model architecture, it repurposes documents from four existing DU datasets (DUDE, SlideVQA, ChartQA, FinanceBench) and augments them with newly collected documents and expert-annotated questions to create a unified long-context DU benchmark.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals that long-context DU is far from solved: even GPT-4o achieves only 44.9% F1, and 12 of 14 LVLMs underperform LLMs fed with lossy OCR text, challenging the assumption that end-to-end multimodal perception is always superior. This provides a critical, standardized evaluation target for future long-context LVLM development and highlights the specific bottlenecks (localization, cross-page reasoning, hallucination) that remain. Limitations include the relatively small scale (135 documents, 1,082 questions) and the absence of a fine-grained capability taxonomy, which the authors acknowledge as trade-offs for annotation quality. Open questions include whether longer context windows or better multi-image handling will close the gap, and whether the OCR+LLM pipeline's competitive performance signals a fundamental limitation in current LVLM architectures.
