## Problem

While μP parameterization provides stable scaling for MLPs, it was unclear which parameterizations of multi-head self-attention admit well-defined infinite limits (infinite key/query dimension N, infinite heads H, infinite depth L) that still allow attention layers to undergo non-trivial feature learning. The paper identifies the admissible parameterizations and characterizes the distinct statistical descriptions that emerge in each limit, revealing that the N→∞ limit forces all attention heads to collapse to identical dynamics.

## Input / Output

The theoretical analysis takes a randomly initialized transformer with L layers, H heads, and N-dimensional keys/queries per head, along with fixed batch size and training steps, and produces deterministic or stochastic limiting equations for residual stream kernels, attention variables, and output logits. Empirically, the models consume image patches (CIFAR-5M, 32×32) or text token sequences (C4) and produce class labels or next-token distributions, with the scaling exponents α_A ∈ {1, 1/2} and α_L ∈ {1/2, 1} controlling how the architecture is parameterized before taking limits.

## Method

The paper derives a dynamical mean field theory (DMFT) for transformer training via a Martin-Siggia-Rose path integral, tracking correlation and response functions of residual stream, key, query, and value variables throughout SGD training. From the DMFT action, they extract three distinct infinite limits: (1) N→∞ requires α_A=1 (μP scaling) and causes all heads to follow identical dynamics, effectively reducing multi-head attention to single-head; (2) H→∞ at fixed N preserves a non-Gaussian distribution over attention heads throughout training, yielding deterministic head-averaged kernels; (3) L→∞ with α_L=1 allows MHSA weights to evolve by Θ(1) while α_L=1/2 freezes them, creating a tension between preserving initialization structure and enabling feature learning within attention blocks. The key finding is that no single infinite limit captures all aspects of multi-head feature learning simultaneously, and the choice of scaling exponent qualitatively determines whether attention diversity is maintained.

## Results

The paper's primary results are theoretical: (1) a stable N→∞ feature-learning limit of transformer SGD requires α_A=1 (μP scaling), and in this limit all attention heads collapse to identical dynamics; (2) the H→∞ limit at fixed N preserves a non-Gaussian distribution over attention heads throughout training, with head-averaged kernels converging at rate O(H^{-1}); (3) the L→∞ limit with α_L=1 allows MHSA weights to evolve by Θ_L(1) while α_L=1/2 freezes them. Empirically, on CIFAR-5M the variance of attention matrices across heads decays as O(N^{-2}) for α_A=1 but remains constant for α_A=1/2, and on C4 (LLM, Adam), scaling H from 8 to 128 consistently reduces train loss toward ~5, while scaling L from 4 to 64 provides little to no improvement for either α_A value.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical paper without external method baselines; the key comparisons are between internal parameterization choices. For the N→∞ limit, μP scaling (α_𝒜=1) is shown to be the only admissible choice for a well-defined SGD limit, but it causes all heads to compute identical attention (variance across heads → 0 as O(N⁻²)), effectively reducing multi-head attention to single-head. The alternative α_𝒜=1/2 preserves head diversity (non-decreasing variance across heads) but does not admit a stable N→∞ limit. For the depth limit, α_L=1 allows attention weights to update by Θ_L(1) and yields better loss at large L on CIFAR-5M, while α_L=1/2 freezes MHSA weights and produces a less structured initial kernel. On C4 LLM training, α_𝒜=1/2 shows a slight advantage over α_𝒜=1 when scaling H, and a substantial advantage when scaling N, consistent with the theoretical prediction that head diversity is beneficial.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the dynamical mean field theory (DMFT) framework developed for infinite-width MLPs (Bordelon & Pehlevan [15], Bordelon et al. [11]) to the transformer architecture, deriving limiting equations for training dynamics across three distinct scaling dimensions: key/query dimension N, head count H, and depth L. It builds directly on the μP parameterization for attention (Yang et al. [10]) and the infinite-head initialization analysis of Hron et al. [18], but crucially extends the latter from initialization to the full feature-learning training regime. The infinite depth analysis generalizes results from residual MLPs (Yang et al. [12], Bordelon et al. [11]) to the transformer's MHSA and MLP blocks, using the Martin-Siggia-Rose path integral formalism [40] to derive saddle-point equations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that the N→∞ limit under μP scaling causes all attention heads to collapse to identical dynamics is a significant conceptual result: it implies that scaling head dimension alone does not yield more diverse attention mechanisms, and that the infinite-head limit at finite N is the correct regime for studying multi-head diversity. The tension between α_L=1 (enables attention learning but loses initialization kernel structure) and α_L=1/2 (preserves structure but freezes attention weights) provides concrete architectural guidance for deep transformers. Key limitations include the exact theory being restricted to SGD (Adam is handled only via heuristic scaling), the assumption of fixed training steps, and the computational intractability of the limiting equations for realistic settings. Open questions include a rigorous Adam theory, compute-optimal scaling where model size and training duration are jointly optimized, and whether the head collapse persists in finite-but-large N regimes relevant to practice.
