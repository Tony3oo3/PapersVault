## Problem

Efficient backward adaptation-based entropy models (e.g., quadtree partition) reduce decoding steps but rely on a single regional hyper latent representation for forward adaptation, which provides insufficient context—especially at the first modeling step where no previously decoded elements are available. Simply increasing the amount of regional context does not help because it violates the independence assumption among hyper latent elements. The paper addresses how to provide diverse, complementary forward contexts without inflating bit-rate.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241460/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The codec takes an RGB image (256×256 patches during training; 768×512 or 512×768 for Kodak; 1200×1200 for Tecnick) and produces a compressed bitstream via ANS entropy coding plus a decoded image. The analysis transform maps the image to a 320-channel quantized latent representation, which is then entropy-coded using adaptive Gaussian distributions whose parameters are predicted by the DCA entropy model. Three additional quantized hyper latent representations (local, regional, global) are also encoded into the bitstream as side information.

## Method

DCA (Diversify, Contextualize, and Adapt) extends the quadtree-partition backward adaptation (4 steps) by extracting three complementary hyper latent representations: a local one (SwinT with 2×2 windows capturing 16×16-patch dependencies), a regional one (multi-scale SwinT with downsampling/upsampling), and a global one (cross-attention Transformer over the full image). During contextualization, the three features are applied step-adaptively in the order regional → global → local, each combined with previously decoded elements via a distinct mechanism (DepthConv blocks, cross-attention, and 1×1 convolutions respectively), yielding per-element Gaussian parameters (μ, σ). The key insight is that diversifying spatial range (rather than scaling up a single context) satisfies the independence assumption while providing sufficient information, and that modeling higher-level (regional) context before lower-level (local/global) context yields better rate-distortion performance.

## Results

On the Kodak dataset, DCA achieves 11.96% average rate savings over VTM-12.1, outperforming the second-best method (Li et al., 8.55%) and third-best (Minnen & Singh, 4.86%). In the head-to-head comparison (Table 1), DCA attains a BD-rate of −3.73% relative to the quadtree-partition baseline, with a decoding time of 82.05 ms and 37.89M parameters. On the Tecnick dataset, DCA also consistently achieves the best rate-distortion performance across all bit-rate regions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DCA outperforms all six compared baselines on rate-distortion performance. Against the direct baseline (Li et al., quadtree partition, CVPR'23), DCA achieves a 3.73% BD-rate improvement while adding only ~15 ms of decoding time and ~5M parameters. Compared to LIC-TCM (Liu et al., CVPR'23), DCA is 3.01% better in BD-rate (−3.73% vs. −0.72%) and nearly 2× faster in decoding (82 ms vs. 139 ms). Against MLIC++ (Jiang et al., ICMLW'23), DCA is 9.49% better in BD-rate and nearly 3× faster (82 ms vs. 243 ms). In the broader rate-distortion curves (Figures 4–5), DCA also dominates the older autoregressive baselines (Minnen et al., NeurIPS'18; Kim et al., CVPR'22) and the channel-wise AR model (Minnen & Singh, ICIP'20), achieving 11.96% rate savings over VTM-12.1 versus 4.86% for ChARM. The key trade-off is that DCA is ~15 ms slower than the quadtree baseline, but this modest overhead yields the largest BD-rate gain among all methods tested.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the quadtree partition-based backward adaptation of Li et al. (CVPR 2023) and the global hyperprior architecture of Informer (Kim et al., CVPR 2022), but repositions the focus from backward to forward adaptation. While prior work on efficient entropy modeling (checkerboard, ChARM, quadtree) has concentrated on reducing the number of backward-adaptation steps, the forward adaptation has remained limited to a single regional hyper latent representation. DCA departs from this convention by extracting three spatially diverse hyper latent representations (local, regional, global) and introducing a step-adaptive contextualization strategy that processes them in a specific semantic order (regional → global → local), drawing on the masked autoencoder literature for the ordering rationale.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 3.73% BD-rate gain over the state-of-the-art quadtree baseline is meaningful in image compression, where single-digit percentage improvements are hard-won, and the key insight—that forward adaptation was the underexploited bottleneck rather than backward adaptation—redirects future optimization efforts. The negative result that simply increasing the regional context size ('Large R') does not help, while diversifying into local and global contexts does, provides a clear design principle. The authors acknowledge that the architectural components (SwinT blocks, cross-attention) are borrowed from prior works, and that architectures specifically designed for multi-scale hyperprior fusion remain an open question, as does exploring alternative diversification criteria beyond spatial range.
