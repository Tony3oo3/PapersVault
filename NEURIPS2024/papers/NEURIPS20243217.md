## Problem

Existing deepfake detectors struggle to generalize to unseen forgery domains because they rely on simulating specific artifacts or designing architectures tailored to particular manipulation techniques. The paper identifies a universal principle underlying all face forgeries—deepfake images inherently amalgamate information from both source and target identities, while genuine faces maintain a consistent identity—and asks whether inverting this generative process can force detectors to learn more generalizable, disentangled representations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243217/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

During training, the method consumes 224×224 RGB face images along with their paired source and target images (identical to the input for genuine faces), while at inference only a single face image is required. The output is a binary real/forged classification score from a standard cross-entropy head. A key design constraint is that the frozen Stable Diffusion model, Feature Filter, Weight Module, and Guide Module are all discarded at inference, so the deployed model introduces zero additional parameters or computational overhead over the base encoder.

## Method

DiffusionFake is a plug-and-play training framework that injects a detector's encoder features into a frozen pre-trained Stable Diffusion model to guide reconstruction of the corresponding source and target images. The encoder output is split by two Feature Filter Modules (convolution + channel/spatial attention + multi-head cross-attention) into source-related and target-related representations, which are then scaled by a Weight Module that estimates the relative information content of each identity via cosine similarity in VAE latent space. A Guide Module, inspired by ControlNet, uses a trainable copy of the U-Net encoder blocks and zero-initialized 1×1 convolutions to inject these weighted features into the frozen diffusion U-Net, compelling the encoder to capture disentangled source and target information. The total loss combines binary cross-entropy, two diffusion denoising losses (one per identity), and two MSE losses for the weight modules, with the diffusion losses weighted by λ_s=0.7 and λ_t=1.0. Because the generative model is frozen and used only as a training-time teacher, the framework is backbone-agnostic (validated on EfficientNet, ViT, and ResNet) and adds no inference cost.

## Results

Trained on FaceForensics++ (HQ) and tested on five unseen datasets, DiffusionFake with EfficientNet-B4 achieves an average AUC of 81.88% and EER of 25.97%, improving over the vanilla En-B4 backbone (75.80% AUC) by ~6%. With ViT-B, the average AUC reaches 83.78% (EER 25.17%), a ~6.3% gain over the vanilla ViT-B (77.44%). On the newest diffusion-based forgery dataset DiffSwap, ViT-B+Ours achieves 86.98% AUC, substantially outperforming the strongest baseline DCL (80.21%). All improvements are achieved without adding any inference-time parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiffusionFake is evaluated against 15 baselines spanning forgery simulation (SBI, Face X-ray), attention mechanisms (MAT, GFF), contrastive learning (DCL, LRL), reconstruction (RECCE), identity-based methods (CAADM, UCF), and frequency-domain approaches (F3-Net). With En-B4, DiffusionFake outperforms the strongest baseline DCL by 1.5% average AUC (81.88 vs. 80.40) and by 7.7% on Celeb-DF (83.17 vs. 82.30). With ViT-B, the gap widens: DiffusionFake surpasses DCL by 3.4% average AUC (83.78 vs. 80.40) and by 6.8% on DiffSwap (86.98 vs. 80.21), the most recent diffusion-based forgery dataset. On WildDeepfake, ViT-B+Ours achieves 80.14% AUC, beating the previous best (ViT-B alone at 75.46%) by 4.7%. The method also outperforms identity-disentanglement approaches (UCF, CAADM) on Celeb-DF, confirming that guided reconstruction via Stable Diffusion captures more generalizable forgery cues than explicit identity modeling. Notably, all gains are achieved with zero additional inference parameters, as the Stable Diffusion model and Guide Module are used only during training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the ControlNet architecture (Zhang et al., ICCV 2023) and frozen Stable Diffusion (Rombach et al., CVPR 2022) in a fundamentally different role: rather than guiding image generation, the diffusion model serves as a training-time reconstruction target that constrains a detection encoder to learn disentangled source and target features. Within the general face forgery detection literature, prior work has pursued generalization through forgery simulation, attention mechanisms, frequency-domain modeling, and contrastive learning, but DiffusionFake is the first to explicitly invert the generative process of face swapping—forcing the detector to reconstruct both source and target identities—as a means of learning domain-invariant representations. The method is positioned as a plug-and-play add-on to existing backbones (EfficientNet, ViT, ResNet), distinguishing it from prior approaches that require architectural redesign.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play design with zero additional inference parameters makes this practically attractive for deploying improved detectors without re-architecting pipelines, and the consistent ~6–10% AUC gains across multiple backbones and unseen datasets (including the recent diffusion-based DiffSwap) suggest broad applicability. However, the framework requires paired source and target images during training, which limits real-world deployment where such pairs are unavailable; the authors acknowledge this and suggest self-supervised generation as a future direction. Open questions remain about robustness to multi-source forgeries, profile-view images, and low-quality inputs, all of which the authors identify as current failure modes. The broader implication—that a frozen generative model can act as a feature-regularization oracle for discriminative tasks—could inspire applications beyond face forgery detection.
