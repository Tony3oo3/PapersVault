## Problem

Prior work on federated learning stability assumed purely selfish clients and left the impact of egalitarian fairness unexplored. This paper asks whether pursuing egalitarian fairness (equalizing model performance across clients with unequal data) inevitably causes data-rich clients to defect from the grand coalition, and how the answer depends on altruistic behaviors and the topology of friends-relationship networks among clients.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241515/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The analysis takes client dataset sizes {n_i}, a friends-relationship network graph, a client behavior type (purely selfish, purely altruistic, or friendly altruistic) with a selfishness parameter w ∈ (0,1), and data distribution parameters (μ_e, σ²) as inputs. It produces tight closed-form egalitarian fairness bounds λ expressed as functions of dataset-size disparities within the relevant friends sub-network relative to the global minimum dataset size, along with explicit core-stability conditions. The bounds are derived under a mean estimation task with exact closed-form errors (Lemma 1), making them directly computable for any FL system satisfying the same error structure.

## Method

The paper models FL with altruistic clients as altruism coalition formation games (ACFGs), where a client's utility depends on the entire friends network regardless of whether friends share the same coalition—distinguishing it from altruistic hedonic games (AHGs) that restrict influence to same-coalition friends and can yield non-Pareto-optimal stable structures. It defines three behavior types (purely selfish, purely altruistic, friendly altruistic) crossed with two altruism aggregations (welfare/min-based and equal/mean-based), yielding six utility function variants. For each variant, the paper derives a tight egalitarian fairness bound (Propositions 2–6) by requiring that no sub-coalition can block the grand coalition, leveraging the closed-form error expression from the mean estimation task. The key insight is that the achievable fairness bound is governed by the gap between the smallest dataset size in the grand coalition and the smallest (or weighted-average) dataset size within the relevant friends sub-network, not by global dataset heterogeneity alone, thereby disproving the misconception that egalitarian fairness inevitably causes instability. The paper further proves that under ACFG, any core-stable coalition structure is Pareto-optimal in local errors, resolving a non-Pareto-optimality pathology present in the AHG formulation.

## Results

The paper validates its theoretically derived egalitarian fairness bounds against empirical results in a mean estimation task with 4 clients (dataset sizes {20, 40, 50, 100}). Under a fully connected friends-relationship network with altruistic clients, the grand coalition achieves a fairness bound of λ ≈ 1.78 (err_max/err_min = 1.306/0.734) and remains core-stable. Under a partially connected network, the grand coalition becomes unstable and the core-stable structure {{1,2,3},{4}} yields a worse fairness bound of λ ≈ 2.06 (1.375/0.666). The theoretical bounds (green dashed lines in Figures 2–3) closely align with empirically achieved fairness (red solid lines) across all tested client behavior types (purely selfish, purely welfare/equal altruistic, friendly welfare/equal altruistic).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical paper without traditional method-to-method baselines; the primary 'comparison' is between the proposed Altruism Coalition Formation Game (ACFG) framework and the prior Altruistic Hedonic Game (AHG) framework [19]. The paper demonstrates that AHG yields a core-stable but non-Pareto-optimal coalition structure (e.g., {{1,2},{3},{4}} in Table 1), whereas ACFG produces a core-stable structure that is also Pareto-optimal (the grand coalition {{1,2,3,4}} under fully connected networks). The theoretical fairness bounds are validated against empirical fair FL training using a modified FedAvg with error-weighted aggregation (coefficient p), showing close alignment between predicted and achieved λ values across all six behavior types. The paper also contrasts its results with prior selfish-client stability analyses [12, 15], showing that altruism relaxes the conditions for core-stability and enables tighter (lower) fairness bounds. No direct numerical comparison with competing fairness methods (e.g., Ditto [4], Agnostic FL [5]) is provided, as the focus is on characterizing stability-fairness tradeoffs rather than optimizing model accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the FL stability analysis framework of Donahue & Kleinberg (2021), which modeled FL as a hedonic game with selfish clients and derived core-stability conditions for mean estimation tasks, by introducing altruistic client behaviors and a friends-relationship network. It departs from the altruistic hedonic game (AHG) formulation of Nguyen et al. (2016) by proposing the altruism coalition formation game (ACFG), where friends outside a client's own coalition still influence utility, thereby avoiding non-Pareto-optimal stable structures. The paper also connects egalitarian fairness (previously studied in FL via optimization objectives) to core stability, a link explicitly noted as unexplored in prior work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first theoretical fairness bounds that guarantee core stability in FL under altruistic behaviors, showing that egalitarian fairness does not inevitably cause instability but depends on client behavior types and network topology. However, the analysis is built on a stylized mean estimation task with closed-form errors, limiting direct applicability to real deep-learning FL settings where overfitting and model architecture introduce uncertainty. Open questions include extending the bounds to more complex training tasks, designing incentive mechanisms to retain clients under high-fairness requirements, and incorporating additional human behavioral factors like reciprocity and bounded rationality.
