## Problem

While Polyak-Ruppert averaged linear stochastic approximation (LSA) iterates are known to be asymptotically normal, the rate of convergence in the central limit theorem (Berry-Esseen bound) was not established for LSA, and existing confidence intervals for SA algorithms relied on loose concentration inequalities or had only asymptotic validity. This paper fills both gaps by providing the first non-asymptotic Berry-Esseen bound for PR-averaged LSA and the first non-asymptotic validity guarantee for a multiplier bootstrap procedure in the SA setting.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a stream of i.i.d. observations Z_k (in the TD learning application, these are state-action-next-state tuples), a polynomially decaying step size schedule α_k = c_0/k^γ, and a set of random multipliers W_k with mean 1 and variance 1 for the bootstrap. It produces a Berry-Esseen bound of order n^{-1/4} (optimal at γ=1/2) for the convex distance between the distribution of √n(θ̄_n − θ*) and its Gaussian limit, along with non-asymptotic confidence sets whose coverage error is also O(n^{-1/4}). The bootstrap is fully online: perturbed LSA trajectories are updated recursively upon each new observation without storing past data.

## Method

The proof decomposes the PR-averaged LSA error √n·Ā(θ̄_n − θ*) into a linear sum W of noise terms and a nonlinear remainder D, then applies the randomized multivariate concentration inequality of Shao and Zhang [63] to bound the convex distance. The remainder D is controlled via stability-of-matrix-products bounds and the perturbation-expansion technique of Aguech et al. [1], which separates the LSA error into transient and fluctuation components at different step-size scales. For the multiplier bootstrap, the key insight is a three-step argument: Gaussian approximation in the real world (Theorem 2), Gaussian approximation in the bootstrap world (Theorem 4, requiring the additional step-size constraint A4), and a Gaussian comparison inequality (Theorem 5, built on Pinsker's inequality and matrix Bernstein) that bridges the two Gaussian approximations. The optimal n^{-1/4} rate is achieved with the aggressive step size α_k = c_0/√k, which minimizes the decay of the second-order remainder term.

## Results

The paper establishes a Berry-Esseen bound for the convex distance ρ_n^Conv of the Polyak-Ruppert averaged LSA iterates, showing the optimal rate is n^{-1/4} (up to logarithmic factors), achieved with the aggressive step size α_k = c_0/√k (γ = 1/2). The multiplier bootstrap procedure is shown to approximate the distribution of √n(θ̄_n − θ*) with non-asymptotic error of the same n^{-1/4} order (Theorem 3), holding with probability at least 1 − 6/n. Numerical validation on the Garnet problem (N_s = 10 states, a = 2 actions, b = 3 branching factor) using N = 6,553,600 TD trajectories confirms that the n^{-1/4} decay of Δ_n is tight for γ = 0.5, while slower decay is observed for γ = 0.65 and γ = 0.7.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical paper without direct head-to-head baseline comparisons in the empirical sense. The authors qualitatively position their results against two prior works: Srikant [65] obtains a Wasserstein-distance rate of n^{-1/4} for LSA with Markov noise, but this implies only a suboptimal n^{-1/8} bound for the convex distance ρ_n^Conv via known metric comparisons, and no counterpart of the key inequality (13) exists for Markov chains. Anastasiou et al. [2] use the Stein method to obtain convergence rates for a smoothed Wasserstein metric (controlling twice-differentiable test functions), which is a stronger metric than ρ_n^Conv but yields less tight dependence on trajectory length n. The paper's contribution is the first non-asymptotic Berry-Esseen bound for LSA and the first non-asymptotic validity guarantee for a bootstrap procedure in stochastic approximation, filling a gap left by prior works [11, 73, 74] that combined non-asymptotic covariance estimation with only asymptotic confidence interval validity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Berry-Esseen framework for nonlinear statistics of Shao and Zhang [63], adapting their randomized concentration inequality to the specific structure of Polyak-Ruppert averaged LSA iterates. The multiplier bootstrap procedure follows the online resampling scheme of Fang et al. [22] for SGD, extended here with a non-asymptotic validity proof that was previously absent. The error expansion technique of Aguech, Moulines, and Priouret [1] is used to decompose the LSA error into transient and fluctuation components, enabling the separation of the statistic into a linear (sum-of-noise) part and a nonlinear remainder. The foundational LSA framework and PR averaging come from Polyak and Juditsky [53], whose asymptotic normality result this paper quantifies with explicit convergence rates.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first fully non-asymptotic confidence bound for parameter estimation in LSA via multiplier bootstrap, closing a gap where prior methods offered only asymptotic guarantees or loose concentration-based intervals. The n^{-1/4} Berry-Esseen rate (achieved with the aggressive step size α_k = c_0/√k) reveals a fundamental limitation of the SA structure that concentration inequalities cannot capture. Key open questions include extending the Berry-Esseen technique to Markovian noise (where the core inequality [63] has no counterpart), generalizing to nonlinear SA and first-order gradient methods, and proving non-asymptotic validity for plug-in covariance estimators. The bounded-noise assumption (A2) is restrictive and its relaxation to finite-moment settings is identified as requiring non-trivial additional work.
