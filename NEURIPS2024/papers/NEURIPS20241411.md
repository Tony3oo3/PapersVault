## Problem

In edge-cloud collaborative inference, a malicious cloud server can reconstruct the device's raw input from intermediate representations (via model inversion) and infer the device's predictions from server-side features (via model completion). Existing defenses—differential privacy, compression, and VIB-based mutual information regularization—suffer substantial accuracy degradation, particularly when the device-side head model is shallow, which is the realistic resource-constrained setting. The paper identifies a fundamental inadequacy of VIB in this context: it regularizes the entire representation distribution toward a fixed Gaussian, inadvertently discarding task-relevant information alongside private information.

## Input / Output

The method operates on 32×32 RGB images (CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100) with a ResNet18 backbone split into three parts: a single convolutional layer (head model) on the device producing a representation r, the middle layers (encoder) on the server producing a feature z, and the last basic block (classifier) on the device producing a class label. During training, the device holds sample pairs (x_i, y_i) and the server holds 40–400 auxiliary labeled samples for attacks; the defense is applied entirely during collaborative training so that at inference time the server's training procedure is unchanged, making the defense concealed from the adversary.

## Method

InfoScissors replaces the VIB upper bound on I(r;x) with the CLUB (Contrastive Log-ratio Upper Bound) estimator, which parameterizes p(x|r) directly rather than p(r|x), aligning the training objective with the actual attack goal of reconstructing x from r. The overall objective combines three terms: a standard prediction loss, an input-protection term minimizing I_vCLUB(r;x) via an adversarial generator g_ψ that tries to reconstruct x from r while the head model θ^h resists, and a prediction-protection term minimizing I_vCLUB(z;y) via a classifier h_φ that tries to predict y from z while the encoder θ^e resists. Training proceeds in three steps per batch: (1) optimize the device classifier and label predictor, (2) optimize the input-reconstruction generator, and (3) jointly optimize θ^h and θ^e against all five loss components weighted by λ_d and λ_l. The paper provides theoretical guarantees (Theorems 1–2) bounding the adversary's best-possible reconstruction MSE and classification cross-entropy in terms of the vCLUB bound and KL divergence, and proves that VIB's fixed-Gaussian regularization causes complementary information loss that CLUB avoids.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 against the KA (black-box) model inversion attack at the strongest defense level, InfoScissors achieves 75.56% model accuracy with SSIM of 0.1425, while the best baseline (DP) drops to 65.31% accuracy for a comparable SSIM of 0.1882. Against the white-box rMLE attack, InfoScissors reduces SSIM below 0.2 with less than 2% accuracy drop on CIFAR-10 and less than 1% on CIFAR-100. For prediction protection, InfoScissors drives the PMC attack accuracy to the random-guess level (10% on CIFAR-10, 1% on CIFAR-100) while sacrificing less than 0.5–1% model accuracy, compared to >4–6% drops for all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

InfoScissors dominates all five baselines (DP, AN, DC, PPDL, MID) on the accuracy–privacy trade-off for both input and prediction protection. At the strongest defense level against the KA attack on CIFAR-10, InfoScissors retains 75.56% accuracy with SSIM 0.1425, whereas DP drops to 65.31% (−10.2 pp) for a worse SSIM of 0.1882, and MID (the VIB-based SOTA) collapses to 61.30% accuracy with SSIM 0.3713. Against the white-box rMLE attack, InfoScissors again achieves SSIM < 0.2 with <2% accuracy loss, while all baselines require >10% accuracy degradation to reach comparable SSIM. For prediction protection (PMC and AMC attacks), InfoScissors reaches random-guess attack accuracy with <1% model accuracy sacrifice, while DP, AN, DC, and MID each need 4–6% accuracy drops to achieve the same defense level. The VIB-based MID baseline is particularly weak: it only performs adequately when the device-side head model is deep, and degrades sharply under the shallow-head setting that is realistic for resource-constrained edge devices.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the CLUB mutual information estimator [39] but departs fundamentally from the VIB-based regularization approach used by prior mutual information defenses in collaborative inference (MID [18], [17], [21]). While those works minimize I(r;x) by pushing the representation distribution toward a fixed Gaussian via the p(r|x) direction, InfoScissors instead parameterizes p(x|r) directly, aligning the training objective with the actual model inversion attack goal of reconstructing x from r. The paper also unifies input and prediction protection under a single mutual information framework, whereas prior works addressed these leakage channels separately or focused on only one.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The theoretical analysis showing that VIB-based defenses are structurally inadequate for input protection in collaborative inference—particularly with shallow head models typical of resource-constrained edge devices—provides a clear diagnostic for why prior MI-regularization methods underperform in realistic settings. The practical impact is significant: less than 3% accuracy degradation while neutralizing strong MI and MC attacks, compared to >10% for all baselines. However, the evaluation is limited to CIFAR-10/100 with ResNet18 and a single edge device, leaving open whether the approach scales to larger models, more diverse architectures, or multi-device collaborative inference scenarios.
