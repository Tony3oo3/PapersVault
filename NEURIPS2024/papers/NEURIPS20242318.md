## Problem

Existing CLIP-based OOD detection methods face a fundamental trade-off: zero-shot scoring functions suffer from domain gaps with downstream ID data, while fine-tuning approaches risk deconstructing CLIP's carefully learned multimodal representations. Sparsification-based techniques that work for CNNs become ineffective in large-scale pretrained models like CLIP, where ID and OOD activation patterns are not sufficiently distinct. The paper asks whether post-hoc modification of CLIP's weight matrices—without any retraining—can systematically improve OOD detection.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and a set of text prompts (one per ID class, formatted as 'a photo of a [CLASS]') as input. It produces a continuous OOD detection score (MCM or GL-MCM) that is thresholded at λ to yield a binary ID/OOD decision. The key operational detail is that SeTAR modifies the W_up weight matrices in each ViT feed-forward sublayer of both the image and text encoders before inference, so the 'input' to the detection pipeline is a pre-modified model rather than a modified input signal. The rank reduction ratio per layer is determined by a greedy search on a small ID validation set (one image per class for ImageNet1K, 10% split for Pascal-VOC).

## Method

SeTAR performs selective low-rank approximation on the W_up matrices in each CLIP ViT layer by computing their SVD and retaining only the top-r principal singular components, where r is determined by a per-layer rank reduction ratio Θ. A top-to-bottom, image-encoder-then-text-encoder greedy search algorithm determines the optimal Θ for each layer from a candidate list (0%–40% in 5% steps), minimizing the LoCoOp loss on an ID validation set. The projection matrix W_p is kept frozen, as ablations show modifying it degrades performance. For the fine-tuning extension (SeTAR+FT), the low-rank approximation Ŵ is frozen and only the discarded minor singular components are parameterized as trainable low-rank matrices A and B (W = Ŵ + BA), giving each layer a data-driven rank budget rather than LoRA's uniform allocation, which yields faster convergence and lower OOD loss during fine-tuning.

## Results

On the training-free ImageNet1K benchmark with CLIP-base, SeTAR+GL-MCM achieves an average FPR95 of 33.12% and AUROC of 91.32%, reducing FPR95 by 2.17 points (6.1% relative) over vanilla GL-MCM (35.29%). On Pascal-VOC, the improvement is more pronounced: SeTAR+GL-MCM reaches 23.86% FPR95 and 94.87% AUROC, a 18.95% relative FPR95 reduction over vanilla GL-MCM (29.44%). In the fine-tuning setting (Table 2), SeTAR+FT with CLIP-base achieves 32.19% FPR95 and 92.31% AUROC under GL-MCM, outperforming LoCoOp (34.14% FPR95) and LoRA (34.36% FPR95). On Swin-base with the Energy score, SeTAR+FT reduces FPR95 by 36.80% relative to LoRA (39.29% vs. 62.17%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SeTAR consistently outperforms both zero-shot baselines: vanilla MCM (Ming et al., 2022) and vanilla GL-MCM (Miyai et al., 2023b) across all six OOD datasets and both ID benchmarks, with the largest relative FPR95 reduction of 18.95% on Pascal-VOC+GL-MCM. In the fine-tuning setting, SeTAR+FT beats LoCoOp (Miyai et al., 2023a) by 3.97% relative FPR95 and LoRA (Hu et al., 2022) by 6.67% on CLIP-base+GL-MCM; the gap widens to 17.92% and 12.45% on CLIP-large. SeTAR+FT also surpasses NPOS (Tao et al., 2023) and CoOp (Zhou et al., 2022) by larger margins (e.g., 38.77% vs. 42.20% FPR95 for NPOS under MCM). On the NegLabel score (Jiang et al., 2024), SeTAR further reduces FPR95 from 25.40% to 23.09% on CLIP-base+ImageNet1K. On ResNet50, SeTAR (combined with ASH search) achieves 22.38% FPR95 and 95.25% AUROC, outperforming ASH-S (Djurisic et al., 2023) at 22.80%/95.12%, ReAct (Sun et al., 2021) at 31.43%/92.95%, DICE (Sun & Li, 2022) at 34.75%/90.77%, Energy (Liu et al., 2020) at 58.41%/86.17%, and Softmax (Hendrycks & Gimpel, 2017) at 66.95%/81.99%.

## Relation to Prior Work

SeTAR repurposes SVD-based low-rank approximation—a technique previously applied to model compression and LLM fine-tuning (LoRA)—as a post-hoc weight modification strategy for OOD detection, departing from the dominant paradigm of designing new scoring functions (MCM, GL-MCM) or fine-tuning strategies (LoCoOp, CoOp) on CLIP. It explicitly contrasts with sparsification-based CNN methods (ReAct, ASH, DICE) that rely on distinct ID/OOD activation patterns, an assumption the authors argue breaks down in large pretrained models. The SeTAR+FT extension builds directly on LoRA's low-rank decomposition framework but replaces its uniform rank allocation with a per-layer selective rank determined by the greedy search, positioning it as a structured initialization for fine-tuning rather than a standalone method.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SeTAR offers a lightweight, training-free plug-in that is complementary to virtually any existing CLIP-based scoring function and generalizes across ViT and CNN backbones, making it practically attractive for deployment where retraining is infeasible. The ~20-minute search cost on a single GPU and the absence of new parameters lower the barrier to adoption significantly. Open questions remain: optimal hyperparameters (λ, top-K) still vary across backbones and require manual tuning, the SeTAR+FT design is acknowledged as a simple initialization rather than an optimal adaptation strategy, and the method has only been validated on visual OOD inputs, leaving multi-modal OOD detection unexplored.
