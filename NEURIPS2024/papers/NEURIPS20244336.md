## Problem

Current time series forecasting models are constrained by fixed-size look-back windows, limiting them to capturing dependencies within a few hundred steps, while the conventional practice of shuffling consecutive training samples into mini-batches destroys the inherent temporal correlations between samples. Simply enlarging the look-back window is impractical due to quadratic complexity in transformer-based forecasters and often degrades performance, leaving a gap in modeling long-range trends spanning thousands of steps.

## Input / Output

The base model receives a multivariate look-back window X_t ∈ R^{L×N} (L=96 for most datasets, 36 for Illness) and predicts S future steps (96–720 for most, 24–60 for Illness). The BSA module is a plug-in that operates on intermediate activations F ∈ R^{D} (or R^{B×D} in batched mode) and returns F' of the same shape, requiring no change to the base model's input/output interface. Unusually, training uses sequential (chronological) sampling rather than random shuffling, and the BSA module maintains persistent EMA state M across mini-batches, so the effective input window extends beyond L to encompass the entire training stream.

## Method

Spectral Attention stores K exponential moving averages (EMAs) of intermediate activations, each with a distinct smoothing factor α_k, functioning as low-pass filters at different cut-off frequencies (e.g., α=0.999 preserves trends over ~6000 steps). A learnable SA-matrix ∈ R^{(2K+1)×D} then applies softmax-weighted attention over the concatenated high-frequency residuals (F−M), original features (F), and low-frequency EMAs (M), allowing the model to selectively attend to specific frequency bands. Batched Spectral Attention (BSA) unfolds the EMA recurrence across B consecutive time steps via a single lower-triangular matrix multiplication, enabling parallel computation and allowing gradients to propagate backward through time within the mini-batch (analogous to BPTT), thereby extending the effective look-back window. The smoothing factors are made learnable in BSA (via sigmoid-parameterized scalars), and the SA-matrix is initialized so that the module acts as an identity function, permitting fine-tuning from a pre-trained base model with minimal additional parameters (<5%) and negligible overhead in training time and memory.

## Results

BSA (Batched Spectral Attention) is applied as a plug-in module to 7 recent TSF models across 11 real-world datasets, with results averaged over prediction lengths S∈{96,192,336,720} and 3 seeds. The average MSE improvement ranges from 0.96% (TimesNet: 0.611→0.605) to 7.2% (RLinear: 0.664→0.619), with linear-based models showing the largest gains. Paired t-tests confirm statistically significant (p<0.05) improvements in 82% of dataset-model combinations for MSE and all MAE comparisons. On the synthetic experiments, BSA achieves up to 58.8% MSE reduction on ETTh2 with a 1000-period sine wave added, demonstrating its ability to capture trends far beyond the 96-step look-back window.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BSA is evaluated as an add-on module to each of the 7 base models, so the primary comparison is base model vs. base model + BSA. DLinear benefits most in relative terms (MSE 0.640→0.614, −4.1%), followed by RLinear (0.664→0.619, −6.8%) and FreTS (0.807→0.783, −3.0%), confirming that simpler architectures gain the most from long-range context. Transformer-based models (iTransformer, Crossformer, PatchTST) and the convolutional TimesNet show smaller but still consistent gains (0.96%–2.5% MSE reduction). BSA wins on 82% of dataset-model pairs with statistical significance (p<0.05), and the improvement is particularly pronounced on datasets with strong long-term trends (e.g., PEMS03: RLinear MSE drops from 0.992 to 0.674, a 32% reduction). On synthetic data with injected 300- and 1000-period sine waves, BSA achieves 23–59% MSE reductions over the base iTransformer, while the base model (96-step look-back) cannot capture these long-range patterns at all.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of frequency-domain time series modeling (FEDformer, TimesNet, FreTS) and sequential training dynamics. Prior frequency-utilizing models confine spectral analysis to the fixed look-back window, while prior long-range dependency work (DLinear, Informer) attempts to extend the input window itself. Spectral Attention departs from both by using an exponential moving average as a persistent low-pass filter that accumulates information across the entire training stream, combined with a learnable attention matrix over multiple frequency bands. The Batched Spectral Attention extension repurposes the BPTT concept from RNNs into a non-recurrent setting by unfolding the EMA computation across a mini-batch, enabling gradient flow between consecutive samples without increasing the input window.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is strong: BSA adds less than 5% computational overhead, is model-agnostic across linear, convolutional, and transformer architectures, and can be fine-tuned onto pre-trained models without retraining from scratch. This makes it a low-risk, high-utility add-on for practitioners already using standard TSF models. However, the paper acknowledges that gains may be limited for datasets dominated by high-frequency information within the look-back window, and the interaction between BSA placement and model architecture remains underexplored. An open question is whether the sequential-sampling requirement (which precludes standard shuffled mini-batching) limits applicability in distributed or online training settings.
