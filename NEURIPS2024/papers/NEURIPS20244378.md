## Problem

Constant-step-size stochastic approximation (SA) algorithms do not converge to the true fixed point but instead oscillate around it with a persistent O(α) bias. While this bias has been characterized for martingale noise or for Markovian noise independent of the parameter, the general case where the Markovian transition kernel K(θ) depends on the current iterate θ_n — as arises in ε-greedy Q-learning — remained without a precise, computable characterization. This paper fills that gap by providing a framework to compute the exact bias vector and to reduce it via extrapolation.

## Input / Output

The method takes as input the SA drift function f(θ, X), a Markovian transition kernel K(θ) on a finite state space 𝒳 (which may depend on θ), a constant step-size α, and an initial parameter θ₀ in a compact set Θ⊂ℝᵈ. It produces three outputs: (i) a uniform-in-n bias bound of O(α) for E[θ_n] − θ*, (ii) a closed-form bias vector V for the Polyak-Ruppert average, characterized as the solution to a Sylvester equation involving the Jacobian and Hessian of the averaged drift at θ*, and (iii) a Richardson-Romberg extrapolated estimate 2θ̄ₙ⁽ᵅ⁾ − θ̄ₙ⁽²ᵅ⁾ whose bias is O(α²). A key structural requirement is that K(θ) be unichain for every θ, ensuring a unique stationary distribution π(θ) that is twice differentiable.

## Method

The core technique is an infinitesimal generator comparison, closely related to Stein's method. The authors construct a hybrid quantity z_{n,k} = E[h(φ_{n−k}(θ_k))] where the first k steps follow the stochastic recurrence and the remaining n−k steps follow the deterministic Euler discretization of the ODE θ̇ = f̄(θ). By telescoping the difference z_{n,k+1} − z_{n,k}, the bias decomposes into a first-order term (involving f − f̄) and a second-order term (involving the martingale covariance Q), each weighted by derivatives of h∘φ_{n−k}. The Markovian averaging is handled via the Poisson equation for K(θ), which allows replacing f(θ_k, X_k) by its stationary average f̄(θ_k) at the cost of a bounded remainder. The bias vector V is then shown to satisfy a continuous-time Sylvester equation H⁽¹⁾W + W(H⁽¹⁾)ᵀ + O = 0, making it explicitly computable from the problem parameters. Finally, running two SA trajectories at step-sizes α and 2α and forming the linear combination 2θ̄⁽ᵅ⁾ − θ̄⁽²ᵅ⁾ cancels the O(α) bias, yielding an O(α²) estimator without requiring a smaller step-size.

## Results

The paper establishes three main theoretical results for constant-step-size stochastic approximation with Markovian noise. Theorem 1 shows that both the bias |E[θ_n] − θ*| and variance E[(θ_n − θ*)²] are bounded by Cα for all n, uniformly in time. Theorem 2 provides a refined expansion for the Polyak-Ruppert average: limsup_n |E[θ̄_n] − θ* − Vα| ≤ C′α², where V is a constant characterized by a Sylvester equation. Theorem 3 gives a high-probability guarantee that θ̄_n converges to θ* + αV with error O(α²). Combining two runs at step-sizes α and 2α via Richardson-Romberg extrapolation (2θ̄_n^(α) − θ̄_n^(2α)) yields an estimator with bias O(α²), as illustrated numerically in Figure 2.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no head-to-head method comparisons or benchmark tables. The numerical illustrations (Figures 1, 2, 5, 6) use synthetic one-dimensional examples to validate the theorems rather than to compare against competing algorithms. The paper positions itself relative to prior work in several ways: it extends the generator-comparison framework of Allmeier & Gast [1] (continuous-time mean-field bias) to the discrete-time SA setting; it generalizes the Richardson-Romberg bias analysis of Dieuleveut et al. [16] and Huo et al. [20] (which assume external Markovian noise) to the case where the Markovian transition kernel K(θ) depends on the current iterate θ_n; and it builds on the Stein's method / Poisson equation techniques of Gast [17] and Kolokoltsov et al. [22] for bounding the distance between stochastic and deterministic dynamics. The key distinguishing contribution is the allowance of θ-dependent Markovian noise, which is relevant for asynchronous Q-learning with ε-greedy policies, a setting not covered by the cited prior works.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the generator-comparison framework of Allmeier & Gast (2023) from continuous-time mean-field models to discrete-time stochastic approximation with Markovian noise, and is methodologically rooted in Stein's method and the hybrid stochastic/deterministic system trick of Gast (2017). The key departure from prior bias-analysis work on constant-step-size SA (Dieuleveut et al. 2020, Huo et al. 2023, Yu et al. 2021) is allowing the Markovian noise kernel K to depend on the current parameter θ_n, which is essential for reinforcement learning settings like ε-greedy Q-learning. The paper also provides an explicit computable expression for the bias vector V via a Sylvester equation, going beyond the existence results in prior work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework gives practitioners a concrete recipe: compute V from the problem's Jacobian, Hessian, and noise covariance, then apply Richardson-Romberg extrapolation to halve the bias order from O(α) to O(α²) without reducing the step-size (and thus without slowing convergence). Key limitations include the requirement of four-times differentiability, a compact parameter space, and a single exponentially stable attractor; the authors note that relaxing compactness or handling multiple attractors would require substantially different techniques. Open questions include non-asymptotic bounds and extending the analysis to non-differentiable drifts where the bias degrades to O(√α).
