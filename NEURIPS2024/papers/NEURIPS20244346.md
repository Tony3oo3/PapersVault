## Problem

Few-shot imitation (FSI) policies deployed in novel environments can cause serious physical damage when they deviate from demonstrated behavior, yet no existing method can reliably detect such behavior errors online. The task is uniquely challenging because (1) normal states in unseen environments are out-of-distribution for one-class methods, (2) errors often produce no notable visual change, and (3) detection must occur frame-by-frame without access to the full rollout, rendering video anomaly detection approaches infeasible.

## Input / Output

PrObe consumes the FSI policy's own feature encoder outputs (history features f_h and task-embeddings f_ζ) rather than raw pixels, operating on RGB-D observations at 120×160 resolution through a shared ResNet-18 backbone. It must produce a binary error probability for the latest frame in an online, streaming fashion—no future frames are available. The benchmark spans 7 cross-domain manipulation tasks (322 base + 153 novel environments) with sequence-level ground truth, since frame-level labels are unavailable in novel environments by design.

## Method

PrObe's key insight is to detect behavior errors within the policy's feature space rather than training an independent visual encoder, leveraging the policy's task-related knowledge. A gated pattern extractor L2-normalizes history features, applies a learnable sigmoid gate for per-cell importance, performs a Hadamard product with the sign of unit embeddings, and applies instance normalization to yield sparse, environment-invariant pattern features. An LSTM flow generator captures temporal dynamics of these patterns, and the resulting pattern flow is fused with transformed task-embeddings (IN → FC → tanh) to compare rollout-to-demonstration consistency. Training combines three objectives: binary cross-entropy for frame-level classification, L1 sparsity on pattern features to make changes observable, and a novel temporal-aware triplet loss whose margin m_t = m·(1 − α·(d_ap − d_an)) scales with the temporal distance between anchor, positive, and negative samples to avoid misleading the model when adjacent frames share similar signals.

## Results

Across 7 cross-domain FSI tasks and 3 demonstration-conditioned policies (21 evaluation cases total), PrObe achieves the highest Top-1 count in 15/21 cases for AUROC and 17/21 for AUPRC, with the best average ranking (1.4 for AUROC, 1.2 for AUPRC) and the largest average performance difference over the worst method (67.29% for AUROC, 78.03% for AUPRC). PrObe uses only 0.65M parameters, roughly 10× fewer than all baselines (11.31M–14.49M). In the error correction pilot study, PrObe is the only AED method that improves the DCT policy's success rate (82.22% vs. 80.56% without correction), while other methods degrade performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PrObe consistently outperforms all four baselines across the 21 task-policy combinations. SVDDED (deep one-class SVM, single-frame OOD) is the weakest baseline, achieving Top-1 in only 1/21 AUROC cases and suffering the largest performance gap (e.g., 0.1411 AUROC on Close Drawer/SCAN vs. PrObe's 0.5829). TaskEmbED (single-frame metric-learning) and LSTMED (temporal-only) are intermediate, with LSTMED slightly ahead on AUPRC (Top-1 in 3/21) but still far behind PrObe. DCTED (temporal + metric-learning transformer) is the strongest baseline, matching or exceeding PrObe on a few individual cases (e.g., DCTED 0.7491 vs. PrObe 0.7782 AUPRC on Close Drawer/SCAN), yet PrObe still dominates in aggregate with 15/17 Top-1 counts versus DCTED's 0/0. PrObe's advantage is most pronounced on AUPRC (78.03% avg. perf. diff. vs. 57.13% for DCTED), reflecting its superior handling of class-imbalanced rollout outcomes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of few-shot imitation (FSI) and anomaly detection, but departs from both lines in a key way: rather than training an independent visual encoder to judge errors from raw observations (as one-class SVMs, metric-learning, and vFSAD methods do), PrObe operates in the FSI policy's own feature space, extracting sparse patterns from the policy's internal representations. The temporal-aware triplet loss is a direct modification of the FaceNet triplet loss [40], adding a margin that accounts for temporal proximity between anchor, positive, and negative samples. The paper explicitly positions AED as distinct from video few-shot anomaly detection (vFSAD) by highlighting three differences: the monitored subject is policy behavior (not scene anomalies), errors produce no notable visual change, and detection must be online rather than over a complete video.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The AED benchmark (7 cross-domain tasks, 322 base + 153 novel environments) fills a genuine gap: no prior benchmark simultaneously presents cross-environment generalization, subtle error signatures, and the online detection constraint that real-world FSI deployment requires. The finding that a 0.65M-parameter model in policy feature space outperforms 11–14M-parameter models with dedicated visual encoders suggests that task-relevant knowledge embedded in the policy is more informative for error detection than raw visual appearance. However, all validation is in simulation (Coppeliasim/PyRep), the error-correction pilot shows only marginal gains (~1.7% SR improvement), and the authors acknowledge that real-world deployment remains untested due to safety and cost constraints. Open questions include whether a unified AED method can generalize across diverse policies and tasks, and how to detect previously unseen error types.
