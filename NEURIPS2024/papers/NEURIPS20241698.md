## Problem

The impact of predictive coding's iterative inference procedure on the learning landscape is not theoretically understood, and the reported convergence speed-ups over backpropagation are inconsistent across datasets, models, and optimizers. This paper addresses this gap by characterizing the geometry of the effective weight landscape on which PC actually learns—the energy evaluated at the inference equilibrium of network activities—rather than the raw MSE loss.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241698/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The theoretical analysis takes deep linear networks (DLNs) with input x ∈ R^{d_x} and target y ∈ R^{d_y} and produces a closed-form equilibrated energy (a rescaled MSE with weight-dependent rescaling S), explicit Hessian matrices at critical points, and their eigenspectra. Empirically, networks are trained on standard image classification tasks (MNIST, Fashion-MNIST, CIFAR-10) and toy regression/matrix completion problems, with the key output being side-by-side comparison of saddle escape dynamics and gradient norms between PC and BP under identical SGD settings and learning rates.

## Method

The core contribution is a closed-form derivation showing that the PC energy at exact inference equilibrium equals a rescaled MSE loss where the rescaling S = I_{d_y} + Σ_{ℓ=2}^{L}(W_{L:ℓ})(W_{L:ℓ})^T arises from the non-identity covariance implicitly modeled by PC's hierarchical Gaussian generative model. From this, the authors derive the full Hessian at the origin of the equilibrated energy, proving it possesses negative eigenvalues for any network depth H ≥ 1 (in contrast to the MSE loss, whose Hessian is identically zero for H > 1). They further prove that all zero-rank saddles of the MSE loss become strict in the equilibrated energy by constructing an explicit negative-curvature direction via a second-order Taylor expansion around the critical point. The key mechanism is that PC's layer-wise Gaussian noise model introduces a weight-dependent output covariance that breaks the flatness of the loss landscape, converting higher-order (non-strict) saddles into first-order (strict) ones that SGD can escape in polynomial time. Experiments on both linear and non-linear networks (including convolutional architectures) validate the theory and support the conjecture that all equilibrated energy saddles are strict.

## Results

The paper proves three main theoretical results for deep linear networks: (1) the equilibrated PC energy equals a rescaled MSE loss with a weight-dependent rescaling S = I + Σ(W_{L:ℓ})(W_{L:ℓ})^T (Theorem 1, validated to excellent numerical precision in Figure 1); (2) the origin is a strict saddle of the equilibrated energy for *any* network depth H ≥ 1, with a non-zero negative Hessian block −Σ̃_{yy} ⊗ I_{n_{L-1}} (Theorem 2), in contrast to the MSE loss where the origin Hessian is zero for H > 1; (3) all zero-rank saddles of the loss become strict in the equilibrated energy (Theorem 3). Empirically, PC escapes the origin saddle substantially faster than BP with the same SGD learning rate on non-linear networks (5-layer FC on MNIST/Fashion-MNIST, convolutional on CIFAR-10), and PC exhibits no vanishing gradients (Figure 11), while BP-trained networks remain stuck near the origin for the full training budget.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is between predictive coding (PC) and backpropagation (BP) trained with identical SGD hyperparameters (same learning rate η=1e-3, batch size 64), initialised close to the origin (σ=5e-3). PC consistently escapes the origin saddle and other non-strict saddles of the MSE loss substantially faster than BP across all tested architectures (linear, Tanh, ReLU, convolutional) and datasets (MNIST, Fashion-MNIST, CIFAR-10). On the matrix completion task (rank-3, H=3, n_ℓ=100), BP visits three successive saddles (rank 0, 1, 2) before converging, while PC initialised at each of these saddles escapes quickly without vanishing gradients (Figure 6). The paper also compares the loss landscape to the equilibrated energy landscape, showing that the Hessian eigenspectrum at the origin matches the theoretical prediction (Eq. 8) exactly for both toy Gaussian data and MNIST/MNIST-1D (Figures 3–4, 8). The key quantitative distinction is that the loss Hessian at the origin is identically zero for H > 1 (making it an H-th order non-strict saddle), while the energy Hessian retains a negative eigenvalue of magnitude proportional to the output covariance Σ̃_{yy} regardless of depth.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the saddle-point characterization program for deep linear networks (Kawaguchi 2016; Achour et al. 2024) to a new object: the PC energy evaluated at the inference equilibrium, rather than the raw MSE loss. It builds on the hierarchical Gaussian energy formulation of Buckley et al. (2017) and corrects a prior error in the literature (Millidge et al. 2022) that conflated the total energy with the output energy. Unlike previous PC theories that relied on approximations (implicit GD for small batches, second-order trust-region), this work derives exact closed-form results under only the linearity assumption, and empirically validates them for non-linear architectures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that PC inference converts non-strict (highly degenerate) saddles into strict ones provides a concrete geometric explanation for why PC can outperform BP on deep, narrow networks, and clarifies when speed-ups should and should not be expected. The conjecture that all equilibrated-energy saddles are strict remains open, as does the question of whether minimizing the equilibrated energy is more compute-efficient or leads to better generalization than the loss. A key limitation highlighted by the authors is that inference convergence becomes increasingly expensive with depth, which may negate the landscape benefits on very deep models.
