## Problem

LLMs frequently produce factually incorrect outputs despite having learned relevant knowledge during pretraining. Existing layer-contrastive decoding methods like DoLa directly substitute the final-layer output distribution with an estimated latent knowledge distribution, which can introduce errors when the latent knowledge is imperfect, and they face a paradoxical layer-selection problem where larger candidate sets degrade performance. SLED addresses both issues by formulating factuality improvement as a single-step gradient-descent optimization over the output logits, guided by the model's own latent knowledge.

## Input / Output

SLED takes a standard text prompt fed into an LLM and, during each decoding step, extracts logits from all N layers (not just the final layer) by applying the same linear projection to each layer's hidden states. It then restricts computation to the top-k tokens by final-layer logit value (the 'evolution scale' parameter, typically k=5–20), sets all other token logits to a large negative value (e.g., −1000), and outputs a single set of modified final-layer logits that replace the original logits for token selection. The method adds negligible latency overhead (0.1%–10% over DoLa) and requires no external knowledge base, auxiliary model, or fine-tuning.

## Method

SLED operates in three phases. Phase 1 estimates a per-layer latent distribution P_latent^(n) by computing the cosine similarity between the layer difference (logits_n − logits_N) and the gradient of KL(P_e_i, P_logits_n) for each candidate token i, then selecting the token whose gradient best aligns with the difference; a soft (squared) version of these similarities is retained for robustness. Phase 2 ensembles across all early layers via a weighted average where weights s^(n) are proportional to the total alignment mass m^(n) of each layer, so layers whose differences more closely match true gradient directions contribute more. Phase 3 applies a single-step gradient descent on the final-layer logits: ℓ̃_(i,N) = ℓ_(i,N) − (α/τ)(p_(i,N) − m_i), where α is the evolution rate that balances the original (training-refined) logits against the latent-knowledge estimate. Unlike DoLa, which replaces the output distribution entirely and selects a single premature layer via JSD, SLED blends the two distributions through the gradient step and ensembles over all early layers, avoiding both the over-reliance on imperfect latent knowledge and the layer-selection paradox.

## Results

On the LLaMA-2 family (7B–70B, Base and Chat), SLED consistently outperforms both vanilla greedy decoding and DoLa across all evaluated benchmarks. On FACTOR (long-paragraph factuality), SLED achieves 67.27% on 7B-Base (vs. 58.15% vanilla, 62.49% DoLa) and 77.49% on 70B-Base (vs. 72.78% vanilla, 61.92% DoLa), representing 5–13% absolute gains. On TruthfulQA multiple-choice, SLED leads on the more sensitive MC1 and MC3 metrics (e.g., 38.31% MC1 on 70B-Chat vs. 35.98% vanilla and 31.58% DoLa). For chain-of-thought reasoning, SLED improves StrategyQA accuracy by 0.1–0.7% and GSM8K by 0.1–0.8% over vanilla, while DoLa generally degrades CoT performance without a repetition penalty.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SLED decisively outperforms DoLa (the closest prior method) on nearly every metric: on FACTOR, SLED gains 5–15 points over DoLa (e.g., 77.49 vs. 61.92 on 70B-Base), and on TruthfulQA MC1 it leads by 1–6 points (e.g., 38.31 vs. 31.58 on 70B-Chat). DoLa in fact degrades CoT performance (GSM8K drops from 56.33 to 43.37 on 70B-Base), whereas SLED maintains or slightly improves it. When combined with other decoding methods, SLED consistently adds 1–12% accuracy: ITI+SLED reaches 43.33% MC1 vs. 36.60% for ITI alone on 7B-Chat; CD+SLED achieves 36.47% MC1 vs. 28.15% for CD alone on 13B-Chat; AD+SLED (36.23% MC1) surpasses both AD alone (35.37%) and AD+DoLa (33.41%) on 7B-Chat; and ICD+SLED (46.87% MC1) edges out ICD alone (46.32%) on 7B-Chat. Overall, SLED is the only method that simultaneously improves factuality across multiple-choice, open-ended, and CoT tasks without requiring a repetition penalty or external knowledge.

## Relation to Prior Work

SLED builds directly on DoLa (Chuang et al., ICLR 2024), which contrasts logits from early and final layers to surface latent factual knowledge. The key conceptual departure is reframing this contrast through an optimization lens: rather than DoLa's direct substitution of the output distribution with the latent distribution, SLED treats the layer difference as an approximate gradient and performs a single-step KL-divergence minimization to blend latent knowledge with the original logits. This positions SLED within the broader family of factuality decoding methods (ITI, AD, CD, ICD) while introducing a principled 'logits evolution' framework that unifies the training-time and inference-time perspectives on how LLMs refine their output distributions across layers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SLED provides a more principled and composable alternative to DoLa for layer-wise contrastive decoding, with the practical advantage of mitigating DoLa's well-known repetition pathology without heavy repetition penalties. Its ability to stack on top of other factuality methods (ITI, AD, CD, ICD) for additional 1–12% gains makes it a useful modular component in decoding pipelines. Open questions remain around theoretical guarantees for the gradient approximation, the sensitivity of the evolution rate α to model scale and task, and whether the approach extends naturally to fine-tuning or domain-adaptation settings. The slight latency overhead (0.1–10% over DoLa) is modest but non-zero, and the method still requires access to all intermediate layer logits, which may be impractical in some deployment settings.
