## Problem

While strong regret in dueling bandits has well-characterized optimal bounds scaling as log T, weak regret minimization—where only one of two presented arms needs to be the Condorcet winner—lacks a complete understanding of its optimal scaling. Existing algorithms (WS-W, BTW) achieve constant regret bounds but their optimality in K and the gap structure remains open, and it is unclear whether the full gap matrix beyond the Condorcet winner's gaps can be exploited to further reduce regret.

## Input / Output

The learner operates on K arms with an unknown skew-symmetric preference matrix Q (q_{i,j} = 1 − q_{j,i}), receiving at each round a single Bernoulli sample indicating which of the two selected arms wins. The weak instantaneous loss is min{Δ_{k*,I_t}, Δ_{k*,J_t}}, which is zero whenever either selected arm is the Condorcet winner k*. The algorithms require no prior knowledge of Q or k* and must select a pair (I_t, J_t) each round to minimize cumulative weak regret R_T^{(w)} = Σ E[ℓ_t^{(w)}].

## Method

The paper introduces two complementary algorithms. WR-TINF adapts the standard reduction of dueling bandits to two coupled MAB problems using online mirror descent with the Tsallis-INF regularizer (α = 2/3, η_t = 2K^{-1/6}/√t), but modifies the sampling scheme: when an internal draw produces I'_t = J'_t (signaling insufficient exploration), the right arm is resampled from a decoupled distribution r_t ∝ p_t^{2/3} rather than p_t, injecting the extra exploration that weak regret requires. This yields a regret bound of O(√(K/Δ*) · √(Σ_{k≠k*} 1/Δ_{k*,k})), matching a new lower bound of Ω(K/Δ_cw) in the regime where the Condorcet winner's gaps dominate. WR-EXP3-IX takes a different route: it cycles the left arm I_t in round-robin order and uses EXP3-IX to select the right arm J_t within each stage, stopping for arm k once the cumulative loss S(k,n,t) hits the threshold −B√t. Sub-optimal arms exhibit negative drift and hit the threshold in O(K/Δ_{j*(k),k}²) rounds, while the Condorcet winner's positive drift prevents it from ever hitting the threshold, trapping the algorithm in a zero-weak-regret loop. This achieves O(log(K/Δ*) · Σ KΔ_{k*,k}/Δ_{j*(k),k}²), which outperforms WR-TINF when inter-sub-optimal-arm gaps are large relative to the Condorcet winner's gaps.

## Results

The paper establishes that WR-TINF achieves a weak regret of order $\sqrt{K/\Delta_*}\sqrt{\sum_{k\neq k^*} 1/\Delta_{k^*,k}}$, which simplifies to $\mathcal{O}(K/\Delta_{\mathrm{cw}})$ under uniform gaps, matching the new lower bound of $\Omega(K/\Delta_{\mathrm{cw}})$ (Theorem 5.1). This improves over the prior state-of-the-art WS-W bound of $K^2/\min_{i\neq j}\Delta_{i,j}^2$ and BTW's $K^2 + K/\min_{i\neq k^*}\Delta_{k^*,i}^4$ in both the dependence on $K$ (linear vs. quadratic) and the gap (first-order vs. second/fourth-order). WR-EXP3-IX achieves $\mathcal{O}(K^2\Delta_{\mathrm{cw}}/\Delta_{\mathrm{sub}}^2)$ in a toy model where sub-optimal arm gaps dominate, outperforming WR-TINF when $\Delta_{\mathrm{cw}}/\Delta_{\mathrm{sub}} \leq 1/\sqrt{K}$. In experiments (K up to 400), WR-TINF shows the best scaling with $K$ in large-problem scenarios without stochastic transitivity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against WS-W [4] (the prior state-of-the-art for weak regret), WR-TINF improves the $K$-dependence from quadratic to linear and the gap-dependence from $\min\Delta^{-2}$ to $\Delta_{\mathrm{cw}}^{-1}$, a strict improvement in both dimensions. In the experiments, WS-W performs well under stochastic transitivity (Scenario 1, K=30) but degrades without it (Scenarios 2–3), and suffers high variance in large-K settings due to its round-based challenger mechanism. Against Versatile-DB [14] (a strong-regret algorithm), WR-TINF demonstrates that optimizing for weak regret with its additional exploration does not sacrifice strong-regret performance: in Scenario 2 (K=150), WR-TINF's strong regret remains competitive with Versatile-DB despite the extra exploration needed for weak regret. WR-EXP3-IX outperforms WR-TINF in the regime where sub-optimal arm gaps $\Delta_{\mathrm{sub}}$ are large relative to Condorcet winner gaps $\Delta_{\mathrm{cw}}$ (specifically when $\Delta_{\mathrm{cw}}/\Delta_{\mathrm{sub}} \leq 1/\sqrt{K}$), achieving $K^2\Delta_{\mathrm{cw}}/\Delta_{\mathrm{sub}}^2$ vs. $K/\Delta_{\mathrm{cw}}$. In the large-K experiment (Scenario 3, K=400, no SST), WR-TINF's linear $K$-scaling gives it an edge over both WS-W and WR-EXP3-IX, confirming the theoretical prediction that the two proposed algorithms are complementary across regimes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Tsallis-INF reduction framework (Saha & Gaillard 2022; Zimmert & Seldin 2021) from strong regret to weak regret in dueling bandits, introducing a decoupled exploration mechanism (inspired by Rouyer & Seldin 2020) that activates when the two sampled arms coincide, thereby increasing exploration without sacrificing exploitation. The second algorithm, WR-EXP3-IX, departs from the reduction paradigm entirely by leveraging the full gap matrix structure via EXP3-IX (Neu 2015), a strategy not previously explored in the weak regret literature. Prior weak regret algorithms (WS-W by Chen & Frazier 2017; BTW by Peköz et al. 2022) achieved constant bounds but with K² dependence and poor gap scaling; this paper improves both to linear K and better gap dependence, while also providing the first lower bound for weak regret.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key conceptual contribution is showing that optimal weak regret depends on the full structure of the gap matrix, not merely the gaps to the Condorcet winner, and that two distinct regimes require fundamentally different strategies (direct targeting vs. exploring sub-optimal arm duels). This resolves a long-standing open question about the optimality of prior constant-bound algorithms. Open questions include developing fully adaptive algorithms that switch between regimes without prior knowledge, extending lower bounds to depend on all individual gaps rather than just the minimum, and removing the identifiability assumption in the lower bound construction.
