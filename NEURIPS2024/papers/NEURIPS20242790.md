## Problem

3D Gaussian Splatting achieves real-time, high-quality view synthesis but provides no uncertainty information about its reconstructed model or rendered predictions, limiting its use in safety-critical applications like robotics navigation and autonomous driving. Existing uncertainty quantification methods (ensemble, variational inference, Laplace approximation) were designed for implicit NeRF representations and cannot be directly transferred to the explicit, per-Gaussian parameter structure of 3DGS.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242790/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a set of calibrated RGB images with corresponding camera poses (obtained via SfM) as input and produces a 3DGS scene representation augmented with an offset table that encodes per-Gaussian posterior uncertainty. At inference, it outputs a novel view rendering accompanied by a pixel-wise predictive uncertainty map, and the learned offset posterior can be used to automatically prune noisy Gaussians (floaters) from the scene. The offset table stores only position, scale, and opacity deviations per finer Gaussian, while color and rotation are shared from the base Gaussian, keeping the parameter overhead minimal.

## Method

The core mechanism is a 'spawn' operation that creates K=10 finer multi-scale Gaussians attached to each selected base Gaussian (chosen by scale and opacity thresholds), parameterized by a compact offset table rather than full new Gaussians. An offset table of K entries is maintained per base Gaussian, and at each training step only M entries are randomly sampled and averaged to form the effective offset distribution, introducing stochasticity that increases sample diversity. Variational inference is performed with a multi-scale prior: the scale offset follows a uniform distribution whose range scales with the base Gaussian's size (ensuring finer Gaussians span a wider relative range when fewer are spawned), and the position offset follows a zero-mean Gaussian. The total training loss combines L1 and SSIM image reconstruction losses with a KL divergence term between the learned offset posterior and the multi-scale prior, optimized via the reparameterization trick. At inference, predictive uncertainty is obtained by marginalizing the rendered color over the learned offset posterior, while the offset posterior itself provides per-Gaussian parameter uncertainty that can threshold and remove noisy components.

## Results

On the LF dataset, the proposed method achieves the best uncertainty calibration with AUSE of 0.26 and NLL of 0.30 for rendered images, while also attaining the highest SSIM (0.914) among all methods. On the LLFF dataset, it achieves the best NLL (0.23) and competitive AUSE (0.32). For depth uncertainty on LF (Table 1), the method achieves the lowest average AUSE of 0.19, outperforming the 10-model Ensemble GS baseline (0.20) at a fraction of the computational cost. Rendering quality (PSNR 27.39 on LF, 23.97 on LLFF) is within ~0.5 dB of the ensemble baseline while requiring only a single model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against three NeRF-based uncertainty methods (CF-NeRF, S-NeRF, Bayes' Ray) and a 10-model 3DGS ensemble. Against all NeRF baselines, the proposed method dominates on every metric: on LF it improves AUSE from 0.49 (CF-NeRF) and 0.62 (S-NeRF) to 0.26, and NLL from 0.37/1.32 to 0.30; on LLFF it improves AUSE from 0.48/0.59 to 0.32 and NLL from 0.58/0.91 to 0.23. Against the strongest baseline, Ensemble GS (×10), the method trades a small PSNR gap (27.39 vs 27.64 on LF; 23.97 vs 24.54 on LLFF) for better uncertainty calibration (AUSE 0.26 vs 0.29 on LF; NLL 0.23 vs 0.26 on LLFF) and substantially lower inference time (0.06 s vs 0.27 s per frame). On depth uncertainty (Table 1), the method achieves the best average AUSE of 0.19 versus 0.20 for the ensemble, with a particularly large margin in the basket scene (0.13 vs 0.22). Bayes' Ray, which only models spatial perturbation, is evaluated solely on depth and trails the proposed method on average AUSE (0.23 vs 0.19).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) framework by Kerbl et al. with a variational inference layer that exploits the explicit, physically-meaningful parameters of Gaussians (position, scale, rotation, opacity, color) to construct diversified model-space samples. Prior uncertainty quantification work in view synthesis (Stochastic NeRF, CF-NeRF, Bayes' Ray) was confined to implicit NeRF representations, and the only prior 3DGS-specific method (FisherRF) relied on Laplace's approximation requiring expensive gradient aggregation over all training views. The key departure here is the offset-table mechanism: rather than training full ensemble models or applying post-hoc approximations, the paper spawns multi-scale finer Gaussians from selected base Gaussians by learning only a subset of attribute offsets (position, scale, opacity) while sharing color and rotation, drawing conceptual inspiration from Level-of-Detail techniques in graphics and multi-scale representations in vision.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method provides a practical path to uncertainty-aware 3DGS reconstruction, which is critical for downstream applications like robotics navigation, autonomous driving simulation, and VR/AR scene editing where confidence in the reconstructed geometry matters. The explicit parameter uncertainty enables automatic floater removal without manual editing, a significant practical benefit for unbounded scene reconstruction. Limitations include evaluation only on relatively small indoor/outdoor scenes (LF, LLFF, Mip-NeRF 360), the preliminary nature of the active learning experiment, and the fact that color attributes are not directly offset (relying on indirect uncertainty propagation). Open questions include whether the multi-scale prior design generalizes to very large or dynamic scenes, how the method integrates with real-time SLAM pipelines, and whether the offset-table approach can be extended to handle temporal consistency in video.
