## Problem

Implicit Neural Representations (INR) suffer from two critical limitations for multispectral-hyperspectral image fusion: the MLP-ReLU structure exhibits spectral bias that suppresses high-frequency information, and the local interpolation paradigm restricts the receptive field, preventing global perceptual modeling. The paper identifies that while Fourier amplitudes of HR-HSI and LR-HSI latent codes are remarkably similar, their phases encode distinct texture patterns, suggesting a frequency-domain fusion strategy could recover the missing high-frequency content.

## Input / Output

The network takes a low-resolution hyperspectral image (LR-HSI, e.g., 16×16×31) and a high-resolution multispectral image (HR-MSI, e.g., 64×64×3) as inputs. The LR-HSI is bicubic-interpolated to HR resolution and channel-concatenated with the HR-MSI before encoding. The output is a high-resolution hyperspectral image (HR-HSI, e.g., 64×64×31) produced as a residual added to the bicubic upsampled LR-HSI, evaluated at scale factors of 4 and 8 on the CAVE and Harvard datasets.

## Method

FeINFN employs two EDSR-based encoders to extract spectral latent codes from LR-HSI and spatial latent codes from the concatenated HR inputs, which are then processed by a dual-branch Spatial-Frequency Implicit Fusion Function (Spa-Fre IFF). The spatial branch applies high-pass filtering to spectral codes, sinusoidal frequency encoding to relative coordinates, and graph-attention-weighted interpolation; the Fourier branch applies FFT to both latent codes, fuses amplitude components via point-wise convolutions (avoiding spectrum leakage) and phase components via 3×3 convolutions separately, then maps back via IFFT—effectively expanding the INR receptive field globally in the spatial domain. A Spatial-Frequency Interactive Decoder (SFID) with a complex Gabor wavelet activation integrates the spatial and frequency features, and the paper proves this activation satisfies a time-frequency tightness bound (|ω₀|·v₀ ≥ 1/4π) that enables the decoder to learn optimal bandwidths for frequency alignment.

## Results

On the CAVE dataset at ×4 scale, FeINFN achieves 52.47 dB PSNR, 1.91° SAM, 0.98 ERGAS, and 0.998 SSIM, outperforming the strongest baselines (DSPNet: 51.18 dB, 3DT-Net: 51.38 dB, MogDCN: 51.63 dB) by 0.84–1.29 dB in PSNR. On the Harvard dataset at ×4, FeINFN reaches 49.06 dB PSNR and 1.78 ERGAS, surpassing HSRNet (48.29 dB) and DSPNet (48.29 dB) by 0.77 dB. The model uses 3.165M parameters, substantially fewer than DHIF (22.462M) and MogDCN (6.840M), while matching or exceeding their quality. At ×8 scale (Appendix Table 5), FeINFN maintains the best PSNR (50.32 dB on CAVE, 46.89 dB on Harvard) and best ERGAS (0.67 and 1.16 respectively).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FeINFN achieves the best PSNR, SAM, ERGAS, and SSIM on CAVE×4, beating the closest deep-learning competitors DSPNet (51.18 dB), 3DT-Net (51.38 dB), and MogDCN (51.63 dB) by 1.29, 1.09, and 0.84 dB respectively, while using fewer parameters (3.165M vs. 6.064M, 3.464M, 6.840M). On Harvard×4, FeINFN leads in PSNR (49.06 dB) and ERGAS (1.78) over all baselines, with a 0.77 dB PSNR margin over HSRNet and DSPNet (both 48.29 dB); however, 3DT-Net achieves a marginally lower SAM (2.04° vs. 2.10°). Among model-based methods (CSTF-FUS, LTTR, LTMR, IR-TenSR), FeINFN outperforms the best (LTMR) by over 15 dB in PSNR on CAVE×4. At the larger ×8 scale, FeINFN maintains its lead in PSNR and ERGAS on both datasets, with the gap over DSPNet widening to 1.14 dB on CAVE and 1.05 dB on Harvard, confirming strong generalization to higher upscaling factors. The moderate parameter count (3.165M) positions FeINFN favorably on the accuracy–efficiency trade-off compared to heavier models like DHIF (22.462M) and MogDCN (6.840M).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the local implicit image function (LIIF) framework from super-resolution to the multispectral-hyperspectral image fusion (MHIF) task, incorporating the complex Gabor wavelet activation from WIRE into a new decoder. Its key departure from prior INR-based methods is the introduction of a Fourier-domain branch that exploits the observation that HR-HSI and LR-HSI latent codes share similar amplitudes but divergent phases, enabling separate amplitude (point-wise convolution) and phase (3×3 convolution) fusion. The graph attention mechanism from JIIF is adapted for interpolation weight computation, and the overall architecture unifies spatial and frequency implicit fusion in a single network rather than treating them independently.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled solution to the well-known spectral bias of MLP-based INR in the context of hyperspectral fusion, with a theoretical guarantee (time-frequency tightness of Gabor wavelet activation) that supports the decoder's ability to learn optimal bandwidths. However, all experiments rely on simulated ground truth (Gaussian blur + downsampling), a limitation shared across the MHIF field but one that constrains real-world generalizability claims. Open questions include whether the Fourier-domain INR approach transfers to other fusion or restoration tasks, and whether the amplitude/phase separation strategy generalizes beyond the specific spectral characteristics of the CAVE and Harvard datasets.
