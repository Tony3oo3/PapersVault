## Problem

No prior visual question answering benchmark exists for organismal biology, leaving it unclear whether pretrained vision-language models possess the domain-specific knowledge needed to identify and reason about biological traits from specimen images. This paper asks whether 12 SOTA VLMs can answer a range of biologically relevant questions—species classification, trait identification, grounding, referring, and counting—without any additional fine-tuning, and how their performance is affected by question format, answer-choice difficulty, prompting strategies, and reasoning hallucination.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20244164/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each evaluation instance pairs a preprocessed biological specimen image (fish with background removed via GroundingDINO + SAM, dorsal-view butterfly wings, or in-the-wild bird photos from CUB-200-2011) with a text question in either open-ended or multiple-choice format. Outputs are task-specific: a scientific species name, a trait label (binary presence/absence for fish, multi-class for birds), bounding box coordinates identifying a trait region, or an integer trait count. The dataset comprises ~469K QA pairs over ~30K images spanning 495 fish species, 188 bird species, and 60 butterfly species.

## Method

The paper constructs VLM4Bio, a benchmark of ~469K question-answer pairs across five biologically motivated tasks (species classification, trait identification, trait grounding, trait referring, and trait counting) for three taxonomic groups. It evaluates 12 SOTA VLMs (GPT-4V, GPT-4o, LLaVA, COG-VLM, MiniGPT-4, BLIP-2, Instruct-BLIP) in a zero-shot setting using a unified prompt template and micro-averaged accuracy. Beyond baseline accuracy, the study introduces three analytical dimensions: (1) difficulty-stratified MC questions (easy/medium/hard) based on taxonomic similarity of answer choices, (2) three prompting techniques (contextual, dense caption, zero-shot chain-of-thought) to probe reasoning capabilities, and (3) two hallucination tests (False Confidence Test and None-of-the-Above Test) to measure whether models can reject incorrect suggestions. A biologically fine-tuned model (BioCLIP) is included as a comparison to quantify the gap between general-purpose and domain-adapted vision models.

## Results

Across 12 SOTA VLMs evaluated zero-shot on VLM4Bio (469K QA pairs, 30K images), GPT-4V achieves the best overall accuracy of 34.24%, followed by Instruct-Vicuna-7B (33.92%) and LLaVA-13B (31.78%), compared to a random-choice baseline of 22.03%. On open-ended species classification, even the best model (GPT-4V) reaches only 17.46% on Bird-10K, while MC-format questions yield substantially higher scores (GPT-4V: 82.58% on Bird-10K). Trait identification is relatively strong (GPT-4V: 82.18% Fish, Instruct-Vicuna-13B: 89.98% Bird), but trait grounding and referring drop sharply to ~25–35% for most models, revealing a critical gap in visual localization of biological traits.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GPT-4V leads overall (34.24%) and dominates Bird-10K MC species classification (82.58%) and Fish-10K trait identification (82.18%), but it is outperformed by Instruct-Vicuna-13B on Bird-10K trait identification (89.98% vs. 62.22%) and by BLIP-Flan-XXL on Fish-500 trait counting MC (78.20% vs. 44.80%). GPT-4o, evaluated only on the prompting and difficulty subsets, achieves 97.60–98.60% on Bird-Prompting MC species classification, far exceeding all other models (next best: LLaVA-13B at 52.00%). The biologically fine-tuned BioCLIP substantially outperforms all pretrained VLMs on Bird-10K MC (93.93% vs. GPT-4V's 82.58%) and Butterfly-10K MC (62.32% vs. 28.91%), confirming that domain-specific fine-tuning yields large gains. On the fine-grained difficulty analysis (Table 3), even GPT-4V and GPT-4o collapse to near-random on medium/hard fish and butterfly sets (e.g., GPT-4V: 3.50% on Fish-Medium, 2.00% on Butterfly-Hard), while BioCLIP maintains 29–95% accuracy. Smaller open-source models (MiniGPT-4, COG-VLM, BLIP-Flan-XL) generally perform at or below the 25% random-choice baseline on most tasks, indicating that current sub-13B VLMs lack sufficient biological knowledge for organismal biology tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the growing VLM benchmarking literature (MMMU, MathVista, MedQA) into the previously unexplored domain of organismal biology, introducing a multi-task VQA benchmark (VLM4Bio) that combines species classification, trait identification, trait grounding, trait referring, and trait counting. Rather than proposing a new model architecture, it repurposes existing VLMs (GPT-4V, LLaVA, BLIP-2, MiniGPT-4, COG-VLM, Instruct-BLIP) as evaluation targets and borrows prompting techniques (chain-of-thought, dense caption, contextual) from the LLM literature to probe their reasoning capabilities on biological images. The comparison with BioCLIP, a domain-specific vision foundation model, positions the work at the intersection of general-purpose VLM evaluation and biologically fine-tuned model analysis.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

VLM4Bio fills a critical gap as the first VQA benchmark for organismal biology, exposing that even SOTA VLMs perform near-randomly on open-ended species classification and struggle to localize traits despite identifying them correctly — a finding with direct implications for trust in AI-assisted scientific discovery. The dramatic performance drop from easy to hard taxonomic discrimination (e.g., GPT-4V: 44.5% → 3.5% on fish) and the fact that a smaller domain-specific model (BioCLIP) substantially outperforms much larger general VLMs on birds and butterflies challenge the assumption that scale and generality suffice for scientific applications. Open questions include whether fine-tuning on VLM4Bio or incorporating RAG/knowledge-infused prompting can close these gaps, and whether findings generalize beyond the three organism groups studied.
