## Problem

In offline reinforcement learning with only trajectory-return pairs (no step-wise rewards), maintaining temporal consistency over long horizons is a key challenge: autoregressive policies suffer from compounding errors without explicit credit-assignment signals like return-to-go (RTG). Existing methods (DT, QDT) rely on RTG or Q-value augmentation to enforce consistency, but these become unreliable when rewards are sparse, delayed, or have non-trivial relations to the final return. The paper asks whether a latent variable abstraction can replace step-wise reward conditioning to achieve coherent long-range planning.

## Input / Output

The model is trained on variable-length trajectory-return pairs (τ, y), where τ is a sequence of (state, action) tuples and y is a scalar total return — no step-wise rewards are provided. At test time, the agent receives only a desired scalar return y and the current state with a finite context window of K recent state-action pairs. The two-stage output first produces a fixed latent plan vector z ∈ ℝ^d (inferred via posterior sampling before execution), then generates an autoregressive action sequence where each action is a single-mode Gaussian conditioned on the context and the fixed z. The latent vector is consulted at every step but never updated during execution, enforcing plan persistence.

## Method

LPT defines a joint distribution p(τ, y, z) = p_α(z) · p_β(τ|z) · p_γ(y|z) where z is a latent plan vector, p_α is a UNet transformation of isotropic Gaussian noise, p_β is a causal Transformer with cross-attention to z (analogous to Decision Transformer), and p_γ is an MLP return predictor. Training uses MLE with an EM-style algorithm: posterior sampling of z_0 given (τ, y) via Langevin dynamics (MCMC) provides the E-step, and gradients for each component are computed in the M-step, with a persistent Markov chain amortizing sampling across iterations. The key insight is that posterior sampling of z naturally aggregates information from all sub-trajectories within the finite context K, forming an episode-level abstraction that enforces temporal consistency without RTG — the latent variable plays the same structural role as RTG in the KL decomposition but is inherently multi-modal and reward-agnostic. At test time, 'planning as inference' means sampling z from p(z_0|y) ∝ p_0(z_0)·p_γ(y|z) via Langevin dynamics, optionally with a classifier-guidance weight w > 1 (Exploitation-inclined Inference) to concentrate sampling around high-likelihood modes and shift the exploration-exploitation tradeoff.

## Results

Under the final-return data specification (no step-wise rewards), LPT-EI achieves 89.93±0.61 on Hopper-medium-replay, 75.68±0.34 on Walker2D-medium-replay, and 45.89±2.98 on Maze2D-large, outperforming all final-return baselines (DT, QDT, CQL) by large margins. On the stochastic Connect Four task, LPT achieves 0.99±0.01, matching the ESPER baseline (0.99±0.03) while substantially exceeding DT (0.80±0.07) and CQL (0.61±0.05). LPT also surpasses step-wise-reward baselines on most Gym-Mujoco tasks, e.g., 89.93 vs. CQL's 48.6 on Hopper-medium-replay.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LPT and LPT-EI consistently outperform CQL under the final-return specification, where CQL scores collapse to near-zero (e.g., 1.0 on halfcheetah-medium, 0.0 on walker2d-medium) while LPT-EI maintains 43–90. Against DT, LPT-EI gains the largest margins on replay datasets: +39.1 on hopper-medium-replay (89.93 vs. 50.8) and +24.1 on walker2d-medium-replay (75.68 vs. 51.6). QDT, which augments DT with Q-value-based credit assignment, is also surpassed by LPT-EI on most tasks (e.g., 89.93 vs. 38.7 on hopper-medium-replay; 45.89 vs. 31.0 on Maze2D-large), though QDT retains an edge on Antmaze-umaze-diverse (84.0 vs. 84.4 is a near-tie). On the stochastic Connect Four benchmark, LPT (0.99±0.01) matches ESPER (0.99±0.03), a latent-variable baseline using adversarial training, while both substantially exceed DT (0.80) and CQL (0.61). In the online fine-tuning setting (Appendix A.5), LPT shows modest but consistent improvements over its offline scores, with the largest gain on Antmaze-umaze (+22.3), though ODT with step-wise rewards still leads on hopper-medium (97.54 vs. 64.84).

## Relation to Prior Work

LPT extends the Decision Transformer lineage by replacing step-wise return-to-go (RTG) conditioning with a latent variable z that acts as an episode-level 'plan,' learned via MLE with Langevin-dynamics posterior sampling. Conceptually, it is closest to the latent trajectory variable approaches of Yang et al. (2022) and Paster et al. (2022), but departs by using an EM-style MLE objective with a flexible UNet prior (rather than adversarial or clustering losses) and by framing planning as explicit posterior inference over z given a target return. The Exploitation-inclined Inference mechanism borrows classifier guidance from diffusion models to control exploration-exploitation tradeoff at test time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that step-wise rewards are not strictly necessary for effective long-horizon planning: a model trained only on trajectory-return pairs can outperform RTG-based methods (DT, QDT) and even step-wise-reward CQL on many benchmarks, while also achieving strong trajectory stitching in sparse-reward mazes. Open questions include scalability to very long horizons (Antmaze-large struggles due to data quality), the computational cost of MCMC-based posterior sampling, and whether the framework extends naturally to online, multi-agent, or real-world robotic settings. The 'planning as inference' framing offers a clean theoretical lens that could inform future work on hierarchical and skill-based planning.
