## Problem

Existing driving datasets (nuScenes, Waymo, ONCE) are curated for perception tasks and contain limited driving maneuvers, sparse multi-agent interactions, and virtually no rare open-world events, which prevents driving world models from learning to follow complex action instructions like lane changes or simulating dynamic agent interplay. This data bottleneck limits the ability of generative world models to serve as reliable real-world simulators for model-based planning in autonomous driving.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240414/cropped_page0_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The world model takes a single initial frame (1920×1080) and a sequence of k ego action prompts, where each action is a 2D trajectory offset (Δx, Δy) per frame at 5 fps. It produces k future video frames as a generated video. The AIF benchmark additionally evaluates outputs by running COLMAP structure-from-motion on the generated video to recover camera trajectories, then comparing them against the input action instructions to compute lateral and longitudinal following errors in meters.

## Method

The baseline model extends Stable Video Diffusion (SVD), a latent diffusion model for image-to-video generation, by adding action conditioning: each action sequence is encoded into a 1024-dimensional vector via an MLP, concatenated with the first-frame image feature, and injected into the U-Net. The DrivingDojo dataset itself is curated from ~7,500 hours of Meituan fleet data across multiple Chinese cities, organized into three subsets targeting action completeness (7.9k clips), multi-agent interplay (6.2k clips), and open-world knowledge (3.7k clips with text descriptions). For evaluation, the AIF metric estimates trajectories from generated videos using offline SfM (COLMAP), recovers an unknown scale factor by minimizing error over the first N frames, and reports mean absolute error between estimated and ground-truth actions. The paper demonstrates that training on DrivingDojo yields significantly better zero-shot action-following accuracy (0.238m lateral vs. 0.255m for ONCE, 0.387m for nuScenes) compared to models trained on perception-oriented datasets.

## Results

On the zero-shot AIF benchmark (evaluated on OpenDV-2K), the model trained on DrivingDojo achieves FID 24.27, FVD 442.67, E_x^AIF 0.238 m, and E_y^AIF 0.136 m, substantially outperforming models trained on ONCE (FID 28.37, E_y^AIF 0.239 m) and nuScenes (FID 37.90, E_y^AIF 0.254 m). In-domain, the baseline SVD model fine-tuned on DrivingDojo limits per-frame action error to 0.100 m (longitudinal) and 0.062 m (lateral), and the SfM-reconstructed trajectory on ground-truth video achieves only 0.036 m / 0.019 m error. For visual prediction quality, SVD fine-tuned on DrivingDojo reaches FID 19.20 and FVD 343.91 on OpenDV-2K, beating the nuScenes-fine-tuned variant (FID 21.05, FVD 395.04) and approaching the OpenDV-2K-fine-tuned upper bound (FID 18.27, FVD 321.05).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares the DrivingDojo-trained SVD baseline against models trained on ONCE and nuScenes under identical zero-shot evaluation on OpenDV-2K. DrivingDojo wins on all four metrics: it achieves 14.5% lower FID than ONCE (24.27 vs. 28.37) and 36% lower than nuScenes (24.27 vs. 37.90), and 43% lower FVD than nuScenes (442.67 vs. 794.36). The advantage is most pronounced in lateral action following (E_y^AIF): DrivingDojo reaches 0.136 m versus 0.239 m for ONCE and 0.254 m for nuScenes, a 43–46% reduction. The authors attribute this gap to the richer distribution of lateral maneuvers (lane changes, turns) in DrivingDojo, noting that the ONCE-trained model degenerates to always generating straight-line motion regardless of turn instructions. In the visual-prediction comparison (Table 3), DrivingDojo-fine-tuned SVD (FID 19.20) also beats nuScenes-fine-tuned SVD (FID 21.05) but remains slightly behind the OpenDV-2K-fine-tuned upper bound (FID 18.27), confirming that the dataset's diversity translates into better generalization without requiring domain-specific fine-tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Stable Video Diffusion (SVD) architecture with a lightweight MLP-encoded action conditioning pathway, but its primary contribution is a carefully curated dataset (DrivingDojo) and a new evaluation benchmark (AIF) rather than a novel model. It departs from prior driving datasets (nuScenes, Waymo, ONCE, OpenDV-2K, nuPlan) that were designed for perception or planning tasks and thus lack the diverse ego maneuvers, multi-agent interplay, and open-world rare events needed to train interactive world models. The AIF benchmark, which uses offline SfM (COLMAP) to extract trajectories from generated videos and compare them against ground-truth action instructions, is a new evaluation protocol not present in prior driving world model literature (GAIA-1, DriveDreamer, Drive-WM).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dataset fills a concrete gap: existing driving datasets are perception-oriented and contain very few lateral maneuvers or multi-agent interactions, which the paper shows directly causes world models to fail at following action instructions (e.g., ONCE-trained models always generate straight-line driving). The AIF benchmark provides a principled, quantitative way to measure action controllability beyond visual quality metrics like FID/FVD. Key limitations include single-camera capture at 5 fps, short-horizon generation, hallucination artifacts (object disappearance, phantom roads), and a deliberately minimal baseline model, leaving open questions about long-horizon prediction, multi-sensor extension, and whether the dataset's diversity translates to improved driving policy performance.
