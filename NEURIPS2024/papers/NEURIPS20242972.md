## Problem

LiDAR perception models struggle to generalize across different sensors due to domain gaps in beam count, field of view, ray-drop behavior, and scene content—creating 'language barriers' that hinder both cross-domain transfer and multi-dataset joint training. Existing solutions are predominantly model-driven, requiring costly re-architecture and retraining for each new domain, which limits scalability. LiT addresses this by translating LiDAR data itself across domains rather than adapting the model.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242972/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

LiT consumes multi-frame LiDAR point cloud sequences from a source domain (with per-frame tracking IDs, bounding boxes, and ego-vehicle poses) along with target LiDAR sensor specifications. Only a small amount of target-domain LiDAR data is needed to perform statistical analysis of vertical ray angles; no full target-domain annotation is required. The output is a set of translated LiDAR point clouds matching the target sensor's beam pattern and ray-drop characteristics, generated via ray casting against reconstructed 3D scene meshes (foreground vehicle meshes from SDF optimization and background meshes from neural kernel fields). A full 200-frame scene is translated in under one minute on a single RTX 4090.

## Method

LiT integrates three components: (1) scene modeling, where foreground vehicles are reconstructed via SDF-based latent code optimization (pre-trained on ShapeNetV2) over fused multi-frame point clouds, and background is reconstructed using a pre-trained hierarchical neural kernel field that generalizes across datasets without per-scene retraining; (2) LiDAR modeling, which statistically identifies H peak vertical ray angles from a small target-domain sample to replicate non-uniform beam distributions, and trains an MLP with positional encoding on (ray direction, hit distance, incident angle) to predict per-ray drop probability; (3) a custom GPU-accelerated ray casting engine (NVIDIA OptiX / Intel Embree) that emits rays per the modeled LiDAR, computes ray-mesh intersections, surface normals, and incident angles at 22–31 Hz, and applies the ray-drop predictor to generate the final translated point cloud. The key distinction from prior work is that LiT operates entirely on the data side—converting source-domain scans into target-domain 'language'—enabling zero-shot detection and multi-source joint training without modifying the downstream detector.

## Results

On single-source domain adaptation, LiT consistently outperforms all baselines across three tasks (Waymo→KITTI, Waymo→nuScenes, nuScenes→KITTI) with both SECOND-IoU and PV-RCNN detectors. The strongest result is Waymo→KITTI where LiT achieves 82.55 AP_BEV / 69.94 AP_3D (SECOND-IoU), closing 95.27%/92.36% of the gap to the oracle. In the multi-source setting (Waymo+nuScenes→KITTI, zero-shot), LiT surpasses the oracle AP_BEV (84.45 vs. 83.29), and with all three sources (W+N+K→K) it exceeds oracle on every metric (87.52/75.76 SECOND-IoU, 90.67/82.67 PV-RCNN).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LiT outperforms all three baselines on every adaptation task where they are reported. Against SN [36] (object-size normalization), LiT gains substantial margins—e.g., +3.6 AP_BEV and +10.7 AP_3D on Waymo→KITTI (SECOND-IoU)—while SN actually degrades performance on nuScenes→KITTI (negative gap). Against ST3D [7] (iterative pseudo-label self-training), LiT closes a larger fraction of the oracle gap in all settings, most notably on nuScenes→KITTI where LiT reaches 91.3% gap closure vs. ST3D's 76.6% (SECOND-IoU AP_BEV). Against ReSimAD [51] (reconstruction + CARLA-based simulation), LiT achieves higher AP_3D on Waymo→KITTI (69.94 vs. 58.42 with PV-RCNN) and is the only method evaluated on nuScenes→KITTI. In the multi-source zero-shot setting (W+N→K), LiT surpasses the oracle AP_BEV by 1.16 points, a result none of the baselines achieve.

## Relation to Prior Work

LiT builds on neural kernel surface reconstruction (Huang et al., CVPR 2023) for background scene modeling and PCGen (Li et al., ICRA 2023) for the ray-drop prediction formulation, but departs from both model-driven domain adaptation (ST3D, SN, 3D-CoCo) and the closest prior data-driven method ReSimAD (Zhang et al., ICLR 2024) by replacing hand-crafted 3D assets and full-scale simulator dependencies with learned SDF-based foreground reconstruction and a custom GPU-accelerated ray casting engine. The key conceptual shift is treating domain adaptation as a data-side 'translation' problem rather than a model-side adaptation problem, enabling multi-source joint training as a first-class capability rather than a secondary benefit.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LiT demonstrates that translating LiDAR data into a unified target-domain 'language' can close 90%+ of the domain gap in zero-shot detection and, remarkably, surpass oracle (fully supervised target-domain) performance when combining multiple source domains, suggesting that data diversity via translation can substitute for target-domain supervision. Limitations include reliance on annotated source datasets, restriction to vehicle objects, and exclusive use of LiDAR modality; open questions include whether the approach generalizes to pedestrians/cyclists, other sensor types, and whether the oracle-surpassing result holds across more target domains and model architectures.
