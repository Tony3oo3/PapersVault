## Problem

DEER, the first method for parallel evaluation of nonlinear RNNs via Newton's method on a fixed-point formulation, suffers from two critical limitations: cubic computational complexity and quadratic memory in the state dimension D, making it infeasible for practical deep learning scales, and numerical instability inherited from undamped Newton's method that causes divergence on even simple autoregressive models. This paper addresses both gaps to enable scalable and stable parallel evaluation of arbitrary nonlinear RNNs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240189/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The methods take a nonlinear RNN (e.g., GRU, LSTM) with its learned parameters, an input sequence of length T with per-step inputs of dimension D, and a fixed initial hidden state s_0, and produce the full hidden state trace s_{1:T} ∈ R^{T×D} and corresponding output sequence. For autoregressive RNNs, the Markovian state must be expanded to jointly include the hidden state and the sampled output value at each step. All parallelized methods operate on the entire T-length sequence simultaneously per Newton iteration (via parallel associative scan or parallel Kalman filter), achieving O(log T) depth per iteration with O(T) processors, rather than the O(T) sequential depth of standard evaluation.

## Method

The paper introduces two complementary techniques that extend DEER's Newton-based parallel scan. First, quasi-DEER replaces the dense D×D Jacobians ∂f/∂s in the linear recurrence with their diagonal entries, reducing work from O(TD³) to O(TD) and memory from O(TD²) to O(TD); a novel proof (Proposition 1) establishes that this quasi-Newton variant still converges globally in at most T iterations regardless of the Jacobian approximation used. Second, ELK (Evaluating Levenberg-Marquardt via Kalman) reframes the Newton step as a trust-region-constrained least-squares problem and shows the Levenberg-Marquardt update is equivalent to MAP estimation in a linear Gaussian state space model, solvable by a parallel Kalman filter in O(log T) time; the trust-region parameter λ directly attenuates the eigenvalues of the Jacobian matrices in the scan, preventing the numerical blowup that destabilizes undamped DEER. Quasi-ELK combines both ideas, yielding O(TD) work and memory with trust-region stabilization, and is empirically the fastest parallel method on wall-clock time while remaining numerically stable in regimes where DEER diverges.

## Results

On the autoregressive GRU evaluation (T=10,000), quasi-ELK achieves the fastest parallel wall-clock time at 221 ms, compared to DEER at 1,255 ms and sequential at 96 ms. Quasi-DEER reduces memory consumption by up to an order of magnitude versus DEER (O(TD) vs O(TD²)) while maintaining comparable wall-clock runtimes for evaluation and 2.5× faster training steps. ELK stabilizes convergence in regimes where DEER diverges, requiring only 172 Newton iterations versus 4,449 for DEER on the trained AR GRU, though each ELK step is ~13× slower per iteration.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is DEER (Lim et al., ICLR 2024), the original Newton-based parallel scan for nonlinear RNNs. Quasi-DEER matches DEER's wall-clock runtime for small networks but uses up to 10× less memory, enabling evaluation at scales where DEER runs out of GPU memory; for training, each quasi-DEER step is 2.5× faster than DEER despite requiring ~2× more Newton iterations. On the trained AR GRU where DEER is numerically unstable, ELK and quasi-ELK stabilize convergence without the reset heuristic, reducing total Newton iterations from 4,449 (DEER) to 172 (ELK) or 1,566 (quasi-ELK). Quasi-ELK is the fastest parallel method overall (221 ms vs. DEER's 1,255 ms), a 5.7× speedup over DEER, though it remains ~2.3× slower than sequential evaluation (96 ms) on this particular autoregressive task. On the Lorenz96 chaotic system, DEER is faster in wall-clock time than ELK due to cheaper per-iteration cost, but ELK is dramatically more stable at intermediate iterations, while quasi-variants are slower across all metrics, suggesting chaotic dynamics benefit from the more accurate dense Jacobian updates.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper is a direct extension of DEER (Lim et al., ICLR 2024), which first cast nonlinear RNN evaluation as a fixed-point problem solvable by Newton's method via parallel associative scans. The authors address two specific weaknesses of DEER—cubic work/memory in state dimension and numerical instability—by introducing diagonal Jacobian approximations (quasi-DEER) and a trust-region stabilization derived from the Levenberg-Marquardt/Kalman smoothing connection (Särkkä & Svensson, 2020). A novel global convergence proof (Proposition 1) for DEER and its quasi-Newton variants provides the theoretical foundation justifying both the diagonal approximation and the reset heuristic used in practice.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Nonlinear RNNs remain essential in neuroscience and scientific computing, and recent results suggest linear RNNs and transformers are fundamentally limited in expressivity compared to their nonlinear counterparts. The quasi-Newton and trust-region techniques enable parallel evaluation at state dimensions and sequence lengths previously infeasible (order-of-magnitude memory reduction), and the eigenvalue-damping interpretation of ELK provides a clear mechanistic understanding of why trust regions stabilize the parallel scan. However, the paper acknowledges that in autoregressive and chaotic regimes, even the best parallel method (quasi-ELK) remains slower than sequential evaluation, leaving open the question of whether fundamental limitations prevent parallel methods from universally outperforming sequential computation for nonlinear dynamics.
