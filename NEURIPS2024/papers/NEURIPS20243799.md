## Problem

Diffusion policies (DP) have become a dominant approach for behavior cloning in robotics, yet their adversarial robustness remains entirely unexplored. Unlike standard deep policy networks, DP uses a chained multi-step denoising process with injected randomness, making it non-trivial to apply existing gradient-based attack methods. This paper asks whether and how effective adversarial attacks can be crafted against visual-based diffusion policies across digital and physical threat scenarios.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243799/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB observation images (84×84 for most Robosuite tasks, 1024×1024 for PushT) along with white-box access to the diffusion policy's parameters and training dataset. It produces either a full-image adversarial perturbation δ (bounded by ℓ∞-norm σ, typically 0.03) added to the observation, or a small adversarial patch (17×17 or 50×50 pixels, covering ~5% of the observation) designed to be robust to random affine transforms. For offline attacks, a single fixed perturbation is trained on the dataset and reused across all rollout frames; for online attacks, a new perturbation is generated at each inference timestep.

## Method

The key technical contribution is replacing the costly end-to-end action loss (which requires backpropagating through the entire K-step denoising chain) with a single-step noise prediction loss: for untargeted attacks, maximize ‖ε_θ(τ*+ε_k, k, P(I)) − ε_k‖², and for targeted attacks, minimize it toward a target action τ_target. Here τ* is a good action sampled from the policy (or from the training dataset for offline attacks), ε_k is random Gaussian noise at a randomly sampled diffusion timestep k, and P(I) is the perturbed observation. PGD is used to optimize the perturbation under an ℓ∞ budget. Two attack families are defined: global attacks (perturbation added to the full image) and patched attacks (a small patch composited into the scene via random affine transforms during training for physical robustness). Ablations reveal that the attack primarily exploits the non-robust image encoder, significantly distorting the encoded conditional feature vector rather than directly manipulating the denoising dynamics.

## Results

On transformer-based diffusion policies across 6 manipulation tasks (10 datasets), DP-Attacker reduces task success rate from 100% to 0% with an ℓ∞ perturbation budget of only σ=0.03 (<3% pixel change). For example, targeted online attacks drop Can (MH) from 0.92→0, Lift (PH) from 1→0.02, Square (PH) from 1→0, and Transport (PH) from 0.72→0. Offline attacks also achieve strong degradation (e.g., Can MH: 0.92→0.08), and physical adversarial patches covering ~5% of the image reduce success rates by 50–100% compared to random noise patches which have negligible effect.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares DP-Attacker against three baselines: (1) random Gaussian noise (σ=0.03), (2) end-to-end DDPM action loss, and (3) end-to-end DDIM-8 action loss. Random noise has negligible impact on most tasks (e.g., Lift stays at 1.0, Can MH actually improves to 0.98), while DP-Attacker drops the same tasks to 0–0.02. Against end-to-end attacks on the Lift (PH) transformer model, DP-Attacker (targeted) achieves a success rate of 0.02 in ~1.3s, versus 0.52 in ~67s for DDPM and 0.24 in ~5.8s for DDIM-8. For untargeted attacks, DP-Attacker reaches 0.62 in ~1.8s compared to 1.0 (DDPM, ~70s) and 0.9 (DDIM-8, ~6.5s), making it both faster (35–40× speedup over DDPM) and more effective. The paper also shows that the vulnerability lies in the image encoder: DP-Attacker perturbations shift the encoded feature vector significantly more than random noise, confirming the attack mechanism.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the noise-prediction adversarial loss developed for attacking text-to-image latent diffusion models (Liang et al. 2023, Xue et al. 2023, Salman et al. 2023) to the fundamentally different setting of attacking the conditional image input of a diffusion policy, rather than the diffused latent value. It also extends the broader adversarial-robotics literature (which has covered DRL, imitation learning, and navigation) to the newly dominant diffusion-policy paradigm. The key conceptual departure is that DP attacks must handle a chained multi-step denoising process conditioned on visual observations and must degrade performance across an entire interactive rollout, not just a single inference.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As diffusion policies are rapidly adopted in real robot platforms (e.g., Mobile ALOHA), this is the first systematic demonstration that they are vulnerable to both digital and physical adversarial attacks, with a <3% perturbation reducing success from 100% to 0%. The finding that the vulnerability resides in the image encoder rather than the multi-step denoising process challenges the intuition that diffusion's iterative refinement provides inherent robustness. Key limitations include simulation-only evaluation, white-box access assumption, and no proposed defenses, leaving open the critical questions of real-world attack feasibility, black-box transferability, and effective mitigation strategies.
