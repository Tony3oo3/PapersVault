## Problem

Existing LLM pruning methods assign uniform sparsity across all layers, limiting how aggressively models can be compressed, while the few non-uniform approaches (e.g., OWL) rely on heuristics tied to outlier activations that degrade when outliers are absent. No theoretically-principled method existed for allocating layer-wise sparsity budgets based on how well-trained each layer actually is.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240289/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes only the weight matrices of a pre-trained LLM (no training data, activations, or gradient backpropagation required). For each transformer block, it computes the empirical spectral density of the correlation matrix W^T W, fits a truncated power-law via the Hill estimator, and outputs a single sparsity ratio per block. These ratios are then fed into any intra-layer pruning algorithm (Wanda, SparseGPT, Magnitude) to produce the final sparse model. The allocation is performed per-block (averaging PL_Alpha_Hill across all matrices within a block) rather than per-matrix, and a linear mapping with tunable range hyperparameters (s₁, s₂) and a normalization factor η ensures the global sparsity target is met exactly.

## Method

AlphaPruning is grounded in Heavy-Tailed Self-Regularization (HT-SR) theory, which establishes that well-trained layers exhibit more heavy-tailed empirical spectral densities (ESDs) in their weight correlation matrices. The method quantifies this heavy-tailedness using the PL_Alpha_Hill metric (the power-law exponent estimated via the Hill estimator on the top-k eigenvalues, with k selected by the Fix-finger method), where lower values indicate more heavy-tailed and thus better-trained layers. A linear mapping function then converts per-block PL_Alpha_Hill values into sparsity ratios: layers with lower PL_Alpha_Hill (more HTed, more learned signal) receive less sparsity, while layers with higher values (closer to random initialization) are pruned more aggressively. A key empirical finding is that shape metrics (PL_Alpha_Hill, Alpha_Hat, Stable_Rank, Entropy) consistently outperform scale metrics (Frobenius norm, spectral norm) for this allocation task, with PL_Alpha_Hill being the most effective. The resulting non-uniform sparsity vector is normalized to hit a target global sparsity and is compatible with any intra-layer pruning technique, enabling LLaMA-7B to be pruned to 80% sparsity with reasonable perplexity—a first in the LLM pruning literature.

## Results

At 70% sparsity on LLaMA-7B, AlphaPruning combined with Wanda achieves a WikiText perplexity of 23.86 (vs. 85.77 for uniform and 24.57 for OWL), and with SparseGPT achieves 18.54 (vs. 26.30 uniform, 19.49 OWL). At 80% sparsity with Wanda on LLaMA-7B, AlphaPruning reaches a perplexity of 698.56, reducing perplexity by 304.31 compared to OWL (1002.87) and by 5190.57 compared to uniform (5889.13). On zero-shot tasks at 70% sparsity with SparseGPT, AlphaPruning achieves 45.48% mean accuracy across 7 tasks, outperforming uniform (41.52%) and OWL (44.65%). At 80% global sparsity, the pruned LLaMA-2-7B model achieves a 3.06× end-to-end decode speedup on the DeepSparse CPU inference engine.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AlphaPruning consistently outperforms all eight baselines, with the largest margins at high sparsity (≥70%). Against OWL (Yin et al., 2023), the primary SOTA non-uniform allocation method, AlphaPruning reduces perplexity by 304.31 at 80% sparsity with Wanda on LLaMA-7B (698.56 vs. 1002.87) and by 0.95 at 70% with SparseGPT (18.54 vs. 19.49). Against uniform sparsity (Zhu & Gupta, 2017), the gains are dramatic: at 70% with Magnitude on LLaMA-7B, perplexity drops from 48419.13 to 231.01. Among the six additional layer-wise allocation baselines—Global thresholding (Frankle & Carbin, 2018), ER (Mocanu et al., 2018), ER-Plus (Liu et al., 2022), LAMP (Lee et al., 2020), rank selection (El Halabi et al., 2022), and layer-wise error thresholding (Ye et al., 2020)—AlphaPruning achieves the lowest perplexity at 70–80% sparsity in every configuration tested (e.g., at 80% with Wanda: 698.56 vs. 39918.56 for Global, 6263.79 for ER, 6013.91 for ER-Plus, 15647.87 for LAMP). On zero-shot accuracy at 70% with SparseGPT, AlphaPruning (45.48%) beats rank selection (42.90%) and layer-wise error thresholding (41.24%). The advantage is most pronounced for Magnitude-based pruning and at higher sparsity levels, where uniform and heuristic-based allocations degrade sharply.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes Heavy-Tailed Self-Regularization (HT-SR) Theory—originally developed for model selection and quality prediction (Martin & Mahoney, 2019, 2021)—as a principled basis for allocating layer-wise sparsity in LLM pruning. It departs from the dominant paradigm of uniform sparsity (Wanda, SparseGPT) and from the outlier-heuristic approach of OWL (Yin et al., 2023) by instead using the power-law exponent of the empirical spectral density (PL_Alpha_Hill) to determine how aggressively to prune each layer. The key conceptual departure is the finding that ESD *shape* metrics (heavy-tail structure) are more informative for layer importance than the *scale* metrics (Frobenius/spectral norms) that have been standard in pruning and regularization literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AlphaPruning is a lightweight, data-free, plug-and-play layer-wise sparsity allocator that can be combined with any existing intra-layer pruning method (Wanda, SparseGPT, magnitude), making it broadly applicable. Achieving 80% sparsity on LLaMA-7B with reasonable perplexity and a 3.06× CPU speedup is a practical milestone for deploying LLMs on resource-constrained hardware. Limitations include reliance on post-training pruning without full fine-tuning in the main experiments (though LoRA recovery is shown), and the linear mapping function and hyperparameter τ require per-model tuning. Open questions include whether the HT-SR-to-pruning connection holds for architectures without the characteristic heavy-tailed ESD structure, and whether the per-block allocation granularity is optimal for very deep or heterogeneous models.
