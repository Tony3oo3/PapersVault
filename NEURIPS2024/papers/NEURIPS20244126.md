## Problem

Existing sparsified Newton methods for entropic-regularized optimal transport (e.g., the SNS algorithm) suffer from three unresolved issues: the sparsified Hessian is not guaranteed to be positive definite (risking singularity in the Newton linear system), the method requires expensive warm initialization via Sinkhorn iterations, and the conjectured super-linear convergence rate lacks rigorous proof. This paper addresses all three gaps simultaneously.

## Input / Output

The method operates on the smooth dual formulation of entropic-regularized OT, taking as input two discrete probability measures (vectors a ∈ R^n, b ∈ R^m with positive entries summing to 1), a cost matrix M ∈ R^{n×m} (normalized to unit infinity norm), and a regularization parameter η > 0. The dual variables are collected into x = (α^T, β̃^T)^T ∈ R^{n+m-1} with β_m fixed to zero for identifiability. The output is the Sinkhorn transport plan T* = τ(α*, β*) recovered from the optimal dual variables, with convergence monitored via the marginal error ||T_k 1_m − a|| + ||T_k^T 1_n − b||, which equals the gradient norm.

## Method

The SSNS algorithm has two core components. First, a novel Hessian sparsification scheme (Algorithm 1) that applies a cumulative-sum thresholding operator select_small to the off-diagonal entries of the transport matrix T column-by-column and then row-by-row, while retaining the full diagonal blocks; this guarantees both a bounded approximation error (‖H(x) − H_δ‖ ≤ η^{-1}δ) and, crucially, positive definiteness of H_δ for any threshold δ ≥ 0 (Theorem 2). Second, a safe Newton update (Algorithm 2) that solves (H_δ + μ_k‖g_k‖I)^{-1}g_k for the search direction, where the shift parameter μ_k is adaptively increased or decreased based on the actual-to-predicted decrease ratio ρ_k, and the sparsification threshold δ_k = ν_0‖g_k‖^γ shrinks as the iterate approaches the optimum. This design eliminates the need for warm initialization, guarantees global convergence from any starting point, and achieves quadratic local convergence matching the dense Newton method, all with per-iteration cost O(n^2) from sparse linear solves.

## Results

On ImageNet (n≈m≈1000, ℓ₁ cost, η=0.01), SSNS converges in 13 iterations versus 217 for BCD/Sinkhorn (16.7× fewer iterations) with comparable runtime (1.52s vs 1.63s). As η decreases to 0.001, BCD fails to meet the tolerance (ε_tol=10⁻⁸) within 500 iterations on ImageNet, while SSNS still converges in 120 iterations (0.77s). On MNIST and Fashion-MNIST (n=m=784), SSNS achieves an order-of-magnitude runtime speedup over BCD. On large-scale synthetic problems (n=m=10000), SSNS exhibits quadratic convergence consistent with theory, while BCD shows only linear-like convergence.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SSNS dominates all first-order and quasi-Newton baselines (BCD/Sinkhorn [6], APDAGD [10], L-BFGS [20]) on both iteration count and runtime across all three datasets (MNIST, Fashion-MNIST, ImageNet), with the gap widening as η decreases. Against BCD specifically, SSNS requires 13 vs 217 iterations on ImageNet at η=0.01, and BCD simply fails to converge (exceeds 500 iterations) for η ≤ 0.0056 while SSNS continues to converge. Compared to the vanilla globalized Newton method, SSNS achieves comparable iteration counts but substantially lower per-iteration cost via sparse Hessian operations, yielding order-of-magnitude runtime savings on MNIST/Fashion-MNIST. Critically, the vanilla Newton method breaks down numerically at η=0.001 on ImageNet (nearly singular Hessian causes line search failure), whereas SSNS's shift parameter λ and sparsification mechanism prevent this failure entirely. The only setting where SSNS's runtime advantage over BCD is modest is ImageNet at η=0.01, where the transport plan is dense enough that sparsification provides less benefit.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends the Sinkhorn-Newton-Sparse (SNS) algorithm of Tang et al. (ICLR 2024), which first proposed using sparsified Hessian matrices to reduce the per-iteration cost of Newton's method for entropic-regularized OT from O(n³) to O(n²). The authors address three specific gaps in SNS: the reliance on warm Sinkhorn initialization, the lack of a guarantee that the sparsified Hessian is invertible, and the unproven conjecture of super-linear convergence. The sparsification design draws on the Levenberg-Marquardt regularization framework, and the convergence analysis builds on classical results for regularized Newton methods (Li et al., 2004) and inexact Newton methods (Dembo et al., 1982).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The positive definiteness guarantee for the sparsified Hessian (Theorem 2) is the most practically significant result: it ensures the Newton linear system is always well-posed regardless of the sparsification threshold, eliminating a key numerical failure mode that caused the vanilla Newton method to break down in the experiments. The quadratic local convergence proof (Theorem 5) confirms the conjecture from Tang et al. and validates that sparsification does not degrade the asymptotic rate. A noted limitation is that for OT problems where the transport plan is inherently dense (e.g., large η), the sparsification provides less benefit and the method's advantage over BCD narrows. Open questions include whether the adaptive sparsification strategy can be further refined for problems with heterogeneous sparsity patterns across the transport plan.
