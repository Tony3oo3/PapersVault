## Problem

Most NTK theory results on neural network generalization rely on a mirrored initialization that forces the network's initial output to be identically zero, a setting that diverges from the standard Gaussian initialization used in practice. This paper asks whether the non-zero initial output under standard initialization materially changes the generalization behavior of wide networks in the kernel regime, and shows that it does: the random initial function acts as an irreducible implicit bias that caps the achievable learning rate at n^{-3/(d+3)}, a rate that suffers from the curse of dimensionality.

## Input / Output

The analysis takes i.i.d. pairs (x_i, y_i) drawn from a bounded domain in R^d with a smooth regression function and sub-Gaussian noise, and a fully-connected ReLU network of L hidden layers with standard i.i.d. N(0,1) parameter initialization. The key unusual feature is that the network's initial output f_0^NN is a random function converging to a centered Gaussian process f^GP with covariance given by the random feature kernel (RFK), rather than the zero function assumed in prior NTK analyses. The outputs are (i) a characterization of the smoothness of f^GP in the NTK RKHS interpolation scale, and (ii) matching upper and lower bounds on the L^2 generalization error of the gradient-flow-trained network.

## Method

The paper establishes three technical pillars. First, it proves uniform convergence of the network function to the NTK kernel gradient flow predictor over all inputs and all training times under standard (non-zero) initialization, extending prior results that only covered the mirrored (zero-output) case. Second, it characterizes the smoothness of the Gaussian process f^GP: using the equivalence between the NTK and RFK RKHS interpolation spaces via Sobolev space theory on the sphere, it shows P(f^GP ∈ [H^NT]^s) = 1 for s < 3/(d+1) and 0 for s ≥ 3/(d+1). Third, it leverages the equivalence (Proposition 4.1) that a non-zero initial function f_0 in kernel gradient flow is exactly equivalent to shifting the regression function to f* − f_0, so the effective smoothness of the problem is always capped at 3/(d+1) regardless of how smooth f* is. Combining these with misspecified spectral algorithm bounds yields the upper bound n^{-3/(d+3)+ε}, and a matching lower bound Ω(n^{-3/(d+3)}) is established on spherical data using analytic spectral algorithm techniques.

## Results

The paper establishes that under standard (non-zero) random initialization, the generalization error of a wide fully-connected network trained by gradient flow is upper-bounded by $n^{-\frac{3}{d+3}+\varepsilon}$ and lower-bounded by $\Omega(n^{-\frac{3}{d+3}})$, even when the true regression function is arbitrarily smooth (Theorems 4.3–4.4). The key mechanism is that the Gaussian process limit of the initial network output has smoothness at most $\frac{3}{d+1}$ with respect to the NTK (Theorem 4.2), which caps the effective smoothness of the biased regression function $f^* - f^{GP}$. Empirically, the estimated smoothness of real data (MNIST: 0.40, CIFAR-10: 0.09, Fashion-MNIST: 0.22) far exceeds $\frac{3}{d+1} \approx 0$ for their respective dimensions, confirming that standard initialization would degrade performance under NTK theory.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is primarily a theoretical paper, so there is no standard baseline comparison table. The central empirical comparison is between standard (non-zero) initialization and mirrored (zero-output) initialization, conducted on artificial data for d=5 and d=10 (Figure 1). The log-log generalization error curves show that standard initialization yields a visibly steeper slope (slower learning rate) than mirrored initialization, consistent with the theoretical prediction that the effective rate is capped at $n^{-\frac{3}{d+3}}$ rather than the faster rate achievable under mirrored initialization. The paper also contrasts its findings with the implicit-bias result of Zhang et al. [56], which only analyzed the $t=\infty$ (full overfitting) case; the present work extends this to any finite training time $t$, showing the bias persists throughout training. The real-data smoothness estimates (Table 1) serve as an indirect comparison: since the estimated smoothness of MNIST, CIFAR-10, and Fashion-MNIST all exceed $\frac{3}{d+1} \approx 0$, the NTK theory with standard initialization predicts poor generalization that contradicts the strong empirical performance of neural networks on these tasks, highlighting a limitation of the NTK framework.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the NTK generalization analysis of Arora et al. [4], Lai et al. [35], and Li et al. [41] by replacing their mirrored (zero-output) initialization with the standard non-zero random initialization used in practice. It generalizes Zhang et al. [56], who identified an implicit bias from non-zero initialization only at the interpolation limit (t=∞), to the full training trajectory via a clean equivalence (Proposition 4.1) between non-zero initialization and a shifted regression function. The smoothness characterization of the initialization Gaussian process (Theorem 4.2) builds on the eigenvalue decay results of Bietti and Bach [6] and Li et al. [41], combined with Sobolev-space interpolation theory, to pin down a sharp threshold at s = 3/(d+1).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central finding—that standard initialization caps the generalization rate at n^{-3/(d+3)} regardless of target smoothness—exposes a fundamental tension: NTK theory with realistic initialization predicts a curse of dimensionality that real networks empirically overcome. This suggests the NTK framework, at least for fully-connected ReLU networks with Gaussian initialization, is insufficient to explain the strong high-dimensional generalization observed in practice. Open questions include whether the gap closes in the feature-learning (non-lazy) regime, whether alternative initialization schemes or architectures escape this bound, and whether the 3/(d+1) smoothness threshold of the initialization GP is specific to ReLU or holds more broadly. The result also provides a concrete theoretical argument in favor of mirrored initialization as a practical choice.
