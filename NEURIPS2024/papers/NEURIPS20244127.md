## Problem

DPO's performance is highly sensitive to its trade-off parameter β, yet the optimal β value varies with the informativeness of pairwise preference data — low-gap (informative) pairs benefit from aggressive updates (low β), while high-gap (less informative) pairs require conservative updates (high β). Prior work has largely treated β as a fixed, population-level hyperparameter, overlooking this joint dependence on data quality and the destabilizing effect of outliers present in real-world preference datasets.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard DPO preference triplets (prompt x, chosen response y_w, rejected response y_l) and produces an aligned autoregressive language model. A key operational detail is that the individual reward discrepancy M_i is computed on-the-fly from the implicit reward induced by the current DPO policy (log-ratio of policy to reference model probabilities), requiring no external reward model. The filtering ratio ρ (default 0.8) controls what fraction of each batch is retained, and the scaling factor α (typically 0.6–1.0) governs the magnitude of β adjustment relative to the base β_0 = 0.1.

## Method

β-DPO introduces two coupled mechanisms operating at the batch level. First, dynamic β calibration computes β_batch = [1 + α(E_batch[M_i] − M_0)]·β_0, where M_i is the per-sample reward discrepancy and M_0 is a moving-average threshold (momentum m=0.9), so that batches dominated by low-gap data receive smaller β (aggressive updates) and high-gap batches receive larger β (conservative updates). Second, β-guided data filtering scores each sample via a Gaussian p(M_i) centered at M_0 with dynamically estimated σ, then samples b×ρ samples without replacement to exclude outlier triplets whose reward discrepancies fall in the tails. The batch-level (rather than instance-level) design is critical: instance-level β personalization provably amplifies outlier effects and causes training instability, while batch-level averaging smooths the signal and introduces only one new hyperparameter (α) with no additional computational overhead beyond the standard DPO forward pass.

## Results

On the Anthropic HH dataset evaluated by GPT-4 win rate, β-DPO achieves 30.18% (Pythia-410M), 48.67% (Pythia-1.4B), and 57.07% (Pythia-2.8B), improving over standard DPO by +15.23%, +13.77%, and +10.79% respectively. On AlpacaEval 2 with larger models, β-SimPO reaches 30.48% LC / 32.13% WR on Mistral-7B and 46.03% LC / 40.18% WR on Llama3-8B, outperforming SimPO by +1.98/+1.57 and +1.65/+1.21 points respectively. The method also demonstrates robustness to sampling temperature, maintaining a 54% win rate at temperature 1.0 where standard DPO drops to 25%.

|---|---|---|---|

## Baselines & Comparisons

β-DPO consistently outperforms standard DPO across all three Pythia model sizes, with the largest gain (+15.23%) on the smallest model (410M) where data filtering contributes most, and a more balanced contribution from dynamic β on the 2.8B model. Against SimPO on AlpacaEval 2, β-SimPO improves LC win rate by +1.98 points on Mistral-7B and +1.65 points on Llama3-8B, confirming the dynamic β strategy generalizes beyond the DPO loss. For the DPO variants tested in Figure 5 (Middle), dynamic β substantially elevates all baselines: β-IPO gains 17.9% over IPO, while β-KTO and β-SPPO also show clear improvements, demonstrating the plug-and-play nature of the approach. The paper does not report a single unified table for IPO, KTO, and SPPO comparisons (these appear only in Figure 5), but the text confirms β-DPO wins on all three. Notably, the data filtering component alone (without dynamic β) already yields +10.84% on 410M, suggesting that outlier removal is particularly critical for smaller models with less capacity to absorb noisy gradients.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends DPO (Rafailov et al., 2023) by identifying that the KL penalty coefficient β should not be a fixed hyperparameter but should adapt to the informativeness of pairwise preference data. It departs from prior data-quality work in LLM alignment (LIMA, fDPO, RSO) that relies on external 'gold models' for filtering, instead leveraging the implicit reward discrepancy already computed within the DPO training loop. The batch-level calibration strategy draws on minibatch optimization principles, and the method is shown to be a plug-and-play extension applicable to DPO variants including SimPO, IPO, KTO, and SPPO.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that β-DPO is a minimal, zero-overhead modification to standard DPO that yields 10%+ win-rate improvements across model sizes from 410M to 8B parameters, making it immediately adoptable by practitioners. Key open questions include whether the approach extends to self-play settings where negative samples evolve iteratively, whether it scales to models beyond 8B parameters, and whether the GPT-4-based evaluation protocol (the sole metric used) adequately captures the quality gains. The finding that instance-level β personalization actively degrades performance (up to 42% drop) while batch-level calibration consistently helps provides a useful design principle for future adaptive-hyperparameter methods in preference learning.
