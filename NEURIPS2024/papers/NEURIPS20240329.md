## Problem

The fundamental privacy-utility trade-off (PUT) for private sampling under local differential privacy had not been characterized in a reference-free, minimax sense. The prior work of Husain et al. (2020) defined optimality relative to an arbitrarily chosen reference distribution Q0, making the resulting mechanism's utility depend on that external choice and leaving the question of the best Q0 open. This paper removes that ambiguity by formulating and solving the exact minimax PUT over all ε-LDP mechanisms without any dependency on a reference distribution.

## Input / Output

The mechanism takes a client's probability distribution P over a sample space X (either finite [k] or continuous R^n) and a privacy budget ε, and for the continuous case additionally requires a known reference function h with bounds c1, c2 such that c1·h(x) ≤ p(x) ≤ c2·h(x). It outputs a single private sample drawn from a sampling distribution Q(P) that satisfies ε-LDP. The key structural property is that the sampling distribution is obtained by clipping the density (or pmf) of P against bounds determined solely by ε and the space parameters, with a normalization constant r_P found via bisection so the output integrates/sums to one.

## Method

The core mechanism is a clipping (projection) operator: for finite spaces, Q*(x|P) = max(P(x)/r_P, 1/(e^ε+k−1)); for continuous spaces, q*(x) = clip(p(x)/r_P; b·h(x), b·e^ε·h(x)), where b is a constant determined by c1, c2, and ε. The paper proves this mechanism is universally optimal for every f-divergence simultaneously (KL, TV, Hellinger, χ², etc.), yielding a closed-form minimax PUT. The achievability proof shows Q*(P) is the f-divergence projection of P onto the feasible set of ε-LDP-compatible distributions, while the converse proof uses the data-processing inequality on indicator functions of extreme distributions combined with a novel combinatorial packing argument to lower-bound the worst-case divergence of any ε-LDP mechanism. The result is that a single mechanism, independent of the chosen f-divergence, achieves the exact minimax PUT for both finite and continuous data spaces.

## Results

The paper characterizes the exact minimax privacy-utility trade-off (PUT) for private sampling under ε-LDP, providing closed-form expressions for the optimal worst-case f-divergence in both finite (Theorem 3.1) and continuous (Theorem 3.3) data spaces. The proposed clipping-based mechanism is universally optimal under any f-divergence (KL, TV, Hellinger, χ², etc.). In numerical experiments over finite space (k=10) and 1D Gaussian mixtures (N=100, K=10), the proposed mechanism achieves strictly lower worst-case f-divergence than the baseline [35] across all three canonical f-divergences and all tested privacy budgets ε ∈ {0.1, 0.5, 1, 2, 5}, with the largest gap in the medium privacy regime ε ∈ [0.5, 2].

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is the mechanism of Husain et al. [35] (AISTATS 2020), which projects the original distribution onto a 'relative mollifier' defined by an arbitrary reference distribution Q₀. The proposed mechanism outperforms this baseline on all evaluated metrics: for finite space (k=10), the theoretical worst-case f-divergence is lower for the proposed mechanism across KL divergence, total variation distance, and squared Hellinger distance at every ε value tested; for 1D Gaussian mixtures (empirical worst-case over 100 random distributions), the proposed mechanism shows 'much lower' worst-case f-divergence than the baseline (using MBDE) for all f-divergences and ε values. The advantage is most pronounced in the medium privacy regime (ε ∈ [0.5, 2]), where the gap between the two mechanisms is largest. The paper also notes that the baseline's closed-form solution (equation 6) is incomplete—it fails to produce a valid probability distribution for some (P, Q₀) pairs, e.g., when P and Q₀ are point masses at different points—whereas the proposed mechanism is always well-defined.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the private sampling framework of Husain et al. [35] (AISTATS 2020), which required a fixed reference distribution Q₀ and achieved only local optimality within a 'relative mollifier' centered at Q₀. The key departure is formulating the privacy-utility trade-off in a minimax sense that eliminates the arbitrary Q₀ dependency, yielding a mechanism that is universally optimal across all f-divergences (KL, total variation, Hellinger, χ², etc.) rather than being tied to a single divergence. The proof technique draws on extreme-point analysis and a novel combinatorial packing argument, while the achievability proof leverages a generalization of [35]'s KL-projection result to arbitrary f-divergences via convex optimization (KKT conditions).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This provides the first exact, closed-form characterization of the minimax PUT for private sampling under LDP for both finite and continuous spaces, resolving an open question left by [35] about the optimal choice of reference distribution. The universal optimality across f-divergences means practitioners need not commit to a specific utility metric in advance. However, the continuous-space result requires the restrictive assumption that all client distributions are bounded between c₁h and c₂h for a known h, and the paper only handles single-sample release per client. Open directions include extending to multiple samples, incorporating geometric utility measures (e.g., Wasserstein distance) that capture the structure of the sample space, and Bayesian formulations.
