## Problem

Existing gradient-variation online learning results require a global L-smoothness condition (a fixed upper bound on gradient Lipschitzness), which fails to model optimization dynamics for important function classes such as LSTMs and Transformers. This paper asks how to design first-order online algorithms that exploit generalized smoothness—where the smoothness constant correlates with the gradient norm via a link function ℓ—to obtain gradient-variation regret guarantees, and further how to achieve universal (curvature-free) guarantees for both convex and strongly convex functions simultaneously.

## Input / Output

At each round the learner receives a convex function f_t over a bounded domain X (diameter D, containing the origin) and can query gradients ∇f_t(x) and the link function ℓ_t(x). The universal algorithm additionally takes a curvature pool H = {2^{i-1}/T} and a scale parameter B_0, but does not require prior knowledge of Lipschitz or smoothness constants. The output is a sequence of decision points x_t and, in the universal setting, a single algorithm that simultaneously achieves O(√V_T) regret for convex functions and O((1/λ) log V_T) regret for λ-strongly convex functions, up to doubly logarithmic factors in T.

## Method

The base-level algorithm extends optimistic OMD by setting the optimism vector at the intermediate decision x̂_t (rather than the previous submitted decision) and tuning the step size using a locally estimated smoothness constant L̂_{t-1} = ℓ_{t-1}(2‖∇f_{t-1}(x̂_t)‖). A trajectory-wise stability analysis ensures ‖x_t − x̂_t‖ is small enough to invoke the local smoothness lemma, so the gradient deviation term is bounded by O(L̂²‖x_t − x̂_t‖²) and cancelled by the negative Bregman-divergence term. For the universal algorithm, a new Lipschitz-adaptive meta-algorithm (Algorithm 1) is designed by combining optimistic Adapt-ML-Prod with a clipping mechanism that adds B_t² to the learning-rate denominator, eliminating the need for forced restarts while handling potentially unbounded gradients. A function-variation-to-gradient-variation conversion (inspired by dynamic regret techniques) decouples the meta and base levels, allowing the meta-regret to be bounded directly by gradient variation without cancellation-based analysis. The final two-layer algorithm (Algorithm 2) ensembles N = O(log T) base-learners with different curvature parameters, achieving optimal gradient-variation bounds for both convex and strongly convex functions without knowing λ.

## Results

The paper establishes gradient-variation regret bounds under generalized (ℓ-)smoothness: O(D√V_T + L̂_max·D²) for convex functions (Theorem 1) and O((1/λ)log V_T + L̂_max·D²) for λ-strongly convex functions (Theorem 2). The universal algorithm (Theorem 4) simultaneously achieves O(√V_T · log B_T) for convex and O((1/λ)log V_T + Ĝ_max²·log²(B_T)/λ + B_T·log B_T) for strongly convex functions without knowing λ. For the SEA model (Corollary 1), the universal algorithm yields O((√σ̃²_{1:T} + √Σ²_{1:T})·log(Ĝ_max·D)) for convex and O((σ̃²_max + Σ²_max)·log(σ̃²_{1:T} + Σ²_{1:T})) for strongly convex functions. The min-max game application (Corollary 2) achieves O(1/T) convergence rate under generalized smoothness.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical baselines; comparisons are between regret bound guarantees. Under global L-smoothness (the special case ℓ(·)≤L), the paper's convex bound O(D√V_T + LD²) matches the best-known first-order results of Chiang et al. (2012) and Yan et al. (2023) in both V_T and L dependence. The key advance over Yan et al. (2023) is that the universal algorithm uses a two-layer structure (vs. their three-layer) while still achieving near-optimal gradient-variation bounds for both convex and strongly convex functions simultaneously, at the cost of additional doubly-logarithmic factors (log B_T) and extra function-value queries. Compared to Zhang et al. (2022a), who handle unknown curvature but only under global smoothness with a simpler meta-algorithm, this paper's new Lipschitz-adaptive meta-algorithm (Algorithm 1) handles potentially unbounded smoothness and heterogeneous inputs at the meta level. For the SEA model, the paper relaxes the global smoothness requirement of Sachs et al. (2022) and Yan et al. (2023) to generalized smoothness, though the stochastic variance term degrades slightly from σ²_{1:T} to σ̃²_{1:T} due to the function-variation-to-gradient-variation conversion. The min-max game result extends Syrgkanis et al. (2015) from global to generalized smoothness while maintaining the O(1/T) rate.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies three previously separate research threads: gradient-variation online learning (Chiang et al. 2012; Rakhlin & Sridharan 2013), generalized smoothness conditions (Zhang et al. 2020b; Li et al. 2023), and universal online learning (Zhang et al. 2022a; Yan et al. 2023). Prior gradient-variation results required a fixed global Lipschitz bound on the Hessian, which the authors show is unrealistic for architectures like LSTMs and Transformers. The key technical departure is a trajectory-wise stability analysis that exploits local smoothness at intermediate decisions, combined with a function-variation-to-gradient-variation conversion (inspired by Bai et al. 2022) that decouples the meta and base levels in the universal algorithm, avoiding the cancellation-based analysis of Yan et al. 2023.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The results provide the first systematic gradient-variation regret guarantees under ℓ-smoothness, which is the most general smoothness condition currently in use for neural network optimization. The universal algorithm (Algorithm 2) removes the need for curvature knowledge while simultaneously achieving optimal rates for both convex and strongly convex functions, with applications to the SEA model and fast-rate min-max games under generalized smoothness. Open questions include extending the framework to exp-concave functions under generalized smoothness (where the flexible optimism and step-size tuning create technical obstacles) and accommodating the one-gradient feedback model. The new Lipschitz-adaptive meta-algorithm (Algorithm 1) is noted as potentially of independent interest for the PEA literature.
