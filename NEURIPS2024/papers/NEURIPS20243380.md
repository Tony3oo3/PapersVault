## Problem

LLM context windows have grown to hundreds of thousands or millions of tokens, but existing long-context benchmarks (LongBench, L-Eval) scale only to ~40K tokens, and needle-in-a-haystack tasks are too simplistic to differentiate advanced models. There is no scalable benchmark that tests whether models can actually reason across multiple facts scattered throughout extremely long natural documents.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243380/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each sample consists of a long natural-language document (drawn from PG19 books) with bAbI-style fact sentences interleaved at arbitrary positions, followed by a question requiring 1–10 supporting facts to answer. The benchmark is generative and scalable: the same base task can be padded to any target length (0K to 50M tokens) by inserting additional background sentences, with predefined splits at 0K, 4K, 8K, 16K, 32K, 64K, 128K, 512K, 1M, and 10M tokens. Output is a free-text answer scored by exact match against the ground-truth entity.

## Method

BABILong extends the 20 bAbI reasoning tasks (fact chaining, induction, deduction, counting, list/set operations) by embedding their short fact sequences into long PG19 book passages, forcing models to filter relevant facts from natural distractor text before performing multi-step reasoning. The paper evaluates 30+ models spanning standard LLMs (GPT-4, Gemini 1.5 Pro, Llama-3.1, Qwen-2.5, Phi-3, Mistral, Yi), context-extension methods (YaRN, Activation Beacons, LongChat), RAG pipelines (sentence- and chunk-level retrieval with FAISS), and fine-tuned small architectures (RMT and ARMT with a 137M GPT-2 backbone, Mamba-130M). Recurrent memory models are trained with a curriculum schedule that progressively increases sequence length from 1 to 32 segments (512 tokens each), then evaluated on sequences up to 600× the training length. Key findings: LLMs effectively use only 10–20% of their claimed context, RAG fails on multi-fact tasks due to inability to retrieve all supporting facts in order, while fine-tuned RMT/ARMT maintain high accuracy up to 10M and 50M tokens respectively.

## Results

On BABILong (average accuracy over QA1–QA5), popular LLMs effectively utilize only 10–20% of their claimed context window: the majority of 34 tested models maintain ≥85% accuracy on the simplest single-fact task (QA1) only up to ~4K tokens, while GPT-4 and Llama-3.1-70B extend to 16K and Qwen-2.5-72B / Gemini 1.5 Pro to 64K. When two supporting facts are required (QA2), only GPT-4 and Gemini 1.5 Pro exceed 85% without background text; for three facts (QA3) the best scores fall below 80%. Retrieval-Augmented Generation achieves a modest ~60% accuracy on QA1 regardless of context length, while fine-tuned Recurrent Memory Transformer (RMT, 137M params) sustains high accuracy up to 10M tokens and Associative RMT (ARMT) reaches a record 50M tokens.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among pre-trained LLMs, GPT-4 and Gemini 1.5 Pro lead on multi-fact reasoning (QA2/QA3) but both degrade sharply beyond 64K tokens; Llama-3.1-70B and Qwen-2.5-72B are the strongest open-source models, with Qwen-2.5 outperforming Llama-3.1 of similar size. Context-extension methods (YaRN, Activation Beacons, LongChat, LongAlpaca) all fail to maintain performance at their target lengths—YaRN and LongChat drop below 40% at 32K, and Activation Beacons achieve <40% on 32K contexts. RAG (both chunk-based and sentence-based retrieval) plateaus at ~60% on QA1 and collapses below random on QA2/QA3 due to inability to retrieve multiple temporally-ordered facts. Fine-tuned small models dramatically outperform all LLMs: Mamba-130M achieves near-perfect accuracy up to 128K but is too slow beyond that; RMT (137M) maintains >90% up to 10M tokens; and ARMT (137M) extends to 50M tokens, surpassing GPT-4 by a wide margin at all lengths. In the correlation analysis (Figure 4), BABILong diverges from MMLU starting at 2K tokens (correlation drops from 0.928 to 0.435 at 128K), whereas RULER maintains near-constant correlation with MMLU, confirming BABILong's sensitivity to long-context degradation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the bAbI reasoning benchmark (Weston et al., 2016) by embedding its 20 fact-based reasoning tasks within natural long documents from PG19, creating a scalable long-context evaluation that goes far beyond the 40K-token ceiling of existing benchmarks like LongBench and L-Eval. It departs from the dominant needle-in-a-haystack paradigm (RULER, Counting-Stars, InfinityBench) by requiring multi-hop reasoning over multiple supporting facts rather than single-fact retrieval, and by using natural narrative text as distractors rather than synthetic repetition. The evaluation also positions in-context reasoning against RAG pipelines and recurrent memory architectures (RMT, ARMT, Mamba), drawing on the recurrent transformer lineage of Bulatov et al. (2022, 2024) and Rodkin et al. (2024).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that popular LLMs effectively utilize only 10–20% of their claimed context window, with sharp degradation as reasoning complexity increases, provides a concrete and reproducible metric for the gap between advertised and actual long-context capability. The dramatic failure of RAG on multi-hop tasks (QA2/QA3) challenges the assumption that retrieval is a sufficient complement to in-context reasoning for compositional questions. The demonstration that 137M-parameter recurrent memory transformers, after task-specific fine-tuning, outperform GPT-4 and process up to 50M tokens suggests that architecture and training strategy may matter more than raw parameter scale for long-context tasks. Open questions include whether the limited bAbI vocabulary inflates fine-tuned model scores, whether results generalize beyond English and PG19-style backgrounds, and whether the benchmark's algorithmic simplicity underestimates the difficulty of real-world long-document reasoning.
