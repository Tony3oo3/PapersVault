## Problem

A long-standing open question in constrained online convex optimization (COCO) is whether an online policy can simultaneously achieve O(√T) regret and Õ(√T) cumulative constraint violation (CCV) without restrictive assumptions such as Slater's condition. Prior DPP-based methods either required Slater's condition (precluding non-negative constraint functions) or yielded suboptimal O(T^{3/4}) CCV bounds, while the best prior results without Slater's condition (Guo et al., 2022) still achieved only O(T^{3/4}) CCV at the cost of solving a convex optimization problem per round.

## Input / Output

On each round, the policy selects an action x_t from a closed convex admissible set X ⊆ ℝ^d of finite diameter D, after which the adversary reveals a G-Lipschitz convex cost function f_t and k G-Lipschitz convex constraint functions g_{t,i}. The policy must produce an action sequence {x_t} while guaranteeing bounds on static regret (relative to any feasible benchmark x* ∈ X*) and CCV (the maximum over constraints of the sum of positive parts). Computationally, each round requires only two gradient evaluations and one Euclidean projection onto X, making it substantially cheaper than prior methods that solve a full convex optimization problem per round.

## Method

The core technique constructs a sequence of convex surrogate cost functions f̂_t(x) = V·βf_t(x) + Φ'(Q(t))·β(g_t(x))⁺, where Q(t) tracks the cumulative constraint violation, Φ is a Lyapunov potential function, and V is a tuning parameter. The policy then runs an adaptive OCO subroutine (AdaGrad for convex costs, or a strongly-convex OGD variant) as a blackbox on these surrogate costs, requiring only a single gradient step and one Euclidean projection per round. The key analytical tool is a regret decomposition inequality (Eq. 6) that simultaneously upper-bounds both the Lyapunov potential Φ(Q(t)) and the scaled regret from a single inequality, derived by summing drift-plus-penalty inequalities and plugging in off-the-shelf adaptive regret bounds. For convex costs, an exponential Lyapunov function Φ(x) = exp(x/(2√T)) − 1 yields O(√T) regret and O(√T log T) CCV; for α-strongly convex costs, a quadratic Φ(x) = x² improves regret to O(log T) while maintaining the same CCV bound, and under a non-negative regret assumption further tightens CCV to O(log T / α).

## Results

For the COCO problem with convex cost and constraint functions, Algorithm 1 achieves O(√T) regret and O(√T log T) CCV (Theorem 1), breaking the long-standing O(T^{3/4}) CCV barrier. For strongly convex cost functions, the regret improves to O(log T) while maintaining O(√T log T) CCV (Theorem 2), and under a non-negative regret assumption, the CCV further improves to O(log T/α). A matching lower bound of Ω(√T) for both regret and CCV is established (Theorem 3), confirming optimality up to a logarithmic factor. Empirically, the algorithm achieves an AUC of ≈0.92 on the credit card fraud detection task (284,807 transactions, 0.17% fraud rate) using a simple single-hidden-layer neural network trained entirely online.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary advantage over all prior COCO methods is achieving O(√T log T) CCV without requiring Slater's condition or time-invariant constraints, while using only a single gradient step and one Euclidean projection per round (vs. solving a full convex optimization problem). Compared to Mahdavi et al. [2012], Sun et al. [2017], and Guo et al. [2022], which all achieve O(T^{3/4}) CCV, this paper breaks that barrier by a factor of T^{1/4}/log T. Against Neely and Yu [2017] and Yu and Neely [2020], which achieve O(√T) and O(1) CCV respectively, this paper matches or approaches their CCV rates but without the restrictive Slater's condition and time-invariance assumptions, and with substantially lower per-round computational cost (projection vs. Conv-OPT). For strongly convex costs, the paper matches the O(log T) regret of Guo et al. [2022] and Yi et al. [2023] while improving the CCV from O(√T log T) to O(√T log T) (same) or O(log T/α) under non-negative regret, and again with only a projection step rather than a convex optimization solve. The lower bound (Theorem 3) confirms that the O(√T) regret and CCV rates are information-theoretically optimal in high dimensions, validating that the logarithmic gap in CCV is essentially tight.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on Neely's drift-plus-penalty (DPP) framework from stochastic network optimization, repurposing the Lyapunov drift inequality as the analytical backbone for constrained online learning. It departs sharply from prior COCO approaches (Neely & Yu 2017, Liakopoulos et al. 2019, Guo et al. 2022, Yi et al. 2023) that either required Slater's condition, solved a full convex optimization problem per round, or achieved suboptimal O(T^{3/4}) CCV bounds. The key conceptual move is to treat an adaptive OCO policy (AdaGrad) as a blackbox subroutine operating on a surrogate cost function that encodes both the original cost and the Lyapunov drift, reducing the entire constrained problem to a single regret decomposition inequality.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper resolves a long-standing open question by showing O(√T) regret and Õ(√T) CCV are simultaneously achievable without Slater's condition, using only a gradient step and one Euclidean projection per round — a dramatic simplification over prior Conv-OPT-based policies. The matching lower bound (Theorem 3) confirms near-optimality up to a logarithmic factor. Open questions include extending to dynamic regret, bandit feedback, and the non-convex setting (as in the fraud detection experiment where neural network losses are non-convex). The OCS problem with relaxed S-feasibility and P_T-constrained adversaries opens a new sub-area for constraint-only online learning.
