## Problem

One-stage fairness-aware meta-learning methods (LtR, FORML, Meta-gDRO) suffer from hypergradient conflicts where the aggregated outer-optimization gradient opposes the descent direction of individual sensitive groups, causing unstable convergence and degraded performance-fairness trade-offs. No principled mechanism existed to resolve these conflicts within the meta-learning framework, and prior Nash Bargaining Solution work for gradient aggregation (Navon et al., 2022) relied on linear independence assumptions that are generally violated when groups share common objectives or features.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a training set, a small demographically- and label-balanced validation set partitioned into K sensitive groups, and initial model parameters. It outputs trained model parameters θ and a per-sample reweighting vector w that reweighs each training example in the current minibatch during the inner optimization. The bargaining phase (Stage 1) occupies the first T̄ epochs (15 of 50 in experiments), after which the fairness protocol β₀ is restored for Stage 2; if the bargaining game is infeasible at any step, the method falls back to the default fairness protocol adaptively.

## Method

The paper proposes a two-stage Nash-Meta-Learning framework. In Stage 1, group-wise hypergradients g_i are aggregated via the Nash Bargaining Solution: the update direction ∇L_α = Σ α_i g_i is found by solving G^T G α = 1/α (element-wise reciprocal), which maximizes the product of each group's utility (the projection of the update onto its hypergradient) and guarantees ∇L_α^T g_i > 0 for all groups, thereby resolving conflicts and steering the model toward the Pareto front. A key theoretical contribution is a proof of this NBS derivation that dispenses with the linear independence assumption on {g_i} required by prior multi-task learning work. In Stage 2, the weight vector β is switched back to the original fairness protocol β₀ (e.g., FORML's max-min gap or LtR's uniform average) to optimize the targeted fairness objective without deviating from the Pareto front. The NBS solution carries an implicit ℓ²-norm of √K, and under Lipschitz-smoothness and boundedness conditions, the paper proves Pareto improvement of the outer weights and monotonic decrease of the validation loss for any fixed β with finite norm.

## Results

Across seven dataset settings (six fairness datasets, with Adult Income evaluated on two sensitive attributes), the two-stage NBS-enhanced meta-learning improves overall AUC by up to 10.34% (FORML on Bank Telemarketing: 0.706→0.779) and reduces maximum group AUC disparity by up to 67% (FORML on Titanic: 0.033→0.011). The method also tightens 95% confidence intervals substantially (e.g., FORML on Bank Telemarketing: CI narrows from ±0.101 to ±0.027 on Overall AUC). Exceptions occur on Credit Default and Communities and Crime, where low feature-label mutual information in the validation set limits gains for most methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The two-stage NBS-enhanced method consistently outperforms its one-stage counterparts across most dataset-metric combinations. FORML benefits most dramatically: on Bank Telemarketing, Overall AUC jumps from 0.706 to 0.779 (+10.3%) and Max-gAUCD drops from 0.039 to 0.029 (−26%), while on Adult Income (race) Max-gAUCD falls from 0.290 to 0.134 (−54%) and Worst-gAUC rises from 0.540 to 0.688 (+27%). Meta-gDRO also improves on most settings (e.g., Adult Income race: Overall AUC 0.775→0.793; Bank Telemarketing: Worst-gAUC 0.649→0.683), while LtR shows smaller but consistent gains. Against the DRO baseline [18], the two-stage methods generally achieve higher Overall AUC and lower disparity, though DRO occasionally wins on Credit Default and Communities & Crime due to noisy validation data. In synthetic gradient-aggregation comparisons (Figure 5, Appendix A.2), NBS outperforms PCGrad [55] and CAGrad [29] by steering all initialization points toward the Pareto front, whereas those methods tend to favor groups with larger loss magnitudes and fail to converge for some initializations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the fairness-aware meta-learning framework of Ren et al. (LtR, ICML 2018) and Yan et al. (FORML, 2022) by identifying hypergradient conflict as a fundamental instability in one-stage approaches and proposing a two-stage resolution. The Nash Bargaining Solution for gradient aggregation is adapted from Navon et al. (ICML 2022), who applied it to multi-task learning under a linear independence assumption on task gradients; here the authors provide an assumption-free proof that makes NBS applicable to the dependent hypergradients typical in group-level fairness. The paper positions itself as the first to integrate cooperative bargaining into fairness-aware meta-learning, bridging economic mechanism design with bi-level optimization for group fairness.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of hypergradient conflict (less than 3% alignment after initial epochs) as a systematic failure mode in one-stage fairness meta-learning reframes why existing methods produce unstable or suboptimal results, and the two-stage NBS-then-fairness design offers a principled remedy with theoretical guarantees (Pareto improvement, monotonic validation loss decrease). The assumption-free NBS proof is of independent interest for any gradient aggregation problem where task/group gradients are dependent. Limitations include sensitivity to noisy validation sets (Credit Default, Communities and Crime) and the requirement of sensitive group labels in validation data; open questions include generalizing to non-zero disagreement points, understanding why Stage 2 training stays near the Pareto front, and scalability to many groups.
