## Problem

As LLM context lengths grow, KV cache activations become the dominant memory bottleneck during inference, yet existing quantization methods fail to represent these activations accurately at sub-4-bit precision due to outlier channel structures and suboptimal signpost placement. This limits the achievable context length on practical hardware, making long-context inference prohibitively expensive.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240040/cropped_page1_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes KV cache activations (Keys and Values) produced during LLM inference, along with 16 offline calibration samples of 2K sequence length to derive per-layer non-uniform datatypes and per-channel scaling factors. Keys are quantized per-channel before RoPE is applied, while Values are quantized per-token with online scaling factor computation. The output consists of dense low-precision tensors (2–4 bit indices into lookup tables) plus sparse outlier matrices stored in CSR/CSC format, which together reconstruct the full-precision KV cache for attention computations during autoregressive generation.

## Method

KVQuant combines four key techniques: (i) per-channel Key quantization to group same-magnitude values and mitigate outlier channels, (ii) pre-RoPE Key quantization to avoid the channel-mixing distortion introduced by rotary positional embeddings, (iii) sensitivity-weighted non-uniform quantization (nuqX) where per-layer datatypes are derived offline via Fisher-information-weighted k-means on normalized activations, and (iv) per-vector dense-and-sparse quantization that isolates ~1% of outliers per channel (Keys) or per token (Values) into a separate sparse matrix, restricting the dynamic range of the dense component. An attention-sink-aware variant retains the first token in fp16 to reduce sensitivity to quantization error at the sink position. Custom CUDA kernels fuse dequantization, RoPE application, and sparse-dense matrix-vector multiplication, achieving up to ~1.7× speedup over fp16 baselines while enabling 4.8× KV cache compression with <0.1 perplexity degradation at 3-bit precision.

## Results

On Wikitext-2, KVQuant achieves 3-bit KV cache quantization with only 0.07 perplexity degradation (5.75 vs. 5.68 fp16 baseline) on LLaMA-7B, and 4-bit quantization with just 0.01 degradation (5.69 vs. 5.68), while delivering 4.8× and 3.7× KV cache memory compression respectively. At 2-bit, KVQuant-2bit-1% achieves 6.01 PPL (0.33 degradation) on LLaMA-7B, compared to 11,779 for uniform int2 and 37.37 for ATOM-2bit. The method enables serving LLaMA-7B with 1M context on a single A100-80GB GPU and 10M context on an 8-GPU system, with up to ~1.7× kernel speedup over fp16 matrix-vector multiplications.

## Baselines & Comparisons

KVQuant consistently outperforms all four baseline families across every bit width and model size. At 3-bit on LLaMA-7B, KVQuant-3bit-1% (5.75 PPL) beats FlexGen-3bit (5.93), ATOM-3bit (6.17), nf3 (7.33), and int3 (10.87) by 0.18, 0.42, 1.58, and 5.12 PPL respectively. The gap widens dramatically at 2-bit: KVQuant-2bit-1% achieves 6.01 PPL versus 11.09 (FlexGen), 37.37 (ATOM), 3210 (nf2), and 11779 (int2), making it the only method that remains usable at 2-bit. At 4-bit, KVQuant-4bit-1% (5.69) edges out FlexGen-4bit (5.73) and ATOM-4bit (5.77) with the smallest KV cache footprint (17.3 GB vs. 17.3 and 16.6 GB). On long-context tasks (passkey retrieval, LongBench, RULER), KVQuant-3bit-1% also outperforms KIVI-2bit at a similar average bit-width, achieving 14% higher RULER average score (53.65 vs. 39.78) and matching or exceeding fp16 passkey retrieval rates up to 32K context.

## Relation to Prior Work

KVQuant extends the dense-and-sparse and sensitivity-weighted non-uniform quantization frameworks from SqueezeLLM [17] to the KV cache setting, adapting them to handle the unique challenges of dynamic activations (as opposed to static weights) and the interaction with rotary positional embeddings. It departs from prior KV cache quantization methods (KIVI, ATOM, FlexGen) that relied on per-token quantization and post-RoPE caching, instead introducing per-channel pre-RoPE Key quantization and per-vector outlier detection. The sensitivity analysis derivation follows Novak et al. [29], extended with minor modifications for the quantization context. The paper also builds on the attention sink phenomenon identified by Xiao et al. [42] to motivate keeping the first token in full precision.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work makes ultra-long-context inference (up to 10M tokens) practically feasible on commodity GPU clusters by achieving 4.8× KV cache compression with under 0.1 perplexity degradation at 3-bit, a regime where prior methods suffered unacceptable accuracy loss. The key open question is whether the offline calibration requirement (16 samples of 2K tokens) generalizes well to domain-shifted workloads, and whether the approach extends to architectures without RoPE or with grouped-query attention variants. The custom CUDA kernels achieving ~1.7× speedups over fp16 matvecs suggest the method is not just memory-saving but also latency-positive, which is important for production serving. A limitation is that the evaluation focuses on decoder-only LLaMA-family and Mistral models; generalization to encoder-decoder or MoE architectures remains untested.
