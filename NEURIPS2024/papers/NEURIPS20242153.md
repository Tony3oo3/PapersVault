## Problem

Data-constrained RNNs trained on partial neural recordings are widely used as surrogate models for inferring circuit mechanisms, but it remained unclear whether observing only a subset of neurons could distort the recovered attractor structure. This paper shows that partial observation can induce mechanistic mismatches—specifically, spurious line-attractor or hyperplane-attractor dynamics—even when the student's single-unit activity perfectly matches the teacher's, and even under ideal conditions with known inputs or white-noise driving.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single-trial recording of d out of D neurons (with d ≪ D, e.g., 5% subsampling) from a teacher RNN driven by either a known scalar input or isotropic Gaussian noise, and produces a d-dimensional student dynamics matrix whose eigenspectrum, time constants, and flow-field geometry are compared against the teacher's ground-truth attractor structure. A key constraint is that the student has fewer neurons than the teacher, so it cannot replicate feedforward-chain memory mechanisms and must instead encode long timescales through eigenvalue structure, which is the root cause of the mechanistic mismatch.

## Method

The core analysis uses a teacher-student setup of rate-based linear RNNs (Ornstein-Uhlenbeck processes) and derives a closed-form MAP estimate of the student dynamics matrix, Â_∞ = PBS Pᵀ(PSPᵀ + ρI_d)⁻¹, in terms of the teacher's stationary covariance S. For normal (e.g., symmetric) teachers, the student's eigenvalues are provably bounded within the teacher's spectral support, and approximate line attractors are faithfully recovered even under heavy subsampling. For non-normal teachers—specifically feedforward chains and low-rank connectivity with null overlap—the student's leading eigenvalues approach 1 as teacher size D grows, producing spurious line or hyperplane attractors despite all teacher eigenvalues being zero. The analysis extends to nonlinear (tanh) networks where eigenvalue overestimation beyond 1 yields spurious limit cycles and fixed points, and is validated across multiple inference methods (MAP, CORNN, BPTT, FORCE) to confirm the effect is not an artifact of any single estimator.

## Results

The paper demonstrates that data-constrained models fitted to only 5% of neurons from a functionally-feedforward integrator (all eigenvalues = 0) produce a line attractor score exceeding 6, identical to the score for a true line attractor teacher, making the two mechanistically distinct circuits indistinguishable. For noise-driven linear teachers, partial observation of a feedforward chain with D=500 neurons by a d=25 student drives the student's leading eigenvalue toward 1 as D increases, while for rank-r low-rank non-normal teachers at 5% subsampling, the student learns r eigenvalues approaching 1 (creating spurious r-dimensional hyperplane attractors). In the nonlinear (tanh) setting at 10% subsampling, the student learns a conjugate eigenvalue pair with real part greater than 1, yielding spurious limit cycles absent in the teacher.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's central finding is that the spurious attractor failure is not an artifact of any single inference method. MAP estimation (the paper's primary analytical tool) discovers spurious line attractors from feedforward and low-rank non-normal teachers, and spurious limit cycles from nonlinear low-rank teachers. CORNN [14], BPTT [52], and the FORCE algorithm [51] all reproduce the same qualitative failure—eigenvalue overestimation leading to spurious limit cycles—in the nonlinear low-rank setting (Fig. 5b–d). LDS variational inference [18], a more sophisticated Bayesian method, also learns vastly enlarged timescales from the same low-rank non-normal teacher (Fig. G.5). The one case where all methods agree with ground truth is the symmetric (normal) teacher: line attractor dynamics are faithfully recovered even at 5% subsampling, confirming that the failure is specific to non-normal connectivity. This universality across methods strengthens the paper's argument that the mechanistic mismatch is a fundamental consequence of partial observation combined with low-dimensional student architectures, rather than a fixable estimation bias.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the line attractor (Seung 1996; Mante 2013; Nair 2023) and functionally-feedforward chain (Goldman 2009; Ganguli 2008) literatures as canonical mechanistic hypotheses for neural integration, and on LDS variational inference (Linderman 2019) as the standard fitting procedure. It departs from prior system identification work, which has focused on predictive accuracy or qualitative recovery under full observation, by analytically characterizing how partial observation specifically distorts the inferred eigenspectrum for non-normal teacher dynamics. The paper also connects to the broader linear system identification literature (Simchowitz 2018, Sarkar 2021) but reframes the question from parameter recovery to mechanistic fidelity of attractor structure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result directly challenges the interpretive framework used in several recent high-profile papers claiming line attractor dynamics in biological circuits (Nair 2023, Liu 2024, Mountoufaris 2024), since partial observation alone can manufacture spurious slow eigenvalues in the student model. The finding that this failure is robust across MAP, LDS variational inference, CORNN, FORCE, and BPTT suggests it is a fundamental limitation of low-dimensional student networks rather than a methodological artifact. Open questions include how specifically targeted optogenetic perturbations must be to distinguish line attractor from feedforward mechanisms in vivo, and how to relate in silico latent-state perturbations to in vivo patterned stimuli. The paper underscores that explained-variance-based model selection is insufficient for diagnosing mechanistic mismatches.
