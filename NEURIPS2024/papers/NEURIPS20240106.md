## Problem

Existing remote sensing vision-language datasets are limited to single tasks, provide only brief captions lacking detailed object information, or rely on auto-generated annotations without human verification. Visual grounding datasets feature simplistic scenarios where objects are unique within their category, and VQA datasets use limited auto-generated question-answer pairs. This paper addresses these gaps by introducing a unified, large-scale, human-verified benchmark covering captioning, grounding, and VQA simultaneously.

## Input / Output

All images are 512×512 pixel patches derived from DOTA-v2 and DIOR detection datasets, with 26 merged object categories. For captioning, the model receives a single image and produces a 3–7 sentence description (avg. 52 words) covering scene attributes and per-object details (color, shape, size, absolute and relative position). For grounding, the model takes an image plus a referring sentence and outputs a bounding box (HBB for evaluation, OBB supported in the dataset). For VQA, the model takes an image plus a natural-language question and outputs a single word or phrase answer across 10 question types ranging from object-level to reasoning-level.

## Method

The core contribution is a semi-automatic annotation pipeline with four stages: (1) attribute extraction from DOTA-v2 and DIOR detection labels (category, bounding box, color, absolute/relative position and size, and uniqueness within category); (2) prompt engineering with structured instructions that feed image metadata and object lists to GPT-4V; (3) GPT-4V inference with iterative regeneration (up to 5 retries) to eliminate hallucinated or uncertain terms; and (4) human verification by domain experts (~120 s per image, 1,004 hours total, $6,200 cost) with a secondary re-evaluation of 2,000 images. For benchmark evaluation, state-of-the-art LVLMs (LLaVA-1.5, MiniGPT-v2, GeoChat, Mini-Gemini) are fine-tuned on the VRSBench training split using LoRA (rank 64) for 5 epochs, with CLIP-ViT(L-14) as the vision encoder and Vicuna-7B as the LLM, under joint training across all three tasks.

## Results

On the VRSBench captioning task, LLaVA-1.5 fine-tuned on VRSBench achieves the best BLEU-1 (48.1) and CIDEr (33.9), while GPT-4V (without object information in prompts) attains the highest CLAIR score (0.83). For visual grounding, GeoChat achieves the best overall accuracy at 49.8% (IoU@0.5), though even this best result remains far from satisfactory, highlighting the difficulty of grounding among multiple same-category objects. On VQA, Mini-Gemini achieves the highest overall accuracy of 77.8%, with the un-fine-tuned GeoChat baseline at only 40.8%, demonstrating a 35+ point improvement from VRSBench fine-tuning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper evaluates five models (plus an un-fine-tuned GeoChat baseline) on three VRSBench tasks. LLaVA-1.5 leads on captioning with BLEU-1 of 48.1 and CIDEr of 33.9, outperforming GeoChat (46.7/28.2) and Mini-Gemini (47.6/33.5) by a clear margin. GPT-4V, despite being a powerful closed-source model, underperforms fine-tuned open models on captioning (BLEU-1: 37.2) and grounding (Acc@0.5: 5.1%), largely because it receives no object information in its prompts. GeoChat dominates visual grounding with 49.8% Acc@0.5 overall, beating LLaVA-1.5 (41.6%) and MiniGPT-v2 (35.8%), though all models struggle significantly on non-unique object referring. Mini-Gemini achieves the best VQA overall accuracy (77.8%), narrowly edging out LLaVA-1.5 (76.4%) and GeoChat (76.0%), while MiniGPT-v2 lags far behind at 38.2%. The un-fine-tuned GeoChat baseline scores 40.8% on VQA and 12.9% on grounding, confirming that VRSBench fine-tuning yields substantial gains across all tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies three previously fragmented remote sensing vision-language tasks—detailed image captioning, visual grounding, and VQA—into a single benchmark, departing from prior datasets (UCM-Captions, RSICD, DIOR-RSVG, RSVQA, RSIVQA) that each target only one task. It builds on the semi-automatic annotation paradigm introduced by RSGPT (GPT-4V generation + human verification) but scales it to ~30k images and extends it to grounding and VQA. A key conceptual departure is the use of DOTA-v2's oriented bounding boxes and its higher object density (14.2 instances/patch vs. 3.3 in DIOR) to create more challenging, realistic referring scenarios where objects are not unique within their category, addressing a known weakness of DIOR-RSVG where 38.36% of objects are trivially identifiable by category alone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

VRSBench provides the first large-scale, human-verified, multi-task benchmark for remote sensing VLMs, filling a gap where prior large-scale datasets (RS5M, RemoteClip) relied on unverified automatic captions and smaller human-annotated sets (RSGPT: 2,585 pairs) were too limited for robust training. The benchmark reveals that even the best fine-tuned model (GeoChat) achieves only 49.8% grounding accuracy at IoU 0.5, underscoring that non-unique object localization remains an open challenge. The dataset is currently limited to RGB imagery; the authors identify multi-spectral, SAR, and temporal extensions as critical future directions. The semi-automatic pipeline (GPT-4V + ~120s human verification per image at ~$0.21/image) offers a practical, scalable recipe for building high-quality domain-specific VLM datasets.
