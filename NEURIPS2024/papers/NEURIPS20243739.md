## Problem

Learning control policies for nonlinear neural dynamical systems that can be formally verified safe over a finite horizon remains a major open problem. Existing safe control synthesis approaches either rely on forward invariance proofs that are intractable beyond the simplest dynamics, or provide only empirical (average-case) safety guarantees without formal certificates. No prior method can learn K-step verified safe neural network controllers for nonlinear systems with K large enough to be practically meaningful while maintaining high task performance.

## Input / Output

The method takes as input a ReLU neural network dynamics model F, a box-shaped initial state region S₀ (e.g., x∈[-0.5,0.5], θ∈[-0.2,0.2], v∈[0,0.5]), and axis-aligned unsafe region constraints S_unsafe. It produces a mapping dictionary H that assigns each initial state region to a specific verified safe controller π_θ, along with a K-step reachability safety certificate confirming no trajectory from S₀ intersects S_unsafe within K steps. The output is not a single universal policy but a collection of initial-state-dependent controllers, with the number of controllers (|Θ|) growing as the initial state region or task complexity increases.

## Method

The approach combines three key innovations. First, a curriculum learning scheme with memorization trains the policy in K phases (one per forward step), where a buffer B stores states that nearly violate safety at each phase to ensure safety is maintained across all prior steps; the loss function integrates standard safe RL loss with a differentiable bound loss L_Bound that penalizes α,β-CROWN verifier output bounds intersecting unsafe regions. Second, incremental verification decomposes the k-step reachability computation into sub-intervals (k₁, k₂, …, k_n), building computational graphs only for (k_{i+1}−k_i) steps to reduce GPU memory and avoid local optima from policy inertia. Third, after curriculum training, the method performs full K-step verification, clusters unverified regions by safety violation type, and fine-tunes separate controllers per cluster, yielding a collection of initial-state-dependent verified safe policies. The entire pipeline leverages the differentiability of α,β-CROWN overapproximation to backpropagate through the reachability bounds during gradient-based policy optimization.

## Results

VSRL achieves 100% verified safety (Verified-K) across all five environments, with Verified-Max horizons of 80, 50, 50, 50, and 15 steps for Lane Following, Vehicle Avoidance, 2D Quadrotor (fixed/moving), and 3D Quadrotor respectively — an order of magnitude beyond the best baselines (max 8, 13, 14, 8, and 4). Empirical safety over the full 500-step episode (Emp-500) is 100% for VSRL in every environment, whereas baselines range from 21.3% (RESPO on 3D Quadrotor) to 99.9%. Average reward is lower for VSRL in Lane Following (214±5 vs. 326–383 for baselines) but competitive or best in the remaining four environments (e.g., 401±4 in Vehicle Avoidance, 401±20 in 2D Quadrotor fixed).

## Baselines & Comparisons

VSRL decisively outperforms all six baselines on verified safety metrics across every environment. On Verified-Max, VSRL achieves 80 steps in Lane Following vs. a best of 8 (PPO-PID), 50 in Vehicle Avoidance vs. 13 (CAP), 50 in 2D Quadrotor (fixed) vs. 14 (RESPO), 50 in 2D Quadrotor (moving) vs. 8 (CAP), and 15 in 3D Quadrotor vs. 4 (CAP) — margins of 6–12×. On Emp-500 (full-episode empirical safety), VSRL reaches 100% in all environments while the best baselines still show violations: PPO-Lag drops to 87.8% in Vehicle Avoidance, MBPPO to 84.5% in 2D Quadrotor (fixed), and RESPO to 21.3% in 3D Quadrotor. On average reward, VSRL is the only method that underperforms in Lane Following (214±5 vs. 382±5 for MBPPO), but it matches or exceeds all baselines in the other four environments (e.g., 401±4 in Vehicle Avoidance vs. 393±35 for CAP; 401±20 in 2D Quadrotor fixed vs. 411±25 for PPO-PID). No baseline achieves both 100% verified safety and 100% empirical safety simultaneously in any environment.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant forward-invariance paradigm in safe control synthesis by instead targeting finite-horizon reachability verification, enabling the use of differentiable overapproximation tools (α,β-CROWN / auto_LiRPA) that are far more scalable for deep compositions of ReLU dynamics. It builds on curriculum learning (Bengio et al., 2009) but introduces a verification-specific twist: a memorization buffer that tracks near-violation states across all prior steps, ensuring safety is maintained throughout the entire K-step horizon rather than only at the current step. The incremental verification mechanism, previously explored in the verification literature (Wang et al., 2023b; Althoff, 2015), is here repurposed for the first time in the context of training provably safe controllers, and the initial-state-dependent multi-controller strategy addresses a fundamental expressivity limitation of single universal policies that prior safe RL work (PPO-Lag, CBF-RL, RESPO, etc.) does not confront.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ability to certify K-step safety for neural-network-controlled nonlinear systems is a critical gap in trustworthy autonomy; this paper demonstrates that reachability-based verification, combined with the proposed training techniques, can push certified horizons to 50–80 steps (an order of magnitude beyond prior art) while maintaining perfect empirical safety over full 500-step episodes. The key limitation acknowledged by the authors is that finite-horizon guarantees are strictly weaker than forward invariance, and the approach is tied to ReLU dynamics models, limiting generality. Open questions include whether the curriculum + memorization scheme scales to horizons beyond 100 steps, how it behaves with non-ReLU or stochastic dynamics, and whether the reward trade-off observed in lane following (214 vs. ~380 for best baselines) persists in more complex tasks.
