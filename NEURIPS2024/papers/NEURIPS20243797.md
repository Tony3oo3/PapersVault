## Problem

Identifying the relevant macroscopic variables that best characterise cognitive processes in complex systems has traditionally relied on intuition and expert knowledge, making it more an art than a science. While recent information-theoretic metrics (e.g., causal emergence via Partial Information Decomposition) provide quantitative criteria for detecting emergence in time series, it remains highly non-trivial to automatically discover which coarse-grained variables satisfy these criteria. This paper addresses the gap by proposing an end-to-end differentiable, data-driven method that learns maximally emergent representations without requiring a priori specification of the macroscopic variables.

## Input / Output

The method consumes multivariate time series X_t = (X_t^1, ..., X_t^n) where n is the number of constituent parts (e.g., 64 ECoG electrodes, 100 fMRI regions from the Schaefer atlas, or n bits in a synthetic bit-string). It produces a low-dimensional emergent representation V_t = f_θ(X_t) (typically 1–32 dimensional) along with the estimated emergence value Ψ. For the diverse-feature variant, the method additionally outputs a second representation V_t^B that is statistically independent from the first. The approach is fully self-supervised: no labels or external targets are required, and the only supervision signal is the information-theoretic objective itself.

## Method

The core architecture comprises a representation network f_θ that maps the joint microstate X_t to a coarse-grained variable V_t, a macroscopic critic g_φ that estimates the predictive mutual information I(V_t; V_{t+1}), and a set of n microscopic critics h_{ξ^i} that each estimate I(X_t^i; V_{t+1}). All mutual information terms are estimated using the SMILE differentiable lower-bound estimator, enabling end-to-end gradient-based optimisation of the emergence objective Ψ^S = I^S(V_t; V_{t+1}) − Σ_i I^S(X_t^i; V_{t+1}). The training dynamics are adversarial (analogous to GANs), with the microscopic critics pushing f_θ to minimise information about individual parts while the macroscopic critic pushes it to maximise predictive information about the whole; stability is ensured via skip connections in f_θ, critic pre-training, and multiple critic updates per representation update. To learn diverse emergent features, a fourth critic k_σ penalises mutual information between the new representation and a previously learned one, encouraging statistically independent features. Post-hoc verification uses the adjusted emergence criterion Ψ_A, which corrects for double-counted redundancy via Minimum Mutual Information, confirming that the learned feature is genuinely emergent.

## Results

On the synthetic bit-string dataset, the method recovers the ground-truth emergence value Ψ and successfully decodes the parity bit (the known emergent feature) with high accuracy, while the prediction-only baseline fails to recover it. On Conway's Game of Life, the learned emergent feature achieves 53% accuracy in predicting the glider's state (vs. 25% chance and 35% for the prediction-only objective). On real brain data, all three modalities yield positive post-hoc Ψ_A values: ECoG (1.2±0.19), MEG (2.6±0.52), and fMRI (3.3±0.44), confirming emergent features were learned across spatial scales.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares the emergence objective against three baselines. A prediction-only objective (akin to Deep InfoMax) fails to learn the emergent parity bit, encoding only the non-emergent extra bit and failing to reach the full predictive information of the system (~1.84 bits). A standard RNN trained with MSE loss on the bit-string dataset yields Ψ_A = 0, encoding negligible information about both emergent bits (parity and bonus) while capturing only the non-emergent extra bit. A standard MLP autoencoder on the fMRI dataset produces Ψ_A = −28 ± 5, confirming it does not capture emergent properties. Notably, concatenating the emergent feature with the RNN hidden state encodes strictly more mutual information about the future system state X_{t'} than either representation alone, suggesting the two capture complementary microscale and macroscale structure.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of information-theoretic emergence theory (Rosas et al. 2020; Mediano et al. 2021) and differentiable representation learning. The core emergence criterion Ψ, derived from Partial Information Decomposition, was previously only amenable to post-hoc analysis on fixed variables; here the authors make it end-to-end optimisable by pairing it with the SMILE mutual information estimator (Song & Ermon 2019), which was designed to address the high-variance problem of earlier estimators like MINE. The adversarial critic-representation dynamics draw on GAN-like training, but the authors show that the boundedness of SMILE's lower bound removes the usual GAN instability concern. The diversity mechanism for learning multiple independent emergent features extends the single-feature formulation in a straightforward but effective way.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first method to automatically discover causally emergent macroscopic variables from raw time series without a priori specification, and it scales to real brain recordings (ECoG, MEG, fMRI) across spatial and temporal scales. The finding that pure predictive-information maximisation (Deep InfoMax-style) systematically fails to recover emergent features, while the full Ψ objective succeeds, suggests that the marginal MI penalty is not merely a regulariser but is essential for steering the representation toward synergistic, whole-level structure. Open questions include training stability at larger scales, whether the method captures all emergent features (the bonus-bit discovery hints at under-exploration), and how learned features map onto interpretable cognitive or neural processes. The combination result—emergent and non-emergent representations being complementary for prediction—points toward hybrid architectures that may outperform either alone.
