## Problem

In many real-world RL settings (transactions, navigation), agents observe the immediate reward or transition realizations for all actions before committing to one, yet no provably-efficient learning algorithms existed for unknown environments with such one-step lookahead information. Prior work (Merlis et al., 2024) showed lookahead can multiply collected reward by up to AH, but only addressed planning with a known model. The naive fix of embedding lookahead observations into the state space is intractable—exponential for discrete rewards and continuous for continuous ones—leaving a gap between the known value gain and any practical learning algorithm.

## Input / Output

At each timestep h and state s, the agent receives either the full reward vector R(s) = {R(s,a)}_{a∈A} (reward lookahead) or the full next-state vector s'(s) = {s'(s,a)}_{a∈A} (transition lookahead) before selecting an action. The algorithm maintains empirical joint distributions over these vectors (sampled from past visits to the same state at the same step) and uses them to compute optimistic value functions via backward induction. The output is an action chosen by maximizing the observed immediate reward (or estimated reward) plus the optimistic continuation value, with the value function truncated at H.

## Method

The core contribution is deriving Bellman equations in the original state space that take the form V_h(s) = E_{R~R_h(s)}[max_a {R(a) + P V_{h+1}(s,a)}] for reward lookahead (and analogously for transitions), avoiding the intractable state augmentation. Building on Monotonic Value Propagation (MVP), the algorithms MVP-RL and MVP-TL perform planning by sampling from the empirical joint distribution of reward/transition observations rather than using only estimated expectations, with variance-based transition bonuses designed to be monotone in the optimistic value. The key technical tool is a uniform concentration inequality bounding |E_{R~R̂}[max_a{R(a)+u(a)}] − E_{R~R}[max_a{R(a)+u(a)}]| for all u in a bounded set, serving as a 'change of measure' between empirical and true distributions without requiring Wasserstein distances. For transition lookahead, a list-representation argument reduces the residual analysis from S^A possible next-state combinations to (SA)^2 list rankings, yielding tight regret bounds of Õ(√(H³SAK)) and Õ(√(H²SK)(√H+√A)) respectively.

## Results

The paper provides two main theoretical guarantees. For reward lookahead (Theorem 1), MVP-RL achieves a regret bound of Õ(√(H³SAK)) after K episodes, which matches the standard episodic RL lower bound of Ω(√(H³SAK)) up to log-factors. For transition lookahead (Theorem 2), MVP-TL achieves a regret bound of Õ(√(H²SK)(√H + √A)), which decomposes into a 'bandit rate' of Õ(√(H²SAK)) and a 'transition learning rate' of Õ(√(H³SK)), both matching natural lower bounds adapted to the lookahead setting. In illustrative examples, lookahead agents can collect up to AH times more reward (reward lookahead) or exponentially more reward (transition lookahead) compared to no-lookahead agents.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no experimental baselines; comparisons are made against known lower bounds and prior theoretical results. MVP-RL's regret bound of Õ(√(H³SAK)) is minimax-optimal, matching the standard episodic RL lower bound of Domingues et al. [2021] (proved for known deterministic rewards, hence also applicable to reward lookahead). For transition lookahead, MVP-TL's bound decomposes into two rates that each match a natural lower bound: a contextual-bandit rate of Ω(H√(SAK)) and a transition-learning rate of Ω(√(H³SK)). Compared to prior prophet-problem results, MVP-RL's bound is slightly weaker than Agarwal et al. [2023]'s Õ(n√T) (which exploits deterministic transitions) but applies to general Markovian dynamics, whereas Gatmiry et al. [2024]'s Õ(n³√K) requires a weaker feedback model. Any no-lookahead agent suffers linear regret (Ω(K)) in environments with non-trivial lookahead value gain, making the sub-linear bounds of MVP-RL and MVP-TL strictly superior.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the planning-only analysis of Merlis et al. (2024) on the value of lookahead information to the learning setting with unknown environments, providing the first regret bounds for RL with one-step reward or transition lookahead in general tabular MDPs. Algorithmically, it adapts the Monotonic Value Propagation (MVP) framework of Zhang et al. (2021b) by replacing estimated reward expectations with the full empirical reward/transition distribution during planning, and borrows variance-based concentration techniques from Efroni et al. (2021) for the transition analysis. Conceptually, it connects to prophet inequalities, the Canadian traveler problem with resampling, and stochastic action sets, but departs from all of these by handling general Markovian dynamics rather than deterministic chains or specific graph structures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes that lookahead information can be exploited with provable efficiency in unknown environments, closing a gap between planning theory and learning algorithms. The key technique—planning with empirical distributions rather than just estimated means—is a general-purpose idea that could transfer to other settings where the agent observes more than a single scalar per action. Open questions include extending to multi-step lookahead (where efficient planning is unclear), deriving model-free variants to reduce per-episode computation from O(KS²AH) to O(AH), and connecting to practical world-model-based RL systems that already simulate multi-step futures. The tabular restriction and one-step horizon limit immediate practical impact, but the theoretical framework is a clean foundation for future work.
