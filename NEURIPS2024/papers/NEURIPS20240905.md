## Problem

Mainstream LLM reasoning frameworks such as Chain-of-Thought, Tree of Thoughts, and Graph of Thoughts operate exclusively in the verbal domain, leaving them unable to exploit the visual-spatial processing channel that humans naturally engage for spatial and geometric reasoning. This single-modality limitation causes significant performance degradation on tasks that inherently require visual-spatial interpretation, such as geometry intersection counting, grid-based puzzles, and spatial optimization problems.

## Input / Output

VAP takes a purely textual problem description as input (e.g., a geometry problem stated in natural language with coordinates, a Sudoku board described with digits and dots, or a set of TSP city coordinates) and produces a final answer alongside an iteratively synthesized image. The image is rendered through external programmable tools (Python Turtle, Matplotlib) or a generative model (DALL-E 3) via API calls, and is refined across multiple iterations. A self-alignment gate checks whether the MLLM's literal description of the final image matches the initial high-level plan; if misaligned, the image is discarded and the process restarts, or the system degrades to text-only reasoning after a fixed number of failed trials.

## Method

VAP extends the reasoning exploration space from a single verbal domain to a dual-modality domain (verbal ∪ visual-spatial) through a three-step pipeline. First, a planning step prompts the LLM to produce a structured JSON plan specifying the drawing tool, initialization API call, per-iteration drawing and thinking prompts, and a termination condition. Second, an iterative reasoning loop has the MLLM alternately generate a textual thought and an image-update API call at each step, progressively building the figure while maintaining a trajectory of thoughts; the last step's input/output is appended as an in-context example to improve instruction-following stability. Third, a conclusive reasoning step applies self-alignment—the MLLM first describes the synthesized image, then a binary classifier checks alignment with the original plan—before jointly feeding the image, thought trajectory, and problem to the MLLM for the final answer. The entire framework is training-free and relies solely on prompt engineering over GPT-4V, with no fine-tuning or task-specific architecture.

## Results

Across four diverse reasoning tasks, VAP establishes new state-of-the-art among training-free LLM-based methods. On Geometry Intersection Counting (BIG-bench), VAP achieves 16.5% accuracy, outperforming the best baseline G-LLaVA (14.0%) by 2.5 points. On Sudoku, VAP reaches 35.5% correct rate with a 26.0% collision rate, surpassing ToT (22.6%) by 12.9 points. For Time Series Prediction (Darts), VAP achieves MAE of 556, and when combined with LLMTime preprocessing (VAP+LLMTime), it outperforms all baselines including LLMTime alone. On TSP with 10 cities, VAP achieves a 6.0% optimality gap, compared to 10.4% for CoT-SC and 4.1% for the traditional Fastest Insertion solver.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VAP outperforms all six compared baselines across the four tasks. Against CoT (Wei et al., NeurIPS 2022) and CoT-SC (Wang et al., ICLR 2023), VAP consistently wins: on Geometry, 16.5% vs. 10.0% (CoT) and 11.5% (CoT-SC, k=20); on Sudoku, 35.5% vs. 17.3% and 20.6%; on TSP (N=10), 6.0% gap vs. 11.0% and 10.4%. Against the task-specific geometry solvers Inter-GPS (Lu et al., ACL 2021) and G-LLaVA (Gao et al., 2023), VAP achieves 16.5% accuracy versus 6.0% and 14.0% respectively, with the gap widening further when the number of shapes increases to four or more. On Sudoku, VAP surpasses ToT (Long et al., 2023) by 12.9 points in correct rate and reduces collision rate from 44.0% to 26.0%. For Time Series Prediction, VAP alone (MAE 556) is slightly inferior to LLMTime (Gruver et al., 2023), but the combined VAP+LLMTime variant outperforms LLMTime and all other methods, confirming the two approaches are orthogonal and complementary.

## Relation to Prior Work

VAP extends the Chain-of-Thought prompting paradigm [3] from a purely verbal reasoning domain to a dual-modality space (verbal + visual-spatial), drawing an explicit analogy to the dual-subsystem architecture of human working memory (phonological loop and visual-spatial sketchpad). It departs from prior MLLM-based reasoning work (ViperGPT, VisProg, LLaVA-Plus) that uses MLLMs to call external tools on pre-existing visual inputs, by having the LLM *synthesize* the visual representation from scratch using programmable drawing tools (Turtle, Matplotlib, DALL-E 3). The iterative drawing-and-thinking loop with a self-alignment verification step distinguishes VAP from Visual Chain of Thought [32], which targets temporal reasoning over sequential visual data rather than spatial problem-solving from text.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first systematic, training-free demonstration that automatically synthesized images can serve as a genuine reasoning channel for LLMs across diverse spatial and combinatorial tasks, with consistent gains over CoT, CoT-SC, and ToT baselines. However, absolute performance remains low (16.5% on geometry, 35.5% on Sudoku), and the framework is validated primarily on GPT-4V with limited cross-model evidence, leaving open whether the gains transfer to open-source MLLMs. The black-box nature of the framework and its dependence on API access to proprietary models limit practical adoption, and the authors themselves flag interpretability and the relationship to cognitive science as important future directions.
