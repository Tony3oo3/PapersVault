## Problem

Converting scientific figures or hand-drawn sketches into semantics-preserving TikZ graphics programs remains unsolved: existing image vectorization methods produce low-level SVG primitives that lack higher-level semantics, and no tool exists to recover editable graphics code from compiled figures or to assist researchers in creating TikZ code from sketches, despite TikZ being the most discussed topic on TeX Stack Exchange (~10% of all questions).
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242700/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts a single RGB image as input—either a rasterized scientific figure or a hand-drawn sketch (real or synthetically generated via InstructPix2Pix)—and produces a self-contained TikZ/LaTeX graphics program as text output. The image is processed by a SigLIP vision encoder whose patch embeddings are concatenated and projected into the LLM's word embedding space via a feed-forward connector, so the LLM conditions on a fixed-length visual token sequence rather than raw pixels. The generated TikZ code is expected to compile under TeX Live 2023 and reproduce the input figure's structure and content.

## Method

DETikZIFY combines a frozen SigLIP-400M vision encoder with a pretrained code LLM (CodeLlama-7B, DeepSeek-7B, TinyLlama-1.1B, or DeepSeek-1.3B) connected by a trainable feed-forward layer, trained in two stages: connector pretraining on 734k figure-text pairs from METAFIG, followed by LLM fine-tuning on 360k TikZ programs from DATikZv2 with 50% of figures randomly replaced by on-the-fly synthetic sketches. The key inference-time contribution is an MCTS algorithm adapted to graphics program synthesis, where each tree node represents a partial TikZ program (n lines of code), rollouts are generated by the LLM conditioned on the node state, and two reward functions guide search: a compiler-diagnostics reward (±1 based on compilation success) and a SelfSim reward (cosine similarity between SigLIP embeddings of input and rendered output). The MCTS uses UCT selection, multi-node expansion with backtracking nodes to enable unbounded search at any depth, dynamic min-max rescaling of visual rewards, and preemptive stopping of rollouts that hit fatal compilation errors, allowing iterative refinement within a fixed computational budget without additional training.

## Results

On the DATikZ_v2 test set (1k samples), DETikZIFY-DS7B achieves the best overall performance with an AVG score of 0.965 for both reference figures and synthetic sketches, substantially outperforming GPT-4V (0.612 / 0.15) and CLAUDE 3 (0.148 / 0.189). In terms of code quality, DETikZIFY-DS7B reaches a cBLEU of 1.815 and TED of 57.227 on figures, while GPT-4V and CLAUDE 3 only achieve 6.5–18.5% of the cBLEU of the weakest DETikZIFY model. For efficiency, all DETikZIFY models achieve a Mean Token Efficiency (MTE) of 82–91%, meaning only 1–2 out of 10 inference runs require a second MCTS simulation, compared to 50–62% for the commercial baselines. Time-budgeted inference (TI) with MCTS further improves SELFSim by 2.2–5.6pp and DREAMSim by 4.9–8.7pp over output-driven inference without additional training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GPT-4V, DETikZIFY-DS7B wins decisively on all metrics: AVG 0.965 vs. 0.612 on figures and 0.965 vs. 0.150 on sketches, with DREAMSim 73.01 vs. 69.74 and KID 5.95 vs. 6.71 on figures. GPT-4V does outperform the 1B DETikZIFY variants on reference figures (AVG 0.612 vs. 0.207 for TL₁.₁B and 0.572 for DS₁.₃B), but loses to them on synthetic sketches (0.150 vs. 0.454 and 0.642). Against CLAUDE 3, all DETikZIFY models dominate across every metric, with the gap being largest on cBLEU (1.17–1.82 vs. 0.111) and MTE (82–91% vs. 51.8%). The SVG-based LIVE baseline (Ma et al., 2022) underperforms all models: its DREAMSim of 57.08 is 7.8–16pp below the weakest DETikZIFY model, and its KID of 324 is over 18× higher than DETikZIFY-DS7B's 5.95, confirming that low-level vectorization is ill-suited for semantic scientific figure synthesis. In the human evaluation on SketchFIG, DETikZIFY-DS7B with MCTS (TI) achieves a mean BWS score of 0.39, surpassing GPT-4V in both OI (0.09) and TI (−0.16), while GPT-4V's self-refinement actually degrades its output quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the visual instruction tuning paradigm (Liu et al., 2023b,a) to the specific domain of TikZ graphics program synthesis, building directly on the authors' prior AutomaTikZ work (Belouadi et al., 2024) which focused on text-guided generation with images as secondary input. It departs from prior image-to-graphics-program work (Ellis et al., 2018) by targeting real scientific figures and hand-drawn sketches rather than synthetic basic shapes, and from image vectorization approaches by arguing that semantics-preserving TikZ programs are superior to low-level SVG primitives for scientific contexts. The MCTS-based iterative refinement adapts the framework from Brandfonbrener et al. (2024) to graphics programs, introducing compiler-diagnostics and perceptual-similarity reward functions that are specific to the TikZ/LaTeX compilation pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first viable system for recovering semantics-preserving TikZ programs from rasterized scientific figures or hand-drawn sketches, addressing a clear unmet need in the scientific publishing community (nearly 10% of TeX Stack Exchange questions involve TikZ). The DATikZv2 dataset (360k+ graphics) and SketchFig (549 figure-sketch pairs) provide substantial resources for future research in graphics program synthesis. Open questions include whether the MCTS refinement strategy generalizes to other graphics languages (MetaPost, PSTricks, Asymptote), whether reinforcement learning from the perceptual similarity reward can replace the inference-time search, and how to handle the inherent difficulty of comparing open models against proprietary systems with unstable performance. The finding that GPT-4V's self-refinement actively degrades quality while MCTS consistently improves it raises broader questions about the limits of self-correction in general-purpose LLMs for structured code generation.
