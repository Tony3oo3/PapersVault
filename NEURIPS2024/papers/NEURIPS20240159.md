## Problem

Existing radar semantic segmentation (RSS) frameworks underutilize the temporal information inherent in sequential radar data, typically relying on 3D convolution or ignoring time entirely. The paper identifies that 3D convolution is suboptimal for RSS because it naturally predicts at the center of its receptive field rather than at the current (latest) time step, and that self-attention's O(T²) pairwise computation is wasteful when only the target frame requires prediction. This motivates a new temporal modeling paradigm tailored to the causal, current-frame-prediction nature of RSS.

## Input / Output

The network consumes a sequence of τ+1 consecutive multi-view radar frames (default τ=4, i.e., 5 frames), where each frame is a 3D RAD tensor (256×256×64) compressed into three 2D views: RD (256×64), AD (256×64), and RA (256×256). The output is pixel-wise (cell-wise) segmentation masks on the RD and RA views for the current/target frame only, with 4 classes (pedestrian, cyclist, vehicle, background) on CARRADA. A key design property is that the input time length is adjustable at inference without changing the parameter count, since all temporal operations use shared kernels.

## Method

TARSS-Net is a CAED-based multi-view network whose core novelty is the Temporal Relation-Aware Module (TRAM), which implements a 'data-driven temporal information aggregation with learned target-history relations' paradigm. TRAM consists of two blocks: (1) TH-TRE, which uses a temporal-relation-inception convolution (TRIC) to project all frames into a common feature space via a shared 2D conv (K1), then concatenates the target frame's features with each historical frame's features and applies a second shared conv (K2) to produce per-pair relation embeddings in O(T) time; and (2) TRAP, a learnable attentive pooling layer that estimates the contribution weight of each historical frame's relation embedding (via depth-compressed or spatio-compressed pathways with softmax normalization) and performs a weighted summation with a skip connection to produce the enhanced target representation. Two TRAP variants are provided: Depth-TRAP (operating on the channel/semantic dimension with sinusoidal position encoding) and Spatio-TRAP (operating on the spatial dimension with 2D softmax contribution maps). The entire TRAM module is plug-and-play into any 2D/3D convolutional RSS backbone and maintains constant parameter count regardless of input sequence length.

## Results

On CARRADA, TARSS-Net_D (6.3M params) achieves the best RD-view mIoU of 63.4% and mDice of 75.2%, outperforming all baselines including PKCIn-Net (60.7% mIoU) and TransRSS (60.4% mIoU). On the RA view, TARSS-Net_D reaches 41.4% mIoU, slightly trailing PKCIn-Net (43.1%) and T-RODNet (43.5%), but the global average across both views remains the highest. On KuRALS (single-view RD), TARSS-Net_D achieves 58.4% mIoU and 67.1% mDice, again the best among compared methods. The model runs at 23 FPS (multi-view) on a single RTX 3090, sufficient for real-time deployment.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TARSS-Net_D clearly dominates on the RD view, achieving 63.4% mIoU versus 60.7% for PKCIn-Net (same 6.3M param scale), 60.4% for TransRSS, and 56.1% for TMVA-Net — a 2.7–7.3 point margin over the nearest competitors. On the RA view, however, TARSS-Net_D (41.4% mIoU) trails PKCIn-Net (43.1%), T-RODNet (43.5%), and TransRSS (43.0%), which the authors attribute to the limited temporal variation in RA data from low-cost FMCW radars. In terms of parameter efficiency, TARSS-Net matches PKCIn-Net at ~6.3M parameters while surpassing it on RD view, and is 25× smaller than T-RODNet (162M) yet competitive on RA view. On CARRADA-RAC (corrected RA annotations), TARSS-Net_D achieves the best global mIoU (55.1%) and mDice (66.7%), confirming robustness to annotation quality. On the out-of-domain KuRALS dataset (Ku-band, UAV/sea monitoring), TARSS-Net_D again leads with 58.4% RD mIoU, demonstrating generalization beyond the driving-scenario CARRADA data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the multi-view CAED framework of Ouaknine et al. (TMVA-Net, ICCV 2021), adopting its basic encoders, latent space encoder, and decoder architecture, while replacing the 3D-convolution-based temporal aggregation with a novel Temporal Relation-Aware Module (TRAM). Conceptually, it departs from both causal temporal modeling (RNN/HMM) and parallelized sequence representation (3DConv, full self-attention) by proposing a target-history relation encoding paradigm: instead of modeling pairwise relations across all time steps (O(T²) as in Transformers) or relying on local receptive fields (3DConv), TRAM explicitly encodes relations between the current target frame and each historical frame in parallel (O(T)), then uses a learnable attentive pooling to reweight their contributions. The module is designed to be independently insertable into arbitrary 2D/3DConv-based networks, positioning it as a drop-in temporal enhancement rather than a full architecture redesign.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine underexploited dimension in radar semantic segmentation—temporal information—by providing a principled design framework (five explicit principles) and a practical module that achieves SOTA with only ~6.3M parameters while supporting variable input sequence lengths without parameter growth, which is important for real-time deployment. Key limitations include weaker RA-view performance (where temporal cues are less informative due to poor angular resolution), inference speed still trailing the simpler TMVA-Net, and the absence of classical radar signal processing priors that PeakConv exploits. Open questions include whether the target-history relation paradigm transfers to other sensing modalities, how to further improve RA-view segmentation, and whether pre-filtering low-importance historical frames could extend effective temporal context without additional cost.
