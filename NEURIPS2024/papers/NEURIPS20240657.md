## Problem

Pre-trained federated learning still requires hundreds of communication rounds to converge, and simply increasing local training steps hits a ceiling because client drift under data heterogeneity degrades optimization consistency. Existing model interpolation methods (Model Soups, DiWA) can locate connected low-loss valleys but demand training dozens of models plus a time-consuming selection phase, making them impractical within FL's per-client compute budget. The paper asks whether the connected low-loss valley benefits of model soups can be achieved inside FL's local training loop without redundant model training or a separate selection step.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240657/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

Each client receives a pre-trained model (ResNet-18/50, ViT-Base, or Llama-2-7b with LoRA) as initialization and fine-tunes it on its local Non-IID partition (Dirichlet-distributed for label shift, domain-specific for feature shift). During local training the client maintains a pool of N models (default 4) trained sequentially with random interpolation; the local output sent to the server is the uniform average of all pool members. The server performs standard FedAvg weighted averaging across clients to produce the global model, which outputs class labels (vision) or generated text (LLM instruction tuning).

## Method

LSS replaces standard local SGD with a sequential random model interpolation procedure: a growing pool of models is maintained, and at each gradient step a uniformly-weighted random interpolation of the pool is used for the forward/backward pass while only the newest model's weights are updated, eliminating the separate model-selection phase required by Model Soups and DiWA. Two regularization terms are added to the local loss: a diversity term that maximizes the L2 distance between the current model and other pool members (expanding the low-loss region to cover more of the basin), and an affinity term that minimizes the L2 distance to the pre-trained initialization (preventing the basin from drifting away from the shared starting point and ensuring overlapping connected regions across clients). Theoretically, the paper derives a convergence bound showing these terms bound the inter-client update drift, and proves a proposition characterizing the condition under which increasing local steps actually reduces communication rounds. The approach is architecture-agnostic and compatible with parameter-efficient fine-tuning (LoRA), demonstrated on ResNet, ViT, and Llama-2-7b across four vision datasets and a multilingual LLM instruction-tuning benchmark.

## Results

On FMNIST (label shift), LSS achieves 72.66% accuracy after just 1 communication round, surpassing the best baseline DiWA (63.21%) by 9.45 points. On CIFAR-10, LSS reaches 65.96% at R=1 and 75.16% at R=3, outperforming DiWA (61.32% and 68.05%) by 4.64 and 7.11 points respectively. Under feature shift, LSS achieves 72.86% on Digit-5 (R=1) and 41.35% on DomainNet (R=3), consistently leading all baselines across both distribution shift scenarios.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LSS outperforms all 11 baselines across all four datasets and both distribution shift settings. Against conventional FL methods (FedAvg, FedProx, MOON, FedBN, FedFomo, FedRep, FedBABU), LSS achieves substantially higher accuracy — e.g., 72.66% vs. 36.20% (FedRep, best FL baseline) on FMNIST at R=1, nearly doubling the accuracy. Against weight-averaging-based domain generalization methods (SWA, SWAD, Soups, DiWA), LSS still leads by a clear margin — 65.96% vs. 61.32% (DiWA) on CIFAR-10 at R=1, and 75.16% vs. 68.05% at R=3. The gap is most dramatic at R=1, where LSS gains 9.45 points over DiWA on FMNIST and 11.32 points on Digit-5 (feature shift). Under feature shift (Table 2), LSS maintains its lead over DiWA by 2.99 points on DomainNet at R=3 (41.35% vs. 38.32%) and 1.14 points on Digit-5 at R=3 (92.97% vs. 88.83%). The paper also shows LSS generalizes to ViT architectures (67.48% vs. 60.35% FedAvg on CIFAR-10 at R=1) and to LLM instruction tuning with Llama-2-7b + LoRA on the Aya multilingual dataset.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts centralized model interpolation techniques (Model Soups, DiWA) to the federated learning setting, specifically for pre-trained model fine-tuning under data heterogeneity. It departs from prior soups-based methods by eliminating the two major computational bottlenecks—time-consuming model selection from a large pool and extensive redundant training—through sequential random interpolation during training and diversity/affinity regularization that guides which models to train. The convergence analysis builds on the federated optimization framework of Wang et al. [50] but introduces a novel inter-client update bound that accounts for the proposed regularization terms, connecting pre-trained initialization (which reduces gradient dissimilarity ζ) with connectivity-preserving local training to justify extreme communication round reduction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method offers a practical path to reducing FL communication from hundreds of rounds to roughly 6–8, which is significant for edge-device and cross-silo deployments where each round incurs real network and energy costs. The theoretical analysis clarifies why simply increasing local steps fails under Non-IID data (the ζ+c error term is irreducible by more local updates) and why pre-trained initialization is a necessary precondition for the method's effectiveness. Key limitations include a 4× memory overhead from maintaining the model pool, convergence analysis restricted to convex local objectives, and preliminary (single-dataset) evidence for LLM applicability. Open questions include whether the approach extends to truly non-convex deep networks, scales to hundreds of clients without memory blowup, and whether the memory cost can be reduced via low-rank or quantized model pools.
