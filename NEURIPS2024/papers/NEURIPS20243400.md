## Problem

Structured pruning of LLMs effectively reduces parameter count and inference cost on conventional hardware, but the Optimal Brain Surgeon (OBS) framework—which provides closed-form parameter compensation to mitigate pruning loss—has not been adapted to structured pruning. Two obstacles prevent direct application: OBS operates at single-parameter granularity while structured pruning removes entire columns or heads (causing large numerical errors), and OBS's layer-wise locality prevents rational allocation of non-uniform pruning ratios across layers. The paper addresses both challenges to enable near-optimal structured pruning of billion-parameter models within one hour on a single GPU.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pretrained Transformer LLM (e.g., LLaMA-7B/13B/30B) and a small calibration set of 256 sequences of 2048 tokens sampled from C4. It produces a structurally pruned model with reduced attention head count and FFN intermediate dimension, where remaining weights are updated via second-order compensation. The entire pruning process is layer-wise (only one layer's weights and input features are loaded at a time), requiring ~7 GB GPU memory for a 7B model and ~18 minutes for 20% pruning. An optional LoRA fine-tuning step on Alpaca (1 epoch, 2×A100) can further recover performance.

## Method

SlimGPT extends OBS to structured column pruning by deriving a column-wise compensation formula (Eq. 4) that removes one column and updates all remaining columns simultaneously. The core acceleration is Batched Greedy Pruning: for attention heads, a grouped Cholesky decomposition of the inverse Hessian (split into per-head principal submatrices and decomposed in parallel via GPU) pre-computes all head-wise pruning errors at once, avoiding the infeasible sequential column-by-column search; for FFNs, a Dynamic Group Size strategy (starting at 1024 columns per iteration and shrinking to 8) balances pruning speed against near-optimality. Two algebraic lemmas—permutation invariance of matrix inverses and the principal-submatrix property of Cholesky decomposition—enable the grouped computation without repeated full matrix inversions. To address error accumulation inherent in layer-wise pruning, SlimGPT applies an Incremental Pruning Ratio that follows a logarithmically increasing schedule from the first to the last layer, reducing aggressive pruning in shallow layers where errors propagate most severely.

## Results

On LLaMA-7B pruned by 20%, SlimGPT achieves a WikiText2 PPL of 16.68 and a zero-shot commonsense reasoning average of 65.07, outperforming all baselines. At the more aggressive 50% pruning ratio, SlimGPT reaches PPL 31.07 and zero-shot average 53.76, surpassing LLM-Pruner (PPL 40.64, avg 48.35) by ~10 PPL points and ~5.4 points on zero-shot. Even without post-training (SlimGPT w/o tune), the 50%-pruned model attains PPL 38.83 and zero-shot average 52.23, still exceeding the best baseline. On LLaMA-30B at 20% pruning, SlimGPT's zero-shot average (72.56) slightly exceeds the unpruned model (71.92).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SlimGPT consistently outperforms LLM-Pruner on both PPL and zero-shot average across all four pruning ratios (20%–50%); at 50% pruning the gap widens to ~9.6 PPL points (31.07 vs 40.64) and ~5.4 zero-shot points (53.76 vs 48.35). Against Compresso, SlimGPT leads by 5.6 zero-shot points at 20% (65.07 vs 59.51) and by 6.8 points at 33% (61.41 vs 54.59); Compresso does not report PPL. Versus LoRAPrune, SlimGPT wins on zero-shot average at every ratio (e.g., 65.07 vs 60.06 at 20%; 53.76 vs 49.72 at 50%), while LoRAPrune achieves a marginally lower PPL at 50% (30.12 vs 31.07), indicating a trade-off between language modeling and reasoning. Notably, SlimGPT without any post-training already surpasses LLM-Pruner (which includes LoRA fine-tuning) at 50% pruning on zero-shot average (52.23 vs 48.35), highlighting the strength of the OBS-based parameter compensation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Optimal Brain Surgeon (OBS) framework—originally designed for single-weight removal in small networks (Hassibi et al., 1993) and later adapted for row-wise pruning (OBC, Frantar & Alistarh, 2022) and quantization via Cholesky reformulation (GPTQ, Frantar et al., 2022)—to the structurally constrained setting of LLM head and FFN channel pruning. The key departure is that structured pruning introduces block-level dependencies (an attention head must be removed as a unit) that break the column-wise comparability assumed by GPTQ/SparseGPT, necessitating the grouped Cholesky decomposition and Dynamic Group Size mechanisms. The paper explicitly notes that no prior work had applied OBS to structured LLM pruning, positioning SlimGPT as filling this gap while contrasting with first-order gradient-based methods (LLM-Pruner, LoRAPrune) and regularization-based approaches (Compresso).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SlimGPT offers a practically attractive compression recipe—single A100, ~1 hour, 256 calibration sequences—that achieves consistent SOTA over gradient-based baselines across LLaMA-7B/13B/30B, Vicuna, LLaMA2, and Baichuan, making it a strong default for practitioners who need structured compression without full retraining. The error-accumulation analysis (super-linear growth of per-layer output error with depth under uniform pruning) provides a principled justification for non-uniform layer-wise ratios, though the authors acknowledge the logarithmic schedule is a heuristic rather than an optimal solution. Open questions include whether the approach can be extended to higher pruning ratios (>50%) without severe degradation on complex tasks like MMLU and LongBench, and whether the calibration-data sensitivity (pre-training vs. instruction data) can be resolved for instruction-tuned models.
