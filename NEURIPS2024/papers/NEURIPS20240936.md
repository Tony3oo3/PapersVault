## Problem

Compositing a foreground subject into a novel background video traditionally requires expensive virtual production pipelines (3D scene creation, LED-wall studios). Existing video editing methods perform holistic changes that cannot retain the subject while replacing the background, and video inpainting/outpainting methods focus on small-region pixel harmonization rather than synthesizing large, dynamic backgrounds with realistic subject-scene interaction. No prior method can take a foreground motion sequence and a single condition image to generate a coherent video where the subject physically interacts with the new scene.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240936/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes three inputs: a 16-frame foreground segmentation sequence (256×256×3, with background pixels set to grey 127), a corresponding binary mask sequence (256×256×1, foreground=0, background=1), and a single 256×256×3 condition frame that may be either a background-only image or a composite frame containing both subject and background. The output is a 16-frame 256×256 video in which the subject dynamically interacts with the synthesized background. The segmentation and mask are concatenated with noisy latents to form a 9-channel U-Net input, while the condition frame is consumed only through CLIP image features injected via cross-attention, not through pixel-level conditioning.

## Method

ActAnywhere builds a 3D denoising U-Net on top of AnimateDiff's temporal attention modules, initialized from Stable Diffusion's inpainting checkpoint and AnimateDiff v2 motion modules. The foreground segmentation is encoded by the VAE encoder and the mask is downsampled 8×, then both are channel-concatenated with the noisy latent to form a 9-channel input, explicitly telling the network which region to preserve and which to generate. The condition frame is encoded by a CLIP ViT-L/14 image encoder (1024-dim features projected to 768-dim via a two-layer MLP) and injected through cross-attention, which the authors show empirically yields better temporal consistency than VAE-feature alternatives. Training is self-supervised: the model predicts the original video from its own foreground segmentation, conditioned on a randomly sampled frame, enabling it to retain the subject, hallucinate missing details from imperfect masks, and adhere to the condition while being robust to the subject's pose within it. The model is trained on 2.4M human-scene interaction videos (HiC+) with random rectangular cut-out augmentation on segmentations, 5×5 erosion to reduce boundary leakage, and 10% random condition dropping for classifier-free guidance.

## Results

On 30 DAVIS videos, ActAnywhere achieves the best scores across all three metrics: CLIP_cond 0.862 (condition-image consistency), CLIP_temp 0.945 (temporal consistency), and FVD 313.4 (generation quality). The largest margin is on CLIP_cond, where ActAnywhere leads Gen1 (0.827) by 0.035 and Control-A-Video (0.643) by 0.219, confirming tight adherence to the condition frame. In a user study against Gen1, ActAnywhere is preferred 97.47% of the time on condition consistency, 72.13% on visual quality, and 64.36% on temporal consistency.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ActAnywhere outperforms all three quantitative baselines on every metric. Against Gen1 (the closest competitor, which also uses image conditioning), ActAnywhere gains +0.035 on CLIP_cond and −23.8 on FVD, while temporal consistency is nearly matched (0.945 vs. 0.943). TokenFlow trails by a wider margin on CLIP_cond (0.762 vs. 0.862) and FVD (323.0 vs. 313.4), reflecting its text-only conditioning that cannot enforce strict scene guidance. Control-A-Video is the weakest baseline, with CLIP_cond of only 0.643 and FVD of 381.8, as its Canny-edge + text conditioning fails to preserve the subject or follow the target background. Three additional baselines (Text2LIVE, AnimateDiff, VideoCrafter1) are compared qualitatively in Figure 4: video-editing methods (Text2LIVE, TokenFlow, Control-A-Video) inherit the source video's spatial structure and cannot perform fine-grained scene changes, while unconstrained generation methods (AnimateDiff, VideoCrafter1) produce incoherent motion without foreground guidance.

## Relation to Prior Work

ActAnywhere builds on the latent video diffusion architecture of AnimateDiff (temporal attention modules inserted into a Stable Diffusion U-Net) and the CLIP-based conditioning paradigm from image diffusion models. It departs from video editing methods (Gen1, TokenFlow, Text2Live, Control-A-Video) that perform holistic transformations on an entire source video, and from video inpainting/outpainting methods (MAGVIT, ProPainter, M3DDM) that focus on pixel-level harmonization of small masked regions. The key conceptual departure is using a foreground segmentation sequence as explicit motion control while conditioning on a single background image via CLIP features, creating a clean separation between what to preserve (foreground) and what to generate (background) — a formulation the authors argue is absent from prior work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work addresses a practically important bottleneck in filmmaking and VFX where compositing a subject into a novel scene traditionally requires expensive virtual production pipelines (3D scene creation, LED-wall studios, or engine rendering). The zero-shot generalization to non-human subjects, gaming clips, and multi-subject videos despite training exclusively on human-scene interactions suggests the learned interaction priors are more general than expected. Key limitations include the 16-frame / 256×256 resolution constraint, sensitivity to segmentation quality (failure cases with complex textures), and the reliance on a single condition frame that limits long-range scene coherence. Open questions include scaling to longer/higher-resolution sequences, handling dynamic camera motions more explicitly, and whether the CLIP-based conditioning can be replaced by more fine-grained spatial controls.
