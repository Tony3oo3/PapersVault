## Problem

Unsupervised representation learning for time series that captures inter-individual shape variability remains an open problem, particularly for irregularly sampled multivariate time series of variable lengths. A fundamental obstacle is that applying a general diffeomorphism to a time series graph can destroy its structure (a single time value may map to multiple space values), so the class of admissible deformations must be restricted to preserve the temporal axis. Existing shape analysis tools (LDDMM, SRV, Shape-FPCA) either do not respect this graph structure, require equal-length inputs, or produce entangled space-time representations that are difficult to interpret.

## Input / Output

The method takes a population of N multivariate time series, each potentially irregularly sampled at different timestamps and of different lengths (n_j varies across individuals). It jointly learns a reference time series graph (n_0 samples, where n_0 is the median length) and, for each individual, a feature vector in R^{d·n_0} given by the momentum parameters (α_j) of the initial velocity field that encodes the diffeomorphism from the reference to that individual's graph. These feature vectors can then be fed into any downstream statistical or machine-learning model (PCA, LDA, kernel SVM, etc.) for classification, clustering, or longitudinal analysis.

## Method

TS-LDDMM tailors the LDDMM geodesic-shooting framework to time series by (1) proving a representation theorem (Theorem 1) showing that any two time series graphs are linked by a composition of a temporal reparametrization Ψ_γ and a spatial deformation Φ_f, and that this composition always yields a valid time series graph; (2) designing a block-diagonal RKHS kernel K_G whose time component acts only on the temporal coordinate and whose space component couples time and space, guaranteeing (via Lemma 1) that learned diffeomorphisms preserve the graph structure; and (3) using an oriented varifold distance as the data-fidelity loss, which handles sets of different sizes and irregular sampling by comparing segment-level positional and tangential information without requiring point-to-point correspondence. The joint optimization over the reference graph and all momentum parameters is performed by gradient descent (Adabelief) with a warmup-then-cosine-decay schedule, and the resulting sparse, kernel-parameterized velocity fields yield interpretable deformations that can be visualized by shooting along principal component directions.

## Results

On 15 shape-based UCR/UEA datasets, TS-LDDMM achieves the best average macro F1-score of 0.83±0.18 (rank 2.93) under regular sampling, outperforming the second-best method LDDMM (0.72±0.20) by approximately 15%. This advantage persists under irregular sampling: at 30% missing rate TS-LDDMM scores 0.80±0.18 (rank 2.07) vs. LDDMM's 0.70±0.21, and at 50% missing rate 0.70±0.26 vs. 0.57±0.25. In the shape-analysis-only comparison on regularly sampled data, TS-LDDMM outperforms all baselines (TCLR, LDDMM, Shape-FPCA) on 12 out of 15 datasets. At 70% missing rate, Neural LNSDE (0.66±0.23) surpasses TS-LDDMM (0.51±0.27), indicating the varifold loss becomes less effective under extreme data sparsity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TS-LDDMM consistently outperforms LDDMM (Glaunes 2008), its direct predecessor for general curves, by approximately 10–15% F1-score at 0% and 30% missing rates, demonstrating that the time-series-specific kernel structure (separating time and space transformations) is critical. Against the strongest neural ODE baseline, Neural LNSDE (Oh 2024), TS-LDDMM leads at 0% (0.83 vs. 0.70) and 30% (0.80 vs. 0.68) missing rates but falls behind at 70% (0.51 vs. 0.66), where the varifold loss degrades under extreme sparsity. Among shape-analysis methods, TS-LDDMM beats TCLR (Heo 2024, SRV-based) and Shape-FPCA (Wu 2024) on 12 of 15 regularly sampled datasets; Shape-FPCA is consistently the weakest performer (e.g., 0.08 on FacesUCR vs. 0.86 for TS-LDDMM), while TCLR is competitive on univariate data but does not extend to the multivariate case. Recurrent and attention-based baselines (RNN, LSTM, GRU, MTAN, MIAM) are uniformly outperformed by TS-LDDMM across all missing-rate regimes, with GRU being the strongest among them (0.71 at 0% vs. 0.83 for TS-LDDMM).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LDDMM framework (Beg et al. 2005; Miller et al. 2006) from general shape analysis to time series by proving a representation theorem (Theorem 1) showing that any two time series graphs can be linked by a composition of a time reparametrization and a space transformation, and by designing a structured RKHS kernel (Eq. 9) that enforces this decomposition. It departs from standard LDDMM, which uses general diffeomorphisms that can destroy the graph structure of time series (Figure 1), and from FDA approaches like Shape-FPCA that separate space and time representations, making post-hoc interpretation difficult. The discrete parameterization follows Durrleman et al. (2013), and the loss function uses oriented varifolds (Kaltenmark et al. 2017) to handle variable-length, irregularly sampled data without requiring point-to-point correspondence.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TS-LDDMM provides an interpretable, unsupervised representation for studying inter-individual shape variability in biomedical time series, demonstrated compellingly on mice respiratory data where principal components carry clear physiological meaning (cycle duration, inspiration/expiration trade-off, post-inspiration pauses). Key limitations include sensitivity to noise, performance degradation at high missing rates (70%), the need to tune five kernel hyperparameters plus varifold parameters, and computational cost from kernel operations. Open questions include extending to heterogeneous datasets via hierarchical clustering, developing adaptive hyperparameter selection, and scaling to very large high-dimensional time series such as video.
