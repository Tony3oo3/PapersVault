## Problem

Existing model merging methods either apply uniform scaling coefficients across all parameters and tasks, or address only one dimension of parameter competition (within-task or between-task) but not both. This limits their ability to resolve conflicts and exploit correlations among parameters when integrating multiple fine-tuned models into a single multitask model.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242690/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method requires multiple fine-tuned model checkpoints sharing the same pretrained initialization (identical architecture), along with the original pretrained weights. It operates on task vectors (element-wise differences between fine-tuned and pretrained parameters) and produces a single merged model checkpoint. The process is entirely training-free and dataless, requiring no access to original training data or validation sets (though optional validation can tune hyperparameters λ and mask ratio r). It supports both full fine-tuning and parameter-efficient fine-tuning (e.g., IA³, LoRA) configurations.

## Method

PCB-MERGING constructs a per-task, per-parameter balancing matrix β by combining two components: intra-balancing, which applies softmax to the normalized squared task vector magnitudes (scaled by the number of merged tasks N) to emphasize important parameters within each task; and inter-balancing, which computes the sum of softmax-normalized element-wise products between each task vector and all others to capture cross-task parameter similarity. A drop step then masks parameters whose combined score falls below the (1−r)-quantile threshold, and the surviving parameters are rescaled by their scores. The final merged model is θ_pre + λ·τ_m, where τ_m is the score-weighted sum of modulated task vectors. Optionally, CMA-ES evolutionary search can optimize per-task coefficients λ_i to further improve validation performance.

## Results

PCB-MERGING achieves average improvements of 4.3% (T5-Base) and 3.5% (T5-Large) over the strongest baseline (TIES-Merging) on 7 NLP tasks without validation, and 3.5% on 8 vision tasks (ViT-B/32). With validation, gains are 1.8% on both T5 sizes, 2.7% on ViT-B/32, and 0.9% on LLaMa2-7B (3 LLM tasks). Adding evolutionary strategy (CMA-ES) for coefficient search further boosts performance to +3.1% (T5-Base) and +3.4% (ViT-B/32) over the best baseline. Out-of-domain generalization improves by 1.9–2.1% on 6 held-out NLP tasks, and cross-domain emotion classification gains 1.1–1.3% in-domain and 0.7–0.8% out-of-domain.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PCB-MERGING consistently outperforms all six baselines across every modality and model size tested. Against TIES-Merging (the strongest task-vector baseline), PCB-MERGING gains +4.3% on T5-Base and +3.5% on T5-Large without validation, and +1.8% on both with validation; on vision tasks the gap is +3.5% (ViT-B/32) and +0.9% (ViT-L/14) without validation. Against Task Arithmetic, improvements are larger (e.g., +20.3% on T5-Base without validation) since Task Arithmetic applies a uniform coefficient and ignores parameter-level competition. Fisher Merging and RegMean, which require gradient-based matrix computations, are outperformed by PCB-MERGING by 1.8–7.1% on NLP tasks and 2.7–4.5% on vision tasks, while PCB-MERGING is training-free and lighter to implement. On LLaMa2-7B LLM merging, PCB-MERGING achieves 35.1% average (no DARE) vs. 34.3% for TIES-Merging, with the largest single-task gain of +3.7% on HumanEval code generation. AdaMerging (compared only on vision classification) is surpassed by PCB-MERGING by 2.2% (ViT-B/32) and 1.4% (ViT-L/14) when both use task-wise coefficients, and PCB-MERGING further enhances AdaMerging's layer-wise results by 0.6–1.4%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the task-vector-based model merging lineage (Task Arithmetic, TIES-Merging, DARE) by introducing parameter-level coefficient modulation through a competition-balancing mechanism, departing from the uniform per-task scaling used by prior methods. It draws conceptual inspiration from DARE's drop-and-rescale strategy but replaces random Bernoulli dropping with competition-aware scoring that considers both intra-task parameter importance (self-awareness) and inter-task parameter similarity (cross-awareness). Unlike Fisher Merging and RegMean, which require gradient-based matrix computations, PCB-MERGING operates purely on task vectors, maintaining the lightweight, training-free character of the task-vector family while adding the granularity that TIES-Merging and DARE lacked.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PCB-MERGING provides a practical, training-free merging technique that consistently outperforms existing methods across NLP, vision, and LLM settings without requiring original training data or validation sets, making it broadly applicable to practitioners who hold multiple fine-tuned checkpoints. The parameter competition framing (self-awareness vs. cross-awareness) offers a useful diagnostic lens for evaluating and designing future merging methods. Key limitations include the requirement for identical architectures and shared initialization, the lack of theoretical grounding for why task vector magnitudes proxy parameter importance, and the reliance on heuristic drop operations rather than principled redundancy removal. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to heterogeneous model families and whether the competition-balancing objective can be derived from first principles.
