## Problem

Reward hacking (overoptimization) in RLHF stems from reward misgeneralization, where proxy reward models latch onto spurious features (e.g., length bias) that correlate with training labels but are irrelevant to true human preferences. Existing mitigations—KL penalties, RM ensembles, scaling—address symptoms rather than this root cause, leaving RMs vulnerable to distribution shift during the RL stage.

## Input / Output

The model consumes preference pairs (chosen and rejected text responses) from human-annotated datasets. The LLM backbone (e.g., Vicuna-7B or Pythia) is modified at the final layer to output a K-dimensional IB latent vector (mean and log-variance of a diagonal Gaussian) rather than a single scalar; an MLP decoder then maps this latent to a scalar reward. Beyond the reward score, the IB latent representation itself is retained for overoptimization detection via the Cluster Separation Index (CSI), which quantifies how far RLHF-generated sample clusters drift from the SFT distribution in that latent space.

## Method

InfoRM reformulates reward modeling as a variational information bottleneck objective: maximize I(S;Y) − β·I(X;S|Y), where S is the IB latent representation, X the input, and Y the preference ranking. Concretely, the LLM encoder produces a K-dimensional Gaussian posterior (diagonal covariance) over S, and a reparameterization trick yields a deterministic latent h_φ(x,ε) fed to an MLP decoder for Bradley-Terry preference prediction; the training loss combines the standard preference log-likelihood with a KL penalty pulling the IB posterior toward a standard normal prior, applied to both chosen and rejected samples. The key architectural change is replacing the standard RM's final linear scalar head with a two-output head (mean + log-variance) plus a separate MLP decoder. A secondary contribution is the discovery that overoptimized samples manifest as outliers in this compact IB latent space, enabling the CSI metric (DBSCAN clustering + weighted centroid-to-nearest-SFT distances) as a real-time overoptimization detector that guides hyperparameter selection and early stopping without requiring a gold RM.

## Results

Under GPT-4 evaluation, InfoRM achieves win rates of 57.0% (Anthropic-Helpful), 57.1% (Anthropic-Harmless), 48.9% (AlpacaFarm), and 73.1% (TL;DR Summary) against the SFT model. Against the Standard RM baseline, InfoRM wins 54.5% of comparisons on Anthropic-Helpful and 70.4% on TL;DR Summary. On out-of-distribution RM benchmarks, InfoRM significantly outperforms Standard RM on AlpacaEval (66.63% vs. 65.38%) and TruthfulQA (46.87% vs. 40.63%), while matching in-distribution accuracy (73.72% vs. 73.62% on Anthropic-Helpful).

## Baselines & Comparisons

InfoRM consistently outperforms all four baselines across all four evaluation datasets. Against Standard RM (the most direct comparison), InfoRM wins 54.5% vs. 12.0% on Anthropic-Helpful and 70.4% vs. 11.6% on TL;DR Summary, demonstrating that the IB bottleneck effectively prevents the reward misgeneralization that causes overoptimization. Against Standard RM w/ KL, InfoRM still wins on all datasets (e.g., 49.0% vs. 19.5% on Anthropic-Helpful), suggesting that KL penalties, while stabilizing RL, restrict the optimization landscape more than the IB approach. Against Ensemble RM, InfoRM achieves higher win rates on all four datasets (e.g., 43.1% vs. 23.8% on Anthropic-Helpful), and combining InfoRM with Ensemble RM further improves results (48.7% win vs. 15.6% lose). Against WARM, InfoRM wins on Anthropic-Helpful (41.1% vs. 25.5%) and TL;DR (63.1% vs. 18.3%), and the InfoRM+WARM combination achieves a striking 67.9% win rate on Anthropic-Harmless, indicating the methods are complementary.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on two research threads: (1) reward overoptimization mitigation in RLHF, where prior work has focused on KL divergence penalties, RM scaling, ensembling, and length-bias correction, and (2) variational information bottleneck methods originating from Alemi et al. (2016). InfoRM departs from the first thread by targeting the root cause—reward misgeneralization via spurious features—rather than its symptoms, and from the second by being the first to apply the IB principle to the Bradley-Terry ranking formulation in reward modeling. A further departure is the repurposing of the IB latent space geometry (outlier structure) as a detection signal for overoptimization, which prior IB work did not explore.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

InfoRM offers a principled, single-model solution to reward hacking that outperforms ensemble-based approaches without additional computational cost, and the CSI indicator provides a practical, gold-RM-free tool for online mitigation (e.g., early stopping) during RLHF training. Key limitations include testing only up to 7B parameters, the CSI's reliance on inference over a held-out set (introducing latency), and sensitivity of GPT-4-based evaluation to prompt structure. Open questions include whether the IB formulation and CSI detection scale to frontier models (100B+), whether real-time lightweight overoptimization metrics can replace the current batch-based CSI, and how the approach interacts with newer alignment paradigms like DPO that bypass explicit reward modeling.
