## Problem

Conformal prediction methods that achieve conditional validity (e.g., group-conditional or covariate-shift coverage) often produce unnecessarily large prediction sets, while length-efficient methods typically sacrifice conditional guarantees. No principled framework existed that simultaneously optimizes prediction set length and enforces conditional validity across a class of covariate shifts.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243157/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a fixed conformity score S(x,y), n i.i.d. calibration samples, a finite-dimensional affine class of covariate shifts F = {⟨β, Φ(x)⟩ | β ∈ ℝ^d}, and a hypothesis class H (e.g., a neural network). It outputs prediction sets C(x) = {y | S(x,y) ≤ h*(x)} where h*(x) is a covariate-adaptive threshold learned from data, rather than a single global quantile as in split conformal. The threshold function h is parameterized by a model from H (e.g., a linear head on GPT-2 or ResNet-50 features), making the prediction set size vary with the input x.

## Method

The core contribution is a minimax formulation: minimize over f ∈ F (ensuring conditional validity) and maximize over h ∈ H (optimizing length) of a Lagrangian-like objective g_α(f,h). In the infinite-sample regime, strong duality proves the optimal prediction sets are level sets of f*(x)·p(y|x), and the outer minimization selects the f* that enforces exact conditional coverage. For the finite-sample algorithm (CPL), the objective is smoothed via Gaussian error functions to enable gradient-based optimization, and the minimax is solved by alternating SGD steps on h (maximizing) and β (minimizing). A PAC-style guarantee of O(1/√n) conditional coverage error is established using covering numbers of H and Lipschitz assumptions on the conditional score distribution.

## Results

Across three experimental settings, CPL consistently produces significantly shorter prediction sets while maintaining valid conditional coverage. In the LLM multiple-choice QA setting (Part I), CPL achieves substantially smaller mean prediction set sizes than the split-conformal baseline while ensuring proper 90% marginal coverage on TruthfulQA, MMLU, OpenBookQA, PIQA, and BigBench. In the synthetic regression setting with 20 overlapping groups (Part II, 150K train / 50K calib / 50K test, 100 trials), CPL's average interval length approaches the numerically computed Optimal Oracle, whereas BatchGCP performs similarly to Split Conformal. In the RxRx1 image classification setting (Part III, 1,339 classes, 51 experiments, 20 calibration splits), CPL achieves significantly smaller average prediction set sizes than both Conditional Calibration and Split Conformal while retaining valid coverage under covariate shift.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the LLM split-conformal baseline of Kumar et al. [33], CPL wins decisively on mean prediction set size across all five QA datasets (TruthfulQA, MMLU, OpenBookQA, PIQA, BigBench) while maintaining the target 90% marginal coverage (Figure 3). Against BatchGCP [4] on the synthetic group-conditional regression task, CPL wins on average interval length, approaching the Optimal Oracle solution, while BatchGCP's intervals remain comparable in size to Split Conformal (Figure 4). Against Split Conformal [22], CPL wins on both axes in the group-conditional setting: Split Conformal under-covers in high-variance groups and over-covers in low-variance groups, whereas CPL achieves near-perfect per-group coverage with much shorter intervals (Figure 4); in the RxRx1 setting, Split Conformal also produces larger prediction sets (Figure 5). Against Conditional Calibration [8] on RxRx1, both methods maintain valid coverage under covariate shift, but CPL achieves significantly smaller average prediction set sizes by learning an adaptive threshold via a linear head on ResNet-50 features rather than relying on quantile regression (Figure 5).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the conditional coverage framework of Gibbs et al. [8] (finite-dimensional affine class of covariate shifts) and the minimax level-set estimation theory of Willett & Nowak [46], unifying them into a single optimization problem for prediction set construction. It departs from conformal training approaches [65, 34] that optimize the conformity score itself; instead, CPL keeps the score fixed and learns an adaptive covariate-dependent threshold h(x), positioning itself at the prediction-set-construction stage of the CP pipeline. The structured prediction sets C_h^S = {y | S(x,y) ≤ h(x)} directly generalize split conformal's fixed-threshold sets [22], and the minimax formulation provides a principled derivation of why adaptive thresholds should outperform uniform ones.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first principled framework that jointly addresses conditional validity and length efficiency in conformal prediction, filling a gap the authors identify as missing from the CP literature. The strong duality results (infinite sample) and O(1/√n) finite-sample guarantees provide theoretical grounding, while the practical algorithm is simple (alternating SGD on a smoothed objective) and composable with any existing conformity score. Open questions include: the fundamental gap between structured (score-thresholded) and unstructured prediction sets when the score is misaligned with 1/p(y|x), the realizability assumption needed for bounded-complexity H, and whether the approach extends to sequential or online settings. For practitioners, the key takeaway is that even under marginal coverage, adaptive thresholds can yield substantially smaller sets than split conformal.
