## Problem

While spectral regularization algorithms (ridge regression, gradient descent, principal component regression) are well-understood for scalar-valued regression, their behavior in high- and infinite-dimensional output spaces remains largely uncharacterized. Specifically, the saturation effect of kernel ridge regression—where the algorithm fails to exploit smoothness beyond a threshold—had not been rigorously confirmed via lower bounds in the vector-valued setting, and no general upper learning rates existed for arbitrary spectral filters with vector-valued output, including the misspecified case where the true regression function lies outside the hypothesis space.

## Input / Output

The method takes i.i.d. samples {(x_i, y_i)} where y_i belongs to a potentially infinite-dimensional separable Hilbert space Y, a scalar-valued positive-definite kernel k, a spectral filter function g_λ (e.g., Tikhonov, Landweber, or truncation), and a regularization parameter λ. The output is a vector-valued function estimate F_λ: X → Y computed in closed form via an extended representer theorem (Proposition 1) that works even when Y is infinite-dimensional, yielding F_λ(x) = Σ y_i α_i(x) with coefficients α(x) = (1/n) g_λ(K/n) k_x. The theoretical output consists of high-probability upper and lower bounds on the L_2(π;Y) risk expressed as functions of sample size n, smoothness β, eigenvalue decay p, and filter qualification ρ.

## Method

The paper makes two complementary theoretical contributions. First, it proves a lower bound (Theorem 3) showing that vector-valued kernel ridge regression saturates at rate n^{-2/(2+p)} for smoothness β ≥ 2, using a bias-variance decomposition where the variance is lower-bounded via a covering number argument over the Hölder-continuous kernel class and the bias via a concentration inequality on the empirical covariance operator. Second, it derives upper learning rates (Theorem 4) for general spectral filters with qualification ρ by working in vector-valued interpolation spaces [G]^β that characterize smoothness without requiring the well-specified assumption F_* ∈ G. The key technical mechanism decomposes the estimation error into a noise term controlled via Bernstein's inequality on Hilbert-Schmidt operator-valued random variables and an approximation term bounded using the filter's qualification property, with the regularization parameter λ chosen to optimally trade off the two. Algorithms with infinite qualification (gradient descent, principal component regression) achieve the full smoothness-dependent rate n^{-(β-γ)/(β+p)} without saturation, while Tikhonov regularization (ρ=1) recovers the known saturation at β=2.

## Results

The paper establishes that vector-valued kernel ridge regression (Tikhonov, qualification ρ=1) saturates at rate $n^{-2/(2+p)}$ for smoothness β≥2 (Theorem 3), confirming a gap with the information-theoretic lower bound $n^{-\beta/(\beta+p)}$ (Theorem 2). For general spectral filters with qualification ρ, the upper rate is $n^{-(\beta-\gamma)/(\beta+p)}$ in the regime β+p>α (Theorem 4), which is minimax optimal when β∈(α−p, 2ρ]. Algorithms with infinite qualification (gradient descent, principal component regression) bypass saturation entirely and achieve optimal rates for all β>0.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not run empirical comparisons but provides a theoretical comparison of learning rates across spectral algorithms. Kernel ridge regression (Tikhonov, ρ=1) is shown to saturate: its upper rate plateaus at $n^{-2/(2+p)}$ for β>2, and the new lower bound in Theorem 3 rigorously confirms this saturation is unavoidable, closing a gap left by prior lower bounds [6, 5, 31] that did not reflect the saturation effect. In contrast, gradient descent and principal component regression (both with infinite qualification ρ=∞) achieve the optimal rate $n^{-\beta/(\beta+p)}$ for all smoothness levels β, matching the known real-valued behavior [3, 5, 30]. The upper rates for Tikhonov regularization recover the state-of-the-art results of [31] (Li et al., JMLR 2024), confirming consistency. The paper also shows that in the misspecified setting (F*∉G), the spectral algorithm rates match those of [31], while extending the analysis to general filters via the qualification framework of [5, 34].

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the spectral regularization framework from real-valued to vector-valued (Hilbert-space-valued) output, building directly on the vector-valued interpolation space machinery of Li et al. [30, 31] and the qualification-based filter function framework of Lin et al. [34] and Blanchard & Mücke [5]. It generalizes the saturation lower bound of Li et al. [29] from scalar to Hilbert-space-valued regression, and extends the Tikhonov-specific rates of Li et al. [31] to the full class of spectral filters (gradient descent, principal component regression, etc.), using the extended representer theorem of Baldassarre et al. [2] to ensure computability even in infinite dimensions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper closes a notable gap in the theoretical literature: prior to this work, no general upper or lower rates existed for spectral algorithms with infinite-dimensional output, despite the fact that most practical applications (conditional mean embeddings, Koopman operator learning, causal inference) operate in exactly this regime. The confirmation that saturation is unavoidable for vector-valued KRR but bypassable by gradient descent or principal component regression provides concrete algorithmic guidance for practitioners in these infinite-dimensional settings. Open questions include obtaining minimax-optimal rates in the low-smoothness regime (β < α − p), which remains unresolved even for real-valued outputs, and extending the analysis to more general operator-valued kernels beyond the scalar-times-identity form.
