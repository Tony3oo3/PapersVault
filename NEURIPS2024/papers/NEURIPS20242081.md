## Problem

Large language models exhibit pervasive cultural bias because their training corpora are dominated by English-language data reflecting Western values, leaving other cultures underrepresented. Existing remedies—prompt engineering and multilingual pre-training—are either unreliable across tasks or prohibitively expensive, and prior cultural data collection relies on costly extraction from existing datasets or human-annotated platforms that are difficult to scale. The paper asks whether a cost-efficient, LLM-driven pipeline can generate diverse, high-quality cross-cultural data sufficient to fine-tune culture-specific models that outperform general-purpose LLMs like GPT-4.

## Input / Output

The pipeline takes 4.1k seed questions from the World Values Survey and Pew Global Attitudes surveys as initial prompts, augmented with self-calibration instructions that anchor each agent's responses to a known cultural attitude. The multi-agent dialogue produces 41k input-output pairs (one per dialogue) spanning human beliefs (59.68%), norms (29.54%), and customs (10.78%) across 8 cultures. After refinement—opinion extraction, entailment-based verification against the seed, and K-means deduplication on text-embedding-3-small vectors—the cleaned samples are used to fine-tune GPT-3.5-turbo (via OpenAI's fine-tuning API) or Llama2-70b, yielding culture-specific models that output moderation labels, Hofstede dimension scores, or open-ended educational dialogue.

## Method

CulturePark is a multi-agent communication framework grounded in Cognitive Conflict Theory and Social Cognitive Theory. A main-contact agent (Lily, English-speaking) converses with a cultural delegate (e.g., Abdul for Arabic) over multiple turns, with the cultural difference deliberately triggering cognitive conflict that prompts deeper, more novel reasoning. Two prompting mechanisms maintain quality: self-calibration prompts that constrain each agent to conform to its target culture's known stance on the seed question, and alternating self-guidance prompts (e.g., 'Do you agree? Provide more reasons') with free-chat turns that let the LLM's internal creativity drive topic expansion. The raw dialogues pass through a three-stage refinement pipeline—GPT-4-based opinion extraction, NLI-style entailment checking against the seed datum, and K-means clustering to remove semantically redundant samples—before fine-tuning. The key insight is that structured cross-cultural multi-agent communication naturally produces both novel questions and more comprehensive answers than direct single-model generation, yielding data with significantly higher diversity than prior semantic-augmentation approaches like CultureLLM.

## Results

On content moderation across 41 datasets (48,895 samples) spanning 8 cultures, the GPT-3.5-turbo-based CulturePark models outperform GPT-4 on 5 cultures (Arabic, Bengali, Chinese, Korean, Turkish) and approach GPT-4 on the remaining 3 (German, Portuguese, Spanish). On cultural alignment via Hofstede's VSM 13, CulturePark models achieve substantially lower Euclidean distance than both GPT-3.5-turbo and GPT-4 across all 8 cultures. In the situated learning study, participants using CulturePark models achieve an average distance of 175.75 vs. 215.59 for GPT-4, with higher user satisfaction (4.33/5 vs. 3/5).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CulturePark's GPT-3.5-turbo fine-tuned models outperform GPT-4 on 5 of 8 cultures in content moderation (Arabic, Bengali, Chinese, Korean, Turkish) and approach it on German, Portuguese, and Spanish, despite being fine-tuned on data generated by the weaker GPT-3.5-turbo. Against culture-specific LLMs, CulturePark achieves 0.692 avg F1 on Chinese vs. SeaLLM (0.297) and TaiwanLLM (0.394), and 0.643 on Korean vs. Synatra-7B (0.428), EEVE-Korean (0.560), and CultureBank (0.579). On Arabic, CulturePark (0.602) edges out CultureBank (0.591). On cultural alignment (Hofstede's VSM 13), CulturePark models outperform both GPT-3.5-turbo and GPT-4 by a large margin across all 8 cultures. In the situated learning experiment, CulturePark models yield lower Euclidean distance (175.75 vs. 215.59) and higher user satisfaction (4.33 vs. 3.0) than GPT-4, with participants noting GPT-4 responses were vague while CulturePark models provided straightforward cultural opinions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from two main lines of work on cultural bias in LLMs: (1) approaches that extract cultural data from existing sources (Wikipedia, social media, surveys like WVS/PCT) or perform semantic augmentation (CultureLLM), and (2) costly pre-training in non-English languages (SeaLLM, TaiwanLLM, MaLA-500). CulturePark instead repurposes multi-agent LLM communication—grounded in Cognitive Conflict Theory and Social Cognitive Theory—as a data generation mechanism, where agents from different cultural backgrounds create cognitive conflicts that trigger deeper, more diverse cultural reasoning. The key conceptual departure from CultureLLM is that multi-agent dialogue produces genuinely novel questions and comprehensive answers rather than semantically equivalent paraphrases of seed examples.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CulturePark offers a scalable, cost-effective pipeline for generating culturally specific training data without requiring large-scale pre-training or manual annotation, which is significant for democratizing culturally aware AI across many cultures. However, the framework is currently limited to 8 (mostly high-resource) cultures, relies on GPT-3.5/4 for data generation (introducing potential model-specific biases), and the fine-tuning is done via OpenAI's API rather than open-source models at scale. Open questions include whether the multi-agent communication approach generalizes to low-resource cultures, whether the generated data faithfully represents intra-cultural diversity, and whether the approach can be extended beyond the Hofstede framework to capture more nuanced cultural dimensions.
