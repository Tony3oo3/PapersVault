## Problem

Adversarial perturbations disproportionately damage phase patterns in the frequency domain—where crucial semantic information resides—while leaving amplitude patterns relatively intact, making it difficult for models to correctly classify adversarial examples. While adversarial training (AT) improves robustness, it does not explicitly exploit the opportunity to steer the model toward phase patterns that remain unaffected by attacks. A key challenge is selecting appropriate distractor amplitudes for mixing: too dissimilar and phase semantics are disrupted, too similar and the model cannot be forced to attend to phase features.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 64×64 for Tiny ImageNet) and outputs class labels via a softmax classifier. During training, each image is decomposed via DFT into amplitude and phase spectra; the amplitude is linearly mixed with a generated adversarial amplitude, and the recombined image is reconstructed via IDFT to serve as an additional training sample. The model employs split batch normalization (separate BN parameters for original vs. recombined samples) to handle the large distributional gap between the two, and both the original and recombined images (along with their respective adversarial examples) are fed through the network in each training step.

## Method

DAT introduces an Adversarial Amplitude Generator (AAG), a four-layer linear network conditioned on sample logits and Gaussian noise, that synthesizes an adversarial amplitude spectrum to maximize model loss while preserving phase semantics. The generated amplitude is linearly mixed with the original (λ·A_G + (1−λ)·A) to retain some original amplitude information, and the recombined image is reconstructed via IDFT. An efficient AE generation loss augments cross-entropy with KL divergence between benign and adversarial predictions, enabling AEs to reach the ℓ∞-ball boundary in only 5 PGD steps instead of 10. The joint training objective L_DAT combines adversarial training loss (CE + KL) on both original and recombined samples with a Jensen-Shannon divergence consistency term that enforces prediction agreement between the two, further pushing the model to rely on phase features. A theoretical analysis proves that minimizing empirical risk under amplitude perturbation regularizes amplitude-feature weights toward zero, formally establishing that the model must shift reliance to phase-derived features for convergence.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with ResNet-18, DAT achieves 57.55% PGD-20 robust accuracy and 51.36% AutoAttack (AA) accuracy, outperforming the best baseline (SCARL at 54.24% PGD-20) by ~3.3% and ~0.9% respectively. On CIFAR-100, DAT reaches 33.37% PGD-20 and 27.11% AA, improving over the best baseline by ~2.5% and ~1.3%. On Tiny ImageNet, DAT attains 25.47% PGD-20 and 17.51% AA, surpassing the best baseline (SCARL) by ~2.8% and ~1.2%. The paper reports average robustness improvements of ~2.1%, ~2.2%, and ~2.3% on CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and Tiny ImageNet respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DAT consistently outperforms all common baselines (PGD-AT, TRADES, MART, ST, SCARL, LAS-AT) across all three datasets and all attack types, with the largest margins on PGD-20 (~2.5–3.3%) and smaller but consistent gains on C&W∞ (~0.7–1.6%) and AA (~0.9–1.2%). When combined with AWP, DAT+AWP surpasses TRADES+AWP and SCARL+AWP by ~1.3–2.9% on PGD-20 across datasets. Against the strongest complex-strategy baselines (DAJAT, OA-AT, IDBH) in Table 3, DAT+AWP+SWA achieves the best PGD-20 accuracy on all four dataset-architecture combinations (e.g., 58.84% vs. IDBH's 57.48% on CIFAR-10/ResNet-18, and 38.59% vs. DAJAT's 37.05% on CIFAR-100/WRN-34-10), while matching or slightly exceeding them on AA. On the larger WRN-34-10 architecture (Table 2), DAT maintains its lead over all baselines, achieving 61.32% PGD-100 on CIFAR-10 (vs. 58.72% for MART, the closest competitor) and 36.75% PGD-100 on CIFAR-100 (vs. 34.19% for SCARL). DAT also improves natural accuracy over most baselines (e.g., 84.17% vs. 83.10% for ST on CIFAR-10), indicating that the frequency-domain amplitude mixing does not sacrifice clean accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the frequency-domain adversarial training line initiated by Chen et al. (ICCV 2021) and Zhou et al. (ICML 2023), which showed that adversarial perturbations primarily damage phase patterns while leaving amplitude relatively intact. Rather than using randomly selected distractor images for amplitude mixing (as in prior amplitude-phase recombination work), DAT introduces a learned Adversarial Amplitude Generator (AAG) that is adversarially optimized to maximize model loss while preserving phase semantics, resolving the tradeoff between disrupting phase patterns and being too similar to the original. The efficient AE generation module adapts the KL-divergence-based step enlargement from Sriramanan et al. (NeurIPS 2020/2021), and the overall training framework builds on the PGD-AT/TRADES min-max structure with an added Jensen-Shannon consistency regularization between benign and recombined samples.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DAT provides a principled, generative alternative to hand-crafted amplitude mixing, and the theoretical analysis (Theorem 3.2) cleanly formalizes why amplitude augmentation regularizes amplitude feature weights toward zero, forcing the model to rely on phase patterns. The consistent ~2% robustness improvement across CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and Tiny ImageNet against diverse attacks (PGD, C&W, AutoAttack) is meaningful, though within the typical range of AT improvements. Limitations include evaluation only on small-scale datasets (32×32 and 64×64 images), the additional computational overhead of the AAG and dual AE generation, and the non-trivial engineering requirement of split batch normalization. Open questions include whether the frequency-domain amplitude perturbation strategy scales to larger datasets like ImageNet, whether it generalizes to other modalities (audio, video), and whether the AAG can be made more efficient or replaced by a closed-form solution.
