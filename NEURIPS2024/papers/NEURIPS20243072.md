## Problem

Reinforcement learning for fixed-wing aircraft control requires massive training samples, but existing simulation platforms (JSBSim, ArduPilot, XPlane) rely on CPU-based flight dynamics computation and lack GPU acceleration, making large-scale parallel simulation infeasible. This bottleneck prevents RL algorithms from reaching the sample throughput needed for complex, high-fidelity fixed-wing control tasks under extreme conditions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243072/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The agent observes a normalized state vector s_t = normalize[x_t − x_target, x_t] built from the 13-dimensional 6-DoF state (body-frame velocities u,v,w; angular rates p,q,r; NED position; attitude quaternion) and outputs a normalized control action a_t = normalize(u_t) mapping to aileron, elevon, rudder deflections and throttle. The platform wraps these in a Gym-style interface where the reward is the negative L2 distance (or negative rotation angle for attitude tasks) to the target, with terminal rewards of ±200 for reaching or violating safety limits (AOA, altitude, speed, G-force).

## Method

The core contribution is a GPU-accelerated Flight Dynamics Model that stores all state, control, and aerodynamic parameters in tensor format so that the full 6-DoF ODE system (CLMEs, CAMEs, FPEs, KEs) is evaluated in parallel across up to 10⁶ aircraft per step. The key enabler is replacing traditional aerodynamic coefficient lookup tables—which introduce branching logic that serializes computation—with a multi-layer perceptron trained to approximate each coefficient (C_L, C_m, C_n, C_x, C_z) to R² > 0.99, converting the lookup into a differentiable tensor operation. On top of this simulation core, the platform provides PPO for Heading and Control tasks and a two-level hierarchical RL scheme for Tracking (upper planner converts target position to desired pitch/yaw/speed; lower PPO policy drives surfaces), alongside a traditional TECS+L1+PID baseline. At 10⁶ parallel rollouts, a single simulation step completes in 0.2 s on an A100, and PPO converges in roughly 100 iterations (~1 day) with 3000 parallel rollouts.

## Results

NeuralPlane achieves a single-step simulation time of 0.2 seconds at a parallel scale of 10⁶ aircraft, far exceeding JSBSim, ArduPilot, and XPlane. The MLP-based aerodynamic parameter fitting achieves R² values above 0.99 for all coefficients (Cl: 0.9949, Cm: 0.9962, Cn: 0.9926, Cx: 0.9967, Cz: 0.9991), confirming high-fidelity simulation. PPO converges in approximately 100 iterations (~1 day on A100) with a success rate above 95% across all task scenarios, and significantly outperforms the traditional TECS+L1+PID controller in maneuverability metrics (G-force, task completion time, rate of climb) under all tested conditions (Normal, HighSpeed, HighAltitude, Windy, Noisy).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against JSBSim [21], NeuralPlane demonstrates a decisive advantage in parallel simulation: when the number of parallel simulations exceeds 1000, NeuralPlane's computation time is significantly lower, and its GPU memory usage scales far more efficiently, since JSBSim relies entirely on CPU-based FDM without GPU acceleration. Against XPlane [20], NeuralPlane wins on all four platform dimensions (FDM, headless mode, RL integration, GPU acceleration); XPlane's commercial closed-source nature and lack of headless mode make it unsuitable for large-scale RL training. Against ArduPilot [22] as a control baseline, PPO trained on NeuralPlane significantly outperforms the traditional TECS+L1+PID hierarchical controller in maneuverability metrics (G-force, task completion time t, and rate of climb RoC) across all five flight conditions, while the traditional method retains a slight edge in safety margins (ASM, SSM, OSM). However, PPO maintains a >95% task success rate, indicating its safety is entirely acceptable. The paper also shows that without large-scale parallel rollouts (n=32 vs. n=3000), PPO fails to converge, underscoring that NeuralPlane's GPU-accelerated parallelism is essential for practical RL training in this domain.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by transplanting the GPU-accelerated parallel simulation paradigm from robotics (Isaac Gym) and autonomous driving (GPUDrive, Waymax) into the fixed-wing aircraft domain, where all existing simulators (JSBSim, ArduPilot, XPlane, QPlane) rely on CPU-based FDM computation. The key technical departure is replacing traditional aerodynamic lookup tables with MLP approximations to enable tensor-parallel computation on GPUs, a design choice not present in any prior fixed-wing simulator. Conceptually, the platform inherits the hierarchical control structure (TECS + L1 + PID) from ArduPilot as its traditional baseline and applies standard PPO/hierarchical RL as its learning baseline, positioning itself as an infrastructure contribution rather than a new algorithmic one.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

NeuralPlane is the first platform to simultaneously offer high-fidelity FDM, headless operation, native RL integration, and GPU acceleration for fixed-wing aircraft, removing the primary bottleneck (sample throughput) that has limited RL research in this domain. The demonstrated 0.2s/step at 10^6 parallel scale and the finding that RL fails to converge below ~256 parallel rollouts underscore that scale is a prerequisite, not an optimization, for fixed-wing RL. Open questions remain: the platform currently supports only a handful of aircraft models, lacks multi-aircraft scenarios, and provides no sim-to-real validation, leaving the critical gap between high-fidelity simulation and actual flight unaddressed.
