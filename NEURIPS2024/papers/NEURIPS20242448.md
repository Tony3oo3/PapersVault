## Problem

Standard attention mechanisms scale quadratically with sequence length in both compute and memory, limiting their applicability to long-context tasks such as genomics and long-form generation. While sub-quadratic alternatives (sparse attention, SSMs, static long convolutions) reduce complexity, they often sacrifice the data-dependent expressivity that makes attention effective for in-context learning and long-range dependency capture. The paper seeks a mixing operator that is simultaneously data-dependent, shift-equivariant, and quasi-linearly scalable.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242448/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Orchid consumes a length-L sequence of d-dimensional embeddings (text tokens, 4×4 or 16×16 image patches, or raw 16 kHz speech frames) and produces a same-length mixed embedding sequence. The convolution kernel spans the full sequence length and is computed in the frequency domain (DCT or FFT), so the input is zero-padded to 2L for linear convolution or treated circularly. For the generalized cross-attention variant, a second sequence u of length N < L is zero-padded to length L before feeding the conditioning network, enabling sequence-pair mixing without explicit attention matrices.

## Method

The core operator replaces the static convolution kernel with one generated by a shift-invariant conditioning network h_θ(x) = NN_θ(x), so that y = (h_θ(x) + h_0) * x, where h_0 is a fixed positional kernel (CKConv-style). Two conditioning designs preserve shift-invariance: (I) Phase Suppression applies a short depthwise Conv1d in the spatial domain, takes the magnitude of the DCT/FFT (eliminating the linear phase shift), then applies another short Conv1d in the frequency domain; (II) Cross-Correlation computes the circular cross-correlation of two shift-equivariant Conv1d mappings of the input, equivalently k^F*(x) ⊙ q^F(x) in the frequency domain. The Orchid block sandwiches this data-dependent convolution between two element-wise gated multiplications (f_⊗² ∘ f_* ∘ f_⊗¹), and all global convolutions are executed via FFT/DCT for O(L log L) complexity. In practice, the block replaces the self-attention layer in BERT and ViT architectures, with dense MLP matrices swapped for block-diagonal Monarch matrices, yielding models with 25–30% fewer parameters than their attention-based counterparts while matching or exceeding GLUE and ImageNet accuracy.

## Results

On the GLUE benchmark, Orchid-BERT-base (77M params) achieves an average score of 80.6, outperforming BERT-base (110M) at 79.6 with 30% fewer parameters, and Orchid-BERT-large (254M) reaches 82.7 versus BERT-large (340M) at 82.1. On ImageNet-1K, Orchid (48M) attains 80.2% Top-1 accuracy, surpassing ViT-b (87M) at 78.5% and M2-ViT-b (45M) at 79.5%. On CIFAR-10 with 4×4 patches, Orchid-s (735K) achieves 88.5% Top-1, substantially exceeding ViT (1.2M) at 78.6% and M2-ViT (741K) at 80.8%. On the associative recall in-context learning task, Orchid outperforms H3, CKConv, and Hyena across all sequence lengths and vocabulary sizes, and successfully learns at sequence lengths up to 131K tokens where Transformer models fail.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On GLUE, Orchid-BERT-base (80.6) outperforms both BERT-base (79.6) and M2-BERT-base (79.9) by +1.0 and +0.7 points respectively, while using 30% fewer parameters than BERT-base. On ImageNet-1K, Orchid (48M, 80.2% Top-1) beats ViT-b (87M, 78.5%), Hyena-ViT-b (88M, 78.5%), and M2-ViT-b (45M, 79.5%), achieving the best accuracy with fewer parameters than all attention-based baselines. On CIFAR-10 (4×4 patches), Orchid-s (735K, 88.5%) substantially outperforms ViT (1.2M, 78.6%), Hyena-ViT (1.3M, 80.6%), and M2-ViT (741K, 80.8%) by 7.7–9.9 points. On the associative recall task, Orchid surpasses H3, CKConv, and Hyena at all tested sequence lengths and vocabulary sizes, closing the gap with Transformer models especially at short sequences (128) with large vocabularies. On Speech Commands (1-second raw audio), Orchid (97.7%) is on par with S4-M2 (97.9%) and S4 (97.5%), while dramatically outperforming Performer (30.8%) and CKConv (71.7%), though it slightly trails the best SSM baseline by 0.2 points.

## Relation to Prior Work

Orchid extends the long-convolution lineage established by CKConv (Romero et al., 2021) and Hyena (Poli et al., 2023), which parameterize global convolution kernels implicitly via feedforward networks and positional embeddings. The key departure is making the kernel explicitly data-dependent—conditioned on the full input sequence through a shift-invariant conditioning network—thereby recovering the input-adaptive filtering property of attention while retaining convolution's O(L log L) FFT-based efficiency. This contrasts with prior dynamic convolutions (Wu et al., 2019; Chen et al., 2020) that use short, locally-conditioned kernels, and with input-dependent SSMs (Mamba, Griffin) whose conditioning is restricted to the current token or local neighborhood, preventing global sequence mixing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Orchid demonstrates that the quadratic cost of attention is not strictly necessary for its expressivity in sequence mixing, offering a quasi-linear alternative that outperforms BERT and ViT with 25–30% fewer parameters and handles sequences up to 131K tokens where dense attention becomes infeasible. Key open questions include adapting the architecture to causal/autoregressive settings (GPT-style), using it as a cross-attention replacement in seq2seq models, and extending to 2D/3D data. The paper also raises the broader question of whether attention's dominance reflects genuine necessity or over-parameterization, suggesting that structured, input-adaptive linear operators may suffice for many tasks.
