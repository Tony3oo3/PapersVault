## Problem

Temporal action segmentation of procedural videos has been studied almost exclusively in offline settings where the entire sequence is available at inference. Capturing and leveraging temporal context in an online setting—where only past frames are accessible—remains under-explored, and direct adaptation of online action detection models (e.g., LSTR) to segmentation fails because their fixed-token memory and frame-level training objective produce severe over-segmentation on procedural activities with long-range temporal dependencies.

## Input / Output

The method consumes pre-extracted I3D frame features (D-dimensional vectors) grouped into non-overlapping clips of w frames (64 for GTEA, 128 for 50Salads and Breakfast), and produces per-frame action labels that partition the video into contiguous, non-overlapping temporal segments. Two inference modes are supported: online (stride δ=1, only the last frame's prediction is retained per step) and semi-online (stride δ=w, all w frame predictions within each clip are preserved). A lightweight post-processing pass then adjusts boundaries by suppressing low-confidence predictions shorter than a minimum segment length ℓ_min = σ × T_max.

## Method

The framework combines a Context-aware Feature Augmentation (CFA) module with an Adaptive Memory Bank on top of a single-stage causal TCN backbone. At each clip step, a GRU accumulates temporal context, which is then refined through Swin self-attention within the local clip window and a Transformer decoder that encodes the memory bank; cross-attention between the self-attended clip features and the decoded memory produces context-enhanced representations that are added back to the GRU output. The memory bank maintains both short-term context (the previous clip's enhanced features) and long-term context (Conv1D-compressed memory tokens appended per clip), with an adaptive budget that caps long-term storage at two-thirds of the total memory size and allocates the remainder to short-term information, allowing the model to shift emphasis as the video grows. Training is end-to-end with a cross-entropy loss plus a log-probability smoothing loss computed per clip. A post-processing step enforces a confidence threshold θ and minimum segment length to suppress over-segmentation fragments, which is critical because raw online predictions yield F1@50 scores as low as 8–10% on Breakfast.

## Results

Under semi-online inference with post-processing, the method achieves 79.4% Acc, 75.0% Edit, and 68.0% F1@50 on 50Salads; 76.0% Acc, 79.7% Edit, and 69.2% F1@50 on GTEA; and 53.8% Acc, 57.5% Edit, and 31.4% F1@50 on Breakfast. Post-processing is critical for segmental metrics: on Breakfast, F1@50 nearly triples from 10.1% (without p.p.) to 31.4% (with p.p.), while on 50Salads Edit jumps from 32.8% to 75.0%. The CFA module alone (without post-processing) improves Edit by 9.2% over the causal TCN baseline on 50Salads (28.8% vs. 19.6%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against LSTR [44], the closest online TAD baseline, the method achieves a dramatic improvement in segmental metrics: Edit on 50Salads goes from 5.0% (LSTR) to 75.0% (Ours semi+p.p.), and F1@50 from 4.1% to 68.0%, confirming that direct adaptation of online detection models to segmentation suffers from severe over-segmentation. Compared to the causal TCN baseline, the CFA module adds ~4% Acc and ~13% Edit on 50Salads (79.4% vs. 75.2% Acc; 75.0% vs. 19.6% Edit with p.p.). On 50Salads, the semi-online + p.p. configuration surpasses the offline MS-TCN [11] on all segmental metrics (Edit 75.0 vs. 65.8; F1@50 68.0 vs. 61.7) while remaining ~5% behind the strongest offline method DiffAct [25] (Acc 79.4 vs. 87.4). On Breakfast, even the unmodified causal TCN baseline (55.3% Acc) outperforms MV-TAS [13] (41.6% Acc), and the full method reaches 57.5% Edit versus 18.7% for the baseline. The largest remaining gap is on Breakfast, where offline DiffAct achieves 75.1% F1@50 versus 31.4% for the online method, highlighting the difficulty of online segmentation on longer, more complex videos.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the offline TAS backbone MS-TCN to an online setting via causal convolutions, then layers a Context-aware Feature Augmentation (CFA) module on top that combines a GRU for local temporal dynamics with a Transformer decoder and Swin attention for memory interaction. The adaptive memory bank (short-term + long-term tokens) is conceptually inspired by LSTR's long/short-term memory for online TAD, but is redesigned for procedural videos where actions have sequential dependencies spanning long durations, unlike the independent actions in TAD benchmarks. The post-processing step draws on rule-based over-segmentation mitigation from offline TAS but is specifically tailored to the online constraint of only past frames being available.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first dedicated online TAS framework, filling a gap between offline segmentation (which requires the full video) and online detection (which ignores procedural structure). The dramatic over-segmentation in the online setting (F1@50 dropping from 47.5% offline to 8.3% online on Breakfast) and the fact that a simple confidence-threshold + minimum-length post-processing nearly triples segmental scores highlight both the severity of the problem and a practical, transferable fix. Key open questions include handling interrupted actions, scaling to arbitrarily long streams within a fixed memory budget, and closing the remaining large gap to offline SOTA (e.g., DiffAct), particularly on complex datasets like Breakfast.
