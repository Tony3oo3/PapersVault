## Problem

Binary statistical decision making (BDM) problems—from the Neyman-Pearson hypothesis test to selective classification with out-of-distribution data—each require separate, often lengthy derivations of their optimal decision strategies, with some taking over a decade to resolve. No unified framework existed that could characterize optimal solutions across the full family of BDM formulations in a single theorem. This paper fills that gap by presenting a comprehensive generic formulation and a complete characterization of all optimal strategies that subsumes every known BDM instance.

## Input / Output

The generic problem operates on arbitrary Lebesgue measurable functions R, p, q over a measurable set X with no continuity or differentiability requirements, alongside non-negative constraint bounds δ and σ. The output is an optimal binary decision strategy c*(x) ∈ [0,1] structured as a threshold rule on a score s(x) = R(x)/p(x) + μ·q(x)/p(x), where μ is a multiplier determined by the constraint structure and λ is a threshold set by the bounds. In practical ML applications, the score decomposes into a conditional risk r(x) and an OOD/ID likelihood ratio g(x) = p_O(x)/p_I(x), both approximable by existing methods, reducing the problem to tuning a small number of scalars.

## Method

The main result (Theorem 1) proves that every optimal solution to the generic constrained problem is a threshold rule on the score s(x) = R(x)/p(x) + μ·q(x)/p(x), accepting when s(x) < λ and rejecting when s(x) > λ. The proof avoids Lagrange duality (which requires differentiability) by mapping points to the plane via κ(x) = (q(x)/p(x), R(x)/p(x)) and showing through a series of forbidden-point claims—built on Carathéodory's theorem—that any configuration violating linear separability of the accept, reject, and randomize regions contradicts optimality. The framework then reduces all BDM instances to five canonical problem forms (Problems 1–5), from which the optimal strategies for Neyman-Pearson, bounded-abstention, bounded-improvement, and SCOD formulations follow as immediate corollaries, and two novel SCOD variants (bounded TPR-FPR and bounded Precision-Recall) are solved for the first time.

## Results

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical experiments or quantitative benchmarks. The main result (Theorem 1) characterizes all optimal solutions to a generic constrained binary decision making problem (17), showing that any optimal strategy c* must equal 1 on the set where R(x)/p(x) + μ·q(x)/p(x) < λ and equal 0 where it exceeds λ, up to measure-zero sets. Lemma 1 further establishes that an optimal strategy can always be written as a threshold rule on a single score function s(x) = R(x)/p(x) + μ·q(x)/p(x). The paper demonstrates that six distinct BDM formulations — Neyman-Pearson, Bounded-Abstention, Bounded-Improvement, SCOD, bounded TPR-FPR SCOD, and bounded Precision-Recall SCOD — are all special cases of this generic problem, yielding optimal strategies of the form c*(x) = 1[s(x) < θ] with problem-specific score functions (e.g., s(x) = r(x) + β·g(x) for SCOD variants).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper contains no empirical comparisons or baseline evaluations, as its contribution is entirely theoretical. However, it provides a conceptual comparison with prior work: the heuristic SIRC strategy from the original SCOD paper [24] (which combines two detectors via non-linear functions) is shown to be suboptimal compared to the linear combination s(x) = r(x) + β·g(x) derived here, a finding empirically confirmed in [16]. The paper also notes that the Bounded-Abstention model [19] was formulated in 2005 but its optimal threshold strategy was not derived until 14 years later in [8], illustrating how the generic framework in this paper can shortcut such derivations. The two novel SCOD variants (bounded TPR-FPR, eq. 14, and bounded Precision-Recall, eq. 16) have no prior optimal solutions in the literature; their optimal strategies are derived for the first time using the proposed framework.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies a lineage of binary decision making (BDM) results spanning from the Neyman-Pearson lemma (1928) through the Bounded-Abstention and Bounded-Improvement models (2005, with optimal strategies derived in 2019) to the recent SCOD problem (2022, with optimal solutions in 2024) under a single generic constrained optimization framework over Lebesgue measurable functions. The key departure from prior work is methodological: rather than relying on Lagrange duality (which requires differentiability and is limited to finite-dimensional settings), the authors prove their main theorem via a direct argument using forbidden point configurations in the plane, yielding a result valid for arbitrary measurable spaces without regularity assumptions. The two novel SCOD variants (bounded TPR-FPR and bounded Precision-Recall) are straightforward applications of the framework, demonstrating its extensibility beyond previously studied formulations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides a practical recipe for any new BDM formulation: identify the three measurable functions (objective, lower-bound constraint, upper-bound constraint), compute the two ratio functions, and the optimal strategy is always a threshold on their linear combination. This reduces the problem from solving an infinite-dimensional optimization to approximating two component functions (e.g., conditional risk and likelihood ratio) and tuning a small number of scalars on held-out data. The main open question is whether the result extends to more than two constraints, which would require handling significantly greater combinatorial complexity in the point-configuration proof. For practitioners in OOD detection and selective classification, the framework confirms that the optimal selector always has a simple parametric form, validating the use of existing OOD and uncertainty estimation methods as building blocks.
