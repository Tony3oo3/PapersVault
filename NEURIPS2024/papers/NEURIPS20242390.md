## Problem

Existing text-to-3D generation methods rely on synthetic datasets with predefined, controlled camera trajectories (e.g., orbiting objects in Objaverse), leaving the potential of real-world multi-view captures largely unexplored. Real-world scenes introduce three compounding challenges: complex and scene-specific camera trajectories that cannot be pre-defined, unbounded backgrounds that break common bounded 3D representations, and limited dataset diversity that hinders open-world generalization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242390/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a free-form text prompt as input and produces two coupled outputs: a dense-view camera trajectory (29 views with full intrinsics and extrinsics, normalized so the first pose is identity and max translation distance is 1) and a 3D Gaussian scene (pixel-aligned Gaussians at 256×256 per view across 8 sparse views, with depth, rotation quaternion, scale, opacity, and spherical harmonics coefficients). Rendered images are at 256×256 resolution during generation and 512×512 during the SDS++ refinement stage. The framework also supports user-provided camera trajectories (pre- or post-generation) for novel view rendering.

## Method

Director3D decomposes the joint distribution p(X, C | y) into three sequential stages. First, Traj-DiT (a DiT with 8 blocks, hidden size 512) treats camera parameters as temporal tokens and performs conditional x0-prediction denoising to generate scene-specific trajectories from CLIP text embeddings. Second, GM-LDM (fine-tuned from Stable Diffusion v2.1) models the image sequence over a sparse 8-view subset using cross-view self-attention and ray-map conditioning, with a Gaussians decoder that outputs pixel-aligned 3D Gaussians as an immediate 3D representation; it supports dual-mode denoising (2D-based for generalization, rendering-based for 3D consistency, toggled at 1/10 steps) and is co-trained on multi-view and single-view data. Third, the SDS++ loss refines the 3D Gaussians over 1000 iterations by combining three mechanisms: classifier-free guidance for target distribution alignment, a learnable source prediction ε_src for adaptive current-distribution estimation, and a weighted combination of latent-space and image-space reconstruction objectives.

## Results

On T3Bench (object-level, 100 prompts), Director3D achieves BRISQUE 32.3, NIQE 4.35, and CLIP-Score 85.5, substantially outperforming the best prior method ProlificDreamer (BRISQUE 61.5, NIQE 7.07, CLIP-Score 69.4). On scene-level evaluation (64 prompts), Director3D attains the highest CLIP-Score (83.9) and the shortest inference time (5 min) among all baselines, while achieving the second-best NIQE (4.09 vs. LucidDreamer's 3.40). Even without the SDS++ refining step, the method already surpasses all baselines on CLIP-Score (80.0), indicating strong base generation quality from GM-LDM alone.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Director3D outperforms all six object-level SDS-based baselines (DreamFusion, Magic3D, LatentNerf, SJC, Fantasia3D, ProlificDreamer) on every metric in Table 1, with the largest gap over ProlificDreamer (BRISQUE 32.3 vs. 61.5; CLIP-Score 85.5 vs. 69.4). In the scene-level comparison (Table 2), Director3D achieves the best CLIP-Score (83.9) and fastest inference (5 min vs. 15–90 min for others), but trails LucidDreamer-BackForth on NIQE (4.09 vs. 3.40), which the authors attribute to LucidDreamer's multi-view inconsistency and edge artifacts visible in qualitative comparisons. Against GaussianDreamer, Director3D wins on both NIQE (4.09 vs. 6.96) and CLIP-Score (83.9 vs. 71.8) while being 3× faster. Qualitative comparisons against GRM and DreamScene show Director3D producing more realistic scenes with proper backgrounds, shadows, and lighting, whereas GRM is limited to object-only outputs and DreamScene yields overly saturated, cartoonish results.

## Relation to Prior Work

Director3D sits at the intersection of three active research threads: (1) SDS-based text-to-3D methods (DreamFusion, ProlificDreamer, HIFA, LODS) that optimize 3D representations using 2D diffusion priors, (2) feed-forward multi-view diffusion models (MVDream, DMV3D, GRM, GS-LRM) that generate 3D assets without per-scene optimization, and (3) 3D Gaussian Splatting as a fast, differentiable scene representation. The key departure is the use of real-world multi-view datasets (MVImgNet, DL3DV-10K) with complex, scene-specific camera trajectories rather than synthetic object-centric data with predefined orbits, necessitating a learned trajectory generator (Traj-DiT) and a rendering-based multi-view diffusion model (GM-LDM) that directly produces pixel-aligned Gaussians during denoising. The SDS++ loss further extends prior SDS variants by combining a learnable source distribution (from LODS), classifier-free-guided target prediction, and dual latent/image-space objectives (from HIFA) into a single refinement objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first framework to jointly generate adaptive camera trajectories and realistic 3D scenes from text using real-world captures, addressing a gap where prior scene-level methods either relied on user-defined trajectories or produced multi-view-inconsistent results from inpainting/depth pipelines. The 5-minute inference time (vs. 15–90 min for baselines) makes it practically relevant for content creation pipelines. However, the reliance on a 1000-iteration SDS++ refinement step for open-world generalization, the limited view count of GM-LDM, and struggles with compositional prompts (exact object counts, articulated objects) remain open challenges. Future work on larger multi-view datasets, direct joint modeling of trajectories and images, and eliminating the refinement stage would be natural next steps.
