## Problem

Traditional causal effect estimation frameworks (structural causal models, do-calculus, truncated factorization) are formulated exclusively for i.i.d. data and do not extend to exchangeable non-i.i.d. generative processes that naturally arise in multi-environment settings. While the Causal de Finetti theorems established that exchangeable data satisfying independent causal mechanisms (ICM) enables unique causal structure identification, the operational meaning of interventions, feasible intervention targets, and causal effect identifiability in this exchangeable setting remained undefined.

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-environment data where each environment e provides a finite sequence (X_{1;n}^e, ..., X_{d;n}^e)_{n=1}^{N_e} of exchangeable random variable arrays, along with a specified intervention set X and target set Y. The output is a jointly estimated causal DAG Ĝ and the full post-interventional distribution P(Y|do(X=x)) computed via the new truncated factorization. Unlike i.i.d. settings where position indices can be dropped, the exchangeable formulation requires tracking position indices because P(X,Y) alone cannot characterize the joint distribution across positions.

## Method

The paper defines the operational meaning of do-interventions in ICM generative processes as assigning delta-distributions to the intervened variables' conditional sampling densities (rather than deleting structural equations as in SCMs), since fixing causal de Finetti parameters θ does not uniquely determine observables. The central result (Theorem 1) is a truncated factorization formula that decomposes the post-interventional joint distribution into products of pre-interventional conditional distributions over variable indices, with the key distinction that intervened variable indices retain their conditional distributions at non-intervened positions while non-intervened indices keep all positions. The Do-Finetti algorithm then combines the Causal-de-Finetti procedure (for unique DAG identification from exchangeable data) with this truncated factorization (for effect computation), enabling simultaneous graph recovery and causal effect estimation from multi-environment data alone. A critical property shown is that in exchangeable data, conditioning on observations at other positions changes the interventional distribution (e.g., P(Y₁|do(X₁), X₂, Y₂) ≠ P(Y₁|do(X₁))), a phenomenon absent in i.i.d. data, illustrated concretely through a causal Pólya urn model.

## Results

The paper's empirical validation (Section 5, Figure 4) uses synthetic data from a causal Pólya urn model with 100 repeated experiments across varying numbers of environments. Do-Finetti achieves near-zero mean squared error (MSE) in causal effect estimation and near-perfect accuracy in DAG identification, converging to the analytic solution as the number of environments increases. In contrast, the i.i.d. baseline (PC algorithm + standard truncated factorization) produces high and persistent estimation error that does not decrease with more environments. Even when the true DAG is provided, the i.i.d. truncated factorization still fails to recover the correct post-interventional distribution, confirming that the failure is due to the i.i.d. assumption itself rather than graphical misclassification.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares Do-Finetti against two i.i.d.-based baselines: (1) the standard i.i.d. pipeline using the PC algorithm for graph discovery followed by Pearl's truncated factorization (Eq. 2) for effect estimation, and (2) the same i.i.d. truncated factorization but with the true DAG provided (eliminating graph-discovery error). Do-Finetti decisively outperforms both baselines on both metrics (DAG identification accuracy and causal effect MSE). The i.i.d. baseline with PC algorithm suffers from both graphical misclassification and incorrect effect estimation. Critically, even the i.i.d. method with the true DAG and effectively infinite data never achieves the analytic solution, demonstrating that the standard truncated factorization is fundamentally inapplicable to ICM generative processes regardless of graph knowledge. Do-Finetti, by contrast, achieves near-zero MSE and correct DAG recovery, validating that the exchangeable framework is both necessary and sufficient for correct causal effect estimation in this setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends the Causal de Finetti framework of Guo et al. (2023a), which established ICM generative processes and proved unique causal structure identification in exchangeable data, by addressing the complementary problem of causal effect identification and estimation. It generalizes Pearl's truncated factorization (g-computation formula) from i.i.d. structural causal models to exchangeable ICM generative processes, showing that the standard do-calculus formalism does not transfer to the non-i.i.d. setting. The Do-Finetti algorithm composes the Causal-de-Finetti structure discovery algorithm with the new truncated factorization, and the causal Pólya urn model provides a concrete illustration of how interventions propagate differently under exchangeability versus i.i.d. assumptions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work fills a critical gap in the causal de Finetti line of research: prior work established that exchangeable ICM data enables unique graph identification, but the meaning and computation of interventions in this setting were undefined. The result that i.i.d. data alone is insufficient for simultaneous graph and effect identification, while exchangeable ICM data suffices, reframes multi-environment data as a strictly more informative resource for causal inference. However, the empirical validation is limited to bivariate synthetic data with Beta-Bernoulli conjugate priors, and the authors explicitly note that counterfactual queries, semi-Markovian models, and scalable algorithms remain open. The practical gap between the theoretical framework and real-world multi-environment applications is substantial.
