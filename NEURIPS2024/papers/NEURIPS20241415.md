## Problem

Existing parameter-free optimization methods only eliminate the need to tune the stepsize, but clipped gradient descent requires tuning both the stepsize and the gradient clipping threshold to achieve its favorable convergence rate under (L₀,L₁)-smoothness. No prior work had investigated parameter-free methods for the clipping threshold, leaving practitioners to perform expensive grid searches over two coupled hyperparameters. The paper asks whether a parameter-free method can match the asymptotic L-independence of well-tuned clipped gradient descent without any problem-specific parameters.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes the gradient of the loss function at each iterate, the initial parameter vector x₀, the total number of iterations T, and a lower bound l* on the optimal loss (typically set to 0 for non-negative losses). Rather than returning the final iterate, it tracks and returns the best iterate x^best — the parameter achieving the lowest observed loss over all T steps. The √T factor in the stepsize denominator means the method requires knowing T in advance, distinguishing it from fully online parameter-free methods.

## Method

The paper first proves (Theorem 4) that the exact Polyak stepsize η_t = (f(x_t)−f*)/‖∇f(x_t)‖² achieves the same convergence rate as clipped gradient descent with optimally tuned stepsize 1/L₀ and clipping threshold L₀/L₁ under (L₀,L₁)-smoothness — a connection no prior work had identified. To remove the problem-specific f*, the authors propose Inexact Polyak Stepsize, which replaces f* with the lower bound l* and scales the stepsize by 1/√T: η_t = (f(x_t)−l*)/(√T‖∇f(x_t)‖²). Crucially, unlike DecSPS and AdaSPS which enforce a monotonically non-increasing stepsize (destroying L-independence), this method keeps the stepsize adaptive and instead returns the best iterate over all T steps. The resulting convergence rate O((L₀‖x₀−x*‖²+σ²)/√T + LL₁²‖x₀−x*‖⁴/T + L₁²Lσ⁴/(L₀²T)) is asymptotically independent of L, matching the favorable property of clipped gradient descent while requiring no hyperparameter tuning.

## Results

Theoretically, Inexact Polyak Stepsize achieves a convergence rate of O((L₀‖x₀−x⋆‖² + σ²)/√T + LL₁²‖x₀−x⋆‖⁴/T + L₁²Lσ⁴/(L₀²T)), which is asymptotically independent of the global smoothness constant L under (L₀,L₁)-smoothness, matching the rate of clipped gradient descent with optimally tuned hyperparameters. On the synthetic function f(x) = (L₀L₁²/72)x⁴ + (L₀/4)x², Inexact Polyak Stepsize's convergence is invariant to L₁ (tested up to L₁=1000), while DecSPS and AdaSPS show clear degradation as L₁ increases. On neural networks (LSTM/Penn Treebank, Nano-GPT/Shakespeare, T5/C4), Inexact Polyak Stepsize consistently achieves the lowest final test loss among all parameter-free methods across all three architectures.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Inexact Polyak Stepsize matches the asymptotic convergence rate of clipped gradient descent with well-tuned stepsize 1/L₀ and clipping threshold L₀/L₁ (Koloskova et al., 2023), but without requiring knowledge of L₀ or L₁. Compared to exact Polyak stepsize (Polyak, 1987), Inexact Polyak Stepsize removes the need for the problem-specific minimum loss f⋆ while preserving the favorable (L₀,L₁)-smoothness-dependent rate; however, exact Polyak stepsize diverged on LSTM and Nano-GPT in practice. Inexact Polyak Stepsize consistently outperforms both DecSPS (Orvieto et al., 2022) and AdaSPS (Jiang and Stich, 2023) on all three neural network architectures, and the synthetic function experiments confirm that DecSPS and AdaSPS lose the asymptotic independence from L (their convergence degrades as L₁ grows), whereas Inexact Polyak Stepsize does not. Against DoG (Ivgi et al., 2023), Inexact Polyak Stepsize is more stable: DoG achieved the best result for LSTM among parameter-free methods but exhibited very unstable training on Nano-GPT with much higher loss values, while Inexact Polyak Stepsize succeeded consistently across all architectures.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of parameter-free optimization (DecSPS, AdaSPS, DoG) and clipped gradient descent theory under (L₀,L₁)-smoothness (Koloskova et al., Zhang et al.). The key conceptual contribution is revealing that Polyak stepsize achieves exactly the same convergence rate as clipped gradient descent with optimally tuned stepsize and clipping threshold under (L₀,L₁)-smoothness—a connection no prior work had identified. The paper then departs from DecSPS and AdaSPS, which enforce monotonically non-increasing stepsizes, by instead scaling the stepsize by 1/√T and returning the best iterate, preserving the adaptive stepsize behavior that gives clipped GD its L-independence.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first parameter-free method that simultaneously eliminates the need to tune both the stepsize and the gradient clipping threshold, two hyperparameters that are critical for training language models. The practical impact is meaningful for practitioners who must currently grid-search over both parameters, though the O(1/√T) convergence rate is suboptimal compared to O(1/T) for clipped GD with tuned hyperparameters, and the authors explicitly frame this as a 'first step' with room for improvement. Open questions include whether adaptive methods (e.g., Hazan & Kakade's) can recover the O(1/T) rate in a parameter-free setting, and whether the approach extends to stochastic or non-convex regimes.
