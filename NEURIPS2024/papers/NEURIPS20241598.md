## Problem

Knowledge distillation (KD)-based federated learning techniques such as FedNTD and MOON improve global model accuracy under data heterogeneity by aligning local models with the server model, but this same alignment mechanism inadvertently amplifies model poisoning attacks: benign clients unknowingly distill from a poisoned global model, causing post-attack accuracy to drop below even the non-KD FedAvg baseline. The paper identifies this 'attack amplification' vulnerability as inherent to any KD-based FL method that aligns client outputs or representations with the server, and asks how to retain KD's heterogeneity benefits while neutralizing its attack-amplifying effect.

## Input / Output

Each FL client receives its local image dataset (28×28 grayscale MNIST or 32×32 RGB CIFAR-10/100, partitioned non-IID via Dirichlet distribution) plus the current global model's outputs or hidden representations as the distillation target. The client's local model produces a class label prediction and, through newly inserted auxiliary classifiers at shallow convolutional layers, intermediate representations or not-true logits that serve as additional distillation targets. The server aggregates client updates using Trimmed Mean and redistributes the updated global model each round.

## Method

HYDRA-FL modifies the standard KD-based FL local training objective into a three-term hybrid loss: (1) the usual cross-entropy loss on the client's own data, (2) a diminished final-layer KD loss where the original coefficient β (or μ for MOON) is divided by a diminishing factor b to reduce over-reliance on aligning with the potentially poisoned server output, and (3) a new shallow distillation loss γ·L_KD(y_aux, y_s) computed between an auxiliary classifier's output at an early layer and the server's corresponding output. Auxiliary classifiers (small linear heads) are inserted after each convolutional layer, so the shallow distillation aligns low-level features that are less susceptible to adversarial manipulation of deeper layers. The framework is formulated generically (Eq. 16) so it can be plugged into any KD-based FL algorithm; the paper instantiates it for FedNTD (KL-divergence on not-true logits) and MOON (contrastive loss on hidden representations). Theoretical analysis shows that with appropriate b and γ, the derivative of the HYDRA-FL loss with respect to β is strictly smaller than that of vanilla KD under attack, formally bounding the amplification.

## Results

On MNIST (α=0.05), HYDRA-FL achieves 76.67% post-attack accuracy, substantially outperforming FedNTD (58.09%) and even exceeding FedAvg (74.48%), while maintaining 92.69% benign accuracy comparable to FedNTD (93.03%). On CIFAR-10 (α=0.1), HYDRA-FL reaches 25.15% post-attack accuracy versus FedNTD's 21.72%, and on CIFAR-100 it achieves 14.33% versus FedNTD's 13.92%. For the MOON adaptation, HYDRA-FL achieves 43.6% post-attack accuracy on CIFAR-10 (α=0.5) versus MOON's 39.9% and FedAvg's 40.9%, while maintaining 60.1% benign accuracy that surpasses both baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against FedNTD, HYDRA-FL dramatically improves post-attack accuracy across all settings: on MNIST (α=0.05) it gains +18.6 points (76.67% vs. 58.09%), on CIFAR-10 (α=0.1) it gains +3.4 points (25.15% vs. 21.72%), and on CIFAR-100 it gains +0.4 points (14.33% vs. 13.92%), while maintaining nearly identical benign accuracy (within 0.3 points). Against MOON, HYDRA-FL similarly outperforms in attack settings: on CIFAR-10 (α=0.5) it achieves 43.6% vs. MOON's 39.9% (+3.7 points) and on CIFAR-100 it reaches 25.18% vs. 23.81% (+1.4 points), while also matching or exceeding MOON's benign accuracy. Critically, HYDRA-FL also surpasses the FedAvg baseline in most attack scenarios (e.g., 76.67% vs. 74.48% on MNIST, 43.6% vs. 40.9% on CIFAR-10 α=0.5), whereas the original KD-based methods (FedNTD, MOON) perform *worse* than FedAvg under attack — the attack amplification phenomenon this paper identifies. In benign settings, HYDRA-FL is within 0.5 points of the KD baselines, confirming that the robustness gains come at negligible accuracy cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on two KD-based FL techniques—FedNTD (Lee et al., NeurIPS 2022) and MOON (Li et al., CVPR 2021)—which address data heterogeneity by aligning local models with the global model via KL-divergence and contrastive loss, respectively. Conceptually, HYDRA-FL draws from Self-Distillation (Zhang et al., ICCV 2019) and Skeptical Students (Kundu et al., NeurIPS 2021), which use shallow-layer distillation to handle unreliable teachers, but repurposes this idea for a distinct FL-specific threat: untargeted model poisoning that corrupts the global model through aggregation. The key departure from prior work is the identification of 'attack amplification'—a previously unacknowledged vulnerability where the very alignment mechanism that improves benign performance inadvertently propagates poison to benign clients—positioning HYDRA-FL as a bridge between heterogeneity mitigation and Byzantine robustness.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The attack amplification finding is practically significant because it means a widely adopted solution to data heterogeneity (KD-based alignment) silently degrades security, and practitioners deploying FedNTD or MOON in adversarial settings may be unknowingly making their systems more vulnerable than plain FedAvg. HYDRA-FL offers a lightweight, drop-in modification (auxiliary classifiers + diminished final-layer KD loss) that addresses both concerns simultaneously. Limitations include evaluation on only three small image datasets (MNIST, CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100), no error bars across seeds, and testing on only two KD-based techniques; open questions include whether the approach generalizes to language/multimodal FL, other KD variants, and targeted (backdoor) attacks, and whether the theoretical analysis (which is informal rather than a full proof) holds under different aggregation rules or attack strengths.
