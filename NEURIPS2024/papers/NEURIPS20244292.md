## Problem

Vision-Language Models struggle with 3D spatial reasoning tasks such as estimating metric distances, determining relative directions, and comparing object sizes. Two critical gaps persist: most VLMs function as global image parsers that cannot accept region-specific proposals to disambiguate which objects to reason about, and RGB-only inputs are fundamentally insufficient for accurate depth and distance perception.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20244292/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

SpatialRGPT accepts an RGB image (384×384 for VILA variants) alongside user-specified region proposals in the form of bounding boxes or segmentation masks, plus an optional relative depth map, and a natural-language text prompt. The model produces free-form spatial reasoning answers that include qualitative relations (left/right, behind/front, tall/short), quantitative metric measurements (distances in meters/inches/feet, object width and height), and directional estimates expressed as clock positions. At inference, if only a box is provided, SAM is used to generate the corresponding mask.

## Method

The core pipeline constructs 3D scene graphs from single 2D images by chaining open-vocabulary detection (GroundingDINO), metric depth estimation (Metric3Dv2), and camera calibration (WildCamera + PerspectiveFields for canonicalization), then converts these graphs into region-aware spatial QA pairs via both template-based and Llama3-70B-based generation, yielding the 8.7M-sample Open Spatial Dataset. Architecturally, SpatialRGPT builds on RegionGPT's pixel-level region feature extractor (2-layer deconvolution + MaskPooling) and introduces a flexible depth plugin: the same visual encoder (CLIP-L or SigLIP) processes the depth map, and a separate depth-to-language linear connector—trained only on spatial QAs—projects depth features into the LLM embedding space, allowing the model to function with or without depth input. The LLM backbone is LLaMA2-7B or VILA-1.5 (3B/8B), and training follows a three-stage paradigm: connector feature alignment on CC3M, visual language pre-training on MMC4/COYO/OSD, and instruction fine-tuning on a blend of general, region-level, and spatial datasets with random modality sampling (box vs. mask) per sample.

## Results

On SpatialRGPT-Bench, SpatialRGPT-VILA-1.5-8B achieves 92.69% average success rate on qualitative spatial reasoning (e.g., 100% on Left/Right, 99.17% on Below/Above), substantially outperforming GPT-4V (58.14%) and LLaVA-v1.6-34B (43.98%). For quantitative tasks, SpatialRGPT-7B achieves 95.3% success rate on direction estimation with only 15.4° absolute relative error, compared to GPT-4V's 43.9%/69.9°. On the BLINK RelativeDepth benchmark, SpatialRGPT-VILA-1.5-8B reaches 87.9% accuracy, surpassing GPT-4V-Turbo (66.9%) by over 20 points. On general VLM benchmarks (VQAv2, GQA, MME, etc.), SpatialRGPT maintains performance comparable to its VILA-1.5-3B base model, confirming that spatial training does not degrade general capabilities.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SpatialRGPT dramatically outperforms all baselines on spatial reasoning: the 8B variant achieves 92.69% average on qualitative tasks versus GPT-4V's 58.14%, LLaVA-v1.6-34B's 43.98%, and RegionVILA-7B's 40.48% — a gap of 34–76 points. Even the RGB-only variant (no depth) reaches 89.80%, still far above GPT-4V+SoM (54.33%) and KOSMOS-2 (17.04%). On quantitative direction estimation, SpatialRGPT-7B achieves 95.3% success with 15.4° error, while GPT-4V manages only 43.9%/69.9° and LLaVA-v1.6-34B 33.6%/78.2°. On BLINK RelativeDepth, SpatialRGPT-VILA-1.5-8B (87.9%) beats GPT-4V-Turbo (66.9%), GPT-4o (64.5%), and all open-source VLMs by 15–38 points. On region-level classification (COCO-2017), SpatialRGPT-VILA-1.5-8B achieves 72.9 mAP / 82.9% accuracy, slightly surpassing RegionGPT-7B (70.0/80.6%) and GPT4RoI-7B (64.0% acc). On general VLM benchmarks, SpatialRGPT-VILA-1.5-3B matches or slightly exceeds its VILA-1.5-3B base (e.g., 81.1 vs 80.4 on VQAv2, 65.6 vs 63.4 on MMB), confirming no degradation from spatial training.

## Relation to Prior Work

SpatialRGPT builds directly on RegionGPT's pixel-level region feature extraction and VILA's three-stage pre-training paradigm, extending both to incorporate 3D spatial knowledge. It departs from SpatialVLM, which relies on language descriptions of objects and cannot precisely specify regions, by using explicit region proposals (boxes/masks) as inputs. It also diverges from 3D scene graph approaches (3D-LLM, ConceptGraph) that require multi-view images or explicit 3D reconstruction, instead constructing 3D scene graphs from single 2D images via open-vocabulary detection, metric depth estimation, and camera calibration. The flexible depth plugin module is a key architectural departure from prior VLMs that either ignore depth or require full 3D point cloud inputs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a fundamental gap in VLMs: the inability to reason about spatial arrangements at the region level with metric precision, which is critical for robotics, AR, and navigation. The Open Spatial Dataset (8.7M spatial concepts over 5M regions) and SpatialRGPT-Bench (ground-truth 3D annotations across indoor, outdoor, and simulated environments) are substantial resources that the field lacked. Key limitations include reliance on axis-aligned bounding boxes (less accurate than oriented boxes), single-view depth estimation errors, and the absence of open-vocabulary pose estimation. Open questions include whether the approach scales to dynamic scenes, whether the depth plugin's gains persist with more diverse training data, and how to handle the inherent ambiguity of single-view 3D reconstruction.
