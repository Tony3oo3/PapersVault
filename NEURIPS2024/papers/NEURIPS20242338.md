## Problem

Existing diffusion-based inverse problem solvers frame signal recovery as probabilistic posterior sampling, which requires the intractable conditional score ∇_x log p_t(y|x_t) that depends on the noisy intermediate state x_t rather than the final sample x_0. This forces reliance on a chain of approximations (Tweedie's formula, learned score networks) that degrade reconstruction quality, make the solver brittle to discretization step count, and couple performance tightly to the accuracy of the prior score network. The paper asks whether these approximations can be eliminated entirely by reframing the generative process under a different mathematical lens.

## Input / Output

The solver takes a noisy measurement y ∈ R^d, an arbitrary differentiable forward operator A: R^n → R^d (e.g., bicubic downsampling for 4× super-resolution, random 92% pixel masking for inpainting, Gaussian or motion blur kernels, or even a trained neural classifier), and a pretrained unconditional diffusion model, then produces a reconstructed image x_0 ∈ R^n. No conditional score network is trained; the method operates zero-shot for any new forward model. Experiments use FFHQ 256×256 (3-channel) and MNIST 28×28, with Gaussian noise (σ=0.05) added to all measurements, and the solver runs with T=50 diffusion steps and 50–100 iLQR iterations (NFE=2500 or 1000).

## Method

The paper reframes the discretized reverse diffusion PF-ODE as a discrete-time nonlinear dynamical system and applies the Iterative Linear Quadratic Regulator (iLQR) to compute optimal control perturbations u_t injected at each diffusion step (in either input-perturbation or output-perturbation mode). The terminal cost is set to −log p(y|x_0), and iLQR backpropagates this cost through a full forward trajectory rollout, which naturally yields the exact conditional score ∇_{x_t} log p(y|x_0) at every time step without any Tweedie approximation or learned conditional score network. Theoretically, the paper proves that the idealized posterior sampling equation of Song et al. (2021) is recovered as a special case when the Tikhonov regularizer α = 1/(g(t)²Δt) is chosen, establishing that the optimal control formulation strictly generalizes the probabilistic sampler. To handle the extreme dimensionality of image states (~39B parameters for 256×256×3), the authors introduce three computational modifications: randomized low-rank SVD approximations of Jacobian/Hessian matrices, matrix-free evaluations via projected matrix pairs, and Adam-preconditioned action updates, reducing the per-iteration cost to O(k³ + kd²) FLOPs with O(nmk) neural function evaluations.

## Results

On FFHQ 256×256-1K, the proposed method (NFE=2500) achieves the best FID and LPIPS across all five inverse problem tasks: SR×4 (FID 32.47, LPIPS 0.171), Random Inpainting (FID 15.93, LPIPS 0.053), Box Inpainting (FID 20.22, LPIPS 0.122), Gaussian Deblurring (FID 31.80, LPIPS 0.189), and Motion Deblurring (FID 39.40, LPIPS 0.217). At the more practical NFE=1000 budget, the method remains competitive or superior on most metrics, e.g. FID 20.75 on Random Inpainting vs. 21.34 for PSLD and 33.12 for DPS. The method also demonstrates robustness to discretization, producing stable solutions across varying diffusion step counts T where DPS degrades significantly.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all nine baselines on the majority of FID and LPIPS metrics at NFE=2500, establishing a new state-of-the-art on FFHQ 256×256. The closest competitor is PSLD (Rout et al., 2024), which achieves a marginally lower LPIPS on Random Inpainting (0.096 vs. 0.108 at NFE=1000) and a slightly lower FID on SR×4 (34.28 vs. 37.53 at NFE=1000), but the proposed method surpasses it on FID for Random Inpainting (20.75 vs. 21.34) and dominates on all other tasks. DPS (Chung et al., 2023a) is competitive on Box Inpainting FID (21.19 vs. 23.88) and Motion Deblurring FID (39.92 vs. 45.99) at NFE=1000, but the proposed method wins decisively on LPIPS for both tasks (0.164 vs. 0.212 and 0.233 vs. 0.242) and on all other tasks. Non-diffusion baselines (PNP-ADMM, ADMM-TV) and older diffusion methods (DDNM, DDRM, MCG, Score-SDE) are substantially outperformed, with FID gaps often exceeding 30–100 points. The method also handles the nonlinear class-conditional inverse problem (MNIST digit generation from a label) more effectively than DPS, producing images with stronger class alignment and higher sample quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the iterative Linear Quadratic Regulator (iLQR) from optimal control theory to the reverse diffusion process, treating the discretized PF-ODE as a discrete-time dynamical system to be guided by control inputs. It departs from the dominant probabilistic posterior sampling framework (DPS, DDRM, MCG, Score-SDE) by eliminating the need to approximate the intractable conditional score ∇log p(y|x_t) via Tweedie's formula or other surrogates. The connection between optimal control and diffusion processes has been explored in generative modeling (Berner et al. 2022, Chen et al. 2021), but this is the first application to guided conditional diffusion at inference time for inverse problems. The paper further shows that the idealized posterior sampling equation of Song et al. (2021) emerges as a special case of the optimal control formulation, unifying the two perspectives.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By removing the dependence on conditional score approximations, the method is robust to discretization step count, score network quality (even randomly initialized models produce feasible solutions), and can handle arbitrary differentiable forward operators including highly nonlinear neural classifiers. The main practical limitation is the higher NFE budget (2500 vs. 1000 for many baselines) and the requirement that the forward operator be differentiable, which excludes some real-world measurement processes. Open questions include whether the optimal control framing extends to non-differentiable or stochastic forward operators, whether it scales to larger latent diffusion models (e.g., Stable Diffusion at 512×512 or higher), and whether the computational overhead can be further reduced through the proposed low-rank and matrix-free approximations at scale.
