## Problem

Existing temporal knowledge graph (TKG) representation learning approaches model pairwise entity correlations via GNNs or high-order correlations via static derived structures (communities, hypergraphs), but none capture how high-order correlations among entities evolve over time. This is a critical gap because, as the paper illustrates with international political organizations, membership adjustments in such groups are gradual and incremental rather than sudden, requiring models to track the temporal drift of multi-entity groupings.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241751/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a sequence of TKG event quadruples (s, r, o, t) partitioned into discrete timestamps, processing them sequentially with a configurable historical window (1–14 timestamps). At each timestamp, a multi-relational entity graph is constructed from concurrent events, and a static global graph is built from all training events to provide structural priors. The output is a probability distribution over all relations for a given (subject, object, timestamp) query, produced by a ConvTransE decoder operating on integrated entity and relation representations.

## Method

DECRL jointly optimizes TKG representation learning with deep evolutionary clustering. At each timestamp, a relation-aware GCN updates entity representations, which are then clustered via fuzzy c-means into soft overlapping clusters representing high-order correlations. A cluster-aware Hungarian matching algorithm aligns clusters one-to-one across consecutive timestamps, and a weighted fusion with a temporal smoothness loss preserves cluster continuity. An implicit correlation encoder (MLP + convolution) computes latent correlation representations and intensities between all cluster pairs, forming a fully connected cluster graph whose edge weights are further modulated by spectral-cluster similarities from a static global graph. Cluster graph message passing updates entity representations, which are blended with previous-timestamp outputs via a time residual gate, and finally an attentive temporal encoder with sinusoidal position encoding captures cross-timestamp dependencies before a ConvTransE decoder predicts the relation.

## Results

DECRL achieves state-of-the-art event prediction across all seven datasets, outperforming the best baseline (DHyper) by an average of 9.53% in MRR, 12.98% in Hits@1, 10.42% in Hits@3, and 14.68% in Hits@10. On ICEWS14, DECRL reaches 62.61 MRR / 48.73 Hits@1 / 70.57 Hits@3 / 93.03 Hits@10, surpassing DHyper (56.15/43.76/65.46/85.89). On the large-scale GDELT dataset, where DHyper runs out of memory, DECRL achieves 27.58 MRR, beating the next-best method TiRGN (24.61 MRR) by 12.07%. On WIKI and YAGO, DECRL attains 99.67 and 99.56 MRR respectively, edging out DHyper by 0.29% and 0.25%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DECRL outperforms all 12 baselines across all seven datasets. The closest competitor is DHyper (TOIS 2024), the SOTA derived-structure-based method using dual hypergraph neural networks; DECRL beats it by 11.50% MRR on ICEWS14, 16.75% on ICEWS18, and 8.11% on ICEWS14C. On GDELT, DHyper fails with out-of-memory errors and TITer exceeds the 24-hour time limit, leaving TiRGN (IJCAI 2022) as the strongest runnable baseline at 24.61 MRR versus DECRL's 27.58 MRR (+12.07%). Among DNN-based approaches, TiRGN and RE-GCN are the strongest, yet both trail DECRL by 16+ MRR points on ICEWS14/18. Shallow encoder methods (TTransE, HyTE) are far behind, scoring below 26 MRR on ICEWS14. On the near-saturated WIKI and YAGO benchmarks, DECRL still edges out DHyper by 0.29% and 0.25% MRR respectively, confirming consistent gains even when headroom is minimal.

## Relation to Prior Work

DECRL sits at the intersection of two research threads: TKG representation learning (which has evolved from shallow encoders like TTransE/HyTE through GNN-based methods like TeMP/RE-GCN/TiRGN to derived-structure approaches like EvoExplore/GTRL/DHyper) and deep evolutionary clustering (DYNMOGA, sE-NMF, DNETC, CDNE, TRNNGCN). The paper's core positioning is that it is the first to jointly optimize TKG representation learning with evolutionary clustering, repurposing fuzzy c-means for soft overlapping entity clusters and the Hungarian algorithm for cross-timestamp cluster alignment. Unlike prior derived-structure methods that model static high-order correlations (communities, hypergraphs, entity groups), DECRL explicitly models how these high-order groupings evolve over time, using a cluster graph with an implicit correlation encoder to capture inter-cluster latent relationships.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine gap: prior TKG methods model high-order correlations but treat them as static, missing the gradual membership shifts that characterize real-world dynamics (e.g., Brexit). The cluster-aware alignment mechanism and temporal smoothness loss are practical contributions for any setting where group structure evolves. However, the authors acknowledge two significant limitations: the model only considers the immediately previous timestamp (not continuous multi-step temporal influence), and the implicit correlation encoder assumes uniform correlation structure across all cluster pairs, which may not reflect the heterogeneity of real-world interactions. These open questions point toward multi-relation-aware inter-cluster encoders and longer-horizon temporal modeling as natural next steps.
