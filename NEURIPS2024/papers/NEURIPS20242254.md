## Problem

Existing stochastic bilevel optimization algorithms (e.g., StocBiO, SOBA) require mutually independent sampling of examples for each gradient component in the hyper-gradient, forcing multiple redundant backward passes per iteration (seven vs. three). This independent-sampling requirement is inherited from single-level SGD analysis but is not fundamentally necessary for bilevel problems, and it inflates computational cost especially for large-scale models. The paper asks whether without-replacement sampling strategies—well-studied in single-level optimization—can be adapted to bilevel optimization to reduce both computational overhead and convergence complexity.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a finite-sum bilevel problem with outer dataset D_u (m examples) and inner dataset D_l (n examples), along with learning rates η, γ, ρ. A key operational difference from independent-sampling baselines is that at each step the same example pair {ξ_t, ζ_t} is reused across all gradient and Hessian-vector computations, reducing backward passes from seven to three per iteration. The algorithm outputs an ε-stationary point x (‖∇h(x)‖ ≤ ε) along with estimates of the inner minimizer y_x and the hyper-gradient auxiliary variable u_x, achieving O(ε⁻³) sample complexity for standard bilevel optimization and O(ε⁻⁴) for the conditional variant.

## Method

WiOR-BO performs a single-loop y-u-x update (inherited from FLSA) but replaces independent per-component sampling with a fixed example order π generated by random-reshuffling or shuffle-once over the outer and inner datasets. Because the same example is used for ∇_y g, ∇_{y²} g, ∇_{xy} g, ∇_x f, and ∇_y f at each step, the algorithm requires only three backward passes instead of seven. The theoretical analysis extends the aggregated (block-level) technique from single-level without-replacement sampling to the bilevel setting: Assumption 4.5 bounds the average gradient error over a block of length k, yielding α=2 for permutation-based orders (vs. α=1 for independent sampling), which directly improves the convergence rate from O(ε⁻⁴) to O(ε⁻³). For conditional bilevel optimization, WiOR-CBO uses a double-loop structure where the inner problem is re-solved per outer example, achieving O(ε⁻⁴) vs. O(ε⁻⁶) for independent-sampling baselines. A herding-based permutation (GRAB) can further eliminate the dataset-size dependence entirely (q=0).

## Results

The headline theoretical result is that WiOR-BO achieves O((max(m,n))^q ε^{-3}) gradient evaluations to reach an ε-stationary point in bilevel optimization, improving over the O(ε^{-4}) rate of independent-sampling methods like StocBiO and SOBA. For conditional bilevel optimization, WiOR-CBO achieves O((max(m,n))^{2q} ε^{-4}) inner gradient evaluations, improving over the O(ε^{-6}) rate of DL-SGD. In experiments on MNIST (Hyper-Data Cleaning), Omniglot, and MiniImageNet (Hyper-Representation Learning), both shuffle-once and random-reshuffling variants of WiOR-BO/WiOR-CBO consistently outperform independent-sampling baselines in both validation loss and F1 score, with the speedup in wall-clock time attributed to requiring only 3 backward passes per iteration versus 7 for independent sampling.

## Baselines & Comparisons

WiOR-BO strictly improves over StocBiO [25] and SOBA [9] (both O(ε⁻⁴)) by achieving O(ε⁻³) with permutation-based sampling, and over BSA [17] which requires O(ε⁻⁶) inner gradient evaluations. Against TTSA [22] (O(ε⁻⁵)), WiOR-BO also wins with its O(ε⁻³) rate. For conditional bilevel optimization, WiOR-CBO improves over DL-SGD [23] (O(ε⁻⁶) inner gradients) to O(ε⁻⁴), matching the state-of-the-art RT-MLMC [23] when using a herding-based permutation (q=0). In the Hyper-Data Cleaning experiment on MNIST, WiOR-BO-SO and WiOR-BO-RR outperform all baselines including reverse [16], AID-CG [18], MRBO/VRBO [52], and the independent-sampling variant WiR-BO, with the wall-clock speedup coming from reduced backward passes (3 vs. 7 per iteration). In Hyper-Representation Learning on Omniglot and MiniImageNet, WiOR-CBO outperforms DL-SGD and is comparable to RT-MLMC across 5-way-1-shot, 5-way-5-shot, 20-way-1-shot, and 20-way-5-shot settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously separate research threads: (1) single-loop bilevel optimization algorithms (stocBiO, SOBA, BSA, DL-SGD) that all require independent sampling of examples to estimate the hyper-gradient, and (2) without-replacement sampling strategies (random-reshuffling, shuffle-once, herding-based permutations) that were shown to give O(K⁻²) gradient error contraction in single-level finite-sum optimization. The key conceptual departure is demonstrating that the independent-sampling assumption, which was treated as necessary in all prior bilevel methods due to the second-order structure of the hyper-gradient, is in fact not required. The paper also extends the framework to conditional bilevel optimization (building on Hu et al. 2023) and instantiates it for minimax and compositional optimization as special cases.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is concrete: without-replacement sampling reduces the number of backward passes per iteration from 7 to 3, which is significant for large-scale models where backpropagation dominates cost. The theoretical improvement (O(ε⁻³) vs O(ε⁻⁴) for bilevel, O(ε⁻⁴) vs O(ε⁻⁶) for conditional bilevel) is clean and matches or exceeds variance-reduction methods (MRBO, RT-MLMC) with simpler implementation. Open questions include whether the (max(m,n))^q data-size dependence can be eliminated in practice, whether the results extend to non-strongly-convex inner problems, and how the approach scales to very large datasets where generating and storing permutations may become non-trivial. The herding-based permutation (q=0) that achieves the best rate is not experimentally validated in the bilevel setting.
