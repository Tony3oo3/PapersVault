## Problem

Training semantic segmentation models on multiple datasets is hindered by conflicting label spaces: the same object may be annotated at different granularities or under different names across datasets (e.g., 'road' in BDD vs. 'road/lane marking/crosswalk' in Mapillary; 'barrier' and 'curb' as separate classes in Mapillary vs. a single 'curb' in IDD). Existing solutions require manual re-annotation, hand-built universal taxonomies, or iterative pairwise label-relation discovery, all of which are labor-intensive and do not scale to many datasets simultaneously.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images (resized and cropped to 768×768) from up to seven datasets and text label descriptions encoded by llama-2-7B into 4096-dimensional features augmented with learnable dataset embeddings. It produces a segmentation mask in a learned unified label space of 217 nodes (fewer than the 448 total dataset classes), which can be projected to any individual dataset's label space via learned boolean mappings, enabling both unified and dataset-specific predictions from a single model.

## Method

A three-layer GraphSAGE GNN operates on a bipartite graph of dataset-specific label nodes and unified label nodes, with a learnable adjacency matrix (softmax-normalized per dataset) encoding continuous label mappings. The segmentation backbone (HRNet-W48) produces pixel embeddings that are projected into the unified label space via a UniSegHead (matrix product with unified node features), then mapped to dataset-specific logits through the GNN-derived label mappings for supervised training. Training alternates between GNN updates (using the continuous adjacency directly) and SegNet updates (using discrete mappings solved via unbalanced optimal transport with a greedy correction step), preceded by a Multi-SegHead warmup stage. An orthogonality loss on unified node features prevents redundant label nodes, and inactive unified nodes are pruned after training to yield the final compact label space.

## Results

On the seven-dataset multi-dataset training benchmark (CityScapes, Mapillary, SUN RGB-D, BDD100K, IDD, ADE20K, COCO), the proposed method achieves a mean mIoU of 56.4%, outperforming the best prior multi-dataset method MSeg (55.9%) and the strongest manual-taxonomy method Uni NLL+ (51.3%). On the WildDash 2 benchmark, the method achieves state-of-the-art Meta Avg mIoU Class of 50.0% in the trained setting and 40.5% zero-shot, with particularly strong performance on negative test cases (44.6% vs. 40.8% for the next best trained method). The method also demonstrates superior generalization to unseen datasets (KITTI, CamVid, ScanNet-20, PASCAL VOC, PASCAL Context), achieving a mean mIoU of 56.9% across five unseen test sets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all ten baselines across both evaluation settings. On the seven-dataset training benchmark, it surpasses MSeg [23] (55.9% mean mIoU) by 0.5 points, Uni NLL+ [5] (51.3%) by 5.1 points, NLL+ [4] (47.5%) by 8.9 points, and the automatic taxonomy method Auto univ. [6] (47.4%) by 9.0 points, with the largest gains on datasets with many classes (ADE20K: +7.4 over Uni NLL+, COCO: +7.4 over Uni NLL+). On the WildDash 2 benchmark in the trained setting, the method achieves 50.0% Meta Avg mIoU Class, beating MIX6D [26] (48.5%), FAN [45] (47.5%), Uni NLL+ [5] (46.9%), NLL+ [4] (46.8%), and SNpRN152 [3] (45.4%), with the most notable margin on negative test cases (44.6% vs. 40.8% for MIX6D). In the zero-shot setting, it leads UniSeg [19] (39.4%), SeamSeg [34] (37.9%), MSeg [23] (35.2%), and EffPS [31] (32.2%) on Meta Avg mIoU Class, and achieves a substantially higher negative mIoU (43.4% vs. 34.8% for UniSeg), indicating superior robustness to out-of-distribution driving scenarios.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the multi-dataset semantic segmentation line of work (MSeg, NLL+, Uni NLL+) by replacing manual relabeling or hand-constructed taxonomies with an automated GNN-based approach. It builds directly on GraphSAGE for the graph architecture and repurposes the bipartite graph matching / link prediction formulation to learn label mappings between dataset-specific and unified label spaces. The key departure from prior automatic taxonomy methods (Bevandić & Šegvić, Uijlings et al.) is that those operate pairwise or require iterative training, whereas this paper constructs the unified space across all datasets in a single training session using a learnable adjacency matrix with softmax constraints and an optimal-transport-based discretization step.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method removes the most labor-intensive bottleneck in multi-dataset segmentation training—manual taxonomy reconciliation—while achieving SOTA on the WildDash 2 benchmark and strong generalization to unseen datasets. However, it still requires fully annotated training data, relies on ChatGPT-generated label descriptions (which the authors acknowledge can be inaccurate), and the authors themselves flag safety risks for autonomous driving applications due to potential errors in the fully automated label space. Open questions include whether the approach scales to very large numbers of datasets, whether the GNN's contribution is truly necessary (the ablation shows modest gains over a non-GNN baseline), and how to integrate weakly-supervised or unsupervised signals to reduce annotation dependency.
