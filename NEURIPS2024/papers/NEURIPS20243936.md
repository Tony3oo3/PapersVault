## Problem

Existing diversity measures for latent representations—such as average similarity, the Vendi Score, and geometric mean of standard deviations—fail fundamental axiomatic requirements (twin property, monotonicity in observations, effective size) and provide only single-scale snapshots of diversity. Reference-based metrics like precision, recall, and coverage rely on fixed-scale manifold approximations that are sensitive to hyperparameter choices and conflate fidelity with diversity. The paper addresses the need for a theoretically grounded, multi-scale, parameter-free measure that captures the intrinsic geometry of latent spaces across all relevant resolutions.

## Input / Output

The method takes a finite set of n points in a shared latent space (e.g., sentence-transformer embeddings, Inception V3 image features, or GIN graph embeddings) together with a chosen distance or semi-metric (L1, L2, or cosine). It produces a magnitude function Mag_X(t) mapping scale t to the effective number of distinct points, from which two scalar summaries are derived: MAGAREA (the integral of the magnitude function over an automatically determined interval [0, t_cut]) for reference-free diversity, and MAGDIFF (the integral of the difference between two magnitude functions) for reference-based comparison. The evaluation interval is set by the convergence scale t_conv where magnitude reaches |X|−ε, found via TOMS 748 root-finding, eliminating manual parameter selection.

## Method

The core mechanism computes the similarity matrix ζ_X(i,j) = exp(−d(x_i, x_j)) for a finite metric space and defines magnitude as the sum of all entries of ζ_X^{-1}, interpreted as the effective number of points at a given scale. Scaling distances by a factor t yields the magnitude function Mag_X(t), which traces how the effective size of the space evolves from 1 (all points indistinguishable) to n (all points distinct). MAGAREA integrates this function over [0, t_cut] to produce a single multi-scale diversity score, while MAGDIFF integrates the pointwise difference between two magnitude functions to quantify relative diversity. Computation uses Cholesky decomposition of the positive-definite similarity matrix for numerical stability and O(n³) efficiency, and the method is provably stable under perturbations of the distance matrix, isometry-invariant, and satisfies the twin property and monotonicity axioms that existing baselines violate.

## Results

On curvature estimation, MAGAREA with piecewise linear regression achieves an MSE of 0.05±0.03, substantially outperforming the best persistent homology baseline (SVR with all PH features, MSE 0.16±0.03) and deep MLP baselines (MSE 1.15–1.56). For text diversity prediction, MAGAREA achieves a median rank of 1 across 3 tasks × 5 embedding models, outperforming AVGSIM by 0.12 mean R² on story and 0.07 on resp/prompt. In embedding model classification (Table 2), MAGDIFF reaches 90–99% accuracy across four datasets, compared to 63–92% for the next-best baseline (AVGSIM). In graph mode collapse/dropping detection, MAGDIFF achieves the highest Spearman correlation with the degree of perturbation across all five graph datasets, outperforming recall and coverage which only catch up near p≈0.9.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MAGAREA/MAGDIFF consistently outperform all four embedding-based baselines (Vendi Score, AVGSIM, GMSTDS) across every experiment. In text diversity prediction, MAGAREA beats VS (the closest competitor) and AVGSIM by 0.07–0.12 mean R², while GMSTDS produces negative R² for 3 of 5 embedding models due to its failure on constant dimensions. In embedding model classification, MAGDIFF achieves 90–99% accuracy versus 63–92% for AVGSIM, 63–84% for VS, and a flat 66% for GMSTDS, with the gap widening on smaller models (e.g., 0.94 vs. 0.63 on CNN). Against the reference-based metrics recall and coverage [28], MAGDIFF correctly detects diversity decrease in both sequential and simultaneous image mode dropping, whereas coverage only registers simultaneous dropping after ~70% of points shift and both metrics misestimate the rate of diversity loss. In curvature estimation, MAGAREA (MSE 0.05) outperforms the best persistent homology method from Turkes et al. [41] (SVR, all PH features, MSE 0.16) by 3×, and dramatically outperforms MLP baselines (MSE 1.15–1.56). MMD [15] is listed as a classical graph metric but is not the primary comparison; the key graph baselines are recall and coverage, which MAGDIFF surpasses in Spearman correlation across all perturbation levels.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes metric space magnitude, a mathematical invariant from Leinster's work on the 'effective size' of metric spaces, as a multi-scale diversity measure for latent representations. It departs from existing embedding-based diversity metrics (Vendi Score, average similarity, geometric mean of standard deviations) by arguing they fail fundamental axioms of diversity (twin property, monotonicity in observations, effective size) and provide only single-scale snapshots. Prior ML applications of magnitude (boundary detection, edge detection, generalisation error) treated it as a single-scale tool; this work is the first to exploit its full multi-scale nature and to formalise a notion of difference between magnitude functions of two spaces (MAGDIFF).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a parameter-free, theoretically grounded diversity metric that satisfies axioms existing measures violate, with practical efficiency gains from Cholesky-based computation and automated scale selection. Key limitations include the inability to measure fidelity (only diversity), scaling constraints to hundreds of thousands of observations, and reliance on the quality of the underlying embedding. Open questions include extending magnitude to unaligned spaces with incompatible distance notions and integrating it as a regularisation signal in deep learning training, which the authors identify as promising future directions.
