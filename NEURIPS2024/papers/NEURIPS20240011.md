## Problem

CoT decoding is efficient but frequently produces suboptimal reasoning paths, while ToT improves quality through tree search at the cost of over 50× inference latency. Previous distillation approaches such as TS-SFT only use the final best path from ToT for supervised fine-tuning, discarding the rich per-step preference information naturally generated during the search process. The paper asks whether this discarded preference signal can be fully exploited to make CoT match or exceed ToT's reasoning quality without the inference overhead.

## Input / Output

The model takes a text question augmented with 4-shot CoT demonstrations as input and produces a sequential chain of intermediate reasoning thoughts (formatted as 'Step 1,' 'Step 2,' etc.) followed by a final answer. During training, the method consumes paired preference thoughts at each reasoning step, where the preferred thought is the one retained in ToT's selected path and the dispreferred thought is a sibling node pruned from the same parent state. At inference, the fine-tuned model uses standard single-path CoT decoding with no tree search, maintaining the same latency as the base CoT model.

## Method

CPO synthesizes per-step preference data by running ToT's BFS tree search: at each reasoning step, k candidate thoughts are generated and self-evaluated by the LLM, and the n-best are retained for further search. Thoughts appearing in the final selected path are labeled as preferred (winning), while sibling thoughts from the same parent state that were pruned are labeled as dispreferred (losing), creating paired preference data at every step of the reasoning chain. The model is then trained with the DPO objective applied to these per-step pairs, which is critical because applying DPO to full reasoning paths would cause longest-common-prefix (LCP) gradient cancellation, where shared prefix tokens' gradients cancel out and only the diverging token receives a gradient signal. By operating at the per-step level, CPO ensures every token in the reasoning chain receives meaningful gradient updates. The entire pipeline is self-supervised—no ground-truth labels or external reward models are required—and the computational cost of tree search is confined to the training phase, leaving inference at standard CoT speed.

## Results

CPO achieves an average accuracy improvement of 4.3% (max 9.7%) over the CoT baseline across seven reasoning datasets and three LLMs (LLaMA2-7B, LLaMA2-13B, Mistral-7B). On average, CPO matches or exceeds ToT performance (e.g., 40.9% vs. 39.1% for LLaMA2-7B; 49.1% vs. 47.8% for Mistral-7B) while maintaining CoT-level inference latency (~38–56 s/instance vs. ~1,749–4,229 s/instance for ToT, a 57.5× speedup). CPO also surpasses the TS-SFT baseline by an average of 2.7% (max 10.3%), with statistically significant gains (p < 0.01) on most individual datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against **CoT** (Wei et al., 2022), CPO improves average accuracy by 4.3% across all models and tasks (e.g., 40.9% vs. 36.0% for LLaMA2-7B; 49.1% vs. 45.0% for Mistral-7B), confirming that per-step preference supervision meaningfully enhances single-path reasoning. Against **ToT** (Yao et al., 2024), CPO achieves comparable or superior accuracy (40.9% vs. 39.1% for LLaMA2-7B; 49.1% vs. 47.8% for Mistral-7B) while reducing inference latency by ~57.5×, effectively distilling ToT's deliberative search into efficient CoT decoding. Against **TS-SFT** (Feng et al., 2023), which fine-tunes on only the selected ToT paths, CPO wins by an average of 2.7% (max 10.3%), with statistically significant gains (p < 0.01) on 15 out of 21 individual dataset-model combinations, demonstrating the value of leveraging dispreferred thoughts. In the appendix comparison (Table 4), CPO also outperforms **ReST** (Gulcehre et al., 2023) and **Self-Rewarding** (Yuan et al., 2024) under a fair comparison setting where both are adapted to use the LLM itself as reward model/annotator, with CPO achieving 40.9% vs. 37.2% (ReST) and 37.5% (Self-R) average for LLaMA2-7B, and 49.1% vs. 45.4% (ReST) and 45.9% (Self-R) for Mistral-7B.

## Relation to Prior Work

CPO sits at the intersection of two well-developed lines of work: tree-of-thought (ToT) search for deliberative reasoning and direct preference optimization (DPO) for alignment. It departs from prior tree-search-to-fine-tuning methods (TS-SFT, MCTS-based approaches) that only supervise on the single best-discovered path, by instead exploiting the per-step preference structure inherent in the search tree—treating pruned sibling thoughts as dispreferred counterparts to selected thoughts. The key conceptual departure from full-path DPO is the identification of the longest-common-prefix (LCP) gradient cancellation problem, which motivates constructing preference pairs at each reasoning step rather than at the complete-path level.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CPO offers a practical recipe for distilling the deliberative quality of ToT into a single-pass CoT model without human annotation, achieving comparable accuracy at ~57× lower inference latency. The LCP gradient cancellation analysis provides a general warning for any DPO-style training where paired sequences share long prefixes, a concern that extends beyond reasoning to other structured generation tasks. Open questions include whether the approach transfers to vision-language models, whether weaker evaluators can supervise stronger generators (weak-to-strong alignment), and whether the time cost of ToT-based data generation can be reduced via speculative decoding or non-autoregressive generation.
