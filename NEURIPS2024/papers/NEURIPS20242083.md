## Problem

Proximal samplers based on Gaussian oracles achieve high-accuracy (polylog(1/ε)) guarantees for light-tailed targets, but it was unclear whether they can do the same for heavy-tailed densities. This paper establishes that Gaussian-based proximal samplers face a fundamental barrier, requiring poly(1/ε) iterations for heavy-tailed targets, and asks whether high-accuracy heavy-tailed sampling is achievable at all.

## Input / Output

The sampler takes a target potential V (satisfying a fractional Poincaré inequality or Hölder-type growth condition), an initial point x₀, a step size η, and a stability parameter α ∈ (0,2). For the practical α=1 implementation (Algorithm 3), it additionally requires a global minimizer x* of V and operates as a zeroth-order method using only function evaluations of V. The output is a sample x_N that is ε-close to the target in χ²-divergence, with the key distinction that the stable oracle achieves O(log(1/ε)) iterations while the Gaussian oracle requires Ω(ε^{-2/ν}) iterations for Cauchy targets with ν degrees of freedom.

## Method

The Stable Proximal Sampler (Algorithm 2) replaces the Gaussian heat-flow step with an isotropic α-stable Lévy process, performing Gibbs sampling on a joint density π(x,y) ∝ π^X(x) p^{(α)}(η; x, y). Each iteration consists of (i) sampling y_k from the α-stable transition density centered at x_k (an exact fractional heat flow step), and (ii) sampling x_{k+1} from the Restricted α-Stable Oracle (RαSO), which draws from π^X(·) p^{(α)}(η; ·, y_k). Convergence is proven via χ²-divergence analysis under the fractional Poincaré inequality, showing exponential decay at rate exp(-η/(C_FPI(α)+η)) per iteration. For practical implementation at α=1, the RαSO is realized via rejection sampling with Cauchy proposals generated as ratios of Gaussian random vectors, yielding O(log(1/ε)) complexity when ν ≥ 1 and poly(1/ε) when ν ∈ (0,1). Lower bounds built on Hairer's technique confirm that Gaussian oracles cannot beat poly(1/ε) for this target class, establishing a clear separation.

## Results

For generalized Cauchy targets π_ν ∝ (1+|x|²)^(-(d+ν)/2), the Gaussian proximal sampler (Algorithm 1) requires Ω̃(ε^(-1/ν)) iterations to achieve ε-accuracy in χ²-divergence for all ν > 0, establishing a fundamental poly(1/ε) barrier. In contrast, the stable proximal sampler (Algorithm 2) with α ≤ ν achieves O(log(1/ε)) iterations for all ν > 0, and the practical rejection-sampling implementation (Algorithm 3, α=1) achieves O(log(1/ε)) when ν ≥ 1 and Õ(ε^(-1/ν+1)) when ν ∈ (0,1). Lower bounds (Theorem 4, Corollary 7) confirm that the poly(1/ε) rate for the implementable stable sampler when ν ∈ (0,1) is essentially tight, matching the upper bound up to polylog factors.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's sole baseline is the Gaussian Proximal Sampler (Algorithm 1) from Lee, Shen, and Tian (COLT 2021), which uses a Restricted Gaussian Oracle (RGO) and is the state-of-the-art proximal method for log-concave and light-tailed sampling. The stable proximal sampler strictly dominates the Gaussian sampler in all regimes: for ν ≥ 1, the stable sampler achieves O(log(1/ε)) iterations versus Ω̃(ε^(-1/ν)) for the Gaussian sampler, a separation from logarithmic to polynomial in 1/ε. For ν ∈ (0,1), where the practical stable sampler (α=1) degrades to Õ(ε^(-1/ν+1)), it still improves upon the Gaussian sampler's Ω̃(ε^(-1/ν)) by a factor of ε. The lower bound in Corollary 7 (N ≥ Ω̃_ν(d^((ν+8)/(3ν)) ε^(-2(1-ν)/(3ν)))) confirms that the poly(1/ε) complexity of the implementable stable sampler for ν ∈ (0,1) cannot be fundamentally improved, closing the gap between upper and lower bounds up to certain factors.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the proximal sampler framework of Lee, Shen, and Tian (COLT 2021), which introduced the Restricted Gaussian Oracle (RGO) for log-concave sampling, by replacing the Gaussian oracle with a Restricted α-Stable Oracle (RαSO) built on isotropic stable Lévy processes and the fractional heat flow. The upper-bound analysis builds directly on the χ²-divergence framework of Chen, Chewi, Salim, and Wibisono (COLT 2022), recovering their results as the α→2⁻ limit, while the lower-bound technique adapts Hairer's (2010) moment-growth argument to both continuous-time Langevin diffusion and discrete-time proximal iterates. Prior heavy-tailed sampling approaches (transformation-based methods, discretized Itô diffusions) achieved only poly(1/ε) guarantees; this paper is the first to establish a fundamental oracle-dependent separation showing that the Gaussian oracle is inherently limited while the stable oracle overcomes this barrier.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The clean separation between Gaussian and stable oracles for heavy-tailed sampling resolves a key open question about whether high-accuracy (log(1/ε)) guarantees are achievable in the heavy-tailed regime, and identifies the stable oracle as the correct tool. The matching lower bounds for the stable proximal sampler (Corollary 7) confirm the analysis is tight, ruling out the possibility of further fundamental improvements. Important open questions remain: efficiently implementable RαSO for all α∈(0,2) (only α=1 is handled via rejection sampling), a variational/gradient-flow interpretation of the stable proximal sampler, and whether non-standard Itô diffusions with weighted Poincaré inequalities yield competitive rates. The practical implementation requires a global minimizer of the potential and has expected per-iteration cost that can be large, limiting immediate applicability.
