## Problem

Prior theoretical analysis of single-layer GAN training dynamics (Wang et al., 2018) was restricted to single-feature discriminators, which enforce sequential learning of features one at a time. This paper asks what changes when the discriminator has the same dimensionality as the generator, enabling simultaneous (non-sequential) learning of all features, and how inter-feature interactions affect convergence speed, steady-state performance, and the quality of the learned subspace basis.

## Input / Output

The model consumes high-dimensional data samples y_k ∈ R^n drawn from a spiked covariance model (true orthonormal basis U ∈ R^{n×d} plus Gaussian noise) and random latent vectors c̃_k for the generator. For real datasets, MNIST images are flattened to 784-dimensional vectors and Olivetti Faces to 4096-dimensional vectors. The output is a learned orthonormal generator basis V ∈ R^{n×d} (extracted via SVD of the generator weights) that can produce new samples from the data distribution, with performance quantified by Grassmann distance and cosine similarity to the true subspace.

## Method

The core contribution is a rigorous scaling-limit analysis (n→∞) of a single-layer GAN where the discriminator W ∈ R^{n×d} matches the generator's dimensionality, yielding a system of coupled ODEs governing the macroscopic state M = [U,V,W]^T[U,V,W] (a 3d×3d matrix of pairwise Gram matrices). The key finding is that off-diagonal entries in the macroscopic state encode inter-feature interactions that accelerate training and raise the steady-state cosine similarity compared to the sequential single-feature case, particularly under near-zero initialization. An 'uplifting' construction on Grassmannians extends the analysis to the general case where the true subspace, generator, and discriminator have different dimensionalities (d ≤ q ≤ p ≪ n). Training follows standard SGD on a minimax loss with log-cosh regularization that enforces orthonormality in the λ→∞ limit. The paper further positions this GAN as a third 'generative' category of online subspace learning algorithm, distinct from algebraic methods (Oja's) and geometric methods (GROUSE), and shows empirically that the GAN's requirement to generate new samples acts as an implicit regularization yielding more interpretable basis vectors.

## Results

On MNIST (d=36, n=784), the multi-feature GAN achieves a Grassmann distance of 2.46 after just 1 epoch, while the single-feature GAN from Wang et al. reaches only 3.17 after 5 epochs (5× more data). The multi-feature discriminator successfully learns all 36 basis elements in a single epoch, whereas the sequential discriminator fails to learn even half of them in 5 epochs. On Olivetti Faces (d=16), the multi-feature GAN eventually outperforms Oja's method in Grassmann distance, though Oja initially converges faster; the GAN also produces more visually meaningful and diverse learned features.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the single-feature GAN of Wang et al. [6], the proposed multi-feature discriminator wins decisively on every metric: it achieves a lower Grassmann distance (2.46 vs. 3.17) while seeing 5× less data on MNIST, learns all 36 features in 1 epoch versus fewer than 18 in 5 epochs for the sequential model, and the ODE analysis shows the gap widens as initialization approaches zero. Against Oja's method [1], the comparison is more nuanced: Oja converges faster in early training (visible in the Olivetti Faces Grassmann distance curve), but the GAN ultimately reaches a lower steady-state distance and, critically, produces learned features that are far more interpretable and visually representative of the data (e.g., recognizable digit shapes on MNIST), which Oja's features largely lack. The paper attributes this advantage to the GAN's generative objective acting as an implicit regularization on the learned basis.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the scaling-limit analysis of single-layer GANs initiated by Wang et al. (NeurIPS 2018), which characterized training dynamics for a single-feature (sequential) discriminator. The key conceptual departure is replacing the single-feature discriminator with a multi-feature one of equal dimensionality to the generator, enabling non-sequential learning of all features simultaneously. The authors also position GANs as a third category of online subspace learning algorithm—'generative methods'—alongside algebraic methods (Oja's) and geometric methods (GROUSE), and introduce a Grassmannian uplifting trick (inspired by Ye and Lim) to handle mismatched dimensionalities between the true subspace, generator, and discriminator.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a multi-feature discriminator of equal strength to the generator outperforms the standard weaker-discriminator approach challenges a common heuristic in GAN training and suggests that careful learning-rate tuning can make powerful discriminators beneficial rather than harmful. The observation that GANs acquire a more informative basis than Oja's method—attributed to the generative constraint—offers a principled reason to prefer GAN-based subspace learning when interpretability of learned features matters. However, the analysis is confined to single-layer, linear GANs with a specific loss function and orthonormality assumptions on the discriminator, and whether these dynamics persist in multi-layer or nonlinear architectures remains an open question that the authors explicitly flag as future work.
