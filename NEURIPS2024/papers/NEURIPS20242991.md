## Problem

Existing medical multimodal benchmarks are typically built on a single imaging modality or task, sourced from academic literature rather than real clinical data, and lack the categorization needed for customized evaluation across departments and perceptual granularities. This limits their ability to assess whether Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) can actually meet the diverse, multi-faceted demands of real-world clinical practice, and leaves interactive LVLMs without a structured evaluation framework spanning image, box, mask, and contour levels.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242991/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each benchmark item consists of a 2D RGB medical image (standardized from 38 source modalities via the SA-Med2D-20M protocol) optionally overlaid with a bounding box, mask, or contour annotation, paired with a text question that explicitly encodes the modality, clinical task hint, and perceptual granularity level. The model must select from 2–4 multiple-choice options (single- or multi-select), and performance is scored via macro-averaged accuracy for single-choice and hit-rate/recall for multi-choice questions. Questions are templated so that the modality is a placeholder replaced at generation time, ensuring no answer can be inferred without viewing the image.

## Method

GMAI-MMBench is constructed through a three-stage pipeline: (1) collecting and standardizing 284 datasets from public repositories and partner hospitals, converting all 2D/3D images to 2D RGB and unifying labels via MeSH terminology to eliminate ambiguity; (2) building a lexical tree that tags every case along four axes—18 clinical VQA tasks, 18 departments, 38 modalities, and 4 perceptual granularities—enabling users to filter and evaluate any subset; (3) generating QA pairs by prompting GPT-4o with modality-task-granularity templates, sourcing distractor options from same-dataset categories (global view) or shared cross-dataset pools (local view), then applying manual validation and diversity-based selection to yield 25,831 final items. A key design choice is using image-label pairs rather than image-text pairs as the data foundation, which minimizes data leakage risk since labels are not directly convertible to LVLM training samples. The benchmark is evaluated in a zero-shot setting across 50 LVLMs using the VLMEvalKit framework.

## Results

On GMAI-MMBench (25,831 QA pairs across 38 modalities, 18 clinical tasks, 18 departments, and 4 perceptual granularities), the best proprietary model GPT-4o achieves 53.96% macro-averaged accuracy on the test set, while Gemini 1.5 reaches 48.36% and GPT-4V reaches 44.08%. Among open-source models, MedDr (43.69%) and DeepSeek-VL-7B (43.43%) are the strongest, surpassing Claude3-Opus (32.44%) and approaching GPT-4V. Medical-specific models (Med-Flamingo 11.64%, LLaVA-Med 19.60%, RadFM 22.93%) generally underperform general-purpose LVLMs, with only MedDr reaching competitive levels. The random baseline sits at 25.94%, and box-level perceptual granularity consistently yields the worst accuracy across all models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GPT-4o leads all 50 evaluated models with 53.96% overall accuracy, outperforming Gemini 1.5 (48.36%) by ~5.6 points and GPT-4V (44.08%) by ~9.9 points. Among open-source models, MedDr (43.69%) and DeepSeek-VL-7B (43.43%) are the strongest, both surpassing Claude3-Opus (32.44%) by over 11 points and Qwen-VL-Max (42.16%) by ~1.5 points, while remaining within ~10 points of GPT-4o. Medical-specific models (Med-Flamingo 11.64%, LLaVA-Med 19.60%, Qilin-Med-VL 22.06%, RadFM 22.93%) all fall well below the random baseline on several tasks and significantly underperform general-purpose LVLMs, with only MedDr (43.69%) reaching competitive levels by leveraging high-quality medical image classification data for instruction tuning. Notably, GPT-4o excels on Disease Diagnosis (61.45%) and Nervous Tissue (75.25%) but remains weak on Severity Grading (33.93%) and Attribute Recognition (38.32%), where even the best models barely exceed the random baseline. Box-level perceptual granularity produces the worst accuracy across all models, indicating a systematic weakness in local region understanding that no current LVLM has resolved.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by repurposing 284 existing medical image datasets (originally for classification, detection, and segmentation) into a unified VQA benchmark, rather than collecting new image-text pairs as prior medical VQA benchmarks (PathVQA, VQA-RAD, SLAKE, OmniMedVQA) do. It departs from prior work in three conceptual ways: (1) using image-label pairs to minimize data leakage risk, (2) introducing a lexical tree structure (18 tasks × 18 departments × 38 modalities) for customized evaluation, and (3) adding multi-perceptual granularity (image, box, mask, contour) as an explicit evaluation axis. The benchmark construction pipeline draws on MeSH terminology for label standardization and GPT-4o for question generation, but the core contribution is the organizational framework rather than a new modeling technique.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GMAI-MMBench is likely to become a standard evaluation tool for medical LVLMs given its breadth (38 modalities, 284 datasets, 26K questions) and the lexical tree's ability to support department-specific or task-specific evaluation. The finding that GPT-4o achieves only ~54% accuracy underscores a substantial gap between current LVLMs and clinical readiness, while the counterintuitive result that box-level annotations perform worse than no annotation at all highlights a fundamental weakness in how LVLMs process spatial cues. Key open questions include whether the benchmark's manual curation introduces systematic biases, whether human expert baselines will narrow or widen the observed gaps, and whether the perceptual granularity findings generalize to interactive medical AI systems. The paper also notes that medical-specific models largely underperform general-purpose models, raising questions about the value of domain-specific fine-tuning without high-quality instruction data.
