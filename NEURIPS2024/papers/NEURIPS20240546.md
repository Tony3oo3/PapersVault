## Problem

While text-to-text alignment is well-supported by extensive human preference datasets, the text-to-video generation domain lacks comparable resources for studying and validating human value alignment. SAFESORA addresses this gap by introducing the first large-scale human preference dataset for text-to-video tasks, capturing preferences along both helpfulness and harmlessness dimensions evaluated on the joint text-video pair rather than video content alone.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240546/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The dataset comprises 14,711 unique text prompts (44.54% from real users, 55.46% researcher-constructed) paired with 57,333 videos generated by five models (Pika, FullJourney, Mootion, VideoCrafter2, Hotshot-XL), forming T-V pairs as the fundamental annotation unit. Each of the 51,691 preference annotations is a paired comparison between two T-V pairs sharing the same prompt, yielding 12 multi-label harm category assignments per video, four helpfulness sub-dimension preferences (instruction following, correctness, informativeness, aesthetics), and decoupled overall helpfulness and harmlessness judgments. The T-V pair framing is critical: a video may appear harmless in isolation but harmful in the context of its generating prompt.

## Method

The annotation process uses a two-stage heuristic design that decouples helpfulness and harmlessness into parallel tracks to mitigate the well-known tension between these dimensions. In the first stage, crowdworkers annotate four helpfulness sub-dimensions and perform multi-label classification across 12 harm categories; in the second stage, they provide overall preference judgments for each dimension independently. The paper validates the dataset through three baseline applications: a T-V Moderation model fine-tuned from Video-LLaVA with a binary classification head (82.94% agreement with human judgment), Bradley-Terry reward and cost models trained via log-likelihood loss on paired preferences (65.29% and 72.41% agreement), and Best-of-N fine-tuning of both a prompt refiner and the diffusion model itself, where generated outputs are ranked by a weighted sum of reward and cost scores before serving as supervisory signals.

## Results

The SAFESORA dataset (14,711 prompts, 57,333 T-V pairs, 51,691 preference annotations) is validated through three baseline experiments. The T-V Moderation model (fine-tuned from Video-LLaVA) achieves 82.94% agreement with human judgment on the test set. The Bradley-Terry-based reward model (helpfulness) and cost model (harmlessness) achieve 65.29% and 72.41% agreement with crowdworkers, respectively. Analysis reveals that 53.39% of helpfulness preferences on potentially harmful prompts contradict harmlessness preferences, confirming the helpfulness-harmlessness tension in text-to-video generation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares GPT-4o (using extracted video frames) against human crowdworker annotations to assess whether multi-modal LLMs can replace human annotators. GPT-4o shows low agreement with humans on preference labeling tasks: sub-dimension agreement ranges from ~0.52 (Correctness) to ~0.72 (Instruction Following with 4 frames), and overall harmlessness preference agreement is only ~0.59–0.65. As the number of comparison frames increases, agreement tends toward random (0.5), and timeline-dependent dimensions like Informativeness show the lowest agreement. In contrast, GPT-4o achieves high agreement with crowdworkers on the 12 harm-label classification task, likely because most harm judgments are resolvable from a single frame. The authors conclude that general multi-modal LLMs currently cannot reliably replace human annotators for preference labeling in text-to-video tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper transplants the RLHF/preference-learning paradigm well established in LLM alignment (InstructGPT, Llama 2, BeaverTails) into the text-to-video domain, where no comparable human preference dataset existed. It generalizes the 3H (Helpful, Harmless, Honest) framework to video generation and departs from prior video content moderation approaches (VidHarm, EvalCrafter) by evaluating the joint text-video pair rather than the video in isolation. The alignment baselines draw directly on the Bradley-Terry preference model and the Best-of-N fine-tuning procedure from the LLM literature, while the moderation component builds on Video-LLaVA as a multi-modal backbone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SAFESORA fills a critical gap as the first large-scale human preference dataset for text-to-video safety alignment, providing the empirical foundation the field has lacked since Sora demonstrated production-quality video generation. The 53.39% helpfulness-harmlessness contradiction rate and the 'Hard to Reject' observation—that video models fundamentally cannot refuse like text models—highlight open problems with no obvious solutions. The finding that GPT-4o's agreement with human annotators degrades toward chance as more frames are supplied challenges the assumption that simply feeding more visual context to multi-modal LLMs will close the annotation gap. Future work must address how to efficiently leverage this data for alignment and how to define meaningful refusal behavior for generative video models.
