## Problem

Understanding when SGD can learn functions that depend only on the projection of a high-dimensional Gaussian input onto an unknown low-dimensional subspace has been limited to basis-dependent settings (e.g., the merged-staircase property for polynomials on hypercubes). This paper addresses the gap by establishing basis-free, rotation-invariant necessary and sufficient conditions for SGD-learnability of general subspace-sparse polynomials under Gaussian input, where no orthonormal basis of the subspace is specified a priori.

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard Gaussian vector x ~ N(0, I_d) and a target function f*(x) = h*(x_V) where x_V is the orthogonal projection onto an unknown p-dimensional subspace V (p << d), along with a chosen activation function σ. The output is the mean-field parameter distribution ρ_t over (a, w) pairs and the associated loss E(ρ_t). Crucially, the subspace V is not known to the learner, and all conditions and rates must be invariant under rotations of the ambient space, making the analysis fundamentally different from basis-dependent staircase conditions.

## Method

The paper introduces a 'reflective property' of the target polynomial h* with respect to a subspace S, which couples the target's structure with the activation function's expressiveness (via σ') to form a basis-free necessary condition: if h* satisfies this property for nontrivial S, the loss is bounded away from zero throughout training. For sufficiency, a slightly stronger condition requiring the Taylor expansion of a specific ODE flow to not be trapped in any proper subspace guarantees exponential loss decay. The training strategy (Algorithm 1) is two-stage: first, w is trained with fixed a for p independent runs and the results are averaged to achieve algebraic independence (needed because the space of general polynomials is infinite-dimensional, unlike the finite-dimensional hypercube case); second, a is trained with fixed w using a polynomial activation (1+ζ)^n matching the target degree. The non-degeneracy of the resulting kernel matrix is established via the Jacobian criterion for algebraic independence, yielding dimension-free convergence rates.

## Results

The paper establishes two main theoretical results for learning subspace-sparse polynomials f*(x) = h*(x_V) with Gaussian input x ~ N(0, I_d) using two-layer neural networks and SGD. Theorem 3.4 (necessary condition) shows that if the reflective property holds with respect to a nontrivial subspace S ⊂ V, then the loss is bounded away from zero: inf_{0≤t≤T} E(ρ_t) ≥ (1/2)E[|h*(z) − h*_{S⊥}(z_S⊥)|²] − C/d^{1/2}, meaning SGD cannot learn the target. Theorem 4.3 (sufficient condition) proves that if the Taylor expansion of the auxiliary flow ŵ_V(t) is not trapped in any proper subspace of V, then E(ρ_t) ≤ C₁ exp(−C₂ t) for all t ≥ 0, with constants C₁, C₂ independent of dimension d. Both results are dimension-free, and the implied sample complexity is O(d) samples.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no experimental baselines or empirical comparisons. The theoretical contributions are positioned relative to prior work as follows: the reflective property (Definition 3.3) is a basis-free generalization of the merged-staircase property from Abbe et al. [1, COLT 2022], which was defined only for polynomials on the hypercube {−1,1}^d with a fixed coordinate basis. When the activation function is sufficiently expressive, the reflective property recovers the isoLeap ≥ 2 condition from Abbe et al. [2, 2023], making it a slightly weaker (more general) necessary condition. The sufficient condition (Assumption 4.1) is slightly stronger than the negation of the necessary condition, with the gap arising from the use of Taylor expansion of the flow rather than the flow itself. The two-stage training strategy (Algorithm 1) is inspired by [1] but requires p independent training runs and averaging (rather than a single run) to achieve algebraic independence in the general polynomial setting, and uses a modified activation σ̂(ζ) = (1+ζ)^n to constrain the output to polynomials of degree at most n.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper generalizes the merged-staircase property of Abbe et al. [1] (COLT 2022) from sparse polynomials on the hypercube {±1}^d to general subspace-sparse polynomials on R^d with Gaussian input, where no natural coordinate basis exists. The key conceptual departure is introducing the 'reflective property' as a basis-free, rotation-invariant condition that also incorporates the expressiveness of the activation function, whereas prior conditions (merged-staircase, isoLeap, leap complexity) were defined purely on the target function. The mean-field dynamics framework follows Mei et al. [19] for dimension-free bounds connecting SGD to the continuous-time flow, and the two-stage training strategy is adapted from [1] but modified to use p independent runs and algebraic independence (rather than linear independence) to handle the infinite-dimensional space of general polynomials.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first basis-free necessary and (slightly stronger) sufficient conditions for SGD-learnability of subspace-sparse polynomials under Gaussian input, with all rates dimension-free. A notable limitation is the gap between the necessary condition (reflective property) and the sufficient condition (Taylor expansion of the flow not trapped in a proper subspace), which the authors acknowledge and identify as a key open question. The requirement to repeat the first-layer training p times and average parameters is an unusual training strategy that the authors conjecture can be removed with additional preknowledge of the polynomial degree. The results are purely theoretical (mean-field regime, one-pass SGD) and do not directly translate to practical training protocols, but they sharpen understanding of when feature learning succeeds or fails in the most common input distribution.
