## Problem

Mechanistic interpretability methods claim to identify circuits in neural networks, but validating these claims requires knowing the true circuit, which is unavailable for naturally trained models. Tracr provides transformers with known circuits but produces unrealistic architectures (mostly zero weights, no feature superposition, minimal activation usage), making them poor testbeds for tuning circuit discovery algorithms. The paper addresses this gap by creating semi-synthetic transformers that have known ground-truth circuits while exhibiting realistic weight and activation distributions comparable to supervised-learning-trained models.

## Input / Output

The training procedure takes a Tracr-generated transformer (serving as the high-level model with a known circuit) and a larger low-level transformer with 4 attention heads (vs. 1–2 in Tracr) and halved head dimensions, where high-level heads are mapped to randomly selected low-level heads in the same layer. Training uses 20k–120k randomly sampled input sequences with expected outputs generated by running the Tracr transformer, and the model is trained until it reaches 100% Interchange Intervention Accuracy and 100% Strict Interchange Intervention Accuracy on a 20% validation split. The output is a semi-synthetic transformer with a known ground-truth circuit, which can then be used to compute AUC ROC scores, node effect measurements, and normalized KL divergence values for any circuit discovery technique.

## Method

The core contribution is Strict Interchange Intervention Training (SIIT), an extension of IIT that adds a Strictness loss term. While IIT only applies interchange interventions on nodes aligned with the high-level circuit, SIIT additionally samples non-aligned low-level nodes and penalizes the model if intervening on them changes the output relative to the correct label, preventing non-circuit components from performing task-relevant computations. Training combines three losses—standard IIT loss (matching high- and low-level model behavior under interventions on aligned nodes), the new Strictness loss, and a behavior loss—with Adam at learning rate 0.001 and batch size 512. The benchmark comprises 86 models: 85 from Tracr circuits (26 self-generated RASP programs plus 59 from TracrBench) and one simplified IOI circuit from GPT-2 small, all trained on a single RTX A6000 GPU in 1–8 hours. Evaluation on 16 main models shows SIIT correctly isolates circuit nodes (near-zero effect for non-circuit nodes) where IIT fails, and that ACDC significantly outperforms Subnetwork Probing (p≈0.0004) while being statistically indistinguishable from EAP with integrated gradients.

## Results

On INTERPBENCH's 16 main models, SIIT-trained transformers achieve 100% Interchange Intervention Accuracy (IIA) and 100% Strict IIA, with non-circuit nodes showing near-zero effect (median 0.00 across all 16 tasks) while circuit nodes show high effect (medians 0.13–1.00). In contrast, IIT-trained models exhibit significant non-circuit node effects, confirming they do not faithfully implement the target circuit. ## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper evaluates five circuit discovery techniques on INTERPBENCH's 16 SIIT-trained transformers. ACDC significantly outperforms Node SP (p≈0.0004, large effect size Â₁₂≈0.742) and Edge SP (p=0.028, small effect Â₁₂=0.541), resolving ambiguity from prior evaluations where SP appeared comparable to ACDC on Tracr models. ACDC also decisively beats regular EAP (p≈0.000061, very large effect Â₁₂=0.91), consistent with known issues with gradient-based attribution in well-trained models. EAP with integrated gradients (EAP-IG) is the only method statistically indistinguishable from ACDC (p=0.099, Â₁₂=0.555), making it a strong alternative. Against IIT-trained transformers, SIIT models show dramatically cleaner circuit separation: non-circuit node effects drop from consistently high values (IIT) to near-zero (SIIT), matching the ground-truth Tracr circuits. The authors note that ACDC's advantage over gradient-based methods (SP, EAP) may stem from SIIT models having very small gradients after training to 100% IIA/SIIA, though EAP-IG's integrated gradients mitigate this issue.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Interchange Intervention Training (IIT, Geiger et al. 2022) by adding a Strictness loss that also intervenes on non-aligned low-level nodes, preventing them from contributing to the output. It addresses a key limitation of Tracr (Lindner et al. 2023), which compiles RASP programs into transformers with known circuits but produces unrealistic weight distributions (mostly zero, no superposition). The resulting INTERPBENCH benchmark is positioned as complementary to existing MI evaluation resources like RAVEL, ORION, and CAUSALGYM, but uniquely provides ground-truth circuits in semi-realistic transformers, filling a gap that prior benchmarks (which rely on manually hypothesized circuits like IOI) cannot address.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

INTERPBENCH provides the first benchmark with verified ground-truth circuits in realistic transformers, enabling controlled comparison of circuit discovery methods that was previously impossible with naturally trained models. The finding that ACDC significantly outperforms Subnetwork Probing (p≈0.0004) directly contradicts prior evaluations where the two were comparable, suggesting that model realism and circuit complexity matter for method rankings. However, the models are very small (single-circuit, ~6 layers) and may not generalize to the multi-circuit, large-scale settings that the MI community ultimately cares about. Open questions include whether SIIT can operate at sub-head granularity to evaluate feature discovery methods, and whether multi-circuit models can be trained to better approximate real LLMs.
