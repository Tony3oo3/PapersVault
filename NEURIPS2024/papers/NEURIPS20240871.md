## Problem

Most theoretical work on operator learning with neural networks is restricted to Hilbert spaces, yet many PDE solution spaces (e.g., L^p for p≠2) are naturally Banach. Furthermore, while approximation results exist for holomorphic operators (a class central to parametric PDEs), no prior work establishes whether deep learning achieves optimal generalization rates from finite training data in this setting, or whether standard feedforward architectures with ℓ²-loss minimization suffice. This paper closes both gaps by proving near-optimal upper and lower bounds for learning holomorphic operators between general Banach spaces.

## Input / Output

The input is a parameter field a(x) ∈ L^∞(Ω) (e.g., a diffusion coefficient or viscosity) that is encoded via a truncated Karhunen-Loève expansion into a finite parameter vector x ∈ [-1,1]^d. The output is the corresponding PDE solution field (velocity, pressure, or temperature) in a Banach space Y, decoded from R^K where K is the number of finite-element degrees of freedom. The DNN itself operates purely in finite dimensions (R^d → R^K), while the encoder and decoder handle the infinite-to-finite-dimensional mapping; in experiments these are fixed so that encoding-decoding errors vanish and only the approximation error in m is studied.

## Method

The paper constructs a family of tanh feedforward DNNs with width ≲ (m/L)^(1+δ) and depth ≲ log(m/L) (where L = log⁴(m) + log(1/ε)) that are 'problem agnostic'—their size depends only on the number of training samples m, not on the regularity of the target operator. These networks are trained via standard ℓ²-loss minimization (Adam with early stopping), and any approximate minimizer achieves a generalization bound decomposing into approximation, encoding-decoding, optimization, and sampling errors, with the approximation error decaying as m^(1/2 − 1/p) for holomorphy parameter p < 1. A second theorem shows that fully-connected DNNs of comparable size also possess uncountably many minimizers achieving the same optimal bounds, and are stable under parameter perturbations. Lower bounds (Theorems 4.1–4.2) prove that no learning procedure—adaptive or otherwise—can surpass these rates in m up to logarithmic factors, establishing DL as optimal for this class of operators.

## Results

The paper establishes near-optimal generalization bounds for learning holomorphic operators: the approximation error decays as (m/L)^(1/2−1/p) in the L²-norm (Hilbert case) and (m/L)^(1−1/p) in the L∞-norm, with matching lower bounds showing no learning procedure can improve these rates beyond log factors. Numerically, the best-performing DNNs (ELU and tanh, 4×40 architecture) achieve relative L² errors closely tracking the m^(−1) rate on all three PDE problems (elliptic diffusion, Navier-Stokes-Brinkman, Boussinesq) for both d=4 and d=8 parametric dimensions, with no observable deterioration when doubling the parametric dimension. The DNN architectures are problem-agnostic, with width ≲ (m/L)^(1+δ) and depth ≲ log(m/L), depending only on the number of training samples m.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against external operator-learning baselines (e.g., PCA-Net, DeepONet, Fourier Neural Operator) but instead compares DNN architectures internally. Across all three PDE problems, ELU and tanh activations consistently outperform ReLU at the same width and depth, with the 4×40 (width 40, depth 4) architecture generally matching or exceeding the larger 10×100 (width 100, depth 10) network. The observed m^(−1) convergence rate in the elliptic diffusion case matches the theoretical prediction exactly (since b ∈ ℓ_M^p for all p < 2/3, giving a rate arbitrarily close to m^(1/2−1/p) = m^(−1)). For the Navier-Stokes-Brinkman and Boussinesq problems, the same m^(−1) rate is observed, though the authors note that formal (b,ε)-holomorphy guarantees for these cases remain unproven. The theoretical lower bounds (Theorems 4.1–4.2) confirm that the achieved rates are optimal up to log factors for any learning procedure, not just DL-based ones, and that adaptive (active) sampling provides no benefit over i.i.d. sampling.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on learning high-dimensional holomorphic functions via DNNs (Adcock et al., 2024 [5,7]) from Banach-valued functions to Banach-valued operators, and from Hilbert to general Banach spaces — a setting the authors note is largely unaddressed in prior operator learning theory. It also departs from the specialized DNN architectures (DeepONet, PCA-Net, Fourier neural operators) that dominate the operator learning literature by showing that standard, problem-agnostic feedforward networks with constant width exceeding depth suffice to achieve optimal generalization rates. The encoder-decoder-net framework is retained, but the DNN component is deliberately kept simple, with width and depth depending only on the number of training samples m rather than on the regularity of the target operator.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper closes a significant theoretical gap by proving that DL is optimal (up to log factors) for learning holomorphic operators between Banach spaces, and that no alternative recovery procedure — including adaptive/active sampling — can surpass these rates. This provides strong justification for the widespread use of standard feedforward DNNs in PDE surrogate modeling. Key open questions include whether the factor-of-1/2 degradation in the Banach (vs. Hilbert) case is an artifact of the proof, whether the results extend to Gaussian parameter measures, and whether all minimizers (not just uncountably many) are provably good — the latter being a limitation the authors explicitly flag.
