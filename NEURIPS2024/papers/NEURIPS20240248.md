## Problem

Semi-structured (N:M) pruning of LLMs requires selecting one mask from a discrete candidate set per parameter block, a combinatorial problem that is non-differentiable and intractable at LLM scale (e.g., 1.6 billion 2:4 masks in LLaMA-2 7B). Existing one-shot methods (SparseGPT, Wanda) rely on small calibration sets (≤256 samples) and hand-crafted importance proxies that cannot capture the full knowledge embedded in LLMs, and their quality plateaus when calibration data is enlarged. No prior work learns N:M masks end-to-end on large-scale data for frozen LLMs.

## Input / Output

The method consumes frozen LLM weights (no parameter updates), a large-scale text corpus (e.g., 512k samples from blended domains), and optionally a prior mask from a one-shot method. It produces a binary N:M mask per group of M consecutive parameters (6 candidate masks for 2:4), stored at 0.65 bits per parameter via arithmetic coding. The final mask is obtained by argmax over the learned logits and applied via element-wise multiplication, yielding a sparse model deployable with 1.4× GPU speedup and 73% memory footprint at batch size 1.

## Method

MaskLLM reframes discrete mask selection as a learnable categorical distribution over the C(M,N) candidate masks per parameter block, making the problem differentiable via Gumbel-Softmax reparameterization. A soft mask is computed as a weighted average of all candidate masks using the Gumbel-Softmax probabilities, so gradients flow back to the underlying logits through the full forward pass. Training minimizes the language modeling loss on large-scale data minus a sparse weight regularization term (−λ‖W⊙M̃‖²) that prevents gradient vanishing from zeroed parameters. A scaling factor κ on the logits controls the randomness-to-determinism tradeoff of sampling, and a 'mask prior' mechanism initializes logits from pre-computed one-shot masks (via similarity-weighted logit offsets) to enable fast transfer learning of sparsity across domains and tasks.

## Results

On LLaMA-2 7B with 2:4 sparsity and frozen weights, MaskLLM achieves a Wikitext-2 PPL of 6.72, substantially outperforming SparseGPT (10.42), Wanda (11.29), and Magnitude pruning (54.71), while the dense model achieves 5.12. The advantage generalizes across model families: on LLaMA-2 13B, MaskLLM reaches 5.85 PPL vs. 8.20 for SparseGPT; on Nemotron-4 15B, 7.31 vs. 13.38; and on GPT-3 2B, 11.42 vs. 22.14. On zero-shot evaluation (average of HellaSwag, RACE, PIQA, WinoGrande, ARC-E, ARC-C, OBQA), MaskLLM scores 52.09 on LLaMA-2 7B compared to 47.16 (SparseGPT) and 45.98 (Wanda). For downstream task-specific masks on GPT-3 2B, MaskLLM achieves near-lossless compression (average PPL 7.39 vs. dense 7.42) with 1.4× GPU speedup and 73% memory footprint.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MaskLLM outperforms all eight compared baselines across every model and metric. Against the primary one-shot baselines in Table 1, MaskLLM reduces Wikitext PPL by 35–70% relative to SparseGPT and Wanda (e.g., 6.72 vs. 10.42/11.29 on LLaMA-2 7B; 7.31 vs. 13.38/25.05 on Nemotron-4 15B), and by orders of magnitude over Magnitude pruning. In the broader SOTA comparison on LLaMA-2 13B (Table 13), MaskLLM (5.85 PPL) beats Pruner-Zero (7.41), ADMM-Iter (7.78), SparseGPT with weight update (8.32), Wanda (8.27), RIA (8.41), and GBLM (8.80), even though MaskLLM uses no weight update. The gap is most dramatic on smaller models (GPT-3 843M: 15.39 vs. 38.78/51.37) and on non-English domains (Table 4), where one-shot methods degrade severely (e.g., SparseGPT PPL 26.99 on French vs. MaskLLM 9.61, dense 9.71). Notably, MaskLLM's advantage grows with model scale and data diversity, consistent with its ability to leverage large-scale training data that one-shot calibration sets (≤256 samples) cannot capture.

## Relation to Prior Work

MaskLLM extends the learnable sparsity paradigm from vision models (e.g., SR-STE, differentiable indexing) to frozen LLMs, where the combinatorial scale of N:M mask selection (1.6 billion masks for LLaMA-2 7B) makes direct optimization intractable. It departs from one-shot pruning methods (SparseGPT, Wanda, Magnitude) that rely on hand-crafted importance criteria computed over small calibration sets, instead modeling mask selection as a learnable categorical distribution optimized via Gumbel Softmax reparameterization. The mask prior mechanism further connects to transfer learning literature by enabling initialization from pre-computed one-shot masks, bridging the gap between fast heuristic pruning and end-to-end optimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that one-shot pruning methods plateau at ~256 calibration samples while learnable masks continue improving with data scale, revealing a fundamental limitation of fixed importance metrics for LLMs. The practical impact is significant: 1.4× inference speedup, 73% memory reduction, and lossless compression for domain-specific tasks with only 0.65 bits/parameter of mask storage. However, the method requires substantial compute (1,280 GPU hours for a 7B model) compared to near-instant one-shot methods, and the paper acknowledges that improving training efficiency is an important open problem. Whether the approach scales to 100B+ parameter models within reasonable budgets remains untested.
