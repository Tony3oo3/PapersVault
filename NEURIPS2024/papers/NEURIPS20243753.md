## Problem

Existing LLM unlearning methods face two intertwined challenges: knowledge entanglement causes collateral damage to general capabilities when the boundary between retention and forgetting is fuzzy, and gradient-based optimization scales with model size, making unlearning impractical for proprietary models with hundreds of billions of parameters. This paper asks whether an 'unlearned state' can be imposed on a completely intact LLM at inference time, decoupling the unlearning process from any weight modification.

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard text prompt as input. A lightweight prompt classifier (RoBERTa or Llama-3.1-1B) first screens the prompt; if flagged as within the unlearning scope, the prompt's token embeddings are perturbed in embedding space before being fed to the frozen LLM. The output is a text completion that approximates the distribution of a retained model that was never trained on the forget data. Crucially, the LLM's weights are never modified, and the corruption is applied only to the first dimension of each token's embedding vector, making the overhead independent of model size.

## Method

ECO Prompts is a two-step inference-time framework. First, a supervised prompt classifier (fine-tuned on retain/forget splits) determines whether an incoming prompt falls within the unlearning target, with the decision threshold calibrated via either simple thresholding or conformal prediction to control false positive/negative rates. Second, for flagged prompts, a corruption function adds a learned perturbation to the first dimension of each token's embedding vector, parameterized by a single scalar σ. This σ is learned offline via zeroth-order optimization (finite-difference gradient approximation) that minimizes the distance between the corrupted-prompt outputs and a surrogate retained-model metric value, treating the LLM as a black box. The key insight is that corrupting only a handful of embedding dimensions suffices to steer the LLM's output distribution to match that of a model that never saw the forget data, and the learned σ transfers across models (0.5B to 236B parameters) and unlearning tasks without re-optimization.

## Results

On the WMDP hazardous-knowledge benchmark, ECO achieves near-random-guess accuracy (24.7–26.7%) on Biology, Chemistry, and Cybersecurity across five models (Zephyr-7B through DeepSeek-V2-236B) while preserving 100% of original MMLU accuracy (e.g., 58.9% on Zephyr-7B, 73.9% on Mixtral-8x22B). On MMLU subset unlearning, ECO reduces forget-subject accuracy to near-random (Economics 20.6%, Law 24.5%, Physics 23.1%) while perfectly retaining closely related subjects (Econometrics 47.4%, Jurisprudence 74.1%, Math 34.6%). On TOFU entity unlearning, ECO achieves a forget-quality p-value near 1.0 (indistinguishable from the retained model) with zero loss in model utility. On copyrighted-content unlearning, ECO attains an average similarity gap (ASG) of 1.5 (BBC News) and 2.1 (Harry Potter) versus the retained model's 0, while matching the original model's utility scores.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ECO dominates all gradient-based unlearning baselines on WMDP: on Zephyr-7B, ECO reaches 24.7% (Bio) / 26.5% (Chem) / 24.4% (Cyber) versus LLMU (59.5/41.4/39.5), SCRUB (43.8/40.4/39.3), and SSD (50.2/33.8/35.0), while also preserving MMLU at 58.9% (vs. LLMU 44.7%, SCRUB 51.2%, SSD 40.7%). RMU [73] is the strongest competitor, achieving 29.7/47.1/28.1 on Zephyr-7B, but still lags ECO on Chemistry by ~21 points and drops MMLU to 57.5%. On MMLU subset unlearning, RMU reduces forget accuracy to near-random but catastrophically degrades retain subjects (Jurisprudence drops from 74.1% to 37.0%), whereas ECO retains all subjects at their original scores. On TOFU entity unlearning, ECO achieves a forget-quality p-value ≈ 1.0 with zero utility loss, while NPO [149] (the strongest baseline) still incurs non-trivial utility degradation, and GA/GD/KL/PO baselines [90] either fail to forget at small forget-set sizes or collapse in utility at 5–10%. On copyrighted-content unlearning, ECO (ASG 1.5/2.1) outperforms LLMU (18.4/2.3) and SCRUB (12.7/7.1) on BBC News while matching the original model's utility, whereas GA and GD reduce similarity but sacrifice 20+ points of utility.

## Relation to Prior Work

ECO Prompts departs fundamentally from the dominant weight-space unlearning paradigm (gradient ascent/descent, model editing, weight arithmetic) by keeping the LLM entirely intact and instead operating in input space at inference time. It repurposes the embedding-perturbation mechanism from adversarial jailbreak literature (Fort 2023; Zou 2023) for the opposite goal—suppressing knowledge rather than eliciting it—and learns the perturbation strength via zeroth-order optimization (Spall 1992, 2005), avoiding any gradient access to the LLM. The prompt classifier component draws on conformal prediction (Vovk 2005) for calibrated thresholding, and the overall two-step architecture (classify then corrupt) is positioned as a lightweight alternative to in-context unlearning and prompt-injection guardrails that require instruction-tuned models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ECO offers a practically compelling solution to LLM unlearning: zero additional compute that scales with model size, near-zero side effects on general utility, and demonstrated effectiveness across 100 models from 0.5B to 236B parameters, making it applicable to proprietary MaaS models where weight access is unavailable. Key limitations include its dependence on API (gray-box) access—open-weight adversaries can bypass the classifier—and the classifier's vulnerability to adversarial prompt manipulation (e.g., padding to evade the context window). Open questions include whether the classifier can be integrated into the LLM itself for robustness, whether the single-dimension corruption assumption holds for all architectures, and how the method performs under adaptive adversaries who know the corruption mechanism.
