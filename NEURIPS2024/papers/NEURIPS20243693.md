## Problem

Vision Transformer (ViT) backbones are under-explored in cross-domain few-shot learning (CDFSL) due to their poor transferability against large domain gaps. The paper identifies that the query-key attention mechanism in ViT produces ineffective target-domain attention (excessive focus on the CLS token and noisy image regions), and that simply scaling attention by a small temperature—even to zero, yielding a uniform map—consistently improves target-domain accuracy. The paper seeks to explain this phenomenon and leverage it for a practical CDFSL method.

## Input / Output

The model is first trained on miniImageNet (64 classes, 38,400 RGB images) with a DINO-pretrained ViT-S backbone, then evaluated on k-way n-shot episodes sampled from four target domains (CropDiseases, EuroSAT, ISIC2018, ChestX) with only n support images per class. Classification is performed via ProtoNet (distance to class prototypes) or a fine-tuned FC classifier, producing a single class label per query image. The method operates on standard 224×224 RGB patches tokenized into 16×16 patches, with no additional input modalities or resolution constraints beyond the ViT patch size.

## Method

The method has two stages. During source-domain training, it applies stochastic Attention Abandonment: in each ViT block, the un-normalized attention map is multiplied by a binary temperature τ^S(p)∈{0,1} sampled with probability p=0.8 of being zero, randomly downgrading attention to a uniform map. This resists learning of query-key (Q, K) parameters while encouraging learning of non-query-key components (MLP, value projections, input token representations). During target-domain evaluation, a fixed temperature τ^T is applied to attention in selected blocks (e.g., 0.3 for early blocks, CLS-token attention zeroed for later blocks) to remedy the residual ineffective attention. The key insight is that query-key attention is highly discriminative but poorly transferable across domains, whereas non-query-key features are more transferable but less discriminative; the stochastic abandonment rebalances this trade-off without adding any new modules or parameters.

## Results

On the 5-way 1-shot CDFSL benchmark, AttnTemp achieves an average accuracy of 54.12% without finetuning and 55.39% with finetuning, outperforming the best prior ViT-S method (FLoR at 52.80% / 53.85%) by 1.32 / 1.54 points. In the transductive setting, AttnTemp* reaches 57.23% average, surpassing the next best (MEM-FS+RDA* at 55.14%) by 2.09 points. On the 5-way 5-shot setting, AttnTemp achieves 66.62% (no FT) and 67.61% (FT), with the transductive variant reaching 67.93%, consistently leading all compared methods across all four target domains (CropDiseases, EuroSAT, ISIC2018, ChestX).

## Baselines & Comparisons

AttnTemp outperforms all nine compared baselines in every evaluation setting. Among non-finetuning ViT-S methods, it beats FLoR (52.80%) by 1.32 points and StyleAdv (52.34%) by 1.78 points on average 1-shot accuracy, with the largest gains on ISIC2018 (+0.72 over FLoR) and CropDiseases (+2.21 over FLoR). With finetuning, AttnTemp surpasses FLoR (53.85%) by 1.54 points and StyleAdv (53.99%) by 1.40 points, with a particularly strong margin on ISIC2018 (38.05% vs. 35.49% for FLoR, +2.56 points). In the transductive setting, AttnTemp* (57.23%) leads MEM-FS+RDA* (55.14%) by 2.09 points and FLoR* (55.62%) by 1.61 points. The method also generalizes to other ViT variants: with iBOT-pretrained ViT-S it gains +1.16 (1-shot) and +1.96 (5-shot) over baseline, and with DINO-pretrained ViT-Base it gains +0.85 (1-shot) and +1.29 (5-shot).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of ViT-based cross-domain few-shot learning (CDFSL) and the broader discriminability-transferability trade-off literature. Prior CDFSL work with ViT backbones (StyleAdv, MEM-FS, PMF, FLoR) has focused on adding auxiliary losses, adversarial training, or feature transformation modules, while domain generalization studies (e.g., Synthesizer [36], TVT [46]) have noted that self-attention is not always beneficial for transfer. This paper departs from both lines by identifying a specific, minimal intervention—scaling attention logits by a temperature—that directly addresses the query-key mechanism's poor cross-domain transferability, without introducing any new modules or losses. The stochastic attention abandonment during source-domain training is conceptually related to dropout but operates at the level of the entire attention map rather than individual elements.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that query-key attention is systematically less transferable than non-query-key ViT components provides a useful diagnostic for practitioners building ViT-based transfer pipelines: the attention mechanism, while powerful for in-domain discriminability, can actively harm cross-domain generalization. The proposed fix is remarkably simple (a scalar temperature per block) and could be adopted as a lightweight post-hoc adjustment in existing CDFSL or domain generalization systems. Limitations include evaluation on only four target domains and hand-tuned per-block temperatures; open questions include whether the insight generalizes to non-ViT architectures, whether the optimal temperature can be learned end-to-end, and whether the effect persists under even larger domain gaps or with stronger pretraining.
