## Problem

Existing panoramic semantic segmentation methods treat the equirectangular image as a uniform 2D input, addressing projection distortion but ignoring the distinct spatial structure of indoor scenes. The equirectangular projection oversamples floor and ceiling regions (poles) while undersampling equatorial objects, creating a class-size imbalance that biases single-network optimization toward large planar surfaces and degrades accuracy on smaller objects like chairs and tables. No prior method exploits the planar geometry of indoor floors and ceilings as explicit constraints to resolve monocular segmentation ambiguities.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB equirectangular panoramic image at 512×1024 resolution and produces two outputs: a dense pixel-wise semantic segmentation mask covering the full 360°×180° panorama, and a dense depth map at 1/4 resolution. The segmentation is internally decomposed into over-sampled segments (floor, ceiling, and an 'unknown' class) estimated at H/4×W/4, and under-sampled segments (all remaining object classes) also at H/4×W/4, which are then merged with floor/ceiling priority before upscaling to full resolution.

## Method

The core contribution is a geometric decomposition strategy that splits panoramic segmentation into two sub-problems optimized with distinct mechanisms. For over-sampled segments (floor/ceiling), a lightweight decoder jointly estimates dense depth and planar masks, regularized by a Plane Surface Normal Loss that enforces geometric consistency. For under-sampled segments, the paper introduces a novel 'vertical relative distance' representation: after fitting floor and ceiling planes via least squares on the predicted point cloud, each pixel's distance to both planes is computed and stacked as a 2-channel geometric feature. A transformer-based context module (6 stacked encoder layers with multi-head self-attention) aggregates five representations—global image features, cross-task hidden features, 3D point cloud, vertical relative distance, and predicted floor-ceiling masks—while a parallel high-resolution Deformable MLP branch preserves spatial detail; the two are fused via element-wise summation before a lightweight segmentation head. The encoder is built on Trans4PASS+ with Deformable Patch Embedding, and the full network is trained end-to-end with a combined focal loss (segmentation) and Huber loss (depth) at weights [1, 5, 1].

## Results

On Stanford2D3DS (fold 1), the method achieves 56.8% mIoU, surpassing the previous SOTA SGAT4PASS (56.4%) and the baseline Trans4PASS+ (53.6%) by 3.2 points. On Structured3D, it reaches 71.66% test mIoU with RGB-only input, outperforming the best RGB baseline SFSS-MMSI (68.34%) by 3.3 points. On Matterport3D, it attains 33.06% test mIoU with RGB input, exceeding the best RGB method (31.30%) and approaching RGBD-based results (35.92%). The depth estimation side task also improves over FreDSNet across all metrics (e.g., RMSE 0.390 vs. 0.518, δ₁ 0.865 vs. 0.843).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all eight baselines on Stanford2D3DS, with the largest gains over HoHoNet (+3.5% Avg mIoU) and Trans4PASS+ (+1.8% Avg, +3.1% F1 mIoU), while narrowly beating the closest competitor SGAT4PASS by 0.2% Avg and 0.4% F1 mIoU. On Structured3D, it surpasses SFSS-MMSI (the strongest RGB baseline) by 0.92% val and 3.32% test mIoU, and also exceeds the RGBD variant of PanoFormer. On Matterport3D, it leads all RGB-only methods by 1.27% val and 1.76% test mIoU over SFSS-MMSI, and remains competitive with the RGBD variant of SFSS-MMSI (35.92% test) despite using only RGB input. The depth estimation task also outperforms FreDSNet on every metric (MRE, MAE, RMSE, RMSElog, δ₁–δ₃), confirming the multi-task design benefits both objectives.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Trans4PASS+ (Zhang et al., 2022) as its encoder backbone, reusing its Deformable Patch Embedding and Deformable MLP modules, and incorporates the Plane Surface Normal Loss from PlanerecNet (Xie et al., 2021) for geometric regularization of depth. The key conceptual departure from prior PASS methods (Tangent, SphereNet, SGAT4PASS, Trans4PASS) is the explicit decomposition of the equirectangular image into over-sampled (floor/ceiling) and under-sampled segments, each optimized with a tailored strategy, rather than treating the entire image uniformly. The multi-task depth estimation component extends the joint learning paradigm of HoHoNet and FreDSNet, but the introduction of vertical relative distance as a hand-crafted geometric prior fed into a transformer context module is a novel combination not seen in prior panoramic segmentation work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The decomposition into over- and under-sampled segments addresses a practical imbalance in equirectangular panoramas where floor/ceiling distortion dominates training signal, and the vertical relative distance provides a lightweight, interpretable geometric cue that captures indoor scene structure without requiring additional sensors. However, the merging of independently estimated segments can introduce unknown regions, the depth branch adds non-trivial computational overhead (0.053G params, 0.135 TFLOPs), and performance on the more challenging Matterport3D dataset remains modest (33.06% mIoU). Open questions include whether this decomposition generalizes to outdoor panoramic scenes where planar floor/ceiling assumptions break down, and whether the depth branch's contribution is primarily through better feature representations or through the explicit geometric inputs (F_pc, F_dist).
