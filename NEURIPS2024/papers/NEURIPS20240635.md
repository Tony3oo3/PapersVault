## Problem

State-of-the-art human image animation methods rely on inaccessible private datasets, hindering fair benchmarking, and they exclusively model 2D human pose from static-camera videos while ignoring camera motion entirely. This limits controllability and prevents generating realistic videos with moving viewpoints. The paper addresses both gaps by releasing the first large-scale public dataset (HumanVid) with accurate human and camera pose annotations and establishing a unified evaluation benchmark.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240635/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a single character reference image (middle frame of a video), a 2D human pose sequence extracted by DWPose, and a 6-DoF camera trajectory (Plücker ray embeddings computed from intrinsic and extrinsic parameters) as conditioning signals, and outputs a 24-frame animated human video at 896×512 or 512×896 resolution. For real internet data, camera extrinsics are recovered via DROID-SLAM with dynamic-region masking, while synthetic data provides ground-truth camera parameters directly from the rendering engine. The unified test protocol uses the middle frame as reference and predicts frames in range [1,72) with stride 3.

## Method

CamAnimate builds on Animate Anyone's architecture (ReferenceNet for appearance consistency, Pose Guider via ControlNet for pose conditioning) and augments it with CameraCtrl's camera control mechanism: Plücker ray embeddings parameterize the camera trajectory, and a dedicated camera pose encoder injects camera information into the Denoising UNet through zero-convolution. Training follows a two-stage strategy: Stage 1 (30K iterations) trains all modules on 20K real 1080P internet videos to learn appearance and pose, while Stage 2 (10K iterations) freezes most parameters and fine-tunes only the camera encoder and AnimateDiff temporal modules on 75K synthetic videos (50K SMPL-X + 25K anime) with ground-truth camera trajectories. The synthetic pipeline uses a rule-based camera trajectory generator that samples keyframe positions within a semi-cylinder (radius 3–5 m, height 0.6–1.2 m) in front of the character, applies spline interpolation for smooth motion, and adds the human's positional offset per frame to create natural follow-cam effects. This combination of real data for appearance diversity and synthetic data for precise camera supervision is validated through ablations showing that neither component alone suffices.

## Results

On the TikTok test set (static camera), CamAnimate achieves SSIM 0.778, PSNR 18.76, LPIPS 0.247, FVD 691.8, and FID 41.35, outperforming all three baselines on every metric except SSIM where it ties with Champ (0.778). On UBC-Fashion, it reaches SSIM 0.929, PSNR 25.92, LPIPS 0.049, FVD 256.6, and FID 29.30. On the novel moving-camera benchmark (landscape, 4× batch size), CamAnimate achieves FVD 732.7 and FID 46.06, substantially outperforming Animate Anyone (FVD 1248.4, FID 97.74), MagicAnimate (FVD 1325.2, FID 109.33), and Champ (FVD 1985.2, FID 100.59). A user study with 20 participants confirms a dominant preference (0.44 average score, 0.73 top-1) over all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the standard static-camera benchmarks, CamAnimate wins on PSNR, LPIPS, FVD, and FID across both TikTok and UBC-Fashion, with the largest margins on FVD (691.8 vs. 935.6 for Animate Anyone on TikTok; 256.6 vs. 345.4 on UBC-Fashion) and FID (41.35 vs. 52.26 on TikTok). The only metric where a baseline matches or beats CamAnimate is UBC-Fashion FID, where Champ achieves 27.35 vs. 29.30, though CamAnimate leads on all four other UBC-Fashion metrics. On the moving-camera evaluation (the paper's novel contribution), all three baselines degrade severely because they were not designed for camera motion: Champ's FVD jumps to 1985.2 (landscape) and Animate Anyone's FID reaches 97.74, while CamAnimate maintains FVD 732.7 and FID 46.06. The user study further confirms the gap, with CamAnimate receiving 0.73 top-1 preference versus 0.14 for Champ, 0.10 for Animate Anyone, and 0.03 for MagicAnimate.

## Relation to Prior Work

CamAnimate is a straightforward architectural combination of Animate Anyone's character animation framework (ReferenceNet, pose guider) and CameraCtrl's Plücker-embedding-based camera encoder, with AnimateDiff's temporal motion module handling video-level consistency. The key conceptual departure from prior human image animation work (Animate Anyone, MagicAnimate, Champ) is the explicit inclusion of camera motion as a conditioning signal, whereas all prior methods assume a static camera. On the data side, the paper departs from existing synthetic human datasets (BEDLAM, SynBody, AGORA) that were designed for pose estimation or multi-view reconstruction by introducing diverse rule-based camera trajectories and physically simulated clothing, and from existing real-world datasets (TikTok, UBC-Fashion) by scaling to 20K 1080P clips with SLAM-based camera annotations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

HumanVid fills a critical gap as the first large-scale public dataset with paired human pose and camera trajectory annotations for human image animation, enabling fair and transparent benchmarking that was previously impossible due to reliance on private datasets. The two-stage training strategy (Internet videos for appearance, synthetic for camera control) and the finding that camera conditioning is necessary rather than optional when training on moving-camera videos provide practical guidance for future work. Open questions remain around quantitative camera control evaluation (the authors defer this to future work), the noise introduced by SLAM-based annotation on real videos, and whether the synthetic-to-real gap in rendering quality limits further gains.
