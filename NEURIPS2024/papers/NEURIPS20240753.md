## Problem

Existing backdoor attacks on pre-trained classifiers require either retraining with clean data or modifying the model architecture, making them impractical when training data is unavailable, inefficient for large models, and less stealthy due to detectable architecture changes. No prior attack provides a formal theoretical guarantee of undetectability or unremovability against state-of-the-art defenses.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240753/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes white-box access to a pre-trained classifier (FCN, CNN, VGG16, ResNet-18/50/101), a binary trigger mask defining a small patch region (e.g., 4×4 pixels in the bottom-right corner), and an attacker-chosen target class. It outputs a backdoored classifier with only a handful of parameters modified (one neuron per layer plus output-layer weights), requiring no training data, no retraining, and no architecture change. Input images range from 28×28 grayscale (MNIST, Fashion-MNIST) to 224×224 RGB (ImageNet), with pixel values normalized to [0,1].

## Method

DFBA constructs a single-neuron 'backdoor path' from the first layer to the output layer. In the first layer, a 'backdoor switch' neuron is created by zeroing its weights for all non-trigger positions, analytically optimizing the trigger pattern to maximize its activation on backdoored inputs, and setting its bias so the neuron fires only when the trigger is present (governed by a small threshold λ). Middle-layer neurons along the path are reweighted by an amplification factor γ to progressively boost the signal, while output-layer weights are set so the path contributes positively to the target class and negatively to all others. Theoretically, the paper proves the backdoor is undetectable by query-based (MNTD) and gradient-based (Neural Cleanse) defenses and unremovable by fine-tuning, because clean inputs never activate the path, making the backdoored model indistinguishable from a pruned clean model on any defense dataset.

## Results

DFBA achieves 100% attack success rate (ASR) across all five benchmark datasets (MNIST, Fashion-MNIST, CIFAR-10, GTSRB, ImageNet) and all tested architectures (FCN, CNN, VGG16, ResNet-18, ResNet-50, ResNet-101), while incurring less than 3% clean accuracy loss (e.g., 99.03% CA → 99.01% BA on MNIST/CNN; 76.13% CA → 73.51% BA on ImageNet/ResNet-50). The attack is extremely efficient, injecting a backdoor in under 1 second without a GPU (0.0654 s for ResNet-18 on CIFAR-10 on an A100). DFBA bypasses all six evaluated state-of-the-art defenses (Neural Cleanse, MNTD, Fine-tuning, Fine-pruning, I-BAU, Lipschitz Pruning) with 0% detection rate and 100% ASR retained.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole quantitative baseline is Hong et al. [27] (Handcrafted Backdoors, NeurIPS 2022), the state-of-the-art non-data-free parameter-modification attack. On MNIST (FCN), DFBA achieves 95.51% BA and 100% ASR versus Hong et al.'s 95.29% BA and 94.59% ASR, while both share the same 96.49% CA. Under defenses, the gap widens substantially: Neural Cleanse detects 100% of Hong et al.'s backdoored models but 0% of DFBA's; after fine-tuning, Hong et al.'s ASR drops while DFBA's remains at 100%; after fine-pruning, Hong et al.'s ASR degrades while DFBA's stays at 100%. On CIFAR-10 (ResNet-18), after fine-tuning DFBA retains 100% ASR versus 88% for Hong et al., and after fine-pruning DFBA retains 100% versus 84%. DFBA also requires no clean data and modifies fewer parameters, making it strictly more efficient (sub-second vs. 5+ minutes for Hong et al.).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the two dominant paradigms for backdoor injection into pre-trained models: parameter modification attacks that require clean data for guidance (e.g., Hong et al. 2022, Rakin et al.) and architecture modification attacks (e.g., Bober-Irizar et al. 2023). It also distinguishes itself from the closest prior 'data-free' attack (Lv et al. 2023), which still requires a substitution dataset and fine-tuning. DFBA's core conceptual move is to construct a single-neuron-per-layer 'backdoor path' whose activation is controlled entirely by a trigger pattern, enabling a retraining-free, data-free, architecture-preserving injection with provable undetectability guarantees that no prior parameter-modification attack provided.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical threat is concrete: any model shared on platforms like Hugging Face or ModelZoo can be backdoored in under a second without the attacker needing training data or retraining, and the resulting backdoor is provably invisible to the most widely deployed detection and removal defenses (Neural Cleanse, MNTD, fine-tuning, fine-pruning, I-BAU, Lipschitz pruning). Key limitations include the white-box access assumption, restriction to patch triggers and ReLU/Softmax networks, and the need to know the defense for formal guarantees (though an adaptive variant works in practice). Open questions include generalization to NLP, transformers, and non-patch triggers, as well as whether the single-path construction can be hardened against the adaptive defenses the authors themselves sketch (zero-weight anomaly detection, activation monitoring).
