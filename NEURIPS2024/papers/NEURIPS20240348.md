## Problem

The state-of-the-art for adversarially robust L_p estimation on turnstile streams, achieved by Ben-Eliezer, Eden, and Onak (2022) via a dense-sparse tradeoff, uses Õ(m^{p/(2p+1)}) space, and no progress had been made since in either achievability or impossibility. This paper asks whether that bound represents a fundamental barrier and answers negatively, showing that the dense-sparse technique does not capture the true structure of the problem.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes a single-pass turnstile stream of m adaptive updates (i_t, Δ_t) with i_t ∈ [n] and Δ_t ∈ {±1}, where each update may depend on all previous algorithm outputs. It must output a (1+ε)-approximation to the L_p norm of the induced frequency vector at every time step, not just at the end. The space bound is sublinear in both m and n, with the key parameter being the stream length m raised to a constant exponent c < p/(2p+1) for p ∈ (1,2).

## Method

The key insight is that for p ∈ (1,2), when the frequency vector is dense, O(m^{C/p}) updates may fail to change the p-th moment unless most updates concentrate on a small number of heavy-hitter coordinates. By explicitly tracking L_p heavy-hitters, the algorithm reduces the effective flip number of the residual (non-heavy-hitter) vector, enabling a better dense-sparse balance. The method combines three components: (1) a robust heavy-hitter algorithm that uses deterministic DETHH for small universes and a differential-privacy-protected ensemble of CountSketch (ROBUSTCS) for large universes, switching between them via L_0 estimation; (2) a residual estimation algorithm that partitions the tail vector into level sets by magnitude and uses subsampled CountSketch to count items in each significant level set, achieving additive error independent of the tail parameter k; and (3) a top-level dense-sparse switch that uses sparse recovery for sparse vectors and the heavy-hitter plus residual estimation pipeline for dense vectors, yielding space Õ(m^c) with c = (24p²−23p+4)/((4p−3)(12p+3)) < p/(2p+1).

## Results

The main theoretical result (Theorem 1.3) achieves adversarially robust L_p estimation on turnstile streams using space Õ(m^c) where c = (24p²−23p+4)/((4p−3)(12p+3)), improving on the prior dense-sparse bound of m^{p/(2p+1)} from BEO22 for all p∈(1,2). For p=1.5, the exponent improves from 0.375 to ≈0.373; for p=1, the heavy-hitter subroutine (Theorem 1.2) drops from Õ(m^{1/3}) to polylogarithmic space. Empirically on the CAIDA dataset (~3M events), the flip number of the residual vector (which parameterizes the new algorithm's space) is 1.002× to 1.75× smaller than the flip number of the full vector (which parameterizes BEO22's space), with the gap widening as ε decreases (1.17× at ε=10⁻¹ to 1.75× at ε=10⁻⁵) and as the heavy-hitter threshold α decreases (1.002× at α=4⁻¹ to 1.6× at α=4⁻¹⁰).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is the dense-sparse tradeoff framework of Ben-Eliezer, Eden, and Onak (BEO22, SOSA 2022), which achieves Õ(m^{p/(2p+1)}) space for adversarially robust L_p estimation on turnstile streams. This paper's algorithm strictly improves the space exponent for all p∈(1,2), though the quantitative gap is small (e.g., 0.375 → 0.373 at p=1.5). The more dramatic improvement appears in the heavy-hitter subroutine: at p=1, BEO22's framework requires Õ(m^{1/3}) space while the new algorithm (Theorem 1.2) achieves polylogarithmic space. Empirically on the CAIDA traffic dataset, the flip number of the residual vector (governing the new algorithm's space) is consistently lower than the flip number of the full vector (governing BEO22's space), with ratios up to 1.75× at ε=10⁻⁵, confirming that the theoretical improvement translates to meaningful space savings on real data. The paper positions this as a conceptual result: the dense-sparse technique is not an inherent barrier, and further improvements remain possible.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the adversarially robust streaming framework of Ben-Eliezer, Eden, and Onak (BEO22), which introduced a dense-sparse tradeoff achieving Õ(m^{p/(2p+1)}) space for L_p estimation on turnstile streams. The key departure is the use of deterministic heavy-hitter tracking (via DETHH and robust CountSketch) to reduce the effective flip number of the residual vector, combined with a new oblivious residual estimation algorithm based on the subsampling framework of Indyk and Woodruff (IW05). The differential privacy-based robustness machinery of Hassidim et al. (HKM+20) and Attias et al. (ACSS23) is reused as a subroutine, but the paper's core insight—that tracking heavy-hitters breaks the dense-sparse barrier—is new.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper answers a natural open question posed after BEO22: whether the dense-sparse tradeoff represents a fundamental limit for adversarially robust L_p estimation on turnstile streams. The answer is no, but the quantitative improvement is mild (e.g., m^{3/8} → m^{47/126} for p=1.5), so the practical impact is limited. The residual estimation subroutine (Theorem 3.4), which achieves additive error with space independent of the tail parameter k, is a potentially reusable tool for other streaming problems. Open questions include whether the gap can be closed further, whether lower bounds exist beyond the new upper bound, and whether the heavy-hitter tracking approach extends to p > 2 or other streaming tasks.
