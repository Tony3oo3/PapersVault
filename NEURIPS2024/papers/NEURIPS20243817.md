## Problem

While in-context learning for supervised prediction tasks is well-studied, it remains unclear whether general-purpose LLMs can perform exploration—a core capability for sequential decision-making—without any training interventions. Prior work demonstrated in-context RL behavior only in transformers explicitly trained on RL trajectories or expert demonstrations, leaving open whether standard LLMs (GPT-3.5, GPT-4, LLAMA2) naturally exhibit exploratory behavior when deployed as agents in bandit environments.

## Input / Output

The LLM receives a prompt containing the MAB scenario description (buttons or advertisements framing, neutral or suggestive exploration framing, time horizon T) and the interaction history, which can be presented either as a raw round-by-round list of arm choices and rewards or as externally computed per-arm play counts and average rewards. The LLM outputs either a single arm selection or a probability distribution over arms (from which an arm is sampled), with optional zero-shot chain-of-thought reasoning. The prompt design space comprises 32 combinations across five binary axes (scenario, framing, history format, output format, CoT), with temperature (0 or 1) as an additional axis, yielding up to 72 configurations per LLM.

## Method

The paper deploys GPT-3.5, GPT-4, and LLAMA2 as agents in stochastic Bernoulli MABs (K=4–5 arms, gap Δ=0.2–0.5, T=100–500 rounds) with N=10–40 replicates per configuration, comparing against UCB, Thompson Sampling, and GREEDY baselines. The key technical contribution is a set of surrogate statistics—SuffFailFreq (fraction of replicates where the best arm is never selected after time t) and K·MinFrac (rescaled minimum arm selection fraction)—that diagnose long-term exploration failures at moderate experimental scale where accumulated reward is too noisy to be informative. The central finding is that only one configuration (GPT-4 with suggestive framing, externally summarized history, and reinforced chain-of-thought at temperature 0) avoids both suffix failures and uniform-like failures, while all other configurations exhibit one or the other. This suggests that external summarization of interaction history is essential for eliciting robust exploratory behavior, raising concerns about generalization to complex settings where such summarization is itself a non-trivial algorithmic problem.

## Results

Across all LLM configurations tested (GPT-3.5, GPT-4, LLAMA2 with 32+ prompt designs), only a single configuration—GPT-4 with suggestive framing, externally summarized history, and reinforced chain-of-thought (BSSC̃0)—exhibits robust exploratory behavior. At T=200 with N=40 replicates on the hard 5-armed Bernoulli bandit (Δ=0.2), BSSC̃0 achieves a median time-averaged reward of 0.54 with a suffix failure frequency of only 3% and K·MinFrac of 0.29, comparable to Thompson Sampling (reward 0.59, suffix failure 0%, K·MinFrac 0.23). In stark contrast, the basic GPT-4 configuration (BNRN0) suffers suffix failures in 50% of replicates at T=100 and over 60% at T=500, with median reward dropping to 0.63 (T=100) and exhibiting highly bimodal behavior. All other configurations either exhibit suffix failures (failing to ever select the best arm after initial rounds) or uniform-like failures (selecting all arms near-equally without converging).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares LLM configurations against three algorithmic baselines: UCB (Auer et al., 2002), Thompson Sampling (Thompson, 1933), and GREEDY (a non-exploring negative control). On the hard MAB instance, UCB achieves the highest median reward (0.55) with near-zero suffix failures (2%) and low K·MinFrac (0.18), while TS achieves slightly lower reward (0.47) but similarly low failure rates. The single successful LLM configuration (GPT-4-BSSC̃0) at T=200 achieves a median reward of 0.54—between TS (0.59) and UCB (0.70)—with a suffix failure rate of 3% and K·MinFrac of 0.29, making it broadly comparable to TS but not matching UCB's reward. All other LLM configurations perform substantially worse than both UCB and TS: the basic GPT-4 prompt (BNRN0) has a 50% suffix failure rate (vs. 1–2% for baselines), and distributional-output configs (BNRND, BSSCD) exhibit uniform-like failures with K·MinFrac of 0.41–0.49 (vs. 0.18–0.28 for baselines). Many LLM configurations are indistinguishable from or worse than GREEDY, which itself has a 48% suffix failure rate and median reward of only 0.40.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the in-context learning (ICL) literature from supervised prediction tasks to the reinforcement learning setting, specifically asking whether native LLMs can perform exploration in multi-armed bandits without any training interventions. Prior ICRL work (Laskin et al. [42], Lee et al. [44], Raparthy et al. [57]) demonstrated that transformers can learn in-context exploration, but only when explicitly trained on RL trajectories or expert demonstrations; this paper departs by probing off-the-shelf LLMs (GPT-3.5, GPT-4, LLAMA2) under standard training. The closest prior empirical study is Coda-Forno et al. [22], which evaluated GPT-3.5 on a two-armed bandit but at too short a horizon to distinguish greedy from principled exploration. The paper also introduces surrogate statistics (suffix failure frequency, minimum arm fraction) as a methodological contribution for diagnosing long-term exploration failure at moderate experimental scale.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that only one of dozens of prompt configurations (GPT-4 + suggestive framing + externally summarized history + reinforced chain-of-thought) achieves robust exploration challenges the assumption that state-of-the-art LLMs are ready to serve as general-purpose decision-making agents. The critical dependence on external summarization is a significant limitation: in complex settings with high-dimensional observations (e.g., contextual bandits), computing such summaries is itself a non-trivial algorithmic problem, suggesting that non-trivial interventions (fine-tuning, tool use, dataset curation) will be necessary. Open questions include whether few-shot prompting or fine-tuning with optimal demonstrations can close the gap, whether findings generalize beyond simple Bernoulli MABs, and how to design cost-effective evaluation protocols for LLM agents given the combinatorial prompt space and statistical power requirements.
