## Problem

Current HD map constructors are designed and evaluated exclusively under ideal driving conditions (clear weather, fully functional sensors), leaving their reliability under real-world sensor corruptions—adverse weather, motion distortions, and sensor malfunctions—entirely uncharacterized. This gap raises critical safety concerns for autonomous driving systems that depend on accurate HD maps for planning and navigation.

## Input / Output

The benchmark corrupts the nuScenes validation set, applying 8 camera corruptions (e.g., Snow, Fog, Frame Lost, Camera Crash), 8 LiDAR corruptions (e.g., Snow, Crosstalk, Cross-Sensor, Incomplete Echo), and 13 multi-sensor combinations, each at three severity levels (Easy, Moderate, Hard). Camera-only models consume 6-view RGB images, LiDAR-only models consume point clouds (single-scan or temporally aggregated), and fusion models consume both. All models output vectorized BEV map elements—pedestrian crossing polygons, lane divider polylines, and road boundary polylines—scored via mAP, with robustness quantified by Corruption Error (mCE) and Resilience Rate (mRR) relative to a MapTR baseline.

## Method

MapBench is the first comprehensive robustness benchmark for HD map construction, systematically evaluating 31 state-of-the-art constructors (HDMapNet, VectorMapNet, PivotNet, BeMapNet, MapTR, MapTRv2, StreamMapNet, HIMap and their variants) across camera-only, LiDAR-only, and camera-LiDAR fusion configurations. The benchmark introduces two robustness metrics—Corruption Error (relative degradation vs. a baseline) and Resilience Rate (fraction of clean accuracy retained)—computed over all corruption types and severity levels. Beyond the core evaluation, the paper conducts ablation studies on backbone networks (showing Swin Transformer improves mCE by 22–24% over ResNet-50), BEV encoders (GKT and BEVFormer outperform BEVPool), temporal fusion (yielding 8.4% mRR and 14.1% mCE gains), training duration, and data augmentation (PhotoMetric for cameras; Dropout, RTS-LiDAR, and PolarMix for LiDAR). Key findings reveal that Snow corruption causes the most severe degradation across all modalities, sensor failure corruptions (Frame Lost, Cross-Sensor) are particularly threatening, and camera-LiDAR fusion models can collapse when one modality is absent, underscoring the need for robustness-aware design.

## Results

Across 31 HD map constructors evaluated on the nuScenes val set, the best camera-only model (HIMap) achieves 65.5 mAP on clean data but only 56.6% mRR (Resilience Rate) under sensor corruptions, with a mean Corruption Error (mCE) of 56.9 relative to the MapTR baseline. For LiDAR-only, HIMap reaches 64.3 mAP clean and 59.2% mRR; for camera-LiDAR fusion, HIMap achieves 74.3 mAP clean but drops to 41.7% mRR. Snow corruption causes the most severe degradation across all modalities, and sensor failure types (Frame Lost, Camera Crash, Incomplete Echo, Cross-Sensor) consistently produce >80% performance drops for the weakest models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HIMap dominates clean-set accuracy across all three configurations (65.5 camera, 64.3 LiDAR, 74.3 fusion mAP), outperforming StreamMapNet (63.4) and MapTRv2 (61.5) for camera-only, and MapTRv2 (61.5) for LiDAR-only. For camera-only robustness, HIMap achieves the best mRR (56.6%) and lowest mCE (56.9), beating StreamMapNet (54.4% mRR, 64.8 mCE) by ~2.2% mRR; however, the gap narrows for LiDAR-only where HIMap (59.2% mRR) leads MapTRv2 (57.2%) by only 2.0%. A notable finding is that HIMap's fusion model, despite the highest clean mAP (74.3), exhibits the worst fusion robustness (41.7% mRR, 110.6 mCE), underperforming MapTR's fusion variant (57.1% mRR, 100.0 mCE) by 15.4% mRR—indicating that higher clean accuracy does not guarantee better corruption resilience in multi-modal settings. Across all 31 model variants, the best mRR never exceeds 63.4% (VectorMapNet LiDAR-only), confirming that no existing method retains more than ~63% of its clean accuracy under sensor corruptions.

## Relation to Prior Work

MapBench extends the corruption-robustness evaluation paradigm established by RoboBEV (BEV perception), Robo3D (LiDAR corruptions), and RoboDepth (monocular depth) to the vectorized HD map construction task, which had previously been evaluated only under clean conditions. It departs from RoboBEV by introducing 13 multi-sensor corruption combinations that go beyond complete sensor failure, and by incorporating LiDAR-specific corruptions (Cross-Sensor, Crosstalk, Incomplete Echo) from Robo3D. The benchmark evaluates 31 existing HD map constructors (MapTR, HIMap, StreamMapNet, etc.) without proposing a new architecture, positioning itself as a diagnostic tool rather than a methodological advance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals that even the best camera-only model (HIMap) retains only 56.6% of its clean mAP under corruptions, and LiDAR Cross-Sensor corruption causes over 80% performance drops—findings that raise serious safety concerns for deployment. It identifies actionable strategies (Swin Transformer backbones, temporal fusion, PhotoMetric and LiDAR-specific augmentations) that improve robustness, but multi-modal data augmentation and temporally consistent corruption simulation remain open. The benchmark is likely to become a standard reference for HD map robustness evaluation, analogous to ImageNet-C for image classification, and will guide future work on reliable autonomous driving perception.
