## Problem

Wasserstein DRO has been applied to individual fairness, but prior work has not addressed the complications that arise when causal structures and sensitive attributes are present in the learning problem. Specifically, a causally fair metric that accounts for counterfactual perturbations in sensitive attributes can violate the positive-definiteness property required by classical DRO duality theorems, and existing studies are typically restricted to linear SCMs with specific metrics without characterizing the regularizer form in general cases.

## Input / Output

The method consumes tabular feature vectors decomposed into sensitive attributes A and non-sensitive attributes X, along with a known (or estimated) additive noise model specifying the structural equations, a loss function, and a Wasserstein radius δ. The dissimilarity function is not defined directly on the feature space but on a semi-latent space obtained via a bijective map g that inverts the SCM's reduced-form mapping, so that distance depends only on non-sensitive exogenous components. The output is a model parameter θ minimizing the worst-case risk, where the risk itself decomposes into a counterfactual robust loss term (supremum over sensitive-attribute twins) plus a norm-based regularizer on the non-sensitive projection of the model weights.

## Method

The paper introduces a Causally Fair Dissimilarity Function (CFDF) defined via a bijective map g from feature space to a semi-latent space, where dissimilarity is computed solely on non-sensitive exogenous coordinates, ensuring zero distance between counterfactual twins. A new strong duality theorem (Thm. 1) is proved by decomposing the Wasserstein ball into sensitive and non-sensitive components using the disintegration theorem, circumventing the positive-definiteness failure of the CFDF. For linear SCMs, the DRO regularizer is solved in closed form (Thms. 2–3), reducing the min-max problem to a counterfactual robust loss plus δ·‖P_X(M^T θ)‖_* where M is the linear map from the SCM. For non-linear SCMs, a first-order Taylor expansion yields an estimable regularizer involving the counterfactual gradient of the loss (Thm. 4). Finally, finite-sample convergence guarantees are established when both the SCM and the cost function must be estimated from data (Thm. 5), with error bounds depending on the Dudley entropy integral and the metric estimation rate.

## Results

On the Adult, COMPAS, and synthetic LIN datasets, CDRO achieves the lowest Unfair Area Index (UAI) at both Δ=0.05 and Δ=0.01 across all three datasets, outperforming ERM, non-causal Adversarial Learning (AL), and the Ross method. CDRO shows a slight accuracy trade-off relative to ERM, which is a commonly observed phenomenon in fairness-aware learning. Experiments are conducted with 100 random seeds using logistic regression classifiers, with fairness measured via the UAI metric and performance via prediction accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares CDRO against three baselines: Empirical Risk Minimization (ERM), non-causal Adversarial Learning (AL) [Madry et al., 2017], and the Ross method for actionable recourse [Ross et al., 2021]. CDRO achieves the lowest unfair area (UAI) at both Δ=0.05 and Δ=0.01 in all scenarios across the Adult, COMPAS, and LIN datasets, indicating superior individual fairness. Compared to ERM, CDRO trades a small amount of prediction accuracy for substantially better fairness guarantees. Against AL, CDRO's advantage stems from incorporating causal structure and sensitive attributes into the dissimilarity function, whereas AL uses a standard metric-based perturbation that ignores causal amplification effects. The Ross method, designed for actionable recourse rather than individual fairness, also shows higher UAI than CDRO, confirming that CDRO's causally-aware DRO formulation provides stronger fairness guarantees than both adversarial and recourse-based approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Wasserstein DRO framework (Gao et al. 2017; Mohajerin Esfahani & Kuhn 2018) by replacing the standard metric-based transportation cost with a Causally Fair Dissimilarity Function (CFDF) derived from structural causal models and sensitive attributes, building directly on the causal fair metric concept from Ehyaei et al. (2023, 2024) and the counterfactual fairness mechanism of Kusner et al. (2017). A key departure from prior DRO-for-fairness work (Yurochkin et al. 2019, 2021; Yeom & Fredrikson 2021; Li et al. 2023) is that the CFDF violates the positive-definite property (d(v,v')=0 does not imply v=v') required by standard DRO duality theorems, necessitating a new strong duality result (Theorem 1) and new explicit regularizer characterizations. The paper also connects to the Blanchet et al. (2019) duality theorem for the technical proof and positions itself against causal optimal transport literature (Cheridito & Eckstein 2023; Han 2022), distinguishing its use of causality in the cost function rather than the transport plan.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work provides the first principled DRO framework that simultaneously incorporates causal structure, sensitive attributes, and individual fairness, with explicit regularizer forms that eliminate the inner maximization step for practical computation. The finite sample guarantee (Theorem 5) is important because it shows the framework remains valid when the SCM must be estimated from data, addressing a key practical barrier. However, the ANM assumption limits generality (non-additive causal structures are excluded), the exact regularizer results require linear SCMs, and empirical validation is limited to small tabular datasets (Adult, COMPAS) with logistic regression, leaving open whether the framework scales to complex causal graphs or high-dimensional settings. The relationship to causal optimal transport remains an open direction for future work.
