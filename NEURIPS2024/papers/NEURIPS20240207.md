## Problem

Existing convergence analyses for sketching-based distributed learning either scale with the ambient model dimension p (pessimistic for overparameterized deep models) or require restrictive assumptions like heavy-hitter structure and Top-r sparsification to break this dependence. Despite these pessimistic bounds, empirical evidence shows sketch-DL performs competitively with uncompressed SGD, revealing a mismatch between theory and practice. This paper identifies the isotropic smoothness assumption as the root cause of the dimension dependence and provides a sharper analysis that eliminates it without additional restrictions.

## Input / Output

The framework operates on C clients each holding a local dataset D_c of size n_c, with a shared random seed generating a b×p sub-Gaussian sketching matrix R where b = Ω(ε⁻² polylog(p²NT/δ)) ≪ p. Each round, clients perform K local gradient descent steps, compress their accumulated updates via R, and the server aggregates the sketched updates before broadcasting them back for desketching via R^T. The theoretical output is a linear convergence rate and a communication complexity bound that depends on the intrinsic dimension κ (capturing the effective Hessian eigenspectrum width) rather than the ambient dimension p.

## Method

The core contribution replaces the standard β-smoothness assumption with the Restricted Strong Smoothness (RSS) property of deep learning losses, which decomposes the loss Hessian into a Gauss-Newton component and a predictor Hessian component. By exploiting the eigenspectrum of the predictor Hessian (Assumption 4.2: the sum of absolute eigenvalues is bounded by κ·Λ_max with κ = O(1) ≪ p), the analysis bounds sketching-induced error terms without the p/b factor that arises from isotropic smoothness. For the single-local-step case (K=1), this yields linear convergence under the PL condition with sketching dimension b depending on p only polylogarithmically; for the multi-local-step case (K>1), a bounded-gradient assumption handles local drift. The resulting communication complexity is Õ(C·max{1, C₂(ε,m,κ)G²/(2μ²(1-ε)ε)}·log((L(θ₀)−L*)/ε)), eliminating the linear p-dependence in prior work. The analysis holds for any symmetric sub-Gaussian sketching matrix (e.g., Count-Sketch, SRHT) without requiring Top-r sparsification or error feedback.

## Results

The paper's primary contribution is a dimension-independent convergence analysis for sketching-based distributed learning. Under the PL condition and restricted strong smoothness (RSS) assumptions, the sketching dimension required is b = Ω(1/ε² · polylog(p²NT/δ)), depending on the ambient dimension p only polylogarithmically, versus b = Ω(p) in prior work (Song et al. [62]). Empirically, on CIFAR-10 with ResNet-18 and 100 clients performing 5 local iterations per round, Count-Sketch (Algorithm 1) achieves competitive accuracy with FetchSGD without requiring Top-r sparsification or error feedback, confirming the theoretical prediction that dimension dependence does not manifest in practice.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares its Count-Sketch-based distributed learning (Algorithm 1) against two baselines: local Top-r sparsification [44] and FetchSGD [59] (which combines Count-Sketch with a global Top-r step and error feedback). On CIFAR-10 with ResNet-18, 100 clients, and 5 local iterations per round, Count-Sketch performs competitively with FetchSGD in terms of accuracy versus communication cost, as shown in Figure 1. The key advantage is that Count-Sketch alone (without Top-r or error feedback) matches FetchSGD's performance, avoiding the bias correction overhead and enabling compatibility with differential privacy. Theoretically, the paper's analysis eliminates the linear dependence on ambient dimension p present in Song et al. [62], replacing it with dependence on κ (intrinsic dimension) and 1/√m (network width), which is the core improvement over both prior theoretical analyses.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the sketching-based distributed learning frameworks of Song et al. (ICML 2023) and Rothchild et al. (FetchSGD, ICML 2020), both of which established convergence guarantees but suffered from an ambient dimension dependence in their bounds. The key departure is methodological: rather than assuming global isotropic β-smoothness of the loss (which forces the dimension dependence through the desk(sk(·)) operator), the authors exploit the anisotropic restricted strong smoothness (RSS) property and the eigenspectrum structure of the predictor Hessian, drawing on results from Banerjee et al. (ICLR 2023). This allows them to break the dimension dependence without the heavy-hitter assumption or Top-r sparsification that FetchSGD required, yielding a clean unbiased sketching analysis.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result provides the first theoretical justification for why sketching-based distributed learning works competitively with uncompressed SGD on large overparameterized models, resolving a noted gap between theory and practice flagged by Rothchild et al. themselves. The dimension-independent communication complexity bound (scaling with κ, the intrinsic dimension, rather than ambient p) makes sketching theoretically viable for modern deep models where p can be enormous. However, the empirical validation is limited to a single architecture (ResNet-18) on a single dataset (CIFAR-10) with i.i.d. data, and the analysis does not yet handle client dropout or non-i.i.d. federated settings. Open questions include whether the PL condition and eigenspectrum assumptions (κ = O(1)) hold broadly across architectures and training regimes, and whether the framework extends to differential privacy guarantees.
