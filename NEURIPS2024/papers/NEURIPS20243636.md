## Problem

Offline model-based RL suffers from an objective mismatch: dynamics models with low prediction loss fail to guide the planner toward high-reward regions because offline data contains confounders that create spurious correlations between states and actions. Prior joint model-policy training approaches reduce this mismatch but do not address the underlying causal structure, leaving the learned model vulnerable to distribution shift between data collection and deployment environments.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243636/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes an offline trajectory dataset of (s, a, s', r) tuples collected by mixed behavior policies (random, medium, or expert), where observations can be low-dimensional vectors or RGB images (encoded via a 3-layer CNN with 128-dim hidden size). It produces a sparse binary causal transition mask M ∈ R^{d×d'}, bilinear feature embeddings φ(s,a) and μ(s'), and a planned action sequence generated by pessimistic MPC that subtracts an EBM-based uncertainty score from the step return at each planning step.

## Method

BECAUSE formulates offline MBRL as causal representation learning over a bilinear MDP, where the transition kernel T(s'|s,a) = φ(s,a)ᵀ M μ(s') is decomposed into feature maps and a sparse core matrix M that encodes the causal graph. The mask M is estimated via conditional independence tests (χ²-test for discrete spaces, fast CIT for continuous variables) with ℓ₀ sparsity regularization, then reweighted by the behavior policy likelihood to deconfound spurious action-state correlations (u_π). The learned causal representations feed into an Energy-Based Model trained with Langevin-dynamics negative sampling to quantify transition uncertainty, which is subtracted from the reward in a pessimistic MPC planner. Theoretically, the paper proves a sub-optimality bound that scales with the ℓ₀ norm of the true causal mask, the SCM noise level, and the planning horizon, establishing sample efficiency for the sparse estimation problem.

## Results

Across 18 tasks (3 environments × 3 data qualities × 2 settings), BECAUSE achieves the best success rate in 8 out of 9 task groups for both in-distribution (I) and out-of-distribution (O) evaluation. In the visual RL setting (Table 1), BECAUSE reaches 62.0% success on Unlock-I-medium and 73.6% on Unlock-O-expert, substantially outperforming ICIL (5.3% and 17.1%) and IFactor (30.2% and 16.7%). In the ablation (Table 2), full BECAUSE achieves 73.3% overall-I and 43.0% overall-O, compared to 39.3% and 25.2% for the Linear variant without causal discovery.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BECAUSE outperforms all 10 baselines (MOPO, MnM, TD3+BC, ICIL, CCIL, Delphic, GNN, CDL, Denoised MDP, IFactor) in 8 out of 9 task groups for both in-distribution and out-of-distribution settings (Figure 5a). Against MOPO (the strongest model-free baseline without causal structure), BECAUSE maintains a clear margin at every sample ratio tested (Figure 5b) and the gap widens as spurious confounders increase up to 8× (Figure 5c). In the visual RL comparison (Table 1), BECAUSE dominates: on Unlock-O-expert it reaches 73.6% vs. 17.1% for ICIL and 16.7% for IFactor, a 4–5× improvement. Model-based approaches (MOPO, MnM, BECAUSE) generally outperform model-free ones (TD3+BC) across all data qualities, confirming the importance of world model learning. The ablation (Table 2) shows that removing the causal discovery module (Linear variant) drops overall-I from 73.3% to 39.3% and overall-O from 43.0% to 25.2%, while switching from pessimistic to optimistic planning (Optimism variant) costs ~9 points on I and ~11 points on O.

## Relation to Prior Work

BECAUSE builds on the bilinear MDP formulation (Yang & Wang, ICML 2020) as its architectural backbone, repurposing the low-rank transition kernel T(s'|s,a)=φ(s,a)ᵀMμ(s') as a causal structure estimator rather than a pure function approximator. It extends the confounded MDP framework (Wang et al., NeurIPS 2021) by introducing the ASC-MDP, which models confounders in both the behavior-policy channel (u_π) and the environment-dynamics channel (u_c), going beyond prior formulations that address only one. The paper departs from joint model-policy optimization approaches (MnM, Yang et al.) that reweight the entire training loss, instead performing reweighting only on the estimated causal mask M, and differs from prior causal MBRL methods (Denoised MDP, IFactor, CDL) by applying causal structure to both world model learning and uncertainty-aware planning simultaneously.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first systematic theoretical connection between causal representation learning and bilinear MDPs, yielding a finite-sample sub-optimality bound that scales with the ℓ₀-norm of the causal mask rather than the full state-action space, which is a meaningful sample-efficiency guarantee for offline settings. However, the evaluation is limited to three relatively simple environments with vector observations, and the time-homogeneous causal structure assumption may not hold in long-horizon or non-stationary real-world tasks. Scaling the causal discovery pipeline to high-dimensional visual observations and non-stationary confounders remains an open challenge that limits immediate practical impact in robotics or autonomous driving.
