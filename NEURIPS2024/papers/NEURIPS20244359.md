## Problem

Graph diffusion models achieve state-of-the-art generation quality but cannot guarantee that sampled graphs satisfy domain-specific structural properties such as planarity or acyclicity. Existing conditioning approaches guide generation toward desired properties without hard guarantees, and prior constrained methods are limited to narrow property classes or require node orderings. This paper tackles the open problem of enforcing a broad family of edge-deletion invariant structural constraints throughout the entire diffusion trajectory.

## Input / Output

The model takes a randomly sampled number of nodes (drawn from the training set distribution) and initializes from the limit distribution (all edges in the no-edge state, node types sampled from the marginal). It produces attributed graphs with categorical node types (b classes) and edge types (c+1 classes including 'no edge'), guaranteed to satisfy the specified edge-deletion invariant property at every timestep of the reverse process. The constraint is specified as a boolean-valued predicate P over graphs (e.g., planarity, acyclicity, lobster components), and the output is a clean graph G⁰ that is both valid under P and drawn from the learned distribution within the constrained domain.

## Method

ConStruct introduces two components into the graph discrete diffusion framework: (1) an edge-absorbing noise model that forces each edge to either remain in its current state or transition irreversibly to the no-edge state, casting the forward process as pure edge deletion and the reverse as edge insertion; and (2) a projector operator applied at each reverse step that takes the GNN-predicted candidate graph and iteratively inserts newly proposed edges in random order, discarding any that violate the target property P. The projector is theoretically grounded via a graph edit distance analysis showing it recovers an optimal projection onto the constrained set, and its efficiency is enhanced through incremental constraint-satisfaction algorithms (e.g., amortized O(α(q,n)) for planarity) and a blocking edge hash table that prevents redundant checks. The denoising network is a Graph Transformer (DiGress architecture) trained with cross-entropy loss on node and edge type predictions, with additional structural features (degree distribution, Adamic-Adar index, shortest distances) computed on the increasingly sparse noisy graphs.

## Results

On the planar synthetic dataset, ConStruct achieves 100% V.U.N. (valid, unique, novel) and 100% property satisfaction (planarity), with an average MMD ratio of 1.1—nearly matching the training set—while the best unconstrained baseline (DiGress+) reaches only 76.4% V.U.N. and 76.4% property satisfaction. On the lobster dataset, ConStruct attains 100% property satisfaction and 83.2% V.U.N., outperforming DiGress+ (76.8% property, 69.4% V.U.N.). On digital pathology cell graphs, ConStruct achieves 100% planarity and 96.2–99.8% V.U.N. across low/high TLS datasets, compared to 19.8–8.4% planarity and 18.6–7.8% V.U.N. for DiGress+, improving TLS validity by up to 71.1 percentage points over the 1-hop baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ConStruct decisively outperforms all one-shot and diffusion baselines on the planar dataset, achieving the first 100% V.U.N. score versus 95% for HSpectre [7], 77.5% for DiGress [79], and 25% for SPECTRE [54], while also guaranteeing 100% planarity (property satisfaction) where no other method provides such a guarantee. On the tree dataset, ConStruct is slightly outperformed by DiGress+ (ratio 1.3 vs. 1.9) due to the marginally lower expressivity of the edge-absorbing noise model for this specific case, though ConStruct still achieves 100% acyclicity versus 97% for DiGress+. On the lobster dataset, ConStruct leads in both ratio (1.3 vs. 1.8) and V.U.N. (83.2% vs. 69.4%) over DiGress+, and achieves 100% property satisfaction versus 76.8%. On digital pathology graphs, ConStruct dominates all baselines: it achieves 100% planarity versus 19.8% (DiGress+), 51.2% (SPECTRE), and 33.3% (GraphGen) on the low-TLS set, and 100% versus 8.4% (DiGress+), 0% (SPECTRE), and 48.1% (GraphGen) on the high-TLS set. The 1-hop baseline [52] completely fails to capture structural dependencies (0% TLS validity), while ConStruct reaches 96.2% and 88.0% TLS validity on the two pathology datasets respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on graph discrete diffusion (DiGress [79]) as its base generative framework and departs from prior constrained generation approaches in two key ways. Unlike PRODIGY [72], which relaxes graphs into continuous space and projects back (losing discrete structure and failing for combinatorial constraints like planarity), ConStruct operates entirely in discrete state-space using an edge-absorbing noise model that makes the forward process a pure edge-deletion process. Unlike autoregressive approaches (GraphARM [41]) that require learning a node ordering, ConStruct preserves permutation invariance via a GNN denoiser and enforces constraints through a projector operator whose optimality is grounded in graph edit distance analysis. The unifying concept of edge-deletion invariance (Definition 3.1) is the paper's key theoretical contribution, showing that a broad family of structural properties (planarity, acyclicity, lobster components, max degree) can be handled by the same mechanism.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ConStruct provides the first general-purpose framework for enforcing hard structural constraints in graph discrete diffusion, with practical impact demonstrated in digital pathology where it improves cell graph validity by up to 71.1 percentage points and introduces the first open-source TLS-specific graph datasets. The main limitation is that only edge-deletion invariant properties are handled; edge-insertion invariant properties (e.g., minimum cycle count) and joint node-edge constraints (e.g., molecular valency) remain open, as acknowledged in the paper's limitations section. The projector also breaks ELBO tractability, and the tree dataset results show the edge-absorbing noise model can be marginally less expressive than the marginal noise model for certain graph families. Future work on exploiting the induced sparsity for scalability and extending to joint constraints would broaden applicability to molecular and causal discovery settings.
