## Problem

LLMs have achieved strong performance in language and logical reasoning, yet their spatial reasoning capabilities remain underexplored, and existing spatial benchmarks often test linguistic structure of spatial terms rather than genuine spatial awareness. No prompting method explicitly elicits the human-like 'mind's eye' process of creating and manipulating mental images to guide multi-hop spatial reasoning in LLMs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242865/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 2D grid worlds rendered as text using special characters and emoji (not pixel images) as spatial input, paired with a single VoT instruction ('Visualize the state after each reasoning step'). The output is an interleaved sequence of textual reasoning traces and text-based 2D grid visualizations, culminating in a final answer (navigation direction sequence, next-step direction, polyomino variant, or object name). For the GPT-4V baseline comparison, the same grids are rendered as actual color-emoji images to test whether a multimodal model with true visual input can match the text-only VoT approach.

## Method

VoT prompting extends chain-of-thought by inserting a visualization step after each intermediate reasoning step, implementing a 'visuospatial sketchpad' where the model generates a text-based 2D grid representing its current spatial state. Formally, after each reasoning step z_i, a visualization v_i is sampled conditioned on the input, all prior reasoning steps, and all prior visualizations; the next step z_{i+1} is then conditioned on both the full reasoning trace and the visualization sequence, creating a grounded state-tracking loop. This is entirely zero-shot with no few-shot demonstrations or external text-to-image models, relying on the LLM's pre-trained ability to generate and interpret text-based visual representations such as ASCII grids and emoji layouts. The paper shows GPT-4 VoT outperforms GPT-4V CoT on visual navigation and tiling tasks, and that the visualization accuracy (compliance ~51%, accuracy ~25%) is the bottleneck, while correct visualizations yield 65–77% correct final answers.

## Results

GPT-4 VoT achieves 40.77% completing rate and 14.72% success rate on visual navigation route planning, 55.28% on next-step prediction, 63.94% on visual tiling, and 59.00% on natural language navigation. These represent consistent improvements over GPT-4 CoT (37.02/9.48, 48.61, 54.15, 54.00) and GPT-4V CoT (33.36/5.65, 46.59, 49.62) across all tasks. The largest gains appear in visual tiling (+9.79 pp over CoT, +14.32 pp over GPT-4V) and natural language navigation (+5.00 pp over CoT, +23.5 pp over w/o Viz). Statistical significance (p < 0.05) is confirmed for VoT over all baselines via two-sample z-test, except natural language navigation vs. CoT where p < 0.16.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary external baseline is GPT-4V CoT (a state-of-the-art multimodal LLM given actual image inputs). GPT-4 VoT outperforms GPT-4V CoT on every visual task: route planning completing rate 40.77 vs. 33.36, success rate 14.72 vs. 5.65, next-step prediction 55.28 vs. 46.59, and visual tiling 63.94 vs. 49.62 — demonstrating that a text-only LLM with VoT prompting can surpass a multimodal model on spatial reasoning. Against the standard GPT-4 CoT baseline, VoT wins on all five metrics, with the most notable gap in visual tiling (+9.79 pp) and next-step prediction (+6.67 pp). The GPT-4 w/o Viz ablation (where visualization is explicitly suppressed) confirms that the gains come from visual state tracking rather than merely longer reasoning: VoT improves over w/o Viz by +23.5 pp on natural language navigation (59.00 vs. 35.50) and +16.96 pp on visual tiling (63.94 vs. 46.98). Notably, VoT underperforms CoT on a ring-structured navigation subtask (49.5% vs. 52.5%), where the problem reduces to modular arithmetic and does not benefit from spatial visualization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Chain-of-Thought prompting (Kojima et al., 2023) by interleaving text-based 2D grid visualizations between reasoning steps, drawing on the cognitive science concept of the visuospatial sketchpad (Baddeley, 1992) and mental imagery (Shepard, 1978). It departs from prior LLM spatial reasoning work (SPAR-TQA, StepGame, Yang et al. 2023) that treated spatial understanding as a linguistic-to-logical-form conversion problem, arguing instead that genuine spatial awareness requires the model to generate and manipulate internal visual states. The approach also connects to the world-models-of-LLMs literature (Hao et al., 2023; Liu et al., 2022) but differs by requiring no external simulator, predefined state space, or fine-tuning—only a zero-shot prompt instruction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a text-only LLM (GPT-4 VoT) outperforms a multimodal model (GPT-4V) on visual spatial tasks challenges the assumption that pixel-level visual input is necessary for spatial reasoning, suggesting that structured text representations (2D grids) can serve as a sufficient grounding medium. However, absolute performance remains far from human level (e.g., 14.7% success rate on route planning), visualization accuracy is low (~25%), and the method is sensitive to prompt wording and model scale. Open questions include whether VoT transfers to MLLMs, generalizes beyond 2D grids to 3D or continuous spaces, and whether the low visualization accuracy represents a fundamental bottleneck or can be addressed through better instruction tuning.
