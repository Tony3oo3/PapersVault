## Problem

Despite widespread empirical use of message-passing GNNs (MP-GNNs) to approximate strong branching (SB) scores in MILP solvers, no theoretical characterization existed for when such GNNs can or cannot represent SB. This paper asks: given a distribution of MILPs, does there exist a GNN that maps each instance to its SB score with arbitrary precision and probability, and if not, what structural conditions are necessary?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243938/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The input is a MILP encoded as a bipartite MILP-graph: constraint nodes carry (RHS, constraint-type) features, variable nodes carry (objective coefficient, lower bound, upper bound, integer-indicator) features, and edges carry the corresponding entries of the constraint matrix A. The output is an n-dimensional SB score vector where each entry is the product of the LP objective improvements from tentatively branching the variable up and down (zero for non-integer variables). The SB score is defined only for MILPs whose LP relaxation is feasible and bounded, with the optimal solution taken as the minimum-ℓ₂-norm one to ensure uniqueness.

## Method

The paper's core contribution is a theoretical capacity analysis rather than a new architecture. It introduces the notion of MP-tractability: a MILP is MP-tractable if, under the WL-test partition of its graph, every submatrix cell (A_ij)_{i∈I_p, j∈J_q} has identical entries. The main positive result (Theorem 4.4) proves a universal approximation theorem: for any distribution over MP-tractable MILPs, an MP-GNN exists that approximates SB scores to arbitrary accuracy with arbitrary probability, via a generalized Stone-Weierstrass argument combined with Luzin's theorem. A negative result (Theorem 4.5) constructs two 8-variable MILPs with different SB scores that are indistinguishable by any MP-GNN regardless of depth or width, showing MP-tractability is necessary. To remove this restriction, the paper extends the universal approximation to the full MILP space using second-order folklore GNNs (2-FGNNs), which operate on node-pair features and whose separation power is characterized by the 2-FWL test (Theorem 4.7). Small-scale numerical experiments with L=2 layers and 1024-dim embeddings confirm that MP-GNNs fail on the counterexample while 2-FGNNs fit both instances to near-zero loss.

## Results

On 100 randomly generated MP-tractable MILPs (6 constraints, 20 variables), both MP-GNN and 2-FGNN successfully fit SB scores to near-zero error, validating Theorem 4.4 and 4.7. On the two MP-intractable counterexample instances (4.1) and (4.2), 2-FGNN achieves near-zero loss while MP-GNN's training error plateaus regardless of the number of epochs or parameters, confirming Theorem 4.5. On 100 large-scale set covering problems (1,000 variables, 2,000 constraints), MP-GNN reaches a training loss of 1.94×10⁻⁴ (average ℓ₂ norm of errors). For 2-FGNN on the counterexamples, embedding size 1024 requires 980 epochs to reach 10⁻⁶ error and 1,128 epochs to reach 10⁻¹² error.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not compare against external baselines; instead, the central empirical comparison is between MP-GNN and 2-FGNN on the same SB score prediction task. On MP-tractable MILPs, both architectures perform comparably, achieving near-zero training loss. On the MP-intractable counterexamples (4.1) and (4.2), MP-GNN fundamentally fails—its error does not decrease with more epochs or parameters—while 2-FGNN fits both instances simultaneously to near-zero loss. This gap is structural (guaranteed by Theorem 4.5) rather than a matter of model capacity or training duration. The paper also notes that 2-FGNN incurs higher computational cost due to operating on node pairs rather than individual nodes, and that gains in 2-FGNN training efficiency level off beyond an embedding size of 1,024.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper is a theoretical follow-up to the authors' prior work on representing LPs and MILPs by GNNs [11, 12], shifting focus from optimal solution prediction to the specific task of approximating strong branching scores. It builds on the WL-test-based expressiveness framework of Xu et al. [55] and the high-order GNN constructions of Morris et al. [42], applying them to the MILP-graph setting introduced by Gasse et al. [19]. A key departure from [12] is the introduction of MP-tractability as a strictly weaker assumption than unfoldability, broadening the class of MILPs for which MP-GNNs are provably sufficient. The universal approximation proof for 2-FGNNs leverages the generalized Stone-Weierstrass theorem of Azizian and Lelarge [4].

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first theoretical characterization of when GNNs can and cannot approximate SB scores, giving practitioners a polynomial-time diagnostic (WL test) to check before committing to an MP-GNN architecture. The negative result—that no MP-GNN, regardless of size, can distinguish certain MILP pairs—challenges the implicit assumption in the L2B literature that scaling up MP-GNNs will eventually capture all SB behavior. Open questions include the parameter/complexity requirements for the approximating GNNs, efficient training strategies for 2-FGNNs on large instances, and whether sparse or local variants of higher-order GNNs can close the gap at lower computational cost.
