## Problem

Representation intervention methods (e.g., ITI, RepE) can steer LLM behavior by adding offset vectors to hidden representations in a learning-free way, but they are less effective than fine-tuning approaches. Meanwhile, parameter-efficient fine-tuning methods like LoRA and RED modify many parameters without any interpretability-driven localization. The paper asks whether combining interpretability-based head localization with learned offset vectors can match PEFT performance while modifying 20x–200x fewer parameters, and whether the selected heads are task-specific.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard text prompts (questions, stories, or passages) and produces text responses via autoregressive decoding. During both training and inference, learned bias vectors are added to the activations of a sparse subset of attention heads (3%–10% of all heads) at every decoding step. The learned artifacts are per-head offset vectors of dimension d_head, with all pre-trained weights frozen; for TruthfulQA, training uses DPO preference pairs rather than standard SFT.

## Method

LOFiT is a two-step localized fine-tuning procedure. Step 1 (Attention Head Selection) introduces a learnable per-element scaling factor A_l^i for every attention head, trained end-to-end with L1 regularization to encourage sparsity; the top-K heads are selected by the L2 norm of their learned scaling vectors. Step 2 (Bias Tuning) freezes all pre-trained weights and learns a single offset vector v_l^i for each selected head, added directly to the head's activation during the forward pass. The key insight is that the localization step is task-specific: heads selected for TruthfulQA differ substantially from those for MQuAKE or CLUTRR, and using cross-task heads degrades performance. This interpretability-driven sparsity enables LOFiT to match LoRA and RED on truthfulness and reasoning tasks with only 12K–20K learned parameters versus millions.

## Results

On the primary interpretability tasks (TruthfulQA, MQuAKE, CLUTRR), LOFiT achieves average accuracy of 74.0–74.6% across Gemma-7B, Llama 2-7B, and Llama 2-13B, substantially outperforming ITI (46.4–49.1%) and RepE (53.3–55.1%). Against PEFT methods, LOFiT uses only 12K–20K learned parameters (0.0001–0.0002% of model parameters) versus 3.21M–6.55M for LoRA (200× fewer) and 229K–409K for RED (20× fewer), while achieving comparable average accuracy (74.1–75.9% vs. LoRA's 72.6–77.1% and RED's 74.5% on interpretability tasks). On commonsense and math benchmarks (SIQA, ARC-c, BoolQ, SVAMP), LOFiT scores 60.7–69.1% average, slightly below LoRA (62.0–70.4%) and RED (62.4–68.7%) but above ReFT (60.5–67.9%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LOFiT substantially outperforms both representation intervention baselines: on TruthfulQA MC1 it gains +23 to +28 points over ITI (e.g., 60.5% vs. 29.7% on Gemma-7B) and +12 to +18 points over RepE (60.5% vs. 38.5%), with similar margins on MQuAKE and CLUTRR. Against LoRA, LOFiT achieves comparable average accuracy on interpretability tasks (74.1–75.9% vs. 72.6–77.1%) while using 200× fewer learned parameters (12K–20K vs. 3.21M–6.55M); on commonsense/math benchmarks LOFiT trails LoRA by 1–2 points on average. Against RED, LOFiT matches or slightly exceeds performance on interpretability tasks with 20× fewer parameters, and outperforms the half-parameter RED variant on commonsense/math tasks. Against ReFT, LOFiT consistently outperforms across all settings, gaining 3–5 points on average on interpretability tasks (e.g., 75.9% vs. 70.5% on Gemma-7B) and 0.2–1.2 points on commonsense/math tasks. Notably, LOFiT also shows better out-of-domain generalization: after fine-tuning on TruthfulQA, LOFiT preserves base-model performance on TriviaQA (76.7% vs. 76.5% no-FT) and MMLU (40.5% vs. 40.3%), while ITI, RED, and LoRA all degrade by 2–5 points.

## Relation to Prior Work

LOFiT bridges two previously separate research threads: representation intervention methods (ITI, RepE) that add learning-free offset vectors to localized attention heads, and parameter-efficient fine-tuning methods (LoRA, RED, ReFT) that update small parameter subsets without explicit interpretability-driven localization. The key conceptual departure is using a learned scaling-factor step to *select* which heads to modify (borrowing the localization philosophy from interpretability) and then *optimizing* the offset vectors (borrowing the learning-based approach from PEFT), rather than extracting vectors from contrastive representations as ITI/RepE do. Unlike RED and ReFT, which treat the choice of layers or modules as a hyperparameter, LOFiT grounds its module selection in a task-specific scoring function derived from the model's own learned scaling weights.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that task-specific head selection is non-interchangeable—even between closely related tasks like TruthfulQA and MQuAKE—suggests that interpretability-driven localization captures genuine functional structure in LLMs that generic PEFT methods miss, with practical benefits for continual learning and model merging. The 200x parameter reduction relative to LoRA while maintaining comparable accuracy, combined with better out-of-domain generalization (preserving base-model capabilities on TriviaQA/MMLU after TruthfulQA fine-tuning), makes LOFiT a practically attractive option for resource-constrained adaptation. Open questions include whether the localization step generalizes to long-context tasks, non-English settings, and models beyond 13B parameters, and whether the two-step procedure can be unified into a single end-to-end optimization.
