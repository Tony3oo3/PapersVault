## Problem

In overparameterized next-token prediction (NTP), the cross-entropy loss admits infinitely many minimizers, yet gradient-based optimizers select a particular solution. While implicit bias in one-hot classification is well understood, NTP is fundamentally different: contexts repeat with different next tokens, yielding sparse soft-label distributions rather than one-hot targets. This paper asks what structural properties GD selects among the infinite NTP minimizers, and how the answer differs from the one-hot case.

## Input / Output

The method takes fixed d-dimensional context embeddings (one per distinct context) and, for each context, a sparse V-dimensional empirical conditional probability vector over a finite vocabulary. It produces a decoder matrix W ∈ R^{V×d} whose rows act as word embeddings. The output decomposes into two orthogonal components: a finite projection W* onto the data subspace F (encoding log-odds of in-support token pairs) and an unbounded-norm directional component along the NTP-SVM vector in F⊥ (separating in-support from out-of-support tokens). Overparameterization (d > m distinct contexts) guarantees both components exist.

## Method

The paper introduces two conditions—NTP-H-compatibility (logit differences between in-support tokens equal their log-odds) and NTP-separability (in-support and out-of-support tokens are linearly separable in a lifted (e_z − e_v)h_j⊤ space)—that are necessary and sufficient for the CE loss to reach its entropy lower bound. It then defines an NTP-SVM program that maximizes the margin between in-support and out-of-support tokens while being constrained to the orthogonal complement F⊥ of the data subspace. The main theorems prove that both the norm-constrained regularization path and GD iterates converge directionally to the NTP-SVM solution in F⊥ while their projection onto F converges to the unique W* satisfying the log-odds equations. This extends one-hot implicit bias results by showing the data subspace F is non-trivial (due to soft labels) and the margin operates on a different geometric structure than class separation.

## Results

The paper's main contributions are theoretical: Theorem 1 shows the regularization path of CE minimization converges in direction to the NTP-SVM solution W^mm, and Theorem 2 proves that GD iterates also converge in direction to W^mm while their projection onto the data subspace F converges to the unique solution W* of the NTP H-compatibility equations. Lemma 2 establishes that under NTP-compatibility and NTP-separability, the CE loss converges to the empirical entropy lower bound H while the parameter norm diverges to infinity. Synthetic validation (d=60, m=50, V=10, S_j=6) confirms all predictions: CE loss reaches H≈1.445 nats, norm grows unboundedly, and iterates align with W^mm, with NGD and Adam exhibiting the same bias but faster convergence than GD.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical paper with no direct method-vs-method benchmark comparisons. The paper extends the implicit bias framework from one-hot classification (Soudry et al. [76], Ji & Telgarsky [34], Ji et al. [37], Rosset et al. [68]) to the NTP setting, where the key structural difference is that contexts are repeated with sparse multi-token support sets rather than unique one-hot labels. The synthetic experiments compare three optimizers (GD, normalized GD, Adam) on the same overparameterized dataset; all three exhibit the same implicit bias (convergence to W^mm direction and W* on F), but NGD and Adam converge faster than vanilla GD, consistent with analogous findings in one-hot classification. The paper also contrasts its results with Liu et al. [49] (which assumes population loss and Hessian-trace minimization, not applicable to CE or sparse supports) and Malach [52] (which assumes each context has a unique next token, ruling out the NTP setting). No quantitative head-to-head comparison against prior methods is provided, as the contribution is a new theoretical characterization rather than an improved algorithm.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the implicit bias literature for one-hot classification (Soudry et al. 2018; Ji & Telgarsky 2018; Ji et al. 2020; Rosset et al. 2003) to the next-token prediction setting, where the fundamental data structure differs: contexts are repeated with sparse, multi-token next-token distributions rather than single labels. The authors explicitly position their work as extending the foundational proofs of [68, 34, 37] to a setting that is neither binary nor one-hot, introducing NTP-separability and NTP-compatibility conditions that decompose the parameter space into a finite data subspace and a divergent orthogonal complement. Recent concurrent work on implicit bias in transformers (Tarzanagh et al. 2023; Li et al. 2024) studies architecture-specific mechanisms, whereas this paper isolates the NTP training paradigm itself, orthogonal to the embedding architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first rigorous characterization of which solution gradient descent selects among infinitely many NTP minimizers, revealing a two-component structure (finite log-odds matching in the data subspace + max-margin divergence in the orthogonal complement) that has no direct analogue in one-hot classification. The key limitation is the restriction to linear decoders with fixed context embeddings; the most practically relevant question—how transformer-generated embeddings interact with these biases—remains entirely open. The paper also leaves open exact NTP-separability thresholds, generalization analysis of the NTP-SVM solution, and extensions to adaptive optimizers like Adam, all of which are necessary before these results can inform practical language model training.
