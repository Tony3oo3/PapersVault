## Problem

Generalist multimodal large language models (MLLMs) consistently underperform specialist models on most vision-language tasks due to task interference, a fundamental multi-task learning challenge. Prior mixture-of-experts approaches to mitigate this issue only introduce experts in the LLM to handle textual differences between tasks, while overlooking the equally significant visual feature discrepancies across task groups.

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB image and a free-form text instruction as input. The image is simultaneously processed by three parallel vision encoders (CLIP-ViT, DINOv2, Pix2Struct) that produce feature maps of differing spatial dimensions and sequence lengths, while the instruction is encoded by Sentence-BERT into a fixed-length embedding used for routing decisions. The model outputs free-form text (captions, VQA answers, document descriptions) and, for referring expression comprehension tasks, precise bounding box coordinates.

## Method

MoME introduces two complementary expert mixtures. MoVE (Mixture of Vision Experts) aligns heterogeneous encoder features via an Adaptive Deformable Transformation module—combining 2D adaptive average pooling with six layers of deformable cross-attention that sample 2D points from original feature maps—then aggregates them using an instance-level soft router (MLP + SoftMax) conditioned on the instruction's Sentence-BERT embedding to produce a weighted fusion of visual tokens. MoLE (Mixture of Language Experts) inserts K LoRA-style bottleneck adapters in parallel with each FFN layer of the Vicuna-7B LLM, with an instance-level top-1 sparsely-activated router selecting a single adapter per sample based on the same sentence embedding, keeping inference cost nearly unchanged. Training proceeds in two stages: first training MoVE with a single adapter, then replicating adapter weights to initialize MoLE experts for joint fine-tuning on a mixed 24-dataset instruction-tuning benchmark spanning four task groups.

## Results

MoME achieves the best or near-best performance across the broadest range of VL tasks among all compared models. Headline numbers include 50.8 ANLS on DocVQA (vs. 47.1 for DocPedia), 57.2 Relax Accuracy on ChartQA (vs. 46.9 for DocPedia), 130.8 CIDEr on TextCaps (vs. 108.8 for LION), 94.6 CIDEr on Flickr30K (vs. 87.4 for LION), 61.4 accuracy on IconQA (vs. 54.9 for LION), and 83.2 accuracy on RefCOCO (vs. 82.5 for Ferret). MoME is the only model evaluated on all nine benchmark tasks, demonstrating its generalist capability. On the MoVE ablation, adding the instruction-based soft router over simple addition yields an average gain of 12.87 points across four task groups, with over 20-point improvement on the Document group.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MoME outperforms all document-specialist baselines (DocPedia, LLaVA-MoLE) by large margins on DocVQA (50.8 vs. 47.1/30.0) and ChartQA (57.2 vs. 46.9/42.4), and surpasses LION on TextCaps (130.8 vs. 108.8), Flickr30K (94.6 vs. 87.4), and IconQA (61.4 vs. 54.9). On RefCOCO, MoME edges out Ferret (83.2 vs. 82.5) and Shikra (83.2 vs. 80.2). However, MoME trails LLaVA-v1.5 on TextVQA (53.2 vs. 58.2) and GQA (59.7 vs. 62.0), and falls behind LION on VSR (61.9 vs. 73.8) and MoCLE on VSR (61.9 vs. 64.7). The key differentiator is breadth: MoME is the only model reporting scores on all nine tasks, whereas each baseline covers only 2–5 tasks, confirming its generalist positioning. Among MoE-based MLLMs (MoE-LLaVA, MixLoRA, MoCLE, LLaVA-MoLE), MoME consistently leads on the tasks they share, with the largest gap over MixLoRA on TextVQA (53.2 vs. 40.0).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Mixture-of-Experts paradigm from the LLM side (as in Octavius, LLaVA-MoLE, MoE-LLaVA, MixLoRA, MoCLE) to the vision encoder side, arguing that prior MoE-in-MLLM works overlook task interference in the visual perception process. For the vision side, it builds on multi-encoder MLLMs (LION, Sphinx) but departs from their naive feature concatenation by introducing an Adaptive Deformable Transformation (ADT) module that combines adaptive pooling with deformable cross-attention (inspired by Deformable DeTR) to align heterogeneous encoder outputs. On the language side, it repurposes parameter-efficient adapters (LoRA-style bottleneck modules) as sparsely-activated experts rather than full FFN replacements, keeping inference cost nearly unchanged.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled, well-ablated answer to the well-recognized problem that generalist MLLMs underperform specialists due to task interference, and does so by addressing both modalities simultaneously rather than just the language side. The clear expert specialization patterns (Pix2Struct for documents, DINOv2 for grounding, CLIP for general captioning) validated through routing visualizations give practitioners a concrete recipe for building multi-encoder generalist models. Key limitations include the restriction to three vision encoders, a 7B LLM, and 24 datasets; open questions include whether the approach scales to more encoders, video/audio modalities, and larger models, and whether the soft router generalizes to unseen task types.
