## Problem

LLMs have made significant progress on vertical (logical, step-by-step) reasoning tasks, but their lateral thinking—creative, non-linear problem-solving—remains under-explored and difficult to measure. Existing open-ended evaluation paradigms (e.g., LLM-as-a-Judge) require the evaluator to be stronger than the model being tested, creating a fundamental bottleneck for assessing state-of-the-art models. This paper addresses both the measurement gap and the evaluation bottleneck by introducing situation puzzles as a structured vehicle for lateral thinking assessment.

## Input / Output

The player model receives an incomplete story (S₀) and must iteratively pose yes/no/irrelevant questions to a judge model that holds the full reference scenario (Ŝ). The player's final output is a free-form deduced scenario, evaluated not by exact match but by semantic alignment with the reference via the judge. Performance is quantified by three metrics: Accuracy (binary correctness), Average Rounds (interaction count to reach the answer, capped at 15), and an OverAll composite (O/A) that divides the correctness indicator by rounds taken, rewarding both correctness and efficiency. The dataset comprises 975 puzzles across three difficulty tiers (Easy 1–3, Medium 4–6, Hard 7–9), with average question length of ~80 tokens and reference answer length of ~46 tokens.

## Method

The core contribution is a multi-turn player-judge framework that restructures evaluation from a single-shot generation task into an interactive game. The player (the LLM under test) asks sequential yes/no questions; the judge (a separate LLM, e.g., WizardLM-2 8x22B) answers strictly from the reference scenario or performs a binary semantic-alignment check on the player's final guess. Because the judge's role is reduced to simple yes/no/irrelevant responses and a final NLI-style match check, the framework does not require the judge to be stronger than the player, enabling evaluation of frontier models. The framework also serves a second purpose: the question-answer pairs generated during play are extracted as auxiliary reasoning prompts (5 pairs per puzzle) and prepended to prompts on other lateral-thinking benchmarks (RiddleSense, BrainTeaser), yielding consistent accuracy gains (e.g., Llama3-70B on RiddleSense: 83.34% → 85.21%), demonstrating that the benchmark both evaluates and actively elicits lateral thinking.

## Results

On the SPLAT benchmark, GPT-4 achieves the highest overall performance with 70.96% accuracy on Easy, 36.88% on Medium, and 41.70% on Hard puzzles, yielding O/A scores of 15.25, 6.71, and 8.16 respectively. GPT-4 Turbo follows closely (60.36%/28.39%/32.91% Acc), while Llama3-70B and WizardLM-2 are competitive in the mid-range (64.05%/28.54%/34.50% and 58.52%/31.48%/35.45% Acc). Even the strongest model (GPT-4) solves fewer than 42% of Hard puzzles, highlighting the persistent challenge of lateral thinking for current LLMs. When auxiliary reasoning prompts from SPLAT are applied, Llama3-70B improves from 83.34% to 85.21% on RiddleSense and from 87.76% to 91.51% overall on BrainTeaser (Sentence).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GPT-4 outperforms all other models across every difficulty level on SPLAT, achieving 70.96% Easy accuracy vs. 64.05% for Llama3-70B and 60.36% for GPT-4 Turbo, and 41.70% Hard accuracy vs. 35.45% for WizardLM-2. GPT-4 Turbo is competitive with Llama3-70B on Easy (60.36% vs. 64.05%) but trails on Hard (32.91% vs. 34.50%), while WizardLM-2 edges out Llama3-70B on Medium (31.48% vs. 28.54%) and Hard (35.45% vs. 34.50%). The Qwen1.5 models (32B and 110B) consistently underperform, with Qwen1.5-32B achieving only 29.95% Easy accuracy, suggesting that chat-tuned configurations struggle with the open-ended lateral thinking required by SPLAT. In the elicitation experiments, auxiliary reasoning prompts from SPLAT improve all tested models on RiddleSense (e.g., Llama3-70B: 83.34%→85.21%, GPT-4: 88.83%→89.23%) and BrainTeaser (e.g., Llama3-70B overall: 87.76%→91.51% on Sentence, 71.20%→79.54% on Word), confirming that the benchmark's data and reasoning processes transfer to other lateral thinking tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the lateral thinking evaluation line (BrainTeaser, RiddleSense, Oogiri) by shifting from multiple-choice question answering to open-ended, multi-turn scenario deduction via situation puzzles. It departs from the dominant LLM-as-a-Judge paradigm (MT-Bench, Chatbot Arena) that requires a stronger evaluator model, instead proposing a player-judge framework where the judge only performs simple yes/no responses and semantic alignment checks. The 'eliciting' angle—using intermediate reasoning traces as auxiliary prompts to boost performance on other lateral thinking benchmarks—is a novel repurposing of the evaluation interaction as a training signal.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The player-judge framework offers a practical solution to the 'stronger evaluator' bottleneck that limits model-based evaluation of frontier LLMs, and the >80% human agreement validates its reliability. However, the eliciting gains are modest (~2% on RiddleSense, ~4% on BrainTeaser), and the benchmark's 975 web-crawled puzzles raise questions about coverage and contamination. Open questions include whether the framework scales to longer/more complex scenarios, whether the eliciting effect generalizes beyond closely related lateral thinking tasks, and whether the difficulty grading (crowd-sourced, ~55 annotators) is stable enough for fine-grained model comparison.
