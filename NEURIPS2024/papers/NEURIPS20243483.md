## Problem

Federated learning algorithms for nonconvex manifold-constrained optimization (e.g., PCA on the Stiefel manifold) are nearly absent from the literature. The sole prior method, RFedSVRG, relies on computationally expensive exponential/inverse exponential mappings and parallel transport for model averaging, and its convergence guarantees are restricted to either a single local update or single-client participation. This paper addresses the gap by designing an FL algorithm that handles heterogeneous data, multiple local updates, and full client participation under nonconvex manifold constraints.

## Input / Output

Each client i holds a local dataset D_i (e.g., a data matrix A_i ∈ R^{p×d} for kPCA, or a block of partial matrix observations for LRMC) and computes stochastic Riemannian gradients over mini-batches of size b. The model parameter x ∈ R^{d×k} is constrained to a compact smooth submanifold M (e.g., the Stiefel manifold St(d,k) = {x : x^T x = I_k}), and the global loss is a smooth nonconvex average of per-client losses. The output is the projected global model P_M(x^{R+1}) on the manifold, which for kPCA yields the top-k principal components and for LRMC yields the low-rank factor X.

## Method

The proposed algorithm replaces the expensive exponential-mapping-based averaging of RFedSVRG with a simple Euclidean projection operator P_M onto the manifold, which is 2-Lipschitz continuous within a γ-tube around M (guaranteed by proximal smoothness of compact submanifolds). Each client performs τ local updates using stochastic Riemannian gradients and a locally constructed correction term c_i^r that eliminates client drift via a variance-reduction mechanism (replacing stale per-client gradients with the current cross-client average), requiring no extra communication. The server fuses local models by averaging the accumulated variables and projecting back to M, ensuring feasibility of all global iterates at low cost. Theoretically, the paper establishes a sub-linear convergence rate of O(1/(√n · τ · R)) to a neighborhood of a first-order stationary point, with the neighborhood size governed by the stochastic gradient variance σ²/(nτb), under no data-similarity assumptions. The analysis exploits the manifold's curvature (via the normal inequality) and the Lipschitz continuity of P_M to derive new descent inequalities that bridge the federated iterate with a virtual centralized projected Riemannian gradient descent sequence.

## Results

The paper's main results are presented as convergence plots (Figs. 1–10) rather than a single numerical table. On kPCA with MNIST (d=784, k=2, n=10, τ=10), the proposed algorithm matches RFedSVRG in convergence rate per communication round but requires only 1 d×k matrix transmission per client per round versus 2 for RFedSVRG, yielding substantially lower communication quantity and run time. On low-rank matrix completion (d=100, k=2, T=1000, n=10), the proposed algorithm again matches RFedSVRG in per-round convergence but is faster in communication quantity and wall-clock time. Theoretically, the algorithm achieves a convergence rate of O(1/(√n·τ·R)) for τ>1 with full client participation, improving upon the O(1/R) (τ=1) and O(1/(τR)) (single-client) rates of RFedSVRG [16].

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against three baselines: RFedavg, RFedprox, and RFedSVRG. RFedavg and RFedprox both suffer from client drift under heterogeneous data and fail to converge to high accuracy on both kPCA and LRMC tasks, making them clear losers. RFedSVRG [16] is the strongest baseline and achieves comparable convergence per communication round to the proposed method on both tasks. However, the proposed algorithm decisively outperforms RFedSVRG in communication quantity (transmitting 1 d×k matrix per client per round vs. 2) and run time, because it avoids the expensive inverse exponential mapping, exponential mapping, and parallel transport that RFedSVRG requires for model averaging and correction-term placement. The proposed method also supports arbitrary τ>1 with full client participation, whereas RFedSVRG lacks theoretical guarantees in that regime. On the synthetic kPCA experiment (n=30, d=20, k=5), the proposed algorithm is slightly faster in communication rounds and substantially faster in communication quantity and run time compared to RFedSVRG.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the composite federated learning framework of Zhang, Hu, and Johansson (ICASSP 2024) [24], which handles proximal operators in Euclidean space, to the nonconvex manifold-constrained setting by replacing the proximal operator with a manifold projection operator and adapting the client-drift correction to the Riemannian geometry. The primary departure from the only prior FL algorithm for general manifold optimization, RFedSVRG [16], is the use of a simple Euclidean projection operator for server-side model averaging instead of the expensive inverse-exponential → average → exponential pipeline with parallel transport, eliminating both the additional communication of local gradients and the computational cost of parallel transport. The paper also distinguishes itself from decentralized manifold optimization methods [9, 28, 29] by supporting multiple local updates per communication round under data heterogeneity, a setting those methods cannot handle.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is one of the first FL algorithms to simultaneously support nonconvex manifold constraints, heterogeneous client data, full client participation, and multiple local updates, filling a gap where prior work [16] was restricted to either single-client participation or a single local update. The practical simplification of using closed-form projections (e.g., QR-based for the Stiefel manifold) instead of matrix exponentials and nonlinear equation solvers makes manifold FL substantially more deployable. Open questions include extending to partial client participation, manifolds lacking closed-form projections, and adaptive estimation of the proximal smoothness constant γ that governs step-size selection.
