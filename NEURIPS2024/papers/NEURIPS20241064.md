## Problem

Score Distillation Sampling (SDS) and its variants suffer from characteristic artifacts like oversaturation and oversmoothing that limit their utility across generation and translation tasks. The paper identifies that these artifacts arise from two specific errors: a first-order (linear) approximation of the optimal transport path between distributions, and a mismatch between the assumed source distribution (typically the unconditional image distribution) and the actual distribution of the current optimized image. No prior work had provided a unified framework explaining why different SDS variants fail in different ways.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt describing the target content and a parametric image (a NeRF, a painting latent, or a coarse 3D sketch mesh) as the source to be optimized. In generation tasks, it runs a two-stage schedule: the first stage uses standard SDS for a fixed number of iterations to establish geometry, then switches to the proposed bridge gradient for the remainder. In editing tasks (e.g., painting-to-real), the first stage is omitted since the initialization already resembles the source distribution. The source distribution is specified by appending fixed artifact descriptors (e.g., 'oversaturated, smooth pixelated, cartoon, foggy, hazy, blurry, bad structure, noisy, malformed') to the target prompt, with an optional domain descriptor like ', painting' for editing tasks.

## Method

The paper reformulates score distillation optimization as solving a Schrödinger Bridge Problem (SBP) between the current optimized image distribution (source) and the target natural image distribution, where the gradient is the difference between two denoising directions projected onto each distribution. Under this dual-bridge framework, the key insight is that SDS's use of the unconditional distribution as the source proxy is fundamentally wrong when the current image does not resemble a natural image. The proposed fix replaces the unconditional source term with a text-described source distribution: the gradient becomes ε_ours = w · (ε_φ(x_θ,t; y_tgt, t) − ε_φ(x_θ,t; y_src, t)), where y_src is the target prompt augmented with descriptors of the current image's artifacts. This requires no additional model training (unlike VSD's LoRA), no extra neural function evaluations, and uses the same computational budget as SDS while producing quality comparable to VSD. The paper further shows that replacing single-step denoising with full PF-ODE simulation (the 'full-path' approach) independently reduces the first-order approximation error, and that both error sources can be addressed simultaneously.

## Results

On zero-shot text-to-image generation with 5K COCO captions, the proposed method achieves a FID of 67.89 and CLIP FID of 20.31, substantially outperforming SDS (FID 86.02), NFSD (FID 91.70), and CSD (FID 89.96), while remaining 3.5× faster than VSD (FID 59.22) at the same 4.48 min/sample runtime as SDS. On text-guided NeRF optimization, the method achieves the highest CLIP similarity (ViT-L/14: 0.2848) among all compared methods, slightly exceeding VSD (0.2837) and SDS (0.2811). In a 37-user study, the proposed method was preferred over VSD or SDS in 75.7% of pairwise comparisons.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method decisively outperforms SDS, NFSD, and CSD on both FID and CLIP FID for text-to-image generation, closing most of the gap to VSD while matching SDS's 4.48 min/sample runtime (vs. VSD's 16.02 min). Against VSD, the method trades a modest FID increase (67.89 vs. 59.22) for a 3.5× speedup, making it the best quality-per-second option among all score distillation variants. On text-guided NeRF optimization, the method achieves the highest CLIP similarity across all three CLIP model sizes, narrowly beating VSD on ViT-L/14 (0.2848 vs. 0.2837) while matching it on ViT-B/16 (0.3282 vs. 0.3292). When applied as a drop-in replacement in Fantasia3D, Magic3D, and CSD pipelines, the two-stage optimization consistently improves visual quality and reduces oversaturation artifacts without changing optimization time. The method also demonstrates utility in painting-to-real translation and 3D sketch-to-real, where it produces more realistic colors and textures than SDS while maintaining structural faithfulness to the input.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reframes the entire family of Score Distillation Sampling methods (SDS, NFSD, CSD, VSD, DDS) as first-order approximations of a Schrödinger Bridge between the current optimized image distribution and the target natural image distribution, building directly on the Dual Diffusion Implicit Bridge formulation of Su et al. [65]. It departs from VSD's approach of training a LoRA adapter each iteration to estimate the source score by instead using a fixed set of text descriptors of SDS artifacts to condition the source denoising direction, eliminating the 200–300% computational overhead. The analysis unifies previously ad-hoc modifications (negative prompts in NFSD, classifier terms in CSD, reference-image deltas in DDS) under a single error decomposition: linear approximation error and source distribution mismatch.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified Schrödinger Bridge lens gives practitioners a principled diagnostic for why each SDS variant produces its characteristic artifacts, and the text-based source description is a drop-in replacement that narrows the quality gap to VSD at the same wall-clock cost as vanilla SDS. However, the paper acknowledges that neither single-step nor full-path simulation yet matches the quality and diversity of direct reverse-process sampling, and that full-path (multi-step PF-ODE) gradients destabilize 3D optimization despite improving 2D FID. Open questions include formulating idempotent gradients, automatically generating source descriptors via vision-language models, and extending the framework to video diffusion priors as they become the dominant generative backbone.
