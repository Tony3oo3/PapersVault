## Problem

Gradient-boosted decision trees (GBDTs) have long dominated tabular data benchmarks, but deep learning methods require extensive per-dataset hyperparameter optimization to be competitive, hindering practical adoption. This paper asks whether dataset-independent default parameters, tuned via meta-learning on a separate benchmark, can make both an improved MLP and GBDTs perform well without any per-dataset tuning, and whether a carefully designed MLP can close the accuracy gap with GBDTs at comparable training cost.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240837/cropped_page4_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes tabular datasets with 1K–500K samples, up to 10,000 features, and up to 7,019 categorical levels, containing both numerical and categorical columns. Rows with missing numerical values are removed and missing categorical values are encoded as a separate category. For classification (up to 355 classes), the output is a class label evaluated by classification error or 1-AUROC; for regression, the output is a continuous value evaluated by nRMSE. Categorical features with ≤8 distinct values are one-hot encoded (binary features mapped to {−1,1}), while higher-cardinality categories use learned embedding layers.

## Method

RealMLP is a 3-hidden-layer (256-neuron) MLP augmented with several components: robust scaling followed by smooth clipping (f(x)=x/√(1+(x/3)²)) for numerical preprocessing, PBLD periodic-bias-linear-DenseNet embeddings for numerical features, a learnable diagonal scaling layer for soft feature selection, neural tangent parametrization for input-dimension-aware learning rates, parametric SELU (classification) or Mish (regression) activations, and a multi-cycle cos_log₄ learning rate schedule with flat_cos-scheduled dropout and weight decay. Training uses AdamW (β₂=0.95), label smoothing (ε=0.1) for classification, data-dependent he+5 bias initialization, and best-epoch selection over 256 fixed epochs rather than early stopping. Separately, the paper meta-learns tuned default hyperparameters for XGBoost, LightGBM, and CatBoost on 118 meta-train datasets and validates them on 90 disjoint meta-test datasets, showing that simply selecting the best among tuned-default models (Best-TD) achieves a better time-accuracy tradeoff than 50-step per-dataset HPO.

## Results

Using the shifted geometric mean (SGM) error as the primary benchmark metric, RealMLP-TD achieves a 15.2% error reduction over MLP-D on the meta-test classification benchmark and 14.9% on meta-test regression (Table B.5). On the meta-train and meta-test benchmarks, both RealMLP-TD and RealTabR-D outperform all GBDT variants (XGBoost, LightGBM, CatBoost) in SGM error while remaining competitive in training time. On the Grinsztajn et al. (2022) benchmark, RealMLP is competitive with CatBoost for regression but underperforms it for classification, while TabR-HPO achieves the best overall score on that benchmark. Algorithm selection over tuned defaults (Best-TD) is faster on average than 50-step HPO while remaining competitive with the best HPO models, and ensemble selection adds only 0–3% improvement over best-model selection.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the MLP-PLR baseline (Gorishniy et al., NeurIPS 2022), RealMLP-TD delivers substantial improvements on all four benchmarks, with the gap widening on the meta-test set, confirming that the tuned defaults generalize well to unseen datasets. Compared to TabR-S (Gorishniy et al., ICLR 2024), RealMLP-TD wins on most benchmarks while RealTabR-D (which incorporates the paper's preprocessing, scaling layer, and PBLD embeddings into TabR) outperforms TabR-S-D on four of six benchmarks, particularly all regression benchmarks. Against the three GBDT libraries (XGBoost, LightGBM, CatBoost), RealMLP and RealTabR achieve lower SGM error on the meta-train and meta-test benchmarks, but on the Grinsztajn et al. (2022) benchmark CatBoost-TD still edges out RealMLP for classification while being comparable for regression. The meta-learned XGBoost defaults from Probst et al. (JMLR 2019) outperform the paper's XGB-TD on the meta-test classification benchmark, likely because their tuning benchmark is more similar to the meta-test set and they use more estimators (4168) and deeper trees. Among the Gorishniy et al. (NeurIPS 2021) baselines (MLP, ResNet, FT-Transformer), RealMLP-TD clearly dominates in both accuracy and training time, with FT-Transformer being notably slower on CPUs. The paper also shows that simply trying all tuned-default algorithms (Best-TD) provides a better time-accuracy tradeoff than running 50-step HPO on any single model family, supporting the AutoML portfolio approach over single-model hyperparameter search.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on improving MLPs for tabular data (Gorishniy et al.'s MLP, MLP-PLR, and TabR) by combining a 'bag of tricks'—robust scaling with smooth clipping, PBLD numerical embeddings, a learnable scaling layer, neural tangent parametrization, parametric activations, and cyclical learning rate schedules—into a single architecture (RealMLP) with meta-learned defaults. It also departs from the 'NNs vs. GBDTs' framing of Grinsztajn et al. (2022) and McElfresh et al. (2023) by showing that well-tuned defaults for both families, combined with simple model selection, can match or exceed per-dataset hyperparameter optimization. The meta-learning setup for finding defaults follows Vanschoren (2018) and Probst et al. (2019) but is applied jointly to neural and tree-based methods with a proper meta-train/meta-test split.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: practitioners get a ready-to-use MLP and improved GBDT defaults that require no per-dataset tuning, and the finding that selecting the best among several default models (Best-TD) is competitive with 50-step HPO challenges the assumption that extensive hyperparameter search is necessary for strong tabular performance. Limitations include restriction to 1K–500K samples, exclusion of datasets with missing numerical values, and a single train-validation split for HPO (making HPO potentially overfit). Open questions include whether these defaults generalize to very small datasets, distribution shifts, and metrics beyond classification error and nRMSE, and whether the 'NNs vs. GBDTs' gap narrows further as new architectures (ExcelFormer, CARTE, etc.) mature.
