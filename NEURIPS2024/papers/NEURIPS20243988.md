## Problem

Differentially private federated online prediction from experts (OPE) was largely unexplored, despite separate progress in DP online learning and federated learning. The central question is whether collaboration among m clients can yield regret speed-up under DP constraints, and the answer depends critically on the adversary type: stochastic adversaries permit speed-up, general oblivious adversaries do not, and a realizable oblivious setting does.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243988/cropped_page8_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system comprises m clients each selecting from d experts over T time steps, facing adversaries that generate convex, α-Lipschitz, β-smooth loss functions w.r.t. the L1 norm. Clients communicate noisy inner products of local gradient estimates with decision-set vertices to a central server (O(md log T) scalars for the stochastic case, O(mdT/N) for the oblivious case), and the server broadcasts a single global expert prediction per communication round. The output is a per-client regret bound explicitly parameterized by m, T, d, and DP parameters (ε, δ), achieving √m-fold speed-up under stochastic adversaries and m-fold speed-up under realizable oblivious adversaries.

## Method

Fed-DP-OPE-Stoch operates in geometrically growing phases where each client runs a local DP-FW tree-mechanism subroutine to estimate gradients of past loss functions, then communicates noisy inner products (Laplace noise calibrated per tree depth) to the server only at leaf vertices. The server aggregates noisy estimates across all clients to form a global expert prediction via arg-min over simplex vertices, and clients update their local selections via Frank-Wolfe-style convex combinations with step size 2/(k+1). This local privatization ensures DP for all exchanged information, not merely expert selections. For oblivious adversaries, a policy-reduction lower bound proves no speed-up is possible in general, but under a realizability assumption, Fed-SVT uses periodic communication with the Sparse-Vector technique—treating accumulated multi-client losses as a single loss instance—to achieve an m-fold speed-up that matches the lower bound up to logarithmic factors.

## Results

For stochastic adversaries, Fed-DP-OPE-Stoch achieves a per-client regret of O(√(T log d)/m + log d log T/(√m ε)) under ε-DP, representing a √m-fold speed-up over the single-player baseline, with communication cost O(md log T). For oblivious adversaries under the realizability assumption, Fed-SVT achieves per-client regret O((log T log d + log²d)/(mε) + N log d) under ε-DP, an m-fold speed-up over the single-player Sparse-Vector algorithm, with communication cost O(mdT/N). A lower bound of Ω(log d/(mε)) confirms Fed-SVT is nearly optimal up to logarithmic factors. For general oblivious adversaries, a lower bound of Ω(min(log d/ε, T)) shows no regret speed-up is possible from collaboration.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the single-player Limited Updates algorithm (Asi et al., 2022b) for stochastic adversaries, Fed-DP-OPE-Stoch achieves a √m-fold reduction in per-client regret (e.g., from O(√(T log d) + log d log T/ε) to O(√(T log d)/m + log d log T/(√m ε))) while maintaining logarithmic communication cost O(md log T). In experiments (m=10, T=2^14, ε=10, d=100), Fed-DP-OPE-Stoch significantly outperforms Limited Updates in per-client cumulative regret. Against the single-player Sparse-Vector algorithm (Asi et al., 2023) for oblivious adversaries under realizability, Fed-SVT achieves an m-fold regret speed-up (e.g., from O((log T log d + log²d)/ε) to O((log T log d + log²d)/(mε) + N log d)). In experiments (m=10, T=2^9, ε=10, d=100), Fed-SVT significantly outperforms Sparse-Vector, with the advantage persisting even at lower communication costs (N=50). The paper also establishes that for general oblivious adversaries, no federated algorithm can beat the single-player lower bound, providing a clear separation from the stochastic and realizable settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the single-player differentially private online prediction from experts (OPE) framework of Asi et al. (2022b, 2023) to the federated setting, adapting the DP-FW tree mechanism (Asi et al., 2021b) into a local-estimation-plus-local-privatization protocol to reduce communication. It departs from prior federated online learning work (Mitra et al., 2021; Park et al., 2022; Kwon et al., 2023), which did not incorporate differential privacy, and from prior DP online learning, which was exclusively single-player. The paper introduces a 'policy reduction' technique for lower bounds that reduces the federated problem to a single-player one, a methodological contribution not present in either the federated or DP online learning literatures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes a fundamental separation: collaboration among clients yields regret speed-up under stochastic adversaries (√m-fold) but provably does not help against general oblivious adversaries, challenging the core assumption that more clients always improve adversarial online learning. Under a realizability assumption, an m-fold speed-up is recovered, delineating precisely when federation is beneficial. A key limitation is the absence of experiments on real-world federated scenarios (recommender systems, healthcare); the authors explicitly flag this as future work. Open questions include whether the negative result for oblivious adversaries extends to other federated online learning problems (convex optimization, bandits) and whether intermediate adversary models (e.g., partially oblivious) admit partial speed-ups.
