## Problem

Prod, a simple multiplicative-weights algorithm for full-information online learning, was conjectured to be fundamentally sub-optimal for adversarial multi-armed bandits, with the best known Prod-based bandit algorithm (WSU-UX) achieving only T^{2/3} regret and lower bounds suggesting a hard separation from the O(√(KT)) rate achievable by OMD/FTRL methods. This paper disproves that separation conjecture by showing that Prod variants, when properly tuned with second-order corrections, can match the optimal bandit regret rate while retaining the simple arithmetic update rule that makes Prod attractive for incentive-compatible learning.

## Input / Output

The algorithms receive only bandit feedback: in each round the learner selects an arm A_t ~ π_t and observes only the scalar loss ℓ_{t,A_t} ∈ [0,1], never the losses of unselected arms. The output is a probability distribution π_{t+1} over K arms updated via a single multiplicative (product) rule per arm, requiring no optimization solve. The theoretical output is a regret bound: O(√(KT log K)) for adversarial losses, O(√(KT log T)) for the importance-weighting-free variant, and O(log T / Δ_i) in the stochastic regime for the best-of-both-worlds variant.

## Method

The core insight is interpreting Prod as a first-order Taylor approximation of the OMD (or FTRL) update under a chosen Bregman divergence, and then correcting the resulting second-order error via loss biasing. For the KL/negative-entropy potential (WSU-UX), the bias δ_{ti} = ηℓ_{ti}/π̃_{ti} is added to the losses so that the change-of-measure term cancels the uncontrolled ηℓ̂²_{t,i*} in the regret decomposition, yielding O(√(KT log K)) regret. For the Logbarrier potential, the linearization naturally multiplies the importance-weighted loss by π_{t,i}, so the algorithm operates directly on the masked (non-weighted) loss ℓ_{t,i}·𝕀(A_t=i) with a non-symmetric normalization, making it arguably importance-weighting free. For the 1/2-Tsallis potential, the FTRL update is linearized with a correction of order η/√π_{t,i}·(1−π_{t,i}), producing TS-Prod which achieves best-of-both-worlds guarantees (O(√(KT)) adversarial, O(log T/Δ_i) stochastic) and is shown equivalent to the dual stabilized OMD of Fang et al. (2022).

## Results

The paper establishes three main theoretical regret bounds for Prod-family bandit algorithms. The biased WSU-UX (Theorem 1) achieves O(√(KT log(K))) regret, improving over the prior T^{2/3} bound for WSU-UX. LB-Prod (Theorem 2) achieves O(√(KT log(T))) regret using only masked losses (no importance weighting) with a simple arithmetic update. TS-Prod (Theorem 3) achieves best-of-both-worlds guarantees: O(√(KT) + K log(T)) in the adversarial setting and O(Σ_{i≠i*} log(T)/Δ_i) in the stochastic setting, matching the optimal rates of Tsallis-INF while retaining the simple Prod update structure.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper is purely theoretical with no empirical baselines, but the comparisons are against prior algorithmic guarantees. Biased WSU-UX improves the regret of the original WSU-UX from T^{2/3} to O(√(KT log K)), matching the rate of EXP3 while retaining the simple product update and incentive-compatibility properties. LB-Prod matches EXP3's rate (up to a log T vs log K factor) but eliminates the need for importance weighting entirely, using only the observed masked loss. TS-Prod matches the best-of-both-worlds rates of Tsallis-INF (O(√(KT)) adversarial, O(Σ log(T)/Δ_i) stochastic) but replaces the FTRL optimization step with a closed-form product update. All three new algorithms also directly improve the state of the art for incentive-compatible bandits, which was previously stuck at T^{2/3} regret.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions the Prod family of algorithms (simple multiplicative weight updates from Cesa-Bianchi et al. 2007) as first-order approximations to OMD/FTRL updates, a connection that had been noted but not exploited for bandit feedback. It builds directly on WSU-UX (Freeman et al. 2020), the first Prod-based bandit algorithm, which was shown to be fundamentally limited to T^{2/3} regret by lower bounds (Mortazavi et al. 2024), leading to a conjecture of a hard separation between full-information Prod and bandit settings. The paper also draws on the Logbarrier potential (Agarwal et al. 2017) and Tsallis-INF (Zimmert and Seldin 2021) to derive LB-Prod and TS-Prod, and connects TS-Prod to stabilized OMD (Fang et al. 2022). The key departure is showing that a minimal loss-biasing correction to WSU-UX, interpretable as a second-order Taylor correction to the Hedge update, is sufficient to recover optimal √(KT) regret.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper resolves an open conjecture that Prod-type algorithms are fundamentally suboptimal for bandits, showing that the gap was an artifact of the specific WSU-UX analysis rather than an inherent limitation. This has direct practical implications for incentive-compatible online learning, where Prod updates are the only known class guaranteeing truthful expert reporting, immediately improving the state of the art from T^{2/3} to √(KT log K) regret. The algorithms require only basic arithmetic (no optimization solving), making them attractive for deployment. Open questions include whether the linearization framework extends to more sophisticated settings (second-order bounds, path-norm bounds, graph feedback) and whether the second-order correction principle generalizes to other potential functions.
