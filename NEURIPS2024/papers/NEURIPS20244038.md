## Problem

Mamba achieves strong vision performance with linear complexity, while linear attention Transformers with the same O(N) complexity have long been considered inferior. Despite this performance gap, the two mechanisms share a surprisingly close mathematical structure. The paper asks: which specific design choices within Mamba's selective state space model are actually responsible for its superiority over linear attention?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20244038/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes standard RGB images and produces class labels (ImageNet-1K, 50K validation images), bounding boxes with instance masks (COCO, 118K training images at 1280×800), and pixel-level semantic segmentation masks (ADE20K, 25K images at 512×2048). Internally, the 2D image feature maps are flattened into 1D sequences for the linear attention computation, and the hierarchical 4-stage architecture progressively downsamples spatial resolution from 56×56 to 7×7 while expanding channel dimensions from 64 to 512–768 depending on model size.

## Method

The paper first unifies the selective SSM and linear attention formulations, revealing that Mamba is essentially single-head linear attention with six modifications: an input gate (Δᵢ), a forget gate (Ãᵢ), a learnable shortcut (D⊙xᵢ), no attention normalization, single-head design, and a modified block architecture. Ablation experiments on a Swin-based baseline identify the forget gate (+0.8 Top-1) and Mamba's block design (+1.8 to +3.3 Top-1) as the two core contributors, while the other four designs are marginal or harmful. The proposed MILA model replaces the recurrent forget gate—which forces sequential computation and restricts receptive field—with parallelizable positional encodings (LePE for local bias, CPE for input-dependent position, RoPE for global position), and adopts Mamba's block design (depth-wise convolution, gating, and expanded MLP) around a standard parallel linear attention sub-block. This yields a fully parallelizable architecture that outperforms VMamba, LocalVMamba, and other vision Mamba models across classification, detection, and segmentation while achieving 1.5–4.5× faster inference.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K, MILA-T achieves 83.5% top-1 accuracy with 25M parameters and 4.2G FLOPs, outperforming all vision Mamba models at comparable scale (VMamba-T: 82.5%, LocalVMamba-T: 82.7%). At the larger scale, MILA-B reaches 85.3% top-1, surpassing VMamba-B (83.9%) by 1.4 points and Mamba2D-B (83.0%) by 2.3 points. On COCO object detection, MILA-B achieves 50.5 AP^b and 45.0 AP^m, and on ADE20K semantic segmentation, MILA-B achieves 51.9 mIoU (single-scale) and 52.5 mIoU (multi-scale), both leading among Mamba-based backbones.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MILA consistently outperforms all vision Mamba baselines across all three model scales on ImageNet-1K: MILA-T (83.5%) beats VMamba-T (82.5%) by 1.0, LocalVMamba-T (82.7%) by 0.8, and Mamba2D-S (81.7%) by 1.8; MILA-S (84.4%) surpasses VMamba-S (83.6%) by 0.8 and LocalVMamba-S (83.7%) by 0.7; MILA-B (85.3%) leads VMamba-B (83.9%) by 1.4 and Mamba2D-B (83.0%) by 2.3. MILA also outperforms the MambaOut family (which removes selective SSM in favor of gated convolution) by 0.8 (T), 0.3 (S), and 1.1 (B) points, confirming that the gains come from the specific Mamba-inspired designs rather than simply removing SSM. Among Transformer baselines, MILA-T exceeds NAT-T (83.2%) by 0.3 and CSwin-T (82.7%) by 0.8, while MILA-B surpasses NAT-B (84.3%) by 1.0. On COCO, MILA-B achieves 50.5 AP^b, beating VMamba-B (49.2) by 1.3 and MambaOut-B (47.4) by 3.1. On ADE20K, MILA-B reaches 52.5 mIoU (multi-scale), exceeding VMamba-B (51.6) by 0.9 and MambaOut-B (51.0) by 1.5.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously disconnected lines of work: linear attention Transformers (Katharopoulos et al., ICML 2020), which were widely regarded as underperforming, and Mamba's selective state space model (Gu & Dao, 2023), which achieved strong results with linear complexity. By reformulating both within a unified algebraic framework, the authors show Mamba is essentially single-head linear attention augmented with an input gate, forget gate, shortcut, and a modified block design. The paper then departs from prior vision Mamba work (VMamba, LocalMamba, MambaOut) by arguing that the recurrent forget gate is unnecessary for non-causal vision data and can be replaced by standard positional encodings (LePE, RoPE, CPE), yielding the parallelizable MILA architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that Mamba's superiority over linear attention reduces to just two design choices (forget gate and block design) provides a clear, actionable recipe for building efficient vision backbones without recurrence. Practically, MILA outperforms all compared vision Mamba models across classification, detection, and segmentation while being 1.5–4.5× faster at inference due to fully parallelizable computation. Open questions include whether the forget-gate-as-positional-encoding substitution generalizes to causal NLP settings, whether the analysis extends to other SSM variants (e.g., Mamba-2, H3), and whether the six-design decomposition captures all relevant differences or misses implementation-level details the authors acknowledge in their limitations.
