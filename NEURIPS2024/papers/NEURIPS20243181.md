## Problem

In interactive decision-making with anonymous delayed rewards, the learner cannot attribute observed rewards to the decisions that generated them, and the delay distribution models used for attribution may be misspecified. The paper asks whether a consistent (sub-polynomial regret) algorithm can be robust to any level of delay model misspecification, and characterizes precisely when this is possible. The central finding is that the Graves-Lai constant being zero — the same condition that enables bounded regret without delays — is both necessary for any delay robustness and, for linear problems, sufficient for achieving bounded regret without knowing delay models at all.

## Input / Output

At each round, the learner observes a context or state o_k and a set R_k of reward arrivals whose provenance is unknown (anonymous feedback); the learner does not observe the delay d_k or which past decision produced each reward. The model class F is known a priori (realizability assumption), but the ground-truth f* within F is unknown. The algorithm outputs a decision (arm or policy) per round, and the guarantee is a bounded (O(1)) expected regret that holds regardless of the true delay distributions, provided the Graves-Lai constant vanishes for all instances in F.

## Method

The paper establishes a negative result (Theorem 4.1) showing that epsilon-contamination of delay models induces delta-contamination of reward distributions, which breaks consistency unless C(f*)=0, under mild regularity assumptions on reward families (Assumptions 4.2–4.5). For the positive result, the authors introduce the Simply-Test-to-Commit (ST2C) algorithm, which sequentially tests candidate models in F by computing log-likelihood ratios of observed rewards under the assumption that all arrivals come from the current candidate's optimal action, and commits to a new model when the accumulated evidence exceeds a threshold scaled by max-contamination. The key enabling condition is cross-informativeness (Assumption 4.7): the KL divergence between any two distinct models under their respective optimal actions grows faster than log n, which is implied by C(f)=0 for all f in F. For contextual linear bandits and linear MDPs with Bellman closure, this cross-informativeness reduces to a spanning condition on the feature vectors of optimal actions, yielding a clean a-priori checkable criterion that simultaneously guarantees bounded regret and full delay robustness.

## Results

The paper establishes two main theoretical results. The negative result (Theorem 4.1) shows that no consistent algorithm can be ε-delay robust (for any ε > 0) unless the Graves-Lai constant C(f*) = 0. The positive result (Theorem 4.11) shows that the ST2C algorithm achieves bounded regret of Δ(1 + 5·4c⁴e⁻²/W(2c²)²)·π²/6 without any knowledge of the delay distribution model, under the cross-informativeness assumption. For linear problems (contextual linear bandits and linear MDPs with Bellman closure), the condition C(f) = 0 for all f ∈ F is shown to be both necessary and sufficient for achieving bounded regret under any level of anonymous delayed reward misspecification (Corollaries 5.3 and 5.7).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no experimental baselines or empirical comparisons. The key theoretical 'comparison' is between the delay robustness requirement and the bounded regret requirement: the paper proves they are governed by the same condition (C(f) = 0) in linear settings. Specifically, the negative result extends the instance-optimality lower bound of Dong and Ma [2] and Graves-Lai [9] to show that even infinitesimal delay model misspecification (ε-contamination) destroys consistency unless C(f*) = 0. The positive result extends the bounded regret characterizations of Hao et al. [10] (contextual linear bandits) and Papini et al. [11] (linear MDPs) to the anonymous delayed reward setting, showing the same spanning conditions that guarantee bounded regret without delays also guarantee bounded regret with arbitrary delay model misspecification. The ST2C algorithm is the first to achieve this 'best of both worlds' property without requiring knowledge of delay distributions, unlike prior work [4] which requires exact knowledge of delay means.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of the DMSO (Decision-Making with Structured Observations) framework from Wagenmaker & Foster [1] and Dong & Ma [2], the Graves-Lai instance-optimality theory [9], and the delayed anonymous feedback (DAAF) setting introduced by Pike-Burke et al. [4]. It departs from prior DAAF work (which required precise knowledge of delay means or non-anonymous feedback) by studying robustness to arbitrary ε-contamination of delay distributions, and from prior instance-optimality work (which assumed no delays) by showing how the Graves-Lai constant C(f) governs both bounded regret and delay robustness simultaneously. The positive result for linear problems extends the bounded-regret characterizations of Hao et al. [10] and Papini et al. [11] to the anonymous delayed reward setting via a new ST2C algorithm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The near-equivalence between C(f)=0 (bounded regret) and ε-delay robustness provides a clear design principle: if you can achieve bounded regret in the no-delay setting, you automatically get robustness to any level of delay model misspecification in linear problems. The strong negative result (no consistent algorithm survives any nonzero ε-contamination when C(f)≠0) cautions against pursuing delay-robust algorithms in settings where bounded regret is infeasible. A key open question, noted by the authors, is whether the positive result extends beyond linear problems to general DMSO instances, which would significantly broaden the practical applicability of the ST2C algorithm.
