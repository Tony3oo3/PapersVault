## Problem

Existing programming feedback generation systems rely on cloud-hosted LLMs (e.g., GPT-4), incurring per-request costs of $0.01–0.1, latencies exceeding 30 seconds, and data-privacy risks from sending learner code to external servers. No prior work has benchmarked language models for this task across the full set of real-world deployment criteria—quality, cost, time, and data privacy—nor explored whether in-browser inference can make such systems viable at scale.

## Input / Output

The technique consumes a programming task T (natural-language specification with test suite Ω_T), a learner's buggy Python program P_b, and the subset of failing test cases ω_{T,P_b}. It produces a concise natural-language hint H that must satisfy four binary quality attributes (correct, informative, concealing, comprehensible) without revealing the fix, along with an intermediate repaired program P_r selected from n_r candidates by test-suite pass rate and minimum token-edit distance to P_b, and a chain-of-thought explanation X used during hint generation.

## Method

The paper introduces a fine-tuning pipeline that generates synthetic training data by prompting GPT-4 to produce buggy programs (mimicking real learner errors), then repairs, explanations, and hints for each; these are assembled into four instance types per tuple and used for LoRA-based supervised fine-tuning of Llama-3-8B and Phi-3-3.8B. The fine-tuned models are then quantized to 4-bit and converted for the WebLLM in-browser inference engine, which leverages WebGPU for execution entirely within the user's browser alongside Pyodide for in-browser Python execution. The feedback generation technique itself follows a repair-then-hint pipeline: the model generates n_r candidate repairs (temperature t_r=0.7), the best repair is selected by passing the full test suite and minimizing Levenshtein token-edit distance to P_b, and a second query (temperature t_h=0.1) produces the hint with chain-of-thought reasoning. This 'hints-in-browser' workflow eliminates per-request monetary cost, keeps all learner data local, and achieves competitive inference times (20–70 s on consumer GPUs) while fine-tuned Llama-3-8B-dom-web surpasses GPT-3.5 and approaches GPT-4o-mini on hint quality.

## Results

On the aggregated HGood% metric (all four hint quality attributes satisfied), fine-tuned Llama-3-8B-dom achieves 77(10)%, surpassing GPT-4o-mini (72%) and GPT-3.5 (34%), while GPT-4 leads at 89(7)%. After 4-bit quantization for in-browser deployment, Llama-3-8B-dom-web retains 64(10)% HGood, still well above GPT-3.5, whereas Phi-3-3.8B-dom-web drops to 36(14)%. For program repair, Llama-3-8B-dom achieves 90–100% RPass across domains, matching or exceeding GPT-4 on INTROPYNUS (100% vs. 97%). Inference times for the in-browser workflow range from 20 s (Phi-3-3.8B-dom-web on M3 Max) to 71 s (Llama-3-8B-dom-web on M1 Pro), competitive with server-based GPT-4o mini (15 s) and GPT-4 (34 s) at zero per-request cost.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GPT-4 (89% HGood), the best in-browser model Llama-3-8B-dom-web (64%) trails by 25 points, but the non-web fine-tuned Llama-3-8B-dom (77%) narrows the gap to 12 points and matches GPT-4 on INTROPYNUS RPass (100% vs. 97%). Against GPT-4o-mini (72%), Llama-3-8B-dom (77%) wins on all three domains, and Llama-3-8B-dom-web (64%) remains competitive on INTROPYNUS (84% vs. 88%). Against GPT-3.5 (34%), both fine-tuned in-browser models substantially outperform: Llama-3-8B-dom-web leads by 30 points and Phi-3-3.8B-dom-web by 2 points. The fine-tuning pipeline itself is the largest single gain: Llama-3-8B improves from 36% to 77% HGood (+41 pts) and Phi-3-3.8B from 27% to 51% (+24 pts). Web conversion (16-bit → 4-bit) costs Llama-3-8B-dom 13 points (77→64) but costs Phi-3-3.8B-dom 15 points (51→36), indicating Llama-3-8B is more robust to quantization for this task.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the programming feedback generation pipeline established by Phung et al. (ICER 2023, LAK 2024), which introduced the repair-then-hint technique and quality metrics (HGood, RPass, REdit) for benchmarking LLMs against human tutors. The key departure is shifting the evaluation lens from quality alone to a multi-criteria benchmark spanning cost, inference time, and data privacy, motivated by the practical infeasibility of server-based GPT-4 deployments at scale. The paper also introduces a fine-tuning pipeline using GPT-4 synthetic data to close the quality gap of small open-access models (Llama-3-8B, Phi-3-3.8B) and positions in-browser inference via WebLLM/WebGPU as a new deployment paradigm that eliminates external data transmission entirely.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work provides the first systematic benchmark showing that in-browser LLM inference is practically viable for programming feedback generation, with inference times (20–70 s) competitive with server-based workflows on GPU-equipped consumer hardware, while offering zero running cost and full data privacy. The finding that fine-tuned Llama-3-8B-dom surpasses GPT-4o-mini across all three domains suggests that domain-specific fine-tuning can make small models competitive with much larger proprietary ones for educational feedback. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond Python, how it performs on mobile/edge hardware (e.g., Llama-3.2), how it compares to ONNX Runtime, and what the energy/sustainability profile looks like relative to cloud-based inference.
