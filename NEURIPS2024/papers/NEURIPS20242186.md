## Problem

Existing pixel-level classification frameworks for tubular structure extraction (e.g., retinal vessels, road networks) cannot effectively capture the complex topological nature of tubular objects, leading to persistent broken branches, redundant branches, and topological errors. Pixel-level loss functions like softDice and Focal Loss are particularly ineffective at addressing these structural errors because they lack explicit modeling of the branch-level connectivity that defines tubular structures.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242186/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images (384×384 crops for medical datasets, 768×768 for roads) and internally samples R=64 ROIs of size H×H (32×32 or 48×48) from multi-scale segmentation network features via ROI Align. It produces three outputs: a graph G defined by node coordinates V and a binary adjacency matrix A, a single-pixel-width centerline mask M generated by the Morph Module, and a refined segmentation mask obtained by iteratively dilating M within the thresholded segmentation boundaries. The centerline mask enforces the skeleton property (all interior path points have exactly 2 neighbors).

## Method

GraphMorph comprises a Graph Decoder and a training-free Morph Module. The Graph Decoder uses a modified Deformable DETR (coordinate head replacing box head, 3-layer encoder, 6-layer decoder) to detect junction and endpoint nodes, followed by a dynamic link prediction module where ConditionMLP generates per-node linear weights and ValueMLP maps queries to a value space, yielding the adjacency matrix in O(P×C²) time versus O(P²×C²) for RelationFormer's [rln]-token approach. The Morph Module applies a novel SkeletonDijkstra algorithm that finds minimum-cost paths between each pair of connected nodes on the centerline probability map while constraining all intermediate path points to have exactly 2 neighbors (skeleton property) and discarding paths whose average cost exceeds a threshold p_thresh. Training jointly optimizes pixel-wise losses (softDice + clDice), Hungarian bipartite matching losses for node coordinates, and weighted-BCE losses for adjacency matrices. For segmentation, a post-processing strategy iteratively dilates the topologically accurate centerline mask within the segmentation boundary to suppress isolated false-positive regions.

## Results

On the segmentation task, GraphMorph (softDice+Ours) achieves Dice of 0.8238 on DRIVE, 0.8216 on ISBI12, 0.8210 on STARE, and 0.7849 on MassRoad, consistently outperforming all baselines. The most striking gains are in topological metrics: β₀ error drops from 1.191 (softDice) to 0.692 on DRIVE, from 0.569 to 0.361 on ISBI12, and from 0.479 to 0.386 on MassRoad. On the centerline extraction task, the Morph Module reduces β₀ error by 74–76% compared to softDice alone (e.g., 2.169→0.555 on DRIVE, 4.045→0.665 on ISBI12). The method also extends to 3D, improving Dice from 0.7968 to 0.8196 and β₀ error from 1.087 to 0.536 on the PARSE pulmonary artery dataset.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GraphMorph outperforms all eight baselines across every dataset and every metric. Against the strongest pixel-level baselines, the gains are most pronounced in topological metrics: on DRIVE, β₀ error drops from 0.821 (TopoLoss) and 0.910 (clDice) to 0.692, while on ISBI12 it drops from 0.422 (clDice) to 0.361. PointScatter, the previous SOTA for tubular structure extraction, is beaten by a large margin on topological metrics (e.g., β₀ error 1.360 vs. 0.692 on DRIVE; 0.456 vs. 0.361 on ISBI12) while also matching or exceeding its volumetric scores. DSCNet and TransUNet, which use novel architectures (dynamic snake convolution, transformer encoder), are consistently outperformed on both volumetric and topological metrics. The method also surpasses LinkNet34 and D-Linknet34 on MassRoad, achieving the best Dice (0.7849) and lowest β₀ error (0.386) among all compared methods. Notably, the improvements are consistent across three different backbone networks (UNet, ResUNet, CS-Net), confirming that the gains stem from the graph-based branch-level learning rather than a specific architecture.

## Relation to Prior Work

GraphMorph departs from the dominant pixel-level classification paradigm for tubular structure extraction (softDice, clDice, TopoLoss) by explicitly predicting branch-level topology as a graph, then morphing that graph into a centerline mask. It builds on Deformable DETR for node detection (replacing the box head with a coordinate head) and on Dijkstra's algorithm (adapted into SkeletonDijkstra to enforce single-pixel-width paths). Compared to image-to-graph methods like RelationFormer, it uses only junctions/endpoints as nodes, introduces the Morph Module to handle curved tubular geometry, and replaces the O(P²) [rln]-token with a dynamic O(P) link prediction module for efficiency.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Topological fidelity is critical in clinical diagnosis (e.g., detecting broken or redundant vessel branches) and urban planning, yet pixel-level losses have been shown to be fundamentally limited at capturing it. GraphMorph's modular design (pluggable into any segmentation backbone) and the simple post-processing strategy make it practically adoptable, while the dramatic β₀-error reductions (e.g., 2.17→0.56 on DRIVE) validate the graph-based approach. Open questions remain: the segmentation task still relies on a post-processing dilation step rather than directly consuming the predicted graph, the sliding-window inference may miss global context, and the 3D extension (PARSE) is only preliminary. Future work on eliminating the post-processing dependency and scaling to larger fields of view would strengthen the method's clinical applicability.
