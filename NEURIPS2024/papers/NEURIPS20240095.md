## Problem

Detecting text generated by recent powerful aligned LLMs (e.g., GPT-4, Claude) remains challenging because prior detectors either overfit to specific LLMs or target non-aligned models. The paper identifies that alignment training (RLHF) imposes a common signature across aligned LLMs: they generate texts with higher predicted human-preference scores than human-written texts, a property that prior detection methods have not exploited.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240095/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The detector takes a context (prompt) x and a response text y as input and outputs a single scalar reward score r_φ(x,y) from a ~500M-parameter DeBERTa-based reward model; a higher score indicates the text is more likely LLM-generated. Unlike perturbation-based methods (DetectGPT, Fast-DetectGPT) that require 100+ forward passes per sample, ReMoDetect needs only one forward pass, making it 7.2× faster than the prior best method. The same single trained model is applied across all six text domains and twelve aligned LLMs without per-domain or per-model retraining.

## Method

ReMoDetect builds on the observation that a pre-trained Bradley-Terry reward model already assigns higher scores to aligned LGTs than to human-written texts, and enhances this separation through two training components. First, continual preference fine-tuning further pushes the reward model to prefer LGTs over human texts, while an L2 replay-regularization term (λ=0.01) constrains the model's predictions on the original training buffer to prevent overfitting to specific LLMs. Second, the method generates Human/LLM mixed texts by partially rephrasing 50% of sentences in human responses using Llama3-70B-Instruct, creating median-preference samples (y_LM ≻ y_MIX ≻ y_HU) that act as near-decision-boundary data; the final loss adds Bradley-Terry terms for both (y_MIX, y_HU) and (y_LM, y_MIX) pairs with weight β=0.3 each. At inference, the reward score alone serves as the detection criterion, requiring no perturbation or ensemble.

## Results

On the Fast-DetectGPT benchmark (PubMed, XSum, WritingPrompts-small), ReMoDetect achieves 95.8% average AUROC across six aligned LLMs, improving over the prior best Fast-DetectGPT (91.9%) by 3.9 points. On MGTBench (Essay, Reuters, WritingPrompts), ReMoDetect reaches 99.9% average AUROC versus 94.9% for Fast-DetectGPT. For the hardest detection targets, ReMoDetect improves GPT-4 detection from 90.6% to 97.9% and Claude3 Opus from 92.6% to 98.6% average AUROC. Inference is also 7.2× faster than Fast-DetectGPT (8.7 s vs. 62.7 s for 300 samples) using a 0.5B-parameter model versus 6B.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ReMoDetect outperforms all zero-shot baselines (Log-likelihood, Rank, DetectGPT, LRR, NPR, Fast-DetectGPT) by a wide margin across every model and domain; the gap is largest on GPT-4, where Fast-DetectGPT scores 90.6% average AUROC versus ReMoDetect's 97.9%. Supervised detectors (OpenAI-Detector, ChatGPT-Detector) perform poorly on unseen LLMs—ChatGPT-Detector drops to 24.1% AUROC on unseen Claude3 Opus + WritingPrompts-small—while ReMoDetect retains 99.5%, confirming the replay-buffer regularization prevents overfitting. Against the commercial GPTZero API, ReMoDetect wins on 5 of 6 aligned LLMs (e.g., 97.9% vs. 88.5% for GPT-4; 98.6% vs. 95.7% for Claude3 Opus), with the sole exception being Gemini pro where the two are nearly tied (82.4% vs. 82.9%). On the MGTBench, ReMoDetect achieves near-perfect 99.9% average AUROC, surpassing Fast-DetectGPT (94.9%) and Log-likelihood (95.2%) by 5+ points. ReMoDetect also demonstrates superior robustness to rephrasing attacks (T5-3B), with only a 4.4–7.4% relative AUROC drop versus 8.3–37.0% for other methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the RLHF reward model (Ouyang et al., 2022) as a detection instrument, building on the Bradley-Terry preference modeling framework but applying it to a fundamentally different task: distinguishing aligned LLM outputs from human text. It departs from the dominant zero-shot detection paradigm (DetectGPT, Fast-DetectGPT, NPR) that measures probability curvature under perturbation, and from supervised binary classifiers (OpenAI-Detector, ChatGPT-Detector) that risk overfitting to specific LLMs. The continual fine-tuning component adapts experience replay (Rolnick et al., 2019) to the reward model setting, while the mixed-text rephrasing draws on near-decision-boundary sample generation from OOD detection literature. Unlike Panickssery et al. (2024), who showed LLM evaluators prefer self-generated text, this work generalizes to detecting multiple aligned LLMs with a single reward model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical efficiency is striking: a 0.5B-parameter model requiring a single forward pass outperforms 6B+ multi-forward methods by a large margin, making aligned LGT detection far more deployable. The conceptual finding that alignment training causes LLMs to *overshoot* human preference (generating texts the reward model scores higher than actual human writing) provides a new mechanistic understanding of alignment's side effects. Key limitations include dependence on the availability of open-source reward models (still limited compared to open-source LLMs) and somewhat weaker performance on expert domains like PubMed and on Gemini. Open questions include whether the reward model's robustness to rephrasing attacks can be formalized, and whether the training objective could be inverted to make LLMs generate more human-like (less over-aligned) text.
