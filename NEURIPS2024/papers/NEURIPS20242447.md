## Problem

Ensemble-based models (OSE, WSE) for fine-tuning zero-shot vision-language models like CLIP still exhibit ID-OOD trade-offs: the optimal mixing coefficient for in-distribution accuracy differs from that for out-of-distribution accuracy, so no single constant weight simultaneously maximizes both. This paper asks whether a sample-wise ensembling strategy can eliminate this trade-off and attain peak ID and OOD accuracy simultaneously.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242447/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image as input and produces a class label over K categories. At inference, the image is encoded by both the zero-shot CLIP model and the fine-tuned model to obtain l2-normalized feature vectors; the k-NN distance from the fine-tuned feature to a precomputed Zero-Shot Failure set (bounded in [0,2]) determines a per-sample weight in (0,1). The final prediction is a convex combination of the two models' softmax outputs, so the output is a standard probability vector over classes, but the weighting is adaptive per sample rather than a fixed global coefficient.

## Method

VRF constructs a Zero-Shot Failure (ZSF) set from training samples where the fine-tuned model predicts correctly but the zero-shot model fails, storing their fine-tuned feature representations. For each test sample, it computes the k-NN l2 distance to this ZSF set and maps it through a sigmoid function ω(x) = σ(−(d(x)−a)/b) to obtain a sample-wise weight. The final prediction is ω(x)·P_ft(y|x) + (1−ω(x))·P_zs(y|x), assigning higher weight to the fine-tuned model when the sample is close to the ZSF set. The theoretical justification shows that this weighting minimizes the ensemble's residual variance V[η_vrf(x)] = g_zs²·V[η_zs] + g_ft²·V[η_ft], with the optimal weight proportional to Acc_ft/Acc_zs, which empirically decreases monotonically with d(x). This sample-wise approach outperforms constant-weight ensembles by 1.5–2.0 pp on OOD accuracy while maintaining or improving ID accuracy across ImageNet shifts, CIFAR-10→STL-10, and Entity-30.

## Results

On ImageNet (ID) and five derived distribution shifts (OOD) with CLIP ViT-B/16, VRF applied to E2E-FT achieves 82.3% ID accuracy and 61.8% average OOD accuracy, improving OOD by +1.6 pp over the best ensemble baseline (WSE at 60.6%) while matching ID accuracy. For LP-FT, VRF reaches 82.1% ID and 62.4% average OOD, a +1.5 pp OOD gain over WSE (61.2%). On CIFAR-10→STL-10, VRF achieves 98.6% ID and 98.4% OOD (ViT-B/16), surpassing both the zero-shot OOD (98.4%) and WSE OOD (97.8%), while on Entity-30 it reaches 72.9% OOD vs. 72.1% for WSE.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VRF consistently outperforms both ensemble baselines (WSE and OSE from Wortsman et al. [28] and Kumar et al. [14]) by 1.5–2.0 pp on average OOD accuracy while maintaining or slightly improving ID accuracy, e.g., E2E-FT+VRF achieves 61.8% avg OOD vs. 60.6% (WSE) and 60.2% (OSE) on ViT-B/16. Compared to the zero-shot CLIP model [20], VRF improves ID accuracy by ~14 pp (82.3% vs. 68.3%) while also exceeding zero-shot OOD (61.8% vs. 58.4%), resolving the trade-off that pure fine-tuning introduces. Against LP-FT [15], VRF applied to LP-FT gains +1.5 pp OOD over LP-FT+WSE (62.4% vs. 61.2%) with no ID loss. On selective prediction baselines (MSP [8], Energy [18], MD [16], kNN [24], RMD [22]), VRF achieves 82.3% ID and 61.8% OOD, substantially outperforming the best OOD-detector-based method (kNN/RMD at 80.8–81.1% ID, 58.4% OOD). When combined with FLYP [5], VRF further improves OSE by 1.1 pp on distribution shifts (64.0% vs. 62.8%) without ID degradation, confirming orthogonality with other robust fine-tuning strategies.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends the ensemble-based robust fine-tuning framework of Wortsman et al. (CVPR 2022), which introduced weight-space ensembling (WSE) and output-space ensembling (OSE) with a constant mixing coefficient α. The key departure is replacing this global α with a per-sample weight ω(x) derived from the distance to a Zero-Shot Failure (ZSF) set, motivated by the empirical observation that the fine-tuned/zero-shot accuracy ratio monotonically decreases with this distance. The theoretical justification draws on the variance decomposition of Tumer & Ghosh (1996), showing that sample-wise weighting minimizes ensemble variance more effectively than a fixed coefficient. The paper also positions itself against LP-FT (Kumar et al., ICLR 2022) and selective prediction via OOD detectors, arguing that binary ID/OOD decisions discard complementary knowledge from both models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that even ensemble methods (OSE/WSE) exhibit persistent ID-OOD trade-offs—achieving peak ID and OOD accuracy at different α values—challenges the assumption that ensembling fully resolves the robustness-accuracy dilemma. VRF offers a simple, post-hoc, model-agnostic technique that can be layered on top of any fine-tuned model without retraining, making it practically accessible. However, the method doubles inference cost (running both zero-shot and fine-tuned models), depends on the zero-shot model retaining meaningful predictive power on the downstream task, and the improvements, while consistent (1.5–2.0 pp on OOD), are modest in absolute terms. Open questions include whether the ZSF-based weighting generalizes to settings where pre-training and downstream domains are highly dissimilar, and whether the doubled inference overhead can be further reduced.
