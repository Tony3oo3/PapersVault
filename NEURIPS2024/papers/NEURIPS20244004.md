## Problem

Adversarial contextual bandits with an abstention option (where one action always yields zero reward) had not been studied before, and prior confidence-rated predictor algorithms only provided bounds on weighted sums of per-trial rewards rather than on expected cumulative reward. This paper fills both gaps by introducing the first algorithm with cumulative reward bounds for general confidence-rated predictors and extending the framework to adversarial contextual bandits with abstention, leveraging arbitrary inductive biases encoded as a basis over the context set.

## Input / Output

At each trial the learner receives a context (e.g., a graph node) and E expert predictions, each a probability distribution over K+1 actions where the abstention action □ always has zero reward; the L1-norm of each expert's distribution encodes its confidence level. The learner outputs a single action from [K] ∪ {□} and observes the scalar reward in [-1,1]. The basis structure (metric balls, communities, or geodesic intervals) is fixed a priori and determines the E = NK experts, each corresponding to a (basis element, action) pair that deterministically selects that action when the context falls in the basis element and abstains otherwise.

## Method

CBA is a modification of mirror descent on the unnormalized relative entropy (a Bregman divergence) over non-normalized weight vectors in R_+^E. The key departure from standard mirror descent is that instead of projecting onto the simplex, CBA projects into the set V_t of weight vectors whose weighted sum of expert confidences is at most 1, solved via a Lagrange multiplier that yields the update w̃_{t,i} = w_{t,i} exp(−λ c_{t,i}). The gradient is estimated using the EXP4-style unbiased reward estimator, and the weight update is the standard exponential tilt w_{(t+1),i} = w̃_{t,i} exp(η e_t^i · r̂_t). Because CBA compares against any valid (not necessarily normalized) linear combination of experts, it can dramatically outperform EXP4 when expert confidences are small. For metric-space contexts, a balanced binary tree data structure with lazy propagation of multiplicative updates reduces the per-trial time complexity from O(KN²) to O(KN log N).

## Results

The paper evaluates CBA on four graph-based settings (Stochastic Block Model, Gaussian graph, Cora, and LastFM Asia) using the number of mistakes over time as the primary metric, with 95% confidence intervals over 20 runs. On both synthetic graphs, CBA with d∞-balls (shortest-path metric) delivers the strongest performance, clearly outperforming all baselines. On the Cora dataset, CBA outperforms GABA-II only when using the community detection (LVC) basis, while on LastFM Asia (7,624 nodes), CBA with LVC bases outperforms all baselines. The per-trial time complexity is O(KN) for the direct implementation and O(KN ln N) for the efficient metric-ball implementation (Theorem 5.3), improving from quadratic to near-linear in the number of contexts.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CBA is compared against three baselines: EXP3 instantiated per context, Contextual Bandit with Similarity (CBSim, Slivkins 2011), and GABA-II (Herbster et al. 2021). On the Stochastic Block Model and Gaussian graph, CBA (particularly with d∞-balls) consistently achieves fewer mistakes than all three baselines across the entire time horizon, with the gap widening over time. On the Cora dataset, CBA matches or slightly outperforms GABA-II only when the community detection (LVC) basis is used; other bases (d1, d2, d∞, INT) show comparable or slightly worse performance, which the authors attribute to the dataset's inherent lack of noise. On the larger LastFM Asia graph, CBA with LVC bases outperforms all baselines, confirming practical feasibility at scale. The key takeaway is that CBA's advantage is basis-dependent: it excels when the chosen inductive bias (e.g., shortest-path balls, community structure) aligns with the true context structure, but can underperform when the basis is poorly matched.

## Relation to Prior Work

CBA is a modification of the mirror descent / EXP3 framework for prediction with expert advice under bandit feedback, building directly on EXP4 (Auer et al., 2002) and the confidence-rated predictor literature (Blum & Mansour, 2007; Gaillard et al., 2014; Luo & Schapire, 2015). The key conceptual departure is treating abstention as a proper zero-reward action rather than as another arm with a cost, which prior work (Cortes et al., 2018, 2020; Neu & Zhivotovskiy, 2020) handled differently. This allows CBA to bound expected cumulative reward for general confidence-rated predictors—something prior algorithms could only do for weighted sums of per-trial rewards—and to compare against any valid linear combination of experts, not just normalized ones as EXP4 requires. The extension to adversarial contextual bandits with abstention is explicitly framed as unstudied, going beyond the context-free adversarial setting of SPECIALISTEXP (Herbster et al., 2021) and the stochastic settings of Cortes et al.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first cumulative reward bounds for general confidence-rated predictors and a novel specialist bound that can dramatically improve on SPECIALISTEXP when the background (abstained) region is large relative to the foreground. The near-linear speedup for metric-space contexts (from O(KN²) to O(KN log N)) makes the approach more practical for large-scale graph and structured-data problems. However, the experimental evaluation is explicitly preliminary, the method's performance is highly sensitive to the choice of basis (inductive bias), and the adversarial contextual bandit setting remains a relatively niche area. Open questions include whether the basis-selection problem can be automated, whether the bounds tighten under stochastic or structured reward assumptions, and whether the approach extends to infinite or continuous context spaces.
