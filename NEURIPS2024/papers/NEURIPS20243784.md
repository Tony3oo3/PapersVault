## Problem

Constrained allocation tasks require distributing limited resources over entities while strictly satisfying linear constraints that define a convex polytope action space. Existing safe RL methods use soft constraints and cannot guarantee constraint satisfaction, while hard-constraint methods rely on projecting infeasible actions back into the valid space, introducing sampling bias and restricting applicability to off-policy algorithms. The paper addresses the open challenge of directly parameterizing a stochastic policy over arbitrary convex polytope action spaces without any post-hoc correction.

## Input / Output

Each sub-policy network receives an MLP embedding of the continuous environment state concatenated with the previously sampled scalar allocations (a_1, ..., a_{i-1}) and outputs the shape parameters (α, β) of a four-parameter beta distribution whose support is the feasible interval [a_i^min, a_i^max] computed via linear programming. The full allocation vector is produced autoregressively over n entities; the final entity's allocation is implicitly determined by the simplex constraint Σa_i = 1, so only n−1 explicit sampling steps are needed. The method guarantees every generated action lies exactly within the constrained polytope.

## Method

PASPO decomposes the polytope action space autoregressively: at each step i, it solves two linear programs to find the feasible interval for entity i, samples a_i from a beta distribution over that interval, then updates the remaining polytope by removing the fixed dimension and subtracting the allocated amount from constraint limits (Eq. 2). The policy is a sequence of conditional sub-policies π_θ^i(a_i | s, a_1,...,a_{i-1}), each parameterized by a separate MLP, jointly optimized with PPO using an empirically estimated entropy term from the beta distributions. A key insight is that naive random initialization of the beta parameters introduces a strong sequential bias (earlier entities receive disproportionately large allocations), so the authors propose a de-biasing mechanism that uses maximum likelihood estimation on uniformly sampled points from the polytope to set initial α and β values, ensuring the initial policy approximates a uniform distribution over the full action space and preventing premature convergence.

## Results

Across all three environments (synthetic benchmark, portfolio optimization on NASDAQ-100 data, and compute load distribution), PASPO converges faster and reaches a higher average episode reward than all five Safe RL baselines (CPO, CUP, IPO, P3O, PPO+Lagrangian) and the projection-based OptLayer. Critically, PASPO and OptLayer are the only methods that guarantee zero constraint violations at all times, while all soft-constraint methods exhibit persistent violations throughout training. PASPO also exhibits notably lower variance across seeds compared to other methods, with the exception of the stochastic portfolio optimization environment where some variance is attributable to the hidden Markov model sampling.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PASPO outperforms all five Safe RL baselines (CPO, CUP, IPO, P3O, PPO with Lagrangian relaxation) on average episode reward across all three environments, while also being the only method alongside OptLayer to guarantee zero constraint violations. The soft-constraint methods all exhibit persistent constraint violations during training, which is a fundamental limitation since the allocation tasks require hard constraints to be satisfied at every time step. OptLayer, the only other hard-constraint method, also achieves zero violations but consistently yields lower rewards than PASPO, likely due to the sampling bias introduced by its projection-based correction mechanism. The ablation in Figure 5a confirms that without the de-biasing initialization, PASPO converges more slowly and to a sub-optimal policy, highlighting that the autoregressive decomposition alone is insufficient without proper initialization. The allocation order (forward vs. reversed) has negligible impact when the de-biasing mechanism is used, but causes significant performance differences without it (Figure 8 in Appendix E).

## Relation to Prior Work

PASPO departs from the dominant Safe RL paradigm (Lagrangian relaxation, penalty methods, CPO) that treats constraints as soft cumulative costs, and from projection-based hard-constraint methods (OptLayer, reduced gradient approaches) that generate infeasible actions and then correct them. Instead, it draws on the action-space factorization literature [19, 16, 24] but extends it to arbitrary convex polytope action spaces by autoregressively sampling each allocation from a feasible interval computed via linear programming. It also generalizes prior allocation-specific methods [27, 28] that were restricted to at most two binary constraints, handling an arbitrary number of linear constraints with a single unified mechanism.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PASPO provides the first general-purpose, parameterizable stochastic policy over arbitrary convex polytope action spaces that guarantees zero constraint violations by construction, which is critical for safety-sensitive deployment (e.g., financial regulation, data-center SLAs). The de-biasing mechanism addresses a non-obvious initialization pathology in autoregressive policies that causes premature convergence, and the finding that allocation order becomes irrelevant with proper de-biasing simplifies practical use. Key limitations include the per-step LP cost (limiting scalability to very high-dimensional allocation problems) and the restriction to static, state-independent constraints; extending to state-dependent constraints and combining with soft cumulative constraints are natural next steps.
