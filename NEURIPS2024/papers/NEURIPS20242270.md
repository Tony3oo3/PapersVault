## Problem

Existing post-training quantization (PTQ) methods for image super-resolution were designed for CNN-based backbones (EDSR, SRResNet) and fail to handle Transformer-based models like SwinIR, whose weights and activations exhibit coexisting symmetry/asymmetry and long-tail distributions that cause severe degradation under standard quantization. This paper is the first to thoroughly explore and address PTQ for Transformer-based SR models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242270/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on a pre-trained SwinIR-light model and requires only a small calibration set (32 images from DF2K, randomly cropped to 3×64×64) for both bound searching and distillation training. At inference, the quantized model takes a low-resolution RGB image and produces a high-resolution reconstruction at scale factors ×2, ×3, or ×4, with all compute-intensive operators (linear layers and batch matrix multiplications, ~86% of FLOPs) converted to integer arithmetic at 2, 3, or 4-bit precision.

## Method

2DQuant is a two-stage coarse-to-fine PTQ pipeline. Stage 1 (DOBI) performs an MSE-based bound search with distribution-aware strategies: for symmetric bell-shaped weight distributions it narrows both bounds symmetrically, while for asymmetric exponential activation distributions (from Softmax/GELU) it fixes the lower bound at the data minimum and only traverses the upper bound, achieving O(MK) complexity. Stage 2 (DQC) fine-tunes the clipping bounds via knowledge distillation, where the quantized student minimizes an L1 loss on the final SR output and an L2 loss on L2-normalized intermediate feature maps against the FP teacher, with gradients propagated through the non-differentiable quantization operator via the Straight-Through Estimator. The two stages are complementary: DOBI avoids local optima inherent to training-based optimization, while DQC refines bounds toward the task objective.

## Results

On Set5 (×2), 2DQuant at 4-bit achieves 37.87 dB PSNR / 0.9594 SSIM, closing the gap to the FP SwinIR-light model (38.15 dB) to just 0.28 dB. At 2-bit, 2DQuant reaches 36.00 dB PSNR / 0.9497 SSIM on Set5 (×2) and 28.62 dB on Urban100 (×2), outperforming the previous SOTA PTQ method (DBDC+Pac) by 1.45 dB and 0.99 dB respectively. The method achieves a 3.60× compression ratio and 5.08× speedup at 2-bit with no additional modules. Results are consistent across all five benchmarks (Set5, Set14, B100, Urban100, Manga109) and scale factors (×2, ×3, ×4).

## Baselines & Comparisons

2DQuant consistently outperforms all three baselines (MinMax, Percentile, and DBDC+Pac) across every bit width, scale factor, and benchmark. Against the previous SOTA PTQ method DBDC+Pac [41], 2DQuant gains +0.69 dB PSNR at 4-bit and +1.45 dB at 2-bit on Set5 (×2), with the advantage growing at lower bit widths (e.g., +1.62 dB at 2-bit on Set5 ×4). Compared to Percentile [27], the improvement is even more pronounced at extreme low bits: +5.18 dB at 2-bit on Set5 (×2), where Percentile drops to 30.82 dB while 2DQuant maintains 36.00 dB. MinMax [22] performs worst overall, with 2DQuant exceeding it by 3.48 dB at 4-bit and 2.12 dB at 2-bit on Set5 (×2). The DOBI-only stage already matches or exceeds DBDC+Pac, and adding DQC provides an additional 0.4–0.7 dB boost, confirming the complementary nature of the two stages.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends post-training quantization for image super-resolution from CNN-based backbones (EDSR, SRResNet) to Transformer-based models (SwinIR), a gap explicitly identified as unaddressed by prior PTQ work such as DBDC+Pac [41]. Methodologically, it combines an MSE-based bound search (DOBI) tailored to the coexisting symmetric/asymmetric and long-tail distributions observed in SwinIR's weights and activations with a distillation-based calibration stage (DQC) that uses the FP model as a teacher via L1 output loss and normalized feature loss, leveraging the straight-through estimator for gradient flow through quantization. The two-stage coarse-to-fine design departs from single-stage PTQ methods by first minimizing value heterogenization and then optimizing toward the task objective, drawing on knowledge distillation [15] in a quantization-specific context.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first thorough PTQ study for Transformer-based SR models, providing a practical path to deploy high-quality SR on edge devices with up to 3.60× compression and 5.08× speedup at 2-bit. The observation that quantized models can occasionally outperform their FP counterparts on specific challenging patterns (e.g., stripe direction) suggests quantization acts as a beneficial regularizer, though this is evidenced by a single example. Key limitations include the dependence on a calibration set, the assumption that distributions approximate bell or exponential shapes, and evaluation restricted to SwinIR-light; generalization to other Transformer SR architectures (Restormer, Uformer) and real-world photography deployment remain open.
