## Problem

Classical data selection for finetuning relies on variance minimization, which is insufficient in high-dimensional settings where the parameter dimension r exceeds the coreset size n. In this regime, selected data necessarily fails to span the full parameter space, introducing an additional bias term from low-rank approximation that variance minimization alone cannot address. The paper asks whether the low intrinsic dimension r̄ ≪ min{N, r} of finetuning gradients can be leveraged to achieve fast-rate generalization O(r̄/n) independent of r.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained model φ, a dataset of N samples with features and labels, a target coreset size n ≪ N, a sketching dimension m < n, and a slackness parameter c_S ∈ [n/N, 1]. Gradients of the finetuning parameters at zero initialization are computed on-the-fly and projected via a Johnson-Lindenstrauss transform to m dimensions without storing the full r-dimensional gradient matrix. The output is a weight vector s ∈ Δ_N (probability simplex with entries bounded by 1/n) from which n samples are drawn without replacement to form the coreset D_S.

## Method

SkMM is a two-stage data selection algorithm that balances the variance-bias tradeoff in high-dimensional finetuning. Stage 1 (exploration): gradient sketching via a Gaussian JLT projects the r-dimensional finetuning gradients to m ≈ r̄ dimensions, implicitly identifying a low-dimensional subspace S with small bias (low-rank approximation error). Stage 2 (exploitation): moment matching in the sketched space selects data by solving a quadratic objective with linear constraints that enforces the sketched coreset moment matrix to dominate the full-dataset moment matrix (Σ̃^φ ≼ c_S Σ̃_S^φ), a continuous relaxation of V-optimality that avoids pseudoinverses and integer constraints. The relaxation is optimized via projected gradient descent with per-sample weight bounds 0 ≤ s_i ≤ 1/n. Theoretically, the sketching dimension m = max{√(tr(Σ^φ)/γ_S), 11r̄} balances variance against sketching error, and the resulting generalization bound O(m/n) = O(r̄/n) is independent of the ambient parameter dimension r.

## Results

On the synthetic GMM dataset (r=2400, intrinsic dim=8), SkMM achieves the best empirical risk across all coreset sizes, reaching 3.54e-3 at n=48 where uniform sampling gives 1.14e-1 and herding gives 7.40e+2. On UTKFace age estimation (MAE, lower is better), SkMM achieves 3.25 at n=3000, outperforming R-leverage (3.53) and Herding (3.53). On StanfordCars classification (LP over CLIP, 196 imbalanced classes), SkMM-LP reaches 76.34% accuracy and 74.02% F1 at n=4000, beating the best baseline Glister (76.00% Acc, 73.69% F1) and uniform sampling (75.40% Acc, 73.14% F1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SkMM consistently outperforms all 10+ baselines across all three experimental settings. On the synthetic GMM dataset, SkMM dominates at small coreset sizes (n=48–120) where bias reduction is critical: it achieves 3.54e-3 risk vs. 1.14e-1 for uniform and 7.40e+2 for herding, confirming that pure variance reduction (herding) or pure bias reduction (k-center, adaptive) fail without balancing both. On UTKFace, SkMM beats R-leverage by 0.28 MAE at n=3000 (3.25 vs. 3.53) and adaptive sampling by 0.15 at n=500 (4.23 vs. 4.40), while matching or exceeding herding at larger n. On StanfordCars, SkMM-LP leads all methods at every coreset size, with the largest gap at n=2000 (68.27% vs. 67.95% for Entropy, the closest baseline) and a clear 0.34% accuracy advantage over Glister at n=4000. Notably, uniform sampling remains a surprisingly strong baseline in the low-dimensional LP setting (consistent with Proposition 2.1), but SkMM still edges it out by ~0.9% accuracy at n=4000, demonstrating the benefit of the variance-bias balance even when r ≤ n.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate threads—variance minimization in coreset selection/optimal experimental design (for low-dimensional linear regression) and bias reduction via low-rank approximation (for high-dimensional matrices)—into a single variance-bias tradeoff framework for data selection in high-dimensional finetuning. Gradient sketching via Johnson-Lindenstrauss transforms, previously applied empirically to influence-function-based data selection [16, 34], is given its first theoretical generalization guarantee in the data selection context. The moment matching stage draws on V-optimality from optimal experimental design but replaces the intractable discrete/pseudoinverse formulation with a continuous quadratic relaxation, distinguishing it from prior OED methods [28, 29] that suffer from numerical instability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first rigorous generalization analysis showing that data selection for high-dimensional finetuning requires balancing bias (from low-rank approximation) and variance (from sampling), not just minimizing variance as in the classical low-dimensional setting. This reframing explains why purely variance-focused methods (herding, uniform sampling) or purely bias-focused methods (k-center, adaptive sampling) can fail in high dimensions, and motivates the two-stage SkMM algorithm. Limitations include the kernel-regime assumption, the commutativity heuristic in the moment matching relaxation, and the focus on unsupervised selection; open questions include whether other low-dimensional selection strategies (greedy, sampling-based) can be combined with sketching as a preprocessing step, and whether the theory extends beyond the first-order Taylor approximation.
