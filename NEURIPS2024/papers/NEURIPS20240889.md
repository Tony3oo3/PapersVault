## Problem

Parameter space symmetries (e.g., hidden neuron permutations) are known to influence linear mode connectivity, Bayesian inference, metanetworks, and loss landscape geometry, but their causal role is hard to isolate because standard networks always possess them. Prior methods for removing symmetries (weight constraints, post-processing, Riemannian geometry) alter optimization in ways that confound the analysis. This paper introduces new network architectures that inherently lack parameter symmetries while remaining trainable with standard backpropagation, providing a clean counterfactual for empirical study.

## Input / Output

The asymmetric architectures consume the same inputs as their standard counterparts (32×32 RGB images for CIFAR-10/100, 28×28 grayscale for MNIST, graph node features for ogbn-arXiv, or flattened weight vectors for metanetworks) and produce the same outputs (class labels, node predictions, predicted test accuracy, or Gaussian posterior parameters). The key experimental outputs are loss-landscape diagnostics: midpoint interpolation barriers, monotonicity/convexity of initialization-to-trained trajectories, Bayesian NLL and ECE, and metanetwork R²/Kendall τ. For W-Asym networks, the fixed entries F are sampled from N(0, κI) with κ tuned to be orders of magnitude larger than typical weight initialization scale, and the mask M must have pairwise distinct rows per layer.

## Method

Two symmetry-breaking mechanisms are proposed. W-Asymmetric networks fix a subset of entries in each linear layer to random constants: W′ = M ⊙ W + (1−M) ⊙ F, where M is a binary mask with unique nonzero rows per layer and F ~ N(0, κI); this eliminates all nontrivial computation-graph automorphisms (Theorem 1). σ-Asymmetric networks replace the elementwise nonlinearity with FiGLU, σ(x) = η(Fx) ⊙ x, where η is sigmoid and F is a fixed random matrix; this removes permutation and diagonal equivariances of the activation (Proposition 2), which are the primary sources of parameter symmetries in MLPs. Both architectures are trained with standard Adam and backpropagation, require no manifold optimization or post-processing, and retain universal approximation (proven for W-Asym, Theorem 2). The paper then runs a comprehensive empirical suite—linear mode connectivity, Bayesian variational inference, metanetwork prediction, and monotonic linear interpolation—across MLPs, ResNets, and GNNs to quantify the effect of removing symmetries on each phenomenon.

## Results

On linear mode connectivity (midpoint test loss barrier), W-Asymmetric networks achieve near-perfect interpolation across all architectures: −0.012±.00 on MLP/MNIST, 0.031±.05 on ResNet-8x/CIFAR-10, and 0.095±.03 on GNN/ogbn-arXiv, consistently outperforming both standard networks and Git-ReBasin-aligned networks. For monotonic linear interpolation, 100% of 300 W-Asymmetric ResNets exhibit monotonic loss trajectories (vs. 26.3% for standard ResNets), and all have globally convex trajectories. In Bayesian neural networks, W-Asymmetric 16-layer MLPs train successfully (55.16% test accuracy) while standard 16-layer MLPs fail entirely (13.54%), and W-Asymmetric ResNets consistently improve test accuracy over their standard counterparts.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is Git-ReBasin [1], which aligns two networks via learned permutation before interpolation. W-Asymmetric networks outperform Git-ReBasin on every task: on ResNet-8x/CIFAR-10 the barrier drops from 0.509 (Git-ReBasin) to 0.031 (W-Asym), a ~16× improvement; on GNN/ogbn-arXiv it drops from 0.269 to 0.095; and on MLP/MNIST W-Asym achieves a slightly negative barrier (−0.012) vs. Git-ReBasin's −0.006. The σ-Asymmetric approach improves over standard networks but consistently underperforms both Git-ReBasin and W-Asym, suggesting that breaking symmetries at the weight level (W-Asym) is more effective than at the nonlinearity level (σ-Asym). In the Bayesian setting, W-Asymmetric networks improve test accuracy over standard counterparts across all model sizes (e.g., 57.08% vs. 56.37% for MLP-8 on CIFAR-10; 77.40% vs. 75.71% for ResNet-110), with the most dramatic gain being the complete failure of standard 16-layer MLPs to train. For metanetworks, W-Asymmetric ResNets are substantially easier to predict: a simple MLP metanetwork achieves R²=0.594 on W-Asym ResNets vs. only 0.330 on standard ResNets, and even symmetry-equivariant metanetworks (DeepSets, StatNN) improve on W-Asym inputs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper synthesizes two theoretical threads—computation graph automorphisms (Lim et al., ICLR 2024) and nonlinearity equivariance characterizations (Godfrey et al., NeurIPS 2022)—into concrete architectural modifications (W-Asymmetric and σ-Asymmetric networks) that provably remove parameter symmetries by construction. It departs from prior constraint-based or post-processing approaches (which require manifold optimization or Riemannian interpolation) by producing networks trainable with standard Adam, making them a clean counterfactual for studying symmetry effects. The FiGLU nonlinearity is a fixed-parameter variant of the Gated Linear Unit (Dauphin et al., ICML 2017), and the W-Asymmetric masking directly operationalizes the DAG automorphism framework. Unlike alignment-based methods like Git-ReBasin, which permute weights after training, this work eliminates the need for alignment entirely by removing the symmetries at the architecture level.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This paper provides the first systematic empirical evidence that parameter symmetries are a major driver of non-convexity in neural network loss landscapes: removing them yields 100% monotonic linear interpolation and near-perfect mode connectivity without any alignment step. The Bayesian result—16-layer MLPs that completely fail to train under standard parameterization but train well with W-Asymmetric masking—suggests a practical path toward deeper Bayesian networks. Open questions include why σ-Asym underperforms W-Asym despite both being theoretically justified, whether the findings extend to generalization and robustness, and whether the fixed-entry hyperparameters (κ, n_fix) can be chosen adaptively. The paper also acknowledges that the fixed entries being much larger than typical initializations may confound the symmetry-breaking effect with other optimization changes.
