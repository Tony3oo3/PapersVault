## Problem

Deployed service models face adversarial attacks that require rapid defense, yet existing methods demand heavy adversarial training, large datasets, or model retraining—impractical for frozen or pruned production models. The paper formalizes the Rapid Plug-in Defender (RaPiD) problem: swiftly counter adversarial perturbations without altering the deployed model, using only a sparse set of adversarial examples and minimal computation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20244448/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The defender receives an RGB image (28×28 for MNIST, 32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 224×224 for ImageNet-1k) and produces a residual feature map of the same spatial resolution, which is added to the original image before it enters the frozen service model. The final output is a class label from the service model. Training requires only one-shot (one per class) or few-shot adversarial examples, with no clean training data needed by default.

## Method

CeTaD (Considering Pre-trained Transformers as Defenders) initializes a plug-in defender with pre-trained transformer weights (BERT, ViT, or GPT-2) and fine-tunes only the layer normalization parameters (48 groups, 36,864 variables) using the Lion optimizer over 500 epochs on a single GPU in under 30 minutes. The architecture comprises a ViT-derived embedding, a frozen transformer encoder, and a parameter-free PixelShuffle decoder, connected via a residual path that adds the defender's output to the original input before the frozen service model. The key insight is that pre-trained transformers provide sufficient generalization and universal computation ability to serve as effective adversarial purifiers or prompt generators, while minimal parameter tuning prevents overfitting on the extremely limited adversarial training set. The residual connection is critical—removing it drops both clean and adversarial accuracy to near-random levels.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 (BERT-base defending WideResNet-28-10 against l∞-PGD, one-shot adversarial examples), CeTaD-BERT-large achieves the highest adversarial accuracy (AA) of 48.83% with 66.02% clean accuracy (CA), while CeTaD-VIT achieves the best CA of 82.81% with 30.27% AA. All CeTaD variants substantially outperform the strongest non-CeTaD baseline (FD, AA 23.83%) and training-free methods (R&P, AA 2.34%). Training completes in 14–32 minutes on a single NVIDIA RTX A5000 GPU using only one-shot (10) adversarial examples, fine-tuning just 36,864 layer-norm parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CeTaD decisively outperforms all four external baselines on adversarial accuracy. Against R&P [45] (random resize-and-pad), which preserves CA at 93.16% but yields only 2.34% AA, CeTaD-BERT-large achieves 48.83% AA—a 20× improvement. Against Random Noise [32], which sacrifices CA heavily (down to 48.24% at std=0.07) for a modest 13.67% AA, CeTaD-VIT retains 82.81% CA while reaching 30.27% AA. Against Feature Denoising [46] (the strongest non-CeTaD baseline at 23.83% AA), CeTaD-BERT and CeTaD-BERT-large exceed it by 20.5 and 25.0 percentage points respectively. Against DiffPure [31], which achieves 71.88% AA when trained on the same CIFAR-10 domain but collapses to 0.59% AA when trained on ImageNet-1k (Table 3), CeTaD demonstrates far superior cross-domain transferability, maintaining 17.38% AA (BERT-large) on StAdvAttack without domain-specific training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes pre-trained transformer models (BERT, ViT, GPT-2) as plug-in adversarial defenders, drawing on the universal computation ability of transformers [26] and evidence that pre-training fortifies robustness [16]. It departs from existing purification methods (DiffPure, DensePure, DISCO) that require heavy training on large datasets, and from adversarial training approaches (DM-Improves-AT, DyART) that modify the victim model, by freezing the deployed model entirely and fine-tuning only layer normalization parameters (36,864 variables) on one-shot adversarial examples. The residual connection architecture and the use of PixelShuffle as a parameter-free decoder connect to super-resolution and feature-mapping literature, while the few-shot adversarial training paradigm builds on meta-learning robustness work [13] but applies it to a separate plug-in module rather than the victim model itself.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The RaPiD formulation addresses a practically critical gap: deployed service models (especially post-pruning or in production) cannot be retrained or fine-tuned during an active attack, yet current defenses require hours-to-days of training. CeTaD's sub-30-minute defense on a single GPU with one-shot examples is a meaningful step toward operational rapid response. However, the absolute adversarial accuracy remains modest (44% on CIFAR-10, 30% on CIFAR-100), clean accuracy drops substantially, and the method is validated only on image classification with a single primary seed. Open questions include whether the approach extends to non-differentiable systems, whether continuous learning can handle adaptive attacks, and why the 'transfer gap' phenomenon (unrelated data sometimes outperforming related data) occurs.
