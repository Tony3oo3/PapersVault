## Problem

Existing active finetuning methods like ActiveFT optimize sample selection purely for global distribution alignment, selecting high-density central samples while neglecting local decision boundaries. This causes selected samples to become increasingly redundant and less informative as data volume grows, degrading model performance. The paper addresses how to identify and select boundary-uncertain samples in the pretraining-finetuning paradigm without access to ground-truth labels or a trained classifier head.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241133/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an unlabeled image pool (resized to 224×224), a pretrained feature extractor (e.g., DeiT-Small pretrained with DINO on ImageNet-1k), and an annotation budget B. Each image is mapped to a normalized feature vector in R^d, and the algorithm operates entirely in this feature space. The output is a one-shot set of B sample indices for annotation, which are then labeled by an oracle and used for supervised finetuning—no iterative retraining or re-selection is required.

## Method

BiLAF operates in two stages. Stage 1 (Core Sample Selection) uses ActiveFT to identify K pseudo-class centers ensuring global diversity. Stage 2 (Boundary Sample Selection) proceeds per pseudo-class: first, an Iterative Density-based Clustering (IDC) algorithm denoises by growing a cluster from the center outward via density distance, then removing the last P_rm% of added samples as peripheral noise. Next, an iterative selection loop picks samples by a Boundary Score—(d_inter − d_intra)/max(d_inter, d_intra)—where d_intra is the mean distance to same-class samples and d_inter is the distance to the nearest opposing pseudo-class center; a smaller score indicates closer proximity to the decision boundary. An opponent penalty (scaling d_inter by δ^{t_l} for previously targeted opposing classes) and removal of nearest neighbors after each pick prevent over-concentration on a single boundary region, ensuring diverse boundary coverage across all class pairs.

## Results

On CIFAR-100, BiLAF achieves 62.8% top-1 accuracy at 5% annotation budget, outperforming the strongest baseline ActiveFT (54.6%) by 8.2 points, and 73.7% at 10% budget vs. 71.0% for ActiveFT. On ImageNet, BiLAF reaches 66.2% at 5% budget vs. 65.3% for ActiveFT, a consistent ~1% gain. On CIFAR-10, BiLAF achieves 92.5% at 2% budget (vs. 90.1% ActiveFT), though it trails ActiveFT slightly at the extreme 0.5% budget (81.0% vs. 85.0%). Across object detection (PASCAL VOC mAP), semantic segmentation (ADE20K mIoU), and long-tail classification (CIFAR-100LT), BiLAF also consistently surpasses all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BiLAF outperforms all five traditional active learning baselines (CoreSet, VAAL, LearnLoss, TA-VAAL, ALFA-Mix) across every dataset and budget setting; for example, on CIFAR-100 at 5% budget, BiLAF reaches 62.8% vs. 50.4% (ALFA-Mix), 48.3% (CoreSet), and 38.2% (LearnLoss). Against the primary SOTA baseline ActiveFT [41], BiLAF achieves a consistent ~3% absolute gain on CIFAR-100 (e.g., 62.8% vs. 54.6% at 5%, 73.7% vs. 71.0% at 10%) and ~1% on ImageNet (66.2% vs. 65.3% at 5%), though it trails ActiveFT by 4 points at the extreme 0.5% CIFAR-10 budget (81.0% vs. 85.0%), which the authors attribute to boundary samples introducing instability at very low budgets. On PASCAL VOC object detection, BiLAF achieves 69.2 mAP at 5000 samples vs. 69.1 for ActiveFT; on ADE20K segmentation, 25.71 mIoU at 15% vs. 25.03 for ActiveFT; and on CIFAR-100LT long-tail, 37.33% at 15% vs. 37.01% for ActiveFT. The gap over ActiveFT widens substantially as the annotation budget increases, confirming that boundary-aware selection becomes increasingly valuable with more data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on ActiveFT (Xie et al., CVPR 2023), which formulated the Active Finetuning task and proposed distribution alignment in pretrained feature space for sample selection. BiLAF departs from ActiveFT by adding a second-stage boundary sample selection that addresses ActiveFT's tendency to over-select high-density central regions while neglecting decision-boundary samples. The boundary selection mechanism draws on density-based clustering concepts (DBSCAN-inspired iterative clustering for denoising) and introduces a novel boundary score metric combining intra-class and inter-class distances, while the core sample selection stage remains pluggable (ActiveFT, K-Means, etc.).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The one-shot selection strategy (vs. iterative active learning) has direct practical value by eliminating repeated training cycles and multiple annotation rounds, reducing both computational cost and operational overhead in real-world pretraining-finetuning pipelines. The ~3% absolute improvement on CIFAR100 and ~1% on ImageNet over the prior SOTA (ActiveFT) is meaningful for annotation-budget-constrained settings. However, limitations remain: the method shows diminished advantage in multi-instance tasks (object detection) where global features may not capture spatial structure, and the denoising step risks removing minority-class samples in long-tail distributions. Open questions include task-specific adaptations of the boundary score and adaptive determination of the core-to-boundary sample ratio.
