## Problem

Existing equivariant spatio-spherical networks for dMRI deconvolution, such as RT-ESD, achieve high-quality fiber recovery but are computationally prohibitive for clinical deployment, requiring roughly a day on an A100 GPU per subject. The core gap is that full spherical convolutions are redundant given the antipodal symmetry of neuronal fibers, and no prior method combines efficient hemispherical equivariant convolutions with explicit spatial regularization to enable practical, robust deconvolution at both research-grade and clinically feasible angular resolutions.

## Input / Output

The method takes a shell-sampled dMRI volume (spatio-spherical signal on R³×S² with B shells and N_b gradient directions per shell), normalizes it by a white-matter B0 response function, and interpolates each shell onto a fixed hemispherical HEALPix grid (384 vertices per hemisphere at resolution 8). It outputs per-tissue fODFs on the same hemispherical HEALPix sampling, which are then used for peak detection and fiber tractography. The input and reconstruction shell samplings need not match, enabling angular super-resolution during training (e.g., training on 90-gradient multi-shell data and testing on 29-gradient single-shell clinical protocols).

## Method

The key architectural insight is exploiting the antipodal symmetry of fODFs to replace RT-ESD's full spherical graph convolution with a hemispherical one: the hemispherical Laplacian L⁺(p,q) = L(p,q) + L(p,−q) is provably equivalent to the full spherical operation on antipodally symmetric signals, halving the vertex count and quartering the Laplacian matrix size. Two further efficiency gains are introduced: substituting sparse with dense matrix multiplication (since dMRI Laplacians are dense) and precomputing Chebyshev polynomials of the fixed Laplacian before training. These are embedded in a 4-level E(3)×SO(3)-equivariant U-Net trained unsupervised via a reconstruction loss combined with non-negativity, sparsity, and a novel spatial total-variation regularizer on the fODF output. The TV term explicitly promotes spatial smoothness and yields the largest performance gains at clinically low angular resolutions where the inverse problem is most ill-posed.

## Results

On the DiSCo synthetic benchmark (SNR=30), SHD-TV achieves the best fiber detection performance across all metrics: PR AUC of 0.70 (high-res) and 0.62 (low-res), F1 of 0.65/0.58, and angular error of 17.5°/20.7°, outperforming the next-best RT-ESD (PR AUC 0.64/0.60, F1 0.60/0.57, angle 20.4°/22.0°) and the classical CSD baseline (PR AUC 0.64/0.50, F1 0.56/0.45, angle 23.0°/29.8°). The efficiency-focused SHD variant (without TV regularization) matches RT-ESD performance (PR AUC 0.64/0.61, F1 0.60/0.57) while being 2.5× faster and using 4× less memory. On Tractometer, SHD-TV yields the highest number of valid streamlines and bundle overlap in tractography, and the spatio-hemispherical convolution is 2–5× faster than RT-ESD per layer with 2.5× lower memory on large spherical graphs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SHD-TV dominates all baselines on DiSCo at both resolutions: it improves PR AUC by 0.06 over RT-ESD (0.70 vs 0.64 high-res) and by 0.12 over CSD (0.62 vs 0.50 low-res), while reducing angular error from 20.4° to 17.5° (high-res) and from 29.8° to 20.7° (low-res vs CSD). The efficiency-focused SHD variant matches RT-ESD on F1 (0.60 vs 0.60 high-res) and slightly beats it on angular error (20.0° vs 20.4°) while being 2.5× faster and 4× less memory-intensive. Against the voxel-wise ESD baseline, SHD-TV gains 0.06 PR AUC and 3.4° angular accuracy at high resolution, and the gap widens dramatically at low resolution (PR AUC 0.62 vs 0.47, angle 20.7° vs 27.9°), highlighting the importance of spatial context for clinical protocols. On Tractometer, SHD-TV outperforms all methods in both fODF estimation and tractography (valid streamlines and bundle overlap), while non-equivariant CNN/CNN-TV surprisingly beat unregularized equivariant methods when no generalization is required. PONITA is 10× slower and more memory-intensive than SHD on large spherical graphs common in dMRI, making it impractical for high-angular-resolution deconvolution.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper is a direct efficiency-focused extension of RT-ESD [20], which introduced E(3)×SO(3)-equivariant spatio-spherical convolutions for dMRI deconvolution using DeepSphere's [53] graph-based spherical filtering. The key conceptual departure is exploiting the antipodal symmetry of fODFs to halve the spherical graph (hemispherical Laplacian), combined with dense matrix multiplication and precomputed Chebyshev polynomials to eliminate redundant computation. The training framework follows ESD [19]'s unsupervised reconstruction-based approach, with the addition of explicit spatial total-variation regularization not present in prior equivariant deconvolution networks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: RT-ESD required ~1 day on an A100 GPU for subject-specific optimization, while SHD enables amortized single-network inference across subjects, making equivariant dMRI deconvolution viable for clinical and large-scale retrospective studies. The method's robustness to low-angular-resolution (single-shell, 29-gradient) protocols addresses a key gap between research-grade and clinical dMRI acquisition. Open questions include generalization to other spatio-spherical tasks beyond deconvolution, handling of pathological anatomy, and whether the antipodal symmetry assumption holds in all tissue contexts.
