## Problem

Standard federated learning (FL) achieves high accuracy but incurs substantial communication cost through iterative model exchanges, while one-shot federated learning (OFL) reduces communication to a single round at the cost of significant accuracy degradation, particularly under high data heterogeneity. The key gap is the absence of a method that simultaneously approaches FL-level accuracy and OFL-level communication efficiency, especially when client data distributions are highly non-IID.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242187/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each client holds a local dataset (32×32 RGB images for CIFAR-10/100/SVHN, text for AG-News, or medical slides/records for FLamby) and is split 90/10: the 90% portion trains the local model to convergence (one-shot upload), while the 10% portion is reserved for iterative aggregator training. The aggregator's input is the concatenated logit vector of dimension MC (M clients × C classes) produced by running all M frozen client models on a given sample; its output is a C-dimensional prediction vector. The final global model is the composition of the M client models with the trained aggregator, producing a class label.

## Method

FENS operates in two phases. Phase 1 mirrors OFL: each client trains a local model (e.g., ResNet-8) to convergence on its 90% data split and uploads the parameters in a single round. Phase 2 introduces a lightweight iterative FL loop in which the server distributes all M client models (quantized FP32→INT8 to cut download cost by 4×) to every client, and clients collaboratively train a shallow aggregator function f_λ via FedAdam using only their 10% reserved data. The aggregator is either a 2-layer MLP with ReLU (hidden width k=40) operating on the concatenated MC-dimensional logit vector, or a per-client per-class weight matrix; only the aggregator parameters (q ≪ d) are exchanged per round, making the iterative phase far cheaper than full-model FL. This design instantiates stacked generalization in a federated setting: the trainable level-1 aggregator corrects biases of the frozen level-0 client models, closing the OFL–FL accuracy gap (within 0–3.1% of FedAdam) while incurring only 3.8–4.3× the communication of OFL, compared to 10.9–22.1× for FL.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with 20 clients, FENS achieves 44.20% (α=0.01), 68.22% (α=0.05), and 75.61% (α=0.1) test accuracy, surpassing the best one-shot baseline (Co-Boosting) by 13.7–26.9% while remaining only 0–3.1% below iterative FL (FedAdam). On CIFAR-100, FENS outperforms the best OFL baseline by 8.7–15.4% (e.g., 44.46% vs. 28.98% at α=0.01). In terms of communication, FENS incurs only 3.6–4.3× the cost of one-shot FL, whereas FedAdam requires 10.9–22.1× more communication than FENS to reach comparable accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against one-shot FL baselines, FENS consistently dominates: it outperforms Co-Boosting (SOTA OFL) by 13.7–26.9% on CIFAR-10 and 8.7–15.4% on CIFAR-100, and beats FedKD by up to 26.9% (68.22% vs. 41.25% at α=0.05 on CIFAR-10). The only exception is SVHN at α=0.01, where FedCVAE-ENS (69.71%) edges out FENS (57.35%) due to the simpler task and extreme heterogeneity. Against iterative FL, FENS matches FedAdam on CIFAR-10 at α=0.01 and α=0.05 (44.20% vs. 39.32%; 68.22% vs. 68.74%) while trailing by only 3.1% at α=0.1 (75.61% vs. 78.73%), all at roughly 4× the communication of OFL versus 30–80× for FedAdam. On the FLamby healthcare benchmark, FENS achieves 0.715 AUC on Fed-Camelyon16 (vs. 0.584 for the best FL baseline) and 0.781 accuracy on Fed-Heart-Disease (on par with FedAvg at 0.794), but underperforms on Fed-ISIC2019 (0.579 vs. 0.750) due to extreme client data imbalance. On AG-News, FENS closes the OFL-to-FL gap from 10.8% to 3.5% (78.8% vs. FedKD 71.5% and FedAdam 82.3% at α=0.1). Gradient compression (FedAvg STC) fails to preserve FL accuracy under high heterogeneity, further highlighting FENS's favorable accuracy–communication trade-off.

## Relation to Prior Work

FENS sits at the intersection of one-shot federated learning (OFL) and stacked generalization. It inherits the one-shot local training and model upload phase from OFL (Guha et al. [11], Co-Boosting [7], FedKD [10]), but departs from all prior OFL methods by replacing their static or distillation-based aggregation (uniform averaging, weighted averaging, voting, synthetic-data distillation) with a trainable neural network aggregator. This aggregator is then refined through a lightweight iterative FL phase, effectively importing Wolpert's stacked generalization [44] into the federated setting. The paper also contrasts with ensemble-in-FL work (FedDF, FedBE, FedBoost) that assumes full iterative communication, positioning FENS as a communication-constrained variant that preserves the single-round model upload.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FENS offers a practical middle ground for communication-constrained federated settings: it achieves within 3.1% of iterative FL accuracy while incurring only 3.8–4.3× the communication of OFL (vs. 10.9–22.1× for FL), making it attractive for bandwidth-limited or latency-sensitive deployments. The method also supports model heterogeneity across clients, enables rapid unlearning by simply retraining the lightweight aggregator, and is applicable to model-market scenarios. Key limitations include the memory overhead of storing the full ensemble on each client during aggregator training (mitigated by INT8 quantization and downsizing) and the expanded attack surface from the iterative phase. Open questions include designing aggregators that avoid downloading all client models and extending the approach to very large client counts.
