## Problem

Existing representation engineering methods (RepE) can shift a single LLM behavior at inference time by manipulating PCA directions in MLP outputs, but they fail catastrophically when multiple trustworthiness dimensions (safety, factuality, bias) must be controlled simultaneously because mixed principal directions lose task-specific semantic meaning. The paper identifies that this control conflict stems from operating on coarse-grained, overlapping components rather than on sparse, task-specific ones.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240502/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference time the method intercepts the output activations of a small set of pre-identified attention heads (typically 5–15 per task) and applies a Gaussian coordinate transform before the residual stream continues. The input is a standard text prompt consumed by the unmodified Llama2-13B-Chat or Qwen2 model; the only additional data are pre-computed GMM parameters (mean vector and covariance matrix per head per task) derived offline from ~1,200–1,800 stimulus samples. The output is a token-by-token text response whose behavioral tendencies (refusal rate, correction rate, not-refusal rate) are shifted without any weight modification.

## Method

The pipeline has three stages. First, path patching (a causal-mediation technique) identifies which attention heads are causally responsible for each task's output logit by swapping a single head's activation between a reference and a counterfactual input while freezing all other heads; this yields a sparse set of 4–14% overlapping heads across tasks. Second, for each identified head, a single-component Gaussian Mixture Model is fitted separately on stimulus-pair activations (opposing behavioral prompts), capturing the full covariance structure rather than only the first PCA direction. Third, at inference the head output is mapped from the 'undesired' Gaussian to the 'desired' Gaussian via a closed-form affine transform involving the square-root covariance matrices and means, effectively rotating and translating the activation into the target behavioral subspace. Because the task-relevant heads are largely disjoint, the transforms compose without interference, enabling simultaneous multi-dimension control while leaving general capabilities (MMLU, CSQA) within ~2% of baseline.

## Results

On Llama2-13B-Chat, SAC achieves 89.47% Correct Rate (Adv Factuality), 62.5% Refusal Rate (Preference Bias), and 96% Not Refusal Rate (Exaggerated Safety) in single-task control, matching or exceeding RepE on two of three tasks. In the critical multi-task setting, SAC maintains 86.12% CR, 53.75% RR, and 88.5% NRR simultaneously, while RepE-Mean and RepE-Merge collapse to 72.59%/71.08% CR, 5%/10% RR, and 61%/63% NRR—performing worse than no control at all. General capability is minimally affected, with MMLU dropping only ~1.6% (52.45%→50.80%) and CSQA ~2.2% (62.67%→60.50%) under multi-task SAC.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is RepE (Zou et al., 2023), which uses PCA on MLP-layer outputs to extract concept directions. In single-task control, SAC slightly trails RepE on Adv Factuality (89.47% vs 90.43% CR) but substantially outperforms it on Preference Bias (62.5% vs 39.17% RR, a +23.3 pp gap) and marginally leads on Exaggerated Safety (96% vs 95% NRR). The decisive comparison is in multi-task control: RepE-Mean and RepE-Merge both degrade below the No Control baseline on all three trustworthiness metrics (e.g., Pref Bias drops to 5% and 10% respectively, below the 10.83% uncontrolled rate), whereas SAC retains 86.12% CR, 53.75% RR, and 88.5% NRR—only ~10% below its single-task performance. This demonstrates that RepE's shared PCA directions lose task-specific semantic meaning when fused, while SAC's sparse, task-specific attention-head control avoids interference. General capability (MMLU, CSQA) remains largely intact across all methods, confirming that neither approach significantly impairs the model's broader reasoning ability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Representation Engineering (RepE, Zou et al. 2023) by replacing its coarse-grained PCA-on-MLP-outputs approach with a finer-grained GMM-on-attention-heads approach, and by using causal intervention (Path Patching, Wang et al. 2022; Goldowsky-Dill et al. 2023) instead of classification-based probing to identify task-relevant components. The key conceptual departure is the hypothesis that different trustworthiness dimensions are mediated by sparse, nearly disjoint sets of attention heads, which RepE's holistic MLP-level control cannot exploit. This positions the work at the intersection of mechanistic interpretability (causal mediation analysis) and practical LLM alignment, bridging the gap between circuit-level understanding and inference-time behavior control.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap: existing representation control methods fail when multiple trustworthiness dimensions (safety, factuality, bias) must be controlled simultaneously, with naive fusion actually degrading performance below the uncontrolled baseline. The training-free nature of SAC (no RLHF or fine-tuning required) and its demonstrated generalizability across model families (Llama2-13B, Qwen2-7B, Qwen2-72B) and trustworthiness dimensions (including robustness and privacy) make it a potentially widely adoptable technique. Open questions include the computational cost of path patching at scale, whether the sparsity property holds for more than 5–8 simultaneous tasks, applicability to proprietary models without internal access, and whether the GMM simplification (single Gaussian per class) is sufficient for more complex behavioral axes.
