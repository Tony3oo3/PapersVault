## Problem

Causal discovery in systems where causal mechanisms change across contexts is well-studied under the assumption that the context variable is exogenous. However, many real-world systems feature endogenous context variables (e.g., soil moisture regime influenced by precipitation), where naive masking of data by context introduces selection bias and pooling all data loses context-specific structure. No existing method recovers context-specific causal graphs when the context indicator is itself a collider or descendant of system variables.

## Input / Output

The method takes observational data samples (continuous and/or categorical variables) from a multi-context system along with a user-specified categorical context indicator R that partitions the data into contexts. It produces one context-specific skeleton graph per context value r (with edges present only in that context marked as dashed) and a union graph combining all edges found across contexts. The context indicator R must be observed and chosen to satisfy regime-sufficiency assumptions; the method handles both acyclic and cyclic (edge-flip) union graphs.

## Method

The paper proposes PC-AC, an adaptive modification of the PC-stable algorithm that applies a simple decision rule per separating set: if the context indicator R is in the conditioning set, the conditional independence test is run on context-specific data (X⊥Y|Z, R=r); otherwise, it is run on the pooled data (X⊥Y|Z). This avoids selection bias (which arises from conditioning on an endogenous collider) while still detecting context-specific independencies, and avoids inflating the number of tests compared to exhaustive CSI search. The theoretical contribution introduces three new graphical objects (descriptive, physical, and counterfactual graphs) that formalize the connection between context-specific independencies and SCMs, along with a hierarchy of regime-sufficiency assumptions (strong, single-graph, weak) under which the algorithm is proven sound for recovering the physical graph. The approach is not specific to PC and can be combined with other constraint-based algorithms.

## Results

The paper evaluates its adaptive context method (PC-AC) against masking (PC-M), intersection baseline (PC-B), and pooled (PC-P) approaches on synthetic linear SCMs with D+1=8 nodes and density s=0.4, reporting TPR and FPR for both union and context-specific graphs. PC-AC achieves the lowest FPR among all methods on context-specific graphs (avoiding selection bias that inflates PC-M's FPR) and competitive TPR, particularly when context-children links are known (adj. R-ch.). Without any link assumptions, FPR increases significantly for all methods, but switching to a non-parametric conditional independence test substantially reduces FPR across the board. For cyclic union graphs, PC-P's FPR rises (it only recovers an acyclification), while PC-AC and PC-B maintain the best TPR on context-specific graphs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against PC-M (masking), PC-AC consistently achieves lower FPR on context-specific graphs because masking introduces selection bias when the context variable is endogenous; PC-M also cannot detect links involving R (which is constant per context), lowering its TPR. Against PC-B (the intersection-of-masked-and-pooled baseline), PC-AC achieves comparable or slightly better FPR with fewer total independence tests, giving it better finite-sample performance. Against PC-P (pooled PC-stable), PC-AC matches or exceeds TPR on union graphs while additionally recovering context-specific structure that PC-P cannot provide. Against FCI-JCI0, PC-AC achieves higher TPR on union graphs but FCI has lower FPR. Against CD-NOD, PC-AC slightly outperforms on union graph recovery, with CD-NOD assuming acyclicity that limits its applicability to cyclic union graphs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the PC(-stable) algorithm with an adaptive testing strategy to handle context-specific causal discovery when the context variable is endogenous (influenced by system variables), a setting that prior multi-context CD methods (JCI, LDAGs, masking-based approaches) either assume away or cannot handle without introducing selection bias. Conceptually closest to Labeled DAGs (Pensar et al., Hyttinen et al.), it departs by distinguishing system variables from the context indicator, allowing cycles in the union graph, and reducing the number of required independence tests through an adaptive decision rule. The theoretical framework introduces novel graphical objects (descriptive and physical graphs) that formalize the connection between context-specific independencies and structural causal models, going beyond the independence-structure-only perspective of LDAGs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Endogenous context variables are prevalent in real-world dynamical systems (e.g., soil moisture regimes in climate science), yet naive approaches—masking data by context or pooling all data—produce either selection-biased spurious edges or lose context-specific information entirely. The paper's identification of the 'support problem' (CSIs arising from genuinely disabled mechanisms vs. merely restricted observational support) and the corresponding descriptive/physical graph distinction is a subtle but important theoretical clarification. Key limitations include the causal sufficiency assumption (no hidden confounders), difficulty with large union cycles, and sensitivity to the choice of context indicator; extensions to time-series settings and relaxation of causal sufficiency are identified as important future directions.
