## Problem

Existing online IRL algorithms that estimate the feasible reward set exhibit sample complexity that scales with the cardinality of the state space, making them infeasible for large or continuous state spaces even under the Linear MDP structure. This paper proves that this dependence is fundamental to the feasible set formulation and introduces a new framework that removes this barrier.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241738/cropped_page6_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The algorithm receives a dataset of expert trajectories, a single reward function r to classify, access to the environment via a forward model, and a compatibility threshold Δ. Rather than recovering a single reward, it outputs a boolean verdict (whether r makes the expert policy at most Δ-suboptimal) along with an estimated (non)compatibility value Ĉ(r). The estimation decomposes into two independent components: J^E(r) computed solely from expert demonstrations and J*(r) computed solely from self-collected exploration data, with the final classification applying Δ to their difference.

## Method

The paper introduces the Rewards Compatibility framework, which defines the (non)compatibility of a reward r as C̄(r) = J*(r) − J^πE(r), a continuous generalization of the feasible set (where C̄ = 0). This reframes IRL as a classification problem: given a threshold Δ, determine whether C̄(r) ≤ Δ. CATY-IRL solves this in two phases: an exploration phase that collects environment samples using RFE subroutines (RFLin for Linear MDPs, BPI-UCBVI or RF-Express for tabular MDPs), and a classification phase that estimates C̄(r) by separately computing the expert's expected return from demonstrations and the optimal return from the explored dynamics. The key insight is that this per-reward classification avoids explicitly constructing the feasible set, and the sample complexity depends on the feature dimension d rather than the state space cardinality S, yielding minimax-optimal rates in the tabular setting up to logarithmic factors.

## Results

The paper is purely theoretical with no empirical evaluation. The headline result (Theorem 5.1) shows CATY-IRL achieves sample complexity Õ(H³d/ε² · log(1/δ)) expert trajectories and Õ(H⁵d/ε² · (d + log(1/δ))) exploration episodes in Linear MDPs, with no dependence on the state space cardinality |S|. In tabular MDPs, the exploration complexity is Õ(H³SA/ε² · (S + log(1/δ))), which is shown to be minimax optimal up to logarithmic factors (Theorem 6.1). As a by-product, the paper improves the RFE lower bound from Ω(H³SA/ε² · (S/H + log(1/δ))) to Ω(H³SA/ε² · (S + log(1/δ))), a factor of H improvement over the state of the art.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical paper with no empirical baselines; comparisons are between sample complexity bounds. Against the feasible set formulation (Metelli et al. 2021 [39], Metelli et al. 2023 [38], Lazzati et al. 2024 [31]), CATY-IRL eliminates the Ω(S) dependence on state space cardinality that plagues all prior feasible-set algorithms, even under the Linear MDP structure (Theorem 3.2 proves this is unavoidable for the feasible set). Against the prior RFE lower bound of Ω(H³SA/ε² · (S/H + log(1/δ))) from Jin et al. [21] and Darwiche Domingues et al. [12], the new RFE lower bound (Theorem 6.2) improves by a factor of H, and is matched by RF-Express [37]. In the tabular setting, CATY-IRL's exploration complexity matches the new IRL classification lower bound (Theorem 6.1) up to logarithmic factors, establishing minimax optimality. The paper does not provide a lower bound for Linear MDPs, so minimax optimality in that setting remains open.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the feasible-set IRL literature (Metelli et al. 2021, 2023; Lazzati et al. 2024), which established PAC frameworks for estimating the set of rewards making an expert policy optimal. It departs from this line by proving a fundamental negative result: the feasible set requires Ω(|S|) expert samples even under Linear MDP structure, making it infeasible for large or continuous state spaces. To overcome this, the paper introduces 'rewards compatibility' as a soft generalization of the feasible set, reframing IRL as a classification problem (IRL Classification) rather than a set-estimation problem, and leverages Reward-Free Exploration algorithms (Jin et al. 2020; Wagenmaker et al. 2022; Ménard et al. 2021) as exploration subroutines to achieve state-space-independent sample complexity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central contribution is a clean negative result showing that the standard feasible-set formulation of IRL is fundamentally unlearnable at scale, followed by a constructive alternative (rewards compatibility + CATY-IRL) that scales to large/continuous state spaces under Linear MDPs. This opens a path toward theoretically grounded IRL in realistic settings where state spaces are large. Key limitations include the reliance on the (strong) Linear MDP assumption, the absence of any empirical validation, and the lack of a matching lower bound in the Linear MDP setting. The Objective-Free Exploration framework, while conceptually appealing as a unifying lens for RFE and IRL, remains underdeveloped and is left as a future direction.
