## Problem

In practical applications, good clusterings must often satisfy multiple conflicting objectives simultaneously (e.g., geographically compact regions that are also data-homogeneous), yet the theoretical study of multi-objective clustering is extremely limited. This paper addresses the gap by developing the first algorithms with provable approximation guarantees for computing approximate Pareto fronts across most combinations of two k-clustering objectives (k-center, k-diameter, k-median, k-means, k-MSR, k-separation), each potentially under a different metric.

## Input / Output

The method takes a finite point set P in a metric space, a target number of clusters k, two metrics d₁ and d₂ on P, and two objectives f₁, f₂ from {rad, diam, med, mean, msr, sep}, and produces an α-approximate Pareto set: a diverse collection of clusterings such that for every true Pareto-optimal solution, some output solution approximates it within factor α₁ on f₁ and α₂ on f₂. For combinations involving sep, rad, or diam, the Pareto set has size O(n²) (tight in the worst case), while for med/mean combinations where the Pareto set can be exponential, the method instead outputs a polynomial-size approximate convex Pareto set whose size is polynomial in 1/ε and |P|.

## Method

The core technique is to compose existing single-objective approximation algorithms into bi-objective Pareto front approximations by enumerating candidate objective values and solving constrained subproblems. For sep + rad/diam, a two-phase algorithm first enforces separation via Single Linkage merges, then applies an adapted Hochbaum-Shmoys 2-approximation to reduce cluster count while bounding diameter/radius, yielding (1,2)-approximate Pareto sets. For rad/diam + rad/diam, a new O(n³) algorithm improves over the naive O(n⁶) approach by restricting to O(n²) relevant radius pairs and maintaining the threshold graph dynamically with amortized O(n) updates. For rad/diam + med/mean, the Jain-Vazirani primal-dual algorithm is adapted by fixing a radius R and removing infeasible assignments from the LP, achieving (9,6+ε) for k-median and (9,54+ε) for k-means. For sep + med/mean, the nesting technique of Lin et al. combines a separation-constrained clustering with an approximate k-median/k-means solution. For sep + msr, the Buchem et al. primal-dual algorithm is modified to operate on preformed clusters rather than individual points, preserving the (3+ε) guarantee.

## Results

The paper provides provable approximation guarantees for computing approximate Pareto sets across most bi-objective clustering combinations. Key theoretical results include (1,2)-approximate Pareto sets for k-separation with k-center/k-diameter, (1, 2+δ)-approximate sets for k-separation with k-median (δ=2.67059), and (9, 6+ε)-approximate sets for k-center with k-median. Experimentally, on 8 datasets with ground truth, the best solution C* on the approximate Pareto front (k-separation + k-means) achieves NMI scores of 0.4728–0.9779, consistently outperforming both k-means++ (0.4693–0.9065) and Single Linkage (0.0000–0.7260) across all datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares the best Pareto-front solution C* against two baselines: Single Linkage (SL) and k-means++ (kM++), evaluated via NMI on 8 datasets. C* outperforms kM++ on all 8 datasets, with the largest gains on Iris (+11.7 percentage points: 0.8578 vs 0.7405) and 2d-10c-no4 (+7.3 points: 0.9779 vs 0.9052), and smaller but consistent gains on the remaining datasets (e.g., +0.35 on Rice, +0.67 on Dry Bean). C* also dominates SL on every dataset, with particularly dramatic improvements on Iris (0.8578 vs 0.0000) and Rice (0.4728 vs 0.0007), where Single Linkage's chaining effect produces near-random clusterings. The paper notes that on non-synthetic datasets, the best Pareto solution typically uses a small separation value, suggesting that only a few steps of Single Linkage before k-means++ suffice to capture most of the benefit.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper systematizes the theoretical treatment of bi-objective clustering by adapting well-known single-objective approximation algorithms (Hochbaum-Shmoys for k-center/k-diameter, Jain-Vazirani primal-dual for k-median, Buchem et al. for k-MSR, and the Lin et al. nesting technique) into subroutines that compute approximate Pareto fronts for most pairs of clustering objectives. It extends the single specific combination studied by Alamdari and Shmoys (k-center + k-median with a (4,8) guarantee via LP rounding) to a comprehensive matrix of objective pairs with generally tighter guarantees, and introduces a new O(n³) algorithm for the (2,2) k-center/k-diameter Pareto set that improves over the naive O(n⁶) enumeration. The convex Pareto set approach for k-median/k-means combinations draws on Diakonikolas's framework, while the experimental evaluation builds on the k-means++ and Single Linkage baselines used in prior multi-objective clustering heuristics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to provide provable approximation guarantees for approximate Pareto fronts across most combinations of standard clustering objectives, filling a gap where prior multi-objective clustering relied entirely on heuristics without theoretical backing. The inapproximability constructions (showing that k-separation and any minimization objective cannot be simultaneously constant-factor approximated) clarify the fundamental tension that motivates Pareto-based trade-offs. Open questions include whether the gap problem for k-median/k-means combinations admits a polynomial-time solution (needed for the Papadimitriou-Yannakakis framework), whether centers can be extended to the ambient space, and whether the small-separation-then-k-means++ heuristic observed empirically can be formalized with tighter guarantees.
