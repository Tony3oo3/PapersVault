## Problem

High-dimensional generalized eigenvalue problems (GEPs) arising in PCA, FDA, and CCA are typically solved under sparsity assumptions, but many real-world signals (e.g., natural images) are better captured by generative models. Prior work on generative-prior inverse problems only addressed the simpler PCA case (where B is the identity), while the singular B̂ in a general GEP prevents direct application of those methods. This paper fills the gap by studying the full GEP under a Lipschitz generative prior, providing both optimal statistical rates for the global optimum and a provably convergent algorithm.

## Input / Output

The method takes an approximate matrix pair (Â, B̂) ∈ R^{n×n} constructed from m independent observations, a pre-trained L-Lipschitz generative model G: R^k → S^{n-1} with latent dimension k ≪ n (e.g., a VAE with k=20 for 784-dim MNIST images), and an initial vector u₀. It outputs the estimated leading generalized eigenvector u_T ∈ R(G), which in the image experiments corresponds to a reconstructed 28×28 grayscale or RGB face image. The projection step P_G(·) onto the range of G is approximated in practice via gradient descent with Adam (100 steps, lr=0.1), since exact projection is intractable.

## Method

The core algorithm, Projected Rayleigh Flow Method (PRFM), iteratively computes the Rayleigh quotient ρ_t = u_t^T Â u_t / (u_t^T B̂ u_t), performs a gradient ascent step u_t + η(Â − ρ_t B̂)u_t, and projects the result onto the range of the generative model via P_G. This generalizes the truncated Rayleigh flow method for sparse GEPs by replacing the sparsity-support truncation with a projection onto the generative manifold, and handles the singular B̂ that blocks standard PCA solvers. Theoretically, the paper proves that any global optimum of the constrained problem achieves the minimax rate O(√(k log L / m)), and that PRFM converges linearly to this rate under conditions on the eigenvalue gap (λ₁ > λ₂), the condition number κ(B), and the step size η (requiring γ₁+γ₂ < 2 and γ₂+3γ₁ > 3). The convergence analysis requires novel auxiliary lemmas to bound terms involving both Â and B̂ simultaneously, going beyond the simpler single-matrix bounds used in GPCA.

## Results

The paper evaluates PRFM on MNIST (n=784, k=20 VAE) using Cosine Similarity as the metric, varying the number of measurements m from 100 to 350. In both the spiked covariance setting (Eq. 24) and the phase retrieval setting (Eq. 25), PRFM achieves the highest Cosine Similarity across all values of m, outperforming both PPower and the sparsity-based Rifle methods. The key qualitative finding is that when m is small relative to n (e.g., m=100–200), Rifle20 and Rifle100 produce poor reconstructions, while PRFM and PPower maintain reasonable quality, with PRFM consistently superior to PPower. The experiments are explicitly framed as proof-of-concept rather than exhaustive benchmarks, as the paper's primary contributions are theoretical (optimal statistical rate O(√(k log L / m)) and linear convergence guarantees).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PRFM is compared against two baselines: PPower [Liu et al., ICLR 2022] and Rifle20/Rifle100 [Tan et al., JRSS-B 2018]. Against PPower, PRFM consistently achieves higher Cosine Similarity in both the spiked covariance and phase retrieval settings. The paper attributes this advantage to PPower's incompatibility with the case where B̂ is not the identity matrix — PPower simply uses Â as its input and ignores B̂, whereas PRFM properly handles the generalized eigenvalue structure through the Rayleigh quotient ρ_t. Against the sparsity-based Rifle methods (Rifle20 with cardinality 20, Rifle100 with cardinality 100), PRFM substantially outperforms when m is small relative to n, as the sparsity assumption is violated for natural image signals that are better captured by the generative prior. The paper notes that PRFM's step size η=7/32 is chosen to satisfy both the convergence condition (γ₁+γ₂<2) and the initialization condition (γ₂+3γ₁>3), while Rifle's step size is set to η′=35/32 so that η′/ρ_{t-1}≈7/32 for fair comparison. All methods use the same initialization u₀ = [1,1,…,1]ᵀ/√n and 10 random restarts to mitigate local minima.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate research threads: the sparse generalized eigenvalue problem (SGEP) solved via the truncated Rayleigh flow method (Rifle, Tan et al. 2018) and generative-prior inverse problems such as generative PCA (GPCA, Liu et al. 2022). The key technical departure from GPCA is handling the singular B̂ matrix inherent to GEPs, which prevents direct application of PCA-style projected power methods. The proposed Projected Rayleigh Flow Method (PRFM) generalizes Rifle's two-stage approach by replacing the sparsity-based support selection with projection onto the range of a Lipschitz generative model, while the convergence analysis requires new auxiliary lemmas to manage the coupled Â and B̂ terms absent in either prior work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first theoretical framework showing that generative priors can replace sparsity assumptions in the full GEP setting (covering PCA, FDA, CCA, and phase retrieval as special cases), with provable optimal statistical rates of O(√(k log L / m)). However, the empirical validation is explicitly framed as proof-of-concept on small-scale image datasets (MNIST, CelebA), and the projection step onto the generative model range—assumed exact in theory—remains a significant practical bottleneck that the authors acknowledge as an open problem. Future work on efficient projection methods with guarantees and multi-eigenvector recovery would be needed to translate these theoretical results into broadly applicable algorithms.
