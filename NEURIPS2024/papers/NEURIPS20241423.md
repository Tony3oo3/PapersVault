## Problem

Parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) of large vision foundation models yields accurate but severely under-confident predictions in few-shot settings, with ECE consistently above 0.3 and confidence rarely exceeding 80%. Existing calibration methods target over-confidence from overfitting and are ineffective for this under-confidence, which stems from the model under-estimating the contribution of rich pre-training prior knowledge to downstream tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241423/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, standard resolutions for Food-101 and Flowers-102) and a few-shot training set of K examples per class (K ∈ {1, 2, 5, 10, 20}) as input. It produces a class label along with fine-grained calibrated confidence expressed as a Dirichlet evidence vector, from which belief masses, vacuity (epistemic uncertainty), and dissonance (aleatoric uncertainty) are derived. The final prediction is obtained by Bayesian model averaging across an ensemble of evidential components, each outputting its own evidence vector.

## Method

B-PEFT integrates two Bayesian components with standard PEFT (VPT, Adapter, or Bias fine-tuning) on a frozen ViT backbone. First, a base-rate adjustment transforms the subjective-logic base rate as χ_i = ((e_i − e_min)/e_min)^m, which strengthens the prior belief from pre-training by widening the Dirichlet parameter gap between the top-1 and top-2 classes while preserving their relative order (guaranteed by Lemma 2 and Theorem 3), thereby converting under-confidence into well-calibrated confidence without sacrificing accuracy. Second, a diversity-inducing evidential ensemble trains P components with different incorrect-evidence regularization strengths λ_p, which acts as an implicit repulsive force in evidence space equivalent to SVGD (Lemma 4), pushing components to learn complementary discriminative features. The evidential head replaces the softmax layer with an exp activation to output non-negative evidence, and training uses a Type-II MLE evidential loss combined with KL-based incorrect-evidence regularization.

## Results

On CIFAR-100, B-PEFT reduces ECE from 0.393 (CE-based VPT) to 0.067 at 1-shot and from 0.501 to 0.031 at 10-shot, a nearly 6× improvement in calibration while maintaining or slightly improving accuracy (70.2% vs 69.6% at 1-shot). On CIFAR-10, B-PEFT achieves 93.83% accuracy with ECE of 0.011 at 20-shot. Across all four datasets (CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, Food-101, Flowers-102) and five few-shot settings (1/2/5/10/20-shot), B-PEFT consistently achieves the lowest ECE among all compared methods, with ECE values typically below 0.07.

## Baselines & Comparisons

B-PEFT outperforms all calibration baselines on ECE across every few-shot setting on CIFAR-100: it beats Temperature Scaling (0.067 vs 0.092 at 1-shot; 0.031 vs 0.036 at 10-shot), Parameterized Temperature Scaling (0.067 vs 0.145 at 1-shot), and IR-MC (0.067 vs 0.091 at 1-shot). Compared to the uncalibrated CE-based VPT [32], B-PEFT reduces ECE by roughly 6× at 1-shot (0.067 vs 0.393) while matching or slightly exceeding accuracy (70.2% vs 69.6%). The raw evidential model [56] actually worsens calibration (ECE 0.499 at 1-shot) due to its inherent under-confidence, which B-PEFT's base-rate adjustment corrects. For alternative PEFT techniques (Bias fine-tuning [9] and Adapter [51]), B-PEFT similarly reduces ECE from ~0.30–0.39 to ~0.05–0.07 while also improving accuracy by 1–4 points (e.g., Bias 1-shot: 37.8% vs 35.5% accuracy, ECE 0.050 vs 0.296). The key finding is that existing post-hoc calibration methods (TS, PTS, IR-MC) are designed for over-confidence and are less effective here; B-PEFT's evidence-based approach directly addresses the under-confidence root cause.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of evidential deep learning (Sensoy et al., 2018), subjective logic theory (Jøsang, 2016), and parameter-efficient fine-tuning of vision transformers (Jia et al., 2022; Pfeiffer et al., 2020). It departs from the standard calibration literature, which predominantly targets over-confidence in overfit models, by identifying and addressing the opposite pathology—severe under-confidence in PEFT-adapted foundation models. The base rate adjustment mechanism is a novel use of the subjective-logic base rate as a tunable prior-strengthening knob, and the diversity-inducing ensemble (via varying incorrect-evidence regularization strengths) is shown to be theoretically equivalent to SVGD-based repulsive ensembles (D'Angelo et al., 2021) without requiring explicit inter-particle communication.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that PEFT-adapted foundation models are systematically under-confident (ECE > 0.3 even at 75%+ accuracy) challenges the common assumption that limited fine-tuning data leads to over-confidence, and opens a practical gap for deploying these models in safety-critical or high-stakes decision-making settings where calibrated confidence is essential. The proposed B-PEFT framework is lightweight (no backbone retraining, only a post-hoc base-rate transform plus a small ensemble), making it broadly applicable to any PEFT pipeline. Limitations include evaluation only on mid-scale datasets (CIFAR-10/100, Food101, Flowers102) without ImageNet-scale validation, the need to tune the transformation exponent m per setting, and the added inference cost of the ensemble. Open questions include whether the under-confidence phenomenon persists (or changes character) with self-supervised pretraining at scale, and whether the base-rate adjustment generalizes to non-Dirichlet evidential priors or regression settings.
