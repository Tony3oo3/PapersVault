## Problem

Existing object-driven navigation benchmarks restrict targets to objects present at environment acquisition time and define goals as general semantic categories, failing to capture the realistic scenario where an agent must locate a specific user-owned instance among visually similar distractors in multiple possible locations. No prior dataset combines photo-realistic scenes, injected personalized objects with variable placement, multimodal references, and same-category distractors that force instance-level discrimination.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240358/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The agent receives three rendered reference images of the target object on a neutral background (front, back, and top views at 30° yaw increments) and three manually annotated textual descriptions (averaging 10.7 words each) that highlight distinguishing features. Navigation proceeds in a 3D photo-realistic HM3D scene using six discrete actions (stop, move ahead, turn left, turn right, tilt up, tilt down) over a budget of 1,000 timesteps, with success defined as stopping within 1 meter Euclidean distance of the target. Modular agents additionally produce a per-observation matching score and localization coordinates that gate the transition from exploration to exploitation.

## Method

The paper defines the PIN task and constructs PInNED by procedurally injecting 338 manually rescaled photo-realistic 3D objects from Objaverse-XL into 180 HM3D scenes, spawning them on curated surfaces (tables, sofas, beds, etc.) with up to 6 same-category and 13 cross-category distractors per episode. For evaluation, two agent families are adapted: modular agents that decouple navigation into frontier-based exploration, a swappable detection module (keypoint matching via SuperGlue/LightGlue, patch-level cosine similarity with DINOv2/CLIP, or open-vocabulary detection via PerSAM/OWL), and Fast Marching Method exploitation; and end-to-end RL agents (ZSON, RIM) finetuned on PInNED with CLIP-extracted multimodal features. The key finding is that modular agents outperform end-to-end counterparts because the latter's imitation training cannot exploit location priors when targets appear in arbitrary positions, and DINOv2 achieves the best success rate (14.84%) among modular agents yet still confuses same-category instances, confirming the task remains largely unsolved.

## Results

On the PInNED validation set (1,193 episodes with same-category distractors), the best modular agent DINOv2 (visual) achieves 14.84% SR and 7.94% SPL, while the best end-to-end agent ZSON (visual) reaches 9.14% SR and 7.18% SPL. DINOv2's high category error (CE=26.15%) and category match rate (CM=42.00%) reveal that it frequently confuses the target with same-category distractors. Overall, no method exceeds 15% success rate, demonstrating that the PIN task remains far from solved for current object-driven navigation approaches.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among modular agents, DINOv2 (visual) dominates with 14.84% SR, outperforming CLIP-visual (9.64%), OWL-textual (7.29%), and all keypoint-matching or segmentation-based methods (SuperGlue 3.27%, IEVE 3.52%, PerSAM 2.77%). However, DINOv2's high CE (26.15%) and CM (42.00%) indicate it frequently stops at same-category distractors rather than the true target. Among end-to-end agents, ZSON (visual) achieves the best SPL (7.18%) and competitive SR (9.14%), slightly outperforming RIM-visual (8.80% SR, 6.80% SPL). The paper concludes that modular agents generally outperform end-to-end agents in SR because end-to-end policies struggle with backtracking and cannot exploit the systematic exploration that modular frontier-based agents perform. Notably, even the best method (DINOv2) succeeds in fewer than 1 in 7 episodes, and removing same-category distractors (Table 3) boosts DINOv2's SR to 23.13%, confirming that instance-level discrimination within a category is the core unsolved challenge.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of object-driven embodied navigation (ObjectNav, InstanceImageNav, GOAT-Bench) and personalized object recognition, but departs from all prior formulations by requiring the agent to distinguish a specific instance from same-category distractors using references on a neutral background rather than in-context photos. It builds on the modular agent architecture of SemExp/Mod-IIN/CoW (frontier-based exploration + detection + exploitation) and end-to-end approaches like ZSON and RIM, adapting their detection or conditioning modules to handle multimodal (image + text) instance references. The dataset construction leverages HM3D's photo-realistic scenes and Objaverse-XL's 3D object library, extending the object-injection paradigm of THDA and MultiON with manually curated, rescaled, photo-realistic personal objects placed on semantically appropriate surfaces.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The PIN task exposes a clear gap between category-level and instance-level recognition in embodied settings: even DINOv2, with its strong semantic correspondence properties, achieves only ~15% success rate and predominantly matches the correct category rather than the correct instance. This suggests that current self-supervised and multimodal feature spaces encode category-level but not instance-level identity, a limitation that will need to be addressed before personalized domestic robots can reliably find user-specific objects. The benchmark also reveals that keypoint-matching methods (SuperGlue) that work well for in-context references (InstanceImageNav) degrade significantly when references are on neutral backgrounds, highlighting an underexplored sensitivity. Open questions include whether larger or differently-trained feature spaces can close the instance-discrimination gap, and how agents should handle the inherent ambiguity between visual and textual references.
