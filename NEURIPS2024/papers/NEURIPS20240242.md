## Problem

No AI system exists that can generate parametric, human-editable CAD models from natural language design descriptions. Existing text-to-3D methods produce non-parametric meshes or solid bodies that cannot be decomposed into intermediate sketch-and-extrusion steps, and no large-scale dataset pairs natural language instructions with CAD construction sequences, making it impossible to train a text-to-CAD model.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240242/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts natural language text prompts at four levels of specificity (L0 abstract shape descriptions through L3 detailed parametric instructions with coordinates and dimensions), tokenized to a maximum of 512 BERT tokens. It autoregressively outputs a parametric CAD construction sequence of up to 272 2D tokens in sketch-and-extrude format, where each token is a quantized 8-bit value (256 classes) encoding 2D curve coordinates, extrusion parameters (euler angles, translation, scale, distances, boolean operation), or structural end tokens (curve/loop/face/sketch/extrusion).

## Method

The framework has two components: a data annotation pipeline and a generative network. The pipeline uses LLaVA-NeXT to generate shape descriptions from multi-view renderings of DeepCAD models, then Mixtral-50B with chain-of-thought prompting to produce multi-level text instructions from minimal metadata augmented with those descriptions. The Text2CAD Transformer encodes input text via a pretrained BERT encoder followed by a trainable Adaptive Layer (one transformer encoder block) that aligns embeddings to the CAD token vocabulary. The decoder consists of 8 blocks with layer-wise cross-attention where CAD sequence embeddings serve as queries and text embeddings as keys/values, enabling the model to attend to relevant prompt details at each generation step; the first two blocks omit cross-attention. Training uses teacher-forcing with cross-entropy loss over the quantized token vocabulary, and inference uses top-1 sampling to autoregressively produce the full CAD sequence.

## Results

On the DeepCAD test set with Expert-level (L3) prompts, Text2CAD achieves F1 scores of 81.13 (Line), 36.03 (Arc), 74.25 (Circle), and 93.31 (Extrusion), substantially outperforming the adapted DeepCAD baseline (76.78, 20.04, 65.14, 88.72 respectively). The median Chamfer Distance drops from 32.82×10⁻³ (DeepCAD) to 0.37×10⁻³ (Text2CAD), an ~88.7× improvement, while the Invalidity Ratio decreases from 10.00% to 0.93% (~10.75× reduction). In GPT-4V visual evaluation, Text2CAD is preferred over DeepCAD by 27.18% at the expert level and 18.6% at the intermediate level, with the gap narrowing to near-parity at abstract and beginner levels.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole external baseline is DeepCAD [56], adapted for text input by training its Adaptive Layer to map BERT embeddings to the latent vector, then decoding with the pre-trained DeepCAD decoder. Text2CAD wins decisively on all metrics: it improves Arc F1 by 80% (36.03 vs. 20.04), Circle F1 by 14% (74.25 vs. 65.14), Line F1 by 5.6% (81.13 vs. 76.78), and Extrusion F1 by 5% (93.31 vs. 88.72). The geometric accuracy gap is even more pronounced — median Chamfer Distance is reduced by a factor of ~88.7 (0.37 vs. 32.82 ×10⁻³), indicating DeepCAD recognizes primitive types but fails to parameterize them correctly. The Invalidity Ratio drops from 10.00% to 0.93%, meaning DeepCAD produces unusable CAD sequences roughly 10× more often. In the GPT-4V and user-study evaluations, Text2CAD leads by 27.18% and 17.72% at the expert level respectively, but the advantage shrinks to near-parity (within ~3%) at abstract and beginner levels where prompts contain fewer parametric details.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on CAD-SIGNet [19] for its CAD sequence representation (sketch-and-extrude tokenization with 8-bit quantization) and adopts its architectural choice of omitting cross-attention in the first two decoder blocks. It departs from the two-stage paradigm of DeepCAD [56] (encode-to-latent-then-decode) by proposing an end-to-end autoregressive transformer with layer-wise cross-attention between text and CAD embeddings. The data annotation pipeline is novel, repurposing LLaVA-NeXT for shape description and Mixtral-50B for multi-level instruction generation via chain-of-thought prompting, filling a gap where no textual annotations existed for CAD construction sequences. Unlike text-to-3D methods (Magic3D, ProlificDreamer) that produce non-parametric meshes, or LLM-based scripting approaches that generate non-designer-friendly code, Text2CAD targets editable parametric CAD construction sequences.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first framework to generate parametric, human-editable CAD models from natural language, opening a path toward AI-assisted design tools accessible to all skill levels via multi-level prompts (L0–L3). Key limitations include the dataset's restriction to sketch-and-extrude operations (no fillet, loft, or other CAD operations), the imbalance toward simple rectangular/cylindrical shapes, and the sensitivity of VLM-based annotations to perspective distortion. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to richer CAD operation sets, whether standardized benchmarks for text-to-CAD can be established, and how numerical tokenization in NLP can be improved for parametric precision. The ~660K annotated prompts on DeepCAD provide a useful starting resource for the community.
