## Problem

Conformal prediction provides distribution-free prediction sets with coverage guarantees, but the relationship between prediction set size and the intrinsic uncertainty of the data-generating process (conditional entropy H(Y|X)) had not been formally established. This gap left conformal training objectives ad hoc and offered no principled mechanism for incorporating auxiliary side information into the prediction set construction.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243202/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an input image (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 28×28 for MNIST variants), a pre-trained classifier f, a disjoint calibration set for computing nonconformity score quantiles, and optionally a side information variable Z (e.g., superclass label or device ID) available at test time. It outputs a variable-size prediction set of class labels guaranteed to contain the true label with probability at least 1−α, where the set size serves as the uncertainty measure. When side information is unavailable, the method gracefully falls back to the original model without retraining.

## Method

The paper proves three upper bounds on H(Y|X) by connecting split conformal prediction to variable-size list decoding: a DPI bound derived from the data processing inequality for f-divergences (tighter than cross-entropy), a model-based Fano bound using the predictive model's class probabilities, and a model-agnostic simple Fano bound using only prediction set sizes. These bounds are made differentiable via soft prediction sets and differentiable sorting, yielding principled conformal training loss functions that generalize ConfTr and enable end-to-end training of ResNets from scratch. The information-theoretic framing also yields a side information mechanism: an auxiliary model Q_{Z|X,Y} is trained separately, and at test time Bayes rule updates the class probabilities to Q_{Y|X,Z}, producing smaller prediction sets without retraining the main classifier. In the federated setting, the device ID serves as side information, and the global entropy bound decomposes into local functions amenable to federated averaging.

## Results

The paper evaluates conformal training objectives (DPI, MB Fano, simple Fano bounds) against cross-entropy (CE) and ConfTr on five classification datasets, measuring inefficiency (mean prediction set size) at α=0.01. On the hardest dataset, CIFAR-100, MB Fano achieves 14.61±0.84 (THR) and DPI achieves 17.41±0.62 (APS), substantially outperforming CE (19.70±2.05 THR, 26.02±1.31 APS) and ConfTr (32.80±2.75 THR, 40.58±1.23 APS). On CIFAR-10, DPI achieves 1.64±0.07 (THR) and 1.97±0.08 (APS), beating CE (1.69±0.11, 2.34±0.22) and ConfTr (9.90±0.02, 9.98±0.00). In the federated setting, the simple Fano bound achieves the best results (e.g., 2.63±0.02 on EMNIST THR), while side information reduces CIFAR-100 THR inefficiency from 19.70 to 10.28 at 100% availability.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ConfTr (Stutz et al., ICLR 2022), the paper's information-theoretic bounds deliver dramatic improvements on the challenging CIFAR datasets: on CIFAR-100 THR, MB Fano achieves 14.61 vs. ConfTr's 32.80 (a 55% reduction in set size), and on CIFAR-100 APS, DPI achieves 17.41 vs. ConfTr's 40.58 (a 57% reduction). ConfTr_class performs even worse on CIFAR-100 THR (66.48), suggesting its additional classification loss term is counterproductive when training from scratch. On simpler datasets (MNIST, Fashion-MNIST, EMNIST), all conformal training methods achieve similar results, with the paper's bounds being more consistent (lower variance) than ConfTr. Against Group CP (Vovk et al., 2005) for side information incorporation, the paper's approach wins on EMNIST (1.06 vs. 1.14 THR at 100% SI) but loses slightly on CIFAR-100 (10.28 vs. 9.07 THR), though both substantially outperform standard CP without side information (19.70). In the federated setting, the paper's bounds are more robust to hyperparameter transfer from centralized tuning, while ConfTr methods degrade significantly (e.g., ConfTr reaches 10.00 on CIFAR-10 THR, indicating complete failure to converge).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper establishes the first theoretical bridge between conformal prediction and information theory by recasting split conformal prediction as variable-size list decoding, a connection the authors note has gone unnoticed in the literature. It builds directly on the conformal training framework of Stutz et al. (ConfTr, ICLR 2022) by showing that ConfTr's size loss is equivalent to minimizing a looser upper bound on the conditional entropy H(Y|X), and then derives three tighter, principled alternatives via the data processing inequality for f-divergences and two variants of Fano's inequality. The side information mechanism, which uses an auxiliary model Q_{Z|X,Y} to condition the conformal score, is a novel addition to the CP toolkit with no prior counterpart in the conformal prediction literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The information-theoretic reinterpretation of CP provides a unifying lens that could import further tools from coding theory and network information theory into uncertainty quantification, and the derived bounds enable end-to-end conformal training from scratch on complex architectures (ResNet-34/50 on CIFAR) where ConfTr previously required pretrained models. However, the paper acknowledges that no single bound consistently dominates across datasets and settings, that evaluating bound tightness is difficult without knowing H(Y|X), and that experiments are limited to classification. Open questions include extending the framework to regression, understanding which bound is optimal under what conditions, and whether the list-decoding connection yields further practical algorithms beyond training objectives and side information.
