## Problem

While correlated equilibria in normal-form games and extensive-form correlated equilibria (EFCEs) are known to be computable in polynomial time, the general question of whether exact Φ-equilibria can be computed efficiently for arbitrary sets of linear strategy transformations Φ ⊆ Φ_LIN remained open. In particular, Farina and Pipis (2023) introduced the linear-deviation correlated equilibrium (LCE) and proved efficient no-regret dynamics for it, but left open whether an exact polynomial-time algorithm exists. The Ellipsoid Against Hope algorithm does not apply directly to extensive-form games because they lack the required 'polynomial type' property.

## Input / Output

The algorithm takes a polyhedral game (e.g., an extensive-form game tree or a succinct normal-form utility matrix), a polytope Φ of linear strategy transformations with a polynomial-time separation oracle, and assumes the game satisfies the polynomial utility gradient property. The output is an exact Φ-equilibrium represented as a convex combination of at most N = Σ_p d_p² pure strategy profiles, where d_p is the dimension of player p's strategy polytope. Crucially, even though the joint strategy space can be doubly-exponential in the game size, the final solution is sparse and has encoding length polynomial in the game size.

## Method

The paper introduces a generalization of Ellipsoid Against Hope to bilinear zero-sum games with exponentially large strategy spaces, replacing the best-response oracle with a weaker Good-Enough-Response (GER) oracle that only needs to return a response achieving non-negative utility. The Φ-equilibrium is cast as a min-max problem in a Correlator-Deviator bilinear game, and the ellipsoid method is run on the infeasible dual LP using a composite separation oracle that combines a polytope separation oracle with the GER oracle. The GER oracle constructs a product distribution by computing fixed-points of each player's deviation transformation (via Brouwer's theorem and LP solving), then purifies to a pure strategy profile using Carathéodory's theorem to select at most d_p+1 vertices per player. This sidesteps the NP-hardness of the best-response oracle (proven via a SAT reduction) while still producing sparse, polynomially-encoded solutions.

## Results

The paper establishes that exact Φ-equilibria in polyhedral games can be computed in poly(N, log u, ψ) time, where N = Σp dp² is the total dimension of the transformation polytopes, log u bounds utility encoding length, and ψ bounds facet-complexity. The algorithm produces an equilibrium represented as a convex combination of at most N pure strategy profiles. As a key application, the paper gives the first polynomial-time algorithm for computing exact linear-deviation correlated equilibria (LCEs) in extensive-form games, resolving an open question from Farina and Pipis (2023). The framework also subsumes prior polynomial-time results for CEs in normal-form games and EFCEs in extensive-form games, providing a conceptually simpler proof in both cases.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical comparisons or baselines. Conceptually, the framework generalizes and simplifies three prior polynomial-time algorithms: (1) Papadimitriou and Roughgarden's (2008) Ellipsoid Against Hope for CEs in normal-form games, (2) Jiang and Leyton-Brown's (2015) refinement for compact games, and (3) Huang and von Stengel's (2008) specialized algorithm for EFCEs in extensive-form games. The key advantage is generality: a single framework handles all three cases plus the new LCE result, whereas prior approaches required game-specific combinatorial arguments. The paper also proves (Theorem 4.7) that replacing the Good-Enough-Response oracle with a best-response oracle is NP-hard, establishing that the weaker oracle is not merely a convenience but a necessity for tractability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper generalizes the Ellipsoid Against Hope algorithm (Papadimitriou and Roughgarden, 2008; Jiang and Leyton-Brown, 2015) from normal-form games to arbitrary polyhedral games, and subsumes the specialized EFCE algorithm of Huang and von Stengel (2008) as a special case. The key conceptual departure is replacing the best-response oracle with a weaker 'Good-Enough-Response' (GER) oracle, which the paper proves is not merely sufficient but necessary (best-response is NP-hard to construct). The framework parallels the Φ-regret minimization framework of Gordon et al. (2008) but for exact equilibrium computation rather than approximate learning, and leverages the Hart-Schmeidler (1989) Correlator-Deviator reduction generalized to polyhedral strategy sets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This resolves the open question from Farina and Pipis (2023) on whether exact linear-deviation correlated equilibria (LCE) can be computed in polynomial time in extensive-form games, establishing that the exact-computability frontier extends up to LCE. The GER oracle framework provides a clean, general template that unifies previously ad-hoc algorithms (for CE, EFCE) under a single conceptual umbrella, potentially guiding future algorithm design for other equilibrium notions. Limitations include the high-degree polynomial complexity of the ellipsoid-based approach, the restriction to linear transformations (non-linear Φ remains open), and the requirement of polyhedral (not general convex) strategy sets. Open questions include whether the framework extends to Markov games or to non-linear deviation sets like polynomial swap deviations.
