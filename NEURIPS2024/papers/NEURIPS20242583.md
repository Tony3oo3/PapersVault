## Problem

Existing human preference datasets for aligning text-to-image generative models are either prohibitively expensive to annotate, limited in scale, or provide only coarse single-dimension rankings without fine-grained feedback across multiple quality aspects. The paper asks whether multimodal large language models (MLLMs) can serve as scalable, human-aligned preference annotators that produce fine-grained, multi-dimensional feedback for text-to-image generation.

## Input / Output

The VisionPrefer construction pipeline takes 179K text prompts (polished from DiffusionDB via GPT-4 to remove stylistic biases and filtered for NSFW content) and generates 4 images per prompt using four different diffusion models (SD 1.5 at 512×512, SD 2.1 and Dreamlike at 768×768, SDXL at 1024×1024) with randomized classifier-free guidance scales (3–12). GPT-4V then annotates each image with scalar scores (1–5) across four aspects—Prompt-Following, Aesthetic, Fidelity, and Harmlessness—along with a pairwise ranking and a short textual rationale per aspect, yielding 1.2M preference choices. The downstream VP-Score reward model takes a (prompt, image) pair and outputs a single scalar reward; the fine-tuned generative model takes a text prompt and outputs a 512×512 image.

## Method

The core contribution is a three-stage RLAIF pipeline: (1) prompt curation via GPT-4 polishing and NSFW filtering of DiffusionDB, (2) diverse image generation across four diffusion models with varied CFG scales to stress-test generalization, and (3) structured GPT-4V annotation using detailed per-aspect rubrics that elicit both numerical scores and textual explanations. VP-Score is trained on VisionPrefer using a BLIP backbone with a pairwise ranking loss over the average of the four aspect scores, mirroring the ImageReward architecture. The reward model is then used to fine-tune Stable Diffusion v1.5 via PPO (ReFL) or the preference data is used directly for DPO (D3PO). A key finding is that the multi-aspect fine-grained design outperforms single-aspect reward models on every dimension, and the textual rationales enable a secondary editing pipeline (GPT-4 → InstructPix2Pix) that improves low-scoring images without retraining the generator.

## Results

VP-Score achieves a preference prediction accuracy of 66.3% on ImageRewardDB (best among all models), 79.4% on HPD v2, and 67.1% on Pick-a-Pic, yielding a harmonic-mean average of 70.46% — second only to HPS v2 (71.32%) and ahead of PickScore (70.40%). In PPO fine-tuning experiments, the VP-Score-guided model achieves a Win+Tie ratio exceeding 50% against all competing reward models across three test benchmarks. In DPO experiments, VisionPrefer-optimized models similarly exceed 50% Win+Tie against ImageRewardDB, HPD, and Pick-a-Pic fine-tuned baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VP-Score outperforms ImageReward, HPS, CLIP, and the Aesthetic classifier on all three evaluation benchmarks, with the largest margin over ImageReward (+5.4 pp on HPD v2, +6.0 pp on Pick-a-Pic). Against the strongest baseline HPS v2, VP-Score wins on ImageRewardDB (66.3 vs. 65.7) but trails on HPD v2 (79.4 vs. 83.3) and Pick-a-Pic (67.1 vs. 67.4), resulting in a slightly lower harmonic-mean average (70.46 vs. 71.32). Compared to PickScore, VP-Score leads on ImageRewardDB (66.3 vs. 62.9) and matches on average (70.46 vs. 70.40) but lags on Pick-a-Pic (67.1 vs. 70.5). In the downstream PPO fine-tuning study (Table 4), VP-Score achieves the highest #Win count on DiffusionDB (329) and ReFL (177) benchmarks, with Win+Tie ratios above 50% against every other reward model. In the DPO study (Table 5), VisionPrefer matches or exceeds Pick-a-Pic on all three benchmarks (e.g., 59.44% vs. 58.23% on HPD v2), confirming that MLLM-annotated preferences are competitive with large-scale human-annotated data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the RLAIF paradigm established in NLP (Constitutional AI, RLAIF) to the text-to-image domain, where prior alignment work relied almost exclusively on human-annotated preference datasets (HPD v1/v2, Pick-a-Pic, ImageRewardDB). The reward model VP-Score directly builds on ImageReward's BLIP-based architecture, while the fine-tuning pipeline reuses ReFL (PPO) and D3PO (DPO) implementations. The key conceptual departure is replacing human annotators with GPT-4V to produce multi-aspect (prompt-following, aesthetic, fidelity, harmlessness) fine-grained feedback at scale, addressing the cost and diversity limitations of existing human-preference datasets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that MLLM-annotated preference data yields alignment performance competitive with large-scale human-annotated datasets, this work opens a scalable, low-cost pathway for improving text-to-image alignment without the bottleneck of human annotation. The fine-grained multi-aspect design and inclusion of textual explanations add versatility (e.g., enabling image editing via InstructPix2Pix) that single-scalar human datasets lack. Open questions include whether further scaling VisionPrefer can surpass human-annotated baselines, how well the approach generalizes to newer generative architectures (e.g., SDXL, FLUX), and whether reliance on a single annotator model (GPT-4V) introduces systematic biases that human diversity would mitigate.
