## Problem

Existing methods for aligning text-to-image diffusion models with human preferences rely on either expensive pairwise preference data or a separately trained reward model, both of which limit scalability. Per-image binary feedback (e.g., likes and dislikes) is abundantly available on the internet but has not been effectively leveraged for diffusion model alignment. This paper asks whether a utility-maximization objective, previously shown to work for LLMs via KTO, can be adapted to the multi-step denoising process of diffusion models to enable alignment from simple per-sample binary signals.

## Input / Output

At inference, the method takes a free-form text prompt and produces a single RGB image via the standard Stable Diffusion sampling pipeline (50 denoising steps). At training time, the input is a collection of unpaired (image, caption) pairs each labelled as desirable or undesirable, rather than the paired (preferred, non-preferred, prompt) triples required by DPO-style methods. The training signal is a single binary label per image, and the model is fine-tuned from a pretrained Stable Diffusion checkpoint (v1-5 or v2-1) using the standard latent diffusion architecture.

## Method

Diffusion-KTO extends the Kahneman-Tversky utility maximization framework from KTO to diffusion models by treating the reverse denoising process as a Markov Decision Process, where each denoising step is an action. Instead of maximizing expected reward, the objective maximizes a non-linear utility function U(Q(a,s) − Q_ref) applied at each individual sampling step, which avoids back-propagating a reward through the entire multi-step diffusion chain. The binary feedback signal w(x₀) ∈ {+1, −1} modulates the utility argument, and the reference point Q_ref is estimated as the batch-averaged log-likelihood ratio between the current and reference policies. The authors explore three utility families—loss-averse (concave), risk-seeking (convex), and the Kahneman-Tversky sigmoid (concave in gains, convex in losses)—and find the sigmoid performs best empirically. Training uses biased sampling (80% desirable, 20% undesirable) and a KL penalty with β = 5000, fine-tuning Stable Diffusion v1-5 on 237K desirable and 690K undesirable samples from Pick-a-Pic v2.

## Results

On the Pick-a-Pic v2 test set, Diffusion-KTO (SD v1-5) achieves win-rates of 86.0% (LAION Aesthetics), 85.2% (PickScore), and 87.2% (ImageReward) over the base SD v1-5 model, and 96.6% (PickScore) and 96.2% (CLIP) over AlignProp. In user studies, Diffusion-KTO is preferred 75% of the time over SD v1-5, 72% over SFT, and 69% over Diffusion-DPO. On the HPS v2 and PartiPrompts test sets, Diffusion-KTO maintains consistent advantages, with win-rates above 70% on most metrics against the base model and above 85% against AlignProp.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Diffusion-KTO decisively outperforms the base SD v1-5 model across all five metrics (win-rates 62–87%) and the user study (75% preference). Against AlignProp, Diffusion-KTO achieves the largest margins, winning 96.6% on PickScore and 96.2% on CLIP, indicating that reward-backpropagation methods significantly degrade image-text alignment. Versus D3PO, Diffusion-KTO wins on all metrics with moderate margins (55.6–73.6%). The closest comparison is Diffusion-DPO: Diffusion-KTO leads on Aesthetic (74.2%), ImageReward (78.4%), and PickScore (61.8%), but the advantages on CLIP (53.2%) and HPS v2 (51.6%) are modest, suggesting the two methods are competitive on image-text fidelity. In the user study, Diffusion-KTO is preferred 69% of the time over Diffusion-DPO, confirming a meaningful but smaller human-perceived gap compared to the other baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

Diffusion-KTO extends the Kahneman-Tversky utility maximization framework from KTO (Ethayarajh et al., 2024), which aligned LLMs using per-sample binary feedback, to the setting of diffusion models. It builds on the MDP formulation of the denoising process introduced by D3PO (Yang et al., 2023) and the reward-model-free objective structure of Diffusion-DPO (Wallace et al., 2023), but departs from both by replacing the pairwise preference requirement with per-image binary labels. The key technical contribution is applying the utility maximization objective at each individual denoising step, circumventing the need to back-propagate through the full reverse diffusion process as required by reward-based methods like AlignProp and ReFL.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By showing that per-image binary feedback (likes/dislikes) can match or exceed pairwise preference data for alignment, Diffusion-KTO unlocks the vast reservoir of per-sample feedback already collected on the internet, dramatically lowering the data collection barrier for aligning T2I models. The paper also demonstrates that the choice of utility function (loss-averse, risk-seeking, Kahneman-Tversky) materially affects alignment quality, leaving open the question of which utility model best captures human preference in the generative setting. Limitations include inheriting biases from the Pick-a-Pic training distribution, a marginal increase in NSFW content, and evaluation restricted to SD v1-5 and v2-1, leaving open whether the approach generalizes to newer architectures like SDXL or FLUX.
