## Problem

Existing group recommendation methods require a pre-defined, fixed number of user groups and rely on supervised training with expensive user-group and group-item annotations. In real-time industrial systems (e.g., 130M page views/day), the group count is unknown and shifts daily, making both the fixed-cluster assumption and the annotation pipeline impractical. The paper asks whether group discovery and group recommendation can be performed in a purely unsupervised manner without any group labels.

## Input / Output

The method consumes only the user-item interaction matrix P ∈ R^{n×m} along with the user and item sets; it explicitly does NOT require the user-group distribution A or group-item interaction Q that supervised baselines depend on. The number of groups k' is not specified in advance but is discovered dynamically during training. Outputs include the identified group set G' with learned group embeddings G' ∈ R^{k'×d}, ranked item recommendations for each discovered group, and ranked item recommendations for individual users.

## Method

ITR is a two-stage unsupervised framework. Stage 1 (Group Identification Module) treats each user embedding as an initial candidate center, estimates an adaptive density per candidate via quantile-based radius proposals (Eq. 1–2), then applies a heuristic merge-and-split strategy governed by an explore-and-exploit schedule (greedy parameter α decays with exploration steps) to dynamically create, merge, and split group candidates without a fixed cluster count. Stage 2 (Self-supervised Group Recommendation Module) introduces two pre-text losses: a pull-and-repulsion loss that pulls user embeddings toward their nearest group centers while repelling distinct group centers from each other, and a pseudo-group recommendation loss that estimates the user-group assignment from embedding distances, computes pseudo group-item interactions via A'ᵀP, and trains the group-item scoring function against those pseudo labels. The overall objective (Eq. 12) jointly minimizes the pull-and-repulsion loss, the pseudo-group recommendation loss, and a standard BPR user-item ranking loss, allowing the group embeddings and user/item embeddings to be co-optimized end-to-end.

## Results

On the group recommendation task, ITR achieves 0.9062 average score on Mafengwo and 0.7146 on CAMRa2011, outperforming all 12 supervised baselines without any group annotations. The headline improvements are 22.95% NDCG@5 and 20.40% NDCG@10 on CAMRa2011 group recommendation, and 22.22% NDCG@5 on CAMRa2011 user recommendation. On Mafengwo, ITR gains 8.37% NDCG@5 over the runner-up (CoHeat) for group recommendation and 4.74% NDCG@5 over MeLU for user recommendation. ITR is the only unsupervised method in the comparison, requiring no user-group or group-item labels.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ITR outperforms all 12 supervised baselines on nearly every metric across both datasets, despite using no group annotations. On Mafengwo group recommendation, ITR beats the closest competitor CoHeat (0.8992 HR@5, 0.7924 NDCG@5) by 3.45% HR@5 and 8.37% NDCG@5. On CAMRa2011 group recommendation, the gap is much larger: ITR surpasses GroupIM (the runner-up at 0.6552 HR@5) by 4.00% HR@5 and 22.95% NDCG@5, indicating that supervised methods struggle significantly when group structure is less explicit. The one exception is CAMRa2011 HR@10, where GroupIM (0.8407) and ConsRec (0.8412) slightly edge out ITR (0.7993), which the authors attribute to GroupIM's weaker top-5 ranking precision. For user recommendation, ITR similarly dominates, beating ConsRec (the best supervised method on CAMRa2011) by 22.22% NDCG@5. The motivation experiment (Figure 1) further shows that removing group annotations from ConsRec causes its group recommendation HR@5 to collapse from 0.8844 to 0.0563 on Mafengwo, underscoring the fragility of supervised GR methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the group recommendation (GR) literature by removing two assumptions that all prior deep GR methods share: a predefined group count and supervised group annotations (user-group distribution and group-item interaction). Methodologically, it draws on density-based clustering (adaptive density estimation reminiscent of DBSCAN's core idea, but with automatically estimated radii) and self-supervised pre-text tasks (pull-and-repulsion for embedding optimization, pseudo-label generation for recommendation), while following ConsRec's BPR loss for the user-item recommendation component. The heuristic merge-and-split strategy with an explore-and-exploit schedule is a novel procedural contribution that distinguishes it from both fixed-k clustering and prior group-discovery methods (CFAG, DiRec, GTGS) that still consume group labels.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine industrial pain point: real-time recommendation systems with dynamic, unknown group structures cannot afford the annotation costs of supervised GR, and the authors demonstrate deployment on a 130M-PV/day livestreaming platform with ~2.45% average metric gains. However, the evaluation is limited to two small public datasets (Mafengwo, CAMRa2011), and the comparison is structurally favorable since all baselines are supervised while ITR is unsupervised, making it difficult to isolate how much of the gain comes from the method versus the absence of label leakage. The authors acknowledge that ITR still requires user-item interaction data, leaving fully unsupervised or zero-shot group recommendation as an open direction.
