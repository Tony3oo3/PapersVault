## Problem

Existing multivariate time series models struggle to simultaneously capture long-term trends, seasonal patterns, and dynamic inter-variate dependencies while maintaining linear training complexity. Classical SSMs are limited to univariate settings with linear dependencies and static parameters, while deep SSM variants are either univariate, input-independent, or fail to model seasonal and cross-variate dynamics adaptively. The paper asks whether a single architecture can provably recover classical methods (SARIMA, exponential smoothing), model input-dependent variate selection, and train efficiently on very long sequences.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243809/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Chimera consumes a multivariate time series X ∈ R^{N×T} (N variates, T time steps) and treats it as a 2D grid where the first axis is time and the second is variates. For forecasting, it outputs the next H time steps for each variate via a closed-loop 2D SSM decoder that explicitly models future input time-steps with additional projection matrices D₁ and D₂. For classification and anomaly detection, it produces a scalar class label or binary anomaly score. The model operates on sequences up to 16K steps (speech) and 9K variates (brain activity), where Transformer baselines hit out-of-memory limits.

## Method

Chimera is a three-headed 2D SSM that decomposes the input into trend and seasonal components using two 2D-SSM modules with different learnable discretization steps (Δ₁ for long-term progression, Δ_s for seasonal frequency), combined via residual connections. Transition matrices A₁, A₂ use companion structure along the time axis while A₃, A₄ use diagonal structure along the variate axis, enabling provable recovery of SARIMA, ARIMA, and exponential smoothing. Parameters B, C, and Δ are made input-dependent (via linear projections and Softplus), providing a data-driven selection mechanism that filters irrelevant variates. To handle the 2D recurrence efficiently, the paper reformulates it as a 2D parallel prefix sum using a novel associative operator *, achieving ~4× faster training than naive recurrence while remaining close to convolutional-form speed. A bi-directional recurrence along the variate axis and a SwiGLU gating head further enhance expressiveness, and a closed-loop decoder extends the model to long-horizon autoregressive forecasting.

## Results

On long-term forecasting (Table 1), Chimera achieves the best or second-best MSE across all 8 benchmark datasets (ETTm1: 0.355, ETTm2: 0.252, ETTh1: 0.408, ETTh2: 0.321, ECL: 0.154, Exchange: 0.311, Traffic: 0.403, Weather: 0.219), outperforming all baselines in 5 out of 8 datasets. On ECG classification (PTB-XL), Chimera reaches 94.1% overall accuracy, surpassing SpaceTime (93.6%) and S4 (93.8%). On the brain activity case study (BVFC, 9K variates), Chimera achieves 69.41% accuracy versus 41.20% for SpaceTime and 40.89% for S4, while Transformer-based methods hit OOM. On M4 short-term forecasting, Chimera attains the best weighted-average SMAPE (11.618), MASE (1.528), and OWA (0.827).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On long-term forecasting, Chimera and TCN (ModernTCN) are the top two methods, with Chimera winning on ETTm2, ETTh2, ECL, and Weather while TCN edges ahead on ETTm1 (0.351 vs 0.355), ETTh1 (0.404 vs 0.408), Exchange (0.302 vs 0.311), and Traffic (0.398 vs 0.403); both are clearly ahead of Transformer-based baselines like iTransformer and PatchTST, and significantly ahead of TimesNet and DLinear. On ECG classification, Chimera (94.1%) beats SpaceTime (93.6%), S4 (93.8%), and all CNN/RNN baselines, with the largest gap on the Form task (90.1% vs 88.3% for SpaceTime). On speech classification, Chimera (98.40%) narrowly beats S4 (98.32%) and SpaceTime (97.29%), while LSSL and Transformer fail with OOM at 16K sequence length. On the brain activity task (BVFC, 9K variates), Chimera's data-dependent parameters yield a dramatic 69.41% vs 41.20% (SpaceTime) and 40.89% (S4), with Transformer-based methods unable to run due to memory constraints. On M4 short-term forecasting, Chimera achieves the best SMAPE (11.618) and OWA (0.827), outperforming ModernTCN (11.698/0.838) and all other baselines including N-HiTS, N-BEATS, and DLinear. The ablation study confirms that removing input-dependent parameters (convolution form) degrades ETTh1 MSE from 0.408 to 0.473, highlighting the critical role of data-dependent selection.

## Relation to Prior Work

Chimera unifies three previously separate lines of work: (1) 1D selective state space models (Mamba/S6, S4) that introduced input-dependent parameters and parallel scan training, (2) discrete SSMs for time series (SpaceTime) that established companion-matrix parameterization for capturing autoregressive dependencies, and (3) 2D state space layers (2DSSM, S4ND) that extended SSMs to two spatial dimensions. The key departure is the introduction of a ZOH discretization step in the 2D setting (absent in prior 2D SSM work), which enables resolution control for seasonal patterns and a gating-like mechanism along the variate axis, combined with a novel 2D parallel selective scan that makes input-dependent 2D recurrence tractable. Theoretically, Chimera is positioned as a strict generalization: it provably recovers SARIMA, ARIMA, exponential smoothing, SpaceTime, S4ND, TSM2, and TSMixer as special cases.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Chimera offers a principled, linear-complexity alternative to quadratic-attention transformers for multivariate time series, with the practical advantage of a single architecture spanning forecasting, classification, and anomaly detection without task-specific redesign. The 2D selective scan algorithm is a reusable contribution that could accelerate other 2D recurrent architectures. Open questions include whether the companion+diagonal matrix structure is truly optimal (vs. other structured parameterizations), how the method scales to very large variate counts (e.g., >10K brain channels) where even linear-in-V may become costly, and whether the discretization-based seasonal module generalizes to irregularly sampled or non-stationary series. The paper does not extensively explore failure modes or compare against the most recent 2024 SSM-based time series methods beyond Simba.
