## Problem

Existing tool-use benchmarks for LLM agents rely on AI-generated queries with explicitly stated tool steps, simulated or virtual tools, and text-only interactions, failing to capture the reasoning, planning, and multimodal understanding required in real-world scenarios. This makes it difficult to assess whether agents can actually solve end-to-end tasks where the appropriate tools and their invocation sequence must be inferred from an implicit, human-written request accompanied by authentic visual context.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242411/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each task provides a human-written natural-language query (step-implicit and tool-implicit) paired with one or two authentic image files (spatial scenes, web screenshots, tables, code snippets, handwritten materials) and descriptions of 14 available tools. The agent must produce a multi-step ReAct-format tool invocation chain and a final answer, which is evaluated under two modes: step-by-step (predicting the next action given prior steps) and end-to-end (actually executing tools and comparing the result). Queries are typed as objective (unique answer checked via whitelist/blacklist), subjective (scored by cosine similarity against three reference answers using All-MPNet-Base-V2), or image-generation (scored by parameter accuracy of creative tools).

## Method

GTA introduces 229 human-designed tasks constructed via an exemplar-expansion pipeline: experts create initial queries spanning diverse tool combinations, and annotators diversify scenarios while preserving the tool set. Each task is paired with a manually verified, executable ground-truth tool chain (tool name, arguments, return values per step) built by actually invoking 14 deployed tools across perception, operation, logic, and creativity categories. Evaluation uses the Lagent framework with ReAct prompting on the OpenCompass platform, and introduces fine-grained metrics—InstAcc, ToolAcc, ArgAcc, SummAcc for step-by-step mode, and AnsAcc plus per-category F1 scores for end-to-end mode—that decompose the tool-use process to pinpoint where models fail. The paper evaluates 16 LLMs (GPT-4, GPT-4o, Claude-3, Llama-3, Qwen1.5, Mistral, Mixtral, Yi, DeepSeek, etc.) and identifies argument prediction as the dominant bottleneck, with GPT-4 completing fewer than 50% of tasks and most models below 25%.

## Results

On the GTA benchmark (229 real-world tasks with 14 executable tools), GPT-4-1106-Preview achieves the highest end-to-end answer accuracy (AnsAcc) of 46.59%, while GPT-4o follows at 41.52%. All other evaluated LLMs score below 25%, with the best open-source model (Qwen1.5-72B-Chat) reaching only 13.32% AnsAcc. The step-by-step analysis reveals that argument prediction accuracy (ArgAcc) is the dominant bottleneck: even GPT-4 achieves only 37.88% ArgAcc, and most open-source models score below 10%, confirming a bucket-effect where the weakest step governs overall task completion.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GPT-4-1106-Preview leads all 16 evaluated LLMs with 46.59% AnsAcc, outperforming GPT-4o (41.52%) by ~5 points despite GPT-4o having higher ToolAcc (70.38 vs. 61.40) and SummAcc (72.77 vs. 75.00), illustrating that argument prediction (ArgAcc: 37.88 vs. 35.19) and instruction following (InstAcc: 85.19 vs. 86.42) jointly determine final performance. Among API-based models, GPT-3.5-Turbo (23.62%) and Claude-3-Opus (23.44%) form a second tier, while Mistral-Large trails at 17.06%. In the open-source category, Qwen1.5-72B-Chat (13.32%) substantially outperforms Llama-3-70B-Instruct (8.32%) and Deepseek-LLM-67B-Chat (9.51%), despite Llama-3-70B having higher ToolAcc (36.80 vs. 24.96), again pointing to ArgAcc as the decisive factor. The Agent-Flan-7B fine-tuning experiment (Table 7) shows that instruction tuning on ReAct/JSON data improves InstAcc from 30.86 to 71.60 and ToolAcc from 16.34 to 41.11 over Llama-2-Chat-7B, yet ArgAcc remains low (6.82), confirming that format compliance alone is insufficient for real-world tool-use.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of tool-use benchmarking (ToolBench, APIBench, API-Bank, m&m's) by addressing three gaps the authors identify: AI-generated queries with explicit tool hints, virtual/simulated tools, and text-only interactions. It is most closely related to GAIA, which also targets real-world scenarios with multimodal inputs, but GTA narrows the focus specifically to tool-agent evaluation with executable deployed tools and fine-grained tool-chain metrics rather than general AI assistant capability. The evaluation infrastructure builds on the ReAct prompting schema and the Lagent/AgentLego agent framework, positioning the contribution as a benchmark and empirical study rather than a new agent architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals that even GPT-4 completes fewer than 50% of real-world tool-use tasks, and identifies argument prediction (not tool selection or instruction following) as the dominant bottleneck via a 'bucket effect' analysis. The finding that Agent-Flan fine-tuning improves format compliance but leaves argument prediction largely unchanged suggests a specific open problem: how to teach models to generate correct tool arguments in context. Limitations include English-only queries, a modest 229-task scale, and a 14-tool set, leaving open whether findings generalize to larger tool ecosystems and multilingual settings.
