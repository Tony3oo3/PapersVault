## Problem

Existing prompt optimization methods focus on generating candidate prompts but largely ignore the cost of selecting among them, typically evaluating all prompts on the full development set without regard for the financial, temporal, or API-quota constraints of LLM access. No principled framework has been established for efficiently allocating a limited evaluation budget across candidate prompts to identify the optimal one.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243160/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pre-generated pool of candidate prompts (e.g., from APE or APO pipelines), a hard budget N specifying the maximum number of LLM interactions, and optionally sentence embeddings of the prompts (via text-embedding-ada-002, projected to 64 dimensions for GSE). It outputs a single selected prompt (or, in the few-shot extension, a set of M examples) that maximizes expected task performance under the budget constraint. The budget is the critical constraint: when |P| > N, methods without embeddings cannot even allocate one evaluation per prompt, making embedding-based variants essential.

## Method

TRIPLE establishes a formal equivalence between prompt selection and fixed-budget best arm identification (BAI-FB) in multi-armed bandits: each prompt is an arm, each LLM call is a pull, and the score function provides the reward. Two BAI-FB algorithms are instantiated as TRIPLE-SH (Sequential Halving, dividing the budget into log|P| phases and halving the active set each phase) and TRIPLE-CR (Continuously Reject, adaptively eliminating prompts after each pull using large-deviation criteria). For large prompt pools where |P| may exceed N, two embedding-based enhancements are proposed: TRIPLE-CLST clusters prompts by embedding similarity and runs BAI-FB in two phases (first selecting the best cluster, then the best prompt within it), while TRIPLE-GSE trains a reward function on prompt embeddings to guide elimination decisions across phases. The key distinction from prior MAB-based prompt selection (e.g., UCB, NeuralUCB) is that BAI-FB optimizes the probability of correctly identifying the best arm, which matches the prompt optimization objective, whereas regret-minimization methods optimize cumulative reward over the interaction sequence and are provably suboptimal for identification.

## Results

On 12 representative tasks across GPT-3.5, Llama2, Gemma, and Mistral, TRIPLE methods consistently achieve the best or near-best average performance rank. Without prompt embeddings (|P|=30, N=150), TRIPLE-SH and TRIPLE-CR achieve average ranks of ~2.0–2.3 vs. Uniform (3.1–3.3) and UCB (2.5–2.7), corresponding to 3–16% performance improvements. With prompt embeddings (|P|=150, N=100), TRIPLE-CLST and TRIPLE-GSE achieve average ranks of 1.5–2.3 vs. BO (2.7–2.8) and NeuralUCB (3.2–3.9), corresponding to 16–45% improvements. In end-to-end APE and APO pipelines, TRIPLE-CR/CLST/GSE improve average performance rank from 4.00 (Uniform) to 1.50–1.58 (APE) and from 2.75 (UCB) to 1.25–2.00 (APO).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TRIPLE outperforms all five baselines across all tested LLMs and both budget settings. Against UCB (Auer et al. [5]), which is designed for regret minimization rather than best-arm identification, TRIPLE-SH/CR achieve 3–7% average improvements without embeddings, confirming the paper's argument that regret-minimization algorithms are suboptimal for the selection objective. Against BO with EI/PI (Chen et al. [13]), TRIPLE-CLST/GSE achieve 10–56% improvements when |P|<N and 21–42% when |P|>N, with the gap widening as the budget becomes tighter relative to the pool size. Against NeuralUCB (Lin et al. [48]), TRIPLE-CLST/GSE achieve 17–27% improvements (|P|<N) and 16–45% improvements (|P|>N), again showing that UCB-based exploration is less efficient for pure identification. In end-to-end APE (Zhou et al. [90]), replacing Uniform selection with TRIPLE-CR/CLST/GSE improves average rank from 4.00 to 1.50–1.58; in APO (Pryzant et al. [60]), replacing UCB with TRIPLE-CR/GSE improves average rank from 2.75 to 1.25–2.00. The gains are most pronounced under tight budgets (5 evaluations per prompt) and diminish as budget increases to 30 evaluations per prompt, where all methods converge to near-optimal selection.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the prompt optimization literature (APE, APO, GrIPS) where the standard pipeline is to generate a candidate prompt pool and then select from it, but departs by reframing the selection step as a fixed-budget best arm identification (BAI-FB) problem rather than a regret minimization problem. Prior MAB-based prompt selection work (Pryzant et al. using UCB, Lin et al. using NeuralUCB) implicitly treated prompt selection as cumulative reward maximization, which the paper argues is the wrong objective since the goal is to identify the single best prompt, not to maximize interaction quality over time. The embedding-based extensions (TRIPLE-CLST, TRIPLE-GSE) draw on structured bandits and function approximation literature to handle large prompt pools, and the few-shot example selection extension connects to combinatorial bandits.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By explicitly incorporating budget constraints and providing a principled BAI-FB framework, this work makes prompt optimization more accessible for practitioners facing API costs, rate limits, and expensive evaluation. The conceptual reframing (BAI rather than regret minimization) corrects a mismatch in prior MAB-based prompt selection and opens the door to the entire BAI-FB toolbox (including Bayesian, cost-aware, and multi-objective variants) for future prompt optimization research. Open questions include handling prompt-specific costs (token-based pricing), contextual bandits for input-dependent prompts, and whether the fixed-pool assumption can be relaxed to joint generation-and-selection. The finding that modest prompt pools (~100) suffice for selection is practically useful but limits the need for expensive large-scale generation.
