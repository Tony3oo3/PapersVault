## Problem

Diffusion models trained via behavioral cloning suffer severe sample-quality degradation when generation steps are reduced, because the inference trajectory deviates from the fine-grained training trajectory. Existing IRL-based fine-tuning for diffusion models relies on policy gradients (slow, memory-intensive back-prop through time) or adversarial training (limited exploration), and the marginal entropy required for maximum entropy RL is intractable to estimate for discrete-time samplers like DDPM. This paper addresses both the formulation gap (maximum entropy IRL for diffusion) and the algorithmic gap (an efficient update that avoids back-propagation through time and marginal entropy computation).

## Input / Output

The method fine-tunes pretrained diffusion backbones (DDPM, DDGAN, EDM) to generate images in as few as 4–10 NFE on CIFAR-10 (32×32), ImageNet (64×64), and LSUN Bedroom (256×256), starting from publicly available checkpoints with adjusted noise schedules. For anomaly detection on MVTec-AD, 224×224 RGB images are projected to 272-dimensional features via a frozen EfficientNet-b4, and the trained EBM (an autoencoder reconstruction error) produces per-spatial-location energy scores that are max-pooled for detection and bilinearly upsampled to 224×224 for localization. The EBM simultaneously serves as the log-density reward for the diffusion model during training and as a standalone anomaly detector at test time.

## Method

DxMI formulates diffusion model fine-tuning as a Generalized Contrastive Divergence (GCD) minimax problem that jointly optimizes a diffusion model π(x) and an EBM q(x): the EBM supplies log-density as reward while the diffusion model supplies negative samples, reaching Nash equilibrium when both converge to the data distribution. The core algorithmic contribution is DxDP, a dynamic programming subroutine that (i) replaces the intractable marginal entropy with easily computable conditional entropies via a data-processing-inequality upper bound, and (ii) eliminates back-propagation through time by introducing value functions updated with Bellman-residual (temporal-difference) losses, turning the diffusion model update into a one-step policy improvement guided by the value function. An Adaptive Velocity Regularization (AVR) mechanism analytically tightens the auxiliary Gaussian distribution at each step, and a time cost R(t) stabilizes early-training value learning. Entropy maximization (temperature τ) plays a dual role: it encourages exploration in the diffusion model and stabilizes EBM training by ensuring diverse negative samples, with ablations showing that τ=0 collapses to GAN-like behavior and τ too large produces noise-like samples.

## Results

On ImageNet 64×64, DxMI fine-tuned from EDM achieves FID 2.68 at 10 NFE and FID 3.21 at 4 NFE, dramatically surpassing the unadjusted EDM Ancestral starting points (FID 50.27 and 82.95 respectively). On CIFAR-10, DxMI at 10 NFE reaches FID 3.19, matching the full 1000-step DDPM (FID 3.21) while using 100× fewer evaluations. On MVTec-AD anomaly detection, DxMI achieves 97.0% detection AUC and 97.1% localization AUC, outperforming UniAD (96.5/96.8) and MPDR (96.0/96.7).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DxMI substantially outperforms its own starting point: on ImageNet 64, fine-tuning EDM Ancestral from FID 50.27 (10 NFE) to 2.68 represents a ~19× FID reduction, and from 82.95 (4 NFE) to 3.21. Against distillation-based methods, DxMI at 10 NFE (FID 2.68) beats Consistency Model at 2 NFE (FID 4.70) and 1 NFE (FID 6.20), and PD at 1 NFE (FID 15.39), while using only 10 evaluations. Against the IRL baseline SFT-PG on CIFAR-10 (same backbone and checkpoint), DxMI achieves FID 3.19 vs. SFT-PG's 4.82 at 10 NFE, attributed to dynamic programming over policy gradients and the maximum entropy principle. On CIFAR-10, DxMI at 10 NFE (FID 3.19) matches full-length DDPM at 1000 NFE (FID 3.21) and outperforms DDIM (FID 13.36) and FastDPM (FID 35.85) at the same 10 NFE. On MVTec-AD, DxMI leads all anomaly detection baselines by 0.5–0.8 AUC points over UniAD and MPDR, and by ~9 points over DRAEM, with the gap widening dramatically when entropy regularization is removed (τ=0 drops DET to 67.9). DxMI does not support single-step generation, so it cannot directly compete with 1-NFE methods like Consistency Model or BigGAN, but at 4 NFE it already surpasses them on FID.

## Relation to Prior Work

DxMI unifies three previously separate threads—maximum entropy IRL (Ziebart 2008, Finn 2016), Contrastive Divergence for EBM training (Hinton 2002), and discrete-time diffusion model training (Ho 2020)—into a single minimax objective (Generalized CD) where a diffusion model serves as the sampler replacing MCMC. The key algorithmic departure is DxDP, which applies discrete-time dynamic programming with value functions to update the diffusion model, avoiding both marginal entropy estimation and back-propagation through time that plague policy-gradient approaches like SFT-PG. While the GCD objective has appeared in EBM literature under names like variational maximum likelihood, no prior work had used a diffusion model as the sampler π(x), where the optimization and entropy estimation challenges are substantially harder.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: DxMI fine-tunes diffusion models to generate competitive samples in as few as 4 NFE on ImageNet 64 (FID 3.21) and 10 NFE on CIFAR-10 (FID 3.19, matching 1000-step DDPM), offering a principled alternative to distillation that can surpass the teacher. The MCMC-free EBM training is a useful byproduct, stabilizing energy function learning and improving anomaly detection (97.0 AUC on MVTec-AD). Open questions include the lack of convergence guarantees for the minimax objective, the sensitivity to multiple hyperparameters (τ₁, τ₂, time cost form, value function design), and the inability to vary generation steps at test time—limitations the authors acknowledge but leave for future work.
