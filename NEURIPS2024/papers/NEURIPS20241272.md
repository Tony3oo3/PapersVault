## Problem

Standard pre-training for object detection relies on random initialization and uniform sampling, which fails under long-tailed class distributions where tail classes are severely underrepresented. A critical but overlooked issue is simplicity bias, where networks favor easy predictive patterns and neglect the complex features needed to recognize rare objects. Existing pre-training methods either ignore the long-tailed imbalance during pre-training or fail to align the pre-training objective with the downstream detection task.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images that are randomly augmented into two views (x and x_+) for contrastive learning, with a class-agnostic detector generating 8 bounding box proposals per image for the local contrastive and reconstruction components. During pre-training, the network is trained end-to-end on these augmented pairs plus a detection loss; at inference, the fine-tuned Faster R-CNN or Mask R-CNN outputs bounding boxes, class labels, and (for Mask R-CNN) pixel-level segmentation masks. The generator for appearance reconstruction is a 4-layer deconvolutional network (2048→512→256→64→3 channels) that reconstructs the input image from the encoder's dense features.

## Method

2DRCL combines three mechanisms: (1) Holistic-Local Contrastive Learning, where image-level MoCo-style contrastive learning (HCL) learns generic representations while object-level contrastive learning (LCL) aligns the backbone and detection head with object proposals via a queue-based negative dictionary; (2) Dynamic Rebalancing, which computes a combined score per class as a harmonic mean of image-level and instance-level frequency ratios, with a dynamic weight α_d = T/T_max that progressively shifts sampling emphasis from image-level to instance-level balancing as training advances; (3) Dual Reconstruction, where an auto-encoder enforces pixel-level MSE reconstruction (Appearance Reconstruction) to capture fine visual details, and a masked-reconstruction feature-matching loss across P network layers (Semantic Reconstruction) enforces self-consistency to counter simplicity bias. The total loss is L_HLCL + L_DRC + L_det, with all weights set to 1, and the entire network is trained end-to-end.

## Results

On LVIS v1.0 (Faster R-CNN R50-FPN, 2× schedule), 2DRCL achieves 27.3% AP^bb and 18.6% AP_r^bb, outperforming the strongest baseline ECM (26.7% / 17.5%) by 0.6% and 1.1% respectively. On COCO (Mask R-CNN R50-FPN, 1× schedule), 2DRCL reaches 41.4% AP^bb and 37.4% AP^mk, surpassing AlignDet (39.4% / 35.3%) and the supervised ImageNet-pretrained baseline (38.3% / 34.3%). With Mask R-CNN R101-FPN on LVIS v1.0, the method attains 28.8% AP and 21.1% AP_r, maintaining consistent gains over all competitors across rare, common, and frequent class groups.

## Baselines & Comparisons

2DRCL consistently outperforms all 23 compared baselines on LVIS v1.0. Against the strongest long-tailed detection method ECM [24], 2DRCL gains +0.6% AP^bb and +1.1% AP_r^bb (27.3/18.6 vs 26.7/17.5) with Faster R-CNN R50-FPN, and +0.4/+0.4 with Mask R-CNN R101-FPN (28.8/21.1 vs 28.7/21.9). Against pre-training baselines on LVIS (Table 2), 2DRCL improves over AlignDet [27] by +1.3% AP^bb and +1.6% AP_r^bb (23.9/11.9 vs 22.6/10.3), and over SoCo [52] by +6.3% AP^bb and +6.6% AP_r^bb. On COCO (Table 1), 2DRCL surpasses AlignDet by +2.0% AP^bb and +2.1% AP^mk while using only 12 pre-training epochs versus 200–800 epochs required by from-scratch methods like DenseCL [51] and SoCo [52]. The gains are most pronounced on rare classes (AP_r), where 2DRCL leads ECM by 1.1% and Seesaw [47] by 1.8%, confirming the method's targeted effectiveness on tail classes without sacrificing frequent-class performance (AP_f: 32.6 vs 32.2 for ECM).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the self-supervised pre-training line for object detection (SoCo, AlignDet, DenseCL) by explicitly targeting long-tailed distributions, a gap those methods leave unaddressed. It builds on MoCo's momentum contrastive framework for holistic learning and on RFS's repeat-factor sampling for rebalancing, but departs from both by introducing instance-level (object-proposal) rebalancing alongside image-level sampling and by adding a dual reconstruction objective (appearance + semantic) to counter simplicity bias. The positioning is that prior pre-training methods either ignore tail-class imbalance or only pre-train the backbone, while prior long-tailed detection methods (EQL, Seesaw, ECM) operate purely at fine-tuning without a dedicated pre-training stage.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies simplicity bias as an underexplored contributor to tail-class failure in detection pre-training and proposes a concrete mechanism (dual reconstruction) to mitigate it, which could inform future pre-training designs beyond long-tailed settings. However, the computational overhead is non-trivial (106 GB VRAM, ~20.6 h for 6+6 epochs vs. 94 GB / 16.1 h for ECM alone), and no code is released, limiting immediate reproducibility. Open questions include whether the dynamic rebalancing and dual reconstruction components transfer to one-stage or transformer-based detectors at scale, and whether the simplicity-bias framing generalizes to other imbalanced recognition tasks.
