## Problem

Deep ensembles benefit from both low sharpness (flat minima) of individual members and high diversity among them, but prior work has not characterized how these two properties interact. This paper identifies and formally proves a sharpness-diversity trade-off: applying sharpness-aware training (e.g., SAM) to reduce individual model sharpness simultaneously diminishes inter-member diversity, thereby undermining the ensemble's collective improvement. The gap is both theoretical (no prior analysis of the joint dynamics) and practical (no method that reduces sharpness without sacrificing diversity).

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 64×64 for TinyImageNet) paired with class labels, plus a per-sample sharpness signal computed as the squared L2 norm of the per-sample gradient (a Fisher Information Matrix trace approximation). A distinctive operational detail is that each ensemble member's training set is dynamically partitioned into a sharpness-aware subset and a normal subset, where the sharpness-aware subset is the union of top-k% sharpest samples identified from all *other* ensemble members. The ensemble outputs averaged logits for classification and uncertainty estimates, evaluated on both in-distribution and corrupted (OOD) test sets.

## Method

SharpBalance trains each ensemble member f_θi by applying SAM (sharpness-aware minimization) only on a data subset D_SAM^i and standard gradient descent on the complement D_Normal^i. The subset D_SAM^i is constructed adaptively: for every other ensemble member, the top-k% training samples with highest per-data-sample sharpness (squared L2 gradient norm) are selected, and D_SAM^i is the union of all such subsets excluding member i's own sharpest samples. This diversification ensures each member minimizes sharpness on different data, preserving inter-member diversity. Theoretically, Theorem 1 proves that SAM on the full dataset causes diversity to shrink as sharpness decreases, while Theorem 2 shows that training on S random subsets yields a strictly better trade-off with the sharpness upper bound scaling as 1/S. Empirically, SharpBalance adds only ~1% training overhead over SAM while improving ensemble accuracy on both ID and OOD benchmarks across ResNet18, WideResNet, ViT, and ALBERT.

## Results

On CIFAR-10-C (severity 5), SharpBalance achieves 60.78% OOD accuracy, outperforming Deep Ensemble (58.63%) and Deep Ensemble+SAM (58.77%) by 2.01 and 2.01 points respectively. On CIFAR-100-C (severity 5), SharpBalance reaches 36.20% versus 34.16% (Deep Ensemble) and 34.81% (Deep Ensemble+SAM). The improvement grows with corruption severity, reaching up to 1.30% when ensembling 5 models on CIFAR-100-C. SharpBalance adds only ~1% extra training time over SAM-based ensembles.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SharpBalance consistently outperforms all four baselines across CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and TinyImageNet (both ID and OOD). Against the standard Deep Ensemble (Lakshminarayanan et al., 2017), SharpBalance improves OOD accuracy by 1.1–2.1 points on CIFAR-10-C and 1.1–2.0 points on CIFAR-100-C, with gains increasing as corruption severity rises. Against Deep Ensemble+SAM (Foret et al., 2021), the key finding is that SAM improves individual model accuracy but reduces ensemble diversity, shrinking the ensemble improvement rate; SharpBalance recovers this lost diversity while retaining the sharpness benefit, yielding net gains of 0.3–2.0 points over SAM. Against EoA (Arpit et al., 2022) and GSAM (Zhuang et al., 2022), SharpBalance significantly outperforms both, as reported in Appendix F. The advantage becomes more pronounced with larger ensemble sizes (up to 1.30% with 5 models on CIFAR-100-C) and with more severe corruptions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Sharpness-Aware Minimization (SAM) and the deep ensemble framework, identifying a previously uncharacterized interaction between the two: applying SAM to each ensemble member reduces individual sharpness but simultaneously diminishes inter-model diversity. While DiWA (Rame et al., 2022) noted diversity loss from SAM in the weight-averaging context, and DASH (Bui et al., 2024) addressed diversity in flat ensembles via KL constraints, this work is the first to provide a rigorous random-matrix-theoretic characterization of the sharpness-diversity trade-off in unconstrained deep ensembles and to derive a data-subset-based training strategy (SharpBalance) from that analysis. The theoretical tools draw on Wishart moment analysis and Narayana numbers, extending the SAM analysis of Behdin and Mazumder (2023) to the multi-model, data-partitioned setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of the sharpness-diversity trade-off reveals a fundamental limitation of naively applying flat-minima optimizers to ensembles: the very mechanism that improves individual model generalization undermines the ensemble's collective benefit. SharpBalance offers a practical remedy with only ~1% additional training cost, and the theoretical framework provides provable guarantees on the improved trade-off. Key limitations include the Gaussian data and quadratic objective assumptions in the theory, and the empirical gains, while consistent across datasets and architectures, are modest (0.3–2% OOD accuracy). Open questions include whether the trade-off persists under non-quadratic losses, whether the data-subset strategy can be made fully adaptive during training, and whether the theoretical bounds tighten under weaker distributional assumptions.
