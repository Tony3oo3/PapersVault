## Problem

Active learning research lacks a standardized, cross-domain evaluation framework: prior benchmarks are confined to a single domain (mostly images), employ too few experimental repetitions (often 3) to draw statistically valid conclusions, and do not account for the interplay between AL and semi-supervised learning. This makes it impossible to determine whether a method's superiority is domain-specific or general, and whether reported performance gains are real or artifacts of seed selection.

## Input / Output

Each AL method receives at every iteration the current labeled set, unlabeled pool, classifier parameters, optimizer state, and meta-information (remaining budget, seed set size, current and initial accuracy), but is forbidden from accessing the test/validation set or external data. Datasets span image (CIFAR-10, FashionMNIST with ResNet18), text (TopV2, News Category with BiLSTM), tabular (Splice, DNA, USPS with MLP), and two synthetic tasks, each available in both raw and pre-encoded (SimCLR-embedded, linear classifier) forms. The benchmark outputs per-dataset AUC scores averaged over 50 repetitions and multiple query sizes, aggregated into rank tables and Wilcoxon-signed-rank critical difference diagrams for statistical significance testing.

## Method

CDALBench introduces three key technical components. First, a greedy oracle algorithm that at each iteration uniformly samples τ=20 candidate points, trains the classifier on each candidate addition, and selects the one yielding the highest test accuracy (falling back to margin sampling if no point improves performance), achieving a ~5% lift over the best AL method at O(Bτ) cost. Second, a novel seeding strategy that decouples random number generators for the AL method, data splitting/mini-batch sampling, and model initialization/dropout, ensuring that different AL methods receive identical starting conditions while still introducing controlled stochasticity across the 50 repetitions. Third, two synthetic datasets—Honeypot (a noisy region placed on the decision boundary to trap uncertainty-based methods) and Diverging Sine (diverging sinusoidal class distributions that penalize diversity-based clustering methods)—generated fresh each experiment to expose principled algorithmic shortcomings without memorization.

## Results

CDALBench evaluates 12 AL methods (plus a greedy oracle) across 9 real-world datasets spanning tabular, image, and text domains, with 50 repetitions per experiment. No single method achieves SOTA across all domains: the top-3 methods (Margin, Galaxy, Badge) have average ranks of 4.8, 4.9, and 5.1 respectively on un-encoded datasets, with the Top-3 set differing for every domain. The image domain is a notable outlier where LeastConfident sampling (rank 2.6 on CIFAR-10) dramatically outperforms Margin sampling (rank 7.2), while Margin dominates tabular data (rank 2.7 on USPS) and Galaxy leads on text (rank 2.0 on News). The greedy oracle outperforms the best AL method by at least 5% AUC on every dataset, confirming substantial headroom for future research.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Margin sampling [27] is the strongest overall method (avg rank 4.8) but is severely outperformed on images: LeastConfident sampling [27] achieves rank 2.6 on CIFAR-10 and 4.4 on FashionMNIST, while Margin drops to 7.2 and 4.9 respectively. Galaxy [33] dominates the text domain (rank 2.6 on TopV2, 2.0 on News) and is competitive on tabular data (2.4 on USPS), but underperforms on images (2.7 on CIFAR-10). BADGE [1] is the most consistent mid-tier method (avg 5.1) with no catastrophic failures in any domain. BALD [15] shows extreme variance: it ranks 4.1 on Splice (second-best) but collapses to 13.0 (worst) on CIFAR-10. Clustering-based methods Coreset [26] and TypiClust [10] are consistently among the worst (ranks 9.3 and 11.4), confirming their vulnerability to the Honeypot and Diverging Sine synthetic tasks. The paper's key finding is that the Top-3 set of methods differs for every domain, making it impossible to identify a universal SOTA and highlighting the inadequacy of image-only benchmarks for assessing AL methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

CDALBench extends the line of AL benchmarking work by Ji et al. (2023) and Lüth et al. (2024), which identified evaluation pitfalls and recommended more repetitions, but were limited to the image domain. It also builds on single-domain benchmarks (Beck et al. for images, Rauch et al. for text, Bahri et al. for tabular) and the oracle concept from Zhou et al. (2021), replacing their computationally expensive simulated-annealing oracle with a greedy sequential construction. The key departure is unifying all five data domains (image, text, tabular, synthetic, semi-supervised) under one protocol with 50 repetitions per experiment, whereas prior benchmarks covered at most two domains with 3–5 runs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that no single AL method dominates across domains—and that the image domain, which has received the most research attention, is actually an outlier where least-confident sampling (a method that performs poorly everywhere else) is the top performer—challenges the field's implicit assumption that image-domain results generalize. The demonstration that 3 repetitions can make a well-established method appear significantly better or worse than random is a practical warning for the entire AL literature. Open questions include robust AL methods resistant to adversarial/noisy samples (exposed by Honeypot), the interplay between semi-supervised learning and AL at scale, and learned (RL-based) AL methods, which the authors defer to future work.
