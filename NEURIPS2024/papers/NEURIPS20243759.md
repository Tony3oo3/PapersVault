## Problem

Despite decoder-only LLMs like LLaMA3-8B demonstrating superior text understanding over CLIP and T5 encoders, directly using them as prompt encoders in diffusion models paradoxically degrades prompt-following performance. The paper identifies two root causes: a misalignment between next-token-prediction pre-training and the need for discriminative, summarizing text features, and an inherent positional bias from causal attention that causes the model to underweight objects and attributes appearing later in the prompt.

## Input / Output

The system takes a natural-language text prompt (English or Chinese) as input, prepends a fixed instruction directing the LLM to focus on image-relevant attributes (color, shape, spatial relations, etc.), and encodes it through two frozen multi-modal LLMs (LLaMA3-8B and Qwen1.5-7B). The output is a high-resolution RGB image (up to 1024×1024), with the diffusion transformer supporting arbitrary resolutions via a convolution-based positional embedding rather than fixed 2D position encodings.

## Method

The LLM-infused Diffuser framework addresses both identified issues with three components: (1) an instruction prefix that leverages the LLM's instruction-following ability to shift its representations toward image-relevant semantics; (2) a Linguistic Token Refiner — a stack of transformer blocks with full (non-causal) self-attention and timestep-conditioned adaptive gating — that eliminates the positional bias by allowing all tokens to attend bidirectionally; and (3) a Collaborative Refiner that fuses representations from multiple LLMs via parallel cross-attention branches. The resulting text embeddings are injected into LI-DiT, a diffusion transformer following the DiT paradigm with a VAE latent space, cross-attention layers for text conditioning, and QK-norm with bfloat16 mixed-precision training for numerical stability at scale.

## Results

LI-DiT-1B achieves a DPG-Bench score of 81.65 and GenEval overall of 0.60, outperforming all open-source baselines including SDXL (DPG 74.65, GenEval 0.55) and PixArt-α (DPG 71.11, GenEval 0.48). The larger LI-DiT-10B reaches DPG-Bench 84.60, GenEval overall 0.76, and T2I-CompBench spatial 39.69, surpassing DALL-E 3 (DPG 83.50, GenEval 0.67) and SD3-8B (GenEval 0.74). On the T2I-CompBench spatial relation metric, LI-DiT-10B (39.69) more than doubles the score of SDXL (20.32), highlighting the benefit of LLM-based prompt encoding for complex compositional prompts.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against open-source models, LI-DiT-1B leads on every reported metric: it beats SDXL by 7.0 points on DPG-Bench (81.65 vs. 74.65) and by 0.05 on GenEval overall (0.60 vs. 0.55), and outperforms PixArt-α by 10.5 points on DPG-Bench (81.65 vs. 71.11) and 0.12 on GenEval (0.60 vs. 0.48). The T2I-CompBench spatial relation score improves from 20.32 (SDXL) to 27.57 (LI-DiT-1B), a 36% relative gain. At the 10B scale, LI-DiT-10B surpasses DALL-E 3 on DPG-Bench (84.60 vs. 83.50) and GenEval overall (0.76 vs. 0.67), and edges out SD3-8B on GenEval (0.76 vs. 0.74) while reporting a T2I-CompBench spatial score of 39.69 where SD3-8B provides no number. In human evaluation, LI-DiT-10B is preferred over both DALL-E 3 and Stable Diffusion 3 on image-text alignment and image quality, and matches Midjourney V6 on quality while leading on alignment.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the DiT/PixArt-α lineage of transformer-based diffusion models and the established use of T5/CLIP as prompt encoders, but departs sharply by diagnosing why decoder-only LLMs (LLaMA3, Qwen) actually underperform smaller T5 encoders in this role. Unlike prior LLM-in-diffusion work that either generates layouts (LayoutGPT, LLM-Grounded Diffusion) or trains adapters to bridge LLMs to frozen diffusion models (SUR-Adapter, LLM4GEN, ELLA), this paper identifies two root causes—next-token-prediction misalignment and causal-attention positional bias—and addresses them with a lightweight instruction prompt plus a bidirectional linguistic token refiner, rather than retraining the LLM or the diffusion backbone. The collaborative refiner for fusing multiple LLMs extends the single-encoder paradigm used in SD3 and PixArt-α.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first systematic diagnosis of why powerful LLMs fail as diffusion prompt encoders, which is a conceptual finding that reframes how the community should think about integrating language models into generative pipelines. The LLM-infused Diffuser framework is modular and can flexibly swap in different LLMs, making it a practical recipe for future diffusion model developers. Key open questions include whether the approach scales to 13B/70B LLMs (the authors only test 7B), whether the instruction prompt generalizes across languages and domains without re-tuning, and whether the positional-bias diagnosis holds for non-causal LLM variants. The framework already powers SenseMirage, suggesting near-term practical impact.
