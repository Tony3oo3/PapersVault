## Problem

Existing LLM quantization methods (both PTQ and QAT) suffer severe performance degradation when bit-width drops below 2 bits, with no prior work successfully quantizing pre-trained LLMs to 1-bit while preserving useful capabilities. The core difficulty is that 1-bit representation leaves only two values (±1) per weight, causing drastic precision loss in the linear projection W·X that underpins every transformer layer.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242121/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes pre-trained LLM weight matrices in FP16/FP32 format along with a synthesized training corpus (132k sequences of up to 2048 tokens generated by the teacher model via next-token sampling). The output is a 1-bit quantized model in W1A16 mode where each Linear layer's weight matrix is stored as a ±1 INT1 sign matrix plus two FP16 value vectors (g and h), yielding an average bit-width of approximately 1.0073 bits per parameter for a 4096×4096 matrix. During inference, the sign matrix is packed in INT1 and the Sign(·) operation is eliminated, while activations remain in FP16.

## Method

OneBit introduces a novel 1-bit Linear layer architecture in which the original weight matrix W is decomposed via Sign-Value-Independent Decomposition (SVID) into a ±1 sign matrix W_{±1} (INT1) and two FP16 value vectors g and h, with the forward pass computed as Y = [(X ⊙ g) W_{±1}^T] ⊙ h followed by Post-LayerNorm. The sign matrix preserves the high-rank structure of W at minimal storage cost, while the value vectors supply the floating-point precision that pure 1-bit quantization loses, at a negligible overhead. For initialization, SVID approximates the value component |W| as a rank-1 outer product ab^T (via NMF or SVD), so that W ≈ W_sign ⊙ (ab^T), providing a principled starting point that is provably closer to W in Frobenius norm than a direct rank-1 approximation of W alone. Knowledge transfer from the pre-trained teacher to the 1-bit student is performed via quantization-aware distillation combining cross-entropy on output logits with MSE on L2-normalized hidden states across all layers, without using a language modeling loss. Post-LayerNorm (rather than Pre-LayerNorm) is used to prevent floating-point overflow during training, and the value vectors additionally stabilize both forward and backward passes by bounding the magnitude of quantized matrix products.

## Results

On LLaMA-13B, OneBit (W1A16) achieves a WikiText2 perplexity of 9.18 and C4 perplexity of 10.25, retaining approximately 81% of the FP16 performance (5.09 / 6.61), while all W2A16 baselines degrade far more severely. On LLaMA-7B, OneBit reaches 10.19 Wiki2 PPL and 51.33% average zero-shot accuracy versus FP16's 5.68 PPL and 64.06%. The compression ratio reaches 90.4% for LLaMA-7B (13.5 GB → 1.3 GB) and 93.4% for LLaMA-65B (130.6 GB → 9.2 GB). On LLaMA2-13B, OneBit achieves 8.76 Wiki2 PPL and 55.03% average zero-shot accuracy, again outperforming all 2-bit baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OneBit (W1A16) outperforms all three W2A16 quantization baselines across every model size tested. Against OmniQuant (the strongest 2-bit baseline), OneBit achieves substantially lower perplexity — e.g., 9.18 vs. 13.43 Wiki2 PPL on LLaMA-13B and 10.19 vs. 15.34 on LLaMA-7B — while also using half the weight bit-width. GPTQ and LLM-QAT catastrophically fail at 2-bit, producing perplexities in the hundreds to thousands (e.g., GPTQ: 3.2e3 Wiki2 PPL on LLaMA-13B), rendering them unusable. In the problem-solving comparison (Figure 3), OneBit-7B outperforms Pythia-1.0B (1.54× larger memory footprint) on MMLU world knowledge in most domains and matches or exceeds TinyLLaMA-1.1B (2× larger) on commonsense reasoning. BitNet's 1-bit architecture, when applied to knowledge distillation from LLaMA-7B, exhibits severe training instability across all tested learning rates (Figure 6), whereas OneBit converges stably due to its value vectors and Post-LayerNorm design.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends BitNet's 1-bit transformer architecture (which was designed for training from scratch) into a framework for quantizing existing pre-trained LLMs via knowledge distillation. The key departure is the introduction of two FP16 value vectors (g, h) alongside the ±1 sign matrix, which compensates for the catastrophic precision loss that makes pure 1-bit RTN quantization infeasible. The SVID initialization method, grounded in a Frobenius-norm proof showing sign-aware decomposition outperforms direct rank-1 approximation, bridges the gap between the original FP16 weights and the 1-bit architecture. Unlike prior QAT methods (LLM-QAT, OmniQuant) that target 2-bit, OneBit pushes to the theoretical minimum of 1-bit weight representation while maintaining a W1A16 inference setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

OneBit demonstrates that 1-bit quantization of pre-trained LLMs is practically viable for the first time, achieving ~90% memory compression (e.g., 13.5 GB → 1.3 GB for LLaMA-7B) while retaining at least 81% of original performance. This opens a path toward deploying large language models on consumer hardware (phones, laptops) where even 4-bit models are too large. Key limitations remain: the method cannot be naturally extended to higher bit-widths, activation quantization is unaddressed, and the KD training cost (~7 days on 8 A100s for 7B) is non-trivial though one-time. Open questions include whether the value-vector mechanism generalizes to W1A1 (full 1-bit inference) and whether the mathematical structure of optimal 1-bit parameters can be characterized analytically to eliminate the need for KD entirely.
