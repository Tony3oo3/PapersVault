## Problem

Transformer encoder-based language models struggle to explicitly encode and interpret hierarchical structures latent in language, as revealed by probing studies showing limited transitive reasoning and subsumption knowledge. Prior work has used hierarchical information only as implicit training signals (classification fine-tuning, contrastive objectives), but no existing approach explicitly encodes hierarchies into the embedding geometry of LMs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240469/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes entity names as variable-length text, processed through a sentence-transformer-style architecture with mean pooling over token embeddings (deliberately omitting the normalisation layer to preserve variable Euclidean norms). It produces entity embeddings confined within a Poincaré ball of radius √d that circumscribes the LM's tanh-bounded output hypercube, requiring no projection layer. For subsumption prediction, a probing function scores entity pairs by combining their hyperbolic distance and the difference in their hyperbolic norms (distance to origin).

## Method

The core contribution is re-training pre-trained transformer encoder LMs as Hierarchy Transformer encoders (H1Ts) by exploiting the fact that tanh activation confines LM outputs to a d-dimensional hypercube, which is naturally circumscribed by a Poincaré ball of radius √d (curvature c=1/d). Two hyperbolic losses are jointly optimised: a clustering loss in triplet form that pulls parent–child embeddings closer than parent–negative embeddings in the Poincaré metric, and a centripetal loss that enforces parents to sit closer to the manifold origin than their children, mirroring the natural expansion of hierarchies from root to leaves. No additional learnable parameters are introduced beyond the LM itself, and the curvature is fixed by the embedding dimension rather than learned. A lightweight probing function then converts the geometric structure into subsumption scores by penalising both large hyperbolic distances and violations of the expected norm ordering (child farther from origin than parent).

## Results

On WordNet Multi-hop Inference, H1T models achieve F-scores of 0.871–0.916 (random negatives) and 0.899–0.908 (hard negatives), substantially outperforming pre-trained LMs (0.188–0.339) and standard fine-tuned LMs (0.615–0.687). On the more challenging Mixed-hop Prediction task, H1Ts reach F-scores of 0.856–0.900 (random) and 0.862–0.871 (hard), again far exceeding pre-trained (0.188–0.347) and fine-tuned (0.627–0.669) baselines. In cross-domain transfer (WordNet→DOID), all-MiniLM-L12-v2+H1T attains an F-score of 0.704 (random) and 0.566 (hard), surpassing PoincaréGloVe (0.287/0.299) and fine-tuned LMs (0.603/0.297).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against PoincaréEmbed [12], H1T (all-MiniLM-L12-v2) achieves a higher F-score on hard negatives (0.908 vs. 0.830) and a comparable one on random negatives (0.903 vs. 0.864) in Multi-hop Inference; PoincaréEmbed cannot be evaluated on Mixed-hop Prediction due to its static, non-inductive nature. Against HyperbolicCone [13], H1T matches performance on random negatives (0.903 vs. 0.898) but dramatically outperforms it on hard negatives (0.908 vs. 0.383), where HyperbolicCone's low precision (0.243) reveals difficulty distinguishing sibling entities. Against PoincaréGloVe [32] in transfer evaluation, H1T wins on all three target ontologies: Schema.org (0.553 vs. 0.441 random F-score), FoodOn (0.554 vs. 0.207), and DOID (0.704 vs. 0.287), with the gap widening on harder domains due to PoincaréGloVe's limited vocabulary. Against the BERTSubs fine-tuning approach [8], H1T consistently outperforms by 0.194–0.301 F-score points on WordNet tasks and by at least 0.101 on transfer tasks, demonstrating that standard classification-based fine-tuning captures single-hop subsumptions well but fails to generalise to transitive and unseen relationships.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the sentence-transformers architecture (Reimers & Gurevych, 2019) by removing the post-pooling normalisation layer and re-training the encoder in a curvature-adapted Poincaré ball whose radius is determined by the tanh-bounded output space of the LM. It departs from prior work that incorporated hierarchical information into LMs only as implicit signals (contrastive learning, classification fine-tuning, masked LM on hierarchical text) by introducing the first explicit hyperbolic encoding of hierarchies via two purpose-designed losses (clustering and centripetal). The hyperbolic geometry toolkit (Möbius addition, Poincaré distance) is inherited from Nickel & Kiela (2017) and Ganea et al. (2018), but those static embedding methods lack inductive prediction and textual input, which this work addresses by leveraging pre-trained transformer encoders.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The demonstrated cross-domain transfer (e.g., WordNet-trained H1T achieving F-score 0.704 on DOID despite minimal entity overlap) suggests a practical path toward general-purpose hierarchy encoders for ontology reasoning and hierarchy-based semantic search. Key open questions include whether re-training causes catastrophic forgetting of general language understanding, how to scale to multi-hierarchy or multi-relation settings, and whether the approach extends to decoder-based LLMs. The parameter-free design (no new learnable layers) makes the method lightweight and easy to adopt, but the reliance on a fixed Poincaré ball geometry tied to the tanh activation may limit applicability to architectures with different output normalisations.
