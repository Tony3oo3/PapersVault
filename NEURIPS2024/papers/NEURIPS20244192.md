## Problem

Methods that regularize second-order loss landscape information (e.g., SAM, gradient penalties, weight noise) show inconsistent generalization benefits, and prior analyses have largely ignored the indefinite Nonlinear Modeling Error (NME) component of the Hessian. This paper identifies the NME as the missing piece that explains why gradient penalties are sensitive to activation function choice and why weight noise underperforms compared to Gauss-Newton-only penalties.

## Input / Output

The experiments use standard RGB image classification benchmarks (CIFAR-10 at 32×32, Fashion MNIST at 28×28, ImageNet at 224×224) with WideResNet-28-10 or ResNet-50 architectures. The key analytical input is the Hessian decomposition into the PSD Gauss-Newton matrix and the indefinite NME matrix; the output is test accuracy as a function of regularization strength (ρ for gradient penalties, σ² for Hessian/weight noise penalties), with ablations that selectively remove or replace NME contributions in the update rule.

## Method

The paper decomposes the loss Hessian into a PSD Gauss-Newton (GN) term and an indefinite Nonlinear Modeling Error (NME) term, showing the NME is the only component encoding second-order information about model features (∇²θ z). For gradient penalties (PSAM), the update rule involves a Hessian-vector product whose NME component depends on the activation function's second derivative; ReLU's ill-defined second derivative (a Dirac delta) causes PSAM to fail while GELU succeeds, confirmed by ablating GELU's second derivative to zero. For Hessian trace penalties (weight noise), the NME is implicitly minimized, which is shown to be detrimental because the indefinite NME has no lower bound and drives the trace negative during training. The GN trace penalty, which excludes the NME from the objective but retains it in the gradient (via an NME-vector product), consistently outperforms both weight noise and full Hessian trace penalty by up to 1.6% on ImageNet. A synthetic Gaussian second derivative for ReLU partially restores NME information and recovers PSAM's performance.

## Results

The paper's central quantitative finding is that the Gauss-Newton Trace Penalty (which excludes the NME) outperforms both Weight Noise and the full Hessian Trace Penalty by a significant 1.6% top-1 accuracy on ImageNet (ResNet-50), while the Hessian Trace Penalty degrades as early as σ²=10⁻⁶, at least two orders of magnitude below the optimal GN regularizer values (≥10⁻⁴). For gradient penalties, PSAM closely tracks SAM performance with GELU activations on ImageNet, but with ReLU activations PSAM performs significantly worse than SAM at larger ρ values, even falling below the unregularized baseline. Ablating the activation second derivative (NME) from GELU networks reproduces the ReLU failure mode, confirming the NME as the causal mechanism.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Compared to SAM [Foret et al., 2020], PSAM (the gradient penalty approximation) achieves similar test accuracy on ImageNet when the network uses GELU activations, but significantly underperforms SAM with ReLU activations at larger ρ values — the gap widens with ρ and PSAM can fall below the unregularized baseline. This discrepancy is attributed to ReLU's ill-defined second derivative, which zeros out the NME in the Hessian-vector product used by PSAM's update rule. Compared to Weight Noise [An, 1996], the GN Trace Penalty (which deliberately excludes the NME) achieves a 1.6% top-1 accuracy improvement on ImageNet and consistently outperforms Weight Noise across all three datasets (ImageNet, CIFAR-10, Fashion MNIST). The full Hessian Trace Penalty, which includes the NME, performs comparably to or worse than Weight Noise and exhibits severe instability at small σ² values, confirming that minimizing the NME component is counter-productive for generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the SAM algorithm (Foret et al., 2020) by deriving Penalty SAM (PSAM) as its first-order Taylor approximation, and on Bishop's (1995) analysis of weight noise as implicit Tikhonov regularization. It departs from the standard practice in second-order optimization of approximating the Hessian with only the Gauss-Newton (PSD) component, arguing instead that the indefinite Nonlinear Modeling Error (NME) term—previously called the functional Hessian—cannot be neglected when analyzing curvature-based regularizers. The paper also connects to the broader literature on Hessian structure in overparameterized networks (Sagun et al., 2017; Singh et al., 2021) but provides the first systematic empirical and theoretical evidence that the NME governs the behavior of gradient penalties and weight noise in practice.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that the NME is critical to gradient penalty performance—and that minimizing it is actively detrimental to generalization—challenges a widely-used simplification in the analysis of second-order regularizers and has direct implications for designing future curvature-based methods. The activation-function sensitivity result (ReLU vs. GELU) opens a new axis for architecture design: activation functions may need to be chosen not just for forward/backward pass efficiency but for well-posed second derivatives compatible with second-order regularization. Open questions include whether the NME analysis extends to convolutional and transformer architectures, whether implicit second-order methods like SAM can be made more robust to activation choice, and whether the Gaussian-approximation intervention for ReLU generalizes to other poorly-conditioned second derivatives.
