## Problem

Concave Utility Reinforcement Learning (CURL) extends RL to convex performance criteria over state-action distributions, but all existing CURL algorithms assume stationary environments. No prior work addresses online CURL in non-stationary MDPs where both the objective function and probability transitions change over time, and none handle adversarial losses while achieving dynamic regret without prior knowledge of the environment's variation.

## Input / Output

At each episode, the learner observes the current state, the external noise trajectory (ε_n^t) from the known deterministic dynamics g_n, and the objective function F^t revealed at episode's end. The probability transition kernel p^t is unknown and non-stationary—only the deterministic structure g_n is known, with uncertainty arising solely from the noise distribution. The algorithm outputs a policy π^t and an aggregated state-action distribution μ^t (not a policy directly, since F is non-convex over policies but convex over distributions satisfying the Bellman flow).

## Method

MetaCURL is a parameter-free meta-algorithm that simultaneously runs |Λ|×t instances of a black-box CURL algorithm (Greedy MD-CURL) with different learning rates and different starting episodes, then aggregates their state-action distributions via a sleeping expert scheme (EWA) that upweights recently restarted instances to adapt to changes. The key technical hurdle is partial information: since p^t is unknown, the learner cannot directly compute losses of non-played expert policies. This is resolved with a second layer of sleeping experts that estimates p^t using empirical transition counts from different intervals, combined with a novel smoothed logarithmic loss and a Pinsker-based regret analysis. The learning rate grid is exponentially spaced (|Λ|=O(log T)), and the algorithm achieves near-optimal dynamic regret Õ(√(Δ^π*·T) + min{√(Δ_∞^p·T), T^(2/3)(Δ^p)^(1/3)}) without requiring prior knowledge of the non-stationarity degree.

## Results

MetaCURL achieves a dynamic regret bound of Õ(√(Δ^{π*} T) + min{√(T Δ_∞^p), T^{2/3}(Δ^p)^{1/3}}) for non-stationary CURL with adversarial losses, without requiring prior knowledge of the MDP's non-stationarity degree. The bound decomposes into a policy-variation term √(Δ^{π*} T) (improving on the (Δ^{π*})^{1/3} T^{2/3} rate in prior RL work) and a transition-kernel non-stationarity term that is near-optimal. The paper also establishes the first dynamic regret upper bound for Greedy MD-CURL in a near-stationary interval, enabling it to serve as a black-box subroutine. No empirical experiments are conducted; all results are theoretical regret guarantees.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is with Wei & Luo (COLT 2021) [54], the state-of-the-art in non-stationary RL without prior knowledge. MetaCURL strictly dominates this baseline on three axes: (1) it extends from linear RL to the general CURL setting with convex objective functions over state-action distributions; (2) it handles fully adversarial losses, whereas [54]'s regret bound depends on the magnitude of loss-function variation (Δ^l) and thus degrades under adversarial losses; and (3) it improves the dependency on policy variation from (Δ^{π*})^{1/3} T^{2/3} to √(Δ^{π*} T). The trade-off is that MetaCURL does not handle full exploration (it assumes known deterministic dynamics g_n with unknown external noise), while [54] supports exploration via UCRL-type methods. No other baselines are directly compared, as no prior work addresses non-stationary CURL.

## Relation to Prior Work

MetaCURL extends the CURL (Concave Utility Reinforcement Learning) framework—which generalizes standard RL to convex objectives over state-action distributions—from stationary to non-stationary MDPs, a setting previously unaddressed. The meta-aggregation mechanism is drawn from the sleeping experts literature in online learning [23, 3], adapted here to handle the partial-information challenge of MDPs where the learner cannot observe losses under unplayed policies. The black-box subroutine is Greedy MD-CURL [39], whose static regret analysis the authors extend to dynamic regret (the first such result for any CURL algorithm). Compared to the closest non-stationary RL work [54], MetaCURL departs by handling adversarial losses and bounding regret in terms of policy-sequence variation rather than loss-function variation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first algorithm and first dynamic regret guarantee for non-stationary CURL, opening a path for convex-objective RL in environments where both rewards and transition dynamics shift over time. The sleeping-experts meta-framework for MDPs is a reusable conceptual tool that the authors explicitly hope will interest the broader RL community. A key limitation is the dynamic assumption: uncertainty must arise solely from external noise independent of the agent's state-action pair, ruling out full exploration. The authors identify the open problem of combining non-stationarity, adversarial losses, and full exploration in a computationally efficient algorithm as the main direction for future work.
