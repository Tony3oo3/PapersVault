## Problem

Optimizing black-box, expensive-to-evaluate functions over k-node subsets in graphs is a combinatorial problem that existing methods handle poorly: greedy and proxy-based approaches are task-specific, ignore the underlying function's characteristics, and require full graph knowledge. Bayesian optimization, a sample-efficient black-box solver, has never been applied to this combinatorial setting on generic (potentially unknown) graphs, leaving a gap in function-agnostic, query-efficient optimization for network-based problems.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a graph whose structure may be only incrementally revealed through queries (not fully known a priori), a black-box function f(S) defined on k-node subsets, a subset size k, and a total query budget T. It outputs the best-queried k-node subset S*_T (or k-edge subset, handled by converting the original graph to its line graph). The function evaluation is the expensive bottleneck—e.g., 100 SIR simulations or 1000 independent cascading runs per query—so the framework is designed to minimize the number of such evaluations. A combo-subgraph size Q (fixed at 4000 in experiments) and maximum hop ℓ_max bound the local search window at each iteration.

## Method

GraphComBO constructs a combinatorial graph (combo-graph) where each node represents a k-node subset from the original graph, and two combo-nodes are adjacent iff they differ in exactly one element that are neighbors in the original graph. A recursive sampling algorithm (Algorithm 1) builds a local ego-subgraph of size Q centered at a focal combo-node by iteratively swapping one element with its graph neighbors, requiring no prior knowledge of the full graph. A graph Gaussian Process surrogate is fitted on this combo-subgraph using a diffusion kernel derived from the normalized graph Laplacian eigendecomposition, and Expected Improvement selects the next query. The framework operates in a trust-region style: if the best-queried value improves, the combo-subgraph is resampled at the new center; otherwise it remains fixed. A failtol counter and restart_method (random, best-visited, or initial location) provide additional exploration-exploitation control, and the combo-node degree's linear scaling with k (Lemma 3.3) ensures the local subgraph remains tractable.

## Results

GraphComBO is evaluated on four synthetic tasks (BA/eigenvector centrality, WS/degree centrality, SBM/PageRank, 2D-Grid/Ackley) and five real-world tasks (epidemic curve-flattening, patient-zero tracing, influence maximization, road resilience, GNN adversarial attack) with subset sizes k ∈ {4, 8, 16, 32} over 300 queries repeated 20 times. The method consistently achieves the lowest regret (synthetic) or highest cumulative optimum (real-world) across all tasks, with the performance gap over baselines diminishing as k increases. On the large OGB-arXiv network (|V| = 1.7×10⁵), GraphComBO maintains a clear advantage up to k = 128, while local-search baselines underperform even random search. In the least favorable cases (WS, SBM, molecule GNN attack), GraphComBO performs comparably to combo-graph local search, consistent with the low signal smoothness of those underlying functions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GraphComBO outperforms all six in-paper baselines (Random, k-Random Walk, k-Local Search, Local Search on combo-graph, BFS, and DFS) across all synthetic and real-world experiments, with the largest margins on BA/eigenvector centrality and the epidemic curve-flattening task. Against combo-graph Local Search (the strongest baseline, equivalent to BO with a random surrogate), the gap narrows substantially as k grows from 4 to 32, and in low-smoothness settings (WS, SBM, molecule GNN) the two methods perform nearly identically. BFS exhibits heavy exploitation (small travel distance) while DFS performs large exploration, and both are consistently outperformed by GraphComBO's adaptive trust-region strategy. In the dedicated comparison with COMBO (Appendix H, small BA and WS networks with |V|=500), GraphComBO shows a clear advantage because COMBO's Cartesian-product construction over k identical graph copies introduces a search space of N^k with many redundant and invalid configurations, whereas GraphComBO's combo-graph operates directly on the correct C(N,k) space.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the combinatorial Bayesian optimization line initiated by COMBO (Oh et al., 2019) from the Cartesian product of k distinct graphs to the combination space of k-node subsets within a single graph, introducing the 'combo-graph' as the search space. It generalizes BayesOptG (Wan et al., 2023), which handles only single-node (k=1) optimization on graphs, to the full combinatorial setting. The local modeling strategy is borrowed from trust-region BO in continuous spaces (Eriksson et al., 2019), and the graph GP surrogate relies on the spectral kernel formulation of Smola & Kondor (2003). A key departure from COMBO is that the underlying graph structure need not be fully known a priori, enabling incremental revelation through queries.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides the first general-purpose, function-agnostic BO tool for expensive black-box optimization over node subsets in potentially unknown graphs, with direct applications to epidemic control, influence maximization, infrastructure resilience, and adversarial GNN attacks. Key open questions include how to handle the combinatorial explosion as k grows (where performance gains diminish), whether injecting global structural information (e.g., via GNN embeddings) into the surrogate could improve over the purely local Laplacian-based approach, and how to adaptively tune the trust-region size Q and hop limit during search. The finding that BO degenerates to local-search-level performance on low-smoothness functions (WS, SBM, GNN outputs) highlights a fundamental tension between GP smoothness assumptions and real-world graph signals.
