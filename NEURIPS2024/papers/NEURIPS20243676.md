## Problem

BPTT for training deep SNNs requires storing and backpropagating through all temporal activations across both network depth and time, leading to prohibitive memory and compute costs that scale linearly with timesteps. While online training methods reduce memory by decoupling temporal dependencies, they still require iterative backward computations at every timestep. The paper identifies that rate-coding is the dominant information representation in BPTT-trained SNNs, motivating a training strategy that exploits this structure to eliminate temporal backpropagation entirely.

## Input / Output

RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR, 224×224 for ImageNet) are directly encoded as binary spike inputs at each timestep without ANN-to-SNN conversion, while DVS event streams (128×128, reduced to 48×48) are integrated into frames before spike encoding. The network produces class labels by averaging the linear classifier outputs across all T timesteps, with cross-entropy loss computed on these temporal-mean predictions. The method supports two training modes: multi-step (T loops embedded within layers, tensors shaped [T×B, S]) and single-step (T loops external to layers), each with corresponding batch normalization strategies (tdBN and spatial BN respectively).

## Method

Rate-based backpropagation replaces BPTT's full temporal-spatial gradient computation with a single spatial backpropagation by approximating all timestep inputs with their temporal mean under a rate-coding assumption. During the forward pass, two iterative eligibility traces are maintained per layer: ρ_t^l accumulates the product of temporal membrane-potential derivatives (capturing how past inputs influence future spikes), and g_t^l accumulates the surrogate gradient weighted by ρ_t^l. At the final timestep, only the firing rates e_T^l and accumulated gradients g_T^l are retained, collapsing the backward computation graph from O(L×T) to O(L) complexity. The backward pass then performs one spatial backpropagation through the network, computing weight gradients as (∂L/∂c^l)(e_T^{l-1})^⊤ without revisiting any temporal dimension. Theoretical analysis (Theorems 1–2) establishes that under mild independence assumptions between error signals and temporal derivatives, the rate-based gradient equals the BPTT gradient up to a 1/T scaling factor, with bounded approximation error O(k²ε) over k layers.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, rate_M (ResNet-18, T=4) achieves 95.61% top-1 accuracy, matching BPTT_M (95.64%) while using one-step backpropagation. On CIFAR-100, rate_M reaches 78.26%, slightly exceeding BPTT_M (77.93%). On ImageNet, rate_M with PreAct-ResNet-34 (T=4) achieves 70.01% top-1 accuracy, surpassing all online training baselines (OTTT 65.15%, SLTT 66.19%, OS 67.54%). On CIFAR10-DVS, rate_M (VGG-11, T=10) achieves 76.96%, comparable to BPTT_S (76.86%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed rate-based backpropagation matches or slightly exceeds BPTT accuracy across all four benchmarks (e.g., 95.61% vs 95.64% on CIFAR-10, 78.26% vs 77.93% on CIFAR-100) while reducing backward computation to a single spatial pass. Against online training methods, the gains are substantial: on CIFAR-100, rate_S (77.73%) outperforms OTTT (71.05%) by 6.7% and SLTT (74.38%) by 3.4%; on ImageNet, rate_M (70.01%) surpasses SLTT (66.19%) by 3.8% and OS (67.54%) by 2.5%. Among one-step methods, rate_M (95.61%, T=4) beats DSR (95.10%, T=20) and SSF (94.90%, T=20) on CIFAR-10, and on CIFAR-100 rate_M (78.26%) exceeds SSF (75.48%) by 2.8% while using only 4 timesteps versus 20. The method also outperforms the ANN-to-SNN conversion baseline QCFS (94.82%) on CIFAR-10. The only case where a baseline edges ahead is DSR on CIFAR-100 (78.50% vs 78.26%), but DSR requires 5× more timesteps (T=20 vs T=4).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the observation (Li et al., 2023) that BPTT-trained SNNs predominantly encode information via rate coding rather than precise spike timing, and on the Straight-Through Estimator framework for handling non-differentiable spike events. It departs from prior efficient-training methods (OTTT, SLTT, OS, DSR, SSF) that partially decouple temporal dependencies but still require per-timestep backward computations, by fully collapsing the temporal dimension into a single spatial backpropagation using eligibility traces and rate-based surrogate gradients. The theoretical grounding draws on independence assumptions between error signals and temporal dynamics, formalized through two theorems that bound the approximation error relative to exact BPTT.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for SNN deployment in resource-constrained neuromorphic settings: backward memory and time become independent of the number of timesteps, enabling training of deeper or longer-duration SNNs on modest hardware. A key open question is whether the rate-coding approximation remains valid for tasks where temporal precision is genuinely critical (e.g., audio, motor control), since the CIFAR10-DVS results, while encouraging, still show a small accuracy gap. The bounded-error analysis (Theorem 2) suggests the approximation degrades gracefully with depth, but the quadratic scaling in network depth (O(k²ε)) could become problematic for very deep architectures.
