## Problem

Supervised causal discovery methods typically compress observational data into fixed-size vectors and predict edges independently, losing the geometric structure that encodes conditional dependencies. Classical GLASSO methods optimize inverse covariance matrices in the SPD space where entries directly encode conditional independence, but require solving an optimization problem per dataset and assume Gaussianity. The paper addresses how to bring this SPD-space geometric insight into a neural network that learns shape-invariant transformations from data and performs inference in a single forward pass.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20244092/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts an observational data matrix X ∈ R^{M×d} of arbitrary shape (trained across M ∈ [50,1000] and d ∈ [10,100]) and outputs a three-class one-hot encoded adjacency tensor Ã ∈ {0,1}^{d×d×3} assigning each variable pair to no-edge, skeleton-edge, or moralized-edge. A second, smaller network then takes the identified immoralities plus local neighborhood data to produce a CPDAG refined by Meek rules. The architecture is permutation-invariant over variables and shape-agnostic, so no retraining is needed when M or d change at test time.

## Method

The core contribution is a Bilinear Attention Mechanism (BAM) that processes channelwise covariance matrices on the SPD manifold: keys and queries are formed as non-negative linear combinations of SPD matrices, combined via the bilinear product K⊗Q = KQK, and normalized by a custom SPD-preserving softmax that regularizes eigenvalues to have maximum 1. The full pipeline alternates axial attention layers (between attributes and between samples) in Euclidean space with BAM layers in SPD space, then maps back via a Log-Eig eigendecomposition layer before a dense+softmax head. Training is supervised on synthetic data generated from Erdős-Rényi graphs with Chebyshev-polynomial SEM coupling functions and Gaussian-mixture noise, using a combined categorical cross-entropy, binary edge-presence, and moral-edge-consistency penalty loss. A two-step procedure first estimates the undirected moral graph (skeleton + moralized edges) in one forward pass, then a second network tests only the identified immoralities to orient edges into a CPDAG, drastically reducing the number of independence tests compared to exhaustive PC-style approaches.

## Results

For undirected graph estimation, BAM consistently achieves the highest AUC across all tested dependency functions (Chebyshev, linear, sine, cosine, x², x³, multiplicative), dimensions (d=10 to d=100), and sample sizes (M=50 to M=1000), outperforming all 13 baselines. In the CPDAG estimation task, BAM's two-step approach remains competitive but with a less pronounced advantage; in the high-dimensional regime (d=100, M=50), no algorithm including BAM surpasses a zero-graph baseline in SHD. Table 2 shows BAM's selective immorality testing outperforms exhaustive testing (AM) by 0.03–0.16 AUC across dependency types and dimensions, e.g., 0.85±0.05 vs. 0.69±0.02 for Chebyshev at d=20. Inference requires less than a few seconds per data matrix, compared to iterative optimization for unsupervised baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BAM outperforms all 13 baselines (PC, PCHSIC, rcot, rcit, ccdt, GES, GIES, LiNGAM, MMPC, CAM, SAM, GLASSO, DAG-GNN) on undirected graph estimation AUC across all tested dependency functions, dimensions, and sample sizes, with the largest margins on non-monotonic dependencies (sine, cosine, x², x³). Against the closest supervised competitor AVICI [42], BAM achieves superior AUC on the training distribution (Chebyshev) and maintains competitive performance on held-out dependencies, while also being shape-invariant (no retraining needed for new d or M values, unlike AVICI which requires fixed M=150). For CPDAG estimation, BAM's advantage narrows: it remains competitive with PC and AVICI but does not dominate as clearly as in the undirected setting, and in the extreme high-dimensional regime (d=100, M=50) all methods including BAM fail to beat a zero-graph in SHD. GLASSO and MMPC are excluded from CPDAG comparisons as they only produce undirected graphs. The ablation (Table 1) confirms the bilinear attention layer contributes the largest single-component improvement (loss 0.173→0.202 when removed), while the Log-Eig layer is critical for SPD-to-Euclidean transition (loss 0.173→0.271 when both BAM and Log-Eig are removed).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the classical Graphical Lasso paradigm—where inverse covariance matrices in SPD space encode conditional independence—into the neural network era by introducing a bilinear attention mechanism (BAM) that operates directly on covariance matrices while respecting SPD manifold geometry. It builds on axial attention for observational data processing (Kossen et al., Ho et al.) and the Log-Eig layer from Riemannian SPD networks (Huang & Van Gool), but departs from prior supervised causal learning methods (AVICI, Ke et al.) that predict edges from compressed embedding vectors via dot products. The key architectural departure is that BAM is the first shape-agnostic SPD layer, using trainable C×C weights to generate non-trainable d×d attention matrices, enabling a single forward pass for inference across arbitrary input dimensions without retraining. The two-step CPDAG estimation follows the PC-algorithm's skeleton-then-orientation principle but replaces sequential conditional independence tests with a learned network that identifies immoralities jointly in SPD space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The single-forward-pass inference (vs. iterative optimization in GLASSO) and shape-invariance (handling any M×d input) offer practical advantages for real-time or high-throughput causal discovery applications. The finding that selective immorality testing (only those identified by the first network) outperforms exhaustive local Markov blanket testing suggests that global graph structure information improves local conditional dependency identification—a useful insight for future two-stage causal discovery pipelines. However, high-dimensional CPDAG estimation (d > M) remains unsolved for all methods tested, the ~80 GB GPU training requirement limits accessibility, and the identifiability guarantee relies on the universal approximation theorem in ways that may not directly apply to the attention-based architecture. Open questions include scaling via local attention, handling confounding, and whether end-to-end directional inference is possible without the information loss inherent in covariance computation.
