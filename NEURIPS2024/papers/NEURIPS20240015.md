## Problem

Existing time series forecasting methods either treat all variables equally (multivariate paradigms) or ignore external information entirely (univariate paradigms), yet real-world forecasting frequently depends on exogenous variables that provide causal context without needing to be predicted themselves. Prior deep models that do incorporate exogenous variables (e.g., TFT, NBEATSx, TiDE) typically concatenate features at each time point, requiring strict temporal alignment and failing to handle the irregularities common in practice—missing values, frequency mismatches, and differing look-back lengths.

## Input / Output

The model takes an endogenous series of look-back length T and C exogenous series of potentially different look-back length T_ex (explicitly relaxed to T_ex ≠ T to accommodate frequency mismatches and newly introduced sensors). Only the endogenous series is predicted over a future horizon S; exogenous variables serve purely as covariates. For multivariate forecasting, the same architecture is applied in parallel via channel independence, treating each variable as endogenous with all others as exogenous.

## Method

TimeXer repurposes the canonical Transformer encoder without architectural modifications by assigning different embedding granularities to endogenous and exogenous variables: the endogenous series is split into non-overlapping patches projected to temporal tokens, while each exogenous series is embedded as a single variate-level token via a linear projection. A learnable global token is introduced for the endogenous variable to bridge the granularity gap—it participates in self-attention with patch tokens (capturing intra-endogenous temporal dependencies) and in cross-attention as query against exogenous variate tokens (ingesting external causal information). This two-level attention design (patch-wise self-attention + variate-wise cross-attention) allows the model to simultaneously model fine-grained temporal patterns and coarse-grained inter-variable correlations, while the variate-level exogenous embedding naturally tolerates missing values, misaligned timestamps, and heterogeneous frequencies. Training uses standard L2 loss on the endogenous forecast only, with a linear projection head over the final [patch tokens, global token] representation.

## Results

On the primary short-term electricity price forecasting (EPF) benchmark with exogenous variables, TimeXer achieves an average MSE of 0.307 and MAE of 0.265 across five markets (NP, PJM, BE, FR, DE), outperforming all nine baselines. On the secondary multivariate long-term forecasting task (look-back 96, prediction lengths {96, 192, 336, 720}), TimeXer achieves the best average MSE on 6 of 7 datasets (e.g., ECL 0.171, ETTh2 0.367, ETTm2 0.274), with the sole exception being Traffic where iTransformer achieves a lower MSE (0.428 vs. 0.466). The model also demonstrates scalability on a large-scale weather dataset with 3,850 stations and 36 exogenous variables at mismatched frequencies (hourly vs. 3-hourly).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TimeXer achieves consistent state-of-the-art performance across all five EPF markets, with the largest margins over SCINet (MSE 0.533 vs. 0.307) and Autoformer (0.453 vs. 0.307). Among Transformer-based baselines, iTransformer (MSE 0.335) and PatchTST (0.330) are the closest competitors, but both lack explicit exogenous-variable modeling; Crossformer (0.354) treats all variables at patch granularity, introducing unnecessary noise. On the multivariate long-term task, TimeXer wins on 6 of 7 datasets (ECL, Weather, ETTh1, ETTh2, ETTm1, ETTm2) but loses to iTransformer on Traffic (MSE 0.466 vs. 0.428), which the authors attribute to patch-level representations struggling with spike prediction. Against GNN-based baselines (MTGNN, CrossGNN, MSGNet, FourierGNN), TimeXer achieves the best MSE on all available datasets in Table 10. TiDE, the only other baseline explicitly designed for exogenous variables, is outperformed by TimeXer on all EPF datasets (e.g., NP: 0.236 vs. 0.335) and most multivariate datasets. NBEATSx and TFT, classical exogenous-variable forecasters, are also consistently outperformed (Table 9: TimeXer AVG MSE 0.307 vs. NBEATSx 0.330, TFT 0.402).

## Relation to Prior Work

TimeXer synthesizes two complementary lines of Transformer-based time series modeling: the patch-wise temporal representation from PatchTST (for capturing intra-series dependencies) and the variate-level global representation from iTransformer (for capturing inter-series correlations). The key departure is the asymmetric treatment of endogenous and exogenous variables—endogenous series receive fine-grained patch tokens plus a learnable global token, while exogenous series are embedded only at the coarser variate level—unlike prior exogenous-variable methods (TFT, NBEATSx, TiDE) that concatenate features at each time step and require strict temporal alignment. The canonical Transformer encoder is used without any architectural modification; the novelty lies entirely in the embedding strategy and the dual self-attention/cross-attention information pathway.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically critical gap: real-world forecasting (electricity prices, weather, traffic) almost always involves exogenous covariates, yet Transformer-based forecasters have largely ignored this setting or handled it through brittle feature concatenation. TimeXer's variate-level exogenous embedding naturally accommodates irregularities—missing values, frequency mismatch, differing look-back lengths—that plague real sensor data, and the channel-independence mechanism unifies exogenous and multivariate forecasting under one paradigm. Open questions include the acknowledged weakness on spike-heavy series (Traffic dataset MSE gap), the quadratic self-attention complexity over patches, and whether the global-token bridge generalizes to settings with many endogenous variables simultaneously.
