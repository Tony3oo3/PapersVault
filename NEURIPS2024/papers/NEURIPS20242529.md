## Problem

Prior approximate machine unlearning methods require (strong) convexity of the objective function and rely on sensitivity analysis of the optimal parameter, which is not even unique in non-convex settings. It remains unclear whether fine-tuning a differentially private model on the updated dataset provides a certified unlearning guarantee, how the initial DP guarantee affects unlearning efficiency, and whether this approach offers computational savings over retraining from scratch.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242529/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a model trained to convergence via projected noisy gradient descent (PNGD) on dataset D, along with the updated dataset D' after data removal, and produces unlearned model parameters after K iterations of PNGD on D'. The model is constrained to a closed ball of radius R, and the privacy guarantee is expressed as a Rényi unlearning bound d_α(ρ, ν_{D'}) ≤ ε, convertible to the standard (ε, δ)-unlearning definition. Key hyperparameters include the step size η, noise variance σ², and the number of unlearning iterations K, with the initial RDP level ε₀ determined by the learning process.

## Method

Langevin unlearning uses the same projected noisy gradient descent (PNGD) update for both learning and unlearning, creating a unified geometric framework where the RDP guarantee of learning (ε₀) sets the initial Rényi distance between ν_D and ν_{D'}, and K unlearning iterations exponentially decay this to the target ε via a phenomenon termed 'privacy recuperation.' The key technical contribution is tracking the log-Sobolev inequality (LSI) constant along the unlearning process and proving it has an iteration-independent upper bound even for non-convex objectives, by leveraging the boundedness of the projected set C_R through results on mixture distributions. This yields the guarantee ε ≤ exp(−(1/α)Σ R_k) ε₀ where R_k is a strictly positive, iteration-independent privacy-improving rate. The framework naturally extends to sequential and batch unlearning via the weak triangle inequality of Rényi divergence, and provides a provable computational saving over retraining since ε₀ = O(1/n²) while the retraining initialization distance is Ω(1).

## Results

On MNIST (binary 3-vs-8 logistic regression) and CIFAR-10 (10-class with ResNet-18 features), Langevin unlearning achieves test accuracy of roughly 0.9 and 0.98 respectively after unlearning 100 points under a (1, 1/n)-unlearning guarantee, matching retraining from scratch. For unlearning a single point with only K=1 iteration, Langevin unlearning significantly outperforms D2D in accuracy across ε ∈ [0.1, 5] while requiring only 1 iteration versus T=10,000 for retraining. In the batch unlearning setting (S=20 points per request), Langevin unlearning saves approximately 40% of unlearning iterations compared to D2D. The privacy-utility-complexity trade-off shows that even with a very weak initial DP guarantee (ε₀≈100 at σ=0.05), unlearning iterations can efficiently recuperate privacy to ε=1 with K up to ~2500 iterations, still well below the 10,000 iterations needed for retraining.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is Delete-to-Descent (D2D) [Neel et al., ALT 2021], the state-of-the-art gradient-based certified approximate unlearning method. Langevin unlearning wins on utility: with K=1 unlearning iteration, it achieves higher test accuracy than D2D across the full ε range (0.1 to 5) on both MNIST and CIFAR-10, while D2D requires K=2 or K=5 to reach comparable accuracy. Langevin unlearning also wins on computational efficiency: for batch unlearning of 100 points with S=20, it saves ~40% unlearning iterations over D2D's sequential approach. However, Langevin unlearning loses on complexity for very small batch sizes (S=5), where the weak triangle inequality overhead makes it more expensive than D2D. A key structural advantage is that Langevin unlearning does not require a non-private internal state, whereas D2D's stronger privacy accounting (Theorem 9 in [8]) depends on maintaining such a state, which Langevin unlearning avoids by default.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate research threads: (1) Langevin dynamics analysis for DP guarantees of noisy gradient descent (Chourasia et al. 2021, Ye & Shokri 2022, Altschuler & Talwar 2022) and (2) certified approximate machine unlearning (Sekhari et al. 2021, Guo et al. 2020, Neel et al. 2021). The key conceptual departure is reframing unlearning as a convergence problem in distribution space: the learning process's RDP guarantee sets an initial Rényi 'distance' ε₀ between adjacent-dataset stationary distributions, and unlearning iterations perform 'privacy recuperation' by exponentially decaying this distance toward the target ε. This geometric view, combined with careful tracking of log-Sobolev inequality constants along the PNGD trajectory, extends the analysis from the convex/strongly-convex settings required by all prior unlearning work to general non-convex objectives.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first certified approximate unlearning framework that is theoretically valid for non-convex problems, a gap that has blocked prior sensitivity-based approaches. However, the non-convex privacy bounds are too loose (exponential dependence on hyperparameters) for practical deployment, so empirical validation is restricted to strongly convex logistic regression. Open questions include tightening LSI constant analysis under additional structural assumptions, extending to mini-batch SGD (where mixture-distribution LSI tracking is technically difficult), and establishing whether the empirical privacy-utility trade-off observed in the convex case transfers to deep networks. The computational saving over retraining—O(log(1/n²)) vs. O(log(1)) iterations—becomes significant for large datasets but has not been validated at scale.
