## Problem

Split federated learning (SFL) combines the parallel training of federated learning with the model splitting of split learning, yet no convergence analysis existed in the literature. The analysis is more challenging than standard FL due to dual-paced model aggregation (SFL-V1) and dual-paced model updates (SFL-V2) at the client-side and server-side, which prior FL and SL theories cannot directly handle. This paper provides the first comprehensive convergence guarantees for SFL under heterogeneous data, both with full and partial client participation.

## Input / Output

Each client n holds a private dataset D_n and trains only the client-side portion x_c of a ResNet-18 model (split at a configurable cut layer L_c), while a main server trains the server-side portion x_s. Clients send intermediate activations (not raw data) to the main server, which returns cut-layer gradients; the fed server aggregates client-side models via weighted averaging. The theoretical output is a convergence rate bound—O(1/T) for strongly convex objectives and O(1/T^{1/3}) for general convex objectives—while the empirical output is a trained classifier evaluated on CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100 (32×32 RGB images).

## Method

The key technical contribution is a convergence decomposition (Proposition 3.5) that bounds the global model's performance gap by separately bounding the server-side and client-side parameter gaps, thereby decoupling the dual-paced update dynamics that make SFL harder to analyze than FL or SL. Under standard assumptions (S-smoothness, bounded stochastic gradient variance, bounded gradient norms, and a heterogeneity bound ε²), the authors derive explicit convergence rates for both SFL-V1 (where the main server maintains per-client server-side models aggregated at frequency τ̃) and SFL-V2 (where the main server maintains a single server-side model updated sequentially across clients). For partial participation, a bias-corrected aggregation scheme (weighting by a_n/q_n) is introduced, and the convergence bound acquires an additional G²/q_n term reflecting the cost of client unavailability. Experiments on CIFAR-10/100 with Dirichlet-distributed non-IID data confirm the theory and reveal that SFL-V2 outperforms both FL and SL when data heterogeneity is high and the client cohort is large.

## Results

The paper establishes the first convergence guarantees for SFL: O(1/T) for strongly convex objectives and O(1/∛T) for general convex objectives (where T is the total number of rounds), matching the order of existing FL and SL bounds. Experiments on CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100 with ResNet-18 validate these rates and reveal two key empirical findings: (i) SFL performance improves as clients retain a larger portion of the global model (larger cut layer L_c), and (ii) SFL-V2 outperforms both FL (FedAvg) and SL under highly heterogeneous data (β=0.1) with large client cohorts (N=50, 100). Partial participation (q_n < 1) degrades convergence stability and final accuracy, consistent with the additional G²/q_n term in the theoretical bounds.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares SFL-V1 and SFL-V2 against FedAvg (FL) and Split Learning (SL) on CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100. Under mildly heterogeneous data (β=0.5), SFL and FL achieve similar convergence rates and final accuracy, while SL underperforms both due to catastrophic forgetting in its sequential training. Under highly heterogeneous data (β=0.1), SFL-V2 clearly outperforms FL and SL, with the advantage growing as the number of clients increases from N=10 to N=100. The paper attributes FL's bottleneck to client drift under non-IID data and SL's bottleneck to catastrophic forgetting, while SFL-V2's hybrid design (parallel client training + sequential server training) achieves a better tradeoff between these two failure modes. SFL-V1 and SFL-V2 achieve similar accuracy in most settings, with SFL-V2 pulling ahead specifically under high heterogeneity and large N.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper provides the first convergence analysis for Split Federated Learning (SFL), building directly on the SFL-V1 and SFL-V2 algorithms proposed by Thapa et al. (AAAI 2022). The analysis draws on convergence frameworks from federated learning (FedAvg, McMahan et al. 2017) and sequential split learning (Li & Lyu, NeurIPS 2023), but must handle the unique challenge of dual-paced model updates and aggregation that distinguishes SFL from both FL and SL. The key technical contribution is a decomposition result (Proposition 3.5) that separates the global performance gap into client-side and server-side components, enabling independent analysis of each side's convergence behavior.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work fills a critical gap in the SFL literature by providing theoretical guarantees under heterogeneous data and partial participation, giving practitioners confidence in deploying SFL for resource-constrained edge devices. A notable limitation is that the derived bounds achieve the same order in T as standard FL, yet experiments show SFL-V2 outperforming FL under high heterogeneity—suggesting tighter, SFL-specific bounds remain to be derived. Open questions include theoretically characterizing how the cut layer choice affects convergence, whether diminishing learning rates improve rates, and how to jointly optimize system parameters (accuracy, communication, computation) using the derived bounds.
