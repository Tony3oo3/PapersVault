## Problem

Underwater object tracking (UOT) lacks a large-scale, diverse benchmark: existing datasets (UOT32, UOT100, UTB180, VMAT, UVOT400) cover at most 400 videos and 50 categories, with limited scenarios and no training splits or language annotations. Moreover, state-of-the-art open-air trackers degrade severely underwater due to domain gaps (color deviation, low visibility, similar distractors, camouflage), and no prior work has effectively transferred open-air knowledge to UOT via knowledge distillation.

## Input / Output

The tracker consumes a pair of image patches cropped from underwater video frames — a template patch z ∈ R^{H_z×W_z×C} and a search region patch x ∈ R^{H_x×W_x×C} — along with an initial bounding box B_0 in [x, y, w, h] format, and outputs predicted bounding boxes for all subsequent frames. During training, the teacher additionally receives enhanced frames E produced by a semi-supervised restoration method, while the student operates on raw underwater frames only. The WebUOT-1M dataset further provides per-video language prompts (describing color, behavior, attributes, surroundings) and per-frame absent labels, enabling vision-language tracking and long-term tracking evaluation beyond standard OPE.

## Method

OKTrack employs a multi-view teacher (a modified All-in-One unified backbone) that jointly encodes tokens from both underwater and enhanced frames, and a unimodal student (plain ViT-B with 12 encoder layers) that processes only underwater frames. Knowledge is transferred through four complementary distillation losses: (1) Token-based Contrastive Distillation (CKD) aligns underwater and enhanced token representations via bidirectional InfoNCE losses; (2) Similarity-based Distillation (SKD) matches per-layer attention similarity matrices; (3) Feature-based Distillation (FKD) minimizes L2 distance between teacher and student feature embeddings; (4) Response-based Distillation (RKD) uses a Gaussian weighted focal loss to mimic the teacher's localization response map. At inference, a training-free Motion-aware Target Prediction (MATP) module based on Kalman filtering (prediction + correction stages) is applied to suppress model drift caused by dense similar distractors common in underwater scenes. The total training objective combines the omni-KD loss with standard GIoU, focal, and L1 tracking losses, and the student is trained for 300 epochs with AdamW on 8× A6000 GPUs.

## Results

On WebUOT-1M (Protocol II, retrained), OKTrack achieves 57.5 Pre / 60.0 AUC, outperforming the best retrained open-air tracker CiteTracker-256 (54.2/57.7) by 3.3 Pre and 2.3 AUC. On UTB180, OKTrack reaches 67.3/69.7, surpassing retrained OSTrack (60.8/63.2) and CiteTracker-256 (61.6/66.3). In the cross-domain evaluation (Protocol I) on WebUOT-1M, the top-5 trackers are all Transformer-based (OKTrack, UOSTrack, All-in-One, GRM, OSTrack), with UOT-specific methods beating SOTA open-air trackers due to the domain gap. The VL variant OKTrack++ further improves to 63.4 AUC / 64.4 mACC by incorporating language prompts.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OKTrack consistently outperforms all 28 compared trackers across both evaluation protocols. In the retraining protocol (Table 3), OKTrack leads by 5.7 Pre over CiteTracker-256 and 6.5 Pre over OSTrack on UTB180, and by 3.3 Pre over CiteTracker-256 on WebUOT-1M. In the cross-domain protocol (Figs. 6–7), UOT-specific trackers (OKTrack, UOSTrack) surpass all open-air SOTA methods (CiteTracker-256, SeqTrack-B256, MixFormerV2-B), confirming that domain-specific training is critical for underwater tracking. Among open-air trackers, the VL-based All-in-One [79] achieves the best cross-domain performance, demonstrating the value of language modality. In the VL tracking sub-task (Table 4), All-in-One leads with 58.0 mACC, while language-prompt-only methods (JointNLT, UVLTrack) score only ~31 mACC, indicating multi-modal underwater tracking remains underexplored. CNN-based trackers (SiamFC, ATOM, ECO) lag far behind Transformer-based methods, with gaps exceeding 30 AUC points on WebUOT-1M.

## Relation to Prior Work

OKTrack builds directly on OSTrack [70] as its student ViT backbone and on All-in-One [79] as its multi-view teacher Transformer, adapting the omni-knowledge distillation paradigm from RGB-event tracking [62] to the underwater domain. The method combines four distillation losses (contrastive token alignment, similarity matrix, feature embedding, and response map) with a training-free Kalman-filter-based motion-aware target prediction module to address the model drift problem identified in prior UOT work [41]. On the dataset side, WebUOT-1M extends the lineage of UOT benchmarks (UOT32, UOT100, UTB180, VMAT, UVOT400) by scaling from hundreds to 1,500 videos and 408 categories, and is the first to include language prompts and absent-frame labels for underwater sequences.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As the first million-scale, fully open-source UOT benchmark with train/test splits, WebUOT-1M removes the primary bottleneck (data scarcity) that has limited UOT research and enables systematic comparison of 30 trackers under both cross-domain and within-domain protocols. The finding that UOT-specific ViT trackers outperform SOTA open-air trackers (including VL methods) confirms a substantial domain gap that retraining alone only partially closes, suggesting that underwater-specific inductive biases or larger underwater pre-training remain necessary. Open questions include whether the modest gains from language prompts will grow with richer annotations, how long-term tracking (where performance drops notably on VMAT) can be improved, and whether the benchmark's long-tail category distribution adequately represents rare species in practice.
