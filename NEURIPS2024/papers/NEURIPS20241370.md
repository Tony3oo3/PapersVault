## Problem

Converting pre-trained dense Transformers into sparse Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) variants via MoEfication yields suboptimal cost-performance trade-offs because the base model's activation sparsity is neither enforced nor exploited, the router is trained as a classifier rather than predicting expert contributions, and static top-k gating wastes compute on easy tokens. The paper asks: what is the optimal way to convert a generic Transformer into an equivalent sparse variant that better leverages the inherent and induced activation sparsity?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241370/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on pre-trained Transformer checkpoints (ViT-B, BERT-base, GPT-2-base, Gemma-2B) and converts their FFN and MHA projection layers into dynamic-k MoE layers. At inference, the number of executed experts per token is determined by a threshold τ on the router's predicted expert output norms, so the same input image (224×224 RGB) or text sequence can trigger different amounts of computation depending on token difficulty. The output format is unchanged from the original dense model: class labels for classification or next-token logits for language modeling.

## Method

D2DMoE combines four key components: (1) a lightweight fine-tuning phase that enforces higher activation sparsity via square Hoyer regularization on intermediate MLP activations; (2) expert clustering using balanced k-means on the first-layer (or gating-layer, for GLU variants) weights to split each MLP into n experts; (3) a regression-based router that directly predicts the ℓ²-norm of each expert's output rather than using artificial classification labels; and (4) a dynamic-k gating rule that selects experts whose predicted norm exceeds a fraction τ of the maximum, adapting compute per token. The method further extends to MHA projection layers by replacing each dense projection with a small two-layer MLP trained via distillation, and to gated FFNs by clustering on the gate weights. An efficient Triton-based GPU implementation translates the FLOPs savings into up to 3× wall-clock speedup.

## Results

D2DMoE reduces inference cost by up to 60% without significantly impacting performance across NLP and vision tasks. On BoolQ (Gemma-2B), D2DMoE retains 90.89% relative accuracy at 10% compute budget, while MoEfication drops to 77.53%. On ImageNet-1k (ViT-B), D2DMoE outperforms MoEfication, ZTW, and A-ViT across all computational budgets in the FLOPs-accuracy tradeoff. The efficient Triton implementation achieves up to 3× wall-clock speedup over the equivalent dense MLP while preserving 99% of original accuracy, and up to 30% end-to-end processing time reduction for ViT-B on ImageNet-1k.

## Baselines & Comparisons

D2DMoE consistently outperforms MoEfication [52] across all evaluated benchmarks: on ImageNet-1k (ViT-B), CARER (BERT-base), OpenWebText (GPT-2-base), and C4 (Gemma-2B), D2DMoE achieves better accuracy at every computational budget, with the gap widening at lower budgets (e.g., on BoolQ, D2DMoE retains 90.89% relative accuracy at 10% compute vs. 77.53% for MoEfication). Against the early-exit baseline ZTW [49], D2DMoE is superior at medium compute budgets on both ImageNet-1k and CARER, while ZTW only wins at the very lowest budgets. Against the token-dropping method A-ViT [51], D2DMoE achieves a better FLOPs-accuracy tradeoff on ImageNet-1k. The paper also demonstrates that D2DMoE is complementary to structured pruning (CoFi) and post-training quantization (8-bit, 16-bit), maintaining its efficiency gains when stacked with these techniques.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on MoEfication (Zhang et al., 2022), which converts dense Transformer FFN layers into MoE layers via weight clustering and independently trained routers. It departs from MoEfication in three conceptual ways: (1) it introduces a sparsity-enforcement preprocessing phase using Hoyer regularization to make the base model more amenable to expert pruning, (2) it replaces the classification-based router training with a regression objective that directly predicts expert output norms, and (3) it substitutes fixed top-k gating with a dynamic-k rule that adapts the number of executed experts per token. Additionally, the method extends the conversion scope beyond FFN layers to MHA projection matrices and gated (GLU) MLP variants, which prior dense-to-MoE conversion work did not address.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in the combination of a 60% inference cost reduction with an efficient Triton GPU implementation that translates FLOPs savings into real wall-clock speedup, making the approach deployable rather than purely theoretical. The finding that MoEfication's classification-based routing collapses on larger models (Gemma-2B) due to massive activations provides a useful diagnostic for why prior conversion methods fail at scale. Key limitations include the additional training overhead for sparsity enforcement and router training, the sensitivity of MHA projection replacement for language modeling tasks, and the restriction of experiments to models up to ~2B parameters, leaving open whether the approach scales to 70B+ models. The compatibility demonstrated with pruning (CoFi) and quantization suggests the method can compose with existing compression pipelines, but the interaction at larger scale remains untested.
