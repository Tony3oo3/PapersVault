## Problem

Multi-output regression methods typically assume training and test data share the same distribution, but in practice the training distribution often fails to cover all combinations of input features, creating a combinatorial distribution shift (CDS) where test feature pairs have zero probability under the training distribution. This paper is the first to formally define this problem and shows that existing tensor completion tools (e.g., t-SVD) are inapplicable because they require discrete, finite feature domains, while real-world MOR problems involve infinite or continuous feature spaces.

## Input / Output

The method consumes pairs of input features (x, y) drawn from potentially infinite or continuous domains X ⊂ R^{D1} and Y ⊂ R^{D2}, along with n training samples {(x_i, y_i, z_i)} from D_train that cover only a subset of feature combinations (specifically D_{1⊗1}, D_{1⊗2}, D_{2⊗1}). It produces a K-dimensional output vector z = h*(x,y) ∈ R^K for any test pair (x, y), including unseen combinations from D_{2⊗2}. The outputs are naturally structured as tubes in a 3-way tensor Z(x,y,:) ∈ R^K, and the problem is cast as tensor completion under the Missing-Not-At-Random (MNAR) setting where entire tubes are unobserved.

## Method

The paper introduces the Functional t-Singular Value Decomposition (Ft-SVD) theorem, which extends classical t-SVD to infinite and continuous feature domains by representing vector-valued functions as infinite series of t-rank-one components with t-singular functions and t-singular values, with convergence in the L² sense. Under Sobolev smoothness assumptions, the t-singular values exhibit polynomial spectral decay, enabling controlled finite-rank approximation. The MOR-under-CDS problem is then formulated as low-rank tensor estimation in a Hilbert t-Module via t-bilinear embeddings f*: X→M and g*: Y→M, where the ground truth is the inner product ⟨f*(x), g*(y)⟩_M. To solve this, the paper proposes ERM-DS, a four-stage algorithm (overparameterized training, t-covariance estimation, dimension reduction via low-rank t-projections, and distillation) that uses separate hypothesis classes per frequency component to capture varying spectral decay rates across the K outputs, yielding excess risk bounds that scale with the per-component decay exponents γ_i.

## Results

The paper's experiments are purely synthetic, serving as conceptual validation of the Ft-SVD framework rather than benchmarking on real datasets. On a synthetic tensor of size 400×400×10 with DCT transformation, ERM-DS consistently achieves lower test risk than single-stage ERM across all covariate shift intensities (κ ∈ [0.1, 3]) and training data percentages (50%–90%). The performance gap between ERM-DS and single-stage ERM widens as κ increases, indicating that the double-stage approach is particularly beneficial under severe combinatorial distribution shift. No standard benchmark metrics (e.g., RMSE on real data) are reported, as the paper is primarily a theoretical contribution.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three algorithms on synthetic tensor data: (1) single-stage ERM (Eq. 5), (2) overparameterized training (Step 1 of ERM-DS only), and (3) the full ERM-DS algorithm. ERM-DS outperforms single-stage ERM across all experimental settings — both when varying the covariate shift intensity κ and when varying the percentage of observed training data. The advantage of ERM-DS is most pronounced at larger κ values (more severe distribution shift), where single-stage ERM's test risk rises sharply while ERM-DS maintains a significant margin. Overparameterized training alone (without the dimension reduction and distillation steps) also underperforms the full ERM-DS pipeline, confirming that the two-stage structure is essential. However, these comparisons are limited to synthetic data with known ground-truth tensor structure, and no comparison to existing multi-output regression methods (e.g., multi-task learning, kernel methods, or neural network approaches) is provided.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the ERM-DS framework of Simchowitz et al. [46] from single-output to multi-output regression under combinatorial distribution shift, introducing a Hilbert t-Module representation to capture interdependencies among K outputs. The core theoretical tool, Functional t-SVD (Ft-SVD), generalizes the discrete t-SVD framework of Kilmer et al. [26] and Kernfeld et al. [25] to infinite and continuous feature domains, enabling tensor completion-style reasoning for vector-valued functions. The formulation as low-rank tensor estimation under MNAR connects the distribution-shift literature with the tensor completion literature, but the specific combination of multi-output structure, continuous domains, and CDS is new.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first formal treatment of multi-output regression under CDS, providing a theoretical foundation (Ft-SVD theorem, spectral decay guarantees, excess risk bounds) that was previously absent. However, the practical impact is currently limited: experiments are purely synthetic, the framework handles only two input features, and the authors acknowledge that extending to more than two combinatorial features remains an open problem requiring new mathematical tools. The spectral decay connection between Sobolev smoothness and approximation error is a natural but useful bridge that could inform future work on function approximation under distribution shift.
