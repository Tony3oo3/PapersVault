## Problem

Transformer-based in-context learners for tabular data, exemplified by TabPFN, suffer from two scaling failures: memory grows quadratically with context length, limiting performance on large datasets, and the model underfits complex decision boundaries even when the full dataset fits in context. This contrasts with classical tree-based methods that improve with more data, leaving a gap where deep learning cannot match well-tuned gradient-boosted trees on medium-to-large or complex tabular benchmarks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243441/cropped_page1_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a tabular training set of feature-label pairs (x_i, y_i) with at most 100 features and 10 classes (TabPFN's architectural constraints), plus one or more query feature vectors. For each query, it retrieves k nearest neighbours from the training set as a local context and outputs a softmax posterior over class labels. During fine-tuning, the input is restructured into batches of shape (B, L_ctx + L_qy, d) where multiple queries share an approximate local context, enabling parallel gradient computation that would be infeasible with exact per-query neighbours.

## Method

LoCalPFN layers two modifications on top of the TabPFN transformer: (1) retrieval, which replaces the single global context with a per-query local context of k nearest neighbours, controlling the bias/variance trade-off and enabling the model to exploit larger training sets even when k << N; and (2) end-to-end fine-tuning with an approximate neighbour scheme where B anchor points are sampled, their kNNs are computed, and the resulting neighbourhoods are shuffled and split into shared context and query segments, allowing efficient backpropagation without the O(N_qy) separate forward passes that exact per-query contexts would require. The key insight is that joint retrieval and fine-tuning (rather than fine-tuning on random context followed by kNN at inference) is essential for maximum gains, and that simple standardized-feature embeddings with Euclidean distance work as well as learned embeddings for the retrieval step.

## Results

Across 95 datasets from TabZilla/OpenML, LoCalPFN achieves an IQM AUC of 0.958 and Mean AUC of 0.908, establishing a new state-of-the-art that surpasses both the base TabPFN (0.917 IQM AUC) and the strongest tree-based baselines (XGBoost 0.943, CatBoost 0.942). On the 48 medium/large datasets where TabPFN degrades most severely, LoCalPFN reaches 0.968 IQM AUC vs. 0.927 for TabPFN and 0.965 for XGBoost. The retrieval-only variant TabPFN-kNN (no fine-tuning) already achieves 0.943 IQM AUC, matching XGBoost, while adding fine-tuning yields a further +1.5 IQM AUC gain.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LoCalPFN outperforms all seven baselines on the primary IQM AUC metric across 95 datasets. Against the base TabPFN (0.917 IQM AUC), LoCalPFN gains +4.1 points, with the gap widening to +4.1 on medium/large datasets where TabPFN underfits most severely. Against the strongest tree-based methods—XGBoost (0.943) and CatBoost (0.942)—LoCalPFN leads by 1.5–1.6 IQM AUC points, and this advantage is consistent on both small (+3.0 over XGBoost) and medium/large (+0.3 over XGBoost) subsets. The retrieval-only TabPFN-kNN variant (0.943) already matches XGBoost on overall IQM AUC but trails LoCalPFN by 1.5 points, confirming that joint fine-tuning with retrieval provides additional gains beyond kNN context alone. Against deep learning baselines (VIME 0.771, TabNet 0.881 IQM AUC on 71 datasets), LoCalPFN leads by 8–19 points. The in-context distillation method (TabPFN-ICD, 0.946 IQM AUC) is the closest competitor among TabPFN variants but still trails LoCalPFN by 1.2 points, and requires task-specific context optimization that is computationally comparable to LoCalPFN's fine-tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on TabPFN (Hollmann et al., ICLR 2023), a transformer-based in-context learner trained via prior-fitting that classifies tabular data in a single forward pass. The key conceptual move is transplanting two techniques from the LLM literature—retrieval (as in RAG, Lewis et al. 2020) and task-specific fine-tuning—onto a tabular ICL model, replacing the global context with per-query kNN neighbours and then fine-tuning end-to-end with an approximate shared-context scheme. It extends den Breejen et al. (2023), who showed limited gains from fine-tuning TabPFN's retrieval, by tightly coupling retrieval and fine-tuning, and contrasts with concurrent work by Xu et al. (2024) that uses K-Means clustering for routing, and Ma et al. (2024) that optimizes a single global context via in-context distillation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LoCalPFN is the first neural (ICL-based) method to consistently outperform well-tuned tree-based ensembles (XGBoost, CatBoost, LightGBM) across a 95-dataset benchmark, a milestone for deep learning on tabular data. The approach is simple and transferable—kNN retrieval plus fine-tuning—suggesting it could be applied to future tabular foundation models as they emerge. Key limitations include dependence on TabPFN's architectural constraints (≤100 features, ≤10 classes, classification only), slower inference than tree methods, and the fact that the base model is trained on synthetic data, raising questions about generalization to highly structured or high-dimensional real-world tables. Open questions include whether the retrieval-fine-tuning recipe transfers to regression, to models with larger context windows, and to settings where the feature space is too large for kNN to be informative.
