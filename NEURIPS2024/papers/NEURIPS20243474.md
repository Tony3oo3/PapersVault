## Problem

Existing amortized Bayesian experimental design methods optimize for information gain about latent parameters, but in many practical settings (e.g., personalized treatment selection, top-k hyperparameter identification) the true objective is a downstream decision. Because the designs are not shaped by the decision utility, the gathered information is suboptimal for the final decision-making step, and the predictive distribution needed to evaluate utility must be computed separately at deployment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243474/cropped_page1_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes an unordered set of past design-outcome pairs (context), a set of candidate designs for the next step (query), a set of designs for which the predictive distribution is needed (prediction), and a global information vector (e.g., the test point or patient covariates). The query head outputs a softmax policy over the candidate designs, while the prediction head outputs per-design Gaussian mean and variance parameters that jointly approximate p(y_Ξ | h_{1:t}). At deployment, a single forward pass yields both the next design and the predictive distribution needed to compute the optimal decision, with no iterative optimization required.

## Method

TNDP is a Transformer-based neural process with a shared self-attention backbone and two output heads: a query head that maps candidate-design embeddings to a policy distribution, and a prediction head that maps prediction-set embeddings to Gaussian parameters for the joint predictive distribution. An attention mask restricts prediction and query tokens to attend only to context and global information (not to each other), preserving independence across candidate designs. The key training innovation is the Decision Utility Gain (DUG) reward, which measures the improvement in maximum expected utility after observing a new outcome, replacing the standard EIG objective. The query head is trained with REINFORCE using a non-myopic discounted cumulative reward (discount α=0.99) over the full T-step trajectory, while the prediction head is trained by negative log-likelihood; the two heads are coupled because the DUG reward is computed using the prediction head's approximate predictive distribution.

## Results

On the decision-aware active learning task, TNDP achieves significantly higher proportion of correct decisions than all non-amortized baselines (GP-RS, GP-US, GP-DUS, GP-TEIG, GP-DEIG) across 100 test points over 10 acquisition steps. On the top-k (k=3) hyperparameter optimization task over HPO-B, TNDP consistently achieves the best average utility across all four meta-datasets (ranger, svm, rpart, xgboost), with particularly strong gains in the first 10 queries. In terms of deployment speed, TNDP requires only 0.015 s per design (0.31 s total for 10 rounds), compared to 572 s per design for GP-DEIG and 1558 s for GP-TEIG, demonstrating the key advantage of amortization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the decision-aware active learning task, TNDP outperforms both GP-TEIG (Sundin et al., 2018) and GP-DEIG (Filstroff et al., 2024) in proportion of correct decisions across all acquisition steps, with the gap widening as more queries are made. GP-DEIG, which directly targets reducing uncertainty on the posterior of the optimal decision, is the strongest non-amortized baseline but still lags behind TNDP, confirming that the DUG-guided policy and amortized predictive head together yield better decision accuracy. On the top-k HPO task, TNDP surpasses PFNs4BO (Müller et al., 2023), a transformer-based amortized BO method that does not account for the downstream top-k decision, achieving higher average utility on all four HPO-B meta-datasets, especially in early queries. TNDP also outperforms standard GP-based acquisition functions (UCB, EI, PI) and random sampling on the same HPO-B benchmark. The computational cost comparison further highlights TNDP's advantage: its 0.015 s per-design inference time is roughly 4 orders of magnitude faster than GP-TEIG (1558 s) and 4 orders faster than GP-DEIG (572 s), making it suitable for real-time decision-making settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of amortized Bayesian experimental design (Foster et al. 2021; Blau et al. 2022) and loss-calibrated inference (Lacoste-Julien et al. 2011), repurposing the latter's principle of aligning inference with downstream utility to the experimental design phase. Prior amortized BED methods optimize for parameter information gain via sPCE or RL objectives, while decision-aware BED (Lindley 1972; Filstroff et al. 2024; Neiswanger et al. 2021) remains non-amortized and computationally expensive. The TNDP architecture extends Transformer Neural Processes (Nguyen & Grover 2022; Müller et al. 2023) by coupling a policy head (for design) with a predictive head (for decision-making) under a shared Transformer backbone, and introduces Decision Utility Gain as a novel training signal that replaces EIG.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework addresses a practical gap: in applications like personalized medicine or hyperparameter optimization, the goal is not to characterize parameters but to make a good decision, and prior amortized methods are misaligned with this objective. The ~1000× speedup over non-amortized decision-aware methods (0.015 s vs. 572 s per acquisition) makes decision-aware design feasible in real-time settings. Key open questions include training stability under sparse rewards (basic REINFORCE is used), scalability to high-dimensional or variable-dimensionality design spaces, robustness to model misspecification, and extension to infinite-horizon settings. The quadratic Transformer complexity also limits the size of candidate design sets that can be evaluated simultaneously.
