## Problem

Diffusion models require many iterative denoising steps for sampling, making real-time generation impractical. Existing one-step distillation methods either rely on trajectory distillation that degrades sharply at one step, require access to training data, or minimize mode-seeking divergences (reverse KL) that cause mode collapse and fail to capture the full data distribution. This paper addresses the gap of a principled maximum-likelihood (mode-covering) distillation framework that produces high-quality one-step generators without training data.

## Input / Output

The distilled one-step generator takes a class label (ImageNet 64×64 or 128×128) or a text prompt (Stable Diffusion v1.5 latent space) and produces a single image in one forward pass through the network, with no iterative denoising. The student generator reuses the teacher diffusion denoiser architecture, conditioned on a fixed noise level t*, so the input is a standard Gaussian latent z ∈ R^d mapped directly to pixel or latent space. Training is data-free: only the pre-trained teacher score function and (for text-to-image) text prompts from LAION-Aesthetics-6.25+ are needed.

## Method

EM Distillation (EMD) frames one-step distillation as maximum likelihood estimation of the teacher's marginal distribution, using an EM algorithm where the E-step draws joint (x, z) samples from ρ_θ(x,z) = q(x)p_θ(z|x) via Langevin MCMC initialized from student samples, and the M-step updates the generator by gradient ascent on the log-likelihood. Two key technical innovations stabilize training: (1) a reparameterization into (ε, z) space that admits a single constant step size across all noise levels and accelerates MCMC mixing, and (2) a noise cancellation technique that analytically removes the accumulated Langevin noise from the MCMC-corrected x samples, reducing gradient variance without introducing bias. The paper further proves that the one-step, no-z-update special case of EMD recovers the exact gradient of VSD/Diff-Instruct, showing that the number of MCMC steps K continuously interpolates between mode-seeking (K=1) and mode-covering (K→∞) divergences. An auxiliary score network s_φ is trained alternately via denoising score matching to approximate the student's marginal score, which is needed for the MCMC drift term.

## Results

On ImageNet 64×64, EMD-16 achieves a FID of 2.20 with Recall 0.59 using a single NFE, outperforming all prior one-step distillation methods (DMD: 2.62, Diff-Instruct: 5.57, CD-LPIPS: 6.20). On ImageNet 128×128, EMD-16 reaches FID 6.0 and IS 140±2.83 in one step, surpassing CD (FID 7.0). For text-to-image generation distilled from Stable Diffusion v1.5, EMD-8 achieves FID-30k of 9.66 and CLIP Score 0.316 on MSCOCO, beating DMD (FID 11.49, CLIP 0.320) and all other accelerated single-step methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EMD-16 (FID 2.20) outperforms all prior one-step distillation methods on ImageNet 64×64, beating DMD (2.62) by 0.42 FID, Diff-Instruct (5.57) by 3.37 FID, and CD-LPIPS (6.20) by 4.0 FID. It approaches the 2-step Multistep CD (FID 2.0) while using only 1 NFE, and achieves a competitive Recall of 0.59 among distribution-matching methods (though trajectory-based methods like PD at 0.62 maintain higher recall). On ImageNet 128×128, EMD-16 (FID 6.0) improves over CD (7.0) and its own EMD-1 baseline (6.3). For text-to-image, EMD-8 (FID 9.66) beats DMD (11.49), InstaFlow-0.9B (13.10), UFOGen (12.78), and LCM-LoRA 1-step (77.90), while its CLIP Score of 0.316 is within 0.004 of DMD (0.320) and the teacher (0.322). The paper also shows that even the EMD-1 baseline (equivalent to Diff-Instruct/VSD) can be improved through hyperparameter tuning (FID 3.1 vs. reported 5.57), demonstrating that the EM framework provides a principled path to better mode-covering generation.

## Relation to Prior Work

EMD reframes one-step diffusion distillation through the Expectation-Maximization lens, showing that existing mode-seeking methods (VSD, Diff-Instruct) are exactly the 1-step MCMC special case of a more general framework that interpolates toward mode-covering forward KL as MCMC steps increase. The reparameterized (ε,z)-space Langevin sampling draws on neural transport MCMC techniques from energy-based model learning (Nijkamp et al., Xiao et al.), while the noise cancellation trick exploits the closed-form accumulation of Langevin noise to produce cleaner regression targets. Conceptually, the paper positions itself as a principled generalization of the score-distillation family rather than a competing paradigm, with the EM framework providing the unifying theoretical backbone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The clean interpolation between mode-seeking and mode-covering divergences via MCMC step count gives practitioners a tunable knob to balance fidelity against diversity in one-step generation, addressing a known failure mode of reverse-KL-based distillation (mode collapse). However, EMD still requires teacher initialization and is sensitive to the fixed timestep t*, limiting its flexibility for novel architectures or lower-dimensional latent spaces. The training cost scales linearly with MCMC steps (EMD-16 roughly doubles Diff-Instruct wall-clock time), leaving open the question of whether the Pareto frontier of training cost versus generation quality can be pushed further. The method's dependence on a learned auxiliary score network also introduces approximation error that could limit performance in high-dimensional or multi-modal settings.
