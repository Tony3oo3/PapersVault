## Problem

Prior recurrent solvers for algorithm extrapolation (Schwarzschild et al. 2021; Bansal et al. 2022) are restricted to same-size problems where input and output dimensions match, making them inapplicable to classification and decision-making tasks with fixed-size outputs. Additionally, these methods suffer from overthinking—performance degrades when test-time iterations exceed the training distribution—requiring auxiliary loss terms like progressive loss to mitigate.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240114/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single image observation of arbitrary spatial size (from 7×7 during training up to 512×512 at test time) and produces either a same-size image (for tasks like maze pathfinding or prefix-sum) or a fixed-dimension one-hot action vector of 3 or 4 elements (for different-size classification tasks). The critical constraint is that the model is trained exclusively on small observations (e.g., 15×15) and must extrapolate to arbitrarily larger inputs at test time by simply running more recurrent iterations, with no architectural changes or fine-tuning.

## Method

NeuralSolver combines three components: (1) a recurrent module built from a single layer-normalized convolutional LSTM that iteratively processes the input at multiple scales while always re-injecting the original observation (analogous to a recall module), maintaining constant spatial dimensionality across iterations; (2) a processing module of three 3×3 convolutional layers that reduces channel count, followed by an optional global max-pooling aggregation layer that collapses spatial dimensions to 1×1 for different-size tasks; and (3) a curriculum-based training scheme that progressively increases observation size every N epochs with a 20% probability of resampling previously seen sizes to prevent catastrophic forgetting. Unlike Bansal et al., the architecture uses a single convolutional layer in the recurrent block (rather than five) and requires no progressive loss or delta loss to prevent overthinking, achieving 90–99% fewer parameters while matching or exceeding prior state-of-the-art extrapolation accuracy.

## Results

On the same-size benchmark, NeuralSolver achieves 100% extrapolation accuracy on Prefix-Sum (32→512) and Maze (24→124), 99.97% on Thin-Maze (11→61), and 84.30% on Chess, outperforming Bansal et al. on all four tasks. On the novel different-size tasks, NeuralSolver reaches 100% accuracy on all four (1S-Maze, GoTo, Pong, Doorkey) when extrapolating to 128×128/129×129 observations, compared to 64–97% for Bansal et al. The model requires only 0.17–0.70M parameters versus 0.78–12.06M for Bansal et al. (a >90% reduction), and maintains 100% accuracy even at extreme 512×512 extrapolation where the baseline drops to 57–79%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is Bansal et al. [6] (NeurIPS 2022), the prior state-of-the-art recurrent solver using a recurrent ResNet with a recall module and progressive loss. NeuralSolver stochastically dominates Bansal et al. on every task in both same-size and different-size settings: on same-size tasks it improves from 86.63%→100% (Maze), 46.78%→99.97% (Thin-Maze), and 79.99%→84.30% (Chess); on different-size tasks it improves from 64–97% to 100% across all four novel tasks. A secondary FeedForward (non-recurrent ResNet) baseline confirms the necessity of recurrence, achieving 0% on same-size extrapolation and 49–79% on different-size tasks. In the sequential decision-making evaluation (Minigrid Doorkey), NeuralSolver maintains oracle-level reward (98–99%) at all execution sizes up to 128×128, while Bansal et al. degrades to 24.71% at 128×128 due to error accumulation. NeuralSolver also requires 90–99.6% fewer parameters than the baselines and does not suffer from overthinking without any progressive loss term.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the recurrent solver paradigm introduced by Schwarzschild et al. (NeurIPS 2021) and refined by Bansal et al. (NeurIPS 2022), which used recurrent ResNets with a recall module and progressive loss to extrapolate on same-size tasks. NeuralSolver departs from this lineage in two key ways: it replaces the multi-layer recurrent ResNet with a single layer-normalized ConvLSTM (eliminating the need for progressive loss to prevent overthinking), and it adds a processing module with an optional global max-pooling aggregation layer that enables application to different-size tasks (classification, decision-making) where prior recurrent solvers could not operate. The curriculum-based training scheme draws on prior work in neural algorithm learning (Kaiser & Sutskever, Zaremba & Sutskever) to address the reduced training signal in different-size settings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By extending recurrent solvers to different-size tasks, this work opens the algorithm-extrapolation paradigm to a much broader class of problems (classification, sequential decision-making) that were previously inaccessible to this family of methods. The 90%+ parameter reduction and elimination of progressive loss make the approach significantly more practical for deployment. The introduced different-size benchmark (GoTo, 1S-Maze, Pong, Doorkey) provides a standard for evaluating future extrapolation methods beyond the same-size setting. Open questions include whether the approach scales to online reinforcement learning (the authors note this as future work), whether the ConvLSTM advantage over recurrent ResNets persists in more complex or continuous-action settings, and how training efficiency can be further improved for very small training sizes.
