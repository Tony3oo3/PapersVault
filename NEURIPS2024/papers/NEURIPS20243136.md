## Problem

Dynamic regret minimization in online convex optimization lacks a unifying framework for analyzing and designing algorithms that adapt to comparator variability. In particular, it was unknown whether one could achieve a regret guarantee that cleanly separates the variance penalty (from loss gradients) from the variability penalty (from comparator movement), and whether the ideal squared path-length dependence is achievable. This paper proves that squared path-length adaptation is fundamentally impossible and characterizes the full frontier of achievable variance/variability trade-offs.

## Input / Output

The algorithm receives a stream of linear losses g_t ∈ R^d (with ||g_t||_2 ≤ G) over T rounds and operates over an unbounded convex feasible set W ⊆ R^d. It outputs a prediction w_t ∈ W each round, with the key structural detail that internally it operates in the extended dT-dimensional space W^T, embedding each loss as g̃_t = e_t ⊗ g_t. The per-round computation is O(d log T) due to the sparse support of the Haar basis, and the output guarantee is a dynamic regret bound that fully decouples the comparator variability term from the gradient variance term, with no pessimistic max_{t,t'}||u_t - u_t'|| multiplicative penalty.

## Method

The core contribution is a dynamic-to-static reduction (Proposition 1): the comparator sequence u_1,...,u_T is concatenated into ũ ∈ W^T, and each loss g_t is embedded as g̃_t = e_t ⊗ g_t ∈ R^{dT}, making dynamic regret exactly equal to static regret in the extended space. Algorithm design then reduces to selecting a dual-norm pair (||·||_M, ||·||_{M^{-1}}) for the static problem, with the variability penalty given by ||ũ||_M and the variance penalty by Tr(M^{-1}). A novel lower bound (Theorem 1), based on anti-concentration of Rademacher chaos of order 2, establishes a fundamental frontier: any choice of M trading a small variability penalty for a small variance penalty is impossible, and specifically the squared path-length norm requires Tr(M^{-1}) = Ω(T²), yielding vacuous regret. To achieve a non-trivial point on this frontier, the paper sets S = [H_n H_n^T]^{-1} where H_n is the unnormalized Haar basis matrix, producing a locally-smoothed squared path-length measure P̄(u, τ) at multiple timescales τ with variance penalty only O(log T)·Σ||g_t||², and implements the resulting algorithm via the 1-dimensional reduction of Cutkosky and Orabona with scale-free FTRL, achieving O(d log T) per-round computation.

## Results

The paper establishes a fundamental lower bound (Theorem 1) showing that for any algorithm, there is an irreducible trade-off between a variability penalty ||ũ||_M and a variance penalty Tr(M^{-1}), yielding regret Ω(Gε_T + G√(P[Tr(M^{-1}) + ||M̃^{-1}||_F · log^{1/2}(√(PV_T)/2ε_T)])). Proposition 2 proves that adapting to the squared path-length P_T^{||·||²} necessarily requires Tr(M^{-1}) = T(T+1)/2 = Ω(T²), making the ideal guarantee O(√(P_T^{||·||²} · Σ||g_t||²)) impossible. The main positive result (Proposition 3 + Theorem 2) achieves dynamic regret Õ(√((||ū||² + max_τ P̄(ū,τ)) · Σ||g_t||²)) using a Haar-based weighted norm, with only an O(log T) variance penalty, and in the worst case recovers Õ(√((||ū||² + D̄·P_T) · Σ||g_t||²)), matching prior unconstrained guarantees up to polylogarithmic factors.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical baselines. In terms of theoretical guarantees, the paper's main contribution is to decouple the variance and variability penalties that prior unconstrained dynamic regret algorithms [20, 47] couple together in the form Õ(√(P_T · Σ||g_t||² · ||u_t - ū||)). The proposed Haar-based algorithm achieves a fully decoupled guarantee Õ(√((||ū||² + D̄·P_T) · Σ||g_t||²)) in the worst case, matching the path-length dependence of prior work up to polylogarithmic terms while removing the pessimistic max_{t,t'}||u_t - u_{t'}|| multiplicative penalty. The paper also proves (via Theorem 1 and Proposition 2) that the seemingly natural improvement to a squared path-length dependence O(√(P_T^{||·||²} · Σ||g_t||²)) is fundamentally impossible for arbitrary comparator sequences, as it would require an Ω(T²) variance penalty. The algorithm is computationally efficient at O(d log T) per round, matching the per-step cost of existing dynamic regret methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the parameter-free online learning framework of Cutkosky and Orabona [9] and the Haar OLR algorithm of Zhang et al. [47], but introduces a fundamentally different perspective: a simple dynamic-to-static reduction (Proposition 1) that embeds the comparator sequence into a high-dimensional space, making dynamic regret equivalent to static regret. This reformulation, while elementary, is the key conceptual departure from prior work, which analyzed dynamic regret through direct path-length arguments or frequency-domain decompositions without explicitly connecting to the static regret literature. The lower bound technique, leveraging Rademacher chaos anti-concentration (Dinur et al. [10]), is novel in this context and enables a frontier of variance-variability trade-off lower bounds that were previously inaccessible.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The impossibility result showing that adapting to squared path-length necessarily incurs a vacuous O(GT) variance penalty is a significant negative result that redirects the field toward more nuanced variability measures. The Haar-wavelet-based framework provides the first fully decoupled variance/variability guarantee for unconstrained dynamic regret, and the general dual-norm-pair framework gives a concrete, extensible recipe for designing new algorithms. Open questions include developing tools for the infimal-convolution potential functions that characterize coupled guarantees (Section 5), extending beyond weighted norms, and tightening the logarithmic dependencies in the lower bounds, which the authors note proved very challenging.
