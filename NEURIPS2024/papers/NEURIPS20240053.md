## Problem

Existing dynamics modeling methods for visual RL (e.g., DeepMDP) are designed for single-modality inputs and fail to handle the complex, volatile relationships between multiple modalities. In multi-modal scenarios, different sensors share correlated scene descriptions while simultaneously preserving unique, task-critical observations (e.g., traffic lights visible only in RGB, cars hidden in darkness visible only in event signals). Prior multi-modal RL approaches either enforce modality alignment—discarding inconsistent information—or model modalities independently—ignoring their complementarity—both of which degrade dynamics modeling accuracy.

## Input / Output

The method consumes d visual modalities (RGB frames, event signals, depth images, LiDAR BEV) as 3 stacked consecutive frames each (128×128 on CARLA, 84×84 on DMControl), processed by modality-specific CNN encoders with identical architectures but different input channel counts. Each modality's latent feature is decomposed into a 50-dimensional consistent component and a 50-dimensional inconsistent component. The final output is a concatenated state representation across all modalities fed into a Soft Actor-Critic policy, which produces a stochastic action; the method also yields per-modality full-state predictions and a scalar reward prediction used as auxiliary supervision.

## Method

DDM decomposes each modality's features into modality-consistent and modality-inconsistent components via two parallel network branches, then treats them through separate dynamics pathways. Consistent features are regularized by mutual prediction (L_mp) and cross-modality transition prediction (L_tp) constraints that enforce shared temporal dynamics; inconsistent features are regularized by pairwise orthogonality across different modalities (L_orth) rather than within the same modality, avoiding overly aggressive separation that would reduce expressiveness. For dynamics, consistent features predict cross-modality next states, while merged consistent+inconsistent features per modality feed full-state prediction heads (L_fp) that capture modality-specific evolution. A reward prediction head (L_r) filters task-irrelevant content from both feature types. All objectives are jointly optimized with SAC, where the concatenated state representation replaces raw observations in the actor-critic update.

## Results

On the CARLA autonomous driving benchmark (RGB + event signals), DDM achieves an average episode return of 272.75 and driving distance of 304.75 m across four weather conditions, outperforming the strongest multi-modal RL baseline HAVE (245.50 ER, 275.25 m) by ~11% in return and ~11% in distance. On DMControl (RGB + depth), DDM reaches an average return of 781.67 on the easy group and 508.67 on the hard group, surpassing the best single-modality baseline SPR (777.00 / 458.33). The ablation study confirms that each component contributes: adding the orthogonality loss L_orth on top of all other losses yields the largest single gain, lifting average ER from 253.00 to 272.75 on CARLA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DDM wins against all nine baselines on both CARLA and DMControl. Against single-modality RL methods (SAC, DrQ, DeepMDP, SPR), DDM gains 27–108 ER on CARLA average, with the largest margin over SAC (+108.5 ER) and a clear advantage over the strongest single-modality dynamics method SPR (+51 ER). Against multi-modal computer-vision baselines adapted to RL (TransFuser, EFNet), DDM improves by 41–48 ER, showing that modality alignment alone is insufficient for dynamics modeling. Against multi-modal RL baselines (MuMMI, MAIE, HAVE), DDM leads by 27–64 ER; the gap over HAVE (the closest competitor) is +27.25 ER and +29.5 m driving distance, and DDM also exhibits notably lower variance (±35–61 vs. ±67–77 for HAVE), indicating more stable learning. On DMControl, DDM outperforms SPR by +4.67 ER (easy) and +50.34 ER (hard), with the hard-group gap driven primarily by Reacher Hard (224 vs. 187) and Quadruped Run (489 vs. 468).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the DeepMDP framework for latent-space dynamics modeling from single-modality visual RL to multi-modal settings, introducing an explicit decomposition of each modality into consistent (shared across modalities) and inconsistent (modality-specific) components. It departs from prior multi-modal RL methods (MuMMI, MAIE, HAVE) that primarily enforce modality alignment or mutual information maximization, and from conventional multi-modal CV approaches (TransFuser, EFNet) that treat cross-modal differences as noise to be suppressed. The key conceptual departure is treating modality inconsistencies as first-class signals for dynamics modeling rather than artifacts to be aligned away, using cross-modality orthogonality rather than within-modality mutual exclusivity to extract them.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Multi-modal visual RL remains under-explored, and this paper provides one of the first systematic treatments of how modality heterogeneity affects dynamics modeling, showing that both shared and unique modality content play distinct but equally important roles. The finding that enforcing within-modality orthogonality (the standard practice) is overly aggressive and degrades feature quality, while cross-modality orthogonality works better, offers a practical design principle for future multi-modal representation learning. Limitations include the absence of a planning mechanism (unlike MuZero or Dreamer), restriction to vision-based modalities, and evaluation on relatively small-scale benchmarks; extending to non-visual modalities (text, audio) and integrating long-horizon planning are natural next steps.
