## Problem

It is commonly assumed that solving vanishing and exploding gradients is sufficient for RNNs to learn long-term dependencies, but this paper identifies a previously understudied failure mode: as the network's memory length increases (|λ|→1), hidden states become increasingly sensitive to parameter perturbations even when the recurrent dynamics remain stable. This 'curse of memory' makes gradient-based optimization extremely difficult and provides a new explanation for why some architectures (deep SSMs, LSTMs) outperform others beyond the standard gradient-flow narrative.

## Input / Output

The theoretical analysis takes a linear diagonal RNN with recurrence parameter λ and an input sequence with a specified autocorrelation function R_x(Δ), and produces closed-form expressions for E[h_t²], E[(d_λ h_t)²], and the Hessian structure as functions of |λ| and input correlation. Empirically, the framework is instantiated on a 1D and nD linear teacher-student task with controllable time constants (ν₀), and on a 4-block deep recurrent stack performing next-token prediction on natural language, where signal propagation metrics are measured at initialization across cRNN, LRU, and GRU layers.

## Method

The paper derives that d_λ h_t is a double low-pass filter of the input, causing its second moment to diverge as (1−|λ|²)^(−3) compared to (1−|λ|²)^(−1) for h_t itself, and extends this to fully-connected networks via complex diagonalization, showing the explosion concentrates on eigenvalues but spreads across all entries of A. Two mitigations are identified: an input normalization factor γ(λ) that keeps forward and backward signal magnitudes O(1), and an eigenvalue reparametrization (e.g., λ=exp(−exp(ω))) that makes the gradient with respect to the reparametrized parameter bounded. A key structural finding is that diagonal connectivity aligns the high-curvature Hessian directions with coordinate axes, enabling Adam's per-parameter adaptive learning rates to compensate for residual sensitivity, whereas fully-connected RNNs distribute curvature across coupled directions where Adam cannot help. The analysis is validated through a teacher-student experiment showing LRUs outperform linear RNNs by 10× in sample efficiency at long memory scales, and through signal propagation measurements at initialization confirming that LRU and GRU keep gradient magnitudes independent of the characteristic time constant while cRNNs do not.

## Results

The paper's central theoretical result shows that the second moment of the gradient sensitivity E[(d_λ h_t)²] diverges as (1−|λ|²)^(−3) as the network encodes longer memories, while the hidden state variance E[h_t²] diverges only as (1−|λ|²)^(−1), meaning gradient sensitivity grows three orders of magnitude faster than activation magnitude. Empirically, on the teacher-student task with ν₀=0.99 (long-range dependencies), an LRU with 64 hidden units significantly outperforms a fully-connected linear RNN with the same hidden dimension, despite the linear RNN having 10× more parameters. In a 4-layer deep network at initialization, LRU and GRU (Chrono init) maintain constant E[(d_θ L)²] across all ν₀ values, while the complex diagonal RNN (cRNN) exhibits uncontrolled gradient explosion that grows with ν₀, confirming the curse of memory in practice.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares LRU against a fully-connected linear RNN on the teacher-student task (Figure 3). LRU significantly outperforms the linear RNN when the teacher encodes long memories (ν₀→1), and an ablation study reveals that the performance gap comes primarily from making the recurrent matrix block-diagonal (i.e., diagonal connectivity) rather than from the specific exponential reparametrization or input normalization. In signal propagation at initialization (Figure 5), LRU almost perfectly mitigates both forward-pass activation explosion and backward-pass gradient explosion (except for the complex angle parameter), while the cRNN baseline shows uncontrolled growth in both. GRU with Chrono initialization also maintains well-behaved signal propagation, with gradient magnitudes independent of ν₀, consistent with the theory that gated RNNs implicitly incorporate normalization and reparametrization. The Hessian analysis (Figure 4) reveals that while cRNN and LRU have similar eigenvalue spectra at optimality, the LRU's sensitive directions are concentrated on few coordinates (aligned with the canonical basis), enabling Adam to use larger effective learning rates, whereas the cRNN's diffuse sensitivity forces Adam to use uniformly small learning rates, severely slowing convergence.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the classical vanishing/exploding gradients analysis of RNNs (Hochreiter 1991; Bengio et al. 1994; Pascanu et al. 2013) by identifying an additional, previously under-studied optimization difficulty: the 'curse of memory,' where parameter sensitivity grows as the network encodes longer dependencies even when the recurrent Jacobian is spectrally bounded. It builds directly on the deep state-space model line (S4, S5, LRU, Mamba) and the signal-propagation/initialization literature for deep networks (Glorot & Bengio; He et al.; Yang et al.), but departs from the standard narrative that controlling gradient magnitudes is sufficient for well-behaved RNN training. The analysis also connects to the gated RNN literature (LSTM, GRU) by showing that their gating mechanisms implicitly implement the normalization and reparametrization needed to mitigate the curse of memory.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a unifying theoretical explanation for why diagonal recurrent architectures (SSMs, LRUs) outperform fully-connected linear RNNs and why gated architectures like GRUs/LSTMs are well-behaved, offering practitioners a principled design rationale beyond empirical trial-and-error. The finding that the angle of complex eigenvalues is fundamentally harder to learn than the magnitude helps explain why recent SSMs (Mamba, Hawk) abandoned complex parametrizations. Key limitations include the restriction to linear diagonal recurrences and the WSS assumption, and the theory does not address generalization or memory capacity. Open questions include whether the curse-of-memory analysis extends to nonlinear or input-dependent recurrences (as in Mamba's selective mechanism) and how to increase the expressivity of small diagonal modules while preserving their optimization properties.
