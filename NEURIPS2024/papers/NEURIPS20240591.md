## Problem

Existing physics-based human-scene interaction methods are limited to static objects or a single specific object dynamic (e.g., carrying one box, throwing one ball), and rely on privileged state information (exact object poses, goal coordinates) that is unavailable in real-world humanoid deployment. This paper addresses both gaps by studying general-purpose object rearrangement across diverse object geometries and weights, directed solely by egocentric vision and natural language.

## Input / Output

The student model HumanVLA consumes a 256×256 egocentric RGB frame (90° FoV, camera offset [0.103, 0, 0.175] from the head), a natural language instruction (encoded by a frozen BERT-base-uncased), a 223-dim proprioception vector (root height/rotation/velocities plus 14 link positions, rotations, and velocities in local coordinates), and the previous 28-dim action. It outputs 28 target joint positions for PD controllers at 30 Hz. The teacher, by contrast, additionally receives object BPS geometry (200×3 delta vectors), object pose/velocity, goal position/rotation, and A*-planned navigation waypoints in global coordinates.

## Method

A two-stage teacher-student framework is used. The teacher is trained with goal-conditioned PPO in IsaacGym using an AMP adversarial discriminator for motion realism, augmented with four key techniques: Basis Point Set geometry encoding for object-agnostic interaction, a carry-curriculum pre-training phase (locomotion + contact only, excluding ground-level objects) to bootstrap the harder three-step rearrangement, style-reward clipping that caps the adversarial reward at the current task reward to prevent the policy from mimicking irrelevant hand motions, and in-context A* path planning over a 20 cm grid occupancy map for collision-free navigation. The student HumanVLA is then distilled via DAgger (online behavior cloning with exponential β-scheduling) to mitigate covariate shift, using an EfficientNet-B0 image encoder, frozen BERT text encoder, and a 6-layer MLP action decoder. A novel active-rendering mechanism analytically computes the head-to-object direction, solves neck-joint IK, and blends the resulting gaze action with the teacher action (weight 0.2) to ensure the egocentric camera actually looks at the target object during supervision.

## Results

On unseen HITR tasks, HumanVLA-Teacher (state-based) achieves 79.3% success rate with 19.3 cm precision and 4.6 s execution time, while the full vision-language HumanVLA achieves 60.2% success rate with 57.0 cm precision and 5.8 s. On the box rearrangement subset, HumanVLA-Teacher reaches 98.1% success with 4.2 cm precision, outperforming InterPhys's reported 94.3% / 8.3 cm. The vision-language HumanVLA outperforms the offline GC-BC baseline (10.2% success, 152.3 cm precision) by a large margin on unseen tasks, demonstrating the critical role of online DAgger training and active rendering.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against InterPhys [15], HumanVLA-Teacher wins on all three metrics for unseen tasks: 79.3% vs. 59.5% success rate, 19.3 cm vs. 52.5 cm precision, and 4.6 s vs. 5.9 s execution time. On the box rearrangement subset, HumanVLA-Teacher also dominates with 98.1% success and 4.2 cm precision versus InterPhys's 94.3% / 8.3 cm (official) or 97.8% / 12.6 cm (reimplementation). Against the Offline GC-BC baseline [29], HumanVLA achieves a 50-point success rate improvement (60.2% vs. 10.2%) and a 95 cm precision reduction (57.0 cm vs. 152.3 cm), highlighting the severe covariate shift problem that offline behavior cloning suffers from. The full HumanVLA slightly outperforms InterPhys on success rate (60.2% vs. 59.5%) but trails on precision (57.0 cm vs. 52.5 cm), reflecting the inherent information loss from replacing privileged states with egocentric vision and language.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the physics-based human-scene interaction line (InterPhys, UniHSI, InterScene) from static or single-object tasks to general multi-object rearrangement, and introduces a teacher-student distillation pipeline that bridges goal-conditioned RL+AMP training with a vision-language-action student. The teacher policy builds directly on AMP's adversarial motion prior and InterPhys's task-conditioned reward structure, while adding geometry-aware encoding (BPS), a carry curriculum, style reward clipping, and in-context A* path planning to handle diverse object dynamics. The student HumanVLA is the first VLA model applied to a full-body physical humanoid, departing from prior VLAs (RT-2, Open X-Embodiment) that target simple gripper embodiments, and uses DAgger-based online behavior cloning with an active rendering technique to close the perception gap between privileged state and egocentric vision.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work establishes a concrete pipeline for vision-language-directed manipulation on a bipedal humanoid, a setting that prior HSI and VLA literature has not addressed simultaneously, and the HITR dataset (615 tasks, 34 movable objects across four room types) provides a reusable benchmark for the community. However, significant limitations remain: the spherical-hand humanoid cannot perform dexterous manipulation, only one object is rearranged per task, and generalization to unseen object geometry collapses to 20% success rate, indicating that geometry encoding helps but does not fully solve the novel-object problem. Open questions include whether the teacher-student gap (85.9% → 74.8%) can be narrowed with larger VLA backbones, whether multi-object and long-horizon rearrangement is feasible, and how the approach transfers to real-world deployment beyond simulation.
