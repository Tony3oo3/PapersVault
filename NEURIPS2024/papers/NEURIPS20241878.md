## Problem

Despite MAE's strong performance, it remains unclear what and how MAE actually learns during pretraining, and prior informed-masking approaches that improve MAE rely on external pre-trained models or labeled data, undermining its self-supervised nature. This paper asks whether MAE can generate its own informed masks by leveraging internal signals about its learning progress, without any external resources.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241878/cropped_page1_idx6.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images (224×224, split into 196 patches of 16×16 pixels) and produces a trained ViT-B encoder for downstream tasks. Unusually, the masking strategy is not fixed: for the first ~50 epochs the model uses standard random masking, after which it internally switches to object-centric informed masks derived from its own learned patch similarity structure, with a small set of hint tokens left visible to preserve reconstructability.

## Method

The paper first demonstrates through token-relation analysis (cosine similarity and attention matrices) that MAE learns pattern-based patch-level clustering from extremely early pretraining epochs, verified by tracking KL divergence of token relations and bi-partitioning gaps across training. It then introduces an 'exploitation rate' metric that recursively accumulates attention weights on mask tokens through decoder layers, identifying the epoch T (≈50) at which mask tokens carry shared information comparable to visible tokens. After T, the model bi-partitions patches via Normalized Cut on the second-last encoder layer's cosine similarity matrix, identifies the object-centric cluster (the one containing the token with the largest absolute eigenvector element), and masks it out using a relevance score (cosine similarity to the cluster mean) to robustly cover the entire object even under imperfect partitioning. A few hint tokens are retained to prevent reconstruction collapse, and the entire process runs in a single uninterrupted training stage with no external models or labels.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K linear probing, the proposed self-guided MAE achieves 62.9% top-1 accuracy, outperforming vanilla MAE (61.4%) and AMT (61.7%). On fine-tuned ImageNet-1K, it reaches 83.2% top-1 accuracy. For object detection on COCO, it achieves 43.3 AP^box and 39.3 AP^mask, and for semantic segmentation on ADE20K, it reaches 45.2 mIoU, all surpassing both baselines across every downstream task.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against vanilla MAE [22], the proposed method wins on every metric: +1.5 pp on ImageNet-1K linear probing (62.9 vs 61.4), +0.7 pp on fine-tuned ImageNet-1K (83.2 vs 82.5), +0.2 AP^box on COCO detection (43.3 vs 43.0), and +0.2 mIoU on ADE20K (45.2 vs 45.0). Against AMT [38], the gains are larger, particularly on fine-grained tasks: +2.9 pp on iNaturalist (78.9 vs 76.0), +2.2 pp on CUB200 (82.8 vs 80.8), and +2.7 AP^mask on COCO segmentation (39.3 vs 36.6). In the broader comparison (Appendix E, Table IV), SemMAE [32] achieves 68.7 linear probing at 800 epochs but requires an external pre-trained model and 6.3× training time, while the proposed method reaches 65.9 at 800 epochs with only 2× training time and no external resources. HPM [50] scores 63.2 at 400 epochs with 1.5× training time and an additional parameterized module (138M params), whereas the proposed method achieves 62.9 at 400 epochs with 1× training time and the same 112M parameter count as vanilla MAE.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends MAE (He et al., CVPR 2022) by replacing its random masking with self-generated informed masks, building on the hierarchical latent variable model interpretation of MAE (Kong et al., CVPR 2023). It departs from prior informed-masking methods (MST, AttMask, SemMAE, AutoMAE, ADIOS) that all rely on external pre-trained models, labels, or adversarial modules, by deriving the mask entirely from MAE's own internal patch-clustering progress. The bi-partitioning step borrows Normalized Cut (Shi & Malik, 2000), and the timing mechanism introduces a novel 'exploitation rate' metric that tracks how much shared information the decoder's mask tokens carry relative to visible tokens.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The discovery that MAE establishes pattern-based patch clustering from remarkably early training stages (with clear bi-partitioning gaps and rapid KL-divergence convergence within the first ~50 epochs) provides a concrete mechanistic understanding of what MAE actually learns, complementing prior theoretical analyses. The self-contained informed masking strategy offers a practical, drop-in improvement that preserves MAE's fully self-supervised nature without external dependencies. However, the gains are modest (~1.5 LP points on ImageNet-1K) and the authors note the method may degrade on highly fragmented images where numerous clusters make informed masking approach random masking. Open questions include whether the exploitation-rate-based timing generalizes across architectures (e.g., video MAE) and whether the object-centric masking bias limits applicability to non-object-centric scenes.
