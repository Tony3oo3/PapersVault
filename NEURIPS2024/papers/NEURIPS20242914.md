## Problem

Building unified contrastive representations for more than three modalities is hindered by the high cost of collecting large-scale paired data and the architectural limitation of prior methods like C-MCR, which project non-overlapping modalities into a new shared space and thereby discard alignment information from the original pre-trained spaces. This makes it difficult to incrementally integrate additional modalities into a single coherent embedding space.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242914/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes only unimodal data (no paired samples) from four modalities: audio clips, text descriptions, RGB images, and 3D point clouds, each encoded by a frozen pre-trained encoder (CLAP, CLIP, ULIP). It outputs a unified contrastive embedding in the frozen base space (CLIP's image-text space), where all modalities share the same 512-dimensional representation. The only trainable parameters are lightweight projectors (a linear layer plus a 2-layer MLP) per leaf space, requiring ~3 GB GPU memory and ~1.5 hours of training on a single GPU.

## Method

Ex-MCR extends a leaf space into a fixed base space via overlapping modalities, rather than connecting two spaces into a new one, thereby preserving all alignment within the base space. Three key enhancements drive the alignment: (1) various modality-centric data, where each modality (not just the overlapping one) is used as a query to retrieve semantically consistent pseudo-pairs, yielding a more comprehensive view of the representation space; (2) a decoupled projector that separates a linear layer for closing the intra-space modality gap from an MLP for the inter-space mapping, reducing optimization conflicts; and (3) a dense InfoNCE contrastive loss applied across all four possible modality pairs (audio-image, audio-text, text-image, text-text) in the base space, which stabilizes the learned alignment. The base space is entirely frozen, so the method is orthogonal to and composable with data-driven pre-training approaches like ImageBind.

## Results

On audio-image retrieval, Ex-MCR-huge achieves R@1 of 1.80 (FlickrNet), 1.89 (AVE), and 3.26 (VGGSS), substantially outperforming C-MCR (1.39/1.25/1.94) and data-driven methods like AudioCLIP (1.37/0.61/1.25) and WAV2CLIP (0.82/0.95/2.51) without using any paired audio-image data. On audio-text retrieval (AudioCaps), Ex-MCR-huge reaches R@1=26.95 and R@5=59.60, surpassing C-MCR (15.76/41.37) and AudioCLIP (3.53/11.30). For 3D-image-text tasks, Ex-MCR-base achieves 66.53% Acc@1 on ModelNet40 (beating ULIP's 60.40%) and R@1=2.54 on Objaverse-LVIS 3D-image retrieval, while fully preserving CLIP's image-text performance (R@1=40.24 on COCO). When combined with ImageBind's data-driven training, Ex-MCR-huge+ImageBind achieves the best audio-image R@1 of 7.92 on FlickrNet, demonstrating complementary gains.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Ex-MCR-base outperforms C-MCR (the direct predecessor) across all audio-image and audio-text metrics: e.g., R@1 improves from 1.39→1.57 on FlickrNet and 15.76→19.07 on AudioCaps, while also fully preserving CLIP's image-text alignment (R@1=40.24 on COCO vs. C-MCR's degraded 16.67). Against data-driven methods AudioCLIP and WAV2CLIP, Ex-MCR-base achieves 5–20× higher audio-image R@1 (e.g., 1.57 vs. 1.37 and 0.82 on FlickrNet) without any paired audio-image data. On 3D tasks, Ex-MCR-base surpasses ULIP (Acc@1: 66.53 vs. 60.40 on ModelNet40; R@1: 2.54 vs. 1.45 on Objaverse-LVIS) but trails ULIPv2 (Acc@1: 66.53 vs. 73.06; R@1: 2.54 vs. 6.00), which was trained on large-scale 3D-image-text triplets. Ex-MCR-huge matches ImageBind's image-text performance (R@1=57.28 on COCO) but falls short on audio-image (R@1=1.80 vs. 7.68 on FlickrNet); however, combining Ex-MCR-huge with ImageBind yields the best audio-image R@1 of 7.92, demonstrating that the two approaches are complementary. Notably, Ex-MCR preserves the native audio-text alignment of CLAP (R@1=40.25) only when used as the base space; when CLAP is the leaf space, Ex-MCR-huge achieves 26.95 R@1 on AudioCaps, still far exceeding C-MCR's 15.76.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly builds on C-MCR (Wang et al., 2023), which connects two pre-trained contrastive spaces by projecting both onto a new shared space via overlapping modalities. The key conceptual departure is replacing this 'connecting' paradigm with an 'extending' paradigm: one space (leaf) is mapped into a fixed base space rather than both being projected into a novel latent space, thereby preserving the base space's original alignment. The method also draws on the broader MCR literature (CLIP, CLAP, ULIP, ImageBind) as the source of pre-trained spaces to be extended, and positions itself as orthogonal to data-driven approaches like AudioCLIP and ImageBind that require large-scale paired triplets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Ex-MCR addresses a practical bottleneck in multi-modal representation learning: the prohibitive cost of collecting paired data and training from scratch when adding new modalities to an existing unified space. The extremely low compute requirement (~1.5 hours on a single GPU with 3 GB memory) and the ability to complement data-driven methods make it a practical tool for incrementally building unified spaces. However, the approach is constrained by the quality of the pre-trained base and leaf spaces, and the emergent cross-modal alignment (e.g., audio-3D) is demonstrated only qualitatively without a dedicated quantitative benchmark. Open questions include how the method scales to many leaf spaces simultaneously and whether stronger base models (e.g., larger CLIP variants) further improve the ceiling.
