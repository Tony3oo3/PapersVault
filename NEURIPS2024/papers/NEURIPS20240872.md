## Problem

Prior multi-agent imitation learning (MAIL) work reduces the problem to matching expert behavior via the value gap, which is sufficient when agents are non-strategic but provides no guarantees when agents can strategically deviate from recommendations. The paper identifies that the regret gap—a measure of the maximum incentive any agent has to deviate—is the correct objective for MAIL with strategic agents, and proves that value equivalence can coexist with an arbitrarily large regret gap (Ω(H)), establishing that 'regret is hard' in a way that has no single-agent analog.

## Input / Output

The algorithms operate in a finite-horizon Markov Game with m agents, taking as input expert demonstrations D_E and a deviation class Φ_i for each agent. MALICE additionally requires β-coverage of the expert's state distribution (every state visited with probability ≥ β), while BLADES requires interactive access to a queryable expert who provides recommendations at states induced by the learner's policy under each deviation. The output is a mediator joint policy σ: S → Δ(A) that achieves an O(εuH) regret gap bound, where u is the recoverability constant bounding the expert's advantage under any single-step deviation.

## Method

The key insight is that the regret gap depends on counterfactual expert recommendations at states reachable only under agent deviations—states the expert never visits on its own policy—making it fundamentally harder than the value gap. MALICE extends ALICE by defining a convex loss that takes the maximum over all agent deviations of an importance-sampled BC loss reweighted by the density ratio d^{π_{σ,φ_i}}(s)/d^{π_{σ_E}}(s), then optimizes it via no-regret OCO. BLADES extends DAgger by, at each iteration, sampling trajectories under the learner's policy composed with every possible deviation φ_i, querying the expert for recommendations at those shifted states, and minimizing the aggregated deviation-aware loss via OCO. Both algorithms achieve O(εuH) regret gap bounds matching the strongest known value gap results in single-agent IL, and the paper proves matching lower bounds showing these rates are tight.

## Results

The paper establishes tight upper and lower bounds on the regret gap for multi-agent imitation learning algorithms. Under β-coverage, J-BC and J-IRL achieve O(1/β · εuH) regret gap bounds, while the proposed MALICE algorithm improves this to O(εuH) by removing the 1/β coverage factor. Under a queryable expert assumption, BLADES also achieves O(εuH). All bounds are tight, with matching Ω(εuH) or Ω(1/β · εuH) lower bounds. A key negative result shows that even perfect value equivalence (matching occupancy measures) can yield an Ω(H) regret gap, establishing that regret is fundamentally harder than value in MAIL.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares the proposed MALICE and BLADES algorithms against J-BC and J-IRL, which are direct multi-agent extensions of standard single-agent imitation learning. Under β-coverage, both J-BC and J-IRL suffer an O(1/β · εuH) regret gap bound, where 1/β can grow exponentially with horizon; MALICE removes this 1/β factor entirely, achieving O(εuH) by using importance-weighted losses (analogous to ALICE in single-agent IL). BLADES achieves the same O(εuH) bound but under a different assumption (queryable expert rather than full coverage), making it applicable when demonstrations lack coverage. A critical distinction from single-agent IL is that J-IRL's interactive training does NOT improve the regret gap bound beyond J-BC's (both are O(1/β · εuH)), unlike the value gap where IRL enjoys O(εH) versus BC's O(εH²). The paper also proves a fundamental separation: value equivalence provides no guarantee on the regret gap (Theorem 4.3), while regret equivalence implies value equivalence under complete reward/deviation classes (Theorem 4.1).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the regret gap concept from Waugh et al.'s Normal Form Game formulation [27] to the more general Markov Game setting, and builds on the OCO reduction framework from DAgger [18] and ALICE [20] to derive multi-agent analogues (BLADES and MALICE). It departs fundamentally from prior MAIL work [19, 12, 4, 26, 11], which treats the problem as a value-gap minimization over joint policies, by showing this objective is insufficient when agents are strategic. The u-recoverability assumption generalizes the standard recoverability condition from single-agent IL [18, 21, 20] to account for the additional distribution shift induced by agent deviations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes that the standard imitation learning objective (value gap) provides essentially no guarantees on the regret gap in multi-agent settings, even under perfect occupancy measure matching — a fundamental limitation that prior MAIL work had not identified. This reframes the core question of what a multi-agent learner should optimize and motivates the two new algorithms with matching O(εuH) bounds. Key limitations include the purely theoretical treatment on tabular MGs, the need for either full demonstration coverage or a queryable expert, and the absence of practical implementations. Open questions include whether the coverage/queryable-expert assumptions can be relaxed, how the algorithms scale to continuous action spaces, and whether the u-recoverability assumption holds in realistic multi-agent domains.
