## Problem

Learning image features that are consistent across viewpoints of the same 3D location is critical for spatial reasoning, but the smooth Average Precision (sAP) ranking loss used by prior work (e.g., LoCUS) requires computing a full |P_B| × (|P_B| + |N_B|) similarity-difference matrix, consuming tens of gigabytes of GPU memory and precluding training of larger models. This paper identifies that bottleneck and asks whether the sAP objective can be reformulated and approximated to reduce memory by orders of magnitude without sacrificing the ranking signal.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243950/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At test time the trained extractor takes a single RGB image and outputs a 30×40 feature map of 768-dimensional patch vectors (for the DINO-ViT-Base/8 backbone). At training time, each image patch is additionally backprojected to a 3D world point using camera intrinsics, extrinsics, and depth, which defines positive pairs (3D distance ≤ 0.5 m) and negative pairs (0.5 m < distance ≤ 5.0 m) within the same environment. The training images are 256×320 crops from Matterport3D panoramas, and the auxiliary geometric information is discarded at inference.

## Method

The core contribution is a pair-based reformulation of the smooth AP loss that (i) introduces batch correction factors |P|/|P_B| and |N|/|N_B| to make the batched loss an unbiased ratio estimator of the full-dataset loss, and (ii) decouples the small set of 'query' positive pairs P′_B (|P′_B|=32) from the large reference sets P_B and N_B, shrinking the similarity-difference matrix from |P_B|×(|P_B|+|N_B|) to |P′_B|×(|P_B|+|N_B|). A second efficiency trick thresholds the sigmoid: pairs whose similarity difference exceeds Δ=0.076 (where the gradient is <0.2% of maximum) are replaced by their saturated values (0 or 1) and removed from the computation graph, with correction terms δ⁺_α and δ⁻_α compensating for the non-uniform sampling. Together these reduce peak memory from ~51 GB to ~500 KB for the loss matrix, a ~2000× improvement. The trained model is a 6-layer fully convolutional residual network (28.9 M parameters) that learns additive corrections to frozen DINO ViT-Base/8 features, supervised entirely by the ranking loss on patch pairs sampled via FAISS nearest-neighbour search in 3D.

## Results

On the pixel correspondence task (ScanNet Paired split), LoCo achieves 61.8% recall at 0°–15° viewpoint change, substantially outperforming DINO (45.0%), DINOv2 (37.0%), CroCo-v2 (16.8%), and LoCUS (23.5%). On scene-stable panoptic segmentation, LoCo attains 66.5 Jaccard / 67.5 IoU / 84.5 AP on Matterport3D and 83.5 Jaccard / 76.3 IoU / 88.8 AP on ScanNet, surpassing DINO (81.6 AP) and LoCUS (40.5 AP) on Matterport3D while remaining competitive with CroCo-v2 (87.0 AP). The method achieves these gains with only 28.9M trainable parameters (a small CNN on frozen DINO features) and requires 48 GB GPU memory, compared to 11 GB for LoCUS but enabling ~2000× larger effective batch sizes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LoCo decisively outperforms LoCUS [26] on pixel correspondence (61.8 vs. 23.5 at 0°–15°) and scene-stable panoptic segmentation (84.5 vs. 40.5 AP on Matterport3D), demonstrating that the memory-efficient loss reformulation enables training larger models that far exceed the original method. Against DINO [5], LoCo gains 16.8 points on pixel correspondence recall (61.8 vs. 45.0) and 2.9 AP on Matterport3D panoptic segmentation, confirming that location-consistent training adds value beyond semantic features. DINOv2 [31] performs worse than DINO on both tasks, which the authors attribute to its later layers encoding more semantic (less location-specific) information. CroCo-v2 [44] is the closest competitor on panoptic segmentation (87.0 vs. 84.5 AP on Matterport3D) but is far weaker on pixel correspondence (16.8 vs. 61.8), because its decoder-based training does not incentivise cosine-similarity-based location consistency. On Visual Place Recognition, LoCo (46.8 R@1) slightly edges out DINO (45.7) on Matterport3D but trails MixVPR (72.4 R@1) on ScanNet, suggesting VLAD aggregation partially erases the spatial-arrangement advantage of location-consistent features.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends LoCUS (Kloepfer et al., ICCV 2023), the same group's prior work on learning 3D-consistent patch features via a smooth average precision (sAP) ranking loss. The key conceptual departure is reformulating the sAP objective in terms of patch *pairs* rather than individual patches around 'tentative 3D landmarks,' which enables a saturation-based approximation that cuts memory by ~3 orders of magnitude. It also corrects a theoretical gap present in both the original Smooth-AP (Brown et al., ECCV 2020) and LoCUS: the absence of batch correction factors that are necessary for the batched loss to approximate the full-dataset loss. The training strategy adapts the DINO-Tracker architecture, learning additive CNN residuals on top of frozen DINO ViT features rather than training a full model from scratch.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 3-order-of-magnitude memory reduction is practically significant: it transforms a loss that required ~51 GB (forcing 10% negative-pair subsampling in LoCUS) into one that fits in ~500 KB, enabling 768-dimensional features and larger backbones within the same GPU budget. However, the method still requires ground-truth camera poses and depth maps at training time, only trains small residual CNNs (28.9M params) on frozen features, and is validated on just 59 training environments—leaving open whether the approach scales to truly large-scale, fully self-supervised training from scratch. The finding that DINOv2 features are *less* location-consistent than DINO (despite being semantically stronger) is a useful caution for practitioners assuming newer foundation models are uniformly better for geometric tasks.
