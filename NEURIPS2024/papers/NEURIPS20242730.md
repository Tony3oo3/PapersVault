## Problem

Existing compositional text-to-image benchmarks rely on fixed prompt templates with restricted concept categories (2–6) and cap prompts at five concepts, yielding low prompt diversity and poor discriminative power between models. This makes it difficult to measure how T2I models degrade as compositional complexity increases beyond a handful of visual concepts, and to identify specific concept categories where models struggle.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242730/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark takes a difficulty parameter k (1–7) and produces 300 randomly generated compositional text prompts per k value, each combining one object with k additional visual concepts drawn from eight categories (objects, colors, numbers, shapes, sizes, textures, styles, spatial relationships). For each prompt, a T2I model generates a 1024×1024 image, which is then graded by GPT-4o answering one yes/no question per concept. The final outputs are two aggregate scores: a full-mark score (fraction of images satisfying all k+1 concepts) and a concept fraction score (mean fraction of concepts satisfied per image).

## Method

CONCEPTMIX operates in two stages. In prompt generation, it randomly samples k+1 concept categories (always including one object), binds concepts to specific objects via a JSON structure, and uses GPT-4o to write a coherent natural-language prompt, followed by a GPT-4o validation step that rejects physically impossible combinations (e.g., a triangle-shaped person) while retaining creative ones (e.g., a square-shaped cloud). In concept evaluation, because the prompts are constructed from known concepts, the decomposition into per-concept yes/no questions is exact—avoiding the nonsensical questions that plague prior decomposition-based evaluators on arbitrary prompts. GPT-4o then answers each question against the generated image in a fresh conversation, and scores are aggregated. The exponential growth of concept combinations (~145 billion across k=1–7) makes memorization infeasible, and the benchmark supports easy/hard variants by splitting each category's concepts based on per-concept performance.

## Results

On CONCEPTMIX, DALL-E 3 achieves the highest full-mark score at 0.83 (k=1), dropping to 0.50 (k=3), 0.27 (k=4), and 0.08 (k=7). Open-source models degrade much faster: SD v1.4 falls from 0.52 (k=1) to 0.08 (k=3) and 0.00 (k≥6), while SDXL Base, Playground v2.5, and DeepFloyd IF XL v1 cluster around 0.18–0.22 at k=3 and below 0.10 at k=4. The concept fraction score (average proportion of concepts satisfied) shows a more gradual decline, with DALL-E 3 maintaining 0.71 at k=7 versus 0.36 for SD v1.4. Analysis of LAION-5B captions reveals an average of only 2.75±0.90 concept categories per caption, with shape (24/882) and spatial (20/882) concepts severely underrepresented, explaining the sharp performance drop at k≥3.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against T2I-CompBench [19], CONCEPTMIX demonstrates substantially stronger discriminative power: T2I-CompBench's 3-in-1 and GPT4v scores cluster tightly across models (Figure 8), making it difficult to differentiate performance, whereas CONCEPTMIX scores spread widely with gaps that widen as k increases, clearly separating DALL-E 3 from open-source models. CONCEPTMIX also supports up to 8 concept categories and unlimited concepts per prompt, versus T2I-CompBench's 6 categories and ≤5 concepts per prompt. Against T2VScore [26] as a grading method, CONCEPTMIX's GPT-4o-based per-concept VQA grading achieves a Pearson correlation of r=0.93 with human majority-vote scores across k values (vs. r=0.47 for T2VScore) and r=0.81 across models at k=3 (vs. r=0.69 for T2VScore). T2VScore fails to capture the expected performance decline as k increases, while CONCEPTMIX's grading faithfully tracks the monotonic degradation observed by human evaluators. The human evaluation (10 participants, 350 image-prompt pairs) confirms that GPT-4o grading aligns with human majority vote at 0.81 consistency, comparable to the 0.85 inter-annotator agreement among humans.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of compositional T2I benchmarks (CC-500, ABC-6K, T2I-CompBench, HRS-comp) that rely on fixed templates combining at most 5 visual concepts. It departs from this template-based paradigm by using GPT-4o to generate free-form, coherent prompts from randomly sampled concept tuples, and by grading via per-concept VQA questions rather than holistic similarity scores. The design is partly inspired by SKILL-MIX, a compositional generalization benchmark for LLMs, but requires substantially more complex prompt construction and image-level evaluation. The use of a VLM (GPT-4o) as both prompt generator and grader represents a shift from the embedding-based (CLIPScore) and pixel-similarity (FID, LPIPS) metrics that dominated earlier T2I evaluation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CONCEPTMIX provides a scalable, controllable, and extensible benchmark that reveals a sharp performance cliff at k≥3 for most T2I models, with even DALL-E 3 dropping to 17% full-mark at k=5. The finding that LAION-5B captions average only 2.75 concept categories and severely underrepresent spatial and shape concepts offers a concrete, actionable explanation for why models struggle with compositional generation, pointing toward the need for more diverse training data. Open questions include whether the GPT-4o grader's slight leniency at high k (where both human and automatic scores converge near zero) masks residual capability differences, and whether the benchmark's reliance on a single VLM for grading introduces systematic biases that could shift as VLMs evolve.
