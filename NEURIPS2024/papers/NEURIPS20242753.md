## Problem

Computing Partial Information Decomposition (PID) terms generally requires solving a constrained minimization over probability distributions, which is computationally intractable for continuous variables. The only known analytical PID result is for jointly Gaussian systems, leaving a large gap for other distribution families widely used in neuroscience (Poisson, Cauchy, Lévy-stable) and statistics. This paper addresses that gap by identifying structural conditions under which the BROJA-PID minimization admits a closed-form solution.

## Input / Output

The method takes a joint distribution P(M,X,Y) where the conditionals P(X|M) and P(Y|M) belong to a stable or convolution-closed family with an affine (or linear) dependence on M, specified by parameters such as scale, rate, or shape coefficients. It produces an explicit Markov chain Q_MC(M,X,Y) ∈ Δ_P that certifies one unique-information term is zero, from which all four PID atoms (UI, RI, SI) follow by solving the linear system in Eq. (1). For non-affine systems satisfying a weaker bias-decomposition condition, the method instead outputs an analytically minimizable upper bound on the BROJA-PID objective, yielding an approximate PID.

## Method

The core insight is that an 'affine dependence structure'—where P(X|M) and P(Y|M) are stable or convolution-closed distributions whose parameters are affine functions of M—guarantees the existence of a Markov chain M→X→Y (or M→Y→X) within the constraint set Δ_P, forcing one UI term to zero and reducing PID to a linear solve. For stable distributions, the Markov chain is constructed by adding independent stable noise to the higher-SNR channel (generalizing the Gaussian noise-addition argument). For convolution-closed distributions, the construction leverages the dilation/thinning operation from Neufeld et al., splitting X into independent dilated components whose sum recovers the lower-SNR channel. For exponential-family distributions, the conjugate-prior reversal strategy from Leiner et al. (data fission) provides the analogous decomposition. For non-affine systems, the paper decomposes X and Y into dilated sub-components via convolution-closed structure, applies the data-processing inequality to obtain an upper bound on I_Q(M;[X,Y]), and shows this bound is analytically minimizable, yielding a tight approximation validated within <1% of numerical ground truth on Poisson systems.

## Results

The paper's primary contribution is theoretical: Theorems 1–7 establish that at least one unique information (UI) term is zero for affine systems of stable distributions, convolution-closed distributions, and certain exponential family distributions, enabling closed-form PID computation. The only empirical validation (Sec. 6.2) compares the analytical upper bound Q_A against the numerical ground-truth Q_N for Poisson systems across 20 non-linear function pairs and 75 P(M) distributions. The upper bound is within <1% of the numerical ground-truth for 16 out of 20 function pairs, with the median ratio of the gap to the ground-truth value shown in Figure 1c.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares its analytical upper bound Q_A(M,X,Y) against the numerical ground-truth Q_N(M,X,Y) computed using the BROJA-2PID solver from Makkeh et al. [50] (Apache 2.0-licensed code). The comparison is conducted on Poisson systems with 20 non-linear function pairs (f₁, f₂) and 75 discrete P(M) distributions (subdivided into groups of 2, 4, and 8 outcomes). The analytical upper bound Q_A is very tight: for 16 of 20 function pairs, the gap I_QA − I_QN is less than 1% of the numerical ground-truth I_QN. The remaining 4 function pairs show slightly larger gaps but still remain small in absolute terms. This demonstrates that the analytical construction from the convolution-closed dilation framework provides a near-exact solution to the BROJA-PID minimization even for non-affine dependence structures, where the exact theorems (Sec. 4–5) do not apply.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the sole prior analytical PID result for jointly Gaussian systems (Barrett 2015; Venkatesh & Schamberg 2022) by identifying that the key enabling property is not Gaussianity itself but a particular 'affine dependence structure' between sources and target. It then shows this structure generalizes to the much broader stable distribution family (Theorems 1–5) and, via a novel theoretical link to data thinning (Neufeld et al. 2024) and data fission (Leiner et al. 2023), to convolution-closed and certain exponential-family distributions (Theorems 6–7). The BROJA-PID minimization framework of Bertschinger et al. (2014) provides the optimization target that all results analytically solve.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first analytical PID computation for non-Gaussian distributions, including fat-tailed stable distributions and Poisson/Cauchy models widely used in neuroscience for neural spikes and network dynamics, providing a large test bed for validating numerical PID estimators. The upper bound for non-affine convolution-closed systems (Sec. 6) offers a practical approximation tool where exact solutions are unavailable, with simulation showing <1% error for most tested function pairs. Key limitations include the requirement of affine dependence on M for exact results, predominantly univariate M, and the open question of whether analytical solutions exist for non-affine dependence structures. The cross-field connection between PID and data thinning/fission opens bidirectional research avenues that may yield further decomposition strategies.
