## Problem

Existing spatial image-to-text (SI2T) and spatial text-to-image (ST2I) methods treat these tasks independently and rely on 2D feature modeling, which fails to capture 3D spatial relationships such as object pose, perspective, and layout. Although SI2T and ST2I are naturally dual processes (each task's input is the other's output), no prior work has exploited this duality to share 3D spatial representations and let the easier sub-processes of one task guide the harder sub-processes of the other.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243887/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For ST2I, the method takes a free-form text prompt (up to 77 CLIP tokens) and produces a 256×256 spatial-faithful image decoded from VQ-VAE latent codes (32×32 tokens from a 2886-entry codebook). For SI2T, it takes an RGB image and produces a spatial description decoded by a GPT-2 language model. Both tasks share an intermediate 3D scene graph (3DSG) representation that encodes objects, attributes, and 3D spatial relationships as a structured graph, which is generated by a graph diffusion model from either a visual scene graph (parsed from the image) or a textual scene graph (parsed from the prompt).

## Method

SD³ comprises three discrete diffusion models operating on quantized representations: a shared graph diffusion (using a Discrete Graph Auto-Encoder) that evolves a 2D scene graph into a 3DSG, an image diffusion (VQ-VAE latent space) for ST2I, and a text diffusion (word-embedding space) for SI2T. The key architectural insight is dual intermediate sharing: during training, the intermediate latent features of the easier '3D→Image' and '3D→Text' diffusion steps are fed as conditioning signals into the harder 'Image→3D' and 'Text→3D' graph diffusion steps via KL-alignment losses, so the two tasks mutually reinforce 3D scene estimation. A probabilistic duality regularization (derived from dual supervised learning) enforces consistency between the ST2I and SI2T marginal-conditional factorizations. Training follows a four-stage curriculum: (1) DGAE pre-training on gold 3DSG data, (2) spatial alignment of image/text decoders with 3DSG-augmented reconstruction losses, (3) 2DSG→3DSG graph diffusion training on aligned image-text-3DSG triplets, and (4) joint dual learning of all three diffusion models. At inference, either task can run standalone since the graph diffusion has already learned robust 3D estimation from the prior stages.

## Results

On the VSDv2 benchmark (256×256, 200 DDIM steps), SD³ achieves FID 10.09, IS 29.76, and CLIP 71.10 for ST2I, and BLEU4 27.63 and SPICE 48.03 for SI2T, outperforming all baselines. On VSDv1, SD³ reaches FID 11.04, IS 29.20, CLIP 68.31 (ST2I) and BLEU4 56.23, SPICE 68.02 (SI2T). In human evaluation, SD³ scores 4.03/5 on ST2I spatial accuracy and 3.86/5 on SI2T spatial accuracy, compared to 3.04 (Frido) and 3.03 (3DVSD) respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

For ST2I, SD³ outperforms the strongest T2I baseline Frido [15] by 1.82 FID, 3.28 IS, and 3.66 CLIP on VSDv1, and by 1.32 FID, 3.74 IS, and 4.09 CLIP on VSDv2. It also surpasses VQ-Diffusion [28] by 7.30 FID on VSDv1 and 5.57 FID on VSDv2, and DALLE [62] by over 21 FID on both versions. For SI2T, SD³ beats 3DVSD [97] by 1.38 BLEU4 on VSDv1 (56.23 vs. 54.85) and by 1.23 BLEU4 and 1.06 SPICE on VSDv2 (27.63/48.03 vs. 26.40/46.97), though it trails 3DVSD by 0.74 SPICE on VSDv1 (68.02 vs. 68.76). Against DDCap [99], the best discrete-diffusion captioning baseline, SD³ leads by 18.30 BLEU4 on VSDv1 and 4.49 BLEU4 on VSDv2. In human evaluation, SD³ achieves 4.03/5 ST2I spatial accuracy vs. 3.04 for Frido, and 3.86/5 SI2T spatial accuracy vs. 3.03 for 3DVSD, confirming consistent gains across both automatic and perceptual metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies spatial image-to-text (SI2T) and spatial text-to-image (ST2I) under a dual learning framework, drawing on dual supervised learning (Xia et al., ICML 2017) for the probabilistic duality constraint and discrete diffusion (Austin et al., NeurIPS 2021) as the generative backbone for all three components (graph, image, text). The 3D scene graph (3DSG) representation, encoded via a discrete graph auto-encoder (DGAE, Nguyen et al. 2024), serves as a shared intermediate that bridges 2D visual/textual scene graphs to 3D spatial semantics. The key departure from prior work is the mutual intermediate-feature sharing: the 'easy' 3D→X diffusion steps feed their latent states back into the 'hard' X→3D graph diffusion, a mechanism not present in standalone SI2T or ST2I pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to jointly optimize SI2T and ST2I through a shared 3D spatial representation, demonstrating that the two tasks can mutually reinforce each other's 3D feature estimation. However, the approach is heavily dependent on the quality of gold 3DSG training data (the authors show sharp performance degradation under noise), and evaluation is confined to a single benchmark (VSD) with modest scale (~30K pairs). Open questions include whether the 3DSG bottleneck can be alleviated via data augmentation or self-supervised 3D estimation, and whether the dual discrete-diffusion framework generalizes to richer 3D scenes or scales to multimodal large language model settings the authors mention as future direction.
