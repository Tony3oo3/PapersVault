## Problem

Existing protein backbone generative models (RFDiffusion, FrameDiff, FOLDFLOW) operate purely on 3D structure and cannot condition on amino acid sequences, limiting their utility for biologically relevant design tasks such as folding, motif scaffolding, and target-specific de novo design. The central question is how to leverage the rich biological inductive bias encoded in sequences—available at a scale far beyond the PDB—to inform conditional protein structure generation within a geometrically principled SE(3) framework.

## Input / Output

The model takes two modalities: a discrete amino acid sequence (N tokens over 20 residues, encoded by a frozen ESM2-650M language model) and an SE(3) structure input (N rigid frames, each a rotation-translation pair for 4 heavy atoms) that can be pure noise, a partial structure, or a fully specified motif. The sequence is randomly masked at 50% probability during training, enabling the same model to perform unconditional generation (fully masked), folding (unmasked), or in-painting/scaffolding (partially masked). Output is a protein backbone as N rigid frames in SE(3)_0^N (center-of-mass removed for translation invariance), produced by integrating a learned vector field via Euler steps (50 steps with inference annealing).

## Method

FOLDFLOW-2 is an SE(3)^N-invariant flow matching model with a three-part architecture: (1) a structure encoder using SE(3)-equivariant Invariant Point Attention (IPA) transformers alongside a frozen ESM2-650M sequence encoder, both producing single and pair representations; (2) a multi-modal fusion trunk that projects and concatenates the two modalities' representations, then refines them through Folding blocks (triangular self-attention); and (3) an IPA-based geometric decoder with a critical skip connection from the structure encoder that outputs per-residue SO(3) and R^3 vector fields. Training uses a flow matching objective with minibatch optimal transport coupling, decomposed into rotation and translation losses, with 50% sequence masking to jointly learn conditional and unconditional generation. The model is trained on a 160K-structure dataset (~8× prior work) combining PDB with a three-step filtered AlphaFold2/SwissProt synthetic set (global pLDDT threshold, per-residue masking, and a learned quality classifier). A Reinforced Fine-Tuning (ReFT) procedure further aligns generations to auxiliary rewards (e.g., secondary structure diversity) by filtering a curated dataset with the reward and performing supervised fine-tuning on the high-scoring subset.

## Results

On unconditional protein backbone generation, FOLDFLOW-2 achieves 97.6% designability (scRMSD < 2.0 Å), 36.8% novelty (fraction of designable proteins with TM-score < 0.3), 0.205 average pairwise TM-score, and 0.348 MaxCluster fraction—outperforming all baselines on every metric. On protein folding, FOLDFLOW-2 achieves 3.24 Å RMSD on 268 unseen PDB proteins, approaching ESMFold (2.32 Å) and outperforming MultiFlow (15.00 Å) by ~4×. On the challenging VHH nanobody CDR scaffolding task, fine-tuned FOLDFLOW-2 solves 9/25 refoldable motifs versus 5/25 for RFDiffusion, with lower motif (2.78 vs 3.94 Å) and scaffold (1.67 vs 2.40 Å) scRMSD.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FOLDFLOW-2 outperforms RFDiffusion (the prior gold standard) on novelty by an absolute 25.2 percentage points (36.8% vs 11.6% TM<0.3), on pairwise TM-score by 19.9% relative improvement (0.205 vs 0.256), and on MaxCluster by 102.3% relative improvement (0.348 vs 0.172), while matching its designability (97.6% vs 96.9%). Against FOLDFLOW (its direct predecessor), FOLDFLOW-2 improves designability by 15.6 points and novelty by 18.0 points. On folding, FOLDFLOW-2 (3.24 Å) approaches purpose-built ESMFold (2.32 Å) but is ~4.6× better than MultiFlow (15.00 Å), the most comparable sequence-conditioned flow matching model. On motif scaffolding, FOLDFLOW-2(+FT) solves all 24/24 benchmark motifs (matching RFDiffusion) with higher diversity (0.445 vs 0.345) and lower scaffold RMSD (1.67 vs 2.40 Å), and outperforms FrameFlow(+FT) which solves only 21/24. On VHH nanobody scaffolding, FOLDFLOW-2(+FT) solves 9/25 refoldable motifs versus 5/25 for RFDiffusion. On zero-shot conformation sampling, FOLDFLOW-2 is competitive with ESMFlow-MD (pairwise RMSD r: 0.297 vs 0.293; PCA W2: 10.85 vs 11.51) but lags behind AlphaFlow-MD (pairwise RMSD r: 0.297 vs 0.468; PCA W2: 10.85 vs 10.67), while requiring 4.5× less GPU training time and 33× fewer trainable parameters than ESMFlow-MD.

## Relation to Prior Work

FOLDFLOW-2 extends the FOLDFLOW line of SE(3)-equivariant flow matching models by introducing sequence conditioning through a frozen ESM2 protein language model, a multi-modal fusion trunk combining structure and sequence representations, and a geometric transformer decoder with critical skip-connections. The closest prior work is MultiFlow (Campbell et al., 2024), which also leverages sequences for co-generation, but FOLDFLOW-2 departs by using a large pre-trained protein LLM rather than learning sequence representations jointly, and by employing a masked-sequence training strategy that unifies unconditional generation, folding, and in-painting in a single model. The ReFT fine-tuning objective draws on RLHF-style alignment but replaces reward-model-based RL with filtered supervised fine-tuning, and the dataset construction (filtered AlphaFold2 structures from SwissProt) represents a new scaling strategy beyond the PDB.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FOLDFLOW-2 establishes a single model that handles unconditional generation, protein folding, motif scaffolding, and zero-shot conformation sampling, which is practically significant for de novo drug design pipelines where conditional generation around target motifs is the core challenge. The VHH nanobody scaffolding result (9/25 vs. 5/25 for RFDiffusion) demonstrates progress on a therapeutically relevant task. Key limitations include the dependence on a competitive pre-trained language model, the potential mismatch between ProteinMPNN's PDB-only training and the synthetic-augmented dataset, and the small benchmark sizes for conditional tasks. Open questions include whether the ReFT framework generalizes to more complex multi-objective rewards and whether the zero-shot conformation sampling capability transfers to proteins with large conformational changes not represented in PDB.
