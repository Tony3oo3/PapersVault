## Problem

Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) LLMs face critical system-level inefficiencies during inference: layer-wise routers prevent pre-scheduling of expert loading and complicate token batching, while the conventional approach of training MoEs from scratch is prohibitively expensive. The paper identifies that layer-wise expert selections across adjacent layers are highly correlated (near-deterministic), making per-layer routing redundant, and that creating a *smaller* MoE from a larger pre-trained dense model (as opposed to upcycling smaller models into a larger MoE) remains underexplored.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pre-trained dense LLM checkpoint (Llama-2-7B, 6.9B parameters) and domain-labeled training data (RedPajama split into 7 sub-domains: Arxiv, Books, C4, Common Crawl, Github, StackExchange, Wikipedia) to produce a refactored MoE model with 4.7B activated and 17B total parameters, plus a single 18M-parameter autoregressive router. At inference, the router pre-computes the full routing path for an entire token sequence before any MoE layer executes, enabling the serving system to schedule expert loading, batching, and caching ahead of time rather than reacting to per-layer routing decisions.

## Method

Read-ME constructs domain-specialized experts by structured pruning of the dense model's FFN layers: for each data domain, it selects the top-activated neurons (via average channel magnitude) to form aligned expert subnetworks across all layers, plus a permanent expert for universally important channels. A single autoregressive transformer block (1 layer, 4 heads, 18M params) replaces all per-layer routers, computing gating weights for every MoE layer simultaneously from the full token context, which exploits the observed redundancy in layer-wise routing. Training uses an iterative 8-round schedule alternating router tuning (with a KL-divergence routing distillation loss that aligns router decisions to the dense model's activation sparsity) and expert tuning (causal LM loss), consuming only 1.04B tokens total. The pre-gating design enables two system-level optimizations: an expert-aware batching algorithm that groups tokens sharing the same expert to minimize unique expert activations per batch (reducing from ~5.1 to 3.51), and a Belady-inspired cache replacement policy that evicts the expert whose next use is farthest in the future across all queued requests.

## Results

Read-ME (4.7B activated / 17B total params, trained on only 1B tokens) achieves 38.9% on MMLU and 55.5% average across seven benchmarks, outperforming all smaller open-source models (Sheared-Llama 53.2%, Pythia-2.8B 48.3%, Open-Llama-v2-3.4B 49.3%) and the MoE baseline OpenMoE (26.2% MMLU). On the system side, expert-aware batching reduces mean end-to-end latency by 5.0–6.1% and p95 latency by 9.5–10.0% compared to decoding- and prefill-prioritized batching, while the Belady-inspired caching policy achieves 44.16% cache hit ratio at capacity 2 versus 33.90% for LRU. Refactoring the dense Llama-2-7B MLP into MoE yields a net 19% per-token latency reduction, and prefetching enabled by pre-gating delivers up to 30% lower end-to-end latency over on-demand loading.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against smaller dense models, Read-ME (4.7B activated) decisively outperforms Sheared-Llama (2.7B, 53.2% avg) by 2.3 points and Pythia-2.8B (48.3%) by 7.2 points on average, while using 50× fewer training tokens than Sheared-Llama. Compared to the MoE baseline OpenMoE (2.1B–8B, 1.1T tokens), Read-ME improves MMLU from 26.2% to 38.9% with 1000× less training. At the 6.9B scale, Read-ME trails the original Llama-2-7B (61.4% avg, 45.3% MMLU) by 5.9 points on average, but surpasses Open-Llama-v2-6.9B (52.0% avg) by 3.5 points and Pythia-6.9B (49.2%) by 6.3 points, all while activating only 4.7B parameters. Among compression techniques (LLM-Pruner, SliceGPT, LaCo, Compresso), Read-ME achieves the best MMLU score for models with fewer than 5B activated parameters (Figure 4). On the system side, expert-aware batching reduces mean latency by 5.0–6.1% and p95 by 9.5–10.0% over both DeepSpeed's decoding-prioritized and PagedAttention's prefill-prioritized batching, primarily by cutting average unique experts per batch from ~5.1 to 3.51. The Belady-inspired cache policy outperforms LRU by 10.3 points at capacity 2 (44.16% vs 33.90% hit ratio) and by 5.2 points at capacity 5 (88.03% vs 83.48%), confirming that global cross-request scheduling enabled by pre-gating is critical for multi-request serving.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the 'upcycling' paradigm (which scales dense models *up* into larger MoEs) by tackling the inverse problem: refactoring a pre-trained dense LLM into a *smaller* MoE with fewer activated parameters. It builds on activation-sparsity-based expert construction (Deja Vu, HiRE) and MoE-fication methods, but critically identifies a design redundancy in the ubiquitous layer-wise router architecture used by Mixtral, OpenMoE, and similar systems, replacing it with a single pre-gating router decoupled from the backbone. On the systems side, it extends LLM serving techniques (Orca-style batching, vLLM paged attention) and expert offloading/caching (Mixtral-offloading, MoE-Infinity) by showing that pre-gating enables Belady-optimal cache replacement and expert-aware batching—optimizations that are provably infeasible under layer-wise routing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The pre-gating router is a clean architectural insight that unlocks system-level optimizations (optimal caching, lookahead scheduling, expert-aware batching) previously thought to require speculative or heuristic approaches, with only 18M additional parameters. The 1B-token training cost to match or exceed models trained on 300B–1T tokens makes MoE conversion practically accessible. Key limitations include single-accelerator serving (distributed extension is open), dependence on domain metadata for expert construction (though the Mistral ablation shows robustness without it), and moderate absolute latency gains (~6% mean, ~10% tail) that may narrow at larger scales. Open questions include whether the router redundancy finding generalizes to much larger MoEs (e.g., 100B+ total parameters) and how pre-gating interacts with speculative decoding or multi-node serving.
