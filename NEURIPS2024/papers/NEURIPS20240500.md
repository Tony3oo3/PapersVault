## Problem

Existing gaze following methods process only one person at a time, operate on single static frames, and treat social gaze inference (looking-at-humans, looking-at-each-other, shared attention) as ad-hoc post-processing rather than a jointly learned task. No prior framework simultaneously predicts gaze targets and social gaze labels for all people in a scene while leveraging temporal dynamics, leaving open whether joint multi-task, multi-person, temporal modeling can improve performance over specialized single-task approaches.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240500/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a sequence of T=5 RGB frames at 224×224 (temporal stride 3), along with pre-extracted head bounding box tracks and 224×224 head crops for each of up to 4 people per scene. Outputs are per-person gaze heatmaps at full resolution, binary in-out-of-frame gaze labels, and pairwise social gaze scores (LAH, LAEO, SA) for every person pair in every frame. The gaze heatmap decoder is person-conditioned via a Hadamard product between frame feature maps and projected person tokens, producing distinct heatmaps from shared ViT outputs without separate inference passes.

## Method

The architecture treats each person as a token that interacts with scene (frame) tokens through a three-module pipeline: a Person Module that encodes head crops via a ResNet18 gaze backbone and a temporal transformer to capture gaze dynamics; an Interaction Module of B=4 blocks that alternates person-to-scene and scene-to-person cross-attention (inspired by ViT-Adapter) with spatio-temporal social interaction via self-attention over person tokens; and a Prediction Module that uses a person-conditioned DPT decoder (Hadamard product of frame features with projected person tokens at each scale) for gaze heatmaps and 3-layer MLP decoders over concatenated multi-scale person token pairs for social gaze scores. Training follows a two-stage protocol: static pre-training on GazeFollow (20 epochs, lr 1e-4), then temporal multi-task fine-tuning on the newly constructed VSGaze dataset (20 epochs, lr 3e-6, frozen ViT) with combined losses (heatmap MSE, gaze vector cosine, social gaze BCE, in-out BCE). The VSGaze dataset unifies gaze following and social gaze annotations across VAT, ChildPlay, VideoCoAtt, and UCO-LAEO by extending head tracks with YOLOv5+ByteTrack and deriving pairwise LAH/LAEO/SA labels from gaze point geometry.

## Results

On the unified VSGaze benchmark, MTGS achieves a gaze distance of 0.107 (best overall), F1_LAH of 0.795, F1_LAEO of 0.590, and AP_SA of 0.576 (best by a large margin over the next-best 0.335). On GazeFollow, the static variant achieves AUC 0.929 and Min.Dist 0.059, outperforming the only other multi-person method (Jin 2021: AUC 0.919, Min.Dist 0.076). On UCO-LAEO, MTGS sets a new state of the art with AP_LAEO of 0.963, far exceeding dedicated LAEO methods (best prior: 0.867). The model also achieves the best multi-person distance on VAT (0.105) and ChildPlay (0.117).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MTGS outperforms all 12 baselines on at least one primary metric. Against the closest multi-person competitor Jin (22, FG 2021), MTGS wins on every metric: distance 0.105 vs 0.134 on VAT, AUC 0.929 vs 0.919 on GazeFollow, and AP_LAEO 0.963 vs not reported. Against single-person SoTA methods (Fang 14, Tonini 50, Jin 23, Bao 4, Hu 21, Tafasca 48), MTGS achieves competitive or better distance while processing all people in a single forward pass. On the LAEO task, MTGS (AP_LAEO 0.963) substantially surpasses dedicated LAEO methods: Marin-Jimenez (31, 0.795), Doosti (11, 0.762), and Marin-Jimenez (30, 0.867). The only metric where MTGS does not lead is AP_IO on VAT (0.869 vs Chong_T's 0.853 and Gupta's 0.864), where it is competitive but not dominant. Notably, the SA decoder (AP_SA 0.576) dramatically outperforms post-processing of gaze points (0.352), highlighting the value of explicit social gaze modeling over ad-hoc geometric heuristics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the multi-person gaze following line initiated by Tafasca et al. (Sharingan, CVPR 2024), which itself adapted the DPT dense-prediction decoder to produce per-person heatmaps from a shared ViT backbone. The key departure is the introduction of person-specific tokens that interact with scene tokens via cross-attention, enabling joint prediction of gaze targets and pairwise social gaze labels (LAH, LAEO, SA) in a single forward pass, rather than treating social gaze as ad-hoc post-processing of gaze heatmaps. The temporal dimension is handled through a dedicated temporal gaze encoder and spatio-temporal social interaction blocks, going beyond the static single-frame formulations that dominate prior gaze following work. The VSGaze dataset unifies annotation types across four previously separate datasets, addressing the fragmentation that prevented joint multi-task training in prior studies.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that joint training of gaze following and social gaze prediction is mutually beneficial—adding social gaze losses improves gaze following distance, and removing gaze following losses degrades social gaze F1—providing empirical evidence for a unified formulation that prior work treated as separate problems. The finding that distance-based gaze following metrics do not reliably predict social gaze performance (e.g., ChongS has better distance than ChongT but worse SA) motivates the proposed pair-wise evaluation protocol and suggests the field needs semantic metrics beyond pixel distance. Open questions include why temporal information yields only marginal gains (especially for SA), how to resolve the 13% misalignment between gaze heatmap argmax and social gaze decoder predictions, and whether the framework scales to larger scenes or more diverse social behaviors. The VSGaze dataset and its unified annotation protocol are practically valuable for enabling multi-task gaze research.
