## Problem

Existing PDE solvers—both classical numerical methods and data-driven neural operators—require complete observations of coefficients or solutions to produce predictions, yet real-world measurements are typically sparse (1–3% of the domain). This paper addresses the gap of simultaneously solving forward and inverse PDE problems under highly partial observations, where missing data must be filled in and the governing equation must be satisfied at the same time.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20244139/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts sparse point measurements (as few as 500 out of 128×128 grid points, ~3%) on the coefficient field a, the solution field u, or both, along with the known PDE residual function f. It outputs the full coefficient field and full solution field simultaneously from a single pre-trained network, handling arbitrary observation densities and spatial patterns without retraining. For the 1D Burgers' equation, the output extends to the full time-series u_{0:T} (128 spatial × 128 temporal points) reconstructed from just 5 fixed sensors.

## Method

DiffusionPDE trains an EDM-based denoiser on the joint distribution of the coefficient space A and solution space U (concatenated along the channel dimension), learning a single generative prior over both spaces. At inference, it samples from this joint distribution via a deterministic ODE denoising process guided by two physics-informed terms: an observation loss that pulls the denoised estimate toward the sparse measurements, and a PDE loss that enforces the governing equation residual. The guidance is scheduled so that observation loss dominates the first 80% of denoising steps, after which the PDE loss takes primary control to refine the solution's physical consistency. This unified formulation means one network and one algorithm handle forward, inverse, and joint recovery from observations on either side, without separate operator networks or retraining for different sparsity patterns.

## Results

On sparse observations (~3% of grid points), DiffusionPDE achieves relative errors of 2.5–22.6% across all six PDE families in both forward and inverse directions, while all deterministic baselines (FNO, PINO, DeepONet, PINNs) produce errors of 31–232%. The strongest results are on Darcy Flow (2.5% forward, 3.2% inverse) and bounded Navier-Stokes (3.9% forward, 2.7% inverse). For the 1D Burgers' equation with only 5 sensors across all time steps, DiffusionPDE recovers the full time-series solution at 2.68% relative error, far outperforming baselines trained on neighboring snapshot pairs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiffusionPDE decisively outperforms all four deterministic operator-learning baselines (FNO, PINO, DeepONet, PINNs) on every PDE family in both forward and inverse directions under sparse observations, with errors typically 10–50× lower (e.g., 2.5% vs. 28.2% on Darcy Flow forward; 4.5% vs. 100.9% on Poisson forward). Against GraphPDE on the bounded Navier-Stokes inverse problem, DiffusionPDE reduces relative error from 12.0% to 2.7% with only 1% observed points. For the unbounded Navier-Stokes equation, DiffusionPDE (6.9% forward, 10.4% inverse) substantially outperforms the physics-informed diffusion approach of Shu et al. (~13% final-state error) and the transformer-based OFormer (17–23% error). The RBF kernel baseline achieves 14–23% forward errors but degrades to 34–143% on inverse problems, confirming that simple interpolation methods cannot handle the inverse setting. Notably, with full observations DiffusionPDE is competitive with PINO and FNO (e.g., 2.2% vs. 4.0% on Darcy Flow forward), showing that the generative framework does not sacrifice accuracy when data is complete.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the diffusion posterior sampling (DPS) framework by adding a PDE residual loss as a second guidance term alongside the observation loss, enabling simultaneous reconstruction of both coefficient and solution fields. It builds on EDM's denoiser architecture and the score-based SDE formulation for the underlying generative model, but departs from prior PDE solvers (FNO, PINNs, DeepONet, PINO) that learn deterministic input-to-output mappings requiring full observations. The key conceptual departure from GraphPDE, the closest prior work on inverse PDE with sparse sensors, is the use of a high-capacity diffusion model trained on the joint distribution of (a, u) rather than a GNN-based latent prior, and the unification of forward and inverse solving with a single network that handles arbitrary sparsity patterns without retraining.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap: real-world physical measurements are sparse (1–3% of the domain), yet all prior learning-based PDE solvers assume complete observations and fail catastrophically (100%+ error) when this assumption is violated. The demonstration that a single generative model can solve both forward and inverse problems across six PDE families with arbitrary observation patterns is a step toward deployable PDE solvers for real sensing scenarios. Key limitations include slow inference (~140s per sample), restriction to 2D spatial domains and single time-slice pairs for dynamic PDEs, and degraded accuracy in unconstrained coefficient spaces (e.g., Poisson/Helmholtz inverse at ~20% error). Open questions include extending to full time-interval recovery for 2D/3D dynamic PDEs, accelerating sampling, and handling higher-dimensional or more complex geometries.
