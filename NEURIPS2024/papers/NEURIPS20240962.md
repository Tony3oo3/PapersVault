## Problem

Prompt-based federated learning with vision-language models (e.g., CLIP) has shown strong empirical results, yet lacks a theoretical framework to explain why it works, how to evaluate its performance, or how to optimally balance global and local prompts under severe data heterogeneity. This paper fills that gap by constructing an analytical framework grounded in feature learning theory and deriving provable conditions under which combining prompts improves generalization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240962/cropped_page6_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images as input, encoded by a frozen CLIP image encoder into a shared latent space, and produces class labels by selecting the class whose text feature (generated from a learnable prompt plus a fixed class prompt through the frozen CLIP text encoder) has the highest cosine similarity to the image feature. Only the low-dimensional prompt vectors are trained; the entire pretrained vision-language backbone remains frozen, keeping communication and computation costs minimal in the federated setting.

## Method

The paper introduces PromptFolio, which maintains two learnable prompts per client—a global prompt aggregated via FedAvg across all clients and a local prompt trained independently—and combines their text features with a mixing coefficient θ at inference time. The key theoretical contribution is a feature-learning-theory analysis that decomposes each prompt into task-relevant and task-irrelevant feature coefficients, showing that test performance is governed by the ratio of these coefficients. By drawing an analogy to Markowitz portfolio optimization (task-relevant coefficients as return, task-irrelevant as risk), the authors prove that the mixed prompt achieves lower test loss than any convex combination of the individual prompts' losses, and derive a closed-form optimal θ that increases with data heterogeneity (lower inter-client feature similarity χ_k) and decreases with the number of clients.

## Results

On CIFAR-100, PromptFolio achieves 80.17% accuracy, outperforming PromptFL (78.16%) and CoOp (76.88%). Across five datasets (CIFAR-100, DomainNet, Office-Caltech10, OxfordPets, DTD), PromptFolio consistently achieves the highest accuracy, with the largest gains on OxfordPets (+3.26% over PromptFL) and DTD (+0.33% over PromptFL). The optimal mixing coefficient θ is found to be approximately 0.2 across datasets and shot settings, indicating a slight preference for the global prompt.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PromptFolio outperforms both CoOp and PromptFL on all five main-body datasets, with the most substantial gain on OxfordPets (92.17% vs. 88.91% for PromptFL and 87.85% for CoOp). In the extended appendix comparison (Table 2), PromptFolio also beats FedTPG (86.50% vs. 86.59% on Food101, essentially tied; 61.04% vs. 52.01% on DTD) and all PromptFL variants (PromptFL+FT, +FedProx, +FedPer, +FedAMP). Notably, PromptFolio's advantage over PromptFL is most pronounced under severe non-IID conditions (Dirichlet α=0.01), where the local prompt contributes more signal. On Office-Caltech10, PromptFolio (97.24%) slightly edges out CoOp (97.10%) while PromptFL underperforms both (95.51%), suggesting that when data is relatively homogeneous, the global prompt dominates and the mixing coefficient should be small.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the empirical line of work on prompt-based federated learning (PromptFL, CoOp, pFedPrompt, FedOTP) by providing the first theoretical analysis framework grounded in feature learning theory. It departs from prior federated prompt learning studies, which are almost entirely empirical, by decomposing learnable prompts into task-relevant and task-irrelevant feature coefficients and tracking their dynamics through a two-stage analysis. The PromptFolio method itself is a simple linear mixing of global and local prompts, but its justification is novel: the authors draw an explicit analogy to Markowitz portfolio optimization, treating task-relevant coefficients as 'income' and task-irrelevant coefficients as 'risk,' thereby importing the diversification principle into the prompt-learning setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The derived optimal mixing coefficient θ* gives practitioners a principled, data-dependent rule for balancing generalization and personalization in federated prompt learning, replacing the current practice of manual hyperparameter tuning. The feature-learning-theory framework also provides a general lens for analyzing other federated fine-tuning strategies beyond prompts. Key limitations include the binary-classification assumption in the theory, the simplified single-activation text encoder model, and the orthogonality assumption on pretrained features (supported empirically but not guaranteed). Open questions include whether the framework extends to multi-class settings, deeper prompt structures (e.g., multi-token or contextual prompts), and whether the portfolio analogy holds under more realistic non-orthogonal feature geometries.
