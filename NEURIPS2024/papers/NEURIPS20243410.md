## Problem

Gloss-free sign language translation methods significantly underperform gloss-based approaches because visually similar but semantically distinct sign gestures are densely packed in feature space, making it difficult for models to learn discriminative boundaries without explicit gloss supervision. This paper identifies this 'representation density problem' as a systematic bottleneck across existing feature extraction methods and demonstrates that higher density directly correlates with worse recognition and translation accuracy.

## Input / Output

The method consumes continuous sign language video (e.g., 224×224 RGB frames) and outputs a translated text sentence, operating entirely without gloss annotations. The video is processed through a ResNet backbone followed by 1D convolutions and a transformer encoder to produce frame-level features, which are then decoded via a transformer decoder into text tokens. SignCL operates on these frame-level features, treating temporally adjacent frames as positive pairs and temporally distant frames as negative pairs, with the margin dynamically scaled by the video-to-text length ratio.

## Method

SignCL is a margin-based contrastive learning loss that pulls representations of frames within the same gesture closer together and pushes representations of frames from different gestures apart, using a simple temporal proximity rule: adjacent frames (|Δf| ≤ 1) form positive pairs, while frames separated by more than 2×margin form negative pairs. The margin is dynamically estimated as max(10, len(frames)/len(text) × 2.3), grounded in Zipf's Law for speech-to-gesture correspondence. The loss (Eqn. 5) uses a fixed margin m=64 to prevent negative pairs from being pushed excessively far, and is integrated into the training objective as a weighted term (λ=0.01) alongside the original VLP or SLT loss. SignCL can be applied during the visual-language pretraining stage, the downstream finetuning stage, or both, without adding any model parameters, and is compatible with existing transformer-based SLT frameworks such as NSLT and GFSLT-VLP.

## Results

On the CSL-Daily dataset, integrating SignCL into both pretraining and finetuning of GFSLT-VLP achieves a BLEU-4 score of 16.16, a 45.58% relative improvement over the GFSLT-VLP baseline (11.10), while reducing the Sign Density Ratio (SDR) from 58.20 to 54.61. On PHOENIX-2014T, the same approach yields a BLEU-4 of 22.74 versus 21.25 for the baseline (+7.0%). Notably, SignCL with ~600M parameters outperforms Sign2GPT (~1.7B parameters) on CSL-Daily (B@4: 16.16 vs. 15.40) using only 35% of the parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SignCL consistently outperforms all compared gloss-free baselines on both datasets. On CSL-Daily, it surpasses GFSLT-VLP (B@4: 16.16 vs. 11.10, +45.6%), GASLT (16.16 vs. 4.07), and NSLT (16.16 vs. 7.56) by large margins. Against the strongest baseline Sign2GPT (B@4: 15.40), SignCL achieves a 0.76 BLEU-4 advantage while using only ~600M parameters versus Sign2GPT's ~1.7B. On PHOENIX-2014T, SignCL improves over GFSLT-VLP from 21.25 to 22.74 BLEU-4 and also edges out Sign2GPT (22.74 vs. 22.52). For gloss-based feature extraction, SignCL applied to SMKD features reduces SDR from 66.23 to 62.18 and improves BLEU-4 from 22.79 to 23.46, confirming that the contrastive strategy benefits both gloss-based and gloss-free pipelines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the gloss-free sign language translation (SLT) line of work, specifically the GFSLT-VLP framework (Zhou et al., ICCV 2023) and the Sign Language Transformer (Camgoz et al., CVPR 2020), by introducing a contrastive learning objective (SignCL) that operates on visual gesture duration as the unit of contrast. It departs from prior contrastive learning in SLT—which has focused on signer-independence, token-level text representations, or video-text alignment—by targeting the visual encoder's feature space directly and framing the problem as one of representation density. The empirical contribution of formally defining and measuring the 'representation density problem' via a Sign Density Ratio (SDR) metric is novel within the SLT literature, though it draws conceptually on Fisher's Discriminant Ratio from classification.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a useful diagnostic (SDR) and a simple, parameter-free remedy (SignCL) for a bottleneck that has kept gloss-free SLT far behind gloss-based approaches, potentially guiding future work on feature discriminability in sign language. However, absolute BLEU scores remain low (e.g., 2.53 on CSL-Daily), and the method's assumption that adjacent frames share semantics may break down for repetitive or extended gestures. Open questions include whether the representation density framing generalizes to other visual-language tasks with fine-grained temporal distinctions, and whether accounting for semantic similarity between gestures (rather than treating all non-adjacent frames as negatives) could further improve results.
