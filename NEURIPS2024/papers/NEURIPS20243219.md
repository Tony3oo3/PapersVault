## Problem

Robust essential matrix estimation from point matches with very high outlier fractions (often >95%) has been addressed by deep learning methods that rely on complex architectures involving local/global graphs, transformer attention layers, and hard pruning steps. This paper asks whether a simpler, graph-free architecture can match or exceed these methods while also explicitly modeling and removing positional noise in inlier keypoints.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243219/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The network takes as input an unordered set of n 4D point correspondences (x_i = (p_i, q_i, p̃_i, q̃_i)), where each element is a matched keypoint pair from two calibrated images. It produces the 3×3 essential matrix via a differentiable weighted DLT, per-match inlier/outlier probabilities with confidence weights, and per-match 4D displacement vectors that correct inlier keypoint positions. The architecture is permutation-equivariant and invariant to set cardinality (via averaging rather than summing), so it generalizes to varying numbers of matches without retraining.

## Method

NACNet stacks three Noise-Aware Consensus (NAC) blocks, each comprising a Deep Sets encoder (12 permutation-equivariant linear layers with shared weights, SoftPlus activation, layer normalization, and skip connections at dimension 512), a two-layer MLP noise head that predicts 4D displacement vectors to denoise inlier positions, and a two-layer MLP classification head that outputs inlier/outlier scores and per-match weights. The third block's outputs feed a differentiable weighted DLT regression module that computes the essential matrix using softmax-normalized confidence scores. Training uses a two-stage noise-aware scheme: first on noise-free inlier positions (with outliers present) using only classification and epipolar losses, then on the original noisy data with an additional denoising loss that pulls predicted positions toward OTM-derived ground-truth inlier locations. This design eliminates the need for graph construction, attention mechanisms, or post-hoc RANSAC, while the denoising head and pretraining stage together yield consistent gains over both single-stage training and architectures without explicit noise modeling.

## Results

On the YFCC/SIFT benchmark, NACNet achieves 60.10% mAP@5° in-scene and 66.14% cross-scene, outperforming the previous SOTA (BCLNet: 52.62%/66.08%) by 7.5% in-scene. On SUN3D/SIFT, it reaches 34.15% in-scene and 25.36% cross-scene, improving over the prior best (NCMNet: 26.12%/20.66%) by 8.0% and 4.7% respectively. With SuperPoint descriptors, NACNet attains 59.10% cross-scene on YFCC (vs. NCMNet's 52.20%) and 24.67% cross-scene on SUN3D (vs. ConvMatch's 21.71%). Cross-dataset generalization to PhotoTourism yields 60.81% (SIFT) and 49.03% (SuperPoint), both state-of-the-art.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NACNet outperforms all 18 baselines on nearly every evaluation setting. On YFCC/SIFT in-scene, it leads BCLNet (52.62%) and NCMNet (52.33%) by over 7.5 mAP points; on YFCC/SIFT cross-scene it essentially ties BCLNet (66.14% vs. 66.08%) while surpassing MGNet (64.63%) and NCMNet (63.43%). The largest margins appear on SUN3D, where NACNet beats the next-best method by 8.0% in-scene (34.15% vs. NCMNet 26.12%) and 3.6% cross-scene (25.36% vs. ConvMatch 21.71%). With SuperPoint, NACNet improves over NCMNet by 6.9% on YFCC cross-scene (59.10% vs. 52.20%) and over MGNet by 4.3% on SUN3D cross-scene (24.67% vs. 20.65%). The sole exception is YFCC/ORB cross-scene generalization, where MGNet (20.00%) slightly edges NACNet (19.17%). In terms of efficiency, NACNet runs in 11.12 ms per sample—4–6× faster than NCMNet (67.43 ms) and BCLNet (46.94 ms)—while using less GPU memory (130.75 MB vs. 174.52 MB and 140.91 MB respectively).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant trend in deep consensus learning (NCMNet, MGNet, BCLNet, CLNet) that relies on graph message passing, attention/transformer layers, and iterative correspondence pruning. Instead, it adopts the Deep Sets framework (Zaheer et al., 2017) as its core set encoder, combined with simple MLP heads for inlier/outlier classification and a novel noise-regression head for keypoint denoising. The weighted DLT regression module follows the formulation from LFGC (Yi et al., 2018), and the two-stage noise-aware training scheme (pretrain on denoised data, then fine-tune on noisy data) is a new training strategy not present in prior consensus learning methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is substantial: a simpler architecture (no graphs, no attention, no pruning) achieves SOTA accuracy while being 4–6× faster and using less GPU memory than graph-based competitors, making it more deployable in real-time SFM/SLAM pipelines. The finding that iterative correspondence pruning—standard in CLNet, NCMNet, and BCLNet—actually degrades performance here challenges a working assumption in the field. Open questions include whether the approach extends to multi-view SFM integration (acknowledged as future work), whether a degeneracy-aware block could further improve the DLT step, and whether the cross-descriptor generalization gap (slight underperformance with ORB) can be closed.
