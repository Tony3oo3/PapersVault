## Problem

Prior work on structured matrices (Monarch, BTT, Tensor-Train, Kronecker) studied a handful of hand-crafted structures in isolation and in non-compute-optimal settings (controlling for model size or memory), leaving open whether any structure can surpass dense layers when compute is optimally allocated between model size and training data. No unified framework existed to search the full space of structured linear operators or to understand which algebraic properties govern compute-optimal scaling behavior.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240126/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each structured linear layer takes a hidden state vector x ∈ R^{d_in} and produces y ∈ R^{d_out} via a two-factor Einsum whose structure is specified by a 7-dimensional vector θ ∈ [0,1]^7 encoding how input and output dimensions are factored across the two weight tensors. The BTT-MoE variant additionally computes a dense gating vector e = Linear(X) ∈ R^E and selects the top-k entries (k=2) to determine which of E experts contribute to the output. Experiments use GPT-2 on OpenWebText (96-token vocabulary, sequence length 128), autoregressive pixel prediction on CIFAR-5M at 8×8×3, and synthetic MLP regression on R^8.

## Method

The paper introduces a continuous parameterization of all two-factor Einsum linear operators via θ ∈ [0,1]^7, then develops a taxonomy characterized by three scalars: ω (parameter-sharing exponent), ψ (rank exponent), and ν (compute-intensity exponent). Empirically, they show that ω=0 (no parameter sharing) and ψ=1 (full-rank) are necessary and sufficient conditions for a structure to match dense compute-optimal scaling, while ν has negligible effect. Building on this, they propose BTT-MoE: a sparse top-k mixture over the BTT rank dimension ρ, placing an MoE gate in every single linear layer of the transformer (including attention projections W^Q, W^K, W^V, W^O) rather than only over entire FFNs as in standard MoE. They further extend μP theory to derive closed-form initialization and Adam learning-rate scaling rules for arbitrary Einsums by decomposing each into a sequence of batched matrix multiplications, enabling zero-shot hyperparameter transfer across structures and model widths.

## Results

The paper's headline quantitative result is the compute multiplier (λ) of BTT-MoE relative to dense GPT-2: with E=16 experts and k=2, BTT-MoE achieves λ=5.3±0.3, meaning it matches the loss of a dense model trained with 5.3× more FLOPs. Standard MoE with E=16 achieves λ=4.1±0.3 under the same protocol. In the scaling-law analysis (Figures 3–5), full-rank BTT structures (ω=0, ψ=1) match dense scaling laws across GPT-2 language modeling, CIFAR-5M autoregressive pixel generation, and synthetic MLP regression, while structures with parameter sharing (ω>0) or reduced rank (ψ<1) consistently underperform dense.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BTT-MoE outperforms standard MoE (Switch Transformer-style, operating only over FFNs) by a clear margin: λ=5.3 vs. λ=4.1 at E=16, and the gap widens with more experts (Figure 6 right). Against dense GPT-2, BTT-MoE provides over 5× compute savings, while standard MoE provides ~4×. In the scaling-law comparison (Figure 4), Monarch, Tensor-Train, Kronecker product, and low-rank structures all underperform dense in the compute-optimal regime due to parameter sharing (ω>0) or rank deficiency (ψ<1); only full-rank BTT (ω=0, ψ=1) matches dense. Among structured MoE variants (Figure 7), BTT-MoE substantially outperforms both low-rank-MoE and dense-MoE (~2× each), confirming that the base structure must be full-rank, parameter-sharing-free, and sub-quadratic (ν<1) for the MoE sparsification to be effective. On CIFAR-5M autoregressive pixel generation, Einsums with larger embedding dimensions (enabled by sub-quadratic compute) actually outperform dense at small scales, a regime where prior structured-matrix work [6, 19] also showed advantages.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies the fragmented landscape of structured matrix research—Monarch, BTT, Tensor-Train, Kronecker, low-rank—into a single continuous parameterization over all Einsum-expressible linear operators, building directly on the CoLA library and the structure-aware learning rate framework from Qiu et al. (2024). It departs from prior work in two key ways: (1) it evaluates structures under compute-optimal scaling (performance vs. total training FLOPs) rather than the model-size or memory-controlled settings used in earlier structured-matrix papers, and (2) it extends μP initialization and learning-rate scaling to arbitrary Einsums via a BMM decomposition. The BTT-MoE architecture extends the standard sparse MoE paradigm (Shazeer et al., Fedus et al.) from operating over entire FFNs to operating within every individual linear layer, including attention projections.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that no Einsum structure significantly outperforms dense matrices in the compute-optimal regime redirects the field's focus away from searching for a single superior structure and toward the taxonomy variables (ω, ψ) that explain performance differences. The BTT-MoE result—5.3× compute savings over dense with 16 experts—provides a practically deployable architecture that is more fine-grained than standard MoE and could influence how future large-scale models allocate sparsity. Open questions include whether the compute-optimal equivalence between full-rank BTT and dense persists at much larger scales (beyond 76M parameters), whether the structured MoE can be made more hardware-efficient, and whether the taxonomy generalizes to non-transformer architectures or multimodal settings.
