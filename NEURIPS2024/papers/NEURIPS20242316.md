## Problem

Existing generative retrieval (GR) methods rely on standard sequence-to-sequence likelihood objectives that assume binary relevance, where a query is paired with one or more equally relevant docids. This ignores the multi-graded relevance structure present in real-world search, where documents exhibit varying degrees of relevance. Two specific challenges arise: docids of different lengths have incomparable likelihood scores, and semantically similar documents may share identical or near-identical pseudo-query docids, making it impossible for the model to distinguish them across relevance grades.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242316/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a natural-language text query as input and autoregressively generates a ranked list of variable-length pseudo-query docids, constrained at inference time by a prefix trie over the corpus docids (beam size 20). The docids themselves are generated offline via a regularized fusion of a query-generation model and an autoencoder, ensuring each document in the corpus receives a unique identifier. The ranking order is determined by the generation likelihood of each docid conditioned on the query, with the MGCC loss shaping the embedding-space geometry so that higher-grade docids are pulled closer to the query representation.

## Method

GR² addresses multi-graded generative retrieval through two coupled components. First, a regularized fusion approach jointly trains a docid-generation (QG) model and a transformer autoencoder with a shared decoder, adding a relevance term (pulling document and docid representations together) and a distinctness term (pushing different documents and different docids apart in their respective latent spaces); at inference, random perturbations around the document embedding yield diverse pseudo-query docids. Second, the multi-graded constrained contrastive (MGCC) loss operates in embedding space rather than likelihood space: it computes query and docid representations via average-pooled ReLU-transformed hidden states, then applies a grade-weighted contrastive objective where the penalty λ_l = 1/l² scales the pull strength per relevance grade, combined with a hierarchical max-constraint (L_Max) that ensures the loss for any higher-grade pair never exceeds that of a lower-grade pair. The full training objective combines MGCC with standard MLE losses for query-to-docid and document-to-docid mapping, and the framework supports both supervised (GR²S) and pre-train-then-fine-tune (GR²P) paradigms, with the latter using 1.18M pseudo query-docid pairs constructed from English Wikipedia with four relevance grades.

## Results

On the multi-graded relevance Gov 500K dataset, GR²P achieves nDCG@20 of 0.4912 and P@20 of 0.5506, outperforming the SOTA generative retrieval baseline RIPOR (nDCG@20=0.4578, P@20=0.4831) by approximately 14% in relative P@20 improvement. GR²S also surpasses RIPOR by ~11% on P@20 (0.5364 vs. 0.4831). On the binary relevance MS 500K dataset, GR²P achieves Hits@1 of 0.3821 and MRR@20 of 0.3835, outperforming RIPOR (Hits@1=0.3421, MRR@20=0.3626) by 11.7% in Hits@1. GR²P also outperforms BM25 on ClueWeb 500K in low-resource settings, demonstrating robustness even with limited supervision.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GR² (both GR²S and GR²P) outperforms all generative retrieval baselines (DSI-Num, DSI-Sem, DSI-QG, NCI, SEAL, GENRE, Ultron-PQ, LTRGR, GenRRL, GenRet, NOVO, RIPOR) across all three multi-graded relevance datasets and both binary relevance datasets. On Gov 500K, GR²P surpasses RIPOR by 14% relative P@20 (0.5506 vs. 0.4831) and 7.4% relative nDCG@20 (0.4912 vs. 0.4578); GR²S achieves 11% relative P@20 improvement over RIPOR. On binary relevance MS 500K, GR²P outperforms RIPOR by 11.7% on Hits@1 (0.3821 vs. 0.3421) and 5.5% on MRR@20 (0.3835 vs. 0.3626). GR²P also beats the best sparse retrieval baseline QLM on Gov 500K (nDCG@20: 0.4912 vs. 0.4822) and the best dense retrieval baseline PseudoQ on ClueWeb 500K (nDCG@20: 0.2969 vs. 0.2704). However, GR² still trails the full-ranking BM25+monoBERT baseline (nDCG@5: 0.6953 on Gov 500K, MRR@20: 0.5862 on MS 500K), acknowledging that generative retrieval remains in its early stages relative to multi-stage pipelines. In low-resource settings (15–60 queries), GR²P outperforms BM25 on ClueWeb 500K in nDCG@20, while all GR methods underperform BM25 in the zero-resource setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the generative retrieval (GR) paradigm, which encodes an entire corpus into model parameters and autoregressively generates document identifiers, from its standard binary-relevance formulation to multi-graded relevance. It builds on the pseudo-query docid design from docT5query and the shared-decoder architecture from Gao et al. (2019) for its regularized fusion docid generation, while drawing conceptual inspiration from pairwise LTR methods (LambdaRank) to design the MGCC contrastive loss. The key departure from prior GR work (RIPOR, LTRGR, GenRRL) is that those methods either restrict themselves to binary relevance or require complex multi-stage optimization, whereas GR² handles graded relevance in a single unified objective that reduces to supervised contrastive loss in the binary case.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first GR framework to natively incorporate multi-graded relevance, addressing a gap that limits GR's applicability to real-world search where documents have varying degrees of relevance. The unified MGCC loss (which subsumes supervised contrastive learning for binary relevance) provides a clean theoretical bridge between LTR and GR. However, significant open questions remain: the docid design is not jointly optimized with the retrieval model, the method still trails full-ranking pipelines (BM25+monoBERT) by a wide margin, and scalability to billion-document corpora is untested. The paper also flags the need to explore soft grade assignments and the societal implications of scaled GR technology.
