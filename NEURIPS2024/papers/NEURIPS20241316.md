## Problem

Most MLWP models produce only deterministic forecasts, ignoring the inherent uncertainty of the chaotic atmosphere. Existing probabilistic approaches either rely on ad-hoc initial-state perturbations that yield poorly calibrated ensembles, or use diffusion models whose multi-step sampling is prohibitively slow (e.g., 8 minutes per 15-day forecast for GenCast). The paper addresses how to efficiently sample arbitrarily large, spatially coherent, and well-calibrated ensemble forecasts from a single forward pass per time step.

## Input / Output

The model takes two consecutive weather states (83 variables at 1.5° global resolution or 17 variables at 10 km LAM resolution) plus forcing inputs (time of day, orography) and, for limited-area modeling, boundary forcing along the outermost 10 grid positions. It outputs an ensemble of K members (80 global, 100 LAM), each a full spatial field of all forecast variables rolled out autoregressively. The key structural detail is that stochasticity is injected only at the top level of the hierarchy (|V_L| × d_z latent vectors), so a single sample of Z^t per time step per member suffices to generate the full-resolution forecast.

## Method

Graph-EFM combines a hierarchical mesh graph (L levels of icosahedral or quadrilateral grids with inter-level edges) with a latent-variable formulation: a GNN-based latent map predicts the mean of an isotropic Gaussian over Z^t at the top level, and a deterministic predictor propagates a sampled Z^t down through one sweep of the hierarchy to produce the next weather state. Spatial coherence of ensemble members emerges because independent latent components are diffused across the full domain via the GNN layers during the downward pass, rather than being imposed through a complex joint distribution over Z^t. Training uses a VAE-style variational objective (KL + Gaussian NLL) followed by CRPS fine-tuning to correct under-dispersion. The authors also introduce Propagation Networks, a modified GNN layer that defaults to neighbor-averaging when MLPs are zero-initialized, ensuring information reliably flows from the grid up to the latent space and from Z^t back down to the output.

## Results

On global 1.5° ERA5 forecasting, Graph-EFM achieves RMSE of 695 m and CRPS of 299 m for z500 at 10-day lead time, outperforming the deterministic GraphCast* (RMSE 808, CRPS 498) and the SWAG ensemble (RMSE 960, CRPS 590). For 2 m temperature at 10 days, Graph-EFM reaches RMSE 2.32 K and CRPS 1.00 K versus 2.82 K and 1.69 K for GraphCast*. Critically, Graph-EFM achieves a Spread-Skill-Ratio of 1.15 (z500) and 0.99 (2t) at 10 days, indicating well-calibrated ensemble spread, while GraphCast*+SWAG is severely under-dispersed (SpSkR ≈ 0.12). On the 10 km MEPS limited-area task, Graph-EFM matches or beats GraphCast* on CRPS (115 vs 138 for z500 at 57 h) while providing calibrated uncertainty information absent from deterministic baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GraphCast* (a reimplementation of Lam et al. 2023 trained on identical data), Graph-EFM achieves substantially lower CRPS at longer lead times—299 vs 498 m for z500 and 1.00 vs 1.69 K for 2t at 10 days—while also improving RMSE (695 vs 808 m for z500). The ensemble mean of Graph-EFM thus matches or beats the deterministic baseline on point-forecast accuracy while additionally capturing the full forecast distribution. Against GraphCast*+SWAG (a multi-model ensemble via Stochastic Weight Averaging from Maddox et al. 2019), Graph-EFM dominates on every metric: the SWAG ensemble is severely under-dispersed (SpSkR 0.07–0.13 vs 0.99–1.18 for Graph-EFM) and actually degrades RMSE relative to the deterministic GraphCast* (e.g., 960 vs 808 m for z500 at 10 days), indicating that parameter perturbation does not produce physically meaningful ensemble diversity. The ablation Graph-EFM(ms), which replaces the hierarchical graph with a multi-scale graph, shows that the hierarchical construction is essential for spatially coherent samples and proper calibration (SpSkR 0.83 vs 1.15 for z500 at 10 days).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the graph-based MLWP framework established by Keisler [20] and GraphCast [23] by replacing the multi-scale mesh graph with a hierarchical graph (inspired by U-Net and multiscale meshgraphnets [14, 28]) that processes different spatial scales at separate levels with independent parameters. For probabilistic forecasting, it departs from ad-hoc perturbation methods and diffusion-based approaches (GenCast [39], SwinVRNN [19]) by introducing a latent-variable formulation trained with a VAE-style objective, where the latent variable lives at the top of the hierarchy to ensure spatially coherent samples. The paper also introduces Propagation Networks as a targeted modification of Interaction Networks [2] to address information-propagation failures in the GNN layers, and extends the framework to limited-area modeling with boundary forcing—a setting not previously addressed by graph-based MLWP.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The single-forward-pass-per-timestep sampling makes large ensemble forecasts computationally feasible in operational settings, addressing a key bottleneck that has limited MLWP ensemble adoption. The extension to limited-area modeling at 10 km resolution opens a path toward MLWP surrogates for regional operational systems like MEPS, which are critical for energy forecasting and extreme-event warnings. Limitations include residual blurriness in forecasts (trading off visual fidelity for sampling speed), under-dispersion in the LAM setting, and non-trivial training schedules involving λ_KL and λ_CRPS tuning. Open questions include whether other autoencoder formulations or direct scoring-rule optimization could further improve calibration, and whether the hierarchical construction generalizes to even higher resolutions or different geometries.
