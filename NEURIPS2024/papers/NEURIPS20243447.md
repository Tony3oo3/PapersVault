## Problem

Stereotactic EEG (sEEG) recordings from different subjects have highly variable electrode counts and placements determined solely by clinical needs, making it impossible to establish correspondence between neural activity sites across individuals. This heterogeneity has forced prior sEEG decoding work to rely on within-subject models that neither scale with data size nor generalize across subjects, despite evidence from other modalities that multi-subject training substantially improves performance.

## Input / Output

The model consumes bipolar sEEG voltage traces (z-scored, downsampled to 400 Hz) from a 1500 ms post-stimulus window, with the number of electrodes varying per subject (3–28 after high-γ SNR-based selection) and a fixed 105 temporal tokens per electrode. Each electrode's 3D MNI coordinate is provided as a spatial positional signal. The output is a continuous trial-wise response time prediction (in milliseconds), regressed via subject-specific heads from a shared low-dimensional neural representation.

## Method

The architecture (seegnificant) uses a 1D temporal convolutional tokenizer applied independently within each electrode to produce K-dimensional tokens, avoiding cross-electrode blending that would be meaningless given random placement. Self-attention in the time dimension captures long-range temporal dependencies within each electrode, followed by a radial-basis-function positional encoding that imprints each electrode's 3D MNI location onto its tokens. A second self-attention in the electrode dimension then captures cross-electrode spatial dependencies, with masking handling variable electrode counts. The resulting spatiotemporal latents are compressed via an MLP into a shared representation, from which subject-specific regression heads produce per-trial response time predictions. This shared-trunk-plus-personalized-heads design is trained jointly on combined data from 21 subjects (29 sessions, >3600 trials), and the pretrained backbone can be transferred to new subjects via few-shot finetuning of only the subject-specific head.

## Results

On the sEEG color-change detection dataset (21 subjects, 29 sessions, >3600 trials), the multi-subject model achieves a combined test R² of 0.54 ± 0.01 and a per-subject R² of 0.39 ± 0.05, with an RMSE of 82 ± 2 msec against a mean response time of 410 ± 5 msec. Finetuning the multi-subject model to individual subjects further improves per-subject R² to 0.41 ± 0.05 (ΔR² = 0.11 over single-subject models). In a leave-one-out transfer setting, the pretrained model achieves R² = 0.48 ± 0.006 on the held-out subject's data, and after few-shot finetuning reaches per-subject R² = 0.38 ± 0.05, nearly matching the full multi-subject model (ΔR² = −0.01).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares seegnificant against six baselines: Wiener Filter, Ridge Regression, Lasso Regression, Gradient-Boosting (XGB), a 4-layer MLP, and a CNN+MLP, all trained per-subject. In every setting, seegnificant outperforms all baselines: single-subject models exceed baselines by ΔR² ≥ 0.03, the multi-subject model by ΔR² ≥ 0.12, the finetuned multi-subject model by ΔR² ≥ 0.14, and the transfer-learned model by ΔR² ≥ 0.11. The advantage is most pronounced in the multi-subject and transfer settings, where the transformer architecture's ability to handle heterogeneous electrode counts and placements provides a clear edge over fixed-input baselines. The paper also shows that the separated time/space attention architecture outperforms a unified 2D attention variant by ΔR² = 0.06 while training ~5.5× faster (25.7 vs. 141.6 minutes).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the shared-trunk architecture (a global backbone plus task-specific heads) from multi-task learning into a multi-subject learning framework, where the 'tasks' are individual sEEG participants. It draws on transformer-based neural decoding (Azabou et al. 2023 for microelectrodes; Song et al. 2023, Cui et al. 2023 for non-invasive EEG) but departs from both: unlike microelectrode work, sEEG lacks a natural spike-based tokenization, and unlike EEG, sEEG has no fixed electrode grid across subjects. The key architectural novelty is a per-electrode 1D convolutional tokenizer combined with a novel RBF-based spatial positional encoding over MNI coordinates, enabling self-attention to operate over variable-length electrode sequences without requiring anatomical correspondence between subjects.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that multi-subject sEEG decoding is feasible and beneficial despite the extreme heterogeneity in electrode count and placement, opening a path toward scalable, clinically translatable sEEG decoders. The few-shot transfer result (R²≈0.38 with only a new subject's trials) is directly relevant to real-world epilepsy monitoring where only minutes of behavioral data are available per patient. Key limitations include a single behavioral task (reaction time), a modest cohort (21 subjects), and the finding that the spatial positional encoding, while intuitively motivated, does not yield a statistically significant performance gain (p=0.73). Open questions include whether multi-task pretraining and self-supervised objectives on the vast unsupervised sEEG recordings (5–10 days per patient) can further improve generalization.
