## Problem

Existing VLM benchmarks evaluate only a narrow subset of capabilities (typically perception and reasoning) and use inconsistent evaluation protocols, making it impossible to obtain a comprehensive, comparable picture of any model's strengths and weaknesses across dimensions like bias, fairness, multilinguality, robustness, toxicity, and safety. No prior benchmark simultaneously covers all these aspects under a unified, standardized procedure.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20244463/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework consumes 22 VLMs (closed-API and open-weight) and feeds them interleaved image-text prompts drawn from 21 existing datasets, capped at 1,000 instances per scenario (915K total instances, 51.6M input tokens, 915K images per full run). Outputs are structured as per-scenario scores—exact-match accuracy for multiple-choice questions, Prometheus Vision similarity scores (1–5) for open-ended captioning, and Perspective API toxic fraction for safety—aggregated into a mean win-rate leaderboard that ranks each model against all others uniformly at random across every scenario.

## Method

VHELM extends the HELM/HEIM holistic-evaluation framework to vision-language models by defining four evaluation components: aspect (9 evaluative dimensions), scenario (a specific dataset-task-usage-category triple), adaptation (zero-shot prompting only, to mirror layperson usage), and metric (automated: exact match, Prometheus Vision judge, or Perspective API toxicity classifier). To reduce output variance, open-ended questions are reframed as multiple-choice wherever possible, and the Prometheus Vision VLM serves as a calibrated automatic judge for free-form responses. The framework is deliberately lightweight and fully automated—no human annotation is required—so that a complete evaluation of all 22 models across all 21 datasets can be executed cheaply and reproducibly. The key design choice is standardizing inference parameters, prompting format, and scoring across every model, enabling true apple-to-apple comparison that prior fragmented benchmarks could not provide.

## Results

GPT-4o (0513) achieves the highest mean win rate of 0.793 across all 21 scenarios, with a 100% win rate on robustness and top scores in knowledge (A-OKVQA: 0.905), reasoning (MathVista: 0.551), and visual perception (VQAv2: 0.844). However, it trails on bias (PAIRS: 0.873 vs. GPT-4o 0806's 0.954) and toxicity (Hateful Memes: 0.611 vs. IDEFICS 2's 0.622). Closed-API models dominate open-weight ones across nearly all aspects; the worst closed-API model (Gemini 1.0 Pro Vision, win rate 0.478) outperforms the best open-weight model (IDEFICS 2 8B, win rate 0.344) on reasoning, knowledge, and toxicity. Efficiency-focused models show notable gaps: Claude 3.0 Haiku scores only 8.0% on PAIRS (bias) versus 58.7% for Claude 3.0 Opus, and Gemini 1.5 Flash scores 74.0% versus 91.4% for Gemini 1.5 Pro.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among OpenAI models, GPT-4o (0513) leads with a 0.793 mean win rate, while GPT-4o (0806) slightly trails overall (0.766) but surpasses it on bias (95.4% vs. 87.3% on PAIRS) and multilinguality (EXAMS-V: 46.3% vs. 37.1%). GPT-4V and GPT-4 Turbo occupy the mid-tier (0.602 and 0.679 win rates) and are notably weaker on reasoning (MathVista: 48.1% and 49.3%) compared to GPT-4o. Among Google models, Gemini 1.5 Pro (0409) ranks third overall (0.720) and leads on fairness (Crossmodal-3600: 3.600 PV), while Gemini 1.5 Flash models (0.576/0.557) trail their Pro counterparts by ~17 percentage points on bias but lead on robustness (Unicorn: 88.6% vs. 87.1%). Anthropic's Claude 3.5 Sonnet (0.700) is the top Claude model, but the Claude 3.0 family shows dramatic internal gaps: Opus (58.7%) vs. Haiku (8.0%) on PAIRS bias. Palmyra Vision (0.652) sits mid-pack, competitive on visual perception (VQAv2: 81.6%) but weaker on reasoning. Among open-weight models, IDEFICS 2 (8B) (0.344) is the strongest, achieving the best Hateful Memes score (62.2%) and competitive VQAv2 (86.1%), but it lags far behind closed-API models on knowledge and reasoning. PaliGemma (3B) and IDEFICS (80B/9B) show severe instruction-following failures, with near-zero scores on multiple-choice tasks (e.g., IDEFICS 80B scores 0.1% on VQAv2), while Qwen-VL Chat (0.089) is the worst overall performer across nearly all scenarios.

## Relation to Prior Work

VHELM extends the Holistic Evaluation of Language Models (HELM, Liang et al., TMLR 2023) and Holistic Evaluation of Text-to-Image Models (HEIM, Lee et al., NeurIPS 2024) frameworks to vision-language models, applying the same aspect-scenario-adaptation-metric decomposition. Unlike prior VLM benchmarks (VQAv2, MMMU, MathVista, POPE, etc.), which each test a narrow slice of capability on a limited model set with heterogeneous protocols, VHELM aggregates 21 existing datasets under a single standardized evaluation pipeline (zero-shot prompting, fixed inference parameters, automated metrics). The key methodological departure is standardization: by fixing prompting strategy and scoring method across all 22 models, VHELM enables the first true apple-to-apple multi-dimensional comparison that was previously impossible due to protocol variation across studies.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

VHELM provides the first comprehensive, reproducible snapshot of 22 VLMs across 9 societal and technical dimensions, revealing that no single model dominates all aspects and that closed-API models substantially outperform open-weight ones. The finding that efficiency-focused models (Claude 3 Haiku, Gemini 1.5 Flash) degrade specifically on bias resistance while maintaining performance elsewhere suggests that model compression may selectively erode ethical reasoning, a concern with direct deployment implications. Open questions remain: the authors acknowledge that fairness may not be genuinely probed by current benchmarks, that automated metrics (Prometheus Vision, exact match) can miss nuance, and that the benchmark is vulnerable to overfitting/gaming. The framework is explicitly designed as a living benchmark, and its extensibility invites community contributions of new scenarios and models.
