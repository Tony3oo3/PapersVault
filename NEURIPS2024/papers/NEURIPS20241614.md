## Problem

Existing human video generation methods focus on either facial animation or full-body pose transfer, failing to produce realistic conversational videos that simultaneously capture frequent hand gestures and varied facial movements. Dual-stream diffusion approaches that do attempt full-body animation struggle with three specific issues: hand synthesis degrades due to sparse pixel-level motion guidance, facial identity leaks during cross-identity animation because of entanglement between identity and 2D driving signals, and 3D-based alternatives introduce pose estimation errors and costly per-frame optimization.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single reference image (body cropped to 512×512, face cropped and aligned to 256×256) and a sequence of 2D pose heatmaps extracted by DWPose (covering face, body, and hand key points) as driving signals. It outputs a sequence of F video frames at 512×512 resolution depicting the reference person performing the driving motions. Notably, the driving signal is purely 2D key points with no 3D reconstruction, and for cross-identity driving the poses are rescaled to match the reference person's body proportions.

## Method

ShowMaker builds on a dual-stream diffusion backbone (Reference U-Net for appearance encoding, Denoising U-Net for denoising) initialized from Stable Diffusion, with two novel fine-grained modules. The Key Point-based Fine-grained Hand Modeling module applies Fourier positional encoding to raw hand key point coordinates to create resolution-independent structural guidance, then uses a key point-based codebook of orthogonal learnable basis vectors to produce per-key-point texture compensation that is injected into the Denoising U-Net via masked cross-attention. The Face Recapture module extracts facial texture (via VAE) and global identity (via ArcFace) features from the aligned reference face, fuses them with driving pose features through separate cross-attention blocks, and injects the result into a dedicated face attention layer also constrained by a face mask. Training proceeds in two stages: an image-level stage (F=1) for appearance-to-pose mapping, followed by a temporal stage that trains only the temporal attention layers (initialized from AnimateDiff) for inter-frame coherence.

## Results

ShowMaker achieves the best performance across all metrics on the combined test set: SSIM 0.85, PSNR 32.23, FID 15.43, FVD 197.43, and motion accuracy L_body 2.27, L_face 0.19, L_hand 0.77. On the Seth-specific comparison, ShowMaker further improves to FID 9.83 and FVD 193.25, substantially outperforming Make-Your-Anchor (FID 32.32, FVD 428.84). The FVD of 197.43 is roughly 1.8× lower than the next-best method (AnimateAnyone at 365.83), and the hand motion error L_hand of 0.77 is nearly half that of AnimateAnyone (1.10) and MagicPose (1.12).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ShowMaker outperforms all six baselines on every reported metric. Against the closest diffusion-based competitors, MagicPose (SSIM 0.82, FID 16.37, FVD 370.75) and AnimateAnyone (SSIM 0.80, FID 16.87, FVD 365.83), ShowMaker improves FVD by roughly 46–47% and reduces hand motion error L_hand from ~1.10–1.12 to 0.77, while also achieving the best face accuracy (L_face 0.19 vs. 0.62–0.68). The warp-based method TPS performs worst overall (FVD 1120.37, L_hand 17.99), struggling with complex hand postures. Disco and MagicAnimate, which lack fine-grained hand/face modeling, show intermediate quality (FVD 540–665) with notably higher hand errors (3.02–4.33). In the Seth-specific comparison, ShowMaker (FID 9.83, FVD 193.25) substantially surpasses Make-Your-Anchor (FID 32.32, FVD 428.84), confirming advantages even when the competitor is fine-tuned on the same subject.

## Relation to Prior Work

ShowMaker builds directly on the dual-stream diffusion paradigm established by AnimateAnyone, MagicPose, MagicAnimate, and CHAMP, which separate appearance encoding (Reference U-Net) from motion conditioning (Pose Encoder) and fuse them via cross-attention in a Denoising U-Net initialized from Stable Diffusion. It departs from this lineage in two key ways: (1) it replaces 3D body reconstruction or optical-flow-based driving signals with 2D DWPose key points, arguing this is more stable and efficient for conversational scenarios, and (2) it introduces region-specific fine-grained modules (a Fourier-encoded key point codebook for hands and an ArcFace-based Face Recapture module) that are absent from prior dual-stream methods, which treat hands and faces as generic local regions subject to the same downsampling degradation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap in digital human technology: generating realistic half-body conversational avatars (TV shows, stand-up comedy) where simultaneous hand gestures and facial expressions must be high-fidelity, a setting where prior full-body or talking-head methods fall short. The FVD improvement (~2× over the strongest baselines) and the dramatic face-accuracy gain (0.19 vs. 0.62 Euclidean key-point distance) suggest that targeted fine-grained conditioning can substantially outperform uniform pose guidance. Open questions include robustness to severe motion blur (where DWPose degrades), handling of challenging visual effects like glasses reflections, and whether the key-point codebook approach generalizes to other fine-grained body parts or to full-body generation at higher resolutions.
