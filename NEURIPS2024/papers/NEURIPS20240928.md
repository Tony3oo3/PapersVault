## Problem

Existing text-to-video diffusion models struggle with compositional prompts that require precise spatial positioning of multiple objects with distinct attributes, and long-video generation methods fail when object counts change dynamically, producing bizarre transformations or ignoring new characters entirely. No prior approach simultaneously handles multi-object spatial composition, progressive scene changes, and cross-frame object consistency in a unified framework.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240928/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts compositional text prompts (e.g., 'A talking sponge on the left and a superhero baby on the right') that are decomposed into frame-wise sub-prompts with per-object region masks, optionally via an LLM-based auto-decomposer. For long video generation, it takes a progressive prompt sequence where objects appear, disappear, or reposition across segments, plus reference images of objects for consistency. Outputs are 16-frame compositional videos (training-free, applied to pre-trained backbones like VideoCrafter2) or longer auto-regressive videos with seamless scene transitions and dynamic object counts.

## Method

The core contribution is Spatio-Temporal Compositional Diffusion, a training-free mechanism that decomposes prompts temporally (per-frame) and spatially (per-object with region masks), computes per-object cross-attention values in the denoising network, then composes them by spatial summation and temporal concatenation, blending with the original full-prompt cross-attention via a tunable α weight to balance global coherence with local object fidelity. For long video generation, the paper trains an auto-regressive ControlNet-like backbone on an enhanced Panda-70M subset filtered by RAFT optical flow scores (0.25–0.75) and recaptioned with three multimodal LLMs plus a local LLM for richer compositional semantics. A Reference Frame Attention module encodes reference frames with the same VAE autoencoder (bypassing CLIP's text-aligned embeddings) and inserts per-object cross-attention blocks with region masks into each attention layer, maintaining object appearance consistency across frames and positions in latent space.

## Results

On compositional short-video generation (Table 1), VideoTetris achieves the best VBLIP-VQA (0.5563) and VUnidet (0.2350) scores, outperforming all open-source and commercial baselines. Its CLIP-SIM of 0.9312 surpasses the backbone VideoCrafter2 (0.9249) and LVD (0.8873) but remains below Pika (0.9736) and Gen-2 (0.9421). For long-video generation with progressive compositional prompts (Table 2), VideoTetris leads in VBLIP-VQA (0.4839) and VUnidet (0.2137), while FreeNoise achieves a higher CLIP-SIM (0.9706 vs. 0.9521) due to its noise rescheduling strategy that the authors note damages natural story transitions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VideoTetris outperforms all open-source baselines (AnimateDiff, VideoCrafter2, LVD) on both VBLIP-VQA and VUnidet, with the largest margin over AnimateDiff (+0.1729 VBLIP-VQA, +0.0429 VUnidet). Against commercial models Gen-2 and Pika, VideoTetris wins decisively on compositional accuracy metrics (VBLIP-VQA: 0.5563 vs. 0.4427/0.4219; VUnidet: 0.2350 vs. 0.1503/0.1782) but trails on CLIP-SIM (0.9312 vs. 0.9421/0.9736), suggesting the commercial models produce more temporally consistent frames at the cost of weaker compositional fidelity. For long-video generation, VideoTetris dominates StreamingT2V across all three metrics (VBLIP-VQA 0.4839 vs. 0.2412; VUnidet 0.2137 vs. 0.1367; CLIP-SIM 0.9521 vs. 0.6720), while FreeNoise achieves a slightly higher CLIP-SIM (0.9706) but substantially lower compositional scores (VBLIP-VQA 0.4372, VUnidet 0.1823). The authors attribute FreeNoise's higher CLIP-SIM to its noise rescheduling producing near-static scenes, which inflates frame-to-frame similarity without reflecting genuine narrative coherence.

## Relation to Prior Work

VideoTetris builds on two distinct backbones: VideoCrafter2 for short compositional video generation (where the Spatio-Temporal Compositional Diffusion is applied in a training-free manner) and StreamingT2V's ControlNet-like branch for auto-regressive long video generation. Conceptually, it departs from layout-based compositional methods (LVD, VideoDirectorGPT) that impose rigid bounding boxes, instead manipulating cross-attention maps spatially and temporally to allow objects to naturally blend during denoising. The attention-manipulation idea itself draws from compositional T2I work (RPG, Attend-and-Excite, training-free layout control), but the paper's key contribution is extending this to the video domain with both spatial and temporal decomposition, and combining it with a consistency mechanism (Reference Frame Attention) that operates in the latent space rather than relying on CLIP embeddings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Compositional text-to-video generation—handling multiple objects with distinct attributes, spatial relationships, and dynamic object count changes—is a practically important but underexplored capability that current T2V models handle poorly. The training-free nature of the Spatio-Temporal Compositional Diffusion makes it broadly applicable to any pre-trained T2V model, lowering the barrier for adoption. However, the auto-regressive long video component inherits significant computational overhead from the ControlNet architecture, and the authors acknowledge bad cases and excessive transition frames. Open questions include whether the cross-attention manipulation approach scales to scenes with many more than two objects, whether the LLM-based decomposition can be made more reliable for highly complex narratives, and whether the Reference Frame Attention can be made more efficient for real-time or near-real-time generation.
