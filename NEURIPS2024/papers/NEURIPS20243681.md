## Problem

Existing multimodal foundation model benchmarks focus almost exclusively on end-to-end workflow automation, ignoring the broader business process management (BPM) tasks—documentation, knowledge transfer, and process improvement—that consume 60% of typical BPM project time. No large-scale dataset with the annotation depth required for these tasks (step-by-step written guides, quality rankings, full action traces) existed, and prior case studies were anecdotal with fewer than 50 examples.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243681/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each workflow demonstration provides a multimodal bundle: a full screen recording (MP4), key frame PNGs synced to action timestamps, a JSON action trace of all clicks/keystrokes/scrolls with HTML element metadata, a natural-language intent, and a human-written SOP at 'five-year-old' detail level. Inputs are systematically ablated (intent only → intent+keyframes → intent+keyframes+trace) to isolate the contribution of each modality. Outputs vary by task: generated SOPs scored per-step against a reference, binary completion/trajectory labels, Kendall-τ quality rankings, free-text answers scored on four axes (completeness, soundness, clarity, compactness), frame-to-workflow cluster assignments scored by adjusted rand index, and iteratively improved SOPs rated 1–5 against a rubric.

## Method

WONDERBREAD curates 2,928 human demonstrations across 598 web-navigation workflows (filtered from WebArena's 812) using 13 annotators who record themselves via a custom Python script capturing video, action traces, keyframes, and SOPs simultaneously, followed by three rounds of quality assurance over two months. For 162 'Gold Workflows,' annotators additionally rank all demonstrations by perceived quality and write a high-quality 'Gold SOP.' The benchmark defines six tasks spanning three BPM applications—Documentation (SOP Generation, Demo Segmentation), Knowledge Transfer (Question Answering, Demo Validation), and Improvement (SOP Ranking, SOP Improvement)—each with an automated evaluation pipeline combining programmatic metrics (F1, adjusted rand index, Kendall τ) and GPT-4-as-a-judge scoring validated at ρ>0.8 correlation with human raters. The key design choice is evaluating intermediate workflow reasoning (e.g., whether a specific step was performed correctly) rather than only end-to-end task completion, enabling finer-grained diagnosis of model failures such as hallucinated steps or inability to detect shuffled/truncated trajectories.

## Results

On the SOP Generation task, GPT-4 achieves the best F1 of 0.82 (precision 0.80, recall 0.88) when given intent, keyframes, and action trace, outperforming Claude 3 Sonnet (F1 0.76) and Gemini Pro 1 (F1 0.58). On Demo Validation for workflow completion, GPT-4 reaches F1 0.90, while Gemini Pro 1 and Claude 3 Sonnet both drop to F1 0.40. However, on the finer-grained Trajectory validation task (checking exact SOP step adherence), even GPT-4 achieves only F1 0.27, and on SOP Ranking, the best Kendall τ correlation with human judgments is a mere 0.06, indicating essentially random performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GPT-4 consistently outperforms Claude 3 Sonnet and Gemini Pro 1 across all six BPM tasks. On SOP Generation, GPT-4 leads with F1 0.82 vs. 0.76 (Claude 3) and 0.58 (Gemini Pro 1); the gap widens when the action trace is removed (GPT-4 drops to 0.71, Gemini to 0.46). On Demo Segmentation, GPT-4 achieves Adj. RI 0.85 while Gemini Pro 1 reaches only 0.55, and adding SOP/intent context actually hurts Gemini's performance. On Demo Validation (Completion), GPT-4's F1 of 0.90 far exceeds both Claude 3 Sonnet (0.40 with SOP, 0.63 without) and Gemini Pro 1 (0.40). The most striking gap appears on Trajectory Validation, where GPT-4 (F1 0.27) and Gemini Pro 1 (F1 0.25) both fail to reliably detect whether a demonstration followed an SOP exactly. On SOP Ranking, all three models perform at chance level (Kendall τ between 0.03 and 0.06), with GPT-4 marginally ahead at 0.06. On SOP Improvement, GPT-4 shows the largest self-improvement gain (3.43 → 4.82 on a 5-point scale), followed by Claude 3 Sonnet (3.43 → 4.26) and Gemini Pro 1 (3.43 → 3.65).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on WebArena [73] as its data foundation (filtering 812 workflows to 598) but departs sharply from the dominant paradigm in workflow benchmarks, which focus almost exclusively on end-to-end agent automation (e.g., 'can the model complete the task?'). Prior benchmarks like AITW, Mind2Web, OSWorld, and WebArena themselves evaluate only workflow execution success rates, lacking the step-by-step SOP annotations, human quality rankings, and intermediate-reasoning evaluations needed for BPM tasks. The paper also positions itself against traditional process mining tools (event-log analysis, manual review) and recent small-scale LLM-for-BPM case studies [21, 53, 25], arguing that those works are anecdotal (<50 examples) and do not consider multimodal inputs like screen recordings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark fills a clear gap: documentation accounts for ~60% of typical BPM project time and the BPM market is 4× larger than the automation-tools market, yet no prior ML benchmark evaluates models on these tasks. The key open question the results surface is the stark capability gap between high-level workflow summarization (F1 0.82) and fine-grained step-level validation (F1 0.27), suggesting current multimodal FMs lack the low-level process understanding needed for enterprise-grade accuracy (0.99+). Additional open threads include human-model alignment for workflow quality judgment (SOP ranking is essentially random, Kendall τ ≈ 0.05), long-context reasoning over multi-minute recordings, and whether these findings generalize beyond the four web environments used. Limitations include the absence of open-source model baselines and the constraint to web-based workflows rather than real enterprise systems.
