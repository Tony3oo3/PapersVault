## Problem

Nearly all existing sampling-based Bayesian inference circuit models ignore the rich diversity of inhibitory interneurons in canonical cortical microcircuits, leaving it unclear how different interneuron types contribute to sampling. Additionally, prior analytical treatments relied on linear dynamics or purely numerical methods, so the sampling algorithm implemented by nonlinear recurrent circuits with multiple interneuron types remained unidentified.

## Input / Output

The circuit receives Poisson feedforward spikes with Gaussian tuning profiles that parametrically encode a 1D periodic stimulus feature z (e.g., orientation), where the spike count sets the likelihood precision and the spike centroid sets the likelihood mean. The output is the instantaneous position of the E-neuron population bump on the stimulus feature manifold, read out via a population vector decoder, which serves as a single posterior sample; the time series of these samples approximates the full posterior p(z|r_F). The model operates in a rate-based continuum limit with internal multiplicative Poisson variability as the sole noise source driving sampling.

## Method

The paper constructs a nonlinear ring attractor circuit with E neurons receiving divisive (global) inhibition from untuned PV neurons and additive (tuning-dependent) inhibition from stimulus-tuned SOM neurons, then performs perturbative eigen-analysis to project the full nonlinear dynamics onto a low-dimensional stimulus feature manifold. On this manifold, the reduced E+PV circuit is shown to implement Langevin sampling of the posterior, while the addition of SOM neurons upgrades the dynamics to a mixed Langevin–Hamiltonian form in which the SOM position acts as an auxiliary momentum variable that accelerates sampling by reducing temporal sample correlation. A key theoretical constraint emerges: Hamiltonian sampling requires SOM neurons to receive no direct feedforward input (w_SF = 0), matching the sparse feedforward connectivity observed in neuroanatomy. The authors further derive a low-dimensional linear manifold in the connection-weight space (Eq. 21) under which the circuit correctly samples the posterior, and show that in the linear input-output regime the circuit with fixed weights flexibly samples posteriors of varying uncertainty by simply changing the feedforward input rate.

## Results

The paper analytically identifies that the reduced circuit (E + PV neurons only) implements Langevin sampling on the stimulus feature manifold, with the feedforward weight constrained to w_EF = (2/√3)³ F_E (Eq. 15). Adding SOM neurons with tuning-dependent additive inhibition upgrades the dynamics to a mixed Langevin–Hamiltonian sampling (Eqs. 17–18), where the SOM position z_S acts as the auxiliary momentum variable. A key theoretical constraint is that Hamiltonian sampling requires w_SF = 0 (no feedforward input to SOM neurons), consistent with neuroanatomy. Numerically, the full circuit's sample cross-correlation decays faster than the reduced circuit (Fig. 3C), and the KL divergence to the posterior decreases more rapidly (Fig. 3D), confirming accelerated sampling. The PV inhibitory weight w_EP monotonically increases the slowest eigenvalue (faster sampling), while the SOM inhibitory weight w_ES has a non-monotonic effect with an optimal value maximizing sampling speed and inducing temporal oscillations in samples.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical neuroscience paper without standard ML baselines or benchmark comparisons. The primary 'comparison' is an ablation: the reduced circuit (E + PV, Fig. 2A) performs first-order Langevin sampling, while the full circuit (E + PV + SOM, Fig. 1B) performs second-order Hamiltonian sampling with faster convergence. The paper positions its contribution relative to prior sampling-circuit work: unlike [14, 17] which sample in the full neural response space, this circuit samples in a low-dimensional stimulus feature manifold; unlike [53, 55] which achieve accelerated sampling via intracellular mechanisms (spike-frequency adaptation, potassium channels), the acceleration here comes from a distinct interneuron population (SOM) that is externally modifiable by VIP neurons. The coupled-circuit extension for bivariate posteriors builds on [34], which considered the same architecture but did not identify the underlying sampling algorithm. No head-to-head numerical benchmark against prior models is provided; validation is via matching theoretical predictions (eigenvalue spectra, cross-correlation functions, weight manifolds) to circuit simulations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of neural circuit models for sampling-based Bayesian inference (Aitchison & Lengyel 2016; Echeveste et al. 2020; Zhang et al. 2023) by being the first to analytically identify the specific sampling algorithm performed by a nonlinear ring attractor circuit containing distinct interneuron types. Prior work either relied on linear dynamics for analytical tractability (Zhang et al. 2023) or performed numerical analysis of nonlinear circuits without identifying the algorithm (Echeveste et al. 2020), and none considered how the diversity of inhibitory interneurons (PV vs. SOM) maps onto different sampling algorithms. The foundational ring attractor architecture (Ben-Yishai et al. 1995) and the probabilistic population code framework (Ma et al. 2006) provide the representational substrate, while the key departure is the perturbative eigen-analysis of the full nonlinear E-PV-SOM dynamics to extract the low-dimensional sampling dynamics on the stimulus feature manifold.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first analytical bridge between specific cortical interneuron types and specific MCMC algorithms: PV-mediated divisive normalization yields Langevin sampling, while SOM-mediated additive, tuning-dependent inhibition upgrades it to Hamiltonian sampling, accelerating convergence. The theoretical requirement that SOM neurons must receive no direct feedforward input for Hamiltonian sampling to hold is a non-trivial prediction that matches the known scarcity of feedforward synapses onto SOM cells, lending biological credibility to the model. Open questions include the role of VIP neurons (which modulate SOM gain and could act as a sampling-speed 'knob'), how to store non-uniform priors by breaking neuronal homogeneity, and extension to dynamic (hidden Markov) stimuli. The model is limited to 1D static stimuli and a linear input-output regime for flexible posterior sampling, leaving the full nonlinear regime and higher-dimensional posteriors as future work.
