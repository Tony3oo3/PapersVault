## Problem

Indoor air pollution claims over 3.2 million lives annually, yet publicly available indoor air quality datasets are either in-situ (focusing on thermal comfort and energy efficiency rather than pollution), survey-based (suffering from delayed user responses that prevent real-time alignment with pollution events), or small-scale and scenario-specific. No comprehensive, multi-site, multi-season dataset with real-time activity annotations exists for low to middle-income communities in developing countries like India, where unplanned urbanization, cramped living conditions, and compromised ventilation create unique and severe indoor pollution dynamics.

## Input / Output

The system ingests 1 Hz time-series readings from up to 7 co-located DALTON sensors per site (measuring PM1, PM2.5, PM10, CO2, NO2, CO, VOC, C2H5OH, temperature, and humidity) along with free-text speech-to-text activity annotations submitted via an Android app and floor plan images encoding sensor adjacency. The output is a spatiotemporal dataset of 89.1M samples spanning 13,646 hours across 30 sites in four Indian regions, with 3,957 timestamped activity labels, derived validity flags (Valid, Valid_CO2), and KLCPD-computed change-point markers (bkps) that flag potential pollution events for downstream analysis.

## Method

The authors designed a low-cost, remotely maintainable DALTON sensing platform built around an ESP-WROOM-32 MCU with a 3D-printed honeycomb shell, integrating a DFRobot laser-scattering PM sensor, a Seeed Studio multi-channel MEMS gas sensor (NO2, ethanol, VOC, CO), and a Winsen MH-Z16 infrared CO2 sensor, all calibrated against reference devices before deployment. To capture spatial spread of pollutants, multiple sensors are strategically placed at chest height in each room of a household (up to 7 per site), and occupants annotate activities in real time via a speech-to-text Android application (vocalAnnot) that triggers alerts on significant pollution changes. The preprocessing pipeline merges log replicas, validates readings against sensor ranges, imputes gaps under 15 minutes with nearest-fill, applies a 60-second rolling-window smoothing, and marks change-points using the KLCPD algorithm from the ruptures package. For the ML benchmarking, the authors compute features from the multi-sensor time series and evaluate seven supervised models (SVM, Naive Bayes, Decision Tree, KNN, Logistic Regression, Random Forest, and multi-layer Neural Networks) on activity detection (8 classes) and food-item classification (11 classes) using both 70-30 random splits and 5-fold cross-validation, with Random Forest achieving 97.7% and 98.3% testing F1-scores respectively.

## Results

The DALTON dataset contains 89.1M spatiotemporal air quality samples (13,646 hours) from 30 indoor sites across four Indian regions over six months, with 3,957 activity annotations from 24 participants. In the activity classification benchmark (8 classes in research lab R1), Random Forest (max estimators 50, max depth 10) achieves the best 70-30 split testing F1-score of 97.7%, while a 3-layer Neural Network [64,64,64] reaches 97.8%. For food item classification (11 classes in household H1), the Neural Network [128,128,128] achieves 99.5% testing F1-score and Random Forest (max estimators 100) reaches 99.4% in the 70-30 split. Performance drops substantially in 5-fold cross-validation due to class imbalance and limited cooking instances (e.g., food detection test accuracy falls to ~44% for NN).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a dataset paper with no external method comparisons; the ML experiments serve as internal benchmarks to demonstrate the dataset's utility for downstream tasks. Among the seven model families evaluated, ensemble and deep methods (Random Forest, Neural Network, Decision Tree, k-NN) consistently outperform linear and probabilistic models (SVM, Logistic Regression, Naive Bayes) on both activity and food item classification. Random Forest and the 3-layer Neural Network are the top performers, with testing F1-scores above 97% for activity detection and above 99% for food item detection in the 70-30 split. However, 5-fold cross-validation reveals significant performance degradation (e.g., food detection test accuracy drops from ~99% to ~44% for NN), attributed to class imbalance and the limited number of cooking events (typically twice daily) in a single household. The paper does not compare against prior indoor air quality datasets or external baselines, positioning the contribution as the dataset itself rather than a new method.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by combining three elements absent from prior indoor air quality datasets: multi-city deployment across rural/suburban/urban India, real-time speech-to-text activity annotations (via the vocalAnnot app), and multi-sensor spatiotemporal coverage across five indoor environment types. It departs from in-situ datasets that focus on thermal comfort and energy efficiency rather than pollution, and from survey-based datasets whose delayed responses make real-time pollution-event alignment impractical. While small-scale studies from developing countries exist (e.g., single-ventilation Indian households, Mongolian ger settlements), none release a comparable open dataset spanning 30 sites over six months with the breadth of pollutant types and activity context provided here.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first large-scale, open-sourced indoor air pollution dataset from a developing country with real-time activity annotations, filling a critical gap for ML researchers, environmentalists, and architects working on pollution dynamics in low-to-middle-income settings. Key limitations include significant missing data from frequent power outages (mean 11.82%, max 97.91%), incomplete participant annotations, and coverage of only summer and winter seasons (no monsoon or autumn). Open questions include how to robustly handle the missing-data and mislabeling scenarios inherent to field deployments in resource-constrained settings, and whether the observed pollution patterns generalize beyond the specific Indian contexts studied.
