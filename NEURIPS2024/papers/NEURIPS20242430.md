## Problem

Traditional knowledge distillation strategies in semi-supervised continual learning (SSCL) rely on sample-wise representations or pairwise similarities that are sensitive to noise and biased feature-space shifts between tasks. When unlabeled data carries inaccurate or noisy information, these distillation losses propagate errors and degrade generalization on previously learned classes. The paper addresses the need for a distillation mechanism that preserves intrinsic structural information in unlabeled data while being inherently insensitive to such perturbations.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images at 32×32 (CIFAR-10/100) or 256×256 (ImageNet-100) in both labeled and unlabeled form, plus a memory buffer of replayed samples (500–5120 images) from previous tasks. The distillation loss operates not on individual sample features or pairwise distances but on the topological structure of local k-hop neighborhoods in the logit space, comparing persistence diagrams between the old and new feature extractors. The final output is a standard class label prediction, with the total training objective combining cross-entropy, FixMatch semi-supervised loss, representation distillation on the buffer, and the proposed persistence homology distillation loss.

## Method

PsHD constructs a Vietoris-Rips simplicial complex over each sample's weighted k-hop neighborhood (defined via a cosine-similarity threshold on logits) and computes its persistence diagram to capture multi-scale topological features (connected components and 1-dimensional holes). The distillation loss is the p-Wasserstein distance (p=1) between the persistence diagrams of the old and new models over the same neighborhood, measuring how much the topological structure has shifted. An acceleration algorithm partitions the memory buffer into non-overlapping neighborhood groups, reducing computation from O(|M|·n^2.5) to O(|S|·n^2.5) where |S| ≈ number of classes in the buffer. A stability theorem proves that the PH-based distance is bounded by a combinatorial factor times the pairwise similarity distance, formally establishing greater robustness to representation perturbations than relation-based distillation. The method is plugged into the DSGD semi-supervised continual learning pipeline, replacing its sub-graph distillation loss on unlabeled buffer samples.

## Results

On the primary SSCL benchmark (Table 2, 500-sample buffer), PsHD achieves 74.5% / 77.6% / 78.0% average accuracy on CIFAR-10 (0.8% / 5% / 25% label ratio) and 27.7% / 42.4% / 47.8% on CIFAR-100, outperforming the strongest baseline DSGD by 0.4–1.5 points. Across the three datasets and both backbone architectures (Table 1), PsHD yields an average improvement of 3.9% over the state-of-the-art NNCSL, and retains a 1.5% advantage even when the memory buffer is reduced by 60% (5120→2000 samples). On the highest-resolution benchmark ImageNet-100 (7.7% labels), PsHD reaches 60.6% average accuracy versus 59.9% for DSGD.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PsHD consistently outperforms all 12 baselines across every dataset and label ratio tested. Against the closest competitors—DSGD (relation distillation, AAAI 2024) and NNCSL (nearest-neighbor framework, ICCV 2023)—PsHD gains 0.4–3.2 points on average accuracy, with the largest margin (+3.2) on CIFAR-100 5% under the iCaRL backbone. Compared to earlier distillation methods (iCaRL, FOSTER, Podnet, LUCIR, R-DFCIL), the advantage is substantially larger (e.g., 81.5% vs. 57.9% for Podnet on CIFAR-10 5%), confirming that topological structure distillation is more robust than sample-wise or pairwise-similarity distillation. Against the topology-based TopKD (ICML 2024), PsHD leads by 2.8 points on CIFAR-10 5% and 3.4 points on CIFAR-100 5%, and the paper proves a b² computational advantage over TopKD as the number of old classes grows. Under Gaussian noise (σ=1.2), PsHD retains 67.8% accuracy with only 18.3 BWT, while DSGD drops to 64.1% and Podnet to 34.6%, validating the theoretical stability guarantee.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the semi-supervised continual learning (SSCL) line of work initiated by NNCSL and DSGD, which distill class-instance or local-neighborhood similarity on unlabeled replay data. It departs from these relation-distillation approaches by replacing pairwise similarity with persistence homology diagrams computed over Vietoris-Rips filtrations of k-hop neighborhoods, motivated by the topological invariance of PH under homeomorphism and its known stability to perturbations. The acceleration algorithm (grouping samples into disjoint neighborhoods) is a practical contribution that reduces the O(|M|n^2.5) cost to O(|S|n^2.5), and the theoretical stability bound (Theorem 1) formally connects PH distillation to pairwise similarity distillation, showing PH is inherently more robust.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete failure mode in SSCL—noisy or biased unlabeled representations degrading distillation—by providing a noise-insensitive structural signal with a formal stability guarantee. The 60% memory buffer reduction while maintaining or improving accuracy is practically significant for deployment-constrained settings. However, the reliance on the CPU-bound GUDHI package for persistence diagram computation remains a bottleneck, and the authors acknowledge that learnable alternatives like RipsNet could further accelerate the pipeline. Open questions include whether the topological distillation signal generalizes beyond image classification (e.g., to video or 3D data) and whether the k-hop neighborhood construction is optimal for high-dimensional feature spaces.
