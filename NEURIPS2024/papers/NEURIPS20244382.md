## Problem

Incorporating heuristic rewards into RL training is essential for complex robotic tasks, but the standard approach of enforcing optimal policy invariance (e.g., via PBRS) only guarantees convergence to the task-optimal policy in the infinite-data limit. In practice, with finite training data, these methods often fail to outperform policies trained solely on heuristic rewards, and manually tuning the balance coefficient λ between task and heuristic objectives is fragile and task-sensitive. The paper asks whether a different constraint—directly requiring the learned policy to outperform the heuristic-only policy on the task objective—can reliably improve task performance in finite-data settings.

## Input / Output

The method consumes continuous robot states along with two reward functions: a sparse task reward r (e.g., progress distance or binary goal-reach indicator) and a denser heuristic reward h (e.g., shaped terms for uprightness, heading, action penalties). It outputs a policy π mapping states to continuous actions that is guaranteed to achieve J(π) ≥ J(π_H) on the task objective. Unusually, the algorithm trains two policies simultaneously—the enhanced policy π and the heuristic policy π_H—sharing the same trajectory buffer (half collected by each), so no additional environment interactions are needed beyond standard PPO.

## Method

HEPO formulates policy training as a constrained optimization: maximize J(π) + H(π) subject to J(π) ≥ J(π_H), then converts it to a min-max Lagrangian problem where the Lagrangian multiplier α adaptively reweights the task reward. The enhanced policy is trained with the modified per-step reward (1+α)r + h, so when the policy underperforms the heuristic policy (J(π) < J(π_H)), α increases to emphasize task rewards, and when it outperforms, α decreases to allow heuristic rewards to guide exploration. Both π and π_H are updated concurrently using off-policy importance ratio corrections over shared trajectories, implemented within PPO with GAE-computed advantages. The α gradient is estimated via the performance difference lemma using advantages from both policies' trajectories, and smoothed with a median-of-K accumulator plus Adam to prevent explosive updates. This design eliminates the need to manually tune λ and ensures the learned policy never exploits heuristic rewards at the expense of task performance.

## Results

Across 29 tasks (9 IsaacGym + 20 BI-DEX), HEPO achieves the highest interquartile mean (IQM) of normalized task return and a 62% probability of improvement over the heuristic-only policy (H-only), with the lower confidence bound above 50% indicating statistical significance. In the robustness study with 12 human-designed heuristic reward functions on FrankaCabinet, HEPO achieves an IQM of 0.94 (95% CI: 0.85–1.03) and a 73% probability of improvement over H-only (IQM 0.44, PI 0.50), while PBRS and HuRL both collapse to IQM 0.00. HEPO also outperforms EIPO on Montezuma's Revenge, matching PPO+RND convergence performance using only 20% of the training data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HEPO decisively outperforms all four baselines. Against H-only (standard PPO with heuristic rewards), HEPO more than doubles the IQM (0.94 vs. 0.44) and achieves a 73% probability of improvement versus the 50% baseline, with non-overlapping confidence intervals. PBRS (potential-based reward shaping) and HuRL (heuristic-guided RL) both fail catastrophically in the robustness setting, achieving IQM of 0.00 and probabilities of improvement below 50% (0.22 and 0.33 respectively), confirming that optimal-policy-invariance guarantees do not hold in finite-data practice. Against EIPO, HEPO wins on the most challenging task (Montezuma's Revenge) and in ablation studies on locomotion/manipulation tasks, primarily due to its choice of the heuristic policy (rather than task-only policy) as the constraint reference and its joint trajectory collection strategy. The J+H baseline (fixed λ mixing) performs slightly worse than H-only, confirming that manual weight tuning is fragile across tasks, whereas HEPO's adaptive Lagrangian multiplier eliminates this sensitivity.

## Relation to Prior Work

HEPO departs from the dominant paradigm of optimal policy invariance (PBRS, Ng et al. 1999) by arguing that guaranteeing the same optimal policy under infinite data is the wrong objective in finite-data regimes. It is most closely related to EIPO (Chen et al., NeurIPS 2022), which also uses Lagrangian constrained optimization to prevent exploitation of auxiliary rewards, but HEPO replaces EIPO's task-only reference policy with the heuristic policy itself, and simplifies trajectory collection from an alternating to a joint strategy. The method is agnostic to the specific heuristic reward function and can be layered on top of existing approaches like HuRL's scheduling or PBRS-shaped rewards.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

For RL practitioners in robotics, HEPO removes the need to manually tune the task/heuristic reward balance (λ), which is a significant practical burden when integrating human-designed heuristics. The human-subject study demonstrating robustness to poorly designed heuristics (9/12) suggests HEPO could reduce the iterative reward-design loop that currently dominates robotic RL development. A key limitation is the absence of a theoretical convergence guarantee to the optimal task policy, which the authors identify as future work potentially addressable via recent reward-engineering theory. Open questions include whether the constraint formulation generalizes to multi-task or hierarchical settings where the 'heuristic policy' reference becomes ambiguous.
