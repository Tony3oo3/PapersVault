## Problem

Existing DP-SCO algorithms require uniformly bounded Lipschitz constants, an assumption violated by heavy-tailed data prevalent in biomedicine and finance. Prior heavy-tailed DP-SCO results are suboptimal: LR23 loses a polynomial factor in dimension for any constant k, and KLZ22 requires nonstandard smoothness and condition-number assumptions. This paper achieves the first near-optimal rates (matching lower bounds up to logarithmic factors) for heavy-tailed DP-SCO with no additional structural assumptions.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes n i.i.d. samples from a distribution P where each sample induces a convex, differentiable loss f(·;s) with only k-th moment bounds G_j^j on the per-sample Lipschitz constant L_s (no uniform bound), a convex domain X ⊂ R^d of diameter D, and a ρ-CDP privacy budget. It outputs x̂ ∈ X with a high-probability (≥ 1−δ) excess population loss of G_2·D·√(log(1/δ)/n) + G_k·D·(√d·log(1/δ)/(n√ρ))^(1−1/k), querying O(max(n², n³ρ/d)) sample gradients. Unlike the uniform-Lipschitz setting where the privacy term scales with a fixed G, here it depends on G_k and the rate degrades gracefully as k → ∞, recovering the standard rate in the limit.

## Method

The core contribution is a population-level localization framework (Algorithm 2) that iteratively refines distance bounds to the population minimizer via increasing regularization, combined with geometric aggregation to boost constant-probability subproblem solutions to high-probability success. The key technical insight is bypassing the need to bound E[b_D²] (which naively requires 2k-th moment bounds) by instead using Markov's inequality to obtain constant-probability bounds on the clipping bias b_D depending only on G_k, then aggregating across multiple dataset splits where a constant fraction succeed. The subproblem solver is a clipped DP-SGD algorithm that clips only sample gradients while leaving regularization and noise unchanged, achieving ρ-CDP with utility parameterized by b_D. Additional results include a simple truncation-based reduction for the known-Lipschitz setting (eliminating log factors), and smooth-function algorithms that employ the sparse vector technique to privately count clipping operations, yielding linear-time methods for smooth GLMs.

## Results

The paper's main result (Theorem 1) achieves excess population loss $G_2 D \cdot \sqrt{\log(1/\delta)/n} + G_k D \cdot (\sqrt{d}\log(1/\delta)/(n\sqrt{\rho}))^{1-1/k}$ under $\rho$-CDP with probability $\geq 1-\delta$, matching the lower bound of [LR23] up to logarithmic factors. In the known-Lipschitz setting (Theorem 2), the algorithm achieves the optimal rate $G_2 D/\sqrt{n} + G_k D \cdot (\sqrt{d}/(n\sqrt{\rho}))^{1-1/k}$ without any logarithmic overhead. For smooth generalized linear models (Theorem 4), the algorithm attains both linear gradient query complexity and the optimal rate.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's main contribution is closing the gap between the best known upper bound and the lower bound for heavy-tailed DP-SCO. The prior best upper bound from [LR23] scales as $(\sqrt{d}/(n\sqrt{\rho}))^{1-2/k}$, which is polynomially worse than the optimal $(\sqrt{d}/(n\sqrt{\rho}))^{1-1/k}$ rate for any constant $k$ (e.g., for $k=2$, [LR23] gives a $1/n$ rate while the optimal is $1/\sqrt{n}$). This paper's Theorem 1 matches the lower bound up to $\log(1/\delta)$ factors, and Theorem 2 (known-Lipschitz) removes even those logarithmic factors to achieve the exact optimal rate. The [KLZ22] result requires additional smoothness and strong convexity assumptions with rates depending on the condition number $\kappa = \beta/\lambda$, making it inapplicable to the general non-smooth setting. For smooth functions, this paper's Theorem 3 requires $\beta = O(G_k/D \cdot \varepsilon^{1.5}\sqrt{n/d})$, which improves with more samples, whereas [LR23]'s Theorem 15 requires $\beta = O(G_k/D \cdot (d^5/(\varepsilon n))^{1/18})$, which degrades with larger $n$.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the heavy-tailed DP-SCO line initiated by [WXDX20] and [ADF+21], and most directly departs from [LR23], whose suboptimal rate stemmed from needing to bound E[b_D²] (requiring 2k-th moment bounds). The key technical departure is a new population-level localization framework (inspired by [FKT20]'s empirical localization) combined with geometric aggregation from [KLL+23], which allows using only constant-probability bounds on the clipping bias via Markov's inequality with G_k alone. The clipped DP-SGD subroutine modifies the convergence analysis of [LSB12], and the known-Lipschitz reduction simply reuses the optimal uniform-Lipschitz algorithm of [FKT20] as a black-box subroutine after replacing high-Lipschitz samples with dummies.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first algorithm achieving near-optimal rates (matching the [LR23] lower bound up to log factors) for heavy-tailed DP-SCO without requiring smoothness, known Lipschitz constants, or condition-number assumptions, closing a gap that had persisted since the problem was formalized. The population-level localization framework is a reusable tool that also recovers a tight high-probability SCO rate (matching [CH24]) as a corollary, suggesting broader applicability. Open questions include whether the logarithmic overhead can be eliminated, whether near-linear-time algorithms exist for the non-smooth case, and whether the framework extends to other private optimization problems. The paper is purely theoretical with no empirical validation, limiting immediate practical impact.
