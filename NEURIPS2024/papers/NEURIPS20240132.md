## Problem

Reference-based scene stylization with 3D Gaussian Splatting is challenging because discrete Gaussians tightly entangle appearance with geometry, so simply optimizing colors (as NeRF-based stylization methods do) fails to capture continuous texture details in the reference image. Meanwhile, NeRF-based approaches that handle this well suffer from slow volume rendering, making real-time stylized navigation impractical.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240132/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pretrained 3DGS model of a target scene and a single content-aligned 2D reference image as input, and produces a stylized 3DGS model that bakes the reference texture into the scene geometry. During stylization, higher-order spherical harmonics are discarded and only diffuse color is rendered. Training runs for 3000 iterations on a single A5000 GPU (~5–6 minutes), after which the stylized scene renders at 91–134 FPS for free-view navigation.

## Method

The core contribution is a Texture-Guided Gaussian Control mechanism that replaces the default positional-gradient densification with color-gradient-based identification of Gaussians exhibiting texture underfitting. When a Gaussian's accumulated color gradient exceeds a threshold, it is replaced via structured densification: nine tiny Gaussians (eight placed in the octants of the parent ellipsoid plus one at the center) are created with scales shrunk by a factor of 8, preserving approximate spatial coverage while enabling high-frequency detail infilling. A depth-based L1 regularization (weight 10) penalizes geometric drift by comparing rendered depth maps of the original and stylized models. View consistency is enforced through depth-warped pseudo-view supervision and a Template Correspondence Matching (TCM) loss using VGG16 features, with a color-matching loss handling occluded regions. The full training objective combines reconstruction, depth, pseudo-view, TCM, and color-matching losses with empirically tuned weights.

## Results

On the reference-based stylization benchmark (12 scenes from Blender, LLFF, and Tanks and Temples), ReGS achieves a Ref-LPIPS of 0.202, substantially outperforming Ref-NPR (0.339), ARF (0.394), and SNeRF (0.405). ReGS also attains the highest Robustness score of 31.27 (vs. 28.11 for Ref-NPR) and renders at 91.4 FPS on a single A5000 GPU, approximately 5.5× faster than all NeRF-based baselines (~16 FPS). The per-scene Ref-LPIPS results (Table 2) confirm consistent improvements across all 8 evaluated scenes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ReGS is compared against three NeRF-based baselines built on Plenoxels: ARF [8] and SNeRF [4] (general stylization without content correspondence), and Ref-NPR [10] (reference-based stylization, the closest prior work). ReGS outperforms all three baselines on every reported metric. Against the most relevant baseline Ref-NPR, ReGS reduces Ref-LPIPS by 40% (0.202 vs. 0.339) and improves Robustness by 11% (31.27 vs. 28.11), while rendering 5.5× faster (91.4 vs. 16.4 FPS). Against the general stylization methods ARF and SNeRF, the Ref-LPIPS gap is even larger (0.202 vs. 0.394/0.405), reflecting that these methods ignore content alignment and produce semantically inconsistent results. Qualitatively, Ref-NPR still produces artifacts and fails on challenging high-frequency details (e.g., miscoloring a green tree as white), whereas ReGS faithfully reproduces the reference texture including fine details like individual leaves.

## Relation to Prior Work

ReGS adapts 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) to the reference-based scene stylization task previously addressed by NeRF-based methods (ARF, SNeRF, Ref-NPR). It inherits the training framework from Ref-NPR (TCM loss, pseudo-view supervision, color-matching loss) but introduces a novel texture-guided control mechanism that structurally densifies Gaussians in texture-underfitting regions—a departure from prior methods that fix geometry and optimize only appearance. The structured densification (replacing one Gaussian with nine tiny ones) is a new design choice distinct from the default positional-gradient-based splitting in 3DGS, motivated by the observation that color gradients, not positional gradients, are the relevant signal for locating texture errors in a pre-reconstructed scene.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ReGS demonstrates that 3DGS can serve as a practical representation for reference-based stylization with real-time rendering (91 FPS vs. ~16 FPS for NeRF-based baselines), opening the door to interactive stylized scene navigation. The texture-guided control mechanism is a general-purpose tool for high-fidelity appearance editing in 3DGS beyond stylization, as shown by the freehand editing application. However, training efficiency remains unchanged (TCM feature extraction is the bottleneck), and the method cannot handle geometry changes—extending it to joint geometry-and-appearance editing or designing a universal zero-shot 3D Gaussian stylizer are open directions the authors identify.
