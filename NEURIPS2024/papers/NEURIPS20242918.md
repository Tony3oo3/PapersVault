## Problem

In the normal means model, the sharp minimax rate of noise variance estimation under square loss was unknown when only boundedness of the means is assumed (no sparsity, smoothness, or other structure). This is a fundamental gap because variance estimation is necessary for mimicking the oracle Bayes estimator via Tweedie's formula, constructing confidence intervals, and hypothesis testing, yet the honest-sequence setting (one observation per mean) makes the problem substantially harder than in regression or replicated-data settings.

## Input / Output

The method takes n observations X_1,...,X_n from the compound decision model X_i ~ N(μ_i, σ²) with the sole structural constraint ||μ||_∞ ≤ 1 and σ bounded by a universal constant. It produces a variance estimate σ̂² that achieves the minimax rate (log log n / log n)². The estimator includes a truncation step: when max_i X_i ≤ 4√(log n / r), it defaults to σ̂² = 0, effectively handling the small-variance regime where the cumulant-based procedure is not informative.

## Method

The estimator exploits the fact that Gaussian noise has all cumulants (beyond the second) equal to zero, so the marginal data cumulants directly recover the signal cumulants except the second. The key insight is a variational representation: the signal variance γ₂ equals sup{γ ∈ [0,1] : |M_r(γ)| ≤ 1 for all r ≥ 2}, where M_r is a Bell-polynomial function of the cumulant sequence with γ substituted for the second cumulant. The practical estimator estimates O(log n / log log n) cumulants via sample moments, then approximates the infinite-constraint variational problem by a single finite-r constraint, balancing the stochastic error in cumulant estimation against the O(1/r) approximation bias. The matching lower bound constructs a D-atomic prior via Gaussian quadrature (roots of the D-th Hermite polynomial) that matches 2D−1 moments of a Gaussian N(0,τ²), with D ≈ 1/(4τ²) chosen so the χ²-divergence between the two mixture models is O(1/n), yielding the (log log n / log n)² separation.

## Results

The paper establishes the sharp minimax rate for variance estimation in square error over the parameter space Θ = {(μ,σ): ||μ||_∞ ≤ 1, σ ≤ L} as (log log n / log n)² (Theorem 1 upper bound, Theorem 2 lower bound). The rate-optimal estimator (Theorem 1) achieves this by estimating O(log n / log log n) cumulants of the marginal data distribution and leveraging a variational representation of the noise variance in terms of those cumulants (Proposition 1). The lower bound (Theorem 2) is proved via a moment-matching construction using Gaussian quadrature, showing that a compactly supported prior can match Ω(1/τ²) moments of a Gaussian N(0,τ²), yielding the (log log n / log n)² separation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no experimental baselines, but the rate (log log n / log n)² is strictly faster than the 1/log n rate previously achieved by Matias [42] (who used the empirical moment generating function) and by the simpler extreme-order-statistic estimator max_i X_i / √(2 log n) noted in [63]. Compared to Butucea and Matias [4], who obtained (log n)^{r−2} under Fourier-transform smoothness conditions on the prior G, the present result requires only bounded support (||μ||_∞ ≤ 1) and achieves a faster rate in the rough case. Relative to Wu and Yang [63], who showed k²n^{−1/k} for k-atomic priors with k ≲ log n / log log n, the present result handles the fully nonparametric (non-atomic) case and confirms the logarithmic rate is indeed sharp. The paper also contrasts with Kong and Valiant [35] in the linear regression setting, where a (log log n / log n)⁴ rate is achievable due to the availability of random covariates as a source of fresh randomness, whereas the sequence model (1) with deterministic design yields the slower (log log n / log n)² rate.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the variance estimation problem in the normal means model from settings requiring smoothness (Butucea–Matias), atomicity (Wu–Yang), or sparsity/regularity assumptions to the minimal identifiability condition of bounded means (‖μ‖∞ ≤ 1). The upper bound departs from prior Fourier- or moment-based approaches by exploiting the cumulant structure of Gaussian noise: since all cumulants beyond the second vanish for a Gaussian, the signal cumulants are directly recoverable from the data, and a variational representation (Proposition 1) identifies the signal variance from the remaining cumulants. The lower bound draws on the moment-matching and Gaussian quadrature machinery of Wu and Yang [64], constructing a compactly supported prior that matches O(log n / log log n) moments of a Gaussian to make the two hypotheses indistinguishable.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first sharp minimax characterization of variance estimation under the weakest natural identifiability assumption, resolving a gap that had persisted since the problem was recognized as challenging. The rate (log log n / log n)² is strictly faster than the 1/log n rate achievable by simpler estimators (e.g., extreme order statistics), and the proof that noise-agnostic estimation is impossible (Section 4) establishes that exploiting the Gaussian structure of the noise is fundamentally necessary, not merely convenient. Open questions include whether the sequence-model rate gap relative to the regression setting (Remark 2) is inherent to deterministic designs, and whether the cumulant-based approach can be adapted to non-Gaussian noise with known higher-order structure.
