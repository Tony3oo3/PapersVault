## Problem

While RAG has become a standard approach to mitigate LLM hallucinations and outdated knowledge, it remains unclear how LLMs handle inter-context conflicts—contradictions between retrieved passages from the same trusted source with equal credibility. Prior work on knowledge conflicts has focused on context-memory conflicts and relied on artificially generated datasets with surface-level contradictions, leaving the behavior of LLMs on real-world, nuanced inter-context conflicts largely unexplored.

## Input / Output

Each benchmark instance provides a question paired with either a single Wikipedia passage (for single-passage RAG) or two decontextualized, contradictory Wikipedia passages (for dual-passage RAG). The LLM produces a free-form text response, which is then evaluated as correct, partially correct, or incorrect. Critically, a 'correct' response must present both conflicting answers, explicitly acknowledge their contradictory nature, and avoid indicating a preference for one over the other—merely listing both answers without noting the conflict is scored as incorrect.

## Method

WikiContradict is built from ~1,200 Wikipedia articles flagged with inconsistency maintenance tags (inconsistent, self-contradictory, contradict-other), which are verified and annotated by the authors using a Label Studio pipeline that extracts contradictory passages, resolves anaphoric references to create stand-alone texts, categorizes each instance along a four-dimensional taxonomy (semantic entity type, modality, origin, and explicit/implicit reasoning), and crafts questions that elicit different answers from each passage. Evaluation employs five prompt templates spanning internal-knowledge QA, single-passage RAG, and dual-passage RAG with and without explicit conflict-awareness instructions. For scalable evaluation, WikiContradictEval uses few-shot in-context learning to teach Llama-3-70b-instruct as a judge LLM, achieving an F-score of 0.8 against human annotations, enabling automated assessment of over 1,500 responses from seven LLMs across all 253 instances.

## Results

On WikiContradict_HumanEval (48 instances, 5 LLMs), all models struggle dramatically when given two contradictory passages: under Prompt Template 4 (no explicit conflict instruction), correct rates range from only 2.1% (Mistral-7b) to 10.4% (Llama-3-70b-inst), with GPT-4 at just 6.3%. When explicitly instructed to consider conflicting information (Prompt Template 5), Llama-3-70b-inst jumps to 43.8% correct (60.0% on explicit conflicts), while GPT-4 improves only marginally to 10.4%. In contrast, with a single non-conflicting passage (Templates 2–3), models achieve 87.8–97.6% correct, confirming that the bottleneck is specifically inter-context conflict resolution. The automated Llama-3-70b judge achieves an F-score of 0.80 against human annotations, enabling scalable evaluation across all 253 instances.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a benchmark paper evaluating five LLMs (Mistral-7b-instruct, Mixtral-8x7b-instruct, Llama-2-70b-chat, Llama-3-70b-instruct, GPT-4-turbo) rather than comparing a proposed method against baselines. The key comparison is between prompt strategies: explicitly instructing models to consider conflicting context (Template 5) yields large gains over the neutral prompt (Template 4), with Llama-3-70b-inst improving from 10.4% to 43.8% correct and Llama-2-70b-chat from 4.2% to 22.9%. GPT-4 shows the smallest improvement (6.3% → 10.4%), attributed to a 'stubborn' reliance on internal knowledge over retrieved context. Across all models, explicit conflicts are handled far better than implicit ones (e.g., Llama-3-70b-inst: 60.0% vs. 17.6% correct on Template 5), and the automated Llama-3-70b judge slightly overestimates Mistral-7b's performance (39.6% vs. 20.8% human-judged correct) due to its tendency to present both answers without clearly flagging the contradiction.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant line of LLM knowledge-conflict research, which has focused on 'context-memory conflicts' (retrieved context vs. parametric knowledge) and relied on synthetically generated contradictions via entity substitution or LLM-fabricated counterfactuals. It shifts the focus to 'inter-context conflicts' drawn from real Wikipedia inconsistencies flagged by editors, arguing that synthetic datasets fail to capture the nuance of genuine real-world conflicts. The evaluation protocol borrows the relaxed scoring mode from FreshLLMs, and the automatic judge (WikiContradictEval) applies few-shot in-context learning with Llama-3-70b-instruct, while the contradiction taxonomy adapts OntoNotes NER definitions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

WikiContradict fills a genuine gap by providing the first benchmark of real-world inter-context conflicts from a single trusted source, moving beyond the binary true/false factuality evaluation that dominates prior work. The finding that even GPT-4 achieves only ~10% fully correct responses on conflict-aware QA (and that models struggle to detect the *absence* of contradictions) highlights a substantial and underexplored weakness in current LLMs. Key limitations include English-only coverage, text-text modality restriction, and potential bias from Wikipedia's specific contradiction tags; open questions include how to properly resolve (rather than merely surface) contradictions and whether confidence scoring across sources could improve handling.
