## Problem

Training data attribution, Hessian eigenvalue estimation, and intrinsic dimension computation all require storing N-dimensional gradient or Hessian-vector-product vectors, creating prohibitive memory demands for large language models. Existing sketching methods—FJL and the Fastfood Transform (FFD)—suffer from lookup-based memory bottlenecks that cause unacceptable runtimes on TPUs, while FFD provably fails to satisfy the norm-concentration property required for valid sketching. Dense random projections, the natural alternative, scale linearly in target dimension and require custom hardware kernels.

## Input / Output

The methods consume N-dimensional gradient or HVP vectors (N being the full parameter count, e.g., ~770M for GPT-2L) and project them into D-dimensional subspaces (D typically 2^10 to 2^20), preserving inner products and norms with high probability. For TDA, the output is a D-dimensional sketched gradient per training example whose dot products approximate full-gradient influence scores; for Hessian analysis, the sketched HVP operator maps R^D → R^D and supports Arnoldi iteration to extract eigenvalues. The intrinsic dimension search produces a scalar estimate D* within a factor of 2 of the true intrinsic dimension in a single fine-tuning run, and the Hessian pipeline yields top-10 eigenvalue estimates with <5% relative mean absolute error at D=2^10.

## Method

The paper introduces three sketching algorithms—AFFD, AFJL, and QK—that eliminate the sparse-matrix and permutation lookups responsible for FJL's and FFD's poor accelerator performance, replacing them with Kronecker-product decompositions of row/column-permuted Hadamard matrices (AFFD, AFJL) or Haar-distributed orthogonal matrices (QK). AFFD achieves a 64× wall-time reduction over FFD on TPUs and AFJL a 100× reduction over FJL, while QK uses the least peak memory (1.1 GB vs 2.7–3.0 GB). Theoretically, the paper proves AFFD and QK satisfy the Johnson-Lindenstrauss norm-concentration property (Thms. 3.2, 3.3) via Hanson-Wright and orthogonal-group concentration inequalities, while showing FFD provably fails for certain inputs (Thm. 3.1). A key practical finding is that explicit sketching (materializing the gradient then projecting) outperforms implicit sketching by ~70% wall-time, and swapping the Hadamard preconditioner for FFT or the Q orthogonal preconditioner yields further 40–62% GPU speedups. The framework also enables a single-run intrinsic dimension search via progressive dimension doubling and 1k-dimensional Krylov subspaces for Hessian analysis—3 TB of fp32 storage would be needed without sketching.

## Results

On TPUv2, AFJL achieves a 100× wall-time reduction over FJL (89 ms vs. 8997 ms) and AFFD a 64× reduction over FFD (134 ms vs. 8694 ms), while QK is the fastest overall at 64 ms with only 1.1 GB peak memory. For sketching quality, achieving Pearson correlation r ≥ 0.95 on gradient inner products requires D = 2^10 for FJL/FFD/AFFD, D = 2^12 for AFJL, and D = 2^14 for QK; for Hessian eigenvalue estimation with <5% relative error, the corresponding dimensions are 2^10, 2^12, and 2^13 respectively. On the intrinsic dimension front, classification (Roberta on SNLI) yields D_int = 2^13 ≪ N, while the generative task (BART on XSUM) shows D_int approaching the full model dimension N ≈ 2^27 for Rouge2, challenging prior assumptions from classification-based studies.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against FJL [2], the proposed AFJL replaces the sparse Gaussian matrix G_s with a diagonal Gaussian G_v, eliminating the lookup bottleneck: this yields a 100× speedup on TPU (89 ms vs. 8997 ms) and a 1.96× speedup on GPU (116 ms vs. 123 ms), while also reducing peak GPU memory from 6.9 GB to 2.9 GB. Against FFD [13], AFFD removes the permutation matrix Π and uses row/column-permuted Hadamard Kronecker factors, achieving a 64× TPU speedup (134 ms vs. 8694 ms) and a 1.76× GPU speedup (205 ms vs. 197 ms is actually slower on GPU, but memory drops from 4.1 GB to 3.2 GB). Against TRAK [19] (on-the-fly dense random projections), AFFD and QK exhibit constant wall-time (~210 ms and ~85 ms respectively) across target dimensions D from 2^12 to 2^18, whereas TRAK[CUDA] scales linearly from 359 ms to 15500 ms over the same range, and TRAK[Triton] is even slower (879 ms to 12900 ms) and fails beyond D = 2^15. QK, the lightest algorithm, consistently uses the least memory (1.7 GB GPU, 1.1 GB TPU) but requires a larger sketching dimension (D = 2^14 vs. 2^10 for AFFD) to match sketching quality, consistent with its weaker theoretical concentration bound (Thm. 3.3 vs. Thm. 3.2).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper redesigns the FJL and FFD sketching algorithms for modern accelerators by eliminating lookup-based memory bottlenecks (sparse Gaussian matrices, permutation matrices) and replacing them with Kronecker-decomposed Hadamard or random orthogonal factors. It extends the Kronecker-product sketching ideas of Lotfi et al. (2022) from two factors to K factors and provides the missing theoretical proof that QK satisfies the Johnson-Lindenstrauss concentration property. The HVP sketching framework builds on Swartworth & Woodruff (2023) for eigenvalue approximation, while the intrinsic dimension search algorithm generalizes the binary-search approach used in prior FFD-based studies. A key departure is the demonstration that explicit gradient sketching outperforms the implicit (transpose-based) formulation previously assumed to be more efficient, and that FFD provably fails the sketching concentration property for certain inputs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that the intrinsic dimension of a generative summarization task (XSUM with BART) approaches the full model parameter count directly challenges the widely-held assumption from classification-based studies that D_int ≪ N, suggesting that non-linear projection methods may be needed to uncover lower-dimensional structure in generative tasks. The demonstration that layer selection distorts influence scores (correlations rarely exceeding 90%) and eigenvalue estimates (≥20% relative error) undermines a common practical shortcut in TDA. Open questions include whether the high intrinsic dimension finding generalizes beyond the single generative task tested, whether the Hessian spectral deviations from smaller-network conjectures persist at even larger scales, and whether the sketching methods transfer to non-Transformer architectures. The practical toolkit (AFFD, AFJL, QK with Jax implementations) lowers the barrier to large-scale TDA and Hessian analysis, enabling 1k-dimensional Krylov subspaces for 770M-parameter models where prior methods were limited to ~90 dimensions.
