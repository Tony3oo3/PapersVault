## Problem

Spatial transcriptomics data is fragmented across dozens of repositories in inconsistent formats, with no unified resource large enough to support large-scale deep learning or fair model comparison. The morphological information encoded in paired H&E whole-slide images is routinely overlooked in ST studies, and existing pathology benchmarks (e.g., Gleason grading, metastasis detection) have saturated, leaving no challenging task to differentiate next-generation foundation models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241703/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes H&E WSIs (converted to pyramidal TIFF at 20×, 0.5 μm/px) and raw transcript counts from Visium (55 μm spots) or Xenium (sub-cellular) assays, producing 2.1 million aligned 112×112 μm image-expression pairs and 76 million nuclei segmented and classified into five classes (neoplastic, non-neoplastic epithelial, inflammatory, stromal, necrotic). For the benchmark, each 224×224-pixel patch is mapped to the log1p-normalized expression of the top 50 most variable genes in that cancer type; for multimodal fine-tuning, 47,051 patch-expression pairs (238 common genes) from five Xenium breast cancer cases yield a fine-tuned CONCH encoder whose mean-pooled slide embedding is scored by logistic regression for ER/PR/HER2 status.

## Method

The HEST-Library unifies heterogeneous legacy ST data through a multi-stage pipeline: YOLOv8 (fine-tuned on 119 annotated fiducial regions) performs automatic corner-fiducial detection for Visium alignment, VALIS registers DAPI to H&E for Xenium, a fine-tuned DeepLabV3 (ResNet50 backbone) segments tissue from background, and CellViT segments and classifies nuclei. All expression data is converted to ANNDATA/scanpy objects with re-estimated pixel resolution, and tissue is tessellated into fixed-size patches centered on each spot. The HEST-Benchmark evaluates 11 foundation models (from 23M to 1.13B parameters) via patient-stratified k-fold cross-validation, mapping patch embeddings to gene expression through PCA(256)+Ridge regression or XGBoost, scored by Pearson correlation. For multimodal representation learning, CONCH (ViT-Base) is fine-tuned on the last 3 layers with a layer-wise learning decay of 0.7, aligned to a 3-layer MLP expression encoder via InfoNCE contrastive loss (temperature 10⁻², batch 1024) over 50 epochs with cosine scheduling.

## Results

On the HEST-Benchmark (9 tasks, 8 cancer types, 9 organs), H-Optimus-0 achieves the best average Pearson correlation of 0.4146 using PCA+Ridge regression, outperforming UNIv1.5 (0.4090) by 0.56% and ResNet50-IN (0.326) by 8.9%. Performance varies substantially across tasks, ranging from 0.6432 (SKCM) to 0.2292 (READ) for H-Optimus-0. A model scaling law is observed with R=0.81 (p<0.01) between encoder parameters and average performance, while data scaling (number of pretraining patches) shows a weaker correlation of R=0.48 (p=0.13). In the multimodal fine-tuning experiment, CONCH-FT (fine-tuned on 5 Xenium breast cancer cases) improves over CONCH on ER AUC (0.884 vs 0.881) and PR AUC (0.818 vs 0.810) on the BCNB cohort (n=1,058 WSIs).

## Baselines & Comparisons

H-Optimus-0 (ViT-giant, 1.13B params, DINOv2 on 273M patches) achieves the highest average Pearson correlation (0.4146), narrowly beating UNIv1.5 (0.4090) by 0.56% and Virchow/Virchow 2 (0.3977/0.3984) by ~1.6%. All recent DINOv2-based models (UNI, GigaPath, Virchow, Virchow 2, H-Optimus-0, UNIv1.5) consistently outperform legacy domain-specific models: CTransPath (0.3447), Phikon (0.3656), and Remedis (0.3742), with an absolute gap of 7.0% between the best and worst domain-specific models. ResNet50-IN, the only model not pretrained on histology, trails all pathology-specific models by 8.9% on average, confirming the value of domain-specific pretraining. Notably, among ViT-giant models of identical size (1.13B params), H-Optimus-0 outperforms GigaPath by 2.9% despite GigaPath being trained on 5x more patches (1.3B vs 273M), suggesting pretraining data quality and morphological diversity matter beyond raw patch count. In the multimodal fine-tuning setting, CONCH-FT marginally improves over CONCH on ER (AUC 0.884 vs 0.881) and PR (AUC 0.818 vs 0.810) but slightly underperforms on HER2 (AUC 0.724 vs 0.715), with the embedding space rank increasing from 144.66 to 146.47, indicating better latent space expressivity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from existing spatial transcriptomics analysis pipelines (Scanpy, Seurat, Squidpy) by creating a unified, deep-learning-ready dataset (HEST-1k) that standardizes 1,229 heterogeneous ST samples across formats, resolutions, and alignment protocols — a gap the authors explicitly identify as unaddressed by prior tools. For the gene-expression-prediction-from-histology task, prior work (e.g., [55, 152, 116]) was confined to cohorts of 1–10 patients; HEST-Benchmark scales this to 9 tasks across 8 cancer types and 9 organs, enabling systematic evaluation of 11 foundation models. The multimodal contrastive fine-tuning builds directly on CONCH's vision-language architecture and InfoNCE loss, repurposing them for expression-guided patch encoder specialization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

HEST-1k is likely to become a standard resource for spatial transcriptomics–histology research, given its scale (2.1M expression-morphology pairs, 76M nuclei) and the HEST-Library's tooling for continuous expansion. The HEST-Benchmark addresses a real gap: existing pathology benchmarks (Gleason grading, CAMELYON) have saturated, and no prior benchmark tested foundation models on the harder task of expression prediction. Key open questions remain: batch effects across technologies and institutions are acknowledged but not mitigated in the main results; performance on some tasks (READ, ccRCC) is very low, raising the question of whether morphology genuinely encodes expression for all cancer types; and the multimodal fine-tuning evidence rests on only 5 paired samples, limiting confidence in the CONCH-FT improvement.
