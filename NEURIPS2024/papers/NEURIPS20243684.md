## Problem

Existing multi-modal learning methods focus exclusively on either inter-modality dependencies (cross-modal interactions with the label) or intra-modality dependencies (within-modality relationships with the label), yet neither assumption holds universally across datasets. The paper identifies that the relative strength of these dependencies varies by task and is typically unknown a priori, leading to inconsistent performance where multi-modal models sometimes underperform their uni-modal counterparts or simple ensembles.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243684/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes task-specific modality pairs (e.g., image+audio for AV-MNIST, magnitude+phase k-space for FastMRI, static features+hourly time-series for MIMIC-III, image pair+text for NLVR2, image+text question for VQA-VS) and outputs a single class label. Each modality is fed independently to its own encoder, while the concatenated pair is fed to a shared multi-modal encoder; the three resulting probability distributions are combined multiplicatively (additively in log-space) to produce the final prediction.

## Method

I2M2 is derived from a generative model in which the label y generates each modality independently (p(x|y), p(x'|y)) and also induces a selection variable v that captures the statistical dependency between modalities given the label. The conditional p(y|x,x',v=1) factorizes into a product of three terms: two unimodal predictors q_x(y|x) and q_x'(y|x') capturing intra-modality dependencies, and one multimodal predictor q_{x,x'}(y|x,x') capturing inter-modality dependencies. These three classifiers are combined as a product of experts (equivalently, a log-ensemble), so the final score is the sum of their log-probabilities. The approach is modality-agnostic and requires no prior knowledge of which dependency type dominates; when one type is uninformative (e.g., NLVR2's unimodal experts at chance level), the corresponding expert contributes near-constant log-probability and is effectively ignored. Training uses a two-stage procedure: each modality-specific classifier is pre-trained independently before joint fine-tuning, which mitigates model-dominance and learner-collusion effects observed in end-to-end training.

## Results

On AV-MNIST, I2M2 with LRTF achieves 72.38% accuracy, outperforming the inter-modality LRTF baseline (71.49%), intra-modality modeling (68.63%), and image-only (64.73%). On MIMIC-III, I2M2 reaches 78.10% mortality prediction accuracy and 68.88% on ICD-9 codes 460–519, surpassing both intra- and inter-modality-only variants. On VQA-VS, I2M2 scores 68.63% IID and 48.74% OOD VQA score, improving over inter-modality modeling by 0.59% IID and 2.70% OOD. On NLVR2, I2M2 (85.36%) matches inter-modality modeling (85.29%) while both far exceed intra-modality (54.31%), confirming that I2M2 gracefully handles datasets where one dependency type dominates.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline comparison is against LRTF (Liu et al., ACL 2018), a low-rank tensor fusion method used as the inter-modality component. I2M2 with LRTF as its multi-modal expert achieves 72.38% on AV-MNIST, a 0.89-point improvement over LRTF used standalone as an inter-modality model (71.49%). More broadly, the paper's central comparison is between three modeling paradigms: intra-modality-only (product of unimodal experts), inter-modality-only (joint fusion), and I2M2 (both combined). I2M2 consistently wins or ties across all five datasets: it gains ~1% on AV-MNIST, outperforms both alternatives on FastMRI (where inter-modality actually degrades performance), improves MIMIC-III mortality by 0.24–0.44 points, matches inter-modality on NLVR2 (where intra-modality is near chance-level), and leads VQA-VS by 0.59% IID and 2.70% OOD. The key finding is that I2M2's advantage is largest when both dependency types are informative (AV-MNIST, MIMIC-III, VQA-VS) and it gracefully degrades to the better of the two when one type is uninformative (NLVR2, FastMRI).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new conceptual ground by reframing supervised multi-modal learning through a probabilistic graphical model with a selection variable, unifying two previously separate lineages—inter-modality modeling (early/intermediate fusion architectures) and intra-modality modeling (conditional independence given the label)—as special cases of a single generative process. The I2M2 framework draws on product-of-experts ensembles and the multi-view mutual information literature, but departs from prior fusion architectures by explicitly constructing separate uni-modal classifiers alongside a multi-modal classifier and combining them via a log-ensemble, rather than relying on a single fused network to implicitly capture all dependencies. The selection-variable mechanism borrows terminology from causal inference and selection bias literature, though here it encodes the strength of cross-modality interaction rather than sample selection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled explanation for the long-standing puzzle of why multi-modal models sometimes underperform their uni-modal counterparts, attributing it to incomplete generative assumptions, and offers a modality-agnostic recipe that adapts to whichever dependencies (inter- or intra-) dominate a given dataset. Practically, I2M2's ability to gracefully ignore irrelevant dependency types (e.g., disregarding inter-modality signals in fastMRI where they hurt, or intra-modality signals in NLVR2 where they are uninformative) makes it a robust default for multi-modal classification. Key open questions include the linear growth in model size with additional modalities, the initialization sensitivity that necessitates separate pre-training before joint fine-tuning, and whether end-to-end training can overcome the model-dominance and learner-collusion effects the authors identify.
