## Problem

Neural approaches to combinatorial optimization typically trade off between data adaptivity and worst-case approximation guarantees: heuristic GNNs adapt to distributions but lack provable guarantees, while exact solvers provide guarantees at exponential worst-case cost. This paper asks whether a single neural architecture can simultaneously learn to adapt to a data distribution over instances and capture algorithms with UGC-optimal worst-case approximation guarantees for Max-CSPs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242327/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The input is a constraint graph G_Λ constructed from a Max-CSP instance, where nodes correspond to variable subsets and assignments within each predicate, and edges connect nodes whose variables co-occur in a predicate. The network produces node embeddings (a fractional SDP solution) that are converted to discrete assignments via randomized hyperplane rounding at inference. Additionally, the learned embeddings yield dual certificates providing provable upper bounds on the optimal solution value, computed in a single forward pass rather than a full SDP solve.

## Method

The core insight is that the gradient of the quadratically penalized Lagrangian of the UGC-optimal SDP (Raghavendra 2008) decomposes as a message-passing update on the constraint graph, where each node's update depends only on its neighbors' embeddings. OptGNN parametrizes this message-passing algorithm with a stack of learnable linear maps M_t applied to the concatenation of a node's embedding and its aggregated neighborhood gradient, followed by normalization to the unit sphere. Training is fully unsupervised: the network minimizes the penalized Lagrangian loss over a distribution of problem instances using standard backpropagation, with no labels or expert solutions required. By construction, OptGNN with sufficient depth and embedding dimension captures the UGC-optimal approximation algorithm, and the paper proves polynomial PAC-learnability via a perturbation analysis bounding the smoothness of the cubic-polynomial aggregation function.

## Results

On Max-Cut (Table 1), OptGNN achieves cut sizes within ~0.5% of Gurobi (e.g., 2197.99 vs 2208.11 on BAa(400,500)) while outperforming Greedy by 75%+ (1255.22), with runtimes of 14–225 ms. On Max-3-SAT (Table 2), OptGNN achieves 4.46±1.68 unsatisfied clauses at r=4.00, beating ErdősGNN (5.46±1.91) and Low-Rank SDP (12.38±1.06) but trailing WalkSAT (0.14±0.36) and Survey Propagation (3.32±0.81). On Min-Vertex-Cover (forced RB instances), OptGNN consistently outperforms state-of-the-art unsupervised neural baselines and matches Gurobi with a 0.5 s time limit.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On Max-Cut, OptGNN clearly outperforms the Greedy heuristic by 75–80% across all eight datasets and is within 0.5–1% of Gurobi at equal or shorter runtimes (14–225 ms vs. 100 ms–1 s). On Max-3-SAT, OptGNN (4.46 unsatisfied clauses at r=4.00) beats ErdősGNN (5.46), the Autograd SDP baseline (6.85), and the Low-Rank SDP solver of Wang & Kolter (12.38), but is outperformed by WalkSAT (0.14) and Survey Propagation (3.32). On the GSET Max-Cut benchmark, OptGNN outperforms the Goemans-Williamson algorithm, greedy heuristics, and low-rank SDP solvers, and is competitive with neural baselines, though it does not surpass ANYCSP (Tönshoff et al., 2022), the current state-of-the-art neural method. On Min-Vertex-Cover (forced RB instances), OptGNN consistently outperforms the unsupervised baselines from Wang & Li (2023) and Brusca et al. (2023) and matches Gurobi with a 0.5 s time limit. The paper also demonstrates that OptGNN's dual certificates track SDP certificates closely (within ~20 nodes on 100-node graphs) while being ~25× faster to produce (0.02 s vs. 0.5 s).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously separate threads: the Burer-Monteiro low-rank factorization for SDPs (which provides an efficient nonconvex optimization formulation) and Raghavendra's UGC-optimal SDP relaxation for Max-CSPs. The key conceptual move is showing that gradient descent on the quadratically penalized Lagrangian of the UGC-optimal SDP naturally decomposes into a message-passing update on the constraint graph, which can then be parameterized as a GNN (OptGNN). This departs from prior neural CO work that either parametrizes heuristics without guarantees (ErdősGNN, ANYCSP) or leverages exact solvers with exponential worst-case complexity, and from theoretical results showing constant-depth GNNs cannot solve certain CO problems (Gamarnik, 2023), by demonstrating that a specific overparameterized message-passing architecture can capture provably optimal algorithms.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first result showing a polynomial-sized GNN can capture UGC-optimal approximation algorithms for the entire class of Max-CSPs, providing a principled bridge between approximation theory and neural architecture design. The practical benefit of a unified framework (implement the loss, use autograd for messages) lowers the barrier to applying SDP-based methods across problems. However, empirically OptGNN does not beat the strongest classical baselines (WalkSAT, Survey Propagation on Max-3-SAT; ANYCSP on Max-Cut), and the gap between the theoretical full-rank setting and practical low-rank deployment remains unexplained. Open questions include designing rounding procedures that preserve approximation guarantees, extending neural certificates to larger instances, and understanding whether the PAC-learning bound is tight or merely a stepping stone.
