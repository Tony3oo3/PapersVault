## Problem

Existing time series forecasting models either ignore timestamps entirely or incorporate them in ways that damage their physical significance (e.g., summing with position embeddings or embedding across time points). When real-world observations contain anomalies such as point spikes or contextual deviations (holidays, short circuits), the absence of properly modeled global temporal information causes mainstream models to produce significantly degraded predictions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240700/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

GLAFF consumes four inputs: history observations X (h×c), history timestamps S (h×6, where each timestamp is decomposed into month, day, weekday, hour, minute, second), future timestamps T (p×6), and the backbone's local prediction Ŷ (p×c). It outputs the final forecast Y (p×c) as an adaptively weighted combination of a global timestamp-derived mapping and the backbone's local prediction. The framework is model-agnostic and operates as a plug-and-play module appended to any forecasting backbone, with the only additional hyperparameter being the quantile q (default 0.75) in the Robust Denormalizer.

## Method

GLAFF employs a late-fusion (decision-level) strategy with three components. First, an Attention-based Mapper—a simplified encoder-only Transformer with stacked multi-head self-attention blocks—individually models the 6-dimensional timestamp features to capture global periodic dependencies, projecting them into initial mappings that conform to a standard distribution. Second, a Robust Denormalizer inverse-normalizes these mappings using median and inter-quartile range (IQR) rather than mean and standard deviation, making the denormalization resistant to anomalies and data drift. Third, an Adaptive Combiner (a two-layer MLP with Softmax) dynamically computes per-channel weights between the global mapping and the local backbone prediction based on the residual between the final history mapping and actual observations, allowing the model to emphasize global information when patterns are stable and local information when patterns are ambiguous. The entire framework is trained end-to-end with L2 loss and requires only ~25 MB additional memory regardless of dataset size or prediction length.

## Results

GLAFF, a plug-and-play global-local fusion framework, improves the average MSE of four mainstream forecasting backbones (Informer, DLinear, TimesNet, iTransformer) by 12.5% across nine real-world datasets spanning five domains. The improvement is most pronounced for Informer (+23.8% MSE) and DLinear (+13.1% MSE), which underutilize or ignore timestamps, while the previous SOTA iTransformer still benefits by 5.5% MSE. On the Traffic dataset (862 channels, clear weekly periodicity), GLAFF+iTransformer achieves MSE 0.2828 at prediction length 96, and on Electricity (321 channels), it reaches MSE 0.1197. GLAFF also extends to LLM-based backbones (FPT/GPT2), yielding consistent gains (e.g., 13.4% MSE reduction on Electricity with GPT2(3)).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GLAFF is evaluated as a plug-and-play add-on to four architecturally diverse backbones: Informer (Transformer, 2021), DLinear (Linear, 2023), TimesNet (Convolution, 2023), and iTransformer (Transformer, 2024, previous SOTA). GLAFF+iTransformer outperforms the standalone iTransformer by 5.5% average MSE across all nine datasets, establishing a new SOTA. The largest gains come from Informer (+23.8% MSE) and DLinear (+13.1% MSE), which either sum timestamp embeddings with data embeddings or ignore timestamps entirely, confirming that GLAFF's late-fusion strategy compensates for their underutilization of global temporal information. TimesNet, which also uses simple timestamp summation, sees a more modest 7.5% improvement. On LLM-based backbones (FPT with GPT2(3) and GPT2(6)), GLAFF similarly reduces MSE by 8–13% (e.g., Electricity: 0.194→0.168; ILI: 1.585→1.393), demonstrating generality beyond traditional architectures. The paper notes a diminishing marginal benefit as backbone modeling capacity increases, consistent with the complementary (not redundant) nature of global timestamp information and local observation modeling.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in time series forecasting where timestamps are either omitted entirely (DLinear, FPT) or fused early by summing/concatenating their embeddings with data or position embeddings (Informer, TimesNet, iTransformer). GLAFF reframes timestamps as an independent global information source modeled by a dedicated Transformer encoder, then fused at the decision level via an adaptive combiner — a late-fusion strategy previously validated in multimodal learning but not applied to time series. The Robust Denormalizer extends the reversible instance normalization idea (Kim et al., 2022) by replacing mean/std with median/quantile-range statistics to handle anomalies, and the overall architecture repurposes the standard Transformer encoder for a novel timestamp-to-observation mapping task rather than for sequence-to-sequence prediction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play design means practitioners can drop GLAFF onto any existing forecasting backbone without architectural redesign, and the demonstrated robustness to contextual anomalies (holidays) and point anomalies (short circuits) addresses a real deployment gap that benchmark-only evaluations miss. However, the diminishing returns as backbone quality increases (23.8% for Informer vs. 5.5% for iTransformer) suggest the approach is most valuable for weaker or simpler backbones, and its effectiveness likely depends on the presence of clear periodic structure in the data. Open questions include how the method generalizes to non-periodic or multi-resolution time series, whether the quantile-based denormalization remains robust under extreme distribution shifts, and whether the adaptive combiner's MLP-based weight generation can be replaced by more interpretable mechanisms.
