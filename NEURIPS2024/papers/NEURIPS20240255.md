## Problem

Deep predictive models of visual cortex learn shared nonlinear features with neuron-specific linear readouts whose weight vectors serve as functional embeddings. Because these models are highly overparameterized, it is unclear whether clustering these embeddings yields consistent, biologically meaningful cell types across different initializations and architectural choices. No prior study had systematically quantified how readout type, regularization strength, and overparameterization affect the reproducibility of embedding-based clustering.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240255/cropped_page5_idx4.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes grayscale natural images (from the Sensorium competition, recorded via two-photon calcium imaging in 7 mice) concatenated with four behavioral channels (running speed, pupil center x/y, pupil dilation, and its time-derivative) as a single input tensor. The rotation-equivariant CNN core (4 conv layers, 16 channels × 8 rotations) produces 128-dimensional feature maps, from which the readout extracts per-neuron weight vectors (embeddings) and predicted Poisson spike counts. For tuning analysis, the model is fed parametric Gabor and grating stimuli to compute four tuning indices: orientation selectivity, phase invariance, surround suppression, and cross-orientation inhibition.

## Method

The paper introduces two key modifications to the standard Gaussian-readout predictive model. First, an adaptive L1 regularization scheme replaces the uniform penalty with per-neuron coefficients β_n drawn from a log-normal hyperprior LogNormal(1, σ), so that neurons with larger learned receptive-field masks receive weaker embedding regularization and vice versa; σ controls the spread of per-neuron strengths around the global γ. Second, an iterative feature-pruning procedure masks one core output channel at a time, removes the channel whose removal causes the smallest performance drop, finetunes the reduced network, and repeats until performance degrades—reducing the core by roughly half without loss in predictive correlation. Consistency is evaluated by training three models per configuration with different seeds, orientation-aligning embeddings, clustering with k-means (k=5–100), and computing pairwise adjusted Rand indices, alongside response correlations and normalized mean absolute error of tuning indices.

## Results

The adaptively regularized Gaussian readout (γ=10, σ=0.1) achieves the best consistency–performance trade-off: a response correlation of 0.85 (matching the non-regularized Gaussian at 0.76 and exceeding the factorized readout at 0.81) while maintaining the lowest NMAE among regularized models for cross-orientation inhibition (0.81) and orientation tuning (0.81). Strong uniform L1 regularization (γ=50) pushes response correlation to 0.86 but inflates tuning-index NMAE to 1.10 (cross-orientation) and 1.26 (surround suppression), indicating biased tuning curves. Iterative feature pruning reduces core dimensionality by ~50% without performance loss and further improves ARI for regularized models, though the adaptive model does not benefit from additional pruning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Compared to the factorized readout [Klindt et al., 2017], the adaptively regularized Gaussian readout achieves higher response correlation (0.85 vs. 0.81) and substantially better ARI for embedding clustering, but the factorized readout still produces the most consistent tuning indices (NMAE 0.50–0.78 vs. 0.81–1.02 for adaptive Gaussian), suggesting the older architecture better preserves known nonlinear functional properties across seeds. Against the non-regularized Gaussian readout [Lurz et al., 2020], the adaptive regularization improves ARI consistency of neuronal clusters while matching its response correlation (0.85 vs. 0.76), and pruning the non-regularized Gaussian further raises correlation to 0.85 with comparable tuning NMAE. Strong uniform L1 regularization (γ=50, 100) on the Gaussian readout increases response correlation to 0.86 but degrades tuning-index consistency by 40–60% relative to the factorized baseline, confirming that aggressive sparsification biases predicted tuning curves toward their mean. Overall, the adaptive regularization occupies a middle ground: it recovers structured embeddings and clustering consistency lost in the non-regularized Gaussian readout without the severe tuning distortion seen under uniform L1 penalties.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two lines of work: deep predictive models of visual cortex (from Antolik et al. 2016 through the Sensorium competition baseline of Willeke et al. 2022) and the use of learned readout weights as neuronal embeddings for functional cell type discovery (Klindt et al. 2017, Ustyuzhaninov et al. 2019, 2022). It departs from prior work by systematically investigating how architectural choices—readout type (factorized vs. Gaussian), regularization strength, and feature pruning—affect the consistency of embedding-based clustering across independent model fits, a question no prior study had addressed. The adaptive per-neuron L1 regularization and iterative channel pruning are novel technical contributions that build on the factorized readout's implicit mechanism of jointly regularizing spatial mask and feature weights.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper raises a fundamental concern for the field: if neuronal embeddings are not stable across model initializations and architectures, then clustering them to define functional cell types may not yield an objective taxonomy. The finding that the older, less performant factorized readout actually reproduces biologically meaningful tuning properties more consistently than the state-of-the-art Gaussian readout challenges the assumption that higher predictive accuracy implies more faithful functional representation. The authors acknowledge that even with adaptive regularization and pruning, absolute consistency remains low, and that only one core architecture (rotation-equivariant CNN) was tested, leaving open whether identifiability can be fundamentally improved through novel architectures or training paradigms.
