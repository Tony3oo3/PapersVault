## Problem

Diffusion classifiers have demonstrated superior empirical robustness against adversarial attacks, but lack rigorous theoretical guarantees, leaving open whether their robustness is overestimated or vulnerable to future adaptive attacks. No prior work had derived certified robustness bounds for diffusion classifiers, making it impossible to quantify how much perturbation they provably withstand.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241583/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a D-dimensional RGB image in [0,1]^D corrupted with Gaussian noise at a specified level τ, along with a pre-trained conditional diffusion model (EDM UNet), and produces a class probability distribution over K classes plus a certified ℓ₂ robustness radius R. The classifier operates entirely at inference time using a single off-the-shelf diffusion model without any additional training data or adversarial training, and the certified radius is computed via the strong law of randomized smoothing using Clopper-Pearson bounds over N Monte Carlo samples.

## Method

The paper first proves that standard diffusion classifiers possess an O(1) Lipschitz constant (Theorem 3.1), establishing inherent but loose certified robustness. To obtain tighter, non-constant Lipschitzness, the authors generalize diffusion classifiers to classify Gaussian-corrupted data by deriving the ELBO for log p(x_τ|y) at arbitrary noise level τ, yielding the Exact Posterior Noised Diffusion Classifier (EPNDC). They then propose the Approximated Posterior Noised Diffusion Classifier (APNDC), which approximates the posterior q(x_t|x_{t+1},x_τ) by substituting the denoised estimate h_θ(x_τ,σ_τ) for x_0, simplifying the ELBO to a sum of pairwise reconstruction losses between h_θ(x_τ,σ_τ) and h_θ(x_t,σ_t,y). This approximation makes APNDC functionally equivalent to a free ensemble of EPNDC instances, requiring only one extra UNet forward pass. Finally, combining these noisy-data classifiers with randomized smoothing yields state-of-the-art certified radii, and variance reduction (shared noisy samples across classes) plus a sift-and-refine class-pruning algorithm reduce time complexity by 10× on CIFAR-10 and 1000× on ImageNet.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, APNDC (T'=1000) achieves 82.2%, 70.7%, and 54.5% certified accuracy at ℓ₂ radii of 0.25, 0.5, and 0.75 respectively, with 91.2% clean accuracy. These results surpass the previous state-of-the-art DensePure [47] by absolute margins of 5.6%, 6.1%, and 4.1% at the corresponding radii, while also exceeding it by 3.6% in clean accuracy. On ImageNet-64×64, APNDC with Sift-and-Refine achieves 46.3% certified accuracy at R=0.25 and 38.3% at R=0.5, substantially outperforming all prior training-free and training-based methods. Notably, these results are obtained with only N=10,000 samples (10× fewer than prior works) and a single off-the-shelf EDM diffusion model without any extra training data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

APNDC (T'=1000) decisively outperforms the previous state-of-the-art DensePure [47] (which uses ImageNet-21k as extra data) by 5.6% at R=0.25 (82.2% vs 76.6%), 6.1% at R=0.5 (70.7% vs 64.6%), and 4.1% at R=0.75 (54.5% vs 50.4%) on CIFAR-10, while also achieving 3.6% higher clean accuracy (91.2% vs 87.6%). Against Carlini et al. [2] (also using extra data), APNDC leads by 8.4% at R=0.25 and 14.5% at R=0.5. Among training-based baselines, APNDC surpasses Boosting [12] by 11.6% at R=0.25 and MACER [50] by 11.2% at R=0.25, despite requiring no adversarial training. The heuristic DiffPure+DC baseline (denoise then classify) is outperformed by APNDC by 13.4% at R=0.25 and 17.6% at R=0.5, confirming the value of the theoretically-derived ELBO over ad-hoc denoising pipelines. On ImageNet-64×64, APNDC with Sift-and-Refine achieves 46.3% at R=0.25, exceeding DensePure [47] (35.4%) by 10.9% and Carlini [2] (37.5%) by 8.8%, while using only 1/10th the sample size of prior methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the diffusion classifier framework of Chen et al. [3], which demonstrated strong empirical robustness using a single off-the-shelf diffusion model but lacked theoretical guarantees. The key conceptual departure is twofold: (1) deriving the first Lipschitz constant and certified radius for diffusion classifiers, establishing their inherent O(1) smoothness, and (2) generalizing the ELBO from τ=0 to arbitrary noise levels τ≠0, enabling the classifier to process Gaussian-corrupted inputs and thereby integrate with randomized smoothing for tighter certified bounds. The EPNDC and APNDC constructions draw on the ELBO derivations of Ho et al. [11] and Kingma et al. [17], while the certified radius computation follows the strong law of randomized smoothing from Salman et al. [38].

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work provides the missing theoretical foundation for why diffusion classifiers are empirically robust, resolving concerns about their vulnerability to future adaptive attacks. The finding that APNDC is functionally equivalent to an ensemble of EPNDC at the cost of a single extra forward pass is a practically significant efficiency insight. Open questions remain: the optimal weight in EPNDC cannot be determined theoretically, the gap between certified and empirical robustness (0.156 average certified radius vs. much higher empirical robustness) is still substantial, and whether the O(1) Lipschitz bound can be tightened further to close this gap. The 1000× time complexity reduction on ImageNet makes certified diffusion classification more tractable for large-scale deployment.
