## Problem

As LLMs proliferate with heterogeneous accuracy, cost, and latency profiles, users face the challenge of selecting which model and prompt to use for each question while respecting a long-term monetary and latency budget. Existing cascade methods like FrugalGPT lack explicit long-term budget constraints, do not jointly select prompting strategies, and ignore response-consistency signals that could inform when to stop querying. TREACLE addresses this gap by formulating LLM and prompt selection as a budget-constrained policy optimization problem.

## Input / Output

The policy consumes a state vector comprising the question's text embedding (capturing query type/difficulty), normalized frequency counts of previous responses (consistency signal), input/output token lengths, per-model-prompt re-query counts, and a normalized remaining-budget estimate computed as total remaining budget divided by (questions remaining × average per-query cost for each model-prompt pair). The action space has three discrete choices: return the current response as final, re-query the same model-prompt pair, or advance to the next model-prompt in a pre-sorted cascade. The cost function is a weighted combination of latency and monetary price (cost = latency + β × price), with β adjustable per user preference.

## Method

TREACLE trains a two-layer Deep Q-Network (DQN) under a Constrained MDP formulation, where the reward combines final-answer correctness with a scaled intermediate-response correctness term (λ=5) to avoid sparse rewards during multi-step re-querying. Two key design choices are theoretically justified: (1) model-prompt pairs are ordered in the cascade by decreasing accuracy-to-cost ratio p_k/c_k (Proposition 1), so the policy only advances to the next element in this sorted list rather than choosing freely; (2) response consistency is included as a state feature because Proposition 2 shows a simple 2-consistent policy achieves accuracy within Ω²ε/2 of the optimal budget-constrained policy. The policy is trained once with ε-greedy exploration (ε decaying from 0.9 to 0.05) and γ=0.99, then adapts to new budgets, price changes, or new LLMs via lightweight fine-tuning on a few hundred new trajectories without retraining from scratch.

## Results

TREACLE achieves up to 85% cost savings compared to individual LLM baselines while maintaining high accuracy across GSM8K, CSQA, and LLC. At a $1 budget on GSM8K, TREACLE leaves only 1 question unanswered (vs. 2–3 for baselines) and spends $0.97 of budget, closely matching the Offline Knapsack upper bound that assumes perfect knowledge of question costs and accuracies. A single trained TREACLE policy adapts to different budgets, cost parameters (α, β), and question difficulty shifts without retraining. For tight budgets ($0.05), TREACLE selectively answers only questions it can solve correctly (52.7% accuracy on answered questions vs. 23.65% for the cheapest model alone).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TREACLE consistently outperforms all practical baselines across all three datasets and budget settings. Compared to FrugalGPT [3], TREACLE wins because it jointly selects both LLM and prompt (FrugalGPT only selects LLMs), supports re-querying for response consistency, and uses the full context (text embedding, response history, remaining budget) rather than a threshold on a DistilBERT accuracy estimate. Against the Calibrated Cascade (a modified FrugalGPT using TREACLE's state vector with a threshold policy), TREACLE still wins because its RL policy can make non-threshold decisions including re-querying the same model. TREACLE also outperforms Majority Voting (fixed N=2 re-queries) by adaptively deciding when to stop re-querying based on response consistency and remaining budget. The Online Knapsack baseline, which has perfect knowledge of per-question costs and accuracies but no re-querying, is outperformed by TREACLE, particularly when question difficulty is sorted from hard-to-easy. TREACLE approaches the Offline Knapsack upper bound (which assumes perfect knowledge and no re-querying), confirming that the RL policy learns near-optimal routing decisions.

## Relation to Prior Work

TREACLE carves new ground at the intersection of LLM cascading and reinforcement learning. Prior cascade methods (FrugalGPT, AutoMix, Mixture of Thought) use threshold-based or heuristic policies to select among LLMs, but none jointly optimize model and prompt selection under a long-term budget constraint. TREACLE repurposes DQN-based RL with a rich state representation (query text embeddings, response consistency, remaining budget) to solve this joint selection problem, and provides theoretical justification for its cascade ordering (Proposition 1) and the sufficiency of 2-consistency (Proposition 2). The formulation as a budget-constrained MDP, where re-querying is an explicit action, departs from the per-query cost minimization framing of prior cascade work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

With LLM API costs a major barrier to deployment, TREACLE's demonstrated 85% cost savings while maintaining accuracy offers a practical path for cost-sensitive users to access high-quality reasoning. The framework's adaptability—requiring only ~300 samples to fine-tune when new LLMs or price changes appear—addresses a critical practical concern in the rapidly evolving LLM landscape. Limitations include the focus on reasoning tasks (extension to open-ended generation is left open), the exclusion of training data collection costs from the budget, and reliance on a fixed set of prompt types. Open questions include whether the approach scales to hundreds of LLMs, how it handles privacy-sensitive queries, and whether the DQN policy can be replaced by more sample-efficient methods.
