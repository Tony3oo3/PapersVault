## Problem

Instruction tuning data generation typically relies on a single LLM, producing stylistically homogeneous samples with a narrow difficulty range, while existing complexity-escalation methods (e.g., Evol-Instruct) can generate instructions that exceed the learning capacity of smaller target models, actually degrading their performance. The paper asks how to automatically produce diverse, high-quality instruction data whose complexity is tailored to a specific target model's capability level.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240149/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework consumes a seed dataset of instruction-response pairs (Evol-Instruct 70K or Alpaca 52K), a pool of 6 heterogeneous LLM agents (Phi-2 2.7B through Qwen 14B plus ChatGPT via API), and two evaluation models (a small target model and a larger reference model). For each seed sample, M agent-pairs are sampled to produce diverse rewrites, from which only the single highest-scoring sample is retained, yielding a same-size optimized dataset T. The dual-model IFD computation requires forward passes through both the target and reference LLMs on every candidate, and the LLM referee performs pairwise comparison against a fixed base agent-pair output.

## Method

Star-Agents operates in three coupled steps. First, an Agent-Pair strategy samples instruction-rewriting and response-generating LLMs independently (with evolving joint probabilities p_jk) to produce diverse rewrites of each seed sample, while a fixed set of base agent-pairs guarantees a quality floor. Second, a dual-model evaluation computes the IFD metric (Eq. 4) under both a small target model and a large reference model; the normalized gap between their IFD scores (Eq. 5) identifies samples at 'tailored complexity'—challenging for the small model yet within the large model's range—and this is multiplied by an LLM-referee quality score (Eq. 6) that compares each candidate against the base output to filter out noisy samples. Third, an evolutionary loop updates agent-pair sampling probabilities proportionally to their composite scores (Eq. 8) and maintains an Instruction Memory Bank that retrieves task-similar high-quality instructions via embedding similarity, biasing future sampling toward agent-pairs proven effective on analogous tasks.

## Results

On the 1B scale, Pythia-1B trained with Star Instruct achieves an average score of 4.17 across Vicuna-bench (5.93), WizardLM testset (3.90), and MT-bench (2.69), surpassing the Evol-Instruct baseline (3.73 avg) by ~12% and the Alpaca baseline (3.10 avg) by ~34%. On the 7B scale, Llama-2-7B-star_instruct reaches 6.95 average (Vicuna 8.24, WizardLM 6.87, MT-bench 5.74), outperforming Llama-2-7B-evol_instruct (6.35 avg) by ~9.4%. The paper reports up to 40% improvement on Fermi problem-solving subtasks within Vicuna-bench and MT-bench.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Star Instruct outperforms the Evol-Instruct (WizardLM) baseline on all three benchmarks at both 1B and 7B scales: at 1B, average improves from 3.73 to 4.17 (+12%), and at 7B, from 6.35 to 6.95 (+9.4%). Against the Alpaca baseline, gains are larger (3.10→4.17 at 1B; 5.01→6.95 at 7B), confirming that the tailored-complexity data is more effective than simpler self-instruct data. The IFD-based selection (Cherry LLM) underperforms Star Instruct by 0.91 points on average at 1B (3.26 vs. 4.17), and random selection trails by 0.55 points (3.62 vs. 4.17). At 7B, Llama-2-7B-star_instruct (MT-bench 5.74) surpasses all prior 7B instruction-tuned models listed, including sRecycled-Wiz-7B-v2 (5.56), XGen-7B-8k-Inst (5.55), and the DEITA-trained zephyr-beta-sft (5.32) and mpt-7B-chat (5.45). On the Open LLM Leaderboards (Appendix A.4), Llama-2-7B-star_instruct also edges out Llama-2-7B-evol_instruct on ARC (54.44 vs. 51.88) and MMLU (46.94 vs. 45.76), while matching on HellaSwag and TruthfulQA.

## Relation to Prior Work

Star-Agents sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: LLM-based instruction data generation (Evol-Instruct, Self-Instruct, Lion) and data selection for instruction tuning (IFD/Cherry LLM, DEITA, LIFT). It departs from prior generation methods by replacing a single LLM with a multi-agent 'Agent-Pair' strategy that samples diverse instruction-rewriting and response-generating LLMs, and from prior selection methods by introducing a dual-model IFD gap metric that targets 'tailored complexity' rather than absolute difficulty. The evolutionary sampling mechanism, which dynamically reweights agent-pair probabilities based on composite quality scores, is a novel addition not present in either the generation or selection literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a practical, automated pipeline for practitioners who need to optimize instruction-tuning data for a specific target model without manual curation, and the dual-model evaluation principle (data should be challenging for the small model yet within the large model's capability) provides a reusable heuristic. However, the evaluation is limited to single-turn instructions and two model families (Pythia-1B, Llama-2-7B), with no multi-turn or domain-specific validation, leaving open whether the tailored-complexity principle generalizes to longer conversations or specialized domains. The reliance on ChatGPT as both a generator and the LLM referee for quality scoring also raises questions about circularity and cost at scale.
