## Problem

Lumina-T2X (Flag-DiT) suffers from training instability at scale, slow inference, and severe extrapolation artifacts (content repetition, loss of detail) when generating images beyond its training resolution. The root causes are a lossy 1D RoPE positional encoding, uncontrolled activation growth across transformer layers, a suboptimal NTK-Aware Scaled RoPE extrapolation strategy, and the use of uniform time discretization with first-order Euler's method for flow ODE sampling.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20244171/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts natural-language text prompts (including multilingual and long-form prompts) and optionally a reference image for multi-view generation, producing images at arbitrary resolutions and aspect ratios up to 4× the training resolution in a tuning-free manner. It also generates multi-view image sets (1–8 views at 512×512), mel-spectrogram-based audio and music waveforms, and 3D point clouds of variable density. A key design choice is using a decoder-only LLM (Gemma-2B) as the text encoder rather than CLIP or T5, which enables zero-shot multilingual understanding without additional training data.

## Method

Next-DiT replaces 1D RoPE and learnable [nextline]/[nextframe] tokens with a 3D RoPE that independently encodes spatial (x, y) and temporal (z) positions, and introduces sandwich normalization (RMSNorm before and after each attention and MLP layer, with tanh gating on the post-norm scale) to control activation magnitudes across depth. For resolution extrapolation, the paper proposes Frequency-Aware Scaled RoPE (which interpolates at the wavelength boundary matching the training sequence length rather than the lowest frequency) and Time-Aware Scaled RoPE (which linearly interpolates the frequency base from position-interpolation at t=0 to NTK-Aware at t=1, aligning with the diffusion model's global-to-local denoising progression). Sampling efficiency is improved via a piecewise sigmoid time schedule that allocates larger steps near pure noise and clean data, combined with a second-order midpoint ODE solver, and a Time-Aware Context Drop that progressively reduces KV token count via spatial average pooling from 75% compression at t=0 to 0% at t=1, achieving ~2× inference speedup at 1K resolution.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K classification with flexible resolution inference, Next-DiT achieves 84.2% top-1 accuracy, substantially outperforming DeiT-base (67.2%) under the same setting. For text-to-audio generation on AudioCaps, Lumina-Next achieves the best FAD of 1.03 (vs. 1.75 for AudioLDM 2-Large) and the highest MOS-Q of 77.53 and MOS-F of 76.52. For text-to-music on MusicCaps, it attains the lowest FAD of 3.75 and the highest MOS-Q (83.56) and MOS-F (85.69), surpassing AudioLDM 2 (FAD 3.81) and MusicGen (FAD 4.50). On ImageNet-256, Next-DiT converges significantly faster than both Flag-DiT and SiT in FID and IS.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Lumina-Next clearly outperforms its predecessor Lumina-T2X on resolution extrapolation (4× and 2K generation) and ImageNet-256 convergence speed, attributed to 3D RoPE and sandwich normalization. Against SDXL and PixArt-α, Lumina-Next wins on tuning-free resolution extrapolation (both produce repetitive content beyond training resolution), few-step generation quality at 10–20 NFEs with the midpoint solver, and zero-shot multilingual generation. Among dedicated extrapolation methods, Lumina-Next surpasses MultiDiffusion (lacks global awareness), DemoFusion (requires multiple runs), and ScaleCrafter (relies on dilated convolution unsuitable for transformers). On ImageNet-1K, Next-DiT beats DeiT-base by 0.5% at fixed 224×224 (82.3% vs. 81.8%) and by a dramatic 17% under flexible resolution (84.2% vs. 67.2%). For audio generation, Lumina-Next achieves the best FAD (1.03) but slightly trails Make-An-Audio 2 and AudioLDM 2-Large on KL divergence (1.45 vs. 1.32/1.33) and CLAP score (0.630 vs. 0.645/0.652). For point cloud generation on ShapeNet, Lumina-Next is competitive but slightly behind PDiffusion on MMD (3.371 vs. 3.276 for Airplane; 12.975 vs. 12.276 for Chair) while matching or exceeding it on coverage.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper is a direct architectural successor to Lumina-T2X (Flag-DiT), systematically identifying and fixing suboptimal components: replacing 1D RoPE with 3D RoPE (building on 2D RoPE ideas from EVA-02 and FiT), introducing sandwich normalization from CogView to control activation magnitudes, and adopting grouped-query attention for efficiency. The context extrapolation strategy is explicitly grounded in the LLM length-extrapolation literature (NTK-Aware Scaled RoPE, Position Interpolation, YaRN), but the authors are the first to conduct a systematic comparison of these methods in the diffusion/flow transformer setting and to introduce frequency- and time-awareness tailored to the denoising process. The Time-Aware Context Drop simplifies Token Merging by replacing bipartite soft matching with spatial average pooling on keys and values only, and the sigmoid time schedule departs from diffusion-model schedules (EDM, Cosine) based on empirical error analysis specific to the Flow ODE.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a 2B Next-DiT with Gemma-2B outperforms the 5B Flag-DiT with LLaMA-7B predecessor while reducing both training and inference costs, and the tuning-free 2K/4K resolution extrapolation eliminates the need for expensive high-resolution retraining. The open-source release of all code and weights positions this as a reference implementation for the next generation of flow-based DiT architectures. However, the authors acknowledge a substantial gap to closed-source systems (Midjourney, DALL-E 3) in text-image alignment and aesthetics, attributed primarily to training data scale (20M vs. much larger proprietary datasets) and the absence of preference-based fine-tuning. Open questions include whether the sandwich normalization and 3D RoPE design choices generalize to video generation at scale, and whether the time-aware context drop can be made adaptive per-layer rather than per-timestep.
