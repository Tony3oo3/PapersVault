## Problem

Standard computer vision datasets (ImageNet and successors) suffer from mislabeled images, geographic/cultural biases, and poor coverage of biodiversity-relevant taxa, while existing curated biodiversity datasets (iNat2021, BIOSCAN-1M, TREEOfLIFE-10M) remain limited in scale and taxonomic diversity. There is a critical need for a large, accurately annotated, AI-ready dataset spanning multiple kingdoms and taxonomic levels to enable robust zero-shot species identification across diverse ecosystems.

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB biodiversity image and a text prompt constructed from dual-language annotations (common name + scientific name + flattened taxonomic hierarchy) as input, producing a zero-shot classification score over species-level labels. The text prompts are deliberately structured to pair Latin scientific names with English common names and hierarchical taxonomic context, addressing the low-resource nature of Latin in text encoders. Evaluation spans 7 taxonomic categories (Aves, Arachnida, Insecta, Plantae, Fungi, Mollusca, Reptilia) across 8 benchmarks with class counts ranging from 9 (DeepWeeds) to 2,253 (BIOTROVE-BALANCED).

## Method

The paper curates 161.9M research-grade images from iNaturalist into an AI-ready parquet format with reconstructed common names (via DarwinCore Archive cross-referencing), full taxonomic hierarchies, and S3 URLs, then trains CLIP-style vision-language models via contrastive learning on a 40M-image, 33K-species subset (BIOTROVE-TRAIN). Three model variants are trained using transfer learning: ViT-B/16 from OpenAI CLIP (40 epochs), ViT-B/16 from BioCLIP (8 epochs), and ViT-L/14 from MetaCLIP (12 epochs), all using AdamW with cosine scheduling and bfloat16 precision on 8×H100 GPUs. A semi-global shuffling strategy organizes data into ~50K-sample mini-batches to mitigate the long-tailed species distribution inherent in citizen-science data. The paper also introduces three new non-overlapping benchmarks (BIOTROVE-BALANCED, BIOTROVE-UNSEEN, BIOTROVE-LIFESTAGES) to evaluate generalization to balanced species sets, rare unseen species, and intra-species life-stage variation.

## Results

The best BIOTROVE-CLIP variant (BT-CLIP-O, initialized from OpenAI CLIP) achieves a weighted-average zero-shot top-1 accuracy of 70.8% across eight benchmarks, substantially outperforming the OpenAI CLIP baseline (14.7%) and MetaCLIP (25.0%). Its standout result is 91.1% top-1 accuracy on BIOTROVE-BALANCED (2,253 species), demonstrating strong fine-grained classification. However, BioCLIP (58.5% weighted avg) retains advantages on rare/unseen species benchmarks (68.2% on BIOTROVE-UNSEEN, 30.2% on BioCLIP-Rare) and on Birds-525 (68.7%), while MetaCLIP leads on the Life-Stages benchmark (64.3%). All models perform at or below random chance on the Confounding Species benchmark.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BT-CLIP-O (70.8% weighted avg) decisively outperforms both OpenAI CLIP (14.7%) and MetaCLIP (25.0%) across nearly all benchmarks, with the largest margin on BIOTROVE-BALANCED (91.1% vs. 7.3% and 15.4%). Against BioCLIP (58.5%), BT-CLIP-O wins on BIOTROVE-BALANCED (91.1% vs. 62.2%) and the overall weighted average, but BioCLIP retains clear advantages on BIOTROVE-UNSEEN (68.2% vs. 47.1%), BioCLIP-Rare (30.2% vs. 22.9%), Fungi (45.1% vs. 43.2%), Insects-2 (20.8% vs. 16.5%), Birds-525 (68.7% vs. 47.8%), and DeepWeeds (19.9% vs. 17.0%). The authors attribute BioCLIP's edge on rare/unseen species to its training on ~450K species (TREEOfLIFE-10M) versus BT-CLIP's 33K, suggesting a breadth-of-taxa advantage. MetaCLIP uniquely leads on the Life-Stages benchmark (64.3%), highlighting that general-purpose web-trained CLIP models retain strengths in hybrid linguistic-visual tasks that domain-specialized models lose. On the Confounding Species benchmark, all models—including all baselines—perform at or below random chance, indicating a shared limitation for visually similar species discrimination.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the biodiversity vision-language dataset lineage (iNat2021 → BIOSCAN-1M → TREEOfLIFE-10M) by curating BIOTROVE, a 161.9M-image dataset from iNaturalist that is ~13.5× larger than TREEOfLIFE-10M while maintaining comparable species diversity. Methodologically, it follows the CLIP training paradigm (Radford et al., 2021) and the dual-language labeling strategy from BioCLIP (Stevens et al., 2023), initializing from OpenAI CLIP, BioCLIP, and MetaCLIP checkpoints. The key conceptual departure is the emphasis on an AI-ready curation pipeline (BIOTROVE-PROCESS) that handles class imbalance, taxonomic filtering, and semi-global shuffling, positioning the dataset as immediately usable rather than requiring substantial preprocessing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

BIOTROVE's scale (161.9M images, 366.6K species) and the accompanying pipeline make it a likely standard resource for biodiversity AI research, enabling applications in pest control, crop monitoring, and conservation that were previously constrained by data availability. However, the mixed benchmark results—where BioCLIP (trained on 450K species) still outperforms BIOTROVE-CLIP (33K species) on unseen and rare species—raises an open question about whether horizontal scaling (more species) or vertical scaling (more images per species) is more critical for generalization. The finding that all models perform at or below random chance on the confounding-species benchmark highlights a fundamental limitation in current fine-grained recognition that remains unsolved.
