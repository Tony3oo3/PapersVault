## Problem

Prior online control results with adversarial (non-stochastic) disturbances apply only to discrete-time systems, while existing continuous-time online control work assumes Brownian motion noise that may not hold in practice. This paper asks whether sublinear regret can be achieved for continuous-time linear systems under fully adversarial, non-stochastic disturbances, and provides the first nonasymptotic regret bound in this setting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241524/cropped_page7_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes the system state x_t at finite sampling intervals of length h, the known dynamics matrices A and B, and estimated disturbances computed from consecutive state differences over each interval (rather than point-wise noise). It outputs a piecewise-constant control action u_t held fixed over each interval [rh, (r+1)h], structured as a fixed strongly stable feedback gain K plus a weighted sum of past estimated disturbances. The theoretical guarantee is a regret bound of O(√T log T) relative to the best fixed strongly stable linear controller in hindsight.

## Method

The paper proposes a two-level online controller: a higher-level OCO-with-memory loop updates the Disturbance-Action Policy (DAC) parameter M_t once every m = Θ(1/h) steps, while a lower-level DAC policy applies high-frequency feedback using interval-based disturbance estimates that cancel the second-order Taylor residue of the continuous dynamics. The key technical challenge is bounding states and their derivatives in continuous time (where only a differential equation is available, not a recurrence), solved via Gronwall's inequality combined with induction. The two-level structure with the skip parameter m decouples the OCO memory horizon from the discretization grid, allowing H = Θ(log T) memory steps without inflating the OCO regret beyond O(√T log T). Empirically, the 'stack & skip' principle—augmenting the current observation with h past observations sampled every m steps—is applied to SAC in domain randomization environments (Hopper, Half-Cheetah, Walker2D in MuJoCo), consistently outperforming standard SAC across all tested parameter variations.

## Results

Theoretically, the proposed two-level online controller achieves an O(√T log T) regret bound for continuous-time linear systems with adversarial disturbances, matching the discrete-time rate. Empirically, the Stack & Skip SAC algorithm (frame stack h=3, frame skip m=3) consistently outperforms standard SAC across all three MuJoCo locomotion benchmarks (Hopper, Half-Cheetah, Walker2D) under domain randomization. Ablation studies show that increasing the frame stack number from 1 to 3 yields significant performance gains, while the optimal frame skip number is 3 (both smaller and larger values degrade performance).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares its Stack & Skip SAC algorithm against standard SAC trained in the same domain randomization environments. The proposed method outperforms the standard SAC baseline in all evaluated test environments across Hopper, Half-Cheetah, and Walker2D (Figures 5 and 6). The advantage is consistent across different domain parameter perturbations (joint damping, foot friction, torso size, density), indicating that leveraging past observations with strategic skipping provides a robust adaptation mechanism. The improvement is most pronounced in environments where the domain randomization introduces larger distributional shifts, confirming that the DAC-inspired policy (using historical state information) better handles the fixed-but-unknown per-episode disturbances characteristic of domain randomization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the discrete-time online non-stochastic control framework of Agarwal et al. (ICML 2019) and Cohen et al. (ICML 2018) to continuous-time linear systems, where no prior non-asymptotic analysis existed. The core technical contribution is a two-level architecture: a high-level OCO-with-memory loop (from Anava et al., NeurIPS 2015) that updates the Disturbance-Action Policy parameter at low frequency, and a low-level DAC feedback controller that operates at the sampling rate to counteract discretization error. The paper also draws a conceptual bridge to domain randomization for sim-to-real transfer, framing the fixed-but-unknown per-episode environment parameters as adversarial disturbances in a non-stochastic control problem, and validates this framing empirically using a SAC variant (Nikishin et al., ICML 2022) with frame stacking and skipping.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result provides the first non-asymptotic regret bound (O(√T log T)) for continuous-time linear systems under adversarial disturbances, closing a gap between the well-studied discrete-time setting and the continuous dynamics that govern real physical systems. The practical implication—that frame stacking with strategic skipping (h=3, m=3) consistently improves SAC in domain randomization environments—gives theoretical grounding to a widely used but poorly understood RL heuristic. Key limitations include the assumption of known linear dynamics and convex costs; extending to unknown dynamics, nonlinear systems, or competitive-ratio objectives are natural next steps. The connection to sim-to-real transfer suggests a broader program of using online control theory to analyze and improve domain randomization training.
