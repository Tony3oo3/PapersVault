## Problem

Existing LLM pruning methods (Wanda, SparseGPT) apply a uniform sparsity ratio across all layers, ignoring the well-known fact that front layers are more critical than later ones. Non-uniform alternatives like OWL rely on handcrafted empirical rules with manually tuned hyperparameters, while BESA requires hours of differentiable optimization. The paper asks how to automatically discover effective, model-agnostic sparsity allocation functions without expensive trial-and-error or expert design.

## Input / Output

The method consumes element-wise importance scores S(W_l, X_l, G_l) already computed by an existing pruning metric (e.g., Wanda's weight×activation product or SparseGPT's Hessian-based score) along with a global sparsity budget C, and produces a per-layer sparsity ratio φ_l for each layer l, which is then thresholded into a binary sparse mask M_l of the same shape as W_l. The discovered allocation function is a single composition of four operations (pre-process → reduction → transform → post-process) that is transferable across model architectures, sparsity levels, and downstream tasks without re-searching, requiring only ~0.5 day on one GPU to discover.

## Method

DSA formulates sparsity allocation as an expression-discovery problem: the allocation function A maps element-wise scores to per-layer importances V via a pre-process T_pre (e.g., log, abslog, normalize) followed by a reduction T_reduce (e.g., geometric mean, variance, entropy), then maps V to sparsity ratios φ via a transform T_trans (e.g., cosine, sigmoid, exponential) and a post-process T_post (e.g., exp, rank). An evolutionary algorithm searches this ~10⁴-combination space by initializing a diverse population of operation compositions, evaluating each candidate's pruned-model perplexity on a held-out validation set, and iteratively applying crossover (swapping beneficial sub-expressions between top performers) and mutation (randomly perturbing one operation slot). Acceleration via program checking, memoization, parallel evaluation, and surrogate models keeps the search tractable. The top discovered function (e.g., V* = geometric_mean(|ln(ln(S))|), φ* = exp(cos(V*))) is then plugged into any existing uniform pruning method as a drop-in replacement for the fixed sparsity ratio, yielding up to 14.58% accuracy gains on LLaMA-3 8B under magnitude pruning and 10.65% under SparseGPT at 50% sparsity.

## Results

At 50% sparsity on 7 zero-shot tasks, DSA integrated with SparseGPT achieves the largest gains: 10.65% on LLaMA-3 8B (62.41% vs 51.77%), 6.18% on LLaMA-2 13B (64.10% vs 57.92%), and 4.73% on LLaMA-1 7B (58.33% vs 53.60%). With magnitude pruning, DSA yields a peak 14.58% gain on LLaMA-3 8B (55.50% vs 40.91%). On WikiText-2 perplexity at 80% sparsity for LLaMA-1-7B, DSA achieves 736.81, substantially outperforming OWL (1002.87), BESA (2208.75), and Uniform (3499.88).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DSA consistently outperforms all three uniform pruning baselines (Magnitude, Wanda, SparseGPT) across every model and sparsity level tested, with the most dramatic gains on LLaMA-3 8B where SparseGPT+DSA improves by 10.65% and Magnitude+DSA by 14.58%. Against the non-uniform allocation baselines on WikiText-2 perplexity (Table 5), DSA achieves the lowest perplexity at every sparsity level from 65% to 80%: at 80% sparsity, DSA reaches 736.81 versus OWL's 1002.87, BESA's 2208.75, Uniform's 3499.88, ER's 11151.18, and ER-plus's 6013.91. OWL is the closest competitor, with DSA maintaining a 3.6% perplexity advantage at 65% sparsity (12.62 vs 13.05) that widens to 26.6% at 80% sparsity. BESA, while competitive at moderate sparsity (18.52 at 65%), degrades rapidly at higher sparsity, confirming DSA's superior robustness under aggressive pruning. On multimodal benchmarks (VQAv2, SQA, VQA) with LLaVA-1.5/Vicuna-7B at 50% sparsity, DSA surpasses Wanda by 0.36%, 1.58%, and 1.31% respectively, and outperforms SparseGPT by 0.22%, 1.65%, and 0.67%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes AutoML expression discovery and evolutionary search for the problem of non-uniform layer-wise sparsity allocation in LLMs, a task previously addressed only through hand-designed heuristics (OWL) or expensive iterative optimization (BESA). It builds directly on the uniform pruning metrics from Wanda, SparseGPT, and magnitude-based methods, treating their element-wise importance scores as input to a discovered allocation function rather than applying a single global sparsity ratio. The conceptual departure is framing the allocation function itself as a searchable object over a space of pre-process, reduction, transform, and post-process operations, rather than fixing the functional form a priori as prior non-uniform methods do.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DSA provides a practical, training-free, plug-and-play mechanism that can be layered onto any existing uniform pruning method without modification, and the discovered allocation function transfers across tasks and models without re-searching (search cost ~0.5 GPU-day). The main limitation is that the search still requires a validation set and GPU resources, and the paper does not explore whether the discovered functions generalize to entirely new model families or to structured sparsity patterns beyond what is tested. Open questions include whether the evolutionary search can be made more sample-efficient, whether domain knowledge can further constrain the search space, and whether the discovered functions reveal interpretable principles about layer importance in LLMs.
