## Problem

When LLMs encounter contradictions between their parametric knowledge (encoded in weights) and new information provided in the prompt context, they tend to default to their internal knowledge, producing hallucinated or outdated answers. Existing remedies—fine-tuning, model editing, and contrastive decoding—either require expensive retraining, target only specific knowledge entries, or operate at the coarse level of output probabilities without offering neuron-level interpretability. This paper asks whether specific neurons within FFN layers are responsible for processing contextual cues, and whether selectively amplifying them can redirect the model toward context-faithful generation.

## Input / Output

The method takes a (context, question) pair as input, where the context contains potentially conflicting or updated knowledge and the question is either a completion prompt or a multiple-choice question with options. During the offline identification phase, the model is run twice per example—once with question only and once with context plus question—to record FFN neuron activations in both settings. The output is a set of reweighted FFN weights (identified context-aware neurons scaled by a factor β), which are then used at inference time to produce context-faithful completions or answer selections without any additional inference-time cost.

## Method

IRCAN operates in three phases. First, it computes a context-aware attribution score for every FFN neuron using integrated gradients: the activation of each neuron is linearly interpolated from its question-only value to its context-plus-question value, and the accumulated gradient of the correct-answer probability along this path yields the score (Eq. 2–3, with m=20 Riemann steps). Second, it identifies context-aware neurons by retaining those above a 10% threshold, selecting the top 20 per example, and then taking the top h neurons by cross-example co-occurrence count, producing a fixed set shared across all inputs. Third, it reweights the identified neurons by multiplying their FFN weight matrices by a scalar β (typically 2–20), amplifying their influence on information flow during generation. The key distinction from prior work is the neuron-level granularity: unlike CAD, which adjusts final output probabilities, IRCAN intervenes at the representational level within FFNs, providing interpretability and enabling seamless combination with CAD (IRCAN-CAD) for further gains.

## Results

On the MemoTrap completion task, IRCAN achieves 56.08% accuracy on LLaMA-2-7B (vs. 24.52% for the original model, a 129% relative improvement) and 47.76% on LLaMA-3-8B (vs. 20.26%, a 136% improvement). When combined with CAD (IRCAN-CAD), accuracy further rises to 61.83% on LLaMA-2-7B and 54.37% on LLaMA-3-8B. On the multiple-choice tasks (COSE_KRE and ECARE_KRE), IRCAN-CAD sets new state-of-the-art results, e.g., 80.96% on ECARE_KRE with LLaMA-2-7B-Chat (vs. 55.04% original). General-ability benchmarks (ARC, MMLU, GSM8K, etc.) show negligible degradation, with average scores dropping by less than 3 points for most models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IRCAN substantially outperforms CAD (Shi et al., 2023) on the MemoTrap completion task across all five models, e.g., 56.08% vs. 44.56% on LLaMA-2-7B and 47.76% vs. 39.66% on LLaMA-3-8B; combining the two (IRCAN-CAD) yields further gains, confirming they operate at complementary granularities (neuron-level vs. output-probability-level). ITI (Li et al., NeurIPS 2023) performs poorly: the Mass Mean Shift variant causes complete failure (0% accuracy) on Gemma-2B and Amber, and even the Probe Weight Direction variant lags IRCAN by 20+ points on LLaMA-2-7B. On the multiple-choice task, Opin (Zhou et al., 2023) achieves mixed results (e.g., 60.23% on COSE_KRE with LLaMA-3-8B-Instruct but only 55.04% on ECARE_KRE with LLaMA-2-7B-Chat), while IRCAN-CAD consistently leads or matches the best baseline. Prompt-engineering variants (Based_on, Utilizing_Formatted) provide only marginal or inconsistent gains and are outperformed by IRCAN on most model–dataset combinations.

## Relation to Prior Work

IRCAN extends the knowledge-neuron paradigm (Dai et al., ACL 2022) from factual knowledge storage to context processing, using integrated gradients (Sundararajan et al., ICML 2017) as the attribution mechanism to identify which FFN neurons respond to contextual input. It departs from contrastive decoding methods like CAD, which operate at the output-probability level, by intervening at the finer granularity of individual neurons, thereby offering interpretability. It also contrasts with inference-time intervention (ITI), which shifts activations along a global direction, by targeting a small set of specific neurons for reweighting. The paper positions itself as complementary to CAD rather than a replacement, demonstrating that the two can be combined for further gains.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

IRCAN provides a lightweight, interpretable, and plug-and-play mechanism for improving LLM faithfulness to context without any inference-time overhead, which is practically attractive for deployment in RAG and knowledge-conflict scenarios. However, the evaluation is limited to synthetic datasets (MemoTrap, COSE_KRE, ECARE_KRE), and the method shows diminished or even negative effects on more capable models (LLaMA-3-8B-Instruct, LLaMA-2-13B-Chat) on multiple-choice tasks, possibly due to noisy ChatGPT-generated context. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to long-context settings, real-world RAG pipelines, and whether the identified context-aware neurons remain stable across different domains and knowledge types.
