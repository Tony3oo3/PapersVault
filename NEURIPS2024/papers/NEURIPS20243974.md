## Problem

Visualizing posterior uncertainty in high-dimensional inverse problems (e.g., image inpainting, denoising) is challenging: posterior sampling is computationally expensive and produces large sample sets that are hard to navigate, while PCA-based projections capture only a small fraction of reconstruction error. No existing method provides an efficient, hierarchical summary of the posterior that lets a user interactively explore uncertainty at multiple granularities in a single forward pass.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243974/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives a single degraded measurement (noisy image, masked/partial image, grayscale image, or edge map) at resolutions ranging from 32×32 (MNIST) to 256×256 (CelebA-HQ, Edges-to-Shoes) or 128×128 patches (bioimaging). It outputs K^d image predictions (the tree leaves) plus K^d probability scalars in one forward pass; all intermediate tree nodes up to the root (the MMSE point estimate) are then computed in closed form via recursive weighted averaging, requiring no additional network evaluations.

## Method

A U-Net with group convolutions in the decoder predicts K^d leaf images and their probabilities simultaneously; the full tree of degree K and depth d is then assembled bottom-up using the law of total expectation, so each parent is a probability-weighted average of its K children and the root equals the posterior mean. Training employs a hierarchical oracle (winner-takes-all) loss: the ground-truth image is propagated down the tree, and at each level the MSE to the nearest child is accumulated, which amortizes a hierarchical centroidal Voronoi tessellation of the posterior. An annealed epsilon (Eq. 8) scales gradients of non-selected predictions to prevent hypothesis collapse, and a non-stochastic weighted sampler rebalances leaf visitation frequencies for imbalanced posteriors when using Adam. The entire tree is produced in a single ~14 ms forward pass with 1.3 GB memory, achieving PSNR and NLL comparable to a two-step baseline of diffusion-based posterior sampling plus hierarchical K-means while being 10³–10⁷× faster.

## Results

On FFHQ (100 test images), posterior trees achieve PSNR of 24.6/25.7/26.4 dB at depths 0/1/2 for colorization, 20.1/20.5/20.4 dB for mouth inpainting, and 19.6/19.9/19.8 dB for eyes inpainting, with sample NLL of 0.9/2.0, 0.9/2.0, and 1.3/2.5 nats respectively at depths 1/2. The method requires a single forward pass of ~14 ms with 1.3 GB memory on an A6000 GPU, compared to 68–15,538 s and 3,700–845,450 GB for the diffusion/GAN-based baselines. In batched settings, the method infers ~430 test images in 1.15 s, a 557× speedup over the fastest diffusion baseline (DDRM).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares posterior trees against four posterior-sampling baselines (DDNM, DDRM, RePaint, MAT) followed by hierarchical K-means clustering. On colorization, the method matches DDNM on PSNR (24.6 vs 24.6 at d=0; 26.4 vs 26.1 at d=2) and NLL (0.9 vs 0.9 at d=1) while being ~24,000× faster (0.014 s vs 340 s) and using 14,000× less memory. On mouth inpainting, the method achieves the best PSNR at d=0 (20.1 dB, beating RePaint's 19.8 and DDNM's 19.3) and ties RePaint at d=1 (20.5 dB), though RePaint edges ahead at d=2 (20.7 vs 20.4 dB); NLL is best for the proposed method (0.9 vs 1.0 for RePaint). On eyes inpainting, RePaint leads on PSNR (20.1 vs 19.6 at d=0) and NLL (1.0 vs 1.3 at d=1), but the proposed method is ~1,100,000× faster. Across all tasks, the speed advantage ranges from 10³× (vs MAT) to 10⁷× (vs RePaint), with memory reduced by 3–6 orders of magnitude, making interactive uncertainty exploration feasible in real time.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Multiple Choice Learning (MCL) from a flat set of K predictions to a hierarchical tree of degree K and depth d, where each node's prediction is the weighted mean of its children (a direct generalization of the CVT interpretation from Rupprecht et al. [45]). The key architectural insight is that only the K^d leaves need to be predicted by the network; all parent nodes are recovered in closed form via bottom-up weighted averaging, making the entire tree a single forward pass. This departs from the dominant posterior-sampling paradigm (diffusion models like DDNM, DDRM, RePaint) which requires hundreds of iterative denoising steps per sample, and from PCA-based uncertainty visualization which projects onto a low-dimensional subspace. The weighted sampler and epsilon-annealing regularization address known failure modes of MCL training (hypothesis collapse, imbalanced leaf quality) in a principled way.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method provides a practical, real-time (≈14 ms) alternative to diffusion-based posterior sampling for uncertainty visualization in safety-critical imaging tasks (medical, bioimaging), where users need to explore multiple plausible reconstructions without waiting minutes per image. Key limitations include the fixed tree layout (K, d must be chosen a priori), the fact that prototypes are cluster centers rather than realistic images (especially at shallow depths), and the limited tree depth achievable in a single forward pass. Open directions include input-adaptive tree structures, application in VQ-VAE latent space for sharper prototypes, and iterative inference for deeper trees.
