## Problem

Existing shared component analysis (SCA) methods provably identify modality-invariant latent components only when cross-modality samples are aligned/paired, a condition that is often unavailable in cross-lingual retrieval, domain adaptation, and biological data translation. The prior unaligned SCA result [8] relies on ICA with stringent assumptions (element-wise independence of all latent components, at most one Gaussian), which are overly restrictive when only the shared subspace (not individual components) needs to be recovered. This paper asks whether shared components can be provably identified from unaligned multimodal linear mixtures under considerably milder conditions.

## Input / Output

The method consumes unpaired samples from two modalities/domains, typically as pre-trained encoder embeddings (2048-dim ResNet50 features for images, 300-dim fastText vectors for word embeddings, or raw 815/2613-dim RNA/ATAC sequences). In the weakly supervised variant, as few as d_C aligned pairs are additionally provided as a regularization term. The output is a pair of linear projection matrices Q^(1) and Q^(2) (or a single shared Q in the homogeneous-mixing case) whose products Q^(q)x^(q) yield the shared component representation Theta*c, usable directly for downstream classification, k-NN alignment, or cross-lingual retrieval.

## Method

The core mechanism replaces the sample-level matching objective of CCA with a distribution matching (DM) constraint: find Q^(q) such that Q^(1)x^(1) and Q^(2)x^(2) have identical distributions, enforced via a GAN-style adversarial loss with a whitening regularizer. The key identifiability guarantee rests on a 'modality variability' assumption (Assumption 1) that characterizes when the joint distributions P_{c,p^(1)} and P_{c,p^(2)} differ on all linearly-constructed stripe sets, ensuring the DM constraint forces Q^(q)A^(q) = [Theta, 0]. To further relax conditions on the shared component c, two structural constraints are introduced: (i) a homogeneous mixing model A^(1)=A^(2) (motivated by pre-trained encoders mapping data to a shared linearly separable space) eliminates the need for any distributional assumptions on c, and (ii) weak supervision with at least d_C aligned pairs pins down the rotation ambiguity Theta^(1)=Theta^(2) without requiring independence or hyper-rectangle support conditions. The theoretical results are validated on synthetic data and three real-world tasks: domain adaptation (Office-31, Office-Home), single-cell RNA/ATAC alignment (A549), and cross-lingual word retrieval (MUSE).

## Results

On Office-31 domain adaptation, the proposed method achieves 87.3% average target-domain classification accuracy, outperforming the best baseline (MCC at 86.3%) by 1.0% and showing the largest gains on A→W (90.4% vs. 88.6% for SDAT) and A→D (90.1% vs. 89.9% for MDD). On Office-Home, the average accuracy reaches 68.7%, surpassing the best baseline MDD (66.8%) by 1.9%, with the most notable improvement on Ar→Cl (51.0% vs. 48.5% for ELS). For cross-lingual word retrieval on MUSE, the proposed method with CSLS scoring achieves 54.9 average P@1, a 9.8-point improvement over the Adv baseline (45.1), with gains exceeding 10 points on low-resource pairs like en→ar (29.1 vs. 16.7) and vi→en (33.0 vs. 4.4). On single-cell RNA/ATAC alignment (A549), the method achieves roughly 3× higher k-NN accuracy than the CMAE baseline with zero paired samples, and ~5× with just one paired sample.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method consistently outperforms all five domain adaptation baselines (DANN, MDD, MCC, SDAT, ELS) on Office-31, with the largest margins on A→W (+1.8% over SDAT) and A→D (+3.8% over SDAT), while the gap narrows on easier transfers like D→W (+0.2% over MDD). On Office-Home, the advantage is more pronounced, with the proposed method beating the best baseline by 2.5% on Ar→Cl and 4.8% on Ar→Pr. For cross-lingual retrieval, the proposed method (using the homogeneous-domain model with a single shared Q) substantially outperforms the Adv baseline [Lample et al., ICLR 2018] on low-resource language pairs (en→ar: +12.4, vi→en: +28.6 P@1) but shows a smaller or negative gap on high-resource pairs like it→en (66.0 vs. 63.8 for CSLS, but 51.9 vs. 55.2 for NN). On single-cell alignment, the proposed method with zero paired samples already achieves ~3× the k-NN accuracy of the CMAE baseline [Yang et al., 2021], and adding just d_C=256 paired samples (per Theorem 3) further improves accuracy steadily, whereas CMAE requires 256–770 paired samples plus additional class labels.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the aligned shared component analysis (SCA) framework of CCA-based identifiability [1, 2] to the unpaired/unaligned setting, where cross-modality samples are not matched. The most directly related prior work is Sturma et al. [8], which also tackles unaligned SCA but relies on ICA identifiability conditions (element-wise independence of all latent components, at most one Gaussian). The key departure here is replacing ICA with a distribution-matching (adversarial) loss and a geometric 'modality variability' condition that is substantially milder, while also introducing structural constraints (homogeneous mixing matrices, weak supervision) to further relax assumptions. The modality variability condition is also distinguished from the 'domain variability' in [32, 41] by restricting to a smaller class of stripe-shaped sets, making it easier to satisfy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first provable identifiability guarantees for unaligned SCA that do not require ICA-level independence assumptions on all latent components, which is practically significant for applications like cross-lingual retrieval, domain adaptation, and single-cell multi-omics alignment where paired data are scarce or unavailable. The weak supervision result (only d_C paired samples needed vs. d_C + d_P^(1) + d_P^(2) for CCA) offers a practical recipe for leveraging minimal auxiliary data. Key limitations include the linear mixture model assumption (requiring pre-processing via pretrained encoders), the sufficient-but-not-necessary nature of the identifiability conditions, and the sensitivity of GAN-based optimization to hyperparameters. Open questions include extending to nonlinear mixtures, finite-sample analysis, and developing more stable distribution-matching objectives.
