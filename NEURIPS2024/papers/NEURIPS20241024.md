## Problem

While LMMs have advanced IQA through absolute quality rating, directly fusing multiple IQA datasets for training is fundamentally flawed because each dataset has a different perceptual scale due to varying subjective testing methodologies. The paper addresses how to leverage the reliability of relative quality comparisons to seamlessly combine diverse IQA datasets and how to convert discrete comparative levels into continuous perceptual quality scores.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241024/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the model takes a single test image and compares it against five pre-selected anchor images (one per quality interval, chosen to minimize MOS variance from KonIQ-10k). The LMM processes each test-anchor pair with a text instruction and outputs a qualitative comparative level (inferior, worse, similar, better, superior). These five token probabilities are aggregated via a weighted softmax into a probability matrix, from which a continuous quality score is derived through MAP estimation under Thurstone's Case V model.

## Method

During training, the paper generates 180K comparative instruction-response pairs by comparing MOSs within each IQA dataset (avoiding cross-dataset scale ambiguity), using an empirical rule with ±σ and ±2σ thresholds to assign five discrete comparative levels. The mPLUG-Owl2 architecture (CLIP-ViT-L14 encoder + LLaMA-2-7B decoder with a visual abstractor reducing tokens to 65) is fine-tuned on these pairs. At inference, a soft comparison method replaces binary 2AFC by computing the likelihood of the test image being preferred over multiple anchor images via a weighted summation of softmax-transformed log probabilities across all five comparative tokens. The resulting probability matrix (rather than a traditional count matrix) is fed into MAP estimation under Thurstone's Case V model to yield the final continuous score. This probability-matrix-based conversion is shown to improve accuracy not only for Compare2Score but also for zero-shot general-purpose LMMs.

## Results

On the six standard IQA datasets (intra-dataset setting), Compare2Score achieves median SRCC of 0.972 (LIVE), 0.950 (CSIQ), 0.952 (KADID-10k), 0.919 (BID), 0.914 (CLIVE), and 0.931 (KonIQ-10k), outperforming all baselines on SRCC for four of six datasets and on PLCC for all six. Under the cross-dataset generalization setting, Compare2Score achieves SRCC of 0.823 (TID2013), 0.906 (SPAQ), and 0.730 (AGIQA-3K), surpassing the strongest baselines LIQE and Q-Align by 1.2–9.3 percentage points. The probability-matrix-based soft comparison also boosts zero-shot LMMs (e.g., Co-Instruct from 0.582 to 0.822 SRCC on LIVE), demonstrating its intrinsic effectiveness beyond the trained model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Compare2Score outperforms the strongest learning-to-rank baseline LIQE [10] on SRCC across four of six datasets (e.g., +2.2 pts on KADID-10k: 0.952 vs 0.930; +1.4 pts on CSIQ: 0.950 vs 0.936) and on PLCC across all six. Against the closest LMM-based competitor Q-Align [12], Compare2Score wins on SRCC for LIVE (+5.9 pts), CSIQ (+3.5 pts), KADID-10k (+8.3 pts), and BID (+1.5 pts), but trails on SRCC for CLIVE (0.914 vs 0.931) and KonIQ-10k (0.931 vs 0.935), while still leading on PLCC for both. On the cross-dataset generalization benchmarks, Compare2Score achieves the best SRCC on all three unseen datasets (TID2013: 0.823, SPAQ: 0.906, AGIQA-3K: 0.730), exceeding LIQE by up to 2.5 pts and Q-Align by up to 9.3 pts (SPAQ). Opinion-unaware methods (NIQE, ILNIQE, Ma19) and per-dataset regressors (DBCNN, HyperIQA, TreS) are consistently outperformed, with the largest gaps on realistic and generative distortions. The soft-comparison probability matrix also improves zero-shot LMMs substantially (e.g., Co-Instruct SRCC on LIVE rises from 0.582 to 0.822), confirming the conversion strategy's broad utility.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from Q-Align's approach of training LMMs with absolute quality ratings by arguing that mixing IQA datasets with rescaled MOS is fundamentally flawed due to differing perceptual scales across subjective testing methodologies. Instead, it adapts the paired-comparison / learning-to-rank tradition (UNIQUE, LIQE) to the LMM setting, building on the mPLUG-Owl2 architecture and Thurstone's Case V statistical model for score derivation. The key methodological departure from Co-Instruct (which performed open-ended visual quality comparison) is the introduction of a soft comparison inference scheme that converts discrete comparative token probabilities into continuous scores via MAP estimation, rather than relying on binary forced-choice outputs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The probability-matrix-based inference conversion demonstrates intrinsic value beyond the trained model, improving zero-shot general-purpose LMMs (IDEFICS2, LLaVA-1.5, XComposer-VL-2) without any fine-tuning, suggesting a broadly applicable technique for extracting quantitative judgments from qualitative LMM outputs. However, the approach requires a 7B-parameter LMM at inference time and the soft comparison computation does not scale linearly with the number of images, limiting real-time deployment. Open questions include whether the anchor-image strategy generalizes to video quality assessment or other perceptual tasks, and whether the interpretability gap of LMM-based scoring can be addressed for safety-critical applications.
