## Problem

Federated learning on non-IID data suffers from divergent client model updates and inconsistent global aggregation, degrading accuracy and slowing convergence. Existing remedies such as proximal regularization (FedProx), contrastive alignment (MOON), and synthetic data generation (FedGen) reduce but do not eliminate distribution disparities, particularly under severe heterogeneity (e.g., Dirichlet β=0.1). The paper targets the gap of jointly harmonizing local data distributions and selecting reliable clients without requiring centralized knowledge of all client distributions or convexity assumptions on the loss.

## Input / Output

Each client contributes local image batches (28×28 grayscale for MNIST/FashionMNIST, 32×32 RGB for CIFAR-10/100, or person-detection frames for NIPD) partitioned via a Dirichlet distribution to simulate non-IID splits. The global model parameters θ_t are additionally fed into an autoregressive encoder that compresses them into a 16-dimensional meta-model vector, which is concatenated with the per-sample indicator function to form the diffusion input z_i. Outputs include the aggregated global model, per-class predictions (10 or 100 classes), person-detection mAP on NIPD, and—critically—per-sample importance sampling weights w_i = P_i*/P_0 that reweight local training data each round.

## Method

CRFed couples three mechanisms. First, an indicator function I_λ(l_i, σ_i) = (l_i − τ)σ_i + λ(log σ_i)² assigns adaptive weights to samples via self-paced learning; its optimal uncertainty σ_i* is obtained in closed form through the Lambert W function, yielding a curriculum that progressively emphasizes harder samples. Second, a diffusion-based data harmonization module encodes the global model into a meta-model φ_t, concatenates it with I_λ to form z_i, then applies a forward Gaussian-noise schedule (T steps) and a learned reverse denoising network to recover the optimal sampling probability P_i* for each client, effectively aligning local data distributions with a target without explicit distributional assumptions. Third, a confusion-resistant strategy selects clients whose indicator values fall below a dynamic threshold γ, samples their data using importance weights w_i = P_i*/P_0, and scales each client's learning rate proportionally to its indicator value, ensuring that reliable, well-aligned updates dominate aggregation. Theoretical analysis (Theorems 3.1–3.2) proves that the induced step-size schedule guarantees monotonic loss decrease and a tighter convergence bound than FedAvg under L-smoothness and bounded-below assumptions.

## Results

Under a highly heterogeneous Dirichlet setting (β=0.5, K=10 clients), CRFed achieves the best test accuracy across all five benchmarks: 98.5% on MNIST, 87.8% on FashionMNIST, 68.3% on CIFAR-10, 38.9% on CIFAR-100, and 88.2 mAP on NIPD. Compared to the standard FedAvg baseline, CRFed yields relative gains of 0.9% (MNIST), 3.7% (FashionMNIST), 5.1% (CIFAR-10), 7.5% (CIFAR-100), and 7.4% (NIPD mAP). CRFed also converges faster than all competing methods, reaching target accuracy in fewer global rounds on FMNIST, CIFAR-10, and CIFAR-100.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CRFed outperforms all 13 baselines on every dataset and metric reported. Against the closest competitor FedDPMS (Chen & Vikalo, CVPR 2023), CRFed leads by 0.2% on MNIST, 0.2% on FashionMNIST, 0.3% on CIFAR-10, 0.3% on CIFAR-100, and 1.1 mAP on NIPD. The gap widens substantially against earlier methods: MOON (Li et al., CVPR 2021) trails by 0.5% on MNIST but 1.7% on CIFAR-10 and 1.7% on CIFAR-100, while FedAvg (McMahan et al., AISTATS 2017) lags by up to 7.5% on CIFAR-100. Among the recent non-IID-specific baselines, G-FML (Yang et al., ACM TKDD 2023) and FedNP (Wu et al., AAAI 2023) are the strongest challengers on CIFAR-10 (68.1% and 68.0% vs. CRFed's 68.3%), but both fall further behind on CIFAR-100 and NIPD. In the importance-sampling comparison (Table 3, CIFAR-100), CRFed (0.389 at β=0.5) surpasses Harmony (Tian et al., MICRO 2022; 0.354), FedIR (Hsu et al., ECCV 2020; 0.352), ISFL (Zhu et al., IEEE IoT J. 2024; 0.314), and ISFedAvg (0.305), with the margin growing as β decreases (e.g., 0.280 vs. 0.258 for FedIR at β=0.1).

## Relation to Prior Work

CRFed synthesizes ideas from three distinct research threads: (1) diffusion models, repurposed here from generative modeling to a data-distribution alignment tool for FL; (2) self-paced/curriculum learning, which inspires the indicator function that adaptively weights samples by loss and uncertainty; and (3) importance sampling in FL (ISFL, FedIR, Harmony), which CRFed extends by deriving sampling weights through a reverse-denoising process rather than requiring explicit distributional assumptions or centralized access to client data. Unlike prior heterogeneity-handling methods (FedProx, MOON, FedGen) that adjust local objectives or generate synthetic data, CRFed operates at the data-sampling level, using the diffusion mechanism to iteratively harmonize local distributions toward a target before each training round. The Lambert W closed-form solution for optimal uncertainty (Theorem 3.2) provides a mathematical grounding that prior importance-sampling FL methods, which typically assume convex losses, do not offer.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Non-IID data remains the central practical barrier to deploying federated learning in healthcare, finance, and IoT, and CRFed's consistent (if modest) gains across five datasets and multiple heterogeneity levels suggest a viable path toward more robust FL. However, the computational overhead of running a full diffusion forward-reverse cycle per global round is a significant concern for resource-constrained edge devices, and the paper acknowledges this without providing a detailed efficiency analysis. Open questions include whether the diffusion-based harmonization scales to larger client populations or higher-dimensional data (e.g., video, medical imaging), whether the indicator function's self-paced weighting generalizes beyond image classification to NLP or tabular FL, and whether the modest 1–3% accuracy gains over strong baselines like FedDPMS justify the added complexity in production settings.
