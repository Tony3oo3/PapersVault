## Problem

While web-scale text and image datasets have enabled large foundation models, no comparable unified, large-scale, multimodal scientific dataset exists for astronomy. Existing astronomical data archives are experiment-specific, non-uniformly stored, and not optimized for ML, creating a steep learning curve that hinders the development of advanced ML models capable of leveraging the full diversity of observational data.

## Input / Output

The pipeline ingests raw observations from 20+ surveys in heterogeneous formats—multi-band images at resolutions from 63×63 to 160×160 pixels, 1-D spectra with variable or fixed wavelength grids (e.g., 7081 bins for DESI, 110 basis coefficients for Gaia BP/RP), irregularly sampled multivariate time series, and tabular catalogs—and outputs a standardized 100 TB dataset with per-modality schemas (flux, inverse variance, PSF/LSF parameters, wavelength/time arrays). Cross-matching utilities keyed on sky coordinates let users generate arbitrary multimodal pairings, while benchmark tasks produce morphology class labels, physical property estimates (redshift, stellar mass, metallicity, age, sSFR), and transient type classifications.

## Method

The core contribution is a community-driven data curation pipeline where domain scientists contribute download-and-format scripts via GitHub pull requests, and all data is standardized into Hugging Face Datasets with a common per-modality schema, eliminating the need for each researcher to navigate individual survey archives. Multimodality is achieved through coordinate-based cross-matching utilities that generate paired subsets (e.g., Legacy Survey images + DESI spectra) on demand, since full cross-matching is limited by survey overlap. For benchmark baselines, the paper employs standard architectures per modality—EfficientNetB0/ResNet18/DenseNet121 for image classification and regression, Conv+Att for spectrum-based property prediction, Random Forest and the Connect Later transformer for time-series classification—and reproduces AstroCLIP contrastive image-spectrum pretraining to demonstrate zero-shot cross-modal retrieval and property prediction via k-NN on learned embeddings.

## Results

On the Galaxy10 DECaLS morphology classification benchmark, ConvNext-nano with Galaxy Zoo pretraining achieves 89.3% top-1 accuracy, substantially outperforming the same architecture without pretraining (75.6%) and other architectures (EfficientNetB0: 80.9%, ResNet18: 73.9%). For galaxy property prediction, DESI spectra with a Conv+Att model achieve the highest R² values (e.g., 0.982 for redshift, 0.871 for stellar mass), outperforming Legacy Survey images (best R²: 0.774 for redshift with DenseNet121) and PROVABGS photometry (best R²: 0.696 for redshift with MLP). On PLAsTiCC 14-way time-series classification, the Connect Later transformer reaches 79.9% accuracy versus 77.4% for the Avocado random forest, and achieves RMSE 0.247 for redshift estimation from light curves.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper positions its baselines as references rather than competitors, since the primary contribution is the dataset itself. For morphology classification, the ConvNext-nano result with Galaxy Zoo pretraining (89.3%) from [144] is cited as the state-of-the-art reference, while the paper's own no-pretraining baselines (EfficientNetB0 at 80.9%, ConvNext-nano at 75.6%) establish a lower bound. For spectrum-based property prediction, the Conv+Att model [104] on DESI spectra dominates all image-based and photometric baselines by a wide margin (e.g., R² 0.982 vs. 0.774 for redshift), highlighting the information content of spectroscopy. On PLAsTiCC time-series classification, Connect Later [117] (79.9% accuracy) outperforms Avocado [20] (77.4%) by 2.5 percentage points, and also handles redshift regression (RMSE 0.247). BTSbot [120] achieves a strong AUC of 0.985 for transient candidate identification on BTS data. Notably, the AstroCLIP zero-shot results (Table 6) slightly exceed the supervised image baselines from Table 4 (e.g., R² 0.801 vs. 0.774 for redshift from images), demonstrating the value of contrastive multimodal pretraining enabled by the dataset.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on large-scale astronomical ML datasets (e.g., the 76M-image self-supervised learning dataset, PLAsTiCC light curves, Galaxy Zoo morphology labels) by unifying them into a single, standardized, multimodal resource. It draws conceptual inspiration from web-scale foundation-model datasets (LAION-5B, etc.) and analogous cross-domain efforts like Major-TOM for remote sensing, but departs from prior astrophysics datasets by spanning five modalities (images, spectra, time-series, hyperspectral, tabular) with a common schema and cross-matching utilities. The AstroCLIP contrastive pretraining demonstration [112] is reproduced as a proof-of-concept for what the unified dataset enables, rather than being the paper's primary methodological contribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 100 TB scale and multimodal standardization directly address the fragmentation barrier that has prevented large-scale scientific foundation models in astronomy, where prior datasets were experiment-specific and non-uniform. Key limitations acknowledged by the authors include the absence of any textual/caption data and the incomplete cross-matching across modalities (a consequence of survey design), both of which constrain the dataset's utility for truly unified multimodal models. Open questions include whether the current cross-match overlap is sufficient to train effective multimodal models at scale, and whether the standardized schema will hold as next-generation surveys (e.g., Rubin/LSST) introduce new data products.
