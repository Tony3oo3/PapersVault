## Problem

Prevailing RL fine-tuning methods for LLMs, primarily PPO and its variants, suffer from training instability and vulnerability to distribution collapse, where the model becomes over-optimized and exhibits highly biased behavior. This is exacerbated by the large discrete action space (e.g., 32,000 tokens for Llama-2) and sparse reward signals received only after generating a complete response. The paper asks whether reframing single-agent RL fine-tuning as a sequential cooperative multi-agent problem can mitigate these pathologies.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained LLM (GPT-2-Large/XL or Llama-2-7b/13b-chat) and a text query as input — either the first 2–8 tokens of a movie review (IMDB) or a grade-school math word problem (GSM8K). It produces a fine-tuned LLM that generates task-optimized text responses, scored by either a subjective reward (sentiment score from a distilbert-imdb reward model) or an objective reward (binary correctness of the extracted numerical answer). During training the LLM is duplicated into two copies, doubling the parameter count, though either copy can be deployed independently at inference time.

## Method

CORY extends single-agent RL fine-tuning to a sequential cooperative multi-agent framework by duplicating the target LLM into two agents: a pioneer that generates a response from the query alone, and an observer that generates a response conditioned on both the query and the pioneer's output (knowledge transfer). The two agents share a collective reward defined as the sum of their individual task rewards and are each optimized independently via PPO with token-level KL penalties. To prevent the observer from developing a prompt bias toward the combined (query, pioneer-response) format, CORY periodically exchanges the roles of the two agents, forcing each to adapt to both input formats. This role exchange acts as a bootstrapping mechanism: the observer learns a better policy by leveraging the pioneer's exploration, and the exchange transfers those gains back, reducing the pioneer's optimization pressure. The approach is algorithm-agnostic and plug-and-play, requiring only three modifications to any existing RL fine-tuning pipeline.

## Results

On IMDB Review (subjective reward, GPT-2-Large), CORY achieves task reward levels comparable to single-PPO after 100 iterations while maintaining a KL divergence less than half that of PPO, resulting in a consistently rising combined reward versus PPO's declining curve. On GSM8K (objective reward, 4-bit Llama-2-7b-chat), CORY achieves a pass@1 of 18% on the test set, with a stable task reward curve that outperforms PPO, whose reward peaks around iteration 50 before declining due to distribution collapse. CORY also demonstrates superior robustness, maintaining stable training across learning rates of 1e-4 and 1e-5 on both Llama-2-7b and Llama-2-13b, whereas PPO collapses at 1e-4 and shows accelerating KL divergence even at 1e-5.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against single-agent PPO (Schulman et al., 2017), CORY consistently wins on KL divergence and combined reward while matching task reward on IMDB and surpassing it on GSM8K. Specifically, PPO's KL divergence reaches more than twice CORY's on IMDB, and on GSM8K PPO's task reward peaks around iteration 50 before declining due to distribution collapse, while CORY maintains a stable, higher reward throughout. CORY also demonstrates markedly superior robustness: at learning rate 1e-4, PPO collapses on both Llama-2-7b and 13b, whereas CORY trains stably; even at 1e-5, PPO's KL divergence shows an accelerating upward trend after 100 iterations, while CORY's remains low and flat. Against Elastic Reset (Noukhovitch et al., 2024), CORY is more reliable: ER can partially recover performance after collapse but exhibits high training volatility, making it difficult to determine when to stop training and save parameters, whereas CORY stabilizes both KL divergence and task reward from early iterations without requiring periodic resets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends single-agent PPO-based RL fine-tuning of LLMs (as used in InstructGPT and subsequent RLHF work) into a sequential cooperative multi-agent reinforcement learning framework. It draws conceptual inspiration from cooperative MARL literature on coevolution and emergent behavior, and architecturally from asymmetric training frameworks for Stackelberg games (Gao et al., 2023), where one agent observes the other's action. Unlike prior attempts to fix PPO instability in LLM fine-tuning (maxentropy regularization, reward shaping, hyperparameter tuning), CORY reframes the problem structurally by duplicating the model into two cooperating agents with knowledge transfer and periodic role exchange, rather than modifying the underlying RL algorithm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CORY addresses a well-recognized practical pain point—PPO's vulnerability to distribution collapse and hyperparameter sensitivity during LLM fine-tuning—with a plug-and-play, algorithm-agnostic wrapper that requires only three modifications to an existing pipeline. The Pareto frontier analysis between task reward and KL divergence provides a useful diagnostic lens for evaluating fine-tuning methods. Key limitations include the doubling of computational resources (two full model copies), evaluation on relatively small models (GPT-2, Llama-2-7b), and the open question of whether the approach scales to more agents or benefits from parameter sharing. Competitive MARL and population-based methods are identified as promising but unexplored extensions.
