## Problem

Existing methods for evaluating multiple General Value Functions (GVFs) in parallel rely on fixed behavior policies or pre-collected offline datasets, which become data-inefficient when the behavior policy diverges from the GVF target policies. The question of how to adaptively design a single behavior policy that minimizes the total mean squared error across multiple GVF predictions simultaneously has remained largely unexplored, as prior exploration work focuses on single-objective policy optimization rather than multi-GVF evaluation.

## Input / Output

The method takes as input N target policies π_i, N scalar cumulants c_i, and a single stream of environment transitions (s, a, s', c) collected under a learned behavior policy. It produces N GVF value estimates V_i(s) = Σ_a π_i(a|s) Q_θ^i(s,a) and an iteratively updated behavior policy μ. The Q and M networks each have N heads (one per GVF), and the behavior policy is updated every step using the latest variance estimates, with a minimum probability ε for each action to ensure support coverage required by importance sampling.

## Method

GVFExplorer iteratively updates a single behavior policy to minimize the total return variance across all N GVFs, using a TD-based variance estimator M_π^μ that bootstraps variance via the squared TD error. The key update rule (Theorem 4.1) sets μ_{k+1}(a|s) proportional to √(Σ_i π_i(a|s)² M_πi^μ_k(s,a)), making the policy visit high-variance state-action pairs more frequently. The procedure alternates between variance evaluation (updating M_w via Expected Sarsa) and behavior policy improvement, analogous to policy iteration, and Theorem 4.2 guarantees each update is non-increasing in total variance. In practice, two networks (Q_θ for value, M_w for variance) with N heads each are trained with MSE losses, experience replay, and target networks; for continuous action spaces, the method integrates with Soft Actor-Critic by adding a variance critic and KL regularization to prevent the behavior policy from diverging from target policies.

## Results

In the tabular gridworld setting, GVFExplorer achieves 79% lower average MSE than the best baseline (RoundRobin) for two distinct policies with identical cumulants (0.24 vs. 1.15), and 91% lower MSE for distinct policies with distinct cumulants (0.04 vs. 0.44). In the continuous-state environment with non-linear function approximation, GVFExplorer reduces average MSE by 69% over the best baseline with standard replay (5.19 vs. 16.78) and by 66% with Prioritized Experience Replay (3.87 vs. 11.62). In Mujoco (Walker and Cheetah), GVFExplorer consistently lowers averaged MSE compared to UniformPolicy and RoundRobin baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GVFExplorer outperforms the SR baseline (McLeod et al., 2021) across all tabular settings, with the gap widening as the number of GVFs increases: SR achieves MSE 53.3 on the 40-GVF task versus GVFExplorer's 1.66, and SR degrades severely with PER in the continuous environment (MSE 112 vs. 3.87). The BPS baseline (Hanna et al., 2017), adapted for multiple GVFs, is consistently the worst performer in tabular settings (e.g., MSE 26.13 vs. 0.24 for identical cumulants), limited by its REINFORCE-style variance estimator and scalability issues. Among the simpler baselines, RoundRobin and MixturePolicy are the strongest competitors in the tabular setting (MSE ~1.15 and ~0.44 respectively), but GVFExplorer still achieves 79–91% lower MSE. In the continuous environment, GVFExplorer beats RoundRobin by 69% (standard replay) and 66% (PER), and the advantage is further amplified when combined with PER, suggesting the two techniques are complementary.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the variance-minimization principle from optimal Monte Carlo sampling (Kahn & Marshall, 1953) and single-policy behavior policy search (BPS, Hanna et al., 2017) to the multi-GVF evaluation setting. It builds directly on the TD-based off-policy variance function M_π^μ from Sherstan et al. (2018) and Jain et al. (2021), repurposing it as the signal for an iterative behavior policy update rather than as a diagnostic. The closest prior work is McLeod et al. (2021), which uses Successor Representation weight changes as an intrinsic reward to shape a behavior policy for multiple predictive tasks; GVFExplorer departs by directly optimizing total return variance across GVFs, yielding a simpler variance-proportional sampling rule without the SR machinery.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method provides a principled, theoretically grounded (monotonic variance decrease per update) way to allocate a single experience stream across many GVFs, which is directly relevant to applications like personalized recommender systems where many policy-cumulant pairs must be evaluated simultaneously. Key limitations include the doubled computational cost of maintaining separate value and variance networks, untested sensitivity to large cumulant value-range disparities, and restriction to total (unweighted) MSE. Open questions include extension to multi-dimensional cumulants, state-dependent discount factors, and control settings where target policies are unknown.
