## Problem

Maximizing the Return on Investment (ROI) of a policy—the ratio of expected return to accumulated cost—remains largely unexplored in sequential decision-making. Existing offline RL frameworks either maximize return alone (ignoring cost) or maximize return under a fixed cost constraint (requiring manual threshold tuning), neither of which directly optimizes the efficiency ratio. The fractional objective makes standard policy gradient approaches inapplicable, as it requires computing gradients of a ratio of two long-term expected values.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a fixed offline dataset of transitions (s, a, r, c, s'), an initial state distribution p0, and a strictly positive cost function c(s,a) > 0 for all state-action pairs (a requirement of the linear-fractional programming formulation). It outputs a policy π* that maximizes ROI, extracted via weighted behavior cloning from the optimal stationary distribution d*(s,a) = d'*(s,a)/t*. The cost function must be positive everywhere; in practice, a small constant is added to environment costs to satisfy this constraint.

## Method

ROIDICE formulates ROI maximization as linear-fractional programming over the policy's stationary distribution, then applies the Charnes-Cooper transformation to obtain an equivalent linear program (ROI-LP) with a new normalization constraint Σ d'(s,a)c(s,a) = 1. For the offline setting, the key technical challenge is that the transformed variable d'(s,a) is not a valid probability distribution (it is the stationary distribution scaled by t), so standard f-divergence regularizers lose convexity. The paper introduces a novel convex regularization f(x,t) (e.g., ½(x−t)²) that measures distribution shift while preserving convexity in both x and t, with the property that it vanishes exactly when x = t. Lagrangian duality yields a closed-form solution for the weight function w(s,a) = max(0, (f')⁻¹((e_ν(s,a) − μc(s,a))/α, t)), and the policy is trained via weighted behavior cloning with weights w*/t. This avoids the need to manually search over cost thresholds as constrained RL requires.

## Results

ROIDICE achieves the highest ROI across most tasks in Table 1, with particularly large margins in Hopper (9.21 ± 0.49 on medium data vs. 3.58 ± 0.03 for OptiDICE) and the financial task (47.2 ± 8.67 on high-quality data vs. 41.8 ± 7.23 for OptiDICE and 27.3 ± 6.41 for COptiDICE). In HalfCheetah expert, ROIDICE (12.69 ± 0.09) essentially ties OptiDICE (12.74 ± 0.13) while incurring comparable cost. The return-cost breakdown (Table 5) confirms that ROIDICE's ROI advantage comes from achieving competitive returns at substantially lower accumulated cost (e.g., Hopper medium: return 102.4 vs. 134.6, but cost 11.1 vs. 37.6 for OptiDICE).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against OptiDICE (offline RL), ROIDICE achieves substantially higher ROI in Hopper (9.21 vs. 3.58 on medium data, a 2.6× improvement) and the financial task (47.2 vs. 41.8 on high data), while OptiDICE achieves higher raw return but at 2–3× the accumulated cost. Against COptiDICE (offline constrained RL at 50th/80th percentile cost thresholds), ROIDICE wins in 9 of 11 task settings; COptiDICE is competitive only in HalfCheetah medium (8.04 vs. 8.48) and Hopper medium-expert (7.88 vs. 8.29), and requires manual tuning of the cost threshold to find the best ROI. Against CDT (Constrained Decision Transformer), ROIDICE dominates: CDT frequently produces negative ROI (Hopper expert, Walker2D expert, HalfCheetah medium) or overfits to high-cost policies, while ROIDICE maintains positive and competitive ROI across all settings. In the appendix comparison with VOCE and CPQ (Table 7), ROIDICE's Hopper medium ROI of 9.21 far exceeds VOCE (1.81) and CPQ (1.13), suggesting constrained offline RL methods are overly conservative due to cost overestimation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the DICE-RL framework (OptiDICE, COptiDICE) and the V-LP linear programming formulation of RL (Nachum & Dai) by replacing the return-maximization objective with a Return on Investment (ROI) objective, i.e., the ratio of return to accumulated cost. The key technical departure is the use of the Charnes-Cooper transformation to convert the resulting linear-fractional program into an equivalent LP (ROI-LP), along with a novel convex regularization f(x,t) designed to handle the fact that the transformed variable d'(s,a) is not a valid probability distribution. The paper positions ROI maximization in sequential decision-making as largely unexplored, with prior work limited to multi-armed bandit settings where reward and cost are immediately observable.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ROI maximization is a natural efficiency metric with clear practical relevance (fuel efficiency in autonomous vehicles, inference cost vs. quality in LLMs, energy consumption in robotics), yet it had no principled optimization framework in the MDP setting. The paper demonstrates that directly optimizing ROI yields qualitatively different and more efficient policies than return maximization or constrained RL, and that a single run suffices where constrained RL requires searching over cost thresholds. Key limitations include the offline-only setting, sensitivity to reward/cost function design (constant cost reduces to standard RL; highly variable cost can induce 'laziness'), and dependence on dataset quality. Open questions include extending to online settings, handling stochastic costs, and whether the regularization design generalizes to other fractional objectives.
