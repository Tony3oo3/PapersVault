## Problem

Existing scene text recognition methods struggle with artistic and severely distorted characters because synthetic training data (MJSynth, SynthText) is morphologically monotonous and models remain sensitive to character shape variations. Additionally, the widely used Character Contrastive Loss contains a theoretical formula error that mistakenly treats same-class positive samples as negatives in the denominator, inducing harmful sparsity in intra-class feature distributions and exacerbating ambiguity on challenging samples.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243347/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes cropped scene text images resized to 100×32 pixels (patch size 8×4) and produces a character-level transcription over a 36-character set (10 digits + 26 alphabets) with a maximum sequence length of 25. During training, the student model consumes strongly augmented images while the teacher model receives weakly augmented real unlabeled images and OGS-generated background-free base images; at inference, only the student model is used. All images are rotated 180° with probability 0.5 during training to handle multi-orientation text.

## Method

ViSu adopts a Mean Teacher architecture where the teacher's parameters are an EMA of the student's, and introduces two key mechanisms. For the 'viewing' process, an Online Generation Strategy (OGS) generates 1.6M background-free samples with random character-level fonts and instance-level unified orientations from synthetic data annotations, which are fed exclusively to the teacher model as noise-free base images to guide feature alignment; a Unified Representation Forms (URF) step reduces eight possible text orientations to two primary forms via aspect-ratio-based rotation. For the 'summarizing' process, a Character Unidirectional Alignment (CUA) Loss is proposed that theoretically corrects the CC Loss error: instead of symmetrically clustering all characters in a minibatch (where same-class positives in the denominator receive negative gradients), CUA Loss unidirectionally aligns character features from strong augmented images (student) toward base image features (teacher), with gradient analysis showing M1, M2 > 0 and M3 < 0 so that all same-class features attract while cross-class features repel. The overall training objective combines character-level cross-entropy recognition loss, KL-divergence character consistency regularization (CCR), and the CUA loss weighted by λ=0.1.

## Results

ViSu achieves 94.7% weighted average accuracy (WAVG) on six common benchmarks (IIIT, SVT, IC13, IC15, SVTP, CUTE) and 70.9% average accuracy on the challenging Union14M-Benchmark, using only synthetic labeled data (MJSynth + SynthText) and real unlabeled data (Union14M-U) without any human annotation. On challenging benchmarks, ViSu reaches 80.5% on WordArt, 78.8% on ArT, 74.9% on COCO-Text, and 67.2% on Uber-Text. The model has 24.4M parameters and runs at 17.7 ms/image, outperforming the heavier MAERec-B (135.5M params, 308.1 ms/image) by 1.7% on Union-B while being ~1/5 the size and ~1/17 the inference time.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ViSu outperforms all 16 compared baselines on both common and challenging benchmarks. Against the strongest fully supervised methods, ViSu (94.7% WAVG, 70.9% Union-B AVG) surpasses LPV* (94.0%, 59.1%), CLIP-OCR* (93.8%, 55.2%), and ParSeq† (93.3%, 53.4%) by 0.7–1.4% on common benchmarks and 11.8–17.5% on Union-B, with the largest gap on Multi-Oriented (85.8% vs 21.0% for LPV*) and Artistic (66.8% vs 59.6% for LPV*). Against the closest semi-supervised competitor TRBA-cr† (reproduced with identical data), ViSu gains 1.8% on WAVG and a substantial 16.2% on Union-B AVG (70.9% vs 54.7%), with particularly large margins on Multi-Oriented (85.8% vs 23.1%) and Artistic (66.8% vs 62.7%). On challenging benchmarks (Table 2), ViSu leads TRBA-cr† by 3.8% on WordArt (80.5% vs 76.7%), 6.8% on ArT (78.8% vs 72.0%), 5.6% on COCO (74.9% vs 69.3%), and 27.2% on Uber (67.2% vs 40.0%). Compared to the pretraining-based MAERec-B (135.5M params), ViSu achieves higher Union-B accuracy (70.9% vs 85.2% in the real-labeled setting of Table 5; in the synthetic-labeled setting of Table 1, MAERec is not directly compared) while using only 24.4M parameters and 17.7 ms/image inference. The framework is also shown to be model-agnostic: applying ViSu to CRNN and TRBA backbones yields consistent gains of at least 1.6% on common benchmarks and 3.5% on Union-B over their respective baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Mean Teacher semi-supervised framework (Tarvainen & Valpola, 2017) and the character-level consistency regularization introduced by TRBA-cr (Zheng et al., CVPR 2022) for scene text recognition. It departs from prior semi-supervised STR methods by introducing an Online Generation Strategy that synthesizes background-free, style-diverse samples from existing synthetic labels, and by proposing a Character Unidirectional Alignment Loss that theoretically corrects a formula error in the Character Contrastive Loss from CornerTransformer (Xie et al., ECCV 2022), where same-class characters in the denominator were mistakenly treated as negatives. The 'viewing and summarizing' paradigm is a conceptual reframing of the standard supervised + consistency-regularization training loop, with the key methodological novelty lying in the data generation strategy and the unidirectional (student-to-teacher) alignment direction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that the performance ceiling of semi-supervised STR can be raised by enriching the supervised branch with generated diverse-glyph samples rather than relying solely on real unlabeled data, which is a practical insight for settings where unlabeled data is limited. The theoretical identification of the CC Loss error is a clean contribution, though its practical impact is modest (0.8% gain over the corrected CC² variant). A notable limitation is the failure on extreme-aspect-ratio (very long) text images due to fixed-size resizing, which remains an open problem. The framework's generality—applying to CRNN, TRBA, and a custom baseline with consistent gains—suggests the OGS and CUA components could be adopted by other STR or sequence-recognition pipelines.
