## Problem

Adapting Mamba's 1D selective state space model to vision is hindered by two properties of image data: strict 2D spatial locality and large variation in information granularity across tokens. Existing vision Mamba methods either flatten patches via raster scan (destroying local adjacency) or use fixed-size window partitions (imposing a single granularity that cannot adapt to objects of varying scales or ignore uninformative regions). The paper asks how to construct 1D token sequences that adaptively preserve 2D locality at multiple granularities while remaining end-to-end trainable.

## Input / Output

QuadMamba takes a standard RGB image (224×224 for classification, 512×512 for dense prediction) and patches it into 4×4 tokens, producing a hierarchical multi-scale feature pyramid across four stages (56×56 → 28×28 → 14×14 → 7×7). As a generic backbone, it outputs class logits for ImageNet-1k, bounding boxes and instance masks via Mask R-CNN on COCO, and pixel-wise segmentation maps via UperNet on ADE20K. The total sequence length L=H×W is preserved throughout, maintaining the linear O(L) complexity of the underlying Mamba scan.

## Method

QuadMamba introduces a learnable quadtree-based selective scan within a hierarchical backbone of QuadVSS blocks. A lightweight prediction module (Norm-Linear-GELU projector plus adaptive pooling for quadrant context) estimates a per-token locality score, which drives a bi-level quadtree partition: the image is split into 4 coarse quadrants, and the quadrant with the highest average score is recursively subdivided into 4 fine sub-quadrants, while the remaining three stay at coarse granularity. To make this discrete selection differentiable, a Gumbel-Softmax mask is applied via Hadamard product and element-wise summation to merge coarse- and fine-level token sequences into a single 1D sequence with negligible overhead. An omnidirectional window shifting scheme (extending Swin Transformer's single-direction shift to multiple complementary directions across successive blocks) handles informative regions that span adjacent quadrants. QuadVSS blocks are concentrated in the first two higher-resolution stages to exploit locality modeling where spatial detail is richest, while plain VSS blocks handle deeper stages.

## Results

On ImageNet-1k, QuadMamba-T achieves 78.2% Top-1 accuracy with only 10M parameters and 2.0G FLOPs, surpassing PVT-T (75.1%) and LocalVim-T (76.2%) by 3.1% and 2.0% respectively. At the small scale, QuadMamba-S reaches 82.4% Top-1, outperforming Swin-T (81.3%) and DeiT-S (79.8%) with comparable FLOPs. The largest variant, QuadMamba-B, attains 83.8% Top-1 with 50M parameters, matching or exceeding VMamba-B (83.7%) and PlainMamba-L3 (82.3%) while using significantly fewer parameters and FLOPs. On COCO detection, QuadMamba-S achieves 46.7 AP^box, and on ADE20K segmentation, QuadMamba-S reaches 47.2% mIoU (SS).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CNN baselines, QuadMamba-S (82.4%) surpasses RegNetY-8G (79.9%) by 2.5% and ResNet-50 (79.0%) by 3.4% Top-1 with fewer FLOPs. Against Transformer baselines, QuadMamba-S outperforms Swin-T (81.3%) by 1.1% and DeiT-S (79.8%) by 2.6%, while QuadMamba-B (83.8%) edges out Swin-S (83.3%) by 0.5% with fewer parameters. Among Mamba-based methods, QuadMamba-T (78.2%) beats LocalVim-T (76.2%) by 2.0% and Vim-Ti (76.1%) by 2.1% at similar or lower complexity; QuadMamba-B (83.8%) matches VMamba-B (83.7%) while using 25M fewer parameters and 5.9G fewer FLOPs, and exceeds PlainMamba-L3 (82.3%) by 1.5%. On COCO detection, QuadMamba-T (42.3 AP^box) leads EfficientVMamba-S (39.3) by 3.0% and matches ViL-T (41.4) with fewer FLOPs; QuadMamba-S (46.7) ties LocalVMamba-T (46.7) and beats EfficientVMamba-B (43.7) by 3.0%. On ADE20K segmentation, QuadMamba-S (47.2% mIoU) outperforms Vim-S (44.9%), LocalVim-S (46.4%), and PlainMamba-L2 (46.8%), but trails LocalVMamba-T (47.9%) by 0.7% and VMamba-T (47.3%) by 0.1%, though without the NAS search cost that LocalMamba requires.

## Relation to Prior Work

QuadMamba extends the line of vision Mamba backbones (ViM, VMamba, LocalMamba, PlainMamba) that adapt 1D selective state space models to 2D images, but departs from their fixed or handcrafted scan strategies (raster scan, 4-direction cross-scan, uniform window partition) by introducing a learnable quadtree-based partition that adaptively preserves 2D spatial locality at multiple granularities. The differentiable sequence construction via Gumbel-Softmax and the omnidirectional window shifting (inspired by Swin Transformer's shifted windows but extended to multiple complementary directions) are the key technical departures. Unlike LocalMamba, which relies on DARTS-based architecture search to find optimal window sizes and scan directions, QuadMamba learns the partition end-to-end with negligible overhead, making it more practical and generalizable.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

QuadMamba addresses a core challenge in adapting sequence models to vision: how to construct 1D token sequences that respect 2D spatial structure without sacrificing the linear complexity that makes Mamba attractive. By matching or exceeding LocalMamba's performance without architecture search, it offers a more practical and versatile backbone for dense prediction tasks. However, the two-level quadtree partition is rigid in shape and limited in depth, leaving open questions about handling arbitrary region shapes, higher-resolution inputs (e.g., remote sensing), and whether deeper hierarchical partitions would further improve dense prediction. The approach's reliance on fixed coarse/fine resolution ratios (1/2, 1/4) also limits its adaptability across input scales.
