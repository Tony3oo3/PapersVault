## Problem

Existing multi-relational data synthesis methods (e.g., SDV, PrivLava) suffer from poor scalability with large numbers of tables and fail to capture long-range dependencies—correlations between attributes spread across multiple tables connected by foreign keys. Meanwhile, diffusion models, despite their strong generative capabilities, have been restricted to unconditional single-table synthesis, leaving the multi-table problem unaddressed.

## Input / Output

The method takes a multi-relational database of m tables connected by foreign key constraints (forming a DAG) as input, where tables contain mixed numerical and categorical attributes with varying row counts. It produces a synthetic database with identical schema and foreign key structure, preserving inter-column correlations within tables, intra-group correlations within foreign key groups, and inter-table correlations up to the maximum depth of the constraint graph. Categorical features are label-encoded into a unified numerical space for the diffusion backbone, and foreign key group size distributions are explicitly modeled and preserved in the output.

## Method

ClavaDDPM introduces a three-phase pipeline: (1) relationship-aware GMM clustering on the weighted joint parent-child space learns low-dimensional latent cluster labels c that serve as intermediaries, with a majority-voting step ensuring consistency within each foreign key group; (2) a unified Gaussian diffusion model (label-encoding categorical data) is trained on the augmented parent table, while a separate child diffusion model and a cluster-label classifier are trained for conditional generation; (3) synthesis proceeds top-down, sampling parent rows from the diffusion model, then for each latent label, sampling group sizes and child rows via classifier-guided diffusion (perturbing the reverse process with classifier gradients). For multi-level databases, latent variables are learned bottom-up and propagated through the DAG, enabling long-range dependency capture. A multi-parent dilemma is resolved via approximate nearest neighbor matching between divergent synthetic child tables, selecting pairs that are close in feature space as joint samples from both parent conditionals.

## Results

On the five multi-relational datasets, ClavaDDPM achieves the best AVG 2-WAY scores on 4 out of 5 datasets (Instacart 05: 76.02, Berka: 89.21, Movie Lens: 97.11, CCS: 65.64), with the largest margins on long-range dependencies: outperforming the best baseline by 58.29% on 2-hop correlations (Instacart 05) and 20.24% on 3-hop correlations (Berka). On single-table metrics (CARDINALITY, 1-WAY), ClavaDDPM is competitive but not always best (e.g., 99.19 vs. 99.94 on California CARDINALITY). The method completes all experiments within 2 days, while TabDDPM-based baselines exceed the 7-day time limit on multiple datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PrivLava [5], the state-of-the-art marginal-based method, achieves the best CARDINALITY on California (99.90) but fails to converge (DNC/TLE) on all four remaining datasets, making it non-competitive on multi-hop correlations. SDV [35] is limited to at most 5 tables and depth 2, and produces the worst AVG 2-WAY scores across all datasets (e.g., 52.56 on California, TLE elsewhere). Among the pipeline baselines, ST-CTGAN and D-CTGAN are the strongest single-table performers (e.g., 99.93 CARDINALITY on California) but collapse on long-range dependencies: on Instacart 05, ST-CTGAN scores only 24.82 on 2-HOP vs. ClavaDDPM's 39.29, and on Berka, D-CTGAN reaches 73.94 on 3-HOP vs. ClavaDDPM's 89.21. TabDDPM-based baselines (ST-TabDDPM, D-TabDDPM) exceed the 7-day time limit on multiple datasets, while ClavaDDPM completes all runs within 2 days. On the CCS dataset, ClavaDDPM's CARDINALITY (26.70) is comparable to other methods, reflecting the inherent difficulty of that dataset's multi-parent structure, but it still leads on 1-HOP (57.77) and AVG 2-WAY (65.64).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the classifier-guided DDPM framework (Dhariwal & Nichol) and the TabDDPM backbone for single-table synthesis, extending them to the multi-relational setting by introducing GMM-based latent variables as intermediaries between parent and child tables. It departs from prior multi-table synthesis approaches (SDV's Gaussian copula iteration, PrivLava's graphical models under differential privacy) that struggle with scalability and long-range dependencies, instead leveraging the controlled generation capability of diffusion models with a relationship-aware clustering step. The multi-parent matching via approximate nearest neighbor search is a novel contribution not present in prior relational synthesis work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Multi-table synthesis is a practical bottleneck in finance and healthcare where real databases contain tens to hundreds of interconnected tables, yet prior methods either fail to scale (PrivLava, SDV) or ignore cross-table correlations entirely (single-table pipelines). The introduction of long-range dependency as an explicit evaluation metric and a comprehensive multi-table benchmark fills a gap in how synthetic data quality is assessed. Key limitations include the assumption that foreign key constraints are known a priori, the conditional independence assumption on child rows given parent, and the restriction to single-attribute keys; open questions include extending to discovered relationships, other integrity constraints, and formal privacy/fairness guarantees.
