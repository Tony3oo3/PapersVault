## Problem

Few-shot font generation methods rely on disentangling content and style features from a source glyph, but the complex structure of CJK characters makes this separation unreliable, producing artifacts such as missing strokes, blur, and residual source-font style. This paper eliminates the disentanglement problem entirely by replacing the source glyph with an Ideographic Description Sequence (IDS), a structural grammar that encodes character composition without any style information.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240454/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes the IDS of a target character (a prefix-notation sequence of Ideographic Description Characters and basic components, padded to a fixed length of 35) together with k reference glyphs (128×128 grayscale images, k ∈ {1, 3, 8}) as input, and produces a 128×128 grayscale glyph image as output. Reference glyphs are quantized into 256 VQGAN tokens (16×16 grid from 8× downsampling), and the autoregressive decoder predicts 256 glyph tokens that are decoded back to an image. A distinctive capability is that users can supply novel IDSs for characters that do not exist in any training font (e.g., Japanese kokuji), enabling glyph creation beyond the training vocabulary.

## Method

IF-Font reframes font generation as autoregressive token prediction over a VQGAN codebook (size 256), where the IDS of the target character serves as the initial token sequence and a pre-trained VQGAN decoder reconstructs the final image. Three modules drive the architecture: (1) IDS Hierarchical Analysis (IHA) resolves equivalent IDS representations (e.g., left-middle-right decomposed as two left-right structures) via random selection to regularize training; (2) Structure-Style Aggregation (SSA) computes similarity weights between the target IDS and each reference IDS, then fuses reference style features through a global branch (softmax-weighted average capturing layout and stroke thickness) and a local branch (cross-attention from IDS tokens to reference features capturing stroke-level details); (3) Style Contrast Enhancement (SCE) applies a supervised contrastive loss with a momentum encoder to sharpen style representations. The decoder is a 10-block Transformer (8 heads, 384-dim) where IDS tokens act as queries and style features are injected via cross-attention at each block, trained with cross-entropy on next-token prediction plus the contrastive loss (λ=0.5).

## Results

On the 3-shot setting, IF-Font achieves FID of 6.84 (UFSC) and 8.49 (UFUC), with SSIM of 0.5021 and 0.4782 respectively, outperforming all baselines on L1, RMSE, SSIM, and user preference. FontDiffuser attains a slightly lower FID (3.70 / 7.64) but trails IF-Font on perceptual and structural metrics (e.g., SSIM 0.4370 vs. 0.5021 on UFSC). In the user study, IF-Font is selected as closest to ground truth 22.78% of the time on UFSC and 21.28% on UFUC, the highest among all methods. Performance degrades gracefully from 1-shot to 8-shot, with consistent gains as more reference glyphs are provided.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IF-Font outperforms all seven baselines on L1, RMSE, SSIM, and user preference across both datasets and all few-shot settings. Against the closest competitor FontDiffuser (AAAI 2024), IF-Font achieves substantially higher SSIM (0.5021 vs. 0.4370 on UFSC 3-shot) and lower L1 (0.1478 vs. 0.1774), though FontDiffuser reports a lower FID (3.70 vs. 6.84), suggesting its samples are slightly more diverse in distribution. FS-Font (CVPR 2022) performs worst overall (FID > 25 in 3-shot), as its reliance on predefined content-reference mappings fails when reference glyphs are randomly selected. NTF (CVPR 2023) and VQ-Font (ICCV 2023) are heavily constrained by the source font layout, producing flat or narrow glyphs with structural errors. CG-GAN (CVPR 2022), LF-Font (TPAMI 2022), and CF-Font (CVPR 2023) all follow the content-style disentanglement paradigm and exhibit stroke artifacts and style inconsistency, particularly when target styles differ significantly from training fonts. In the user study, IF-Font is preferred 22.78% of the time on UFSC versus 18.32% for FontDiffuser, a 4.5-point margin that reflects its superior stroke clarity and structural correctness.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs fundamentally from the dominant content-style disentanglement paradigm that has governed few-shot font generation since EMD and SA-VAE, where a source glyph is morphed to match a reference style. Instead of treating font generation as image-to-image translation, IF-Font replaces the source glyph with an Ideographic Description Sequence (IDS) and reframes the task as autoregressive token prediction over a VQGAN codebook. The method builds on VQGAN for glyph quantization and decoding, and borrows the autoregressive transformer architecture from the LLM literature, but the key conceptual move is using a style-neutral structural grammar to control semantics, thereby eliminating the disentanglement problem entirely.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paradigm shift from disentanglement to sequence prediction offers a cleaner formulation for CJK font generation and uniquely enables the creation of non-existent characters (e.g., kokuji) by simply specifying a legal IDS, a capability no prior method supports. However, the approach is currently limited to CJK scripts, struggles with highly decorative or calligraphic fonts, and suffers from slow autoregressive inference with quadratic attention complexity. Open questions include whether the IDS-based formulation generalizes to other writing systems, whether the VQGAN reconstruction ceiling can be lifted with better quantizers, and how to handle the remaining ambiguities in IDS spatial descriptions.
