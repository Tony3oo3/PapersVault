## Problem

Existing parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) methods like LoRA and DoRA apply additive low-rank updates that are agnostic to the geometry of the weight matrices they modify, resulting in a persistent performance gap versus full fine-tuning. Closing this gap typically requires adding more learnable parameters, which undermines the parameter-efficiency goal. The paper asks whether a structure-aware update that exploits the specific singular vector decomposition of each weight matrix can recover most of full fine-tuning performance with far fewer trainable parameters.

## Input / Output

SVFT operates on individual pre-trained weight matrices (e.g., Q, K, V, U, D projections in transformer blocks) by first computing their full SVD, then training only a sparse set of coefficients in the singular-vector basis. For language tasks, the model takes text prompts and generates answers (GSM-8K, MATH, commonsense reasoning, GLUE classification); for vision tasks, it takes RGB images and outputs class labels (CIFAR-100, Food101, Flowers102, RESISC45). The singular vectors U and V are frozen after the one-time SVD computation, so at inference time the updated weights can be fused back into the original matrix with no additional latency.

## Method

Given a pre-trained weight matrix W₀ = UΣVᵀ, SVFT parameterizes the update as ΔW = UMVᵀ where U and V are the frozen left and right singular vectors and M is a sparse trainable matrix with a fixed sparsity pattern Ω. Four sparsity patterns are explored: Plain (diagonal only, adapting singular values), Banded (diagonal plus d off-diagonals on each side, enabling local singular-vector interactions), Random (k randomly selected entries), and Top-k (entries with highest uᵢᵀvⱼ alignment). The key distinction from prior SVD-based methods (SVF, SVDiff) is that off-diagonal elements of M are made learnable, allowing the update to mix singular vector directions and achieve a smooth trade-off between parameter count and expressivity. Theoretically, for a fixed number of trainable parameters k, the rank of UMVᵀ is at most min{k, min(d₁,d₂)}, which can exceed the rank achievable by LoRA (rank r with d₁+d₂ parameters per rank-one term), making SVFT a higher-rank perturbation in a structured subspace defined by the original matrix's geometry.

## Results

On GSM-8K with Gemma-2B, SVFT_R (d=16) achieves 50.03% accuracy recovering 95% of Full-FT (52.69%) while training only 6.35M parameters (0.25% of total), compared to DoRA_r=16 which recovers only 84% (44.27%) with 2× more parameters. On commonsense reasoning with Gemma-7B, SVFT_B (d=8) attains 83.35% average accuracy with 9.8M parameters, closely matching LoRA_r=32 (83.69%) at ~7× fewer parameters. On GLUE with DeBERTaV3-base, SVFT_P achieves 88.54% average with just 0.06M trainable parameters, matching LoRA_r=8 (88.50%) which uses 22× more parameters. On vision benchmarks, SVFT_B (d=4) with ViT-Base reaches 83.87% average accuracy, slightly exceeding Full-FT (83.72%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against LoRA, SVFT_P (0.19M params) outperforms LoRA_r=1 (0.82M) by 18% relative on GSM-8K with Gemma-2B, and SVFT_R matches LoRA_r=32 (26.2M) with only 6.35M parameters. Against DoRA, SVFT_R achieves 50.03% on GSM-8K (Gemma-2B) versus DoRA_r=16's 44.27% with 2× fewer parameters, recovering 95% of Full-FT versus DoRA's 84%. Against VeRA, SVFT_P delivers a 15.5% relative improvement on average across GSM-8K and MATH while using 3.3× fewer parameters. Against BOFT, SVFT_P (0.19M) surpasses BOFT (1.22M) by 4.33 points on GSM-8K with Gemma-2B. Against SVF (the closest prior SVD-based method), SVFT_P matches its performance exactly (confirming design equivalence), but SVFT_R significantly outperforms SVF (50.03% vs 39.12% on GSM-8K), demonstrating the critical benefit of learning off-diagonal elements in M.

## Relation to Prior Work

SVFT extends the SVD-based PEFT line (SVF, SVDiff, SAM-Parser) that only adapts singular values, by additionally making off-diagonal elements of the SVD coefficient matrix learnable as a sparse weighted combination of singular vectors. It departs from the dominant low-rank additive family (LoRA, DoRA, VeRA) by being structure-aware: the perturbation directions are determined by the specific weight matrix being modified rather than learned from scratch or drawn randomly. The orthogonal-update family (OFT, BOFT) is positioned as a multiplicative alternative, while SVFT remains additive but constrains updates to the singular-vector subspace of W, achieving higher rank per parameter than LoRA at the same budget.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SVFT offers a compelling parameter-efficiency story—recovering up to 96% of full fine-tuning with as little as 0.006% of parameters—making it attractive for multi-task deployment where adapter storage is a bottleneck. The finding that truncating singular vectors (even to 75% of full rank) causes significant performance degradation suggests all singular directions carry task-relevant information, which has implications for any SVD-based adaptation. Key open questions include principled sparsity-pattern selection (beyond the four heuristics explored), reducing the 1.2× memory overhead from storing singular vectors via quantization, and whether the approach extends to non-linear or attention-specific structures.
