## Problem

CLIP and similar vision-language models suffer significant performance degradation under domain shifts (corruptions, style changes, simulated-to-real gaps), yet existing test-time adaptation methods often require large batches, additional trainable modules, or dataset-specific hyperparameter tuning. The paper identifies that different text prompt templates encode complementary class information and that adapting the model separately under each template produces diverse weight configurations whose average converges to a flatter, lower-loss region—offering a principled way to consolidate adaptation knowledge without extra architecture.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a batch of unlabeled RGB test images (32×32 for CIFAR variants, variable resolution for VisDA-C, PACS, VLCS, OfficeHome) together with 8 pre-defined text prompt templates (e.g., 'a photo of a {class k}', 'a origami {class k}') that are encoded by CLIP's frozen text encoder. It outputs a single class label per image via cosine similarity between the adapted visual embedding and an ensemble text embedding (the mean of all 8 template embeddings). Notably, the method can operate with a batch size of 1, adapting only the Layer Normalization parameters (~0.026% of total parameters) of the ViT visual encoder.

## Method

WATT comprises three components: (1) a transductive TTA loss that constructs image-to-image and text-to-text pairwise similarity matrices within a batch, generates pseudo labels via softmax of their average, and minimizes cross-entropy against the model's inter-modality predictions to update LayerNorm parameters; (2) Multi-Template Weight Averaging (MTWA) in two variants—Parallel (WATT-P) adapts H separate models from the same initialization using distinct templates for L iterations then averages their weights, repeated M times, while Sequential (WATT-S) applies templates in random order without resetting, averaging weights periodically; (3) a text-ensemble evaluation step that averages the text embeddings from all templates before computing the final cosine-similarity logits. The key insight is that template-diverse adaptation explores different regions of the loss landscape, and weight averaging consolidates these into a more robust solution, with the Sequential variant (L=2, M=5) proving most effective under strong corruptions.

## Results

On CIFAR-10-C (mean over 15 corruptions), WATT-S achieves 73.82% accuracy, outperforming the CLIP baseline (59.22%) by 14.60% and the strongest prior method CLIPArTT (71.17%) by 2.65%. On CIFAR-100-C, WATT-S reaches 45.57% mean accuracy versus 29.43% for CLIP and 41.51% for CLIPArTT. On the natural-shift dataset CIFAR-10.1, WATT-P attains 87.78% (vs. 83.25% CLIP, 87.60% TENT). Across domain-generalization benchmarks (VisDA-C, OfficeHome, PACS, VLCS), WATT consistently improves over CLIP by 0.5–1.3% on average, with the largest gains on PACS Sketch (+3.77% over CLIP).

## Baselines & Comparisons

WATT-S achieves the best overall performance on the most challenging corruption benchmarks: CIFAR-10-C (73.82% vs. 71.17% CLIPArTT, 67.56% TENT) and CIFAR-100-C (45.57% vs. 41.51% CLIPArTT, 35.19% TENT), with margins of 2.65% and 4.06% over the strongest prior method respectively. WATT-P leads on CIFAR-10 (91.41%) and CIFAR-10.1 (87.78%), narrowly beating TENT (91.69% and 87.60%) on CIFAR-10.1 but losing to TENT by 0.28% on CIFAR-10. Both WATT variants substantially outperform TPT, TDA, and DiffTPT across all datasets, with TDA and DiffTPT performing below the unadapted CLIP baseline on several corruption types. On domain-generalization datasets (VisDA-C, OfficeHome, PACS, VLCS), WATT-P and WATT-S achieve the highest mean accuracy (85.00/85.03, 78.88/78.83, 94.81/94.80, 80.25/81.14 respectively), surpassing CLIPArTT and TENT by 0.2–1.3%. Notably, WATT operates without image augmentations or dataset-specific hyperparameter tuning, unlike TPT (which requires augmentations) and TDA (which requires tuning α and β per dataset).

## Relation to Prior Work

WATT sits at the intersection of two established research threads: test-time adaptation (TTA) of CLIP and weight averaging from training. For TTA, it builds directly on CLIPArTT's transductive pseudo-label loss and the broader family of entropy-minimization methods (TENT, TPT, TDA, SAR, MEMO), but departs from them by introducing multi-template weight averaging as the core adaptation mechanism. For weight averaging, it repurposes ideas from SWA, SWAD, and the Lookaround optimizer—techniques previously applied only during training—into a test-time setting where multiple text prompt templates generate diverse model hypotheses whose weights are periodically averaged. This combination of prompt diversity and weight averaging in a TTA context is the paper's conceptual novelty.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in WATT's ability to adapt CLIP with only 0.026% of parameters learnable, no dataset-specific hyperparameter tuning, and even at batch size 1—capabilities that prior TTA methods (TDA, DiffTPT, MEMO) lack. The consistent ~2–4% improvement over CLIPArTT across 155 evaluation scenarios (corruptions, domain generalization, simulated/video shifts) makes it a strong default TTA choice for CLIP-based pipelines. However, TENT still outperforms WATT on CIFAR-10 with no domain shift, and the method is tied to CLIP's text-prompt architecture, limiting direct transfer to VLMs without a text encoder. Open questions include whether the template-diversity + weight-averaging principle extends to segmentation, detection, or VLMs with different prompt structures.
