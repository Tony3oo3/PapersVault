## Problem

Large instruction fine-tuning datasets contain substantial redundancy and harmful instances, yet existing data selection methods either rely on manual curation, commercial LLMs, or score samples in isolation without accounting for how different data combinations interact during fine-tuning. The Shapley value naturally captures these combinatorial interactions, but its exponential computation (O(|D|·2^|D|)) makes it infeasible for datasets with tens of thousands of instances.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243152/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a large instruction fine-tuning dataset (e.g., MMLU with 99.8k instances or WizardLM with 70k) and a pre-trained LLM (e.g., LLaMA-7B) that serves as the evaluation proxy during Shapley value computation. It produces a compact curated subset (1k–20k instances) along with per-cluster Shapley value quality scores. The value function v(P) is configurable—defaulting to fine-tuning accuracy but extensible to fairness metrics—allowing the same pipeline to target different optimization objectives without architectural changes.

## Method

SHED reduces Shapley computation cost through a three-stage pipeline: (1) model-agnostic clustering via Sentence-BERT embeddings and K-means (C≈3√|D| clusters), selecting the embedding closest to each centroid as a proxy representative; (2) a proxy-based Shapley calculator that approximates values by iteratively removing groups of n instances from the proxy set k times and averaging the performance deltas, reducing the problem from |D| instances to C proxies; (3) optimization-aware sampling that either greedily fills the target budget from highest-scoring clusters (QOCS) or draws probabilistically via a softmax over cluster scores with a tunable temperature f (QWCS). The key insight is that cluster-level Shapley scores suffice as quality proxies, and because the embeddings are model-agnostic, the curated subset transfers across LLM families and scales—datasets selected with LLaMA-7B fine-tune LLaMA-13B, Vicuna-7B, and GPT-2 competitively, amortizing the selection cost.

## Results

On the MMLU dataset (10k samples), SHED-QOCS achieves 44.80 MMLU accuracy and 47.10 ARC-challenge accuracy, closely matching the full 99.8k dataset (45.56 / 45.99) with only 10% of the data. On the WizardLM dataset, SHED-QOCS with just 4k samples reaches 35.92 MMLU accuracy, surpassing the full 70k dataset (33.16) by 2.76%. On ARC-challenge, SHED-QWCS (9k from MMLU) achieves 49.21, exceeding the full dataset (45.99) by 3.22%, and SHED-QOCS (1k from WizardLM) reaches 51.36, surpassing the full dataset (47.95) by 3.41%. On MT-Bench, SHED-QWCS sampled from WizardLM scores 5.24, matching the full-dataset score of 5.21.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SHED consistently outperforms all baselines at the same sample size (10k). On MMLU accuracy, SHED-QOCS (44.80) beats DQ (39.88) by ~5 points and DSIR (40.24) by ~4.5 points; on ARC-challenge, SHED-QWCS (47.23) leads DQ (46.35) and DSIR (45.67). In the best-selected comparison, SHED-QOCS (44.80 at 10k) surpasses DQ (43.50 at 7k) and DSIR (40.23 at 13k) on MMLU, and SHED-QWCS (49.21 at 9k) exceeds DQ (46.50 at 3k) and DSIR (46.90 at 3k) on ARC-challenge. Against LIMA (manual curation, 1k samples), SHED(1k) achieves 41.71 MMLU vs. LIMA's 34.9 and 48.12 ARC-challenge vs. 46.16. Against IFD (7k from WizardLM), SHED(7k) scores 35.63 MMLU vs. IFD's 33.08, though SHED(1k) at 51.36 ARC-challenge is slightly below IFD(7k) at 52.90 on that specific task.

## Relation to Prior Work

SHED extends the Shapley-value-based data selection approach of TS-DSHAPLEY (Schoch et al., 2023) by computing Shapley values only on cluster proxy representatives rather than every individual data point, reducing computational cost from O(|D|·2^|D|) to a tractable approximation. It departs from prior coreset selection methods (geometry-based, uncertainty-based, gradient-matching) and instruction-tuning data selection methods (indicator-based, training-dynamics-based, LLM-judged) by explicitly modeling combinatorial interactions among data samples through the Shapley framework. The model-agnostic clustering via Sentence-BERT embeddings distinguishes SHED from model-specific selection methods and is the key design choice enabling cross-model transferability of curated datasets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for resource-constrained practitioners: a 10% subset selected by SHED matches or exceeds full-dataset performance, and the curated data transfers across model families (LLaMA, Vicuna, GPT-2), allowing a small model to drive selection for larger ones. Open questions include whether the clustering-based proxy approach captures rare but critical samples, how the method scales to datasets with millions of instances, and whether integrating fairness or other non-accuracy objectives into the value function degrades the efficiency gains. The reliance on sufficiently representative Sentence-BERT embeddings remains a practical dependency that limits applicability in domains where such embeddings are inadequate.
