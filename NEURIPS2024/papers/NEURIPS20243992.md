## Problem

Existing diversity-boosting approaches in recommender systems (re-ranking, diversity-aware objectives, uniform traffic) assume static user preferences and content, ignoring that recommendations simultaneously shift user preferences and incentivize creators to alter their content. No prior work models this dual influence jointly, leaving open whether standard diversity interventions still function—or even backfire—when both sides of the platform are dynamic.

## Input / Output

The dynamics model takes m user vectors and n creator vectors on the unit sphere S^{d-1} as its state, along with recommendation sensitivity β (softmax temperature), user update rate η_u, and creator update rate η_c. At each step, a recommendation is sampled via softmax over inner products, then both user and creator vectors are updated and re-projected onto the sphere. The system is evaluated using four measures: Creator Diversity (average pairwise distance), Recommendation Diversity (weighted variance of recommended items), Recommendation Relevance (weighted average inner product), and Tendency to Polarization (average absolute inner product between creators, where 1 indicates full polarization). For real-world experiments, initial embeddings come from a 16-dimensional two-tower model trained on MovieLens 20M ratings.

## Method

The paper defines a user-creator feature dynamics where users update toward (or away from) recommended creators via a sign-consistent impact function f, and creators update toward the weighted average of their recommended users' preferences via a sign function g. The central theoretical result (Theorem 3.3) proves via an absorbing Markov chain argument that any system with a uniform lower bound p_ij ≥ p_0 > 0 on recommendation probabilities must converge to R-consensus or R-bi-polarization from almost all initial states, using induction on the number of creators with a potential function base case. Counter-intuitively, the paper shows that top-k truncation—which sets some p_ij = 0 and thus breaks the uniform lower bound—admits absorbing states with ⌊n/k⌋ clusters, preventing full polarization and improving long-term creation diversity. Conversely, diversity-boosting objectives and uniform traffic (which enforce p_ij > 0) satisfy the theorem's conditions and therefore accelerate polarization, explaining why myopic diversity optimization can harm long-term ecosystem health.

## Results

On synthetic data (n=50 creators, m=100 users, d=10), top-k truncation with k=1 reduces Tendency to Polarization (TP) from 1.00 (no truncation, k=50) to 0.27, while increasing Creator Diversity (CD) from 1.00 to 1.40 at β=1. On MovieLens 20M (n=2000, m=2000, d=16), the same trend holds: k=1 achieves TP=0.20 and CD=1.40 versus TP=1.00 and CD=1.00 for k=2000 at β=1. The paper also shows that diversity-boosting (increasing ρ in the diversity-aware objective) paradoxically increases TP, confirming that myopic diversity optimization worsens long-term polarization under dual influence.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not compare against external baselines; instead, it contrasts four real-world recommender system design choices under the dual-influence dynamics model. The key comparison is between diversity-boosting (increasing ρ in the diversity-aware objective) and top-k truncation: diversity-boosting increases short-term recommendation diversity but worsens long-term polarization (higher TP), while top-k truncation with small k reduces TP from ~1.00 to ~0.27 and increases CD from ~1.00 to ~1.40. A second comparison shows that lower β (more uniform recommendations) accelerates polarization: at β=0, TP reaches 1.00 quickly, whereas at β=3, TP stabilizes around 0.27–0.34 with top-k truncation. Threshold truncation at τ=0 (excluding only negatively-relevant items) performs poorly (TP≈0.97–0.99), while τ=0.707 achieves TP≈0.28–0.33, comparable to top-k truncation. Uniform traffic (which guarantees p_ij ≥ p_0 > 0) is shown theoretically to be a corollary of Theorem 3.3, meaning it inevitably causes polarization, in stark contrast to the common intuition that random exploration improves diversity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the performative-effects line of work on recommender systems (Dean & Morgenstern 2022, Eilat & Rosenfeld 2023, Hron et al. 2023, Yao et al. 2023) by modeling the *dual* influence of recommendations on both users and creators simultaneously, whereas all prior works consider only one-sided adaptation. The user-update rule generalizes Dean & Morgenstern's inner-product-based preference shift to an arbitrary impact function f, and the creator-update rule is novel. The proof technique (absorbing Markov chain argument) departs from the ODE-stability methods used in opinion-dynamics literature, and the paper explicitly positions itself against the static diversity-boosting literature (re-ranking, DPP-based objectives) by showing those approaches fail in a dynamic setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a theoretical guarantee (Theorem 3.3) that any recommender system with dual influence and non-zero recommendation probabilities must converge to polarization, and empirically demonstrates that standard diversity-promoting interventions (uniform traffic, diversity-aware objectives) actually *accelerate* this convergence. The counter-intuitive finding that efficiency-oriented top-k truncation *improves* long-term creation diversity offers a concrete design lever for platform operators. Open questions include the rate of convergence to polarization, generalization beyond the sign-function creator update, and whether the simplified unit-sphere model captures the relevant dynamics of real platforms with heterogeneous content and multi-armed feedback.
