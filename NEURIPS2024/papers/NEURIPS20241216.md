## Problem

Transformer encoder self-attention modules in multi-scene absolute pose regression (MS-APR) are underutilized: attention maps collapse to low-rank matrices, and ablation shows removing encoder self-attention does not degrade (or even improves) performance. The paper identifies the root cause as distortion of the query-key embedding space, where queries and keys are mapped into completely separate regions with only a few keys blended into the query region, causing all queries to attend to the same handful of keys and wasting the encoder's representational capacity.

## Input / Output

The model takes a single RGB image (resized to 256×256, center-cropped to 224×224) and outputs a 6-DoF camera pose consisting of a 3D translation vector and a 4D unit quaternion, along with a scene index prediction from a multi-scene dataset. The CNN backbone (EfficientNet-B0) extracts features at two different levels: a 14×14×112 feature map for the position branch (196 tokens) and a 28×28×40 feature map for the orientation branch (784 tokens), which are projected and reshaped into transformer-compatible token sequences.

## Method

The paper proposes two complementary solutions to activate encoder self-attention in MSTransformer. First, a Query-Key Alignment (QKA) auxiliary loss minimizes the L2 distance between the mean vectors of queries and keys across all encoder layers and heads, forcing both into a shared compact region so that all queries and keys are encouraged to interact rather than a few keys dominating. Second, fixed 2D sinusoidal positional encoding replaces the undertrained learnable positional embeddings, providing reliable absolute positional clues from the start of training so the model can learn geometric self-relations (e.g., long edges, corners) rather than treating shuffled patches as equivalent. The full training objective combines the uncertainty-weighted pose regression loss, scene classification cross-entropy, and the QKA auxiliary loss (weighted by λ_aux=0.1), applied to both position and orientation transformer encoders without any additional inference-time memory.

## Results

On Cambridge Landmarks (outdoor), the proposed method achieves a median position/orientation error of 1.19 m / 2.29° on average, improving over MSTransformer's 1.28 m / 2.73°. On 7Scenes (indoor), it reaches 0.17 m / 6.64° versus MSTransformer's 0.18 m / 7.28°. In terms of localization recall, the method improves (1 m, 5°) recall from 32.6% to 35.8% on Cambridge Landmarks and from 28.8% to 32.6% on 7Scenes, with consistent gains across all recall thresholds on both datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms both prior MS-APR baselines across all scenes. Against MSPN [19], the improvement is substantial: 1.19 m / 2.29° vs. 2.47 m / 5.34° on Cambridge Landmarks and 0.17 m / 6.64° vs. 0.20 m / 8.41° on 7Scenes. Against the direct baseline MSTransformer [20], the gains are consistent but more modest—roughly 0.09 m and 0.44° on outdoor, and 0.01 m and 0.64° on indoor—yet appear in every individual scene. In the ablation against alternative collapsed-self-attention fixes (Table 5a), the QKA loss (0.17 m / 6.64°) beats Improved SN [23] (0.18 m / 7.04°), 1/√L-scaling [24] (0.18 m / 6.87°), and σ-Reparam [22] (0.19 m / 6.81°) on 7Scenes. For positional encoding, fixed 2D sinusoidal PE (0.17 m / 6.64°) outperforms T5PE [25] (0.18 m / 6.97°) and Rotary PE [26] (0.18 m / 6.94°), confirming that absolute positional cues are more beneficial than relative or learnable alternatives for this task.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on MSTransformer (Shavit et al., ICCV 2021), which introduced a DETR-inspired transformer architecture for multi-scene absolute pose regression. Rather than proposing a new architecture, the authors diagnose why the encoder self-attention in MSTransformer is underutilized—identifying a distortion in the query-key embedding space where queries and keys occupy separate regions with only a few keys bleeding into the query region. This reframes the well-known collapsed self-attention problem (rank collapse, entropy collapse) from a task-specific perspective, showing that general-purpose remedies (Improved SN, 1/√L-scaling, σ-reparameterization) fail in the MS-APR setting. The fix—a simple query-key alignment auxiliary loss combined with fixed sinusoidal positional encoding—sits on top of the existing MSTransformer pipeline without altering its architecture or inference cost.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that the transformer encoder's self-attention was effectively dead weight in MSTransformer is practically important: it means the architectural commitment to transformers was not delivering its promised representational benefits for pose regression, and a lightweight training-time intervention can recover them. The QKA loss adds no inference overhead, making it immediately adoptable. However, the approach assumes images contain many informative key features; the authors acknowledge that scenes dominated by dynamic objects may not benefit. An open question is whether this query-key space distortion diagnosis and the alignment-loss remedy generalize to other transformer-based regression or detection tasks beyond MS-APR.
