## Problem

Existing protein pocket generation methods either rely on time-intensive physical energy calculations and template matching, or use deep generative models that overlook critical domain knowledge such as protein-ligand interaction types and their geometric constraints. Moreover, most approaches are restricted to small-molecule ligands, leaving peptides and nucleic acids unaddressed. This paper tackles both gaps by building an interaction-aware, generalized pocket generator that explicitly models binding physics during both training and sampling.

## Input / Output

PocketFlow takes a ligand (represented as atom coordinates, element types, and covalent bond orders — accommodating small molecules, peptides, and RNA uniformly) and a protein scaffold (all residues outside the 3.5 Å pocket region) as input. It jointly outputs the pocket's backbone frames (Cα positions and SO(3) orientations), up to four sidechain torsion angles per residue, 20-way residue type probabilities, 5-way interaction type labels (hydrogen bond, salt bridge, hydrophobic, π-π stacking, none), and refined ligand atom coordinates. The number of designed residues is fixed to match the reference pocket, and the ligand structure is held fixed for peptide/RNA tasks.

## Method

PocketFlow defines conditional flow matching on three distinct manifolds simultaneously: SE(3) for backbone frames (geodesic interpolation in R³ and SO(3)), a hypertorus for sidechain torsion angles (shortest-path interpolation), and the probability simplex for residue/interaction types (Euclidean interpolation with cross-entropy loss). The network architecture is a modified FrameDiff with Invariant Point Attention and transformer blocks, treating each ligand atom as a pseudo-residue for unified representation. The key innovation is a prior-informed sampling scheme that augments the learned vector field with two guidance terms: (1) a binding affinity gradient from a lightweight 3-layer EGNN predictor, and (2) interaction geometry gradients enforcing distance and angle constraints for hydrogen bonds, salt bridges, π-π stacking, and hydrophobic contacts. To resolve the non-differentiability of discrete residue type sampling during geometry guidance, the authors introduce a sidechain ensemble that computes weighted geometric guidance across all 20 residue types using the predicted type probabilities. The model is SE(3)-equivariant and, when pretrained on small-molecule data, generalizes to peptide and RNA pocket design without fine-tuning.

## Results

On the CrossDocked dataset, PocketFlow achieves 52.19% AAR, 0.67 scRMSD, and −8.236 Vina Score, outperforming the strongest baseline RFDiffusionAA (50.85% AAR, 0.68 scRMSD, −7.012 Vina) by 3.3% in AAR, 0.01 in scRMSD, and 1.29 in Vina Score. On Binding MOAD, the improvements are even larger: 54.30% AAR, 0.68 scRMSD, and −9.370 Vina versus RFDiffusionAA's 49.09%, 0.70, and −8.020. For generalization to peptide and RNA ligands (PPDBench and PDBBind RNA), PocketFlow achieves the best ΔΔG scores (−1.06 and −0.78 kcal/mol) while remaining competitive on AAR and scRMSD, demonstrating that the interaction-prior guidance transfers across ligand modalities without fine-tuning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PocketFlow outperforms all four baselines on every metric across both small-molecule benchmarks. Against the template-matching method DEPACT, PocketFlow gains ~21% in AAR and ~1.6 in Vina Score on CrossDocked, reflecting the advantage of learned generative modeling over substructure enumeration. Compared to the iterative-refinement methods dyMEAN and FAIR, PocketFlow improves AAR by ~13–14% and Vina by ~1.2–1.4, attributed to the flow-matching architecture and explicit interaction-prior guidance. The closest competitor is RFDiffusionAA (diffusion-based, post-hoc sequence design via LigandMPNN); PocketFlow surpasses it by 3.3% AAR and 1.29 Vina on CrossDocked, and by 5.2% AAR and 1.35 Vina on Binding MOAD, while matching or slightly improving scRMSD. On the peptide (PPDBench) and RNA (PDBBind RNA) generalization tasks, PocketFlow achieves the best ΔΔG (−1.06 vs. −0.62 for RFDiffusionAA on PPDBench; −0.78 vs. −0.45 on PDBBind RNA) without any fine-tuning, confirming that the learned interaction priors generalize across ligand modalities.

## Relation to Prior Work

PocketFlow builds on the Riemannian flow matching framework (Lipman et al. 2022; Chen & Lipman 2023) and the FrameDiff architecture (Yim et al. 2023) with Invariant Point Attention from AlphaFold2, adapting them to the joint protein-ligand pocket design setting. It departs from prior pocket design methods (DEPACT, FAIR, RFDiffusionAA, dyMEAN) by explicitly encoding protein-ligand interaction types (hydrogen bonds, salt bridges, hydrophobic contacts, π-π stacking) as a learned categorical variable and by applying classifier-guided sampling—borrowed from the diffusion literature (Dhariwal & Nichol 2021)—to both binding affinity and interaction geometry constraints during generation. The sidechain ensemble technique, which replaces non-differentiable residue-type sampling with a probability-weighted geometric computation, is a novel practical contribution that enables the geometry guidance to operate in a co-design (sequence + structure) setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PocketFlow provides a unified generative framework that generalizes across small molecules, peptides, and RNA without task-specific fine-tuning, addressing a gap where prior methods were largely restricted to small-molecule ligands. The finding that interaction priors are the key enabler of this cross-modality transfer (AAR drops from 48.19% to 36.41% on PPDBench when interaction learning is removed, versus a much smaller drop on the in-domain CrossDocked set) suggests that explicit physics-based constraints capture universal binding principles rather than modality-specific patterns. Open questions include whether the approach scales to metal-binding or multi-ligand pockets, whether integration with pretrained protein language models (ESM) or structure models (ESMFold) further closes the gap to wet-lab-validated designs, and whether the current 50-step Euler sampling can be accelerated without sacrificing the guidance benefits.
