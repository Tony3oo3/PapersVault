## Problem

Causal representation learning provides provable identifiability guarantees for latent variables but has no successful real-world application, while machine learning for dynamical systems scales to real data but offers no identifiability analysis for the parameters it recovers. This paper bridges the two fields by showing that parameter estimation in dynamical systems is formally equivalent to latent variable identification in CRL, enabling the import of identifiability theory into scalable mechanistic models.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an entire time series trajectory x = (x(t₁),…,x(t_T)) as a single input to the encoder, rather than processing it step-by-step; for the wind simulation this is a 3D grid (47 lat × 96 lon × 1 level) over 121 time steps, and for SST-V2 a 180×360 grid over 1727 weekly steps, both pre-processed with a discrete cosine transform retaining only the lowest 25–50% of frequencies. For multiview training, paired trajectories are constructed to share specific physical parameters (e.g., same layer thickness but different location, or same latitude but different longitude). Outputs include the identified trajectory-specific parameter vector θ, a decoded/forecasted trajectory from the mechanistic solver, and downstream causal quantities such as climate-zone labels and average treatment effect estimates.

## Method

The core contribution is a formal translation of ODE parameter estimation into a CRL latent-variable identification problem, justified by aligning the standard assumptions of both fields (existence/uniqueness → determinism, structural identifiability → injectivity). The architecture pairs a smooth encoder g: X^T → Θ that maps the full trajectory to a parameter vector with a mechanistic neural network (MNN) solver as decoder, which approximates the unknown ODE as a family of explicit equations with learnable coefficients solved via a time-parallel neural relaxed linear programming solver. Training uses a multiview CRL objective combining an alignment loss (L₂ distance on the shared parameter partition between paired views) and a sufficiency loss (reconstruction error through the MNN solver), yielding partial identifiability of the shared parameters under Corollary 3.2. When the functional form is known, the problem reduces to a simple least-squares fit of the ODE solver output, giving full identifiability (Corollary 3.1). The framework is modular: the latent regularizer can be swapped to accommodate other CRL principles (sparsity, interventions, etc.) while retaining the same mechanistic decoder.

## Results

On the SST-V2 sea surface temperature dataset, the proposed Mechanistic Identifier achieves 90.2% in-distribution and 82.4% out-of-distribution climate-zone classification accuracy, while also delivering the best long-term forecasting error (0.007 ± 0.003), outperforming TI-MNN (0.024) and Ada-GVAE (0.043) by 3.4× and 6.1× respectively. On the wind simulation, both the Mechanistic Identifier and the Contrastive Identifier isolate the layer-thickness parameter into the correct latent partition with ~100% classification accuracy, whereas Ada-GVAE and TI-MNN plateau at ~60% across all partitions. On ODEBench (63 known-form systems), the full-identifiability estimator (Cor. 3.1) achieves RMSE as low as 1e-6 (e.g., interacting bar magnets) and typically below 1e-3, validating exact parameter recovery.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The Mechanistic Identifier matches the Contrastive Identifier on in-distribution accuracy (90.2% vs. 90.4%) but trails slightly on OOD accuracy (82.4% vs. 86.1%); however, it is the only method that also provides a differentiable decoder, yielding a forecasting error of 0.007 — roughly 3.4× lower than TI-MNN (0.024) and 6.1× lower than Ada-GVAE (0.043). Both CRL-based identifiers (Contrastive and Mechanistic) dramatically outperform the non-identifiable baselines on classification: Ada-GVAE achieves only 46.8% ID accuracy and TI-MNN 69.7%, confirming that the multiview alignment/sufficiency training construct is essential for isolating the shared latent parameters. On the wind simulation, the Mechanistic Identifier correctly localizes the layer-thickness parameter in partition S₁ with ~100% accuracy, while TI-MNN and Ada-GVAE show no such localization (~60% uniformly), demonstrating that the mechanistic solver alone (without CRL) is insufficient for identifiability. The Contrastive Identifier, lacking a decoder, cannot perform forecasting at all, highlighting the complementary strengths of the two CRL approaches that the Mechanistic Identifier unifies.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper establishes a conceptual bridge between causal representation learning (CRL) and dynamical systems parameter estimation by showing that the foundational assumptions in both fields (determinism, injectivity, full support) are structurally aligned, allowing CRL identifiability theorems to be directly imported into ODE parameter estimation. It builds on multiview CRL identifiability results (Von Kügelgen et al., Yao et al., Daunhawer et al.) and adapts them by replacing the standard decoder with a differentiable mechanistic neural network solver (Pervez et al., 2024), while positioning against SINDy-like and gradient-matching ODE discovery methods that explicitly refrain from making identifiability claims. The authors are explicit that they do not introduce a new method per se, but rather reframe an existing parameter estimation problem in the language of latent variable identification to inherit provable guarantees.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstrated application of CRL to real-world data (climate time series), addressing the field's most widely acknowledged limitation of being confined to synthetic visual benchmarks. The framework provides a practical recipe for equipping scalable mechanistic models with identification guarantees, opening a path for CRL to answer domain-specific causal questions (climate zone classification, treatment effect estimation) that were previously inaccessible. Key limitations remain: the theory requires determinism (excluding stochastic differential equations), assumes noiseless observations, and operates in the infinite-data regime. Future work on multi-environment CRL for identifiable neural emulators and explicit noise modeling would strengthen the framework for broader scientific application.
