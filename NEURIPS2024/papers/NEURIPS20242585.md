## Problem

Existing federated learning methods assume a single global model, which fails under distributed concept drift where different clients experience different changes in the conditional distribution P(Y|X) at different times. While FedDrift addresses this with multiple models, it suffers from high computation and storage overhead and can incorrectly merge models when data heterogeneity inflates cluster distances. The paper tackles the joint challenge of adapting to distributed concept drift while simultaneously handling data heterogeneity (label distribution skew) across clients.

## Input / Output

Each client provides local image data (28×28 grayscale for Fashion-MNIST; 32×32 RGB for CIFAR-10/CINIC-10) along with its balanced classifier weights (trained on 5 samples per class for s=5 iterations) and per-class local feature anchors. The server produces a shared global feature extractor, per-cluster aggregated class classifiers, and per-cluster global feature anchors that are distributed back to clients. The final output for each client is a class label predicted by the shared extractor followed by its cluster-assigned classifier, with the feature alignment loss computed against the client's cluster-specific anchors during training.

## Method

FedCCFA decouples the CNN into a shared feature extractor f_θ and per-client linear classifiers f_φ, training them in a classifier-then-extractor sequence so that concept drift is absorbed by the classifier before small gradients propagate to the extractor. For classifier collaboration, it performs class-level clustering: each client trains a balanced classifier (5 samples/class), the server separates classifiers per class, computes MADD-based cosine distances between class classifiers, and applies DBSCAN to group clients sharing the same P(Y|X); aggregated class classifiers are then shared within each cluster. For feature alignment, it computes per-class local anchors, aggregates them per cluster to form clustered feature anchors, and applies a contrastive-guiding loss (adapted from FedFM) that pulls each sample's representation toward its class anchor while pushing away from non-corresponding anchors. The alignment weight is adaptively scaled by the entropy of each client's label distribution P_k(Y), reducing the alignment penalty under severe heterogeneity to prevent it from impeding main task learning.

## Results

Under the sudden distributed concept drift setting with Dir(0.5) heterogeneity, FedCCFA achieves 89.50% generalized accuracy on Fashion-MNIST, 76.95% on CIFAR-10, and 51.12% on CINIC-10 under full participation (20 clients). Under partial participation (100 clients, 20% sampling), it reaches 89.39%, 73.21%, and 52.62% respectively. Compared to the no-drift baseline (Table 1), FedCCFA's accuracy drops by only 0.31% on Fashion-MNIST and 1.43% on CIFAR-10 under full participation, whereas FedAvg drops by 22.76% and 17.6% respectively, demonstrating strong drift adaptability.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedCCFA outperforms all 13 baselines under the sudden drift setting. The largest margins are on CIFAR-10, where FedCCFA beats the second-best method (Ditto) by 9.50% under full participation and 8.48% under partial participation. On Fashion-MNIST, it leads IFCA (the second-best) by 2.48% (full) and 1.14% (partial). On CINIC-10, the advantage is smaller: 0.06% over Ditto under full participation but 3.34% over Flash under partial participation. Single-model methods (FedAvg, FedProx, SCAFFOLD, FedFM) suffer 12–23% accuracy drops under drift, while FedCCFA drops only 0.3–1.4% from its no-drift performance. FedDrift, the prior dedicated solution for distributed concept drift, collapses on CIFAR-10 (48.17%) and CINIC-10 (34.23%) under full participation due to data heterogeneity disrupting its model-merging mechanism. Personalized FL methods (Ditto, FedPAC) are more drift-robust than single-model approaches but still trail FedCCFA by 5–10% on CIFAR-10, confirming the benefit of class-level classifier collaboration.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the decoupled FL architecture (shared extractor + personal classifier) introduced by FedPAC [43] and FedRep [6], and adapts the contrastive-guiding feature alignment loss from FedFM [45], but departs from both by introducing class-level classifier clustering and clustered feature anchors rather than whole-classifier collaboration or global anchors. It also builds on the MADD distance measure [34] for high-dimensional clustering, applying it to classifier weights rather than gradients or losses as in prior clustered FL methods [9, 13, 35]. The key departure from FedDrift [19], the first method for distributed concept drift, is avoiding multiple entire global models and loss-based drift detection in favor of lightweight classifier-weight clustering, which the paper shows is more robust to data heterogeneity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important but underexplored setting where distributed concept drift and data heterogeneity co-occur (e.g., medical diagnosis varying across hospitals and over time), and provides a well-motivated decoupled training strategy (classifier-then-extractor) that is broadly applicable to any FL method facing conditional distribution shifts. Two open questions remain: the balanced classifier training step adds non-trivial computation that the authors acknowledge should be eliminated, and feature alignment can actively harm main-task learning under severe heterogeneity (Dir(0.01)), suggesting the alignment paradigm itself needs rethinking for extreme non-IID settings. The method's reliance on DBSCAN with a fixed epsilon and the assumption of real drift (P(Y|X) changes) limit generality to other drift types.
