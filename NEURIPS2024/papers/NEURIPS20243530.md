## Problem

In semi-supervised learning, how to select which unlabeled samples to annotate significantly impacts performance under extremely low annotation budgets, yet this problem has been under-explored. Existing approaches either focus exclusively on representativeness or diversity, use fixed trade-off ratios without theoretical justification, or rely on iterative model-dependent selection that wastes budget and requires repeated re-selection.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243530/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a full unlabeled image dataset (e.g., 50,000 CIFAR-10 images) and an annotation budget m (typically m/n ≤ 0.2, ranging from 40 to 100,000 samples). It extracts [CLS] token features via a pre-trained CLIP vision transformer and applies a Gaussian kernel with bandwidth set to the median pairwise distance. The output is a single subset of m indices for one-time annotation, which is then fed into a downstream SSL framework (FlexMatch or FreeMatch) for training.

## Method

RDSS introduces α-MMD, a novel criterion that unifies representativeness (measured by MMD between the selected subset and the full dataset) and diversity (measured by average pairwise similarity within the subset) through a single trade-off parameter α. The key insight is that minimizing α-MMD can be reformulated as a convex optimization problem over the simplex, enabling solution via a modified Frank-Wolfe algorithm called Generalized Kernel Herding without Replacement (GKHR), which greedily selects one sample per iteration in O(mn) time. The parameter α is set to 1 − 1/√m based on a theoretical finite-sample error bound, ensuring the MMD upper bound does not degrade relative to classical MMD. The method is model-free and requires only a single annotation request, decoupling sample selection from downstream training.

## Results

RDSS achieves the highest top-1 accuracy across all datasets and budget settings when integrated into FlexMatch and FreeMatch. Under the most constrained budget (40 samples on CIFAR-10), RDSS+FreeMatch reaches 95.05% accuracy, surpassing USL (93.81%) by 1.24% and ActiveFT (78.13%) by 16.92%. On STL-10 with only 40 labels, RDSS+FlexMatch achieves 77.96%, a 4.95% improvement over USL (75.65%) and a 22.39% improvement over ActiveFT (55.57%). On ImageNet (100k budget), FreeMatch+RDSS attains 58.24% vs. 56.86% for FreeMatch+USL, a 1.38% gain.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RDSS outperforms USL [50] on nearly all dataset–budget combinations, with the largest gaps at low budgets (e.g., +2.96% on CIFAR-10/FlexMatch at 40 samples, +4.95% on STL-10/FlexMatch at 40 samples). ActiveFT [57] is substantially weaker under constrained budgets, losing 16.9–22.4% to RDSS at 40-sample settings, though the gap narrows at higher budgets. In the supervised-learning-only comparison (Table 2), RDSS beats all four AL baselines—CoreSet [36], VAAL [39], LearnLoss [60], and MCDAL [8]—on both CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100 at 7500 and 10000 budgets (e.g., 89.77% vs. MCDAL's 89.40% on CIFAR-10 at 10k; 56.04% vs. MCDAL's 54.14% on CIFAR-100 at 10k). In the SSAL comparison (Table 3, 40-sample budget on CIFAR-10), RDSS surpasses all four SSAL methods—CoreSetSSL [36], MMA [42], CBSSAL [12], and TOD-Semi [17]—by 7.03–20.08% over random sampling, whereas most SSAL baselines actually underperform random sampling (e.g., MMA drops 12.69% below random with FlexMatch). On ImageNet, RDSS provides a 1.38% absolute gain over USL (58.24% vs. 56.86% with FreeMatch at 100k budget).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends kernel herding (Chen et al., 2012) and the Frank-Wolfe/conditional gradient equivalence (Bach et al., 2012) to a new sampling criterion, α-MMD, which unifies representativeness (via MMD, Gretton et al., 2006) and diversity into a single trade-off parameter. It departs from prior SSL sample-selection methods (USL, ActiveFT) that use fixed representativeness-to-diversity ratios or require iterative model-dependent cycles, and from AL core-set approaches (Sener et al., 2018) by providing a model-free, one-shot selection procedure with a theoretical generalization bound built on the core-set loss decomposition. The use of CLIP features as a fixed embedding space for kernel computation positions the method as a plug-in preprocessing step independent of the downstream SSL framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap in SSL—how to choose which unlabeled samples to annotate under tight budgets—by providing both a theoretically grounded criterion (α-MMD with a proven generalization bound) and an efficient O(mn) algorithm. A notable finding is that published SSAL methods (MMA, CBSSAL, TOD-Semi) actually underperform random sampling at extremely low budgets (40 samples), suggesting that model-dependent selection is counterproductive when the model is weak. The main limitation is that α is set purely from the budget size m without adapting to data distribution complexity, which the authors acknowledge may reduce effectiveness on datasets with intricate structure. Open questions include whether the approach transfers to non-image modalities or to settings where CLIP-style pre-trained features are unavailable.
