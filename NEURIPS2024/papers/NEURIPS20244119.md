## Problem

Prior legal LLMs were constrained to small model scales (7–12B parameters) and limited training corpora (≤30B tokens), leaving open whether the specialization gains from domain adaptation scale with both model size and data volume. This paper asks how much legal-task performance can be improved by jointly scaling to 54B/141B Mixture-of-Experts models and a 500B+ token legal corpus, and whether the full adaptation pipeline (continued pretraining → instruction tuning → preference alignment) transfers effectively to MoE architectures.

## Input / Output

The models consume legal documents, case law, statutes, and user instructions as text at a fixed 8,192-token training context length, and produce free-form legal reasoning responses structured to match the six LegalBench-Instruct task categories (issue-spotting, rule-recall, rule-application, rule-conclusion, interpretation, rhetorical understanding). During instruction fine-tuning, inputs follow a multi-turn dialogue format where a user queries a legal document, the assistant reformulates with metadata (document type, issue date), and the user probes the reasoning further. Evaluation uses balanced accuracy on LegalBench-Instruct and MMLU legal subsets (international law, professional law, jurisprudence).

## Method

The pipeline adapts Mixtral-54B (32 layers, 4096 hidden dim) and Mixtral-141B (56 layers, 6144 hidden dim), each with 8 experts and 2 active per token, through three sequential stages. First, continued pretraining on ~520B legal tokens (FreeLaw, MultiLegal Pile, EDGAR, web-scraped legal content, etc.) with 2% general replay data (Wikipedia, StackExchange, GitHub) and 5% math data to prevent catastrophic forgetting, using AdamW (β₁=0.99, β₂=0.90, lr=2×10⁻⁵) and 8192-token sequences. Second, instruction fine-tuning on ~1M instructions mixing general data (UltraInteract, Dolphin) with synthetic legal dialogues generated by Mistral-54B/141B-Instruct following a three-turn legal-analysis recipe, trained for one epoch at lr=1×10⁻⁵. Third, DPO preference alignment on synthetic legal scenarios where Mixtral-142B-Instruct scores candidate responses for factual accuracy, relevance, and logical coherence, at lr=1×10⁻⁶. A model annealing phase with high-quality LawInstruct data and UltraChat precedes the final checkpoint, and the entire training runs on 384 AMD MI250 GPUs with DeepSpeed ZeRO-3 and Flash Attention.

## Results

On LegalBench-Instruct (balanced accuracy), SaulLM-medium (54B) outperforms both GPT-4 and Llama3-70B, demonstrating that domain-specific adaptation at the instruction-tuning and preference-alignment stages enables a smaller model to surpass much larger general-purpose models. SaulLM-large (141B, with only 44B active parameters due to MoE) further improves over SaulLM-medium, though the paper notes inverse scaling on ~20% of conclusion, interpretation, and rule tasks. Continued pretraining on the 520B-token legal corpus yields an approximate +7% improvement over the Mixtral base at both the IFT and DPO stages, consistent across all five LegalBench-Instruct categories.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares SaulLM-medium and SaulLM-large against four baselines on LegalBench-Instruct (balanced accuracy): Mixtral-54B-Instruct, Mixtral-141B-Instruct, GPT-4 (May 2024), and Llama3-70B-Instruct. SaulLM-medium (54B) outperforms its base Mixtral-54B-Instruct and also surpasses the substantially larger GPT-4 and Llama3-70B, validating that full-pipeline domain adaptation (continued pretraining + IFT + DPO) is more effective than raw scale for legal reasoning. SaulLM-large (141B) similarly outperforms Mixtral-141B-Instruct, confirming the approach generalizes to the larger MoE configuration. The authors note that while SaulLM models lead on legal benchmarks, they are slightly weaker at following generic instructions compared to Llama3 and Mixtral-Instruct, likely due to differences in proprietary alignment data. The paper also highlights that SaulLM-141B's MoE architecture (44B active parameters) makes it more efficient to serve than a dense 141B model, and suggests that applying the same recipe to Llama3-70B could yield even stronger results.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper scales up the SaulLM-7B domain adaptation pipeline (continued pretraining → instruction fine-tuning → DPO alignment) from 7B parameters and ~30B tokens to 54B/141B parameters and 500B+ tokens, applying it to the Mixtral MoE architecture for the first time in the legal domain. It departs from prior legal LLM work (LegalBERT, InCaseLawBERT, SaulLM-7B) which was constrained to small model sizes and limited corpora, and from general domain adaptation studies (Meditron, Galactica) by covering the full modern LLM training stack rather than just pretraining. The synthetic data generation for IFT and preference data follows the recipe established in SaulLM-7B, while the use of UltraInteract, UltraFeedback, and Orca for general capability retention reflects the broader alignment literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Releasing 54B and 141B legal LLMs under MIT license provides the first open-source, large-scale legal models, enabling research that was previously blocked by the 7B ceiling. The finding that the full pipeline (not just pretraining) is necessary, and that domain-specialized 54B models can outperform GPT-4 and Llama3-70B on legal benchmarks, validates a practical recipe for domain adaptation at scale. Open questions include whether the unsaturated pretraining loss implies further gains from longer training, whether the observed inverse scaling on ~20% of legal tasks (conclusion, interpretation, rules) is a fundamental property of MoE scaling in narrow domains, and how to close the gap in generic instruction-following that domain specialization introduces.
