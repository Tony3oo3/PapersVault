## Problem

EEG signals suffer from extremely low signal-to-noise ratio, high inter-subject variability, and inconsistent channel configurations across acquisition devices, making it difficult for standard masked autoencoder approaches (borrowed from NLP and CV) to learn robust, universal representations. Existing self-supervised EEG models (BENDR, BIOT, LaBraM) either rely on convolutional encoders that cannot decouple channel-signal correlations across devices, or use frequency-domain tokenization that loses time-domain information critical for ERP tasks. This paper tackles the gap of learning high-quality, paradigm-agnostic EEG representations that generalize across subjects, tasks, and hardware configurations.

## Input / Output

The model consumes multi-channel EEG signals (58 electrodes at 256 Hz, 4-second windows yielding 1024 time points) which are divided into non-overlapping spatio-temporal patches of 64 time points (250 ms) per channel. During pretraining, 50% of time patches and 80% of channel patches are randomly masked; the model outputs both reconstructed raw patches for the unmasked region and aligned representation features. For downstream tasks, the frozen encoder produces summary-token features (S tokens × embedding dimension per time patch) that are mapped to class logits via a lightweight linear head with an adaptive spatial filter (1×1 convolution) to handle channel count mismatches between the 58-channel pretrained model and downstream datasets with fewer channels.

## Method

EEGPT introduces a dual self-supervised pretraining objective that combines (1) spatio-temporal representation alignment, where a predictor (using RoPE positional encoding) predicts full-signal features from masked-patch encoder outputs and aligns them with a momentum encoder's output via MSE loss, and (2) mask-based reconstruction, where a reconstructor with a skip connection from the encoder regenerates raw patches for the unmasked region. The hierarchical design decouples spatial processing (encoder operates on all masked channels at each time step) from temporal processing (predictor and reconstructor operate across time steps), reducing computational complexity. A local spatio-temporal embedding with a learnable Codex book of channel vectors provides flexible channel-to-model mapping, enabling the model to adapt to different electrode configurations without architectural changes. The 10M+ parameter ViT-based model is pretrained on a mixed multi-task dataset (MI, SSVEP, emotion, multi-paradigm) and evaluated via linear probing, which freezes all pretrained weights and trains only the linear classification head, demonstrating that the encoder alone captures rich universal features.

## Results

On the five universal EEG benchmark tasks (BCIC-2A, BCIC-2B, Sleep-EDFx, KaggleERN, PhysioP300), EEGPT (25M params, linear-probing) achieves balanced accuracies of 58.46%, 72.12%, 69.17%, 58.37%, and 65.02% respectively, outperforming all three universal baselines (BENDR, BIOT, LaBraM) on every task. On the Temple University event-type classification (TUEV), EEGPT reaches 62.32% balanced accuracy and 81.87% weighted F1, improving over BIOT by 9.5% and 6.9% respectively. The model also demonstrates scaling laws: downstream accuracy follows ACC = (33.6·N)^0.029 and reconstruction loss follows L_R = (0.72·N)^(-0.014) as parameter count N increases from 0.4M to 101M.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EEGPT is compared against three universal EEG pretrained models (BENDR, BIOT, LaBraM) and five task-specific models on the Temple University datasets. Against BENDR (which used full fine-tuning), EEGPT (using only linear-probing) improves balanced accuracy by 9.4% on BCIC-2A, 1.5% on BCIC-2B, 2.6% on Sleep-EDFx, 2.6% on KaggleERN, and 3.9% on PhysioP300. Against BIOT, the gains are larger: 12.5% (BCIC-2A), 8.1% (BCIC-2B), 2.9% (Sleep-EDFx), 7.2% (KaggleERN), and 10.2% (PhysioP300). Against LaBraM, EEGPT leads by 2.3% (BCIC-2A), 3.6% (BCIC-2B), 1.4% (Sleep-EDFx), and 3.0% (KaggleERN), while being nearly tied on PhysioP300 (0.6502 vs. 0.6477 BAC). On TUEV, EEGPT surpasses BIOT by 9.5% in balanced accuracy and 6.9% in weighted F1, though LaBraM (which used a larger pretraining corpus including TUEG) still leads EEGPT by 1.8% BAC on TUEV. On TUAB, EEGPT is comparable to BIOT (0.7983 vs. 0.7959 BAC) but trails LaBraM (0.8140 BAC), which the authors attribute to LaBraM's access to a larger and more distributionally similar pretraining dataset.

## Relation to Prior Work

EEGPT extends the masked autoencoder paradigm (BERT, MAE) to EEG by introducing a dual self-supervised objective that combines spatio-temporal representation alignment via a momentum encoder with standard mask-based reconstruction, departing from the single-objective approaches in BENDR, BIOT, and LaBraM. The hierarchical encoder–predictor–reconstructor architecture decouples spatial and temporal processing, drawing on the ViT patching strategy and rotary position embeddings, while the local spatio-temporal embedding with a learnable channel codex book addresses cross-device channel mismatch in a way that prior convolutional-encoder-based methods (BENDR, EEG2VEC) could not. Conceptually, the alignment branch is inspired by the MVEB principle of encouraging minimal sufficient representations, but applied to EEG's spatio-temporal structure rather than multi-view data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first systematic scaling-law analysis for EEG pretrained transformers (both parameter size and pretraining data size), giving the community a concrete roadmap for scaling EEG foundation models. The finding that linear-probing (frozen encoder + linear head) matches or exceeds full fine-tuning across all downstream tasks is practically important: it means the pretrained representations are genuinely transferable, reducing overfitting risk on small BCI datasets. Key limitations include the 4-second input window (restricting applicability to long recordings), the relatively modest 25M parameter scale compared to vision/language foundation models, and the fact that LaBraM still outperforms EEGPT on TUAB/TUEV when given a larger and more domain-aligned pretraining corpus—suggesting data scale and domain coverage remain critical open questions.
