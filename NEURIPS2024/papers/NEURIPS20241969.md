## Problem

Existing lane topology reasoning methods predominantly rely on vanilla MLP to predict connectivity directly from lane queries, a paradigm the authors term 'perception over reasoning.' This approach is fragile to small endpoint shifts in lane detection, causing connected lanes to be misclassified as disconnected and leading to systematically under-predicted topology. The geometric structure intrinsic to lane endpoints is entirely ignored by the MLP-based reasoning head.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 6 or 7 surround-view RGB images (resized to 1550×2048) and optionally a standard-definition navigation map, processed through a ResNet-50/FPN backbone and BEVFormer view transformer into a 200×100 BEV grid. It outputs 200 directed 3D lane centerlines (each 11 points in x,y,z), a 200×200 lane-to-lane topology connectivity matrix, and lane segment predictions. The topology matrix entry (i,j) encodes the probability that lane i's endpoint connects to lane j's startpoint, and is fed back into a GNN to modulate lane feature aggregation across decoder layers.

## Method

TopoLogic replaces the vanilla MLP topology head with two interpretable, complementary mechanisms fused by learnable coefficients. First, a geometric distance approach computes the Euclidean distance between each lane's endpoint and every other lane's startpoint, then maps this distance to connectivity probability via a learnable function f(x) = exp(−x^α/(λ·σ)) that is more tolerant of endpoint shifts than Gaussian, sigmoid, or tanh alternatives. Second, a semantic similarity approach encodes each lane query through two independent three-layer MLPs (decoupling start and end representations) and computes their dot product, mapped through sigmoid to yield a topology matrix. The fused topology matrix is injected into a GNN that aggregates features from topologically adjacent lanes to enhance lane learning in subsequent decoder layers. Training uses focal loss + L1 for detection and a topology loss applied only to the similarity branch, while the geometric distance parameters are updated indirectly through the detection loss via the GNN pathway.

## Results

On OpenLane-V2 subset_A (v2.1.0), TopoLogic achieves a lane topology score (TOP_ll) of 23.9 and overall score (OLS) of 44.1 without SDMap, and 28.9 / 47.5 with SDMap, substantially outperforming the previous best (TopoNet: 10.9 / 39.8; SMERF: 15.4 / 42.9). On subset_B (v2.1.0), TopoLogic reaches TOP_ll of 21.6 and OLS of 42.3. For lane segment perception, TopoLogic attains mAP of 33.2 and TOP_lsls of 30.8 (v2.1.0), surpassing LaneSegNet (32.6 mAP, 25.4 TOP_lsls).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TopoLogic outperforms all eight baselines on the primary topology metric TOP_ll. Against the closest competitor TopoMLP (19.0), TopoLogic gains +4.9 points in TOP_ll and +1.9 in OLS, though TopoMLP retains a slight edge in DET_t (50.0 vs. 47.2). Compared to TopoNet (10.9), the improvement in TOP_ll is more than 2× (23.9 vs. 10.9), and the geometric-distance post-processing alone lifts TopoNet from 10.9 to 22.3 without retraining. With SDMap, TopoLogic (28.9 TOP_ll, 47.5 OLS) surpasses SMERF (15.4, 42.9) by a wide margin. On lane segment, TopoLogic beats LaneSegNet by +0.6 mAP and +5.4 TOP_lsls (v2.1.0), and outperforms MapTRv2 (28.5 mAP) by +4.7 mAP. The older baselines (STSU, VectorMapNet, MapTR) are significantly behind on all metrics, with TOP_ll scores below 6 versus TopoLogic's 23.9.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the topology reasoning pipeline established by TopoNet, TopoMLP, and SMERF, which all use a Deformable DETR-style decoder with GNN-based lane enhancement and a vanilla MLP head to predict lane connectivity from lane queries. The key departure is replacing that opaque MLP with two interpretable, complementary signals: a learnable mapping from geometric endpoint distances and a dot-product similarity between dual-MLP-encoded lane queries in semantic space. The geometric distance component is also shown to function as a plug-and-play post-processing module on already-trained models (TopoNet, SMERF, LaneSegNet), a usage mode not explored in prior topology reasoning work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical takeaway is that a simple geometric-distance post-processing step can more than double the topology reasoning score of existing SOTA models without any retraining, suggesting that the 'reasoning' head (MLP) has been the bottleneck rather than the perception pipeline. The interpretable formulation also makes failure modes easier to diagnose in safety-critical driving contexts. However, the authors note the GNN-based feature aggregation improves topology but does not substantially elevate lane detection accuracy, and the method is explicitly scoped to research rather than deployment. Open questions include whether the geometric distance criterion generalizes to denser or more occluded scenes where endpoint localization is unreliable, and whether the dual-MLP similarity mechanism scales to larger query sets.
