## Problem

Existing scene-graph-to-image (SG2I) methods face a fundamental trade-off: layout-based approaches model spatial topology well but cannot capture non-spatial interactions (e.g., 'playing', 'chasing'), while semantics-based approaches encode interactions via graph convolutions but frequently lose independent nodes or place them unreasonably. No prior method simultaneously disentangles spatial layout from interactive semantics in a one-to-many manner, limiting both generation diversity and controllability for complex scenes.

## Input / Output

The method takes a textual scene graph as input, where nodes carry object category labels and edges carry relationship labels, both augmented with CLIP text embeddings; during training, bounding box coordinates are additionally provided for node embeddings, but at inference the layout is generated by the model itself. Optional per-object attribute embeddings (e.g., color) can be appended for fine-grained control. The output is a full scene image (256×256 latent space decoded via VQ-VAE), and the framework also supports object-level graph manipulation—adding nodes or changing attributes—while preserving the visual content of unmodified objects.

## Method

DisCo builds on Stable Diffusion 1.5 and introduces three coupled components. First, a Semantics-Layout Variational AutoEncoder (SL-VAE) based on triplet-GCN encodes the scene graph into a shared Gaussian latent space, then decodes it into two disentangled streams: a layout decoder predicting bounding boxes and a semantic decoder producing interactive object embeddings, enabling one-to-many diverse generation from a single graph. Second, a Compositional Masked Attention (CMA) layer is inserted between self-attention and cross-attention in the U-Net; it concatenates visual tokens with object-level embeddings (semantics ⊗ Fourier-encoded boxes ⊗ attributes) and applies a binary attention mask derived from bounding boxes so that each visual token only attends to its own object's embedding, preventing relational confusion and attribute leakage. Third, a Multi-Layered Sampler (MLS) decomposes the scene into per-object layers, samples N_l diverse (layout, semantics) pairs from the SL-VAE, performs locally masked denoising per layer, and aggregates results via weighted averaging to achieve isolated editing (node addition, attribute change) while keeping the rest of the scene consistent. The entire pipeline is trained end-to-end with a combined objective of diffusion noise-prediction loss, KL divergence on the latent, and L1 layout reconstruction loss.

## Results

On the Visual Genome dataset, DisCo achieves an Inception Score of 22.3 and FID of 21.9, outperforming the best prior diffusion-based SG2I method R3CD (IS 18.9, FID 23.4) and all GAN-based methods. On COCO-Stuff, DisCo reaches IS 23.1 and FID 30.8, surpassing R3CD (IS 19.5, FID 32.9) and SceneGenie (IS 22.2, FID 63.3). On T2I-CompBench, DisCo leads all compared text-to-image methods on all four compositional metrics (UniDet 0.2376, CLIP 0.3217, B-VQA 0.6959, 3-in-1 0.4143), and in a 50-participant user study it scores 0.8533 versus 0.7055 for the next-best method (MIGC).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DisCo outperforms all 19 compared baselines across every reported metric. Against GAN-based SG2I methods (SG2Im, PasteGAN, SOAP, WSGC, KCGM), DisCo achieves substantially lower FID (e.g., 30.8 vs. 99.1 for SG2Im on COCO) and higher IS, reflecting the generative quality advantage of the diffusion backbone. Among diffusion-based SG2I methods, DisCo beats R3CD by 2.1 FID points on COCO (30.8 vs. 32.9) and 1.5 on VG (21.9 vs. 23.4), and surpasses SceneGenie by a large margin (FID 30.8 vs. 63.3 on COCO), confirming that jointly modeling layout and semantics is more effective than either alone. On T2I-CompBench, DisCo exceeds the strongest text-to-image baseline GORS on all four compositional scores (e.g., 3-in-1: 0.4143 vs. 0.3328), demonstrating that structured scene-graph conditioning captures spatial and non-spatial relationships better than linear text prompts. In the user study, DisCo (0.8533) leads MIGC (0.7055), R3CD (0.6928), and SD-XL (0.6684) by a wide margin, with no baseline coming within 0.15 points.

## Relation to Prior Work

DisCo sits at the intersection of two established SG2I paradigms: layout-based methods (SG2Im, SOAP, SceneGenie) that explicitly predict spatial arrangements but cannot model non-spatial interactions, and semantics-based methods (SGDiff, R3CD, KCGM) that encode relational semantics via GCNs but struggle with independent node placement. The paper's key architectural departure is using a Conditional VAE (SL-VAE) built on triplet-GCN to jointly model and then disentangle both spatial layout and interactive semantics from a single scene graph, enabling one-to-many generation rather than the one-to-one mapping typical of prior layout predictors. The diffusion backbone is Stable Diffusion 1.5, the graph encoding follows the triplet-GCN formulation from SOAP, and the Multi-Layered Sampler adapts the per-object layered denoising idea from SceneDiffusion by replacing random layout scrambling with SL-VAE-sampled coherent layouts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The disentanglement of layout and semantics through a VAE provides a principled mechanism for diverse yet faithful scene generation, addressing a long-standing limitation where prior methods had to choose between spatial fidelity and interaction modeling. The CMA mechanism offers a practical solution to attribute leakage between objects in compositional generation, and the MLS enables object-level graph manipulation (node addition, attribute control) while preserving visual consistency—capabilities that are directly useful for controllable content creation. However, the paper acknowledges that overlapping objects still suffer from attribute leakage in subsequent layers, and the nearly 3× inference time overhead (108 ms vs. 38 ms for vanilla SD) limits real-time applicability. Open questions include whether the approach scales to scenes with many more objects, whether the VAE's Gaussian parameterization is the optimal disentanglement strategy, and how the method generalizes to 3D scene generation.
