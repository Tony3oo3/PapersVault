## Problem

Group equivariant CNNs require the symmetry group to be specified in advance, which is often unrealistic for real-world data where exact symmetries are absent or only partially present. Fixed equivariance constraints can be overly restrictive and hurt performance when the assumed group does not match the data. The paper addresses the need for a weight-sharing scheme that dynamically discovers and applies symmetries as soft, learnable constraints without requiring prior knowledge of the group structure.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard images (28×28 grayscale MNIST, 32×32 RGB CIFAR-10) and outputs class labels via a classification head. Internally, the weight-sharing mechanism operates on flattened convolutional kernel weights of shape |X|×C_out×C_in, where |X| is the kernel support size, and applies a learned representation stack of shape |G|×|X|×|X| to generate the full kernel stack. For image data, a semi-direct product parametrization (R^n,+ ⋊ H) is used so that the representation stack only covers the compact sub-group H, with translations handled by standard Conv2D operators.

## Method

The core mechanism replaces fixed group representations in G-CNNs with a learnable stack of doubly stochastic matrices, each produced by applying K iterations of the Sinkhorn operator to a learnable parameter tensor. These matrices act as soft permutation matrices on a set of base kernel weights, generating the full kernel stack via matrix multiplication (W = R·θ). When the data exhibits exact group symmetries, the learned matrices converge to regular group representations and the network becomes a standard G-CNN; for partial or approximate symmetries, the doubly stochastic structure naturally captures relaxed equivariance. Two regularizers are applied: an entropy term encouraging sparsity (pushing matrices toward true permutations) and a normalization term accelerating Sinkhorn convergence. The approach is jointly optimized with the downstream task, requiring no meta-learning or separate symmetry-discovery phase, and can represent G-CNNs as a special case while remaining flexible to unknown or partial symmetries.

## Results

On MNIST with full SO(2) rotations, WSCNN+norm+ent achieves 98.04% accuracy (vs. 98.96% for C4-GCNN and 98.48% for a standard CNN), while on MNIST with scaling it reaches 99.24%, matching the unconstrained CNN (99.30%) and substantially outperforming the mis-specified C4-GCNN (97.50%). On CIFAR-10, WSCNN+norm achieves 78.80% accuracy with 2.1M total parameters, matching the 6.5M-parameter CNN-128 (78.83%) and outperforming the C4-GCNN (76.72%). On CIFAR-10 with horizontal flips, WSCNN reaches 82.38%, beating the C4-GCNN by 6.33 points and approaching the 6.5M CNN-128 (82.60%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is the C4-GCNN (Cohen & Welling, ICLR 2017), which imposes fixed 90°-rotation equivariance. On MNIST rotations, the C4-GCNN (98.96%) slightly edges out WSCNN+norm+ent (98.04%), as expected when the data symmetry aligns with the fixed group. However, on MNIST scaling, where the C4 constraint is mis-specified, WSCNN+norm (99.27%) outperforms the GCNN (97.50%) by nearly 2 points while matching the unconstrained CNN. On CIFAR-10, WSCNN+norm (78.80%) surpasses the C4-GCNN (76.72%) by 2.1 points and matches the 4×-larger CNN-128 (78.83%) at a fraction of the parameter budget. The advantage is most pronounced on CIFAR-10 with horizontal flips, where WSCNN (82.38%) beats the C4-GCNN (76.05%) by 6.33 points, since flips cannot be represented by C4 rotations. On partial rotations (0–180°), WSCNN (99.25%) also outperforms the C4-GCNN (98.72%), demonstrating its ability to adapt to incomplete symmetry groups.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the weight-sharing formulation of group equivariance (Cohen & Welling 2016; Ravanbakhsh et al. 2017) by replacing fixed permutation matrices with learnable doubly stochastic matrices enforced via the Sinkhorn operator, grounded in Birkhoff's theorem connecting doubly stochastic matrices to convex combinations of permutations. It departs most directly from Yeh et al. (2022), which also learns weight-sharing patterns but uses only row-stochastic matrices, requires meta-learning, and cannot jointly optimize the sharing pattern with downstream weights. Unlike partial-equivariance methods (Romero & Lohit 2021; Wang et al. 2022) that still require a pre-specified group, WSCNN needs no prior group structure and can represent regular group convolutions as a special case while also capturing partial or approximate symmetries.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method removes the practical bottleneck of specifying exact symmetry groups before training, which is often infeasible for real-world data with partial or noisy symmetries. Key limitations include quadratic scaling with group size and kernel dimension, a uniform representation stack across channels (preventing learning of transformations like color jitter), and sensitivity to regularization hyperparameters. Open questions include whether hierarchical or shared group structures across layers (e.g., via Cayley tensors) can improve both efficiency and interpretability, and whether the approach scales to higher-resolution or 3D data where the doubly stochastic tensor becomes prohibitively large.
