## Problem

Existing Video Corpus Moment Retrieval (VCMR) benchmarks rely on coarse-grained text queries, leading to many-to-many correspondences where multiple video moments could plausibly match a single query. This ambiguity makes ground-truth annotations unreliable and prevents models from learning distinctive fine-grained video features needed to discriminate between semantically similar moments in a large corpus.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241278/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The VERIFIED pipeline takes a video moment (defined by start/end timestamps within an untrimmed video) and its original coarse caption as input, and produces N_s fine-grained static caption candidates and N_d fine-grained dynamic caption candidates, each paired with a confidence score from the noise evaluator. For the downstream VCMR task, a fine-grained text query is matched against a large untrimmed video corpus, and the model outputs a temporal interval (t_s, t_e) for the best-matched moment. The benchmark datasets (Charades-FIG, DiDeMo-FIG, ActivityNet-FIG) contain approximately twice the content words and significantly more adjectives than their coarse-grained counterparts, with many-to-many pair counts reduced by over 85%.

## Method

VERIFIED is a three-module automatic annotation pipeline. The Statics Enhanced Captioning module extracts key frames via PySceneDetect, prompts an image LMM (LLaVA-1.6-Mistral-7B) to describe foreground and background attributes guided by the original coarse caption, then uses an LLM (Mistral-7B) to rewrite these into diverse fine-grained static captions. The Dynamics Enhanced Captioning module first has the LLM generate dynamics-oriented VQA questions, feeds sequential video frames (8 fps or 64 uniform frames) to a video LMM (Gemini-1.5-Pro) to answer them, and rewrites the answers into dynamic captions. To filter LLM/LMM hallucinations, the Fine-Granularity Aware Noise Evaluator fine-tunes UMT (ViT-L/16) with hard-negative augmented contrastive and matching losses, where hard negatives are LLM-generated captions with perturbed static or dynamic attributes selected via SentenceBERT semantic distance; the fine-tuned model then scores each generated caption, and only the highest-scoring caption per moment is retained.

## Results

On the proposed fine-grained VCMR benchmark, all state-of-the-art methods struggle significantly. The best VCMR performance (0.5/r5) reaches only 15.45% (CONQUER on DiDeMo-FIG), 12.87% (SQuiDNet on ActivityNet-FIG), and 7.98% (SQuiDNet on Charades-FIG), while 0.5/r100 peaks at 51.63% (CONQUER on DiDeMo-FIG). For Video Retrieval, SQuiDNet achieves the highest r1 scores (32.57% on ActivityNet-FIG, 16.94% on DiDeMo-FIG, 11.67% on Charades-FIG), but its VCMR performance is unstable. Training XML on fine-grained (FIG) vs. coarse-grained (COG) data yields large VCMR gains (e.g., 0.5/r5 on Charades-FIG: 2.63 vs. 0.89) but only marginal SVMR improvements, confirming that fine-grained details are critical for cross-video discrimination rather than within-video localization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CONQUER achieves the strongest overall VCMR performance, leading on DiDeMo-FIG (0.5/r5 = 15.45, 0.5/r100 = 51.63) and ActivityNet-FIG (0.5/r100 = 31.12, 0.7/r100 = 17.01), validating its two-stage query-context integration design. SQuiDNet excels in Video Retrieval (r1 = 32.57 on ActivityNet-FIG, 16.94 on DiDeMo-FIG) due to its learned video reranking, but shows highly unstable VCMR performance—dropping to 0.5/r1 = 0.52 on DiDeMo-FIG—suggesting that entangling video-level and moment-level learning harms fine-grained localization. XML and ReLoCLNet deliver comparable VCMR results across all three datasets, with ReLoCLNet's multi-granularity contrastive learning providing a slight edge on ActivityNet-FIG (0.5/r5 = 10.66 vs. 7.86). HERO, relying on a simple Temporal Transformer, consistently performs worst (e.g., 0.5/r5 = 0.27 on Charades-FIG), confirming that basic architectures lack capacity for fine-grained cross-modal alignment. The paper's ablation (Table 6) further shows that training on fine-grained FIG data improves VCMR 0.5/r5 by 2–3× over COG training (e.g., Charades-FIG: 2.63 vs. 0.89) while SVMR gains are modest, underscoring that the benchmark's difficulty lies in cross-video discrimination rather than within-video temporal grounding.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Video Corpus Moment Retrieval (VCMR) from its established coarse-grained formulation to a fine-grained setting where queries must discriminate among partially matched candidates. For annotation, it builds on the UMT video foundation model [17] as the backbone of a noise evaluator, and leverages recent LLMs (Mistral-7B) and LMMs (LLaVA, Gemini-1.5-Pro) for automatic captioning, departing from prior large-scale video captioning efforts (InternVid, Panda-70M, MVid) that lacked fine-grained dynamic details. The VQA-guided dynamics extraction and hard-negative-augmented contrastive fine-tuning for the evaluator represent novel combinations not seen in prior VCMR or video annotation pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals that state-of-the-art VCMR models trained on coarse annotations degrade substantially on fine-grained queries, with the many-to-many ambiguity dropping dramatically (e.g., Charades: 8805→422 instances), yet absolute performance remains very low (best VCMR 0.5/r1 ≈ 5%), indicating a large gap in fine-grained video understanding. The finding that fine-grained training data significantly boosts cross-video retrieval (VCMR/VR) but only marginally improves within-video localization (SVMR) suggests the core challenge is learning distinctive features across similar clips rather than temporal precision. Open questions include closing the gap between perturbation-based hard negatives and real LLM hallucinations, and whether an end-to-end captioning model can replace the multi-model VERIFIED pipeline.
