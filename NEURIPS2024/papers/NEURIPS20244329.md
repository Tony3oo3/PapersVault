## Problem

Poisson regression is the standard model for count data, yet no sublinear data reduction technique with rigorous (1±ε) approximation guarantees was known for it. While impossibility results existed for the canonical log-link, the practically important ID-link (p=1) and square root-link (p=2) remained unanalyzed. This paper provides the first complete characterization: Ω(n) lower bounds in the worst case, and sublinear coreset constructions under a natural model-fit assumption.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a design matrix X ∈ R^{n×d} (with an intercept column), nonnegative integer count labels y ∈ N₀^n, a link parameter p ∈ {1,2}, and a target accuracy ε. It outputs a weighted coreset (K, w) of size k that approximates the negative log-likelihood within (1±ε) for all parameter vectors β in a shifted feasible domain D(η) = {β : ∀i, x_iβ > η}. Crucially, the extreme points on the convex hull of X must be stored separately to define the feasible domain, and the coreset size depends on a data-dependent complexity parameter ρ that quantifies how well the realized data fits the Poisson model.

## Method

The approach combines sensitivity sampling with three novel ingredients. First, a complexity parameter ρ (Equation 2) measures the ratio of predicted rate to residual under the pth-root-link, and small ρ implies the data is well-captured by the Poisson model. Second, a domain shifting technique restricts optimization to D(η) with η = Θ(ε), avoiding the infinite asymptote of the −log(z) term near z = 0 that causes Ω(n) lower bounds. Third, the loss function is bounded using a translated lower envelope (z − y^{1/p})^p / λ rather than z^p / λ, which keeps λ sublinear in y_max: λ = O(√(y_max/log(y_max))) for p=1 (tight, proven via a new square-root bound on the Lambert W₀ function) and λ = 1 for p=2. VC dimension is bounded near-linearly by grouping y-values into powers of (1+ε) and sensitivities into powers of 2, reducing each group to a hyperplane-classifier argument.

## Results

The main result (Theorem 3.8) establishes that for ρ-complex datasets, a weighted coreset of size k = Õ(ε⁻²d · min{d, ε⁻¹log(n)log(y_max)} · m) achieves a (1±ε) approximation for Poisson pth-root-link regression. For the ID-link (p=1), the parameter m = ρd·log log(d)·√(y_max/log(y_max)) + log log(1/η), while for the square root-link (p=2), m = ρd + log(y_max) + log log(1/η). Lower bounds (Lemmas 6.1–6.2) show Ω(n) points are necessary without the ρ-complexity assumption, and the λ parameter is tightly characterized as Θ(√(y/log(y))) for p=1 via a novel Lambert W₀ bound (Lemma 6.3). The VC dimension is bounded by Õ(d/ε) (Corollary 3.6), and the domain shifting approach (Theorem 4.2) converts the coreset on D(ε) into a (1+ε) approximation on the original domain D(0).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no experimental baselines or empirical comparisons. The key theoretical contrasts are: (1) against the log-link Poisson model, which was shown incompressible (Ω(n)) in [31], the pth-root-link admits sublinear coresets under the ρ-complexity assumption; (2) against previous GLM coreset work on logistic [36] and probit [34] regression, this is the first rigorous (1±ε) finite-sample analysis for Poisson models; (3) between the two practical link choices, the square root-link (p=2) achieves a strictly better dependence on y_max — O(log(y_max)) versus Θ̃(√(y_max)) for the ID-link (p=1) — because the slope parameter λ is constant for p=2 but grows as √(y/log(y)) for p=1; (4) the domain shifting technique is shown to fail for p≥3 (Lemma 6.5), indicating a fundamental limitation of the current analysis to p∈{1,2}.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the sensitivity-sampling coreset framework [26, 19]—previously applied to ℓ_p regression [12, 16], logistic regression [36], and probit regression [34]—to Poisson regression with pth-root-link functions, a setting where prior work had only established impossibility results for the log-link [31] or asymptotic (non-finite) guarantees [3, 27]. The novel complexity parameter ρ is directly inspired by the μ-complexity parameter from [36], and the well-conditioned basis machinery is inherited from [12]. The key departure is the domain shifting technique, which has no analogue in prior GLM coreset work, motivated by the unique logarithmic singularity of the Poisson loss that creates infinite sensitivities near zero.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first rigorous finite-sample (1±ε) coreset construction for Poisson regression, resolving a gap left by the impossibility results for the log-link [31] and the asymptotic-only guarantees of [3, 27]. The domain shifting approach and the tight separation between p=1 (√(y_max/log y_max) dependence) and p=2 (log y_max dependence) provide clear guidance on which link function admits the most compressible data summaries. The p≥3 intractability result (Lemma 6.5) indicates that the current analytical toolkit is fundamentally limited to the two most practically relevant link functions, leaving open whether any data reduction is possible for higher-order root-links. The improved Lambert W₀ bound, while technical, may find independent use in optimization and control theory.
