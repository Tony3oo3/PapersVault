## Problem

Blockwise parallel decoding (BPD) accelerates LLM inference by drafting multiple tokens in parallel, but the independently-generated block drafts suffer from consecutive token repetition and inconsistent cross-head coherence, limiting the number of tokens accepted per verification step. This paper identifies these failure modes through systematic analysis of draft token distributions and proposes lightweight rescoring algorithms that improve draft quality without modifying the underlying model parameters.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241081/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a blockwise parallel LM with h prediction heads (e.g., 5 heads for Vicuna 7B/13B) and a text prompt, and produces refined block drafts that are verified against the base LM's greedy decode. The top-k predictions at each head form a 'sausage lattice' (a weighted directed graph with k^h paths), which is the intermediate structure consumed by the rescoring algorithms. The final output is identical to the base LM's autoregressive output (guaranteed by the verification step), but produced with fewer serial forward passes. For the p-best n-gram variant, multiple candidate drafts are verified in parallel via tree-attention.

## Method

Two complementary rescoring algorithms refine the top-k block draft lattice. Local neural rescoring (Algorithm 2) uses a small decoder-only transformer (32M–94M parameters) to autoregressively rescore each head's top-k candidates by interpolating the rescorer's logits with the block draft logits via a weight α, conditioning on previously drafted tokens to encourage inter-head coherence. Global n-gram rescoring represents the k^h lattice as a weighted finite-state automaton and uses dynamic programming (via OpenFST) to extract the p most probable rescored sequences in poly(k,h) time, enabling parallel verification of multiple drafts. The key empirical insight driving both methods is that block head entropy increases monotonically with head index (correlating with block efficiency at R=0.77), and that even a trivial 2-gram rescorer eliminates 50–75% of consecutive token repetitions. Applied to Vicuna 7B/13B with Medusa decoding, the approach yields 5–25% relative gains in mean accepted token length and over 3× wall-clock speedup versus standard autoregressive decoding.

## Results

On the 1.5B blockwise parallel LM, lattice rescoring improves block efficiency by 5–25% relative to vanilla BPD, with the largest gains on NewsRoom (+21.3% with 16-best 4-gram BPD) and MultiNews (+13.6% with 61M neural rescoring). On open-source Vicuna 7B/13B LLMs, Medusa + local neural rescoring achieves up to 3.01× wall-clock speedup over standard autoregressive decoding (GSM8K, Temp=1.0, Vicuna 13B), and consistently outperforms both BPD and Medusa alone across all five evaluation datasets (MT-Bench, S-Sum, QA, GSM8K, RAG) at temperatures 0.0, 0.7, and 1.0.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against vanilla BPD (Stern et al., 2018), local neural rescoring consistently improves speedup by 3–9% on Vicuna models (e.g., 1.745→1.819 on MT-Bench for Vicuna 13B) and by up to 21% in block efficiency on the 1.5B model. Against Medusa (Cai et al., 2024), the proposed rescoring adds a further 2–7% speedup (e.g., 2.383→2.467 on MT-Bench, 1.945→2.068 on RAG for Vicuna 13B), confirming the two techniques are complementary. In the cross-method comparison (Table 7), Medusa + Local rescoring dominates Speculative Sampling (Chen et al., 2023) by 0.5–1.0× across all datasets, and substantially outperforms Lookahead Decoding (Fu et al., 2024), which degrades below 1× speedup on several Vicuna 13B tasks. Prompt Lookup Decoding (Saxena, 2023) is highly inconsistent—strong on S-Sum (2.707×) but weak on QA (1.162×)—whereas the proposed method maintains 1.5×+ speedup uniformly. Overall, the paper's method is the only one to deliver consistent >2× speedup across all five datasets and both model sizes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Blockwise Parallel Decoding (BPD) [Stern et al., 2018] and its recent popularization via Medusa [Cai et al., 2024] by inserting a lightweight rescoring step between draft generation and verification. It borrows the 'sausage lattice' (confusion network) representation and finite-state composition machinery from the ASR/MT literature [Koehn et al., 2007; Shen et al., 2006], applying OpenFST-based n-gram rescoring and small neural LM interpolation to refine top-k block drafts. Unlike speculative decoding, which relies on a separate draft model, and unlike Medusa's tree-attention verification, this work targets the quality of the draft itself without modifying base model weights, making it composable with existing BPD/Medusa pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical takeaway is that a 68M-parameter rescoring model can consistently improve the accepted-token length of 7B/13B LLMs by 5–25%, yielding over 3× wall-clock speedup over autoregressive decoding without any retraining of the base model. The diagnostic finding that consecutive-token repetition is a primary failure mode of vanilla BPD drafts—and that even a 2-gram rescorer substantially reduces it—gives practitioners a concrete lever for improving draft quality. Open questions include whether the approach scales to 70B+ models, how it interacts with non-greedy sampling (the paper only briefly touches temperature sampling), and whether better-trained draft heads (e.g., with block-level context) would reduce the need for external rescoring. The absence of released code and error bars limits immediate reproducibility.
