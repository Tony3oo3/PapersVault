## Problem

Existing structure-based drug design (SBDD) methods treat target proteins as rigid bodies, ignoring the conformational changes that occur upon ligand binding (the 'induced fit' mechanism). This rigidity assumption produces generated complexes with excessive steric clashes, restricts the chemical search space to predefined pocket geometries, and creates a large gap between computational predictions and real-world binding behavior. FlexSBDD addresses this by jointly modeling protein flexibility and de novo ligand generation within a unified generative framework.

## Input / Output

The method takes an apo (ligand-free) protein structure represented compactly at the residue level—Cα coordinates, SO(3) backbone frame orientations, and up to 4 sidechain dihedral angles per residue—along with an initialized ligand whose atom count is sampled from the training distribution, atom types are set to a uniform distribution, and coordinates are drawn from a Gaussian within the binding pocket. The output is a complete protein-ligand complex: a 3D ligand molecule with predicted atom types and coordinates, plus an updated holo protein structure with adjusted backbone positions, orientations, and sidechain conformations. The entire generation is a single ODE integration from t=0 to t=1 using 20 Euler steps, making it substantially faster than diffusion-based alternatives that may require 1000+ steps.

## Method

FlexSBDD formulates ligand generation and protein conformational change as a Riemannian flow matching problem, defining separate conditional probability paths on different manifolds: linear interpolation in R³ for Cα and ligand atom coordinates, geodesic paths via exponential/logarithmic maps on SO(3) for backbone frame orientations, regularized linear interpolation on the hypertorus T^(4Np) for sidechain dihedral angles, and linear interpolation on the probability simplex for discrete atom types. The vector field is parameterized by an E(3)-equivariant graph neural network using scalar-vector dual features (GVP/GVL building blocks) over a KNN graph of the protein-ligand complex, with attention-based message passing that preserves geometric equivariance. A key practical contribution is a data augmentation pipeline that generates synthetic apo conformations via OpenMM structure relaxation and Rosetta sidechain repacking (9 variants per holo structure), addressing the scarcity of natural apo-holo pairs. The training loss is a weighted sum of the four component losses (atom type cross-entropy, coordinate L2, orientation SO(3) distance, and sidechain angle L2), and the model is trained with Adam for 500k iterations on a single A100 GPU.

## Results

On CrossDocked, FlexSBDD achieves an average Vina Dock score of −9.12, surpassing the strongest baseline DecompDiff (−8.39) by 0.73, and a High Affinity rate of 78.5% vs. 64.4% for DecompDiff. On Binding MOAD, the gap widens to 0.92 in Avg. Vina Dock (−9.38 vs. −8.46). FlexSBDD also generates ligands with far fewer steric clashes (1.39 vs. 6.43 for DecompDiff) and more hydrogen bond acceptors (1.96 vs. 1.18), while maintaining competitive QED (0.58) and SA (0.69) scores.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FlexSBDD outperforms all five SBDD baselines on every binding-affinity metric across both CrossDocked and Binding MOAD. Against the strongest diffusion-based baseline DecompDiff, FlexSBDD gains 0.73 in Avg. Vina Dock on CrossDocked and 0.92 on Binding MOAD, while also improving High Affinity by 14.1 and 18.1 percentage points respectively. Compared to the autoregressive baselines (AR, Pocket2Mol), FlexSBDD leads by 2.37 and 1.97 in Avg. Vina Dock on CrossDocked, and also achieves higher diversity (0.76 vs. 0.70/0.69), indicating broader chemical-space exploration enabled by flexible protein modeling. Against the VAE-based LiGAN, FlexSBDD improves Vina Dock by 2.79 and High Affinity by 57.4 percentage points. In terms of physical realism, FlexSBDD reduces steric clashes from 6.43 (DecompDiff) to 1.39 and increases hydrogen bond acceptors from 1.18 to 1.96, demonstrating that the flexible protein adjustment directly yields more favorable protein–ligand interactions. For sidechain validity, FlexSBDD achieves lower MAE on all four dihedral angles (χ₁–χ₄) compared to NeuralPlexer, the state-of-the-art protein–ligand complex structure predictor.

## Relation to Prior Work

FlexSBDD extends the SBDD task from rigid-protein ligand generation (TargetDiff, DecompDiff, Pocket2Mol) to joint protein-ligand co-generation by introducing Riemannian flow matching over multiple manifolds (SO(3) for backbone orientation, hypertorus for sidechain dihedrals, R³ for coordinates, and simplex for atom types). The generative framework builds on flow matching (Lipman et al., Albergo et al.) and its Riemannian extension (Chen & Lipman), while the protein backbone parameterization follows SE(3) flow matching work (Yim et al., Bose et al.). The E(3)-equivariant encoder-decoder with scalar-vector dual features (GVP/GVL) is inherited from geometric deep learning for 3D molecules (Jing et al., Peng et al.), and the data augmentation via OpenMM relaxation and Rosetta repacking is a novel contribution to address the scarcity of apo-holo structure pairs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By explicitly modeling protein conformational change during ligand generation, FlexSBDD addresses a fundamental disconnect between computational SBDD and the induced-fit reality of protein-ligand binding, reducing steric clashes by ~4.5× and increasing hydrogen bond acceptors by ~66% over the strongest baseline. The KRAS G12C case study demonstrating cryptic pocket rediscovery (H95 sidechain rotation matching experimental PDB 6P8Z) suggests practical utility for drugging previously 'undruggable' targets. Key limitations include restriction to small molecules (no antibodies/peptides), dependence on limited training data (~40k–100k pairs), and the single-GPU training cost (~36 hours on A100). Open questions include whether the flexible modeling generalizes to larger proteins, whether the approach scales with data from AlphaFold 3 or RoseTTAFold All-Atom, and whether the discovered cryptic pockets translate to experimentally validated inhibitors.
