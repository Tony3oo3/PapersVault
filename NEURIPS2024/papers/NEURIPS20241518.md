## Problem

Standard Bayesian optimisation algorithms waste samples on invariant objectives by repeatedly querying points that are equivalent under a known group of transformations, and constrained BO (which restricts the search to a fundamental domain) still fails to share information across domain boundaries. No prior work provided regret or sample-complexity bounds for kernelised bandit optimisation with general group-invariant kernels, leaving the quantitative gain from invariance uncharacterised.

## Input / Output

The learner receives a finite group of isometries G acting on a compact domain X (e.g., the hypersphere S^{d-1}), a simultaneously invariant base kernel k (e.g., Matérn), and sequential noisy observations y_t = f(x_t) + η_t of a G-invariant objective f. The algorithm outputs a single point x* ∈ X (in the full domain, not the fundamental domain) after T rounds, with the guarantee that f(x*) is ε-optimal with high probability. The group size |G| directly controls the sample-efficiency gain, but also the per-step computational cost of evaluating the invariant kernel.

## Method

The core mechanism is the symmetrization operator S_G, which averages a simultaneously invariant kernel k over all group elements to produce a totally invariant kernel k_G(x,y) = (1/|G|) Σ_σ k(σ(x), y), whose associated RKHS contains exactly the G-invariant functions. Standard BO algorithms (UCB and MVR) are then run with k_G in place of k, so that each observation at x automatically informs the posterior at all transformed locations σ(x). The theoretical contribution derives an upper bound on the maximum information gain γ_T^G = Õ((1/|G|) T^{(d-1)/β_p*}) via the spherical-harmonic spectral decomposition of the invariant kernel, yielding a sample-complexity bound T = Õ((1/|G|)^{β_p*/(β_p*-d+1)} ε^{-2β_p*/(β_p*-d+1)}). A matching lower bound is obtained by constructing a family of symmetrized bump functions with disjoint supports and applying a Fano-type packing argument, confirming that the 1/|G| improvement is essentially tight.

## Results

The paper derives novel upper and lower bounds on sample complexity for invariance-aware BO, showing that the number of samples needed for ε-optimality scales as Õ((1/|G|)^((2ν+d−1)/(2ν)) · ε^(-(2ν+d−1)/ν)) for Matérn-ν kernels, explicitly quantifying the gain from group size |G|. Empirically, in the PermInv-6D task (720-element permutation group), non-invariant UCB and MVR fail to locate the true optimum, while the invariant-kernel versions converge with far fewer samples. In the nuclear fusion application, non-invariant UCB 'totally fails to find the small region of high-performance solutions,' whereas invariant UCB achieves significantly higher objective scores. Partially specified subgroup kernels (2-block, |G|=6; 3-block, |G|=24) achieve sample-efficiency gains comparable to the full invariant kernel at roughly 2× lower computational cost.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against standard GP-UCB with a non-invariant Matérn kernel [37], the invariant-kernel UCB consistently achieves lower regret across all three synthetic tasks (PermInv-2D, PermInv-6D, CyclInv-3D), with the gap widening in higher dimensions and for larger groups; in the fusion application, non-invariant UCB queries many permutation-equivalent suboptimal profiles and never reaches the high-performance region, while invariant UCB does. Against standard GP-MVR [42], the invariant-kernel MVR similarly outperforms, and in PermInv-6D the non-invariant MVR fails to find the optimum entirely. Against Constrained BO (CBO) [16], which restricts the acquisition function's search to the group's fundamental domain, the invariant-kernel method performs better: CBO's kernel is unaware of the full function-class structure, so observations contribute no information across region boundaries, and in high dimensions CBO becomes effectively identical to standard non-invariant BO. The paper also shows that subgroup-invariant kernels (incorporating only partial invariance) still outperform the fully non-invariant baseline, confirming that even approximate symmetry exploitation yields meaningful sample-efficiency gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the invariant kernel framework (Haasdonk & Burkhardt 2007; Kondor 2008) from the regression setting to kernelised bandit optimisation, combining it with the Matérn kernel family and the regret-bound machinery of Srinivas et al. (2010) and Vakili et al. (2021). The upper bound on maximum information gain for invariant kernels generalises the spectral-decay analysis of Vakili et al. (2021) and the NTK-invariance results of Kassraie & Krause (2022) to arbitrary finite subgroups of O(d) with polynomial eigendecay kernels. The lower bound adapts the packing-based construction of Cai & Scarlett (2021) to the invariant RKHS via the symmetrization operator, a step that is non-trivial because the tightest packing of invariant bump functions is a priori unclear. The paper also positions itself against constrained BO (Gardner et al. 2014), showing that restricting the search domain without modifying the kernel is strictly weaker than kernel-level invariance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The clean 1/|G| factor in the sample complexity bound gives practitioners a principled way to quantify the benefit of exploiting known symmetries, and the empirical finding that partial (subgroup) invariance captures most of the gain at a fraction of the computational cost is directly actionable for problems where the full symmetry group is too large (e.g., 12! for the fusion application). The gap between the upper and lower bound exponents on 1/|G| (1+(d-1)/ν vs. 1+(d-1)/(2ν)) remains open and likely reflects the difficulty of optimal packing of invariant functions. A key limitation is that the method requires the group action to be known and the kernel to be simultaneously invariant, which may not hold in all physical settings; the quasi-invariance experiments suggest robustness but lack a formal guarantee. The nuclear fusion application demonstrates that the sample-efficiency gain is not merely theoretical—non-invariant BO fails entirely on the 12-dimensional launcher design problem while the invariant variant succeeds.
