## Problem

DDPM-based time series generation methods, while state-of-the-art in quality, suffer from excessively long inference times due to many denoising steps and still leave room for improvement in generated sample fidelity. This paper explores whether discrete token modeling (DTM)—a technique proven effective in image and video generation—can be adapted to multivariate time series to simultaneously address both the speed and quality gaps.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20244200/cropped_page3_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a multivariate time series X_{1:τ} ∈ R^{τ×d} (τ time steps, d variables ranging from 5 to 50 across datasets) and first tokenizes it into a shorter discrete sequence Y_{1:L} of length L = τ/r via a VQ-VAE encoder with a codebook of K entries. For unconditional generation, a random sample initializes the autoregressive or masked decoder to produce a full synthetic sequence; for conditional forecasting, a historical segment X_{1:l} is prepended as context, and the model predicts the remaining τ−l steps without any retraining. The tokenizer's temporal downsampling rate r (typically 4) means the transformer operates on a sequence roughly 4× shorter than the original time series.

## Method

SDformer is a two-stage pipeline. Stage 1 pre-trains a VQ-VAE tokenizer whose encoder (1D convolutions + dilated ResNet blocks) maps time series to latent vectors, which are quantized by a codebook using a similarity-driven objective—selecting the code that maximizes cosine similarity rather than minimizing L2 distance—and whose decoder reconstructs the time series; EMA codebook updates and periodic code reset prevent collapse. Stage 2 freezes the tokenizer and trains a discrete Transformer over the token sequence in one of two modes: SDformer-ar uses a decoder-only Transformer with autoregressive next-token prediction plus a random-replacement augmentation to close the train-inference gap, while SDformer-m uses an encoder-only Transformer trained with random masking and decoded via iterative unmasking with a decreasing mask schedule. The key architectural insight is that similarity-driven quantization yields substantially better discrete representations than the standard distance-driven VQ-VAE, and that the resulting compact token sequences allow even a lightweight Transformer to capture the data distribution effectively, enabling fast single-pass inference and zero-shot adaptation to forecasting tasks.

## Results

SDformer-ar achieves the best Discriminative Score across all six datasets, with values ranging from 0.003 (ETTh) to 0.017 (fMRI), substantially outperforming the strongest baseline Diffusion-TS (0.006–0.167). On Context-FID Score, SDformer-ar scores between 0.001 (Sines) and 0.015 (fMRI), compared to Diffusion-TS's 0.006–0.105. The paper reports an average improvement of 60.8% in Discriminative Score and 86.5% in Context-FID Score over competing methods. SDformer-ar also matches or approaches the original data's Predictive Score (e.g., 0.093 vs. 0.094 on Sines), indicating high-fidelity generation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SDformer-ar dominates all six baselines on both Discriminative Score and Context-FID Score across every dataset. Against the strongest competitor Diffusion-TS [42], SDformer-ar reduces the Discriminative Score by 95% on Energy (0.006 vs. 0.122) and 90% on fMRI (0.017 vs. 0.167), and cuts Context-FID by 97% on Energy (0.003 vs. 0.089) and 86% on fMRI (0.015 vs. 0.105). GAN-based methods (TimeGAN [39], Cot-GAN [37]) and VAE-based methods (TimeVAE [7]) are consistently far behind, with Discriminative Scores often approaching 0.5 (random chance) on high-dimensional datasets like Energy and fMRI. Diffusion-based baselines Diffwave [19] and DiffTime [6] perform better than GAN/VAE methods but still trail SDformer-ar by 5–10× on Context-FID for Stocks and ETTh. Notably, SDformer-ar also achieves faster inference (≈2.7 s vs. ≈27–33 s for Diffusion-TS) and transfers to conditional forecasting without retraining, a capability none of the baselines demonstrate.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper transplants the Discrete Token Modeling (DTM) paradigm—established for image generation (DALL-E, Parti, MaskGIT, MUSE) and video generation (MAGVIT, Phenaki)—into the time series generation domain, where the current state of the art is dominated by DDPM-based methods (Diffusion-TS, DiffTime, DiffWave). The tokenizer stage builds directly on VQ-VAE [31] with EMA codebook updates and Code Reset from hierarchical quantized autoencoders [34], while the second stage adopts autoregressive modeling with random replacement from T2M-GPT [43] and masked modeling with iterative decoding from MaskGIT [5]. The key conceptual departure is the introduction of similarity-driven (cosine) vector quantization in place of the standard L2-distance-driven quantization, motivated by the observation that time series latent representations benefit from direction-based rather than magnitude-based matching.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SDformer addresses two practical pain points of DDPM-based time series generators: prohibitively long inference times (reduced from ~30s to ~2.7s) and a quality ceiling that discrete token modeling appears to break. The finding that a single model trained for unconditional generation can be directly repurposed for forecasting at multiple horizons without retraining is practically valuable for deployment. However, the approach requires a large codebook for best performance (creating memory pressure), has only been validated on six datasets of moderate dimensionality (5–50 features), and the generality of the similarity-driven VQ advantage to other modalities remains untested. Open questions include whether more powerful language model backbones (e.g., LLMs with attention sinks or mixture-of-experts) can further improve generation quality, and whether the method scales to very long sequences or high-dimensional settings beyond 50 variables.
