## Problem

Existing DDPM-based pansharpening methods treat PAN and LrMSI as undifferentiated fusion conditions, failing to exploit the distinct spatial and spectral information each source provides. While dual-branch CNN architectures can hierarchically learn from each source separately, their cumbersome structure makes localized fine-tuning difficult, and no prior diffusion-based method has been designed around the discriminative spatial-spectral decomposition that pansharpening fundamentally requires.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242477/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a high-resolution panchromatic (PAN) image and a low-resolution multispectral (LrMSI) image as separate inputs to the spatial and spectral branches respectively, rather than concatenating them. During training, the denoising target is the difference between the ground-truth HrMSI and the up-sampled LrMSI (following FusionNet's detail-injection strategy), and the final output is reconstructed by adding the up-sampled LrMSI back. The diffusion process uses 1000 timesteps for training and 10 timesteps for inference, with the model predicting x₀ directly rather than the noise ε.

## Method

SSDiff reframes pansharpening as a subspace decomposition problem, routing PAN through a spatial branch (ResNet-based encoder-decoder) and LrMSI through a spectral branch, then fusing them via an Alternating Projection Fusion Module (APFM) that generalizes self-attention into alternating vector projections between spatial and spectral domains (Theorem 1). A Frequency Modulation Inter-branch Module (FMIM) applies a Fourier mask to extract high-frequency components from the spatial branch and injects them into the spectral branch with channel-wise scaling constants (1.2/1.4/1.6) to prevent low-frequency dominance during denoising. For fine-tuning, the proposed LoRA-like Branch-wise Alternative Fine-tuning (L-BAF) alternately updates one branch while detaching gradients at the APFM, enabling discriminative feature learning without increasing parameter count. The theoretical grounding connects vector projection (Lemma 1) and subspace decomposition (Definition 1) to the self-attention mechanism, showing that APFM naturally decouples spatial and spectral information into a low-rank matrix factorization analogous to LoRA.

## Results

On the WorldView-3 reduced-resolution dataset (20 test images), SSDiff achieves SAM 2.84, ERGAS 2.11, Q2n 0.9156, and SCC 0.9867, outperforming the best competing DL method DCFNet (SAM 3.03, ERGAS 2.16) and the prior diffusion-based method PanDiff (SAM 3.30, ERGAS 2.47). On full-resolution WV3, SSDiff attains HQNR 0.9565 with Dλ 0.0132 and Ds 0.0307, substantially surpassing MMNet (HQNR 0.9143) and DCFNet (HQNR 0.8771). On GaoFen-2 reduced resolution, SSDiff reaches SAM 0.67, ERGAS 0.60, Q2n 0.9836, and SCC 0.9915, and on QuickBird reduced resolution it achieves SAM 4.46, ERGAS 3.63, Q4 0.9346, and SCC 0.9829 — all state-of-the-art among the 12 compared methods. Inference takes 7.417 s per image at 10 timesteps, roughly 35× faster than PanDiff (261.4 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SSDiff outperforms all 12 baselines across every dataset and resolution setting. Against the strongest DL-based competitor DCFNet, SSDiff reduces SAM by ~6% on WV3 (2.84 vs 3.03) and by ~24% on GF2 (0.67 vs 0.89), while improving HQNR on full-resolution WV3 from 0.877 to 0.957. Compared to the only other diffusion-based pansharpening method, PanDiff, SSDiff achieves substantially lower SAM (2.84 vs 3.30 on WV3; 0.67 vs 0.89 on GF2) and higher HQNR (0.957 vs 0.920 on WV3 full-res), while also running ~35× faster (7.4 s vs 261.4 s per image). Traditional methods (BDSD-PC, MTF-GLP-FS, BT-H) are consistently the weakest, with SAM values 1.5–2× higher than SSDiff on WV3 and 2–3× higher on GF2. On the cross-dataset generalization test (trained on WV3, evaluated on WV2), SSDiff again leads all DL baselines, achieving SAM 5.06 vs DCFNet's 5.62 and ERGAS 3.99 vs 4.49, confirming that the spatial-spectral decoupling generalizes beyond the training sensor.

## Relation to Prior Work

SSDiff builds on the DDPM generative framework (Ho et al., 2020) and the dual-branch pansharpening paradigm (PMACNet, U2Net), but departs from prior DDPM-based pansharpening methods (e.g., PanDiff) by introducing discriminative spatial and spectral branches rather than treating PAN and LrMSI as undifferentiated fusion conditions. Conceptually, it repurposes the low-rank decomposition idea from LoRA (Hu et al., 2021) not as a parameter-adaptation tool but as an alternating projection fusion mechanism (APFM), grounded in a vector-projection/subspace-decomposition interpretation of the self-attention operation. The frequency modulation inter-branch module (FMIM) addresses a practical gap in dual-branch diffusion models where low-frequency spectral information can dominate during denoising.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a useful theoretical bridge linking subspace decomposition, vector projection, and self-attention in the pansharpening context, offering a principled justification for the APFM design. The L-BAF alternating fine-tuning strategy is a practical contribution for dual-branch models, showing that full fine-tuning can overfit while branch-wise alternating updates improve discriminative feature learning. However, the 7.4-second inference time (vs. ~0.3–0.5 s for CNN baselines) and the additional FMIM hyperparameters limit immediate deployment, and the authors note the method has not yet been extended to other fusion tasks such as multispectral-hyperspectral fusion. Open questions include whether the projection-based fusion framing generalizes beyond pansharpening and whether faster sampling (e.g., DDIM) can close the efficiency gap without quality loss.
