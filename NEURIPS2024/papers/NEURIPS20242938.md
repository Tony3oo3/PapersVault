## Problem

Existing video causal reasoning tasks are confined to question-answering paradigms on short videos containing a single event or simple causal links, leaving no method to produce a comprehensive, structured causal diagram for long multi-event videos. This paper introduces the Multi-Event Causal Discovery (MECD) task and benchmark to uncover which chronologically distributed events causally lead to a final result event in long-form videos.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242938/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes N chronologically ordered events (4–11 per video), each comprising a video clip (50 frames) and a text caption (~13.5 words), and produces a binary causal-relation vector r = [r_1, …, r_{N-1}] indicating which premise events causally contribute to the final event. The setting is generalizable: truncating the video and redefining any event as the 'last' event yields a full causal diagram without retraining. During inference, a premise event is masked by zeroing its video frames and replacing its caption with constant characters, and the model compares the two prediction streams to infer causality.

## Method

VGCM (Video Granger Causality Model) is a multi-modal transformer that runs two parallel streams—an unmasked stream containing all premise events and a masked stream with one event removed—through a shared VideoBERT-based decoder, then compares the resulting output features via a relation head to estimate causal contribution (the Event Causality Test, inspired by Granger Causality). To handle causality confounding introduced by masking, the model applies front-door adjustment to compensate for the lost causal effect of the preceding event e_{k-1} and counterfactual inference to remove the redundant causal effect of the subsequent event e_{k+1}. Illusory temporal causality is suppressed by feeding a GPT-4-generated chain-of-thought reasoning trace into the do-operation, while illusory existence causality is mitigated by replacing the masked event's caption with a GPT-4-generated existence-only description that preserves object identity without causal semantics. Training uses a composite loss combining caption prediction, visual reconstruction, supervised causal-relation classification, and an auxiliary similarity term that enforces feature invariance when non-causal events are masked.

## Results

On the MECD test set, the full VGCM framework achieves 71.2% top-1 accuracy and an average Structural Hamming Distance (Ave SHD) of 4.19, outperforming GPT-4o (65.5%, SHD 4.69) by 5.7% and fine-tuned VideoLLaVA (67.1%, SHD 4.73) by 4.1%. The VGCM without causal inference (VGCM†) already reaches 67.0% accuracy, showing the base Granger-causality design is competitive, while the addition of front-door adjustment and counterfactual intervention adds a further 4.2% accuracy gain. Inference speed is 0.76 s/sample, 3–6× faster than all compared Video LLMs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VGCM outperforms all 10 baselines on both accuracy and Ave SHD. Against the strongest few-shot LLM/VLLM baselines, VGCM beats GPT-4o by 5.7% accuracy (71.2 vs. 65.5) and GPT-4-0613 by 11.6% (71.2 vs. 59.6), with Gemini-1.5-Pro trailing by 11.9%. Among fine-tuned VLLMs, VGCM surpasses VideoLLaVA by 4.1% and VideoChat2 by 4.3%, while also improving over the fine-tuned CLIP (ViT-L/14) by 8.3% and VideoBERT by 10.3%. The closest fine-tuned multi-modal baseline, VAR (57.3%), is outperformed by a wide 13.9% margin. Notably, VGCM's inference speed (0.76 s/sample) is 3–6× faster than all Video LLM baselines (2.12–4.72 s), making it practical for real-time multi-event causal analysis. The gap to human performance (87.2%) remains substantial, indicating room for further improvement in higher-level semantic causal reasoning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the Granger Causality framework—originally designed for low-dimensional time-series data—to the multi-modal video domain, where temporal causality is estimated by comparing event predictions with and without a premise event masked. It departs from prior video causal reasoning work (CLEVRER, CATER, VAR, BiGED), which is confined to short videos, single-event QA, or virtual scenes, by formulating a structured multi-event causal diagram discovery task over long-form daily-life videos. A key methodological departure is the explicit integration of Pearl-style causal inference techniques (front-door adjustment and counterfactual intervention) into a video model to address confounding and illusory causality, rather than relying solely on predictive comparison as in VAR or standard Granger tests.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MECD provides the first structured benchmark for event-level causal discovery in long videos, filling a gap between single-event QA and full causal graph learning. The finding that GPT-4o (65.5%) and GPT-4 (59.6%) underperform a purpose-built 0.76s-inference model (71.2%) suggests that current LLMs have genuine limitations in multi-event video causal reasoning, not just a prompting problem. Open questions include extending to unsupervised or weakly-annotated settings, handling higher-level semantic causality (emotional/mental influences), and whether the discovered causal graphs can transfer to other video understanding tasks beyond VQA.
