## Problem

Joint-embedding architecture (JEA) self-supervised learning methods have been assumed to require hand-crafted photometric data augmentations (color jitter, Gaussian blur, solarization, etc.) to enforce invariance and prevent model collapse. Prior ablation studies at small scale (ImageNet-1k, ResNets) showed large performance drops when removing augmentations, cementing the belief that invariance learning is the core signal. This paper challenges that assumption by asking whether augmentations are truly necessary or merely serve to artificially inflate dataset size, and whether sufficient data and compute can compensate for their absence.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images and produces visual feature embeddings via a Vision Transformer backbone (ViT-S through ViT-H). The critical input-side detail is the augmentation pipeline: the minimal 'Crop' setting resizes each image to 256×256, then applies a fixed RandomCrop to 224×224 (global views) or 98×98 (local views) without any random rescaling, color jitter, blur, or other photometric transforms. Optionally, a fraction of patches are replaced with [MASK] tokens for the iBOT reconstruction loss. The output is a global embedding per image used for linear-probe evaluation across classification, segmentation, and depth estimation tasks.

## Method

The paper conducts a controlled empirical study on DINOv2 (DINO global loss + iBOT latent-space masked reconstruction loss with multicrop: 2 global + 8 local views) across four augmentation configurations—Original (full photometric pipeline), Shared (same photometric augmentations applied identically to all views of one image), Crop+Resize (RandomResizedCrop only), and Crop (fixed resize + RandomCrop, no random rescaling)—trained on three dataset scales (ImageNet-1k, ImageNet-22k, LVD-142M) and multiple ViT sizes. The key finding is that the 'Shared' configuration, which increases sample count without enforcing invariance, nearly matches 'Original' at scale, proving that the primary role of augmentations is artificial dataset expansion rather than invariance learning. With sufficient data (ImageNet-22k or LVD-142M) and 500 training epochs, the 'Crop' setting converges to within ~1% of the fully augmented baseline and achieves state-of-the-art results among methods without hand-crafted augmentations. The paper further demonstrates that hyperparameter optimization must be matched to the specific data/compute regime: 'high-compute' hyperparameters tuned for large datasets cause DINOv2 to fail entirely on ImageNet-1k with minimal augmentations, while 'low-compute' hyperparameters recover strong performance, revealing that prior negative conclusions about removing augmentations were artifacts of scale mismatch.

## Results

Trained on ImageNet-22k with only RandomCrop (no resizing, no photometric augmentations) and masking, the ViT-L model achieves 84.0% top-1 accuracy on ImageNet-1k val, 74.8% on ImageNet-v2, 88.6% on ImageNet-Real, 65.1% on Places205, 75.1% on iNaturalist-18, 43.5 mIoU on ADE20k segmentation, and 0.411 RMSE on NYU-Depth v2. These results are obtained with the least amount of data augmentation in the literature, outperforming all reconstruction-based and JEPA baselines that still rely on RandomResizedCrop. The gap between the 'Crop' setting and the full 'Original' DINOv2 augmentation pipeline shrinks below 1% on ImageNet-1k when training on LVD-142M for 500 epochs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method (ViT-L, ImageNet-22k, Crop-only) outperforms all four baselines by large margins across nearly every benchmark. Against MAE (the strongest reconstruction baseline at 78.0% IN-1k), the gap is +6.0 points on ImageNet-1k, +8.3 on ImageNet-v2, and +10.2 mIoU on ADE20k. Against I-JEPA (79.3% IN-1k, the closest competitor), the advantage is +4.7 points on ImageNet-1k, +8.8 on ImageNet-v2, and +12.5 mIoU on ADE20k. BEIT (73.5%) and AIM (70.0%) trail by even wider margins (10.5 and 14.0 points respectively on ImageNet-1k). Notably, the proposed model uses strictly fewer augmentations than all baselines: while MAE, I-JEPA, and BEIT all employ RandomResizedCrop (and some add ColorJitter or RandomHorizontalFlip), the proposed method uses only RandomCrop without resizing plus latent-space masking, making it the least augmented JEA in the literature.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on DINOv2, the state-of-the-art joint-embedding architecture (JEA) for self-supervised visual representation learning, and challenges the foundational assumption inherited from SimCLR, BYOL, and DINO that hand-crafted data augmentations are the core learning signal in JEAs. It departs from the invariance-learning narrative supported by theoretical work (Kügelgen et al., Saunshi et al.) by introducing a 'Shared' augmentation configuration that decouples the invariance-enforcement role from the dataset-size-increasing role of augmentations. The paper also positions itself against reconstruction-based methods (BEIT, MAE, I-JEPA) that already avoided photometric augmentations but still relied on random resizing, showing that even that can be removed at sufficient scale.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that JEAs reach SOTA performance with only cropping (no resizing) and optional masking, this work removes a major barrier to applying joint-embedding architectures to non-image modalities (graphs, time-series, medical imaging) where domain-specific augmentation design is infeasible. The finding that augmentations primarily increase effective dataset size rather than enforce a necessary invariance signal reframes how the community should think about SSL theory. Open questions remain: why do augmentations still ease optimization in shorter trainings, why does a small gap persist for the largest models (ViT-H), and whether these conclusions transfer to other JEA algorithms beyond DINOv2. The paper also highlights a meta-lesson about compute-constrained experimental research: hyperparameters tuned for one scale can produce conclusions that are the opposite of those at another scale.
