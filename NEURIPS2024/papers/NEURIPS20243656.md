## Problem

Meta-tuning pre-trained vision transformers for out-of-domain few-shot classification has not simultaneously addressed adversarial robustness and clean generalization. Prior adversarial few-shot methods either degrade in-domain clean accuracy by several percentage points or fail to transfer robustness across unseen domains, leaving a persistent trade-off between adversarial defense, clean accuracy, and OOD generalization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243656/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At test time the method consumes a support set of K labeled RGB images per class (N-way) and a query set of unlabeled images, both at 128×128 resolution. Rather than running gradient descent, it non-parametrically computes a convex combination of top-k LoRA modules from a pre-trained pool using intra-class compactness and inter-class divergence on the support set, merges them into the frozen ViT-Small weights, applies singular-value trimming, and then performs nearest-centroid classification to emitit class labels. The merged task-specific weight matrix is the intermediate output that replaces the original pre-trained backbone for inference.

## Method

AMT meta-tunes P=4 LoRA modules (rank r=8) in parallel on ImageNet, each under a distinct l∞ adversarial budget (ε ∈ {0.01/255, 0.1/255, 6/255, 8/255}), while keeping the DINO-pretrained ViT-Small frozen. The key mechanism is a dual perturbation: PGD adversarial perturbations are applied to query images, and simultaneously a SAM-style worst-case perturbation is injected into the singular values and vectors of the LoRA's A and B matrices (initialized via SVD of the pre-trained MHA/FFN weights), explicitly reinforcing principal components under worst-case tasks. The meta-objective combines cross-entropyntropy on clean queries with a KL-divergence term on adversarial queries to balance clean and robust performance. At test time, the top-2 LoRAs are selected by a non-parametric criterion blending intra-class compactness and inter-class divergence on the support set, merged as a convex combination into the frozen weights, and trimmed by zeroing all but the top-ρ% singular values to suppress interference from redundant components.

## Results

On the Meta-Dataset benchmark (5-way 1-shot, tuning-free), AMT achieves 65.16% average clean accuracy across 10 domains, outperforming PM (60.35%) by 4.81% and StyleAdv (57.58%) by 7.58%. In the 5-way 5-shot setting, AMT reaches 81.70% average, and with test-time fine-tuning (ATTNSCALE+AMT-FT) the average climbs to 85.06%. For adversarial robustness (PGD-10, ε=4.5/255), AMT achieves 28.09% average in 1-shot and 45.23% in 5-shot, compared to 13.54% and 23.51% for PM respectively — a gain of up to 49.72% on Omniglot (5-shot). The method also improves in-domain ImageNet accuracy (68.80% vs 65.07% for PM in 1-shot), showing no robustness-accuracy trade-off.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against PM (the standard meta-tuning baseline [12]), AMT wins on all 10 domains in both 1-shot and 5-shot tuning-free settings, with the largest gap on Omniglot (+12.92% in 1-shot, +9.70% in 5-shot) and Sign (+8.61% in 1-shot). Against StyleAdv [86], AMT dominates across nearly all domains and settings; StyleAdv actually degrades in-domain ImageNet accuracy (56.10% vs 65.07% for PM in 1-shot), while AMT improves it to 68.80%. Against ATTNSCALE [59] in the test-time fine-tuning setting, AMT-FT provides a consistent 3.9–4.3% average boost over the standalone fine-tuning baselines (PMF, ATTNSCALE), confirming that the adversarial meta-tuned initialization is a stronger starting point. In adversarial robustness (Table 3), AMT outperforms PM by 14.55% average (1-shot) and 21.72% (5-shot), and outperforms StyleAdv by 13.98% (1-shot) and 21.64% (5-shot), with the most dramatic gain on Omniglot (65.01% vs 15.29% in 5-shot, a 49.72% improvement). The only setting where a baseline slightly edges AMT is 5-shot tuning-free on CUB (PM 92.23% vs AMT 93.12% — actually AMT still wins) and EuroSAT in BSCD-FSL (AMT 88.38% vs PM 89.19%), indicating near-parity on some in-distribution-like domains.

## Relation to Prior Work

AMT sits at the intersection of adversarial meta-learning (Goldblum et al., Wang et al.), parameter-efficient fine-tuning via LoRA (Hu et al., PiSSA), and OOD few-shot learning (PMF, FLUTE, StyleAdv). It departs from prior adversarial meta-learning by perturbing singular values and vectors in spectral space rather than input pixels or full weight matrices, and from FLUTE's gradient-based test-time adaptation by introducing a non-parametric merging criterion based on intra-class compactness and inter-class divergence. The robust LoRAPool design—maintaining separate LoRAs at different adversarial budgets—differs from both single-model adversarial training and ensemble approaches by avoiding interference between perturbation levels while preserving the frozen pre-trained backbone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that adversarial meta-tuning can simultaneously improve clean OOD generalization, adversarial robustness, and in-domain accuracy without the typical trade-offs, which is practically important for safety-critical deployments (medical imaging, autonomous driving). The modular LoRAPool with non-parametric merging is compatible with existing test-time fine-tuning methods, making it a drop-in enhancement rather than a replacement. Key limitations include the manual specification of adversarial budgets per LoRA module, restriction to l∞-bounded perturbations, and evaluation limited to ViT-Small with a single source domain (ImageNet), leaving open whether the approach scales to larger backbones or diverse perturbation families.
