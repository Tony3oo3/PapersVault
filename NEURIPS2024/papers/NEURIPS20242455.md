## Problem

Training large AI models (LLMs, DLRMs) demands massive GPU memory and compute time, making it affordable only to large organizations. While low-rank matrix compression methods like GaLore reduce memory, they do not achieve real GPU speedup, and tensor-compressed training—despite offering higher compression ratios—suffers from severe runtime overhead because GPUs are poorly optimized for the small-size tensor contractions it requires. No prior method has demonstrated simultaneous reduction of both memory and per-epoch training time on GPU.

## Input / Output

CoMERA takes standard neural network architectures (six-encoder transformers, DLRMs, BERT-like LLMs) and reshapes their weight matrices and embedding tables into high-order tensors during training. For transformers, linear layers (e.g., 768×768 attention, 768×3072 feed-forward) are compressed into Tensor-Train format with shape (12,8,8,8,8,12), while embedding tables (e.g., 30527×768) use Tensor-Train-Matrix format with shape (64,80,80,60). The output is a rank-adaptive compressed model whose tensor cores can be further pruned in a late-stage optimization to hit a target compression ratio, with some layers potentially eliminated entirely (rank → 0).

## Method

CoMERA formulates rank-adaptive tensor-compressed training as a multi-objective optimization balancing loss and compressed model size, using diagonal matrices to control TT/TTM ranks with an ℓ1 relaxation and ℓ2 regularization to ensure a well-posed problem. A two-stage scalarization scheme is employed: an early stage with linear scalarization for stable convergence, and an optional late stage with achievement scalarization to drive the model toward a user-specified target size and accuracy. To achieve real GPU speedup despite low FLOPS, three optimizations are applied: (1) TTM embedding lookup is accelerated by eliminating redundant computation at both the row-index and tensor-index levels (4–5× speedup); (2) near-optimal contraction paths are derived for TT forward and backward propagation, precomputing partial contractions to minimize intermediate tensor sizes; and (3) CUDA Graph is used to eliminate the kernel-launch overhead that dominates small tensor operations. Together these yield 2–3× per-epoch speedup over standard training while achieving 43–99× model compression.

## Results

On the six-encoder transformer (MNLI, batch 128), CoMERA early-stage training achieves 63.3% validation accuracy (vs. 62.2% uncompressed) with a 74× compression ratio on tensorized layers; late-stage training reaches up to 361× compression (80× whole model) with only a 0.7% accuracy drop. On DLRM (Criteo, batch 10,000), CoMERA compresses the model by 99× (4.081 GB → 0.041 GB) and reduces peak memory by 7× (18.275 GB → 2.612 GB) while matching uncompressed accuracy (78.76% vs. 78.68%). In CodeBERT pre-training, CoMERA achieves 4.23× overall compression with 2.3× and 1.9× speedup in Phase 1 and Phase 2 respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoMERA is compared against two recent low-rank training frameworks: GaLore [41] and LTE [20]. On the six-encoder transformer, CoMERA is approximately 2× faster per training epoch than GaLore and 3× faster than LTE across all batch sizes (32–128), while all methods use CUDA Graph for fair comparison. In the single-batch setting adopted by GaLore, CoMERA is 9× more memory-efficient (peak memory including data and backend overhead). Both CoMERA and GaLore reach ~64% validation accuracy on MNLI, but LTE does not converge on this task with its default settings. On DLRM, CoMERA achieves 99× model compression and 7× peak memory reduction versus uncompressed training, though standard training is slightly faster in wall-clock time due to DLRM's embedding-lookup-heavy computation pattern.

## Relation to Prior Work

CoMERA extends the rank-adaptive tensor-compressed training line initiated by Hawkins and Zhang (2022), which used a Bayesian formulation with a heuristic fixed-rank warm-up. The key departure is replacing that heuristic with a principled multi-objective optimization (linear scalarization early, achievement scalarization late) that simultaneously controls compression ratio and accuracy, and—critically—adding three GPU-level optimizations (optimized TTM embedding lookup, near-optimal TT contraction paths, and CUDA Graph) that the prior work lacked. Compared to matrix-based low-rank training methods like GaLore and LTE, CoMERA uses higher-order tensor decompositions (TT/TTM) to achieve substantially higher compression ratios, while compared to post-training tensor compression (HEAT) or structure search (SVDinsTN), it operates end-to-end during training without requiring a pre-trained model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to demonstrate that tensor-compressed training can be genuinely faster on GPU (2–3× per epoch), not merely cheaper in FLOPS, by addressing the kernel-launch overhead that has historically negated the theoretical speedup. The automatic architecture-search by-product (entire layers pruned to rank zero) and the 4.23× compression in CodeBERT pre-training with only a modest loss gap suggest a practical path toward reducing LLM training costs. However, all experiments are on a single RTX 3090 with models up to 357M parameters; the authors acknowledge that HPC-level optimization is needed for truly large LLMs, and the small-tensor-kernel bottleneck on current GPUs remains unresolved. Open questions include whether the optimization landscape of tensorized training requires purpose-built optimizers and whether the speedup persists at 7B+ scale.
