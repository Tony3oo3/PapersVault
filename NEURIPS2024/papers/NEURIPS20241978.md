## Problem

Existing graph generative models cannot simultaneously handle all task types (generation, regression, classification) and all structural levels (node, edge, graph), and they struggle with discrete graph structures and heterogeneous feature types. Unlike NLP where foundation models unify all tasks through a generative framework, no analogous unified framework exists for graph data, requiring separate models for each task type and level.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241978/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For unconditional generation, the model takes pure Gaussian noise in the latent space and outputs a full graph with node features, edge features (including a special 'no-edge' type for all n² node pairs), and graph-level attributes in one shot. For conditional generation, a target property (e.g., molecular dipole moment) is encoded and injected via cross-attention. For prediction tasks, a partially masked graph (with known node/edge features but missing labels) serves as the condition, and the model generates the complete graph, effectively performing graph inpainting to recover the missing labels.

## Method

LGD encodes graphs into a continuous latent space via a pretrained augmented-edge-enhanced graph transformer, then trains a DDPM/DDIM diffusion model on the latent representations, sidestepping the discrete structure problem. The denoising network uses a novel self-attention mechanism (Eq. 58) that simultaneously updates node, edge, and graph representations by treating all n² node pairs as 'augmented edges' with a dedicated edge-type feature for absent edges. A key conceptual contribution is formulating regression and classification as conditional generation (graph inpainting), for which the paper proves a generalization bound (Theorem 3.1) showing the MAE decomposes into forward-process convergence, discretization, score estimation, and encoder errors. Conditional generation is enabled through two cross-attention designs: one for general property conditions and one for masked-graph conditions that aligns node/edge correspondence between the conditioning and target graphs. Training proceeds in two stages: joint encoder-decoder pretraining with reconstruction and regularization losses, followed by diffusion training in the frozen latent space.

## Results

On node-level classification, LGD achieves 96.94% on Photo, 98.55% on Physics, and 73.17% on OGBN-Arxiv (169K nodes), outperforming all baselines including Exphormer and NAGphormer. On ZINC12K regression, LGD-DDPM achieves 0.065±0.003 MAE, beating the strongest baseline GPS (0.070±0.004). For unconditional molecular generation on QM9, LGD-large attains the best FCD (0.10) and NSPDK (0.0002) while maintaining 99.13% validity. In conditional generation, LGD achieves the lowest MAE on 4 out of 6 molecular properties compared to EDM and GeoLDM.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LGD decisively outperforms all node-classification baselines: it beats Exphormer by +1.59% on Photo, +1.66% on Physics, and +0.73% on OGBN-Arxiv, and surpasses NAGphormer by +1.45% on Photo and +1.21% on Physics; notably, Graphormer, SAN, and GraphGPS all OOM on Physics and OGBN-Arxiv while LGD scales to 169K nodes. On ZINC12K regression, LGD-DDPM (0.065 MAE) outperforms GPS (0.070), CIN (0.079), and GNN-AK+ (0.080), while LGD-DDIM (0.081) is slightly worse but much faster. For unconditional QM9 generation, LGD-large achieves the best FCD (0.10 vs. 0.11 for GruM) and ties for best NSPDK (0.0002), though DiGress and HGGT report marginally higher validity (99.01%, 99.22% vs. 99.13%); LGD-small shows substantially higher novelty (56.35%) than DiGress (35.46%), HGGT (24.01%), and GruM (24.15%). In conditional generation, LGD beats both EDM and GeoLDM on 4 of 6 properties (μ, Δε, c_v, and tied on α), despite not using 3D coordinates. On MOSES large-scale generation, LGD achieves 97.4% validity vs. 85.7% for DiGress, while maintaining competitive novelty (95.9% vs. 95.0%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the latent diffusion paradigm from Stable Diffusion (Rombach et al., 2021) to graph data, replacing the discrete noise processes used by prior graph diffusion models (DiGress, GDSS) with continuous Gaussian noise in a learned latent space. It builds on DDPM's forward/reverse diffusion framework and the theoretical convergence guarantees from Chen et al. (2022), but departs from prior graph generative work by (a) encoding graphs into a continuous latent space via a pretrained augmented-edge graph transformer, and (b) formulating regression and classification as conditional generation rather than treating them as separate deterministic tasks. The augmented-edge self-attention mechanism (Eq. 58) and the graph-to-graph cross-attention for masked conditions (Eq. 60) are novel architectural contributions that enable simultaneous node, edge, and graph-level generation in one shot.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unification of generation, regression, and classification under a single generative formulation is a conceptual step toward graph foundation models, and the theoretical guarantee (Theorem 3.1) that latent diffusion can provably outperform deterministic regression models is a first for this setting. However, the paper still trains separate models per task and dataset, so the 'foundation model' vision remains aspirational; scaling to a truly unified multi-domain model requires substantial additional compute and engineering. Open questions include whether the diffusion-based prediction advantage persists in other modalities (e.g., vision), whether the latent space can be shared across heterogeneous graph domains, and whether the modest empirical margins over strong deterministic baselines (e.g., 0.065 vs 0.070 MAE on ZINC12k) will widen with scale.
