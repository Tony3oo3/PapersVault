## Problem

Theoretical results on benign or tempered overfitting of neural networks have so far required either very high input dimensions (exceeding the number of samples) or univariate (1D) inputs, leaving the intermediate regime unexplored. This paper asks whether deep neural networks with binary weights can provably exhibit tempered overfitting—generalization error that is suboptimal but far better than trivial—when the input dimension is neither very high nor very low, and when the training set contains label noise.

## Input / Output

The method operates on binary feature vectors x ∈ {0,1}^{d_0} with binary labels generated by a constant-size teacher network plus label-flip noise of level ε*. The training set must be consistent (no two points share the same feature vector with different labels), which holds with high probability when N = o(1/√D_max). The output is an interpolating binary threshold network (depth ≥ 16, binary weights, ternary neuron scalars, threshold activations) along with a provable upper bound on its expected generalization error under the noisy distribution D, and under independent noise, a tight concentration around 2ε*(1−ε*).

## Method

The core technical contribution is a near-optimal bound on the size of a constant-depth threshold circuit consistent with an arbitrary partial function (Theorem 3.1): a depth-14 binary threshold network with O(log C(N,N₁)) weights can memorize any set of N label flips, constructed via hitting-set generators for conjunctions of literals and k-wise independent pseudorandom generators. This memorizing network is combined with the teacher via an XOR construction (Lemma 3.3) to produce an interpolating student network whose total weight count is w(d*) + N·H(ε*) + lower-order terms. Generalization is then bounded using information-theoretic arguments: the mutual information between the model and training set is upper-bounded by the model's complexity (weight count for min-size interpolators, or negative log interpolation probability for posterior sampling), and lower-bounded by a quantity depending on the population error. For min-size interpolators this yields error ≤ 1 − 2^{−H(ε*)} under arbitrary noise and concentration around 2ε*(1−ε*) under independent noise; for overparameterized random interpolators (posterior sampling), the same bounds hold with an additional O(√(n(d)·log(d_max+d_0)/N)) term, showing that even highly overparameterized typical interpolators generalize well.

## Results

The paper proves that both min-size NN interpolators and random (posterior-sampling) NN interpolators exhibit tempered overfitting. Under arbitrary label noise, the expected generalization error is bounded above by 1 − 2^{−H(ε*)} + o(1), where H(ε*) is the binary entropy of the noise level. Under independent label noise, the expected generalization error concentrates around 2ε*(1−ε*), which is exactly twice the Bayes-optimal error ε* (via Lemma A.9). For posterior sampling with an overparameterized student, the independent-noise bound includes an explicit O(√(n(d̲)·log(d̲_max + d₀)/N)) error term, and the arbitrary-noise bound includes O(n(d̲)·log(d̲_max + d₀)/N).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical baselines or experimental comparisons. The closest prior theoretical results it builds upon are: (1) Manoj and Srebro [58], who proved tempered overfitting for shortest-program interpolators (Turing machines) but did not address deep NNs; and (2) Buzaglo et al. [13], who proved that typical interpolating NNs generalize well in the realizable (noise-free) setting with a narrow teacher. The present paper extends both: it applies the information-theoretic framework of [58] to deep binary threshold networks with label noise, and extends the posterior-sampling analysis of [13] from the noise-free to the noisy-label regime. The key new technical contribution is Theorem 3.1, a bound on the size of a constant-depth threshold circuit consistent with a partial function, which has no direct analogue in prior circuit-complexity results for constant-depth threshold networks (the closest being Chashkin's result [17] for unbounded-depth circuits).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends two complementary lines of work on overfitting of interpolators: Manoj and Srebro's [58] result that shortest-program (Turing machine) interpolators exhibit tempered overfitting, and Buzaglo et al.'s [13] result that typical (random) interpolating NNs generalize well in the realizable (noise-free) setting. The key technical contribution is a new circuit-complexity result showing that a constant-depth binary threshold network can memorize N label flips with roughly H(ε*)·N weights, which is the first constant-depth threshold-network analog of Chashkin's theorem for general circuits. The paper deliberately positions itself as the first theoretical treatment of tempered overfitting for deep NNs that does not require the input dimension to be very high (as in most benign overfitting results) or very low (as in 1D ReLU analyses).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work fills a notable gap in the overfitting taxonomy by providing the first provable tempered-overfitting guarantees for deep (depth ≥ 16) binary threshold networks with noisy labels in the middle-dimensional regime, matching empirical observations from Mallinar et al. [57]. The finding that both min-size and highly overparameterized random interpolators achieve similar generalization (close to 2ε*(1−ε*) under independent noise) suggests that the specific interpolator selected matters less than the model class's capacity to memorize noise efficiently. Key limitations include the requirement for depth ≥ 16, width ω(N^{3/4}), binary weights, and consistent training sets; extending to other architectures (CNNs, ReLU), higher quantization, and non-interpolating models remains open and would require tighter complexity bounds.
