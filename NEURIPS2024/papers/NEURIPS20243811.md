## Problem

Pretrained diffusion behavior models capture diverse but suboptimal actions from offline datasets, and existing methods either augment them with separate guidance networks or train entirely new policies from scratch, failing to directly fine-tune the pretrained behavior. LLM alignment techniques like DPO cannot be transferred to continuous control because diffusion models lack a tractable density calculation in continuous action space, and control tasks use scalar Q-functions rather than binary preference labels. The paper asks whether pretrained diffusion behaviors can be directly fine-tuned into optimized policies using Q-function annotations.

## Input / Output

During pretraining, the method consumes reward-free state-action pairs (s, a) from a behavior dataset, where a is a continuous vector in R^|A|. During fine-tuning, for each state s it draws K=16 action samples from the frozen behavior model and annotates them with scalar Q-values from a separately trained critic (e.g., IQL), forming contrastive tuples {s, a^{1:K}, Q(s, a^k)}. The output is a continuous action sampled from the aligned diffusion policy via a standard ODE/SDE solver over the learned score field, with optional rejection sampling among 4 candidates at evaluation time.

## Method

The key architectural insight is the Bottleneck Diffusion Model (BDM), which redefines the noise predictor ε_φ(a_t|s,t) as −σ_t ∇_{a_t} f_φ(a_t|s,t), the negative gradient of a scalar network f_φ with respect to action inputs. This makes f_φ's output equal to log μ_t(a_t|s,t) + C(s,t) at convergence, enabling direct density estimation in a single forward pass and making diffusion policies compatible with log-probability-ratio-based alignment. For policy fine-tuning, EDA constructs a K-way contrastive classification task: the prediction logit for each noisy action a_t^k is the density gap β[f_θ^π(a_t^k|s,t) − f_φ^μ(a_t^k|s,t)], and a cross-entropy loss aligns these logits with Q-value-derived optimality probabilities e^{Q(s,a^k)}/Σ_i e^{Q(s,a^i)}. A theoretical proposition guarantees that at t=0 the learned policy converges to π* ∝ μ_φ e^{Q/β}, and at t>0 it asymptotically models the correct diffused distribution of π*. The policy network is initialized from the pretrained behavior weights (θ=φ), enabling convergence in ~20K gradient steps with as little as 1% of Q-labelled data.

## Results

On the D4RL benchmark, EDA (BDM) achieves an overall average normalized score of 83.7, surpassing the strongest diffusion-based baselines Diffusion-QL (82.1) and QGPO (80.7). In MuJoCo locomotion tasks, EDA reaches 87.3 average, outperforming Diffusion-QL (88.0 is close but EDA wins on 7 of 9 individual tasks) and IDQL (82.1). Notably, EDA maintains ~95% of its full-data performance using only 1% of Q-labelled data during fine-tuning, and converges in roughly 20K gradient steps versus the typical 1M steps for from-scratch policy training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EDA outperforms all eight baselines in overall D4RL average score (83.7 vs. 82.1 for Diffusion-QL, 80.7 for QGPO, 71.4 for IQL, 69.7 for CQL, 39.7 for BCQ). Among diffusion-based methods, EDA beats Diffusion-QL by 1.6 points overall and QGPO by 3.0 points, with the largest margins on HalfCheetah Medium (+2.9 over QGPO) and Kitchen Partial (+3.3 over QGPO). EDA trails Diffusion-QL on Kitchen Complete (81.5 vs. 84.0) and D-Diffuser on Hopper Medium-Replay (92.7 vs. 100.0), but wins on the majority of individual tasks. Against the Gaussian-policy baselines (IQL, CQL, BCQ), EDA's advantage is substantial—12+ points on average—validating the expressivity of diffusion policies over single-mode Gaussians. In the sample-efficiency ablation (Fig. 5a), EDA with only 1% of Q-labelled data still surpasses IQL and QGPO trained on the full dataset, confirming that the pretraining–fine-tuning paradigm transfers generalization from the behavior model to the aligned policy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper transplants the LLM alignment paradigm (pretrain a generative model, then fine-tune with preference/reward signals) into offline continuous control, building directly on DPO's log-probability-ratio parameterization and the score-based diffusion framework of Song et al. The critical technical departure is the Bottleneck Diffusion Model (BDM) representation, which redefines the noise predictor as the gradient of a scalar network, thereby enabling direct density estimation that standard diffusion models lack. The theoretical proof leverages the Contrastive Energy Prediction framework (Lu et al., ICML 2023) to show the contrastive loss recovers the optimal policy. Unlike prior diffusion-based offline RL methods (IDQL, Diffusion-QL, QGPO, D-Diffuser) that augment or replace the behavior model with separate guidance networks or distillation, EDA directly fine-tunes the pretrained behavior policy in place.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The pretrain-then-align decomposition for continuous control is a practically important paradigm: maintaining ~95% performance with only 1% Q-labelled data and converging in ~20K steps (vs. 1M for from-scratch training) could dramatically reduce annotation costs in real-world robotics and manipulation. The BDM representation is a clean, drop-in architectural constraint that makes diffusion policies compatible with the entire alignment literature, potentially enabling future work to import other LLM alignment techniques (KTO, IPO, etc.) into continuous control. Key limitations include restriction to continuous action spaces, the requirement that the diffusion model be a gradient of a scalar network, and per-task hyperparameter tuning (especially β). Open questions include whether the approach extends to discrete or hybrid action spaces and whether a single set of hyperparameters can generalize across tasks.
