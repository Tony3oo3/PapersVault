## Problem

While heterophily in node classification is well understood, its impact on GNN-based link prediction remains largely unexplored because existing homophily definitions rely on class labels that are unavailable in link prediction. This paper asks how feature similarity between connected nodes affects GNN link prediction performance across the full spectrum from homophilic to heterophilic settings, and which encoder/decoder designs adapt best to non-homophilic conditions.

## Input / Output

The framework takes an undirected graph with an adjacency matrix and a node feature matrix, along with a candidate node pair, and outputs a scalar link probability score. Feature similarity is measured via mean-centered cosine similarity between node feature vectors, and the task is categorized by how the distributions of edge and non-edge similarity scores are separated in score space (homophilic, heterophilic, or gated). Evaluation uses Hits@50 or MRR depending on the dataset, with edges split into training/validation/test sets.

## Method

The paper introduces formal definitions of homophilic, heterophilic, and gated link prediction based on the separation of feature similarity score distributions between edges and non-edges, rather than relying on class labels. A theoretical framework (Theorems 1–3) shows that homophilic and heterophilic tasks require opposite optimization directions for link scores relative to feature similarity, that linear decoders (DOT, DistMult) cannot perfectly separate gated tasks, and that GNNs without ego-neighbor embedding separation generalize poorly under degree shifts in heterophilic settings. Empirically, the paper benchmarks six real-world datasets (88K–30M edges) and synthetic graphs, demonstrating that MLP decoders outperform DOT by up to 55% in heterophilic regimes, DistMult serves as a scalable alternative for purely homophilic/heterophilic tasks, and encoders with ego-neighbor separation (GraphSAGE, SIGN) consistently outperform GCN by up to 44% across the feature similarity spectrum.

## Results

On the six real-world benchmarks, SAGE+MLP achieves the best or near-best GNN4LP performance across most datasets: 48.64 Hits@50 on ogbl-collab, 83.67 MRR on ogbl-citat2, 54.60 MRR on e-comm, 50.12 MRR on facebook, 35.30 MRR on PPI, and 58.38 MRR on amazon-computers. BUDDY-SIGN (subgraph sketching + SIGN encoder + MLP) achieves the highest scores on ogbl-collab (66.64), ogbl-citat2 (87.53), and PPI (66.42), while SAGE+DistMult leads on facebook (50.64) and amazon-computers (58.42). On synthetic graphs spanning the full feature-similarity spectrum, all feature-consuming methods exhibit a U-shaped performance trend, with the best performance at the positive (homophilic) and negative (heterophilic) extremes and a significant drop near zero similarity (gated regime). SAGE consistently outperforms GCN by up to 44% on synthetic graphs, and MLP decoder outperforms DOT by up to 50% in the heterophilic regime.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SAGE+MLP outperforms GCN+MLP on all six datasets, with the largest gaps on ogbl-collab (48.64 vs. 30.17, +61%) and PPI (35.30 vs. 17.67, +100%), confirming that ego-neighbor embedding separation in message passing is critical for non-homophilic link prediction. Among decoders, MLP consistently dominates DOT (e.g., SAGE+MLP 58.38 vs. SAGE+DOT 42.25 on amzn-comp, +38%) and is competitive with or slightly better than DistMult, while DistMult substantially outperforms DOT on heterophilic datasets (SAGE+DistMult 58.42 vs. SAGE+DOT 42.25 on amzn-comp, +38%). BUDDY-SIGN achieves the highest scores on structurally rich datasets (ogbl-collab 66.64, ogbl-citat2 87.53, PPI 66.42) but underperforms SAGE+MLP on e-comm (10.95 vs. 54.60) and facebook (51.81 vs. 50.12), suggesting subgraph sketching adds limited benefit when feature similarity drives connectivity. Heuristic baselines (CN, AA, RA) are competitive on PPI (62–65 MRR) and facebook (54–56 MRR) but are far outperformed by GNN methods on ogbl-collab, ogbl-citat2, and e-comm, where node features carry substantial signal. The graph-agnostic NoGNN+MLP baseline is the weakest method on all datasets, confirming that GNN message passing provides essential structural information beyond raw features.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously separate research threads: GNN-based link prediction (building on VAE/GCN encoders, GraphSAGE, and BUDDY's subgraph sketching) and the heterophily literature that has been almost exclusively applied to node classification with class labels. The key departure is introducing a feature-similarity-based definition of homophily/heterophily for link prediction, where class labels are unavailable, and formally categorizing tasks into homophilic, heterophilic, and gated regimes. The ego-neighbor embedding separation design is transferred from heterophilic node classification [Zhu et al., 2020] to the link prediction setting, and the DistMult decoder is repurposed from knowledge graph completion as a scalable alternative to MLP decoders.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first systematic theoretical and empirical characterization of how feature heterophily affects GNN link prediction, yielding actionable design guidance (MLP or DistMult decoders over DOT; SAGE/SIGN over GCN encoders) across the full similarity spectrum. The six real-world benchmarks spanning 88K to 30M+ edges fill a gap in available non-homophilic link prediction datasets. Open questions include the interplay between feature similarity and structural proximity in real-world graphs (where both coexist, unlike the controlled synthetic setting), the need for more strongly heterophilic benchmarks, and whether the U-shaped performance degradation near zero similarity can be mitigated by architectural innovations beyond the tested designs.
