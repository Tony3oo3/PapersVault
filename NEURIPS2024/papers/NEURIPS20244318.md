## Problem

Continuous-time recurrent neural networks (RNNs) are the standard model in cognitive neuroscience for capturing the temporal dynamics of biological circuits, but they cannot efficiently process the spatial hierarchies in images, leaving vision neuroscience dominated by feedforward CNNs that lack dynamical realism. The specific dynamical capabilities of RNNs—attractors, noise suppression, autonomous inference—have remained inapplicable to visual processing models. This paper asks whether a single architecture can inherit both the spatial processing power of CNNs and the continuous-time dynamical behavior of RNNs.

## Input / Output

The model consumes standard RGB images (28×28 MNIST, 32×32 CIFAR, 224×224 ImageNet) presented as a 200 ms stimulus within a 300 ms continuous-time simulation at 2 ms Euler steps. Rather than producing a single static prediction, the network is trained to maintain a stable classification indefinitely after stimulus onset and to return to a spontaneous baseline after stimulus offset. In the multi-area application, inputs are the actual experimental stimuli (e.g., dot-motion displays, object images) seen by monkeys, and outputs include interpretable neural trajectories in a downstream fully-connected RNN that encode cognitive decisions such as quantity, category, spatial location, or motion direction.

## Method

CordsNet replaces every convolutional layer with a continuous-time dynamical RNN whose recurrent and input weight matrices are convolutions: T dr_l/dt = −r_l + σ(Conv_rec(r_l) + b + Conv_inp(h_inp)). Training uses a three-step initialization that avoids the cost of simulating the full 300-step dynamics from scratch: (1) train a standard feedforward CNN, (2) fold batch normalization into the convolution weights and replace each layer with a linear RNN whose steady state is matched analytically to the CNN output, then (3) anneal a PReLU nonlinearity from identity (a=1) to ReLU (a=0) to introduce nonlinearity. The loss combines a log-weighted cross-entropy over the last 60 ms of stimulus presentation with a small MSE penalty driving post-stimulus activity back to a spontaneous baseline, enforcing a mono-stable fixed point. For dynamical-systems analysis, the authors develop an Arnoldi-iteration-based eigenvalue computation that operates directly on the convolutional kernel form, avoiding the infeasible expansion to a full recurrent weight matrix.

## Results

On ImageNet, the largest model CordsNet-R8 achieves 57.90% validation accuracy after fine-tuning, compared to 63.16% for its feedforward CNN counterpart and 0.26% for a parameter-matched fully-connected RNN. On CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100, CordsNet-R8 reaches 94.56% and 77.32% test accuracy respectively, within ~1.5% of the CNN baselines (95.99% and 78.44%). The proposed three-step initialization method consistently outperforms direct training (Control C) across all datasets except MNIST, e.g., 41.24% vs 23.21% on ImageNet for R8. CordsNet-R8 also demonstrates >10× greater noise robustness than the feedforward CNN, maintaining classification accuracy at high noise levels where the CNN degrades sharply.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CORNet-RT [38] (discrete-time recurrent CNN), CordsNet demonstrates fundamentally different temporal behavior: it classifies images indefinitely across time, self-resets to baseline when stimuli are removed, and correctly classifies sequential image changes, whereas CORNet-RT is accurate only at its single trained time step and fails on the second image in a sequence. CordsNet also shows >10× smaller activation deviations under white noise compared to the feedforward CNN, maintaining accuracy at high noise levels where the CNN degrades. Against parameter-matched fully-connected RNNs (Control R), CordsNet-R6/R8 wins decisively on all datasets, with the gap most dramatic on ImageNet (57.90% vs 0.26% for R8), attributed to the poor scaling of fully-connected architectures with image size. On the five cognitive tasks (perceptual decision-making, working memory, multi-sensory decision-making, contextual decision-making, delayed match-to-sample), CordsNets produce neural trajectories highly similar to fully-connected, low-rank [62], and sparse [15] RNNs when aligned via CCA; representational similarity metrics indicate CordsNets are actually closer to fully-connected RNNs than to the low-rank or sparse variants, confirming that the convolutional weight structure does not constrain dynamical expressiveness.

## Relation to Prior Work

CordsNet directly extends the continuous-time RNN dynamical system framework established by Soo et al. (2023) for cognitive tasks by replacing fully-connected recurrent weights with convolutional kernels, thereby importing the spatial hierarchy processing of CNNs into the biologically-motivated recurrent dynamics tradition. It departs from the dominant CNN paradigm in vision neuroscience (Yamins, Kriegeskorte, DiCarlo) and from discrete-time recurrent CNNs like CORNet-RT, which lack the continuous-time ODE structure and its associated dynamical regimes (oscillations, chaos, attractors). The paper also builds on the low-rank and sparse RNN literature (Dubreuil et al. 2022, Song et al. 2016) to demonstrate that the convolutional weight constraint does not reduce dynamical expressivity, positioning CordsNet as a unifying architecture rather than a new task formulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work provides the first principled bridge between two long-separated research traditions in computational neuroscience: dynamical RNNs for cognition and CNNs for vision, enabling image-computable models that can be analyzed as dynamical systems and trained on cognitive tasks with real visual stimuli. The finding that noise robustness emerges naturally from continuous-time dynamics (an order-of-magnitude reduction in activation deviation) suggests a potential biological mechanism for visual stability, though the Brain-Score temporal results remain tentative (inconclusive on V1/V2). Key open questions include closing the ~5-point ImageNet accuracy gap with feedforward CNNs, reducing the substantial MAC overhead (CordsNet-R2 exceeds ViT-H-14 in compute), and determining whether the captured temporal signatures in V4/IT reflect genuine biological dynamics or are artifacts of the training protocol.
