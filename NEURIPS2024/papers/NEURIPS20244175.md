## Problem

Model-based RL methods achieve high sample efficiency but suffer from sequential computation bottlenecks: RNN-based world models cannot be parallelized over sequence length, transformer-based alternatives incur quadratic complexity, and eligibility trace estimation in policy learning is inherently sequential. No prior work has simultaneously parallelized both the model learning and policy learning stages of MBRL while preserving sample efficiency and avoiding extra inference overhead.

## Input / Output

The framework consumes 64×64 RGB images (Atari) or low-dimensional proprioception vectors (DMControl) along with discrete actions (up to 18 dimensions) or continuous actions, and produces actions, predicted rewards, and continuation flags. The world model operates on latent representations (32×32 categorical stochastic states) rather than raw observations, with the decoder reconstructing observations only for the prediction loss. During imagination, the policy samples actions autoregressively over a 16-step horizon, which cannot be parallelized due to the non-linear actor network.

## Method

PaMoRL introduces two parallelization techniques over sequence length. The Parallel World Model (PWM) replaces the RSSM's GRU with a modified linear attention module that incorporates a data-dependent decay rate (via RWKV-style token mixing and GRU gating) and RMSNorm for training stability, satisfying the algebraic conditions for parallel scan (associative ⊕, semi-associative ⊗, distributivity). The Parallelized Eligibility Trace Estimation (PETE) is the first to show that TD-λ, Retrace, and GAE return computations can be expressed as first-order linear recurrences and thus computed via parallel scan. The Odd-even scanner is used for PWM (massively parallel batch×hidden computation) while the Kogge-stone scanner is used for PETE (scalar-dimension returns), achieving 7–17× training speedups with O(L) complexity and O(1) recurrent inference, matching RNN-level inference cost without the sequential training bottleneck.

## Results

On the Atari 100K benchmark, PaMoRL achieves a mean human-normalized score of 126.64% and a median of 71.75%, surpassing all no-look-ahead MBRL and model-free baselines, and reaches superhuman performance on 11/26 games. On the DeepMind Control Suite, PaMoRL achieves a mean score of 611.2 (Proprio Control) and 538.7 (Vision Control) across 16 tasks, outperforming DreamerV3 (510.8 and 470.7 respectively). The parallel scan-based training yields 7.2×–16.6× speedups in sequence model computation and 2×–3× speedups in eligibility trace estimation compared to sequential rollout, without increasing inference cost.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PaMoRL outperforms all no-look-ahead MBRL baselines on Atari 100K: it beats DreamerV3 [17] by 14.6 points in mean (126.64% vs 112%) and 22.75 points in median (71.75% vs 49%), STORM [22] by 4.34 points in mean and 29.12 in median, IRIS [20] by 21.64 in mean and 42.75 in median, TWM [21] by 30.64 in mean and 20.75 in median, and the model-free SimPLe [55] by a wide margin (126.64% vs 44% mean). On DeepMind Control, PaMoRL surpasses DreamerV3 [17] by 100.4 points in Proprio Control (611.2 vs 510.8) and 68 points in Vision Control (538.7 vs 470.7), and also beats SAC [50] (611.2 vs 538.4) and DrQ-v2 [51] (538.7 vs 426.8). Against planning-based methods (EfficientZero [7], EfficientZero V2 [9]) and the larger-network BBF [8], PaMoRL trails in aggregate mean/median scores (e.g., 126.64% vs 267.97% for EfficientZero V2) but leads on 13 out of 26 individual Atari games, confirming its competitive per-game performance. The REM baseline [25] is shown in Figure 1 as a comparable no-look-ahead method, with PaMoRL matching or exceeding it across both benchmarks.

## Relation to Prior Work

PaMoRL builds directly on the DreamerV3/RSSM paradigm for MBRL, replacing the RNN-based sequence model with a modified linear attention module (drawing on Katharopoulos et al. and RWKV's token mixing) that satisfies the algebraic conditions for parallel scan. The key conceptual departure is twofold: (1) the world model's sequential computation is parallelized over sequence length using Kogge-stone or Odd-even scanners from parallel computing, and (2) eligibility trace estimation (TD-λ returns) is shown for the first time to be expressible as a parallel scan, decoupling policy learning from sequential rollout. This positions PaMoRL at the intersection of efficient sequential modeling (linear RNNs, SSMs) and the Dyna-style MBRL framework, while explicitly contrasting with transformer-based world models (IRIS, TWM, STORM) that trade quadratic training complexity for parallelism.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that PaMoRL achieves 7–16× training speedups over sequential RNN-based world models while maintaining or exceeding DreamerV3's sample efficiency, making MBRL more accessible on modest hardware. The parallelization of eligibility traces is a general technique applicable to any return-based RL method (TD-λ, Retrace, GAE), not just MBRL. However, the framework cannot incorporate planning-based policy learning (MPPI, MCTS) since action sampling through a non-linear actor breaks the linear recurrence structure, and the end-to-end joint training of encoder and sequence model limits scalability to larger architectures. Open questions include whether hybrid architectures (combining transformer expressiveness with linear-attention parallelism) could close the remaining gap to planning-based methods, and whether the parallel scan approach extends to off-policy or asynchronous settings.
