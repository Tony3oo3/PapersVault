## Problem

Matrix factorization-based student cognitive models achieve high performance prediction accuracy but produce uninterpretable latent factors that cannot identify students' knowledge proficiency on specific concepts. The prior SNMCF model attempted to address this by adding a coverage function, but it solved performance prediction and cognitive diagnosis as separate sequential tasks, so the latent factors were never shaped by the diagnostic objective. This paper asks how to jointly learn interpretable knowledge proficiency and exercise predictions within a single optimization framework while respecting the psychometric monotonicity principle.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a binary student scoring matrix X ∈ {0,1}^{N×M} (with missing entries for unattempted exercises) and an expert-labeled Q-matrix Q ∈ {0,1}^{N×K} encoding which exercises map to which knowledge concepts. It produces a continuous knowledge proficiency matrix A = V^T U ∈ R^{K×M} giving each student's level on each concept, plus predicted response probabilities for unobserved exercise entries via a probit link. The Q-matrix is binary but the model learns a continuous exercise-knowledge association matrix B ∈ R^{N×K} to capture the strength of each exercise–concept link, and a per-exercise difficulty vector M ∈ R^N.

## Method

AE-NMCF structures learning as an autoencoder: the encoder jointly co-factorizes X and a continuous B matrix (constrained by Q) through a shared nonnegative exercise-characteristic matrix E, yielding student-proficiency U, exercise-characteristic E, and knowledge-requirement V matrices, from which knowledge proficiency A = V^T U is derived. The decoder then reconstructs X by mapping Δ_nm = B_{n:}A_{:m} + μ_n through a probit link function, and the negative log-likelihood of this reconstruction enforces monotonicity—higher proficiency must correspond to higher success probability. The combined objective is nonconvex with nonnegative constraints, so the authors develop a projected gradient block coordinate descent method (PG-BCD+Lipschitz) that uses analytically derived Lipschitz constants (via the maximum singular value of U^T V and the Q-matrix row norms) to set step sizes, with a convergence guarantee to a critical point under the KL inequality. The key architectural insight is that the autoencoder's self-reconstruction principle naturally accommodates the monotonic constraint, creating an iterative feedback loop where the encoder's proficiency estimates are refined by the decoder's reconstruction error.

## Results

AE-NMCF achieves the best overall rank (1.33 on ACC, 1.67 on RMSE via Friedman test) across six real-world datasets spanning Math, Biology, History, and English. On ACC, AE-NMCF is top-2 on all datasets (e.g., 0.7065 on Junyi-s, 0.7531 on Quanlang-s, 0.8072 on SLP-His-s), and on RMSE it achieves the best or second-best value on five of six datasets (e.g., 0.4067 on Quanlang-s, 0.3665 on SLP-His-s). For knowledge proficiency estimation (r_c), AE-NMCF significantly outperforms SNMCF (p=0.031, Wilcoxon test) and matches or exceeds CDM-based approaches (DINA, DIRT, DeepCDF, QRCDM) across all datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AE-NMCF outperforms all data-mining baselines (NMF, MCF, GNMF, NMMF) on every dataset for both ACC and RMSE, with the largest margins over MCF variants (e.g., +0.13 ACC on FrcSub vs. MCF-Gra). Against the closest data-mining competitor SNMCF, AE-NMCF wins on 5/6 datasets for ACC (losing only on FrcSub: 0.8267 vs. 0.8548) and 5/6 for RMSE (losing only on FrcSub: 0.3476 vs. 0.3349), while significantly surpassing SNMCF on knowledge estimation r_c (p=0.031). Among CDM baselines, AE-NMCF dominates DINA and DIRT on all datasets by large margins (e.g., +0.19 ACC on Junyi-s vs. DINA), and beats DeepCDF on 5/6 datasets for ACC (losing only on SLP-Eng RMSE: 0.4262 vs. 0.3691). QRCDM is the strongest CDM competitor, but AE-NMCF still wins on 5/6 datasets for ACC and 4/6 for RMSE, while QRCDM's prediction accuracy is notably weaker on sparse datasets (e.g., 0.4717 ACC on Junyi-s vs. AE-NMCF's 0.7065). The ablation (Table 2) confirms both encoder and decoder contribute: removing the decoder drops r_c by 0.05–0.15 and ACC by 0.01–0.21 across datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends SNMCF (Yu et al., IEEE TKDE 2023), which first applied nonnegative matrix co-factorization to student cognitive modeling but solved performance prediction and knowledge diagnosis as separate, sequential tasks. The key departure is the introduction of an autoencoder-like encoder-decoder architecture that creates an iterative feedback loop between latent factors and knowledge proficiency, thereby embedding the psychometric monotonicity constraint directly into the optimization. The co-factorization structure follows Singh & Gordon (KDD 2008), while the optimization methodology (projected gradient with Lipschitz constants) draws on Lan et al. (JMLR 2014) and the convergence theory of Xu & Yin (SIAM 2013). Unlike prior MF-based cognitive models that treat latent factors as uninterpretable, AE-NMCF is the first to exploit monotonicity within a data-mining matrix factorization framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AE-NMCF provides a principled, end-to-end data-driven mechanism for estimating continuous (rather than binary) student knowledge proficiency while maintaining competitive prediction accuracy, addressing a long-standing gap where MF-based models excelled at prediction but failed at diagnosis. The practical impact is significant for intelligent tutoring systems and computerized adaptive testing that require interpretable cognitive profiles. However, the model incurs substantially higher computational cost than baselines, and the authors acknowledge that diagnostic results can be overoptimistic when knowledge prerequisite structures are ignored. Open questions include incorporating prerequisite dependencies among knowledge concepts, exploring alternative link functions beyond probit, and improving scalability for very large-scale educational deployments.
