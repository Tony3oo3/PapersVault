## Problem

Eigendecomposition and SVD are 4–5 orders of magnitude slower than matrix multiplication, making them the dominant computational bottleneck when embedded inside a neural network's training loop (e.g., for nuclear-norm regularization, latent-space PCA, or graph structure learning). The paper observes that during training the inner eigensolver need not fully converge at every step—only the final trained model requires the correct eigenpair—yet existing frameworks (PyTorch, JAX) still enforce exact eigendecomposition at each iteration.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a data matrix X (MNIST/Celeb-A-HQ images or graph node features with an adjacency/Laplacian matrix) and an encoder h_θ that produces a symmetric matrix A (covariance, similarity, or graph Laplacian) whose eigenpair is needed by the downstream task. Instead of returning an exact (V, Λ) from eigh/svd, the method outputs an orthonormal matrix U (obtained via QR of a free parameter W) and approximate eigenvalues Σ = diag(UᵀAU), which converge to the true eigenpair as training proceeds. The final outputs are task-specific: reconstructed images, whitened latent representations, or node-classification labels on Cora/Citeseer/PubMed.

## Method

The core idea is to replace the expensive eigendecomposition in the computational graph with a cheap QR decomposition that reparameterizes the orthogonal matrix U on the Stiefel manifold (U = QR(W)), and to add an 'eigen loss'—either Brockett's weighted trace tr(MUᵀAU) or a convex trace loss tr(f(UᵀAU))—that is jointly minimized with the task loss. Theorems 1 and 2 prove that the optima of these eigen losses exactly recover the k largest (or smallest) eigenpairs of A, so full convergence of the eigensolver is deferred to the end of training rather than enforced at every step. Three relaxation regimes are identified: (i) loss depends on both eigenvalues and eigenvectors → add Brockett/convex eigen loss; (ii) loss depends only on eigenvectors → add eigen loss with stop-gradient on A; (iii) loss is itself a non-uniform linear or convex/concave function of eigenvalues (e.g., nuclear norm) → no extra penalty needed, the task loss alone drives U to the correct eigenvectors. This yields speed-ups of 4×–615× over exact eigh/svd baselines while producing nearly identical final results.

## Results

The amortized eigendecomposition approach achieves speed-ups of 4.06×–105.4× on nuclear norm regularization (128×128 to 512×512), 1.39×–1.47× on latent-space PCA (256×2 to 1028×2), and 23.4×–615.2× on low-rank GCN (2708×16 to 19717×16) compared to standard eigh/SVD, while producing nearly identical training outcomes. Convergence experiments on 1000×1000 symmetric matrices confirm that both Brockett's cost and convex trace loss (f(x)=x^1.5) recover the 50 largest eigenvalues to a mean square error of 10⁻⁹. The scalability study on CelebA-HQ (up to 1B parameters, 64-layer autoencoder, 4096-dim latent space) shows that eigendecomposition is the dominant computational bottleneck, with the proposed method's overhead remaining close to the backbone-only cost.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline across all experiments is the standard eigendecomposition (eigh) or singular value decomposition (svd) function provided by JAX, which the paper replaces with a QR-based reparameterization plus an eigen loss. On the graph adversarial learning task, the paper specifically compares against the SVD-based low-rank Laplacian approach of Jin et al. (KDD 2020), which requires a full SVD at every iteration; the amortized method achieves a 615.2× speed-up on the largest graph (PubMed, 19717 nodes) while maintaining comparable node classification accuracy under adversarial edge perturbations. On nuclear norm regularization (MNIST autoencoder), the method matches the SVD-based reconstruction loss curves while reducing per-iteration cost from 2.616E-1 s to 7.366E-2 s at 512×512 dimension. On latent-space PCA, the speed-up is more modest (1.39×–1.47×) because the eigendecomposition operates on a small 2-dimensional subspace, but the reconstruction loss trajectories remain nearly indistinguishable from the eigh-based baseline. The convergence study confirms that the relaxed optimization reaches the same eigenvalue accuracy (MSE ~10⁻⁹) as direct eigensolvers, validating that the amortized approach does not sacrifice solution quality for speed.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on Brockett's cost function (1991) and the classical trace inequalities of von Neumann and Ruhe, repurposing them as differentiable loss terms within a neural network training loop rather than as standalone optimization objectives. It departs from prior manifold optimization approaches (which use retraction to maintain orthogonality constraints) by reparameterizing the Stiefel manifold via QR decomposition, which is cheaper and has well-supported autograd in modern frameworks. The key conceptual departure is the 'amortization' principle: eigendecomposition need not fully converge at each training step, since the network parameters are themselves still being optimized, so the eigen loss can be jointly minimized with the task loss over the course of training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Eigendecomposition is 4–5 orders of magnitude slower than matrix multiplication, making it a dominant bottleneck whenever it appears inside a training loop; this paper shows that replacing it with a QR-based reparameterization plus an eigen loss yields speedups of 4× to 615× while producing nearly identical results across nuclear norm regularization, latent-space PCA, and graph adversarial learning. The approach is particularly valuable in frameworks like JAX and PyTorch where backward gradients for top-k eigendecomposition are poorly supported or unavailable. A stated limitation is that as a standalone numerical eigensolver the method offers no advantage over dedicated solvers, and the paper does not explore non-symmetric or complex-valued matrices, leaving open how the relaxation behaves in those settings.
