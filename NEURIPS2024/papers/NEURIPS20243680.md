## Problem

Clinical notes are subject to strict privacy regulations (IRB approval, cross-institutional sharing restrictions), while structured clinical concepts extracted from them are more freely shareable. This raises the question of whether the accessible form (concept lists) can serve as a faithful proxy for the restricted form (clinical notes). Additionally, existing clinical LLMs are trained independently on different corpora and never exchange knowledge, leaving complementary information in each model untapped.

## Input / Output

The framework consumes paired data consisting of segmented clinical text (discharge notes split into 14 sections, radiology reports into 7) and corresponding lists of clinical concepts extracted by NILE, each annotated with positive/negative mention flags. Concepts are further augmented with UMLS relationships (7 broad relation types) to form a structured graph. The output is a unified representation embedding in a shared space, enabling cross-form proxy generation: when only the concept list is available, the LINE-projected CODER embedding serves as a stand-in for the missing clinical text embedding in downstream tasks.

## Method

The LINE (LIghtweight kNowledge alignmEnt) module projects embeddings from two frozen teacher models into a shared space: a simple fully-connected layer for the general-purpose LLM (BGE or GPT-4) and a multihead graph attention layer for the domain-specific LLM (CODER) that assigns each of 7 UMLS relation types to a separate attention head. Training uses a two-stage, few-epoch strategy (3 + 2 epochs) with only the LINE module updated. Stage 1 minimizes a triplet alignment loss weighted by a data-dependent factor ρ (fraction of text words captured by the concept list) plus a relational contrastive loss preserving known UMLS edges; Stage 2 recovers residual concepts (cosine similarity ≥ 0.9 to the text-concept gap) from the projected UMLS dictionary and refines training pairs. Negation is handled by creating separate negative-concept embeddings constrained to remain below a cosine threshold δ from their positive counterparts.

## Results

On the primary alignment validation task (275K text-concept pairs from MIMIC-IV), LINE BGE+CODER achieves a mean rank of 1.437 and Top-10 accuracy of 99.7%, substantially outperforming BGE alone (mean rank 5.526, Top-10 84.5%) and CODER alone (mean rank 43.79, Top-10 18.1%). LINE GPT-4+CODER similarly improves over GPT-4 alone (mean rank 1.778 → 1.477, Top-10 98.8% → 99.5%). On the i2b2 2006 and 2014 NER benchmarks, LINE achieves the best F1 scores of 98.1% (test) on both datasets, surpassing CODER (98.0/97.7) and BGE (97.2/97.1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LINE BGE+CODER outperforms BGE by a factor of ~3.8× in mean rank (5.526 → 1.437) and by 15.2 points in Top-10 accuracy (84.5% → 99.7%), while simultaneously improving CODER's alignment from a mean rank of 43.79 to 1.437. LINE GPT-4+CODER improves over GPT-4 alone (mean rank 1.778 → 1.477, Top-10 98.8% → 99.5%), confirming that even the stronger general-purpose teacher benefits from alignment with the domain-specific teacher. On the clinical concept similarity task (Table 2), LINE BGE+CODER exceeds BGE's AUC in 6 of 7 relation classes (e.g., 0.926 vs. 0.840 for 'May Treat/Prevent') and exceeds GPT-4 in 4 of 7, with deviations < 0.008 in the remaining classes. On i2b2 NER, LINE achieves the best F1 (98.1%) on both 2006 and 2014, narrowly beating CODER (98.0/97.7) and BGE (97.2/97.1). For renal cancer recurrence detection, LINE reduces the performance gap between concept-only and sentence-based classification: the F1 drop from sentence to concept embeddings shrinks from 0.062 (BGE) to 0.037 (LINE BGE+CODER) and from 0.097 (GPT-4) to 0.075 (LINE GPT-4+CODER), demonstrating effective cross-form proxy generation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by proposing a 'teacher-teacher' mutual learning paradigm, departing from both standard continual pretraining (BioBERT, PubMedBERT, CODER) and the more common teacher-student distillation setup (Reimers & Gurevych). Conceptually, it repurposes contrastive alignment ideas from CLIP-style multimodal learning and cross-lingual embedding alignment, but applies them to two frozen pretrained LLMs connected only by a lightweight trainable module (LINE) that includes a multihead graph attention layer over UMLS relations. The two-stage residual recovery mechanism—using the first stage's residuals to augment training pairs in the second stage—is a distinctive procedural contribution not seen in prior alignment work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework addresses a practical bottleneck in clinical NLP: privacy regulations prevent sharing full clinical notes across institutions, but de-identified concept lists are shareable. By showing that concept lists can serve as effective proxies for clinical text (reducing the performance gap from ~0.06–0.10 to ~0.04–0.07 in F1), the paper offers a concrete path toward collaborative clinical research without IRB-heavy data sharing. Key limitations include the requirement for paired training data and a comprehensive knowledge graph (UMLS), which constrains applicability to domains where such resources exist. Open questions include whether the framework generalizes to truly unpaired modalities (e.g., CT vs. MRI via clinical notes as intermediary) and whether the alignment benefits persist when the knowledge graph is incomplete or noisy.
