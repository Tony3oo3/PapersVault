## Problem

Cost-aware Bayesian optimization lacks acquisition functions that are both theoretically principled and computationally efficient. Existing multi-step-lookahead methods are expensive and numerically brittle, while the most popular cost-aware acquisition function (expected improvement per unit cost) has been shown to perform arbitrarily worse than the optimal policy on certain problems. The paper addresses this gap by deriving a new acquisition function class from the Pandora's Box problem in economics, which admits an explicit Bayesian-optimal solution.

## Input / Output

The method takes a black-box objective function f, a spatially-varying evaluation cost function c(x) (which may be stochastic or unknown, in which case it is modeled via an independent log-Gaussian process), and a budget constraint B, over a compact continuous domain X. At each iteration it produces the next evaluation point x_{t+1} = argmax α_t^{PBGI}(x), where the acquisition function is computed via bisection search on the equation EI_{f|x_{1:t},y_{1:t}}(x; g) = λc(x). A closed-form gradient expression enables standard gradient-based acquisition optimization without additional inner-loop optimization.

## Method

The core contribution is a novel connection between cost-aware Bayesian optimization and the Pandora's Box problem: the Gittins index α*(x) = g where g solves EI_f(x; g) = c(x) is proven Bayesian-optimal for Pandora's Box (Theorem 1), and via Lagrangian duality extends to the expected budget-constrained setting with a multiplier λ (Theorem 2). The adaptation to general Bayesian optimization is to substitute the GP posterior f|x_{1:t},y_{1:t} in place of the prior, yielding the PBGI acquisition function α_t^{PBGI}(x) = g where g solves EI_{f|x_{1:t},y_{1:t}}(x; g) = λc(x). Three variants are defined: fixed-λ budget-constrained, cost-per-sample (λ as unit-conversion), and adaptive decay (PBGI-D) where λ_t decreases by a factor β each time the Pandora's Box stopping rule triggers. In the λ→0 limit, PBGI reduces to a UCB-like form μ(x) + σ(x)√(2 log(σ(x)/(λc(x)))), providing automatic cost-dependent tuning of the confidence parameter. The approach is a spatial simplification (assuming posterior independence) rather than the usual temporal lookahead, making it most effective in higher dimensions where most points are far apart and correlations matter less.

## Results

The paper evaluates PBGI and PBGI-D via median regret curves (with quartiles) across three experiment suites: Bayesian regret at d∈{8,16,32}, synthetic benchmarks (Ackley, Levy, Rosenbrock) at d=16, and empirical objectives (Pest Control d=25, Lunar Lander d=12, Robot Pushing d=14). In the cost-aware setting, PBGI-D decisively outperforms all baselines on most medium-hard problems, while PBGI matches or outperforms LogEIPC and LogEICC. In the uniform-cost setting, PBGI and PBGI-D perform comparably to the strongest baselines (LogEI, UCB) at d=8 and d=16, but at d=32 all deterministic methods degrade to near-random-search performance. On empirical problems, PBGI matches or outperforms baselines on Pest Control and Lunar Lander, but underperforms LogEI variants on Robot Pushing.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against LogEIPC (the standard cost-aware baseline), PBGI substantially outperforms on the adversarial construction of Astudillo et al. [4] (Figure 2), where EIPC over-samples low-value low-cost points, and is comparable on most other problems. Against the non-myopic BMSEI baseline, PBGI-D matches or outperforms on Levy and Rosenbrock, while PBGI outperforms on Ackley; at d=16 Bayesian regret, PBGI variants decisively outperform BMSEI. Against LogEI and UCB (the strongest uniform-cost baselines), PBGI and PBGI-D perform comparably at d=8 and d=16, with PBGI-D sometimes edging ahead on Rosenbrock. Against MSEI, PBGI variants outperform at d=16 in both cost-aware and uniform-cost settings. Against MFMES, LogEICC, TS, MES, and KG, PBGI variants are generally comparable or slightly better. The main weakness is on problems with low multimodality (Rosenbrock, Robot Pushing), where the one-step optimality of EI-based methods gives them an edge and PBGI (without decay) underperforms, though PBGI-D recovers much of the gap.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper draws a novel connection between cost-aware Bayesian optimization and the Pandora's Box problem from economics, whose Bayesian-optimal solution is given by the Gittins index (Weitzman 1979; Gittins et al. 2011). It departs from the standard cost-aware acquisition function EIPC (Snoek et al. 2012), which has been shown to have arbitrarily poor approximation ratios, and from computationally expensive multi-step lookahead methods (BMSEI, MSEI). The key conceptual departure is that PBGI arises from a *spatial* simplification (treating the posterior as uncorrelated, as in Pandora's Box) rather than the usual *temporal* simplification (one-step lookahead) used to derive expected improvement, yielding a non-multiplicative incorporation of costs via a Lagrange-multiplier-like parameter λ.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PBGI provides a theoretically-grounded, computationally lightweight alternative to EIPC with a principled risk-aversion knob (λ) that EIPC lacks, and its performance on medium-to-high-dimensional problems suggests the spatial-uncorrelated approximation is often adequate in practice. The surprising transfer to uniform-cost (classical) Bayesian optimization broadens its applicability beyond the cost-aware setting it was designed for. Limitations include degraded performance when prior-objective multimodality mismatches are severe (Rosenbrock, Robot Pushing), and the method's optimality guarantee holds only for the uncorrelated Pandora's Box setting, not the general correlated GP posterior. Open questions include extending the Gittins-index framework to handle spatial correlations more faithfully, adapting to almost-sure budget constraints, and exploring connections to other Gittins-index applications in queueing theory.
