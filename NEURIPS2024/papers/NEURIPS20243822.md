## Problem

Existing image fusion methods rely on fixed, scene-specific constraint designs that limit adaptability across diverse scenarios and require substantial training resources. A key unexplored question is whether a single training-free framework can dynamically select and inject different fusion conditions at each denoising step to produce sample-customized, controllable fused images without task-specific retraining.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243822/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts pairs or sets of H×W×N images (visible+infrared for multi-modal fusion, multi-focus image sets, or multi-exposure image sets) and outputs a single fused image of the same spatial dimensions. No fine-tuning or task-specific training is required; the pre-trained DDPM is used as-is, and all control is exercised through the condition bank and SCS mechanism during the reverse diffusion sampling loop. Task-specific conditions (e.g., YOLOv5 detection features) can be optionally added to bias the output toward downstream objectives.

## Method

CCF builds a condition bank of three tiers—basic (always active, e.g., MSE reconstruction), enhanced (dynamically selected, e.g., SSIM, SD, edge, frequency), and task-specific (manually added, e.g., detection feature distance)—and injects them into a pre-trained unconditional DDPM via classifier-guidance score decomposition (∇log p(c|x_t) ≈ ∇log p(c|x̂₀)). A sampling-adaptive condition selection (SCS) mechanism computes a per-condition gate weight at each denoising step using the gradient of the condition loss relative to its running expectation, then selects the top-k conditions whose weights are changing most rapidly, ensuring that structurally relevant constraints (MSE, SSIM) dominate mid-sampling while texture constraints (edge, EI) take over in late stages. The selected conditions are minimized via gradient descent on the estimated x̂₀ at every reverse step, progressively calibrating the fused image without any parameter updates to the diffusion backbone.

## Results

On the LLVIP multi-modal fusion benchmark, CCF achieves the best SSIM (1.22), MSE (1694), and CC (0.705) among all compared methods, outperforming the second-best by 0.02 in SSIM and 0.035 in CC. On the MFFW multi-focus dataset, CCF attains SOTA across all four metrics (SD 79.02, AG 8.18, SF 24.30, SCD 2.78), with SD exceeding the runner-up by 11.19. On the MEFB multi-exposure dataset, CCF similarly leads all metrics (SD 71.88, AG 5.70, SF 20.28, SCD 3.00). The only metric where CCF trails is PSNR on LLVIP (32.71 vs. DDFM's 36.10), though it remains competitive with other training-based methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CCF outperforms all 13 baselines on the primary LLVIP benchmark in SSIM, MSE, and CC, with the largest margins over DDFM (the closest training-free diffusion baseline) of +0.04 SSIM and −362 MSE. On MFFW and MEFB, CCF achieves SOTA on every metric, beating U2Fusion (the strongest general-purpose baseline) by 11.19 in SD and 2.11 in SF on MFFW. On the TNO dataset, CCF leads in SD, EN, SSIM, and SCD but trails U2Fusion in SF (10.20 vs. 11.86) and EI (8.29 vs. 12.78). Among training-based methods (SwinFusion, CDDFuse, TC-MoA, MUFusion), CCF consistently surpasses them in structural fidelity (SSIM, CC) while remaining competitive in PSNR. The only notable loss is PSNR on LLVIP, where DDFM's 36.10 exceeds CCF's 32.71, likely due to DDFM's fixed reconstruction paradigm favoring pixel-level fidelity over structural preservation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the diffusion-based image fusion line initiated by DDFM and FusionDiff, which apply a pre-trained DDPM with fixed reconstruction constraints to fuse multi-source images. The key departure is the introduction of a condition bank (basic, enhanced, task-specific) and a sampling-adaptive condition selection (SCS) mechanism that dynamically selects which constraints to enforce at each denoising step, drawing on classifier-guidance score decomposition (Dhariwal & Nichol) and diffusion posterior sampling (Chung et al.) for condition injection without retraining. The SCS gate is inspired by multi-task learning gradient balancing (GradNorm), repurposing it to resolve conflicts among competing fusion constraints across diffusion stages. Unlike prior unified fusion frameworks (U2Fusion, SwinFusion, TC-MoA) that require task-specific training, CCF operates entirely training-free on a frozen DDPM.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free, conditionally controllable paradigm offers a practical path to deploying image fusion in rapidly changing or resource-constrained environments where retraining per scenario is infeasible. The demonstration that task-specific conditions (e.g., YOLOv5 detection features) can be plugged in to improve downstream mAP without retraining the fusion model suggests a modular, plug-and-play design for fusion-as-a-service pipelines. However, the condition bank remains empirically constructed and manually categorized, the reliance on iterative DDPM sampling limits efficiency (~222s per image), and the authors acknowledge that automatic condition classification and high-risk deployment (medical, autonomous driving) remain open. Future work on learned condition selection and faster samplers would be needed for broader adoption.
