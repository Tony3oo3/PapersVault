## Problem

Existing environment-based OOD methods for dynamic graphs suffer from two coupled limitations: they rely on predefined discrete environment codebooks to infer unseen factors (introducing human bias and potentially unrealistic results), and they exploit inferred environments merely as additive features rather than capturing how environment shifts fundamentally alter asymmetric structural correlations between nodes. No prior work systematically addresses both the inference and utilization of environment factors in spatio-temporal graph learning.

## Input / Output

The method consumes T historical graph snapshots, each consisting of a node feature matrix X^t ∈ R^{N×D} and a binary adjacency matrix A^t ∈ {0,1}^{N×N}, which are decomposed into observable features X_X and observed evolution labels Y_X. For traffic forecasting, it predicts 24 future steps from 12 historical steps (12→24); for social link prediction, it predicts next-step links from past graph snapshots. The framework is pluggable and can be paired with different spatio-temporal backbones (AGCRN for traffic, DDGAN for social networks).

## Method

EpoD introduces two tightly coupled mechanisms. First, a self-prompted learning (SPL) module assigns a learnable prompt token p_i ∈ R^d to each node (shared across time steps) and uses a cross-attention decoder to 'squeeze' continuous environment representations Z_E from historical observations, with an objective that maximizes I(Z_E; Y_X) while minimizing I(Z_E; X_X), eliminating the need for predefined environment scales. Second, a structural causal model (SCM) treats a node-centered dynamic subgraph X_S as a mediating variable between historical observations and future evolution: an asymmetric correlation matrix S^t is computed via KL divergences between each node's environment embedding and the mean environment distribution, then L-hop subgraphs are extracted by masking the original topology with S^t. This design captures how environment shifts alter directional node dependencies, and the overall framework is interpretable as a backdoor adjustment P(Y|do(X)) = Σ_{x_S} P(Y|X, X_S)P(X_S), with theoretical guarantees (Theorem 3.1) proving that the true causal environment Z_E* is the optimal solution of the SPL objective.

## Results

On traffic flow prediction (12→24 steps), EpoD achieves the best MAE across all four datasets: 16.92 on PEMS08, 21.12 on PEMS04, 23.58 on SD (2019→2020), and 27.26 on GBA (2019→2020), with corresponding RMSE of 25.66, 34.02, 38.25, and 40.14. The improvement is most pronounced on the large-scale datasets with COVID-19-induced distribution shift, where EpoD reduces MAE by 1.8–2.4 over the best baseline. On social link prediction, EpoD achieves AUC of 83.21% (COLLAB), 80.85% (Yelp), and 83.85% (ACT), outperforming all baselines on Yelp and ACT while trailing EAGLE by 1.2 points on COLLAB.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EpoD outperforms all six traffic flow prediction baselines on every dataset. The closest competitor is CaST [53], which achieves the best MAE on PEMS04 (20.79 vs. EpoD's 21.12) but is outperformed by EpoD on PEMS08 (17.28 vs. 16.92), SD (25.38 vs. 23.58), and GBA (28.67 vs. 27.26). Among standard STGNNs, AGCRN [3] and DSTAGNN [19] are the strongest baselines, with EpoD improving MAE by 0.38 and 0.64 respectively on PEMS08; GWNET [52], Z-GCNETs [8], and STGNCDE [9] trail by 2–3 MAE points. On social link prediction, EpoD beats DySAT [33], IRM [1], VREx [17], GroupDRO [32], and DIDA [58] on all three datasets, with the largest margin over IRM (7.8 AUC on COLLAB). EAGLE [58] is the only baseline that surpasses EpoD, achieving 84.41% AUC on COLLAB versus EpoD's 83.21%, though EpoD reverses the lead on Yelp (80.85 vs. 77.26) and ACT (83.85 vs. 82.70). In the backbone sensitivity analysis (Table 9), EpoD consistently improves over raw ASTGCN [13], DSTAGNN [19], and AGCRN [3] by 0.35–1.59 MAE across all four traffic datasets, confirming its pluggability.

## Relation to Prior Work

EpoD extends the environment-aware OOD generalization line for dynamic graphs (CaST, DIDA, EAGLE, DDGAN) by addressing two limitations the authors identify: reliance on predefined discrete environment codebooks and the failure to capture environment-induced structural changes. It repurposes prompt learning—previously applied to spatio-temporal prediction for multi-attribute forecasting or generalization—into a self-prompted mechanism for continuous environment inference without preset scale. The dynamic subgraph-as-mediating-variable causal pathway departs from both feature-augmentation strategies (CaST, EAGLE) and partition-based subgraph extraction (DDGAN), drawing instead on static-graph subgraph learning and backdoor adjustment from causal inference.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By jointly solving environment inference and utilization within a single causal framework, EpoD offers a more principled and interpretable route to temporal OOD generalization than prior environment-centered methods. The pluggable architecture (compatible with AGCRN, DSTAGNN, ASTGCN backbones) and linear time complexity make it practically deployable across traffic and social-network domains. A key limitation acknowledged by the authors is the strong dependence on pre-existing graph topology for subgraph discovery (K-hop neighbor filtering), which may not adapt well to rapidly evolving node relationships; the authors propose forming subgraphs from empty topology as future work. Open questions include whether the continuous environment representations generalize to scenarios with truly novel (not merely shifted) environment factors, and whether the KL-divergence-based asymmetry metric remains informative at very large node counts.
