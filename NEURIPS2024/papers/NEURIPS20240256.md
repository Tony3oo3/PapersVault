## Problem

Tensor parallelism in multi-device inference introduces AllReduce collective operations that are expensive and represent phases where compute cores sit idle. Standard Transformer architectures require two AllReduce operations per layer (one after MHA, one after FFN), and neither can be overlapped with useful computation, making them a direct bottleneck for Time To First Token latency. The paper asks whether the Transformer architecture itself can be redesigned so that the remaining inter-device communication is minimized and hidden behind compute.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a token sequence (up to 2048 tokens) from a 51,200-token vocabulary and produces a next-token probability distribution, as in standard autoregressive language modeling. A key structural constraint is that the degree of intra-layer parallelism N is fixed at training time and must match the target hardware topology (e.g., 4-way for a 4-GPU node, 8-way for an 8-GPU node); deploying on a mismatched device count would negate the latency gains. Each of the N sub-layers independently processes the full input sequence, so the KV cache is N times larger than an equivalent standard Transformer.

## Method

Kraken replaces the standard sharded MHA+FFN layer with N independent, smaller sub-layers, each containing its own complete Multi-Head Attention and FeedForward Network block. This reduces inter-device communication to a single AllReduce per layer (versus two in standard tensor-parallel Transformers), and critically, the AllReduce output is consumed only by the FFN of the *next* layer rather than the MHA, allowing the collective to be fully overlapped with the MHA computation via a dedicated CUDA stream. To maintain a comparable parameter budget, the FFN hidden expansion is reduced from 4d to 2d, and the embedding dimension d is solved for via a closed-form expression given the target parameter count, number of layers, and degree of parallelism. A final linear projection W_concat ∈ R^{d×N × d} combines the N sub-layer outputs before the unembedding, introducing the only remaining blocking collective in the forward pass.

## Results

Kraken models trained on OpenWebText achieve perplexity comparable to or better than similarly sized GPT-2 models (e.g., Kraken 761M 4-way: 13.09 vs. GPT-2 Large: 13.69), while preserving language modeling capabilities on SuperGLUE (e.g., Kraken 355M 4-way: 55.92 avg zero-shot vs. GPT-2 Medium: 55.73). On multi-GPU inference, Kraken achieves a geomean 35.6% speedup in Time To First Token (TTFT) over standard GPT-3-like Transformers, with individual speedups ranging from 10.9% to 63.7% across model sizes (1.3B–175B), context lengths (128–2048), and parallelism degrees (4-way and 8-way) on an 8×A100 NVSwitch system.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GPT-2 (Radford et al., 2019), Kraken models match or slightly exceed perplexity at comparable parameter counts (13.09 vs. 13.69 at ~760M params; 14.40 vs. 14.87 at ~350M), though the 6-way variant shows a small degradation (19.22 vs. 18.56 for 4-way at 124M). On SuperGLUE zero-shot, Kraken 355M 4-way (55.92 avg) marginally beats GPT-2 Medium (55.73), but Kraken 760M 4-way (51.7) underperforms GPT-2 Large (56.34), likely due to training on only 150B vs. ~300B tokens. Against the GPT-3-like standard Transformer (Brown et al., 2020), Kraken achieves the headline 35.6% geomean TTFT speedup, with the largest gains (up to 63.7%) at 175B scale and 2048 context length under 8-way parallelism. Against GPT-J (Wang & Komatsuzaki, 2021), which also uses a single AllReduce per layer by running Attention and FFN in parallel, Kraken consistently wins on TTFT (e.g., 158.9 ms vs. 230.8 ms for 175B at context 2048, 4-way) because Kraken's AllReduce can be overlapped with the next layer's MHA compute, whereas GPT-J's collective remains on the critical path.

## Relation to Prior Work

Kraken extends the standard GPT-2/GPT-3 Transformer architecture by replacing the tensor-parallel sharding of Multi-Head Attention and FeedForward Network blocks (as in Megatron-LM) with independent, smaller sub-layers per device, introducing a fixed degree of innate model parallelism. Conceptually, it extends the IO-aware philosophy of FlashAttention from the single-device memory hierarchy to the multi-device interconnect, and contrasts with GPT-J's parallel attention+FFN design by ensuring the single remaining AllReduce per layer can be fully overlapped with compute rather than sitting on the critical path. The architecture is designed to complement, not replace, existing tensor parallelism schemes in frameworks like TensorRT-LLM.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 35.6% geomean TTFT speedup across model sizes and parallelism degrees is practically significant for latency-sensitive applications (search augmentation, interactive assistants) where even sub-second improvements matter. Key limitations include the fixed degree of parallelism (constraining deployment to matching hardware topologies), the need to train from scratch without distillation from existing checkpoints, and a larger KV cache due to replicated attention blocks. Open questions include whether communication-aware NAS can automate the parallelism-degree choice, whether the approach composes well with MoE routing or SSM layers, and whether weight initialization from pretrained Transformers can eliminate the from-scratch training cost.
