## Problem

Diffusion-based RL methods (diffusion policies, diffusion planning, diffusion world models) all require iterative sampling from the diffusion model at inference time, incurring tens to hundreds of denoising steps per query and making them impractical for real-time or high-dimensional settings. Moreover, the exploration problem—implementing optimism in the face of uncertainty—has not been addressed in existing diffusion-based RL algorithms. The paper asks whether the flexibility of diffusion models can be harnessed for RL from a representation learning perspective, enabling efficient planning and exploration while entirely bypassing the sampling cost.

## Input / Output

In the MDP setting, the method consumes (state, action, next-state) transitions from a replay buffer to train the representation; at deployment it takes the current state and action to produce a finite-dimensional spectral feature φ_θ(s,a) ∈ ℝ^d that linearly parameterizes the Q-function. In the POMDP setting, raw observations (continuous state vectors or RGB images) are first encoded into 1-D latent embeddings (via a VAE for image inputs), and L=3 consecutive embeddings are concatenated to form the effective state, which is then fed through the same diffusion representation pipeline. The final output is a policy π(·|s) trained via standard actor-critic updates on the linear Q-function Q(s,a) = φ_θ(s,a)ᵀξ, with no iterative diffusion sampling required at test time.

## Method

Diff-SR parameterizes the transition kernel P(s'|s,a) as an energy-based model exp(ψ(s,a)ᵀν(s') − log Z(s,a)), then applies random Fourier features to the resulting Gaussian kernel to obtain a spectral decomposition ⟨φ_ω(s,a), μ_ω(s')⟩ that linearly represents the Q-function for any policy. The key training trick is Tweedie's identity: by corrupting next-states with Gaussian noise at random levels β and matching the denoised reconstruction, the paper derives a simple MSE objective (Eq. 18) that learns ψ(s,a) and the score network ζ(s̃',β) without ever sampling from the diffusion model. The intractable partition function Z(s,a) is eliminated via Proposition 2, which shows it is itself linearly representable by random features of ψ, leading to the final finite-dimensional representation φ_θ(s,a) = elu(W₂ sin(W₁ᵀψ(s,a))) that approximates the infinite-dimensional random feature. Policy optimization then proceeds with standard double-Q TD learning on the linear critic, and exploration is supported by UCB-style bonuses computed directly from the learned representation, all without any iterative denoising at inference time.

## Results

On the MBBL MuJoCo benchmark (200K steps, 4 seeds), Diff-SR achieves 7223.5±437.1 on HalfCheetah, 4788.6±623.1 on Ant-ET, 2800.5±95.4 on Hopper-ET, and 3722.2±406.1 on Walker-ET, outperforming the strongest representation baseline LV-Rep by 90% on Ant and 48% on Walker. On the Meta-World image-based POMDP benchmark, Diff-SR exceeds 90% success rate on 7 of 8 tasks, surpassing the second-best baseline μLV-Rep by 4 additional tasks. In runtime, Diff-SR is approximately 4× faster than the diffusion-based PolyGRAD across all environments, since it avoids iterative sampling from the diffusion model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Diff-SR outperforms the strongest representation-learning baseline LV-Rep on 8 of 10 MuJoCo tasks, with the largest margins on Ant-ET (4788.6 vs. 2511.8, +90%) and Walker-ET (3722.2 vs. 2523.5, +48%), while LV-Rep retains an edge on Humanoid-ET (1086 vs. 821.3). Against the diffusion-based PolyGRAD, Diff-SR wins on nearly all environments (e.g., HalfCheetah 7223.5 vs. 2563.5; Ant-ET 4788.6 vs. 433.6) and runs ~4× faster. Compared to the best model-free baseline SAC, Diff-SR dominates on locomotion tasks (HalfCheetah +81%, Ant-ET +138%, Hopper-ET +54%, Walker-ET +68%) but trails on Humanoid-ET (821.3 vs. 1794.4). Against the best model-based result (Best MBBL), Diff-SR surpasses it on HalfCheetah, Ant-ET, Hopper-ET, S-Humanoid-ET, and Walker-ET, while Best MBBL leads on Humanoid-ET. On the Meta-World image-based POMDP tasks, Diff-SR achieves >90% success on 7/8 tasks versus 3/8 for μLV-Rep, and on state-based POMDP tasks it wins 4 of 6 (HalfCheetah, Walker, Ant, Hopper) while losing on Humanoid (650.1 vs. 806.7) and tying on Pendulum.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two active lines of work: spectral representation learning for RL (LV-Rep, SPEDE, DeepSF) and diffusion models for sequential decision-making (PolyGRAD, diffusion policy, diffusion world models). It departs from both by showing that the direct latent-variable reduction from diffusion to spectral representation is impossible (Section 3.1), then pivoting to an energy-based model view of diffusion combined with random Fourier features and Tweedie's identity to extract a nonlinear spectral representation. The key conceptual departure from prior diffusion-based RL is treating the diffusion model purely as a representation learner rather than a generative sampler, and the departure from prior spectral methods is the use of a general (non-Gaussian) EBM whose partition function introduces the necessary nonlinearity for Q-function representation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: by eliminating iterative diffusion sampling, Diff-SR achieves ~4× speedup over PolyGRAD while matching or exceeding its performance, making diffusion-based RL substantially more deployable. The framework unifies MDP and POMDP settings and provides a principled exploration bonus without additional sampling cost. However, the main limitation acknowledged is the absence of evaluation on real-world robotic control or multi-task settings, and the representation is still learned via a neural network approximation of an infinite-dimensional random feature, leaving open questions about theoretical guarantees on approximation quality and sample complexity in the general (non-Gaussian) EBM case.
