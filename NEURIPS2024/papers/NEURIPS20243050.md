## Problem

For stochastic submodular maximization with bandit feedback and a cardinality constraint, the best known regret upper bound was O(kn^{1/3}T^{2/3}), but no minimax lower bound existed to confirm optimality. The paper asks whether a T^{2/3} rate is unavoidable for small T, whether the trivial sqrt(binomial(n,k))T rate from treating each k-subset as an arm is optimal for large T, and what the correct interpolation between these two regimes is.

## Input / Output

At each round t of a T-round horizon, the algorithm selects a subset S_t ⊆ [n] with |S_t| ≤ k and observes a single noisy scalar reward f(S_t) + η_t where η_t is mean-zero 1-sub-Gaussian noise. The performance metric is robust greedy regret R_gr, which measures cumulative loss relative to the best ε-approximate greedy set (not the true optimum), with an additive T·1^T ε penalty that accounts for the noise tolerance in the greedy construction. The algorithm's key tunable parameter is the greedy stop level l, which determines after how many greedy stages to switch to UCB over all size-k super-arms containing the current set.

## Method

SUB-UCB interpolates between two regret regimes by choosing a greedy stop level l = k − i*. In the first l stages, it runs a UCB-style greedy exploration: at each stage i, it pulls every candidate set S^{(i−1)}∪{a} for a ∉ S^{(i−1)} until each has been pulled m = T^{2/3}n^{−2/3}(log T)^{1/3} times, then commits to the arm with the highest UCB value. After reaching size l, it switches to standard UCB over all binomial(n−k, k−l) size-k super-arms that contain S^{(l)}. The critical insight is that i* is chosen as the largest value satisfying (16/(n²k⁶))·binomial(n−k, i*)³ ≤ T, which balances the l·T^{2/3}n^{1/3} regret from greedy exploration against the sqrt(T·binomial(n−k, k−l)) regret from UCB on super-arms. The paper proves this achieves R_gr = Õ(min_L(Ln^{1/3}T^{2/3} + sqrt(binomial(n−k, k−L))T)), matching a minimax lower bound for a restricted algorithm class up to logarithmic factors.

## Results

The paper establishes the first minimax lower bound for stochastic submodular maximization with bandit feedback: $\tilde{\Omega}(\min_{L\leq k}(L^{1/3}n^{1/3}T^{2/3}+\sqrt{\binom{n}{k-L}}\,T))$, and a stronger $\tilde{\Omega}(\min_{L\leq k}(Ln^{1/3}T^{2/3}+\sqrt{\binom{n}{k-L}}\,T))$ for algorithms with non-adaptive greedy error thresholds. The proposed SUB-UCB algorithm achieves a matching upper bound of $\tilde{O}(\min_{L\leq k}(Ln^{1/3}T^{2/3}+\sqrt{\binom{n}{k-L}}\,T))$, closing the gap with the restricted-class lower bound up to logarithmic factors. In experiments on synthetic submodular functions (n=15, k=4 and n=20, k=5), SUB-UCB with the theoretically motivated stop level $k-i^*$ consistently outperforms both ETCG and naive UCB across all time horizons.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's main theoretical comparison is against the prior best upper bound of $kn^{1/3}T^{2/3}$ (from [28], [31], [36]), which SUB-UCB improves upon by introducing the interpolating $\sqrt{\binom{n}{k-L}}T$ term that dominates for large $T$. In the empirical comparison (Figure 1, n=15, k=4, weighted set cover), SUB-UCB with stop level $k-i^*$ outperforms ETCG [28] across all $T$ values, and outperforms naive UCB on all size-$k$ arms for moderate $T$ before converging to similar performance at very large $T$. For small $T$ (where $\binom{n}{k}T^{1/2} > T^{2/3}$), both SUB-UCB and ETCG achieve sublinear $O(T^{2/3})$ regret while naive UCB suffers linear regret due to insufficient exploration budget. As $T$ grows large, the $\sqrt{\binom{n}{k}}T^{1/2}$ regime takes over and SUB-UCB's adaptive switching to full UCB on size-$k$ super-arms matches the performance of naive UCB, while ETCG remains suboptimal. Figure 2 further confirms that the theoretically derived stop level $k-i^*$ is near-optimal empirically, with the true optimal stop cardinality gradually shifting from $k$ to $0$ as $T$ increases.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the explore-then-commit (ETC) greedy framework for submodular maximization established by Streeter & Golovin [36] and Nie et al. [28], which achieved O(kn^{1/3}T^{2/3}) regret but could not capture the correct trade-off with the trivial √(C(n,k))·T bound from treating each k-subset as an arm. The key departure is the SUB-UCB algorithm, which interpolates between these two regimes by greedily constructing a set up to an intermediate size i* and then running UCB over all size-k completions of that set. The robust greedy regret (R_gr) framework, while implicitly used in all prior upper-bound proofs, is here made explicit as the natural benchmark, and the paper provides the first minimax lower bound matching the upper bound for a restricted algorithm class.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first minimax lower bound for stochastic submodular bandits, establishing that the T^{2/3} rate (unusual compared to the standard T^{1/2} in MAB) is unavoidable for small T, and that the √(C(n,k))·T rate is optimal for large T, with a clean interpolation in between. The practical insight that the optimal greedy stop level k−i* depends on T and n gives practitioners a concrete parameter to tune. Open questions include closing the remaining k^{2/3} gap between the general lower bound and upper bound, extending to matroid constraints, non-monotonic submodular functions, and DR-submodular continuous settings, and whether the non-adaptive threshold restriction can be removed without losing the stronger lower bound.
