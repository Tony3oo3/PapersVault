## Problem

Existing parallel-in-time (PinT) solvers based on Parareal struggle to scale to high-dimensional PDE systems: the original Parareal converges slowly, while its Gaussian-process-based improvements (GParareal, nnGParareal) incur cubic matrix-inversion costs, expensive per-dimension hyperparameter optimization, and require d separate scalar models that cannot be parallelized when the ODE dimension d far exceeds the number of available processors N. This limits practical application to systems with d ≤ N, excluding most real-world PDE discretizations on fine spatial meshes.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a d-dimensional ODE system (obtained by spatial discretization of a PDE), an initial condition vector, a coarse solver G and fine solver F (e.g., RK1 and RK8), and a time interval [t₀, t_N] partitioned into N subintervals. The ODE/PDE system is rescaled so all coordinates lie in [-1,1] before training. The output is the converged set of initial conditions {U_i^K} at each sub-interval boundary, which together reconstruct the full time-dependent solution on the spatial mesh; the method is validated on meshes up to 10⁵ discretization points.

## Method

RandNet-Parareal replaces the GP/nnGP correction function in the Parareal predictor-corrector rule with a single-hidden-layer random neural network (RandNet) that learns the discrepancy F−G. The hidden-layer weight matrix A and bias vector ζ are sampled once from uniform distributions and held fixed; only the output (readout) weights W are trained by solving a closed-form ridgeless least-squares (minimum-norm interpolation) problem, eliminating backpropagation and all hyperparameter optimization. At each prediction, only m nearest neighbors (m=3–4) of the current initial condition are used for training, and the network jointly predicts all d output coordinates in a single multi-output model rather than d independent scalar models. This design reduces model training cost from O(d·m³) with per-dimension hyperparameter tuning (nnGParareal) to O(M²·m + M·d·m) with no tuning, and removes the d≈N hardware constraint, enabling speed-ups of up to ×125 over the serial fine solver and ×22 over standard Parareal on systems with d up to 10⁵.

## Results

RandNet-Parareal achieves parallel speed-ups of up to ×125 over the serial fine solver and ×22 over standard Parareal across five PDE systems. For the viscous Burgers' equation at d=1128, N=128, it converges in 4 iterations with a speed-up of 29.98 (vs. 91 iterations and 1.41 for Parareal, and 6 iterations and 8.26 for nnGParareal). The model training cost is ~1 second for RandNet-Parareal versus ~1 hour 25 minutes for nnGParareal at the same dimensionality. For the Diffusion-Reaction equation at d=110,450, N=512, RandNet-Parareal reaches a speed-up of 124.52 in 10h48m, while nnGParareal fails to converge within a 48-hour budget.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Parareal (Lions et al., 2001), RandNet-Parareal consistently wins on all metrics: it requires 3–20× fewer iterations to converge, eliminates model overhead entirely (T_model ≈ 0s vs. the fine solver cost embedded in Parareal's iterations), and achieves speed-ups of ×8.6–21.2 (Burgers'), ×3–5 (Diffusion-Reaction), and ×1.3–3.6 (SWE) over Parareal. Against nnGParareal (Gattiglio et al., 2024), the advantage is more dramatic: RandNet-Parareal's model training cost is ~700× lower (1s vs. 12m at d=128; 1s vs. 1h25m at d=1128), and it uses a single multi-output network instead of d independent scalar nnGPs, eliminating the queuing bottleneck when d ≫ N. In the critical high-dimensional regime (d ≫ N, e.g., d=110,450 with N=512), nnGParareal fails to converge within a 48-hour budget, while RandNet-Parareal completes in under 11 hours with a speed-up of 124.52. The paper also notes that RandNet-Parareal requires no hyperparameter tuning (no kernel scale optimization, no regularization search), whereas nnGParareal requires per-dimension log-likelihood maximization with multiple random restarts.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two research threads: parallel-in-time (PinT) PDE solvers and random neural networks. On the PinT side, it directly extends the Parareal → GParareal → nnGParareal lineage, where each successive method improves the learning of the fine-minus-coarse discrepancy (F−G) to accelerate convergence. The key departure is replacing the nearest-neighbor Gaussian Process (nnGP) correction in nnGParareal with a single multi-output RandNet, which eliminates the need for d independent scalar models, removes expensive hyperparameter optimization, and yields a closed-form ridgeless least-squares solution for the readout weights. On the RandNet side, the paper leverages the universal approximation theory of Gonon et al. (2023) to justify the architecture (ReLU activation, uniform sampling of hidden weights), but applies it in a novel PinT context where the network is retrained at every prediction step on a small nearest-neighbor subset.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RandNet-Parareal removes the critical d≈N hardware constraint that limited nnGParareal to systems where the ODE dimension is comparable to the number of processors, enabling PinT solving of PDEs on spatial meshes up to 10^5 points with only ~512 cores and negligible model-training overhead. The practical impact is substantial: speed-ups of x125 over the serial fine solver and x22 over Parareal, with nnGParareal failing to converge entirely in the high-dimensional regime. A key limitation is that convergence still depends on the quality of the coarse solver G, and for hyperbolic PDEs (Burgers', shallow water) where standard Parareal is known to struggle, system-specific coarse solvers may still be needed—an avenue the authors defer to future work. The robustness to the number of neurons M, the number of nearest neighbors m, and the random seed is a practical advantage that makes the method genuinely out-of-the-box, but the theoretical approximation bounds require rescaling the system to unit norm, which may not always be straightforward.
