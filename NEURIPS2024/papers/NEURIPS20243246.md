## Problem

While diffusion-model-generated data has been shown to substantially improve empirical adversarial robustness, it remains unclear whether this approach transfers to deterministic certified defenses, and no systematic study of how certified robustness scales with generated data, model capacity, and training duration has been conducted. This paper fills that gap by analyzing scaling behavior across four Lipschitz-bound architectures and two threat models, revealing that certified and empirical methods exhibit fundamentally different data-scaling dynamics.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243246/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 32×32 RGB images from CIFAR-10 or CIFAR-100, where each training batch is composed of 30% original images and 70% EDM-generated images (up to 10 million generated samples). At inference, the model outputs a class label together with a deterministic certified robustness radius, computed as the logit margin divided by the network's Lipschitz constant, guaranteeing no misclassification within that ℓ₂ or ℓ∞ perturbation ball.

## Method

The paper augments the training of four deterministic Lipschitz-bound architectures (ℓ∞-dist Net, SortNet for ℓ∞; LOT, GloroNet for ℓ₂) with images generated by an Elucidating Diffusion Model (EDM) trained solely on the CIFAR-10 training set, using a 70/30 generated-to-real ratio per batch. Key empirical findings include: (1) certified accuracy saturates at ~1 million generated images regardless of model size, unlike empirical adversarial training where gains continue to 100 million; (2) removing regularization (e.g., dropout) and switching to cosine annealing improves certified accuracy when auxiliary data is present, because the generated data closes the generalization gap; (3) increasing training epochs has the strongest consistent effect on certified accuracy; and (4) the reduction in generalization gap correlates linearly with the certified accuracy gain. The approach achieves state-of-the-art deterministic certificates on CIFAR-10: 41.78% for (ℓ∞, ε=8/255) and 69.05% for (ℓ₂, ε=36/255).

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with the ℓ∞ (ε=8/255) threat model, SortNet without dropout trained with 10M EDM-generated images achieves 41.78% certified accuracy (54.75% clean), a new state-of-the-art improving upon the previous best by +1.39 percentage points. For the ℓ2 (ε=36/255) threat model, LOT-L trained with 5M generated images reaches 69.05% certified accuracy (81.42% clean), surpassing the prior best by +3.95 percentage points. On CIFAR-100, the largest relative gain is for SortNet (ℓ∞), where certified accuracy jumps from 9.20% to 17.28% (+8.08 pp). Across all four architectures and both threat models, adding generated data consistently improves certified accuracy, with clean accuracy also improving in most cases (up to +4.83 pp).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper augments four existing certified-robustness architectures with EDM-generated data and compares each against its own no-auxiliary-data baseline. For ℓ∞ (ε=8/255) on CIFAR-10, SortNet w/o dropout improves from 36.50% to 41.78% certified accuracy (+5.28 pp), and ℓ∞-dist Net improves from 34.25% to 37.53% (+3.28 pp). For ℓ2 (ε=36/255), LOT-L improves from 64.39% to 69.05% (+4.66 pp) and GloroNet-L from 65.21% to 68.12% (+2.91 pp). The paper's headline claim is a new state-of-the-art on the SoK Certified Robustness leaderboard: +1.39 pp over the previous best for ℓ∞ and +3.95 pp for ℓ2 on CIFAR-10. Notably, removing dropout from SortNet *decreases* certified accuracy without auxiliary data (39.72% → 37.44%) but *increases* it with auxiliary data (41.32% → 41.78%), demonstrating that generated data acts as a regularizer that enables larger, less-regularized models to perform better. The paper also notes that Hu et al. report a slightly higher ℓ2 accuracy of 70.1% using DDPM-generated data, so the ℓ2 result is not a strict SOTA.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the generated-data-augmented training recipe from empirical adversarial robustness (Gowal et al. 2021, Wang et al. 2023) to deterministic Lipschitz-bound certified defenses, a transfer that had only been explored once before (Hu et al. 2023). It systematically applies EDM-generated CIFAR-10 data to four existing certified architectures (LOT, GloroNet, SortNet, ℓ∞-dist Net) and ablates training hyperparameters (epochs, model size, regularization, scheduler) to identify what drives the gains. A notable departure from the empirical-robustness literature is the finding that convex bound propagation (MTL-IBP) does not benefit from generated data at all, indicating that scalability results do not transfer trivially across certification paradigms.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first systematic scaling analysis for certified robustness with generated data, yielding concrete recommendations (scale epochs, increase capacity, reduce regularization, use cosine annealing) that practitioners can immediately apply. The discovery that certified accuracy saturates around 1M generated images—unlike empirical robustness which keeps improving to 100M—suggests a fundamental difference in how the two paradigms consume data, with implications for compute allocation in future certified-robustness research. Open questions include whether the saturation point is dataset-specific, whether fully synthetic training is viable, and whether theoretical scaling laws can be derived for Lipschitz-constrained networks. The work is limited to CIFAR-10/100 and single-seed runs, so generalization to ImageNet or other datasets remains unverified.
