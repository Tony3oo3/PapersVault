## Problem

Despite the empirical success of data-driven regularizers learned via critic-based (unsupervised) losses, there is little theory explaining what geometric structure these regularizers acquire and how it relates to the underlying data distributions. This paper initiates a study of the optimal regularizer within the family of star body gauges, asking whether the learned regularizer can be exactly characterized in terms of the clean and noisy data distributions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242275/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes two probability distributions over R^d: a clean data distribution D_r (e.g., MNIST training samples) and a user-defined noisy distribution D_n (e.g., D_r convolved with Gaussian noise). It produces a learned regularizer of the form ‖·‖_K, the gauge of a star body K, parameterized by a deep convolutional network with positively homogenous Leaky ReLU activations. At inference time, a noisy measurement y is reconstructed by minimizing ‖x−y‖²_ℓ₂ + λ‖x‖_K via gradient descent, yielding a denoised signal x̂.

## Method

The paper restricts the regularizer family to gauges of star bodies and shows that the adversarial (Wasserstein-inspired) loss E_{D_r}[‖x‖_K] − E_{D_n}[‖x‖_K] equals d·Ṽ_{−1}(L_{r,n}, K), a dual mixed volume between K and a data-dependent star body L_{r,n} whose radial function is built from the densities of D_r and D_n. Applying Lutwak's dual Brunn-Minkowski inequality then yields an exact characterization of the unique optimal star body (up to scaling) under a containment condition on the distributions. The authors further derive new critic-based losses from α-divergences (notably the Hellinger distance), showing they also admit dual mixed volume interpretations and can be competitive in practice. Finally, they establish that the squared gauge ‖·‖²_K is ρ-weakly convex iff a certain harmonic 2-combination star body is convex, and identify neural network architectures (positively homogenous, injective layers with Leaky ReLU or residual connections) that guarantee the learned sublevel set is a valid star body.

## Results

On MNIST denoising (Gaussian noise, σ²=0.05), the Hellinger-based loss achieves 23.06 PSNR and 0.0052 MSE over 100 test images, outperforming the standard adversarial loss (22.32 PSNR, 0.0064 MSE) and Total Variation regularization (20.52 PSNR, 0.0091 MSE). The adversarial regularizer with a further tuned regularization parameter λ achieves the best overall performance at 24.14 PSNR and 0.0041 MSE. These results demonstrate that the new α-divergence-based critic loss is competitive with the established Wasserstein-based adversarial loss for learning star body regularizers.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares the proposed Hellinger-based critic loss against two baselines on MNIST denoising. Against the standard adversarial regularizer from Lunz et al. [53] (Wasserstein-based loss with the recommended λ=2λ̃), the Hellinger-based loss wins by 0.74 dB in PSNR (23.06 vs. 22.32) and reduces MSE from 0.0064 to 0.0052. Against Total Variation regularization [44], the Hellinger-based loss wins by a larger margin of 2.54 dB in PSNR (23.06 vs. 20.52) and nearly halves the MSE (0.0052 vs. 0.0091). However, when the adversarial regularizer is given a further tuned regularization parameter (λ=5.1λ̃²), it slightly outperforms the Hellinger-based loss by 1.08 dB in PSNR (24.14 vs. 23.06), suggesting that the Hellinger loss is competitive but not yet superior to a well-tuned adversarial loss in this simple denoising setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the adversarial regularization framework of Lunz et al. (NeurIPS 2018), which learns 1-Lipschitz regularizers via a Wasserstein-distance-inspired critic loss, by restricting the function class to star body gauges and leveraging dual Brunn-Minkowski theory to characterize the optimal solution. It also builds on the star body gauge framework of Leong et al. (2022), which studied optimal regularization for a single data source, by introducing critic-based losses derived from α-divergences (notably the Hellinger distance) that create more complex dependencies on the star body geometry. The paper departs from prior work by providing the first exact characterization of optimal regularizers under these critic-based losses and by connecting the learned geometry to dual mixed volumes, a tool not previously applied to regularizer learning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work provides the first theoretical lens for understanding what geometric structure critic-based regularizer learning actually discovers, bridging a gap between the empirical success of adversarial regularization and the lack of structural guarantees. The dual mixed volume interpretation offers a principled way to reason about optimality and could guide the design of new loss functions. However, the empirical validation is limited to a single small-scale MNIST denoising experiment with 100 test images, and the theoretical results require strong assumptions (absolute continuity, a 'containment' condition on densities, positive continuity of radial functions) that may not hold in practice. Open questions include extending the analysis to other divergences, characterizing global minimizers for the Hellinger loss, relaxing distributional assumptions, and demonstrating practical gains in realistic inverse problems beyond denoising.
