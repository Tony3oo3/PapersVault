## Problem

State-of-the-art deep state-space models face a trade-off between scalability and expressivity: achieving linear-time inference typically requires restricting the variational posterior to diagonal covariances or the dynamics to linear form, both of which degrade the model's ability to capture the dense spatiotemporal covariance structure needed for accurate forecasting. This paper addresses how to learn nonlinear Gaussian state-space models with dense posterior covariances at scale, where the state dimensionality L is large enough that O(L³) operations become prohibitive.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a sequence of T observations (neural spike counts of dimension N=182 or 54, or 16×16 pixel images flattened to N=256) and produces S Monte Carlo samples of the full latent trajectory z_{1:T}∈R^{L×S} (L=4–100), along with per-time-step posterior means, predictive means, and Cholesky factors of the structured covariance. A key design choice is the decomposition of the inference network into a local encoder (processing y_t alone) and a backward encoder (a GRU processing future observations in reverse), which naturally handles missing observations by zeroing the local update. The causal variant additionally yields filtered (causal) marginal statistics as a by-product, enabling real-time streaming inference.

## Method

XFADS parameterizes the variational posterior as the product of the prior dynamics and data-dependent Gaussian pseudo-observations, so that the posterior inherits the latent dependency structure of the generative model. The key insight is 'smoothing as filtering': by making each pseudo-observation depend on current and future data y_{t:T}, a single forward filtering pass yields approximate smoothed marginals, avoiding intractable backward recursions. The variational predict step uses a forward-KL (moment-matching) objective approximated via the reparameterization trick, producing a low-rank covariance structure (rank ≤ S samples); the update step exploits conjugacy to add low-rank precision updates (rank r = r_α + r_β) from the local and backward encoders. By never instantiating full L×L covariance matrices and instead working with the structured factors (centered sample matrix M^c and low-rank K), all filtering operations and the closed-form KL evaluation reduce to O(L(Sr + S² + r²)) per timestep, yielding overall O(TL(Sr+S²+r²)) complexity that scales linearly in L.

## Results

On the monkey reaching (MC_Maze) dataset, XFADS achieves R²=0.89 for smoothed hand velocity decoding, R²=0.88 for causally filtered decoding (without future data), and R²=0.74 for fully predicted trajectories unrolled from the learned dynamics—demonstrating that the learned nonlinear dynamics capture predictive structure. On the pendulum and bouncing ball benchmarks, XFADS consistently outperforms DKF, DVBF, and SVAE on prediction-window R² while matching their smoothing performance. On the DMFC_RSG timing task, XFADS achieves the highest bits-per-spike (BPS) in both context and prediction windows. The inference algorithm scales O(TL(Sr+S²+r²)) in wall-clock time, empirically confirmed as linear in L, versus the O(L³) scaling of standard dense-covariance message passing.

## Baselines & Comparisons

XFADS outperforms all four baselines (SVAE, DKF, DVBF, and LFADS) on prediction metrics across every dataset tested. On the pendulum and bouncing ball benchmarks, all methods achieve comparable smoothing R², but XFADS shows a 'noticeable margin' in prediction-window R², indicating it learns dynamics that propagate latent features irrelevant to the likelihood (e.g., angular velocity). On the monkey reaching dataset, XFADS achieves the highest BPS and R² in the 500 ms prediction window, while the causal variant decodes hand velocity at R²=0.88 from filtered states alone—nearly matching the 0.89 smoothing R². On DMFC_RSG, XFADS produces condition-averaged PSTHs that remain faithful to data even during lengthy prediction windows, outperforming DKF, DVBF, SVAE, and LFADS in BPS. SVAE is restricted to linear dynamics and cannot model the nonlinear systems considered; DKF and DVBF use diagonal covariance approximations that cannot capture the dense second-order statistics important for prediction; LFADS, while designed for neural data, lacks the structured covariance and amortized smoothing framework that gives XFADS its predictive advantage.

## Relation to Prior Work

XFADS extends the structured VAE (SVAE) framework of Johnson et al., which parameterizes the variational posterior via conjugate Gaussian potentials, from linear to nonlinear dynamics by replacing exact message passing with a differentiable approximate filtering algorithm. It also builds on the DVBF idea of forcing posterior samples through the learned dynamics to enable gradient flow to the generative model, but departs from both DKF and DVBF by using dense (low-rank structured) covariance posteriors rather than diagonal or black-box parameterizations. The key conceptual departure is the 'smoothing as filtering' trick: by encoding future data into the pseudo-observation parameters, the backward smoothing pass is eliminated entirely, reducing the problem to a single forward pass with O(L) per-step complexity via Woodbury-identity-based linear algebra.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The O(L) scaling makes nonlinear Gaussian state-space modeling practical for high-dimensional latent spaces (L=100+) where exact or cubic-complexity methods become infeasible, and the causal inference network variant enables real-time streaming inference and online optimal experimental design in neuroscience. A notable limitation is that the approximate ELBO no longer lower-bounds the true log-marginal likelihood, and the paper acknowledges that quantifying this gap or developing corrections is an open problem. Future directions include more parameter-efficient precision update architectures for moderate ranks, extending to S > L regimes, and applying the framework to other exponential family dynamics beyond Gaussians.
