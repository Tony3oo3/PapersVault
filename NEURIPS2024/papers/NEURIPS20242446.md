## Problem

Prior in-context learning (ICL) research was confined to the few-shot regime (a handful of examples) due to limited context windows in LLMs. With the advent of 1M-token context windows (e.g., Gemini 1.5 Pro), the many-shot regime—hundreds to thousands of in-context examples—becomes feasible but remains largely unexplored. This paper asks how ICL performance scales with the number of shots, whether the dependence on high-quality human-generated rationales can be reduced, and how the learning dynamics of ICL change from few-shot to many-shot.

## Input / Output

The model (Gemini 1.5 Pro, 1M-token context) receives a prompt containing K in-context examples (up to 8192 shots) followed by a test input, and generates a prediction autoregressively via greedy decoding. A key structural constraint is that any K-shot prompt is nested: it includes all examples from smaller prompts, so adding shots only appends new information. For Reinforced ICL, the in-context examples are (problem, model-generated rationale) pairs filtered by final-answer correctness; for Unsupervised ICL, the prompt lists only unsolved problems followed by a short format-specifying preamble. KV caching is used to reduce inference cost, and runtime scales linearly (not quadratically) with the number of shots.

## Method

The paper introduces two novel ICL variants to reduce reliance on human-written rationales. Reinforced ICL replaces human chain-of-thought solutions with model-generated rationales: a few-shot CoT prompt is used to sample multiple rationales per problem, and only those yielding the correct final answer are retained as in-context demonstrations. Unsupervised ICL goes further by removing rationales entirely, prompting the model with only a list of domain-specific problems plus a brief output-format instruction, hypothesizing that the problems alone suffice to 'locate' latent task knowledge acquired during pretraining. The core empirical contribution is a systematic scaling study across nine task families (math, QA, translation, summarization, planning, algorithmic reasoning, sentiment analysis, code verification, and synthetic high-dimensional functions), showing that many-shot ICL yields large performance gains over few-shot, can override pre-training biases (e.g., flipped sentiment labels), learns non-NLP tasks like 20-digit sequential parity and high-dimensional linear classification, and performs comparably to supervised fine-tuning on low-resource translation. The paper also demonstrates that next-token prediction loss (NLL) on ground-truth outputs is an unreliable proxy for ICL task performance, as NLL continues decreasing even when accuracy plateaus or degrades.

## Results

Many-shot ICL with Gemini 1.5 Pro yields large gains over few-shot across all evaluated tasks. On Big-Bench Hard, Reinforced ICL achieves 83% average success rate vs. 72.1% for standard 3-shot CoT prompting and 77.1% for Unsupervised ICL. On GPQA (diamond split), 125-shot ICL with ground-truth or model-generated rationales surpasses Claude-3 Sonnet's 40.4% accuracy, approaching Claude-3 Opus. On MATH500, many-shot ICL outperforms the 4-shot Minerva baseline (55.7%), with Reinforced ICL plateauing ~5% above standard ICL at ~25 shots. On low-resource English→Bemba translation, 997-shot ICL improves chrF2++ by 15.3% over the 1-shot Gemini prompt, establishing new state-of-the-art.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On GPQA, 125-shot ICL with ground-truth or model-generated rationales surpasses Claude-3 Sonnet (40.4%) and approaches Claude-3 Opus, while Reinforced ICL outperforms ground-truth rationales up to 25 shots. On MATH500 and GSM8K, many-shot ICL clearly outperforms the 4-shot Minerva prompt (55.7% and 90.6% respectively), with Reinforced ICL showing the strongest transfer to GSM8K. On Big-Bench Hard, Reinforced ICL (83%) beats both Unsupervised ICL (77.1%) and the standard 3-shot CoT prompt from Suzgun et al. (72.1%), indicating model-generated rationales can exceed human-written ones. On low-resource translation, many-shot ICL outperforms NLLB (35% chRF2++ on Bemba) and Google Translate (40% on Kurdish), and Gemini 1.5 Pro at 997 shots surpasses both Claude-3-Opus and GPT-4-Turbo on Bemba despite starting lower in few-shot. On sequential parity, 8192-shot ICL surpasses a GPT-2 Medium transformer trained from scratch on 20× more examples. On summarization, peak many-shot ICL performance is remarkably close to PEGASUS and mT5 (specialized fine-tuned models), though XSum performance degrades beyond 50 shots. For planning, many-shot ICL improves success rate from 42% to 62%, though still far below Fast-Downward; the 1-shot GPT-4 reference from Valmeekam et al. is reported for context.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the in-context learning paradigm from the few-shot regime (constrained by GPT-3-era 2K-token context windows) to the many-shot regime (hundreds to thousands of shots) enabled by 1M-token context windows in Gemini 1.5 Pro. The Reinforced ICL technique directly adapts the generate-rationales-and-filter-by-correctness pipeline from Singh et al.'s self-training work [54] to the ICL setting, while Unsupervised ICL is motivated by the task-recognition view of ICL [63, 21, 60] that prompts merely 'locate' latent concepts. The paper also extends prior analyses of pre-training bias in ICL [29] and high-dimensional function learning [61] from few-shot to many-shot, and challenges long-context scaling laws [65, 2, 26] by showing NLL does not reliably predict downstream ICL performance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Many-shot ICL emerges as a practical, training-free alternative to supervised fine-tuning (comparable performance on translation) while also enabling capabilities few-shot ICL cannot achieve: overriding pre-training biases, learning high-dimensional numerical functions, and reducing dependence on scarce human-written rationales. The finding that next-token prediction loss fails to predict ICL performance on reasoning tasks has methodological implications for how the field evaluates long-context models. Key open questions include why performance sometimes degrades with more shots (e.g., MATH beyond ~125 examples), the sensitivity to example ordering in many-shot prompts, and whether these findings generalize beyond Gemini 1.5 Pro to other long-context architectures.
