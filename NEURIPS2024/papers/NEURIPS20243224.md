## Problem

Existing methods for achieving logarithmic regret by exploiting the curvature of feasible sets (e.g., FTL under growth conditions on loss vectors) are limited to online linear optimization, require curvature over the entire boundary of the feasible set, and suffer Ω(T) regret when ideal conditions on loss vectors are not met. This paper asks whether algorithms adaptive to the curvature of loss functions can exploit feasible-set curvature more generally, with only a local geometric condition, while retaining robustness in adversarial and corrupted stochastic environments.

## Input / Output

At each round the learner receives a convex loss function f_t: K → R and observes only the gradient ∇f_t(x_t) at its chosen decision. The regret bound depends on two problem-specific quantities: ρ, the radius of the smallest Euclidean sphere that contains the optimal decision x_★ and encloses K, and ‖∇f°(x_★)‖₂, the norm of the expected gradient at the optimum. The sphere-enclosed condition is a local property defined only around x_★ on the boundary of K, in contrast to global strong convexity, and the same algorithm simultaneously yields O(ρ ln T) in stochastic settings, O(√T) in adversarial settings, and O(ρ ln T + √(Cρ ln T)) in corrupted stochastic settings with corruption level C.

## Method

The core mechanism combines a universal online learning algorithm (MetaGrad or Maler), which provides the adaptive bound ∑⟨∇f_t(x_t), x_t − x_★⟩ = O(c_sc √(∑‖x_t − x_★‖² ln T) + c_sc' ln T), with a new geometric condition called (ρ, x_★, f°)-sphere-enclosedness: there exists a sphere of radius ρ containing K, passing through x_★, whose center lies along ∇f°(x_★). This condition yields the key inequality ⟨∇f°(x_★), x − x_★⟩ ≥ γ_★‖x − x_★‖₂² for all x ∈ K, which lower-bounds regret in terms of ∑‖x_t − x_★‖₂². Substituting into the universal bound and solving the resulting quadratic inequality gives R_T = O(c_sc²ρ/‖∇f°(x_★)‖₂ · ln T). The analysis extends to (κ,q)-uniformly convex sets via a q-th power version of the curvature inequality, yielding the matching bound O(T^{(q−2)/(2(q−1))}(ln T)^{q/(2(q−1))}) that interpolates between O(ln T) for strongly convex sets (q=2) and Õ(√T) for non-curved sets (q→∞).

## Results

The paper establishes that any algorithm satisfying the universal online learning bound (e.g., MetaGrad or Maler) achieves O(ρ/||∇f°(x*)||₂ ln T) regret in stochastic environments when the feasible set is sphere-enclosed facing the optimal decision, matching the existing lower bound Ω(1/(λL) ln T) for ellipsoids (Corollary 12). For q-uniformly convex feasible sets, the regret improves to O(T^((q-2)/(2(q-1))) (ln T)^(q/(2(q-1)))), which is strictly better than the prior O(T^((q-2)/(q-1))) bound of Kerdreux et al. and interpolates between O(ln T) (q=2, strongly convex) and Õ(√T) (q→∞, non-curved). In corrupted stochastic environments with corruption level C, the regret degrades gracefully to O(ρ ln T + √(Cρ ln T)), and in fully adversarial environments the algorithm still guarantees O(√T) regret where FTL-based methods suffer Ω(T).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Compared to Huang et al. [9], the paper's approach overcomes three key limitations: it handles general convex (not just linear) loss functions, requires only local curvature (sphere-enclosedness around x*) rather than global strong convexity of the entire feasible set, and guarantees O(√T) regret in adversarial environments where FTL-based methods suffer Ω(T) when the growth condition on loss vectors is violated. Against Molinaro [17], the paper's bound is comparable in the linear stochastic setting but extends to convex losses and corrupted stochastic environments. The most direct quantitative improvement is over Kerdreux et al. [11] for uniformly convex sets: the paper achieves T^((q-2)/(2(q-1))) (ln T)^(q/(2(q-1))) versus their T^((q-2)/(q-1)), which is strictly better for all q > 2 (their bound only beats O(√T) for q ∈ (2,3), while the paper's bound does so for all q > 2). The paper also provides a matching lower bound (Theorem 9) for corrupted stochastic environments, confirming near-optimality of the upper bound up to logarithmic factors.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the universal online learning framework (MetaGrad, Maler) that provides adaptive regret bounds depending on the curvature of loss functions, and combines it with the line of work on exploiting feasible set curvature initiated by Huang et al. and Molinaro for OLO. The key departure is twofold: (i) it moves from OLO to full OCO, enabling simultaneous exploitation of both loss and feasible-set curvature without linearization, and (ii) it replaces the global strong convexity requirement with a local 'sphere-enclosed' condition at the optimal point, which is strictly weaker and applies to polytopes and non-smooth boundaries. For uniformly convex sets, it improves the O(T^{(q-2)/(q-1)}) bound of Kerdreux et al. to a matching O(T^{(q-2)/(2(q-1))}(ln T)^{q/(2(q-1))}) rate.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The sphere-enclosed condition is a clean, local, and verifiable geometric property that broadens the class of feasible sets for which logarithmic regret is achievable, and the robustness guarantee (O(√T) fallback in adversarial settings where FTL suffers Ω(T)) makes the approach practically safer. The matching lower bounds for both stochastic and corrupted stochastic environments confirm tightness. Open questions include extending the analysis to the more general stochastically extended adversary (SEA) model, establishing lower bounds for general sphere-enclosed sets (current lower bounds are only for ellipsoids), and understanding whether the sphere-enclosed condition can be verified or estimated from data in practice.
