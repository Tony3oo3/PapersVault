## Problem

Despite Adam's widespread use in deep learning, its implicit bias—the direction in which iterates converge on separable data—remains theoretically uncharacterized in the practical regime where the stability constant ε is negligible. Prior results either required a non-negligible ε (making Adam asymptotically equivalent to gradient descent and converging to the ℓ₂-margin) or studied simplified variants like sign gradient descent that lack Adam's momentum terms. This paper asks: what does full Adam (with momentum, without ε) converge to in direction on linearly separable data?

## Input / Output

The analysis takes a linearly separable binary classification dataset {(x_i, y_i)} with x_i ∈ ℝ^d and y_i ∈ {±1}, a diminishing learning rate schedule η_t = Θ(t^{-a}) for a ∈ (0,1], and standard Adam hyperparameters (β₁, β₂ with β₁ ≤ β₂). The output is the sequence of weight vectors w_t whose normalized direction w_t/||w_t||_∞ converges to the unique maximum ℓ∞-margin solution w*. The convergence rate of the margin gap depends on the decay exponent a: polynomial in t for a < 1 (e.g., O(t^{-a/2}) for a < 2/3) and logarithmic for a = 1.

## Method

The paper proves that Adam without the stability constant ε converges to the maximum ℓ∞-margin direction by establishing four key lemmas. First, it derives tight entry-wise characterizations of Adam's first and second moments m_t and v_t, showing they approximate the current gradient and its absolute value with error bounds scaled by G(w_t) (a quantity that vanishes at convergence). Second, it shows the training loss decreases monotonically after a fixed number of iterations, with a rate governed by γ·η_t·G(w_t). Third, it establishes a lower bound on the un-normalized margin min_i ⟨w_t, y_i x_i⟩ that grows linearly in the cumulative learning rate. Fourth, it proves an upper bound on ||w_t||_∞ that also grows linearly in the cumulative learning rate. Dividing the margin lower bound by the norm upper bound yields convergence of the normalized ℓ∞-margin to γ. The critical technical difficulty is handling the entry-wise ratio m_t/√v_t when individual gradient entries can be zero, requiring a careful case-split analysis over coordinates.

## Results

The main theoretical result (Theorem 4.5) shows that Adam on linearly separable data converges to the maximum ℓ∞-margin direction, with the normalized margin gap bounded by O((Ση_τ^{3/2})/(Ση_τ)) for exponential loss. For the specific schedule η_t = (t+2)^{-a}, the margin convergence rate is O(t^{-a/2}) for a < 2/3, O(t^{-1/3} log t) for a = 2/3, O(t^{1-a}) for 2/3 < a < 1, and O(1/log t) for a = 1. The loss converges at rate O(e^{-γt^{1-a}/(4(1-a))}) for a < 1, which is exponentially faster than GD's O(1/t) rate. Experiments on n=50, d=50 Gaussian data confirm that Adam (with and without ε=10^{-8}) converges to the ℓ∞-margin while GD and GDM converge to the ℓ₂-margin.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares Adam against GD (Soudry et al., JMLR 2018) and GDM (Wang et al., NeurIPS 2022) on the implicit bias question. Adam converges to the maximum ℓ∞-margin direction, while both GD and GDM converge to the maximum ℓ₂-margin direction — a fundamentally different solution. In terms of convergence speed, Adam achieves polynomial-time margin convergence (e.g., O(t^{-a/2}) for a < 2/3) whereas GD/GDM only achieve O(1/log t) margin convergence. The loss convergence also differs: Adam achieves an exponential rate O(e^{-γt^{1-a}/(4(1-a))}) for a < 1, compared to GD's O(1/t). Experiments in Figure 1 confirm these distinctions empirically: Adam's normalized ℓ∞-margin converges to γ while GD/GDM's does not, and vice versa for the ℓ₂-margin. Notably, the paper shows that adding the stability constant ε=10^{-8} does not change Adam's ℓ∞-margin behavior in practice, contradicting the theoretical prediction in [45] that ε makes Adam equivalent to GD.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the implicit bias analysis framework from gradient descent (which converges to the maximum ℓ₂-margin, Soudry et al. 2018) and sign gradient descent (which converges to the maximum ℓ∞-margin, Gunasekhar et al. 2018) to full Adam with momentum, in the critical setting where the stability constant ε is set to zero. It departs sharply from Wang et al. (2022), who showed that Adam *with* ε asymptotically mimics gradient descent and recovers the ℓ₂-margin; here, removing ε reveals a fundamentally different ℓ∞-margin bias. The analysis also subsumes and generalizes the AdamW result of Xie and Li (2024), which required a non-zero regularization parameter and could not be extended to vanilla Adam.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result resolves a long-standing open question about Adam's asymptotic behavior in the practically relevant regime where ε ≈ 10⁻⁸ is negligible, showing that Adam's implicit bias is genuinely distinct from gradient descent's (ℓ∞ vs. ℓ₂ margin) and that convergence to the optimal margin occurs in polynomial time for a broad class of learning rates, versus O(1/log t) for GD. Key limitations include the restriction to linear models, full-batch optimization, and separable data; the authors identify extension to homogeneous neural networks, stochastic Adam, and matching lower bounds as natural next steps. The sharp dichotomy—where a vanishingly small ε completely flips the asymptotic solution—has implications for understanding why Adam and GD produce different generalization behavior in practice.
