## Problem

Open-vocabulary multi-object tracking (OVMOT) lacks a comprehensive evaluation benchmark: the sole existing dataset OV-TAO-val contains only 330 categories (295 base, 35 novel), sparse 1 fps annotations, and at most 10 objects per frame, making it insufficient to assess a model's true open-vocabulary tracking capability. Additionally, the first OVMOT method OVTrack relies exclusively on appearance features for association, ignoring motion cues that are critical in classical MOT.

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes RGB video frames (360–1440p resolution, 5–30 fps annotation rate, up to 86 objects per frame) together with text embeddings generated by DetPro for open-vocabulary detection. It outputs per-frame bounding boxes with open-vocabulary class labels (1,048 categories split into 534 base and 514 novel) and persistent track IDs across frames. Evaluation uses the TETA metric (arithmetic mean of LocA, ClsA, AssA) computed separately for base and novel classes, with no training/test split since OVT-B serves purely as a test benchmark.

## Method

The paper contributes two components: the OVT-B benchmark (1,973 videos, 637,608 boxes, 1,048 categories sourced from seven existing datasets and unified under the TAO annotation protocol) and a baseline tracker OVTrack+. OVTrack+ augments the appearance-only OVTrack association with a Kalman-filter motion model: for each track, the predicted position yields an IoU distance to detections, which is combined with an appearance distance (weighted bi-softmax plus cosine similarity) via D = D_app·(1−w) + w·D_IoU (w=0.03). The resulting cost matrix is solved with the Hungarian algorithm, and matched track embeddings are updated via EMA (α=0.2). The detector is a two-stage ResNet50-FPN with the ViLD detection head and OVTrack tracking head, using pre-trained OVTrack weights and DetPro text prompts at inference.

## Results

On the newly introduced OVT-B benchmark, OVTrack+ achieves a TETA of 47.0 (LocA 62.0, AssA 67.7, ClsA 11.3), outperforming the prior open-vocabulary tracker OVTrack (TETA 46.1) and all motion-based MOT baselines (StrongSORT 24.8, ByteTrack 20.1, OC-SORT 16.0). On OV-TAO-val, OVTrack+ reaches TETA 38.4 versus OVTrack's 36.1, with the largest gain in AssA (40.8 vs. 37.3). Classification accuracy (ClsA) remains the weakest sub-task across all methods, with novel-class ClsA below 10 on OVT-B, highlighting the difficulty of open-vocabulary recognition.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OVTrack+ consistently outperforms the prior open-vocabulary tracker OVTrack on TETA, LocA, and AssA on both OVT-B (+0.9 TETA, +1.2 AssA) and OV-TAO-val (+2.3 TETA, +3.5 AssA), confirming the benefit of integrating Kalman-filter motion cues into appearance-based association. Against the three classical MOT baselines, OVTrack+ dominates on association accuracy: on OVT-B it achieves AssA 67.7 versus StrongSORT's 30.7, ByteTrack's 12.4, and OC-SORT's 4.3, reflecting the critical role of learned appearance embeddings for open-vocabulary tracking. However, OVTrack+ slightly trails OVTrack on ClsA (11.3 vs. 11.5 on OVT-B; 16.9 vs. 17.3 on OV-TAO-val), indicating that the motion model does not aid classification. Among the motion-only baselines, StrongSORT is the strongest (TETA 24.8 on OVT-B) but still lags OVTrack+ by over 22 TETA points, underscoring that appearance features remain essential for open-vocabulary tracking. On OV-TAO-val, the gap between OVTrack+ and motion-based methods is even wider (38.4 vs. 24.3 for OC-SORT), while on OVT-B the relative ordering of baselines shifts, suggesting the new benchmark captures different scene characteristics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on OVTrack (Li et al., CVPR 2023), the first open-vocabulary multi-object tracking method, which relied solely on appearance features (via QDTrack-style bi-softmax and cosine similarity) for object association. The proposed OVTrack+ extends this by integrating a Kalman-filter-based motion model into the association step, following the classical MOT recipe of combining appearance and motion cues (as in DeepSORT, StrongSORT). On the benchmark side, the paper departs from the OV-TAO-val/test datasets (derived from TAO and restricted to LVIS-overlapping categories) by constructing OVT-B from seven heterogeneous video datasets, expanding the vocabulary to 1,048 categories with novel classes deliberately placed outside LVIS, and increasing annotation density from 1 fps to 5–30 fps.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

OVT-B addresses a critical bottleneck: the sole prior OVT benchmark (OV-TAO-val) had only 330 categories, 1 fps annotations, and a small number of novel classes, making it insufficient for evaluating true open-vocabulary generalization. The paper reveals that novel-class classification accuracy remains extremely low (~9–10%), highlighting that OVT is far from solved and that the three sub-tasks (localization, classification, association) are poorly balanced by current methods. Open questions include how to improve novel-class recognition in tracking, whether joint detection-tracking architectures can handle the three-way task coupling, and whether a new evaluation metric is needed given the difficulty imbalance among sub-tasks.
