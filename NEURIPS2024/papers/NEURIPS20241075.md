## Problem

Prior contextual MNL bandit results are restricted to (generalized) linear value functions, and their regret bounds depend on a problem-dependent constant κ that can be exponentially large in the weight norm. No prior work considers a general value function class under realizability, despite the success of such frameworks in classic contextual bandits.

## Input / Output

At each round the learner receives a context x_t from an arbitrary space and a reward vector r_t ∈ [0,1]^N, then selects an assortment S_t ⊆ [N] of size at most K. The customer's purchase i_t is drawn from the MNL distribution μ(S_t, f*(x_t)), and the learner only observes i_t (not the underlying value vector), receiving reward r_{t,i_t}. The learner accesses the unknown function class F = {f: X → [0,1]^N} exclusively through regression oracles (offline or online), never directly optimizing over F.

## Method

The paper reduces contextual MNL bandits to log-loss regression (rather than squared loss) and proposes two exploration strategies: a computationally efficient ε-greedy uniform exploration over singletons, and a log-barrier regularized policy that maximizes expected reward minus a penalty on item selection probabilities, yielding a low-regret-high-dispersion guarantee. A key technical ingredient is a 'reverse Lipschitzness' lemma showing the MNL mapping from value vectors to purchase distributions has a bounded minimum singular value, so controlling log-loss regression error suffices to bound value estimation error. In the adversarial setting, the same log-barrier strategy is shown to achieve a small Decision-Estimation Coefficient (DEC) without being the exact DEC minimizer, and a Feel-Good Thompson Sampling variant achieves the best √T regret with only logarithmic dependence on the weight norm B. All results eliminate the exponential κ-dependence present in prior linear-case bounds.

## Results

For the linear value function class with d-dimensional contexts, N items, and size-K assortments, the paper achieves five new regret bounds. The best result (Corollary 4.8, Feel-Good Thompson Sampling) gives O~(K²√(dNT)) regret with only logarithmic dependence on the weight norm B, a potential doubly-exponential improvement over prior work's exp(2B) dependence. The computationally efficient algorithms achieve O~(K²√(dBNT)) (stochastic) and O(K²√(NB)·T^(3/4)) (adversarial) regret, both dimension-free in d for the adversarial case. All results eliminate the problem-dependent constant κ = exp(2B) that appears in every prior bound.

## Baselines & Comparisons

All three prior baselines (Perivier & Goyal [23], Chen et al. [7], Oh & Iyengar [20]) depend on a problem-dependent constant κ that equals exp(2B) in the worst case, while none of the paper's five new results depend on κ at all. The paper's best bound (Corollary 4.8) achieves O~(K²√(dNT)) with only log(B) dependence, representing a potential doubly-exponential improvement over the exp(2B) factor in prior work. The paper is the first to handle completely adversarial context-reward pairs (both x_t and r_t adversarial), a setting none of the three baselines addresses. For the stochastic x_t / adversarial r_t setting, the paper's O~(K²√(dBNT)) bound (Corollary 3.8) matches the optimal √T dependence and removes the κ⁴ or κ² factors present in [7] and [20]. The dimension-free adversarial bound O(K²√(NB)·T^(3/4)) (Corollary 4.7) has no dependence on d at all, unlike all prior results which scale with d or d².

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends contextual MNL bandits from the (generalized) linear value function setting studied by Cheung & Simchi-Levi [8], Chen et al. [7], Oh & Iyengar [20], and Perivier & Goyal [23] to a general value function class under a realizability assumption. It adapts the offline/online regression oracle frameworks from Simchi-Levi & Xu [25] and Foster & Rakhlin [10], Foster & Krishnamurthy [11] to the MNL setting, replacing squared loss with log loss to match the MNL likelihood structure. The DEC framework of Foster et al. [14] is used for the adversarial setting, and the Feel-Good Thompson Sampling idea of Zhang [30] is extended to MNL bandits as a third algorithmic approach.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper removes the exponentially large dependence on the weight-norm bound B (via the constant κ = exp(2B)) that plagued all prior linear MNL bandit results, and is the first to handle fully adversarial context-reward pairs. A key open question is whether a computationally efficient algorithm achieving √T regret without exponential B-dependence exists, which the authors identify as a major future direction. The poly(K,N) dependence in the regret bounds also remains suboptimal and may require new techniques to improve.
