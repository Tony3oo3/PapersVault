## Problem

While substantial effort has been devoted to compressing text tokens in LLMs for efficient inference, the redundancy of visual tokens in multi-modal LLMs (MLLMs) has remained largely unexplored. This paper asks whether visual tokens can be safely reduced and, if so, how to integrate such compression into the training process itself—not just inference—without degrading multi-modal performance.

## Input / Output

The model consumes RGB images at 336×336 resolution (yielding 576 visual tokens via a frozen CLIP ViT-L/14 encoder) paired with multi-turn text instructions, or 8 video frames with a text question for the video extension. The Visual Context Compressor is inserted inside the LLM's Transformer stack (not at the vision encoder), so the input to the LLM still contains full-resolution visual tokens; compression occurs at a chosen layer K, reducing the sequence length for all subsequent layers. The final output is a standard autoregressive text answer, with the compressed token count (e.g., 7,848 vs. 18,432 for the baseline) determining inference TFLOPs and latency.

## Method

The Visual Context Compressor is a parameter-free average pooler with stride S applied to visual hidden states at Transformer layer K, projecting L tokens to L/S tokens; despite its simplicity, it outperforms attention-based (FastV, VCC) and parametric (Q-Former) compressors during training because in-training attention scores are unstable and the deeper layers attend less to visual tokens anyway. To prevent information loss under high compression ratios, LLaVolta introduces a staged training schedule: Stage I uses heavy compression (e.g., layer 2, stride 8, CR≈557%) for the first third of training, Stage II shifts to lighter compression in deeper layers (e.g., layer 16, stride 8, CR≈178%), and Stage III trains with no or subtle compression to align with inference. The 'last-stage compression' variant keeps a mild compressor active at inference (layer 16, stride 4, CR≈160%), achieving the best average benchmark score (62.1%) while cutting training time by 16% and inference latency by 24% relative to the uncompressed LLaVA-1.5-7B baseline.

## Results

On 13 image-language benchmarks, the best LLaVolta scheme (three-stage last-stage compression) achieves 61.9% average performance, matching or slightly exceeding the uncompressed LLaVA-1.5-7B baseline (61.8%), while reducing training time from 15.3h to 12.2h (~20%) and inference cost from 8.26 to 5.47 TFLOPs (~34%). The four-stage 'deeper then wider' scheme reaches 61.9% average with 170% compression ratio and 12.8h training. On video-language tasks (MSVD-QA, MSRVTT-QA, ActivityNet-QA), LLaVolta applied to VideoLLaVA achieves competitive scores (e.g., 3.74/69.8 on MSVD-QA) while reducing training time by 9% (40.7h → 37.1h).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the uncompressed LLaVA-1.5-7B baseline, LLaVolta (last-stage compression) matches average performance (61.9% vs 61.8%) while cutting training time by 20% (12.2h vs 15.3h) and inference TFLOPs by 34% (5.47 vs 8.26). Among compression methods (Table 3), average pooling dramatically outperforms attention-based alternatives during training: it achieves 60.4% average at 556% CR versus 57.0% (FastV) and 58.5% (VCC), because attention scores are unstable during training. Against the parametric Q-Former (105M params, 1800% CR), the non-parametric average pooler (0 params, 2156% CR) achieves higher average performance (57.8% vs 56.2%) with less training time (9.2h vs 10.4h). On video-language tasks, LLaVolta applied to VideoLLaVA matches or slightly exceeds the no-compression baseline (e.g., 3.74 vs 3.69 MSVD-QA score) while reducing training time by 9%. Scaling to LLaVA-13B, the four-stage scheme achieves 64.0% average (vs 63.9% baseline) with 16% training time savings (17.6h vs 21.1h), and on Mini-Gemini (MGM-2B) it reduces training time by 18% (14.8h vs 18.1h) with comparable performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the LLaVA/LLaVA-1.5 framework and extends it to VideoLLaVA, inserting a Visual Context Compressor (a simple average pooler) into the LLM's Transformer layers to reduce visual token count. It departs from prior token-compression work (FastV, VCC, Pyramid-BERT, Funnel-Transformer) in two key ways: (1) it targets visual tokens in MLLMs rather than text tokens in LLMs, and (2) it designs compression for training efficiency rather than inference-only acceleration. The staged training scheme (LLaVolta) draws conceptual inspiration from staged training for LLMs (Shen et al.) but applies it to progressively reducing visual compression ratio across training stages, a formulation not previously explored.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to systematically study visual token redundancy in MLLMs and to design an efficient training scheme around it, reducing training cost by 16% and inference latency by 24% while matching or exceeding baseline performance across 13+ benchmarks. The practical simplicity of the approach (a zero-parameter average pooler at a fixed layer) makes it easy to adopt in existing MLLM pipelines. Open questions include whether the optimal compression layer and stride generalize across architectures beyond the LLaVA family, whether the staged scheme extends to pretraining (not just instruction tuning), and whether the redundancy findings hold for higher-resolution or longer-context visual inputs where token counts are substantially larger.
