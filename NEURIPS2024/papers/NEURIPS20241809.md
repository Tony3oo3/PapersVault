## Problem

Generating animated 3D assets (4D content) from monocular video remains slow and fragile: optimization-based score distillation methods require hours per prompt and are sensitive to prompts, while multiview video reconstruction approaches demand expensive multi-camera data. No feed-forward model existed that could reconstruct a temporally consistent 4D representation from a single video in a single forward pass.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241809/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a monocular video of T frames (T=8 during training, T=16 at inference) at 256×256 resolution, plus four multiview images generated by ImageDream from the first frame (aligned via an LGM-based azimuth correction). The camera is assumed static; only the object moves. Output is a sequence of T sets of 3D Gaussians (65,536 per frame at 128×128×4), rendered at 512×512 for supervision. A separate 4D interpolation model upsamples the output to 3× the input framerate by predicting intermediate Gaussian sets between consecutive frames.

## Method

L4GM extends the pretrained 3D LGM (an asymmetric U-Net with cross-view self-attention) by inserting temporal self-attention layers after each cross-view attention block, where the view axis is reshaped into the batch dimension so attention is computed independently across time for each viewpoint. The key architectural insight is that only the initial timestep requires actual multiview input; the remaining T−1 frames use replicated copies of the first-frame multiview images, and temporal attention propagates 3D information forward. Training uses a per-timestep multiview rendering loss (LPIPS + MSE on RGB and segmentation masks) over both input and supervision camera poses, on the newly curated Objaverse-4D dataset of 12M rendered videos. A 4D interpolation model is fine-tuned on top to produce intermediate Gaussians, and autoregressive chunked processing extends reconstruction to arbitrarily long videos by rendering the last Gaussian set as the next chunk's multiview input.

## Results

On the Consistent4D video-to-4D benchmark, L4GM achieves LPIPS 0.12, CLIP similarity 0.94, and FVD 691.87, outperforming all baselines on every reported metric. Inference takes only 3 seconds per video, making it 100–1,000× faster than optimization-based methods (6 min to 2 hr). In a user study on 24 diverse videos (from Sora, Veo, Emu, and ActivityNet), L4GM is preferred over DG4D, STAG4D, and OpenLRM on all five evaluation axes, with 65.4% preference over DG4D on overall quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

L4GM outperforms all nine compared baselines on every available metric. Against the closest feed-forward competitor Efficient4D [33], L4GM improves LPIPS from 0.14 to 0.12 and CLIP from 0.92 to 0.94 while being 120× faster (3 s vs. 6 min). Against optimization-based methods, the speedup is dramatic: Consistent4D [21] takes 2 hours and yields LPIPS 0.16 / FVD 1133.44, while STAG4D [67] takes 1 hour with LPIPS 0.13 / FVD 992.21, and DG4D [39] takes 10 minutes with LPIPS 0.16. The older NeRF-based baselines D-NeRF [36] and K-Planes [13] are far behind, with mean LPIPS of 0.51 and 0.38 respectively versus L4GM's 0.12. In the user study, L4GM is preferred over OpenLRM [19] (which applies a 3D reconstruction model per-frame) on overall quality (57.9% vs. 33.3%), 3D appearance (58.8% vs. 31.7%), and motion realism (54.2% vs. 35.0%), confirming that the temporal self-attention and 4D representation provide meaningful gains over frame-independent 3D reconstruction.

## Relation to Prior Work

L4GM extends the 3D Large Reconstruction Model (LRM) paradigm—specifically LGM, a pretrained U-Net that outputs 3D Gaussians from multiview images—into the 4D domain by inserting temporal self-attention layers between the existing cross-view attention blocks. It departs from the dominant optimization-based 4D reconstruction approaches (score distillation, per-scene fitting) by training a single feed-forward model on a new large-scale synthetic dataset (Objaverse-4D, 12M multiview videos). A key conceptual departure from prior 4D methods is the use of only a single set of multiview images at the initial timestep (generated via ImageDream) rather than requiring full multiview video input, with temporal consistency learned through the added attention layers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

L4GM establishes that feed-forward 4D reconstruction is viable at interactive speeds (~3 seconds), making animated 3D asset generation from video practical for the first time and opening the door to 4D content editing tooling. The Objaverse-4D dataset (44K objects, 110K animations, 12M videos) is a significant resource for the community. However, the model still struggles with motion ambiguity, multi-object occlusions, and ego-centric viewpoints, and the authors note that human-in-the-loop 4D editing capabilities remain a major open challenge for professional use.
