## Problem

In non-stationary online convex optimisation, switching regret (the sum of static regrets over segments of an unknown segmentation) is the appropriate performance measure. Prior tracking algorithms achieved a switching regret of O(Σ√(Λ_k)·ln(T)), a factor of O(√(ln(T))) worse than the O(Σ√(Λ_k)) bound achievable when the segmentation is known a-priori. This paper closes that gap, achieving the asymptotically optimal switching regret for every possible segmentation simultaneously.

## Input / Output

On each trial the algorithm receives a convex loss function ℓ_t: X → [0,1] whose sub-gradients lie in a known bounded set G ⊂ ℝ^d, and must output an action x_t ∈ X (a bounded convex set). RESET is a parameter-free meta-algorithm: it wraps any base algorithm that accepts a horizon parameter Λ and guarantees O(√Λ) static regret (e.g., online gradient descent or Hedge), and the only user-specified quantity is the total time horizon T (assumed a power of two). The per-trial time and space complexity are O(ξ·ln(T)) and O(ξ′·ln(T)) respectively, where ξ and ξ′ are those of the base algorithm.

## Method

RESET (Recursion over Segment Tree) maintains τ+1 = log₂(T)+1 levels, where level i hosts an instance of the base algorithm with parameter 2^i that resets every 2^i trials, plus a mixing weight μ_t^i updated by a two-expert Hedge instance. On each trial, the output action x_t is built recursively: starting from the level-0 base action, each level i forms a 'propagating action' z_t^i as the convex combination μ_t^i·w_t^i + (1−μ_t^i)·z_t^{i−1}, and the final output is z_t^τ. The mixing weight update uses a learning rate scaled by 1/2^i (the segment length of that level), which is the critical design choice that yields the optimal bound. The analysis maps the algorithm onto a balanced binary segment tree: vertices fully contained within a single segment of the chosen segmentation ('fundamental vertices') anchor the regret via the base algorithm's guarantee, and an induction up the tree accumulates O(√(2 ln(2)·2^h)) terms per level, which telescope to the O(Σ√(Λ_k)) switching regret. The same framework, with OGD as the base algorithm, yields an adaptive dynamic regret bound of O(Σ√((1+P_k)·Λ_k)) that tracks the local path length of the comparator in each segment.

## Results

RESET achieves the asymptotically optimal switching regret of O(Σ_k √Λ_k) for every possible segmentation simultaneously, matching the lower bound of Theorem 2.1 and removing the O(√ln T) gap that all prior tracking algorithms had. For dynamic regret, RESET with online gradient descent achieves O(Σ_k √((1+P_k)Λ_k)), which is adaptive to variations in the rate of change of the comparator sequence (improving on ADER's O(√((1+P)T))). The algorithm is parameter-free with O(ln T) per-trial time and space complexity. For prediction with expert advice, RESET+Hedge yields the novel O(Σ_k min(Λ_k, √(ln N · Λ_k))) switching regret bound.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical baselines; all comparisons are between regret bounds. RESET improves on the prior best switching regret bound of O(Σ_k √Λ_k · ln T) (from strongly adaptive algorithms [4,8,9]) by removing the ln T factor entirely, achieving the minimax-optimal O(Σ_k √Λ_k). For dynamic regret, RESET improves on ADER [10] which gives O(√((1+P)T)): RESET's bound O(Σ_k √((1+P_k)Λ_k)) is strictly tighter when the comparator's rate of change is non-uniform across the horizon. Unlike SAOL [4] and its improvements [8], RESET does not bound the static regret on any individual segment (so it is not strongly adaptive), but this is the trade-off that enables the optimal switching regret. For the expert-advice special case, RESET+Hedge achieves a novel bound that is asymptotically optimal for every N simultaneously, whereas prior methods could not achieve this uniformity.

## Relation to Prior Work

RESET is a meta-algorithm that builds directly on the Hedge algorithm (Freund & Schapire, 1997) for its mixing-weight updates and on online gradient descent (Zinkevich, 2003) as a base learner for the dynamic regret result. It departs from the strongly adaptive online learning line (SAOL, Daniely et al. 2015; Jun et al. 2016) by using a segment-tree structure with recursive convex combinations rather than directly updating per-segment coefficients, which is the key structural difference that eliminates the O(√ln T) gap. The dynamic regret result extends ADER (Zhang et al., 2018) by being adaptive to local variation in comparator path length rather than using a single global path-length parameter.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Closing the O(√ln T) gap between the best simultaneous switching-regret bound and the per-segmentation optimal bound is a clean resolution of a long-standing open problem in non-stationary online learning, and the segment-tree meta-algorithm is parameter-free with only O(log T) overhead. The dynamic regret bound, being adaptive to local rate-of-change, is strictly stronger than ADER's global path-length bound. Open questions include whether the approach extends to non-convex settings, whether the constants can be tightened, and whether the segment-tree structure can be adapted to other meta-learning or adaptive-optimization contexts beyond OCO.
