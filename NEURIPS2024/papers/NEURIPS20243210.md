## Problem

Causal representation learning requires Ω(d_z) interventional datasets to recover all latent generative factors, which is infeasible when the latent dimension is large (e.g., natural images or text). This paper asks whether a smaller set of human-interpretable concepts can be provably identified from conditional data alone, without recovering the full generative model or performing interventions on every latent factor.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243210/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an observational dataset X^0 = f(Z^0) from an arbitrary base distribution p, plus m concept-conditional datasets X^e where data is filtered by noisy Gaussian observations of specific concept valuations. The output is the set of linear maps A^e defining each concept (up to permutation, scaling, and a global linear reparametrization T) and the per-datapoint concept valuations A^e f^{-1}(x). Crucially, only n+2 environments are needed where n is the number of atomic concepts of interest, regardless of the latent dimension d_z.

## Method

Concepts are defined geometrically as affine subspaces AZ = b in the latent space, grounded in the empirically supported linear representation hypothesis. The main theoretical result (Theorem 1) proves that n linearly independent atomic concepts are identifiable from n+2 concept-conditional distributions with Gaussian noise filtering, under two mild environment-diversity assumptions (rank condition on the environment-concept matrix and pairwise separability of atoms). The proof proceeds by showing the log-density differences between environments encode the environment-concept matrix M and environment-valuation matrix B, which can be recovered from identifiable submanifold dimensions and pairwise valuation differences. For practical learning, a contrastive algorithm trains a neural encoder h^θ with a final layer parametrized to match the theoretical log-odds form (quadratic + linear in concept valuations), encouraging the network to discover the concept directions end-to-end via cross-entropy loss.

## Results

On synthetic data, the contrastive learning algorithm achieves R² of 0.94–0.99 and MCC of 0.86–0.98 across linear and nonlinear mixing functions with latent dimensions d_z up to 8 and observed dimensions d_x up to 10, using only n+1 concept-conditional datasets (where n is the number of atomic concepts, well below d_z). On the 3d-Shapes dataset embedded via CLIP, learned concept valuations concentrate tightly around the predicted affine hyperplanes, confirming the geometric concept structure. For LLM alignment, replacing steering vectors with steering matrices (motivated by the framework) improves TruthfulQA accuracy on LLaMA over the original Inference-Time Intervention baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper explicitly states there are no direct empirical baselines because the setting (identifying a small number of concepts from conditional data without interventions) is novel. The closest theoretical comparison is to the iVAE framework of Khemakhem et al. [53], which requires 2n+1 environments (rank-2n condition on the combined environment matrix) versus the n+2 environments needed here under the weaker Assumption 4. The authors note their R² and MCC metrics are 'comparable to and often surpass' what is typically reported in highly nonlinear ICA settings (citing Willetts & Paige [132] and Buchholz et al. [14]). For the LLM alignment experiment, the proposed steering-matrix approach improves upon the original Inference-Time Intervention (ITI) technique of Li et al. [66] on TruthfulQA, though exact accuracy numbers are deferred to the appendix. The CLIP experiments serve as qualitative validation rather than a head-to-head comparison, showing that concept valuations cluster around the predicted hyperplanes as Theorem 1 predicts.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from causal representation learning (CRL), which requires Ω(d_z) interventional datasets to recover all generative factors, by replacing interventions with conditioning and targeting only a small subset of n linear concepts (n << d_z). Theoretically, it builds directly on the iVAE framework of Khemakhem et al. [53], adapting their proof techniques to a setting where concepts are defined as affine subspaces rather than individual latent factors, and improves the environment requirement from 2n+1 to n+2. Empirically, it is grounded in the extensive linear representation hypothesis literature showing that foundation models encode interpretable concepts as linear projections of their embeddings, and it extends the Inference-Time Intervention technique [66] from steering vectors to steering matrices for LLM alignment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This provides the first rigorous identifiability theory for concept-based representation learning, bridging the CRL and XAI/concept-discovery communities that have largely developed in parallel. The practical implication is significant: learning n specific concepts requires only n+2 conditional datasets rather than d_z interventional ones, making concept recovery feasible in high-dimensional settings where full causal recovery is intractable. Key limitations include the Gaussian noise assumption, the requirement that concepts be linear in the latent space, and the need to know n in advance. Open questions include whether the n+2 bound is tight, whether non-Gaussian filtering distributions preserve identifiability, and how the framework extends to non-linear or compositional concepts.
