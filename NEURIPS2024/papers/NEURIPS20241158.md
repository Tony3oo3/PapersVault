## Problem

Concept-erased diffusion models, produced by existing machine unlearning methods (ESD, FMN, UCE, etc.), remain vulnerable to adversarial prompt attacks that regenerate the very content targeted for erasure. No prior work has systematically integrated adversarial training into DM unlearning to close this robustness gap while preserving image generation quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241158/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained latent diffusion model (default SD v1.4), a target concept prompt to erase, and a curated retain set of 243 LLM-filtered prompts sourced from COCO or ImageNet. It produces a robustified text encoder that can be plugged into multiple DM architectures (SD v1.4, SD v1.5, DreamShaper, Protogen) without retraining, yielding generated images that resist adversarial prompt jailbreaking while maintaining FID and CLIP scores close to the base model.

## Method

AdvUnlearn formulates robust concept erasure as a bi-level optimization: the upper level minimizes an ESD unlearning loss augmented with a utility-retaining regularization term that penalizes deviation from the original model on retain prompts, while the lower level generates adversarial prompts via gradient-based optimization in the token embedding space (30-step or one-step FGSM). The key architectural insight is that optimizing the text encoder rather than the UNet yields substantially better robustness (ASR drops from 64.79% to 21.13% for nudity) at comparable FID, and the resulting text encoder transfers plug-and-play across different DMs. A utility-retaining regularization with weight γ (0.3 for nudity, 0.5 for style/object) on a batch of 5 retain prompts per iteration prevents the image quality degradation that naive adversarial training causes. The fast AT variant replaces the 30-step lower-level attack with a single FGSM step, reducing per-iteration training time from 78.57s to 12.13s with modest robustness loss.

## Results

For nudity unlearning on SD v1.4, AdvUnlearn achieves 21.13% ASR against UnlearnDiffAtk (vs. 73.24% for ESD and 100% for the base model) while maintaining FID of 19.34 and CLIP score of 0.290, closely matching the base model's utility (FID 16.7, CLIP 0.311). For Van Gogh style unlearning, AdvUnlearn reduces ASR to just 2% (vs. 36% for ESD) with FID 16.96, nearly identical to the base model. For Church object unlearning, AdvUnlearn achieves 6% ASR (tied with ScissorHands) but with FID 18.06 versus SH's 68.02, demonstrating a far better robustness-utility tradeoff.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AdvUnlearn decisively outperforms all standard unlearning baselines (FMN, SPM, UCE, ESD) on ASR for nudity unlearning, reducing attack success rate from 73–98% down to 21.13%, while keeping FID within ~1.6 of the base model. The closest competitor is SalUn, which achieves a lower ASR (11.27%) but at a severe utility cost (FID 33.62 vs. 19.34, CLIP 0.287 vs. 0.290), making AdvUnlearn the only method that balances both robustness and generation quality. For style unlearning, AdvUnlearn achieves 2% ASR versus ESD's 36% and FMN's 52%, with FID (16.96) and CLIP (0.308) nearly matching the unmodified base model. For object unlearning (Church), AdvUnlearn ties ScissorHands on ASR (6%) but dramatically outperforms it on FID (18.06 vs. 68.02) and CLIP (0.305 vs. 0.277), confirming that SH's robustness comes at an unacceptable utility cost. Across all three task categories, AdvUnlearn consistently occupies the Pareto-optimal region of the robustness-utility tradeoff.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the concept erasure line of work (ESD, FMN, UCE, SalUn, SPM) by integrating adversarial training into the unlearning objective, formulating robust concept erasure as a bi-level optimization problem. It departs from prior DM unlearning methods that exclusively optimize the UNet, identifying the text encoder as a more effective and efficient module for robustification. The utility-retaining regularization draws on the AT literature showing that unlabeled data improves the robustness-accuracy tradeoff (Carmon et al., 2019), adapted here to a curated retain prompt set. The fast AT variant leverages FGSM-based one-step attack generation from the bi-level optimization perspective of Zhang et al. (ICML 2022), distinguishing it from Receler [30] which applies AT to UNet adapters rather than the full unlearning pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first systematic treatment of adversarial robustness in DM unlearning, addressing a vulnerability that prior concept-erasing methods entirely overlooked. The plug-and-play text encoder (trained once on SD v1.4, transferable to SD v1.5, DreamShaper, and Protogen) offers a practical deployment advantage for safety-critical applications. Key limitations include the computational overhead of multi-step AT (78.57s/iteration vs. 12.13s for fast AT), the performance gap between fast and standard AT, and evaluation primarily on SD v1.4-scale models. Open questions include whether the text-encoder-centric approach generalizes to next-generation architectures (e.g., DiT-based models), how robustness scales with model size, and whether the utility-retaining regularization can be made more adaptive.
