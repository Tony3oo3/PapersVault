## Problem

Scientific papers are inherently multimodal, combining text, equations, figures, and tables, yet existing question-answering datasets for these components are not conversational in nature and do not capture the depth of situated interaction that research scientists require. No prior dataset provides grounded, conversational question-answer pairs specifically tied to equations, figures, and tables within scientific documents, leaving a gap for developing situated and multimodal interactive conversation (SIMMC) methods over scientific content.

## Input / Output

The system takes a reviewer question (typically 23–26 tokens) and pairs it with multimodal context extracted from arXiv LaTeX sources: equations and tables are serialized as raw LaTeX strings, figures are converted to PNG images with their captions, and additional context includes the immediately preceding/following paragraphs and all in-document referring text via \ref lookups. Because ordinal references in reviews (e.g., 'Equation 3') may not align with LaTeX source positions across paper versions, the model receives a weakly-grounded subset—neighboring equations or tables (i−1, i, i+1)—rather than a single exact match. The output is a free-form conversational answer (typically 80–100 tokens) in the style of author rebuttals.

## Method

The paper's core contribution is a seven-step scalable pipeline that extracts question-answer pairs from OpenReview reviews and rebuttals (NeurIPS/ICLR 2020–2023) using regex filtering, LLM-based extraction (LLaMA-2-70B-Chat), and Amazon Mechanical Turk quality control, then links them to corresponding LaTeX sources on arXiv. For baselines, the authors evaluate zero-shot prompting with LLaMA-2-70B and parameter-efficient fine-tuning with QLoRA (rank 64, α=16) on LLaMA-2-7B, independently per modality. A key design choice is the 'neighboring' grounding strategy: rather than providing all equations or tables from a paper (which exceeds context limits), the model receives only the target equation/table plus its immediate ordinal neighbors, reconciling the mismatch between reviewer ordinal references and LaTeX source ordering. Experiments show that neighboring grounding yields up to 2× improvement over full-document grounding on ROUGE, METEOR, and BERTScore, and that for equations the equation itself is the most informative context, while for tables and figures the question alone often suffices after fine-tuning.

## Results

Zero-shot LLAMA-2-70B achieves ROUGE-1 scores of 0.194 (EQNS), 0.192 (TBLS), and 0.185 (FIGS) using question-only input, with BERTScore consistently around 0.83. Fine-tuning LLAMA-2-7B with QLoRA substantially improves performance, reaching ROUGE-1 of 0.317 (EQNS with Q+Equation), 0.315 (TBLS with Q only), and 0.329 (FIGS with Q only), with BERTScore in the 0.86–0.87 range. A key finding is that providing neighboring (±1) equations/tables outperforms providing all equations/tables by up to 2× on ROUGE and METEOR, while for tables and figures, the question alone often outperforms adding the multimodal content.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper evaluates two paradigms—zero-shot prompting (LLAMA-2-70B) and parameter-efficient fine-tuning (LLAMA-2-7B with QLoRA)—across four context settings (question only, question + equation/table/figure, question + surrounding context, question + referring text). Fine-tuning consistently outperforms zero-shot by approximately 12–14 points on ROUGE-1 across all three splits (e.g., 0.317 vs. 0.194 on EQNS). For equations, adding the neighboring equation to the question yields the best ROUGE-1 (0.317) and BERTScore (0.861), outperforming question-only (0.309) and context-only (0.297). In contrast, for tables and figures, the question alone achieves the best ROUGE-1 (0.315 and 0.329 respectively), suggesting that authors' answers to table/figure questions rely more on general knowledge than on the specific table or figure content. The neighboring context setting (±1 equation/table) dramatically outperforms the all-content setting, with up to 2× improvement in ROUGE and METEOR, indicating that context-length constraints make full-document grounding counterproductive for current LLMs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of conversational QA and scientific document understanding. Prior work addresses individual components—tabular QA (TAT-QA, FeTaQA, HiTab), figure QA (VQA, SciCap), and equation modeling (MathGPT, Nougat)—but none combine conversational question-answering with multimodal grounding in scientific paper components (equations, figures, tables) simultaneously. The data collection pipeline, which links OpenReview peer-review interactions with arXiv LaTeX sources, is a novel contribution that repurposes existing public infrastructure (OpenReview API, arXiv API) in a way not previously exploited for dataset construction. The baseline approaches draw on standard LLM prompting and QLoRA fine-tuning, positioning the work primarily as a dataset and benchmark rather than a methodological advance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

cPAPERS provides the first large-scale (5,030 QA pairs, 2,350 papers) conversational QA benchmark grounded in the multimodal components of scientific papers, addressing a gap where prior datasets were either non-conversational or not situated in scientific documents. The key limitation—mismatched ordinal references between OpenReview comments and arXiv LaTeX versions due to paper revisions—introduces inherent noise that complicates evaluation and may understate true model capability. The finding that even LLAMA-2-70B achieves ROUGE-1 scores below 0.20 on zero-shot and that 'neighboring' context outperforms 'all' context suggests the task remains substantially unsolved and that naive context augmentation is counterproductive. Open questions include whether the dataset's reliance on a single source (OpenReview) limits generalizability, and whether the weakly-grounded evaluation protocol (providing all equations/tables rather than the specific one referenced) adequately measures true multimodal understanding.
