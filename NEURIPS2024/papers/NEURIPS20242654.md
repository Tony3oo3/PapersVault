## Problem

Supervised fine-tuning (SFT) for specialized domains (mathematics, medicine, code) requires large, domain-specific datasets, but existing data selection methods rely on feature representations (perplexity, hidden states, confidence) from reference models that are both less informative when SFT data deviates from the pretraining distribution and computationally prohibitive to generate for large target models. This paper addresses the gap of scalable, effective data selection for SFT that does not require training or extracting features from a large reference model.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a training dataset of prompt-response pairs (e.g., 262K MathInstruct examples) and a data budget B specifying how many examples to retain. A small proxy model (as small as 70M parameters, ~100× smaller than the 7B target) is trained on the full or a subset of the data, and per-example loss trajectories are recorded at fixed intervals (every 500 iterations). The output is a subset S ⊆ D_train with |S| ≤ B, selected by balanced random sampling across K-means clusters of loss trajectories, with clustering performed independently per data source to handle multi-source imbalanced datasets.

## Method

S2L exploits the empirical finding that training dynamics (loss trajectories) are consistent across differently sized models of the same family. It trains a small proxy model, records each example's loss at T fixed time points to form a trajectory vector, clusters these vectors via K-means (K=100, Euclidean distance), and then samples an equal number of examples uniformly at random from each cluster, prioritizing smaller clusters to ensure balanced coverage. The theoretical contribution proves that examples with similar proxy-model loss trajectories have bounded gradient differences during target-model training (Theorem 4.1), and that incremental gradient methods on the S2L-selected subset converge to a neighborhood of the full-data optimum with an explicit error bound (Corollary 4.2). This avoids the need for expensive hidden-state extraction or perplexity computation from large models, reducing the reference-model cost proportionally to the proxy model's size.

## Results

On MathInstruct with Phi-2 (2.7B), S2L selects 50K examples (19% of the full 262K dataset) and achieves 55.8% in-domain average accuracy (GSM8K 69.1%, MATH 32.6%, NumGLUE 65.7%) and 57.3% out-of-domain average, surpassing full-dataset training (55.1% / 57.7%) on in-domain metrics. With only 30K examples (11% of the dataset), S2L already exceeds full-dataset performance on Pythia-410M. On the MATH benchmark, S2L attains 32.7% accuracy with 50K data, improving pretrained Phi-2 by 16.6 percentage points. For clinical text summarization on MIMIC-III, S2L-selected 30K examples outperform training on the full 61.5K dataset on ROUGE-L and BERTScore.

## Baselines & Comparisons

S2L outperforms all four one-shot baselines (Facility Locations, Least Confidence, Middle Perplexity, High Learnability) and Random Sampling across all six evaluation datasets, with an average margin of 4.7% over the runner-up (Facility Locations) at the 50K budget. Facility Locations is the closest competitor, achieving 53.3% in-domain and 54.5% out-of-domain average versus S2L's 55.8% and 57.3%, but it lags most on GSM8K (66.2 vs. 69.1) and SVAMP (74.4 vs. 79.6). High Learnability and Middle Perplexity are substantially weaker, with in-domain averages of 48.9% and 50.0% respectively. Against full-dataset training (262K examples), S2L with only 50K examples matches or exceeds performance on in-domain metrics (55.8% vs. 55.1%) while using 81% less data, though full training retains a slight edge on SimulEq (44.2% vs. 40.1%). The paper also reports that at the 30K budget, all SOTA baselines except S2L fail to beat Random Sampling, underscoring S2L's advantage under tight data constraints.

## Relation to Prior Work

S2L builds on the empirical observation from Xia et al. (2023) that training dynamics are consistent across differently sized models, repurposing this cross-scale consistency for data selection rather than analysis. It departs from the dominant paradigm of data selection methods that extract static feature representations (hidden states, perplexity, confidence) from a reference model, instead using dynamic loss trajectories recorded during proxy model training. The clustering-then-uniform-sampling strategy connects to coreset selection literature but applies it to trajectory space rather than embedding space, and the balanced per-cluster sampling addresses the multi-source imbalance problem that prior one-shot methods (Facility Locations, EL2N, Middle Perplexity) do not explicitly handle.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: using a 70M proxy model to guide data selection for a 7B target model (100× scale gap) across different model families (Pythia→Phi-2, Llama-2) dramatically reduces the computational cost of data selection, which is a major bottleneck for specialized-domain SFT. The theoretical guarantee (bounded gradient error and convergence to a neighborhood of the optimal solution) provides a principled foundation that most heuristic data selection methods lack. However, the method is validated only on two domains (math, clinical summarization) and models up to 7B parameters, with a fixed training schedule; whether the 100× proxy-to-target scale gap holds for larger models or more heterogeneous domains remains an open question. The finding that all prior SOTA selection algorithms collapse to random-baseline performance at small data budgets (30K) while S2L remains effective highlights a practical gap that practitioners face when compute is constrained.
