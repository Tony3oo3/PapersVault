## Problem

Existing fMRI-based visual reconstruction methods rely on subject-specific models trained over multiple trials, flatten 3D brain volumes into 1D vectors (losing critical spatial structure), and lack semantic interpretability. These limitations prevent cross-subject generalization, increase data collection costs, and block integration with Large Language Models for natural-language interaction with brain signals.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243112/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes raw fMRI BOLD signals (3D volumes resized to 83×104×81 via trilinear interpolation, then segmented into 14×14×14 patches and masked to the 'nsd-general' visual cortex region) alongside natural language instructions. It produces 512×512 reconstructed images (downsampled to 425×425 for pixel-level metrics), free-form brain captions and detailed descriptions, GradCAM heatmaps localizing semantic concepts within the 3D brain volume, and multi-turn dialogue responses. Notably, the model operates on a single fMRI trial per stimulus rather than averaging across repetitions, and a single trained model generalizes across all four NSD subjects without subject-specific parameters.

## Method

The core architecture is a 16-layer Transformer Encoder (hidden size 768) that processes 3D-patched fMRI data, preserving spatial structure via ViT3D-style patching instead of flattening. A dual-stream design uses two lightweight two-layer perceptrons to align the encoder's class-token output simultaneously with CLIP ViT-L/14 image embeddings and AutoencoderKL VAE latent features under a weighted MSE loss (α=1/64), consolidating multi-level visual features into one backbone. For LLM integration, the penultimate hidden states are projected through a separate two-layer perceptron into the embedding space of Llama-3-8B, trained in two stages (projector-only, then joint fine-tuning at lr 2e-5). Visual reconstruction is performed by having the LLM generate a text prompt that, together with the aligned VAE and CLIP features, conditions a frozen UnCLIP-2 diffusion decoder with a noise-mixing hyperparameter β=0.93. Concept localization is achieved by having the LLM extract target keywords, encoding them via the CLIP text encoder, and applying GradCAM on the fMRI encoder to produce 3D activation heatmaps; ablating the identified voxels and re-running reconstruction confirms the semantic specificity of the localized regions.

## Results

On the NSD dataset, the proposed method achieves state-of-the-art high-level visual reconstruction scores using a single cross-subject model: CLIP 97.5%, Inception 96.8%, and SSIM 0.357, surpassing all subject-specific baselines (4 models) on these metrics. For brain captioning evaluated against BLIP2-generated ground-truth captions, the method attains CIDEr 83.94, SPICE 18.27, and CLIP-S 68.72, outperforming the strongest prior cross-subject method UMBRAE (CIDEr 53.87, SPICE 12.27). Under the more stringent single-trial setting, the method still leads on Inception (94.6% vs. 91.3% for MindEye) and CLIP (95.3% vs. 91.6%), confirming robustness without signal averaging.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest subject-specific baselines (MindEye, DREAM, Brain-Diffuser — all using 4 per-subject models), the proposed single cross-subject model wins decisively on high-level semantic metrics: CLIP 97.5% vs. 94.1–95.2%, Inception 96.8% vs. 87.2–94.8%, and SSIM 0.357 vs. 0.323–0.356. However, it trails on low-level pixel fidelity (PixCorr 0.265 vs. 0.288–0.309) and self-supervised feature metrics (EffNet-B 0.633 vs. 0.638–0.775; SwAV 0.321 vs. 0.367–0.423), suggesting the LLM-driven reconstruction prioritizes semantic coherence over pixel-level accuracy. Among cross-subject methods (1 model), the advantage over UMBRAE and MindBridge is most pronounced on CLIP (+3.4 and +2.8 points) and Inception (+3.4 and +4.4 points), while MindBridge retains an edge on EffNet-B (0.712 vs. 0.633) and SwAV (0.418 vs. 0.321). In the single-trial setting, the method outperforms MindEye on SSIM (0.336 vs. 0.308), Inception (94.6% vs. 91.3%), and CLIP (95.3% vs. 91.6%) with only a marginal PixCorr difference (0.257 vs. 0.255). For brain captioning, the method surpasses UMBRAE by a wide margin on CIDEr (83.94 vs. 53.87) and SPICE (18.27 vs. 12.27) when evaluated against BLIP2-generated captions, and also leads on all BLEU variants and ROUGE.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the fMRI-to-image reconstruction line (Takagi et al., MindEye, DREAM, Brain-Diffuser) by replacing the typical pipeline of multiple independent alignment modules with a single ViT3D backbone and dual alignment heads (CLIP + VAE), preserving 3D spatial structure that prior methods discard by flattening to 1D voxel vectors. It departs from cross-subject decoding efforts (UMBRAE, MindBridge) by eliminating subject-specific parameters entirely through trilinear interpolation and a shared mask, and it builds on the LLaVA-style multimodal LLM paradigm to couple brain features with Llama-3-8B for language interaction. The concept-localization component repurposes GradCAM on the ViT3D encoder, leveraging the CLIP alignment learned during training to map natural-language keywords to specific brain regions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in removing the per-subject training bottleneck and enabling single-trial decoding, which substantially lowers the cost and time of fMRI experiments and moves the field closer to real-time brain-computer interfaces. The language-interaction layer (captioning, reasoning, concept localization) opens a new interpretability axis: researchers can now query brain signals in natural language and verify that ablating localized regions removes the corresponding semantics from reconstructions. Key open questions include generalization beyond the four NSD subjects and the COCO stimulus distribution, computational feasibility for real-time use, and whether the concept-localization signal is robust enough for clinical or neuroscientific inference. Ethical concerns around decoding private mental content are acknowledged but remain underexplored.
