## Problem

While LLM alignment research has heavily focused on helpfulness and harmlessness, honesty—ensuring models proactively refuse to answer questions beyond their knowledge without becoming overly conservative—remains underexplored. Two core challenges persist: there is no precise operational definition of 'honesty' for LLMs, and identifying a model's knowledge boundaries (what it knows versus does not know) is non-trivial due to the opacity of pretraining data and the gap between memorized knowledge and flexible retrieval.

## Input / Output

The method takes a knowledge-based question as input and produces either a correct answer, a predefined idk refusal (e.g., 'I apologize, but I'm not able to provide an answer to the question'), or a confidence-prefixed answer (e.g., 'I'm about 90% confident that...'). The evaluation framework is evolutionary: it compares the model's response type (correct/wrong/idk) before and after alignment on the same questions, using a 3×3 transition matrix to compute prudence, over-conservativeness, and composite honesty scores rather than a single static accuracy metric.

## Method

The paper formalizes alignment for honesty by defining a categorization function c(x,y) that classifies responses as correct, wrong, or idk, and a value function v(x,y) that rewards honest behavior (correct answers for known questions, idk for unknown ones). Knowledge boundaries are approximated externally via expected accuracy over m=10 sampled responses at temperature 1, with a threshold τ=0.1 determining known/unknown status. Three supervised fine-tuning strategies are proposed: ABSOLUTE (binary known→correct answer, unknown→idk), CONFIDENCE (prefixing numerical or verbal confidence expressions to known answers), and MULTISAMPLE (expanding the training set by a factor of m, pairing each sampled response with its own correct/idk label). Trained on 8,000 balanced TriviaQA samples using full-parameter fine-tuning (CoLLiE, AdamW, lr=1e-6), these methods are applied to LLaMA2-Chat models and evaluated in-distribution, out-of-distribution (Non-AmbigQA, PUQA, PKQA), and for alignment tax on helpfulness and harmlessness.

## Results

On the TriviaQA evaluation set (LLaMA2-Chat-13B), MULTISAMPLE achieves the highest honesty score of 75.91 (prudence 67.72, over-conservativeness 15.89), while CONFIDENCE-VERB achieves the best accuracy (73.34) among all aligned methods, nearly matching the unaligned baseline (73.71). All supervised fine-tuning methods substantially outperform the training-free PROMPT-BASED approach (honesty 60.64) and the unaligned baseline (honesty 50.00). The alignment tax is minimal: helpfulness scores on Eval-P⁻ remain nearly identical (e.g., CONFIDENCE-VERB scores 8.61 vs. 8.62 for UNALIGNED under GPT-4 judging).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares four honesty-oriented fine-tuning methods (ABSOLUTE, CONFIDENCE-NUM, CONFIDENCE-VERB, MULTISAMPLE) against three baselines: UNALIGNED (no alignment), FINE-TUNED (fine-tuned with gold answers instead of idk responses), and PROMPT-BASED (training-free prompting). All four proposed methods dramatically outperform the baselines in honesty score: MULTISAMPLE (75.91) and CONFIDENCE-NUM (74.37) lead, followed by CONFIDENCE-VERB (74.12) and ABSOLUTE (68.88), versus 60.64 for PROMPT-BASED and 50.00 for both fine-tuning baselines. CONFIDENCE-VERB is the standout for preserving accuracy (73.34 vs. 73.71 unaligned), while MULTISAMPLE achieves the best prudence (67.72) at a slight accuracy cost (68.88). The FINE-TUNED baseline, which forces the model to answer unknown questions with gold answers, actually degrades accuracy on PKQA by 10 points, suggesting it teaches hallucination. On out-of-distribution datasets (Non-AmbigQA, PUQA, PKQA), the fine-tuning methods remain transferable, with CONFIDENCE-VERB consistently outperforming baselines across all three.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself within the HHH (helpful, harmless, honest) alignment framework, noting that honesty has received comparatively less systematic attention than helpfulness and harmlessness. It builds on prior work on calibration and uncertainty expression (Lin et al., Jiang et al., Kadavath et al.) but departs by shifting the focus from calibrated confidence scores to a binary known/unknown decision with explicit 'idk' refusal responses, and by using the model's own multi-sample accuracy as a self-supervised proxy for knowledge boundaries rather than relying on external annotations. The evolutionary evaluation framework (comparing M_t vs. M_{t+1} response-type transitions) is a novel contribution not present in prior alignment work, which typically evaluates absolute performance rather than alignment-induced behavioral shifts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first systematic framework for honesty alignment with concrete, reproducible training methods and evolutionary metrics (prudence score, over-conservativeness score) that the field can adopt to measure alignment progress. The finding that non-honesty-oriented SFT (replacing unknown answers with gold answers) actively degrades accuracy by teaching hallucination is a practical warning for alignment practitioners. Open questions remain around better definitions of the knowledge-boundary function k(·), calibrated confidence expressions for free-form generation, and representation-level approaches that could achieve honesty alignment with less intervention. The demonstrated low alignment tax on helpfulness and harmlessness makes the approach practically deployable.
