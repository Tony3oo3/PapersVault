## Problem

Standard LLMs face quadratic attention complexity and fixed context windows that make prolonged multi-turn dialogue infeasible. While StreamingLLM introduced attention sinks for efficient streaming, it progressively discards historical tokens, causing dialogue consistency to degrade as conversations lengthen. The paper asks whether dialogue-specific structural tokens can serve as lossy compression points that preserve long-term memory without retaining full utterance histories.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242732/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a dialogue history organized as a sequence of utterances delimited by End-of-Utterance (EoU) tokens (e.g., "</s>" or "\n"). During inference, the KV cache retains only the first token, all EoU tokens (conv-attn sinks), and the full tokens of the two most recent utterances, reducing cached tokens from O(TL) to O(T + 2L) where T is the utterance count and L is average utterance length. The output is a standard autoregressive dialogue response generated token-by-token, with the model attending to compressed historical sinks for long-range context.

## Method

The key insight is that EoU separator tokens naturally attract disproportionate attention in dialogue contexts, functioning as 'conversational attention sinks' that can aggregate utterance-level information. The attention mask is restructured so each token attends to the first token, all preceding conv-attn sinks, and tokens from the previous and current utterances, enabling compression of T utterances into T sink tokens. Two co-training strategies enhance this compression: Short-Memory Reconstruction (SMR) forces the model to reconstruct an utterance using only its EoU sink as context, and Long-Memory Reactivation (LMR) requires recalling a historical response from conv-attn sinks given a query utterance. Training proceeds in three stages—SMR & LMR co-training (1 epoch, attention layers only), supervised dialogue fine-tuning (2 epochs), and inference with the compressed cache—achieving 4× speedup and 18× memory reduction versus dense attention with recomputation.

## Results

On the Multi-Session Chat (MSC) dataset, StreamingDialogue achieves a BLEU-avg of 19.33%, ROUGE-L of 15.86%, USL-H of 90.48%, and Dial-M of 1.76, outperforming the strongest baseline StreamingLLM (16.76%, 14.21%, 86.91%, 1.71 respectively). On PersonaChat, it reaches BLEU-avg 13.63%, Distinct-2 37.23%, and USL-H 17.96%, with a PPL of 8.71 comparable to dense attention (8.41). In terms of efficiency, the method achieves a 4× speedup in per-token latency and an 18× reduction in memory usage compared to dense attention with recomputation at 2,048 input tokens.

## Baselines & Comparisons

StreamingDialogue outperforms StreamingLLM on both datasets across most metrics: on MSC, B-avg improves from 16.76% to 19.33% and USL-H from 86.91% to 90.48%, while on PC, D-2 jumps from 32.64% to 37.23%. Against dense attention, StreamingDialogue achieves comparable quality (ROUGE difference < 0.02% on PC) while delivering 4× speedup and 18× memory reduction; on MSC it even surpasses dense attention on R-1 (17.18 vs 16.93) and R-L (15.86 vs 15.48). Local attention and Big Bird are substantially outperformed, with Local attention showing particularly large gaps (e.g., B-avg 13.34 vs 19.33 on MSC). MemBART, a memory-augmented encoder-decoder, is consistently weaker, with PPL of 13.73 on MSC versus 7.99 for StreamingDialogue. The only metric where StreamingDialogue trails a baseline is Dial-M on MSC (1.76 vs 1.71 for StreamingLLM), a marginal difference. Under the non-training setting (Table 3), StreamingDialogue also consistently beats StreamingLLM across Llama-2-7B-Chat, Llama-3-8B-Instruct, and Mistral-7B, confirming the generality of the conv-attn sink approach.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends StreamingLLM's attention-sink mechanism by identifying End-of-Utterance (EoU) tokens as 'conversational attention sinks' that naturally aggregate utterance-level information in structured dialogue text, shifting the caching target from initial tokens to EoU separators. Unlike prior efficient-attention methods (Longformer, Big Bird, Linformer) that trade accuracy for speed without preserving dialogue history, or memory-augmented approaches (MemBART, MemGPT) that require external storage or retraining, StreamingDialogue compresses each utterance into a single EoU token and introduces two co-training strategies (SMR and LMR) to explicitly teach the model to encode and retrieve information through these sinks. The method reduces space complexity from O(TL) to O(T) and time complexity from O(T²L²) to O(T²L), where T is the number of utterances and L is average utterance length.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 4× speedup and 18× memory reduction over dense attention with recomputation make prolonged multi-session dialogue practically feasible on standard hardware, and the method's compatibility with existing long-context LLMs (e.g., 200K-token windows) opens the door to thousands of utterances per session. The conv-attn sink phenomenon's generality across architectures (Llama2, GLM, BERT) and training objectives suggests it is a structural property of dialogue rather than an artifact of a specific model. Open questions include whether selective caching of only high-information conv-attn sinks can further reduce overhead, how the approach generalizes to non-dialogue structured text, and whether the SMR/LMR training cost (~2 hours on 2×A100) can be eliminated entirely. The paper acknowledges that its exploitation of dialogue structure remains limited and that lifelong/never-ending dialogue is a natural next step.
