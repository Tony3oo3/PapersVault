## Problem

Existing Predict+Optimize frameworks assume all unknown optimization parameters are revealed simultaneously, excluding applications where parameters are disclosed sequentially (e.g., daily patient demand in nurse scheduling, quarterly market prices in investment). This paper introduces Multi-Stage Predict+Optimize, where parameters are revealed across T stages, new optimization decisions are committed at each stage, and parameter predictions can be updated in light of newly revealed information and past committed actions.

## Input / Output

At each stage t, the prediction model (a neural network) takes the feature matrix A, the prior stage solution x̂^(t-1), and optionally the revealed true parameters θ[1:t] as input, and outputs a prediction θ̂^(t) of only the yet-unrevealed future parameters θ[t+1:T]. The Stage t optimization problem then solves a MILP (via Gurobi) that incorporates the revealed parameters, the predicted future parameters, a penalty term for modifying prior soft commitments, and non-anticipativity constraints fixing all prior hard commitments. The final output is the sequence of stage solutions x̂^(0),...,x̂^(T), evaluated by a generalized post-hoc regret that sums the objective gap to the in-hindsight optimum plus all inter-stage modification penalties.

## Method

The paper proposes three training algorithms for neural network predictors under the assumption that all stage optimizations are MILPs. The Baseline trains a single NN whose predictions are reused across all stages, directly generalizing the two-stage approach. The Sequential Coordinate Descent (SCD) and Parallel Coordinate Descent (PCD) algorithms train one NN per stage, treating each stage as a coordinate in a coordinate-descent scheme to handle the interdependency between predictors (a prediction's quality depends on decisions at other stages, which depend on their predictions). SCD trains networks cyclically in order; PCD trains all networks in parallel using copies from the previous descent step, trading slight accuracy for speed. Gradient computation through the MILP stage optimizations uses the convex relaxation of Hu et al. [16] (adapting Mandi and Guns [22]), with the post-hoc regret serving as the differentiable loss via the law of total derivative and recursive chain-rule decomposition across stages.

## Results

Across three benchmarks (production/sales LP, investment MILP, nurse rostering MILP), the proposed Sequential Coordinate Descent (SCD) method consistently achieves the lowest post-hoc regret. On the production and sales problem (low-profit, 4 stages), SCD achieves 293.78±99.21 post-hoc regret versus 355.56±103.78 for the best classic regression method (BAS), a 17.4% improvement. On the nurse rostering problem (extra nurse payment=15), SCD achieves 607.66±142.19 versus 662.34±169.17 for BAS. The performance ordering SCD > PCD > Baseline > BAS holds across all benchmarks, with SCD winning in 96.67%–100% of 30 simulations against BAS.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares its three methods (Baseline, SCD, PCD) against the best of five classic non-Predict+Optimize regression models (Ridge, k-NN, CART, Random Forest, and a plain NN trained with ℓ₂ loss, all from Friedman et al. [10]), reported as 'BAS.' SCD outperforms BAS in all 30 simulations on the production/sales LP (win rate 96.67%–100%) and in >96.67% of simulations on the investment MILP. PCD and Baseline also beat BAS in the majority of simulations (80%–93.33% win rates). Even the simplest Baseline (a single NN trained with Predict+Optimize regret loss, reusing the same prediction across all stages) outperforms all classic methods, confirming that the regret-based training objective itself provides a substantial advantage over standard regression. The gap between SCD and BAS grows with the number of stages for LPs (e.g., 17.4% at 4 stages to 23.4% at 12 stages for low-profit), but this trend reverses for MILPs, which the authors attribute to accumulated approximation error from convex relaxation of integrality constraints.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Predict+Optimize lineage from Elmachtoub and Grigas [9] and the Two-Stage adaptation of Hu et al. [16], both of which assume all unknown parameters are revealed simultaneously. The key conceptual departure is modeling sequential parameter revelation across T stages, where each stage induces new optimization decisions and updated predictions, with non-anticipativity constraints locking in prior commitments. For gradient computation through the per-stage MILPs, the paper reuses the convex relaxation technique from Hu et al. [16] (itself adapting Mandi and Guns [22]), while the novel algorithmic contribution is the coordinate-descent strategy for training interdependent per-stage neural networks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first framework that formalizes multi-stage Predict+Optimize, opening the door to practical applications like nurse rostering, investment planning, and production scheduling where information arrives incrementally. A notable open question is the observed asymmetry: for LPs, the advantage over classical regression grows with the number of stages, but for MILPs it shrinks—likely due to accumulating convex relaxation gaps—which the authors flag as important future work. The framework also clarifies the boundary with multi-stage stochastic programming (supervised vs. unsupervised learning), providing a principled supervised-learning alternative when predictive features are available.
