## Problem

Event-intensity asymmetric stereo matching suffers from overfitting and poor generalization because the event domain lacks the large-scale, diverse labeled datasets available in the image domain. Existing methods require supervised training or fine-tuning on limited event data, restricting their ability to generalize to new scenes, unseen disparity ranges, or challenging conditions. This paper asks whether off-the-shelf image-domain stereo and monocular depth models can be applied to event-intensity stereo in a fully zero-shot manner without any weight modification.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a rectified RGB frame from the left view and an asynchronous event stream from the right view (e.g., 640×480 on DSEC, 346×260 on MVSEC, 1280×720 on M3ED), producing a dense disparity map at the frame timestamp. The asymmetric modality pairing is central: the left view provides absolute intensity values while the right view provides per-pixel relative log-intensity changes triggered above a threshold. No ground-truth disparity or training data is required at inference time, and the framework is modular so that the stereo and monocular backbones can be swapped independently.

## Method

ZEST introduces two key components. First, a representation alignment module exploits the physical link between frames and events: the temporal difference of consecutive log-intensity frames (left view) approximates the temporal integral of events (right view), yielding an intermediate representation that serves as a visual prompt for frozen off-the-shelf stereo models (CREStereo or DynamicStereo) without any architectural or weight modification. Second, a monocular cue-guided disparity refinement module rescales the relative depth from a frozen monocular model (Depth Anything or MiDaS) via a locally optimized scale-and-shift map, where the optimization is weighted by an event-density confidence map derived from the frame temporal difference, ensuring that stereo-reliable regions anchor the refinement while textureless or sparse-event regions are filled by monocular cues. The entire pipeline is training-free, with the refinement solved by 500 iterations of Adam on a per-image loss combining L1 consistency with an edge-aware smoothness regularizer.

## Results

On the DSEC benchmark (41 sequences), ZEST consistently outperforms all cross-dataset baselines across EPE, RMSE, 3PE, and 2PE metrics, with the only exceptions being DAEI† (trained in-dataset on DSEC) and the CR-E2VID baseline on some 3PE/2PE sequences. On MVSEC, Ours-CR-DA achieves EPE 5.22, RMSE 5.76, 3PE 29.82%, and 2PE 46.19% across all three subsets, substantially outperforming HSM (EPE 10.57) and CR-E2VID (EPE 19.32). On M3ED, Ours-CR-DA reaches EPE 2.06 and RMSE 3.39, marginally beating CR-E2VID (EPE 2.10, RMSE 4.02) while DAEI† degrades severely (EPE 20.07) when transferred out-of-dataset.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ZEST (Ours-CR-DA) decisively outperforms the traditional baselines HSM [17] and SHEF [30] on all datasets and metrics, with EPE improvements of roughly 2× on MVSEC (5.22 vs. 10.57) and 6× on M3ED (2.06 vs. 12.39). Against the deep-learning baseline DAEI [40], ZEST wins on cross-dataset transfer: DAEI achieves EPE 1.08 on its in-domain MVSEC S1 subset but degrades to EPE 20.07 on M3ED, while ZEST maintains EPE 5.22 on MVSEC and 2.06 on M3ED. The strongest baseline is CR-E2VID (CREStereo + E2VID event-to-frame reconstruction), which is competitive on M3ED (EPE 2.10 vs. 2.06) and occasionally matches ZEST on 3PE/2PE for individual DSEC sequences, but ZEST wins on all DSEC sequences overall and achieves lower RMSE (3.39 vs. 4.02 on M3ED). DAEI† (DAEI fine-tuned on DSEC for 34 epochs) is the only method that beats ZEST on some DSEC metrics, but it is the sole in-dataset method and thus not directly comparable under the cross-dataset protocol. The representation alignment module is critical: without it, feeding raw E2VID-reconstructed frames to DynamicStereo yields EPE 43.02 on interlaken_00_c, versus 1.41 with the full ZEST pipeline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of event-intensity asymmetric stereo (DAEI, HSM, SHEF) and the broader visual prompting literature, but departs fundamentally from prior event-stereo methods that require supervised training or fine-tuning on scarce labeled event data. The key conceptual departure is the use of the physical formulation of events (log-intensity threshold triggering) to derive an intermediate representation—temporal difference of frames for the left view, temporal integral of events for the right view—that serves as a 'visual prompt' enabling frozen image-domain stereo models (CREStereo, DynamicStereo) to operate without any weight modification. The monocular cue-guided refinement module further builds on the zero-shot cross-dataset transfer capabilities of foundation monocular depth models (Depth Anything, MiDaS), combining them with binocular estimates via a locally linear scale-and-shift transformation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The zero-shot framing directly addresses the data scarcity bottleneck in event-based vision, where large-scale labeled datasets simply do not exist, and the modular design means the framework automatically benefits from future improvements in stereo or monocular depth models without retraining. However, the computational cost is a significant practical limitation (DynamicStereo alone takes ~8.5 s/frame), and the method struggles with very sparse or noisy events where the visual prompt degrades. Open questions include whether learned or adaptive alignment could replace the fixed physical transformation, whether the approach extends to symmetric event-event stereo, and whether the refinement module can be made real-time for deployment in autonomous driving or robotics.
