## Problem

Despite LLMs being integrated into multi-agent systems with critical decision-making roles, there is limited understanding of how they achieve and maintain cooperation in realistic, multi-turn social dilemmas. Prior multi-agent LLM research has focused on highly constrained scenarios (board games, narrow collaborative tasks) or single-agent safety benchmarks, leaving open how LLMs balance reward maximization with long-term sustainability in open-ended, communicative settings and whether they can serve as a platform for testing theories of human cooperative behavior.

## Input / Output

The simulation takes an LLM model as the agent backbone, a scenario configuration (fishery, pasture, or pollution, each with 5 agents and 12 time steps), and carefully designed prompts that avoid priming cooperative or greedy behavior. Each time step interleaves three phases—strategy, simultaneous harvesting, and open-ended group discussion—where agents submit private resource-extraction quantities, receive individual rewards, and then engage in unstructured natural-language dialogue mediated by a moderator who controls turn-taking. Outputs include quantitative metrics (survival time/rate, total gain, efficiency, Gini-based equality, over-usage percentage) and the full dialogue transcripts, which are further analyzed via GPT-4-turbo classification into information, negotiation, and relational utterance categories.

## Method

GOVSIM is a generative simulation platform built as a partially observable Markov game with three mathematically equivalent common-pool-resource scenarios (fishery, pasture, pollution) where a shared resource regenerates by doubling each step up to a capacity of 100, and collapses below a threshold of 5. The agent architecture adapts the Generative Agents framework to a structured, phase-based environment with strategy, concurrent harvesting (simultaneous private submissions with random allocation when demand exceeds supply), and a town-hall discussion phase where a moderator orchestrates multi-party dialogue. The key empirical finding is that all but the most powerful LLMs (GPT-4o at 53.3% survival rate) fail to reach sustainable equilibrium, and ablations reveal that removing communication increases resource over-use by 22%. A critical intervention—augmenting agent memory with a 'Universalization' prompt that makes the collective consequence of over-harvesting explicit—significantly improves survival time by 4 months on average, suggesting that LLMs struggle to spontaneously simulate the long-term group-level effects of greedy individual actions.

## Results

Across 15 LLMs tested in GOVSIM (aggregated over three common-pool-resource scenarios and five seeds), GPT-4o achieves the best survival rate of 53.3% and mean survival time of 9.3±2.2 months, with total gain of 66.0±14.6 and efficiency of 55.0±12.2%. No model achieves a 100% survival rate across all seeds; the best open-weights model (Qwen-110B) reaches only 20% survival rate and 4.5±2.3 months. Ablations show that removing inter-agent communication increases resource over-usage by 22%, and adding a Universalization prompt improves mean survival time by 4 months (p < 0.001).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GOVSIM is a benchmark rather than a method, so the 'baselines' are the 15 LLMs evaluated under identical conditions. GPT-4o dominates all closed-weights models: it outperforms GPT-4-turbo (53.3% vs. 40.0% survival rate; 9.3 vs. 6.6 months survival time) and Claude-3 Opus (53.3% vs. 46.7%; 9.3 vs. 6.9 months), while also achieving the lowest over-usage (10.8% vs. 15.7% and 21.0%). Among open-weights models, Qwen-110B is the clear leader (20% survival rate, 4.5 months) but still trails GPT-4o by over 33 percentage points in survival rate and nearly 5 months in survival time. All smaller models (Llama-3-8B, Llama-3-70B, Mistral-7B, Mixtral-8x7B, Claude-3 Haiku, GPT-3.5) collapse in the first month with 0% survival rate, indicating a sharp capability threshold. The paper further shows that adding a Universalization prompt to the agent's memory closes much of the gap: GPT-4-turbo and GPT-4o both reach 100% survival rate in the fishery scenario, and several open-weights models (Llama-3-70B, Mixtral-8x7B, Qwen-110B) also achieve 100% survival in at least one scenario.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the generative agents architecture (Park et al., 2023) from open-ended social simulation to a structured, phase-based multi-agent environment grounded in common-pool resource economics (Ostrom, Hardin). It departs from prior multi-agent LLM evaluations that focused on board games or narrow collaborative tasks by introducing realistic, multi-turn social dilemmas with open-ended natural-language negotiation. The evaluation metrics follow Perolat et al. (2017) for common-pool resource games, while the universalization intervention draws on moral psychology (Levine et al., 2020) to test whether a philosophical principle of moral reasoning transfers to LLM agent behavior.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GOVSIM provides the first systematic benchmark for evaluating whether LLMs can achieve sustainable cooperation in multi-agent resource-management settings, a capability increasingly relevant as LLMs are deployed in collaborative and governance contexts. The finding that even GPT-4o fails to reach sustainable equilibrium in most runs (≤54% survival) highlights a concrete gap in current LLM capabilities for long-horizon strategic reasoning about collective outcomes. Open questions include whether the simplified 5-agent, 12-month scenarios generalize to larger, more heterogeneous populations, whether norms remain robust to adversarial perturbations, and how human-AI mixed groups would fare. The universalization result suggests that targeted moral-reasoning prompts could be a practical lever for improving cooperative LLM behavior, though its transferability to more complex real-world dilemmas remains untested.
