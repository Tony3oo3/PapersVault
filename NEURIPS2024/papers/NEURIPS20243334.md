## Problem

Existing diffusion purification methods for certified robustness face a fundamental efficiency-effectiveness trade-off: one-step DDPM is fast but produces off-manifold outputs with ambiguous classification, while PF-ODE-based methods guarantee on-manifold purification but require multiple ODE-solving steps. This paper seeks a Pareto-superior purifier that achieves on-manifold, semantically aligned purification in a single network evaluation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243334/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a Gaussian-noised RGB image x_σ = x + ε (ε ~ N(0, σ²I)) at a discrete noise level σ (e.g., {0.25, 0.5, 1.0} for CIFAR-10 32×32, {0.05, 0.15, 0.25} for ImageNet-64 64×64) and produces a purified image in a single forward pass through the consistency model. The purified image is then fed to an arbitrary off-the-shelf classifier (e.g., ViT-B/16) to yield a class label, and randomized smoothing over N=10000 samples computes the certified ℓ₂ radius. The key constraint is that σ must be mapped to the nearest discrete training timestep t* of the consistency model.

## Method

Consistency Purification uses a consistency model—distilled from a pre-trained EDM via PF-ODE trajectory simulation—as a one-step purifier that maps any noisy input x_t directly to the data manifold point x_ε in a single network evaluation, eliminating the multi-step ODE solving required by PF-ODE. To address the semantic misalignment inherent in generation-trained consistency models, the paper introduces Consistency Fine-tuning: the purifier is further optimized by minimizing LPIPS loss between clean training images and their purified counterparts across the discrete noise levels used in randomized smoothing. The choice of LPIPS over ℓ₁/ℓ₂ is critical, as it preserves the on-manifold property (verified via FID) while aligning perceptual semantics. Theoretically, the paper proves (Theorem 3.3) that lower transport T_{π_t}(p) between the data and purified distributions upper-bounds the probability of large purification displacement, and empirically shows both the consistency model integration and LPIPS fine-tuning reduce this transport compared to one-step DDPM.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, Consistency Purification with Consistency Fine-tuning achieves 90.2% certified accuracy at ε=0, 79.4% at ε=0.25, 62.4% at ε=0.5, 43.8% at ε=0.75, and 35.4% at ε=1.0, all with a single network evaluation. On ImageNet-64, the same method reaches 68.6% at ε=0, 58.0% at ε=0.05, and 23.4% at ε=0.25. The paper reports an average ~5% gain over onestep-DDPM at the same single-step computational cost, while also outperforming multi-step methods like PF-ODE EDM in certified accuracy at most ε levels.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against onestep-DDPM [25], the primary single-step baseline, Consistency Purification with Fine-tuning wins at every ε level on both datasets (e.g., 79.4% vs. 73.6% at ε=0.25 and 35.4% vs. 29.6% at ε=1.0 on CIFAR-10; 58.0% vs. 44.8% at ε=0.05 on ImageNet-64), confirming the theoretical advantage of on-manifold purification. Against Diffusion Calibration [38], another one-step method, Consistency Fine-tuning wins at ε=0.25–1.0 on CIFAR-10 (e.g., 62.4% vs. 57.2% at ε=0.5) while Diffusion Calibration edges ahead only at ε=0.0 (90.2% vs. 90.2%, essentially tied). Against non-diffusion baselines—Randomized Smoothing [19], Consistency Regularization [22], and Aces [42]—Consistency Purification dominates across all ε levels (e.g., 90.2% vs. 74.8% at ε=0 and 35.4% vs. 22.0% at ε=1.0 on CIFAR-10), while also being applicable to arbitrary off-the-shelf classifiers without per-noise-level retraining. Against multi-step PF-ODE EDM, Consistency Fine-tuning matches or exceeds its certified accuracy at ε=0.25–1.0 (79.4% vs. 77.0% at ε=0.25) while requiring only one forward pass instead of multiple ODE solver steps.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: diffusion purification for certified robustness (Carlini et al. 2022, Xiao et al. 2022, Zhang et al. 2023) and consistency models for one-step generation (Song et al. 2023). It identifies a specific gap in the purification literature—onestep-DDPM is efficient but off-manifold, while PF-ODE-based methods are on-manifold but multi-step—and bridges it by repurposing consistency models (which are distilled from PF-ODE trajectories) as single-step purifiers that inherit the on-manifold guarantee. The LPIPS-based Consistency Fine-tuning step is a practical addition that departs from prior purification fine-tuning approaches (e.g., Diffusion Calibration) by being classifier-agnostic rather than classifier-guided.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a clean, drop-in replacement for onestep-DDPM in any randomized smoothing pipeline, achieving ~5% higher certified accuracy at identical single-step computational cost while guaranteeing purified images lie on the data manifold. The transport-based theoretical analysis (Theorem 3.3) provides a principled, purifier-agnostic lens for evaluating semantic alignment that could inform future purification design. Key limitations include evaluation only on CIFAR-10 and ImageNet-64 (no 256×256 ImageNet results due to missing consistency model checkpoints), and the reliance on a pre-trained consistency model whose quality bounds the purification ceiling. An open question is whether the LPIPS fine-tuning strategy generalizes to higher-resolution or multi-modal purification settings.
