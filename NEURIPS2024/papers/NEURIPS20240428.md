## Problem

Unsupervised SDF learning from raw point clouds tends to over-smooth local geometric details because the discrete point cloud provides an ambiguous and incomplete signal for the network to infer accurate signed distances. Existing overfitting-based methods (e.g., NeuralPull, GridPull) rely on single-scale query pulling and lack mechanisms to progressively recover fine structure without ground-truth SDF or normal supervision.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240428/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single raw 3D point cloud (no normals, no ground-truth SDFs) as input. Query points are constructed by sampling 40 Gaussian-distributed points around each input point, and the network is trained to predict SDF values at these queries. The output is a continuous SDF field from which a watertight mesh is extracted via the Marching Cubes algorithm; training runs for 40,000 iterations (~24 min on a single RTX-3090) per object.

## Method

MultiPull consists of two modules: (1) a Frequency Feature Transformation (FFT) module that maps query points into multi-level frequency features via a Fourier-layer network using Hadamard products (following the MFN architecture), with a novel sinusoidal initialization scheme Ψ_i = √(η·sin(iπ/N_L)) to prevent gradient vanishing in deeper layers; and (2) a Multi-Step Pulling (MSP) module that progressively pulls query points toward the surface over I=3 steps, where at each step i the SDF prediction f(Q_i, y_i) and its gradient ∇f(Q_i, y_i) are conditioned on the i-th level frequency feature y_i, enabling coarse-to-fine detail recovery. The training loss combines a distance-aware reconstruction term (softmax-weighted to emphasize underfit outlier points), a cosine gradient-consistency term (enforcing parallel level sets across pulling steps via a min-similarity constraint), and a surface constraint driving the final-step SDF to zero.

## Results

On ShapeNet, MultiPull achieves CD_L2 of 0.0075×100, NC of 0.9737, and F-Score of 0.9906/0.9932 (at thresholds 0.002/0.004), outperforming the closest competitor GP (CD_L2 0.0086, NC 0.9723). On FAMOUS, it reaches CD_L2 of 0.035×100 and NC of 0.953, surpassing GP (0.040, 0.945) and PCP (0.044, 0.933). On D-FAUST, MultiPull achieves CD_L1 of 0.009 and NC of 0.988, beating GP (0.015, 0.978) by a wide margin. On the large-scale 3DScene dataset, it attains CD_L2 of 0.094×100 and NC of 0.918, outperforming GP (0.10, 0.903) and PCP (0.11, 0.886).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MultiPull consistently outperforms all 13 baselines across every evaluated dataset. Against the closest competitor GP (GridPull), it improves CD_L2 by ~13% on ShapeNet (0.0075 vs 0.0086) and by ~12% on FAMOUS (0.035 vs 0.040), while also achieving higher NC on all benchmarks. The overfitting-based NP method is substantially outperformed, with MultiPull reducing CD_L2 by over 80% on ShapeNet (0.0075 vs 0.038) and by ~84% on FAMOUS (0.035 vs 0.220). PCP, which uses local context priors, is beaten on all shape datasets (e.g., CD_L2 0.0075 vs 0.0136 on ShapeNet, 0.035 vs 0.044 on FAMOUS) and on 3DScene (0.094 vs 0.11). Supervised methods like DSDF and ATLAS are orders of magnitude worse (CD_L2 0.766 and 1.368 on ShapeNet), confirming the advantage of the unsupervised multi-step pulling strategy. On D-FAUST, MultiPull achieves CD_L1 of 0.009, nearly halving the error of GP (0.015) and reducing SAP's error (0.043) by ~79%.

## Relation to Prior Work

MultiPull extends the NeuralPull (NP) and GridPull (GP) line of work on learning SDFs by pulling query points onto surfaces, but introduces a multi-scale dimension absent in both: a Frequency Feature Transformation module (drawing on Multiplicative Filter Networks and BACON's band-limited coordinate networks) that decomposes query points into multi-level Fourier features via Hadamard products. The key architectural departure is the Multi-Step Pulling module, which uses these frequency features at different levels to progressively refine query points from coarse to fine across multiple pulling steps, rather than performing a single-scale pull as in NP or a grid-interpolated pull as in GP. A novel initialization scheme for the frequency network addresses gradient vanishing in deep MFN layers, and new loss terms (distance-aware weighting, cosine gradient consistency, and surface constraint) are introduced to stabilize the multi-scale optimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a persistent weakness of unsupervised SDF learning—over-smoothing of local geometric details—by providing a principled coarse-to-fine mechanism that does not require ground-truth signed distances or normals. The practical impact is moderate: improvements over the strongest baseline (GP) are consistent but modest (roughly 10–15% in CD_L2 across benchmarks), and the per-shape optimization time (~24 min) remains a barrier for large-scale deployment. Open questions include whether the multi-step pulling strategy can be made more computationally efficient (the authors acknowledge this in their limitations), how it scales to very large scenes beyond KITTI, and whether the frequency-level selection (L4, L6, L8) generalizes across shape categories without manual tuning.
