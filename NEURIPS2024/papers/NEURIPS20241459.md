## Problem

Prior work on implicit regularization in overparameterized matrix factorization produced seemingly conflicting results: some analyses showed a low nuclear norm bias while others demonstrated a low rank bias, without a unified account of when each effect arises. This paper identifies the connectivity of the observed data—formalized as the connectedness of a bipartite graph over observed entries—as the key structural property that determines which implicit bias the model exhibits, revealing a transition from low nuclear norm to low rank as observations increase and the graph becomes connected.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a partially observed d×d matrix M together with a binary observation pattern P (indicating which entries are revealed), and trains an overparameterized asymmetric factorization W = AB with A, B ∈ R^{d×d} from infinitesimal Gaussian initialization (σ² as small as 10⁻¹⁶) under gradient flow. The output is the completed matrix W = AB, whose implicit property—minimum rank versus minimum nuclear norm—is entirely determined by the connectivity structure of the observation graph G_M (rows and columns as bipartite nodes, observed entries as edges) rather than by any explicit regularizer. The overparameterized setting (r = d, no rank constraint) is essential, as the implicit bias emerges precisely because no explicit rank or norm penalty is imposed.

## Method

The core technical contribution is the identification of a hierarchy of intrinsic invariant manifolds Ω_k, defined by the alignment row(A) = col(B) = span{α₁,…,α_k}, which are invariant under gradient flow for any data and form a nested sequence Ω₀ ⊊ Ω₁ ⊊ … ⊊ Ω_d. In the connected case, the training trajectory follows a Hierarchical Invariant Manifold Traversal (HIMT): it reaches the optimal solution within each Ω_k before escaping along the dominant Hessian eigendirection into Ω_{k+1}, generalizing the Greedy Low-Rank Learning equivalence of Li et al. (2020) to the full connected setting. In the disconnected case, each connected component of the observation graph induces a strict sub-Ω_k invariant manifold that traps the trajectory at sub-optimal critical points, preventing convergence to the global minimum-rank solution. Theorem 2 rigorously characterizes the escape dynamics via linearized Hessian analysis, showing the trajectory generically enters Ω_{k+1} with probability 1. The paper then establishes that minimum rank is guaranteed when the trajectory achieves the optimum at every Ω_k level (Theorem 3), and minimum nuclear norm is guaranteed in the special disconnected-with-complete-bipartite-components case (Theorem 4), thereby providing a unified theoretical framework that explains both empirical observations.

## Results

The paper's key empirical finding is that data connectivity determines the implicit regularization regime: for connected observed data, the overparameterized matrix factorization model (A, B ∈ R^{d×d}) consistently converges to the minimum-rank solution, while for disconnected data with complete bipartite components it converges to the minimum nuclear norm solution, and for general disconnected data it converges to neither. Theoretically, the training dynamics are characterized as a Hierarchical Invariant Manifold Traversal (HIMT) process through nested Ω_k manifolds, generalizing the Greedy Low-Rank Learning (GLRL) equivalence of Li et al. (2020) to the disconnected case. Theorem 3 provides a dynamic characterization guaranteeing minimum rank when the trajectory achieves optimal solutions on each Ω_k, and Theorem 4 guarantees minimum nuclear norm for the disconnected-with-complete-bipartite-components case, extending Gunasekar et al. (2017) beyond the commutative observation setting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is with the GLRL algorithm of Li et al. (2020), which claims mathematical equivalence to matrix factorization gradient flow dynamics. In the connected case, both GLRL and the matrix factorization model reach the same rank-1 saddle point and ultimately the minimum-rank solution, confirming the equivalence. However, in the disconnected case (Fig. 4), GLRL fills unobserved entries with 0 after reaching the rank-1 saddle point, yielding a unique rank-2 solution, whereas the matrix factorization model learns symmetric solutions with residual degree of freedom depending on the random seed. The paper identifies the root cause: the first critical point reached in the disconnected case is a sub-optimal point on the rank-1 manifold (not a second-order stationary point as assumed by Li et al.), so the GLRL equivalence breaks down. Additionally, the paper's Theorem 4 extends Gunasekar et al. (2017), who proved minimum nuclear norm only under commutative observations, to the broader disconnected-with-complete-bipartite-components setting where observations need not be commutative.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two seemingly contradictory lines of work on implicit regularization in matrix factorization: the low-nuclear-norm bias (Gunasekar et al., 2017) and the low-rank bias (Li et al., 2020; Arora et al., 2019). It generalizes Li et al.'s Greedy Low-Rank Learning equivalence—which only holds for connected observations—by introducing Hierarchical Invariant Manifold Traversal (HIMT) that accounts for disconnected data, and extends Gunasekar et al.'s nuclear norm guarantee from commutative observations to the broader disconnected-with-complete-bipartite-components case. The loss landscape proof for asymmetric factorization (Theorem A.3) directly extends Du and Lee's (2018) symmetric-case result, which was previously inapplicable due to a dimensionality constraint.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper resolves a long-standing apparent conflict in the literature by showing that data connectivity is the key variable determining whether implicit regularization favors low rank or low nuclear norm, providing a practical diagnostic for practitioners. However, the minimum-rank guarantee under connectivity is explicitly left as future work (Theorem 3 requires the trajectory to achieve optima at each rank level, which is not yet proven to follow from connectivity alone), and the theoretical conditions (unique top singular value, second-order stationarity) are somewhat restrictive. Preliminary evidence suggests the HIMT process extends to deep multi-layer factorization and Transformer attention, but this remains unexplored.
