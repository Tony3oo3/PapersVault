## Problem

Global structure-from-motion synchronization has traditionally relied on pairwise measurements (fundamental or essential matrices), which encode only relative direction but not scale, making location estimation notoriously difficult. Trifocal tensors capture richer three-view geometry including relative scales, yet no prior work has developed a global synchronization pipeline that operates directly on trifocal tensors or exploits their collective algebraic structure across all camera triplets.

## Input / Output

The method takes a partially observed 3n×3n×3n block trifocal tensor where each 3×3×3 block corresponds to a camera triplet and is known only up to an unknown multiplicative scale; unobserved blocks are zeroed out and tracked via a binary observation mask. Essential matrix estimates for camera pairs are optionally included as the jii blocks. The output is a 3n×4 stacked camera matrix (i.e., all camera poses) determined up to a global 4×4 projective transformation and per-camera scale, which in practice is rounded to calibrated cameras for comparison with ground truth.

## Method

The core theoretical contribution is an explicit Tucker factorization of the block trifocal tensor with multilinear rank (6,4,4) independent of the number of cameras, where the factor matrices are the stacked camera matrices (3n×4) and stacked line projection matrices (3n×6). A key theorem proves that this rank constraint uniquely determines the unknown per-block scales (they must factor as α_i β_j γ_k), making the constraint sufficient for full camera recovery. The synchronization algorithm alternates between two steps: (1) a hard-thresholded HOSVD (HOSVD-HT) that projects the current tensor onto the rank-(6,4,4) manifold while simultaneously completing missing blocks via imputation, and (2) a per-block least-squares scale update that aligns each observed block to its rank-truncated counterpart. For robustness against corrupted estimates, the SVD in each mode flattening can be replaced by a subspace-constrained Tyler's M-estimator. Final camera poses are extracted as the leading four singular vectors of the mode-2 or mode-3 flattening of the converged tensor.

## Results

On the EPFL benchmark, the proposed trifocal tensor synchronization method achieves the best mean translation error on 4 of 6 datasets, with particularly dramatic improvements on FountainP11 (0.008 vs. 0.91 for LUD), HerzP8 (0.02 vs. 5.06), and EntryP10 (0.05 vs. 3.08). On the Photo Tourism benchmark, the method achieves competitive translation errors on 8 of 14 datasets, with clear advantages when the trifocal tensor estimation percentage exceeds 60% (winning 6 of 8 such datasets). The method underperforms on sparse observation graphs (CastleP19: 9.64 vs. LUD 4.58; CastleP30: 11.00 vs. LUD 4.27) and on Photo Tourism scenes with low estimation percentages (<60%), where it wins only 2 of 6 datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against LUD [12] (convex programming for camera locations), the trifocal tensor method achieves substantially lower translation errors on dense EPFL scenes (e.g., 0.008 vs. 0.91 on FountainP11, a >100× improvement) and on 6 of 8 Photo Tourism datasets with >60% estimation coverage, but loses on the two Castle EPFL scenes where the observation graph is too sparse for tensor completion to work well. Against NRFM [18] (nonconvex rank-constrained optimization on fundamental matrices), the proposed method wins on nearly all datasets; NRFM with random initialization is particularly unreliable (e.g., 3.37–16.54 mean translation error on EPFL vs. 0.008–11.00 for the proposed method). Against BATA+MPLS [43] (baseline-desensitizing translation averaging initialized by message-passing least squares), the trifocal method dominates on EPFL FountainP11, HerzP8, and EntryP10 but is outperformed on CastleP19 and CastleP30. On Photo Tourism, the comparison with BATA is mixed: the trifocal method wins on Piazza del Popolo, Ellis Island, Yorkminster, and Piccadilly but loses on Alamo, Notre Dame, and Montreal N.D. Overall, the method's advantage is most pronounced on smaller, denser image sets where the block trifocal tensor can be well-completed, while pairwise methods retain an edge on large, sparsely-connected scenes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the global SfM synchronization paradigm from pairwise measurements (fundamental/essential matrices, as exploited by NRFM, LUD, BATA, and SE-Sync) to higher-order triplet measurements encoded in trifocal tensors. Theoretically, it builds on the Tucker decomposition framework and the HARD-IMPUTE matrix completion strategy, while practically it leverages the subspace-constrained Tyler's estimator for robust subspace recovery in place of standard SVD. The key departure is the explicit Tucker factorization of the block trifocal tensor revealing a multilinear rank of (6,4,4) independent of the number of cameras, which to the authors' knowledge has no prior analogue in the SfM synchronization literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that a simple HOSVD-based projection onto the (6,4,4) multilinear rank manifold can simultaneously recover scales, rotations, and translations from trifocal tensors provides a clean theoretical foundation for higher-order synchronization. However, the method's cubic scaling in the number of cameras and its clear degradation on sparse observation graphs (CastleP19/30, low-estimation-percentage Photo Tourism scenes) limit its practical applicability to dense, small-scale reconstructions. Open questions include proving convergence of the alternating projection algorithm, extending the theory to quadrifocal or higher-order tensors, and developing distributed/parallel strategies to handle large-scale datasets where the cubic cost becomes prohibitive.
