## Problem

In traditional federated learning, updating and averaging all model parameters in every communication round causes a phenomenon the authors term 'layer mismatch': after aggregation, the averaged layers fail to cooperate effectively, leading to large gradient spikes, slower convergence, and degraded final accuracy. This issue is particularly pronounced on complex datasets and persists across all standard FL algorithms (FedAvg, FedProx, FedMOON), yet had not been previously identified or addressed.

## Input / Output

The method operates on standard classification inputs: RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 64×64 for Tiny-ImageNet) and text documents (AG News, SogouNews), producing a single class label per sample. The key operational difference from standard FL is that in each communication round, clients train and upload only the parameters of one or a few designated layers (e.g., one of 10 partitioned groups in ResNet-8), while the remaining layers stay frozen, reducing per-round communication to roughly 1/M of the full-model size.

## Method

FedPart partitions the global model into M groups of layers and, in each partial-update round, trains and aggregates only one group using a binary mask that zeroes gradients for frozen parameters. Two layer-selection strategies drive the training schedule: (1) sequential updating, which trains layers from shallow to deep to mirror the natural convergence order of neural networks, and (2) multi-round cycle training, which repeats the shallow-to-deep sweep multiple times (inspired by Block Coordinate Descent) so that shallow layers are not forced to encode high-level semantics while deep layers are frozen. An initial warm-up phase of full-network updates precedes the partial-update cycles. Theoretically, FedPart achieves a convergence rate of O(1/√(MNT)) under non-convex assumptions, improving over the O(1/√(NT)) rate of full-network FedAvg, while reducing communication cost to 1/M and computation cost to approximately 2/3 of the full-update baseline.

## Results

On Tiny-ImageNet with ResNet-8, FedPart (FedAvg variant) achieves 21.4% best accuracy at C=5, outperforming FedAvg-FNU (17.7%) by 3.7 percentage points, while reducing communication overhead by 72% (25.1→7.02 GB) and computation by 27% (87.7→64.3 TFLOPs). On CIFAR-100, FedPart reaches 37.2% vs. 35.6% for FedAvg-FNU at C=5. With the deeper ResNet-18 architecture, FedPart achieves a 24.8% relative improvement on Tiny-ImageNet (17.1% vs. 13.7% at C=3) with 85% communication reduction. The method also extends to NLP tasks (Transformer on AG News/SogouNews), maintaining comparable accuracy while cutting communication by 66% and computation by 25%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedPart consistently outperforms FedAvg-FNU on the more complex datasets: +3.7% on Tiny-ImageNet (21.4% vs. 17.7% at C=5) and +1.6% on CIFAR-100 (37.2% vs. 35.6% at C=5), while on the simpler CIFAR-10 the gap is smaller (+2.1% at C=4). Against FedProx-FNU, FedPart wins on CIFAR-100 (+1.3%) and Tiny-ImageNet (+3.1%) at C=5, but roughly ties on CIFAR-10 (62.3% vs. 62.8%). Against FedMOON-FNU, FedPart achieves the largest relative gains on Tiny-ImageNet (+3.3%) and CIFAR-100 (+2.2%), though it trails slightly on CIFAR-10 (60.5% vs. 62.3% at C=4). The authors attribute the weaker CIFAR-10 results to client drift being the dominant issue on simpler datasets, whereas layer mismatch becomes more prominent on complex data. Across all comparisons, FedPart reduces communication by ~72% and computation by ~27% relative to FNU baselines, with even larger savings (85% communication) when using the deeper ResNet-18 architecture.

## Relation to Prior Work

FedPart builds directly on the standard federated averaging framework (FedAvg, FedProx, FedMOON) by modifying the parameter update rule from full-network to partial-network updates. It departs from prior partial-training work (PVT, FedPT, HeteroFL, FjORD) which aimed to reduce client-side overhead by assigning different layers to different clients, and from progressive training (ProgFed, ProFL) which grows the model incrementally. The key conceptual departure is identifying 'layer mismatch'—a degradation in inter-layer cooperation after parameter averaging—as the root cause of FL's suboptimal convergence, and showing that restricting updates to a single layer per round (with sequential and multi-round cycling strategies inspired by Block Coordinate Descent) actually improves final accuracy rather than merely reducing cost.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The layer mismatch diagnosis provides a new explanatory lens for why federated learning underperforms centralized training even under i.i.d. data, and the partial-update remedy simultaneously improves accuracy (up to 24.8% on Tiny-ImageNet/ResNet-18) while cutting communication by 85% and computation by 27%. However, the accuracy advantage diminishes on simpler datasets (CIFAR-10) and largely vanishes under extreme data heterogeneity (α=0.1), suggesting that client drift remains the dominant challenge in non-i.i.d. settings. Open questions include whether the sequential shallow-to-deep ordering generalizes to non-CNN architectures beyond the tested Transformer, whether the method scales to very deep or very wide networks, and how it interacts with other FL improvements like SCAFFOLD or adaptive client selection.
