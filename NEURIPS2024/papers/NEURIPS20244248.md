## Problem

LLMs process long-form contexts through a causal, sequential lens, which limits their ability to locate and reason over intricate information in lengthy inputs. Unlike human cognition, which consolidates text into hierarchical knowledge structures for targeted information retrieval, LLMs lack an explicit structural representation of context. This paper asks whether reorganizing input text into a structured hierarchy—without modifying the LLM itself—can close this cognitive gap.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20244248/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a plain, unordered long-form text passage (typically 4K–18K tokens) and produces a three-layer JSON hierarchy: a top-level scope (a short noun phrase summarizing the topic), a middle layer of numbered aspects (concise noun phrases), and a bottom layer of faithful description sentences attached to each aspect. This structured JSON is then reassembled into natural language using linguistic markers—bold for scope, numbered lists for aspects, and indented subclauses for descriptions—so the output remains compatible with standard LLM input formats while preserving navigable hierarchy cues.

## Method

The core mechanism is a single-round context structurization: an auxiliary model (StruXGPT-7B) reorganizes the input passage into the scope–aspect–description hierarchy, and the result is fed to the target LLM unchanged. StruXGPT-7B is obtained by supervised fine-tuning a LLaMA2-7B or Qwen-7B chat model on 22,547 raw/structurized text pairs collected via few-shot prompting of the Qwen-Max commercial LLM, inheriting ~97% of the teacher's structurization quality at a fraction of the cost. The approach is task-agnostic and model-agnostic: the target LLM (whether a 7B decoder-only LLaMA2, a 70B LLaMA2, a 32K-window ChatGLM3, or even a ~110M BERT-like encoder for dense retrieval) is never fine-tuned on structurized data. The paper further shows compatibility with Chain-of-Thought prompting and introduces BERTScore as a lightweight quality gate to filter low-quality structurization outputs at inference time, reducing degradation cases from 3.5% to 3.0%.

## Results

On the AttrScore hallucination evaluation benchmark, structurization boosts LLaMA2-7B from 26.9% to 33.3% average accuracy (+6.4%), LLaMA2-70B from 58.7% to 63.0% (+4.3%), and GPT-3.5-1106 from 58.0% to 62.1% (+4.1%). On LongBench long-context QA, consistent gains are observed across all tested models (e.g., LLaMA2-13B improves from 22.9 to 26.0 average F1). On BEIR dense retrieval, all three retrievers improve (BERT: 29.3→30.3, SimLM: 26.7→28.3, coCondenser: 32.0→33.1 nDCG@10), with SimLM gaining up to 4.5% on SciFact.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GPT-3.5-Turbo (45.0% avg on AttrScore), LLaMA2-70B+StruXGPT (63.0%) and GPT-3.5-1106+CoT+StruXGPT (65.4%) substantially outperform it, validating the paper's headline claim of matching or exceeding GPT-3.5-Turbo as a hallucination evaluator. Against GPT-4 (74.0%), the best structured result (65.4%) still trails by ~8.6 points, indicating a remaining gap at the frontier. Against ABS/QBS summarization baselines (Yang et al.), structurization wins on all five LongBench QA subsets (avg 27.0 vs. 21.2 for ABS and 24.7 for QBS), while ABS actually degrades performance below vanilla (24.5). Against AdaptLLM (Cheng et al.), the comparison is mixed: structurization wins on BoolQ (58.2 vs. 53.9) and SCOTUS micro-F1 (30.6 vs. 30.0) but loses on ConvFinQA (36.5 vs. 41.5) and PubMedQA (63.0 vs. 63.3); the paper argues its advantage is task-agnostic generalizability without domain-specific retraining. Against Alpaca-7B/13B (21.0%/25.3%), LLaMA2-7B+StruXGPT (33.3%) clearly surpasses both open-source baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the cognitive science concept of structurization (organizing text into hierarchical knowledge structures) as an input transformation for LLMs, positioning itself distinct from both summarization-based context augmentation (ABS/QBS, which lose information) and structured data extraction (knowledge graphs, entity-relation triplets). Methodologically, it combines few-shot prompting on commercial LLMs with knowledge distillation into a 7B student model, following the Alpaca/Vicuna paradigm but targeting a specific syntactic reorganization capability rather than general instruction-following. The approach is explicitly task-agnostic and model-agnostic, operating purely at the inference stage without altering the target model's weights, which differentiates it from training-based methods like AdaptLLM.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a simple three-layer linguistic restructuring (scope-aspect-description) consistently improves performance across fundamentally different architectures—autoregressive LLMs (7B–70B) and masked encoder models (~110M BERT)—suggests that information organization is a more fundamental mechanism for language understanding than architecture-specific processing. However, the method is limited to inference-stage augmentation, introduces non-trivial latency (1.2–2.9s per sample), and does not help on context-free tasks (MMLU). Open questions include whether training LLMs to natively capture intrinsic context structure could unlock larger gains, whether more elaborate structures (mindmaps, knowledge graphs) would further improve performance, and how to reduce the inference overhead for real-time deployment.
