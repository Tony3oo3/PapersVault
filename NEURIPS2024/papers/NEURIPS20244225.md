## Problem

When visual information is insufficient to answer a question, VLMs typically produce a forced one-shot response rather than assessing sufficiency and guiding the user to acquire more information. While prior work has explored teaching VLMs to label questions as unanswerable, no existing benchmark or method addresses the critical follow-up: what specific action (e.g., which direction to reframe the camera) the model should recommend to resolve the information gap. This gap is especially consequential for visually impaired users who rely on assistive VLMs to iteratively adjust their camera framing.

## Input / Output

The model receives an RGB image paired with a natural-language question and must select one of six options formatted as a multiple-choice prompt: 'Leave it unchanged' (sufficient information present), four cardinal directions (Left, Right, Up, Down) indicating where to move the camera, or 'None of the other options' (inherently unanswerable regardless of framing). The benchmark test set comprises 821 samples drawn from real VizWiz queries by visually impaired users, with labels assigned by 26 human annotators plus a two-round validation process. During training, the model is fine-tuned to emit a single letter (A–F) as its prediction, with letter-to-option mappings randomly shuffled per sample to prevent positional memorization.

## Method

The core contribution is an automated data-augmentation framework that synthesizes training pairs by exploiting bounding-box groundings from VizWiz-grounding. Each target object's bounding box is divided into a 10-zone grid; a directional crop is applied to remove part of the object, and the model's prediction is checked before and after perturbation. If the model's answer degrades (precision drops below a threshold), the reverse of the crop direction becomes the guidance label; if the answer is preserved, the label is 'Leave it unchanged.' A second augmentation creates 'None of the other options' samples by mismatching rephrased questions (via GPT-4) with semantically irrelevant images. The resulting synthetic dataset is used for LoRA fine-tuning of LLaVA-1.5 (7b/13b) and InstructBLIP-7b, with loss computed only on the answer token and EOS, while a CLIP+linear-probe variant is tested as a non-generative baseline.

## Results

On the Directional Guidance benchmark (821 samples from VizWiz), the best fine-tuned model (LLaVA-1.5-13b, perturbation range 0.1–0.9, no shuffle) achieves 0.63 F1 and 0.63 overall accuracy, with 0.43 accuracy on reframing directions (ACC(F)). This outperforms the GPT-4o zero-shot baseline (0.56 F1, 0.60 ACC, 0.19 ACC(F)) by 3% in overall accuracy and 24 points in reframing-direction accuracy. All fine-tuned open-source models show substantial gains over their zero-shot baselines, with LLaVA-1.5-7b improving from 0.39 F1 to 0.57 F1 and InstructBlip-7b from 0.27 F1 to 0.46 F1.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares fine-tuned open-source VLMs against zero-shot baselines including GPT-4o, GPT-4v, and the models' own zero-shot performance. The fine-tuned LLaVA-1.5-13b (0.63 ACC) surpasses GPT-4o zero-shot (0.60 ACC) by 3% in overall accuracy and dramatically outperforms it on reframing-direction accuracy (0.43 vs. 0.19 ACC(F)), indicating GPT-4o struggles specifically with directional guidance. LLaVA-1.5-7b fine-tuned (0.58 ACC) also exceeds GPT-4v zero-shot (0.59 ACC) on F1 (0.57 vs. 0.52) while matching its overall accuracy. InstructBlip-7b shows the largest relative improvement, jumping from 0.27 to 0.46 F1 (+70% relative), though it remains the weakest fine-tuned model. The CLIP linear-probe baseline (0.32 ACC) confirms that a non-generative architecture without language modeling is insufficient for this task, performing near the 16.6% random-guess level on the six-way classification.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the VQA self-awareness line of work (e.g., MMSAP, VizWiz unanswerable detection) by moving beyond binary answerable/unanswerable classification to a third cognitive level: determining *where* to acquire missing information. It departs from prior directional visual understanding research, which typically requires heavy data collection or auxiliary models, by using a simple bounding-box cropping augmentation on existing VQA datasets with grounding annotations. The data augmentation strategy also builds on the self-knowledge literature for LLMs (negative/unknown data generation via GPT-4), but shifts the perturbation into the visual modality rather than the language modality.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap in assistive technology for visually impaired users: current VLMs can flag an image as unanswerable but cannot tell the user which direction to move the camera, a critical need in iterative VQA interactions like VizWiz and Be My Eyes. Key limitations include the small benchmark (821 samples), the simplification to single-axis directions without magnitude, and the restriction to reframing (excluding exposure, focus, orientation). Open questions include whether the framework generalizes to multi-directional or quantitative guidance, whether it transfers to other VQA domains beyond visually impaired users, and whether the synthetic data approach scales to more complex spatial reasoning tasks.
