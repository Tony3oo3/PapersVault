## Problem

Semantic segmentation and semantic image synthesis are natural reverse problems, yet existing methods treat them with entirely separate architectures (discriminative decoders vs. GANs/diffusion generators). Diffusion-based segmentation models in particular suffer from a fundamental contradiction: the stochastic nature of diffusion outputs conflicts with the deterministic requirement of pixel-level labels, while also incurring high inference costs and offering no reversibility back to images.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20244409/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For segmentation, the model takes a single RGB image (512×512 or 512×1024) and outputs a per-pixel semantic mask; for synthesis, it takes a semantic layout and outputs a photorealistic RGB image. Semantic masks are encoded as 3-channel pseudo masks via an anchor scheme (Eq. 7) with spacing s=50 and division coefficient k=6, mapping category indices to distinct RGB triplets. No text prompts, bounding boxes, or auxiliary image features are used as conditions—both directions rely solely on the image-mask pair, and the same trained model handles both tasks by simply reversing the ODE direction.

## Method

SemFlow builds on the Stable Diffusion 1.5 UNet and VAE, replacing the standard denoising objective with a rectified flow (ODE) training loss that learns straight-line trajectories between the latent distributions of images and pseudo masks. Because the loss (Eq. 10) is time-symmetric, a single trained velocity field v_θ(z_t, t) supports both forward transport (image→mask, i.e., segmentation) and reverse transport (mask→image, i.e., synthesis) by simply flipping the sign of the velocity. To enable multi-modal image generation from a single layout, a finite perturbation (uniform noise with amplitude β < s/2) is added to the pseudo mask before encoding, sampling from a semantic-invariant neighborhood rather than a fixed point. Inference uses the forward Euler method over the ODE, and the straightened trajectories allow competitive results even with a single step (28.3 mIoU on COCO-Stuff), dramatically reducing the inference cost gap with discriminative segmentation models.

## Results

On COCO-Stuff semantic segmentation, SemFlow achieves 38.6 mIoU using only 25 Euler steps, substantially outperforming diffusion-based segmentation models (DSM-DDIM: 16.1, DSM-DDPM: 20.2) while remaining close to the discriminative MaskFormer (41.9 mIoU). On CelebAMask-HQ semantic image synthesis, SemFlow obtains 32.6 FID and 0.393 LPIPS, beating GAN-based baselines pix2pixHD (54.7 FID) and SPADE (42.2 FID) but trailing specialist diffusion/GAN methods SC-GAN (19.2 FID) and BBDM (21.4 FID). A single model handles both tasks by reversing the sign of the learned velocity field, requiring no separate architectures or training runs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

For semantic segmentation, SemFlow (38.6 mIoU) dramatically outperforms both DSM variants (16.1 and 20.2 mIoU) while using only 25 inference steps versus 200, narrowing the gap to the discriminative MaskFormer (41.9 mIoU) to just 3.3 points. For semantic image synthesis on CelebAMask-HQ, SemFlow (32.6 FID, 0.393 LPIPS) clearly surpasses pix2pixHD (54.7 FID, 0.529 LPIPS) and SPADE (42.2 FID, 0.487 LPIPS), but falls short of the specialist methods SC-GAN (19.2 FID) and BBDM (21.4 FID, 0.370 LPIPS). The paper acknowledges this performance gap versus specialist synthesis models but emphasizes that SemFlow is a single unified model handling both tasks simultaneously, whereas CycleGAN (used as a qualitative comparison on Cityscapes) requires two separate unidirectional generators. The ablation in Table 2 further shows SemFlow retains 28.3 mIoU even with a single Euler step, whereas DSMs collapse to 4.0–5.3 mIoU at 5 steps, highlighting the advantage of the straight-trajectory rectified flow formulation.

## Relation to Prior Work

SemFlow builds directly on rectified flow (Liu et al., ICLR 2023) and the Stable Diffusion LDM architecture (Rombach et al., CVPR 2022), repurposing the straight-trajectory ODE framework from image generation to bridge semantic segmentation and image synthesis. It departs from prior diffusion-based segmentation models (DSMs, UniGS, LDMSeg) that treat segmentation as conditional generation from Gaussian noise to a mask, which introduces irreconcilable randomness; instead, SemFlow learns a direct image-to-mask transport. It also contrasts with GAN-based synthesis methods (pix2pixHD, SPADE, SC-GAN) and CycleGAN, which require separate unidirectional generators for each direction, whereas SemFlow's time-symmetric training objective yields both directions from a single velocity field.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unification of segmentation and synthesis into a single reversible ODE is conceptually clean and eliminates the need for task-specific decoders or adversarial training, while the straight trajectories enable competitive segmentation with as few as one Euler step (28.3 mIoU vs. 20.2 for 200-step DSM). However, SemFlow still trails strong discriminative models (38.6 vs. 41.9 mIoU on COCO-Stuff) and is evaluated on only three datasets at 512×512 resolution, leaving open whether the framework scales to larger vocabularies, higher resolutions, or more complex dense prediction tasks. The finite perturbation trick for multi-modal synthesis is a lightweight but effective design choice that could inform other conditional generation settings where semantic invariance must be preserved.
