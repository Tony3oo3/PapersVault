## Problem

Deploying Vision Transformers on heterogeneous devices currently requires training, storing, and switching between multiple independently trained models of different sizes (e.g., ViT-Ti, ViT-S, ViT-B), which is computationally expensive and offers limited granularity. The paper asks whether a single universal ViT can be trained such that reducing the number of attention heads and their corresponding embedding dimension gracefully degrades accuracy, enabling one model to serve a wide spectrum of hardware constraints.

## Input / Output

The model takes a 224×224 RGB image and a runtime-specified number of attention heads k (where k ≤ H, the maximum heads in the universal model). The embedding dimension scales proportionally with k (each head occupies 64 dimensions), so selecting k heads automatically determines the active embedding width, MLP width, and normalization scope. At inference, the user simply specifies k (or a percentage of available hardware), and the model extracts the corresponding subnetwork without loading new weights, enabling dynamic switching mid-stream (e.g., across video frames on a mobile device).

## Method

HydraViT stacks attention heads so that the first k heads and their corresponding 64k embedding dimensions form a valid, self-contained subnetwork at every layer (MHA, NORM, MLP). During training, a stochastic dropout regime samples k from a uniform discrete distribution over {1,…,H} each batch, then performs forward and backward passes only through the first k heads and their associated embedding slices, inducing an importance ordering where earlier heads capture the most significant features. Unlike baselines (DynaBERT, SortedNet) that keep the number of heads fixed while shrinking the embedding, HydraViT couples head count to embedding width so each head always receives the same 64-dimensional information, removing scaling inconsistencies in MHA. The approach also supports weighted subnetwork sampling to bias training toward specific submodels and optional separate classifier heads per subnetwork, all within a single training run that replaces the need to train each ViT variant independently.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K, HydraViT (10 subnetworks, 3–12 heads, trained from DeiT-tiny for 300 epochs) achieves 70.56% top-1 accuracy at 3 heads (1.25 GMACs, 5.7M params), 79.28% at 6 heads (4.6 GMACs, 22.1M params), and 81.10% at 12 heads (17.56 GMACs, 86.6M params). Training for 800 epochs improves these to 71.67%, 80.15%, and 81.60% respectively, approaching the individually trained DeiT-tiny/small/base (72.2%, 79.9%, 81.8%). When restricted to 9–12 heads, HydraViT reaches 82.25% at 12 heads, surpassing MatFormer's 82.04%. Across the full scalability range, HydraViT achieves up to 5 p.p. higher accuracy at the same GMACs and up to 7 p.p. higher accuracy at the same throughput compared to baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HydraViT is compared against three scalable Transformer baselines: MatFormer, DynaBERT, and SortedNet. Against MatFormer, which only flexes the MLP hidden layer and keeps MHA heads fixed, HydraViT 9–12 heads (82.25%) surpasses MatFormer (82.04%) at 12 heads, though MatFormer retains a slight edge within its narrower 3-subnetwork range. Against DynaBERT, which adjusts MHA heads and MLP width but retains the full intermediate embedding dimension, HydraViT achieves substantially lower MACs and parameters at small sizes (1.25G/5.7M vs. 3.43G/22.8M at 192 dim) and higher throughput (10,048 vs. 4,945 img/s), though DynaBERT edges out HydraViT on raw accuracy at the same embedding dimension (73.00% vs. 70.56% at 192 dim). Against SortedNet, which flexes all embedding dimensions but keeps 12 heads fixed, HydraViT achieves 0.3–0.7 p.p. higher accuracy at the smallest subnetworks (e.g., 70.56% vs. 70.20% at 192 dim) and dramatically higher throughput (10,048 vs. 5,898 img/s). Overall, HydraViT delivers the best accuracy-per-GMACs and accuracy-per-throughput curves across the full 10-subnetwork range, with the key advantage being that reducing heads is coupled to reducing embedding dimension, preserving per-head information density and avoiding the scaling inconsistencies present in the baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

HydraViT builds directly on the ViT/DeiT architecture and training recipe, but departs from prior scalable-transformer work (MatFormer, DynaBERT, SortedNet) in a key conceptual way: rather than keeping the number of attention heads fixed while varying embedding widths, it couples the head count to the embedding dimension so that each head always receives the same 64-dimensional slice. This 'stacked heads' formulation draws on Matryoshka Representation Learning and nested dropout ideas but applies them specifically to the MHA structure, with a stochastic tail-drop training regime that orders heads by importance. The paper positions itself as the first approach to adjust every single layer (MHA, MLP, NORM) simultaneously without requiring per-subnetwork fine-tuning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical value is clear: one trained model replaces three (or more) separately trained, tuned, and stored ViT variants, enabling runtime adaptation to fluctuating hardware without model reloading. However, the approach requires substantially more training epochs than individual models to reach comparable accuracy, struggles with from-scratch training at the smallest subnetworks, and has only been validated on DeiT-scale architectures (12 layers, ≤768 dim). Open questions include whether the technique scales to deeper/wider models like DeiT-large, how it performs on actual mobile hardware with memory bandwidth constraints, and whether the stochastic training overhead can be reduced without sacrificing subnetwork quality.
