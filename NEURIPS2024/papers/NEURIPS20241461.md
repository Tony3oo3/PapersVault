## Problem

Existing goal-conditioned RL algorithms rely on Hindsight Experience Replay (HER) to densify sparse rewards and build learning curricula, but HER fundamentally requires the reward to be Markovian—determinable from a single state. For non-Markovian rewards (NMR), where goal achievement depends on sequences of states and actions (e.g., stable UAV velocity holding over multiple steps), HER cannot relabel failed trajectories with meaningful hindsight goals, causing the replay buffer to fill with uninformative failed trajectories and preventing any effective curriculum from forming.

## Input / Output

The policy receives a 9-dimensional state vector (pitch, roll, yaw, flight path azimuth, flight path elevator angle, altitude, roll angular velocity, true airspeed, and the current goal) concatenated with a 3-dimensional continuous goal specification (target speed, elevation angle, azimuth angle) and outputs a 4-dimensional continuous control action (aileron, elevator, rudder, power level). The reward is binary sparse (0 for success, -1 otherwise), and can be configured as either Markovian (single-state check) or non-Markovian (all N_r consecutive states must satisfy the goal tolerance), with N_r=10 in the primary experiments.

## Method

GCPO is an on-policy GCRL framework that replaces HER with two components: (1) Pre-training from Demonstrations, where Behavioral Cloning on expert trajectories gives the policy an initial goal-achieving capability, serving the same purpose as HER's reward densification but in an on-policy-compatible way; and (2) Online Self-Curriculum Learning, which periodically evaluates the policy on N sampled goals, estimates the achieved-goal distribution p_ag via a weighted Gaussian Mixture Model (with exponential decay factor κ on historical weights), and samples progressively harder behavioral goals using MEGA (inverse probability weighting on p_ag) to bias exploration toward under-explored goal regions. The policy is then fine-tuned with KL-regularized PPO (λ=10⁻³), anchoring updates to the pre-trained policy to prevent catastrophic forgetting of acquired skills. This design makes GCPO applicable to both MR and NMR problems without requiring state-space augmentation, and it avoids the replay buffer entirely by operating on-policy.

## Results

On the fixed-wing UAV Velocity Vector Control (VVC) task under non-Markovian reward (NMR), GCPO achieves 45.87% success rate with the base demonstration set D_E^0, outperforming SAC+HER+MEGA (8.32%), BC (17.08%), and GCPO without pre-training (0.04%). With the best demonstration set D_E^3, GCPO reaches 59.90% success rate. On the Reach task under NMR, GCPO achieves 80.26% success rate versus 0.72% for SAC+HER+MEGA, and on PointMaze NMR it reaches 47.50% versus 4.17% for SAC+HER+MEGA. Under Markovian reward settings, GCPO matches SAC+HER+MEGA (both reach 100% on Reach MR).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GCPO decisively outperforms SAC+HER+MEGA on all NMR settings: on VVC (D_E^0), GCPO achieves 45.87% vs. 8.32% (a 5.5× margin); on Reach NMR, 80.26% vs. 0.72%; and on PointMaze NMR, 47.50% vs. 4.17%. This confirms the paper's central claim that HER-based methods fail under non-Markovian rewards because they cannot densify rewards by relabeling single states as hindsight goals. Against BC, GCPO also wins on NMR (45.87% vs. 17.08% on VVC), showing that pre-training alone is insufficient without the self-curriculum mechanism. Under Markovian reward settings, GCPO ties SAC+HER+MEGA on Reach (both 100%) and is slightly behind on PointMaze (93.33% vs. 100%), indicating no degradation when HER is applicable. The ablation showing GCPO without pre-training collapses to 0.04% on VVC NMR highlights that the pre-training component is essential for on-policy learning to function, while the self-curriculum component adds an additional ~8.2% improvement over BC+KL-regularized RL alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

GCPO departs from the dominant HER-based off-policy GCRL paradigm (SAC+HER+MEGA, RIG, DISCERN) by proposing an on-policy framework that replaces hindsight replay with pre-training from demonstrations (Behavioral Cloning) and replaces replay-buffer-based capability estimation with off-policy evaluation via Gaussian Mixture Models. The self-curriculum mechanism draws on MEGA's inverse probability weighting for goal sampling, and the policy optimization uses KL-regularized PPO to prevent catastrophic forgetting of pre-trained skills. Conceptually, GCPO reinterprets HER's two roles—reward densification and curriculum construction—as pre-training and online self-curriculum, respectively, making the framework compatible with non-Markovian rewards where HER is inapplicable.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GCPO addresses a concrete and theoretically grounded limitation of HER (inability to densify non-Markovian rewards), providing a general on-policy GCRL framework validated on a challenging long-horizon multi-goal task (fixed-wing UAV velocity vector control) that prior NMR research had not tackled. Key limitations include reliance on demonstrations for successful training under sparse rewards, use of relatively simple components (BC, GMM) whose alternatives remain unexplored, and the absence of an explicit NMR-handling mechanism in the main implementation (only a basic frame-stacking extension is shown in Appendix F). Open questions include whether integrating state-of-the-art NMR techniques (Reward Machines, Split-MDP) within GCPO would yield further gains, and how the framework scales when demonstration quality or quantity is severely limited.
