## Problem

Standard VAEs parameterize latent variables as continuous Gaussians, which deviates sharply from the discrete, all-or-none spike counts that biological neurons actually produce. This paper asks whether replacing Gaussian latents with Poisson-distributed spike counts—combined with a predictive coding residual parameterization—yields a more biologically faithful generative model that also exhibits desirable computational properties such as sparse coding and reduced posterior collapse.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241425/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes 16×16 whitened natural image patches (van Hateren, CIFAR-10) or 28×28 MNIST digits and produces a reconstructed image plus a K-dimensional vector of non-negative integer spike counts. During training, a temperature parameter T>0 yields a continuous relaxation of the Poisson distribution for gradient flow; at test time T=0 recovers genuine integer Poisson samples. The encoder outputs only the residual rate δr(x), which is multiplied element-wise by a learned prior rate r to form the posterior rate λ = r ⊙ δr, mirroring the feedforward/feedback interaction in cortical circuits.

## Method

The P-VAE replaces Gaussian latents with Poisson-distributed spike counts and introduces a Poisson reparameterization trick (Algorithm 1) that samples exponential inter-event times, computes cumulative arrival times, applies a temperature-controlled sigmoid threshold, and sums to obtain differentiable spike counts. A predictive coding residual parameterization sets the posterior rate as r ⊙ δr(x) (Hadamard product of a learned prior rate and the encoder output), making the KL divergence take the closed form r·f(δr) where f(y)=1−y+y·log y—a metabolic cost term that penalizes firing rates and naturally reduces to the sparse coding L1 penalty when the decoder is linear and the latent space is overcomplete. The full ELBO is derived analytically, and for linear decoders the reconstruction term admits a closed-form expression using only the first and second moments of the Poisson posterior, enabling exact gradient computation to validate the reparameterization. The model is trained with temperature annealing (T from 1.0 to 0.05) and KL annealing, and empirically avoids posterior collapse (~2% dead neurons vs. ~80% for Gaussian VAEs) while learning Gabor-like basis filters comparable to classical sparse coding.

## Results

On MNIST (K=10 latent dim), P-VAE achieves 81.5% KNN accuracy with only N=200 labeled samples, whereas G-VAE requires N=1,000 samples to reach comparable accuracy (~81.3%), demonstrating a 5× sample efficiency advantage. P-VAE also achieves the highest shattering dimensionality (0.797) among all models, indicating superior linear separability of learned representations. On natural image datasets (van Hateren, CIFAR₁₆×₁₆), P-VAE retains ~98% active latent dimensions versus only ~22% for G-VAE and ~19% for L-VAE, largely avoiding posterior collapse. In the sparse coding comparison, P-VAE matches LCA/ISTA sparsity levels (s≈0.94) but shows an amortization gap in reconstruction MSE that is closed when LCA inference is applied to P-VAE's learned dictionary.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against G-VAE (standard Gaussian VAE), P-VAE wins decisively on all metrics: 81.5% vs 67.3% KNN accuracy at N=200 (a 14.2-point gap), higher shattering dimensionality (0.797 vs 0.758), and dramatically fewer dead neurons (98.4% active vs 21.8% on van Hateren linear). Against L-VAE (Laplace VAE), P-VAE again leads on KNN accuracy (81.5% vs 75.7% at N=200) and shattering dim (0.797 vs 0.751), while L-VAE learns Gabor-like filters but with many noisy basis vectors. Against C-VAE (Categorical VAE), P-VAE achieves higher KNN accuracy (81.5% vs 70.5% at N=200) though shattering dimensionality is nearly identical (0.797 vs 0.795). Against traditional sparse coding (LCA and ISTA), P-VAE achieves comparable lifetime sparsity (s=0.94 vs 0.91 for LCA, 0.96 for ISTA) but LCA outperforms P-VAE on reconstruction MSE at matched sparsity, revealing an amortization gap; however, applying LCA inference to P-VAE's learned dictionary nearly closes this gap, confirming the dictionary quality is competitive.

## Relation to Prior Work

The P-VAE extends the standard VAE framework (Kingma & Welling, 2013) by replacing continuous Gaussian latents with Poisson-distributed spike counts, while incorporating the predictive coding principle (Rao & Ballard, 1999) through a multiplicative residual parameterization where posterior rates are prior rates scaled by encoder-predicted deviations. The Poisson reparameterization trick adapts the sigmoid relaxation strategy from Gumbel-Softmax (Jang et al., 2017) to the Poisson setting. Crucially, unlike prior attempts to connect sparse coding and VAEs (Geadah et al., 2024; Tonolini et al., 2020; Xiao et al., 2024) that imposed sparsity-inducing priors or added explicit L1 penalties, the P-VAE's rate penalty emerges naturally from the Poisson KL divergence, requiring only a linear decoder and overcomplete latent space to recover the sparse coding objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The P-VAE provides a principled, biologically grounded generative model where the discrete, non-negative spike-count representation enables direct comparison to neuronal circuits and supports in silico perturbation experiments. The finding that Poisson latents largely avoid posterior collapse (~2% dead neurons vs. ~80% for Gaussian/Laplace VAEs) and yield 5× better sample efficiency in downstream classification suggests that the discrete, rate-penalized structure is not merely a biological curiosity but a practical advantage. Key open questions include closing the amortization gap between P-VAE and traditional sparse coding (LCA/ISTA), extending to hierarchical architectures, and validating whether the Poisson assumption holds at the timescales relevant to higher cortical areas.
