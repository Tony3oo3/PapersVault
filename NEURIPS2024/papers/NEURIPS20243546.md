## Problem

Skill chaining for long-horizon robotic manipulation decomposes complex tasks into pre-trained sub-task skills executed sequentially, but errors accumulate when sub-task skills are imperfectly learned or when environmental disturbances shift states between skills. Existing skill chaining methods (e.g., T-STAR) only enforce uni-directional inter-skill dependencies (aligning terminal states toward the next skill's initial states) while ignoring intra-skill dependencies within each sub-task, leading to brittle chaining under perturbations.

## Input / Output

For IKEA furniture assembly tasks, the 72-dimensional observation comprises 29-dim robot proprioception (joint angles, velocities, gripper state/position/quaternion/velocity), 35-dim object states (positions and quaternions of five furniture pieces), and 8-dim one-hot task phase encoding; the 9-dim action space includes 7-dim joint velocity, 2-dim gripper control, plus a discrete connect action. For Kitchen tasks, the observation is 30-dim (11-dim proprioception + 19-dim object states) with a 9-dim action (7 joint velocity + 2 gripper velocity). Each sub-task operates as a finite-horizon MDP of 150 steps, and the policy is trained with 200 expert demonstrations per sub-task collected via teleoperation or procedural policies.

## Method

SCaR applies dual regularization across two phases. In pre-training, an Adaptive Equilibrium Scheduling (AES) mechanism dynamically balances the RL environmental reward and the least-squares GAIL predicted reward by monitoring the agent's imitation progress via longest-increasing-subsequence (LIS) alignment between agent and expert trajectories, then scheduling the reward weights through a piecewise exponential mapping function—shifting emphasis toward RL when imitation is poor and toward IL when imitation is strong, thereby reinforcing intra-skill dependencies. In fine-tuning, a bi-directional adversarial learning mechanism trains two separate discriminators that simultaneously pull the terminal state set of skill i toward the initial state set of skill i+1 (constraint C1) and pull the initial state set of skill i+1 toward the terminal state set of skill i (constraint C2), establishing a balanced bidirectional mapping that reinforces inter-skill dependencies. The combined objective (Eq. 7) sums the AES-regularized RL+IL reward with a bi-directional regularization reward term, and the fine-tuning iteratively refines both the policies and the bi-directional discriminator using collected initial/terminal state buffers.

## Results

On six IKEA furniture assembly tasks, SCaR achieves an average success rate of 0.94±0.03, outperforming the closest baseline T-STAR (0.85±0.09) by 12%. On two kitchen organization tasks (Kitchen and Extended Kitchen), SCaR reaches 0.78±0.12 average, surpassing T-STAR (0.58±0.10) by 18%. The best individual task performance is 0.98±0.02 on chair_agne and 0.97±0.03 on table_lack. Under perturbations (Table 2), SCaR retains 0.85±0.11 success on chair_bernhard and 0.80±0.13 on chair_ingolf, compared to T-STAR's 0.60±0.08 and 0.59±0.04 respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SCaR outperforms all six baselines across all 8 long-horizon tasks. Against the closest competitor T-STAR (uni-directional terminal state regularization), SCaR gains +12% average on furniture assembly (0.94 vs. 0.85) and +18% on kitchen tasks (0.78 vs. 0.58), with the largest per-task margin on toy_table (+0.21) and Extended Kitchen (+0.25). Policy Sequencing, which only expands initial state sets, trails SCaR by 0.24 on furniture and 0.34 on kitchen averages. Scratch-training baselines (PPO, GAIL, Fixed-RL-IL) and the model-based SkiMo all fail on tasks with 4+ sub-tasks, achieving near-zero success on toy_table and Extended Kitchen, confirming that skill decomposition is essential. Under perturbation tests, SCaR retains 80–85% success while T-STAR drops to ~60% and Policy Sequencing to ~50%, demonstrating that dual regularization (both intra-skill AES and inter-skill bi-directional alignment) provides substantially greater robustness than uni-directional approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

SCaR extends the skill chaining paradigm established by Konidaris & Barto (2009), Policy Sequencing (Clegg et al., 2018), and most directly T-STAR (Lee et al., CoRL 2022), which regularizes terminal states uni-directionally to align sequential skills. The key departure is twofold: (1) introducing bi-directional adversarial regularization that constrains both terminal→next-initial and initial→previous-terminal alignments simultaneously, and (2) replacing fixed RL/IL reward weighting (as in AMP, Peng et al. 2021) with Adaptive Equilibrium Scheduling (AES), which dynamically rebalances environmental and imitation rewards based on measured imitation progress. The AES mechanism itself is adapted from Automatic Discount Scheduling (ADS, Liu et al. 2023), repurposed from observation-based IL to the skill pre-training setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dual regularization framework provides a practical, lightweight recipe for stable skill chaining that outperforms uni-directional baselines by 12–18% on simulation benchmarks and shows meaningful robustness gains under perturbation (80–85% vs. 50–60% success). However, the approach relies on predefined sub-task decompositions, state-space observations (no visual/semantic processing), and the real-world validation is limited to simple tabletop pick-and-place, leaving open whether the framework scales to visually-grounded, contact-rich real-world assembly. A key open question is whether the bi-directional alignment can be learned end-to-end without manually specified sub-task boundaries, and whether the AES scheduling generalizes to tasks with highly variable imitation difficulty across sub-tasks.
