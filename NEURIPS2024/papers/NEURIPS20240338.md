## Problem

While Multiple Instance Learning (MIL) research has extensively studied bag-level learnability, no general theoretical framework exists to determine whether a given Deep MIL algorithm can actually learn at the instance level. Prior work validated instance-level learning only for specific algorithms without providing universal conditions, leaving practitioners without principled guidance on which architectures guarantee reliable instance-level predictions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240338/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The theoretical framework operates on bag domain spaces (independent or general with inter-instance dependencies) and corresponding hypothesis spaces, deriving conditions on risk functions rather than processing raw data. Empirically, the paper constructs synthetic datasets from MNIST where bags of 10 images receive labels based on inter-instance relationships (e.g., 'if the bag contains both 3 and 5, label is 1'), and uses the WebTraffic time-series dataset with per-timepoint ground-truth labels. The output is a theoretical verdict (learnable or not) for each pooling architecture, validated by measuring the performance gap between bag-level and instance-level predictions.

## Method

The paper proposes a PAC learning theory framework that derives necessary and sufficient conditions for instance-level learnability under two domain space assumptions: independent instances (where bag risk must equal the sum of instance risks) and general instances with dependencies (where bag risk must equal a weighted sum of instance risks). It then classifies five Deep MIL pooling techniques—Instance-Pooling, Embedding-Pooling, Attention-Pooling, Additive-Pooling, and Conjunctive-Pooling—against these conditions. The key finding is that Conjunctive-Pooling, which multiplies attention weights on instance predictions rather than on features, is the unique pooling method learnable for instances in the general domain space, while Embedding-Pooling is never learnable and Attention/Additive-Pooling fail because they inject bag-level hypothesis space into individual instance predictions. The framework further shows that RNN-based feature extraction and positional encoding violate a subset condition on hypothesis spaces, rendering models unlearnable for instances even when using Conjunctive-Pooling.

## Results

The paper's central empirical finding is that Conjunctive MIL achieves the smallest gap between bag-level and instance-level prediction performance (−0.1486 Macro-F1, +0.0053 AUROC), confirming its instance-level learnability per Theorem 9. In contrast, Attention-Pooling methods show large bag-to-instance degradation: TransMIL drops by −0.6323 Macro-F1 and −0.4253 AUROC, SA-AbMILP by −0.4352 Macro-F1 and −0.4088 AUROC, and AttentionMIL by −0.5219 Macro-F1. On the WebTraffic dataset, incorporating positional information into features (RNN or PE) degrades instance prediction (AOPCR drops from 13.04 to 9.01 for RNN-All), while using positional info only in attention improves it (AOPCR 17.50 for RNN-Att).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper evaluates nine Deep MIL algorithms across five pooling categories to test instance-level learnability. Instance-Pooling methods (mi-Net, CausalMIL, MIREL) achieve poor bag-level Macro-F1 (0.23–0.36) in the general domain D_XY^Gen, confirming they are not learnable for bags without attention (Theorem 5). Attention-Pooling methods (AttentionMIL, Loss-Attention, SA-AbMILP, TransMIL) achieve strong bag-level performance (Macro-F1 0.75–0.85) but suffer severe instance-level degradation (Macro-F1 drops of 0.34–0.63), confirming they are not learnable for instances (Theorem 8). Additive MIL [9] shows a moderate gap (−0.2456 Macro-F1), also failing instance learnability. Conjunctive MIL [10] is the only method that maintains strong performance at both levels (bag Macro-F1 0.79, instance Macro-F1 0.64; instance AUROC 0.95 even exceeding bag AUROC), uniquely satisfying the conditions for instance-level learnability in both independent and general domain spaces (Theorem 9).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the theoretical analysis of MIL learnability from the bag level (addressed by Blum & Kalai 1998, Sabato & Tishby 2012, Babenko 2008) to the instance level, which the authors identify as a gap in prior work. It builds directly on PAC learning theory (Mohri et al. 2018) and the Azuma-Hoeffding inequality to derive necessary and sufficient conditions for instance-level learnability under independent and general (dependent) bag domain spaces. The paper also provides theoretical grounding for the empirical reproducibility observations of Raff et al. (2024), who showed that Instance-Pooling methods like mi-Net exhibit instance-level reproducibility while Embedding-Pooling methods do not, but could not explain why.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework gives practitioners a concrete design criterion: Conjunctive-Pooling is the unique pooling technique theoretically guaranteed to be instance-learnable in the general (dependent) domain space, while the dominant Attention-Pooling and Additive-Pooling approaches are not. The finding that RNN-based feature extraction and positional encoding—commonly used to capture temporal/spatial dependencies—break instance-level learnability has direct implications for how MIL models should be architected. Limitations include reliance on Assumption 1 (bag-level PAC learnability), experimental validation primarily on synthetic MNIST-based datasets, and the abstract nature of Conditions 4 and 7, which are difficult to verify in practice for complex architectures. Open questions include whether the framework extends to multi-label or hierarchical MIL settings and whether the conditions can be relaxed for approximate instance-level learnability.
