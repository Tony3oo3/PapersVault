## Problem

Existing flow straightening methods for generative modeling either require iterative Flow Matching procedures that accumulate approximation error (Rectified Flow) or rely on minibatch OT heuristics that produce biased, non-guaranteed straight paths (OT-CFM). The paper asks whether one can recover the exact Optimal Transport displacement with perfectly straight trajectories in a single FM step, without iterative refinement or heuristic coupling.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243309/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a source distribution p0, a target distribution p1, and an arbitrary initial transport plan π (independent, minibatch OT, or any other coupling) as inputs, and produces a convex Brenier potential Ψ parametrized by an ICNN whose gradient ∇Ψ is the OT transport map. At inference, transporting a point x0 requires only a single forward pass through the ICNN to compute ∇Ψ(x0) — no ODE integration is needed. In the image-to-image experiment, inputs are 512-dimensional ALAE latent codes of adult faces from FFHQ (1024×1024), and outputs are decoded child face images.

## Method

OFM restricts the standard Flow Matching loss to a class of 'optimal vector fields' — those generated by gradients of convex functions Ψ, which produce straight trajectories by construction. The key theoretical result (Theorem 1) proves that minimizing this restricted FM loss over any transport plan π yields the same minimizer as the dual OT loss, so a single FM iteration recovers the exact quadratic-cost OT map. Practically, Ψ is parametrized by an Input Convex Neural Network; at each training step, the flow map inversion (recovering z0 from the interpolated point xt) is solved as a strongly convex subproblem via LBFGS, and the loss gradient is computed using a closed-form formula involving the Hessian of Ψ. Unlike Rectified Flow, no ODE simulation is needed during training after the first step, and unlike OT-CFM, the choice of initial plan π is theoretically irrelevant to the final solution.

## Results

On the Wasserstein-2 benchmark (L²-UVP, lower is better), OFM with minibatch OT plan achieves 0.15% at D=2 and 2.9% at D=256, outperforming all Flow Matching baselines across every dimension. In particular, at D=256, OFM MB (2.9%) beats OT-CFM (27.5%), c-RF (76.48%), and RF (83.13%) by large margins. On the unpaired Adult→Child image-to-image translation task (FID, lower is better), OFM MB achieves 11.0, outperforming OT-CFM (12.9), c-RF (13.5), and RF (21.0). OFM is competitive with dedicated dual OT solvers (MMv1: 2.6% at D=256; Amortization ICNN: 2.0%), though the MLP-based OT solver (0.59%) remains slightly ahead in the highest dimensions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OFM decisively outperforms all Flow Matching baselines on the Wasserstein-2 benchmark. At D=256, OFM MB (2.9%) is roughly 10× better than OT-CFM (27.5%), ~26× better than c-RF (76.48%), and ~29× better than RF (83.13%); the gap widens with dimensionality. Against dedicated dual OT solvers, OFM is competitive: it slightly trails MMv1 (2.6% vs 2.9% at D=256) and Amortization ICNN (2.0% vs 2.9%), but the MLP-based OT solver (0.59%) remains the strongest overall—though the authors note that using MLPs instead of ICNNs is an empirical trick not theoretically justified, and OFM with MLPs fails to converge. On the I2I translation task, OFM MB achieves the best FID (11.0) among all FM-based methods, beating OT-CFM (12.9) and c-RF (13.5), while RF performs worst (21.0). A key theoretical advantage is that OFM's solution is provably independent of the initial transport plan π, whereas OT-CFM's minibatch OT heuristic introduces bias and RF accumulates error across iterations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Flow Matching framework (Lipman et al., ICLR 2023) and the ICNN parametrization of convex potentials (Amos et al., ICML 2017), but departs from both Rectified Flow (Liu et al., ICLR 2023) and OT-CFM (Tong et al., TMLR 2024) by restricting the FM optimization domain to optimal vector fields—gradients of convex functions—that yield straight trajectories by construction. The key conceptual move is showing (Theorem 1) that this restricted FM loss is equivalent to the dual OT loss up to a plan-dependent constant, thereby unifying the dynamic OT (Benamou-Brenier) and FM perspectives in a single minimization. Unlike iterative RF/c-RF methods that accumulate approximation error and require ODE simulation after the first round, or OT-CFM which relies on a biased minibatch OT heuristic, OFM recovers the exact quadratic-cost OT map in one FM step with any initial transport plan.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The theoretical equivalence between OFM and dual OT provides a clean bridge between two previously separate literatures, and the plan-independence guarantee means practitioners can use the simplest (independent) coupling without sacrificing optimality—a property no prior straightening method offers. At inference, OFM requires no ODE integration since trajectories are exactly linear, which is practically significant for high-throughput sampling. Open questions include whether the approach extends to non-quadratic cost functions, whether ICNN expressiveness limitations (acknowledged by the authors) can be overcome with more expressive convex architectures, and whether the Hessian inversion cost in the gradient computation can be mitigated for very high-dimensional problems.
