## Problem

Score matching, the standard training objective for score-based diffusion models, requires computing a Jacobian trace that is prohibitively expensive in high dimensions. Existing workarounds each carry significant drawbacks: sliced score matching (SSM) and its finite-difference variant suffer from high variance due to random projection, while denoising score matching (DSM) learns an approximation of the true score and forces the SDE drift and diffusion coefficients to be affine, limiting model design flexibility.

## Input / Output

The score network s_θ(x_t', t) takes a perturbed data point x_t' sampled from a Gaussian N(x_0, σ_t² I_d) centered at a training image x_0, and outputs a score vector of the same dimension as the input. For image generation, the model starts from a random noise vector x_T ~ p_T and integrates the reverse SDE to produce RGB images at resolutions ranging from 32×32 (CIFAR-10) to 1024×1024 (CelebA-HQ). A single Monte Carlo sample of x_t' suffices for the expectation in the loss, making each training step a single forward pass through the score network.

## Method

LCSS replaces the intractable Jacobian trace Tr(∇s_θ(x)) in the score matching objective with a computationally cheap inner product s_θ(x')^T · (x'−x)/σ², derived by three steps: (1) adding a local curvature smoothing regularization term ½σ²‖∇s_θ(x)‖_F², (2) recognizing via Kingma and LeCun's lemma that this regularization is equivalent to taking the expectation of the score matching loss over a Gaussian N(x, σ²I) centered at the data point, and (3) applying Stein's identity for the Gaussian to convert the expected trace into the inner product. For SDMs, σ is replaced by a time-varying σ_t (set to the standard deviation of the forward process marginal p_t), and the loss is integrated over t with weight λ(t)=g(t)², which cancels σ_t² in the denominator to avoid numerical instability. Unlike DSM, LCSS learns the true score ∇log p_t(x_t) rather than a denoised approximation, and unlike SSM/FD-SSM it avoids random projection, eliminating the associated high variance. The method imposes no affine constraint on SDE coefficients, opening the door to more flexible forward process designs.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, LCSS achieves FID 4.90/4.72/5.06/4.61, IS 9.88/9.95/9.63/9.80, and BPD 4.17/3.61/2.47/2.58 across NCSN++, NCSN++ deep, DDPM++, and DDPM++ deep respectively, compared to DSM's FID 4.45/4.29/4.81/4.49, IS 9.86/9.86/9.62/9.58, and BPD 3.62/3.38/2.64/2.64. LCSS is the most training-efficient method, completing 1000 iterations on FFHQ in 1075 ms versus 1381 ms (DSM), 1367 ms (FD-SSM), and 1838 ms (SSM). LCSS enables realistic 1024×1024 image generation on CelebA-HQ, a resolution at which SSM and FD-SSM completely fail.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SSM and FD-SSM, LCSS wins decisively: SSM produces only black images after ~65k steps on CelebA (64×64) and ~95k steps on CIFAR-10, while FD-SSM fails entirely on FFHQ (256×256) and degrades to black images on CIFAR-10 at 495k steps; LCSS maintains stable, high-quality generation throughout training. Against DSM, the comparison is nuanced: LCSS slightly outperforms DSM on Inception Score across all four model variants (e.g., 9.95 vs 9.86 for NCSN++ deep) and on BPD for DDPM++ variants (2.47 vs 2.64 for DDPM++, 2.58 vs 2.64 for DDPM++ deep), but underperforms on FID (e.g., 4.72 vs 4.29 for NCSN++ deep) and BPD for NCSN++ variants (4.17 vs 3.62 for NCSN++). The authors conclude there is no decisive quantitative superiority between LCSS and DSM on CIFAR-10, but LCSS demonstrates superior training stability (no sudden quality degradation observed with DSM around 210k iterations) and is the only method besides DSM that handles high-dimensional data (256×256 and 1024×1024) without failure.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three threads: Hyvärinen's score matching objective, Kingma and LeCun's local curvature smoothing regularization (and its equivalence to a Gaussian expectation), and Stein's identity from the Stein discrepancy literature. It departs from the dominant denoising score matching (DSM) paradigm by showing that the Jacobian trace can be bypassed via Stein's identity rather than by learning a denoising approximation, thereby removing the affine constraint on SDE coefficients that DSM implicitly imposes. Unlike SSM and FD-SSM, which use Hutchinson's random projection and suffer from high variance, LCSS avoids random projection entirely, learning the true score rather than an approximation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key open question is whether the freedom from affine SDE constraints actually translates into better generation quality; the paper acknowledges this and leaves the design of non-affine SDEs for future work, using only existing VE and subVP SDEs in all experiments. The quantitative CIFAR-10 results show LCSS is roughly on par with DSM (slightly worse FID, slightly better IS), so the practical advantage is primarily in training stability and the conceptual door opened for flexible SDE design. The 1024×1024 CelebA-HQ generation is a strong demonstration of feasibility, but since it uses standard affine SDEs, it does not yet showcase the method's unique advantage over DSM.
