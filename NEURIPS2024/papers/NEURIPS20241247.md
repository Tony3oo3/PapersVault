## Problem

Prior multi-group online learning algorithms (e.g., Blum & Lykouris 2020, Acharya et al. 2023) require explicit enumeration of the group family G, making them infeasible when G is exponentially large or infinite—as arises in fairness applications with expressive subpopulation definitions. This paper asks whether oracle-efficient algorithms exist that access groups only through an optimization oracle while still achieving sublinear regret on every group simultaneously, across i.i.d., smoothed, and transductive settings.

## Input / Output

The algorithm receives contexts x_t sequentially and has access to two black-box oracles: a (G,H)-optimization oracle that returns a group-hypothesis pair maximizing a weighted loss over past rounds plus perturbations, and an H-optimization oracle that finds the best hypothesis for a singleton dataset. Neither G nor H is ever enumerated; the (G,H)-oracle is called M times per round to build an empirical distribution over G×H, and the H-oracle is called twice per round to identify achievable actions. The output is a binary prediction ŷ_t per round, with the guarantee that expected regret on every group g ∈ G is sublinear in T (or T_g in the transductive setting).

## Method

The algorithm is built on the adversary-moves-first (AMF) framework of Lee et al. 2022, cast as a two-player game between a (G,H)-player and an H-player. The (G,H)-player runs a follow-the-perturbed-leader (FTPL) strategy using the perturbation construction of Block et al. 2022 (fake examples drawn from a base measure B), which implicitly maintains a distribution over G×H; this distribution is sparsely approximated by M oracle calls per round. The H-player then solves a constant-size (two-variable) linear program to choose a Bernoulli parameter p, from which it samples its action—equivalent to the minimax optimal response against the worst-case label. The key technical insight is that FTPL's perturbation randomness provides the implicit distribution over G×H needed by AMF, eliminating the need for multiplicative weights over an explicit enumeration of objectives. In the transductive setting, a generalized FTPL (GFTPL) with a γ-approximable perturbation matrix yields the stronger O(√T_g) regret bound.

## Results

In the σ-smooth setting (Theorem 4.1), the proposed algorithm achieves O(√(dT log T / σ) + αT) expected multi-group regret for all g ∈ G simultaneously, matching the single-group regret of [Blo+22] while extending it to the multi-group case with oracle efficiency in both H and G. In the γ-approximable adversarial setting (Theorem 5.1), the algorithm achieves O(√(T_g) max{γ, log|H||G|, √(N log|H||G|)} + αT) regret per group, with dependence on T_g (group-specific round count) rather than T. In the transductive setting (Corollary 5.1.1), the regret becomes O(N^{1/4}√(T_g log|H||G|) + αT), which is asymptotically smaller than √T whenever T_g = o(√T). All results require only poly(T) calls to the (G,H)-optimization oracle, avoiding explicit enumeration of either H or G.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Compared to [BL20] (Blum & Lykouris, ITCS 2020), the proposed method achieves the same √(T_g log|H||G|) regret in the adversarial setting but reduces computation from O(|H||G|) time and space to poly(T) oracle calls, and is oracle-efficient in both H and G rather than neither. Compared to [Ach+23] (Acharya et al., 2023), which is oracle-efficient in H but still requires explicit enumeration of G (O(|G|) time/space), the proposed method eliminates the need to enumerate G entirely via the joint (G,H)-optimization oracle. In the σ-smooth setting, the paper matches the regret bound of [Blo+22] (Block et al., COLT 2022) — O(√(dT log T / σ)) — but extends it from single-group to multi-group regret for all g ∈ G simultaneously, something [Blo+22] does not handle. Similarly, [Hag+22] (Haghtalab et al., NeurIPS 2022) achieves a slightly weaker σ^{-1/4} dependence but also does not address the multi-group objective. The key differentiator across all comparisons is that this is the first algorithm to be oracle-efficient in both H and G simultaneously, enabling multi-group online learning even when G is infinite.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the online multi-group learning framework of [BL20] and [Ach+23] by introducing a joint (G,H)-optimization oracle that avoids explicit enumeration of either the hypothesis class or the group family, whereas prior work required enumerating at least one. The algorithmic core adapts the adversary-moves-first (AMF) framework of [Lee+22] for multi-objective online learning, replacing the multiplicative-weights distribution over objectives with a sparsely approximated FTPL distribution from [Blo+22], and further extends to the GFTPL framework of [Wan+22] for small-loss regret in the transductive setting. The i.i.d. warm-up repurposes the batch LISTUPDATE algorithm of [GKR22] in an online fashion, connecting the batch multi-group learning literature to the online setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first algorithm achieving multi-group online learning with a potentially infinite group family, providing exponential computational speedups over prior methods even in the finite case. The oracle-efficiency in both H and G simultaneously is a meaningful step toward practical fairness guarantees over intersectional subpopulations, where the number of relevant groups grows exponentially with the number of demographic attributes. The main open question left by the authors is achieving o(T_g) group-specific regret for infinite H and G in fully adversarial settings beyond the smoothed and transductive cases considered here, which would close the gap to the finite-case guarantees of [BL20].
