## Problem

Few-shot 3D Gaussian Splatting fails to learn 3D-consistent scene structure when only a handful of views are available: Gaussian primitives overfit to individual training views, producing 'walls' near cameras and multi-view-inconsistent geometry. Existing remedies relying on monocular depth priors (e.g., DNGaussian) are hampered by the inherent scale ambiguity and cross-view inconsistency of monocular depth, leaving the core structural problem unsolved, especially in complex large scenes.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 3 sparse RGB images with known camera intrinsics/extrinsics, plus a matching prior (ray-to-ray correspondences) extracted by a pre-trained matcher such as GIM, LoFTR, SuperGlue, or DKM. It outputs a hybrid 3D Gaussian scene representation that renders novel views at ~200 FPS (504×378) and simultaneously produces rendered depth maps. Unlike vanilla 3DGS which initializes from SFM points, SCGaussian requires no SFM initialization and instead initializes ray-based primitives directly from the matching rays.

## Method

SCGaussian introduces a hybrid Gaussian representation combining ordinary non-structure primitives (for single-view-visible background) with ray-based primitives whose positions are parameterized as μ' = o + zd along a bound matching ray, restricting optimization to the 1-D ray direction. The key mechanism is a dual optimization: a Gaussian position loss (L_gp) that projects each ray-based primitive into the partner view and penalizes the reprojection error against the matched pixel, forcing primitives to converge to the true surface intersection; and a rendering geometry loss (L_rg) that lifts rendered depth back to 3D and enforces the same reprojection consistency, correcting shape/opacity errors that decouple rendered geometry from primitive positions. A caching-and-filtering strategy in the first 1k iterations guards against mismatched ray pairs, and the full loss combines photometric, position, and geometry terms with scheduled weighting (δ ramps from 0 to 0.3 after 1k iterations).

## Results

On LLFF with 3 training views, SCGaussian achieves PSNR 20.77, SSIM 0.705, and LPIPS 0.218, outperforming the best NeRF-based method (SparseNeRF, PSNR 19.86) and the best 3DGS-based method (FSGS, PSNR 20.43). On the more challenging Tanks and Temples large-scale dataset, SCGaussian reaches PSNR 22.17 and LPIPS 0.257, a 2.16 dB improvement over FSGS (20.01) and 3.58 dB over DNGaussian (18.59). On Blender (surrounding views), SCGaussian achieves the best PSNR of 25.62 and LPIPS of 0.086. Training converges in ~1 minute on a single RTX 3090 with ~3 GB memory, and rendering reaches >200 FPS at 504×378 resolution.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SCGaussian outperforms all 11 baselines across every dataset and metric. Against the closest 3DGS-based competitor FSGS (which uses SFM point initialization), SCGaussian gains +0.34 dB PSNR on LLFF, +1.75 dB on IBRNet, +2.16 dB on T&T, and +0.98 dB on Blender, while also achieving lower LPIPS in all cases. Against DNGaussian (monocular depth regularization), the advantage is even larger: +1.65 dB on LLFF, +2.58 dB on IBRNet, and +3.58 dB on T&T, confirming that matching priors are more effective than monocular depth for enforcing 3D consistency. Among NeRF-based methods, SCGaussian surpasses the best (SparseNeRF) by +0.91 dB on LLFF and +1.69 dB on IBRNet, while also being orders of magnitude faster (~1 min vs. ~10 hours training). Notably, even when compared against structured 3DGS variants initialized with triangulation points (ScaffoldGS: 19.41, OctreeGS: 19.61 on LLFF), SCGaussian's learned ray-based representation still achieves the best PSNR of 20.77 without requiring any explicit 3D point initialization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) to the few-shot novel view synthesis setting by introducing a hybrid Gaussian representation that combines ordinary non-structure primitives with ray-based primitives bound to matching rays. It departs from prior few-shot 3DGS methods (FSGS, DNGaussian) that rely on monocular depth priors or SFM point initialization, instead exploiting matching priors from pre-trained feature matchers (GIM, LoFTR, SuperGlue) to directly constrain Gaussian positions along rays. The dual optimization strategy—simultaneously optimizing rendering geometry and Gaussian primitive positions—addresses the attribute interdependence problem identified in vanilla 3DGS, where the model tends to inflate Gaussian size rather than correct position.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SCGaussian provides a practical, highly efficient (1-minute training, 200+ FPS rendering) solution to few-shot NVS that avoids the scale and multi-view inconsistency problems inherent to monocular depth priors, which is particularly valuable for complex large scenes where depth estimation is unreliable. The finding that vanilla 3DGS can outperform DNGaussian on Tanks and Temples (Table 9) highlights a useful negative result: monocular depth regularization can actively harm reconstruction in unbounded scenes. The main limitation is the requirement for accurate camera poses, and an open question is whether the matching-prior-based structure constraint can be extended to pose-free or dynamic settings.
