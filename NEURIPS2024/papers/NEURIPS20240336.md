## Problem

Model growth methods for accelerating LLM pre-training have been evaluated almost exclusively on encoder-based BERT models, leaving their viability for decoder-only LLMs untested at scale. Three critical gaps remain: no comprehensive standardized comparison across growth operators on LLMs, no evidence that acceleration persists at 7B parameter sizes or beyond 750B training tokens, and no empirical guidelines for practitioners to choose growth timing and growth factor under a fixed compute budget.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240336/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a small base LLM (e.g., 400M parameters, 6 layers) already trained on d tokens from SlimPajama-627B, along with a growth factor g (typically 4) and a target training budget D tokens. The output is a deeper target LLM (e.g., 1.1B/24 layers, 3B/32 layers, or 7B/32 layers) whose layers are formed by concatenating g copies of the base model's layer stack, which then undergoes continued self-supervised pre-training on the same corpus. The growth is strictly depthwise (layer-wise), not widthwise, and the embedding and output head are shared across all stacked copies.

## Method

The paper categorizes all existing model growth techniques into four atomic operators—G_direct (copy/split existing parameters), G_learn (learn a linear mapping), G_zero (zero-initialize new params), and G_random (randomly initialize new params)—each applicable in depthwise or widthwise directions, and systematically evaluates them on a standardized 400M→1.1B Llama-like pre-training testbed. The key finding is that the simplest depthwise operator, G_stack (directly duplicating the entire small model's layers g times, violating function preservation), consistently outperforms all other operators in training loss, speedup (up to 54.6% at 7B/300B tokens), and eight Harness NLP benchmarks. The authors further demonstrate scalability by training up to 7B models with 300B tokens and a 410M model with 750B tokens, fitting a scaling law that shows G_stack achieves the same target loss at substantially lower FLOPs than scratch training. For practical deployment, they derive a logarithmic equation log₁₀(d) = 0.88·log₁₀(N) + 163.27/log₁₀(C) − 5.74 to determine optimal growth timing d given model size N and compute budget C, and recommend a constant growth factor g=4.

## Results

On the primary 1.1B LLM evaluation (100B tokens, SlimPajama), depthwise stacking G_stack achieves an average accuracy of 52.80 across seven NLP benchmarks and a training loss of 2.151, yielding a 49.1% speedup over the scratch baseline (50.12 avg, 2.204 loss). At 7B scale with 300B tokens, G_stack converges to the same loss as a scratch-trained 7B model using only 194B tokens, a 54.6% speedup. The 410M model trained with 750B tokens still shows 31.0% acceleration at 700B tokens, and the fitted loss-difference curve predicts sustained gains beyond 8T tokens.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against StackedBERT's gradual stacking [14], the paper's one-hop G_stack achieves 2.4 points higher average accuracy and 0.6 lower Wikitext PPL after 100B tokens of large-model training, confirming that a single stacking step outperforms iterative train-stack-train-stack. Compared to Pythia-1B [51] (trained on Pile-300B for 100B tokens), G_stack-1.1B (SlimPajama, 100B tokens) leads by 2.27 points in average accuracy (52.80 vs. 50.53), with particularly large margins on ARC-e (+7.0) and SciQ (+3.8). Against TinyLlama-1.1B [45] (103B tokens), G_stack-1.1B also wins on average accuracy (52.80 vs. ~50.1 for the scratch baseline at the same token count). At 7B scale, Gstack-7B outperforms Pythia-6.9B, OLMo-7B [53], and Amber-7B [54] on standard NLP benchmarks at ~130B tokens, demonstrating that the stacking advantage persists at larger model sizes. All widthwise growth operators (G_direct→, G_zero→, G_random→, G_learn→) fail to beat the scratch baseline (negative speedup), while all depthwise operators except G_random↑ achieve positive speedup, with G_stack consistently leading.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies the diverse model growth literature (Net2Net, StackedBERT, Bert2Bert, Lemon, LiGO, MSG) into four atomic operators and repositions them for decoder-only LLM pre-training, where prior work was almost exclusively evaluated on BERT-scale encoders. It departs from the field's foundational assumption of function preservation by showing that the simplest FP-violating operator—direct depthwise stacking (G_stack)—outperforms all FP-preserving alternatives. The work also extends the evaluation regime from preliminary LLM experiments (e.g., LiGO's 15B-token GPT-2) to 7B models trained on 300B+ tokens, and provides the first empirical guidelines (analogous to scaling laws) for choosing growth timing and growth factor.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

A 54.6% pre-training speedup at 7B scale with concrete, equation-based guidelines makes G_stack immediately actionable for practitioners training LLMs, particularly in the 'overtraining' regime (e.g., Llama-3-8B at 15T tokens) where the paper predicts the advantage persists. The conceptual finding that function preservation—a principle underpinning most growth methods since Net2Net—is not necessary (and may even be slightly harmful) opens a new research direction decoupling 'accelerating larger-model training' from 'preserving the smaller model's function.' Key open questions include the lack of theoretical explanation for why stacking works, the limited exploration of growth factors (only g=4 recommended due to compute constraints), and whether the approach composes with other efficiency techniques like dynamic learning-rate scheduling during continual pre-training.
