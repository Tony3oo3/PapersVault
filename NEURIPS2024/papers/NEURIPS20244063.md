## Problem

Existing Temporal Domain Generalization (TDG) methods assume domains are collected at fixed, discrete time intervals, which fails to capture the continuous, irregular dynamics of real-world data (e.g., disaster events, healthcare episodes, political occurrences). This paper formalizes Continuous Temporal Domain Generalization (CTDG), where both seen and unseen domains reside at arbitrary continuous time points, and tackles three coupled challenges: characterizing continuous data-to-model dynamics, learning high-dimensional nonlinear parameter evolution, and jointly optimizing the model with inductive-bias-driven control over its generalization behavior.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a sequence of labeled data domains {D(t₁), …, D(t_T)} collected at arbitrary positive real timestamps (not fixed intervals), along with a target time s ∉ {t₁, …, t_T}. It outputs the full predictive model parameter vector θ(s) at the requested future moment, which is then used to produce class labels or regression values on unseen data at time s. A key structural detail is that the Koopman operator K and the encoder/decoder (φ, φ⁻¹) are also learned outputs, enabling post-hoc spectral analysis and constraint injection (e.g., forcing purely imaginary eigenvalues for periodic dynamics) without retraining the predictive model.

## Method

Koodos models the continuous evolution of predictive model parameters θ(t) as an ODE, then linearizes this nonlinear dynamics via Koopman theory: an autoencoder maps θ into a low-dimensional latent space z = φ(θ) where a finite-dimensional Koopman operator K governs linear dynamics dz/dt = Kz. Training jointly optimizes five loss terms—per-domain prediction error, Koopman-space reconstruction, dynamic fidelity (Koopman-space trajectory matching), parameter-space consistency, and integral prediction error—using gradient descent with Neural ODE solvers for the time integrals. At inference, the model integrates in Koopman space from the nearest observed timestamp to the target time, then decodes back to parameter space. A distinguishing feature is the ability to impose structural constraints on K (e.g., K = B − Bᵀ for periodicity, or low-rank K = UVᵀ for dimensionality control), translating human prior knowledge directly into the generalization trajectory without modifying the predictive architecture.

## Results

On the six continuous temporal domain datasets, Koodos achieves the best performance across all tasks: 2.8% error on 2-Moons (vs. 12.8% for the next-best V-REx), 4.6% error on Rot-MNIST (vs. 8.3% for CIDA), 0.71 AUC on Twitter (vs. 0.64 for DeepODE), 6.6% error on Yearbook (vs. 8.3% for IRM), 16.4 MAE on Cyclone (vs. 17.0 for CIDA), and 9.0 MAE on House (vs. 9.8 for DRAIN). The gains are most dramatic on synthetic datasets with continuous concept drift (18°/s rotation), where discrete TDG methods like DRAIN (53.2% on 2-Moons) and TKNets (39.6%) fail catastrophically. On the discrete TDG benchmark (Table 4), Koodos also leads all baselines, e.g., 1.3% error on 2-Moons vs. 3.2% for DRAIN and 4.8 MAE on Appliance vs. 6.4 for DRAIN.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Koodos outperforms all seven baseline categories across every dataset. Against discrete TDG methods, the gap is largest on synthetic data: DRAIN (53.2%) and TKNets (39.6%) are far behind Koodos (2.8%) on 2-Moons, and DRAIN-Δt (46.2%) and DeepODE (17.8%) trail on Rot-MNIST, confirming that discrete-step or naive ODE approaches cannot capture continuous concept drift. Among the strongest baselines, CIDA (8.3% on Rot-MNIST, 17.0 MAE on Cyclone) and V-REx (12.8% on 2-Moons, 17.7 MAE on Cyclone) are the closest competitors but still lag Koodos by 3.7–10 percentage points on classification and 0.6–1.3 MAE on regression. On the Twitter AUC task, Koodos (0.71) surpasses the next-best CIDA (0.63) by 0.08, a meaningful margin for an imbalanced classification problem. On the discrete TDG benchmark (Table 4), Koodos also leads DRAIN (3.2% vs. 1.3% on 2-Moons; 6.4 vs. 4.8 MAE on Appliance) and GI (3.5% on 2-Moons), demonstrating that the continuous formulation generalizes well even when time is regular.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Temporal Domain Generalization (TDG) line of work (DRAIN, TKNets, CIDA, LSSAE) from discrete, fixed-interval time points to a continuous-time setting where domains are observed at arbitrary, irregular timestamps. Methodologically, it combines Neural ODEs (Chen et al., 2018) for continuous-time state evolution with Koopman operator theory (Koopman, 1931; Brunton et al., 2021) to linearize the high-dimensional nonlinear dynamics of model parameters into a low-dimensional invariant subspace. The key conceptual departure from prior TDG methods is the use of topological conjugation to explicitly synchronize data distribution drift with model parameter dynamics, rather than treating domain transitions as discrete probabilistic steps.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CTDG captures a practically important and previously unaddressed setting where data is collected at irregular, event-triggered times (disasters, medical episodes, political events), making it relevant beyond the fixed-interval assumption of all prior TDG work. The Koopman operator provides a unique interface for spectral analysis and inductive-bias control of the generalization process (e.g., constraining eigenvalues to the imaginary axis to enforce periodicity), which is a capability absent in prior TDG methods. However, the framework assumes smooth, predictable concept drift, limiting its applicability to abrupt or stochastic distribution shifts, and the OOM issues when bypassing the Koopman space on larger models raise scalability questions for very deep architectures.
