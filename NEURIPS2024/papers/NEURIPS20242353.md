## Problem

Neural network pruning has developed along two largely orthogonal directions—differentiable importance scoring and combinatorial optimization over sparse supports—without a unified theoretical framework connecting them. The paper asks how these two approaches can work in concert for structured (block) sparsification, and provides both a theoretical unification showing that popular differentiable pruning techniques are equivalent to nonconvex group regularization, and a practical algorithm that leverages this connection.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242353/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes dense weight matrices from a trained neural network (e.g., ResNet50 convolutional kernels reshaped from 4D H×W×C_in×C_out tensors to 2D HWC_in×C_out matrices, or fully connected layers in a Criteo MLP) and produces block-sparse approximations where nonzero entries are confined to B×B blocks at a target sparsity p. Block sizes range from 8×8 to 64×64 (ImageNet) and 5 to 20 (Criteo), with sparsities from 58% to 99%. During training, each block is additionally scaled by a learned softmax attention mask, so the effective output is a block-sparse matrix whose surviving blocks are selected by the attention scores rather than raw magnitudes.

## Method

SequentialAttention++ fuses Sequential Attention (a softmax mask trained jointly with network weights) with the ACDC alternating compressed/decompressed phase structure, replacing magnitude-based block selection with attention-score-based selection. The key architectural change is that each weight block is multiplied by a per-block softmax attention score A_i = nm·exp(L_i)/Σexp(L_j), and a new SPARSIFICATION phase with an exponential pruning schedule (sparsity_t = s·(1−e^{−ct})/(1−e^{−c}), c=4) is inserted between DENSE and SPARSE phases to gradually prune low-attention blocks rather than abruptly thresholding. Theoretically, the paper proves that unnormalized softmax masking, ℓ₁-regularized masks, and group powerpropagation are all equivalent to specific nonconvex group regularizers (log-sum, ℓ_{2/3}, and group LASSO respectively), and establishes that for a broad class of strictly increasing subadditive regularizers q, the nonconvex-regularized objective shares a unique 1-sparse global minimum with the corresponding group LASSO problem, which is supported on the block maximizing the gradient ℓ₂ norm—yielding provable guarantees for a local search (OMPR) algorithm.

## Results

On ImageNet (ResNet50), SequentialAttention++ outperforms ACDC across all tested block sizes (8×8 to 64×64) and sparsities (58%–95%), with the dense baseline at 76.90% validation accuracy. On Criteo, SequentialAttention++ achieves the lowest validation loss across all sparsity levels (90%–99%) and block sizes (5, 10, 20), with the dense baseline at 0.4489. The advantage over ACDC grows with block size and sparsity: at 99% sparsity with 20×20 blocks, SequentialAttention++ reaches 0.5253 validation loss versus 0.5427 for ACDC and 0.5482 for magnitude pruning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SequentialAttention++ is compared against three baselines: magnitude pruning (Frobenius-norm block selection), a block generalization of Powerpropagation (squaring weights to encourage sparsity), and ACDC (alternating compressed/decompressed training with iterative hard thresholding). On Criteo, SequentialAttention++ wins across all 15 sparsity–block-size configurations, with the largest margins at high sparsity and large blocks (e.g., 0.5253 vs. 0.5427 for ACDC at 99% sparsity, 20×20 blocks — a 0.0174 gap). On ImageNet, SequentialAttention++ also outperforms ACDC at every tested setting, with gains of up to ~3+ percentage points in validation accuracy at 64×64 block sizes. Magnitude pruning and Powerpropagation are consistently the weakest baselines, particularly at larger block sizes where their importance scores degrade most. The paper notes that SequentialAttention++ introduces one extra trainable parameter per block (the attention logit), which is negligible for block sizes ≥8 but could double parameters at block size 1.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously orthogonal lines of pruning research: differentiable pruning (importance scoring via learned masks, e.g., Sequential Attention's softmax parameterization) and combinatorial optimization (iterative sparse/dense phase alternation, e.g., ACDC's IHT-based local search). Theoretically, it shows that a wide class of differentiable pruning techniques—unnormalized softmax, L1-regularized masks, and powerpropagation—all reduce to nonconvex regularization that generalizes group LASSO, and proves that for strictly increasing subadditive regularizers, the unique sparse global minimum coincides with the group LASSO solution. Empirically, SequentialAttention++ fuses Sequential Attention's softmax mask with ACDC's alternating phases, adding a novel SPARSIFICATION phase with an exponential pruning schedule to smooth the dense-to-sparse transition.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The theoretical unification provides the first principled explanation for why diverse differentiable pruning heuristics work: they all identify the same 1-sparse group LASSO solution, connecting them to orthogonal matching pursuit with provable guarantees under restricted strong convexity. The unique sparse global minimum result for nonconvex regularizers is of independent interest in nonconvex optimization. However, the theory assumes convex objectives, which limits direct applicability to deep networks, and the paper acknowledges open questions about characterizing local minima and whether nonconvex regularization provably outperforms LASSO in optimization dynamics. Practically, the algorithm consistently outperforms ACDC across block sizes and sparsities on ImageNet and Criteo, with gains widening at larger blocks and higher sparsity, though it introduces one extra trainable parameter per block.
