## Problem

Differentially private subspace estimation has a known polynomial dependency on the ambient dimension d for worst-case inputs, but prior work never provided a proper measure of how 'easy' a specific dataset is for this task. While exact k-subspace instances can be recovered with dimension-independent sample complexity, no smooth, distribution-free criterion existed to quantify when the dimension dependency can be eliminated for approximately low-dimensional data. This paper introduces the first such measures based on multiplicative singular-value gaps and establishes matching upper and lower bounds.

## Input / Output

The mechanism receives n unit-norm points in R^d, a rank parameter k, an easiness parameter γ bounding the relevant singular-value gap, and privacy parameters (ε,δ), and outputs a rank-k projection matrix Π that is λγ-useful (i.e., its Frobenius-norm squared projection of the data is within λγ·n of the optimal k-rank projection). Two distinct easiness predicates are defined: a weak one requiring √(Σ_{i>k} σ_i²) ≤ γσ_k (aggregate tail energy) and a strong one requiring σ_{k+1} ≤ γσ_k (single adjacent gap). The analysis assumes σ_k² ≥ 0.01·n/k, ensuring at least k significant directions exist in the data.

## Method

The algorithm follows a sample-and-aggregate paradigm: it randomly partitions the n rows into t subsets of size Õ(k), computes the non-private top-k projection matrix for each subset, and then privately aggregates them. The key technical insight (Lemma 2.1) is that under multiplicative singular-value gaps, a uniformly random subset of size Õ(k) induces a projection matrix that is close (in spectral or Frobenius norm) to the full matrix's top-k projection with high probability, enabling the aggregation step. Two aggregation strategies are provided: a naive one that treats each d×d projection as a d²-dimensional vector and applies FriendlyCore DP averaging, and a more efficient one that samples q=Õ(k) Gaussian reference points, privately averages their projected coordinates via FriendlyCore (resilient to a constant fraction of outliers), and recovers the projection from the averaged matrix. For lower bounds, the paper combines the Fingerprinting Code framework of Peter et al. [2024] with a padding-and-permuting construction that plants the FPC codebook inside one of k independent near-rank-1 blocks, then extracts a strongly-correlated vector from any useful projection matrix using the shared random permutation and block index.

## Results

The paper establishes the first upper and lower bounds for differentially private subspace estimation parameterized by multiplicative singular-value gaps. For weak estimators (gap measured by √(Σ_{i>k} σᵢ²)/σₖ), the sample complexity is Õ_{ε,δ}(k + k²√d/λ) upper and Ω̃(√(kd)/λ) lower. For strong estimators (gap measured by σ_{k+1}/σₖ), the bounds are Õ_{ε,δ}(k + k³d/λ²) upper and Ω̃(k√d/λ) lower. In the special case of computing a 0.001-useful projection, the weak-estimator bounds become Õ_{ε,δ}(k + γ₁k²√d) vs. Ω̃(γ₁√(kd)), and the strong-estimator bounds become Õ_{ε,δ}(k + γ₂²k³d) vs. Ω̃(γ₂k√d), where γ₁ and γ₂ are the respective easiness parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's main theoretical comparison is against the additive-gap-based approach of Dwork et al. [2014], which requires n = Õ_{ε,δ}(k√d/(σₖ² − σ_{k+1}²)) points and inherently retains a polynomial dependency on the ambient dimension d even for the easiest instances (where points exactly lie in a k-dimensional subspace). In contrast, the new multiplicative-gap-based estimators can achieve dimension-independent sample complexity when the gap is sufficiently small (e.g., σ_{k+1}/σₖ ≤ Õ(1/√d) for strong estimators with small k). Empirically, on the task of private mean estimation of points close to a k-dimensional subspace (d = 10⁴, k = 4, n = 250k), EstSubspace eliminates the √d dependency that Dwork et al. [2014]'s algorithm exhibits as d grows, while both methods perform similarly when d is fixed and k varies. The advantage of EstSubspace is most pronounced in high-dimensional regimes (large d) where the data is very close to a low-dimensional structure (large τ), as shown in the third panel of Figure 2.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the DP subspace estimation line initiated by Dwork et al. [2014] (who established the fundamental Õ(k√d) lower bound and additive-gap-based algorithms) and Singhal and Steinke [2021] (who handled exact k-subspace recovery and introduced distributional stability for the approximate case). The key conceptual departure is introducing multiplicative singular-value gaps as input-dependent 'easiness' measures, which prior work had not properly addressed—additive gaps inherently retain polynomial d-dependency even for trivially easy instances. For the lower bounds, the paper adapts the Fingerprinting Code framework of Peter et al. [2024] (padding-and-permuting technique) to the multi-dimensional subspace setting, and for the upper bounds it replaces the histogram-based aggregation of Singhal and Steinke with the more practical FriendlyCore averaging of Tsfadia et al. [2022].

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to identify which types of singular-value gaps are sufficient and necessary for achieving dimension-independent sample complexity in DP subspace estimation, providing a clean theoretical answer to when the curse of dimensionality can be avoided. The practical implication for DP-SGD is significant: if gradients are sufficiently close to a low-dimensional subspace (as empirical evidence suggests for some architectures), the ambient dimension cost can be eliminated. However, substantial gaps remain between upper and lower bounds (k^1.5 for weak estimators, rate mismatch for strong estimators), and the method requires data to be very close to a k-dimensional structure, which may not hold in typical deep learning training. Open questions include whether the projection-matrix estimation approach can be replaced by direct α-usefulness optimization to close the k-dependency gap, and whether network architecture can be leveraged to boost gradient subspace concentration.
