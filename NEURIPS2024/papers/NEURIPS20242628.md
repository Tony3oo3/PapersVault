## Problem

Existing multiple clustering methods produce multiple partitions but require users to manually identify which clustering matches their specific interest (e.g., color vs. species). The closest prior work, Multi-MaP, uses CLIP to align user interests with visual data but demands a contrastive concept from the user and separates representation learning from clustering into two disconnected stages, yielding sub-optimal results.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242628/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an unlabeled image dataset, a single high-level user preference string (e.g., 'color', 'species'), and K reference words auto-generated by GPT-4 as common categories under that concept. It produces end-to-end cluster assignments tailored to the user's interest, along with per-image proxy word embeddings (linear combinations of reference word token embeddings) and refined image embeddings from a partially trained CLIP projection layer. No ground-truth labels, contrastive concepts, or manual cluster selection are required at inference time.

## Method

Multi-Sub operates in two alternating phases. In Phase I (Proxy Learning and Alignment), a trainable latent factor p_i for each image computes softmax weights over GPT-4-generated reference word embeddings, forming a proxy word w_i as their linear combination; the CLIP image encoder's projection layer is then trained (all other parameters frozen) to maximize alignment between the image embedding and the prompt embedding 'a fruit with the color of *' via a contrastive loss. In Phase II (Clustering), pseudo-labels are assigned by matching each proxy word to its nearest reference word, and the projection layer is further refined using an intra-cluster compactness loss and a margin-based inter-cluster hinge loss over concatenated [w_i, x_i] representations. The two phases iterate (100 epochs Phase I, 10 epochs Phase II) until convergence, jointly optimizing both the user-specific subspace representation and the clustering objective in an end-to-end fashion.

## Results

Multi-Sub achieves the best NMI and RI across all 16 dataset-clustering combinations in Table 2, consistently outperforming the strongest baseline (Multi-MaP) by 1–10 NMI points. Headline results include NMI 0.9693 / RI 0.9964 on Fruit-Color (vs. DDMC's 0.8973/0.9383), NMI 0.7441 / RI 0.9834 on CMUface-Identity (vs. Multi-MaP's 0.6625/0.9496), and NMI 0.5271 / RI 0.7394 on CIFAR-10-Type (vs. Multi-MaP's 0.4969/0.7104). On Fruit-Species, Multi-Sub achieves perfect NMI 1.0000, matching Multi-MaP. The method also surpasses zero-shot CLIP with ground-truth labels (CLIP_label) in most settings (Table 3), demonstrating that learned proxy word embeddings capture user-specific aspects better than direct label-based classification.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Multi-Sub wins on all 16 dataset-clustering combinations against all seven clustering baselines. Against the closest competitor Multi-MaP (Yao et al., CVPR 2024), Multi-Sub improves NMI by 0.04–0.11 on most tasks (e.g., 0.9693 vs. 0.8619 on Fruit-Color; 0.7441 vs. 0.6625 on CMUface-Identity), and ties on Fruit-Species (both 1.0000). Against the strongest deep baseline DDMC (Yao & Hu, SDM 2024), Multi-Sub gains 0.07–0.17 NMI (e.g., 0.6654 vs. 0.4981 on Fruit360-Color; 0.7533 vs. 0.6899 on Stanford Cars-Color). Traditional baselines MSC (Hu et al., 2017) and MCV (Guérin & Boots, 2018) are outperformed by large margins (0.2–0.9 NMI) due to their reliance on handcrafted features. Among deep baselines, ENRC (Miklautz et al., 2020), iMClusts (Ren et al., 2022), and AugDMC (Yao et al., 2023) all trail Multi-Sub by 0.1–0.5 NMI. In the text-encoder ablation (Table 4), ALIGN (Jia et al., 2021) slightly outperforms CLIP on some tasks (e.g., Fruit360-Color: 0.7031 vs. 0.6654), while BLIP-2 (Li et al., 2023) is generally competitive but weaker on identity and pose tasks, indicating that encoder choice matters but does not change the overall ranking of Multi-Sub as the top method.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the multiple clustering literature by introducing user-personalized clustering, building directly on Multi-MaP (Yao et al., CVPR 2024), which first used CLIP to align user textual interests with visual representations. The key departures are: (1) eliminating Multi-MaP's requirement for a contrastive concept by instead using GPT-4-generated reference words as subspace basis vectors, and (2) unifying representation learning and clustering into an end-to-end iterative process rather than the two-stage pipeline used by Multi-MaP and most prior deep multiple clustering methods (ENRC, iMClusts, AugDMC, DDMC). The subspace proxy learning mechanism draws on the broader CLIP-based prompt learning literature (CoOp, InMaP) but repurposes it for the unsupervised, label-free setting of clustering.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap in multiple clustering: users must manually inspect multiple clusterings to find the one matching their application need, and this work automates that selection via a single high-level keyword. The end-to-end formulation (alternating proxy learning and clustering refinement) is a meaningful architectural improvement over the two-stage paradigm dominant in the field. However, the approach is constrained by its reliance on GPT-4 for reference word generation (introducing potential biases), the small scale of available multiple clustering datasets (largest is 60K CIFAR-10 images), and the assumption that a user's interest can be captured by a single high-level concept. Open questions include whether the method generalizes to non-visual data, more abstract or compositional user interests, and whether the GPT-4 dependency can be replaced by lighter-weight alternatives.
