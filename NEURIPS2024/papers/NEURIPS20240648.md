## Problem

Prior work on online DR-submodular maximization with long-term constraints (Raut et al. 2021, Sadeghi et al. 2020, Feng et al. 2022) all require first-order full-information feedback with √T gradient queries per round and assume adversarial or deterministic utility functions. This paper is the first to study the stochastic setting where only the expected utility is DR-submodular, combined with semi-bandit feedback where only the gradient at the chosen action is observed, reducing per-round query complexity from √T to 1.

## Input / Output

At each round t, the algorithm commits to an action x_t in a known convex set K ⊂ R^d_+, then observes a stochastic reward f_t(x_t), an unbiased gradient estimate ∇̃f_t(x_t) (semi-bandit) or ∇̃f_t(·) at any point (full-information), and a cost vector p_t drawn i.i.d. from a bounded distribution. The long-term constraint is linear: the cumulative cost Σ⟨p_t, x_t⟩ must not exceed a fixed budget B_T = bT. The algorithm outputs the action sequence {x_t} and a dual variable λ_t that adaptively penalizes constraint violation, achieving O(√T) α-regret and O(T^{3/4}) total constraint violation with high probability.

## Method

The proposed OLSGA algorithm is a primal-dual stochastic gradient ascent method built on a regularized Lagrangian L(x,λ) = f(x) − λg(x) + (δη/2)λ², where the quadratic term controls dual variable growth without requiring a bounded domain. Since the true mean cost vector p is unknown, the algorithm substitutes an empirical estimate p̂_t and adds a high-probability correction γ_t = √(2C²log(2T/ε)/t) to the constraint. For semi-bandit feedback, the primal update uses the 1/2-approximation property of DR-submodular functions (f(y) − 2f(x) ≤ ⟨∇f(x), y−x⟩), yielding 1/2-regret. For full-information feedback, the algorithm instead uses the non-oblivious function F (defined by ∇F(x) = ∫₀¹ e^{z−1}∇f(zx)dz) with an unbiased sampling-based gradient estimator, achieving the optimal (1−1/e)-regret. Both variants require only 1 gradient query per round and do not assume bounded smoothness or f(0)=0, unlike prior Frank-Wolfe-based approaches.

## Results

Under semi-bandit feedback (Theorem 1), the proposed OLSGA algorithm achieves O(√T) 1/2-regret and O(T^{3/4}) constraint violation with high probability, requiring only 1 gradient query per round. Under first-order full-information feedback (Theorem 2), the algorithm achieves O(√T) (1−1/e)-regret and O(T^{3/4}) constraint violation with high probability, also with just 1 gradient query per round. Both results hold over a general convex set (not requiring 0 ∈ K) and accommodate stochastic gradient noise, with the step size η = d/(U√T) and regularization δ = 8β² (semi-bandit) or δ = 4β² (full-information).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary advantage over all three baselines (Raut et al. [29], Sadeghi et al. [31], Feng et al. [10]) is a dramatic reduction in per-round query complexity from √T (or 2√T for [31]) gradient evaluations down to just 1, while maintaining the same O(√T) regret bound. All prior works require the constraint set to contain the origin (0 ∈ K) and assume exact (noise-free) gradients; this paper handles general convex sets and stochastic gradient noise simultaneously. On regret, the proposed methods match the O(√T) rate of the noise-free baselines and strictly improve upon the noisy variant of [29], which degrades to O(T^{3/4}) regret. The trade-off is a slightly weaker constraint violation bound of O(T^{3/4}) versus O(√T) or Õ(√T) for the noise-free baselines, though those baselines do not handle stochastic gradients. The semi-bandit variant (Theorem 1) achieves a 1/2 approximation ratio (the best known when queries are restricted to K), while the full-information variant (Theorem 2) recovers the optimal 1−1/e ratio via the non-oblivious function technique.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the primal-dual OCO framework of Mahdavi et al. [20] to the DR-submodular setting, building directly on the first study of online DR-submodular maximization with stochastic constraints by Raut et al. [29] and its improvements by Sadeghi et al. [31] and Feng et al. [10]. The key departure is in the feedback model: all prior works require √T gradient queries per round under full-information feedback, while this paper achieves comparable regret with only 1 gradient query per round under semi-bandit feedback. For the (1−1/e) approximation ratio, the paper adapts the non-oblivious function technique of Zhang et al. [42] to the online constrained setting, and leverages the 1/2-approximation property of DR-submodular functions from Hassani et al. [12] for the semi-bandit case.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The reduction from √T to 1 gradient queries per round is practically significant for applications where gradient evaluations are expensive (e.g., sensor placement, ad allocation), making the algorithm feasible in settings where prior methods would be computationally prohibitive. However, the constraint violation bound degrades from O(√T) to O(T^{3/4}), reflecting a fundamental trade-off between query efficiency and constraint tightness. The paper also handles general convex sets not containing the origin, which prior works required, broadening applicability. Open directions include extending to upper-linearizable functions [27] and to adversarial (non-i.i.d.) utility functions, which would require fundamentally different analysis.
