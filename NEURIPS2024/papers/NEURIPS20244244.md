## Problem

The generalization properties of exact Gauss-Newton (GN) optimization in deep neural networks remain poorly understood because prior theoretical analyses were confined to simplified settings (linear networks, constant NTK limit) where feature learning is impossible, while practical applications require structured approximations (Kronecker-factored, truncated) that confound whether observed behavior stems from GN itself or from the approximation. This paper asks: what does exact, unapproximated GN actually do in realistic, overparameterized deep networks trained on real data?

## Input / Output

The RevMLP models consume flattened 1D vectors—28×28 MNIST digits, 32×32 CIFAR-10 images, or tabular feature vectors from UCI regression datasets—and produce 10-way class labels or scalar regression predictions. The architecture uses stacked volume-preserving coupling layers with ReLU non-linearities and fixed inverted bottlenecks of size 8000, yielding 12M (MNIST) or 147M (CIFAR-10) parameters; the inverted bottlenecks guarantee the overparameterization (p ≥ nd_y) required for the tractable GN update to be theoretically valid.

## Method

The paper derives an exact, computationally tractable GN update for deep reversible networks (RevMLPs) by exploiting two key properties: (1) any right inverse of the Jacobian yields the same loss dynamics as the Moore-Penrose pseudoinverse, provided the NTK remains non-singular (Theorem 4.3), and (2) in reversible coupling-layer architectures, a specific right inverse can be computed via a single reverse-mode differentiation pass through the inverse network plus a small per-layer matrix pseudoinverse (Proposition 4.4), giving O(Lnd² + Ln²d) compute at the same memory cost as SGD. Empirically, the authors show that exact GN converges rapidly in full-batch settings but saturates early in mini-batch training, overfitting each batch without learning new features—the NTK and neural representations (measured by CKA) remain essentially unchanged from initialization, placing GN firmly in the 'lazy' regime and explaining its poor generalization relative to Adam and SGD.

## Results

The paper trains RevMLP models with 12M parameters (MNIST) and 147M parameters (CIFAR-10) using exact Gauss-Newton (GN), Adam, and SGD. In the full-batch setting (n=1024), GN converges substantially faster than both Adam and SGD on both datasets. In the mini-batch setting (n=1024, full datasets), GN exhibits early saturation of both training and test loss, while Adam continues to improve and eventually reaches lower loss than GN. Even after 1000 epochs on CIFAR-10, GN remains stuck at its ~50-epoch loss level and its training loss actually increases after 150 epochs, while plain SGD overtakes GN. NTK and CKA analyses confirm that GN operates in the 'lazy' regime: neural representations and the NTK remain nearly unchanged from initialization, unlike Adam which learns new features.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares exact GN against Adam and SGD as first-order baselines across four datasets (MNIST, CIFAR-10, Superconductivity, Wine). In the full-batch regime, GN clearly wins, achieving much faster convergence on training loss than both Adam and SGD, consistent with its theoretical O(1) error decay. In the mini-batch (stochastic) regime, the picture reverses: GN saturates early on both training and test loss, while Adam reaches the lowest final loss and SGD eventually overtakes GN on CIFAR-10 (confirmed over 1000 epochs). GN overfits each mini-batch more aggressively than either baseline (Figure 3), and this overfitting persists for 80+ epochs. The NTK analysis shows that both GN and SGD remain in the lazy regime (minimal NTK change), while Adam actively learns new features; CKA confirms that GN's internal representations stay close to initialization, similar to SGD but unlike Adam. The authors also test whether starting GN from an Adam-pretrained checkpoint (a 'good' initialization) helps, but GN still saturates at a higher loss than continued Adam training, ruling out a simple bad-local-minimum explanation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on exact second-order optimization in deep networks (Bernacchia et al. 2018 for deep linear networks; Karakida and Osawa 2020 for the constant-NTK limit; Arbel et al. 2023 for the mean-field regime) by deriving a tractable generalized inverse of the Jacobian specifically for deep reversible coupling-layer networks (RevMLPs), without assuming a constant NTK. It departs from prior approximate GN methods (Kronecker-factored, truncated CG) by providing exact updates at near-SGD cost, and from prior reversible-network work (NICE, RevNets) by repurposing the reversibility property not for memory savings but for analytical Jacobian inversion. The paper is the first to study exact GN generalization in the feature-learning regime on real data with networks up to 147M parameters.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that exact GN remains in the lazy regime and overfits each mini-batch provides the first clean, unconfounded evidence (no approximation artifacts) that the second-order nature of GN updates does not inherently promote feature learning or generalization in stochastic training. This has direct implications for the ongoing debate about whether second-order methods offer generalization advantages beyond faster convergence. Open questions include whether damping or truncation of the generalized inverse (which the current reversible formulation does not easily accommodate) could restore feature learning, and whether the gap between the generalized inverse used here and the Moore-Penrose pseudoinverse matters at finite learning rates. The tractable GN framework itself is a useful tool for future theoretical studies of second-order optimization in overparameterized networks.
