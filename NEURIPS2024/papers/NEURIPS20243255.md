## Problem

Previous theoretical analyses of self-consuming generative models (iterative retraining on synthetic data) assumed raw, unfiltered synthetic samples, ignoring the fact that synthetic data on the web is routinely curated by human users before being uploaded (e.g., users selecting one of four Midjourney outputs to upscale). This paper asks: what distribution does a generative model provably learn when it is iteratively retrained on human-curated synthetic data, and how does this curation step interact with the underlying reward model that drives human preferences?

## Input / Output

At each retraining iteration, the procedure samples K synthetic points from the current model distribution p_t, applies a Plackett-Luce discrete K-choice curation step (selecting one sample with probability proportional to exp(r(x_k)) over the K candidates), and then trains the next model via maximum likelihood on a mixture of a reference distribution (real data p_data or the initial model p_0) and the curated synthetic samples, controlled by hyperparameter λ. The reward function r(x) is never directly optimized; it only governs the curation selection. The output at each step is the updated model distribution p_{t+1}, and the paper tracks the evolution of expected reward, reward variance, and KL divergence from the reference distribution across iterations.

## Method

The paper models the curation phenomenon using a Plackett-Luce (Bradley-Terry) discrete K-choice model and derives the exact density of the curated distribution as p_t(x)·H^K_{p_t}(x), where H is an expectation over K−1 competing samples. The key theoretical results show: (1) in the fully synthetic loop (λ→∞), the expected reward increases proportionally to its variance at each step (Lemma 2.2), the variance vanishes, and the distribution converges in KL to the initial density restricted to the maximum-reward level set (Theorem 2.1); (2) in the K→∞ limit, one curation step is exactly equivalent to RLHF with regularization β=1/t, revealing that maximum likelihood on curated data implicitly performs preference optimization without access to the reward or negative samples; (3) when real data is re-injected at each step (λ<∞), the KL divergence from the reference distribution remains bounded by −log(1−λ(K−1)) (Theorem 2.4) while the expected reward still increases (Theorem 2.3), establishing a direct connection to the RLHF objective of reward maximization plus KL regularization. Experiments on Gaussian mixtures, two moons (DDPM), and CIFAR-10 (conditional flow matching with VGG11 confidence as reward) confirm reward increase, variance collapse, and bias amplification when the reward is implicitly correlated with class identity.

## Results

This is primarily a theoretical paper with illustrative experiments rather than a benchmark comparison. On CIFAR-10 (using a VGG11 classifier with 92.39% test accuracy as the reward model), filtering by a single class probability r(x)=γ·q₀(x) with γ=5 causes the generative model to collapse to the 'plane' class over 10 retraining iterations, confirming Lemma 2.2's prediction of monotonic reward increase. Using classifier confidence r(x)=γ·maxᵢ qᵢ(x) as reward produces increasingly heterogeneous class proportions (bias emergence) in the fully synthetic loop, while mixing with real data (λ=1/2) keeps class proportions approximately uniform while still increasing the average reward. Theoretically, the expected reward increases proportionally to its variance at each step (Lemma 2.2), the KL divergence to the maximum-reward level set converges to 0 (Theorem 2.1), and the KL divergence from the reference distribution remains bounded by −log(1−λ(K−1)) (Theorem 2.4).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against external baseline methods; instead, it contrasts different retraining settings to validate its theoretical predictions. The fully synthetic loop (λ→∞) with curation is compared against the mixed loop (λ=1/2) on CIFAR-10: the fully synthetic setting exhibits bias amplification (class proportions become heterogeneous) and eventual collapse to high-reward regions, while the mixed setting maintains stability (approximately uniform class proportions) with bounded KL divergence from the data distribution. The paper also improves upon Bertrand et al. (2024) by extending their stability guarantee from λ<1/2 to any λ<α/(2Lε) and expressing the result in distribution space rather than parameter space. A key conceptual comparison is drawn with RLHF: the paper shows that iterative retraining on curated data in the K→∞ limit is equivalent to RLHF with β=1/t, establishing that maximum likelihood on a curated distribution implicitly performs reward maximization with KL regularization, without requiring access to the reward function or negative samples.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the self-consuming generative model literature (Alemohammad et al., 2024; Shumailov et al., 2023; Bertrand et al., 2024) by introducing a previously overlooked dimension: human curation of synthetic data before it re-enters training datasets. While Bertrand et al. (2024) established stability conditions for iterative retraining on mixed real/synthetic data, this work departs by modeling the curation step via a Plackett-Luce discrete choice model and showing it acts as an implicit preference optimization mechanism. The key conceptual departure is the proven equivalence between iterative retraining on curated samples and RLHF (with β = 1/t), a connection the authors explicitly flag as surprising since maximum likelihood on a curated distribution is a priori distinct from reward maximization with KL regularization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that web-scale curation of synthetic data automatically performs implicit RLHF for future models has significant societal implications: it means human preferences (and their biases) are being baked into generative models through a feedback loop that no one is explicitly controlling. The experiments demonstrate that even a reward 'uncorrelated' with class identity (classifier confidence) amplifies class biases through implicit statistical correlation, raising urgent questions about fairness in self-consuming training pipelines. Key open questions include how to mitigate bias amplification in this setting, how different reward structures (confidence vs. quality vs. diversity) affect the dynamics, and whether the results extend to multi-reward or non-transitive preference settings.
