## Problem

Existing Neuro-Symbolic AI benchmarks rely on fixed propositional rules over limited entities, lack flexible reasoning complexity, and rarely support realistic multi-agent interactions or both long-horizon and visual reasoning tasks simultaneously. This makes it difficult to evaluate whether NeSy systems can truly learn abstract, composable rules that generalize to new agent compositions and complex scenarios.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242231/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For the Safe Path Following (SPF) task, the ego agent receives a binary predicate grounding vector (85–205 dimensions depending on mode) from its limited field-of-view and must output a 4-way discrete action (Slow/Normal/Fast/Stop) sequentially over >40 steps. For the Visual Action Prediction (VAP) task, the model receives a rendered RGB image plus per-agent metadata (position, scale, direction, priority) and must simultaneously predict actions for all N agents in a single step. Critically, training and test agent compositions differ across both tasks, so models must learn abstractions rather than memorize entity-specific patterns.

## Method

LogiCity is a configurable first-order-logic urban simulator where users specify semantic/spatial predicates, FOL rule clauses (in Prolog syntax), and agent sets as independent configurations. At each timestep, an SMT solver (Z3) grounds the predicates over the ego agent's field-of-view and infers action truth values from the rule set, while GPT-4-generated descriptions fed into Stable Diffusion XL produce diverse RGB renderings of the grid map. The paper introduces two evaluation tasks: a POMDP-based sequential decision-making task with multi-agent interaction and a one-step visual reasoning task on high-dimensional RGB inputs. As a novel NeSy RL agent, NLM-DQN embeds Neural Logic Machines as a feature extractor within a DQN framework, learning implicit logical representations from abstract observations. The benchmark is evaluated across four difficulty modes (easy→expert) with progressively more predicates and rules, testing both from-scratch and continual learning of new abstractions.

## Results

On the primary Safe Path Following (SPF) task, the best NeSy method (NLM-DQN) achieves a trajectory success rate (TSR) of 0.53 (Easy), 0.47 (Medium), 0.29 (Hard), and 0.15 (Expert), compared to the Oracle's perfect 1.00 across all modes. In the Visual Action Prediction (VAP) task, NLM with random agent configurations and end-to-end supervision reaches 0.50 weighted accuracy on the hard mode, outperforming GNN (0.54) in modular settings but trailing it in E2E. Performance degrades sharply with increasing rule complexity: even the strongest baselines drop from near-perfect TSR in Easy mode to below 0.25 in Expert mode, highlighting the difficulty of long-horizon multi-agent abstract reasoning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

In the SPF task, the NeSy method NLM [7] consistently outperforms pure neural baselines (GNN [38], MLP) across all four difficulty modes in the behavior cloning track, achieving 0.75 vs. 0.26 TSR in Easy and 0.22 vs. 0.19 in Expert. Among RL methods, the proposed NLM-DQN [7,35] is the strongest, beating DQN [35] (0.53 vs. 0.35 Easy TSR; 0.29 vs. 0.09 Hard TSR) and significantly outperforming on-policy methods PPO [33] and A2C [34], as well as model-based DreamerV2 [39] and MB-shooting [37]. Symbolic ILP methods Popper [10] and MaxSynth [36] match the Oracle on Easy mode but fail to scale: Popper times out beyond Easy, while MaxSynth drops to 0.09 TSR in Expert. HRI [11] shows moderate performance on Easy/Medium (0.37/0.48 TSR) but collapses on Hard (0.08) and fails entirely on Expert. In the VAP task, NLM [7] surpasses GNN [38] in the hard mode (0.36 vs. 0.23 weighted accuracy with modular supervision), while GNN slightly edges out NLM in the easy mode. On the LLM comparison, GPT-4 [1] achieves only 39.6% overall accuracy on VAP hard mode, far below Human performance (81.2%), with a notable bias toward conservative 'Slow' predictions and near-zero accuracy on the hardest 'Stop' reasoning (2.2%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on Neural Logic Machines (NLM) and DQN to construct NLM-DQN, a NeSy reinforcement learning agent, and positions LogiCity against a spectrum of prior NeSy benchmarks (Visual Sudoku, CLEVR, BlocksWorld, BabyAI, Highway) and game/simulation environments (NetHack, StarCraft, CARLA, SCENIC). The key conceptual departure is making all rules and concepts true first-order-logic abstractions decoupled from specific entities, enabling compositional generalization through different train/test agent compositions — a property absent from prior propositional-rule simulators. The rendering pipeline repurposes GPT-4 and Stable Diffusion XL to generate diverse RGB inputs, bridging the gap between structured FOL reasoning and high-dimensional perceptual data in a way prior NeSy benchmarks (which use simple grids or synthetic shapes) did not.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LogiCity is the first benchmark to simultaneously stress-test long-horizon multi-agent sequential decision-making and one-step visual reasoning under customizable FOL abstractions, exposing that even the strongest NeSy methods (NLM) degrade sharply as rule complexity and agent diversity increase. The LLM comparison (GPT-4o at 59% vs. human 81.2% on inductive rule reasoning) highlights a concrete gap between large language models and formal inductive logical reasoning, suggesting a promising direction of coupling LLMs with symbolic inductive reasoners. Open questions include extending to temporal logic, stochastic/fuzzy rules, and closing the sim-to-real gap; the deterministic, conflict-free rule requirement also limits immediate applicability to real urban driving.
