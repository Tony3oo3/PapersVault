## Problem

Real-world tabular data frequently violates the i.i.d. assumption due to temporal distribution shifts (e.g., policy changes, seasonal cycles, equipment updates), yet no tabular method has consistently outperformed classical supervised learning that simply ignores these shifts. This gap is compounded by the fact that tree-based models dominate tabular prediction while existing distribution-shift methods rely almost exclusively on neural networks, leaving a methodological void for small-to-moderate tabular datasets.

## Input / Output

The model consumes the entire training dataset—features, binary labels, and Time2Vec-encoded temporal domain indices—as a single context sequence, along with test features and their domain indices, and produces calibrated class probabilities in one forward pass with no per-dataset hyperparameter tuning. Because it inherits TabPFN's transformer architecture, it is constrained to small-to-moderately sized datasets (quadratic attention scaling) and requires domain indices to be approximated from temporal features in real-world settings where ground-truth domains are unknown.

## Method

Drift-Resilient TabPFN extends TabPFN's structural causal model (SCM) prior by introducing a 2nd-order SCM that takes Time2Vec-encoded temporal domain indices as input and outputs sparse, nonlinear, correlated edge-weight shifts for the primary SCM's functional graph, thereby generating synthetic datasets whose causal mechanisms drift over time. The transformer is pre-trained via in-context learning on 30.7 million such synthetic datasets, learning to approximate Bayesian posterior predictive inference under a prior that explicitly encodes temporal mechanism shifts. Rather than architecting explicit shift-handling modules, the model learns the prediction algorithm itself: it observes the full training context (including domain indices) and extrapolates decision boundaries into unseen future domains. This sparse mechanism-shift prior naturally unifies covariate shift, prior probability shift, and concept shift, and the resulting model requires no hyperparameter tuning at inference time, completing training and prediction in ~10.9 seconds on average.

## Results

Across 18 datasets (8 synthetic, 10 real-world), Drift-Resilient TabPFN achieves OOD accuracy of 0.744, OOD F1 of 0.689, OOD ROC AUC of 0.832, and OOD ECE of 0.091 (all with domain indices), outperforming the strongest baseline TabPFN-base (0.688 / 0.62 / 0.786 / 0.119) by +5.6% accuracy, +6.9% F1, +4.6% ROC AUC, and −2.8% ECE. On synthetic datasets alone, OOD accuracy rises from 0.665 (best baseline, CatBoost) to 0.754, and OOD ROC AUC from 0.749 to 0.844. The method requires no hyperparameter tuning and completes training + prediction in ~10.9 s on average.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Drift-Resilient TabPFN outperforms every baseline on OOD data across all four metrics. Against the closest competitor TabPFN-base, it gains +5.6 pp OOD accuracy and +4.6 pp ROC AUC while reducing ECE by 24% (0.119→0.091), indicating substantially better calibration under shift. All three GBDTs (CatBoost 0.677, XGBoost 0.664, LightGBM 0.650 OOD accuracy) are beaten by 7–9 pp in accuracy and 16–24 pp in ECE, confirming that tree-based methods remain overconfident on OOD samples. The Wild-Time benchmark methods (ERM, SWA, EWC, SI, A-GEM, CORAL-T, GroupDRO-T, IRM-T, Mixup, LISA) all fall 10–25 pp behind on OOD accuracy, with the NN-based continual-learning methods (EWC 0.498, SI 0.478, A-GEM 0.513) performing worst due to limited training data. On the Rotated Two Moons dataset, the method achieves 0.98 OOD accuracy versus 0.968 for DRAIN and 0.965 for GI, the two dedicated temporal-DG methods. The only trade-off is a slight ID accuracy drop (0.879 vs. 0.885 for TabPFN-base), which the authors attribute to the prior being optimized for OOD robustness rather than in-distribution fit.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends TabPFN (Hollmann et al., ICLR 2023) and the broader Prior-Data Fitted Network framework (Müller et al., ICLR 2022) by modifying the structural causal model prior to incorporate temporal distribution shifts. Rather than designing purpose-built invariances or robustness objectives as in classical DG methods (IRM, DANN, CORAL, GroupDRO) or temporal-specific approaches (DRAIN, GI), it learns the prediction algorithm itself via in-context learning on synthetic datasets where a 2nd-order SCM governs how edge weights drift over time. This represents a conceptual departure: the model is not told how to handle shifts but instead infers shift dynamics from the prior, unifying covariate, prior-probability, and concept shifts under a single generative mechanism.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first tabular method to consistently outperform both classical supervised learning and purpose-built DG baselines under temporal distribution shifts, while requiring no hyperparameter tuning and running in seconds. The strong OOD calibration (ECE reduced from 0.164 to 0.126 on synthetic data) is practically important for deployment in healthcare, finance, and climate science where overconfident predictions are dangerous. Key limitations constrain broader impact: quadratic attention scaling restricts applicability to small-to-moderate datasets, the black-box nature limits interpretability of detected shifts, and the SCM prior may not capture all real-world shift complexities. Open questions include extending to transductive/online settings, spatial shifts, and scaling to larger datasets via data distillation or context compression.
