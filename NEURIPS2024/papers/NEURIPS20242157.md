## Problem

Generating diverse, realistic, and complex 3D interactive environments for Embodied AI and robotics remains challenging: manual datasets don't scale, procedural methods rely on hand-crafted rules, and LLM-guided approaches lack 3D spatial reasoning to produce nuanced object arrangements (e.g., cluttered shelves, organized tables). No existing method simultaneously achieves open-vocabulary generation, fine-grained small-object placement, and full interactivity without training on fixed scene datasets.

## Input / Output

The pipeline accepts a text scene description, a floor plan, or a pre-arranged scene as starting input, and produces a fully interactable 3D simulation environment with placed and articulated objects (retrieved from Objaverse/PartNet-Mobility or generated via text-to-3D). Internally, the method renders images at 84° FOV from up to three corner-to-corner views for large furniture and from object-specific close-up views for small objects, then back-projects depth into 3D point clouds using ground-truth camera intrinsics from the simulation renderer. The final output includes per-object 3D bounding boxes, semantic labels, and asset UIDs that can be serialized for LLM-based scene editing.

## Method

ARCHITECT resolves the camera-parameter and depth-scale ambiguity of 2D generative models by conditioning SD-XL inpainting on a simulation-rendered background image that carries known geometric cues, so the inpainted foreground inherits correct camera pose and metric depth. The pipeline is hierarchical and iterative: it first places large furniture across multiple room-wide views, then zooms into individual furniture bounding boxes to inpaint small objects at finer scale. For each inpainted image, GPT-4V identifies objects, Grounded-SAM segments them, and Marigold estimates relative depth which is rescaled against reference pixels from the rendered background to recover metric depth. Back-projected point clouds are clustered with DBSCAN to yield 3D bounding boxes, and a constraint-based DFS search (deriving global, distance, relation, alignment, and rotation constraints from bounding-box geometry) places large furniture collision-free, while small objects are placed directly at their bounding-box centers with orientation and scale matched to the viewing direction.

## Results

ARCHITECT achieves the best CLIP score (0.7173), BLIP score (0.5859), and GPT-4o ranking (1.36, lower is better) among all compared methods, while also leading in all four user-study metrics (Visual Quality 3.87, Semantic Correctness 3.76, Layout Coherence 3.65, Overall Preference 3.71). The VQA score is 0.8073, marginally below Text2Room's 0.8149 but well above Holodeck (0.5696) and DiffuScene (0.7561). In the PhyScene comparison (Table 4), ARCHITECT scores 72.96 CLIP, 63.62 BLIP, and 94.58 VQScore, outperforming all baselines. For task diversity, ARCHITECT achieves a lower Self-BLEU (0.198) than RoboGen (0.284), indicating more diverse generated task descriptions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Holodeck, ARCHITECT wins decisively on every metric: CLIP improves from 0.6502 to 0.7173, BLIP from 0.3463 to 0.5859, VQA from 0.5696 to 0.8073, and GPT-4o ranking from 1.91 to 1.36; user-study scores are also uniformly higher (e.g., Overall Preference 3.71 vs. 3.07). Against Text2Room, ARCHITECT dominates on CLIP (0.7173 vs. 0.6491), BLIP (0.5859 vs. 0.1223), GPT-4o ranking (1.36 vs. 2.64), and all user-study dimensions, with Text2Room retaining only a marginal VQA edge (0.8149 vs. 0.8073) likely due to its direct RGB-to-3D projection preserving photorealism at the cost of geometric artifacts. Against DiffuScene, ARCHITECT leads on CLIP (0.7173 vs. 0.6785), BLIP (0.5859 vs. 0.4310), VQA (0.8073 vs. 0.7561), and all user-study scores, reflecting DiffuScene's limitation to a fixed training distribution and lack of small-object placement. In the PhyScene comparison (Table 4), ARCHITECT outperforms PhyScene on CLIP (72.96 vs. 71.42), BLIP (63.62 vs. 46.51), and VQScore (94.58 vs. 88.72). For embodied task generation, ARCHITECT produces more diverse tasks than RoboGen (Self-BLEU 0.198 vs. 0.284) and achieves a higher placement VQScore (80.81 vs. 74.93 for LLM-based placement), confirming that the 2D inpainting pipeline yields more spatially coherent small-object arrangements than pure LLM reasoning.

## Relation to Prior Work

ARCHITECT departs from the dominant LLM-guided scene generation paradigm (Holodeck, RoboGen, AnyHome) by replacing language-space spatial reasoning with 2D diffusion-based inpainting, arguing that pre-trained image models encode richer scene and inter-object layout priors from massive visual data. It also moves beyond procedural generation (ProcTHOR) and 3D diffusion approaches (DiffuScene) by operating entirely in 2D image space and lifting to 3D via depth estimation, while borrowing Holodeck's constraint-based placement search and RoboGen's task-generation framework for downstream robotics. The key methodological departure is the use of simulation-rendered backgrounds as geometric anchors during inpainting, which resolves the camera-parameter and depth-scale ambiguity that plagues direct 2D-to-3D lifting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ARCHITECT provides a practical, zero-shot pipeline for generating open-vocabulary, interactive 3D environments with multi-scale object detail (large furniture and small items on shelves/tables), addressing a gap where prior methods either lack interactivity, produce only static meshes, or cannot handle cluttered small-object arrangements. The hierarchical inpainting strategy is a reusable technique for controlling generative models with 3D geometric constraints. Key limitations include dependence on the Objaverse asset database for furniture diversity, the computational cost of iterative multi-view inpainting, and the fact that small-object 3D generation from low-resolution segmented crops remains suboptimal. Open questions include whether the approach scales to multi-room or outdoor environments and whether fully generative 3D asset creation can replace retrieval.
