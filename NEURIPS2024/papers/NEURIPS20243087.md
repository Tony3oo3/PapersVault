## Problem

Existing masked diffusion Transformer methods (MDT, MaskDiT, SD-DiT) reconstruct masked noisy patches from unmasked noisy patches, but this noisy-to-noisy objective degrades contextual information extraction as noise variance increases. The paper demonstrates via mutual information analysis and a mask-graph contrastive formulation that the information in masked patches rarely originates from unmasked patches under high noise, making the reconstruction task fundamentally insufficient for learning spatial context.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243087/cropped_page2_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

During training, the model operates in the latent space of a pretrained Stable Diffusion VAE (mapping 256×256×3 images to 32×32×4 and 512×512×3 to 64×64×4). Uniquely, the DiT decoder receives both unmasked noisy patches (from the encoder) and masked clean patches (concatenated directly) as input, a dual-source design absent in prior masked diffusion methods. At inference, only Gaussian noise and a class label are provided, and the model generates 256×256 or 512×512 RGB images via the EDM sampler with 40 steps.

## Method

MC-DiT replaces the noisy-to-noisy mask-reconstruction objective with a clean-to-clean one: the DiT decoder reconstructs unmasked clean patches from masked clean patches, ensuring the mutual information between source and target remains at the ideal I(x₀¹; x₀²) regardless of the noise level t. To prevent model collapse (where the model shortcuts by ignoring noisy patches), two complementary EMA decoder branches are introduced—one processing full noisy patches for denoising and one processing clean patches for contextual reconstruction—whose outputs constrain the main decoder via weighted MSE losses. The overall training loss combines clean reconstruction, noisy reconstruction, and two consistency terms with hyperparameters λ₁=0.1, λ₂=0.1, λ₃=0.05. Theoretical support comes from Propositions 2 and 3, which show via KL-divergence decomposition and first-order Taylor expansion that noisy-to-noisy reconstruction introduces noise-weighted perturbation terms that corrupt the contrastive alignment objective. After masked pretraining, a brief unmasking finetuning phase (mask ratio 0%) resolves the training-inference discrepancy.

## Results

On ImageNet-256×256, MC-DiT-XL/2 achieves a FID of 4.14 (unconditional) and 1.78 (with classifier-free guidance), the best FID among all DiT-based methods. With guidance, it also attains the highest Inception Score of 290.17 and Precision of 0.81. On ImageNet-512×512, MC-DiT-XL/2-G achieves a FID of 2.03, outperforming MaskDiT-XL/2-G (2.50) and DiT-XL/2-G (3.04). At matched training iterations (400K), MC-DiT-B/2 reduces FID-50K to 18.88 versus 28.62 for SD-DiT-B/2 and 43.47 for vanilla DiT-B/2.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MC-DiT-XL/2 outperforms all masked-diffusion baselines on ImageNet-256×256: it reduces FID from 5.69 (MaskDiT) and 6.23 (MDT) to 4.14 in the unconditional setting, and from 2.28 (MaskDiT-G) and 1.79 (MDT-G) to 1.78 with classifier-free guidance. Against vanilla DiT-XL/2, the FID drops from 9.62 to 4.14 (unconditional) and from 2.27 to 1.78 (with guidance). On ImageNet-512×512, MC-DiT-XL/2-G achieves FID 2.03, beating MaskDiT-XL/2-G (2.50) and DiT-XL/2-G (3.04). At matched 400K iterations with the B/2 backbone, MC-DiT achieves FID-50K of 18.88 versus 28.62 (SD-DiT-B/2) and 43.47 (DiT-B/2), demonstrating substantially faster convergence. On CIFAR-10 and CelebA (200K iterations), MC-DiT-B/2 achieves FID 9.28 and 5.36 respectively, outperforming MaskDiT-B/2 (11.52 and 7.14), confirming generalization beyond ImageNet.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the masked-diffusion-Transformer line initiated by MDT, MaskDiT, and SD-DiT, all of which adopt a noisy-to-noisy mask-reconstruction objective (reconstructing masked noisy patches from unmasked noisy patches) atop the DiT backbone and EDM training framework. The key departure is a theoretical and empirical argument that noisy-to-noisy reconstruction degrades contextual information extraction, particularly at high noise variances, formalized via mutual information bounds (Proposition 2) and mask-graph contrastive analysis (Proposition 3). MC-DiT replaces the reconstruction target with clean-to-clean patch reconstruction, drawing on the MAE encoder-decoder paradigm but applied within the diffusion denoising schedule, and introduces two EMA decoder branches to prevent the model from collapsing onto a clean-patch shortcut.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that the standard noisy-to-noisy mask-reconstruction objective actively undermines contextual learning provides a principled rationale for revisiting how masked training is integrated into diffusion Transformers, and the clean-to-clean alternative is a drop-in modification that practitioners can adopt. The SOTA FID improvements across multiple model scales (S, B, XL) and resolutions (256, 512) with faster convergence suggest the technique is broadly applicable. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to video or 3D generation, whether the EMA branches can be replaced by lighter regularization, and how to reduce the additional training overhead (~8% FLOPs) without sacrificing the denoising balance.
