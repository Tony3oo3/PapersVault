## Problem

While one-step self-distillation for linear regression has been shown to strictly outperform ridge regression, the performance gains from repeatedly applying self-distillation remain uncharacterized in closed form. The fundamental question is: how much additional gain is possible by applying multiple steps of self-distillation, and what structural properties of the problem instance determine whether such gains are achievable?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240249/cropped_page3_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on fixed-design linear regression where the covariate matrix X ∈ R^{d×n} is treated as fixed and only the response Y is random. The k-step SD estimator takes the ridge penalty λ and a vector of k imitation parameters ξ^(k) ∈ R^k (not restricted to [0,1]) as hyperparameters, and produces a learned parameter vector θ̂ that is a pre-conditioned version of the ridge solution. A key structural detail is that the excess risk is quadratic in a reparameterization ξ̄^(k) of the imitation parameters, with a coefficient matrix of rank at most r = rank(X), which enables practical hyperparameter selection from O(k²) validation evaluations.

## Method

The paper shows that k-step self-distillation in linear regression admits a closed-form expression as a pre-conditioner P(λ, ξ^(k)) applied to the standard ridge estimator, where the pre-conditioner involves increasing powers of Ω_λ^{-1}XX^T (Eq. 8). The key insight is that the sequence of ξ parameters provides additional degrees of freedom to independently control the eigenvalues of the linear estimator along each singular direction of X, thereby reducing the variance component of the excess risk. Under the assumption that X has distinct singular values and θ* is well-aligned with one eigenvector, r-step SD (where r = rank(X)) achieves a fundamental lower bound on excess risk, yielding an Ω(r) multiplicative separation from both ridge and 1-step SD. The necessity of both assumptions is established via matching lower bounds showing no separation when singular values collide or θ* is equally misaligned across all eigenvectors. Finally, the quadratic form of the excess risk in ξ̄^(k) (Theorem 5) provides a practical strategy for selecting optimal ξ values on real-world datasets using only O(k²) validation set evaluations.

## Results

Theoretically, the paper proves that r-step self-distillation (where r = rank(X)) can reduce excess risk by a factor of Ω(r) compared to both ridge regression and 1-step SD (Theorem 1), with the gap scaling linearly with the input dimension d when r = d. Empirically, on the Air Quality dataset, 2-step SD achieves a test MSE of 1.06, a 47.2% reduction over optimal ridge (MSE 2.01) and a 46.7% reduction over 1-step SD (MSE 1.99). On Airfoil, 2-step SD (MSE 1.19) modestly outperforms 1-step SD (1.22) and ridge (1.34), while on AEP no SD variant improves over ridge (all ≈ 0.62–0.63).

| Air Quality | 2.01 | 1.99 | **1.06** |

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares 2-step SD against two baselines: optimal ridge regression and optimal 1-step SD (the method of Das and Sanghavi [9]). On Air Quality, 2-step SD dramatically outperforms both baselines, reducing MSE from 2.01 (ridge) and 1.99 (1-step SD) to 1.06 — a 47.2% improvement over ridge. On Airfoil, 2-step SD (1.19) provides a modest but consistent gain over 1-step SD (1.22) and a 11.2% gain over ridge (1.34). On AEP, however, 2-step SD (0.63) actually performs slightly worse than ridge (0.62), and 1-step SD matches ridge exactly; the authors attribute this to the true parameter θ* being poorly aligned with any eigenbasis direction (β ≈ 0.65), violating the alignment condition (Assumption 2.2) needed for SD gains. Overall, 2-step SD wins on 2 of 3 datasets and ties/loses on 1, with the largest gains occurring when the signal is concentrated along a single eigenvector direction.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the theoretical analysis of self-distillation from Das and Sanghavi (ICML 2023), who characterized 1-step SD for fixed-design linear regression, to the multi-step setting. It also provides the first rigorous theoretical grounding for the empirical repeated self-distillation procedure (Born-Again Networks) introduced by Furlanello et al. (ICML 2018). A key departure from prior theoretical work (Mobahi et al., Borup and Andersen) is the finding that with independently optimized ξ parameters at each step, additional SD steps always improve performance, whereas those works showed diminishing or negative returns when ξ is held fixed across steps.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes a clean Ω(d) upper bound on the multiplicative gain from multi-step SD over 1-step SD, and shows that r steps (where r = rank(X)) are both necessary and sufficient to achieve the fundamental lower bound for any pre-conditioning-based linear estimator. However, the analysis is restricted to fixed-design linear regression with specific spectral assumptions (distinct singular values, strong alignment of θ* with an eigenvector), and the empirical validation on three small UCI datasets shows gains only in 2 of 3 cases. Extending the theory to random design, non-linear models, and larger-scale empirical studies remain important open questions for translating these theoretical insights into practical deep learning settings.
