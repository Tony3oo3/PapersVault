## Problem

Recurrent world models (e.g., Dreamer, MuZero) provide step-level interactivity for model-based RL but lack the capacity for large-scale pre-training on real-world video data, while Internet-scale video generation models (e.g., VideoGPT, Stable Video Diffusion) use non-causal temporal modules that only support trajectory-level control, preventing agents from intervening step-by-step during simulation. The paper asks how to bridge this gap: how can the scalability of modern video generative models be harnessed to build interactive visual world models suitable for model-based reinforcement learning?

## Input / Output

The model takes T0 context video frames (typically 1–2 frames at 64×64 or 256×256 resolution) and optionally a sequence of actions and a goal observation, producing predicted future video frames one at a time via autoregressive next-token generation. Context frames are tokenized into 16×16=256 discrete tokens each, while future frames require only 4×4=16 tokens (a 16× compression), with special slot tokens [S] inserted at frame boundaries to carry action embeddings and reward prediction heads. The output is a sequence of discrete video tokens that are decoded back to pixels, plus scalar reward values predicted from the last token's hidden state of each observation.

## Method

iVideoGPT combines a novel compressive tokenizer with a GPT-like autoregressive transformer (LLaMA architecture at GPT-2 scale: 12–24 layers, 768–1024 hidden dim). The tokenizer is a conditional VQGAN with dual encoder-decoder pairs: context frames are independently encoded into 16×16 tokens, while future frames are encoded by a conditional encoder that uses multi-scale cross-attention to context features, compressing to only 4×4 tokens that capture dynamics (object motion, pose) while the decoder retrieves context to reconstruct full pixels. This context-dynamics decoupling reduces sequence length by ~16× and lets the transformer focus on motion prediction. Pre-training uses action-free next-token prediction (cross-entropy) on 1.4M trajectories from Open X-Embodiment and Something-Something v2; fine-tuning adds action conditioning by linearly projecting actions into slot token embeddings and a reward prediction head, enabling the model to serve as an interactive world model for video prediction, visual MPC, and MBPO-style model-based RL.

## Results

On BAIR (action-free, 64×64), iVideoGPT achieves FVD 75.0, PSNR 20.4, SSIM 82.3, and LPIPS 9.5, outperforming MCVD (FVD 89.5), FitVid (FVD 93.6), and MaskViT (FVD 93.7), though trailing MAGVIT (FVD 62.0) on FVD while leading on PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS. On RoboNet (action-conditioned, 64×64), iVideoGPT reaches FVD 63.2, PSNR 27.8, SSIM 90.6, closely matching FitVid (FVD 62.5) and substantially outperforming GHVAE (FVD 95.2), SVG (FVD 123.2), and MaskViT (FVD 133.5). On the VP² visual planning benchmark, iVideoGPT outperforms all baselines on two RoboDesk tasks (Open drawer: 0.375 vs. 0.253 FitVid; Red button: 0.922 vs. 0.760 SVG') and achieves comparable average performance to the strongest baseline SVG'. On Meta-World MBRL, iVideoGPT matches or exceeds DreamerV3 while dramatically improving sample efficiency over the model-free DrQ-v2 baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On BAIR action-free prediction, iVideoGPT (FVD 75.0) substantially outperforms VideoGPT (103.3), MaskViT (93.7), FitVid (93.6), and MCVD (89.5), but trails MAGVIT (62.0) on FVD; however, iVideoGPT leads MAGVIT on PSNR (20.4 vs. 19.3), SSIM (82.3 vs. 78.7), and LPIPS (9.5 vs. 12.3), and uniquely supports step-level interactivity. On RoboNet, iVideoGPT (FVD 63.2) is nearly on par with FitVid (62.5) but FitVid achieves a lower LPIPS (2.4 vs. 4.9); iVideoGPT clearly beats GHVAE (95.2), SVG (123.2), and MaskViT (133.5) on FVD and leads all baselines on SSIM (90.6). On VP² visual planning, iVideoGPT wins decisively on Open drawer (0.375 vs. 0.253 FitVid, 0.167 SVG') and Red button (0.922 vs. 0.760 SVG', 0.587 FitVid), but loses to SVG' on Open slide (0.161 vs. 0.573), which the authors attribute to low-resolution discretization limitations and imperfect benchmark reward design. On Meta-World MBRL, iVideoGPT matches or exceeds DreamerV3 (with and without pre-training) across six tasks, while the model-free DrQ-v2 baseline lags significantly in sample efficiency; the paper reports this as the first successful application of MBPO to visual continuous control. Compared to recurrent world models (DreamerV3-XL pre-trained on the same data), iVideoGPT produces substantially sharper and more physically plausible predictions, whereas DreamerV3 yields blurred, low-quality rollouts that fail to benefit downstream RL.

## Relation to Prior Work

iVideoGPT positions itself at the intersection of two previously disconnected lines of work: recurrent world models (Dreamer, MuZero) that provide step-level interactivity but lack scalability, and Internet-scale video generation models (VideoGPT, Stable Video Diffusion) that offer scalability but only trajectory-level control. The paper builds on VQGAN's discrete tokenization by introducing a conditional dual-encoder/decoder with cross-attention to achieve compressive tokenization (16× reduction), and adopts the LLaMA-style autoregressive transformer architecture to unify visual observations, actions, and rewards into a single token sequence. For the MBRL component, it adapts MBPO's replay-buffer augmentation strategy with DrQ-v2 as the actor-critic backbone, decoupling model rollouts from policy learning in a way that prior latent-imagination systems (Dreamer, MuZero) do not.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a single pre-trained autoregressive transformer can serve as a versatile interactive world model across video prediction, visual planning, and model-based RL, suggesting a path toward general-purpose world models that simplify the MBRL design space by eliminating latent imagination. Key limitations include reliance on relatively low-resolution (64×64) observations, limited pre-training data diversity (1.4M trajectories from robotic and human manipulation), and an assumption that initial context frames suffice for future frame reconstruction—potentially problematic for long-horizon or large-camera-motion scenarios. Open questions include whether scaling to larger models and more diverse data (e.g., Ego4D) yields the expected power-law improvements, and whether the context-dynamics decoupling generalizes beyond manipulation to more complex embodied tasks.
