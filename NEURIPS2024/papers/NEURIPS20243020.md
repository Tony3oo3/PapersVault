## Problem

Existing permanental processes face three coupled limitations: they inherit the cubic computational complexity of GPs, they require either specific kernel families (e.g., squared exponential) or a stationarity assumption to obtain analytical intensity integrals, and they rely on shallow kernels that limit expressiveness. No prior work has applied nonstationary or deep nonstationary kernels within the Gaussian Cox process framework, leaving a gap for modeling event data with spatially varying similarity structures.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a set of N event locations (1D or 2D coordinates) within a bounded spatial region X, along with hyperparameters specifying the spectral network width R (number of frequencies per layer) and depth L (number of stacked layers). It outputs the posterior intensity function λ(x*) = (f(x*)+α)² at any query point, characterized by a closed-form mean μ²(x*)+σ²(x*) and variance 2σ⁴(x*)+4μ²(x*)σ²(x*). For the single-layer NSSPP variant the intensity integral is analytically computable, while the multi-layer DNSSPP variant requires numerical integration over the domain.

## Method

The core mechanism replaces the GP kernel with a sparse spectral representation of a general nonstationary kernel, where the spectral density p(ω₁,ω₂) is parameterized via a symmetrized form g(ω₁,ω₂)+g(ω₂,ω₁)+g(ω₁,ω₁)+g(ω₂,ω₂) to guarantee positive semi-definiteness, and the frequencies {ω₁ᵣ,ω₂ᵣ} are treated as learnable parameters optimized jointly with other hyperparameters. The spectral feature mapping φ^(R)(x) combines two sets of cosine/sine basis functions weighted by σ/(2√R), and the deep variant DNSSPP stacks L such mappings hierarchically—each layer applying two weight-bias pairs followed by cosine activation and summation—recovering the deep spectral kernel structure. Posterior inference uses a Laplace approximation around the mode β̂, with the precision matrix Q computed analytically from the Hessian of the log-posterior, reducing complexity from O(N³) to O(NR²). Hyperparameters (frequencies, biases, variance σ², and intensity offset α) are learned by maximizing the marginal likelihood via bi-level optimization: an inner loop for the Laplace approximation and an outer loop for gradient-based updates of Θ.

## Results

On the real-world Taxi dataset (3,000 2D points), DNSSPP-[30,50,30] achieves the highest test log-likelihood of 7246 (±37), outperforming the best stationary baseline GSSPP-M12 (6599) by ~647 points and the nonstationary baseline NSMPP (6492) by ~754 points. On the 1D Coal dataset, DNSSPP-[50,30] scores 225.85 (±2.61), beating NSMPP (223.28) and VBPP (219.15). On Redwoods, DNSSPP-[100,50] achieves 79.56 (±0.014), narrowly trailing LBPP (80.40) but surpassing all spectral-based baselines. On the nonstationary synthetic data, DNSSPP-[100,50] reaches L_test = 175.70 (±26.89) with RMSE 0.076, outperforming all stationary baselines by 5–10 L_test points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DNSSPP outperforms the nonstationary spectral mixture baseline NSMPP [Remes et al., NeurIPS 2017] on all three real datasets: by ~2.5 L_test on Coal, ~1.9 on Redwoods, and ~754 on Taxi, with the gap widening substantially on the more complex Taxi data. Against the stationary spectral baselines SSPP and GSSPP variants [Sellier & Dellaportas, AISTATS 2023], DNSSPP leads by 4–10 L_test on Coal, 1–7 on Redwoods, and 500–1000 on Taxi, confirming that the nonstationary kernel assumption is critical for real-world point process data. The Nyström-based LBPP [Walder & Bishop, ICML 2017] is the only method that beats DNSSPP, achieving 80.40 vs. 79.56 on Redwoods, but it trails by 1110 L_test on Taxi and 7.5 on Coal. The inducing-point VBPP [Lloyd et al., ICML 2015] is consistently outperformed by DNSSPP across all datasets, with gaps of 6.7 (Coal), 2.5 (Redwoods), and 1090 (Taxi) L_test points. Overall, DNSSPP's advantage grows with dataset complexity and dimensionality, suggesting the deep nonstationary kernel captures spatial heterogeneity that shallow stationary approximations miss.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the sparse spectral permanental process (Sellier & Dellaportas, AISTATS 2023) from stationary to nonstationary kernels by leveraging the generalized Fourier transform framework for nonstationary positive-definite functions (Yaglom, 1987; Remes et al., NeurIPS 2017). The deep kernel variant directly recovers the deep spectral kernel architecture of Xue et al. (IJCAI 2019), applied here for the first time within a Gaussian Cox process. The inference procedure adapts the Laplace approximation of Sellier & Dellaportas to the nonstationary setting, while the learnable-frequency approach follows Lázaro-Gredilla et al. (JMLR 2010). The paper explicitly notes that no prior work has applied nonstationary kernels within the permanental process framework, positioning this as a natural but previously unexplored generalization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Removing the stationarity assumption from permanental processes addresses a practical limitation for real-world event data where intensity patterns vary across space or time. The deep kernel variant offers enhanced expressiveness for complex, large-scale datasets (e.g., the Porto Taxi data), though it introduces a numerical integration bottleneck that limits computational speed. Open questions include whether the numerical integration for DNSSPP can be approximated more efficiently, how the method scales to very high-dimensional or temporally dynamic point processes, and whether the bi-level optimization (Laplace inner loop + hyperparameter outer loop) can be made more robust to local optima.
