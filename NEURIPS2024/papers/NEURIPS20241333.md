## Problem

The classical prophet inequality assumes rejected items are irrecoverable, which is overly pessimistic for real-world online selection where one can revisit past candidates at a reduced value (e.g., backtracking to a restaurant, reconsidering a real estate offer, or exercising a lookback put option). This paper asks how the ability to look back at rejected items—quantified by general decay functions—changes the achievable competitive ratio across adversarial, random, and IID order models, and what conditions on the decay functions are necessary to surpass the classical upper bounds.

## Input / Output

The method takes as input a finite sequence of known probability distributions (F_1, …, F_n) and a sequence of non-negative, non-increasing, non-decreasing decay functions (D_1, D_2, …) that map a rejected value x to the recoverable amount after j subsequent steps. The output is a stopping-time algorithm (specifically a single-threshold rule) together with tight or near-tight competitive ratio bounds expressed as explicit functions of the scalar parameter γ_D = inf_{x>0} inf_{j≥1} D_j(x)/x, which fully characterizes the problem's difficulty. The key structural result is that the entire infinite sequence of decay functions collapses to this single constant γ_D for the purpose of competitive ratio analysis.

## Method

The core contribution is a two-step reduction that collapses the general D-prophet inequality to the γ-prophet inequality (all decay functions equal to x ↦ γx). First, Theorem 2.1 shows that any D-prophet instance can be simulated by a D_∞-prophet instance (where D_∞ is the pointwise limit of the decay sequence) by inserting arbitrarily many zero-valued observations between real ones, so that the algorithm's lookback window always spans many steps. Second, Proposition 3.3 shows that upper bounds in the γ-prophet inequality using instances with support in {0, a, b} extend to the D_∞-prophet inequality via a rescaling argument that exploits the ratio D_∞(rx)/(rx) → γ. For the γ-prophet inequality itself, the paper derives matching upper and lower bounds of 1/(2−γ) in the adversarial order model (fully solving it), and provides upper bounds of (1−γ)^{3/2}(√(3−γ)−√(1−γ))+γ and a weaker IID bound, with single-threshold algorithms achieving matching or near-matching lower bounds in each model.

## Results

In the adversarial order model, the paper fully solves the γ-prophet inequality: the optimal competitive ratio is exactly 1/(2−γ), achieved by a single-threshold algorithm (Theorem 4.3). In the random order model, the upper bound is (1−γ)^(3/2)(√(3−γ)−√(1−γ))+γ (≈0.732 at γ=0, 1 at γ=1) while the lower bound is 1−(1−γ)p_γ (≈0.632 at γ=0, 1 at γ=1), leaving a gap (Theorem 4.4). In the IID model, the upper bound is U(γ)≈0.778 at γ=0 and 1 at γ=1, with the same lower bound as random order (Theorem 4.6). A key structural result (Theorem 2.1) shows the general D-prophet inequality reduces to the D_∞-prophet inequality, and Proposition 3.3 further reduces upper bounds to the γ-prophet inequality where all decay functions equal x↦γx.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is against the classical prophet inequality (γ=0), which serves as the baseline in all three order models. In the adversarial order, the classical bound of 1/2 (Krengel & Sucheston, 1977) is improved to 1/(2−γ) for any γ>0, and the bound is tight. In the random order model, the classical lower bound of 1−1/e≈0.632 (Esfandiari et al., 2017) and upper bound of √3−1≈0.732 (Correa et al., 2021c) are both improved as functions of γ, though a gap remains. In the IID model, the classical tight bound of ≈0.745 (Correa et al., 2021b) is matched at γ=0, and the upper bound is slightly weaker (≈0.778) but still improves with γ. The paper also shows that decay functions with γ_D=0 (e.g., D_j(x)=x−c_j with c_j>0, or D_j(x)=λ^j x with λ<1) provide no improvement over the classical prophet inequality, establishing a necessary condition for any gain from lookback.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper generalizes the classical prophet inequality (Krengel and Sucheston, 1977) by introducing a lookback mechanism where rejected items can be revisited at a decayed value governed by arbitrary decay functions. The closest prior work is Ekbatani et al. (2024) on prophet inequalities with cancellation costs, which handles a specific buyback scenario; the present paper's decay-function framework subsumes that case and many others (distance-based backtracking, probabilistic buyer interest, exponential discounting). The competitive-ratio analysis builds on the standard adversarial, random-order, and IID prophet inequality literature, with upper bounds derived via the rescaling technique of Proposition 3.3 and lower bounds via single-threshold algorithms whose analysis leverages the structural identity (4) specific to threshold strategies.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central reduction showing that the entire family of decay functions is characterized by the single scalar γ_D is a clean and powerful theoretical result: it means the lookback prophet inequality in adversarial order is completely solved (tight bound 1/(2−γ)), and it immediately rules out improvements for natural decay models like D_j(x)=x−c_j or D_j(x)=λ^j x with λ<1 (which yield γ=0). Open questions include closing the gap between upper and lower bounds in the random-order and IID models, extending the analysis beyond single-threshold algorithms, and determining whether upper bounds proven on {0,a,b}-supported instances can be generalized to arbitrary distributions in the D_∞-prophet inequality. The framework also provides a principled lens for evaluating when lookback capabilities genuinely improve online selection guarantees versus when they are illusory in the worst case.
