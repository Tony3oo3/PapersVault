## Problem

Generative modeling over discrete data (language, DNA sequences, molecules) has been dominated by autoregressive models, while diffusion and flow-matching approaches that excel on continuous data have underperformed in the discrete setting. Existing discrete flow-matching methods either rely on complex Markov-chain transition kernels or relax data to the probability simplex with Dirichlet paths, which cannot accommodate non-uniform source priors, suffer from numerically unstable vector fields near simplex boundaries, and require restrictive network architectures. The paper asks whether a continuous reparameterisation of discrete data exists that enables learning a push-forward map between arbitrary source and target distributions in a principled, numerically stable manner.

## Input / Output

The method consumes discrete sequences (DNA bases, text tokens, molecular atom types/bonds/charges) as one-hot encoded categorical samples, optionally conditioned on a continuous signal (e.g., a transcription profile or cell-type label). Each position in a sequence is treated as a categorical distribution over the probability simplex, and the full sequence lives on a product manifold. At inference, the flow pushes a prior on the positive orthant of the hypersphere forward to the data distribution, and a discrete output is decoded by selecting the nearest simplex vertex (or sampling from the resulting categorical). For molecule generation, the output includes continuous 3D coordinates alongside discrete atom types, charges, and bond orders.

## Method

FISHER-FLOW endows the probability simplex with its natural Fisher-Rao Riemannian metric and exploits the isometry between the simplex interior and the positive orthant of the d-hypersphere S_+^d via the sphere map φ(p)=√p. On S_+^d, conditional probability paths are closed-form geodesics (great circles restricted to the positive orthant), and the target vector field u_t = log_{x_t}(x_1)/(1−t) is computed analytically, enabling numerically stable regression with an unconstrained network whose output is orthogonally projected onto the tangent space. Riemannian optimal transport (solved via Sinkhorn on minibatches) couples source and target samples to produce straighter flows with lower-variance gradients. Theoretically, the paper proves that the induced gradient flow is optimal for reducing forward KL divergence (Proposition 1), showing that the Fisher-Rao metric recovers natural-gradient behaviour for free, and that the OT-coupled flow minimises kinetic energy (Proposition 2).

## Results

On DNA promoter sequence design, FISHER-FLOW achieves the best MSE of 0.029 ± 0.001 and PPL of 1.4 ± 2.7, outperforming Dirichlet FM (MSE 0.034, PPL 1.978) and DDSM (MSE 0.033). On DNA enhancer design, FISHER-FLOW achieves PPL of 1.4 ± 0.1 (Melanoma) and 1.4 ± 0.66 (FlyBrain), substantially beating Dirichlet FM (2.25 on both). On LM1B language modelling, FISHER-FLOW achieves PPL ≤26.51 (33B tokens) and ≤22.42 (327B tokens), outperforming MDLM (≤27.04 and ≤23.00 respectively) but remaining behind autoregressive Transformers (22.32 and 20.86).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FISHER-FLOW consistently outperforms Dirichlet FM [68] across all DNA tasks: on promoter design it reduces MSE from 0.034 to 0.029 and PPL from 1.978 to 1.4, and on enhancer design it cuts PPL from ~2.25 to ~1.4 on both Melanoma and FlyBrain. Against D3PM [8], FISHER-FLOW wins on promoter MSE (0.029 vs 0.038) and on the toy density-learning experiment in high dimensions where D3PM exhibits unstable (NaN) behaviour. On LM1B, FISHER-FLOW beats MDLM [62] at both scale points (PPL ≤26.51 vs ≤27.04 at 33B tokens; ≤22.42 vs ≤23.00 at 327B tokens) and substantially outperforms Diffusion-LM [47] (PPL ≤118.62). On QM9 de novo molecule generation, FISHER-FLOW beats FlowMol [29] on stable molecules (88.2% vs 84.2%) but trails JODO [44] (98.7%) and EquiFM [67] (93.2%), with the authors noting that more extensive hyperparameter exploration could close this gap. Against Multinomial Flow [41], FISHER-FLOW achieves lower KL divergence in the toy experiment, particularly in high dimensions where Multinomial Flow also becomes unstable.

## Relation to Prior Work

FISHER-FLOW extends Riemannian Flow Matching (Chen & Lipman, 2023) and standard Flow Matching (Lipman et al., 2023) to the discrete-data setting by identifying the probability simplex under the Fisher-Rao metric with the positive orthant of a hypersphere via the sphere map p → √p. This departs from prior discrete flow-matching approaches (Dirichlet FM, Stark et al. 2024; D3PM, Austin et al. 2021) that either use Dirichlet conditional paths on the simplex or continuous-time Markov chains with hand-designed transition kernels. The key conceptual departure is that the Fisher-Rao geometry yields closed-form geodesics on the sphere, enabling any source distribution (not just uniform) and numerically stable vector-field regression without architectural constraints. The minibatch optimal transport coupling (Tong et al., 2023) is adapted to the Riemannian setting to produce straighter flows.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled geometric unification for discrete generative modeling: the Fisher-Rao metric is shown (Proposition 1) to be the optimal choice for KL-divergence minimization, connecting the flow-matching objective to natural gradient descent without explicit second-order computation. Empirically, FISHER-FLOW improves over Dirichlet FM on DNA promoter and enhancer design, and slightly outperforms MDLM on LM1B language modeling. However, the gap to autoregressive models in language modeling remains substantial (PPL 22.42 vs 20.86), and molecule generation results are competitive but not superior to all baselines (e.g., JODO). Open questions include scaling to longer sequences and larger vocabularies, and whether the geometric advantages persist at scale.
