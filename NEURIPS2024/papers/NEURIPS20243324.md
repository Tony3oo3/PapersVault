## Problem

Current monocular metric depth estimation (MMDE) models exhibit scene dependency: they learn implicit mappings between training scenes and metric scales, causing significant performance degradation on unseen scenes. The paper identifies that the core bottleneck is metric scale recovery (as opposed to relative depth estimation, which generalizes well), and proposes to address this by distilling scene-independent metric scale priors from generative painting models using humans as universal landmarks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243324/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image (H×W×3) and its camera intrinsics (3×3) as input, requiring no metric depth annotations at test time. It produces a pixel-wise metric depth map in meters (H×W×1). Internally, the pipeline generates N painted images (default 32) with humans via Stable Diffusion v2, recovers human meshes via HMR 2.0, and optimizes a per-image metric head over 50 L-BFGS steps before producing the final depth map for the original input.

## Method

MfH is a generate-and-estimate test-time adaptation pipeline that bridges generalizable MRDE (Depth Anything) to zero-shot MMDE without any metric supervision. In the test-time training phase, N humans are painted onto the input image using Stable Diffusion v2 with random text prompts ('a man'/'a woman') and rectangular mask prompts; HMR 2.0 then recovers SMPL meshes (whose shape space is defined in meters) from each painted image, and rasterization yields pseudo metric ground truths. A simple affine metric head (s·D_rel + t) is trained per-image using the SI_log loss against these pseudo ground truths, while per-image affine alignment parameters {s_n, t_n} enforce consistency between relative depth predictions of painted and original images on unpainted regions. The key insight is that humans serve as scene-independent metric landmarks because their sizes are relatively universal across scenes and SMPL's meter-defined shape space provides an absolute scale reference, allowing the generative painting model's implicit scale knowledge to be extracted without any metric depth annotations.

## Results

On NYU-Depth V2, MfH achieves δ₁=83.2, AbsRel=13.7, SIlog=9.78, and RMSE=0.487 in a fully zero-shot setting, outperforming the only other zero-shot baseline DepthCLIP (δ₁=39.4, AbsRel=38.8) by a wide margin. On KITTI, MfH reaches δ₁=81.2 and AbsRel=13.3, again far surpassing DepthCLIP (δ₁=28.1, AbsRel=47.3). On ETH-3D (a mixed indoor/outdoor benchmark), MfH achieves AbsRel=24.0, beating all many-shot baselines including ZeroDepth (32.6) and UniDepth-C (35.5), demonstrating superior cross-domain generalization without any metric depth annotations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MfH decisively outperforms all zero-shot and few/one-shot baselines: on NYUv2 it achieves AbsRel=13.7 vs. DepthCLIP's 38.8 (zero-shot), Hu et al.'s 34.7 (one-shot), and LORN's 101 (few-shot); on KITTI the gap is even larger (13.3 vs. 47.3 for DepthCLIP). Against many-shot methods, MfH trades some in-domain accuracy for cross-domain robustness: it loses to UniDepth-V (AbsRel 5.78 vs. 13.7) and UniDepth-C (6.26 vs. 13.7) on NYUv2, and to UniDepth-C (14.8 vs. 23.3) on iBims-1, but wins on ETH-3D where it achieves AbsRel=24.0 versus 32.6 (ZeroDepth), 35.5 (UniDepth-C), and 43.1 (UniDepth-V). This pattern confirms the paper's central claim that fully supervised MMDE methods are scene-dependent and degrade on unseen domains, while MfH's test-time adaptation provides more consistent zero-shot generalization across diverse indoor and outdoor scenes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by repurposing two well-established but previously disconnected toolchains—generative image painting (Stable Diffusion) and human mesh recovery (HMR 2.0/SMPL)—as a joint oracle for metric scale, rather than learning scale discriminatively from annotated scenes as prior MMDE methods (ZoeDepth, UniDepth, ZeroDepth, Metric3D) do. It builds directly on the MRDE backbone (Depth Anything) and the observation that MMDE decomposes into MRDE plus scale recovery, but departs from the supervised paradigm by treating scale recovery as a test-time adaptation problem solved via a generate-and-estimate loop. The conceptual lineage is closest to zero-shot MMDE works that use language priors (DepthCLIP, WordDepth), but replaces coarse linguistic grounding with pixel-accurate metric measurements from painted humans.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that metric scale information is implicitly encoded in generative painting models and can be extracted without any metric depth annotations, offering a path to truly zero-shot MMDE that generalizes across indoor, outdoor, and in-the-wild scenes without domain-specific fine-tuning. Key limitations include the assumption that humans can plausibly exist in the scene (failing on close-ups), the computational cost of painting and processing 32 images per input, and reliance on MRDE predictions being affine-invariant. Open questions include extending the landmark strategy to non-human objects, better metric head parameterizations beyond a simple affine transform, and whether more advanced generative models will further improve the extracted scale priors.
