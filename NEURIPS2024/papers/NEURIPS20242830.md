## Problem

Zero-shot learning methods assume test-time classes come from the same distribution as training classes, but real-world deployments often face class distribution shifts driven by unknown attributes (e.g., demographic changes in a face recognition system). Existing fairness-aware approaches require knowing the shift attribute in advance, and prior class-distribution-shift work addresses only closed-world settings, leaving the open-world zero-shot scenario with unknown shift attributes unaddressed.

## Input / Output

The method operates on pairs of data points (e.g., face images from CelebA or butterfly images from ETHEC) and learns a representation function g: Z → Ẑ such that same-class pairs have small distance and different-class pairs have large distance under a normalized cosine metric. Training consumes hierarchically sampled subsets of k classes per synthetic environment (with r positive and r negative pairs per class), and the final output is a threshold-agnostic AUC score for verification. The key unusual aspect is that the training data is reorganized into many small class-subset environments each iteration rather than drawn uniformly from all classes.

## Method

The paper first provides a parametric Gaussian model proving that standard ERM training on a contrastive loss assigns disproportionate weight to features that separate the majority attribute type in training, yielding poor separation after a distribution shift. To address this, the proposed algorithm constructs n synthetic environments per iteration by sampling k << N_c classes without replacement, which stochastically produces diverse mixtures of any unknown attribute. It then optimizes a penalized objective combining the average contrastive loss across environments with a VarAUC regularizer—the variance of a soft-AUC (sigmoid-approximated) performance estimate across environments—encouraging the representation to separate classes consistently regardless of attribute composition. A theoretical bound (Eq. 10) determines the minimum number of environments needed to guarantee, with high probability, that at least one environment contains two minority-type classes, enabling learning of negative-pair separation within the minority group.

## Results

On the CelebA face recognition dataset (blond-hair distribution shift, 95%→5%), the proposed VarAUC penalty achieves 0.697 AUC under distribution shift, outperforming the ERM baseline (0.666) by 3.1 percentage points, while maintaining competitive in-distribution performance (0.836 vs. 0.826). On the ETHEC butterfly species dataset (Nymphalidae family shift, 10%→90%), VarAUC reaches 0.838 AUC under shift versus 0.786 for ERM, a 5.2-point improvement. In the parametric simulation (ρ=0.1), VarAUC achieves 0.838 AUC under shift compared to 0.808 for ERM, with all improvements statistically significant (FDR-adjusted p < 0.04).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three OOD environment-balancing penalties (IRM, CLOvE, VarREx) adapted to zero-shot verification, alongside an ERM baseline and a hierarchical-sampling-only baseline. VarAUC (the proposed soft-AUC variance penalty) consistently outperforms all baselines under distribution shift: on CelebA it achieves 0.697 AUC vs. 0.677 (CLOvE), 0.676 (VarREx), and 0.659 (IRM); on ETHEC it reaches 0.838 vs. 0.805 (VarREx), 0.800 (CLOvE), and 0.795 (IRM). VarREx converges to high AUC faster during training but plateaus below VarAUC, while IRM exhibits noisier convergence due to high-variance gradients in contrastive learning. CLOvE provides modest gains over ERM but falls short of the variance-based penalties. Importantly, VarAUC does not sacrifice in-distribution performance (0.836 on CelebA, 0.872 on ETHEC), confirming that the robustness gain comes without a trade-off.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper recasts class distribution shifts in zero-shot verification as an out-of-distribution environment balancing problem, drawing directly on IRM, VarREx, and CLOvE penalties from the OOD generalization literature. However, it departs from these methods in two key ways: (1) it constructs synthetic environments via hierarchical subsampling of class subsets rather than relying on naturally occurring diverse environments, and (2) it proposes a VarAUC penalty that balances performance (soft-AUC) rather than raw loss, motivated by the observation that contrastive/metric learning losses plateau early while distances still vary substantially. The theoretical analysis builds on a parametric Gaussian model to characterize when ERM produces non-robust representations, and the soft-AUC differentiability trick is borrowed from Calders & Jaroszewicz (2007).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to formally address class distribution shifts in zero-shot learning where the shift-inducing attribute is unknown, filling a gap between closed-world DRO/IRM methods (which assume fixed class sets) and fairness-aware re-identification work (which assumes the shift attribute is known in advance). The practical relevance is clear for open-world deployment scenarios (face recognition, species identification) where demographic or ecological shifts are inevitable but unpredictable. Key limitations include testing only binary attributes, using very small synthetic environments (2 classes), and a theoretical model restricted to diagonal Gaussian covariances; extending to multi-attribute or correlated shifts, and validating at larger scale, remain open.
