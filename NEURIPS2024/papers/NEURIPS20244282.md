## Problem

Stochastic algorithms for distributionally robust optimization (DRO) face a fundamental tension: the coupled term q^T ℓ(w) is non-bilinear and the dual feasible set is non-separable (weights must sum to 1), preventing direct application of standard variance-reduction or coordinate-descent techniques. Existing methods either achieve only sublinear rates, require a stringent lower bound of Ω(nG²/μ) on the dual regularization parameter ν for linear convergence, or are restricted to specific uncertainty sets. This paper asks whether a single stochastic algorithm can achieve linear convergence for any ν > 0 across all common closed, convex uncertainty sets.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes n training examples (x_i, y_i) accessed through first-order oracles (ℓ_i, ∇ℓ_i), a specification of the uncertainty set Q (e.g., CVaR tail probability θ, f-divergence radius ρ, or spectral risk vector σ), and two regularization parameters μ > 0 and ν > 0. It produces a primal-dual pair (w*, q*) where w* is the optimal model parameter vector and q* is the optimal reweighting over training examples. Per-iteration cost is O(n + bd) elementary operations with O(n + bd) space (by storing only n/b past primal iterates rather than the full gradient table), and the dual update requires solving a constrained maximization over Q that is set-specific but efficiently computable for standard divergences and risk measures.

## Method

DRAGO is a minibatch primal-dual algorithm that achieves linear convergence through three coupled innovations: (1) minibatch stochastic gradient estimates with tunable batch size b that trade per-iteration cost against variance, (2) a hybrid of randomized block sampling (for unbiased gradient corrections) and cyclic block updates (for maintaining loss/gradient/weight tables at O(bd) cost per iteration), and (3) a novel 'annular' regularization term in the primal update that penalizes distance to all iterates within the last n/b steps. This third component is the key to 'coupled variance reduction': the annular term in the primal update telescopes with the dual noise bound arising from the cyclic loss-table updates, converting what would be a non-vanishing variance term into a telescoping sum. The entire algorithm is governed by a single hyperparameter α (interpreted as a learning rate), and the convergence rate O([n/b + κ_Q L/μ + (n/b)√(nG²/(μν))] ln(1/ε)) captures fine-grained dependence on both the primal condition number L/μ and the dual smoothness parameter nG²/(μν) independently, rather than through a max-over-min composite.

## Results

DRAGO achieves a state-of-the-art linear convergence rate of O([n/b + κ_Q L/μ + (n/b)√(nG²/(μν))] ln(1/ε)) iterations for the penalized DRO problem, with per-iteration cost O(n + bd). Empirically, on the kin8nm regression dataset (n=6553, d=8), DRAGO with b=n/d reaches a 10⁻⁷ normalized primal suboptimality gap within 1 second of wall-clock time, whereas LSVRG only reaches 10⁻² in the same budget. On the CARER text classification task (n=8000, d=270), DRAGO achieves a ~10⁻⁵ primal gap within 4 seconds while SGD and LSVRG gaps remain 2–3 orders of magnitude larger, and DRAGO maintains this advantage even as the dual regularization ν drops by 3 orders of magnitude (from 1.0 to 0.001).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DRAGO is compared against two baselines: distributionally robust SGD (Levy et al., 2020) and LSVRG (Mehta et al., 2023), both evaluated on the CVaR uncertainty set. On all six regression datasets, DRAGO with b=n/d performs best or near-best in wall-clock time; the advantage is most dramatic on kin8nm where DRAGO reaches 10⁻⁷ primal gap in ~1 s while LSVRG only reaches 10⁻² in the same time (a ~5-order-of-magnitude gap). On the CARER text classification task, DRAGO outperforms both SGD and LSVRG by 2–3 orders of magnitude in normalized primal suboptimality within 4 seconds. LSVRG is particularly vulnerable to ill-conditioning: as ν decreases from 1.0 to 0.001, LSVRG is actually outperformed by plain SGD in wall-clock time, whereas DRAGO with b=n/d (≈30 oracle queries per iteration) maintains strong performance across all three ν values. In terms of oracle-query efficiency (left panels of Figs. 2–3), DRAGO with b=1 matches baselines on small datasets (n≤1000) and with b=16 on larger ones, while the b=n/d variant is penalized in raw query count but dominates in wall-clock time when per-query cost is low.

## Relation to Prior Work

DRAGO extends the line of variance-reduced primal-dual methods for distributionally robust optimization initiated by LSVRG (Mehta et al., 2023) and Prospect (Mehta et al., 2024), both of which achieved linear convergence only under a stringent lower bound ν ≥ Ω(nG²/μ) on the dual regularizer. It also builds on the broader variance reduction literature (SVRG, SAGA, stochastic PDHG) and the DRO-specific stochastic methods of Levy et al. (2020) and Carmon & Hausler (2022), which achieved sublinear rates. The key departure is a novel coupled variance reduction mechanism combining cyclic table updates with randomized minibatch corrections and a non-standard regularization term in the primal update, enabling unconditional linear convergence for any ν > 0 across arbitrary closed, convex uncertainty sets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DRAGO removes the ν lower-bound requirement that limited prior linearly convergent DRO algorithms, providing the first method with a clean, fine-grained dependence on both the primal condition number L/μ and the dual smoothness parameter nG²/(μν) separately rather than through a max-over-min composite. This is practically significant because ν controls the trade-off between robustness and conditioning, and practitioners previously had no linearly convergent option for small ν. Open questions include extending the analysis to non-convex objectives, handling Wasserstein-based uncertainty sets directly (which require reformulation), and whether the cyclic+randomized update design generalizes to other non-separable min-max problems beyond DRO.
