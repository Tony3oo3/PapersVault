## Problem

The infinitesimal generator of stochastic diffusion processes is an unbounded operator, rendering standard Hilbert-Schmidt analysis and kernel regression techniques inapplicable. Prior kernel-based approaches to generator learning either restricted to specific SDEs (Laplacian, Langevin), required full knowledge of both drift and diffusion coefficients, or produced numerous spurious eigenvalues that made spectral estimation unreliable. No existing framework simultaneously handled the unboundedness, allowed partial knowledge (diffusion known, drift unknown), and provided dimension-free spectral learning guarantees.

## Input / Output

The method consumes i.i.d. samples drawn from the invariant distribution π, the diffusion coefficient (or equivalently the Dirichlet form operator B = s^T∇), and a C² kernel defining the RKHS; the drift is explicitly not required. It produces the generator's eigenvalues (recovered from resolvent eigenvalues via λ̂_i = μ − 1/ν_i), corresponding eigenfunctions in L²_π, and conditional expectation forecasts E[h(X_t)|X_0=x] as finite spectral sums. A key structural detail is that the risk is measured in the energy space W_π^μ(X) rather than L²_π(X), so the empirical covariance W_μ incorporates both the kernel Gram matrix and derivative-kernel Gram matrices N and M computed from the Dirichlet form.

## Method

The core idea is to 'fight fire with fire': instead of estimating the unbounded generator L directly, the method learns the compact resolvent (μI−L)^{−1} embedded in an energy space W_π^μ(X) whose norm balances the L²_π mass against the Dirichlet energy ||s^T∇f||². A novel energy-based risk functional R(G) = E_μ[||χ_μ(·) − G*φ(·)||_H] replaces the intractable L²_π mean-squared error, and its empirical surrogate decomposes into a variance term (a regularized kernel ridge problem) plus a constant bias term, yielding closed-form KRR and rank-constrained RRR estimators. The RRR estimator is W_{μ,γ}^{−1/2}[⌊W_{μ,γ}^{−1/2}C⌋]_r, where the truncated SVD is computed via a generalized eigenvalue problem on finite kernel matrices K, N, M. Theoretical analysis introduces a metric distortion η(h) = ||h||_H/||h||_W that quantifies how the RKHS-to-energy-space norm gap inflates spectral error, and proves the first finite-sample eigenvalue/eigenfunction bounds for IG learning that are independent of state-space dimension and free of spurious eigenvalues.

## Results

The paper's headline theoretical result (Theorem 2) establishes the first finite-sample spectral learning bounds for the infinitesimal generator, achieving rates of O(n^{-α/(2(α+β))}) when α ≥ τ and O(n^{-α/(2(β+τ))}) when α < τ, which are independent of the state-space dimension d. In contrast, prior methods exhibit dimension-dependent rates: [1] gives O(n^{-d/(2(d+1))}), [19] has variance O(d²/(γ²√n)), and [43] achieves only O(n^{-1/4}). The proposed RRR estimator also reduces time complexity to O(rn²d²) versus O(n³d³) for [19] and [43], and is the first method to provide non-spurious spectral estimation guarantees with partial knowledge of the SDE.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the three prior IG-learning methods ([1] Galerkin for Laplacian, [19] KRR with full knowledge, [43] kernelized diffusion maps for Langevin), the proposed energy-based RRR estimator is the only one that avoids spurious eigenvalues (Fig. 1d shows [19] and [1] producing numerous spurious eigenpairs near zero, while the proposed method recovers clean spectra). The paper also demonstrates that [19] and [43] cannot forecast any dynamical quantity (e.g., conditional pdf) due to spurious eigenpairs preventing relaxation to the Boltzmann distribution, whereas the proposed method successfully predicts the four-well potential dynamics. Against the transfer operator approach [27], the proposed physics-informed RRR method outperforms TO on the Muller-Brown potential (Fig. 1g–i), where TO fails to recover the second eigenfunction (metastable state) while RRR succeeds with the same number of samples. On the real US mortgage rates dataset, the proposed method with imperfect partial knowledge (diffusion estimated via least-squares CIR calibration) outperforms the fully fitted CIR model in continuous ranked probability score for 2017–2018 forecasts (Fig. 1f). The proposed method also achieves dimension-independent learning bounds, overcoming the curse of dimensionality present in [1] and [19].

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the RKHS operator regression framework developed for transfer/Koopman operator learning (Kostic et al., NeurIPS 2022, 2023) to the fundamentally harder problem of learning the infinitesimal generator, which is unbounded. The key departure from prior IG learning work (Hou et al. ICML 2023, Cabannes et al. AISTATS 2024, Pillaud-Vivien & Bach COLT 2023) is the introduction of an energy-based risk functional derived from Dirichlet forms, which replaces the standard L²_π MSE and enables learning under partial knowledge (diffusion coefficient known, drift unknown). The bias-variance decomposition and spectral decay assumptions are adapted from the Koopman operator learning literature, but the analysis is conducted in the energy space W_π^μ rather than L²_π, reflecting the different operator structure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to provide finite-sample spectral learning bounds for infinitesimal generator estimation, and the bounds are independent of state-space dimension—a property absent from all prior kernel-based IG methods. The energy-based framework also resolves the spurious eigenvalue problem that renders prior methods (KRR, Galerkin) unreliable for spectral estimation, as demonstrated empirically. Limitations include the O(n²d²) computational cost, the requirement that the Dirichlet form be known (or estimable with bounded relative error), and empirical validation restricted to 1D and 2D problems. Open questions include extending to non-gradient Dirichlet forms, non-self-adjoint generators, and scaling to higher-dimensional systems.
