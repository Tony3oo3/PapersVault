## Problem

While learning guarantees for first-order SCO algorithms (SCGD, SCSC) have been partially studied, the impact of the derivative-free (black-box) setting on these guarantees remains entirely unexplored. The only prior generalization analysis [21] required impractically large iteration counts (T = O(max{n^{7/2}, m^{7/2}})) and covered only convex cases, leaving a gap for both non-convex settings and all three black-box variants (outer, inner, full).

## Input / Output

The analysis takes as input a compositional training dataset S = {z_1,...,z_n} ∪ {z̄_1,...,z̄_m} with inner and outer samples, initial model parameters (w_1, v_1), and a step-size schedule {η_t}. In the black-box setting, gradients are replaced by zeroth-order estimators constructed from b random unit directions at perturbation distance μ, so the 'input' effectively becomes function evaluations at v + μu rather than ∇f(v). The output is a closed-form excess risk bound decomposed into a generalization term (stability-driven) and an optimization term, with explicit dependence on μ, b, and a dimension-dependent constant d_2 that captures the quality of the gradient estimate.

## Method

The paper develops a new uniform model stability analysis framework that is sharper than prior work [21] under milder parameter conditions. For the convex case, it exploits the co-coercivity property of smooth convex functions to derive O((n⁻¹+m⁻¹)log T) stability bounds with practical step sizes η_t = O(t⁻¹). For the non-convex case, it introduces the almost co-coercivity lemma (from [37,38]) as a surrogate, yielding O((n⁻¹+m⁻¹)T^{1/2}log T) stability. For the black-box variants, the key mechanism is a Taylor-expansion decomposition of the zeroth-order gradient estimator (Eq. 4) combined with Assumption 4 (bounded adjacent function/gradient differences) to upper-bound the Hessian norm of the composite surrogate function, which then feeds into the almost co-coercivity argument. The optimization analysis under the PL condition (Assumption 5) produces excess risk bounds of the form O((n⁻¹+m⁻¹)T^{1/2}log T + n^{-1/2} + μ² + b⁻¹d_2) for outer/inner black-box and O(... + μ⁴ + b⁻²d_2² + b⁻¹d_2) for full black-box, revealing that more unknown gradients amplify sensitivity to estimation quality. The framework is then instantiated for FOO-based VFL and VFL-CZOFO to produce the first stability-based generalization and optimization guarantees for first-order and zeroth-order vertical federated learning.

## Results

For convex SCO, the paper establishes a generalization bound of O((n⁻¹+m⁻¹)log T + n⁻¹ᐟ²) for both SCGD and SCSC (Theorem 2), improving upon the prior O(T⁻¹ᐟ⁷ + (n⁻¹+m⁻¹)T¹ᐟ⁷ + m⁻¹ᐟ²) of Yang et al. (2023) while requiring only T=O(max{n,m}) iterations instead of T=O(max{n⁷ᐟ²,m⁷ᐟ²}). For the non-convex case, the bound becomes O((n⁻¹+m⁻¹)T¹ᐟ²log T + n⁻¹ᐟ²) (Theorem 3). For black-box variants, the excess risk bound is O((n⁻¹+m⁻¹)T¹ᐟ²log T + n⁻¹ᐟ² + μ² + b⁻¹d₂) for outer/inner black-box (Theorem 4, Corollary 1) and O((n⁻¹+m⁻¹)T¹ᐟ²log T + n⁻¹ᐟ² + μ⁴ + b⁻²d₂² + b⁻¹d₂) for full black-box (Corollary 2), where μ is the estimation distance and b is the number of random directions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Compared to the prior SCO generalization analysis by Yang et al. (2023), this paper achieves strictly tighter bounds under milder parameter selections: the convex bound improves from O(T⁻¹ᐟ⁷ + (n⁻¹+m⁻¹)T¹ᐟ⁷ + m⁻¹ᐟ²) to O((n⁻¹+m⁻¹)log T + n⁻¹ᐟ²), and the required iteration count drops from T=O(max{n⁷ᐟ²,m⁷ᐟ²}) to T=O(max{n,m}), which aligns with empirical practice. For optimization, the first-order SCGD and SCSC baselines (Wang et al. 2017, Chen et al. 2021) achieve O(T⁻¹ᐟ⁴) and O(T⁻¹ᐟ²) convergence rates respectively; this paper's black-box variants retain the same stability-based generalization structure but add an estimation-quality term O(μ² + b⁻¹d₂) (outer/inner) or O(μ⁴ + b⁻²d₂² + b⁻¹d₂) (full black-box), revealing that a larger proportion of unknown gradients leads to a stronger (higher-order) dependence on gradient estimation quality. The non-convex generalization bound O((n⁻¹+m⁻¹)T¹ᐟ²log T + n⁻¹ᐟ²) matches the rate of non-compositional non-convex SGD results (Zhou et al. 2022, Lei et al. 2023), confirming that the compositional structure does not fundamentally worsen the generalization rate beyond a constant factor. For VFL applications, the paper provides the first stability-based generalization bound O(n⁻¹T¹ᐟ²log T) for FOO-based VFL and the first excess risk bound O(n⁻¹T¹ᐟ²log T + μ² + b⁻¹d₂) for VFL-CZOFO, with no prior theoretical guarantees existing for these algorithms.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the stability-based generalization analysis of SCO algorithms pioneered by Yang et al. [21] in two key directions: (1) it provides sharper convex bounds with far more practical parameter selections (T = O(max(n,m)) vs. O(max(n^{7/2}, m^{7/2}))), and (2) it extends the analysis to the non-convex regime via an almost co-coercivity lemma, and to the derivative-free (black-box) setting where gradients of f or g are estimated via zeroth-order sampling. The black-box variants build directly on the SCGD [Wang et al., 2017] and SCSC [Chen et al., 2021] algorithms, while the VFL applications connect to the FOO-based VFL [Chen et al., 2020] and VFL-CZOFO [Wang et al., 2023] literature. The stability framework itself draws on the classical Hardt et al. [2016] uniform stability paradigm adapted to the compositional structure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first theoretical work to characterize how the derivative-free (black-box) setting degrades learning guarantees for SCO algorithms, revealing that the full black-box case (both f and g unknown) incurs a μ⁴ dependence versus μ² for single-function black-box, quantifying the cost of losing gradient information. The practical implication is that VFL practitioners using zeroth-order methods now have formal guarantees, and the milder parameter requirements make the theory more actionable. Open questions include whether the PL condition (Assumption 5) can be relaxed, whether the bounds are tight, and whether the analysis extends to multi-level compositional or non-smooth settings. The absence of empirical validation limits immediate practical impact, though the theoretical insights on estimation quality trade-offs are broadly applicable to any zeroth-order compositional problem.
