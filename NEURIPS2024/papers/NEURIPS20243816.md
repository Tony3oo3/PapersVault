## Problem

Fine-tuning large foundation models for specific tasks is prohibitively expensive in both compute and memory, increasingly excluding smaller organizations and individual researchers. The paper asks whether the foundation model can be completely decoupled from the training process by caching its feature representations once and training a lightweight classifier on those cached features, and whether image augmentations can be meaningfully replaced by operations on the cached tensors.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243816/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes RGB images (resized to 256×256 or 512×512) which are passed through a frozen foundation model (DINOv2 or OpenCLIP ViT-B/ViT-G) to produce cached feature tensors of shape k×d (tokens × embedding dimension). The lightweight DeiT-S classifier then operates on these spatial tensors—reshaped to preserve spatial structure—rather than raw pixel data, and outputs a class label. An optional pooling step (max or average) reduces spatial dimensions of the cached features, enabling 512×512 inputs at the same training cost as 256×256.

## Method

LOFF-TA separates feature extraction from classification: training images are passed through a frozen foundation model once, and the resulting feature tensors are cached to disk. During classifier training, spatial tensor augmentations (horizontal/vertical flips, random crops, rotations, shears, translations via nearest-neighbor or linear interpolation) and additive zero-mean Gaussian noise are applied online to the cached tensors, treating them as low-resolution hyper-spectral images. A modified DeiT-S classifier (with the patchifier replaced by a linear projection + LayerNorm) is trained on these augmented tensors using cross-entropy loss, with the foundation model's CLS token summed into the classifier's CLS token. The approach achieves up to 37× faster training and 26× lower GPU memory compared to fine-tuning the foundation model, and in several cases (6 out of 10 configurations) LOFF-TA with pooling at 512×512 outperforms the unfrozen fine-tuned baseline.

## Results

Using DINOv2 ViT-G with 512×512 pooled features, LOFF-TA achieves 91.8 κ on APTOS, 94.6% accuracy on AID, 96.3 AUC on DDSM, 79.9% recall on ISIC, and 90.1% accuracy on NABirds, surpassing the unfrozen+linear fine-tuning upper bound in 6 out of 10 dataset-model configurations. Training throughput reaches 218–228 images/sec with only 13.2 GB GPU memory, regardless of whether the foundation model is ViT-B or ViT-G, yielding up to 37× faster training and up to 26× lower memory compared to full fine-tuning (which requires 345.2 GB for ViT-G). Tensor augmentations consistently improve over no augmentation (LOFF), e.g., 90.4 vs. 89.6 κ on APTOS with ViT-B at 256 resolution.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the three foundation adaptation methods (VPT, SSF, AdaptFormer) in Table 4, standalone LOFF-TA is competitive but not uniformly superior: it wins on APTOS and DDSM versus all three, but loses on ISIC and NABirds to SSF and AdaptFormer (e.g., ISIC recall 72.8 vs. 82.6 for AdaptFormer; NABirds accuracy 83.5 vs. 88.2 for SSF). The key finding is that combining adaptation methods with LOFF-TA yields consistent gains over the adaptation methods alone—SSF+LOFF-TA reaches 97.2 AUC on DDSM (vs. 96.7 for SSF alone) and 81.6 recall on ISIC (vs. 76.4), while AdaptFormer+LOFF-TA improves ISIC recall from 82.6 to 83.5. Compared to the unfrozen+linear fine-tuning upper bound, LOFF-TA+Pool (ViT-G, 512) exceeds it on APTOS (91.8 vs. 89.6), AID (94.6 vs. 96.2—loses), DDSM (96.3 vs. 96.7—loses), ISIC (79.9 vs. 87.3—loses), and NABirds (90.1 vs. 90.2—tie), so the paper's claim of outperforming fine-tuning in 'some cases' is supported primarily by the APTOS and AID results with ViT-B. The dominant advantage is computational: LOFF-TA trains at 218–228 im/sec using 13.2 GB, versus 6 im/sec and 345.2 GB for ViT-G fine-tuning, a 37× speedup and 26× memory reduction.

## Relation to Prior Work

LOFF-TA extends the parameter-efficient fine-tuning lineage (LoRA, adapters, prompt tuning) to its logical extreme by introducing zero intermediate parameters, completely decoupling the foundation model from the training loop. It revives the classical pre-deep-learning paradigm of feature extraction followed by lightweight classifier training, but applies it to modern vision transformers. The tensor augmentation strategy departs from prior feature-space augmentation work (Manifold Mixup, DeVries & Taylor) that relied on interpolation and noise, instead treating cached feature tensors as low-resolution hyper-spectral images and applying spatial transforms (flips, crops, rotations, shears) directly to them. The approach is explicitly complementary to existing adaptation methods like VPT, SSF, and AdaptFormer, and can be stacked on top of them.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 37× training speedup and 26× GPU memory reduction make arbitrarily large foundation models (e.g., ViT-G) accessible in resource-limited settings, with practical implications for medical imaging and remote sensing where high-resolution processing is essential but compute is scarce. The finding that LOFF-TA occasionally outperforms full fine-tuning (6/10 configurations) challenges the assumption that more trainable parameters always help, though this is not universal. Open questions include why spatial tensor augmentations work despite disrupting positional encodings, whether auto-augment strategies can be developed for feature tensors, and whether the approach extends beyond classification to detection or segmentation tasks.
