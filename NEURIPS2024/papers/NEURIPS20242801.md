## Problem

Existing AI-generated text detection methods rely on binary classification (human vs. AI) or hand-crafted statistical features, which bottleneck performance and fail to generalize to out-of-distribution data or newly emerged LLMs. The paper argues that treating all LLM outputs as a single category ignores the distinct writing styles each model family and individual model produces, and that the detection task should instead be reformulated as distinguishing diverse writing styles within a high-dimensional feature space.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242801/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a query text of up to 512 tokens as input and produces a human/AI classification label via K-Nearest Neighbors (KNN) similarity search against a pre-encoded feature database (built with Faiss for efficient retrieval). During training, the encoder also outputs a d-dimensional embedding that encodes multi-level writing-style relations; during inference, the cosine similarity between the query embedding and each database entry serves as the writing-style similarity score that drives the KNN classification decision.

## Method

DeTeCtive fine-tunes a pre-trained text encoder (e.g., SimCSE-RoBERTa) with a novel multi-level contrastive loss that enforces hierarchical similarity constraints: text from the same specific model should be closer than text from the same company's models, which should be closer than text from any LLM, which should be closer than human-written text. This is combined with a multi-task auxiliary binary cross-entropy classifier to encourage discriminative features. At inference, instead of using the classification head, the method employs a dense retrieval pipeline: the query is encoded and compared via cosine similarity to all pre-encoded training samples stored in a feature database, with KNN determining the final label. A key practical contribution is Training-Free Incremental Adaptation (TFIA), where newly encountered OOD data is simply encoded with the frozen model and appended to the database, improving OOD detection without any retraining.

## Results

DeTeCtive achieves state-of-the-art AvgRec on all four primary benchmarks: 98.44 on M4-monolingual (surpassing the runner-up SCL by 6.52%), 93.42 on M4-multilingual (by 7.15%), 99.74 on TuringBench (by 0.15%), and 96.15 on Deepfake Cross-domains & Cross-models (by 2.66%). In out-of-distribution detection, the margins widen dramatically: 92.19 AvgRec on Unseen Models (vs. 86.61 for Longformer, a 5.58% gap) and 82.60 on Unseen Domains (vs. 68.40, a 14.20% gap). With Training-Free Incremental Adaptation, Unseen Domains improves further to 89.63, a 21.23% advantage over the next best method.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DeTeCtive outperforms all eight baselines on every dataset tested. Against the strongest training-based competitor, SCL (supervised contrastive learning, ICLR 2021), it leads by 6.52% AvgRec on M4-monolingual and 7.15% on M4-multilingual, and by 5.56% on Deepfake. The statistical methods GLTR (55.42) and DetectGPT (60.48) and the manual-feature method FastText (78.80) trail by 27–41% on Deepfake Cross-domains & Cross-models. In OOD scenarios the gap is most pronounced: on Unseen Domains, DeTeCtive scores 82.60 versus Longformer's 68.40 and DetectGPT's 60.48, a 14.20% and 22.12% margin respectively. Even under paraphrasing attacks (OUTFOX), DeTeCtive retains 97.0% AvgRec while the next-best method (OUTFOX itself) drops to 61.8%, confirming robustness advantages over all baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant binary classification paradigm for AI-generated text detection (GPT-Sentinel, SCL, CoCo) by reframing the problem as multi-level writing-style discrimination within a continuous feature space. It builds on SimCLR's contrastive learning framework and SimCSE's text-embedding approach, but introduces a hierarchical contrastive loss that encodes four levels of similarity (same model > same company > any LLM > human), drawing an analogy to familial kinship. The inference pipeline replaces a learned classifier with dense retrieval via KNN over a pre-encoded feature database, a design choice that also enables the Training-Free Incremental Adaptation (TFIA) mechanism for OOD data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 14.20% AvgRec improvement on Unseen Domains OOD detection and the TFIA mechanism (simply appending OOD data to the database without retraining) address a critical practical gap: current detectors degrade sharply on new LLMs or domains and require costly retraining. The robustness to paraphrasing attacks (97.0% AvgRec under OUTFOX attack vs. 61.8% for the OUTFOX detector) further strengthens its practical relevance. Open questions include whether the multi-level hierarchy generalizes beyond the 7 model families in Deepfake, whether interpretability of the learned style features can be achieved, and whether the approach scales to truly novel model architectures with fundamentally different generation paradigms.
