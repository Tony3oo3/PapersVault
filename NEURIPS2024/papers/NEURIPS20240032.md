## Problem

Differentiable audio synthesis methods like DDSP lack physical controllability and cannot capture nonlinear string phenomena such as pitch glide and phantom partials, while physically accurate solvers like FDTD are computationally prohibitive at O(Nx·Nt) and non-differentiable. Modal synthesis is efficient at O(1) per query but is restricted to linear systems. The paper addresses this gap by introducing a differentiable framework that combines the physical inductive bias of modal decomposition with the nonlinear expressiveness of neural modulation.

## Input / Output

The model takes PDE parameters (stiffness κ, tension ratio α, damping coefficients σ₀ and σ₁) encoded via a random Fourier feature layer, initial pluck conditions (position p_x and amplitude p_a), and a query point (x, t) on a 256-point spatial grid at 48 kHz temporal resolution. It outputs a single scalar displacement u(x,t) with O(1) inference cost per query; reading at a fixed x yields a sound waveform, while concatenating over all x at successive t values produces a full spatio-temporal motion visualization. Unlike FDTD which requires sequential temporal recursion, DMSP evaluates any (x,t) independently.

## Method

DMSP decomposes the nonlinear string PDE solution into a physically grounded linear modal skeleton (inharmonic mode frequencies from stiffness) plus learned nonlinear corrections. A Mode Estimator MLP predicts mode frequencies and amplitudes from the initial condition, and separate AM and FM MLP blocks modulate the linear solution's amplitudes and frequencies to approximate the nonlinear dynamics (pitch glide, phantom partials). A gating function tanh(α−1) on the FM block suppresses frequency modulation for linear strings (α=1), preserving the exact modal solution in that regime. Two variants are provided: DMSP-Hybrid precomputes modes offline via root-finding and only learns AM/FM, while fully-neural DMSP also learns the mode estimator end-to-end. Training is supervised against FDTD ground truth using a composite loss of L1 waveform, multi-scale spectral, pitch (L_f0), and mode (L_m) terms, where the pitch loss proves critical for stabilizing FM learning on nonlinear strings.

## Results

On the nonlinear string test set (α>1), DMSP-Hybrid achieves an SI-SDR of 15.67 dB, SDR of 16.46 dB, MSS of 4.77 dB, and pitch error of 1.03 Hz, substantially outperforming all baselines. On the linear test set (α=1), DMSP-Hybrid also leads with SI-SDR of −2.84 dB and SDR of 1.50 dB, while Modal achieves the lowest pitch error (0.42 Hz) due to its oracle frequency information. The fully neural DMSP variant (no pre-computed modes) reaches SI-SDR of −10.32 dB and MSS of 5.66 dB on the nonlinear set, still surpassing Modal (−16.61 dB SI-SDR, 17.25 dB MSS) and both DDSPish variants. The ablation confirms that the pitch loss L_{f0} is critical: removing it degrades DMSP-Hybrid's nonlinear SI-SDR from 15.67 to 1.51 dB and MSS from 4.77 to 8.98 dB.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DMSP-Hybrid decisively outperforms the Modal baseline in the nonlinear regime, improving SI-SDR by ~32 dB (15.67 vs. −16.61), MSS by ~12.5 dB (4.77 vs. 17.25), and pitch error by ~1.3 Hz (1.03 vs. 2.32), because Modal can only represent the linear solution and cannot capture pitch glide or phantom partials. Against DDSPish (harmonic+noise with learned FM), DMSP-Hybrid gains ~41.6 dB in nonlinear SI-SDR and ~5 dB in MSS, showing that learned frequency modulation over integer-multiple harmonics is insufficient without physically grounded mode frequencies. DDSPish-XFM (no FM, purely harmonic) is the weakest baseline, with DMSP-Hybrid surpassing it by ~62.5 dB in nonlinear SI-SDR and ~13 Hz in pitch error. In the linear case, Modal retains a slight edge in pitch accuracy (0.42 vs. 0.79 Hz) because its pre-computed mode frequencies serve as an oracle, but DMSP-Hybrid still leads in SI-SDR and SDR, indicating better modeling of frequency-dependent damping. The fully neural DMSP (no pre-computation) narrows the gap to DMSP-Hybrid in the nonlinear case (SI-SDR −10.32 vs. 15.67 dB), with the gap attributed to the training distribution rarely sampling α=1 exactly.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously separate lineages: differentiable digital signal processing (DDSP, Engel et al. 2019), which provides a differentiable spectral-modeling-synthesis backbone but is constrained to integer-multiple harmonics, and classical modal synthesis (Adrien 1991), which is physically grounded and naturally inharmonic but only solves linear PDEs and requires offline precomputation. DMSP departs from DDSP by replacing the fixed harmonic skeleton with mode frequencies derived from the string's eigenvalue problem, and from modal synthesis by wrapping the modal solution in differentiable AM/FM modulation blocks that approximate the nonlinear Kirchhoff-Carrier PDE. It also extends the differentiable-physical-modeling direction initiated by Schlecht et al. (FNO-based, unconditional) and Jin et al. (FEM-supervised contact sounds) by conditioning on physical parameters and jointly outputting both sound and spatial motion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DMSP achieves O(1) per-query inference for nonlinear string simulation, compared to O(Nx·Nt) for FDTD, while capturing nonlinear phenomena (pitch glide, phantom partials) that DDSP's integer-harmonic structure cannot represent. The ablation showing that the pitch-regression loss (L_f0) is the critical training signal for the FM block—its removal collapses nonlinear SI-SDR from 15.7 dB to 1.5 dB—provides a practical recipe for training differentiable oscillators with non-convex frequency parameters. Key open questions include generalization beyond planar strings to 3D bodies, handling real-world measured parameters (the authors acknowledge this limitation), and whether the modal-decomposition inductive bias transfers to other nonlinear PDE systems in acoustics or structural dynamics.
