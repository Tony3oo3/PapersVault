## Problem

In the multi-armed bandit problem with strategic arms under debt-free reporting (arms can only report values ≤ their observed reward), prior work achieves only O(T^{2/3}) regret because fixed-design exploration requires bonuses proportional to the exploration length to ensure truthfulness. The paper asks whether an adaptive mechanism can recover the classical O(log(T)/Δ) or O(√(KT log(T))) regret bounds while maintaining incentive compatibility for all arms.

## Input / Output

The player observes K arms with bounded [0,1] reward distributions over a horizon T, receiving only the reported values x_{k,t} (which may be strictly less than the true reward r_{k,t} due to strategic withholding). The player outputs an arm selection sequence and end-of-game bonus payments Ψ_k to each arm. Regret is computed relative to μ_2 (the second-highest true mean) rather than μ_1, since the optimal arm only needs to marginally outperform the second-best to be selected, and bonuses are subtracted from the player's revenue in the regret calculation.

## Method

The paper introduces S-SE (Strategic Successive Elimination), which replaces the fixed exploration phase of S-ETC with adaptive Hoeffding-based elimination of arms, creating a strategic tradeoff: an arm that withholds reward inflates its apparent gap from the best arm and is eliminated sooner, capping its cumulative savings. A carefully calibrated bonus function then ensures truthful reporting is a dominant strategy: the best arm receives a second-price-auction-style bonus compensating the gap to the second-best reported mean, while suboptimal arms receive bonuses inversely proportional to their estimated gap from the best. This adaptive design keeps bonus costs sublinear, yielding a dominant-strategy subgame perfect equilibrium with regret O(Σ log(T)/Δ_{1k} + Σ log(T)/Δ_{2k}) problem-dependently and O(√(KT log(T))) worst-case, improving over the O(T^{2/3}) bound of S-ETC.

## Results

Under the dominant truthful SPE established by Theorem 4.1, Algorithm 1 (S-SE) achieves a problem-dependent regret of O(Σ_{k=3}^{K} log(T)/Δ_{2k} + Σ_{k=2}^{K} log(T)/Δ_{1k}) and a worst-case (instance-independent) regret of O(√(KT log(T))). This improves upon the O(T^{2/3}) regret of the prior S-ETC mechanism [8] under the same debt-free reporting setting. Empirically, with 6 arms over 10⁴ rounds averaged over 100 epochs, truthful reporting yields logarithmic cumulative regret, while arbitrary untruthful reporting produces the worst regret, and arms' utilities are confirmed to be higher under the truthful SPE than under untruthful strategies.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole direct baseline is S-ETC (Strategic Explore-Then-Commit) from Braverman et al. [8, COLT 2019], which addresses the same strategic MAB problem under debt-free reporting. S-SE strictly outperforms S-ETC on the regret metric: S-ETC incurs O(T^{2/3}) cumulative regret within a Nash equilibrium, whereas S-SE achieves O(log(T)/Δ) problem-dependent regret (matching the order of classical non-strategic successive elimination up to an additive second term) and O(√(KT log(T))) worst-case regret. The improvement stems from S-SE's adaptive successive elimination design, which creates a strategic trade-off (high savings from dishonesty vs. risk of swift elimination) that reduces the bonus required for incentive-compatibility, in contrast to S-ETC's fixed exploration phase where the bonus is proportional to the exploration length. Both methods operate under the tacit model and debt-free reporting, but S-SE uses payment-based bonuses while S-ETC uses additional rounds; the paper notes that the regret improvement is independent of the bonus type since bonuses are subtracted from player revenue in the regret calculation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the strategic multi-armed bandit framework of Braverman et al. (COLT 2019), who introduced the S-ETC algorithm achieving O(T^{2/3}) regret under debt-free reporting using a fixed-design explore-then-commit approach. The key departure is replacing the fixed exploration phase with adaptive successive elimination (from Even-Dar et al., JMLR 2006), which creates a natural strategic trade-off: arms that misreport are eliminated faster, reducing the bonus needed to incentivize truthfulness. This yields a dominant-strategy subgame perfect equilibrium (stronger than the Nash equilibrium in prior work) and improves regret to O(log(T)/Δ) or O(√(KT log(T))) in the worst case.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The improvement from O(T^{2/3}) to logarithmic regret closes a significant gap in the strategic MAB literature and shows that the non-strategic optimal rate is achievable even with strategic arms under debt-free reporting, provided the mechanism is carefully designed. The dominant-strategy SPE is a stronger guarantee than prior Nash-equilibrium results, making the mechanism robust to any arm strategy. Open questions include whether other no-regret algorithms (e.g., UCB) can be adapted with suitable incentives, and whether the M-bounded savings assumption needed for arbitrary strategy profiles can be relaxed. The practical relevance to e-commerce and clinical trial settings where agents can withhold but not fabricate outcomes makes this a timely contribution.
