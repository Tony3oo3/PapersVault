## Problem

For complex compositional inference queries such as marginal MAP, probabilistic answer set programming, and causal backdoor/frontdoor adjustment, tractability conditions on circuits have been derived in an instance-specific manner, requiring significant effort for each new query. This paper asks whether a unified algebraic framework over arbitrary semirings can systematically characterize when compositions of basic operators remain tractable, and whether this yields simpler or less restrictive conditions than those previously known.

## Input / Output

The framework takes as input one or more algebraic circuits over arbitrary commutative semirings (Boolean, probabilistic, (max,·), etc.), a variable set specifying which variables to aggregate over, an elementwise mapping function τ between semirings (e.g., the support mapping from Boolean to probabilistic), and fully-factorized weight/labeling functions. It produces an output circuit that is guaranteed smooth and decomposable, along with the specific circuit properties (X-determinism, X-compatibility, X-support-compatibility) required for each intermediate step and the overall complexity bound. A key structural constraint is that the number of composed operators must be bounded for the algorithm to remain polynomial-time.

## Method

The paper introduces a compositional inference framework built on three basic operators—aggregation (⊕ over a variable set), product (⊗ of two functions), and elementwise mapping (τ from semiring S to S′)—and proves sufficient tractability conditions for each when applied to circuits. For aggregation, smoothness and decomposability suffice (linear time); for product, the paper introduces X-support-compatibility as a new property enabling linear-time multiplication (complementing the existing compatibility condition which gives quadratic time); for elementwise mapping, the mapping must distribute over sums (via additivity or circuit determinism) and over products (via multiplicativity or a Prod 0/1 condition). The key composability results (Theorem 5) show how X-determinism, X-compatibility, and X-support-compatibility are preserved through each operator, enabling systematic derivation of conditions for arbitrary compositions. Applying this framework, the paper proves that 2AMC with X-firstness alone is #P-hard (correcting a prior claim), that adding X-determinism suffices for linear-time 2AMC, and that causal backdoor/frontdoor adjustment can be computed in linear/quadratic time (improving over prior quadratic/cubic bounds) by exploiting support-compatibility to avoid requiring structured decomposability.

## Results

The paper establishes that 2AMC (second-level algebraic model counting) is #P-hard even for smooth, decomposable, deterministic, and X-first circuits (Theorem 7), correcting a gap in prior work [29]. By adding X-determinism, all 2AMC instances become tractable in O(|C|) time (Theorem 8). For causal inference, the paper reduces backdoor adjustment complexity from O(|C|²) to O(|C|) (with Z-determinism) and frontdoor adjustment from O(|C|³) to O(|C|²), both improving upon prior results [49]. An exponential separation (Theorem 9) shows X-deterministic circuits can be exponentially more succinct than X-first circuits as |X| grows.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical paper with no empirical baselines; comparisons are against prior tractability/complexity results. For 2AMC, the paper proves that the conditions stated in [29] (smooth, decomposable, deterministic, X-first) are insufficient (Theorem 7, #P-hardness), and provides the corrected sufficient condition of adding X-determinism (Theorem 8). For causal backdoor adjustment, prior work [49] required structured decomposability plus (X∪Z)-determinism for O(|C|²) complexity; this paper shows that adding Z-determinism removes the structured decomposability requirement and reduces complexity to O(|C|). For frontdoor adjustment, [49] reported O(|C|³) under structured decomposability; this paper shows the actual complexity is O(|C|²) under the same conditions, via support-compatibility. The paper also shows that X-determinism alone (without X-firstness) suffices for some 2AMC subproblems, and provides an exponential succinctness separation (Theorem 9) demonstrating that X-firstness is strictly stronger than X-determinism.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper generalizes the compositional atlas for probabilistic circuits (Vergari et al., NeurIPS 2021) and the knowledge compilation map (Darwiche & Marquis, JAIR 2002) from specific semirings (probabilistic, Boolean) to arbitrary commutative semirings, introducing elementwise mapping as a third basic operator alongside aggregation and product. It directly corrects the 2AMC framework (Kiesel et al., ICLP 2022) by proving that the stated tractability conditions (smooth, decomposable, deterministic, X-first) are actually insufficient, and provides new sufficient conditions involving X-determinism. It also improves upon the causal inference results of Wang & Kwiatkowska (AISTATS 2023) by reducing backdoor adjustment from quadratic to linear time and frontdoor from cubic to quadratic, using a newly introduced X-support-compatibility property.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides a systematic blueprint for deriving tractability conditions for novel compositional inference queries on circuits, reducing the ad-hoc effort previously required for each new query. The correction of the 2AMC conditions and the exponential separation result (Theorem 9) between X-deterministic and X-first circuits have direct implications for knowledge compilation targets like SDDs. A key limitation is that the sufficient conditions may not be necessary for specific semiring-query combinations, and the paper focuses on exact inference; extending the compositional approach to approximate algorithms (e.g., for MMAP) is identified as an open direction. The framework also motivates new compilation and learning algorithms targeting properties like X-determinism that are not yet standard in practice.
