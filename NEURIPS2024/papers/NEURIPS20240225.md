## Problem

While the saturation effect of kernel ridge regression (KRR) was rigorously proved in fixed dimensions (occurring when source smoothness s > 2τ), its occurrence in large dimensions (n ≍ d^γ) remained only a conjecture. Zhang et al. (2024) showed KRR fails to match the minimax lower bound for s > 1 in large dimensions but could not prove that any spectral algorithm achieves that bound. This paper closes the gap by proving kernel gradient flow is minimax optimal in large dimensions, thereby rigorously establishing the saturation effect and revealing that the threshold shifts from s > 2τ (fixed d) to s > τ (large d).

## Input / Output

The analysis takes n i.i.d. samples (x_i, y_i) drawn from the uniform distribution on the unit sphere S^d, an inner product kernel K(x,x') = Φ(⟨x,x'⟩) with smooth profile Φ, and a regression function f_★ in the interpolation space [H]^s. The large-dimensional scaling is parameterized by γ > 0 with n ≍ d^γ, and the spectral algorithm is characterized by its analytic filter function's qualification τ ≥ 1. The output is a tight Θ_P characterization of the excess risk convergence rate as a function of d, γ, s, and τ, along with an improved minimax lower bound that replaces the previous d^{-ε} gap with a poly^{-1}(ln d) factor.

## Method

The paper establishes three main results: (1) kernel gradient flow with early stopping achieves the rate d^{-min{γ-p, s(p+1)}} · poly(ln d) for all s > 0 and γ > 0, matching the minimax lower bound; (2) the minimax lower bound is tightened from Ω(d^{-min{γ-p, s(p+1)}} · d^{-ε}) to Ω(d^{-min{γ-p, s(p+1)}}) / poly(ln d) via a refined covering-number argument exploiting the spherical harmonic structure; (3) for general spectral algorithms with qualification τ, the exact rate is d^{-min{γ-p, τ(γ-p+1)+p·s̃/(τ+1), s̃(p+1)}} where s̃ = min{s, 2τ}, which is strictly slower than the minimax rate whenever s > τ. The key technical difficulty is that the polynomial eigendecay assumption λ_j ≍ j^{-β} used in fixed-dimensional proofs breaks in large dimensions because the hidden constants depend on d; instead, the analysis leverages the explicit multiplicity structure N(d,k) of spherical harmonics and the analytic functional argument of Li et al. (2024) adapted to this setting.

## Results

The paper establishes that large-dimensional kernel gradient flow (qualification τ = ∞) achieves an excess risk of Θ_P(d^{−min{γ−p, s(p+1)}}) · poly(ln d) for all s > 0 and γ > 0, matching the improved minimax lower bound Ω(d^{−min{γ−p, s(p+1)}}) / poly(ln d) up to a logarithmic factor. In contrast, KRR (τ = 1) achieves only d^{−min{γ−p, (γ−p+1+p·min{s,2})/2, min{s,2}(p+1)}} · poly(ln d), which is strictly slower than the minimax rate whenever s > 1. For general spectral algorithms with finite qualification τ, the saturation threshold shifts from s > 2τ (fixed dimension) to s > τ (large dimension), meaning the algorithm cannot achieve the minimax rate once the source smoothness exceeds its qualification.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Compared to Zhang et al. (2024), which established the KRR rate d^{−min{γ−p, (γ−p+1+p·min{s,2})/2, min{s,2}(p+1)}} and conjectured the minimax lower bound was tight, this paper proves the conjecture by showing kernel gradient flow achieves the minimax rate d^{−min{γ−p, s(p+1)}} · poly(ln d) for all s > 0, rigorously confirming the saturation effect of KRR in large dimensions. Compared to Lu et al. (2023), which proved kernel gradient flow is minimax optimal only for s = 1 and provided a minimax lower bound with an additional d^{−ε} factor, this paper extends the minimax optimality to all s > 0 and γ > 0, and tightens the lower bound by replacing the d^{−ε} term with a poly(ln d)^{−1} factor that does not alter the polynomial convergence rate. The key quantitative gap: for s > 1 and γ ∈ (p(s+1)+1, (p+1)(s+1)), KRR's rate is d^{−min{s,2}(p+1)} while kernel gradient flow achieves d^{−s(p+1)}, a gap that grows with s. The paper also reveals that the saturation threshold in large dimensions is s > τ (vs. s > 2τ in fixed dimensions), a strictly weaker condition that makes saturation more prevalent in high-dimensional settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the fixed-dimensional saturation effect of KRR (conjectured for decades, proved by Li et al. 2022) to the large-dimensional regime where n ∝ d^γ, and generalizes from two specific algorithms (KRR, kernel gradient flow) to a unified class of spectral algorithms parameterized by qualification τ. It builds directly on Zhang et al. (2024), who established upper and lower bounds for large-dimensional KRR but could only conjecture the saturation effect, and on Lu et al. (2023), who proved minimax optimality of kernel gradient flow only for s = 1. The analytic filter function framework from Bauer et al. (2007) and the 'analytic functional argument' from Li et al. (2024) provide the technical backbone, though the authors note that the polynomial eigendecay assumption underlying the fixed-dimensional proofs does not carry over to large dimensions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper resolves a key open conjecture by rigorously proving that KRR is suboptimal in large dimensions whenever s > 1 (not s > 2 as in fixed dimensions), with the saturation threshold shifting from s > 2τ to s > τ. This has direct implications for the 'lazy regime' interpretation of neural networks: since NTK-based gradient flow is minimax optimal while KRR is not, results on large-dimensional KRR cannot be naively transferred to wide neural networks. Limitations include the restriction to inner product kernels on the sphere with uniform input distribution, and the analysis is confined to the optimal-tuning regime; the learning curve for arbitrary λ and the kernel interpolation (λ = 0) case remain open.
