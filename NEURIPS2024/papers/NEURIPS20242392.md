## Problem

Existing image retargeting methods struggle to simultaneously preserve key semantic content and avoid visual artifacts when adjusting aspect ratios. Traditional pixel-shifting approaches like seam-carving lack semantic guidance, causing distortion of important objects, while GAN-based generative methods regenerate entire images without explicit semantic priors, producing inconsistent foregrounds. The paper addresses the gap of achieving content-aware, artifact-free retargeting that generalizes to arbitrary target aspect ratios.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image and a target aspect ratio (tested on 16:9, 4:3, 1:1, and 9:16) and produces a retargeted image at that ratio. A pretrained VST salient object detection model generates a saliency map that drives both the pruning and repainting stages. The output preserves the original foreground structure while locally regenerating only the disrupted regions, with the final height adjusted (via outpainting) when the target ratio requires vertical expansion beyond the original image height.

## Method

PruneRepaint is a two-stage pipeline combining content-aware seam-carving (CSC) with adaptive repainting (AR). CSC modifies the standard seam-carving energy function by adding a saliency prior from VST and a spatial prior that decays from the saliency centroid, enabling hierarchical pruning at both scene-level (background vs. foreground) and object-level (preserving discriminative components within objects); a tolerable saliency loss ratio λ caps how many seams may intersect salient regions. AR consists of an Adaptive Repainting Region Determination (ARRD) module that applies a 1D sliding window over the pruning mask to flag abrupt pixels with high removed-neighbor density, and decides between inpainting and outpainting by comparing the saliency width against the target width; and an Image-guided Repainting (IR) module that uses Stable Diffusion 1.5 with ControlNet-Inpainting (conditioning on visible pixels) and IP-Adapter (CLIP image encoder with decoupled cross-attention on the original image) to repaint only the identified regions. The key design choice is selective local regeneration rather than global image synthesis, which preserves foreground consistency with the original while fixing only the discontinuities introduced by pixel removal.

## Results

On the RetargetMe benchmark, PruneRepaint achieves a Saliency Discard Ratio (SDR) of 0.151 at 16:9, 0.074 at 4:3, 0.031 at 1:1, and 0.006 at 9:16, substantially outperforming all baselines (e.g., seam-carving at 0.490 and InGAN at 0.569 at 16:9). In the subjective user study at 16:9, PruneRepaint obtains the highest average score of 2.582/3 across content completeness, deformation, local smoothness, and aesthetics, compared to 2.370 for cropping, 1.720 for scaling, 1.461 for seam-carving, and 1.126 for InGAN. The method also demonstrates strong generalization, with SDR dropping to near-zero (0.006) at the extreme 9:16 ratio where all other methods retain 16–22% saliency loss.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PruneRepaint decisively outperforms both seam-carving [1] and InGAN [31] on the objective SDR metric across all four aspect ratios: at 16:9, SDR drops from 0.490 (seam-carving) and 0.569 (InGAN) to 0.151, a 3.2× and 3.8× reduction respectively. In the subjective evaluation at 16:9, PruneRepaint (avg 2.582) surpasses seam-carving (1.461) and InGAN (1.126) by a wide margin on every individual criterion, particularly deformation (2.757 vs. 0.973/0.975) and aesthetics (2.538 vs. 1.038/0.866). Against the simpler baselines, cropping achieves the best deformation score (2.905) but sacrifices content completeness (1.295), while scaling preserves content (2.875) but suffers severe deformation (0.975); PruneRepaint strikes a balance, scoring above 2.3 on all four subjective dimensions. The full-repainting baseline (FR), which uses the same SD1.5 + IP-Adapter pipeline but repaints the entire image, still trails PruneRepaint on SDR (0.524 vs. 0.151 at 16:9), confirming that selective adaptive repainting is critical rather than global regeneration.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two well-established lines of work: content-aware image retargeting via pixel-shifting (seam-carving and its semantic extensions like DeepIR and SmartScale) and diffusion-based image editing (Repaint, ControlNet, IP-Adapter). It departs from GAN-based retargeting methods (InGAN, SinGAN, MRGAN, MCGAN) by replacing global regeneration with local, mask-guided diffusion inpainting, and from traditional seam-carving by augmenting the energy function with hierarchical saliency and spatial priors. The key conceptual move is treating retargeting as a two-stage pipeline—semantic-aware pruning followed by adaptive local repainting—rather than a single monolithic operation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method offers a practical, ratio-agnostic solution to a task with clear real-world demand (multi-screen display adaptation), and the adaptive inpaint/outpaint decision based on foreground size is a useful design choice. However, the evaluation is limited to a single 80-image dataset (RetargetMe) and subjective scoring at only one aspect ratio (16:9), leaving generalization claims under-supported. The authors acknowledge that inference speed (~7 s/image) limits real-time use and that the ARRD module lacks global scene understanding, causing residual misalignment artifacts on elongated objects—both open problems for future work.
