## Problem

Global pruning of LLMs is memory-prohibitive because it requires loading the entire model into a single GPU, while existing local pruning methods (SparseGPT, Wanda) minimize per-layer error independently, over-constraining intermediate activations and yielding suboptimal solutions—especially at high sparsity (>60%). No prior method achieves global optimality with the low memory footprint needed for billion-parameter models.

## Input / Output

The method consumes pre-trained LLM weights (OPT, LLaMA-2, LLaMA-3), 128 calibration segments of 2048 tokens from C4, and a target sparsity level (70%–90% unstructured or 3:4 semi-structured). It produces pruned sparse weight matrices and binary sparsity masks for the first 50% of decoder layers. Calibration data is used to compute pseudo-inverses of activation matrices and to drive the alternating optimization; the method operates per-decoder-layer, requiring only one FFN module's worth of memory at a time rather than the full model.

## Method

SparseLLM reformulates global pruning by introducing auxiliary variables z_ℓ (linear-layer outputs) and a_ℓ (activation outputs) that decouple dense linear layers from nonlinear operations, then relaxes the equality constraints with L2 penalties (weighted by α, β) to obtain an unconstrained objective. The FFN module (containing >2/3 of parameters) within each decoder layer is pruned globally via alternating optimization over weights, activations, and outputs, while MHA layers fall back to local pruning. Each subproblem admits a closed-form solution: weight pruning delegates to SparseGPT, activation updates reduce to ridge regression, and output updates exploit the entry-wise nature of ReLU/SiLU for per-element if-then logic or lookup-table evaluation. A tunable set Ω controls the degree of global coupling, interpolating between fully local (Ω=∅) and fully global (Ω=all layers) pruning, with the default configuration coupling only the two linear layers within each FFN block.

## Results

On LLaMA-2 7b at 80% unstructured sparsity, SparseLLM achieves a WikiText2 perplexity of 53.20, compared to 1814.01 for SparseGPT and 6380.27 for Magnitude pruning. At 90% sparsity, SparseLLM reaches 344.97 (WT2) versus 5206.93 for SparseGPT, representing a perplexity reduction of over 93%. At 70% sparsity, SparseLLM is comparable to SparseGPT (16.15 vs. 15.98 on WT2), but the gap widens dramatically as sparsity increases. On zero-shot tasks (e.g., OPT-1.3b at 90% sparsity), SparseLLM achieves a mean accuracy of 40.95% versus 37.72% for SparseGPT, with the largest gains on HellaSwag (38.19% vs. 33.17%) and ARC-e (46.68% vs. 39.98%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SparseLLM is compared against three baselines: Magnitude pruning, Wanda, and SparseGPT. Against Magnitude and Wanda, SparseLLM wins by an overwhelming margin at every sparsity level—for example, at 70% sparsity on LLaMA-2 7b WT2, SparseLLM achieves 16.15 perplexity versus 1058.00 (Magnitude) and 2644.22 (Wanda). Wanda in particular degrades catastrophically at 80%+ sparsity, frequently producing NaN perplexity values. Against SparseGPT (the strongest baseline), SparseLLM is comparable at 70% sparsity (16.15 vs. 15.98 on LLaMA-2 7b WT2) but pulls decisively ahead at higher sparsity: at 80% WT2, SparseLLM achieves 53.20 versus SparseGPT's 1814.01 (a ~34× reduction), and at 90% WT2, 344.97 versus 5206.93. The paper reports that perplexity reductions of up to ~80% over SparseGPT are observed, with the largest gains on the OPT model family. On zero-shot accuracy, SparseLLM consistently matches or exceeds SparseGPT, with the most notable improvements at 90% and 3:4 sparsity (e.g., OPT-1.3b 3:4: 41.92% vs. 37.18% mean accuracy).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant local pruning paradigm for LLMs (exemplified by SparseGPT and Wanda) by reformulating the global pruning objective using auxiliary variables and soft constraints, enabling decomposition into per-layer subproblems that retain inter-layer dependencies. The weight-pruning subproblem directly reuses existing solvers like SparseGPT, while the novel activation and output update steps leverage ridge-regression-style closed-form solutions. Conceptually, the work bridges the gap between memory-prohibitive global pruning (which requires the full model on one GPU) and suboptimal local pruning (which over-constrains intermediate activations), positioning itself as a drop-in enhancement layer over existing local pruning pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework addresses a fundamental tension in LLM compression: achieving global optimality without prohibitive memory costs. The ~80% perplexity reduction at high sparsity (>60%) over SparseGPT is practically significant for deployment scenarios where aggressive compression is needed. However, the method incurs roughly 10× the per-layer computation time of SparseGPT, and the authors acknowledge limitations including the homogeneous sparsity assumption and the restriction to pruning only the first 50% of decoder layers. Open questions include extending to heterogeneous per-layer sparsity, scaling to the full model without the 50% layer cutoff, and whether the gains persist under fine-tuning or task-specific evaluation beyond perplexity and zero-shot accuracy.
