## Problem

Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) frequently generate responses that are not factually grounded in the input image (object hallucination). The paper identifies a previously unexplored root cause: Rotary Position Encoding (RoPE) introduces a long-term decay in attention, so that visual tokens far from instruction tokens in the 1-D sequence receive progressively weaker information flow, degrading multimodal alignment. Existing mitigation strategies—post-hoc revisors, data augmentation, RLHF alignment, and training-free decoding—are either labor-intensive, costly, or slow at inference, leaving the positional-encoding mechanism itself unaddressed.

## Input / Output

The model consumes an RGB image (encoded by CLIP ViT-L/14 at 336×336 resolution into a grid of visual tokens) and a free-form text instruction, and produces a natural-language text response. The key structural detail is that the visual token grid is reorganized into a concentric-square ordering (periphery → center) before being concatenated with instruction tokens, and a modified 2-D causal mask replaces the standard 1-D left-to-right mask so that spatially adjacent visual tokens remain causally correlated. No additional inputs or outputs are introduced beyond the standard LVLM interface.

## Method

Concentric Causal Attention (CCA) reorganizes visual token positions from the conventional raster-scan order into a 2-D concentric-square layout that starts at the image periphery and spirals inward to the center, reducing the maximum relative distance between any visual token and the instruction tokens from V to √V/2 + T − 1 and thereby counteracting RoPE's long-term decay. A companion concentric causal mask replaces the default 1-D causal mask so that attention follows 2-D spatial locality: center tokens attend to all peripheral tokens, and tokens at the same concentric ring attend to each other. Training follows the standard LLaVA two-stage pipeline (pre-training on CC-558K, then instruction tuning on the 665K multi-turn dataset) with a CLIP ViT-L/14 encoder, Vicuna-7B LLM, and a 2-layer MLP projector; no additional parameters or decoding-time overhead are introduced. The approach is validated on POPE, CHAIR, MME, LLaVA-Bench, and six multiple-choice perception benchmarks, consistently outperforming the LLaVA baseline and prior hallucination-mitigation methods (VCD, OPERA, LLaVA-RLHF) without bells and whistles.

## Results

On the POPE object hallucination benchmark, CCA-LLaVA achieves average accuracy of 86.86%, 86.99%, and 85.69% on MSCOCO, A-OKVQA, and GQA respectively, surpassing the LLaVA baseline by 5.48%, 7.86%, and 6.70% in accuracy. On the CHAIR captioning metric (greedy, 512 tokens), CCA-LLaVA reduces sentence-level hallucination (C^S) from 46.2 to 43.0 while maintaining comparable object recall (80.4% vs. 80.3%). On the MME hallucination subset, CCA-LLaVA scores 641.66 total, outperforming the baseline (565.33) by 76.33 points. On LLaVA-Bench (In-the-Wild), CCA-LLaVA achieves an overall GPT-4-judged score of 64.3 vs. 58.9 for the baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CCA-LLaVA outperforms the LLaVA baseline [46] by 5.48–7.86% in POPE accuracy and 5.89–7.71% in F1 across all three datasets, with the largest gains on A-OKVQA. Against the training-free decoding method VCD [34], CCA-LLaVA leads by 1.02–4.51% in POPE accuracy (e.g., 86.86 vs. 84.66 on MSCOCO average) and by 3.09–5.01% in F1 on the adversarial subset, which is the hardest setting. Compared to LLaVA-RLHF [62] (which uses a larger Vicuna-13B backbone and RLHF alignment), CCA-LLaVA achieves comparable or superior POPE scores (86.86 vs. 84.13 avg accuracy on MSCOCO) while using only a 7B model and no preference data. On CHAIR, CCA-LLaVA matches or slightly beats LLaVA-RLHF on C^S (43.0 vs. 43.6) while maintaining higher object recall (80.4% vs. 78.0%). On MME, CCA-LLaVA (641.66) surpasses VCD (604.66) by 37 points and OPERA [26] (592.33) by 49 points. On LLaVA-Bench, CCA-LLaVA (64.3) outperforms both VCD (61.6) and OPERA (61.3), with the largest margin on the conversation sub-task (69.4 vs. 57.3 and 44.0 respectively). On multiple-choice benchmarks (Table 5), CCA-LLaVA consistently exceeds the LLaVA baseline across all six datasets (e.g., SEED-Bench 61.7 vs. 58.6, VizWiz 57.6 vs. 50.0) and also outperforms Seva [85] on most metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the LLaVA architecture (CLIP ViT-L/14 + Vicuna-7B + 2-layer MLP projector) and its two-stage training pipeline, but departs from prior hallucination mitigation strategies that operate at the decoding level (VCD, OPERA) or the data/preference level (RLHF, contrastive instruction tuning). Instead, it identifies RoPE's long-term decay—a property well-known in LLM context-window research—as a previously under-explored contributor to object hallucination in LVLMs, and addresses it through a training-time positional reorganization (concentric ordering) and a corresponding 2-D causal mask. The conceptual lineage is thus from positional encoding theory (RoPE, relative position encodings) rather than from the hallucination-mitigation literature per se.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a mechanistic diagnosis (RoPE long-term decay attenuates visual-to-instruction attention for spatially distant tokens) that reframes hallucination as a positional alignment problem, offering a training-time fix with zero inference overhead. This is practically attractive for LVLM developers who want to reduce hallucination without adding decoding complexity or curating additional data. However, the approach is validated only on the LLaVA-7B architecture with square visual token grids, and the authors explicitly note it has not been extended to video, audio, or non-square token layouts. Open questions include whether concentric ordering generalizes to variable-resolution encoders, whether it interacts with other positional encoding schemes (ALiBi, NoPE), and whether the 2-D causal mask is necessary or whether the concentric reordering alone suffices.
