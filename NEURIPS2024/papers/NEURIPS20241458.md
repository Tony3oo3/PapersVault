## Problem

Multi-Head Attention in LLMs incurs substantial KV cache memory that scales linearly with context length, making inference prohibitively expensive. Existing head-sharing methods like GQA and MQA reduce this cost but rely on mean-pooling initialization that discards parameter knowledge, necessitating expensive continued pre-training to recover performance. The paper asks: how can we construct a more efficient attention architecture from an existing MHA checkpoint while keeping transformation costs as low as possible?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241458/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pre-trained MHA checkpoint (e.g., LLaMA2-7B with 32 heads × 32 layers) and unlabeled text data (RedPajama, 4B tokens for fusion + up to 50B for continued pre-training). It produces a DHA model where each layer independently allocates different numbers of key and value heads (e.g., 25% budget means 8 KV heads per layer on average, but distributed non-uniformly across 32 layers). The fusion coefficients ω are expanded from scalar to per-dimension (d_k) vectors, adding only a negligible number of parameters while enabling finer-grained knowledge retention.

## Method

DHA exploits the observation that MHA heads form clusters of high CKA similarity, with redundancy varying across layers and differing between key and value heads. Rather than uniform mean-pooling (as in GQA), DHA learns linear fusion operators that combine similar heads within clusters, initialized as identity (Kronecker delta) so the DHA forward pass is exactly equivalent to MHA at step zero. An Augmented Lagrangian adversarial game progressively enforces head sharing: a decaying margin t = max(0, b^s(1−s/k)) allows exploration early in training, then tightens to force intra-group fusion variables to converge. The three-stage pipeline—Search (240 steps of simulated annealing for head grouping + dynamic programming for per-layer budget allocation), Fusion (1000 steps optimizing ω and λ), and Continued Pre-training—transforms MHA into DHA using only 0.2B tokens for fusion, achieving 5× training acceleration over GQA and 96.1% of MHA performance at 2.5% of the original pre-training budget.

## Results

DHA-7B-50% achieves an average accuracy of 65.8% across 9 downstream tasks after only 50B tokens of continued pre-training (2.5% of LLaMA2-7B's 2T pre-training budget), reaching 96.1% of the original MHA model's 67.4% average while saving 75% of KV cache. At the 1B token budget (0.05% of pre-training), DHA-7B-25% scores 60.8% average versus GQA-7B-25%'s 58.0%, a 5% relative improvement. DHA converges 5× faster than GQA during fusion training, achieving GQA's 1B-token performance with only 0.2B tokens (13.93% improvement at 0.01% pre-training budget).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GQA (the primary baseline), DHA consistently wins across all model scales (7B, 2.7B, 1.3B) and both compression rates (50%, 25%) at the 1B token budget. The advantage is largest at 25% compression: DHA-7B-25% achieves 60.8% average vs. GQA-7B-25%'s 58.0% (+2.8 pts, 5% relative), DHA-2.7B-25% scores 55.4% vs. 52.4% (+3.0 pts), and DHA-1.3B-25% reaches 50.8% vs. 48.3% (+2.5 pts). At 50% compression the gap narrows (e.g., 63.3% vs. 62.3% at 7B), reflecting that GQA's mean-pooling initialization is less harmful when fewer heads are merged. Against the original MHA models (LLaMA2-7B, Sheared-LLaMA), DHA-7B-50% after 50B tokens retains 96.1% of MHA's average accuracy (65.8% vs. 67.4%) while using only 2.5% of the pre-training budget and 75% less KV cache. DHA also outperforms GQA in training speed: it converges to GQA's 1B-token performance level with just 0.2B tokens (5× acceleration), and at the 5B token budget DHA-7B-25% (62.4% avg) edges out GQA-7B-25% (62.1% avg) in the ablation table.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the GQA/MQA line of work on parameter-sharing attention architectures by introducing two key departures: (1) a decoupled architecture where key and value heads receive different, layer-specific compression budgets rather than a uniform grouping, and (2) a learned linear fusion operator (optimized via Augmented Lagrangian) that replaces GQA's lossy mean-pooling initialization. Conceptually, it draws on the head-clustering observations from CHAI and CRaSh, the progressive model transformation ideas from Net2Net and LiGO, and the authors' own prior work on linear parameter fusion (LEMON), but applies them specifically to the attention head dimension with an adaptive per-layer allocation strategy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for LLM deployment: 75% KV cache reduction with only 2.5% of the original pre-training budget to recover 96.1% of MHA performance, and a 5× training speedup over GQA at equivalent compression rates. The decoupled K/V allocation insight (value heads are more redundant than key heads, and redundancy varies across layers) provides a principled basis for future efficient-attention design. Limitations include testing only up to 7B parameters, reliance on linear fusion (nonlinear methods unexplored), and validation only on LLaMA-family decoder architectures. Open questions include whether inter-layer head fusion (discussed but not implemented) could yield further compression, and whether the approach scales to 70B+ models where KV cache is most prohibitive.
