## Problem

Despite the widespread success of latent diffusion models like Stable Diffusion for text-to-image generation, the internal working mechanism—particularly how and when text prompt tokens influence the denoising process—remains largely unexplored. This paper asks: which tokens in the encoded prompt actually drive image generation, and at which denoising steps is the textual information conveyed?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241756/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The input is a natural-language text prompt encoded by a CLIP text encoder into a fixed-length sequence of 77 tokens ([SOS] + up to 75 semantic tokens + [EOS]), which is injected into every cross-attention block of the UNet denoiser. The output is a VQ-GAN image latent (decoded to a natural RGB image), produced over 50 DDIM or 25 DPM-Solver denoising steps. The paper's analysis focuses on intermediate latents x_t at each step t, examining cross-attention maps, frequency decompositions, and text-image alignment scores (CLIPScore, BLIP-VQA, MiniGPT4-CoT) to trace how prompt information propagates.

## Method

The paper combines frequency-domain analysis with controlled token-swap experiments to dissect the denoising process into two stages: (1) early steps reconstruct the overall image shape (low-frequency signal), and (2) later steps fill in fine details (high-frequency signal). A theoretical proposition shows that Gaussian noise has uniformly negligible spectral energy, so low-frequency image content survives the forward noising process longer and is recovered first in reverse. Empirically, swapping the [EOS] token between two prompts causes generated images to align with the [EOS] source rather than the semantic tokens, revealing that [EOS]—which aggregates the full prompt via the autoregressive CLIP encoder—dominates shape determination. Varying the step at which classifier-free guidance is removed (setting w=0 for t < a) confirms that all textual information is conveyed within the first ~20–30 steps. This observation yields a training-free acceleration: skipping the conditional forward pass ε_θ(t,x_t,C) in the detail-filling stage saves 25%+ inference time with minimal quality degradation, validated across SD v1.5, SD v2.1, and Pixart-Alpha on MS-COCO.

## Results

The paper's primary quantitative result is a training-free sampling acceleration: by removing the conditional noise prediction ε_θ(t,x_t,C) in the later denoising steps (t < a), the method saves 25%+ inference cost with minimal quality loss. Specifically, on MS-COCO with SD v1.5 + DDIM (50 steps), setting a=20 saves 18.10% latency while maintaining Image-CLIPScore of 0.996 and FID of 13.805 (vs. 13.772 baseline); with DPM-Solver (25 steps), a=15 saves 26.11% latency with FID 14.985 (vs. 14.297 baseline). The key mechanistic finding is that the [EOS] token dominates text-prompt influence: when [EOS] is swapped between two prompts, generated images align more strongly with the target prompt (CLIPScore 0.2758 vs. 0.2363; BLIP-VQA 0.4441 vs. 0.3325), and this information is conveyed within the first few denoising steps.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is an empirical analysis paper rather than a method paper, so it does not compare against other acceleration or generation baselines. The sole comparison is between the standard (unaccelerated) diffusion sampling (a=0, full text guidance at all steps) and the proposed accelerated variant (a>0, text guidance removed for t<a). The results show a clear quality-speed trade-off: at a=20 (DDIM/SD v1.5), the FID increases by only 0.033 (13.772→13.805) while saving 18.10% latency, and Text-CLIPScore drops by just 0.146 points. At a=30, the FID rises to 14.012 (+0.24) with 27.18% savings, marking the practical sweet spot for the claimed 25%+ acceleration. Beyond a=30, quality degrades rapidly (FID 15.048 at a=40, 19.296 at a=50), confirming that text information is indeed conveyed in the early denoising steps. The same pattern holds across SD v2.1 and Pixart-Alpha backbones, and with both DDIM and DPM-Solver samplers, indicating the finding is model-agnostic.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Stable Diffusion framework (Rombach et al., 2022) and its classifier-free guidance mechanism (Ho & Salimans, 2021), applying DDIM (Song et al., 2022) and DPM-Solver (Lu et al., 2022) as sampling backbones. It extends prior empirical observations that low-frequency signals are recovered first in denoising (Yang et al., 2023; Si et al., 2023) and that cross-attention maps converge during generation (Zhang et al., 2024) by providing a frequency-domain theoretical explanation and connecting these phenomena to the specific role of individual text tokens. The finding that [EOS] dominates over semantic tokens draws an analogy to the 'attention sink' phenomenon in LLMs (Xiao et al., 2023), but is the first to systematically characterize token-level influence in cross-modal T2I diffusion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical acceleration (25%+ inference cost reduction by removing text guidance in later steps) is a simple, training-free technique applicable across multiple samplers and backbones, making it immediately useful for practitioners. However, the [EOS] dominance finding may be specific to CLIP's auto-regressive encoding and the 76-token padding scheme, and its generalizability to other text encoders or architectures (e.g., T5-based models) remains untested. Open questions include whether the two-stage 'shape-then-details' decomposition holds for video diffusion or 3D generation, and whether the frequency-based explanation fully accounts for the observed token behavior in more complex compositional prompts.
