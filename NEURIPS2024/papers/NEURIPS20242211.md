## Problem

Prior Dikin walks for log-concave sampling over polytopes either had mixing times depending on the number of constraints n (e.g., Õ(nd + dL²R²)) or required exact Hessian computation, making per-iteration cost prohibitive. The soft-threshold Dikin walk of Mangoubi and Vishnoi achieved near-optimal mixing but was specialized to the log-barrier, leaving it unclear whether the faster Lee-Sidford barrier (with complexity Õ(d) vs. n) could be adapted for log-concave sampling. The paper asks whether per-iteration cost can be significantly reduced via spectral approximation of the Hessian while preserving the fast mixing rate.

## Input / Output

The algorithm takes a convex body (a polytope Ax ≤ b or a spectrahedron {x : ΣxᵢAᵢ ≽ C}), an L-Lipschitz convex function f with an evaluation oracle, a ν-self-concordant barrier g, a w-warm start point x₀, and a target accuracy δ. It outputs a single sample from π ∝ exp(−f) over the body with TV distance at most δ from the target. Crucially, the Hessian of g is never explicitly formed; only a spectral approximation within (1±1/d) is computed at each step, and the acceptance probability is evaluated exactly using these approximations to preserve the correct stationary distribution.

## Method

The paper introduces a robust soft-threshold Dikin walk that generalizes beyond the log-barrier by proving three sufficient conditions on the barrier Hessian (ν-symmetry, convexity of log det(H+I), and bounded local norm) that guarantee Õ(νd + dL²R²) mixing. The key mechanism is a modified Metropolis acceptance probability that uses the approximate Hessian at both the current and proposed points, which preserves reversibility and ensures the stationary distribution remains exactly π despite the approximation. For polytopes, the Lee-Sidford barrier is instantiated with Lewis weights approximated via leverage-score-based spectral sparsification, yielding Õ(d² + dL²R²) mixing at Õ(nd^{ω−1}) per-step cost. For spectrahedra, a novel tensor-based sketching approach (TensorSRHT applied row-by-row) approximates the log-barrier Hessian in Õ(n^ω + n²d^{3ω−5}) time, improving both mixing and per-iteration cost over prior work.

## Results

For log-concave sampling over a d-dimensional polytope with n constraints, the proposed soft-threshold Dikin walk with the Lee-Sidford barrier achieves a mixing time of Õ(d² + dL²R²) steps with a per-iteration cost of Õ(nd^(ω−1)), where ω ≈ 2.37 is the fast matrix multiplication exponent. For spectrahedra (dual SDP constraint sets with n×n symmetric matrices), the walk mixes in Õ(nd + dL²R²) steps with per-iteration cost Õ(n^ω + n²d^(3ω−5)). These bounds are near-optimal up to polylogarithmic factors and the dependence on L and R.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the prior best Dikin walk for polytopes (Mangoubi & Vishnoi 2023/2024), this work improves the mixing time from Õ(nd + dL²R²) to Õ(d² + dL²R²), a strict improvement whenever n ≥ d, by extending the soft-threshold Dikin walk beyond the log-barrier to the Lee-Sidford barrier (ν = O(d log⁵n) vs ν = n). Against Narayanan & Rakhlin (2017) for spectrahedra, the paper improves the mixing time from Õ(n²d³ + n²dL²R²) to Õ(nd + dL²R²), an nd² improvement on the n,d-dependent term and a removal of the quadratic n-dependence on the L,R term, while also reducing per-iteration cost from O(n^ωd + n²d^(ω−1)) to Õ(n^ω + n²d^(3ω−5)) in the regime n ≫ d. Compared to hit-and-run (Lovász & Vempala 2006), the proposed walk mixes strictly faster in the regime L = O(1) and r = O(1) (where r is the inscribed ball radius). The result is incomparable to Kook & Vempala (2024), which achieves Õ(νdβ log(w/δ)) mixing but requires f to be α-relative strongly-convex and the density to be β-Lipschitz, stronger assumptions than the L-Lipschitz convexity assumed here.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the soft-threshold Dikin walk of Mangoubi and Vishnoi (2023, 2024), which achieved near-optimal mixing for log-concave sampling but was restricted to the log-barrier via a polytope-sequence argument that fundamentally breaks for more complex barriers like Lee-Sidford or volumetric barriers. The key conceptual departure is a barrier-based proof strategy (relying on ν-symmetry, bounded local norm, and convexity of log-det(H+I)) that generalizes the mixing analysis to arbitrary self-concordant barriers, enabling the first use of the Lee-Sidford barrier for log-concave sampling. Additionally, the paper introduces a robustness framework showing that spectral approximation of the Hessian (via matrix sketching and leverage-score sampling) preserves the mixing rate, connecting the MCMC literature to the numerical linear algebra sketching toolkit in a way prior Dikin walk analyses did not.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result removes the n-dependence from the dominant mixing term for polytopes (achieving Õ(d²+dL²R²) vs. prior Õ(nd+dL²R²)), and improves spectrahedron mixing from n²d³ to nd, with practical implications for differentially private learning, simulated annealing, and online convex optimization. The modular framework means any future improvement in Hessian approximation (e.g., faster Lewis weight computation) translates directly into runtime gains without re-analysis. Open questions include eliminating the dependence on the bounding radius R, extending to non-log-Lipschitz densities, and adapting Riemannian HMC to the log-concave setting for potentially faster mixing.
