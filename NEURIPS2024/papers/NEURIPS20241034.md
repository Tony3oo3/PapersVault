## Problem

Vanilla few-shot jailbreaking (FSJ) achieves near-zero attack success rates on well-aligned models like Llama-2-Chat, while many-shot jailbreaking requires long-context capabilities (hundreds of demonstrations) unavailable in most open-source LLMs with ≤8192-token windows. The paper asks whether a small number of demonstrations (2–8 shots) can be made effective against strongly aligned LLMs and their system-level defenses.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a harmful request (e.g., from AdvBench), a pre-built pool of 520 harmful (query, response) pairs generated by Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.2, and the target LLM's conversation-template special tokens. It outputs an optimized n-shot prompt (n ∈ {2, 4, 8}) consisting of semantically natural-language demonstrations with injected special tokens prepended to the harmful request, which the target LLM then processes to produce a jailbroken response. Because the search is demo-level rather than token-level, the crafted inputs retain low perplexity and remain undetectable by perplexity-based filters.

## Method

I-FSJ combines three strategies: (1) constructing a demonstration pool by prompting a less-aligned model (Mistral-7B) to generate harmful responses for 520 AdvBench requests; (2) injecting the target LLM's conversation-template special tokens (e.g., [/INST] for Llama-2-Chat, <|end_of_turn|> for OpenChat) between user and assistant messages in each demonstration, exploiting the model's learned tendency to initiate generation upon encountering these tokens; and (3) performing batch demo-level random search (Algorithm 1) that iteratively replaces one demonstration at a random position with another sampled from the pool, accepting the change only if it reduces the cross-entropy loss of generating the initial harmful token (e.g., "Step"). An ensemble variant (Algorithm 2) extends the objective to minimize loss across K independently perturbed copies of the input, yielding demonstrations that are simultaneously effective and robust to perturbation-based defenses such as SmoothLLM. The entire pipeline is fully automated, requires only output logits (no gradients), and runs on a single A100 GPU within a couple of hours.

## Results

On Llama-2-7B-Chat with the correct system prompt, I-FSJ with demo-level random search achieves 100% rule-based and 96% LLM-based ASR at 4-shot and 8-shot settings, compared to 38%/38% without random search. On the more strongly aligned Llama-3-8B-Instruct, I-FSJ reaches 94% rule-based and 88% LLM-based ASR at 32-shot. Against seven jailbreaking defenses on Llama-2-7B-Chat (including SmoothLLM, PPL filters, SafeDecoding, and Llama Guard), I-FSJ maintains >95% ASR in most configurations at 4-shot or 8-shot. On GPT-4, I-FSJ achieves 94% rule-based and 86% LLM-based ASR with just 2-shot demonstrations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

I-FSJ with demo-level random search decisively outperforms all five compared jailbreaking attacks on Llama-2-7B-Chat with the correct system prompt: it achieves 100% rule-based ASR versus PAIR (18%), GCG (32%), AutoDAN (2%), PAP (26%), and PRS (90%). The closest competitor is PRS (Andriushchenko et al.), which reaches 90% rule-based / 74% LLM-based ASR, but PRS's adversarial suffixes have high perplexity and are completely defeated by a single windowed PPL filter, whereas I-FSJ's semantically meaningful inputs remain undetected. Against ICA (Wei et al.), I-FSJ at 8-shot (100% ASR) matches ICA at 64-shot (84% rule-based), yielding an 8× efficiency improvement in context usage. On GPT-4, I-FSJ (94% rule / 86% LLM at 2-shot) also surpasses the ICA baseline (46% ASR at 10-shot). Overall, I-FSJ is the only method that simultaneously achieves near-perfect ASR, resists perplexity-based detection, and operates within a few-shot context budget.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the few-shot jailbreaking (FSJ) paradigm introduced by Wei et al. [62] and the many-shot jailbreaking of Anil et al. [3], bridging the gap between the two by making few-shot demonstrations effective within limited context windows (≤8192 tokens). The two key technical contributions—injecting the target model's conversation-template special tokens (e.g., [/INST]) into demonstrations and performing demo-level random search—draw on the random search optimization framework of Andriushchenko et al. [2] and Rastrigin [48], but operate at the demonstration level rather than the token level, preserving semantic coherence. This positions the work as a departure from adversarial-suffix methods (GCG, PRS) that produce meaningless suffixes vulnerable to perplexity filters, and from many-shot approaches that require long-context capabilities absent in most open-source models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that two simple modifications (special-token injection + demo-level RS) elevate ASR from 0% to >95% on heavily aligned models like Llama-2-7B-Chat and Llama-3-8B-Instruct, even under stacked defenses (PPL filters, SmoothLLM, SafeDecoding, Llama Guard), underscores the fragility of current alignment and defense strategies. The method's query efficiency (~88 queries vs. 256K for GCG) and full automation make it a practical threat and a strong baseline for future defense research. Open questions include whether the approach transfers to closed-source models with unknown conversation templates, whether future models will obfuscate or randomize special tokens to mitigate this attack vector, and whether the observed 'transferability' of special tokens across model families can be exploited more systematically.
