## Problem

Existing certified defenses for Deep Equilibrium Models (DEQs) rely on deterministic methods like interval bound propagation and Lipschitz bounding, which produce trivial certified radii on large-scale datasets and are restricted to specific DEQ architectures. Randomized smoothing offers a more flexible and scalable alternative, but naively applying it to DEQs incurs prohibitive computation costs because each of the ~10,000 Monte Carlo samples requires a full fixed-point solve (up to 88 seconds per ImageNet image). This paper provides the first randomized smoothing certification for DEQs and identifies the computation redundancy that makes it intractable.

## Input / Output

The method takes standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10, 224×224 for ImageNet) and produces a class prediction together with a probabilistic ℓ2-norm certified radius R, guaranteeing that the smoothed classifier's prediction is unchanged for all perturbations with ‖δ‖₂ < R with probability at least 1−α (α=0.001). Unlike standard forward-pass networks, each input is consumed by an iterative fixed-point solver (Anderson, Broyden, or Naive) that must converge to a tolerance of ~0.001, and the certification requires N=10,000 independently noised copies of the input to estimate class probabilities via Monte Carlo sampling.

## Method

Serialized Randomized Smoothing (SRS) exploits the fact that the ~10,000 Gaussian-noised copies of a single image produce highly similar fixed-point solutions, so the solver for sample i can be initialized with the converged representation z_{i-1}^S from sample i−1 rather than from zero, reducing the required iterations from ~30 to 1–3 per sample. This introduces a correlation chain across predictions that violates the independence assumption underlying the standard randomized smoothing radius formula. To restore theoretical guarantees, the authors develop a correlation-eliminated certification: during Monte Carlo sampling, a small subset of K samples is stored and later re-evaluated with the standard (independent) DEQ to estimate an upper bound p̄_m on the probability that SRS misclassifies a sample relative to the true fixed-point prediction. The certified radius is then computed using a conservative lower confidence bound on the effective count N_A^E = N_A − p̄_m·N_A, yielding a provably valid radius (Theorem 3.1) while achieving up to 7× speedup with negligible certified-accuracy loss.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with MDEQ-LARGE (σ=0.5), SRS-MDEQ-3A achieves 66% certified accuracy at r=0 and 11% at r=1.5 (ACR=0.62), matching the full MDEQ-30A baseline (67%/12%, ACR=0.62) while reducing per-image certification time from 12.89s to 2.55s (5× speedup). SRS-MDEQ-1A achieves 63%/12% (ACR=0.59) in just 1.79s (7× speedup). On ImageNet with MDEQ-SMALL (σ=1.0), SRS-MDEQ-3B matches MDEQ-14B exactly (44%→11% across radii 0→3.0) in 27.48s vs. 88.33s (3× speedup), while SRS-MDEQ-1B achieves 40%→10% in 15.21s (6× speedup).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary baseline of standard randomized smoothing (Cohen et al., 2019) applied to MDEQ, SRS-MDEQ-3A matches MDEQ-30A's certified accuracy almost exactly (ACR 0.62 vs. 0.62 on CIFAR-10 LARGE) while being 5× faster, and SRS-MDEQ-1A achieves 95% of the baseline ACR (0.59 vs. 0.62) at 7× speedup. On ImageNet, SRS-MDEQ-3B reproduces MDEQ-14B's certified accuracy identically (44%→11% across radii) at 3× speedup. Against deterministic Lipschitz-bound methods (SLL and LBEN from Appendix D), SRS-MDEQ-3A dominates across all certified radii: at r=108/255 it achieves 52% vs. SLL's 36% and LBEN's 21%, and at r=1.0 it reaches 25% vs. 11% and 4% respectively. The paper notes these comparisons are not entirely apples-to-apples since randomized smoothing provides probabilistic (not deterministic) guarantees, but the certified radii are substantially tighter.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: randomized smoothing for certified robustness (Cohen et al., 2019) and Deep Equilibrium Models (Bai et al., 2019, 2020). Prior certified defenses for DEQs relied on deterministic methods—interval bound propagation (Wei & Kolter, 2021; Li et al., 2022) and Lipschitz bounding (Havens et al., 2023; Jafarpour et al., 2021)—which are restricted to specific DEQ architectures and produce trivial certified radii on large-scale datasets. The paper departs from this by being the first to apply randomized smoothing to DEQs, and then introduces Serialized Randomized Smoothing (SRS), which exploits the similarity between noisy-sample representations to reuse previous fixed-point solutions as solver initialization, a mechanism absent from both the standard randomized smoothing and prior DEQ certification literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: SRS enables the first certified robustness evaluation of DEQs on ImageNet, a scale where prior deterministic methods (IBP, LBEN) yield near-zero certified radii. The 7× speedup with minimal certified-accuracy loss makes DEQ certification feasible in real-world deployment scenarios. However, the method is tightly coupled to the fixed-point solver structure of DEQs and cannot be directly transferred to other implicit architectures or explicit networks, which the authors acknowledge as a limitation. Open questions include whether the serialization idea generalizes to other iterative solvers (e.g., in diffusion models or neural ODEs) and whether the correlation-eliminated certification can be tightened further to reduce the small accuracy gap versus standard randomized smoothing.
