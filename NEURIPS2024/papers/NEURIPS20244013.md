## Problem

Existing policy-based primal-dual methods for Constrained Reinforcement Learning suffer from three critical limitations: their convergence rates depend on the cardinality of state and action spaces (restricting them to tabular CMDPs), they are restricted to softmax policies, and they handle only a single constraint. Additionally, the parameter-based exploration paradigm (PGPE) remains entirely unexplored in the CRL literature, and risk-based constraints (CVaR, mean-variance, chance) lack a unified treatment with convergence guarantees.

## Input / Output

The method takes a CMDP defined over continuous state and action spaces with U cost functions and corresponding threshold values b_i, along with a choice of exploration paradigm (action-based or parameter-based) and risk measure (expected cost, CVaR_α, mean-variance, or chance). It produces the parameters of a feasible (hyper)policy that maximizes expected return while satisfying all U constraints simultaneously. The regularization parameter ω controls the trade-off: setting ω = O(ε) yields an overall ε error on both the objective gap and constraint violation, at the cost of a two-timescale learning rate schedule.

## Method

C-PG is an exploration-agnostic primal-dual algorithm that optimizes a ridge-regularized Lagrangian via an alternate ascent/descent scheme: a projected gradient descent step on the primal variable v (policy or hyperpolicy parameters) followed by a projected gradient ascent step on the dual variable λ (Lagrange multipliers). The key technical insight is that the ridge regularization −(ω/2)‖λ‖² makes the Lagrangian strongly concave in λ, yielding a closed-form primal function H_ω(v) = J_0(v) + (1/2ω)‖(J(v)−b)⁺‖² whose minimization directly controls both objective gap and constraint violation. Under a weak ψ-gradient domination assumption (ψ∈[1,2], encompassing both GD and PL conditions), C-PG achieves dimension-free last-iterate global convergence with sample complexity O(ω^{-1}ε^{-2/ψ+1}) for exact gradients and O(ω^{-3}ε^{-4/ψ+1}) for stochastic gradients. The action-based (C-PGAE) and parameter-based (C-PGPE) instantiations employ REINFORCE/GPOMDP-style and PGPE-style estimators respectively, and extend to risk-constrained problems by merging auxiliary risk variables η into the primal update via a unified risk measure formulation.

## Results

On the Discrete Grid World with Walls (DGWW), C-PGAE reaches the constrained optimization objective with fewer trajectories than both NPG-PD and RPG-PD, which require an additional O(|S|+|S||A|) trajectories per iteration. On the bidimensional CostLQR environment, both C-PGAE and C-PGPE solve the constrained problem with fewer trajectories than the actor-critic baselines NPG-PD2 and RPG-PD2, which need 500 extra trajectories for their inner critic loop. On Swimmer-v4 with risk constraints, CVaRα yields the lowest average costs (23.07±0.26 for C-PGPE, 23.08±0.24 for C-PGAE) compared to expected cost (26.91±0.09 / 25.80±0.14), while Mean-Variance achieves the absolute minimum cost (0.75±0.26 for C-PGAE) at the expense of poor return performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares C-PGAE against NPG-PD (Ding et al., 2020) and RPG-PD (Ding et al., 2024) on DGWW, and against NPG-PD2 (Ding et al., 2022) and its ridge-regularized variant RPG-PD2 on CostLQR. In both environments, C-PGAE (and C-PGPE on CostLQR) achieves the constrained objective with strictly fewer trajectories than all baselines. The advantage is structural: NPG-PD and RPG-PD require O(|S|+|S||A|) additional trajectories per iteration for their natural policy gradient computation, while NPG-PD2 and RPG-PD2, being actor-critic methods, need 500 extra trajectories per iteration for the inner value-function loop. The baselines are also noted to be highly sensitive to hyperparameter selection (especially inner-loop length and step size), whereas C-PGAE/C-PGPE use simple constant or Adam-scheduled step sizes. No baseline outperforms the proposed methods on any reported metric.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies the action-based (REINFORCE/GPOMDP) and parameter-based (PGPE) policy gradient paradigms under a single primal-dual framework (C-PG) with ridge-regularized Lagrangian optimization, building directly on the unified risk measure formulation of Bisi et al. (2022). It departs from prior last-iterate convergence results (Ding et al. 2024, Ying et al. 2022, Gladin et al. 2023) by replacing their dimension-dependent, softmax-specific, or single-constraint analyses with dimension-free guarantees under a general (weak) ψ-gradient domination assumption applicable to arbitrary (hyper)policy parameterizations. The paper is the first to bring parameter-based exploration into the CRL setting and the first to enforce risk-based constraints (CVaR, mean-variance, chance) in the parameter-based paradigm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dimension-free last-iterate convergence guarantee with general policy parameterization removes a key barrier to applying provably convergent CRL methods to realistic continuous-control problems, and the extension to parameter-based exploration opens a previously unexplored axis of policy search for constrained settings. However, the two-time-scale requirement when ω = O(ε) and the gap to the Vaswani et al. (2022) lower bound (Ω(ε⁻²)) leave room for improvement, and the empirical validation is limited to relatively small-scale environments (tabular DGWW, 2D LQR, Swimmer/Hopper). Open questions include whether single-time-scale variants can match these rates and whether the (weak) gradient domination assumption holds broadly for the risk-based objectives the paper introduces.
