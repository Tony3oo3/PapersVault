## Problem

Standard self-attention applies the same softmax mapping to every query, coupling semantic similarity (angle between key-query pairs) with contextual sparsity (spikiness of the attention map). The paper argues these two objectives can be at odds: achieving different sparsity levels for similar tokens forces the attention weight matrix to grow in norm, slowing optimization and increasing sensitivity to perturbations. This uniform treatment also causes attention dilution as context length grows, where the top attention probability is upper-bounded by 1/(1+(L-1)e^{-2‖W‖}).

## Input / Output

The method consumes a standard causal token sequence X ∈ R^{L×d} and produces the usual attention-weighted value output, but with per-token scalar temperature factors τ_q(x_n) and τ_v(x_i) modulating the query-key dot products and value contributions respectively. The temperatures are data-dependent (computed from token embeddings via a small MLP) and position-dependent (via a log(n) term), and are not constrained to be non-negative, effectively acting as scalar gates that can fully suppress a token. A weight-sharing variant reuses the existing W_q, W_k, W_v matrices inside the temperature module, adding only 3 vectors per attention head rather than full matrices.

## Method

SSA inserts learnable inverse-temperature scalars into the attention computation: instead of S(QK^T/√d)V, it computes S(τ_q(X) ⊙ Q · K^T/√d) · (τ_v(X) ⊙ V), where τ_q and τ_v are elementwise per-token scalars. The query temperature decomposes into a token-aware term tanh(f(x)) (capturing per-token specificity) and a position-aware term 1+σ(α)log(n) (counteracting attention dilution with growing context length); keys are deliberately left unmodified to preserve the cosine-similarity ordering of semantic relevance. Theoretically, Proposition 1 proves that query-temperature decouples the required weight-norm growth from semantic distance between tokens, and Proposition 2 shows that position-aware logarithmic scaling is provably necessary to solve imbalanced-token retrieval tasks that flat temperature cannot. A weight-sharing strategy sets f(x)=W_tmp·GeLU(W_{q/v}x), reusing attention weights so that SSA adds fewer than 0.5% parameters and no extra matrix multiplications, making it directly fine-tunable on existing LLMs (GPT-2, Pythia, Llama, Llama-3) with consistent perplexity and accuracy gains.

## Results

SSA consistently improves average accuracy across all evaluated models in both fine-tuning and pre-training settings. In fine-tuning, average accuracy improves from 0.406→0.431 (GPT-2), 0.462→0.477 (Pythia-160m), 0.489→0.501 (Pythia-410m), 0.504→0.518 (Llama), and 0.632→0.639 (Llama3-8B). In pre-training, improvements are similarly consistent (e.g., Pythia-160m: 0.389→0.405). On passkey retrieval, SSA yields a substantial gain from 56.89% to 74.41% for Pythia-160m and from 77.62% to 89.53% for Llama, while the weight-sharing variant retains most benefits with <0.5% additional parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SSA outperforms all vanilla baselines consistently. Against GPT-2, SSA base improves average accuracy by 2.5 points in fine-tuning (0.406→0.431) and 1.3 points in pre-training (0.349→0.362). Against Pythia-160m, gains are 1.5 points (fine-tune) and 1.6 points (pre-train); against Pythia-410m, 1.2 and 0.8 points respectively. Against Llama and Llama3-8B, fine-tuning gains are 1.4 and 0.7 points in average accuracy, with Llama3-8B also showing a notable Wikitext perplexity drop from 12.416 to 10.982. In the ablation comparison (Table 8), SSA (Wikitext ppl 26.514) outperforms YaRN's temperature scaling (27.602) and a constant-temperature baseline (28.058) on Pythia fine-tuning. The weight-sharing variant retains most of the gains (e.g., 0.425 vs 0.431 for GPT-2 fine-tune) while adding <0.5% parameters, confirming the benefit is architectural rather than parameter-driven.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the canonical Transformer self-attention mechanism by inserting per-token, data-dependent temperature scaling on query and value embeddings, effectively decoupling semantic similarity (controlled by projection matrices) from contextual sparsity (controlled by temperature). It provides a formal justification for the logarithmic temperature scaling rule proposed in YaRN (Peng et al., 2023) via Proposition 2, while making the approach differentiable and eliminating the grid search required by prior length-extrapolation methods. Conceptually, the temperature module is positioned as a form of scalar gating analogous to mechanisms in Mamba and Griffin, but applied specifically to the softmax attention computation rather than to state-space or linear recurrence layers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SSA offers a principled, parameter-efficient (<0.5% overhead with weight sharing) drop-in modification that can be fine-tuned onto existing LLMs without architectural redesign, and the consistent gains across GPT-2, Pythia, Llama, and Llama3 suggest the benefit is architectural rather than model-specific. The theoretical results (Propositions 1–2) give a clean explanation for why uniform softmax treatment is suboptimal and why per-query temperature is the right lever, which could inform future attention variants. Open questions include extending the method to linear/sparse attention to address the quadratic cost, understanding how SSA interacts with interpretability of attention maps, and whether the gains persist at much larger scales beyond 8B parameters.
