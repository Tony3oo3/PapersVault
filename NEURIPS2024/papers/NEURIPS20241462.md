## Problem

Scribble-supervised semantic segmentation has shown strong results with minimal annotation cost, yet the field lacks diverse, challenging benchmarks: only ScribbleSup (PascalVOC) exists as a scribble-labeled dataset, and PascalVOC is too simple to expose the true limitations of scribble-supervised methods. This single-dataset bottleneck prevents meaningful generalization studies and conclusive evaluation of new scribble-based segmentation approaches.

## Input / Output

The scribble generation algorithm consumes dense pixel-wise semantic segmentation masks (the ground-truth class maps from existing datasets) and produces scribble labels represented as 4th-order polynomial curves parameterized over x-coordinate intervals. Each scribble is a set of points forming a single stroke that traverses the interior of an object instance. The algorithm is parameterized per dataset (erosion amounts, minimum blob size, line thickness) to account for differences in image resolution and object scale, e.g., Cityscapes requires a minimum blob size of 400 px versus 80 px for PascalVOC.

## Method

The algorithm iteratively erodes each object mask (size-dependent initial erosion followed by fixed-rate erosion), computes the edge and center-of-mass of the remaining blob, then samples Q edge points to find the approximate farthest pair. A 2nd-order polynomial is fitted through the pair and a noise-perturbed COM, from which two additional points are sampled with area-dependent Gaussian noise. A final 4th-order polynomial is fitted through all five points, and the curve is validated to lie entirely within the eroded blob without violating class boundaries. If validation fails, the process repeats up to N times with new random samples; if the blob is fully eroded, the object is skipped. This probabilistic, iterative design with hard boundary constraints produces scribbles that statistically match hand-drawn labels (verified against ScribbleSup) while generalizing to any fully-annotated segmentation dataset.

## Results

On s4Pascal (synthetic scribbles), the best method TEL achieves 84.6% mIoU, matching ScribbleSup's 84.6% and reaching 90.8% of the fully supervised 86.6% mIoU. On the more challenging s4Cityscapes, TEL reaches 78.3% mIoU (82.4% relative to the 83.8% supervised baseline), while on s4ADE20K the best method (TEL) achieves 46.8% mIoU (84.6% relative to 51.1% supervised). The gap between scribble-supervised and fully supervised performance widens from ~91% relative on Pascal to ~74–86% relative on Cityscapes, KITTI360, and ADE20K, revealing that PascalVOC is a saturated benchmark for scribble segmentation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary validation compares s4Pascal (synthetic scribbles) against ScribbleSup (hand-drawn): three of four methods (EMA, SASformer, TEL) produce mIoU within 0.5% between the two label sets, confirming functional equivalence. AGMM-SASS is the outlier, dropping ~3% mIoU on s4Pascal (76.8 vs. 76.4), indicating sensitivity to label distribution differences. Among the SOTA methods on the harder datasets, TEL consistently achieves the highest relative performance (82–92%), while SASformer is the most sensitive to scribble length (losing ~7% mIoU when scribbles are shortened to 10%). AGMM-SASS shows the largest variance across datasets (69–87% relative), and the naive EMA mean-teacher baseline trails the specialized methods by 5–15% mIoU on Cityscapes and ADE20K. Overall, no single method dominates across all four datasets, highlighting the need for further research enabled by the new benchmarks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the scribble-supervised semantic segmentation (SASS) line of work initiated by ScribbleSup (Lin et al., 2016), which provided hand-drawn scribble labels exclusively for PascalVOC. The key conceptual departure is an automatic scribble generation algorithm that converts any dense segmentation annotation into functionally equivalent scribble labels via morphological erosion, farthest-point sampling, and iterative polynomial fitting, thereby removing the human annotation bottleneck that limited prior SASS research to a single dataset. The benchmarking builds on recent SASS methods (TEL, AGMM-SASS, SASformer) and a mean-teacher EMA baseline, positioning the contribution as a data infrastructure paper rather than a new segmentation architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper reveals that PascalVOC is effectively saturated for scribble segmentation (models reach ~92% relative performance to full supervision), masking the true difficulty of the task, while more complex datasets like Cityscapes and ADE20K expose a larger ~20% gap that demands further research. The automatic generation algorithm makes scribble supervision universally accessible to any dataset with dense annotations, potentially enabling scribble-based fine-tuning of vision foundation models for custom applications. Open questions include closing the remaining performance gap on complex scenes, understanding why methods like SASformer are far more sensitive to scribble length than TEL, and whether the synthetic labels introduce subtle distributional biases that affect method rankings differently across datasets.
