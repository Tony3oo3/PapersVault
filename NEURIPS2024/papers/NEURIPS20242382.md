## Problem

Existing open vocabulary 3D semantic segmentation methods rely on global feature alignment or vision-language model distillation to bridge 3D and text representations, but these approaches impose only approximate correspondence and produce coarse segmentation boundaries. Point-wise contrastive learning is noise-prone, and patch-wise alignment is ambiguous because a single 2D patch can map to multiple discontinuous 3D regions. The paper addresses this by proposing mask-level alignment that is simultaneously fine-grained, semantically coherent, and robust to outliers.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a 3D point cloud, a corresponding multi-view RGB image, category name text strings, and camera intrinsics/extrinsics for 2D-to-3D back-projection. It produces per-point 3D semantic segmentation scores (computed as cosine similarity between fused features and CLIP text embeddings) along with intermediate 2D class-agnostic binary masks and their mask-level embeddings. The 2D masks are generated by a pre-trained diffusion model's denoising UNet operating on noisy latent representations, and are back-projected to 3D via the camera projection matrix to form per-point binary mask indicators.

## Method

XMask3D introduces a cross-modal mask reasoning framework with three coupled techniques. First, it repurposes the denoising UNet of a pre-trained Stable Diffusion model as a 2D open-vocabulary mask generator, replacing the standard CLIP image embedding condition with an Implicit 3D Captioner that maps global point cloud features into the diffusion model's text-condition space, yielding geometry-aware masks. Second, it back-projects the generated 2D masks to 3D, extracts mask-level 3D embeddings via average pooling over masked points, and applies a cosine contrastive loss against MaskCLIP ground-truth mask features to explicitly align 3D features with the 2D-text embedding space. Third, a fusion block concatenates and MLP-projects the 3D geometric features with pseudo 2D mask features at corresponding points, combining fine-grained geometry with open-vocabulary semantics. The 3D branch uses MinkUNet, the 2D branch follows ODISE with Mask2Former as the mask decoder, and training combines supervised segmentation loss, the proposed mask-level contrastive loss (introduced after 50 warm-up epochs), view-level contrastive loss, and a binary base/novel head.

## Results

On ScanNet B12/N7, XMask3D achieves 61.7 hIoU (70.2 base mIoU, 55.1 novel mIoU), outperforming PLA (55.3 hIoU) and OpenScene (56.8 hIoU). On the more challenging ScanNet200 B170/N30 benchmark, XMask3D reaches 18.0 hIoU (27.8 base, 13.3 novel), surpassing PLA (11.4 hIoU) and OpenScene (14.2 hIoU). On S3DIS B8/N4, XMask3D attains 46.8 hIoU (63.1 base, 37.2 novel), leading all baselines. The method shows the largest relative gains on the long-tail ScanNet200 dataset, where the diffusion-model-based mask generator provides a comprehensive text-2D embedding space.

## Baselines & Comparisons

XMask3D outperforms its direct baseline PLA on all reported benchmarks, with novel-category mIoU improvements of 7.4–20.0% on ScanNet, 70.5–72.7% on ScanNet200, and 51.8–33.0% on S3DIS, demonstrating the effectiveness of mask-level alignment over PLA's hierarchical contrastive learning. Against OpenScene, XMask3D wins on every benchmark where both methods report results (e.g., 61.7 vs. 56.8 hIoU on B12/N7; 18.0 vs. 14.2 on B170/N30). The concurrent method OV3D achieves higher hIoU on ScanNet20 (72.4 vs. 70.0 on B15/N4; 68.5 vs. 61.7 on B12/N7), but it relies on a pre-aligned 3D-text space via PointBind and CLIP2Point, whereas XMask3D operates solely with the standard 2D-text CLIP space; moreover, OV3D does not report results on ScanNet200 or S3DIS. XMask3D substantially surpasses the earlier baselines LSeg-3D, 3DGenZ, and 3DTZSL by large margins across all benchmarks (e.g., 61.7 vs. 0.9 hIoU on B12/N7 vs. LSeg-3D). On S3DIS B8/N4, XMask3D leads OpenScene by 4.4 hIoU (46.8 vs. 42.4) and PLA by 12.2 hIoU (46.8 vs. 34.6).

## Relation to Prior Work

XMask3D extends the ODISE paradigm (using a pre-trained diffusion model's denoising UNet for open-vocabulary 2D mask generation) into the 3D domain by replacing the 2D image condition with global 3D point cloud features, creating geometry-aware masks. It builds on PLA's training pipeline (binary head, view-level contrastive loss) but departs from the broader 3D open-vocabulary literature's reliance on global, patch-wise, or point-wise contrastive alignment by introducing mask-level regularization as a finer-grained and more semantically consistent intermediate. The key conceptual departure is treating the diffusion model not merely as a feature extractor but as a conditional mask generator whose outputs directly supervise 3D feature alignment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The mask-level alignment strategy addresses a concrete weakness of prior 3D open-vocabulary methods—coarse boundaries and ambiguous patch-to-point correspondences—and the modular design (any 3D backbone + 2D mask generator + fusion) makes it broadly applicable. However, the computational cost of the diffusion UNet is a practical barrier, and the method is only validated on indoor semantic segmentation, leaving open whether it transfers to instance/panoptic tasks, outdoor scenes, or more advanced 3D backbones. A lighter 2D mask generator that preserves the open-vocabulary capability without the diffusion model's latency would be a natural next step.
