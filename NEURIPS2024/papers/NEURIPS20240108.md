## Problem

Sherman et al. [2023a] achieved rate-optimal regret for policy optimization in linear MDPs but required a costly reward-free pure exploration warm-up phase that is computationally expensive, relies on intricate Catoni estimators, and is not reward-aware. This paper eliminates that warm-up entirely, replacing it with a simple sigmoid-based contraction mechanism integrated directly into the PO algorithm, while simultaneously improving the dependence on horizon and feature dimension in the regret bound.

## Input / Output

The agent interacts with an episodic linear MDP over K episodes of horizon H, observing d-dimensional state-action features φ(x,a) with ‖φ‖≤1, along with either full-information loss functions (adversarial setting) or instantaneous bandit losses (stochastic setting). At each episode the algorithm outputs a stochastic Markov policy π^k = (π_h^k)_{h∈[H]} mapping states to action distributions, updated via online mirror descent. The algorithm operates in epochs triggered by determinant growth of covariance matrices, resetting the policy to uniform at each epoch boundary and redefining the contraction coefficients.

## Method

The core contribution is a sigmoid-based feature contraction: at each epoch, features are scaled by σ(−β_w‖φ(x,a)‖_{(Λ_h)^{−1}} + log K), shrinking them in high-uncertainty regions and effectively creating a 'contracted (sub) MDP' where transitions become sub-probability measures. This contraction ensures Q-value estimates remain bounded without a separate warm-up while preserving a low-dimensional (d-parameter) representation of the softmax policy class, which is critical for tight regret bounds. The algorithm (CFPO) computes optimistic Q-estimates via regularized least-squares on the contracted features, then performs OMD policy updates over these estimates. The analysis introduces a novel regret decomposition over the contracted sub-MDP, showing the contraction cost is only poly(d,H)log K rather than the √K cost of the warm-up truncation in Sherman et al., yielding Õ(√(H⁴d³K)) regret versus the previous Õ(√(H⁷d⁴K)).

## Results

CFPO achieves a regret bound of $\tilde{O}(\sqrt{H^4 d^3 K \log(K)\log(KH/\delta)} + \sqrt{H^5 d K \log(K)\log|\mathcal{A}|})$, improving upon the previous best PO bound of $\tilde{O}(\sqrt{H^7 d^4 K})$ by a factor of $\sqrt{H^3 d}$. In the stochastic (bandit feedback) setting, this matches the rate-optimal bound of the value iteration approach of Jin et al. [2020b]. The remaining gap to the $\Omega(\sqrt{H^3 d^2 K})$ lower bound for linear MDPs is only $\sqrt{Hd}$, which the authors conjecture is closed by variance reduction techniques not yet applied to PO.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is against Sherman et al. [2023a], the first rate-optimal PO algorithm for linear MDPs, which required a costly reward-free warm-up phase of $\text{poly}(d,H)\sqrt{K}$ episodes. CFPO eliminates this warm-up entirely via a sigmoid-based feature contraction, reducing the regret from $\tilde{O}(\sqrt{H^7 d^4 K})$ to $\tilde{O}(\sqrt{H^4 d^3 K})$ — a $\sqrt{H^3 d}$ improvement. In the stochastic setting, CFPO matches the rate-optimal $\tilde{O}(\sqrt{H^3 d^2 K})$ bound achieved by the value iteration method of Jin et al. [2020b], though CFPO's bound carries an additional $\sqrt{Hd}$ factor. The contraction mechanism contributes only a lower-order $\text{poly}(d,H)\log K$ term to the regret, whereas the warm-up in Sherman et al. [2023a] contributes the leading term. No empirical baselines are compared, as this is a purely theoretical contribution.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends Sherman et al. [2023a], the first rate-optimal policy optimization algorithm for linear MDPs, by replacing their costly reward-free warm-up phase (based on Wagenmaker et al.'s exploration algorithm) with a simple sigmoid-based feature contraction mechanism. The contraction coefficients are inspired by Zanette et al. [2020]'s variance-aware value iteration, but are repurposed here to shrink features in high-uncertainty regions rather than to bound value estimates. The analysis introduces a novel 'contracted (sub) MDP' framework that enables a regret decomposition where the contraction cost is bounded via elliptical potential arguments, yielding a lower-order contribution compared to the warm-up's leading-order cost.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The elimination of the reward-free warm-up removes a major practical barrier to deploying policy optimization in linear MDPs, since the warm-up required intricate Catoni estimators, binary search, and uniform exploration that ignored reward signals. The improved regret dependence (H⁴d³ vs. H⁷d⁴) narrows the gap to the known lower bound to only √(Hd), and the reward-awareness of the contraction mechanism offers a principled path toward more efficient exploration in practice. Key open questions include whether variance reduction techniques (effective for value iteration) can be adapted to PO algorithms, and whether the contraction mechanism can be heuristically applied to drive exploration in deep RL settings where the linear structure does not hold.
