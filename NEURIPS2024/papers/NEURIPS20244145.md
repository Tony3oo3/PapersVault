## Problem

Thompson sampling in contextual bandits relies on posterior sampling from a prior over model parameters, but the standard Gaussian prior cannot represent complex, multimodal distributions. While diffusion models can serve as expressive priors, existing posterior sampling methods for diffusion models (e.g., DPS) rely on likelihood scores that grow linearly with the number of observations, causing instability and divergence in the online setting. The paper asks: can we sample from a diffusion-model posterior efficiently and stably in an online bandit loop?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20244145/cropped_page7_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained diffusion model (T-stage reverse process with learned means μ_t and fixed covariances Σ_t), an online observation history h_k = {(φ_ℓ, y_ℓ)} accumulated over k rounds, a context x_k, and a finite action set A. It produces a single posterior sample θ̃_k of the d-dimensional model parameter, which is then plugged into the reward function to select the argmax action. The sampling traverses all T reverse-diffusion stages sequentially, so each posterior draw costs roughly T times a single Gaussian draw, and the diffusion model itself is trained offline on ~10,000 samples from the prior before the online phase begins.

## Method

The core idea is to factor the intractable posterior p(s_{0:T}|h) into a chain of T conditional posteriors p(s_{t-1}|s_t, h) and approximate each one in closed form. The key approximation (Eq. 6) replaces the intractable marginal ∫ p(h|s_0) p(s_0|s_t) ds_0 with p(h|s_t/√ᾱ_t), effectively treating the clean sample as the scaled diffused sample and dropping the noise term. For linear models, each conditional posterior becomes a product of two Gaussians—the pre-trained diffusion prior at stage t and the diffused empirical evidence—yielding closed-form means and covariances (Theorem 2). For GLMs, the same structure is recovered via the Laplace approximation (IRLS), where the prior mean and covariance are rescaled by √ᾱ_{t-1} and ᾱ_{t-1} at each stage (Theorem 4). The resulting algorithm, LaplaceDPS (called DiffTS in the bandit setting), is gradient-free and avoids the unstable likelihood-score term used by DPS, and the paper proves asymptotic consistency: the sampled parameter converges to the true θ_* as the number of observations grows (Theorem 3).

## Results

DiffTS achieves lower cumulative regret than all baselines across all experimental settings: synthetic 2D linear bandits (Figure 2), MovieLens 1M linear and logistic bandits (Figure 3), and MNIST linear and logistic bandits (Figure 5). In the non-bandit evaluation (Figure 7), DiffTS's earth mover's distance from the true posterior is comparable to MixTS, which has an exact closed-form posterior in that setting. The computational cost of DiffTS is approximately 120× that of standard TS (12 s vs. 0.1 s for 100 runs on the synthetic problems), scaling linearly with the number of diffusion stages T. In the cross-shaped prior problem, regret drops from 26 at T=1 to 15 at T=50, stabilizing around T=100.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiffTS outperforms all four baselines on every main experiment. Against TS (uninformative Gaussian prior) and TunedTS (learned Gaussian prior), DiffTS consistently achieves lower regret, with the gap widening in higher-dimensional settings (MovieLens, MNIST) where a single Gaussian cannot capture the prior's structure. Against MixTS (learned Gaussian mixture prior), DiffTS still wins on all problems, including the synthetic mixture-of-two-Gaussians case where MixTS can represent the prior exactly; in higher dimensions, MixTS barely outperforms TunedTS, highlighting the limitation of mixture priors. Against DPS (Chung et al., ICLR 2023), the contrast is stark: DPS diverges as the number of rounds increases due to instability of the likelihood-score-based approximation, while DiffTS remains stable and converges. In the non-bandit EMD evaluation (Figure 7), DiffTS matches MixTS (the exact posterior baseline), outperforms SMC (which degrades with sample size), and substantially outperforms DPS (which also degrades with sample size).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Thompson sampling in contextual bandits from Gaussian priors to diffusion model priors, building on the forward/reverse diffusion framework of Ho et al. [19] and the IRLS-based Laplace approximation for GLM posteriors. It departs from the score-based diffusion posterior sampling (DPS) of Chung et al. [12] by replacing the likelihood score—which grows linearly with the number of observations and causes instability—with a chain of per-stage Laplace approximations that mix the conditional prior and diffused evidence in closed form. It also generalizes the non-contextual diffusion-prior Thompson sampling of Hsieh et al. [23] to the contextual setting with vector-valued model parameters.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work addresses a practical limitation of Gaussian priors in bandits (inability to represent multimodal parameter distributions) while avoiding the instability of score-based posterior sampling as the observation history grows. The T-stage chain introduces a T× computational overhead over standard Gaussian-prior Thompson sampling, and the diffusion prior must be pre-trained on a representative sample of the parameter distribution, adding a learning cost and hyperparameter sensitivity (number of stages, training samples). Open questions include deriving regret bounds for DiffTS, extending the Laplace approximation beyond GLMs to general observation models, and understanding finite-sample behavior when the diffusion prior is only approximately correct.
