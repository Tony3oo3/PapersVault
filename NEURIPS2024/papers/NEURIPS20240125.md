## Problem

High-level cortical areas represent abstract concepts (objects, shapes, colors) that must flexibly modulate low-level sensory processing to guide goal-directed behavior, but no existing computational framework links these two levels of representation in a recurrent, temporally unfolding manner. Prior models are either stimulus-computable (operating on raw pixels) or grounded in biological circuit realism, but rarely both, and purely feedforward architectures cannot explain phenomena like reaction time variations or within-trial perceptual sharpening.

## Input / Output

The model receives sequential visual inputs: a cue image (a color swatch, geometric shape, or color-shape conjunction) presented for T discrete time steps, followed by a scene image (3–10 rendered 3D geometric objects) for another T steps, all at 128×128 px. The primary output is an object count (0–5) read from the last layer's activity at the final time step via a linear readout. For psychophysics tasks, the readout is replaced with binary target-present/absent classification, and output entropy across the trial's time steps serves as a reaction-time proxy.

## Method

DCnet is a 4-layer recurrent convolutional network (~1.8M parameters) where each layer is a retinotopic hypercolumn with separate tuned excitatory and weakly-tuned inhibitory populations (4:1 ratio) obeying Dale's law, connected via local lateral, feedforward, and recurrent synapses with learnable cell-type-specific time constants. The key architectural innovation is a higher-order layer that receives spatially pooled input from all sensory areas and injects low-rank multiplicative modulations into inter-area feedforward projections: it computes two linear projections of the pooled cue-phase activity, takes their outer product, and pointwise-scales the scene-phase inputs, effectively using the network's own earlier sensory responses as context cues. This mechanism is trained end-to-end with cross-entropy loss and AdamW (no normalization layers), and the same trained model is fine-tuned on classic psychophysics attention tasks by swapping the readout to binary classification. The low-rank structure is critical: removing top-down feedback drops accuracy from 99% to 38%, and lesion analysis reveals an emergent cortical selectivity gradient where early areas encode color and late areas encode shape.

## Results

On the vis-count task, DCnet (~1.8M params) achieves 99%, 95%, and 73% accuracy on color, shape, and conjunction trials respectively, consistently outperforming all state-of-the-art DNN baselines with orders of magnitude more parameters. On a harder generalization test with both novel cues and novel scenes, DCnet retains 55% accuracy while the best baselines drop to chance (16.67%). On the psychophysics fine-tuning tasks, DCnet achieves 97% accuracy on the distractor heterogeneity task and 95% on the target-distractor color difference task, with output entropy faithfully replicating known reaction time patterns. As a scaling check, the DCnet sensory backbone reaches 84.79% test accuracy on CIFAR-10 without standard ML embellishments.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DCnet outperforms all four implicit feedforward baselines (6-Layer CNN, ResNet-18, Swin-T, ViT-B/16) on every trial type of vis-count, despite having 1.5× to 48× fewer parameters. The gap is most dramatic on the harder generalization test: DCnet achieves 55% on novel cues and scenes while even the largest baseline (ViT-B/16, 86M params) collapses to the 16.67% chance level. The Convolutional RNN baseline (~8.8M params), which lacks the explicit low-rank top-down modulation mechanism, also underperforms DCnet, confirming the importance of the higher-order modulatory layer. GPT-4 in a zero-shot setting achieves only 37% on 30 trials, well below DCnet's performance. On the psychophysics tasks, DCnet's output entropy replicates the 'pop-out' effect (invariant RT at high target-distractor difference, linearly increasing RT at low difference) that feedforward models cannot capture, and the EDL-based RT metric confirms these findings (Pearson's r = 0.92 for low-difference trials, r = 0.2 for high-difference trials).

## Relation to Prior Work

DCnet extends the line of stimulus-computable recurrent vision models (Linsley et al., 2020) by adding a higher-order modulatory layer that injects low-rank, context-dependent perturbations derived from the model's own earlier sensory responses, operationalizing the 'modulatory homunculus' concept from theoretical work on low-rank recurrent dynamics (Mastrogiuseppe & Ostojic, 2018; Logiaco et al., 2021). It departs from purely feedforward DNN baselines and phenomenological visual search models by incorporating biologically grounded circuit properties—separate E/I populations obeying Dale's law, lateral inhibition, learnable cell-type-specific time constants, and hypercolumnar organization—while remaining end-to-end trainable on raw pixels. The EDL-based reaction time metric from Goetschalckx et al. (2024) is adopted as a secondary behavioral measure, linking the model's internal dynamics to psychophysical observables.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work provides one of the first frameworks that simultaneously achieves biological circuit-level realism, stimulus-computability, and the ability to generate testable in silico electrophysiology predictions (selectivity gradients, E-I co-tuning, timescale gradients) that map onto known neurophysiology. The emergence of opposing color/shape selectivity gradients from counting supervision alone, and the faithful replication of classic pop-out and distractor-heterogeneity psychophysics after fine-tuning, suggest the architecture captures genuine computational principles of cortical attention. Key limitations include the absence of long-range inter-area feedback, no compartmental separation of feedforward/feedback inputs, a simplified RT metric, and restriction to a single sensory modality—each representing a concrete direction for future work toward a more complete cortical model.
