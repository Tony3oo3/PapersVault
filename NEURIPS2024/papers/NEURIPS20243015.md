## Problem

Pretrained image diffusion models are trained on Internet-scale datasets but cannot directly generate non-image visual content such as 3D textures, panoramas, or ambiguous images without finetuning on small, domain-specific datasets that cause overfitting and loss of the rich generative prior. Previous diffusion-synchronization approaches each addressed only one specific synchronization point for one specific application, with no unified framework or systematic comparison across projection types (1-to-1, 1-to-n, n-to-1) to determine which synchronization strategy generalizes most broadly.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt plus a structural conditioning signal (a 3D mesh, a 360° depth map, or a set of 3D Gaussian splats) and produces the corresponding textured output in a canonical space: a 1024×1024 texture image for meshes, a 1024×2048 equirectangular panorama, or optimized RGB colors for Gaussian splats. Internally, the diffusion model (a depth-conditioned ControlNet on Stable Diffusion) operates on perspective view images of 768×768 or 512×512, which are projected from and unprojected to the canonical space via rendering or spherical mapping. For n-to-1 projections (e.g., volume rendering of Gaussian splats), instance variables are initialized directly from a standard Gaussian rather than projected from the canonical latent to avoid variance collapse.

## Method

SyncTweedies identifies five possible synchronization points within the DDIM denoising step—after noise prediction ε_θ, after Tweedie's formula φ^(t), after the posterior mean ψ^(t), and two variants operating in the canonical versus instance space—and proves that for 1-to-1 projections all five are mathematically equivalent. For 1-to-n projections (e.g., mesh rendering with nearest-neighbor sampling), the denoising process is highly sensitive to noise predictions and intermediate noisy latents but robust to Tweedie's formula outputs, so Cases 1, 3, and 4 degrade while Cases 2 and 5 remain stable. For n-to-1 projections (e.g., volume rendering of Gaussian splats), Case 5 suffers a variance-decrease problem because the weighted sum of Gaussian samples has strictly lower variance than its components, producing blurry outputs; SyncTweedies (Case 2), which averages Tweedie's formula outputs in the canonical space while denoising in instance spaces, is the only case that works across all three projection types. The entire pipeline is zero-shot: no finetuning is performed, and the pretrained diffusion model's prior is fully preserved.

## Results

On 3D mesh texturing (429 mesh-prompt pairs), SyncTweedies achieves FID 21.76, KID 1.46, and CLIP-S 28.89, outperforming all baselines including the closest competitor SyncMVD (FID 22.76, KID 1.74). On depth-to-360-panorama generation (500 panoramas), SyncTweedies reaches FID 42.11 and KID 21.19, roughly halving the FID of the finetuning-based MVDiffusion (FID 80.51). On 3D Gaussian splats texturing (8 scenes, 50 views), SyncTweedies achieves FID 106.47 and KID 14.62, the only method that handles the n-to-1 projection without the variance-decrease degradation that plagues Case 5 (FID 116.73, KID 18.35).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SyncTweedies wins across all three main applications and all 11 baselines. Against the closest diffusion-synchronization competitor SyncMVD (Case 5), SyncTweedies achieves a small but consistent edge in 3D mesh texturing (FID 21.76 vs. 22.76; KID 1.46 vs. 1.74) and a larger margin in 3D Gaussian splats texturing (FID 106.47 vs. 116.73; KID 14.62 vs. 18.35), where Case 5 suffers from the variance-decrease issue inherent to n-to-1 projections. Against finetuning-based methods, SyncTweedies outperforms Paint3D by ~10 FID points in mesh texturing (21.76 vs. 31.66) and MVDiffusion by ~38 FID points in panorama generation (42.11 vs. 80.51), demonstrating that zero-shot synchronization preserves the rich prior of Internet-scale pretrained models better than domain-specific finetuning. Optimization-based baselines (SDS, MVDream-SDS, Paint-it) consistently produce over-saturated textures with higher FID/KID; SyncTweedies beats SDS by ~4 FID and MVDream-SDS by ~35 FID in Gaussian splats texturing. Iterative-view-updating methods (TEXTure, Text2Tex, IN2N) accumulate errors across views, yielding FID scores 14–28 points worse than SyncTweedies in mesh texturing and ~3 FID worse in Gaussian splats. In the 1-to-1 projection regime (ambiguous images, arbitrary-sized images), all five synchronization cases are mathematically equivalent and empirically produce identical outputs, so SyncTweedies ties with MultiDiffusion and Visual Anagrams in those settings but uniquely extends to 1-to-n and n-to-1 projections where the others degrade or fail.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on DDIM's deterministic denoising framework and Tweedie's formula to propose a unified diffusion synchronization framework that subsumes prior methods as special cases: Visual Anagrams (Case 4, aggregating noise predictions), MultiDiffusion/SyncDiffusion (Case 3, aggregating posterior means), and SyncMVD (Case 5, denoising in canonical space with Tweedie aggregation). The key conceptual departure is identifying that averaging Tweedie's formula outputs while denoising in instance spaces (Case 2, SyncTweedies) is the only synchronization strategy universally applicable across all projection types (1-to-1, 1-to-n, n-to-1), and that this zero-shot approach outperforms finetuning-based methods (Paint3D, MVDiffusion, SyncDreamer) that sacrifice the rich prior of Internet-scale pretrained diffusion models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By providing a single zero-shot framework applicable to ambiguous images, 360° panoramas, 3D mesh textures, and 3D Gaussian splat textures without task-specific finetuning, SyncTweedies removes the need for expensive target-domain datasets and preserves the generalizability of pretrained diffusion models. The clean variance-decrease analysis explaining why n-to-1 projections (e.g., volume rendering) break canonical-space denoising offers a useful theoretical tool for future synchronization designs. Open questions include whether the framework can jointly update geometry and appearance of 3D objects, and whether the approach extends to other canonical spaces beyond those mappable to images.
