## Problem

Existing AI-based data assimilation methods can only assimilate observations at the same resolution as the forecasting model, requiring interpolation as a preprocessing step that introduces information loss and prevents out-of-domain generalization. Since real-world observations (e.g., satellite data) typically have much higher resolution than the model grid, this limitation is a critical practical bottleneck. The paper addresses how to assimilate observations at arbitrary resolutions directly, without interpolation or retraining.

## Input / Output

The model takes a background state from FengWu at 1.40625° (128×256 grid, 69 variables across 13 pressure levels plus surface) and sparse observations at any resolution (tested at 1.40625°, 0.703125°, and 0.25°) as coordinate-value pairs, along with target grid coordinates. It outputs the posterior mean and variance of the atmospheric state at the background resolution. A key property is that the model trained at one resolution generalizes to unseen resolutions without fine-tuning, and the same architecture handles observational information reconstruction (no background) by simply omitting the background condition.

## Method

FNP builds on the ConvCNP neural process architecture, replacing standard CNN convolutions with Neural Fourier Layers (NFL) that combine a frequency-domain linear operation branch with an identity-mapping branch to preserve high-frequency details. A Spatial-Variable Decoupled (SVD) representation separates per-variable spatial features from cross-variable features, reducing training difficulty. The Dynamic Alignment and Merge (DAM) module aligns the background and observation functional representations to the target domain by interpolating in feature space (not observation space), then uses Euclidean-distance similarity in the channel dimension to dynamically select which features to retain at each grid point. Data embedding uses the SetConv layer, which is permutation-invariant and operates on arbitrary coordinate-value pairs, enabling native handling of off-grid and multi-resolution inputs. The model is trained with negative log-likelihood loss under a Gaussian likelihood, using AdamW at lr=1e-4 for 20 epochs on 4 A100 GPUs.

## Results

On the ERA5 dataset with 10% observations, FNP achieves the best overall MSE (0.0202) and MAE (0.0664) at the standard 1.40625° resolution, outperforming Adas (MSE 0.0221) and ConvCNP (MSE 0.0252). The advantage grows dramatically at higher observation resolutions: at 0.25°, fine-tuned FNP reduces MSE to 0.0058 and MAE to 0.0339, while Adas (0.0231) and ConvCNP (0.0280) degrade or remain flat. For the q700 variable, FNP achieves a 54.2% WRMSE reduction at 0.25° resolution compared to the 1.40625° baseline, and is the only model that consistently improves as observation resolution and quantity increase.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FNP outperforms both baselines (Adas and ConvCNP) on all metrics (MSE, MAE, and per-variable WRMSE) at every tested resolution. Against Adas, FNP's advantage is most pronounced at 0.25° resolution, where FNP achieves MSE 0.0058 versus Adas's 0.0231 (a ~4× improvement), while Adas actually degrades relative to its 1.40625° performance due to interpolation-induced information loss. Against ConvCNP, FNP also dominates: at 0.25°, FNP's MSE (0.0058) is nearly 5× lower than ConvCNP's (0.0280), and FNP's q700 WRMSE (2.9614) is more than half that of ConvCNP (6.3612). A key differentiator is out-of-distribution generalization: without fine-tuning, FNP at 0.25° still achieves MSE 0.0196, whereas ConvCNP without fine-tuning degrades severely to MSE 0.0651, and Adas cannot handle different resolutions at all without retraining on interpolated data. FNP is also the only model that monotonically improves as observation resolution increases, while Adas worsens at 0.25° and ConvCNP shows inconsistent gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

FNP builds directly on the convolutional conditional neural process (ConvCNP) framework, which provides the SetConv-based data embedding and flexible coordinate-value conditioning that enables grid-agnostic input. It departs from prior AI-based data assimilation methods (Adas, DiffDA, Fuxi-DA) that are locked to a single observation resolution by replacing their fixed-grid architectures with the neural process paradigm, and from ConvCNP itself by introducing the neural Fourier layer (borrowed from FNO) to capture high-frequency atmospheric signals and the Dynamic Alignment and Merge module to fuse background and observation representations at the target resolution. The spatial-variable decoupled representation is a novel architectural choice specific to multi-variable weather fields, not present in prior neural process applications to geoscience.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

In operational weather systems, observations routinely arrive at higher resolution (0.1° or finer) than the model grid (0.25°), forcing existing methods to interpolate and lose information; FNP eliminates this bottleneck and is the only tested model whose accuracy improves monotonically as observation density increases, meaning all deployed instruments can be fully exploited. The ability to generalize across resolutions and to the observational information reconstruction task without fine-tuning suggests a single model could serve multiple operational roles. Key limitations remain: experiments use simulated observations on a single reanalysis dataset with a single background model, the method performs 3D-Var without a temporal dimension, and the field still lacks standardized benchmarks for data assimilation, making cross-method comparisons difficult.
