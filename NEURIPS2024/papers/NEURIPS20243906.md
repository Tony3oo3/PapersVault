## Problem

Surgical video-language pretraining suffers from two intertwined challenges: (1) textual information loss at multiple hierarchical levels—noisy ASR transcriptions, overly terse keystep labels, and redundant abstracts—limiting the quality of language supervision in an already small dataset (~300 hours); and (2) the strong procedural/temporal dependency in surgical workflows (e.g., dissecting before clipping) that standard contrastive objectives ignore, preventing the model from learning cross-modal procedural alignment.

## Input / Output

The pretraining consumes surgical lecture video clips (4, 16, or 64 frames sampled depending on hierarchy) paired with three levels of LLM-augmented text: corrected narration sentences (clip-level), expanded keystep descriptions (phase-level), and compressed abstracts (video-level). The dual-encoder (ResNet50 visual + ClinicalBERT textual) produces a shared embedding space where downstream tasks extract frozen visual features for linear probing or compute cosine similarities against manually crafted class prompts for zero-shot phase classification and cross-modal retrieval at all three hierarchical granularities.

## Method

PeskaVLP introduces two complementary mechanisms. First, hierarchical knowledge augmentation prompts an LLM with role-specific behaviors—'recipe' to generate pseudo-steps that correct narration typos, 'dictionary' to expand terse keysteps into descriptive sentences, and 'summarizer' to compress abstracts—thereby diversifying and denoising the text supervision without altering original surgical concepts. Second, the LecNCE loss is hierarchy-specific: at clip-level it combines InfoNCE language supervision with SimSiam visual self-supervision (two augmented views of the same clip) to compensate for the small dataset; at phase- and video-level it adds a DTW-based contrastive regularization that computes the minimum alignment cost between video frames and a child-level text sequence, then enforces via a hinge loss that this cost is lower than the cost when the text sequence is temporally reversed (hard negative). Training alternates 25/15/115 batches across clip/phase/video levels, optimizing a single shared encoder pair for all three granularities.

## Results

On zero-shot surgical phase recognition, PeskaVLP achieves an average accuracy of 41.0% and F1 of 27.1% across four downstream datasets (Cholec80, AutoLaparo, StrasBypass70, BernBypass70), outperforming the prior surgical VLP method HecVL (28.7% acc / 19.3% F1) by 12.3% in accuracy and 7.8% in F1. On cross-modal retrieval, PeskaVLP achieves R@1 of 38.8% for video-to-abstract retrieval, surpassing HecVL (28.2%) and CLIP (1.2%). In 10%-shot linear probing, PeskaVLP reaches 61.9% accuracy on Cholec80 and 63.8% on StrasBypass70, exceeding HecVL (56.1% and 60.2%) and MoCo v2 (57.6% and 63.1%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PeskaVLP consistently outperforms all video-language pretraining baselines on zero-shot phase recognition. Against the closest surgical VLP baseline HecVL [75], PeskaVLP gains +12.3% accuracy and +7.8% F1 on average, with the largest absolute improvement on StrasBypass70 (+19.8% acc) and BernBypass70 (+22.9% acc), demonstrating strong cross-center generalization. Compared to CLIP [52] pretrained on SVL, PeskaVLP improves average accuracy from 19.6% to 41.0%, showing that hierarchical knowledge augmentation and the DTW-based procedure-aware loss are critical for small surgical datasets. Against MIL-NCE [46] (Howto100M), PeskaVLP achieves 6.4x higher average accuracy despite training on a dataset 10,000× smaller. In linear probing (Table 3), PeskaVLP surpasses MoCo v2 [55] (visual self-supervision on SVL) by +1.7% on Cholec80 and +1.8% on AutoLaparo at 100% shot, and outperforms SurgVLP [76] by +6.4% on Cholec80, confirming that combining language supervision with visual self-supervision yields superior representations. The fully-supervised references (TransVNet [17] at 90.3% on Cholec80, ResNet50 [31] at 90.2% on StrasBypass70) remain substantially higher, but PeskaVLP narrows the gap to within ~45 points without any task-specific fine-tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the authors' own HecVL framework for hierarchical surgical video-language pretraining by addressing two specific weaknesses: noisy/limited textual supervision and the absence of procedural temporal modeling. The LLM-based knowledge augmentation draws on the broader trend of using large language models to enrich textual inputs (LaCLIP, K-Lite) but applies it hierarchically to correct, explain, and summarize texts at different granularities. The DTW-based procedure-aware loss (LecNCE) departs from Paprika's knowledge-graph-based procedural pretraining by embedding temporal ordering directly into the contrastive objective via reversed-text hard negatives, and complements clip-level language supervision with SimSiam visual self-supervision to compensate for the small SVL dataset.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a ~300-hour surgical lecture dataset, when augmented with LLM knowledge and trained with procedure-aware objectives, can produce zero-shot surgical phase recognition competitive with fully-supervised models on specific datasets, and generalizes across three surgical procedures and two clinical centers. The finding that noisy ASR-derived narrations actually degrade visual representations below pure self-supervision (SurgVLP < MoCo v2 in linear probing) is a useful caution for the field. Open questions include whether the approach scales to non-laparoscopic surgery (endoscopic, microsurgical), whether DTW-based alignment generalizes to longer or less routined procedures, and whether LLM augmentation risks over-standardizing the linguistic diversity needed for robust real-world deployment.
