## Problem

Foundation models are expensive to train, so model families offer only a handful of fixed sizes (e.g., Llama-2: 7B/13B/34B/70B), forcing practitioners to settle for a less accurate model than their latency or cost budget allows. Existing remedies like quantization, pruning, or distillation require additional training per deployment constraint and do not yield a truly elastic, single-model solution that spans the full accuracy-vs-compute tradeoff.

## Input / Output

For language models, the input is a tokenized text sequence (max 1024 tokens, 256k SentencePiece vocabulary) and the output is generated text; for vision models, the input is an RGB image and the output is a class label or [CLS] embedding for retrieval. The distinguishing output feature is that a single trained MatFormer model yields a family of nested submodels of varying FFN widths (ratios 0.5, 1, 2, 4) that can be extracted at inference time with zero additional training, and the nested structure preserves metric-space geometry so that smaller submodels remain compatible with embeddings produced by the full model.

## Method

MatFormer imposes a matryoshka nested structure on the Transformer FFN block: the d_ff hidden neurons are ordered so that sub-block T_i uses only the first m_i neurons, giving T_1 ⊂ T_2 ⊂ … ⊂ T_g with g=4 exponentially spaced granularities (FFN ratios {0.5, 1, 2, 4}). Training randomly samples one granularity per step and applies standard SGD, so each smaller submodel receives gradient updates whenever a larger one is sampled, effectively giving the smallest model 4× the tokens of an independently trained baseline at no extra cost. At inference, Mix'n'Match selects a per-layer granularity configuration (e.g., M for the first 8 layers, L for the last 8) to form g^l distinct submodels; the recommended heuristic is to use non-decreasing granularities with minimum slope across layers, which empirically outperforms evolutionary NAS. The nested weight sharing also enables attention-cache sharing between draft and verifier models in speculative decoding and preserves distance structure across granularities for adaptive image retrieval.

## Results

On the 850M-parameter MatLM, all four nested granularities (S/M/L/XL) outperform their independently trained vanilla Transformer counterparts on validation loss: MatLM-S achieves 2.970 vs. Baseline-S 3.017, MatLM-M 2.934 vs. 2.971, MatLM-L 2.886 vs. 2.910, and MatLM-XL 2.846 vs. 2.840. Mix'n'Match submodels (e.g., 55% of XL compute) approximate XL performance with only ~1% accuracy drop, compared to 2% for the fixed MatLM-M granularity. For vision, MatViT-L/16 achieves 83.67% top-1 on ImageNet-1K (vs. 83.17% for ViT-L/16), and its 175M Mix'n'Match submodel reduces retrieval compute by 40% with <0.5% accuracy loss. Speculative decoding with MatLM submodels yields 1.14× speedup on LAMBADA (vs. 1.10× for independently trained baselines), further boosted to 1.16× with shared attention cache.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against independently trained vanilla Transformers [41], MatLM outperforms at every granularity: the smallest submodel (MatLM-S, 582M) achieves 0.047 lower validation loss than Baseline-S, while MatLM-XL essentially matches Baseline-XL (2.846 vs. 2.840), confirming that the nested training does not sacrifice the largest model's quality. Against DynaBERT [27], MatFormer wins by 0.01 log perplexity on the 850M model because DynaBERT's joint optimization of all four granularities per step yields only one-fourth the gradient updates; even with 15% extra compute, DynaBERT still trails MatFormer. Against OFA [9], MatFormer avoids the bell-shaped loss curve (OFA underperforms at both S and XL extremes due to random subnetwork sampling) and eliminates the need for costly NAS-based submodel selection, with Mix'n'Match matching or exceeding NAS-predicted architectures. Against standard ViT [21], MatViT-L/16 improves ImageNet-1K top-1 accuracy by up to 0.35% (83.67% vs. 83.17%), and uniquely preserves metric-space structure across granularities—enabling a 40% compute reduction in adaptive image retrieval with <0.5% accuracy drop, a capability the independently trained ViT baselines cannot provide (they yield near-zero retrieval accuracy when used as query encoders against a larger database encoder).

## Relation to Prior Work

MatFormer extends Matryoshka Representation Learning (MRL) from the output/embedding space into the weight space of Transformer FFN blocks, creating nested sub-blocks T₁ ⊂ T₂ ⊂ … ⊂ T_g. It departs fundamentally from prior many-in-one network approaches: unlike OFA (which samples random non-nested subnetworks and requires NAS for selection) or DynaBERT (which jointly optimizes all granularities in a single step), MatFormer samples and optimizes only g=4 nested granularities across training steps, then extracts exponentially more submodels at inference via the Mix'n'Match heuristic. This nested, sampled-optimization strategy is the key architectural and methodological distinction from the CNN-based elastic network literature (Slimmable Networks, HAT) and the encoder-only Transformer work (ShARC).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MatFormer offers a practical path to elastic deployment: a single training run yields hundreds of accurate submodels at zero additional cost, addressing the real-world problem that practitioners must choose between discrete model sizes that may not match their latency budget. The consistency property of nested submodels enables concrete inference speedups (speculative decoding) and a novel adaptive retrieval application (40% compute reduction with <0.5% accuracy loss), broadening the utility beyond mere model compression. Limitations include evaluation only up to 850M parameters, modest absolute improvements over independently trained baselines (~0.01 log perplexity, ~1% accuracy), and the fact that standard scaling laws do not capture the value of non-optimized Mix'n'Match submodels. Open questions include whether the approach scales to 10B+ parameter models, dynamic per-token routing, and hardware-level optimizations for the nested FFN structure.
