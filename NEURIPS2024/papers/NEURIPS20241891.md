## Problem

Backpropagation, the standard training algorithm for GNNs, suffers from biological implausibility (weight symmetry and update locking) and limits parallelism and scalability. While non-BP methods like Direct Feedback Alignment (DFA) work for Euclidean data in fully-connected and convolutional networks, extending them to graph data is non-trivial because graph data violates the i.i.d. assumption (tying gradients to topology) and semi-supervised settings lack ground-truth errors for unlabeled nodes.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a graph G=(V,E,X) specified by an adjacency matrix A, a node feature matrix X in R^{n x d}, and partial class labels y_L for a subset of labeled nodes. It outputs predicted class labels for all nodes. Unusually, the training signal for unlabeled nodes is not a true error but a pseudo error generated by iteratively spreading labeled-node residuals along graph edges via a label-spreading process, then rescaled to match the average labeled-node error magnitude and filtered by a confidence mask that discards nodes whose corrected predictions do not yield a single dominant class.

## Method

DFA-GNN replaces the sequential backward pass of BP with parallel direct feedback links that incorporate graph topology: instead of propagating errors through transposed weight matrices, each hidden layer receives an error signal of the form (S^T)^k E B^{(l)}, where S is the normalized adjacency matrix and B^{(l)} are fixed random feedback matrices, so that all layers update simultaneously in a single forward pass. For semi-supervised learning, a pseudo error generator applies iterative label spreading (Z^{(t+1)} = (1-alpha)E + alpha*S*Z^{(t)}) to diffuse residual errors from labeled to unlabeled nodes, followed by L1-norm rescaling to preserve error magnitude. A node filter then masks out poorly predicted nodes whose pseudo errors are unreliable, restricting the update to well-predicted nodes only. The paper proves convergence by showing that under right-orthogonal feedback matrices, the DFA gradient direction aligns with the true BP gradient (angle < 90 degrees), and empirically validates this on 10 benchmarks where DFA-GNN matches or exceeds BP while outperforming all prior non-BP GNN methods (FORWARDGNN, PEPITA, CaFo, FF).

## Results

On 10 benchmark datasets for semi-supervised node classification, DFA-GNN achieves the best or second-best accuracy across all datasets, outperforming standard backpropagation (BP) on 8 out of 10. Headline results include 87.72% on Cora (vs. 86.04% BP), 80.49% on CiteSeer (vs. 78.20% BP), and 79.51% on Texas (vs. 54.26% BP), with the largest gains on heterophilic graphs. On large-scale datasets (Flickr, Reddit, ogbn-arxiv), DFA-GNN matches or exceeds BP while using significantly less memory than competing non-BP methods. The method also demonstrates superior robustness to over-smoothing and random structural attacks (edge add/remove/flip at perturbation rates up to 0.8).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against standard BP, DFA-GNN wins on 8 of 10 datasets, with the largest margins on heterophilic graphs (Texas: +25.25%, Cornell: +3.93%, Actor: +2.13%) and smaller gains on homophilic ones (Cora: +1.68%, CiteSeer: +2.29%). BP retains an edge on Computer (89.48% vs. 86.72%) and is essentially tied on Chameleon (41.28% vs. 41.19%). Against the most competitive non-BP baseline, FORWARDGNN-SF, DFA-GNN wins on 9 of 10 datasets (e.g., Texas: 79.51% vs. 37.71%, Cornell: 75.24% vs. 60.66%) but loses narrowly on Chameleon (41.19% vs. 42.35%). The remaining non-BP methods (PEPITA, CaFo, FF) are substantially outperformed across all datasets, with PEPITA and CaFo often falling 10–60 points below DFA-GNN, reflecting their inability to handle the non-i.i.d. and non-Euclidean nature of graph data. The paper attributes these gaps to the layer-wise greedy strategies and data augmentation (virtual nodes) used by those methods, which disrupt graph topology and fail to exploit cross-layer correlations in GNNs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Direct Feedback Alignment (DFA), originally designed for fully-connected and convolutional networks on Euclidean data, to graph neural networks by incorporating the graph adjacency matrix into the random feedback links to handle the non-i.i.d. structure of graph data. It departs from the most recent non-BP GNN training method, FORWARDGNN (Park et al., 2023), which relies on data augmentation with virtual nodes and layer-wise forward-forward training, by instead directly propagating prediction errors through topology-aware feedback and generating pseudo errors for unlabeled nodes via label spreading. The pseudo error generator draws on the label spreading framework of Zhou et al. (2003) and residual propagation ideas from graph regression, adapting them to the classification setting with a rescaling and filtering mechanism.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DFA-GNN provides a biologically plausible, parallelizable alternative to backpropagation for GNN training that matches or exceeds BP accuracy across 13 datasets and 5 GNN architectures, while demonstrating superior robustness to over-smoothing and structural attacks. The finding that a non-BP method can consistently outperform BP on heterophilic graphs (Texas, Cornell, Actor) where layer-wise methods fail suggests that global error propagation through topology-aware feedback is better suited to the correlated structure of graph data. Open questions include whether the approach extends to inductive/transductive settings beyond semi-supervised node classification, whether the pseudo error generator can be made more principled for multi-class or regression tasks, and whether the modest computational overhead (pseudo error generation, mask filtering) can be further reduced for very large graphs.
