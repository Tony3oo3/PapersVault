## Problem

Deep learning models exhibit both sudden emergence of new skills and smooth power-law scaling of loss, yet no analytically tractable model has connected the underlying training dynamics of neural networks to these two phenomena. Michaud et al. proposed a quanta model for emergence and empirically measured scaling laws on the multitask sparse parity problem, but left the link between actual network dynamics and the quanta model unestablished.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241253/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives binary bit strings of length n_s + n_b, where the first n_s bits are one-hot control bits selecting which of n_s parity tasks to solve and the remaining n_b bits are uniformly random skill bits; task frequencies follow a Zipfian power-law with exponent α+1. The target is a scalar parity over m=3 relevant sparse bits, scaled by S. The analytical outputs are closed-form expressions for per-task skill strength R_k(T) and full scaling laws (exponents and prefactor constants) for loss as a function of training time T, dataset size D, parameter count N, and optimal compute C = T×N.

## Method

The core contribution is a multilinear model f_T(i,x) = Σ a_k(T)b_k(T)g_k(i,x) expanded in orthogonal skill basis functions g_k, where the product structure a_k·b_k mimics the layered architecture of neural networks and produces sigmoidal (emergent) saturation dynamics. Because the g_k have mutually exclusive supports (g_k·g_k' = δ_kk'), the empirical loss decomposes into independent skill losses, decoupling the dynamics of each skill and making the system exactly solvable under gradient flow. To match real neural networks that must 'discover' skills rather than having them built in, the authors introduce extended models with calibration constants: B² rescales time dynamics, D_c makes the model a D_c-shot learner for data emergence, and N_c requires N_c basis functions per skill for parameter emergence. A single calibration on the first skill (n_s=1) suffices to predict the ordered emergence of all subsequent skills in a 2-layer MLP and a transformer. The paper derives rigorous scaling laws with prefactor constants for all four resources, yielding exponents −α/(α+1) for T and D, −α for N, and −α/(α+2) for optimal compute C.

## Results

The multilinear model yields closed-form scaling law exponents for the multitask sparse parity problem: −α/(α+1) for time T and data D, −α for parameters N, and −α/(α+2) for optimal compute C = T×N (Table 2). With a single calibration constant B²=1/22 (time), D_c=800 (data), or N_c=4 (parameters), the model reproduces the sigmoidal emergence of all 5 skills in a 2-layer MLP trained with SGD (width 1000, n_s=5, m=3, n_b=32, α=0.6), matching the ordered emergence sequence and saturation times observed in direct simulations. The same power-law relationship τ_emerge(k) ∝ k^(α+1) holds for a transformer architecture (α=0.9), confirming the generality of the decoupled dynamics.

**Table 2: Summary of the scaling laws for the multilinear model.**

| Bottleneck | Condition 1 | Condition 2 | Exponent | Prefactor | Scaling law |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Time (T) | D ≫ NT², T³ | N^(α+1) ≫ T | −α/(α+1) | Thm. 4 | Thms. 2, 3 |
| Data (D) | T ≫ D(log D)^(1+ε) | N^(α+1) ≫ D | −α/(α+1) | Thm. 5 | Thm. 5 |
| Parameter (N) | D ≫ T³ | N^(α+1) = o(T) | −α | Thm. 1 | Thm. 1 |
| Compute (C) | D ≫ T³ | N^(α+1) ≈ T | −α/(α+2) | Cor. 5 | Cor. 4 |

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not benchmark against competing analytical models; instead, it validates its multilinear model predictions against direct simulations of a 2-layer MLP (SGD, width 1000) and a decoder transformer on the multitask sparse parity dataset. The multilinear model, calibrated only on the first skill (n_s=1), successfully predicts the ordered emergence of all 5 skills in the n_s=5 MLP for time, data, and parameter scaling (Fig. 1), with the sigmoidal skill-strength curves matching within one standard deviation over ≥10 runs. For the transformer (α=0.9), the emergent time τ_emerge(k) follows the same k^(α+1) power law as the multilinear model (Fig. 4). The scaling exponents match those of Michaud et al. [18] for T, D, and N, and of Hutter [20] for D, but the multilinear model additionally provides explicit prefactor constants and rigorous error bounds (Appendix J) that the quanta model of Michaud et al. does not. The main limitation noted is that the NN takes longer to fully saturate each skill (multiple sigmoidal modes from individual neurons) and shows higher variance in parameter emergence due to less strict bias toward frequent skills.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Saxe et al.'s exact solution for two-layer linear network dynamics (the sigmoidal growth of a_k·b_k products) and Michaud et al.'s multitask sparse parity dataset with power-law skill frequencies. The key departure is representing skills as mutually exclusive orthogonal basis functions g_k, which decouples the dynamics of each skill and makes the full system analytically solvable under gradient flow. While Michaud et al. proposed a 'quanta model' of abrupt skill acquisition and empirically measured scaling laws, they did not establish a mechanistic link to network dynamics; this paper fills that gap by showing the multilinear model in the skill basis reproduces both the sigmoidal emergence and the power-law scaling exponents (−α/(α+1) for T and D, −α for N, −α/(α+2) for optimal compute) with explicit prefactors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides a rare analytically tractable bridge between the continuous scaling of loss and the discontinuous emergence of skills, showing that stage-like training (one skill saturating before the next emerges) naturally produces power-law scaling from power-law skill frequencies. A single calibration constant per resource (B²=1/22 for time, D_c=800 for data, N_c=4 for parameters), fit on a one-skill system, predicts the ordered emergence of all five skills in a 2-layer MLP and the emergent-time power law in a transformer. Key limitations include the idealized power-law dataset, the inability to capture non-linear interactions among skills, and the narrow validation (n_s=5, m=3, small architectures). Open questions include whether the decoupled stage-like dynamics persist in deeper/wider networks, how 'complex skills' in language decompose into linearly independent basis functions, and whether the framework extends to non-MSE losses and non-convex training dynamics.
