## Problem

Specular highlight removal for document images suffers from a critical data gap: existing real-world datasets require expensive multi-illumination experimental setups, while synthetic data exhibits poor generalizability to real-world conditions. No openly available real-world specular highlight dataset exists specifically for document images, a domain where glare is pervasive yet data is scarce.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243071/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The pipeline takes printed FUNSD document forms (199 forms, 31,485 words) placed inside transparent filing pockets of varying quality and textures, illuminated by LED arrays behind fixed-angle polarizing filters. A Sony IMX250MZR polarization sensor captures four greyscale 1224×1024 frames per shot at angles 0°, 45°, 90°, and 135°. The output per scene is an aligned pair: the S₀ reconstruction (i₀ + i₉₀, representing the normal image with highlights) and a deglared counterfactual obtained by minimum pooling across all four polarization angles per pixel, yielding 3,184 scenes and 19,104 images total.

## Method

The core contribution is a polarization-based data generation method that exploits the physics of specular reflection: highlights from polarized light are suppressed when the light's polarization angle is orthogonal to the sensor's polarization direction. An enclosed setup with LED arrays behind fixed-angle polarizing filters minimizes unpolarized ambient light, ensuring most reflected glare is polarized and thus filterable by the sensor. The deglared image is produced algorithmically via 2×2 minimum pooling over the four polarization-angle frames, yielding near-perfect counterfactual alignment without requiring separate capture passes. The benchmark evaluates five specular highlight removal models (Fu et al., M2-Net, TSHRNet, Hu et al., MIMO-UNetPlus) on both image quality metrics (PSNR, SSIM, UIQI) and downstream OCR performance (WER, CER, LED) across Textract, EasyOCR, and Tesseract. A two-part generalizability study retrains MIMO-UNetPlus on SHDocs and evaluates cross-dataset transfer to SHIQ and PSD, demonstrating competitive performance with a 64.6 MB model against 400+ MB baselines.

## Results

On the SHDocs evaluation set, most specular highlight removal models perform worse than the no-enhancement baseline on both image quality (PSNR/SSIM/UIQI) and downstream OCR metrics. M2-Net achieves the best PSNR (32.72 vs. 32.18 baseline) but still underperforms on SSIM (0.9426 vs. 0.9589) and UIQI (0.7201 vs. 0.8241). On OCR, MIMO-UNetPlus—a generic deblurring U-Net never trained on specular highlight data—achieves the best WER across all three OCR engines (0.582 Textract, 0.942 EasyOCR, 0.824 Tesseract), outperforming the no-enhancement baseline (0.593, 0.950, 0.846). Fu et al.'s SHIQ model is the worst performer, with WER reaching 0.960–1.00 across all OCR engines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper benchmarks five models on SHDocs. MIMO-UNetPlus [5], a generic deblurring U-Net with no specular highlight training, is the only model that beats the no-enhancement OCR baseline across all three OCR engines (e.g., Textract WER 0.582 vs. 0.593), attributed to its deblurring effect improving character-level recognition. TSHRNet [9] achieves a marginal improvement over baseline (Textract WER 0.591 vs. 0.593) but is essentially on par. M2-Net [18] and Hu et al. [17] both degrade OCR performance substantially (Textract WER 0.698 and 0.708 respectively), with their diffuse/blurring effects harming character detail. Fu et al. [11] is the clear worst performer, with WER near 1.0 on all OCR engines, likely due to severe blurring and resolution loss in its outputs. On image quality metrics (Table 2), only M2-Net exceeds the baseline PSNR (32.72 vs. 32.18), while all other models score lower—highlighting the paper's central finding that standard image quality metrics do not correlate with downstream OCR utility.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends polarization-based data collection methods (Wen et al., Yang et al., Lei et al.) by devising an enclosed setup with fixed-angle polarized LED illumination and a polarization sensor (Sony IMX250MZR) that captures four polarization angles simultaneously, enabling near-perfect aligned specular highlight pairs through Stokes parameter reconstruction and minimum pooling. It departs from prior dataset efforts (Wu et al., Fu et al.) that required expensive multi-illumination rigs or produced synthetic data with known generalizability gaps, and from prior document-specific glare work (Hou et al., Chen et al.) that relied entirely on synthetic training data. The paper carves a specific niche: the first openly available real-world specular highlight dataset for document images, built on FUNSD forms placed in transparent filing pockets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals a critical disconnect: standard image quality metrics (PSNR, SSIM, UIQI) do not predict downstream OCR performance, and most specialized specular highlight removal models actually worsen OCR accuracy compared to no enhancement. This motivates rethinking how image enhancement for text is evaluated and suggests that deblurring-aware architectures may be more relevant than highlight-specific ones for document processing. Open questions include extending the dataset beyond English/US documents, handling unpolarized light sources that the polarization method cannot filter, and whether the findings generalize to other text-heavy domains (receipts, forms in other scripts). The hardware dependency on a specific polarization sensor remains a practical barrier to replication.
