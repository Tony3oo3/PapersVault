## Problem

Existing invisible image watermarking methods face a fundamental trade-off: frequency-domain methods resist traditional attacks (Gaussian noise, JPEG) but are easily removed by regeneration attacks using generative models, while latent-space methods resist regeneration but are vulnerable to noise. No prior approach simultaneously achieves strong robustness against both attack categories while maintaining acceptable image quality and flexible payload capacity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243563/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image (upscaled to 512×512) and a k-bit binary message (default 48 bits, configurable from 32 to 128) as input, and produces a watermarked image at approximately 31 dB PSNR along with a decoded k-bit message. Decoding is performed by a frozen DINOv2-small encoder whose first 128 output dimensions are projected onto k predefined unit direction vectors; the sign of each dot product yields one bit. No trained decoder network is required, and the number of direction vectors directly controls the payload size.

## Method

FreqMark embeds watermarks by optimizing a single perturbation tensor δ_m in the frequency domain of the VAE latent representation: the image is encoded by a frozen Stable Diffusion KL-VAE, transformed via FFT, perturbed by δ_m, then reconstructed via iFFT and the VAE decoder. The key insight is that latent-frequency perturbations are more semantically embedded than pixel-space perturbations (resisting regeneration) while retaining the global spectral structure that resists traditional attacks, yielding a synergistic dual-domain advantage. Training uses only δ_m as a free parameter with all networks frozen; the objective combines a hinge loss aligning DINOv2 features with target direction vectors, PSNR and LPIPS losses for quality preservation, and Gaussian noise augmentation in both latent and pixel spaces for robustness. The approach requires no network training, runs in ~5 minutes per image on a single A100 GPU, and allows users to freely trade off image quality, payload bits, and robustness via loss weights and bit count.

## Results

On ImageNet, FreqMark achieves an average bit accuracy of 0.973 across all attacks with PSNR 31.27 dB and SSIM 0.857, substantially outperforming SSL (0.914), Stable Signature (0.809), and DwtDctSvd (0.739). The headline advantage is against regeneration attacks: FreqMark maintains 0.938/0.924/0.969 bit accuracy under VAE-B/VAE-C/Diffusion attacks respectively, versus 0.793/0.777/0.743 for SSL and 0.630/0.645/0.534 for Stable Signature. On DiffusionDB, FreqMark achieves 0.967 average bit accuracy with similar margins. With 48-bit encoding, FreqMark exceeds 90% bit accuracy under all tested attack scenarios.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DwtDctSvd [14], FreqMark trades ~8 dB PSNR (31.3 vs 39.7) for dramatically superior robustness: average bit accuracy jumps from 0.739 to 0.973 on ImageNet, with regeneration attack accuracy improving from ~0.55 to ~0.93. Against SSL [20] (the closest competitor in paradigm), FreqMark matches PSNR (31.27 vs 31.04) and wins decisively on all three regeneration attacks (e.g., Diffusion: 0.969 vs 0.743 on ImageNet) while also matching or exceeding SSL on Gaussian noise. Against Stable Signature [19], FreqMark wins on every metric: higher PSNR (31.27 vs 28.74), higher SSIM, and substantially better regeneration robustness (Diffusion: 0.969 vs 0.534). Against StegaStamp [46] (Table 8, 100-bit setting), FreqMark wins on the critical Diffusion attack (0.933 vs 0.857) but concedes slightly on VAE-B (0.976 vs 0.981) and VAE-C (0.934 vs 0.984). Against Tree-Ring [50] (1-bit TPR@1%FPR), FreqMark achieves a higher average (0.994 vs 0.989) and a perfect 1.000 on Diffusion, while encoding 48× more information. On image quality metrics (Table 9), DwtDct [4] retains the best CLIP-FID (2.36 vs 3.84) and L2 (7.71 vs 52.95), reflecting its minimal pixel-level perturbation, but at the cost of near-random bit accuracy under most attacks.

## Relation to Prior Work

FreqMark sits at the intersection of two established watermarking paradigms: frequency-domain methods (DwtDctSvd, Cox et al.) that resist traditional signal-processing attacks but fail under regeneration, and latent-space methods (Stable Signature, RoSteALS) that survive regeneration but are fragile to Gaussian noise. It also inherits the training-free optimization philosophy of FNNS and SSL, which perturb the image directly rather than training an encoder-decoder pair. The key departure is optimizing a perturbation in the *latent frequency* space (FFT of the VAE latent), a domain not previously explored for watermarking, thereby combining the robustness profiles of both parent approaches without requiring any network training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As generative models make regeneration-based watermark removal trivially effective, FreqMark offers a practical, training-free defense that maintains >90% bit accuracy under 60-step diffusion regeneration while keeping PSNR near the VAE reconstruction floor (~31 dB). Its flexibility—user-selectable bit count, quality-robustness trade-off via loss weights, and customizable decoding vectors—makes it adaptable to diverse deployment scenarios. Open questions include whether spatial invariance (rotation, cropping) can be improved without sacrificing regeneration robustness, whether a higher-fidelity VAE would eliminate the residual quality loss, and whether the approach generalizes to video or 3D content.
