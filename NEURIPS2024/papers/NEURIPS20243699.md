## Problem

Differentiable mask pruning (DMP) methods optimize a continuous relaxation (soft network) as a proxy for the final pruned discrete network (hard network), but the discretization process creates a significant performance gap between the two that has been long overlooked. Existing mitigations such as decaying temperature or straight-through estimators introduce side effects—vanishing gradients or coarse mask updates—that undermine the very optimization they aim to fix.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243699/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard CNN or Transformer backbone (ResNet-50, MobileNetV3, WRN28-10, ViT, Swin Transformer) and a target FLOPs budget (e.g., 15%, 35%, 55%) as input, and produces a channel-wise binary pruning mask over dependency groups along with the corresponding pruned network. The mask is generated from learnable parameters normalized via softmax, where each element encodes the probability of retaining a prefix of channels, and the pruning threshold is derived as the mean mask value. The resulting pruned network is directly deployable without any fine-tuning stage.

## Method

S2HPruner formulates pruning as a joint optimization over weights θ and a continuous mask w, where the loss combines task loss on the soft network (θ⊙w), a resource regularization on FLOPs, and a KL-divergence gap measure between the hard network (θ⟨m̂⟩) and the soft network. During each training step, both the soft and hard networks are forwarded, and the hard network is distilled under the supervision of the soft network in a soft-to-hard paradigm. The key mechanism is a decoupled bidirectional knowledge distillation: the gradient path that would pull the soft network's weights toward the hard network (g_{G→θ⊙w→θ}) is explicitly blocked to prevent capacity degradation, while the gradient path that updates the mask parameters toward the hard network (g_{G→θ⊙w→w}) is preserved to refine the structure. This selective gradient routing allows the mask to be optimized by the discretization gap signal without sacrificing the soft network's representational capacity, effectively bridging the gap in a single training stage.

## Results

On CIFAR-100 with ResNet-50 at 15% FLOPs, S2HPruner achieves 79.77% Top-1 accuracy, surpassing the next-best method OTOv2 (77.04%) by 2.73% and RST-S (75.02%) by 4.75%. On Tiny ImageNet at the same 15% FLOPs constraint, the method reaches 67.02% Top-1, a 3.99% gain over OTOv2 (60.38%). On ImageNet with ResNet-50 at ~15% FLOPs, S2HPruner attains 73.23% Top-1 (only 2.92% drop from the unpruned 76.15%), outperforming OTOv2 (70.10%, 6.00% drop) and Refill (66.83%, 9.01% drop) by 3.13% and 6.40% respectively, all without any fine-tuning stage.

## Baselines & Comparisons

S2HPruner consistently outperforms all 23 compared baselines across CIFAR-100, Tiny ImageNet, and ImageNet without requiring any fine-tuning. On CIFAR-100 and Tiny ImageNet, the largest margins are over Group-SL (DepGraph), which collapses to near-chance accuracy (1.43% on MBV3 at 15% FLOPs), and over OTOv2, the strongest structured-pruning baseline, by 2.73% (CIFAR-100) and 3.99% (Tiny ImageNet) at 15% FLOPs. On ImageNet at ~15% FLOPs, S2HPruner (73.23%) beats OTOv2 (70.10%) by 3.13% and Refill (66.83%) by 6.40%; at ~25% FLOPs it edges out OTOv2 (74.43% vs 74.30%) and GReg (73.75–73.90%) while using fewer FLOPs (25.31% vs 28.70–32.68%). At higher FLOPs budgets (~55–76%), S2HPruner achieves negative accuracy drops (77.01% at 54.38% FLOPs, 77.53% at 76.19% FLOPs), exceeding the unpruned ResNet-50 baseline, whereas methods like SSS (75.44% at 84.94% FLOPs) and Refill (74.46% at 60% FLOPs) still suffer degradation. The method also generalizes to Transformers (ViT, Swin), outperforming RST-S by 1.87–2.76% on CIFAR-100, though no other Transformer-specific baselines are included in the comparison.

## Relation to Prior Work

S2HPruner extends the differentiable mask pruning (DMP) paradigm established by DMCP and AutoPruner, which treat a continuous-relaxation soft network as a performance proxy for the final discrete hard network. The paper draws a direct analogy to the discretization gap identified in DARTS and argues that DMP inherits the same problem but has overlooked it. The key departure is the introduction of a decoupled bidirectional knowledge distillation mechanism that jointly optimizes both the soft and hard networks in a single stage, rather than relying solely on the soft network's task loss or on coarse gradient approximations like STE or temperature annealing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By explicitly bridging the discretization gap during training, S2HPruner eliminates the need for a separate fine-tuning stage while achieving consistent gains (1–4%) over strong baselines across five architectures and three datasets, making it a practical single-stage pruning solution. The ablation showing that naive bidirectional KD (aligning soft toward hard) degrades accuracy by over 14% is a useful cautionary result for the community. Open questions remain: the method only prunes channel width (not block depth), is validated solely on image classification, and uses FLOPs rather than hardware-aware latency as the resource metric, limiting its applicability to more complex compression scenarios.
