## Problem

Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning in autoregressive language models generates intermediate steps left-to-right, leading to error accumulation, limited self-correction, and rigid computation-cost trade-offs. It remains unexplored whether diffusion language models, which denoise representations in parallel over timesteps, can support a CoT-style reasoning paradigm that offers greater flexibility and inherent self-correction.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243342/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text problem statement (e.g., a GSM8K math word problem, a multi-digit multiplication, or a boolean logic query) and produces a sequence of intermediate reasoning rationales followed by a final answer. During generation, the problem context is concatenated with the rationale tokens but kept noise-free (anchored as condition), while Gaussian or discrete noise is applied only to the rationale portion. In the multi-pass variant, rationales are generated one at a time, with each newly produced rationale appended to the conditioning context for the next pass, and a special <EOS> token lets the model decide the number of rationales dynamically.

## Method

DoT integrates CoT into diffusion language models by treating the rationale sequence as the target of the backward denoising process, using DiffuSeq-style partial noising so that the problem statement remains fixed while only the rationale tokens are corrupted and recovered. A multi-pass variant (DoT^MP) disentangles rationale generation into sequential passes, introducing causal inductive bias so later rationales are conditioned on previously generated ones. To improve self-correction, the paper introduces two training-time sampling strategies: scheduled sampling, which exposes the model to its own denoised predictions at earlier timesteps during training, and coupled sampling, which adds noise to previously generated rationales so the model learns to recover from upstream errors. At inference, a conditional ODE solver (adapted from DPM-Solver++) accelerates continuous diffusion sampling, and self-consistency decoding aggregates multiple diverse reasoning paths via majority vote over final answers.

## Results

DoT achieves 100% accuracy on 4×4 and 5×5 digit multiplication and Boolean Logic tasks while maintaining up to 27× higher throughput than GPT-2 CoT (e.g., 62.5 it/s vs. 2.3 it/s for DoT-from-scratch vs. GPT2-small CoT on 4×4). On the more complex GSM8K-Aug dataset, SEDD-medium DoT reaches 53.5% accuracy (59.4% with self-consistency), outperforming GPT2-medium CoT (43.9%) and GPT2-large CoT (44.8%) by roughly 9–10 percentage points. The multi-pass variant DoT^MP on SEDD-medium achieves 53.3%, confirming that the causal inductive bias helps on harder tasks. DoT also demonstrates flexible reasoning-efficiency trade-off: on simple tasks only 1–2 diffusion steps suffice, while on GSM8K accuracy improves continuously with more timesteps.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GPT-2 CoT (the primary autoregressive baseline), DoT wins decisively on GSM8K: SEDD-medium DoT (424M) achieves 53.5% accuracy versus 43.9% for GPT2-medium CoT (355M) and 44.8% for GPT2-large CoT (774M), a ~10-point gap despite the diffusion model being smaller. On simple tasks (4×4, 5×5 multiplication, Boolean Logic), DoT matches GPT-2 CoT at 100% accuracy but with 27× higher throughput (e.g., 62.5 vs. 2.3 it/s). Against Implicit CoT, DoT is substantially stronger on 5×5 multiplication (100% vs. 9.5% for GPT2-small Implicit CoT) and GSM8K (53.5% vs. 21.9% for GPT2-medium Implicit CoT), though Implicit CoT retains a throughput advantage on GSM8K (16.4 vs. 0.5 it/s). In the supplementary comparison with larger open-source LLMs, SEDD-medium DoT (424M) scores 53.5% on GSM8K-Aug, below Llama-7B CoT (59.0%) and Mistral-7B CoT (68.8%), but the authors note this is an unfair comparison given the 16–17× parameter gap and that DoT already outperforms same-scale autoregressive GPT-2.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions DoT as the first reasoning technique specifically designed for diffusion language models, drawing on DiffuSeq's partial noising and classifier-free conditioning for seq2seq generation, Plaid's VDM framework for continuous diffusion, and SEDD's discrete score matching for discrete diffusion. It is conceptually adjacent to Implicit CoT (Deng et al., 2023), which compresses reasoning into transformer layers, but DoT instead distributes reasoning across diffusion timesteps, enabling parallel denoising of all thoughts simultaneously. The paper also adapts DPM-Solver++ into a conditional ODE solver and repurposes scheduled sampling (Bengio et al., 2015) for the diffusion setting to improve self-correction, representing a departure from the gradient-based classifier guidance used in prior diffusion LM work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that diffusion language models are not limited to text generation but can perform structured multi-step reasoning with a natural self-correction mechanism absent in autoregressive models, where errors in early tokens irreversibly propagate. The finding that a 424M-parameter SEDD-medium DoT outperforms a 774M GPT2-large with CoT on GSM8K suggests that the diffusion paradigm may offer a more compute-efficient path to reasoning. However, the results are constrained by small base models (≤1.3B parameters) and require task-specific fine-tuning; whether DoT generalizes to open-ended reasoning or scales to LLM-level diffusion models remains an open question. The flexible computation-performance tradeoff via timestep control and the natural diversity enabling self-consistency are promising properties that could become important as diffusion LMs mature.
