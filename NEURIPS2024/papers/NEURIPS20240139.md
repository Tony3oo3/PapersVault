## Problem

CLIP's zero-shot generalization degrades under domain shift, and standard fine-tuning further harms OOD performance. Existing VLM-based DG methods (prompt learning, ensemble, full fine-tuning) do not explicitly enforce domain-invariance in the visual feature space, leaving domain-specific and class-irrelevant channels intact. The paper asks whether selectively refining CLIP's feature channels to retain only domain-invariant, class-relevant information can improve generalization without the cost of full encoder fine-tuning.

## Input / Output

The model consumes 224×224 RGB images and produces class labels via inner-product scoring against text embeddings. The text encoder output is computed by averaging 80 ImageNet template prompts per class to reduce prompt sensitivity. At inference, the final logits are an equal-weight ensemble of the adapter-refined visual embedding and CLIP's original final-layer embedding, both projected against the same text embedding matrix. Only the lightweight adapter (one Transformer layer + one MLP, ~786→512) is trained; both CLIP encoders remain frozen.

## Method

CLIPCEIL introduces a lightweight adapter g that fuses multi-scale CLIP visual features (the [cls] tokens from all 12 ViT layers) via a single self-attention Transformer layer, then projects the fused feature to 512-d and adds it residually to CLIP's original final embedding. Training optimizes three losses jointly: (1) a cross-entropy loss aligning refined image features with the correct text embedding, (2) a channel refinement loss that minimizes inter-domain variance while maximizing inter-class variance across all 512 channels (log-ratio formulation), and (3) a direction loss that aligns pairwise cosine directions in image space with those in text space using cross-domain, cross-class sample pairs to further strip domain-specific information. Parameters are updated with Beta Moving Average (BMA) following CLIPood, and the method requires no inference-time adaptation—only the small adapter is stored per task.

## Results

On the five standard domain generalization benchmarks (PACS, VLCS, OfficeHome, TerraIncognita, DomainNet), CLIPCEIL with a frozen CLIP ViT-B/16 encoder achieves an average accuracy of 77.3%, surpassing the best frozen-encoder baseline DPL (75.0%) by 2.3% and CLIP Zero-Shot (70.2%) by 7.1%. The fine-tuned variant CLIPCEIL++ reaches 79.1% average accuracy, outperforming the strongest full-fine-tuning baselines CLIPood (78.6%) and UniDG* (78.6%) by 0.5%. The largest individual gains appear on VLCS (88.4% vs. 86.9% for StyLIP) and TerraIncognita (53.0% vs. 52.6% for DPL), where domain shift is most pronounced.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among frozen-encoder methods, CLIPCEIL achieves the best average (77.3%), beating DPL (75.0%) by 2.3%, CoCoOp (74.7%) by 2.6%, and CLIP Zero-Shot (70.2%) by 7.1%. On individual datasets, CLIPCEIL leads on VLCS (88.4% vs. 86.9% StyLIP) and OfficeHome (85.4% vs. 84.6% StyLIP), though StyLIP edges it on PACS (98.1% vs. 97.6%). In the fine-tuned-encoder group, CLIPCEIL++ (79.1%) surpasses CLIPood (78.6%), UniDG* (78.6%), CAR-FT (78.5%), and VLV2-SD (77.7%) on average. CLIPCEIL++ wins on OfficeHome (87.7% vs. 87.4% VLV2-SD), TerraIncognita (62.0% vs. 60.4% CLIPood), and DomainNet (63.6% vs. 63.5% CLIPood), but trails UniDG* slightly on VLCS (85.2% vs. 86.3%) and TerraIncognita (62.0% vs. 62.4%). Notably, CLIPCEIL requires only a lightweight adapter (one Transformer layer + MLP) while CLIPood and CAR-FT fine-tune the entire ViT-B/16 encoder, making CLIPCEIL substantially more parameter-efficient per task.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on CLIP's frozen vision-language encoders and positions itself within the growing line of work on enhancing VLMs for domain generalization. It departs from the dominant prompt-learning paradigm (CoOp, CoCoOp, DPL, StyLIP) and full-encoder fine-tuning approaches (CLIPood, CAR-FT) by instead operating at the individual feature-channel level: a lightweight adapter fuses multi-scale ViT features via self-attention, while a channel refinement loss explicitly minimizes inter-domain variance and maximizes inter-class variance. The image-text alignment component, which uses a pairwise direction loss inspired by prior semantic-structure alignment work, addresses the misalignment introduced by the adapter. Conceptually, the channel-level invariance criterion connects to the broader DG representation-learning tradition (domain-invariant features, disentanglement) but applies it within a frozen VLM rather than training a new encoder from scratch.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CLIPCEIL offers a practical, lightweight path to improving CLIP's OOD generalization without the storage and compute burden of storing a full fine-tuned model per task, and without inference-time adaptation. The channel-refinement perspective provides an interpretable mechanism for understanding *why* certain CLIP features generalize poorly across domains. However, the method is limited to multi-source DG (inter-domain variance requires multiple source domains), and the gains over the strongest full-fine-tuning baselines (CLIPood, CAR-FT) are modest (~0.5% average). An open question is whether the channel-variance criterion can be adapted to single-source or few-source settings, and whether the approach transfers to other VLM backbones or tasks beyond classification.
