## Problem

Prior computational models of working memory relied on abstract categorical (one-hot) inputs and examined only single or few tasks, leaving it unclear how naturalistic, multidimensional object information is encoded, maintained, and dynamically updated in recurrent networks during concurrent encoding, retention, and retrieval. This paper asks how task-optimized RNNs select and transform object properties in their latent space when performing dynamic N-back tasks with ecologically relevant stimuli.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243190/cropped_page3_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes sequences of 6 rendered 3D object images (ShapeNet: 4 categories × 2 identities × multiple view angles × 4 spatial locations) passed through a frozen ImageNet-pretrained ResNet50, whose 2048-channel penultimate features are reduced via point-wise convolution and concatenated with a 6-digit binary task index vector (encoding feature type and N-back depth). At each executive timestep the RNN outputs a three-way decision (match, non-match, no action), while the RNN hidden states at every timestep serve as the primary object of geometric analysis for decoding and transformation studies.

## Method

The architecture is a two-stage sensory-cognitive model: a frozen ResNet50 provides perceptual embeddings that feed a trainable RNN (vanilla RNN, GRU, or LSTM) trained via supervised cross-entropy loss on nine N-back variants (N∈{1,2,3} × feature∈{Location, Identity, Category}) under three training regimes (single-task-single-feature, single-task-multi-feature, multi-task-multi-feature). The core analytical contribution is a suite of decoder-based geometric probes: SVM decoders identify which object properties are represented in encoding versus memory subspaces, cross-task decoding reveals whether representations are shared or task-specific, a Frobenius-norm orthogonalization index quantifies feature separability relative to the perceptual space, and orthogonal Procrustes analysis extracts rotation matrices characterizing how encoding subspaces transform into memory subspaces across time and stimuli. The Procrustes results show that the encoding-to-memory transformation is shared across different stimuli but varies across time steps, supporting a chronological memory subspace mechanism (H2) over both slot-based (H1) and stimulus-specific (H3) alternatives.

## Results

All sensory-cognitive models (CNN+RNN) achieved >95% training accuracy and >90% validation accuracy on novel object angles across the 9 N-back task variants. In multi-task multi-feature (MTMF) models, task-irrelevant object properties were decoded at >85% accuracy from RNN hidden states, confirming simultaneous maintenance of full object representations. Cross-task decoding revealed that vanilla RNNs share a common representational subspace across all 9 tasks, while GRUs and LSTMs use highly task-specific subspaces. Procrustes analysis confirmed that the encoding-to-memory transformation is a structured rotation that is consistent across stimuli (Eq. 3 holds) but not stable over time (Eq. 2 fails), supporting the chronological memory subspace hypothesis (H2) over slot-based (H1) or stimulus-specific (H3) mechanisms.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not propose a new method to benchmark against external baselines; instead, it compares three RNN architectures (vanilla RNN, GRU, LSTM) and three training regimes (STSF, STMF, MTMF) on the same N-back task suite. Vanilla RNNs (512 units) achieve lower accuracy than gated counterparts (256 units) at comparable parameter counts, but uniquely produce task-general representations: cross-task decoder generalization is high for vanilla RNNs but low for GRUs and LSTMs. In terms of representational geometry, the RNN encoding space is less orthogonalized than the CNN perceptual space (lower Frobenius-norm-based orthogonalization index O), yet this does not impair task performance. The chronological memory hypothesis (H2) is supported over the slot-based model (H1) because decoders trained on encoding-space activity do not generalize to later memory timesteps, and over the stimulus-specific hypothesis (H3) because encoding-space representations are shared across all stimuli in the sequence (validation ≈ cross-stimulus generalization accuracy in Fig. 4d).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of RNN-based working memory models (Mante et al. 2013, Yang et al. 2019, Xie et al. 2022, Piwek et al. 2023) by replacing the categorical/one-hot stimuli used in prior work with naturalistic 3D-rendered objects from ShapeNet, and by scaling from single-task to a 9-task N-back suite. It repurposes standard sensory-cognitive architectures (ResNet-50 + vanilla RNN/GRU/LSTM) and applies Procrustes-based representational geometry analyses to the RNN latent space, a methodological toolkit borrowed from population coding analyses in neuroscience. The paper positions itself against both the slot-based WM model (Luck & Vogel, 1997) and the resource-based model (Alvarez & Cavanagh, 2004), using the N-back paradigm specifically because it demands concurrent encoding, retention, and retrieval rather than the stable-fixation maintenance studied in delayed-match-to-sample tasks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The findings provide concrete, testable predictions for neural recordings in prefrontal cortex: gated RNNs should show task-specific representational subspaces while gateless circuits should show shared ones, and memory maintenance should follow chronological rather than slot-based dynamics. The de-orthogonalization result challenges the intuitive assumption that task-optimized networks should increase separability of feature axes, suggesting that lower-dimensional, less-orthogonal representations may be more efficient for readout. Key open questions include whether these geometric patterns generalize beyond N-back to other WM paradigms, how they scale with network size, and whether biological circuits exhibit the same gated-vs-gateless representational trade-off. The absence of released code and the restriction to three RNN architectures limit immediate reproducibility and broader architectural comparisons.
