## Problem

Open-set 3D object retrieval requires generalizing to entirely unseen categories at test time, but existing methods either assume closed-set conditions or neglect the part-assembly structure that could bridge seen and unseen categories. Part-level shapes share commonalities across object categories, yet this bottom-up assembly perspective remains underexploited for open-set retrieval, where distribution skew between seen and unseen categories severely degrades performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243972/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes 3D point clouds (2048 points for OP-SHNP/OP-INTRA, 1024 for OP-COSEG) with part segmentation annotations, extracting per-part features as the average PointNet point feature over each region (top 4 parts per object). It produces a fuzzy embedding vector per object, which is used for L2-distance-based similarity ranking between query and target sets drawn from unseen categories. The retrieval objective minimizes a margin-based expected risk over query-target pairs, pulling same-category pairs together and pushing different-category pairs apart in embedding space.

## Method

The HAFR framework operates in two stages. First, the Isomorphic Assembly Embedding (IAE) module applies Hypergraph Isomorphism Convolution (HIConv) over an assembly hypergraph (vertices = part features, hyperedges = parts of the same object) to produce order- and repetition-invariant isomorphism embeddings, then compresses them via per-part auto-encoders and aggregates into a single assembly embedding, trained with an Assembly Loss (pulling part codes together) and a Cross-Part Loss (intra-part reconstruction plus inter-part decoder consistency). Second, the Structured Fuzzy Reconstruction (SFR) module builds a leverage hypergraph combining local-certainty hyperedges (all vertices of the same seen category) and global-uncertainty hyperedges (KNN neighborhoods), performs hypergraph convolution to propagate information from seen to unseen categories, and finally reconstructs propagation embeddings in a fuzzy space via a memory bank of invariant anchors, trained with a Fuzzy Reconstruction Loss and Cross-Entropy Loss to mitigate distribution skew.

## Results

HAFR achieves the best retrieval performance across all three OpenPart datasets on every metric. On OP-COSEG, HAFR reaches 0.7015 mAP, 0.7629 NDCG, and 0.3604 ANMRR, improving mAP by ~8.7% over the second-best method (HGM²R at 0.6452). On OP-SHNP, HAFR attains 0.5947 mAP and 0.2239 ANMRR, and on OP-INTRA it reaches 0.5750 mAP and 0.6382 NDCG, consistently outperforming all six baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HAFR outperforms all six baselines on every metric across all three datasets. The closest competitor is HGM²R (the only other open-set 3DOR method), which HAFR surpasses by 2.1 mAP points on OP-SHNP (0.5947 vs 0.5736), 2.1 points on OP-INTRA (0.5750 vs 0.5545), and a substantial 5.6 points on OP-COSEG (0.7015 vs 0.6452). The closed-set baselines (MMJN, TCL, SDML, MMSAE) cluster tightly around 0.56–0.57 mAP on OP-SHNP and 0.63–0.64 on OP-COSEG, confirming that open-set generalization is the primary bottleneck. PROSER, the open-set recognition method adapted for retrieval, performs comparably to the closed-set baselines, suggesting that its placeholder-based approach does not transfer well to the assembly-retrieval setting. The ANMRR gap is particularly pronounced on OP-COSEG, where HAFR achieves 0.3604 versus 0.4355 for HGM²R, indicating substantially better ranking quality for unseen categories.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three established lines of work: 3D object retrieval (MMJN, TCL, HGNN), open-set learning (PROSER, HGM²R), and 3D shape assembly from computer graphics. It departs from prior 3DOR methods by replacing global object-level features with a bottom-up part-assembly representation, and from prior open-set methods by explicitly modeling the distribution skew between seen and unseen categories through a leverage hypergraph and fuzzy reconstruction. The hypergraph isomorphism convolution (HIConv) draws on graph isomorphism network ideas but applies them to a hypergraph structure where parts of the same object form hyperedges, enabling order-invariant assembly. The fuzzy reconstruction via a memory bank borrows from prototype-based open-set recognition but applies it to mitigate geometric-semantic distribution skew rather than simply rejecting unknown classes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The introduction of three part-annotated open-set 3D retrieval benchmarks (OP-SHNP, OP-INTRA, OP-COSEG) provides a much-needed evaluation protocol for assembly-based open-set retrieval, a setting where no prior benchmarks existed. The part-assembly perspective offers a practical pathway for open-set 3D retrieval when object-level labels are incomplete, which is common in real-world applications. However, the method currently assumes a fixed number of parts (top-4) and relies on pre-computed part segmentation, limiting its applicability to objects with variable or unknown part structures. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to truly variable part counts, whether the part segmentation quality is a bottleneck, and whether the leverage hypergraph construction scales to larger category spaces.
