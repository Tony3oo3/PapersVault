## Problem

Existing subgraph-based DDI prediction methods rely on a fixed k-hop subgraph selection strategy and hand-designed encoding functions for all drug pairs, failing to capture the diverse and nuanced interaction semantics (e.g., asymmetric metabolic vs. symmetric phenotypic interactions) present in dense drug interaction networks. This paper addresses the underexplored problem of customizing both subgraph selection and encoding components per query, leveraging neural architecture search to find data-adaptive configurations.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a multi-relational drug interaction network G_DDI with drug nodes and typed interaction edges, along with a query drug pair (u, v) whose interaction type is to be predicted. Beyond the predicted interaction label(s), the method outputs a per-query subgraph scope (i, j) specifying the ego-network hop counts for each drug, and a data-specific encoding function architecture composed of selected operations from the message-passing space. The subgraph selection space is bounded by a threshold η (set to 3), yielding η² candidate subgraphs per query, while the encoding space spans four operation categories (MES, AGG, COM, ACT) with multiple candidate functions each.

## Method

CSSE-DDI formulates a bi-level optimization problem that jointly searches over a subgraph selection space (unions of i-hop and j-hop ego-networks) and a subgraph encoding space (combinations of message-computing, aggregation, combination, and activation operations). To make the discrete subgraph selection differentiable, the method applies a Gumbel-Softmax relaxation over encoded subgraph representations, and avoids explicit subgraph sampling by approximating each candidate subgraph representation as the concatenation of i-layer and j-layer node embeddings from a single full-graph encoding pass. For efficient and stable search, the method adopts a single-path one-shot (SPOS) supernet training strategy combined with a message-aware partitioning scheme that splits the supernet into four sub-supernets based on the message-computing function type, improving performance estimation consistency. Architecture search is then performed via natural gradient descent on the validation set, with the optimal subgraph per query selected by preserving the highest-probability scope.

## Results

On DrugBank (multi-class), CSSE-DDI achieves 92.08% F1, 95.56% Accuracy, and 94.72% Cohen's κ, outperforming the best subgraph-based method KnowDDI (90.06% F1) and the best GNN-based method TransFOL (89.97% F1). On TWOSIDES (multi-label), CSSE-DDI reaches 95.47% ROC-AUC, 94.21% PR-AUC, and 89.76% AP@50, surpassing KnowDDI (95.05% ROC-AUC) and TransFOL (94.16% ROC-AUC). The method also outperforms its own ablated variants (CSSE-DDI-FS: 86.31% F1; CSSE-DDI-FF: 80.96% F1 on DrugBank), confirming the necessity of jointly customizing both subgraph selection and encoding.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CSSE-DDI consistently outperforms all 15 baselines across both datasets and all metrics. Against the strongest subgraph-based competitor KnowDDI, CSSE-DDI gains +2.02 F1 on DrugBank (92.08 vs 90.06) and +0.42 ROC-AUC on TWOSIDES (95.47 vs 95.05). Against the best full-graph GNN method TransFOL, the gains are larger: +2.11 F1 on DrugBank and +1.31 ROC-AUC on TWOSIDES. Among NAS-based baselines, CSSE-DDI surpasses AutoGEL by +15.21 F1 on DrugBank (92.08 vs 76.87) and +1.36 ROC-AUC on TWOSIDES (95.47 vs 94.11), and MR-GNAS by +17.84 F1 on DrugBank. In the inductive S1 setting (Table 4), CSSE-DDI still outperforms all GNN and subgraph baselines (e.g., 88.33 ROC-AUC vs KnowDDI's 84.23 on TWOSIDES), though it trails the purpose-built EmerGNN (87.42 ROC-AUC on DrugBank vs CSSE-DDI's 37.24 F1), indicating that specialized inductive designs retain an edge for novel-drug scenarios.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the subgraph-based DDI prediction framework established by SumGNN and KnowDDI, which extract fixed k-hop ego-network subgraphs and apply hand-designed message-passing encoders. It also draws on the one-shot NAS paradigm (DARTS, SPOS) and multi-relational message-passing search spaces (MR-GNAS, AutoGEL), but departs from both by jointly searching over a discrete subgraph selection space (varying hop counts per drug in a pair) and a continuous encoding function space simultaneously. The key conceptual departure is that prior subgraph-based DDI methods use a single fixed subgraph scope for all queries, while prior GNAS methods search only over encoding operations without adapting the input subgraph itself.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that data-specific subgraph scopes and encoding functions can be automatically discovered, the paper provides a more interpretable and adaptive pipeline for DDI prediction, with searched subgraphs revealing pharmacokinetic and metabolic reasoning paths. However, the method is evaluated on only two relatively small datasets (1,710 and 604 drugs), and the inductive (S1) setting results show CSSE-DDI still trails the purpose-built EmerGNN, suggesting the NAS approach may not fully transfer to unseen-drug scenarios. Open questions include whether the joint search scales to much larger drug interaction networks, whether hyperparameter co-search would yield further gains, and whether the relaxation and approximation strategies generalize beyond ego-network-based subgraph spaces.
