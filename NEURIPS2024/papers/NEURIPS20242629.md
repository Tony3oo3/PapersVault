## Problem

For general Latent MDPs (LMDPs) with M=O(1) latent contexts and no structural assumptions, no algorithm was known to provably match the Ω(SA)^M lower bound, with existing approaches either requiring separation assumptions (H ≫ Ω(SA)) or identical transition dynamics across contexts. The paper resolves this open question by providing the first sample-efficient exploration algorithm for LMDPs without any additional distributional assumptions, breaking the curse of horizon that plagues general POMDPs.

## Input / Output

The algorithm operates on an episodic LMDP with state space S, action space A, horizon H > 2M, and M constant latent contexts selected at the start of each episode but never revealed to the agent. The agent observes only states and bounded rewards (|r| ≤ 1) during interaction. The output is an ε-optimal history-dependent policy with high probability, accompanied by a sample complexity bound of Õ((MSAH·log(1/ε))^O(M)) episodes, which matches the known lower bound up to polynomial factors in the exponent.

## Method

The core contribution is a new off-policy evaluation (OPE) lemma for LMDPs that bounds the TV distance between trajectory distributions of any target policy by a sum over partial trajectory distributions of segmented behavioral policies, weighted by a novel LMDP coverage coefficient defined over tuples of at most d=2M−1 state-action pairs. The LMDP-OMLE algorithm iteratively: (i) finds a memoryless policy whose trajectory distribution differs between models in the confidence set, (ii) collects data using segmented exploration policies that switch between previously found test policies at random checkpoints with optional uniform-action interventions, and (iii) refines the confidence set via maximum likelihood estimation. A key sufficiency lemma shows that matching trajectory distributions for all memoryless policies suffices to bound the TV distance for all history-dependent policies, reducing the search space and enabling a coverage-doubling argument that guarantees termination in O(MS²H)·log(MSAH/ε) iterations.

## Results

The main theoretical result (Theorem 4.5) establishes that LMDP-OMLE terminates after at most K = O(MS²H)·log(MSAH/ε) iterations, generating N ≳ (M⁴S⁶A⁴H⁷·log(MSAH/ε))^(2M−1) · M⁴H²·log(K|Θ|/η)/ε² episodes, and outputs an ε-optimal policy with probability at least 1−η. This is the first sample-efficient algorithm for general LMDPs without separation assumptions, breaking the curse of horizon (Ω(A^H)) that plagued prior approaches. The upper bound scales as Õ(MSAH·log(1/ε))^O(M), which matches the known lower bound of Ω(SA)^M [41] up to polynomial factors in the exponent.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical experiments or baseline comparisons. The key comparison is against the existing lower bound of Ω(SA)^M established by Kwon et al. [41], which the proposed LMDP-OMLE matches up to polynomial factors. Prior LMDP algorithms either require strict separation assumptions (e.g., H ≫ Ω(SA) [24]) or assume similar transition dynamics across latent contexts [40, 42, 43], both of which are dropped here. The MDP-OMLE analysis (Theorem 3.3) yields Õ(H⁶S³A³/ε²) sample complexity, which is suboptimal compared to the minimax rate [7] but demonstrates the OPE-based analysis framework. The paper explicitly notes a remaining polynomial gap between the upper and lower bounds in the exponent, identifying this as future work.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Optimistic MLE (OMLE) framework [Liu et al., STOC 2023] for partially observable sequential decision making, adapting its iterative confidence-set refinement procedure to the Latent MDP (LMDP) setting first formalized by Kwon et al. [NeurIPS 2021], which also established the Ω(SA)^M lower bound. The key departure is analytical: rather than relying on eluder-dimension-type conditions or core-test policies (which are unavailable for general LMDPs), the paper introduces a new off-policy evaluation (OPE) lens—specifically, a novel LMDP coverage coefficient defined over segmented policies with interventions—to bound the number of OMLE iterations. This sidesteps the two fundamental limitations of existing POMDP approaches: the need for a priori core-tests and the full-rankness of state-observation emission matrices.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first sample-efficient algorithm for general LMDPs without separation or similar-dynamics assumptions, resolving a stated open question and breaking the curse of horizon (Ω(A^H)) for the M=O(1) case. The OPE-based analytical framework is potentially transferable to other partially observed settings where core-tests are unavailable, offering a new tool for the broader POMDP literature. However, the upper bound still has a polynomial gap in the exponent relative to the lower bound, the result is restricted to tabular LMDPs with constant M, and no regret guarantees or practical algorithms are provided—leaving open whether the OPE perspective can yield tighter bounds or extend to general history-dependent behavioral policies.
