## Problem

Message-passing GNNs suffer from over-squashing (topological bottlenecks limiting long-range information flow) and over-smoothing (repeated aggregation making nodes indistinguishable), which prior work assumed were diametrically opposed: adding edges to increase the spectral gap alleviates over-squashing but accelerates over-smoothing. This paper challenges that assumption by showing, via the Braess paradox, that certain edge deletions can simultaneously increase the spectral gap and reduce detrimental smoothing, thereby addressing both pathologies at once.

## Input / Output

The method consumes only the graph adjacency matrix (and optionally node features/labels for downstream GNN training) and produces a rewired or pruned adjacency matrix. A key design choice is that the spectral gap optimization is entirely feature-agnostic: the PROXYDELETE and PROXYADD algorithms rank candidate edges using only the current spectral gap, its leading eigenvector, and node degrees, requiring no access to node features or labels. This enables pruning at initialization before any training data is seen, which is the setting exploited for graph lottery tickets.

## Method

The core mechanism is a greedy edge-modification algorithm that scores each candidate edge using a first-order matrix perturbation proxy: λ' ≈ λ + Δw_{u,v}((f_u − f_v)² − λ(f_u² + f_v²)), where Δw = −1 for deletion and +1 for addition, and f is the leading eigenvector of the normalized Laplacian. After selecting the edge that maximizes the proxy spectral gap change, the algorithm updates the eigenvector and eigenvalue via a few power-iteration steps (ARPACK), keeping per-iteration cost at O(|E|). A second, more conservative variant (ELDANADD/ELDANDELETE) uses Eldan's Lemma 3.1 criterion, which provides a sufficient condition guaranteeing spectral gap increase upon deletion but requires computing projections onto the perturbed graph's top eigenvector, making it O(|V|) per edge and less scalable. The paper proves via a minimal ring-graph example that deleting an inter-class edge increases the spectral gap while reducing the expected mean-aggregation distortion between classes, formally establishing that over-squashing and over-smoothing need not be traded off.

## Results

On the Long Range Graph Benchmark, ProxyAdd+GCN achieves 0.2213 F1 on PascalVOC-SP and 0.2465 MAE on Peptides-Struct, outperforming FoSR (0.2157 F1, 0.2499 MAE) and DRew (0.1848 F1, 0.2781 MAE). On the large heterophilic Roman-Empire dataset, GAT+ProxyGap reaches 86.07% accuracy with 5 layers (vs. 81.88% for GAT+FoSR) and 86.44% with 20 layers (vs. 86.70% for GAT+Eldan), showing that spectral pruning maintains performance at depth where baselines degrade. For graph lottery tickets, PROXYDELETE-UGS achieves 69.26% accuracy on Cora at ~79% graph sparsity, slightly outperforming the data-dependent UGS baseline (68.46%) without using any node features or labels.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against FoSR (Karhadkar et al., 2023), the proposed PROXYADD and PROXYDELETE methods outperform on heterophilic datasets (e.g., Texas: 74.21% vs. 63.50% for GCN; Chameleon: 54.30% vs. 46.64%) and on LRGB (PascalVOC-SP: 0.2213 vs. 0.2157 F1), but FoSR wins on homophilic datasets (Cora: 91.44% vs. 89.10% for PROXYADD). Against DRew (Gutteridge et al., 2023), both ProxyAdd and ProxyDelete clearly outperform on all three LRGB tasks. Against SDRF (Topping et al., 2022) and DIGL (Gasteiger et al., 2019), the proposed methods achieve substantially higher accuracy on all six heterophilic datasets (e.g., Actor: 43.45–43.65% vs. 31.67% SDRF and 26.33% DIGL). Against BORF (Nguyen et al., 2023), PROXYDELETE outperforms on Texas (72.36% vs. 49.40%) and Chameleon (55.88% vs. 61.50% for BORF on Squirrel only). Against UGS (Chen et al., 2021) for lottery tickets, PROXYDELETE-UGS matches or slightly exceeds UGS on Cora (69.26% vs. 68.46%) and Pubmed (75.25% vs. 76.90%), while ELDANDELETE-UGS is competitive on all three datasets without requiring node features or labels.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on Eldan et al.'s (2017) formalization of the Braess paradox for spectral gaps in random graphs, adapting their Lemma 3.1 as a scoring criterion for edge deletions, and on Bojchevski & Günnemann's (2019) matrix perturbation theory approach for efficient eigenvalue updates in adversarial graph editing. It departs from the dominant graph rewiring paradigm (FoSR, SDRF, DIGL) that exclusively adds edges to increase the spectral gap, by showing that edge deletions can also increase it. The paper further bridges three previously disconnected literatures—over-squashing mitigation, over-smoothing analysis, and graph lottery tickets—arguing that spectral-gap-based pruning explains why moderate graph sparsification improves generalization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical contribution is a computationally efficient (O(N·|E|)) spectral rewiring framework that outperforms FoSR in spectral gap improvement while simultaneously slowing over-smoothing, particularly in heterophilic settings where inter-class edges are preferentially deleted. A notable limitation, acknowledged in Appendix B, is that the method is purely structural and can be counterproductive when label configurations actively conflict with graph topology, suggesting that feature-aware variants may be needed. Open questions include whether the Braess-paradox-based deletion criterion generalizes to directed or weighted graphs, and whether the spectral gap proxy remains accurate under repeated perturbations without full eigendecomposition updates.
