## Problem

Existing debiasing methods rely on identifying bias-conflicting samples to amplify task-relevant features, but their detection precision is limited, risking amplification of malignant bias instead. Directly applying Self-Influence (a standard mislabeled-sample detection tool) to biased datasets fails because bias-conflicting samples still share task-related features with bias-aligned samples, unlike truly mislabeled samples. The paper asks: under what conditions can Self-Influence effectively separate bias-conflicting from bias-aligned samples?

## Input / Output

The method consumes a biased training dataset (images at 32×32 to 224×224, or text for NLP benchmarks) and a pre-trained biased model (from ERM or any prior debiasing method). It first trains a lightweight detection model for only 5 epochs with GCE loss, then computes Self-Influence using only the last-layer Hessian to rank samples. The output is a small pivotal subset (top-k=100 per class, intersected across 3 random initializations) with high bias-conflicting concentration, plus a fine-tuned debiased model after ~100 additional iterations. No bias annotations, group labels, or unbiased validation set are required at any stage.

## Method

The key insight is that Self-Influence can detect bias-conflicting samples if the model is deliberately made heavily biased by (a) computing SI at an early training epoch (t=5, within the 500–2000 iteration window where primary weight directions form) and (b) using Generalized Cross Entropy loss to emphasize easier-to-learn bias-aligned samples. This Bias-Conditioned Self-Influence (BCSI) is computed via the last-layer Hessian inverse for efficiency. The top-k BCSI-ranked samples per class are intersected across three randomly initialized detection models to form a pivotal set with ~16% higher precision than a single run. The pre-trained biased model is then fine-tuned for 100 iterations using a combined loss: cross-entropy on the pivotal set plus a counterweighted (λ=0.1) cross-entropy on a mini-batch from the remaining training set, which provides robustness across both high- and low-bias severities.

## Results

The paper's method (applied as a post-hoc fine-tuning step on top of existing debiasing methods) consistently improves accuracy across all datasets. On CIFAR-10C (1% bias-conflicting), Ours+SelecMix achieves 46.18% accuracy vs. SelecMix alone at 40.14% (+6.04 pp), and on CIFAR-10C (5%) reaches 59.66% vs. 54.86% (+4.80 pp). On Waterbirds, Ours+SelecMix achieves 89.67% worst-group accuracy vs. SelecMix's 74.72% (+14.95 pp). On BFFHQ, Ours+SelecMix reaches 65.80% minority-group accuracy vs. SelecMix's 63.07% (+2.73 pp).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's method is designed as a complementary post-hoc remedy, so all comparisons are framed as 'baseline' vs. 'Ours + baseline.' Against SelecMix (the strongest baseline), Ours+SelecMix wins on every dataset: +6.04 pp on CIFAR-10C (1%), +4.80 pp on CIFAR-10C (5%), +2.73 pp on BFFHQ, and a dramatic +14.95 pp on Waterbirds (89.67% vs. 74.72% worst-group accuracy). Against LfF, Ours+LfF improves CMNIST (1%) from 89.50% to 94.10% and CIFAR-10C (1%) from 31.15% to 35.29%. Against DFA, Ours+DFA gains +5.96 pp on CIFAR-10C (20% bias-conflicting: 66.30% vs. 60.34%) and +1.10 pp on NICO (45.69% vs. 44.59%). Against ERM, Ours+ERM improves CIFAR-10C (20%) from 59.47% to 62.78% and NICO from 39.56% to 43.54%. The only case where the method underperforms the baseline is Ours+ERM on CIFAR-10C (90%, near-unbiased), where ERM already achieves 76.03% and Ours+ERM drops to 73.57%, reflecting that fine-tuning on a small pivotal set can be slightly harmful when bias is negligible.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes Self-Influence (SI), a standard tool from noisy-label detection (Koh & Liang, 2017), for identifying bias-conflicting samples in biased datasets, drawing a conceptual parallel between mislabeled samples and bias-conflicting samples as minority points contradicting dominant learned features. The critical departure is the finding that naive SI fails in bias settings (precision <25%), and that Bias-Conditioned Self-Influence (BCSI)—computed at early training epochs under Generalized Cross Entropy to force the model to exploit bias before learning task features—restores effective separation. For the remedy step, the paper builds on last-layer retraining (Kirichenko et al., 2023) and surgical fine-tuning (Lee et al., 2023), but replaces the impractical requirement of a curated unbiased set with a small pivotal subset constructed via BCSI, and extends to full fine-tuning when the feature extractor is also biased.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method offers a practical, annotation-free post-hoc debiasing procedure that is complementary to existing techniques (LfF, DFA, SelecMix) and can further rectify already-debiased models, addressing a gap where prior methods assume sufficient bias-aligned samples and degrade in low-bias regimes. The systematic analysis of why SI fails and what conditions restore it (early epoch + GCE) provides a reusable diagnostic for influence-function-based sample identification beyond bias. Limitations include the reliance on a fixed top-k without knowledge of bias severity, the basic fine-tuning remedy (sample weighting or curriculum learning are left as future work), and the fact that the pivotal set is imperfect—its precision varies substantially across bias ratios (45% at 0.5% to 97% at 5% for CIFAR10C).
